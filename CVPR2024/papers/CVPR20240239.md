## Problem

It remains an open question whether the scaling law observed in high-level vision tasks also applies to IQA, which is closely tied to low-level distortion cues. Large-scale pretrained ViTs excel at modeling non-local dependencies but lack the local structure and inductive bias that IQA critically requires, while the scarcity of labeled IQA data makes full fine-tuning of such models impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240239/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a scalar no-reference quality score. The image is simultaneously fed into a frozen pretrained CNN (yielding multi-scale feature maps at multiple depths) and a frozen large-scale pretrained ViT (yielding patch tokens at 16×16 resolution). The final score is regressed from the ViT's CLS token through a single-layer head, trained with a PLCC-induced loss that directly optimizes rank correlation with ground-truth MOS values.

## Method

LoDa introduces a parameter-efficient adaptation framework (~9M trainable parameters) that injects CNN-derived local distortion features into a frozen large-scale pretrained ViT via cross-attention. A local distortion extractor applies trainable convolutions and average pooling to multi-scale CNN features, producing compact multi-scale distortion tokens. A local distortion injector then uses multi-head cross-attention where ViT tokens serve as queries and distortion tokens as keys/values, with an adapter-style down-projection to a smaller dimension to keep parameter overhead minimal. A learnable scalar gate (initialized near zero) controls the injection strength, preserving the pretrained ViT's feature distribution. Only the extractor, injector, and regression head are trained; both the ViT and CNN backbones remain frozen throughout.

## Results

LoDa (9M trainable parameters) achieves state-of-the-art SRCC on KADID-10k (0.931), KonIQ-10k (0.932), LIVE (0.975), LIVEC (0.876), SPAQ (0.925), and FLIVE (0.578), outperforming all prior methods with substantially fewer trainable parameters. On TID2013, DEIQT (24M) retains a slight edge (SRCC 0.892 vs. 0.869). In cross-dataset generalization (Table 2), LoDa achieves the best SRCC on all four transfer settings (e.g., 0.805 on FLIVE→LIVEC, 0.811 on KonIQ→LIVEC), and in data-efficient settings (Table 5), LoDa trained on only 60% of KonIQ-10k matches its full-data performance (SRCC 0.928 vs. 0.932).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoDa (9M trainable params) outperforms the strongest prior method DEIQT (24M) on KADID-10k (SRCC 0.931 vs. 0.889, +4.2%), KonIQ-10k (0.932 vs. 0.921), SPAQ (0.925 vs. 0.919), and FLIVE (0.578 vs. 0.571), while DEIQT retains a narrow lead on TID2013 (0.892 vs. 0.869) and LIVE (0.980 vs. 0.975). Against the much larger LIQE (151M) and TReS (152M), LoDa achieves higher SRCC on KADID-10k (0.931 vs. 0.930 and 0.859) and KonIQ-10k (0.932 vs. 0.919 and 0.915) with roughly 17× fewer trainable parameters. Compared to QPT-ResNet50 (24M), which requires IQA-specific pretraining, LoDa matches or exceeds it on KonIQ-10k (0.932 vs. 0.927) and FLIVE (0.578 vs. 0.575) without any task-specific pretraining. Among efficient fine-tuning methods (Table 6), LoDa surpasses Adapter-ViT (0.931 vs. 0.914 on KADID-10k), LoRA-ViT (0.931 vs. 0.913), and VPT-ViT (0.931 vs. 0.889), with the largest gap on KADID-10k where local distortion features are most critical. In cross-dataset transfer (Table 2), LoDa leads all baselines on every setting, e.g., 0.805 SRCC on FLIVE→LIVEC vs. 0.781 for DEIQT.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoDa carves new ground at the intersection of three established threads: (1) IQA methods that have historically relied on smaller backbones (ResNet-50, ViT-B) with full fine-tuning or IQA-specific pretraining (DEIQT, Re-IQA, QPT-ResNet50); (2) large-scale pretrained vision models (ImageNet-21K ViTs, MAE) that have not previously been applied to IQA; and (3) parameter-efficient adaptation techniques from NLP (Adapter, LoRA, VPT) that have only recently been ported to vision. The key conceptual departure is the explicit injection of multi-scale CNN local distortion features into a frozen large-scale ViT via cross-attention, rather than relying on the ViT alone or performing full fine-tuning. The adapter-inspired dimension reduction for the cross-attention mechanism is a practical architectural choice that keeps trainable parameters to ~9M.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that the scaling law for high-level vision tasks extends to IQA, a task traditionally viewed as low-level, by showing that ImageNet-21K-pretrained ViT-B outperforms ViT-T and ViT-S with the same adaptation framework. This opens the door to leveraging ever-larger foundation models for perceptual quality tasks without prohibitive training costs. A notable open question is why multi-modal (CLIP-style) pretrained weights underperform single-modal ImageNet-21K weights, suggesting that abstract semantic representations may conflict with low-level distortion sensitivity. The data-efficiency result (60% training data matching full-data SOTA) is practically important given the extreme cost of IQA annotation, and the technique of cross-attention-based feature injection from a frozen CNN into a frozen ViT is a reusable pattern for other low-level vision tasks.
