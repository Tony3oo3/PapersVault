## Problem

Existing multi-view diffusion models like MVDream use unconstrained cross-view self-attention, which causes content copying between views when camera poses are close and restricts generation to fixed 90-degree azimuth intervals. These models lack explicit 3D geometric understanding, preventing arbitrary camera control and producing flipped predictions under large viewpoint changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240552/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In text-conditioned mode, SPAD takes a text prompt and relative camera poses (ΔE ∈ R^{3×4}) and jointly denoises N views simultaneously; in image-conditioned mode, it takes one clean source image plus relative camera poses and denoises a target novel view. Training uses 256×256 multi-view renders with 2 views, but the model generalizes to generating an arbitrary number of views at any elevation and azimuth. The system can also produce 3D NeRF assets via multi-view SDS optimization (~minutes) or a feed-forward triplane generator (~10 seconds).

## Method

SPAD fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net by replacing standard self-attention with a two-part spatially-aware mechanism. First, Epipolar Attention constrains cross-view interactions so each feature point only attends to positions along its epipolar line in other views (dilated with a 3×3 kernel), eliminating content copying and enabling arbitrary camera control. Second, Plücker Ray Embeddings represent each pixel's camera ray as (o×d, d) and inject them as positional encodings into the attention layers via zero-initialized linear projections, providing 3D spatial priors that bias attention toward nearby rays and suppress attention to opposite-side rays, thereby preventing flipped predictions. Camera poses are embedded via an MLP and fused with the timestep embedding to condition residual blocks. The model is trained on a curated 200K-object subset of Objaverse rendered at 256×256 with random elevations and azimuths.

## Results

On image-conditioned novel view synthesis over 1,000 unseen Objaverse objects, SPAD achieves PSNR 20.29, SSIM 0.84, and LPIPS 0.166, outperforming all baselines on LPIPS and matching SyncDreamer on SSIM. On the real-scanned Google Scanned Objects (GSO) dataset, SPAD reaches PSNR 17.99, SSIM 0.83, and LPIPS 0.169, again leading on perceptual quality. For text-conditioned multi-view generation, SPAD attains a CLIP-score of 29.87 and IS of 11.18, surpassing MVDream (v1.5) and SyncDreamer on text-image alignment while maintaining competitive image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAD outperforms SyncDreamer on LPIPS (0.166 vs. 0.174 on Objaverse; 0.169 vs. 0.178 on GSO) while matching it on SSIM (0.84 on both datasets), indicating superior perceptual fidelity without sacrificing structural accuracy. Against iNVS, SPAD trades a small PSNR gap (20.29 vs. 20.52 on Objaverse) for substantially better SSIM (0.84 vs. 0.81) and LPIPS (0.166 vs. 0.178), since iNVS's depth-based pixel copying inflates PSNR but introduces deformations under large viewpoint changes. SPAD clearly beats Zero-1-to-3 across all three metrics on both datasets (e.g., PSNR 20.29 vs. 18.16, LPIPS 0.166 vs. 0.201 on Objaverse). In the text-conditioned setting, SPAD surpasses MVDream (v1.5) on CLIP-score (29.87 vs. 28.55) and IS (11.18 vs. 9.72), and edges out SyncDreamer on CLIP-score (29.87 vs. 27.76), confirming that the epipolar-geometry constraints do not degrade 2D generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPAD extends the multi-view diffusion paradigm introduced by MVDream, which repurposed a pretrained 2D diffusion model by concatenating feature maps across views for joint self-attention. The key departure is that SPAD identifies and addresses two failure modes of this naive approach—content copying at small viewpoint differences and flipped predictions at large viewpoint differences—by constraining cross-view attention with epipolar geometry (inspired by the Epipolar Feature Transformer) and injecting Plücker ray coordinates as positional encodings (borrowed from neural light field representations). This positions SPAD between the fixed-camera approaches (MVDream, SyncDreamer) and the depth-dependent approaches (iNVS, Zero-1-to-3), achieving arbitrary camera control without requiring external depth estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPAD's ability to generate views at arbitrary camera poses (rather than fixed 90-degree intervals) is practically important for downstream 3D generation pipelines, as it enables denser view coverage and more flexible text-to-3D distillation without the Janus problem. The ablation showing that both epipolar attention and Plücker embeddings are independently necessary (neither alone resolves both content-copying and flipping) provides a clear recipe for future multi-view diffusion architectures. Open questions include whether these geometric constraints scale to larger models (e.g., SDXL), whether monocular depth could further refine epipolar correspondences, and whether the approach extends to dynamic 4D content or multi-object scenes.
