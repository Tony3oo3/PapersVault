## Problem

Prior VQ-based generative models assumed that improving reconstruction fidelity of VQ tokenizers would automatically improve generation quality, but no systematic analysis had examined this relationship. The paper identifies that reconstruction and generation optimization have conflicting objectives—reconstruction favors latent spaces with larger variance while generation (a classification task) favors smaller variance—revealing two competing goals: semantic compression and details preservation, with prior work prioritizing only the latter.

## Input / Output

The VQ tokenizer takes 256×256 RGB images (ImageNet) or 1024×1024 images (FFHQ) and compresses them into a 16×16 grid of discrete indices from a 1024-entry codebook (latent dimension 256). For conditional generation, a class label is additionally provided to the generative transformer. The generative transformer (AR or NAR) outputs a sequence of discrete indices, which are decoded back to a full-resolution RGB image via the VQ decoder. The tokenizer uses a 16× spatial compression ratio, meaning each token represents a 16×16 pixel patch.

## Method

SeQ-GAN is a two-phase VQ tokenizer that balances semantic compression and details preservation. In Phase 1, the tokenizer is trained with a semantic-enhanced perceptual loss (L_per^sem) that removes shallow VGG layers (relu1_2 through relu4_3) and retains only relu5_3 and the logit feature, forcing the codebook to encode semantic structure rather than fine-grained pixel details. In Phase 2, the encoder and codebook are frozen to preserve the learned discrete latent space, while an enhanced decoder (with interleaved regional and dilated attention blocks) is finetuned using the standard low-level perceptual loss to restore color fidelity and high-frequency details. An entropy regularization term on the soft codebook usage distribution (γ=0.01) is added to achieve 100% codebook utilization. The key insight is that fixing the encoder/codebook during Phase 2 allows detail restoration without degrading the separability of the latent space that the generative transformer relies on.

## Results

On 256×256 ImageNet conditional generation, SeQ-GAN+AR-L (364M params, 256 steps) achieves FID 6.25 and IS 140.9, a remarkable improvement over VIT-VQGAN (714M, 256 steps) at FID 11.20 and IS 97.2. With the NAR transformer, SeQ-GAN+NAR-L (364M, 12 steps) reaches FID 4.55 and IS 200.4, surpassing MaskGIT (227M, 8 steps) at FID 6.18 and IS 182.1. On unconditional generation (FFHQ, LSUN), SeQ-GAN+NAR achieves FID 3.62 on FFHQ and 1.44 on LSUN-Bedroom, competitive with StyleGAN2 and ADM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeQ-GAN+AR-L (364M) substantially outperforms VIT-VQGAN (714M) on ImageNet, reducing FID from 11.20 to 6.25 and increasing IS from 97.2 to 140.9, despite using fewer parameters. Against MaskGIT (227M, 8 steps), SeQ-GAN+NAR (229M, 12 steps) achieves a better FID of 4.99 vs. 6.18 and higher IS of 189.1 vs. 182.1. SeQ-GAN+NAR-L (364M) decisively beats MoVQ+NAR (389M) with FID 4.55 vs. 7.22 and IS 200.4 vs. 130.1. However, SeQ-GAN+AR (229M) slightly trails MoVQ+AR (389M) on FID (7.55 vs. 7.13) and IS (121.3 vs. 138.3), and SeQ-GAN+NAR-L (4.55 FID) is marginally behind the much larger VIT-VQGAN 1.7B model (4.17 FID) though it wins on IS (200.4 vs. 175.1). On unconditional generation, SeQ-GAN+NAR matches or exceeds StyleGAN2 and ADM on FFHQ and LSUN benchmarks with far fewer sampling steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQGAN framework (Esser et al., CVPR 2021) and its VQ-VAE predecessor (Van Den Oord et al., NeurIPS 2017) for the tokenizer, and on MaskGIT (Chang et al., CVPR 2022) for the NAR generative transformer. It departs from the dominant line of work (VIT-VQGAN, RQ-VAE, MoVQ) that has focused on improving reconstruction fidelity through larger codebooks, factorized codes, or enhanced decoders, by arguing that this objective conflicts with the generation objective. The semantic-enhanced perceptual loss draws an analogy to visual pretraining tokenizers (BEiT-v2, iBOT) that discard low-level features, but adapts the idea to image synthesis where some detail preservation is still necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reconstruction fidelity and generation quality are in tension challenges a widely-held assumption in the VQ-based generative model community and provides a principled explanation for why prior tokenizer improvements have not translated into proportional generation gains. The two-phase SeQ-GAN approach is simple and drop-in compatible with existing AR and NAR transformers, making it practically adoptable. Open questions include whether the semantic/detail trade-off generalizes to video generation, text-to-image, or higher resolutions (512×512 and beyond), and whether the specific VGG layers used for semantic features are optimal or dataset-dependent.
