## Problem

Multimodal LLMs rely on CLIP as their visual encoder, yet CLIP's contrastive training paradigm produces systematic visual blind spots that cascade into downstream MLLM failures. The paper asks whether these shortcomings are inherent to CLIP's representation learning and whether scaling model size or training data alone can resolve them, or whether a fundamentally different visual representation is needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240942/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The MMVP benchmark presents pairs of images (sourced from ImageNet-1K and LAION-Aesthetics) alongside two straightforward VQA questions probing visual differences between the pair; a model scores correctly only if both questions are answered accurately. The MMVP-VLM variant takes a single text description and two candidate images, requiring the model to select the correct image-text match. The MoF method operates on standard RGB images at 224×224 or 336×336 resolution with a text question, producing a free-form text answer.

## Method

The paper identifies CLIP-blind pairs by finding image pairs with cosine similarity >0.95 in CLIP embedding space but <0.6 in DINOv2 embedding space, then uses GPT-4 to categorize the resulting failures into nine visual patterns (orientation, counting, viewpoint, etc.). To address these shortcomings, the authors propose Mixture-of-Features (MoF): Additive-MoF linearly blends CLIP and DINOv2 features with a tunable ratio before the adapter, while Interleaved-MoF spatially interleaves the visual tokens from both encoders after separate adapter processing, preserving each model's spatial structure. Interleaved-MoF achieves a 10.7% improvement on MMVP visual grounding while maintaining instruction-following performance, demonstrating that vision-only SSL features complement CLIP's language-aligned representations rather than replace them.

## Results

On the MMVP benchmark (300 VQA questions from CLIP-blind pairs), human participants achieve 95.7% accuracy, while most MLLMs score below the 25% random-guess threshold; GPT-4V is the best-performing model but still trails humans by over 50 percentage points. On MMVP-VLM, 7 out of 9 visual patterns (e.g., orientation, counting, viewpoint) remain unsolved by all tested CLIP variants, including the largest (EVA02 ViT-bigE-14+, 5B params, 82.0% IN-1k zero-shot). The proposed Interleaved MoF (I-MoF) improves LLaVA's MMVP score from 5.5 to 16.7 (+10.7) and LLaVA-1.5's from 24.7 to 28.0 (+3.3) while maintaining or slightly improving LLaVA-Bench instruction-following scores (81.8→82.8 and 84.7→82.7 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among MLLMs evaluated on MMVP, GPT-4V and Gemini are the only models above the 25% random-guess line, yet both still fall far short of the 95.7% human accuracy; LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, and MiniGPT4 all score below random. On MMVP-VLM, no CLIP variant (OpenAI ViT-L-14, SigLIP ViT-SO-14, DFN ViT-H-14, MetaCLIP ViT-H-14, EVA01 ViT-g-14, EVA02 ViT-bigE-14+) resolves more than 2 of 9 visual patterns, with the best MMVP average being 39.3% (DFN ViT-H-14 at 224²). The proposed I-MoF consistently outperforms the LLaVA and LLaVA-1.5 baselines on MMVP (+10.7 and +3.3 points respectively) and POPE (+1.0 and +0.4), while LLaVA-Bench scores remain within ~2 points, confirming that visual grounding improves without sacrificing instruction-following. Notably, simply increasing image resolution from 224² to 336² in vanilla LLaVA yields only a 0.5-point MMVP gain (5.5→6.0), whereas I-MoF at 224² achieves 16.7, demonstrating that feature interleaving is far more effective than token count scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the failure-mode analysis methodology of Tong et al. [57]—which identified erroneous agreements in text embedding spaces—to the visual encoder side, introducing the concept of CLIP-blind pairs (images with similar CLIP embeddings but divergent DINOv2 embeddings). It builds directly on LLaVA as the MLLM framework and on the CLIP + adapter architecture that underpins most open-source multimodal systems. The Mixture-of-Features (MoF) approach departs from prior work that treats the vision encoder as a fixed, off-the-shelf component, instead proposing to spatially interleave features from a vision-only SSL model (DINOv2) with CLIP features to address the identified bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 7 of 9 identified visual patterns remain unsolved even by the largest CLIP variants (up to 5B parameters) challenges the prevailing assumption that data and model scaling will eventually resolve visual understanding gaps. The strong Pearson correlation (>0.7) between CLIP per-pattern failures and MLLM failures provides a clear diagnostic: the vision encoder, not the LLM, is the bottleneck for visual grounding. The MMVP benchmark is likely to become a standard probe for visual grounding in future MLLMs, and the MoF approach—while modest in absolute gains—opens a concrete path toward integrating complementary visual representations. Open questions include whether fundamentally new visual representation learning objectives (beyond contrastive or masked prediction) are needed, and how to design evaluation metrics that go beyond ImageNet zero-shot accuracy to capture the fine-grained visual details that matter for downstream multimodal tasks.
