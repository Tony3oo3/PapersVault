## Problem

Diffusion model backbones have been shown to perform well on discriminative vision tasks, but the optimal way to use their text prompting interface for perception remains unclear. Prior methods like VPD feed unaligned prompts (averaged EOS token embeddings for all dataset classes) into the cross-attention layers, and it is unknown whether this misalignment degrades the feature maps that the task head relies on. This paper asks whether aligning the text prompt to the actual image content improves downstream perception performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a per-image text caption generated by BLIP-2 (minimum 40 tokens for best results) as input. The image is encoded to a latent representation, scaled by a fixed factor of 0.18215, and passed through a single denoising step of the Stable Diffusion U-Net conditioned on the CLIP-encoded caption. The output is a task-specific prediction—pixel-wise segmentation masks, a depth map, or object detection boxes—produced by a lightweight FPN head operating on concatenated cross-attention and multi-scale U-Net features. In cross-domain settings, the caption is further modified with target-domain style information (e.g., a learned Textual Inversion token or a DreamBoothed backbone).

## Method

TADP replaces the standard averaged-EOS-token prompting with BLIP-2-generated image captions, which are aligned to the actual visual content of each image. The image latent is scaled to a standard normal distribution and passed through one denoising step of the Stable Diffusion U-Net, where the caption conditions the cross-attention layers; the resulting cross-attention maps and multi-scale features are concatenated and fed to a task-specific head trained with a standard task loss. The key finding is that unaligned prompts (all class names regardless of presence) introduce semantic shifts in the feature maps that interfere with the task head, while image-aligned captions produce sharper, more relevant cross-attention maps. For cross-domain transfer, the method appends target-domain information to the source-domain caption using progressively stronger alignment strategies: hand-crafted style strings, a learned Textual Inversion token, or a DreamBooth-fine-tuned backbone, with the latter two requiring only unlabeled target-domain images. An oracle ablation (listing all ground-truth class names present in the image) reveals that recall of present classes matters far more than precision, and that BLIP-2 captions approximate a precision-1.0 / recall-0.5 operating point.

## Results

On ADE20K semantic segmentation, TADP-40 achieves 54.8 mIoU (single-scale) and 55.9 mIoU (multi-scale), outperforming VPD(LS) by 1.1 mIoU and setting SOTA among diffusion-pretrained models. On Pascal VOC2012, TADP-40 reaches 87.11 mIoU versus 82.34 for VPD(R), a 4.8 mIoU gain. On NYUv2 depth estimation, TADP-40 achieves 0.225 RMSE, improving over VPD(LS) (0.235) by ~4% relative. In cross-domain settings, TADP sets SOTA on Dark Zurich-val (42.8 mIoU), Nighttime Driving (60.8 mIoU with Textual Inversion), and Watercolor2K (72.1 AP50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TADP-40 outperforms all diffusion-based baselines (VPD, VPD(R), VPD(LS)) on every single-domain task: +1.1 mIoU on ADE20K, +4.8 mIoU on Pascal VOC, and 0.010 RMSE improvement on NYUv2 depth. On ADE20K, TADP-40 (54.8 mIoU) still trails the strongest multi-modal (ONE-PEACE, 62.0) and self-supervised (InternImage-H, 62.5) methods, but the TADP-Oracle upper bound (72.0 mIoU) far exceeds all of them, suggesting substantial headroom. For cross-domain segmentation, TADPnull (no target-domain info) already surpasses PTDiffSeg (37.0 mIoU on Dark Zurich) and DAFormer/Refign-DAFormer on Nighttime Driving, while TADPTextualInversion further improves ND to 60.8 mIoU. For cross-domain detection, TADPnull achieves 72.1 AP50 on Watercolor2K, beating DASS-Detector (71.5 AP50) which relies on extra target-domain training data, and outperforming all CDWSOD and UDAOD baselines. On Comic2K, TADP (33.2 AP with Textual Inversion) is competitive with H2FA R-CNN (46.4 AP50) but remains below DASS-Detector with extra data (64.2 AP50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VPD (Zhao et al., 2023), which first demonstrated that Stable Diffusion's denoising U-Net can serve as a feature-extraction backbone for perception tasks like segmentation and depth estimation. The key departure is in the prompting strategy: VPD and subsequent work (e.g., PTDiffSeg for cross-domain segmentation) use averaged CLIP EOS-token embeddings over all dataset classes as the text conditioning, whereas TADP shows this unaligned prompting degrades cross-attention maps and instead uses BLIP-2 to generate image-specific captions. For cross-domain transfer, the paper positions itself against adversarial and self-training-based domain adaptation methods by introducing a purely text-based alignment mechanism (caption modifiers plus optional Textual Inversion/DreamBooth personalization), requiring no labeled target-domain data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the standard averaged-EOS prompting used across the diffusion-perception literature is suboptimal—and that a simple captioner fixes it—provides a practical, drop-in improvement for anyone building on VPD-style architectures. The cross-domain results (SOTA on Watercolor2K, Dark Zurich, and Nighttime Driving with no target-domain labels) suggest diffusion pre-training encodes more generalizable scene structure than previously exploited, opening the door to label-free domain transfer. Open questions include closing the large gap between BLIP-2 captions and the oracle (89.85 vs. 86.36 mIoU on Pascal), extending the framework to multiple simultaneous unseen domains, and whether task-specific or closed-vocabulary captioners could approach oracle-level alignment.
