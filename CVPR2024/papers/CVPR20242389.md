## Problem

Estimating blur maps from dual-pixel (DP) image pairs for defocus deblurring has traditionally required either synthetic supervision data or pre-calibrated blur kernels, limiting practical applicability. While CLIP has shown remarkable zero-shot capability for high-level vision tasks, its potential for low-level geometric tasks like blur quantization from stereo pairs remains entirely unexplored, and its monocular-image training makes direct application to DP pairs non-trivial.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dual-pixel image pair (left view B_L and right view B_R, each h×w×3 RGB) and produces an all-in-focus restored image (h×w×3) along with a dense blur map (h×w×1). A key design detail is that the DP pair is transformed into a single monocular image by horizontally concatenating B_L with a flipped B_R, allowing the monocular-trained CLIP image encoder to process it without any architectural modification or fine-tuning. The blur map is then sampled back to the original DP pair resolution.

## Method

The core insight is that sharp pixels in a DP pair exhibit horizontal symmetry between the left view and the flipped right view, while blurred pixels break this symmetry due to disparity. The paper exploits this by feeding the concatenated image to CLIP's ViT-B/32 image encoder and querying with text prompts describing symmetry (e.g., 'A horizontally symmetrical image'), then inverting the similarity score to obtain a blur map—entirely zero-shot with no CLIP fine-tuning. An ensembling strategy combines four blur-aware prompts (e.g., 'A blurry image') with four DP-aware symmetry prompts for more robust estimation. For restoration, a NAFNet-based encoder-decoder is augmented with Blur Prior Attention (BPA) blocks that modulate self-attention maps with the estimated blur map to assign spatially-varying deblurring kernels. Training uses a Charbonnier loss plus two CLIP-derived losses: a blur-weighting loss that adaptively penalizes heavily blurred regions, and a blur-aware loss that re-runs CLIP on the restoration to penalize any residual blur.

## Results

On the DPD-blur dataset (76 real-world scenes), the large model achieves 27.24 dB PSNR, 0.840 SSIM, and 0.028 MAE on average, outperforming the previous best K3DN (27.06 dB) and Restormer (26.66 dB). On the synthetic DDD-syn dataset, the large model reaches 38.62 dB PSNR and 0.971 SSIM, surpassing K3DN (38.23 dB) and Restormer (36.44 dB). On RDPD, the large model attains 33.44 dB PSNR versus 32.84 dB for K3DN. Inference time is 0.042 s (small) and 0.054 s (large) on a single RTX 4090, substantially faster than K3DN (0.198 s) and Restormer (0.255 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The large model consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across all three main datasets and all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, MAE, RMSE_rel). On DPD-blur, it beats the closest competitor K3DN by 0.18 dB PSNR and 0.006 SSIM, and Restormer by 0.58 dB PSNR. On DDD-syn, the margin over K3DN is 0.39 dB PSNR, and over Restormer it widens to 2.18 dB. On RDPD, the large model leads K3DN by 0.60 dB PSNR and Restormer by 1.17 dB. The small model already matches or exceeds Restormer and K3DN on MAE (0.032 vs. 0.035/0.034) while being 4–6× faster in inference. Notably, BAMBNet achieves the best LPIPS (0.141) among baselines on DPD-blur, but the large model's LPIPS of 0.168 is competitive while leading on all pixel-level metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: dual-pixel (DP) defocus deblurring and CLIP-based zero-shot vision. Prior DP deblurring methods (Abuolaim et al., Pan et al., Liang et al., Yang et al.) either required synthetic supervision, pre-calibrated kernels, or limited network branches to estimate blur/disparity maps, while CLIP applications had been confined to high-level semantic tasks (segmentation, detection, depth). The key departure here is reformulating blur estimation as a symmetry-detection problem on a horizontally-flipped DP pair, allowing the monocular-image-trained CLIP to produce a dense blur map without any fine-tuning. The deblurring backbone builds on NAFNet with a novel blur prior attention (BPA) block that injects the CLIP-derived blur map into self-attention as a spatially-varying kernel prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a large-scale vision-language model can be repurposed for a low-level geometric task (disparity/blur quantification) purely through prompt engineering and input reformulation, opening a path for CLIP to inform other stereo or multi-view low-level tasks without task-specific training. A stated limitation is that CLIP's classification-oriented pre-training makes it insensitive to textureless regions (walls, ceilings), degrading blur map quality there. Open questions include whether the DP-aware symmetry trick generalizes to other stereo configurations, whether a more texture-sensitive vision-language backbone would close the gap on flat regions, and whether the approach scales to video or higher-resolution DP captures.
