## Problem

Existing text-guided 3D editing methods rely on per-instance test-time optimisation (3D distillation), requiring tens of minutes to hours per asset to transfer knowledge from 2D diffusion priors into a 3D representation. This makes interactive or large-scale editing impractical. The paper asks whether a single feed-forward network can perform semantic 3D editing directly in a learned latent space, eliminating test-time optimisation entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object (mesh or NeRF) and a natural-language editing instruction. The 3D object is first encoded into a 1024×1024 latent vector via Shap-E's autoencoder; the latent editor then produces an edited latent in a single forward pass (~1 second). The edited latent is decoded back into a 3D representation (NeRF, signed distance function, or texture field). At inference, no 2D diffusion model is invoked—only the trained latent editor and the Shap-E decoder are needed.

## Method

SHAP-EDITOR learns a feed-forward latent editor g: (r^s, y) → r^e that operates in Shap-E's latent space, decomposing the full editor as f = h* ∘ g ∘ h. The editor is initialised from Shap-E's transformer-based denoising network, with the source latent concatenated to a noised copy to match the original architecture's input format. Training distills knowledge from 2D diffusion editors via a Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss computed on differentiable renders of the source and edited latents from random viewpoints. For global edits, a TI2I SDS loss (InstructPix2Pix) is combined with a depth regularisation term to preserve geometry; for local edits, the loss additionally includes a T2I SDS loss (Stable Diffusion) for semantic adherence and a masked photometric/depth regularisation guided by cross-attention maps extracted from the TI2I model to confine changes to the edited region. The paper further demonstrates that the learned latent space is partially linear, enabling strength control via residual scaling and compositional editing via sequential application.

## Results

On the evaluation set of 20 unseen instance-instruction pairs, SHAP-EDITOR (single-prompt) achieves the best local editing scores (CLIPsim 0.292, CLIPdir 0.097) and the best global editing scores (CLIPsim 0.272, CLIPdir 0.070, Structure Dist. 0.008) among all compared methods. Crucially, it does so in ~1 second per edit via a single feed-forward pass, compared to 14–53 minutes for the optimisation-based baselines. The multi-prompt variant (CLIPsim 0.279 local, 0.255 global) remains competitive while supporting multiple editing instructions in one model. Even the test-time optimisation variant of the same losses (CLIPsim 0.290 local, 0.268 global, ~19 min) is outperformed by the feed-forward single-prompt editor, suggesting that learning the editor function itself acts as a regulariser.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHAP-EDITOR (single-prompt) outperforms all three optimisation-based baselines on every reported metric. Against Text2Mesh [44], it improves local CLIPsim by +0.053 (0.292 vs 0.239) and global Structure Dist. by 9× (0.008 vs 0.073), while running ~14× faster. Against Instruct-NeRF2NeRF [17], gains are even larger: local CLIPdir improves from 0.051 to 0.097 (+90%) and global Structure Dist. drops from 0.095 to 0.008, with a ~36× speedup. Against the strongest baseline Vox-E [59], the single-prompt variant wins on local CLIPsim (0.292 vs 0.277), local CLIPdir (0.097 vs 0.075), and global Structure Dist. (0.008 vs 0.026), while matching global CLIPsim (0.272 vs 0.271) and improving global CLIPdir (0.070 vs 0.066), all at ~40× lower inference cost. Notably, the feed-forward editor also beats its own test-time optimisation counterpart (same losses, direct latent optimisation), confirming that amortised learning regularises the editing process beyond what per-instance optimisation achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Shap-E 3D autoencoder (which maps objects to a 1024-dim latent space) and repurposes its latent space as an editing substrate, a use not explored in the original work. It departs from the dominant paradigm of test-time 3D editing (Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, Vox-E, Text2Mesh), which iteratively optimises a 3D representation against 2D diffusion priors, by instead learning a single feed-forward latent editor via Score Distillation Sampling from multiple 2D teachers (InstructPix2Pix, Stable Diffusion, MagicBrush). The method also draws on cross-attention guidance from controllable 2D generation to construct spatial masks for local edits, and on the SDS loss from DreamFusion as the distillation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: editing drops from tens of minutes to ~1 second, enabling batch modification of large 3D asset libraries and interactive workflows. The finding that 3D latent edits are partially linear (a single additive vector can apply 'turn it pink' to any object) suggests the latent space has exploitable geometric structure for semantic operations, opening the door to cheaper, more interpretable editing pipelines. Key limitations remain: quality is bounded by Shap-E's reconstruction fidelity, the multi-prompt editor still lags the single-prompt version, and a truly open-ended editor would require training at a scale (hundreds of GPUs) the authors could not afford. Whether the linearity property generalises beyond the ~30 classes tested, and whether it survives with stronger 3D autoencoders, are open questions.
