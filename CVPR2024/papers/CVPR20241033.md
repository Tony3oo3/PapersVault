## Problem

Existing multi-depth ensembles in monocular 3D object detection suffer from a coupling phenomenon where approximately 95% of depth predictions share the same error sign, preventing errors from canceling out and limiting combined depth accuracy. This coupling arises because all local depth clues (heights, keypoints, direct estimates) are derived from the same local features around the object center in CenterNet-style architectures, making their errors highly correlated.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and camera intrinsic parameters (focal lengths, principal point) as input, with no additional sensors or data required. It outputs 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation) and a final depth estimate computed as an uncertainty-weighted average of seven depth predictions: one direct depth, three keypoint-based geometric depths, and three complementary depths augmented by diagonal columns. The horizon heatmap prediction uses dilated convolutions (dilation rate 2) to expand the receptive field for global scene understanding.

## Method

MonoCD extends the CenterNet framework with a new complementary depth branch that exploits global scene information rather than local object features. A Horizon Heatmap head predicts the ground plane equation (Ax + By + Cz + 1.65 = 0) from the entire image, from which a global y-coordinate (y_glo) is derived for each object's projected bottom center. The complementary depth z_comp is then computed via a formula (Eq. 14) in which the 3D height H and top-center pixel v_t appear with signs opposite to those in the keypoint depth z_key (Eq. 13), creating 'complementarity in form' so that errors in shared geometric quantities push the two depths in opposite directions. All seven depth branches are combined using uncertainty-based weights (w_i = 1/σ_i) following Kendall et al. The paper provides a mathematical proof that complementary (opposite-sign) depth errors always yield a smaller combined error than coupled (same-sign) errors, and validates this with flipping experiments showing up to 50% error sign opposition between global and local branches.

## Results

On the KITTI test set (Car category), MonoCD achieves 25.53/16.59/14.53 AP₃D and 33.41/22.81/19.57 AP_BEV (Easy/Moderate/Hard), setting a new state-of-the-art among methods without extra data. Compared to the previous best no-extra-data method MonoDETR, MonoCD improves AP₃D by +0.53/+0.12/+0.58 and AP_BEV by −0.19/+0.70/+0.57 across difficulties, while running faster (36 ms vs. 43 ms). Against the multi-depth baseline MonoFlex, the improvement is 19.44% in AP₃D and 15.49% in AP_BEV (relative). The complementary depth module also provides consistent plug-and-play gains of +0.25 to +1.87 AP_BEV when added to MonoDLE, MonoFlex, and MonoCon on the validation set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoCD outperforms the closest no-extra-data competitor MonoDETR [39] on AP₃D across all difficulties (+0.53 Easy, +0.12 Mod., +0.58 Hard) and on AP_BEV Moderate/Hard (+0.70/+0.57), while trailing by only 0.19 on AP_BEV Easy; it also runs 7 ms faster. Against the ground-plane-based GPENet [35], MonoCD gains +1.15 AP₃D Mod. and +2.02 AP_BEV Mod., confirming that the complementary depth formulation extracts more value from the same geometric prior. Compared to the multi-depth baseline MonoFlex [40], the improvement is substantial: +5.59 AP₃D Easy and +3.02 AP_BEV Mod. MonoCD also surpasses the Transformer-based MonoDTR [8] (which requires LiDAR supervision) by +3.54 AP₃D Mod. and +2.43 AP_BEV Mod. without any extra data. When used as a plug-and-play module, the complementary depth branch adds +1.43 AP₃D Mod. to MonoDLE, +0.76 to MonoFlex, and +0.36 to MonoCon on the validation set, demonstrating consistent gains across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CenterNet-based monocular 3D detection lineage (CenterNet → MonoFlex → MonoDLE → MonoCon) by identifying a systematic 'coupling' problem: existing multi-depth branches derived from local geometric clues (heights, keypoints) produce errors that share the same sign ~95% of the time, preventing error cancellation in weighted averaging. It departs from prior work by introducing a global-clue depth branch (horizon heatmap → ground plane equation → y_glo) that is structurally independent of local per-object features, and by designing a complementary geometric form (Eq. 14) where shared quantities like 3D height H enter with opposite signs across branches. The ground plane prior is borrowed from GPENet [35], but repurposed here specifically to generate a depth branch with complementary error characteristics rather than as an auxiliary constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complementary depth module is lightweight and plug-and-play, yielding consistent gains when added to MonoFlex, MonoDLE, and MonoCon, which makes it immediately adoptable by practitioners working on real-time monocular 3D detection. The mathematical analysis (Sec. 3.2) cleanly formalizes why opposite-sign errors reduce combined depth error, providing a principled design criterion beyond ad-hoc ensemble tuning. However, the method relies on a flat-ground assumption (fixed 1.65 m offset) and is validated only on KITTI, so its behavior on undulating terrain or in urban scenes with significant elevation changes remains an open question. Broader generalization to other datasets, object categories, and non-planar ground scenarios would strengthen the contribution.
