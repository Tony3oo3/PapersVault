## Problem

Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) must adapt a pretrained source model to a target domain using only unlabeled target data, but conventional methods relying on pseudo-labeling or auxiliary supervision from the source model are inherently error-prone under domain shift. No prior SFDA approach has leveraged the rich, heterogeneous knowledge encoded in off-the-shelf vision-language foundation models (e.g., CLIP) as an external knowledge source to transcend the limited information boundary of the source model and target data alone.

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a frozen pretrained source model (initialized as the target model), unlabeled target-domain RGB images, and a fully frozen CLIP ViL model. No source data is accessed at any point, and the only learnable parameters are per-class text prompts within CLIP's text encoder. The output is an adapted target model that maps target images to class labels, evaluated under closed-set, partial-set, and open-set SFDA protocols across four benchmarks.

## Method

DIFO alternates between two steps per epoch: (i) task-specific customization of the frozen CLIP model via prompt learning, where prompts are optimized by maximizing mutual information between the target model's and CLIP's predictions (rather than KL divergence, which biases toward one model), and (ii) memory-aware knowledge distillation from the customized CLIP to the target model. The distillation step combines two regularizations: a most-likely category encouragement loss that fuses historical predictions from both models in a prediction bank with asymmetric exponential weighting to identify top-N probable categories, then applies product/sum logit calibration to amplify attention on those categories; and a predictive consistency loss that further aligns target and ViL predictions via mutual information. The prediction bank updates target-model predictions per-iteration and ViL predictions epoch-wise, balancing stability of the ViL guidance with capturing adaptation dynamics.

## Results

On the closed-set SFDA setting, DIFO-C-B32 (ViT-B/32 backbone) achieves average accuracies of 92.5% on Office-31, 90.3% on Office-Home, 90.3% on VisDA, and 80.0% on DomainNet-126, surpassing the previous best methods (CoWA, TPDS, PLUE, GKD) by 2.2%, 9.6%, 2.0%, and 11.3% respectively. The weaker DIFO-C-RN variant (ResNet backbone) still outperforms prior SOTA on Office-Home (88.8%), VisDA (88.8%), and DomainNet-126 (76.7%). On partial-set and open-set SFDA settings, DIFO-C-B32 improves over the previous best by 2.4% and 2.7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFO-C-B32 outperforms all 13 SFDA baselines (SHOT, NRC, GKD, HCL, AaD, AdaCon, CoWA, SCLM, ELR, PLUE, TPDS, CRS) across all four datasets, with the largest margin of 11.3% over GKD on DomainNet-126. It also surpasses the three multimodal UDA methods that require source data (DAPL, PADCLIP, ADCLIP) on Office-Home and DomainNet-126, confirming that source-free adaptation with a frozen ViL model can match or exceed methods that have access to labeled source data. Compared to zero-shot CLIP, DIFO-C-B32 improves by 12.7% (Office-31), 7.0% (Office-Home), 7.4% (VisDA), and 3.7% (DomainNet-126), demonstrating that task-specific customization is essential. A simple Source+CLIP averaging strategy also falls short of DIFO by at least 3.2%, and no weighting coefficient between 0 and 1 can match DIFO's performance (Fig. 4). The weaker DIFO-C-RN variant still beats the previous best SFDA methods on three of four datasets, showing robustness to the choice of CLIP backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFO extends the SFDA literature, which has traditionally relied exclusively on the pretrained source model and unlabeled target data for self-supervised adaptation, by introducing a frozen off-the-shelf vision-language model (CLIP) as an external heterogeneous knowledge source. Methodologically, it builds on prompt learning for ViL models (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022) to perform task-specific customization of CLIP without any target labels, and on knowledge distillation to transfer the customized ViL knowledge into the target model. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from DAPL (which requires labeled source data and thus is not source-free) and from PADCLIP/ADCLIP (which are UDA methods), positioning DIFO as the first SFDA method to leverage a multimodal foundation model in a fully unsupervised manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a frozen ViL model can be effectively customized via mutual-information-based prompt learning and distilled into a target model without any source data or target labels, DIFO opens a pathway for SFDA methods to transcend the knowledge boundary of a single pretrained source model. The consistent gains across four benchmarks (up to 11.3% on DomainNet-126) and across closed-set, partial-set, and open-set settings suggest the approach is robust rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other foundation models (e.g., SAM, DINOv2), whether the memory-aware predictor's exponential weighting strategy is optimal, and whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks such as segmentation or detection where the category-encouragement mechanism would need rethinking.
