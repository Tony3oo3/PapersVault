## Problem

Existing weakly supervised point cloud semantic segmentation methods are fundamentally constrained by the fixed, sparse initial annotations, which place an upper bound on achievable performance. While prior work focuses on better exploiting this limited information through pseudo-labeling or self-supervised learning, no method has explored dynamically expanding the label set during training by leveraging an external source of segmentation knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud with extremely sparse annotations (as few as 1 point per object, ~0.004% of all points) plus multi-view RGB images, depth maps, and camera projection matrices from the same scene. The RGB-D data is used exclusively during training to enable the 2D oracle; at inference time, only the point cloud is required. The output is a per-point semantic class assignment over the entire point cloud, evaluated with mIoU across 13 (S3DIS) or 20 (ScanNetV2) categories.

## Method

The core contribution is the REAL framework, which repurposes the Segment Anything Model (SAM) as an artificial oracle within an active learning loop. A Projection-based Point-to-Segment (PP2S) module bridges 2D and 3D by projecting sparse 3D points onto images, querying SAM with point prompts to obtain 2D masks, back-projecting those masks to 3D, and resolving multi-view conflicts via mask-wise voting. During training, the model samples query points that are both highly confident (margin-based reliability > 0.95) and located in regions not yet correctly labeled in the previous timestep, then requests PP2S to segment around those points. A cautious label-update rule accepts an artificial label only when it agrees with the model's own prediction, preventing noise propagation. This iterative exploration progressively expands and corrects the pseudo-label set without any manual annotation beyond the initial sparse points.

## Results

On S3DIS, REAL with PTv2 achieves 62.7% mIoU under the 0.004% sparsity setting (1 point per object), surpassing the previous best weakly supervised method CPCM (62.3% mIoU) which uses 5× more labels (0.02%). Under the 0.02% setting, REAL reaches 65.2% mIoU, outperforming CPCM by 2.9%. On ScanNetV2 (20 pts/scene), REAL achieves 66.3% mIoU on validation and 65.5% on test, exceeding CPCM by 3.6% and 2.7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The headline result is that REAL at 0.004% sparsity (62.7% mIoU on S3DIS) outperforms CPCM at 0.02% (62.3%), meaning it achieves superior segmentation with 5× fewer annotated points. Against the strongest weakly supervised baseline CPCM, REAL gains +2.9% mIoU at 0.02% and +3.6%/+2.7% on ScanNetV2 val/test. Compared to MIL (51.4% at 0.02%) and HybridCR (51.5% at 0.03%), REAL at 0.004% (62.7%) exceeds them by over 11% and 11% respectively. The PP2S preprocessing alone (without the active REAL loop) already lifts PTv2 from 39.7% to 53.0% at 0.004%, while the full REAL framework adds another ~10% on top. On ScanNetV2, REAL (65.5% test) surpasses all prior weakly supervised methods including CPCM (62.8%) and MIL (54.4%), approaching fully supervised RFCR (70.2%) with only 20 sparse points per scene. The gains are backbone-agnostic: REAL with Closer also improves from 38.7% to 57.8% at 0.004%, confirming the framework's generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established WSPCSS line of work (PSD, OTOC, MIL, HybridCR, CPCM) that focuses on squeezing more signal from fixed sparse labels via self-training, contrastive regularization, or masked modeling. Instead, it introduces an external information source—SAM—into the pipeline, framed as an active learning loop where a pretrained 2D model replaces the human oracle. The PP2S module connects to prior 2D-3D cross-modal projection approaches but is specifically designed around SAM's promptable segmentation interface, requiring no 3D adaptation or fine-tuning of the foundation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework breaks the performance ceiling imposed by sparse annotations by dynamically expanding label coverage during training, achieving 0.004% annotation performance that surpasses methods using 5× more labels. Key limitations include the dependency on synchronized RGBD camera data and accurate calibration matrices during training, which constrains applicability to settings where such data is naturally available (indoor RGBD reconstruction, LiDAR+camera rigs). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor/autonomous driving scenes with noisier calibration, whether the active learning loop can be made more sample-efficient, and whether the reliance on SAM's 2D mask quality introduces a hard ceiling that future foundation model improvements could raise.
