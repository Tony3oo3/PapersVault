## Problem

Existing driving world models are limited to single-view video or multiview image generation, and none are compatible with existing end-to-end planning pipelines. The paper tackles three intertwined challenges: generating high-fidelity multiview video in pixel space (rather than low-resolution or vectorized state spaces), ensuring strict geometric consistency across overlapping camera views, and flexibly conditioning on heterogeneous signals (actions, layouts, text, reference views) to enable safe planning by foreseeing multiple futures before committing to a trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241229/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multiview context frame (first frame of a clip at 384×192, cropped from 1600×900), per-frame ego actions (Δx, Δy), projected 3D boxes/BEV maps, a CLIP text prompt encoding view/weather/lighting, and reference-view images. It outputs a 6-view, ~20-second future video and, in the planning mode, an optimal trajectory selected by a tree-based rollout that scores generated futures with image-based rewards (map reward × object reward computed by BEVFormer and MapTR on the generated frames). All heterogeneous conditions are encoded into a single concatenated d-dimensional embedding sequence fed via cross-attention, so new condition types can be added without architectural changes.

## Method

Drive-WM extends a Stable Diffusion image model into a multiview latent video diffusion model by adding temporal layers (3D convolution + temporal self-attention) and multiview layers (self-attention across the view dimension), trained in two stages: first a standard image diffusion model, then freezing spatial parameters and fine-tuning only the temporal and multiview parameters on video data. The key architectural insight is factorized multiview generation: views are partitioned into non-overlapping reference views {F, BL, BR} generated jointly, and stitched views {FL, B, FR} generated autoregressively conditioned on their two neighboring reference views, which raises the keypoint-matching consistency score from 45.8% to 94.4% while preserving FID/FVD quality. A unified condition interface encodes images (ConvNeXt), projected layouts, text (CLIP), and actions (MLP) into a common embedding space and injects them frame-wise via cross-attention in the 3D UNet. For planning, the world model performs tree-based rollouts: candidate trajectories from VAD are fed as action conditions, the model generates the corresponding multiview futures, and an image-based reward (product of map and object rewards from perception on generated frames) selects the optimal branch at each node.

## Results

On nuScenes, Drive-WM achieves FID 12.99 for multiview image generation, substantially outperforming MagicDrive (16.20), BEVControl (24.85), and BEVGen (25.54). For multiview video generation, it reaches FID 15.8 and FVD 122.7, a large improvement over single-view baselines DriveDreamer (FID 52.6, FVD 452.0) and DriveGAN (FID 73.4, FVD 502.3). In controllability, Drive-WM attains mAP_obj 20.66, mIoU_fg 27.19, and mIoU_bg 65.07, surpassing all prior methods. For planning, the tree-based world model achieves average L2 error of 0.80 m and 0.26% collision rate, approaching ground-truth-command performance (0.72 m, 0.22%) while using only sampled commands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Drive-WM outperforms all multiview image generation baselines on FID: it beats MagicDrive by 3.21 points (12.99 vs 16.20), BEVControl by 11.86 points, and BEVGen by 12.55 points. Against single-view video baselines, the improvement is dramatic—FID drops from 52.6 (DriveDreamer) and 73.4 (DriveGAN) to 15.8, and FVD drops from 452.0 and 502.3 to 122.7, reflecting both higher fidelity and temporal consistency. In controllability (Table 1b), Drive-WM leads on mAP_obj (20.66 vs 19.64 for BEVControl, 15.42 for GLIGEN) and mIoU_bg (65.07 vs 61.05 for MagicDrive), while also being the only method reporting mAP_map (37.68). For planning, the tree-based world model (L2 0.80 m, collision 0.26%) clearly beats VAD with random commands (1.02 m, 0.93%) and sampled commands (0.87 m, 0.47%), approaching the ground-truth-command upper bound (0.72 m, 0.22%). The factorized generation ablation shows KPM consistency jumping from 45.8% to 94.4% over joint modeling, a key differentiator not available in any prior baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends latent video diffusion models (VideoLDM, Stable Diffusion) into the multiview driving domain by adding temporal and cross-view attention layers, and departs from prior single-view driving video generators (DriveGAN, GAIA-1, DriveDreamer) by jointly modeling six surround-view cameras. It also builds on the world-model-for-planning lineage (Dreamer series, MILE) but is the first to operate in high-resolution pixel space with multiview consistency for real-world driving, rather than in simulators or vectorized state spaces. The factorized generation strategy—splitting views into non-overlapping reference views and overlapping 'stitched' views—repurposes the autoregressive conditional generation idea from language modeling to enforce spatial consistency across camera perspectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Drive-WM opens a path toward using generative world models as neural simulators for safe autonomous driving planning, demonstrating that image-based rewards from generated futures can select better trajectories than blind command following and partially recover from OOD ego deviations. However, the planning evaluation is limited to three discrete commands on nuScenes, the reward function relies on standard perception modules (BEVFormer, MapTR), and the OOD recovery still leaves a 4× collision gap versus normal operation. Open questions include whether this approach scales to continuous trajectory optimization, longer horizons, and whether the pixel-space world model can replace or complement vectorized planners in production systems.
