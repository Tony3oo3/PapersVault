## Problem

Efficient Vision Transformers that rely on stacking local or spatially-downsampled attention layers suffer from depth degradation in residual connections, preventing sufficient cross-layer information mixing and producing unnatural window-partitioning artifacts in feature maps. Additionally, these window-based designs lack the pixel-wise translational equivariance inherent in biological foveal vision, where acuity is consistently highest near the visual focus regardless of position.

## Input / Output

TransNeXt consumes standard RGB images and supports multi-scale inference: in normal mode the pooling feature size scales with input resolution (H/32 × W/32), while in linear mode it is fixed at 7×7 for constant-complexity inference on arbitrarily large inputs. Outputs are task-dependent: class logits for ImageNet-1K classification, bounding boxes and instance masks via Mask R-CNN or DINO heads on COCO, and per-pixel class maps via UperNet or Mask2Former on ADE20K. The architecture is hierarchical with four stages producing feature maps at 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32 spatial resolution.

## Method

The core token mixer, Aggregated Attention, uses a dual-path design per pixel: a fine-grained 3×3 sliding window attention path and a coarse-grained path attending to a pooled (H_p × W_p) feature map, with both paths' query-key similarities computed in a single shared softmax to create multi-scale competition. This is augmented with a learnable query embedding (LKV attention) added to all queries and a set of learnable positional tokens (QLV attention) that interact with queries to produce dynamic position bias, unifying three attention paradigms in one layer at only 0.2–0.3% extra FLOPs. The channel mixer, Convolutional GLU, inserts a 3×3 depthwise convolution into GLU's gating branch, giving each token a unique channel-attention signal derived from its nearest-neighbor spatial features rather than a shared global pooling signal. Length-scaled cosine attention (scaling factor τ·log N) and log-CPB positional encoding on the pooling path enable robust extrapolation to unseen input resolutions. The full TransNeXt backbone follows a four-stage hierarchical layout with overlapping patch embedding, using Aggregated Attention in stages 1–3 and a modified MHSA in stage 4, with ConvGLU as the channel mixer throughout.

## Results

TransNeXt-Tiny (28.2M params) achieves 84.0% ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy at 224², surpassing ConvNeXt-B (83.8%) with 69% fewer parameters. At 384² resolution, TransNeXt-Small/Base achieve 86.0%/86.2% ImageNet-1K accuracy after only 5 epochs of fine-tuning, outperforming MaxViT-Base (85.7%, 30 epochs) by 0.3%/0.5%. On the challenging ImageNet-A robustness benchmark at 384², TransNeXt-Small/Base reach 58.3%/61.6% top-1, exceeding ConvNeXt-L (50.7%) by 7.6%/10.9%. For dense prediction, TransNeXt-Base attains 54.7 mIoU on ADE20K (UperNet) and 57.1 AP^b on COCO (DINO, 5-scales), approaching Swin-L pretrained on ImageNet-22K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransNeXt-Tiny (84.0%) outperforms ConvNeXt-B (83.8%) and BiFormer-S (83.8%) on ImageNet-1K at 224² while using 69% and 50% fewer parameters respectively, and also leads on all five robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-A: 39.9% vs. 36.7%/39.5%). At 384², TransNeXt-Small (86.0%) surpasses MaxViT-Base (85.7%) by 0.3% with only 42% of its parameters and 5 epochs of fine-tuning versus 30. On ImageNet-A at 384², TransNeXt-Base (61.6%) exceeds ConvNeXt-L (50.7%) by 10.9% and MaxViT-Base (55.1%) by 6.5%, establishing a new robustness benchmark. For COCO detection with DINO, TransNeXt-Tiny achieves 55.1 AP^b, surpassing ConvNeXt-L (53.4) by 1.7 with only 14% of its backbone parameters. On ADE20K semantic segmentation (UperNet), TransNeXt-Base reaches 54.7 mIoU, surpassing ConvNeXt-B pretrained on ImageNet-22K (52.6) and approaching Swin-L (ImageNet-22K) performance. TransNeXt-Base also outperforms MaxViT-Base on ImageNet-1K (84.8% vs. 84.9% at 224², 86.2% vs. 85.7% at 384²) while using 25% fewer parameters and 34% fewer FLOPs at 384².

## Relation to Prior Work

TransNeXt builds directly on the PVTv2 hierarchical backbone (overlapping patch embedding, four-stage design) and reinterprets its spatial-reduction attention through a biomimetic foveal-vision lens, replacing window-partitioned attention with a pixel-wise dual-path design (sliding window + pooled global path) that enforces translational equivariance. It aggregates three attention paradigms—standard QKV, learnable-query (LKV) from QnA, and learnable-key (QLV) from Involution/VOLO—into a single mixer layer, and adopts Swin V2's log-CPB and scaled cosine attention for multi-scale extrapolation. The channel mixer extends GLU with a 3×3 depthwise convolution, drawing on the conditional positional encoding insight from Twins and the gated-channel-attention lineage of SE, to provide per-token local channel attention without increasing FLOPs over ConvFFN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that avoiding depth degradation through per-pixel global perception (rather than deeper stacking) yields substantially better robustness—TransNeXt-Base outperforms ConvNeXt-L by 10.9% on ImageNet-A with only 45% of the parameters—suggesting that the quality of information mixing, not just model depth, drives generalization. The multi-scale inference results, where TransNeXt outperforms large-kernel CNNs (e.g., RepLKNet-31B) at 640², challenge the long-held assumption that pure convolutions handle resolution changes more gracefully. Open questions include whether the biomimetic foveal framing is essential or merely a convenient inductive bias, whether the approach scales beyond the Base size tested here, and whether the robustness advantage persists under self-supervised or foundation-model pretraining regimes.
