## Problem

Existing multimodal learning methods (e.g., CLIP) require paired or strongly aligned data across modalities, leaving open whether completely irrelevant data from another modality can improve a transformer's performance on a target modality. This paper asks whether the sequence-to-sequence modeling knowledge learned by a transformer on one modality contains modality-complementary information that can transfer to another modality without any sample-level correspondence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242068/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts modality-specific inputs—RGB images (H×W×C), N-frame video clips, point clouds (P points with 3D coordinates and attributes), or audio spectrograms (T×F)—and tokenizes them into D-dimensional sequences using modality-specific tokenizers (patch embedding for images/video/audio; Farthest Point Sampling + KNN grouping for point clouds). A modality-specific head then decodes the processed token sequence into task-appropriate outputs: class labels, bounding boxes with masks, pixel-level segmentation maps, or part labels. Crucially, during training only target-modality data is fed through the network; the auxiliary modality's contribution enters solely through pre-trained weights in the transformer blocks.

## Method

M2PT constructs a 'multimodal pathway' between a target transformer and an auxiliary transformer (both ViT-Base) by augmenting every linear layer in the target's transformer blocks with its structural counterpart from the auxiliary model. The concrete implementation, Cross-Modal Re-parameterization, replaces each linear layer's operation y = xW with y = x(W + λW′), where W′ is initialized from the auxiliary model's corresponding layer and λ is a learnable scalar (Cross-Modal Scale) initialized to 0. Both W and W′ remain trainable during optimization, but the forward pass only computes a single merged matrix, so training cost is marginal (one extra matrix addition per layer). After training, the model is merged by saving Ŵ = W + λW′, yielding a standard ViT with identical parameter count and zero additional inference latency. The tokenizer and classification/detection/segmentation head are modality-specific and unchanged; only the transformer blocks are re-parameterized.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (pretrained setting), M2PT-Point achieves 83.9% top-1 accuracy (finetuned) and 67.8% (frozen backbone), improving over the MAE baseline (83.3%/65.6%) by 0.7% and 3.4% relatively. On downstream tasks, M2PT-Point raises MSCOCO box AP from 47.3 to 50.0 (+5.7%) and ADE-20K mIoU from 46.1 to 47.9 (+3.9%). Across all four modalities, M2PT consistently improves over the respective MAE-style baselines: ShapeNetPart mIoU 84.2→85.6, PartNet mIoU 47.4→50.1, AudioSet top-1 35.3→35.6, and Kinetics-400 top-1 81.5→82.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet, M2PT-Point (83.9% tune / 67.8% fix) outperforms MAE (83.3/65.6), SemMAE (83.4/65.0), and MFF (83.6/67.0) in the pretrained setting, and beats from-scratch ViT (76.5%) by 7.1% in the from-scratch setting. On MSCOCO and ADE-20K, M2PT-Point surpasses MAE by +5.7% box AP and +3.9% mIoU, and matches or exceeds MFF (48.1/47.9) on both metrics. For point cloud, M2PT-Video (85.6/87.5 on ShapeNetPart, 50.1 on PartNet) outperforms Point-MAE (84.2/86.1, 47.4), Point-BERT (84.1/85.6), Point-MLP (84.6/86.1, 48.1), and PointNet++ (81.9/85.1, 42.5). On AudioSet-2k, M2PT-Point (35.6%) edges out AudioMAE (35.3%) and AST (34.7%), and substantially exceeds PSLA (31.9%) and SSAST (31.0%). On Kinetics-400, M2PT-Point/Audio (82.3%) surpasses VideoMAE (81.5%), MViTv2 (81.2%), TimeSFormer (80.7%), and SlowFast (79.8%) by at least 0.8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in multimodal learning (CLIP, VideoBERT, etc.) that requires paired or aligned data across modalities, instead proposing that completely irrelevant data from another modality can improve a target-modality transformer. Methodologically, it builds on the structural re-parameterization lineage (RepVGG and successors) but repurposes the concept: rather than adding auxiliary layers during training, it merges a separately pretrained cross-modal model's weights into the target model via a learnable scale factor. The universal sequence-to-sequence modeling ability of transformers (shared ViT block structure across modalities) is the key architectural assumption that makes cross-modal weight transfer feasible without architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a previously unexplored direction—leveraging irrelevant cross-modal data as a free performance boost—with the practical advantage of zero additional inference cost after a simple weight merge. The consistent improvements across four modalities (image, video, point cloud, audio) provide empirical evidence that modality-complementary knowledge (hierarchical sequence-to-sequence transformation) genuinely exists in transformers beyond modality-specific features. However, the theoretical explanation for why this works remains absent, the method requires same-architecture transformers, and absolute gains are modest (0.4–0.7% on ImageNet in the pretrained setting), leaving open whether the effect scales to larger models or extends to non-transformer architectures like CNNs.
