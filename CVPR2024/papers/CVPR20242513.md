## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation and pruning methods for LiDAR-based 3D detection suffer from three limitations: they rely on homogeneous distillation pairs (same architecture with different widths) that cap the teacher's capacity, they obtain student architectures by uniformly reducing layer widths rather than searching for optimal compact structures, and they leave a large number of false positive predictions unaddressed after compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242513/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes LiDAR point clouds that are voxelized and processed through a 3D backbone followed by a 2D backbone and detection head. During inference, the student detector outputs 3D bounding boxes with category confidence scores and predicted IOU values; the IOU scores are used as a binary mask (threshold δ) to suppress false positives before NMS, rather than rescoring all predictions. The pruning step produces a customizable architecture where the number of retained filters per layer is determined globally by importance ranking across all 2D and 3D convolutions simultaneously.

## Method

CaKDP comprises three components. First, student-motivated category-aware KD (SKD) distills between heterogeneous detectors: NMS selects representative samples from the one-stage student each epoch, their positions query the two-stage teacher's first stage to form ROIs, and the teacher's second-stage category predictions supervise the selected student predictions, bridging the dense-anchor-to-sparse-ROI gap. Second, category-aware pruning (CaPr) evaluates each filter's importance by the category prediction gap on a validation set before and after its removal, then removes the least important filters globally across all layers (3D and 2D) to achieve a target retaining ratio, followed by fine-tuning. Third, a modified IOU-aware refinement module trains an IOU head on all samples and uses predicted IOU only to mask out low-IOU false positives during inference, preserving the KD-refined category predictions rather than rescoring them.

## Results

On KITTI, CaKDP with Voxel-RCNN as teacher and SECOND as student at 0.50 retaining ratio achieves 71.77 moderate mAP@R40 with only 1.5M parameters and 30.2 GFLOPs, surpassing the uncompressed SECOND baseline (66.72 mAP) by 5.05% while reducing parameters by 3.5×. On WOD-mini, CaKDP with PV-RCNN++ as teacher and CenterPoint as student at 0.50 RR achieves 65.48 L2 mAPH with 2.8M parameters and 55.6 GFLOPs. On full WOD, the pruned CenterPoint (0.50 RR) + CaKDP reaches 67.27 L2 mAPH, outperforming the original CenterPoint (65.66) by 1.61% with 2.8× fewer parameters and 2.1× fewer FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaKDP consistently outperforms all four prior KD methods (Vanilla KD, GID, PD, SparseKD) across every teacher-student pair on both KITTI and WOD-mini. On KITTI with Voxel-RCNN→SECOND, CaKDP at 0.50 RR achieves 71.77 mAP versus 67.43 (GID, best prior) at 0.75 RR, a 4.34-point gain while using half the parameters (1.5M vs 3.0M) and fewer FLOPs (30.2G vs 45.7G). On WOD-mini with PV-RCNN++→CenterPoint, CaKDP at 0.50 RR reaches 65.48 mAPH versus 63.46 (SparseKD, best prior) at 0.65 RR, a 2.02-point improvement with fewer parameters (2.8M vs 3.4M). The teacher models (Voxel-RCNN, PV-RCNN, PartA2, PV-RCNN++) serve as knowledge sources rather than direct competitors; CaKDP's student detectors surpass the uncompressed student baselines (SECOND: 66.72→71.77 mAP; CenterPoint on WOD: 65.66→67.27 mAPH) despite having 2.8–3.5× fewer parameters. For IOU-aware refinement, CaKDP's modified approach (filtering FPs by IOU threshold) outperforms the previous rescoring scheme (AFDetV2, IOU-Aware, CIA-SSD) by 4.46 points on KITTI (71.77 vs 67.31) and 3.05 points on WOD-mini (65.48 vs 62.43), because rescoring disrupts the KD-corrected category predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on knowledge distillation and structured pruning for LiDAR-based 3D detection (SparseKD, PointDistiller, ITKD) by departing from the homogeneous-pair assumption: instead of distilling between same-architecture detectors of different widths, CaKDP transfers category predictions from a two-stage teacher to a one-stage student via student-motivated NMS-based sample selection. For pruning, it moves beyond per-module proportional width reduction (used in prior 3D KD methods) to a global, category-prediction-gap-based filter ranking that simultaneously evaluates 2D and 3D convolutions. The IOU-aware refinement module repurposes the IOU head from prior work (AFDetV2, CIA-SSD) not for rescoring category predictions but for binary false-positive filtering, which the authors show is orthogonal to and compatible with KD-based category correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical recipe for deploying high-accuracy 3D detectors on resource-constrained platforms, achieving ~2× FLOPs reduction with minimal or even positive accuracy gains across KITTI and Waymo. The finding that heterogeneous (two-stage → one-stage) KD outperforms homogeneous KD challenges the prevailing assumption that distillation pairs must share architecture, opening the door to leveraging stronger but heavier two-stage teachers for lightweight one-stage students. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that CaKD is restricted to detectors within the same model family (e.g., CNN-based), and does not generalize to transformer-based architectures, which is an important open question as 3D detection increasingly adopts attention-based backbones.
