## Problem

Privacy-preserving visual localization via 3D line clouds (e.g., uniform line clouds, paired-point lifting) defends against scene inversion attacks but suffers from slow single-image localization due to weak geometric constraints requiring the computationally expensive p6L minimal solver (up to 64 algebraic solutions). No prior line-based approach achieves real-time single-image camera relocalization while maintaining strong privacy guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241441/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image with extracted 2D SIFT keypoints and a pre-built 3D ray cloud (constructed from an SfM point cloud where each point is lifted to a ray passing through one of two K-means-derived centers) as input, and outputs the 6-DOF camera pose (rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation t ∈ ℝ³). Camera intrinsics K are assumed known. The ray cloud is a static scene representation shared across queries, and localization is performed per query image via 2D-to-3D-ray correspondences matched by SIFT descriptors.

## Method

The core contribution is the 3D ray cloud representation, where each SfM point is lifted to a ray intersecting one of two predefined centers (found via K-means clustering), effectively modeling the scene as two virtual omnidirectional cameras. This structure transforms camera localization into perspective relative pose estimation between the query image and a calibrated two-camera rig, enabling a novel p5+1R minimal solver: the 5-point algorithm estimates relative pose to the first virtual camera (yielding ≤10 solutions vs. 64 for p6L), and one additional 2D-to-ray correspondence resolves the unknown scale factor α. The solver is wrapped in LO-RANSAC with an MLESAC cost based on squared epipolar distances, achieving ~137 FPS real-time performance. For enhanced privacy, a voxel-based ray sampling technique partitions the scene into a 3D checkerboard grid, assigning neighboring voxels to different ray centers so that true k-nearest points share the same center and are excluded from the adapted inversion attack's peak-finding, disrupting the nearest-neighbor statistics that the density-based attack relies on.

## Results

On the Energy Landscape dataset, the proposed ray cloud achieves the lowest pose error among all privacy-preserving line-based methods (ΔR = 0.262° mean, Δt = 0.010 m mean) while running at 6 ms per query — roughly 15× faster than ULC (90 ms) and PPL (88 ms). On Cambridge, ray cloud shows slightly higher pose errors (ΔR = 0.855°, Δt = 0.219 m) compared to ULC and PPL but still runs at 12 ms versus 186–289 ms for baselines. For privacy, the geometric error (e_g) of recovered points is 0.669 m for ray cloud versus 0.045 m for ULC on Energy Landscape, and 9.614 m versus 0.745 m on Cambridge, indicating substantially degraded scene recovery. The voxel-based variant further improves privacy (e_g = 0.770 m, PSNR = 10.05) at a small cost in pose accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Uniform Line Cloud (ULC) [50], ray cloud wins decisively on runtime (6 ms vs 90 ms on Energy Landscape; 12 ms vs 289 ms on Cambridge) and on privacy (e_g of 0.669 m vs 0.045 m on Energy Landscape; 9.614 m vs 0.745 m on Cambridge), meaning the scene is far harder to recover from ray clouds. On pose accuracy, ray cloud slightly outperforms ULC on Energy Landscape (ΔR 0.262° vs 0.272°) but lags on Cambridge (ΔR 0.855° vs 0.775°), likely due to the larger baseline between ray centers in outdoor scenes. Against PPL [26], ray cloud again dominates on speed (6 ms vs 88 ms; 12 ms vs 186 ms) and privacy (e_g 0.669 m vs 0.056 m on Energy Landscape), while matching or slightly exceeding PPL's pose error on Energy Landscape (ΔR 0.262° vs 0.292°) but showing a larger gap on Cambridge (0.855° vs 0.775°). The voxel-based variant trades a small amount of pose accuracy (ΔR increases by ~0.03–0.035°) for the best privacy metrics across both datasets (lowest PSNR, SSIM, and highest e_g), confirming that the voxel sampling effectively disrupts the adapted inversion attack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line-cloud lineage for privacy-preserving visual localization, which began with uniform line clouds (Speciale et al. [50]), was challenged by the geometry inversion attack (Chelani et al. [7]), and was partially addressed by paired-point lifting (Lee et al. [26]). The key departure is a geometric reformulation: by constraining all lifted lines to pass through one of two fixed ray centers, camera localization becomes a standard perspective relative pose problem solvable with the 5-point algorithm (adapted as p5+1R), rather than the generalized relative pose estimation (p6L) required by prior line clouds. The paper also builds on the minimal 5+1 solver [59] and adapts the geometry inversion attack of Chelani et al. [7] to expose residual vulnerabilities in the ray cloud representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ray cloud achieves the first real-time single-image localization (~137 FPS) among line-based privacy-preserving methods, an order-of-magnitude speedup over p6L-based approaches, making privacy-preserving localization practically deployable in AR/robotics settings. However, the privacy gains over PPL are modest: the adapted inversion attack still recovers scene geometry with measurable (if degraded) fidelity, and the authors acknowledge a potential new attack vector via cylindrical/spherical projection of rays. Open questions include whether more ray centers can defend against such projection-based attacks and whether the voxel size trade-off between privacy and accuracy can be automated rather than manually tuned per dataset.
