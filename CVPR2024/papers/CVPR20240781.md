## Problem

Existing deep learning BVQA methods process videos in aggressively subsampled formats—spatially resized or cropped to fixed sizes and temporally subsampled at rates proportional to frame rate—rendering them insensitive to the actual spatial resolution and frame rate of the input. When two videos of the same scene at different resolutions are resized to the same lower resolution, their perceptually distinct quality differences vanish, and similarly, proportional temporal subsampling makes different frame rate videos appear identical to the model.

## Input / Output

The model takes a full video sequence at its native spatial resolution and frame rate as input. Internally, the base quality predictor consumes M uniformly sampled key frames resized to 224×224, the spatial rectifier builds Laplacian pyramids (K=5 levels) at the actual resolution, and the temporal rectifier extracts M video chunks of L consecutive frames at the actual frame rate. The output is a scalar quality score, with the modular design allowing four distinct scores (q_b, q_s, q_t, q_st) depending on which rectifiers are active, enabling per-dimension quality analysis.

## Method

The proposed modular BVQA model comprises three components: a base quality predictor (CLIP ViT-B on downsampled key frames with a two-layer MLP regressor), a spatial rectifier (Laplacian pyramid at actual resolution processed by the first two stages of ResNet-18, producing scale α_s and shift β_s), and a temporal rectifier (SlowFast fast pathway on video chunks at actual frame rate, producing scale α_t and shift β_t). Each rectifier applies an affine transformation (q = α·q_b + β) to the base score, and the final score aggregates active rectifiers via geometric mean of scales and arithmetic mean of shifts. A key training strategy randomly drops out each rectifier with probability 0.2, forcing the base predictor to function as a standalone BVQA model that performs better when combined with rectifiers. The model is pretrained on LSVQ and fine-tuned per dataset, optimized with PLCC loss via Adam for 30 epochs.

## Results

On the weighted average across seven UGC datasets, the full model q_st achieves SRCC/PLCC of 0.882/0.890, outperforming DOVER (0.860/0.870), FastVQA (0.856/0.860), and Li22 (0.849/0.847). On the large-scale LSVQ-test set, q_st reaches 0.895/0.895, surpassing DOVER (0.888/0.889) and FastVQA (0.876/0.877). On PGC datasets targeting specific distortions, the spatial rectifier q_s achieves 0.837/0.891 on BVI-SR and the temporal rectifier q_t achieves 0.620/0.782 on BVI-HFR, both substantially exceeding all competing methods. The full model also attains 0.933/0.950 on ETRI and 0.802/0.811 on LIVE-Livestream, the best results among all tested methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The full model q_st achieves the best weighted-average SRCC/PLCC (0.882/0.890) across seven UGC datasets, beating the two strongest baselines DOVER (0.860/0.870) and FastVQA (0.856/0.860) by 2.2 and 2.6 SRCC points respectively. On the large-scale LSVQ-test set, q_st (0.895/0.895) edges out DOVER (0.888/0.889) and clearly surpasses FastVQA (0.876/0.877) and SimpleVQA (0.866/0.863). On PGC datasets, the modular design proves decisive: the spatial rectifier q_s (0.837/0.891) dominates all six knowledge-driven baselines on BVI-SR (best competitor BTURA at 0.737/0.851), and the temporal rectifier q_t (0.620/0.782) outperforms FAVER (0.556/0.639), the only baseline designed for frame-rate variation, on BVI-HFR. On ETRI, q_st (0.933/0.950) far exceeds TLVQM (0.270/0.251) and BRISQUE (0.248/0.207), demonstrating the advantage of learned features over handcrafted ones for streaming artifacts. In cross-dataset generalization (trained on LSVQ, tested without fine-tuning), q_st maintains competitive or superior performance, e.g., 0.878/0.884 on KoNViD-1K versus DOVER's 0.884/0.883, while also being faster (0.159 s vs. 0.047 s for DOVER on an 8-second 1080p video). The base predictor q_b alone already matches or exceeds several baselines on spatially-dominated datasets like CVD2014 (0.870/0.892 vs. FastVQA 0.883/0.901) and KoNViD-1K (0.864/0.875 vs. DOVER 0.892/0.900), confirming that the rectifiers add targeted gains rather than being universally necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in deep-learning BVQA, where videos are aggressively subsampled (resized, cropped, temporally downsampled) to fit fixed-size pretrained backbones, rendering models insensitive to actual spatial resolution and frame rate. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes three pretrained components—CLIP ViT-B (base predictor), ResNet-18 on Laplacian pyramids (spatial rectifier), and the SlowFast fast pathway (temporal rectifier)—into a modular system where rectifiers output affine (scale, shift) corrections to a base score. The modular dropout training strategy, which randomly disables rectifiers so the base predictor must function standalone, is a novel training mechanism not seen in prior BVQA work. Conceptually, it extends the knowledge-driven tradition of explicitly modeling resolution and frame rate effects (Q-STAR, FAVER) into a learned, extensible framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design enables a useful computational analysis of existing UGC datasets in terms of spatial versus temporal complexity, revealing an 'easy dataset problem' where near-blind-image-quality models achieve competitive scores because distortions are dominated by spatial artifacts insensitive to resizing. This meta-observation has broader implications for how the field constructs and evaluates VQA benchmarks. Open questions highlighted by the authors include adaptive (data-driven) routing of rectifiers, learned feature-level aggregation instead of the handcrafted geometric/arithmetic mean, and parameter-efficient addition of new rectifiers (e.g., for dynamic range or color gamut) without full retraining.
