## Problem

Existing view synthesis methods are either scene-specific, requiring per-scene volumetric rendering optimisation (NeRF, InstantNGP, Gaussian Splatting), or severely constrained in flexibility, supporting only a single reference-to-single-target view (Zero-1-to-3) or fixed target poses (SyncDreamer, MVDream). The paper asks how to learn a general, coordinate-system-independent 3D representation that enables scalable view synthesis with arbitrary numbers of reference and target views at arbitrary camera poses, without any expensive 3D operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240088/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EscherNet takes N reference RGB images with their camera poses (4 DoF object-centric or 6 DoF SE(3)) and M target camera poses as input, producing M 3D-consistent novel view images. Although trained with a fixed 3-to-3 view configuration, the model generalises to any N and M at inference, generating over 100 simultaneous target views on a single consumer GPU. Reference views are compressed by a lightweight ConvNeXt-v2-Tiny encoder into conditioning tokens at reduced resolution, while target views are generated at the latent resolution of Stable Diffusion v1.5.

## Method

EscherNet repurposes the Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net by redirecting self-attention to enforce target-to-target consistency across M views and cross-attention to enforce reference-to-target consistency, with no additional learnable parameters. The core innovation is Camera Positional Encoding (CaPE), which applies RoPE-style block-diagonal rotation matrices to token features based on camera poses, so that dot-product attention between query and key directly encodes the relative camera transformation independent of any global coordinate system. For 4 DoF poses, rotations use standard 2×2 rotation matrices and radius uses a log-scaled rotation; for 6 DoF, the full 4×4 SE(3) matrix is tiled into a block-diagonal structure with transposed-inverse applied to queries. A fine-tuned ConvNeXt-v2-Tiny encoder captures both high-level semantics and low-level textures from reference views as conditioning tokens, replacing the single-image concatenation strategy of prior 3D diffusion models. The model is trained via standard denoising diffusion on Objaverse-1.0 (800K objects, 12 rendered views each) with no 3D convolutions or volumetric rendering.

## Results

On GSO-30 novel view synthesis with a single reference view, EscherNet achieves PSNR 20.24, SSIM 0.884, and LPIPS 0.095, outperforming Zero-1-to-3-XL (PSNR 18.93, trained on 10× more data) and RealFusion (PSNR 12.76). Performance scales gracefully with more reference views, reaching PSNR 25.90 and LPIPS 0.036 with 10 views. For 3D reconstruction on GSO, EscherNet achieves Chamfer distance 0.0314 and Volume IoU 0.5974 from a single reference view, improving to Chamfer 0.0167 and Volume IoU 0.7478 with 10 views, surpassing the best baseline SyncDreamer (Chamfer 0.0400) by ~21%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EscherNet outperforms all 3D diffusion baselines on novel view synthesis: with 1 reference view on GSO, it beats Zero-1-to-3 (PSNR 20.24 vs 18.51) and Zero-1-to-3-XL (20.24 vs 18.93) despite using 10× less training data, and substantially outperforms RealFusion (20.24 vs 12.76) without requiring expensive SDS optimisation. Against scene-specific neural rendering methods on NeRF Synthetic, EscherNet wins with fewer than 5 reference views (e.g., PSNR 17.16 vs InstantNGP 18.17 at 5 views is close, but at 3 views EscherNet achieves 16.19 vs 14.27), but loses to both InstantNGP and Gaussian Splatting as reference views increase beyond 10, where scene-specific optimisation dominates. For 3D reconstruction on GSO, EscherNet surpasses SyncDreamer (Chamfer 0.0314 vs 0.0400, ~21% improvement) and One-2-3-45 (0.0314 vs 0.0632, ~50% improvement) from a single view, and with 10 views achieves Chamfer 0.0167, beating even NeuS trained directly on 10 ground-truth views (0.0195). Point-E and Shape-E are the weakest baselines, with Chamfer distances around 0.044–0.045, roughly 40% worse than EscherNet's single-view result.

## Relation to Prior Work

EscherNet repurposes the Stable Diffusion U-Net architecture for multi-view generation, replacing text-conditioned cross-attention with reference-view conditioning and repurposing self-attention for target-to-target consistency. Its key architectural novelty, Camera Positional Encoding (CaPE), directly adapts the rotary position encoding (RoPE) formulation from NLP to encode 4 DoF and 6 DoF camera poses, enabling dot-product attention to operate on relative camera transforms without any coordinate-system dependency. This departs fundamentally from scene-specific neural rendering (NeRF, InstantNGP, 3DGS) that couple representations to global 3D coordinates, and from prior 3D diffusion models (Zero-1-to-3, SyncDreamer, MVDream) that are restricted to a single reference view or a fixed set of target poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying novel view synthesis, single/multi-image 3D reconstruction, and text-to-3D generation under a single any-to-any generative framework, EscherNet offers a scalable alternative to per-scene optimisation that can leverage large-scale 2D image data without 3D operations. The CaPE mechanism is a clean, parameter-free way to inject geometric structure into transformer attention and is likely to be adopted in subsequent multi-view and 3D-aware architectures. Key open questions remain: the model is trained on 4 DoF object-centric data and has not yet been validated on 6 DoF real-world scenes; autoregressive generation degrades quality; and matching the photo-realistic fidelity of scene-specific methods with many views remains an unsolved challenge.
