## Problem

Existing Remote Sensing Foundation Models (RSFMs) are limited by single-modality pre-training, static (non-temporal) inputs, and the neglect of geo-context, which collectively hamper their ability to handle the diverse range of Earth Observation tasks that require multi-modal temporal data and region-aware reasoning. SkySense addresses this by being the first billion-scale multi-modal RSFM that jointly models optical, multispectral, and SAR temporal sequences with explicit geo-context integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242512/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a spatially-aligned tuple {x_HR, x_Ms, x_SAR} consisting of a static high-resolution RGB image (WorldView-3/4), a 20-frame Sentinel-2 multispectral temporal sequence, and a 10-frame Sentinel-1 SAR temporal sequence, augmented with geo-location and acquisition date metadata. Because the encoder is factorized, any subset of modalities can be used independently or in combination, and the geo-context prototype module is optional at inference. Downstream outputs range from pixel-level segmentation masks and oriented bounding boxes to change-detection masks and scene-level class labels, depending on the attached head.

## Method

SkySense employs a factorized multi-modal spatiotemporal encoder that first extracts spatial features independently per modality (Swin-H for HSROI, ViT-L for TMsI and TSARI) and then fuses them via a Naive Transformer with date-specific temporal positional encoding, exploiting the spatial alignment of co-registered RSI to avoid expensive 3D convolutions. Pre-training uses a DINO-style teacher-student framework with three complementary objectives: Multi-Granularity Contrastive Learning (contrastive losses at pixel, object, and image levels across each modality and the fused representation), Cross-Modal Alignment (a multi-modal contrastive loss pulling same-location features from different sensors together), and Geo-Context Prototype Learning (region-specific prototypes learned via Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport and updated by EMA, then attended to at inference to inject region-aware semantics). The 2.06B-parameter model is trained on 21.5 million multi-modal temporal sequences, and its modular design allows individual components (spatial encoder, fusion module, geo-context prototypes) to be frozen or fine-tuned independently for downstream tasks.

## Results

SkySense achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 16 evaluation datasets and 7 task types. On semantic segmentation, it reaches 70.91 mIoU on iSAID (vs. 68.71 for SatLas) and 46.5 mIoU on DynamicEarthNet-Planet test set (vs. 45.6 for GFM). On object detection, it attains 78.73 mAP50 on DIOR and 54.57 mAP on FAIR1M, surpassing the previous best by 2.83 and 3.99 points respectively. On scene classification, it achieves 97.68% OA on AID (TR=20%) and 64.38% Top-1 accuracy on fMoW-S2, outperforming all 18 compared RSFMs in every test scenario.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkySense outperforms all 18 compared RSFMs on every dataset and task, with no losses or ties reported. Against the strongest recent baselines, the average margins are 2.76% over GFM, 3.67% over SatLas, and 3.61% over Scale-MAE. On object detection (Table 2b), SkySense leads CMID by 3.99 mAP on FAIR1M (54.57 vs. 50.58) and RingMo by 2.83 mAP50 on DIOR (78.73 vs. 75.90). On change detection (Table 2c), it surpasses GFM by 0.24 F1 on LEVIR-CD (92.58 vs. 91.73) and MATTER by 0.69 F1 on OSCD (60.06 vs. 59.37). On multi-modal segmentation (Table 4), SkySense improves over GFM by 0.9 mIoU on Dyna.-MM (46.5 vs. 45.6) and over Garnot et al. by 0.6 OA on PASTIS-MM (84.8 vs. 84.2), while also achieving the top DynamicEarthNet leaderboard ranking. The consistent wins across single-modal, multi-modal, static, and temporal settings confirm the generality of the multi-modal spatiotemporal pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkySense builds on the DINO teacher-student self-supervised framework (Caron et al., ICCV 2021) and the multi-modal contrastive alignment loss from Li et al. (NeurIPS 2021), but departs substantially from prior RSFMs in three ways. Where models like GFM, SatLas, and Scale-MAE pre-train on single-modality static imagery, SkySense introduces a factorized multi-modal spatiotemporal encoder that jointly processes temporal optical (RGB + multispectral) and SAR sequences. It further extends the pre-training objective with Multi-Granularity Contrastive Learning (pixel/object/image levels) and Geo-Context Prototype Learning via Sinkhorn-Knopp clustering, addressing gaps the authors identify in prior work's neglect of spatial granularity diversity and geo-contextual information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first 2B-parameter multi-modal temporal RSFM, SkySense establishes a new reference point for the remote sensing foundation model community and its modular design (spatial encoder, fusion module, geo-context prototypes usable independently or combined) lowers the barrier for task-specific deployment. The finding that SAR data yields a 13% OA gain over optical-only only when cloud coverage exceeds 50% clarifies the conditional value of multi-modal fusion in practice. Open questions include whether geo-context prototypes generalize to geographically unseen regions, how the model scales beyond 21.5M training sequences, and the authors' stated plan to incorporate language modality for broader EO task coverage.
