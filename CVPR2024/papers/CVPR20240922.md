## Problem

Existing VLM-based HOI detectors rely on handcrafted templates (e.g., 'a photo of a person [action] a/an [object]') that encode only action-specific vocabulary-level text, causing similar interactions like 'sitting a bed' and 'lying a bed' to occupy nearly identical positions in the embedding space. This learning ambiguity prevents the text encoder from providing discriminative guidance to the visual encoder, limiting HOI detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240922/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces HOI triplets (human bounding box, object bounding box, interaction class label). DETR and BLIP are kept frozen during training; only the SICHOI-specific modules (multi-view extractor, dual cross-attention decoder, interaction decoder) are trained end-to-end. The syntactic interaction bank is pre-generated offline via ChatGPT for each HOI category in the dataset, so at inference time the model consumes only the image while the textual priors are fixed per category.

## Method

SICHOI introduces three components to align fine-grained textual interaction context with multi-scale visual features. First, a Syntactic Interaction Bank (SIB) replaces the simple action prompt with three levels of syntactic descriptions—spatial relationship, action-oriented posture, and situational condition—generated by querying ChatGPT, which increases the average cosine distance between HOI text embeddings from ~0.4 to ~0.7. Second, a Multi-View Visual Extractor (MVVE) uses SAM for instance segmentation and the BLIP image encoder to extract visual features at instance, interaction (union box), and full-image levels, then fuses them via an FFN to produce interaction queries. Third, a Dual Cross-Attention Decoder (DCAD) performs bidirectional cross-attention between the syntactic text features and image patch tokens, augmented with instance and union mask embeddings to inject spatial structure, enabling mutual context propagation before a final interaction decoder classifies each human-object pair using a focal loss.

## Results

On HICO-DET (default full setting), SICHOI with R101+ViT-L/16 achieves 45.04 mAP, outperforming the previous best PViC (Swin-L) by 0.72 mAP and the best VLM-based method DiffHOI (Swin-L+ViT) by 3.54 mAP. On V-COCO, SICHOI reaches 71.1 AP#1role and 75.6 AP#2role, surpassing RLIPv2 (68.8/70.8) and CQL (66.5/69.9). In zero-shot evaluation on HICO-DET, SICHOI (R101+ViT-L/16) achieves 43.02 mAP under Rare-First and 39.07 mAP under Non-Rare-First settings, exceeding the best prior method ADA-CM by 9.99 and 7.68 mAP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SICHOI (R101+ViT-L/16) achieves the best HICO-DET Full mAP of 45.04, surpassing the strongest transformer-based baseline PViC (Swin-L, 44.32) by 0.72 mAP and the best VLM-based method DiffHOI (Swin-L+ViT, 41.50) by 3.54 mAP. On V-COCO, SICHOI leads in both AP#1role (71.1 vs. 68.8 for RLIPv2, 66.5 for CQL) and AP#2role (75.6 vs. 70.8 for RLIPv2, 69.9 for CQL), with the largest gap over RLIPv2 on AP#2role (+4.8). In zero-shot HICO-DET, SICHOI (R101+ViT-L/16) outperforms ADA-CM (R50+ViT-B/16) by 9.99 mAP under Rare-First (43.02 vs. 33.01) and 7.68 mAP under Non-Rare-First (39.07 vs. 31.39), demonstrating strong generalization to unseen interaction categories. The paper attributes the V-COCO AP#2role advantage to the syntactic interaction bank's ability to infer occluded or missing interaction cues from scene context.

## Relation to Prior Work

SICHOI extends the VLM-based HOI detection line (HOICLIP, ViPLO, AGER, DiffHOI, ADA-CM) by replacing the standard handcrafted template ('a photo of a person [action] a/an [object]') with a three-level syntactic interaction bank (spatial relationship, action-oriented posture, situational condition) generated via ChatGPT. It builds on BLIP as the frozen VLM backbone, DETR for object detection, and SAM for instance segmentation, combining these into a multi-view visual extractor and a dual cross-attention decoder. The key conceptual departure from prior VLM-based methods is the argument that vocabulary-level action prompts suffer from embedding-space ambiguity for similar interactions, and that fine-grained syntactic context (prepositions, body posture, environment) provides the discriminative signal that simple templates lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The syntactic interaction bank offers a practical recipe for enriching text prompts in VLM-based detection beyond naive templates, and the cosine-distance analysis (0.7 vs 0.4) provides a clean empirical justification. The strong zero-shot gains on rare/unseen HOI categories (40.11 vs 33.01 mAP) suggest the approach generalizes well to long-tail interactions, which is a persistent pain point in HOI detection. Limitations include reliance on ChatGPT for bank construction (raising reproducibility and domain-transfer questions), the need for three external models (DETR, BLIP, SAM) increasing inference cost, and evaluation confined to two benchmarks with fixed action/object vocabularies. Open questions include whether the three-level decomposition is optimal, whether the bank can be generated without LLM assistance, and whether the approach transfers to truly open-vocabulary or video HOI settings.
