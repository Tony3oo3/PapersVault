## Problem

Optimization-based stereo matching methods require dozens of GRU iterations to converge in challenging areas such as occluded regions, textureless surfaces, and object boundaries, because pixel correspondence is ambiguous from local appearance alone. Existing slant-plane representations capture only planar geometry and fail to model non-planar structures like curved surfaces and edges, limiting their ability to guide disparity propagation in these difficult regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240145/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a stereo image pair at resolution sH×sW (s=4) and additionally consumes a monocular depth estimate from an off-the-shelf MiDaS network (384×384) as a geometric prior for initializing local structure. The output is a full-resolution disparity map at sH×sW, produced by optimizing at 1/4 resolution and then applying convex upsampling followed by LSGP refinement at full resolution.

## Method

LoS introduces a Local Structure Information (LSI) representation S = {G, O, R} that extends the traditional slant plane (disparity gradients G) with disparity offset details O and neighbouring relations R to explicitly model non-planar structures such as edges and curved surfaces. The key propagation mechanism, LSGP, updates high-uncertainty disparities by weighted aggregation from low-uncertainty neighbours using the LSI, eliminating costly feature warping and correlation sampling operations that slow down GRU-based propagation. The architecture combines a PAM-based binocular feature extractor (producing raw disparity and uncertainty), a MiDaS monocular depth prior for LSI initialization, and a multi-level ConvGRU that iteratively refines disparity, uncertainty, and LSI over N iterations, with LSGP applied after each GRU update. Training uses supervised L1 losses on disparity, disparity offsets, and disparity gradients across all intermediate iterations, with a weighted least-squares solution for generating gradient labels that works directly with semi-dense ground truth.

## Results

On ETH3D, LoS achieves the best Bad1.0-all of 1.03% (vs. CREStereo 1.09%, CroCo-Stereo 1.14%) and AvgErr-all of 0.15 px. On Middlebury, LoS attains the lowest AvgErr-all of 1.75 px (vs. DLNR 1.91, CREStereo 2.10, RAFT-Stereo 2.71) and Bad2.0-all of 8.03%. On KITTI 2012, LoS ranks first on out-3-all (1.38%) and reflective regions (4.45% vs. IGEV 5.00%). In the Robust Vision Challenge setting, LoS achieves the best overall performance across ETH3D, Middlebury, and KITTI 2015 combined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoS outperforms all optimization-based baselines on ETH3D Bad1.0-all (1.03% vs. CREStereo 1.09%, CroCo-Stereo 1.14%, IGEV 1.51%) and on Middlebury AvgErr-all (1.75 px vs. DLNR 1.91, CREStereo 2.10, RAFT-Stereo 2.71). On KITTI 2012, LoS achieves the best out-3-all (1.38%) and a substantial margin on reflective regions (4.45% vs. IGEV 5.00%, CREStereo 7.27%), though PCWNet edges it slightly on out-3-all (1.37%). On KITTI 2015, IGEV and PCWNet/ACVNet remain slightly ahead on D1-bg-all (1.37–1.38% vs. LoS 1.42%), but LoS is competitive on D1-all-all (1.65% vs. IGEV 1.59%). In the Robust Vision Challenge setting, LoS surpasses CREStereo++RVC on ETH3D Bad1.0-all (1.47% vs. 1.70%) and KITTI 2015 D1-all-all (1.83% vs. 1.88%), confirming that local structure guidance improves cross-dataset robustness. LoS also runs faster than RAFT-Stereo (0.31 s vs. 0.43 s at 960×640) and CREStereo (0.41 s) while achieving better cross-dataset accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoS extends the optimization-based stereo matching lineage initiated by RAFT-Stereo's multi-level ConvGRU framework and CREStereo's cascaded architecture, but departs from their reliance on dozens of GRU iterations for convergence. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Local Structure Information (LSI) — an extension of the classical slant plane representation (used in PatchMatch Stereo, HITNet, and SPNet) — by augmenting disparity gradients with per-pair offset details and learned neighbor relations to capture non-planar geometries. The binocular feature extraction borrows the Parallax Attention Mechanism, and monocular depth from MiDaS serves as a structural prior for LSI initialization, a use of monocular cues in stereo that is relatively recent in the optimization-based camp.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoS demonstrates that explicit local structure guidance can replace much of the iterative GRU propagation, yielding both accuracy gains on challenging regions (occlusions, textureless areas, edges) and a meaningful speedup (0.31s vs 0.43s at 960×640). The LSI representation offers a more complete local geometry model than the slant plane alone, and the parameter-free LSGP module is a clean, interpretable mechanism that could be adopted in other iterative correspondence tasks. Open questions remain: the method still struggles with interocclusions and dense vegetation, the lack of constraints on offset residuals limits inference flexibility, and whether the monocular depth prior (MiDaS) can be replaced by learned features without performance loss is unexplored.
