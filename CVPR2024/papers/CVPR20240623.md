## Problem

The Sampson error, a linearized approximation of the geometric reprojection error, is widely used in computer vision for model fitting and pose refinement, yet no explicit theoretical bounds existed on its approximation quality. This paper asks: under what conditions does the Sampson approximation remain tight, and can we derive concrete, computable bounds relating it to the true geometric error?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240623/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a measurement vector z (e.g., a pair of 2D point correspondences), model parameters θ (e.g., an essential matrix), and optionally a positive-definite covariance Σ, and produces a scalar Sampson error E_S² = C(z)²/||J||² (or its Mahalanobis-weighted generalization). The theoretical contribution outputs explicit upper and lower bounds on ||ε^G||/||ε^S|| expressed in terms of the Hessian spectral radius ρ, the Jacobian norm ||J||, and the constraint value C(z) at the data point. For multiple constraints, the method selects a subset of n−m constraints with full-rank Jacobian to form the linearized system.

## Method

The core contribution is a family of explicit bounds on the Sampson approximation error. For a single quadratic constraint, Proposition 3.1 shows ||ε^S|| ≤ ||ε^G|| + (ρ/2||J||)||ε^G||², while Proposition 3.2 gives the reverse bound ||ε^G|| ≤ 2||ε^S|| under the condition ||J||⁴ ≥ 2|C(z)||JHJᵀ|, which is intuitively that the constraint is locally near-linear relative to its violation. These results generalize to polynomial constraints of arbitrary degree (Proposition 3.4) and to multiple constraints, where the authors propose selecting n−m constraints with full-rank Jacobian (motivated by algebraic geometry) rather than using all N constraints. The key practical insight is that approximation quality is governed by the ratio ρ|C(z)|/||J||²: points close to the constraint manifold or with low curvature yield tight approximations. The theory is validated on two-view relative pose, three-view trifocal tensor estimation, vanishing point refinement, and 2D/3D reprojection with covariances, where the Sampson error consistently outperforms symmetric epipolar and algebraic losses in pose refinement.

## Results

For two-view relative pose, the Sampson error achieves an AUC@1px of 0.999 in approximating the true reprojection error, compared to 0.902 for the symmetric epipolar error (Table 1). In pose refinement on IMC-PT, MegaDepth-1500, and ScanNet-1500, the Sampson loss yields AUC@10° of 0.678, 0.732, and 0.657 respectively, outperforming the initial DLT estimate (0.361, 0.321, 0.274) and all other losses (Table 2). For three-view estimation, the Sampson approximation on the three pairwise epipolar constraints (E_3) achieves AUC@1px of 0.998 at σ=1px, far exceeding the naive pairwise average E_pair (0.764) and the four-constraint formulation E_4 (0.765) (Table 3). In 2D/3D pose refinement on 7Scenes, the Sampson approximation matches the full covariance-weighted optimization (AUC@5px: 0.367 vs 0.367) while running 4× faster (2.4ms vs 10.1ms per query).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the Sampson approximation against the symmetric epipolar error (distances to epipolar lines in both images) as the primary baseline for two-view geometry. The Sampson error dominates across all thresholds: AUC@1px of 0.999 vs 0.902, and the gap is even larger at tighter thresholds (0.991 vs 0.620 at 0.1px). For pose refinement, the Sampson loss outperforms algebraic (squared epipolar constraints), cosine, and symmetric epipolar losses on all three datasets, e.g. 0.678 vs 0.673 (Sym. Epipolar) on IMC-PT. In the three-view setting, the Sampson approximation applied jointly to the three pairwise epipolar constraints (E_3) substantially outperforms the naive pairwise average (E_pair: 0.998 vs 0.764 AUC@1px at σ=1px) and the four independent trifocal constraints (E_4: 0.765), confirming the paper's theoretical recommendation to use the minimal set of full-rank constraints. For 2D/3D reprojection with covariances, the Sampson approximation produces nearly identical pose errors to the full optimization over 3D points (median rotation 1.01° vs 1.01° at τ=5px) while eliminating hundreds of extra optimization variables and reducing runtime from 10.1ms to 2.4ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Sampson's 1982 conic-fitting approximation and its subsequent adoption in epipolar geometry by Luong and Faugeras (1996), as well as extensions to homographies (Sturm; Chum et al.) and trifocal tensors (Leonardos et al.). While the Sampson error has been widely used as a practical surrogate for geometric residuals, no prior work provided explicit theoretical bounds on its approximation quality. The paper also draws on algebraic geometry concepts (Euclidean distance degree, variety dimension, Jacobian rank conditions) to generalize the analysis from single quadratic constraints to arbitrary polynomial and multi-constraint settings, positioning itself at the intersection of applied algebraic geometry and computer vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit bounds (factor-of-2 guarantee under mild curvature conditions) give practitioners a principled criterion for when the Sampson approximation is trustworthy, which was previously only understood empirically. The three-view results showing that pairwise epipolar constraints (C3) outperform the full trifocal tensor formulation (C4) for Sampson approximation are practically useful for multi-view estimation pipelines. Open questions include extending the bounds to non-polynomial constraints (e.g., general camera models), tightening the constants in the multi-constraint case, and understanding whether the algebraic-geometry-based constraint selection strategy generalizes beyond the trifocal setting.
