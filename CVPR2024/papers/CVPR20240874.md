## Problem

Correspondence-based pose estimation (e.g., LoFTR + RANSAC) achieves sub-degree precision but fails under large view changes and cannot recover translation scale, while learned pose regression is more robust and produces scale but sacrifices precision. No existing method simultaneously delivers the precision of solvers, the robustness of learned regression, and absolute translation scale in a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240874/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two overlapping RGB images with known camera intrinsics, along with either dense pairwise features (from LoFTR or 8-Point ViT) and correspondences (from LoFTR or SuperGlue), or correspondences with descriptors alone. It outputs a full 6DoF relative pose T ∈ SE(3) — rotation in SO(3) and translation in R³ with absolute scale — using a 6D rotation representation (Zhou et al.) to enable linear combination of rotation estimates before Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization. The method is agnostic to the specific feature or correspondence backbone, requiring only that correspondences be available as 2D point pairs.

## Method

FAR's core is a Pose Transformer (either an 8-Point ViT with dense features or a Vanilla Transformer with correspondences only) that predicts both a 6DoF pose T_t and a learned weight w ∈ [0,1] (separate w_r and w_t for rotation and translation) controlling how much to trust the solver. A key innovation is the Prior-Guided RANSAC: the Transformer's intermediate pose T_1 is injected into the solver by (a) modifying the scoring function to add a β(H|T_1) term measuring agreement between the hypothesized model and the prior, and (b) biasing RANSAC sampling toward correspondences consistent with T_1 via exponential weighting of Sampson error. The final output linearly combines the updated solver pose T_u and the Transformer pose T_t, with solver translation scaled by the Transformer's predicted magnitude to recover absolute scale. Training is progressive — first the Transformer alone, then jointly with a vanilla solver, then with the prior-guided solver — and the Gating MLP receives inlier counts as input so the network learns to shift reliance from solver to regression as correspondence quality degrades.

## Results

On Matterport3D, FAR achieves a median translation error of 0.25 m (89.2% within 1 m) and median rotation error of 0.20° (95.8% within 30°), reducing mean translation error by ~50% (0.94→0.49 m) and mean rotation error by ~48% (9.49→4.93°) over the best prior methods. On InteriorNet (large overlap), FAR reaches 0.60° median / 1.16° mean rotation error (98.5% ≤10°), outperforming 8-Point ViT (1.83°/2.90°) and Cai et al. (1.10°/2.89°). On the challenging StreetLearn dataset, FAR achieves 1.81° median rotation error vs. 2.43° for 8-Point ViT and 2.91° for Cai et al. On Map-free Relocalization, FAR(LoFTR) attains 148 cm average translation error and 18.1° median rotation error, improving over 6D Reg (168 cm / 22.5°) and LoFTR (199 cm / 30.6°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAR outperforms all 12 baselines on Matterport3D across both translation and rotation metrics. Against the strongest solver-based method LoFTR [67], FAR nearly halves mean rotation error (9.49°→4.93°) while matching its median (0.23°→0.20°), and additionally produces translation scale (0.25 m median) that LoFTR cannot. Against the strongest regression-based method NOPE-SAC-Reg [68], FAR reduces median translation from 0.52 m to 0.25 m and mean rotation from 14.37° to 4.93°. Against 8-Point ViT [60], FAR improves median rotation from 8.01° to 0.20° and median translation from 0.64 m to 0.25 m. On InteriorNet and StreetLearn [4], FAR beats 8-Point ViT [60] and Cai et al. [10] by 1.2–2.3° in median rotation, with the gap widening on StreetLearn where LoFTR correspondences fail to generalize. On Map-free Relocalization [2], FAR(LoFTR) improves over the 6D Reg backbone [2] by 20 cm in average translation and 4.4° in median rotation, and over LoFTR [67] by 51 cm and 12.5° respectively. Even in the correspondence-only setting (Vanilla TF), FAR outperforms all prior methods by a large margin, confirming flexibility across input modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAR sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: correspondence-based pose estimation (LoFTR, SuperGlue + RANSAC/5-Point) which achieves sub-degree precision but cannot recover translation scale and degrades under large view changes, and end-to-end learned pose estimation (8-Point ViT, correlation-volume methods) which produces scale and is more robust but less precise. The paper builds directly on 8-Point ViT as its Transformer backbone and on Arnold et al.'s 6D Reg for Map-free Relocalization, but departs from prior hybrid approaches (DSAC, MAGSAC, differentiable RANSAC) by making the final output an explicit learned weighted combination of solver and Transformer poses, and by injecting the learned prediction as a prior into both the sampling and scoring stages of RANSAC rather than differentiating through the solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By recovering translation scale while matching solver-level rotation precision, FAR enables downstream applications like 3D reconstruction and neural rendering from image pairs that previously required choosing between precision and scale. The flexibility to plug in arbitrary feature extractors and correspondence estimators makes it a practical drop-in improvement for existing pipelines. Limitations include slow inference (3.3 it/s on 10 GPUs) due to the Prior-Guided RANSAC implemented in Kornia, and potential bias from training on affluent Matterport3D homes. Open questions include whether the learned weighting and prior mechanism can be made more efficient, and how the approach generalizes to multi-view or online settings.
