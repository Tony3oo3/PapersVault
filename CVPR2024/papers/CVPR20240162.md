## Problem

Event-guided low-light image enhancement is severely limited by the absence of large-scale, real-world datasets with precise spatial-temporal alignment between paired low-light and normal-light image-event sequences. Existing datasets are either synthetic, restricted to static scenes, or suffer from misalignment errors that hinder robust training. Additionally, prior event-guided methods fuse event and image features uniformly without accounting for region-specific noise discrepancies between the two modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240162/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB image at 346×260 resolution (from DAVIS346) and a paired event stream, which is converted into a two-channel event voxel grid with bin size 32. The output is a single enhanced RGB image at the same resolution. The SDE dataset provides 31,477 spatially and temporally aligned pairs (indoor and outdoor) captured under both low-light (ND8 filter) and normal-light conditions, with spatial alignment error under 0.03 mm and 90% of temporal alignment errors below 0.01 s.

## Method

EvLight consists of three stages: (1) a Retinexformer-based initial light-up followed by SNR map estimation via mean-filter denoising; (2) SNR-guided Regional Feature Selection, where an IRFS block multiplies image features by a thresholded SNR map to retain high-SNR (low-noise) regions, while an ERFS block multiplies event features by the inverse SNR map to extract edge structure from low-SNR (high-noise) regions; and (3) a UNet-like Holistic-Regional Fusion Branch that uses multi-head channel-wise self-attention (HFE) to capture long-range dependencies and a spatial-attention HRF block to fuse the up-sampled holistic features with the selected regional image and event features at each scale. The key insight is that image and event modalities carry complementary but differently corrupted information across regions, so a single SNR prior can guide which modality to trust where. Training uses a combined L2 loss and AlexNet perceptual loss with Adam optimizer over 80 epochs.

## Results

On the proposed SDE dataset, EvLight achieves 22.44 dB PSNR and 0.7697 SSIM (SDE-in) and 23.21 dB PSNR and 0.7505 SSIM (SDE-out), outperforming the best Image+Event baseline (Liu et al.) by 0.65 dB and 0.29 dB PSNR respectively, and surpassing the best frame-based method Retinexformer by 1.14 dB on SDE-in. On the SDSD dataset, EvLight reaches 28.52 dB PSNR and 0.9125 SSIM (SDSD-in) and 26.67 dB PSNR and 0.8356 SSIM (SDSD-out), leading the best baseline by over 0.94 dB and 0.59 dB PSNR. The PSNR* metric (which accounts for global brightness fitting) shows even larger margins: +0.93 dB on SDE-in and +1.21 dB on SDE-out over the strongest competitor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvLight outperforms all eight baselines across all four dataset splits (SDE-in, SDE-out, SDSD-in, SDSD-out) on PSNR, PSNR*, and SSIM. Among Image+Event methods, the closest competitor is Liu et al. (AAAI'23), which EvLight surpasses by 0.65 dB PSNR on SDE-in and 0.94 dB on SDSD-in; ELIE (TMM'23) and eSL-Net (ECCV'20) trail by even larger margins (2.46 dB and 1.19 dB on SDE-in respectively). Against the strongest frame-based method Retinexformer (ICCV'23), EvLight gains 1.14 dB PSNR on SDE-in and 2.62 dB on SDSD-in, confirming that event guidance provides substantial structural detail recovery. Notably, on SDSD-out, ELIE and Liu et al. exhibit a large PSNR-to-PSNR* gap (e.g., ELIE: 23.29 vs 28.26), indicating overfitting to brightness rather than true structural restoration, whereas EvLight maintains a tighter gap (26.67 vs 30.30), demonstrating more faithful enhancement. The event-only baseline E2VID+ lags by over 7 dB, underscoring the necessity of the RGB image input for color and texture fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends event-guided low-light image enhancement by departing from the direct feature-fusion paradigm used in ELIE [18] and Liu et al. [25], which blend event and image features uniformly regardless of local noise characteristics. Instead, it introduces an SNR-guided regional selection mechanism (inspired by SNR-Net [48]) that adaptively routes image features from high-SNR regions and event features from low-SNR regions, addressing the previously unacknowledged discrepancy in noise distributions between the two modalities. The preprocessing stage directly adopts Retinexformer [4]'s illumination estimation, and the holistic fusion branch leverages a UNet-like architecture with channel-wise self-attention (analogous to Restormer [51]). On the data side, it moves beyond the static-scene LIE dataset [18] and the linear-motion SDSD [39] by introducing non-linear robotic trajectories with sub-0.03mm spatial precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SDE dataset (31,477 spatially and temporally aligned pairs with 90% temporal error < 0.01s) fills a critical gap in real-world event-image benchmarks, enabling supervised training for event-guided LIE in dynamic scenes — a setting previously only accessible through synthetic events or static captures. The SNR-guided selective fusion offers a principled answer to why naive event-image fusion degrades in mixed-illumination regions, a practical concern for deployment. Limitations include DAVIS346-specific chromatic aberrations and moiré patterns, and the reliance on a robotic arm for data collection limits scalability; the authors note that synchronous hardware triggering would reduce labor costs. Open questions include whether the SNR-guided routing generalizes to other event-camera hardware with different noise profiles and whether the approach extends to video-level enhancement with temporal consistency.
