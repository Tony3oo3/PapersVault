## Problem

Existing transformer-based point cloud pretraining models such as Point-BERT rely solely on autoencoding (masked prediction) and cannot perform autoregressive generation, limiting their versatility. The paper asks whether the GLM-style unification of autoencoding and autoregressive tasks in NLP can be transferred to point clouds, enabling a single model to handle both representation learning and unconditional/conditional generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240266/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw point cloud (1024 points sampled from ShapeNet) and partitions it into 64 patches of 32 points each via farthest point sampling and kNN. A pre-trained dVAE tokenizer (4-layer DGCNN with an 8192-entry codebook) discretizes each patch into a single token, so the transformer operates on a sequence of 64 discrete tokens plus a [CLS] token. Downstream, the [CLS] embedding yields class labels or per-point part labels, while the autoregressive head generates a full point cloud token-by-token (reconstructed via the dVAE decoder into 3D coordinates).

## Method

GPM uses a two-stage pipeline: first, a dVAE with DGCNN tokenizer and DGCNN-FoldingNet decoder is trained with Chamfer Distance and KL losses (Gumbel-Softmax relaxation for discrete tokens) to learn a point cloud vocabulary; second, a 12-layer, 384-dim, 6-head transformer is pretrained by concatenating two segments—PartA (autoencoding) and PartB (autoregressive)—under a GLM-inspired attention mask. In PartA, 25–45% of consecutive patches are replaced with [M] and predicted bidirectionally (masked point modeling with contrastive learning via MoCo); in PartB, the same masked span is generated sequentially with causal attention, conditioned on PartA as a prefix. The joint loss combines cross-entropy for both the autoencoding prediction and the autoregressive generation of the masked tokens, allowing the model to capture interdependencies among masked regions that pure autoencoding misses.

## Results

On ModelNet40 (1k points), GPM achieves 94.0% classification accuracy, outperforming Point-BERT (93.2%), MaskPoint (93.8%), and Point-MAE (93.8%). On ScanObjectNN, GPM reaches 90.2% (OBJ-BG), 90.0% (OBJ-ONLY), and 84.8% (PB-T50-RS), improving over Point-BERT by 2.77%, 1.88%, and 1.73% respectively. For unconditional point cloud generation on ShapeNet, GPM achieves the best JSD, MMD, Coverage, and 1-NNA scores across Airplane, Chair, and Car categories compared to Point-Flow and Point-BERT. In few-shot learning (5-way 10-shot), GPM attains 97.2% accuracy, surpassing Point-BERT (94.6%) and Point-MAE (96.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPM outperforms Point-BERT on ModelNet40 at 1k points (94.0% vs. 93.2%) and 8k points (94.3% vs. 93.8%), but slightly underperforms at 4k points (93.1% vs. 93.4%). On ScanObjectNN, GPM leads Point-BERT by 1.7–2.8% across all three variants, though Point-MAE edges out GPM on PB-T50-RS (85.2% vs. 84.8%). In few-shot classification, GPM achieves the best 5-way results (97.2% 10-shot, 98.7% 20-shot) but ties with Point-MAE and Point-M2AE on 10-way 20-shot (95.0%). For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, GPM (84.20/85.8 mIoU) marginally beats Point-BERT (84.11/85.6) but trails Point-MAE (86.1 Inst. mIoU). In unconditional generation, GPM dominates both Point-Flow and Point-BERT on all four metrics (JSD, MMD-CD, MMD-EMD, Coverage, 1-NNA) across all three ShapeNet categories, with the largest gains on the Chair category (JSD 0.98 vs. 1.23 for Point-BERT and 1.74 for Point-Flow).

## Relation to Prior Work

GPM directly extends Point-BERT's dVAE-based tokenizer and Masked Point Modeling (MPM) framework, but departs from it by incorporating an autoregressive generation objective inspired by GLM's multi-task attention mask design in NLP. While Point-BERT and Point-MAE focus exclusively on autoencoding-style masked prediction for representation learning, and Point-Flow handles generation separately, GPM unifies both paradigms within a single transformer by concatenating an autoencoding segment (PartA) and an autoregressive segment (PartB) with a structured attention mask. The conceptual lineage traces from BERT's masked language modeling and GPT's next-token prediction, bridged by GLM's demonstration that both can coexist in one model, now transplanted to the point cloud domain via vector-quantized patch tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPM is the first point cloud transformer to jointly learn representations and perform generation (unconditional, and potentially conditional) within a single architecture, eliminating the need for separate models for understanding vs. generation. However, the generation evaluation is limited to three ShapeNet categories with modest sample sizes, and conditional generation (text- or image-conditioned) is only demonstrated as a potential rather than thoroughly validated. Open questions include whether the dual-task pretraining scales to larger, more diverse 3D datasets (e.g., indoor scenes, LiDAR), whether the autoregressive component's benefit over pure autoencoding persists at scale, and how the approach compares to diffusion-based point cloud generators that have since become dominant.
