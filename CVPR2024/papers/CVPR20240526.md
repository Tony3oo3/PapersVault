## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based HDR deghosting methods (e.g., DiffHDR) reconstruct the entire HDR image from pure Gaussian noise, requiring thousands of denoising iterations and ~7.5 s per image on an A100 GPU. Since multi-exposure LDR inputs already supply most of the scene content, full-image denoising is both unnecessary and computationally prohibitive for practical deployment. The paper asks how to harness the distribution-estimation strength of diffusion models for HDR reconstruction while avoiding the cost of generating every pixel from scratch.

## Input / Output

The method takes three LDR images with exposure offsets of {−2, 0, +2} or {−3, 0, +3}, applies gamma correction to obtain linear-domain HDR references, and concatenates each LDR with its corresponding HDR reference into a 6-channel tensor that is fed through an alignment module. The output is a single ghost-free HDR image in the linear domain. During training the network processes 128×128 patches; at inference it handles full-resolution frames (e.g., 1000×1500) in 0.72 s on a single GPU, with the diffusion component operating on a compact H/4 × W/4 × 3 latent representation rather than the full image.

## Method

LF-Diff applies the diffusion model in a highly compact latent space to predict only a Low-frequency Prior Representation (LPR) of size H/4 × W/4 × 3, extracted by LPENet via PixelUnshuffle on the tonemapped-and-linear concatenated ground truth. A regression-based DHRNet then reconstructs the full HDR image, using a Prior Integration Module (cross-attention between the LPR and intermediate DHRNet features) and stacked Feature Refinement Modules that decompose features into high-frequency (processed by residual blocks) and low-frequency (processed by self-attention) branches. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 jointly optimizes LPENet and DHRNet with ground-truth LPRs; Stage 2 trains a lightweight U-Net denoiser to predict the LPR from aligned LDR features via DDIM (10 sampling steps), jointly optimized with DHRNet under a combined diffusion + reconstruction loss. This design reduces the diffusion burden from full-image denoising to a small latent prediction, yielding a 10× speedup over DiffHDR while adding only 2.39 M parameters over the regression baseline.

## Results

On Kalantari's dataset, LF-Diff achieves the best PSNR-μ of 44.76 dB, PSNR-L of 42.59 dB, SSIM-μ of 0.9919, and HDR-VDP-2 of 66.54, outperforming the runner-up HyHDR by 0.12 dB in PSNR-μ and 0.12 dB in PSNR-L. On Hu's dataset, LF-Diff reaches PSNR-μ of 48.74 dB, PSNR-L of 52.10 dB, and HDR-VDP-2 of 77.35, surpassing HyHDR by 0.28 dB and 0.19 dB respectively. Inference time is 0.72 s per image (1000×1500), approximately 10× faster than DiffHDR (7.53 s), while using only 7.48M parameters versus DiffHDR's 74.99M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LF-Diff outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against the closest competitor HyHDR [43], LF-Diff gains 0.12 dB PSNR-μ and 0.12 dB PSNR-L on Kalantari, and 0.28 dB / 0.19 dB on Hu, while also leading in SSIM and HDR-VDP-2. Compared to the prior diffusion-based method DiffHDR [44], LF-Diff improves PSNR-μ by 0.65 dB and PSNR-L by 0.86 dB on Kalantari while running 10× faster (0.72 s vs. 7.53 s) with 10× fewer parameters (7.48M vs. 74.99M). Among CNN-based methods, LF-Diff surpasses AHDR [41] by 1.14 dB PSNR-μ on Kalantari and 2.98 dB on Hu, and outperforms HDR-GAN [21] by 0.84 dB PSNR-μ on Kalantari. The transformer-based CA-ViT [16] trails LF-Diff by 0.44 dB PSNR-μ on Kalantari and 0.64 dB on Hu. Traditional patch-based methods (Sen [25], Hu [9]) and optical-flow-based Kalantari [11] are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 3 dB in PSNR-μ, confirming that learned generative priors are critical for handling large motions and saturation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LF-Diff extends the diffusion-model-based HDR reconstruction paradigm introduced by DiffHDR [44], which reconstructs full HDR images from pure Gaussian noise, by restricting the diffusion process to a compact latent space that predicts only low-frequency prior representations. The frequency decomposition strategy in the Feature Refinement Module (FRM) directly follows the high/low-frequency split from Lu et al. [18] for super-resolution, while the latent-space diffusion concept draws on Rombach et al. [23]. The key conceptual departure is that DMs need not estimate the entire image distribution; instead, they provide a lightweight prior that a regression-based network (DHRNet) leverages via cross-attention to produce the final HDR output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup over DiffHDR (0.72s vs. 7.53s) while simultaneously improving PSNR by 0.65–0.86 dB makes diffusion-based HDR deghosting practically deployable, addressing the primary barrier to real-world adoption. The ablation showing that joint training of the DM and DHRNet is critical (1.51 dB drop with split training) highlights an underexplored interaction between generative and regression components in hybrid pipelines. Open questions include whether the low-frequency-prior formulation generalizes to other multi-frame reconstruction tasks (e.g., light field, video HDR) and whether the 10-step DDIM requirement can be further reduced without quality loss.
