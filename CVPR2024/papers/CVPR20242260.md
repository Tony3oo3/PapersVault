## Problem

Traditional Structure-from-Motion pipelines rely on a non-differentiable, incremental registration loop (keypoint detection, pairwise matching, RANSAC, PnP, triangulation, bundle adjustment) that has been hand-engineered over decades. While deep learning has improved individual components like feature matching, the overall pipeline remains non-differentiable, preventing end-to-end training and forcing each stage to produce outputs optimized for its successor only in a heuristic sense. This paper asks whether a fully differentiable SfM pipeline can be both simpler and more accurate than the classical incremental framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242260/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an unordered set of N_I RGB images (no temporal ordering or video assumption) and outputs per-camera projection matrices (8-DoF: quaternion rotation, translation, and log focal length, with principal point fixed at image center) plus a 3D point cloud. At test time, SuperPoint and SIFT keypoints are extracted from a selected query frame to seed the tracker, and the entire reconstruction function is iterated multiple times—each iteration re-selecting the query frame as the one farthest from the previous—until sub-pixel bundle-adjustment reprojection error is reached.

## Method

VGGSfM replaces the incremental SfM loop with four fully differentiable stages: (1) a Transformer-based point tracker (adapted from CoTracker/PIPs) that directly predicts multi-view tracks with aleatoric-uncertainty confidence scores and a coarse-to-fine sub-pixel refinement, eliminating pairwise matching and track chaining; (2) a cross-attention Transformer camera initializer that jointly estimates all camera poses from global ResNet-50 image features and track-point descriptors, seeded with an 8-point-algorithm prior; (3) a Transformer triangulator that refines a DLT-initialized point cloud using track descriptors and positional harmonic embeddings; and (4) a differentiable Levenberg-Marquardt bundle adjustment implemented via the Theseus library, which backpropagates through the nested optimization loop using the implicit function theorem. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined loss over 3D point errors (pseudo-Huber), camera pose errors (Huber on parameterizations), and negative log-likelihood of track points under predicted 2D Gaussians, using a multi-stage curriculum (tracker on Kubric → fine-tune on target data → camera initializer → triangulator → joint training).

## Results

On Co3Dv2, VGGSfM achieves 92.1% RRE@15°, 88.3% RTE@15°, and 74.0% AUC@30°, surpassing the best prior method (RelPose++ at 82.3/77.2/65.1) by 9+ points on every metric. On IMC Phototourism, it reaches 45.23/58.89/73.92 AUC@3°/5°/10°, outperforming the strongest incremental SfM baseline DFSfM (46.55/58.74/72.19) on AUC@5° and AUC@10° while trailing by 1.3 points on AUC@3°. On ETH3D, VGGSfM attains 96.52% accuracy and 33.96% completeness at the 5 cm threshold, exceeding the best prior method DFSfM (95.83%/29.54%) on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Co3Dv2, VGGSfM dominates all baselines by a wide margin: it outperforms RelPose++ [36] (the strongest prior deep method) by 9.8 points on RRE@15° and 8.2 on AUC@30°, and beats COLMAP [59] by over 60 points, reflecting the difficulty of wide-baseline object-centric scenes for incremental SfM. On IMC Phototourism, where views overlap well and traditional SfM excels, VGGSfM edges out DFSfM [25] on AUC@5° (58.89 vs 58.74) and AUC@10° (73.92 vs 72.19) but trails by 1.32 points on AUC@3° (45.23 vs 46.55); it also surpasses PixSfM with SP+SG [39] on all three thresholds. On ETH3D triangulation, VGGSfM achieves the best accuracy at every threshold (e.g., 80.62% at 1 cm vs 80.38% for DFSfM) and a substantial completeness lead at 5 cm (33.96% vs 29.54%), outperforming PatchFlow [17], PixSfM [39], and DFSfM [25] regardless of their matching backend. The end-to-end joint training contributes a further 3.4–5.6 point gain over the non-joint variant, confirming the synergy between the differentiable components.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGGSfM departs fundamentally from the incremental SfM paradigm (COLMAP and its deep-learning extensions like PixSfM and DFSfM) by replacing the hand-engineered pipeline of pairwise matching, track chaining, and sequential camera registration with a single fully differentiable function. It repurposes video point-tracking architectures (PIPs, TAPIR, CoTracker) for unordered image sets, borrows the Transformer-based joint camera estimation idea from deep pose estimators (PoseDiffusion, RelPose++), and substitutes the non-differentiable Ceres solver with the Theseus library's differentiable Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer. The key architectural departure is that all four stages—tracking, camera initialization, triangulation, and bundle adjustment—are trained end-to-end, allowing gradients to flow through the entire reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully differentiable SfM pipeline to achieve state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks, demonstrating that a simpler learned pipeline can outperform decades of hand-engineered incremental SfM. The practical impact is significant given SfM's role as a front-end for NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting, and AR applications. However, the current scalability limitation (unable to process thousands of images) means it cannot yet replace COLMAP in large-scale photogrammetry, and the modest margins on IMC (narrow-baseline regime) suggest the advantage is most pronounced in wide-baseline or object-centric settings. Open questions include whether the differentiable formulation can be extended to large-scale scenes and whether the end-to-end training recipe generalizes to new domains without retraining.
