## Problem

High-quality video-text pairs are far harder to collect than image-text pairs: manual annotation requires watching entire videos, and the dominant large-scale datasets (HD-VILA-100M, HowTo100M) rely on ASR subtitles that frequently fail to describe the main visual content and action. No single automatic captioning model performs well across all video types, yet the community lacks a large-scale dataset with precise, semantically coherent captions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests 3.8M high-resolution (720p) long videos from HD-VILA-100M along with their associated metadata (title, description, subtitles) and individual frames, and produces 70.8M semantically coherent video-caption pairs averaging 8.5 seconds per clip and 13.2 words per caption. The student captioning model accepts a video clip plus optional text inputs (title, description, subtitles) and outputs a single descriptive sentence; the retrieval model takes a video clip and a set of candidate captions and selects the best-matching one.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, a two-stage semantics-aware splitting algorithm cuts long videos into coherent clips using shot boundary detection followed by ImageBind-embedding-based merging of adjacent clips that were incorrectly separated. Second, eight cross-modality teacher models (drawn from five base architectures: VideoLLaMA, VideoChat, VideoChat Text, BLIP-2, and MiniGPT-4) each generate a candidate caption using different combinations of visual and textual inputs, exploiting the finding that no single model exceeds 31% coverage while the ensemble covers 84.7% of videos. Third, a fine-grained video-to-text retrieval model (Unmasked Teacher finetuned with hard negative mining on a 100K human-annotated subset) selects the most accurate caption from the candidates. A two-branch student model (VideoLLaMA-style vision encoder plus a BLIP-2-style Q-Former text branch) is then trained via knowledge distillation on the full 70M dataset to replicate the multi-teacher pipeline in a single forward pass.

## Results

On zero-shot video captioning, Video-LLaMA pretrained on Panda-2M achieves BLEU-4 of 23.5 on MSR-VTT and 31.2 on MSVD, a 17.7% and 18.5% improvement over the official 2.5M-video pretraining weight (5.8 and 12.7). The student model trained on the full Panda-70M further improves to 25.4 and 32.8 BLEU-4. For zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, UMT pretrained on Panda-5M reaches 37.2% R@1 on MSR-VTT (vs. 30.2% for the official weight), and for text-to-video generation, AnimateDiff with Panda-2M pretraining achieves FVD of 421.9 on UCF101, a 77.4-point reduction over the official weight (499.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Panda-70M pretraining consistently outperforms all baselines across the three downstream tasks. In video captioning (Table 3), Video-LLaMA with Panda-2M pretraining surpasses its official 2.5M-video weight by 17.7% BLEU-4 on MSR-VTT (23.5 vs. 5.8), and the Panda-70M student model further extends this lead. In retrieval (Table 5), UMT with Panda-5M pretraining beats AlignPrompt (37.2 vs. 24.1 R@1 zero-shot T2V on MSR-VTT), BridgeFormer (37.2 vs. 26.0), and the official UMT weight (37.2 vs. 30.2); in the finetune setting it also surpasses CLIP4Clip (58.4 vs. 44.5), X-CLIP (58.4 vs. 49.3), and InternVideo (58.4 vs. 55.2) R@1 on MSR-VTT despite using far fewer pretraining pairs. In text-to-video generation (Table 6), AnimateDiff with Panda-2M achieves FVD 421.9 on UCF101, outperforming CogVideo (701.6), MagicVideo (699.0), LVDM (641.8), ModelScope (639.9), VideoLDM (550.6), and the official AnimateDiff weight (499.3). In the teacher-vs-student user study (Table 4), the student model (18.4% preference ratio with video input; 21.4% with video+text) outperforms every individual teacher—Video-LLaMA (9.4/7.0), VideoChat (7.7), BLIP-2 variants (10.7/9.0/9.9), and MiniGPT-4 (3.1)—though the ensemble of all eight teachers (23.3%) still edges out the student by a small margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the HD-VILA-100M dataset as its video source and departs from prior large-scale video-language datasets (HowTo100M, YT-Temporal-180M) that rely on ASR subtitles, which the authors show fail to describe main video content. The captioning pipeline draws on multiple cross-modality teacher models (Video-LLaMA, VideoChat, BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4) and introduces a fine-grained retrieval stage using a finetuned Unmasked Teacher to select the best caption, addressing the observation that generic contrastive retrieval models fail when all candidates are highly relevant. The student model's dual-branch architecture (video via Video-LLaMA-style encoder, text via a BLIP-2-style Q-Former) represents a new design for multimodal captioning that distills knowledge from all teachers into a single efficient model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Panda-70M provides 70M high-resolution, semantically coherent video-caption pairs with rich 13.2-word captions, filling a critical gap between small manually-annotated datasets (<120K) and large ASR-annotated datasets whose text poorly describes visual content. The student model's ability to outperform all individual teachers by 7.7% preference ratio while being a single forward pass makes scalable captioning practical. Key limitations include the dataset's bias toward vocal-intensive content (news, TV, documentaries) inherited from HD-VILA-100M, and the fine-grained short-clip design that may hinder long-video generation and dense captioning tasks. Extending the pipeline to unvocal videos and longer temporally coherent segments remains an important open direction.
