## Problem

Existing text-to-video (T2V) generation models are evaluated using only a handful of distribution-matching metrics like FVD or Inception Score, which fail to capture text-video alignment, motion quality, or temporal consistency. No comprehensive benchmark or multi-aspect evaluation framework exists for fairly comparing the diverse capabilities of large T2V models, especially when some models are closed-source services with no access to internal parameters.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consists of 700 text prompts (average 12.3 words) spanning four meta-types (human, animal, object, landscape) with three sub-types each, augmented with 50 styles and 20 camera-motion variants, and annotated with ground-truth metadata (object counts, colors, celebrity identities, expected text). T2V models generate short clips (1–4 s, 8–24 fps, 256×256 to 1280×720) from these prompts, and the evaluation pipeline outputs 17 per-metric scores across four aspects, which are combined via learned regression coefficients into a single human-aligned score and a final model ranking.

## Method

EvalCrafter constructs its prompt set by analyzing 200k real-world user prompts from Discord servers, then uses GPT-4 to expand four meta-subject classes into diverse, annotated prompts with a self-check loop for metadata consistency. Evaluation employs 17 objective metrics grouped into four aspects: visual quality (Dover VQA_A/VQA_T, Inception Score), text-video alignment (CLIP-Score, a novel SD-Score comparing video frames to SDXL-generated reference images, BLIP-BLEU caption similarity, SAM-Track-based Detection/Count/Color scores, DeepFace Celebrity ID, and PaddleOCR text recognition), motion quality (VideoMAE V2 action recognition, RAFT average flow, and a motion-amplitude classification score), and temporal consistency (RAFT warping error, CLIP-Temp semantic similarity, and face-identity consistency). A user study with 7 annotators scoring 2.5k videos on a 1–5 scale provides ground-truth preferences, and linear regression coefficients are fitted per aspect (80/20 train/test split) to align objective metrics with human judgments. The aligned scores achieve higher Spearman and Kendall correlations with human ratings than simple metric averaging, particularly for motion amplitude where the weighted combination flips from negative to positive correlation.

## Results

On the EvalCrafter benchmark (700 prompts), no single model dominates all four evaluation aspects: Gen2 leads in Visual Quality (62.51) and Motion Quality (56.43), Show-1 leads in Text-Video Alignment (62.07), and PikaLab leads in Temporal Consistency (65.41). The proposed human-alignment weighting method outperforms simple metric averaging in correlation with user scores, most dramatically for Motion Amplitude where the weighted score achieves Spearman's ρ = 45.0 versus −38.2 for the average, and for Temporal Consistency (56.7 vs. 54.4). For Text-Video Alignment, the CLIP-Score shows very weak correlation with human judgment (ρ = 6.3) compared to BLIP-BLEU (ρ = 26.7), highlighting the inadequacy of widely-used metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gen2 (Esser et al., 2023) achieves the highest Visual Quality (62.51) and Motion Quality (56.43) scores, outperforming the next-best VideoCrafter1 (60.85) and PikaLab (55.77) respectively, but ranks only 5th in Text-Video Alignment (60.98), trailing Show-1 (62.07) by over 1 point. PikaLab leads Temporal Consistency (65.41), beating Gen2 (64.41) by 1 point and Show-1 (60.83) by 4.6 points, yet scores poorly on Text-Video Alignment (55.80, rank 6). Show-1 achieves the best Text-Video Alignment (62.07) but has the lowest Visual Quality (52.19, rank 8), illustrating the trade-off between content fidelity and visual appeal. Floor33 Pictures is the weakest overall, ranking last in Motion Quality (49.16) and Temporal Consistency (50.24). ModelScope and ZeroScope, both open-source 256×256 models, consistently rank in the bottom two across most aspects, with ZeroScope scoring lowest in Text-Video Alignment (51.21). The paper's key finding is that rankings shift dramatically across aspects—no model wins all four—making multi-dimensional evaluation essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the evaluation methodology from text-to-image benchmarks (HRS-Bench, DALL-Eval, TIFA) and LLM evaluation to the text-to-video domain, where prior work relied almost exclusively on distribution-matching metrics like FVD and Inception Score. It repurposes a suite of existing computer vision tools—CLIP, SAM-Track, RAFT, Dover, BLIP2, VideoMAE, PaddleOCR, DeepFace—as multi-aspect evaluation metrics, and introduces a human-alignment step via linear regression to weight these metrics toward user preferences. The benchmark construction itself is novel in combining real-world user prompt analysis with GPT-4-assisted generation to produce a diverse, annotated prompt set, departing from the small hand-crafted prompt lists used in earlier T2V papers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EvalCrafter establishes the first multi-aspect, human-aligned evaluation framework for T2V models, providing a template that future benchmarks can build upon. The finding that CLIP-Score—the most widely cited alignment metric in T2V literature—has a Spearman's ρ of only 6.3 with human judgment (versus 26.7 for BLIP-BLEU) challenges a core assumption in the field and suggests that many published comparisons may be misleading. Key open questions include how to scale the benchmark beyond 700 prompts, how to better evaluate general motion quality (beyond action recognition), and whether larger video-understanding models can replace the current patchwork of specialized metrics. The limited annotator pool and 2023-era model set also leave room for updated, more diverse evaluations.
