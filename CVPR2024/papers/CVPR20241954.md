## Problem

Score distillation-based text-to-3D generation (e.g., SDS, VSD) suffers from the Janus artifact, where generated objects display multiple front faces across different views. The paper reveals that this stems from a fundamental statistical issue: the KL-divergence objective in existing methods degenerates to independent maximum likelihood estimation per view because the rendered image distribution is a Dirac delta with fixed entropy, making it inherently prone to mode collapse toward the highest-likelihood (canonical) view.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and optimizes a NeRF parameter θ (using a pre-trained latent diffusion model as the 2D prior) to produce a 3D scene representation. During optimization, random camera poses are sampled to render views, which are then noised and scored by both the frozen pre-trained diffusion model and a LoRA-fine-tuned copy. The final output is the optimized NeRF, from which arbitrary multi-view renderings can be produced; evaluation renders N views uniformly distributed over a unit sphere for quantitative metrics.

## Method

Entropic Score Distillation (ESD) re-introduces an entropy term on the marginal rendered-image distribution (averaged over all camera poses) into the variational objective, which couples all views together and encourages diversity. The resulting gradient (Eq. 8) involves the score of the text-conditioned data distribution minus a weighted score of the rendered distribution that is *not* conditioned on camera pose. The key practical insight (Theorem 1) is that this gradient decomposes into a weighted combination of a camera-conditioned score and an unconditional score, enabling implementation via the Classifier-Free Guidance trick: a single LoRA-adapted diffusion model is trained alternately on conditional (camera-pose-conditioned) and unconditional denoising objectives, and the two scores are mixed with coefficient λ during the 3D update step. This avoids the instability of directly fitting an unconditioned score function while preserving the entropy-regularization effect that distinguishes ESD from VSD.

## Results

Across 120 text prompts, ESD achieves the best CLIP distance (0.714 vs. 0.725 for VSD and 0.737 for SDS), the lowest FID (235.9 vs. 265.1 and 291.9), and the highest Inception Gain (0.327 vs. 0.137 and 0.123), indicating superior view distinguishability. The human-evaluated Successful Generation Rate (SR) jumps dramatically from 15.0% (SDS) and 19.2% (VSD) to 55.8% for ESD. ESD also achieves the best Inception Variety (IV = 4.03) while maintaining competitive Inception Quality (IQ = 3.14), striking the best quality–diversity balance among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESD outperforms both SDS (DreamFusion) and VSD (ProlificDreamer) on every quantitative metric in Table 1. Compared to VSD, ESD reduces FID by ~28% (235.9 vs. 265.1), more than doubles the Inception Gain (0.327 vs. 0.137), and nearly triples the human-evaluated Successful Generation Rate (55.8% vs. 19.2%), while matching VSD's Inception Quality (3.135 vs. 3.149). Compared to SDS, ESD improves CLIP distance by 3.1% and FID by 19%, and achieves a 3.7× higher SR. Debiased-SDS [12] and Perp-Neg [2] are included only in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 4); the paper reports they are effective for some prompts but less consistent than ESD, which alleviates Janus artifacts more uniformly across the 120-prompt suite.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the score distillation lineage from DreamFusion (SDS) and ProlificDreamer (VSD), which use pre-trained 2D diffusion models to guide 3D NeRF optimization. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical reframing: the authors show that both SDS and VSD degenerate to per-view maximum likelihood estimation because the rendered image distribution is a Dirac delta, making the entropy term in the KL objective constant. This reinterprets the well-known Janus artifact as a mode collapse problem rather than merely a data-bias issue. The proposed fix (ESD) re-introduces entropy by marginalizing over camera poses, and the authors' surprising result is that this is implementable via the Classifier-Free Guidance trick applied to VSD's fine-tuned score model — repurposing a text-to-image technique as an entropy regularizer in 3D generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled statistical explanation for the Janus artifact, moving the field beyond empirical prompt-engineering workarounds (Debiased-SDS, Perp-Neg) toward a theoretically grounded fix. The practical takeaway — that adding CFG to VSD's LoRA-tuned score model mitigates mode collapse — is simple enough to adopt immediately in existing pipelines. However, the evaluation is confined to NeRF representations within the threeestudio framework, and the λ hyperparameter (balancing quality vs. diversity) requires per-prompt tuning. Open questions include whether the entropy-regularized objective transfers to other 3D representations (meshes, 3D Gaussians) and whether it composes cleanly with emerging multi-view diffusion priors that may already address view consistency differently.
