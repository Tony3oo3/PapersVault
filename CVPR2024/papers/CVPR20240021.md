## Problem

Standard adversarial training focuses exclusively on worst-case adversarial examples, causing previously defended or correctly classified samples to become vulnerable in subsequent epochs — a phenomenon the authors term 'hiders.' These hidden high-risk regions within the secure area prevent the model from identifying the true worst cases, leading to compromised robustness and accuracy that no existing adversarial training method addresses.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN) with an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of 8/255, producing standard class labels. Beyond the primary model, HFAT consumes an auxiliary model whose weights are derived from the current model via a single reverse-gradient step, and it samples hider positions from a fitted Gaussian distribution over the relative distance ratio between hiders and adversarial examples in the gradient direction.

## Method

HFAT redefines the adversarial training min-max objective to jointly minimize loss over both worst-case adversarial examples and worst-case hiders (samples inside the current decision boundary that will fail in future epochs). An Iterative Evolution Optimization Strategy (Theorem 1) reduces the intractable future-epoch maximization to considering only the next epoch's worst-case hider. To obtain a usable gradient for this hider term, HFAT constructs an auxiliary model by reverse-training the current model on samples drawn from an empirically fitted Gaussian distribution of hider relative positions, then performs adversarial training on the auxiliary model to extract a momentum direction that exposes hidden threat regions. An adaptive weighting mechanism based on KL divergence between natural and adversarial outputs dynamically balances the standard AT branch and the auxiliary-model branch, shifting emphasis toward hider defense as training progresses.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PreAct ResNet-18, HFAT applied to AT_PGD improves PGD-20 robust accuracy from 52.64% to 56.39% (+3.75 pp) and AutoAttack accuracy from 48.25% to 50.61% (+2.36 pp), while also slightly boosting natural accuracy (81.66% → 81.88%). With WideResNet-34-10, the gains are larger: PGD-20 rises from 55.60% to 60.06% (+4.46 pp) and AutoAttack from 52.06% to 55.58% (+3.52 pp). HFAT consistently improves all five baselines (AT_PGD, TRADES, MART, AWP, HELP) across both architectures and all attack methods tested (FGSM, PGD-20/100, C&W, MIM, AA_rand, AA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HFAT is applied as a plug-in to five existing adversarial training baselines and consistently improves robust accuracy across all attack methods. The largest absolute gains appear when HFAT is added to AT_PGD: +3.75 pp on PGD-20 and +2.36 pp on AutoAttack (PreAct R18), and +4.46 pp on PGD-20 and +3.52 pp on AutoAttack (WRN-34-10). For TRADES, HFAT improves PGD-20 by +1.70 pp (PreAct R18) and +1.81 pp (WRN-34-10), though natural accuracy drops slightly (81.24→80.39 on PreAct R18). MART, AWP, and HELP all see uniform improvements of roughly 1–3 pp in robust accuracy with HFAT, with no degradation in natural accuracy. In the ablation (Table 3), directly adding hider samples as data augmentation (AT_Hiders) actually hurts robust accuracy (48.90% vs. 52.64% for AT_PGD), while Mixup yields only marginal gains (52.92%), confirming that the auxiliary-model-based optimization direction is essential rather than simple data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard min-max adversarial training framework (AT_PGD, TRADES, MART, AWP, HELP) by identifying a previously overlooked failure mode: samples that are defended in one epoch but fail in subsequent epochs, which the authors term 'hiders.' Rather than proposing a new attack or a fundamentally different training paradigm, HFAT redefines the optimization objective to jointly defend against worst-case adversarial examples and these hidden high-risk regions, using an auxiliary model trained via reverse gradient steps to expose hider locations. The iterative evolution optimization strategy and the KL-divergence-based adaptive weighting mechanism are new technical contributions that couple the two optimization branches, but the overall architecture and training pipeline remain within the established adversarial training family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hider concept provides a concrete diagnostic for why adversarial training can degrade natural accuracy and fail to find genuine worst cases, offering practitioners a new lens for understanding training dynamics. The plug-and-play nature of HFAT (applicable to five existing baselines) makes it practically useful, though the ~3-4 point robust accuracy gains are moderate and the auxiliary model adds ~50% training time. Open questions include whether the Gaussian distribution assumption for hider relative positions holds at scale (ImageNet, deeper networks), whether the iterative evolution strategy generalizes beyond the next-epoch simplification, and whether self-supervised hider detection (suggested in the paper's future work) could eliminate the need for the auxiliary model entirely.
