## Problem

In fashion-domain vision-language pretraining, datasets like FashionGen pair multiple images of a single item with one text description, so individual image-text pairs contain information that does not co-occur across modalities. Random masking in MLM and MIM can therefore mask elements irrelevant to the paired modality, undermining cross-modal alignment. Additionally, standard grouped batch sampling (GRIT) introduces false negatives when multiple images share the same caption, degrading ITC and ITM training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256 RGB fashion item images (encoded by ViT-B16 into 256 patches) and tokenized text descriptions (encoded by the first 6 blocks of BERT-base). Pretraining produces joint visual-textual representations via a multi-modal encoder (last 6 BERT blocks augmented with cross-attention layers). Downstream, these representations support cross-modal retrieval (R@1 on FashionGen), text-guided image retrieval (R@10/R@50 on FashionIQ triplets of query image + modified description → target image), and category/subcategory classification via a linear probe on the [CLS] token.

## Method

SyncMask replaces random masking in MLM and MIM with attention-guided masking derived from the cross-attention maps of a momentum (teacher) model's multi-modal encoder. Specifically, it computes two synchronized attention weight vectors—one mapping text tokens to image patches (Eq. 2) and one mapping image patches to text tokens (Eq. 3)—then selects the top-K indices above a threshold L for masking, ensuring only co-occurring visual-textual elements are masked. The momentum model's output features also serve as soft distillation targets for the masked regions, providing richer supervision than discrete labels. For batch sampling, the method modifies GRIT's grouping phase to select the s-th highest (semi-hard) pairwise similarity rather than the hardest, and explicitly excludes true positives by checking item indices, thereby reducing false negatives inherent to fashion datasets with shared captions.

## Results

On FashionGen cross-modal retrieval (Table 2), SyncMask achieves 75.00 R@1 for image-to-text and 71.00 R@1 for text-to-image on the 1K subset, yielding a mean of 73.00, surpassing the previous best FashionSAP (71.63 mean) by 1.37 points. On the full FashionGen set, it reaches 59.73 mean R@1 vs. FashionSAP's 58.63. On FashionIQ text-guided image retrieval, SyncMask attains 38.13 mean R@10 and 65.14 mean R@50, outperforming FashionSAP (36.26 / 64.23) and FashionVLP (34.27 / 62.51). For category and subcategory recognition on FashionGen, it achieves 90.31 and 87.83 Macro-F1 respectively, marginally exceeding FashionSAP (89.84 / 87.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncMask outperforms all 17 baselines across the three downstream tasks. On FashionGen cross-modal retrieval, it beats the strongest prior fashion-specific method FashionSAP by +1.86 R@1 (I2T) and +0.88 R@1 (T2I) on the subset, and by +0.96 / +1.24 on the full set; the gap over FashionViL is larger (+9.46 / +9.12 subset mean). On FashionIQ text-guided image retrieval, SyncMask leads FashionSAP by +1.87 mean R@10 and +0.91 mean R@50, with the largest margin on the Toptee category (+2.91 R@10 over FashionSAP). For category/subcategory recognition, SyncMask edges out FashionSAP by +0.47 Macro-F1 on CR and +0.16 on SCR, while FashionSAP retains a slight +0.12 advantage on SCR accuracy. Compared to general-domain VLMs (ALBEF, EI-CLIP), SyncMask gains +10.80 mean R@1 on the FashionGen subset, confirming the benefit of domain-specific masking and sampling strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ALBEF momentum-distillation framework by repurposing the teacher model's cross-attention maps—previously used only for pseudo-label generation—to produce synchronized masks for MLM and MIM, targeting co-occurring image-text segments rather than random positions. It also adapts GRIT-VLP's hardest-negative grouped batch sampling to a semi-hard variant with false-negative exclusion, specifically to handle the multi-image-per-caption structure prevalent in fashion datasets. Within the fashion VLP lineage (FashionBERT → Kaleido-BERT → FashionViL → FashionSAP), SyncMask is positioned as a refinement of attention-guided masking that avoids the fixed mask-pair overfitting of Kaleido-BERT's alignment generator by updating masks end-to-end during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete data-structure mismatch in fashion VLP—multiple images sharing one caption—that causes random masking and hardest-negative grouping to degrade training, and offers targeted fixes. However, the improvements over the strongest baseline (FashionSAP) are modest (≈1–2 points on retrieval, near-parity on classification), and all experiments are confined to the fashion domain with a single architecture (ALBEF-based ViT-B16 + BERT-base). Open questions include whether SyncMask's cross-attention-guided masking generalizes to other domains with similar multi-view-per-caption structures, whether the momentum model's attention is the optimal mask signal versus learned or contrastive alternatives, and whether the semi-hard grouping strategy transfers to other data-scarce, high-redundancy settings.
