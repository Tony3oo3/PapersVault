## Problem

Inverting DDPM for real-image editing is challenging because the native noise space (i.i.d. standard-normal maps) is not edit-friendly: fixing those noise maps and changing the text prompt destroys image structure. Most existing editing pipelines sidestep this by relying on approximate DDIM inversion, which is deterministic, requires many timesteps for accuracy, and cannot naturally produce diverse outputs. The paper asks: what consistent DDPM inversion actually preserves structure under condition changes?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242574/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image (512×512×3, encoded to a 64×64×4 latent via a pretrained autoencoder in Stable Diffusion), a source text prompt describing the image, and a target text prompt. It outputs a sequence of T+1 edit-friendly noise maps {x_T, z_T, …, z_1} that perfectly reconstruct the input under the DDPM reverse process, and when replayed with the target prompt (optionally skipping the first T_skip steps) produce a structure-preserving edited image. Because the auxiliary noise in the inversion is stochastic, multiple distinct inversions of the same image are possible, enabling diverse edited outputs for a single prompt change.

## Method

The key insight is to construct the auxiliary noisy sequence x_t directly from x_0 using *independent* Gaussian noise (x_t = √ᾱ_t·x_0 + √(1−ᾱ_t)·ε̃_t) rather than the correlated noise inherent in the forward diffusion process. This makes consecutive x_t values farther apart, so the z_t extracted from the DDPM recursion (z_t = (x_{t−1} − μ̂_t(x_t))/σ_t) carry higher per-pixel variance and exhibit negative correlations across consecutive timesteps. These properties cause the image structure to be more strongly imprinted into the noise maps, so fixing them while swapping the text condition preserves layout and texture. The inversion requires no optimization—just one forward pass through the denoiser per timestep—and is extremely fast. For text-guided editing, the extracted noise maps are held fixed while the denoiser is conditioned on the target prompt, with a T_skip parameter controlling how many initial high-noise steps are re-sampled to trade off fidelity versus prompt adherence.

## Results

On the modified Zero-Shot I2IT dataset (60 image-text pairs, 50 diffusion steps), the proposed DDPM inversion integrated with Zero-Shot I2I achieves an LPIPS of 0.27 versus 0.35 for the original Zero-Shot method, while maintaining the same CLIP score of 0.88, indicating better structural fidelity without sacrificing text compliance. On the modified ImageNet-R-TI2I dataset (212 image-text pairs, 100 steps), the method achieves a favorable LPIPS–CLIP trade-off compared to all baselines, with the best balance at guidance strength 15 and T_skip=36. Editing time is approximately 10–15 seconds per image, substantially faster than PnP and null-text inversion which require over 2.5 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CycleDiffusion [30], the closest DDPM-inversion baseline, the proposed method clearly wins on both metrics: CycleDiffusion's noise maps follow the native DDPM distribution and fail to preserve structure when the text prompt changes, while the edit-friendly inversion maintains structure with higher-variance, negatively-correlated noise vectors. Against PnP [27] and null-text inversion [19], the proposed method achieves comparable or better LPIPS (structure preservation) while being dramatically faster (~10 s vs. >2.5 min per image). Against EDICT [29], the proposed method avoids the artifacts visible in EDICT's outputs and requires only a single forward pass rather than coupled alternating inversions. Against plain DDIM inversion and DDIM mid-way, the proposed method achieves lower LPIPS at comparable or higher CLIP scores across all T_skip settings tested. When integrated into Prompt-to-Prompt [9], the proposed inversion improves both LPIPS and CLIP over P2P with standard DDIM inversion, and when integrated into Zero-Shot I2I [21], it reduces LPIPS from 0.35 to 0.27 at identical CLIP accuracy (0.88), recovering fine textures like fur and flowers that the original method loses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and departs from the dominant DDIM inversion paradigm used by most diffusion-based editing methods (PnP, EDICT, null-text inversion, Prompt-to-Prompt, Zero-Shot I2I). Its closest conceptual predecessor is CycleDiffusion [30], which also performs DDPM inversion but extracts noise maps with the native standard-normal distribution. The key departure is the observation that this native distribution is not edit-friendly, and that constructing auxiliary noisy images directly from x_0 with independent (rather than correlated) noise yields noise maps with higher variance and negative cross-timestep correlations that better encode image structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a fast, optimization-free DDPM inversion that is immediately integrable into existing editing pipelines (demonstrated with Prompt-to-Prompt and Zero-Shot I2I), improving both fidelity and detail preservation without model fine-tuning. Its stochastic nature also enables diverse outputs for the same edit, a capability absent from deterministic DDIM inversion. Limitations include sensitivity to the T_skip and guidance-strength hyperparameters, and the approach is validated primarily on text-guided editing with Stable Diffusion; whether the negative-correlation property generalizes to other modalities or to more complex multi-step editing chains remains open.
