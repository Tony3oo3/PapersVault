## Problem

RGB-IR multimodal object detection on UAVs suffers from weakly misalignment between image pairs, but existing alignment methods face two key limitations: (1) they estimate spatial offsets in the raw feature space where the modality gap between RGB and IR corrupts cross-modal spatial matching, and (2) they require strict alignment with extra supervision (e.g., ground-truth offsets), making them costly and fragile. This paper asks whether adaptive, non-strict feature alignment can achieve superior detection robustness without additional annotation overhead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of weakly misaligned images — a 3-channel RGB image and a 1-channel IR image — both resized to 640×640, with IR designated as the reference modality and RGB as the sensed modality whose features are shifted. It outputs oriented bounding boxes (OBBs) with class labels for five vehicle categories (car, truck, bus, van, freight-car) using a YOLOv5s detection head. Training labels are taken from the IR ground-truth annotations, and the auxiliary semantic detection branch used during training is discarded at inference to maintain 33.1 FPS throughput.

## Method

OAFA comprises two modules. The Cross-modality Spatial Offset Modeling (CSOM) module first decouples each modality's features into invariant and specific components via a shared-parameter invariant encoder (single conv + SiLU) and modality-specific encoders (conv + C3 + bottlenecks), constrained by a central-moment-discrepancy similarity loss, a Frobenius-norm difference loss, and an auxiliary YOLOv5 detection semantic loss. Spatial offsets are then estimated from the invariant features after spatial and channel attention enhancement, operating in a common subspace that suppresses modality-gap-induced noise. The Offset-guided Deformable Alignment and Fusion (ODAF) module uses these offsets as explicit initialization for a deformable convolution that adaptively samples RGB features at learned positions (with a per-kernel modulation scalar), followed by a 1×1 convolution on the concatenated invariant features before final channel concatenation with specific features. Training follows a two-stage strategy: Stage I (50 epochs) trains only the backbone and fusion with detection loss to build rich unimodal representations; Stage II (100 epochs) jointly optimizes the full network with the decoupling losses, avoiding the instability of training deformable alignment from scratch.

## Results

On the DroneVehicle dataset, OAFA achieves 79.4% mAP (IoU=0.4), outperforming the second-best method SLBAF-Net (76.1%) by 3.3 percentage points and the best unimodal detector YOLOv5s-IR (70.7%) by 8.7 points. The method runs at 33.1 FPS on an NVIDIA RTX A6000, enabling real-time detection. OAFA shows particularly strong gains on confusing vehicle categories: 76.8% on truck and 73.3% on freight-car, versus 72.0% and 68.6% for SLBAF-Net respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OAFA outperforms all 14 compared baselines on mAP. Against the strongest unimodal detector (YOLOv5s-IR, 70.7%), OAFA gains 8.7 mAP points, and against the best prior fusion method SLBAF-Net (76.1%), it improves by 3.3 points. The margin is especially large on the truck (76.8 vs. 72.0) and freight-car (73.3 vs. 68.6) categories, which the authors attribute to OAFA's ability to identify optimal spatial correspondences between multimodal features. Among fusion baselines that explicitly address weak misalignment (TSFADet, C²Former, AR-CNN), OAFA leads by 6.3, 5.2, and 7.8 mAP points respectively. In terms of speed, OAFA (33.1 FPS) is slower than SLBAF-Net (63.2 FPS) but faster than UA-CMDet (9.1 FPS), AR-CNN (18.2 FPS), and TSFADet (18.6 FPS), while C²Former does not report a speed figure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the modality-invariant/specific feature decomposition framework from MISA (Hazarika et al., 2020) and adapts it to the RGB-IR detection setting, using central moment discrepancy as the similarity constraint and YOLOv5 detection losses as the semantic constraint. The alignment mechanism draws on deformable convolution (Dai et al., 2017) as used in video super-resolution (BasicVSR++, TDAN), but repurposes it for cross-modality spatial correction rather than temporal alignment. The key conceptual departure from prior weakly-misalignment methods (AR-CNN, TSFADet, C2Former) is the rejection of strict alignment in favor of offset-guided adaptive sampling, eliminating the need for ground-truth offset annotations that those methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical path to multimodal UAV detection without the costly annotation of per-pair spatial offsets, which is a significant barrier for real-world deployment. However, validation is limited to a single dataset (DroneVehicle) and a single task (vehicle detection), leaving open whether the common-subspace offset estimation generalizes to other modality pairs, object categories, or more severe misalignment regimes. The two-stage training strategy, while effective, adds implementation complexity and raises questions about whether a single-stage variant with better initialization could match the performance. Broader applicability to non-vehicle detection or to modalities with larger appearance gaps (e.g., RGB-LiDAR) remains untested.
