## Problem

Existing image editing models lack a dedicated mechanism for human-object interaction, producing low-quality or non-interactive human figures when editing scenes. While skeleton-guided models (e.g., ControlNet) improve human rendering, they require manually authored HOI skeleton maps, making automated HOI image editing infeasible. This paper is the first to tackle HOI image editing by automatically generating object-interactive skeleton guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241975/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 256×256×3 RGB crop aligned to a person bounding box, the person bounding box itself, and one or more object bounding boxes. It outputs a 2D object-interactive skeleton map (joint positions with confidence scores) and a final edited image where the masked person region is filled with a human naturally interacting with the specified object. The skeleton is produced by an iterative diffusion denoising process (10 steps), and the final image is generated by plugging the skeleton into any off-the-shelf skeleton-guided inpainting model (ControlNet-Inpaint in experiments).

## Method

The core contribution is an object interactive diffuser built around a novel Associative Attention (A.A.) module. The A.A. computes an alignment matrix by using image conditioning plus noise pose embedding as query and object conditioning (from ROI-pooled ResNet features) as key, then propagates this per-joint alignment through a GNN that uses the MSCOCO skeleton adjacency matrix to enforce structural consistency among connected joints. This propagated alignment is softmax-normalized and multiplied by the value (object conditioning) to produce denoised joint embeddings, repeated iteratively over 10 denoising steps. A second novelty is the HOI loss, which scales the joint L1 loss by a Joint_param factor computed as the softmax of inverse Euclidean distance from each GT joint to the object bounding-box center, explicitly rewarding joints that are closer to the interaction point. The entire pipeline from feature extraction (pretrained ResNet) through the diffuser is trained end-to-end on V-COCO with λ=10⁴, and the generated skeleton is then fed to a frozen ControlNet-Inpaint model for the final image synthesis.

## Results

On V-COCO, the proposed framework (SD-Inpainting + Ours) achieves FID 24.04, KID 0.0054, and CLIP Score 30.48, outperforming all text-free and text-guided editing baselines. Compared to the same backbone without the proposed skeleton guidance (vanilla SD-Inpainting: FID 28.16, KID 0.0087, CS 29.24), the method improves FID by 4.12, KID by 0.0033, and CS by 1.24. Notably, the method also surpasses the stronger SDXL-Inpainting (FID 25.01, KID 0.0082, CS 29.63) on all three metrics, demonstrating that the generated object-interactive skeleton guidance provides a meaningful advantage over simply using a more powerful inpainting backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 10 baselines spanning text-free and text-guided editing. Among text-free methods, CoModGAN [3] is the strongest (FID 52.30), yet the proposed method still achieves a 28.26 FID improvement. Among text-guided methods, Instruct-Pix2Pix [18] (FID 45.37), MagicBrush [19] (FID 60.01), HIVE [20] (FID 56.38), and GLIDE [25] (FID 63.14) all underperform the proposed method by 20+ FID points, confirming that general-purpose text-guided editing is insufficient for HOI synthesis. The closest competitors are the diffusion-based inpainting models: BLDM [24] (FID 25.52), SDXL-Inpainting [27] (FID 25.01), and vanilla SD-Inpainting [26] (FID 28.16). The proposed method beats all three, with the most notable margin over SDXL-Inpainting (−0.97 FID, −0.0028 KID, +0.85 CS), showing that the generated object-interactive skeleton guidance adds value even when paired with a weaker backbone than SDXL. LaMA [1] and MAT [2], the weakest text-free baselines, are outperformed by a wide margin (FID 59.30 and 77.55 respectively), underscoring the difficulty of HOI editing without explicit interaction guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of skeleton-guided image generation (ControlNet, HumanSD, T2I-Adapter) and human-object interaction research (PaStaNet, GPNN, Humanise), which had previously been treated as separate domains. It repurposes the diffusion denoising paradigm—typically applied to image synthesis—for the novel sub-task of generating object-interactive skeleton maps, introducing an associative attention (A.A.) module that fuses object conditioning (as key/value) with a GNN-based joint propagation mechanism to capture both object-joint and joint-joint relationships. The HOI loss with the J_param scaling parameter is a targeted departure from standard L1 joint regression, explicitly weighting joints by their proximity to the object center. The second stage deliberately reuses off-the-shelf inpainting models (ControlNet-Inpaint, SD-Inpainting) rather than training a new image generator, positioning the contribution squarely in the skeleton-generation stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By automating the creation of object-interactive skeletons, this work removes the manual annotation bottleneck that has limited skeleton-guided HOI generation, making the pipeline more accessible and composable with any downstream skeleton-conditioned editor. The extension to human-to-human interaction, manual skeleton rearrangement, and SMPLify-based 3D mesh optimization suggests a broader utility beyond single-object scenes. However, evaluation is confined to V-COCO at 256×256 resolution with a single inpainting backbone, and the baselines (text-free inpainters, basic text-guided editors) are not purpose-built for HOI, making the quantitative margins harder to interpret. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions, handling of occluded or multi-object interactions, and whether the A.A. mechanism generalizes to 3D pose or video settings.
