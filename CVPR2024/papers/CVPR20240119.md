## Problem

Existing camera-based occupancy estimation methods only represent the current 3D scene without forecasting how it will change, while prior occupancy forecasting approaches rely on LiDAR point clouds and semantic/instance prediction is confined to 2D BEV format for predefined object categories. No benchmark exists for camera-only 4D occupancy forecasting that simultaneously predicts future occupancy of both movable and static objects in full 3D space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240119/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N_p=2 past plus the current frame of 6 surround-view camera images and 6-DOF ego-car poses as input, producing a current occupancy grid O_c ∈ R^{1×512×512×40} and N_f=4 future occupancy grids O_f ∈ R^{4×512×512×40} at 0.2 m voxel resolution over a 102.4 m × 102.4 m × 8 m range, each voxel labeled as GMO, GSO, or free. Additionally, OCFNet outputs a 3D backward centripetal flow field M_t ∈ R^{(N_f+1)×3×H×W×L} pointing each occupied voxel at time t toward its corresponding instance center at t−1, enabling downstream instance-level motion prediction.

## Method

OCFNet is an end-to-end spatio-temporal CNN with two main modules. The Multi-Frame Feature Aggregation Module lifts 2D image features into 3D voxel volumes via a 2D-3D lifting module, warps them to the current coordinate frame using 6-DOF poses, collapses the temporal and channel dimensions into a single axis, and concatenates relative inter-frame poses to form a motion-aware feature F_pm. The Future State Prediction Module downsamples F_pm to four scales with a voxel encoder, expands each scale's channel dimension from (N_p+1)c_i to (N_f+1)c_i via stacked 3D residual convolutions, fuses with upsampled decoder features, and applies a softmax occupancy head and a 1×1-conv flow head to produce per-timestep occupancy and flow predictions. Training uses a multi-task loss combining cross-entropy occupancy loss, smooth-L1 flow loss, and an explicit depth loss (applied only at t=0 to save memory), and the ablation shows the flow head improves occupancy IoU by ~4% by providing an auxiliary motion signal that guides the network's temporal reasoning.

## Results

On the primary task of forecasting inflated GMO, OCFNet† (trained on all sequences) achieves IoU_c of 31.30 and IoU_f̃ of 27.98 on nuScenes, and IoU_c of 36.41 and IoU_f̃ of 34.60 on Lyft-Level5, outperforming the strongest baseline PowerBEV-3D by 13.3% and 21.8% in IoU_f̃ respectively. Even OCFNet trained on only 1/6 of the training data surpasses PowerBEV-3D by 12.4% in IoU_f̃ on nuScenes. The static-world baseline OpenOccupancy-C achieves only 11.74 IoU_f̃ on nuScenes, while the point-cloud-prediction pipeline (SPC) fails entirely on future forecasting. On the fine-grained GMO task (Table 2), OCFNet† reaches 10.10 IoU_f̃, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCFNet decisively outperforms all four baseline types across both datasets. Against the static-world baseline OpenOccupancy-C [45], OCFNet† gains roughly 16–21 points in IoU_f̃, confirming that assuming a static environment is a poor proxy for future occupancy. The point-cloud-prediction pipeline (SurroundDepth [46] + PCPNet [33] + Cylinder3D [52]) is the weakest baseline, achieving only 1.27 IoU_c on nuScenes and failing entirely on future steps due to shape inconsistency in sparse point-level predictions. The 2D-3D instance-based baseline PowerBEV-3D [25] is the strongest competitor, reaching 21.86 IoU_f̃ on nuScenes and 25.06 on Lyft-Level5, but OCFNet† surpasses it by 13.3% and 21.8% respectively, demonstrating the advantage of native 3D end-to-end forecasting over lifting 2D BEV results. On the fine-grained GMO task (Table 2), the gap narrows somewhat (OCFNet†: 10.10 vs. OpenOccupancy-C: 8.53 IoU_f̃), yet OCFNet† still leads, while SPC drops to 1.12 and PowerBEV-3D to 5.49. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that adding the 3D flow prediction head improves OCFNet by ~4% in IoU_f̃, validating the multi-task learning design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem space at the intersection of three adjacent research threads: camera-based 3D occupancy estimation (OpenOccupancy, SurroundOcc, TPVFormer), LiDAR-based occupancy forecasting (Khurana et al.), and 2D BEV instance prediction (FIERY, PowerBEV, BEVerse). None of these threads alone addresses camera-only spatiotemporal occupancy prediction in full 3D space. The OCFNet architecture repurposes standard components—multi-frame feature aggregation, voxel encoder-decoder stacks, and flow prediction heads—into a unified end-to-end pipeline, while the benchmark itself reorganizes existing datasets (nuScenes, nuScenes-Occupancy, Lyft-Level5) into a new format with 3D backward centripetal flow annotations inspired by 2D flow work in PowerBEV.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first standardized benchmark for camera-only 4D occupancy forecasting, Cam4DOcc provides the field with a common evaluation protocol (four-level tasks, IoU metrics with temporal weighting) and four diverse baselines that establish initial performance ceilings. The practical finding that inflated GMO labels substantially outperform fine-grained voxel-level annotations for forecasting suggests that current camera-only methods cannot yet resolve fine 3D structure over time, pointing to a clear gap for future work. The task remains very challenging—best IoU values are in the 25–35% range—and the authors acknowledge that longer time horizons with many moving objects are particularly difficult, leaving substantial room for methodological advances.
