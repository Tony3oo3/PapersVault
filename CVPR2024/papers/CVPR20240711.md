## Problem

Existing federated domain generalization methods impose two impractical restrictions: a strict one-to-one mapping between clients and source domains (K=M), and the availability of domain labels for every local sample. In real-world deployments with numerous edge devices, multiple clients may share the same source domain, and annotating domain membership is both costly and a privacy risk, making these assumptions untenable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240711/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed through CLIP's frozen visual encoder, e.g., ResNet50 or ViT-B/32) as input and outputs a class label prediction. Crucially, no domain label is provided at either training or test time; the system must infer the latent domain of each sample. The number of federated clients K can significantly exceed the number of source domains M (e.g., K=20, M=3), breaking the conventional one-to-one client-domain correspondence.

## Method

DiPrompT introduces three disentangled prompt types within CLIP's text encoder: a single Global Prompt (G-Prompt) optimized across all clients for domain-invariant knowledge, a pool of Domain Prompts (D-Prompts) with one prototypical prompt per source domain trained with a contrastive loss to preserve domain-specific discriminative features, and a learnable Query Prompt (Q-Prompt) that performs a two-substep image-text alignment to automatically assign each sample to its latent domain without explicit labels. During federated aggregation, D-Prompts are combined via domain-wise weighted averaging (only from clients holding that domain's data) and stabilized with beta momentum averaging to prevent client drift. At inference, a collaborative ensemble dynamically weights the G-Prompt and each D-Prompt based on the target sample's feature distance to each domain, providing complementary generic and specific knowledge for improved generalization to unseen target domains.

## Results

On PACS, DiPrompT achieves 93.88% average accuracy across four leave-one-domain-out settings, outperforming the best FL+DG method FedGMA (82.21%) by 11.67 points and the best prompt-based FL method FedCLIP (92.37%) by 1.51 points. On OfficeHome, DiPrompT reaches 76.18% average accuracy, surpassing FedCLIP (73.76%) and PromptFL (73.99%). On VLCS, DiPrompT attains 83.70% average accuracy, exceeding FedCLIP (81.73%) and the best centralized method VAUE (79.40%). Notably, DiPrompT achieves these results without domain labels and with K=20 clients (K >> M=3 source domains), a setting where all prior FL+DG methods degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiPrompT decisively outperforms all FL+DG baselines: on PACS it exceeds FedGMA (82.21%) by 11.67 points, FedSR (81.19%) by 12.69 points, and FedADG (77.50%) by 16.38 points, indicating that existing federated domain generalization methods struggle severely when K >> M. Against the closest prompt-based competitors sharing the same CLIP backbone, DiPrompT beats FedCLIP by 1.51% on PACS and 2.42% on OfficeHome, and beats PromptFL by 1.75% on PACS and 2.19% on OfficeHome, with the gap widening on VLCS (83.70% vs. 81.73% for FedCLIP, 80.53% for PromptFL). Remarkably, DiPrompT also surpasses centralized domain generalization methods that have access to domain labels and all source data in a single pool: it exceeds PCL (88.70% PACS avg) by 5.18 points and SWAD (88.79%) by 5.09 points, and on VLCS it beats VAUE (79.40%) by 4.30 points. The paper notes that FL+DG methods (FedADG, FedSR) actually perform worse than plain FedAvg/FedProx in the K >> M setting, suggesting their domain-invariant feature extraction becomes counterproductive when multiple clients share a domain. DiPrompT wins in 11 out of 12 individual target-domain settings across all three datasets, with the sole exception being a marginal case on OfficeHome Clipart where it still leads by 2.02 points over FedCLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiPrompT extends the CoOp-style learnable prompt tuning paradigm (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022) from centralized few-shot recognition into the federated domain generalization setting, where prior FL+DG methods (FedSR, FedGMA, FedADG) assumed a strict one-to-one mapping between clients and source domains and required explicit domain labels. The disentangled design—separating a single global prompt for domain-invariant knowledge from per-domain prototypical prompts for domain-specific knowledge—draws on prototype learning and the beta momentum averaging mechanism from CLIPood to mitigate client drift. The dynamic Q-Prompt query mechanism, which uses image-text alignment to infer latent domain assignments without annotation, departs from prior label-free DG approaches that rely on style-based clustering or pseudo-labeling and cannot exploit textual domain descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the impractical K=M constraint and the domain-label requirement, DiPrompT addresses a more realistic deployment scenario where edge devices far outnumber source domains and cross-client annotation is infeasible or privacy-risky. The 830× communication reduction over FedADG makes the approach practical for bandwidth-constrained federated settings. However, the method still requires knowing the set of domain names (for text prompts like 'a photo of a [class] with the domain of <domain>'), which limits its applicability to truly unknown domain taxonomies. Open questions include scalability beyond four domains, robustness when domain names are partially incorrect, and whether the disentangled prompt decomposition generalizes to non-CLIP backbones or non-classification tasks.
