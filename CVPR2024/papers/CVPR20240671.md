## Problem

Existing image-to-3D methods face a fundamental trade-off: per-shape optimization approaches (e.g., DreamFusion, Magic3D) achieve reasonable quality but require tens of minutes to hours, while feed-forward pipelines like One-2-3-45 run in seconds but produce inconsistent multi-view predictions that degrade 3D reconstruction fidelity. No prior method simultaneously delivers sub-minute generation speed and high geometric and textural fidelity to the input image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240671/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of an arbitrary object (or a text prompt for the text-to-3D variant) and produces a textured 3D mesh in approximately 60 seconds on a single A100 GPU. Internally, the pipeline first generates six consistent views tiled into a 3×2 composite image, then lifts them into a 64³ occupancy volume and a 128³ sparse SDF-plus-color volume, from which a mesh is extracted via Marching Cubes. A final TensoRF-based texture refinement pass bakes high-resolution color onto the fixed geometry using the multi-view images as photometric supervision.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage feed-forward pipeline. First, a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion model generates six consistent views jointly by tiling them into a single 3×2 composite image, using reference attention for local conditioning, CLIP image embeddings for global conditioning, and fixed absolute elevations (±30°, −20°) with relative azimuths to avoid pose ambiguity. Second, a two-stage 3D diffusion model (following LAS-Diffusion) denoises a 64³ occupancy volume and then a 128³ sparse SDF+color volume, conditioned on a 3D feature volume constructed by projecting DINOv2 2D patch features from all six views into 3D space via known camera poses and concatenating them into the UNet. Third, a lightweight TensoRF optimization refines texture quality by rasterizing the fixed mesh and minimizing L2 loss against the predicted multi-view images. The 3D diffusion networks are trained on Objaverse with ground-truth renderings as multi-view conditions, with random projection perturbations to build robustness against imperfect predicted views.

## Results

On the GSO dataset (1,030 objects), One-2-3-45++ achieves an F-Score of 93.6% and CLIP similarity of 81.0, outperforming all baselines in both geometric and perceptual fidelity. In user studies (2,385 pairs, 53 participants), the method earns an 87.6% preference rate, substantially ahead of the next-best method (Zero123 XL at 58.6%). For text-to-3D on 50 DreamFusion prompts, One-2-3-45++ reaches CLIP similarity 26.8 and 84.1% user preference in 60 seconds, compared to 2 hours for MVDream and 10+ hours for ProlificDreamer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

One-2-3-45++ wins on every metric against all six image-to-3D baselines. Against the closest feed-forward competitor Shap-E (F-Score 91.8, CLIP-Sim 73.1, 40.8% user preference), it gains +1.8 F-Score, +7.9 CLIP-Sim, and +46.8 percentage points in user preference at the cost of 33 s extra runtime (60 s vs 27 s). Versus the direct predecessor One-2-3-45, the improvement is +3.2 F-Score, +10.2 CLIP-Sim, and +34.9 pp user preference, with only a 15 s increase in inference time. Optimization-based methods (Zero123 XL, SyncDreamer, DreamGaussian) are dominated on both quality and speed: One-2-3-45++ is 30× faster than Zero123 XL (60 s vs 30 min) while scoring higher on all metrics. In the text-to-3D setting, One-2-3-45++ surpasses MVDream by +2.0 CLIP-Sim and +17.9 pp user preference while being 120× faster (60 s vs 2 h), and outperforms ProlificDreamer on both CLIP-Sim and user preference with a >600× speedup. For multi-view generation specifically, the method achieves PSNR 22.12 and Mask IoU 0.878, beating Zero123 (20.32 / 0.856), SyncDreamer (21.67 / 0.894 at a single elevation), and Wonder3D (18.67 / 0.635).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends One-2-3-45 by replacing its two key components: (1) Zero123's independent per-view prediction is swapped for a tiled multi-view generation strategy where six views are stitched into a single image and generated jointly by a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion model, and (2) the generalizable NeRF (SparseNeuS) reconstruction is replaced by a multi-view-conditioned 3D native diffusion model following the coarse-to-fine architecture of LAS-Diffusion. The 3D diffusion is guided by DINOv2 patch features projected into a 3D volume via known camera poses, a design that leverages local 2D priors to compensate for limited 3D training data. The texture refinement stage borrows TensoRF for a lightweight optimization pass supervised by the consistent multi-view images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: generating a high-fidelity textured mesh in under 60 seconds on a single A100 GPU makes single-image-to-3D viable for interactive applications, a regime previously only accessible to optimization-based methods requiring hours. The tiling trick for enforcing multi-view consistency is simple and potentially transferable to other view-synthesis tasks. Open questions remain around geometry robustness for complex shapes, the reliance on Objaverse-scale 3D data for the diffusion module, and whether the approach generalizes well to in-the-wild photos with challenging lighting or backgrounds. The authors note that incorporating additional 2D diffusion priors beyond RGB could further improve geometric detail.
