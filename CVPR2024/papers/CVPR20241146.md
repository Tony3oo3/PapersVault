## Problem

Existing unsupervised semantic segmentation methods such as STEGO and Hidden Positives operate exclusively in pixel space, ignoring the 3D spatial layout of the scene. Since humans perceive the world in 3D and supervised segmentation benefits from spatial information, the authors ask whether incorporating depth-derived structural knowledge into the contrastive training process can improve unsupervised segmentation without requiring depth at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes only an RGB image and produces a pixel-level feature map that is clustered via k-means and refined with a CRF to yield a segmentation mask (or linearly probed for class labels). During training, depth maps are generated on-the-fly by a zero-shot monocular depth estimator (ZoeDepth) and used solely to guide the loss and sampling — they are never fed into the network as an input channel, so the model has no depth dependency at test time. The ViT backbone uses 8×8 patches, and depth maps are average-pooled to match the feature-map resolution for non-overlapping patch-level sampling.

## Method

Building on STEGO's contrastive feature distillation with a DINO-pretrained ViT, the paper introduces two depth-informed mechanisms. First, a Depth-Feature Correlation loss constructs a depth correspondence tensor from two image crops and penalizes the segmentation-head output for failing to align feature-space distances with depth-space distances, effectively pulling apart features that are far apart in 3D and pulling together those on the same depth plateau. Second, Farthest-Point Sampling (FPS) is applied to a point cloud derived from the depth map to select N² feature locations that evenly cover the 3D scene, replacing STEGO's random sampling and increasing diversity at depth discontinuities. A guidance schedule exponentially decays the depth loss weight so the model first learns rough spatial structure before the feature-distillation loss takes over. An optional 3D-Local Hidden Positives variant propagates the learning signal to the nearest patches in 3D space (rather than 2D attention neighborhoods), further sharpening segmentation boundaries.

## Results

On COCO-Stuff 27 with ViT-B/8, DepthG achieves 29.0% unsupervised mIoU and 58.6% unsupervised accuracy, outperforming STEGO (28.2% mIoU, 56.9% acc) by +0.8% mIoU. On Cityscapes, the method reaches 23.1% unsupervised mIoU and 81.6% accuracy, surpassing STEGO by +2.1% mIoU and +8.4% accuracy, and also beating STEGO+Hidden Positives (18.4% mIoU) by a substantial +4.7% mIoU margin. On Potsdam-3 (aerial imagery), DepthG improves unsupervised accuracy from 77.0% (STEGO) to 80.4%, despite the out-of-distribution viewing angle for the monocular depth estimator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STEGO (the direct baseline), DepthG consistently improves unsupervised mIoU: +1.1% on COCO-Stuff 27 (ViT-S/8), +0.8% on COCO-Stuff 27 (ViT-B/8), +2.1% on Cityscapes, and +3.4% accuracy on Potsdam-3. Against STEGO+Hidden Positives (Seong et al.), the comparison is more nuanced: on COCO-Stuff ViT-S/8, Ours w/ 3D-LHP wins mIoU by +1.7% (26.7 vs 24.6) but trails on accuracy (55.1 vs 57.2); on Cityscapes, DepthG dominates with +2.1% accuracy and +4.7% mIoU over HP, which actually degraded mIoU relative to plain STEGO. On Potsdam-3, DepthG (80.4% acc) outperforms STEGO (77.0%) by +3.4%, though HP with a larger ViT-B/8 backbone reaches 82.4%. Against older methods (IIC, PiCIE, DINO alone), DepthG leads by large margins (e.g., +15.1% mIoU over PiCIE on COCO-Stuff ViT-S/8). The method also slightly improves linear probing mIoU over STEGO on ViT-B/8 (41.6 vs 41.0), suggesting the depth-guided training yields more class-discriminative features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends STEGO (Hamilton et al., 2021), which distills DINO-pretrained ViT feature correspondences via contrastive learning for unsupervised segmentation, by injecting 3D spatial structure through two mechanisms: a Depth-Feature Correlation loss that aligns latent feature distances with metric depth distances, and Farthest-Point Sampling on the depth map to replace STEGO's random feature selection. It also builds on Hidden Positives (Seong et al., 2023), which improved STEGO's sampling by discovering hidden positives, but departs from it by proposing a depth-informed local variant (3D-LHP) that propagates learning signals along 3D proximity rather than attention similarity. The key conceptual departure from all prior unsupervised segmentation work is incorporating scene geometry without encoding depth as a network input, thereby avoiding input dependency at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the clean separation of depth as a training-time signal only: the model requires no depth sensor or depth input at test time, making it deployable on standard RGB pipelines while benefiting from spatial structure during learning. The use of off-the-shelf zero-shot monocular depth estimators (ZoeDepth) keeps the pipeline simple and avoids domain-specific retraining. Limitations are acknowledged for domains where monocular depth estimation is unreliable (medical CT slices, aerial imagery), and the authors note that the Potsdam-3 results, while positive, likely underperform due to out-of-distribution depth predictions. Open questions include whether the depth-correlation principle generalizes to other contrastive learning settings beyond segmentation, and what alternative spatial priors could substitute for depth in domains where it is not a meaningful signal.
