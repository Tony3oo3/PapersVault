## Problem

In open-world visual recognition, an embedding model trained on closed-set classes is tested on entirely unseen classes whose intra-class and inter-class distance structures differ substantially from training. Existing posthoc threshold calibration methods (Platt scaling, isotonic regression, histogram binning, etc.) are inductive and assume the calibration data shares a similar distance distribution with the test data—an assumption that breaks down when test classes are disjoint and their embeddings are less compact. The paper formally defines this gap as the open-world threshold calibration problem and shows that inductive methods fail under distribution shift.

## Input / Output

OpenGCN consumes 128-dimensional L2-normalized DML embeddings (from a ResNet-50 trained with Smooth-AP) of unlabeled test samples, which are organized into fully connected sub-graphs of batch size 256 for efficient inference. The GAT encoder produces node features that are concatenated with the original embeddings and passed through a 2-layer MLP to yield per-pair connectivity probabilities p_ij and per-node density estimates s^avg and s^nbr. These predictions are then used to compute TPR(d) and TNR(d) curves over the full distance range [0, 2], from which the optimal threshold d^opt is extracted via grid search (step 0.01) to meet a user-specified TPR or TNR target.

## Method

OpenGCN is a Graph Attention Network that performs transductive threshold calibration by directly modeling pairwise connectivity over the unlabeled test set, eliminating the need for calibration data to match the test distribution. The architecture jointly predicts three quantities via multi-task learning: pairwise edge connectivity (balanced cross-entropy loss) and two instance-wise representation densities—average density s^avg and neighborhood density s^nbr (MSE losses)—where Theorems 1 and 2 formally link these densities to class-specific TPR and TNR, justifying their inclusion as auxiliary tasks that capture intra-class compactness and inter-class separation simultaneously. Training follows a two-stage protocol: pretraining on the large closed-world dataset D_train (same data used for the DML model), followed by resetting the 2-layer MLP head and fine-tuning it on a small open-world calibration set D_cal with classes disjoint from both D_train and D_test, adapting the model to open-world embedding geometry without overfitting. At test time, the trained model transductively estimates TPR and TNR as functions of distance threshold for the specific test distribution, and the connectivity threshold τ is fixed via 10-fold cross-validation on D_cal.

## Results

On the SameDist scenario, OpenGCN achieves MAE_comb of 5.25e-3 (Cars), 6.50e-3 (CUB), and 4.82e-3 (iNaturalist), reducing global error by 59.30%, 66.49%, and 59.15% respectively over the best baseline (Hi-LANDER), for an average improvement of 69.14%. Under ShiftDist (13 corruption types on Cars), OpenGCN maintains a 55.03% average error reduction over the best baseline. In the most challenging DiffDist scenario (cross-dataset, sketch, long-tail), OpenGCN still achieves 31.40%, 87.65%, and 12.92% error reductions, with the cross-dataset setting (iNaturalist→CUB) showing the largest gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenGCN outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and every evaluation scenario (SameDist, ShiftDist, DiffDist). Among traditional posthoc calibration methods, Isotonic regression and Histogram calibration are the strongest (nearly identical numbers), but OpenGCN still reduces their MAE_comb by roughly 60% in SameDist. Hi-LANDER, the GNN-based clustering baseline, is the closest competitor, achieving rank 2 in SameDist, yet OpenGCN still halves its error (e.g., 5.25e-3 vs. 1.29e-2 on Cars). DBSCAN is consistently the worst method, with errors 2–10× higher than OpenGCN. In the DiffDist cross-dataset setting (iNaturalist→CUB), OpenGCN achieves 1.42e-2 MAE_comb versus 1.15e-1 for all four parametric calibration methods and 1.30e-1 for Hi-LANDER, an 87.65% improvement over the best baseline. The only scenario where OpenGCN's margin is smallest is iNaturalist long-tail (12.92% improvement), where the distribution shift is milder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the inductive posthoc calibration paradigm (Platt scaling, isotonic regression, histogram binning, beta calibration) by reframing distance threshold calibration as a transductive inference problem. It repurposes the GNN-based pairwise connectivity and representation density framework from supervised visual clustering [52, 49] for the calibration task, and borrows the two-stage pretrain-then-fine-tune strategy from domain adaptation literature. The transductive inference perspective draws on prior work in few-shot and open-world recognition [8, 18, 28, 38], but applies it specifically to threshold calibration rather than classification or clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: in open-world recognition systems, a single distance threshold calibrated on training data fails for unseen classes with different representation geometry, and conventional calibration methods break down when test distributions diverge from calibration data. The transductive paradigm eliminates the distribution-matching assumption, which is critical for real-world systems serving diverse user populations. Limitations include higher computational cost, susceptibility to over-parameterization, and the continued need for a small open-world calibration set. Open questions include scalability to very large test sets, whether the approach extends to non-visual modalities, and how to handle the case where no open-world calibration data is available at all.
