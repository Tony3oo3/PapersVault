## Problem

Existing video summarization methods operate on short clips (1–11 min) and typically produce a single modality output (frames or text), while no prior work addresses extracting multiple connected sub-stories from full-length (~40 min) TV episodes with simultaneous video and text outputs. The paper also identifies a practical labeling challenge: manually annotating story importance across hundreds of episodes is infeasible, motivating the use of professionally edited TV recaps as a scalable source of supervision.

## Input / Output

The model ingests an entire TV episode (~40 min) decomposed into ~800–1000 video shots (each with up to 25 sub-sampled frames) and ~500–600 dialog utterances (averaging ~7–8 words). It produces a binary importance score in [0,1] for every shot and every utterance via a shared linear classifier with sigmoid. Labels are derived by matching recap shots to episode shots via frame-level embedding similarity, then smoothing the resulting binary vector with a triangular filter to capture contiguous sub-stories rather than isolated frames.

## Method

TaleSumm is a two-level hierarchical Transformer with 1.94M parameters. At level 1, a shot encoder fuses features from three pretrained backbones (DenseNet169, MViT, CLIP) via learned attention weights, then compresses the frame sequence through a single-layer shot Transformer whose CLS token yields the shot representation; a fine-tuned RoBERTa-large with mean pooling encodes each dialog utterance. At level 2, an episode-level Transformer (6 layers, 8 heads, D=128) processes all shot and utterance tokens arranged in temporal order, but uses a block-diagonal sparse attention mask that restricts each token to attend only within its local story group (~20 tokens), reducing noise from temporally distant elements. Learnable group tokens appended to each group communicate across the full episode via an augmented attention mask (A + ooᵀ), enabling long-range story continuity without full O(S²) attention. Additional embeddings encode modality type, binned real-time position (Fourier encoding), and group membership. Training is end-to-end with weighted binary cross-entropy over both modalities, requiring only 4 RTX-2080 Ti GPUs at batch size 4.

## Results

On the PlotSnap dataset (24 series, intra-season 5-fold CV), TaleSumm achieves 50.1±2.8 AP on video shots and 46.0±2.1 AP on dialog utterances (test set), outperforming all baselines by a significant margin. On the SumMe benchmark, TaleSumm attains the best F1 (57.5) and Spearman's ρ (23.8), while on TVSum it achieves the highest F1 (64.0) though PGLSUM leads on SP (40.5 vs. 26.7) and KT (28.2 vs. 18.2). Cross-series transfer (24→Prison Break) yields 36.7 AP on video and 35.7 AP on dialog, well above the random baseline of ~21.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaleSumm outperforms all four baselines on the primary PlotSnap test set: it beats the video-only PGLSUM by 3.0 AP (50.1 vs. 47.1) and MSVA by 4.6 AP (50.1 vs. 45.5) on shots, and beats the dialog-only PreSumm by 4.4 AP (46.0 vs. 41.6) on utterances. The multimodal A2Summ is the weakest baseline, trailing TaleSumm by 16.3 AP on video and 14.4 AP on dialog, likely because it was designed for short creative videos rather than 40-minute narrative episodes. On SumMe, TaleSumm achieves the best F1 (57.5) and SP (23.8), surpassing PGLSUM (56.2 F1, 17.3 SP) and MSVA (52.4 F1, 12.3 SP). On TVSum, TaleSumm matches PGLSUM on F1 (64.0 vs. 63.9) but falls behind on SP (26.7 vs. 40.5) and KT (18.2 vs. 28.2), which the authors attribute to the greater video diversity in TVSum (documentaries, how-to videos) versus the narrative structure of PlotSnap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing TV show recaps as supervision signals for a task—multimodal story summarization (VT2VT)—that the authors explicitly state has no prior formulation. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of video summarization (which handles short clips and emphasizes visual diversity), screenplay text summarization (SummScreen, SummScreen3D), and multimodal summarization (A2Summ, VideoXum), but departs from all by operating on 40-minute narrative episodes and producing both video and text importance scores. The hierarchical architecture with block-diagonal attention and group tokens is a novel design choice for managing the ~1500-token sequence length of a full episode, drawing on Transformer self-attention and Fourier position encodings but introducing the sparse group structure to balance local context with long-range story dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PlotSnap dataset (205 episodes, ~150 hours) and the recap-as-label methodology offer a scalable, low-cost annotation strategy that could extend to other narrative video domains beyond the two crime thrillers studied. The demonstration that a 1.94M-parameter model trained on consumer GPUs can generalize across seasons and even across TV series (24 → Prison Break) suggests the approach is not overfit to a single show's visual style. However, the work is limited to one genre, does not explicitly model characters or their relationships (central to narrative understanding), and early experiments on condensed movies (CMD) show only limited transfer, leaving open whether the recap-based paradigm scales to more diverse or longer-form content.
