## Problem

Sparse LiDAR scans suffer from severe loss of static structure detail, reducing the density of static points available for SLAM-based navigation. Existing generative models represent LiDAR as range images and are locally receptive, failing to identify and preserve the global static topology that persists even under high sparsity. No prior work explicitly enforces global topological consistency when augmenting new static points along LiDAR structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242400/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse dynamic LiDAR scan in range-image format (3×64×1024 for KITTI/CARLA-64, 3×16×1024 for ARD-16, further sparsified 8× along the azimuth to 128 columns) and a corresponding ground-truth static scan for supervised training. It produces an augmented static LiDAR point cloud and, as a byproduct, a binary segmentation mask separating static from dynamic points obtained by subtracting the dynamic input from the augmented output. The range-image representation enables a simple absolute-error loss in place of the memory-intensive Chamfer or Earth Mover's Distance, which are impractical for the ~110,000 points per 64-beam scan.

## Method

GLiDR represents the LiDAR scan as an undirected graph where points are nodes connected to their k-nearest neighbors, then stacks four graph convolution layers with message passing and max-pooling to progressively diffuse global topology information into 512-dimensional node embeddings, allowing distant nodes on the same structure to become neighbors in embedding space. A 0-dimensional Persistent Homology constraint is applied via sub-level set filtration on the augmented output and on intermediate encoder representations (128-dim and 512-dim layers): the topology loss minimizes the persistence of all 0-dim connected components except the first, forcing the filtration to converge to a single connected component that acts as a global static backbone. The total loss combines three topology terms (output, layer 2, layer 4) with an absolute-error augmentation loss against the ground-truth static scan. A four-layer 1D convolutional decoder projects the 512-dim embeddings back to 3D coordinates, and the resulting augmented scan is guaranteed to follow the topologically consistent backbone rather than producing locally plausible but globally inconsistent points.

## Results

GLiDR achieves the best or near-best results across all five metrics (CD, JSD, MMD, RMSE, EMD) on three datasets (KITTI, CARLA-64, ARD-16) in both sparse and dense settings. On KITTI sparse, GLiDR achieves CD of 1.60 (vs. 3.57 for MOVES, the next best) and EMD of 1.32 (vs. 1.95 for MOVES). On CARLA-64 sparse, GLiDR achieves CD of 0.91 (vs. 15.67 for MOVES) and EMD of 2.57 (vs. 18.85 for MOVES), showing particularly large margins. On ARD-16, GLiDR achieves the lowest CD (0.22 sparse) and EMD (0.05 sparse), with JSD of 0.02 (vs. 166.99 for DSLR), demonstrating robustness to extreme sparsity (8-beam).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLiDR outperforms all five baselines on Chamfer's Distance across every dataset and sparsity setting, with the largest margins on CARLA-64 sparse (CD 0.91 vs. 15.67 for MOVES, a 17× improvement) and KITTI sparse (CD 1.60 vs. 3.57 for MOVES). On EMD, GLiDR dominates: 0.05 vs. 1.40 (MOVES) on KITTI dense, 2.57 vs. 18.85 (MOVES) on CARLA-64 sparse, and 0.05 vs. 0.70 (MOVES) on ARD-16 sparse. GLiDR also wins on JSD for CARLA-64 (0.22 vs. 1.62 for MOVES sparse) and ARD-16 (0.02 vs. 159.31 for MOVES sparse), where baselines struggle severely with distributional fidelity. The only metrics where GLiDR does not achieve the best score are KITTI dense MMD (0.06 vs. 0.016 for DSLR) and ARD-16 dense RMSE (18.80 vs. 14.69 for MOVES), both narrow gaps. Overall, GLiDR is the clear winner on 24 out of 30 dataset-metric-sparsity combinations, with the 0-dim PH topological regularization providing the most benefit in sparse settings where global topology is hardest to preserve.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLiDR sits at the intersection of two research threads: graph-based point cloud representations (Dynamic Graph CNN, Point-GNN, DGCNN) that capture long-range dependencies via message passing, and Persistent Homology as a topological regularizer in deep learning (Topo-AE, topology layers). It departs from prior LiDAR completion/generation work (LiDARGen, NeRF-LiDAR, UltraLiDAR) that relies on range-image or NeRF representations and from earlier static-point augmentation methods (DSLR, MOVES) that fail to preserve global LiDAR topology under sparsity. The key conceptual departure is using a 0-dim PH-derived single connected component as an explicit backbone prior that constrains where new points may be placed, rather than relying solely on local reconstruction losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in sparse-LiDAR autonomous driving: reduced static point density degrades SLAM performance, and the proposed backbone-guided augmentation directly mitigates this without requiring dense sensors or heavy compute. The byproduct segmentation mask (static vs. dynamic) is useful for navigation safety in constrained environments, and the 4 ms/scan inference rate makes it deployable. A key limitation is domain-specificity: models trained on outdoor, industrial, or indoor scans do not transfer across environments, leaving domain adaptation as an open problem. Whether higher-dimensional PH (1-dim loops, 2-dim voids) would further improve augmentation, or whether the 0-dim backbone generalizes to non-LiDAR sparse point clouds, remains unexplored.
