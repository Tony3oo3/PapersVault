## Problem

Reconstructing detailed 3D tree geometry from a single photograph remains difficult because trees have complex topologies, self-occlusions, and intricate internal branching that are invisible in a 2D view. Existing single-image methods either require user interaction to identify branches or produce only coarse outer envelopes (e.g., radial bounding volumes) that miss fine-grained interior structure and foliage morphology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241439/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB photograph of a tree, first segmented by SAM to isolate the tree instance against a white background. It produces a 64³ Semantic Voxel Structure (SVS) where each voxel carries one of four semantic labels (trunk, branch, leaf, empty), which is then converted into a full 3D tree model comprising a skeleton graph and procedurally grown foliage with leaves distributed along branches.

## Method

The core contribution is a Semantic Voxel Diffusion Model: a U-Net-based denoising diffusion network (6 levels, 64³→2³) that regresses per-voxel classification values from a masked tree image, with image features from a ViT encoder injected via five mix blocks using multi-head attention. Casting the four-class voxel labeling as regression (trunk=1, branch=0.5, leaf=0, empty=−1) rather than classification mitigates the severe class imbalance (95% empty voxels). The predicted SVS drives a hybrid geometry reconstruction: a structure-oriented branch step builds a minimum spanning tree over trunk and branch voxels (processed separately to exploit their differing prediction accuracy), followed by a space colonization algorithm that grows twigs and distributes leaves strictly within the leaf-voxel volume. The model is trained supervised on 4,000 synthetic tree models generated procedurally, with randomized camera poses, textures, and lighting to reduce domain gap.

## Results

On the synthetic test set of 100 images, the full SVDTree model achieves a mean average precision (mAP) of 0.546 across all four semantic classes, compared to 0.297 for the cGAN baseline. Per-class precision for trunk, branch, and leaf is 0.538, 0.176, and 0.461 respectively, with a mean absolute error of 0.0134 (std 0.198) between predicted and ground-truth SVS values. The comparison with the RBV method [26] is qualitative: the paper reports that SVS captures finer interior branch details and foliage spatial morphology, but no numerical metrics are provided for that comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the cGAN baseline [31], SVDTree wins decisively on every reported metric: trunk precision improves from 0.130 to 0.538 (4.1×), leaf precision from 0.137 to 0.461 (3.4×), mAP from 0.297 to 0.546 (1.8×), and mean absolute error drops from 0.0468 to 0.0134 (3.5× lower). The multi-head attention in the mix block contributes an additional gain over the no-attention variant, most notably on branch precision (0.061 → 0.176) and leaf precision (0.313 → 0.461). Against the RBV method [26], the comparison is purely qualitative (Figure 9): the authors argue that SVS encodes finer interior branch structure and foliage spatial morphology that RBV's radial bounding volumes cannot capture, and that their pipeline requires no manually specified species parameters. No numerical reconstruction-quality metric (e.g., IoU, Chamfer distance) is reported for the RBV comparison, so the magnitude of improvement over [26] remains unquantified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) for the core pixel-to-voxel prediction and on the space colonization algorithm (Palubicki et al., 2009) for foliage synthesis, but departs from prior single-image tree reconstruction by replacing binary voxel or radial bounding volume representations with a Semantic Voxel Structure (SVS) that encodes trunk, branch, and leaf labels per voxel. Unlike Li et al. (2021) which learns species-specific radial bounding volumes, or Liu et al. (2021) which uses a cGAN to predict depth, SVDTree casts the 3D inference as a semantic regression problem within a diffusion process, guided by ViT-encoded image features through attention-based mix blocks. The hybrid reconstruction pipeline—combining minimum-spanning-tree skeletonization for branches with space colonization for the crown—is specifically tailored to exploit the semantic structure in the SVS rather than treating the tree as a monolithic shape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SVS representation offers a principled way to recover occluded internal branches from a single view, which binary occupancy or bounding-volume approaches fundamentally cannot capture. However, the evaluation is limited to 100 synthetic test images for quantitative metrics, with real-world results being purely qualitative, and the training set is only 4,000 procedurally generated trees excluding difficult species (e.g., palms). Open questions include whether the domain gap between synthetic training and real photographs is adequately bridged, whether the approach generalizes to species outside the training distribution, and how it would perform with additional 3D priors such as depth maps. The paper also leaves texture, color, and relative multi-tree positioning as future work, limiting immediate practical deployment.
