## Problem

Existing trajectory prediction methods treat forecasting as a static task, independently predicting future trajectories at each time step from a fixed window of historical frames despite heavy input overlap between adjacent steps. This static paradigm causes temporal inconsistency and instability in successive predictions, which is detrimental to safe autonomous driving decisions. The paper argues that successive predictions share an intrinsic dynamic relationship—overlapping segments should be consistent, or differ while sharing the same motion goal—and that this relationship can be explicitly modeled to improve both accuracy and stability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240691/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a fixed-length sequence of agent states (position, velocity, orientation, semantic attributes) and HD map lane segments, all encoded in local polar coordinates with relative spatio-temporal edge features. Unusually, it also ingests historical prediction embeddings (not raw trajectories) from prior time steps, which extends the effective observation window beyond the visible historical frames without additional computational cost. The output is K multimodal future trajectories of F time steps each, produced in two stages: an initial proposal decoded by an MLP, then a refinement that re-enters the full pipeline, yielding final trajectories plus per-mode probability scores.

## Method

HPNet's core contribution is the Historical Prediction Attention module, which performs self-attention between the current prediction embedding and historical prediction embeddings within a temporal span I₂, modeling the dynamic correlation between successive predictions. This module is combined with Agent Attention (cross-agent interaction) and Mode Attention (cross-mode interaction) into the Triple Factorized Attention, repeated twice for full interaction. A key property is that Historical Prediction Attention effectively doubles the observation window: when I₂ = I₁, the visible range extends from [t−I₁, t] to [t−2I₁, t] because each historical prediction embedding already encodes information from its own prior frames. The architecture is built on GNNs with relative spatio-temporal position encoding, uses learnable mode queries for multimodal output, and is trained with a winner-takes-all strategy applying Huber loss to both the initial proposal and the refined trajectory alongside cross-entropy for probability scores.

## Results

On the Argoverse test set, HPNet (single model) achieves the best b-minFDE of 1.7375, minFDE of 1.0986, MR of 0.1067, and minADE of 0.7612, outperforming the previous best single model GANet by 0.052 in b-minFDE and 0.062 in minFDE. In the ensemble setting, HPNet achieves b-minFDE 1.6768, surpassing QCNet (1.6934) by 0.017 on the official ranking metric. On the INTERACTION dataset for joint multi-agent prediction, HPNet achieves minJointFDE 0.8231 and minJointADE 0.2548, improving over the second-best FJMP by 0.099 and 0.020 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Argoverse (single model), HPNet outperforms all 8 baselines across all four metrics, with the largest gains over GANet (0.052 b-minFDE, 0.062 minFDE) and HiVT (0.105 b-minFDE, 0.071 minFDE). In the ensemble setting, HPNet achieves the best b-minFDE (1.6768) over QCNet (1.6934) and ProphNet (1.6942), though QCNet retains a slight edge on minFDE (1.0666 vs. 1.0856) and minADE (0.7340 vs. 0.7478), suggesting QCNet's ensemble diversity is marginally stronger on point-wise accuracy while HPNet's temporal consistency gives it the confidence-weighted advantage. On INTERACTION, HPNet substantially outperforms FJMP (0.8231 vs. 0.9218 minJointFDE) and HDGT (0.8231 vs. 0.9580), with the gap over FJMP being the most notable at ~11% relative improvement. The paper notes that HPNet's ensemble gain over its single model is smaller than other methods (only 0.013 in minFDE), attributed to the inherent stability of its predictions reducing the benefit of ensemble averaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

HPNet builds directly on QCNet's query-centric encoding paradigm and two-stage refinement pipeline, adopting its location-independent node features and relative spatio-temporal edge encoding. It departs from the dominant 'static paradigm' where each timestep is predicted independently from a fixed history window, and generalizes DCMS's rigid temporal consistency constraint (overlapping waypoints must be identical) into a flexible attention-based mechanism that allows successive predictions to be consistent, slightly different, or share the same motion goal without forced waypoint matching. The core architectural contribution—the Triple Factorized Attention decomposing interactions into Agent, Historical Prediction, and Mode dimensions—follows the axis-separated attention design popularized by HiVT and QCNet but introduces the Historical Prediction Attention as a new axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal consistency in trajectory predictions is critical for downstream autonomous driving decision-making, and HPNet demonstrates that a lightweight attention module over prediction embeddings can improve both accuracy and stability without serializing training. The observation that Historical Prediction Attention effectively doubles the observation window at no additional compute cost is a practically useful property for real-time deployment. However, the approach is limited to two datasets and a fixed number of attention layers (N_attn=2); whether the extended attention window benefit persists at longer prediction horizons or in more complex multi-agent scenarios remains open. The reliance on prediction embeddings rather than actual trajectories (to preserve parallel training) is a design trade-off whose impact on very long temporal dependencies is unexplored.
