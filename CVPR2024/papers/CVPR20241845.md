## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D point cloud understanding methods transfer VLM representations from 2D pixels to 3D points via naive pooling operations, treating points as isolated entities without leveraging the inherent geometric structure of the point cloud. No prior training-free method incorporates geometric information during the VLM representation aggregation phase, leaving a gap in how spatially and structurally similar regions can share semantic information to improve downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241845/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud along with multi-view rendered depth maps (or original RGBD images) from which dense per-pixel CLIP representations are extracted and projected onto 3D points, plus FPFH geometric descriptors computed per point. The output is a set of geometrically-coherent point-level VLM representations that remain aligned with the original CLIP feature space (preserving compatibility with text embeddings), which can then be used directly for classification (via max pooling), part segmentation, or semantic segmentation without any additional training.

## Method

GeoZe is a training-free, iterative aggregation pipeline that first partitions the point cloud into superpoints via an optimal transport formulation (solved with the Sinkhorn algorithm) jointly over coordinate, FPFH geometric, and VLM representation spaces. Local aggregation refines VLM representations within each superpoint's kNN neighborhood using Sinkhorn-weighted linear combinations of geometric and semantic similarities, while global aggregation exchanges information among superpoints sharing similar geometric structures. Mean-Shift clustering over combined geometric and VLM features establishes VLM representation anchors that serve as semantic prototypes. A final anchor projection step corrects any offsets introduced during aggregation, ensuring the refined representations remain in the original CLIP feature space and maintain alignment with their corresponding language embeddings.

## Results

On zero-shot 3D shape classification, GeoZe achieves 70.2% accuracy on ModelNet40 (+6.0 over PointCLIPv2) and 59.3% on ObjectScanNN OBJ-ONLY (+9.2). For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, GeoZe reaches 57.4 mIoU, surpassing PointCLIPv2 by +7.9 mIoU. On semantic scene segmentation, GeoZe attains 45.6 mIoU on ScanNet (+4.2 over OpenScene) and 22.6 mIoU on nuScenes (+2.0 over OpenScene), establishing new state-of-the-art across all nine experimental configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoZe is compared against three training-free baselines: PointCLIPv2, OpenScene, and ConceptFusion. Against PointCLIPv2, GeoZe wins on all four classification benchmarks (e.g., +6.0 on ModelNet40, +9.2 on ObjectScanNN OBJ-ONLY) and on part segmentation (+7.9 mIoU on ShapeNetPart), with the largest per-class gains on chair (+14.6), mug (+15.2), and laptop (+13.5). Against OpenScene (2D Fusion variant), GeoZe outperforms on ScanNet by +4.2 mIoU (45.6 vs. 41.4) and on nuScenes by +2.0 mIoU (22.6 vs. 20.6), with notable per-class improvements on floor (+8.7), bathtub (+6.2), and drivable surface (+15.2). Against ConceptFusion, GeoZe achieves +3.1 mIoU on average on ScanNet, winning on 19 out of 20 classes with the largest margin on bathtub (+18.5). GeoZe does not lose on any headline metric, though on nuScenes it underperforms OpenScene on a few minor classes (e.g., pedestrian −5.6, man-made object −6.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoZe extends the training-free zero-shot point cloud understanding line (PointCLIP, PointCLIPv2, OpenScene, ConceptFusion), which transfers per-pixel VLM representations to 3D points via naive average pooling, by introducing the first geometrically-driven aggregation that explicitly leverages 3D structure (FPFH descriptors, normals) to denoise and refine those representations. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures: point cloud geometric aggregation (PointNet++, Point Transformer, superpoint graphs) and VLM-based 2D-to-3D feature transfer, using optimal transport (Sinkhorn) and Mean-Shift clustering as the mathematical machinery. The key departure from prior work is the assumption that VLM representations should exhibit local smoothness and global consistency where geometric structure is similar, an assumption no prior training-free method exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoZe provides a plug-and-play, training-free module that can be layered on top of any existing VLM-to-point-cloud pipeline, making it practically accessible without retraining. The consistent SOTA improvements across synthetic/real, indoor/outdoor, and LiDAR/RGBD settings (9 experiments) demonstrate broad applicability. Limitations include dependence on upstream VLM quality, potential degradation for small objects in large scenes, and added inference time (~2-3 ms per point cloud for ShapeNet, ~37 ms for ScanNet). Open questions include whether richer geometric descriptors or learned (but lightweight) geometric features could further close the gap, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or dynamic point clouds.
