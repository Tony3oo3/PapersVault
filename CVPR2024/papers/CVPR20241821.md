## Problem

Generating natural human motions that reach arbitrary 3D goals remains unsolved: RL-based methods produce unnatural motions and require prohibitive training, while data-driven methods fail to generalize beyond their training distribution. A core obstacle is the scarcity of datasets that combine locomotion with goal-reaching, and the inability to jointly train on datasets with and without explicit goal labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241821/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial SMPL-X body pose (135-dim vector of translation, 6D root orientation, and 21×6D joint rotations), a 3D goal position, and a user-specified time-to-reach as input, and produces an autoregressive sequence of SMPL-X poses at 30 fps. At each generation step, the model predicts a 135-dim pose delta (translation, orientation, and joint deltas with global z-orientation removed) that is integrated with the previous pose to yield the next frame, with intention features recalculated in a closed loop from the current predicted state.

## Method

WANDR is a conditional VAE that autoencodes frame-to-frame pose deltas in an autoregressive manner, conditioned on the current dynamic state (pose + previous delta) and three intention features: wrist intention (average velocity needed to reach the goal in remaining time), orientation intention (desired body facing direction relative to the goal), and pelvis intention (exponentially saturated horizontal distance to the goal). The key insight is a Hindsight Experience Replay-inspired training scheme: for unlabeled AMASS sequences, a random future wrist position is hallucinated as the goal, creating a unified training objective with the explicitly labeled CIRCLE reaching dataset. This allows the model to learn locomotion skills from AMASS and precise reaching from CIRCLE under the same distribution, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen goal locations without any explicit goal-reaching loss.

## Results

On the full evaluation suite of 3,750 generated motions (covering 5 angles × 5 heights × 5 distances × 6 initial poses × 5 samples), WANDR achieves a 32% success rate (wrist within 10 cm of goal), 16% foot-skating rate, and a mean closest distance to goal of 24.8 cm. Training on CIRCLE alone yields 0% success rate with 56% foot skating, while AMASS alone gives 16% success rate with 19% foot skating, confirming that combining both datasets is essential. The full WANDR model reduces distance-to-goal by roughly 2× compared to AMASS-only training (24.8 cm vs. 48.0 cm) while maintaining low foot skating.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares WANDR against one external baseline, GOAL [32], a method trained on the GRAB dataset that generates whole-body motions for hand-object grasping. WANDR decisively outperforms GOAL on all three metrics: success rate (32% vs. 0%), foot skating (16% vs. 29%), and distance to goal (24.8 cm vs. 149.2 cm). GOAL fails entirely on the evaluation protocol because its training data (GRAB) contains only small-variation grasping motions with the human already near the object, so it cannot generalize to the diverse goal positions and long-range navigation required here. The internal ablations further show that WANDR's full intention vector (wrist + pelvis + orientation) is necessary: removing any single component degrades success rate from 32% to 15–19%, and a latent-space optimization baseline without intention signals achieves only 3% success rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

WANDR positions itself within the data-driven branch of goal-reaching motion generation, departing from RL-based methods (MotionVAE, AMP, GAMMA) that struggle with naturalness and training cost. Its autoregressive c-VAE architecture with pose-delta encoding builds directly on MotionVAE [15] and HuMoR [26], while the key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'intention features' that unify training across goal-labeled (CIRCLE) and unlabeled (AMASS) datasets, borrowing the Hindsight Experience Replay paradigm from robotics [3]. Unlike prior data-driven methods (GOAL, CIRCLE) that require explicit goal annotations, WANDR hallucinates pseudo-goals from future wrist positions, enabling a single training objective across heterogeneous motion corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The intention mechanism offers a practical recipe for combining large-scale unlabeled motion data with smaller goal-specific datasets, addressing a real bottleneck in goal-reaching motion synthesis. However, the 32% success rate and acknowledged error accumulation limit immediate practical deployment, and the model lacks obstacle avoidance, grasping, and scene interaction. Open questions include whether the autoregressive error accumulation can be mitigated, whether the approach scales to bimanual or multi-agent scenarios, and how it interacts with emerging diffusion-based motion generators that have shown strong results on text- and spatial-conditioned synthesis.
