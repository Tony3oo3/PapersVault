## Problem

Universal Cross-Domain Retrieval (UCDR) requires generalizing to both unseen domains and unseen categories simultaneously, a challenge that existing prompt tuning methods (CoOp, VPT) fail to address because their static prompts cannot adapt to unknown domain-category combinations. While CLIP's large-scale pre-training provides strong general knowledge, full fine-tuning distorts these features and incurs high computational cost, leaving an open question of how to design a prompt tuning strategy that effectively mines CLIP's generalized knowledge for UCDR.

## Input / Output

At retrieval time, the method takes a query image from an unseen domain and a gallery of real images (either all unseen classes or mixed seen/unseen classes), producing a ranked list via cosine similarity between image embeddings. Notably, the CLIP text encoder is entirely discarded during inference—only the image encoder with dynamically generated prompts is used. The Content-aware Dynamic Prompts are generated per-sample by a lightweight 2-layer ViT simulator, making the prompt content-dependent rather than fixed.

## Method

ProS introduces a two-stage training paradigm to simulate content-aware dynamic prompts (CaDP) for a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32. In the Prompt Units Learning stage, K domain prompt units and |C_train| semantic prompt units are trained with a mask-and-align objective: for each input, only the prompt corresponding to its true domain and class is unmasked, forcing each prompt to capture specific source knowledge while aligning features across domains in a shared semantic space. In the Context-aware Simulator Learning stage, a 2-layer ViT-based Content-aware Prompt Simulator (CaPS) is trained under a simulated test scenario where the relevant prompt units are masked out, compelling it to reconstruct domain and semantic prompts from the remaining units plus image patches. At retrieval, CaPS dynamically generates one CaDP for domain and one for semantic from the input image, which are inserted into the first Transformer layer of the frozen CLIP image encoder to produce generalized embeddings, using only 12.84% of model parameters as learnable.

## Results

On DomainNet under the UCDR protocol, ProS achieves an average mAP@200 of 0.6052 (Unseen Gallery) and 0.5546 (Mixed Gallery), outperforming the strongest prompt-tuning baseline VPT (0.5720 / 0.5225) by 3.21% and 3.21% respectively. Under U^d CDR, ProS reaches 0.6332 mAP@200, surpassing VPT by 5.36%. On the U^c CDR setting, ProS attains 0.6991 mAP@200 on Sketchy and 0.6675 mAP@all on TU-Berlin, setting new state-of-the-art results across all three benchmark datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProS consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and evaluation protocol. Against the strongest prompt-tuning competitor VPT [17], ProS gains +3.21% mAP@200 on UCDR (average) and +5.36% on U^d CDR, while also leading on Sketchy (+4.03% mAP@200) and TU-Berlin (+11.01% mAP@all). Compared to CoOp [49], the improvement is even larger: +8.14% mAP@200 on UCDR and +19.6% on TU-Berlin, indicating that static text prompts are insufficient for open-set retrieval. Against the ViT-based SASA [41], ProS wins by +18.63% mAP@200 on UCDR and +19.6% on TU-Berlin, though the gap on Sketchy is narrower (+0.81%), which the authors attribute to CLIP's weaker visual backbone on that dataset. The ResNet-based SnMpNet [32] is the weakest baseline, trailing ProS by over 30% mAP@200 on UCDR, underscoring the advantage of leveraging large-scale pre-trained CLIP knowledge for cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Universal Cross-Domain Retrieval (UCDR) line initiated by SnMpNet [32] and SASA [41], which relied on ResNet or ViT backbones fine-tuned with semantic guidance. It departs from prior UCDR work by being the first to leverage CLIP's large-scale pre-trained knowledge and, more specifically, the first to apply prompt tuning to this task. Methodologically, it builds on CoOp [49] and VPT [17] but identifies that their static, content-agnostic prompts fail under UCDR's dual domain-and-category shift, motivating the two-stage Prompt Units Learning + Context-aware Simulator design with a mask-and-align objective that separately encodes domain and semantic knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that CLIP's general knowledge yields substantial gains over ViT for UCDR (22.27% mAP@200), validating the use of large-scale vision-language models for open-set retrieval. However, it also reveals that naively applying existing prompt tuning methods does not transfer well to UCDR, highlighting that prompt design must explicitly account for both domain and category generalization. Limitations include evaluation on only three datasets with a small ViT-B/32 backbone, and the Quickdraw domain still shows relatively weak performance due to data quality. Open questions include whether the simulation paradigm scales to larger CLIP variants (ViT-L/14) and whether the two-stage prompt decomposition generalizes to other open-set tasks beyond retrieval.
