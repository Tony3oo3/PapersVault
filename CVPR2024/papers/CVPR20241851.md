## Problem

Existing learning-based subspace simulation methods train neural networks to reconstruct pre-computed simulation data using geometry-based losses (e.g., L2 autoencoder reconstruction). This approach ignores the physical equilibrium manifold, producing high-energy configurations with stress concentrations, suffers from overfitting that degrades generalization, and yields entangled, uninterpretable latent dimensions. No prior method learns nonlinear modal subspaces in a fully self-supervised, physics-driven manner.

## Input / Output

The network takes modal coordinates z ∈ ℝ^m (sampled uniformly from a predefined domain during training) and outputs a correction vector y ∈ ℝ^{3n} that is added to the linear mode combination l = Σ z_i e_i to produce deformed vertex positions x = X + l + y. The correction is constrained to be orthogonal to l via a penalty term, ensuring the output lies on the nonlinear equilibrium manifold. For dynamics, the trained subspace n(z) is plugged into a variational implicit Euler update solved with L-BFGS in the reduced m-dimensional space, yielding real-time stepping at ~28–40 ms per step depending on model size.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-supervised training objective that directly minimizes the mechanical energy E(X + l + y[θ](z)) over randomly sampled modal coordinates, eliminating the need for any curated simulation dataset. The architecture is a standard MLP (5–10 layers, 64–256 hidden units, GELU activations) mapping z to the correction y, with the full subspace defined as n(z) = l + y(z). The loss combines the elastic energy, a quadratic penalty λ(l^T y)^2 enforcing orthogonality to the linear mode subspace, and an origin-regularization term η‖y(0)‖² that ensures Neural Modes reduce to linear modes at zero displacement. This formulation extends Dünser et al.'s single-mode nonlinear compliant modes to fully coupled m-dimensional modal spaces, and the energy-minimizing training naturally produces interpretable, orthogonal latent directions without mode collapse—unlike prior unsupervised approaches that require isometric regularizers.

## Results

On the square sheet benchmark, Neural Modes (grid sampling) achieves a mean elastic energy error of 199 (validation) and 245 (test), roughly 9× lower than PCA+AE (1814 / 2259) and 6.7× lower than the L2-supervised variant (1630 / 2074). On the Armadillo deformable solid, Neural Modes yields an average test-set energy of 55.8×10⁴ versus 987.5×10⁴ for PCA+AE (ground truth: 11.3×10⁴), a ~17.7× improvement. In terms of internal stress, Neural Modes (grid) reduces the mean per-element stress error to 6×10⁶ (weighted) and 16×10⁶ (max), compared to 25×10⁶ and 67×10⁶ for PCA+AE. Subspace dynamics run at ~35 ms per timestep, two orders of magnitude faster than full-space simulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PCA+AE (Fulton et al. [8]), Neural Modes wins decisively on every physical metric: mean energy error is ~9× lower on the sheet and ~17.7× lower on the Armadillo solid, while mean stress error drops from 25×10⁶ to 6×10⁶. The paper further shows that PCA+AE overfits—its test-set L2 loss, energy, and stress all increase during training—whereas Neural Modes steadily reduces test energy and stress, confirming that the geometric L2 loss fails to capture equilibrium constraints. Against Sharp et al. [32] (data-free unsupervised learning with an isometric regularizer), Neural Modes avoids mode collapse: the correlation matrix of Sharp et al.'s 5D Armadillo subspace has eigenvalues (2.3, 1.6, 1.1, 0.0, 0.0), revealing only 3 effective dimensions, while Neural Modes produces near-orthogonal latent directions with all eigenvalues close to 1.0, preserving full subspace expressiveness for both arm and leg motions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Nonlinear Compliant Modes (Dünser et al., 2022) from single-mode to multi-dimensional modal subspaces, and departs from the dominant supervised-learning paradigm for subspace construction (Fulton et al., 2019; Holden et al., 2019) by replacing geometry-based reconstruction losses with direct mechanical energy minimization. The lineage runs from classical linear modal analysis through nonlinear compliant modes to the present learned, self-supervised formulation. It also positions itself against Sharp et al. (2023), another data-free approach, by showing that their isometric regularizer cannot prevent mode collapse, whereas the energy-based formulation naturally yields orthogonal latent directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for curated simulation data and resolving the overfitting that plagues supervised subspace learning, this work makes nonlinear subspace simulation substantially more practical and automatable. The interpretable, singularity-free latent spaces open the door to gradient-based navigation and keyframe animation without ad-hoc interpolation schemes. Limitations include restriction to differentiable, weakly convex energy functions, reliance on LBFGS rather than exact Hessian-based solvers in the reduced space, and evaluation on relatively small meshes; extending to contact-rich or inelastic systems and to inverse design remain open.
