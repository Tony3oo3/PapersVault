## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods largely ignore the spatial scale of anomalies, performing reconstruction or prediction in RGB space where severe background noise interferes with learning subtle anomalous changes. Coarse-grained modeling paradigms (autoencoders, frame prediction) are inadequate for detecting small-scale anomalies that occupy only limited localized regions with minimal deviation from normal patterns, resulting in missed detections that scale-aware methods have not adequately addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240432/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes video clips of T frames (default T=12) resized to 256×256 pixels with values normalized to [0,1]. During training, both continuous clips (T frames) and discontinuous clips (T−1 frames with one uniformly sampled missing frame) are constructed from the same video. At inference, the model produces per-frame anomaly scores by computing PSNR for each frame within a T-frame window and normalizing the PSNR of the frame with the largest MSE across the window, yielding a score in [0,1] where higher values indicate greater anomaly likelihood.

## Method

The architecture uses a shared I3D-RGB 3D-ConvNet backbone with three projection heads, each solving a self-supervised proxy task derived from video continuity: (1) continuity judgment (binary classification of continuous vs. discontinuous clips) for coarse-grained global motion learning, (2) discontinuity localization (T−2 class classification identifying the missing frame position) for fine-grained local motion learning, and (3) missing frame estimation formulated as a contrastive learning task in feature space rather than RGB reconstruction, using triplet loss for motion similarity and InfoNCE-style contrastive loss for appearance similarity. The key design choice is performing frame estimation in the learned feature space via cosine similarity comparisons (discontinuous clip vs. missing frame as positive, continuous clip as negative for motion; same-scene clips as positive, cross-scene clips as negative for appearance), which avoids background noise interference inherent to pixel-space reconstruction. All three losses are jointly optimized with equal weights, and the resulting scale-aware spatio-temporal features are used at inference to compute per-frame anomaly scores via PSNR-based normalization.

## Results

On Avenue, the full method (three proxy tasks + multi-task backbone + virtual data) achieves 94.3% Micro AUC and 94.5% Macro AUC, setting a new state of the art. On ShanghaiTech, it reaches 87.5% Micro AUC and 93.0% Macro AUC, improving over the previous best by 2.5% Micro AUC. On UCF-Crime, the method attains 80.6% Micro AUC and 83.9% Macro AUC, outperforming the second-best method by 5.1% and 5.6% respectively. On the challenging Campus dataset (scene-dependent anomalies), the method with a scene-conditioned backbone achieves 70.1% Micro AUC and 72.2% Macro AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Avenue, the full configuration (Ours∇* Three Tasks) achieves 94.3/94.5 AUC, surpassing the previous best Liu et al. [24] (93.6/93.9) by 0.7% Micro and 0.6% Macro, and Acsintoae et al. [1] (93.0/93.2) by 1.3% Micro. On ShanghaiTech, the method improves over Liu et al. [24] (85.0/91.4) by 2.5% Micro and 1.6% Macro, and over Georgescu et al. [11] (82.4/89.3) by 5.1% Micro and 3.7% Macro. On UCF-Crime, the method (80.6/83.9) outperforms the second-best Sun et al. [39] (75.5/78.3) by a substantial 5.1% Micro and 5.6% Macro AUC. On Campus, the scene-conditioned variant (70.1/72.2) beats Cao et al. [2] (68.2/–) by 1.9% Micro AUC, while the non-scene-conditioned version (67.3/70.9) still surpasses all RGB reconstruction/prediction baselines including Lv et al. [28] (64.4/–) and Wang et al. [41] (61.9/64.2). Notably, even without the multi-task backbone or virtual data, the method (92.4/92.9 on Avenue; 85.1/89.8 on ShanghaiTech) remains competitive with or exceeds methods that rely on those additional components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the video continuity framework of Liang et al. (AAAI 2022) for self-supervised spatiotemporal representation learning, repurposing it to construct three proxy tasks (continuity judgment, discontinuity localization, missing frame estimation) for video anomaly detection. It departs from the dominant reconstruction-based and prediction-based VAD paradigms by formulating frame estimation as a contrastive learning task in feature space rather than RGB space, explicitly to avoid background noise interference that plagues small-scale anomaly detection. The method also positions itself against multi-scale VAD approaches that rely on pooling or feature pyramids [41, 52], arguing those provide resolution diversity but do not specifically target the motion and appearance features critical for anomaly discrimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: small-scale anomalies (e.g., a person running in a corner of a surveillance frame) are systematically under-detected by existing methods because background noise dominates RGB-space reconstruction or prediction errors. The contrastive feature-space formulation is a clean and potentially transferable idea that could be integrated into other VAD or video understanding pipelines. However, the method is evaluated primarily with an I3D backbone, and it remains unclear whether the proxy task design and contrastive estimation transfer effectively to transformer-based or foundation-model backbones. An open question is whether the three-task joint optimization is truly necessary or whether a simpler two-task variant (e.g., discontinuity localization + contrastive estimation) would suffice, as the ablation shows diminishing returns from adding continuity judgment on top of the other two.
