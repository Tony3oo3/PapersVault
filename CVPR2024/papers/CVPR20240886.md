## Problem

Existing general-purpose vision-language models fail to unify fine-grained segmentation (dense mask outputs) and multi-image inputs with coarse-level tasks like captioning and VQA in a single framework. Prior systems either handle only single images, omit segmentation entirely, or cannot reason across multiple input images, leaving a gap for a truly general-purpose vision system that addresses both coarse and fine-grained grounding over arbitrary numbers of images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240886/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts one or more 224×224 RGB images alongside a text instruction that may embed point, box, or mask references as interleaved coordinate tokens. Outputs are generated as natural language sequences: text answers/captions, bounding boxes as four quantized coordinates, or segmentation masks serialized as 32 quantized contour points (1000 bins per axis). The LLM vocabulary is extended with tokens 10–999 to represent quantized coordinates, and during inference these are dequantized back to image-space for metric computation.

## Method

VistaLLM combines three modules: an EVA-CLIP image encoder producing 256×1408 spatial features, an instruction-guided QFormer tokenizer with 32 learnable queries per image (768-dim hidden) that cross-attends to image features conditioned on the task instruction to refine and compress them, and a Vicuna LLM (7B/13B) decoder trained via next-token prediction. The key technical contribution for segmentation is a gradient-aware adaptive contour sampling scheme: the mask boundary is first densely discretized, then points are ranked by the angle between adjacent contour segments, and only the N points with the sharpest curvature are retained—preserving fine geometric detail where uniform sampling would lose it (97.26 vs. 94.70 mIoU upper bound on RefCOCO val with 32 points). Training proceeds in two stages: stage 1 trains all parameters except the frozen encoder on single-image data for 2 epochs, and stage 2 fine-tunes only the QFormer tokenizer on multi-image data for 5 epochs, using the 6.8M-sample CoinIt dataset spanning four task categories including the newly proposed AttCoSeg benchmark (804k samples) for multi-image region-level grounding.

## Results

VistaLLM-13B achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 15 evaluated benchmarks. On referring expression comprehension (REC), it reaches 89.9/92.5/85.0 mIoU on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, surpassing the strongest general-purpose baseline Ferret-13B (89.5/92.4/84.4). On referring expression segmentation (RES), it achieves 77.2/78.7/73.9 mIoU on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, outperforming the best specialist model PolyFormer (76.0/77.1/73.2). On COCO captioning, VistaLLM gains 10.9 CIDEr points over Shikra-13B (128.4 vs. 117.5), and on object co-segmentation (iCoSeg) it achieves 95.1 J-index, beating CycleSegNet by 3.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VistaLLM-13B outperforms all 50 compared baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Among general-purpose MLLMs, it consistently beats the strongest competitor Ferret-13B [111] on REC (by 0.1–2.2 mIoU across splits) and POPE hallucination (by 0.6–0.9 F1), while surpassing Shikra-13B [8] by 10.9 CIDEr on COCO captioning and 1.1–1.1 points on PQA/BQA. Against specialist (fine-tuned) models, VistaLLM exceeds PolyFormer [58] on RES by 0.4–2.0 mIoU, MDETR [35] on GREC by 13.1 Pr and 34.7 N-acc, LAVT [107] on GRES by 6.7 gIoU and 20.7 N-acc, and CycleSegNet [115] on iCoSeg by 3.0 J-index. On multi-image tasks, it beats ALBEF [44] on NLVR by 0.6–0.8 accuracy and GPT4RoI-7B [118] on VCR by 0.3–0.5 accuracy. Notably, VistaLLM is the first general-purpose system to report results on RES, GRES, CoSeg, and NLVR, and it achieves competitive or superior performance to task-specific models on all of them without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

VistaLLM extends the trajectory of general-purpose vision-language models (MLLMs) that have progressively moved from coarse-level tasks (VQA, captioning) to region-level grounding (detection, referring segmentation). It builds directly on BLIP-2's QFormer as the instruction-guided image tokenizer and on the LLaVA/Shikra codebase for the instruction-tuning pipeline, but departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it is the first system to unify segmentation masks (via gradient-aware adaptive contour sampling) alongside multi-image inputs in a single sequence-to-sequence LLM framework, and (2) it introduces a task-conditioned feature filtering mechanism where the tokenizer uses the language instruction to select relevant visual information rather than passing all global features to the decoder. The AttCoSeg task and the 6.8M-sample CoinIt dataset fill a gap in multi-image region-level training data that prior MLLMs lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper demonstrates that a single general-purpose model can match or exceed specialist systems across 15 diverse benchmarks spanning coarse and fine-grained, single- and multi-image tasks, which is a practical milestone for deploying unified vision-language systems. The gradient-aware adaptive sampling technique (3–4 mIoU gain over uniform sampling) and the instruction-guided tokenizer (5.4 J-index drop when removed) are concrete, reusable components for future work. Limitations include inherent information loss from representing masks as finite point sequences, and the model still trails GPT-4V on knowledge-based QA, suggesting that scale and pre-training breadth remain important. Open questions include whether the sequence-based mask representation can be further improved (e.g., hierarchical or variable-length encoding) and whether the instruction-guided tokenizer generalizes to even more heterogeneous multi-modal inputs.
