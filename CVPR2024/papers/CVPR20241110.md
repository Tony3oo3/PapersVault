## Problem

Data-driven 3D instance shape reconstruction from real-world scans is bottlenecked by the absence of a large-scale dataset with geometrically aligned CAD annotations. Existing real-world datasets like Scan2CAD retrieve CADs from synthetic databases and manually adjust poses, introducing shape misalignment that biases reconstruction, while synthetic datasets suffer from domain gaps. This paper addresses both the data gap and the methodological challenge of fusing complementary modalities for robust real-world reconstruction.

## Input / Output

For reconstruction (DisCo), the method takes a partial point cloud (K=20,000 surface points in canonical object space) and 2–5 posed RGB-D images per object instance, and outputs a continuous 3D occupancy field decoded from a triplane latent representation. For detection (OccGOD), the input is a full scene scan partitioned into 384×384×96 voxel grids at 4 cm resolution, and the output is oriented 3D bounding boxes augmented with a per-voxel occupancy prediction. All reconstruction evaluation normalizes shapes to the [−0.5, 0.5] interval before computing mIoU, Chamfer L2, and F-score.

## Method

DisCo is a latent triplane diffusion model: a triplane VAE (PointNet + UNet encoder, occupancy-field decoder with 3D-aware convolutions) first compresses shapes into a triplane latent space, then a 2D UNet denoiser trained with the EDM objective reconstructs the clean triplane from noise conditioned on both modalities. The key architectural contribution is the Hybrid Feature Aggregation Layer, which expands the triplane into a volumetric grid, projects grid cells onto multi-view images via camera poses to sample image features, and fuses them through a cross-attention transformer (voxel features as queries, image features as keys/values) before pooling back to triplane form—enabling effective local feature alignment between point clouds and images. Training follows a two-stage transfer-learning strategy: pretraining on synthetic data (ShapeNet, ABO, 3D-Future models embedded in HM3D scenes) followed by fine-tuning on LASA. For detection, OccGOD plugs an occupancy prediction head into CAGroup3D's backbone, supervised by scene-level occupancy labels derived from LASA's full CAD coverage, and concatenates occupancy features with backbone features during ROI pooling for second-stage box prediction.

## Results

On the LASA test set for instance-level shape reconstruction, DisCo (Ours-pts+imgs) achieves the best mIoU across 5 of 6 categories, with standout improvements on Table (41.5 mIoU vs. 30.9 for 3DShape2VecSet) and Chair (38.6 vs. 32.5). The method also attains the lowest Chamfer L2 distance in 5 of 6 categories (e.g., 3.57 on Chair vs. 6.43 for 3DShape2VecSet) and the highest F-score in all 6 categories. For 3D object detection, OccGOD improves overall mAP from 55.72 to 57.36 and mAR from 66.43 to 67.47 over the CAGroup3D baseline at IoU > 0.5, with the largest gains on Toilet (+8.47 AP) and Sink (+6.27 AP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IFNet [8], DisCo (pts+imgs) wins on 5 of 6 categories in mIoU, with the largest margin on Table (+13.2 mIoU) and Chair (+9.7 mIoU), while IFNet retains a slight edge on Bed (64.0 vs. 62.5 mIoU). Compared to 3DShape2VecSet [61], DisCo dominates across all categories in both mIoU and F-score, with particularly large gains on Table (+10.6 mIoU) and Cabinet (+7.0 mIoU). Against the LAS Diffusion variants [64], DisCo achieves substantially lower Chamfer L2 (e.g., 3.57 vs. 10.7 on Chair) and higher F-scores in every category, confirming the benefit of the Hybrid Feature Aggregation Layer over direct triplane-to-image projection. For 3D object detection, OccGOD outperforms CAGroup3D [49] in overall mAP (+1.64) and mAR (+1.04), with notable improvements on occluded or sparse objects like Toilet (+8.47 AP), Sink (+6.27 AP), and Bed (+3.47 AP), though it slightly underperforms on a few categories (TV Monitor, Bathtub, Washer) where the baseline already achieves high scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of triplane latent diffusion (building on 3DGen's triplane VAE and EDM's continuous diffusion formulation) and cross-modal shape reconstruction, extending the detect-then-reconstruct pipeline from prior work like RevealNet and RFD-Net. Its key departure from Scan2CAD and CAD-Estate is rejecting retrieved CAD models in favor of manually crafted, geometrically aligned annotations, addressing the shape misalignment that biases data-driven reconstruction. For detection, it builds directly on CAGroup3D by adding an occupancy prediction head, repurposing the dataset's full scene coverage as a completeness prior rather than treating detection and reconstruction as independent tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LASA addresses a genuine bottleneck: prior real-world datasets either lack alignment (Scan2CAD) or are too small for data-hungry training (ScanSalon, Aria's Digital Twin), and the 10,412 manually crafted CADs with 0.161 Chamfer distance (vs. 0.269 for Scan2CAD) provide a quality leap that should benefit the entire indoor reconstruction community. The occupancy-guided detection result, while modest in absolute terms (+1.64 mAP), validates the hypothesis that shape completeness priors transfer to detection, opening a path toward tighter coupling of reconstruction and detection pipelines. Open questions include whether the 920-scene, 17-category scope generalizes to outdoor or more diverse indoor settings, and whether the manual annotation cost (~69 min/model, 35 artists, 4 months) limits the dataset's extensibility.
