## Problem

Existing gait recognition methods depend on task-specific supervised upstream models (segmentation, human parsing, pose estimation) to produce explicit gait representations like silhouettes or skeletons, incurring expensive annotation costs and accumulating estimation errors. This paper asks whether effective gait representations can instead be extracted from task-agnostic Large Vision Models (LVMs) that learn all-purpose features via self-supervised pre-training, eliminating the need for any supervised upstream.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241718/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BigGait takes raw RGB walking videos as input, processing each frame in parallel after a Pad-and-Resize operation to 448×224 that preserves truthful body proportions. The frozen DINOv2 backbone produces multi-scale feature maps (32×16 tokens gathered from four ViT layers, upsampled to 64×32), which the GRE module transforms into two complementary gait representations—a parsing-like appearance map and a skeleton-like denoised map—fused via cross-attention. The final output is a gait identity embedding used for rank-1 recognition under multi-view evaluation protocols.

## Method

BigGait couples a frozen DINOv2 (ViT-S/14 or ViT-L/14) upstream with a modified GaitBase downstream, bridged by the Gait Representation Extractor (GRE). GRE contains three branches: a Mask branch that uses an auto-encoder on the highest-level ViT features to unsupervisedly infer a foreground mask (analogous to PCA finding two mutually exclusive components), an Appearance branch that linearly projects the masked multi-scale features into a compact parsing-like representation, and a Denoising branch that applies non-linear transformations regularized by a Sobel-based smoothness loss (suppressing high-frequency texture) and an information-entropy diversity loss (preventing trivial uniform solutions). The two branch outputs are fused via a cross-and-select attention block before feeding into GaitBase, which is trained with triplet and cross-entropy losses alongside the GRE's reconstruction, smoothness, and diversity losses. The key distinction from prior work is that all gait-relevant structure is induced through soft geometric constraints on LVM features rather than explicit supervised segmentation or pose estimation.

## Results

On the CCPG benchmark (the primary evaluation), BigGait achieves a mean Rank-1 accuracy of 87.2% across four cross-clothing settings (CL, UP, DN, BG), substantially outperforming the best silhouette-based method GaitBase (75.5%) and the best multi-modal method SkeletonGait++ (83.7%). In the most challenging full-clothing-change (CL) setting, BigGait reaches 82.6%, surpassing GaitBase by +11.0% and SkeletonGait++ by +3.5%. Cross-domain evaluation (Table 4) shows BigGait trained on CCPG achieves 77.4% NM and 71.5% BG on CASIA-B*, and 43.7% Clothing / 44.8% Night on SUSTech1K, outperforming all compared methods in those settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BigGait (RGB-only input) outperforms all 13 compared baselines across all four CCPG settings. Against silhouette-based methods, BigGait leads GaitBase [13] by +11.0% (CL), +10.9% (UP), +10.3% (DN), and +14.5% (BG), and surpasses the strongest silhouette method DeepGaitV2 [11] by +4.0% (CL) and +6.4% (DN). Against multi-modal methods, BigGait beats SkeletonGait++ [14] (skeleton+silhouette) by +3.5% (CL) and +3.2% (UP), and GaitBase^p+s [13] (parsing+silhouette) by +9.0% (CL) and +16.9% (BG), despite using only a single RGB input without any auxiliary modality. Against video-based ReID methods, the margins are largest: BigGait exceeds AP3D [18] by +29.2% (CL) and +17.4% (DN), and PSTA [46] by +40.4% (CL). Against skeleton-based methods, BigGait's advantage is overwhelming (e.g., +53.6% over MSGG [36] in CL). The only setting where the gap narrows is BG (bag change), where AP3D [18] reaches 91.4% vs. BigGait's 93.1%, a margin of just +1.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in gait recognition where task-specific supervised upstream models (segmentation, human parsing, pose estimation, SMPL) produce explicit gait representations like silhouettes or skeletons. Instead, it repurposes the frozen, task-agnostic features of DINOv2 (a self-supervised LVM) as the upstream, introducing the Gait Representation Extractor (GRE) module with three branches (mask, appearance, denoising) that transform all-purpose features into gait representations via soft geometric constraints rather than explicit supervision. The downstream GaitBase is minimally modified to accept the two-stream output, preserving the established gait metric learning framework while decoupling representation construction from supervised upstream tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive per-pixel annotations (silhouettes, skeletons, parsing maps) and avoiding error accumulation from supervised upstream models, BigGait offers a more scalable and practical paradigm for gait representation learning. However, the paper reveals that training data distribution critically affects cross-domain performance—BigGait trained on datasets with few cross-dressing pairs (CASIA-B*, SUSTech1K) still latches onto clothing cues, limiting its robustness. Open questions include improving interpretability of the learned multi-channel representations, handling low-frequency color noise (beyond the high-frequency texture noise addressed here), and systematically exploring how different LVMs and their pre-training data influence gait-specific feature quality.
