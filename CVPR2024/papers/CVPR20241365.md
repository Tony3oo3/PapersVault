## Problem

Existing scene-level datasets for deep learning-based 3D vision are either synthetic (Blender) or cover a narrow selection of real-world scenes (LLFF, Tanks and Temples, ScanNet++), making it impossible to comprehensively benchmark NVS methods on complex real-world scenarios (non-Lambertian surfaces, unbounded outdoor scenes, high-frequency textures) or to learn universal 3D priors at scale. This gap hinders both fair evaluation of SOTA methods and the development of generalizable 3D representation learning.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 10,510 4K-resolution videos (51.2M frames total) with calibrated camera poses, captured via consumer mobiles (10,407 scenes) and drones (103 scenes). For NVS evaluation, each scene yields 300–380 RGB images at 960×560 resolution split 7/8 train and 1/8 test. Beyond raw imagery, each scene carries fine-grained annotations: POI category (65 secondary classes), indoor/outdoor setting, reflection and transparency levels (4-class each), lighting type (natural/artificial/mixed), and a continuous texture-frequency metric computed via 2D bi-orthogonal wavelet transform on 100 sampled frames.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a large-scale data acquisition and annotation pipeline rather than a new rendering architecture. Videos are captured following strict quality guidelines (4K/60fps, ≥60s duration, 180°–360° horizontal coverage at multiple heights, ≤3s moving objects) across 65 POI categories, then screened for motion blur, overexposure, and privacy. Scene complexity is quantified through wavelet-based frequency estimation and manual labeling of reflectivity/transparency ratios. The DL3DV-140 benchmark samples 140 scenes balanced across all complexity indices to enable fair NVS evaluation. For the generalizable NeRF study, IBRNet and MVSNeRF are pre-trained on progressively larger subsets of DL3DV-10K (270, 1K, 2K scenes) before fine-tuning on their original evaluation sets, demonstrating that scale and diversity of real-world pre-training data consistently improve generalization over training from scratch or pre-training on the more limited ScanNet++.

## Results

On the DL3DV-140 benchmark (140 real-world scenes, 960×560 resolution), Zip-NeRF achieves the best average PSNR of 31.22 dB and SSIM of 0.921, while 3DGS attains the highest SSIM (0.919) with the fastest training time (2.1 hr) and moderate memory (16.8 GB). Mip-NeRF 360 reaches a competitive PSNR of 30.98 but requires 48 hours of training, making it 23× slower than 3DGS. Instant-NGP (PSNR 25.01) and Nerfacto (PSNR 24.61) trail the top methods by over 5 dB in PSNR, indicating significant difficulty on complex real-world scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Zip-NeRF (default batch 65536) leads all methods in PSNR (31.22) and SSIM (0.921), but reducing its batch size to 4096 (matching other methods) drops PSNR to 29.07, revealing sensitivity to ray-sample count. 3DGS is the best quality-to-speed trade-off: it matches or exceeds Mip-NeRF 360 in SSIM (0.919 vs. 0.911) and LPIPS (0.120 vs. 0.132) while training 23× faster (2.1 hr vs. 48 hr). Mip-NeRF 360 is notably more robust in low-frequency scenes (smaller standard deviation) and outperforms Zip-NeRF specifically in that subset. Instant-NGP and Nerfacto are clearly outperformed by all three top methods across every metric, with PSNR gaps of 5–6.6 dB. In the generalizable NeRF pilot study, pre-training IBRNet and MVSNeRF on DL3DV-10K subsets (1K, 2K scenes) consistently improves PSNR on both LLFF and DTU/360° benchmarks over training from scratch, whereas pre-training on ScanNet++(270) yields no improvement—demonstrating that the diversity and scale of DL3DV-10K are critical for learning a useful universal scene prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the scene-level dataset landscape for NVS by addressing a gap that no prior dataset fills: large-scale, real-world, multi-POI coverage with fine-grained complexity annotations. Existing datasets are either synthetic (Blender, ShapeNet) or real-world but narrow in scope (LLFF's 24 forward-facing scenes, Tanks and Temples' 21 scenes, ScanNet's indoor-only focus, RealEstate10K's single POI category). DL3DV-10K departs from these by capturing 10,510 scenes across 65 POI categories at 4K resolution, annotated with reflection, transparency, lighting, and texture-frequency indices. For the generalizable NeRF pilot, it repurposes IBRNet and MVSNeRF as baselines, positioning DL3DV-10K as the missing large-scale real-world training corpus that prior learning-based methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DL3DV-10K and its DL3DV-140 benchmark provide the field with the first comprehensive, diversity-controlled evaluation of NVS methods across bounded/unbounded, reflective/transparent, and high/low-frequency scenes, revealing that outdoor unbounded scenes remain the hardest setting for all current methods. The pilot study showing that pre-training on DL3DV-10K (even at 270 scenes) improves generalizable NeRF while ScanNet++ does not suggests that scene diversity, not just scale, is the critical ingredient for learning universal 3D priors. Open questions include whether the generalizable NeRF gains scale further with the full 10K scenes, whether the dataset's moving-object contamination (2,446 scenes with 3–10s of motion) limits its utility, and whether a true foundation model for 3D representation can be trained on this data alone or still requires complementary synthetic or object-level data.
