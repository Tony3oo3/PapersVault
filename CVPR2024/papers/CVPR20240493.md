## Problem

Existing robust subspace recovery methods, particularly Tyler's M-estimator (TME), are guaranteed to recover the underlying subspace only when the dimension-scaled signal-to-noise ratio (DS-SNR) exceeds 1, which translates to impractically high inlier fractions in low-dimensional settings (e.g., ~89% for fundamental matrix estimation with d=8, D=9). This paper asks whether a subspace-constrained variant of TME can provably recover the true subspace at strictly lower DS-SNR values, bridging the gap between theoretical guarantees and the inlier fractions encountered in real computer vision data.

## Input / Output

STE takes a centered N×D data matrix, a target subspace dimension d, and a regularization parameter γ ∈ (0,1), and outputs the d-dimensional subspace spanned by the top d eigenvectors of the converged estimator. For fundamental matrix estimation, the input is a set of vec(xx'^T) vectors in R^9 with d=8, and the output is the 1-dimensional orthogonal complement yielding the fundamental matrix. For camera screening, the input is the n-view essential matrix E of size 3n×3n treated as N=D=3n data points with d=6, and the output is the set of outlying column blocks (each 3n×3) whose corresponding cameras are removed. The algorithm requires the data to be centered and uses a fast top-d eigendecomposition to achieve O(KNDd) complexity.

## Method

STE is an iterative fixed-point algorithm that, at each step, computes a weighted covariance Z^(k) = Σ x_i x_i^T / (x_i^T (Σ^(k-1))^{-1} x_i), performs its eigendecomposition, and then collapses the bottom D−d eigenvalues to a single value γ·σ_{d+1,D} while preserving the top d eigenvalues, before rescaling to unit trace. This subspace constraint reduces the degrees of freedom from a full D×D covariance to effectively d+1 parameters, yielding a more accurate subspace estimator at lower computational cost. The resulting weights w_i^STE fuse TME's Mahalanobis-based weighting (capturing inlier shape within the subspace) with an FMS p=0 distance-based term (penalizing points far from the subspace), giving STE a hybrid robustness profile. Under a generalized haystack model with TME initialization, the paper proves that STE recovers the true subspace whenever DS-SNR ≥ η < 1, a regime where TME alone provably fails, with r-linear convergence of the subspace angle to zero.

## Results

On the 14 Photo Tourism datasets, STE achieves the lowest median relative rotation error among all RSR methods (TME, FMS, SFMS) for fundamental matrix estimation, and is competitive with the best RANSAC variants (LO-RANSAC, DEGENSAC) on most scenes. For the camera-removal task, LUD+STE reduces mean absolute rotation and translation errors relative to the unmodified LUD pipeline, with the most notable gains on Roman Forum and Gendarmenmarkt. STE also outperforms LUD+FMS, LUD+SFMS, and LUD+TME in the camera-screening setting, and LUD+STE and LUD+SFMS both show significant runtime improvements over plain LUD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STE clearly dominates the three RSR baselines (TME, FMS, SFMS) on fundamental matrix estimation, with substantially lower median rotation errors across all 14 Photo Tourism scenes. Against vanilla RANSAC, STE wins on 12 of 14 datasets but loses on Ellis Island and Vienna Cathedral. Compared to the state-of-the-art RANSAC extensions LO-RANSAC and DEGENSAC, STE is broadly competitive but trails on Notre Dame, Ellis Island, and Vienna Cathedral. In the camera-removal experiment, LUD+STE improves mean rotation and translation errors over the LUD baseline and over LUD+FMS, LUD+SFMS, and LUD+TME, with the largest gains on Roman Forum and Gendarmenmarkt. Both LUD+STE and LUD+SFMS also reduce runtime relative to plain LUD, indicating that outlier removal helps preserve parallel rigidity of the viewing graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

STE is explicitly positioned as a fusion of Tyler's M-estimator (TME) and a variant of Fast Median Subspace (FMS with p=0), combining TME's shape-aware weighting within the subspace with FMS's distance-based outlier suppression in the orthogonal complement. Theoretically, it builds on the generalized haystack model framework of Maunu et al. [33] and the SSE-hardness threshold of Hardt and Moitra [16], extending the recovery guarantee of Zhang [50] (which requires DS-SNR > 1) to the regime DS-SNR < 1 under a well-conditioned initialization. For applications, it departs from the dominant RANSAC paradigm (and its specialized variants LO-RANSAC, DEGENSAC) by offering a direct subspace-recovery approach, and introduces a novel use of the rank-6 n-view essential matrix property [39] within the LUD pipeline [36] for camera screening.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to recover subspaces below the DS-SNR = 1 threshold is practically significant for fundamental matrix estimation (d=8, D=9), where the [16] threshold demands ~89% inliers—far above what real data provides. However, the empirical gains over strong RANSAC variants (LO-RANSAC, DEGENSAC) are modest and dataset-dependent, and the camera-removal application, while conceptually novel, is acknowledged by the authors as not yet practical and may not improve accuracy over the existing LUD pipeline. Open questions include the optimal choice of γ, better initialization strategies, handling of degenerate configurations (dominant planes), and whether the approach scales to modern feature-matching pipelines beyond SIFT-based Photo Tourism.
