## Problem

Existing perception systems require explicit human instructions or pre-defined categories to identify target objects and cannot actively reason about implicit user intentions. This paper introduces reasoning segmentation, a new task where the query text involves complex reasoning or world knowledge (e.g., 'the food with high Vitamin C') rather than a straightforward object reference, demanding that the model jointly reason over text and image before producing a segmentation mask.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240831/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and an implicit text query as input, where the query can range from short phrases to long sentences involving multi-step reasoning or world knowledge. It produces a text response (optionally with explanatory reasoning) followed by one or more binary segmentation masks, triggered by a special <SEG> token in the generated text. Unlike standard referring segmentation, the query does not directly name the target object, so the model must infer which object is being referenced before segmenting it.

## Method

LISA introduces an embedding-as-mask paradigm that injects segmentation capability into a multimodal LLM (LLaVA) by expanding its vocabulary with a <SEG> token. When the LLM generates this token, its last-layer hidden embedding is projected through an MLP and fed, together with dense visual features from a frozen SAM ViT-H backbone, into a SAM-style decoder that produces the final mask. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined loss of autoregressive cross-entropy for text and a weighted BCE+DICE loss for the mask, using LoRA to efficiently fine-tune the LLM while preserving its original language capabilities. Notably, the model is trained exclusively on standard semantic segmentation, referring segmentation, and VQA datasets (no reasoning data), yet achieves strong zero-shot performance on reasoning segmentation, with further gains from fine-tuning on only 239 reasoning samples.

## Results

On the ReasonSeg benchmark (val set), LISA-7B achieves 44.4 gIoU and 46.0 cIoU without any reasoning-specific training data, surpassing the best prior method (OVSeg at 28.5 gIoU) by over 15 points. After fine-tuning on just 239 reasoning segmentation samples, LISA-7B improves to 52.9 gIoU / 54.0 cIoU (val) and 47.3 gIoU / 48.4 cIoU (test). Scaling to LLaVA v1.5 as the backbone and fine-tuning yields the best result: LISA-13B-LLaVA1.5(ft) reaches 65.0 gIoU / 72.9 cIoU on val and 61.3 gIoU / 62.2 cIoU on test. On standard referring segmentation (refCOCO), LISA-7B achieves 74.1 cIoU (val), matching or exceeding prior SOTA methods like ReLA (73.8) and LAVT (72.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LISA substantially outperforms all five prior segmentation methods on the ReasonSeg benchmark. The strongest baseline, OVSeg, achieves only 28.5 gIoU (val) and 26.1 gIoU (test), while LISA-7B without any reasoning training data already reaches 44.4/36.8 gIoU — a gap of roughly 16 and 11 points respectively. GRES (22.4/21.3), X-Decoder (22.6/21.7), SEEM (25.5/24.3), and Grounded-SAM (26.0/21.3) all fall in the 21–29 gIoU range, confirming that existing segmentors cannot handle implicit reasoning queries. The two-stage LLaVA1.5+OVSeg pipeline (38.2/39.7 gIoU) is closer but still trails LISA-7B-LLaVA1.5 (53.6/48.7) by 15+ points, validating the benefit of end-to-end training over decoupled text-mediated pipelines. On standard referring segmentation (Table 2), LISA-7B matches or slightly exceeds ReLA (74.1 vs. 73.8 cIoU on refCOCO val) and LAVT (74.1 vs. 72.7), and after fine-tuning on ReferSeg reaches 79.1 cIoU on refCOCO testA, setting a new SOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

LISA sits at the intersection of two mature research threads: multimodal LLMs (LLaVA, Flamingo, BLIP-2) that excel at reasoning but can only emit text, and segmentation models (SAM, Mask2Former, X-Decoder, SEEM) that produce fine-grained masks but lack implicit-reasoning capability. The paper departs from VisionLLM's polygon-sequence approach (which requires 4×8 A100 GPUs and 50 epochs) by proposing the embedding-as-mask paradigm, where a single <SEG> token's hidden state is decoded into a mask via a lightweight MLP and a frozen SAM decoder. It also contrasts with two-stage pipelines (LLM → text → OVSeg) by showing that end-to-end training with hidden-embedding communication substantially outperforms text-mediated information transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The embedding-as-mask paradigm offers a general, low-cost recipe for grafting fine-grained visual outputs onto any existing multimodal LLM, potentially extending beyond segmentation to detection, depth, or other pixel-level tasks. The zero-shot result—strong reasoning-segmentation performance from training exclusively on reasoning-free data—suggests that LLMs' world knowledge transfers to visual grounding without task-specific supervision, a finding with broad implications for how we think about LLM capability transfer. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object or panoptic settings, whether the 13B-over-7B gap indicates a fundamental reasoning bottleneck in current LLMs, and whether the small ReasonSeg benchmark (1,218 samples) is sufficient to validate the claimed generalization.
