## Problem

Unsigned Distance Fields (UDFs) are non-differentiable at the zero level set, causing neural networks to produce inaccurate distances and ill-defined gradients near the surface, which leads to fragmented and discontinuous reconstructions of open surfaces. Signed distance approaches avoid this non-differentiability but are inherently limited to closed surfaces. No prior work has successfully formulated a differentiable Eikonal problem that accurately approximates unsigned distance functions while preserving open-surface representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an oriented point cloud (100k–200k surface points with associated normals) as supervision and evaluates the network at arbitrary 3D spatial coordinates (x, y, z) to produce the hyperbolic-scaled distance value t_S. The learned field exhibits quadratic behavior near the isosurface and linear growth far from it, controlled by a scaling parameter α. From this single scalar field, the method extracts 3D meshes via gradient-based Marching Cubes (recovering true distance by dividing by α and taking the square root), computes unoriented surface normals as the principal Hessian eigenvector, derives mean and Gaussian curvature via divergence and determinant formulas, and renders directly via sphere tracing without an intermediate mesh.

## Method

The key insight is to learn a hyperbolic scaling of the unsigned distance, t_S(x) = d_S(x)·tanh(α·d_S(x)), which is differentiable everywhere including at the zero level set where the gradient vanishes. This defines a new heterogeneous Eikonal equation ‖∇f‖ = φ(x) with boundary conditions f|_S = 0 and ∇f|_S = 0, where φ is the analytically computed L2 norm of the gradient of t_S. The network is an 8-layer 256-unit MLP with sine (SIREN) activations, trained with a composite loss combining the Eikonal residual, Dirichlet values, Neumann (zero-gradient) constraint, and a maximum-curvature alignment term that enforces the principal Hessian eigenvector to match surface normals. A second refinement stage minimizes the mean and standard deviation of network outputs at the isosurface to suppress residual oscillations. Because the gradient is zero at the surface, the method bypasses the implicit function theorem, enabling representation of sharp features and non-orientable open surfaces that SIREN-based SDF methods cannot capture.

## Results

On the open-surface benchmarks, DUDF (MC1) achieves L1 Chamfer distances of 9.01, 8.70, and 12.3 (×10³) on DeepFashion, Multi-Garment, and ShapeNet cars respectively, with L2 Chamfer distances of 0.025, 0.024, and 0.057. Training time is approximately 317–326 seconds across all three datasets, roughly 4× faster than CAP-UDF (1040–1440 s) and comparable to or faster than SIREN (374–751 s). Normal Consistency scores are 0.024, 0.026, and 0.387, substantially outperforming both baselines on DeepFashion and Multi-Garment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIREN (signed distance), DUDF dominates on every metric across all three datasets: L1 Chamfer distance improves by 3–4× (e.g., 9.01 vs 27.3 on DeepFashion, 8.70 vs 40.5 on Multi-Garment), and training time is comparable or lower. Against CAP-UDF (the primary open-surface baseline), DUDF achieves roughly 2× lower L1CD and 40–45× lower L2CD on DeepFashion and Multi-Garment, while training ~4× faster (326 s vs 1390 s). On ShapeNet cars, CAP-UDF retains a slight edge in L1CD (10.9 vs 12.3) and Normal Consistency (0.288 vs 0.387), but DUDF still wins on L2CD (0.057 vs 0.071) and is 3× faster. The ablation study further confirms that directly learning the raw unsigned distance d_S (with either sine or ReLU activations) degrades L1CD to 31.2 or 47.5, validating the hyperbolic scaling formulation as essential to the performance gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SIREN architecture (periodic activation functions for implicit neural representations) and the Eikonal-equation formulation used for signed distance fields in closed-surface reconstruction. It departs from the UDF line of work (NDF, CAP-UDF) by identifying the non-differentiability at the zero level set as the core obstacle and resolving it through a hyperbolic (tanh) scaling that transforms the UDF into a differentiable function with a modified Eikonal problem. The key conceptual departure from CAP-UDF is reframing the problem as a heterogeneous Eikonal equation with tailored boundary conditions (zero gradient at the surface, maximum-curvature alignment with normals) rather than a field-consistency optimization on raw point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the unsigned distance field differentiable at the isosurface, DUDF unlocks direct computation of normals, mean curvature, and Gaussian curvature during inference without an intermediate mesh reconstruction step, which is valuable for rendering and downstream geometric processing. The order-of-magnitude speed improvement over CAP-UDF (≈320 s vs ≈1400 s) makes neural UDF training more practical. Open questions remain: the Eikonal loss still requires ground-truth distance supervision (unlike SDFs where the norm is constant), the quadratic near-isosurface behavior can slightly inflate reconstructions, and Hessian-based curvature computation is computationally expensive (three forward passes plus eigen-decomposition). Whether alternative scaling functions or tangency-aware reconstruction strategies can further close the gap to SIREN-level smoothness on closed shapes is left for future work.
