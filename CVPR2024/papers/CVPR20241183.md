## Problem

Selecting the optimal pre-trained model for a downstream task requires ranking models by their post-fine-tuning performance, but brute-force fine-tuning is computationally prohibitive. Existing transferability metrics either treat network outputs as static or simulate fine-tuning dynamics in feature space via linear transformations, which misalign with the actual classification objective and cannot capture the nonlinear changes induced by gradient-based optimization.

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained model's backbone features on downstream images and their class labels, LEAD computes per-class NTK matrices (using S=64 samples per class) and initial logits via a multi-class SVM classifier. The output is a predicted final-state logit vector per sample, from which a Cross-Entropy loss score is derived as the transferability metric; lower scores indicate better predicted transferability, and models are ranked by this score using weighted Kendall's tau against ground-truth fine-tuning accuracy.

## Method

LEAD models the logit evolution during fine-tuning as a continuous dynamical system by converting the discrete gradient-descent update into an ordinary differential equation (ODE) of the form dF/dt = -η·Φ·dL/dF, where Φ is the Neural Tangent Kernel. Leveraging NTK's constant-preserving property in the infinite-width limit, the ODE admits a closed-form solution that interpolates between one-hot labels and initial logits with exponential decay coefficients determined by Φ. To make this practical for finite-width networks, a class-aware decomposition computes per-class NTK eigenvalues and uses their average as the convergence rate, decoupling the optimization dynamics across classes with different difficulty. The predicted final-state logits are then fed into Cross-Entropy loss to produce a single-step transferability score, bypassing the entire fine-tuning process.

## Results

On the supervised model zoo (12 ImageNet-pretrained CNNs), LEAD achieves an average weighted Kendall's τ_w of 0.747 across 10 downstream datasets, outperforming the previous SOTA ETran by 18% relative. LEAD attains the best τ_w on 7 of 10 datasets, with particularly large gains on Flowers (0.786 vs. 0.534 for ETran) and Food-101 (0.892 vs. 0.844 for GBC). On the self-supervised model zoo (12 SSL models), LEAD achieves an average τ_w of 0.746, a 15% relative improvement over PED (0.648), with consistent gains on 7 datasets. In the low-data regime (N/K=10), LEAD reaches 0.64 average τ_w on the supervised zoo, matching full-data SOTA performance with less than 5% of the data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAD outperforms the two most recent simulation-based methods (PED and SFDA) on 8 of 10 supervised datasets, with the largest margins on Flowers (+0.335 over PED), Food-101 (+0.319), and Pets (+0.216), where the linear-transformation approximation in those methods breaks down. Against the prior SOTA ETran (ICCV'23), LEAD achieves a 18% relative average τ_w improvement (0.747 vs. ~0.633), winning on 7 datasets but trailing on Cars (0.586 vs. 0.557 is actually a win; the losses are on SUN397 where Nleep leads at 0.770 vs. LEAD's 0.609). GBC (CVPR'22) remains stronger on two fine-grained datasets—Cars (0.735 vs. 0.586) and CIFAR-10 (0.866 vs. 0.791)—where its Bhattacharyya separability measure captures class geometry that the logit-evolution model underestimates. Nleep (CVPR'21) still leads on SUN397 (0.770 vs. 0.609), a scene-classification dataset with 397 coarse categories. On the self-supervised zoo, LEAD's advantage over PED widens to 15% relative (0.746 vs. 0.648), and methods that ignore fine-tuning dynamics (PARC, Nleep) degrade to negative τ_w on VOC2007 and CIFAR-100, whereas LEAD maintains positive correlations across all 10 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Neural Tangent Kernel framework (Jacot et al., NeurIPS 2018) to derive a closed-form ODE describing logit evolution during fine-tuning, departing from prior transferability metrics that either treat network outputs as static (RSA, Logme, Nleep, GBC) or simulate dynamics in feature space via linear transformations (SFDA, PED). The key conceptual shift is moving the analysis from feature space to logit space, where the optimization objective (cross-entropy) is directly aligned and the NTK naturally captures nonlinear gradient dynamics. The class-aware decomposition further bridges the infinite-width NTK assumption to practical finite-width networks by using per-class eigenvalue averages as convergence rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEAD provides a theoretically grounded, single-step prediction of final-state logits that bypasses the costly iterative fine-tuning process, making model selection practical for large model zoos. Its robustness in extreme low-data regimes (N/K=2) where competing methods like PED and SFDA completely fail to produce valid rankings is a practical differentiator for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the NTK-based ODE framework extends beyond classification to detection or segmentation tasks, whether the SVM-based log_init computation can be replaced with lighter alternatives, and whether the class-aware decomposition generalizes to very fine-grained or imbalanced class distributions.
