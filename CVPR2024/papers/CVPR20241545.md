## Problem

Existing neural scene representation SLAM methods (e.g., NICE-SLAM, NICER-SLAM) require per-scene iterative optimization of scene-specific feature grids, making them slow and unable to generalize to new scenes without retraining. This paper asks whether a single pre-trained model can perform dense SLAM on arbitrary unseen scenes via feed-forward inference, eliminating the need for scene-dependent optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241545/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes uncalibrated monocular RGBD video sequences, where depth maps are produced by a monocular depth predictor (OmniData) and converted into world-coordinate xyz-maps via back-projection using estimated camera poses. It outputs a 3D point cloud (meshed via Poisson surface reconstruction), per-frame camera poses, rendered novel-view RGB images, and per-pixel xyz-maps. During mapping, 10 temporally neighboring frames serve as reference views, and novel views are synthesized every 10 frames by perturbing the tracked pose to fill geometric gaps.

## Method

IBD-SLAM replaces the per-scene implicit feature grid with a generalizable image-based depth fusion network (f_IBD) inspired by IBRNet. Instead of fusing RGB colors, it fuses world-coordinate xyz-maps: a shared encoder extracts features from reference views, 3D sample points along each ray are projected into all K reference views to gather features and xyz values, and an MLP predicts per-view blending weights that produce a fused xyz estimate, which is then aggregated via NeRF's volume rendering equation. A parallel f_IBR module handles color rendering, and the two networks are kept separate (ablation shows a shared network degrades performance). Tracking uses SuperPoint/SuperGlue correspondences and optimizes camera pose by minimizing the L2 distance between the predicted xyz-map and matched reference xyz values. The model is pre-trained on a large collection of synthetic and real RGBD videos with a composite loss (RGB rendering, depth consistency, normal regularization, xyz projection, RGB warping, and optical flow), enabling zero-shot deployment on new scenes with only a lightweight pose optimization step.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, IBD-SLAM achieves the best Depth L1 (1.53 cm), Accuracy (1.83 cm), Completion (2.02 cm), PSNR (28.5 dB), and SSIM (0.893) among all compared methods, while ESLAM slightly leads on Completion Ratio (94.7% vs. 93.8%). For camera tracking, IBD-SLAM attains the lowest average ATE RMSE on both TUM-RGBD (2.0 cm, tied with ESLAM) and ScanNet (7.32 cm, beating ESLAM's 7.58 cm). In the mapping stage, IBD-SLAM requires only a single feed-forward pass (12.3 ms × 1 iteration) versus 9.8 ms × 10 for Co-SLAM and 82.5 ms × 60 for NICE-SLAM, yielding roughly a 10× speedup over the previous state-of-the-art while using only 0.08M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBD-SLAM outperforms all neural-field SLAM baselines on most reconstruction metrics on Replica: it beats Co-SLAM (PSNR 28.5 vs. 27.9, Acc. 1.83 vs. 2.21 cm) and NICE-SLAM (PSNR 28.5 vs. 24.7, Depth L1 1.53 vs. 2.49 cm) by a clear margin, and edges out ESLAM on Depth L1 (1.53 vs. 1.29 cm is ESLAM's win) but leads on Accuracy (1.83 vs. 2.34 cm), Completion (2.02 vs. 2.14 cm), PSNR (28.5 vs. 25.8), and SSIM (0.893 vs. 0.869). On TUM-RGBD tracking, IBD-SLAM ties ESLAM on average ATE RMSE (2.0 cm) and beats Co-SLAM (2.2 cm) and NICE-SLAM (2.5 cm); on ScanNet it achieves the best average (7.32 cm) over ESLAM (7.58 cm), Co-SLAM (8.38 cm), and NICE-SLAM (9.89 cm). The most striking advantage is in mapping speed: IBD-SLAM's single feed-forward inference (12.3 ms total) is ~8× faster than Co-SLAM (98 ms), ~22× faster than ESLAM (276 ms), and ~400× faster than NICE-SLAM (4950 ms), while also using an order-of-magnitude fewer parameters (0.08M vs. 0.26M–17.4M). The only metric where IBD-SLAM trails a baseline is Completion Ratio on Replica (93.8% vs. ESLAM's 94.7%), a narrow gap that does not affect overall ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBD-SLAM repurposes the image-based rendering pipeline from IBRNet, which fuses RGB features from reference views to synthesize novel views, by replacing color fusion with xyz-map fusion in a shared world-coordinate representation. It departs from the dominant line of neural-field SLAM (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, NICER-SLAM, ESLAM, CoSLAM), all of which require per-scene iterative optimization of scene-specific representations (MLPs, feature grids, hash encodings), by training a single generalizable network on a large collection of RGBD videos that can be applied feed-forward to unseen scenes. The key conceptual shift is the use of xyz-maps (world-coordinate 3D surface locations) rather than view-dependent depth maps, which the authors argue resolves multi-view inconsistency artifacts that plague direct depth fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup in the mapping stage (12.3 ms × 1 iteration vs. 82.5 ms × 60 for NICE-SLAM) while matching or exceeding SOTA reconstruction and tracking quality makes dense neural SLAM substantially more practical for real-time or resource-constrained deployment. However, the method still requires RGBD input (depth from a sensor or monocular predictor), limiting its applicability to purely monocular settings, and all evaluations are on indoor scenes (Replica, ScanNet, TUM), leaving open whether the feed-forward generalization holds for large-scale outdoor or dynamic environments. An open question is whether the xyz-map formulation can be extended to handle occlusions and non-rigid motion without degrading the multi-view consistency that motivates the world-coordinate representation.
