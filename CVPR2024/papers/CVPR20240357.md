## Problem

Differentially private SGD (DP-SGD) significantly degrades image classification accuracy, and while in-distribution (ID) public data can help, prior work has focused on using the limited public subset (e.g., 4% of training data) to guide optimization rather than to amplify the data itself. The paper identifies that with such a small public set, the critical bottlenecks are insufficient data diversity in any synthetic augmentation and a generalization gap between the public/synthetic distribution and the private distribution, both of which have not been systematically addressed.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 32×32 RGB images in two stages: first, an EDM diffusion model is trained on a 2K-sample in-distribution public subset (4% of the training set, uniformly drawn per class) to synthesize 50K class-conditional images; second, a classifier (WRN-16-4 or a pre-trained ViT) is warm-up trained on the synthetic set before DP-SGD fine-tuning on the remaining private data. The final output is a standard class label prediction, with privacy guaranteed at (ε, 10⁻⁵)-DP over the private training phase only.

## Method

The framework has three components applied sequentially during the warm-up phase before DP-SGD. (1) Synthesis: EDM replaces DDPM as the generative backbone, producing higher-fidelity images (FID 7.80 vs. 12.80) from the same 2K public samples. (2) Diversity: discriminator guidance (DG) with a tunable weight w_d increases recall of generated samples, and Cutout + Common augmentation further diversifies the synthetic set during classifier warm-up, yielding the largest single accuracy gain. (3) Optimization: SAM is applied during warm-up to find flat minima in the loss landscape, which reduces gradient norms on both synthetic and private data and narrows the public-private generalization gap. A supporting theorem (Theorem 3.1) formalizes why, with a small seen-to-entire data ratio r₁, the model must actively generate unseen data (high p̂_θ^u) rather than merely fitting the seen distribution to approximate the full data manifold.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with (2, 10⁻⁵)-DP, the proposed method (EDM synthesis + Cutout augmentation + SAM optimization) achieves 85.48% test accuracy, outperforming the previous SOTA of 75.1% (Nasr et al.) by over 10 percentage points. Across the full privacy budget range, the method achieves 84.30% (ε=1), 86.03% (ε=3), 86.49% (ε=4), and 87.06% (ε=6). On CIFAR-100 without pre-trained models, the method reaches 48.61% at ε=2, and with ImageNet-pretrained vision transformers, it achieves up to 83.06% (DeiT-base) at ε=2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all baselines across every privacy budget tested. On CIFAR-10 at ε=2, it achieves 85.48%, surpassing the strongest prior method (Nasr et al. [43], WarmSyn with DDPM + DOPE-SGD) by 10.38 points, Mirror GD with synthesis [2] by 14.98 points, and the cold-start WRN-16-4 baseline [12] by 20.58 points. The gap is even more pronounced at tighter budgets: at ε=1, the method reaches 84.30% while no prior public-data method reports a result at this level. On CIFAR-100 at ε=2, the method (48.61%) outperforms the cold-start baseline (18.19%) by over 30 points and the ResNet-9 baseline from Knolle et al. [35] (24.9%) by 23.7 points. Notably, the extended optimization methods (Mirror GD, DOPE-SGD) provide no additional benefit on top of the proposed synthesis + augmentation + SAM pipeline, with their results falling within the standard deviation of plain DP-SGD (Table 11), suggesting that the warm-up phase already extracts sufficient side information from public data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WarmSyn framework of Nasr et al. [43], which first introduced using a generative model (DDPM) to amplify limited in-distribution public data for DP-SGD warm-up training. The key departure is shifting focus from optimization-side tricks (DOPE-SGD, Mirror GD, low-rank gradient estimation) to the data side: replacing DDPM with EDM for higher-fidelity synthesis, then identifying data diversity and the public-private generalization gap as the critical bottlenecks. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) on seen/unseen data ratios provides a principled justification for why diversity in generation matters when public data is scarce, complementing the empirical findings from discriminator guidance [32] and SAM [20] applied during warm-up.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10+ point accuracy gain over prior SOTA (75.1% → 85.48% on CIFAR-10 at ε=2) demonstrates that the utility-privacy trade-off in DP image classification can be substantially narrowed by investing in data synthesis quality rather than optimization complexity. The finding that extended optimization methods (DOPE-SGD, Mirror GD) become redundant once public data is properly synthesized and augmented challenges the assumption that gradient-side tricks are necessary, simplifying the practical pipeline. Open questions include scaling to more sensitive or larger datasets, reducing the computational cost of training diffusion models per dataset, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks beyond image classification (e.g., language, tabular data).
