## Problem

Existing depth completion methods (both 1-stage and 2-stage) directly apply spatially invariant convolutions on irregularly sampled sparse depth maps, introducing ambiguity between valid and invalid measurements and degrading multi-modal fusion performance. While propagation-based post-processing has become the dominant refinement strategy, it still relies on an initial dense depth produced by convolving on sparse data, leaving the fundamental sparsity issue unresolved at the earliest processing stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242243/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a synchronized RGB image and a sparse depth map (typically from LiDAR or SfM, with irregularly distributed valid points) and produces a dense per-pixel depth map. On NYUv2, inputs are 320×256 (padded from 304×228) with 500 randomly sampled depth points; on KITTI, training uses 1216×256 crops while testing uses full-resolution frames. The sparse depth is consumed by the bilateral propagation module which selects only the N=4 nearest valid neighbors per target pixel, rather than treating the full map as a regular tensor.

## Method

BP-Net is a 3-stage, 6-scale (coarse-to-fine) architecture where each scale performs: (1) a bilateral propagation pre-processing stage that explicitly propagates depth from the 4 nearest valid sparse measurements using a non-linear combination model D'_i = Σ ω_ij(α_ij·S_j + β_ij), with coefficients generated by a 4-layer MLP conditioned on image encodings at both target and source pixels, depth encoding, and 2D spatial offset; (2) a U-Net multi-modal fusion stage (early fusion of image and inverse-projected depth features) that predicts a residual depth; and (3) a CSPN++-style iterative propagation refinement with multi-kernel (3,5,7) and multi-step combination. The bilateral propagation is fully differentiable and trained end-to-end with a multi-scale L2 loss (λ_s = 4^{-s}), and the key design choice is performing propagation at the earliest stage so that subsequent convolutional stages operate on a dense, content-aware depth rather than raw sparse data.

## Results

On the KITTI depth completion benchmark, BP-Net achieves the best RMSE of 684.90 mm, outperforming the previous best (LRRU at 696.51 mm) by 11.6 mm, and ranks 1st on the public leaderboard at the time of submission. On NYUv2, BP-Net achieves the best RMSE of 0.089 m (tied with BEV@DC) and the best δ₁.₂₅ of 99.6%. The method also reports competitive iRMSE (1.82 1/km) on KITTI, though it trails LRRU and BEV@DC on MAE (194.69 vs. 189.96/189.44 mm) and iMAE (0.84 vs. 0.81/0.82 1/km).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BP-Net achieves the best KITTI RMSE (684.90 mm), beating the closest competitors LRRU (696.51 mm) and BEV@DC (697.44 mm) by approximately 11–13 mm, and also leads on KITTI iRMSE (1.82 vs. 1.83/1.87 1/km). However, it does not dominate all KITTI metrics: LRRU and BEV@DC achieve lower MAE (189.96/189.44 vs. 194.69 mm) and iMAE (0.81/0.82 vs. 0.84 1/km). On NYUv2, BP-Net ties BEV@DC for the best RMSE (0.089 m) and matches multiple methods (NLSPN, RigNet, DySPN, BEV@DC, LRRU) at δ₁.₂₅ = 99.6%, while LRRU edges ahead on REL (0.011 vs. 0.012). Compared to earlier propagation-based methods like CSPN++ (743.69 mm) and NLSPN (741.68 mm), BP-Net provides a substantial 50–59 mm RMSE improvement on KITTI. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that the pre-processing bilateral propagation stage contributes 2.12 mm RMSE reduction over MF-only, outperforming the post-processing refinement stage (1.68 mm) at lower computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

BP-Net extends the depth completion pipeline from the dominant 2-stage paradigm (multi-modal fusion followed by propagation-based refinement, as in CSPN, CSPN++, NLSPN, and DySPN) to a 3-stage design that inserts a bilateral propagation pre-processing stage before the fusion network. The propagation mechanism draws directly from classical bilateral filtering, but replaces hand-crafted kernels with an MLP that generates content- and spatial-distance-dependent coefficients, and the refinement stage reuses the CSPN++ iterative propagation module. The multi-modal fusion itself is deliberately kept simple (a U-Net in early-fusion scheme), positioning the contribution squarely in the sparse-data-processing stage rather than in fusion or refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that propagating depth at the earliest stage—before the convolutional fusion network ever sees the sparse input—is more effective and cheaper than the refinement-stage propagation that has dominated recent SOTA methods, as shown by the ablation where Pre.+MF. outperforms MF.+Post. at lower compute cost. This reframes where in the pipeline the sparsity problem should be addressed. The authors acknowledge that BP-Net's reliance on local propagation and convolution limits long-range information delivery, and suggest combining it with Transformer-based fusion or non-local propagation as future directions. The method's strong generalization across sparsity levels (250–750 points) makes it practically useful for real LiDAR/SfM pipelines where point density varies.
