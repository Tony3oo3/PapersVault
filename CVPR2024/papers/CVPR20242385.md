## Problem

Existing Test Time Adaptation methods (normalization-based, entropy-based, pseudo-labeling-based) fail to address the root cause of degraded generalization under distribution shift: the model's reliance on the marginal distribution of training data. This unaddressed covariate shift impairs model calibration and introduces confirmation bias, yet prior TTA approaches lack any direct connection to marginal distributions. The paper asks whether an energy-based reformulation can inject test distribution perception into a frozen classifier without access to training data or process.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained CNN classifier (WRN-28-10, ResNet-50, or ResNet-18) and a stream of unlabeled test images, updating only the normalization layer parameters (~1% of total parameters) via Adam. The energy function is computed as the negative log-sum-exp of the classifier's logits, and SGLD generates negative samples by iteratively following the negative energy gradient from an initial noise distribution p₀. Output is the adapted classifier producing class predictions, with the side effect that the model's implicit distribution p_θ(x) gradually aligns with the test distribution.

## Method

TEA reinterprets a trained classifier as an Energy-Based Model by defining the energy function E_θ(x) = −log Σ_y exp(f_θ(x)[y]), i.e., the negative log-sum-exp of logits. The adaptation objective is Contrastive Divergence: minimize the energy of incoming test samples while simultaneously maximizing the energy of negative samples generated by T steps of Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) from the model's own distribution. This min-max formulation prevents the trivial collapse of uniformly lowering energy across the entire input space, instead focusing likelihood increase on the target test distribution. Only normalization layer parameters are updated, leveraging their direct influence on data distribution statistics. Unlike entropy-based methods that apply softmax in label space and reduce calibration, TEA operates in data space via log-sum-exp, introducing per-class uncertainty that empirically improves both generalization and confidence calibration.

## Results

On image corruption (WRN-28-10, BatchNorm), TEA achieves 83.34% accuracy at severity 5 on CIFAR-10(C) and 65.10% on CIFAR-100(C), outperforming the best baseline (TENT at 81.41% and 63.09%) by an average of 4.7% at the most severe level. On Tiny-ImageNet(C), TEA reaches 51.65% severity-5 accuracy, a substantial margin over TENT (39.54%). For domain generalization on PACS (ResNet-18), TEA improves average accuracy by 7.1% (Photo source) and 8.5% (Art source) over the best baseline, and uniquely maintains gains in the challenging Sketch source setting where all baselines degrade.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEA outperforms all seven baselines on the vast majority of corruption settings. Against the strongest entropy-based baseline TENT, TEA gains +1.93% on CIFAR-10(C) severity 5 (83.34 vs 81.41) and +2.01% on CIFAR-100(C) (65.10 vs 63.09), with a dramatic +12.11% on Tiny-ImageNet(C) (51.65 vs 39.54). The normalization-based BN method is competitive on CIFAR-10(C) (79.56 vs 83.34) but trails TEA by 5–10 points on the other two datasets. Pseudo-labeling methods PL and SHOT are consistently weaker: PL degrades below source on CIFAR-10(C) severity 5 (51.42 vs 56.47), while SHOT reaches only 74.77% vs TEA's 83.34%. On domain generalization (PACS), TEA beats the best baseline SHOT by 1.84% (Photo source), 3.22% (Art source), 2.28% (Cartoon source), and 1.70% (Sketch source) in average accuracy; notably, in the Sketch source setting where TENT drops 14.73% below source, TEA still improves by 4.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEA builds on the energy-based model reinterpretation of classifiers established by Grathwohl et al. (JEM), which showed that any discriminative model implicitly defines an EBM via its logits. It departs from the three established TTA families—normalization-based (BN, DUA), entropy-based (TENT, EATA, SAR), and pseudo-labeling-based (PL, SHOT)—by introducing an energy-based objective (contrastive divergence with SGLD-generated negatives) that directly targets covariate shift, a gap the authors argue all prior TTA methods leave unaddressed. The method also draws a conceptual link to entropy minimization through the convex conjugacy between negative entropy and log-sum-exp, positioning TEA as operating in data space rather than label space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TEA offers a principled mechanism for TTA that simultaneously improves generalization and confidence calibration, addressing a known weakness of entropy-based methods that overconfidently sharpen class probabilities. The energy-based framing provides a new design axis for future TTA work beyond the current normalization/entropy/pseudo-label trichotomy. However, experiments are confined to small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet, PACS) with modest architectures (WRN-28-10, ResNet-18/50), leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level or foundation-model settings. The reliance on multi-step SGLD sampling for negative generation also raises practical efficiency questions for high-dimensional inputs.
