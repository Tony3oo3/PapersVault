## Problem

Existing text-guided 3D scene editing methods (e.g., Instruct-NeRF2NeRF) rely on 2D diffusion models that apply edits globally to the entire rendered image, making localized and delicate editing of specific regions unreliable. Additionally, NeRF-based representations introduce coupling effects between spatial positions, further hindering precise region control. This paper addresses both limitations by exploiting the explicit, per-Gaussian structure of 3D Gaussian Splatting to confine edits to precisely identified regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241195/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian scene (N Gaussians with position, covariance, color, and opacity), a free-form text instruction that may be indirect or relational (e.g., 'Turn the thing next to the bike orange'), and optionally user-provided 3D boxes or point selections to refine the editing region. The output is a fully edited 3D Gaussian scene that preserves all non-target regions exactly and can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints. Training completes in under 20 minutes on a single V100 GPU.

## Method

GaussianEditor is a three-stage optimization pipeline. First, a scene description is generated by rendering multiple views, captioning them with BLIP-2, and merging captions via GPT-3.5; the LLM then extracts a text RoI from the instruction given the scene context. Second, the text RoI is grounded to an image mask via Grounding DINO + SAM, then lifted to 3D by training a binary attribute r on each Gaussian (initialized to 0) with a projection loss that encourages the rendered RoI to match the image mask; users can further refine the 3D RoI with add/delete selections and 3D bounding boxes. Third, InstructPix2Pix edits randomly sampled rendered views conditioned on the original scene image and text instruction, and the L1 + D-SSIM loss between edited and rendered images is backpropagated only to Gaussians whose r attribute is nonzero (via element-wise multiplication of gradients with the RoI mask R), ensuring that only the target region is modified. This per-Gaussian gradient masking is the key mechanism that enables precise, localized editing impossible with implicit NeRF representations.

## Results

On the bicycle scene from Mip-NeRF360, GaussianEditor (3DGS) achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CTIDS 0.28, IIS 0.95, FID 51, and training time of 20 minutes. This outperforms Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (CTIDS 0.22, IIS 0.85, FID 103, 51 min) and the DVGO-based variant (CTIDS 0.11, IIS 0.82, FID 148, 40 min). In a user study with 21 participants, GaussianEditor received 87.07% of votes versus 12.93% for IN2N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (IN2N), the leading text-instruction 3D scene editing method at the time. GaussianEditor wins on every reported metric: it improves CTIDS from 0.22 to 0.28 (a 27% relative gain), IIS from 0.85 to 0.95, and reduces FID from 103 to 51 (roughly halving the distribution distance). Training time drops from 51 minutes to 20 minutes, more than 2× faster. Qualitatively, IN2N tends to alter unintended regions (e.g., distorting faces or bleeding edits into the background), whereas GaussianEditor preserves non-target areas thanks to its Gaussian RoI constraint. A secondary ablation variant using DVGO instead of 3DGS underperforms on all metrics, confirming that the explicit per-Gaussian control is essential for delicate localized editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-guided 3D scene editing line initiated by Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (IN2N), which applied 2D diffusion models (InstructPix2Pix) to edit NeRF representations but suffered from global, uncontrolled changes. The key conceptual departure is leveraging the explicit, per-Gaussian structure of 3D Gaussian Splatting to localize edits spatially, rather than relying on implicit NeRF fields where different spatial positions are coupled through shared MLPs or voxel vertices. The RoI lifting mechanism adapts the projection loss from SA3D, and the overall pipeline integrates BLIP-2, GPT-3.5, Grounding DINO, and SAM as off-the-shelf submodules into a coherent editing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GaussianEditor demonstrates that the explicit nature of 3D Gaussians makes precise, localized 3D scene editing tractable in a way that was fundamentally difficult with NeRF, and it does so with a 2×+ speedup over IN2N (20 min vs. 45 min–2 h). However, the system remains limited to appearance-level edits (color, texture) and cannot handle drastic geometric changes where grounding segmentation or the diffusion model fails. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes (as 3D-GS has been extended to), how to resolve cross-view description inconsistencies in the scene-description module, and whether the LLM-based RoI extraction generalizes to highly ambiguous or compositional instructions.
