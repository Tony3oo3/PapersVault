## Problem

Post-Training Sparsity (PTS) methods, which recover sparse network performance using only a small calibration set, suffer severe degradation at high sparsity rates—POT drops to 3.9% Top-1 accuracy on ResNet-50 at 90% sparsity, near random—while traditional full-data sparsity methods retain reasonable accuracy. The paper identifies three root causes: a localized layer-wise MSE objective that accumulates bias, a fixed or heuristic sparsity distribution that cannot adapt to the limited data, and a static sparse structure that fails to explore alternative weight configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241798/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained dense network (ResNet-18/50, MobileNet-V2, VGGNet-16, MobileNet-V1), a small calibration set of 10,240 images (from ImageNet-1K or PASCAL VOC), and a target global sparsity rate P. It outputs a sparse network defined by a binary mask M over all weights plus an optimized per-layer sparsity distribution {r^l}. The calibration set is used both for the evolutionary fitness evaluation (with added Gaussian noise to prevent overfitting) and for the final dynamic sparse training, requiring no access to the full training data.

## Method

UniPTS unifies three modifications to the PTS pipeline. First, it replaces POT's layer-wise MSE with a global base-decayed KL divergence between dense and sparse output distributions, where the log base γ^t decays over epochs to smoothly transition from strong to weak supervision. Second, it searches the optimal layer-wise sparsity distribution via an evolutionary algorithm that first over-prunes all layers to P_e > P (reducing search space), then regrows weights proportionally to candidate ratios, evaluating fitness with noise-perturbed calibration accuracy and recalibrated BN statistics. Third, it performs dynamic sparse training with iteration-wise mask updates (ΔT=1) using weight magnitude for both pruning and regrowth, a Straight-Through Estimator for gradient flow through pruned weights, and a decay factor α on pruned weight updates to constrain structural fluctuation while preserving exploration diversity.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, UniPTS achieves 68.60% top-1 accuracy for ResNet-50 at 90% sparsity, dramatically outperforming POT (3.90%), RigL (61.68%), and STR (30.24%). For MobileNet-V2 at 90% sparsity, UniPTS reaches 42.46% versus POT's 0.2% and RigL's 30.44%. On PASCAL VOC object detection at 90% sparsity, UniPTS improves mAP by 3.3 points for Faster-RCNN (54.59 vs. 51.29) and 7.4 points for SSD (64.42 vs. 57.02) over POT. UniPTS also achieves superior pruning efficiency, completing ResNet-50 pruning in 317 minutes versus POT's 484 minutes while delivering far higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPTS consistently outperforms all three baselines across every sparsity rate and architecture tested. Against POT (the sole established PTS method), the gap is most dramatic at 90% sparsity: UniPTS lifts ResNet-50 from 3.90% to 68.60% and MobileNet-V2 from 0.20% to 42.46%, indicating POT's layer-wise MSE objective collapses under high sparsity. Against RigL (a classical dynamic sparse training method applied in a post-training setting), UniPTS leads by 6.92 points on ResNet-50 at 90% sparsity (68.60 vs. 61.68) and by 12.02 points on MobileNet-V2 (42.46 vs. 30.44), confirming that dynamic sparse training with the proposed base-decayed objective and iteration-wise mask updates is critical. Against STR (a differentiable sparsity distribution search), UniPTS dominates by a wide margin (e.g., 68.60 vs. 30.24 on ResNet-50 at 90%), demonstrating that differentiable training for sparsity distribution is impractical in data-limited PTS scenarios. On object detection, UniPTS beats POT by 3.3 mAP on Faster-RCNN and 7.4 mAP on SSD at 90% sparsity, confirming generalization beyond classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends POT (Lazarevich et al., CVPR 2021), the first post-training sparsity method, by replacing its layer-wise MSE calibration with a global base-decayed KL divergence objective, adding an evolutionary search for layer-wise sparsity distribution, and substituting fixed-structure retraining with a modified dynamic sparse training scheme. The dynamic training component draws on RigL (Evci et al., ICML 2020) but departs from it by using weight magnitude for both pruning and regrowth (rather than gradient-based regrowth), updating masks every iteration instead of periodically, and introducing a decay term on pruned weights to stabilize training. Conceptually, the paper unifies three previously independent design axes—sparsity objective, sparsity distribution, and sparsity structure—into a single framework tailored to the data-limited PTS setting, where traditional full-data sparsity methods (e.g., STR, RigL) degrade substantially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that PTS, long overshadowed by post-training quantization, can achieve near-full-data sparsity performance at high sparsity rates (e.g., 68.6% vs. 76.12% dense for ResNet-50 at 90% sparsity) using only 10K calibration images, making network compression viable in data-constrained deployment scenarios. The finding that iteration-wise mask updates outperform periodic updates in PTS—contrary to standard DST practice—highlights that data-limited settings demand fundamentally different training dynamics. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CNNs on ImageNet and PASCAL VOC, and the evolutionary search, while faster than POT, still adds non-trivial overhead (260–357 min). Open questions include whether the framework extends to transformers, higher sparsity regimes (>95%), and whether the base-decay schedule generalizes across architectures without manual tuning.
