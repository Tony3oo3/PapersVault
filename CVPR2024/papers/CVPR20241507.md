## Problem

Few-shot NeRFs struggle in 360° outward-facing indoor scenes where sparse views exhibit large viewpoint variations, severely reducing inter-view consistency. Existing approaches either rely on general monocular depth models whose inherent geometry ambiguity prevents reliable global consistency, or on in-domain trained depth networks that sacrifice generalization to unseen scenes. The paper addresses how to extract both global and hierarchical geometry consistency priors from pretrained models to overcome these limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241507/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 18–20 sparse posed RGB images per scene (ScanNet) or 20 images sampled at 100-frame intervals (Replica), along with SfM camera poses, DPT monocular depth predictions, and LoFTR image-matching correspondences. Training proceeds in two phases: a short warm-up phase (first 1536 iterations, applied every 3rd iteration) that uses only the matching-based keypoint depth prior, followed by full training with all losses active. The output is a trained Mip-NeRF radiance field that renders novel-view images and depth maps from arbitrary camera poses.

## Method

P²NeRF builds on a Mip-NeRF backbone and introduces two complementary geometry consistency priors. First, a matching-based global geometry warm-up uses LoFTR to find sparse correspondences across view pairs, triangulates a coarse point cloud, and applies a keypoint depth loss (L_kpts) that maximizes ray weights within a window around the triangulated depth while suppressing weights outside it, preventing early geometric collapse. Second, two hierarchical priors derived from DPT monocular depth are applied throughout training: a group depth ranking loss (L_rank) that partitions pixels into 32 percentile-based depth groups and penalizes pairwise depth-ordering violations across groups, and a ray weight mask regularization (L_mask) that constructs binary masks on each ray to suppress weight in regions where the surface provably cannot exist, thereby pushing the rendered depth toward the correct direction. The total loss combines photometric color loss with these three geometric terms weighted by λ₀=0.1, λ₁=0.02, and λ₂=0.002.

## Results

On ScanNet (18–20 training views), P²NeRF achieves the best performance across all four metrics: PSNR 21.03, SSIM 0.719, LPIPS 0.209, and depth RMSE 0.213, outperforming the closest competitor DS-NeRF (PSNR 20.85, RMSE 0.447) by a notable margin in geometric accuracy. On the synthetic Replica dataset (20 training views), P²NeRF attains the best PSNR (31.15), SSIM (0.909), and LPIPS (0.061), while SparseNeRF retains a slight edge in depth RMSE (0.151 vs. 0.185). The method is built on Mip-NeRF and trained for 100k iterations (~6.5 h on an RTX 3090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanNet, P²NeRF outperforms all six baselines on every metric. The closest competitor is DS-NeRF (PSNR 20.85, RMSE 0.447), which P²NeRF surpasses by 0.18 dB in PSNR and more than halves the depth RMSE (0.213 vs. 0.447). SCADE is competitive in PSNR (20.75) but lags significantly in LPIPS (0.306 vs. 0.209) and RMSE (0.481 vs. 0.213). SparseNeRF and InfoNeRF show the weakest geometry (RMSE 0.991 and 1.212 respectively), confirming that local depth ranking alone is insufficient for large-viewpoint indoor scenes. On Replica, P²NeRF wins on PSNR (31.15 vs. 29.86 for SparseNeRF), SSIM (0.909 vs. 0.887), and LPIPS (0.061 vs. 0.117 for FreeNeRF), but SparseNeRF achieves a lower depth RMSE (0.151 vs. 0.185), attributed to Replica's smoother surfaces where SparseNeRF's local smoothing excels. SCADE performs poorly on Replica (PSNR 22.49, RMSE 1.822), indicating its in-domain absolute-depth prior does not generalize well to synthetic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of few-shot NeRF methods that leverage geometric supervision (DS-NeRF, SparseNeRF, SCADE, FreeNeRF) by introducing a two-level prior strategy: a global geometry warm-up via LoFTR-based image matching and hierarchical geometry consistency via DPT monocular depth. It departs from in-domain approaches like SCADE and DDP, which train dedicated depth networks and suffer poor generalization, by relying entirely on pretrained, domain-agnostic models. The ray weight mask regularization is a new mechanism that directly constrains the volume density distribution along rays, going beyond the pairwise local ranking losses used in SparseNeRF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, generalizable recipe for few-shot NeRFs in challenging 360° indoor scenes where viewpoint variation is large. The finding that the geometric warm-up is a broadly transferable component (it dramatically improves SparseNeRF from 17.54 to 20.30 PSNR) suggests it could be adopted as a standard preprocessing step in other few-shot NeRF pipelines. Open questions remain about how the biases from coarse triangulation and monocular depth errors propagate into the final geometry, and whether the approach generalizes beyond indoor scenes to outdoor or unbounded environments.
