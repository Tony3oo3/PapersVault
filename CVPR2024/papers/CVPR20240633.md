## Problem

Existing deepfake video detectors that rely on low-level visual artifacts (spatial inconsistencies, frequency anomalies) and temporal artifacts (flickering, discontinuity) suffer significant performance degradation against newer, higher-quality generation algorithms that effectively suppress these cues. This paper identifies that generated facial videos exhibit a suppressed variance in the temporal flow of GAN-inversion style latent vectors—a high-level signal arising from the temporal smoothness required to produce stable deepfake videos—that remains detectable across diverse generation methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 32-frame video clip as input. Faces are cropped via RetinaFace and aligned with Dlib, then preprocessed with FFHQ conditions and resized to 256×256 for the style branch and 224×224 for the content branch. The output is a single binary real/fake probability produced by a sigmoid on the Temporal Transformer Encoder's classification head, evaluated via AUC across cross-dataset and cross-manipulation benchmarks.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. In Stage 1, the pSp encoder extracts per-frame style latent vectors from the preprocessed clip; frame-to-frame differences (style flow) are fed into a bidirectional two-layer GRU (4096 hidden units) trained with supervised contrastive learning (triplet loss pulling same-label clips together and pushing different-label clips apart, plus a BCE classification loss). In Stage 2, a 3D ResNet-50 (pre-trained via FTCN) extracts content features encoding visual and temporal artifacts, which serve as the query in a Style Attention Module where the StyleGRU output provides key and value, enabling content-driven attention over style dynamics. The fused representation passes through a Temporal Transformer Encoder (16 time steps) to produce the final binary prediction. The key differentiator is exploiting the suppressed temporal variance of style latent vectors—a high-level semantic signal—rather than low-level pixel artifacts, yielding superior cross-dataset and cross-manipulation generalization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best 1-Lipschitz method (Cayley transform) achieves 65.2% clean accuracy and 42.1% certified robust accuracy at epsilon=8/255. On Tiny-ImageNet, the top method reaches 28.7% clean accuracy. Training time varies from 1.1x (spectral normalization, fastest) to 3.8x (LOT, slowest) compared to unconstrained ResNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five 1-Lipschitz construction methods on a common protocol. The Cayley transform orthogonalization [3] achieves the best certified robust accuracy across all datasets but has moderate training overhead. Almost-orthogonal layers [7] are faster to train but sacrifice 2-3% certified accuracy. LOT [18] achieves competitive certified accuracy with the highest training cost (3.8x). Spectral normalization is the fastest but provides the weakest certified bounds. The unified algebraic approach [15] shows that several methods are mathematically equivalent, explaining their similar empirical performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FTCN framework (Zheng et al., ICCV 2021) for 3D ResNet-50 content feature extraction and Temporal Transformer Encoder architecture, and on the pSp encoder (Richardson et al., CVPR 2021) for inverting images into StyleGAN's style latent space. It departs from the dominant paradigm in deepfake detection—exploiting low-level spatial artifacts (frequency anomalies, blending boundaries) or temporal artifacts (flickering, discontinuity)—by instead targeting the suppressed temporal variance in high-level style latent vectors, which is an inherent byproduct of GAN-based generation's need for temporal smoothness. The StyleGRU module with contrastive learning and the Style Attention Module are novel architectural contributions that bridge this high-level semantic signal with conventional content features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the critical generalization gap in deepfake detection, where methods trained on one generator fail on others, by identifying a signal (style flow variance) that is shared across GAN-based generators rather than specific to one. However, the approach is inherently tied to StyleGAN's latent space and the pSp encoder, limiting applicability to diffusion-model-based or non-GAN generators, and the pSp preprocessing step introduces significant computational overhead. The modest SOTA margin (95.8 vs. 95.2 AUC over AltFreeze) suggests the style flow signal, while conceptually clean, provides incremental rather than transformative gains over strong temporal-coherence baselines. Open questions include whether analogous high-level semantic flows exist for non-GAN generators and whether the approach extends beyond facial content.
