## Problem

Existing nuclear instance segmentation methods struggle to accurately separate densely overlapping nuclei because they lack a differentiated representation between adjacent instances. Additionally, these methods rely on expensive pixel-level annotations and do not transfer easily to point-supervised settings, where only coarse labels are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241313/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes H&E-stained pathology image patches (256×256 for CPM17, 512×512 for NuInsSeg) and produces per-nucleus instance masks. The network outputs two intermediate maps—a binary foreground mask and a four-channel collapse field (distance maps in horizontal, vertical, and two diagonal directions)—which are then processed by a deterministic post-hoc collapse algorithm to yield final instance assignments rather than a direct pixel-wise instance prediction.

## Method

Mudslide introduces a collapse field that models each nucleus as a slope (flat center, steep edges) using four directional distance maps. From this field, an eight-directional force map is extracted via argmax and an initial boundary is detected with a Sobel operator, together providing differentiated representations for neighboring instances and coherent representations within each instance. A pixel-by-pixel iterative collapse algorithm then propagates forces from the boundary inward: pixels that collapse into the same region are assigned to the same instance, simultaneously enforcing inter-instance separation and intra-instance coherence. The backbone is a Trans-UNet with dual branches (binary foreground + collapse field), trained with cross-entropy plus MSE/gradient losses, and the same architecture transfers to point supervision by replacing ground-truth masks with cluster and Voronoi pseudo-labels derived from point annotations.

## Results

On the fully-supervised task, Mudslide achieves 0.714 PQ and 0.740 AJI on CPM17, and 0.599 PQ and 0.647 AJI on NuInsSeg, outperforming all compared baselines. In the point-supervised setting, Mudslide reaches 0.529 PQ and 0.581 AJI on CPM17 and 0.487 PQ and 0.558 AJI on NuInsSeg, again leading all point-annotation-based methods. The method uses a Trans-UNet backbone with a dual-branch architecture (binary foreground + four-channel collapse field) and a post-hoc mudslide collapse algorithm for instance separation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mudslide outperforms all 13 baselines on both datasets in the fully-supervised setting. The closest competitor is CellViT [10], which Mudslide edges out by 0.001 PQ and 0.003 AJI on CPM17 and by 0.003 PQ and 0.009 AJI on NuInsSeg. Against Hover-Net [6], Mudslide gains 0.017 PQ and 0.035 AJI on CPM17 and 0.031 PQ and 0.036 AJI on NuInsSeg. CDNet [7] reports only AJI (0.733 on CPM17, 0.640 on NuInsSeg), both surpassed by Mudslide by 0.007. In the point-supervised setting (Table 6), Mudslide leads SCNet [12] by 0.033 PQ / 0.027 AJI on CPM17 and 0.031 PQ / 0.032 AJI on NuInsSeg, and outperforms SPN+IEN [13] by 0.041 PQ / 0.038 AJI on CPM17. The largest margins are over older methods: Mudslide exceeds U-Net [23] by 0.136 PQ on CPM17 and 0.163 PQ on NuInsSeg, and surpasses DCAN [3] by 0.169 PQ on CPM17.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the nuclear instance segmentation literature, building on distance-map-based approaches (Hover-Net, CDNet, DIST) that encode directional information to separate touching instances, and on contour-aware methods (DCAN, CIA-Net, TopoSeg) that predict explicit boundaries. Its key departure is the 'collapse field' formulation: rather than regressing per-pixel distances or predicting binary boundaries, it learns a four-channel normalized slope map from which a discrete eight-direction force map and an initial boundary are derived, then executes an iterative pixel-by-pixel collapse algorithm. The Trans-UNet backbone is borrowed from CellViT, and the point-supervised pipeline reuses standard cluster/Voronoi pseudo-label generation from prior weakly-supervised work, making the collapse field the primary conceptual contribution over existing architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mudslide framing offers a unified, interpretable mechanism for instance separation that naturally accommodates both fully-supervised and point-supervised training without architectural changes, which is a practical advantage for labs with limited annotation budgets. However, the SOTA margins are thin (e.g., 0.714 vs. 0.713 PQ on CPM17 over CellViT), and point-supervised performance (0.529 PQ) still lags fully-supervised by a wide gap, leaving the core challenge of overlapping-nucleus separation under sparse labels largely unresolved. Whether the collapse-field + iterative-collapse paradigm generalizes beyond H&E nuclear segmentation to other dense-instance tasks (e.g., cell tracking, mitosis detection) remains an open question the paper does not explore.
