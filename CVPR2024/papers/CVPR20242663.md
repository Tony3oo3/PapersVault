## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for image super-resolution require access to source data during adaptation to retain source knowledge, which is often infeasible due to privacy policies or transmission restrictions. While source-free domain adaptation has been explored for classification, segmentation, and detection, no method exists for pixel-wise regression tasks like SR where the notion of classes is absent. This paper introduces the first source-free domain adaptation framework for image SR (SODA-SR).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242663/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained source model (weights only, no source data) and unlabeled target LR images at ×4 scale as input, producing HR images as output. During training, each target LR image is augmented with seven geometrically transformed copies (rotations and flips) fed to the teacher, and the teacher runs N=5 stochastic passes via Gumbel-Softmax to generate pseudo-labels with uncertainty estimates. The WAT module is active only during training; at inference, the student model operates as a standard SR network with no additional computational cost.

## Method

SODA-SR employs a teacher-student architecture where the student contains a Wavelet Augmentation Transformer (WAT) that performs multi-level wavelet packet decomposition on latent features, applies Batch Augmentation Attention (BAA) across the batch dimension on low-frequency sub-bands to implicitly mix information between samples, and fuses cross-level features via Multi-Head Deformable Attention before wavelet reconstruction. An uncertainty-aware self-training mechanism updates the teacher via EMA of the student, uses Gumbel-Softmax stochasticity to generate N pseudo-labels per input, and computes a pixel-level confidence map from the variance to down-weight unreliable regions in the L1 reconstruction loss. Two frequency-domain regularization losses—L1 in the low-frequency wavelet sub-band and an adversarial loss in the high-frequency sub-band—constrain the student from overfitting pseudo-labels by preserving structural and textural consistency with the target LR input.

## Results

On the DRealSR benchmark for ×4 SR, SODA-SR achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all six real→real camera-to-camera adaptation tasks (e.g., 32.24 PSNR / 0.851 SSIM on Panasonic→Sony, 32.13 PSNR / 0.850 SSIM on Olympus→Sony), while ranking second on LPIPS. In the synthetic→real setting, SODA-SR also outperforms all UDA baselines (e.g., 31.61 PSNR on Panasonic→Sony vs. 31.50 for DADA). Notably, SODA-SR operates under a source-free constraint (no access to source data during adaptation), whereas all competing UDA methods require source data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SODA-SR outperforms all four UDA baselines (CinCGAN, DASR, DRN-Adapt, DADA) on PSNR and SSIM across all six real→real adaptation tasks, while being the only method that does not require source data access. The closest competitor, DADA [60], is surpassed by SODA-SR by 0.11 dB PSNR on Panasonic→Sony (32.24 vs. 32.13) and 0.14 dB on Olympus→Panasonic (31.41 vs. 31.27). DASR [57] achieves the best LPIPS (e.g., 0.269 on Panasonic→Sony) but at the cost of significantly lower PSNR (30.08) and SSIM (0.777), and often produces artifacts. In the synthetic→real setting, SODA-SR again leads all UDA methods (e.g., 31.61 PSNR vs. 31.50 for DADA on Panasonic→Sony). Against self-supervised baselines ZSSR [2] and MZSR [46], SODA-SR achieves substantially higher PSNR (e.g., 32.24 vs. 31.34 for ZSSR on the Sony test set), demonstrating that source-free domain adaptation with a pre-trained model is far more effective than training from a single LR image.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) paradigm—previously established for classification (SHOT, SHOT++), semantic segmentation, and object detection—to the pixel-wise regression task of image super-resolution, where class-based pseudo-labeling strategies are inapplicable. It builds on the teacher-student pseudo-labeling framework from semi-supervised learning (Mean Teacher, FixMatch) and the UDA-for-SR line (CinCGAN, DRN-Adapt, DASR, DADA), but removes the requirement for source data access. The Wavelet Augmentation Transformer is a novel architectural contribution that repurposes wavelet packet decomposition and deformable attention (from Deformable DETR) as a learnable feature-space augmentation, while the uncertainty estimation via Gumbel-Softmax stochasticity creatively repurposes a categorical reparameterization trick for pixel-wise confidence mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source-free constraint addresses a genuine practical bottleneck: in deployed SR systems, source training data is often locked behind privacy policies or transmission restrictions, making UDA infeasible. The architecture-agnostic design (validated on both CDC and SwinIR) increases practical adoptability. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (DRealSR) with only three cameras and ×4 scaling, and the margins over source-accessing UDA baselines are modest (typically <0.2 dB PSNR), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more diverse degradation types, larger scale factors, or other restoration tasks. The modest LPIPS advantage (DASR sometimes wins on perceptual quality) also suggests the frequency-domain regularization may not fully capture perceptual fidelity.
