## Problem

Existing Robust Super-Resolution (RSR) methods rely on adversarial training with a specific attack (e.g., PGD), making them sensitive to other perturbation types and limiting their generalization to unseen real-world corruptions. This paper asks which robustness method exhibits the highest degree of adaptability across a wide range of adversarial attacks and natural corruptions, and empirically demonstrates that no single adversarial training technique achieves universal robustness in SR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242643/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (120×120 pixels) and produces a 4× upscaled high-resolution image (480×480). At inference, the input LR image is consumed by adding 21 i.i.d. Gaussian noise samples (with a task-specific standard deviation σ) and computing the pixel-wise median of the resulting predictions, rather than passing the image through the network once. The base architecture is ESRGAN with RRDB blocks, trained with L1, perceptual (VGG19), and relativistic GAN losses.

## Method

CertSR applies Median Randomized Smoothing (MRS) to a pre-trained ESRGAN generator in two phases: (1) MRS fine-tuning, where the model is trained on LR images augmented with i.i.d. Gaussian noise at multiple standard deviations (σ=0.03 and σ=0.2), computing the pixel-wise median of predictions across noise samples as the training target; and (2) MRS inference, where 21 Gaussian samples are added to the test input and the pixel-wise median of the 21 predictions is taken as the final HR output. The key insight is that the median (unlike the mean) is nearly unaffected by outliers in corrupted LR images, and that SR's small pixel-wise prediction variance means only ~21 Monte Carlo samples suffice (versus ~2000 needed for classification or detection). This yields certifiable l2-norm robustness guarantees via percentile bounding (Lemma 4.1), and the paper shows CertSR outperforms all adversarial-training-based RSR variants (FGSM, BIM, PGD, CW) across every attack type and real-world corruption benchmark.

## Results

On the DIV2K validation set, CertSR achieves LPIPS of 0.12 on clean images (matching ESRGAN) and 0.19 under Gaussian sensor noise (vs. 0.67 for ESRGAN and 0.37 for AD-L-PGD). On real-world NTIRE 2020 and AIM 2019 validation sets, CertSR attains the best LPIPS of 0.21 and 0.33 respectively, without any training or fine-tuning on those datasets. Against adversarial attacks (FGSM, BIM, PGD, CW), CertSR achieves the best or second-best LPIPS across all four attack types, with the strongest margin on CW (0.18 vs. 0.24 for AD-L-PGD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CertSR outperforms ESRGAN [33] by a large margin on corrupted inputs: LPIPS drops from 0.67 to 0.19 under Gaussian noise and from 0.48 to 0.44 under blur, while matching ESRGAN on clean images (0.12 vs. 0.12). Against the prior RSR method AD-L-PGD [4], CertSR wins on LPIPS across all three DIV2K conditions (noisy: 0.19 vs. 0.37; blurry: 0.44 vs. 0.50) and on both real-world datasets (NTIRE: 0.21 vs. 0.23; AIM: 0.33 vs. 0.37). On real-world SR, CertSR surpasses the dataset-specific methods ESRGAN-FS [10] and Impressionism [14] on LPIPS for both NTIRE (0.21 vs. 0.25/0.23) and AIM (0.33 vs. 0.39/0.41) without any fine-tuning on those datasets. Among the adversarial-training baselines the authors construct, AD-L-CW is the weakest (LPIPS 0.63 on NTIRE) while AD-L-BIM is the strongest (0.26/0.36), yet CertSR still leads both. The one exception is FGSM-attack robustness on DIV2K, where AD-L-FGSM (LPIPS 0.19) narrowly beats CertSR (0.27), though CertSR remains competitive on all other attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Robust Super-Resolution (RSR) paradigm introduced by Castillo et al. [4], which first applied PGD-based adversarial training to real-world SR. It broadens the adversarial attack landscape in SR by adapting FGSM, BIM, and Carlini-Wagner from the classification literature, and introduces Median Randomized Smoothing (MRS) from the certified object detection literature [5] as an alternative robustness mechanism. The key conceptual departure is reframing robustness in SR as a universality question—asking which defense generalizes across diverse attack types—rather than optimizing for a single attack, and showing that a certified smoothing approach outperforms all adversarial-training variants on this axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MRS provides more universal robustness than any single adversarial training strategy offers a practical, certifiable alternative for deploying SR models in safety-critical or open-world settings where the corruption distribution is unknown. The practical observation that only ~21 Monte Carlo samples suffice (versus ~2000 in detection) makes the approach computationally tractable at inference. Open questions include whether the universality advantage persists for larger architectures (e.g., SwinIR, HAT), for higher scaling factors, and for other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring), and whether the certified radius can be tightened to cover larger perturbation budgets without degrading clean-image quality.
