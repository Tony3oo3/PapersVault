## Problem

Medical vision-language pre-training requires large paired image-text datasets, but radiology data is scarce due to privacy constraints and labelling complexity. Existing augmentation methods target either image or text modality in isolation, which fails to increase semantic information gain because the non-augmented modality remains unchanged and semantic congruence is constrained. This paper asks whether simultaneously augmenting both modalities in a semantically coherent manner can substantially improve medical VLP performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes original chest X-ray images (resized to 224×224) paired with free-text radiology reports from MIMIC-CXR (377k pairs). The LLM branch takes each original report as a text prompt to generate a semantically modified report (≤50 words, partial opposite meaning), while the RoentGen diffusion model generates 512×512 images from these reports using 75 PNDM denoising steps at guidance scale 4. The output is 187,922 synthetic image-report pairs (44,279 InterAug + 143,643 IntraAug) that are merged with the real data for VLP pre-training, after which the learned ViT-B/32 + Transformer representations produce zero-shot or linear-probing disease classification scores on ChestXpert, PadChest, and RSNA.

## Method

PairAug comprises two branches that jointly expand paired data without redundancy. The InterAug branch uses ChatGPT to generate plausible reports with partial opposite meanings from originals, then synthesises entirely new chest X-ray images via RoentGen (a Stable Diffusion model post-trained on radiology data), creating novel patient cases; pairs are pruned by a MedCLIP semantic-alignment threshold. The IntraAug branch preserves patient identity by reusing the same diffusion model but swapping the cross-attention map from the original report with that of the modified report at diffusion steps t < η (η = 0.5), thereby altering the depicted medical condition while retaining anatomical structure; pairs are pruned by a hybrid consistency score combining semantic alignment, image-image similarity, and directional change consistency in CLIP space. The final augmented set is combined with MIMIC-CXR to pre-train a CheXzero-style contrastive model (ViT-B/32 image encoder, 12-layer Transformer text encoder, CLIP-initialized), which is then evaluated under zero-shot and linear-probing fine-tuning protocols.

## Results

On the ChestXpert zero-shot protocol, PairAug (single model) achieves a mean AUC of 88.34%, accuracy of 84.97%, and F1 of 65.73% across five diseases, surpassing the CheXzero base model by 2.10% AUC. On PadChest (193 diseases), PairAug reaches 70.79% AUC, 84.90% accuracy, and 7.51% F1, improving over the base by 4.50% AUC. On the RSNA Pneumonia fine-tuning protocol, PairAug achieves 85.89% AUC with only 1% labelled data and 88.63% AUC at 100% labelled data, consistently outperforming both ImageNet pre-training and CheXzero. The ensemble variant (PairAug-E) further pushes ChestXpert AUC to 89.97% and PadChest AUC to 72.51%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PairAug (single model) outperforms MGCA by 4.05% AUC on ChestXpert and 4.67% on PadChest, and beats CXR-CLIP by 2.14% AUC on ChestXpert and 2.29% on PadChest. Against the CheXzero base model (single), PairAug gains 2.10% AUC on ChestXpert and 4.50% on PadChest; the ensemble variant (PairAug-E) surpasses CheXzero-E by 1.05% AUC on ChestXpert and 1.27% on PadChest. On the RSNA fine-tuning task, PairAug achieves 85.89% AUC at 1% labelled data, exceeding CheXzero-S (83.45%) by 2.44% and ImageNet pre-training (74.98%) by 10.91%. Notably, CXR-CLIP retains a slight edge over PairAug on PadChest accuracy (86.04% vs 84.90%) and F1 (8.62% vs 7.51%), though PairAug leads on AUC. The paper also shows that combining image-only and text-only augmentation (Base+AugText+AugImg) yields only marginal gains over either alone, whereas PairAug's concurrent paired augmentation delivers substantially larger improvements (minimum 3.82–5.08% AUC gain over all single-modality baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the medical VLP line of work (CheXzero, MGCA, CXR-CLIP) for the base contrastive pre-training framework, and on recent text-to-image generation models (RoentGen/Stable Diffusion) for synthetic radiology image synthesis. It departs from prior medical data augmentation work, which has treated image and text expansion as separate problems, by formulating a unified paired augmentation task. The IntraAug branch specifically repurposes the cross-attention map swapping mechanism from Prompt-to-Prompt (Hertz et al., 2022) for controlled intra-patient image editing, while the InterAug branch combines LLM-based report generation with T2I synthesis to create entirely new patient cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in medical VLP—the scarcity of paired image-text data—by showing that concurrent, semantically coherent augmentation of both modalities yields substantially larger gains than single-modality approaches. A key practical finding is that raw generated data without quality pruning is actually harmful (InterAug alone decreases AUC by 1.59%), underscoring the importance of the hybrid consistency filtering. Limitations include reliance on RoentGen (chest X-ray only), dependence on generation model quality, and the human radiologist study showing only 61% semantic alignment accuracy, leaving open questions about generalization to other body parts, imaging modalities, and clinical deployment.
