## Problem

Existing visual rearrangement methods rely on pre-trained detectors to assign category labels and construct semantic scene graphs for identifying and matching scene changes, which introduces error accumulation, restricts the agent to a fixed set of known categories, and limits real-world applicability. This paper asks whether visual rearrangement can be performed entirely without any category concepts, by directly comparing geometric and appearance information between the goal and shuffled configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240937/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGBD observations at each pose during two sequential stages: a walkthrough of the goal environment followed by an unshuffle stage where objects have been moved. From these, the system constructs geocentric point clouds and per-pixel visual feature maps (via ResNet-18), then builds a diff-cloud of moved (blue) and protruding (red) points. The output is a set of matched entity pairs—each specifying a goal state and a current state for one object instance—followed by Dijkstra-planned navigation paths that sequentially restore each object to its goal position.

## Method

CAVR decomposes visual rearrangement into three modules operating on point clouds rather than semantic labels. First, a closer-aligned-retrace exploration policy optimizes an observation distance map to guide the agent toward unobserved or distantly-observed regions, ensuring close-range detail capture. Second, scene change detection compares geocentric point clouds at aligned poses across the two stages, allocating points with increased distance (object removal) to the moved part and decreased distance (object addition) to the protruding part of a diff-cloud, each annotated with ResNet-18 visual features. Third, scene change matching applies DBSCAN to extract entity clusters from each diff-cloud part, then solves a maximum-weight bipartite graph matching (via Kuhn-Munkres) where edge weights combine cosine similarity of geometric features (from a PointNet++-based extractor trained on 77K synthetic point-cloud pairs from AI2THOR) and visual features, yielding rearrangement goals that are executed sequentially with obstacle-avoiding navigation.

## Results

On the RoomR dataset, CAVR achieves 14.2% success rate and 33.1% Fixed Strict, outperforming TIDEE (11.7% / 28.9%) and MaSS (4.7% / 16.5%). On the more challenging multi-room MrMiR dataset, CAVR reaches 5.0% success and 28.7% Fixed Strict, compared to TIDEE (1.0% / 14.1%) and MaSS (0.6% / 10.5%). CAVR also achieves the lowest Energy Remaining (0.714 on RoomR, 0.733 on MrMiR) and Misplaced scores (0.707 / 0.713), indicating closer restoration to the goal configuration even when full success is not achieved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAVR outperforms both baselines on every metric across both datasets. On RoomR, CAVR improves success rate by 2.5 points over TIDEE (14.2% vs. 11.7%) and by 9.5 points over MaSS (14.2% vs. 4.7%), while also reducing Energy Remaining from 0.715 (TIDEE) to 0.714 and from 1.016 (MaSS) to 0.714. On the more complex MrMiR dataset with multi-room, multi-instance scenes, the gap widens substantially: CAVR achieves 5.0% success versus 1.0% for TIDEE and 0.6% for MaSS, and 28.7% Fixed Strict versus 14.1% and 10.5% respectively. Notably, TIDEE required modification to handle multiple instances of the same category on MrMiR, whereas CAVR operates without any category information, making it inherently more generalizable to unseen object types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in visual rearrangement (MaSS, TIDEE) that relies on pre-trained object detectors to assign category labels and match semantic scene graphs between goal and shuffled configurations. Instead, it repurposes point cloud geometry and appearance features as the scene change representation, building on PointNet++ for geometric embedding and ResNet-18 for visual features, then applies classical bipartite graph matching (Kuhn-Munkres) at the entity level. The exploration policy extends coverage-based approaches by introducing an observation distance map that explicitly optimizes for close-range observation, and the diff-cloud construction draws on scene change detection literature but adapts it to the two-stage walkthrough/unshuffle setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the dependency on category detectors addresses a fundamental practical limitation: real-world environments contain objects outside any fixed taxonomy, and detector errors propagate through the pipeline. The MrMiR dataset (multi-room, multi-instance, up to 300+ m²) is a meaningful step toward more realistic evaluation beyond the single-room, single-instance-per-category RoomR benchmark. However, absolute success rates remain very low (14.2% on RoomR, 5% on MrMiR), and the method is validated only in simulation, leaving open whether the point-cloud-based matching generalizes to real-world sensor noise, partial observability, and dynamic scenes. A key open question is whether the category-agnostic approach can close the gap with ground-truth semantic methods as scene complexity grows further.
