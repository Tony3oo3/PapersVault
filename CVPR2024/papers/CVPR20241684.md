## Problem

Existing 3D shape assembly methods reason purely about holistic part geometry, ignoring the physical contact surfaces (joints) that connect parts. This geometric-only perspective is agnostic to rotational and reflective symmetry, producing upside-down or flipped part poses that leave joints misaligned and unusable for autonomous assembly. The multi-part multi-joint setting further introduces a large bipartite matching search space, interleaved discrete-continuous optimization, and severe error compounding when a single joint mismatch propagates through the entire structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N part point clouds (1,000 FPS-sampled points each, canonicalized via PCA) where each part is annotated with peg and/or hole joint point sets (the 50 closest points between connected parts at distance < 0.05). It outputs a 6-DoF SE(3) pose (rotation + translation) for every part such that the union of transformed parts forms the target shape and all peg-hole joint pairs are spatially aligned. The joint annotations are automatically extracted from ground-truth part positions rather than manually labeled, and the task requires solving a one-to-one bipartite matching over O(M²) possible peg-hole pairs simultaneously with continuous pose regression.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level hierarchical graph network that decouples shape-structure and joint-alignment objectives. A part graph (fully connected, PointNet-encoded nodes) performs iterative message passing to propose and refine part poses for the desired shape structure. A joint graph (bipartite edges between all peg and hole nodes) performs message passing with a learned connectivity matrix r_ij that weights edges by joint geometry and relative part positions, explicitly modeling which joints should connect. Joint-level features are aggregated per part via MaxPool to form joint-centric part features, which are concatenated with part-graph features for joint-aware pose prediction. The network operates in a coarse-to-fine manner through interleaved part-graph and joint-graph iterations (part-joint-part-joint-joint), where each iteration predicts a pose refinement operator δq applied to the previous pose. Training uses a composite loss combining shape terms (translation L2, rotation CD, holistic CD with Hungarian matching for congruent parts) and joint terms (flip correction, coarse centroid L2, fine joint CD), with a graph-traversal matching algorithm to maintain order invariance across congruent part classes.

## Results

Under the fair comparison setting (joint-annotated inputs and joint-aware losses for all methods), the proposed method achieves an average Shape Chamfer Distance of 0.006 and Part Pose Accuracy of 69.2% across Chair, Table, and Cabinet categories, substantially outperforming the best baseline B-NSM (Shape CD 0.024, Part Acc 15.0%). On the joint alignment objective, the method achieves a Joint Matching Accuracy of 51.4% (average), compared to the best baseline B-Complement at 16.3%, and a Joint Chamfer Distance of 0.505 versus 0.561 for B-Complement. The gap is particularly dramatic on Part Pose Accuracy, where the method scores 69.2% versus 7.0% for B-GNN and 15.0% for B-NSM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all six baselines on both shape structure and joint alignment metrics under the fair joint-annotated setting. On Part Pose Accuracy, the method achieves 69.2% average versus 15.0% for B-NSM (the strongest baseline), 7.0% for B-GNN, and 9.6% for B-Global — a gap of 4–62 percentage points. On Joint Matching Accuracy (Table 2), the method reaches 51.4% average, nearly tripling the best baseline B-Complement (16.3%) and B-GNN (15.4%). B-NSM, which uses a transformer with adversarial training, is the closest competitor on Shape CD (0.024 vs. 0.006) but still lags by a factor of four. The paper also shows that when baselines are evaluated under their original settings (without joint inputs/losses), B-GNN achieves 61.7% Part Acc, but this is not a fair comparison since it lacks joint supervision entirely. The authors note that baselines under the joint setting produce collapsed shapes (all parts clustered together), indicating that the joint loss creates a local minimum that traps methods without the proposed hierarchical coarse-to-fine graph structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on category-level 3D shape assembly (Huang et al. 2020, Li et al. 2020) by introducing explicit joint annotations and a joint-alignment objective that prior geometric-only methods ignore. The closest prior work, Willis et al. (Joinable, CVPR 2022), considers peg-hole joints but only for two parts with a single joint pair, making the pairing trivial; this paper generalizes to multi-part, multi-joint assembly where bipartite matching in an O(M²) space becomes non-trivial. Methodologically, it departs from single-level graph approaches by proposing a hierarchical two-level graph (part graph for shape structure, joint graph for joint matching) with iterative coarse-to-fine refinement, rather than a single unified graph or sequential decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a joint-annotated PartNet benchmark and dedicated evaluation metrics (Joint CD, Joint Acc.) that fill a gap in the assembly literature, and reveals a fundamental loss-landscape tension: shape structure wants to spread parts apart while joint alignment wants to contract them, causing re-purposed baselines to collapse into degenerate clustered predictions when both objectives are imposed simultaneously. This insight motivates the coarse-to-fine hierarchical design and suggests that future multi-objective assembly methods must explicitly manage the expand/contract conflict. Limitations include restriction to simple peg-hole joints, point-cloud-only representation, and no sequential/programmatic planning, leaving open the question of how to handle more complex joint types (threads, dovetails) and how to integrate the learned assembly into closed-loop robotic manipulation.
