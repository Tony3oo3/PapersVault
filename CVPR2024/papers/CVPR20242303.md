## Problem

Object detectors trained on well-lit data suffer severe performance degradation on low-light images, yet existing remedies (image enhancement, fine-tuning) all require real low-light data that is scarce and expensive to annotate. This paper tackles the problem in a zero-shot day-night domain adaptation (ZSDA) setting, where the detector is trained exclusively on well-lit source-domain images and must generalize to an unseen low-light target domain with no access to any real low-light data, knowing only that the target scenario is dark.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes paired RGB images: a well-lit source image and its low-light counterpart synthesized by a physics-inspired Dark ISP pipeline. During inference, a single low-light RGB image is fed through the standard detection backbone and head, producing object bounding boxes and class labels identical in format to the base detector (DSFD or YOLOv3). An auxiliary reflectance decoder, active only during training, outputs per-pixel reflectance maps supervised by a frozen pretrained Retinex decomposition network.

## Method

DAI-Net augments an existing detector (DSFD or YOLOv3) with a lightweight reflectance decoding branch that branches off the second convolutional layer of the backbone, forcing the shared early features to encode illumination-invariant (reflectance) information. A pretrained Retinex decomposition net provides pseudo ground-truth reflectance and illumination maps to supervise this branch stably. Two key losses reinforce invariance: a mutual feature alignment loss (symmetric KL-divergence) that aligns well-lit and low-light feature distributions, and an interchange-redecomposition-coherence procedure that performs two sequential Retinex decompositions—interchanging reflectances between the pair to reconstruct and redecompose images—then penalizes inconsistency between the two rounds of reflectance via an L1 cohering loss. The total objective combines standard detection losses with the reflectance learning loss, decomposition loss, and the two proposed invariance losses, all trained end-to-end on well-lit and synthesized low-light pairs without any real low-light data.

## Results

On WIDER FACE→DARK FACE (test set, DSFD backbone), DAI-Net achieves 28.0% mAP under zero-shot day-night domain adaptation, outperforming the best prior ZSDA method Sim-MinMax (25.7%) by 2.3 points and the base DSFD generalization (16.1%) by 11.9 points. On COCO→DARK FACE (validation set, YOLOv3 backbone), DAI-Net reaches 57.0% mAP, surpassing MAET (55.8%) and YOLO_L (54.0%). On ExDark for general object detection, DAI-Net achieves 78.3% mAP, exceeding MAET (74.0%) by 4.3 points. On CODaN for dark image classification, DAI-Net attains 68.44% Top-1 accuracy, beating Sim-MinMax (65.87%) by 2.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAI-Net outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. On WIDER FACE→DARK FACE, it beats the strongest ZSDA competitor Sim-MinMax [40] by 2.3 mAP points (28.0 vs 25.7) and CIConv [27] by 9.6 points; against generalization baselines like DSFD [29] and SmallHardFace [83] (both 16.1%), the gain is 11.9 points. On COCO→DARK FACE, DAI-Net surpasses MAET [8] (55.8%) by 1.2 points and YOLO_L (54.0%) by 3.0 points, with the largest margin over YOLO_N+Zero-DCE [16] (54.2%) at 2.8 points. On ExDark, DAI-Net leads MAET [8] by 4.3 mAP (78.3 vs 74.0) and YOLO_L by 6.7 points, with per-class gains up to 7.6% on the difficult Chair category. On CODaN, DAI-Net exceeds Sim-MinMax [40] by 2.6 Top-1 points (68.44 vs 65.87) and MAET [8] by 12.0 points. In the fully supervised fine-tuning setting, fine-tuned DAI-Net (52.9%) still outperforms fine-tuned DSFD (46.0%) by 6.9 points, establishing a new baseline for DARK FACE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Retinex theory—a low-level image decomposition framework (reflectance × illumination)—for the high-level task of object detection, building on established detectors (DSFD, YOLOv3) and a pretrained Retinex decomposition network (RetinexNet) to generate pseudo ground truth. It extends the zero-shot day-night domain adaptation (ZSDA) setting, previously applied only to image classification [27, 40], to object detection, and departs from prior low-light detection methods (e.g., HLA-Face, MAET) that require access to real low-light target data. The interchange-redecomposition-coherence procedure is a novel loss design that goes beyond the vanilla reflectance-interchange penalty used in prior Retinex-based enhancement work [69].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ZSDA-for-detection formulation addresses a practical bottleneck: low-light datasets are sparse and expensive to annotate, yet practitioners know in advance that the deployment scenario is dark. The plug-and-play design (auxiliary reflectance decoder + two losses) can be attached to any existing detector without architectural overhaul, making it broadly applicable. Limitations include reliance on a physics-inspired synthetic degradation pipeline (Dark ISP) that may not capture all real-world corruptions, and the method is validated primarily on face and general object detection with a small number of base architectures. Open questions include whether the reflectance-invariance principle transfers to other domain shifts (weather, sensor) and whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or transformer-based detectors.
