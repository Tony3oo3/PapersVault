## Problem

Existing deepfake detection methods are limited to binary real/fake classification and cannot identify which specific facial components were manipulated or in what sequential order. The emergence of sequential component-wise manipulation datasets (e.g., Seq-DeepFake) exposes this gap, as methods relying on blending artifacts or paired source-target information fail when only the final manipulated image is available and multiple components are altered in a specific sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241375/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB facial image (resized to 384×384 for binary classification tasks) and produces three outputs: a scalar deepfake probability estimated via contrastive MIL over patch-token similarities, an L-dimensional vector of per-component manipulation probabilities, and the manipulation order recovered by sorting the probability vector in descending order. The number of class tokens L is set to the number of facial components under consideration (e.g., 5 for Seq-FaceComp), and for pure binary classification L is reduced to 1 with the ranking loss disabled.

## Method

A CNN+FPN backbone extracts spatial features that are reshaped into N patch tokens, which are jointly processed with L learnable class tokens through a transformer encoder. The contrastive MIL loss computes a pairwise similarity matrix over patch tokens, sorts the upper-triangle entries, and contrasts the mean of the k smallest (positive) responses against the remaining (negative) responses via a temperature-scaled ratio to yield a well-defined deepfake probability. For localization and ordering, a rank-aware loss penalizes the discrepancy between the predicted probability ordering and the ground-truth manipulation sequence, weighted by the absolute rank difference, so that higher-probability components correspond to earlier manipulations. The total loss combines the contrastive MIL classification loss, multi-label BCE losses from both patch-token and class-token streams, and the ranking loss, enabling end-to-end multi-task training without autoregressive decoding.

## Results

On the sequential deepfake manipulation task (Seq-FaceComp), the method with Swin Transformer backbone achieves 84.36% multi-label accuracy and 74.97% ranking (Fixed-Acc) accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline MMNet (73.93% ranking) by 1.04 points and SeqFakeFormer (72.65% ranking) by 2.32 points. On Seq-FaceAttr, it reaches 90.74% multi-label and 70.02% ranking accuracy. For binary deepfake classification, the method achieves 99.82% AUC on FF++ (intra-testing) and, more critically, 91.56% AUC on Celeb-DF and 75.17% AUC on DFDC in cross-testing (trained only on FF++), surpassing the previous best (SBIs) by 29.76 and 26.19 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 13 baselines across two task settings. For sequential deepfake manipulation, the method outperforms all baselines on both multi-label and ranking metrics: it beats MMNet (the strongest prior method) by 1.04 points on Seq-FaceComp ranking and 0.75 points on Seq-FaceAttr ranking, and surpasses SeqFakeFormer by 2.32 and 1.16 points respectively. The multi-label accuracy gains are even larger (4.82 points over Multi-Cls on Seq-FaceComp). For binary deepfake classification, the method achieves the best cross-testing AUC across all four target datasets (CDF, WDF, DFDC, DFD), with particularly large margins over Xception (+29.76 on CDF, +26.19 on DFDC) and SBIs (+2.38 on CDF, +3.75 on DFDC). The only setting where gains are marginal is intra-testing on FF++ (99.82% vs. 99.72% for SBIs), which the authors attribute to the task being near saturation. The ablation confirms that the ranking loss L_Rank contributes ~2 points to Fixed-Acc and the contrastive MIL loss L_CLS contributes ~1.9 points on top of the multi-label BCE baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multiple instance learning (MIL) paradigm—previously dominant in weakly-supervised medical imaging—to formulate binary deepfake detection as a contrastive bag-level problem, where an image is a bag and spatial patches are instances. For the sequential manipulation subtask, it departs from SeqFakeFormer's autoregressive decoding [32] by instead coupling standard multi-label classification with a custom rank-aware loss that enforces probability ordering to reflect manipulation sequence. The overall framework unifies these two threads (binary classification via contrastive MIL and component-wise localization via multi-label ranking) under a single CNN+Transformer architecture with shared feature extraction, rather than treating them as separate pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified formulation offers a practical single-model solution that simultaneously answers 'is this fake?', 'which components were altered?', and 'in what order?', which is more informative for forensic and content-moderation applications than binary detection alone. However, the evaluation is confined to the Seq-DeepFake dataset (generated by StyleMapGAN) for the sequential tasks, leaving open whether the ranking loss generalizes to real-world, non-sequential, or multi-tool manipulations. The large cross-testing gap (e.g., ~92% on CDF vs. ~75% on DFDC) also suggests residual sensitivity to generator identity, and the O(N⁴) similarity-matrix computation, while mitigated by sampling, may limit scalability to very high-resolution inputs.
