## Problem

Vision-language models trained on image-level objectives (contrastive loss, image-text matching) exhibit an inverse vision-language relation for localization: prompt embeddings are closer to background patches than to the target object, making zero-shot open-vocabulary segmentation and point prediction unreliable. While CLIPSurgery showed that value-value attention partially fixes this, the underlying mechanism was unexplained and the approach was limited to a single projection type without principled controls over group formation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240938/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 448px shorter side) and a text prompt of the form 'a photo of a {class name}' as input, producing a dense per-patch similarity map that is bilinearly upsampled to the original image resolution to form a segmentation mask. For point prediction on OpenImagesV7, the same per-patch scores are thresholded at 0.5 to yield binary predictions evaluated against positive/negative point annotations. No fine-tuning, retraining, or hyperparameter tuning is performed; the adaptive temperature is computed analytically from the input token norms at inference time.

## Method

GEM (Grounding Everything Module) generalizes CLIPSurgery's value-value attention to a unified self-self attention framework that applies the same mechanism to query-query, key-key, and value-value projections, then ensembles the three outputs. Each self-self attention step L2-normalizes the projected tokens and applies an adaptive temperature τ = n·√d / Σ‖x_i‖₂ to control cluster granularity without hyperparameter tuning. The operation is applied iteratively (one iteration by default), and the paper shows via simulation and Lipschitz-constant analysis that self-self attention acts as a differentiable soft clustering: because the ViT projection matrices are contractions (spectral norm < 1), repeated self-self attention pulls semantically similar tokens toward a shared cluster center while preserving alignment with the language space. The module runs as a parallel pathway alongside the frozen ViT backbone, and final localization is obtained by the dot product between GEM patch outputs and the text encoder's CLS embedding.

## Results

GEM achieves 46.2 mIoU on PascalVOC, 32.6 mIoU on PascalContext, and 15.7 mIoU on ADE20K for zero-shot semantic segmentation using CLIP ViT-B/16, outperforming all training-free baselines (CLIPSurgery: 41.2/30.5/12.9; MaskCLIP: 28.6/23.8/10.2). With MetaCLIP, GEM reaches 46.8/34.5/17.1 mIoU. On the large-scale OpenImages-V7 point prediction benchmark (5827 classes), GEM-MetaCLIP achieves 51.9 p-mIoU, surpassing all fine-tuned methods (GroupViT: 36.5, SegCLIP: 32.1, OVSeg: 22.5) and even the fine-tuned GroundingSAM (53.3) when combined with SAM (GEM-SAM-MetaCLIP: 55.2 p-mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods, GEM consistently outperforms CLIPSurgery by 5.0 mIoU on VOC (46.2 vs 41.2), 2.1 on Context (32.6 vs 30.5), and 2.8 on ADE20K (15.7 vs 12.9), and beats MaskCLIP by a wider margin (17.6 mIoU on VOC). Against fine-tuned methods, GEM surpasses GroupViT (52.3/22.4/9.2), SegCLIP (52.6/24.7/8.7), and OVSegmentor (53.8/20.4/5.6) on Context and ADE20K, though PACL (72.3/50.1/31.4) and OVSeg (94.5/55.7/29.6) remain stronger on VOC and Context due to their use of localization annotations. On OpenImages-V7 point prediction, GEM-MetaCLIP (51.9 p-mIoU) outperforms all fine-tuned segmentation models (GroupViT 36.5, SegCLIP 32.1, OVSeg 22.5) and CLIPSurgery (47.8), and when paired with SAM, GEM-SAM-MetaCLIP (55.2) surpasses the fine-tuned GroundingSAM (53.3). The paper attributes the advantage of training-free methods on OpenImages-V7 to the fact that fine-tuning on smaller, cleaner datasets reduces effective vocabulary, hurting performance on the 5827-class benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free localization line initiated by MaskCLIP (which showed that discarding the final MLP avoids vision-language inversion) and CLIPSurgery (which introduced a value-value attention 'surgery pathway' in parallel with the ViT backbone). The key conceptual departure is generalizing value-value attention to a unified self-self attention framework (q-q, k-k, v-v), connecting it formally to soft clustering via Lipschitz-constant analysis, and adding regularizations (L2 normalization, adaptive temperature, iterative refinement, qkv ensemble) that make the approach generalize across backbones without hyperparameter tuning. Unlike fine-tuning approaches (SegCLIP, PACL, GroupViT) or localization-first pipelines (OVSeg, OpenSeg, GroundingSAM), GEM requires no additional training data or architecture modification beyond a lightweight parallel module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a purely training-free method outperforms fine-tuned segmentation models on the large-vocabulary OpenImagesV7 benchmark (~6K classes) challenges the prevailing assumption that fine-tuning on smaller, cleaner datasets is necessary for localization, suggesting that fine-tuning may actually reduce open-vocabulary coverage. The clustering interpretation of self-self attention provides a theoretical grounding that was missing from CLIPSurgery and opens the door to differentiable clustering formulations for future fine-tuning of GEM. Limitations include sensitivity to text encoder ambiguities (e.g., 'Human body' segmenting car body), degraded performance on multi-objective models like BLIP, and the fixed single-iteration choice that trades off optimality for true zero-shot generality.
