## Problem

Despite VLMs like CLIP being trained on billions of image-text pairs, the vision encoder has remained vanilla ViT without systematic investigation of alternative architectures, driven by an unverified assumption that ViTs scale best in the vision-language era. ImageNet-era conclusions about model design may not transfer to the VLM setting due to fundamentally different data scales and training objectives, and no comprehensive evaluation protocol exists for benchmarking diverse vision models under CLIP-style contrastive pretraining.

## Input / Output

ViTamin takes RGB images at 224–384 resolution and text prompts, producing L2-normalized image embeddings aligned with text embeddings via a symmetric contrastive loss. Unlike ViT/14 which uses output stride 14, ViTamin produces feature maps at output stride 16, yielding 196 tokens at 224×224 and 576 tokens at 384×384. The frozen backbone can be plugged into downstream frameworks (F-ViT, FC-CLIP, LLaVA-1.5) for open-vocabulary detection, segmentation, and multimodal language modeling without retraining the vision encoder.

## Method

ViTamin is a 3-stage hybrid architecture: a convolutional stem (two 3×3 convs), two MBConv-LN blocks at stride 4, four MBConv-LN blocks at stride 8, and N stacked TFB-GeGLU blocks at stride 16. MBConv-LN simplifies the standard MobileNetV2 block by removing all BatchNorm and Squeeze-and-Excitation layers, replacing them with a single LayerNorm. TFB-GeGLU upgrades the Transformer FFN by substituting the first linear layer with GeGLU (a gated linear unit with 2× expansion), which improves accuracy while using 12% fewer parameters than a standard FFN, enabling deeper stacks. The scaling rule is controlled by two hyperparameters—width (channel sizes C, 2C, 6C where 6C ∈ {384, 768, 1024, 1152}) and depth (N TFB-GeGLU blocks)—yielding S/B/L/XL variants from ~25M to 436M parameters. Additionally, Locked-Text Tuning (LTT) initializes the text encoder of smaller variants from a pretrained ViTamin-L and freezes it, distilling knowledge into the randomly initialized image encoder at no extra training cost.

## Results

ViTamin-L@256 achieves 81.2% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy, surpassing ViT-L/14 OpenCLIP (79.2%) by +2.0% and CLIPA-v2 (79.7%) by +1.5% under identical DataComp-1B training. Scaling to 384 input size, ViTamin-L reaches 81.8% ImageNet and 67.2% average across 38 datasets, exceeding the larger ViT-H/14 CLIPA-v2 (66.8%) by +0.4% with only half the parameters. The largest variant, ViTamin-XL (436M params) trained on 40B seen samples, attains 82.9% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy, surpassing EVA-E (82.0%) which has 10× more parameters (4.4B). On downstream tasks, ViTamin-L improves open-vocabulary detection by +3.1% on OV-LVIS and segmentation by +2.6% on average 8 datasets over ViT-L/14, and sets new SOTA on 7 open-vocabulary segmentation benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary same-dataset baselines, ViTamin-L@256 outperforms ViT-L/14 OpenCLIP [30] by +2.0% ImageNet (81.2 vs 79.2) and ViT-L/14 CLIPA-v2 [60] by +1.5% (81.2 vs 79.7); at 384 input, ViTamin-L further beats CLIPA-v2 by +1.5% (81.8 vs 80.3). ViTamin-L@384 surpasses the much larger ViT-H/14 CLIPA-v2 [60] (633M image params) by +0.4% on average 38 datasets (67.2 vs 66.8) while using only half the parameters. At the XL scale, ViTamin-XL (436M) beats EVA-E [94] (4.4B params) by +0.9% ImageNet (82.9 vs 82.0), though it trails ViT-G/14 CLIPA-v2 [60] (1.8B params) by −0.2% (82.9 vs 83.1) and SoVIT-400M/14 [2] by −0.2% (82.9 vs 83.1). In the data-quality comparison (Table 4), ViTamin-B with Locked-Text Tuning achieves +23.3% ImageNet gain over the DataComp baseline, dwarfing the +2.3% from the top DataComp challenge filtering solution [119] and +0.6% from SIEVE [75]. On downstream tasks, ViTamin-L beats ViT-L/14 CLIPA-v2 by +3.1% AP on OV-LVIS detection and +2.6% mIoU on average 5 semantic segmentation datasets, and simply replacing LLaVA-1.5's [67] image encoder with ViTamin-L yields new SOTA across 12 LMM benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViTamin is a hybrid architecture that combines MobileNetV2's MBConv blocks (modified with LayerNorm instead of BatchNorm/SE) in its first two stages with ViT's Transformer Blocks (upgraded with GeGLU feed-forward networks) in its final stage, drawing design inspiration from CoAtNet's convolution-attention marriage. The paper departs from the prevailing assumption in the VLM community that vanilla ViT is the optimal and only viable image encoder, motivated by a systematic re-benchmarking of ViT, ConvNeXt, and CoAtNet under the CLIP/DataComp-1B setting that reveals hybrid architectures can outperform pure transformers. It also introduces Locked-Text Tuning, a training scheme that distills knowledge from a frozen pretrained text encoder into a randomly initialized image encoder, extending the knowledge distillation paradigm to the contrastive pretraining setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's strong bias toward data filtering over architecture design in VLMs, showing that a well-designed hybrid model with LTT can yield a +23.3% gain on the same dataset where the best DataComp challenge solution achieved only +2.3% through 24 filtering rules. This suggests the community may be under-investing in vision backbone design for the vision-language era. Open questions include whether the hybrid architecture advantage persists at even larger scales (beyond 436M parameters), whether the findings generalize to non-CLIP training objectives, and whether the output-stride-16 design remains optimal for dense prediction tasks at higher resolutions. The comprehensive downstream evaluation suite (open-vocabulary detection, segmentation, LMMs) also addresses a gap in prior VLM benchmarks that focused almost exclusively on zero-shot classification and retrieval.
