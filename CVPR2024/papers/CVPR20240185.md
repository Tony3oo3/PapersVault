## Problem

Existing contrastive language-image-3D pre-training methods (e.g., ULIP, OpenShape) represent 3D objects solely through point cloud features, ignoring the complementary semantic cues captured by multi-view rendered images. Additionally, the training recipe for this paradigm—batch size, temperature parameters, learning rate schedule, and regularization—remains underexplored, leaving significant performance on the table.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes triplets of (multi-view RGB images rendered from 12 preset camera poses, text descriptions generated by BLIP and filtered by GPT-4, and point clouds of 10,000 surface-sampled points with interpolated colors). At inference, the model flexibly accepts point cloud alone, image alone, or both; the 3D sculptor produces a joint embedding that is matched against text-encoded class labels via cosine similarity for zero-shot recognition, retrieval ranking, or caption generation through an off-the-shelf ClipCap model.

## Method

MixCon3D constructs a holistic 3D object-level representation by first fusing features from M multi-view images via view-pooling (g^MV), then concatenating the fused image features with point cloud features and projecting them through a single fully connected '3D sculptor' layer (g^3D). A new 3D-to-text contrastive loss (L^{3D↔T}) is added on top of the conventional point-to-image and point-to-text losses, and the CLIP image-text loss is retained with an additional learnable projection head on the frozen encoder. The training recipe is substantially improved with separate learnable temperature parameters per modality, a ~2k batch size, cosine LR decay with warmup, and exponential moving average (EMA) to stabilize training across synthetic and real-scanned domains. This combination yields consistent gains of 5–6% Top-1 over OpenShape across Objaverse-LVIS, ScanObjectNN, and ModelNet40.

## Results

On the challenging 1,156-category Objaverse-LVIS benchmark, MixCon3D achieves 52.5% top-1 accuracy with the PointBERT encoder and full Ensemble training data, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art OpenShape (46.8%) by 5.7%. On the real-scanned ScanObjectNN dataset, MixCon3D reaches 58.6% top-1 accuracy, a 6.4% improvement over OpenShape (52.2%). On ModelNet40, MixCon3D attains 86.8% top-1 accuracy, outperforming OpenShape (84.4%) by 2.4%. The improved training recipe alone (separate temperature, large batch size, cosine LR schedule, EMA) boosts the OpenShape baseline by 3.3% on Objaverse-LVIS before any architectural changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixCon3D consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the closest competitor OpenShape (NeurIPS 2024), MixCon3D gains +5.7% top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS (52.5% vs 46.8%), +6.4% on ScanObjectNN (58.6% vs 52.2%), and +2.4% on ModelNet40 (86.8% vs 84.4%) with the PointBERT encoder. Against ULIP, the earlier contrastive pre-training method, the margins are even larger: +25.7% on Objaverse-LVIS and +7.0% on ScanObjectNN. The depth-inference-only methods (PointCLIP, PointCLIP v2) and ShapeNet-only training methods (ReCon, CG3D, CLIP2Point) are substantially weaker, with top-1 accuracies below 5% on Objaverse-LVIS, confirming that the contrastive language-image-3D paradigm with large-scale pre-training is essential. Notably, MixCon3D also reports Top1-C (class-averaged) accuracy, a metric not provided by prior methods, achieving 38.8% on Objaverse-LVIS and 62.3% on ScanObjectNN, further demonstrating robustness across the long-tailed category distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

MixCon3D extends the contrastive language-image-3D pre-training paradigm established by ULIP and OpenShape, which train a 3D encoder against frozen CLIP image and text encoders on (image, text, point cloud) triplets. The key departure is the construction of a holistic 3D object-level representation by fusing multi-view rendered image features with point cloud features via a simple '3D sculptor' (concatenation + FC layer), rather than treating point cloud and single-view image as independent modalities. Additionally, the paper provides the first systematic study of training recipes (separate temperatures, large batch size, cosine LR with warmup, EMA) for this paradigm, showing that recipe choices contribute as much as architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a strong, well-ablated baseline for 3D contrastive pre-training that the community can build upon, and the finding that multi-view image features complement point cloud geometry in a simple concatenation is a practical recipe likely to be adopted by subsequent work. The 5.7% gain on the long-tailed Objaverse-LVIS benchmark and 6.4% on ScanObjectNN demonstrate that representation completeness matters as much as encoder capacity. Open questions include whether more sophisticated fusion (beyond view-pooling + FC) could further close the gap, whether the approach extends to scene-level or dynamic 3D understanding, and how the method behaves when the image modality is unavailable at test time (the paper retains L^{P↔T} for this case but the holistic representation is inherently degraded).
