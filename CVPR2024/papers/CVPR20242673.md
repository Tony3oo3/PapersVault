## Problem

Existing neural human reconstruction methods (e.g., AniSDF, Neural Actor) produce animatable avatars from sparse views but bake shading and shadows into the appearance, making them non-relightable. Inverse rendering methods that recover materials (e.g., NeRFactor, Relighting4D) rely on sphere tracing over SDFs, which fails on deformed SDFs used by animatable avatars because the SDF is defined in canonical space and yields incorrect world-space distances. This paper bridges the gap by enabling both relighting and animation from sparse or monocular video under unknown illumination.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse-view (12 of 36 synchronized phone cameras) or monocular (single view) RGB video of a dynamic human, along with per-frame SMPL-H poses and foreground masks. It produces a canonical-space neural avatar comprising a signed distance field, albedo and roughness MLPs, and a displacement field, plus an optimizable 16×32 light probe. The avatar can be rendered under arbitrary environment maps and driven with arbitrary motion sequences; a 512×512 frame renders in ~5 s on an RTX 3090 after 20 hours of training.

## Method

The core contribution is a Hierarchical Distance Query (HDQ) algorithm that approximates the world-space SDF under arbitrary poses by smoothly blending a coarse distance from geodesically-aware KNN on posed SMPL-H vertices (valid far from the surface) with a fine canonical neural SDF queried after inverse LBS warping and a learned displacement field (valid near the surface), using a smooth cutoff at 0.1 m. This consistent world-space distance enables standard sphere tracing (16 iterations) for pixel-surface intersection and, critically, for light visibility via an extension of Distance Field Soft Shadow (DFSS) to the deformed SDF, producing realistic soft shadows with a single light sample per direction. Material properties (albedo, roughness) are stored as canonical-space MLPs and warped to world space, combined with a Microfacet BRDF and a 512-direction light probe in the rendering equation. Training is two-stage: geometry (SDF + displacement) is optimized first with eikonal and sparsity losses, then material and light probe are refined, making the ill-posed inverse rendering problem more tractable.

## Results

On the SyntheticHuman++ dataset (sparse-view, 12 of 20 views), the proposed method achieves 12.44° normal error, 29.01 dB albedo PSNR, 22.69 dB relighting PSNR, and 20.20 dB visibility PSNR, outperforming all baselines on every metric. In the monocular setting, it achieves 18.71° normal error, 22.45 dB relighting PSNR, and 17.95 dB visibility PSNR, again leading on normal, relighting, and visibility (though Relighting4D variants marginally exceed it on albedo PSNR at 25.37 vs. 23.42 dB). The largest margins are on visibility (20.20 vs. 5.37 dB for Relighting4D) and normal accuracy (12.44° vs. 93.83° for Relighting4D), reflecting the failure of prior methods' normal and visibility MLPs to generalize to novel poses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Relighting4D (ECCV 2022), NeRFactor (TOG 2021, adapted to 1 frame), and AniSDF (arXiv 2022, non-relightable). In the sparse-view setting, the proposed method wins on all four metric groups: it achieves 12.44° normal error vs. 29.38° (Relighting4D*) and 93.83° (Relighting4D), 29.01 dB albedo PSNR vs. 24.70–24.71 dB, 22.69 dB relighting PSNR vs. 20.70–22.13 dB, and 20.20 dB visibility PSNR vs. 5.37–15.22 dB. The visibility gap is the most dramatic, with Relighting4D's dedicated visibility MLP producing near-useless results (5.37 dB) under novel poses. In the monocular setting, the method still leads on normal (18.71° vs. 26.17°), relighting (22.45 vs. 21.85 dB), and visibility (17.95 vs. 17.10 dB), but Relighting4D variants narrowly outperform it on albedo PSNR (25.37 vs. 23.42 dB). AniSDF, being non-relightable, bakes illumination into appearance and thus cannot be fairly compared on relighting or visibility; its relighting PSNR (17.55 dB) is the lowest among all methods, confirming that baked shading degrades under novel lighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: animatable neural avatars (AniSDF, ARAH, Neural Actor) that use canonical SDFs deformed via LBS and displacement fields but bake lighting into appearance, and relightable inverse-rendering methods (NeRFactor, Relighting4D) that recover materials but struggle with dynamic geometry. The key technical departure is the Hierarchical Distance Query (HDQ), which resolves the long-standing infeasibility of sphere tracing on deformed SDFs by smoothly blending coarse world-space GS-KNN distances (far from surface) with fine canonical-space neural SDF values (near surface), exploiting the observation that SDF values are locally invariant to LBS deformation. The paper also extends Distance Field Soft Shadows (DFSS) to this deformed setting, which prior animatable methods could not support because they lacked world-space distance queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to produce both relightable and animatable avatars from sparse-view or monocular video, removing the need for light stages or dense camera arrays that previously constrained relightable human capture to professional studios. The HDQ algorithm is a general-purpose technique that could benefit any pipeline requiring sphere tracing on deformed implicit surfaces. However, the 20-hour training time and 5-second-per-image rendering limit practical deployment, and the method still requires known poses and masks as input. Open questions include whether the approach extends to real-time rendering (e.g., via 3D Gaussian Splatting or other acceleration), whether it generalizes to multi-person scenes, and whether the two-stage training strategy can be made end-to-end.
