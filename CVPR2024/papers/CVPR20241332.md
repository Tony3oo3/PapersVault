## Problem

Vision encoders in existing vision-language models remain around 1B parameters while LLMs have scaled to hundreds of billions, creating a parameter-scale disparity that underutilizes LLM capacity. Additionally, vision models trained on pure-vision data or aligned with BERT-family encoders exhibit representation inconsistencies with LLMs, and lightweight 'glue' layers (QFormer, linear projections) are too small to capture rich cross-modal interactions. The paper asks whether scaling the vision encoder to match LLM scale and progressively aligning their representations can yield a unified foundation model for both contrastive and generative vision-language tasks.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at 224×224, 336×336, or 504×504 resolution, video clips sampled as 1 or 8 frames, and text queries/prompts. It operates in three inference modes: InternVL-C (contrastive, using InternViT-6B alone with attention pooling for global visual features and [EOS] token features for text, producing similarity scores for retrieval or class labels for zero-shot classification), InternVL-G (generative, using InternViT-6B + QLLaMA where 96 learnable queries reorganize visual tokens as prefix for sequential text generation, producing captions or responses), and InternVL-Chat (connecting the full model to an LLM decoder via MLP for multi-modal dialogue). For dense prediction, InternViT-6B outputs a feature map at H/14 × W/14 resolution for segmentation.

## Method

InternVL comprises a 6B-parameter vision transformer (InternViT-6B: width 3200, depth 48, 25 heads) and an 8B language middleware (QLLaMA) initialized from multilingual LLaMA-7B with 96 learnable queries and cross-attention layers (1B params) added on top. Training follows a three-stage progressive alignment strategy: (1) contrastive pre-training on 4.98B noisy web-scale image-text pairs using CLIP-style symmetric cross-entropy loss with all parameters trainable; (2) generative pre-training on 1.03B filtered high-quality pairs where InternViT-6B and QLLaMA are frozen and only the queries and cross-attention layers are trained with BLIP-2-style ITC+ITM+ITG loss; (3) supervised fine-tuning on ~4M instruction samples (captioning, VQA, grounding, conversation) with an LLM decoder (Vicuna or InternLM) connected via MLP. The key design insight is that initializing the middleware with LLM weights ensures the visual feature space is inherently aligned with the LLM's representation, enabling strong multi-modal dialogue performance even when the LLM decoder is frozen during SFT.

## Results

InternViT-6B (5.9B params) achieves 88.2% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K linear probing and 58.9 mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation (full tuning), surpassing the much larger ViT-22B (21.7B) which requires the private JFT-3B dataset. For zero-shot image-text retrieval, InternVL-G reaches 88.8 average R@1 across English benchmarks (Flickr30K + COCO) and 90.9 on Chinese benchmarks, outperforming EVA-02-CLIP-E+ (85.1). In multi-modal dialogue, InternVL-Chat with QLLaMA + Vicuna-13B scores 1586.4 on MME and 87.6 on POPE, leading all compared VLLMs. On zero-shot video classification (single frame), InternVL-C achieves 71.0/71.3/65.7% average accuracy on Kinetics-400/600/700, exceeding EVA-02-CLIP-E+ by 1.2–2.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternViT-6B (5.9B) outperforms ViT-22B (21.7B) on ADE20K semantic segmentation by +3.6 mIoU (58.9 vs 55.3, full tuning) and +12.6 mIoU in linear probing (47.2 vs 34.6), despite using 3.7× fewer parameters and no private JFT-3B data. On zero-shot image classification, InternVL-C (83.2% IN-1K) trails CoCa (86.3%) and LiT-22B (85.9%) which use private data, but leads all public-data models including EVA-02-CLIP-E+ (82.0%) by +1.2 points, with notably smaller distribution-shift gap (Δ=0.8 vs 1.1). For image-text retrieval, InternVL-G achieves 88.8 average R@1 on English benchmarks, beating EVA-02-CLIP-E+ (85.1) and OpenCLIP-G (85.0) by 3.7–3.8 points, and 90.9 on Chinese benchmarks vs CN-CLIP-ViT-H (86.1). In multi-modal dialogue, InternVL-Chat with QLLaMA surpasses LLaVA-1.5 (1531.3 MME / 85.9 POPE) by +55.1 MME and +1.7 POPE, and outperforms Qwen-VL-Chat (1487.5 MME) by +98.9 MME. On zero-shot video classification with a single frame, InternVL-C (71.0% K400 avg) exceeds EVA-02-CLIP-E+ (69.8%) by +1.2 points and ViCLIP (64.8% with 8 frames) by +6.2 points, demonstrating strong temporal-agnostic visual understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

InternVL extends the vision-language pretraining lineage from CLIP (contrastive alignment) and BLIP-2 (generative alignment with frozen encoders) by scaling the vision encoder to 6B parameters (InternViT-6B, built on the ViT architecture) and replacing lightweight glue layers (QFormer, linear projections) with an 8B LLM-initialized language middleware (QLLaMA, based on multilingual LLaMA-7B). The key conceptual departure is the 'parameter-balanced' design: rather than connecting a ~1B vision encoder to a 7–13B LLM via a small adapter, both the vision and language components are scaled to comparable magnitudes, with the middleware serving as a substantial reorganization layer. The progressive alignment strategy—contrastive learning on noisy web-scale data followed by generative learning on filtered high-quality data—synthesizes the objectives of CLIP and BLIP-2 into a single coherent training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InternVL addresses a critical bottleneck in VLLM development: the parameter-scale and representation gap between vision encoders and LLMs. By demonstrating that a 6B vision encoder can match or exceed 22B-parameter models (ViT-22B) on linear probing and segmentation using only public data, the paper suggests that architectural alignment and training strategy can compensate for raw parameter count. The 'Swiss Army knife' flexibility—functioning as a standalone vision backbone, a contrastive retriever, a generative captioner, or a dialogue front-end—makes it a practical foundation for diverse downstream applications. Open questions include whether the progressive strategy is strictly necessary versus end-to-end joint training, the computational cost of training a 6B vision encoder at scale, and whether further scaling the vision component (beyond 6B) continues to yield diminishing or accelerating returns.
