## Problem

Existing cross-entropy losses for stereo matching model the ground-truth disparity distribution as uni-modal (Laplacian or Gaussian) for every pixel, ignoring the fact that edge pixels inherently carry ambiguous depth information from multiple objects at different depths. This uni-modal assumption causes the network to produce confusing multi-modal outputs at edges, leading to over-smoothing and misalignment artifacts, while also degrading performance on non-edge pixels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242273/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a calibrated stereo image pair (left and right RGB images) and outputs a per-pixel disparity map. Internally, the network produces a distribution volume of size D×H×W (where D is the maximum disparity range), and the final disparity is extracted via a dominant-modal estimator rather than full-band soft-argmax. The ground-truth distribution for training is constructed per-pixel from a 1×9 horizontal window of disparity labels, with the window enlarged to 3×9 for sparse ground-truth datasets like KITTI.

## Method

The paper proposes an adaptive multi-modal cross-entropy loss that constructs the ground-truth distribution for each pixel as a weighted mixture of Laplacians. Disparities within a local window are clustered via DBScan (ε=3, minPts=1) into K disjoint subsets, each yielding a Laplacian centered at the cluster mean; the central pixel's cluster is anchored to its exact ground-truth disparity. Weights are assigned proportionally to cluster cardinality with a fixed α≥0.5 guaranteeing the central pixel's modal dominates, so non-edge pixels naturally degenerate to a single Laplacian. At inference, a dominant-modal disparity estimator (DME) replaces the standard SME by selecting the modal with maximum cumulative probability (object-level winner-take-all) rather than maximum peak probability, then normalizing within that modal before applying soft-argmax. The entire approach is plug-and-play: replacing the loss and estimator in existing cost-volume networks (PSMNet, GwcNet, GANet) yields state-of-the-art results without architectural changes.

## Results

GANet with the proposed ADL loss and DME estimator achieves D1-all of 1.55 (All) and 1.40 (Noc) on KITTI 2015, and >3px Out-All of 1.29 on KITTI 2012, ranking 1st among all published methods on both benchmarks. On SceneFlow, GANet+Ours reaches EPE 0.50, >1px 4.25, and >3px 1.81, improving EPE by 35.9% over the original GANet (0.78). For cross-domain generalization (trained only on SceneFlow), GANet+Ours achieves ETH3D >1px of 2.31, surpassing IGEVStereo (3.6) and all generalization-specific methods (e.g., ITSA-CFNet at 5.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On KITTI 2015, GANet+Ours (D1-all 1.55) outperforms IGEVStereo (1.59), ACVNet (1.65), GANet+LaC (1.67), and all other published methods, establishing a new state-of-the-art. On KITTI 2012, GANet+Ours (>3px Out-All 1.29) similarly beats IGEVStereo (1.44), ACVNet (1.47), and GANet+LaC (1.42). On SceneFlow, GANet+Ours (EPE 0.50) trails IGEVStereo (0.47) and ACVNet (0.46) on EPE but wins decisively on >3px (1.81 vs. 2.47 and 1.98) and >1px (4.25 vs. 4.89 for ACVNet), indicating fewer large errors. For cross-domain generalization, GANet+Ours surpasses all generalization-specific methods (DSMNet, CFNet, FC-GANet, Graft-GANet, ITSA-CFNet) on all four test datasets, with the largest margin on ETH3D (2.31 vs. 5.1 for ITSA-CFNet) and KITTI 2012 (3.93 vs. 4.2 for ITSA-CFNet). The method also outperforms multi-modal approaches like SMDNet (D1-all 2.08) and the retrained PSMNet+[32] (1.88) on KITTI 2015, confirming that adaptive multi-modal supervision at the fine level is more effective than coarse-level multi-hot or fixed two-Laplacian parameterizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the uni-modal cross-entropy loss paradigm introduced by PDSNet [28] and PSMNet+ [2], which model the ground-truth disparity as a single Laplacian or Gaussian distribution centered on the GT value and pair it with the Single-Modal Estimator (SME) for inference. The key departure is the argument that edge pixels are inherently multi-modal due to photometric ambiguity from multiple objects at different depths, and therefore enforcing a uni-modal constraint across the entire image is counterproductive. The authors replace the fixed uni-modal pseudo-ground-truth with an adaptive mixture of Laplacians whose number of components and weights are determined by DBScan clustering within a local window, and introduce a Dominant-Modal Estimator (DME) that selects the modal by cumulative probability rather than peak probability. The method is architecture-agnostic and plugs into any cost-volume-based stereo network (PSMNet, GwcNet, GANet) without modifying the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: simply swapping the training loss and inference estimator (no architectural changes, no extra parameters) lifts three classic networks to state-of-the-art on KITTI 2015/2012 and beats specialized cross-domain generalization methods (DSMNet, CFNet, ITSA) without any domain-adaptation modules. The robustness to sparse ground-truth (only ~10% degradation at 20% GT density versus ~24% for the L1 baseline) suggests a path toward reducing the expensive LiDAR annotation pipeline for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the multi-modal modeling principle transfers to other ambiguous-correspondence tasks (e.g., optical flow, multi-view stereo at fine resolution), whether the DBScan hyperparameters (ε=3, minPts=1) generalize across disparity ranges and resolutions, and whether the approach complements or conflicts with iterative refinement architectures (RAFT-style) that already produce multi-modal intermediate states.
