## Problem

Estimating full-body human motion from sparse AR/VR tracking signals (head and hands only) is an inherently ambiguous one-to-many mapping, and existing methods produce implausible lower-body motions—legs penetrating scene geometry or moving uncorrelated with the upper body—because no direct observations constrain the legs. This paper addresses the gap by introducing 3D scene geometry as an additional modality to resolve these ambiguities and by explicitly regularizing lower-body motion during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240575/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse tracking signals from three 6D trackers (head HMD and left/right hand controllers), from which position, rotation, linear velocity, and angular velocity are extracted, together with a 3D scene point cloud cropped to a 2 m × 2 m × 2 m bounding box centered on the human. The output is a full-body SMPL motion sequence over 22 joints using 6D rotation representations, produced at the same temporal resolution as the input signals.

## Method

S²Fusion is a conditional diffusion model whose reverse process is initialized from a VAE-based motion prior trained on AMASS (rather than pure Gaussian noise), which both improves sample diversity and reduces the number of denoising steps needed. The conditioning input combines raw tracking signals, periodic motion features extracted by a Periodic Autoencoder (PAE) that decomposes signals into frequency-domain parameters (amplitude, phase, frequency) to capture temporal alignment between upper and lower body, and a scene feature vector from a PointNet++ encoder. The denoiser predicts the clean signal x̂ (not residual noise) and is trained with an L_simple objective plus a forward-kinematics geometric loss. During sampling, two custom losses—scene-penetration loss (KNN-based distance to scene points for ankle/knee joints) and phase-matching loss (aligning upper-body and lower-body periodic features)—are injected as gradient guidance to regularize the lower body without requiring any lower-body tracking signals.

## Results

On CIRCLE, S² Fusion achieves the best performance across all five metrics: MPJRE 4.65°, MPJPE 57.8 mm, MPJVE 235.7 mm/s, Jitter 10.1, and FS 1.39. On GIMO, it similarly leads with MPJRE 2.32°, MPJPE 19.2 mm, MPJVE 117.6 mm/s, Jitter 5.8, and FS 1.48. The most notable margin is in MPJPE, where S² Fusion reduces error by 12.9 mm (18%) over the next-best method (AvatarJLM) on CIRCLE and by 5.4 mm (22%) on GIMO, demonstrating the benefit of scene-aware conditioning for resolving sparse-to-dense ambiguities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S² Fusion outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both CIRCLE and GIMO. Against AvatarPoser [28] (ECCV 2022, Transformer-based regression), the gains are largest: MPJPE drops from 91.3 to 57.8 mm on CIRCLE (−37%) and from 30.5 to 19.2 mm on GIMO (−37%), while Jitter improves from 16.4 to 10.1 and 11.5 to 5.8 respectively. Against AGRoL [17] (CVPR 2023, diffusion-based without scene), S² Fusion reduces MPJPE by 30.8 mm on CIRCLE and 9.5 mm on GIMO, with a 12% improvement in MPJVE on GIMO (141.1→117.6). Against the strongest baseline AvatarJLM [76] (ICCV 2023, joint-level modeling), the advantage narrows but remains consistent: MPJPE improves by 12.9 mm on CIRCLE and 5.4 mm on GIMO, and FS (foot sliding) improves from 1.41→1.39 and 1.53→1.48. The per-body-part breakdown (Table 2) further confirms that the lower-body improvement is the most pronounced, with Lower PE dropping from 132.6 to 107.9 mm on GIMO and from 69.1 to 57.3 mm on CIRCLE, validating the scene-penetration and phase-matching losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: sparse-to-dense motion estimation (AvatarPoser, AGRoL, AvatarJLM), diffusion-based motion generation (Tevet et al., 2022), and loss-guided sampling (Song et al., 2023). It departs from prior sparse-signal methods by introducing 3D scene geometry as an additional conditioning modality, arguing that scene context resolves the one-to-many ambiguity that purely sensor-based approaches cannot. The periodic autoencoder from DeepPhase (Starke et al., 2022) is repurposed here not for motion synthesis but to extract time-alignment features from heterogeneous sparse signals (gyroscope rotations + optical positions), bridging the upper-body observations to lower-body generation. The motion prior (a VAE trained on AMASS) addresses the data-scarcity problem of scene-motion datasets by jumpstarting the reverse diffusion from a structured, non-Gaussian distribution rather than pure noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important AR/VR problem where only head and hand trackers are available, and demonstrates that scene geometry can substantially reduce lower-body estimation error (Lower PE drops from 132.6 to 107.9 mm on GIMO). The phase-matching loss is a lightweight, differentiable regularizer that enforces upper-lower body coordination without requiring any lower-body sensors, making it broadly applicable to other sparse-sensing scenarios. Limitations include evaluation on only two relatively small datasets (CIRCLE, GIMO), a simple KNN-based penetration loss that may not handle complex concave geometries well, and the absence of real-time performance analysis. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-person scenes, whether more physically grounded constraints (contact dynamics, balance) can replace the heuristic losses, and whether the scene encoder (PointNet++ on a 2m crop) is sufficient for large or dynamic environments.
