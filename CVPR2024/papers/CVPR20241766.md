## Problem

Long-tail recognition suffers from tail-class data scarcity and limited feature diversity, causing models to underperform on rare categories. Existing approaches—resampling, loss re-weighting, data augmentation, and CLIP-based transfer learning—struggle to obtain correct and diverse knowledge for tail classes, and directly querying LMMs for recognition fails on complex fine-grained categories. The paper addresses how to extract the rich but implicit knowledge in large models to generate diverse, high-quality tail-class content and effectively incorporate it into fine-tuning despite the domain gap with original data.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB images from tail classes along with their class labels, feeding them sequentially into GPT-4V for description extraction and into GPT-4 for description extension. It produces structured textual descriptions following a fixed template (class + distinctive features + specific scenes), generated tail-class images via DALL-E, and a class feature template per class. The final fine-tuning stage takes mixed pairs of original and generated images (combined via BalanceMix) and outputs a class label prediction from the LoRA-adapted CLIP ViT-B/32 visual encoder.

## Method

LTGC is a generative-and-fine-tuning framework with three stages. First, GPT-4V analyzes existing tail images to produce a descriptions list, then GPT-4 extends it with missing features and scenes using a self-reflection loop (number-checking iterates until a per-class cap K_y is reached; repetition-checking filters duplicates). Second, DALL-E converts the extended descriptions into images, and an iterative evaluation module uses CLIP's cosine similarity against a per-class feature template to detect low-quality images, prompts GPT-4 to refine their descriptions, and regenerates them. Third, the BalanceMix module combines balanced sampling with Mixup (λ from a Beta distribution) to merge original and generated data, addressing the domain gap, and the CLIP ViT-B/32 visual encoder is fine-tuned via LoRA on the resulting mixed pairs.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT, LTGC achieves 80.6% overall top-1 accuracy (70.5% few-shot), surpassing the previous SOTA VL-LTR by 3.4%. On Places-LT, it reaches 54.1% overall (52.1% few-shot), outperforming LPT by 4.0%. On the challenging fine-grained iNaturalist 2018, LTGC attains 82.5% overall accuracy (82.6% few-shot, 83.9% medium-shot), exceeding the best CLIP-based method RAC by 2.3% and the best traditional method MDCS by 6.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTGC outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among CLIP-based long-tail methods, it beats VL-LTR by 3.4% on ImageNet-LT (80.6% vs. 77.2%) and by 5.7% on iNaturalist (82.5% vs. 76.8%), and surpasses RAC by 2.3% on iNaturalist (82.5% vs. 80.2%) and by 4.0% on Places-LT (54.1% vs. 50.1%). Against LPT, LTGC gains 4.0% on Places-LT and 6.4% on iNaturalist. Among traditional deep-learning baselines on iNaturalist, LTGC exceeds MDCS (75.6%), PaCo (73.0%), RIDE (71.8%), and LADE (52.3%) by 6.9%, 9.5%, 10.7%, and 30.2% respectively. In the direct LMM-querying comparison (Table 3), LTGC also surpasses GPT-4 (72.1% on ImageNet-LT, 64.3% on iNaturalist), MiniGPT4-v2 (68.5%, 27.1%), MiniGPT4 (60.4%, 20.9%), and LENS (69.5%, 17.4%), confirming that the generative pipeline extracts far more useful knowledge than simply querying a large model for a label.

## Relation to Prior Work

LTGC departs from the dominant long-tail recognition paradigm of re-weighting losses, resampling, or transferring features from head classes/CLIP (e.g., LPT, VL-LTR, RAC) by instead *generating* new tail-class training data. It repurposes the knowledge embedded in LMMs (GPT-4V) and LLMs (GPT-4) not as classifiers but as controllable description generators, feeding those descriptions into a T2I model (DALL-E) to synthesize diverse tail images. The fine-tuning stage borrows from Mixup and LoRA, while the iterative quality-check loop draws on chain-of-thought prompting and CLIP's image-text alignment as a feedback signal. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of generative AI and long-tail learning, arguing that the 'potential knowledge' in large models is more useful for data augmentation than for direct recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a generative pipeline can close much of the gap between few-shot and many-shot accuracy on fine-grained long-tail benchmarks (e.g., 82.6% few-shot on iNaturalist 2018), suggesting that data scarcity rather than model capacity is the binding constraint for tail classes. However, the heavy reliance on proprietary, expensive models (GPT-4V, GPT-4, DALL-E) limits reproducibility and scalability, and the domain gap between generated and real images—partially addressed by BalanceMix—remains an open challenge. The authors note future directions in long-tail semantic segmentation and dissent detection, but it is unclear whether the pipeline generalizes beyond image classification or to settings where T2I models lack sufficient class-specific knowledge.
