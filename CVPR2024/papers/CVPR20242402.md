## Problem

Binary Neural Networks rely on the sign function for binarization, whose zero-almost-everywhere gradient is incompatible with backpropagation. Existing differentiable surrogates (e.g., STE) introduce a forward-backward mismatch that corrupts gradient direction, and progressively tightening the approximation to reduce quantization error leads to divergent gradients and training instability. The paper asks whether an alternative binarization function can simultaneously offer controllable quantization error and stable gradients without requiring additional network modules or regularization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a single class label (10 or 1000 classes). All convolutional layers except the first and last are binarized to 1-bit weights and 1-bit activations (1W/1A), with a single real-valued scaling factor γ applied per layer to recover magnitude information. The binarized convolution is computed via XNOR and BITCOUNT bit-wise operations, yielding 32× memory compression over full-precision models.

## Method

BiPer replaces the standard sign function with a binary periodic (square wave) function Sign(sin(ω₀w)) for the forward pass, so that the latent weight can oscillate between +1 and −1 across its full domain rather than being monotonically mapped. For backpropagation, the continuous sinusoidal surrogate sin(ω₀w) is used as the differentiable gradient, whose magnitude is proportional to the frequency ω₀. A key theoretical contribution is a closed-form derivation of the quantization error as a function of ω₀ and the Laplace distribution parameter b of the latent weights, showing that QE can be explicitly controlled by tuning the frequency rather than by progressively tightening a surrogate. Training follows a two-stage protocol: stage 1 optimizes real-valued weights through the sinusoidal function, and stage 2 switches to the full binary periodic binarization with a warm-start initialization, where an intermediate frequency (ω₀=20) empirically balances initial quantization error against final classification accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, BiPer achieves 93.75% Top-1 accuracy with ResNet-18 (1W/1A), outperforming the previous best (MST at 93.2%) by 0.55% and ReCU (92.8%) by nearly 1%. On ImageNet, BiPer attains 61.4% Top-1 and 83.14% Top-5 with ResNet-18, and 65.73% Top-1 and 86.39% Top-5 with ResNet-34, setting new state-of-the-art in the 1W/1A setting. The accuracy gap to full-precision models is reduced to ~1% on CIFAR-10 and ~8% on ImageNet, without requiring additional network modules or regularization terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiPer outperforms all 18 compared baselines on ImageNet for both ResNet-18 and ResNet-34, with the closest competitor being ReCU (61.0% vs 61.4% Top-1 on ResNet-18; 65.1% vs 65.73% on ResNet-34). On CIFAR-10, BiPer achieves the best Top-1 on ResNet-18 (93.75%) and ResNet-20 (87.5%), beating MST (93.2%) and ReCU (92.8%/87.4%) respectively, but is marginally behind ReCU on VGG-small (92.11% vs 92.2%). The gap over the strongest prior methods (ReCU, ReSTE, DIR-Net) is consistent at 0.4–0.63% on ImageNet and up to ~1% on CIFAR-10. Notably, BiPer achieves these gains without introducing extra network modules, auxiliary losses, or regularization terms, relying solely on the periodic binarization function and its sinusoidal surrogate for backpropagation. The two-stage training with frequency-controlled initialization (ω₀=20) is critical: ablations show that using the sign function instead of the periodic function degrades accuracy by ~0.9% from stage 1 to stage 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard BNN binarization paradigm, which relies on the sign function and its various differentiable surrogates (STE, DSQ, BNN+, ReSTE, etc.), by replacing the sign function with a binary periodic (square wave) function in the forward pass and a sinusoidal function of the same frequency as the backward surrogate. Conceptually, it draws on the use of periodic activations in DNNs (SIREN, Pi-GAN) but repurposes them for the binarization problem rather than as layer activations. The two-stage training protocol and activation binarization follow established BNN practice (Bi-Real Net, IR-Net), while the quantization error analysis via the Laplace-weight assumption and frequency parameterization is a new theoretical contribution not present in prior BNN work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that the frequency of the periodic function provides a tunable knob to control quantization error during weight initialization, offering a principled alternative to the ad-hoc progressive QE reduction used in prior methods. The finding that an intermediate frequency (ω₀=20) outperforms both the lowest-QE and highest-QE settings suggests a non-trivial trade-off between initialization quality and gradient signal strength that the field has not previously characterized. However, the absolute improvements remain modest (≤1% on CIFAR-10, ≤0.63% on ImageNet), and the paper explicitly leaves open the question of smoothly transitioning from the sine surrogate to the square wave during training, which could further close the gap to full-precision models. Extension to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation) is mentioned but not validated.
