## Problem

While diffusion-based image editing has matured, video editing with pre-trained text-to-image models remains hampered by temporal inconsistency, prohibitive memory requirements for long videos, and slow inference. Existing zero-shot approaches rely on sparse-causal attention or optical flow, which degrade in quality over longer sequences or complex shape transformations, and none achieve both fast inference and global temporal consistency without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241148/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video of K frames, a free-form text prompt describing the desired edit, and a ControlNet structural condition (depth, lineart, or softedge) extracted from the source video. Frames are arranged into 2×2 grids (for 8-frame clips) or 3×3 grids (for longer videos) to fit within GPU memory on a single A40. The output is an edited video of the same length that preserves the original motion and semantic layout while applying the requested style, attribute, or shape change, produced in approximately 4 minutes 28 seconds for a 90-frame video.

## Method

RAVE leverages the 'grid trick'—arranging multiple video frames into a single image grid so that a standard T2I diffusion model's spatial self-attention and convolutional layers implicitly capture inter-frame correlations. The key novelty is a randomized noise shuffling strategy: at every DDIM denoising step, the frames are randomly redistributed across the available grids, so that over the full sampling trajectory every frame interacts with every other frame through both convolutional smoothing and self-attention, achieving global spatio-temporal consistency without ever processing all frames simultaneously. The pipeline performs DDIM inversion on the input video, extracts ControlNet conditions, then iteratively denoises the shuffled latent grids conditioned on the target prompt and structural guidance for T=50 steps, finally rearranging the latents back to their original temporal order. This design is training-free, compatible with any pre-trained T2I model (e.g., Stable Diffusion 1.5), and avoids the memory scaling of full spatio-temporal attention while matching or exceeding its consistency, resulting in ~25% faster inference than prior methods.

## Results

On the RAVE Video Evaluation Dataset (186 text-video pairs across 8-, 36-, and 90-frame videos), RAVE achieves the best Q_edit scores at all video lengths: 21.08×10⁻⁵ (8f), 14.60×10⁻⁵ (36f), and 23.95×10⁻⁵ (90f), outperforming the closest competitor TokenFlow (20.92, 14.41, 23.74). RAVE also leads in CLIP-T (textual alignment) at all lengths (29.51, 29.93, 29.76 ×10⁻²) and in CLIP-F (frame consistency) at 90 frames (95.99×10⁻²). In the user study (130 participants), RAVE is selected as the top-2 edit in 90.51% of general editing, 82.82% of temporal consistency, and 86.67% of textual alignment judgments. RAVE completes a 90-frame edit in 4 min 28 s (3 min 13 s without preprocessing), roughly 1 minute faster than TokenFlow (5 min 24 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAVE outperforms all four baselines on the composite Q_edit metric across every video length, with the largest margins over Text2Video-Zero (e.g., 23.95 vs. 21.27 at 90 frames) and Rerender (23.95 vs. 20.51 at 90 frames). Against the closest competitor TokenFlow, RAVE wins on CLIP-T at all lengths (29.76 vs. 29.53 at 90f) and on Q_edit at all lengths, while TokenFlow retains a slight edge on WarpSSIM for shorter videos (74.03 vs. 71.44 at 8f; 50.97 vs. 48.81 at 36f) due to its feature-level smoothing. Pix2Video is limited to 8-frame clips due to GPU memory constraints and trails RAVE substantially on every available metric (e.g., Q_edit 5.61 vs. 21.08). In the user study, RAVE is preferred in 90.51% of general-editing votes versus 44.10% for TokenFlow and 47.95% for Text2Video-Zero, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceived quality. RAVE is also the fastest method, completing a 90-frame edit in 4 min 28 s compared to 5 min 24 s for both TokenFlow and Rerender.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAVE builds on the standard latent diffusion model pipeline (Stable Diffusion + DDIM inversion + ControlNet) that underpins most recent zero-shot video editing methods, but departs from the dominant strategy of modifying attention mechanisms (sparse-causal attention in Pix2Video/FateZero, cross-frame attention in Text2Video-Zero, feature-level smoothing in TokenFlow) for temporal consistency. Instead, it repurposes the 'grid trick'—a community hack for consistent character generation in Stable Diffusion—by arranging frames into spatial grids so that the model's existing spatial self-attention implicitly captures inter-frame correlations. The key conceptual departure is the random noise shuffling across grids at each denoising step, which enforces global spatio-temporal interaction without the quadratic cost of explicit multi-frame attention, positioning RAVE as a lightweight alternative to the attention-engineering approaches that dominate the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAVE offers a practical, training-free video editing pipeline that is ~25% faster than the closest competitor and handles longer videos (90+ frames) where prior methods hit memory walls, making it more accessible for real-world content creation. The introduction of a diverse evaluation dataset spanning object-centric scenes, complex human motion, and dynamic backgrounds addresses a gap in standardized video editing benchmarks. However, the method still suffers from flickering under extreme shape edits due to DDIM inversion error accumulation and LDM compression, and its reliance on a single pre-trained T2I model limits the ceiling of edit quality. Open questions include whether the shuffling strategy generalizes to 3D or multi-modal editing and whether it can be combined with newer diffusion architectures beyond U-Net-based LDMs.
