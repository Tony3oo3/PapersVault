## Problem

Masked autoencoding (MAE) pre-training suffers from two limitations: high masking ratios (75% for images, 90% for videos) cause the encoder to lose important structural detail, and the paradigm typically requires large ViT backbones (ViT-L/H) to perform well, incurring prohibitive computational cost for downstream fine-tuning. The paper asks whether smaller MAE models can be pre-trained more effectively by leveraging a teacher model that sees richer context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241591/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes video clips (T frames of H×W×3, patchified into 2×16×16×3 cubes) or RGB images. The student encoder receives only a subset of visible tokens (90% masked for video, 75% for image), while the teacher encoder receives a superset (75% masked for video, 50% for image), so the student's visible token set is a strict subset of the teacher's. The student must simultaneously reconstruct masked pixels and align intermediate features with the teacher; at inference, the encoder alone produces features for downstream classification or action detection.

## Method

AMD introduces an asymmetric masking strategy where the teacher operates at a lower masking ratio than the student, granting it access to additional context tokens that the student never sees. To transfer knowledge across this asymmetric token set, the paper designs a serial two-stage feature alignment: (1) direct alignment projects student features to the teacher's dimension and matches them on the shared visible tokens, and (2) generation alignment uses a lightweight decoder-like generator (multi-head self-attention over student features concatenated with a limited number of [MASK] tokens) to synthesize features for the teacher-only tokens. The serial ordering—direct alignment first, then generation alignment on the projected features—reduces the difficulty of the generative task. The total loss combines pixel reconstruction (MSE), direct alignment, and generation alignment across multiple encoder layers (middle and last), with layer-specific projection and generator parameters.

## Results

On Something-Something V2 (SSV2), AMD with ViT-B achieves 73.3% top-1 accuracy after 800 epochs of pre-training, surpassing VideoMAE-B (69.6% at 800 epochs, 70.8% at 2400 epochs) by 3.7% and 2.5% respectively, while remaining only 1% below the ViT-L teacher (74.3%). On Kinetics-400, AMD-ViT-B reaches 82.2% top-1, outperforming VideoMAE-B (80.0% at 800 epochs) by 2.2%. On ImageNet-1K, AMD with a MAE-L teacher achieves 84.6% top-1 with ViT-B, exceeding ImageMAE-B (83.6%) by 1.0%. On AVA action detection, AMD achieves 29.9 mAP without extra labels and 33.5 mAP with intermediate fine-tuning, versus 26.7/31.8 for VideoMAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMD consistently outperforms VideoMAE at the same training budget: +3.7% top-1 on SSV2 (73.3% vs 69.6% at 800 epochs) and +2.2% on K400 (82.2% vs 80.0%), and even beats VideoMAE trained for 3× longer (2400 epochs) by 2.5% on SSV2. Against the symmetric distillation baseline DMAE, AMD gains +3.3% on SSV2, +1.4% on K400, and +2.8% on ImageNet-1K (with MAE-B teacher), confirming that the asymmetric masking strategy captures more useful context than symmetric masking. AMD-ViT-B (73.3% on SSV2) surpasses all ViT-L-based methods tested (Motionformer 68.1%, TimeSformer 62.4%, ViViT 65.9%, VIMPAC 68.1%) despite using 3–17× fewer parameters. In the 55–100M parameter group on K400, AMD (82.2%) beats MAE-ST (81.3%), VideoMAE-1600e (81.5%), and DMAE (80.8%). On transfer learning, AMD pre-trained on K400 improves SSV2 by +4.1%, UCF101 by +1.0%, and HMDB51 by +6.3% over VideoMAE, indicating reduced overfitting risk. The only method that outperforms AMD is the ViT-L VideoMAE teacher itself (74.3% on SSV2, 85.2% on K400), leaving a gap of just 1% on SSV2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoding pre-training paradigm (MAE for images, VideoMAE for video) by integrating knowledge distillation into the pre-training loop itself, rather than applying it post-hoc. The key departure from the closest prior work, DMAE (Bai et al., 2023), is the asymmetric masking strategy: while DMAE uses identical masking for teacher and student, AMD gives the teacher a lower masking ratio so it retains more contextual information, with the student's visible tokens forming a strict subset of the teacher's. The serial feature alignment (direct alignment followed by a decoder-like generation alignment for the extra teacher tokens) is a novel mechanism designed specifically to handle this input-length mismatch, distinguishing AMD from both symmetric distillation and parallel multi-branch approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMD demonstrates that a ViT-B student can approach the performance of a ViT-L teacher (73.3% vs 74.3% on SSV2) while being substantially cheaper to fine-tune on downstream tasks, which is practically important for deployment-constrained settings. The versatility across both image (ImageNet-1K) and video (SSV2, K400, UCF101, HMDB51, AVA) domains suggests the asymmetric masking principle is generalizable beyond any single architecture. Open questions include whether the approach benefits from even smaller teachers (e.g., ViT-B as teacher for ViT-S), whether the teacher must be from the same architecture family, and how the method scales to larger masking ratios or longer video sequences where the generation alignment cost grows.
