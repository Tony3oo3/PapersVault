## Problem

PCA and its many variants—robust PCA, dual PCA, tangent PCA, and principal geodesic analysis—were developed in isolation with incompatible formulations, making it difficult to combine their properties or discover new hybrid algorithms. Furthermore, while flag manifolds naturally encode the nested subspace structure of principal components, efficient optimization over them remained an open problem, preventing practical joint optimization of the full flag.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241380/cropped_page7_idx12.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a set of p centered data points (in R^n or on a Riemannian manifold) plus a flag type (n1,...,nk;n) that specifies the nested subspace dimensions and controls the robustness level between L1 and L2. It outputs a flag of orthonormal principal directions (a point on the Stiefel manifold St(nk,n)), from which outlier labels are derived by thresholding reconstruction residuals (for primal methods) or projection norms (for dual methods), and data reconstructions are obtained by projecting onto the flag subspaces and, for manifold data, mapping back via the exponential map from the Karcher median.

## Method

The paper introduces 'flagification,' recasting PCA, robust PCA, dual PCA, and their tangent/manifold variants as a single optimization over flag manifolds FL(n1,...,nk;n), where the flag type parameter interpolates between L1 (full Stiefel flag) and L2 (Grassmann flag) robustness. The key computational insight is a reduction from flag optimization to Stiefel manifold optimization: the flagified objective is rewritten using diagonal weight matrices Wi whose entries are inverse projection norms (for variance-maximizing) or inverse residual norms (for reconstruction-minimizing), yielding an iteratively reweighted scheme (Algorithm 1) solved via Stiefel conjugate gradient descent. For manifold data, the method operates in the tangent space at the Karcher median, producing tangent robust and dual PCA variants (fRTPCA, fWTPCA, fTDPCP) that inherit the flag-type robustness control. A convergence guarantee is established for the fDPCP case under a mild non-degeneracy condition, and the framework naturally generates a family of novel intermediate algorithms by choosing flag types other than the two extremes.

## Results

On the Euclidean recovery experiment (100 points in R^5, k=2), fRPCA(1,...,k) matches L1-RPCA's objective (54.66 vs 54.69) while running ~370× faster (0.19s vs 70.66s). fDPCP(k) achieves a strictly better objective than L2-DPCP (34.66 vs 34.83) with comparable runtime (0.38s vs 0.26s). On the Grassmannian Gr(2,4) outlier detection task, fTDPCP(1,...,k) produces the highest AUC and is most stable as outlier contamination increases. On the 2D Hands Kendall pre-shape space, fTDPCP variants achieve the best outlier detection AUC and the lowest reconstruction error among all tested methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against L1-RPCA (bit-flipping algorithm [45]), fRPCA(1,...,k) recovers a nearly identical objective (54.66 vs 54.69) but is approximately 370× faster (0.19s vs 70.66s), making it the most dramatic speedup. Against L2-WPCA (alternating scheme [72]), fWPCA(k) matches the objective within 0.03 (42.92 vs 42.89) at a modest 1.9× runtime cost (0.45s vs 0.24s). Against L2-DPCP (DPCP-IRLS [69]), fDPCP(k) achieves a strictly lower objective (34.66 vs 34.83) despite random initialization versus SVD-initialization of the baseline, at comparable speed. Against direct Riemannian optimization (Stiefel CGD and Flag RTR via Pymanopt [68,47]), Alg. 1 converges faster and to more optimal objective values across all three flagified formulations (fRPCA, fWPCA, fDPCP) as shown in Fig. 1. On the UCMerced Land Use outlier detection task, the novel fDPCP(1,40) shows a slight but consistent AUC improvement over the benchmark RPCA of Candès et al. [11], and on Yale Face DB-B the dual flag variant dominates as outlier contamination increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a broad family of principal direction methods—classical PCA, L1/L2 robust PCA (Markopoulos, Polyak, Neumayer), dual PCA (Tsakiris & Vidal), and tangent PCA (Fletcher et al.)—under a single flag manifold formulation, where the flag type (nested subspace signature) acts as a continuous parameter interpolating between L1 and L2 robustness. The Stiefel-manifold optimization strategy for flag problems builds directly on Mankovich & Birdal's chordal averaging work and addresses a gap left by Ye et al. and Szwagier & Pennec, who identified optimization on flag manifolds as an open problem. The conceptual departure from prior work is the 'flagification' of PCA eigenvectors: rather than treating principal directions as an ordered set, the paper exploits the nested flag structure to introduce subspace-level weighting schemes and new algorithmic variants (e.g., tangent DPCP) that were not previously accessible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single, convergent Stiefel-based algorithm that subsumes multiple previously separate PCA variants, reducing implementation overhead and enabling systematic exploration of intermediate robustness levels via flag type. However, the empirical gains are modest—slight AUC improvements on small-scale datasets (UCMerced, Yale Face DB-B, 2D Hands)—and the convergence guarantee is established only for the fDPCP case. Key limitations include the inability to handle nonlinear flags (nested spheres, hyperbolic spaces) and the exclusion of barycentric subspace analysis, leaving the full geometric picture incomplete. The flag-type-as-robustness-knob idea is conceptually appealing but its practical advantage over simply tuning p in Lp-norms remains underexplored.
