## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation methods attempt to directly align visual and audio representations, which is inherently difficult and leads to incorrect sound semantics, poor temporal synchronization, monotonous outputs, and no support for user-editable off-screen sounds. The paper addresses this by reframing the problem: instead of learning a direct video-to-audio mapping, it decomposes the task into the better-studied sub-problems of video-to-text (via VLMs) and text-to-audio (via diffusion models), with explicit temporal control.

## Input / Output

The system takes a silent video as primary input, from which four random frames are extracted and encoded with temporal cue prompts (First, Then, After that, Finally) for the VLM. Users can additionally supply text-timestamp pairs for off-screen sounds through an interactive editing interface. The output is a multi-track audio waveform (via MelGAN vocoder from mel-spectrograms) where on-screen sounds are automatically time-aligned to visible actions and off-screen sounds are placed at user-specified timestamps, with individual sound events typically under 10 seconds.

## Method

SonicVisionLM decomposes video-to-audio generation into three stages: (1) a VLM (MiniGPT-v2 with adapted LLaMA-2 conversation templates) identifies sound events from four temporally-cued video frames; (2) a ResNet(2+1)-D18 network detects per-frame sound presence/absence to produce binary timestamp vectors; (3) a Time-controllable Adapter, inspired by ControlNet, injects temporal conditioning into a frozen Tango latent diffusion model. The key mechanism is the Audio Time-condition Embedding (A_ct), constructed by masking mel-spectrogram features with the binary timestamp vector and encoding the result. The adapter mirrors Tango's encoder and middle blocks, and its outputs are concatenated to corresponding decoder blocks via zero-convolution layers (i+j=13 pairing), allowing the adapter to learn temporal guidance without disrupting Tango's pre-trained audio generation capability. The adapter is trained on the newly introduced CondPromptBank dataset (10,276 single-source sound effects across 23 categories with fine-grained text labels and start/end timestamps).

## Results

On the conditional generation task (Greatest Hits), SonicVisionLM-full achieves 39.7% IoU, 78.1% Onset AP, and 43.8% Time Acc, substantially outperforming CondFoleyGen (22.4% IoU, 68.6% Onset AP, 37.4% Time Acc). On the unconditional task, SonicVisionLM-full attains the best MKL (4.67) and IoU (39.5%) on Greatest Hits, and the best IS (12.7) and IoU (42%) on CountixAV. In subjective evaluation, SonicVisionLM scores 75/69/87 on OVL/REL/Time-sync, far exceeding Diff-Foley (48/64/58) and SpecVQGAN (37/25/31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CondFoleyGen [9] on the conditional task, SonicVisionLM-full wins on every metric, with the largest gains in CLAP-top (36.8% vs. 16.3%, a >20-point improvement) and IoU (39.7% vs. 22.4%, a 16-point rise), indicating far better semantic matching and temporal alignment. Against SpecVQGAN [14] on the unconditional task, SonicVisionLM-full dominates on Greatest Hits (MKL 4.67 vs. 6.80, IoU 39.5% vs. 25.8%) and on CountixAV in IS (12.7 vs. 5.3) and IoU (42% vs. 34.9%), though it trails on MKL (9.71 vs. 7.39) due to CountixAV's noisy, multi-source ground truth. Against Diff-Foley [25], SonicVisionLM-full wins on Greatest Hits in MKL (4.67 vs. 5.68) and IoU (39.5% vs. 22.0%), and on CountixAV in IS (12.7 vs. 5.2) and IoU (42% vs. 31.3%), but concedes MKL (9.71 vs. 4.9) and FID (19.7 vs. 15.9) on CountixAV, which the authors attribute to Diff-Foley's tendency to reproduce mixed-source ground truth. In subjective evaluation (Table 4), SonicVisionLM outperforms both baselines on all three criteria, with the most striking margin in Time-sync (87 vs. 58 for Diff-Foley, 31 for SpecVQGAN).

## Relation to Prior Work

SonicVisionLM departs from the dominant video-to-audio paradigm (SpecVQGAN, Im2Wav, Diff-Foley, CondFoleyGen) that directly aligns visual features with audio representations, instead routing through a VLM (MiniGPT-v2) to produce textual sound descriptions that condition a text-to-audio latent diffusion model. The time-controllable adapter is explicitly inspired by ControlNet's zero-convolution and parallel-encoder-to-decoder concatenation mechanism, but adapted to the temporal structure of audio via a novel Audio Time-condition Embedding. The base diffusion backbone is Tango's instruction-tuned LDM, whose frozen UNet parameters are augmented by the adapter trained on the newly introduced CondPromptBank single-sound dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VLM-as-bridge decomposition (video→text→audio) offers a practical path to editable, multi-soundtrack video post-production that prior end-to-end V2A methods cannot support, and the CondPromptBank dataset (10K+ single-source clips with temporal annotations) fills a gap in training data for time-controllable sound generation. However, the authors acknowledge that visual understanding and timestamp detection remain imperfect, and the framework's reliance on a VLM for event identification introduces a potential bottleneck for fast or subtle sound events. Open questions include whether the text-bridge approach generalizes to highly complex or overlapping sound scenes, and whether the time-controllable adapter can be extended to finer-grained (sub-frame) temporal control.
