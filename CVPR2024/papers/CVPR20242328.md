## Problem

Gated cameras deliver accurate metric depth but at megapixel resolution, while high-resolution RGB cameras lack metric precision and struggle in low light. Existing gated depth methods are constrained by the gated imager's resolution and absence of color information, resulting in diminished detail at long distances. This paper bridges the gap by combining active NIR gated imaging with high-resolution HDR RCCB stereo to produce dense, metric depth maps at RCCB resolution across all lighting conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242328/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two stereo image pairs: an 8-megapixel HDR RCCB stereo pair (no IR-cut filter, sensitivity 380–1050 nm, 140 dB on-sensor HDR) and a stereo gated NIR pair (active 808 nm flood illumination with nanosecond-scale temporal gating), both with a 0.76 m baseline. It outputs metric depth maps (in meters) at the full 8-megapixel RCCB resolution for all four camera views, which is an order of magnitude higher resolution than the gated imager alone. The depth maps are evaluated up to 220 m using accumulated LiDAR point clouds as ground truth.

## Method

The core contribution is a Cross-Spectral Matching (CSM) module that registers and fuses RCCB and gated features in three stages: (1) coarse alignment of RCCB features into the gated frame using calibrated poses and an intermediate depth estimate, (2) pose refinement via a lightweight CNN (PoseNet) that predicts residual rotation/translation from the time offset between the two sensor systems, and (3) attention-based feature fusion that combines the aligned features while preserving complementary spectral information. The fused features feed into a modified CREStereo architecture with MPViT backbones, where the network dynamically alternates between modalities during iterative disparity refinement. Training alternates between uni-modal and multi-modal configurations to enforce domain-independent features, and combines self-supervised losses (left-right reprojection consistency across both modalities, and a gated reconstruction loss that analytically models the time-of-flight signal from predicted depth, albedo, and ambient light) with sparse LiDAR supervision.

## Results

On the Gated Stereo dataset, the proposed Gated RCCB Stereo method achieves MAE of 2.03 m (night) and 1.95 m (day) with RMSE of 6.23 m and 6.89 m respectively, outperforming the next-best method Gated Stereo [80] by 9.7% (night) and 13.3% (day) in MAE. On accumulated dense LiDAR ground-truth up to 220 m, the method reduces MAE by 39.6% over Gated Stereo and 39.2% over CREStereo in the 100–220 m range (night: 11.15 m vs. 18.36 m and 20.24 m). The method also achieves the best δ1 accuracy of 96.69% (night) and 97.18% (day), surpassing all mono-RGB, stereo-RGB, and cross-spectral baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 17 baselines across both night and day conditions. Against the closest competitor Gated Stereo [80] (stereo gated only), it reduces MAE by 9.7% at night (2.03 vs. 2.25 m) and 13.3% during day (1.95 vs. 2.25 m), with the gap widening dramatically at long range (100–220 m) where it achieves a 39.6% MAE reduction. Compared to CREStereo [48] (stereo RCCB only), the improvement is even larger: 60.8% MAE reduction at night (2.03 vs. 5.18 m) and 49.7% during day (1.95 vs. 3.88 m). All mono-RGB methods (DPT, DepthFormer, AdaBins, etc.) and stereo-RGB methods (RAFT-Stereo, ACVNet, HSMNet) are substantially outperformed, with the best mono-RGB method (DepthFormer) still 2.5× worse in MAE at night. Cross-spectral stereo baselines CS-Stereo [92] and UCSSM [55] perform poorly (MAE 11.48 and 14.63 m at night), confirming that naive cross-spectral matching without the proposed CSM module is insufficient. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that removing the cross-spectral training, pose refinement, or attention fusion each degrades performance, with the full pipeline contributing cumulatively to the 39% long-range advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gated stereo line of work (Walz et al. [80]) by adding a high-resolution passive RCCB visible-spectrum stereo pair alongside the active NIR gated stereo pair, and builds its stereo matching backbone on CREStereo [48] with modifications for cross-modal operation. The cross-spectral fusion module (CSM) with attention-based feature fusion and pose refinement is novel in the depth estimation context, though cross-spectral matching has been explored in face recognition and pedestrian detection. The gated reconstruction loss is adapted from Gated2Gated [78], but replaces measured range-intensity profiles with an analytical gating model. The key departure from prior cross-spectral stereo methods (CS-Stereo [92], UCSSM [55]) is the inclusion of active illumination and time-of-flight cues, which prior passive cross-spectral approaches lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 39% MAE improvement at 100–220 m over the next-best method, validated on accumulated dense LiDAR, is practically significant for autonomous driving where long-range detection of small objects (e.g., lost cargo) is safety-critical and current LiDAR resolution is insufficient. The system uses only low-cost CMOS sensors and flood illumination, making it economically viable compared to high-end LiDAR. An open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific sensor hardware (AR0820, 808 nm laser) and whether the 20 ms synchronization offset handled by PoseNet becomes a bottleneck in dynamic scenes. The method's dependence on calibrated multi-camera rigs also limits its applicability to single-camera or uncalibrated settings.
