## Problem

Deep embeddings (e.g., CLIP) are the standard cross-modal interface between images and language, but they are opaque to humans and require adaptive fine-tuning to connect with downstream models. Image captions are interpretable yet capture only salient concepts, failing the content-preserving requirement. The paper asks whether text itself—precise and comprehensive enough to reconstruct an image—can serve as a strong, modular cross-modal interface that off-the-shelf LLMs and text-to-image tools can consume without any joint training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at 288×288 resolution and produces a sequence of 75 BPE-encoded text tokens drawn from the 49K-token CLIP vocabulary (punctuation excluded), flanked by [SOS] and [EOS] to fill the 77-token context window. The output is not a grammatical caption but a 'scrambled caption' that mixes semantic concepts (objects, colors, spatial relations, textures) in a non-syntactic order. Because the tokens are standard CLIP vocabulary entries, the text can be directly pasted into any text-to-image prompt field or LLM prompt without further processing.

## Method

De-Diffusion is an autoencoder whose decoder is a frozen, pre-trained Imagen-based text-to-image diffusion model (600M U-Net + OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 text encoder) and whose latent space is discrete text. The encoder consists of a frozen CoCa ViT-L image backbone feeding a 5-block attentional pooler with 75 positional-sensitive learnable queries, which are projected via a linear layer to CLIP vocabulary tokens; Gumbel-softmax (temperature annealed 2.0→0.3) provides the continuous relaxation for backpropagation through the discrete latent. Training minimizes the diffusion model's v-prediction reconstruction loss on images only (WebLI, no captions), so the text must be simultaneously precise and comprehensive to allow the frozen decoder to reconstruct the input. This 'analysis by synthesis' design—using a powerful generative decoder as a fixed target—forces the encoder to produce interpretable, information-dense text rather than a private cipher code, and the entire trainable component is only 135M parameters (the attentional pooler).

## Results

On open-ended few-shot VQA, De-Diffusion ViT-L paired with PaLM 2-L achieves 68.4% accuracy on VQAv2 and 60.6% on OKVQA at 32-shot, setting new state-of-the-art with only 135M trainable parameters (vs. 10B for Flamingo-80B). For text-to-image reconstruction, De-Diffusion text achieves FID 6.43 on MS-COCO using Stable Diffusion v2-base, significantly outperforming human-annotated COCO captions (FID 8.08) and BLIP-2. On open-ended 5-way 1-shot classification on miniImageNet, De-Diffusion with PaLM 2-L and task induction reaches 97.0% accuracy, surpassing the previous closed-form SOTA of 95.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

De-Diffusion (135M params) outperforms Flamingo-80B (10B params) on both VQAv2 (68.4% vs. 67.6%) and OKVQA (60.6% vs. 57.8%) at 32-shot, while using 100× fewer learnable weights and no interleaved image-text supervision; however, Flamingo retains an edge on MS-COCO captioning CIDEr (113.8 vs. 103.7). Against BLIP-2 (108M params, fine-tuned on COCO), De-Diffusion leads by 3.4 points on VQAv2 and 14.7 points on OKVQA at 32-shot. On text-to-image reconstruction FID, De-Diffusion text (6.43) beats human-annotated COCO captions (8.08), BLIP-2 (comparable to human), and PaLI-X, demonstrating that its 'scrambled captions' are more comprehensive than any captioning method. On miniImageNet open-ended classification, De-Diffusion with PaLM 2-L (97.0%) dramatically surpasses SPAE+PaLM 2-L (67.0%), LQAE+GPT-3.5 (45.9%), and Frozen (33.8%), and even exceeds the closed-form SOTA of P>M>F (95.3%). The consistent advantage over IDEFICS-80B (65.9% VQAv2, 57.8% OKVQA) and AnyMAL (64.2%, 42.6%) further confirms that text-as-interface with a strong LLM outperforms jointly-trained vision-language models on reasoning-heavy tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

De-Diffusion extends the autoencoding paradigm (and specifically the 'de-rendering' idea from Neural De-Rendering) by replacing the learned decoder with a frozen pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (Imagen-based), making the latent space directly interpretable text rather than a learned embedding. It departs from prior text-latent autoencoders like SPAE and LQAE, which jointly train encoder and decoder and produce cipher-like tokens, by fixing the decoder so the text must be semantically meaningful to any text-to-image model. Conceptually, it also contrasts with the dominant cross-modal interface of deep embeddings (CLIP, Flamingo, BLIP-2) by arguing that natural language itself can serve as the interface, enabling modular composition with off-the-shelf LLMs without joint training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 135M-parameter encoder producing 75 text tokens can match or exceed 10B-parameter vision-language models on open-ended VQA, suggesting a radically more efficient and modular path to multi-modal understanding. The finding that machine-optimized 'scrambled captions' outperform human-annotated COCO captions for text-to-image reconstruction challenges the assumption that human descriptions are the most comprehensive verbalization of an image. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and video, whether the scrambled format truly supports human interpretability as claimed, and whether the reliance on a specific frozen diffusion decoder limits generality. The modular design (swap any LLM without retraining) is practically attractive as LLMs evolve rapidly.
