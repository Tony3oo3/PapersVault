## Problem

In multimodal representation learning, joint optimization of all modalities simultaneously causes 'modality laziness,' where dominant modalities overshadow subordinate ones, leading to suboptimal fusion performance. Existing balancing methods (e.g., OGM-GE, QMF) still require simultaneous updates of all modality-specific encoders, which inherently limits adaptation for subordinate modalities. The paper asks whether decomposing joint multimodal training into an alternating unimodal process can eliminate cross-modal interference while still capturing cross-modal interactions.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes only one modality's data per iteration (audio clip, video clip, RGB image, or text), cycling through modalities in a fixed round-robin schedule, and does not require paired multimodal samples. At inference, all available modalities for a test example are processed independently through their respective encoders and the shared head, and the final output is a single class label obtained by uncertainty-weighted fusion of per-modality predictions. This design naturally extends to missing-modality scenarios where some modalities are absent at test time.

## Method

MLA replaces joint multimodal optimization with an alternating unimodal learning paradigm: at each training step t, only the encoder h_{m_t} for modality m_t = t mod M is updated, while a shared head g is updated continuously across all modalities to capture cross-modal information. To prevent the shared head from forgetting previously learned modality representations (modality forgetting), a gradient modification matrix P_t computed via Recursive Least Square orthogonalizes the head's gradient direction against the average encoded feature of the current modality before each update. At test time, an uncertainty-based dynamic fusion mechanism computes per-modality prediction entropy and assigns inverse-entropy weights (via a softmax over negated entropies) to combine the individual modality predictions into a final class label, so that more confident modalities contribute more to the output.

## Results

On the five multimodal classification benchmarks, MLA achieves the best multimodal accuracy across all datasets: 79.70% on CREMA-D, 71.35% on Kinetic-Sound, 93.33% on Food-101, 79.94% on MVSA, and 78.92% on IEMOCAP. The largest gains appear on CREMA-D (+11.56 pp over OGM-GE) and Kinetic-Sound (+5.57 pp over QMF), where modality laziness is most severe. Under missing-modality conditions on IEMOCAP (Table 2), MLA also leads at every missing rate from 10% to 70%, reaching 55.89% at 70% missing versus 55.44% for the best dedicated missing-modality method (MMIN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLA outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset in the multimodal setting. Against the strongest joint-optimization baselines, MLA beats OGM-GE by 11.56 pp on CREMA-D (79.70 vs 68.14), 5.57 pp on KS (71.35 vs 65.74), and 2.75 pp on IEMOCAP (78.92 vs 76.17); it beats QMF by 5.99 pp on KS, 0.46 pp on Food-101, and 1.98 pp on MVSA. Conventional fusion methods (Sum, Concat, FiLM, BiGated) suffer severe modality laziness—for example, on CREMA-D the video modality drops to 17–19% under Sum/Concat/FiLM/BiGated, whereas MLA lifts it to 64.91%. Late Fusion avoids laziness but cannot capture cross-modal interactions, capping at 66.32% on CREMA-D versus MLA's 79.70%. In the missing-modality setting (Table 2, IEMOCAP), MLA surpasses both general methods (Late Fusion, QMF) and dedicated missing-modality methods (MMIN, IF-MMIN, TATE, CPM-Net, CRA, AE, DCCA, DCCAE, CCA) at every missing rate from 10% to 70%, with the margin over MMIN narrowing from 3.23 pp at 10% to 0.45 pp at 70%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of joint multimodal optimization (as in OGM-GE and QMF) by reframing multimodal learning as an alternating unimodal process, where each modality's encoder is optimized independently at each step. The gradient modification mechanism for the shared head borrows the Recursive Least Square algorithm from adaptive filtering literature and draws conceptual parallels to catastrophic forgetting mitigation techniques. The test-time uncertainty-based fusion extends the idea of dynamic weighting from ensemble methods to the multimodal setting, where each modality's predictive entropy determines its contribution to the final prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MLA offers a conceptually clean and practically simple solution to modality laziness that is compatible with both complete and missing modality scenarios without requiring paired data during training. The consistent improvements across five diverse datasets (audio-video, image-text, audio-image-text) and the demonstration that the modality gap widens under MLA provide mechanistic insight into why the approach works. Open questions include scalability to more than three modalities, applicability to generative or retrieval tasks, and whether the alternating schedule (simple round-robin via t mod M) is optimal or whether adaptive scheduling could further improve performance. The method's reliance on a shared head also raises questions about how it handles modalities with vastly different representational capacities.
