## Problem

Existing grasp detection methods largely ignore natural language as a conditioning signal, and the few language-driven approaches either rely on two-stage grounding-then-grasping pipelines or depend on commercial foundation models unavailable on resource-constrained robots. No large-scale dataset pairs diverse scene images with fine-grained grasp instructions at both the object and part level, making it difficult to train end-to-end language-conditioned grasp detectors.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB scene image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'grasp the knife at its handle' or 'give me the coffee cup') and outputs a 2D grasp pose parameterized as a rectangle, compatible with parallel-jaw grippers. The text can reference either a whole object or a specific part, and the model must disambiguate among multiple objects in cluttered scenes. For real-robot deployment, the 2D rectangle is transformed into a 6DoF pose via a RealSense D435i depth camera.

## Method

LGD formulates language-driven grasp detection as a conditional diffusion process that denoises a noisy grasp pose x_T back to the clean pose x_0, conditioned on the image and text. A vision encoder and text encoder feed into an ALBEF fusion module whose attention mask serves as the estimated clean signal x̃₀. The key novelty is a contrastive loss (a hinge on the rescaled distance between x̃₀ and x_T) added to the standard diffusion denoising loss, which explicitly pulls the ALBEF-derived estimation toward the ground-truth grasp during training. Proposition 1 proves that driving this contrastive loss to zero guarantees x̃₀ converges to x₀. The full training objective is L_total = L_contrastive + L_diffusion, where the diffusion term follows the signal-prediction formulation of Ramesh et al. and the contrastive term operates on the final timestep T with the variance schedule α_T.

## Results

On the Grasp-Anything++ dataset, LGD with CLIP text embeddings achieves a success rate of 0.48 (seen), 0.42 (unseen), and 0.45 (harmonic mean), outperforming the best baseline CLIP-Fusion (0.40/0.29/0.33) by margins of +0.08, +0.13, and +0.12 respectively. The success rate metric requires IoU > 25% with the ground truth grasp and an offset angle < 30°. In real-world robotic experiments with a KUKA LBR iiwa R820, LGD achieves 0.43 (single object) and 0.42 (cluttered scene) success rates, again surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGD with CLIP embeddings dominates all five baselines across seen, unseen, and harmonic-mean success rates. The closest competitor, CLIP-Fusion (0.40/0.29/0.33), is outperformed by LGD by +0.08 on seen and +0.13 on unseen classes, with the gap widening notably on unseen objects where language grounding is most critical. CLIPORT (0.36/0.26/0.29) and GR-ConvNet+CLIP (0.37/0.18/0.24) trail further behind, while Det-Seg-Refine+CLIP (0.30/0.15/0.20) and GG-CNN+CLIP (0.12/0.08/0.10) show substantially weaker performance, particularly on unseen classes. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing the contrastive loss drops H from 0.45 to 0.42, and removing the x̃₀ prediction entirely collapses performance to 0.10, validating both design choices. In the zero-shot (no-text) setting (Table 5), LGD without the language branch still leads on all five datasets (e.g., 0.68 H on Grasp-Anything++, 0.77 on Jacquard), and cross-dataset transfer (Table 6) shows training on Grasp-Anything++ yields 10–33% improvements over training on any single conventional dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the language-driven grasp detection line initiated by CLIPORT and CLIP-Fusion, which typically decompose the problem into visual grounding followed by grasp synthesis. It departs from this two-stage paradigm by formulating grasp detection as a conditional diffusion generation problem, where a contrastive training objective explicitly guides the denoising process using vision-language feature maps (via ALBEF) as a pseudo-estimate of the target pose. The dataset creation pipeline repurposes foundation models (ChatGPT, Stable Diffusion, SAM, OFA, VLPart) in a novel compositional pipeline, building on the earlier Grasp-Anything dataset but scaling it to 10M prompts with part-level annotations. Theoretically, the work connects contrastive learning for fine-grained discrimination with the variational bound of conditional diffusion models, providing a Proposition that guarantees convergence of the predicted pose to ground truth as the contrastive loss vanishes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Grasp-Anything++ dataset (1M images, 10M prompts, 3M objects) fills a critical gap: prior grasp datasets largely ignore the text modality, and existing language-driven datasets (e.g., DailyGrasp) are small and lack part-level granularity. The demonstration that a fully synthetic, foundation-model-generated dataset outperforms real-image datasets (Jacquard, Cornell) for cross-dataset training is practically significant for scaling grasp research. Key limitations include the absence of depth information (hindering direct 3D robotic deployment), dependence on ChatGPT API access for dataset creation, and the gap between 2D rectangle grasps and true 6DoF manipulation. Open questions include extending to 3D via image-to-depth or text-to-3D models, and whether the contrastive-diffusion formulation generalizes beyond parallel grippers to dexterous hands.
