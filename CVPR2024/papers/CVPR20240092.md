## Problem

Appearance-based gaze estimation CNNs suffer from severe cross-domain performance degradation because the final fully connected regression layer (mapping 512D features to 3D gaze) overfits to gaze-irrelevant factors during end-to-end training. Existing domain adaptation methods require target domain data, which is often inaccessible in real-world deployment. This paper addresses the gap by replacing the FC layer with an analytical, low-parameter projection that generalizes to unseen domains without any target domain samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB face images (normalized to [0,1], no data augmentation) and outputs a 3D unit gaze direction vector. At inference, the pretrained CNN extracts a 512D feature, which is projected to 3D via Isomap using a geodesic distance map built from source domain samples; target domain features are simply concatenated to this precomputed map. The final gaze is obtained by a Sphere Alignment step involving only 10 parameters (sphere center, 3×3 rotation matrix, 2 scale factors, 2 biases), making the regression head extremely lightweight compared to a standard FC layer with thousands of weights.

## Method

The AGG framework consists of two modules. The Geodesic Projection Module (GPM) replaces the FC layer by projecting 512D image features into 3D space via Isomap (geodesic distance), producing a 'PGF Sphere' whose surface distribution mirrors the gaze unit sphere; gaze is then predicted by aligning this sphere to the gaze sphere through rotation and linear fitting of Euler angles (10 learnable parameters total). The key insight is that geodesic distance between extracted features is proportional to angular gaze differences, a relationship verified across four datasets and multiple architectures. The Sphere-Oriented Training (SOT) module further optimizes the CNN by training it to produce features whose Isomap projection matches the analytically computed ideal PGF positions derived from gaze labels; since Isomap is non-differentiable, a 3-layer MLP (Isometric Propagator) is first trained to approximate Isomap and then used as a fixed surrogate during backpropagation. At test time, the Isometric Propagator is discarded and the true Isomap is used, while the Sphere Alignment parameters remain fixed.

## Results

The AGG framework is evaluated on 12 cross-domain settings across four datasets (ETH-XGaze, Gaze360, MPIIFaceGaze, EyeDiap), with gaze estimation error in degrees as the metric (lower is better). ResNet-18+AGG achieves the best overall cross-domain performance, reaching 7.07° on D_E→D_D, 7.87° on D_G→D_M, and 7.93° on D_G→D_D, outperforming all SOTA baselines in 3 out of 4 settings. ResNet-50+AGG achieves the single best result of 5.91° on D_E→D_M. The framework improves the baseline model's cross-domain accuracy by up to 35.79% without using any target domain data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PureGaze [8] (the closest SOTA generalization method), ResNet-18+AGG wins on D_E→D_D (7.07° vs 7.48°), D_G→D_M (7.87° vs 9.28°), and D_G→D_D (7.93° vs 9.32°), while tying on D_E→D_M (7.10° vs 7.08°). Against ADL [12], AGG wins on all four settings, with the largest margin on D_G→D_D (7.93° vs 11.86°, a 33% reduction). Against Full-Face [38], AGG achieves substantially lower errors across all settings (e.g., 7.07° vs 30.15° on D_E→D_D). CA-Net [6] and LatentGaze [18] are either unavailable for certain settings or perform considerably worse (CA-Net reaches 27–31° on D_G settings). ResNet-50+AGG further narrows the gap, achieving the best single result of 5.91° on D_E→D_M, though it underperforms ResNet-18+AGG on D_G→D_D (11.36° vs 7.93°), suggesting the smaller backbone generalizes better from the Gaze360 source domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the gaze generalization line initiated by PureGaze (Cheng et al., AAAI 2022), which purifies gaze features during source-domain training, and departs from the broader domain-adaptation literature (adversarial, contrastive, collaborative methods) that requires target-domain data. Conceptually, it repurposes the classical Isomap dimensionality-reduction technique as a drop-in replacement for the final fully-connected regression layer, grounded in the physical observation that geodesic distance in the learned feature space is proportional to angular gaze difference — a connection to earlier work by Lu et al. on measuring eye rotation via geodesic distance. The Sphere-Oriented Training module then closes the loop by back-propagating through a learned surrogate of Isomap to refine the feature extractor, a design that has no direct precedent in prior gaze estimation architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10-parameter analytical replacement for a thousands-parameter FC layer offers an explainable, overfitting-resistant alternative that consistently improves cross-domain accuracy across 12 settings without any target-domain data, which is practically important for deployment in unseen environments. However, the approach is constrained to 3D unit-vector gaze representation (Euler-angle labels change the geometry to a plane), relies on an MLP-based Isometric Propagator as a training-time compromise, and requires O(N²) Isomap computation at test time, all of which limit immediate adoption. The authors note the framework could extend to other physically-grounded regression tasks such as pose estimation, but this remains unexplored. A key open question is whether a differentiable or amortized geodesic projection could eliminate the Isometric Propagator entirely and further close the within-dataset accuracy gap.
