## Problem

Class-conditional GANs trained on long-tailed data suffer from mode collapse in tail classes, producing low-quality and undiverse samples because the discriminator receives insufficient examples from rare classes. While long-tail recognition has been extensively studied, generative modeling under heavy class imbalance remains underexplored, and existing regularization techniques (GSR, NoisyTwins) provide only limited relief when tail classes contain as few as 2–5 training images.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a latent noise vector z and a class label y and produces a single high-resolution synthetic image (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for AnimalFaces, 128×128 for LSUN). During training, the generator simultaneously emits a low-resolution unconditional image (e.g., 8×8) alongside the high-resolution conditional image; the low-res output is fed to a separate unconditional discriminator pathway and discarded at test time. The discriminator thus operates on two inputs: the full-resolution image for the conditional loss and the downsampled low-res image (passed through a 1×1 'fromRGB' convolution) for the unconditional loss.

## Method

UTLO dissects the generator's synthesis network into a low-resolution subnetwork G_l and a high-resolution subnetwork G_h, and generates two style vectors from the same latent z via a shared style-mapping network: w_z with class information zeroed (fed to G_l) and w_z,y with class conditioning (fed to G_h). This blocks class-conditional information from the early layers, forcing them to learn class-independent features (backgrounds, object configurations, spatial layouts) that are shared across head and tail classes. The discriminator is analogously split into D_h and D_l, where D_l is shared between the conditional and unconditional pathways but terminates in two separate fully-connected prediction heads. The final training objective is a weighted sum of the standard class-conditional GAN loss and an unconditional GAN loss computed on the low-resolution images, with a hyperparameter λ controlling the balance. The key insight is that head–tail similarity is highest at low resolutions, so unconditional training at those layers acts as a knowledge-sharing channel from abundant head classes to scarce tail classes without sacrificing class-specific control at higher resolutions.

## Results

On CIFAR10-LT (ρ=100), UTLO achieves FID 6.8, FID-FS 13.4, KID 2.8, and KID-FS 5.4, outperforming all baselines. On the more challenging CIFAR100-LT (tail classes with as few as 5 training images), UTLO reaches FID 9.9 and FID-FS 21.8. On the highly imbalanced LSUN5-LT (ρ=1000, 128×128), UTLO reduces FID from 15.0 to 10.9 and FID-FS from 60.2 to 43.6 compared to the PGAN+DA baseline, a ~30% improvement. On AnimalFaces-LT, UTLO achieves FID 26.2 and FID-FS 48.4, the best across all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UTLO consistently outperforms all four StyleGAN2-ADA-based baselines (Transitional, GSR, NoisyTwins) across every dataset and metric. The most notable margin is against GSR on CIFAR10-LT (ρ=100), where UTLO achieves FID-FS 13.4 vs. GSR's 24.3 (a ~45% reduction), and against NoisyTwins on AnimalFaces-LT where UTLO reaches FID 26.2 vs. 29.4. On LSUN5-LT, UTLO surpasses the PGAN(FastGAN)+DA baseline by ~30% on all metrics (FID 10.9 vs. 15.0; FID-FS 43.6 vs. 60.2), and notably GSR actually degrades the PGAN baseline (FID 15.7 vs. 15.0), while UTLO still improves it. The paper also shows that an unconditional StyleGAN2-ADA baseline achieves a lower overall FID (39.4) than the conditional baseline (51.4) on AnimalFaces-LT, but performs far worse on FID-FS (104.1 vs. 87.1), demonstrating that standard FID/KID can be misleading in long-tail settings and motivating the proposed FID-FS/KID-FS metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on StyleGAN2-ADA and Projected GAN architectures, modifying their generator and discriminator to split into unconditional (low-resolution) and conditional (high-resolution) pathways. It departs from prior long-tail GAN work (GSR, NoisyTwins) that addressed mode collapse through regularization, and from Transitional-cGAN which used a temporal schedule to gradually inject class information. UTLO's key conceptual departure is architectural rather than temporal: it permanently removes class-conditional information from low-resolution layers, making the generator partially unconditional by design rather than transitioning between training phases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely underexplored setting (long-tail cGAN training) with a simple, architecture-level solution that is orthogonal to existing regularization techniques, making it potentially composable with them. The proposed FID-FS/KID-FS metrics fill a practical gap in evaluating long-tail generative models, where standard FID/KID are dominated by head classes. Limitations include the need for architectural modification (not plug-and-play), a resolution hyperparameter that requires tuning, and evaluation primarily on datasets up to 128×128 resolution. Open questions include whether the approach extends to diffusion models, scales to very high resolutions, or generalizes beyond class-conditional generation to other conditioning signals.
