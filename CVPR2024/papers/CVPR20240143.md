## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal video grounding methods (TubeDETR, STCAT, STVGFormer) are trained in closed-set settings on small datasets (e.g., VidSTG has only 5.4k training videos) with predefined vocabularies, limiting their ability to generalize to unseen objects and actions. This paper introduces the Open-Vocabulary Spatio-Temporal Video Grounding task, asking how video grounding models can achieve robust performance in both closed-set and open-vocabulary scenarios without requiring large-scale video annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240143/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (128 sampled frames resized to 448 on the shorter side) and a natural language text prompt as input. It outputs per-frame bounding boxes (x, y, w, h) for each frame within a predicted temporal interval, along with the temporal start and end frames (t_s, t_e) determined by maximizing the joint start/end probability distribution. Invalid temporal combinations where t_e ≤ t_s are masked out during inference.

## Method

The architecture is a DETR-like pipeline built on DINO, with all spatial modules and cross-attentions initialized from the pretrained Grounding DINO model while the Swin Transformer vision encoder and BERT text encoder remain frozen. A Cross-Modality Spatio-Temporal Encoder (6 layers) models temporal relations via MHSA along the frame axis and spatial relations via Deformable Attention, then fuses visual and textual features through a GLIP-style joint cross-attention. A Language-Guided Query Selection module initializes decoder queries using encoder outputs with sinusoidal temporal positional encoding. A Cross-Modality Spatio-Temporal Decoder (6 layers) refines queries through alternating temporal/spatial self-attention and visual/textual cross-attention, feeding into separate MLP heads for bounding box regression (L1 + GIoU loss) and temporal grounding (KL divergence on Gaussian heatmaps). The key insight is that freezing the foundation model's encoders while fine-tuning only the video-specific adapter modules preserves the rich open-vocabulary representations learned from ~26.5M image-text pairs, enabling strong generalization despite the limited video grounding training data.

## Results

On the VidSTG test set (closed-set), VideoGrounding-DINO achieves 51.97 m_tIoU and 34.67 m_vIoU for declarative sentences, and 50.83 m_tIoU and 29.89 m_vIoU for interrogative sentences, surpassing the previous best (STCAT) by ~1.1 m_tIoU and ~1.5 m_vIoU. On HC-STVG V1, it reaches 38.25 m_vIoU (vs. 36.90 for STVGFormer), and on HC-STVG V2, 39.88 m_vIoU (vs. 38.70 for STVGFormer). In the open-vocabulary setting (trained on VidSTG, evaluated zero-shot), it achieves 27.46 m_vIoU on HC-STVG V1 (vs. 22.58 for STCAT, +4.88) and 57.73% accuracy on YouCook-Interactions (vs. 55.90% for STCAT, +1.83%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoGrounding-DINO outperforms all 14 compared baselines across all four evaluation datasets. On VidSTG (closed-set), it beats STCAT (NeurIPS'22) by +1.15 m_tIoU and +1.53 m_vIoU (declarative), and +1.16 m_tIoU and +1.67 m_vIoU (interrogative); it also surpasses STVGFormer (CVPR'23) by +0.97 m_vIoU (declarative) and +1.39 m_vIoU (interrogative). On HC-STVG V1, it leads STVGFormer by +1.35 m_vIoU and +1.34 vIoU@0.5, and STCAT by +3.16 m_vIoU. On HC-STVG V2, it exceeds STVGFormer by +1.18 m_vIoU and +0.69 vIoU@0.5. In the open-vocabulary setting, the gains are substantially larger: +4.88 m_vIoU over STCAT and +10.62 m_vIoU over TubeDETR on HC-STVG V1, and +1.83% accuracy over STCAT on YouCook-Interactions, demonstrating that the frozen Grounding DINO backbone provides strong generalization that purely video-trained methods lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR-based video grounding lineage (TubeDETR, STCAT, STVGFormer) by initializing spatial modules from the Grounding DINO open-set detector and borrowing the joint visual-textual attention mechanism from GLIP. The key departure is treating video grounding as a temporal adaptation problem on top of a frozen spatial grounding foundation model, rather than training a full video-specific encoder from scratch. Prior video grounding methods are confined to closed-set supervised training on small datasets (VidSTG, HC-STVG), whereas this work explicitly targets the open-vocabulary generalization gap by leveraging the large-scale image-text pretraining that underpins spatial grounding models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first open-vocabulary evaluation protocol for spatio-temporal video grounding (on HC-STVG V1 and YouCook-Interactions), the paper highlights a critical generalization weakness in existing methods and provides a practical recipe—freeze a strong spatial grounding backbone, add temporal aggregation adapters—that practitioners can adopt. The authors acknowledge that the approach is ultimately limited by its reliance on image-text pretraining rather than video-language pretraining, and that a large-scale video grounding dataset with diverse natural language expressions remains an open prerequisite for truly robust open-vocabulary video understanding.
