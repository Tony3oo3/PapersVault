## Problem

Existing PEFT methods (Adapter, Prefix-tuning, LoRA) directly alter intermediate hidden states or inject out-of-distribution features into LLMs during instruction fine-tuning, which degrades pretrained knowledge and causes catastrophic forgetting—especially when training data is low-quality or narrowly scoped. The paper asks whether instruction-following ability can be improved by only modulating how the LLM attends to its own pretrained representations, rather than changing what those representations are.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For language-only tuning, the input is a text instruction formatted in the Stanford Alpaca template (instruction + context + response), processed through LLaMA-7B's 32 attention layers with Excitor blocks inserted in the topmost 30 layers. For multimodal tuning, an RGB image is encoded by a frozen CLIP/14-L encoder into a sequence of 768-dim visual embeddings (both CLS and patch tokens), which are concatenated with learnable prompts to form the Excitor's key and value. Outputs are free-form text responses, COCO-style image captions, or multiple-choice answers on ScienceQA, all generated via top-p sampling (temperature 0.1, top-p 0.75).

## Method

LLaMA-Excitor introduces 'indirect feature interaction': instead of adding trainable outputs to layer hidden states (Adapter/LoRA) or prepending tokens (Prefix-tuning), it only modifies the attention score matrix while leaving the pretrained Value vectors untouched, so each layer's output remains a linear combination of original Values within the LLM's pretrained distribution. Each Excitor block generates an extra similarity matrix S_l^extra by computing a low-rank Excitor Query from the input T_l, attending over fixed-length learnable prompts P_l (serving as both Key and Value) to reconstruct a variable-length Key_extra matching the input sequence, then dotting with the original Query. The extra scores are gated by a cold-start factor g_l (initialized N(0, 10⁻²) to avoid FP16 gradient vanishing) and added to the original scores before softmax. For the multimodal extension, frozen CLIP visual embeddings are low-rank projected and concatenated with P_l to form Key_excitor and Value_excitor, eliminating the need for a separate vision-language alignment module while preserving the purity of textual intermediate features.

## Results

On COCO Caption, LLaMA-Excitor achieves 49.7 BLEU@4 and 157.5 CIDEr, surpassing the previous SOTA (BLIP-2) by +6.0 BLEU@4 and +12.2 CIDEr using only 0.6M training data and no multi-modal alignment module. On ScienceQA, LLaMA-Excitor reaches 85.41% accuracy (base) and 88.39% with CLIP-L/14@336px + LoRA, approaching LLaVA's 90.92% (which uses a 13B backbone, full fine-tuning, and 558k pretraining data). On MMLU, LLaMA-Excitor is the only PEFT method that preserves inherent LLM capabilities, showing a +3.12% relative improvement over the original LLaMA-7B, while full fine-tuning drops by −6.63% and LLaMA-Adapter by −3.02%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On COCO Caption, LLaMA-Excitor (157.5 CIDEr) outperforms all 8 baselines, most notably BLIP-2 (145.3 CIDEr) by +12.2 CIDEr and +6.0 BLEU@4, despite using zero pretraining data versus BLIP-2's 129M. On ScienceQA, LLaMA-Excitor (85.41%) slightly trails LLaVA CoT w/o pretrain (85.81%) by 0.4%, but with the stronger CLIP-L/14@336px encoder plus LoRA it reaches 88.39%, still below LLaVA's full CoT result (90.92%) which uses a 13B backbone and 558k pretraining data; it surpasses LLaMA-Adapter (85.19%) and all other VQA baselines. On AlpacaEval (GPT-4 judged), Excitor wins 73/80 against Full-Finetuning and gains the majority of wins over both Alpaca-LoRA and LLaMA-Adapter. On MMLU, Excitor is the only method that does not degrade the original LLaMA-7B's multi-task accuracy (+3.12%), while Full-Finetuning (−6.63%), Alpaca-LoRA, and LLaMA-Adapter (−3.02%) all suffer measurable drops. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that combining Excitor with LoRA in the multi-modal setting adds +2.98% on ScienceQA, but in the text-only setting this combination causes a −4.14% MMLU drop, highlighting the tension between instruction-following gains and knowledge preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaMA-Excitor departs from the dominant PEFT paradigm (Adapter, LoRA, prefix-tuning) that directly modifies intermediate hidden states or prepends learnable tokens to inputs. Instead, it repurposes the learnable-prompt idea from prefix-tuning and the zero-init attention stabilization from LLaMA-Adapter [51] into a bypass module that reconstructs Keys and adjusts attention scores while leaving Values untouched, ensuring outputs remain within the pretrained LLM's distribution. For the multimodal extension, it avoids the standard vision-language alignment pipeline (as in BLIP-2, LLaVA) by feeding raw CLIP embeddings directly into the Excitor's Key/Value reconstruction, treating visual instruction following as a unified variant of textual instruction following.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a PEFT method can improve (rather than degrade) MMLU scores after instruction-tuning on 52K automatically-generated data is practically significant, as it suggests a path to instruction-following without the catastrophic forgetting that plagues full fine-tuning and most PEFT variants. The multimodal extension without a dedicated alignment module offers a lower-budget alternative to the standard CLIP-projector-LLM pipeline. However, evidence is limited to LLaMA-7B, a single 52K training set, and relatively small evaluation datasets; the authors themselves note the work is preliminary and open questions include generalization to other LLM families, multi-scale visual features, and whether the MMLU preservation holds at larger scale or with higher-quality instruction data.
