## Problem

Single-view 3D reconstruction with NeRF remains an open problem because occluded regions are invisible to the sensor. Existing approaches either hallucinate geometry via data priors (statistically plausible but not physically accurate) or exploit RGB shadows, which degrade under ambient light and low-albedo backgrounds. No prior method reconstructs both visible and occluded geometry from a single view using physically-grounded measurements that are robust to the low spatial and temporal resolutions of consumer-grade sensors.

## Input / Output

The method takes K transient images (each N_u × N_v × N_t, e.g. 512×512×391 bins at 128 ps resolution) captured from a fixed SPAD sensor while a pulsed laser sequentially illuminates K scene points, along with calibrated sensor and laser positions. From the trained NeRF volume density field, the method outputs metric depth maps from arbitrary viewpoints and 3D meshes via marching cubes. Unlike standard NeRF which consumes multi-view RGB images, here the 'single view' is a fixed sensor position with the laser scanned across the scene, and the supervision signal is time-of-flight histograms rather than pixel intensities.

## Method

PlatoNeRF trains a vanilla NeRF MLP (predicting volume density σ at 3D points) via analysis-by-synthesis on two-bounce lidar transients. Rendering proceeds in two stages: primary rays from the sensor estimate scene depth d₃ via standard NeRF compositing, and secondary rays from the estimated scene point to the virtual light source compute a shadow probability via transmittance along that ray. The loss combines a distance term (L2 on predicted vs. measured two-bounce time-of-flight, applied only to unshadowed pixels) and a shadow term (L2 on rendered shadow probability vs. a binary mask, applied to all pixels), with the shadow loss activated after 25k iterations once depth estimates stabilize. This unified single-pipeline approach simultaneously recovers visible depth from ToF peaks and occluded geometry from shadow presence/absence, eliminating the need for data priors or a separate shadow-carving step, and generalizes to lower sensor resolutions through the implicit representation's natural interpolation.

## Results

PlatoNeRF achieves the lowest L1 depth error across all four simulated scenes, with test-view L1 ranging from 0.0601 m (Bunny) to 0.0870 m (Dragon), and PSNR from 26.58 dB (Chair) to 30.26 dB (Bunny). On the point cloud metric, PlatoNeRF achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.0280 m, roughly 40% lower than BF Lidar (0.0465 m) and an order of magnitude lower than S³-NeRF (0.4129 m). On the real-world Bounce-Flash dataset, PlatoNeRF produces comparable geometry to BF Lidar but with substantially fewer artifacts and holes, particularly near edges and specular regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlatoNeRF consistently outperforms both baselines across all four simulated scenes and both depth and point-cloud metrics. Against BF Lidar [7] (the closest prior work using two-bounce lidar without learning), PlatoNeRF reduces test-view L1 depth error by 35–61% (e.g., 0.0862 vs 0.1837 m on Chair; 0.0836 vs 0.2151 m on Occlusion) and improves PSNR by 5–9 dB, while also achieving a 40% lower Chamfer distance (0.0280 vs 0.0465 m). Against S³-NeRF [44] (an RGB shadow-based NeRF method), PlatoNeRF reduces L1 error by 27–61% and improves PSNR by 3.8–4.8 dB; the gap is largest on the Occlusion scene where S³-NeRF struggles with relative depth. The advantage over BF Lidar is most pronounced on the Occlusion scene (L1 0.0836 vs 0.2151 m), where the implicit NeRF representation interpolates across regions lacking two-bounce signal that cause holes in BF Lidar's point cloud. On the real-world dataset, PlatoNeRF matches BF Lidar's overall geometry but produces far fewer artifacts near edges and specular surfaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

PlatoNeRF unifies two previously separate lines of work: the analytical two-bounce lidar reconstruction of Henley et al. (Bounce-Flash Lidar), which estimates visible depth and occluded geometry in a two-step pipeline using geometric constraints and shadow carving, and the NeRF-based shape-from-shadow literature (S3-NeRF, ShadowNeUS, DeepShadow) that learns scene geometry from RGB shadow observations. By replacing the analytical two-step approach with a single volumetric rendering framework supervised by lidar transients, the paper inherits NeRF's implicit smoothness and generalization while retaining lidar's physical grounding. It also departs from the broader non-line-of-sight imaging literature (one-bounce and three-bounce ToF) by focusing specifically on two-bounce paths, which encode both depth and shadow information in a single measurement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As single-photon lidars become embedded in consumer devices (phones, tablets, headsets), a method that extracts full 3D geometry—including occluded regions—from a single view without data priors or controlled lighting is practically significant for XR, robotics, and AV. The released simulated single-photon lidar dataset lowers the barrier for future research in learning-based lidar methods. Key limitations remain: the method assumes Lambertian reflectance, indoor scenes with multiple reflecting surfaces, and laser scanning (not flash illumination), and vanilla NeRF floaters persist. Open questions include whether the approach extends to specular surfaces, textured geometry, and whether combining lidar with RGB or data priors can further improve reconstruction quality.
