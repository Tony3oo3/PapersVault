## Problem

Existing prompt tuning methods for vision-language models generate domain- and task-specific features, which conflicts with the domain generalization requirement of producing domain-invariant representations that transfer to unseen domains. The paper addresses how to leverage the controllable language modality of VFMs like CLIP to disentangle invariant from domain-specific features in a way that is both interpretable and effective for generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed at 224×224 through CLIP's ViT-B/16 backbone) and outputs a class label over the target dataset's category set. Training uses a few-shot protocol with 16 samples per class across multiple source domains. The final prediction is a weighted sum of a domain-invariant softmax (from visual-text contrastive scoring) and a domain-specific prototype-based score, rather than a single classifier head.

## Method

The DPR framework operates in three stages. First, GPT-3 generates fine-grained class descriptions per domain, which are then summarized into domain-invariant descriptions; these guide CoOp-style learnable text prompts via L2 distillation and contrastive loss to produce disentangled domain-invariant and domain-specific text embeddings. Second, two separate VPT-based visual encoders extract domain-invariant and domain-specific image features, where the invariant branch is regularized by a domain-confusion loss (pushing features toward uniform domain distribution) and a knowledge-distillation loss anchoring to original CLIP features. Third, Domain-Specific Prototype Learning (DSPL) maintains a memory bank of per-class, per-domain prototypes (initialized as class means and fine-tuned via SGD), and at inference computes relevance-weighted prototype matching to produce a domain-specific prediction that is combined with the domain-invariant prediction via a learned trade-off parameter.

## Results

The proposed DPR method achieves the best average accuracy of 77.83% across five DG benchmarks, setting new SOTA on all datasets: 97.45% on PACS, 86.43% on VLCS, 86.13% on OfficeHome, 62.05% on DomainNet, and 57.10% on TerraIncognita. The largest gains over the strongest competitor (GESTUR, avg 75.52%) are on VLCS (+3.63%) and DomainNet (+3.15%). All experiments use a 16-shot few-shot training protocol with ViT-B/16 CLIP backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPR outperforms all 10 CLIP-based baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest competitor GESTUR (ICCV'23), DPR gains +1.45% on PACS, +3.63% on VLCS, +1.93% on OfficeHome, +3.15% on DomainNet, and +1.40% on TerraIncognita. On PACS, DPR (97.45%) narrowly edges out DPL (97.30%) and DUPRG (97.10%), while on DomainNet it leads DUPRG (59.60%) and CoOp (59.80%) by over 2%. The margin over the zero-shot CLIP baseline is particularly large on TerraIncognita (+23.70%), highlighting the benefit of domain-aware prompt tuning. Compared to the ResNet-50-based methods (e.g., DANN at 65.56% avg, SAGM at 66.10%), DPR's CLIP-based approach achieves a 11–12% average improvement, underscoring the advantage of leveraging vision-language foundation models for DG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP-based prompt tuning (CoOp for text prompts, VPT for visual prompts) and repurposes them for domain generalization by introducing a disentanglement objective that prior prompt-tuning-for-DG methods (e.g., DUPRG, DPL) lacked. It departs from adversarial or GAN-based disentangled representation learning (DADA, etc.) by using LLM-generated text descriptions as a natural, interpretable guide for separating domain-invariant from domain-specific visual features, rather than relying on complex disentangling losses. The domain-specific prototype learning module draws on the key-value cache paradigm from Tip-Adapter, but applies it to a relevance-inspired selection of domain prototypes at inference time rather than training-free adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that the text modality of a vision-language model is inherently easier to disentangle and can therefore serve as a supervisory signal for visual disentanglement—offers a practical, loss-free alternative to adversarial disentanglement objectives, potentially simplifying the training pipeline for DG. However, the method's dependence on GPT-3 for generating class and domain descriptions introduces a non-trivial external dependency and limits reproducibility; it also remains untested beyond image classification and few-shot (16-shot) settings. Open questions include whether the text-guided disentanglement transfers to dense prediction tasks, whether the prototype bank generalizes to truly novel domains with no visual overlap to any source domain, and whether the approach scales to the hundreds of classes in DomainNet without the prototype memory becoming a bottleneck.
