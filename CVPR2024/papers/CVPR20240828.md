## Problem

Existing constrained offline RL algorithms are designed for single-constraint (cumulative cost) problems, but real-world high-risk scenarios such as autonomous driving and quantitative investing require simultaneous satisfaction of both cumulative and state-wise cost constraints. Moreover, standard multi-constraint solvers like Lagrange multipliers and penalty functions are sensitive to initialization and introduce costly hyperparameter tuning, while OOD actions in offline settings further degrade Q-value estimation accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-collected offline dataset of transitions (augmented state, action, reward, cumulative cost, state-wise cost increment, next augmented state, done flag), where the augmented state is the original state concatenated with the running maximum state-wise cost. A safety factor φ controls the proportion of safe trajectories in the dataset to vary behavioral diversity. The output is a deterministic policy network that maps augmented states to continuous actions, evaluated on reward return, cumulative cost, and state-wise cost violation rate.

## Method

POCE reframes the multi-constraint objective via a Maximum MDP (MMDP) that converts state-wise costs into non-negative cost increments D_i(s̃,a) = max{c_i(s,a) − M_i, 0} accumulated over an augmented state s̃ = (s, M_i), reducing the problem to two cumulative constraints. For policy optimization, it avoids Lagrange multipliers by directly applying policy gradient to the primal problem: if both cost Q-values satisfy their thresholds, the policy maximizes reward; otherwise it minimizes the violated cost Q-value. For Q-value estimation, reward uses a CQL-style pessimistic penalty on the marginal distribution of unseen actions, while cumulative and state-wise cost Q-values use a novel conditional Bellman operator that applies standard Bellman backup for in-distribution actions but assigns pseudo-targets (max Q over N behavioral-policy samples) for OOD actions, provably preventing underestimation while guaranteeing convergence to a unique fixed point. A CVAE is trained to approximate the unknown behavioral policy needed by the conditional Bellman operator.

## Results

On the PointGoal and CarGoal tasks across safety factors φ ∈ {0.8, 0.6, 0.4}, POCE achieves a mean reward of 13.06, mean cumulative cost of 4.51 (below the threshold of 5), and mean state-wise cost violation rate of 0.09%, outperforming all three baselines on every metric. Under varying state-wise cost thresholds (Table 2), POCE maintains a mean reward of 13.01 and mean cumulative cost of 3.77, while baselines see their cost violation rates spike to 0.71–1.46% as thresholds tighten. POCE is the only method that consistently satisfies the cumulative cost constraint (cost < 5) across all experimental settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POCE outperforms all three baselines (BCQ-Lag, CCRR, CPQ-Lag) on every metric across both PointGoal and CarGoal tasks. In terms of reward, POCE achieves a mean of 13.06 versus 11.78 (BCQ-Lag), 11.92 (CCRR), and 12.64 (CPQ-Lag). On cumulative cost, POCE's mean of 4.51 is the only value below the threshold of 5, while baselines range from 5.61 to 7.44, meaning none of the baselines reliably satisfy the cumulative cost constraint. The state-wise cost violation rate gap is even more pronounced: POCE achieves 0.09% mean violation versus 0.19% (BCQ-Lag), 0.21% (CCRR), and 0.28% (CPQ-Lag). CPQ-Lag achieves the highest single-task reward on CarGoal (14.66 at φ=0.8) but at the cost of the worst cumulative cost (8.59) and highest violation rate (0.38%), illustrating a reward–safety trade-off that POCE avoids. Under tighter state-wise thresholds (Table 2), baselines' violation rates degrade sharply (e.g., CPQ-Lag reaches 2.98% at D̄ᵢ=0.4 on PointGoal), while POCE remains more stable, further confirming its advantage in safety-critical settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of constrained RL—CMDP for cumulative costs and SCMDP for state-wise costs—into a single multi-constraint offline RL formulation (MMDP), drawing the state-wise-to-increment transformation from SCPO [61]. For the optimization side, it departs from the dominant primal-dual (Lagrange multiplier) paradigm by adopting the primal policy gradient approach of CRPO [45], and for Q-value estimation it combines CQL's pessimistic penalty for rewards [20] with a conditional Bellman operator adapted from MCQ [28] to prevent underestimation of cost Q-values under OOD actions. The deterministic policy gradient parameterization follows DDPG [35], and the CVAE-based behavioral policy estimation is a practical addition for the offline setting where the true behavioral policy is unknown.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, robotics, quantitative investing) require simultaneous enforcement of both cumulative and per-step cost limits, yet prior offline safe RL methods handle only one type. The MMDP formulation and the primal optimization approach reduce hyperparameter sensitivity compared to Lagrange-based methods, which is a meaningful practical advantage. However, experiments are limited to relatively low-dimensional Safety-gym and Mujoco tasks, no standardized multi-constraint offline dataset yet exists, and the CVAE approximation of the behavioral policy introduces an additional modeling assumption. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional continuous control, the tightness of the Remark 4.2 condition (D̄ᵢ ≤ (1−γ)c̄ᵢ) in practice, and whether the conditional Bellman operator's conservatism is sufficient for safety-critical deployment.
