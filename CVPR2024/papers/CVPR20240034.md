## Problem

Existing fair disentanglement methods assume target labels and sensitive attributes are independent, but real-world data bias creates inherent correlation between them, making it impossible to simultaneously achieve latent code independence and perfect information recovery. Features that encode both sensitive and target information (e.g., mustache relating to both gender and attractiveness) further make perfect separation unattainable, leading to contradictory optimization objectives and degraded performance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (CelebA, UTKFace, Dogs and Cats) or tabular records (Adult income) jointly with their sensitive attribute and target labels in a fully supervised setting. It outputs a four-part disentangled latent vector [z_X, z_Y, z_R, z_A] of 512 dimensions (ResNet-18 encoder for vision) or 32 dimensions (3-layer MLP for tabular), where z_R is the novel sensitive-relevant subspace. Downstream, z_Y feeds a classifier for fair prediction, while swapping z_A and z_R between source and reference samples enables controlled counterfactual image generation.

## Method

FADES introduces a sensitive relevant code z_R that absorbs the information shared between target Y and sensitive attribute A, then enforces conditional independence Y ⊥ A | z_R by minimizing a conditional mutual information (CMI) loss computed via batch-level marginalization over z_Y and z_A. A total correlation loss (inherited from FactorVAE) additionally enforces factorized independence across all four latent subspaces using in-batch permutation. An information bottleneck regularization term −(H(Ŷ|z_R) + H(Â|z_R)) prevents z_R from trivially capturing all information, forcing it to retain only the correlated portion. The final objective combines the VAE ELBO (with separate decoders for Y from z_Y∪z_R and A from z_A∪z_R), the CMI loss, the total correlation loss, and the bottleneck term, all weighted by tunable hyperparameters λ_CMI, λ_TC, and β.

## Results

On the four downstream classification benchmarks, FADES achieves the best Equalized Odds (EOD) across all datasets: 0.032 on CelebA, 0.057 on UTKFace, 0.055 on Dogs and Cats, and 0.096 on Adult, while maintaining competitive accuracy (0.918, 0.802, 0.769, 0.845 respectively). On the C-MNIST color-bias reconstruction task, FADES recovers digits at 93.46% accuracy, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: FairFactorVAE at 87.33%), though FairDisCo achieves higher color recovery (99.31% vs. 95.31%). FADES also achieves the best Demographic Parity on UTKFace (0.102) and Dogs and Cats (0.086).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FADES achieves the lowest EOD on all four classification datasets, with particularly large margins over GVAE on UTKFace (0.057 vs. 0.207) and CelebA (0.032 vs. 0.049), and over ODVAE on Adult (0.096 vs. 0.257). GVAE is the closest competitor, matching or slightly exceeding FADES accuracy on CelebA (0.919 vs. 0.918), UTKFace (0.819 vs. 0.802), and Adult (0.851 vs. 0.845), but consistently shows 2–4× higher EOD. FFVAE and FairDisCo show the weakest fairness on UTKFace (EOD 0.271 and 0.266) and the lowest accuracy on Dogs and Cats (0.727 and 0.680). On C-MNIST reconstruction, FADES leads in digit recovery (93.46%) by a wide margin over GVAE (85.98%) and FFVAE (77.39%), but FairDisCo achieves the best color recovery (99.31% vs. 95.31%), likely because it uses the ground-truth color label at decode time. Overall, FADES provides the best accuracy–fairness trade-off without requiring adversarial training or domain-specific causal graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

FADES extends the VAE-based fair disentanglement lineage (β-VAE, FactorVAE, FFVAE, GVAE, ODVAE) by replacing the standard mutual information / total correlation objective with a conditional mutual information (CMI) loss, introducing a dedicated sensitive-relevant code z_R that absorbs the information shared between target label Y and sensitive attribute A. This departs from prior disentanglement methods that assume Y ⊥ A (making the fairness and performance objectives contradictory under data bias) and from correlation-aware causal-graph approaches (e.g., Kim et al. 2021) that require domain knowledge to specify which features are descendants of the sensitive attribute. The CMI formulation is conceptually closer to Moyer et al.'s mutual-information-based invariant learning but applied in a conditional, multi-code setting, and the information-bottleneck regularizer on z_R prevents the trivial solution of encoding all information in the sensitive-relevant code.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental and previously underappreciated contradiction in fair disentanglement: under realistic data bias, enforcing independence between target and sensitive codes while simultaneously demanding full recovery of both from their respective codes is mathematically impossible, leading to unstable optimization and degraded utility. By routing the correlated information through z_R and minimizing CMI, FADES resolves this tension without adversarial training or hand-crafted causal graphs, offering a simpler and more general recipe. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous sensitive attributes, whether the information-bottleneck weight β can be set automatically rather than via grid search, and how the method behaves when the sensitive attribute is multi-class or partially observed. The evaluation, while spanning four datasets and three task types, is limited to relatively small-scale benchmarks (CelebA, UTKFace, C-MNIST, Adult), leaving scalability to larger vision or language tasks untested.
