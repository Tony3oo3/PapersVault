## Problem

Large pretrained visual foundation models for remote sensing are typically trained on optical RGB imagery, yet the domain relies heavily on heterogeneous modalities such as SAR and multispectral data. Adapting these models to unseen modalities via full fine-tuning is computationally prohibitive and fails on small labeled datasets, leaving a gap in how to efficiently bridge the source-to-target distribution shift without retraining the entire model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240086/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 remote sensing patches (RGB, multispectral, or SAR) along with a frozen pretrained ViT-L foundation model (MAE, SatMAE, or Scale-MAE). During self-supervised adaptation, 75% of image tokens are randomly masked and only the reconstruction loss on masked patches is computed. The output is a set of lightweight SLR adapter parameters (≈1–2% of model size) that, when combined with a task-specific head, produce class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks on the target domain.

## Method

The core contribution is the Scaled Low-Rank (SLR) adapter, which augments each linear projection in a ViT block with two learnable element-wise scaling vectors (s_i^1 before, s_i^2 after) and a pair of low-rank matrices (W_i^1 ∈ R^{D×r}, W_i^2 ∈ R^{r×D}) placed in parallel with the frozen original weight matrix W_i. This design combines the activation rescaling of (IA)³ with the low-rank decomposition of LoRA, yielding 2·D·r + 2·D trainable parameters per adapter versus D² for the original transform. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) self-supervised continual pre-training with the MAE reconstruction objective on unlabeled target-domain imagery, where only adapter parameters are updated via SGD while all pretrained weights remain frozen; (2) supervised fine-tuning of the adapters (optionally only the scaling vectors) together with a task head on small labeled sets. Across 8 remote sensing datasets and three foundation models, this 1–2% parameter budget outperforms full-model fine-tuning by 0.35–1.49% on average and improves few-shot (k=10) accuracy by 6–10%.

## Results

Across 8 remote sensing datasets, SLR adapters improve average linear evaluation accuracy by +6.37% (MAE: 63.46→69.83%), +2.93% (SatMAE: 62.81→65.74%), and +3.68% (Scale-MAE: 64.69→68.37%) over the unadapted foundation models. In the fine-tuning setting, SLR adapter fine-tuning outperforms full-model fine-tuning on average by +0.35% (MAE), +1.12% (SatMAE), and +1.49% (Scale-MAE) while training only 1–2% of parameters. In few-shot (k=10) experiments, SLR fine-tuning improves accuracy by +6% on EuroSAT and +8% on EuroSAT-SAR over full fine-tuning, and the SLR Scale variant (training only 0.5M of 303M parameters) achieves +10% over full fine-tuning on EuroSAT-SAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the adapter design comparison (Table 5, EuroSAT-SAR), SLR achieves 87.14±0.1% accuracy, outperforming LoRA (85.86±0.3%) by +1.28%, BitFit (80.34±2.4%) by +6.80%, NormTuning (79.00±3.1%) by +8.14%, and (IA)³ (76.72±3.5%) by +10.42%. SLR also exhibits the lowest variance (±0.1) among all methods, indicating more stable training. In the main fine-tuning comparison (Table 1), SLR FT surpasses full-model fine-tuning on average across all three foundation models (MAE +0.35%, SatMAE +1.12%, Scale-MAE +1.49%), while training only 1–2% of parameters versus 100%. In few-shot settings, SLR Scale (training only 0.5M / 303M parameters) outperforms full fine-tuning by +5% on EuroSAT and +10% on EuroSAT-SAR at k=10, demonstrating that the scaling-vector-only variant is particularly effective when labeled data is scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) lineage—particularly LoRA's low-rank decomposition—by augmenting it with learnable scaling vectors (SLR adapters) and coupling it with a self-supervised masked-autoencoding pre-training phase on the target domain. It builds directly on geospatial foundation models (SatMAE, Scale-MAE) that were pre-trained on optical remote sensing data, and positions itself against both full fine-tuning and other PEFT methods (BitFit, (IA)³, NormTuning) in the remote sensing domain. The key conceptual departure from prior PEFT work is the two-stage design: self-supervised adapter training on unlabeled target-domain data before supervised task adaptation, which draws on continual pre-training literature for geospatial models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for remote sensing practitioners who face a combinatorial explosion of sensor modalities (RGB, multispectral, SAR, hyperspectral) and cannot afford to fine-tune a full 300M+ parameter model for each. The finding that 1–2% of parameters, trained self-supervised, can match or exceed full fine-tuning across 8 datasets and 3 foundation models provides a concrete, low-cost recipe for modality adaptation. The modular adapter design also hints at a broader framework where a single foundation model carries lightweight, swappable adapters per modality, reducing storage and compute for multi-modal deployment. Limitations include potential degradation when the source–target distribution gap is extreme, and the method is validated only on ViT-based MAE models, leaving open whether it generalizes to other architectures or truly heterogeneous modalities (e.g., vision to audio).
