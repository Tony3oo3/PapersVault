## Problem

Existing coral reef analysis relies on labor-intensive sparse point annotations (CPCe, CoralNet) that introduce estimation bias and cannot delineate irregular coral boundaries, while generic segmentation models like SAM produce over-segmented, semantically agnostic masks that fail on the complex, crowded, and visually degraded coral imagery. No foundation model existed that could perform dense, semantically-aware coral segmentation with zero-shot generalization to unseen reef sites.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241910/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

CoralSCOP accepts coral reef images (downsampled to 1024 px shortest side) with optional prompts: random points inside coral masks, bounding boxes, free-text descriptions (e.g., 'a red branching coral'), or a reference coral mask for mask-referring segmentation. It outputs dense per-coral-instance masks with 4096-dim feature vectors per mask (enabling cosine-similarity-based mask matching), binary or multi-class semantic labels (coral/non-coral, growth form, genus), and downstream coral composition statistics computed from mask areas. In automatic mode no prompt is required; in instruction-following mode a single BBOX_image prompt combined with a CLIP text embedding yields one targeted mask.

## Method

CoralSCOP builds on SAM (ViT-B/L/H backbone) and introduces a parallel semantic branch inside the lightweight mask decoder: a shared MLP projects the 4096-dim mask feature vectors F_o into a semantic space, with attention-based feature interaction between the mask-generation and semantic-discrimination paths. A key training strategy—'training with negatives'—uses 978,968 SAM-generated masks that do not overlap labeled corals as 'non-coral' negatives, computed with a binary cross-entropy loss alongside Dice, Focal, and IoU losses, which alleviates over-segmentation while preserving zero-shot generalization. For downstream adaptation, user-defined tuning freezes the image encoder and optimizes only the semantic MLP on few-shot (10–40 image) samples for growth-form or genus classification, while instruction-following tuning adds a frozen CLIP text embedding (projected via a linear layer) element-wise to the BBOX_image prompt tokens and trains the full decoder on 46,610 positive/negative text-mask pairs to suppress hallucination. The entire model is pre-trained for 5 epochs on the newly curated CoralMask dataset (41,297 images, 330,144 coral masks) using composite prompts of 1–4 random points plus BBOX_mask.

## Results

On the CoralMask test set (941 images), CoralSCOP with ViT-L backbone achieves the best coral mask generation quality: IoU 79.13, pixel accuracy 81.11%, and MAE 0.0764 under the 1-point + BBOX prompt setting. Under the automatic (no-prompt) setting, CoralSCOP (ViT-L) reaches IoU 46.46 and MAE 0.1814, substantially outperforming SAM (ViT-H) at IoU 31.16 and MAE 0.5057. For sparse-to-dense conversion with 100 sparse points, CoralSCOP achieves the lowest average prediction error (e.g., 2.014 for non-coral vs. 3.052 for CPCe and 7.436 for SAM). In few-shot growth-form segmentation (10-shot), CoralSCOP improves mIoU by +19.62 over SegFormer (52.16 vs. 32.54).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoralSCOP (ViT-L, 1 pt + BBOX) outperforms SAM (ViT-H, same setting) by +6.33 IoU (79.13 vs. 72.80) and reduces MAE from 0.1173 to 0.0764, demonstrating that the parallel semantic branch effectively mitigates SAM's over-segmentation. Against the strongest specialist baseline SegFormer (Mit-B5, IoU 71.51), CoralSCOP (ViT-B) already leads by +2.49 IoU, and CoralSCOP (ViT-L) extends the gap to +7.62 IoU. Under the automatic (no-prompt) setting, CoralSCOP (ViT-B) achieves IoU 59.02 versus SAM's 31.16, a nearly 2× improvement, confirming that training-with-negatives addresses the false-positive problem that fine-tuning SAM alone cannot solve. In sparse-to-dense conversion (Table 3), CoralSCOP consistently yields the lowest prediction error across all growth-form categories and point counts (10–100), while SAM actually performs worse than the simple CPCe point-count baseline. For few-shot semantic tuning (Table 2), CoralSCOP surpasses SegFormer by +19.62 mIoU on growth form (10-shot) and +5.58 mIoU on genus (10-shot), validating the user-defined tuning strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoralSCOP builds directly on SAM as its backbone, extending the promptable segmentation paradigm to the underwater coral domain. It departs from SAM's semantic-agnostic design by introducing a parallel semantic branch in the mask decoder and a 'training with negatives' strategy that leverages SAM-generated false positives as negative supervision, addressing the over-segmentation that plagues SAM on coral imagery. The paper also positions itself against the long-standing sparse-point coral analysis pipeline (CPCe, CoralNet) and earlier dense coral segmentation efforts (CoralSeg, PLAS), which relied on fixed semantic categories and limited dataset scale, by framing coral segmentation as a foundation-model task with zero-shot generalization and user-defined multi-granularity semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoralSCOP and the accompanying CoralMask dataset (41,297 images, 330,144 masks) address a genuine scalability bottleneck in coral reef monitoring, replacing hours of manual sparse-point annotation with automated dense segmentation that produces more accurate coral statistics. The sparse-to-dense conversion capability is particularly impactful, as it allows existing CPCe point annotations to be upgraded to dense masks without re-annotation, reducing estimation bias. Open questions include whether the zero-shot generalization holds across vastly different reef biomes and coral species not represented in CoralMask, how the approach extends to video segmentation for time-lapse monitoring, and whether the parallel semantic branch design generalizes to other domain-specific foundation model applications beyond corals.
