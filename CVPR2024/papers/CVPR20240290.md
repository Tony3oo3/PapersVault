## Problem

Accurate markerless capture of hand-object contacts remains challenging because existing representations (skeletons, meshes, parametric models like MANO) cannot faithfully model hand shape, leading to misaligned and inaccurate contact estimates. Additionally, prior grasp datasets provide too few camera views to resolve self-occlusions, which is critical for neural field-based reconstruction. The paper tackles both gaps by introducing a new articulated Gaussian hand representation and a large multi-view dataset designed specifically to support it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB video (50+ synchronized cameras at 120 FPS, 1280×720) of a hand performing a grasp, along with a hand skeleton pose estimated via AlphaPose and inverse kinematics, and a separate object-only RGB capture. It produces an articulated 3D Gaussian hand model (21 bones, 26 DOF), a static 3D Gaussian object model, and two types of contact maps (instantaneous per-frame and temporally accumulated) computed as thresholded pairwise distances between Gaussian primitives. The rendered grasp image is a differentiable rasterization of the concatenated hand and object Gaussians, and contact maps are rendered by mapping contact distance to color.

## Method

MANUS-Hand extends 3D Gaussian splatting to an articulated hand by attaching Gaussian primitives to a 21-bone skeleton and deforming them via forward kinematics and linear blend skinning. Instead of directly assigning MANO skinning weights (which causes artifacts), the method allocates weights to a canonical grid (inspired by Fast-SNARF) and retrieves per-Gaussian weights via trilinear interpolation. Each subject's hand is optimized from ~20 poses using a composite loss of pixel L1, SSIM, perceptual, and an isotropic regularizer that penalizes highly anisotropic Gaussians to preserve contact-rendering quality. For grasp capture, the posed hand Gaussians and static object Gaussians are simply concatenated and rasterized together, exploiting the fact that Gaussian splatting naturally supports set union—something implicit representations cannot do. Contact estimation reduces to a nearest-neighbor distance query between hand and object Gaussian positions with a fixed threshold (τ=0.004), making it far cheaper than ray-marching or sampling-based approaches used by implicit hand models.

## Results

On the MANUS-Grasps contact evaluation (15 wet-paint sequences, 3 subjects), MANUS achieves mIoU of 0.206/0.152/0.275 and F1 of 0.335/0.251/0.424 across Subjects 1–3, outperforming MANO (mIoU 0.161/0.135/0.208; F1 0.270/0.228/0.338) and HARP (mIoU 0.173/0.148/0.224; F1 0.289/0.247/0.361) on every subject and metric. For visual quality on InterHand2.6M, MANUS-Hand scores PSNR 26.32, SSIM 0.9872, LPIPS 0.068 with a test time of 0.049 s, surpassing TAVA (PSNR 22.85, 11.00 s) but trailing LiveHand (PSNR 31.16, LPIPS 0.0278) on photorealism metrics. An ablation in Table 4 confirms that contact quality degrades monotonically as camera views drop from 30+ to 5, validating the need for dense multi-view capture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MANUS consistently outperforms both MANO and HARP on contact estimation across all three subjects and both metrics (mIoU and F1). Against MANO, MANUS gains roughly 0.04–0.07 mIoU and 0.05–0.09 F1; against HARP, the margins are smaller (≈0.03–0.05 mIoU, ≈0.04–0.06 F1) but still consistent. For visual quality on InterHand2.6M, MANUS-Hand beats TAVA on PSNR (+3.47 dB) and SSIM (+0.004) while being ~224× faster at inference (0.049 s vs 11.00 s), but it trails LiveHand on PSNR (26.32 vs 31.16) and LPIPS (0.068 vs 0.028), which the authors attribute to the absence of ambient occlusion and shadow mapping in their rendering pipeline. The paper explicitly frames the LiveHand/TAVA comparison as a sanity check on visual fidelity rather than the primary claim, since the core contribution is contact accuracy rather than photorealism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from static and dynamic scene reconstruction to articulated hand modeling, departing from the dominant implicit neural field approaches (LiveHand, TAVA, LISA) that are computationally expensive for contact estimation. It also moves away from parametric hand models (MANO) and skeleton-based representations that the authors argue cannot faithfully capture individual hand geometry, instead learning per-subject Gaussian states with grid-based skinning weights inspired by Fast-SNARF. The key conceptual departure is that explicit Gaussian primitives with known positions enable direct nearest-neighbor contact computation, avoiding the costly ray-marching or sampling required by implicit representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate hand-object contact estimation is a bottleneck for robotics grasp planning and mixed-reality interaction, and the paint-transfer ground truth protocol offers a physically grounded evaluation that prior datasets (thermal imaging, mocap) lack. The MANUS-Grasps dataset (7M+ frames, 50+ cameras, 30+ scenes) is a substantial practical resource that could benefit the broader hand-object interaction community. However, the method is limited to single-hand, static-object, direct-contact scenarios without modeling skin deformation or bimanual manipulation, and the contact metric improvements over MANO/HARP, while consistent, remain modest in absolute terms (mIoU ~0.15–0.28), leaving significant room for future work.
