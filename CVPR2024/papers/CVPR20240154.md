## Problem

Existing hyperbolic multimodal models (e.g., MERU) align image and text embeddings via geodesic-distance contrastive loss, which the authors prove theoretically and show empirically causes exponential collapse of the spatial region occupied by image embeddings, destroying hierarchical structure. The fundamental issue is that forcing spatial proximity between two inherently different modalities (abstract text vs. concrete images) is an ill-posed objective that conflicts with the hierarchical organization hyperbolic space is meant to capture.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (encoded by a ViT-S backbone) and natural language text prompts (encoded by a transformer text encoder) and maps them into the Lorentz model of d-dimensional hyperbolic space with learnable curvature. For zero-shot classification, the output is a class label selected by minimizing the average exterior angle between the image embedding and a set of text-prompt embeddings. For retrieval, the output is a ranked list of images or texts ordered by exterior angle relative to a query embedding. The network only parameterizes the space component of the Lorentz model; the time component is derived analytically via the hyperboloid constraint.

## Method

The core contribution is an angle-based contrastive loss that replaces geodesic distance as the cross-modal similarity metric. Rather than pulling matching image-text pairs closer in spatial proximity, the loss aligns them along a shared geodesic emanating from the origin: for positive pairs it minimizes the exterior angle α while maximizing β = π − α, and for negative pairs it does the reverse. This accepts the modality gap by allowing images to sit further from the origin than their textual counterparts while still encoding conceptual alignment through angular proximity. A centroid regularizer (using the Einstein midpoint) further enforces that the batch centroid of text embeddings is closer to the origin than that of image embeddings. The authors prove two propositions showing that the prior geodesic-contrastive + entailment-cone combination forces at least one image embedding outside the cone and causes exponential area collapse, whereas the angle-based loss has no such conflict since both its terms are mutually satisfiable.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification across 19 datasets, the proposed angle-based contrastive loss achieves the best Top-1 accuracy on 13 out of 19 benchmarks, with notable gains on CIFAR-10 (61.8% vs. 55.8% CLIP, 53.7% MERU) and ImageNet (29.7% vs. 28.5% CLIP, 28.6% MERU). For zero-shot retrieval, the method achieves the best Recall@5 on 3 of 4 tasks, with a particularly large margin on image-to-text COCO (32.6% vs. 26.5% MERU, 24.8% CLIP). On image hierarchy accuracy (Car Parts, depth-1, curvature 0.1), the method reaches 94.0% versus only 19.2% for MERU, and maintains near-constant performance across curvatures up to 3.0 where MERU fails to converge entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (Euclidean/spherical), the proposed method wins on 13 of 19 zero-shot classification datasets and 3 of 4 retrieval tasks, with the largest gains on fine-grained and structured datasets (CIFAR-10: +6.0%, CUB: +2.6%, EuroSAT: +12.7%). Against MERU (the primary hyperbolic baseline using geodesic contrastive + entailment loss), the method also wins on 13 of 19 classification datasets and 3 of 4 retrieval tasks, but the most dramatic differences appear in hierarchy preservation: on Car Parts depth-1 image hierarchy, the method achieves 94.0% accuracy versus MERU's 19.2% at curvature 0.1, and on OpenImages depth-2, 31.7% vs. 0.6%. Critically, MERU fails to converge at curvatures ≥ 0.5 (Table 5), while the proposed method maintains near-constant ImageNet accuracy (28.5–29.7%) across all tested curvatures up to 3.0, confirming that the geodesic contrastive loss is fundamentally incompatible with high-curvature hyperbolic geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MERU (Desai et al., 2023), the first attempt to port CLIP's contrastive framework into hyperbolic space, by replacing MERU's geodesic-distance contrastive loss with an angle-based contrastive loss that aligns cross-modal embeddings along geodesic rays from the origin rather than enforcing spatial proximity. The angle metric is derived from the exterior angle formalism of Ganea et al.'s entailment cones, effectively recasting the hard cone constraint as a smooth, contrastive objective. The paper departs from the broader hyperbolic contrastive learning line (Ge et al., Yue et al.) by arguing that minimizing geodesic distance between modalities is fundamentally incompatible with preserving hierarchical structure, a claim supported by two formal propositions about embedding-space collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that geodesic contrastive + entailment loss forces exponential contraction of the image embedding region (Proposition 2) provides a principled explanation for why prior hyperbolic multimodal models underutilize the space and only converge at near-Euclidean curvatures. The practical implication—that the proposed angle-based loss enables stable training at high curvatures where MERU fails—opens the door to exploiting the full exponential volume growth of hyperbolic space for hierarchical multimodal representations. Open questions include whether the modality-gap-accepting formulation generalizes beyond image-text pairs (e.g., audio, video), whether the curvature advantage persists at the scale of billion-parameter models, and whether the centroid regularizer is necessary or merely a helpful inductive bias.
