## Problem

Existing robustness benchmarks for semantic segmentation only evaluate global attribute variations (e.g., adverse weather, scene style) while ignoring local object-level attribute changes (color, material, pattern) that are equally prevalent in real-world deployment. Moreover, prior synthetic data generation methods (simulators, style transfer) produce images inconsistent with realistic scenes and cannot simultaneously cover both local and global variations while preserving ground-truth segmentation labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241067/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an RGB image from Pascal VOC validation set along with its pixel-level semantic segmentation mask and a text attribute prompt (e.g., 'change horse color to black'). It outputs an edited image where the specified attribute is altered while the original segmentation mask remains pixel-accurate, enabling direct reuse of labels without re-annotation. The editing is performed in a tuning-free manner on a pre-trained diffusion model, and the variation degree is controllable via a coefficient c that scales the strength of attribute modification.

## Method

The core contribution is a Mask-Guided Attention (MGA) mechanism integrated into the U-Net decoder of a Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion via PnP). MGA rectifies attention maps using binary object masks: in cross-attention, it strengthens or weakens the influence of text tokens corresponding to the target object on the masked region (Eq. 3); in self-attention, it suppresses attention scores between tokens belonging to different object regions while preserving intra-region interactions (Eq. 4). This is combined with ControlNet to further enforce semantic layout adherence. The text manipulation stage uses BLIP-2 to caption the source image and GPT-3.5 to generate attribute-specific prompt variations. The entire pipeline is tuning-free, requiring no retraining of the diffusion model, and produces edits that preserve object structure and background integrity as measured by DINO Dist and LPIPS.

## Results

On the Pascal-EA benchmark, the best close-set model (Segmenter, ViT-B) achieves 80.66 mIoU on the original validation set and 67.09 overall under all attribute variations, while the best open-vocabulary model (CATSeg, Swin-B) reaches 95.59 and 82.98 respectively. Material variations cause the largest local-attribute performance drops (−18.74% to −31.22% mIoU), far exceeding color (−8.27% to −12.01%) and pattern (−7.96% to −8.95%) variations. Sketch-style global variation is the most damaging perturbation overall, reducing average mIoU by 56.53 points, compared to only 7.25 and 8.14 for painting and snow. Notably, CNN-based models (DeepLabV3+, OCRNet) exhibit smaller overall performance declines than transformer-based counterparts (Segformer, Mask2Former), and stronger open-vocabulary backbones (SAM, Focal-T) do not guarantee better robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among close-set segmentation models, Segmenter (ViT-B) achieves the best overall robustness (67.09 mIoU, −13.57 drop), while Mask2Former (Swin-B) leads on most individual attributes but suffers the largest overall decline (−24.17) due to its extreme sensitivity to sketch style (21.82 mIoU). Surprisingly, CNN-based DeepLabV3+ and OCRNet show smaller overall drops (−17.86, −16.14) than transformer-based Segformer and Mask2Former (−16.38, −24.17), contradicting the assumption that transformers are inherently more robust. Among open-vocabulary methods, CATSeg (82.98) and OVSeg (79.38) substantially outperform the diffusion-based ODISE (69.79) and the holistic frameworks X-Decoder (73.57) and SEEM (73.13/67.19), with the SAM backbone in SEEM performing worse than the Focal-T backbone. For image editing quality (Table 4), the proposed PnP w/ MGA + ControlNet achieves the lowest DINO Dist (0.003 material, 0.001 color) and LPIPS (0.185, 0.156), outperforming PnP baseline (0.052/0.047 DINO), Prompt-to-Prompt (0.044/0.030), DiffEdit (0.068/0.053), and Blended-LDM (0.081/0.079). For weather generation realism (Table 5), the pipeline achieves CLIP Acc of 0.989–1.000 and FID of 137–187 across snow/rain/fog/night, beating simulator-based SHIFT (FID 242–274) and Fog Cityscapes (FID 164), though slightly trailing Multi-weather Cityscapes on night (FID 187 vs. 154). Against Stylized COCO (Table 6), the method achieves 0.895 vs. 0.574 CLIP Acc for object-level and 0.965 vs. 0.678 for background-level style transfer, with DINO Dist reduced from 0.075/0.084 to 0.002/0.004.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Plug-and-Play diffusion (PnP) framework for image-to-image translation and the Latent Diffusion Model architecture, adding a Mask-Guided Attention mechanism and ControlNet to enforce structural preservation during attribute editing. It departs from prior robustness benchmarks (ACDC, SHIFT, Multi-weather Cityscapes) that exclusively evaluate global scene-level shifts like adverse weather, by extending the evaluation scope to local object-level attributes (color, material, pattern). It also differentiates from diffusion-based counterfactual explanation methods (LANCE, ImageNet-E) that use text-guided editing but inevitably disturb irrelevant regions, making them unsuitable for segmentation evaluation where pixel-level label consistency is required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central finding—that local object attribute variations (especially material) cause performance drops comparable to or exceeding global weather changes—challenges the field's implicit assumption that robustness evaluation should focus primarily on global scene shifts. The observation that CNN-based models (DeepLabV3+, OCRNet) exhibit less overall degradation than transformer-based counterparts (SegFormer, Mask2Former), and that stronger backbones (SAM, Swin) with more training data do not guarantee better robustness, provides a useful counterpoint to the prevailing narrative that scale and architectural sophistication improve generalization. Limitations include the benchmark's restriction to Pascal VOC (object-centric, relatively small), the diffusion model's difficulty editing person attributes, and occasional spurious co-changes of unrelated attributes, leaving open the question of how to achieve perfectly isolated single-attribute edits at scale.
