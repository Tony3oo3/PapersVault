## Problem

Text-guided object inpainting models require users to manually craft prompts describing the object to insert, which is a barrier for inexperienced users and a time cost for experienced ones. Without explicit prompts, these models default to dominant training-distribution patterns (e.g., filling a sky mask with clouds rather than a bird). The paper asks whether a model can automatically suggest diverse, contextually-plausible prompts given only an image and a mask, balancing accuracy with creative diversity.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and an object mask (tight mask, convex hull, or bounding box) and produces K CLIP-text embeddings (768-dim) that can be directly injected into any CLIP-based inpainting model (e.g., Stable Diffusion, SmartBrush), or decoded into natural-language prompts via a GPT-2 text decoder or a category classifier. The image is encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder using 256-D patch embeddings (not the <cls> token) at both global (512×512) and local (center-cropped around the mask with 1.5× expansion) scales, which are concatenated as conditioning. Masks smaller than 1% or larger than 50% of the image area are filtered out during training.

## Method

The core contribution is a diffusion prior network (a 12-layer transformer with 12 heads, 128 hidden dims) trained to denoise CLIP-text embeddings conditioned on masked-image CLIP embeddings, effectively learning a masked-image-to-text mapping in CLIP space — the reverse direction of DALLE-2's text-to-image prior. Two variants are trained: CatDiff (diffusing category-label embeddings, decoded by a small transformer classifier) and CapDiff (diffusing local-caption embeddings, decoded by a fine-tuned GPT-2 language model). Two key design choices enable the accuracy-diversity tradeoff: (1) concatenating global and local CLIP image embeddings to balance scene context with mask-region precision, and (2) randomly augmenting mask shapes during training (tight, dilated, convex hull, bounding box) so the model learns to modulate output diversity based on how much shape information the mask provides. The stochastic nature of diffusion sampling naturally yields diverse prompt suggestions per input, and the 768-dim output embeddings are directly compatible with any CLIP-encoder-based inpainting pipeline.

## Results

On the OpenImages dataset (Brush2PromptBench), CapDiff achieves the highest quality scores among all methods: BLEU 0.177, ROUGE 0.427, and BERTScore 0.732 under tight mask, and BLEU 0.149, ROUGE 0.383, BERTScore 0.715 under bounding box. For diversity, CapDiff attains the lowest Self-BLEU (0.169 tight / 0.141 bbox) and highest Div-4 (0.858 / 0.885), indicating the most varied sentence-level outputs. The only metric where CapDiff underperforms is Dist-1 (word-level diversity), scoring 0.845/0.838 versus 0.998 for BLIP-VQA and LLaVA-5-Prompt, which the authors attribute to limited training vocabulary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CapDiff decisively outperforms all three baseline families on prompt quality. Against BLIP-VQA, CapDiff improves BLEU by 35× (0.177 vs 0.005) and BERTScore by 0.195 (0.732 vs 0.537) under tight mask, reflecting BLIP-VQA's inability to generate contextually appropriate insertion prompts. Against InstructBLIP, the strongest quality baseline, CapDiff gains +0.106 BLEU, +0.119 ROUGE, and +0.028 BERTScore, while also producing substantially more diverse outputs (Self-BLEU 0.169 vs 0.466; Div-4 0.858 vs 0.570). Against LLaVA (both resample and 5-prompt variants), CapDiff leads on all quality metrics (e.g., BLEU 0.177 vs 0.031/0.023) and matches or exceeds diversity (Div-4 0.858 vs 0.715/0.785; Self-BLEU 0.169 vs 0.286/0.163). The sole exception is Dist-1 (word-level diversity), where LLaVA-5-Prompt and BLIP-VQA score near 1.0 versus CapDiff's 0.845, which the authors attribute to the limited vocabulary in their training captions rather than a fundamental architectural limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the diffusion prior paradigm from DALLE-2, which translates text embeddings into image embeddings, by training a diffusion prior that maps masked-image CLIP embeddings into CLIP-text embeddings. It departs from prior object inpainting work (Qiu et al. 2020, Shukla et al. 2023) that treated object prediction as a deterministic single-category classification problem, instead reformulating it as a stochastic generative process that samples diverse prompts. The mask shape augmentation strategy and the global-local CLIP embedding concatenation are novel training and conditioning techniques specific to this task, while the overall pipeline design draws on image-to-text diffusion methods and the CLIP shared embedding space for plug-and-play compatibility with existing inpainting models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical plug-and-play component that simplifies the user workflow for text-guided inpainting tools (Stable Diffusion, Generative Fill), and introduces the first dedicated benchmark (Brush2PromptBench) for evaluating context-aware and mask-aware prompt generation. Key limitations include restricted vocabulary diversity (attributed to training data size), single-object focus, and dependence on CLIP-compatible inpainting models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object insertion, whether more complex multi-modal architectures can close the gap with generic VLMs on broader tasks, and how the accuracy-diversity tradeoff governed by mask shape can be further exploited for interactive user control.
