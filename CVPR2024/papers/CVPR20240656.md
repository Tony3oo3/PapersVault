## Problem

Audio-visual early fusion architectures, which integrate auditory and visual cues at early network layers to capture fine-grained cross-modal interactions, have remained under-explored because their increased expressivity makes them difficult to train effectively. The dominant self-supervised paradigm (contrastive learning) is fundamentally incompatible with early fusion, and prior masked-reconstruction approaches (CAV-MAE, MAViL) only achieve late fusion, limiting their ability to model local audio-visual correspondences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240656/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3-second audio clips converted to 128×196 log-mel spectrograms (16 kHz, ~50 ms STFT windows, 15 ms hop) paired with a single randomly sampled RGB frame resized to 224×224. During pre-training, the encoder produces three sets of tokens—visual, audio, and fusion—which are fed to modality-specific decoders to reconstruct masked patches. At inference, the same representations are fine-tuned with task-specific UNet decoders to yield STFT magnitude separation masks, pixel-level segmentation maps, or class logits (averaged over visual, audio, and fusion pooled representations).

## Method

DEEPAVFUSION introduces a three-branch transformer where learnable fusion tokens are updated at every layer via cross-attention over factorized audio-visual interactions: each audio and visual token is first aggregated into a small set of 8 aggregation tokens per modality, then pairwise linear combinations (W^a X̄^a + W^v X̄^v) form the interaction latents that the fusion tokens attend to, reducing the interaction space by ~700× versus dense n_a×n_v pairs. The architecture is trained with multi-modal masked auto-encoding, simultaneously masking tokens from both audio and visual inputs and reconstructing both from the visible tokens plus fusion tokens, which forces the model to learn deeply integrated representations. Uni-modal branches are initialized from large-scale pre-trained encoders (ViT-Base/MAE for vision, Spec-MAE for audio), and the fusion tokens are observed to emergently encode high-level semantics while unimodal tokens retain low-level reconstruction details.

## Results

On sound source separation, DEEPAVFUSION achieves 7.95 SDR on MUSIC, 6.95 SDR on VGGSound-Instruments, and 5.79 SDR on VGGSound-Music, outperforming the strongest prior method (OneAVM) by 1.68, 1.06, and 2.12 SDR respectively. On audio-visual segmentation (AVSBench), it reaches 89.94 mIoU on S4 and 52.05 mIoU on AVSS, surpassing the best prior method (MMVAE) by 8.2 and 22.3 mIoU. On audio-visual classification, DEEPAVFUSION achieves 65.32% linear-probing accuracy on VGGSound-Music, exceeding CAV-MAE (59.18%) by 6.14 points and the best unimodal baseline AudioMAE (52.73%) by 12.59 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEEPAVFUSION outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated task and metric. In sound source separation, it beats the strongest prior method OneAVM [38] by 1.68 SDR on MUSIC and 2.12 SDR on VGGSound-Music, while also surpassing task-specific methods like CCoL [55] (by 2.68 SDR on MUSIC) and MP-Net [64] (by 3.97 SDR on MUSIC). For audio-visual segmentation on AVSBench, DEEPAVFUSION achieves 89.94 mIoU on S4, exceeding MMVAE [36] (81.74), LAVISH [34] (80.10), and AVS [67] (78.74) by 8.2, 9.8, and 11.2 mIoU respectively; on the harder AVSS task it reaches 52.05 mIoU versus 29.77 for AVS. In audio-visual classification, DEEPAVFUSION surpasses the late-fusion CAV-MAE [20] by 6.14 LP accuracy on VGGSound-Music (65.32 vs. 59.18) and by 3.25 LP on VGGSound-All (53.08 vs. 49.83), while outperforming unimodal MAE [22] and AudioMAE [28] by 17.07 and 12.59 LP points on VGGSound-Music. Notably, DEEPAVFUSION uses fully unsupervised pre-training, whereas several baselines (OneAVM, CCoL, SoP) rely on supervised ImageNet pre-training for their visual encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-modal masked auto-encoder line of work (CAV-MAE, MAViL) by replacing their late-fusion or separate-encoder architectures with a single early-fusion transformer that processes audio and visual tokens jointly from the first layer. The key architectural departure is the introduction of learnable fusion tokens that attend to dense local audio-visual interactions (via linear aggregation of all pairwise token combinations), rather than the token-level cross-attention used in MBT or the simple concatenation in AV-MAE. To make dense interactions tractable, the paper factorizes them through modality-specific aggregation tokens, reducing the interaction space by ~700× while preserving performance. The framework is bootstrapped from unimodal MAE pre-training (ViT-Base on ImageNet, Spec-MAE on AudioSet), which ablations show is critical for the early-fusion encoder to converge effectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that early fusion—long considered difficult to train due to its high expressivity—can be effectively learned through masked reconstruction, opening a path to deeply integrated audio-visual models that outperform late-fusion baselines across separation, segmentation, and classification. The emergent finding that fusion tokens encode high-level semantics while unimodal tokens retain low-level reconstruction detail is a useful inductive bias for downstream task design. Limitations include the use of single image frames rather than video (acknowledged by the authors), and the factorization, while efficient, still approximates the full dense interaction space. Open questions include whether the emergent semantics property generalizes to other modality pairs or to video-level modeling, and whether the framework scales to longer temporal contexts.
