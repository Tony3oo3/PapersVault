## Problem

Content-aware layout generation suffers from data scarcity because high-quality layered graphic designs are created in proprietary tools and are not publicly available at scale. Existing generative models (GANs, diffusion, autoregressive) struggle to capture the sparse, highly structured layout distribution with limited training data, producing artifacts such as misaligned underlays and overlapping text. The paper hypothesizes that retrieval augmentation—referencing real layout examples—can effectively mitigate this data scarcity without requiring larger datasets or more parameters.

## Input / Output

The model takes a canvas image (350×240 RGB) and a saliency map derived from it via an off-the-shelf detector, plus optional constraint tokens specifying element categories, coordinates, or spatial relationships. Output is a flattened autoregressive token sequence Z = (bos, c₁, x₁, y₁, …, w_T, h_T, eos) where each bounding box coordinate is quantized into B discrete bins, yielding 5T+2 tokens total. During inference, K=16 nearest-neighbor layouts are retrieved from the training set using DreamSim image similarity and fed as additional context to the generator.

## Method

RALF is an autoregressive Transformer generator with a retrieval augmentation module. The image encoder (ResNet-50 FPN backbone + Transformer encoder, following CGL-GAN) produces a spatial feature f_I. The retrieval module retrieves K=16 image-layout pairs by DreamSim similarity, encodes each retrieved layout with a frozen self-supervised layout encoder into a d-dimensional vector, and computes a cross-attended feature f_C where the image feature acts as query and retrieved layout features serve as key and value. The final augmented feature f_R = Concat(f_I, f̃_L, f_C) is fed to a Transformer decoder that generates layout tokens one-by-one with full attention over the entire flattened sequence (unlike element-wise attention in ICVT or single-step decoding in GANs). An optional constraint encoder serializes user-specified requirements into a fixed-length sequence that is concatenated with f_R, enabling controllable generation within the same architecture. The layout encoder F is pre-trained in a self-supervised manner and frozen, while the rest of the model is trained with standard next-token log-likelihood.

## Results

On the PKU annotated test split, RALF achieves a FID of 3.45 (vs. 27.09 for LayoutDM, 11.80 for DS-GAN, and 34.51 for CGL-GAN) and an underlay effectiveness of 0.92 (vs. 0.63 for DS-GAN, the next best). On the CGL annotated test split, RALF reaches a FID of 1.32 and underlay effectiveness of 0.98, approaching the real-data upper bound (FID 0.79, Und 0.98). RALF also requires less than half the training data (3,000 samples) to outperform the Autoreg Baseline trained on the full 7,734-sample PKU set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RALF dominates all four external baselines on both datasets across nearly every metric. On PKU, RALF's FID of 3.45 is roughly 8× better than LayoutDM (27.09) and 10× better than CGL-GAN (34.51), while its underlay effectiveness (0.92) nearly doubles DS-GAN's (0.63) and ICVT's (0.49). On CGL, RALF achieves FID 1.32 versus LayoutDM's 2.36 and CGL-GAN's 66.75, and matches the real-data underlay score (0.98). The only metric where RALF does not lead is CGL Occlusion, where ICVT edges it out by 0.001 (0.124 vs. 0.125). Against the Autoreg Baseline (RALF without retrieval), the gains are substantial: FID drops from 13.59 to 3.45 on PKU and from 2.89 to 1.32 on CGL, confirming that retrieval augmentation is the primary source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

RALF builds on the autoregressive layout generation paradigm established by LayoutTransformer and LayoutFormer++ (flattened 1D token sequences, constraint serialization) and adopts the CGL-GAN image encoder (ResNet-50 + FPN + Transformer encoder) for content-aware conditioning. Its key departure from prior content-aware layout generators (ContentGAN, CGL-GAN, DS-GAN, ICVT, RADM) is the introduction of retrieval augmentation—k-NN layout retrieval via DreamSim image similarity—borrowed from NLP (REALM) and image synthesis (RDM, KNN-Diffusion) but applied to the layout domain for the first time. A cross-attention mechanism bridges the modality gap between image features and retrieved layout features, a design choice specific to this task since no joint image-layout embedding space exists analogous to CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that retrieval augmentation is a broadly applicable, architecture-agnostic remedy for data scarcity in layout generation, improving autoregressive, GAN, and diffusion baselines alike, and that RALF trained on only 3,000 samples outperforms a baseline trained on 7,734. This is practically significant because large-scale layered design data is inherently scarce due to proprietary authoring tools. Open questions include extending to open-vocabulary element categories, ensemble retrieval, and diversifying retrieval modalities (e.g., language), as well as addressing the acknowledged weakness that content metrics can be gamed by near-duplicate real examples.
