## Problem

Existing RL-based structural pruning methods (e.g., AMC, LFP, N2N) require a pretrained CNN before pruning and rely on standard RL algorithms that assume a stationary environment. When weights are jointly trained with the pruning policy, the reward function changes every epoch, violating this stationarity assumption and making off-the-shelf episodic RL methods inapplicable. This paper addresses how to jointly learn weights and prune architecture from scratch while handling the resulting non-stationary reward dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241822/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrained CNN (ResNet-18/34/56 or MobileNet-V2), a training dataset (CIFAR-10 or ImageNet), and a target FLOPs budget as input. The RL agent outputs a continuous pruning ratio in [0,1) for each layer, which is bounded to feasible ranges computed from the remaining FLOPs budget; the final output is a structurally pruned CNN whose channels are removed by norm ranking. The agent's reward is the pruned model's accuracy on a small held-out subset of the training set, evaluated once per full episodic trajectory rather than per action.

## Method

The method iteratively alternates between training the CNN's weights for one epoch and running P episodic pruning trajectories for a Soft Actor-Critic agent whose actions set per-layer pruning ratios. To handle the non-stationary reward caused by evolving weights, a GRU recurrent model consumes a sequence of learnable epoch embeddings and outputs a representation z of the current environment state; this representation is trained via a decoder that reconstructs observed rewards from (state, action, z) tuples. The agent's policy and critic networks are conditioned on z, allowing SAC to learn a policy that accounts for how the environment has changed. During weight training, a Group Lasso soft-regularization term (L_align) penalizes the norms of channels the agent has pruned, aligning the full model's weights with the selected sub-network so that the pruned model recovers performance quickly in fine-tuning.

## Results

On ImageNet, RLAL prunes ResNet-18 to 50% FLOPs with only -0.80% Δ-Acc Top-1 (vs. -1.10% for GNNRL at 51% FLOPs) and ResNet-34 to 50% FLOPs with -0.14% Δ-Acc Top-1 (vs. -1.14% for GP at 51.1% FLOPs). On CIFAR-10, RLAL prunes ResNet-56 to 50% FLOPs while *improving* accuracy by +0.45% over its baseline, and prunes MobileNet-V2 to 49.4% FLOPs with +0.37% Δ-Acc. The method uniquely does not require a pretrained model before pruning, unlike RL-based baselines such as AMC and GNNRL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ResNet-18 (ImageNet), RLAL achieves the best Δ-Acc Top-1 (-0.80%) at 50% FLOPs, outperforming GNNRL (-1.10% at 51% FLOPs) and GP (-1.40% at 43.9% FLOPs) while pruning more FLOPs than most competitors. On ResNet-34 (ImageNet), RLAL's -0.14% Δ-Acc Top-1 at 50% FLOPs substantially beats GP (-1.14% at 51.1%) and ISP (-0.45% at 44.0%), and it prunes 6% more FLOPs than ISP while maintaining higher accuracy. On MobileNet-V2 (ImageNet), RLAL achieves -0.50% Δ-Acc Top-1 at 29.4% FLOPs, beating AMC (-1.00% at 30%) and MetaPruning (-0.80% at 30.7%) with a comparable pruning rate. On CIFAR-10, RLAL prunes ResNet-56 to 50% FLOPs with +0.45% Δ-Acc (an improvement over baseline), outperforming DCP-Adapt (+0.01%), DTP (+0.10%), and AMC (-0.90%) at the same FLOPs budget. For MobileNet-V2 on CIFAR-10, RLAL achieves +0.37% Δ-Acc at 49.4% FLOPs, beating DDNP (+0.23% at 43%) and DCP (+0.22% at 40.3%) while pruning significantly more FLOPs. A key practical advantage over RL-based baselines (AMC, GNNRL, N2N, LFP) is that RLAL does not require a pretrained model, since it jointly trains weights and prunes architecture from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-based structural pruning (AMC, LFP, N2N, GNNRL) by removing their shared dependency on a pretrained model, instead jointly training weights and pruning architecture in an iterative loop. The key technical departure is addressing the non-stationary reward function that arises when weights evolve between pruning episodes: prior RL pruning methods (using DDPG or SAC) assume a stationary environment and fail under joint training. The authors introduce a GRU-based environment model trained via a reward-reconstruction decoder to provide the agent with a representation of the evolving dynamics, and a Group Lasso soft-regularization term to align weights with the agent-selected sub-network—two components absent from prior pruning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the pretrained-model requirement reduces the total compute cost of the pruning pipeline, which is practically significant for resource-constrained deployment workflows. However, the accuracy gains over strong baselines are modest (≤0.45% on CIFAR-10, ≤0.80% Top-1 on ImageNet), and the method is restricted to channel-level pruning of CNNs, leaving open whether the non-stationary RL formulation generalizes to transformers, weight-level sparsity, or more aggressive compression budgets. The GRU environment model is a reasonable but relatively simple mechanism; whether it scales to longer training horizons or more complex non-stationarities (e.g., learning-rate schedules, data augmentation changes) remains unexplored.
