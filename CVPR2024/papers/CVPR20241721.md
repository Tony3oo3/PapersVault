## Problem

Compositional Zero-Shot Learning (CZSL) generalizes only to unseen compositions of seen attributes and objects, leaving the broader challenge of recognizing unseen attributes, unseen objects, and their compositions entirely unaddressed. No prior work simultaneously handles all three types of unseen concepts (unseen attributes, unseen objects, and unseen compositions) in a unified evaluation framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241721/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces three classification outputs: an attribute label, an object label, and a joint attribute-object composition label. Evaluation is performed across five distinct test splits—seen pairs (AO), unseen compositions of seen concepts (AO)*, seen-attribute/unseen-object (AO*), unseen-attribute/seen-object (A*O), and unseen-attribute/unseen-object (A*O*)—using AUC with bias calibration and harmonic mean to balance seen and unseen accuracy. Neighborhood search for the expansion loss draws on an external vocabulary of ~118,650 attribute-object compositions aggregated from Visual Genome, Flick30k, COCO-captions, and LocalizedNarratives.

## Method

The architecture combines a frozen ResNet18 visual backbone with BERT text embeddings, using the OADis disentanglement module to separate attribute and object visual features and a LabelEmbedder MLP for pair embeddings. The key contribution is the Neighborhood Expansion Loss (NEL), which augments standard cross-entropy by combining label smoothing (reducing overconfidence on seen classes) with label propagation (pulling the visual embedding toward the text embeddings of k semantically nearest neighbors from an external open vocabulary). For each seen pair, k=5 neighbors are identified via GloVe cosine similarity over individual attribute and object embeddings, then validated against the external composition list; the correct label retains the highest weight while neighbors receive intermediate weights governed by a propagation term T. NEL is applied simultaneously to the attribute, object, and pair embedding spaces, making it a plug-and-play regularizer that can be attached to any existing CZSL baseline (demonstrated with LE, CompCos, OADis, CANet, and even CLIP) to improve generalization on unseen compositions without sacrificing seen-pair accuracy.

## Results

On MIT-States (Top-1 AUC), the proposed OADis+NEL method achieves HM=10.94, outperforming all baselines (CANet: 10.52, CompCos: 10.22, OADis: 9.55). The most significant gains appear on the unseen attribute-unseen object split (A*O*), where the method reaches 3.54% vs. 0.58% (CANet) and 0.63% (CompCos). On C-GQA, the method achieves HM=12.11 with a particularly strong A*O score of 12.86% (vs. 4.95% for CANet). On VAW-CZSL (Top-3 AUC), the method attains HM=11.35, leading all baselines on A*O (7.86%), AO* (3.37%), and A*O* (1.36%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed OADis+NEL method achieves the best HM on all three datasets (MIT-States: 10.94, C-GQA: 12.11, VAW-CZSL: 11.35), with the largest margins on the A*O* split (unseen attribute + unseen object), reaching 3.54% on MIT-States vs. 0.58% (CANet) and 0.63% (CompCos). CompCos [28] remains the strongest on the pure CZSL split (AO)* (21.09% on MIT-States) but collapses on unseen-primitive splits (0.63% A*O*). CANet [49] is the closest competitor on seen compositions (AO: 16.56% vs. 14.11% on MIT-States) but trails by 6× on A*O*. OADis [43], the base architecture, is improved by NEL across all splits (+2.9 AO, +2.8 (AO)*, +1.8 A*O, +2.2 A*O*). SCEN [45] excels on (AO)* (30.02% on MIT-States) but is nearly zero on A*O* (0.08%). For ZSL baselines, SEKG [50] and TF-VAEGAN [34] score below 6% on unseen attributes/objects, while the proposed method reaches 13.67% (A*) and 10.07% (O*) with BERT embeddings. CLIP [40] substantially outperforms all ZSL methods (34.93% A*O*) but is not a valid ZSL comparison since it has already seen most attributes and objects during pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Compositional Zero-Shot Learning (CZSL), which evaluates only unseen compositions of seen attribute-object primitives, by introducing Open Vocabulary-CZSL (OV-CZSL) that additionally tests on unseen attributes, unseen objects, and their compositions. Architecturally, it builds directly on OADis for the disentanglement module and on Nagarajan & Grauman's Label Embedder for the joint embedding space, adding a novel Neighborhood Expansion Loss that combines label smoothing with label propagation over external vocabulary neighbors. The approach draws conceptual inspiration from query expansion in image retrieval and from neuroscience-inspired concept learning, where abstract categories (e.g., 'fruit') are formed by linking concrete instances via language.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OV-CZSL task formulation is a natural and more realistic generalization of CZSL, and the three new benchmark splits provide a useful evaluation protocol for the community. However, the method's practical impact is limited: it relies on a frozen ResNet18 backbone, the datasets are small (53k–92k images), and the paper itself acknowledges that CLIP-based models already achieve far higher accuracy on unseen splits, making the strict ZSL setup somewhat moot. Open questions include whether NEL transfers effectively to larger vision-language models, whether the neighborhood expansion strategy scales to truly open-ended vocabularies, and how to improve discrimination between semantically close but visually distinct concepts (e.g., 'peel' vs. 'slice').
