## Problem

Existing co-speech gesture generation methods are conditioned on a single emotion label and cannot model the more realistic scenario where a speaker's emotion shifts mid-utterance. Moreover, no large-scale dataset pairs emotion-transition speech with 3D gesture annotations, and constructing such annotations for dynamically synthesized transition audio is infeasible, leaving the transition segment without direct supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242557/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 10-second emotion-transition speech audio (a 4-second head clip, a 2-second dynamically inpainted transition, and a 4-second tail clip) converted to mel-spectrograms (FFT 1024, hop 512), along with M=4 initial pose frames as a seed. It outputs a 150-frame 3D upper-body gesture sequence (71 joints on BEAT-ETrans, 43 joints on TED-ETrans). Only the head and tail segments have ground-truth pose supervision; the 30-frame transition segment is weakly supervised via a pre-trained emotion classifier.

## Method

The core contribution is a weakly-supervised pipeline built on a transformer encoder-decoder with a ResNetSE34 audio encoder. A Motion Transition Infusion Mechanism computes temporal correlation matrices between head-chunk/transition and tail-chunk/transition embeddings, multiplies them into a global dependency, encodes it into a style-guidance vector, and injects it into transition features via AdaIN to enforce temporal coherence. An Emotion Mixture Strategy derives a motion deformation matrix S from head-tail cross-correlation, uses it to produce learnable emotion embeddings, applies softmax to obtain soft emotion weights (σ, 1−σ), and feeds these as pseudo-labels to a pre-trained pose-based emotion classifier for weak supervision of the transition. A VAE-based keyframe sampler generates diverse initial postures by reconstructing chunks from randomly sampled keyframes, enabling non-deterministic output. Training combines L1 reconstruction on head/tail, an adversarial motion discriminator loss, and the weak emotion classification loss on the transition.

## Results

On BEAT-ETrans, the proposed method achieves FGD_h+t of 4.42, FGD_trans of 18.84, BC of 0.881, and Diversity of 124.93, outperforming all six baselines on every metric. On TED-ETrans, it reaches FGD_h+t of 12.19 (a 34.8% improvement over the next-best DiffGesture at 18.69), FGD_trans of 23.54, BC of 0.906, and Diversity of 93.79. The largest relative gains appear in FGD_trans (e.g., 25.03→18.84 on BEAT-ETrans) and BC (e.g., 0.788→0.881), reflecting the effectiveness of the motion transition infusion mechanism and emotion mixture strategy for the unsupervised transition segment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both datasets. The closest competitor is DiffGesture (CVPR'23), which also achieves low FGD_h+t (6.68 on BEAT-ETrans, 18.69 on TED-ETrans) but suffers notably higher FGD_trans (25.03 vs. 18.84 on BEAT-ETrans; 25.13 vs. 23.54 on TED-ETrans) due to the absence of any transition-specific supervision. On TED-ETrans, the gap widens substantially: the method achieves a 34.8% reduction in FGD_h+t over DiffGesture (12.19 vs. 18.69) and a 10.4% improvement in BC (0.906 vs. 0.818). Earlier baselines (Seq2Seq, S2G, Trimodal) are far behind, with Seq2Seq showing particularly poor BC (0.141 on BEAT-ETrans) and high FGD values, indicating poor speech-gesture alignment. CAMN and HA2G produce reasonable head/tail gestures but lack mechanisms for coherent transition modeling, resulting in FGD_trans values 20–40% higher than the proposed method. The keyframe sampler contributes a modest but consistent diversity boost (e.g., 122.29→124.93 on BEAT-ETrans) over DiffGesture, confirming the benefit of diverse initial posture conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of single-emotion or emotion-agnostic co-speech gesture generation (Seq2Seq, S2G, Trimodal, CAMN, HA2G, DiffGesture) by introducing the problem of modeling gestures across an emotion transition within a long sequence. Conceptually, it repurposes the weakly-supervised action transition learning paradigm from [33] (temporal correlation matrices between adjacent action segments) and adapts it to the speech-driven gesture domain, where no ground-truth transition poses exist. The dataset construction pipeline is novel in combining ChatGPT-4 for semantic inpainting with audioLDM2 for timbre-consistent audio synthesis, a combination not previously applied to gesture datasets. The overall architecture is a standard transformer-GAN-VAE stack, with AdaIN [18] borrowed from style transfer to inject motion transition cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two new datasets (BEAT-ETrans: 161.3 h, TED-ETrans: 59.8 h) fill a clear gap in the co-speech gesture literature, where no prior resource contained emotion-transition speech paired with 3D poses. The weakly supervised formulation is practical and likely to be adopted by follow-up work that also lacks transition annotations. However, the approach is limited to short (2-second) transitions between a neutral head and a single-emotion tail, and the transition audio is entirely synthesized rather than captured from real human speech, raising questions about ecological validity. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to multi-step emotion sequences, longer transition durations, and whether the pre-trained emotion classifier's weak supervision remains reliable as transition complexity grows.
