## Problem

Existing INR-based arbitrary-scale super-resolution methods (LIIF, LTE, CiaoSR) decode each HR pixel independently in high-dimensional space, causing computational cost to grow quadratically with the scale factor. This quadratic scaling contradicts the practical need for real-time, arbitrary-resolution image resizing and makes high-scale SR (e.g., ×12, ×18) prohibitively expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241780/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image and a continuous target scale factor (any real value, not limited to integer multiples) and produces a high-resolution image at that arbitrary scale. The encoder is interchangeable (EDSR-b, RDN, or SwinIR without their upsampling heads), and the decoder operates on 48×48 LR patches during training. During inference, the scale factor can be any value up to ×30, and the CMSR algorithm allows the user to trade off rendering precision for speed at test time without retraining.

## Method

LMF decouples the HR-HD decoding into two stages: (1) a high-dimensional latent MLP in LR space that processes each LR feature vector once to produce a compressed latent code and a set of FiLM modulations (per-layer scale and shift parameters), and (2) a low-dimensional (16-dim) render MLP in HR space whose hidden layers are modulated by the FiLM parameters to adapt to each local region. This means the expensive HD computation happens once per LR pixel rather than per HR pixel, while the tiny render MLP handles the per-coordinate prediction with minimal cost. The latent modulation effectively lets a 7-layer, 16-dim render MLP match or exceed the quality of a 5-layer, 256-dim decoder. Additionally, the CMSR algorithm exploits the observed positive correlation between modulation intensity and local signal complexity to skip rendering of simple regions at high scales, making computational cost proportional to image content rather than output resolution.

## Results

On the DIV2K validation set (EDSR-b encoder), LM-LIIF achieves 34.65 dB PSNR at ×2 with only 125.32G MACs, compared to LIIF's 34.67 dB at 3.567T MACs — a 96.5% reduction in computational cost with negligible quality loss. At ×18, LM-LIIF reaches 22.19 dB with 2.95G MACs versus LIIF's 22.17 dB at 3.567T MACs, a 99.98% MAC reduction while actually improving PSNR by 0.02 dB. Across all three converted methods (LM-LIIF, LM-LTE, LM-CiaoSR), computational cost is reduced by 90.4%–99.9%, inference is accelerated by 2.4×–56.9×, and parameters are saved by 45.1%–76.0%, while maintaining competitive PSNR. With the SwinIR encoder, LM-LIIF and LM-LTE even slightly outperform their original counterparts on in-scale benchmarks (e.g., LM-LIIF: 38.31 vs 38.28 dB on Set5 ×2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LIIF, LM-LIIF matches or slightly exceeds PSNR at all scales (e.g., 22.19 vs 22.17 dB at ×18) while reducing MACs by 96.5%–99.98% and parameters by 52.1%. Against LTE, LM-LTE achieves near-identical PSNR (within 0.01–0.04 dB) with 91.1%–99.8% MAC reduction and 45.1% fewer parameters. Against CiaoSR-1.4M, LM-CiaoSR shows a small PSNR drop of 0.05–0.10 dB (e.g., 34.81 vs 34.91 at ×2) due to relocating attention-in-attention modules to latent space, but still achieves 90.7%–99.8% MAC reduction and 47.3% parameter savings. With the SwinIR encoder, LM-LIIF and LM-LTE actually surpass their originals on in-scale benchmarks (e.g., LM-LIIF: 38.31 vs 38.28 dB on Set5 ×2; LM-LTE: 38.32 vs 38.33 dB), while LM-CiaoSR matches or slightly exceeds CiaoSR-3.1M (38.37 vs 38.38 dB on Set5 ×2). On out-of-scale benchmarks with SwinIR, all LMF variants remain within 0.01–0.10 dB of their originals, confirming that the efficiency gains do not come at a meaningful quality cost. Fixed-scale baselines (EDSR-b, RDN, SwinIR) are outperformed by all ASSR methods at their respective in-scale factors, confirming the advantage of continuous representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the INR-based arbitrary-scale super-resolution line initiated by LIIF, LTE, and CiaoSR, which all decode pixel values by running a high-dimensional MLP independently at every HR coordinate. The key departure is the introduction of FiLM-style latent modulations (borrowed from hyper-base network designs in INR literature such as COIN++ and modulated periodic activations) to decouple the decoding into a shared latent-space stage and a lightweight render-space stage, rather than applying modulations at the image level as prior hyper-network approaches do. LMF is explicitly framed as a universal conversion paradigm: any existing INR-based ASSR decoder can be restructured into the latent+render form without architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The up-to-99.9% MACs reduction and 57× speedup while preserving PSNR make ASSR practically deployable in real-time image-resizing applications, a gap that has kept SISR methods from replacing simple interpolation in practice. The CMSR algorithm adds a useful test-time knob for trading precision against speed without retraining. Open questions include whether the latent-modulation paradigm extends beyond ASSR to other INR tasks (e.g., NeRF, video), whether the slight PSNR degradation in LM-CiaoSR (from relocating attention modules) can be closed, and whether the Scale2Mods table generalizes across domains or requires per-dataset calibration.
