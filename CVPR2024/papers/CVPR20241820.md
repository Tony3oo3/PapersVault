## Problem

Existing injective 3D deformation methods face a fundamental trade-off: mesh-based approaches from geometry processing are geometrically stable but cannot be learned or optimized end-to-end, while neural-network-based methods (normalizing flows, NeuralODE, i-ResNet) are learnable but suffer from slow Jacobian computation, numerical instability, and reduced expressivity in 3D. No prior method simultaneously guarantees injectivity, supports efficient gradient-based learning/optimization, provides fast access to the full Jacobian and its inverse, and remains numerically robust.

## Input / Output

In the optimization setting (NeRF deformation), the method takes a NeRF density field and user-specified handle constraints (a selected region shifted by a rigid motion) and produces an injective piecewise-linear 3D deformation applied during ray-traced rendering, requiring both the inverse map and its Jacobian at each sample point. In the learning setting, a neural network receives multi-view renders of a source SMPL mesh concatenated with target pose parameters and predicts the per-layer Tutte parameters (Laplacian weights and boundary conditions) that define the deformation. The final output is always a piecewise-linear map over the unit cube whose Jacobian is constant per triangle, enabling exact (non-sampled) integration of distortion energies.

## Method

TutteNet composes k 'prismatic layers,' each of which deforms a 2D triangulated unit square via Tutte's embedding (guaranteed injective in 2D) and lifts the result to 3D by acting on two coordinates while preserving the third, rotated by a plane orientation R^i. Each layer is parameterized by unconstrained Laplacian weights L_ij (sigmoid-bounded to stay positive) and boundary conditions b_i (parameterized via positive angle increments ensuring a convex polygon), making the entire pipeline differentiable and amenable to gradient-based optimization or neural-network prediction. The key insight is that while 3D Tutte embeddings are not injective, composing multiple 2D injective maps over different planes yields an expressive family of injective 3D piecewise-linear deformations. This design provides an explicit inverse (by swapping source/target vertices per layer), a fast full Jacobian (2n small 3×3 matrix multiplications), and exact layer-wise distortion regularization via enumerable per-triangle Jacobians—properties that neural-flow baselines lack.

## Results

On the SMPL human deformation learning task, TutteNet achieves a vertex error of 0.16×10⁻³ and gradient error of 7.7×10⁻³, outperforming NeuralODE (1.24 and 25.4), RealNVP (4.21 and 35.2), and i-ResNet (13.4 and 92.3) by large margins. In the single-deformation fitting experiment, TutteNet attains the lowest vertex error (0.15×10⁻³) while NeuralODE achieves a slightly lower gradient error (2.6 vs. 4.4×10⁻³). TutteNet also computes the full Jacobian in 0.02 s per point, versus 0.19 s for NeuralODE, 39 s for i-ResNet, and 136 s for RealNVP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TutteNet outperforms all three injective baselines on the learning experiment: it reduces vertex error by ~7.7× over NeuralODE (0.16 vs 1.24), ~26× over RealNVP (0.16 vs 4.21), and ~84× over i-ResNet (0.16 vs 13.4). In the fitting experiment, TutteNet achieves the lowest vertex error (0.15) but NeuralODE attains a slightly lower gradient error (2.6 vs 4.4), making it the only baseline that is competitive on a single metric. For NeRF deformation (Figure 3), TutteNet produces artifact-free results, while non-injective methods (NeRF-Editing, Deforming-NeRF, SPIDR) exhibit visible rendering artifacts from self-intersections, and NeuralODE—though also injective—causes geometric squashing of the T-Rex's tail and the robot's eye. TutteNet's Jacobian computation is ~10× faster than NeuralODE (0.02 s vs 0.19 s) and ~2000× faster than RealNVP (0.02 s vs 136 s), which is critical for the elastic-energy optimization loop used in NeRF deformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

TutteNet bridges two previously separate lines of work on 3D deformations: geometry-processing mesh deformations (which offer geometric interpretability and stability but lack learnability and injectivity guarantees in 3D) and neural invertible representations like normalizing flows (RealNVP), NeuralODE, and i-ResNet (which are learnable and injective but abstract, slow, and less accurate for 3D geometric tasks). The core conceptual move is to exploit the fact that Tutte's embedding guarantees injectivity in 2D (a well-known result from Floater 2003 and Tutte 1963), then compose multiple such 2D layers over different projection planes to achieve 3D injectivity — a 'deep' composition strategy borrowed from normalizing flows but instantiated with geometrically meaningful mesh-deformation layers. The differentiable parameterization of each Tutte layer (via mesh Laplacian and boundary conditions) follows directly from Aigerman & Groueix 2023 (Generative Escher Meshes), which was originally designed for 2D generative tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TutteNet provides the first injective 3D deformation representation that simultaneously supports efficient Jacobian computation, exact inverse evaluation, differentiable optimization, and robustness — properties that no single prior method (neural or mesh-based) offered together. This enables artifact-free NeRF and SDF deformation without proxy geometry, a practically important capability for interactive 3D content creation. Key limitations remain: the method is not real-time (preventing interactive rendering), and deformations are not fully localizable (deforming one region can affect others). Open directions include real-time evaluation, applications to long-range optical flow and non-rigid registration, and extending the framework to surface-to-surface mappings.
