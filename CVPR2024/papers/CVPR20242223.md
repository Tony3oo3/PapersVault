## Problem

Existing CAD-based novel object coarse pose estimation methods, particularly MegaPose, suffer from two critical limitations: (1) linear complexity in the number of templates, requiring a forward pass per image-template pair and yielding >1.6 s per detection, and (2) high sensitivity to segmentation errors caused by occlusions, which introduce scale/translation mismatches and outlier matches. These issues make real-time, robust pose estimation of novel objects impractical for industrial deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242223/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB crop of a segmented object (produced by CNOS) and a CAD model (or a 3D model predicted from a single image via Wonder3D) as input. During a one-time 11.5 s onboarding stage, 162 rendered templates are generated and their dense features are precomputed. At inference (48 ms per detection), the method outputs a full 6D pose (3D rotation + 3D translation) by first recovering out-of-plane rotation from template matching, then recovering in-plane rotation, 2D scale, and 2D translation from a single 2D-2D patch correspondence, with RANSAC for robustness.

## Method

GigaPose decouples the 6 DoFs into out-of-plane rotation (azimuth, elevation) estimated via a compact set of 162 templates on a regular icosphere, and the remaining 4 DoFs estimated from patch correspondences. A DINOv2-based ViT feature extractor F_ae is trained with local contrastive learning (InfoNCE loss) to produce features invariant to scale and in-plane rotation but sensitive to out-of-plane rotation, enabling fast sublinear nearest-neighbor template search instead of per-pair network scoring. For the remaining DoFs, a second feature extractor F_ist (modified ResNet18) computes patch-level features at matched locations, and two lightweight MLPs directly regress in-plane rotation (as cos/sin) and 2D scale from a single 2D-2D correspondence; 2D translation is read from patch positions. RANSAC over all correspondences selects the optimal affine transformation M_{t→q}, from which the full 6D pose is recovered analytically. This hybrid template-plus-correspondence design yields a 35× speedup over MegaPose while improving accuracy and robustness to segmentation noise.

## Results

On the seven core BOP datasets, GigaPose achieves a mean AR score of 26.8 for coarse pose estimation (no refinement), outperforming MegaPose's 20.8 by 6.0 points, while running 35× faster (48 ms vs 1.68 s per detection). With MegaPose's refinement (1 hypothesis), GigaPose reaches 54.7 mean AR vs. 50.9 for MegaPose; with GenFlow + 5 hypotheses, it reaches 60.5 vs. 57.0. The largest per-dataset gains over MegaPose appear on T-LESS (+8.7 coarse, +6.4 refined) and IC-BIN (+7.1 coarse, +8.3 refined).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MegaPose (the primary baseline), GigaPose wins on every configuration: +6.0 mean AR for coarse estimation (26.8 vs 20.8), +3.8 with MegaPose refinement (54.7 vs 50.9), and +3.5 with GenFlow+5-hypothesis refinement (60.5 vs 57.0), while being 35× faster on the coarse stage (48 ms vs 1.68 s). Against ZS6D, GigaPose is nearly tied on LM-O (29.6 vs 29.8) and clearly ahead on T-LESS (26.4 vs 21.0), but trails on YCB-V (27.8 vs 32.4); ZS6D does not report results on the remaining four datasets. Against OSOP, GigaPose leads on LM-O (29.6 vs 27.4) but falls behind on T-LESS (26.4 vs 40.3) and YCB-V (27.8 vs 29.6), though OSOP does not report run-time or results on the other four datasets. Overall, GigaPose is the only method evaluated on all seven datasets with a refinement-compatible pipeline, and it achieves the best mean AR in every tested setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

GigaPose restructures the template-based coarse pose estimation paradigm established by MegaPose, which scores every query-template pair with a feed-forward network over 576 templates covering both in-plane and out-of-plane rotations. The key departure is a decoupling: only 162 out-of-plane templates are used, matched via sublinear nearest-neighbor search in a contrastively-trained DINOv2 feature space (invariant to scale and in-plane rotation by design), while the remaining 4 DoFs are recovered from a single 2D-2D patch correspondence using a lightweight ResNet18-based regressor and RANSAC. This hybrid template-plus-correspondence approach sits between pure template matching (MegaPose, ZS6D) and pure feature-matching/PnP methods, and the paper explicitly positions itself against both families on the BOP novel-object benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 35× speedup (48 ms vs. 1.68 s per detection) makes real-time CAD-based novel object pose estimation practical for industrial deployment, and the demonstrated robustness to segmentation errors (stable AR across IoU thresholds where MegaPose degrades sharply) addresses a critical failure mode in cluttered, occluded scenes. The ability to accept 3D models predicted from a single image by Wonder3D relaxes the CAD-model requirement, broadening applicability to objects without digital twins. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to very small or highly textured objects (the LM-O 'Cat' failure case), whether the contrastive feature training transfers to domains beyond the BOP training distribution, and whether the coarse-estimator error distribution (which differs from MegaPose's) affects downstream refinement performance as observed on TUD-L.
