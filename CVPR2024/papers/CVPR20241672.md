## Problem

3D synthetic-to-real UDA segmentation suffers from sensor sampling pattern mismatches (different beam counts and points-per-beam) that create distinct point densities between domains. Existing methods either attempt to simultaneously generate target-like data and align features within unstable adversarial training, or rely on poorly initialized source-only models for self-training, producing unreliable pseudo-labels that limit performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241672/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw 3D LiDAR point clouds (XYZ coordinates only) from both synthetic (SynLiDAR, 64-beam Velodyne HDL-64E) and real (SemanticKITTI 64-beam, SemanticPOSS 40-beam Pandora) domains, voxelized at 0.05 m for MinkUNet34. The DGT module pre-processes inputs by area-wise point discarding and XY-axis noise injection before any network forward pass. Output is a per-point semantic label over 19 (SemanticKITTI) or 13 (SemanticPOSS) classes, evaluated by mIoU.

## Method

DGT-ST is a two-stage pipeline built around a non-learnable Density-Guided Translator (DGT) that partitions each scan into distance-based areas, computes per-area point-count ratios between domains, and randomly discards points to equalize densities, plus XY noise for realism. Stage 1 (PCAN) performs category-level adversarial alignment using source-domain class prototypes: well-aligned target points (high-confidence predictions) are reweighted by their cosine distance to the corresponding prototype, adaptively reducing adversarial loss on already-aligned points to prevent negative transfer. Stage 2 (SAC-LM) loads the PCAN-initialized model and performs self-training with a mean-teacher framework, mixing source and target scans via LaserMix (intertwining inclination-angle sectors) to construct an intermediate domain, and enforcing a source-aware consistency loss (KL divergence) between the student's prediction on the DGT-translated target and the teacher's prediction on the raw target, thereby extracting source knowledge from unlabeled target data.

## Results

On SynLiDAR → SemanticKITTI, DGT-ST achieves 43.1% mIoU, outperforming the source-only baseline (20.4%) by 22.7% and the best prior self-training method LaserMix (36.0%) by 7.1%. On SynLiDAR → SemanticPOSS, DGT-ST reaches 50.8% mIoU, a 12.5% gain over source-only (38.3%) and a 5.3% improvement over the strongest baseline PMAN (46.5%). The intermediate PCAN stage alone achieves 37.0% and 44.4% mIoU on the two tasks respectively, already surpassing all adversarial baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGT-ST outperforms all nine baselines on both tasks. On Syn→Sk, it beats the strongest adversarial method PMAN (33.7%) by 9.4% and the best self-training method LaserMix (36.0%) by 7.1% in mIoU. On Syn→Sp, DGT-ST (50.8%) surpasses PMAN (46.5%) by 4.3% and LaserMix (45.5%) by 5.3%. Among adversarial baselines, PCAN (37.0% on Sk, 44.4% on Sp) consistently leads, confirming that category-level prototype-guided alignment is more effective than global adversarial matching (ADVENT 30.5%, CLAN 30.5% on Sk). Among self-training baselines, LaserMix benefits most from DGT-ST's two-stage pipeline, while PolarMix underperforms even the source-only model on Syn→Sp (30.4% vs 38.3%), highlighting the sensitivity of self-training to initialization quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines in 3D UDA segmentation: adversarial training (ADVENT, CLAN, PMAN) and self-training (CoSMix, PolarMix, LaserMix). It departs from adversarial methods that perform global distribution alignment by introducing prototype-guided category-level alignment (PCAN) to prevent negative transfer, and departs from self-training methods that rely on source-only pretrained models by using PCAN as an adversarial warm-up stage. The density-guided translator (DGT) is positioned as a non-learnable, statistical alternative to learnable generators (e.g., CycleGAN in ePointDA, PCT's dual generators) for bridging the input-level sampling-pattern gap, while SAC-LM extends LaserMix from its original semi-supervised setting into the UDA framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DGT (validated by boosting CoSMix by +0.6% without architectural changes) makes it a practical tool for any 3D UDA pipeline facing sensor mismatch, and the two-stage PCAN→self-training recipe addresses a previously overlooked initialization problem that limits pseudo-label quality. Limitations include evaluation on only two synthetic-to-real pairs with a single backbone (MinkUNet34), and the DGT's statistical partitioning assumes distance-based density variation that may not hold for all sensor geometries. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-sensor or multi-modal settings, and whether learnable input-level translators could further improve upon the non-learnable DGT.
