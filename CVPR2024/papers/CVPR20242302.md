## Problem

Existing text-to-video generation methods rely on 3D U-Net architectures or autoregressive temporal modeling, which demand large text-video paired datasets and GPU memory that scales with the number of frames. This makes high-quality video generation prohibitively expensive and data-hungry compared to text-to-image generation. The paper asks whether video generation can be reduced to image generation by eliminating the temporal dimension from the architecture entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242302/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt and produces a video of 28 frames (or more via autoregressive extension) at 254×254 resolution per frame. Internally, the video is encoded as a 512×512 grid image containing four 254×254 inside frames arranged in a 2×2 layout, so the diffusion model always processes a fixed-size image regardless of how many frames the final video contains. An SD-XL extension can raise per-frame resolution to 510×510.

## Method

The core idea is to represent a video as a 2×2 grid image, collapsing the temporal dimension into spatial structure so that a standard 2D U-Net (fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 1.5 on only 0.1M Webvid-10M samples) can generate four temporally ordered key frames in a single image. A two-stage autoregressive interpolation pipeline then fills the gaps: a 1-step model inserts two frames between adjacent key frames, and a 2-step model inserts four frames, each conditioned on the previously generated grid image by concatenating their latent embeddings (InstructPix2Pix-style) and using a 'Fill in the blanks' instruction. Because every stage processes a fixed-resolution image, GPU memory remains constant regardless of total frame count. A next-key-grid model autoregressively generates subsequent 2×2 grids to extend videos beyond 28 frames while preserving temporal continuity.

## Results

On MSR-VTT (16 frames, 254×254), the proposed grid diffusion model achieves CLIPSIM 0.3096 and FVD 375, outperforming all 11 baselines while training on only 0.1M samples (vs. 5.4M–27M for prior methods). On UCF-101, it reaches IS 62.88 and FVD 340, surpassing the next-best PYoCo (IS 47.76, FVD 355). For longer videos (64 and 128 frames on MSR-VTT), the model achieves B-FVD-16 of 370 and 364 respectively, substantially outperforming VideoFusion (1106 and 1239) and FreeNoise (517 and 726).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins across all quantitative benchmarks. On MSR-VTT, it outperforms the closest competitor Show-1 [43] by 0.0024 CLIPSIM (0.3096 vs. 0.3072) and by 163 FVD (375 vs. 538), while using 100× less training data (0.1M vs. 10M). Against the primary baseline VideoFusion [17], it gains 0.0166 CLIPSIM and 175 FVD on MSR-VTT, and 299 FVD on UCF-101 (340 vs. 639). On UCF-101, the IS score of 62.88 exceeds PYoCo [11] (47.76) by 15.12 points and Make-A-Video [29] (33.00) by nearly 30 points. For extended-length generation (64/128 frames), the model beats VideoFusion [17] by 736–875 B-FVD-16 and FreeNoise [21] by 147–362 B-FVD-16, while remaining competitive on CLIPSIM (0.3044 vs. 0.2996 at 64 frames; 0.3000 vs. 0.3034 at 128 frames). In human evaluation (Table 4), participants preferred the proposed method over both VideoFusion [17] and VideoCrafter [7] on all four criteria (text matching, video quality, temporal consistency, motion quality) across all three datasets, with win rates of 47–60% versus loss rates of 17–30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant text-to-video paradigm of 3D U-Net architectures (VideoFusion, VideoLDM, Make-A-Video) and autoregressive frame-by-frame generation (CogVideo, LatentShift) by eliminating the temporal dimension from the model architecture entirely. Instead, it repurposes a pre-trained 2D Stable Diffusion U-Net and VAE to generate a 2×2 grid image where four spatially-arranged frames encode temporal order, and borrows the InstructPix2Pix 'fill in the blanks' conditioning mechanism for autoregressive interpolation between grid frames. The key conceptual move is treating video generation as a pure image generation problem, leveraging the VAE latent's ability to capture global spatial structure to implicitly encode temporal dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fixed GPU memory cost regardless of frame count and the 100× data reduction (0.1M vs 10M+ samples) make text-to-video generation accessible on consumer hardware and with limited paired data, which are practical barriers that have slowed adoption. The grid-image representation also opens a path to directly transplanting the rich ecosystem of image editing and manipulation tools (InstructPix2Pix, ControlNet, style transfer) into the video domain without retraining. Limitations include the modest per-frame resolution (254×254), reliance on a pre-trained text-to-image model that constrains quality, and the 28-frame base length before autoregressive extension introduces drift. Open questions include whether the grid approach scales to higher resolutions via SD-XL (hinted at but not fully evaluated), longer videos, and additional modalities like audio.
