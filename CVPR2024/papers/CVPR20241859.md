## Problem

Supervised Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) requires expensive human-annotated triplets of (reference image, visual delta, target image), which are far less available than standard image-text pairs, limiting scalability. Zero-shot CIR avoids annotation costs but achieves substantially lower accuracy and cannot specialize to specific domains. No prior work bridges this gap by generating high-quality visual delta descriptions at scale from unlabeled image pairs.

## Input / Output

The CIR model takes a reference image and a free-form textual visual delta (e.g., 'change the background to a beach') and produces a composed embedding that is matched against a gallery of image embeddings to yield a ranked retrieval list. The VDG component takes a pair of RGB images (reference and target) processed through a Q-Former into 32 learnable query tokens each, and autoregressively generates a natural-language description of their visual difference using the LLaMA2 vocabulary. The method is model-agnostic: the generated pseudo triplets can augment training for any supervised CIR backbone (CLIP+Combiner or BLIP).

## Method

The Visual Delta Generator (VDG) couples InstructBLIP's Q-Former (ViT-G/14) as a vision projector with LLaMA2-13B, trained in two stages: (1) alignment via next-token prediction on 595K CC3M image-caption pairs, and (2) instruction tuning with LoRA using a fixed prompt template that asks the model to describe how to transform a reference image into a target image. Pseudo triplets are constructed by retrieving top-20 visually similar images per anchor via ResNet-152 cosine similarity from auxiliary galleries (COCO, NLVR2, DeepFashion, FashionIQ), forming subgroups of 6 with dense pairwise connections, and feeding each pair through VDG to generate the visual delta. For CIR training, the paper introduces a target-composed contrastive loss (tcc) that computes HN-NCE separately on supervised and pseudo batches before concatenating them, and a target-delta matching loss (tdm) that aligns target image patch tokens with the visual delta text via a binary classification head, ensuring BLIP's cross-attention encoder fully processes target image features.

## Results

On the CIRR test set, the semi-supervised BLIP†tdm+NLVR2'se model achieves R@1=50.96, R@5=80.15, R@10=86.86, R@50=94.46, and Rs@1=77.45, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (CASE+LasCo.Ca. at R@1=49.35 and CoVR+WebVid at R@1=49.69). In the zero-shot (unseen) setting, BLIPtdm trained solely on VDG-generated pseudo triplets from NLVR2' reaches R@1=45.74 and R@10=82.52, substantially exceeding prior zero-shot methods like SEARLE (R@1=24.22) and CoVR (R@1=38.48). On FashionIQ, the semi-supervised BLIPtdm+FashionIQ'se achieves R@10 of 47.89 (Dress), 51.36 (Shirt), and 53.29 (Toptee), outperforming all supervised and zero-shot baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed semi-supervised BLIP†tdm+NLVR2'se outperforms the strongest supervised baselines: CASE [28] (R@1=48.00) and CoVR [49] (R@1=48.84) by 2.96 and 2.12 points respectively on CIRR R@1, and also surpasses their externally-pretrained variants (CASE+LasCo.Ca. R@1=49.35, CoVR+WebVid R@1=49.69). In the zero-shot setting, the method dramatically exceeds Pic2Word [42] (R@1=23.90) by 21.84 points and SEARLE [3] (R@1=24.22) by 21.52 points, while also beating CoVR's zero-shot variant (R@1=38.48) by 7.26 points. On FashionIQ, the semi-supervised BLIPtdm+FashionIQ'se (Dress R@10=47.89, Shirt R@10=51.36, Toptee R@10=53.29) surpasses all supervised baselines including CoVR [49] (43.51/48.28/51.53), FashionVLP [14] (32.42/31.89/38.51), and DCNet [22] (28.95/23.95/30.44). The VDG-augmented supervised Combiner† (R@1=36.91) improves over the original Combiner [2] (R@1=33.59) by 3.32 points, confirming that pseudo-triplet augmentation alone yields consistent gains. Overall, the method achieves new state-of-the-art results in both the semi-supervised and zero-shot CIR settings across both benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM-based vision-language models (building directly on InstructBLIP's Q-Former and LLaVA's alignment strategy) and the established CIR literature (Combiner, BLIP, CASE, CoVR, SEARLE). It departs from supervised CIR, which requires expensive human-annotated triplets, and from zero-shot CIR, which relies on noisy image-caption pairs without explicit visual-delta supervision, by introducing a semi-supervised middle ground where an instruction-tuned LLM (LLaMA2 + Q-Former + LoRA) generates pseudo visual deltas from image pairs. The target-delta matching loss (tdm) is a new training objective specific to BLIP's image-grounded text encoder, and the supervised/pseudo separated contrastive loss (tcc) is a novel loss design that isolates the contribution of noisy pseudo triplets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VDG provides a practical, model-agnostic mechanism to scale CIR training data without proportional annotation cost, which is a significant bottleneck in the field. The finding that VDG-generated deltas match or occasionally exceed human-annotated ones (attributed to annotation consistency) validates LLMs as reliable pseudo-label generators for fine-grained visual comparison. Open questions include how well the approach transfers to domains with more complex or compositional visual modifications, whether the pseudo-triplet noise floor limits further gains, and whether the saturation observed at pseudo-triplet scale ≈ training-set size reflects a fundamental information ceiling or merely a distribution mismatch with the test set.
