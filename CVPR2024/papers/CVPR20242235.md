## Problem

Existing SLAM datasets are collected in controlled, well-lit environments (e.g., KITTI in sunny weather, EuRoC in small rooms) and fail to capture the sensor degradations, aggressive motions, and extreme weather that cause real-world SLAM failures. Additionally, standard evaluation metrics like ATE only measure trajectory accuracy and cannot detect velocity spikes that would be catastrophic for robot safety control, leaving no principled way to assess SLAM robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242235/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides time-synchronized (≤3 ms gap via PPS) multimodal streams from a sensor pack of 4 Leopard Imaging fisheye cameras, 1 Velodyne Puck LiDAR, 1 Epson M-G365 IMU, and 1 FLIR Boson thermal camera, with optional depth cameras. Ground truth is a FARO Focus 3D S120 scan (±2 mm range error, 976K pts/s) registered to robot trajectories via GICP point-to-point/plane/line correspondences, fused with VO, LO, and IMU. The proposed robustness metric outputs two scalar scores—R_p (position) and R_r (rotation)—computed as the AUC of the F-1 score over velocity-error thresholds, where B-spline smoothing is applied to trajectories before differentiating to obtain linear and angular velocities.

## Method

The paper introduces SubT-MRS, a 5-year (2019–2023) real-world dataset spanning 500+ hours and 100+ km across 30+ scenes (tunnels, caves, deserts, forests, urban, off-road) collected by heterogeneous platforms (aerial, legged, wheeled, RC) under multi-degraded conditions (smoke, dust, fog, snow, darkness, overexposure, structureless corridors). The key methodological contribution is a velocity-based robustness metric: for a threshold T, precision P(e<T) measures how closely estimated velocity tracks ground truth, recall R(e<T) measures trajectory completeness, and the F-1 score is integrated over T (scaled via exp(−10T) to [0,1]) to yield AUC-based scores R_p and R_r that jointly capture accuracy and completeness. Multi-robot extrinsic calibration is performed by having each robot run Super Odometry to build local LiDAR maps, then estimating inter-robot transforms via GICP on overlapping regions. The dataset is validated through the ICCV'23 SLAM Challenge with 29 submissions across LiDAR, visual, and sensor-fusion tracks, revealing that no current method achieves both high accuracy and real-time performance under mixed degradation.

## Results

In the ICCV'23 SLAM Challenge on SubT-MRS, the best LiDAR method (FAST-LIO2 + HBA by Liu et al.) achieves an ATE of 0.588 m but requires 51.3 s per iteration, while the best visual method (DVI-SLAM by Peng et al.) achieves 0.547 m ATE at 183 s per iteration. No submission met the success criteria in the sensor-fusion track, indicating a significant gap in robust multimodal SLAM. The proposed robustness metric (R_p, R_r) reveals different rankings than ATE alone: e.g., in geometric degradation, average R_p drops from 0.710 (Urban) to 0.073 (Sewerage simulation), and in mixed degradation the average R_p/R_r (0.290/0.473) is far below geometric-only (0.664/0.754), confirming that current SLAM systems lose robustness under combined sensor failures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAST-LIO2 (Xu et al.) dominates the LiDAR track: Liu et al.'s variant (FAST-LIO2 + HBA) achieves the best ATE (0.588 m) and best robustness (R_p=0.517, R_r=0.770), while Kim et al.'s variant (FAST-LIO2 + Point-LIO + Quatro) degrades to 3.825 m ATE in simulation-heavy sequences, showing sensitivity to aggressive motion. Faster-LIO (Bai et al.) with a pose graph achieves competitive accuracy (0.663 m) at real-time speed (0.125 s), making it the best accuracy–speed trade-off among LiDAR methods. kiss-icp/LIO-EKF (Vizzo et al.) is the fastest (0.006 s) but suffers the worst ATE (4.313 m), indicating that simple point-to-point ICP is insufficient for degraded environments. DLO (Chen et al.) with Scan-Context++ achieves 1.209 m ATE but has the lowest robustness (R_p=0.276), failing in mixed-degradation sequences. On the visual track, DVI-SLAM (Peng et al.) leads with 0.547 m ATE and the best rotation robustness (R_r=0.788), but at 183 s/iteration it is impractical for real-time use. VINS-Mono (Qin et al.) combined with LET-NET achieves 1.093 m ATE in real time but has very low robustness (R_p=0.078), collapsing in low-light and smoke scenes. ORB-SLAM3 (Campos et al.) performs worst overall (8.975 m ATE), particularly failing in overexposure (1.757 m) and outdoor night (10.766 m) sequences. The key takeaway is that no single method balances accuracy, robustness, and real-time performance across all degradation types, and the sensor-fusion track received no qualifying submissions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DARPA SubT Challenge data (2019–2021) with two additional years of collection (2022–2023) to create SubT-MRS, a 5-year, 500+ hour, 100+ km dataset. It departs from prior SLAM benchmarks (KITTI, EuRoC, TUM-VI, Hilti SLAM, TartanAir) by being the first real-world dataset to simultaneously incorporate thermal cameras, heterogeneous robot platforms (aerial, legged, wheeled), and a wide spectrum of perceptual and geometric degradations (smoke, dust, snow, structureless corridors, overexposure). Conceptually, it also introduces a velocity-based F-1/AUC robustness metric that goes beyond the standard ATE evaluation, repurposing precision-recall thinking from detection into trajectory reliability assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ICCV'23 SLAM Challenge results reveal that no submitted sensor-fusion system met the success criteria, and the best-accuracy solutions (0.588 m ATE for LiDAR, 0.547 m for visual) require 51–183 seconds per iteration, exposing a critical gap between offline accuracy and real-time deployability. The robustness metric produces rankings that diverge from ATE, suggesting that current evaluation can mask dangerous velocity spikes that would crash an aerial platform. Open questions include how to actively select informative measurements under mixed degradation, how to handle unpredictable aggressive motion patterns that break fixed-velocity assumptions, and whether the sim-to-real gap (TartanAir-derived sequences yielding 5.2 m ATE vs. 0.8 m in the real world) can be closed. As a 5-year, multi-robot, multi-sensor benchmark with centimeter-level ground truth, SubT-MRS is positioned to become a standard reference for all-weather SLAM research.
