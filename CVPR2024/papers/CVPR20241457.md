## Problem

Multimodal data in each modality's embedding space contains inherent aleatoric noise that corrupts cross-modal interactions during fusion, yet existing fusion strategies either ignore this uncertainty or use it only for alignment without exploiting it for robustness. The paper asks whether this intrinsic uncertainty can be quantified and actively leveraged to learn more stable unimodal embeddings and more robust joint representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241457/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is modality-agnostic and handles three configurations: tri-modal video+audio+text (using pre-extracted FACET, COVAREP, and BERT features) for sentiment regression, and bi-modal RGB+text or RGB+depth (using ResNet-152 and BERT features) for classification. For regression tasks it outputs a continuous sentiment strength score; for classification tasks it outputs a class label. The framework is designed to be plug-and-play across any modality combination, with the only requirement being a per-modality feature extractor.

## Method

EAU consists of two modules. SUFA represents each unimodal sample as a multivariate Gaussian (mean μ and variance σ²) via two fully connected heads, aligns cross-modal distributions with KL divergence, then uses the quantified uncertainty to generate augmented samples via the reparameterization trick and trains a self-supervised contrastive objective (InfoNCE-style) so that the learned unimodal embeddings are insensitive to the natural diversity within each modality. RMFI first fuses the stable unimodal means with attention weights inversely proportional to each modality's uncertainty (lower σ → higher weight), then applies a Variational Information Bottleneck to compress the preliminary joint representation, explicitly penalizing redundancy introduced by the cross-modal distributional alignment in SUFA. The final compressed latent is passed to a task-specific head (cross-entropy for classification, MSE for regression) with an additional KL regularizer toward a standard normal prior.

## Results

On the classification-based MSA task (MVSA-Single), EAU achieves 79.15% accuracy and 78.36% F1, outperforming the strongest baseline QMF (78.07% Acc, 77.18% F1) by over 1% absolute. On the MIC tasks, EAU reaches 93.20% Acc on Food101 and 72.05% Acc on NYU Depth v2, surpassing QMF by 0.28% and 1.96% respectively. On the regression-based MSA tasks, EAU attains state-of-the-art performance on CMU-MOSI (48.8 Acc7, 86.2 F1, 0.809 Corr) and CMU-MOSEI (54.8 Acc7, 86.9 F1, 0.816 Corr), with the largest margin in correlation coefficient over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAU outperforms all 14 baselines across the three classification datasets. Against the strongest recent method QMF [5], EAU gains +1.08% Acc on MVSA-Single, +0.28% Acc on Food101, and +1.96% Acc on NYU Depth v2, with the largest margin on the depth-image task. Compared to MMBT [2] (78.50% on MVSA-Single) and TMC [3] (76.06%), EAU improves by 0.65% and 3.09% respectively. On the regression MSA tasks, EAU beats MIB [28] (48.6 Acc7) by 0.2% and DMD [9] (45.6 Acc7) by 3.2% on CMU-MOSI, while on CMU-MOSEI it surpasses ConFEDE [8] (54.9 Acc7) in F1 (86.9 vs 85.8) and correlation (0.816 vs 0.780) despite a 0.1% Acc7 gap. The single-modality BERT baseline [49] (75.61% on MVSA-Single) confirms that multimodal fusion via EAU adds ~3.5% over text-only. Notably, EAU also shows the smallest variance across 10 random seeds (±0.60 on clean MVSA-Single), indicating superior training stability compared to QMF (±1.10) and TMC (±2.24).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) framework for learning compact representations and on distributional (Gaussian) embeddings for uncertainty quantification, combining them in a novel pipeline for multimodal fusion. It departs from prior uncertainty-aware multimodal methods (e.g., QMF, MAP) that treat uncertainty primarily as a signal for weighting cross-modal interactions, by instead leveraging aleatoric uncertainty as a constructive input to self-supervised contrastive learning for stable unimodal embeddings. The dynamic attention-based integration strategy is inspired by recent dynamic fusion work, but the key conceptual shift is reframing intrinsic data noise from a nuisance to be suppressed into a resource that augments representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: most multimodal fusion methods degrade significantly under realistic noise, and this work provides a generic, modality-agnostic mechanism to improve robustness without task-specific tuning. However, the improvements on cleaner datasets (e.g., Food101) are modest, suggesting the method's benefit is most pronounced when data quality is genuinely poor. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to foundation-model-based multimodal pipelines, how it interacts with epistemic uncertainty, and whether the added distributional parameterization and contrastive objective introduce unnecessary complexity compared to simpler noise-robust training strategies.
