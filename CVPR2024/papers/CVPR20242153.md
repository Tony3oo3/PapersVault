## Problem

Existing training acceleration methods for Vision Transformers either sacrifice accuracy, alter the model architecture, or require model-specific hyperparameter tuning and additional training strategies (e.g., EMA, optimizer state resets), limiting their universality across different Transformer networks. The paper asks how to achieve genuine, plug-and-play training speedup while preserving the original training consistency in hyperparameters, architecture, and strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242153/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1K, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) and produces class labels, identical to the original ViT pipeline. During training, after the first Transformer block, only a reduced subset of tokens (controlled by a kept rate r) participates in subsequent blocks, but at inference time the ToE module is removed entirely, restoring the full-token architecture with no architectural change. The method is parameter-free and introduces no additional trainable components.

## Method

ToE is a token growth scheme applied to the output tokens of the first Transformer block, following an 'initialization-expansion-merging' pipeline. First, spatial-distribution token initialization selects a small seed set (rate r₀) via uniform index sampling. Second, widest feature-distribution token expansion iteratively adds the unselected token whose cosine distance to the nearest selected token is largest, ensuring the selected set's feature distribution spans as wide a space as possible; this is parallelized via top-k selection for efficiency. Third, feature-distribution token merging averages each unselected token into its closest selected token, retaining information from all tokens in the reduced set. The token count grows across N_g training stages from r₀ to 1.0, and the entire module is parameter-free, requires no changes to learning rate, architecture, or training strategy, and is removed at inference to recover the original full-token ViT.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, ToE (r₁=0.5) achieves 72.6% top-1 accuracy on DeiT-tiny (+0.4% over baseline) with 1.24× practical training speedup (44.2h vs 54.6h), and 79.8% on DeiT-small (matching baseline) with 1.22× speedup. For DeiT-base, ToE achieves 81.6% (−0.2%) with 1.27× speedup, saving over 60 GPU hours. When combined with EfficientTrain, ToE further boosts DeiT-tiny to 73.5% (+1.0%) at 1.63× speedup, and DeiT-small to 80.4% at 1.53× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToE outperforms all five baselines on accuracy while maintaining or exceeding their practical training speed. Against S²ViTE [10], ToE gains 2.5% top-1 on DeiT-tiny (72.6% vs 70.1%) while preserving full training consistency (hyper-parameters, architecture, strategy), whereas S²ViTE breaks both hyper-parameter and architecture consistency. Against ToMe [11], ToE achieves higher accuracy (72.6% vs 71.7% on DeiT-tiny) and substantially faster practical training (44.2h vs 53.3h, i.e., 1.24× vs 1.02× speedup), since ToMe's layer-wise weighted-average attention is computationally unfriendly. Against NetworkExpansion [12], ToE wins by 2.3% on DeiT-tiny (72.6% vs 70.3%) and 1.0% on DeiT-small (79.8% vs 78.8%) at comparable speedup ratios, while NetworkExpansion requires an extra EMA strategy that inconsistently helps or hurts across model sizes. Against StackBERT [13] on DeiT-base, ToE achieves 81.6% vs 80.8% (1.0% gain) with similar 1.27× vs 1.26× speedup. Finally, ToE is complementary to EfficientTrain [30]: combining them yields 73.5% on DeiT-tiny (+1.0% over baseline) at 1.63× speedup, surpassing EfficientTrain alone (73.3%, 1.44×).

## Relation to Prior Work

ToE departs from the dominant structure-growth paradigm (NetworkExpansion, StackBERT, S²ViTE) that progressively increases the number of trainable parameters, instead operating on the token dimension: it starts with a sparse token set and grows it to full size while keeping all parameters active from the start. The feature-distribution token merging step is directly inspired by ToMe's layer-by-layer token merging, but ToE performs merging only once at the end of the pipeline rather than at every layer, preserving the original attention operator and final architecture. The paper explicitly positions itself against prior methods that break training consistency by altering hyperparameters, modifying the model architecture, or introducing auxiliary strategies like EMA or optimizer-state resets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of ToE—requiring no changes to learning rate schedules, model architecture, or training strategies—makes it immediately applicable to any existing ViT training pipeline, a practical advantage over prior acceleration methods that demand per-model retuning. The finding that early-stage token reduction can slightly improve final accuracy (e.g., +0.4% for DeiT-tiny) suggests that token redundancy in early training is not merely wasteful but actively harmful, a useful insight for the broader efficient-training community. Limitations include the modest ~1.3× speedup ceiling, the restriction to applying ToE only at the first block, and evaluation limited to ViTs on ImageNet and CIFAR; whether the approach transfers to NLP transformers, larger ViT variants (e.g., ViT-H), or other architectures remains open.
