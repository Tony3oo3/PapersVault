## Problem

Correcting lens aberrations computationally requires handling spatially varying, non-stationary blur that differs across the field of view and across diverse lenses. Existing blind methods either target specific aberration types, require lens-specific model training, or rely on expertise-demanding PSF calibration, leaving no general end-to-end solution that works across a wide range of low-end lenses without any prior knowledge of the PSF.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242194/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality RGB image (e.g., 1280×1280) captured by an unknown low-end lens and produces an aberration-corrected high-quality image. Internally, the input is partitioned into 5 concentric annular rings, each warped into a rectangular stripe and cropped into 128×128 patches; the network is fully convolutional and handles arbitrary image sizes. No PSF or lens metadata is required at inference time, making the pipeline entirely blind.

## Method

The approach exploits the rotational symmetry of optical lenses by dividing the field of view into concentric annular rings and warping them into rectangular stripes, transforming spatially varying PSFs into approximately uniform ones. Orthogonal Non-negative Matrix Factorization (ONMF) is then applied to a library of 1,840 PSF patches from 20 simulated lenses to learn 9 orthogonal non-negative basis kernels that span the aberration space. At inference, a U-Net-based decomposition network splits the blurry input into components attributed to each basis, and a set of pre-trained adaptive feature-domain Wiener deconvolution modules (cascaded encoder-decoder networks) independently reverse the degeneration for each basis. An attention-based fusion network, guided by a learned decomposition-confidence signal, aggregates the multiple deconvolved estimates into the final output. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end in a supervised manner on 9,200 sharp-blurry patch pairs generated by convolving Flickr2K images with Zemax-simulated PSFs.

## Results

On the synthetic Flickr2K-based evaluation, the proposed blind method achieves 30.96 dB PSNR and 0.952 SSIM, outperforming all blind baselines (DeblurGANv2: 23.04 dB, MPRNet: 28.67 dB, Eboli's: 29.42 dB) and approaching the non-blind methods DPIR (31.86 dB) and DWDN (31.78 dB) within ~0.9 dB. The method uses 9 ONMF bases with 5 annular rings, preserving 96.6% of PSF variation across 20 training lenses while generalizing to unseen lenses without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all three blind baselines: it surpasses DeblurGANv2 by 7.92 dB PSNR, MPRNet by 2.29 dB, and Eboli's by 1.54 dB, confirming that generic deblurring networks struggle with the structured non-uniformity of lens aberrations. Against the two non-blind methods, the gap is much smaller: the proposed method trails DPIR by 0.90 dB and DWDN by 0.82 dB in PSNR, and by only 0.008–0.010 in SSIM, while requiring no PSF calibration. This near-parity with non-blind approaches is the paper's central empirical claim, demonstrating that the physics-informed low-rank decomposition effectively recovers the information that explicit PSF knowledge would otherwise provide. On real-world images from compact cameras, the method consistently improves both center and periphery quality across multiple lenses, further validating generalization beyond the synthetic training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the annular partitioning and ring-to-rectangle warping strategy introduced by Yue et al. [45], which exploits the rotational symmetry of optical lenses to convert spatially varying aberrations into approximately uniform ones. It departs from that work by replacing the iterative kernel-estimation-and-deblurring loop with a learned low-rank PSF representation via Orthogonal Non-negative Matrix Factorization and a divide-and-conquer deep network (decompose → feature-domain Wiener deconvolution → attention-based fusion). The feature-domain deconvolution design draws on DWDN [6], while the overall blind, lens-agnostic framing extends prior non-blind or lens-specific approaches (DPIR, DWDN, Schuler et al.) into a single universal model requiring no PSF calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for low-budget and low-payload platforms (UAVs, compact surveillance cameras) where high-end multi-element lenses are infeasible: a single trained model corrects aberrations across diverse lenses without expert calibration. However, the ~1 dB PSNR gap to non-blind methods (30.96 vs. 31.86) and the assumption of sufficient exposure and high pixel count limit immediate deployment in challenging conditions. Open questions include extending the framework to handle realistic sensor noise, chromatic aberration, and downsampling, as well as reducing model size for on-chip or real-time inference on embedded hardware.
