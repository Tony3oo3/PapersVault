## Problem

Current online HD map estimation methods produce deterministic point predictions without any uncertainty or confidence information, forcing downstream consumers like trajectory predictors to implicitly treat all inferred map elements as equally reliable. This is problematic because mapping errors (occlusions, sensor range limits, lighting changes) can cause errant downstream behaviors, and there is no principled way to represent uncertainty in vectorized map formats the way rasterized BEV maps naturally encode it as probability heatmaps.

## Input / Output

The mapping models consume multi-camera RGB images (6 cameras, nuScenes) and output vectorized map elements (road boundaries, lane dividers, pedestrian crosswalks, and optionally lane centerlines) within a 60 m × 30 m perception range, where each polyline vertex is now parameterized by a 2D Laplace distribution (location μ and scale b per axis) plus a class probability vector. The prediction models take these uncertainty-augmented map elements together with 2 seconds of agent trajectory history (upsampled to 10 Hz via trajdata) and produce 6 multimodal trajectories 3 seconds into the future. The Laplace scale parameter b is transformed to standard deviation σ = √2·b and rotated consistently with the ego-vehicle coordinate frame.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the deterministic MLP regression head in vectorized map estimation models with a probabilistic head that outputs per-vertex, per-dimension Laplace distribution parameters (μ, b), trained with a Negative Log-Likelihood loss in place of the standard L1 regression loss. The Laplace distribution is chosen over Gaussian for training stability, heavier tails to handle outliers, and natural alignment with the L1 losses already used by MapTR, MapTRv2, and StreamMapNet. Downstream, trajectory prediction models (DenseTNT with GNN encoding and HiVT with Transformer cross-attention) are augmented to encode the full Laplace parameters and class probabilities into vertex embeddings rather than just coordinates; for HiVT specifically, the map is re-input as a point set (rather than a vector set) so that per-point scale b can be directly concatenated with the mean values. This formulation is applied to three state-of-the-art mapping models and two prediction models, yielding up to 50% faster training convergence and up to 15% better prediction accuracy on nuScenes without degrading pure mapping performance.

## Results

On nuScenes, incorporating map uncertainty into trajectory prediction yields up to 15% improvement in Miss Rate (MR) and up to 5% improvement in minADE/minFDE across all mapping-prediction combinations. The best overall configuration (HiVT + MapTRv2-Centerline + uncertainty) achieves minADE 0.3727, minFDE 0.7492, and MR 0.0726. Training convergence is accelerated by up to 50% (e.g., DenseTNT with MapTR converges in 4 epochs vs. 8 without uncertainty). Notably, DenseTNT trained on MapTRv2-Centerline + uncertainty (minADE 0.8135, MR 0.1593) outperforms the same model trained on ground-truth maps (minADE 0.8809, MR 0.1903).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares each mapping model (MapTR, MapTRv2, StreamMapNet) with and without the proposed Laplace uncertainty output, paired with two prediction models (HiVT, DenseTNT). Against MapTR, adding uncertainty consistently improves HiVT (MR −15%, minFDE −6%) and DenseTNT (minFDE −3%, MR −1%). Against MapTRv2, HiVT benefits substantially (MR −14%, minFDE −4%), while DenseTNT shows mixed results (MR −5% but minADE +4%). Against StreamMapNet, both predictors improve across all metrics (HiVT: MR −7%; DenseTNT: minADE −5%, minFDE −5%, MR −8%). The most consistent and largest gains appear in Miss Rate, indicating that uncertainty helps prediction models avoid large endpoint errors. An interesting finding is that MapTR (the weaker mapper) actually yields better prediction performance than MapTRv2 without uncertainty, suggesting upstream mapping accuracy does not directly translate to downstream prediction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: vectorized online HD map estimation (MapTR, MapTRv2, StreamMapNet) and map-informed trajectory prediction (DenseTNT, HiVT). Prior map estimation methods produce deterministic point regressions with no confidence signal, while prediction models implicitly assume map inputs are ground truth. The paper's conceptual departure is introducing a probabilistic (Laplace) regression head into the map estimation output and a corresponding uncertainty-aware encoding in the prediction encoder, bridging the two without architectural changes to either backbone. The Laplace choice is specifically motivated by the L1 regression losses already standard in these mapping models, making the extension a natural rather than ad hoc modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for AV stacks operating outside pre-mapped regions: uncertainty-aware prediction allows the system to degrade gracefully when map elements are occluded or distant, rather than confidently following erroneous geometry. The 50% training convergence speedup is a tangible efficiency gain for practitioners. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (nuScenes) and a small set of model combinations, and the finding that a predicted map with uncertainty can outperform the GT map (Table 3) is intriguing but rests on a single data point. Open questions include whether the Laplace formulation captures inter-vertex correlations, how the approach scales to higher-speed driving, and whether map model calibration can be formally assessed given the fuzzy point-set matching problem the authors identify.
