## Problem

Existing depth completion methods either operate purely in 2D feature space, losing fine-grained 3D geometric information, or directly incorporate raw 3D point clouds whose extreme sparsity (<6%) and distance-varying distributions severely limit their effectiveness. Neither paradigm adequately captures the 3D geometry needed for accurate dense depth reconstruction, particularly in outdoor scenarios where LiDAR density is as low as 5%.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242019/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse depth map (from LiDAR, TOF, or structured light) and an aligned RGB image as input. The sparse depth is first back-projected into a 3D point cloud, then decomposed into three 2D orthogonal views (top W×D, side D×H, front H×W), where the front view contains the original sparse depth. The output is a dense depth map at the front-view resolution (e.g., 1216×256 on KITTI, 304×228 on NYUv2, 512×384 on TOFDC), with the top and side views also producing auxiliary depth predictions used for geometric consistency.

## Method

TPVD decomposes the 3D point cloud into three 2D orthogonal views (top, side, front) processed by separate 2D UNets, enabling efficient 2D convolution to densify sparse measurements while preserving 3D geometry. A recurrent TPV Fusion module performs 2D→3D→2D aggregation: 2D features are projected back to 3D Cartesian space, re-projected into spherical coordinates where a Distance-Aware Spherical Convolution (DASC) applies 3D convolutions over spherical subareas whose volume scales with distance to handle varying LiDAR point density, then re-projected to 2D to update the view features. A plug-and-play Geometric Spatial Propagation Network (GSPN) further refines the output by constructing deformable neighbor affinity simultaneously across all three 2D TPV spaces and their joint 3D projection, propagating geometric consistency in a manner that preserves both local neighborhood structure and global 3D shape.

## Results

On the KITTI online depth completion leaderboard, TPVD achieves the best results across all four metrics: RMSE 693.97 mm, MAE 188.60 mm, iRMSE 1.82, and iMAE 0.81, with 31.2M parameters. On NYUv2, TPVD attains RMSE 0.086 m and REL 0.010, outperforming the closest competitors (BEV@DC and PointDC at 0.089 m RMSE) by 3 mm. On the newly introduced TOFDC dataset, TPVD reduces RMSE by 15.6% and REL by 33.3% over the second-best 2D-3D joint method (PointDC), and is 21 mm superior in RMSE to the best 2D-based method (CFormer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPVD outperforms all 25 baselines on KITTI, beating the strongest 2D method (LRRU, 696.51 mm RMSE) by 2.54 mm and the strongest 2D-3D joint method (BEV@DC, 697.44 mm RMSE) by 3.47 mm. Against lightweight 2D-3D joint methods (FuseNet, ACMNet, PointFusion), TPVD reduces RMSE by an average of 52.59 mm and MAE by 20.86 mm. On NYUv2, TPVD (0.086 m RMSE) edges out BEV@DC and PointDC (both 0.089 m) by 3 mm, and reduces REL by 20% over those two methods. On TOFDC, TPVD (0.092 m RMSE) substantially outperforms the best 2D-3D joint PointDC (0.109 m) by 17 mm and the best 2D-based CFormer (0.113 m) by 21 mm. In depth-only settings on KITTI, TPVD (948.6 mm RMSE) surpasses the second-best depth-only method LRRU (957.4 mm) by 8.8 mm, confirming robustness without RGB guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPVD departs from both 2D-only depth completion methods (which lack 3D geometric awareness) and 2D-3D joint approaches (which directly process raw sparse point clouds) by decomposing the 3D point cloud into three orthogonal 2D views—top, front, and side—thereby densifying sparse geometry in 2D space while retaining 3D structure. The tri-perspective view projection and 3D aggregation mechanism are adapted from the TPV framework for semantic occupancy prediction [13, 61], while the deformable neighbor search in GSPN extends NLSPN [30] from a single 2D plane to a joint multi-view 3D setting. The point feature extraction pipeline (MLP + continuous convolutions) follows FuseNet [3], and the overall architecture is a CNN-based UNet with spatial propagation, placing it within the established SPN lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TPV decomposition offers a principled way to bridge the gap between 2D efficiency and 3D geometric fidelity, and the DASC module specifically addresses the well-known problem of non-uniform LiDAR point density across distances. The introduction of TOFDC, a smartphone-based depth completion dataset, opens a practical avenue for studying depth completion on consumer hardware where TOF sensors are increasingly common. Open questions include whether the TPV decomposition generalizes to even sparser inputs (below 5% density), whether the recurrent 2D-3D-2D fusion can be made more efficient for real-time edge deployment, and whether the approach transfers to other 3D perception tasks beyond depth completion.
