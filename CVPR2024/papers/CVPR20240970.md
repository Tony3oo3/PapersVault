## Problem

Existing arbitrary motion style transfer methods face two intertwined challenges: (1) the content-style duality, where disentangling and recombining content and style in latent space is difficult, and (2) the granularity of trajectory details, where conventional methods either directly copy the content trajectory onto stylized motions (causing foot sliding artifacts) or ignore trajectory altogether. No prior diffusion-based approach had integrated trajectory awareness into the style transfer pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a content 3D motion sequence and a style 3D motion sequence, each represented as 3D joint rotations, positions, velocities, and foot contact over L frames, with trajectory derived from root-node rotation and velocity. During training, the same motion serves as both content and style input (self-reconstruction), while at inference the style motion provides only the style condition. The output is a stylized 3D human motion decoded from a 7×256 latent space via a pre-trained motion VAE decoder, with the guidance scale λ (default 2.5) controlling the degree of style transfer.

## Method

MCM-LDM operates in the latent space of a pre-trained motion VAE (from MLD) and introduces a priority-based multi-condition denoiser where content features are the primary condition (concatenated with the noisy latent at every step) while trajectory and style features are secondary conditions injected via AdaLN-Zero modulation at each of the 9 transformer layers. A Multi-condition Extraction module disentangles the three conditions: content is encoded by the VAE encoder followed by a StyleRemover (Instance Normalization + transformer + linear projection) to strip style information; style is extracted by a pre-trained MotionCLIP encoder aligned with text/image latent spaces; and trajectory is encoded by a dedicated transformer-based Trajectory Encoder. Training is self-supervised (self-reconstruction) with classifier-free guidance, where the style condition is randomly dropped 25% of the time to jointly train full-condition and dual-condition models. At inference, the predicted noise is a weighted combination of the full-condition and style-free predictions, enabling continuous control over style intensity while preserving content structure and learned trajectory dynamics.

## Results

On the HumanML3D dataset, MCM-LDM achieves the best FMD of 27.69 (lower is better), substantially outperforming 1DConv+AdaIN (42.68), Motion Puzzle (113.31), and STGCN+AdaIN (129.44). It also attains the highest Style Recognition Accuracy (SRA) of 58.00% and the second-best Content Recognition Accuracy (CRA) of 35.75%, while achieving the second-lowest Foot Sliding Factor (FSF) of 1.28. The Trajectory Similarity Index (TSI) is 0.40, which is higher than baselines that directly copy trajectories, but the paper argues this reflects a learned trade-off that avoids the foot-sliding artifacts inherent in copy-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCM-LDM clearly wins on motion quality (FMD: 27.69 vs. 42.68 for 1DConv+AdaIN, 113.31 for Motion Puzzle, 129.44 for STGCN+AdaIN) and style transfer (SRA: 58.00% vs. 57.00%, 46.33%, 17.66% respectively). On content preservation (CRA), STGCN+AdaIN leads at 60.43%, but this comes at the cost of the worst SRA (17.66%), indicating it largely reconstructs the original motion rather than transferring style; MCM-LDM's CRA of 35.75% is second-best and the paper attributes the gap to intentional minor content modifications for more integrated style. On foot sliding (FSF), MCM-LDM (1.28) is second only to STGCN+AdaIN (0.93) and substantially better than 1DConv+AdaIN (2.05) and Motion Puzzle (2.43). On trajectory similarity (TSI), MCM-LDM scores 0.40, worse than all baselines, but the authors explain that baselines achieve low TSI by directly copying the content trajectory, which causes foot-sliding artifacts, whereas MCM-LDM learns trajectory preservation as a soft condition, trading some raw similarity for physically plausible foot contact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motion style transfer line of work (Aberman et al., Park et al., Jang et al.'s Motion Puzzle) by replacing their convolutional/graph-based architectures with a latent diffusion model, building directly on MLD's motion VAE and latent-space diffusion framework. It departs from prior AMST methods in two key ways: (1) training via self-reconstruction from a single motion rather than requiring separate content and style inputs, and (2) introducing trajectory as a learned diffusion condition rather than directly copying it from the content motion. The multi-condition denoiser design draws on AdaLN-Zero from scalable diffusion transformers and classifier-free guidance, while the StyleRemover concept is adapted from ArtFusion's dual-condition approach to the motion domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical problem in motion style transfer—foot sliding artifacts from trajectory copying—by reframing trajectory preservation as a learned condition within a diffusion process, which is a conceptually clean solution. However, limitations remain: the model cannot generate arbitrary trajectories independent of the content, struggles with motions beyond the training temporal scope, and has not been tested on motions with complex environmental interactions. The primary/secondary condition priority mechanism (content as primary via concatenation, style and trajectory as secondary via AdaLN-Zero) offers a reusable design pattern for multi-condition diffusion in other domains, and the use of pre-trained MotionCLIP for style extraction demonstrates the value of cross-modal alignment in motion understanding.
