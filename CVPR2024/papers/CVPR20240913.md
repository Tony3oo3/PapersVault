## Problem

The development of supervised underwater image/video enhancement is bottlenecked by the absence of large-scale, high-quality paired real-world datasets, with existing benchmarks (e.g., UIEB, LSUI) containing only hundreds to thousands of frames. Additionally, no supervised method existed that efficiently exploits the redundant inter-frame information in underwater videos to improve enhancement quality without the heavy computational cost of frame alignment or multi-frame aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240913/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UVE-Net consumes a temporal window of 2t+1 degraded underwater video frames (t=1, so 3 frames) at full resolution (up to 3840×2160 4K UHD) and outputs the corresponding enhanced frames. The middle frame is additionally downsampled by 4× to feed the auxiliary branch, so the network simultaneously processes both full-resolution and quarter-resolution representations. The UVEB benchmark provides 453,874 paired frame pairs spanning resolutions from 960×528 to 3840×2160, with 38% being UHD 4K, plus 2,616 manually annotated quality scores for both raw and GT videos.

## Method

UVE-Net employs a dual-branch architecture: an upper Video Enhancement Network (VE-Net) processes the full-resolution frame window, while a lower Auxiliary Enhancement Network (AE-Net) enhances a 4×-downsampled middle frame. The key mechanism is converting AE-Net's intermediate features into dynamic 3×3 convolutional kernels via Feature Extraction Guidance Module (FEGM) and Feature Restoration Guidance Module (FRGM), which are then applied to all frames in the window—effectively transferring the low-resolution enhancement 'action instructions' to guide full-resolution processing without any frame alignment or feature aggregation. Resolution transitions use PixelUnshuffle (4×↓ for kernel generation, 2×↓ for feature preparation) and PixelShuffle (2×↑, 4×↑ for upscaling), while group convolutions keep the guidance modules lightweight. Training uses Charbonnier loss on both branches with an Adam optimizer at lr=2×10⁻⁴ over 150K iterations on 4 A40 GPUs.

## Results

On the UVEB test set (100 video pairs), UVE-Net achieves a PSNR of 26.27 dB and MSE of 0.4059×10³, outperforming the best baseline PUIE (24.21 dB, 0.4335×10³) by 2.06 dB in PSNR. The simplified variant UVE-Net-s achieves 24.43 dB PSNR with only 0.091 s per-frame inference time and 5.6 GB memory, enabling 11 FPS on 4K and 25 FPS on 2K videos. UVE-Net's full model runs at 0.675 s per frame with 11.04 GB GPU memory, while the ablation shows that adding FEGM and FRGM reduces TFLOPs from 13.95 to 11.42 while improving PSNR from 25.15 to 26.27 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UVE-Net outperforms all 20 compared baselines on both PSNR and MSE. The closest competitor is PUIE [3] (24.21 dB PSNR, 0.4335×10³ MSE), which UVE-Net surpasses by 2.06 dB in PSNR; URanker [36] (23.93 dB) and USUIR [45] (21.64 dB) follow. Among learning-free methods, CLAHE [5] (19.71 dB) and Red Channel [30] (19.61 dB) are the strongest, yet still trail UVE-Net by over 6 dB. The simplified UVE-Net-s (24.43 dB) also beats PUIE and URanker while using only 5.6 GB memory and 0.091 s inference time—faster than all deep learning baselines except FspiralGAN [26] (0.0474 s) and CLAHE [5] (0.0503 s). Notably, UVE-Net achieves the best quality with moderate memory (11.04 GB), whereas LANet [10] and PUIE [3] require over 33 GB for comparable or inferior performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the underwater image enhancement (UIE) literature—anchored by paired benchmarks like UIEB (2019) and LSUI (2023)—to the video domain, where no prior supervised benchmark or method existed. Architecturally, UVE-Net departs from the dominant video restoration paradigm of frame alignment or feature aggregation by instead generating dynamic convolutional kernels from a downsampled middle frame and transmitting them as 'action instructions' to adjacent frames, drawing on the dynamic convolution idea (DCKG) but repurposing it for inter-frame guidance rather than spatial adaptivity. The dataset construction strategy (selecting the best of 20 enhancement outputs as pseudo-GT) directly follows UIEB and LSUI, but scales it to 453K+ high-resolution frame pairs across 20+ countries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UVEB addresses the primary bottleneck in underwater vision research—the scarcity of large-scale, real-world, paired training data—by providing 1,308 video pairs with 4K resolution and diverse degradation types, making it the largest underwater video dataset to date. The UVE-Net architecture demonstrates that inter-frame information can be exploited at the kernel level without costly alignment, offering a practical path toward real-time underwater video enhancement (11 FPS on 4K with the simplified model). Key open questions include whether the pseudo-GT construction (best-of-20-methods) introduces systematic bias, whether the dynamic-convolution guidance generalizes to other degradation types or video tasks, and whether the provided quality scores can be leveraged for quality-aware training.
