## Problem

Existing one-shot face stylization methods focus primarily on color and texture transfer while neglecting geometric deformation, a key characteristic of artistic styles. Their cross-domain guidance relies on GAN inversion of the style reference, which produces unfaithful mappings for artistic images—especially under strong exaggerations—thereby misleading structural deformation. The paper asks whether reliable deformation guidance can be built from a single real-style image pair using a more robust structural representation than GAN inversion.

## Input / Output

For training, the method requires a single paired reference: one real face and its corresponding artistic style image. At inference, a real facial image is inverted into W+ space (18×512 latent codes) via a pre-trained e4e encoder, and a single forward pass through the fine-tuned generator yields the stylized output. The STN warping fields support a continuous deformation control parameter α ∈ [0,1], allowing users to interpolate between the undeformed and fully deformed stylized result. Fine-tuning completes in approximately 10 minutes on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090.

## Method

The framework fine-tunes a StyleGANv2 generator with thin-plate-spline spatial transformer (TPS-STN) modules inserted at 32×32 and 64×64 resolutions to enable structural warping of intermediate feature maps. The key insight is that DINO-ViT tokens (not keys) provide consistent structural semantics across real and artistic face domains, outperforming CLIP and FaRL, due to DINO's self-distillation training. Two novel DINO-space losses constrain the fine-tuning: a directional deformation loss that aligns the cosine direction of the real-to-style deformation vector in DINO space between the reference pair and generated pairs, and a relative structural consistency loss that matches self-similarity matrices of DINO tokens across domains to prevent overfitting. Color alignment is achieved by swapping fine-level (9th–18th) W+ codes with those of the inverted reference, and a patch-level (22×22) discriminator provides adversarial supervision for local texture and color fidelity.

## Results

Across three stylization examples, the proposed method achieves the best directional content consistency (dir-CC) in all cases (0.196, 0.194, 0.191) and the best directional identity similarity (dir-ID) in two of three (0.468, 0.379, 0.330), substantially outperforming one-shot baselines that use only a style reference. LPIPS is competitive (0.353, 0.379, 0.342) though DiFa achieves a lower LPIPS of 0.293 on Example-2. Against the paired variants (MTG-pair, JoJoGAN-pair), the method still leads on dir-CC and dir-ID while maintaining comparable or better LPIPS, confirming the advantage of DINO-based structural guidance over simple inversion-based guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four one-shot baselines (MTG, JoJoGAN, DiFa, OneshotCLIP) that train with only a style reference, the proposed method achieves the highest dir-CC in all three examples (e.g., 0.196 vs. 0.123 for MTG on Example-1) and the highest dir-ID in two of three (0.468 vs. 0.264 for MTG on Example-1), indicating substantially better structural deformation and identity preservation. On LPIPS, the method is competitive but not uniformly best: DiFa achieves a lower LPIPS of 0.293 on Example-2 compared to 0.379 for the proposed method, suggesting DiFa produces slightly more perceptually similar outputs in that specific case. Against the paired variants (MTG-pair, JoJoGAN-pair), which replace the unfaithful GAN inversion with a ground-truth real face, the proposed method still leads on dir-CC (0.196 vs. 0.195/0.180 on Example-1) and dir-ID (0.468 vs. 0.392/0.405 on Example-1), while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.353 vs. 0.423/0.381). The user study further confirms these quantitative findings, with preference rates of 73.0%–83.7% over the one-shot baselines and 70.7%–76.7% over the paired variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot face stylization line (MTG, JoJoGAN, DiFa, OneshotCLIP) by replacing the single style reference with a paired real–style reference, thereby avoiding the unfaithful GAN inversion that prior methods rely on to construct cross-domain guidance. Architecturally, it builds on StyleGANv2 with plug-in thin-plate-spline spatial transformers (inspired by StyleCariGAN and Vtoonify) but departs from those works by requiring no additional large-scale training data. The key conceptual departure is the use of DINO self-supervised ViT tokens as a consistent structural representation across the real and artistic domains, which the authors show empirically outperforms CLIP and FaRL features for facial semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical ~10-minute fine-tuning recipe on a single GPU that simultaneously handles appearance transfer and geometric exaggeration, addressing a gap where prior one-shot methods preserved structure rigidly. However, the method still requires a paired reference (a real face and its stylized counterpart), which is a stronger assumption than the strict single-image setting of prior work; the paper's own paired examples are sourced from other generative models, leaving open how well the approach generalizes when a true human-created pair is unavailable. Broader open questions include whether the DINO-based directional guidance transfers to non-facial domains or to more extreme style shifts beyond caricature-level exaggeration.
