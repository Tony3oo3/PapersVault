## Problem

Most state-of-the-art visual tracking algorithms rely on hand-crafted components—such as confidence-score-based template update strategies with manual hyperparameters or historical appearance integration via concatenation and memory networks—to exploit spatio-temporal information. These approaches introduce design complexity, additional hyperparameters, or error accumulation, leaving spatio-temporal information far from being fully explored in a principled, end-to-end manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242322/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a template image (128×128 or 192×192) and a search region image (256×256 or 384×384) as spatial inputs, plus a sliding window of m=4 autoregressive target queries propagated from previous frames. At inference, a Hamming window is applied to incorporate positional priors. The output is a center-based prediction: a classification score map, bounding box size, and sub-pixel offset, from which the final target location and scale are derived.

## Method

AQA-Track combines a HiViT-Base spatial encoder (hierarchical 4×4 patch downsampling with 8 MLP layers and 2 merging layers) with a 3-layer temporal decoder that processes learnable autoregressive target queries in a sliding-window fashion. The key novelty is a temporal attention mechanism where the query vector Q is derived from the current-frame query Q_cur while keys and values come from the full set Q_all (concatenation of previous-frame queries Q_pre and Q_cur), enabling the model to generate a new query that encodes instantaneous appearance changes. A parameter-free spatio-temporal fusion module (STM) then computes a dot-product similarity between search spatial features and the temporal representation, using it as an element-wise gate to enhance spatial features before a center-based prediction head. Training uses video-level sampling (n sequences × m frame pairs per batch) with focal loss and GIoU loss, and the entire pipeline requires no hand-designed update rules or hyperparameters for temporal modeling.

## Results

AQATrack-256 achieves 71.4% AUC on LaSOT, outperforming all same-resolution trackers (next best: ARTrack-256 at 70.4%). AQATrack-384 reaches 72.7% AUC on LaSOT, 76.0% AO on GOT-10k, 59.3% AUC on TNL2K, and 71.2% AUC on UAV123, setting new state-of-the-art on all six benchmarks. On TrackingNet, AQATrack-384 attains 84.8% AUC and the best Pnorm of 89.3%. The tracker runs at 67.6 FPS (256) and 44.2 FPS (384) on a Tesla V100, more than 2× faster than STARK.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AQATrack-256 outperforms all same-resolution baselines on LaSOT (71.4% vs. ARTrack-256's 70.4%, F-BDMTrack-256's 69.9%, SeqTrack-B256's 69.9%) and LaSOT_ext (51.2% vs. SeqTrack-B256's 49.5%), with the largest margin on LaSOT_ext where similar-object interference is most severe. At 384 resolution, AQATrack-384 leads on LaSOT (72.7% vs. ARTrack-384's 72.6%), GOT-10k (76.0% AO vs. ARTrack-384's 75.5%), TNL2K (59.3% vs. ARTrack-384's 59.8% — a narrow loss), and UAV123 (71.2% vs. F-BDMTrack-384's 70.9%). On TrackingNet, AQATrack-384 achieves the best Pnorm (89.3%) and P (84.3%) but trails ARTrack by 0.3% in AUC (84.8% vs. 85.1%). Against older baselines like STARK (67.1% LaSOT AUC) and TransT (64.9%), the gains are substantial (4.3–6.6% AUC), confirming that autoregressive temporal queries provide a clear advantage over purely spatial or single-frame attention approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

AQA-Track carves new ground by transplanting the query-based paradigm from DETR-style detection and MOTR-style multi-object tracking into single object tracking (SOT) for the first time, using autoregressive target queries to model spatio-temporal information in a sliding-window fashion. It departs from the two dominant strategies for temporal modeling in trackers—dynamic template updating (SeqTrack, ROMTrack) and historical appearance integration (STMTrack, UpdateNet)—by replacing hand-crafted update rules with learnable queries propagated frame-by-frame. The spatial backbone leverages HiViT's hierarchical downsampling rather than the standard ViT patch embedding, and the parameter-free dot-product fusion (STM) replaces learned cross-attention for combining spatial and temporal features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The autoregressive query formulation offers a clean, hyperparameter-free alternative to template updating for capturing appearance changes, and the parameter-free STM module demonstrates that simple arithmetic can match or exceed learned fusion layers. However, the practical ceiling is constrained by GPU memory: the optimal spatio-temporal window is only m=4 frames, and extending to m=8 or 16 degrades performance due to reduced batch diversity, leaving long-range temporal modeling largely unexplored. Whether this query-based SOT paradigm can scale to longer sequences with memory-efficient attention or KV-cache strategies remains an open question that could unlock substantially stronger temporal reasoning.
