## Problem

Existing motion capture datasets either annotate foot contact via purely visual distance thresholding (inheriting pose and scene-reconstruction errors) or rely on pressure sensors restricted to slow, small-range movements. There is no dataset that simultaneously provides synchronized vision and dense pressure signals for large-range, fast human motion with accurate per-vertex foot-contact labels, leaving a gap for physically plausible motion capture under dynamic conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240887/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The RGBD-P fitting pipeline consumes synchronized Azure Kinect RGBD video (30 Hz) and Xsensor insole pressure (242 sensors per foot, up to 150 Hz), producing SMPL pose (θ∈R⁷²), shape (β∈R¹⁰), global rotation, and translation, with dense contact defined on 192 selected foot-surface vertices. The VP-MoCap baseline takes only monocular RGB video, internally extracting 2D keypoints (RTMPose), initial pose/shape (CLIFF), and a depth map (ZoeDepth), then outputs optimized SMPL pose and pelvis trajectory. FPP-Net specifically maps a sequence of 2D keypoints to a 192-dimensional contact vector and a corresponding pressure distribution.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary components. First, an RGBD-P SMPL fitting method that augments standard depth (DoubleFusion), 2D projection, and GMM (SMPLify) losses with a dense contact loss penalizing the vertical distance of contacted foot vertices from the ground and a temporal contact-consistency loss that enforces smoothness of foot-plane vertices across consecutive contact frames; shape blending (AGORA-style α-interpolation between adult and child templates) handles the teenage subjects. Second, VP-MoCap, a monocular baseline where FPP-Net (a per-frame encoder → GRU → MLP decoder trained with binary cross-entropy on contact and MSE on pressure) predicts dense foot contact from 2D keypoint sequences, and a subsequent optimization jointly minimizes 2D keypoint reprojection, pose mimicry, 3D foot-to-ground contact (via ZoeDepth point clouds), and temporal foot-joint consistency to recover stable global translation and reduce foot sliding.

## Results

On MMVP, VP-MoCap achieves the best MPJPE (83.0 mm) and PVE-Feet (91.9 mm) among all compared monocular MoCap methods, and dramatically reduces trajectory error (Traj) to 129.3 mm versus 211.4 mm (CLIFF), 598.0 mm (TRACE), and 679.7 mm (SMPLer-X). For foot contact estimation, FPP-Net attains the highest F1 (0.532) and IOU (0.522) on MMVP-test, surpassing the fine-tuned BSTRO[FT] (F1 0.496, IOU 0.496). The RGBD-P SMPL fitting also outperforms PROX and LEMO on both 3D depth error (0.043 vs. 0.045/0.150) and contact F1 (0.518 vs. 0.475/0.423).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VP-MoCap outperforms all three monocular MoCap baselines (CLIFF, TRACE, SMPLer-X) on MPJPE, PVE-Feet, and Traj, with the most dramatic gain in trajectory accuracy: 129.3 mm vs. 211.4 mm (CLIFF), 598.0 mm (TRACE), and 679.7 mm (SMPLer-X). On PMPJPE, SMPLer-X (51.1) and CLIFF (58.7) are slightly ahead of VP-MoCap (56.0), indicating a small trade-off in Procrustes-aligned pose accuracy for the large translation improvement. For foot contact estimation, FPP-Net beats BSTRO (F1 0.318) and DECO (F1 0.191) by a wide margin, and narrowly surpasses the fine-tuned BSTRO[FT] (F1 0.496 → 0.532; IOU 0.496 → 0.522), with the advantage attributed to temporal keypoint sequences rather than single-frame regression. In the RGBD-P fitting comparison, the proposed method dominates PROX (MFCE 7.97 vs. 9.35; F1 0.518 vs. 0.475) and both LEMO stages (MFCE 7.97 vs. 8.20/8.23; F1 0.518 vs. 0.423/0.377), confirming that pressure-derived contact constraints yield more accurate and stable foot-ground alignment than purely visual scene fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: vision-based MoCap datasets that annotate foot contact via visual fitting and distance thresholding (PROX, RICH, BSTRO, DECO), and pressure-sensor datasets from biomechanics that capture plantar force but lack synchronized visual signals (PSU-TMM100, MOYO). It departs from the visual-thresholding paradigm by using direct Xsensor insole measurements (242 sensors per foot at 150 Hz) to produce dense contact labels, and from the slow-movement constraint of prior vision-pressure datasets by capturing running, jumping, and skipping. The FPP-Net inherits the sequential-2D-keypoint formulation from PhysCap and TMM100 for contact prediction, while the RGBD-P fitting extends DoubleFusion's depth alignment and SMPLify's GMM prior with explicit dense-contact and temporal-contact loss terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMVP is the first dataset to provide synchronized RGBD video and dense plantar pressure for large-range, fast human motion, filling a gap that limits the accuracy of foot-contact supervision in current MoCap pipelines. The demonstrated ~39% reduction in translation error (Traj 129.3 vs. 211.4 for CLIFF) and the ablation showing that 3D contact constraints trade a small amount of pose accuracy for large gains in foot and translation accuracy highlight a practical design tension for future systems. Limitations include static-camera-only capture, homogeneous indoor scenes, and manual synchronization of the two sensor streams, leaving open questions about moving-camera generalization, outdoor scene diversity, and whether pressure sequences alone can encode full body dynamics.
