## Problem

Self-supervised semantic correspondence methods (e.g., DINOv2) produce identical features for visually similar but semantically distinct parts, failing to distinguish object symmetries (left vs. right side) and repeated part instances (e.g., four wheels). This occurs because 2D-based training objectives cannot learn 3D-aware representations. Additionally, the standard PCK evaluation metric fails to penalize symmetry-related errors since it only scores keypoints visible in both images simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a category label (one-hot embedding) as input. A frozen DINOv2 or DINOv1 backbone extracts dense features, which are then passed through a lightweight prediction head to produce per-pixel spherical coordinates on S². At inference, correspondences between two images are computed by combining cosine distances in both the SSL feature space and the spherical coordinate space (weighted with α=0.2 for the sphere term). Training additionally requires segmentation masks (from Mask R-CNN) and coarse camera viewpoint annotations discretized into a small number of bins.

## Method

The core contribution is mapping object features onto a sphere S² to inject a weak 3D geometric prior without requiring full mesh supervision. A spherical prototype network (a visual transformer with cross-attention to a category token) maps each point on S² back to the SSL feature space, and a prediction head on the frozen backbone produces the pixel-to-sphere mapping. Training enforces four losses: a reconstruction loss ensuring the prototype recovers SSL features, a viewpoint regularization loss aligning the mean spherical map direction with coarse camera pose, a relative distance triplet loss preserving image-plane distance ordering on the sphere, and an orientation loss preventing mirror-image inversions. At inference, the low-dimensional spherical map is combined with the high-dimensional SSL features to break symmetry and repetition ambiguities while retaining fine-grained part discrimination.

## Results

On SPair-71k (PCK@0.1, macro-averaged), the method with a DINOv2 backbone achieves 63.9, outperforming the DINOv2-only baseline (56.2) by +7.7 points and the DINOv2+SD baseline (63.3) by +0.6. With Stable Diffusion features added at inference, the combined method reaches 67.3 PCK. Under the proposed KAP@0.1 metric, which better penalizes symmetry-induced errors, the method scores 51.5 vs. 46.5 for DINOv2 alone and 53.8 with SD, showing larger relative gains on symmetric categories (bus +21.0, car +15.7 PCK over DINOv2). On the unseen AwA-pose dataset, the method generalizes to 69.8 PCK / 60.6 KAP (Ours+SD), surpassing DINOv2+SD (68.1 / 56.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DINOv2 backbone alone, the method improves PCK by +7.7 (63.9 vs. 56.2) and KAP by +5.0 (51.5 vs. 46.5), with the largest gains on symmetric, repeated-part categories: bus (+21.0) and car (+15.7). Compared to DINOv2+SD (63.3 PCK), the method is marginally ahead on PCK but clearly ahead on KAP (51.5 vs. 49.6), indicating better handling of spurious symmetry matches. Against the SD-only baseline (56.1 PCK / 45.4 KAP), the method wins by +7.8 PCK and +6.1 KAP. The method also surpasses all fully supervised baselines (CATS 43.0, SCorrSan 54.4) by a wide margin. Performance degrades on highly deformable categories (person: −4.3 vs. DINOv2) and high-genus objects where a sphere is a poor prior (bicycle: −0.8), confirming the geometric assumption's limits. On the unseen AwA-pose dataset, the method generalizes well (69.8 PCK / 60.6 KAP with SD), outperforming DINOv2+SD (68.1 / 56.8) despite being trained only on SPair-71k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of SSL-based semantic correspondence (DINOv1/v2, ASIC, Neural Congealing) by replacing the flat 2D atlas alignment with a spherical surface S², drawing on the earlier spherical mapping idea from Thewlis et al. (2017). The key conceptual departure is that instead of requiring dense 3D mesh supervision or precise camera poses (as in render-and-compare pipelines like Kulkarni et al. or Zhou et al.), the method uses only coarse relative viewpoint bins to enforce geometric consistency, making the 3D prior dramatically weaker but far more scalable. At inference, the spherical coordinates are combined with the frozen SSL features via a weighted cosine distance, positioning the sphere as a symmetry- and repetition-breaking mechanism rather than a standalone descriptor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proposed KAP metric exposes a blind spot in the standard PCK evaluation: symmetric and repeated-part failures go largely unpenalized because the corresponding keypoints are often self-occluded in the target view, meaning the field may have been overestimating the robustness of SSL-based correspondence on objects like cars and buses. The method's practical appeal lies in its minimal overhead (a few transformer layers on top of DINOv2, ~3.3 pairs/s vs. 0.35 for diffusion-based baselines) while achieving competitive or superior accuracy. Open questions include whether the spherical prior can be generalized to non-spherical topologies (humans, bicycles, high-genus objects where it underperforms), whether viewpoint supervision can be eliminated entirely, and whether the KAP metric will be adopted as a standard alongside PCK.
