## Problem

State-of-the-art Referring Expression Comprehension methods perform dense perception over the entire image, processing all visual patches regardless of their relevance to the language query, which introduces substantial redundant computation—especially costly in Transformer-based models where self-attention scales quadratically with token count. Existing efficient Transformer approaches (e.g., token merging, coarse-to-fine ViTs) target fundamental visual tasks like classification and rely on heuristics such as [CLS] attention, making them difficult to extend to vision-language grounding tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image resized to 640×640 and a referring expression truncated or padded to 40 tokens as input, and directly outputs normalized bounding box coordinates [cx, cy, w, h] without any post-processing. The image is decomposed into a 3-level scale pyramid, and at each level the model processes only a subset of patches (e.g., ~270 out of 400 on average in RefCOCOg), with the [REG] token at each scale producing a progressively refined box prediction. The entire pipeline uses a single unified Transformer (initialized from ViLT weights) rather than separate visual and linguistic encoders.

## Method

ScanFormer performs coarse-to-fine iterative perception over a 3-scale image pyramid, where at each level a selection mechanism (trained via improved semantic hashing with Gumbel-style sampling) predicts which finer-grained patches are linguistically relevant and discards the rest. Discarded patches are replaced by a learnable constant token rather than zeroed out, and during inference these constant tokens are merged into a single key-value pair via a log-weighted concatenation trick, provably preserving attention outputs while reducing computation. A position-scale embedding encodes each patch's normalized coordinates and scale level, and a causal cache propagates key-value features across iterations so that new tokens attend to all previously cached multi-scale features. A [REG] token at each scale feeds a shared MLP to regress the bounding box, and the total loss combines multi-scale L1 and GIoU regression losses with a sparsity regularization term that controls the fraction of selected tokens per scale.

## Results

On RefCOCO, ScanFormer achieves 83.40/85.86/78.81 Acc@0.5 on val/testA/testB, outperforming most transformer-based baselines (e.g., SeqTR: 81.23/85.00/76.08; Word2Pix: 81.20/84.39/78.12) and one-stage methods (PLV-FPN: 81.93/84.99/76.25). On RefCOCOg, ScanFormer reaches 74.10/74.14 (val/test), surpassing QRNet (71.89/73.03), LADS (71.56/71.66), and SeqTR (71.35/71.58). With large-scale pre-training, ScanFormer achieves 82.75 on RefCOCOg test, exceeding MDETR (80.89) and approaching OFA (82.31). Inference speed reaches 34.9 FPS on a 1080 Ti, more than 2× faster than QRNet at comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScanFormer outperforms all transformer-based baselines on RefCOCOg (74.10 val / 74.14 test vs. QRNet 71.89/73.03, LADS 71.56/71.66, SeqTR 71.35/71.58, Word2Pix 70.81/71.34) and on RefCOCO+ val (72.96 vs. QRNet 72.94, LADS 71.16, SeqTR 68.82). Against QRNet, the closest comparable method, ScanFormer wins on RefCOCO+ testA (77.57 vs. 76.17) and RefCOCOg (74.14 vs. 73.03) but trails on RefCOCO testB (78.81 vs. 82.34) and ReferItGame (68.85 vs. 74.61). M-DGT remains the strongest on RefCOCO testB (87.11) and RefCOCOg (79.06), but does not report ReferItGame results. With large-scale pre-training (Table 2), ScanFormer surpasses MDETR on RefCOCOg test (82.75 vs. 80.89) and RefCOCO testA/testB (89.99/82.89 vs. 89.58/81.41), while OFA retains a lead on RefCOCO+ (87.15/74.29 vs. 84.04/70.63). In terms of efficiency, ScanFormer achieves 34.9 FPS on a 1080 Ti—more than 2× faster than QRNet—while maintaining competitive accuracy, demonstrating the benefit of its coarse-to-fine token pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScanFormer extends the line of efficient Vision Transformer research (bottom-up token merging, top-down coarse-to-fine methods like DVT and CF-ViT) into the vision-language grounding domain, where prior efficiency work has been largely absent. It departs from the dense-perception paradigm of transformer-based REC methods (TransVG, QRNet, SeqTR) that process all image patches uniformly, instead adopting a coarse-to-fine iterative perception over an image scale pyramid. The patch selection mechanism borrows the improved semantic hashing formulation from Kaiser & Bengio [19], and the unified transformer architecture is initialized from ViLT pre-training weights, connecting to the trend of eliminating separate visual and linguistic encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constant-token replacement and merging strategy offers a practical, provably exact inference acceleration that could be adopted in other token-based vision-language models beyond REC. The paper demonstrates that a single unified transformer can match dedicated visual-encoder-based methods (e.g., QRNet with Swin backbone) at roughly 2× inference speed, suggesting architectural simplification is viable for grounding tasks. However, two limitations remain open: the absence of a reliable early-exit criterion across pyramid scales, and the restriction to single-object prediction, which blocks extension to phrase grounding or multi-object referring. Whether the coarse-to-fine token selection generalizes to higher-resolution inputs or more complex multi-modal tasks remains unexplored.
