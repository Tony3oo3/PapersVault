## Problem

Neural video codecs (NVCs) based on conditional coding have surpassed traditional codecs in compression ratio, but two critical issues block their practical deployment: (1) the supported quality range in a single model is too narrow (only ~3.8 dB PSNR for the previous SOTA DCVC-DC), making rate control infeasible, and (2) severe quality degradation occurs under the standard intra-period = -1 setting (single intra-frame for the entire video), where error accumulation along long prediction chains is uncontrolled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242694/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec takes a sequence of video frames in either RGB or YUV420 colorspace (tested at 1080p) along with a per-frame integer quantization parameter q_t ∈ [0, 63] that controls the quality level. It outputs a compressed bitstream (motion vectors + entropy-coded latent features) and reconstructed frames. A single trained model handles both colorspaces without fine-tuning, and the per-frame q_t enables fluctuated target-bitrate rate control by simply adjusting the quantization scaler.

## Method

Built on the DCVC-DC conditional coding framework (optical flow → temporal context extraction → frame coding), DCVC-FM introduces two feature modulation mechanisms. First, a learnable global quantization scaler s_t^enc is generated from q_t via exponential interpolation and multiplies the latent feature during encoding, while the entropy model learns a spatial-channel-wise scaler w_t^enc for content-adaptive modulation; a uniform q_t sampling strategy during training harmonizes the encoding-quantization loop and expands the quality range from 3.8 dB to 11.4 dB. Second, a periodic refresh mechanism (every 32 frames) replaces the propagated feature F_{t-1} with a fresh context extracted from the reconstructed pixel frame x̂_{t-1} via a separate feature extractor, forcing the model to discard accumulated errors and uncorrelated information. Additional improvements include depthwise separable convolutions and smaller kernels for 16% MAC reduction, joint RGB+YUV training with a weighted distortion loss, and a re-implemented grid_sample enabling 16-bit inference with only 0.9% BD-Rate increase.

## Results

Under the most practical setting (all frames, intra-period = -1, YUV420), DCVC-FM achieves an average BD-Rate of −25.5% over VTM-17.0, outperforming ECM-11.0 (−20.5%) and ECM-5.0 (−16.0%). Compared to the previous SOTA NVC DCVC-DC, DCVC-FM delivers a 29.7% bitrate saving while reducing MACs by 16% (2225G vs. 2642G). The supported quality range expands from 3.8 dB (DCVC-DC) to 11.4 dB (DCVC-FM) on average across all test datasets, enabling practical rate control in a single model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCVC-FM decisively outperforms all traditional codecs under the intra-period = −1 setting: it saves 25.5% bitrate over VTM-17.0, 42.4% over HM-16.25, and 5.0% over the latest ECM-11.0. Against the previous SOTA NVC DCVC-DC, the gap is dramatic — DCVC-DC actually incurs a 12.8% bitrate *increase* over VTM when tested on all frames (with a catastrophic 84.4% increase on HEVC E), while DCVC-FM maintains a 25.5% saving, yielding a net 29.7% improvement. Under the less challenging 96-frame / intra-period 32 setting (Tables 1–2), DCVC-FM still leads: −20.3% average BD-Rate in RGB and −24.1% in YUV420, both surpassing DCVC-DC (−16.1% and −17.0% respectively). Among other NVC baselines (CANF-VC, DCVC, DCVC-TCM, DCVC-HEM), all are substantially outperformed, with DCVC-HEM being the closest at +10.0% average BD-Rate in RGB. The 16% MAC reduction (2225G vs. 2642G) means DCVC-FM is also more computationally efficient than DCVC-DC, and 16-bit inference further halves memory usage with only 0.9% BD-Rate penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCVC-FM is a direct successor to DCVC-DC (CVPR 2023), the previous SOTA neural video codec from the same group, and inherits its conditional coding framework where temporal features serve as learned contexts rather than pixel-domain predictions. The key departure is the introduction of 'feature modulation' in two forms: a learnable quantization scaler that modulates latent features to support a wide quality range (3.8 dB → 11.4 dB), and a periodic refresh mechanism that re-extracts temporal context from the reconstructed frame to combat error accumulation over long prediction chains. The paper also extends practicality by training a single model for both RGB and YUV420 colorspaces and enabling 16-bit inference, moving beyond the RGB-only, fp32-only regime of prior NVCs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is a landmark step toward making NVC practically deployable: it is the first neural codec to handle the standard intra-period = −1 setting (single intra-frame for the entire video) without catastrophic quality degradation, and it supports a quality range comparable to VTM's QP 25–49, enabling real rate control. The 29.7% BD-Rate saving over DCVC-DC under this setting, combined with 16% MAC reduction and 16-bit inference halving memory, makes it a strong candidate for future standardization efforts. However, the authors acknowledge that speed remains far from real-time and that entropy coding has cross-platform portability issues, leaving the path to production deployment still open.
