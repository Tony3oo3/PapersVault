## Problem

Existing NeRF-based stylization methods only modify the color/radiance while leaving the density (and thus geometry) unchanged, producing stylizations that lack meaningful geometric transformation. Meanwhile, 2D image-to-painting methods using vectorized strokes cannot guarantee multi-view consistency. The field of automated stroke-based 3D art generation that mimics the progressive, brushstroke-by-brushstroke process of human painting remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240706/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view 2D RGB images with known camera poses (dozens to hundreds of views from Blender synthetic scenes or LLFF face-forwarding scenes) and produces a vectorized 3D stroke representation consisting of N parameterized strokes, each defined by shape (SDF parameters for primitives or spline control points), RGB color, and density. Because the representation is vector-based, it can be rendered at any desired resolution and from any novel camera pose, and the separate shape/color/density parameters enable downstream tasks like color transfer (fixing geometry, tuning color) or text-driven generation via CLIP.

## Method

The scene is represented as a set of N vectorized 3D strokes whose shapes are defined by Signed Distance Fields, either from basic geometric primitives (spheres, cubes, tetrahedra, capsules, etc.) transformed by translation/rotation/scale, or from parametric spline curves (quadratic/cubic Bézier, Catmull-Rom) with variable radius. A key differentiability challenge is converting the discrete SDF indicator into a smooth density field; the paper uses a Laplace CDF approximation with an adaptive width δ that scales with the cone-tracing pixel footprint (analogous to MipNeRF) to ensure meaningful gradients at all depths. Strokes are composed via alpha overlay (or optionally softmax/max blending) and rendered with standard NeRF volume rendering. To address vanishing gradients far from stroke boundaries, the method trains an auxiliary error field that guides the incremental initialization of new strokes at high-error locations, and reinitializes strokes that collapse to near-zero density. The entire stroke parameter set is optimized end-to-end via gradient descent on a Charbonnier color loss plus mask and density regularization losses.

## Results

The paper primarily reports qualitative results for its 3D stroke-based scene stylization. The full method (with adaptive δ and error field) achieves PSNR 23.13, SSIM 0.825, and LPIPS 0.171 on novel view synthesis, as shown in the ablation table. Experiments use 500 strokes on synthetic Blender scenes and 1000 strokes on LLFF face-forwarding scenes. The comparison against 2D painting baselines (DiffVG, Stylized Neural Painting) is purely visual, with no quantitative metrics reported for those comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two 2D vectorized image-to-painting methods: DiffVG [Li et al., ACM TOG 2020] and Stylized Neural Painting (SNP) [Zou et al., CVPR 2021]. The comparison in Fig. 7 is entirely qualitative — no quantitative metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) are reported for these baselines. The authors state that all three vectorized methods produce recognizable stroke-style reconstructions, but their 3D stroke method recovers more fine-grained details and, critically, maintains strict multi-view consistency across different camera viewpoints, a property inherently guaranteed by the 3D representation that 2D methods cannot provide. The 2D baselines are applied independently to each training view, so their outputs can be inconsistent when viewed from novel angles. No head-to-head numerical comparison is given, making it difficult to quantify the magnitude of the advantage beyond visual inspection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the NeRF volume rendering framework (density + color fields rendered via ray integration) but replaces the MLP-based implicit representation with a collection of parameterized 3D strokes defined by SDFs of geometric primitives and spline curves. It borrows the Laplace CDF smoothing from VolSDF to make SDF-based region functions differentiable, and adopts Mip-NeRF's cone-tracing strategy to adaptively control boundary smoothness. Conceptually, it bridges the 2D image-painting literature (Haeberli, Hertzmann) with 3D scene reconstruction, positioning itself as the first automated stroke-based 3D scene stylization method, in contrast to NeRF-based stylization approaches (ARF, StylizedNeRF, StyleRF) that only manipulate color at the voxel level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is demonstrating that a vectorized stroke representation can achieve both geometric and appearance stylization simultaneously—something NeRF-based color-only stylization fundamentally cannot do—while maintaining multi-view consistency. The vector-based nature also enables resolution-independent rendering and natural extensions to color transfer and CLIP-driven text-to-3D generation. However, the method requires manual stroke design, struggles with complex scenes due to local minima (needing thousands of strokes), and the optimization is sensitive to initialization. Open questions include whether generative models can automatically produce diverse stroke shapes (ink, oil brush) and whether globally-aware losses like optimal transport in 3D can reduce the stroke count needed for convergence.
