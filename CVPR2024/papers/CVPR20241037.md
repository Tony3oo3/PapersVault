## Problem

SAM is pre-trained at a fixed 1024×1024 resolution, yet downstream segmentation datasets often have substantially larger image sizes, causing severe performance degradation at inference. Existing remedies—resizing all images to a fixed size or adjusting patch sizes—require full retraining and discard the rich prior knowledge embedded in SAM's pre-trained weights. The paper reframes this resolution-variation challenge as a token-sequence length extrapolation problem, where patch size stays constant but the number of tokens grows with image resolution.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images at arbitrary resolutions (evaluated at 1024, 2048, and 4096) together with a visual prompt (a random point sampled from ground truth during fine-tuning, or a box from Deformable-DETR for COCO instance segmentation). It outputs a binary segmentation mask per prompt. Crucially, the patch size remains fixed across all resolutions; only the token sequence length N changes, which is the variable the method is designed to handle. The approach operates in two modes: zero-shot (no additional training, β fixed) and lightweight fine-tuning (β becomes trainable, only ~0.01M extra parameters).

## Method

BA-SAM introduces two lightweight modifications to the attention layers of SAM's ViT encoder, requiring no structural changes. First, a New Scaling Factor (NSF) replaces the standard 1/√d_k with λ = (log_{N_train} N_test)/√d_k, derived by enforcing that the information entropy of the attention distribution remains invariant to sequence length N; this keeps the dot-product magnitude—and hence the Softmax gradient regime—consistent when N grows beyond the training length. Second, a Bias-Mode Attention Mask (BM-AM) adds a distance-proportional bias b_{i,j} = β|i−j| to the query-key dot product before Softmax, progressively penalizing attention to distant tokens so that each token relies primarily on its trained neighborhood rather than untrained far-position encodings. In zero-shot mode β is a fixed hyperparameter (default 1); during fine-tuning β becomes a single trainable scalar per head. Both components are embedded directly into the existing attention computation, adding negligible parameters (~0.01M) and inference overhead (~0.3 ms), and are compatible with both SAM and MobileSAM backbones.

## Results

On the four segmentation benchmarks (ISIC, DUTS, DIS-TE4, COD10K), BA-SAM with fine-tuning achieves MAE of 0.053, 0.031, 0.055, and 0.029 respectively, outperforming all task-specific SOTA methods except MSA on ISIC (0.049). As a single generalized model fine-tuned jointly on all four datasets, BA-SAM reaches MAE of 0.054, 0.030, 0.054, and 0.054, dramatically surpassing the unmodified SAM baseline (0.419, 0.298, 0.373, 0.217). In the zero-shot setting on COCO instance segmentation, BA-SAM improves AP from 42.5 to 43.0 (SAM backbone) and from 40.8 to 41.2 (MobileSAM backbone). The method also reduces performance degradation under resolution scaling: at 4096×4096, MobileSAM degrades by 23.0% on ISIC while BA-SAM degrades by only 20.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BA-SAM (specialized, fine-tuned per dataset) outperforms all task-specific SOTA baselines on three of four datasets: it beats VST and ICONet on DUTS (0.031 vs 0.037), Gate and IS-Net on DIS-TE4 (0.055 vs 0.109/0.072), and SINet and SegMaR on COD10K (0.029 vs 0.092/0.034). On ISIC, MSA achieves a slightly lower MAE (0.049) than BA-SAM (0.053), though BA-SAM still substantially outperforms CASS (0.086) and DINO (0.081). Against the SAM and MobileSAM baselines, BA-SAM consistently reduces MAE across all four datasets in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings, with the largest gains appearing at higher resolutions (e.g., 4096×4096 on DUTS: 0.283 vs 0.328 MAE). As a single generalized model, BA-SAM achieves near-specialized performance (within 0.001–0.025 MAE of its per-dataset variants) while SAM degrades by 0.2–0.3 MAE, demonstrating that the method's length-extrapolation design transfers across tasks without task-specific architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Segment Anything Model (SAM) by reframing the resolution-variation problem as a length extrapolation problem, drawing a direct conceptual bridge from NLP's ALIBI (Press et al., ICLR 2021) to the vision domain. Where prior SAM-based approaches (MSA, SAM-Adapter) resize images or FlexiViT adjusts patch sizes—both requiring costly retraining—BA-SAM keeps the patch size fixed and instead modifies the attention mechanism with a theoretically derived scaling factor (extending Vaswani et al.'s 1/√d_k) and a linear distance-based bias mask. The method is positioned as a parameter-efficient, structure-preserving intervention on the SAM/MobileSAM encoder, requiring no architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant: SAM is widely deployed, and resolution mismatch between training (1024) and inference is a common deployment bottleneck that prior work addressed only through expensive retraining. BA-SAM's negligible overhead (0.01M extra params, ~0.3 ms) makes it immediately adoptable in production pipelines. Open questions include whether the entropy-derived scaling factor remains well-calibrated at extreme length ratios (e.g., 8× or 16× training length), whether the fixed β in zero-shot mode is truly architecture-agnostic, and whether the approach transfers to non-SAM ViT backbones or video segmentation settings.
