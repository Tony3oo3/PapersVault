## Problem

Prior image geolocalization models, despite leveraging vision transformers and contrastive pretraining, remain heavily dependent on distributional alignment between training and test data and fail to generalize to unseen places. The most capable systems are constrained to narrow landmark distributions, leaving planet-scale geolocalization of arbitrary images from anywhere in the world unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240860/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PIGEON consumes a four-image 360° Street View panorama (four non-overlapping patches separated by 90° compass directions) while PIGEOTTO takes a single RGB image; both are processed by a CLIP ViT-L/14 336 backbone. The system outputs geographic coordinates (latitude, longitude) through a two-stage pipeline: first a geocell classification over topK candidate cells, then intra-geocell refinement via location cluster retrieval that narrows the prediction to a single training point. The refinement stage computes L2 distances between the input image embedding and cluster centroids across multiple geocells, weighted by a calibrated temperature softmax.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-task geolocalization system built on CLIP with three key innovations: (1) semantic geocells created by merging GADM administrative boundaries and subdividing via OPTICS clustering + Voronoi tessellation, making geocell boundaries semantically meaningful at any dataset scale; (2) a haversine-smoothing loss that replaces one-hot labels with continuous targets decaying by great-circle distance to the true location, implicitly sharing parameters across geographic hierarchy levels; and (3) multi-task contrastive pretraining of CLIP using synthetic captions encoding location, climate, compass direction, season, and traffic direction, plus explicit auxiliary heads for Köppen-Geiger climate, month, and six regression targets (temperature, precipitation, elevation, population density). At inference, a hierarchical retrieval mechanism over OPTICS-clustered location representations refines predictions within and across the topK predicted geocells, analogous to prototypical networks with fixed parameters.

## Results

PIGEOTTO achieves state-of-the-art results on all five benchmark datasets, with the largest gains on GWS15k (predominantly unseen locations): +38.8 pp on country-level accuracy (65.7% vs. 26.9%) and +22.5 pp on region-level (31.2% vs. 8.7%) over the previous SOTA GeoDecoder. On YFCC26k, PIGEOTTO improves country-level accuracy by 13.6 pp (63.2% vs. 49.6%) and more than halves the median error (333.3 km vs. ~750 km). PIGEON, trained on Street View panoramas, places 40.4% of predictions within 25 km and achieves 92.0% country accuracy on a 5,000-location holdout, ranking in the top 0.01% of GeoGuessr players and defeating a world-class professional in 6/6 matches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIGEOTTO outperforms all five baselines (PlaNet, CPlaNet, ISNs, Translocator, GeoDecoder) on the majority of distance-based metrics across all five benchmarks. Against the strongest prior method, GeoDecoder [7], PIGEOTTO wins on 9 of 10 metrics on YFCC4k and YFCC26k combined, with country-level gains of +12.2 pp and +13.6 pp respectively. The most dramatic margin appears on GWS15k, where 92% of test locations are unseen in training data: PIGEOTTO gains +38.8 pp on country accuracy and +34.6 pp on continent accuracy over GeoDecoder, while the gap shrinks to near-zero at street level (0.7% vs. 0.7%), confirming the advantage comes from generalization rather than memorization. On IM2GPS (a small, landmark-only set of 237 images), PIGEOTTO actually underperforms GeoDecoder on street, city, and region metrics (−7.3, −9.3, −5.7 pp) but still leads on country and continent, suggesting the model's strength is in broader geographic reasoning rather than fine-grained landmark matching. Translocator [27] and ISNs [25] are consistently outperformed by 5–15 pp on country-level accuracy across all datasets, while PlaNet [38] and CPlaNet [30] trail by even wider margins (20–40 pp on country level), reflecting the generational gap between CNN-era and transformer-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic geocell design of Theiner et al. [33] by combining administrative boundary hierarchies with OPTICS clustering and Voronoi tessellation, addressing the critical limitation that purely semantic geocells become unbalanced on large datasets. It builds directly on Haas et al. [13]'s synthetic-caption contrastive pretraining of CLIP for geolocalization, extending it to a multi-task setting with geographic, climate, and demographic auxiliary data. The haversine-smoothing loss and location-cluster retrieval mechanism depart from prior hierarchical geolocalization approaches (ISNs, Translocator, GeoDecoder) that treat geocells in isolation, instead explicitly encoding spatial adjacency into the training signal and adding a non-parametric prototypical-network-style refinement stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIGEOTTO's 38.8-percentage-point improvement on GWS15k country-level accuracy—on a dataset where 92% of locations are unseen in training—demonstrates the first effective generalization to unseen places in image geolocalization, closing a gap that has persisted since the field's inception. The finding that haversine smoothing is the single most impactful component (removing it drops country accuracy from 92% to 72%) suggests that encoding spatial continuity into the loss is more critical than architectural complexity. Open questions include whether street-level accuracy can be meaningfully improved, how these systems behave under adversarial or privacy-sensitive deployment, and whether the approach transfers to cross-view (satellite-to-ground) geolocalization settings.
