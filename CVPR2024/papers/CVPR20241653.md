## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP are trained with image-level supervision, creating a fundamental gap when applied to pixel-level segmentation. Existing methods either freeze CLIP encoders to preserve the joint embedding space or fine-tune them but suffer severe overfitting to seen classes, misaligning the space for unseen classes. This paper identifies that aggregating the cosine-similarity cost volume (rather than raw features) provides a robust basis for fine-tuning CLIP without degrading generalization to unseen classes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a variable set of free-form text class descriptions (N_C can vary at inference), producing a per-pixel class label map. Dense image embeddings are extracted by modifying the last attention layer of CLIP's ViT image encoder to eliminate the pooling effect, yielding a (H×W)×d feature map. The cost volume is computed as cosine similarity between each spatial image embedding and each text embedding, forming an (H×W)×N_C tensor that is then refined through the aggregation pipeline.

## Method

CAT-Seg computes a cost volume via cosine similarity between dense CLIP image embeddings and text embeddings, then refines it through alternating spatial and class aggregation. Spatial aggregation applies a pair of Swin Transformer blocks (local + shifted window self-attention) independently per class to enforce spatial smoothness, while class aggregation uses a linear Transformer without position embeddings to capture inter-class relationships and guarantee permutation invariance. CLIP embeddings are injected as query/key guidance into both aggregation stages to provide spatial and semantic context. An FPN-style upsampling decoder concatenates the aggregated cost with intermediate CLIP ViT feature maps (extracted via a single learnable transposed convolution) to produce high-resolution predictions. For efficient fine-tuning, only the query and value projection matrices of both CLIP encoders are updated (70.3M parameters), which the paper shows outperforms full fine-tuning while preserving the embedding space for unseen classes.

## Results

CAT-Seg achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all standard open-vocabulary segmentation benchmarks. With CLIP ViT-L/14, it reaches 16.0 mIoU on A-847 and 23.8 mIoU on PC-459, representing +3.6 and +8.1 mIoU improvements (29% and 52% relative gains) over the previous best (SAN). On the MESS multi-domain benchmark, CAT-Seg attains a mean score of 34.70 (ViT-L/14), outperforming SAN (30.06) and OVSeg (26.94). The method is also over 3.7× faster at inference than region-text baselines, with only 2,121 GFLOPs versus ~19,000–22,000 for competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAT-Seg outperforms all 12 compared baselines on every standard benchmark. Against the strongest prior method SAN (ViT-L/14), CAT-Seg gains +3.6 mIoU on A-847 (16.0 vs 12.4), +8.1 on PC-459 (23.8 vs 15.7), +5.8 on A-150, and +5.6 on PC-59. Compared to ODISE, which relies on Stable Diffusion for mask generation, CAT-Seg leads by +4.9 on A-847 and +9.3 on PC-459 while being 3.7× faster at inference. On the MESS multi-domain benchmark, CAT-Seg (ViT-L/14) achieves a mean of 34.70, beating SAN (30.06) by +4.64 and OVSeg (26.94) by +7.76, with particularly large gains in General (+8.51) and Agriculture/Biology (+9.97) domains. The only domain where CAT-Seg underperforms is Medical Sciences (24.70 vs SAN's 30.27), which the authors attribute to limited CLIP knowledge in that domain. Notably, CAT-Seg achieves these gains without any additional backbone or mask generator, using only 70.3M learnable parameters versus 103–409M for competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes cost aggregation—a technique well-established in stereo matching and visual correspondence (Kendall et al., 2017)—for the fundamentally different problem of aggregating an image-to-text cost volume derived from CLIP embeddings. It departs from the dominant paradigm in open-vocabulary segmentation, where prior methods (ZegFormer, ZSseg, SAN, OVSeg) either rely on separate region proposal generators or add adapter/decoder layers to CLIP while freezing its encoders due to overfitting. The key conceptual shift is that by operating on cosine similarity scores rather than raw feature embeddings, the aggregation layers become robust to overfitting, thereby enabling end-to-end fine-tuning of CLIP encoders for the first time in this setting. The spatial aggregation leverages Swin Transformer for local-global receptive fields, while class aggregation uses a linear transformer to handle variable numbers of classes with permutation invariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cost aggregation (operating on similarity scores) enables effective fine-tuning of CLIP without the severe overfitting to seen classes that plagued prior attempts challenges a widely held assumption in the field and opens a path for adapting vision-language models to dense prediction tasks more generally. The 3.7× inference speedup over region-text methods and the strong multi-domain generalization (MESS benchmark) make the approach practically attractive for deployment. However, the paper acknowledges that CLIP's knowledge gaps in domains like medical sciences and engineering limit performance there, and whether the cost aggregation principle transfers to other foundation models or dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth estimation, panoptic segmentation) remains an open question.
