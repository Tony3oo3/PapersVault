## Problem

Embodied agents for robotic manipulation typically depend on large amounts of task-specific training data to develop their capabilities, whereas humans solve new problems by recalling analogous past experiences. Existing foundation models for robotics store knowledge implicitly in network parameters, making them data-hungry and poorly scalable compared to LLMs. The paper asks how to equip robots with an external, multi-embodiment policy memory that can be queried at inference time to augment their behavior with limited demonstrations.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a query composed of an instruction segment (text or audio) and an observation segment (RGB image, video clip, or point cloud), which are jointly encoded via ImageBind into a unified latent embedding for retrieval. The output is a sequence of continuous robot actions; retrieved policies additionally carry action and proprioceptive-state tokens (capped at 9 dimensions each) that are fed into the generator as contextual examples. At the start of a task frame the full instruction-observation pair is used for retrieval, while subsequent frames rely on observation alone until the next user interaction.

## Method

RAEA comprises two modules: a policy retriever and a policy generator. The retriever is a bi-encoder that uses ImageBind to map all modalities (text, audio, image, video, point cloud) into a shared embedding space, then performs Maximum Inner Product Search over an external policy memory bank built from Open X-Embodiment; diversity is enforced by skipping candidates whose relevance score exceeds 0.9 with already-selected items and by randomly dropping ~70% of query tokens during training. The generator is a Transformer that tokenizes each retrieved policy (instruction, observation, action, proprioception) and concatenates them in relevance-score order, then injects this context into the main policy network via a cross-attention mechanism where retrieved-policy features supply the query and key projections while the current input supplies the value projection. Training uses behavior cloning with mean-squared-error loss, and the feature representations from the retrieval stage are reused in the generator to avoid redundant encoding.

## Results

On Maniskill-2 (six rigid/soft-body tasks), RAEA achieves an average success rate of 60.6% with image-only observations and 63.6% with point-cloud+image observations, outperforming ResNet152 (49.3%/54.5%) and Swin-Base (50.0%/52.3%) across all six tasks. The largest gains appear on PickCube (+16.4 pp over ResNet152, image-only) and Fill (+17.3 pp over ResNet152, image-only). On Franka Kitchen and MetaWorld, RAEA trained with only 10 or 25 demonstrations surpasses R3M, BLIP-2, and Embodied-GPT, with the advantage being most pronounced in the 10-demonstration regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAEA is compared against five baselines across three benchmarks. On Maniskill-2, RAEA wins on all six tasks against both ResNet152 and Swin-Base under image-only and point-cloud+image settings; the margin is largest on PickCube (56.7% vs. 40.1%/41.3%) and Fill (63.8% vs. 52.4%/49.0%) in the image-only condition. On Franka Kitchen and MetaWorld, RAEA outperforms R3M, BLIP-2, and Embodied-GPT in both the 10-demonstration and 25-demonstration settings, with the gap widening in the low-data (10-demo) regime, indicating that retrieval augmentation is most beneficial when training data is scarce. The paper does not report any setting where a baseline matches or exceeds RAEA.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAEA transplants the retrieval-augmented generation paradigm from NLP and vision (DPR, Re-Imagen, MuRAG, RA-CLIP) into the robotics domain, where the 'retrieved documents' become analogous manipulation policies from a cross-embodiment memory bank (Open X-Embodiment). For the embodied-agent side, it positions itself as a complementary module to existing foundation models like R3M, BLIP-2, and Embodied-GPT rather than a replacement, using a bi-encoder retriever (built on ImageBind for unified multi-modal encoding) and a Transformer-based generator with cross-attention to inject retrieved policies as in-context examples. The key conceptual departure from prior retrieval-augmented work is that the retrieved items are executable action sequences with proprioceptive states, not static text or image passages, and the generator must fuse them into a continuous control signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to reducing the data burden in robotic manipulation by leveraging the growing pool of open-source, multi-embodiment datasets as an external memory, which is timely given the community's push toward scalable robot learning. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively simple manipulation tasks (pick-and-place, stacking, drawer opening) with modest success-rate gains (~10–15 points over baselines), and the memory bank is static with no mechanism for online updating or forgetting. Open questions include whether retrieval latency is acceptable for real-time control, how the approach scales to longer-horizon or dexterous tasks, and whether the cross-embodiment transfer benefit persists as the target robot's own data grows.
