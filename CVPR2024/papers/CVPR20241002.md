## Problem

Existing personalization methods for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) overfit the entire subject from few reference images, coupling all visual attributes together and making it impossible to selectively extract a single attribute such as color, pattern, or structure. The paper introduces user-specified visual appearance personalization (U-VAP), where a user provides a text query specifying which attribute to preserve, enabling fine-grained, textually controllable attribute disentanglement that prior methods lack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241002/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of reference images (typically 3–5), a free-text attribute query (e.g., 'the color of the object'), and a new-concept prompt at inference. The text query is fed to GPT-3.5-turbo to auto-generate ~200 candidate prompts, from which 2–6 images per attribute set are hand-selected after CLIP-similarity filtering. Output is a standard Stable Diffusion v1.5 image (512×512) carrying the target attribute on the new concept, generated in ~5 s with DDIM (T=50, guidance scale 7.5).

## Method

U-VAP builds on DreamBooth pre-training to obtain an initial concept-aware model G₀, then introduces a decoupled self-augmentation strategy: an LLM generates two prompt sets—target prompts that preserve the user-specified attribute while altering non-target attributes, and non-target prompts that do the reverse. G₀ renders candidate images for both sets, which are filtered by CLIP image similarity to the references and curated into target (D₊) and non-target (D₋) training sets. Two new identifiers, tgt and ngt, are jointly optimized on D₊ and D₋ respectively, binding each to its corresponding attribute direction. At inference, a semantic adjustment shifts the tgt embedding away from ngt along the vector (v_tgt − v_ntg) scaled by λ (default 0.3), further suppressing residual non-target attribute leakage in the generated image.

## Results

Across three attribute categories (color, pattern, structure), U-VAP achieves the highest Inception Score for color (4.138) and pattern (3.434), and the highest CLIP-T (prompt fidelity) for structure (0.284). For color and pattern, DreamBooth edges out U-VAP on CLIP-T (0.303 vs 0.292; 0.299 vs 0.270), but the paper notes DB's higher CLIP-T reflects over-editing of non-target attributes rather than precise target-attribute control. U-VAP consistently outperforms TI and ProSpect on CLIP-T by 0.09–0.14 across all categories, while maintaining competitive CLIP-I (image fidelity) scores that indicate meaningful attribute transfer without excessive modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U-VAP outperforms TI by a large margin on CLIP-T across all three attribute categories (e.g., 0.292 vs 0.187 for color, 0.270 vs 0.152 for pattern), indicating TI fails to transfer the target attribute to new concepts while overfitting to the reference (highest CLIP-I). Against DreamBooth, U-VAP trades a small CLIP-T deficit on color/pattern (0.292 vs 0.303; 0.270 vs 0.299) for superior generation quality (IS 4.138 vs 3.890; 3.434 vs 3.793) and better structure-attribute control (CLIP-T 0.284 vs 0.265); the paper argues DB's higher CLIP-T on color/pattern reflects over-editing of non-target attributes rather than precise target control. ProSpect is consistently outperformed on CLIP-T (by 0.03–0.11) and IS, with particular difficulty decoupling attributes of similar frequency characteristics. In qualitative comparisons, GPT-4V struggles to maintain precise target attributes (e.g., producing cartoon style instead of realistic), NTI+P2P is constrained by the original image's structure and layout, and Concept Decomposition produces less accurate attribute-specific images than U-VAP. Overall, U-VAP wins on controllability and precision of target-attribute transfer, while DreamBooth remains competitive on raw prompt fidelity for simpler attributes like color.

## Relation to Prior Work

U-VAP builds directly on DreamBooth for initial concept-level personalization and Stable Diffusion as the underlying generative backbone, but departs from the whole-concept paradigm by introducing a user-specified attribute extraction setting. It contrasts with ProSpect and XTI, which achieve attribute-aware personalization through heuristic selection of embeddings in neural or timestep spaces, and with Concept Decomposition, which offers an uncontrollable decomposition. The key methodological departure is the decoupled self-augmentation strategy: using an LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo) to generate target and non-target prompt sets, then fine-tuning separate identifiers on LLM-augmented data, combined with a simple semantic-space shift at inference to push the target embedding away from non-target attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in personalization: existing methods overfit the entire reference subject and cannot selectively transfer a single visual attribute (color, pattern, structure) to novel concepts under user control. The plug-and-play design (compatible with both DreamBooth and Textual Inversion) makes the approach broadly applicable without requiring architectural changes. However, the method's effectiveness is bounded by the quality of the initial concept-aware model, and the reliance on LLM-generated prompts plus manual data curation introduces a non-trivial pipeline overhead. Open questions include whether the self-augmentation strategy generalizes to more complex multi-attribute compositions and whether the semantic adjustment can be learned rather than manually tuned via λ.
