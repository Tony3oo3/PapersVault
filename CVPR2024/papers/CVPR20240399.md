## Problem

Existing blind super-resolution methods rely on supervised pre-training—either on paired LR-HR image datasets or on labeled kernel datasets—to learn image or kernel priors, making them expensive, inflexible, and infeasible for scenarios like high-speed targets or medical imaging where ground truths are unavailable. Even kernel-prior-based approaches (e.g., FKP-DIP) still require supervised training on kernel datasets, limiting generalization across varying kernel categories. This paper asks whether a fully unsupervised, pre-training-free kernel prior can be learned on-the-fly during inference to achieve competitive BSR performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (no ground-truth HR image or kernel) and produces both a restored high-resolution image and an estimated blur kernel. In experiments, the scale factor is s=4 with kernel sizes of (4s+3)×(4s+3), covering anisotropic Gaussian kernels (width range [0.175s, 2.5s], rotation [0, π]) and random motion kernels. The approach is plug-and-play: for DIP-DKP, both the DIP network and DKP are trained from scratch during inference; for Diff-DKP, an off-the-shelf ImageNet-trained DDPM is used as a fixed image restorer while only the lightweight DKP kernel network (562K parameters) is optimized, requiring no retraining or fine-tuning of the diffusion model.

## Method

The DKP model comprises two modules operating in alternating iterations. The Random Kernel Sampling (RKS) module performs MCMC sampling over random kernel distributions: it draws L random kernels conditioned on latent variables (e.g., Gaussian variance or motion length), weights each by the inverse of its data-consistency loss (LR reconstruction error with the current HR estimate), and outputs a weighted average as the kernel prior. The Prior Kernel Estimation (PKE) module uses a lightweight 3-layer FCN to estimate the blur kernel, optimized via a network-based Langevin dynamics (NLD) update that combines a data-consistency gradient (from LR reconstruction error) with a kernel-prior gradient (from the RKS output), where the RKS-sampled kernel acts as a structured noise term to prevent convergence to poor local modes. This design eliminates the need for any labeled training data: the kernel prior is learned adaptively through the MCMC process during inference, and the kernel network is refined via NLD rather than supervised backpropagation. The DKP module can be plugged into existing IR frameworks—replacing the kernel estimator in DIP-based methods (DIP-DKP) or inserted as an external data-consistency refiner into the reverse diffusion process (Diff-DKP)—without modifying the underlying image restoration model.

## Results

On the Gaussian kernel scenario (s=4), Diff-DKP achieves the best PSNR/SSIM across all four datasets: 29.44/0.8592 on Set5, 26.76/0.7400 on Set14, 26.63/0.7057 on BSD100, and 23.92/0.6875 on Urban100. DIP-DKP achieves the second-best results, e.g., 28.03/0.8039 on Set5. On the motion kernel scenario, Diff-DKP's advantage widens substantially, reaching 28.74/0.8313 on Set5 and 26.03/0.6719 on Set14, while DIP-DKP scores 25.30/0.7417 and 24.52/0.6434 respectively. Kernel estimation PSNR on Set14 further confirms superiority: Diff-DKP reaches 56.76 (Gaussian) and 40.37 (motion), versus 54.46 and 37.52 for the next-best DIP-FKP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-DKP outperforms all seven baselines by a wide margin on both Gaussian and motion kernel scenarios. On Set5 (Gaussian), Diff-DKP achieves 29.44 PSNR, surpassing the next-best BSRDM (27.81) by 1.63 dB and DIP-FKP (27.77) by 1.67 dB. The advantage is even more pronounced on motion kernels: on Set5, Diff-DKP reaches 28.74 PSNR versus DCLS's 24.78 (a 3.96 dB gap) and DIP-FKP's 24.61 (4.13 dB gap). DIP-DKP, being fully unsupervised and trained from scratch, still edges out all supervised baselines (DASR, DIP-FKP, BSRDM, DCLS) by 0.2–0.5 dB on Gaussian kernels and 0.5–0.8 dB on motion kernels. DiffBIR, which requires fine-tuning on BSR-labeled data, underperforms both proposed methods by 2–4 dB, suggesting that supervised fine-tuning of diffusion models is less effective than the plug-and-play DKP data-consistency refinement. DARM and Double-DIP, which rely on internal GAN or untrained kernel networks, show the weakest performance, particularly on motion kernels where Double-DIP drops to 18.92 PSNR on Set5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the alternative-optimization framework for blind super-resolution established by Double-DIP and FKP-DIP, where kernel estimation and image restoration are solved iteratively. The key departure is replacing the supervised kernel prior (FKP's flow-based network pre-trained on labeled kernels) or the untrained kernel network (Double-DIP) with an unsupervised, pre-training-free kernel prior generated via MCMC sampling over random kernel distributions. The Langevin dynamics update for the kernel network repurposes the MCMC-sampled kernel as a stochastic perturbation, drawing on Bayesian optimization literature. The image restoration side leverages both untrained DIP and off-the-shelf ImageNet-trained DDPM, positioning DKP as a plug-in module rather than a standalone pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for paired LR-HR training data or pre-trained kernel networks, DKP addresses a practical bottleneck in BSR deployment for scenarios where labeled data is infeasible (satellite imagery, medical imaging). The plug-and-play design means the kernel estimation module can be swapped into existing restoration pipelines without retraining the image prior. However, the approach is validated primarily on synthetic Gaussian and motion kernels at a single scale factor (s=4), and the substantial performance gains of Diff-DKP are partly attributable to the strong ImageNet diffusion prior rather than the kernel estimation alone. Open questions include whether the MCMC kernel prior generalizes to more complex real-world degradations (e.g., mixed blur, sensor noise) and whether the sampling-based prior can be extended to other inverse problems beyond BSR.
