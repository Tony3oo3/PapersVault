## Problem

Existing data-free knowledge distillation (D-KD) methods rely on an adversarial inversion-and-distillation paradigm that requires generating a large number of synthetic samples (comparable to the original training set size) to achieve competitive student accuracy, making overall training prohibitively expensive. No prior work has jointly optimized both the data inversion and knowledge distillation stages to reduce the required synthetic data scale, leaving a critical gap in end-to-end training efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained teacher network (with no access to the original training data) and a randomly initialized student network as inputs, and produces a trained student network as output. Synthetic training samples are generated on-the-fly from latent noise vectors via a GAN generator, with the teacher network serving as the discriminator. For image classification (CIFAR-10/100, 32×32 RGB), the student outputs class labels; for semantic segmentation (NYUv2), it outputs pixel-wise masks. The key constraint is that only an extremely small set of synthetic samples (e.g., 5,000 for CIFAR-10, 10% of the original dataset) is used per training epoch, stored in a fixed-size dynamic replay buffer.

## Method

SSD-KD introduces two interdependent modules to enable efficient distillation from a small-scale synthetic dataset. First, a modulating function φ(x) applied to the task loss during data inversion combines a diversity-aware term (penalizing samples whose teacher-predicted category is over-represented in the replay buffer) and a difficulty-aware term (a focal-loss-inspired factor (1−p_T(x))^γ that upweights hard samples with low teacher confidence), jointly balancing category and difficulty distributions of synthetic samples. Second, a priority sampling function δ(x) = w(x)·KL(f_t‖f_s) adapted from prioritized experience replay selects which samples from the dynamic replay buffer to use for distillation, with importance-sampling weights correcting for the non-uniform sampling bias. The overall optimization is min_{f_s} max_g E[L_BN + L_KD + φ(x)·L_Task], where the generator is trained adversarially with the teacher as discriminator and the student is trained on a small prioritized mini-batch, achieving 1–2 orders of magnitude speedup over mainstream D-KD methods while retaining competitive accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-34→ResNet-18), SSD-KD achieves 94.26% top-1 accuracy in 4.20 hours, outperforming Fast10 (94.05%, 4.72h) and matching CMI (94.84%, 15.49h) while being ~3.7× faster. On the harder CIFAR-100 (ResNet-34→ResNet-18), SSD-KD reaches 75.16% in 4.22h, surpassing Fast10 (74.34%) and approaching CMI (77.04%, 15.42h). At the extreme small-scale setting (5000 synthetic samples, 10% of original data), SSD-KD is 1.9–3.9× faster than Fast2 while achieving higher accuracy on 4/5 CIFAR-10 pairs and all 5 CIFAR-100 pairs. On NYUv2 semantic segmentation, SSD-KD attains 0.384 mIoU with only 16K synthetic samples, outperforming Fast10 (0.366, 17K) and even the standard KD baseline trained on all 1,449 original images (0.380).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Fast2 (the prior most efficient D-KD method), SSD-KD achieves 1.9–3.9× training speedup while gaining +0.30% to +10.19% top-1 accuracy across all 10 teacher-student pairs on CIFAR-10/100. Against the accuracy-focused baselines CMI and DFQ, SSD-KD is 3–4× faster (e.g., 4.20h vs 15.49h for CMI on CIFAR-10 ResNet-34→18) at a small accuracy cost of ~0.6% on CIFAR-10 but actually surpasses them on CIFAR-100 (75.16% vs 77.04% for CMI, though CMI still leads by ~1.9%). Against the slower adversarial methods DeepInv, DAFL, and ZSKT, SSD-KD is 6–70× faster while matching or exceeding their accuracy (e.g., 94.26% vs 93.26% for DeepInv, 94.26% vs 92.22% for DAFL on CIFAR-10). On NYUv2 semantic segmentation, SSD-KD (0.384 mIoU, 16K synthetic samples) outperforms Fast10 (0.366, 17K), DFND (0.378, 14M ImageNet), DFAD (0.364, 960K), and DAFL (0.105, 960K), and even slightly beats the standard KD baseline trained on all 1,449 original NYUv2 images (0.380). Overall, SSD-KD is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier accuracy and the lowest training time across all evaluated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adversarial inversion-and-distillation paradigm for data-free knowledge distillation established by DeepInv [30] and refined by subsequent methods (CMI, DAFL, DFQ, ZSKT) that improve the data inversion process through contrastive learning, momentum, or augmentation. It departs from the most efficient prior work (Fast2/Fast5/Fast10 [11]), which accelerates the inversion step via meta-learning but still requires generating a large synthetic dataset, by instead attacking the overall training efficiency from a 'small data scale' perspective. The key conceptual departure is adapting prioritized experience replay from reinforcement learning [26] to select which synthetic samples to distill, combined with a modulating function inspired by focal loss [16] to balance sample diversity and difficulty during generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1–2 orders of magnitude reduction in end-to-end training time (e.g., ~1.1 h vs. ~4 h for Fast2, and ~4.2 h vs. ~15–300 h for conventional methods) makes data-free KD substantially more practical for real-world deployment where compute budgets are tight. The empirical finding that synthetic samples at 10% data scale outperform original samples at the same scale challenges the implicit assumption that more data is always better for distillation, suggesting that teacher-guided synthesis captures distributional information more efficiently than raw data at small budgets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large datasets (e.g., ImageNet-scale) or more complex tasks beyond classification and segmentation, and whether the GAN-based inversion bottleneck becomes limiting when the teacher-student gap is very large.
