## Problem

Diffusion models, unlike GANs, have not been designed or evaluated for latent space smoothness, meaning small perturbations to the input noise produce large, unpredictable visual changes in the output. This non-smoothness degrades downstream tasks such as image interpolation (causing abrupt visual jumps), image inversion (introducing reconstruction errors), and image editing (altering unedited content). The paper formalizes this gap and proposes a training-time mechanism to enforce smoothness without sacrificing generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model operates in the VAE latent space of Stable Diffusion V1.5, taking a text prompt (encoded via CLIP) and a Gaussian noise vector ε ∈ R^{4×64×64} as input, and producing a 512×512 RGB image after 50 denoising steps with classifier-free guidance (scale 7.5). For downstream tasks, a source image is first inverted into a latent via NTI or DDIM inversion; the smoothness property then governs how intermediate latents map to outputs during interpolation, reconstruction, or prompt-switching for editing. The method is implemented as a plug-and-play Smooth-LoRA (rank 8) adapter on the UNet, leaving the VAE and text encoder frozen.

## Method

The core contribution is Step-wise Variation Regularization, which enforces that a fixed-size perturbation Δx_t on the noisy latent at any diffusion step t produces a proportionally bounded change in the predicted clean image x̂_0, with the proportionality constant scaled by √(1−ᾱ_t). Because standard diffusion training only back-propagates through a single t-step snapshot rather than the full T-step process, the global smoothness objective (‖Δx̂_0‖₂ ∝ ‖Δε‖₂) is reformulated into this step-wise form. The regularization loss L_reg minimizes (√(1−ᾱ_t)‖J_ε^T Δx̂_0‖₂ − a)², where J_ε = ∂x̂_0/∂ε is the Jacobian and a is an online EMA of the quantity, computed efficiently via the identity ‖∇_ε(√(1−ᾱ_t) x̂_0 · Δx̂_0)‖₂. The total training objective is L_base + λL_reg (λ=1), and the model is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 8) on 625K LAION Aesthetics 6.5+ pairs for 30K iterations. The paper proves that minimizing L_reg drives J_ε toward orthogonality (up to a global scale), which exactly recovers the desired step-wise smoothness relation.

## Results

On the MS-COCO validation set, Smooth Diffusion reduces the proposed ISTD metric from 38.63 (Stable Diffusion) to 16.54, a 57% improvement in latent space smoothness, while simultaneously improving FID from 12.70 to 12.10 and CLIP Score from 31.46 to 31.54. For image inversion with DDIM, Smooth Diffusion achieves PSNR 16.17 vs. 13.97 and SSIM 0.3418 vs. 0.2662 over Stable Diffusion. With NTI inversion, Smooth Diffusion reaches PSNR 25.74 and SSIM 0.6102, approaching the VAE reconstruction upper bound (PSNR 25.98, SSIM 0.6136).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Smooth Diffusion outperforms Stable Diffusion on all three headline metrics (ISTD, FID, CLIP Score), demonstrating that latent smoothness and generation quality are complementary rather than competing objectives. In image inversion, Smooth Diffusion + DDIM substantially beats Stable Diffusion + DDIM across MSE (0.1086 vs. 0.1756), LPIPS (0.4326 vs. 0.5385), SSIM (0.3418 vs. 0.2662), and PSNR (16.17 vs. 13.97), while Smooth Diffusion + NTI edges out Stable Diffusion + NTI (PSNR 25.74 vs. 25.63, SSIM 0.6102 vs. 0.6068). For text-based image editing, the paper compares against six SOTA methods (SDEdit, P2P, PnP, Disentangle, Pix2Pix-Zero, Cycle Diffusion) qualitatively, reporting that Smooth Diffusion with a simple prompt-switching pipeline delivers competitive results while better preserving unedited content. For drag-based editing within the DragDiffusion framework, Smooth Diffusion enables operations (e.g., growing a tree taller, rotating a cat's head) that fail entirely with Stable Diffusion. The VAE-space interpolation and ANID baselines for image interpolation both produce significantly degraded or blurred results compared to Smooth Diffusion's smooth transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: GAN latent-space regularization (StyleGAN2's path-length penalty, Jacobian clamping) and diffusion model training. While GANs have long exploited smooth latent spaces to enable interpolation, inversion, and editing, diffusion models had no analogous mechanism. The authors adapt the GAN-style Jacobian-bounding idea into a Step-wise Variation Regularization that is compatible with diffusion's per-step training paradigm, and implement it as a lightweight LoRA on top of Stable Diffusion. The work also introduces ISTD, a new metric for quantifying latent smoothness, filling a gap where no standard evaluation existed for this property in diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play Smooth-LoRA offers a practical, low-cost path to improving downstream diffusion tasks (interpolation, inversion, editing) without retraining from scratch, which is immediately useful for the community. However, the evidence is confined to Stable Diffusion 1.5 on a single 625K-image subset of LAION, and the full fine-tuning ablation shows the regularization is fragile (FID degrades from 12 to 27). Open questions include whether the step-wise formulation transfers to larger models (SDXL, video diffusion), whether the ISTD metric generalizes beyond 2D images, and whether the smoothness benefit persists under different schedulers or guidance scales.
