## Problem

Previous Domain Generalized Semantic Segmentation (DGSS) methods relied on outdated backbones (ResNet, VGGNet, MobileNetV2) and left the potential of modern Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) largely unexplored. While frozen VFMs already outperform prior DGSS methods, full fine-tuning on small DGSS datasets degrades generalizability, and existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies are not designed for instance-level feature refinement required by dense prediction tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (cropped to 512×512 during training) and produces dense per-pixel semantic segmentation masks over 19 Cityscapes classes. The backbone is a frozen VFM (ViT-Large or ViT-Huge) whose intermediate feature maps are refined layer-by-layer by the Rein module before being passed to a Mask2Former decode head. Training uses only basic data augmentation following Mask2Former, with a batch size of 4 and 40,000 iterations on a single GPU.

## Method

Rein is a plug-and-play module inserted between consecutive layers of a frozen VFM backbone. It maintains a set of m=100 learnable tokens per layer, each factorized as a product of two low-rank matrices (rank r=16) to reduce parameters. Each token computes a softmax-normalized dot-product similarity map with the current layer's feature vectors, enabling instance-level attention that selectively refines features for different objects within a single image. The first token column is discarded after softmax, allowing the model to skip refinement when features are already adequate. Layer-wise queries derived from the tokens are aggregated (max, average, last-layer) and fed into the Mask2Former decode head to explicitly link tokens to instances. MLP weights are shared across all layers, yielding only ~3M trainable parameters (~1% of the backbone) while surpassing full fine-tuning in generalization.

## Results

Under the GTAV → Cityscapes + BDD100K + Mapillary generalization setting, Rein with DINOv2-Large achieves 64.3% average mIoU (66.4% Citys, 60.4% BDD, 66.1% Map) using only 2.99M trainable parameters in the backbone, surpassing full fine-tuning (61.7% avg, 304.20M params) by 2.6 points. With EVA02-Large, Rein reaches 63.6% average mIoU, again exceeding full fine-tuning (60.9%) by 2.7 points. In the synthetic-to-real setting (UrbanSyn+GTAV+Synthia → Cityscapes), Rein achieves 78.4% mIoU on the Cityscapes validation set without accessing any real urban-scene data, and 82.5% with just 1/16 of the Cityscapes training set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rein consistently outperforms full fine-tuning across all five VFMs: with DINOv2-Large it gains +2.6 mIoU (64.3 vs 61.7 avg), with EVA02-Large +2.7 (63.6 vs 60.9), and with SAM-Huge +1.0 (57.9 vs 56.9), while using only ~1% of the backbone's trainable parameters. Against PEFT baselines in Table 4, Rein (64.3% avg with DINOv2) surpasses VPT (63.3%), AdaptFormer (62.7%), and LoRA (62.7%); with EVA02, Rein (63.6%) beats AdaptFormer (62.6%), VPT (60.8%), and LoRA (55.5%). Traditional DGSS methods (AdvStyle, PASTA, GTR-LTR) show minimal or negative gains when applied to frozen VFMs, confirming that data augmentation and consistency strategies designed for ResNet-era backbones do not transfer well to VFMs. In the Cityscapes → BDD+Map real-to-real setting (Table 7), Rein with DINOv2-Large achieves 68.7% avg mIoU, outperforming HGFormer with Swin-L (66.8%) and all ResNet-based DGSS methods (52.8–56.4%). On Cityscapes-C level-5 corruption (Table 3), Rein with DINOv2 achieves 60.0% avg, exceeding HGFormer's 59.4%, though both struggle on Gaussian noise (6.2% vs 27.2% for HGFormer on Gaussian blur, suggesting Rein is more sensitive to extreme noise corruptions).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: domain generalized semantic segmentation (DGSS), which has historically relied on data augmentation and domain-invariant feature extraction with classic backbones (ResNet, VGGNet); Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like DINOv2 and EVA02, which have not been systematically evaluated in the DGSS context; and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA and AdaptFormer, which were designed for LLMs or classification rather than instance-level feature refinement in segmentation. Rein departs from prior PEFT by operating on learnable tokens linked to instances via DETR-style object queries, rather than injecting low-rank matrices into weight layers. The low-rank token decomposition borrows the mathematical structure of LoRA but applies it to a fundamentally different object (token sequences vs. weight matrices), and the layer-shared MLP design is a novel parameter-reduction strategy specific to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that frozen VFMs already outperform all prior DGSS methods fundamentally reframes the field: much of the prior DGSS literature was compensating for weak backbones, and the new baseline is substantially higher. Rein's ability to surpass full fine-tuning with only ~1% additional trainable parameters, while preserving pre-trained knowledge, offers a practical and efficient recipe for leveraging VFMs in domain generalization tasks. Open questions include whether the token-instance linkage generalizes beyond urban-scene segmentation to other domain generalization settings (e.g., medical imaging, remote sensing), and whether the approach can be combined with the DGSS-specific techniques (style transfer, adversarial augmentation) that showed limited gains here but might complement it in more challenging cross-domain scenarios.
