## Problem

Existing place recognition methods rely on 2D cameras or 3D LiDAR, which degrade under rain, fog, and low-light conditions. Although 4D millimeter-wave radar is inherently robust to such adverse weather, no end-to-end encoding architecture or benchmark dataset has been proposed specifically for 4D radar place recognition, and the unique multimodal attributes (geometry, intensity, Doppler velocity) of 4D radar scans have not been jointly exploited in a learnable framework.

## Input / Output

The input is a raw 4D radar scan providing five per-point attributes (range, azimuth, altitude, Doppler velocity, intensity). After RANSAC-based ego-velocity regression and dynamic-point removal, each point is represented by Cartesian coordinates, a scalar relative azimuth angle (cosine similarity between the point's radial direction and the ego-velocity direction), and a normalized intensity value. The output is a fixed-length 256-dimensional global descriptor produced by GeM pooling, which is invariant to point ordering and independent of the total number of points, enabling direct Euclidean-distance retrieval.

## Method

TransLoc4D is a three-stage differentiable encoder: (1) a MinkLoc4D backbone that voxelizes the refined 4D point cloud into sparse tensors carrying two numerical channels (velocity azimuth angle and intensity), then extracts local features via a Feature Pyramid Network of sparse 3D convolutions with Efficient Channel Attention; (2) a linear Transformer that first interpolates sparse voxel features back to the original point count, then applies multi-head self-attention with an ELU+1 kernel to capture long-range dependencies in O(N) time, and merges the attention residuals back via LayerNorm; (3) GeM pooling with a learnable power parameter to aggregate enhanced local features into the final descriptor. The key design insight is decoupling ego-velocity magnitude from direction: radial velocity is replaced by a direction-only attribute (cosine similarity with ego-velocity direction) so the representation is invariant to the vehicle's speed at the same location. Training uses the Truncated Smooth-AP loss with multistaged backpropagation for large-batch metric learning.

## Results

TransLoc4D achieves r@1 of 97.1% on NYL-Night, 86.8% on NYL-Rain, 94.5% on SRC-Night, 90.8% on SJTU4DPR-TestA, and 85.9% on SJTU4DPR-TestB, consistently outperforming all adapted 3D LiDAR/radar baselines. The most significant gains appear under adverse conditions: +5.8% r@1 over fine-tuned MinkLoc3Dv2 on NYL-Rain and +5.5% on SRC-Night. On the cross-domain SJTU4DPR-TestA, TransLoc4D leads fine-tuned PTC-Net-L by 11.0% r@1 (90.8% vs. 79.8%), demonstrating superior generalization to sparser 4D radar data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransLoc4D outperforms all five adapted baselines on the majority of evaluation sets. Against the strongest fine-tuned competitor MinkLoc3Dv2, TransLoc4D gains +5.8% r@1 on NYL-Rain (86.8 vs. 81.0) and +5.5% on SRC-Night (94.5 vs. 89.0), while matching or slightly exceeding it on NYL-Night (97.1 vs. 96.6). Against fine-tuned PTC-Net-L, TransLoc4D is competitive on NTU splits (within 0.8% on NYL-Rain) but dominates on the sparser SJTU4DPR-TestA by +11.0% r@1 (90.8 vs. 79.8), indicating better cross-domain generalization. TransLoc3D and AutoPlace trail TransLoc4D by 5–15% r@1 across all sets, confirming that architectures designed for dense 3D LiDAR or 2D radar images struggle with the sparsity and noise of 4D radar. Pre-trained 3D LiDAR models (PTC-Net-L*, MinkLoc3Dv2*) show noticeable domain-shift degradation, particularly on rainy and nighttime subsets, underscoring the necessity of 4D-radar-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

TransLoc4D extends the sparse-convolution-based place recognition lineage (MinkLoc3D → MinkLoc3Dv2 → MinkLoc3D-SI) from 3D LiDAR to 4D millimeter-wave radar, introducing velocity as a third numerical attribute alongside geometry and intensity. The Transformer module borrows the linear self-attention formulation from LOFTR and the sparse-feature interpolation strategy from PTC-Net, while the training pipeline reuses the TSAP loss and multistaged backpropagation from MinkLoc3Dv2. The key conceptual departure is the RANSAC-based ego-velocity regression that decouples radar motion from scene geometry, producing a velocity-azimuth attribute that is invariant to the vehicle's own speed — a preprocessing step with no direct analogue in the 3D LiDAR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end method and the first pair of open-source benchmarks (NTU4DPR, SJTU4DPR) for 4D radar place recognition, filling a gap that previously forced practitioners to adapt 3D LiDAR models to a fundamentally sparser, noisier, non-panoramic modality. The demonstrated robustness to rain and nighttime conditions highlights 4D radar's potential as a weather-independent localization sensor, which is practically important for autonomous vehicles operating in GPS-denied or inclement-weather environments. Open questions include whether the intensity attribute can be made more weather-invariant, how the method scales to much larger maps, and whether the RANSAC preprocessing step can be replaced by a differentiable alternative to enable fully end-to-end optimization.
