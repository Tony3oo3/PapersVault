## Problem

Generating realistic foreground shadows in composite images remains difficult because shadow appearance depends on complex lighting and geometry factors that are unavailable at inference time. Existing non-rendering methods trained on small paired datasets (e.g., DESOBA with only 1,012 images) produce shadows with inaccurate shapes and inconsistent intensities, while rendering-based methods require explicit user-specified lighting that cannot be inferred automatically from a background image.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 512×512 composite image (foreground pasted onto background without shadow) and a binary foreground object mask as input, and produces a 512×512 composite image with a plausible foreground shadow plus a predicted foreground shadow mask. Internally, the composite image is encoded to a 64×64 latent representation via a VAE, and the intensity encoder additionally consumes a background shadow mask (all-black when no background shadows exist). During inference, the initial noisy latent is obtained by adding noise to the composite image's own latent code rather than sampling from a standard Gaussian, preserving more of the input structure.

## Method

SGDiffusion adapts ControlNet (built on Stable Diffusion's latent U-Net) by feeding the concatenated composite image and foreground object mask into the control encoder, and introduces a weighted noise loss that assigns a higher weight (w=10) to a dilated foreground shadow region to prevent the model from generating oversized shadows while neglecting boundary details. A key innovation is an intensity modulation module: a ResNet-18 encoder takes the composite image and background shadow mask to predict channel-wise scale and bias vectors, which are applied to the predicted noise map within the foreground shadow region to align foreground shadow darkness with existing background shadows. The foreground shadow mask needed for this modulation is predicted from the U-Net's decoder feature maps (concatenated with the foreground object mask) using BCE + Dice loss, applied only at early denoising steps (t < 0.7T). A lightweight post-processing network then corrects residual color shift and background variation by blending the rectified image with the original composite outside the predicted shadow region. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on the newly constructed DESOBAv2 dataset (21,575 images, 28,573 pairs), which is built by detecting object-shadow pairs in real photos, inpainting shadows to create deshadowed versions, and synthesizing composite/ground-truth pairs.

## Results

On the DESOBAv2 dataset, SGDiffusion achieves the best performance across all six metrics on both BOS and BOS-free test images. On BOS images, it attains GR of 6.098, LR of 53.611, GS of 0.971, LS of 0.370, GB of 0.245, and LB of 0.487, outperforming the strongest baseline SGRNet (GR 7.184, LR 68.255, LS 0.206, GB 0.301, LB 0.596). On BOS-free images, SGDiffusion records GR 15.110, LR 55.874, LS 0.117, GB 0.233, and LB 0.452, again leading all baselines. The local SSIM improvement is particularly notable: 0.370 vs. 0.241 (ARShadowGAN) on BOS and 0.117 vs. 0.104 (ARShadowGAN) on BOS-free, indicating substantially better shadow intensity and texture fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGDiffusion outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both BOS and BOS-free settings. Against the strongest non-diffusion baseline SGRNet [12], SGDiffusion reduces global RMSE from 7.184 to 6.098 (−15%) and local RMSE from 68.255 to 53.611 (−21%) on BOS images, while improving local SSIM from 0.206 to 0.370 (+80%) and local BER from 0.596 to 0.487 (−18%). ARShadowGAN [22] is the closest competitor on local RMSE (58.037 vs. 53.611) and local SSIM (0.241 vs. 0.370) for BOS images, but SGDiffusion still leads by a clear margin. MaskShadowGAN [13] performs worst overall, with the highest RMSE values and a local BER of 1.000 (i.e., it fails to produce any meaningful shadow mask). ShadowGAN [53] sits in the middle, with moderate RMSE but notably poor local SSIM (0.197) and local BER (0.890) on BOS images, indicating inaccurate shadow shapes and intensities. The consistent advantage across both test splits suggests the diffusion-based approach generalizes well regardless of whether background object-shadow pairs are present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply a conditional diffusion model (ControlNet) to the shadow generation task, departing from the established GAN-based paradigm (ShadowGAN, ARShadowGAN, SGRNet) that has dominated the field. It builds directly on ControlNet's architecture and Stable Diffusion's latent space, adapting the control encoder input (composite image + foreground mask) and introducing a weighted noise loss to focus on the shadow region. The intensity modulation module, which uses a ResNet-18 encoder to predict channel-wise scale/bias for the noise map, is a novel architectural addition not present in prior ControlNet applications. The DESOBAv2 dataset extends the original DESOBA by an order of magnitude using an automated inpainting-based pipeline, addressing the data scarcity that limited prior GAN-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DESOBAv2 dataset (21,575 images, 28,573 pairs) is a substantial practical contribution that removes the data bottleneck constraining shadow generation research, and the code/model release makes the approach reproducible. However, the method is limited to 512×512 resolution, relies on an inpainting-based data pipeline that introduces subtle artifacts, and the post-processing network is needed to correct color shift and background variation—indicating the base diffusion model does not fully preserve the input. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, whether the intensity modulation generalizes to scenes with complex multi-source lighting, and whether the paired-data requirement can be relaxed with unpaired or text-conditioned training.
