## Problem

Existing editable 3D GANs for human avatars either require users to manually paint semantic masks for local editing, or train multiple independent generators per semantic region, leading to prohibitive memory costs. Additionally, human datasets exhibit strong style entanglement between attributes (e.g., dresses always paired with high-heel shoes), causing misleading discriminator punishment and artifacts during editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240511/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes StyleGAN2 latent codes, SMPL shape (β) and pose (θ) parameters, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and a user-selected subset of attribute one-hot labels for editing. It outputs 512×512 high-resolution RGB images, per-attribute semantic segmentation masks, and 3D meshes via compositional volume rendering. A key structural detail is that all 11 attributes (body, hair, shirts, pants, skirts, dress, shoes, etc.) are generated jointly in a single 4D space-attribute field and then extracted via learned per-attribute indexes, rather than being produced by separate networks.

## Method

The core architecture formulates human avatar generation as a 4D space-attribute field (x, y, z, a) and decomposes it into six feature planes (XY, YZ, XZ, XA, YA, ZA) using a tensor decomposition inspired by Hexplane, generated by a single StyleGAN2-based CNN. An implicit indexing module (8-layer MLP) maps one-hot attribute labels to learned index vectors in the attribute dimension, with an orthogonal projection regularization (L_orth) enforcing mutual orthogonality between attribute indexes to ensure disentanglement. Feature extraction for each attribute is performed by element-wise multiplication of the attribute-specific index with the corresponding feature planes, followed by a shared multi-head MLP that decodes residual SDF, semantic mask, and RGB features. To combat dataset-level style entanglement, the method introduces a hyper-latent training strategy that conditions the StyleGAN linear mapping on gender labels and an attribute-specific sampling strategy that restricts each attribute's feature sampling to a pre-defined bounding box. The framework is built on EG3D with SMPL-based canonical-to-observation deformation and a per-attribute non-rigid offset network, and editing is achieved by simply swapping the feature planes of the target attribute with those from a new latent code.

## Results

On the DeepFashion dataset, AttriHuman-3D achieves a FID of 16.85, PCK of 89.91%, and pseudo-depth error of 0.0302, approaching the best non-editable baseline EVA3D (FID 15.91, PCK 87.50%, depth 0.0272) while uniquely supporting interactive attribute-level editing. For editing quality, the FID of edited images (FIDedit) is 17.43, substantially lower than CNeRF's 30.31, indicating far more faithful attribute swaps. In terms of efficiency, the model uses 9 GB memory and 59 M parameters with 0.09 s per forward pass, compared to CNeRF's 16 GB and 0.23 s, and a naive 11-generator baseline that runs out of memory entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EG3D (FID 25.90, PCK 79.63%) and StyleSDF (FID 26.32, PCK 75.13%), both designed for rigid/half-rigid objects like faces, AttriHuman-3D improves FID by roughly 9 points and PCK by 10+ points, reflecting the difficulty of high-variance human poses and outfits. Compared to CNeRF, the only other editable baseline, AttriHuman-3D wins on every metric: FID drops from 27.69 to 16.85, FIDedit from 30.31 to 17.43, PCK rises from 71.56% to 89.91%, and depth error improves from 0.0421 to 0.0302, while also halving memory (9 GB vs 16 GB) and inference time (0.09 s vs 0.23 s). Against EVA3D, the strongest non-editable human-avatar method, AttriHuman-3D is slightly worse on FID (16.85 vs 15.91) and depth (0.0302 vs 0.0272) but actually surpasses it on PCK (89.91% vs 87.50%), all while adding the editing capability that EVA3D lacks. The ablation rows in Table 1 further show that each proposed component (implicit indexing, OPR, hyper-latent training, attribute-specific sampling) contributes incremental gains, with the full model achieving the best FIDedit of 17.43.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the tensor decomposition framework from 4D dynamic NeRFs (Hexplane, TensoRF) by replacing the time dimension with a discrete attribute dimension, enabling a single StyleGAN2-based generator to produce all semantic attributes (hair, clothes, body, etc.) in a shared 4D space-attribute field rather than requiring N independent generators as in CNeRF. It builds on EG3D's geometry-aware 3D GAN architecture and EVA3D's compositional human NeRF representation, but departs from both by introducing an implicit indexing module with orthogonal projection regularization to extract disentangled per-attribute features from the shared feature planes. The hyper-latent training strategy and attribute-specific sampling are novel training-time interventions specifically designed to counteract style correlations (e.g., dresses paired with high heels) prevalent in fashion datasets like DeepFashion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing editable 3D GANs either handle only rigid/half-rigid objects (faces) or require prohibitively expensive parallel generators for multi-attribute humans, making interactive full-body avatar editing infeasible in real-world applications like gaming and e-commerce. The tensor decomposition approach achieves a 44% memory reduction over CNeRF (9G vs 16G) while maintaining comparable generation quality, suggesting the 'all-to-one' design could generalize to other multi-part 3D generation tasks. However, the method is evaluated on a single dataset (DeepFashion) with 11 predefined attributes, relies on SMPL template priors for geometry, and the disentanglement quality may degrade with more diverse or larger-scale datasets. Open questions include whether the implicit indexing generalizes to open-vocabulary attributes and whether the approach scales to multi-view or video-based training.
