## Problem

Prior motion retrieval work has focused almost exclusively on dual-modality (text–motion) alignment, where the large semantic gap between language and 3D pose data makes joint embedding construction difficult without massive labeled datasets. This paper asks whether introducing a third modality—human-centric video—can serve as a spatial intermediary that narrows the distance between text and motion, and whether a unified tri-modal embedding space can simultaneously enable text-to-motion, video-to-motion, and their reverse retrieval tasks.

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input types: natural-language text descriptions (tokenized via DistilBERT), uniformly sampled RGB video frames (512×512, rendered from animated SMPL avatars since the source datasets contain no real video), and 3D motion sequences parameterized by SMPL (joint velocities, local positions, 6D rotations, foot-contact labels at 20 FPS for HumanML3D or 12.5 FPS for KIT-ML). At inference, a single text query or video clip is encoded and matched against a database of motions (or vice versa), returning ranked retrieval results; the reconstruction branch additionally outputs a full 3D pose sequence from the fused embedding.

## Method

LAVIMO builds a joint embedding space across text, video, and motion using three independently initialized encoders (MotionCLIP transformer for motion, DistilBERT for text, CLIP image encoder + temporal transformer for video) and aligns them via a symmetric KL-divergence contrastive loss over all three modality pairs. A key design choice is a modified negative-filtering scheme: instead of discarding text pairs above a similarity threshold, the paper retains their cosine scores in the target matrix, preventing semantically similar captions from being treated as hard negatives. For the reconstruction branch, a custom cross-modal attention mechanism uses the motion embedding as the query while drawing keys and values from both text and video embeddings, so that modality-specific information (e.g., speed adjectives in text, visual context in video) is selectively injected before a transformer decoder reconstructs the pose sequence. The total training objective combines the tri-modal alignment loss with an MSE reconstruction loss weighted by λ_recon, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with a learnable temperature parameter.

## Results

On HumanML3D (protocol 'All'), LAVIMO (3-modal) achieves R@1=6.37, R@5=21.95, R@10=33.67, and MedR=24.00 for text-to-motion retrieval, surpassing TMR (R@1=5.68, MedR=28.00) and all other baselines. On KIT-ML (protocol 'All'), the 3-modal version reaches R@1=10.16, R@10=49.80, and MedR=11.00, a substantially larger margin over TMR (R@1=7.23, MedR=17.00). For the novel video-to-motion retrieval task on HumanML3D, LAVIMO achieves R@1=21.25 and MedR=3.00, outperforming MotionSet (R@1=8.26, MedR=16.00) and MotionCLIP (R@1=4.96, MedR=43.00) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAVIMO (3-modal) outperforms all five baselines across every protocol on both HumanML3D and KIT-ML. Against TMR [28], the closest prior method, LAVIMO gains +0.69 R@1 and reduces MedR from 28.00 to 24.00 on HumanML3D text-to-motion, and +2.93 R@1 with MedR dropping from 17.00 to 11.00 on KIT-ML. The 2-modal ablation already surpasses TMR, confirming that the framework itself (not just the extra modality) contributes, while the 3-modal version adds a further +0.44 R@1 on HumanML3D and +1.57 R@1 on KIT-ML over the 2-modal variant. For video-to-motion retrieval, LAVIMO dominates MotionSet [36] by +12.99 R@1 (21.25 vs. 8.26) and MotionCLIP [42] by +16.29 R@1 (21.25 vs. 4.96) on HumanML3D, with MedR of 3.00 versus 16.00 and 43.00 respectively. TEMOS [27] and Guo et al. [12] are significantly weaker baselines (R@1 ≈ 2.1 and 1.8 on HumanML3D), reflecting their design for motion generation rather than retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAVIMO extends the contrastive-learning joint embedding space paradigm established by TMR (text-motion retrieval) and MotionCLIP (motion in CLIP space) from two modalities to three, introducing human-centric video as an intermediary modality to narrow the representational gap between text and 3D motion. The motion encoder is directly inherited from MotionCLIP, and the negative filtering strategy for handling semantically similar text pairs is adapted from TMR. The key conceptual departure is the tri-modal attention-based feature fusion module, which uses motion embeddings as queries to extract complementary information from text and video during reconstruction, drawing on the QKV framework from NLP rather than the single-modality reconstruction branches common in prior multi-modal alignment work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing video as a bridging modality, LAVIMO reduces the dependence on large, high-quality text-motion paired datasets that have been a bottleneck for cross-modal motion retrieval. The proposed video-to-motion retrieval task offers a practical alternative to human pose estimation, providing motions from curated databases rather than noisy per-frame predictions. However, the video modality is currently synthesized via avatar rendering rather than sourced from real footage, and the authors acknowledge that retrieval precision still lags significantly behind image/video retrieval benchmarks, leaving open the question of whether scaling to authentic human-centric video corpora can close this gap.
