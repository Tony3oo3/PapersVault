## Problem

Existing multi-instance point cloud registration methods treat the scene as a whole, causing superpoint features to be polluted by background points and other instances, which makes heavily-occluded instances nearly impossible to register in cluttered scenes. Additionally, these methods rely on multi-model fitting to cluster correspondences into instances, which struggles under heavy occlusion and incurs high computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241316/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a model point cloud P (N points) and a scene point cloud Q (M points) that may contain an unknown number K of instances of P embedded in background. It outputs per-instance 6-DoF rigid transformations (rotation in SO(3) and translation in R³) aligning P to each detected instance, along with instance-aware dense point correspondences and per-superpoint instance masks that partition the scene into individual instances.

## Method

MIRETR is a coarse-to-fine pipeline whose core is an Instance-aware Geometric Transformer that jointly learns superpoint features and predicts instance masks in an iterative refinement loop. At the coarse level, a geometric encoding block restricts intra-instance context aggregation to neighbors within the same predicted instance (via a binary mask added to attention scores), a cross-attention block models inter-point-cloud geometric consistency, and an instance masking block refines the masks using geodesic self-attention and an MLP confidence scorer. At the fine level, each superpoint correspondence is expanded into an instance candidate by collecting all neighboring superpoints within the same instance mask, and dense correspondences are extracted via optimal transport within that candidate scope. A non-maximum-suppression-style candidate selection and refinement algorithm then merges duplicates and iteratively refines poses, entirely bypassing multi-model fitting.

## Results

On the challenging ROBI benchmark (cluttered industrial bin-picking), MIRETR's full pipeline achieves 39.80% mean F1 score, surpassing the previous best (GeoTransformer + ECC at 23.20%) by 16.6 points. On Scan2CAD, MIRETR reaches 93.40% MF (vs. 83.57% for GeoTransformer+ECC), and on ShapeNet it achieves 94.44% MF (vs. 70.37%), demonstrating strong generalization to unseen categories. The method also improves inlier ratio by 12 pp over GeoTransformer on Scan2CAD (56.59% vs. 44.02%), indicating substantially more reliable correspondences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIRETR outperforms all seven baselines across all three benchmarks and all four metrics. Against the strongest prior correspondence method GeoTransformer [38], MIRETR gains 16.6 pp on MF on ROBI (39.80 vs. 23.20), 9.8 pp on Scan2CAD (93.40 vs. 83.57), and 24.1 pp on ShapeNet (94.44 vs. 70.37). Among multi-model fitting baselines, ECC [44] is the strongest pairing partner for prior methods, but MIRETR's full pipeline still surpasses GeoTransformer+ECC by 16.6 pp on ROBI MF and 11 pp on MP. PointCLM [55] achieves comparable MP to MIRETR on ROBI (40.11 vs. 41.19) but significantly worse MR (18.68 vs. 38.51), registering only 6.2 instances per scene on average versus 13.7 for MIRETR. CoFiNet [54] and FCGF [14] are substantially weaker, with CoFiNet+ECC achieving only 4.66% MF on ROBI, highlighting the difficulty of cluttered scenes for methods without instance-aware feature encoding. T-Linkage [33] and RansaCov [34] are consistently the weakest multi-model fitting partners, confirming that the correspondence quality (not the fitting algorithm) is the primary bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRETR extends the coarse-to-fine superpoint matching paradigm established by GeoTransformer and CoFiNet, which encode global geometric context via transformer attention to learn distinctive superpoint features. The key departure is restricting intra-point-cloud attention to an instance scope via learned instance masks, rather than aggregating context over all superpoints as in pairwise registration. It also departs from the standard two-stage pipeline (correspondence extraction followed by multi-model fitting) by directly generating instance candidates from superpoint correspondences and resolving duplicates with an NMS-like selection, eliminating the need for RANSAC-based or clustering-based multi-model fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16.6-point F1 improvement on the cluttered ROBI benchmark and consistent gains across three datasets demonstrate that instance-aware context encoding is critical for multi-instance registration in realistic cluttered scenes, with direct implications for robotic bin picking. Bypassing multi-model fitting offers both accuracy and efficiency gains, as the instance masks provide a natural grouping mechanism that avoids the sampling-heavy hypothesis testing of RANSAC variants. Open questions include whether the iterative mask refinement scales to scenes with very large numbers of instances, whether the approach extends to multi-modal (RGB-D) inputs as the authors note as future work, and how the method behaves when instance geometry is extremely incomplete (below the ~30% overlap where improvements diminish).
