## Problem

Existing learned image compression methods perform transform coding in pixel space, where pixel-level distortion does not align with human perception at ultra-low bitrates. Human observers prioritize semantic consistency and texture realism, but pixel-space transforms cannot adequately exploit these cues, causing generative codecs like MS-ILLM to struggle with visual quality below ~0.04 bpp. The paper asks how to compress images in a representation space that is inherently aligned with human perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242282/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (256×256 patches for natural-image training, 512×512 for facial-image training) and produces a compressed bitstream at ultra-low rates (below 0.04 bpp on CLIC 2020, below 0.01 bpp on CelebAHQ) along with a reconstructed image. Rate-variable compression is supported through a scalar quantization parameter q, allowing the same model to operate at different bitrates. The pipeline also supports auxiliary applications: a restoration encoder can map a distorted image into clean latents for joint restoration-and-compression, and a stylization decoder can decode latents into a different visual style.

## Method

GLC relocates the entire transform-coding pipeline from pixel space into the latent space of a generative VQ-VAE (Taming Transformers), exploiting the latent's sparsity, semantic richness, and perceptual alignment. The architecture encodes the image into a VQ-VAE latent, applies analysis/synthesis transforms (g_a, g_s) with scalar quantization and entropy coding, then decodes back through the VQ-VAE. Two key components differentiate it: (1) a categorical hyper module that replaces the standard factorized hyperprior with a discrete hyper codebook C_h, encoding high-level semantic priors at significantly lower bit cost; and (2) a code-prediction-based supervision used only during training (not inference) that requires the compressed latent to predict the correct VQ-indices, thereby injecting semantic consistency without constraining the decoder at test time. Training proceeds in three progressive stages: learning the VQ-VAE, learning the latent transform coder with code-prediction latent supervision, and jointly fine-tuning the full network with code-prediction pixel supervision.

## Results

On the CLIC 2020 test set, GLC achieves a bitrate below 0.04 bpp while delivering superior DISTS, FID, and KID scores compared to all baselines, saving approximately 45% bits relative to the previous SOTA (MS-ILLM) at equivalent FID. On CelebAHQ, GLC reaches an even more extreme bitrate of less than 0.01 bpp while outperforming all compared methods across every metric (DISTS, FID, KID, LPIPS) by a large margin. In the restoration application, GLC achieves FID 8.62 and DISTS 0.1081 at 0.0299 bpp, outperforming the cascaded Restormer + GLC codec (FID 10.79, DISTS 0.1174 at 0.0314 bpp) without additional model parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLC achieves 45% bit savings over MS-ILLM [46] at the same FID on CLIC 2020, making it the most significant comparison; MS-ILLM remains competitive on LPIPS but trails on DISTS, FID, and KID. Against HiFiC [44], GLC matches LPIPS performance but clearly wins on perceptual metrics (DISTS, FID, KID), and qualitatively produces fewer artifacts in complex-texture regions. Text+Sketch [35] achieves strong perceptual quality but deviates significantly from the input in terms of semantic fidelity, whereas GLC preserves both realism and fidelity. Traditional and neural codecs (VVC [54], TCM [41], EVC [20], FCC [26]) all produce noticeably blurry or artifact-laden reconstructions at ultra-low bitrate, while GLC maintains sharp, semantically consistent results. For the restoration application, GLC's dedicated restoration encoder outperforms the straightforward cascade of Restormer [57] followed by the GLC codec, reducing FID from 10.79 to 8.62 and DISTS from 0.1174 to 0.1081 while using fewer total parameters (109M vs. 25M+109M).

## Relation to Prior Work

GLC departs from the dominant pixel-space transform coding paradigm (Ballé et al. 2017, 2018) by relocating the analysis/synthesis transform into the latent space of a generative VQ-VAE (Esser et al. 2021; Van Den Oord et al. 2017), so that compression operates on discrete semantic codes rather than raw pixels. It inherits the rate-variable scalar-quantization mechanism from the DCVC video compression series (Li et al. 2021–2024) and the quadtree spatial context model (Li et al. 2023), but replaces the standard factorized hyper prior with a categorical (codebook-based) hyper module. The code-prediction-based supervision draws on CodeFormer (Zhou et al. 2022) and Jiang et al. (2023), but repurposes it as a training-only auxiliary loss rather than an inference-time module, a deliberate departure from those works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 45% bit reduction over MS-ILLM at matched FID on CLIC2020, combined with sub-0.01 bpp operation on faces, demonstrates that semantic latent spaces can serve as a more compression-friendly domain than pixels at extreme rate constraints. The demonstrated versatility—swapping encoders/decoders for restoration or style transfer without architectural changes—suggests a unifying substrate where compression and other vision tasks share a common representational bottleneck. However, the authors acknowledge limited generalization (e.g., screen content), and the approach inherits the dataset-dependence of the underlying VQ-VAE, leaving open how to make the latent space robust across diverse content without sacrificing the sparsity that enables ultra-low-bitrate coding.
