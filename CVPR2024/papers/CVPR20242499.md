## Problem

Existing text-to-3D face synthesis methods either generate geometry and texture simultaneously (causing loss of geometric detail) or support only coarse-grained editing without fine-grained control. This prevents users from iteratively refining a 3D face through sequential text instructions, as each edit introduces unintended changes to irrelevant facial attributes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., a celebrity name or character description) for generation, and subsequent text editing instructions (e.g., 'let him wear a purple Zorro mask') for sequential editing. The output is a parametric 3D face mesh parameterized by HIFI3D identity coefficients β and a UV-mapped texture map decoded from a latent code u via the Stable Diffusion VAE decoder. Geometry and texture are independently editable, and the resulting face integrates directly into standard CG pipelines for animation and relighting.

## Method

FaceG2E decouples generation into two phases: a geometry phase that renders the mesh with texture-less shading (surface normals or constant grey diffuse) so SDS focuses exclusively on geometric detail, and a texture phase that uses depth-conditioned ControlNet (GaSDS) to enforce geometry-texture alignment, combined with fine-tuned diffusion models in both RGB and YUV color spaces (trained on 500 facial textures with a fixed keyword) to improve texture quality. For editing, the method substitutes InstructPix2Pix into the SDS framework and introduces a self-guided consistency preservation mechanism: cross-attention scores for a region-indicating token are projected to the UV domain to compute a per-region consistency weight, which is fused across viewpoints via a moving average and applied as a Hadamard-product regularization on both texture and vertex-position differences, allowing fine-grained edits while preserving unrelated facial attributes across sequential rounds.

## Results

On the CLIP-based evaluation with 20 prompts (10 generation, 10 editing), FaceG2E achieves a CLIP Score of 36.95 and 80% Ranking-1 for generation, and 35.50 and 70% Ranking-1 for editing, outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, TADA, scores 34.85 (generation) and 33.73 (editing) with only 10–20% Ranking-1. A user study with 100 participants further confirms that FaceG2E is the most preferred method for both generation and editing tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaceG2E outperforms all three baselines on both CLIP Score and Ranking-1 in generation and editing. Describe3D [47] (a mapping-network approach trained on text-face pairs) is the weakest, scoring 29.81 in generation and 0% Ranking-1, as it cannot generalize beyond its training distribution to celebrity or character prompts. DreamFace [53] produces realistic textures via SDS but lacks an editing stage and has insufficient geometric fidelity, yielding 33.22 (generation) and 33.14 (editing) with only 10% Ranking-1. TADA [26] is the strongest baseline, achieving 34.85 in generation and 33.73 in editing, but it still trails FaceG2E by ~2.1 points in generation and ~1.8 in editing; qualitatively, TADA exhibits geometric distortions and fails at sequential editing (e.g., unintended skin color changes in later rounds), whereas FaceG2E's self-guided consistency preservation enables precise multi-round editing without disrupting prior modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DreamFusion SDS framework and InstructPix2Pix editing model to the 3D face domain, departing from prior works (DreamFace, TADA, HeadSculpt) that either generate geometry and texture simultaneously or offer only coarse-grained editing without precise control. The key conceptual departure is the geometry-texture decoupling: by rendering texture-less geometry for the geometry phase and using depth-ControlNet for the texture phase, the paper addresses a specific failure mode (geometric detail loss due to texture-geometry coupling) that prior joint-optimization approaches suffer from. The self-guided consistency preservation via cross-attention projection to UV space is a novel regularization mechanism not present in prior editing methods, which relied on mixture-of-gradients or lacked explicit consistency constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to enable sequential, fine-grained text-guided 3D face editing, opening the door to iterative customization workflows analogous to 2D image editing. However, the parametric HIFI3D representation limits expressiveness (no hair, accessories, or non-skin geometry), and each editing round adds ~4 minutes of optimization, making multi-step customization impractical for interactive use. Open questions include whether the decoupling and consistency-preservation ideas transfer to non-parametric or neural-representation faces, and whether the cross-attention-based region localization generalizes to more complex or ambiguous editing instructions.
