## Problem

Existing face anonymization methods face a fundamental privacy-utility tradeoff: they either over-modify identity-independent attributes (expression, ethnic, makeup) to guarantee anonymity, or under-protect identity to preserve data utility. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an attention-distraction framework that selectively suppresses only identity-correlated information while minimally perturbing identity-independent features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240544/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image (256×256) and produces an anonymous face image of the same resolution. The input is decomposed into three channels for processing: an identity feature vector (from a pre-trained FaceNet), a visual appearance image (segmented foreground face), and a geometry structure (2D facial landmarks). The output is a realistic face that preserves expression, ethnic, gender, age, and makeup while being unrecognizable to both machine verifiers and human observers.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline: (1) anonymization in the feature and visual spaces, and (2) conditional face synthesis via a GAN. For identity feature anonymization (IFA), Grad-CAM heatmaps from a pre-trained FaceNet identify identity-correlated feature maps at the last convolutional layer; a modulation term derived from the CAM importance weights is added to suppress those activations while preserving identity-independent ones, jointly distracting the top-K predicted identities. For visual clue anonymization (VCA), an Instance-Level Probabilistic Delegate (IPD) sampler—based on the differential privacy exponential mechanism—selects a delegate appearance image and geometry structure from k-nearest neighbors, balancing anonymity against utility via task-specific utility functions. The anonymized identity feature, appearance, and geometry are then fed into a U-Net-based conditional translator with AdaIN fusion to synthesize the final anonymous face, trained with a multi-task loss combining adversarial, perceptual, appearance, identity, and background-preservation terms under a 1:1 reconstruction and cycle swap-reconstruction strategy.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, the proposed method achieves 0.0% ReID rate on ArcFace (cos>0.30 and cos>0.35) and 0.1% on FaceNet (l2<0.9), while maintaining the lowest IDS rate of 34.9% on FaceNet l2<0.9 (vs. 58.6% for PIFD). For utility preservation, it attains the best expression (84.2%), makeup (80.1%), LPIPS (0.120), and SSIM (0.799) scores among all compared methods. On VGGFace2 and LFW, the method generalizes well with 0.0% ReID and competitive IDS (10.7% and 4.7% respectively) while outperforming baselines on attribute preservation and image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the best or near-best ReID (0.0–0.1%) across all face recognition backbones, matching PIFD and Riddle on ArcFace while outperforming CIAGAN (1.7%), LDFA (4.9%), and FALCO (6.7%). On identity intrusion (IDS), the method substantially outperforms PIFD on FaceNet l2<0.9 (34.9% vs. 58.6%) and Riddle (34.9% vs. 77.7%), though PIFD edges it slightly on ArcFace cos>0.35 (19.1% vs. 19.6%). For utility preservation, the method leads all baselines on expression (84.2% vs. 82.6% FALCO, 82.3% PIFD), makeup (80.1% vs. 78.9% DP2, 63.7% PIFD), LPIPS (0.120 vs. 0.124 PIFD/LDFA, 0.300 Riddle), and SSIM (0.799 vs. 0.771 PIFD, 0.530 Riddle). DP2 is the closest competitor on image quality (LPIPS 0.127, SSIM 0.779) but lags significantly on expression (59.1%). On VGGFace2 and LFW, the method maintains 0.0% ReID while achieving the best attribute scores (75.6% and 80.9%) and SSIM (0.838 and 0.895), confirming generalization across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Grad-CAM, originally developed for visualizing CNN predictions, as an active mechanism to identify and suppress identity-correlated features in the feature space, shifting it from a post-hoc interpretability tool to a pre-synthesis anonymization operator. It also generalizes differential privacy from the standard noise-adding paradigm (Laplace/Gaussian on pixels or embeddings) to an instance-level probabilistic delegate sampling strategy for both appearance and geometry structure. The two-step architecture—performing anonymization (IFA + VCA) before feeding a dedicated conditional GAN for synthesis—deliberately decouples the privacy-utility tradeoff from the generative model's optimization, departing from prior methods like CIAGAN and PIFD that embed PU constraints directly into the adversarial training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the persistent privacy-utility tension in face anonymization by minimizing changes to identity-independent attributes, and its controllable geometry/appearance manipulation offers a practical path toward user-directed anonymization in applications like autonomous driving and telemedicine. However, identity intrusion (IDS) rates remain substantial (19–35% depending on threshold), and the method still struggles to preserve ethnic identity and eye gaze direction, leaving a gap between 'fooling the machine' and 'fooling the human' that the user study partially confirms. Open questions include whether the Grad-CAM-based feature suppression generalizes to newer recognition backbones (e.g., foundation models) and how to retain higher-order signals (psychological, physiological) that may leak identity beyond pixel-level features.
