## Problem

Circular patterns offer sub-pixel control point detection for camera calibration, but radial lens distortion deforms projected circles into non-conic shapes, biasing centroid estimates and degrading calibration accuracy. Existing remedies either ignore distortion, resort to 3D spherical targets, or rely on iterative numerical integration (Kannala & Brandt) that is computationally expensive and still approximate. This paper provides the first closed-form analytic solution for the unbiased centroid of a conic under radial polynomial distortion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB or thermal-infrared (TIR) images of a planar target bearing a grid of circles (a PCB with varying heat conductivity is used for TIR, since thermal cameras cannot resolve color patterns). Given the known target geometry (circle centers and radii in the target coordinate frame), it jointly optimizes camera intrinsics (f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y), radial distortion coefficients (d_1 through d_{n_d}), and per-image 6D target poses by minimizing squared reprojection error. The predicted control point for each circle is the analytically computed unbiased centroid of the distorted projected shape, not a pixel-weighted average.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that under any invertible polynomial mapping, the moment of a transformed shape is a linear combination of the original shape's moments. Specializing to radial polynomial distortion k(x_n,y_n) = Σ d_i·s_n^i, the centroid of the distorted ellipse in the normalized plane reduces to a weighted sum of integrals ∫x_n·s_n^r dA_n and ∫y_n·s_n^r dA_n over the undistorted ellipse, with weights w_{1r} derived from the distortion coefficients. These ellipse integrals are evaluated in closed form by decomposing the problem into: (i) computing moments of an axis-aligned unit ellipse at the origin via binomial-coefficient expansions and trigonometric integrals (Lemmas 3–4, Theorem 5), and (ii) rotating the result to match the actual ellipse orientation using a rotation-equivariance property (Theorem 2). All binomial and trigonometric factors are precomputed, yielding O(1) evaluation per circle per moment order. The full calibration pipeline initializes intrinsics via Zhang's zero-distortion method, then refines all parameters with Ceres Solver using the unbiased estimator as the reprojection model.

## Results

On synthetic images with high radial distortion (d₁=−0.4), the proposed unbiased estimator recovers intrinsic parameters with errors of ±0.03–0.10 pixels (e.g., fₓ = 599.9±0.09 vs. GT 600), while the point-based and conic-based estimators exhibit systematic biases of 3.1 and 6.9 pixels respectively in fₓ. On real-world 6D pose evaluation, the method achieves the lowest rotation error (0.24° RGB, 0.45° TIR) and translation error (2.5 mm RGB, 3.1 mm TIR) across all tested cameras. The method runs in 2.0 s per calibration, compared to 86.6 s for the 1600-iteration numerical baseline that achieves comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the checkerboard method (Zhang [28]), the proposed estimator ties on RGB 6D pose (2.5 mm translation, 0.24° vs. 0.32° rotation) but substantially outperforms it on TIR images (3.1 mm vs. 16.5 mm translation; 0.45° vs. 1.63° rotation), where boundary blur degrades corner detection. Against the numerical integration baseline of Kannala & Brandt [13], the method achieves comparable or slightly better parameter accuracy (±0.03–0.10 vs. ±0.06–0.18) while being ~43× faster (2.0 s vs. 86.6 s at 1600 iterations). The point-based and conic-based estimators, which are the standard approaches for circular patterns, show systematic biases of 3–7 pixels in focal length under high distortion, confirming that distortion-induced bias dominates over perspective bias. Under Gaussian blur (σ=2), the checkerboard method fails on ~15% of images, while the proposed method maintains zero failures and near-GT accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper completes a gap left by Kannala and Brandt (2006), who formulated the unbiased estimator for circular patterns under distortion as an integral equation but could not derive an analytic solution. It builds on the conic-based calibration lineage (Heikkila 2000, Kim et al. 2005) that handles perspective bias but leaves distortion bias unresolved, and on Zhang's (2000) checkerboard method used for initialization. The key departure is the introduction of moment theory into calibration: by proving that the first moment of a distorted conic is a linear combination of the undistorted conic's moments under any polynomial mapping, the authors obtain a closed-form unbiased estimator that no prior geometric or numerical approach had achieved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes the last algorithmic barrier preventing circular patterns from fully exploiting their sub-pixel detection advantage in calibration, with particularly strong practical impact for TIR cameras where checkerboard corner detection fails under blur and noise. The moment-based formulation is general (any polynomial distortion model, any conic shape) and differentiable, suggesting applicability beyond standard pinhole calibration to broader nonlinear projection pipelines. Limitations include the restriction to radial (not tangential) distortion and the pinhole model; open questions include whether the moment approach extends to more general ray-tracing distortion models or to higher-order conic properties (e.g., full ellipse fitting) under distortion.
