## Problem

Anchor-based multi-view clustering methods generate anchors independently per view, ignoring the correlation structure among views, which leads to poor initial anchor quality and distribution bias. Existing iteration-optimization strategies improve the anchor–graph combination but remain sensitive to the initial anchor state and do not directly enhance anchor quality. The paper identifies that similar views should yield similar anchor distributions, yet no prior method exploits this inter-view correlation to improve anchors.

## Input / Output

The method takes v views of n samples, each with its own dimensionality d_i (ranging from 9 to 4819 across the seven benchmark datasets), and produces cluster assignments via k-means on the left singular vector of the final anchor graph. A key constraint is that anchors are generated per view and must be aligned across views before cross-view information can be transferred; the number of anchors m is typically set to k, 2k, or 5k where k is the number of clusters. The method is designed to scale linearly in n, demonstrated on datasets up to 50,000 samples (CIFAR-100) and 36,287 samples (VGGFace2).

## Method

AEVC is a plug-and-play anchor enhancement strategy with two modules. First, the view-graph learning module aligns per-view anchors to a common ordering using permutation matrices (solved via the assignment problem on concatenated-view anchor graphs), then constructs a v×v view graph where edge weights are the inverse Frobenius distances between aligned anchor graphs. Second, the anchor enhancement module reconstructs each view's anchors as a convex combination of the original anchors and anchors rebuilt from the current view's data multiplied by neighboring views' anchor graphs, weighted by normalized view-graph similarity over the ζ-nearest views. A revised anchor graph construction step then fuses views with learned importance weights (sum of view-graph similarities) and a regularization term toward the concatenated-view anchor graph, solved as n independent QP problems. Two theoretical properties are proven: anchor enhancement strictly reduces inter-view anchor graph disparity and brings each view's anchor graph closer to the optimal consistent anchor graph.

## Results

AEVC achieves the best ACC on all seven datasets, with headline values of 93.20% (Dermatology), 82.53% (ForestTypes), 60.65% (BDGP), 29.91% (Reuters), 88.01% (Hdigit), 8.35% (VGGFace2), and 10.74% (CIFAR-100). The paper reports that AEVC surpasses the second-best method by up to 15.8% ACC on VGGFace2 and 9.5% on CIFAR-100. In NMI, AEVC leads on five of seven datasets but trails FastMICE on Reuters (7.07 vs. 7.23) and LMVSC on Hdigit (78.09 vs. 89.33). The method also demonstrates plug-and-play transferability, improving four existing anchor-based methods by 6–28% ACC when the AEVC module is attached.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEVC outperforms all eight baselines in ACC across all seven datasets. The largest margins are against FMCNOF (which uses fixed anchors with nonnegative orthogonal factorization), where AEVC gains 31.2% ACC on Dermatology and 84.5% on VGGFace2, and against FPMVSCAG (consensus anchor guidance), where AEVC leads by 12.6% on Reuters. Among the stronger iteration-optimization baselines, AEVC beats FMVACC (anchor matching correspondences) by 8.81% on Dermatology and 1.67% on VGGFace2, and UDBGL (discrete bipartite graph learning) by 9.12% on Dermatology and 2.91% on CIFAR-100. FastMICE (ensemble-based) is the closest competitor on Dermatology (87.15 vs. 93.20) and CIFAR-100 (9.81 vs. 10.74), while LMVSC (k-means anchors) is the nearest rival on Hdigit (86.87 vs. 88.01). In NMI, AEVC does not dominate: FastMICE edges out AEVC on Reuters (7.23 vs. 7.07) and LMVSC leads on Hdigit (89.33 vs. 78.09), suggesting the anchor enhancement strategy trades some view-specific NMI for stronger overall ACC consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two dominant paradigms in anchor-based multi-view clustering: fixed-anchor methods (LMVSC, FMCNOF) and iteration-optimization methods (SMVSC, UDBGL, FMVACC). It departs from the iteration-optimization line by arguing that jointly updating anchors and anchor graphs still suffers from initialization sensitivity, and instead proposes a one-shot structural enhancement that borrows anchor graph information from neighboring views via a learned view graph. The anchor alignment mechanism is directly adopted from FMVACC [39], and the final k-means-on-singular-vector clustering step follows LMVSC [14], making the contribution specifically the intermediate view-graph-learned anchor enhancement module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design is the paper's most practically valuable aspect: it can be bolted onto existing anchor-based MVC pipelines (both fixed-anchor and iteratively-optimized variants) without architectural redesign, and the transferability experiments show consistent gains across four different base methods. The theoretical properties (reduced inter-view anchor graph disparity and convergence toward the optimal consensus graph) provide clean guarantees, though they are derived under a simplified two-view, exact-reconstruction assumption. Open questions include whether the view-graph approach extends to incomplete multi-view settings, whether the enhancement rate γ can be learned adaptively (acknowledged as future work), and whether the linear-in-n complexity advantage is preserved when the number of views grows large.
