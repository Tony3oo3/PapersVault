## Problem

Remote sensing object detection faces large object scale variation (from soccer fields to vehicles) and diverse contextual requirements, yet prior backbones that enlarge receptive fields via large-kernel convolutions introduce background noise for small objects, while dilated convolutions produce overly sparse feature representations. No existing backbone simultaneously captures dense multi-scale local texture and long-range context without these trade-offs.

## Input / Output

PKINet serves as a plug-and-play feature extraction backbone that consumes RGB remote sensing images (aerial or satellite) cropped into 1024×1024 patches (DOTA) or 800×800 (HRSC2016, DIOR-R) and outputs multi-scale feature maps at four spatial resolutions (1/4× to 1/32× input). These features feed into standard oriented bounding box detectors (e.g., Oriented RCNN, S2ANet, R3Det) that produce rotated bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores.

## Method

PKINet is a four-stage CNN backbone using a Cross-Stage Partial (CSP) structure, where each stage contains a sequence of PKI Blocks. Each PKI Block combines two components: (1) a PKI Module that applies a small 3×3 convolution for local texture followed by four parallel depth-wise convolutions of increasing sizes (5×5, 7×7, 9×9, 11×11) without dilation, then fuses them via a 1×1 convolution to capture dense multi-scale context; and (2) a Context Anchor Attention (CAA) Module that uses global average pooling followed by two 1D depth-wise strip convolutions (kernel size expanding with depth) to generate a spatial attention map that enhances long-range pixel relationships. The strip convolution design keeps the CAA lightweight while facilitating detection of slender objects like bridges. Two variants (PKINet-T with 4.1M params and PKINet-S with 13.7M params) are trained via ImageNet-1K pretraining followed by fine-tuning within OBB detector frameworks.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, PKINet-S integrated with Oriented RCNN achieves 78.39% mAP with 30.8M parameters, surpassing the previous best LSKNet-S (77.49%) by 0.90% and O-RCNN with ResNet-50 (75.87%) by 2.52%. On the more challenging DOTA-v1.5 (with minuscule instances), PKINet-S reaches 71.47% mAP, outperforming LSKNet-S (70.26%) by 1.21%. On HRSC2016, PKINet-S achieves 98.54% mAP(12) with 30.8M parameters, edging out LSKNet (98.46%), and on DIOR-R it attains 67.03% mAP, the best among all compared methods. As a general-purpose backbone on COCO 2017, PKINet-S (13.7M params) reaches 43.4 mAP, outperforming ConvNeXt-N (41.3) and PVT-T (36.7) with comparable or fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PKINet-S consistently outperforms ResNet-50 as a backbone across all tested detectors: +3.71% mAP with S2ANet (77.83 vs 74.12), +2.41% with Rotated FCOS (74.86 vs 72.45), and +2.52% with O-RCNN (78.39 vs 75.87) on DOTA-v1.0. Against the strongest prior backbone LSKNet-S (ICCV 2023), PKINet-S achieves a 0.90% mAP gain on DOTA-v1.0 (78.39 vs 77.49) with fewer parameters (30.8M vs 31.0M), and a 1.21% gain on DOTA-v1.5 (71.47 vs 70.26). Compared to the adaptive rotated convolution backbone ARC-R50 (ICCV 2023), PKINet-S matches or exceeds its performance (78.39 vs 77.35 with O-RCNN) while using roughly half the parameters (30.8M vs 74.4M). On HRSC2016, PKINet-S (98.54% mAP(12)) narrowly surpasses LSKNet (98.46%) and ReDet (97.63%), and on DIOR-R it achieves the best mAP of 67.03%, beating DCFL (66.80%), Oriented RCNN (66.71%), and GGHL (66.48%). On COCO 2017, PKINet-S (43.4 mAP) outperforms ConvNeXt-N (41.3), PVT-T (36.7), and ResNet-18 (34.0) with comparable parameter counts, confirming its generality beyond remote sensing.

## Relation to Prior Work

PKINet builds on the cross-stage partial (CSP) structure from CSPNet and the parallel multi-branch design of Inception modules, but departs from the dominant remote sensing backbone design (LSKNet) that relies on large-kernel or dilated convolutions to expand receptive fields. The key conceptual shift is replacing those mechanisms with parallel depth-wise convolutions of varying sizes (3×3 to 11×11) without dilation, arguing that large kernels introduce background noise for small objects while dilation produces overly sparse features. A Context Anchor Attention module, inspired by LSKNet and GhostNetV2, is added in parallel to capture long-range context via global average pooling and 1D strip convolutions, making PKINet the first to combine inception-style multi-scale convolution with global context attention specifically for remote sensing detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PKINet offers a practical, lightweight backbone (4.1M–13.7M parameters) that consistently outperforms ResNet-50 and the prior SOTA LSKNet across four remote sensing benchmarks while also generalizing to COCO, making it a drop-in replacement for existing oriented detection pipelines. The ablation showing that dilated convolutions actively degrade performance (−1.09%) despite enlarging the receptive field challenges the common assumption that larger receptive fields are always beneficial. The authors acknowledge that compute constraints prevented scaling beyond the Tiny and Small variants, leaving open whether the architecture's advantages persist at larger model capacities, a question that could be critical for high-stakes remote sensing applications.
