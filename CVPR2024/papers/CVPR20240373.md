## Problem

Pre-trained visual-language models like CLIP excel at zero/few-shot anomaly detection in natural images but fail to transfer to medical imaging due to a substantial domain gap and a fundamental task mismatch: CLIP's visual encoder captures object semantics, whereas medical anomaly detection requires discerning irregularities across diverse modalities and anatomical regions. No prior work has adapted VLMs for medical anomaly detection in zero- or few-shot settings where the model must generalize to unseen modalities and body parts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240373/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image at 240×240 resolution, a set of text prompts describing 'normal' and 'anomalous' states (averaged over 35 templates), and optionally a small set of K reference normal images (K ∈ {2,4,8,16}) for few-shot mode. It produces two outputs: an image-level anomaly classification score and a pixel-level anomaly segmentation map, each computed as a weighted average (β₁=β₂=0.5) of a zero-shot branch (softmax similarity to text features) and a few-shot branch (nearest-neighbor distance in a multi-level memory bank).

## Method

MVFA inserts three residual bottleneck adapters (two linear layers each) at the intermediate stages of a frozen ViT-L/14 CLIP visual encoder, plus a final-level projector, with a residual ratio γ=0.1 to preserve pre-trained knowledge. A dual-adapter design produces parallel feature streams for global classification and local segmentation at each of four levels. Training uses a multi-level pixel-wise visual-language alignment loss that combines Dice, Focal, and BCE terms at every level, jointly optimizing all adapters through gradient propagation across encoder stages. At test time, a two-branch comparison architecture fuses zero-shot text-similarity scores with few-shot nearest-neighbor distances in a multi-level memory bank to yield the final anomaly score and segmentation map.

## Results

Under the few-shot setting (K=4), MVFA achieves an average AUC of 88.97% for anomaly classification (AC) and 98.67% for anomaly segmentation (AS) across six medical datasets. In the zero-shot setting (leave-one-out across modalities), MVFA attains an average AUC of 78.63% for AC and 92.05% for AS, surpassing the strongest baseline (April-GAN) by 6.24% and 2.03% respectively. In the few-shot setting, MVFA outperforms April-GAN by 7.33% (AC) and 2.37% (AS) on average. On the in-domain MVTec AD benchmark, MVFA achieves 96.19% AC / 96.32% AS, comparable to state-of-the-art methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVFA outperforms all ten baselines on the majority of datasets. Against the closest few-shot competitor April-GAN [9], MVFA gains 7.33% average AC AUC and 2.37% AS AUC, with the largest margin on LiverCT AC (81.18 vs. 53.05, +28.13%). Against WinCLIP [26], MVFA improves by 12.60% AC and 2.81% AS on average, showing the multi-level adapter design is critical over simple prompt-based comparison. Against BGAD [57], MVFA leads by 9.18% AC and 3.53% AS on datasets with pixel-level annotations. Full-normal-shot methods (PatchCore, MKD, CFlowAD, RD4AD) are consistently outperformed despite using substantially more training data, highlighting the value of even a few abnormal samples. MedCLIP [52] is the sole exception where MVFA does not win on every dataset: MedCLIP achieves 84.06% on ChestXray AC versus MVFA's 81.95%, attributed to MedCLIP's training on large-scale overlapping chest X-ray data, but MVFA dominates on all other five datasets. On the in-domain MVTec AD benchmark, MVFA (96.19/96.32) slightly edges out WinCLIP (95.16/96.27) and April-GAN (92.77/95.89), confirming no significant in-domain degradation from the medical adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based anomaly detection line initiated by WinCLIP (which uses crafted text prompts for zero/few-shot AD on natural images) and April-GAN (which projects visual features into text space for pixel-level AD) into the medical domain. The key architectural departure is the adoption of multi-level residual adapters (inspired by CLIP-Adapter) inserted at multiple encoder stages, replacing the single-level feature projectors used by April-GAN. Unlike prior work that treats medical AD as a one-class classification problem requiring large normal-only datasets, this paper frames it as a visual-language alignment task with lightweight adaptation, enabling cross-modality generalization without per-task retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single lightweight adapter-based model can generalize across five distinct medical modalities and anatomical regions in zero/few-shot settings, addressing a critical practical gap where per-modality models are infeasible. The ablation showing that multi-level residual adapters outperform isolated projectors by ~13.6% in AC AUC provides a clear architectural principle for adapting VLMs to new domains. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric data (CT, MRI volumes), whether it generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLMs, and whether the zero-shot cross-modality gap narrows with more diverse pre-training data.
