## Problem

Existing diffusion model training optimizes instance-level denoising score matching losses, treating each sample independently and ignoring the pairwise manifold structures within a minibatch. This structural information is critical for aligning the learned distribution with the true data distribution, yet no prior method explicitly enforces the model to preserve these inter-sample relationships during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241914/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise (and an optional class label for conditional generation) as input and produces synthetic RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 512×512 (ImageNet). During training, the key additional input is a minibatch of ground-truth images whose pairwise relationships in a pre-trained feature space (Inception-V3) define the structural target. The method applies to both pixel-space diffusion (EDM, DDPM) and latent-space diffusion (LSGM, LDM), where latent codes are decoded to images before computing structural distances.

## Method

SADM augments standard denoising score matching with a structural constraint: samples are projected into an embedding space via a trainable encoder, pairwise affinity matrices are computed for both ground-truth and predicted clean samples, and an L2 distance between these matrices is added to the loss. To prevent the denoiser from converging to trivial structural solutions, a novel comparison-based structure discriminator is introduced in a minimax game: the discriminator (encoder + distance metric) maximizes the structural distance between real and generated sample sets to find a more discriminative embedding, while the denoiser minimizes this distance to produce structurally faithful batches. The training alternates between a non-adversarial structural guidance phase (Eq. 6) and an adversarial phase (Eq. 8) with a time-dependent weighting η_t = 1/t. Theoretically, the approach is shown to be equivalent to a joint sample diffusion model conditioned on a relation variable, providing a variational upper bound on the negative log-likelihood of the joint sample distribution.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, SADM achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.58 (IS 298.46, Precision 0.86, Recall 0.66), outperforming the previous best MDT-G (FID 1.79) and DiT-G++ (FID 1.83). On ImageNet 512×512, SADM reaches FID 2.11 (IS 251.82), surpassing DiT-XL/2-G (FID 3.04) and SimpleDiffusion (FID 3.02). On CIFAR-10, SADM in data space achieves FID 1.54 (NLL 2.28), beating EDM-G++ (FID 1.77) and LSGM-G++ (FID 1.94). In cross-domain fine-tuning across 8 target datasets, SADM (full) achieves an average FID of 14.15, outperforming DiffFit (15.39) and full fine-tuning with DDPM (16.59).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SADM outperforms all 25 compared baselines across every dataset and resolution. On ImageNet 256×256, SADM (FID 1.58) improves over the closest competitors MDT-G (1.79) and DiT-G++ (1.83) by 0.21 and 0.25 FID respectively, while also achieving the highest IS (298.46) and best precision-recall balance (0.86/0.66). On ImageNet 512×512, the gap widens: SADM (2.11) beats DiT-XL/2-G (3.04) by nearly 1.0 FID and SimpleDiffusion (3.02) by 0.91 FID. On CIFAR-10, SADM in data space (FID 1.54, NLL 2.28) surpasses EDM-G++ (1.77) and LSGM-G++ (1.94), and in latent space (1.78) beats LSGM-G++ (1.94). In cross-domain fine-tuning, SADM (full, avg FID 14.15) and SADM (parameter-efficient, 14.27) both outperform DiffFit (15.39) and full fine-tuning with DDPM (16.59), with the largest margin on ArtBench-10 (18.23 vs. 20.87 for DiffFit), demonstrating strong out-of-distribution generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SADM extends the standard denoising score matching training of diffusion models (DDPM, score-based SDEs) by introducing a structural-level objective that captures pairwise manifold relationships within a batch, rather than optimizing per-instance denoising quality alone. It borrows the adversarial minimax paradigm from GANs but replaces the conventional classification-based discriminator with a novel comparison-based structure discriminator that operates on relational embeddings. The theoretical reinterpretation as a relation-conditioned joint sample diffusion process connects the method to conditional score matching, while the practical implementation builds on existing backbones (DiT, EDM, LSGM) without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structural perspective offers a principled way to improve diffusion model training that is orthogonal to and composable with existing weighting schemes and regularization, potentially becoming a standard component of future diffusion training pipelines. The consistent gains across image and latent diffusion architectures, as well as strong cross-domain fine-tuning results, suggest broad applicability. Open questions include whether the reliance on a frozen pre-trained feature extractor (Inception-V3) limits the method's effectiveness on out-of-domain or novel data, whether the adversarial component is necessary at scale, and how the approach extends to text-conditional or video generation where structural relationships are more complex.
