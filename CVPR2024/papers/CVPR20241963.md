## Problem

Dual-pixel sensors capture two slightly different views of a scene in a single shot, enabling passive snapshot depth estimation, but the defocus blur size is directly proportional to the disparity between views. This creates a fundamental trade-off: large disparity (needed for accurate depth) produces severe blur (hurting all-in-focus recovery), and existing DP deblurring methods are limited by the poor conditioning of the native DP defocus kernel, restricting usable depth-of-field.

## Input / Output

The method takes coded dual-pixel left and right images as input — 2 channels for the monochrome variant (CADNet-Mono) or 6 channels for the full RGB variant (CADNet-RGB, one pair per color channel). It produces a normalized defocus map in [-1, +1] (convertible to physical depth via known camera parameters) and a deblurred all-in-focus image (1 or 3 channels). The input images are already coded by the learned amplitude mask placed in the lens aperture plane, so the network never sees the mask explicitly; it only observes the resulting coded blur patterns.

## Method

CADS introduces a learned amplitude mask in the aperture plane of a dual-pixel camera, where the coded DP PSFs are formed as Hadamard (element-wise) products of the naive DP PSFs with the mask pattern. The mask is parameterized as a continuous-valued map passed through a sigmoid (with a temperature scalar for smooth optimization) and is jointly learned end-to-end with the reconstruction network CADNet, a U-Net with residual blocks and a pyramid pooling module at the bottleneck. Training uses a differentiable MPI-based forward simulator on FlyingThings3D scenes, with a composite loss combining L1 and gradient L1 terms for both AIF and defocus outputs, plus a heavy-weighted regularizer enforcing ≥50% light transmission through the mask. The key insight is that the learned code reshapes the DP blur kernel to be better conditioned for deblurring while preserving the disparity cue needed for depth, yielding >1.5 dB PSNR gain in AIF and 5–6% improvement in depth accuracy over naive DP sensing across f/4 to f/10 apertures.

## Results

On simulated FlyingThings3D scenes (20 cm depth range, f/4 aperture), CADS achieves 31.20 dB PSNR and 0.865 SSIM for all-in-focus (AIF) image recovery, and 5.15 mm MAE with 0.972 δ¹ accuracy for depth estimation. Compared to naive dual-pixel sensing, CADS improves AIF PSNR by 1.48 dB (31.20 vs. 29.72) and depth MAE by 0.36 mm (5.15 vs. 5.51). Across aperture sizes from f/4 to f/10, CADS consistently outperforms naive DP, coded SP, and naive SP on both depth and AIF metrics, with the largest AIF gains at wider apertures (e.g., +1.67 dB at f/4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADS outperforms all five existing dual-pixel baselines on the simulated FlyingThings3D dataset. On AIF PSNR, CADS (31.20 dB) surpasses DPDNet [2] (24.34 dB) by 6.86 dB, DDDNet [20] (21.35 dB) by 9.85 dB, and Xin et al. [32] (18.13 dB) by 13.07 dB; Punnappurath et al. [22] and Kim et al. [15] do not report AIF. On disparity estimation (AI(1) metric, lower is better), CADS (0.0177) dramatically outperforms Punnappurath et al. [22] (0.1940), Kim et al. [15] (0.2866), DDDNet [20] (0.2769), and Xin et al. [32] (0.3018), with the latter two designed for unidirectional disparity and thus degrading on bidirectional scenes. Even the naive DP variant of CADNet (without coded mask) achieves 29.72 dB AIF PSNR and 0.0190 AI(1), already exceeding all prior DP methods, indicating that the U-Net-based CADNet architecture itself is a strong baseline. The coded mask then provides an additional ~1.5 dB AIF gain and marginal depth improvement over naive DP, confirming the benefit of the learned aperture code.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: dual-pixel (DP) sensing for single-shot depth and deblurring (Abuolaim et al., Pan et al., Xin et al., Kim et al.) and coded aperture imaging for depth recovery (Levin et al., Shedligeri et al., Wu et al.). Prior DP works focus on post-processing the inherently ill-conditioned defocus blur, while prior coded aperture works design masks for standard single-pixel sensors. CADS is the first to jointly learn an amplitude mask specifically for the DP sensor geometry, using the Hadamard product structure of coded DP PSFs, and to do so in an end-to-end differentiable framework that simultaneously optimizes the mask pattern and the reconstruction network (CADNet, a U-Net variant with PPM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental trade-off in DP sensing—larger disparity improves depth accuracy but worsens deblurring—by showing that a learned coded mask can improve both simultaneously, enabling more practical passive snapshot 3D imaging in compact form factors. The proof-of-concept prototypes for 3D endoscopy and extended-DOF dermoscopy demonstrate relevance to medical imaging where size and passive operation are critical constraints. However, the amplitude mask imposes a ≥50% light-efficiency constraint that limits SNR, the depth range tested is relatively narrow (32–52 cm), and real-world results require per-hardware PSF calibration and fine-tuning. The authors note that replacing the amplitude mask with a phase mask could improve light efficiency and SNR, which remains an open direction.
