## Problem

Quantization-conditioned backdoors (QCBs) remain dormant in full-precision models but activate after standard quantization, evading existing detection and purification defenses. Detection methods fail because the backdoor is invisible before quantization, while purification defenses are infeasible on quantized models due to discrete values and poor gradient propagation. This paper presents the first practical defense specifically targeting QCBs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240328/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full-precision model (potentially compromised), a small unlabeled calibration set (1% of training data, no labels required), and a quantization scale s. It produces standard quantized model weights (8-bit or 4-bit integers) along with a learned per-neuron rounding strategy. Optimization proceeds layer-by-layer, requiring no information about subsequent layers, and completes in ~7 minutes for ResNet-18 on Tiny-ImageNet with a single GPU.

## Method

EFRAP's key insight is that QCB activation is driven by nearest rounding errors: neurons with larger truncation errors (|W − s·⌊W/s⌋|) are more correlated with backdoor effects. The method learns a non-nearest rounding strategy via three objectives: (1) an error-guided flipped rounding loss that minimizes cross-entropy between the learned rounding and the flipped nearest-rounding, weighted by per-neuron error norms; (2) an activation preservation loss derived from second-order Taylor expansion, approximated as MSE between full-precision and quantized layer activations; and (3) a quadratic penalty that drives continuous relaxation variables to binary values. The NP-hard binary optimization is solved via Lagrangian relaxation with continuous variables C ∈ [0,1], optimized layer-by-layer with Adam, requiring only unlabeled calibration data.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, EFRAP achieves ASR below 3% across all six attack settings (3 attacks × 8-bit/4-bit) while maintaining CDA within 1–4% of the no-defense baseline, yielding DTM scores of 89.79–95.72. On Tiny-ImageNet, EFRAP similarly reduces ASR to 0.50–4.25% with DTM of 75.23–78.12, outperforming all seven baselines. Notably, EFRAP requires only 1% clean unlabeled data (matching standard PTQ practice), whereas backdoor-defense baselines are given 5% clean labeled data. The hardest setting (4-bit PQBackdoor on CIFAR-10) sees EFRAP achieve CDA 85.16 / ASR 2.33 / DTM 89.79, compared to the next-best MCR at CDA 82.69 / ASR 66.10 / DTM 56.67.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EFRAP dominates all seven baselines on the most challenging setting (4-bit PQBackdoor): it achieves ASR 2.33% vs. MCR (66.10%), NAD (16.09% but CDA collapses to 37.58%), I-BAU (52.12%), and all three robust-quantization methods (ASR 32–99%). On 8-bit PQBackdoor, EFRAP (ASR 2.38%, DTM 91.63) outperforms MCR (ASR 78.14%), I-BAU (ASR 37.30%), and NAD (ASR 6.57% but CDA drops to 39.74%, DTM 66.14). Among backdoor defenses, FT and MCR handle the simpler CompArtifact and Qu-Anti-zation attacks well (ASR < 9%) but completely fail on PQBackdoor; FP preserves CDA but barely reduces ASR on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 99.86% ASR on 8-bit CompArtifact). Robust quantization baselines (OMSE, OCS, ACIQ) are inconsistent: OCS catastrophically destroys the network on Tiny-ImageNet 4-bit (CDA drops to 0.50–1.12%), while ACIQ and OMSE show high ASR (53–99%) on most settings. EFRAP is the only method that simultaneously achieves ASR < 3% and CDA within 4% of the no-defense baseline across all six settings on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the adaptive rounding insight from Nagel et al. (ICML 2020), who showed that nearest rounding is not always optimal for quantization, and redirects it toward a security objective: preventing quantization-conditioned backdoor activation. It departs from the existing backdoor defense literature (detection-based and purification-based methods like FinePruning, NAD, I-BAU) by operating entirely at the quantization step rather than modifying model weights, and from robust quantization methods (OMSE, OCS, ACIQ) by explicitly targeting backdoor neutralization rather than general accuracy preservation. The threat model builds directly on the QCB attack line (CompArtifact, Qu-Anti-zation, PQBackdoor), which established that standard quantization can be weaponized to activate dormant backdoors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical defense against quantization-conditioned backdoors, a growing supply-chain threat that exploits the standard PTQ workflow every practitioner uses. The method's practicality (1% unlabeled data, ~7 minutes on a single GPU, no retraining) makes it deployable alongside existing quantization pipelines. Open questions include whether the defense generalizes to other conditioned backdoor types (pruning, fine-tuning, multi-exit), whether it scales to very large models or non-CNN architectures, and whether more sophisticated adaptive attacks that encode backdoor functionality across multiple rounding patterns could circumvent the selective-flipping strategy.
