## Problem

Existing video anomaly understanding benchmarks focus almost exclusively on detecting and localizing anomalies, leaving the underlying cause and downstream effect of anomalous events largely unexplored. These benchmarks also lack proper multimodal evaluation metrics for free-text causal explanations and typically use very short clips (<30 s) that fail to capture the long-range temporal dependencies present in real-world anomalies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes ~117-second video clips (averaging 3,345 frames extracted at 60 FPS) paired with natural-language questions, and produces structured free-text outputs covering three dimensions: what anomaly occurred (type label + event description with timestamps), why it happened (causal explanation), and how severe it is (importance curve reflecting temporal severity). The dataset spans 42 anomaly subcategories across 11 domains, with each video annotated with 65.7 tokens across 4.3 sentences on average, and includes audio alongside the visual stream.

## Method

A-Guardian is a prompt-based method built on Video-ChatGPT (CLIP-L/14 encoder + Vicuna-7B initialized with LLaVA weights) that combines hard and soft prompts to address two challenges: capturing key cues in long videos and building a cause-effect logic chain. Hard prompts use a ChatGPT-assisted multi-turn dialog mechanism with instruction examples to iteratively focus the VLM on anomaly-relevant segments. Soft prompts employ a MIST-style selector that extracts CLIP patch-level features, pools them hierarchically to frame and segment representations, then applies cross-modal temporal attention with top-k selection to retrieve question-relevant video segments. During training, only the selector is finetuned with softmax cross-entropy loss while the VLM backbone remains frozen. The paper also introduces MMEval, a multimodal evaluation metric that uses Video-ChatGPT with task-specific natural-language prompts and importance-curve-guided dense sampling to score, rank, and explain model outputs from a video-aware perspective.

## Results

On the proposed CUVA benchmark, Video-ChatGPT + A-Guardian achieves the best MMEval scores on Description (79.65) and Cause (58.92), substantially outperforming the next-best baseline Video-ChatGPT (78.55 and 44.57 respectively). On the Effect task, PandaGPT leads with 69.45 MMEval, while A-Guardian scores 50.64. In the ablation study, removing the hard prompt drops Cause MMEval from 58.92 to 44.57, confirming that the multi-turn dialog mechanism is the primary driver of causal reasoning gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five VLM baselines (mPLUG-Owl, Video-LLaMA, PandaGPT, Otter, and Video-ChatGPT) against the proposed A-Guardian method on the CUVA benchmark. A-Guardian achieves the highest MMEval on Description (79.65 vs. 78.55 for Video-ChatGPT) and Cause (58.92 vs. 44.57 for Video-ChatGPT), with the Cause gap of ~14 points being the most dramatic improvement, indicating the hard-prompt multi-turn dialog is critical for causal reasoning. However, A-Guardian loses on the Effect task to PandaGPT (50.64 vs. 69.45 MMEval), suggesting PandaGPT's instruction-following design better captures downstream consequences. On traditional BLEURT, A-Guardian leads on Description (47.10) and Effect (48.28) but trails Video-ChatGPT on Cause (48.13 vs. 49.52), highlighting that text-only metrics fail to capture the multimodal causal reasoning that MMEval rewards. Otter performs worst on Cause (3.53 MMEval) despite competitive BLEU scores, illustrating the paper's argument that traditional metrics cannot distinguish semantically incorrect answers from correct ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Video-ChatGPT as its foundation VLM and borrows the cross-modal temporal attention and top-k selection mechanism from MIST for its soft-prompt component, while following Jin et al.'s prompt-based learning paradigm for combining hard and soft prompts. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant line of video anomaly understanding (VAU) research—which has focused almost exclusively on detection and temporal/spatial localization with frame- or pixel-level annotations—by introducing free-text causation reasoning (what, why, how) as the primary evaluation target. The MMEval metric extends the evaluation paradigm from unimodal text metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, BLEURT) to a multimodal VLM-based assessment, positioning itself against the growing family of GPT-based evaluation metrics that still operate on a single modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CUVA fills a clear practical gap: real-world anomaly response requires understanding not just *when* something went wrong but *why* and *how severe*, yet no prior benchmark captured this. The finding that all tested VLMs score dramatically lower on the Cause task (16–59 MMEval) than on Description (65–79) highlights a significant and persistent reasoning gap in current video-language models. Open questions include whether the importance-curve annotation (relying on CLIP similarity and ChatGPT ranking) is sufficiently reliable for downstream tasks, whether the 1,000-video scale is adequate for robust benchmarking, and whether the prompt-based A-Guardian approach generalizes beyond the Video-ChatGPT backbone. The benchmark is likely to become a reference point for evaluating causal reasoning in video understanding.
