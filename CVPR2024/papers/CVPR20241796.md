## Problem

Non-rigid Structure-from-Motion (NRSfM) suffers from two persistent issues: (1) the inherent rotation ambiguity in the factorization framework is typically resolved either by explicit camera motion recovery with extra constraints or by General Procrustean Analysis (GPA), which requires a reference mean shape and overpenalizes non-isotropic deformations; (2) global low-rank modeling of the 3D shape sequence overpenalizes drastic spatially-variant deformations, degrading reconstruction accuracy in regions with severe local motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241796/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D keypoint observation sequence W ∈ R^{2F×P} (F frames, P points) under orthographic projection (scale and translation removed) and produces a 3D deforming shape sequence in a canonical coordinate Ŝ ∈ R^{3F×P} along with per-frame camera rotation matrices Q_i ∈ SO(3). Missing keypoints are handled by restricting the reprojection data term to visible points only. The entire pipeline is optimization-based (ADMM) with no training data, requiring only a BMM-style initialization of camera motion and shape.

## Method

The paper proposes a unified spatial-temporal framework with two key modules. First, a Temporally-smooth Procrustean Alignment (TPA) module replaces GPA by minimizing the Frobenius distance between rotated consecutive 3D shapes (‖Q_i S̃_i − Q_{i+1} S̃_{i+1}‖²_F), exploiting temporal smoothness to correct camera rotation without needing a reference mean shape. Second, a Spatial-Weighted Nuclear Norm (SWNN) module segments the object into nearly-rigid and non-rigid regions via DFT-based trajectory frequency analysis, then constructs proxy shapes through a kernel-based weight matrix Λ that collapses non-rigid points toward a super-point, thereby relaxing the low-rank constraint adaptively where deformation is severe. The complete model (reprojection + SWNN + TPA smoothness) is solved via ADMM with alternating updates of shape, proxy shape, and correction rotations.

## Results

On the MoCap benchmark (8 sequences), the proposed method achieves the best or second-best 3D reconstruction error (e_3d) across all sequences, with notable wins on Drink (0.0031 vs. 0.0037 for PND), Yoga (0.0109 vs. 0.0128 for PMP), and Shark (0.0258 vs. 0.0551 for R-BMM). On the NRSfM Challenge dataset, the method achieves the lowest reconstruction error (in mm) on Articul. (10.69), Stretch (5.43), and Tearing (10.77), while trailing OPM on Balloon (7.28 vs. 6.29) and BP on Paper (7.91 vs. 6.70). On the H3WB whole-body dataset, the method achieves a mean e_3d of 0.1857, outperforming R-BMM (0.2054) and BMM (0.2143).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the matrix factorization baselines (BMM, R-BMM, OPM), the proposed method consistently outperforms on MoCap sequences with severe deformation: Shark (0.0258 vs. 0.0551 for R-BMM, 0.0550 for OPM), Walking (0.0710 vs. 0.0882 for R-BMM, 0.0816 for OPM), and Dance (0.0921 vs. 0.1491 for R-BMM). Against the Procrustean alignment baselines (PND, PMP, PR), the method wins on Yoga (0.0109 vs. 0.0128 PMP), Stretch (0.0114 vs. 0.0124 PMP), and Face (0.0144 vs. 0.0164 PR), though PMP remains slightly better on Drink (0.0018 vs. 0.0031). On the NRSfM Challenge, the method beats OPM on Articul. (10.69 vs. 12.18 mm), Stretch (5.43 vs. 6.36 mm), and Tearing (10.77 vs. 10.91 mm), but loses to OPM on Balloon (7.28 vs. 6.29 mm) and to BP on Paper (7.91 vs. 6.70 mm). On H3WB, the method achieves the best mean e_3d of 0.1857, beating R-BMM (0.2054) by ~10% and BMM (0.2143) by ~13%, with the largest margin on Waiting2 (0.1097 vs. 0.1478 for R-BMM). The older factorization methods (CSF1, CSF2, KSTA) are substantially outperformed across all datasets, with errors typically 2–20× higher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two historically separate NRSfM paradigms—matrix factorization (BMM, R-BMM, OPM) and Procrustean alignment (PND, PMP, PR)—into a single optimization framework. It departs from GPA-based alignment by replacing the reference-shape-dependent Procrustean step with a temporally-smooth consecutive-frame alignment (TPA), and from global low-rank modeling by introducing a spatially-weighted nuclear norm that relaxes the constraint in high-deformation regions identified via DFT-based trajectory segmentation. The rectification algorithm from R-BMM [26] and the factorization initialization from BMM [11] are retained as building blocks, but the core contribution is the spatial-temporal coupling that neither family previously exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent practical failures of optimization-based NRSfM: rotation ambiguity corrupting shape recovery, and global low-rank over-penalization of locally drastic deformations (e.g., a talking mouth vs. a static nose). The TPA module's robustness to large camera-motion perturbations (Table 6) suggests it could serve as a drop-in correction step for existing factorization pipelines. However, the method remains purely optimization-based with heuristic DFT segmentation, and the authors themselves note that more accurate trajectory partitioning and explicit complex-deformation modeling remain open. For practitioners, the unified ADMM framework is a clean engineering target, but the computational cost on semi-dense data (Table 3) and the sensitivity of SWNN to the α_r hyperparameter limit immediate broad adoption.
