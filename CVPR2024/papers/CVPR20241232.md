## Problem

Current vectorized HD map construction frameworks rely on sparse deformable attention to regress lane point positions, which leads to coarse localization and loss of fine-grained structural details—especially for irregular boundaries and sudden corner changes. The subtle and sparse nature of map annotations makes it difficult for these detection-based approaches to identify relevant feature scopes and preserve detailed lane shapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241232/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surround-view RGB images (nuScenes) or 7 (Argoverse2) and optionally 32-beam LiDAR point clouds, projected into a 200×100 BEV grid covering a 60m×30m perception range. It outputs up to 50 lane instances, each represented as an ordered sequence of 20 2D points with a class label (lane-divider, ped-crossing, or road boundary), alongside auxiliary instance segmentation masks and a binary lane mask used during training.

## Method

MGMap introduces learned masks at two granularities to guide vectorized map construction. At the instance level, a Mask-Activated Instance (MAI) decoder dynamically initializes lane queries by multiplying learned instance masks with enhanced multi-scale BEV features (produced by a 3-layer Enhanced Multi-Level neck using CBAM), then iteratively refines them through a cascaded multi-scale deformable transformer decoder. At the point level, a Position-Guided Mask Patch Refinement (PG-MPR) module extracts dense local patch features via ROIAlign from a binary-mask-augmented feature map centered on each predicted point, then refines coordinates and class scores through multi-head attention over these patches. This two-level mask guidance replaces the purely sparse sampling of standard deformable attention with dense, region-specific feature extraction, enabling precise localization of detailed lane structures.

## Results

On nuScenes validation (60m×30m), MGMap achieves 61.4 mAP (AP_chamfer) with camera input (R50, 30 epochs), outperforming the MapTR baseline by 10.3 mAP. Under camera-LiDAR fusion (R50&Sec, 24 epochs), MGMap reaches 71.7 mAP, and with LiDAR alone (Sec, 24 epochs) it achieves 67.9 mAP. On Argoverse2, MGMap obtains 62.8 mAP, a 5.4 mAP gain over MapTR. The method also reports 37.2 AP_raster (camera, 30 epochs) and 53.8 AP_raster (fusion, 24 epochs), demonstrating consistent gains across both point-level and instance-level evaluation metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGMap consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every modality and training budget. Against the primary baseline MapTR [23], MGMap gains +10.3 mAP (camera, 30 ep), +7.8 mAP (camera, 110 ep), +12.3 mAP (LiDAR, 24 ep), and +9.2 mAP (fusion, 24 ep). Against the strongest camera-only competitors at 30 epochs—BeMapNet [32] (59.8) and PivotNet [9] (57.4)—MGMap leads by 1.6 and 4.0 mAP respectively, while at 110 epochs it surpasses BeMapNet (64.8) by 1.7 mAP. MGMap also beats MapVR [49] by 10.2 mAP (camera, 30 ep) and 8.2 mAP (fusion, 24 ep), and substantially outperforms earlier methods: VectorMapNet [26] (+15.4 mAP), InstaGraM [39] (+19.7 mAP), and HDMapNet [19] (+38.4 mAP). On Argoverse2, MGMap (62.8 mAP) surpasses MapTR (57.4) by 5.4 mAP, VectorMapNet (42.7) by 20.1 mAP, and HDMapNet (18.8) by 44.0 mAP, confirming cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGMap extends the vectorized online HD map construction paradigm established by MapTR, which represents map elements as fixed-length point sequences decoded by a cascaded deformable transformer. It inherits BEVFormer's PV-to-BEV feature transformation and Deformable DETR's multi-scale attention structure, but departs from the purely sparse attention-based feature extraction by introducing learned masks at two granularities: instance-level (MAI decoder) to inject shape priors into lane queries, and point-level (PG-MPR) to refine individual point positions via dense local patch features. The Enhanced Multi-Level Neck with hybrid channel-spatial attention is a novel architectural addition that constructs multi-scale BEV features in BEV space rather than PV space, a design choice validated by ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10 mAP improvement over MapTR across camera, LiDAR, and fusion modalities, along with consistent gains under enlarged perception ranges, demonstrates that mask-guided refinement is a broadly applicable strategy for sparse-target vectorization tasks. The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of deformable attention (under-sampling for sparse, irregular targets) and provides a principled remedy, which could inform other sparse-prediction tasks beyond HD maps. Open questions include whether the mask supervision overhead scales to denser map elements, whether the approach transfers to 3D map construction, and how it interacts with temporal fusion for dynamic scene understanding.
