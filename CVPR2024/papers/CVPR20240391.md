## Problem

Stacked structures in deep networks (e.g., Transformer encoders/decoders, ResNet blocks) replicate identical architectures with independently trained parameters, causing parameter counts to scale linearly with depth. While this stacking is essential for model capacity, it creates a substantial parameter overhead that hinders practical deployment, especially as models grow to dozens of layers. The paper asks whether the commonality inherent in stacked modules can be exploited to share most parameters while retaining only a small per-layer unique component.

## Input / Output

The method is applied to the decoder of AdaMixer, a query-based object detector. The decoder receives object queries (vectors of dimension d_q=256) and sampled image features (P_in×C tensors from group sampling over the backbone feature map), and produces refined queries that are decoded into bounding boxes and class labels. LORS specifically targets the three parameter-heavy linear transformations within each decoder layer—Adaptive Channel Mixing (M_c), Adaptive Spatial Mixing (M_s), and the output projection (L_output)—replacing their full per-layer weights with shared-plus-low-rank-private decompositions.

## Method

LORS decomposes each stacked layer's weight matrix W_i into a cross-layer shared component W^shared and a layer-specific private component W_i^private computed as a sum of K low-rank products B_ik·A_ik (with r ≪ d,h). Two variants handle different parameter types: LORS^S for static weights (e.g., L_output) and LORS^A for adaptive weights generated from the object query (e.g., M_c, M_s), where the private component additionally includes a query-dependent low-rank matrix Ê_ik = Linear(q) ∈ R^{r×r} sandwiched between B and A. Unlike LoRA, which fine-tunes a frozen pretrained weight with a low-rank delta, LORS trains the shared and private components jointly from scratch, making it a structural design choice rather than an adaptation technique. The grouped low-rank mechanism (varying K per layer) avoids rank bottlenecks while keeping parameter growth linear in K rather than quadratic in r. Applied to AdaMixer's 6-layer decoder, LORS reduces decoder parameters by ~70% (110M→35M) while matching or exceeding the original AP on COCO.

## Results

Under the 3× training scheme (36 epochs) on COCO 2017 val, AdaMixer+LORS consistently outperforms vanilla AdaMixer across all backbone and query configurations while reducing total parameters by ~50–70%. With ResNet-50 and 300 queries, LORS achieves 47.6 AP vs. 47.0 AP (+0.6) while cutting parameters from 139M to 60M. With the stronger Swin-S backbone and 300 queries, LORS reaches 51.8 AP vs. 51.3 AP (+0.5) with parameters halved from 164M to 85M. Under the lighter 1× scheme (12 epochs), LORS matches or slightly trails the baseline (42.6 vs. 42.7 AP at 100 queries) while still reducing decoder parameters by ~70% (35M vs. 110M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is AdaMixer [Gao et al., CVPR 2022], the query-based detector whose stacked decoders LORS modifies. Under the 3× training scheme, AdaMixer+LORS wins on every configuration tested: +0.5 AP with R-50/100 queries, +0.6 AP with R-50/300 queries, +0.2 AP with R-101/300 queries, and +0.5 AP with Swin-S/300 queries, while reducing total parameters by 50–70%. The gains are consistent across all sub-metrics (AP50, AP75, APs, APm, APl), with the largest improvements on small objects (APs: +1.0 with Swin-S). Under the 1× scheme, LORS is essentially a tie at 300 queries (44.1 vs. 44.1 AP) and marginally behind at 100 queries (42.6 vs. 42.7 AP), but still achieves a ~70% decoder parameter reduction. The authors attribute the 3× advantage to LORS acting as an implicit low-rank regularizer that benefits from longer training to fully exploit the shared-parameter structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

LORS repurposes the low-rank decomposition mechanism from LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) and its variants (AdaLoRA, DyLoRA, VeRA, etc.) in a fundamentally different way: rather than adapting frozen pretrained weights via a low-rank delta, LORS trains a shared full-rank matrix plus per-layer low-rank private matrices entirely from scratch to form parameter-efficient stacked architectures. This positions it closer to the parameter-sharing literature (e.g., ALBERT) but with a structured residual decomposition that preserves per-layer expressiveness. The experimental vehicle is AdaMixer (Gao et al., CVPR 2022), a query-based detector whose adaptive channel and spatial mixing modules contain large per-layer linear parameters that LORS targets for compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 70% decoder parameter reduction while matching or slightly exceeding baseline AP is practically significant for deployment of query-based detectors, and the observation that the low-rank constraint acts as beneficial regularization (improving performance with longer training) offers a conceptual insight into why stacked layers need less capacity than assumed. However, the method is validated only on AdaMixer's decoders on COCO, inference speed slightly increases due to redundant computations, and the performance gains over the vanilla model are modest (≤0.6 AP). Open questions include whether LORS generalizes to NLP transformers, backbone networks, and whether the inference overhead can be eliminated through kernel fusion.
