## Problem

Systematic high-confidence misclassifications on out-of-distribution subgroups are inherently difficult to detect because no training or test data covers them, and existing prompt-based search methods are limited to pre-defined attribute templates. Additionally, visualizing the semantic meaning of individual neurons or generating visual counterfactual explanations for image classifiers is extremely challenging because direct pixel-space optimization produces adversarial samples rather than natural-looking images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242636/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes one or two image classifiers (any differentiable architecture), a target class name, and optionally an input image (for VCEs) or a neuron index (for neuron visualization). It produces realistic, natural-looking images at the resolution supported by the underlying Stable Diffusion model (512×512) that optimize a classifier-derived loss. Unlike adversarial example methods, the outputs lie on the natural image manifold approximated by the diffusion model, and unlike prior VCE methods, no dataset-specific robust classifier or generative model is required.

## Method

DiG-IN exploits the fact that DDIM sampling is deterministic, making the entire diffusion process a differentiable function of the initial latent z_T, per-timestep text conditionings C_t, and null-text tokens ∅_t. This allows formulating any explanation task (classifier disagreement, VCE generation, neuron maximization/minimization) as a single optimization problem over these variables, with gradients backpropagated through the full denoising chain using gradient checkpointing. For VCEs, initialization combines Null-Text Inversion for image reconstruction with a modified Prompt-to-Prompt step that swaps the class name in the caption to inject target-class structure, followed by joint optimization of classifier confidence and a foreground-aware L2 distance (computed via HQ-SAM segmentation masks in both VAE-latent and pixel space) that penalizes background changes while allowing large foreground edits. The framework is entirely plug-and-play: no retraining of the diffusion model is needed, and it works with any vision classifier on any natural-image dataset.

## Results

The primary quantitative evaluation is a user study on ImageNet VCEs (Table 1), where DiG-IN UVCEs are rated as more realistic (76.0% vs 40.4% for DVCEs), showing better target-class features (81.3% vs 63.7%), and changing mainly the class object (89.1% vs 73.8%). UVCEs are preferred over DVCEs in 59.5% of cases (vs 18.1% for DVCEs, 22.5% equal). Qualitatively, the framework validates the shape bias of adversarially robust ViT-S models, identifies systematic zero-shot CLIP errors (e.g., 14% of 'viaduct' validation images misclassified as 'steel arch bridge'), and produces interpretable neuron visualizations for SE-ResNet-D152 and spurious-feature counterfactuals for ResNet-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DVCE [5] (the prior state-of-the-art diffusion-based VCE method), DiG-IN UVCEs win decisively on all user-study metrics: realism improves from 40.4% to 76.0%, target-class feature quality from 63.7% to 81.3%, and foreground-only editing from 73.8% to 89.1%. UVCEs are preferred in 59.5% of pairwise comparisons versus 18.1% for DVCEs. Beyond quality, UVCEs are strictly more versatile: they require no adversarially robust classifier and no dataset-specific diffusion model, working on ImageNet, Cars, CUB, Food-101, and FFHQ out of the box. Against the feature-attack method of Singla & Feizi [76] for neuron visualization, DiG-IN produces realistic images that clearly convey the semantic concept (e.g., 'sand' for a fiddler-crab spurious neuron) while the feature attack yields extreme, artificial patterns that reduce class confidence; DiG-IN instead preserves the class object and reveals dramatic confidence shifts tied to the spurious feature, making the harmful-spurious-feature interpretation far more actionable.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiG-IN unifies several interpretability tasks (classifier disagreement, visual counterfactual explanations, neuron visualization) under a single optimization-over-diffusion-process framework, building directly on Stable Diffusion's latent space, the deterministic DDIM solver, Null-Text Inversion for image-to-image initialization, and Prompt-to-Prompt for structure-preserving editing. It departs from prior systematic-error detection methods (HIBug, 3DB, ModelDiff) that rely on fixed prompt templates or predefined attribute sets by instead optimizing freely over the latent and conditioning variables, removing the need for manual prompt engineering. For VCEs specifically, it removes the requirement of a dataset-specific adversarially robust classifier that prior methods like DVCE [5] depend on, and for neuron visualization it replaces the unrealistic feature-attack images of [76] with diffusion-generated photorealistic examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the framework means any differentiable classifier can be probed without retraining the generative model, making it broadly applicable across architectures and datasets. The discovery that zero-shot CLIP systematically misclassifies images matching only parts of a compound class name (e.g., 'waffle' → 'waffle iron') reveals a concrete, text-embedding-driven failure mode with practical implications for deploying CLIP in production. Key limitations include the dependence on the diffusion model's coverage of the natural image manifold (failure modes outside its training distribution remain invisible) and the computational cost of backpropagating through the full diffusion chain, which requires gradient checkpointing. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or 3D classifiers and whether the optimization landscape becomes intractable for very deep or multi-modal models.
