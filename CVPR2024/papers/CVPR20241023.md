## Problem

NeRF-based novel view synthesis assumes static scenes, but real-world captures inevitably contain transient distractors (pedestrians, vehicles, shadows) that corrupt 3D geometry. Existing remedies either require prior knowledge of distractor classes or temporal ordering (segmentation-based) or rely on hand-crafted heuristics that produce imprecise, blurry boundaries (heuristics-based). No prior method achieves accurate, boundary-sharp static/transient separation without any external priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes unordered multi-view RGB images of a static scene containing transient distractors (no temporal ordering or semantic labels required) and produces per-image binary static maps (pixel value 1 = static, 0 = transient) that serve as loss weights during NeRF training. The static maps are generated by prompting SAM with SfM feature points and filtering its segmentation masks against heuristic cues, yielding sharp object boundaries rather than the soft/ambiguous masks produced by prior heuristics. The final trained NeRF (Nerfacto or Mip-NeRF 360) then renders novel views of the clean static scene.

## Method

HuGS combines two complementary heuristics to produce rough static cues, then refines them into precise masks via SAM. The SfM-based heuristic (using COLMAP/SIFT) identifies static objects with high-frequency textures by thresholding the cross-view matching frequency n_i^j/N_I of each feature point; the color-residual heuristic from a partially trained Nerfacto (a few thousand iterations) identifies static objects with low-frequency textures via low reconstruction error. The two heuristics are unioned and then intersected with a high-quantile residual upper bound to eliminate false positives. These combined cues guide SAM: SfM feature points serve as point prompts, and only SAM masks whose overlap with the heuristic region exceeds a threshold T_m are retained, producing the final binary static map. This map weights the NeRF reconstruction loss (Eq. 4), effectively excluding transient pixels from training while preserving all static geometry.

## Results

On the Kubric dataset, Ours (Mip-NeRF 360) achieves the best average PSNR of 41.21 dB, SSIM of 0.975, and LPIPS of 0.032, outperforming the previous best (RobustNeRF at 39.64 dB PSNR) by 1.57 dB. On the Distractor dataset, Ours (Mip-NeRF 360) leads with 27.29 dB PSNR and 0.832 SSIM, surpassing RobustNeRF (26.47 dB) by 0.82 dB. On the Phototourism dataset, Ours (Mip-NeRF 360) achieves 24.43 dB PSNR, the highest among all methods. For segmentation quality on Kubric, HuGS achieves 0.944 mIoU and 0.934 F1, substantially exceeding the best prior method (D2NeRF at 0.812 mIoU) and even prior-knowledge-based models like DINO (0.789 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ours (Mip-NeRF 360) outperforms all baselines on average across all three datasets. On Kubric, it beats RobustNeRF (the strongest prior method) by 1.57 dB PSNR and 0.006 SSIM, and HA-NeRF by 8.36 dB PSNR. On the Distractor dataset, Ours (Mip-NeRF 360) leads RobustNeRF by 0.82 dB PSNR, while Ours (Nerfacto) also surpasses RobustNeRF by 0.81 dB. On Phototourism, the gap narrows (Ours Mip-NeRF 360: 24.43 vs. RobustNeRF: 23.73 dB) because the landmark main bodies are less affected by transient distractors, but Ours still wins. For segmentation on Kubric, HuGS achieves 0.944 mIoU / 0.934 F1, dramatically outperforming heuristics-only methods (RobustNeRF: 0.743/0.647, HA-NeRF: 0.811/0.742) and even prior-knowledge-based models (DINO: 0.789/0.695, Grounded-SAM: 0.747/0.631), confirming that the heuristics-guided segmentation paradigm surpasses both pure heuristics and pure segmentation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a middle ground between two established paradigms for handling transient distractors in NeRF: segmentation-based methods (which require semantic priors or temporal ordering) and heuristics-based methods (NeRF-W, RobustNeRF, HA-NeRF, which are prior-free but imprecise). The HuGS paradigm repurposes hand-crafted heuristics not as the final discriminator but as rough point/region prompts to guide SAM's segmentation, effectively using heuristics for what they do well (coarse localization) and SAM for what it does well (sharp boundaries). The novel SfM-based heuristic—using feature-point matching frequency across views as a temporal 'minority' signal—repurposes a standard SfM byproduct (COLMAP/SIFT matching counts) in a way not previously exploited for NeRF distractor removal, complementing the spatial-area-based heuristics of NeRF-W and RobustNeRF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, prior-free pipeline that eliminates the need for manual pixel-level annotation of transient objects, which is a significant bottleneck in real-world NeRF deployment (e.g., cultural heritage, outdoor scenes). The complementary SfM + color-residual heuristic design robustly covers both high- and low-frequency textures, addressing a known failure mode of prior heuristics. Limitations include per-dataset threshold tuning (T_SfM, T_CR) and reduced gains when distractors minimally overlap the main scene content (as in Phototourism). Open questions include scalability to very large or highly dynamic scenes, whether the approach transfers to 3D Gaussian Splatting pipelines, and whether the partially-trained Nerfacto step can be replaced by cheaper alternatives.
