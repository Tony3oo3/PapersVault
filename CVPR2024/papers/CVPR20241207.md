## Problem

Existing diffusion-based pose-guided person image synthesis methods simply align source appearance to the target pose via cross-attention without any high-level semantic understanding of the person image, making them prone to overfitting and producing distorted outputs for extreme or rare poses. Additionally, the standard text-to-image diffusion training paradigm requires labor-intensive image-caption pairs and introduces inherent ambiguity due to the information-density gap between language and vision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241207/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source person RGB image (resized to 256×256) and a 2D OpenPose target pose as input, producing a pose-transferred person image at 256×176 or 512×352 resolution. No textual prompts or image-caption pairs are required; conditioning is purely image-based. The source image is encoded by a Swin-B backbone into four multi-scale feature maps that feed both the coarse-grained prompt decoder and the fine-grained attention module, while the target pose is processed by a lightweight T2I-Adapter-style pose encoder.

## Method

CFLD decouples fine-grained appearance and pose controls at different stages of the UNet to prevent overfitting. A Perception-Refined Decoder (PRD) progressively refines a set of randomly initialized learnable queries via cross-attention with the source image's last-scale features, producing a coarse-grained prompt that captures person-specific semantics (body parts, clothing, age, gender) and serves as the conditional embedding in both down-sampling and up-sampling blocks. A Hybrid-Granularity Attention (HGA) module then biases the cross-attention queries in up-sampling blocks with multi-scale fine-grained appearance features from the source encoder, following a coarse-to-fine curriculum enabled by zero-convolution gating. The pose adapter is added only to down-sampling blocks, so the HGA learns pose-irrelevant texture details exclusively during up-sampling. Only 1.2% of the pre-trained Stable Diffusion parameters (W_k and W_v projections in up-sampling blocks) are trainable, and training uses a combined MSE denoising loss plus a source-to-source self-reconstruction loss over 100 epochs.

## Results

On the DeepFashion benchmark at 256×176 resolution, CFLD achieves FID 6.804, LPIPS 0.1519, SSIM 0.7378, and PSNR 18.235, outperforming all GAN-based and diffusion-based baselines on LPIPS, SSIM, and PSNR. At 512×352 resolution, CFLD reaches FID 7.149, LPIPS 0.1819, SSIM 0.7478, and PSNR 17.645, again leading on all metrics. In the user study, CFLD achieves a Jab score of 52.5%, exceeding the second-place method by +34.9 points, and over half of its generated images are recognized as real (G2R). CFLD uses only 248.2M trainable parameters with 2D OpenPose annotations, compared to 688.0M for PIDM and 395.9M for PoCoLD (which requires 3D DensePose).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFLD outperforms all GAN-based baselines (PATN, ADGAN, GFLA, PISE, SPGNet, DPTN, NTED, CASD) across every metric at both resolutions, with the closest GAN competitor NTED trailing by 1.7 FID, 0.025 LPIPS, and 0.022 SSIM at 256×176. Against the two diffusion-based methods, CFLD beats PoCoLD [9] by 1.26 FID, 0.012 LPIPS, and 0.007 SSIM at 256×176, while using only 2D OpenPose instead of PoCoLD's 3D DensePose annotations and 147.7M fewer trainable parameters. Compared to PIDM [1], CFLD achieves a slightly higher FID (6.804 vs. 6.440 for the authors' released images) but substantially better LPIPS (0.1519 vs. 0.1686), SSIM (0.7378 vs. 0.7109), and PSNR (18.235 vs. 17.399), indicating superior perceptual quality and structural fidelity. The paper notes that PIDM's own checkpoint reproduces with severe overfitting, widening the gap further. In the user study, CFLD's Jab score of 52.5% exceeds the runner-up by +34.9 points, and its G2R rate (over 50% of generated images judged as real) confirms strong perceptual realism over all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and the T2I-Adapter for lightweight pose conditioning, but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based PGPIS methods (PIDM, PoCoLD) that inject multi-scale source appearance features directly as cross-attention conditionings at every UNet scale. The key conceptual departure is a coarse-to-fine decoupling: a perception-refined decoder extracts a small set of learnable queries as a semantic 'prompt' (analogous to text conditioning but learned purely from images), while a hybrid-granularity attention module injects fine-grained texture details only as query biases in the up-sampling path. This eliminates the need for image-caption pairs and avoids the overfitting that arises when pose and appearance controls are entangled at all scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The coarse-to-fine decoupling offers a practical recipe for reducing overfitting in conditional diffusion models more broadly: separate high-level semantic guidance (down-sampling) from low-level texture injection (up-sampling), and use zero-convolution gating to let the semantic pathway converge before fine details are introduced. Limitations include evaluation on a single fashion-domain dataset (DeepFashion), reliance on a frozen pre-trained SD backbone, and the need for a Swin-B encoder (87M params) that adds inference cost. The authors note potential extensions to person re-identification and domain adaptation, but the approach's transferability to other domains or tasks remains untested.
