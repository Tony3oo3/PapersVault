## Problem

Existing NeRF-based methods for autonomous driving suffer from long training times (hours to days), require dense semantic supervision, or fail to generalize across datasets and 360° multicamera rigs. They also lack realistic lidar sensor modeling—specifically ray dropping, beam divergence, and rolling shutter effects—which is critical for closing the real-to-sim gap in closed-loop simulation and data augmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241784/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed camera images, lidar point clouds (including rays that returned no points), and per-actor 3D bounding boxes with SO(3) poses. It renders a lower-resolution volumetric feature map per ray, which is then decoded by a CNN into full-resolution RGB images, while separate small MLPs predict per-ray lidar depth, intensity, and ray-drop probability. The unified representation supports editing ego-vehicle pose, actor trajectories, or adding/removing actors after training.

## Method

NeuRAD uses a single unified neural feature field (NFF) where static background and dynamic actors share all network weights and are distinguished solely by an actor-aware 4D multiresolution hash grid (the fourth dimension encodes the actor index), enabling parallel sampling of all actors. For anti-aliasing in multiscale scenes, each ray is modeled as a pyramid frustum whose cross-sectional area grows with distance, and hash-grid features are downweighted by the ratio of cell size to frustum volume—avoiding the costly multisampling of Zip-NeRF. Sensor realism is achieved through per-ray time assignment for rolling shutter, learned ray-drop probability via a small MLP on rendered features, and per-sensor appearance embeddings applied post-rendering. Two rounds of proposal sampling with a lightweight NFF refine sample locations along each ray, and the SDF-based opacity is trained jointly with camera (patch-wise L2 + VGG perceptual) and lidar (depth, intensity, ray-drop BCE, weight decay) losses in ~1 hour on a single A100.

## Results

NeuRAD achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis across all five evaluated datasets using a single set of hyperparameters. On PandaSet front-camera, NeuRAD reaches 26.58 PSNR / 0.778 SSIM / 0.190 LPIPS, outperforming UniSim (25.63/0.745/0.288) and Instant-NGP (24.03/0.708/0.451). On KITTI, NeuRAD achieves 27.00 PSNR and 0.082 LPIPS, a large margin over MARS (24.00/0.164) and SUDS (23.12/0.135). The larger NeuRAD-2x variant further improves results, reaching 30.59 PSNR on ZOD and 27.91 PSNR on KITTI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuRAD outperforms UniSim [42] on every dataset and metric where they overlap: on Panda FC, PSNR improves from 25.63 to 26.58 and LPIPS drops from 0.288 to 0.190; on the 360° setting, PSNR jumps from 23.50 to 25.97. Against S-NeRF [41] on nuScenes, NeuRAD wins on PSNR (26.99 vs 26.21) and LPIPS (0.225 vs 0.228) but trails slightly on SSIM (0.815 vs 0.831), while training in ~1 hour versus S-NeRF's 17 hours. On KITTI, NeuRAD surpasses MARS [39] by 3.0 dB PSNR (27.00 vs 24.00) and SUDS [31] by 3.9 dB (27.00 vs 23.12), with LPIPS more than halved. Compared to Instant-NGP [24] on Panda FC, NeuRAD gains 2.55 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 0.26. On Argoverse 2 and ZOD, where no prior dynamic-actor method exists, NeuRAD improves over the UniSim reimplementation by 3.0 dB and 1.5 dB PSNR respectively, confirming generalizability without dataset-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuRAD builds directly on the Instant-NGP hash-grid encoding [24] and Zip-NeRF's anti-aliasing and proposal-sampling strategies [4], adapting them to the dynamic, multi-scale setting of autonomous driving. It departs from prior AD-specific NeRF methods (NSG, S-NeRF, UniSim, MARS) by unifying static background and all dynamic actors into a single neural feature field differentiated only by an actor-aware 4D hash grid, rather than maintaining separate networks per scene element. The paper also extends the NVS-for-AD task by jointly modeling camera and lidar sensor characteristics—rolling shutter, beam divergence, and ray dropping—in a unified framework, whereas prior methods either ignored these effects or handled only a subset (e.g., UniSim's front-camera-only setting).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuRAD provides a practical, open-source (Nerfstudio-based) pipeline that trains in ~1 hour on a single A100 and achieves SOTA across five diverse AD datasets with no per-dataset tuning, making NeRF-based closed-loop simulation and data augmentation far more accessible than prior day-long training approaches. The unified sensor modeling (especially ray dropping and rolling shutter) narrows the sim-to-real gap for lidar, which is critical for downstream AD testing. Key limitations remain: actors are assumed rigid (no deformation), and the method's assumptions break down in harsh weather (heavy rain, snow), leaving open questions about generalization to deformable objects and adverse conditions.
