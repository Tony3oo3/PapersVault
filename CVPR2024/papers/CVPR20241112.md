## Problem

Snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) reconstruction of multispectral images remains ill-posed, with existing methods failing to recover details in inadequately illuminated regions and sharp edges, and supervised models suffering from train-test distribution gaps on real data. No prior work has leveraged the generative capacity of diffusion models for multispectral SCI, largely because pre-trained diffusion models operate on 3-channel RGB images while MSIs span dozens of spectral bands with different wavelength ranges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241112/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D CASSI measurement of size H×(W+d×(B−1)) (e.g., 256×283 for 28 bands with shift step d=2) and the corresponding coded aperture mask Φ as input, and produces a full 3D multispectral image of size H×W×B (256×256×28 for simulation; 660×660×28 cropped to 256×256 patches for real data). The pre-trained diffusion model is a 3-channel RGB denoiser, so each MSI band must be grouped into overlapping 3-band triplets before being fed to the model, and the output band is extracted from the center channel of the denoised triplet.

## Method

DiffSCI embeds a pre-trained RGB score-based diffusion model (from Zhu et al., CVPR 2023) as the generative denoiser prior within a Plug-and-Play half-quadratic splitting (HQS) optimization, requiring no retraining on MSI data. Three key adaptations bridge the RGB-to-MSI gap: (1) spectral correlation modeling, where overlapping 3-band groups [B_{i−1}, B_i, B_{i+1}] are constructed to preserve inter-band continuity; (2) wavelength matching (WM), which merges each target band with two bands whose wavelengths align with RGB peaks (450, 520, 660 nm) to reduce spectral mismatch; and (3) a Nesterov-type acceleration for the data subproblem that accumulates residuals across iterations and exploits the diagonal structure of ΦᵀΦ in CASSI systems for a closed-form update via Woodbury identities. The reverse diffusion uses DDIM sampling with a score-based prediction step per band, followed by the accelerated data-fidelity update, iterating from t=600 down to t=0.

## Results

On the 10-scene KAIST simulation dataset, DiffSCI (zero-shot) achieves an average PSNR of 35.28 dB and SSIM of 0.916, outperforming all zero-shot PnP baselines (DIP-HSI: 29.75 dB, PnP-CASSI: 28.55 dB) by over 5 dB in PSNR. It also surpasses the best self-supervised method (HLRTF: 33.69 dB) and all supervised CNN/deep-unfolding methods (TSA-Net: 31.35 dB, ADMM-Net: 33.26 dB). On SSIM, DiffSCI (0.916) remains below the strongest supervised Transformers (MST++: 0.955, CST-L+: 0.960), though its PSNR gap to MST++ (35.72 dB) is only 0.44 dB despite requiring no task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffSCI decisively outperforms all zero-shot and model-based baselines: it gains +5.53 dB PSNR over DIP-HSI (29.75→35.28) and +6.73 dB over PnP-CASSI (28.55→35.28), while also improving SSIM from 0.803 to 0.916. Against the self-supervised tensor network HLRTF, DiffSCI leads by 1.59 dB in PSNR (35.28 vs 33.69) with matching SSIM (0.916). Among supervised methods, DiffSCI surpasses all CNN-based (TSA-Net: 31.35 dB) and deep-unfolding (ADMM-Net: 33.26 dB) approaches, and is within 0.44 dB PSNR of the best supervised Transformer MST++ (35.72 dB) and 0.43 dB of CST-L+ (35.71 dB). However, on SSIM the supervised Transformers maintain a clear edge (0.946–0.960 vs 0.916), indicating that DiffSCI's generative prior introduces slightly more structural deviation despite its competitive pixel-level fidelity. The key distinction is that DiffSCI achieves near-supervised performance without any task-specific training on MSI data, relying solely on a pre-trained RGB diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: Plug-and-Play (PnP) iterative reconstruction for snapshot compressive imaging (building on Chan et al. 2016, Yuan et al. 2020, Zheng et al. 2021) and denoising diffusion models (DDPM, DDIM, score-based SDEs). The key departure is replacing the conventional CNN denoiser in the PnP-HQS loop with a pre-trained RGB diffusion model (Zhu et al., CVPR 2023), which had not been attempted for multispectral SCI. To make this feasible, the paper introduces wavelength matching and overlapping spectral-band grouping to bridge the channel-count and spectral-range gap between RGB and MSI, and adds a Nesterov-style residual accumulation to accelerate the data-fidelity subproblem. Compared to prior PnP-SCI methods that rely on hand-crafted or separately trained denoisers, DiffSCI inherits the generative prior of a large-scale RGB diffusion model without any MSI-specific training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot nature of DiffSCI—requiring no training on multispectral data—is practically significant given the scarcity of MSI training corpora and the high cost of retraining generative models for each spectral configuration. However, the method remains computationally expensive (tens to hundreds of diffusion sampling steps per reconstruction), limiting real-time or on-device deployment. Open questions include whether the wavelength-matching heuristic generalizes to arbitrary band configurations beyond 28-band CASSI, whether the approach extends to other SCI architectures (e.g., coded-aperture temporal imaging), and whether the spectral correlation modeling can be learned rather than hand-designed. The gap to the best supervised transformers (MST++, CST-L+) is small but consistent, suggesting that further tuning of the diffusion prior or band-adaptive conditioning could close it entirely.
