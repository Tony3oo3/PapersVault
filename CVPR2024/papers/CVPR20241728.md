## Problem

Manipulating facial action unit (AU) intensities in images requires disentangling target attributes from identity and other attributes in latent space, but publicly available datasets with multi-level AU intensity annotations (e.g., DISFA) contain very few subjects (18 for training), making it difficult to learn disentangled editing directions. Existing methods circumvent this by using pretrained AU estimators to generate pseudo-labels on larger datasets, sacrificing the precision of expert-annotated intensities.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a source face image (inverted into StyleGAN2's W+ latent space via e4e) along with either explicit AU intensity values (float or negative, beyond the 6-level training scale) or a target face image for expression transfer. It produces two outputs: a neutral face image with all 12 AUs deactivated, and a final edited image with the specified target AU intensities applied. All editing occurs in the W+ multi-level latent space (M ≤ 18 levels), and the pipeline requires no network retraining or external AU estimators at inference time.

## Method

AUEditNet employs a dual-branch architecture in the W+ latent space: a Source Branch removes the original AU status from the source embedding, and a Target Branch adds the new AU status using a randomly sampled image from a different subject (I_rnd) to implicitly disentangle identity from target attributes without additional loss functions or large batch sizes. Each of the M latent levels is handled by an independent editing module (Φ_enc^j, Φ_dec^j), and a label latent space (Ψ_enc/Ψ_dec) maps global AU labels to level-wise pseudo-labels to match the hierarchical structure of W+. The source and target embeddings are decoded into residuals (ΔW_src^j, ΔẆ_tar^j) that are subtracted from and added to the source latent vector, respectively, enabling a two-step removal-then-addition editing process. Training uses a composite loss combining pixel-wise, perceptual, pretrained-function (AU estimator), identity (ArcFace), and label consistency terms, all supervised with only 18 subjects' data from DISFA.

## Results

On DISFA, AUEditNet achieves the best average ICC(3,1) of 0.628 and average MSE of 0.283 among all editing methods across 12 AUs, surpassing ReDirTrans (ICC 0.617, MSE 0.290) and DeltaEdit (ICC 0.179, MSE 0.561). For the deactivation task (all AUs set to zero), AUEditNet achieves MSE 0.102, a 38.9% improvement over ReDirTrans (0.167). On FFHQ smile manipulation, AUEditNet achieves the best manipulation efficiency (ρ=121.3) while matching the best identity preservation (Ed=0.099), outperforming Do et al. (ρ=96.9) and StyleFlow (ρ=88.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among editing methods, AUEditNet clearly outperforms DeltaEdit [21] on AU intensity accuracy (avg ICC 0.628 vs 0.179; avg MSE 0.283 vs 0.561), though DeltaEdit preserves identity better (distance 0.396 vs 0.468). Against ReDirTrans [14], AUEditNet wins on both AU accuracy (ICC 0.628 vs 0.617; MSE 0.283 vs 0.290) and identity preservation (0.468 vs 0.505 real; 0.435 vs 0.479 inverted), with a 38.9% MSE reduction in the deactivation task. Against the three estimation baselines (HR [26], Aps [32], MAE-Face [22]), AUEditNet's editing results, when evaluated by the same external estimator, surpass HR and Aps on average ICC but fall slightly short of MAE-Face (0.628 vs 0.674), which benefits from direct supervised estimation rather than image editing. On FFHQ smile manipulation, AUEditNet achieves the best ρ (121.3) while tying for best Ed (0.099), beating Do et al. [7] (ρ=96.9), StyleFlow [2] (ρ=88.9), and Talk-to-Edit [13] (Ed=0.212, ρ=40.9). The e4e inversion [39] baseline confirms that image similarity metrics alone cannot capture AU editing quality, as inverted images already show low LPIPS despite incorrect AU intensities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the StyleGAN2 W+ latent space editing paradigm (Abdal et al., 2019) and the two-step removal-then-addition editing pipeline from ReDirTrans (Jin et al., 2023) to the specific problem of multi-AU intensity manipulation. It departs from prior latent-space editing methods (InterFaceGAN, GANSpace, DeltaEdit) that assume a subject pool far exceeding the number of target attributes, by inverting this ratio: 18 subjects for 12 AUs. The key architectural departure is the dual-branch design that physically routes source and target features through separate paths to prevent identity and unrelated-attribute leakage, replacing the explicit disentanglement losses or large-batch constraints used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical bottleneck: expert-annotated AU intensity datasets (DISFA: 27 subjects) are too small for the many-subject assumptions underlying standard latent-space disentanglement. The finding that a pretrained AU estimator loss (L_F) outweighs pixel/perceptual losses for editing accuracy is a useful practical insight for anyone building attribute-editing pipelines. Open questions include whether the dual-branch disentanglement strategy generalizes beyond AUs to other fine-grained attributes, whether it scales to the full 30-AU FACS set, and whether the approach can be made self-supervised to eliminate the need for any expert labels entirely.
