## Problem

Existing autofocus methods for frame-based and event cameras cannot be directly applied to spike cameras due to fundamental differences in data modality (asynchronous binary spike arrays versus grayscale images or discrete events). Moreover, these methods typically assume stable scene illumination, rendering them unreliable in real-world scenarios with light intensity fluctuations. The spike camera's unique capability to continuously record full-time spatial light intensity has not been leveraged for robust focusing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous binary spike streams (w×h arrays where each pixel asynchronously fires based on integrated photocurrent) sampled over adaptive time intervals Δt, along with real-time focal position feedback from the varifocal lens control system. The output is a single optimal focal position Γ(t*) determined without requiring a full traversal of the focus range. Unlike frame-based methods that process complete images, the spike stream is processed incrementally in real-time during lens movement, with the time window Δt adaptively adjusted by the golden ratio during search.

## Method

The paper proposes Spike Dispersion (SD), a focus measure derived from the AC power component of the sensor, computed as the normalized variance of integrated spike values across all pixels within a time interval Δt. By dividing the AC power by the squared DC power, SD eliminates the dependence on absolute scene light intensity, making it robust to illumination fluctuations. The search strategy, Spike-based Golden Fast Search (SGFS), extends the event-based golden search (EGS) by incorporating real-time SD feedback: during lens traversal, the system monitors energy dissipation and stops early when subsequent positions are predicted to be defocused, then applies golden-section search within the collected data. This avoids full focus range traversal and can skip position initialization, achieving convergence more than twice as fast as EGS.

## Results

The paper evaluates its spike dispersion (SD) focus measure and SGFS search strategy against frame-based (gradient, MF-DCT) and event-based (ER) baselines across static and dynamic scenes under three lighting conditions (constant, continuous variation, sudden fluctuation). SD is the only method that maintains a valid focus score peak at the correct focal position in all six scenario combinations; MF-DCT and ER fail entirely under light intensity fluctuations, and gradient-based methods degrade in low-light static scenes. For the search strategy, SGFS converges to the optimal focal position in approximately half the iterations of the full-traversal EGS baseline (Fig. 7), by stopping the traversal early when real-time SD feedback indicates energy dissipation is decreasing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two primary baselines: (1) MF-DCT [16], a frame-based frequency-domain focus measure applied via TFP reconstruction, and (2) ER/EGS [24], the event-rate focus measure and event-based golden search extended to spike streams. SD decisively outperforms both in every scenario involving light intensity variation: under continuous brightness changes, MF-DCT and ER scores are dominated by illumination drift rather than focus quality, while SD retains a clear peak at the true focal position. Under sudden intensity fluctuations, both baselines produce inverted or flat score curves (completely failing), whereas SD still identifies the correct focus. Gradient-based frame measures also degrade under low-light static scenes due to increased noise. For the search strategy, SGFS matches EGS in final accuracy but converges roughly twice as fast by exploiting real-time SD feedback to halt traversal before reaching the full focal range, eliminating the need for a position initialization step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autofocus problem from frame cameras and event cameras to the spike camera modality, building directly on Lin et al.'s event-based golden search (EGS) and event rate (ER) focus measure [24]. The key conceptual departure is that spike cameras, unlike event cameras, record full-time spatial light intensity via an integration sampling mechanism, which the authors exploit to normalize the AC power component and achieve intensity-robust focusing. The spike dispersion (SD) measure draws on the classical AC power formulation from circuit theory (as applied to frame-based focus measures in [7]) but adapts it to binary spike streams without requiring image reconstruction, sidestepping the noise and latency issues of TFP/TFI or neural-network-based reconstruction approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first autofocus solution specifically designed for spike cameras, filling a practical gap for a sensor modality that is gaining traction in high-speed imaging and computational vision. The intensity-robustness property is practically critical for real-world deployment where scene illumination is rarely stable, and the SGFS search strategy's ability to skip full focal traversal and even position initialization offers meaningful efficiency gains for embedded systems. However, the method currently cannot handle multi-target scenes and would need integration with detection/recognition for broader applicability, and the evaluation, while thorough across lighting conditions, is limited to a relatively small set of scenes and a single sensor platform.
