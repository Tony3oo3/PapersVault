## Problem

Fine-tuning diffusion models with human feedback via RLHF typically requires first training a separate reward model, which demands large annotated datasets and extensive compute. While Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) eliminates the reward model for language models, it cannot be directly applied to diffusion models because storing gradients across 20–50 latent image representations during the denoising process exceeds practical GPU memory limits (over 100 GB even with LoRA). This paper addresses how to apply preference-based optimization to diffusion models without a reward model and without prohibitive memory costs.

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes a text prompt and generates two images from the same initial Gaussian noise using the current diffusion model; a human evaluator then selects the preferred image, providing a binary preference label. The fine-tuned model takes a text prompt as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image (via Stable Diffusion v1.5) that better aligns with human preferences for the target objective (e.g., fewer hand deformities, safer content, better prompt-image alignment). No scalar reward or reward model score is ever computed during training—only relative preference between image pairs.

## Method

D3PO reformulates the diffusion denoising process as a multi-step MDP where each denoising step is a state-action pair, then extends the DPO objective to this MDP setting. The key theoretical insight is that the optimal policy under a KL-constrained RL objective can be expressed as π*(a|s) = π_ref(a|s)·exp(Q*(s,a)/β), which allows the action-value function Q* to be replaced by β·log(π*(a|s)/π_ref(a|s)), yielding a DPO-style loss that operates on per-step policy ratios rather than full-sequence likelihoods. To maximize data utilization, the method constructs T sub-segments from each preferred/unpreferred pair, so every intermediate state-action pair contributes a gradient signal, increasing effective data by a factor of T. The resulting loss (Eq. 14) updates the diffusion model's denoising network at each timestep using only the log-ratio of the fine-tuned model's probability to the reference model's probability for the preferred versus unpreferred action, requiring no reward model and only O(1) per-step memory rather than O(T) full-sequence storage.

## Results

On the prompt-image alignment task (Table 1), D3PO achieves a CLIP score of 31.9, BLIP score of 2.06, ImageReward score of 0.27, and a human preference rate of 55.7%, outperforming the Reward Weighted baseline (31.5, 2.01, 0.17, 18.7%) and the no-fine-tune baseline (30.7, 1.95, 0.04, 14.7%). On quantifiable objectives (compressibility, incompressibility, aesthetic quality), D3PO—using only the relative scale of objectives as a proxy for preference—achieves results nearly on par with DDPO, DPOK, and Reward Weighted, which all rely on full reward models during training. For hand deformity reduction, the normalcy rate increases significantly over 5 epochs, and for image safety, the model consistently produces safe images after 10 epochs of fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D3PO is compared against three reward-model-based methods: DDPO, DPOK, and Reward Weighted. On the quantifiable objectives (image compressibility, incompressibility, and LAION aesthetic score), D3PO—using only the relative ordering of objective values rather than the reward magnitudes—achieves performance nearly on par with all three baselines that use the full reward signal during training. On the prompt-image alignment task, D3PO substantially outperforms Reward Weighted across all four metrics: CLIP score 31.9 vs. 31.5, BLIP score 2.06 vs. 2.01, ImageReward score 0.27 vs. 0.17, and human preference 55.7% vs. 18.7%. D3PO also surpasses the simple 'preferred images' fine-tuning baseline (which merely trains on the preferred outputs) on every metric. The key advantage is that D3PO requires no separately trained reward model, making it applicable to tasks like hand deformity reduction and image safety where no reliable automated reward model exists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two distinct research threads: DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), which eliminates reward model training for LLM fine-tuning by directly optimizing policies from preference pairs, and DDPO (Black et al., 2023), which formulates the diffusion denoising process as a multi-step MDP for RL-based fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is that DPO treats an entire LLM output as a single action, whereas diffusion model outputs span 20–50 denoising steps with large latent representations, making the naive DPO formulation computationally infeasible. D3PO resolves this by extending DPO's theoretical framework into the MDP setting, enabling per-step policy updates that avoid storing gradients across the full trajectory. This positions the work as the first method to fine-tune diffusion models with human feedback without any separately trained reward model, departing from the standard RLHF pipeline used by DDPO, DPOK, ReFL, and Reward Weighted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the reward model training step, D3PO significantly lowers the barrier to preference-based fine-tuning of diffusion models, which previously required large curated datasets, architecture selection, and hyperparameter tuning for the reward model alone. The theoretical guarantee (Propositions 1–2) that DPO-style optimization in the MDP is equivalent to learning and then using the optimal reward model provides a principled justification for the approach. However, the method still relies on human feedback for tasks lacking reliable automated evaluators (e.g., hand deformity, image safety), and the assumption that all state-action pairs in a preferred segment are uniformly better than those in a non-preferred segment may not hold for all tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, whether the per-step uniform-reward assumption can be relaxed, and whether AI-generated feedback could substitute for human labels in more settings.
