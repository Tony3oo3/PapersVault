## Problem

Despite strong performance on conventional weakly supervised visual grounding benchmarks, state-of-the-art vision-language models (CLIP, ALBEF, METER, BLIP2) exhibit severe deficiencies in compositional reasoning—failing to distinguish objects by attributes, spatial relations, or syntactic priority—often performing at or below chance level. This gap is attributed to shortcut learning induced by contrastive pre-training objectives, yet no fine-grained grounding benchmark or targeted fine-tuning strategy existed to expose and address it.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language text phrase as input and produces a spatial grounding heatmap at the input image resolution, generated by applying Grad-CAM to the VLM's intermediate attention maps. For CLIP, images are resized to 224×224 with a maximum text length of 77 tokens; for ALBEF, 384×384 with 30 tokens. The heatmap is computed as ReLU(A_z ⊙ G_z) where A_z is an intermediate attention map and G_z is its gradient with respect to the image-text similarity score, then upsampled to full image size.

## Method

The paper contributes three components: (1) a Grad-CAM-based localization pipeline that extracts grounding heatmaps from frozen VLMs by backpropagating the image-text score gradient into the vision transformer's intermediate attention layer, requiring no architectural modification; (2) the ARPGrounding benchmark (11,425 samples from Visual Genome) that pairs two compositional distractor objects per image and scores whether the model's heatmap activates the correct object for each of two text descriptions across attribute, relation, and priority dimensions; and (3) a composition-aware fine-tuning pipeline that uses spaCy dependency parsing to sample text pairs describing distinct objects from existing image captions, then trains with a novel overlap-penalizing loss L = E[1/(w·h) Σ H₀ ⊙ H₁] that encourages the model to produce spatially distinct heatmaps for the two texts, thereby sharpening compositional discrimination without any dense bounding-box annotations.

## Results

On conventional weakly supervised visual grounding (pointing game accuracy), ALBEF achieves the highest mean score of 76.26 (VG 75.04, Flickr 84.49, ReferIt 69.26), surpassing the previous SOTA WWbL++ (72.28), while BLIP2 follows at 74.39. However, on the proposed ARPGrounding benchmark, all four VLMs perform near or below random chance (25%): the best performers are METER (mean 36.19) and ALBEF (mean 35.13), with relation and priority scores as low as 9.19 (CLIP) and 11.24 (CLIP), revealing a severe compositional reasoning deficiency. After composition-aware fine-tuning, ALBEF improves to 66.34/38.65/24.21 on Attribute/Relation/Priority, and CLIP improves to 44.56/13.24/21.30, while also boosting conventional grounding scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On conventional visual grounding, ALBEF (76.26) and BLIP2 (74.39) outperform the prior SOTA WWbL++ (72.28) in mean pointing game accuracy, while CLIP (63.16) and METER (67.40) fall short of WWbL++. On ARPGrounding, all VLMs collapse to near-random performance: even the best (METER at 36.19, ALBEF at 35.13) achieve only ~10% above the 25% random baseline, and CLIP (21.07) and BLIP2 (23.36) actually score below random. The paper further shows that fully supervised grounding models (GLIP-T/L, Grounding-DINO-T/B) do not fare better on ARPGrounding; the strongest, Grounding-DINO-B (47.03/13.78/25.68), still trails ALBEF on Attribute and METER on Priority. Composition-aware fine-tuning narrows the gap: ALBEF caft. reaches 66.34/38.65/24.21, surpassing all other models on Attribute and Relation while remaining competitive on Priority, and also improves conventional grounding (Flickr 85.93, ReferIt 74.67). TSVLC and DAC, two prior compositional-reasoning methods built on CLIP, show negligible or inconsistent gains on ARPGrounding compared to the proposed fine-tuning pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Grad-CAM explainability framework (Selvaraju et al., ICCV 2017) from single-modal CNNs to multimodal vision-language transformers, using gradient flow from text to intermediate vision layers to produce grounding heatmaps. It departs from prior compositional evaluation benchmarks (Winoground, CRePE) that rely on text-image matching by instead using a fine-grained grounding task that explicitly tests whether a model localizes the correct object among compositional distractors. The composition-aware fine-tuning pipeline is novel in combining dependency-parsing-based text pair generation with a heatmap-diversity pretext loss, distinguishing it from prior approaches like TSVLC and DAC that address compositionality through structured captioning or contrastive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SOTA VLMs achieve state-of-the-art on conventional grounding yet perform at or below chance on compositional grounding is a critical diagnostic for the field, suggesting that large-scale pre-training on scene-graph-rich data does not automatically confer compositional understanding. The ARPGrounding benchmark provides a practical, reproducible tool for tracking compositional grounding progress, though its small relation subset (370 samples) limits statistical confidence. Open questions include whether the composition-aware fine-tuning strategy generalizes to larger models and more complex compositions, and whether the modest improvements (e.g., relation scores still near 13–39%) are sufficient for practical deployment. The observation that fully supervised detectors (GLIP, Grounding DINO) do not outperform weakly supervised VLMs on compositional grounding further complicates the picture of what training paradigm is needed.
