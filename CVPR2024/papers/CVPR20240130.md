## Problem

In Lifelong Person Re-identification, updating a model with new data traditionally requires re-indexing all gallery images to produce compatible features. However, data privacy constraints make raw gallery images unavailable, so re-indexing is infeasible, leaving query features from the new model incompatible with gallery features from the old model and causing severe retrieval degradation. This paper formalizes the Re-indexing Free Lifelong Person Re-identification (RFL-ReID) task and addresses the gap of achieving effective continual ReID without ever accessing raw gallery images.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes 256×128 RGB person images (batch size 128, 4 per identity, 500 identities sampled per dataset) and a frozen copy of the previous model's features. The CCT network operates on pre-computed L2-normalized feature vectors (not raw pixels) to transfer old gallery features into the new feature space. At test time, the system produces a person feature embedding per query image and ranks all gallery features (a mix of transferred old features and newly extracted features) against it for re-identification.

## Method

C²R comprises three components built on a ResNet-50 backbone. The Continual Compatible Transfer (CCT) network is a two-stream module: a Knowledge Capturing Module (KCM) uses learnable prototypes with softmax-weighted summation to inject new-domain knowledge, while a Forward Mapping Module (FMM) directly maps old features to the new space via bottleneck FC layers; an adaptive scalar a^m balances the two streams with a residual skip connection, and four cascaded CCT networks handle multi-stage transfer. The Balanced Compatible Distillation (BCD) module aligns transferred features to new features via L2 loss and distills inter-sample relationships via KL divergence on similarity matrices (excluding same-identity pairs to avoid conflicts). The Balanced Anti-forgetting Distillation (BAD) module preserves old discriminative information by distilling classifier logits from the frozen old head and enforces discriminative consistency between transferred and new features via cosine similarity of their differences from the old feature. The total loss combines cross-entropy, triplet, patch-level KD (baseline), BCD, and BAD terms, trained for 50 epochs per stage with Adam.

## Results

On the novel RFL-ReID task (Order-1: Market-1501 → CUHK-SYSU → DukeMTMC-ReID → MSMT17-V2 → CUHK03), C²R achieves an average mAP of 39.5% and R@1 of 48.2%, outperforming the strongest baseline PatchKD* by 3.3% mAP and 3.8% R@1. On Order-2, the method reaches 39.7% mAP / 48.4% R@1, again leading PatchKD* by 3.6% / 3.9%. On the traditional L-ReID task (where re-indexing is allowed), C²R also achieves state-of-the-art average mAP of 44.2% (Order-1) and 44.7% (Order-2), surpassing PatchKD by 0.5% and 1.5% respectively, demonstrating that the compatibility mechanism does not compromise standard lifelong ReID performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²R outperforms all seven baselines on both RFL-ReID and L-ReID tasks across both training orders. The closest competitor is PatchKD* [27], which uses patch-level knowledge distillation for anti-forgetting; C²R beats it by 3.3% mAP / 3.8% R@1 (Order-1) and 3.6% / 3.9% (Order-2) on RFL-ReID, and by 0.5% / 1.5% mAP on L-ReID. CVS* [30], a backward-compatible visual search method, trails C²R by a large margin (12.2% mAP on Order-1 RFL-ReID) and additionally requires access to old data, which C²R does not. AKA* [18] and LwF* [11] suffer severe degradation under the re-indexing-free constraint (average mAP drops to ~22–23%), highlighting the incompatibility problem that C²R addresses. On the traditional L-ReID task, C²R also surpasses SPD [28], CRL [40], MEGE [20], and AKA [18], confirming that the continual compatible transfer mechanism does not sacrifice standard lifelong ReID accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: lifelong person re-identification (L-ReID) methods that use exemplar replay or knowledge distillation (e.g., AKA, PatchKD, MEGE) to prevent catastrophic forgetting, and compatible training (CT) methods (e.g., CVS, backward/forward CT) that ensure feature spaces of old and new models remain aligned. The key departure is that prior L-ReID methods assume re-indexing of old gallery features with the updated model, while prior CT methods either handle only same-dataset compatibility or rely on access to old raw data. C²R introduces a Continual Compatible Transfer network that adaptively maps old gallery features into the new model's space without touching raw images, combining a knowledge-capturing module (for new-domain prototypes) with a forward-mapping module (for old-domain preservation) in a two-stream architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic performance collapse when re-indexing is forbidden (e.g., PatchKD drops from 43.7 to 36.0 mAP on Order-1; LwF from 40.2 to 22.6) quantifies a real deployment barrier in privacy-sensitive ReID systems where raw gallery images cannot be legally reprocessed. By achieving 39.5 mAP on RFL-ReID while maintaining competitive L-ReID performance (44.2 mAP), the paper demonstrates that feature-space transfer can bridge the compatibility gap without raw image access. Open questions remain around few-shot transfer (limited new data) and robustness to noisy new data, both acknowledged by the authors as future directions. The practical relevance is high for surveillance and access-control deployments subject to data-protection regulations.
