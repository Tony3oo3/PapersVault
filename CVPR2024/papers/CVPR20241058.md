## Problem

Existing dialog-based localization methods, such as LingUNet, require the complete dialog to be available before producing a single location prediction, which is impractical for real-time search-and-rescue scenarios. This paper addresses the gap by formulating an iterative embodied dialog localization task where the locator must refine its position estimate after every dialog turn, mirroring how a human operator would progressively narrow down an observer's location.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241058/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a top-down map (resized to 224×224) and a sequence of dialog turns (each turn encoded as up to 100 text tokens via frozen BERT) as input. At each turn t, it produces a location heatmap that is up-sampled to the original map resolution, from which the predicted pixel coordinate is extracted via argmax of the softmax. Unlike single-shot baselines that output one prediction after the full dialog, DiaLoc emits T predictions (one per turn), enabling early termination when the estimate is sufficiently accurate.

## Method

DiaLoc uses a ViT encoder (fine-tuned on top-down maps) and a frozen BERT encoder to produce unimodal visual and text embeddings, which are fused by a stack of Transformer blocks with cross-attention layers where the current turn's text embedding forms queries and the map embedding provides keys and values. Two fusion variants are explored: DiaLoc-e explicitly re-injects the map embedding at each step via element-wise multiplication with the hidden state (S_t = Φ(V ⊙ S_{t-1}, L_t)), while DiaLoc-i updates the hidden state implicitly (S_t = Φ(S_{t-1}, L_t)). Training uses a multi-shot KL-divergence loss with a decay factor α to down-weight early predictions, combined with an auxiliary sigmoid-MSE loss that encourages diverse, non-peaky early heatmaps and prevents over-confident false positives. The explicit variant (DiaLoc-e) with depth-3 fusion encoder, fine-tuned ViT, and GPT-generated dialog augmentation achieves the best generalization on unseen maps.

## Results

On the WAY dataset, DiaLoc-e (multi-shot) achieves 47.15% Acc5 on valUnseen, outperforming the LingUNet-e baseline (36.30%) by +10.85%. In single-shot mode, BLIP(DiaLoc) reaches 40.41% Acc5 on valUnseen versus LingUNet's 33.33% (+7.08%). On valSeen, DiaLoc-e attains 60.00% Acc5 (multi-shot) and BLIP(DiaLoc) reaches 66.02% Acc5 (single-shot). The multi-shot DiaLoc-e also achieves the best geodesic localization error (LE) of 8.44 m on valUnseen among multi-shot methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiaLoc outperforms both baselines across all evaluation splits. In single-shot mode, BLIP(DiaLoc) surpasses LingUNet by +7.08% Acc5 on valUnseen (40.41 vs. 33.33) and by +16.78% Acc5 on valSeen (66.02 vs. 59.29); it also beats the adapted BLIP-Res18 baseline by +5.70% Acc5 on valUnseen. In multi-shot mode, DiaLoc-e achieves the largest margin, beating LingUNet-e by +10.85% Acc5 on valUnseen (47.15 vs. 36.30) and by +13.85% on valSeen (60.00 vs. 46.15). The implicit variant DiaLoc-i also improves over LingUNet-i by +17.68% Acc5 on valUnseen (37.89 vs. 20.21). Notably, the multi-shot DiaLoc-e generalizes best to unseen environments, suggesting that iterative per-turn prediction reduces overfitting compared to whole-dialog approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Localization from Embodied Dialog (LED) task introduced by Hahn et al., which framed dialog-based localization as a single-shot image-to-image translation problem using LingUNet with the complete dialog as conditional input. DiaLoc departs from this by reformulating the task as an iterative, multi-shot process where the locator produces a prediction after every dialog turn, and by replacing the CNN-based LingUNet backbone with Transformer encoders (ViT for the top-down map, BERT for dialog text) connected via cross-attention fusion. The multimodal fusion design draws on Vision-Language Pretraining paradigms (BLIP, ALBEF), while the auxiliary MSE loss and per-turn decay weighting are novel components tailored to the multi-shot setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iterative formulation has direct practical relevance for search-and-rescue scenarios where location estimates must be available before a conversation concludes, and the per-turn predictions enable early termination of dialogs. However, the work is evaluated solely on the WAY dataset (Matterport3D-based, floor-level setting), and the paper acknowledges that neither target-floor information nor per-turn ground-truth locations are available in the test split, limiting real-world validation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to building-level or outdoor environments, how it integrates with learned dialog generation (the locator choosing which questions to ask), and whether the frozen-BERT constraint limits performance in low-resource or noisy-speech settings.
