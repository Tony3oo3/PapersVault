## Problem

Dense BEV-based 3D object detectors have fallen behind sparse query-based methods on benchmarks like nuScenes, despite their advantages in depth estimation and localization robustness. The authors identify three root causes: insufficient 2D modeling due to low-resolution depth supervision, inadequate temporal modeling limited by convolution locality, and feature distortion introduced during the 2D-to-BEV uplifting process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241719/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surround-view RGB images (256×704 for ResNet50, 640×1600 for VoVNet-99) along with BEV features from up to 8 historical frames, and produces 3D bounding boxes with full attributes (position, dimensions, orientation, velocity, class) plus per-pixel depth probability distributions. The depth network operates on F_{1/8} feature maps rather than the typical F_{1/16} to provide higher-resolution input for CRF modulation, and the BEV grid is 128×128 (ResNet50) or 256×256 (larger backbones). LiDAR point clouds are used only as depth supervision during training, not as a direct input.

## Method

BEVNeXt introduces three components that modernize the LSS-based dense BEV pipeline. First, a CRF layer modulates depth probabilities by enforcing a color smoothness prior (pairwise potential weighted by RGB similarity), yielding object-level depth consistency without additional supervision or significant compute. Second, Res2Fusion partitions k historical BEV features into g groups of window size w and applies Res2Net-style multi-scale convolutions across groups, expanding the receptive field for long-term temporal fusion while eliminating ego-motion warping that causes dynamic-object misalignment. Third, a two-stage object decoder first detects object centers via a CenterPoint heatmap on the fused BEV, then refines each object's features by attending back to 2D image features through deformable spatial cross-attention, with CRF-modulated depth probabilities embedded as guidance to focus on discriminative, object-consistent 2D semantics.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set with a VoVNet-99 backbone (640×1600 input), BEVNeXt achieves a state-of-the-art 64.2% NDS and 55.7% mAP, while also producing the lowest mean Average Translation Error (mATE) of 0.409 among all compared methods. On the val split, BEVNeXt reaches 56.0% NDS (ResNet-50, 256×704), 59.7% NDS (ResNet-101, 512×1408), and 62.2% NDS (ViT-Adapter-L, 320×800), consistently outperforming both dense BEV and sparse query-based baselines. The method also demonstrates strong 3D multi-object tracking performance, achieving 0.578 AMOTA with the PolyMot tracker, surpassing the BEV-based DORT tracker (0.576).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the nuScenes test set (V2-99, 640×1600), BEVNeXt (64.2% NDS) outperforms the strongest query-based baseline Sparse4Dv2 (63.8%) by 0.4% NDS, and the dense BEV baseline SOLOFusion (61.9%, ConvNeXt-B) by 2.3% NDS. The localization advantage is particularly pronounced: BEVNeXt achieves 0.409 mATE versus 0.462 for Sparse4Dv2 (5.3% fewer translation errors) and 0.485 for SparseBEV. On the val split with ResNet-50, BEVNeXt (56.0% NDS) surpasses SparseBEV (55.8%) and StreamPETR (55.0%), while with ResNet-101 it edges out Far3D (59.4%) and Sparse4Dv2 (59.4%) by 0.3% NDS. With ViT-Adapter-L, BEVNeXt (62.2% NDS) exceeds StreamPETR (60.9%) by 1.3% NDS. For 3D multi-object tracking, BEVNeXt combined with PolyMot achieves 0.578 AMOTA, marginally beating the specialized BEV tracker DORT (0.576) while also reducing ID switches (519 vs. 774). BEVNeXt consistently produces the lowest mATE across all backbone settings, confirming its superior object localization relative to both sparse query-based and dense BEV competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEVNeXt extends the LSS-based dense BEV detection pipeline (BEVPoolv2/BEVDepth) by addressing three identified weaknesses: insufficient 2D modeling, inadequate temporal modeling, and feature distortion during forward projection. It repurposes CRF (from segmentation and weakly-supervised instance segmentation literature) to enforce object-level depth consistency without additional supervision, adapts Res2Net's multi-scale convolution blocks into a temporal aggregation module (Res2Fusion) that eliminates the need for ego-motion warping, and combines a CenterPoint detection head with BEVFormer-style spatial cross-attention augmented by CRF-modulated depth embedding. Conceptually, the paper positions dense BEV frameworks as under-engineered rather than fundamentally inferior to sparse query-based methods, drawing on techniques from both paradigms to close the gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving SOTA on nuScenes (64.2 NDS test) while maintaining the dense BEV representation, BEVNeXt validates that dense frameworks remain competitive and are better suited for downstream dense prediction tasks like occupancy prediction. The CRF-modulated depth estimation and Res2Fusion are modular contributions that could be adopted by other BEV-based systems. However, the paper acknowledges that BEVNeXt still lags behind query-based methods in inference speed (4.4 FPS vs. 6.4 FPS for StreamPETR-R101), and extending to long-range temporal settings remains an open challenge. The modest margins over strong baselines (0.2–2.6% NDS) suggest the gap between paradigms is narrowing but not yet closed.
