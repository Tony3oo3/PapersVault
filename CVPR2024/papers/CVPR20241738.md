## Problem

Diffusion models require expensive UNet forward passes at every sampling step, making them slow for real-time or on-device applications. While prior work reduces steps or distills the full model, no method exploits the observation that low-resolution UNet representations are far more robust to perturbation than high-resolution ones, leaving substantial compute savings on the table even within a single step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241738/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces a 512×512 RGB image via a latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) using 4–8 DPM++ sampling steps. For text-guided image editing, it takes a reference image plus a target text prompt and produces an edited image, operating within the Plug-and-Play pipeline with DDIM inversion (25–1000 steps) and 50 generation steps. The adaptor itself operates on internal UNet feature maps at 32×32 resolution (stage-1 cut-off), not on pixels or latents directly.

## Method

Clockwork Diffusion replaces the low-resolution path of the diffusion UNet (ε_L) with a lightweight adaptor φ_θ that approximates the low-res output by reusing the corresponding representation from the previous sampling step, conditioned on the current high-res input, time embedding, and pooled CLIP prompt embedding. The adaptor uses a strided convolution, a linear projection of the prompt, two ResNet blocks with additive time conditioning, a transposed convolution, and a residual connection—deliberately avoiding attention to minimize FLOPs. A 'clockwork' schedule alternates between full UNet passes and adaptor passes (default: every other step) to prevent error accumulation. Training uses unrolled sampling trajectories from pure noise rather than forward-noising images, requiring only captions (50K from LAION-5B) and completing in under 24 hours on a single V100 GPU. This combination of model distillation (lighter adaptor) and step distillation (cross-step feature reuse) is termed model-step distillation and is complementary to existing step/guidance distillation and efficient samplers.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2017-5K (512×512, 8 DPM++ steps), Clockwork reduces FLOPs by 32–38% across three model variants while maintaining or improving FID: SD UNet + Clockwork achieves FID 24.11 (vs. 24.64 baseline) at 7.3 TFLOPs, Efficient UNet + Clockwork reaches FID 23.21 at 5.9 TFLOPs, and Distilled Efficient UNet + Clockwork attains FID 24.45 at just 2.9 TFLOPs. GPU latency drops by 25–36% and phone latency by 20–39%. On the image editing task (ImageNet-R-TI2I, PnP baseline), Clockwork with an identity adaptor saves 33% compute at 1000 DDIM inversion steps while improving CLIP score and only slightly degrading DINO structural similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard SD UNet baseline, Clockwork improves FID from 24.64 to 24.11 while cutting FLOPs by 32% and GPU latency by 25%, at a negligible CLIP drop (0.300→0.295). Compared to SnapFusion [20], which is optimized and distilled from the same Stable Diffusion model, Distilled Efficient UNet + Clockwork is significantly more compute-efficient (2.9 vs. 4.0 TFLOPs) and faster (154 vs. 185 ms GPU), while achieving a better FID (24.45 vs. 24.20). Against BK-SDM [16] (all three sizes), Clockwork substantially outperforms on both FID (24.11 vs. 29.26–31.48) and CLIP (0.295 vs. 0.268–0.291). InstaFlow [24] achieves the lowest FLOPs (0.8) and latency (40 ms) due to single-step generation, but Clockwork is significantly better on FID (24.11 vs. 29.30) and CLIP (0.295 vs. 0.283). For image editing, Clockwork applied to Plug-and-Play [47] saves 33% compute at 1000 inversion steps while improving CLIP and only slightly degrading DINO structural distance, demonstrating that the method is complementary to existing acceleration techniques like step distillation, guidance distillation, and efficient schedulers.

## Relation to Prior Work

Clockwork Diffusion unifies two previously separate efficiency axes for diffusion models—model distillation (replacing UNet components with lighter surrogates, as in Dockhorn et al. and Kim et al.) and step distillation (reusing information across sampling steps, as in Salimans & Ho's progressive distillation)—into a single 'model-step distillation' framework. The key conceptual departure is the perturbation-robustness analysis showing that low-resolution UNet features can be approximated by reusing representations from prior steps, motivating a lightweight adaptor that receives both the current high-res input and the previous step's low-res output. The clockwork scheduling (alternating full and approximate passes) draws on the Clockwork RNN and Clockwork ConvNets literature, and the method is demonstrated as complementary to, rather than competing with, existing step distillation, guidance distillation, and efficient backbone designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Clockwork delivers 32–38% FLOP reductions on top of already-heavily-optimized and distilled Stable Diffusion variants, directly targeting the low-step regime (4–8 steps) that matters for real-time and on-device deployment. Training is cheap (under 24 hours on a single V100, no image dataset required), lowering the barrier to adoption. Open questions remain: the method is tied to a fixed operating point (step count, scheduler), the authors acknowledge they do not fully understand why unrolled-trajectory training outperforms standard forward-noising distillation at very low steps, and it is unclear how the approach generalizes to ViT-based diffusion architectures. The identity-adaptor result (simply copying the previous step's features works reasonably well) also hints that the inter-step correlation in low-res features may be exploitable in even simpler ways.
