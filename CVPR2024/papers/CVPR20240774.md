## Problem

Existing robust NAS methods inherit conventional search spaces designed for standard training, which may exclude architectures that are simultaneously accurate and robust under adversarial training. Additionally, prior methods handle the accuracy–robustness tradeoff with a fixed regularization coefficient, which cannot reliably find a common descent direction for both objectives and heavily depends on manual tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240774/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The search operates on small RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet-200) and produces a discrete neural architecture composed of 20 stacked cells (Accurate, Robust, or Reduction) with a 1-2-2 filter ratio. During search, a lightweight 8-cell supernet is optimized; the final architecture is discretized via DARTS rules and then adversarially trained for 200 epochs with 7-step PGD before evaluation under white-box, black-box, and transfer attacks.

## Method

ARNAS introduces two key components. First, a purpose-built search space replaces the single Normal Cell of DARTS with three cell types—Accurate Cell, Robust Cell, and Reduction Cell—placed at fixed positions (Accurate before the second reduction, Robust after) with a 1-2-2 filter ratio that increases FLOPs while keeping parameter count comparable to conventional designs. Second, a differentiable multi-objective search strategy based on MGDA dynamically computes a weight γ* each iteration by solving a quadratic program over the gradients of natural and adversarial validation losses, yielding a common descent direction that simultaneously reduces both objectives without a fixed regularization coefficient. The overall procedure follows DARTS with second-order approximation, alternating between adversarial weight updates (7-step PGD) and architecture-parameter updates via Adam.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under white-box attacks (ε=8/255), ARNAS achieves 85.92% natural accuracy and the highest adversarial accuracy among all competitors: 55.87% under PGD-20, 55.43% under PGD-100, and 52.66% under AutoAttack (AA). This represents a 2.43-point improvement over the next-best robust NAS method (NADAR, 50.44% AA) and a 5.20-point improvement over the best hand-crafted baseline (DenseNet-121, 47.46% AA). On transferability, ARNAS also leads on CIFAR-100 (29.54% PGD-20 vs. 25.83% for PDARTS), SVHN (92.02% PGD-20 vs. 89.58% for PDARTS), and Tiny-ImageNet-200 (54.18% natural accuracy vs. 52.10% for WideResNet-34-12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARNAS achieves the highest adversarial accuracy across all strong-attack metrics (PGD-20, PGD-100, AutoAttack) on CIFAR-10, outperforming the closest robust NAS competitor NADAR by 2.44 points on PGD-20 (55.87% vs. 53.43%) and 2.22 points on AA (52.66% vs. 50.44%). Against standard NAS baselines, ARNAS gains 4.55 points over PDARTS on PGD-20 and 4.14 points over DARTS on AA. Among hand-crafted architectures, ARNAS surpasses DenseNet-121 by 5.38 points on PGD-20 and 5.20 points on AA, while maintaining comparable natural accuracy (85.92% vs. 85.95%). ABanditNAS-10 reports an inflated natural accuracy of 90.64% but collapses under strong attacks (16.03% AA), confirming it is not genuinely robust. On transferability to more complex datasets, ARNAS outperforms PDARTS on CIFAR-100 (29.54% vs. 25.83% PGD-20) and SVHN (92.02% vs. 89.58% PGD-20), and surpasses WideResNet-34-12 on Tiny-ImageNet-200 in natural accuracy (54.18% vs. 52.10%) and FGSM accuracy (50.00% vs. 27.82%), challenging the assumption that NAS-based architectures degrade relative to hand-crafted ones as task complexity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable NAS framework of DARTS to the adversarial robustness setting, building directly on the MGDA multi-objective optimization algorithm for dynamic weight balancing. It departs from prior robust NAS methods (RobNet, AdvRush, RACL, DSRNA, NADAR, ABanditNAS) in two key ways: replacing the conventional single-type Normal Cell search space with position-specific Accurate and Robust Cells, and replacing the fixed-coefficient weighted sum of losses with a dynamically computed common descent direction. The search space design is motivated by Huang et al.'s empirical finding that depth and width at different network positions affect accuracy and robustness differently, which the authors extend to the level of cell structure type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical insight that separable convolutions near the input and dilated convolutions near the output yield simultaneously high accuracy and robustness provides actionable guidance for both hand-crafted and NAS-designed robust architectures, challenging the common practice of stacking identical cells. The transferability results on Tiny-ImageNet-200, where ARNAS substantially outperforms WideResNet-34-12 under FGSM, challenge the assumption that NAS architectures degrade relative to hand-crafted ones as task complexity increases. However, all experiments are confined to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, Tiny-ImageNet) and a single GPU, leaving open whether the cell-placement principle and the multi-objective search strategy scale to ImageNet or larger architectures. The notably high FLOPs (1.27G) of the searched architecture also raise practical deployment concerns that are not fully addressed.
