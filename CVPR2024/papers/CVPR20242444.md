## Problem

Event-based motion deblurring has predominantly treated the task as single-image restoration, ignoring the rich long-range temporal dependencies available in continuous video sequences. Additionally, cross-modal fusion between event and frame features has been performed exclusively in the spatial domain, failing to exploit the fact that events naturally encode high-frequency motion information that aligns with the spectral content of the latent sharp frame.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242444/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes T sequential motion-blurred video frames and their corresponding event streams, which are converted to voxel grids (bin size B) of shape B×H×W. On the REVD dataset, inputs are 1024×768 RGB frames with synchronized Prophesee Gen4 events; during training, 256×256 crops are used with batch size 8. The output is a sequence of T sharp frames, each produced by a final 3×3 convolution on upsampled features plus a residual connection to the original blurred frame.

## Method

The framework extracts multi-scale features via 3D convolutions, then applies three key modules at different resolutions. The Frequency-aware Cross-Modal Feature Enhancement (FCFE) module, operating at the lowest resolution (scale s=2), fuses blur and event features in the frequency domain using 2D FFT for spatial filtering and 1D FFT along a flattened channel dimension for global channel filtering, leveraging the convolution theorem to interpret element-wise spectral multiplication as dynamic large-kernel convolution. The Event-guided Local-windowed Temporal Propagation (ELTP) module aligns adjacent frames via synthesis-based alignment (concatenating supporting blur features with paired event features and passing through ResBlocks), followed by frequency-domain fusion with channel attention. The Bidirectional Temporal Feature Fusion (BTFF) module, operating at higher resolution (scale s=1), propagates information across all frames in both forward and backward directions by fusing current and target event features, enabling long-range temporal consistency. Training uses AdamW with Charbonnier loss on the GoPro (synthetic events via ESIM) and REVD (real events) datasets.

## Results

On the novel REVD real-world dataset, the proposed method achieves 32.99 dB PSNR and 0.9326 SSIM, outperforming the best event-based baseline UEVD (31.97 dB) by 1.02 dB and the best frame-based baseline RNN-MBP (31.77 dB) by 1.22 dB. On the GoPro synthetic dataset, the method reaches 36.70 dB PSNR and 0.978 SSIM, surpassing the strongest event-based competitor UEVD† (35.93 dB) by 0.77 dB. Inference time is 484 ms per frame at 1024×768 with 30.27 T FLOPs for five clips, substantially more efficient than RNN-MBP (2238 ms, 153.5 T FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 13 baselines on both datasets. On REVD, the closest competitor is UEVD [21] at 31.97 dB, which the proposed method surpasses by 1.02 dB; the strongest frame-based method RNN-MBP [66] (31.77 dB) is beaten by 1.22 dB but requires 4.6× more inference time (2238 ms vs. 484 ms) and 5× more FLOPs. On GoPro, the method leads UEVD† [21] (35.93 dB) by 0.77 dB and EFNet† [42] (35.87 dB) by 0.83 dB, while the best frame-based method DSTNet [34] (34.16 dB) trails by 2.54 dB. Among event-based baselines, REDNet [56] (31.90 dB on REVD) and EFNet [42] (31.75 dB) are competitive but consistently underperform the proposed method by 1.0–1.2 dB. The eSL-Net [48] baseline performs poorly on real-world data (26.99 dB on REVD vs. 28.69 dB on GoPro), highlighting the difficulty of real-world event noise. Overall, the frequency-domain cross-modal fusion and dual temporal alignment modules yield consistent gains over both frame-only and single-frame event-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research lines: frame-based video deblurring, which actively exploits long-range temporal dependencies across video sequences, and event-based motion deblurring, which has predominantly operated on single frames or short frame pairs. It repurposes the 'synthesis-based alignment' concept from event-based video interpolation (TimeLens, TimeLens++) to perform temporal feature alignment without optical flow, and introduces frequency-domain cross-modal fusion (FCFE) as a departure from the spatial-domain attention mechanisms used in prior event-based deblurring methods like EFNet and REDNet. The two temporal alignment modules (ELTP at low resolution, BTFF at higher resolution) draw on bidirectional propagation ideas from frame-based video deblurring (RNN-MBP, DSTNet) but are guided by event streams rather than learned optical flows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The REVD dataset is the first real-world, high-resolution (1024×768), long-sequence (up to 299 frames) benchmark for event-based video deblurring, addressing a critical gap where prior real-world datasets (REBlur) had only 6-frame sequences and low resolution, making long-range temporal evaluation impossible. A notable finding is that on real-world data, a purely frame-based method (RNN-MBP) achieves performance nearly on par with event-based methods, suggesting that event noise and saturation in real captures limit the practical advantage of events more than synthetic benchmarks indicate. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain fusion strategy generalizes to other event-based restoration tasks, how the method performs with non-synchronized or lower-quality event streams, and whether the 21-sequence dataset is sufficient for robust generalization claims.
