## Problem

Existing learned image warping methods (SRWarp, LTEW) formulate warping as generalized super-resolution but fail to capture local intensity variations in deformed content, producing distorted images with missing high-frequency details. These methods also exhibit poor generalization to out-of-distribution scaling factors and geometric transformations not seen during training, limiting their practical utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240300/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a predefined geometric transformation (homography matrix, ERP-to-perspective projection parameters, or asymmetric scaling factors) and produces a warped image at the target resolution. During training, 48×48 patches are cropped from DIV2K images; at inference, full-resolution images (e.g., 2048×1024 ERP images projected to 1024×1024) are processed. The output is structured as a bicubic-interpolated coarse image plus progressively added fine-scale residuals from each frequency learning module.

## Method

MFR employs a pretrained SR backbone (RRDB, RCAN, or EDSR) to encode the input into latent features, augmented with coordinate and curvature information via the LTE module. A stack of L frequency learning modules then progressively extracts multi-frequency representations: each module applies a learnable 1-D Gabor wavelet filter (a Gaussian envelope modulated by a complex exponential with learnable center, decay rate, and modulation frequency) to the latent vector, multiplies element-wise with a linearly transformed previous-layer representation, and decodes a residual image patch. A 1-D attention gating mechanism adaptively controls information flow from each frequency representation, and short connections fuse spatial and frequency features before decoding. The final warped image is the sum of a bicubic-interpolated base and all module residuals, yielding a coarse-to-fine generation that captures high-frequency local details while the Gabor wavelet's compact support in both spatial and frequency domains enables effective modeling of local deformations.

## Results

On the primary homography transformation task, MFR-RRDB achieves the best mPSNR across all five benchmark datasets in both in-scale and out-of-scale settings, reaching 31.18 dB on DIV2KW and 29.68 dB on Urban100W (in-scale), and 27.12 dB on DIV2KW and 24.51 dB on Urban100W (out-of-scale). In the out-of-scale setting, MFR outperforms the second-best method (LTEW) by 0.20 dB on DIV2KW and 0.26 dB on Urban100W, demonstrating superior generalization to unseen scaling factors. For asymmetric image super-resolution, MFR-RCAN achieves the highest PSNR across all 12 scale combinations in both in-scale (e.g., 36.13 dB on Set5 ×1.6/×3.05) and out-of-scale (e.g., 30.68 dB on Set5 ×3.2/×6.1) settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFR consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation setting. Against SRWarp [41], MFR gains 0.14–0.23 dB mPSNR in the in-scale setting and 0.17–0.47 dB in the out-of-scale setting, with the largest gap on Urban100W (0.47 dB out-of-scale). Against LTEW [22], the closest competitor, MFR leads by 0.08–0.18 dB in-scale and 0.14–0.26 dB out-of-scale, confirming that the progressive multi-frequency representation and Gabor wavelet filters provide meaningful improvements over the coordinate-based MLP approach. In asymmetric super-resolution, MFR-RCAN surpasses LTEW-RCAN by 0.01–0.08 dB PSNR in-scale and 0.01–0.08 dB out-of-scale, while also beating MetaSR [15] and ArbSR [44] by 0.1–0.3 dB across all scale combinations. The RRDB [45] and RCAN [53] baselines (SR models without dedicated warping) are outperformed by 0.4–0.9 dB, validating the benefit of the learned warping pipeline over simple interpolation-based resampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the line of work that reformulated image warping as a generalized super-resolution problem with spatially varying scaling factors, initiated by SRWarp [41] and extended by LTEW [22]. While SRWarp leveraged pretrained SR features with adaptive warping layers and LTEW employed coordinate-based MLPs with Fourier features in continuous space, MFR departs from both by introducing a progressive multi-frequency filtering architecture that learns representations from distinct frequency subbands in a coarse-to-fine manner. The key architectural departure is the use of learnable 1-D Gabor wavelet filters (rather than SIREN-style sinusoidal or Gaussian filters) to capture local spatial-frequency variations, motivated by the compact support property of Gabor wavelets in both domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Image warping is a foundational operation underlying facial manipulation, image registration, and synthesis, so improvements in fidelity and generalization have broad downstream impact. The demonstrated superiority in out-of-distribution settings (both unseen scaling factors and unseen transformation types like ERP-to-perspective) addresses a practical limitation of prior methods that restricts real-world deployment. However, the absolute improvements over LTEW are modest (0.18–0.26 dB in homography), the method is evaluated only on DIV2K-trained models at 48×48 patch scale, and it remains open whether the progressive Gabor-wavelet frequency decomposition generalizes to higher-resolution or video warping scenarios.
