## Problem

Existing image harmonization methods adjust only global color and brightness while ignoring critical illumination cues such as lighting direction and shadows, producing composites that look unnatural when the background has distinct lighting. Conversely, portrait relighting methods achieve realistic lighting but require HDR panorama environment maps at inference, which are impractical for casual photography. This paper bridges the gap by enabling lighting-aware harmonization that operates on a single background image without external environment maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240832/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a composite image (foreground subject pasted onto a new background at 512×512), the corresponding alpha mask, and the target background image, and outputs a harmonized portrait with corrected color tone and spatially-varying lighting direction. Notably, no HDR environment map or surface normal is required at test time—the lighting representation is extracted entirely from the 2D background image via a learned CNN branch. The model is initialized from InstructPix2Pix's latent diffusion checkpoint and operates in latent space.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage training pipeline on a pretrained latent diffusion UNet. Stage I attaches a spatial lighting representation module (a CNN encoder) that extracts lighting features from the background image and injects them into the UNet at multiple scales, trained with a denoising objective on light-stage rendered relighting pairs. Stage II pretrains a parallel environment-map-conditioned model, then trains an alignment network to map background-derived features toward environment-map-derived features, making the 2D background representation more physically plausible without requiring HDR maps at inference. Stage III introduces a data synthesis pipeline that inpaints the foreground from natural portraits, relights the subject using the Stage I/II model with random backgrounds, and finetunes the UNet backbone on these synthetic pairs to boost photorealism while freezing the lighting conditioning branch.

## Results

On the light stage test set, Relightful Harmonization achieves the best performance across all four metrics: MSE 0.012, PSNR 20.53, SSIM 0.848, and LPIPS 0.159, substantially outperforming the next-best method (Harmonizer, MSE 0.015, LPIPS 0.338). On the natural image test set, the method again leads with MSE 0.005, PSNR 23.56, SSIM 0.913, and LPIPS 0.097, with a particularly large LPIPS gap (0.097 vs. 0.108–0.147 for baselines). In a user study on 70 real-world images (1750 ratings), raters preferred the proposed method over all four harmonization baselines, with preference rates ranging from 63.9% (vs. Harmonizer) to 84.5% (vs. PCT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four harmonization methods (INR, Harmonizer, PCT, PIH) and one relighting method (Total Relighting), plus a transformer-based relighting baseline. On the light stage test set, Relightful Harmonization outperforms all baselines on every metric, with the most striking gap in LPIPS (0.159 vs. 0.327–0.408), indicating far superior perceptual quality in lighting coherence. On the natural image test set, where relighting methods are inapplicable (no HDR maps), the method still leads all four harmonization baselines, achieving 2.0 dB higher PSNR and ~0.01–0.05 lower LPIPS than the best harmonization competitor (Harmonizer). In the user study, the proposed method is preferred over PCT 84.5% of the time, over INR 70.2%, over PIH 71.3%, and over Harmonizer 63.9%, confirming that the lighting-aware approach produces visibly more plausible composites than color-only harmonization. Total Relighting, while competitive on the light stage set (MSE 0.044), is not applicable to real-world background replacement since it requires an HDR environment map at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: image harmonization (which adjusts global color and brightness but ignores lighting direction) and portrait relighting (which captures lighting direction but requires HDR environment maps unavailable in casual photography). It builds on InstructPix2Pix as a pretrained diffusion backbone and introduces a spatial lighting representation module that encodes illumination cues from a single background image, then aligns those features with environment-map-derived representations for physical plausibility. The key departure from prior harmonization methods is the explicit incorporation of directional lighting as a conditioning signal, and the key departure from prior relighting methods is the elimination of the HDR map requirement at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on HDR environment maps during inference, this work makes lighting-aware portrait compositing practical for everyday photography and creative editing workflows, a setting where prior relighting methods are inapplicable. The data synthesis pipeline—using the model itself as an augmenter to generate training pairs from natural images—is a reusable strategy for bridging the synthetic-to-real gap in diffusion-based editing. Limitations include the 512×512 resolution cap (affecting fine facial detail) and subtle identity drift in clothing and skin tones; open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, handles multi-source or animated lighting, and generalizes beyond human portraits.
