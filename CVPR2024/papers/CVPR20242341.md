## Problem

The prevailing paradigm in medical image segmentation trains separate models for each clinical target and modality, constrained by limited single-domain data and unable to exploit relationships across tasks, body regions, and imaging modalities. This paper asks whether a single unified model can handle diverse segmentation tasks across heterogeneous datasets with partial annotations and conflicting class definitions, inspired by how radiology residents build expertise through broad exposure to varied imaging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242341/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D medical image patches (128×128×128, resampled to 1.5 mm isotropic with z-score normalization) along with oracle-guided task IDs and a modality label at inference. Each task is formulated as a binary segmentation problem (71 unique tasks across 11 datasets), and the model outputs per-task binary probability maps via sigmoid of inner products between posterior task prototypes and decoder features, plus an auxiliary modality classification. The key design choice is that priors are selected and combined dynamically based on the specified clinical objective rather than being fixed per dataset.

## Method

Hermes introduces a context-prior pool containing learnable task prior tokens (one per binary task) and modality prior tokens (10 tokens per modality), which are selected via oracle-guided lookup at inference and concatenated into a unified prior vector. A bidirectional cross-attention fusion module (for CNN backbones) or merged attention (for Transformers) adaptively exchanges information between the prior tokens and image feature maps, producing posterior prototypes that serve as class classifiers. The approach is applied hierarchically at 4×, 8×, and 16× down-sampling scales, with posterior prototypes from each scale concatenated and processed by an MLP for final segmentation. Training uses mixed-batch joint optimization across all datasets with a combined BCE+Dice segmentation loss and an auxiliary cross-entropy modality loss (λ=0.001), allowing the backbone and priors to co-adapt. The method is model-agnostic, demonstrated with both ResUNet (CNN) and MedFormer (Transformer) backbones, and the learned priors capture anatomical and imaging relationships consistent with radiological principles.

## Results

On the collective benchmark of 11 datasets (2,438 3D images) spanning CT, MRI, and PET modalities, Hermes-R (ResUNet backbone) achieves an average Dice of 84.93%, outperforming the traditional ResUNet baseline (82.65%) by 2.28% and the SSL-pretrained DeSD (81.64%) by 3.29%. Hermes-M (MedFormer backbone) reaches 85.28% average Dice, surpassing traditional MedFormer (83.39%) by 1.89%. The largest single-dataset gains appear in AutoPET (73.69% vs. 65.52% for ResUNet, +8.17%) and LiTS tumor (67.49% vs. 64.87%, +2.62%), demonstrating cross-modality and cross-task synergy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hermes outperforms all traditional single-dataset baselines (nnUNet, ResUNet, SwinUNETR, MedFormer) across all 11 datasets, with the most substantial gains on cross-modality tasks (AutoPET: +8.17% over ResUNet) and tumor/lesion segmentation (KiTS: +3.57%, LiTS: +2.62%). Against SSL-pretrained methods (DeSD on 10,594 CT scans; UniMiSS on 5,022 CT + 108,948 2D images), Hermes achieves higher average Dice despite training on far fewer images, and notably avoids the cross-modality degradation seen in DeSD (86.81% on DLBS brain MRI vs. Hermes-R's 96.21%) and UniMiSS (60.32% on AutoPET vs. Hermes-R's 73.69%). Among universal-paradigm competitors (DoDNet, CLIP-Driven, MultiTalent, UniSeg, Multi-Decoder), Hermes leads in all six evaluation perspectives shown in Figure 3(A), attributed to its learned task/modality priors capturing inter-task relationships that one-hot embeddings (DoDNet) and CLIP text encoders (CLIP-Driven) cannot encode. In transfer learning on MSD Pancreas, Hermes-R achieves 82.73% Dice at 100% data (vs. 81.97% for DeSD, 81.41% for DoDNet) and 72.71% at 1% data (vs. 67.82% for DeSD). In incremental learning, Hermes-R reaches 79.98% Dice at 100% data while keeping the backbone frozen, substantially outperforming DoDNet (69.87%) and CLIP-Driven (74.72%). For zero-shot generalization from StructSeg to SegTHOR, Hermes leads all universal methods, confirming that diverse upstream training improves cross-dataset transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing task-specific medical image segmentation paradigm (nnUNet, ResUNet, MedFormer) and from prior universal segmentation attempts that condition on one-hot task vectors (DoDNet), CLIP text embeddings (CLIP-driven), or multiple task-specific heads (MultiTalent, UniSeg). Its key conceptual departure is learning task and modality context priors directly from medical data as learnable tokens, rather than injecting task identity through orthogonal embeddings or external text encoders, and addressing both task and modality heterogeneity simultaneously across multiple body regions. The method is architecturally model-agnostic, plugging a prior fusion module (cross-attention for CNNs, merged attention for Transformers) into existing backbones, and draws its motivation from radiology residency training as a metaphor for multi-exposure learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and well-motivated framework for building medical imaging foundation models from the diverse, partially-annotated datasets that actually exist in clinical practice, rather than requiring large fully-annotated single-task corpora. The finding that model scalability benefits emerge only under the universal paradigm (not traditional single-dataset training) suggests a path toward larger medical imaging models that was previously blocked by data diversity constraints. Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly large-scale pretraining (tens of thousands of images), how to handle missing labels via self-supervised or semi-supervised learning, and whether the learned priors remain interpretable as the number of tasks and modalities grows substantially.
