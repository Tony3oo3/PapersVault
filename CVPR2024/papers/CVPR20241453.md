## Problem

Existing view-aware diffusion models for zero-shot novel view synthesis (e.g., Zero-1-to-3, SyncDreamer) are designed for a single input image, making it impossible to control which aspects of an object appear in generated views—hidden geometry and textures remain unpredictable. The paper addresses the lack of a flexible, scalable mechanism to inject an arbitrary number of multi-view conditions into these pre-trained diffusion backbones without retraining them from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241453/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a main-view RGB image (256×256) plus an arbitrary number of additional view images at known camera poses, along with the relative azimuth/elevation angle to the desired target viewpoint. It outputs a synthesized novel-view image at that angle, which can be fed into downstream 3D reconstruction (e.g., NeuS) to produce a mesh. During training, exactly three views (front, back, random) are sampled per iteration, but at inference the model accepts one or more conditioning views, and the diffusion backbone (Zero-1-to-3 or SyncDreamer) remains frozen.

## Method

DreamComposer comprises three plug-in modules: (1) Target-Aware 3D Lifting encodes each input view into Stable Diffusion latent space and lifts the 2D features into triplane 3D representations via a convolutional encoder with self- and cross-attention layers, where the cross-attention is conditioned on the angular difference to the target view so the network focuses on target-relevant 3D structure rather than a full reconstruction; (2) Multi-View Feature Fusion performs composited volume rendering—sampling target-view rays, projecting ray points into each input view's camera space, aggregating 3D point features with cosine-based angular weights, and integrating along rays to produce a single target-view latent feature; (3) Target-View Feature Injection uses a ControlNet-style cloned UNet branch that takes the rendered latent feature and predicts residuals added to the frozen backbone's intermediate outputs, with cross-attention gating on the main-to-target angle to down-weight auxiliary views when they are close to the main view. Training is two-stage: first pre-training the lifting module on sparse-view reconstruction with latent-space MSE, then jointly optimizing all three modules with diffusion loss plus MSE while keeping the diffusion U-Net frozen.

## Results

On the GSO dataset with single-view input, DC-SyncDreamer achieves PSNR 20.52, SSIM 0.828, and LPIPS 0.141, outperforming SyncDreamer (PSNR 20.05, SSIM 0.798, LPIPS 0.146), Zero-1-to-3 (PSNR 18.93, SSIM 0.779, LPIPS 0.166), and RealFusion (PSNR 15.26, SSIM 0.722, LPIPS 0.283). With multi-view ground-truth inputs, DC-Zero-1-to-3 improves PSNR by approximately 4.4 dB over Zero-1-to-3 across all tested elevation angles (e.g., 25.25 vs. 20.82 at 0° elevation), while also reducing LPIPS from ~0.13 to ~0.09. The framework demonstrates consistent gains in both perceptual (LPIPS) and structural (SSIM) metrics regardless of input elevation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-SyncDreamer consistently outperforms all three baselines on the GSO dataset. Against the closest competitor SyncDreamer, it gains +0.47 PSNR, +0.030 SSIM, and −0.005 LPIPS, indicating improved texture fidelity and structural consistency while maintaining multi-view coherence. The gap over Zero-1-to-3 is larger (+1.59 PSNR, +0.049 SSIM, −0.025 LPIPS), reflecting the advantage of multi-view conditioning over single-view generation. RealFusion, an SDS-based reconstruction method, trails significantly (−5.26 PSNR, −0.106 SSIM), confirming that diffusion-based NVS with multi-view conditions substantially outperforms optimization-based approaches. In the multi-view setting (Table 1), DC-Zero-1-to-3 improves PSNR by 4.2–4.5 dB over Zero-1-to-3 at all elevation angles (0°, 15°, 30°), demonstrating that the multi-view conditioning benefit is robust to input viewpoint variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamComposer extends the single-view novel view synthesis paradigm established by Zero-1-to-3 and SyncDreamer by introducing flexible multi-view conditioning, addressing the fundamental ambiguity that arises when only one view is available. Architecturally, it repurposes the triplane representation from EG3D for 2D-to-3D feature lifting, employs volume rendering for cross-view feature fusion, and adopts a ControlNet-style injection mechanism to plug multi-view latent features into a frozen diffusion UNet. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the target-aware 3D lifting design, where the lifting module is conditioned on the target view angle so that it builds only the 3D features relevant to the desired output rather than a complete 3D representation, and the composited volume rendering with azimuth-based weighting to fuse misaligned triplane features from different camera spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means DreamComposer can immediately enhance multiple existing NVS and 3D generation pipelines (Zero-1-to-3, SyncDreamer) without retraining their backbones, lowering the barrier for practitioners to add multi-view controllability. The demonstrated applications—controllable 3D editing via InstructPix2Pix/DragGAN and 3D character modeling from sparse 2D paintings—suggest practical value for content creation workflows. However, evaluation is limited to 30 GSO objects and the 3D reconstruction quality is bounded by the downstream NeuS pipeline; open questions include robustness to inconsistent or noisy multi-view inputs, scalability to very large view counts, and whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or articulated objects.
