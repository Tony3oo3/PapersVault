## Problem

Existing latent diffusion models learn the distribution of fixed-resolution images, requiring a separate super-resolution model to produce higher-resolution outputs. This two-stage pipeline introduces a domain gap between real training images and diffusion-generated low-resolution inputs, degrading super-resolution quality. The paper addresses this by learning the distribution of continuous image neural fields that can be rendered at any resolution, eliminating the need for an external SR model.

## Input / Output

The diffusion model operates entirely on a 64×64 latent code, yet the rendered output can be any resolution up to 2048×2048. The autoencoder encoder accepts images at arbitrary resolution (resampled to 256×256), while the CLIF renderer decodes a 256×256×128 feature map to RGB at query-specified coordinates and pixel sizes. For inverse problems, the model accepts semantic bounding boxes at arbitrary scales and text prompts, rendering only the relevant sub-region at 224×224 for CLIP-based conditioning without decoding the full image.

## Method

The method has two stages: (1) training an image neural field autoencoder consisting of a standard LDM encoder, a decoder producing a 256×256×128 feature map, and a novel Convolutional Local Image Function (CLIF) renderer that replaces LIIF's pointwise MLP with a small convolutional network over local query neighborhoods, enabling photorealistic high-frequency details while maintaining scale consistency; (2) training a standard DDPM diffusion model on the 64×64 latent space. Training uses patch-wise decoding with random downsampling of ground-truth images to supervise all scales simultaneously, combined with L1, perceptual, and GAN losses. At generation time, a sampled latent is decoded to a global feature map, and the CLIF renderer produces the final image tile-by-tile with padding to avoid boundary discontinuities, achieving up to 2K resolution from a 64×64 diffusion process.

## Results

On FFHQ, INFD achieves pFID of 8.07 (256/1K), 6.64 (512/1K), and 5.57 (1K/1K), substantially outperforming the best LDM+SR baseline (LDM+Real-ESRGAN: 18.38, 18.46, 16.04 respectively). At the native 256×256 resolution, INFD's pFID of 5.34 is competitive with LDM's 4.98. On the Mountains dataset, INFD achieves pFID of 7.53, 6.84, and 5.13 at 256/1K, 512/1K, and 1K/1K, again far surpassing LDM+Real-ESRGAN (17.36, 15.11, 10.39). The diffusion process operates on a 64×64 latent representation, enabling 2K-resolution image synthesis without a separate super-resolution model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INFD outperforms all five LDM+SR baselines on every high-resolution pFID metric (256/1K, 512/1K, 1K/1K) by large margins. Against the strongest baseline, LDM+Real-ESRGAN, INFD reduces pFID from 18.38 to 8.07 (256/1K), 18.46 to 6.64 (512/1K), and 16.04 to 5.57 (1K/1K) on FFHQ. The coordinate-based SR methods (LIIF, ITSRN, SwinIR-LTE) perform even worse, with pFID 256/1K values above 50, indicating severe quality degradation when upscaling LDM's 256×256 output. At the native 256×256 resolution, INFD (5.34) is slightly behind the original LDM (4.98), but this is the only resolution where LDM operates natively. On the Mountains dataset, the same pattern holds: INFD (7.53/6.84/5.13) dominates LDM+Real-ESRGAN (17.36/15.11/10.39) across all scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion model (LDM) framework by replacing its fixed-resolution autoencoder with a neural field autoencoder, enabling resolution-agnostic image generation. The key architectural departure from LIIF is the Convolutional Local Image Function (CLIF), which replaces pointwise decoding with a small convolutional network to capture local context and produce photorealistic high-frequency details. Conceptually, the paper brings the any-resolution training paradigm previously explored for GANs (AnyResGAN, ScaleParty) into the diffusion model family, while also drawing on arbitrary-scale super-resolution literature for the coordinate-based rendering mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method eliminates the need for a separate super-resolution model, avoiding the domain gap between real and diffusion-generated images that degrades SR performance, and enables training from mixed-resolution datasets without resizing. The resolution-agnostic latent space also unlocks efficient multi-scale inverse problems (e.g., layout-to-image with CLIP at arbitrary bounding-box scales), which is intractable with fixed-resolution diffusion models. Key limitations include the scale-consistency assumption (low-res images must follow the same distribution as downsampled high-res images) and the preliminary nature of the text-to-image results, which rely on fine-tuning a Stable Diffusion checkpoint on a small high-resolution subset rather than training from scratch.
