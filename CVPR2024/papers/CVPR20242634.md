## Problem

Reconstructing multiple interacting people in detailed 3D from a single monocular video remains largely unsolved: existing single-person neural rendering methods fail under severe human-human occlusions, while multi-person approaches either require expensive multi-view rigs or only recover coarse body shapes. The core difficulty is achieving pixel-level disentanglement of individuals, resolving depth ambiguities, and maintaining complete 3D geometry from short, in-the-wild video clips without any prior knowledge of the subjects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242634/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short monocular RGB video (2–4 people per sequence) together with initial per-frame SMPL pose and shape estimates (from TRACE and ViTPose) as input. It outputs a per-person canonical-space SDF and texture field (deformable via SMPL LBS), refined per-frame poses, and temporally consistent instance segmentation masks. The background is modeled as a separate NeRF++ field, and the final rendered image composites all human layers with the background via self-supervised decomposition.

## Method

MultiPly represents each person as an individual neural implicit field (SDF + radiance) in canonical space, deformed to observation space via SMPL linear blend skinning, forming a layered scene representation. A layer-wise differentiable volume rendering accumulates radiance across all person layers with explicit depth-ordering to handle inter-occlusions. A progressive prompting strategy feeds evolving deformed human meshes and projected SMPL keypoints as mask and point prompts to SAM, creating a closed-loop refinement of instance segmentation in both 2D and 3D. A confidence-guided alternating optimization uses the IoU between projected mesh masks and SAM masks to decide per-frame whether to optimize pose only (unreliable frames) or jointly optimize pose and shape/appearance (reliable frames), preventing corrupted shape updates from bad poses. Two inter-person losses—depth order and interpenetration—are applied on the learned clothed meshes to enforce spatially coherent and physically plausible multi-person arrangements.

## Results

On the Hi4D dataset, MultiPly achieves a volumetric IoU of 0.816, Chamfer distance of 2.53 cm, point-to-surface distance of 2.34 cm, and normal consistency of 0.789 for 3D human reconstruction, outperforming both ECON and Vid2Avatar across all metrics. On the in-the-wild MMM dataset, the method reaches V-IoU 0.826 and C-ℓ₂ 2.89 cm. For pose estimation on Hi4D, MultiPly reduces MPJPE to 69.4 mm and MVE to 83.6 mm, substantially improving over TRACE (95.6/115.7 mm) and CLIFF (85.7/102.1 mm), while also achieving the best contact distance (218.4 mm) and percentage of correct depth relations (0.709).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 3D reconstruction, MultiPly outperforms ECON [43] by a large margin on both datasets (e.g., V-IoU 0.816 vs. 0.787 and C-ℓ₂ 2.53 vs. 3.72 cm on Hi4D), and also surpasses Vid2Avatar [11] (V-IoU 0.816 vs. 0.783, C-ℓ₂ 2.53 vs. 3.02 cm on Hi4D), with the gap widening on the in-the-wild MMM dataset where ECON degrades significantly (V-IoU 0.760). For novel view synthesis, MultiPly beats the adapted multi-view method of Shuai et al. [36] on all rendering metrics (PSNR 20.7 vs. 19.6, LPIPS 0.0798 vs. 0.1099 on Hi4D). In instance segmentation, the progressive SAM prompting achieves IoU 0.963 and F1 0.990, surpassing the pretrained SCHP [26] network (IoU 0.937, F1 0.982). For pose estimation, MultiPly dominates both TRACE [39] (MPJPE 69.4 vs. 95.6 mm, CD 218.4 vs. 249.4 mm) and CLIFF [27] (MPJPE 69.4 vs. 85.7 mm, CD 218.4 vs. 351.7 mm), with the largest gains in contact distance and depth-order accuracy, reflecting better spatial arrangement of interacting people.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiPly extends the neural implicit human reconstruction paradigm (Vid2Avatar, ECON, PIFU-HD) from single-person to multi-person settings, and simultaneously extends multi-person reconstruction from expensive multi-view capture (Shuai et al., DeepMultiCap) to monocular in-the-wild video. The layer-wise volume rendering mechanism draws on layered neural representations (Jiakai et al.) and surface-based SDF rendering (Wang et al.), while the instance segmentation pipeline repurposes SAM's promptability in a closed-loop with evolving 3D human meshes. The confidence-guided alternating optimization is a novel training strategy that departs from prior joint pose-shape optimization by selectively freezing shape updates on unreliable frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to produce temporally and spatially coherent, high-fidelity 3D reconstructions of multiple interacting people from a single monocular video, opening the door to practical AR/VR applications like group telepresence and social activity replay without specialized capture rigs. Key limitations include linear complexity scaling with the number of people (making it impractical for crowds) and the absence of explicit hand modeling. Open questions include whether the confidence-guided strategy generalizes to longer sequences or more than four subjects, and whether integrating expressive body models (e.g., SMPL-X) can close the remaining gap in fine-grained articulation.
