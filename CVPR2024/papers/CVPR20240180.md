## Problem

Data-Free Quantization (DFQ) methods generate synthetic data from batch normalization statistics to replace unavailable original training data, but a noticeable distributional gap between synthetic and original data degrades quantized network performance. No existing DFQ method evaluates the reliability of individual synthetic samples before using them for training, so low-quality samples can mislead the quantization process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240180/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained full-precision CNN (ResNet-20/18/50 or MobileNet-V1) whose frozen BN statistics guide the generation of 5,120 synthetic RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) optimized over 1,000 iterations. It produces a quantized network with 3-bit or 4-bit weights and activations, trained via a knowledge-distillation objective that combines KL divergence, reliability-weighted cross-entropy, and intermediate-feature MSE losses.

## Method

The proposed pipeline has three stages. First, Reliability Filtering computes the self-entropy of the pre-trained network's predictions on each synthetic sample and partitions the dataset into high-reliable and low-reliable subsets using a dynamic threshold that decreases linearly over training epochs. Second, Multiple Pseudo-Labels assign a single argmax label (major pseudo-label) to high-reliable samples, while low-reliable samples receive auxiliary pseudo-labels consisting of both the primary and secondary predicted classes with probability-proportional weights to soften supervision and reduce misleading risk. Third, Pseudo-Label Training integrates these losses within a knowledge-distillation framework: the total objective combines KL divergence between teacher and student outputs, the reliability-weighted cross-entropy (major + auxiliary), and an MSE term on intermediate feature layers, with hyperparameters balancing each component.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20, the proposed method achieves 92.47% top-1 accuracy at 4-bit and 88.04% at 3-bit, outperforming all baselines except HAST at 3-bit (88.34%). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 66.94% (4-bit) and 57.03% (3-bit), surpassing the next-best methods (AdaDFQ, HAST) by 0.13–1.96% and 1.36–4.29% respectively. On ImageNet, the method achieves 67.02% (ResNet-18, 4-bit), 59.51% (MobileNet-V1, 4-bit), and 68.97% (ResNet-50, 4-bit), consistently outperforming all compared data-free quantization methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 12 data-free quantization baselines spanning both Generator-Based (GDFQ, DSG, Qimera, ARC, ARC+AIT, AdaSG, AdaDFQ) and Distill-Based (ZeroQ, IntraQ, HAST) approaches. On CIFAR-10 4-bit, the proposed method (92.47%) edges out HAST (92.36%), AdaDFQ (92.31%), and AdaSG (92.10%) by 0.11–0.37%. On CIFAR-100, the gains are more pronounced: 66.94% at 4-bit beats AdaDFQ (66.81%) and HAST (66.68%), while 57.03% at 3-bit surpasses HAST (55.67%) by 1.36% and AdaSG (52.76%) by 4.27%. On ImageNet, the method leads across all three architectures: ResNet-18 4-bit (67.02% vs. HAST 66.91%), MobileNet-V1 4-bit (59.51% vs. HAST 57.70%), and ResNet-50 4-bit (68.97% vs. AdaSG 68.58%). The only loss is on CIFAR-10 3-bit, where HAST achieves 88.34% versus the proposed 88.04% (−0.30%), which the authors attribute to HAST's focus on generating more hard samples rather than evaluating data reliability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Data-Free Quantization (DFQ) line of work, which itself builds on knowledge distillation frameworks (Hinton et al.) and BN-statistics-based synthetic data generation (ZeroQ, GDFQ, IntraQ, HAST). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a reliability evaluation step: prior DFQ methods (both Generator-Based and Distill-Based) treat all synthetic samples uniformly during quantized-network training, whereas this work uses self-entropy (borrowed from Kim et al.'s domain adaptation work) to partition synthetic data into high- and low-reliable subsets and assigns differentiated pseudo-labels (major vs. auxiliary) accordingly. The training backbone remains standard knowledge distillation with KL divergence and MSE feature matching, so the novelty is concentrated in the data evaluation and labeling pipeline rather than in the quantization or distillation machinery itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the pseudo-label filtering module (demonstrated in Table 6, where it improves GDFQ and IntraQ by 0.3–0.8% when grafted onto their synthetic data) suggests the reliability-evaluation idea could transfer to other data-free compression settings beyond quantization. However, the absolute gains over the strongest baselines (HAST, AdaDFQ) are modest—typically 0.1–1%—and the dynamic threshold schedule (Eq. 5) introduces additional hyperparameters that may be sensitive to dataset and architecture. An open question is whether the self-entropy metric remains a reliable proxy for sample quality in more extreme low-bit regimes (e.g., 2-bit) or for non-CNN architectures where BN statistics carry less distributional information.
