## Problem

Capturing a fully immersive 4D acoustic field of a real room typically requires hundreds of densely placed microphones, making it impractical for consumer use. Unlike visual novel view synthesis, sound is time-varying, travels slowly, and a single microphone records an amalgamation of waves from all directions, so standard sparse-view approaches do not transfer. The paper asks whether a handful of RIR measurements (≈12) plus rough planar geometry suffice to render accurate RIRs and music at arbitrary unseen locations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 12 omnidirectional RIR recordings (captured via sine sweep) with their xyz coordinates and a planar decomposition of the room (a small set of planes); the source position and orientation are unknown and must be inferred. It outputs monaural and binaural RIRs at any novel listener location, rendered music (RIR convolved with arbitrary source audio), and physically interpretable parameters: per-frequency source directivity heatmaps on a unit sphere and per-surface amplitude reflection responses. Binauralization is applied post-hoc by convolving each path's contribution with direction-specific HRIRs from the SADIE II dataset, so the model itself is trained only on monaural supervision.

## Method

DIFFRIR is a fully differentiable inverse-rendering framework that traces all specular reflection paths between source and listener via the image-source method (up to order 5, plus 'axial boosting' to order 50 for parallel-wall pairs to capture axial modes). Each path's contribution is the product of a direction-dependent source directivity (F octave-band heatmaps on 128 Fibonacci-lattice sphere points, interpolated with spherical Gaussians), per-surface reflection coefficients (F sigmoid-constrained amplitude values with linear frequency interpolation), and spherical/air-absorption attenuation from path length; a minimum-phase inverse Fourier transform converts the magnitude response to a time-domain filter. A spatially-invariant residual signal, blended with the traced paths via a 16-point temporal spline γ, absorbs diffuse reflections, diffraction, and higher-order effects. All parameters are optimized end-to-end against the 12 known RIRs using a multiscale log-spectral loss in an analysis-by-synthesis loop, after which the same equation is evaluated at any novel location for inference.

## Results

DIFFRIR outperforms all baselines on both RIR and Music prediction across all four rooms, using only 12 training RIRs. On the RIR Mag metric (lower is better), DIFFRIR achieves 5.22 (Classroom), 1.21 (Dampened), 9.13 (Hallway), and 4.86 (Complex), compared to the best baseline NN at 5.99, 1.36, 10.14, and 5.52 respectively. On the Music Mag metric, DIFFRIR scores 2.71, 1.59, 2.59, and 2.25, again beating the best baseline (NN) on every room. The ENV metric shows consistent improvements as well, with DIFFRIR achieving 0.94, 0.56, 2.95, and 0.92 on RIR across the four rooms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFRIR outperforms all three DNN-based baselines (DeepIR, NAF, INRAS) and both interpolation baselines (NN, Linear) on every metric across all four rooms. DeepIR [40], which predicts RIRs from spatial coordinates alone without geometry, shows the largest gaps, e.g., 9.23 vs. 5.22 Mag on Classroom RIR and 15.71 vs. 9.13 on Hallway RIR. NAF [30], which incorporates local geometric features, is the closest DNN competitor but still trails DIFFRIR by 0.6–1.4 Mag on RIR across rooms. INRAS [45], which explicitly models specular and diffuse reflections on a 3D mesh, performs worst among DNN baselines (e.g., 9.99 Mag on Classroom RIR, 4.20 on Dampened RIR), likely due to its reliance on dense mesh geometry rather than sparse planar representations. The simple NN interpolation baseline is the strongest non-DNN method, but DIFFRIR still improves over it by 0.5–0.7 Mag on RIR and 0.2–0.4 Mag on Music across rooms, demonstrating that the differentiable physical model adds value beyond spatial interpolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFFRIR draws on the classical image-source method for computing specular reflection paths and the analysis-by-synthesis paradigm from differentiable rendering (DDSP, DiffImpact), but departs fundamentally from prior learning-based RIR prediction methods (DeepIR, NAF, INRAS) that use black-box DNNs requiring 1,000+ training RIRs. Instead of learning an implicit coordinate-to-RIR mapping, DIFFRIR explicitly models physically interpretable parameters—source directivity via spherical Gaussian heatmaps, per-surface frequency-dependent reflectivity, and a learned residual—within a fully differentiable renderer optimized against sparse ground-truth RIRs. The paper positions the task as the acoustic analogue of novel view synthesis in vision, but highlights that sound's time-varying, slower propagation and the microphone's omnidirectional capture make direct transfer of visual NVS techniques inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to reconstruct a room's full acoustic field from just 12 RIR measurements and planar geometry makes immersive audio virtualization accessible to ordinary users, with direct applications in VR, acoustics-aware interior design, and robotics. The interpretable parameters (directivity maps, surface reflection coefficients) enable zero-shot virtual speaker rotation, translation, and panel insertion—capabilities unavailable from black-box predictors. Key limitations include the reliance on planar geometry (no full 3D mesh), the assumption of a stationary source, and the spatially uniform residual that cannot capture localized diffuse effects. The authors note that future work could eliminate the need for explicit RIR measurement by modeling acoustics from natural audio recordings, which would significantly broaden the method's applicability.
