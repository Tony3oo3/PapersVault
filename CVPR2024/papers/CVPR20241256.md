## Problem

Existing NeRF color editing methods suffer from three critical limitations: they require over a minute (up to 2 hours) for convergence, making them unsuitable for interactive use; they produce color bleeding beyond object boundaries; and they fail to maintain consistent view-dependent effects across 360° viewpoints. No quantitative benchmark existed to fairly compare these editing approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241256/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Instant-NGP NeRF and a single training image in which the user has selected a region (e.g., via Photoshop) and applied an HSV color shift to that region. It produces a recolored NeRF whose last-layer weights and a small segmentation MLP have been updated, enabling rendering of novel views where the color edit is spatially confined to the target object and view-dependent effects (specular highlights, reflections) remain consistent. Training completes in under 5 seconds (200 Adam iterations at lr=0.01) on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

IReNe selectively re-trains only the last layer of the color MLP rather than the full network, drastically reducing the parameter count. A key insight is that neurons in this last layer specialize: some encode view-dependent appearance while others encode diffuse color. The method automatically classifies each neuron by sweeping the view direction azimuth and flagging neurons whose activation profile shows high relative variance or negative values as view-dependent, then freezes their weights to preserve specular consistency. A lightweight two-layer MLP (32→64→1) is trained on the multiresolution hash grid features to output a per-point soft segmentation probability α, which alpha-blends the original and edited colors to confine the edit to the target region without requiring ground-truth masks. All components are optimized jointly with a standard RGB loss between the rendered and user-edited image.

## Results

On the NeRF Synthetic dataset, IReNe achieves PSNR 30.33, SSIM 0.959, and LPIPS 0.035, outperforming PaletteNeRF (29.92/0.960/0.038) and RecolorNeRF (29.00/0.943/0.048). On real-world datasets, IReNe reaches PSNR 25.63 (LLFF) and 26.80 (Mip NeRF 360), approaching the pre-trained Instant-NGP reference (25.95 and 27.32 respectively), indicating near-lossless editing. In terms of speed, IReNe completes editing in ~5 seconds, achieving 5×–500× speedups over PaletteNeRF (30 min–2 hr) and RecolorNeRF (15–20 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IReNe outperforms PaletteNeRF on all three metrics across all three datasets, with the largest gap on Mip NeRF 360 (PSNR 26.80 vs. 22.24, a 4.56 dB improvement), where PaletteNeRF's global-only editing causes severe color bleeding. Against RecolorNeRF, IReNe leads by 1.33 dB PSNR on NeRF Synthetic and 1.56 dB on LLFF; RecolorNeRF fails to converge entirely on Mip NeRF 360. Qualitatively, ICE-NeRF (compared only visually, as code is unavailable) exhibits noticeable color bleeding at object boundaries in real scenes and degrades pre-existing view-dependent information, whereas IReNe's soft segmentation and view-dependent neuron freezing prevent both issues. On speed, IReNe (~5 s) is 5× faster than ICE-NeRF (~25 s) and 180×–240× faster than PaletteNeRF (30 min–2 hr), making it the only method suitable for interactive use.

## Relation to Prior Work

IReNe builds directly on Instant-NGP's multiresolution hash-grid NeRF architecture, reusing its pre-trained density and color MLPs as a frozen backbone. It departs from prior NeRF color-editing methods (PaletteNeRF, RecolorNeRF, ICE-NeRF) by replacing full-model retraining or palette optimization with selective last-layer fine-tuning, and by introducing a lightweight soft 3D segmentation network to confine edits to user-specified regions. The key conceptual departure is the observation that last-layer neurons specialize into diffuse and view-dependent groups, enabling the authors to freeze view-dependent weights and preserve multi-view consistency without explicit decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing NeRF recoloring from minutes or hours to under 5 seconds, IReNe makes interactive 3D color editing practically viable for creative pipelines, and the first quantitative benchmark for NeRF color editing provides a missing evaluation standard for the field. However, the method still depends on external tools (e.g., Photoshop) for user edits, the soft segmentation can be unreliable in some cases, and it does not handle indirect illumination changes caused by recolored objects. Open questions include whether the neuron-specialization observation generalizes beyond Instant-NGP architectures and whether the approach can be extended to more complex edits (geometry, material, lighting).
