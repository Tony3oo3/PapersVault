## Problem

Existing automatic evaluation metrics for image captioning—whether classic n-gram/scene-graph metrics or data-driven similarity-based ones—exhibit weak correlation with human judgments (SOTA ~0.56 vs. human-human ~0.73 on Flickr8K). This gap arises because similarity-based metrics compute scalar similarities using embeddings pretrained on tasks unrelated to captioning evaluation, and they struggle to handle hallucinations and generalize across diverse image-text pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241424/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Polos takes a candidate caption, N reference captions, and an RGB image as multimodal input. The image is encoded by CLIP ViT-B/16, while text is encoded by both the CLIP text encoder and a RoBERTa model pretrained with supervised SimCSE. The output is a single scalar score in [0,1] (via sigmoid) that directly regresses the human Likert judgment, with the max over references used as the final aggregation.

## Method

Polos is trained under the M²LHF framework, which formulates metric learning as a supervised regression problem over multimodal inputs rather than a ranking or unsupervised similarity task. The key architectural component is a parallel feature extraction mechanism that computes, for each reference, the concatenation of F(c_clip, r_clip), F(c_clip, v), and F(c_rb, r_rb), where F(c,r) = [c; r; |c−r|; c⊙r] combines raw features, element-wise absolute differences, and Hadamard products to capture fine-grained similarity structure in the embedding space. An MLP (chosen over a Transformer based on pilot experiments) maps these features to a per-reference score, which is passed through a sigmoid and aggregated via max across references. The model is trained with MSE loss on the newly constructed Polaris dataset—131,020 human judgments from 550 evaluators over captions from ten image captioning models on MSCOCO and nocaps—making it roughly ten times larger than prior metric-training datasets.

## Results

Polos achieves state-of-the-art Kendall's tau correlation with human judgments across all four primary benchmarks: 57.6 on Composite, 56.4 on Flickr8K-Expert, 37.8 on Flickr8K-CF, and 57.8 on Polaris, outperforming the previous best (RefPAC-S) by 0.3, 0.5, 0.2, and 1.8 points respectively. On PASCAL-50S pairwise ranking, Polos reaches 86.5% mean accuracy (70.0% HC, 79.0% MM), surpassing UMIC (85.1% mean). On the FOIL hallucination detection task, Polos achieves 95.4% accuracy in the 4-reference setting, edging out RefPAC-S (94.9%) and MID (90.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Polos outperforms all 16 baselines on every dataset where they are directly comparable. Among classic metrics, CIDEr (37.7/43.9/24.6/52.1) and SPICE (40.3/44.9/24.4/51.0) are the strongest but trail Polos by 20+ points on Composite and Flickr8K-Expert. Among similarity-based metrics, MID (55.7/54.9/37.3/51.3) and RefCLIP-S (55.4/53.0/36.4/52.3) are the closest, yet Polos leads by 1.9–5.5 points across datasets. The most competitive comparison is against RefPAC-S (57.3/55.9/37.6/56.0), the previous SOTA learning-based metric, which Polos surpasses by narrow margins of 0.3, 0.5, 0.2, and 1.8 points. On PASCAL-50S, Polos beats UMIC (85.1% mean) by 1.4 points and MID (85.2%) by 1.3 points, with the largest gap on HC pairs (70.0% vs. 67.7% for RefPAC-S). On FOIL hallucination detection, Polos (93.3%/95.4%) slightly trails RefPAC-S (93.7%/94.9%) in the 1-ref setting but leads in the 4-ref setting, and substantially outperforms CLIP-S (87.2%/87.2%) and CIDEr (82.5%/90.6%), confirming superior hallucination sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Polos extends the RUSE regression framework (Shimanaka et al., WMT 2018) and the COMET paradigm (Rei et al., EMNLP 2020) from machine translation evaluation to the multimodal image-captioning setting, introducing the M²LHF framework that regresses human judgments from joint image-text inputs. It departs from similarity-based metrics (CLIPScore, MID, PAC-S) that compute scalar cosine similarities over CLIP embeddings, and from ranking-based metrics (UMIC) that struggle with multiple references, by instead using a parallel feature extraction mechanism that combines difference and Hadamard products over both CLIP and SimCSE-pretrained RoBERTa embeddings. The key conceptual shift is treating caption evaluation as a supervised regression problem over richly composed multimodal features rather than an unsupervised similarity computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Polaris dataset (131K judgments from 550 evaluators) is roughly ten times larger than prior metric-training datasets and introduces modern model outputs, addressing a critical data bottleneck for training supervised captioning metrics. However, the metric still overestimates captions that omit fine-grained contextual details, a limitation traced to CLIP's coarse image-text alignment; the authors identify RegionCLIP-style fine-grained alignment as a natural next step. The persistent gap between the best metric–human correlation (~0.58) and human–human correlation (~0.73) remains an open challenge, suggesting that current embedding-based approaches capture only part of what human evaluators assess.
