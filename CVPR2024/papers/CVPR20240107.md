## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic objects from monocular video is severely underconstrained, and no existing method can produce consistent, high-quality novel views from camera positions significantly different from the training views. Prior approaches either rely on unrealistic multi-view cues (e.g., camera teleportation in D-NeRF) or require geometry proxies with a priori knowledge, failing on realistic monocular sequences where strong temporal regularization is essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240107/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video sequence with per-frame object masks (obtainable from SAM) and camera poses (from COLMAP for real captures), requiring only that the object remain in view with no assumption on its 3D location. It produces a deformable 3D Gaussian representation stored in oriented local volumes that can be rendered at 400×400 or 800×800 resolution, along with a coarse parametric point model providing explicit point trajectories and depth maps. The Gaussian parameters are shared across all timesteps, so the final representation is compact and extractable for real-time rendering without per-Gaussian networks.

## Method

NPGs use a two-stage approach. Stage 1 fits a coarse parametric point model where M points at each timestep are expressed as a linear combination of K learnable basis vectors (K << T), with coefficients produced by a 6-layer MLP over temporally encoded time; this is optimized with Chamfer-distance mask loss, a rigidity loss preserving neighborhood distances, and a RAFT-based optical flow consistency loss. Stage 2 places 3D Gaussians inside oriented local volumes (each defined by a point and its 19 nearest neighbors with a fixed triangle orientation), where each Gaussian's position is parameterized as barycentric interpolation weights over the volume points and its rotation lives in the local coordinate frame. The coarse model from Stage 1 drives all temporal motion of the Gaussians, while Gaussian parameters (weights, scales, rotations, spherical harmonics) are shared across the full sequence and optimized with L1 + D-SSIM losses plus 3DGS-style densification. This design avoids per-Gaussian MLPs, keeping rendering at ~20 fps while the low-rank basis provides the strong cross-timestep regularization needed for sparse monocular captures.

## Results

On the challenging Unbiased4D monocular dataset (EMF ω ≈ 25), NPGs achieves 22.35 PSNR, 0.905 SSIM, and 0.095 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline (HyperNeRF) by 3.8 dB in PSNR. On the synthetic D-NeRF dataset (EMF ω ≈ 2135), NPGs reaches an average of 33.92 PSNR, 0.98 SSIM, and 0.03 LPIPS across 7 scenes, surpassing TiNeuVox (31.52 PSNR) and HexPlane (29.81 PSNR). Rendering speed is approximately 0.05 s/frame (20 fps) at 400×400, compared to 2.15 s/frame for TiNeuVox and 0.94 s/frame for HexPlane.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NPGs outperforms all five baselines on both datasets. On D-NeRF (average over 7 scenes), NPGs leads TiNeuVox by 2.4 dB PSNR (33.92 vs. 31.52), HexPlane by 4.1 dB (33.92 vs. 29.81), and D-NeRF by 4.7 dB (33.92 vs. 29.22). On the far more challenging Unbiased4D monocular setting, the margins widen substantially: NPGs beats HyperNeRF by 3.8 dB (22.35 vs. 18.56), Nerfies by 5.0 dB (22.35 vs. 17.36), HexPlane by 6.0 dB (22.35 vs. 16.33), and TiNeuVox by 6.4 dB (22.35 vs. 15.92). The paper attributes these gains to the two-stage design: the low-rank deformation basis in stage 1 provides strong temporal regularization that prevents geometric inconsistencies, while the 3D Gaussians in stage 2 recover high-frequency detail that deformation-based NeRF methods blur out. Notably, on D-NeRF the Lego and T-Rex scenes (which contain a large static ground plane) remain the weakest cases for NPGs, where it slightly trails TiNeuVox and HexPlane on PSNR, though it still leads on SSIM and LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

NPGs extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from static to dynamic scenes by embedding Gaussians in oriented local volumes whose positions are driven by a coarse parametric point model, rather than by per-Gaussian neural deformation fields as in concurrent works (Deformable3DGS, 4DGaussianSplatting). The coarse model itself follows the low-rank deformation basis formulation from KeyTr, but is repurposed here not as the final output but as a regularization scaffold for a second-stage Gaussian optimization. This two-stage decoupling—learn coarse deformation first, then optimize fine appearance on top—departs from methods like HexPlane and TiNeuVox that jointly optimize deformation and radiance, and from template-based approaches (SMPL-guided NeRFs) that require category-specific priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple low-rank parametric point model, when used as an explicit geometric constraint, can dramatically outperform more complex neural deformation fields in the severely underconstrained monocular setting, suggesting that structured regularization may be more effective than expressive-but-unconstrained representations for ill-posed dynamic reconstruction. Practical impact is significant: the method renders at ~20 fps versus 2.15 s/frame for TiNeuVox, and the Gaussian representation is directly extractable for real-time use. Limitations include the requirement that the object remain in view, sensitivity to very static cameras (template collapse to a flat surface), and evaluation with ground-truth novel views on only one of five Unbiased4D sequences, leaving the generalization claim partially unverified.
