## Problem

Prior generative 3D part assembly methods focus on learning representations for individual parts but neglect the inherent part-whole hierarchies in 3D shapes. The paper identifies that super-part poses (e.g., a chair's seat or back) provide strong geometric hints for constituent part poses, and that predicting fewer super-part poses is inherently easier than predicting all part poses directly, yet no prior method exploits this hierarchy in its architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of N part point clouds (1000 points each, sampled via FPS and normalized to canonical space via PCA) along with a random Gaussian noise vector for diverse generation. It outputs 6-DoF poses (3D translation constrained to (-1,1) via tanh, and a unit-norm 4D quaternion for rotation) for every part, plus latent super-part poses that are never directly supervised. Super-parts are constructed unsupervised by grouping parts whose axis-aligned bounding boxes differ by less than 0.2, requiring no semantic labels.

## Method

The architecture consists of two sequential modules. The super-part encoder uses a shared PointNet to obtain permutation-invariant set-level features per super-part, then applies a two-layer 8-head MHA to reason about inter-super-part relationships and predicts latent super-part poses via a 4-layer MLP head. The entire point cloud is then rigidly transformed by these latent poses before being fed to the part encoder, which extracts part-level features via another PointNet, fuses super-part information through a cross-level attention module (part features as queries, super-part features as keys/values), and performs within-level part-to-part message passing where latent super-part poses are concatenated to part features before attention score computation. Training uses a Min-of-N loss over 5 noise samples with only ground-truth part poses as supervision (super-part poses are purely latent), combining translation L2, per-part rotation Chamfer distance, and global shape Chamfer distance with weights 1, 10, and 1 respectively.

## Results

On PartNet (Chair, Table, Lamp) at Chamfer distance threshold 0.01, the proposed method achieves SCD of 0.51/0.28/0.70, part accuracy (PA) of 64.13%/64.83%/38.80%, and connectivity accuracy (CA) of 49.28%/58.45%/64.16%, outperforming the strongest baseline IET [34] across all categories. Averaged over thresholds 0.01–0.05, the method reaches mPA of 76.79%/76.19%/53.31% and mCA of 65.32%/70.26%/78.15%, yielding roughly 2% and 3% absolute gains over IET. The method also leads in diversity metrics (QDS and WQDS), e.g., QDS of 5.62/9.58/7.12 vs. IET's 4.15/9.09/6.98.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on every category and metric. Against the closest competitor IET [34], it improves PA by 1.33/3.16/0.12 points and CA by 0.83/2.27/1.54 points on Chair/Table/Lamp respectively, with the largest gains on Table. RGL [21] and Score [4] trail IET by roughly 10–15 PA points, while DGL [33] lags further behind (e.g., 39.00 vs. 64.13 PA on Chair). The three older baselines (B-Global, B-LSTM, B-Complement) are substantially weaker, with PA below 30% on most categories. In the human study, the proposed method is preferred over IET, RGL, and DGL in 95.2%, 97.6%, and 99.5% of pairwise comparisons respectively, confirming the quantitative advantage translates to perceived visual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based part assembly line initiated by DGL [33] and IET [34], which model part-to-part relationships via attention-based message passing over point cloud features. The key departure is the introduction of a part-whole hierarchy: parts are grouped into super-parts by geometric similarity (following the grouping strategy of [33, 34]), and a two-stage encoder (super-part encoder → part encoder) predicts poses hierarchically, with super-part poses treated as latent variables requiring no ground-truth supervision. The cross-level attention mechanism that fuses super-part representations into part features, and the hierarchy-aware within-level message passing that concatenates latent super-part poses into attention scores, are the architectural novelties not present in prior flat-graph or flat-Transformer approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves state-of-the-art part and connectivity accuracy on PartNet while offering an interpretable intermediate representation (predicted super-part poses) that prior flat-assembly methods lack, which could aid debugging and downstream applications like robotic planning. However, the super-part grouping is purely geometric (axis-aligned bounding box similarity) and may not align with semantic structure, limiting interpretability for objects where geometric similarity diverges from functional grouping. The evaluation is restricted to three PartNet categories (chairs, tables, lamps), and the authors themselves note that lamp parts show limited hierarchical variation, suggesting the approach's gains may be category-dependent. The stated future direction of combining part generation with assembly to create shapes from scratch remains an open challenge.
