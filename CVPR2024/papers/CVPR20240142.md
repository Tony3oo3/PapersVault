## Problem

Existing indoor 3D reconstruction methods, whether classic volumetric approaches (TSDF, Surfels) or neural implicit representations (NeRF-based), reconstruct only visible surfaces and leave large occluded regions—such as furniture backsides, contact surfaces between objects and floors, and hidden wall areas—entirely missing. This incompleteness is a critical barrier for AR/VR and embodied AI applications that require full, editable scene geometry for object manipulation and repositioning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of depth images with associated camera poses as input and produces a complete 3D scene mesh via Marching Cubes over predicted SDFs. The scene is represented by a 9-level hierarchical octree (L=9) split at level j=4 into coarse features (levels 0–4) and fine features (levels 5–9), with latent features stored at the eight corners of each octree node. During testing, only visible depth observations are available as supervision; the complete ground-truth mesh is not required.

## Method

The core architecture is a coarse-fine hierarchical octree feature volume coupled with a dual-decoder design: a scene-specific Geo-decoder (fed fine-level features, optimized online per scene) reconstructs visible surfaces, while a cross-scene-trained 3D Inpainter (fed coarse-level features, trained offline on multiple complete scenes) completes occluded surfaces by learning generalizable contextual structure priors. The 3D Inpainter uses a decoder-only latent optimization structure (discarding BNV-Fusion's encoder-decoder) trained with BCE, eikonal, and smoothness losses on SDFs sampled from complete meshes. At test time, the 3D Inpainter is frozen and only the Geo-decoder parameters and octree features are optimized using visible depth supervision; the coarse features are also refined through the frozen Inpainter. A layer-coverage criterion (threshold α=3 non-retrieved layers) classifies query points as visible or invisible at mesh extraction time, routing them to the appropriate decoder.

## Results

On the 3D-CRS dataset (5 test scenes), the proposed method achieves average Completeness of 89.3% and F1 of 89.9%, outperforming BNV-Fusion (76.5% Comp., 83.7% F1) by 12.8 and 6.2 points respectively. On the iTHOR dataset (6 test scenes), it reaches 89.8% Completeness and 89.3% F1, surpassing BNV-Fusion (72.3% Comp., 80.7% F1) by 17.5 and 8.6 points. The paper reports relative completeness gains of 16.8% on 3D-CRS and 24.2% on iTHOR over the strongest baseline. Accuracy scores are competitive (90.4% on 3D-CRS, 88.8% on iTHOR) but slightly below BNV-Fusion, which benefits from ShapeNet pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TSDF Fusion [28], the proposed method wins decisively on all three metrics across both datasets, with Completeness improving from ~60–66% to ~89–90%. Against Go-Surf [22], the method wins on Completeness (89.3% vs 70.8% on 3D-CRS; 89.8% vs 69.8% on iTHOR) and F1, while Accuracy is roughly comparable (90.4% vs 90.1% on 3D-CRS). Against BNV-Fusion [11], the strongest baseline, the method achieves substantially higher Completeness (89.3% vs 76.5% on 3D-CRS; 89.8% vs 72.3% on iTHOR) and F1 (89.9% vs 83.7%; 89.3% vs 80.7%), but slightly lower Accuracy (90.4% vs 92.5% on 3D-CRS; 88.8% vs 91.3% on iTHOR). The Accuracy gap is attributed to BNV-Fusion's encoder being pre-trained on the large ShapeNet dataset, which improves visible-surface fidelity, whereas the proposed method does not use ShapeNet pre-training. The paper notes that its Accuracy metric includes both visible and occluded regions, making the comparison slightly unfavorable for the proposed method since occluded-region prediction errors lower the average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hierarchical octree feature volume from NGLOD [21] and the neural volume fusion paradigm from BNV-Fusion [11], but departs from both in a key architectural way: rather than a single encoder-decoder pipeline, it introduces a dual-decoder system (Geo-decoder + 3D Inpainter) that explicitly separates visible and occluded surface reconstruction using coarse-fine octree features. It positions itself against the broader line of neural implicit reconstruction methods (NeRF, Neural RGB-D, BNV-Fusion) that handle sensor noise and small gaps but fail at large occluded regions, and against object-centric or 2D-generative completion approaches that lack scene-level geometric fidelity. The cross-scene training of the 3D Inpainter on complete meshes is a deliberate departure from BNV-Fusion's per-scene encoder-decoder, trading per-scene specialization for generalizable occlusion priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Completing occluded surfaces (furniture backsides, floor under objects, walls behind items) is a prerequisite for practical AR/VR scene editing, room rearrangement, and embodied AI navigation, yet prior methods leave these regions as holes or inflated artifacts. The demonstrated cross-scene generalization of the 3D Inpainter—trained on 15 scenes and applied to unseen ones—suggests a path toward a reusable occlusion prior, though the training set is small (20 scenes in 3D-CRS, 13 in iTHOR) and the accuracy in occluded regions remains lower than in visible regions. Open questions include scalability to larger and more diverse scene collections, whether the approach extends to outdoor or multi-room environments, and whether the 3D-CRS dataset (20 scenes) is sufficient to validate the claimed generalization beyond the tested distributions.
