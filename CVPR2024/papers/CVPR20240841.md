## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for pre-trained Vision Transformers has focused heavily on reducing trainable parameters, but has largely neglected the critical trade-off between preserving the pre-trained model's generalization capacity and enabling effective task-specific adaptation. Existing methods either over-adapt (distorting the singular spectrum and losing generalization) or under-adapt (insufficiently modifying the weight structure for downstream tasks), and there has been no unified analytical framework to understand where each method sits on this spectrum.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images resized to 224×224 pixels, processed through ViT or Swin Transformer backbones pre-trained on ImageNet-21K. During fine-tuning, only lightweight scaling vectors (s_left, s_right) and a shifting vector (f) are learned per layer, totaling 0.33–0.47M parameters for ViT-B/16. At inference, these are absorbed into the original weight matrices via re-parameterization, so the deployed model requires no extra storage or computational overhead beyond the standard forward pass.

## Method

RLRR formulates fine-tuning of each weight matrix W as W + s_left ⊙ W ⊙ s_right^T + f, where the frozen pre-trained matrix W is added residually to a low-rank rescaling term. Under SVD, each singular component becomes (1 + s_left[i]·s_right[j])·λ_d·d_d[i]·u_d[j], where the constant '1' anchors the original representation capacity while the product term provides flexible, element-wise perturbation. This dual-sided (row and column) rescaling offers more tuning flexibility than SSF's single-sided scaling, while the residual term prevents the tuned parameters from deviating excessively from the pre-trained model. The paper further provides a unified SVD-based analysis showing how adaptation-based methods (Adapter, LoRA), prompt-based methods (VPT), and scaling/shifting methods (SSF) each interact with the singular spectrum, revealing their respective over- and under-adaptation tendencies.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 tasks, ViT-B/16), RLRR achieves a mean accuracy of 74.5% with only 0.33M trainable parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (65.6%) by 8.9% and the strongest PEFT baseline ARC (73.4%) by 1.1%. With the AugReg-augmented backbone (RLRR*), the mean rises to 75.1%, outperforming ARC* (74.3%) by 0.8%. On the five FGVC datasets, RLRR reaches 90.4% mean accuracy (91.0% with AugReg), beating LoRA (89.5%) and ARC (90.1%) while using a comparable parameter budget (0.47M). On larger backbones (ViT-L/16, ViT-H/14), RLRR leads the state-of-the-art by 2.7% and 2.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLRR clearly outperforms LoRA (74.5% vs. 69.2% on VTAB-1k; 90.4% vs. 89.5% on FGVC) despite having a similar parameter count (0.33M vs. 0.29M), which the authors attribute to the residual design preventing under-adaptation. Against SSF, RLRR wins on VTAB-1k (74.5% vs. 73.1%) but trails slightly on FGVC (90.4% vs. 90.7%); with AugReg, RLRR* reverses this to 91.0% vs. 90.7%. RLRR surpasses ARC by 1.1% on VTAB-1k and 0.3% on FGVC, and RLRR* beats ARC* by 0.8% and 0.3% respectively. Against AdaptFormer and FacT, RLRR leads by 2.2% and 1.3% on VTAB-1k. The largest margin is over VPT-Deep (74.5% vs. 69.4%) and Adapter (74.5% vs. 55.8%), confirming that the SVD-motivated residual rescaling design is substantially more effective than prompt-based or bottleneck-adapter strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SSF (Scaling & Shifting Features) line of work by adding a left-side scaling vector and, crucially, a residual term that anchors the tuned matrix to the frozen pre-trained weights. It also introduces a unified SVD-based analytical lens through which existing PEFT strategies—Adapter, LoRA, VPT, and SSF—are re-expressed, revealing that each method perturbs the singular spectrum and singular vectors in structurally different ways. The key conceptual departure from prior PEFT work is an explicit framing of the over-adaptation vs. under-adaptation trade-off, which the authors argue has been underexplored despite the prevalence of low-rank methods outperforming full fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SVD-based unified framework offers a useful diagnostic for understanding why different PEFT methods succeed or fail, and the residual design is a simple, inference-free mechanism (re-parameterizable into the original weight matrix) that practitioners can adopt with minimal engineering overhead. However, the empirical gains over the strongest baselines (SSF, ARC) are modest—roughly 0.8–1.1% on VTAB-1k—and the method's advantage over LoRA, while consistent, is partly attributable to the residual anchoring rather than the low-rank rescaling per se. Open questions include whether the SVD perspective can inform better initialization of the scaling vectors, whether the approach transfers to generative or multimodal Transformer architectures, and whether the trade-off framing generalizes beyond image classification to tasks like detection or segmentation.
