## Problem

Existing contrastive learning methods for semantic segmentation either rely on single-scale features, capturing only limited local or global context, or treat multi-scale and cross-scale contrastive losses independently, leading to inconsistent feature arrangements across scales. Additionally, prior approaches do not specifically target boundary regions of misclassified areas when selecting negative samples, missing an opportunity to exploit fine-grained spatial details that are critical for accurate segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (e.g., 1024×2048 for Cityscapes, 512×512 crops for ADE20K and COCO-Stuff) and produces pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks. All contrastive learning components (representative anchors, BANE sampling, pixel-anchor loss) are active only during training and are fully detached at inference, so the deployed model incurs no additional computational cost beyond the base segmentation network.

## Method

Contexttrast introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of a standard CNN encoder-decoder. First, Contextual Contrastive Learning (CCL) computes class-wise representative anchors at every encoder layer, then fuses the highest-layer (global) anchors into lower-layer (local) anchors via a weighted sum, so each updated anchor encodes both local and global context. A pixel-anchor InfoNCE loss then pulls intra-class embedding features toward their updated anchor while pushing inter-class features away, operating jointly with standard cross-entropy. Second, Boundary-Aware Negative (BANE) sampling decomposes the prediction into class-wise binary error maps, applies a distance transform to identify pixels near the boundary of incorrectly predicted regions, and selects the K% closest-to-boundary negative samples for the contrastive loss, ensuring harder and more informative gradients that progressively sharpen decision boundaries during training.

## Results

On Cityscapes, HRNet+Contextrast achieves 82.20% mIoU (+3.72 over baseline), outperforming the strongest prior contrastive method (Pissas et al., 81.50%) and region-aware contrastive learning (Hu et al., 81.90%). Across all five datasets (Cityscapes, CamVid, COCO-Stuff, ADE20K, PASCAL-C), Contextrast consistently yields the highest mIoU among contrastive-learning-based methods for all three backbones (DeepLabV3, HRNet, OCRNet). Boundary mIoU (B-mIoU) on Cityscapes improves by +1.83 (5px), +1.76 (7px), and +1.68 (10px) over the closest multi-scale competitor, confirming the benefit of BANE sampling for boundary regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Contextrast outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and backbone. Against the closest multi-scale competitor (Pissas et al., ECCV'22), HRNet+Contextrast gains +0.70 mIoU on Cityscapes (82.20 vs 81.50) and +1.19 on CamVid (84.33 vs 83.14), while Pissas et al. actually degrades on COCO-Stuff for DeepLabV3 (−0.53) and OCRNet (−0.45) due to conflicts between their separate multi-scale and cross-scale losses. Compared to single-scale methods, Contextrast surpasses ContrastiveSeg (Wang et al., ICCV'21) by +1.20 mIoU on Cityscapes (82.20 vs 81.00) and region-aware contrastive learning (Hu et al., ICCV'21) by +0.30 (82.20 vs 81.90). The boundary-aware BANE sampling provides an additional +0.19 mIoU over semi-hard sampling on Cityscapes (82.20 vs 82.01) and a larger +0.95 on CamVid (84.33 vs 83.38), confirming its particular benefit for boundary regions where B-mIoU improves by up to +1.83 over Pissas et al.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive-learning-for-segmentation line initiated by Wang et al. (ICCV'21) and Hu et al. (ICCV'21), which applied InfoNCE-style losses with single-scale (last-layer) features and memory-bank or region-center anchors. It directly addresses the multi-scale gap identified in Pissas et al. (ECCV'22), whose separate multi-scale and cross-scale losses can produce conflicting gradient directions; Contextrast instead unifies scales through a single set of 'representative anchors' that are updated top-down from the last encoder layer, providing a consistent cross-scale criterion. The boundary-aware negative (BANE) sampling draws on boundary-refinement ideas from SegFix [50] and Active Boundary Loss [38], repurposing distance-transformed prediction-error maps to select harder negatives for the contrastive objective rather than for a separate boundary loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Contextrast offers a drop-in, training-only contrastive objective (no extra inference modules) that yields consistent 1–3 mIoU gains across five datasets and three CNN backbones, with the BANE sampling component contributing the largest share of the improvement—suggesting that *where* you sample negatives matters as much as *what* loss you use. The method is limited to CNN encoders (DeepLabV3, HRNet, OCRNet); whether the representative-anchor mechanism transfers to transformer-based segmenters (e.g., Segmenter, Swin-UNet) remains untested. An open question is whether the anchor-fusion weights (w_h, w_l) and the boundary-sampling ratio K can be made adaptive per layer or per training stage rather than fixed hyperparameters, and whether the approach composes with recent foundation-model or self-supervised pretraining pipelines for segmentation.
