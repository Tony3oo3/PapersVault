## Problem

Most pre-trained-model-based class-incremental learning (CIL) methods rely on prompt pools or impose parameter-update constraints (e.g., knowledge distillation) to prevent forgetting, yet the paper shows these constraints actually hinder plasticity for new classes due to similar parameter sensitivity across tasks. The paper asks whether a simple shared adapter, tuned incrementally without any regularization, can serve as a superior continual learner compared to prompt-based or SSF-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224, ViT-B/16 patch size) and outputs a class label over all classes seen so far. Critically, no image samples are stored between sessions: the only retained information is class prototypes and covariance matrices, from which synthetic features are sampled to retrain the classifier. The adapter (1.19M params) is shared across all sessions, so model size remains constant regardless of the number of incremental tasks.

## Method

The method incrementally fine-tunes a shared AdaptFormer-style adapter and a local cosine-margin classifier in each session, deliberately omitting any knowledge-distillation or parameter-constraint loss. After each session, it estimates the semantic shift of old-class prototypes by computing the feature displacement of current-task samples between the previous and current backbone, weighted by Gaussian similarity to each old prototype. These shifted prototypes are then used to sample synthetic features from per-class Gaussian distributions, which retrain a single unified classifier (SSCA) via cross-entropy, aligning all class decision boundaries in the updated feature space without ever accessing past images.

## Results

SSIAT achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four CIL benchmarks with only 1.19M tunable parameters (shared adapter). On ImageNetR, it reaches 79.38% A_Last and 83.63% A_Avg; on ImageNetA, 62.43% A_Last and 70.83% A_Avg; on CUB200, 88.75% A_Last and 93.00% A_Avg; and on CIFAR-100, 91.35% A_Last and 94.35% A_Avg. In the long-sequence setting (20 sessions of 10 classes), SSIAT maintains 75.67% A_Last on ImageNetR and 59.16% A_Last on ImageNetA, substantially outperforming SLCA which drops by 5–10% under the same protocol. On the cross-domain VTAB benchmark, SSIAT achieves 94.21% average accuracy, exceeding Adam-based methods by 12% in the final session.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSIAT outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest full fine-tuning baseline SLCA (86M params), SSIAT achieves comparable or better A_Last on ImageNetR (79.38 vs 79.35) and CIFAR-100 (91.35 vs 91.26) while using only 1.19M parameters, and clearly wins on ImageNetA (62.43 vs 61.05 A_Last, +1.39%) and CUB200 (88.75 vs 84.68 A_Last, +4.07%). Against the closest adapter-based competitor Adam-adapter (same 1.19M params), SSIAT gains +13.59% A_Last on ImageNetR (79.38 vs 65.79) and +13.62% on ImageNetA (62.43 vs 48.81), demonstrating that progressive adapter tuning without first-session freezing is critical. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODAPrompt) lag significantly on domain-shifted datasets: L2P scores only 44.04% A_Last on ImageNetA versus SSIAT's 62.43%, and CODAPrompt (3.84M params) reaches just 52.08% A_Last there. On the cross-domain VTAB benchmark, SSIAT (94.21% avg) surpasses SLCA (93.82%) and Adam-adapter (85.97%), with a 12% gap over Adam methods in the final session, confirming that incremental adapter tuning generalizes better across domains than first-session-only adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant prompt-based CIL paradigm (L2P, DualPrompt, CODAPrompt) by demonstrating that incrementally tuning a shared AdaptFormer-style adapter without any parameter-update constraints outperforms both prompt and SSF tuning in continual settings. It builds on three prior threads: the adapter architecture from AdaptFormer, the semantic drift compensation mechanism from SDC for estimating prototype shifts without past data, and the classifier alignment (CA) retraining strategy from Tang et al. and SLCA. The key conceptual departure is the rejection of knowledge-distillation or regularization losses on shared parameters, justified by a parameter-sensitivity argument showing that constraining updates to previously sensitive parameters hinders plasticity for new classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically attractive recipe for rehearsal-free CIL on pre-trained ViTs: no prompt pool to manage, no image storage, and only ~1.2M tunable parameters, while achieving SOTA on five benchmarks including cross-domain VTAB. The finding that unconstrained incremental adapter tuning beats constrained variants challenges the widespread assumption that shared-parameter updates must be regularized to prevent forgetting, potentially simplifying future CIL pipelines. Open questions include whether the semantic-shift estimation remains accurate over very long sequences (beyond 20 sessions), how the approach extends to domain-incremental or task-incremental settings, and whether the modest margins over SLCA (1–2%) persist under different backbone architectures or pre-training regimes.
