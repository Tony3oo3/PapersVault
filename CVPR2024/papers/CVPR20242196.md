## Problem

Existing active learning methods for object detection either rely on simple uncertainty/diversity heuristics that ignore the joint classification-localisation nature of detection, or they modify the detector architecture and training pipeline to compute model-specific uncertainty, limiting generalisability across different detector frameworks. The paper asks whether an effective AL strategy can be designed that requires no architectural or training-pipeline changes while properly accounting for both detection sub-tasks and the multi-instance structure of images.

## Input / Output

Given a trained object detector (any architecture) and an unlabelled image pool, PPAL operates in a batch AL setting: at each round it scores all unlabelled images and returns a query set of b images for oracle annotation. The detector's per-instance predictions (bounding boxes, class probabilities, detection scores, and extracted feature vectors) are consumed to compute image-level uncertainty and pairwise similarities; no additional model outputs or modified forward passes are required. The final output is a diverse, representative subset of images sent to human annotators.

## Method

PPAL is a two-stage plug-and-play algorithm. In Stage 1 (Difficulty Calibrated Uncertainty Sampling), a category-wise difficulty coefficient is maintained via exponential moving average of per-box training difficulties that combine classification probability and IoU; these coefficients re-weight per-instance classification entropy to produce an image-level uncertainty score, from which the top δ·b most uncertain images form a candidate pool. In Stage 2 (Category Conditioned Matching Similarity), pairwise image similarity is computed by matching each detected object to its most similar same-category counterpart in the other image (cosine similarity of features, weighted by detection score), then ensembling instance similarities symmetrically; a k-Center-Greedy pass followed by k-means++ (with centroids assigned to the image maximising summed intra-cluster similarity) selects the final diverse query set. The key design choice is that neither stage modifies the detector architecture or training loop, making the method directly applicable to any off-the-shelf detector.

## Results

On Pascal VOC with RetinaNet, PPAL (DCUS + CCMS) achieves 72.6±0.2 mAP at 20% labelled data, outperforming the entropy-only baseline (70.9±0.2) and random sampling (66.6±1.0). In early AL rounds (≤3), PPAL outperforms MIAL by 3.4 AP on COCO and 2.8 mAP on Pascal VOC, demonstrating its ability to mine highly informative samples when detectors are poorly trained. The method generalises across architectures: on COCO with Faster R-CNN, FCOS, ATSS, DDOD, and SSD, PPAL consistently surpasses both uncertainty-based and diversity-based baselines, and extends to semi-supervised settings with SoftTeacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPAL outperforms MIAL [50] by 3.4 AP on COCO and 2.8 mAP on Pascal VOC in early AL rounds (≤3), and maintains a lead in later rounds, while also offering superior generalisation since it requires no architecture or training-pipeline modifications. Against MDN [10], which modifies the detector to learn GMMs for uncertainty estimation, PPAL achieves higher mAP across all rounds on both COCO and Pascal VOC with RetinaNet. On the COCO Faster R-CNN setting, PPAL surpasses DivProto [43] (which replaces detection scores with uncertainties in NMS and uses diverse prototypes), following the same experimental protocol. Compared to Learn Loss [48], which predicts loss as an uncertainty proxy, PPAL achieves comparable or better detection performance while being architecture-agnostic. Against the Core-set [39] diversity baseline, PPAL's CCMS-based diversity sampling yields consistently higher mAP across FCOS, ATSS, and DDOD detectors on both Pascal VOC and COCO, confirming that instance-level matching similarity is more effective than global feature-map similarity for multi-instance images.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPAL departs from prior active learning for object detection methods (MDN, MIAL, DivProto) that require modifying detector architectures or training pipelines to compute uncertainty, instead proposing an architecture-agnostic two-stage strategy. The difficulty calibration in DCUS is directly inspired by the per-box training difficulty formulation in DDOD [9], repurposing it as a category-wise re-weighting mechanism for uncertainty sampling. The diversity stage builds on the Core-set [39] k-Center-Greedy framework but replaces the standard global feature-map similarity with the proposed Category Conditioned Matching Similarity, which explicitly handles multi-instance structure. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of uncertainty-based and diversity-based AL, arguing that neither alone suffices for detection and that the two should be sequenced rather than combined in a single objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design is the paper's most practically significant contribution: any existing detector (two-stage, single-stage, anchor-free, transformer-based) can adopt PPAL without architectural changes, lowering the barrier to AL adoption in production pipelines. The ablation finding that uncertainty-based sampling dominates in early rounds while diversity-based sampling becomes critical in later rounds offers a useful heuristic for practitioners tuning AL schedules. Limitations include evaluation only on COCO and Pascal VOC, reliance on a pre-trained detector for uncertainty estimation, and the heuristic nature of the modified k-means++ centroid update. Open questions include whether the difficulty coefficients transfer across domains or scale to very large category spaces, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D detection.
