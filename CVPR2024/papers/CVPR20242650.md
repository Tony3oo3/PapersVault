## Problem

Existing vision foundation models like CLIP and OpenCLIP, trained on general-domain image-text pairs, produce representations too coarse for fine-grained biology tasks (e.g., distinguishing closely related species within the same genus). No large-scale, diverse ML-ready biology image dataset existed that covered the breadth of the tree of life, and no pre-training strategy leveraged the rich hierarchical taxonomic structure to enable generalization to unseen taxa.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242650/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes organism images in diverse formats (field photographs, microscope images, drawings, museum specimens) paired with text labels that can be taxonomic names (flattened kingdom-to-species strings), scientific names, common names, or combinations thereof. At inference, it produces image and text embeddings in a shared 512-dimensional feature space; classification is performed via nearest-centroid matching (few-shot) or direct cosine similarity to text embeddings (zero-shot), with the text formatted in CLIP's standard template (e.g., 'a photo of Pica hudsonia').

## Method

BIOCLIP initializes from OpenAI's CLIP checkpoint (ViT-B/16 vision encoder + 77-token causal autoregressive text encoder) and is continually pre-trained on TREEOFLIFE-10M (10.4M images, 454K taxa) using CLIP's multimodal contrastive learning objective. The key insight is that 'flattening' the taxonomic hierarchy into a single string (e.g., 'Animalia Chordata Aves Passeriformes Corvidae Pica hudsonia') and feeding it through the autoregressive text encoder naturally embeds the hierarchical structure, since later rank tokens condition on all higher-rank tokens. A mixed text type training strategy randomly samples from taxonomic, scientific, common, and combined name types at each training step, retaining the generalization benefits of taxonomic names while providing flexibility at inference. The paper demonstrates that this CLIP-style contrastive objective massively outperforms both plain cross-entropy and hierarchical multi-task classification objectives for few-shot transfer, and that the learned representations visually conform to the tree-of-life hierarchy via t-SNE analysis.

## Results

BIOCLIP achieves a mean zero-shot top-1 accuracy of 39.4% across 10 fine-grained biology classification datasets, outperforming CLIP (21.9%) by +17.5% absolute and OpenCLIP (20.4%) by +19.0%. In the one-shot setting, BIOCLIP reaches 50.3% mean accuracy (+16.7% over CLIP), and in the five-shot setting it reaches 68.8% (+17.3% over CLIP). On the unseen RARE SPECIES task, BIOCLIP achieves 38.0% zero-shot accuracy versus 31.8% for CLIP and 29.8% for OpenCLIP, demonstrating strong generalization to taxa excluded from training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIOCLIP substantially outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against CLIP [69], BIOCLIP gains +17.5% (zero-shot), +16.7% (one-shot), and +17.3% (five-shot) in mean top-1 accuracy, with the largest per-dataset margins on Insects (+25.7% zero-shot) and PlantNet (+32.9% zero-shot). OpenCLIP [42], trained on LAION-400M, is slightly weaker than CLIP on mean zero-shot accuracy (20.4% vs 21.9%) and trails BIOCLIP by +19.0% (zero-shot), +13.6% (one-shot), and +12.9% (five-shot). Among vision-only baselines, Supervised-IN21K [78] and DINO [15] achieve 35.2% and 36.2% one-shot mean accuracy respectively, both well below BIOCLIP's 50.3%; in five-shot, BIOCLIP leads by +14.9% and +13.4% over these two. Notably, BIOCLIP is the only model that can perform zero-shot classification, and it dominates the unseen RARE SPECIES task (38.0% zero-shot) where CLIP and OpenCLIP score 31.8% and 29.8% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIOCLIP directly extends CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) by initializing from its ViT-B/16 checkpoint and repurposing its multimodal contrastive learning objective to match images with flattened taxonomic name strings rather than free-form captions. This departs from the supervised classification paradigm used by ResNet and Swin Transformer, where each class label is an isolated symbol, and from prior hierarchical classification objectives that predict each taxonomic rank separately. The work also sits within the domain-specific CLIP line (QUILT-1M for pathology, fashion CLIP) but scales to 10× more images with far greater class diversity, and introduces a mixed text type training strategy that has no direct precedent in prior CLIP variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.4M-image, 454K-taxon TREEOFLIFE-10M dataset and the RARE SPECIES benchmark fill critical gaps in ML-ready biology data, and BIOCLIP's 17% absolute zero-shot improvement over CLIP makes fine-grained organism classification substantially more accessible to biologists without ML expertise. The finding that the CLIP contrastive objective massively outperforms a natural hierarchical cross-entropy alternative (44.7 vs. 19.3 one-shot) is a useful design lesson for other structured-label domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the 100M+ iNaturalist images, whether richer textual descriptions can yield trait-level representations beyond classification, and how well the model handles the millions of undescribed species that dominate real-world biodiversity.
