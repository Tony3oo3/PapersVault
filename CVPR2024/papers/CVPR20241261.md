## Problem

The original DETR training procedure relies solely on one-to-one supervision, which promotes a single candidate per ground-truth object but provides no explicit supervision for the generation of multiple detection candidates by the decoder cross-attention. Existing one-to-many supervision methods (Group DETR, Hybrid DETR, DN-DETR) apply this supervision to additional parallel decoder branches, leaving the primary decoder's object queries—those actually used at inference—without direct one-to-many signal. This paper asks whether one-to-many supervision can be imposed directly on the primary decoder's queries without adding extra branches or queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241261/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (COCO 2017, 1333×800 resolution) processed through a ResNet-50 backbone and transformer encoder, and produces a fixed set of N predictions (300 or 900 object queries) each with a class score vector and a 4-element bounding box. The one-to-many supervision module adds a parallel box predictor (3-layer MLP) and class predictor (single linear layer) that operate on the same object queries during training but are discarded at inference, so the output format and inference pipeline remain identical to the baseline DETR.

## Method

MS-DETR adds a lightweight one-to-many prediction head (box + class predictors) directly on the object queries of the primary decoder, alongside the existing one-to-one head, and trains both simultaneously. One-to-many matching selects the top-K=6 queries per ground-truth object by a combined IoU and classification score (α=0.4), filtered by a threshold τ=0.4, and the one-to-one matched query is also included. The best implementation swaps the decoder layer order from self-attention→cross-attention→FFN to cross-attention→self-attention→FFN and places the one-to-many supervision on the internal queries immediately after cross-attention (before self-attention), aligning the supervision with the candidate-generation role of cross-attention. No additional decoder branches, extra queries, or NMS post-processing are introduced; the extra module affects only the training loss, and the inference procedure is unchanged. Box and class predictor weights are shared between the one-to-one and one-to-many heads for best performance.

## Results

On COCO val2017, MS-DETR achieves 47.9 mAP on DAB-Deformable-DETR (12 epochs, 300 queries), a +3.7 mAP gain over the 44.2 mAP baseline. On Deformable DETR++ with 900 queries, MS-DETR reaches 50.0 mAP at 12 epochs and 50.9 mAP at 24 epochs, outperforming DINO (49.0 and 50.4 mAP respectively). The method also improves instance segmentation, boosting Mask-Deformable-DETR by +3.2 Mask mAP (28.3→31.5) under 12 epochs. Training overhead is minimal: only +2 minutes per epoch and +127M GPU memory (~2%) compared to the baseline, versus +28–36 minutes and 40–60% memory increases for Hybrid DETR and Group DETR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS-DETR consistently outperforms all compared one-to-many supervision methods. On DAB-Deformable-DETR (12 epochs), MS-DETR (47.9 mAP) beats Group-DETR (45.7) by +2.2 and DN-DETR (46.0) by +1.9. On Deformable DETR, MS-DETR (47.6) surpasses Hybrid DETR (45.9) by +1.7 mAP. On the stronger Deformable DETR++ baseline, MS-DETR (48.8) edges out Hybrid DETR (48.7) by +0.1 mAP. Against DINO on the 900-query Deformable DETR++ setup, MS-DETR gains +1.0 mAP at 12 epochs (50.0 vs 49.0) and +0.5 mAP at 24 epochs (50.9 vs 50.4). MS-DETR is also complementary to these methods: combining it with DN-DETR, Group-DETR, Hybrid DETR, and DINO yields further gains of +2.0, +0.6, +0.8, and +1.3 mAP respectively, confirming that direct one-to-many supervision on the primary decoder is orthogonal to the extra-decoder approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

MS-DETR builds on the DETR family of end-to-end detectors and the broader line of work accelerating DETR training (Deformable DETR, Conditional DETR, DAB-DETR). It departs from prior one-to-many supervision methods—DN-DETR, Group DETR, Hybrid DETR, DINO—all of which impose one-to-many supervision on auxiliary decoder branches or extra queries, leaving the primary decoder's queries supervised only one-to-one. The key conceptual move is drawing an analogy between DETR's self-attention + one-to-one supervision (functioning as an implicit NMS) and traditional detectors' explicit NMS, then adding one-to-many supervision directly to the primary decoder's cross-attention outputs to explicitly supervise candidate generation. This makes MS-DETR complementary rather than competing with both cross-attention modifications and auxiliary-branch one-to-many methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the extreme simplicity and efficiency: a single additional prediction head (no extra decoders, no extra queries) yields gains of 1.8–3.7 mAP while adding only ~2 minutes and ~2% memory versus +36 minutes and +60% memory for Group DETR. The consistent complementarity with DN-DETR, Group DETR, Hybrid DETR, DINO, and IoU-aware losses suggests it can be stacked into existing pipelines without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the specific placement after cross-attention (rather than after self-attention) generalizes to other decoder designs, whether gains persist at very long training schedules (the 50-epoch improvement shrinks to +2.1 mAP), and whether the approach transfers to other transformer-based tasks beyond detection and segmentation.
