## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptation for object detection has been extensively studied for RGB-to-RGB transitions, but the substantially larger domain gap between RGB and thermal images renders conventional single-teacher UDA methods ineffective. Additionally, annotated thermal datasets are scarce, making it difficult to train high-performance thermal detectors directly. This paper tackles the underexplored problem of adapting a detector from a labeled RGB source to an unlabeled thermal target despite this large cross-spectral gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240998/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes labeled RGB images (source) and unlabeled thermal images (target) from paired datasets (FLIR, KAIST), with images resized so the shortest edge is at most 800 pixels. At inference, a single thermal image is fed to the adapted FCOS detector (VGG-16 backbone for FLIR, ResNet-50 for KAIST), which outputs bounding boxes with class labels (person, car, bicycle). The evaluation protocol deliberately uses disjoint RGB and thermal subsets to prevent the model from exploiting aligned image pairs.

## Method

D3T replaces the single Mean Teacher with two domain-specific teachers (RGB and thermal), each updated via EMA only when the student is trained on the corresponding domain, preventing cross-domain interference. A zigzag learning schedule alternates training between the two domains with a progressively shifting ratio—starting RGB-heavy (e.g., 150 RGB vs. 50 thermal iterations) and incrementally increasing thermal iterations by β every 10k steps—so the student gradually transitions from RGB to thermal knowledge. At every step, both teachers generate pseudo-labels for the student's input regardless of which domain is being trained, and a dynamic λ (ramping from 0 to 1) controls how much pseudo-label supervision is mixed into the RGB-domain loss, allowing the student to trust teacher predictions more as training progresses. The base detector is FCOS, and the overall loss switches between a thermal unsupervised loss (combining both teachers' pseudo-labels) and an RGB loss (ground-truth supervised plus weighted pseudo-label terms) depending on the current training domain.

## Results

On the FLIR dataset (RGB→Thermal, VGG-16 backbone), D3T achieves 69.30% mAP, surpassing the strongest baseline HT (65.81%) by 3.49% and the non-teacher-based EPM (44.60%) by 24.70%. On the KAIST dataset (RGB→Thermal, ResNet-50 backbone), D3T reaches 48.96% mAP for person detection, outperforming HT (43.45%) by 5.51% and EPM (39.55%) by 9.41%. The source-only model without adaptation scores only 34.68% mAP on FLIR and 9.09% on KAIST, highlighting the severity of the RGB-thermal domain gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D3T consistently outperforms all four baselines on both FLIR and KAIST. Against HT (Harmonious Teacher, CVPR 2023), the closest competitor using a single teacher-student framework, D3T gains 3.49% mAP on FLIR (69.30 vs. 65.81) and 5.51% on KAIST (48.96 vs. 43.45), with the largest per-class improvement on Bicycles (+9.33% on FLIR). Against EPM (ECCV 2020), a center-aware feature alignment method without a teacher, D3T leads by 24.70% mAP on FLIR and 9.41% on KAIST. The adversarial-based DANN (JMLR 2016) and distribution-alignment SWDA (CVPR 2019) both struggle with the RGB-thermal gap, achieving only 37.14% and 38.29% mAP on FLIR respectively—barely above the source-only baseline of 34.68%—confirming that conventional domain alignment techniques are insufficient for cross-spectral adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean Teacher (MT) framework [37] from its standard single-teacher formulation (as used in UMT [9] and HT [10]) to a dual-teacher architecture, where separate RGB and thermal teachers are maintained and updated independently via EMA. The zigzag learning strategy is conceptually inspired by the switching-teacher idea in [30] (semi-supervised segmentation), repurposed here to manage the much larger cross-modal gap between RGB and thermal domains. Unlike prior UDA-for-detection methods (DANN, SWDA, EPM, HT) that assume same-modality adaptation (e.g., Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes), D3T explicitly targets the RGB-to-thermal transition, a setting the authors note has received 'relatively modest research investment' with only a few prior attempts (Meta-UDA [40], CutMix-based fusion [31]).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical but underexplored gap: enabling reliable object detection in thermal imagery (nighttime driving, search-and-rescue, surveillance) without expensive thermal annotations. The disjointed evaluation protocol (no matched RGB-thermal pairs in training) is a methodological contribution that makes cross-modal UDA benchmarks more rigorous. However, scope is limited: only FCOS is tested, KAIST evaluation reports a single class (Person), and the two-dataset validation leaves open whether the dual-teacher + zigzag strategy generalizes to other cross-modal pairs or two-stage detectors. A key open question is whether the progressive teacher-weighting schedule can be automated or learned rather than hand-tuned via β.
