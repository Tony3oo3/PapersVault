## Problem

Supervised self-driving perception and forecasting methods are bounded by the scale and expressiveness of expensive human annotations restricted to predefined object classes, while prior unsupervised occupancy methods like 4D-Occ rely on quantized voxel grids and regression-based depth rendering that struggle to capture scene dynamics. This paper asks whether a continuous 4D occupancy world model can be learned entirely from unlabeled LiDAR and then effectively transferred to downstream perception and forecasting tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes H past LiDAR sweeps (5 frames at 0.6 s for KITTI/Argoverse 2, 6 frames at 0.5 s for nuScenes) voxelized in BEV as input; sensor intrinsics and extrinsics are used only to construct training pseudo-labels, not as model inputs, so the model must learn object extent independent of viewpoint. The output is a continuous 4D occupancy field queryable at any (x, y, z, t) point via an implicit decoder, which can be rendered into future point cloud depths through a lightweight ray renderer or fine-tuned into per-class BEV semantic occupancy by replacing the z-coordinate with a learnable scalar.

## Method

UNO encodes past LiDAR into a 2D BEV feature map via a ResNet backbone, then uses a lightweight implicit occupancy decoder with deformable attention to predict occupancy logits at arbitrary 4D query points (x, y, z, t) with positional embeddings. Training is fully self-supervised: future LiDAR rays generate pseudo-labels where the segment between sensor and return is negative (unoccupied) and a short buffer (δ = 0.1 m) behind the return is positive (occupied), with equal numbers of positive and negative samples (900,000 each) balanced via binary cross-entropy. The key design choices—continuous implicit representation instead of a quantized voxel grid, classification instead of regression, and decoupling the world model from the point-cloud renderer—avoid quantization errors and let the model learn object dynamics and extent. Transfer to point cloud forecasting freezes UNO and trains a small renderer g_γ that maps a 2000-element occupancy vector along a ray to depth; transfer to BEV semantic occupancy replaces z with a learnable parameter ψ and fine-tunes jointly on labeled data.

## Results

On point cloud forecasting, UNO achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three datasets: on Argoverse 2 it reaches NFCD 0.71 m², CD 7.02 m², AbsRel 8.88%, and L1 1.86 m; on nuScenes it reaches NFCD 0.89 m², CD 1.80 m², AbsRel 8.78%, and L1 1.15 m; and on KITTI it reaches NFCD 0.72 m², CD 0.90 m², AbsRel 9.13%, and L1 1.09 m. For BEV semantic occupancy forecasting on Argoverse 2 Sensor, fine-tuned UNO outperforms fully supervised MP3 and ImplicitO at all supervision levels, achieving mAP 52.3 and Soft-IoU 22.3 with full labels, and surpassing both baselines trained on all data with roughly an order of magnitude fewer labeled frames. In geometric 4D occupancy recall at 70% precision, UNO achieves substantially higher recall than 4D-Occ for nearly all object classes, with particularly large gains on vulnerable road users such as Stroller, Bicyclist, and Motorcycle/Motorcyclist.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNO decisively outperforms 4D-Occ [21] on all metrics across all three datasets; the largest gap is on Argoverse 2 where UNO's CD of 7.02 m² is roughly 10× lower than 4D-Occ's 69.81 m², and NFCD improves from 2.20 to 0.71 m². Against RayTracing [21], UNO improves NFCD from 2.50 to 0.71 m² and L1 from 3.72 to 1.86 m on Argoverse 2, and from 1.66 to 0.89 m² and 2.44 to 1.15 m on nuScenes. On nuScenes, UNO also surpasses SPFNet [33] (NFCD 2.50→0.89, L1 5.11→1.15) and S2Net [31] (NFCD 2.06→0.89, L1 4.78→1.15). On KITTI, UNO beats ST3DCNN [24] by a wide margin (NFCD 4.19→0.72, L1 3.25→1.09) and 4D-Occ (NFCD 0.96→0.72, CD 1.50→0.90). For BEV semantic occupancy, fine-tuned UNO outperforms both MP3 [6] and ImplicitO [1] at every supervision level from 1 frame to the full training set, with the advantage being most pronounced in the few-shot regime (1–10 frames) where UNO with a single labeled frame already separates vehicles from background. In geometric occupancy recall at 70% precision, UNO achieves significantly higher recall than 4D-Occ for almost all classes, with the most notable improvements on rare and small objects (Stroller, Bicyclist, Motorcycle/Motorcyclist, traffic cones).

## Relation to Prior Work

UNO builds directly on 4D-Occ's reformulation of point cloud forecasting as 4D occupancy prediction, but departs in two key ways: it replaces the quantized voxel grid with a continuous implicit field (inheriting the deformable-attention decoder design from ImplicitO) and switches from a NeRF-style depth regression objective to a simple binary classification on ray occupancy. It also decouples the world model from the sensor renderer, in contrast to 4D-Occ which entangles them, and contrasts with direct point cloud forecasting methods that must implicitly learn sensor characteristics. The self-supervised signal—balanced positive/negative occupancy from LiDAR ray geometry—is simpler than both the depth-rendering and free-space-rendering objectives used by prior occupancy forecasting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the few-shot transferability: fine-tuning UNO on as few as 1–10 labeled frames outperforms fully supervised baselines trained on the entire dataset, which is highly relevant given the cost of semantic annotations in self-driving. Open questions include whether the implicit continuous formulation is truly necessary versus a sufficiently fine voxel grid, whether the learned occupancy captures causal dynamics or merely statistical correlations, and whether the approach extends to camera-only or multi-sensor settings without LiDAR. The separate renderer also raises the question of whether end-to-end joint training could further improve point cloud forecasting.
