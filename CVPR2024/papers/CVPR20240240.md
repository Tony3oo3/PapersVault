## Problem

Existing vision-based anomaly detection methods measure image embeddings using distance-based losses in Euclidean space, which cannot adequately exploit the intrinsic geometry of data that lies in non-Euclidean spaces. Industrial images exhibit hierarchical relationships (whole image → local regions → finer details) that are naturally captured by hyperbolic geometry, yet no prior work has explored anomaly detection in this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single industrial RGB image resized to 256×256 and produces two augmented views (via ColorJitter on brightness, contrast, or saturation) during training. At inference, it outputs a pixel-level anomaly map computed as the hyperbolic distance between feature embeddings from two branches, from which an image-level anomaly score is derived by taking the maximum pixel value, and a binary anomaly mask is obtained by adaptive thresholding. The backbone produces 384-dimensional feature vectors that are projected into a Poincaré ball with curvature c=0.01, with feature clipping at radius 2.3 for numerical stability.

## Method

HypAD extends self-supervised contrastive anomaly detection from Euclidean to hyperbolic space by mapping encoder features into a Poincaré ball via exponential mapping and optimizing with the hyperbolic distance metric instead of squared Euclidean distance. The architecture uses a PDN-S backbone (distilled from WideResNet-101) followed by a pooling projection layer, then an exponential mapping module that projects 384-dim Euclidean features into the Poincaré space using gyrovector addition. Training follows a one-class contrastive scheme: two augmented views of the same normal image are encoded, mapped to hyperbolic space, and the model minimizes their hyperbolic distance, leveraging the fact that hyperbolic volume grows exponentially with radius to better encode the hierarchical structure of industrial images. The key theoretical property is that the hyperbolic distance degenerates to Euclidean distance as curvature c→0, making EfficientAD a strict special case of HypAD, while the optimal c=0.01 yields a 3.6% AUPRO gain over the best prior method on MVTec AD.

## Results

On MVTec AD, HypAD achieves a mean pixel-level AUPRO of 97.6%, surpassing the previous best (FAIR at 94.0%) by 3.6 percentage points. On VisA, HypAD reaches 95.4% AUPRO, outperforming EfficientAD (93.1%) by 2.2 points. At the image level, HypAD attains 99.2% AUROC and 99.5% AUPR on MVTec AD, and 98.3% AUROC and 98.5% AUPR on VisA. On MNIST-C (ADBench), HypAD achieves 92.51% AUROC, exceeding EfficientAD (90.96%) and the prior best DevNet (88.04%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HypAD outperforms all 14 compared baselines on every reported metric. The largest gains are over PatchCore (97.6 vs. 92.7 AUPRO on MVTec AD; 95.4 vs. 79.7 on VisA) and SimpleNet (97.6 vs. 89.6; 95.4 vs. 68.9), indicating that the hyperbolic mapping is particularly beneficial for methods with weaker feature representations. Against the strongest recent baselines, HypAD leads FAIR by 3.6 points on MVTec AD and 4.0 points on VisA, and beats EfficientAD (which is a special case of HypAD at c=0) by 4.5 and 2.3 points respectively. On image-level detection, HypAD edges out EfficientAD (99.2 vs. 99.0 AUROC on MVTec AD) and DiffusionAD (98.3 vs. 97.8 AUROC on VisA), though the paper notes image-level metrics are near saturation. On pixel-level AUPR, HypAD achieves 62.5% on MVTec AD (vs. 60.8% for EfficientAD) and is competitive with EfficientAD on VisA (37.6% vs. 38.2%). On MNIST-C, HypAD surpasses both EfficientAD and the ADBench best (DevNet) by 1.55 and 4.47 AUROC points, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised contrastive anomaly detection pipeline (specifically EfficientAD's PDN backbone and teacher-student distillation setup) by replacing the Euclidean distance metric with a hyperbolic one computed in the Poincaré ball model. The geometric machinery—exponential mapping, gyrovector addition, and hyperbolic distance—draws directly from the hyperbolic image embedding literature (Khrulkov et al., 2020; Ermolov et al., 2022), which had previously been applied to retrieval and classification but not to anomaly detection. The authors explicitly note that setting curvature c=0 recovers EfficientAD, positioning HypAD as a strict generalization of that baseline into non-Euclidean geometry, motivated by the observation that industrial images exhibit tree-like hierarchical structure (whole → region → detail) that hyperbolic space can represent more naturally than flat Euclidean space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to frame industrial anomaly detection in hyperbolic space, opening a geometric perspective that could inform future methods beyond the single Poincaré-ball instantiation explored here. However, the evidence for the hierarchical-structure hypothesis is illustrative rather than rigorous: the curvature ablation (Table 4) shows only a 2-point gain at c=0.01 over c=0, and all non-zero curvatures (0.01–0.5) perform nearly identically, suggesting the benefit may come from the non-Euclidean geometry in general rather than from exploiting a specific hierarchical structure. Open questions include whether other hyperbolic models (Lorentz, hyperboloid) or learned curvature would improve results, whether the gain transfers to non-industrial domains, and whether the simple distance-metric swap can be combined with more sophisticated hyperbolic architectures (e.g., hyperbolic transformers) for further gains.
