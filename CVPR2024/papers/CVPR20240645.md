## Problem

ERM-trained classifiers exploit spurious correlations between non-causal image components (e.g., backgrounds) and labels, causing severe performance drops under correlation shift. Prior group-balancing methods either require explicit group annotations or assume ERM always attends to spurious parts—an assumption the paper shows is dataset-dependent. The gap is a method that identifies causal vs. non-causal image components without group labels and intervenes on them to rebalance training data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ResNet-50 on Waterbirds, CelebA, MetaShift, Dominoes) and outputs a class label. Unusually, the training pipeline first runs an ERM-trained ResNet-50 to compute xGradCAM attribution maps per image, then uses those maps to decompose each image into a retained (causal) region and a masked (non-causal) region before composing cross-image patches. The final classifier is the same ResNet-50 backbone with only its linear head retrained, so inference is a standard forward pass producing a single class score vector.

## Method

DaC operates in two stages. First, an adaptive masking procedure (Algorithm 1) computes xGradCAM scores for each pixel, then progressively masks the lowest-scoring fraction p and tracks the cross-entropy loss; the elbow of this loss-vs-p curve (found via the Kneedle algorithm) yields a per-image binary mask separating the most predictive region from the rest. Second, for each batch, the q-portion of lowest-loss samples are selected, and for every sample a partner with a different label is drawn; the causal region of one image is spliced with the non-causal region of the other (gaps filled with the batch mean), producing a counterfactual sample whose non-causal component no longer carries the spurious value for its label. Only the final linear layer is retrained (following DFR) on a weighted sum of the original batch loss and the combined-sample loss, with a causal-flag hyperparameter that inverts the mask when ERM is found to attend to non-causal parts instead. This requires no group labels, no generative model, and no knowledge of which spurious attribute is present.

## Results

On Waterbirds, DaC achieves 92.3% worst-group accuracy and 95.3% average group accuracy, matching DFR (92.3%) while exceeding Group DRO (91.4%) and LISA (89.2%) without requiring group labels. On Metashift, DaC reaches 78.3% worst-group accuracy, outperforming DFR (72.8%), Group DRO (66.0%), and LISA (59.8%). On Dominoes, DaC achieves 89.2% worst-group accuracy, closely matching DFR (90.0%) and substantially surpassing MaskTune (65.8%) and base ERM (72.8%). On CelebA, DaC (81.9% worst) underperforms DFR (88.3%) and Group DRO (88.9%), which the authors attribute to CelebA involving spurious attributes rather than spurious parts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DaC outperforms all group-label-free baselines (MaskTune, CnC, JTT, base ERM) by a large margin on Waterbirds, Metashift, and Dominoes in worst-group accuracy. On Waterbirds, DaC (92.3%) matches DFR (92.3%) and exceeds Group DRO (91.4%) and LISA (89.2%) without needing group labels during training. On Metashift, DaC (78.3%) leads DFR (72.8%) by 5.5 points and Group DRO (66.0%) by 12.3 points. On Dominoes, DaC (89.2%) is within 0.8 points of DFR (90.0%) while MaskTune lags by over 23 points. The main exception is CelebA, where DaC (81.9% worst) trails DFR (88.3%) and Group DRO (88.9%), which the authors explain is because CelebA involves spurious attributes of the causal object (hair volume) rather than spurious parts, a setting outside DaC's design scope.

## Relation to Prior Work

DaC builds directly on DFR (Kirichenko et al., 2022), which demonstrated that retraining only the last layer of an ERM model on group-balanced data suffices for robustness to spurious correlation. The key departure is that DaC eliminates the need for group labels by using xGradCAM attribution maps to decompose images into causal and non-causal parts, then composing counterfactual samples from majority-group data to simulate minority groups. It also explicitly challenges MaskTune's (Asgari et al., 2022) assumption that ERM models always attend to spurious regions, showing instead that attention depends on the relative predictiveness of causal versus non-causal components across the dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DaC achieves worst-group accuracy comparable to methods requiring explicit group annotations (DFR, Group DRO) while only needing standard class labels, making it applicable in settings where group structure is unknown. However, the method is explicitly limited to 'spurious parts' (e.g., background vs. foreground) and does not address 'spurious attributes' (e.g., digit color in CMNIST), as evidenced by weaker CelebA results. Open questions include whether the causal-flag heuristic generalizes to more complex compositional scenes, whether the approach extends to attribute-level spurious correlations, and whether the simple pixel-level composition can be replaced by more principled interventions in feature space.
