## Problem

Visual foundation models such as CLIP, DINOv2, and SAM each excel in distinct domains—zero-shot vision-language, dense spatial features, and open-vocabulary segmentation—but none covers all capabilities. The paper asks whether a single student model can be trained via multi-teacher distillation to absorb the complementary strengths of all three teachers and simultaneously outperform each on its respective benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240927/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios (enabled by Cropped Position Embedding), with the full RADIO ViT-H/16 operating at 432px for CLIP/DINOv2 teachers and 1024px for SAM. Outputs are dense spatial feature maps and a CLS-token summary vector that, through per-teacher adaptor heads (2-layer MLPs), can be projected into any teacher's feature space, enabling drop-in replacement for CLIP zero-shot classification, DINOv2 linear-probe segmentation, or SAM mask decoding without retraining downstream heads.

## Method

AM-RADIO trains a student from scratch by matching both summary and spatial features of three heterogeneous teachers (DFN CLIP-H/14, DINOv2-g/14, SAM-H/16) using per-teacher 2-layer MLP adaptor heads with cosine loss for summaries and a cosine + smooth-L1 combination for spatial features. The student architecture is flexible: the main model is a ViT-H/16 with CPE for multi-resolution support and ViTDet-style windowed attention reformulated as a training augmentation for high-resolution SAM distillation, while E-RADIO is a novel hybrid CNN-Transformer (strided-conv stem, YOLOv8 C2f blocks, interleaved local/global windowed attention, and a 2× deconv upsampling path) that achieves 6×+ speedup over teachers at matched resolution. Training proceeds in two stages on DataComp-1B (300k steps at 256px with CLIP+DINOv2, then 300k steps at 432px+1024px adding SAM), using naive equal-weight loss balancing across teachers and spectral reparametrization to stabilize high-resolution attention.

## Results

RADIO-ViT/H/16 achieves 86.06% k-NN accuracy on ImageNet-1K, 51.34 mIOU on ADE20K, and 84.71 mIOU on Pascal VOC under linear probing, outperforming all individual teacher models (DINOv2-g: 83.41/48.68/82.78; DFN CLIP-H: 85.27/39.00/70.29). In the LLaVA-1.5 VQA setting, RADIO attains 63.01 GQA, 86.20 POPE, 56.32 TextVQA, and 79.28 VQAv2, leading all compared models. The efficient E-RADIO-L variant (391M params, 468 throughput) achieves 83.89 k-NN and 48.22 ADE20K mIOU at 6×+ the throughput of teacher models, while RADIO as a drop-in SAM replacement scores 76.23 mIOU on COCO instance segmentation (vs. SAM-Huge's 77.18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADIO-ViT/H/16 outperforms DINOv2-g/14-reg on k-NN (86.06 vs. 83.41), ADE20K (51.34 vs. 48.68), VOC (84.71 vs. 82.78), GQA (63.01 vs. 61.88), POPE (86.20 vs. 85.62), and VQAv2 (79.28 vs. 76.23), while also adding zero-shot capability that DINOv2 lacks. Against DFN CLIP-H/14, RADIO wins on k-NN (86.06 vs. 85.27), ADE20K (51.34 vs. 39.00), VOC (84.71 vs. 70.29), and VQAv2 (79.28 vs. 78.78), but trails slightly on zero-shot (82.93 vs. 83.90) and TextVQA (56.32 vs. 56.78). On COCO instance segmentation, SAM-H/16 retains a small edge (77.18 vs. 76.23), though RADIO achieves this at 13× higher throughput (158 vs. 12). E-RADIO-L matches or exceeds MetaCLIP-H/14 on k-NN (83.89 vs. 82.12) and ADE20K (48.22 vs. 35.39) at 468 vs. 486 throughput, while also providing zero-shot and COCO capabilities absent in MetaCLIP. Among efficient backbones in Table 7, E-RADIO-L (265M params) achieves 83.73 k-NN and 45.5 ADE20K at 3472 throughput, dominating the speed/quality Pareto front over ConvNext-B (81.73/38.95 at 1805) and MViTV2-B (81.39/41.39 at 975). The paper notes that CNN-like architectures (EfficientNetV2, ResNetv2, RegNetY) are significantly faster but struggle to distill ViT teacher features, particularly on dense tasks where their 32× spatial reduction limits fine-grain representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-teacher knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., Zuchniak) from the homogeneous-teacher setting to a heterogeneous foundation-model setting, where teachers differ in architecture (ViT-H/14, ViT-g/14, ViTDet-H), input resolution (224–1024px), and training objective (contrastive, self-supervised, segmentation). It builds on per-teacher projection heads from Zuchniak and feature-matching distillation from Wei et al., but departs by eliminating ground-truth supervision entirely and allowing the student to adopt an architecture unrelated to any teacher. The E-RADIO hybrid CNN-Transformer backbone draws on YOLOv8 C2f blocks, Swin-style windowed attention, and EdgeViT's local-global-local pattern, repurposing them for VFM distillation rather than supervised ImageNet training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Practitioners currently maintain separate CLIP, DINOv2, and SAM models for different downstream tasks; RADIO demonstrates that a single distilled model can serve as a drop-in replacement for all three while being faster, reducing infrastructure and memory overhead. The E-RADIO architecture's 6× throughput advantage at matched quality makes high-quality dense features accessible in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include the observed 'mode switching' between low- and high-resolution feature spaces (Figure 4), whether the approach generalizes to additional teacher families (e.g., video or 3D), and whether the student-teacher performance gap widens or narrows as teacher quality improves.
