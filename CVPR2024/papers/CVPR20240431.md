## Problem

Existing MLLM benchmarks predominantly evaluate spatial understanding on static images, leaving temporal comprehension in dynamic videos largely unassessed. The few video-specific benchmarks that exist are either limited to basic tasks (action recognition), restricted to particular domains or scenes, or rely on expensive manual annotation. This paper addresses the gap by introducing MVBench, a comprehensive benchmark of 20 temporal tasks that cannot be solved from a single frame, alongside a strong video MLLM baseline (VideoChat2) to establish a meaningful performance reference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240431/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At evaluation, each model receives a 16-frame video clip (sourced from 11 public datasets, filtered to 5–35 s duration for temporal sensitivity) paired with a multiple-choice question containing 3–5 shuffled options. A system prompt instructs the model to attend to cause, event sequence, object movement, and person pose, while an answer prompt ('Best Option: (') forces the model to emit a single parenthesized letter, guaranteeing 100% option-extraction accuracy without resorting to LLM-based parsing. The benchmark produces 200 QA pairs per task (4,000 total), scored purely by exact-match accuracy against ground-truth annotations from the source datasets.

## Method

MVBench is built via a static-to-dynamic paradigm: nine spatial task categories from prior image benchmarks are systematically augmented with temporal context to yield 20 video tasks spanning perception to cognition, and an LLM-driven pipeline automatically converts annotations from 11 public video datasets into multiple-choice QAs, eliminating manual labeling. VideoChat2 bridges a UMT-L video foundation model with a Vicuna-7B LLM through a QFormer cross-attention module, trained in three progressive stages: (1) aligning visual and text tokens via contrastive and matching losses on 25M image/video captions, (2) connecting the visual encoder to the LLM for caption generation while unfreezing the encoder, and (3) instruction tuning on 2M diverse samples (conversation, captioning, VQA, reasoning, classification) using LoRA on the frozen LLM. A key architectural choice is integrating task instructions (but not questions) into the QFormer to extract instruction-relevant visual tokens, and the evaluation protocol uses a simple answer prompt that achieves 100% option extraction across all tested models.

## Results

On MVBench (20 temporal video understanding tasks, 200 QA pairs each), VideoChat2 achieves 51.1% average accuracy, surpassing the best prior video MLLM VideoChat (35.5%) by over 15% and the best image MLLM LLaVA (36.0%) by 15.1%. The text-only ablation VideoChat2_text scores 34.7%, confirming that the visual encoder contributes meaningfully. On the video conversation benchmark, VideoChat2 achieves 2.98/5 average (vs. 2.38 for VideoChatGPT), and on zero-shot video QA it reaches 70.0% on MSVD-QA, 54.1% on MSRVTT-QA, and 49.1% on ActivityNet-QA, all state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoChat2 (51.1%) decisively outperforms all 10 baselines on MVBench. Among video MLLMs, it beats VideoChat (35.5%) by 15.6%, VideoLLaMA (34.1%) by 17.0%, and VideoChatGPT (32.7%) by 18.4%. Among image MLLMs, it surpasses LLaVA (36.0%) by 15.1%, InstructBLIP (32.5%) by 18.6%, Otter-I (33.5%) by 17.6%, BLIP-2 (31.4%) by 19.7%, LLaMA-Adapter (31.7%) by 19.4%, mPLUG-Owl-I (29.4%) by 21.7%, and MiniGPT-4 (18.8%) by 32.3%. The gap is particularly large on action-related tasks (e.g., Action Antonym: 83.5% vs. 63.0% for LLaVA) and scene transition (88.5% vs. 55.0% for Otter-I), while VideoChat2 still lags its own text-only ablation on position, count, and character tasks, suggesting insufficient instruction-tuning coverage for those categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BLIP-2 progressive multi-stage training paradigm (QFormer + LLM) and the VideoChat instruction-tuning framework, while replacing the CLIP-based visual encoder with UMT-L for stronger spatiotemporal representation. It departs from prior MLLM benchmarks (MME, MMBench, SEED-Bench) that focus almost exclusively on spatial understanding in static images, introducing a 'static-to-dynamic' methodology that systematically transforms 9 spatial task categories into 20 temporal video tasks. The automatic QA generation pipeline, which converts ground-truth annotations from 11 public video datasets into multiple-choice questions via LLMs, distinguishes it from labor-intensive annotation approaches used by Perception Test and FunQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MVBench fills a critical gap by providing the first comprehensive, multi-task benchmark specifically targeting temporal understanding in MLLMs, with 20 tasks spanning perception to cognition across diverse domains. The finding that the best prior video MLLM (VideoChat) barely outperforms a text-only variant (35.5% vs 34.7%) reveals that current models are not effectively leveraging video modality for temporal reasoning, a concern that motivates further research. Open questions remain: VideoChat2 still underperforms on position, count, and character tasks (likely due to insufficient training data for these categories), and whether the 15%+ improvement generalizes to longer videos or more complex temporal reasoning beyond the 5–35 second clips used here.
