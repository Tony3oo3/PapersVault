## Problem

Existing human motion transfer methods (GAN-based or ControlNet-based diffusion) are designed for simple, controlled poses and fail on real-world social media dance scenarios. They lack generalizability to unseen human subjects, backgrounds, and poses, and cannot compose arbitrary combinations of subject, background, and pose from different sources. The root cause is the inappropriate entangled integration of multiple conditioning signals (text, image, skeleton) in a single pathway.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241156/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference human image (decomposed into foreground and background via Grounded-SAM) and a sequence of pose skeleton keypoint images (from OpenPose) as input, operating at 256×256 resolution. The foreground is encoded as local CLIP image embeddings (pre-pooling, shape hw×d) rather than a single global vector, while the background is encoded through the pre-trained VQ-VAE encoder to preserve fine details. Output is a generated human dance image or a video of n=8 consecutive frames (with optional temporal modeling), where the human subject, background, and pose can each be sourced independently from different images or videos.

## Method

DISCO introduces a disentangled control architecture with three separate conditioning pathways: (1) the human foreground is injected via local CLIP image embeddings replacing text embeddings in cross-attention layers (initialized from Stable Diffusion Image Variations), (2) the background is controlled through a dedicated ControlNet branch that uses the pre-trained VQ-VAE encoder instead of random convolution layers to preserve intricate background details, and (3) the pose skeleton is controlled through a standard ControlNet branch. A key design choice is that the background ControlNet's condition input is also updated to the local CLIP foreground features, and the two ControlNet outputs are combined via addition into the U-Net's middle and up blocks. To address data scarcity for dance videos, the paper proposes Human Attribute Pre-training (HAP): a proxy reconstruction task on 700K+ diverse human images where the model must reconstruct the full image from separate foreground and background features without pose control, learning to distinguish dynamic subjects from static backgrounds and encode fine-grained human attributes before fine-tuning on the small TikTok dance dataset. Optional 1D temporal convolution/attention layers (zero-initialized, residual-connected) are appended after 2D spatial modules for video generation.

## Results

On the TikTok dance generation benchmark, DISCO† (with human attribute pre-training and classifier-free guidance) achieves a FID of 28.31 and FID-VID of 55.17, substantially outperforming the closest baseline TPS (FID 53.78, FID-VID 72.55) and the most relevant SOTA DreamPose (FID 79.46, FID-VID 80.51). DISCO also leads on SSIM (0.674), LPIPS (0.285), and FVD (267.75) across all compared methods. Adding temporal modeling further reduces FID-VID to 29.37 and FVD to 229.66, demonstrating strong temporal consistency even without explicit video modeling in the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISCO decisively outperforms DreamPose [25], the most relevant diffusion-based baseline, by large margins: FID drops from 79.46 to 28.31 (64% reduction), FID-VID from 80.51 to 55.17, and FVD from 551.56 to 267.75. Against the GAN-based motion transfer methods FOMM [52], MRAA [53], and TPS [73], DISCO achieves the best FID (28.31 vs. 53.78–85.03) and FID-VID (55.17 vs. 66.36–90.09), though MRAA and TPS retain slight advantages on PSNR (29.39/29.18 vs. 29.15) and L1 (3.21E-04/3.23E-04 vs. 3.69E-04), likely because they directly use the target image as input rather than a pose skeleton. On OOD datasets (TaiChi and NTU-120), DISCO also leads all baselines, achieving FID 15.89 and FID-VID 10.45 on TaiChi (vs. MRAA's 17.32/21.55) and FID 68.53 and FVD 458.92 on NTU-120 (vs. FOMM's 80.29/1439.50), confirming strong zero-shot generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISCO builds on the Stable Diffusion + ControlNet paradigm for conditional generation, but departs from prior human motion transfer methods (FOMM, MRAA, TPS) that require paired source-target images and from DreamPose, which is restricted to fashion catwalk poses against empty backgrounds. The key architectural departure is the disentangled control design: rather than naively stacking multiple ControlNets or replacing text with image embeddings, DISCO routes the human foreground through CLIP local features in cross-attention, the background through a VAE-encoded ControlNet branch, and the pose through a standard ControlNet branch. The human attribute pre-training strategy further distinguishes DISCO by leveraging large-scale unpaired human images (700K+) to learn diverse human attributes before fine-tuning on scarce dance video data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DISCO addresses a practical gap between controlled lab settings and real-world social media dance generation, explicitly targeting generalizability to unseen subjects/backgrounds/poses and compositional flexibility across sources. The human attribute pre-training offers a reusable recipe for overcoming data scarcity in human-centric generation tasks. However, the paper acknowledges significant limitations: poor hand posture control, inability to handle multi-person scenes or human-object interactions, and reliance on 256×256 resolution. Open questions include whether the disentangled control design scales to higher resolutions, whether the pre-training strategy transfers to other articulated-body domains, and how to incorporate fine-grained hand and interaction modeling.
