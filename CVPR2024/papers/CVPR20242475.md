## Problem

LVLMs rely on CLIP-based vision encoders pretrained with image-level contrastive learning, which learns holistic representations but fails to capture the fine-grained visual features essential for text-rich document understanding. The paper identifies this as the 'fine-grained feature collapse' issue: whole-image discrimination cannot extract the localized textual and layout cues that VDU tasks demand, leading to suboptimal performance on document benchmarks.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB document image (resized to 448×448) and a text question, producing a free-form text answer or caption. During pre-training, an additional OCR branch (PaddleOCR) supplies bounding boxes and text contents to a LayoutLMv3 multimodal encoder, but this branch is entirely discarded after pre-training, so inference incurs zero extra computational cost over the base LVLM. The method is plug-and-play across different LVLM backbones (Qwen-VL-Chat, mPLUG-Owl) with no architectural modification at test time.

## Method

DoCo introduces a contrastive learning framework that aligns document-object-level visual features (from the LVLM's ViT encoder) with multimodal features (visual + layout + text from a frozen LayoutLMv3_LARGE encoder). A novel ROI Aggregation module replaces standard ROIAlign by computing the overlap ratio between each ViT patch and each OCR bounding box, using this as an additive attention bias to pool patch features into per-object visual embeddings. Two InfoNCE-based losses are optimized: Intra-DoCo aligns visual and multimodal embeddings of individual document objects within a single image, while Inter-DoCo aligns the mean document-object representations across images in a batch. The vision encoder is updated via this contrastive objective during pre-training, after which the multimodal encoder is removed and the enhanced visual features are fed through the standard vision-language adapter into the LLM for fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Qwen-VL-Chat backbone, DoCo pre-training improves the mean score across 8 VDU benchmarks by approximately 2% over the CLIP-pretrained version (e.g., DocVQA ANLS 64.8 vs. 62.2, OCRVQA accuracy 73.2 vs. 71.1, TextVQA accuracy 63.6 vs. 61.7). On the mPLUG-Owl backbone, DoCo yields a 1.8% mean improvement (DocVQA 63.6 vs. 61.8, OCRVQA 72.1 vs. 70.3). The DoCo-enhanced Qwen-VL-Chat achieves the best or near-best results on 6 of 8 benchmarks, with UReader remaining competitive on InfoVQA (42.2 vs. 34.9) and TextCaps CIDEr (118.4 vs. 114.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoCo-enhanced Qwen-VL-Chat outperforms the original Qwen-VL-Chat (70.5/61.5/62.6 on OCRVQA/TextVQA/DocVQA) and mPLUG-DocOwl (52.6/62.2/38.2 on TextVQA/DocVQA/InfoVQA) across all reported benchmarks. Against UReader, the DoCo model wins on OCRVQA (73.2 vs. –), TextVQA (63.6 vs. 57.6), DocVQA (64.8 vs. 65.4, slight loss), ChartQA (68.9 vs. 59.3), and KLC (33.8 vs. 32.8), but trails on InfoVQA (34.9 vs. 42.2), WTQ (26.9 vs. 29.4), and TextCaps (114.5 vs. 118.4). The DoCo-enhanced mPLUG-Owl surpasses the original mPLUG-Owl (28.6/40.2/6.9) by large margins and also beats mPLUG-DocOwl on OCRVQA (72.1 vs. –) and ChartQA (60.1 vs. 57.4). Notably, the non-chat Qwen-VL variant (75.7 OCRVQA, 65.1 DocVQA) still edges out the DoCo-enhanced Qwen-VL-Chat on those two benchmarks, suggesting the chat-tuning pipeline introduces a small regression that DoCo partially recovers. Overall, DoCo consistently narrows the gap between VDU-specialized models and general-purpose LVLMs, with the largest gains observed on DocVQA (+2.6 ANLS) and OCRVQA (+2.1 accuracy) for Qwen-VL-Chat.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoCo repurposes the contrastive learning paradigm of CLIP (image-level instance discrimination) by shifting the unit of discrimination from whole images to individual document objects, and borrows the multimodal feature formulation (visual + layout + text embeddings) from LayoutLMv3 as the target representation. It departs from prior VDU-oriented LVLMs (mPLUG-DocOwl, LLaVAR, UniDoc, UReader) that focus on data curation or task-specific fine-tuning, instead targeting the vision encoder's pre-training to address what the authors term the 'fine-grained feature collapse' caused by CLIP's holistic image-text alignment. The ROI Aggregation module is a novel architectural component that adapts patch-level ViT features to arbitrary bounding boxes via overlap-based attention masking, filling a gap left by CNN-era ROIAlign/ROIPooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DoCo (multimodal encoder discarded after pre-training, zero inference overhead) makes it practically attractive for teams already deploying LVLMs on document workloads. However, the gains are modest (~2% on Qwen-VL-Chat, ~1.8% on mPLUG-Owl), and the method's reliance on PaddleOCR bounding boxes during pre-training introduces a dependency that may limit applicability to scanned or low-quality documents where OCR accuracy degrades. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-document text-rich scenes (e.g., street signs, infographics), whether the OCR dependency can be replaced by learned detection, and whether the benefits compound when combined with other VDU-specific pre-training objectives.
