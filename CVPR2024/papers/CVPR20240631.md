## Problem

Object recognition models like CLIP degrade significantly when deployed in geographies different from their training data, due to shifts in object design, materials, and context driven by cultural, climate, and economic differences. Standard domain adaptation techniques have been shown to fail on geographical shifts, and no effective method existed to improve cross-geography robustness without requiring target-region training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240631/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and outputs a class label from a predefined set of object categories (95 classes on DollarStreet, 600 on GeoNet). Classification is performed by computing cosine similarity between the image embedding (from CLIP's image encoder) and class text embeddings derived from prompts that incorporate country names and LLM-generated visual descriptors. In the soft prompting setting, only 16 source-geography images per class are used for training, and the model generalizes to unseen target geographies at inference time.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary knowledge sources for geographical robustness: (1) probing CLIP's internal knowledge by appending country names to prompts (CountryInPrompt), and (2) querying GPT-3 davinci-003 for geography-specific visual descriptors of each object class (CountryLLM), which are then used as additional prompt variants. For zero-shot inference, class scores are averaged over all geo-specific descriptor prompts. For soft prompting, the key contribution is geography knowledge regularization (gkr): a new loss term that minimizes cosine distance between learned class text embeddings and an ensemble of knowledge embeddings computed by averaging CountryInPrompt+LLM prompt embeddings across all target geographies. This regularizer, added to the standard cross-entropy loss with weight λ, prevents soft prompts from overfitting to a single source geography (e.g., Europe) and enables generalization to unseen target regions (e.g., Africa, Asia, Americas) without any target training data.

## Results

On DollarStreet (soft prompting, 16 shots, ViT-B/16), CountryInPrompt+LLM Reg achieves 64.0% total balanced accuracy, improving over the best soft-prompt baseline (KgCoOp, 62.4%) by +1.6, with gains of +2.8 on Africa, +1.2 on Asia, and +1.6 on Americas. On the hardest classes (<40% CoOp recall), the method gains +8.7% over CoOp versus +4.1% for KgCoOp. On GeoNet (USA→Asia transfer), target accuracy improves by +1.3 (ViT-B/16) and +1.4 (RN50) over the best baseline. A 16-shot source-only regularized model outperforms a 12-shot target-trained CoOp model on DollarStreet (1,140 target images) and a 4-shot target-trained model on GeoNet (2,400 target images).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: Zero-Shot CLIP (default prompts), GeneralLLM (Menon & Vondrick, ICLR 2023), CoOp, CoCoOp, and KgCoOp. In the zero-shot setting, CountryInPrompt+LLM outperforms default CLIP by up to +3.4 (RN50 total) and +5.0 on Africa, and consistently beats GeneralLLM (e.g., 52.6% vs 51.4% total top-1 with ViT-B/32). In the soft-prompting setting, the proposed CountryInPrompt+LLM Reg outperforms CoOp by +3.3% total (ViT-B/16, DollarStreet) and +2.8% on Africa, outperforms CoCoOp by at least +2.8 on Africa, and outperforms KgCoOp by +1.6 total and +2.8 on Africa. The advantage is most pronounced on the hardest classes: +8.7% over CoOp for classes with <40% baseline recall, compared to only +4.1% for KgCoOp. On GeoNet (USA→Asia), the method gains +1.3 (ViT-B/16) and +1.4 (RN50) over the best baseline on target accuracy, while incurring only small source drops (−0.7 to −1.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CoOp's soft prompting framework by introducing a geography knowledge regularization loss that constrains learned class embeddings toward an ensemble of geo-specific knowledge vectors, and adapts Menon & Vondrick's LLM descriptor prompting to be geography-conditioned rather than class-generic. It departs from KgCoOp's distance-based regularization by targeting cross-geography domain generalization (using country-specific LLM descriptors) rather than base-to-new-class generalization. The combination of probing CLIP's internal country knowledge (via country names in prompts) with external GPT-3 descriptive knowledge is novel, and the paper is the first to integrate LLM-derived geographical knowledge into soft prompt optimization for object recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Geographical robustness is critical for equitable AI deployment, yet prior domain adaptation methods have shown negligible or negative gains on geo shifts. This paper provides a practical, lightweight solution requiring no target-domain data, with the notable finding that a 16-shot source-only regularized model outperforms a 12-shot target-trained model—meaningful when acquiring target data is infeasible. However, the method inherits biases from both CLIP's training corpus and LLM hallucinations, and continent-level disparities (especially Africa) remain substantial. Open questions include how to validate and correct LLM-generated geographical descriptions, and whether the approach scales to fine-grained or open-vocabulary settings.
