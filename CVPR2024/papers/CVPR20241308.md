## Problem

Diffusion models produce high-quality images but require tens to hundreds of iterative denoising steps, making them impractical for interactive use. Existing distillation methods (Progressive Distillation, Consistency Models, InstaFlow) reduce the step count but still produce noticeably lower quality than the original multi-step model. This paper asks whether a single forward pass can match the quality of a full diffusion sampling trajectory.

## Input / Output

The one-step generator takes a random Gaussian noise vector (and optionally a text prompt or class label) and produces a single image in one forward pass, eliminating the iterative denoising loop. For text-to-image, it operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.5 at 512×512 resolution with a fixed classifier-free guidance scale; for class-conditional generation it works at 64×64 (ImageNet) or 32×32 (CIFAR-10). With FP16 inference the model achieves 20 FPS on modern GPUs, roughly 30× faster than the 50-step teacher.

## Method

DMD trains a one-step generator G_θ by minimizing an approximate KL divergence between the real data distribution and the generator's output distribution, where the gradient is expressed as the difference between two score functions: a frozen real score (the pretrained diffusion model) and a dynamically retrained fake score (a second diffusion model continuously trained on the generator's current outputs). Both scores are evaluated on Gaussian-noise-perturbed versions of the generated image at randomly sampled timesteps, making the gradient well-defined even where the original distributions do not overlap. A time-dependent weighting normalizes gradient magnitudes across noise levels. To prevent mode collapse and anchor the generator to the teacher's structure, DMD adds an LPIPS regression loss against a small pre-computed set of multi-step diffusion outputs (generated with <1% of training compute). The generator is initialized from the base diffusion denoiser (minus time conditioning), and the entire procedure is compatible with classifier-free guidance by substituting the guided model's mean prediction for the real score.

## Results

On ImageNet-64×64, DMD achieves FID 2.62 in a single forward pass, nearly matching its EDM teacher (FID 2.32 at 512 steps) while delivering a 512× speedup. On zero-shot MS COCO-30k text-to-image, DMD reaches FID 11.49 at 0.09 s latency, within 2.7 FID of Stable Diffusion v1.5 (FID 8.78 at 2.59 s) and approximately 30× faster. With FP16 inference, the model generates 512×512 images at 20 FPS. On CIFAR-10, the class-conditional model attains FID 2.66.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-64×64, DMD (FID 2.62) substantially outperforms every competing one-step distillation method: Consistency Model (6.20), Diff-Instruct (5.57), TRACT (7.43), Meng et al. (7.54), DFNO (7.83), Progressive Distillation (15.39), and BOOT (16.30), representing a 2.4× improvement over the previous best (Consistency Model). On MS COCO-30k, DMD (FID 11.49, 0.09 s) beats all accelerated diffusion methods including InstaFlow-0.9B (13.10), UFOGen (12.78), LCM-LoRA 4-step (23.62), DPM++ 4-step (22.36), and UniPC 4-step (19.57), as well as StyleGAN-T (13.90). The only method with a lower FID on MS COCO is GigaGAN (9.09), a GAN-based approach, though DMD is faster (0.09 s vs. 0.13 s). In the high-guidance-scale setting (guidance 8), DMD (FID 14.93, CLIP-Score 0.320) again outperforms all 4-step solvers and 1-step LCM-LoRA (FID 77.90), while remaining within 1.5 FID of the SDv1.5 teacher (FID 13.45, CLIP-Score 0.322). Overall, DMD is the only one-step method that approaches the quality of its multi-step teacher across all evaluated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMD repurposes Variational Score Distillation (VSD) from ProlificDreamer [80]—originally designed for test-time 3D optimization—into a training objective for a full generative network, using two diffusion models (one fixed on real data, one dynamically trained on fake outputs) to estimate the score difference that drives the KL-divergence gradient. This departs from the dominant distillation paradigm (Progressive Distillation, Consistency Models, InstaFlow) that enforces pointwise noise-to-image correspondences, instead matching distributions globally. The LPIPS regression loss on pre-computed teacher outputs borrows from GAN/pix2pix stabilization techniques, and the overall framework draws on Score-SDE [74] for well-defined score estimation on perturbed distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DMD closes the quality gap between one-step and multi-step diffusion to within 0.3 FID on ImageNet and 2.7 FID on COCO, enabling 20 FPS interactive generation—a practical threshold that opens up real-time creative tools. The approach is model-agnostic (works with EDM, Stable Diffusion, any deterministic-sampling diffusion model) and requires only a modest paired dataset (<1% of training compute). Key limitations include the high training memory cost of simultaneously maintaining the generator and fake-score diffusion model, and a residual quality gap versus 100+ step sampling. Open questions include whether the method extends to video generation, higher resolutions, and whether LoRA-based parameter-efficient variants can reduce the training footprint.
