## Problem

Learnable image matching methods (e.g., SuperGlue, LightGlue) achieve strong performance on conventional benchmarks but generalize poorly to novel image domains such as object-centric or aerial captures, sometimes performing no better than hand-crafted SIFT. The paper identifies two root causes: entanglement of positional and appearance features in keypoint representations, and the absence of domain-agnostic guidance during inter-image feature propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240523/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images and extracts 1024 SuperPoint keypoints (256-dim descriptors) plus DINOv2 dense feature maps interpolated at keypoint locations. It outputs an N×M matching matrix via the Sinkhorn algorithm applied to refined keypoint descriptors, from which keypoint correspondences are derived for downstream pose estimation or image registration.

## Method

OmniGlue introduces two key mechanisms into a 9-block (18-layer) attention-based graph propagation architecture. First, DINOv2 foundation model features guide inter-image graph construction: for each keypoint, only the top-half most similar keypoints in the other image (by channel-normalized DINO similarity) are connected, pruning the dense pairwise graph and focusing cross-attention on potentially matchable regions. Second, a position-guided attention disentangles spatial and appearance signals: keypoint positions are fused into query and key projections (shaping attention weights) but excluded from value projections, so the residual descriptor update contains only appearance information. This prevents the final matching descriptors from overfitting to training-domain positional patterns. The model is trained with negative log-likelihood on the Sinkhorn-refined matching matrix, initialized on Synthetic Homography (SH100), pretrained on SH200, and fine-tuned on MegaDepth.

## Results

On the in-domain MegaDepth-1500 benchmark, OmniGlue achieves 47.4/65.0/77.8 pose AUC@5°/10°/20°, matching LightGlue (47.6/64.8/77.9). On zero-shot generalization to 6 out-of-domain datasets (GSO-Hard, GSO-Easy, NAVI-Multiview, NAVI-Wild, ScanNet, DeepAerial), OmniGlue achieves an average relative gain of 20.9% over SuperGlue and 9.5% over LightGlue at the tightest thresholds. The largest gains appear on DeepAerial (PCK@1%: 22.4% vs. 16.4% for SuperGlue, +36.6% relative) and GSO-Hard (Acc@5°: 8.6% vs. 7.2% for SuperGlue, +19.4% relative). With only 30 fine-tuning instances from a single object category, OmniGlue further improves GSO-Hard Acc@5° from 8.6% to 16.7% (+94.2% relative over its own zero-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary sparse baseline SuperGlue [41], OmniGlue wins on all 6 out-of-domain datasets, with the largest relative gains on DeepAerial (+36.6% PCK@1%) and GSO-Hard (+19.4% Acc@5°). Against LightGlue [27], OmniGlue wins on 5 of 6 out-of-domain domains (GSO-Hard, GSO-Easy, NAVI-Multiview, NAVI-Wild, DeepAerial) but loses on ScanNet (31.3% vs. 36.7% Acc@5°), where LightGlue's rotary positional encoding appears better suited to indoor scenes. Dense/semi-dense methods LoFTR [44] and PDCNet [46] achieve higher in-domain MegaDepth AUC (52.8 and 51.5 vs. 47.4) but generalize worse on 5 of 6 out-of-domain datasets, often falling below SuperGlue. Hand-crafted baselines SIFT+MNN [29] and SuperPoint+MNN [10] are outperformed by OmniGlue on all out-of-domain datasets except DeepAerial PCK@5% where SIFT is slightly higher (32.2% vs. 36.6%—actually OmniGlue still wins). The dense methods' weaker generalization is attributed to their joint learning of descriptors and matching, which over-specializes to the training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniGlue extends the sparse learnable matching lineage initiated by SuperGlue (graph-based attention over SuperPoint keypoints) and refined by LightGlue (faster inference, rotary positional encoding). Its two key departures are: (1) using a frozen DINOv2 foundation model to prune inter-image graphs and guide cross-attention, rather than directly using foundation-model features as descriptors, and (2) disentangling keypoint positional features from local descriptors in the attention mechanism, so that position informs attention weights but does not contaminate the final matching descriptors. This positions OmniGlue as a generalization-focused redesign of the SuperGlue architecture rather than a new matching paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling empirical case that the field's focus on in-domain benchmark performance has masked a critical deployment gap: learnable matchers degrade sharply on out-of-domain data, sometimes to levels comparable to SIFT. The 20.9% average relative gain across six unseen domains and the finding that dense matchers (LoFTR, PDCNet) generalize worse than sparse ones challenge the assumption that end-to-end joint learning is universally beneficial. Open questions remain on how to better fuse coarse foundation-model features with fine-grained local descriptors (the paper shows naive merging hurts), and how to exploit unannotated target-domain data for further adaptation.
