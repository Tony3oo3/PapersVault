## Problem

Generating entire 3D scenes from text remains challenging because scenes contain multiple objects with diverse appearances in complex layouts, and no single 3D representation handles both individual object manipulation and global scene structure well. Additionally, cameras placed within scenes suffer from occlusions and limited viewing angles, causing poor geometry in occluded or boundary regions, and the Janus (multi-face) problem persists at the scene level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241940/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and optionally user-provided 3D objects as input. An LLM automatically suggests object categories (e.g., 'beds', 'sofa') from the prompt, which are then instantiated via off-the-shelf text-to-3D models or replaced with user-supplied assets. The output is a hybrid 3D scene (per-object DMTet meshes plus a NeRF environment) that can be rendered from arbitrary perspective or panoramic viewpoints to produce RGB and depth/disparity images.

## Method

SceneWiz3D introduces a hybrid 3D representation that assigns explicit DMTet (signed distance function with marching tetrahedra) to identified objects of interest and an implicit NeRF to the remaining environment, blending them via z-buffering. For automatic scene layout, it replaces gradient-based configuration optimization with Particle Swarm Optimization guided by CLIP similarity between rendered views and the prompt, avoiding local minima in the low-dimensional pose space. The scene model is optimized by alternating VSD with Stable Diffusion on perspective RGB views, SDS with a panoramic RGBD diffusion model (LDM3D-pano) for geometry-aware supervision, and a MiDaS-based depth regularizer, with PSO updates performed every 3000 iterations over 20,000 total optimization steps.

## Results

On a test set of 10 indoor scene prompts (100 viewpoints each, 1000 images total), SceneWiz3D achieves a CLIP-AP of 97.6, a disparity alignment score of 0.14, and a FID of 10.43, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The method surpasses the strongest appearance baseline (ProlificDreamer, CLIP-AP 95.5) by 2.1 points and the strongest geometry baselines (LDM3D, align 0.24 / FID 11.33) by 0.10 and 0.90 respectively. FID is computed against NYU Depth v2 as the ground-truth depth distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneWiz3D outperforms all four baselines on all three metrics. Against the two SDS-based object-generation methods adapted for scenes, DreamFusion (CLIP-AP 92.6, FID 41.18) and ProlificDreamer (CLIP-AP 95.5, FID 76.22), the gains are substantial: +2.1 to +5.2 CLIP-AP and 30–66 FID points, with the paper attributing the baselines' poor geometry to inadequate multi-view supervision causing floating artifacts and foggy disparity maps. Against the two non-SDS scene methods, Text2Room (CLIP-AP 63.4, FID 26.25) and LDM3D (CLIP-AP 69.3, FID 11.33), SceneWiz3D leads by 28.2 and 28.3 CLIP-AP respectively, while also achieving the best alignment score (0.14 vs. 0.24–0.32). The closest competitor is LDM3D on geometry (FID 11.33 vs. 10.43), but LDM3D's 2D-to-3D projection approach suffers from severe distortion and much lower text alignment, making SceneWiz3D the clear overall winner.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score distillation sampling (SDS/VSD) paradigm from text-to-3D object generation (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer) to full scene synthesis by introducing a hybrid representation: explicit DMTet for user-specified or LLM-suggested objects of interest, and implicit NeRF for the surrounding environment. It departs from prior scene generation work (Text2Room, LDM3D, RoomDreamer) that either projects 2D outputs into 3D or requires predefined layouts, by jointly optimizing object configurations via Particle Swarm Optimization and scene geometry via multi-source score distillation (perspective RGB + panoramic RGBD). The use of PSO, borrowed from the optimization literature, is a notable conceptual departure from the gradient-based optimization that dominates the text-to-3D pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SceneWiz3D demonstrates that combining explicit and implicit representations within a single optimization loop can simultaneously maintain per-object multiview consistency and flexible scene geometry, addressing a fundamental tension in 3D scene modeling. The mitigation of the Janus problem through explicit object modeling is a practical insight applicable beyond this specific pipeline. However, the method inherits SDS limitations (long optimization, color saturation), the CLIP-based configuration scoring limits fine-grained spatial reasoning, and scalability to large or outdoor scenes remains untested. Open questions include whether the hybrid representation can be made differentiable end-to-end for faster convergence, and whether learned layout priors could replace the heuristic PSO search.
