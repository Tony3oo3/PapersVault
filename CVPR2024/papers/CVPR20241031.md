## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient tuning methods for diffusion models, such as LoRA, insert trainable modules throughout the entire U-Net backbone, forcing backward propagation through all encoder and decoder layers and resulting in memory consumption that can exceed full fine-tuning. The paper identifies that skip connections between encoder and decoder carry critical long-range structural information yet remain underexplored as a targeted tuning site, and asks whether editing only these connections can achieve comparable or better generation quality at a fraction of the cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241031/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces a 512×512 RGB image. For controllable synthesis, it additionally accepts one or more condition images (canny edge, depth, segmentation, pose keypoint, color, or masked image) at the same resolution, which are encoded by a cascaded dense convolution network into multi-scale hint features. A distinctive property is that individually trained single-condition models can be combined at inference time without joint retraining, enabling compositional multi-condition generation and interpolation in the condition embedding space.

## Method

SCEdit inserts a lightweight SC-Tuner module at every skip connection of a pre-trained U-Net; each tuner is a residual adapter (down-projection → GELU → up-projection) that edits the encoder feature before it is concatenated into the corresponding decoder block. For controllable synthesis, the CSC-Tuner extends this by adding condition-specific branches whose outputs are weighted-summed (with weights summing to 1) and injected alongside the skip feature, while condition images are encoded by a multi-layer hint block with zero-convolution and SiLU activations to match skip-connection dimensions. Because all trainable parameters reside exclusively in the skip-connection path, backward propagation is confined to the decoder blocks, decoupling the frozen encoder from gradient computation and reducing peak memory by ~52% and training time by ~22% relative to full fine-tuning. The framework is trained with AdamW (lr 5e-5) for 100k steps on SD v1.5 or v2.1, and supports few-shot transfer (30 image-text pairs per style) as well as zero-shot generalization to related tasks like sketch-to-image and outpainting.

## Results

On COCO2017 text-to-image generation, SCEdit achieves the best FID of 13.82 using only 19.68M trainable parameters (2.29% of full fine-tuning's 859.52M), while reducing training memory by 52.1% (29.02G vs. 60.57G for LoRA/r=64) and training 1.6× faster (×0.78 vs. ×1.24). The lightweight variant SCEdit₁₀ (1.98M params) still achieves FID 13.99, outperforming LoRA/r=6 (FID 15.12) by 1.13. On controllable image synthesis (canny edge, LAION), SCEdit/k=1 attains FID 73.18 with 28.82M parameters—only 7.9% of ControlNet's 364.23M—while using 30% less memory (34.78G vs. 49.51G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCEdit outperforms all six baselines on FID in both task settings. Against LoRA/r=64 (FID 13.96), SCEdit achieves a lower FID of 13.82 while using 52% less training memory and completing training 1.6× faster; against LoRA/r=6 (FID 15.12), the lightweight SCEdit₁₀ variant (1.98M params) improves FID by 1.13 points. In controllable synthesis, SCEdit/k=1 (FID 73.18) beats ControlNet (74.86), T2I-Adapter (73.37), ControlNet-XS (75.63), and ControlLoRA (74.14) on FID, while using only 7.9% of ControlNet's parameters and 30% less memory. Notably, ControlLoRA, despite having fewer parameters (21.52M), requires the most memory (68.92G) and is 1.34× slower, whereas SCEdit/k=1 uses 34.78G and trains at ×0.87 speed. The original SD v1.5 zero-shot model (FID 15.48) is improved by 1.66 FID points after SCEdit fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCEdit builds on the efficient tuning paradigm established by Res-Tuning [21] and U-Tuning [20], which demonstrated that lightweight adapter modules can replace full fine-tuning of diffusion U-Nets. However, it departs from the dominant placement strategy of prior methods like LoRA [17], which injects low-rank matrices into attention layers across the entire backbone, by instead targeting the skip connections between encoder and decoder. This positioning is informed by prior analyses of skip connections (ScaleLong [19], FreeU [42]) that identified their role in carrying high-frequency structural information, but SCEdit is the first to exploit this for parameter-efficient adaptation. For controllable synthesis, it unifies multi-condition injection into a single lightweight module, contrasting with ControlNet's [50] parallel network and T2I-Adapter's [29] separate adapter branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 52% memory reduction over LoRA and 30% over ControlNet, combined with the architectural insight that backward propagation can be confined to decoder blocks, offers a practically significant efficiency gain for practitioners with limited compute. The composability of independently trained conditional models (e.g., canny edge + color map) without joint retraining is a useful practical feature for multi-condition pipelines. However, the FID improvements over strong baselines are modest (13.82 vs. 13.96 for LoRA/r=64), and all experiments are conducted at 512×512 on SD v1.5/v2.1, leaving open whether the skip-connection-editing strategy transfers to larger models (SDXL) or transformer-based diffusion architectures (DiT) where the U-Net skip connection structure does not exist.
