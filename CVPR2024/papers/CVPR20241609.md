## Problem

Class Activation Maps (CAMs) provide only class-wise relative attention values that are incomparable across classes and do not reveal the absolute contribution of each image region to the model's final prediction. This limits their utility for fine-grained class discrimination analysis, threshold-based segmentation, and any task requiring meaningful cross-class or region-level quantitative reasoning about model decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241609/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, 448×448 for CUB, 352×352 for CMML) processed through a backbone network (ResNet-50 or Swin Transformer V2-B) and produces a probabilistic class activation map at the backbone's spatial resolution (7×7, 14×14, or 11×11). Unlike standard CAMs where values are arbitrary relative scores normalized per-class, CAPE outputs are true probability values per voxel per class, and the sum over all spatial locations for a given class exactly equals that class's prediction probability, enabling direct quantitative attribution of the model's decision to specific image regions.

## Method

CAPE reformulates the standard softmax classification layer as a probabilistic ensemble: the image-level prediction is decomposed as a sum over all spatial locations of (per-voxel class probability via softmax over classes) × (per-voxel spatial importance via softmax over spatial dimensions of the class-averaged CAM). This introduces only a single trainable scalar (softmax temperature) and requires a single feed-forward pass for explanation generation. Training uses a knowledge-distillation bootstrap loss (cross-entropy plus KL divergence to the vanilla classifier's softened predictions) to close the accuracy gap, with a selective KLD variant that only enforces distribution matching when CAPE and vanilla predictions disagree. A second variant, μ-CAPE, replaces the full probabilistic decomposition with a single-softmax form that restores attention on class-mutual regions, trading the exact composition guarantee for better scores on standard CAM interpretability metrics (AD, IC, ADD, ADCC).

## Results

On CUB200-2011, μ-CAPE(TS) achieves the best Borda Count (BC) of 12, with AD of 10.3, IC of 48.5, ADD of 74.2, and ADCC of 84.4, outperforming all 8 baselines. On ImageNet, μ-CAPE(TS) leads with BC of 9 (AD 10.7, IC 58.3, ADD 58.7, ADCC 73.5), while CAPE(PF) achieves the best mIoU of 11.0. On the CMML cytology dataset, μ-CAPE(TS) again tops BC at 9 (AD 14.1, IC 48.0, ADD 50.1, ADCC 78.4). Computationally, CAPE and μ-CAPE require only ~150 ms per image versus ~15 s for Score-CAM, a 100× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

μ-CAPE(TS) achieves the top Borda Count ranking across all three datasets (CUB: 12, ImageNet: 9, CMML: 9), beating the strongest baseline Score-CAM (6, 5, 1) by a wide margin on AD, IC, ADD, and ADCC. On CUB, μ-CAPE(TS) reduces AD from 16.3 (Score-CAM) to 10.3 and increases IC from 33.0 to 48.5; on ImageNet, it improves AD from 8.5 (Score-CAM) to 10.7 while boosting IC from 46.9 to 58.3. CAPE(PF) uniquely excels on mIoU (13.4 on CUB, 11.0 on ImageNet), reflecting its focus on class-discriminative regions with minimal overlap between top-2 class maps, whereas all gradient-based baselines (Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, SG-CAM++, Layer-CAM) report mIoU near 100% due to heavy overlap. The post-fitting variant μ-CAPE(PF) is competitive with Score-CAM on CUB (BC 5 vs 6) and ImageNet (BC 5 vs 5) while being ~100× faster (150 ms vs 15 s per image). Standard CAM and its gradient-based variants (Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, SG-CAM++) consistently rank last on BC (0) across all datasets, confirming that the probabilistic ensemble formulation provides substantially more informative explanations than raw activation-weighted heatmaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAPE directly reformulates the original CAM formulation (Zhou et al., CVPR 2016) by decomposing the softmax classification layer into a probabilistic ensemble over spatial locations, where each voxel's contribution is an absolute probability rather than a class-relative activation. This departs from the entire CAM variant family (Grad-CAM, Score-CAM, LIFT-CAM, FD-CAM, Layer-CAM) which all produce class-wise relative attention maps that are incomparable across classes. The method also draws on softmax-based aggregation ideas from local importance pooling and attention mechanisms, repurposing the softmax's sum-to-one property to enforce a composition relationship between region-level contributions and the overall prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAPE provides the first CAM-based explanation where region contributions are absolute probabilities that exactly sum to the model prediction, enabling meaningful cross-class comparisons (e.g., quantifying what differentiates two fine-grained classes) and analytical reasoning about model decisions. However, the paper acknowledges a persistent accuracy gap versus the vanilla classifier (e.g., 74.64% vs. 76.13% on ImageNet) and the critical caveat that CAPE explains its own softened predictions rather than the original model's decisions, limiting its use as a drop-in post-hoc tool. Open questions include whether the convergence issues from soft predictions can be resolved, whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks, and whether the accuracy-interpretability trade-off is fundamental to the softmax-ensemble formulation.
