## Problem

Simulating realistic radar scans for RF system prototyping requires accurate scene geometry, material properties, and a radar synthesis function, but existing model-based simulators demand manual annotation of radar-specific material properties that are impractical to survey. Data-driven alternatives (CFAR point clouds, lidar occupancy grids) either lose transmittance information or cannot scale reflector returns to radar-realistic amplitudes. No prior method learns an implicit, view-dependent representation of radar reflectance and transmittance from compact mmWave radar measurements to synthesize novel range-Doppler views.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-antenna (8 TX/RX pairs) range-Doppler heatmaps from a compact FMCW mmWave radar collected along a trajectory through a static scene, paired with accurate position and instantaneous velocity estimates from lidar-based localization. The radar must be in continuous motion, as the Doppler axis is essential for reducing each pixel's spatial ambiguity from a coarse 2D region to a thin circle. Outputs are synthesized range-Doppler images at arbitrary novel poses, plus explicit tomographic grids of reflectance σ and transmittance α sampled from the learned implicit field.

## Method

DART learns a neural implicit world model (based on Instant NGP multiresolution hash encoding) that maps each 3D position to a base reflectance σ, base transmittance α, and shared spherical harmonics coefficients encoding view-dependent angular variation. The key rendering insight is that each range-Doppler pixel corresponds to a circle (intersection of a range sphere and a Doppler cone) rather than a ray, so the forward model integrates the reflectance/transmittance field along this velocity-dependent circle, accounting for free-space path loss (1/r²), cumulative occlusion via transmittance products, and per-antenna beamforming gain. A critical optimization reuses σ and α samples across all range bins sharing the same Doppler value, reducing the sampling cost from O(range × Doppler × antenna × range-integration × Doppler-integration) to a tractable level. Training uses stochastic gradient descent (Adam) with an L1 loss against ground-truth range-Doppler frames, and a custom activation f(α)=exp(max(0,α)) with a tailored gradient estimator ensures numerical stability for the transmittance output.

## Results

DART achieves a mean SSIM of 0.636±0.012 across all 12 collected traces, outperforming the strongest baseline (CFAR point cloud aggregation at 0.545±0.007) by 0.091 SSIM. The Lidar-based raytracing simulator (0.463±0.005) and Nearest Neighbor (0.468±0.006) baselines perform worst, trailing DART by approximately 0.17 SSIM. Training requires only 3 epochs on an RTX 4090 GPU, taking roughly 1–2× the data collection time (~10 minutes per dataset).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DART outperforms all three baselines on every trace in the dataset. CFAR point cloud aggregation is the strongest baseline (0.545 SSIM) but still trails DART by 0.091 SSIM; the paper attributes this to CFAR's inability to model transmittance or capture the spatial 'volume' of reflectors, producing sparse and noisy point clouds. The Lidar-based raytracing simulator (0.463 SSIM) correctly identifies reflector positions but cannot scale their radar returns due to the absence of radar-specific material properties. The Nearest Neighbor baseline (0.468 SSIM) generates radar-realistic images by definition but produces 'misplaced' outputs that do not vary continuously over poses, since exhaustively measuring all (position, orientation, velocity) combinations is impractical. DART's advantage stems from jointly learning view-dependent reflectance and transmittance via spherical harmonics and exploiting the range-Doppler representation to reduce angular ambiguity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DART directly extends the Neural Radiance Field paradigm (Mildenhall et al., 2021) to the mmWave radar domain, adapting the implicit-representation-plus-differentiable-rendering-plus-SGD-inversion framework to a fundamentally different sensing modality. Where NeRF renders pixels by integrating along a 1D ray under a pinhole camera model, DART must integrate along a velocity-dependent circle in range-Doppler space, and replaces RGB/transparency with view-dependent reflectance and transmittance modeled via spherical harmonics. The world representation builds on Instant NGP's multiresolution hash encoding, and the radar-specific physics (Doppler-based angular disambiguation, free-space path loss, antenna gain patterns) departs from all prior NeRF work. Prior radar simulation approaches are either model-based (ray tracing, FEM, FDTD) requiring manual scene specification, or data-driven (CFAR point clouds, SAR, radargrammetry) that lack the implicit, view-dependent material modeling DART provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DART demonstrates that neural implicit representations can learn radar-specific material properties (reflectance and transmittance as functions of incidence angle) from compact mmWave radar scans, enabling realistic novel-view radar simulation without manual scene modeling—a significant step toward data-driven radar prototyping. The ability to sample tomographic reflectance/transmittance maps provides a verification mechanism and a practical mapping tool that captures properties invisible to lidar. Key limitations remain: the method requires a static scene, accurate instantaneous velocity, and a constantly moving radar, all of which constrain real-world deployment. Future work could relax these assumptions (as visual NeRFs have done for dynamic scenes and unknown poses), potentially enabling single-chip radar solutions for simultaneous localization, mapping, and imaging, and opening the door to multimodal implicit scene representations combining radar with other sensors.
