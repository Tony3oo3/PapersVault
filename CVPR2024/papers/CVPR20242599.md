## Problem

Open-vocabulary segmentation methods that leverage CLIP backbones suffer from two efficiency bottlenecks: the large model size of the inherited CLIP image encoder and the expensive fine-tuning required to preserve its generalizable knowledge. Existing efficiency techniques such as iterative magnitude pruning and adapter-based fine-tuning are heuristic and framework-specific, requiring re-training or re-customization for each new OVS architecture. The paper asks whether efficiency can be made principled and seamlessly transferable across OVS frameworks without further customization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (resized to 512×512 during training) paired with free-form text prompts describing target categories, and produces pixel-level segmentation masks with per-pixel class assignments. Text embeddings are extracted by a frozen CLIP text encoder, while vision features come from the pruned and selectively fine-tuned image backbone. Evaluation follows a cross-dataset protocol: the model is trained on COCO Panoptic and zero-shot evaluated on Cityscapes, ADE20K-150/847, Pascal VOC 2012 (PAS-20), and Pascal Context (PC-59, PC-459).

## Method

OpenTrans introduces two complementary efficiency mechanisms. First, for model efficiency, it applies Iterative Magnitude Pruning to the CLIP image encoder using only a knowledge-distillation loss (no semantic supervision), yielding a semantic-agnostic sparse mask that can be directly transferred to heterogeneous OVS frameworks (DeepLabV3, Mask2Former-based, FC-CLIP) without re-pruning or architectural modification. Second, for training efficiency, it performs layer-selective fine-tuning guided by heavy-tail spectrum analysis: the power-law coefficient α is computed from the empirical spectral density of each layer's weight matrix, and layers with small α (heavy-tailed, indicating well-trained weights) are frozen while layers with large α (light-tailed, under-trained) are updated. This plug-and-play strategy introduces zero extra parameters and requires no forward/backward pass to determine which layers to freeze, reducing training FLOPs by up to 32.6% when combined with the pruned backbone.

## Results

On COCO (133 classes), the best variant Ours (FC-CLIP) achieves 56.8% mIoU with only 17.8M parameters and 105.6G FLOPs, compared to FC-CLIP's 58.7% mIoU at 39.0M params and 200.1G FLOPs — a 54% parameter reduction and 47% FLOP reduction for a mere 1.9% mIoU drop. On Pascal-Context-59, Ours (FC-CLIP) reaches 47.4% mIoU versus Cat-Seg's 57.5% (the SOTA), but with 71% fewer parameters (17.8M vs 60.8M) and 97% fewer FLOPs (105.6G vs 3847.8G). The layer-selective fine-tuning further reduces training FLOPs by 12.0% (to 159.6P) and, combined with pruning, by 32.6% (to 122.2P) relative to the full Han et al. baseline (181.4P).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest SOTA baseline Cat-Seg [7], Ours (FC-CLIP) trails by 6.4% mIoU on PAS-20 (87.3 vs 93.7) and 8.4% on ADE20K-150 (18.8 vs 27.2), but uses 71% fewer parameters (17.8M vs 60.8M) and 97% fewer FLOPs (105.6G vs 3847.8G). Compared to FC-CLIP [47], the pruned version sacrifices only 1.9% mIoU on COCO (56.8 vs 58.7) and 2.2% on PAS-20 (87.3 vs 89.5) while halving both parameters (17.8M vs 39.0M) and FLOPs (105.6G vs 200.1G). Versus Han et al. [17], Ours (Han et al.) drops 3.7% on COCO (42.3 vs 46.0) and 6.4% on PAS-20 (64.8 vs 71.2) but cuts parameters by 48% and FLOPs by 35%. Ours (FC-CLIP) substantially outperforms older baselines: +17.3% over SimBaseline [44] on COCO (56.8 vs 39.5), +6.6% over ZegFormer [10] on PAS-20 (87.3 vs 80.7), +49% over ZS3Net [1] on PAS-20 (87.3 vs 38.3), and +40% over LSeg [25] on PAS-20 (87.3 vs 47.4). The pruned DeeplabV3 variant actually surpasses the original DeeplabV3 [2] on COCO (34.2 vs 26.3) and PAS-20 (54.6 vs 44.1) while using 53% fewer parameters. Overall, the paper's core trade-off is a modest 2–8% mIoU loss on the hardest datasets in exchange for 50–97% reductions in model size and inference FLOPs, with an additional 12–33% reduction in training FLOPs from layer-selective fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of model compression (Lottery Ticket Hypothesis / iterative magnitude pruning) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but departs from both in a key way: it makes the efficiency *transferable* across OVS frameworks. Traditional pruning produces task-specific sparse masks that must be re-discovered per architecture, and adapter/LoRA-style fine-tuning requires inserting and customizing extra modules. Here, the authors prune the CLIP image encoder using only a knowledge-distillation loss (no semantic supervision), yielding a semantic-agnostic subnetwork that can be dropped into DeepLabV3, Mask2Former-based, or FC-CLIP pipelines without re-pruning. For layer selection during fine-tuning, they repurpose heavy-tail spectrum analysis of pretrained weights—a tool previously used to *predict* model quality—as a plug-and-play criterion for deciding which layers to freeze, avoiding the extra parameters and design overhead of adapter-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: halving backbone parameters (~21M fewer) and cutting training FLOPs by ~33% while retaining most OVS accuracy makes CLIP-based segmentation more deployable in resource-constrained settings. The transferability claim—one pruning mask serving multiple downstream frameworks—is the most broadly useful idea, potentially saving significant re-training cost as new OVS architectures appear. However, the evidence is currently limited to a single ResNet-50 backbone and three OVS frameworks, and the remaining mIoU gaps (2–4 points on several benchmarks) mean the full CLIP encoder is still needed for top performance. Open questions the authors flag include scaling to ConvNeXt-Large or ViT backbones, finer-grained (channel/element-level) selection, and extension to other open-vocabulary tasks like detection.
