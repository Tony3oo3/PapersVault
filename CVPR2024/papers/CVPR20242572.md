## Problem

Video dataset distillation remains largely unexplored despite the success of image distillation, primarily because the additional temporal dimension dramatically increases computational cost and the optimal compression strategy is unclear. This paper provides the first systematic study of video distillation, revealing that temporal information is poorly learned during distillation and that the temporal dimension of synthetic data contributes little to downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242572/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes real video clips with class labels, sampled to 16 frames at 112×112 (MiniUCF, HMDB51) or 8 frames at 64×64 (Kinetics-400, SSv2), and produces a synthetic video dataset structured as a static memory (one still image per video) plus a dynamic memory (single-channel frames) that are combined by a small 3D CNN (MiniC3D) to reconstruct full-length synthetic clips. The total storage of static memory, dynamic memory, and the combining network is constrained to be less than the storage of the corresponding IPC (instances per class) of real data, typically achieving 42–82% of the baseline storage.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage static-dynamic disentanglement paradigm. Stage 1 (Static Learning) distills each video into a single still image using standard image distillation (DC gradient matching) with N_syn = N_real = K = 1, where a random frame is sampled per epoch so the static memory implicitly observes all frames. Stage 2 (Dynamic Fine-tuning) introduces a learnable dynamic memory of single-channel frames and a MiniC3D network H that fuses the frozen static memory with the dynamic memory to produce multi-frame synthetic clips, which are then aligned with real videos using pluggable matching losses (DM, MTT, or FRePo). The key insight, supported by a taxonomy over four temporal-compression axes (N_syn, N_real, K, interpolation algorithm), is that dense frame correspondence is non-critical: a single static frame captures most distillable information, and a compact dynamic block suffices to recover motion, yielding state-of-the-art accuracy with substantially reduced storage.

## Results

On MiniUCF (IPC=15), the best variant MTT+Ours achieves 23.3% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the full-dataset-trained model's 57.2% by a wide margin but outperforming the strongest baseline FRePo (20.3%) by 3.0 points. On HMDB51 (IPC=15), FRePo+Ours reaches 31.2% top-1 accuracy, exceeding FRePo (30.2%) and MTT (28.4%) while using only 42% of the storage (228 MB vs. 586 MB). On large-scale Kinetics-400 (IPC=1, top-5), MTT+Ours achieves 11.5%, a 2.4-point gain over MTT (9.1%), while on SSv2 (IPC=1, top-5) MTT+Ours reaches 8.3% versus MTT's 6.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against coreset selection baselines (Random, Herding, K-Center), the proposed method consistently wins: MTT+Ours achieves 23.3% on MiniUCF versus 9.9–12.7% for coreset methods, and FRePo+Ours reaches 31.2% on HMDB51 versus 22.9–25.8%. Compared to directly adapted image distillation methods, MTT+Ours improves over MTT by 4.3 points on MiniUCF (23.3% vs. 19.0%) and ties on HMDB51 (28.3% vs. 28.4%); FRePo+Ours beats FRePo by 1.7 points on MiniUCF and 1.0 point on HMDB51 while using only 42% of the storage (48 MB vs. 115 MB). On large-scale Kinetics-400, MTT+Ours gains 2.4 points over MTT (11.5% vs. 9.1% top-5), but DM+Ours underperforms DM (7.0% vs. 9.1%), suggesting the static-dynamic disentanglement is more effective with trajectory-based matching on high-category-count datasets. On SSv2, MTT+Ours improves over MTT by 2.0 points (8.3% vs. 6.3% top-5), while DM+Ours slightly trails DM (3.8% vs. 4.1%). Overall, the method delivers the strongest gains when combined with MTT and FRePo, and the storage advantage is most pronounced with FRePo.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset distillation/condensation literature (DC, DM, MTT, FRePo), which has been developed almost exclusively for image datasets, to the video domain where an additional temporal dimension introduces fundamentally new compression challenges. The static-dynamic factorization is conceptually inspired by prior factorization methods (HaBa, LinBa) that decompose datasets into base and hallucinator components, but here the decomposition is along the temporal axis rather than the spatial or feature axis. The paper also introduces a 'segmented matching and interpolation' framework that unifies various temporal compression strategies under a single taxonomy, providing a systematic lens through which to analyze and compare video distillation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single still image per video can already capture most of the distillable knowledge (with <3% marginal gain from more frames) reframes how practitioners should think about video dataset compression, potentially enabling much cheaper distillation pipelines. The static-dynamic disentanglement paradigm is modular and can be plugged into existing distillation algorithms, making it broadly applicable. However, the method's advantage diminishes on larger, more diverse datasets (Kinetics-400), and the shared H network across all classes may limit scalability; whether the paradigm transfers to other temporal modalities (audio, point clouds over time) or to larger-scale video benchmarks remains open.
