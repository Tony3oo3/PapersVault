## Problem

Existing hand mesh reconstruction methods incorporate numerous complex components (stacked encoders, graph operators, progressive attention masks, topology-aware lifting) that increase system complexity and hinder real-time efficiency without clear evidence of their individual necessity. This paper asks: what are the minimal core structures actually required in a mesh decoder to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy, and can a streamlined architecture outperform complex SOTA methods while running faster?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240615/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces 778 3D mesh vertices, 21 3D hand joints, and 21 2D keypoints. The backbone (HRNet or FastViT-MA36) extracts features at H/32 × W/32 resolution, which are upsampled 4× to H/8 × W/8 for keypoint-guided point sampling. The 3D joints are derived from vertices via a fixed regression matrix (J × V_3d), and the 2D keypoints serve only to guide spatial sampling rather than as a primary output.

## Method

The architecture decomposes the mesh decoder into a token generator and a mesh regressor. The token generator predicts 21 2D keypoints and performs point sampling on a 4× upsampled feature map (28×28 resolution) to produce joint tokens. The mesh regressor is a 3-stage cascaded upsampler where each stage consists of a dimension-reduce MLP → MetaFormer block (attention mixer) → upsample MLP, progressively expanding tokens from 21 → 84 → 336 → 778 with feature dimensions 256 → 128 → 64. Learnable position embeddings are added at each stage to encode spatial relations without manual topology design. Trained end-to-end with weighted L1 losses (weights 10:1:10 for 3D joints, 2D joints, and vertices), the full decoder requires only 1.9M parameters—1 to 2 orders of magnitude fewer than competing transformer-based methods—while achieving 5.8 mm PA-MPJPE on FreiHAND at 33 fps (HRNet) or 70 fps (FastViT-MA36).

## Results

On the FreiHAND dataset, the proposed method achieves a PA-MPJPE of 5.8 mm and PA-MPVPE of 6.1 mm with HRNet (33 fps), and further improves to 5.7 mm / 6.0 mm with FastViT-MA36 (70 fps), outperforming all prior methods by at least 0.3 mm on PA-MPJPE. On DexYCB, it reaches PA-MPJPE 5.5 mm and PA-MPVPE 5.5 mm, while also leading on non-Procrustes-aligned metrics (MPJPE 12.4 mm, MPVPE 12.1 mm). The decoder requires only 1.9 M parameters, roughly 1–2 orders of magnitude fewer than transformer-based competitors (25 M–102 M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FreiHAND, the method beats the closest competitors—PointHMR (6.1 mm) and Deformer (6.2 mm PA-MPJPE)—by 0.3–0.4 mm, and surpasses MeshGraphormer (6.3 mm) and FastMETRO (6.5 mm) by 0.5–0.7 mm. On DexYCB, it edges out H2ONet (5.7 mm) and HandOccNet (5.8 mm) on PA-MPJPE by 0.2–0.3 mm, while pulling ahead by a much larger margin on non-Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (12.4 vs. 14.0 mm, a 1.6 mm gap) and MPVPE (12.1 vs. 13.0 mm). In terms of efficiency, the 70 fps FastViT-MA36 variant is competitive with real-time baselines like MobRecon (80 fps) and FastViT (84 fps) while delivering 0.9–1.1 mm lower PA-MPJPE. The decoder uses only 1.9 M parameters versus 25 M (FastMETRO), 98 M (MeshGraphormer), and 102 M (METRO), a 13×–54× reduction, without sacrificing accuracy. No baseline is beaten on raw FPS (FastViT at 84 fps and MobRecon at 80 fps are faster), but the accuracy gap at comparable speed is substantial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing the hand mesh reconstruction pipeline through a decomposition lens: it abstracts all existing mesh decoders into a token generator and a mesh regressor, then identifies minimal 'core structures' for each (keypoint-guided point sampling at 28×28 resolution, and three-stage cascaded upsampling with 4× token growth). Rather than building on a single prior method, it systematically strips away components borrowed from METRO, MeshGraphormer, FastMETRO, MobRecon, and PointHMR, showing that most of their added complexity is unnecessary. The MetaFormer architecture provides the lightweight token-mixing backbone, and the overall design philosophy departs from the field's trend of adding more sophisticated attention masks, graph convolutions, or multi-stage lifting strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.9M-parameter decoder outperforms 25–102M-parameter transformer decoders by 0.5–0.9 mm on PA-MPJPE while running at 33–70 fps, making high-quality hand mesh reconstruction feasible on resource-constrained hardware. The systematic ablations provide a clear design recipe that practitioners can adopt without trial-and-error. However, the paper acknowledges that failure cases involving self-occlusion and object occlusion remain unresolved, and the method is explicitly scoped to single-hand, in-distribution scenarios. An open question is whether the 'core structure' principle generalizes to other mesh reconstruction tasks (body, face) or to multi-hand and interaction-heavy settings.
