## Problem

Existing multimodal scene flow methods directly fuse RGB and LiDAR features in motion space, but the visual intrinsic heterogeneity between these modalities (low dynamic range in RGB, incomplete contours in LiDAR) creates a large modality gap that deteriorates motion features. The paper identifies that event cameras share homogeneous properties with both RGB and LiDAR in visual (luminance, structure) and motion (correlation) spaces, making them a natural bridge to mitigate this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240884/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an adjacent RGB image pair at timestamps t and t+Δt, the corresponding LiDAR point cloud pair, and the event stream between the two timestamps. The event stream is consumed in two forms: accumulated into intensity frames for luminance fusion and voxelized into T=10 temporal slices for motion correlation fusion. LiDAR points are projected into the event camera coordinate system using intrinsic parameters. The output is a 3D scene flow field for the LiDAR points and a 2D optical flow field for the RGB images, both estimated end-to-end.

## Method

VisMoFlow proposes a three-level hierarchical fusion that progressively improves scene flow from visual to motion space. First, visual luminance fusion combines event's relative luminance with RGB's absolute luminance in a shared luminance space, constrained by a spatiotemporal gradient consistency loss derived from the optical flow basic model, and trained with a GAN for natural appearance. Second, visual structure fusion uses a self-similarity clustering strategy (intra-modal structure neighbor followed by inter-modal bidirectional KNN fusion) to inject event's local boundaries into LiDAR's global shape, implemented via a cross-attention transformer trained with pseudo depth labels. Third, motion correlation fusion maps all three modalities into a common correlation subspace via warp operators, aligns their distributions with K-L divergence, and fuses spatial-dense (RGB), temporal-dense (event), and spatiotemporal-sparse (LiDAR) correlations before GRU decoding. Training proceeds in three sequential stages (luminance → structure → motion) with a composite loss of photometric, adversarial, consistency, pseudo-label, and KL-divergence terms.

## Results

On the synthetic Event-KITTI dataset, VisMoFlow achieves a scene flow EPE of 0.012 (day) and 0.027 (night) with 98.55% and 95.62% ACC, respectively, substantially outperforming the closest multimodal baseline RPEFlow (EPE 0.060/0.056, ACC 81.73%/81.47%). On the real DSEC dataset, VisMoFlow reaches EPE 0.084 (day) and 0.090 (night) with 70.34% and 68.31% ACC, again surpassing RPEFlow (EPE 0.103/0.094, ACC 60.81%/66.49%). For optical flow on Event-KITTI, VisMoFlow achieves EPE 0.198 (day) and 0.353 (night), dramatically beating FlowFormer (0.607/2.085) and SMURF (2.01/11.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisMoFlow outperforms all six baselines on both datasets and both day/night splits. Against the closest same-modality competitor RPEFlow (RGB+PC+EV), VisMoFlow reduces scene flow EPE by ~5× on Event-KITTI day (0.012 vs 0.060) and by ~2× on DSEC day (0.084 vs 0.103), while gaining 16.8 and 9.5 ACC points respectively. Against the RGB+LiDAR-only CamLiFlow, VisMoFlow achieves roughly 2–3× lower EPE on both datasets, confirming that the event modality and hierarchical visual fusion contribute beyond simple motion-space fusion. Unimodal baselines (RAFT-3D for RGB, PV-RAFT for LiDAR) are far behind, with RAFT-3D dropping to 5.04% ACC on DSEC night, highlighting the necessity of multimodal fusion. For optical flow, VisMoFlow (EPE 0.198 day, 0.353 night on Event-KITTI) dominates FlowFormer (0.607/2.085) and SMURF (2.01/11.36) by large margins, with the nighttime gap especially pronounced (3.4× and 32× lower EPE vs FlowFormer and SMURF).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAFT-3D recurrent scene flow architecture (Teed & Deng, CVPR 2021) and its PWC-Net-style warp-operator correlation computation (Sun et al., CVPR 2018) into a multimodal setting. It departs from prior RGB-LiDAR fusion methods (CamLiFlow, RPEFlow) that directly fuse cross-modal features in motion space, arguing that the visual intrinsic heterogeneity between RGB and LiDAR creates a modality gap that degrades motion features. The key conceptual departure is introducing the event camera as a 'bridge' modality and organizing fusion into three 'homogeneous spaces'—luminance (event+RGB), structure (event+LiDAR), and correlation (all three)—rather than performing a single direct fusion step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical visual-to-motion fusion framework provides a physically interpretable structure for multimodal scene flow that could generalize beyond this specific sensor combination. However, the method's complexity (three sequential training stages, five loss terms, multiple sub-networks) and its dependence on a synchronized event camera limit practical deployment. The authors acknowledge failure under adverse weather (rain, fog), and the Event-KITTI experiments rely on synthetic events generated by V2E, leaving open whether the luminance-fusion gains hold with real event data at scale. The 'homogeneous space' framing is a useful conceptual lens but remains validated only on two datasets.
