## Problem

Standard text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) fail to reliably generate all object categories specified in a prompt, especially when those categories rarely co-occur in natural scenes. The root cause is that the denoising objective treats text only as a conditioning signal without explicitly supervising the correspondence between individual tokens and their spatial locations in the image, leaving the model without fine-grained token-level understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241273/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a free-form text prompt and produces a single RGB image through the standard Stable Diffusion sampling pipeline with no additional inference-time cost. During training, each image-caption pair is augmented with automatically extracted binary segmentation masks (one per noun token) generated by Grounded SAM, which are used solely as supervision signals for the cross-attention layers and are not needed at test time.

## Method

TokenCompose augments the standard LDM denoising loss with two grounding objectives applied to cross-attention maps in the U-Net's middle block and decoder. The token-level loss (L_token) normalizes attention activations toward the segmented region of each noun token, encouraging the model to focus on the correct spatial area; the pixel-level loss (L_pixel) adds binary cross-entropy supervision to prevent over-aggregation into subregions. Segmentation masks are extracted automatically via Grounded SAM (Grounding DINO + Segment Anything) from training captions, eliminating the need for human annotation. The three losses are jointly optimized with a single U-Net, and the paper shows that both grounding terms are necessary: L_token alone causes unstable subregion activation, while L_pixel alone provides insufficient spatial guidance.

## Results

On the VISOR benchmark, TokenCompose achieves 52.15% Object Accuracy, surpassing the best inference-time baseline Attend-and-Excite (45.13%) and the frozen Stable Diffusion (29.86%). On the proposed MULTIGEN benchmark, the method leads on MG2–MG4 for both COCO (98.08/76.16/28.81%) and ADE20K (97.75/76.93/33.92%) instance categories, though it trails Attend-and-Excite on the hardest MG5-COCO setting (3.28% vs. 6.01%). Photorealism is preserved or improved (FID-C 20.19 vs. 20.88 for frozen SD), and inference latency remains at 7.56 s—comparable to the unmodified pipeline and roughly 3.4× faster than Attend-and-Excite (25.43 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenCompose outperforms all five baselines on Object Accuracy (52.15 vs. 45.13 for Attend-and-Excite, 43.59 for Layout Guidance, 29.86 for frozen SD) and on MG2–MG4 for both COCO and ADE20K categories. The closest competitor, Attend-and-Excite, edges out TokenCompose only on the most challenging MG5-COCO metric (6.01% vs. 3.28%), likely because the training distribution rarely contains five or more co-occurring categories. Layout Guidance Diffusion is competitive on MG2-ADE20K (96.05 vs. 97.75) but lags substantially on MG3–MG5 and requires user-specified bounding boxes at inference, adding 18.89 s latency. Composable Diffusion and Structured Diffusion are clearly outperformed across nearly all composition metrics, with Composable Diffusion also degrading photorealism (FID-F 75.57 vs. 71.13). Critically, TokenCompose matches the inference speed of the unmodified Stable Diffusion pipeline (7.56 s vs. 7.54 s), whereas inference-time methods like Attend-and-Excite and Layout Guidance impose 3–4× latency overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Latent Diffusion Model framework by adding token-level and pixel-level cross-attention supervision during fine-tuning, drawing segmentation ground truth from vision understanding models (Grounding DINO + SAM). It departs from the dominant line of compositional generation work, which either modifies inference (Composable Diffusion, Layout Guidance, Attend-and-Excite, Structured Diffusion) or adds auxiliary modules (ControlNet), by instead providing dense, interpretable training signals that require no architectural changes or inference-time overhead. The use of foundation understanding models to auto-generate supervision for a generative model also connects to the broader trend of leveraging cross-task knowledge transfer, though the specific application of segmentation grounding to improve text-to-image composition is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple training-time addition (two loss terms on cross-attention maps) can substantially improve multi-category instance composition without any inference cost, offering a practical alternative to the 3×+ latency penalty of inference-time methods. The MULTIGEN benchmark fills a gap in evaluation by testing composition of 2–5 categories rather than the pairwise focus of prior benchmarks. However, the approach is limited to noun tokens, the training set is small (~4.5K pairs), and it remains unclear whether the gains transfer to more complex compositional prompts involving adjectives, spatial relations, or actions. Open questions include whether scaling the supervision to richer token types and larger datasets yields further improvements, and whether the technique generalizes to newer architectures like diffusion transformers.
