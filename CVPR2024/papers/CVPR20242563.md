## Problem

3D shape matching and interpolation are highly interrelated yet typically studied in isolation, with functional map methods producing locally unsmooth correspondences and spatial registration methods relying on expensive iterative optimisation. No prior unsupervised framework jointly learns accurate point-wise correspondences and realistic interpolation trajectories that capture both pose-dominant and shape-dominant deformations without shape-specific priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242563/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D triangle meshes with potentially different vertex counts and non-isometric deformations as input, requiring no ground-truth correspondences or shape-class priors. It outputs a soft point-wise correspondence matrix (via softmax over feature similarity) and a continuous deformation field parameterised by t∈[0,1] that deforms one mesh into the other. At test time, an additional shape-dominant deformation field is optimised per shape pair, and final correspondences are refined via nearest-neighbour search in the spatial domain.

## Method

The framework fuses spectral and spatial maps in a single unsupervised pipeline: a Siamese DiffusionNet extracts per-vertex features used to compute both bidirectional functional maps (spectral domain) and soft point-wise maps (spatial domain via row-wise softmax). Training is driven by a spectral loss combining structural regularisation (bijectivity, orthogonality) with a coupling loss that enforces consistency between the functional map and the point-wise map, plus a spatial loss comprising alignment, ARAP deformation energy, symmetry, and a temporal shape variance term. A novel test-time adaptation step optimises an additional shape-dominant deformation field between X(t) and Y(1−t) using Chamfer distance and Dirichlet energy with the VectorAdam optimiser, then linearly interpolates to produce the final trajectory. This two-stage design (pose-dominant via ARAP during training, shape-dominant via test-time optimisation) is the first to capture both deformation types without volume-preservation or other shape-specific assumptions.

## Results

On near-isometric benchmarks, the method achieves mean geodesic errors of 1.4 (FAUST), 1.8 (SCAPE), and 3.2 (SHREC'19), outperforming the previous best unsupervised method URSSM (1.6, 1.9, 4.6). On non-isometric matching, it achieves 1.9 on SMAL and 3.3 on DT4D-H inter-class, substantially beating the prior best unsupervised method URSSM (3.9, 4.1) and even supervised GeomFMaps (8.4, 4.1). For shape interpolation on FAUST and MANO, the method achieves state-of-the-art conformal distortion and Chamfer distance, outperforming even baselines that require ground-truth correspondences (LIMP, Hamiltonian). On the LUNA16 medical SSM task, it improves generalisation from 2.92 to 2.44 mm and specificity from 6.23 to 5.03 mm over S3M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 15 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the strongest unsupervised shape matching baselines, it improves geodesic error by 2.0× on SMAL (1.9 vs. URSSM's 3.9) and by 2.4× on DT4D-H inter-class (3.3 vs. URSSM's 4.1), while also beating AttnFMaps (5.4, 11.6) and DUO-FMNet (6.7, 15.8) by wide margins. It surpasses the best supervised method GeomFMaps (8.4 on SMAL, 4.1 on DT4D-H inter) by 4.4× and 1.2× respectively, demonstrating that the unsupervised spectral-spatial fusion is more effective than supervised spectral-only approaches under large non-isometry. For near-isometric matching (Table 2), it beats URSSM by 12–30% relative across FAUST, SCAPE, and SHREC'19, and shows superior cross-dataset generalisation (3.2 on SHREC'19 vs. 4.6 for URSSM). In shape interpolation, it outperforms NeuroMorph (the closest unsupervised joint method) and even LIMP and Hamiltonian, which rely on ground-truth correspondences, on both conformal distortion and Chamfer distance metrics. For the medical SSM task, it improves both generalisation (2.44 vs. 2.92 mm) and specificity (5.03 vs. 6.23 mm) over S3M, the most recent statistical shape modeling baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: the deep functional map framework (Roufosse et al., Ovsjanikov et al.) that encodes correspondences in the spectral domain, and spatial deformation models (ARAP, non-rigid registration) that operate directly on vertex positions. It departs from NeuroMorph (Eisenberger et al., CVPR 2011) — the closest prior joint matching+interpolation method — by replacing geodesic distance preservation (computationally expensive and restricted to near-isometry) with spectral regularisation, and by introducing a test-time adaptation step to capture shape-dominant deformations that ARAP alone cannot model. The Siamese DiffusionNet feature extractor and EdgeConv-based interpolator follow established architectural choices from URSSM and NeuroMorph respectively, so the novelty lies in the loss design and the spectral-spatial fusion rather than in new network components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first unsupervised method to jointly produce accurate correspondences and realistic interpolation trajectories without isometry assumptions, this work removes a practical bottleneck for statistical shape modelling and shape generation pipelines that previously required either ground-truth correspondences or near-isometric shape pairs. The demonstrated application to real-world lung CT data (LUNA16) with improved SSM generalisation and specificity over S3M suggests direct utility in medical imaging. The authors acknowledge that the pose/shape separation is imperfect — the interpolation module can slightly alter shape — and identify end-to-end statistical shape analysis and class-specific priors for cleaner disentanglement as open directions.
