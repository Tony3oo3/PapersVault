## Problem

Existing histopathology multi-modal models analyze isolated image patches without spatial grounding of morphological concepts within each patch and without access to the broader whole-slide-image (WSI) context that pathologists use to compound evidence across regions. Current instruction-tuning datasets (e.g., LLaVA-Med from PubMed) lack visually grounded captions and WSI-level diagnostic context, preventing models from reasoning beyond a single patch to guide next diagnostic steps.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a histopathology image patch (a crop from a gigapixel WSI), a natural-language question, and optionally a visual prompt in the form of an ellipse marking a region of interest. Outputs include spatially grounded descriptions with normalized bounding boxes [x1,y1,x2,y2] localizing specific morphological concepts, free-form diagnostic reasoning that references evidence beyond the current patch, and open/closed-set VQA answers. The spatial grounding is derived automatically from narrator cursor trajectories in educational videos rather than manual annotation.

## Method

The pipeline extracts mouse cursor positions from 4,149 educational YouTube videos by detecting stable background chunks via frame differencing with SSIM verification, then subtracting a median background frame to isolate cursor pixels. Spatio-temporal clustering (with exponential temporal decay λ=0.05) maps cursor traces to bounding boxes aligned with narrated words, producing 132K grounded image-caption pairs. From these, GPT-4 generates QUILT-INSTRUCT (107,131 QA pairs) using two prompt families: Independent prompts (conversations, detailed descriptions) constrained to single-patch context, and Reasoning-based prompts (Complex Medical Reasoning, Iterative Abductive Reasoning) that inject WSI-level diagnosis and supporting facts to enable cross-patch diagnostic reasoning while reducing hallucination. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: (1) domain alignment on 723K QUILT image-text pairs with only the MLP projector trained (vision encoder and LLM frozen), using a CLIP encoder pretrained on histopathology (QUILTNET/PLIP); (2) instruction-tuning on QUILT-INSTRUCT with the MLP and 7B LLM (Vicuna or LLaVA checkpoint) jointly fine-tuned while the vision encoder remains frozen.

## Results

On the GPT-4 relative alignment score (instruction-following), QUILT-LLAVA@107K achieves 75.6 overall, outperforming LLaVA-Med (64.8) by 10.8 points and LLaVA (55.7) by 19.9 points across 14 sub-pathologies. On VQA benchmarks, the best QUILT-LLAVA variant (LLaVA checkpoint, 107K instruct, Stage1:1, Stage2:1) achieves 71.30 average open-set recall and 75.00 average closed-set accuracy, surpassing LLaVA-Med (69.44 / 64.81) and LLaVA (51.85 / 60.19). The QUILTNET ViT-B-32 variant achieves 68.52 / 70.37 on the same metrics. On QUILT-VQA specifically, QUILT-LLAVA reaches 64.43% closed-set accuracy (QUILTNET) and 68.81% (LLaVA checkpoint) versus 61.22% for LLaVA-Med and 57.73% for LLaVA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUILT-LLAVA is compared against two baselines: LLaVA (general-domain, 7B) and LLaVA-Med (medical-domain, 7B). On the GPT-4 relative alignment score, QUILT-LLAVA@107K (75.6) outperforms LLaVA-Med (64.8) by 10.8 points and LLaVA (55.7) by 19.9 points. On closed-set VQA, the LLaVA-checkpoint QUILT-LLAVA variant achieves 75.00 average accuracy, beating LLaVA-Med (64.81) by ~10 points and LLaVA (60.19) by ~15 points; the QUILTNET variant also leads with 70.37. On open-set VQA, the LLaVA-checkpoint variant leads with 71.30 average recall (vs. 69.44 for LLaVA-Med), while the QUILTNET variant (68.52) slightly trails LLaVA-Med on this metric but substantially outperforms LLaVA (51.85). Notably, LLaVA-Med scores only 1.34% on PMC-VQA-Subset closed-set (vs. 28.56–33.91 for QUILT-LLAVA variants), suggesting it struggles with multi-choice instruction following. The paper attributes the open-set advantage of the QUILTNET variant to its domain-specific CLIP encoder (QUILTNET/PLIP), while the LLaVA-checkpoint variant's closed-set strength stems from better instruction-following for multi-choice formats.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA instruction-tuning paradigm to histopathology by building on QUILT-1M's video-derived image-text pairs and adding spatial grounding through automatic cursor tracking from educational YouTube videos. It departs from prior medical VQA work (LLaVA-Med, PMC-VQA) that relies on PubMed image-caption pairs lacking spatial annotations and cross-patch context, instead leveraging the sequential narrative structure of whole-slide video walkthroughs. The novel 'subconscious knowledge injection' prompting strategy—constraining GPT-4's reasoning with WSI-level diagnoses and supporting facts without explicitly revealing them—represents a conceptual departure from standard single-image instruction generation, enabling the model to reason beyond individual patches toward a global diagnosis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental gap in histopathology AI: the inability of current models to reason across patches within a whole-slide image, which is how pathologists actually form diagnoses. The QUILT-INSTRUCT dataset (107K pairs) and QUILT-VQA benchmark (1,283 organic Q/A pairs) provide much-needed resources for the community, while the cursor-tracking pipeline offers a low-cost, generalizable method for spatially grounding medical video content. Key limitations include residual GPT-4 hallucination in generated data, noise from video extraction, and the fact that evaluation remains on single patches rather than full WSI navigation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-patch interactive diagnosis and whether pathologist-in-the-loop validation would confirm the model's clinical utility.
