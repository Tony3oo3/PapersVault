## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inpainting methods (e.g., IR-SDE, RePaint) produce semantically meaningful content in masked regions but suffer from a persistent semantic discrepancy with unmasked regions, because the dense unmasked texture is only partially degraded while masked regions become pure noise during the forward diffusion process. The paper asks how unmasked semantics should guide the reverse denoising process and how to reconcile the tension between consistency (favored by sparse structure) and meaningful semantics (favored by dense texture).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241317/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB masked image and a binary mask (0 for masked, 1 for unmasked) as input, using irregular hole masks. The final inpainted image is constructed by merging the masked-region pixels from the denoised output with the original unmasked pixels, so the network only needs to generate plausible content within the masked area. Experiments are conducted on Paris StreetView, CelebA, and Places2 with mask ratios ranging from 10% to 50%.

## Method

StrDiffusion introduces a progressively sparse auxiliary structure (grayscale map at t=0 degrading to edge map plus noise at t=T) that guides the texture denoising process, reformulating the optimal reverse state into a simplified objective with four interpretable terms: a consistency term from the structure, a balance term, a semantics term from the texture, and the unmasked regions. A pre-trained structure denoising network produces the denoised structure x_{t-1}, which conditions the texture noise-prediction U-Net via multi-scale spatially adaptive normalization (SPADE-style) at multiple layers. An adaptive resampling strategy, governed by a discriminator trained with a combined discriminator and triplet loss, monitors the semantic correlation between the denoised texture and structure at each timestep and triggers iterative re-denoising of the structure when the correlation score falls below a threshold, ensuring the structure remains a competent guide throughout the denoising trajectory.

## Results

On Places2 with irregular masks, StrDiffusion achieves the best PSNR (29.45/26.28/24.43/22.37 dB), SSIM (0.973/0.941/0.917/0.874), and FID (8.75/16.16/23.72/36.93) across all four mask-ratio ranges (10–20%, 20–30%, 30–40%, 40–50%). The most notable gain is in FID, where StrDiffusion reduces the score by up to ~20% relative to the closest diffusion-based baselines (RePaint* and IR-SDE), e.g., 8.75 vs. 11.16 at 10–20% mask. PSNR and SSIM improvements over the strongest baselines are more modest (≤1.2 dB and ≤0.006 respectively) but consistent across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StrDiffusion outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric across all four mask-ratio ranges. Against the two closest diffusion-based competitors, RePaint* and IR-SDE, the gains are most pronounced in FID (e.g., 8.75 vs. 11.16 for RePaint* and 12.03 for IR-SDE at 10–20% mask; 16.16 vs. 20.62 and 21.34 at 20–30%), while PSNR and SSIM margins are smaller but consistent (≤0.27 dB and ≤0.006). Among CNN/attention-based methods, CMT (ICCV'23) is the strongest PSNR/SSIM competitor (29.28/0.960 at 10–20%), but StrDiffusion leads by 0.17 dB PSNR and 0.013 SSIM; CMT does not report FID. Structure-guided baselines (MEDFE, CTSDG, ZITS) trail StrDiffusion by 1–3 dB PSNR and 2–10 FID points, which the authors attribute to their lack of explicit structure–texture semantic correlation modeling. The paper also notes that RePaint* and IR-SDE still exhibit visible semantic discrepancy (e.g., color/texture mismatch at mask boundaries) that StrDiffusion largely eliminates, as shown in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 7).

## Relation to Prior Work

StrDiffusion extends the mean-reverting SDE formulation of IR-SDE (Luo et al., ICML 2023) for diffusion-based inpainting by introducing a structure-guided denoising objective, where a progressively sparse auxiliary structure (grayscale-to-edge) conditions the texture denoising network via spatially adaptive normalization (SPADE, Park et al., CVPR 2019). It departs from prior structure-guided inpainting methods (MEDFE, CTSDG, ZITS) that treat structure as a fixed condition, instead exploiting the time-dependent sparsity of structure to provide stage-appropriate guidance: sparse structure enforces consistency early, while dense texture generates semantics late. The adaptive resampling strategy with a discriminator-based correlation measure also distinguishes it from RePaint's unconditional resampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode of diffusion inpainting (semantic discrepancy between masked and unmasked regions) with a principled reformulation of the denoising objective, offering a clear design principle: structure sparsity should increase over time to match the growing discrepancy. Limitations include evaluation only at 256×256 resolution with irregular masks, reliance on a pre-trained structure denoising network plus a discriminator (adding training complexity), and no exploration of whether the approach transfers to video inpainting or larger-scale generation. Open questions include whether the progressive sparsity schedule generalizes to other diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based) and whether the discriminator-based resampling can be replaced by lighter-weight correlation measures.
