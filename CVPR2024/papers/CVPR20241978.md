## Problem

Fully supervised 3D indoor object detection requires expensive point-level or bounding-box annotations, and existing weakly-supervised approaches that augment training with synthetic scenes are limited by the fixed, low-diversity shape libraries of standard datasets like ModelNet40. This paper addresses the gap by leveraging text-to-3D generative models to produce a virtually unbounded, category-diverse 3D shape repository for synthetic scene construction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241978/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes indoor point clouds (ScanNet) with only position-level annotations—object center coordinates and class tags for 22 categories—plus a set of randomly generated text prompts per category. It produces axis-aligned 3D bounding boxes with semantic class labels. Unusually, the real-scene loss omits full bounding-box regression (only center and class are supervised), while the synthetic scenes provide complete point-level annotations that supply the missing geometric supervision during training.

## Method

Prompt3D comprises two modules. The Synthetic Scene Generation (SSG) module uses ChatGPT to produce diverse text prompts per category, converts them to 3D meshes via Shap-E, and assembles virtual scenes that replicate real-scene layouts by matching shapes via minimum-enclosing-rectangle aspect ratios, then applying gravity and collision constraints; full bounding-box annotations are automatically derived from the known mesh geometry. The Prototypical Proposal Feature Alignment (PPFA) module bridges the sim-to-real gap by computing class-specific prototype features (mean of masked proposal features per class) in both domains and minimizing their L1 distance, using an online running-average estimator to avoid accumulating all batch features. This prototype-level alignment replaces the adversarial feature alignment used in prior work (BR), targeting category-specific feature distributions rather than global scene-level features, which the authors argue reduces scene-level bias and improves transfer robustness.

## Results

On the ScanNet validation set (22 indoor categories, mAP@0.25), Prompt3D achieves 37.4% with VoteNet and 51.2% with GroupFree3D as the detector. With VoteNet, this narrows the gap to the fully supervised baseline (45.1%) to just 7.7 percentage points; with GroupFree3D, the gap shrinks to only 3.0 points (51.2% vs. 54.2%). Compared to the weakly supervised baseline (WSB), Prompt3D improves by over 23 percentage points in both detector settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against BR (Xu et al., CVPR 2022), the state-of-the-art weakly supervised 3D detection method that also uses synthetic scenes from ModelNet40 for label enhancement. Prompt3D outperforms BR's mesh version (BRM) by 1.9 mAP@0.25 points with VoteNet (37.4 vs. 35.5) and by 4.1 points with GroupFree3D (51.2 vs. 47.1). Against BR's point-cloud version (BRP), the margins are larger: 6.1 points (VoteNet) and 8.2 points (GroupFree3D). Prompt3D also surpasses the simpler WSBP baseline (pre-training on ModelNet40 scenes without domain adaptation) by 1.3 and 9.7 points respectively, confirming that both the prompt-generated shape diversity and the PPFA alignment module contribute to the gains. Notably, with GroupFree3D, Prompt3D even exceeds the fully supervised baseline on individual categories such as door, laptop, stool, and toilet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly supervised 3D object detection line initiated by BR (Xu et al., CVPR 2022), which augmented position-level annotations with synthetic scenes built from ModelNet40 shapes and aligned features via adversarial domain adaptation. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing the fixed ModelNet40 shape library with a scalable, prompt-generated 3D shape repository produced by Shap-E, and (2) substituting adversarial feature alignment (AFA) with a class-prototype-based proposal feature alignment (PPFA) that operates at the per-class level rather than the global distribution level. The method retains the overall synthetic-scene-augmentation paradigm but re-grounds both the data generation and the transfer mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the weak-to-fully-supervised gap to 3% mAP@0.25 with GroupFree3D, the paper demonstrates that text-to-3D generative models can serve as a practical data engine for 3D detection, reducing reliance on labor-intensive bounding-box annotations. However, the approach is validated only on the indoor ScanNet benchmark with 22 categories, and its dependence on Shap-E's generation quality and ChatGPT for prompt creation introduces practical fragility. Open questions include whether the prompt-based shape diversity advantage persists in outdoor or large-scale multi-category settings, and whether the PPFA alignment generalizes when the synthetic-real domain gap is larger (e.g., different sensor modalities).
