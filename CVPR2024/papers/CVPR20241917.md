## Problem

Existing federated continual learning (FCL) methods assume that each incoming task is entirely new and distinct from previous ones, yet in real-world streaming scenarios tasks frequently repeat across clients. When repetitive tasks appear, standard FCL approaches treat them as novel tasks, incurring unnecessary learning loss and suffering significant accuracy degradation because previously learned features are neither traced nor augmented. This paper introduces the Traceable Federated Continual Learning (TFCL) paradigm to explicitly handle task repeatability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241917/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images in a streaming class-incremental sequence (e.g., CIFAR-100 at 32×32, TinyImageNet at 64×64), where the task label sequence may contain repetitions of previously seen class groups. Rather than exchanging full model weights, clients upload feature maps (extracted after the second BasicBlock of ResNet-18) and logits to the server, which returns aggregated prediction vectors for client-side distillation. The final output is per-task class predictions, with the server maintaining multiple group-specific models whose knowledge is selectively fed back to corresponding clients.

## Method

TagFed decomposes the shared ResNet-18 backbone into a series of marked sub-models via iterative pruning, where each new task is assigned only unoccupied neurons and its weights are frozen upon completion. When a repetitive task is detected by matching label distributions, the system traces back to the corresponding marked sub-model, saves a weight copy to protect other tasks, and retrains only the relevant binary mask and pruned weights to augment features without introducing new parameters. For federation, instead of infeasible weight averaging over frozen sub-models, the server constructs task-similarity groups (clustered by logits) and performs group-wise knowledge distillation: clients upload feature maps and logits, server models within each group minimize a combined cross-entropy and KL-divergence loss, and the aggregated predictions are sent back for client-side distillation. This design reduces the effective number of new tasks (since repetitions are not treated as novel), handles cross-client heterogeneity through selective grouping, and cuts communication cost by roughly 50% since only small prediction vectors are offloaded.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 5 tasks, TagFed achieves 75.99% average accuracy (without noise) and 74.10% with privacy noise (1/dFIL=0.5), substantially outperforming the best baseline GLFC (62.02%) by over 12 percentage points. Across 2–10 tasks on CIFAR-100, TagFed reaches 75.73% average accuracy in the ablation setting. In terms of efficiency, TagFed requires only 10.30 MB upload and 0.05 MB offload per round with 15 federating rounds, saving roughly 50% communication cost and ~50–65% computation rounds compared to baselines that need 32–45 rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TagFed outperforms all five baselines by a large margin across all datasets and task scales. On CIFAR-100 with 5 tasks, TagFed (75.99% without noise) beats GLFC (62.02%) by ~14 points, FedKNOW (31.87%) by ~44 points, PackNet+FedAvg (31.66%) by ~44 points, and FedWeIT (18.86%) by ~57 points. GLFC is the strongest baseline but still degrades significantly under repetitive tasks, while FedWeIT and FedKNOW suffer severe overfitting on smaller datasets like CIFAR-100. In terms of efficiency, TagFed's offloading cost is only 0.05 MB versus 11.70–26.20 MB for all baselines, and it converges in 15 rounds compared to 21–45 rounds for others, yielding roughly 50% total communication savings. The advantage is consistent on TinyImageNet and ImageNetSubset (shown in Figures 4 and 5), and TagFed remains robust to varying repeatability degrees and non-IID conditions where baselines degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated continual learning (FCL) line of work by introducing task repeatability as a first-class concern, departing from prior FCL methods (GLFC, FedWeIT, FedKNOW) that assume each incoming task is novel. Methodologically, it builds on PackNet's iterative pruning to generate frozen sub-models per task, but adds a traceability mechanism (marked neurons, weight copies) that allows selective retraining when a previously seen task reappears. On the federation side, it replaces standard weight averaging (FedAvg) with group-wise knowledge distillation, motivated by the fact that frozen sub-model weights cannot be directly averaged across clients with heterogeneous task histories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical gap—real-world data streams do contain recurring tasks—and shows that existing FCL methods degrade noticeably under this setting, which is a useful diagnostic for practitioners deploying FCL in production. However, the evaluation relies entirely on a manually constructed benchmark with simulated repeatability, a single backbone (ResNet-18), and at most 30 clients, leaving open whether the gains transfer to more complex architectures, larger client populations, or genuinely non-identical (similar-but-different) recurring tasks. The ~50% communication reduction is attractive but is measured against baselines that upload full model weights, so the practical savings depend on deployment context.
