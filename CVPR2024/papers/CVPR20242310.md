## Problem

Existing egocentric motion capture methods estimate only body pose from a single head-mounted fisheye camera, ignoring hand motion entirely. The task of simultaneous body-and-hand estimation is further complicated by severe fisheye distortion, frequent self-occlusion of feet and hands from the egocentric viewpoint, and the absence of large-scale datasets with ground-truth annotations for both body and hand poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242310/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of 256×256 egocentric fisheye images from a single head-mounted camera and produces per-frame 3D body joint positions, 3D hand joint positions, and SMPL-X parameters via inverse kinematics. The pose regressor operates in uvd space (fisheye image u,v coordinates plus depth d) rather than world xyz, so the 3D heatmap voxels are pixel-aligned with the input image and the final 3D joints are recovered through the fisheye reprojection function. Temporal refinement is applied over the full sequence to produce temporally coherent whole-body motion.

## Method

FisheyeViT partitions the fisheye image into N×N undistorted patches by warping to a unit sphere and applying gnomonic projection on tangent planes, then feeds these patches as tokens to a standard ViT, avoiding impractical full-image undistortion. A pixel-aligned 3D heatmap regressor uses deconvolutional layers to produce heatmaps in uvd space where each voxel maps directly to an image pixel, followed by differentiable soft-argmax and fisheye reprojection to yield 3D body joints and per-joint uncertainty. Hand poses are obtained by HRNet-based detection, Hand4Whole-based 3D regression on undistorted crops, and integration via rotation matrices on tangent planes. For temporal refinement, a whole-body motion diffusion model (DDPM with an EDGE-style transformer denoiser) is trained on diverse motion sequences; at inference, a classifier-guided sampling strategy uses the per-joint uncertainty to selectively preserve low-uncertainty joints while generating high-uncertainty joints conditioned on the reliable ones, mitigating self-occlusion errors without requiring retraining on egocentric-specific conditions.

## Results

On the SceneEgo test dataset, the single-frame method achieves 64.19 mm MPJPE and 50.06 mm PA-MPJPE for body pose, while the diffusion-refined version further improves to 57.59 mm MPJPE and 46.55 mm PA-MPJPE. On the EgoWholeBody test dataset, the refined method reaches 60.32 mm MPJPE and 40.35 mm PA-MPJPE. For hand pose on SceneEgo, the refined method achieves 19.37 mm MPJPE and 9.05 mm PA-MPJPE, substantially outperforming the Hand4Whole baseline (49.66 mm MPJPE, 13.85 mm PA-MPJPE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method outperforms all six baselines across both body and hand pose metrics. On SceneEgo, the refined method (57.59 mm MPJPE) beats the best retrained baseline EgoPW* (90.96 mm) by 33.4 mm and SceneEgo* (89.06 mm) by 31.5 mm in MPJPE. For hand pose on SceneEgo, the refined method (19.37 mm MPJPE) more than halves the error of Hand4Whole (49.66 mm). The ablation in Table 3 further shows that replacing the pixel-aligned 3D heatmap head with the Mo²Cap², xR-EgoPose, or SceneEgo heads degrades body MPJPE to 87.47, 116.5, and 77.73 mm respectively, confirming the superiority of the proposed regressor. Comparisons with GlobalEgoMocap [50] and Mo²Cap² [54] on their respective test sets are reported in the supplementary materials, where the paper's method also achieves lower MPJPE. Notably, retraining all previous methods on the EgoWholeBody dataset yields significant improvements (e.g., EgoPW drops from 189.6 to 90.96 mm), yet none close the gap to the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: egocentric 3D body pose estimation (Mo²Cap², xR-EgoPose, EgoPW, SceneEgo), external-view whole-body pose estimation (SMPLer-X, ExPose, OSX), and diffusion-based motion generation (EgoHMR, AGROL, ZeDO). It departs from prior egocentric methods by extending them to simultaneously capture hands, and from external-view whole-body methods by operating under severe fisheye distortion and self-occlusion. The FisheyeViT module repurposes the gnomonic projection to create undistorted patches compatible with standard ViT tokenization, avoiding the impractical full-image undistortion. The uncertainty-aware diffusion refinement differs from prior conditioned-diffusion pose estimators (which require training on specific condition formats) by using per-joint uncertainty as a zero-shot guidance signal, and from body-only diffusion models by learning a joint body-hand motion prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EgoWholeBody dataset (840k+ frames) fills a critical gap in training data for egocentric whole-body capture and is likely to become a standard benchmark for this emerging task. The FisheyeViT approach is generalizable beyond pose estimation to any vision task involving fisheye cameras, and the pixel-aligned 3D heatmap in uvd space offers a simpler, more accurate alternative to V2V-based regressors. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real gap limits deployment on actual head-mounted devices, whether the diffusion refinement's computational cost is acceptable for real-time VR/AR applications, and how the method extends to facial expressions and multi-person scenarios.
