## Problem

High-order ODE solvers for diffusion models (e.g., UniPC, DPM-Solver++) can generate quality images in as few as 5–10 NFEs, but they still rely on fixed discretization schemes (uniform-t, uniform-λ, EDM) that are suboptimal at low step counts. No principled, solver-aware optimization framework existed for choosing the best time step schedule given a specific numerical ODE solver.

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-trained diffusion model (score or data-prediction network), a target number of NFEs (tested at 5–15), and the specification of an explicit ODE solver (its local polynomial structure and per-step orders k_n). It outputs a set of optimized λ-values (equivalently, time steps) that are pre-computed once in under 15 seconds on CPU and then reused for all subsequent sampling calls. The optimized schedule is solver-specific and dataset-agnostic, so it can be transferred across different pre-trained models using the same solver.

## Method

The paper reparameterizes the diffusion reverse ODE in log-SNR space (λ = log(α_t/σ_t)) and expresses the numerical solution as a weighted sum of neural network evaluations, where the weights w_{n;k_n,j} are integrals of e^λ times local polynomial basis functions determined by the solver. Under a bounded score-approximation error assumption, Theorem 1 derives an upper bound on ‖x̃_ε − x_0‖ that decomposes into a fixed term plus a sum over time steps of ε̃_{t_i}·|Σ w_{n;k_n,j}|. Minimizing this bound yields a constrained optimization problem (Eq. 35) over the interior λ-steps with monotonicity constraints, which is solved efficiently via the constrained trust region method. The framework is general: it accommodates any explicit ODE solver expressible through local polynomials of the form (10), allows per-step order variation (e.g., k_n=1,2,3 for a third-order method), and requires no retraining of the diffusion model.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 32×32 with UniPC, the optimized time steps reduce FID from 23.22 (uniform-λ) to 12.11 at 5 NFEs, and from 3.87 to 3.50 at 10 NFEs. On ImageNet 64×64 (ADM), the method achieves FID 10.47 at 5 NFEs, outperforming all baseline schedules. On ImageNet 256×256 (DiT-XL-2), FID improves from 23.48 to 8.66 at 5 NFEs, and on ImageNet 512×512 from 20.28 to 11.40. The optimization itself takes less than 15 seconds (1.9 s at 5 NFEs, 14.1 s at 15 NFEs) on a single CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The optimized schedule dominates all three baseline discretization schemes (uniform-t, uniform-λ, and EDM) across every dataset and NFE count tested. At 5 NFEs on CIFAR-10 with UniPC, the gap is dramatic: FID 12.11 vs. 23.22 (uniform-λ), 25.11 (uniform-t), and 38.24 (EDM) — roughly a 2× improvement over the best baseline. The advantage is most pronounced at low NFEs (5–8) where uniform schedules are far from optimal; at 15 NFEs the gap narrows to ~0.02–0.04 FID over uniform-λ, as expected since more steps reduce discretization sensitivity. On ImageNet 256×256 (DiT-XL-2), the optimized schedule cuts FID from 23.48 (uniform-λ) to 8.66 at 5 NFEs, a 2.7× reduction. The method is solver-agnostic: it improves both UniPC and DPM-Solver++ consistently, though UniPC remains the stronger solver regardless of schedule. The optimization cost is negligible (<15 s on CPU) compared to learning-to-schedule methods [27,30,48,50,51] that require several GPU hours for comparable performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the high-order ODE solver lineage (DPM-Solver [31], DPM-Solver++ [32], UniPC [55]) by treating the time-step discretization as an optimization variable rather than a fixed heuristic. It departs from prior schedule-search methods (dynamic programming [50], reinforcement learning [48], evolutionary search [27], timestep aligners [51]) by deriving a theoretical error upper bound (Theorem 1) from score-approximation assumptions and minimizing it via constrained trust region optimization, yielding a solver-agnostic, training-free procedure that runs in under 15 seconds. The framework is general in that it accommodates any explicit ODE solver expressible through local polynomial approximations, including varying local orders per step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a plug-and-play, near-zero-cost improvement to any pre-trained diffusion model's sampling pipeline, which is practically significant given the widespread deployment of DPMs. The theoretical grounding (Theorem 1) provides a principled surrogate objective rather than a black-box search, though the bound relies on a score-approximation assumption that may not be tight in practice. The authors note the optimization targets an upper bound rather than the true error, leaving open whether a more accurate formulation could yield further gains. The technique is orthogonal to training-based acceleration (distillation, consistency models), suggesting it can be stacked with those approaches for additional speedup.
