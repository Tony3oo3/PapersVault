## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from modality bias, over-relying on language priors or visual details while ignoring the other modality, leading to incorrect or irrelevant responses. Existing debiasing approaches require large-scale supervised fine-tuning that risks eroding the model's existing knowledge, and no preference-optimization dataset or noise-robust algorithm specifically targets modality bias in MLLMs. The paper addresses both gaps: constructing a debiased preference dataset and designing an optimization algorithm robust to the inherent noise in automatically generated biased responses.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images paired with text instructions (e.g., VQA-style questions) fed into LLaVA-v1.5. To generate biased negative samples, the model is queried with one modality masked by [MASK] tokens—either all visual tokens (producing language-biased responses) or all textual tokens (producing vision-biased responses). The final training sample is a triple (x, y_w, y_l) where y_l can be the original rejected response, a language-biased response, or a vision-biased response, and the model outputs a single text response at inference.

## Method

The paper introduces RLAIF-V-Bias, a preference dataset built by masking one modality in RLAIF-V queries to elicit biased responses that serve as negative samples alongside the original data. To handle noise in these automatically constructed samples, NaPO replaces DPO's BCE loss with a negative Box-Cox transformation that interpolates between BCE (q→0, fast convergence) and MAE (q→1, noise-robust), yielding a loss bounded between the two. The noise-robustness coefficient q is computed dynamically per sample as q = 1 − σ(αψ), where ψ is the reward margin (using average logP for language-biased data and sum logP for vision-biased data), so noisier samples with smaller margins receive stronger noise robustness. A margin-based dynamic weight γ_i further balances the DPO loss on original data against NaPO losses on language-biased and vision-biased data within each batch, adapting the optimization emphasis to data quality.

## Results

On VLind-Bench, the proposed method (Lγ with RLAIF-V-Bias) achieves CB=58.9 and LP=44.0 on LLaVA-v1.5-7B, representing absolute improvements of +19.5 and +18.6 over DPO with RLAIF-V (CB=39.4, LP=25.4). On Object HalBench, it reduces CHAIRs from 53.6 (base) to 25.7 and CHAIRi from 25.2 to 6.2. On MMHalBench, the method achieves a quality score of 3.31 with a hallucination rate of 0.35, outperforming both DPO (3.23, 0.38) and MDPO (3.28, 0.42). The 13B variant similarly improves CB from 31.5 to 42.1 and LP from 20.9 to 25.1 over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms DPO with RLAIF-V by +19.5 on CB and +18.6 on LP (VLind-Bench), while also reducing Object HalBench CHAIRs from 32.0 to 25.7. MDPO, despite being designed for hallucination reduction, fails to mitigate modality bias (CB=0.3, LP=0.4), confirming that its approach of lowering preferred-sample probability without images does not address the bias problem. Against the strongest bias-focused baseline RLAIF-V-Iterative (CB=54.3, LP=35.3), the proposed method gains +4.6 on CB and +8.7 on LP, while also achieving lower hallucination rates on Object HalBench (CHAIRs 25.7 vs. 8.5 is worse, but CHAIRi 6.2 vs. 4.3 is competitive). On MMHalBench, the method achieves the best quality score (3.31) among all 7B comparisons, surpassing DPO (3.23) and MDPO (3.28), with a lower hallucination rate (0.35) than both. On the 13B scale, the method improves CB by +5.0 over DPO and LP by +3.9, while maintaining competitive hallucination metrics (CHAIRs=23.7, HalRate=0.33).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes modality bias mitigation in MLLMs as a preference optimization problem, departing from prior debiasing approaches that rely on balanced dataset construction or complex training/inference strategies requiring large-scale SFT. Methodologically, NaPO extends DPO by replacing its BCE loss with a Box-Cox-transformed interpolation between BCE and MAE (building on Generalized Cross Entropy), and introduces a dynamic noise-robustness coefficient derived from logP reward margins. The RLAIF-V-Bias dataset extends RLAIF-V by generating biased negative samples through modality masking, a data construction strategy distinct from MDPO's approach of conditioning on image absence. The paper positions itself against both the debiasing literature (which risks eroding existing model knowledge) and the preference optimization literature (which has largely ignored noise robustness in automatically constructed data).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that modality bias and hallucination are coupled phenomena in MLLMs, and that a single preference optimization pass can address both while preserving the model's existing capabilities—avoiding the knowledge degradation risk of SFT-based debiasing. The complementary roles of language-bias and vision-bias data (each targeting different bias dimensions) provide a practical recipe for constructing targeted debiasing datasets. Open questions remain: whether NaPO's noise-robustness mechanism generalizes beyond the specific masking-based noise in RLAIF-V-Bias to broader LLM-synthesized data, whether the approach scales to larger or architecturally different MLLMs, and whether all forms of modality bias are genuinely harmful or sometimes reflect useful priors.
