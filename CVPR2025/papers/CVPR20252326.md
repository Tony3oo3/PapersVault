## Problem

Existing self-supervised point cloud completion methods assume a uniform distribution of missing points (caused by limited field of view) and operate on small clouds (≤10k points), making them unsuitable for large-scale archaeological structures where missing surfaces from weathering and erosion produce heavily unbalanced point distributions (e.g., wall tops are absent) and no ground truth exists. This paper tackles completion of point clouds with up to 95% missing surfaces, millions of points, and color data under these non-uniform, ground-truth-free conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial point cloud (50k–2M RGB points, up to 95% missing) along with rough boundary annotations from archaeologists, and projects it into a 5-channel MCOP image (RGB + depth + rotation) at 2 cm sampling resolution, with image heights of 256 or 384 pixels corresponding to 4 m or 6 m maximum structure heights. The output is a completed colored point cloud and a watertight textured mesh produced via Poisson reconstruction, with normals computed from finite differences of the MCOP depth channel.

## Method

The core contribution is a patch-based self-supervised inpainting scheme operating on a novel 5-channel MCOP representation that encodes both side and top surfaces into a single slit via a rotation channel, reducing 3D completion to 2D image inpainting. Because the unbalanced pixel distribution causes whole-image adversarial training to collapse (the network fills missing regions with default values), the method instead extracts small patches (64×64 or 96×96) with ≥95% completeness as a near-uniform 'real' distribution and trains a LaMa-based inpainter adversarially to generate patches indistinguishable from these complete ones. Three auxiliary losses enforce quality: a consistency loss preserving known pixels, a rotation regularity loss requiring each column's rotation to sum to π for valid 3D reprojection, and a VGG19 Gram-matrix texture similarity loss ensuring pattern coherence beyond the patch receptive field.

## Results

On the Mawchu Incomplete dataset, the proposed method achieves the best performance across all image-level metrics: MAE of 0.189 (texture) and 0.136 (geometry), SSIM of 0.246 and 0.353, PSNR of 14.28 and 17.87, and FID of 90.81 and 128.41. In point cloud metrics, it achieves the lowest Chamfer distance (8.22 cm), precision (3.80 cm), and coverage (4.42 cm). The method handles structures with up to 95% missing surfaces and point clouds ranging from 50k to 2M points, producing both geometry and color/texture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two self-supervised point cloud methods (ACL-SPC and P2C) and two self-supervised image inpainting methods applied to MCOP images (MCOP+UAIR and MCOP+SSII), plus a patch-based variant (MCOP+SSII(Patch)). The proposed method outperforms all baselines on every reported metric in both image-level and point cloud evaluations. The point cloud methods (ACL-SPC, P2C) cannot produce color and show substantially worse geometry (e.g., ACL-SPC has 26% higher geometry MAE and 2.6× higher Chamfer distance than Ours). Among the MCOP-based inpainting baselines, MCOP+SSII(Patch) is the closest competitor, but Ours still improves FID by ~49% (90.81 vs. 179.05 texture) and SSIM by ~15% (0.353 vs. 0.278 geometry), demonstrating that the full self-supervised scheme with texture similarity and regularity losses provides meaningful gains over simple patch-based adversarial training alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the MCOP (Multiple Center of Projection) image representation [28] from texture mapping into a 2D encoding for 3D point cloud completion, extending it with a rotation channel that unifies side and top views into a single slit. For the inpainting backbone, it builds on LaMa [36] and the self-supervised adversarial inpainting frameworks of UAIR [25] and SSII [42], but departs from them by introducing a patch-based observation function to handle the heavily unbalanced pixel distributions caused by missing surfaces (as opposed to the uniform occlusion assumption in prior work). It also diverges from self-supervised point cloud completion methods like ACL-SPC [16] and P2C [5], which operate directly in 3D and are limited to ~10k points without color, by shifting the completion problem entirely into 2D image space where convolutional networks scale to millions of points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to handle large-scale (up to 2M points, 95% missing) point cloud completion with unbalanced distributions and color output, which is critical for archaeological and heritage preservation where ground truth is unavailable and weathering removes specific structural parts. The patch-based self-supervision insight—that small uniform-completeness windows can serve as self-supervision signals even when the global distribution is heavily skewed—is a practical technique that could transfer to other incomplete-data settings. Key limitations include the need for manual boundary annotations to define the MCOP trajectory, reliance on Poisson reconstruction for the final mesh, and evaluation limited to a single synthetic incomplete dataset; full automation and generalization beyond archaeological structures remain open.
