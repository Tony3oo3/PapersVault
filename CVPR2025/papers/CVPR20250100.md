## Problem

Training diffusion models demands increasingly massive datasets (e.g., billions of image-text pairs for Stable Diffusion), creating prohibitive data acquisition and storage costs. This paper asks whether new diffusion models can be trained entirely from existing pretrained diffusion models as data sources—without accessing any dataset and without restricting the student to the teacher's architecture—a scenario they formalize as Data-Free Knowledge Distillation for Diffusion Models (DKDM).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250100/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the trained student model takes random Gaussian noise (ε ~ N(0, I)) and produces synthetic RGB images via iterative denoising (50 IDDPM steps in pixel space at 32×32 or 64×64, or 200 DDIM steps in latent space at 256×256). During training, the student consumes noisy intermediate samples x̂^t generated by the teacher's reverse diffusion process rather than real data, with timesteps drawn from a uniform distribution via shuffle denoise. The student architecture is unconstrained—experiments include CNN-to-ViT and ViT-to-CNN cross-architecture distillation.

## Method

The DKDM objective replaces both data-dependent terms in the standard DM loss: the diffusion posterior q(x^{t-1}|x^t,x^0) is substituted by the teacher's denoising distribution p_θT(x^{t-1}|x^t), and the diffusion prior x^t ~ q(x^t|x^0) is replaced by the teacher's multi-step reverse generation G_θT(T−t), yielding L_DKDM = L_simple' + λL_vlb' where both terms compare teacher and student outputs directly. To avoid the O(Tb) cost of running T−t denoising steps per training iteration, the paper introduces dynamic iterative distillation: the teacher denoises one step at a time while the student learns from each intermediate output, with shuffle denoise randomizing per-sample timesteps for batch diversity and an enlarged batch set (scaled by factor ρ) with random subset selection to restore i.i.d.-like batch composition. This reduces per-iteration complexity to O(b) while maintaining convergence behavior closely matching standard data-based training.

## Results

On pixel-space benchmarks, Dynamic Iterative Distillation achieves FID 9.56 on CIFAR-10 32×32, 7.07 on CelebA 64×64, and 11.33 on ImageNet 32×32, consistently outperforming the Data-Free (0%) baseline by 2.5–3.9 FID points. In latent space, the method reaches FID 8.69 on CelebA-HQ 256×256 and 11.53 on FFHQ 256×256, surpassing the Data-Free baseline by 6.7 and 4.8 FID points respectively. Notably, on CelebA-HQ the data-free method (FID 8.69) even outperforms full data-based training (FID 9.09), and on CIFAR-10 the Inception Score (8.60) approaches the data-based score (8.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes data-centric baselines rather than comparing to prior KD methods, since DKDM is a new paradigm. Dynamic Iterative Distillation consistently outperforms the Data-Free (0%) baseline across all five datasets and all metrics (FID, sFID, IS), with the largest gains in latent space (e.g., CelebA-HQ FID 8.69 vs. 15.36). It also beats all Data-Limited variants (5%–20% real data), indicating that the iterative distillation strategy extracts more useful knowledge than simply mixing a small fraction of real images with synthetic ones. Against the Data-Based Training reference (100% real data), the method is competitive: it achieves a lower FID on CelebA-HQ (8.69 vs. 9.09) and a comparable IS on CIFAR-10 (8.60 vs. 8.73), while remaining slightly behind on other datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10 FID 9.56 vs. 7.84). The cross-architecture experiment (Table 5) further shows that distillation from a CNN teacher to a ViT student yields FID 17.86, far better than the Data-Free baseline of 63.15, confirming architectural flexibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the hybrid training objective with v-parameterized variance (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021), redesigning both into a data-free distillation objective (L_DKDM) that replaces the intractable data-dependent posterior and prior with teacher-model outputs. It departs from existing knowledge distillation for diffusion models—which typically focuses on model compression or reducing sampling steps and requires dataset access—by introducing the DKDM paradigm where the student is randomly initialized with any architecture and learns from the teacher's intermediate denoising states rather than final generated samples. The closest prior work is BOOT (Gu et al., 2023), which is also data-free KD for diffusion models but retains the teacher's architecture and weights, whereas DKDM explicitly targets architectural flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and growing pain point: the enormous data costs (billions of image-text pairs) required to train modern diffusion models, offering a pathway to train new models without dataset access or storage. The finding that data-free distillation can occasionally match or exceed data-based training (e.g., FID 8.69 vs. 9.09 on CelebA-HQ) suggests that a teacher's learned denoising process may be a more learnable signal than raw data, which could reshape how practitioners think about training data requirements. However, all experiments are conducted on small-scale datasets (32×32 to 256×256) with student models that are downscaled versions of the teacher, leaving open whether the approach scales to billion-parameter text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion or handles complex conditioning (text prompts, multi-modal inputs). The dynamic iterative distillation technique itself is a practical, low-overhead training strategy that could be adopted independently of the full DKDM framework.
