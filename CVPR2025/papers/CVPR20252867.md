## Problem

Two-stage video inpainting methods that combine flow-based pixel propagation with video transformers suffer from error accumulation: warping introduces local distortions that corrupt the priors, and consistency checks that discard inaccurate flows leave insufficient semantic guidance for the generation network. The paper addresses the fundamental tension between retaining more propagated pixels and avoiding artifacts that downstream networks cannot tolerate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252867/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a corrupted video sequence (N frames at 240×432), a binary mask sequence identifying corrupted regions per frame, and a text prompt (generated by GPT-4o from the center frame) as input, and produces a fully inpainted video with spatiotemporally coherent content. For long videos, processing is segmented into overlapping GoPs (Groups of Pictures) with temporal references from adjacent GoPs to maintain cross-GoP consistency. Training uses 5 million video sequences from Shutterstock with synthesized stationary and object-aware masks.

## Method

HomoGen is a two-stage propagate-and-diffuse framework. The first stage (HPP) estimates forward and backward homography matrices via RANSAC on ORB feature matches to warp pixels bidirectionally across frames, extracting propagated pixels as priors that preserve semantic integrity despite global structural distortions. The second stage (PCVDM) is a 3D video diffusion model built on Stable Diffusion inpainting with temporal layers, where a LiteControlNet extracts multi-scale features from the encoded priors and injects them into the 3D U-Net via a spatiotemporal adaptive de-normalization strategy (SPADE-like) combined with step-dependent scaling through Fourier-encoded sampling steps. The key insight is that diffusion models inherently prioritize semantic information over pixel-level details, so the iterative denoising process filters out homography-induced structural artifacts while preserving the richer spatiotemporal context that homography provides compared to flow-based propagation.

## Results

On YouTube-VOS, HomoGen achieves PSNR 34.81, SSIM 0.9735, VFID 0.032, and warping error 0.858×10⁻³, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art ProPainter (PSNR 34.46, VFID 0.042) by 23.81% in VFID. On DAVIS, HomoGen reaches PSNR 34.71 and VFID 0.077, a 21.43% VFID improvement over ProPainter (0.098). On RORD (object removal), HomoGen attains PSNR 37.29, SSIM 0.9872, and VFID 0.023, improving VFID by 17.86% over ProPainter (0.028). HomoGen also shows superior robustness to larger mask ratios and lower motion magnitudes compared to ProPainter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HomoGen outperforms all nine baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and VFID across all three datasets. Against the strongest baseline ProPainter [63], HomoGen improves VFID by 23.81% (0.032 vs 0.042) on YouTube-VOS, 21.43% (0.077 vs 0.098) on DAVIS, and 17.86% (0.023 vs 0.028) on RORD, while also achieving higher PSNR (+0.35, +0.52, +0.53 dB) and SSIM on all three. The only metric where HomoGen does not lead is warping error on DAVIS (1.234 vs ProPainter's 1.195), suggesting slightly less temporal stability in that setting. Among earlier methods, E²FGVI [22] and FuseFormer [24] are the closest competitors on PSNR/SSIM but trail significantly on VFID (e.g., 0.046 and 0.053 vs 0.032 on YouTube-VOS), indicating less perceptually realistic outputs. In a user study with 15 participants over 45 video triplets, HomoGen is preferred over ProPainter in spatial coherence, temporal consistency, visual quality, and overall impression.

## Relation to Prior Work

HomoGen extends the two-stage video inpainting paradigm (propagation + generation) by replacing both stages with more compatible components: homography-based pixel propagation in place of optical-flow warping, and a video diffusion model in place of a video transformer. The generation stage is built on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture, extended to 3D following AnimateDiff and Make-A-Video, with a LiteControlNet (modified from ControlNet) and a spatiotemporal adaptive de-normalization strategy (adapted from SPADE) for injecting propagated priors. The key conceptual departure from ProPainter and similar flow-based methods is the argument that homography-induced artifacts—global structural distortions that preserve semantic integrity—are inherently more compatible with diffusion models, which prioritize semantic content over pixel-level fidelity during denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a non-trivial synergy between homography propagation and diffusion-based generation: despite higher warping error than flow-based methods, homography priors yield substantially better perceptual quality (23.81% VFID improvement over ProPainter) because the diffusion model filters out structural artifacts while retaining semantic guidance. The content-adaptive control mechanism (LiteControlNet + step-aware adaptive de-normalization) provides a reusable recipe for injecting noisy spatial priors into diffusion models. Open questions remain around identity preservation across scene alternations and efficient processing of high-resolution videos, which the authors acknowledge as limitations shared with prior methods.
