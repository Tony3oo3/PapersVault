## Problem

Holistic video understanding (captioning, QA) and pixel-level video understanding (segmentation, tracking) have evolved as separate research streams with distinct datasets, benchmarks, and architectures. No existing dataset simultaneously provides detailed human-written captions with phrase-level grounding and temporally consistent, pixel-accurate segmentation masks, making it impossible to evaluate whether a model can both describe a video and precisely localize the objects it mentions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250886/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives T=32 uniformly sampled video frames and a text prompt. For the captioning task, the prompt requests a video description and the output is an open-ended text summary. For the LG-VIS task, the prompt is a 'Where is?' query (e.g., 'Where is the person who walks from left to right?') generated from grounded captions via GPT-4, and the output is a set of temporally-consistent segmentation masks at 30 fps—one mask per referenced object rather than a single binary mask. Ground-truth masks are annotated at 1 fps by humans and propagated to 30 fps using SAM2.

## Method

Video-LLaVA-Seg extends the LLaVA architecture with a segmentation head, comprising an AM-RADIO ViT-H vision backbone, a Llama3-8B LLM, and a Hiera-Small segmentation backbone with a SAM2-style mask decoder. The key architectural innovation is slow-fast pooling: video features are split into 'slow' features (T_s=8 frames at full spatial resolution, capturing temporal detail) and 'fast' features (all T=32 frames at heavily downsampled spatial resolution, capturing spatial detail), which are concatenated to reduce the total number of video tokens fed to the LLM while preserving both temporal and spatial fidelity. For LG-VIS, the LLM is trained to emit a special <SEG> token for each referenced object; these tokens are cross-attended with multi-scale segmentation backbone features in a SAM2-style decoder to produce per-object mask logits. Training proceeds in three stages: vision-language alignment on the projection MLP, captioning finetuning on 3.5M samples from WebVid10M and Panda70M, and joint captioning+segmentation finetuning on the ViCaS training set, taking ~4 days on 32 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the ViCaS validation set, Video-LLaVA-Seg achieves 20.5 mAP for Language-Guided Video Instance Segmentation (LG-VIS), substantially outperforming the strongest prior segmentation baseline DsHmp (13.3 mAP) by 7.2 points. For Video Captioning, it scores 3.0 CA (out of 5), matching MiniCPM-o 2.6 and slightly exceeding LLaVA-OV (2.9 CA). The model is the only evaluated method that handles both tasks simultaneously with a single end-to-end architecture, and uniquely supports multi-object mask prediction from a single text prompt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For LG-VIS, Video-LLaVA-Seg (20.5 mAP) decisively outperforms all three segmentation baselines: LMPM (8.4 mAP, +12.1), VideoLISA (10.7 mAP, +9.8), and DsHmp (13.3 mAP, +7.2). The gap is especially pronounced at higher IoU thresholds, where Video-LLaVA-Seg reaches 19.0 AP75 versus DsHmp's 11.6 and VideoLISA's 8.5. For Video Captioning, Video-LLaVA-Seg ties MiniCPM-o 2.6 at 3.0 CA and marginally beats LLaVA-OV (2.9 CA), though both zero-shot models are trained on substantially more data and cannot perform segmentation at all. A key qualitative advantage is that Video-LLaVA-Seg predicts a separate mask per referenced object from a single prompt, whereas the Referral-VOS baselines (LMPM, DsHmp, VideoLISA) output only a single binary mask covering all target objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two research streams that have evolved largely in parallel: holistic video understanding (video captioning, QA via MLLMs like Video-LLaVA and LLaVA) and pixel-level video understanding (referral VOS, video instance segmentation via SAM2, MeViS, VideoLISA). Architecturally, Video-LLaVA-Seg extends the LLaVA framework by appending a SAM2-style mask decoder triggered by a <SEG> token, and adopts slow-fast pooling from SlowFast-LLaVA to reduce video token count. The key departure is the dataset itself: no prior video dataset provides both detailed human-written captions with phrase-level grounding and temporally consistent pixel-precise instance masks, and the LG-VIS task explicitly extends referral VOS by requiring separate instance masks per referenced object rather than a single binary mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViCaS fills a genuine gap by providing the first benchmark where a single model must demonstrate both high-level narrative understanding and pixel-accurate localization, which is essential for practical applications like autonomous robotics and video editing. The ablation showing task synergy (multi-task training improves segmentation mAP from 18.5 to 20.5 without hurting captioning) suggests that holistic reasoning and dense prediction are complementary rather than competing objectives, a finding that could inform future unified model design. Limitations include the moderate dataset size (20K videos), 1 fps annotation with SAM2 propagation (introducing potential mask drift), and the modest absolute performance (20.5 mAP), leaving open whether the synergy effect persists at larger scale or with stronger backbones.
