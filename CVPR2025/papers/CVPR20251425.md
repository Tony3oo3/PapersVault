## Problem

Existing 3D thermal reconstruction methods are limited to static, single-time-period reconstruction and cannot represent or predict how scene temperatures evolve over time. They also lack physical grounding, treating temperature as a purely data-driven attribute rather than a quantity governed by thermodynamic heat transfer and radiation laws.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251425/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view aerial TIR images (640×512, captured at 250 m altitude by a DJI H20T sensor) with timestamps across four nighttime intervals, paired with synthetic RGB images rendered from a high-precision UAV4DL 3D texture model, and a tilted-photography point cloud for Gaussian position initialization. It outputs a 4D Gaussian scene in which temperature replaces color as the time-varying attribute, enabling rendering of thermal maps from arbitrary novel viewpoints at any nighttime moment, along with per-Gaussian thermodynamic parameters (emissivity, convective heat transfer coefficient, heat capacity).

## Method

NTR-Gaussian employs a two-stage optimization over a 4D Gaussian Splatting representation where temperature is the learned time-varying attribute. In Stage 1, an MLP (Temp_Net) maps 10-order trigonometric time and position encodings to per-Gaussian radiation temperatures while jointly optimizing Gaussian parameters (position, rotation, scale, opacity) via L1 + SSIM losses. In Stage 2, Gaussian parameters are frozen and a Thermal_Net (comprising E_Net, C_Net, H_Net sub-networks) predicts emissivity, convective heat transfer coefficient, and heat capacity from temperature, Feature_GS semantic features, segmented time encodings, and position encoding. The predicted parameters feed a thermodynamic differential equation T'(t) = (Q_rad + Q_con)/H, whose numerical integration from an initial time t₀ yields a physically consistent temperature trajectory; a smoothing loss enforces continuous temperature variation across the integration intervals, and joint optimization ensures the integrated temperature matches the Temp_Net prediction at each step.

## Results

NTR-Gaussian achieves the best performance across all four scenes (S1–S4) on every metric. On the average across scenes, it reaches PSNR 33.92, SSIM 0.966, and MAE 1.170 °C, with per-scene MAE as low as 0.947 °C (S1) and 0.959 °C (S3). The method requires substantially more training time (33,402 s) and memory (334 GB) than baselines due to the integration-based thermodynamic prediction, but maintains comparable rendering speed (13 FPS vs. 13–14 FPS for static methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTR-Gaussian outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all four scenes. Against the static 3DGS baseline, it improves PSNR by 0.4–5.9 dB and reduces MAE by 0.2–1.3 °C per scene, with the largest gain on S3 (PSNR 35.15 vs. 29.28; MAE 0.959 vs. 2.267). Against the dynamic 4DGS baseline, the advantage is even more pronounced: PSNR improves by 1.2–5.4 dB and MAE drops by 0.5–0.8 °C, confirming that a simple temporal deformation field without physical constraints is insufficient for thermal prediction. Against the closest competitor Thermal3D-GS (a physics-informed static thermal 3DGS), NTR-Gaussian still leads by 0.5–1.9 dB in PSNR and 0.1–0.3 °C in MAE, demonstrating that the two-stage thermodynamic integration adds meaningful accuracy over single-stage physics-guided reconstruction. The trade-off is a ~60× increase in training time and ~40× increase in memory, while rendering FPS remains comparable (13 vs. 13–14).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting lineage (3DGS → 4DGS) into the thermal domain by incorporating thermodynamic physics into the 4D representation. It departs from prior thermal 3DGS methods (Thermal3D-GS, ThermalGaussian) that perform only static reconstruction, and from NeRF-based thermal approaches (ThermoNeRF, Thermal-NeRF) that suffer from slow rendering and implicit representation. The key conceptual departure is using neural networks to predict per-Gaussian thermodynamic parameters (emissivity, convective heat transfer coefficient, heat capacity) and then numerically integrating the resulting ODE to predict temperature evolution, rather than learning temperature as a free parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NTR dataset provides the first benchmark for nighttime dynamic thermal reconstruction from aerial TIR imagery, filling a gap where prior thermal 3DGS work was limited to static single-timepoint reconstruction. The physics-informed integration strategy is a promising direction for making thermal predictions physically consistent, but the method's practical utility is severely constrained by its computational cost (~100× more memory and ~100× longer training than 3DGS) and its per-scene optimization requirement, which limits generalization across scenes and seasons. Open questions include whether the thermodynamic parameters can be predicted in a feedforward manner, whether environmental factors (humidity, wind speed) can be incorporated, and whether the approach scales to larger or more diverse scenes.
