## Problem

Image-referenced sketch colorization methods suffer from spatial entanglement, where spatial mismatches between the reference image and the sketch cause the model to hallucinate extra objects, overflow color regions, and blend reference composition into the output. Existing solutions—text-prompted methods lack precise color/style control, hint-guided methods require manual labor, and prior image-referenced approaches like ColorizeDiffusion still produce severe artifacts—leave a gap for an automated, artifact-free pipeline that fits real-world animation production workflows.

## Input / Output

The method takes a line-art sketch, an RGB reference image (which may be geometrically mismatched with the sketch), and an automatically extracted foreground mask as inputs, and produces a fully colorized image at the sketch's resolution. The foreground mask is generated by an open-source anime segmentation tool with user-tunable thresholds, and the reference image is split into foreground and background regions using a corresponding mask. Three switchable inference modes (Vanilla, Bg2Fig, Fig2Fig) allow users to select the appropriate colorization strategy at test time without retraining, accommodating scenarios ranging from figure-only references to full-scene references.

## Method

The framework builds on a Stable Diffusion backbone (initialized from WaifuDiffusion) and introduces a split cross-attention mechanism that replaces standard cross-attention layers in the U-Net. Spatial masks partition each attention computation into foreground and background branches, each with its own trainable LoRA weights (rank 16 for foreground, rank 0.5·min(D_q, D_kv) for background) applied to the Q, K, V projections, so the two regions attend to independently extracted reference embeddings without interference. A recovery transformer is inserted to process background embeddings before they enter cross-attention, compensating for the different texture and color-distribution characteristics of backgrounds versus figures. Color references are extracted as CLIP local tokens (rather than CLS tokens) to preserve fine-grained texture and stroke information, while a multi-layer sketch encoder injects spatial guidance into the latent denoising layers. During training on 4.8M (sketch, color, mask) triples from Danbooru2021, the backbone is frozen and only the LoRA modules and recovery transformer are optimized, with a dynamic reference drop rate (80%→50%) to prevent distribution shift.

## Results

On the Danbooru2021 validation set (50K sketch-reference pairs with spatially mismatched references), the proposed method achieves a FID of 6.83, substantially outperforming the closest competitor ColorizeDiffusion (9.53) and all adapter-based baselines (27.0–61.6). On the 5K spatially-aligned test set, the method scores 0.6002 MS-SSIM and 0.8829 CLIP similarity, the best among all diffusion-based methods, while the GAN-based Yan et al. method achieves a slightly higher PSNR (29.25 vs. 28.91) due to its conservative, near-average outputs. Inference time is 1513 ms at 512×512 on an H100 GPU, faster than all adapter-based baselines (2073–2617 ms) but slightly slower than ColorizeDiffusion (1387 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on FID, MS-SSIM, and CLIP similarity. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor, ColorizeDiffusion, the gap is 2.7 FID points (6.83 vs. 9.53) with marginal gains in MS-SSIM (+0.009) and CLIP similarity (+0.005), while ColorizeDiffusion is ~126 ms faster at inference. Against the three adapter-based baselines (IP-Adapter, InstantStyle, T2I-Adapter), the proposed method achieves dramatically lower FID (6.83 vs. 38.9–41.2) and higher MS-SSIM (0.60 vs. 0.32–0.55), at roughly half the inference time. Against AnimeDiffusion, the FID advantage is even larger (6.83 vs. 61.60). The only metric where the proposed method does not lead is PSNR, where the GAN-based Yan et al. method scores 29.25 vs. 28.91, attributed to its limited generation capacity producing near-average outputs that minimize pixel-level error; Yan et al. is also far faster (57 ms) but sacrifices texture, color fidelity, and background quality. In the user study (40 participants, 25 image sets), the proposed method received the highest preference count on both overall quality and geometric preservation, with statistical significance p < 0.01 (Kruskal-Wallis test).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ColorizeDiffusion [47], adopting its local-token reference extraction and diffusion-based colorization pipeline, but departs from it by introducing a split cross-attention mechanism with foreground/background-specific LoRA modules to eliminate the spatial entanglement artifacts that plague all existing image-referenced methods (IP-Adapter, T2I-Adapter, InstantStyle). The design is explicitly motivated by the manual animation studio workflow—separating character colorization from background painting—rather than by a purely technical observation, which distinguishes it from prior adapter-based approaches that inject reference embeddings uniformly. The multi-layer sketch encoder for spatial control follows the ControlNet paradigm, and the overall backbone (Stable Diffusion + VAE + DPM++ sampler) is standard, so the novelty is concentrated in the attention-level separation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The split cross-attention with switchable LoRA offers a practical, plug-in solution to spatial entanglement in image-referenced colorization without retraining the diffusion backbone, making it attractive for studios seeking to automate the most labor-intensive step in animation pipelines. However, the method's strong dependence on foreground mask quality (acknowledged by the authors) limits robustness for ambiguous or occluded sketches, and the evaluation is confined to anime-style imagery on Danbooru2021, leaving open whether the foreground/background separation generalizes to photorealistic or mixed-media colorization. The authors identify video colorization as a natural next step, suggesting the framework could extend to temporal consistency if the split-attention mechanism is adapted across frames.
