## Problem

Pre-trained Video LLMs are trained on video-text pairs and lack a mechanism to incorporate side-channel signals such as audio, 3D cues, multi-view videos, or high frame rate inputs that many downstream tasks require. While zero-shot Video LLMs show promising performance on such tasks, they consistently lag behind task-specific models that are trained with these additional signals, and no lightweight framework exists to integrate such signals without modifying the base model's architecture or pre-trained weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251038/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 32 key video frames, a text query, and a task-specific side-channel signal (audio sampled at 16 kHz via ImageBind, camera poses + depth maps encoded by LLaVA-3D's 3D encoder yielding ~18K tokens per scan, densely sampled 2 fps video features via LanguageBind, or multi-view video). The side-channel is encoded by a separate encoder into tokens of arbitrary length, but the fusion function constrains the output to match the shape of the original visual tokens, so the LLM receives the same number of input tokens as the unmodified base model. The output is a standard autoregressive text answer.

## Method

PAVE introduces a lightweight 'patch' adapter that fuses side-channel tokens with visual tokens via temporal-aligned cross-attention: video tokens serve as queries and side-channel tokens as keys and values, with each query attending only to side-channel tokens in its temporal neighborhood (with rotary positional embeddings for alignment). The cross-attention output, which has the same shape as the original visual tokens, is added back via a residual connection, preserving the LLM's input token count and keeping inference overhead to ~0.1% additional FLOPs and parameters. A LoRA module is simultaneously applied to the LLM's weights for task-specific adaptation. The entire patch (fusion function + LoRA) is trained end-to-end with standard negative log-likelihood loss while the base model's architecture and pre-trained weights remain frozen, enabling flexible per-task customization and compact distribution of ~20 MB patches for a 7B model.

## Results

On audio-visual QA, PAVE achieves 152.9 CIDEr on AVSD (vs. 108.5 for the prior best COST) and 93.8% accuracy on AVQA (vs. 92.0% for CAT-7B-FT), while adding only ~9M parameters and 0.1 TFLOPs (~0.1% overhead). On 3D QA, PAVE reaches 103.4 CIDEr and 29.1% EM@1 on ScanQA, and 59.0% EM@1 on SQA3D, surpassing the strongest 3D-specific baseline LLaVA-3D by 2–3% in absolute EM@1. On enhanced video QA, PAVE-7B improves over LLaVA-OneVision-7B by 1.7% on VideoMME (59.9 vs. 58.2), 1.3% on MVBench (58.0 vs. 56.7), and 2.3% on MLVU (67.0 vs. 64.7). Across all tasks, PAVE adds only ~0.1% additional FLOPs and parameters to the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAVE decisively outperforms all task-specific audio-visual baselines: it surpasses COST by 44.4 CIDEr points on AVSD (152.9 vs. 108.5), beats CAT-7B-FT by 1.8% on AVQA (93.8 vs. 92.0), and exceeds VALOR (78.9) and VAST (80.7) on Music-AVQA overall accuracy. On 3D QA, PAVE outperforms every 3D-specific model—LLaVA-3D (91.7 CIDEr, 55.6 SQA3D EM@1), Scene-LLM (80.0 CIDEr, 54.2), LEO (101.4 CIDEr, 50.0), 3D-LLM (74.5 CIDEr, 49.79), and VideoChat2-7B (49.2 CIDEr, 37.3)—achieving the best CIDEr (103.4) and EM@1 (29.1 ScanQA, 59.0 SQA3D). The one notable loss is on Music-AVQA's audio-only and audio-visual splits, where CAT-7B-FT (84.9% and 83.2%) edges out PAVE (79.7% and 78.0%), which the authors attribute to PAVE's video-prior bias when audio and visual cues conflict. Compared to the video-only fine-tuned baseline (LLaVA-OV-7B-FT), PAVE consistently improves by 2–7 points across all three audio-visual datasets, confirming the value of the audio side-channel. For cross-model generalization, PAVE also outperforms the fine-tuned Video-LLaVA-7B baseline on ScanQA (25.2 vs. 21.6 EM@1), demonstrating transferability beyond LLaVA-OneVision.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAVE extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm (LoRA) from text and image generation models to Video LLMs, but introduces a key architectural departure: a temporal-aligned cross-attention 'patch' that fuses side-channel tokens (audio, 3D cues, high frame rate frames, multi-view video) with video tokens before they enter the LLM, rather than simply adding low-rank updates to the LLM weights. This design is conceptually distinct from prior Video LLM adaptation work (e.g., Adapt2Reward, SeViLA) that introduces task-specific modules, and from interleaved-token approaches (e.g., LLaVA-Next-Interleave) that increase input token count. The high frame rate enhancement task draws on the SlowFast network idea of combining low-resolution fast streams with high-resolution slow streams, while side-channel encoding leverages existing multi-modal encoders (ImageBind, LanguageBind, LLaVA-3D's 3D encoder).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The patch-based formulation offers a practical path to extending Video LLMs to new modalities at negligible cost (~0.1% additional parameters and FLOPs), with patches small enough (~20 MB) to be distributed independently of the base model (~16 GB), analogous to LoRA weights in diffusion models. However, the gains over video-only fine-tuning of the base model are modest in several settings, and the multi-task learning demonstration is limited to a single staged experiment (frozen high-frame-rate patch + new audio patch). Open questions include whether joint multi-task training of multiple patches is feasible, how PAVE handles genuinely conflicting cross-modal signals (as seen in the audio-visual split of Music-AVQA), and whether the approach generalizes to side-channel modalities not seen during the base model's pre-training.
