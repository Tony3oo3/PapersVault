## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning (PFL) methods are validated primarily on shallow base models (e.g., four-layer CNNs) and overlook how deeper architectures amplify server aggregation conflicts. The paper identifies a 'deep model degradation' phenomenon where increasing base model depth makes clients more sensitive to non-IID data distributions, causing conflicting updates that reduce overall accuracy across all state-of-the-art PFL methods.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB image classification tasks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Flowers102, CINIC-10) with non-IID data splits across 20–60 clients, producing per-client class labels. The base model architecture ranges from a shallow CNN to ResNet34, and the method processes layer-wise parameter updates (Δθ_i^(k)) uploaded by clients rather than raw images at the server, making it architecture-agnostic and applicable as a drop-in aggregation module for any PFL framework.

## Method

FedCALM is a two-stage conflict-aware layer-wise mitigation strategy applied at the server during aggregation. Stage 1 projects each client's update onto the orthogonal complement of any conflicting peer update (negative cosine similarity) at each layer, producing a conflict-free target vector Δθ̄_⊥^(k). Stage 2 solves a quadratic program that finds the optimal global update d* = Δθ̄_⊥ + Σ λ_i* Δθ_i, balancing proximity to the conflict-free target against a penalty C for clients whose updates conflict with d, with a lower-bound ε ensuring positive contribution to every client. The KKT complementary slackness conditions yield a natural selective mechanism: λ_i = 0 for non-conflicting clients (no adjustment) while λ_i > 0 for conflicting clients (targeted mitigation), so the method only intervenes where conflicts exist. The QP is solved per layer in parallel, and the resulting per-layer updates are concatenated into the final global parameter update.

## Results

FedCALM achieves the highest test accuracy across all deeper base models (ResNet4–ResNet34) on both Flowers102 and CIFAR100. On Flowers102 with ResNet4, FedCALM reaches 75.57%, outperforming the next-best method (FedPAC, 63.55%) by 9.88%; with ResNet10 it reaches 80.24% vs. FedPAC's 77.08%. On CIFAR100, FedCALM leads by 5.31% (ResNet4: 65.20% vs. 59.89%) and 3.67% (ResNet34: 60.79% vs. 57.12%). Notably, all four SOTA baselines show a clear accuracy decline as model depth increases, while FedCALM maintains a convex accuracy curve that peaks at ResNet10 before gradually declining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCALM decisively outperforms all four baselines (FedRep, FedALA, FedCP, FedPAC) on every deeper model (ResNet4 through ResNet34) across both Flowers102 and CIFAR100. The largest margin is 9.88% on Flowers102-ResNet4 (75.57% vs. FedRep's 65.69%), and on CIFAR100-ResNet4 FedCALM leads FedPAC by 5.31% (65.20% vs. 59.89%). Among the baselines, FedPAC is generally the strongest competitor on deeper models (e.g., 77.08% on Flowers102-ResNet10), while FedCP is competitive on CIFAR100 (57.12% at ResNet34). However, all four baselines exhibit the deep model degradation phenomenon: their accuracy drops as depth increases beyond ResNet10, with FedALA and FedRep falling below their own CNN-level performance at ResNet34. On the shallow CNN baseline, FedCALM is slightly weaker than FedPAC (63.11% vs. 68.32% on Flowers102), confirming that its primary advantage lies in deeper PFL scenarios where aggregation conflicts are most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the personalized federated learning (PFL) literature by identifying a previously overlooked failure mode: deeper base models (e.g., ResNet34 vs. four-layer CNNs) systematically degrade PFL accuracy due to amplified server aggregation conflicts. Conceptually, it builds on the layer-wise aggregation idea from pFedLA [22] and the conflict analysis framework from federated optimization, but departs from existing PFL methods (FedRep, FedALA, FedCP, FedPAC) by treating aggregation as a conflict-aware QP problem rather than a weighted average. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to and composable with all four SOTA PFL baselines, adopting FedPAC's classifier combination strategy for personalization while replacing the server-side aggregation step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'deep model degradation' in PFL challenges the common assumption that deeper architectures monotonically improve federated performance, and provides a concrete mechanism (accumulated directional conflicts across layers) that practitioners can diagnose. The QP-based conflict mitigation is a clean, general-purpose module, but its scalability is an open question: the dual problem is O(N²) in the number of clients, which may become prohibitive at the thousands-of-clients scale typical of real FL deployments. Whether the conflict-aware projection generalizes beyond CNN backbones (e.g., to transformers or foundation-model-based PFL) and whether the approach transfers to non-PFL federated settings (e.g., standard FedAvg with non-IID data) remain unexplored.
