## Problem

Existing multi-modal fine-tuning (MMFT) methods for CLIP improve ID accuracy but fail to effectively leverage pretrained textual knowledge for OoDD, because the modality gap persists in the hyperspherical embedding space—image and text embeddings remain separated, impairing the distinction between ID texts and negative (OoD) concept labels. The paper identifies this gap as the root cause and asks how to close it during fine-tuning while preserving ID classification performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251464/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, a single RGB image is encoded by the fine-tuned CLIP image encoder into an L2-normalized embedding on the hypersphere. The method produces two outputs: a class label via cosine similarity to ID text embeddings, and an OoD score computed as the ratio of softmax similarities to C ID text embeddings over the sum of similarities to both ID and M negative text embeddings (obtained via NegMining). No text input is required at test time; all textual information is precomputed as frozen text embeddings.

## Method

The core contribution is a Cross-Modal Alignment (CMA) regularization loss added to the standard CLIP contrastive loss during multi-modal fine-tuning of both image and text encoders. The CMA loss (Eqs. 4–5) removes the negative-log denominator from the per-sample contrastive term, globally increasing similarity between all ID image-text pairs and pulling the two modalities together on the hypersphere, while a hyperparameter λ controls alignment strength. Theoretically, the paper shows the combined objective is equivalent to maximizing the log-likelihood of a joint energy-based model on the hypersphere, where the standard CLIP loss provides the discriminative (conditional) term and the CMA loss provides the generative (marginal density) term under a von Mises-Fisher distribution. At inference, the OoD score follows the NegLabel scheme, comparing image-to-ID-text similarity against image-to-negative-text similarity, which benefits directly from the reduced modality gap.

## Results

On the MOS benchmark (ImageNet-1k ID), CMA+NegLabel achieves an average FPR95 of 19.93% and AUROC of 95.13%, outperforming the previous best MMFT method m²-mix+NegLabel (24.40% FPR95, 94.05% AUROC) and ZS NegLabel (27.97% FPR95, 93.78% AUROC). On the OpenOOD v1.5 Far-OoD benchmark, CMA+NegLabel reaches 15.29% FPR95 and 96.47% AUROC, compared to 17.54%/95.80% for m²-mix+NegLabel and 26.14%/94.34% for ZS NegLabel. For ID accuracy on ImageNet-1k, CMA achieves 82.64%, on par with m²-mix (82.67%) and FLYP (82.58%), and well above ZS (66.60%) and PL methods (~72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMA+NegLabel outperforms all MMFT and PL baselines on the MOS benchmark average: it beats m²-mix+NegLabel by 4.47% FPR95 (19.93 vs 24.40) and 1.08% AUROC (95.13 vs 94.05), FLYP+NegLabel by 6.46% FPR95, and CoOp+NegLabel by 6.35% FPR95. Against ZS NegLabel, CMA+NegLabel improves FPR95 by 8.04% and AUROC by 1.35%, demonstrating that fine-tuning with cross-modal alignment can surpass zero-shot performance when negative labels are used. On OpenOOD v1.5 Far-OoD, CMA+NegLabel leads all methods (15.29% FPR95, 96.47% AUROC), with a 2.25% FPR95 margin over m²-mix+NegLabel. However, on the Near-OoD SSB-hard dataset, FFT+Energy (54.21% FPR95) outperforms CMA+NegLabel (62.80%), indicating that the textual-information advantage diminishes for semantically very similar OoD classes. For ID accuracy, CMA (82.64%) is essentially tied with m²-mix (82.67%) and FLYP (82.58%), all substantially above ZS (66.60%) and PL methods (~72%), confirming that the CMA regularization does not sacrifice classification performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP fine-tuning line initiated by FLYP (Goyal et al., CVPR 2023), which showed that re-applying the original contrastive loss during fine-tuning improves both ID accuracy and OoD generalization. It departs from FLYP and m²-mix by diagnosing a specific failure mode: these methods preserve the modality gap in ID embeddings, which undermines the effectiveness of NegLabel-style OoD scoring that relies on distinguishing ID texts from negative concept texts. The CMA regularization is positioned as adding a generative (marginal density) term to the purely discriminative contrastive loss, with a formal equivalence to maximum likelihood estimation of a joint energy-based model on a hypersphere via the von Mises-Fisher distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that standard MMFT (FLYP) can *degrade* OoDD performance relative to zero-shot NegLabel—despite superior ID accuracy—provides an important diagnostic for practitioners combining fine-tuning with text-based OoD scoring. The modality gap analysis (uniformity/alignment metrics) offers a reusable framework for evaluating whether a fine-tuning strategy preserves or destroys the geometric structure that NegLabel exploits. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (ImageNet-1k) and a single model size (CLIP-B/16), and the Near-OoD results are only moderate, suggesting the benefit is most pronounced in Far-OoD settings. The authors note as future work the potential of incorporating auxiliary negative labels directly into the MMFT training objective.
