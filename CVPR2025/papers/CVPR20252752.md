## Problem

When pruned diffusion models are fine-tuned via knowledge distillation, they inadvertently retain and propagate undesirable behaviors (e.g., copyrighted artist styles, NSFW content) from the base model, even when such content is absent from the fine-tuning dataset. The natural two-stage remedy—first restore generative quality via distillation, then apply concept unlearning—is suboptimal because the parameters optimal for fine-tuning are not the best initialization for unlearning, creating a circular dependency that degrades both objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pruned diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2.1 pruned to 80% MAC budget via APTP), a fine-tuning dataset (MS-COCO-2017 image-caption pairs), a target concept to suppress (e.g., an artist's style or NSFW content), and an anchor concept guiding what should be generated in its place. It produces a fine-tuned pruned model that, given a text prompt, generates a 512×512 synthetic image while suppressing the target concept. The framework is plug-in: it accepts any pruning method's output and any concept unlearning technique in the upper-level step.

## Method

The paper formulates fine-tuning and concept suppression as a bilevel optimization problem: the lower level minimizes the standard fine-tuning loss (diffusion denoising loss plus output and feature knowledge distillation) to restore generative quality, while the upper level minimizes a concept unlearning objective (e.g., ESD-style negative guidance) to steer the model away from the target concept. The key insight is that the upper-level gradient with respect to the pruned parameters is influenced by both the unlearning loss and the fine-tuning loss, breaking the circular dependency that makes sequential approaches suboptimal. They convert the bilevel problem into a penalized constrained formulation and solve it with a first-order double-loop algorithm (Algorithm 1) that requires no second-order information, keeping computational cost comparable to or slightly below standard distillation-based fine-tuning. The framework is agnostic to the specific pruning method and unlearning technique, making it broadly applicable to resource-constrained deployment scenarios.

## Results

On the artist style removal task (removing Van Gogh, Picasso, and Monet styles from a pruned Stable Diffusion 2.1 model), the bilevel method achieves the best scores across all metrics: CLIP similarity of 26.28 (↓), CP score of 97.6 (↑), CSD score of 39.04 (↓), and FID of 22.24 (↓) on MS-COCO validation. For NSFW content removal, the method achieves 91.60% reduction on MMA and 94.32% on Ring-A-Bell while maintaining the best generation quality (FID 26.80, CLIP 29.94) among all methods. The bilevel approach outperforms the strongest two-stage baseline (Distilled+ConceptPrune) by 27% on CSD score and 20% on FID, demonstrating superior simultaneous concept suppression and quality retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The bilevel method decisively outperforms all three two-stage unlearning baselines (ESD, UCE, ConceptPrune) on the primary style removal task, achieving 15% lower CLIP similarity, 16% higher CP score, and 36% lower CSD score compared to Distilled+ESD, while also delivering the best FID (22.24 vs. 30.38 for ESD, 26.63 for UCE, 27.86 for ConceptPrune). Against the strongest baseline Distilled+ConceptPrune, the bilevel method still leads by 27% on CSD (39.04 vs. 53.19) and 20% on FID (22.24 vs. 27.86), indicating that the unified bilevel formulation captures interdependencies that sequential fine-tuning-then-unlearning cannot. On NSFW removal (Table 2), the bilevel method is competitive with ConceptPrune on adversarial robustness (MMA 91.60 vs. 94.12; Ring-A-Bell 94.32 vs. 97.72) but achieves substantially better generation quality (FID 26.80 vs. 29.56, CLIP 29.94 vs. 29.45), offering a more favorable safety-quality trade-off. The reference Stable Diffusion 2.1 model (FID 15.11) sets the upper bound on generation quality, and the bilevel method comes closest to it (FID 22.24) among all unlearning methods, whereas two-stage approaches degrade FID by 40–70% relative to the distilled baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) pruning and distillation for efficient diffusion models (APTP, SPDM, BK-SDM) and (2) concept unlearning/editing in diffusion models (ESD, UCE, ConceptPrune). Prior work treats these as sequential stages—first restore generative quality via distillation, then apply an unlearning method—while this paper identifies the circular dependency between the two and reformulates the problem as a bilevel optimization, building on first-order penalty methods for bilevel problems [35] and the KL-divergence-based unlearning objective from [23]. The key conceptual departure is showing that distillation itself propagates unwanted concepts from the teacher to the student even when they are absent from the fine-tuning data, making the joint formulation necessary rather than merely convenient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in bilevel framework offers a practical path to deploying pruned diffusion models in safety-critical or resource-constrained settings (mobile, enterprise) where both efficiency and content control are required. The finding that distillation silently transfers unwanted behaviors challenges the common assumption that filtering the fine-tuning dataset is sufficient for safety, which has implications for how practitioners design fine-tuning pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continual unlearning of multiple concepts, whether more powerful upper-level unlearning methods yield further gains, and whether the bilevel formulation generalizes beyond the single base model (SD 2.1) and single pruning strategy (APTP) tested here.
