## Problem

Stereo conversion via diffusion models lacks both large-scale training data and a standardized benchmark, leaving the optimal use of DMs (one-stage vs. two-stage) and the accurate evaluation of stereoscopic effects largely unexplored. Existing metrics like RMSE and SSIM measure global pixel differences and fail to capture the subtle disparity regions that actually drive stereo perception, as demonstrated by an identity-mapping model scoring higher than a commercial converter on these metrics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular left-view RGB image (960×540 in the dataset, resized to 640×480 during training) and produces the corresponding right-view stereo image. In the dual-condition variant, the left-view image is VAE-encoded to form a geometric condition, while a disparity-warped version of the left-view (using DepthAnything) forms a viewpoint condition; both latents are concatenated with the noisy right-view latent as UNet input. Inference uses DDIM with 50 steps in latent space, avoiding repeated decoding during training.

## Method

The core model is a dual-condition latent diffusion framework initialized from Stable Diffusion V2, where the UNet receives three concatenated channels: the geometric condition (left-view VAE latent), the viewpoint condition (disparity-warped left-view VAE latent), and the noisy right-view latent. This design lets the model exploit complete structural information for image quality while receiving explicit parallax cues for stereo effect, resolving the trade-off between one-stage and two-stage paradigms. To combat the identity-mapping degradation caused by the small left-right difference, an Edge Consistency loss applies Sobel operators to the velocity predictions in latent space, penalizing edge mismatches without requiring forward passes through the decoder. The paper also introduces SIoU, a metric combining Canny-edge IoU (weight 0.75) and binarized left-right difference-map IoU (weight 0.25), which achieves 0.84 Spearman correlation with human stereo-quality judgments versus ≤0.26 for traditional metrics.

## Results

On the Mono2Stereo benchmark, the proposed Dual Condition model achieves the best SIoU of 0.2836 (stereo quality) while also attaining the lowest RMSE of 5.34 and highest PSNR of 34.09 among all generative methods, demonstrating simultaneous superiority in both stereo effect and image fidelity. On the out-of-domain Inria 3DMovie dataset, the Dual Condition model reaches SIoU 0.3398 and RMSE 6.11, again leading all baselines on both stereo and image quality metrics. The identity mapping baseline (IDM) scores high on traditional metrics (RMSE 5.07, SSIM 0.788) but yields a near-zero stereo effect (SIoU 0.1644), highlighting the inadequacy of conventional metrics for stereo evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Dual Condition model outperforms all four external baselines on both datasets across every metric. Against StereoDiffusion [45] (training-free, SIoU 0.2375 on Mono2Stereo), the proposed method gains +0.046 SIoU while reducing RMSE from 7.42 to 5.34, showing that data-driven training substantially improves both stereo accuracy and image fidelity. Compared to the commercial OWL3D [1] (SIoU 0.2781, RMSE 5.81), the Dual Condition model achieves a modest SIoU improvement (+0.0055) and a notable RMSE reduction (5.34 vs. 5.81), indicating better overall image quality at comparable stereo quality. The two-stage inpainting baselines—3D Photography [41] and RePaint [26]—suffer severe image degradation (SSIM 0.285 and 0.526 respectively) due to occlusion artifacts from warping, while the proposed method maintains SSIM above 0.79. On the out-of-domain Inria 3DMovie set, the Dual Condition model extends its lead, achieving SIoU 0.3398 vs. 0.2862 for OWL3D and 0.2672 for StereoDiffusion, confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Marigold framework [17] for repurposing pretrained latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion V2) in a conditional denoising setting, but redirects it from depth estimation to stereo conversion. It departs from the training-free StereoDiffusion [45] by adopting a fully data-driven approach with 2.4M stereo pairs, and from the warp-and-inpaint pipeline (e.g., RePaint, 3D Photography) by unifying geometric and viewpoint conditioning in a single dual-condition UNet. The Edge Consistency loss is a novel regularization that operates in latent velocity space rather than pixel space, distinguishing it from prior inpainting or warping corrections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Mono2Stereo dataset (2.4M pairs, five scene categories) and the SIoU metric fill a critical gap: no large-scale public benchmark or stereo-specific evaluation metric existed before this work. The demonstration that an identity mapping model outperforms a commercial stereo converter on RMSE/PSNR/SSIM is a strong argument that the field's standard evaluation practice is fundamentally misaligned with stereo perception. Open questions remain around video stereo conversion, improving disparity accuracy in complex/indoor scenes, and whether the dual-condition paradigm generalizes to larger camera baselines or multi-view settings beyond the narrow stereo pair.
