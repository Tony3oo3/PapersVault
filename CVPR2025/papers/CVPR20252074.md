## Problem

EventPS, the first event-based photometric stereo method, treats each event interval independently and assumes events are triggered at all adjacent occurrence times. This makes it fragile to observation noise, attached shadows, and non-Lambertian reflections (specular highlights, cast shadows), where the independence assumption breaks down and outliers cannot be reliably identified or removed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a time-series event stream recorded by a Prophesee EVK event camera under a circularly moving ring light (1 Hz, 60 LEDs), along with per-pixel calibrated event thresholds and the known light trajectory. It produces a pixel-wise surface normal map; for objects without known geometry, depth is subsequently recovered via Bilateral Normal Integration. The method requires 30 averaged measurement cycles to suppress statistical threshold noise and operates entirely at the pixel level without any learned components.

## Method

PS-EIP constructs an Event Interval Profile (EIP) at each pixel by approximating the time derivative of logarithmic intensity via central finite differences of consecutive event intervals, rather than solving each interval's linear constraint independently as in EventPS. The ideal EIP shape is analytically determined by the surface normal and the known light trajectory, so normals are recovered by minimizing the L2 residual between the reconstructed and ideal profiles (Eq. 11). Robustness is achieved through a rule-based outlier detection scheme that inspects the profile shape: a collapsed-events mask retains only the region between the top and bottom peaks, while specularity and cast-shadow masks exclude neighborhoods around characteristic positive-negative or negative-positive peak pairs, respectively. An offset light is added to the illumination to compensate for the non-ideal low-input behavior of the logarithmic amplifier, modifying the EIP formula to include a constant radiance floor. The entire pipeline is physics-based and optimization-driven, requiring no deep learning, yet achieves lower mean angular error than the deep-learning variant EventPS-FCN (8.12° vs. 13.66° across all 3D-printed test objects).

## Results

Across 21 3D-printed objects with known ground-truth normals, PS-EIP achieves a mean angular error (MAE) of 8.12°, compared to 13.66° for EventPS-FCN. For sphere-based shapes (8 objects), PS-EIP reduces MAE from 11.87° to 6.72°; for complex shapes (10 objects), from 15.64° to 9.66°. The method achieves these results without any deep learning, relying solely on per-pixel curve fitting of the event interval profile with rule-based outlier masking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is EventPS-FCN [67], the deep-learning variant of the original EventPS method. PS-EIP outperforms EventPS-FCN on every evaluated category: by 5.15° on sphere-based shapes, 5.98° on complex shapes, and 5.54° overall. The advantage is particularly pronounced on objects with strong non-Lambertian effects (glossy highlights, cast shadows from the Pole object, mixed reflectance on MulRefs), where the proposed profile-shape-based masks explicitly remove distorted regions. PS-EIP also shows a notable edge on flat-like shapes (CV, Floral), which the authors attribute to insufficient flat-object coverage in EventPS-FCN's training data. However, PS-EIP still struggles with inter-reflections in concave regions (e.g., Bunny's legs) and with very flat surfaces that produce few events, areas where neither method performs well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends EventPS (Yu et al., CVPR 2024), the first method to perform photometric stereo using event cameras under a moving directional light source. The key conceptual departure is shifting from treating each event interval independently (solving a per-interval linear system) to modeling the full time-series profile of event intervals as the time derivative of logarithmic intensity, thereby exploiting temporal continuity. Within the broader photometric stereo lineage, the paper follows the physics-based tradition of Woodham's Lambertian model and the outlier-removal strategy common in robust photometric stereo, but applies it in the event-camera domain where the signal structure (asynchronous, threshold-triggered) differs fundamentally from frame-based observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a simple pixel-wise, non-deep-learning method outperforms a learned variant (EventPS-FCN) in robustness to non-Lambertian effects challenges the assumption that learned representations are necessary for handling specularities and shadows in this setting. However, the method remains constrained by its distant-light assumption, sensitivity to inter-reflections and subsurface scattering, and the need for 30 measurement cycles to average out threshold noise. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to near-light conditions, arbitrary (non-circular) trajectories, and real-world scenes with ambient illumination, and whether the empirically designed profile-shape masks can be replaced by adaptive or learned mechanisms.
