## Problem

Existing RAW image processing methods treat denoising and reconstruction as separate sequential stages, assuming a unidirectional dependency and overlooking their intrinsic equivalence and complementarity in signal recovery and visual perception. While multi-task approaches exist, they are computationally intensive and fail to exploit task associativity within a broader shared parameter space, leading to suboptimal trade-offs between noise suppression and color/detail reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251503/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes low-light RAW images in Bayer format (converted to RGBG) as input and simultaneously produces a denoised RAW image and a reconstructed RGB image. A key design property is that during inference, either the denoising or reconstruction head can be completely discarded without incurring additional computational burden compared to single-task methods, making the architecture flexible for deployment in either task alone.

## Method

CANS reformulates denoising and reconstruction from a 'backbone-head' perspective: a shared U-shaped backbone integrates a Chromaticity-and-Noise Perception Module (comprising a Global Chromatic Perceptor for global color patterns via pooled attention and a Refined Detail Extractor for local detail via spatial gating), while two lightweight task-specific heads (denoising and reconstruction, the latter using Pixel Shuffle for resolution enhancement) perform minimal-parameter adaptation. The CANS++ variant adds a dual-head interaction module using cross-attention, where shared key and value features are generated by interaction parameters θ_c and task-specific queries are computed by head-specific parameters, enabling dynamic information exchange and adaptive weight balancing between the two tasks. Training jointly optimizes both L1 losses under a single parameter set {θ_b, θ_n, θ_m}, allowing the backbone to learn generalized representations integrating chromaticity-aware and noise-sensitive features through co-evolving gradient signals.

## Results

On the Sony dataset (reconstruction), CANS++ achieves 30.856 dB PSNR, 0.799 SSIM, 0.163 LPIPS, 0.129 DISTS, and 0.965 FSIM, outperforming the previous best (DNF) by 0.24 dB in PSNR. On the MCR dataset, CANS++ reaches 33.090 dB PSNR, surpassing the MCR method itself by 0.972 dB. For denoising at ×100/×250/×300 noise ratios, CANS++ achieves 42.373/40.503/37.548 dB PSNR, consistently leading all baselines. In terms of efficiency, CANS++ requires only 40.66G FLOPs and 4.93M parameters, substantially fewer than DNF (59.96G, 2.83M) and EEM (715.6G, 40.7M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CANS++ outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. On Sony reconstruction, it leads DNF (the strongest prior method) by 0.24 dB PSNR and 0.002 SSIM, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.163 vs. 0.169) and DISTS (0.129 vs. 0.136). The gap widens substantially on the MCR dataset, where CANS++ reaches 33.090 dB PSNR versus MCR's 32.118 dB (+0.972 dB) and DNF's 32.007 dB (+1.083 dB). On Fuji and LRD, CANS++ similarly edges out DNF by 0.189 dB and 0.253 dB PSNR respectively. For denoising, CANS++ dominates at all three noise ratios (×100, ×250, ×300), beating DNF by 0.246 dB at ×100 and 1.007 dB at ×250. Notably, CANS++ achieves these gains with fewer FLOPs (40.66G) than DNF (59.96G) and far fewer than EEM (715.6G), confirming that the backbone-head design is both more accurate and more efficient than cascade or multi-task alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'divide-and-conquer' paradigm in RAW image processing, where denoising and reconstruction are treated as sequential stages (denoise first, then reconstruct) as in EEM, REENet, LDC, and DNF. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes the backbone-head design pattern from high-level vision tasks (e.g., object detection) to RAW processing, replacing the cascade with a shared U-shaped backbone and lightweight task-specific heads. The dual-head cross-attention interaction in CANS++ draws on transformer attention mechanisms to model inter-task correspondence, going beyond the feature-level coupling used in DNF's decouple-and-feedback design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The backbone-head reframing offers a clean, generalizable architecture for jointly handling denoising and reconstruction with lower FLOPs (39.34G vs. 59.96G for DNF) while matching or exceeding prior SOTA, and the demonstrated plug-in flexibility (improving SGN and SID by ~0.85–0.92 dB) suggests the chromaticity-and-noise perception module could benefit other RAW pipelines. However, the absolute gains over the strongest baseline (DNF) are modest (≤0.42 dB PSNR on reconstruction), and the paper does not explore whether the backbone-head paradigm extends to other multi-task ISP problems (e.g., demosaicing + white balance). Open questions include whether the dual-head interaction is essential or merely adds capacity, and how the approach scales to higher-resolution or video RAW processing.
