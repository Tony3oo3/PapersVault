## Problem

Existing continuous space-time video super-resolution (C-STVSR) methods decouple spatial and temporal implicit neural representations, failing to fully exploit spatiotemporal dependencies in the continuous domain and thus performing poorly at out-of-distribution scales. Meanwhile, event cameras—offering high temporal resolution and high dynamic range—remain largely unexplored for C-STVSR, leaving a gap in leveraging their motion cues for accurate inter-frame feature inference under large or non-linear motions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251451/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two low-resolution RGB frames (I_0^LR, I_1^LR) and their corresponding event stream, which is discretized into M spatiotemporal voxel segments with spatial size aligned to the LR frames via bicubic downsampling. It produces (t+1) high-resolution, high-frame-rate video frames at any spatial scale s ≥ 1 and temporal scale t ≥ 1, including non-integer scales, by decoding arbitrary 3D space-time coordinates into RGB values through a unified continuous video INR.

## Method

EvEnhancer comprises two components. The Event-Adapted Synthesis Module (EASM) first performs event-modulated alignment (EMA) via a 3-level pyramid where event features modulate motion vectors at each level, followed by deformable convolution for coarse-to-fine feature alignment in both forward and backward directions; it then applies bidirectional recurrent compensation (BRC) that iteratively fuses event and frame features across time using channel-attention-gated recurrent blocks to model long-term holistic motion trajectories. The Local Implicit Video Transformer (LIVT) replaces the decoupled spatial/temporal INR paradigm with a unified 3D approach: it selects T^G nearest temporal neighbors to the target timestamp, computes cross-scale 3D attention using 3D trilinear-sampled queries against 2D nearest-sampled keys and values over a local spatial grid, and adds spatiotemporal relative-coordinate biases, finally decoding the attended features into RGB via a 5-layer MLP with cell decoding and previous-query concatenation.

## Results

On the in-distribution setting (t=8, s=4), EvEnhancer achieves 33.52 dB PSNR / 0.9295 SSIM on GoPro-Center and 32.43 dB / 0.9129 on Adobe240-Center, surpassing the best event-based C-STVSR baseline HR-INR by ~1.5 dB PSNR with fewer parameters (6.55M vs. 8.27M). On out-of-distribution scales (e.g., t=16, s=4 on GoPro), EvEnhancer reaches 30.93 dB / 0.8918, outperforming HR-INR (27.29 dB) and MoTIF (25.98 dB) by 3.6 and 4.9 dB respectively. On the real-world BS-ERGB dataset (s=4, t=8), EvEnhancer attains 24.29 dB / 0.7138, exceeding the best frame-based C-STVSR method MoTIF by ~1.9 dB. On ALPIX-VSR (2× VSR), EvEnhancer achieves 40.84 dB / 0.9786 without fine-tuning, surpassing EGVSR by 1.5 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvEnhancer outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated dataset and scale. Against the strongest event-based C-STVSR competitor HR-INR, EvEnhancer gains ~1.5 dB PSNR on in-distribution GoPro (33.52 vs. 31.97) while using fewer parameters (6.55M vs. 8.27M); the gap widens dramatically at OOD scales, e.g., 3.6 dB at t=16, s=4 (30.93 vs. 27.29). Compared to the best frame-based C-STVSR methods (MoTIF, VideoINR), EvEnhancer leads by 2.5–4.9 dB at OOD temporal scales, demonstrating that event-driven motion modeling is critical for generalization. Against two-stage event-based pipelines (TimeLens+EGVSR, REFID+EvTexture), EvEnhancer achieves 2.7–5.1 dB improvements with a single unified network and far fewer total parameters. On the real-world BS-ERGB dataset, EvEnhancer surpasses all frame-based C-STVSR methods (MoTIF, VideoINR) by ~1.9–2.1 dB at t=8, confirming the benefit of event-guided holistic motion trajectory modeling. On ALPIX-VSR (2× VSR), EvEnhancer leads EGVSR by 1.5 dB and MoTIF by 2.2 dB without any fine-tuning, validating its robustness to real-world event data.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvEnhancer extends the C-STVSR line initiated by VideoINR (CVPR 2022) and MoTIF (ICCV 2023), which learn decoupled spatial and temporal implicit neural representations. The key architectural departure is the Local Implicit Video Transformer (LIVT), which replaces this decoupling with a unified 3D spatiotemporal attention over a local sampling grid, building on LIIF's (CVPR 2021) local implicit function and cell decoding. On the feature-extraction side, the Event-Adapted Synthesis Module adapts the progressive conditional deformation (PCD) paradigm from EDVR (CVPR-W 2019) by injecting event-modulated motion cues and bidirectional recurrent compensation, drawing on the event-based VFI/VSR literature (TimeLens, EGVSR, EvTexture) that previously addressed only one of the two sub-tasks in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that event streams, with their high temporal resolution, can substantially improve OOD generalization in C-STVSR—achieving 3+ dB PSNR gains over the prior event-based method HR-INR at large temporal scales—while using fewer parameters (6.55M vs. 11–12M for frame-based C-STVSR baselines). This suggests that the decoupled spatial/temporal INR paradigm, which has been the default since VideoINR, may be a limiting factor for cross-scale generalization, and that unified spatiotemporal attention is a more robust alternative. Open questions include how well the approach transfers to longer video sequences, whether the gains persist with noisy real-world events at scale, and whether the unified 3D INR can be extended to other continuous-reconstruction tasks beyond video.
