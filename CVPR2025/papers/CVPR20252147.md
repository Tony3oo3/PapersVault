## Problem

Existing real-time novel view synthesis methods (3DGS, 2DGS, GaussianShader, 3DGS-DR) rely on Spherical Harmonics or environment maps to model view-dependent appearance, which cannot capture high-frequency specular reflection details or near-field reflections caused by nearby objects. The distant-lighting assumption inherent in environment maps further limits their ability to reconstruct accurate reflections in unbounded real-world scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed RGB images and an SfM sparse point cloud as input, and produces a pair of 2D Gaussian primitive sets (base and environment) that render photorealistic novel views at approximately 26 FPS at 1292×839 resolution on an RTX 4090. Training takes about 2.5 hours per scene, and the output representation supports real-time interactive rendering without requiring a neural network at inference time.

## Method

EnvGS introduces a dual-Gaussian representation: base 2D Gaussians encode scene geometry, base color, and a per-primitive blending weight, while a separate set of environment 2D Gaussians explicitly encodes reflection appearance in 3D space. Rendering proceeds in three stages: (1) rasterize base Gaussians to obtain per-pixel surface position, normal, and blending weight; (2) cast a reflection ray from each surface point and trace it against a BVH built from the environment Gaussians using a custom OptiX-based differentiable ray tracer that integrates Gaussian properties chunk-by-chunk via volume rendering; (3) blend base and reflection colors using the learned weight. The environment Gaussians are initialized by uniformly sampling within the scene bounding box and jointly optimized with the base Gaussians end-to-end, with a front-to-back backward pass that re-casts rays to compute gradients with respect to ray origins and directions. Training is supervised by photometric loss (L1 + D-SSIM), a monocular normal consistency loss (StableNormal), a depth-gradient normal loss, and a VGG-16 perceptual loss.

## Results

On the Ref-Real dataset, EnvGS achieves 24.62 PSNR / 0.671 SSIM / 0.241 LPIPS, outperforming all real-time baselines (best real-time competitor 3DGS: 23.70 PSNR) and matching the non-real-time NeRF-Casting (24.67 PSNR) while rendering at 26 FPS versus <0.1 FPS. On NeRF-Casting Shiny Scenes, EnvGS reaches 30.44 PSNR / 0.886 SSIM / 0.148 LPIPS, competitive with ZipNeRF (31.74 PSNR) and NeRF-Casting (31.02 PSNR) at 100× faster rendering speed. Training completes in 2.5 hours on a single RTX 4090, compared to >47 hours for non-real-time NeRF-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnvGS outperforms all real-time Gaussian Splatting baselines by a large margin on both datasets: on Ref-Real it gains +0.82 PSNR over 2DGS (23.80) and +1.09 PSNR over 3DGS (23.70), while on NeRF-Casting Scenes it leads 3DGS by +1.58 PSNR and 2DGS by +2.17 PSNR. Among reflection-specific real-time methods, EnvGS surpasses GaussianShader by +1.74 PSNR (Ref-Real) and +4.03 PSNR (NeRF-Casting), and 3DGS-DR by +1.10 and +1.96 PSNR respectively. Against the strongest non-real-time method NeRF-Casting, EnvGS is within 0.05 PSNR on Ref-Real (24.62 vs 24.67) and 0.58 PSNR on NeRF-Casting Scenes (30.44 vs 31.02), while rendering 100× faster (26 FPS vs <0.1 FPS). On the self-captured Audi scene, EnvGS achieves 28.63 PSNR, beating 3DGS (26.17) and 3DGS-DR (26.09) by over 2 PSNR. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that replacing the environment Gaussian with an environment map (as in GaussianShader/3DGS-DR) degrades PSNR from 24.62 to 24.15, validating the benefit of explicit 3D reflection representation for near-field and high-frequency details.

## Relation to Prior Work

EnvGS builds directly on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) as its geometric base representation and on 3DGS's optimization framework (adaptive densification, D-SSIM loss), while drawing on 3D Gaussian Ray Tracing for the differentiable tracing pipeline. It departs conceptually from prior reflection-aware Gaussian methods (GaussianShader, 3DGS-DR) that augment 3DGS with a 2D environment map and shading functions, replacing the environment map with an explicit set of 3D Gaussian primitives that can represent near-field and high-frequency reflections. It also contrasts with implicit NeRF-based reflection methods (NeRF-Casting, Ref-NeRF) by achieving comparable quality at real-time speeds through explicit representation and GPU RT-core acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EnvGS is the first method to deliver real-time photorealistic specular reflection synthesis in unbounded real-world scenes, closing a significant gap between offline NeRF-based methods and real-time Gaussian splatting. The explicit 3D environment Gaussian representation eliminates the distant-lighting assumption inherent in environment maps, enabling accurate near-field reflections that prior real-time methods cannot capture. A stated limitation is the inability to handle transparent or refractive materials, as the method only models the reflection direction. Open questions include whether the 2.5-hour training time and the requirement for monocular normal supervision (StableNormal) limit practical deployment, and whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes or multi-bounce reflections.
