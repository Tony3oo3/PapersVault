## Problem

Accurate radial distortion calibration typically requires either solving the full Structure from Motion problem (needing many overlapping images, 3D points, and camera poses) or relying on learning-based methods that lack geometric accuracy. This paper demonstrates that distortion estimation can be fully decoupled from 3D reconstruction by operating in projective space, where all non-distortion camera parameters are absorbed into a homography, eliminating the need for explicit 3D points or pose estimation at any stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250992/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered collection of RGB images from one or more physical cameras and produces a single consistent radial distortion polynomial (division camera model coefficients θ) per camera. It is agnostic to the feature-matching backend (SIFT, LOFTR, SuperGlue, etc.) and does not require point tracks across images—only pairwise 2D-2D correspondences. Notably, it does not estimate focal length or principal point as part of the core pipeline; focal length is matched post-hoc for visualization. The method handles extreme 180° fisheye cameras where traditional SfM pipelines often fail entirely.

## Method

PRaDA operates entirely in projective space, where image relationships are expressed via fundamental matrices without 3D points or poses. The pipeline proceeds in five stages: (1) pairwise 2D-2D correspondences are computed for all image pairs; (2) initial one-parameter division-model distortion estimates are obtained per pair via LO-RANSAC with the F10 minimal solver; (3) each pair's distortion is refined to a higher-order k-degree polynomial by minimizing Sampson error with a regularized undistortion function (local linearity constraint); (4) the key novelty—distortion averaging—fuses all pairwise estimates for a given camera into a single consistent polynomial by solving a weighted least-squares problem in function space over the image plane (Eq. 16), initialized by the weighted mean of pairwise coefficients; (5) a global robust Sampson-error optimization jointly refines all distortion polynomials and fundamental matrices across all image pairs. The entire algorithm avoids 3D point creation, bundle adjustment, and focal length estimation, making it applicable to sparse or heavily distorted image sets where SfM-based calibration fails.

## Results

PRaDA achieves the lowest focal-adjusted reprojection error (FA-RE) across all four datasets. On ScanNet++, it reaches a mean FA-RE of 0.6 px (vs. 1.8 for GLOMAP and 2.0 for COLMAP). On the heavily distorted KITTI-360 fisheye cameras, PRaDA achieves mean FA-RE of 44.8 px (cam2) and 50.2 px (cam3), roughly 2.5–3× better than the next-best baseline (DroidCalib at 102.2 and 128.1 px). On WoodScape, PRaDA outperforms GeoCalib by ~10× on side cameras (7.7/9.4 px vs. 93.0/93.9 px), though DeepCalib is slightly better on the forward/backward cameras due to a degenerate geometry. Initializing GLOMAP with PRaDA reduces mean rotation error from 28.99° to 4.51° on sparse ScanNet++ sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRaDA outperforms all five baselines on every dataset in Table 1. Against the SfM-based methods COLMAP and GLOMAP, PRaDA reduces mean FA-RE by roughly 3–5× on ETH3D (5.3 vs. 26.0/18.4 px) and by ~2.5–3× on KITTI-360 (44.8 vs. 125.5/122.0 px), confirming that decoupling distortion from 3D reconstruction avoids the convergence failures of joint bundle adjustment. Against the learning-based methods, PRaDA dominates on KITTI-360 (44.8 px vs. 102.2 for DroidCalib, 125.5 for GeoCalib, 160.5 for DeepCalib) and on the side cameras of WoodScape (7.7/9.4 px vs. 14.5/21.5 for DeepCalib and 93.0/93.9 for GeoCalib). The one exception is WoodScape forward/backward cameras, where DeepCalib (9.7/14.5 px) slightly beats PRaDA (51.2/22.2 px) due to a degenerate geometry where the distortion center coincides with the other camera's projection. Finally, using PRaDA as an initialization for GLOMAP reduces mean relative rotation error from 28.99° to 4.51° and translation error from 27.39° to 8.70° on sparse ScanNet++ sequences, demonstrating that accurate distortion calibration directly improves downstream SfM quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the division camera model lineage from Fitzgibbon (2001) and the F10 minimal solver of Kukelova et al. (2015) for two-view distortion estimation, extending them to higher-order polynomials following Larsson et al. (2019). The key conceptual departure is from both SfM-based calibration (COLMAP, GLOMAP) and learning-based methods (DeepCalib, GeoCalib, DroidCalib): by working entirely in projective space where geometry is defined up to a homography, the authors decouple distortion estimation from 3D reconstruction, eliminating the need for point tracks, camera poses, or focal length at any stage. The regularization term for polynomial distortion is adapted from Pan et al. (2022), and the overall pipeline echoes the global-averaging philosophy of GLOMAP but applied to distortion parameters in functional space rather than to 3D structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone working with heavily distorted imagery (180° fisheye, panoramic cameras) where standard SfM pipelines fail to converge or produce wildly inaccurate calibrations. The distortion averaging in functional space is a clean, generalizable technique that could be adapted to other parameter-estimation problems involving inconsistent pairwise estimates. Limitations include the radially symmetric distortion assumption (preventing subpixel accuracy on non-radial ground truth), dependence on matcher quality in distorted regions, and the use of fixed RANSAC thresholds rather than adaptive consensus. Open questions raised by the authors include whether σ++ consensus can fully replace threshold-based inlier selection, and whether error distributions in distorted regions can be properly modeled to improve the Levenberg-Marquardt refinement.
