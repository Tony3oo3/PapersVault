## Problem

Point-centric 3D perception models in embodied intelligence are constrained by the inherent sparsity, noise, and acquisition difficulty of point clouds (e.g., reflective surfaces, long-range sensing), as well as the high cost of collecting and annotating large-scale 3D data. Meanwhile, 2D vision foundation models have achieved strong semantic understanding and generalization on abundant image data, yet their capabilities have not been effectively transferred to 3D perception tasks.

## Input / Output

BIP3D accepts an arbitrary number of posed RGB images (18 during training, 50 keyframes at test time), a text instruction (one prompt for grounding, up to 128 class names for detection), and optional depth maps, along with per-view camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters. All input images undergo camera intrinsic standardization via an affine warp to a virtual camera before encoding. The model outputs 9-DoF 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, and roll/pitch/yaw) for objects matching the text query, supporting multiple simultaneous text instructions.

## Method

BIP3D builds on GroundingDINO's architecture (Swin-T image backbone, BERT text encoder, 6-layer transformer decoder) and introduces three key modifications. First, a Spatial Enhancer explicitly encodes camera geometry by uniformly sampling 3D points within each view frustum, projecting them to the perception coordinate system, and weighting the resulting point position embeddings with a predicted discrete depth distribution to produce image position embeddings. Second, the decoder replaces 2D deformable attention with 3D deformable aggregation: each query maintains a 3D bounding box, samples M keypoints (learnable offsets plus fixed geometric priors like face centers), projects them onto all N view feature maps, and aggregates features via learned softmax weights with explicit zeroing of out-of-frustum samples. Third, a depth encoding branch (mini-ResNet34) fuses with image features for multi-modal enhancement. Training uses a one-to-one matching loss combining focal classification, L2 center regression, and a simplified Wasserstein distance for orientation-agnostic box regression, with a denoising auxiliary task; detection is trained as category grounding before fine-tuning for referring grounding.

## Results

On the EmbodiedScan 3D detection benchmark, BIP3D achieves an AP₃D@0.25 of 20.91% overall, surpassing the EmbodiedScan baseline (15.22%) by 5.69%. For 3D visual grounding, BIP3D reaches 54.66% AP on the validation set (vs. 39.41% for EmbodiedScan, a 15.25% gain) and 62.08% with model ensemble on the test set, exceeding the CVPR 2024 Challenge winner DenseG (59.59%) by 2.49%. BIP3D also shows strong category generalization (16.03% AP on tail categories vs. 9.48% for EmbodiedScan) and superior small-object detection (5.72% vs. 3.28% maximum for other methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIP3D outperforms all six compared baselines on both tasks. In 3D detection, it surpasses the point-centric VoteNet (5.18%) by 15.73%, the RGB-only ImVoxelNet (8.08%) by 12.83%, the sparse-voxel FCAF3D (13.86%) by 7.05%, and the multi-modal EmbodiedScan baseline (15.22%) by 5.69% in overall AP₃D@0.25. The advantage is most pronounced on tail categories (16.03% vs. 9.48%) and small objects (5.72% vs. 3.28%), reflecting the benefits of dense 2D image features and 2D foundation model pretraining. In 3D visual grounding on the test set, BIP3D (57.05% without ensemble) beats SAG3D (46.92%) by 10.13%, and with model ensemble (62.08%) surpasses the CVPR 2024 Challenge winner DenseG (59.59%) by 2.49%. Notably, even the RGB-only variant of BIP3D (17.40%) exceeds all point-centric baselines, demonstrating that the image-centric architecture itself provides a substantial performance floor.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIP3D departs from the dominant point-centric paradigm in indoor 3D perception (VoteNet, FCAF3D, EmbodiedScan) by centering the architecture on 2D image features rather than 3D point or voxel features. It builds directly on GroundingDINO's 2D open-set detection framework, inheriting its deformable attention decoder and text-image cross-attention, then adapts these components to 3D through a spatial enhancer (inspired by but extending PETR's position embeddings with depth-distribution weighting) and a 3D deformable attention decoder (adapted from Sparse4D's multi-view fusion). The camera intrinsic standardization and Wasserstein-distance box regression loss are novel practical contributions not present in prior 3D detection or grounding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that image-centric 3D perception can substantially outperform point-centric methods (5.69% on detection, 15.25% on grounding), and that the benefit of 2D foundation model pretraining is architecture-dependent—yielding only 1.11% gain for point-centric EmbodiedScan but 5.66–5.99% for BIP3D. This suggests the 2D-to-3D transfer is most effective when the 3D architecture natively operates on image feature maps rather than projecting them into 3D space. Open questions include extending to dynamic scenes, incorporating more perception tasks (segmentation, occupancy, grasp), and whether the approach generalizes beyond indoor settings. The RGB-only capability also opens a path to crowd-sourced data collection, which could be transformative for scaling embodied AI training data.
