## Problem

Vision-language models frequently produce hallucinated image interpretations and cascading reasoning errors, yet existing self-correction and self-refinement approaches rely solely on the model's own internal capabilities without external supervision. The paper addresses how to introduce high-quality, structured external feedback during VLM generation to catch and correct errors in multimodal reasoning paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250329/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a reasoning response from the Reasoner plus a natural-language critique from the Critic. The interaction is iterative: the Critic's critique is appended to the Reasoner's text prompt for the next round, and the process runs for a fixed two-round conversation with temperature set to 0 (or near 0) for stability. Each critique consumes only a few dozen additional tokens, keeping computational overhead minimal.

## Method

Critic-V decouples reasoning from evaluation into two modules: a Reasoner that generates reasoning paths via dynamic text prompts (updated through TextGrad-style gradient computation rather than fixed parameter updates), and a Critic that produces natural-language feedback identifying specific errors. The Critic is trained with DPO on a 29,012-pair preference dataset (critique-VQA) constructed via the Vision Error Insertion Technique (VEST), where GPT-4o inserts 1–5 fake details into ground-truth VQA answers and multiple VLMs generate critiques that are ranked by a Rule-based Reward combining Jaccard similarity between inserted and detected errors with a GPT-4o quality score. At inference, the Reasoner generates an initial answer, the Critic evaluates it against the image and question, and the resulting critique refines the Reasoner's prompt for the next iteration, forming an Actor-Critic loop where the Critic supplies natural-language gradient signals instead of scalar rewards.

## Results

On 8 multimodal reasoning benchmarks, Qwen2-VL-7B+Critic-V achieves the highest score on 5 out of 8 benchmarks, surpassing GPT-4V in most cases. The most dramatic gains appear in mathematical reasoning: MathVista improves by +11.8% (61.4→73.2) for Qwen2-VL-7B and +17.8% (35.3→53.1) for DeepSeek-VL-7B, while MathVerse improves by +7.1% and +10.5% respectively. LLaVA-v1.5-7B+Critic-V also shows substantial gains, with +12.8% on RealWorldQA (50.7→63.5) and +11.4% on MMT-Bench (36.0→47.4). Across 23 of 24 comparative experiments, Critic-V consistently improves over the unmodified baseline models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4V, Qwen2-VL-7B+Critic-V outperforms on RealWorldQA (74.9 vs 61.4), MMBench (82.8 vs 74.3), SEEDBench (76.5 vs 71.6), MMT-Bench (62.0 vs 55.5), MathVista (73.2 vs 49.9), and MathVerse (32.9 vs 54.4 is the sole loss), while GPT-4V retains an edge on MMStar (57.1 vs 56.2) and MathVerse (54.4 vs 32.9). In the head-to-head comparison with four self-correction/preference methods (POVID, CSR, SIMA, SCL) on LLaVA-v1.5-7B, Critic-V wins on 5 of 6 benchmarks, most dramatically on MMT-Bench (49.7 vs 39.6 for SCL, the strongest baseline) and RealWorldQA (63.5 vs 53.2), but loses on ScienceQA (65.2 vs 67.8 for SCL). Compared to Self-Refine (untrained critic), Critic-V achieves substantially larger gains on MathVista (73.2 vs 63.4) and MMT-Bench (62.0 vs 57.8), confirming that DPO-trained critique quality matters. Among the 10+ standalone VLMs in Table 1, Qwen2-VL-7B+Critic-V ranks first on RealWorldQA, MMBench, SEEDBench, MMT-Bench, and MathVista, while GPT-4V remains strongest on MathVerse (54.4) and MiniCPM-V 2.6 leads on ScienceQA (90.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

Critic-V adapts the Actor-Critic paradigm from classical RL to VLM reasoning by replacing scalar rewards with natural language critiques from a separately trained VLM critic. The data generation pipeline (VEST) directly extends CriticGPT's bug-insertion strategy from code to visual QA, while the critic's training leverages DPO rather than policy-gradient with scalar rewards. The Reasoner's prompt-based policy update draws on ICRL and TextGrad, shifting from parameter-space optimization to text-prompt evolution. Conceptually, the paper departs from self-correction methods (Self-Refine, SCL, SIMA, CSR) by introducing an external, preference-optimized critic that provides proactive error identification rather than relying on the model's own self-evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the critic (applicable to any VLM without retraining the base model) and the released 29K-sample critique-VQA dataset make this a practically useful tool for improving VLM reliability in reasoning-heavy applications like autonomous driving and embodied AI. However, the evaluation is limited to 7B-scale models and a fixed two-round interaction, leaving open whether the approach scales to longer reasoning chains or larger models. The MMStar regression for Qwen2-VL-7B (−4.5) and the modest gains on some benchmarks suggest the critic's benefit is task-dependent, and it remains unclear how the framework performs when the base model already has strong internal self-correction capabilities.
