## Problem

Existing zero-shot scene graph generation models struggle to predict unseen predicate triplets because they focus on invariant features while neglecting the equivariant transformations between seen and unseen predicates in high-dimensional feature space. The paper hypothesizes that unseen predicates are transformations of seen predicates, and that bridging this transformation gap requires explicitly modeling predicate equivariance rather than relying solely on invariance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252419/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, from which a pre-trained Faster-RCNN (ResNeXt-101-RPN) extracts object bounding boxes and visual features. Three modality-specific feature streams are then constructed: visual features from the detector, semantic features via GloVe word embeddings of object labels, and spatial features from a Relative Spatial Encoder. The output is a scene graph consisting of subject-object-predicate triplets with bounding boxes, object class labels, and predicate class labels, evaluated under both graph-constrained (one predicate per pair) and unconstrained (multiple predicates per pair) settings.

## Method

CAPSGG introduces a Three-Stream Pipeline that processes visual, semantic, and spatial predicate features independently before feeding them into a Capsule Feature Encoder (CFE). The CFE replaces the standard 2D convolution in CapsNet with a single-layer MLP and LeakyReLU to form primary predicate capsules per modality, then aggregates them via a Routing Capsule Layer that uses Self-Attention routing (non-iterative, parallelizable) instead of the original dynamic routing, with a squash activation constraining capsule vector lengths to [0,1] to encode predicate existence probability. A novel GroupLoss replaces the global MarginLoss by grouping capsules sharing the same predicate label and computing intra-group loss, which balances learning of invariant (group-level) and equivariant (individual capsule) features while mitigating the long-tail predicate distribution. The key architectural insight is that capsule vectors encode predicate transformations equivariantly on a spatial manifold, allowing the model to generalize from seen to unseen predicate compositions through consistent responses to feature transformations.

## Results

On the Visual Genome dataset (VG150 split), CAPSGG achieves zR@50 of 51.7 on PredCls, 28.8 on SGCls, and 12.5 on SGDet (without graph constraint), outperforming all 13 baselines across all three tasks. The headline result is a 132.26% improvement over the strongest baseline T-CAR on SGCls zR@50 (28.8 vs. 12.4), and a 158.33% improvement on SGDet zR@20 (9.3 vs. 3.6). With graph constraint (Table 2), CAPSGG similarly leads, achieving zR@50/100 of 37.7/46.1 on PredCls and 20.0/24.3 on SGCls.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAPSGG outperforms all 13 baselines across all three SGG tasks (PredCls, SGCls, SGDet) on both zero-shot metrics (zR@K) and constrained metrics (R@K). The most dramatic gains appear on SGCls and SGDet, where the gap between CAPSGG and the strongest baseline T-CAR [21] reaches 132.26% (zR@50: 28.8 vs. 12.4) and 158.33% (zR@20: 9.3 vs. 3.6) respectively, suggesting that equivariant capsule features are particularly critical when the model must predict full triplets rather than just predicates. On PredCls, where bounding boxes and object labels are given, the improvement over T-CAR is more modest (6.93% on zR@20), indicating that the equivariant modeling contributes most when spatial and compositional reasoning is required. Among the zero-shot-specific baselines, KGC* [42] and RGN* [43] are the closest competitors on PredCls (zR@100: 41.3 and 41.1 vs. CAPSGG's 68.0), while EBM [32] leads among general SGG methods. The paper also notes a 'metric inversion' phenomenon where over-weighting negative classes (high λ) degrades unconstrained performance relative to constrained performance, a trade-off not observed in prior baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot SGG line initiated by Lu et al. and advanced by T-CAR (Li et al., 2023) by reframing the core challenge as one of predicate equivariance rather than invariance. It repurposes the CapsNet architecture (Sabour et al., 2017) with self-attention routing (Mazzia et al., 2021) as a higher-dimensional feature encoder for predicates, replacing the 2D convolution front-end with an MLP to handle structured triplet inputs. Conceptually, it departs from T-CAR's dual-branch (visual + spatial) design by adding a semantic/text branch and arguing—contrary to T-CAR—that text features are beneficial when the model is trained to capture equivariant rather than purely invariant predicate representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivariant-features framing offers a principled explanation for why prior zero-shot SGG models struggle: they optimize for invariance and thus cannot generalize across predicate transformations. The 'metric inversion' phenomenon (over-weighting negative classes degrades unconstrained recall) is a practical warning for anyone tuning MarginLoss-style objectives. However, all experiments are confined to the VG150 split with 50 predicate categories, leaving open whether the capsule-based equivariant encoding generalizes to larger predicate taxonomies, other datasets, or non-visual modalities. The approach also inherits the dependency on a frozen Faster-RCNN detector, so end-to-end gains remain untested.
