## Problem

Gradient-based jailbreak attacks on Vision-Language Models assume that minimizing cross-entropy loss yields the most effective adversarial image, and that a single arbitrary image suffices for all attack scenarios. This paper empirically challenges both assumptions, showing that minimal-loss images do not guarantee successful attacks and that semantic mismatch between the image and the target scenario significantly reduces attack effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251717/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful text instruction (drawn from SafetyBench's 13 prohibited scenarios or AdvBench's 521 harmful behaviors) and produces a set of multiple adversarial RGB images used collaboratively in a single query. Initial images are generated at standard resolution via DALL-E 3 using scenario-specific prompts, then refined under an L∞ perturbation budget of 32/255. The final output is not a single image but a curated subset of images whose losses fall within a computed range Θ, all fed simultaneously to the VLM.

## Method

MLAI is a three-stage jailbreak framework. First, it generates scenario-aware initial images via DALL-E 3 using prompts that encode the semantic context of the target jailbreak category (e.g., business meetings for fraud) while avoiding direct harmful depiction, establishing strong semantic alignment with the attack target. Second, it performs gradient-based optimization to minimize cross-entropy loss against a corpus of harmful target responses, saving intermediate images throughout the iterative process rather than only the final minimum. Third, it applies flat minima theory to determine an optimal loss range Θ by fitting local slopes around the minimum loss point and selecting a band of images within that range; these multiple images are then used collaboratively in a single VLM query. The key insight is that images in flat regions of the loss landscape are more robust to test-time distribution shifts than those at sharp minima, and that combining several such images amplifies the attack beyond what any single image achieves.

## Results

MLAI achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 77.75% on MiniGPT-4 and 82.80% on LLaVA-2 across 13 harmful scenarios, substantially outperforming the best prior method (Liu et al.) by 34.37% and 12.77% respectively. On MiniGPT-4, MLAI reaches 100% ASR on Financial Advice and 97.85% on Gov Decision, while on LLaVA-2 it achieves 100% on Health Consultation and Gov Decision. For black-box transferability, adversarial images trained on MiniGPT-4 achieve ASRs of 49.59% (Gemini), 60.11% (ChatGLM), and 54.97% (Qwen) on three high-risk scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLAI is compared against two state-of-the-art white-box jailbreak methods: Liu et al. [21], which uses query-relevant images with aggressive intent alongside typographic text, and Qi et al. [29], which refines visual adversarial prompts using scenario-specific corpora. On MiniGPT-4, MLAI (77.75% avg ASR) outperforms Liu et al. (43.38%) by 34.37% and Qi et al. (41.56%) by 36.19%, with the largest per-scenario gains in Illegal Activity (+55.70% over Liu) and Privacy Violence (+68.42% over Liu). On LLaVA-2, MLAI (82.80%) surpasses Liu et al. (70.03%) by 12.77% and Qi et al. (58.83%) by 23.97%, though the margin is smaller because Liu et al. already achieves high ASR on several scenarios (e.g., 74.77% on Illegal Activity). MLAI wins in 12 out of 13 scenarios on both models; the sole exception is Health Consultation on LLaVA-2 where Liu et al. reaches 100% ASR, matching MLAI's 100%. The Plain Text baseline (no adversarial image) confirms that visual inputs are the primary attack vector, with base ASR ranging from 1.68% to 90.29% depending on scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based adversarial image optimization paradigm for VLM jailbreaking (Liu et al. [21], Qi et al. [29], BAP [37]) by challenging its core assumption that minimizing cross-entropy loss yields the most effective adversarial image. It repurposes flat minima theory from optimization [12] to argue that a range of moderately-lossy images outperforms the single minimum-loss solution, and introduces scenario-aware image generation via DALL-E 3 to establish semantic alignment between visual content and target harmful scenarios. The multi-image collaborative attack strategy departs from prior single-image or single-loss approaches, positioning the work as a methodological refinement within the established VLM jailbreak literature rather than a new attack category.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated transferability to commercial black-box VLMs (up to 60.11% ASR on ChatGLM) reveals that fundamental visual vulnerabilities in safety alignment transcend specific architectures and training pipelines, raising urgent deployment concerns. The proposed similarity-based deduplication defense, while effective (22.99% ASR reduction), is relatively simple and may be circumvented by more sophisticated multi-loss strategies. Open questions include whether the flat-minima insight generalizes to future VLM architectures with stronger safety training, whether the 13-scenario taxonomy captures the full attack surface, and whether more robust defenses (e.g., input-space regularization or adversarial training on the visual modality) can close the gap without degrading legitimate multimodal performance.
