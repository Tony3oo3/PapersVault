## Problem

Existing human-centric volumetric video methods are split into two camps: playback-only approaches that faithfully replay recorded scenes but cannot generalize to unseen motions, and animatable avatar methods that handle novel motions but are restricted to human-only scenes with parametric models like SMPL. No prior work unifies high-fidelity playback of general non-rigid scenes (including human-object interactions) with photoreal re-performance under similar yet novel motions from limited motion examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252757/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes dense multi-view video (up to 81 views) of a dynamic scene, plus a separate driving motion sequence for re-performance, and optional text prompts (e.g., 'right hand') for semantic alignment. Appearance Gaussians are stored as 512×512×3 2D position maps via Morton ordering, with attribute maps (rotation, opacity, scaling, SH coefficients) regressed by three U-Nets. The output is photorealistic volumetric rendering for both original playback and re-performed novel motions, assembled from dense appearance Gaussians (~200K) warped by sparse motion Gaussians (~50K).

## Method

RePerformer hierarchically disentangles the dynamic scene into sparse motion Gaussians (optimized per-frame with ARAP constraints for topology-consistent tracking) and dense appearance Gaussians (trained in canonical space without mesh constraints). A Morton-based 3D-to-2D parameterization projects warped appearance Gaussians into 2D position maps preserving spatial adjacency, which are fed into three specialized U-Nets (Rotation, Shape, Color) with a self-attention layer to regress Gaussian attributes, enabling generalization beyond the training sequence. For re-performance, a semantic-aware alignment module uses GroundingDINO and SAM2 to assign semantic labels to Gaussians, then k-means clustering aligns cluster centroids between source and target, followed by deformation transfer on motion Gaussians with ARAP regularization to preserve topology while matching the driving motion.

## Results

On the DualGS dataset (500-frame sequences, 8 test views), RePerformer achieves PSNR 34.571, SSIM 0.986, and LPIPS 0.0229 for novel-view playback, ranking second only to per-frame-optimized DualGS (PSNR 35.506) while training 7.3× faster (1.68 vs. 12.22 min/frame). For the novel-motion re-performance setting (3,000-frame sequence, trained on frames 1–2500, tested on 2500–3000), RePerformer achieves PSNR 30.06, SSIM 0.976, and LPIPS 0.0277, outperforming all template-free baselines by a wide margin (e.g., +10.88 PSNR over SC-GS on novel motion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the playback (novel-view) setting (Table 1), RePerformer trails DualGS by ~0.94 dB PSNR and 0.004 SSIM but trains 7.3× faster (1.68 vs. 12.22 min/frame); it outperforms V3 by 0.75 dB PSNR and Spacetime Gaussian by 2.88 dB PSNR, while NeuS2 lags by ~5 dB PSNR. In the re-performance (novel-motion) setting (Table 2), RePerformer dominates all three template-free baselines: it beats SC-GS by +10.88 dB PSNR on novel motion (30.06 vs. 19.18), AP-NeRF by +3.21 dB (30.06 vs. 26.85), and TAVA by +8.72 dB (30.06 vs. 21.34). SC-GS is the closest competitor on novel views (30.63 vs. 32.09 PSNR) but degrades severely under novel poses, dropping 11.45 dB from its novel-view score. TAVA and AP-NeRF struggle with both novel views and novel motions, with TAVA showing the weakest performance across all metrics. The key takeaway is that no playback-only method (DualGS, V3, Spacetime Gaussian, NeuS2) can handle novel motions at all, making Table 2 the decisive comparison for the paper's core contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

RePerformer directly extends DualGS [27], which introduced the dual-Gaussian (motion + appearance) decomposition for human-centric volumetric video, by replacing DualGS's per-frame gradient-descent optimization of appearance Gaussians with a generalizable 2D CNN (U-Net) that regresses attributes from Morton-ordered position maps. The motion tracking stage draws on Dynamic 3D Gaussians [44] and SoG [65] for sparse Gaussian-based non-rigid tracking, while the re-performance pipeline extends Sumner & Popović's classical deformation transfer [68] from triangle meshes to Gaussian point clouds. The paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: playback-only methods (DualGS, Spacetime Gaussian, V3) that achieve high fidelity but cannot generalize to new motions, and animatable avatar methods (TAVA, SC-GS, AP-NeRF) that generalize but are restricted to human-only scenes with parametric models like SMPL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying high-fidelity playback and photoreal re-performance for general non-rigid scenes (including human-object interactions like violin playing or balloon manipulation), RePerformer opens a practical pathway for applications such as transferring a professional performer's motion to an unskilled actor. Key limitations remain: the 20-second-per-frame motion tracking is far from real-time, re-performance quality depends heavily on the driving motion being similar in type to the original, and the single self-attention layer (due to GPU memory) likely caps generalization. Open questions include whether the Morton-based 2D parameterization scales to much larger or more topologically complex scenes, whether the tracking stage can be accelerated (e.g., via learned predictors), and whether the semantic alignment module can handle truly arbitrary motion types without manual text prompts.
