## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary segmentation methods rely on mask pooling or mask cropping to extract mask embeddings from CLIP features, but both approaches have a fundamentally limited upper bound: mask pooling captures only positional information without semantic or contextual detail, while mask cropping introduces a domain gap between masked images and the natural images CLIP was pretrained on. The paper demonstrates that even with ground-truth masks, these methods fail to achieve accurate classification, revealing a critical misalignment between segmentation masks and CLIP's feature space.

## Input / Output

The Mask-Adapter takes N class-agnostic binary proposal masks (N×H×W) from a frozen mask generator (e.g., Mask2Former) and CLIP visual features at matching resolution as input. Masks are patchified via two strided 3×3 convolutions to align spatially with CLIP features, and the module outputs K=16 semantic activation maps per mask, which are then used to aggregate CLIP features into mask embeddings (N×C). These embeddings are matched against text embeddings for classification; during training, the module is the only trainable component while CLIP and the mask generator remain frozen.

## Method

Mask-Adapter fuses patchified mask features with CLIP features via element-wise addition, processes the result through three ConvNeXt blocks to enhance semantic representation, and outputs K semantic activation maps via a final convolutional layer. Unlike mask pooling, which simply dot-products the binary mask with CLIP features, the semantic activation maps selectively highlight discriminative regions and incorporate contextual information beyond the mask boundary, yielding richer mask embeddings. Training employs a two-stage strategy: a ground-truth mask warmup phase (20 epochs) followed by mixed-mask training (10 epochs) combining predicted and GT masks. An IoU-based matcher (threshold 0.7) replaces the Hungarian matcher to incorporate previously misclassified samples and reduce overfitting, while a cosine-similarity mask consistency loss enforces that masks with similar IoU produce similar CLIP embeddings, improving robustness to prediction variance.

## Results

On the ADE20K A-150 benchmark (the most challenging open-vocabulary segmentation setting), MAFTP w/ Mask-Adapter achieves 38.2 mIoU with CLIP ConvNeXt-L, outperforming the previous best CAT-Seg (37.9) and MROVSeg (36.9). Without ensemble, Mask-Adapter boosts FC-CLIP from 26.5 to 35.4 mIoU (+8.9) and Mask2Former from 26.0 to 34.0 mIoU (+8.0) on ADE20K. For mask embedding extraction with ground-truth masks, Mask-Adapter reaches 74.1% classification accuracy on ADE20K, surpassing mask pooling (53.0%) and mask cropping (40.1%) by 20.5 and 34.0 points respectively. When integrated into SAM without any training, Mask-Adapter improves A-847 mIoU from 10.1 (SAM w/ MAFT) to 11.4 and A-150 from 29.1 to 31.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With CLIP ConvNeXt-L, MAFTP w/ Mask-Adapter achieves the best A-150 (38.2) and A-847 (16.2) mIoU, edging out CAT-Seg (37.9 / 16.0) and MROVSeg (36.9 / 16.1), though it trails MROVSeg on PC-59 (60.4 vs. 64.1) and PC-459 (22.7 vs. 24.1). FC-CLIP w/ Mask-Adapter improves over the FC-CLIP baseline by +2.5 mIoU on A-150 (36.6 vs. 34.1) and +1.1 on PC-459 (19.3 vs. 18.2), but slightly underperforms on A-847 (14.1 vs. 14.8). Without ensemble, Mask-Adapter delivers the largest relative gains on weaker baselines: Mask2Former improves by +8.0 mIoU (26.0→34.0) and FC-CLIP by +8.9 (26.5→35.4), while the already-strong MAFTP sees a more modest +1.4 (36.3→37.7). Against mask-pooling and mask-cropping baselines in the upper-bound analysis (Table 3), Mask-Adapter with the same ConvNeXt-L backbone outperforms mask pooling by 13.7 points (66.7 vs. 53.0) and mask cropping by 26.6 points (66.7 vs. 40.1). When plugged into SAM without any training, Mask-Adapter surpasses SAM w/ MAFT on A-847 (11.4 vs. 10.1) and A-150 (31.5 vs. 29.1) but falls short on PC-59 (49.5 vs. 53.5), suggesting the advantage is most pronounced on the harder ADE20K categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the segment-then-recognize paradigm for open-vocabulary segmentation, building directly on mask-pooling-based frameworks like FC-CLIP and MAFTP, which aggregate CLIP features within proposal masks for classification. It departs from both mask cropping (ZegFormer, SimpleBaseline) and mask pooling (OpenSeg, FC-CLIP) by introducing semantic activation maps—inspired by SparseInst's activation-based instance segmentation—to selectively highlight informative regions and incorporate full-image context. The IoU-based matcher and mask consistency loss represent a departure from the standard Hungarian matching and cross-entropy-only training used in prior mask classification methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means Mask-Adapter can be dropped into any existing mask-pooling pipeline or even SAM without retraining the mask generator, lowering the barrier for practitioners to improve their open-vocabulary segmentation systems. The upper-bound analysis (Fig. 1, Table 3) provides a useful diagnostic: even with ground-truth masks, mask pooling caps out well below Mask-Adapter's accuracy, suggesting the field has been underestimating how much information is lost by simple positional aggregation. However, the gains over the strongest baselines (MAFTP) are modest (1.1–1.5 mIoU), and the method still relies on a frozen CLIP backbone, leaving open whether jointly adapting the vision-language model could yield larger improvements.
