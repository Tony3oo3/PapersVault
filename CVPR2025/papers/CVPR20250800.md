## Problem

Existing video personalization methods support only a single reference concept (one face or one object), and naively extending them to multiple references by concatenating vision tokens causes severe identity blending, where the model fuses attributes from different reference images into composite characters. This occurs because cross-attention is order-agnostic and lacks any mechanism to associate each concept description in the prompt with its corresponding reference image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250800/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an ordered set of reference images (face, body, and/or animal crops) in one of five supported configurations (face, face-body, face-body-animal, two-face, two-face-body) along with an anchored text prompt where unique tokens like [R1], [R2] are appended after each concept description. It produces a 128-frame, 544×960 personalized video in which all referenced identities are simultaneously preserved without any per-concept fine-tuning. Notably, the prompt contains no explicit appearance descriptions (e.g., hair color, clothing); all identity information is derived solely from the reference images.

## Method

Movie Weaver builds on the 30B-parameter Movie Gen diffusion transformer and introduces two lightweight mechanisms that require no architectural changes beyond the existing cross-attention layer. First, anchored prompts inject unique tokens (e.g., [R1], [R2]) after each concept description in the text prompt, so that when the model encounters [R1] it retrieves the matching reference image as visual context for that specific concept. Second, concept embeddings add a single learnable vector uniformly to all vision tokens originating from the same reference image, encoding the image's position in the reference sequence and distinguishing it from other references (unlike per-token positional encoding, which the authors found less effective). The model is trained via flow matching with optimal transport paths, initialized from a pre-trained single-face Movie Gen checkpoint and continued on 230K curated Shutterstock videos across all five configurations, using an automatic data curation pipeline that leverages Llama-3 for prompt rewriting, Detic/SAM for subject detection and segmentation, and CLIP for linking concept descriptions to the correct reference images.

## Results

On the single-face video personalization benchmark (97 samples), Movie Weaver achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CLIP-T 0.293, CLIP-I 0.659, DINO-I 0.421, and Temporal Consistency 0.997. In the multi-concept setting, a human study on 300 two-face pairs shows that combining Anchored Prompts and Concept Embeddings raises the face-separability score (sep_yes) from 42.9% (baseline) to 99.3%, and increases face1_sim and face2_sim from 3.4/3.0 to 66.8/66.1. Qualitative comparison against the proprietary Vidu 1.5 demonstrates superior identity preservation for both human and animal references, with notably less identity blending in same-gender, same-race two-face scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ID-Animator, Movie Weaver wins on all four metrics, with the largest gap in CLIP-I (0.659 vs. 0.642) and Temporal Consistency (0.997 vs. 0.985), while the CLIP-T advantage is modest (0.293 vs. 0.274). Against DreamVideo, the margins are substantially larger, particularly in DINO-I (0.421 vs. 0.246, a 71% relative improvement) and CLIP-I (0.659 vs. 0.498), indicating far stronger identity fidelity. Against the proprietary Vidu 1.5, the comparison is qualitative: Movie Weaver preserves distinctive features (e.g., white spots on a Corgi's face, clothing logos) that Vidu 1.5 fails to reconstruct, and avoids the severe identity blending that Vidu 1.5 exhibits when generating two characters of the same gender and race. Overall, Movie Weaver outperforms all three baselines, with the most dramatic gains over DreamVideo and the most practically meaningful advantage over Vidu 1.5 in multi-concept identity separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Movie Weaver extends the tuning-free video personalization paradigm established by ID-Animator, Movie Gen, and Video Alchemist, which inject CLIP-encoded reference image tokens into cross-attention layers of diffusion transformers. It departs from prior multi-concept personalization methods (FastComposer, MoA, InstantFamily, Mix-of-Show) that resolve identity blending through architectural modifications such as masked attention, predefined spatial layouts, or per-concept feature concatenation. Instead, Movie Weaver introduces anchored prompts (unique text tokens like [R1], [R2] appended to concept descriptions) and uniform concept embeddings (a single learnable vector added to all tokens from the same reference image), achieving concept-image association with no architectural changes beyond the input pipeline. The data curation pipeline repurposes Llama-3, Detic, SAM, and CLIP to automatically generate anchored prompts and ordered reference images at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-concept video personalization (e.g., two people interacting, a person with a pet) is a practical requirement that prior single-concept methods cannot address, and the identity blending problem was a significant barrier. The anchored prompt mechanism is architecturally lightweight and could be adopted by other multi-concept generation systems beyond video. However, the paper identifies two open limitations: reference images can dominate generation (producing 'big-face' videos with reduced motion and poor prompt alignment), and the model cannot generalize to configurations unseen during training (e.g., three or more people). Balancing reference-image fidelity against prompt-driven motion and generalizing to arbitrary concept counts remain key directions for future work.
