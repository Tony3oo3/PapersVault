## Problem

Large-scale 3D bimanual hand activity datasets are unavailable, preventing the training of large models for hand motion understanding and generation. Existing datasets are limited in scale, lack diverse activity coverage, provide sparse or no text annotations, or rely on marker-based capture that inhibits natural interactions. No prior dataset simultaneously offers 183M+ frames, 50+ camera views, 400+ objects, and dense atomic-level text descriptions.

## Input / Output

The dataset is captured from 51 RGB cameras (1280×720, 30fps) arranged in a 3×3 grid on each face of a cubic volume, with software synchronization under 3 ms. Each of the 14k motion clips yields 3D hand poses (MANO parameters + 42 3D keypoints per hand), 3D object shape and 6D pose, hand/object segmentation masks, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and 5 LLM-augmented text descriptions per clip (84k total). Downstream applications consume text prompts or 3D motion sequences and produce generated hand motions, natural-language captions, or novel-view renderings via 2D Gaussian Splatting.

## Method

The core contribution is the Instruct-to-Annotate pipeline: an LLM procedurally generates temporally smooth instruction scripts (5 scenarios, 25 scenes, 191 activities, 1370 instructions) to elicit diverse bimanual actions during markerless multi-camera filming, minimizing post-capture annotation effort. Hand motion estimation chains YOLOv8 detection, HaMeR-based 2D keypoint extraction, ViTPose handedness assignment, cross-view triangulation, one-euro temporal filtering, and MANO fitting via the EasyMoCap pipeline. Object tracking uses DINOv2 salient-object detection, Grounding DINO template-matching, OpenCLIP negative-prompt filtering, and SAM2 video segmentation, followed by differentiable rendering supervised by multi-view masks with Instant-NGP density-field initialization and FoundPose-style DINOv2 feature matching for rotation. Applications leverage T2M-GPT for text-driven motion synthesis, TM2T for motion captioning, and 2DGS for dynamic radiance field reconstruction, all trained with supervised and contrastive objectives.

## Results

For text-driven hand motion synthesis (Table 2), the model trained on GigaHands achieves 31.2% R-Precision@1, 4.70 FID, and 9.11 Multimodality, substantially outperforming TACO (18.9% RP@1, 11.0 FID, 6.83 MM) and OakInk2 (17.9% RP@1, 19.6 FID, 3.45 MM). For motion captioning (Tables 3–4), GigaHands reaches 57.0% RP@1 and 43.1 BLEU@4, with dramatically higher caption diversity (distinct-1 = 15.3% vs. 2.03% for TACO and 2.76% for OakInk2). The data-scale ablation (Figure 6) shows that all metrics (FID, MM Dist, Top-1, Top-3) improve monotonically as training data increases from 10% to 100% of GigaHands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TACO [58], GigaHands wins on R-Precision@1 (31.2% vs. 18.9%), FID (4.70 vs. 11.0), and Multimodality (9.11 vs. 6.83), while TACO edges out slightly on Diversity (11.1 vs. 10.5). Against OakInk2 [116], GigaHands dominates across nearly all metrics: RP@1 more than doubles (31.2% vs. 17.9%), FID drops from 19.6 to 4.70, and Multimodality nearly triples (9.11 vs. 3.45). In motion captioning, GigaHands achieves the highest RP@1 (57.0% vs. 43.5% TACO, 40.4% OakInk2) and far superior caption diversity (distinct-1 of 15.3% vs. 2.03% and 2.76%), though OakInk2 retains a slight edge on MM Dist (4.55 vs. 5.37) and TACO on ROUGE (61.2 vs. 57.7) due to its simpler triplet-label format. Overall, GigaHands's scale and verb diversity (1,467 unique verbs) translate into consistently stronger text-motion alignment and richer generated outputs across both synthesis and captioning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

GigaHands positions itself at the intersection of two data acquisition paradigms: studio-based multi-view capture (ARCTIC, TACO, OakInk) and in-the-wild egocentric/third-person video (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, HOI4D). It departs from marker-based MoCap by using a fully markerless 51-camera system, and from in-the-wild datasets by providing dense, accurate 3D annotations via a hybrid pipeline combining HaMeR, multi-view triangulation, and MANO fitting. The procedural Instruct-to-Annotate pipeline, which leverages LLMs to generate temporally coherent activity scripts, is a novel data-sourcing strategy that bridges the diversity gap between controlled studio capture and unscripted real-world interaction. For downstream applications, the paper builds on T2M-GPT for motion synthesis, TM2T for captioning, and 2DGS for radiance field reconstruction, using GigaHands as the training substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At 183M frames, 34 hours, and 84k text-annotated clips, GigaHands is the largest bimanual hand activities dataset to date, and its 51-camera multi-view setup uniquely enables dynamic radiance field reconstruction alongside standard motion analysis. The demonstrated scaling trend (Figure 6) suggests that further data growth will continue to improve text-to-motion and captioning quality, making this a foundational resource for hand-centric robotics and generative models. Key limitations include the tabletop-only studio setting (excluding full-body or large-environment motions), incomplete handling of articulated and non-rigid objects, and the right-handed bias in subjects. Open questions include whether the dataset's scale can transfer to robotic manipulation policies and whether the LLM-driven annotation pipeline generalizes to unscripted, long-horizon activities.
