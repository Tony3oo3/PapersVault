## Problem

Hybrid Mamba-Transformer vision backbones combine the scalability of Transformers with Mamba's efficient long-context modeling, but no effective pretraining strategy exists that simultaneously benefits both components. Pilot experiments reveal that MAE strongly improves Transformers yet barely helps Mamba, while autoregressive pretraining does the opposite, leaving the hybrid architecture without a unified self-supervised training paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251629/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes RGB images (224×224 or 384×384) or 3D point clouds (1024 points split into 64 patches of 32 points), applies random masking at a 50% ratio, and reconstructs normalized pixel values via MSE loss. For 3D data, since point clouds lack a natural row structure, 32 patches are randomly ordered each iteration and reconstructed autoregressively. Downstream, the pretrained backbone produces class labels (ImageNet-1K, ModelNet40), per-pixel segmentation masks (ADE20K), and bounding boxes with instance masks (COCO).

## Method

MAP (Masked Autoregressive Pretraining) introduces a hierarchical objective that decomposes reconstruction into two complementary sub-tasks: within each row, all masked tokens are predicted simultaneously (a local MAE objective that trains the Transformer blocks for bidirectional local connectivity), while rows are generated sequentially in an autoregressive fashion (training the Mamba blocks for inter-region contextual modeling). The decoder is a masked Transformer with a row-wise causal mask that activates one full row at a time, chosen over a Mamba decoder because unidirectional scanning cannot reconstruct an entire local region in parallel. A 50% random masking ratio is used as a compromise between MAE's optimal 75% and AR's optimal 20%, and the autoregressive order is aligned with Mamba's row-first scanning direction. The default backbone (HybridNet) stacks eight units of three Mamba layers followed by one Transformer layer (MMMTMMMT), and the same MAP objective also improves pure Mamba and pure Transformer backbones within a unified framework.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, HybridNet-B pretrained with MAP achieves 84.9% top-1 accuracy (224²) and 85.5% (384²), outperforming MAE (83.9%), AR (83.8%), and CL (83.1%) pretraining on the same backbone by 1.0–1.8 points. At the larger scale, HybridNet-L+MAP reaches 86.2% at 384² resolution. On 3D ModelNet40, HybridNet with voting + MAP achieves 95.9% accuracy, surpassing Mamba3D+Point-MAE (95.4%) and Point-BERT (94.4%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, HybridNet-S+MAP attains 46.9 mIoU, exceeding Vim-S (44.9) by 2.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAP clearly wins over all pretraining baselines on the hybrid backbone: HybridNet-B+MAP (84.9%) beats MAE (83.9%), AR (83.8%), and CL (83.1%) by 1.0–1.8 points on ImageNet-1K. On pure Mamba (MambaR-B), MAP (84.0%) surpasses both AR (83.7%) and MAE (83.1%), and on pure Transformer (ViT-L), MAP (86.1%) edges out MAE (85.9%), confirming the method's broad applicability. Against existing hybrid architectures, MAP improves MambaVision-B from 84.2% to 84.9% and MambaVision-L from 85.3% to 86.4%, and HybridNet-B+MAP (84.9%) outperforms VMamba-B (83.9%) and ConvNeXt-B (83.8%). On 3D ModelNet40, HybridNet+MAP (95.9%) and Mamba3D+MAP (95.6%) both exceed Point-BERT (94.4%), Point-MAE (95.4%), and Point-M2AE (94.0%). On ADE20K segmentation, HybridNet-S+MAP (46.9 mIoU) beats Vim-S (44.9) and DeiT-S (44.0); on COCO detection, HybridNet-Ti+MAP (46.4 APbox) surpasses DeiT-Ti (44.4) and Vim-Ti (45.7). The only case where MAP does not strictly win is ViT-B, where it ties MAE at 83.6%, though it pulls ahead at the larger ViT-L scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two established self-supervised pretraining paradigms—MAE for Vision Transformers (He et al., 2022) and autoregressive pretraining for Vision Mamba (ARM, Ren et al., 2024)—into a unified strategy called Masked Autoregressive Pretraining (MAP) tailored to hybrid Mamba-Transformer backbones. It departs from MAE by restricting reconstruction to local (row-wise) regions and from ARM by introducing random masking within each autoregressive step, so that the Transformer component learns bidirectional local context while the Mamba component learns sequential inter-region context. The paper also positions itself against existing hybrid architectures (MambaVision, VMamba) by arguing that the pretraining objective, not just the architecture, is the bottleneck for unlocking hybrid performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAP provides the first systematic, well-ablated pretraining recipe for hybrid Mamba-Transformer vision backbones, and its diagnostic findings (AR order must match Mamba scanning order; 50% masking as a compromise between MAE's 75% and AR's 20%) give practitioners concrete design principles. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the hybrid backbone still trails a pure Transformer pretrained with MAE under identical settings, so the practical value of the hybrid lies in efficiency and long-context scalability rather than peak accuracy. Open questions include whether MAP's advantages grow at larger scales (video, LLM-length sequences) and whether more sophisticated clustering strategies beyond row-wise partitioning can further close the gap.
