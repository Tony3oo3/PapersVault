## Problem

In Learning from Label Proportions (LLP), the mainstream approach of using pseudo-labels for auxiliary instance-level self-training suffers from oversmoothing of predictions, which becomes severe as bag size grows. This produces inaccurate and even meaningless pseudo-labels that degrade classifier performance, and existing adaptive weighting techniques (e.g., SoftMatch) are not specifically designed to counteract this over-smoothing in the LLP setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252591/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes bags of M images (28×28 grayscale for Fashion-MNIST, 32×32 RGB for SVHN/CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, 64×64 for MiniImageNet) paired only with the class-proportion vector for each bag; no per-instance labels are used during training. It outputs a per-instance class prediction, and the training objective jointly enforces bag-level proportion matching and instance-level classification. Each instance is processed through both a weak augmentation (for pseudo-label generation) and a strong augmentation (for the training sample), following the FixMatch-style pipeline.

## Method

L²P-AHIL introduces a Dual Entropy-based Weight (DEW) that adaptively scores each pseudo-label's confidence from two complementary perspectives. At the bag level, it compares the entropy of the L1-normalized predicted distribution for the argmax class against the entropy of a reference distribution (uniform over the m_i^c instances expected in that class), mapping the squared entropy gap through a Gaussian kernel σ(x;β_b)=exp(−x²/β_b). At the instance level, it measures the entropy of the individual prediction vector against a one-hot reference (entropy 0), similarly mapped via σ(x;β_i). The product of these two weights modulates the cross-entropy instance-level self-training loss, which is jointly optimized with the standard DLLP bag-level proportion loss. This dual mechanism simultaneously rewards bags whose predicted proportions match ground truth and penalizes individual predictions that are overly smoothed, preventing low-confidence pseudo-labels from corrupting training.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, L²P-AHIL achieves 95.00% accuracy at bag size 32 (vs. 96.05% fully supervised) and maintains 92.88% at bag size 256, where ROT drops to 63.10% and DLLP to 44.87%. On SVHN, it reaches 97.91% at bag size 16, matching the fully supervised result of 97.77%. On CIFAR-100, it achieves 78.65% at bag size 16 (vs. 79.89% fully supervised) and 72.21% at bag size 128, while SoftMatch collapses to 2.12% at the same setting. The performance degradation from bag size 16 to 256 is only 2.08% on CIFAR-10, compared to over 40% for most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L²P-AHIL consistently outperforms ROT (the closest pseudo-label-based competitor) across all five datasets and all bag sizes, with the gap widening at larger bag sizes (e.g., 92.88% vs. 63.10% on CIFAR-10 at bag 256; 72.21% vs. 11.02% on CIFAR-100 at bag 128). Against SoftMatch, which uses a similar adaptive weighting idea, L²P-AHIL wins on nearly all settings; SoftMatch collapses catastrophically on CIFAR-100 (2.40% at bag 32) and SVHN (~20% across all sizes) due to its inability to handle over-smoothing. Compared to FLMm (which uses a stronger ResNet-50 pretrained encoder), L²P-AHIL surpasses it on CIFAR-10 at all bag sizes (e.g., 95.00% vs. 92.00% at bag 32) and on CIFAR-100 at bag sizes 16–128 (78.65% vs. 66.16% at bag 16), but FLMm retains an advantage on CIFAR-100 at bag 256 (57.10% vs. 23.56%). Against the bag-level-only baselines (DLLP, LLP-VAT, LLPFC), L²P-AHIL achieves substantially higher accuracy in every configuration, with the most dramatic gains on CIFAR-100 and MiniImageNet where those methods degrade to near-chance levels at larger bag sizes. The key differentiator is robustness to bag size: L²P-AHIL's accuracy drops only 2.08% from bag 16 to 256 on CIFAR-10, whereas DLLP drops 46.72%, ROT drops 31.76%, and SoftMatch drops 47.04%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the Learning from Label Proportions (LLP) literature, building directly on the DLLP bag-level cross-entropy loss [1] and the pseudo-label self-training paradigm popularized by ROT [9] and PLOT [24]. It borrows the weak/strong augmentation pipeline from semi-supervised learning (FixMatch [39]) to enhance representational learning. The key departure is the identification of an over-smoothing pathology in pseudo-labels at large bag sizes and the proposal of a Dual Entropy-based Weight (DEW) that jointly measures confidence at both the bag and instance levels, distinguishing it from prior adaptive-weighting methods like SoftMatch [6] that were not specifically designed for the LLP setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in LLP: as bag sizes grow (a realistic scenario when annotation is expensive), existing pseudo-label-based methods collapse because predictions become uniformly smoothed and pseudo-labels lose discriminative power. The DEW mechanism provides a principled, entropy-based filter that preserves performance even at bag size 256, where baselines like DLLP or ROT drop by 40%+. However, the authors acknowledge that the hyperparameters β_b and β_i are sensitive to the number of classes, and the method has not been validated on larger, more complex datasets beyond Mini-ImageNet, leaving open whether the approach scales to real-world LLP applications with thousands of classes or non-image modalities.
