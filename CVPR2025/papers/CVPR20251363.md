## Problem

VLMs struggle with fine-grained semantic grounding in complex scenes, and prior remedies require domain-specific data, fine-tuning, or architectural modifications. While LLM self-correction has been studied, whether VLMs can correct their own grounding mistakes through iterative feedback—without oracle access or any training—remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251363/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLM receives an RGB image, a ground-truth panoptic segmentation mask isolating a single region, and a text prompt specifying the task; it outputs a free-text description that is mapped to a class label via cosine similarity with sentence-transformer embeddings. In the self-correction loop, a Verifier instance of the same VLM receives either a RoI-cropped image or a visually marked image and produces a binary correct/incorrect signal, which is then fed back as text (with zero-shot CoT framing) to the agent VLM for revision.

## Method

The paper introduces an iterative self-correction framework in which a VLM agent and a Verifier (instantiated from the same model) alternate over up to five dialogue rounds: the agent predicts a class label for a masked region, the Verifier performs binary verification (is this label correct?) on a RoI-cropped or visually marked image, and the agent revises its prediction conditioned on the feedback. The key insight is that decomposing the open-ended grounding task into a simpler binary verification step dramatically improves feedback quality (up to 18 F1 points over intrinsic self-correction), and that visual prompting techniques (RoI crop, red-circle marks, Set-of-Marks) must be tailored to each VLM's training distribution. Zero-shot CoT prompting ('After examining the image and the expert analyses, the final answer is…') is used to encourage the agent to incorporate feedback. Across five VLMs on ADE20k and COCO, this framework yields up to 8.4 accuracy points improvement without fine-tuning, oracle access, or architectural changes.

## Results

On ADE20k (out-of-domain for open-source VLMs), the proposed VLM binary verification framework improves semantic grounding accuracy by up to 4.43 points (LLaVA-1.5: 35.86→40.29) after 5 iterative rounds, while oracle binary verification achieves up to 17.34 points (LLaVA-1.5: 35.86→53.20). On COCO, gains reach up to 8.43 points for GPT-4o (39.49→47.92) with VLM verification and 27.69 points with oracle verification. In contrast, the intrinsic self-correction baseline degrades performance by up to 17.41 points (GPT-4V on ADE20k: 40.36→22.95) after 3 rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Intrinsic Self-Correction from Kim et al. [23] (NeurIPS 2023), which prompts the VLM to 'carefully review and refine your answer' in a single additional dialogue turn. The proposed VLM binary verification framework consistently outperforms this baseline across all five VLMs and both datasets. On ADE20k, intrinsic self-correction degrades accuracy by 6.9–17.41 points for four of five models (only GPT-4o shows a marginal +3.68 gain), while VLM verification improves accuracy by 0.39–7.36 points. On COCO, the gap is even starker: intrinsic self-correction loses 4.66–8.12 points for most models, whereas VLM verification gains 1.91–8.43 points. The paper also reports Oracle Verification as an upper bound, showing that the gap between VLM-generated and oracle feedback (e.g., 4.43 vs. 17.34 for LLaVA-1.5 on ADE20k) indicates substantial room for improvement in the verifier's feedback quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM self-correction literature (intrinsic self-correction [23], tool-interactive critiquing [13], and the CoVe decomposition of generation into verification [11]) to the VLM setting, specifically for semantic grounding. It departs from the dominant paradigm of improving VLM grounding via domain-specific data, fine-tuning, or architectural changes [16, 28, 30, 35] by exploring an orthogonal, inference-time-only approach. The framework draws on established prompting techniques—zero-shot CoT [25], visual marks [46], and Set-of-Marks [57]—but repurposes them within an iterative VLM-to-Verifier dialogue loop that had not been systematically studied for grounding tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that iterative binary-verification self-correction improves grounding accuracy across five VLMs without any fine-tuning or external tools, offering a practical, compute-for-accuracy tradeoff. Two findings are particularly informative: intrinsic self-correction (simply asking the model to 'review and refine') consistently degrades performance, and even GPT-4V/GPT-4o retain >40% error rates after three rounds of oracle binary feedback, revealing a fundamental limitation in how current VLMs integrate corrective signals. Open questions include why strong models underutilize explicit ground-truth feedback, whether the binary-verification decomposition generalizes beyond closed-set grounding, and whether the compute cost of multi-round dialogue is acceptable for latency-sensitive applications.
