## Problem

Cross-view completion (CVC) models like CroCo-v2 have achieved state-of-the-art performance on geometric tasks, yet it remains unclear which internal component actually encodes the learned correspondence. Prior works (DUSt3R, MASt3R, CroCoFlow) rely on encoder or decoder features, leaving the role of the cross-attention map unexplored. This paper asks: does the cross-attention map within CVC models serve as a more precise and robust correspondence estimator than the features previously exploited?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250422/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For correspondence, the method takes a pair of RGB images (target and source) at arbitrary resolution and produces a dense flow field via softargmax over a fused cost volume of shape h×w×h×w. For depth estimation, it takes two consecutive video frames and outputs a per-pixel depth map through a DPT head. During CVC pretraining, the target image is masked at ~90% and reconstructed from the unmasked source using a ViT-Large encoder (24 blocks) and a 12-block decoder with self-attention, cross-attention, and MLP layers.

## Method

The key insight is that CVC training is structurally identical to self-supervised correspondence learning: both compute a cost volume (cross-attention map vs. feature correlation), apply softmax to obtain a matching distribution, warp the source to the target, and minimize a reconstruction loss. ZeroCo exploits this by extracting pre-softmax cross-attention scores from all decoder layers for both the original (target, source) and swapped (source, target) input pairs, then fusing them with reciprocity enforcement to produce a final cost volume. For learned matching (ZeroCo-flow), lightweight cost aggregation and target-axis upsampling modules are added on top of the frozen cross-attention map. For depth (ZeroCo-depth), the cross-attention map replaces epipolar-based cost volumes entirely, feeding into a DPT head, which yields robustness to dynamic objects and sensor noise that epipolar methods lack.

## Results

On zero-shot dense matching, ZeroCo (using CroCo-v2 cross-attention maps) achieves 9.41 AEPE on HPatches-240 and 35.39 AEPE on HPatches-Original, dramatically outperforming the next-best zero-shot method DIFT*ADM (26.14 and 100.78 AEPE respectively). On ETH3D, ZeroCo achieves 12.72 AEPE average, roughly 2× better than DIFT-SD (25.69). For learning-based matching, ZeroCo-flow reaches 13.61 AEPE on HPatches-Original (best overall) and 2.88 AEPE on ETH3D, and for multi-frame depth on KITTI, ZeroCo-depth achieves 0.090 AbsRel and 0.915 δ₁, matching or exceeding epipolar-based methods like DepthFormer (0.090 AbsRel, 0.905 δ₁).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroCo's zero-shot cross-attention maps achieve a 2.8× improvement over the best prior zero-shot method (DIFT*ADM: 26.14 vs. 9.41 AEPE on HPatches-240) and a 2.8× improvement on HPatches-Original (100.78 vs. 35.39), with the gap widening under extreme geometric deformation (Scene V: 35.52 vs. 13.26). On ETH3D, ZeroCo (12.72 AEPE) roughly halves the error of DIFT-SD (25.69) and SD-DINO (31.63), and critically maintains near-constant error across viewpoint rates (11.64 at rate=3 to 14.99 at rate=15) while correlation-based methods degrade sharply. For learning-based matching, ZeroCo-flow (13.61 AEPE on HPatches-Original) outperforms PDCNet+ (18.91), DiffMatch (18.84), and GLU-Net (25.05), but MASt3R (17.01) remains competitive on HPatches and superior on ETH3D (2.62 vs. 2.88), likely due to its larger training mixture (MIX14 vs. D*+M). On KITTI depth, ZeroCo-depth (0.090 AbsRel, 0.915 δ₁) matches DualRefine (0.087 AbsRel, 0.914 δ₁) and beats DepthFormer (0.090 AbsRel, 0.905 δ₁) without requiring a monocular depth teacher or segmentation network. On Cityscapes dynamic objects, ZeroCo-depth (0.127 AbsRel, 0.860 δ₁) outperforms all baselines including DynamicDepth (0.143 AbsRel, 0.841 δ₁), and under image noise (Table 6) it achieves 0.100 mDEE vs. 0.102 for DualRefine and 0.118 for ManyDepth, demonstrating robustness that epipolar-based cost volumes lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CroCo/CroCo-v2 cross-view completion pretraining framework, which prior works (DUSt3R, MASt3R, CroCoFlow) have leveraged by extracting encoder and decoder features for downstream geometric tasks. The key departure is a conceptual re-interpretation: by drawing an explicit analogy to self-supervised dense correspondence learning (where a cost volume is constructed and used to warp source to target), the authors show that the cross-attention map inside the decoder — not the encoder or decoder features — is the component that actually encodes correspondence. This reframes CVC not merely as a representation-learning pretext task but as an implicit correspondence estimator, positioning ZeroCo as a method that directly exploits the learned cost volume rather than treating it as a byproduct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cross-attention maps outperform decoder features by a wide margin (e.g., 10.85 vs. 44.63 AEPE on HPatches-240) has immediate practical implications for anyone building on CroCo-v2: the geometric knowledge is in the attention, not the features, and lightweight heads on top of cross-attention can replace expensive epipolar cost volumes for depth estimation. Open questions include whether this insight generalizes beyond the CroCo architecture family, whether the reciprocity trick and dense zoom-in are the only effective post-processing steps, and whether the approach extends to 3D reconstruction pipelines (DUSt3R/MASt3R) where decoder features are currently the standard interface. The learning-based results, while competitive, do not uniformly surpass MASt3R on ETH3D, suggesting the full potential of cross-attention as a cost volume may not yet be realized.
