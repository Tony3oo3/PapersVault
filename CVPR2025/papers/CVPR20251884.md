## Problem

Standard diffusion-based navigation policies initiate denoising from Gaussian noise, which diverges significantly from the target action distribution, causing redundant denoising steps and making it difficult to generate accurate actions from the sparse effective action space. The paper asks whether a structured, task-specific prior distribution can replace Gaussian noise as the denoising starting point, and how to construct such a prior when no inherent source-target pairing exists in imitation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251884/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a sequence of past RGB frames (window of size p) and a goal image specifying the navigation target. Observations are encoded by a Transformer encoder into a contextual vector c_t, which conditions both the prior action generator and the denoising network via FiLM modulation. The output is a future action sequence of length n+1 representing a local path, where each action encodes movement behavior (straight, left/right turn, U-turn) and magnitude, with the denoising process running for k=10 steps by default.

## Method

NaviBridger replaces the standard DDPM reverse process with a Denoising Diffusion Bridge Model (DDBM) that transports samples from an arbitrary source distribution π_s to the target action distribution π, using Doob's h-transform to constrain the diffusion path toward the target. The key architectural change is that denoising starts from prior actions generated by one of three strategies—Gaussian noise, a rule-based parabolic path conditioned on predicted path length and turn classification, or a lightweight CVAE trained on expert data—rather than from isotropic Gaussian noise. The denoising network is a 1-D temporal CNN modulated by FiLM, trained with a weighted MSE bridge loss, a prior generation loss (MSE + cross-entropy for rule-based, or reconstruction + KL for CVAE), and a temporal distance loss. A theoretical error bound shows that the expected denoising error is upper-bounded by a constant times the KL divergence between source and target distributions, formally justifying why more informative priors reduce required denoising steps and improve action quality.

## Results

In the indoor (Stanford 2D-3D-S) basic task, NaviBridger with a learning-based prior achieves 92% success rate and 0.61 average collisions, outperforming NoMaD (86%, 0.74) and ViNT (68%, 1.02). In the indoor adaptation task, the learning-based prior reaches 88% success with only 0.41 collisions, compared to NoMaD's 32% and ViNT's 28%. In the outdoor (Gazebo Citysim) basic task, the learning-based prior achieves 44% success and 0.51 collisions versus NoMaD (22%, 0.58) and ViNT (20%, 0.78), while in the outdoor adaptation task it reaches 64% success with 0.30 collisions versus NoMaD (52%, 0.34) and ViNT (38%, 0.41). Even the Gaussian-prior DDBM variant outperforms both baselines in the outdoor basic task (34% vs. 22%/20%) and the indoor adaptation task (64% vs. 32%/28%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaviBridger with a learning-based prior (CVAE) consistently outperforms both NoMaD and ViNT across all four evaluation settings (indoor/outdoor × basic/adaptation). Against NoMaD (the strongest baseline, a DDPM-based diffusion policy), the learning-based prior improves indoor basic success from 86% to 92% and indoor adaptation success from 32% to 88%, while also reducing collisions in every setting. Against ViNT (a regression-based model), the gains are even larger, e.g., 92% vs. 68% indoor basic and 44% vs. 20% outdoor basic success. Notably, even the Gaussian-prior DDBM variant (which uses standard noise as the source distribution) surpasses NoMaD in the outdoor basic task (34% vs. 22%) and indoor adaptation task (64% vs. 32%), suggesting that the bridge formulation itself provides a structural advantage over vanilla DDPM denoising. The rule-based prior, however, underperforms both baselines in the basic tasks (62% indoor, 14% outdoor), indicating that poorly designed priors can degrade performance, while the learning-based prior offers the most robust and generalizable solution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Denoising Diffusion Bridge Models (DDBM) from Zhou et al. [59], originally developed for paired-data tasks like image translation, to the unpaired setting of robot imitation learning where no natural source distribution exists. It builds on the iterative denoising architecture of Diffusion Policy [13] and the visual navigation frameworks of NoMaD [50] and ViNT [45], but departs from them by replacing the standard Gaussian-noise initialization with informative prior actions generated by auxiliary policies (rule-based or CVAE-based). The theoretical grounding draws on Doob's h-transform and optimal transport theory to formalize why a closer source-to-target distribution reduces denoising error, a connection previously unexplored in the robot learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NaviBridger is the first work to demonstrate that diffusion bridge models can replace standard diffusion policies in visual navigation, offering both a theoretical error-bound analysis (via KL divergence) and practical speedups from fewer denoising steps. The finding that a poorly designed rule-based prior can underperform plain Gaussian noise is a useful caution for practitioners designing auxiliary priors. Open questions remain around dynamic environments with high-speed moving objects, where the current framework's fixed denoising schedule may be insufficient, and whether the CVAE-based prior strategy generalizes to higher-dimensional or continuous action spaces beyond 2D planar navigation.
