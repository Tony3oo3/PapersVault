## Problem

Fully sparse 3D detectors extract features only from non-empty voxels, which creates two critical limitations: distant voxels become isolated because they fall outside each other's convolution kernel range (unlike dense detectors where intermediate empty voxels bridge the gap), and object centers are frequently empty—especially for large objects—causing center feature missing that hampers network optimization. No existing sparse detector simultaneously addresses both long-range interaction and robust optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw LiDAR point clouds as input, converts them to sparse voxels via a VFE module, and operates entirely in sparse representation without any dense feature maps. Detection ranges are 75 m (Waymo), 54 m (nuScenes), and 200 m (Argoverse2), with voxel sizes of (0.08, 0.08, 0.15) m, (0.075, 0.075, 0.2) m, and (0.1, 0.1, 0.2) m respectively. Output consists of 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation) and class labels, predicted directly from upsampled sparse voxels via a lightweight two-layer submanifold sparse convolution head.

## Method

FSHNet combines a sparse convolution encoder (VoxelNeXt or SAFDNet) with a proposed SlotFormer block that partitions downsampled sparse voxels into slots—each covering the full scene extent along one axis—then applies linear attention (O(N) complexity) within each slot to establish global-range interactions, alternating slot direction (X vs. Y) across layers. A dynamic sparse label assignment strategy selects top-k positive samples from n=5 nearest candidate voxels to each ground-truth center, ranked by a combined classification and regression selection cost, replacing the single nearest-voxel assignment used by prior sparse detectors. A sparse upsampling module doubles voxel coordinates and applies a 3×3 sparse convolution to recover fine-grained details lost during downsampling, improving small-object detection. The full pipeline is: VFE → sparse conv encoder → SlotFormer → sparse upsampling → dynamic sparse head, trained end-to-end with focal loss for classification and rotation-weighted IoU loss for regression.

## Results

On the Waymo Open validation set, FSHNet_base achieves 77.1 mAP / 74.9 mAPH (LEVEL 2), outperforming the previous best sparse detector SAFDNet (75.7/73.9) by +1.4 mAP and +1.0 mAPH, and the best dense detector ScatterFormer (75.7/73.8) by +1.4 mAP. On nuScenes, FSHNet_base reaches 71.7 NDS and 68.1 mAP, surpassing DSVT (71.1/66.4) and SAFDNet (71.0/66.3). On Argoverse2 (200 m range), FSHNet_base attains 40.2 mAP, exceeding SAFDNet (38.7) by +1.5 mAP and FSDv2 (37.6) by +2.6 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSHNet_base outperforms all 22 compared baselines on the Waymo Open validation set. Among sparse detectors, it surpasses SAFDNet (75.7/73.9) by +1.4 mAP / +1.0 mAPH, FSDv2 (75.6/73.5) by +1.5/+1.4, and VoxelNeXt (72.2/70.1) by +4.9/+4.8. Among dense detectors, it beats ScatterFormer (75.7/73.8) by +1.4/+1.1, HEDNet (75.3/73.4) by +1.8/+1.5, and DSVT (74.0/72.1) by +3.1/+2.8. On nuScenes, FSHNet_base leads DSVT by +0.6 NDS / +1.7 mAP and SAFDNet by +0.7 NDS / +1.8 mAP, despite using fewer training iterations (no CBGS). On Argoverse2's 200 m long-range setting, FSHNet_base exceeds SAFDNet by +1.5 mAP overall, with notable gains on small objects (pedestrian +3.2, bicycle +5.2) and large objects (bus +1.0, A-bus +2.2). FSHNet_light, using only 4 SlotFormer layers, already surpasses all prior sparse detectors, confirming the efficiency of the proposed architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSHNet sits at the intersection of two lines of work: fully sparse 3D detectors (VoxelNeXt, SAFDNet, FSD) that avoid dense operations for efficiency, and transformer-based 3D detectors (DSVT, ScatterFormer, LION) that use window-partitioned attention for long-range interactions. The key architectural departure is the SlotFormer block, which replaces the conventional square window partition with axis-aligned 'slots' spanning the full scene length, combined with linear attention to keep complexity at O(N). The dynamic sparse label assignment strategy is directly inspired by DCDet's cross-shaped positive sample selection, adapted to the sparse voxel setting where only non-empty voxels are available as candidates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSHNet addresses two persistent weaknesses of sparse 3D detectors—limited receptive field and center feature missing—with practical, modular solutions that can be grafted onto existing sparse encoders. The slot partition + linear attention combination offers a concrete recipe for achieving near-global interactions without quadratic cost, which is broadly applicable beyond this specific detector. However, the added latency (16–29 ms from SlotFormer) and the reliance on a specific sparse convolution backbone limit immediate deployment in latency-critical autonomous driving stacks. An open question the authors flag is whether a more efficient sparse architecture could match FSHNet's long-range capability without the attention overhead.
