## Problem

Large-scale pre-trained multimodal models (e.g., CLIP) extract general-purpose features that contain substantial task-irrelevant information—such as semantic category cues—which causes AI-generated image detectors to learn superficial biases and severely degrade generalization across different generative model families (GANs vs. diffusion models). Existing methods that simply apply linear probing or MLP classifiers on these general features do not explicitly filter out irrelevant components, leaving the classifier vulnerable to overfitting incidental correlations.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input, which are encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder into fixed-dimensional feature vectors. These feature vectors (not raw pixels) are the actual input to the trainable VIB module, which produces a compressed K-dimensional latent representation. The final output is a binary real/fake classification score from a linear decoder, with a fixed decision threshold of 0.5 used for accuracy reporting. Data augmentation (Gaussian blur and JPEG compression) is applied to input images during training to improve robustness.

## Method

VIB-Net couples a frozen CLIP image encoder with a Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) module to filter task-irrelevant features from the general-purpose representations. A shallow MLP maps the CLIP features to parameters of a Gaussian distribution (mean μ and covariance Σ, K-dimensional), and the reparameterization trick samples the compressed latent z. The training objective maximizes mutual information between z and the classification label Y (via a linear decoder q(y|z)) while minimizing mutual information between z and the input X (via KL divergence against a parameterized Gaussian prior), controlled by a tradeoff parameter β. This forces the latent representation to retain only the minimal information necessary for real/fake discrimination, explicitly discarding semantic and other irrelevant features. The approach is validated across 17 generative models spanning GAN and diffusion families, with the VIB module being the only trainable component alongside the final classifier.

## Results

Trained on Stable Diffusion v1.4 images, VIB-Net achieves 95.53% average AP and 85.83% average accuracy across 15 test generators, outperforming the strongest baseline NPR (89.98% AP, 76.50% ACC) by 5.55% in AP and 9.33% in accuracy. On the GAN-trained setting (ProGAN), VIB-Net reaches 93.14% AP and 82.50% ACC over 18 generators, surpassing Univfd (90.88% AP) by 2.26% in AP. In cross-model-genera generalization (testing on GANs when trained on diffusion), VIB-Net improves mAP by 12.48% and accuracy by 23.59% over the SOTA method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIB-Net outperforms all 8 baselines on average AP in both training settings. Against the strongest SOTA baseline NPR (CVPR 2024), VIB-Net gains +5.55% AP (95.53 vs 89.98) and +9.33% ACC (85.83 vs 76.50) in the diffusion-trained setting, and +10.70% AP (93.14 vs 82.44) in the GAN-trained setting. Compared to Univfd (CVPR 2023), the direct CLIP-based baseline, VIB-Net improves AP by 20.22% (95.53 vs 75.31) and ACC by 20.46% (85.83 vs 65.37) when trained on diffusion, with the gap widening to +34.17% AP on GAN test models. CLIPping (ICMR 2024) trails VIB-Net by 14.66% AP in the diffusion-trained setting, while gradient-based methods Lgrad and CNNSpot show severe degradation on modern diffusion models (AP dropping to 50–60% on ADM, VQDM). FreDect and DIRE, which rely on frequency-domain or reconstruction-error cues, perform worst on cross-genera generalization, with FreDect scoring only 70.06% AP and DIRE failing to report results on several test models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) framework [1] and applies it, for the first time, to AI-generated image detection. It departs from the prevailing CLIP-based detection paradigm (Univfd [28], CLIPping [17], Cozzolino et al. [6]) that feeds general-purpose multimodal features directly into a classifier, instead inserting a VIB bottleneck to compress and filter task-irrelevant information before classification. The paper also positions itself against frequency-domain and gradient-based detectors (CNNSpot, FreDect, LGrad, NPR) that struggle to generalize across generative model families, arguing that the core failure mode of large-model-based detectors is superficial bias from redundant features rather than insufficient feature quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of task-irrelevant semantic features (e.g., category information) as the primary source of generalization failure in CLIP-based detectors provides a clear diagnostic that the field can build upon. The substantial generalization gains (+23.59% accuracy on unseen model families) suggest that feature filtering, not just feature extraction, is a critical missing component in current pipelines. Open questions remain: performance still degrades on the most advanced generators (ADM, Midjourney), the bottleneck dimension K is an unexplored hyperparameter, and it is unclear whether the VIB approach transfers to video or 3D content detection. The method's simplicity (a shallow MLP + Gaussian bottleneck on frozen CLIP features) makes it easy to adopt, but its dependence on CLIP's representation quality limits its ceiling.
