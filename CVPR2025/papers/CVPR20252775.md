## Problem

Existing missing modality completion (MMC) methods rely on carefully designed fusion architectures and extensive pre-training on complete data, which limits their out-of-domain (OOD) generalizability and makes them costly to adapt to new domains. The paper asks whether a training-free MMC framework can be both resource-efficient (minimal domain-specific data and compute) and robust to OOD transfer, without requiring retraining or specialized fusion modules.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes available modality data (an RGB image or a text passage) together with optional domain-specific prior knowledge (e.g., histological or clinical diagnostic terms for medical imaging) as structured prompts to an LMM. It produces multiple candidate completions of the missing modality (default n=5), where missing images are generated via conditional diffusion (SDXL for general domains, Cheff for chest X-rays) and missing text is generated by the LMM itself. Each candidate receives a composite quality score combining graph-structure similarity and CLIP/BLIP semantic similarity, and the highest-scoring candidate is returned as the final completion.

## Method

Knowledge Bridger is a three-step, training-free pipeline built around a pre-trained LMM (Qwen2-VL-7B by default). Step 1 (Knowledge Graph Modeling) uses Chain-of-Thought prompting to extract entities, attributes, and inter-entity relationships from the available modality, consolidating them into a modality-specific knowledge graph without any predefined schema. Step 2 (Knowledge-Driven Generation) traverses the graph to select relevant entities as generation subjects, then uses the LMM to produce structured, entity-anchored text descriptions that condition a diffusion model (for images) or the LMM itself (for text), reducing the randomness of direct generation. Step 3 (Knowledge-Based Ranking) scores each candidate by computing cosine similarity between the adjacency matrices of the available and generated knowledge graphs, plus cosine similarity of CLIP and BLIP embeddings, and selects the candidate with the highest weighted score. The key insight is that structured knowledge extraction constrains generation toward semantic fidelity (accuracy over diversity) and provides an interpretable basis for ranking, distinguishing this approach from both direct LMM generation and conventional imputation networks.

## Results

On COCO-2014 at a 0.7 missing rate, Knowledge Bridger (Qwen-VL-7B) achieves 77.9% F1 and 83.5% AP, outperforming the best baseline (MPLMM) by +5.6 F1 and +3.4 AP. On MM-IMDb at the same rate, it reaches 55.2% F1 and 61.8% AP (+6.1 F1 over MPLMM). On the OOD IU X-ray dataset at 0.7 missing rate, the method achieves 46.3% F1 and 70.5% AP, surpassing the strongest baseline (MPMM) by +9.5 F1 and +8.6 AP, while also achieving the highest generation quality score (SS=19.8 vs. 13.3 for DiCMoR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Knowledge Bridger outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and missing rate. Against the non-imputation baselines, it beats MPLMM (ACL'24) by +5.6 F1 on COCO-2014 and +6.1 F1 on MM-IMDb at η=0.7, and surpasses MPMM (CVPR'23) by +6.9 F1 and +12.1 F1 respectively. Against the imputation-based baselines, the advantage is even larger: on IU X-ray (OOD), it exceeds MMIN (ACL'21) by +19.6 F1 and DiCMoR (CVPR'23) by +16.5 F1 at η=0.7, while also achieving substantially higher generation quality (SS=19.8 vs. 10.2 and 13.3). The gap widens with increasing missing rate, indicating that the knowledge-graph-guided generation and ranking strategy becomes increasingly valuable as more information is lost. Notably, the method also outperforms the complete-data baseline on generation quality (SS), suggesting the imputed modalities are semantically coherent even if downstream classification still lags behind the oracle setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established MMC literature (MMIN, DiCMoR, MPMM, MPLMM), which relies on domain-specific pre-training on complete data and carefully designed fusion architectures, by proposing a fully training-free pipeline that repurposes LMMs as a knowledge extraction and orchestration engine. Conceptually, it bridges knowledge graph construction (via CoT prompting) with conditional generation (SDXL, Cheff) and multi-signal ranking (CLIP, BLIP embeddings + graph similarity), treating the LMM as a modality-agnostic 'bridge' rather than a direct generator. The authors position this as the first application of LMMs to MMC, carving new ground by showing that structured knowledge extraction from available modalities can substitute for the extensive supervised training that prior imputation-based and prompt-based methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, modality-agnostic design offers a practical path to MMC in data-scarce or rapidly shifting domains (e.g., rare-disease imaging) where retraining is infeasible, and the strong OOD results on IU X-ray suggest LMM prior knowledge can partially substitute for domain-specific supervision. However, the method is currently limited to image-text pairs, suffers from LMM hallucinations that the authors acknowledge but do not fully mitigate, and exhibits a paradoxical drop in generation similarity at high missing rates despite improved classification F1. Open questions include extending to audio/depth modalities, integrating retrieval-augmented generation to reduce hallucination, and understanding why the ranking signal (graph + semantic similarity) does not fully align with perceptual quality of generated content.
