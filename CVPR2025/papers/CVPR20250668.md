## Problem

Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are proposed as energy-efficient alternatives for visual streaming perception on power-constrained edge devices, but their practical advantage over ANNs remains unproven. Conventional SNN inference resets all membrane potentials after each frame, discarding temporal context and incurring unnecessary memory operations, while the temporal dependencies between neighboring streaming frames are largely underutilized. The paper asks whether SNNs can exploit inter-frame redundancy to reduce computation without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of pre-processed RGB frames sampled at the sensor's native FPS (e.g., 30 or 240 Hz). The first frame is encoded in full (vanilla form), while every subsequent frame is encoded only as the pixel-wise difference from its predecessor (DiffEncode), which is then converted to a single analog spike per pixel. The SNN infers step-by-step within the sensor's sampling window, supporting early exiting before full equilibrium. Outputs are task-specific: bounding boxes (YOLOV3-based detection), tracked object positions (UniTrack-based tracking), binary segmentation masks (DAVIS), or tracked segmentation masks (MOTS).

## Method

VISTREAM combines two key components: (1) a Law-of-Charge-Conservation (LoCC) property discovered in the ST-BIF (bipolar integrate-and-fire with spike tracer) neuron, which guarantees that at equilibrium the network output equals a Q-ReLU ANN regardless of input timing or order, and (2) a differential encoding scheme (DiffEncode) that feeds only the inter-frame pixel difference into the SNN instead of the full frame. Because LoCC-SNN accumulates charge additively, feeding the difference ΔX_i = X_i − X_{i−1} produces the same equilibrium output as feeding the full frame X_i with a per-frame membrane reset, formally proven via Theorems 4.1–4.6. This equivalence eliminates the need for per-frame membrane resets entirely, reducing both SOP and MAC operations. The SNN is obtained by converting a trained ANN (YOLOV3 or UniTrack with Q-ReLU activation) via weight inheritance, so no SNN-specific training is required. The framework supports flexible system configurations where the sensor and SNN hardware operate at different frequencies, with early exiting as a latency–accuracy trade-off knob.

## Results

VISTREAM (Var-IV: LoCC-SNN + DiffEncode) achieves task performance identical to its ANN counterpart (Var-I) across all five VSP benchmarks (object detection, single/multi-object tracking, video segmentation, and tracking segmentation), with no accuracy drop. The key efficiency gain is a reduction of up to 46% in total operations and 51% in estimated energy consumption relative to the LoCC-SNN baseline using vanilla encoding (Var-III). On the NFS240 dataset (240 FPS), the average inter-frame pixel difference drops to 5.19 (vs. 9.75 at 30 FPS), further amplifying the energy savings at higher frame rates. Energy consumption of Var-IV is even lower than the IF-SNN variant (Var-II) on object detection tasks, despite ST-BIF neurons firing both positive and negative spikes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four internal variants rather than external SOTA methods: (1) ANN + VaniEncode (Var-I, baseline), (2) IF-SNN + VaniEncode (Var-II), (3) LoCC-SNN + VaniEncode (Var-III), and (4) LoCC-SNN + DiffEncode (Var-IV, proposed). Var-IV matches Var-I exactly in task performance (mAP, HOTA, J-Means, AUC) due to the proven LoCC equivalence, while reducing operations by up to 46% and energy by up to 51% versus Var-III. Var-II (IF-SNN) suffers significant accuracy degradation on VSP tasks compared to the ANN baseline, confirming that the LoCC property of ST-BIF neurons is essential for correct DiffEncode operation. In terms of raw energy, Var-I (ANN) is the most expensive despite having the fewest operations, because it is dominated by energy-hungry MAC operations (4.6 pJ each) whereas SNNs rely on cheaper SOP operations (0.9 pJ each). Var-IV's energy advantage over Var-II is task-dependent: it is clearly lower on object detection (NFS) but the gap narrows on segmentation tasks where rear-layer spike activity persists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SpikeZIPTF (ICML 2024), which introduced the ST-BIF neuron and proved ANN-to-SNN conversion equivalence with Q-ReLU activations. It departs from the broader SNN-for-video literature (e.g., SVFormer, event-based video reconstruction) that either uses direct training or reset-per-frame inference, by identifying the Law of Charge Conservation property in ST-BIF neurons and showing it enables a differential encoding scheme (DiffEncode) that eliminates membrane resets entirely. The work also positions itself against ANN-based streaming perception methods that rely on dense temporal computations (LSTMs, gated recurrent networks), arguing that SNNs with LoCC certification can match ANN accuracy while being fundamentally more energy-efficient for edge deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LoCC theorem provides a rigorous guarantee that ST-BIF networks are equivalent to Q-ReLU ANNs at equilibrium regardless of input timing or order, which is a clean theoretical foundation for designing neuromorphic hardware that need not track spike timestamps. Practically, the 46–51% energy reduction and elimination of per-frame resets make SNNs more viable for power-constrained edge VSP (autonomous driving, AR/VR), particularly at high frame rates where inter-frame differences shrink. However, the paper is explicit that it does not push SOTA accuracy, the latency advantage is task-dependent (absent for object detection on NFS240), and the framework is restricted to feedforward ST-BIF networks with analog encoding—open questions include whether LoCC extends to recurrent architectures, whether the early-exiting sweet spot can be learned adaptively, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two backbones tested (YOLOV3, UniTrack).
