## Problem

Training-free diffusion-based style transfer methods suffer from two persistent failures: the original content layout degrades because the model lacks direct constraints on content preservation, and content from the style image leaks into the output because style and content cannot be cleanly decoupled without targeted training. The paper identifies that the sampling startpoint (the DDIM latent at the end of inversion) plays a critical but largely unexplored role in governing both of these issues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250845/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image and a style image as input; the text prompt for the content image is automatically generated by BLIP rather than user-provided. It operates within Stable Diffusion XL with 50 DDIM timesteps, using a tile ControlNet for spatial conditioning and the IP-Instruct model (with ViT-L/14 CLIP encoder) to extract style and content embeddings. The output is a single stylized image that preserves the content layout of the content image while adopting the visual style (color, texture, brushwork) of the style image.

## Method

StyleSSP enhances the sampling startpoint (DDIM latent z_T) through two mechanisms. First, Frequency Manipulation applies a 2D FFT to the DDIM latent, reduces low-frequency components by a factor α=0.7 using a Gaussian filter, and adds Gaussian noise scaled by (1−α), producing a startpoint more tightly bound to image layout and contours. Second, Negative Guidance via Inversion replaces the standard noise prediction in the DDIM inversion step with a guided prediction that uses IP-Instruct embeddings of the style image's content and the content image's style as negative conditions (guidance scale ω_i=1.5), pushing the startpoint away from the style image's content. During sampling, style features are injected exclusively into the first upsampling block of the U-Net (following InstantStyle), and a tile ControlNet provides additional spatial content preservation.

## Results

On the MSCOCO (content) × WikiArt (style) benchmark of 800 stylized image pairs, StyleSSP achieves the best scores on all three metrics: ArtFID 21.499, FID 13.448, and LPIPS 0.4881. The closest competitor, InstantStyle plus, records ArtFID 25.886 and FID 16.097, while StyleID leads on LPIPS at 0.5055. StyleSSP thus improves ArtFID by ~17% and FID by ~16% over the strongest baseline, and narrows the LPIPS gap to 0.4881 vs. 0.5055.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyleSSP outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (ArtFID, FID, LPIPS). The closest competitor is InstantStyle plus, which trails by 4.39 ArtFID points (25.886 vs. 21.499) and 2.65 FID points (16.097 vs. 13.448), while StyleID is the nearest on LPIPS (0.5055 vs. 0.4881). The gap widens substantially against non-inversion methods: InstantStyle, DiffuseIT, InST, and DiffStyle all exceed 36 in ArtFID and 0.64 in LPIPS, indicating severe content-preservation failures. Among inversion-based methods (StyleID, InstantStyle plus, InST), StyleSSP consistently leads, confirming that the proposed frequency manipulation and negative-guidance-via-inversion components jointly address both content leakage and layout drift that these baselines still exhibit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free diffusion-based style transfer line (InstantStyle, StyleID, InstantStyle plus) by shifting the focus from feature injection strategies to the sampling startpoint itself. It repurposes frequency manipulation from FlexiEdit (originally for non-rigid editing) to preserve content layout, and introduces a novel application of negative guidance during DDIM inversion—rather than during sampling—to push the startpoint away from style image content. The approach also builds on IP-Instruct for image-based conditioning and ControlNet for spatial constraints, but the core conceptual departure is treating the startpoint as the primary lever for style transfer quality, a dimension prior methods largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The startpoint-centric framing offers a practical, training-free recipe that practitioners can adopt with off-the-shelf SDXL models, and the insight that low-frequency reduction in the DDIM latent improves layout preservation could transfer to other diffusion-based editing tasks. However, the method still depends on ControlNet as an auxiliary content constraint (which the paper acknowledges can fail), and all experiments are confined to SDXL with 50 timesteps, leaving open whether the startpoint sensitivity generalizes to newer architectures (SD3, Flux) or longer sampling schedules. The moderate but consistent gains over strong baselines (e.g., ArtFID 21.5 vs. 25.9) suggest the approach is a useful incremental step rather than a paradigm shift in style transfer.
