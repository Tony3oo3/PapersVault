## Problem

Large VLMs struggle with long-form video understanding because limited context windows force aggressive frame reduction, and the standard uniform sampling strategy wastes the token budget on query-irrelevant segments, degrading VQA accuracy. The paper asks whether better, query-aware frame selection at inference time—without any additional training—can close this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250842/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video sampled at 1 FPS and a natural-language question as input. A lightweight CLIP-L/14 (or SigLIP) encoder computes per-frame similarity scores against the query, and inverse transform sampling selects N frames (typically 8–32) within the LLM's context window. The selected frames are passed to an off-the-shelf VLM (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision-7B), which auto-regressively generates a multiple-choice or open-ended VQA answer. No model weights are modified at any point.

## Method

BOLT's core mechanism is inverse transform sampling (ITS) applied to frame-query similarity scores. After computing cosine similarity between the CLIP text embedding of the query and each frame's visual embedding, scores are min-max normalized and raised to a sharpness power α (2.5–3.5) to control the relevance–diversity trade-off. A cumulative distribution function is built over the refined scores, and N frames are drawn by inverting the CDF at uniform quantiles, so high-similarity frames are oversampled while low-similarity frames retain a non-zero probability of selection. This is contrasted against top-K selection (redundant, low diversity) and watershed grouping (overlooks broader temporal context), and all strategies are applied purely at inference time with no retraining. The paper also introduces a multi-source retrieval evaluation setting that concatenates K unrelated videos to simulate noisy real-world long-form content, revealing that uniform sampling degrades sharply under such conditions while ITS maintains robust gains.

## Results

BOLT (inverse transform sampling) applied to LLaVA-OneVision-7B improves Video-MME accuracy from 53.8% to 56.1% (8 frames), 56.9% to 57.8% (16 frames), and 58.5% to 59.9% (32 frames). On MLVU, gains are larger: 58.9% → 63.4% (8 frames) and 63.2% → 66.8% (32 frames). On LongVideoBench, 32-frame BOLT reaches 59.6% vs. 56.4% baseline. Under the proposed multi-source retrieval evaluation, BOLT with 16 frames boosts accuracy from 47.8% to 53.4% (+5.6%), far exceeding the +0.6% gain from simply increasing to 64 uniform frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With 32 frames, BOLT-equipped LLaVA-OneVision (59.9% Video-MME, 66.8% MLVU) surpasses both Frame-Voyager (57.5%, 65.6%) and LongVU (60.9%, 65.4%) on MLVU while remaining competitive on Video-MME, all without any additional training. At 8 frames, BOLT (56.1% Video-MME, 63.4% MLVU) still trails LongVU (60.9%, 65.4%) but exceeds Frame-Voyager on MLVU (63.4% vs. 65.6% is actually a loss; correction: 63.4% < 65.6%). BOLT consistently outperforms all other baselines on LongVideoBench (59.6% at 32 frames) where Frame-Voyager and LongVU do not report results. On EgoSchema and NextQA (shorter videos), gains are marginal (≤0.3%), confirming that BOLT's advantage is most pronounced on long-form video tasks. The multi-source retrieval evaluation further shows BOLT (+5.6% with 16 frames) dramatically outperforms simply increasing uniform frames from 16 to 64 (+4.3%), validating that query-guided selection is more effective than brute-force token budget expansion.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOLT positions itself within the video token reduction literature for large VLMs, specifically the temporal selection sub-problem. Prior query-aware frame selection methods (Goldfish, LongVU, Frame-Voyager) demonstrate that relevance-based sampling beats uniform sampling but require expensive fine-tuning (e.g., 32 H100 GPUs for Frame-Voyager). BOLT departs from this line by repurposing inverse transform sampling—a standard probabilistic technique from statistics—combined with off-the-shelf CLIP/SigLIP similarity scores, achieving query-guided selection entirely at inference time with no model retraining. The paper also introduces a multi-source retrieval evaluation protocol that extends standard VQA benchmarks by concatenating multiple unrelated videos, a benchmarking approach not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of BOLT makes it immediately applicable to any existing VLM without retraining costs, which is practically significant given the compute barriers of prior adaptive sampling methods. The multi-source retrieval evaluation exposes a real weakness of uniform sampling in noisy, long-form settings that standard trimmed-video benchmarks mask, offering the field a more realistic stress test. However, the method's reliance on CLIP/SigLIP cosine similarity limits its effectiveness for queries requiring causal or multi-hop reasoning where visual-textual alignment is weak, and the authors acknowledge this as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-round VQA, video generation conditioning, or scenarios where the relevant content is semantically distant from the query text.
