## Problem

Existing 3D hand reconstruction pipelines follow a multi-stage paradigm (detection, left-right classification, pose estimation) that introduces redundant computation and cumulative errors; for instance, detection and left-right classification alone produce an 11.2% error rate on HInt before pose estimation even begins. This paper asks whether hand detection, left-right awareness, and pose estimation can be unified into a single end-to-end stage that eliminates these dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251659/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full RGB image (long edge resized to 1280 px, not a cropped hand patch) together with the text prompt 'Hand' that does not distinguish left from right. It outputs 21 2D joint coordinates, 778 3D mesh vertices, and a 3D camera translation vector; 3D joints are then derived from vertices via the MANO joint regressor. Because the detector is frozen and no left-right category is predicted, the left-right identity is recovered post-hoc from the sign of mesh normal vectors.

## Method

HandOS freezes a pre-trained Grounding DINO detector and attaches a learnable side-tuning network (shadow layers of the visual backbone) to supply keypoint-relevant features that the detection-tuned encoder lacks. An interactive 2D-3D decoder then performs three query transformations: (1) instance-to-joint expansion adds learnable joint embeddings to filtered instance queries, (2) 2D-to-3D lifting applies a learnable matrix (initialized with MANO skinning weights) to project 2D joint queries into 3D vertex and camera-translation queries, and (3) hierarchical attention in the four interactive layers lets 2D joints and 3D vertices attend to each other while camera translation attends to both but is invisible to them, preserving translation/scale invariance of keypoints. Training combines supervised 2D/3D losses (L1, OKS, edge, normal) with weak supervision (projection error, normal consistency) so that 2D-only annotations can also contribute to mesh learning.

## Results

On FreiHand, HandOS achieves 5.0 mm PA-MPJPE and 5.3 mm PA-MPVPE, outperforming all prior methods (best prior: Hamba at 5.8/5.5 mm). On DexYCB, it reaches 5.2 mm PA-MPJPE and 0.896 AUC, surpassing Zhou et al. (5.5 mm) and H2ONet (5.7 mm). On HInt-Ego4D, HandOS attains 64.6% PCK@0.05 with only 204K training samples (vs. 2,749K for HaMeR), exceeding HaMeR's 46.9% by a wide margin. On HO3Dv3, HandOS* achieves 6.8 mm PA-MPJPE with extra training data, matching Hamba* (6.9 mm) while operating without the assumption of perfect detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FreiHand, HandOS (5.0 mm PJ) outperforms all 7 baselines, with the largest margin over METRO (6.7 mm) and PointHMR (6.1 mm), and a clear 0.8 mm advantage over the closest competitor Hamba (5.8 mm). On DexYCB, HandOS (5.2 mm PJ, 0.896 AUC) beats all 5 baselines, including Zhou et al. (5.5 mm) and H2ONet (5.7 mm), with a notable 1.6 mm gap over Spurr et al. (6.8 mm). On HInt-Ego4D, HandOS-2D achieves 64.6% PCK@0.05 versus HaMeR's 46.9%, a 17.7-point improvement, despite using less than 1/13th of the training data (204K vs. 2,749K). On HO3Dv3, HandOS without extra data (8.4 mm) trails Hamba* (6.9 mm), but the paper argues this comparison is unfair since prior methods assume perfect GT bounding boxes; with extra training data, HandOS* (6.8 mm) slightly edges out Hamba* (6.9 mm). A key differentiator is that HandOS operates end-to-end without assuming accurate detection or left-right classification, whereas all baselines rely on these as prerequisites, meaning HandOS's reported numbers reflect mixed detection + pose errors while baselines report only reconstruction error.

## Relation to Prior Work

HandOS extends the DETR-based detection paradigm (specifically Grounding DINO) by integrating 2D/3D keypoint estimation directly into a frozen detector, following the one-stage pose estimation trend established by PETR and EDPose. It departs from the dominant multi-stage hand reconstruction pipeline (detection → left-right classification → MANO-based pose estimation) by replacing MANO parameters with a unified keypoint representation (2D joints + 3D vertices) and eliminating the left-right classification stage entirely. The interactive 2D-3D decoder with hierarchical attention and query lifting is a novel architectural contribution that bridges the gap between 2D semantic cues and 3D geometric structure within a single transformer decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-stage formulation eliminates cumulative errors from detection and left-right misclassification (11.2% error rate reported for prior pipelines), making the system more robust for real-world deployment where perfect detection is unrealistic. The data efficiency result—outperforming HaMeR and Hamba on HInt-Ego4D with only 204K training samples versus their 2.7M—is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the frozen-detector + side-tuning strategy generalizes to two-hand or full-body scenarios, and whether the keypoint-based (non-parametric) representation can match MANO's expressiveness for downstream tasks like hand-object interaction modeling.
