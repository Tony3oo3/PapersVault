## Problem

Vanishing point estimation in the Manhattan world requires jointly inferring line-to-VP associations and locating each VP, a coupled problem with many local minima. Existing methods are either sub-optimal (RANSAC, EM, Hough voting) or globally optimal but suffer exponential worst-case runtime (Branch-and-Bound), leaving no algorithm that simultaneously guarantees global optimality, outlier robustness, and practical efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250410/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of m detected image lines, each represented as a unit normal vector n ∈ R³ in the normalized camera coordinate system (requiring known intrinsics K for conversion from homogeneous line coordinates). It outputs three vanishing points as 3D direction vectors d₁, d₂, d₃ ∈ R³ and a binary association label for every line (VP1, VP2, VP3, or Outlier). A tunable maximum inlier threshold c (default 0.03) controls the distance cutoff for inlier assignment, and the Manhattan world prior enforces mutual orthogonality among the three VPs.

## Method

The paper introduces a truncated multi-selection error that jointly optimizes VP locations and discrete line-VP association labels in a single formulation, where outlier lines are capped at a constant penalty c² to avoid bias. This primal problem is reformulated as a QCQP and then relaxed to a convex SDP by dropping the rank-one constraint, with a proof of zero duality gap under mild conditions. To solve the SDP efficiently, GlobustVP iteratively solves a smaller Single Block SDP per vanishing point (treating all other lines as potential outliers), removes identified inliers, and repeats three times. A final Manhattan Post-Refinement step enforces the orthogonality constraint among the three VPs. For large-scale inputs, a random sampling strategy (6 lines per SDP) keeps runtime tractable while preserving accuracy.

## Results

On the York Urban Database (YUD), GlobustVP achieves the best angular accuracy across all thresholds: 67.6% at 3°, 87.3% at 5°, and 96.1% at 10°, outperforming the next-best method (PARSAC at 85.6% at 10°) by over 10 points. On the SceneCity Urban 3D (SU3) dataset, GlobustVP scores 80.2/86.8/92.4% at 3°/5°/10°, which is competitive with traditional baselines but trails learning-based methods like NeurVPS (97.8% at 10°) and PARSAC (95.4% at 10°) that were trained on similar synthetic data. On synthetic experiments, GlobustVP maintains high F1-scores up to 70% outlier ratio while running significantly faster than the globally optimal BnB baseline, achieving a favorable balance between accuracy, robustness, and efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlobustVP decisively outperforms all traditional baselines on YUD: it beats BnB [6] (not in Table 1 but compared in synthetic experiments) on runtime by orders of magnitude while matching its accuracy, and surpasses Quasi-VP [25] (84.3%→96.1% at 10°), J-Linkage [38] (80.5%→96.1%), T-Linkage [30], and RANSAC [42] by 10–16 points in AA@10°. Among learning-based methods, GlobustVP wins on YUD against NeurVPS [44] (78.1%→96.1%), CONSAC [17] (83.4%→96.1%), Lin et al. [27] (85.7%→96.1%), PARSAC [16] (85.6%→96.1%), and Zhai et al. [41] (76.5%→96.1%), demonstrating strong generalization without dataset-specific training. However, on SU3, where NeurVPS and PARSAC were trained on similar synthetic wireframe data, GlobustVP (92.4% at 10°) trails NeurVPS (97.8%) and PARSAC (95.4%), though it still exceeds CONSAC (90.6%) and all traditional methods. The paper attributes this SU3 gap to the learning-based methods' advantage from in-distribution training, while emphasizing that GlobustVP's training-free, globally optimal formulation provides superior robustness and generalization in diverse real-world settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing convex relaxation (SDP) to vanishing point estimation in the Manhattan world, a task previously addressed only through stochastic local solvers (RANSAC, J-Linkage, T-Linkage), exhaustive global search (Branch-and-Bound), or learned models (NeurVPS, CONSAC, PARSAC). The key conceptual departure is the proposed truncated multi-selection error, which unifies line-VP association and VP localization into a single QCQP that admits a convex SDP relaxation with provable tightness. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, the work repurposes well-established SDP relaxation theory from robust geometry estimation (e.g., essential matrix, point cloud registration) and adapts it to the combinatorial structure of multi-model line clustering, then decomposes the resulting full SDP into sequentially solvable single-block subproblems for efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GlobustVP fills a previously empty niche: a vanishing point estimator with a global optimality guarantee (under mild distinguishability conditions) that runs in polynomial time via an off-the-shelf SDP solver, avoiding both the stochasticity of RANSAC-family methods and the exponential worst-case of Branch-and-Bound. The 96.1% angular accuracy at 10° on YUD—roughly 10 points above the best traditional and learning-based baselines—demonstrates that the convex formulation is not merely theoretically appealing but practically competitive. Limitations include the strict Manhattan-world assumption (exactly three orthogonal directions), dependence on known camera intrinsics, and the need for a sampling strategy to handle large line counts, which introduces a small accuracy-efficiency trade-off. Open questions include whether the tight relaxation property holds under heavier noise or non-Manhattan scenes, and whether the sequential single-block decomposition can be parallelized or extended to more than three vanishing points.
