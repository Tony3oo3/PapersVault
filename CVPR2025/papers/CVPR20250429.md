## Problem

Batch-to-global matching methods for dataset distillation (e.g., SRe²L, CDA) synthesize each image independently under the same global supervision signals, causing severe intra-class homogeneity where all synthetic images converge to nearly identical representations. This limits the informativeness and generalization of the distilled dataset, particularly at higher IPC settings where diversity is most critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250429/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled RGB image dataset (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-1K at 224×224) and a pretrained teacher model that serves as a ranker for selecting initialization patches. Real image patches are cropped, scored by the teacher's per-class probability, and those near the per-class median are chosen as starting points for optimization. The output is a compact synthetic dataset with IPC images per class, each paired with soft labels, where images within a class exhibit varying degrees of optimization (from heavily refined to lightly touched), producing a natural difficulty gradient.

## Method

DELT introduces an EarlyLate training scheme that partitions the target IPC images into M sub-batches and optimizes them with progressively fewer iterations: the first batch receives MI iterations (e.g., 4K), while each subsequent batch receives MI − RI fewer, with the final batch receiving only RI iterations (e.g., 500). Later batches join earlier ones in a shared recovery phase via concatenation, so GPU time is reused across all images in a single scan rather than running separate optimization loops. Initialization uses teacher-ranked real image patches (medium-difficulty crops) instead of Gaussian noise, providing semantically meaningful starting points that accelerate convergence and improve realism. The optimization objective follows the CDA batch-to-global matching formulation with SRe²L's BatchNorm distribution regularization, applied between each round. This design produces synthetic images at varying optimization stages—acting as an implicit difficulty regularizer—while reducing total computation by ~39.3% compared to uniform-iteration baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with IPC 50 and ResNet-101, DELT achieves 66.1% top-1 accuracy, outperforming RDED (61.2%) by 4.9% and SRe²L (60.8%) by 5.3%. On CIFAR-10 at IPC 50 with ResNet-101, DELT reaches 54.1%, a 19.2% improvement over SRe²L (34.9%). Across all five benchmarks (CIFAR-10, ImageNette, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-100, ImageNet-1K), DELT establishes state-of-the-art accuracy in 13 out of 15 backbone-IPC configurations using ResNet-101 or MobileNet-v2, while also reducing synthesis time by up to 39.3% compared to prior batch-to-global methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DELT consistently outperforms SRe²L [37] across nearly all settings, with the largest gap on CIFAR-10 IPC 50 ResNet-101 (54.1% vs. 34.9%, +19.2%) and ImageNet-1K IPC 50 ResNet-101 (66.1% vs. 60.8%, +5.3%). Against RDED [29], DELT wins on most configurations (e.g., ImageNet-1K IPC 50: 66.1% vs. 61.2%, +4.9%) but loses on a few small-IPC or small-dataset settings such as Tiny-ImageNet IPC 1 ResNet-18 (9.3% vs. 9.7%) and ImageNet-100 IPC 10 ResNet-18 (28.2% vs. 36.0%). Compared to CDA [36], DELT gains +2.4% without initialization (55.9% vs. 53.5%) and +2.6% with initialization (46.1% vs. 43.5%) on ImageNet-1K IPC 10. Against G-VBSM [26], DELT wins on all five cross-architecture backbones at IPC 10 (e.g., ResNet-18: 46.4% vs. 41.4%, +5.0%). On the ConvNet backbone (Table 2), DELT surpasses MTT [3], TESLA [5], and IDM [42] on all reported settings, though it trails DATM [11] on Tiny-ImageNet IPC 1 (12.4% vs. 17.1%) and RDED on ImageNet-100 IPC 10 (24.7% vs. 29.6%). Finally, DELT outperforms MinimaxDiffusion [10] by 1.8% at IPC 10 and 0.6% at IPC 50 on ImageNet-1K, confirming that optimization-based synthesis with EarlyLate training yields better downstream accuracy than diffusion-generated images.

## Relation to Prior Work

DELT builds directly on the batch-to-global matching paradigm established by SRe²L and CDA, which decouple model training from data synthesis by supervising synthetic images with global BatchNorm statistics rather than per-batch original data. It departs from prior diversity-enhancement efforts like G-VBSM (which multiplies matching signals across backbones) and RDED (which stitches real patches without optimization) by introducing a temporal dimension: the EarlyLate scheme assigns progressively fewer optimization iterations to successive sub-batches of synthetic images, inspired by early-stopping heuristics from noisy-label learning. The use of teacher-ranked real image patches for initialization extends RDED's selection criterion by adding iterative refinement on top, and the concatenation training strategy borrows the incremental learning idea from multi-stage distillation methods like PDD and D3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in simultaneously improving intra-class diversity, boosting accuracy by 2–5% over prior SOTA, and cutting synthesis time by up to 39%—a rare triple win in a field where these objectives typically trade off. The EarlyLate mechanism is simple enough to plug into any batch-to-global matching pipeline, making it broadly adoptable. However, the approach remains tethered to the BN-statistics supervision signal, so it inherits the fundamental representational ceiling of that paradigm; whether the diversity gains translate to improved robustness under distribution shift or adversarial conditions remains unexplored. An open question is whether the EarlyLate principle generalizes beyond image optimization to other synthesis objectives (e.g., diffusion-based or GAN-based distillation).
