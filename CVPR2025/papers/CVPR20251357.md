## Problem

Existing integrative few-shot classification and segmentation (FS-CS) methods rely almost exclusively on visual support data, leaving textual cues from class labels untapped. In FS-CS, ambiguous object boundaries and overlapping classes cause under-segmentation and misclassification because limited visual examples struggle to capture high-level semantics. The paper asks whether text alone can serve as a viable support modality for FS-CS and whether fusing text with visual support yields complementary gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a query image and a support set that can be text-only (class labels alone, no images) or text-augmented (support images with ground-truth masks plus class-label text). A frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder processes both modalities, producing 625 patch tokens per image and 77 text tokens per label. The model outputs per-class binary classification scores (exact ratio) and pixel-level segmentation masks (mIoU) for all N target classes in the support set, operating under N-way K-shot episodic evaluation.

## Method

TA-CST uses a dual correlation transformer: one branch computes unnormalized dot-product correlations between query patch tokens and support image class/patch tokens (vision-only), while the other correlates projected query vision tokens with support text EOS/patch tokens (vision-text). A Feature Enrichment Module then augments each branch's foreground maps via a single multi-head attention layer whose Key and Value are built from concatenated 1×1, 3×3 atrous convolutions (dilation rates 3, 6, 10), and global average pooling, injecting multi-scale inductive bias while preserving global context. In the text-only setting, a zero-shot knowledge distillation loss aligns the model's classification output with CLIP's zero-shot predictions generated from positive/negative prompts ('This is a photo of {class}' / 'This is not a photo of {class}'), preserving CLIP's generalization on small datasets. The text-augmented setting fuses vision-only and vision-text classification and segmentation predictions with learnable scaling factors, and is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy losses under episodic meta-learning (1-way-1-shot training episodes).

## Results

On COCO-20i (1-way-1-shot), the Text-Augmented model achieves 92.1% classification exact ratio and 52.9% segmentation mIoU (averaged over 4 folds), improving over the CST(CLIP-B/16) baseline by +3.7% and +6.6% respectively. On Pascal-5i (1-way-1-shot), it reaches 91.2% cls ER and 66.2% seg mIoU, a +2.2%/+3.8% gain over the same baseline. In the 2-way-1-shot setting, gains are larger: +8.0%/+6.5% on COCO-20i and +4.3%/+4.0% on Pascal-5i. Notably, the text-only variant (no support images) already matches or exceeds the CST(CLIP-B/16) baseline on classification (90.3% vs 88.4% on COCO-20i, 90.2% vs 89.0% on Pascal-5i), demonstrating that class-label text alone is a viable support signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Text-Augmented model outperforms all seven baselines on both COCO-20i and Pascal-5i across both 1-way-1-shot and 2-way-1-shot settings. Against the closest competitor, CST(CLIP-B/16)* (the same architecture re-implemented with CLIP-B/16), it gains +3.7% cls ER and +6.6% seg mIoU on COCO-20i 1-way-1-shot, and +8.0%/+6.5% in the 2-way-1-shot setting. The text-only variant already surpasses CST(CLIP-B/16)* on classification (90.3% vs 88.4% on COCO-20i) and is competitive on segmentation (49.3% vs 46.3%), while trailing slightly on Pascal-5i segmentation (59.3% vs 62.4%). Older baselines (PANet, PFENet, HSNet, ASNet) using ResNet-50 backbones are outperformed by 10–25+ points in segmentation mIoU, largely attributable to the stronger CLIP backbone. On the weakly-supervised Pascal-5i benchmark (Table 3), the text-only model (89.1% cls ER, 40.8% seg mIoU) surpasses GPT-4Vision [17] (86.4% cls ER, 38.2% seg mIoU) in 1-way-1-shot, and the text-augmented variant further extends the lead to 90.4%/42.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the correlation-transformer lineage established by ASNet (Kang et al., CVPR 2022) and CST (Kang et al., CVPR 2023), which generate foreground maps via query-support feature correlations for integrative few-shot classification and segmentation. The key departure is replacing the vision-only DINO backbone with frozen CLIP ViT-B/16, enabling a dual correlation transformer that computes both vision-vision and vision-text correlations, and introducing a feature enrichment module with granular atrous-convolution KV pairs to capture multi-scale and global semantics in a single attention layer. The zero-shot knowledge distillation loss, which aligns the model's text-only classification output with CLIP's zero-shot predictions via positive/negative prompts, is a novel training objective not present in prior FS-CS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that class-label text alone yields FS-CS performance competitive with vision-only methods on Pascal-5i and COCO-20i challenges the assumption that visual support is necessary for few-shot segmentation, opening the door to text-driven few-shot pipelines in data-scarce or privacy-sensitive settings. However, the evidence is confined to two small-scale benchmarks (5 and 20 classes) with 1–2 shot episodes, leaving open whether the text-only advantage persists on larger, more diverse datasets or in multi-class scenes with fine-grained boundaries. The modest runtime and memory overhead of the dual-correlation design (5.6 GB vs. 4.7 GB for CST-CLIP) makes the approach practical, but broader validation across architectures, shot counts, and dataset scales remains to be established.
