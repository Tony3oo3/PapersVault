## Problem

Image-text foundation models like CLIP suffer from spurious correlations that cause severe worst-group accuracy drops, yet existing debiasing methods either require expensive group annotations or retrain entire backbones. The paper addresses two specific gaps: (1) how to more reliably identify minority groups without labels by leveraging the text encoder's class proxies, and (2) how to design a debiasing training algorithm with a principled objective (balanced group error) rather than ad-hoc reweighting heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images encoded by a frozen CLIP backbone (ResNet-50, ViT-L/14, etc.) and class-name text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS]') to obtain class proxy vectors. It outputs a standard K-way class label, but internally the debiased classifier is constructed by summing group-level logit vectors in weight space, so no group inference is needed at test time. Only a linear probe (4,096 parameters for ResNet-50, <0.01% of the full model) or a lightweight adapter/prompt is trained.

## Method

PPA is a three-step pipeline. Step 1 (Project): image features are projected onto the null space of the CLIP text-encoder class proxies (Π = I − Zᵀ(ZZᵀ)⁻¹Z), stripping class information so the resulting biased classifier must rely on spurious features; it is trained with a logit-adjustment loss to further suppress class-prior shortcuts. Step 2 (Probe): misclassified samples from the biased model define pseudo group labels, and a linear group scorer is trained with a group logit adjustment loss that adds τ·ln(β̂) offsets to the softmax, directly targeting the balanced group error objective. Step 3 (Aggregate): the final debiased classifier is obtained by summing the weight rows of the group scorer corresponding to each class, converting group prediction into class prediction in weight space. Proposition 1 proves the projection increases the spurious-feature regression coefficient, and Proposition 2 proves the aggregated classifier is Bayes optimal for minimizing balanced group error.

## Results

On CLIP ResNet-50, PPA achieves worst-group accuracy (WGA) of 84.3% on Waterbirds, 91.1% on CelebA, and 90.8% on MetaShift, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art CFR by 7.4%, 17.4%, and 9.3% respectively. On CLIP ViT-L/14, PPA reaches 87.2%, 90.4%, and 94.8% WGA on the same three benchmarks. The method uses fewer than 0.01% of the foundation model's parameters (4,096 trainable parameters vs. 25.5M for CLIP ResNet-50) while remaining competitive with group-supervised methods like GroupDRO (75.1% WGA on Waterbirds). On Living-17 and BAR, PPA also achieves the highest WGA (65.0% and 76.8% on ResNet-50; 85.0% and 91.5% on ViT-L/14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPA outperforms the previous state-of-the-art CFR [47] by 7.4% (Waterbirds), 17.4% (CelebA), and 9.3% (MetaShift) in WGA on CLIP ResNet-50, with the largest gap on CelebA where CFR's contrastive alignment strategy is less effective. Against CA [48], which uses zero-shot correctness to identify minority groups, PPA leads by 0.6% on Waterbirds, 1.1% on CelebA, and a substantial 12.9% on MetaShift. PPA substantially surpasses JTT [21] (by 22.6–30.9% WGA) and CnC [49] (by 23.1–27.2% WGA), confirming that projecting out class proxies and applying group logit adjustment are both critical improvements over simple ERM-based bias induction. PPA also beats AFR [30] by 35.9–37.7% WGA and Orth-Cali [3] by 10.3–8.9% WGA. Notably, PPA's WGA on Waterbirds (84.3%) exceeds the group-supervised GroupDRO [32] (75.1%) by 9.2% despite not using any group annotations, and it is competitive with DFR [17] (73.2%) on the same metric. The trade-off is a modest drop in in-distribution accuracy (e.g., 88.3% vs. 93.5% Avg on Waterbirds), which the paper attributes to the inherent tension between worst-group and average performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage biased-then-debiased paradigm introduced by JTT (Liu et al., ICML 2021) for group-robust classification without group labels, and adapts the logit adjustment framework of Menon et al. (ICLR 2021) from class priors to group priors. The key conceptual departure is the 'Project' step: rather than amplifying bias through loss design (GCE in LfF) or hyperparameter tuning (JTT), PPA removes CLIP text-encoder class proxies from image features via null-space projection, forcing the biased classifier to rely on spurious features. The 'Aggregate' step—weight-space summation of group-specific linear probes into a class classifier—eliminates the group-inference overhead that plagues prior debiased methods, and the group logit adjustment loss is shown to be Bayes optimal for minimizing balanced group error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPA offers a practically attractive recipe: under 0.01% tunable parameters, no group annotations, and a clean theoretical guarantee (Bayes optimality for BGE), making it a strong default for efficient fine-tuning of CLIP-style models on spurious-correlation benchmarks. The projection trick is simple to implement and generalizes across linear probes, prompt tuning, and adapters, suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include whether the projection step remains effective when class proxies are poorly separated or when spurious correlations are multi-dimensional, and whether the misclassification-as-minority heuristic (inherited from JTT) degrades when the biased model is not sufficiently biased. The modest drop in in-distribution accuracy (avg ~1.8 points) also raises the question of whether the BGE-optimal solution can be achieved without sacrificing average performance.
