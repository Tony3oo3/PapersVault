## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models struggle to generate temporally coherent videos with smooth, natural motion. Existing guidance techniques such as diffusion posterior sampling require backpropagation across all frames, making them computationally prohibitive and unstable for video, while fine-tuning approaches are impractical given model size. There is a lack of a reliable, computationally efficient inference-time guidance mechanism that enforces temporal consistency without retraining the diffusion model.

## Input / Output

For text-to-video generation, the method takes a natural-language prompt (e.g., "Close up video of japanese food") and produces an N-frame video sequence in a compressed latent space, decoded to pixel space. A placeholder string of learnable tokens is appended to the prompt during optimization, and the method operates on denoised frame estimates via Tweedie's formula at selected timesteps. For the image-to-video extension, a reference image is additionally provided to DynamiCrafter alongside the text prompt. The discriminator consumes two-channel optical flow (H×W) computed by RAFT between pairs of decoded frames.

## Method

MotionPrompt replaces direct latent-space guidance with on-the-fly prompt optimization: learnable token embeddings are appended to the text prompt and updated via gradient descent during a subset of DDIM reverse-sampling steps, exploiting the fact that the text condition influences all frames simultaneously so that gradients from only a small subset of frames suffice. A ViT-based discriminator is trained (via transfer learning on a pretrained ViT with a projection layer and 3-layer MLP head) to classify optical flow between random frame pairs as real (from DAVIS/WebVid) or fake (from the target video model). During sampling, a pair of denoised frames is decoded, their optical flow is computed with RAFT, and the discriminator's logit drives the token update alongside a total-variation loss on the flow field and an L2 anchor to the initial token embeddings. Optimization is applied for only ~10–15 of the 50 DDIM steps with 3 inner iterations per step, after which the original prompt is restored to preserve content fidelity.

## Results

Across three text-to-video diffusion models (Lavie, AnimateDiff, VideoCrafter2), MotionPrompt consistently improves temporal consistency metrics: Subject Consistency increases by 0.005–0.004, Background Consistency by 0.002–0.022, and Temporal Flickering by 0.014–0.030 (VBench, 800 prompts, 50-step DDIM). Motion Smoothness also improves (e.g., 0.9690→0.9765 for Lavie, 0.9578→0.9599 for AnimateDiff). However, Dynamic Degree decreases (e.g., 0.5150→0.3963 for Lavie), reflecting a trade-off between motion richness and temporal coherence, while Overall Consistency (text alignment) remains largely stable (±0.01). In a 100-participant A/B user study, the method was preferred 66.5% of the time over AnimateDiff, 55.1% over Lavie, and 53.0% over VideoCrafter2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionPrompt is evaluated as a plug-in on three T2V baselines (Lavie, AnimateDiff, VideoCrafter2) and one I2V model (DynamiCrafter). Against all three T2V baselines, the method wins on Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Temporal Flickering, and Motion Smoothness, with the largest gains seen on Lavie (Temporal Flickering +0.014, Motion Smoothness +0.008) and VideoCrafter2 (Background Consistency +0.022). The consistent trade-off is a reduction in Dynamic Degree (e.g., −0.119 for Lavie, −0.058 for AnimateDiff, −0.015 for VideoCrafter2), indicating slightly less vigorous motion in exchange for smoother, more coherent frames. Overall Consistency (text-prompt alignment) drops marginally (≤0.009), suggesting the prompt optimization preserves semantics well. In the user study, the method is preferred in a clear majority of comparisons (53–67% win rate), with the strongest preference against AnimateDiff (66.5% win). For the I2V extension on DynamiCrafter, the method qualitatively reduces appearance-detail drift and object-boundary artifacts without a dedicated quantitative benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionPrompt repurposes the learnable-token prompt optimization framework from Um and Ye [35] (originally designed for minority image sampling) as an inference-time guidance mechanism for video diffusion models, exploiting the fact that a text prompt influences all frames simultaneously. This sidesteps the computational bottleneck of diffusion posterior sampling (DPS), which requires backpropagation across every frame in a video sequence. The guidance signal itself is novel: rather than a likelihood-based or CLIP-based objective, the authors train a ViT-based discriminator on optical flow to distinguish real from generated motion, then use its output as the loss for prompt optimization. This positions the work at the intersection of prompt tuning, optical flow analysis, and diffusion guidance, but departs from prior optical-flow-in-video-synthesis work (FlowVid, flow-grounded prediction) by using flow as a *discriminative* signal rather than a generative conditioning input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MotionPrompt offers a training-free, plug-and-play improvement to temporal consistency across multiple T2V architectures (AnimateDiff, Lavie, VideoCrafter2, DynamiCrafter) without modifying model weights, making it immediately applicable to existing pipelines. The cross-dataset discriminator generalization result—where a discriminator trained on one model's outputs improves another model's generation—suggests the learned motion-realism signal is somewhat model-agnostic, opening the door to a single shared discriminator. Key limitations remain: the objective is not grounded in physics, so gains in flow realism do not guarantee physically plausible motion, and the method trades some dynamic degree for smoothness. An open question is whether more physically informed discriminators (e.g., leveraging point correspondence or 3D motion priors) could close this gap.
