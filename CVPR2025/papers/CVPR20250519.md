## Problem

Despite advances in 3D indoor scene synthesis and text-to-image generation, no existing method addresses the unique challenges of artistic stage scenography: managing audience sightlines to prevent foreground occlusion of key background elements, maintaining thematic and narrative fidelity to a theater script, and producing spatially coherent 3D layouts that respect the fixed front-facing audience perspective. Traditional stage design remains highly time-consuming and expertise-dependent, with no AI framework purpose-built for this task.

## Input / Output

The system takes a raw theater script (56–1380 words) as sole input and produces two outputs: a set of 3D foreground entities (positioned within a 1000×1000×1000 cm³ stage volume, retrieved from a 50,092-asset Objaverse subset) and a 2D background image generated by a layout-controlled diffusion model. The background is constrained by bounding boxes derived from audience-perspective occlusion projections, ensuring critical atmospheric elements remain visible from the front row. Entity placement uses corner-coordinate bounding boxes with height ranges rather than single-point representations.

## Method

StageDesigner is a training-free pipeline with three modules. Script Analysis uses GPT-4o to decompose the input script into a Scene Description (entities, spatial relationships) and an Imagery Description (themes, emotional tone). Foreground Generation first produces anchor entities with corner coordinates and height ranges, then places non-anchor entities and ornaments via a Multi-level Collision Map that searches unoccupied regions on the stage floor or on anchor surfaces (front, left, right, top) to prevent overlap; entities are then retrieved from Objaverse using combined CLIP image similarity and Sentence-BERT text matching. Background Generation projects each foreground entity's silhouette onto the background plane using parallel sightlines from the leftmost and rightmost front-row audience positions to compute occlusion intervals, then feeds the imagery description plus non-occluded bounding boxes into the ReCo layout-controlled diffusion model to synthesize a thematically aligned background that avoids obstructing key foreground elements.

## Results

On the StagePro-V1 dataset (276 stages), StageDesigner achieves a dramatically lower Out-of-Bound volume (0.0468 m³) and Overlap-Inter-Stage volume (0.756 m³) compared to the adapted LayoutGPT baseline (6.46 m³ and 18.2 m³ respectively), indicating far superior spatial coherence. StageDesigner also generates more diverse entity layouts (11.7 average classes per stage vs. 7.46) and achieves higher CLIP similarity to input scripts (30.3 vs. 29.1). In user studies with 69 general users and 14 stage design experts, StageDesigner received 70% higher favorability than LayoutGPT on both Layout Coherence and Overall Preference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is an adapted version of LayoutGPT [6], originally designed for single-room indoor furniture layout via LLM-generated CSS coordinates. StageDesigner wins decisively on spatial coherence metrics: OOB is reduced by ~99.3% (0.0468 vs. 6.46 m³) and OIS by ~95.8% (0.756 vs. 18.2 m³), attributed to its corner-coordinate representation and multi-level collision map. StageDesigner also leads on Class Diversity (11.7 vs. 7.46) and CLIP-sim (30.3 vs. 29.1), producing richer and more thematically aligned stages. The one metric where LayoutGPT scores higher is IWG (14.5 vs. 9.03 m³), but the authors attribute this to LayoutGPT generating oversized entities that overlap ground truth more while simultaneously causing more out-of-bounds and inter-entity collisions. In the user study, StageDesigner was preferred over LayoutGPT by a wide margin (70% higher favorability) on both Layout Coherence and Overall Preference across 83 participants.

## Relation to Prior Work

StageDesigner sits at the intersection of LLM-driven scene synthesis (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, Anyhome) and layout-controlled diffusion models (ReCo, GLIGEN, BoxDiff), but departs from indoor scene generation by introducing two stage-specific mechanisms: a multi-level collision map for spatial coherence and a foreground projection module that models audience sightlines to prevent occlusion of background elements. The background generation directly leverages ReCo's region-controlled diffusion architecture, while entity retrieval borrows the Objaverse/Holodeck asset pipeline. Conceptually, the paper repurposes general-purpose LLM planning and diffusion-based image synthesis for a domain (theatrical scenography) where audience perspective and narrative atmosphere impose constraints absent from prior indoor scene work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dedicated framework and dataset for script-to-stage generation, filling a gap between generic indoor scene synthesis and the specific spatial, thematic, and perspectival demands of theatrical design. The StagePro-V1 dataset (276 professionally annotated stages) provides a foundational benchmark for this niche, though its small size and single-baseline evaluation (adapted LayoutGPT) limit the strength of the empirical claims. Open questions include whether the training-free LLM pipeline scales to multi-scene productions, whether the foreground projection generalizes to non-proscenium or immersive stage geometries, and how the approach integrates with professional 3D tooling beyond the mentioned Blender import.
