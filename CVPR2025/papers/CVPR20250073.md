## Problem

Monocular video depth estimation suffers from inherent ambiguities and temporal flickering, as existing per-frame depth estimators lack temporal coherence and video depth methods still produce jittery results. While humans intuitively perceive depth changes from how tracked points converge or diverge during motion, no prior method has systematically exploited the depth cues encoded in 2D point trajectories to predict temporally smooth depth over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250073/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video from which an off-the-shelf point tracker (CoTracker or LocoTrack) extracts N query trajectories plus a uniform 24×24 grid of supporting trajectories, each with per-frame occlusion status. The model outputs per-point metric depth over time (i.e., 3D trajectories) by predicting log depth ratios within sliding windows of W=8 frames, accumulating them across the sequence, and finally scale-matching against an MDE model (ZoeDepth or DepthPro) per visible subsequence. Notably, the model is trained exclusively on the synthetic Kubric dataset with no pre-trained feature backbone, yet generalizes zero-shot to real-world driving, egocentric, and studio videos.

## Method

Seurat employs a two-branch spatial-temporal transformer: a supporting branch encodes the uniform grid trajectories to capture global scene motion, while a query branch processes user-defined trajectories and receives global motion context via cross-attention from the supporting branch, preventing bias from uneven query point distribution. A theoretical analysis (Eq. 4) shows that depth ratio is proportional to the square root of projected point density change, motivating the learned approach since explicit density computation is impractical. To handle long sequences, the model operates in sliding windows (W=8, stride S) where supporting trajectories are re-initialized per window while query trajectories persist, and a window-wise L1 log-ratio depth loss trains the model to predict depth change relative to each window's first frame. At inference, per-window log ratios are accumulated and exponentiated, then piecewise scale-matched to an MDE model's metric depth over each point's visible subsequence to recover absolute scale.

## Results

On the TAPVid-3D benchmark (per-trajectory depth scaling, Table 1), Seurat with CoTracker achieves an average APD of 28.6 and 3D-AJ of 18.0, substantially outperforming the best per-frame depth baseline (ZoeDepth + Oracle tracker: APD 23.4, 3D-AJ 15.1) and the best video depth baseline (DepthCrafter + Oracle tracker: APD 22.0, 3D-AJ 13.7). Seurat also achieves dramatically better temporal coherence (TC = 0.05 average) compared to per-frame methods (TC ≈ 0.46–1.50) and video depth methods (TC ≈ 0.26–3.39), with over 40× improvement on DriveTrack. Under affine-invariant evaluation (Table 2), Seurat + DepthPro + CoTracker reaches APD 19.0 and 3D-AJ 12.0, again surpassing all video depth baselines. The oracle depth upper bound sits at APD 69.0, indicating substantial headroom.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seurat decisively outperforms all per-frame depth estimator baselines (ZoeDepth, DepthPro) when paired with the same tracker: with CoTracker, Seurat gains +7.9 APD over ZoeDepth (28.6 vs. 20.7) and +14.6 APD over DepthPro (28.6 vs. 14.0) on average. Against video depth estimators, Seurat also wins clearly: it surpasses DepthCrafter + CoTracker by +8.9 APD (28.6 vs. 19.7) and ChronoDepth + CoTracker by +16.8 APD (28.6 vs. 11.8), while achieving 7–67× lower temporal coherence error. Compared to TAPIR-3D (the only other trajectory-based 3D method), Seurat more than doubles APD (28.6 vs. 15.0) and 3D-AJ (18.0 vs. 8.6). The only method that outperforms Seurat is the oracle depth upper bound (APD 69.0), which uses ground-truth depth and thus is not a fair comparison. Notably, Seurat is trained solely on the synthetic Kubric dataset yet generalizes to all three real-world test domains (Aria, DriveTrack, PStudio), whereas the per-frame baselines were trained on 14–21 large-scale real datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

Seurat repurposes the output of off-the-shelf 2D point trackers (CoTracker, LocoTrack) as the primary input for depth estimation, drawing a conceptual analogy to structured-light scanning where pattern deformation encodes 3D geometry. It departs from per-frame monocular depth estimators (ZoeDepth, DepthPro, MiDaS) that rely on large annotated real datasets and strong pretrained backbones, and from video depth methods (DepthCrafter, ChronoDepth) that inherit temporal consistency from diffusion or large-scale video models. The two-branch transformer with supporting grid trajectories and cross-attention injection, combined with a sliding-window log-ratio depth loss, is a new architectural and training formulation that treats trajectory density variation as an implicit depth signal rather than an explicit geometric measurement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization from a single synthetic dataset (Kubric) to diverse real-world benchmarks (egocentric, driving, studio) without any pretrained backbone challenges the prevailing assumption that large-scale real annotated data is necessary for robust depth estimation. The 40× improvement in temporal coherence over per-frame methods addresses a persistent practical problem in video depth (flickering), making the approach attractive for downstream tasks like 3D reconstruction and robotics. Limitations include dependence on tracker accuracy (errors propagate directly), the local-rigidity assumption that breaks for deformable objects, and the need for an external metric depth model to anchor absolute scale. Open questions include whether the trajectory-density principle extends to highly dynamic or non-rigid scenes, and whether the approach can be made fully self-contained without the MDE anchoring step.
