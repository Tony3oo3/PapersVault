## Problem

Scene-level 3D reconstruction is bottlenecked by training data: the largest clean scene dataset (DL3DV) contains only 10K scenes, far fewer than object-level datasets, and suffers from limited diversity, small camera baselines, and noisy annotations. Collecting more real scene data is labor-intensive and hard to scale, yet effective training requires clean, diverse multi-view images with precise camera annotations. The paper asks whether synthesized data can close this gap and, if so, what properties of the synthetic data matter for reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 32 dense-view RGB images (at 128×128 or 256×256 resolution) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, where input and target views are non-overlapping and evenly sampled around the scene. It outputs pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians (one per input pixel) that encode position, covariance, color, and opacity, which can be rendered to arbitrary novel views. The geometry loss additionally consumes ground-truth depth maps from the synthesized scenes to supervise Gaussian center locations, a supervision signal unavailable in real data.

## Method

MegaSynth is a procedurally generated, non-semantic dataset of 700K scenes built by populating randomized cube-shaped room volumes with shape primitives (cubes, spheres, cylinders, cones) arranged in object bounding boxes, augmented with height-field perturbations, thin wireframe structures, and axis-aligned geometries. Each primitive receives random PBR textures (albedo, normal, roughness, material maps) and scenes are lit with randomized combinations of ambient light, directional sunlight through procedural windows, and emissive objects. Camera poses are sampled with heuristic controls over baseline distance, field-of-view, and inner/outer scene partitioning to balance coverage and diversity. Training uses a mixed-data strategy—either pre-training on MegaSynth followed by fine-tuning on DL3DV, or joint training—applying a combined rendering loss (MSE + perceptual) and a geometry loss (Smooth-L1 on Gaussian locations derived from depth maps) that exploits the exact geometry metadata available only in synthetic data. The approach is applied to both GS-LRM (transformer backbone) and Long-LRM (Mamba backbone), yielding 1.2–1.8 dB PSNR gains over real-data-only baselines across in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks.

## Results

Training GS-LRM and Long-LRM with both DL3DV and MegaSynth yields consistent PSNR gains of 1.2–1.8 dB across all test datasets and resolutions. At resolution 128 with 32 input views, GS-LRM (ours) achieves 25.75 PSNR / 0.859 SSIM / 0.130 LPIPS on in-domain DL3DV and 25.46 PSNR / 0.846 SSIM / 0.154 LPIPS on out-of-domain Hypersim, while Long-LRM (ours) reaches 25.44 / 0.853 / 0.136 and 25.01 / 0.836 / 0.164 respectively. On MipNeRF360 & Tanks and Temples, GS-LRM (ours) scores 21.19 PSNR / 0.672 SSIM / 0.235 LPIPS. Inference time is 0.11–0.12 s per scene, over 2000× faster than per-scene optimization-based 3DGS (5.2 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the optimization-based 3DGS baseline, both MegaSynth-trained models achieve substantially higher PSNR (e.g., GS-LRM ours: 25.75 vs. 24.27 on DL3DV; 25.46 vs. 20.67 on Hypersim) while running ~2000× faster (0.11 s vs. 5.2 min). Compared to Long-LRM trained on DL3DV alone, adding MegaSynth improves PSNR by +1.26 dB (DL3DV), +1.60 dB (Hypersim), and +1.18 dB (MipNeRF360&TT); GS-LRM shows analogous gains of +1.15, +1.57, and +1.26 dB respectively. The out-of-domain gains consistently exceed in-domain gains, indicating MegaSynth enhances generalization. In a direct comparison with other synthetic datasets (Table 5, 8 input views), adding Kubric (1M scenes of realistic 3D assets) or Front3D (6K artist-designed rooms) to DL3DV yields no meaningful improvement (e.g., DL3DV+Kubric: 18.28 PSNR vs. DL3DV alone: 18.31), whereas DL3DV+MegaSynth reaches 19.58 PSNR on DL3DV and 19.88 on Hypersim, confirming that non-semantic geometric diversity and controlled complexity matter more than photorealistic asset quality for reconstruction training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LRM-Zero line of work, which used non-semantic shape primitives to generate object-level training data for large reconstruction models, to the more challenging scene-level setting with controlled lighting, camera poses, and object composition. It departs from prior scene generation methods (Infinigen, Procthor, Front3D) that aim to replicate real-world semantic distributions and are thus constrained by the complexity of modeling object affordances and scene composition. The key conceptual departure is the argument that multi-view reconstruction is fundamentally a non-semantic task—evidenced by the success of COLMAP, MVS, and NeRF—so semantic fidelity in training data is unnecessary, enabling virtually unlimited procedural generation. The base architectures (GS-LRM, Long-LRM) are used as-is; the contribution is entirely in the data pipeline and mixed training strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MegaSynth provides a 50× scale-up over the prior largest scene dataset (DL3DV), offering a practical and scalable path to improving feed-forward 3D reconstruction models without the labor-intensive collection of real scene data. The finding that non-semantic synthetic data can substitute for real data (and that realistic synthetic datasets like Kubric and Front3D fail to help) challenges the field's implicit assumption that training data must be semantically realistic. Limitations include the primarily indoor focus, a residual semantic gap on complex geometry patterns, and the fact that the 'comparability' claim (MegaSynth-only vs. real-only) still shows a ~2.4 dB gap on Hypersim. Open questions include whether outdoor scene characteristics can be incorporated, whether the approach transfers to other 3D tasks beyond reconstruction and depth estimation, and whether the non-semantic insight holds for tasks that do require semantics (e.g., scene understanding, navigation).
