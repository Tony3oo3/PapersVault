## Problem

Existing any-to-any generative models rely on implicit neural representations learned from large-scale training data, making them inflexible for rare or unanticipated cross-modal tasks and non-interruptible for multi-step workflows. Agentic alternatives introduce system instability and lack a unified formal representation of task structure. The paper asks whether an explicit, training-free symbolic language can decompose and execute arbitrary multimodal generative tasks with greater flexibility, editability, and generalizability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252498/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language task description, input data of arbitrary modality (images, text, audio, video, 3D), and a set of system constraints (available functions, valid parameter ranges, model compatibility) as input. It produces an executable symbolic workflow in A-LANGUAGE format—a structured program specifying functions, their parameter configurations, and directed data-flow topology—which is then compiled and run through ComfyUI to yield the final generated artifact (image, video, audio, or 3D mesh). The symbolic workflow itself is a first-class output: it is human-readable, editable at the function or parameter level, and interruptible mid-execution, unlike the opaque forward pass of a unified neural model.

## Method

The core contribution is A-LANGUAGE, a formal symbolic representation that decomposes any generative task into a triple (F, Φ, T): a set of atomic computational functions, their parameter configurations, and a directed topology specifying precise output-to-input data flows between functions. A training-free inference engine built on GPT-4o performs two-stage generation: first inferring the required function and parameter set from the natural language instruction and constraints, then constructing the topological connections among those functions. An iterative refinement module is triggered on compilation or execution failure, feeding error signals back to the LM to correct the symbolic flow. The system uses retrieval-augmented generation over 16 curated reference programs and in-context learning with syntax guidance (following Gupta et al.), and supports three interchangeable syntax styles (declarative, dataflow, pseudo-natural) that preserve full expressiveness while trading off readability and structural rigidity.

## Results

On ComfyBench, the method achieves a 41.00% overall resolve rate, outperforming ComfyAgent (32.50%), LWM (15.50%), and HuggingGPT (11.50%), with the largest gains on Complex (28.33% vs. 21.67%) and Creative (22.50% vs. 15.00%) tasks. In the 120-task user study, the method achieves a 94% win rate over Show-o and 98% over LVM on Text-to-Image, a 67% win rate over commercial Gen-3 on Image-to-Video, and a 100% alignment / 98% quality win rate over Unified-IO on Text-to-Audio. On compilation and execution pass rates (Pass@1), the symbolic approach reaches 0.98/0.87 versus GenAgent's 0.84/0.63, demonstrating higher reliability without multi-agent overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unified multimodal models, the symbolic approach wins decisively: it achieves a 94% win rate over Show-o and 98% over LVM on Text-to-Image, and a 100% alignment win rate over Unified-IO 2 on Text-to-Audio. On Image-to-Video, it surpasses the commercial Runway Gen-3 model with a 67% win rate in text-outcome alignment. On the standardized ComfyBench benchmark, the method outperforms all three baselines—ComfyAgent (32.50%), LWM (15.50%), and HuggingGPT (11.50%)—by 8.5, 25.5, and 29.5 percentage points respectively, with the gap widening on Complex and Creative tasks. In the head-to-head agentic comparison (Table 5), the symbolic inference engine achieves 0.98 compilation and 0.87 execution pass rates versus GenAgent's 0.84 and 0.63, indicating that the simpler two-stage LM inference avoids the error amplification inherent in multi-agent planning. Notably, the method is the only one to successfully handle all 12 task categories (including 3D mesh generation and multi-view image synthesis), whereas Show-o, SEED-X, LWM, and Unified-IO 2 each fail on multiple categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of workflow synthesis (HuggingGPT, VISPROG, GenAgent) and unified multimodal generation (Unified-IO, SEED-X, Show-o), but departs from both paradigms. Rather than learning implicit cross-modal mappings through large-scale training or orchestrating multiple agents with complex coordination, it introduces A-LANGUAGE—a formal symbolic triple (functions, parameters, topology)—and a training-free two-stage LM inference engine that directly maps natural language to executable workflows. The approach is conceptually closest to Visual Programming (Gupta et al., 2023) in its neuro-symbolic decomposition, but extends the idea from single-modality visual reasoning to arbitrary multimodal generative pipelines executed via ComfyUI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, interpretable, and editable alternative to both monolithic unified models and complex agentic systems, with the notable advantage that users can modify individual functions or parameters post-hoc without retraining. However, the evaluation is somewhat constrained: execution is tied to the ComfyUI ecosystem, the 120-task suite and 5-annotator user study limit statistical confidence, and the 41% ComfyBench resolve rate—while best among compared methods—still leaves substantial room for improvement on complex and creative tasks. Open questions include whether the symbolic approach scales to workflows with dozens of interdependent nodes, whether it generalizes beyond ComfyUI's function library, and whether the reliance on a frontier LM (GPT-4o) as the inference engine is a practical bottleneck for deployment.
