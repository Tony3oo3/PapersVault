## Problem

Existing social media popularity prediction datasets predict a single popularity score without consistent temporal alignment, meaning predictions are made at irregular intervals that reduce accuracy and hinder practical deployment. Additionally, prior datasets suffer from limited language diversity (typically single-language) and insufficient multi-modal coverage, failing to reflect the multilingual, multi-modal reality of platforms like YouTube.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252593/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 224×224 cover image, five text fields (category, title, tags, description, user ID) in any of 90+ languages, seven numerical attributes (log-scaled followers, post count, duration, title length, tag count, description length, and day-1 early popularity), and categorical label/language embeddings. It outputs a 30-element sequence of Khosla-transformed popularity scores (log₂(v/d+1)), one per day after release, with temporal alignment enforced by observing each sample at fixed 24-hour intervals via the YouTube API.

## Method

The baseline framework has two stages: (1) multi-modal feature extraction using ResNet-101 (2048-d visual), BERT-Multilingual (768-d per text field, fused via 5×5 convolution), Z-score-normalized numerical features, and categorical features from independent embedding+MLP branches combined by element-wise multiplication, all aligned to a common dimension via MLPs and concatenated; (2) a 30-step LSTM for temporal regression, where the initial hidden/cell states are produced by an MLP on the fused feature vector and each time-step input is generated by a dedicated temporal-encoding MLP, with per-step outputs from an MLP over concatenated hidden+cell states passed through ReLU to enforce non-negativity. Training uses a Composite Gradient Loss (CGL) that combines SmoothL1Loss on values, first- and second-order derivative SmoothL1Loss terms, an L1 penalty on one-hot peak-day encodings, and a Laplacian remainder regularizer, with derivative and peak weights annealed via cosine scheduling. A key empirical finding is that including the day-1 early popularity as a numerical input yields the largest single performance gain (reducing day-30 MAE from ~1.56 to ~0.73), and that the LSTM's backpropagation over the 30-day sequence further refines later-day predictions when day-1 is part of the target sequence.

## Results

On SMTPD, the proposed baseline with Early Popularity (EP) achieves the best temporal prediction performance, reaching 0.713/0.964 (MAE/SRC) on day 7, 0.735/0.959 on day 14, and 0.732/0.959 on day 30. Without EP, the method's performance drops to 1.563/0.848 on day 30, comparable to the strongest GBDT baseline (Lai et al. at 1.495/0.864). The EP input provides a dramatic improvement of approximately 0.76–0.86 in MAE and 0.09–0.12 in SRC over the best existing method on day 30, and uniquely causes MAE to decrease after day 14 rather than increase with prediction horizon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Ding et al. [13] (DNN multi-modal fusion), Lai et al. [26] (CatBoost/GBDT-based HyFEA), and Xu et al. [52] (attention-based multi-modal). Without EP, Lai et al. [26] is the strongest baseline, achieving 1.495/0.864 (MAE/SRC) on day 30, slightly outperforming the proposed method without EP (1.563/0.848). With EP, the proposed method dominates all baselines: it beats Lai et al. + EP by 0.028 MAE and 0.002 SRC on day 30, and beats Ding et al. + EP by 0.017 MAE and 0.028 SRC. The gap widens substantially when comparing the proposed method with EP against baselines without EP, where it improves MAE by ~0.76 over Lai et al. and ~0.86 over Ding et al. on day 30. A notable distinction is that the proposed method's MAE decreases after day 14 (0.735 → 0.732), while all baselines' MAE increases with prediction horizon, suggesting the LSTM temporal structure better captures long-range popularity dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing a temporally-aligned, multilingual benchmark (SMTPD) that departs from the single-point, single-language formulation dominant in prior popularity prediction datasets (SMPD, TPIC17, Mazloom, Sanjo). Methodologically, it repurposes standard feature extractors (ResNet-101 for visual, BERT-Multilingual for text) and wraps them in an LSTM-based temporal regression head with a custom Composite Gradient Loss, rather than building on a specific prior architecture. The key conceptual departure is treating popularity as a 30-day sequence to be predicted jointly, with early popularity (day 1) as an explicit input, rather than as a single scalar to be regressed from static features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SMTPD is the first large-scale (282K samples, 90+ languages) benchmark that enforces temporal alignment, making it directly applicable to real-world deployment where predictions must be made at fixed intervals after a post goes live. The finding that early popularity dominates prediction accuracy (MAE drops from ~1.56 to ~0.73) provides a clear practical signal: platforms should invest in fast, accurate day-1 popularity estimation as a prerequisite for longer-horizon forecasting. Open questions remain around multi-frame video understanding, deeper exploitation of linguistic diversity, and whether the EP-centric finding generalizes beyond YouTube's specific content ecosystem.
