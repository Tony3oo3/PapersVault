## Problem

Existing point cloud segmentation methods rely solely on Euclidean distance for neighbor selection, which misidentifies spatially proximate but semantically irrelevant points as neighbors while overlooking spatially distant yet semantically related ones. Additionally, low-level spatial features provide poor representations for global learning, and the quadratic-complexity attention mechanisms commonly used for global interaction introduce significant computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251847/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw point cloud P = {p_i = (s_i, e_i)} where s_i ∈ R³ are 3D coordinates and e_i are per-point feature embeddings (e.g., intensities, elongation), and produces per-point semantic class labels (13 classes on S3DIS, 20 on ScanNetV2), per-point part labels (ShapeNetPart), or a single object class label (ScanObjectNN, ModelNet40). A key preprocessing step projects all points through 2R virtual cameras (default 2×64) to construct a binary camera visibility vector of dimension 2R per point, which drives both neighbor search and global feature enrichment.

## Method

CamPoint introduces 2R virtual cameras (R placed at FPS-farthest points looking at the centroid, R at the centroid looking outward) and constructs a binary camera visibility feature per point based on depth-occluded projection. For local aggregation, the camera perspective slice distance — the L1 norm (implemented as bitwise AND) between two points' visibility vectors, normalized by a harmonic factor γ and combined with Euclidean distance — replaces pure spatial KNN to select semantically coherent neighbors. For global perception, a learnable 16-dimensional embedding is assigned to each camera; these embeddings are visibility-weighted mean-pooled and added to local features to form high-level representations, which are then processed by a Mamba selective-scan SSM with random point shuffling (instead of sequential or octree ordering) to achieve linear-complexity global interaction. The full pipeline stacks T blocks of local fusion (DeLA-style) and global SSM, with Invert Residual MLP as the encoding backbone.

## Results

On S3DIS, CamPoint achieves 83.3% Area5 mIoU and 94.7% 6-fold mIoU, outperforming the strongest Transformer-based baseline PTv3 (74.7%/80.8%) by 8.6%/13.9% and the SSM-based PCM (79.6%) by 3.7%. On ScanNetV2, CamPoint reaches 77.7% Val mIoU and 77.5% Test mIoU, surpassing PTv3 (77.5%/77.2%) and PCM (75.5%) by 0.2% and 2.2% respectively. On ShapeNetPart, CamPoint attains 87.2% Instance mIoU, the best among all compared methods. On ScanObjectNN, CamPoint achieves 92.1% OA, exceeding Mamba3D (91.8%) by 0.3%, while on ModelNet40 it matches the best OA of 93.6% with the highest mAcc of 91.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CamPoint decisively outperforms all classical CNN-based baselines (MinkUNet, PointNeXt, DeLA, PointHR, OA-CNNs, KPConvX-L) on S3DIS by 9–18 mIoU points, with the largest gap over MinkUNet (65.4%) at +17.9%. Against Transformer-based methods, CamPoint leads PTv3 by 8.6% on S3DIS Area5 mIoU and by 0.2% on ScanNetV2 Val mIoU, while surpassing OneFormer (76.6%) and Swin3D (76.4%) on ScanNetV2. Among SSM-based competitors, CamPoint beats PCM by 3.7% on S3DIS and 2.2% on ScanNetV2, and outperforms POINT MAMBA by 3.1% on ScanNetV2. On ShapeNetPart, CamPoint (87.2% Ins. mIoU) edges out DeLA (87.0%) and PCM (86.9%) but trails DeLA on Class mIoU (85.3 vs. 85.8). On ScanObjectNN, CamPoint (92.1% OA) surpasses Mamba3D (91.8%) and PointMamba (89.3%), while on ModelNet40 it ties PointMamba at 93.6% OA but achieves the highest mAcc (91.3%). The efficiency comparison in Table 2 shows CamPoint uses the fewest parameters (15.78M on S3DIS, 16.06M on ScanNetV2) and the lowest inference latency (22 ms / 28 ms) among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

CamPoint builds on the local-global paradigm established by PointNet++ and refined by DeLA (for local feature fusion) and PointNeXt (for inverted residual encoding), while adopting Mamba's selective state space model as a linear-complexity global operator in place of quadratic-attention Transformers. Its key conceptual departure is the introduction of virtual cameras as a geometric prior: the camera visibility feature serves simultaneously as a semantic similarity metric (camera perspective slice distance) for KNN-based neighbor selection and as a source of learnable embeddings (camera parameter embedding) that elevate low-level spatial features into high-level representations for global interaction. This positions the work at the intersection of classical computer graphics (perspective projection, visibility) and modern point cloud deep learning, departing from prior methods that rely solely on Euclidean distance for neighbor search and raw spatial features for global modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency-accuracy tradeoff: CamPoint achieves SOTA mIoU with roughly 3× fewer parameters and 4× faster inference than PTv3, making it attractive for deployment in resource-constrained settings like autonomous driving or robotics. The camera visibility feature is a simple, interpretable, and dataset-agnostic mechanism that could be adopted as a plug-in component in other point cloud architectures (as demonstrated by the generality experiments with PointNeXt and DeLA). However, the reliance on predefined camera placements limits adaptability to highly complex or dynamic scenes, and the method's generalization to outdoor detection or few-shot settings remains unvalidated. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned camera placement could further improve performance and whether the camera prior transfers to tasks with fundamentally different geometric structures.
