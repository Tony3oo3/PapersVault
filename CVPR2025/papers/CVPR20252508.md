## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves high-quality rendering but produces unstructured point clouds that resist accurate surface reconstruction. Existing remedies—primitive-based regularization (e.g., PGSR, 2DGS) and dual-model frameworks (e.g., GSDF)—either create an inherent conflict that degrades rendering quality or incur prohibitive computational and storage costs. The paper identifies that individual Gaussian primitives contribute asymmetrically to rendering versus reconstruction, and that this contribution conflict, driven primarily by the covariance attribute, is the fundamental bottleneck for a unified model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of calibrated multiview RGB images and a COLMAP-derived sparse point cloud as initialization. It produces a single unified anchor-based Gaussian model that simultaneously yields novel-view RGB renderings (via tile-based alpha-blending rasterization), per-pixel depth and normal maps, and a 3D surface mesh extracted from a TSDF field fused from rendered depth maps and processed with marching cubes. The anchor-based representation (inheriting Scaffold-GS) stores only anchor features and a compact MLP rather than per-Gaussian parameters, keeping storage at 0.16 GB on the TnT Truck scene versus 1.7 GB for GSDF.

## Method

CarGS builds on an anchor-based 3DGS backbone (Scaffold-GS) where each anchor decodes local Gaussian attributes via a view-dependent MLP. The core mechanism, Lite-Geo, decouples the covariance used for rendering from that used for reconstruction by predicting a 7-dimensional residual offset (scale + quaternion rotation) through a lightweight 2-layer MLP, added to the rendering covariance; this residual structure inherits implicit depth information from the pre-trained rendering branch, preventing the geometry overfitting observed when a standalone Geo-MLP is trained solely on reconstruction loss. A geometry-guided densification strategy further controls Gaussian spawning using a composite criterion that combines accumulated gradient magnitude with a Gaussian-weighted SDF term and a normal-difference term, enabling targeted growth in high-frequency regions. Optimization combines RGB photometric loss (L1 + D-SSIM) with plane-constraint and cross-view consistency regularization terms, all within a single unified model that requires no separate SDF network.

## Results

On the Tanks and Temples dataset, CarGS achieves a mean F1 of 0.50 and mean PSNR of 26.01, matching PGSR and NeuralAngelo on F1 while surpassing all other 3DGS-based methods (2DGS: F1 0.30, GOF: F1 0.46) and significantly outperforming PGSR on PSNR (26.01 vs. 24.45). On Mip-NeRF360, CarGS attains 27.68 PSNR, 0.834 SSIM, and 0.209 LPIPS, comparable to Scaffold-GS (28.30 PSNR) and PGSR (27.43 PSNR). In terms of efficiency on the Truck scene, CarGS uses only 0.16 GB storage (vs. 0.42 GB for PGSR and 1.7 GB for GSDF) while achieving the best F1 (0.65) and PSNR (26.41) among all compared methods, at 90 FPS real-time speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dual-model baseline GSDF [30], CarGS achieves substantially better F1 (0.65 vs. 0.46 on Truck) and PSNR (26.41 vs. 26.05) while using only 9% of the storage (0.16 GB vs. 1.7 GB), 40% of the training time (1.2 h vs. 3 h), and running ~90× faster (90 FPS vs. <1 FPS). Against the primitive-based baseline PGSR [3], CarGS wins on F1 (0.65 vs. 0.60 on Truck; 0.50 vs. 0.50 mean on TnT with scene-level wins on Caterpillar, Meetingroom, and Truck) and PSNR (26.41 vs. 26.12 on Truck; 26.01 vs. 24.45 mean on TnT), while using only 38% of the storage (0.16 GB vs. 0.42 GB). Against NeuralAngelo [13], CarGS is competitive on F1 (0.50 vs. 0.50 mean) but far more efficient (1.2 h vs. >24 h training, 0.16 GB vs. 4.2 GB storage, 90 FPS vs. <1 FPS). Against 2DGS [9] and GOF [32], CarGS dominates on both F1 and PSNR across all TnT scenes. On Mip-NeRF360 rendering, CarGS (27.68 PSNR) is slightly below Scaffold-GS (28.30 PSNR) and 3DGS (27.62 PSNR) but matches or exceeds PGSR (27.43) and 2DGS (27.03), confirming that the adaptive contribution mechanism does not sacrifice rendering fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CarGS builds on the anchor-based 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm (Scaffold-GS) and adopts geometry regularization terms (plane and cross-view constraints) from PGSR, but departs from both primitive-based and dual-model strategies for unifying rendering and reconstruction. Rather than sharing Gaussian primitives across tasks (as in SuGaR, 2DGS, PGSR) or maintaining separate 3DGS and SDF models (as in GSDF), it introduces a lightweight residual MLP (Lite-Geo) that predicts a geometry-specific covariance offset on top of the rendering covariance, thereby decoupling task-specific contributions within a single model. The geometry-guided densification strategy further extends prior SDF-only densification by incorporating normal-vector cues to better capture high-frequency geometric details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CarGS demonstrates that a unified, single-model architecture can match or exceed dual-model approaches (GSDF) on both rendering and reconstruction while consuming only 9% of the storage and running 100× faster, making real-time unified scene representation practically viable for AR/VR and robotics applications. The key open question is whether the anchor-based representation and its associated MLP overhead scale gracefully to very large or dynamic scenes, and whether the residual coupling between rendering and geometry covariances remains effective when the two tasks have highly divergent depth requirements (e.g., transparent or reflective surfaces). The observation that a large fraction of Gaussians are 'floaters' essential for rendering but irrelevant for reconstruction suggests future work could explore explicit pruning or task-specific subsampling strategies.
