## Problem

Existing universal segmentation methods either lack the reasoning capability to handle complex vision-language instructions (e.g., Mask2Former, UNINEXT) or are restricted to the image domain without video temporal understanding (e.g., LISA, PSALM). No prior VLLM-based segmentation model simultaneously supports pixel-level image and video perception across generic, referring, and reasoning tasks within a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252859/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images or video clips alongside flexible prompts: text prompts (category lists, referring/reasoning sentences) or visual prompts (boxes, masks) that are encoded by sampling CLIP features at region coordinates and applying adaptive average pooling rather than a separate region encoder. Outputs include per-instance segmentation masks (H×W), K-dimensional class scores, and D-dimensional instance embeddings for video correspondence. Video processing is frame-by-frame for efficiency, with temporal coherence enforced through the temporal adapter rather than explicit video encoders.

## Method

HyperSeg combines a Swin-B pyramid vision encoder, a lightweight VLLM (Mipha with Phi-1.5/Phi-2, fine-tuned via LoRA), and a Mask2Former segmentation predictor. Three key modules differentiate it: (1) Hybrid Entity Recognition instructs the VLLM to generate object names followed by mask tokens, merging the generative semantic recognition of LISA with the decode-stage classification of PSALM; (2) Fine-grained Visual Perceiver (FVP) uses three layers of conditional multi-head cross-attention to fuse multi-scale pyramid features into fixed-length tokens, with a tanh-gated MLP providing conditional weights initialized to zero for stable training; (3) a Temporal Adapter aggregates global prompt embeddings via average pooling over T frames and injects local space-time information through sequential renewal of fine-grained tokens across adjacent frames. The model is trained jointly across all tasks with a unified loss combining autoregressive text CE, mask BCE+DICE, classification CE, and instance contrastive loss.

## Results

On the RefCOCO series, HyperSeg achieves 84.8 cIoU (val), 79.0 (RefCOCO+ val), and 79.4 (RefCOCOg val), surpassing the previous best VLLM-based method PSALM by +1.2, +6.1, and +5.6 respectively. On the challenging reasoning benchmarks, HyperSeg-3B reaches 53.0 J&F on ReVOS-Reasoning (+12.1 over VISA-13B) and 59.2 gIoU on ReasonSeg (+6.5 over VISA-7B). On COCO-Panoptic, HyperSeg attains 61.2 PQ and 77.2 mIoU, outperforming PSALM by +5.3 PQ and +10.6 mIoU. On video tasks, HyperSeg achieves 77.6 J&F on DAVIS17 and 71.2 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17, leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperSeg outperforms all VLLM-based baselines on the RefCOCO series, beating the strongest competitor PSALM by +1.2 cIoU on RefCOCO val, +6.1 on RefCOCO+ val, and +5.6 on RefCOCOg val. On the zero-shot gRefCOCO benchmark, HyperSeg (47.5 val) surpasses PSALM (42.0) and LaSagnA (38.1) but trails GSVA (61.7) and SAM4MLLM (66.3), which were explicitly trained on gRefCOCO data. On reasoning segmentation, HyperSeg-3B achieves 53.0 J&F on ReVOS-Reasoning, exceeding VISA-13B (40.9) by +12.1 and TrackGPT-13B (40.5) by +12.5, while on ReasonSeg it reaches 59.2 gIoU (+6.5 over VISA-7B's 52.7) though slightly below VISA-7B on cIoU (56.7 vs. 57.8). On COCO-Panoptic, HyperSeg leads PSALM by +5.3 PQ and +10.6 mIoU, and on open-vocabulary Cityscapes it gains +16.1 mIoU over PSALM. On video segmentation, HyperSeg surpasses UNINEXT on Ref-YouTube-VOS (+2.3 J&F) and Ref-DAVIS17 (+4.5 J&F), and beats VISA-13B on Ref-DAVIS17 (+0.8 J&F), but trails UNINEXT on YouTube-VIS 2019 (53.8 vs. 64.3 mAP), indicating a gap in dense video instance segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperSeg builds directly on Mask2Former's segmentation predictor and pyramid vision encoder, and on Mipha as the base VLLM, but departs from prior VLLM-based segmentation methods (LISA, PixelLM, PSALM, OMG-LLaVA) in three key ways: a hybrid entity recognition strategy that combines generative and decoding pathways, a Fine-grained Visual Perceiver that injects multi-scale pyramid features into the LLM to overcome CLIP's coarse single-scale representations, and a Temporal Adapter for video. It also extends the universal segmentation line (Mask2Former, UNINEXT, OMG-Seg) by integrating VLLM-based reasoning, positioning itself as the first framework to unify image and video segmentation with complex reasoning perception in a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a compact 3B-parameter model can match or exceed 7B/13B competitors across referring, reasoning, panoptic, and video segmentation benchmarks, HyperSeg suggests that architectural design (FVP, HER, Temporal Adapter) can compensate for model scale in pixel-level perception. The gRefCOCO results (47.5 cIoU vs. 84.8 on RefCOCO) reveal that reasoning generalization remains a significant gap, and the frame-by-frame video processing limits scalability to long sequences. Open questions include whether the hybrid recognition strategy and FVP transfer to 3D or dense prediction tasks, and whether the temporal adapter can be replaced by more efficient video-native encoders.
