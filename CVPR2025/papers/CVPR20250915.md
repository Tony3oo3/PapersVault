## Problem

Camera-driven temporal 3D object detectors achieve strong performance but require thousands to millions of human-annotated frames, hindering deployment to new cities and sensor configurations. Prior semi-supervised work for 3D detection either relies on single-frame detectors that cannot exploit video, depends on expensive LiDAR for pseudo-label generation, or does not address the specific challenge of 3D localization error in the temporal, camera-only setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250915/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera RGB video sequences (6 cameras at 900×1600 on nuScenes, 8 cameras at 1080×1920 on nuPlan) sampled in chunks of 40 consecutive frames at 2 Hz, along with camera intrinsics and IMU-derived extrinsics. It outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized by center location, dimensions, rotation, BEV velocity, and one of 10 (nuScenes) or 2 (nuPlan) class labels. A key operational detail is that during pseudo-labeling the same detector is run both forward and backward through each sequence, and the two passes are ensembled by taking backward predictions for objects ahead of the ego-vehicle and forward predictions for objects behind it.

## Method

Built on StreamPETR (a DETR-style temporal 3D detector with a ConvNeXt-S backbone that propagates object queries across timesteps), the framework introduces four components for semi-supervised training. First, the detector is jointly trained on forward- and time-reversed sequences so that a single model can pseudo-label both ahead-of-vehicle and behind-of-vehicle regions with improved 3D localization, exploiting the fact that the temporal DETR head adapts to the sign of the timestamp difference. Second, instead of confidence thresholding, pseudo-labels are filtered via Hungarian Matching between the model's auxiliary 2D detection head and its 3D head, minimizing focal loss, GIoU, and 2D box parameter difference. Third, a velocity-based 3D tracker with EMA-smoothed tracklet velocities fills in missing detections across frames. Fourth, an object-query-conditioned masked reconstruction loss is added: masked image patches are processed through the CNN, then refined by a transformer decoder that cross-attends to the temporal object queries, creating a complementary self-supervised signal that encourages queries to retain multi-timestep scene information without the task conflict of a standalone MAE head.

## Results

On nuPlan with 800 labeled frames, the proposed method achieves 0.222 mAP and 0.280 NDS, improving over the pseudo-labeling baseline (0.187 mAP, 0.249 NDS) by +3.5 mAP and +3.1 NDS. With 4000 labeled frames, it reaches 0.331 mAP and 0.410 NDS versus 0.304/0.405 for the baseline. On nuScenes with 800 labeled frames, the method achieves 0.137 mAP and 0.221 NDS, outperforming UniPAD+Pseudo-labeling (0.116 mAP, 0.198 NDS) by +2.1 mAP and +2.3 NDS. The 3D localization metric mATE improves most dramatically with forward-backward ensembling (e.g., 0.851 vs 0.916 at 800 labeled on nuPlan).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines. On nuPlan, a straightforward confidence-thresholding pseudo-labeling baseline is used; the proposed method wins on all metrics at all labeled-sample sizes, with the largest gap at 800 labeled frames (+3.5 mAP, +3.1 NDS, −0.065 mATE). On nuScenes, the comparison is against UniPAD (a self-supervised pre-training method using RGB+depth rendering loss) further fine-tuned with pseudo-labeling; the proposed method consistently outperforms UniPAD+Pseudo-labeling by +2.1 mAP and +2.3 NDS at 800 labeled frames, and by +2.4 mAP and +1.1 NDS at 2000 labeled frames. Per-class analysis on nuScenes shows the advantage is especially pronounced on hard-to-localize classes (bicycle: 0.074 vs 0.040, motorcycle: 0.041 vs 0.013 at 800 labeled) while maintaining gains on common classes (car: 0.284 vs 0.267, pedestrian: 0.182 vs 0.138). The method does not lose on any reported metric in either comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-labeling paradigm for 3D detection from the single-image and LiDAR settings to temporal, camera-only 3D detection, building directly on StreamPETR as its base detector and adapting DetMatch's 2D-3D Hungarian Matching to operate within a single model rather than across modalities. It departs from prior camera-based SSL work (DPL, MVC-MonoDet) that relied on single-frame detectors or required LiDAR for pseudo-label quality, and from LiDAR-based temporal SSL that benefits from reliable 3D geometry. The object-query-conditioned masked reconstruction is a novel twist on SparK's masked autoencoder, repurposing the detection head's queries as conditioning signals to avoid the task conflict that arises from a standalone reconstruction decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The forward-backward ensembling insight—that a temporal detector inherently localizes objects behind it better than ahead, and that reversing time inverts this asymmetry—provides a low-cost, generalizable technique for improving 3D pseudo-label quality without additional sensors or training cost. The finding that 2D-3D consistency matching works within a single model (not just across RGB/LiDAR) broadens the applicability of DetMatch-style quality filtering. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond StreamPETR to other temporal architectures (BEVFormer, Sparse4D), whether iterative self-training further improves results, and how the pipeline behaves at even lower label budgets or on datasets with different sensor configurations.
