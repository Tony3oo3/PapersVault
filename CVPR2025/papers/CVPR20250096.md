## Problem

Existing semi-supervised LiDAR segmentation methods either exploit spatial point-cloud distributions or consider only short-term temporal cues (e.g., two adjacent frames), overlooking the rich long-term temporal structure in driving sequences. The paper identifies an overlooked natural phenomenon: nearby objects (roads, vehicles) remain temporally stable in category and shape, while distant objects (buildings, pedestrians, vegetation) exhibit high temporal variability. No prior method explicitly models this high-versus-low temporal sensitivity distinction across distance ranges.

## Input / Output

The method consumes t=5 consecutive LiDAR frames (central frame + t−1 neighbors), each containing P points with Cartesian coordinates and intensity, voxelized into a cylindrical grid of resolution (240, 180, 20) over a 50 m range, (−π, π) azimuth, and (−4, 2) m height. The central frame is the segmentation target; during training, labeled central frames route to the student branch while unlabeled ones route to the teacher branch, though all t frames always pass through the temporal-sensitivity module. Output is per-point K-class confidence scores (K=19 for SemanticKITTI, K=16 for nuScenes) produced by a point-wise refinement head that fuses voxel-level features back to individual points.

## Method

HiLoTs introduces a HiLoTs Embedding Unit (HEU) that splits cylindrical voxel features by range: the farthest 70% of voxels enter a High Temporal Sensitivity Flow (HTSF) using stacked self-attention layers, while the remaining near-range voxels enter a Low Temporal Sensitivity Flow (LTSF) that applies self-attention followed by cross-attention with the HTSF attention map (LTSF as Query, HTSF as Key/Value). To make attention tractable over thousands of voxels, a Multi-Voxel Aggregation step first groups M cylindrical voxels into m super-voxels via nearest-neighbor spatial grouping and temporal average pooling, reducing the token count before attention. The HEU output is fused with the MinkowskiUNet bottleneck via two learnable scalars, then decoded to voxel-level features and refined to point-level predictions. Training follows a Mean Teacher paradigm: the student network is supervised with focal loss on labeled frames, while a consistency L2 loss aligns student and teacher predictions on unlabeled frames, with teacher weights updated via EMA (γ=0.99).

## Results

On SemanticKITTI, HiLoTs achieves mIoU of 58.6% (1% labels), 65.7% (10%), 66.5% (20%), and 67.6% (50%), outperforming the best LiDAR-only SSL baseline DDSemi (59.3/65.1/66.3/67.0) at 10–50% labeling ratios. On nuScenes, HiLoTs reaches 58.7% (1%), 72.2% (10%), 75.2% (20%), and 76.9% (50%), surpassing DDSemi (58.1/70.2/74.0/76.5) across all ratios. Compared to LiDAR+Camera multimodal methods, HiLoTs is competitive with LaserMix++ (63.2/67.5/67.7/68.6 on SemanticKITTI) while using only LiDAR input, and outperforms CyMix+IPSL on SemanticKITTI but trails it on nuScenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fully-supervised baselines retrained with the same limited labels (Cylinder3D, RangeViT, SphereFormer, MarS3D), HiLoTs leads by 10–17 mIoU points at 1% labeling on SemanticKITTI (58.6 vs. 41.2–45.4), confirming the benefit of the Mean Teacher SSL framework. Among LiDAR-only SSL methods, HiLoTs outperforms DDSemi by 0.4–0.6 mIoU at 10–50% on SemanticKITTI and by 0.6–2.0 mIoU across all ratios on nuScenes, while beating FRNet by 1.8–2.2 mIoU on SemanticKITTI (though FRNet leads at 1% on nuScenes by 2.5). Against the LiDAR+Camera multimodal baselines, HiLoTs trails LaserMix++ by 4.6–5.6 mIoU on SemanticKITTI and by 3.1–6.6 on nuScenes, but surpasses CyMix+IPSL by 3.7–5.8 mIoU on SemanticKITTI while falling short by 2.8–3.6 on nuScenes. Overall, HiLoTs is the strongest LiDAR-only SSL method on both datasets at 10–50% labeling, achieving performance within ~1 mIoU of the best multimodal approach on nuScenes at 50% labels (76.9 vs. 80.5 for CyMix+IPSL, 76.3 for LaserMix++).

## Relation to Prior Work

HiLoTs builds on the Mean Teacher semi-supervised framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) and the cylindrical voxelization backbone from Cylinder3D (Zhu et al., 2021), but departs from prior semi-supervised LiDAR segmentation that either exploits spatial distribution priors (SSPC-Net, DDSemi, LaserMix) or fuses only two adjacent frames for temporal context. The key conceptual departure is the explicit modeling of a distance-dependent temporal sensitivity gradient—nearby objects (roads, vehicles) are temporally stable while distant objects (buildings, pedestrians) are temporally dynamic—implemented through separate High and Low Temporal Sensitivity Flows with cross-attention interaction. A Transformer-based Multi-Voxel Aggregation module addresses the O(n²) attention cost over the large number of outdoor voxels, a practical concern not addressed by prior feature-level temporal fusion methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a LiDAR-only semi-supervised method achieves mIoU comparable to LiDAR+Camera multimodal approaches (e.g., 67.6 vs. 68.6 on SemanticKITTI at 50% labels), reducing hardware and annotation costs for autonomous driving perception systems. The distance-based temporal sensitivity observation, while intuitive from driving experience, had not been formally exploited in segmentation architectures, and the ablation confirms HTSF (distant regions) contributes more than LTSF (near regions), validating the hypothesis. A stated limitation is that HiLoTs is specific to autonomous driving with consecutive LiDAR frames and does not generalize to single-frame or general 3D point cloud segmentation tasks. Open questions include whether the 70% far-range threshold is optimal across different driving environments and whether the temporal sensitivity gradient holds in urban vs. highway scenarios.
