## Problem

Decomposing 3D face geometry and physically meaningful reflectance (diffuse, specular, roughness) from sparse-view images remains ill-posed: existing methods like NeuFace often collapse specular reflectance into albedo under few views, and geometric accuracy degrades sharply as view count drops. Prior work either ignores physical rendering properties entirely or applies BRDF decomposition designed for non-biological materials, which fails to capture the complex, view-dependent skin reflectance of human faces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3, 5, or 10 calibrated RGB views of a single face (preprocessed with ImFace masks) and produces a signed distance field for geometry, per-point BRDF parameters (albedo, specular, roughness), spherical-harmonic environment light coefficients, and rendered outputs including novel views, relit images, and specular-edited results. Notably, no reflectance ground truth is required at any stage; Stage 1 is trained on multi-view scans from ~30 individuals while Stage 2 refines a single identity from as few as 3 views, with 10 held-out views for evaluation.

## Method

SFDM is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 learns a shared facial template that encodes both geometry (SDF via a template network plus identity-conditioned deformation) and reflectance (a BRDF template network plus a low-weighted offset network), and introduces an albedo gradient predictor that supervises spatial patterns of albedo change to provide stronger priors. Stage 2 refines per-individual attributes by adding a displacement network on top of the template SDF, a BRDF offset with learnable per-channel weights, an attribute synergy mechanism that cross-feeds hidden features between the geometry and reflectance branches to resolve ambiguous dark regions, and a subsurface scattering offset that approximates multi-layer skin light transport using six-Gaussian scattering profiles integrated over a learnable local domain. Training uses pixel-wise color loss, eikonal regularization, light/specular energy penalties, and L1 constraints on offsets and displacement, with the albedo gradient predictor supervised by gradients computed from the fitted neural field.

## Results

On Facescape with 3 views, SFDM achieves PSNR 25.54, SSIM 0.902, LPIPS 4.46, and Chamfer distance 9.27×10⁻³, outperforming all decomposition-based baselines. At 10 views, SFDM reaches PSNR 27.64, SSIM 0.920, LPIPS 3.86, and CD 5.53×10⁻³, setting the best scores across all four metrics. The method's advantage is most pronounced in perceptual quality (LPIPS) and geometric accuracy (CD), where it leads NeuFace by 1.32 in LPIPS and 6.47×10⁻³ in CD at 3 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFDM outperforms all five baselines on SSIM, LPIPS, and Chamfer distance across every view count (3, 5, 10). Against the closest geometry-oriented competitor DeformHead, SFDM loses PSNR at 3 views (25.54 vs. 26.02) but wins decisively on LPIPS (4.46 vs. 5.76) and CD (9.27 vs. 9.60), and surpasses DeformHead on PSNR at 5 and 10 views. Against the face-specific decomposition method NeuFace, SFDM leads on all four metrics at all view counts, with the largest gap in CD at 3 views (9.27 vs. 15.74×10⁻³), indicating substantially more accurate geometry under extreme sparsity. The general-object decomposition methods PhySG and TensoIR are far behind: PhySG's CD is 70.87×10⁻³ at 3 views (7.6× worse than SFDM), and TensoIR's SSIM drops to 0.645, confirming that generic BRDF models are ill-suited for facial skin. Notably, SFDM is the only method that simultaneously supports relighting while maintaining top-tier rendering and geometry quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFDM extends the template-plus-deformation paradigm from DeformHead [47] by incorporating reflectance attributes (BRDF: albedo, specular, roughness) into the learned facial template, not just geometry. It adopts NeuFace's [58] physically-based face decomposition pipeline (SH environment light, pre-integrated BRDF via Neural-PIL [9], calibration module) but addresses its ill-posedness under sparse views through a two-stage design: a multi-identity template stage followed by a single-person refinement stage. The key conceptual departure from NeuFace is the introduction of an albedo gradient predictor as a shape-agnostic facial prior, an attribute synergy mechanism that lets geometry and reflectance networks exchange features, and a subsurface scattering offset for realistic skin rendering—none of which appear in prior face decomposition work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By robustly disentangling geometry, diffuse, and specular reflectance from as few as three images, SFDM enables downstream applications (relighting, specular editing) that were previously unreliable under sparse-view settings where methods like NeuFace degenerate to a pure Lambertian model. The template-based prior is a practical solution to the fundamental ill-posedness of reflectance decomposition, and the albedo gradient predictor offers a lightweight way to inject cross-identity facial structure without requiring per-identity ground truth. However, the method is explicitly face-specific and the authors note it is difficult to extend to general objects; future work could incorporate hash encodings for speed and leverage fine-grained 3D priors or reflectance ground truth to push decomposition accuracy further.
