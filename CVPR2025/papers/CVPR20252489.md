## Problem

Existing image analogy generation methods (A:A'::B:B') are either restricted to specific models (e.g., VISII requires InstructPix2Pix, Analogist requires inpainting models) or leave residual artifacts from A' in the generated B'. No prior approach can precisely isolate only the difference between A and A' and apply it to an arbitrary query B within general Stable Diffusion frameworks without introducing unwanted information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252489/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only an image triplet {A, A', B} as input with no text instructions, and produces B' satisfying the analogy relation. The difference between A and A' is encoded as a small set of optimized Difference Tokens (5 tokens by default) that are concatenated with B's text prompt at inference time. Because the method is inference-based and only optimizes token embeddings, it is plug-and-play across any Stable Diffusion variant (tested on SD 2.1 and SDXL) without modifying the pretrained diffusion weights.

## Method

Difference Inversion extracts the transformation between A and A' as an Interpolated Delta computed via Slerp between a CLIP Image Delta and a CLIP Text Delta (from BLIP2 captions), then optimizes a small set of hard-prompt Difference Tokens to match this delta. A Token Consistency Loss enforces bidirectional reconstruction: with the DIFF tokens present, A's prompt should reconstruct A', and with the negated DIFF tokens, A' prompt should reconstruct A, ensuring the tokens encode only the difference and not image-specific content. Token embeddings are zero-initialized (inspired by ControlNet) to prevent random initialization from embedding spurious information. At generation time, the optimized DIFF tokens are simply appended to B's prompt, making the approach model-agnostic and free of the instruction-prompt dependency that limits prior methods like VISII.

## Results

On the InstructPix2Pix evaluation set (300 triplets), Difference Inversion achieves a DINO-v2 directional score of 0.5732, surpassing all baselines including text-based SDEdit (0.5374) and InstructPix2Pix (0.5723). Among image-only methods, it leads on both CLIP (0.1024 vs. VISII's 0.1007) and DINO-v2 (0.5732 vs. VISII's 0.5414). In human evaluation (60 participants, 50 images), Difference Inversion is preferred 72% of the time versus 15% (Analogist), 9% (VISII), and 4% (DIA). VLM evaluations (GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL-72B, Llama-3.2-90B) confirm a 60–92% win rate over each baseline in pairwise comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two image+text baselines, Difference Inversion (image-only) trails on CLIP directional score (0.1024 vs. SDEdit's 0.1105 and InstructPix2Pix's 0.2000) but surpasses both on DINO-v2 (0.5732 vs. 0.5374 and 0.5723), demonstrating that the extracted difference tokens capture visual directionality as well as or better than explicit text instructions. Among the three image-only baselines, Difference Inversion clearly dominates: it outperforms DIA by a large margin on both CLIP (0.1024 vs. 0.0294) and DINO-v2 (0.5732 vs. 0.5114), and Analogist similarly (0.1024 vs. 0.0398; 0.5732 vs. 0.5098). The closest competitor is VISII, which achieves a near-identical CLIP score (0.1007) but is outperformed on DINO-v2 (0.5414 vs. 0.5732); the paper notes VISII's CLIP advantage is partly inflated because the evaluation set overlaps with InstructPix2Pix's training data. In human evaluation, Difference Inversion is preferred 72% of the time, far exceeding VISII (9%), Analogist (15%), and DIA (4%). VLM pairwise evaluations (GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL-72B, Llama-3.2-90B) consistently favor Difference Inversion with win rates of 60–92% over each baseline, confirming the quantitative advantage from a perceptual and semantic standpoint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VISII's idea of inverting a visual transformation into learnable tokens, but departs in two key ways: it formulates the output as a 'Full Prompt' (compatible with any Stable Diffusion model) rather than an 'Instruction Prompt' (restricted to InstructPix2Pix), and it enforces bidirectional consistency (A→A' and A'→A) via a Token Consistency Loss to prevent residual artifacts. The Delta Interpolation step (Slerp between CLIP Image Delta and Text Delta) extends the simple embedding-difference approach used in DIA and Analogist, while the zero-initialization of Difference Tokens borrows from ControlNet's design to avoid embedding spurious information. The hard-prompt optimization strategy follows PEZ and Textual Inversion, positioning the method within the broader diffusion-inversion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic formulation is practically significant: practitioners can apply Difference Inversion to SDXL, SD 2.1, or any latent diffusion model without retraining, unlike VISII or grid-based inpainting methods. However, the authors acknowledge that CLIP's embedding space limits the method's ability to capture global (e.g., weather, style) or fine-grained (e.g., subtle expressions) differences, and the DINO-v2 directional score, while competitive, is evaluated on only 300 pairs from a single dataset. Open questions include whether more refined embedding spaces (DINO-v2, foundation-model features) or learned mapping networks can close the gap for complex transformations, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the InstructPix2Pix-style editing pairs used in evaluation.
