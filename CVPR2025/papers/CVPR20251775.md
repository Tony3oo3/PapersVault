## Problem

Self-supervised contrast maximization enables learning monocular depth from event cameras without ground truth, but the computational cost of warping, splatting, and backpropagating through the event stream makes on-device online learning infeasible on resource-constrained platforms like small drones. This paper closes that gap by making the entire contrast maximization pipeline efficient enough to run in real time on an embedded GPU, enabling a robot to adapt its depth model directly in its operational environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251775/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes event streams accumulated into 20 ms windows at 160×120 resolution (a quarter-pixel subsampling of the DVXplorer Micro's 640×480 sensor to keep the rate below 1 Mev/s). It outputs a monocular depth map (softplus-activated, defined up to scale) and a relative pose (rotation in exponential coordinates converted via Rodrigues' formula, plus translation). On the drone, the full events-to-depth pipeline runs at ~30 Hz with a backward pass every 10 forward passes, consuming ~9 W on a Jetson Orin NX.

## Method

The architecture is a 430k-parameter recurrent network with a strided convolutional encoder, a ConvGRU bottleneck, and a two-tailed decoder for depth and ego-motion, trained with a contrast maximization loss plus a geometry consistency loss for scale stability. The core technical contribution is a CUDA implementation of event warping and bilinear splatting that assigns one thread per event, warps all events independently through time without zero-padding, discards events that leave the image, and computes analytical gradients in the backward kernel—yielding ~100× runtime speedup and 2–5× memory reduction over the PyTorch batched approach. This efficiency enables online fine-tuning on board a 5-inch quadrotor: the network is pre-trained on UZH-FPV, then continues learning during flight with truncated BPTT (gradient update every 10 forward passes), converging within ~2 minutes and improving obstacle-avoidance performance by ~30% over pre-training alone.

## Results

On MVSEC, the proposed method achieves MAE of 2.25 m at 10 m depth cutoff (outdoor_day1), outperforming both self-supervised event-based baselines. The dense variant (unmasked depth) further improves to 1.96 m at 10 m and 3.29 m at 30 m. On the DSEC disparity benchmark, the self-supervised method achieves 82.64% 1PE and 4.583 m MAE (best scale), which is substantially below supervised stereo baselines but demonstrates meaningful structure recovery without ground truth. Online learning on the drone improves depth MAE and RSAT within ~2 minutes of flight, and increases distance between pilot interventions by ~30% over pre-training alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two self-supervised event-based depth baselines, the proposed method wins across all MVSEC depth cutoffs: at 10 m (outdoor_day1), it achieves 2.25 m MAE vs. 2.72 m for Zhu et al. [45] (CVPR 2019) and 3.90 m for Zhu et al. [46] (IROS 2023), a 17% and 42% improvement respectively. The dense variant further narrows the gap to the cross-modal method [46] that uses intensity frames (1.96 m vs. 1.40 m). On the DSEC disparity benchmark, the self-supervised method (MAE 4.583 m, best scale) is substantially outperformed by the supervised stereo baselines—Cho et al. [8] (ECCV 2024) achieves 0.501 m MAE and the DSEC baseline [15] achieves 0.576 m—reflecting the inherent gap between self-supervised and supervised learning. However, the paper notes that its method can operate at 100 Hz (vs. 10 Hz ground-truth rate) and produces smoother disparity maps free from boundary artifacts, which is critical for real-time on-device deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrast maximization self-supervised learning pipeline (Gallego et al. 2018; Paredes-Vallés et al. 2023) for event-based depth and ego-motion estimation by making it computationally efficient enough to run on embedded hardware. It incorporates the depth-pose warping formulation from Zhou et al. 2017 and the scale-consistency loss from Bian et al. 2019, but the key departure is a CUDA-parallelized implementation of event warping and splatting that eliminates PyTorch batching overhead, achieving ~100× runtime reduction. This efficiency gain transforms the pipeline from an offline training tool into a viable on-device learning system, a setting not previously demonstrated for event-based depth estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a robot can meaningfully improve its own depth perception within two minutes of flight, reducing the reality gap between training and deployment environments—a practical advantage particularly important for depth estimation, which is notoriously sensitive to domain shift. The CUDA implementation technique (per-event parallel warping with analytical gradients) is broadly transferable to other event-based pipelines involving warping/splatting. Open questions include scaling to higher camera resolutions, handling dynamic objects under the static-scene assumption, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex environments with higher obstacle density or more sophisticated control strategies.
