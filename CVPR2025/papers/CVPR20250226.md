## Problem

Existing HOI synthesis methods rely on simplified object representations (centroid or nearest point) and single-view human-object relations, overlooking the full geometric complexity of objects and the mutual spatiotemporal dynamics between humans and objects. This leads to artifacts such as inconsistent contact, interpenetration, and floating, particularly in multi-stage interactions where contact points change over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250226/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an object mesh (reduced to 24 key points via 8 boundary points nearest to the AABB plus 16 Poisson disk sampled points), 24 SMPL-X human skeleton joints, and a text prompt (encoded by CLIP ViTB/32 into a 512-d vector). It produces a synchronized N-frame motion sequence of 288 dimensions per frame: 204 dims for the human (joint positions + 6D continuous rotations) and 84 dims for the object (translation, rotation, and 24 key point positions). The intermediate representation is a 24×24×N Interactive Distance Field matrix of squared Euclidean distances between every human joint and every object key point across all frames.

## Method

ROG couples a Motion Diffusion Model (MDM-based, 8 transformer layers) with a dedicated Relation Model (a video diffusion transformer with spatial and temporal self-attention) through an Interactive Distance Field (IDF). The IDF is a 24×24×N matrix of squared distances between human joints and object key points; during training, it serves as an auxiliary MSE loss (λ=5.0) alongside the standard reconstruction loss. The Relation Model downsamples the spatial dimensions from 24×24 to 4×4 via a linear projection, then applies transformer blocks with joint spatial-temporal attention to denoise the IDF, learning a dynamic prior over interaction geometry. At inference, during the last 10 denoising steps, the Relation Model refines the IDF computed from the motion model's prediction, and an L-BFGS optimizer back-propagates the guidance loss through forward kinematics to correct the motion, ensuring relation-aware and semantically aligned outputs.

## Results

On the FullBodyManipulation dataset, ROG achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 of 0.706 (vs. 0.630 for CHOIS, the strongest baseline) and the lowest FID of 5.119 among all generated methods. ROG also attains the highest Contact Percentage (C% = 0.466) and the lowest Motion Deviation (MDev = 5.815), indicating more physically plausible and synchronized human-object interactions. Notably, ROG's R-Precision Top-1 even surpasses the real-motion reference (0.651), suggesting strong semantic alignment with text prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROG outperforms all four baselines on R-Precision (Top-1: 0.706 vs. 0.630 for CHOIS, 0.534 for HOI-Diff, 0.495 for MDM, 0.490 for InterGen) and FID (5.119 vs. 5.227 for CHOIS, 9.775 for MDM, 11.875 for HOI-Diff, 19.038 for InterGen). Against the closest competitor CHOIS, ROG improves Contact Percentage from 0.444 to 0.466 while keeping Collision Percentage comparable (0.200 vs. 0.208), and reduces Motion Deviation from 13.408 to 5.815, indicating far better hand-object synchronization. ROG also achieves the best Diversity (8.863, closest to the real-motion value of 8.693), whereas InterGen and HOI-Diff produce overly diverse or inconsistent motions. The only metric where ROG is not the best is Foot Sliding (0.349 vs. MDM's 0.331), though both are well below InterGen (0.603) and HOI-Diff (0.430). Overall, ROG dominates on semantic alignment, physical plausibility, and interaction fidelity across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROG builds directly on the Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) as its motion generation backbone and borrows the spatial-temporal self-attention architecture from Video Diffusion Transformers (VDT) for its relation model. It departs from prior HOI guidance methods (CHOIS, HOI-Diff, CG-HOI, THOR) that rely on simplified object representations—centroids, fixed contact points, or nearest-vertex distances—by introducing a full 24×24 Interactive Distance Field (IDF) matrix computed between human joints and Poisson-disk-sampled object keypoints. The key conceptual shift is treating the human-object spatial relationship as a learnable diffusion prior (the relation model) that actively refines the generated motion's IDF during inference via L-BFGS optimization, rather than imposing hard constraints on a subset of joints or vertices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IDF representation offers a more principled and comprehensive way to encode human-object spatial dynamics than centroid or contact-point heuristics, and the separate relation model provides a clean mechanism for injecting geometric priors into diffusion-based motion generation without modifying the base model. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (FullBodyManipulation) with articulated objects excluded, and the paper acknowledges that hand/finger motions are omitted, leaving fine-grained manipulation fidelity unresolved. Open questions include whether the IDF approach generalizes to articulated or deformable objects, scales to longer multi-stage interactions, and whether the 24-keypoint budget is sufficient for objects with highly non-convex geometry.
