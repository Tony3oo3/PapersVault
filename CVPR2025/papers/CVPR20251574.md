## Problem

Personalized vision-language retrieval requires adapting a pretrained CLIP model to recognize new personal concepts from only a few images while preserving its general knowledge for contextual queries. Prior textual inversion approaches represent concepts as single pseudo-tokens that influence the entire encoding process, limiting their ability to compose personal and general knowledge and often requiring large-scale pretraining or slow online optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251574/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At personalization time, the method takes N_c (typically 5) images of a concept plus its class name, and produces a rank-1 LoRA parameter update (2d parameters) applied to the value transform of the final text encoder layer. At retrieval time, a natural language query referencing the personal concept (using a fixed vocabulary token as placeholder) is encoded and matched against a database of images via cosine similarity, yielding ranked retrieval results. Personalization completes in under 1 second on a V100 GPU since only the final layer is backpropagated.

## Method

POLAR learns a rank-1 LoRA update (V'_L = V_L + B_{L,c} A_{L,c}) to the value transform of the final attention layer in CLIP's text encoder, using a fixed vocabulary token as a placeholder for the concept in queries. Training minimizes an MSE loss between normalized text and image embeddings, augmented with a two-part regularization: a squared L2 penalty on B_{L,c} to minimize deviation from the original representation, and a unit-norm constraint on A_{L,c} so it learns directional selectivity while B controls update magnitude. This structure eliminates the need for additional regularization examples or prompts. For multi-concept queries, individual rank-1 updates are merged by addition (equivalent to a rank-2 update via matrix concatenation), which outperforms averaging, max-pooling, and orthogonal adaptation strategies.

## Results

On the ConCon-Chi benchmark (ViT-L/14), POLAR achieves 46.33 mRR and 32.33 mAP on context queries, and 100.00 mRR / 68.71 mAP on concept-only queries, outperforming the prior best (SEARLE) by 2.4 mRR and 1.6 mAP on context retrieval. On DeepFashion2 (ViT-B/32), POLAR reaches 34.82 mRR and 44.88 recall@5 on context queries, surpassing SEARLE (21.90 mRR) by 12.9 points. The method also preserves general knowledge, maintaining a VLM caption recall@10 of 52.62 (vs. 52.69 for original CLIP), while prompt-tuning baselines drop to 15–31.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POLAR outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both benchmarks. Against PALAVRA [7] (textual inversion with a learned pseudo-token), POLAR gains 10.3 mRR on ConCon-Chi context queries (46.33 vs. 35.99) and 6.4 mRR on DeepFashion2 (34.82 vs. 28.4). Against SEARLE [4] (zero-shot composed retrieval via textual inversion), POLAR leads by 2.4 mRR on ConCon-Chi context queries and by 12.9 mRR on DeepFashion2, with the largest gap on concept-only mAP (68.71 vs. 61.94 on ConCon-Chi). Pic2Word [31] is beaten by 7.7 mRR on ConCon-Chi context queries. COLLIE [32] is a much weaker baseline (7.9 mRR on DeepFashion2 vs. POLAR's 34.82). In the ablation comparing internal parameter updates to input-token learning (Table 3), prompt tuning [43] achieves competitive concept-only mAP (64.49 with 2 tokens) but suffers catastrophic forgetting on general queries (VLM caption r@10 drops to 15.35 vs. POLAR's 52.62), while POLAR maintains near-original CLIP general-knowledge performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

POLAR departs from the dominant textual-inversion paradigm in personalized vision-language retrieval (PALAVRA, SEARLE, Pic2Word), which learns pseudo-text tokens as input to the text encoder, by instead learning a minimal internal parameter update. It repurposes the LoRA low-rank factorization from LLM adaptation (Hu et al., 2021) and draws conceptual inspiration from personalized generation methods (Dreambooth, Custom Diffusion, Perfusion) that tune model weights, but adapts the strategy to the discriminative dual-encoder setting where even sparse updates risk catastrophic forgetting. The key architectural departure is restricting the update to a single rank-1 modification of the value transform in the final text-encoder layer, combined with a structure-aware regularization that exploits the rank-1 decomposition to separate directional selection (A) from magnitude control (B).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency is notable: personalization requires only 2d learned parameters per concept and completes in under one second on a V100, making it feasible for on-device or interactive search applications. The introduced VLM caption recall metric provides a principled way to quantify general-knowledge retention, addressing a gap in how prior personalized retrieval work evaluates forgetting. Open questions include whether the value-transform-in-final-layer finding generalizes beyond CLIP's specific architecture, how the simple additive merging strategy scales to dozens or hundreds of personal concepts, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal retrieval settings. The method is also limited to CLIP-based dual encoders and has not been validated on larger-scale or more diverse personalization scenarios.
