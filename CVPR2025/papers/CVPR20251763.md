## Problem

Existing panorama generation methods are evaluated with InceptionNet- or CLIP-based metrics (FID, CLIP-FID) that perceive image quality but are blind to geometric distortion, leading to a 'visual cheating' phenomenon where newer methods improve visual appeal while progressively degrading distortion accuracy. The paper traces this to the fundamental design flaw of learning distortion and content completion simultaneously in a single network, which causes the model to prioritize the easier content-completion objective.

## Input / Output

The method takes a narrow field of view (NFoV) image — a 90-degree central crop of an equirectangular panorama — as input and produces a full 360-degree equirectangular panorama as output. Beyond the raw NFoV image, the pipeline internally constructs a distortion map (per-pixel azimuth and elevation angles with first-order Taylor positional encoding for seamless left-right edge continuity) and a perspective image embedding (the NFoV image projected via equirectangular-to-perspective projection, then VAE-encoded and dimension-matched to replace the standard text embedding). Training requires only 3K SUN360 samples, and the model generalizes zero-shot to the Laval Indoor dataset and raw web images.

## Method

PanoDecouple decouples panorama generation into two parallel branches fused into a frozen pre-trained U-Net: a DistortNet that injects the distortion map as a position-encoding-like condition into every block of a modified ControlNet (rather than only the first block as in standard ControlNet), and a ContentNet that performs mask-based outpainting with the text embedding replaced by a perspective image embedding for content-style consistency with the NFoV input. A distortion correction loss, computed via the text encoder of a purpose-built Distort-CLIP (fine-tuned with contrastive learning on three distortion types: panorama, perspective, and random distortion), explicitly penalizes outputs that deviate from correct panoramic geometry. The full training objective combines the standard latent diffusion reconstruction loss with this distortion correction term (weighted by λ=0.05), and the entire system is trained from the pre-trained ControlNet weights for 20 epochs on a single GPU.

## Results

On SUN360, PanoDecouple achieves the best FID (62.19) and CLIP-FID (6.21) among all methods, while also attaining a Distort-FID of 0.92—second only to OmniDreamer (0.52) but far superior to all subsequent methods (2.68–8.23). On Laval Indoor in a zero-shot setting, PanoDecouple achieves the best FID (74.45), CLIP-FID (8.88), Distort-FID (2.85), and IS (2.95) across all four metrics. Notably, these results are obtained with only 3K training samples, roughly 15× fewer than OmniDreamer (50K) or ImmerseGAN (250K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OmniDreamer (CVPR 2022), PanoDecouple wins on FID (62.19 vs 75.14), CLIP-FID (6.21 vs 11.13), and IS (6.93 vs 4.58) on SUN360, but OmniDreamer retains a slight edge on Distort-FID (0.52 vs 0.92); on Laval Indoor, PanoDecouple dominates across all four metrics (e.g., FID 74.45 vs 133.15, Distort-FID 2.85 vs 2.95). Against PanoDiff (ACMMM 2023), PanoDecouple improves FID by 1.3 points (62.19 vs 63.49), CLIP-FID by 0.83 (6.21 vs 7.04), and Distort-FID by 1.76 (0.92 vs 2.68) on SUN360, and achieves substantially better Distort-FID on Laval Indoor (2.85 vs 13.53). Against 2S-ODIS (2024), PanoDecouple outperforms on every metric on both datasets, with the largest gap on Distort-FID (0.92 vs 8.23 on SUN360; 2.85 vs 10.21 on Laval Indoor). The paper attributes the progressive distortion degradation in PanoDiff and 2S-ODIS to the 'visual cheating' phenomenon: single-network training prioritizes content quality over geometric accuracy, a problem PanoDecouple addresses through its decoupled DistortNet/ContentNet architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ControlNet-based latent diffusion framework for panorama outpainting (PanoDiff, Zhang et al.) and the distortion map representation introduced by Text2Light and PanFusion, but departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it decouples the single-network paradigm into a DistortNet (modified ControlNet with all-block condition registration for position-encoding-like inputs) and a ContentNet (perspective-image-conditioned outpainting), and (2) it introduces Distort-CLIP, a contrastive-learning-tuned CLIP variant that exposes the inadequacy of standard FID/CLIP-FID metrics for assessing geometric distortion. The work positions itself as a corrective to the field's trajectory, arguing that prior methods progressively degraded distortion accuracy while chasing image-quality metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'visual cheating' phenomenon — where standard evaluation metrics incentivize sacrificing geometric accuracy for perceptual quality — is a useful diagnostic for the panorama generation community and could redirect future evaluation practices. The decoupling strategy achieving SOTA with only 3K training samples (15× fewer than competitors) suggests that task decomposition, not data scale, is the key lever for this problem. Open questions include whether the Distort-CLIP metric generalizes beyond equirectangular panoramas to other projection types, whether the distortion correction loss remains necessary at larger training scales, and whether the decoupling principle transfers to other geometrically-constrained generation tasks (e.g., multi-view synthesis, 3D scene completion).
