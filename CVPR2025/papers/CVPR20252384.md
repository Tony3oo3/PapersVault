## Problem

Existing diffusion-based planners for dexterous manipulation suffer from two complementary failures: state-only methods (e.g., Diffuser, Decision Diffuser) produce physically impossible 'ghost states' where objects move without hand contact in sequential interaction tasks, while action-only methods (e.g., Diffusion Policy) cannot perform explicit state conditioning needed for goal adaptation (e.g., opening a door to 30° when trained only on 90°). No prior method simultaneously ensures physical plausibility in contact-rich interactions and supports flexible, zero-shot goal specification.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a concatenated state-action trajectory where state comprises 24 hand joint angles, 3 position offsets, and task-specific object states (e.g., door hinge angle, pen 6-DoF pose), while action encodes only controllable hand joint and position changes. Goal conditioning is provided as a target angle or pose, and task-specific guidance is supplied as LLM-generated Python functions (produced by Claude Sonnet 3.5 via a text-to-reward pipeline). The output is a full joint state-action trajectory over a receding horizon (8 steps for most tasks, 32 for Door), executed at ~5–7 Hz on an RTX 3090 (accelerable to 36 Hz via DPM Solver++ and command interpolation).

## Method

DexHandDiff jointly diffuses over the concatenated state-action space τ = [(a₀,s₀), …, (a_T,s_T)] using classifier-guided diffusion with a product-of-experts energy function that aggregates multiple guidance objectives. The key architectural insight is a dual-phase interaction-aware guidance mechanism: in the pre-contact phase, alignment guidance steers the hand toward the object's contact point while dynamics guidance prevents premature object motion; upon contact (detected via palm-object distance threshold), additional goal-directed and physical-constraint guidance activates to steer the coupled hand-object system toward the target. A separately trained dynamics model provides an additional energy term ε_dyn = |s_{t+1} − T(s_t, a_t)|² that enforces state-action consistency during both training and inference, directly addressing the action-state coupling problem identified in Diffuser. For in-hand manipulation tasks (pen spinning, block rotation), a simplified single-phase guidance with an active-finger-motion constraint replaces the dual-phase structure. An LLM-based two-stage pipeline (environment description → guidance code generation with execution feedback) automates the design of task-specific energy functions, reducing manual engineering from ~20 iterations to ~5.

## Results

Across 10 dexterous manipulation tasks spanning 5 environments (Door, Pen, Hammer, Relocate, Block), DexHandDiff achieves an average success rate of 70.7%, compared to 58.0% for Conditional DP and 34.0% for Diffuser (Inpainting). On the goal-adaptability evaluation in the Door environment (trained only on 90° opening, tested on 30°/50°/70°/110°/close), DexHandDiff averages 59.2% success rate—over twice the next best method (Diffuser with Guided Sampling at 29.5%). Notable individual results include 70.0% on Open 30° (vs. 16.7% for Diffusion Policy), 93.3% on Pen Full Re-orientation, and 90.0% on Hammer Nail Full Drive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DexHandDiff outperforms all three baselines on the overall average (70.7% vs. 58.0% for Conditional DP and 34.0% for Diffuser Inpainting). Against Diffuser [24] (Janner et al., ICML 2022), which uses classifier-guided state diffusion with inverse dynamics for action generation, DexHandDiff wins on 9 out of 10 tasks, with the largest gaps on goal-adaptive variants (e.g., 70.0% vs. 16.7% on Door 30°, 40.0% vs. 3.3% on Pen Half-side). Against Decision Diffuser [1] (Ajay et al., ICLR 2023), a classifier-free state-diffusion method, DexHandDiff achieves 59.2% vs. 25.0% average on the Door goal-adaptability suite, with the most dramatic difference on Close Door (58.3% vs. 0%). Against Diffusion Policy [14] (Chi et al., IJRR 2023), an action-diffusion method, DexHandDiff wins on all goal-adaptive tasks (e.g., 70.0% vs. 16.7% on Door 30°, 46.7% vs. 33.3% on Hammer Half Drive) but is slightly behind on the in-domain Door 90° task (90.0% vs. 100%), which the authors attribute to the overhead of additional guidance mechanisms needed for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based planning lineage from Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) and Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2023) by jointly diffusing over concatenated state-action trajectories rather than states or actions alone, and by introducing a dual-phase (pre-contact alignment / post-contact goal-directed) guidance mechanism specifically for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. It departs from prior state-only diffusion planners by identifying and addressing the 'ghost state' problem—where objects appear to move without physical contact—as a fundamental limitation when indirect control exists. The LLM-based guidance generation component extends the text-to-reward paradigm from Eureka (Ma et al., 2023) and Text2Reward (Xie et al., 2024) from RL reward shaping to classifier-guided diffusion policy design, automating what was previously manual energy function engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of ghost states as a systematic failure mode of state-diffusion planners in interaction tasks is a useful conceptual contribution that clarifies why prior methods fail on contact-rich dexterous manipulation, and the dual-phase guidance with dynamics-model consistency provides a principled fix. However, the evaluation is limited to five simulation tasks with relatively weak baselines (Diffuser with goal inpainting, Decision Diffuser with embedding), the LLM-generated guidance underperforms hand-crafted guidance by a notable margin (e.g., 40% vs. 70% on Door 30°), and control frequency (~5–7 Hz) remains low for real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real hardware with vision-based state estimation, whether the dual-phase structure generalizes to tasks with multiple sequential contacts, and whether the LLM guidance gap can be closed with better prompting or fine-tuning.
