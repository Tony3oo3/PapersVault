## Problem

Existing SDS-based 3D avatar generation methods rely on text prompts or generic image-to-3D priors, leading to oversaturated colors, identity drift, and insufficient facial detail. No prior SDS method has leveraged a dedicated human face foundation model as the guidance signal, leaving a gap in producing highly realistic, identity-faithful 3D head avatars from a single image with controllable expressions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250734/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB facial image (aligned and cropped to 512×512) from which a 512-dimensional ArcFace identity embedding is extracted. The output is a 3D Gaussian Splat head avatar with dense vertex-to-splat correspondence to the FLAME template, supporting 360° novel-view rendering and blendshape-driven expression generation. An optional 500-iteration SDS correction step can be applied post-blendshape to fill in details like teeth and tongue for extreme expressions.

## Method

Arc2Avatar extends Arc2Face (an ID-conditioned Stable Diffusion model) for diverse-view generation via LoRA fine-tuning (rank 16) on 10,000 synthetic PanoHead IDs rendered at 26 angles each, then uses Interval Score Matching (ISM) rather than standard SDS to optimize a 3DGS representation guided by the identity embedding. The 3DGS setup is modified with a masked strategy: facial splats are initialized one-per FLAME vertex and protected from densification, pruning, and opacity resets, while non-facial regions (hair, ears) remain fully optimizable. Template correspondence is enforced through L2 position regularizers and a Laplacian connectivity regularizer, and view conditioning is achieved by blending the ArcFace identity embedding with directional text embeddings at ratio b=0.85. A two-phase optimization first refines facial splats with zoomed-in views before enabling full-scene optimization, and a low LoRA scale (0.45) during distillation balances novel-view generation against identity preservation.

## Results

On the ISD benchmark (45 celebrities, 20 images each), Arc2Avatar achieves the lowest FID score of 143.86, outperforming the closest competitor ID-to-3D (155.64) by ~12 points and Magic123 (159.56) by ~16 points. The identity similarity distribution (cosine similarity via ArcFace) shows Arc2Avatar attaining both the highest mean scores and the lowest variance across frontal and side views. In a user study with 40 participants evaluating 10 identities, Arc2Avatar received 93% of votes for most realistic and faithful avatar.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Arc2Avatar outperforms all seven baselines on FID, with the largest margins over text-to-3D methods: Fantasia3D (279.58), DreamFace (215.87), and TADA (214.02) all exceed 200 FID, reflecting their well-known oversaturation and identity-drift issues. The closest competitor, ID-to-3D [2] (FID 155.64), also uses ID-embeddings with SDS but relies on an optimizable UV texture, yielding somewhat unrealistic appearance; Arc2Avatar's 3DGS-based representation with Arc2Face guidance closes this gap. Magic123 [62] (159.56) and DreamCraft3D [76] (187.63) are image-to-3D methods that transfer 2D appearance to 3D, producing high frontal-view scores but significant artifacts in side and back views, reflected in their higher variance on the identity similarity distribution. HumanNorm [31] (173.81) uses a normal-generation model with SDS and achieves crisp geometry but still lags in overall realism. In the user study, Arc2Avatar received 93% preference over the three strongest baselines (ID-to-3D, Magic123, HumanNorm), confirming the quantitative advantage in perceived quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: SDS/ISM-based 3D optimization (DreamFusion, LucidDreamer), 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-time head rendering, and ID-conditioned face diffusion models (Arc2Face). The key departure from prior SDS-based avatar work (ID-to-3D, TADA, HumanNorm) is replacing generic text-to-image diffusion with a face-specific foundation model conditioned on ArcFace identity embeddings, which the authors argue resolves the oversaturation and identity-ambiguity problems inherent to text prompts. The masked 3DGS strategy—disabling densification and pruning in the facial region while preserving them elsewhere—combined with Laplacian and L2 template regularizers, is a novel mechanism for maintaining dense splat-to-FLAME-vertex correspondence that prior 3DGS avatar methods (which typically require multi-view input) did not need to address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 2D face foundation model, lightly adapted via LoRA for diverse views, can serve as a sufficiently strong 3D prior for single-image avatar generation, this work shifts the design space away from requiring multi-view data or 3D-specific priors. The practical impact is significant: a single photo yields a real-time-renderable, blendshape-animatable 3D head with state-of-the-art identity fidelity. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 45-celebrity evaluation set, how to handle the stochastic expression neutrality issue in Arc2Face, and whether higher-resolution diffusion backbones can resolve the edge-blurriness limitation. The paper also rightly flags the deepfake risk inherent in generating photorealistic avatars of arbitrary individuals from one image.
