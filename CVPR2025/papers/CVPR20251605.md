## Problem

Text-to-motion generation struggles to capture detailed user-imagined body postures from simple textual descriptions, forcing users to write increasingly verbose prompts and iterate multiple times to refine outputs. This paper introduces hand-drawn stickman figures as a complementary control modality that provides intuitive, spatially-grounded guidance for specific poses within a motion sequence, reducing reliance on extensive textual descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251605/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any subset of four inputs: a free-form textual description and up to three hand-drawn stickman figures, each drawn as six one-stroke lines (head, torso, four limbs) and assigned to an approximate position (start, middle, or end) of the motion sequence. The stickman is encoded via a frozen transformer encoder (pre-trained as an autoencoder), while text is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32. The output is a 3D human motion sequence along with per-frame index scores indicating where each stickman was dynamically placed, allowing users to verify or adjust the stickman-to-frame alignment.

## Method

StickMotion is a DDPM-based diffusion model that generates motion by denoising, with a key architectural contribution in the Multi-Condition Module (MCM): instead of using self-attention with partial masks to handle all four condition combinations (text, stick, both, neither), MCM partitions the batch into four segments and uses only two lightweight Feat Decoders whose outputs are summed to produce all combinations, reducing FLOPs from 0.46 to 0.43 TFlops per MCM while improving FID from 0.31 to 0.14. A Dynamic Supervision strategy lets users specify only approximate stickman positions (start/middle/end within defined frame ranges), while the network learns to assign exact frame indices via a softmax index score supervised by an additional L_index loss that pulls the highest-scored frame's pose toward the ground-truth stickman pose. During the reverse diffusion process, a time-dependent condition mixture (Equ. 4) controls the bias toward stickman versus text: the initial coarse stage (t ∈ [T, T/10]) uses a probabilistic stickman preference parameter, while the final refinement stage (t ∈ [T/10, 0]) applies all conditions jointly. A Stickman Generation Algorithm (SGA) automatically produces training stickmen from 3D joint coordinates by simulating hand-drawing characteristics (stroke jitter, misplacement, scaling), eliminating the need for manual annotation.

## Results

On the HumanML3D test set, StickMotion achieves the best R Precision across all top-k levels (Top-1: 0.518, Top-2: 0.702, Top-3: 0.797) and the lowest MM Dist (2.953), while maintaining a competitive FID of 0.107 (vs. 0.103 for ReMoDiffuse). On KIT-ML, StickMotion again leads in R Precision (Top-1: 0.430, Top-2: 0.654, Top-3: 0.775) and MM Dist (2.763), with an FID of 0.141. The method also achieves the highest MultiModality on HumanML3D (2.256), indicating greater diversity in generated motions for a given text prompt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StickMotion outperforms all five baselines on R Precision (Top-1/2/3) and MM Dist on both HumanML3D and KIT-ML, confirming that the stickman condition improves text-motion alignment. Against the strongest baseline ReMoDiffuse [56], StickMotion gains +0.008 on Top-1 R Precision and −0.021 on MM Dist on HumanML3D, while achieving a slightly better FID (0.141 vs. 0.155) on KIT-ML. The FID gap with ReMoDiffuse on HumanML3D (0.107 vs. 0.103) is marginal, suggesting comparable sample quality. StickMotion substantially outperforms older baselines: FID drops from 1.067 (Guo et al.) and 0.630 (MotionDiffuse) to 0.107, and MM Dist improves from 3.340 and 3.113 to 2.953. In the user study, StickMotion with a stickman input scores 8.5/10 versus 7.3/10 for ReMoDiffuse with detailed text, while reducing total user time from 33.8 s to 16.4 s (~51.5% savings).

## Relation to Prior Work

StickMotion extends the diffusion-based text-to-motion generation line (DDPM, classifier-free guidance as in MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse, T2M-GPT) by introducing a novel stickman input modality for local pose control, complementing the global control provided by text. Its Multi-Condition Module (MCM) departs from the self-attention-with-masking approach used in prior multi-condition diffusion works (e.g., ReMoDiffuse) by partitioning the batch into condition-combination segments and using lightweight feature decoders, reducing both FLOPs and the representational misalignment between heterogeneous conditions. The Dynamic Supervision strategy and Stickman Generation Algorithm have no direct precedent in the motion generation literature, as no prior dataset or method incorporates hand-drawn stickman figures as a control signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine usability gap: users struggle to specify precise limb positions through text alone, and the demonstrated 51.5% time reduction in a user study makes the stickman condition practically valuable for animators and content creators. The MCM is a general, reusable architectural component for any multi-condition diffusion task, and the StiSim metric provides a missing evaluation axis for pose-conditioned generation. Open questions include whether the three fixed positions (start/middle/end) suffice for longer or more complex motions, whether the front-view-only 2D stickman generalizes to arbitrary camera angles, and whether the adversarial text-stickman interaction can be resolved more elegantly than the current two-stage weight schedule.
