## Problem

CLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation suffers from two fundamental limitations: (1) text-derived semantic guidance is inherently coarse-grained (e.g., 'animal' for both cats and dogs), creating bias that prevents fine-grained pixel discrimination, and (2) image-text matching lacks pixel-level supervision, so poorly segmented regions can still align well with their text. Existing methods rely on text alone or on additional models for pseudo-labels, leaving a gap in how to obtain accurate, unbiased pixel-level training signals from image-text data.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes image-text pairs from CC3M (300K sampled pairs), where SAM generates instance masks from each image and NLTK extracts nouns from captions. At inference, only an RGB image and a set of text category names are required; the image is encoded by a ViT-B/16 encoder producing a feature map at patch resolution (H'×W'), and the output is a per-pixel class assignment at full image resolution. An optional Background Screening module additionally produces a binary background prediction map, activated only for datasets that include a background class.

## Method

The method constructs Dual Semantic Guidance from image-text pairs in two parallel streams: (1) visual guidance, where SAM segments each image into instances, a training-free Region Awareness Attention module (RAA) biases CLIP's self-attention toward foreground patches via importance-weighted additive terms in the attention score, and K-Means clustering consolidates the resulting region features into K_v independent visual prototypes with pseudo-masks; (2) textual guidance, where NLTK-extracted nouns are encoded by CLIP's text encoder and matched against the visual prototypes via softmax and cosine-similarity thresholds to yield K_t text prototypes with corresponding masks. During fine-tuning of MaskCLIP (ViT-B/16), textual guidance is supervised with BCE loss plus a Masked Feature Contrastive (MFC) loss that pulls the mean feature within each mask toward its positive text prototype while pushing it away from negatives, while visual guidance is supervised with BCE loss plus a Guidance Enhance step that blends the mask-averaged image feature with the visual prototype to provide an unbiased optimization target. A Background Screening module fuses the cost map (cosine similarity between pixel features and all guidance) with transformer-based spatial attention and learnable background tokens to predict background jointly from spatial and categorical cues, outperforming simple thresholding.

## Results

On three datasets with background (VOC21, PASCAL Context60, COCO Object), the method achieves an average mIoU of 48.3, outperforming the previous SOTA (CLIP-DINOiser, 43.1) by +5.2%. On five datasets without background (VOC20, PASCAL Context59, COCO Stuff, ADE20K, Cityscapes), it achieves an average mIoU of 42.8, surpassing the previous SOTA (PixelCLIP ViT, 38.7) by +6.1%. The largest single-dataset gains are +8.4% on PASCAL Context60 (40.8 vs. 32.4) and +8.6% on PASCAL Context59 (46.5 vs. 37.9). The only dataset where the method does not achieve the top score is VOC20 (85.0 vs. PixelCLIP ViT's 85.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines on the three with-background datasets, with the most significant margins over CLIP-DINOiser (previous SOTA): +5.6 on VOC21 (67.7 vs. 62.1), +8.4 on PASCAL Context60 (40.8 vs. 32.4), and +1.5 on COCO Object (36.3 vs. 34.8). On the five without-background datasets, it beats PixelCLIP (ViT) on four of five: Ctx59 +8.6 (46.5 vs. 37.9), Stuff +2.1 (25.7 vs. 23.6), ADE +2.0 (20.7 vs. 18.7), and Cityscapes +8.8 (36.0 vs. 27.2), but trails by 0.9 on VOC20 (85.0 vs. 85.9). Against text-supervised methods like TCL (35.3 avg w/bg) and CoDe (31.5 avg w/bg), the gains are substantial, exceeding +10 mIoU on average. The comparison with the raw CLIP baseline (12.3 avg w/bg) highlights the critical role of the dual semantic guidance fine-tuning strategy, yielding a +36 mIoU improvement. All results are reported without post-processing (no PAMR or DenseCRF), ensuring a fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-supervised open-vocabulary segmentation line (TCL, CoDe, GroupViT, SegCLIP) by arguing that text alone provides insufficient pixel-level guidance due to inherent semantic coarseness. It departs from model-guided approaches (CLIP-DINOiser, PixelCLIP) that rely on a single auxiliary model for pseudo-labels, instead constructing dual semantic guidance from both SAM-extracted visual instances and text-extracted nouns, each with tailored training losses (MFC for text, Guidance Enhance for visual). The Background Screening module borrows cost-aggregation-with-transformers from CAT-SEG, while the base encoder is MaskCLIP fine-tuned with a training-free Region Awareness Attention that modulates CLIP's self-attention to suppress background interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-guidance formulation addresses a recognized gap—text is too coarse and image-only guidance suffers modality domain shift—by jointly exploiting both modalities with modality-specific losses, yielding consistent SOTA gains (+5.2% avg w/ bg, +6.1% w/o bg) across eight benchmarks without post-processing. Practical impact is notable because inference requires only the CLIP image encoder and text encoder, with no additional decoder or auxiliary model. Open questions include the computational cost of SAM mask generation at scale, the fixed K=5 clustering choice, and whether the approach generalizes to denser or more semantically ambiguous scenes where instance boundaries are ambiguous.
