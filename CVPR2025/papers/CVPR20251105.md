## Problem

While Sora demonstrated the DiT architecture's potential for single-scene video generation, multi-scene long video generation—where distinct text prompts must guide different scenes while maintaining global visual coherence—remains largely unexplored within the DiT framework. Existing multi-scene approaches rely on U-Net-based T2V/I2V models with limited scalability for long-range temporal dependencies, or on keyframe-based pipelines that fail to capture temporal positioning and motion continuity across scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251105/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n=3 text prompts (one per scene, each describing characters and actions) as input and produces a single multi-scene video at 480×720 resolution with 49 frames per scene (147 frames total). Text tokens are concatenated before video tokens in a unified 1-D sequence, and the model can auto-regressively extend beyond the initial 3 scenes by conditioning each new segment on all preceding ones. The base model (CogVideoX) uses a 3D Causal VAE to encode video into visual tokens and a text encoder for prompt tokens.

## Method

Mask² DiT introduces two complementary attention masks within the DiT architecture. First, a symmetric binary attention mask at each layer enforces one-to-one alignment between each text annotation and its corresponding video segment while preserving full attention among all visual tokens for temporal coherence; a grouped attention mechanism replaces the single self-attention with 2n cross-attention operations to reduce memory. Second, a segment-level conditional mask drops noise from the first n−1 segments during training so the model learns to denoise the n-th segment conditioned on clean preceding segments, enabling auto-regressive scene extension. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training on randomly concatenated non-contiguous single-scene clips from Panda70M (1M samples) to adapt to multi-scene frame lengths, followed by supervised fine-tuning on content-relevant consecutive multi-scene videos with the conditional mask applied at probability p=0.5, reducing reliance on large-scale coherent multi-scene data.

## Results

On the multi-scene evaluation dataset (50 scenes, 5,371 videos), Mask² DiT achieves the best Visual Consistency (70.95%), Sequence Consistency (47.45%), and lowest FVD (720.01) among all compared methods. It improves Visual Consistency by 15.94% over CogVideoX (55.01%) and by 1.89% over StoryDiffusion+CogVideoX-I2V (69.06%). The method ranks second in Semantic Consistency (23.94%), trailing only StoryDiffusion (25.59%), while substantially outperforming TALC (20.25%) and VideoStudio (22.64%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mask² DiT outperforms all four baselines on Visual Consistency and FVD, the two metrics most relevant to multi-scene coherence. Against CogVideoX (single-scene generation concatenated), it gains +15.94% Visual Consistency and reduces FVD by 13.8%, confirming that explicit inter-scene attention modeling is critical. Compared to StoryDiffusion+CogVideoX-I2V (keyframe-based pipeline), Mask² DiT achieves higher Visual Consistency (+1.89%) and substantially lower FVD (720 vs. 906), though it trails by 1.65% in Semantic Consistency, which the authors attribute to T2V semantic alignment lagging behind T2I. Versus TALC (U-Net-based), the DiT architecture yields +3.48% Visual Consistency and a dramatically lower FVD (720 vs. 1517), validating the scalability advantage of transformers for long-range temporal modeling. Against VideoStudio (LLM-guided planning), Mask² DiT leads by +9.67% Visual Consistency and +5.49% Sequence Consistency, showing that direct multi-scene generation within a unified DiT outperforms LLM-scripted sequential generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CogVideoX DiT-based T2V model to multi-scene video generation, a task previously addressed almost exclusively through U-Net-based pipelines (TALC, VideoStudio, StoryDiffusion) or keyframe-then-I2V strategies. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a symmetric binary attention mask that enforces one-to-one text-to-segment alignment while preserving cross-segment visual coherence, plus a segment-level conditional mask for auto-regressive scene extension. The authors position this as the first integration of multi-prompt guidance within the DiT architecture, contrasting with prior work that either concatenates independently generated segments or relies on LLM-planned scripts with separate per-scene generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-scene video generation is a practical bottleneck for applications like film production and interactive storytelling, and this work demonstrates that the DiT architecture can handle it natively without the U-Net scalability limitations that constrained per-scene frame counts in prior methods. However, the current implementation is limited to a fixed n=3 scenes at 480×720 with 49 frames per scene, and the auto-regressive extension has not been validated beyond a single additional segment. Open questions include whether the dual-mask approach generalizes to adaptive-length scenes, higher resolutions, and longer video durations, and whether the reliance on ViClip-filtered training data limits real-world generalization.
