## Problem

LiDAR-based 3D object detectors trained on global Cartesian coordinates overfit to absolute object positions and sensor-specific coordinate frames, leading to severe performance degradation when deployed on unseen domains. Existing domain generalization strategies (adversarial augmentation, auxiliary tasks, multi-domain training) all still rely on global coordinates as input features, leaving the potential of explicit local geometry for cross-domain robustness largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard LiDAR point clouds (x, y, z coordinates; intensity excluded for fair comparison) and produces 3D bounding boxes with class labels (Car/Vehicle, Pedestrian, Cyclist) evaluated via KITTI AP metrics. The key I/O detail is that raw point coordinates are replaced by a 12-dimensional Gaussian blob descriptor per voxel (a 3D local offset vector d and a flattened 3×3 covariance matrix Σ), making it a direct drop-in feature swap for any existing voxel- or pillar-based detector without architectural changes.

## Method

GBlobs encodes each local point neighborhood (e.g., all points within a voxel) as a Gaussian distribution N(μ, Σ), where μ is the neighborhood mean and Σ is the covariance matrix. The mean is re-expressed as a local offset d = μ − voxel_center to decouple absolute position from the descriptor, while Σ captures the anisotropic spread and orientation of points within the neighborhood. The final 12-dimensional feature vector [d, Σ_flattened] is permutation-invariant by construction, requiring no point sorting, no surface normal estimation, and no additional learnable parameters. This representation is simply substituted for the standard (x, y, z) input features in any detector (Voxel R-CNN, SECOND, PointPillars, CenterPoint, DSVT, Part-A²) with all other training settings left at defaults, yielding large cross-domain gains (up to 21 mAP) without sacrificing in-domain accuracy.

## Results

On the single-source DG benchmark (Table 2), GBlobs with Voxel R-CNN achieves 73.48/67.82 mAP (BEV/3D) on Waymo→KITTI, surpassing the previous best (Li et al., 65.47/46.67) by over 8 mAP (BEV) and 21 mAP (3D). On nuScenes→KITTI, it reaches 49.48/37.62 mAP, a 12 mAP (3D) gain over the prior state-of-the-art. In multi-source DG (Table 4), CenterPoint w/GBlobs outperforms MDT3D by 17.4 mAP on Waymo, 12.1 mAP on KITTI, and 5.5 mAP on ONCE, though it underperforms on the extremely sparse nuScenes target (8.15 vs. 11.00 mAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GBlobs decisively outperforms all single-source DG baselines (PA-DA, 3D-VF, Li et al.) on Waymo→KITTI and nuScenes→KITTI, with the largest 3D mAP gains of +21.15 and +12.47 over Li et al. respectively. On the dense-to-sparse Waymo→nuScenes transfer, GBlobs is slightly below Li et al. on BEV mAP (16.45 vs. 17.22) but wins on 3D mAP (11.44 vs. 11.26), with the gap attributed to nuScenes' extreme sparsity (94% of voxels have ≤2 points, degenerating the covariance). In multi-source DG, CenterPoint w/GBlobs beats MDT3D by 17.4 mAP on Waymo, 12.1 on KITTI, and 5.5 on ONCE, but loses on nuScenes (8.15 vs. 11.00) due to the same sparsity limitation. In the KITTI→Waymo multi-detector evaluation (Table 3), GBlobs surpasses 3D-VF by 4.3, 13.0, and 4.1 AP on PointPillars, SECOND, and Part-A2 respectively, while also improving in-domain KITTI performance. Among local descriptors (Table 7), GBlobs outperforms relative distance (+2.1 mAP), surface normals (+3.4 mAP), and RepSurf (+1.5 mAP) on Waymo→KITTI, confirming that covariance-based encoding captures richer local geometry than simpler alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the near-universal practice of feeding global Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z) as input features to LiDAR 3D detectors, arguing that this biases models toward absolute position and harms cross-domain generalization. While prior work has augmented global features with local descriptors (relative distances in VoxelNet/PointPillars, surface normals in RepSurf, PointNet encodings), these are typically used *in conjunction with* global coordinates, which the authors show undermines their generalization benefit. GBlobs repurposes the classical Gaussian distribution (mean + covariance) as a permutation-invariant neighborhood descriptor used *exclusively* as input, eliminating the need for point sorting, surface normal estimation, or additional model parameters. For domain generalization specifically, it contrasts with methods relying on adversarial augmentation (3D-VF, PA-DA), auxiliary reconstruction tasks (Li et al.), or multi-domain mixing (MDT3D), demonstrating that a pure input-representation change suffices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a few lines of parameter-free code, applicable to any voxel- or pillar-based detector, yields 12–21 mAP gains in single-source DG and 5–17 mAP in multi-source DG without degrading in-domain accuracy, making it an easy drop-in improvement for practitioners. The conceptual finding that global coordinates are actively detrimental to generalization challenges a foundational assumption in 3D detection and suggests rethinking input representations more broadly. Key limitations include degraded performance on very sparse LiDAR (nuScenes, where <3 points per voxel causes covariance to degenerate to zero) and the untested question of whether GBlobs improves pseudo-label quality in UDA self-training loops, which the authors identify as a natural next step.
