limitations: The paper explicitly acknowledges that the deformable scanning strategy degrades when images contain incomplete object structures or multiple objects arranged in a regular pattern, as the learned offsets become too small and converge toward the predefined scanning order. Additionally, the non-differentiable sorting operation used for token reordering requires a gradient approximation (averaging and replicating gradients), which introduces a theoretical gap in the training signal.

surprise_factor: No particularly surprising findings — DefMamba achieves competitive but modest improvements (0.3–1.6 points) over the strongest SSM baselines (GrootV, QuadMamba) across ImageNet, COCO, and ADE20K, and the ablation confirms the deformable branch contributes ~1.7% over a standard bidirectional scan. The conceptual idea of deformable scanning is clean, but the performance margins are within the range of typical SOTA bumps in this area.

## Problem

Existing visual Mamba methods flatten 2D feature maps into 1D sequences using fixed scanning orders (raster, local, continuous), which breaks spatial adjacency and loses structural information about objects. Even adaptive approaches like QuadMamba and GrootV still rely on predefined scanning patterns within windows or only consider local neighbor relationships, making them insensitive to variations in object details and unable to truly adapt the scanning path to the input content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250648/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images, which are patch-embedded into 2D feature maps at H/4 × W/4 resolution and progressively downsampled through four stages (H/8, H/16, H/32) with channel dimensions C, 2C, 4C, 8C. For classification, features are average-pooled to a single class logit vector; for detection and segmentation, the multi-scale feature pyramid is fed into Mask R-CNN or UperNet heads to produce bounding boxes, instance masks, or pixel-wise semantic maps. A notable design choice is the use of overlapping patch embedding and downsampling layers (following GrootV) rather than standard strided operations.

## Method

DefMamba introduces a Deformable State Space Model (DSSM) that augments the standard forward/backward Mamba branches with a deformable branch performing two parallel operations: (1) Point Offset, where a lightweight subnetwork (depthwise conv + channel attention + 1×1 conv) predicts 2D spatial offsets that shift reference sampling points toward salient regions, with bilinear interpolation to read features at deformed positions and a learnable relative offset bias (inspired by Swin's relative position encoding) to compensate for position-encoding misalignment; and (2) Index Offset, where the same subnetwork predicts a 1D token-index offset that reorders the scanning sequence via sorting, producing a structure-aware 1D sequence aligned with object geometry. Offsets are stabilized with tanh and normalized to single-token range to prevent extreme jumps. The overall architecture is a four-stage multi-scale backbone of Deformable Mamba blocks (each containing LN, DSSM, FFN, and residual connections), trained end-to-end with standard supervised objectives on ImageNet, COCO, and ADE20K.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, DefMamba-T achieves 78.6% Top-1 accuracy with 8M parameters and 1.2G FLOPs, outperforming all SSM baselines at the tiny scale (best prior: PlainMamba-L1 at 77.9%). DefMamba-S reaches 83.5% Top-1 (32M params, 4.8G FLOPs), and DefMamba-B achieves 84.2% Top-1 (51M params, 8.5G FLOPs), matching GrootV-S while using fewer FLOPs. On COCO 2017 (Mask R-CNN, 1× schedule), DefMamba-S attains 47.5 AP^b and 42.8 AP^m. On ADE20K (UperNet, 512×512 crop), DefMamba-S achieves 48.8 mIOU (single-scale) and 49.6 mIOU (multi-scale).

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the tiny scale, DefMamba-T (78.6%) surpasses all SSM baselines by a clear margin: +0.7% over PlainMamba-L1 (77.9%), +1.3% over MSVMamba-N (77.3%), and +2.5% over ViM-T (76.1%), while using 60% fewer FLOPs than PlainMamba-L1. At the small scale, DefMamba-S (83.5%) edges out GrootV-T (83.4%) and beats EffVMamba-B (83.0%), MSVMamba-T (82.8%), and VMamba-T (82.2%) by 0.5–1.3 points. At the base scale, DefMamba-B (84.2%) ties GrootV-S (84.2%) while using the same 8.5G FLOPs, and outperforms VMamba-S (83.6%) and LocalVMamba-B (83.7%) by 0.5–0.6 points. On COCO detection, DefMamba-S (47.5 AP^b) beats VMamba-T (47.4), GrootV-T (47.0), and MSVMamba-T (46.9) with 4% fewer FLOPs than VMamba-T. On ADE20K segmentation, DefMamba-S (48.8 mIOU SS) leads GrootV-T (48.5) by 0.3 points and VMamba-T (47.9) by 0.9 points, confirming consistent gains across all three tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DefMamba extends the visual Mamba lineage (ViM, VMamba, PlainMamba, GrootV, QuadMamba) by replacing their fixed or semi-adaptive scanning orders with a content-aware deformable scanning strategy. The deformable mechanism is directly inspired by deformable convolutions (Dai et al., 2017) and deformable attention (DAT, Xia et al., 2022), but is the first to apply learned spatial offsets and dynamic token reordering within a state space model. Unlike GrootV (which builds a tree topology from adjacent features) or QuadMamba (which adjusts window granularity but keeps fixed intra-window order), DefMamba simultaneously shifts reference points toward salient regions and reorders the 1D sequence to align with object structure, drawing on the selective scan mechanism from Mamba as its sequence-processing backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The deformable scanning strategy addresses a recognized weakness of visual SSMs—loss of 2D spatial structure under fixed flattening—without incurring the quadratic cost of attention, making it a practical path toward structure-aware linear-complexity vision backbones. However, the paper itself acknowledges that the approach degrades on incomplete objects or regularly arranged scenes where inter-token information variation is minimal, and the gradient approximation for the non-differentiable sorting operation remains a theoretical gap. Open questions include whether the deformable mechanism transfers to other SSM variants (e.g., H3, S4), whether the offset network's reliance on local convolution plus channel attention is sufficient for very high-resolution inputs, and whether the modest 0.3–1.6 point gains over GrootV and QuadMamba persist at larger model scales or in downstream tasks beyond the three evaluated here.
