## Problem

Existing GFS-PCS methods rely solely on sparse few-shot support samples to learn novel class representations, limiting their generalization to diverse and challenging novel categories. Meanwhile, 3D vision-language models offer rich open-world knowledge but produce noisy dense predictions that cannot be directly used as supervision. Additionally, current GFS-PCS benchmarks contain only six novel classes, failing to reflect the diversity of real-world novel categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252868/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a voxelized 3D point cloud (0.02 m grid), K-shot support samples per novel class (K=1 or 5), and all base+novel class names as text prompts to the 3D VLM. It outputs per-point semantic labels spanning both base and novel classes simultaneously, without requiring support samples at inference. Unlike prior GFS-PCS evaluations that test on small blocks, this work evaluates on whole scenes to better simulate real-world deployment.

## Method

GFS-VL synergizes dense but noisy 3D VLM pseudo-labels with precise yet sparse few-shot samples through three modules. First, prototype-guided pseudo-label selection computes support prototypes from few-shot samples and filters VLM predictions by retaining only points whose predicted-class prototype exceeds a cosine similarity threshold τ against the corresponding support prototype. Second, adaptive infilling constructs an adaptive prototype set that uses pseudo-label-context prototypes where novel classes are present and falls back to support prototypes otherwise, then assigns labels to remaining unlabeled points whose maximum similarity to any adaptive prototype exceeds threshold δ. Third, novel-base mix embeds locally cropped novel support samples into training scenes by aligning opposite XY-plane corners, preserving contextual cues critical for recognizing challenging novel objects—unlike Mix3D-style augmentation that discards scene context. The segmentor uses a PTv3 backbone with a linear head, pre-trained on base classes for 800 epochs and fine-tuned for 20 epochs with the augmented pseudo-label supervision.

## Results

On the new ScanNet200 benchmark (40 novel classes), GFS-VL achieves 43.12 HM and 31.67 mIoU-N in the 5-shot setting, surpassing the closest baseline GW (14.55 HM, 8.30 mIoU-N) by 28.57% in HM and 23.37% in mIoU-N. On the ScanNet++ benchmark (18 novel classes), GFS-VL reaches 29.47 HM and 19.42 mIoU-N (1-shot), improving over GW by 17.88% in HM. On the original ScanNet benchmark (6 novel classes), GFS-VL attains 60.88 HM and 49.72 mIoU-N (1-shot), a 39.33% gain in HM over GW. The method approaches the fully supervised upper bound (50.02 HM on ScanNet200) while using only 1–5 support samples per novel class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFS-VL decisively outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against GW [66] (the strongest GFS-PCS baseline using geometric prototypes), GFS-VL gains 28.57% in HM and 23.37% in mIoU-N on ScanNet200 (5-shot), and 39.33% in HM on the original ScanNet benchmark (1-shot). COSeg [2], the best FS-PCS baseline, trails GFS-VL by over 30 HM points on ScanNet200 (9.54 vs. 43.12, 5-shot), highlighting the gap between standard few-shot and generalized few-shot settings. PIFS [4] and attMPTI [77] achieve only 6.71 and 8.79 HM respectively on ScanNet200 (5-shot), far below GFS-VL's 43.12, indicating that prototype-refinement-only strategies are insufficient for diverse novel classes. CAPL [56] (evaluated only on the 6-class ScanNet benchmark) scores 20.88 HM (5-shot) versus GFS-VL's 61.91, a 31-point gap. The consistent and large margins across all baselines and datasets underscore that integrating 3D VLM pseudo-labels with few-shot calibration is critical for robust novel-class generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFS-PCS line initiated by GW [66] and CAPL [56], which refine class prototypes through support-query interactions but remain constrained by the sparse knowledge in few-shot samples. The key departure is the integration of 3D vision-language models (RegionPLC, OpenScene) as an additional dense knowledge source, whose noisy pseudo-labels are calibrated by precise few-shot support prototypes via a selection-and-infilling pipeline. The novel-base mix strategy also departs from standard 3D augmentation (Mix3D, object exchange) by deliberately preserving scene context rather than discarding it, arguing that contextual cues are essential for recognizing challenging novel objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two new benchmarks (40 novel classes on ScanNet200, 18 on ScanNet++) expose a critical evaluation gap: prior GFS-PCS benchmarks with only 6 novel classes fail to stress-test generalization, and existing methods show substantially degraded performance on the harder splits. The framework demonstrates that 3D VLM knowledge, when properly filtered, can close much of the gap to fully supervised performance (43.12% vs. 50.02% HM on ScanNet200), suggesting a viable path toward open-world few-shot segmentation. Open questions include computational cost of VLM inference at scale, sensitivity to VLM quality, and whether the approach extends to outdoor or LiDAR point clouds where VLM coverage is weaker.
