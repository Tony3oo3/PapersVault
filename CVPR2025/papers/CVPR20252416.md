## Problem

Existing text-to-audio (T2A) and video-to-audio (V2A) models each capture only part of a realistic soundscape: T2A produces semantically complete audio but lacks temporal alignment with onscreen events, while V2A aligns temporally but omits offscreen ambient sounds. Joint text-and-video approaches that do exist suffer from modality bias (typically favoring visual over textual signals) and assume text-video alignment that rarely holds in practice. Additionally, no benchmark exists to evaluate holistic audio generation that includes both onscreen and offscreen sound sources.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 10-second video clip (processed into per-frame CLIP embeddings, mean optical-flow magnitudes, and sinusoidal frame-index encodings) and a free-form text prompt (encoded via FLAN-T5) as joint conditions. It outputs a full audio waveform in which onscreen sounds are temporally synchronized with the video and offscreen sounds are semantically consistent with the text description. Generation occurs in the latent space of a pretrained Audio VAE, with the final waveform reconstructed by a HiFi-GAN vocoder; classifier-free guidance scales (s_vis, s_txt) at inference allow independent control over the influence of each modality.

## Method

VinTAGe is a flow-matching transformer built on the SiT architecture, extended with a Visual-Text (VT) Encoder and dual cross-attention in every SiT block. The VT-Encoder fuses CLIP visual and FLAN-T5 text embeddings through a gated cross-attention mechanism (tanh-gated with zero-initialized parameters for stable training) and appends optical-flow and frame-index encodings for temporal guidance. Each SiT block applies two cross-attention layers—one attending to text embeddings and one to visual embeddings—weighted by a learnable scalar ω_l that lets the model dynamically balance modality contributions per layer. To counter modality bias, the training objective includes teacher-guided alignment losses that match the joint model's velocity predictions (when one modality is dropped) to those of separately pretrained T2A and V2A teacher models, effectively regularizing against dominance by either signal. Inference uses multi-conditional classifier-free guidance with independent visual and text guidance scales, and training batches are structured so that one-third of samples condition on both modalities, one-third on text only, and one-third on video only.

## Results

On VinTAGe-Bench, VinTAGe achieves the best generation quality with FAD of 3.05 and FID of 16.43, outperforming the next-best Tango2+LLaVA (FAD 4.12, FID 29.1) and FoleyCrafter (FAD 5.81, FID 25.64). It also attains the highest mean alignment score (16.06) and best subjective scores across all three MOS metrics (MOS-Q 3.36, MOS-F 3.58, MOS-T 3.36). On the standard VGGSound V2A benchmark, VinTAGe achieves the best FID (6.65), MKL (4.12), and Isc (63.95%), surpassing FoleyCrafter (FID 9.17, MKL 4.48, Isc 62.49%) and Diff-Foley (FID 9.87, MKL 6.43, Isc 62.37%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VinTAGe outperforms all 10 baselines on generation quality (FAD, FID) and all three subjective MOS metrics on VinTAGe-Bench. Among joint text+video methods, FoleyCrafter is the closest competitor on alignment (15.96 vs. 16.06) and on-screen concept accuracy (64.93% vs. 57.7%), but VinTAGe substantially outperforms it on off-screen accuracy (43.63% vs. 21.69%), revealing FoleyCrafter's strong visual bias. ReWaS exhibits the most severe modality bias, with only 11.32% on-screen and 19.73% mean concept accuracy. Pure T2A models (Tango2, AudioLDM2) achieve higher mean concept accuracy (55.57% and 40.52%) but lack visual alignment (AV scores of 7.19 and 6.65 vs. VinTAGe's 9.83). The simple Tango2+LLaVA extension (concatenating video captions) improves FAD by ~30% over Tango2 alone, confirming that joint conditioning helps, yet VinTAGe's dedicated VT-Encoder and teacher-guided training still yield a further 26% FAD improvement over Tango2+LLaVA. On VGGSound, VinTAGe achieves the best FID (6.65 vs. FoleyCrafter's 9.17) and Isc (63.95% vs. 62.49%), with the largest gap over Diff-Foley (FID 9.87) and ReWaS (FID 28.28).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SiT flow-matching transformer architecture (originally designed for image generation) to the audio domain, adding dual cross-attention layers for joint video and text conditioning. It departs from prior joint VT2A methods (FoleyCrafter, ReWaS) that assume text and video describe the same onscreen content, by explicitly handling the case where text specifies offscreen sounds absent from the video. The teacher-student guidance mechanism, where pretrained T2A and V2A models provide alignment losses, is a novel training strategy not seen in prior audio generation work, drawing conceptually from knowledge distillation to mitigate modality bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a practical gap in post-production sound design: generating a complete soundscape (onscreen + offscreen) from a single video-text pair, which is labor-intensive with traditional Foley techniques. The VinTAGe-Bench, while small (636 pairs), is the first benchmark specifically designed for this holistic generation task, and the finding that existing joint methods exhibit strong visual bias (e.g., FoleyCrafter: 64.9% on-screen vs. 21.7% off-screen accuracy) validates the need for the proposed approach. Open questions include whether the teacher-student modality-bias mitigation generalizes to other multi-modal generation tasks, whether the small benchmark limits conclusions about scalability, and whether the 4 FPS inference speed is sufficient for real-time applications.
