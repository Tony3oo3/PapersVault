## Problem

Existing semantic segmentation architectures cannot simultaneously achieve efficient global context modeling, fine-grained local detail encoding, and rich multi-scale feature extraction. Fixed-window attention methods (e.g., VWFormer) lose global coverage at higher input resolutions, while spatial-reduction approaches (e.g., EDAFormer) sacrifice fine-grained local details, leaving a gap in omni-scale context modeling that adapts to varying input resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251381/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (512×512 crops for ADE20K and COCO-Stuff, 1024×1024 for Cityscapes training, 2048×1024 for Cityscapes evaluation) and outputs a pixel-wise categorical segmentation mask at full input resolution. A key design property is resolution adaptivity: the multi-scale context extraction in the decoder and the global scanning in the encoder automatically scale their receptive fields with input size, unlike fixed-window or fixed-scale alternatives. The encoder produces a four-level feature pyramid at H/4, H/8, H/16, and H/32 spatial resolutions.

## Method

SegMAN introduces two novel components. The SegMAN Encoder uses a LASS (Local Attention and State Space) token mixer that serially stacks Neighborhood Attention (Natten) for sliding-window local detail encoding and SS2D (2D selective scan from VMamba) for dynamic global context modeling, with a residual shortcut around SS2D and a 1×1 convolution for global-local fusion, all at linear time complexity. The MMSCopE decoder module generates multi-scale feature maps via strided convolutions (1/2 and 1/4 resolution), losslessly aligns their spatial dimensions using Pixel Unshuffle, concatenates them along the channel axis, and performs a single SS2D scan to extract multi-scale contexts simultaneously, then injects the result back into stage-specific encoder features. Unlike fixed-window or pooling-based multi-scale methods, both the encoder's global scanning and the decoder's multi-scale extraction adaptively cover the full feature map regardless of input resolution, preserving fine-grained details that are typically lost to spatial reduction.

## Results

SegMAN-B achieves 52.6% mIoU on ADE20K, 83.8% mIoU on Cityscapes, and 48.4% mIoU on COCO-Stuff-164K, setting new state-of-the-art at its scale (51.8M params, 58.1 GFLOPs). The SegMAN-S variant (29.4M params) reaches 51.3% mIoU on ADE20K and 83.2% on Cityscapes with only 25.3 GFLOPs, outperforming larger models like SegNeXt-L (48.9M params) by 0.3% mIoU on ADE20K. On ImageNet-1K classification, the SegMAN-B Encoder achieves 85.1% top-1 accuracy, surpassing VMamba-S (83.6%) by 1.5% with fewer parameters (45M vs 50M). The largest SegMAN-L model attains 53.2% mIoU on ADE20K and 84.2% on Cityscapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegMAN consistently outperforms all compared baselines across all three segmentation benchmarks at every model scale. At the base scale, SegMAN-B surpasses SegNeXt-L (48.9M params) by +1.6% mIoU on ADE20K (52.6 vs 51.0) and +1.9% on COCO-Stuff (48.4 vs 46.5) while using 17% fewer GFLOPs (58.1 vs 70.0); it also beats VWFormer-B3 by +2.3% on ADE20K and +1.6% on COCO-Stuff with lower compute. At the small scale, SegMAN-S (29.4M) exceeds EDAFormer-B (29.4M) by +2.3% mIoU on ADE20K (51.3 vs 49.0) and +1.6% on Cityscapes (83.2 vs 81.6) with 21% fewer GFLOPs (25.3 vs 32.1), and outperforms the larger SegNeXt-L (48.9M) by +0.3% on ADE20K. At the tiny scale, SegMAN-T achieves 80.3% mIoU on Cityscapes, beating EDAFormer-T (78.7%) by +1.6% and SegNeXt-T (78.9%) by +1.4% with comparable FLOPs. On ImageNet-1K classification, the SegMAN-B Encoder (45M params) reaches 85.1% top-1 accuracy, exceeding VMamba-S (50M, 83.6%) by +1.5% and SparX-Mamba-S (47M, 84.2%) by +0.9%. The paper also demonstrates encoder generalizability: pairing the SegMAN-S Encoder with third-party decoders (VWFormer, SegNeXt, EDAFormer) yields +2.1%, +1.9%, and +1.2% mIoU gains respectively over their original encoder-decoder configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegMAN sits at the intersection of two recent trends in vision backbones: state space models (Mamba/VMamba's SS2D) for linear-time global context modeling, and neighborhood attention (Natten) for efficient local feature encoding. Prior segmentation architectures like VWFormer and EDAFormer addressed multi-scale context through fixed window sizes or spatial reduction, but could not simultaneously maintain full feature-map coverage at high resolutions and preserve fine-grained local details. SegMAN's LASS token mixer is the first to serially combine Natten and SS2D within a single block with a residual shortcut, and its MMSCopE decoder repurposes SS2D scanning over channel-concatenated, Pixel-Unshuffled multi-scale feature maps—a design absent from prior decoders that rely on pooling or fixed-scale cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent SOTA gains across ADE20K, Cityscapes, and COCO-Stuff at multiple model scales, combined with linear-time complexity and notably higher inference speed (e.g., ~3× faster than EDAFormer-T at higher mIoU), make SegMAN a practically attractive backbone for real-time segmentation. The finding that the encoder alone outperforms VMamba-S by 1.5% ImageNet accuracy with fewer parameters suggests the Natten+SS2D combination is a broadly useful token-mixing recipe beyond segmentation. Open questions include whether the fixed four-direction SS2D scanning is optimal for all spatial layouts, whether the Pixel-Unshuffle-based multi-scale concatenation generalizes to non-power-of-two resolutions, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D segmentation.
