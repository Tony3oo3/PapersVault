## Problem

Existing PEFT-based continual learning methods like InfLoRA enforce strict orthogonality between task-specific parameter subspaces, which imposes a hard capacity limit of ⌊d/r⌋ tasks and suffers from suboptimal sequential allocation where early tasks may monopolize critical subspaces. The paper asks whether 'almost-orthogonal' task separation achieved probabilistically through a bilinear parameter expansion can replace the need for complex learned orthogonalization while supporting quadratically more tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces class labels in a class-incremental setting where all previously seen classes must be recognized without task identity at inference. Adapters are inserted only in the query and key linear layers of ViT-B/16 attention blocks, and the classification head uses dynamic expansion (adding new output nodes per task while freezing prior class weights). The task-specific parameters are sparse complex-valued frequency matrices constrained by Hermitian symmetry to ensure real-valued weight updates.

## Method

BiLoRA reformulates LoRA's low-rank update ΔW = AB into a bilinear form ΔW_t = F B_t F^H, where F is the fixed DFT matrix and B_t is a sparse complex frequency matrix with only k non-zero entries per task. This bilinear structure expands the effective parameter space from d to d² dimensions, reducing the pairwise task-interference probability from O((k/d)²) to O((k/d²)²) without any learned orthogonalization. Two allocation strategies are supported: without-replacement sampling guarantees zero frequency collisions (supporting ⌊d²/k⌋ tasks) and with-replacement sampling allows unlimited tasks with negligible collision probability. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) bound the Frobenius-norm interference ‖ΔW_i^T ΔW_j‖_F under the constraint k ≤ c(d²/T)log(1/δ), and the entire forward pass is computed via 2D FFT for efficiency.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (10 tasks), BiLoRA achieves 77.95% final accuracy and 81.52% average accuracy, outperforming the previous best (InfLoRA) by 2.3% on final accuracy. On CIFAR-100, it reaches 87.46% final and 92.50% average accuracy, approaching the joint-training upper bound of 91.92%. On DomainNet, it attains 76.56% final and 81.65% average accuracy, again leading all baselines. The method also scales well, maintaining 72.41% final accuracy with 20 tasks on ImageNet-R where baselines degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiLoRA outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is InfLoRA [22], which BiLoRA surpasses by 2.3% final accuracy on ImageNet-R (77.95 vs. 75.65), 0.95% on CIFAR-100 (87.46 vs. 86.51), and 1.1% on DomainNet (76.56 vs. 75.45). Among prompt-based methods, CODA-P [34] is the strongest, but BiLoRA leads it by 6.25% on ImageNet-R and 0.84% on CIFAR-100 final accuracy. C-LoRA [33] and LAE [8] trail further behind, with C-LoRA underperforming even DualPrompt [38] on ImageNet-R average accuracy (75.33 vs. 75.82). L2P [39] is the weakest baseline across all three benchmarks. The gap between BiLoRA and the joint-training upper bound narrows to 4.46% on CIFAR-100, indicating near-optimal sequential learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA-based continual learning line initiated by InfLoRA (Liang & Li, CVPR 2024), which enforces strict orthogonality between task-specific low-rank matrices via gradient-guided subspace allocation. The key departure is replacing learned orthogonal projections with fixed orthogonal bases (specifically DFT, building on Gao et al., ICML 2024) and reformulating the weight update as a bilinear decomposition ΔW = U B_t V^T, which expands the effective parameter space from d to d² dimensions. This shifts the task-separation problem from one of constrained optimization (finding orthogonal subspaces in a fixed space) to one of probabilistic allocation in a much larger space, where near-orthogonality emerges naturally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic parameter-space expansion provides a clean theoretical resolution to the fundamental capacity bottleneck in PEFT-based continual learning (T ≤ ⌊d/r⌋ tasks under strict orthogonality), enabling support for O(d²) tasks with provable interference bounds. However, the method is currently validated only on ViT-B/16 in a class-incremental setting, and the 'almost orthogonal' guarantee is probabilistic rather than deterministic, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other architectures, non-classification tasks, or settings where the d² expansion becomes computationally prohibitive. The finding that with-replacement and without-replacement sampling yield nearly identical performance suggests the method is robust, but the modest empirical gains (~2% over InfLoRA) indicate the practical ceiling may be limited by the backbone rather than the task-separation mechanism.
