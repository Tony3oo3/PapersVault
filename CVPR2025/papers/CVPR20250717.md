## Problem

Reconstructing the 3D geometry of a rigid object manipulated dynamically by one or both hands from monocular RGB video remains challenging because existing methods either rely on SfM (which fails in dynamic scenes with occlusions), assume a firm grasp (limiting to single-hand and causing persistent occlusions), or use incremental segment-based optimization that introduces pose errors at boundaries. No prior approach handles poorly textured, thin-structured, or two-hand interaction scenarios with high fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250717/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video sequence of one or both hands interacting with a rigid object, plus a text description (auto-generated by ChatGPT from the first frame) used to produce a 3D prior mesh via a text-to-3D model. It outputs a textured 3D mesh of the object, per-frame 6DoF object poses (rotation and translation in camera space), and an implicit SDF representation. Unlike static multi-view reconstruction, the object moves independently of the camera, and the method must handle occlusions from the hand throughout the sequence.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) pose initialization by generating a textured 3D mesh prior via text-to-3D (Genie) and aligning it to each frame using a combined projection-mask loss and a semantic consistency loss in DINO feature space, which tolerates shape and texture discrepancies between the generated prior and the real object; (2) joint optimization of a NeuS-style SDF implicit representation and object poses, augmented with monocular normal consistency and multi-view correspondence losses (via DKM) as geometric constraints; (3) a pose outlier voting strategy that computes Sampson distance on 2D correspondences within local temporal windows to detect frames with large pose errors, then reinitializes those poses via spherical interpolation from neighboring inliers. The key insight is that semantic features provide a more robust alignment signal than pixel-level appearance when the generated prior differs in shape and texture from the ground truth, and that Sampson distance avoids the neighbor-bias problem inherent in PSNR-based outlier detection.

## Results

On HO3D, the proposed method achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.51 cm², F5 of 78.2%, and F10 of 95.4%, substantially outperforming HOLD (CD 1.82, F5 63.6%) and IHOI (CD 4.78, F5 53.6%). On the more challenging HOD dataset, the method reduces CD to 0.44 (unit size) versus 1.27 for HOLD and 7.28 for IHOI. For two-hand reconstruction on HOPE, the method achieves CD 0.29 cm² and F5 85.4%, compared to 0.96 and 62.5% for the COLMAP-BARF baseline. Pose accuracy also improves markedly, with ATE of 0.072 m versus 0.265 m for HOLD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against HOLD [9] (SfM-based initialization), it reduces HO3D Chamfer Distance from 1.82 to 0.51 cm² (3.6× improvement) and HOD CD from 1.27 to 0.44, while also halving pose errors (RPEt 2.13 vs 4.14 cm; ATE 0.072 vs 0.265 m). Against IHOI [56] (learning-based single-frame reconstruction), the gap is even larger: CD drops from 4.78 to 0.51 on HO3D and from 7.28 to 0.44 on HOD, with F5 improving from 53.6% to 78.2%. On the same HO3D sequence used by Hampali et al. [13], the method achieves CD 0.6 vs 1.4 and F5 76.0% vs 57.4%, confirming superiority over the incremental segment-based approach. For two-hand reconstruction on HOPE, the method beats the COLMAP-BARF baseline [28] by a wide margin (CD 0.29 vs 0.96, F5 85.4% vs 62.5%), demonstrating that generated-prior initialization plus semantic consistency is far more effective than COLMAP poses in dynamic hand-object scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NeuS SDF-based volume rendering framework for object representation and the BARF paradigm of jointly optimizing implicit representations with camera poses. It departs from prior hand-held object reconstruction methods (HOLD, IHOI, Hampali et al.) that rely on SfM or hand-pose trackers for initialization, which fail on texture-less objects, thin structures, or two-hand interactions. The key conceptual departure is using text-to-3D generated meshes as pose priors, combined with DINO semantic feature alignment to bridge the shape/texture gap between generated and real objects, replacing the brittle SfM/hand-prior pipeline entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that generated 3D priors can serve as effective pose initializers for dynamic hand-object reconstruction, opening a path to handle texture-less and thin objects that defeat SfM-based methods, and extending the problem to two-hand interactions for which no prior solution exists. The ablation showing that semantic consistency (DINO features) is critical—without it, reconstruction quality degrades by roughly 4× in Chamfer Distance—highlights an underexploited role for foundation-model features in geometric alignment. Limitations include dependence on text-to-3D generator quality for novel objects and DINO's inability to distinguish semantically similar object regions, suggesting that advances in semantic correspondence will be needed for broader applicability.
