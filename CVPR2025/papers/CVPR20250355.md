## Problem

Concept erasure in text-to-image diffusion models requires simultaneously removing target concepts (erasure efficacy) while preserving all non-target content (prior preservation). Training-based methods are computationally expensive and require per-concept fine-tuning, making real-time erasure impractical, while existing training-free methods like SLD and Negative Prompt compromise prior preservation or lack fine-grained control, failing to serve as robust standalone erasure tools.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250355/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and one or more target concept strings, operating entirely within a pre-trained T2I diffusion model (e.g., SD v1.4, SDXL) without any parameter updates. The target concept is encoded by the CLIP text encoder and pre-processed by duplicating its last subject token across all positions except [SOT]. During the 30-step DPM-solver denoising loop, the method intercepts each cross-attention layer's value matrix and replaces it with a projected version before the attention-weighted aggregation, producing a final image where the target concept is absent but non-target content remains intact.

## Method

AdaVD performs concept erasure by projecting the original prompt's value vectors onto the orthogonal complement of the target concept's value vectors at every cross-attention layer in the UNet, exploiting the fact that values encode the 'what' (visual content) while keys encode the 'where' (layout). For multi-concept erasure, Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization yields an orthonormal basis for the joint target subspace, and the projection removes all target directions simultaneously. A key innovation is an adaptive token-wise shift factor δ(x,y) = s/(1+exp(-p(cos(x,y)-ε))) that modulates erasure strength per token: when a prompt token's value vector is weakly aligned with the target (low cosine similarity), the shift reduces the projection magnitude, protecting prior knowledge; when alignment is strong, full erasure is applied. This training-free mechanism requires only a one-time basis computation (~4 s for 10 concepts) and adds ~0.8 s per image over standard generation, achieving 2–10× lower FID on non-target concepts than the second-best method while matching or exceeding erasure efficacy.

## Results

On single-concept erasure of 'Snoopy' (SD v1.4), AdaVD achieves the lowest CLIP Score (CS = 20.28) and the lowest FID across all five non-target concepts (range: 2.32–8.56), outperforming the second-best method SPM (FID range: 18.26–34.70) by 2–10×. For multi-concept erasure (up to 3 concepts), AdaVD maintains the best CS and FID simultaneously, e.g., erasing 'Snoopy' + 'Mickey' + 'Spongebob' yields CS of 19.39/19.73/20.34 and FID as low as 2.79 for non-target 'Dog'. In art style erasure (Table 2), AdaVD achieves FID as low as 2.33 (Monet non-target) versus 7.76 for SPM, while matching or approaching the best CS. Total time for 10-concept erasure is 22 s (vs. 10,419 s for ConAbl and 72,867 s for SPM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaVD outperforms all five baselines on both erasure efficacy (CS) and prior preservation (FID) across every tested scenario. Against the strongest training-free baseline SLD, AdaVD reduces FID by roughly 5–20× (e.g., 2.32 vs. 41.95 on 'Dog') while also achieving a lower CS (20.28 vs. 27.84), meaning SLD fails to fully erase the target. Against the best training-based method SPM, AdaVD improves FID by 3–8× (e.g., 5.72 vs. 33.06 on 'Mickey') with a lower CS (20.28 vs. 23.89). MACE achieves competitive CS (20.78) but suffers catastrophic prior preservation (FID up to 118), while ConAbl and NP show moderate erasure but large FID degradation (30–80+). In multi-concept settings, the gap widens: SLD's CS barely drops below the un-erased baseline (27.76 vs. 28.51 for 'Snoopy'), whereas AdaVD consistently achieves the lowest CS and FID even when erasing three concepts simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaVD carves new ground by repurposing a classical linear algebra operation—projection onto the orthogonal complement—within the value space of cross-attention layers in diffusion UNets, a specific intervention point not explored by prior training-free methods. It departs from training-based erasure (ConAbl, ESD, SPM, MACE) that fine-tune parameters with regularization, and from training-free methods (NP, SLD) that adjust classifier-free guidance globally, by performing token-wise, concept-specific decomposition that disentangles target semantics from non-target content. The adaptive shift factor, derived from cosine similarity between prompt and target value vectors, further distinguishes AdaVD from the concurrent SAFREE, which applies orthogonal decomposition to text embeddings with masking and Fourier transforms rather than to the learned value space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of AdaVD enables real-time, on-demand erasure of newly emerging concepts without retraining, addressing a critical practical gap for online T2I platforms where unsafe or copyrighted content can appear unpredictably. The 2-to-10-fold improvement in prior preservation (FID) while maintaining best or near-best erasure efficacy demonstrates that precise concept removal and faithful preservation of non-target content are not inherently in tension, challenging the trade-off assumed by prior methods. Open questions include establishing a rigorous theoretical understanding of why layer-wise orthogonal complement projection accumulates into effective erasure, and whether the approach generalizes to architectures without explicit cross-attention (e.g., DiT-based models).
