## Problem

Existing driving scene generation methods produce only a single data format (e.g., RGB video or LiDAR) from coarse layout conditions, limiting their applicability to downstream tasks that require diverse sensor modalities. Moreover, the direct layout-to-data modeling strategy struggles to capture the complex geometric and appearance distributions of real driving scenes, resulting in suboptimal generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251115/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-frame BEV layout sequences (user-editable geometric conditions such as object positions and road structures) and optional text prompts for attribute control (weather, lighting) as input. It produces three synchronized outputs: a temporally consistent semantic occupancy sequence at 12 Hz, multi-view driving videos in SVD's latent space, and LiDAR point clouds with per-point intensity and ray-dropping attributes. The BEV layouts serve as the sole geometric control signal, while text prompts modulate only the video appearance.

## Method

UniScene decomposes scene generation into a two-level hierarchy centered on semantic occupancy as an intermediate representation. In the first stage, a Latent Occupancy DiT (built on stacked spatial and temporal transformer blocks) generates occupancy latent sequences from BEV layouts concatenated with noise, decoded by a temporal-aware VAE that uses 3D axial attention in the decoder for cross-frame consistency. In the second stage, the generated occupancy is converted into 3D Gaussian primitives and rendered into multi-view semantic and depth maps via tile-based rasterization; these maps condition a ControlNet-style encoder on SVD's video UNet, while a geometric-aware noise prior warps appearance features using depth-based reprojection for cross-view consistency. For LiDAR, a Sparse UNet aggregates occupancy features, and a prior-guided sparse sampling strategy (assigning probability 1 to occupied voxels) resamples points along LiDAR rays, which are then processed by an SDF-based volume rendering head augmented with reflection intensity and ray-dropping sub-heads to simulate realistic LiDAR imaging.

## Results

On the NuScenes benchmark, UniScene achieves the best video generation quality with FID 6.45 and FVD 71.94 using generated occupancy (and FID 6.12 / FVD 70.52 with ground-truth occupancy), outperforming the closest competitor Vista* (FID 13.97, FVD 112.65) by over 50% in FID. For LiDAR generation, UniScene attains MMD 2.40 and JSD 0.108 with generated occupancy (MMD 1.53, JSD 0.072 with GT occupancy), surpassing LiDARDM (MMD 3.51, JSD 0.118) by 31.6% in MMD while running 96× faster (0.47 s vs 45.12 s). For occupancy generation, UniScene achieves mIoU 19.44 and F3D 158.55, beating OccWorld (mIoU 17.13, F3D 145.65) despite using fewer reference frames (2 vs 5). Downstream, augmented data from UniScene improves CONet occupancy prediction by +3.7 mIoU (camera-only), +3.5 mIoU (LiDAR-only), and +3.8 mIoU (camera+LiDAR), and boosts BEVFusion 3D detection by +3.62 mAP / +3.41 NDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniScene outperforms all 14 baselines across every generation task it addresses. In video generation, it beats the strongest multi-view competitor Vista* (FID 13.97, FVD 112.65) by 53.7% in FID and 36.1% in FVD, and surpasses MagicDrive (FID 16.20) and Drive-WM (FID 15.80, FVD 122.70) by wide margins; single-view methods like Vista (FID 6.90, FVD 89.40) are also outperformed. In LiDAR generation, UniScene (MMD 2.40, JSD 0.108) beats LiDARDM (MMD 3.51, JSD 0.118) by 31.6% in MMD and is 96× faster (0.47 s vs 45.12 s), while Open3D ray-casting (MMD 8.15) is far inferior in both quality and realism. For occupancy generation, UniScene (mIoU 19.44, F3D 158.55) surpasses OccWorld (mIoU 17.13, F3D 145.65) using only 2 reference frames versus OccWorld's 5, and its VAE reconstruction (mIoU 92.1 at ratio 32) dramatically exceeds VQVAE-based methods: OccLLama (75.2), OccWorld (65.7), and OccSora (27.4). In downstream tasks, UniScene-augmented training improves CONet occupancy prediction by +3.7 mIoU (camera) and +3.8 mIoU (camera+LiDAR) over the baseline, and BEVFusion 3D detection by +3.62 mAP / +3.41 NDS, outperforming augmentation with MagicDrive (+1.1 mIoU / +2.52 mAP) and Vista* (+0.3 mIoU / +1.16 mAP).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniScene builds on Stable Video Diffusion as its video generation backbone, ControlNet-style conditioning for occupancy-rendered maps, and the DiT architecture for occupancy generation, while drawing on OccWorld's BEV-compressed occupancy representation and 3D Gaussian Splatting for rendering. Its key departure from prior driving scene generation work (BEVGen, MagicDrive, DriveDreamer, LiDARDM, OccSora) is the occupancy-centric hierarchical decomposition: rather than mapping coarse BEV layouts directly to a single output modality, it first generates semantic occupancy as a rich intermediate representation, then conditions video and LiDAR generation on it. This positions the paper at the intersection of volumetric scene generation, neural rendering, and multi-modal sensor simulation, unifying threads that were previously treated in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single occupancy intermediate can faithfully drive three distinct output modalities (occupancy, multi-view video, LiDAR), UniScene offers a practical recipe for generating annotated multi-sensor training data from editable BEV layouts, directly addressing the data-scarcity bottleneck in autonomous driving perception. The substantial downstream gains (e.g., +8.5 IoU on occupancy prediction, +3.62 mAP on 3D detection) validate the quality of generated data. Open questions include whether the hierarchical error propagation (occupancy errors cascading into video/LiDAR) limits scalability to more complex or longer-horizon scenes, whether the approach generalizes beyond the NuScenes benchmark, and whether the Gaussian Splatting rendering step can be made differentiable for end-to-end optimization.
