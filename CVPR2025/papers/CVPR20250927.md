## Problem

Existing 3D fluid reconstruction methods require multi-view synchronized videos, which are impractical to capture in real-world settings. This paper addresses the novel and ill-posed problem of reconstructing and predicting 3D fluid velocity and appearance from a single video, where infinitely many 3D fluid states could correspond to the observed 2D frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250927/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single fixed-viewpoint fluid video (T frames at H×W×3, e.g., 180 frames at 30 FPS) and produces a 4D fluid representation: physical particle positions and velocities (encoding the velocity and density fields) plus visual Gaussian particles (position, color, scale, opacity, orientation) at each timestep. It additionally outputs synthesized novel-view videos (C views at 720×480), future fluid predictions beyond the observed window, and interaction simulations with external forces or inserted objects. The frame-wise synthesis operates at 256×256 before video refinement upscales to 720×480.

## Method

FluidNexus bridges video generation and physics simulation through two components. First, a novel-view video synthesizer uses a camera-conditioned Zero123 diffusion model for frame-wise view synthesis, followed by a video diffusion refiner (CogVideoX-5b fine-tuned with LoRA) that extends SDEdit to video to enforce temporal coherence; long sequences are handled by an unconditional refiner for the first segment and a conditioned refiner for recursive extension. Second, a two-layer particle representation couples physical particles (simulated via differentiable Position-Based Fluids) with visual particles (rendered via 3D Gaussian Splatting), where the physical layer provides velocity/density fields and incompressibility constraints while the visual layer links to rendered images. Reconstruction proceeds as a sequential two-stage optimization per timestep: first solving for particle positions under physics and visual losses, then optimizing appearance attributes under visual and temporal regularization losses. For prediction, a generative simulation pipeline first runs PBF forward simulation to produce rough dynamics, renders multi-view videos, refines them with the video diffusion model, and re-applies the reconstruction algorithm to recover physically consistent future states.

## Results

On the ScalarFlow benchmark, FluidNexus achieves 32.45 PSNR for novel view synthesis, substantially outperforming PINF (22.68), HyFluid (22.23), and SpacetimeGaussians (19.85). For input-view future prediction, FluidNexus reaches 28.51 PSNR vs. HyFluid's 26.84 and PINF's 20.48. On re-simulation, FluidNexus achieves the lowest velocity field divergence (0.0126) compared to PINF (0.0297), HyFluid (0.0619), and STG (0.0973), confirming superior physical plausibility of the reconstructed velocity fields. On the new FluidNexus-Smoke and FluidNexus-Ball datasets, FluidNexus maintains a consistent ~8–10 dB PSNR advantage over all baselines across all four evaluation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FluidNexus decisively outperforms all three baselines across every task and dataset. On novel view synthesis (ScalarFlow), FluidNexus achieves 32.45 PSNR, a ~10 dB improvement over PINF (22.68) and HyFluid (22.23), which fail to disambiguate fluid thickness from multi-view inputs. For future prediction, FluidNexus leads by 1.7 dB over HyFluid (28.51 vs. 26.84) in the input view and by 6.2 dB over HyFluid (26.83 vs. 20.29) in novel views, where baselines degrade sharply due to inadequate 3D velocity fields. On re-simulation, FluidNexus achieves the lowest velocity divergence (0.0126) by a wide margin over HyFluid (0.0619) and STG (0.0973), indicating far more physically consistent reconstructed velocity fields. On the more challenging FluidNexus-Ball dataset with fluid-solid interactions, the gap widens further: FluidNexus reaches 29.89 PSNR for novel view synthesis versus 20.71 for HyFluid and 20.50 for PINF. SpacetimeGaussians, a general-purpose 4D reconstruction method without physics, consistently performs worst on novel view synthesis and re-simulation, confirming that physics-aware modeling is critical for fluid tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of multi-view fluid reconstruction (PINF, HyFluid, GlobTrans) by addressing the single-video setting, which prior methods cannot handle. Architecturally, it combines three previously separate threads: video diffusion models (CogVideoX, SDEdit) for synthesizing virtual novel views, 3D Gaussian Splatting for differentiable rendering of visual particles, and Position-Based Fluids for differentiable physics simulation of physical particles. The two-layer particle representation—coupling a PBF-based physical particle set with a 3DGS-based visual particle set—is the paper's core architectural novelty, bridging simulation and rendering in a way that prior physics-integrated NeRF or Gaussian methods (PhysGaussian, PIE-NeRF) do not achieve for fluids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: multi-view synchronized fluid capture is expensive and often unavailable in industrial monitoring, VFX, and scientific visualization, making single-video reconstruction a genuinely useful capability. The paper's key limitation is generalization—the novel-view synthesizer is fine-tuned per dataset and does not transfer to unseen backgrounds or objects, and reconstruction quality on the more challenging FluidNexus-Smoke and FluidNexus-Ball datasets remains blurry with missing fine details. Open questions include whether large-scale multi-view fluid datasets (synthetic or real) could enable a generalizable novel-view synthesizer, and whether the generative-refinement step could be replaced by more controllable 3D-aware generation as video models mature.
