## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods are overwhelmingly designed for human motion and fail to handle the vast morphological and behavioral diversity of animals, where the same textual description (e.g., 'walking') implies fundamentally different kinematics across species due to varied skeletal structures. No prior model performs purely text-driven animal motion generation without relying on human motion references or mesh-based avatars, and no large-scale dataset with textual annotations spans a broad range of animal species.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt describing the desired motion, an animal species name, and an attribute label (male, female, or juvenile) as input; these three strings are concatenated and encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 text encoder into a single conditioning vector. The output is a 3D skeletal motion sequence of 30 joints spanning 20–300 frames, represented in the T2M format with per-frame dimensionality d=359. Internally, the motion is discretized into 6 layers of RVQ tokens (codebook size 512, downsampling rate 4), so the generation pipeline first predicts discrete token sequences and then decodes them back to continuous joint positions.

## Method

AniMo uses a two-stage pipeline. In Stage 1 (motion tokenization), a joint-aware spatiotemporal encoder reorganizes each frame into root, non-root, and foot-ground-contact components, applies a Spatial Transformer whose attention mask enforces same-limb connectivity from the skeleton tree, and then a TCN for temporal dynamics; a Species-Aware Feature Modulation (FiLM-style) module then adaptively scales the latent features using γ and β parameters derived from CLIP-encoded species and attribute strings, followed by 6-layer Residual Vector Quantization and a GAN discriminator for reconstruction fidelity. In Stage 2 (text-to-motion generation), a Base Transformer uses masked modeling to predict the first-layer motion tokens conditioned on the CLIP text vector, while a Residual Transformer autoregressively predicts tokens for layers 2–6 conditioned on previously predicted tokens and the layer index. The key architectural distinction from prior masked-modeling methods (MoMask, MMM) is the explicit skeleton-aware spatial attention and the species-conditioned feature modulation, which together allow a single model to accommodate quadrupeds, birds, and other morphologies without per-species architectures.

## Results

On the AniMo4D test set, AniMo achieves an FID of 0.029 (vs. 0.143 for the best baseline MoMask) and R-Precision Top-1 of 0.774 (vs. 0.708 for MoMask), demonstrating substantially more realistic and text-aligned animal motions. On the out-of-distribution AnimalML3D dataset, AniMo maintains its lead with FID 2.710 and R-Precision Top-1 0.144, outperforming all six human-motion baselines despite the distributional shift. AniMo also achieves the highest Diversity score (4.664) on AniMo4D, though its MModality (0.368) is slightly below MoMask (0.415), reflecting a trade-off favoring text adherence over extreme per-prompt diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AniMo outperforms all six baselines on FID, R-Precision (Top-1/2/3), and MM-Dist on both AniMo4D and AnimalML3D. The closest competitor is MoMask [12], which shares the same two-stage RVQ + masked-modeling architecture; AniMo improves FID from 0.143 to 0.029 (a 5× reduction) and R-Precision Top-1 from 0.708 to 0.774 on AniMo4D, and from 2.887 to 2.710 FID on the OOD AnimalML3D set. Among diffusion-based baselines, MDM [47] performs worst (FID 1.017 on AniMo4D, 3.134 on AnimalML3D), while T2M [11] and T2M-GPT [54] occupy the middle range. AniMo's only metric where it trails a baseline is MModality: MoMask achieves 0.415 vs. AniMo's 0.368 on AniMo4D, and 1.062 vs. 0.947 on AnimalML3D, suggesting AniMo generates slightly less diverse motions per single prompt but compensates with stronger text-motion alignment. On the OOD AnimalML3D set, all methods degrade substantially (e.g., R-Precision Top-1 drops from ~0.7–0.8 to ~0.1–0.15), yet AniMo retains the best scores across FID, R-Precision, and MM-Dist, confirming that the species-aware design generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

AniMo extends the masked-modeling paradigm for text-to-motion generation (MoMask, MMM) from the human domain to animals, replacing the standard TCN encoder with a joint-aware spatiotemporal encoder (spatial transformer with skeleton-constrained attention + TCN) and adding FiLM-based species-aware feature modulation to handle morphological diversity. In the animal-motion subfield, it departs from OmniMotionGPT, which requires human motion references at inference, and from Motion Avatar, which targets mesh avatars rather than skeletal motion sequences, by being purely text-driven and explicitly conditioning on species and attributes. The AniMo4D dataset follows the synthetic-data pipeline of DeformingThings4D (Blender/Mixamo) but scales it to 114 species via Planet Zoo, with LLM-generated text annotations replacing the manual or absent descriptions in prior animal datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AniMo provides the first large-scale, purely text-driven animal motion generation pipeline and a substantially larger dataset (78K motions, 114 species) than prior animal motion resources, opening the door to applications in wildlife simulation, biomechanics, and animation. However, the data originates from a simulation game rather than real motion capture, and the OOD evaluation on AnimalML3D shows severe performance degradation (R-Precision Top-1 drops from 0.774 to 0.144), raising open questions about whether game-derived kinematics faithfully represent real animal behavior and whether the 30-joint unified skeleton adequately captures species-specific dynamics. Future work should validate on real animal motion capture data and explore whether the species-aware modulation generalizes to truly unseen morphologies beyond the CLIP text-embedding space.
