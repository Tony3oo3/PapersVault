## Problem

Depth completion models trained solely on sparse LiDAR ground truth (covering only ~5–20% of pixels in outdoor scenes) produce incomplete and fragmented depth predictions, failing to capture fine-grained geometric details. While monocular foundation models can generate dense depth maps with rich structural information, their inherent scale ambiguity prevents direct use as metric supervision for real-world depth completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251770/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a sparse LiDAR depth map (covering ~5% of pixels on KITTI) as input and outputs a dense depth map at full image resolution. The base architecture is BP-Net, a ResNet-based bilateral propagation network. During training, the method additionally consumes dense monocular depth predictions from Depth Anything V2 as a distillation teacher signal, and in the first stage, simulated sparse LiDAR scans generated via ray-casting over reconstructed meshes serve as the sparse input.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage knowledge distillation framework. In the first stage, ~360K unlabeled natural images are processed by Depth Anything V2 to obtain dense pseudo-depth; each image is back-projected to a 3D point cloud using randomly sampled camera intrinsics, a mesh is reconstructed via Poisson Surface Reconstruction, and LiDAR scans are simulated by ray-casting from the camera origin to produce synthetic sparse depth inputs. The depth completion network is then pre-trained from scratch with L1 loss against the synthesized dense depth, learning diverse geometric features without any ground-truth depth. In the second stage, the pre-trained model is fine-tuned on labeled datasets (KITTI, NYUv2) using a combined objective: standard L1 loss on sparse ground truth to anchor real-world metric scale, a Scale- and Shift-Invariant (SSI) Loss that minimizes |D_f − (s·D_m + b)| over optimal scale s and shift b to align predictions with dense monocular depth while ignoring its scale ambiguity, and a multi-scale gradient matching regularization to preserve depth discontinuities.

## Results

On the KITTI depth completion benchmark (ranked by RMSE), DMD3C achieves 678.12 mm RMSE, 194.46 mm MAE, 1.82 iRMSE, and 0.85 iMAE, ranking 1st on the official leaderboard. On NYU Depth V2, it achieves 0.085 RMSE, 0.011 REL, and 99.7% δ₁.₂₅, the best RMSE and δ₁.₂₅ among all compared methods. In zero-shot generalization tests on ScanNet, DDAD, and VOID1500, DMD3C consistently achieves the lowest RMSE and MAE, e.g., reducing DDAD RMSE from 8.903 (BP-Net) to 7.766.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMD3C achieves the best KITTI RMSE (678.12 mm), beating the previous best BP-Net (684.90) by 6.78 mm and TPVD (693.97) by 15.85 mm, securing 1st place on the official leaderboard. However, it trails TPVD (188.60), ImprovingDC (187.95), and BEV@DC (189.44) on KITTI MAE by roughly 5–6 mm, and is slightly behind TPVD and LRRU on iMAE (0.85 vs. 0.81). On NYU Depth V2, DMD3C achieves the lowest RMSE (0.085) and ties TPVD on δ₁.₂₅ (99.7%), while TPVD holds a marginal REL advantage (0.010 vs. 0.011). In zero-shot out-of-domain evaluation (ScanNet, DDAD, VOID1500), DMD3C outperforms BP-Net, LRRU, and CFormer on all six RMSE/MAE metrics, with the largest gain on DDAD where RMSE drops from 8.903 (BP-Net) to 7.766. The method also consistently improves upon its own base architecture BP-Net across all KITTI metrics when the two-stage distillation is applied (Table 4), confirming the contribution of the training strategy rather than the network design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the well-established depth completion literature (CSPN, NLSPN, BP-Net, etc.) but shifts the focus from architecture design to training strategy. It builds directly on Depth Anything V2 as a monocular foundation model providing dense geometric supervision, and adapts the scale- and shift-invariant loss formulation from Ranftl et al. (TPAMI 2022) to handle the inherent scale ambiguity of monocular predictions. The key departure from prior teacher-student or multi-frame-fusion approaches is the use of a synthetic data generation pipeline (monocular depth → mesh reconstruction → ray-cast LiDAR simulation) to create training pairs without any ground-truth depth, enabling large-scale pre-training on unlabeled natural images before fine-tuning on sparse real-world annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture-agnostic two-stage distillation framework offers a practical recipe that can be plugged into existing depth completion backbones (demonstrated on BP-Net, LRRU, and CFormer), lowering the barrier for improving fine-grained depth in sparse-annotation regimes. The consistent zero-shot gains on unseen datasets (ScanNet, DDAD, VOID1500) suggest the pre-training stage imparts genuinely transferable geometric priors rather than dataset-specific shortcuts. Open questions include whether the mesh-reconstruction + ray-casting data generation pipeline can be simplified or replaced by direct monocular supervision, and whether the approach extends to other sparse-to-dense sensing tasks (e.g., sparse normal completion, sparse flow). The reliance on a specific monocular foundation model (Depth Anything V2) also raises questions about robustness if the teacher's failure modes shift.
