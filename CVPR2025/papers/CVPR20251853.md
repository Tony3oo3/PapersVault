## Problem

Sparse wearable IMU systems (6 sensors) suffer from pose ambiguity, acceleration drift, and poor generalization across body shapes, while adding UWB distance sensors introduces body-occlusion artifacts that destabilize existing EKF-based fusion. Prior methods treat sensor data as fixed inputs to a pose regressor without exploiting the reverse influence of estimated poses on sensor reliability, leaving a gap in how to jointly refine noisy measurements and uncertain pose estimates in a closed loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251853/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes six co-located IMU-UWB sensors providing 18D filtered accelerations, 54D orientations (9D rotation matrices), and 15 inter-sensor distances, plus one-time anthropometric inputs (height, weight, and 7 selected inter-distances in T-pose) for shape estimation. It outputs 96D 6D-rotation poses for 16 joints (hands and feet excluded), 10D SMPL shape parameters, and 96D per-dimension pose uncertainties. Global translation is deliberately not estimated, as body-worn sensors alone cannot bound drift without external references.

## Method

UMotion is a three-module online framework: (1) an AutoGluon ensemble shape estimator regressing SMPL β from height, weight, and 7 LOS-validated inter-distances; (2) a unidirectional LSTM pose estimator trained with MSE then GNLL loss to output 6D joint rotations and their log-variances, initialized via a CNN that regresses the RNN hidden state from initial global poses and joint positions; (3) a tightly coupled UKF state estimator tracking 15 relative positions, 15 relative velocities, and 6 acceleration biases between sensor nodes, where the measurement update fuses raw UWB distances (with LOS-dependent covariance), IMU-derived relative velocities, and pose-derived relative positions obtained by propagating pose uncertainty through the SMPL model via Van der Merwe sigma points. The key architectural insight is the bidirectional feedback: pose uncertainties act as observations that correct UWB and IMU measurements, and the refined sensor data in turn improves the next pose estimate, creating a self-stabilizing loop that no prior IMU-only or IMU+UWB method implements.

## Results

On the fair distance-augmented comparison (Table 2), UMotion achieves the lowest positional error across all three test datasets: 4.46 cm on TotalCapture (21.5% lower than UIP's 5.49 cm), 3.38 cm on DIP-IMU (35.0% lower than UIP's 5.05 cm), and 10.33 cm on the real-world UIP dataset (vs. UIP's 10.65 cm). On TotalCapture, UMotion also achieves the best SIP error (10.76°) among all distance-augmented methods. In the broader IMU-only comparison (Table 1), UMotion improves over the SOTA PNP by 32.4% in angular error (7.06° vs. 10.45°) and 9.4% in mesh error (4.94 cm vs. 5.45 cm) on TotalCapture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest prior work UIP [1], UMotion wins on positional error across all three datasets (4.46 vs. 5.49 cm on TotalCapture, 3.38 vs. 5.05 cm on DIP-IMU, 10.33 vs. 10.65 cm on UIP) but loses on SIP error on the real-world UIP dataset (25.69° vs. 24.12°). The distance-augmented variants TIP-D and PIP-D, which simply feed ideal synthetic distances into existing IMU-only pipelines, are consistently outperformed by UMotion on positional error by 19–53%, confirming that the UKF-based state estimation provides substantial benefit over naive distance concatenation. Against IMU-only SOTA PNP [37], UMotion achieves 32.4% lower angular error and 9.4% lower mesh error on TotalCapture, though PNP retains a slight edge in SIP error (10.89° vs. 10.76°). On the challenging real-world UIP dataset with low sensor quality and ambiguous motions, UMotion achieves the lowest positional error despite using less-filtered distance measurements, a simpler architecture, and non-optimal default UKF parameters, demonstrating robustness to real-world noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of sparse IMU pose estimation (SIP, DIP, TransPose, TIP, PIP, PNP) and UWB-augmented tracking (SmartPoser, UIP), but departs from both by treating human body shape as a first-class system variable rather than a fixed or mean-shape assumption. Where UIP uses an EKF to fuse IMU and UWB for distance tracking, UMotion introduces a tightly coupled UKF that also ingests pose-estimator outputs and their uncertainties as observations, creating a bidirectional feedback loop between sensor measurements and kinematic constraints. The ensemble-learning shape estimator (AutoGluon) and the direct LSTM pose estimator without intermediate joint-position layers represent a deliberate simplification relative to the multi-stage architectures common in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path toward robust, real-time full-body motion capture from just six body-worn sensors without external infrastructure, addressing the drift and ambiguity problems that limit sparse IMU systems in everyday settings. Key limitations remain: the shape estimator requires retraining for different sensor configurations, the UKF parameters must be tuned per hardware, and the pose estimator's simplified constraints may cap performance on highly dynamic motions. Open questions include whether a physics-aware module (suggested by the authors) could close the remaining gap on ambiguous joints, and whether the uncertainty-driven fusion strategy generalizes to other sensor modalities or larger sensor arrays.
