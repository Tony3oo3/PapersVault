## Problem

Existing NeRF frameworks struggle to simultaneously capture both the overall low-frequency scene structure and high-definition high-frequency details within a single model, as frequency variations in multi-resolution datasets can span orders of magnitude. Methods like Mip-NeRF 360 treat all pixels uniformly, while progressive or spatially partitioned approaches fail to align with the actual 3D frequency distribution, leading to either over-smoothed details or incorrect geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250408/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-resolution image set per scene: dense panoramic images at normal resolution (<2K) covering the full scene structure, plus high-resolution (up to 4K) images focused on areas of interest, with camera poses recovered via SfM. A sparse point cloud provides initial depth and spatial occupancy. The output is photorealistic novel view rendering at both the structure-view and detail-view resolutions, achieving 20 FPS on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Method

FA-NeRF introduces a patch-based 3D frequency quantification that progressively increases NeRF's encoded frequency until each image patch meets an SSIM threshold, then projects the resulting 2D frequency into 3D space via focal length and depth to build a frequency voxel grid. During training, hash-grid features at each resolution level are re-weighted by an erf-based downweighting factor derived from the local frequency, preventing high-frequency components from being wasted on low-frequency content. A frequency-averaged sampling strategy allocates more training pixels to high-frequency patches, and an adaptive ray marching scheme sets the sampling interval to 2f (Nyquist) based on the local frequency value, eliminating manual tuning. The entire pipeline is built on Instant-NGP's multi-resolution hash encoding with a small MLP decoder for density and color.

## Results

On the Multi-frequency Dataset, FA-NeRF achieves PSNR 32.44 / SSIM 0.929 / LPIPS 0.148 on structure views (600×600) and PSNR 26.29 / SSIM 0.843 / LPIPS 0.332 on detail views (4032×3024), outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On the standard MipNeRF-360 dataset, it reaches PSNR 31.20 / SSIM 0.931 / LPIPS 0.165, and on Tanks & Temples it achieves PSNR 24.45 / SSIM 0.821 / LPIPS 0.205. The method maintains 20 FPS rendering on a single RTX 4090 GPU even for high-frequency detail views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FA-NeRF decisively outperforms all five baselines on the Multi-frequency Dataset, gaining +1.47 dB PSNR over the strongest baseline (iNGP-Big, 30.97) on structure views and +2.00 dB over 3D-GS (24.29) on detail views, with the largest gap in LPIPS (0.148 vs. 0.183 for iNGP-Big). On the standard MipNeRF-360 dataset, FA-NeRF slightly trails Mip-NeRF360 in PSNR (31.20 vs. 31.49) but leads in SSIM (0.931 vs. 0.916) and LPIPS (0.165 vs. 0.179), indicating better perceptual quality despite marginally lower pixel-level fidelity. On Tanks & Temples, FA-NeRF edges out 3D-GS in PSNR (24.45 vs. 24.36) and LPIPS (0.205 vs. 0.213) but falls slightly behind in SSIM (0.821 vs. 0.831). TensoRF is consistently the weakest baseline across all datasets, with the lowest PSNR on every evaluation, attributed to its compression of high-frequency spatial information. The paper also qualitatively compares against BungeeNeRF (adapted), showing that its divide-and-conquer multi-scale strategy performs similarly to its Mip-NeRF baseline in multi-frequency scenarios, confirming that spatial partitioning alone cannot capture the true frequency distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

FA-NeRF extends Instant-NGP's multi-resolution hash encoding by replacing uniform feature concatenation with a frequency-aware re-weighting scheme, and builds on the NeRF volume-rendering pipeline. It departs from Mip-NeRF 360's cone-based anti-aliasing (which treats all pixels uniformly) and from BungeeNeRF/Strata's progressive frequency unlocking (which lacks explicit 3D frequency distribution) by introducing a patch-based 3D frequency quantification that maps 2D image frequency to 3D scene frequency via focal length and depth. The method also introduces a Multi-frequency Dataset combining panoramic and 4K close-up captures, a data regime not previously benchmarked in the NeRF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency grid and its associated re-weighting, sampling, and ray-marching adjustments offer a practical recipe for grid-based NeRF practitioners handling scenes with extreme frequency spans (e.g., architectural walkthroughs with fine textures). The Multi-frequency Dataset provides a useful benchmark for this underexplored regime. However, the approach remains tied to hash-grid architectures and 20 FPS rendering on a single GPU, leaving open how well the frequency quantification scales to very large or dynamic scenes, and whether the 2D-to-3D frequency projection remains reliable under sparse or inconsistent multi-view coverage.
