## Problem

Existing bimanual manipulation policies that predict next-best end-effector poses (NBP) in Cartesian space ignore the robot's physical structure (kinematic chain, joint limits, link geometry) and kinematic constraints, leading to self-collisions between arms and kinematically infeasible joint configurations during execution. These structural and kinematic properties are typically treated as post-processing via inverse kinematics rather than being integrated into the policy learning objective itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252780/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a language instruction, multiview RGB-D observation images (with n=2 historical frames for motion context), and the robot's URDF file specifying joint types, limits, and link lengths. It outputs a sequence of m=2 bimanual 6D end-effector poses in SE(3) at keyframe indices (selected via keyframe discovery rather than all timesteps), along with joint configurations and gripper open/close actions. The URDF is parsed offline to construct a static spatial graph that is then extended temporally at each inference step.

## Method

KStar Diffuser augments a standard diffusion policy backbone (Transformer encoders with FiLM conditioning) with two structural modules. First, a spatial-temporal robot graph is built from the URDF: nodes encode joint Cartesian coordinates, pairwise Euclidean distances, and arm-identity one-hot labels, while edges capture both spatial link connections and temporal correlations of the same joint across T historical timesteps; a GCN encodes this graph into a structural feature vector that conditions the denoising process. Second, a kinematics regularizer predicts joint configurations from the fused backbone and graph features, then applies differentiable forward kinematics (DFK) to produce a reference end-effector pose that further conditions the diffusion head, ensuring generated poses remain within the IK-solvable space. Training minimizes a weighted combination of end-effector pose MSE (diffusion denoising loss) and joint-space MSE, with the DFK module enabling gradient flow from pose errors back through the kinematic chain.

## Results

On the RLBench2 bimanual benchmark with 100 training demonstrations, KStar Diffuser achieves an overall success rate of 68.2±2.1%, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline PerAct2 (33.9±1.0%) by over 34 percentage points. With only 20 demonstrations, KStar Diffuser still reaches 58.0±1.4% overall, compared to 24.9±2.1% for the best baseline. On real-world tasks (Lift Plate and Handover), KStar Diffuser achieves 43.1% overall success, surpassing PerAct2 (29.9%) by more than 13 percentage points, while single-arm-adapted methods (ACT, DP, DP3) all fail on the Handover task entirely (0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KStar Diffuser decisively outperforms all four baseline families across both simulation and real-world settings. Against the strongest bimanual-specific baseline PerAct2 [17], KStar Diffuser gains +34.3 points overall (68.2% vs 33.9%) with 100 demos and +33.1 points (58.0% vs 24.9%) with 20 demos; the gap is especially large on coordination-heavy tasks like Lift Ball (+51.0 pts) and Handover (+16.7 pts). Among diffusion-based baselines, DP-EE [9] is the closest competitor at 40.5% overall (100 demos), but KStar Diffuser still leads by +27.7 points, with the largest margin on Pick Laptop (+33.4 pts) where dual-arm coordination is critical. Transformer-based baselines (ACT [58], RVT-LF, PerAct-LF) and DP3 [56] all struggle with bimanual coordination, achieving 0% on Handover and near-0% on Pick Laptop with 20 demos. In real-world evaluation, KStar Diffuser (43.1% overall) beats PerAct2 (29.9%) by +13.2 points, while ACT, DP, and DP3 all score 0% on the Handover task, confirming that single-arm-adapted policies cannot handle dual-arm transfer without structural and kinematic awareness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) from single-arm to bimanual manipulation by introducing two structural innovations: a spatial-temporal graph (encoded via GCN) that models the robot's physical architecture from URDF specifications, and a differentiable forward kinematics regularizer that conditions the denoising process on joint-space predictions. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (predict end-effector poses, then solve IK as post-processing) by integrating kinematic constraints directly into the learning objective, arguing that this 'disjunction' causes infeasible trajectories. The evaluation builds on the RLBench2 benchmark and keyframe discovery methodology from PerAct2 (Grotz et al., 2024), positioning KStar Diffuser as a kinematics-aware alternative to existing bimanual frameworks that treat robot structure as a low-dimensional proprioceptive vector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical failure mode in bimanual manipulation—predicted poses that violate joint limits or cause inter-arm collisions—that is underexplored in the diffusion-policy literature. The spatial-temporal graph encoding of URDF structure is a reusable technique that could transfer to other robot morphologies, and the differentiable FK regularizer offers a general recipe for making any pose-prediction policy kinematically aware. However, real-world success rates remain moderate (43.1% overall), the graph is static per robot (limiting cross-embodiment generalization), and the evaluation covers only five simulated and two real tasks, leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex multi-object or long-horizon bimanual tasks.
