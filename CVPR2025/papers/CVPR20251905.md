## Problem

Existing multi-view graph clustering methods assume node attributes are fully available across all views, yet in practice attributes are frequently missing due to privacy constraints or data collection failures. Prior approaches to missing attributes are either designed for single-view settings, not tailored to clustering objectives, or ignore the relational dependencies between nodes and their neighbors, resulting in unreliable imputations that degrade clustering performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251905/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-view attributed graph where some nodes have entirely missing attribute vectors (not partially missing), along with the shared adjacency matrix and binary missing-feature masks. The second view is synthetically constructed by adding Gaussian noise (σ=0.01) to the first view. Output is a set of K cluster assignments obtained by applying K-Means to the fused node embedding matrix Z, which is produced by adaptively weighting per-view MLP representations. Experiments evaluate missing rates of 0.3, 0.6, and 0.9 across four citation/social-network datasets.

## Method

AMMGC comprises three modules operating in sequence. First, a context-aware imputation module iteratively minimizes the Dirichlet energy via an Euler-scheme update that propagates neighbor information through the normalized adjacency matrix while preserving observed features via the mask, over T iterations. Second, a dual structure consistency module applies a Laplacian filter (I − kL)^t to smooth the imputed attributes, passes them through shared-parameter MLPs to obtain per-view representations, fuses them with learnable adaptive weights, and enforces alignment between cross-view cosine similarity matrices and the adjacency matrix to reduce redundancy. Third, a high-confidence guidance module generates K-Means pseudo-labels, retains only samples close to their centroids, and trains the network by minimizing cross-entropy between Student's t-distribution soft assignments and these reliable pseudo-labels. The total loss combines the structure consistency term and the guidance term with a trade-off parameter α, and final clustering is obtained by NMF factorization of the fused embedding.

## Results

AMMGC achieves the best clustering performance across all four datasets (CORA, CITESEER, AMAC, WIKI) at the most challenging 0.9 missing rate, with ACC of 68.94%, 55.25%, 53.38%, and 38.08% respectively. The advantage is most pronounced at high missing rates: on CORA at 0.9 missing rate, AMMGC surpasses the second-best method (CONVERT) by 18.48% in ACC, 18.49% in NMI, and 25.38% in ARI. At the lower 0.3 missing rate, AMMGC remains competitive (e.g., 73.64% ACC on CORA vs. 73.49% for HSAN) while maintaining far greater stability as missingness increases. On AMAC, AMMGC's accuracy drops only 2.66% as the missing rate goes from 0.3 to 0.9, whereas baselines like SAT drop by over 26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMMGC outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset at the 0.9 missing rate, with the largest margins against CONVERT (the strongest baseline): +18.48% ACC on CORA (68.94 vs. 50.46), +8.18% on CITESEER (55.25 vs. 47.07), and +15.88% on AMAC (53.38 vs. 37.50). At the 0.3 missing rate, AMMGC is competitive but not always the absolute best — on CORA it edges out HSAN by only 0.15% ACC (73.64 vs. 73.49) — yet its advantage grows dramatically as missingness increases, while baselines like CCGC and SCGC collapse (e.g., CCGC drops from 71.95% to 33.21% ACC on CORA). HSAN fails entirely on AMAC due to out-of-memory errors at all missing rates. The single-view imputation methods (SAT, FPGM, T2-GNN) are consistently the weakest, with SAT dropping to 33.23% ACC on CORA and 21.89% on CITESEER at 0.9 missing rate, confirming that methods not designed for multi-view clustering struggle under severe attribute absence.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMMGC sits at the intersection of two established research threads: multi-view graph clustering (MVGC) and graph learning with missing node attributes. It repurposes the Dirichlet-energy-based feature propagation idea from FPGM (Rossi et al., 2022) for iterative imputation, borrows the Laplacian-filter smoothing and high-confidence pseudo-label guidance from CCGC (Yang et al., 2023), and introduces a dual structure consistency loss that aligns cross-view similarity matrices with the adjacency matrix. The key departure is that prior missing-attribute methods (SAT, T2-GNN, ITR) are designed for single-view settings or generic graph tasks, while prior MVGC methods assume complete attributes; AMMGC is the first to explicitly combine both challenges in a unified clustering framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real-world multi-view graphs (social networks, citation networks, biological networks) frequently suffer from missing attributes due to privacy constraints or sensor failures, and no prior method was purpose-built for this setting. However, a significant limitation is that the 'multi-view' data is artificially constructed by adding Gaussian noise to a single view (Eq. 1), which means the method has not been validated on genuinely distinct multi-view graph data. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than two views, to larger graphs beyond the ~14K-node AMAC dataset, and whether the iterative imputation converges reliably under extreme sparsity or disconnected subgraphs.
