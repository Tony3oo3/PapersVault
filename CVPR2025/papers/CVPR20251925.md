## Problem

Point tracking methods typically rely on simple backbones (e.g., ResNet) trained from scratch on synthetic data, missing the robustness of large-scale pre-trained representations, and inject temporal awareness only through expensive per-query iterative refinement stages. This paper asks whether a temporally-aware pre-trained feature backbone can produce accurate tracks directly, eliminating the need for a separate refinement pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251925/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a video sequence of T RGB frames (H×W×3) and a query point (x_q, y_q, t_q) at frame t_q, the method outputs a 2D position estimate for the point in every frame plus a visibility probability. Notably, point prediction is entirely non-parametric: after feature extraction, positions are obtained by computing cosine-similarity correlation maps between the query feature and each frame's feature map, followed by a masked soft-argmax (temperature τ=20, local window M=5 px) with no learnable layers. The temporal adapter operates over a local window of N=13 frames at each spatial location.

## Method

Chrono builds on DINOv2 (ViT-S/14 or ViT-B/14) by inserting a temporal adapter between every transformer block. Each adapter follows a bottleneck design: a 2D convolution downsamples spatial dimensions by stride 4, a 1D local attention layer computes softmax-weighted aggregation over 13 neighboring frames at each spatial location, a second 2D convolution restores spatial resolution, and a residual connection preserves the original DINOv2 features. Training uses Huber loss on soft-argmax positions, supervised on the Kubric Panning-MOVi-E synthetic dataset with 256 query points per batch. The key insight is that 1D attention over a 13-frame window (6× longer than TSM-ResNet's adjacent-frame context) captures long-range motion dynamics directly in feature space, yielding tracks that are as accurate as refiner-based pipelines (e.g., TAPIR, LocoTrack) at 12.5–16.4× higher throughput, and can optionally be combined with an external refiner for further gains.

## Results

On the TAP-Vid benchmark (strided query mode), Chrono (ViT-B/14) achieves average position accuracy of 86.0 on RGB-Stacking, 68.5 on Kinetics, and 70.1 on DAVIS, outperforming all compared backbones. The smaller Chrono (ViT-S/14) variant reaches 84.3, 66.8, and 68.0 respectively. On DAVIS, Chrono (ViT-B/14) improves over TSM-ResNet-18 by +20.9%p in average position accuracy (70.1 vs. 49.2), and at the strictest threshold (<δ⁰) it reaches 31.8% versus 8.2% for TSM-ResNet-18. In throughput, Chrono (ViT-B/14) processes 26,140 points/sec with zero refiner parameters, compared to TAPIR's 2,097 points/sec with 25.9M refiner parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chrono decisively outperforms all backbone baselines across all three TAP-Vid datasets. On DAVIS (strided mode), Chrono (ViT-B/14) achieves 70.1 <δ^x_avg, beating TSM-ResNet-18 (49.2) by +20.9%p, ResNet-18 (53.3) by +16.8%p, CoTracker backbone (58.0) by +12.1%p, and DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (54.4) by +15.7%p. The gap is even more dramatic at the strictest threshold: Chrono reaches 31.8% at <δ⁰ on DAVIS versus 8.2% for TSM-ResNet-18 and 10.0% for DINOv2. Against full pipelines with iterative refiners (Table 3), Chrono (ViT-B/14) achieves 70.1 on DAVIS versus TAPIR's 73.6, while running 12.5× faster (26,140 vs. 2,097 points/sec) and using zero refiner parameters. When plugged into LocoTrack's refiner, Chrono further boosts performance to 91.0 AJ on RGB-Stacking (+6.1 over LocoTrack's 86.9) and 80.2 <δ^x_avg on DAVIS, surpassing FlowTrack (79.8) and CoTracker (79.4). DINOv2 without temporal adapters performs poorly for tracking (45.3–54.4 <δ^x_avg), confirming that spatial features alone are insufficient and that the temporal adapter is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DINOv2, a large-scale self-supervised vision transformer, by inserting lightweight temporal adapters (2D conv + 1D local attention) between its transformer blocks to create a temporally-aware feature backbone for point tracking. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm in point tracking (TAPIR, PIPs, CoTracker) where a simple backbone produces coarse predictions and a heavy iterative refiner injects temporal information post-hoc; instead, Chrono embeds temporal context directly into the feature extraction process, making the refiner optional. The work also contrasts with earlier attempts to use TSM-ResNet in point tracking, which provided only adjacent-frame context and was trained from scratch on small synthetic data, by leveraging a much longer temporal window (13 frames vs. 2) on top of a backbone pre-trained on large-scale real-world data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a non-parametric feature matching step (cosine similarity + soft argmax) on temporally-aware DINOv2 features can match or exceed full pipelines with 25M+ parameter iterative refiners while achieving 12–16× higher throughput, suggesting that much of the computational burden in current point tracking is spent correcting errors that a better backbone could avoid. This reframes the design question for point tracking from 'how to refine tracks' to 'how to build temporally-aware features,' potentially simplifying pipelines for robotics and video editing applications. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer sequences beyond the 13-frame window, whether it handles occlusion prediction as well as dedicated refiners (the paper focuses on position accuracy), and whether the temporal adapter design transfers to other pre-trained backbones or video tasks beyond point tracking.
