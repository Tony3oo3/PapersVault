## Problem

Existing training-free text-to-image methods have made progress on missing objects and mismatched attributes, but the problem of mislocated objects—where generated spatial positions fail to align with textual cues like 'to the left of' or 'above'—remains largely unaddressed. Stable Diffusion consistently produces similar images regardless of positional changes in the prompt, indicating a fundamental lack of spatial understanding. Prior solutions for spatial control rely on fixed templates or predefined layouts, which impose rigid constraints and require additional inputs, limiting flexibility for relative positioning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt describing objects with relative spatial relationships (e.g., 'a car to the left of an elephant') and produces a standard RGB image, requiring no additional layout inputs, bounding boxes, or spatial templates. It operates on Stable Diffusion v1.4 and v2.1 using 16×16 cross-attention maps (the resolution with the most semantically meaningful information), with a Gaussian smoothing filter (kernel size 3, σ=0.5) applied to attention maps. The latent vector is updated at specific early denoising timesteps (t ∈ {5, 10, 15, 20}) out of 50 total steps, with a guidance scale of 7.5.

## Method

STORM introduces a Spatial Transport Optimization (STO) framework that treats each text token's cross-attention map as a probability distribution and uses the Sinkhorn algorithm to compute an optimal transport plan that repositions the source distribution relative to a reference object's centroid. The key novelty is the Spatial Transport Cost (ST Cost) function, which combines a positional cost—using directional δ values with a progressive adaptive weight ω(·) that increases alignment importance across timesteps—and a non-overlap cost that embeds the reference distribution directly into the cost matrix to prevent object overlap. The transport plan is applied bidirectionally for both objects in a pair, and the resulting OT loss is backpropagated to update the latent vector via gradient descent (z'_t ← z_t − α_t · ∇_{z_t} L). The framework also handles non-spatial prompts by randomly placing target distributions and uses standard Euclidean OT cost for attribute-object attention alignment, all without modifying model weights.

## Results

On the VISOR benchmark with Stable Diffusion 1.4, STORM achieves 61.01% Object Accuracy (OA) and 57.58% VISOR-uncond spatial accuracy, compared to 29.86% OA and 18.81% VISOR-uncond for the SD1.4 baseline. The VISOR-4 metric (all four seeds spatially correct) jumps from 1.63% (SD1.4) to 25.70% (STORM), a ~16× improvement. On SD2.1, STORM reaches 62.55% OA and 59.35% VISOR-uncond, outperforming the SD2.1 baseline (47.83% OA, 30.25% VISOR-uncond) and the best training-free competitor CONFORM (60.73% OA, 38.48% VISOR-uncond on SD1.4). On T2I-CompBench, STORM improves spatial relationship scores from 0.1246 to 0.1613 (SD1.4) and from 0.1342 to 0.1981 (SD2.1), while also boosting attribute binding (e.g., color from 0.3765 to 0.6458 on SD1.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods on SD1.4, STORM (61.01% OA, 57.58% VISOR-uncond) outperforms CONFORM (60.73% OA, 38.48% VISOR-uncond) by ~19 points on VISOR-uncond and INITNO (60.40% OA, 35.18% VISOR-uncond) by ~22 points, while also surpassing Divide-and-Bind (46.03% OA) and Attend-and-Excite (42.07% OA) by large margins. On the VISOR-4 metric (all four seeds spatially correct), STORM achieves 25.70% versus 9.52% for CONFORM and 6.72% for INITNO, indicating substantially more consistent spatial accuracy. Methods requiring extra inputs (REVISION at 52.71% VISOR-uncond, Layout Guidance at 38.80%) are also outperformed by STORM without any additional layout or segmentation input. On SD2.1, STORM (62.55% OA, 59.35% VISOR-uncond) beats SPRIGHT (60.68% OA, 42.23% VISOR-uncond, which requires extra training) and REVISION (48.26% OA, 47.11% VISOR-uncond, which requires extra input). In the user study, STORM's spatial correctness score of 35.92% more than doubles the best competitor (Attend-and-Excite at 13.20%), and overall fidelity (22.65%) exceeds all baselines by 5–8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

STORM builds directly on Stable Diffusion's cross-attention mechanism and the Sinkhorn algorithm for optimal transport, repurposing OT from its traditional role in distribution matching to the novel task of repositioning attention maps according to textual spatial cues. It departs from prior training-free T2I methods (Attend-and-Excite, Divide-and-Bind, CONFORM, INITNO) that focus on reducing attention overlap to fix missing objects and attribute binding, by introducing a directional Spatial Transport Cost that encodes relative positioning (left/right/above/below) rather than merely preventing overlap. Unlike layout-based approaches (Layout Guidance, ZestGuide) that require fixed spatial templates or external layout inputs, STORM derives spatial guidance purely from the text prompt's relational language, making it template-free and more flexible for dynamic applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a gap that prior training-free T2I work has largely ignored: while missing objects and attribute binding received substantial attention, relative spatial positioning remained poorly controlled, with SD generating nearly identical images regardless of spatial cues. The finding that spatial configuration must be established in early denoising timesteps (with later steps refining details) provides a practical principle for where to intervene in diffusion pipelines. Limitations include restriction to basic four-directional spatial relationships, dependence on 16×16 attention maps, and evaluation primarily on two SD versions; open questions include whether the OT-based repositioning generalizes to more complex spatial descriptions (e.g., 'between', 'behind', multi-object arrangements) and whether the approach transfers to newer architectures like SDXL or DiT-based models.
