## Problem

Existing one-stage weakly supervised REC methods, such as RefCLIP, rely on a single visual encoder (e.g., YOLOv3/DarkNet) pre-trained on limited categories, which severely limits fine-grained object recognition on attribute-rich or long expressions. While mixing multiple pre-trained visual encoders (CLIP, DINOv2, SAM) can provide complementary benefits, naively combining all of them degrades inference speed and does not always improve accuracy. The paper addresses how to dynamically and sparsely select the most relevant visual features per sample to maximize visual descriptive power while maintaining efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 416×416 RGB image and a natural language referring expression (max length 15 for RefCOCO/RefCOCO+, 20 for RefCOCOg) as input. For REC, it outputs a bounding box decoded from the selected anchor via the YOLOv3 detection head; for the RES generalization experiment, it outputs a segmentation mask via YOLOv5-Seg. During inference, only two visual experts (always DarkNet plus one dynamically selected encoder from CLIP-ViT, DINOv2-ViT, EfficientSAM-ViT, or CLIP-ConvNext) are activated per forward pass, keeping inference at ~29.8 fps on an NVIDIA 3090.

## Method

DViN extends RefCLIP's anchor-text matching by introducing a Dynamic Visual Routing (DVR) mechanism that formulates multi-encoder feature combination as a sparse routing process. A vision-conditioned router computes routing weights from DarkNet features via a linear projection and softmax, then selects the top-1 additional visual encoder (DarkNet is always retained for training stability), yielding a weighted sum of two anchor feature sets. Training uses a diversity-aware routing loss (negative entropy of routing scores) to prevent expert collapse. On top of the standard anchor-text contrastive loss, DViN introduces Routing-based Feature Alignment (RFA): a text-guided L2 loss aligns the routed visual features with text features, and a vision-guided contrastive loss distills the combined feature representation back into the individual DarkNet feature, encouraging it to approximate the dynamic mixture. The final loss is a weighted sum of the contrastive, routing, text-guided, and vision-guided terms, optimized with Adam (lr=1e-4, batch 64) for 25 epochs with cosine decay.

## Results

On the four weakly supervised REC benchmarks, DViN achieves 70.90% (RefCOCO testA), 57.52% (RefCOCO+ testA), 55.04% (RefCOCOg val-g), and 40.63% (ReferItGame test) at IoU@0.5, setting new state-of-the-art results across all datasets. Compared to the strongest one-stage baseline RefCLIP, DViN gains +12.32% on RefCOCO testA (70.90 vs. 58.58) and +17.07% on RefCOCO+ testA (57.52 vs. 40.45), while maintaining a competitive inference speed of 29.8 fps. On weakly supervised RES, DViN reaches 63.81% mIoU on RefCOCO testA, surpassing the previous best (APL at 54.84%) by +8.97%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DViN decisively outperforms all two-stage weakly supervised baselines (ARN, DTWREG, EARN, KPRN, VC, IGN, RelR, NCE+Dist), which rely on expensive region proposal stages and achieve at most ~45% on RefCOCOg val-g versus DViN's 55.04%. Among one-stage methods, DViN surpasses RefCLIP (CVPR'23) by +12.32% on RefCOCO testA and +17.07% on RefCOCO+ testA, and outperforms APL (ECCV'24) by +8.99% on RefCOCO testA and +14.68% on RefCOCO+ testA, while also being faster (29.8 vs. 26.7 fps). In the pseudo-label setting, DViN as teacher yields substantially better student models: DViN→SimREC reaches 73.09% on RefCOCO testA versus 62.70% for RefCLIP→SimREC, and DViN→TransVG achieves 57.36% on RefCOCO+ testA versus 39.54% for RefCLIP→TransVG. On weakly supervised RES, DViN (63.81% mIoU on RefCOCO testA) outperforms all 11 baselines including PKS (52.23%) and APL (54.84%), confirming that the dynamic visual routing generalizes beyond bounding-box localization to pixel-level segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DViN builds directly on RefCLIP (CVPR 2023), which established the one-stage anchor-text matching paradigm for weakly supervised REC using YOLOv3. The key conceptual departure is reframing visual feature extraction as a sparse routing problem over a pool of pre-trained encoders (CLIP, DINOv2, SAM, EfficientSAM), borrowing the mixture-of-experts idea but applying it at the level of entire visual backbones rather than within a single network. The paper also introduces a novel weakly supervised objective (Routing-based Feature Alignment) that combines intra-modal distillation and inter-modal contrastive alignment, going beyond the anchor-text contrastive loss used in RefCLIP. This positions DViN at the intersection of MoE architectures, multi-encoder feature fusion, and weakly supervised vision-language grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete bottleneck in weakly supervised REC—limited fine-grained visual recognition due to single-backbone features—and shows that dynamic combination of complementary pre-trained encoders can close a substantial gap (e.g., +16.5% on RefCOCO+), while maintaining near-baseline inference speed through sparse top-1 routing. The generalization to weakly supervised RES (up to +31% mIoU) suggests the routing mechanism is not task-specific. Open questions include whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or video grounding, whether more sophisticated routing (e.g., learned soft combinations beyond top-1) could further improve performance, and whether the reliance on a fixed YOLOv3 detection backbone limits scalability to more complex scenes.
