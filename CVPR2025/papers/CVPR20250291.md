## Problem

Compositional text-to-video generation—accurately depicting multiple objects with distinct attributes, actions, spatial relationships, and motions in a single video—remains unexplored as a systematic evaluation target. Existing T2V benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter, FETV) rely on single-object prompts and coarse metrics like CLIPScore or FVD that cannot capture fine-grained attribute binding, temporal dynamics, or multi-object compositionality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250291/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 1400 compositional text prompts (200 per category, each containing at least one active verb) and evaluates generated video clips (typically 2–5 seconds). For evaluation, videos are uniformly sampled to 6 frames for MLLM-based scoring, 16 frames for detection-based scoring, and resampled to 8 FPS for tracking-based scoring. Outputs are normalized per-category scores (0–1) across seven compositional dimensions, enabling model ranking within each category.

## Method

T2V-CompBench defines seven compositional categories spanning spatial dimensions (consistent attribute binding, spatial relationships, generative numeracy) and temporal dimensions (dynamic attribute binding, motion binding, action binding, object interactions), with prompts generated by GPT-4 from a vocabulary of 260 nouns, 200 active verbs, and 80 attributes mined from 1.67M real user prompts. Three tailored metric families are designed: (1) MLLM-based metrics—Grid-LLaVA (6-frame image grid fed to LLaVA with chain-of-thought disentangled questions) for attribute/action/interaction binding, and D-LLaVA (frame-by-frame LLaVA scoring of initial-to-final state transitions) for dynamic attributes; (2) detection-based metrics using GroundingDINO for 2D spatial rules and object counting, extended with Segment Anything and Depth Anything for 3D depth-based spatial relationships; (3) a tracking-based metric (DOT) that uses GroundingSAM masks plus dense optical flow to separate foreground object motion from camera motion. All metrics are validated against human annotations (651 videos, 3 annotators each) via Kendall's τ and Spearman's ρ, and 23 T2V models (17 open-source, 6 commercial) are benchmarked.

## Results

The proposed metrics achieve substantially higher correlation with human evaluations than all conventional metrics: Grid-LLaVA reaches Kendall's τ = 0.664 and Spearman's ρ = 0.789 on consistent attribute binding (vs. CLIP's τ = 0.367), G-Dino reaches τ = 0.577 on spatial relationships (vs. VPEval-S's τ = 0.314), and DOT reaches τ = 0.452 on motion binding (vs. M-GDino's τ = 0.206). Across 23 evaluated T2V models, dynamic attribute binding is the most challenging category, with all models scoring below 0.07 (best: Gen-3 at 0.069). Among commercial models, PixVerse-V3 leads in most categories (e.g., 0.872 on action binding, 0.607 on numeracy), while T2V-Turbo-V2 is the top open-source model on consistent attribute binding (0.672) and action binding (0.609).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between its proposed evaluation metrics and seven conventional metrics (CLIP, B-CLIP, B-BLEU, B-VQA, ViCLIP, VPEval-S, M-GDino) plus two MLLM baselines (PLLaVA, LLaVA) on human-evaluation correlation. The proposed metrics dominate: Grid-LLaVA outperforms LLaVA on action binding (τ 0.597 vs. 0.527) and object interactions (τ 0.456 vs. 0.421) by leveraging multi-frame temporal context; G-Dino surpasses VPEval-S on spatial relationships (τ 0.577 vs. 0.314) by adding 3D depth reasoning via Segment Anything and Depth Anything; DOT nearly doubles M-GDino's correlation on motion binding (τ 0.452 vs. 0.206) by separating foreground from background motion. Conventional metrics like CLIP and ViCLIP show near-zero or negative correlation on dynamic attribute binding and motion binding, confirming they cannot capture temporal compositionality. Among the 23 T2V models benchmarked, commercial models (PixVerse-V3, Kling-1.0, Dreamina 1.2) consistently outperform open-source models across most categories, while dynamic attribute binding remains the weakest category for all models (max score 0.069 by Gen-3), indicating a major unresolved challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional evaluation framework established by T2I-CompBench (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2023) from the image domain to the video domain, adding four temporal-dimension categories (dynamic attribute binding, motion binding, action binding, object interactions) that have no image-domain analogue. For evaluation, it repurposes LLaVA as an MLLM-based judge, GroundingDINO for detection-based spatial/numeracy scoring, and DOT for tracking-based motion assessment, combining these into a category-specific metric suite. It departs from existing T2V benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter, FETV) that evaluate single-object prompts and aggregate video quality, by explicitly targeting multi-object compositional prompts with fine-grained per-category scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that compositional T2V generation is a fundamental weakness of all current models: dynamic attribute binding scores are near zero across all 23 evaluated models (best: Gen-3 at 0.069), and motion binding and spatial relationship scores remain below 0.6 even for top commercial systems. This provides a structured, reproducible target for future research on temporal dynamics, motion control, and multi-object composition in video generation. Open questions include whether specialized motion modules or richer training data with detailed spatio-temporal captions can close these gaps, and whether the evaluation metrics themselves will need revision as models improve beyond the current ceiling.
