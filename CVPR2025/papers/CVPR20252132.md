## Problem

Low-bit (2-bit) compression of Large Multimodal Models remains largely unexplored, and existing 2-bit LLM quantization methods (AQLM, QuIP#) require days of fine-tuning to recover accuracy. LMMs exhibit highly sparse attention maps due to redundant visual tokens receiving far less attention than text tokens, but this structural property has not been leveraged for compression. The paper asks whether attention sparsity can theoretically bound compression error and practically enable fine-tuning-free 2-bit LMM compression.

## Input / Output

CASP operates on pre-trained LMMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, LLaVA-Next-7B, LLaVA-Next-Video-7B) and LLMs (Llama2-7B), using 1024 calibration samples (sequence length 4096) from RedPajama to compute data-aware decompositions and layer importance scores. The output is a compressed model where W_q and W_k are low-rank factorized to 25–50% of their original size and all weights are quantized to an average of 2 bits (with per-layer bits varying between 2 and 3 via optimal allocation). The compressed model then accepts the same multimodal inputs (images, videos, text prompts) and produces text responses or captions as the original.

## Method

CASP consists of two phases. First, a data-aware low-rank decomposition is applied to the Query and Key weight matrices: calibration data is whitened via Cholesky decomposition (following SVD-LLM), then SVD truncation reduces W_q and W_k to 25% (for GPTQ/AQLM) or 50% (for QuIP#) of their original size. The paper proves that the Frobenius-norm compression error of the attention map is upper-bounded by a function of attention sparsity, so sparser maps (more visual tokens) permit more aggressive rank truncation with negligible error. Second, an optimal bit allocation assigns 2 or 3 bits per layer by solving a constrained optimization that maximizes importance-weighted parameter counts (using Block Influence scores) subject to a target average bit rate, regularized by entropy to avoid extreme allocations. The resulting per-layer bit assignment is plugged into any standard PTQ method (GPTQ, AQLM, or QuIP#), making CASP an orthogonal, fine-tuning-free wrapper that improves 2-bit quantization by 21% on average across image- and video-language benchmarks.

## Results

On LLaVA1.5-7B at 2-bit precision, CASP_AQLM achieves a LiveBench PPL of 7.9 (vs. 14.9 for AQLM alone) and 52.2% SEED-Bench accuracy (vs. 41.7%), while CASP_QuIP# reaches 7.1 PPL and 63.1% SEED-Bench accuracy. Across all three LLaVA models, CASP delivers average relative improvements of 125.6%/40.0%/141.3% over GPTQ, 38.7%/32.0%/78.6% over AQLM, and 5.7%/2.7%/2.3% over QuIP#. On video-language tasks (LLaVA-Next-Video-7B), CASP_AQLM achieves a 159% relative improvement over AQLM, and CASP_QuIP# improves QuIP# by 21.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASP is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement on top of three state-of-the-art 2-bit quantization methods: GPTQ (2.2-bit), AQLM (2-bit), and QuIP# (2-bit). Against GPTQ, CASP yields the largest gains (125.6%–141.3% average relative improvement) because GPTQ alone suffers catastrophic degradation at 2-bit (e.g., SEED-Bench accuracy drops from 65.4% to 3.0% on LLaVA1.5-7B), while CASP_GPTQ recovers it to 24.2%. Against AQLM, CASP provides 32%–78.6% improvements, with the largest gains on LLaVA-Next-7B (78.6%) where the higher visual-token count increases attention sparsity. Against QuIP#, which is already the strongest baseline, CASP still achieves 2.3%–5.7% relative improvements (e.g., LiveBench PPL drops from 9.2 to 7.1 on LLaVA1.5-7B), though the absolute margin is smaller since QuIP# is already close to the original model's performance. On video-language tasks, CASP_AQLM achieves a 159% relative improvement over AQLM, and on language-only benchmarks (Llama2-7B), CASP improves AQLM by 11.2% and QuIP# by 2.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CASP sits at the intersection of two established post-training compression lines for LLMs: low-rank decomposition (SVD-LLM, ASVD, MoDeGPT) and vector quantization (GPTQ, AQLM, QuIP#). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it targets LMMs specifically, exploiting the fact that visual tokens produce highly sparse attention maps that LLMs do not, and (2) it couples data-aware low-rank factorization of W_q and W_k with a theoretically grounded optimal bit-allocation strategy, rather than applying quantization uniformly. The closest prior work, LQ-LoRA, also combines low-rank and quantized components but for fine-tuning rather than post-training compression, and does not leverage attention sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first study to tackle 2-bit LMM compression in a fine-tuning-free manner, which is practically significant since SOTA 2-bit LLM methods (AQLM, QuIP#) require 10–14 days of fine-tuning for 70B models. The theoretical bound linking attention sparsity to compression error provides a principled explanation for why LMMs are more amenable to aggressive compression of attention weights, and the authors note this insight could inform future LMM architecture design (e.g., more efficient attention mechanisms). Limitations include per-layer (rather than per-weight) bit allocation, a loose theoretical bound, and the fact that gains over the strongest baseline (QuIP#) are modest (~5–7%), suggesting diminishing returns at the frontier of 2-bit quantization.
