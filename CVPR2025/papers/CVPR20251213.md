## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves state-of-the-art rendering quality and speed, but its symmetric Gaussian kernels are fundamentally inefficient at modeling the shape and color discontinuities prevalent at object boundaries and texture-rich areas, producing Gibbs oscillations and blurry edges. The paper asks whether a minimal kernel modification can resolve this without sacrificing the speed and plug-and-play compatibility that make 3D-GS attractive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251213/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with camera poses (from an SfM pipeline) as input, initializing from sparse SfM point clouds. It produces a scene representation as pairs of 3D Half-Gaussian kernels—each pair sharing position, scale, rotation, and spherical-harmonics color, but adding a splitting-plane normal and two independent opacity values—enabling real-time novel-view rendering at speeds comparable to or exceeding the original 3D-GS (e.g., 125 FPS vs. 115 FPS on Mip-NeRF360).

## Method

The core contribution splits each 3D Gaussian by a plane through its center, allowing each half to carry a distinct opacity so the kernel can represent sharp transitions while retaining the original Gaussian's smooth low-frequency behavior. The splitting-plane normal reuses the three unused normal parameters already present in the 3D-GS implementation, so memory increases by only one extra opacity scalar per kernel. The z-axis integration of a half-Gaussian yields a closed-form erfc-based scaling factor multiplied by the standard 2D Gaussian projection, which is incorporated into a modified alpha-blending formula for the rasterizer. The splatting process independently computes the valid bounding region for each half (via projected splitting ellipses and tangent edges) so that fully transparent halves are skipped, preserving rendering speed despite the added geometric complexity.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, 3D-HGS achieves 29.66 PSNR (vs. 28.88 for 3D-GS, +0.78), while HGS-MCMC reaches a new SOTA of 30.13 PSNR. On Tanks & Temples, 3D-HGS scores 24.45 PSNR (+0.85 over 3D-GS) and HGS-MCMC sets a new benchmark at 25.08 PSNR. On Deep Blending, Scaffold-HGS achieves the best PSNR of 30.36. Rendering speed is preserved or improved: 3D-HGS runs at 125 FPS vs. 115 FPS for 3D-GS on Mip-NeRF360, with comparable or lower memory usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Half-Gaussian kernel consistently outperforms the original 3D-GS kernel by +0.78 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360, +0.85 on Tanks & Temples, and +0.35 on Deep Blending. Against Mip-NeRF, 3D-HGS wins on all three datasets (e.g., 29.66 vs. 29.23 on Mip-NeRF360), and against 2D-GS and GES it also leads on all datasets. The one exception is FreGS on Deep Blending, where FreGS (29.93) edges out 3D-HGS (29.76) by 0.17 PSNR. When the kernel is swapped into stronger baselines, HGS-MCMC surpasses GS-MCMC on Mip-NeRF360 (30.13 vs. 29.89) and Tanks & Temples (25.08 vs. 24.29), while Scaffold-HGS beats Scaffold-GS on Deep Blending (30.36 vs. 30.21) and Mip-NeRF360 (29.25 vs. 28.95). Mip-Splatting-HGS improves over Mip-Splatting on Mip-NeRF360 (29.88 vs. 29.39) and Tanks & Temples (24.53 vs. 23.75), confirming the kernel's plug-and-play benefit across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) by replacing the full 3D Gaussian kernel with a 3D Half-Gaussian kernel, where a plane through the Gaussian center splits it into two halves with independent opacities. It is positioned as a plug-and-play kernel swap applicable to multiple existing 3D-GS variants (Scaffold-GS, Mip-Splatting, GS-MCMC), distinguishing itself from 2D-GS (which switches to surface rendering) and GES (which modifies kernel shape without changing the rasterizer) by preserving volumetric rendering while adding a splitting plane. The mathematical foundation draws on the EWA splatting literature for the closed-form integration of the half-Gaussian along the viewing axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the Half-Gaussian kernel makes it immediately applicable to the rapidly growing ecosystem of 3D-GS-based methods without architectural changes, and the consistent PSNR gains across four baselines and three datasets demonstrate broad utility. However, the improvements, while consistent, are modest (0.13–0.85 dB PSNR), and the method is limited to a single planar split per kernel, leaving open whether more complex partitioning (multiple planes, curved surfaces) could yield further gains. The paper does not explore dynamic scenes, large-scale outdoor environments, or the interaction with other recent anti-aliasing strategies, which are natural next steps.
