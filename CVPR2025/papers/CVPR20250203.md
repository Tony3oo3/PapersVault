## Problem

Traditional object detection is confined to visible surfaces within the camera frustum, and even amodal detection only handles partially occluded objects. No prior work addresses detecting objects that are entirely unobserved—fully occluded behind visible surfaces or lying outside the image frame. This paper introduces the novel task of unobserved object detection in 2D, 2.5D, and 3D and benchmarks whether pre-trained generative models can infer the spatio-semantic distribution of such objects from a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250203/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 360×360 center-cropped RGB image with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics as input. It produces a normalized spatio-semantic distribution (SSD) per object category over a discretized domain: a 2D pixel grid expanded to 360×640 (extending 140 px left and right of the original frame), a 2.5D voxel grid (10×10×10) within the expanded frustum, or a full 3D voxel grid (20×20×20, 1.0 m cells) extending beyond the camera frustum. VLM-based outputs are coarser, yielding only region-wise (left/center/right) binary likelihoods aggregated over multiple queries.

## Method

Three pre-trained generative model families are adapted without fine-tuning to estimate the conditional spatio-semantic distribution via repeated sampling. The 3D pipeline uses DFM to generate a 3D scene from the input image, renders RGBD views from k=4 camera poses, runs YOLOv8x detection, back-projects 2D boxes into a 3D point cloud, and aggregates n_s=25 samples into a voxelized 20×20×20 distribution. The 2D pipeline uses SDXL inpainting to outpaint the image from 360×360 to 360×640, detects objects with YOLOv8x, and optionally lifts to 2.5D/3D using DepthAnythingv2 monocular depth. VLMs (ChatGPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Gemini-1.5-Ultra, LLaVA-34B) are queried 100 times per object-region pair with constrained yes/no prompts over a padded 640×360 image. A suite of asymmetric metrics—normalized entropy, cross-entropy, nearest-neighbor peak distance, false negative rate, and region-wise accuracy—captures both the accuracy and diversity of the predicted distribution relative to a single ground-truth realization.

## Results

On the 2D unobserved object detection task, SDXL achieves the best spatial accuracy with a normalized nearest-neighbor distance (Δ) of 0.446% on RealEstate10k and 0.510% on NYU Depth V2, along with a false negative rate (FNR) of 0.000 and region-wise accuracy (A) of 0.918 and 0.944 respectively. The 3D diffusion model (DFM) is competitive in 2D (Δ=5.990%/6.217%) and becomes the best method in 2.5D (Δ=5.128%/4.214%) and the only method capable of 3D detection (Δ=9.190%/3.948%, FNR=0.017/0.000). VLMs (ChatGPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Gemini-1.5-Ultra, LLaVa) struggle with large spatial errors (Δ=16–25%) and higher FNRs (up to 0.262 for LLaVa), reflecting their coarse region-wise output format.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDXL (2D diffusion outpainting) dominates the 2D task, achieving Δ=0.446% on RealEstate10k versus 5.990% for DFM and 16–25% for all VLMs, with a perfect FNR of 0.000. However, DFM (3D diffusion) surpasses SDXL in the 2.5D task (Δ=5.128% vs. 6.277% on RealEstate10k; 4.214% vs. 5.245% on NYU) and is the only method that can produce full 3D distributions, where it achieves Δ=3.948% and FNR=0.000 on NYU Depth V2. Among VLMs, ChatGPT-4o performs best (Δ=25.448% on RealEstate10k, FNR=0.024), while LLaVa-v1.6-34b has the worst FNR (0.262) and Gemini-1.5-Ultra the worst cross-entropy (1.968). The ablation shows that SDXL's 2D performance degrades sharply without object cues in prompts (Δ jumps from 0.470% to 6.037%), and DFM's 3D performance drops significantly when reducing camera poses from 4 to 2 (FNR increases from 0.017 to 0.38). Overall, no single method wins all tasks: SDXL is best for 2D, DFM for 2.5D and 3D, and VLMs remain limited by their coarse spatial granularity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from amodal object detection, which predicts the full geometry of partially visible objects, by formulating the harder problem of detecting objects that are entirely unobserved (fully occluded or outside the frame). It repurposes three families of generative models—3D diffusion (DFM), 2D diffusion (SDXL outpainting), and vision-language models—that were originally designed for photorealistic scene completion or open-ended reasoning—into probabilistic detection pipelines that output spatio-semantic distributions rather than single reconstructions. The conceptual bridge is that the diversity of generative samples approximates the conditional distribution over plausible scene completions, turning a synthesis tool into an inference mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The task is directly relevant to robotics applications (visual search, navigation, planning under uncertainty) where agents must reason about objects they cannot see, yet no prior benchmark or metric suite existed for evaluating such capabilities. Key open questions include the severe computational cost of 3D diffusion (48 days per dataset), the dependence of 2D outpainting on object-specific text prompts (which biases the empirical distribution), and whether VLM evaluations are confounded by training-data memorization. The paper establishes a useful evaluation protocol and metric set (normalized entropy, cross-entropy, nearest-neighbor distance, FNR, region-wise accuracy) that future work on probabilistic scene understanding can adopt.
