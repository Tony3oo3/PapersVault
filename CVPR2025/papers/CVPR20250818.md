## Problem

Diffusion models force the same U-Net parameters to simultaneously encode global structure (early timesteps) and fine texture (later timesteps), unlike traditional architectures where distinct layers specialize in different information levels. This paper investigates whether selectively zeroing out U-Net parameters at each timestep and for each sample can improve generation quality, revealing that even random masking at inference time enhances denoising — a phenomenon previously unexplored.

## Input / Output

The mask generator consumes a timestep embedding (projected via a fully connected layer) and the current noisy latent code (reduced via global average pooling), producing a binary mask of shape matching the U-Net linear layer weights. The masked weights are then applied via batch matrix-multiply to the flattened input feature map, yielding the denoised output. In the training-free variant, mask logits are iteratively optimized (15 iterations per timestep) using ImageReward and HPSv2 scores on decoded intermediate images, requiring no learned mask generator.

## Method

MaskUNet introduces a lightweight 4-layer MLP that generates timestep- and sample-dependent binary masks via Gumbel-Sigmoid activation, which are element-wise multiplied with frozen U-Net linear layer weights to selectively activate parameters relevant to the current denoising stage. The training-based approach optimizes the MLP using the standard diffusion MSE loss on 20.1k Laion-Art pairs, while the training-free approach directly optimizes mask logits by backpropagating a weighted sum of ImageReward and HPSv2 scores through the VAE decoder. Crucially, no U-Net parameters are updated; the method only adds negligible MLP parameters (or none in the training-free variant), preserving the pretrained model's generalization while enabling dynamic parameter selection. Empirical analysis shows that the optimal mask ratio increases with training iterations and that mask locations shift dynamically across timesteps and samples, confirming the timestep- and sample-dependence of effective parameters.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-image generation, MaskUNet achieves the best FID of 11.72 on COCO 2014 (30k images) and 21.88 on COCO 2017 (5k images), improving over SD 1.5 by 1.13 and 1.51 FID points respectively, while maintaining comparable CLIP scores (0.32/0.33). In the training-free setting on T2I-CompBench, SynGen+MaskUNet achieves the best BLIP-VQA scores across all three categories (Color 0.6989, Texture 0.6209, Shape 0.4644), surpassing Attend-and-Excite and standalone SynGen. On GenEval, SynGen+MaskUNet reaches an overall score of 0.50, with a notable 21% improvement in color attribution (0.26 vs. 0.05 for SD 1.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary zero-shot COCO benchmarks, MaskUNet outperforms SD 1.5 by 1.13 FID (COCO 2014) and 1.51 FID (COCO 2017), and beats LoRA by 1.10 and 1.30 FID respectively, while Full Fine-tune actually degrades performance (FID 14.06 vs. 12.85 for SD 1.5), confirming overfitting risk. In the training-free semantic binding setting, MaskUNet alone underperforms SynGen and Attend-and-Excite on T2I-CompBench (e.g., Color 0.4958 vs. 0.6400), but when combined with SynGen it surpasses both on all three BLIP-VQA categories (Color 0.6989, Texture 0.6209, Shape 0.4644 vs. Attend-and-Excite's 0.6400/0.5963/0.4517). On GenEval, SynGen+MaskUNet achieves the best overall score (0.50) and a dramatic 5.2× improvement in color attribution (0.26) over SD 1.5 (0.05). For downstream tasks (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, ReVersion, Text2Video-Zero), MaskUNet is applied as a plug-in enhancement rather than a direct competitor, with qualitative improvements in subject consistency, relational accuracy, and video coherence reported without updating U-Net parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard paradigm of diffusion model fine-tuning (LoRA, DoRA, full fine-tuning) by proposing to leave U-Net parameters entirely untouched and instead dynamically select subsets via learnable binary masks. Conceptually, it draws an analogy to traditional architectures (ResNet, GANs) where different layers capture different levels of abstraction, arguing that diffusion models' use of identical parameters across all timesteps is a design limitation. The training-free variant builds on ReNO's reward-model-guided optimization but redirects it from noise optimization to mask optimization, while the training-based variant is closer to structured pruning but with timestep- and sample-dependent sparsity patterns rather than static ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaskUNet offers a lightweight, parameter-preserving mechanism to improve diffusion model generation without risking the overfitting or generalization loss associated with weight updates, making it attractive as a plug-in for downstream tasks like DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, and video generation. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges that masking cannot inject new knowledge into the model, limiting its ceiling to reweighting existing capabilities. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SD 1.5 to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), how it interacts with low-rank adaptation methods, and whether the observed FID gains (≈1.1 on COCO) translate to perceptual quality improvements at scale.
