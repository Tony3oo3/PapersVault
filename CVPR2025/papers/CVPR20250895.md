## Problem

Existing hard-label fingerprinting methods for DNN tampering detection either rely on gradient-defined sensitivity (insufficient for subtle tampering) or decision-boundary proximity (still not sensitive enough for fine-grained attacks like last-layer fine-tuning with 10⁻⁵ learning rate), while meta-learning-based approaches incur prohibitive computational overhead. No prior work has theoretically characterized the co-optimal sensitivity of fingerprint samples across both the decision-boundary and gradient-defined sensitivity perspectives, leaving the optimal placement of fingerprint samples unprincipled.

## Input / Output

During generation, the method takes normal RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, GTSRB traffic signs, or 224×224 ImageNet) as source samples and produces perturbed fingerprint images with an L_p-norm-bounded perturbation (ε) that keeps them perceptually indistinguishable from the originals, along with their stored top-1 labels. During detection, fingerprint samples are sent as probe queries to a black-box model API that returns only the top-1 hard label; the model is flagged as tampered if any returned label mismatches the stored fingerprint label. The method supports configurable K (number of categories at the decision boundary) and M (number of fingerprint samples per probe) for tuning sensitivity versus query budget.

## Method

SDBF places fingerprint samples near the 'steep decision boundary' by jointly maximizing the L₂ norm of the logit output (which increases sensitivity of f(W,x) to parameter perturbation ΔW via Prop. 4.4) and minimizing the variance of logits over K chosen categories (which drives the sample toward the softmax high-sensitivity region where the Jacobian Frobenius norm is maximized, per Prop. 4.2). The combined objective (Eq. 16) is optimized via projected gradient descent with an L_p norm constraint on the input perturbation, and the K categories are selected as the top-K most confident classes of the original sample to ensure sample-dependent diversity. A Max Boundary Coverage Strategy (MBCS) is applied as post-processing to select M diverse fingerprint samples per probe by iteratively maximizing the entropy of the averaged softmax outputs, ensuring wide exploration of the decision boundary. Theoretically, Prop. 4.5 establishes that simultaneously maximizing both sensitivities inherently drives samples to the steep decision boundary, unifying the strengths of gradient-defined and decision-boundary fingerprinting without the overhead of meta-learning.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with a single fingerprint sample, SDBF at K=10 achieves 62.8% detection rate on the hardest tampering type (fine-tuning last layer at 10⁻⁵ LR), compared to 49.4% for IBSF (K=10) and 45.9% for MiSentry. Across all 9 tampering types on CIFAR-10, SDBF (K=10) achieves an average detection rate of ~88.9%, outperforming IBSF (K=10) at ~85.5% and MiSentry at ~83.3%. On GTSRB, SDBF (K=10) reaches 57.1% on FTLast-5 vs. 42.8% for IBSF (K=10) and 35.2% for MiSentry. Computationally, SDBF costs only 0.20–0.22 s per sample on CIFAR-10, comparable to IBSF (0.19–0.22 s) and far cheaper than MiSentry (1641 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDBF consistently outperforms IBSF (its closest competitor) at every K value on both CIFAR-10 and GTSRB, with the gap widening as K increases: at K=10 on CIFAR-10, SDBF leads IBSF by 13.4 pp on FT-Last-5 (62.8% vs 49.4%) and by 12.6 pp on T-Attack (85.8% vs 73.2%). Against MiSentry, SDBF at K≥6 surpasses it on all tampering types, achieving up to 16.9 pp higher detection on FT-Last-5 (62.8% vs 45.9%); at K=2, MiSentry retains a slight edge on some subtle tampering types due to its meta-learning model zoo. SDBF dramatically outperforms SSF, which scores 0% on FT-Last-5 and below 12% on T-Attack and Unlearning, confirming that gradient-defined sensitivity alone is insufficient. PublicCheck is also clearly dominated by SDBF across all settings. In the multi-sample detection setting (Fig. 5), SDBF with MBCS reaches 100% detection on FT-Last-5 at M=3 (K=2), faster than any baseline, and the MBCS ablation shows a 16.2 pp boost (68.3%→84.5%) at M=2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IBSF's K-category decision-boundary fingerprinting framework (PGD optimization, sample-dependent C_sub selection) but departs from it by adding an L2-norm maximization term to the objective, which drives samples toward the *steep* decision boundary rather than merely any boundary. Conceptually, it unifies two previously separate fingerprinting paradigms—gradient-defined sensitivity (SSF) and decision-boundary proximity (IBSF, PublicCheck, AID)—under a single theoretical lens, showing that maximizing ||y||_2 while minimizing logit variance simultaneously achieves both properties. It also positions itself against the meta-learning-based MiSentry, arguing that the latter's high computational overhead is impractical for the lightweight black-box detection setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical justification (via Softmax Jacobian analysis) for why decision-boundary-adjacent samples are more sensitive to tampering, and the first co-optimal analysis combining gradient and boundary sensitivity contexts—filling a gap the authors explicitly note no prior work addressed. Practically, SDBF matches IBSF's minimal computational cost while consistently outperforming it and the far more expensive MiSentry across 9 tampering types and 3 datasets, making it a deployable solution for MLaaS integrity verification. Open questions include whether the steep-boundary formulation extends to non-classification tasks (regression, generation), whether the linear last-layer assumption in Prop. 4.4 holds for architectures with non-linear final layers, and how detection degrades under more sophisticated adaptive attacks beyond the backdoor scenario tested.
