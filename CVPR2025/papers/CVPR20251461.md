## Problem

While LVLMs increasingly rely on chain-of-thought reasoning, a systematic evaluation of their ability to critique and correct their own visual reasoning remains absent. Prior work critiques entire responses with a single scalar score, ignoring the fine-grained, step-level feedback that educational research shows is more effective for error correction. This leaves the self-improvement pipeline—where critique quality determines correction success—largely uncharacterized for visual reasoning.

## Input / Output

For the critique task, the model receives an image, a question, and a chain-of-thought (≤5 sentence-level steps) with a final answer, and must produce a hierarchical output: a binary correctness label per step, a natural-language explanation for each incorrect step, and a binary label for the final answer. For the correction task, the model additionally receives the critique (either human-annotated or self-generated) and must produce a refined answer. The VISCore metric is the geometric mean of F1 scores at answer, step, and explanation levels, with explanation F1 assessed via LLM-judged alignment against three human-annotated references per incorrect step.

## Method

VISCO is a benchmark of 1,645 QA pairs with 5,604 step-wise annotations spanning 18 datasets across reasoning (math, science) and perception (hallucination, spatial, text recognition) tasks, annotated by three expert annotators achieving 64.76% Cohen's Kappa agreement. The paper evaluates 24 LVLMs on both critique and correction, identifying three failure patterns: inability to critique visual perception, a bias toward judging steps as correct ('reluctance to say no'), and overestimation of error propagation across steps. To address these, the authors propose LOOKBACK, a prompting strategy that iteratively extracts atomic factual claims from each CoT step, reformulates them as verification questions, queries the original image for each question, and synthesizes the resulting inconsistencies into a step-wise critique. LOOKBACK improves VISCore by up to 13.5% (22.2% on perception tasks) and correction gain by up to 11.5% across four LVLMs.

## Results

On the VISCO benchmark, GPT-4o achieves the highest critique score (VISCore = 52.4) among all evaluated LVLMs, followed by Claude-3.5-Sonnet (51.3) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (45.0), while the human expert baseline reaches 86.5. For correction with human-annotated critique, top models correct 73–78% of errors (GPT-4o: 76.2, Gemini-1.5-Pro: 78.0), but self-generated critique yields far lower gains (GPT-4o: 28.8, Claude-3.5-Sonnet: 25.6), confirming critique as the key bottleneck. Non-trivial above-random critique capability only emerges at ~70B parameters; smaller models (7–40B) score 7.5–28.5 on VISCore and often show negative correction gains. The proposed LOOKBACK strategy improves critique by up to 13.5% (InternVL2-26B: 25.2→38.7) and correction by up to 11.5% (Gemini-1.5-Pro: 24.9→36.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 24 LVLMs spanning three size tiers and two critique-specific models. Among open-source models, LLaVA-Critic-72B (VISCore 42.6) outperforms all other 70B+ open models, surpassing Llama-3.2-90B (36.4) and Qwen2-VL-72B (37.4) by 5–6 points, demonstrating that critique-specific fine-tuning is more effective than raw scale. Proprietary models (GPT-4o: 52.4, Claude-3.5-Sonnet: 51.3) lead all open models by 9–10 VISCore points but still trail the human baseline (86.5) by over 34 points. For correction, Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves the highest human-critique correction gain (78.0), slightly ahead of GPT-4o (76.2) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (73.7), while self-critique correction gains drop to 24.9–28.8, a 45–50 point gap that highlights the critique bottleneck. Smaller models (7–8B) consistently show negative or near-zero self-correction gains (e.g., DeepSeek-VL: −17.9, LLaVA-v1.6-7B: −8.7), indicating they cannot reliably self-improve. LOOKBACK applied to InternVL2-26B boosts VISCore from 25.2 to 38.7 (+13.5) and correction gain from 6.0 to 14.4 (+8.4), outperforming the unmodified model by a wide margin on both tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-improvement/self-refinement literature (Self-Refine, self-debug for code) from text-only settings to visual reasoning, and departs from prior critique benchmarks (CriticBench, MetaCritique, CriticEval, MLLM-as-a-Judge) by introducing dense step-wise binary labels with natural language explanations for each chain-of-thought step in a visual context. While prior multimodal evaluation work (MLLM-as-a-Judge, Perception Collection) used LVLMs as scalar evaluators of output quality, VISCO reframes critique as a fine-grained, step-level diagnostic task that feeds directly into a correction pipeline. The LOOKBACK strategy is a novel prompting technique that explicitly revisits the image to verify atomic claims, drawing on the observation that visual perception errors are a dominant source of reasoning failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VISCO fills a clear gap as the first systematic benchmark for fine-grained critique and correction in visual reasoning, and its identification of three concrete failure patterns (perception critique failure, reluctance to say no, exaggerated error propagation) provides actionable diagnostic targets for future work. The finding that self-generated critiques are often detrimental rather than helpful reframes the self-improvement problem: the bottleneck is not correction but critique generation, a gap that remains wide even for frontier models (GPT-4o at 52.4 vs. human 86.5 VISCore). Open questions include whether specialized critique training (as shown by LLaVA-Critic) can close this gap at scale, whether LOOKBACK-style verification generalizes beyond the four models tested, and how to handle the fundamental tension between the reluctance-to-disagree bias and the need for calibrated error detection.
