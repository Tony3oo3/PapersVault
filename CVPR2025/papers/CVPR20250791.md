## Problem

Autonomous driving simulators require 3D vehicle models that are both photorealistic and highly controllable (e.g., opening doors, rotating wheels), but existing approaches force a trade-off: handcrafted CAD models offer part-level controllability yet lack realistic materials, while reconstruction-based methods (NeRF, 3DGS, diffusion) produce photorealistic appearance but lose fine-grained geometric control over individual components. UrbanCAD addresses this gap by preserving expert-designed CAD geometry and procedural material priors while adapting them to match a single real-world observation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single urban RGB image with a user-clicked 2D point on the target vehicle, a library of 26k+ free CAD models (Objaverse), and 13k+ handcrafted procedural material graphs (Adobe Material Library). Optionally, multi-view fisheye camera pairs (providing 360° field of view) and multi-view perspective background images are supplied for lighting estimation and scene reconstruction. The output is a photorealistic 3D vehicle CAD digital twin with part-disentangled geometry and optimized procedural materials, an HDR environment lighting map, and composited novel-view renderings of the full driving scene with the vehicle inserted at arbitrary 3D positions.

## Method

UrbanCAD follows a retrieval-optimization paradigm in three stages. First, CAD model retrieval encodes the SAM-segmented vehicle image with CLIP and all library CAD models with OpenShape (a 3D encoder aligned to CLIP's latent space), selecting the model with highest cosine similarity to preserve part-disentangled geometry and symmetric material design. Second, part-aware material prior retrieval uses ControlNet to render photorealistic previews of the retrieved material design, then GroundedSAM to identify semantic part meanings (windows, wheels, car body) and assign corresponding base procedural graphs (glass, rubber, reflective metal) from the Adobe library. Third, part-aware material optimization converts the procedural graphs into albedo/normal/roughness texture maps via DiffMat v2 and refines them through physics-based differentiable rendering (InvRenderNet) with a composite loss of part-level statistics, masked VGG Gram-matrix similarity, and masked RGB alignment; only albedo of metal and rubber is optimized while glass is directly assigned. For scene insertion, fisheye pairs are converted to HDR panoramas for spatially varying environment lighting, and 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs the background for alpha-composited novel view synthesis in Blender.

## Results

On the photorealism evaluation (360° novel view renderings vs. real car images), UrbanCAD achieves the best FID of 62.80 and best LPIPS of 0.5242, outperforming all reconstruction-based baselines (PixelNeRF, Wonder3D, LRM, HUGS) by a wide margin (FID >220). Among retrieval-based mesh-texturing methods, UrbanCAD beats Paint3D† (FID 67.52) and LatentPaint† (FID 85.62) on FID and LPIPS, though Paint3D† edges it out on KID (0.0417 vs. 0.0479). For downstream perception, UrbanCAD-generated in-distribution scenes yield the highest IOU (74.08–74.15) and PQ (61.60–62.16) for YOLO-Seg, surpassing all other data sources. In out-of-distribution door-opening scenarios, perception accuracy drops sharply (e.g., Mask2Former-R101 instance IOU falls from 96.47 to 83.46), confirming the safety-critical value of the generated corner cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UrbanCAD decisively outperforms all four reconstruction-based baselines: PixelNeRF (FID 264.61), Wonder3D (246.43), HUGS (240.92), and LRM (220.77) all produce FID scores 3.5–4.2× higher than UrbanCAD's 62.80, reflecting their inability to generalize beyond the reference viewpoint. Among retrieval-based texturing methods, UrbanCAD beats PhotoScene† by a large margin on all three metrics (FID 62.80 vs. 170.21; KID 0.0479 vs. 0.1561; LPIPS 0.5242 vs. 0.5422), and surpasses LatentPaint† on FID (62.80 vs. 85.62) and LPIPS (0.5242 vs. 0.5525). The closest competitor is Paint3D†, which achieves a slightly better KID (0.0417 vs. 0.0479) but is worse on FID (67.52 vs. 62.80) and LPIPS (0.5652 vs. 0.5242); the authors attribute Paint3D's KID advantage to its tendency to generate rich but sometimes unrealistic textures. Notably, even UrbanCAD without material optimization (FID 81.05) already outperforms every reconstruction-based baseline, underscoring the value of the CAD retrieval prior. For downstream perception on in-distribution scenes, UrbanCAD (full) yields the best YOLO-Seg IOU (74.08–74.15) and Mask2Former-R50 PQ (34.10–34.71), while removing either material optimization or lighting estimation causes measurable drops, confirming both modules' contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

UrbanCAD sits at the intersection of CAD model retrieval (Scan2CAD, Roca, DiffCAD), differentiable material capture (MATch, DiffMat v2, PhotoScene), and neural scene rendering for autonomous driving (CADSim, UniSim, HUGS). It departs from prior CAD-based reconstruction methods (CADSim, Samp) that convert retrieved CAD geometry into implicit representations, thereby losing part-level controllability; instead, UrbanCAD preserves the native CAD structure and applies part-aware material optimization on top. It also extends single-material procedural graph optimization (MATch, PhotoScene) to multi-material objects like vehicles by introducing semantic-based part material retrieval via GroundedSAM and ControlNet, and adds a fisheye-based spatially varying lighting estimation module absent from prior CAD retrieval pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UrbanCAD addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving simulation: the inability to generate photorealistic, part-controllable vehicle assets from real-world images, which is essential for creating safety-critical OOD scenarios (e.g., opened doors) that stress-test perception systems. The demonstrated performance drop of YOLOv8 and Mask2Former on OOD door-opening data validates the pipeline's utility for robustness evaluation. Key limitations include geometry that is semantically but not exactly matched to the input vehicle, and a coarse spatially varying lighting approximation that degrades when the insertion position is far from fisheye cameras. Open questions include extending the approach to non-vehicle objects, handling dynamic deformations beyond rigid part articulation, and reducing the reliance on large pre-built CAD and material libraries.
