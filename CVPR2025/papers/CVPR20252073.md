## Problem

Learning to predict 3D motion from imagery requires large-scale ground-truth 3D motion trajectories paired with video, but no such dataset exists at scale. Synthetic datasets fail to capture real-world motion distributions, and traditional capture methods (motion capture, multi-view arrays) are accurate but cannot scale to diverse in-the-wild scenes. The paper identifies online VR180 stereoscopic fisheye videos as an untapped, scalable source of real-world dynamic 3D content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252073/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

DynaDUSt3R takes two RGB video frames (I_0 at time t_0, I_1 at time t_1, potentially seconds apart) and a scalar query time t_q ∈ [0,1] as input. It outputs two 3D point clouds aligned in I_0's coordinate frame (one per input frame), a 3D scene-flow (motion) map for each frame displacing points to the intermediate time t_q, plus per-pixel point and motion confidence maps. The ability to query arbitrary intermediate times enables continuous, potentially non-linear motion trajectories between the two input frames rather than only endpoint displacement.

## Method

The paper contributes two components. First, a data pipeline that converts VR180 stereoscopic fisheye videos into pseudo-metric 4D reconstructions: ORB-SLAM2 segments shots, incremental SfM (COLMAP-style) estimates camera poses with optimized stereo rig calibration, RAFT computes per-frame disparity on rectified pairs, and BootsTAP extracts dense 2D tracks that are unprojected into 3D. A per-track optimization solves for per-frame scalar offsets along camera rays using a weighted combination of static (world-space consistency), dynamic (discrete Laplacian along the ray), and disparity-space regularization losses, with weights determined by a sigmoid of the track's 2D motion magnitude. Second, DynaDUSt3R extends DUSt3R by adding a parallel motion head that predicts 3D displacement vectors to a query time t_q, encoded via a 128-D positional embedding injected through linear projections. The model is initialized from DUSt3R weights (motion head from point head weights) and fine-tuned for 49k steps with a confidence-aware scale-invariant 3D regression loss applied to both the pointmaps and the post-motion positions.

## Results

On the 3D motion prediction task (Table 1), DynaDUSt3R trained on Stereo4D achieves an EPE3D of 0.1110 and δ3D^0.05 of 65.07% on the held-out Stereo4D test set, dramatically outperforming the same architecture trained on synthetic PointOdyssey data (EPE3D 0.6191, δ3D^0.05 11.61%). On the structure/depth prediction task (Table 2), DynaDUSt3R trained on Stereo4D achieves Abs Rel of 0.1032 and δ<1.25 of 87.93% on Stereo4D, surpassing DUSt3R (0.2696 / 67.77%) and MonST3R (0.1939 / 72.56%). On the unseen Bonn dataset, the Stereo4D-trained model also leads with Abs Rel 0.0653 and δ<1.25 of 96.02%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaDUSt3R trained on Stereo4D outperforms DUSt3R by a wide margin on both datasets: on Stereo4D it reduces Abs Rel from 0.2696 to 0.1032 (62% improvement) and raises δ<1.25 from 67.77% to 87.93%; on Bonn it improves Abs Rel from 0.1098 to 0.0653 and δ<1.25 from 84.93% to 96.02%. Against MonST3R, the Stereo4D-trained model also wins on all four metrics, with the largest gap on Stereo4D (Abs Rel 0.1032 vs 0.1939, δ<1.25 87.93% vs 72.56%). Compared to the same architecture trained on synthetic PointOdyssey data, the Stereo4D-trained model is substantially better on the in-domain Stereo4D test set (Abs Rel 0.1032 vs 0.3858) and slightly better on Bonn (0.0653 vs 0.0691), confirming that real-world training data transfers more effectively to diverse scenes. On the motion prediction task (Table 1), the Stereo4D-trained model reduces EPE3D by over 5× compared to PointOdyssey training (0.1110 vs 0.6191 on Stereo4D; 0.1231 vs 0.3126 on ADT), and raises δ3D^0.05 from 11.61% to 65.07% on the held-out set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DUSt3R (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), a ViT-based architecture for static 3D point cloud prediction from image pairs, by adding a parallel motion head that predicts 3D scene flow to an intermediate query time. The data pipeline itself composes well-established components—RAFT stereo matching, BootsTAP 2D tracking, COLMAP-style SfM, and ORB-SLAM2 shot detection—into a novel unified framework for extracting pseudo-metric 4D reconstructions from VR180 fisheye videos, with a custom per-track optimization that blends static and dynamic regularization. Conceptually, the work departs from the dominant paradigm of training 3D motion models on synthetic data (PointOdyssey, Kubric) by demonstrating that internet-sourced real-world stereoscopic video, carefully filtered and fused, can serve as a scalable supervision source for dynamic 3D geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Stereo4D dataset (100K+ clips with per-point 3D trajectories, camera poses, and depth maps) is the first large-scale, real-world, pseudo-metric 4D dataset, filling a critical gap that has limited progress in dynamic 3D understanding. The finding that training on this real data yields a ~5.6× reduction in 3D end-point error over the same architecture trained on PointOdyssey challenges the assumption that synthetic data suffices for 3D motion learning, in contrast to 2D optical flow where synthetic data generalizes well. Key limitations include the two-frame-only input (no video-level global optimization), dependence on optical flow quality for distant or occluded regions, and the non-generative nature of the model, leaving open the question of how to handle ambiguous or occluded motion. Extending DynaDUSt3R to video input or integrating generative priors are natural next steps.
