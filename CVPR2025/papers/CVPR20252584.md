## Problem

Existing event-based video frame interpolation (EVFI) methods suffer from poor generalization on unseen data because they are trained on small, custom event-frame datasets (10² scale) with small, specialized models (10⁸ parameters), in stark contrast to the internet-scale commercial datasets and billion-parameter foundation models available in video generation. This paper asks whether pre-trained video diffusion foundation models can be adapted for EVFI to inherit their strong data priors and representational power while preserving ground-truth fidelity required for interpolation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a start frame I_s, an end frame I_e, forward-time events E_s (between start and target), and backward-time events E_e (between target and end) as input. Events are encoded in a 3-stack multi-channel representation where each successive stack halves the accumulated event count, capturing fast-to-slow motion at different temporal scales. The model operates in the 8×-downsampled latent space of Stable Video Diffusion (14-frame video), with per-tile processing at an upsample factor of 2× (BS-ERGB, Clear-Motion) or 3× (HQF) to mitigate VAE detail loss. Output is a set of interpolated RGB frames between the start and end keyframes.

## Method

RE-VDM adapts Stable Video Diffusion via a ControlNet-style architecture: the original denoiser weights are frozen, and a trainable copy of a subset of blocks (augmented with an event encoder that maps multi-stack event representations to a latent E_cond) produces residual corrections added to the frozen blocks' outputs, trained with standard diffusion MSE loss on small EVFI data. At inference, Per-tile Denoising and Fusion divides the upsampled latent canvas into overlapping tiles, denoises each independently, and fuses them via weighted averaging to preserve fine spatial detail and event-control accuracy without prohibitive memory cost. For interpolation (rather than generation), a Two-side Fusion mechanism runs the event-conditioned denoising step (EVDS) from both the start frame with forward events and the end frame with backward events at every denoising step, then combines the two latent sequences with linearly increasing temporal weights (W_f from 0 to 1) so that frames near each boundary rely more on that boundary's information, achieving consistent interpolation without additional training.

## Results

RE-VDM achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all three real-world EVFI datasets: 27.74/0.88 on BS-ERGB (3 skips), 29.04/0.89 on HQF (3 skips), and 22.94/0.88 on Clear-Motion (15 skips). On LPIPS, RE-VDM achieves 0.12 (BS-ERGB), 0.06 (HQF), and 0.37 (Clear-Motion), though several frame-only methods (e.g., GIMM-VFI-R-P at 0.08/0.04/0.26) report lower LPIPS. The largest margin over the strongest event-based baseline CBMNet-Large is on BS-ERGB PSNR (+1.50 dB) and Clear-Motion PSNR (+0.68 dB), while the gap over pre-trained video diffusion baselines (Time Reversal, DynamiCrafter) exceeds 9 dB in PSNR on all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RE-VDM outperforms the strongest event-based baseline CBMNet-Large on PSNR and SSIM across all three datasets (e.g., +1.50 dB PSNR on BS-ERGB, +0.31 dB on HQF, +0.68 dB on Clear-Motion), and also beats it on LPIPS for BS-ERGB (0.12 vs 0.22) and HQF (0.06 vs 0.09), though CBMNet-Large edges ahead on Clear-Motion LPIPS (0.39 vs 0.37). Against pre-trained video diffusion baselines (Time Reversal, DynamiCrafter), RE-VDM achieves dramatically higher PSNR (9–11 dB improvement) and SSIM, confirming that event-based motion control is essential for interpolation fidelity. On LPIPS, several frame-only methods (GIMM-VFI-R-P, PerVFI, InterpAny-Clearer) report lower values than RE-VDM on all three datasets, but the paper argues these methods fail to reconstruct correct object positions and shapes under large motion, as shown qualitatively in Figures 7–8. RE-VDM's advantage over frame-only methods is most pronounced on Clear-Motion (15-frame skip), where PSNR gaps reach 5+ dB, highlighting the ill-posed nature of frame-only interpolation under substantial motion. The method also demonstrates superior frame-to-frame consistency compared to CBMNet-Large, which suffers from per-frame inference artifacts on regular shapes (ball, hand) in the BS-ERGB test sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to adapt a pre-trained video diffusion foundation model (Stable Video Diffusion) for Event-based Video Frame Interpolation, a task previously addressed exclusively by small, custom-trained models like CBMNet and TimeLens. Architecturally, it combines three existing ideas: ControlNet-style frozen-weight-plus-trainable-copy adaptation for event-based motion control, MultiDiffusion's per-tile denoising and weighted fusion for resolution-agnostic generation, and Time Reversal's two-side denoising for interpolation consistency. The key departure from prior EVFI work is the reliance on internet-scale video data priors and billion-parameter backbones rather than specialized models trained on limited paired event-frame datasets, and the key departure from prior video diffusion work is the addition of event-based control and test-time optimization for ground-truth fidelity rather than diverse generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a concrete pathway for the small EVFI community to leverage the rapidly advancing video generation ecosystem, potentially resolving the data-scarcity bottleneck that has limited EVFI generalization. However, significant limitations remain: VAE encoding/decoding causes color shifts and detail loss, the adapted model smooths fine textures, and the computational cost of diffusion-based inference is substantially higher than frame-by-frame EVFI methods. Open questions include whether newer, higher-fidelity video diffusion models will eliminate the VAE bottleneck, whether the test-time optimization overhead (per-tile denoising, two-side fusion) can be reduced, and whether this approach extends to longer sequences or real-time applications.
