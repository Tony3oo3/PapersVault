## Problem

Existing video summarization methods rely primarily on visual features and temporal dynamics, often failing to capture the semantic content of videos and the inherent subjectivity in defining keyframes. The paper asks whether the broad semantic knowledge and reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models can be leveraged to evaluate frame importance in a way that better aligns with diverse human judgments, shifting the summarization paradigm from visual saliency to language-guided semantic assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252861/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of video frames (T×H×W×3) and produces per-frame importance scores (T×1). Each frame is first converted into a single-sentence caption (max 77 tokens) by LLaVA-1.5-7B, and these captions are then consumed by Llama-2-13B-chat in sliding windows of size 7. Rather than using the LLM's final text output, the method extracts intermediate output embeddings (query and answer representations after the RMS Norm layer) as the actual signal for downstream scoring, which are pooled and projected to a 2048-dimensional space before global aggregation.

## Method

LLMVS is a local-to-global framework with three stages: (1) a frozen M-LLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B) generates one-sentence captions for every frame; (2) a frozen LLM (Llama-2-13B-chat) scores each frame's local importance via in-context learning, where a structured prompt (instruction + 3 examples + query) feeds a sliding window of 7 captions and the model's intermediate query/answer embeddings are extracted after the RMS Norm layer; (3) a trainable global context aggregator (3 self-attention blocks, 2 heads) operates on the pooled and MLP-projected embeddings across all T frames to produce final importance scores. The key design choice is using LLM output embeddings rather than direct numerical answers, preserving richer contextual and semantic information. Only the self-attention blocks and MLPs are trained with MSE loss against ground-truth scores, while both the M-LLM and LLM remain frozen to preserve their general domain knowledge.

## Results

LLMVS achieves state-of-the-art performance on both standard video summarization benchmarks: on SumMe, it reaches Kendall's τ = 0.253 and Spearman's ρ = 0.282, and on TVSum, τ = 0.211 and ρ = 0.275. The zero-shot LLM (without the global context aggregator) scores τ = 0.170 / ρ = 0.189 on SumMe but drops sharply to τ = 0.051 / ρ = 0.056 on TVSum, highlighting the critical role of the self-attention-based global context aggregator. On the zero-shot MR.HiSum generalization test, LLMVS (τ = 0.440) outperforms the next-best method PGL-SUM (τ = 0.375) by a substantial margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLMVS outperforms all 12 baselines on both SumMe and TVSum. On SumMe, it edges out the strongest visual-only method CSTA (τ = 0.246, ρ = 0.274) by 0.007 in τ and 0.008 in ρ, and surpasses the best multimodal method SSPVS (τ = 0.192, ρ = 0.257) by a wider margin of 0.061 in τ. On TVSum, LLMVS leads DMASum (τ = 0.203, ρ = 0.267) by 0.008 in τ and 0.008 in ρ, and PGL-SUM (τ = 0.206, ρ = 0.157) by 0.005 in τ and a large 0.118 in ρ. The zero-shot LLM baseline (no global aggregator) is competitive on SumMe (τ = 0.170) but performs poorly on TVSum (τ = 0.051), indicating that the global self-attention aggregator is essential for handling the per-user evaluation protocol of TVSum. On the zero-shot MR.HiSum generalization test, LLMVS (τ = 0.440) beats PGL-SUM (τ = 0.375), VASNet (τ = 0.364), and DSNet-AB (τ = 0.362) by 6–8 points, demonstrating strong transfer to unseen video content.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLMVS departs from the dominant paradigm in video summarization, where prior methods (VASNet, DSNet, CSTA) select keyframes based on visual features and temporal dynamics, and multimodal methods (CLIP-It, A2Summ, SSPVS) treat text as an auxiliary signal fused into visual representations via cross-attention. Instead, LLMVS inverts this hierarchy: it centers summarization around language by generating per-frame captions with an M-LLM and scoring importance via an LLM's semantic reasoning. It also departs from prior LLM-based approaches that consume the model's final textual answer, opting instead to extract intermediate output embeddings and aggregate them through a learned self-attention module for global context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that LLMs can serve as effective frame selectors, achieving SOTA on SumMe and TVSum with a clean local-to-global design that requires training only lightweight self-attention blocks while keeping both LLMs frozen. However, the approach is computationally heavy (LLaVA-7B + Llama-2-13B per frame), the zero-shot LLM struggles with subjective per-user preferences (TVSum tau drops to 0.051), and generalization to longer or more diverse videos remains untested. Open questions include whether the caption bottleneck from the M-LLM limits ceiling performance and whether the method scales to hour-long content.
