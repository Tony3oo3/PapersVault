## Problem

Test-time OOD detection remains challenging when test-time OOD samples differ significantly from training outliers, as existing test-time calibration methods (e.g., TULIP, RTL++) rely on batch-wise parameter updates that cause catastrophic forgetting, unstable detection, and degraded ID classification. The key unexplored question is how to dynamically accumulate and leverage latent OOD features at test time without any fine-tuning or prior outlier samples from auxiliary datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) processed through a pre-trained ResNet encoder to extract d-dimensional latent features, which are then compared against two dictionaries via cosine similarity. The output is a scalar OOD score S(x) = S_in(x) + S_out(x) that is thresholded for an ID/OOD binary decision. The system operates in a streaming mini-batch fashion (batch size 512), updating the OOD dictionary incrementally as new test samples arrive, with the ID dictionary pre-computed once before deployment.

## Method

OODD maintains a dynamic OOD dictionary as a priority queue that accumulates latent features of test samples with the lowest OOD scores (i.e., most likely OOD), replacing the highest-scoring entries as new lower-scoring samples arrive. An Informative Inlier Sampling (IIS) strategy selects high-confidence patches from multiple random crops of training data to build a compact, representative ID dictionary. The final OOD score combines the K-th largest cosine similarity to the ID dictionary (S_in) with the negative K-th largest cosine similarity to the OOD dictionary (S_out), and a Dual OOD Stabilization mechanism uses a fixed memory bank of pre-generated outliers to prevent score fluctuations during early testing. The method replaces Euclidean distance with cosine similarity for a 3× speedup, justified by a monotonic equivalence argument after ℓ₂-normalization, and requires no gradient updates or fine-tuning at test time.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 Far OOD, OODD achieves 93.64% AUROC and 24.74% FPR95, improving FPR95 by 33.4 percentage points over the prior SOTA TULIP (58.17%) and by 28.9 points over KNN (53.65%). On CIFAR-10 Far OOD, OODD reaches 95.77% AUROC and 17.44% FPR95, outperforming KNN (92.96% / 24.27%) and TULIP (92.55% / 24.43%). On ImageNet-200 Far OOD, OODD attains 95.47% AUROC and 20.89% FPR95, and on ImageNet-1K Far OOD it achieves 93.84% AUROC and 27.63% FPR95. In terms of efficiency, OODD runs 3× faster than KNN (10.13s vs. 30.17s on ImageNet-200) while maintaining superior detection accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OODD outperforms all post-hoc baselines (MaxLogits, ODIN, MD, Energy, ViM) by large margins on Far OOD across every dataset; for example, on CIFAR-100 Far OOD it improves AUROC from 79.67% (MaxLogits) to 93.64% and FPR95 from 56.73% to 24.74%. Against the closest KNN baseline [31], OODD gains +11.24 AUROC and −28.9 FPR95 on CIFAR-100 Far OOD, and +2.31 AUROC and −6.38 FPR95 on ImageNet-200 Far OOD, while remaining competitive on Near OOD (slightly higher FPR95 on CIFAR-100 Near: 56.98 vs. 61.22 for KNN, but higher AUROC: 82.10 vs. 80.18). Versus the test-time adaptation baselines, OODD substantially outperforms TULIP [1] on Far OOD (e.g., CIFAR-100: 24.74 vs. 58.17 FPR95; ImageNet-1K: 27.63 vs. 48.01 FPR95) and RTL++ [8] (CIFAR-100: 24.74 vs. 67.14 FPR95), while matching or slightly exceeding them on Near OOD AUROC. When combined with CLIP-based methods, OODD further reduces FPR95 by 28.69 points on iNaturalist (MCM: 30.91→2.22) and 12.89 points on Textures (NegLabel: 43.56→30.67), demonstrating strong complementarity with vision-language OOD detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KNN-based OOD detection framework (Sun et al., ICML 2022) by replacing the static training feature set with a dynamically updated OOD dictionary managed via a priority queue, enabling test-time adaptation without fine-tuning. It departs from concurrent test-time methods like TULIP and RTL++ that perform batch-wise parameter updates (risking catastrophic forgetting) by instead maintaining a fixed-size feature dictionary with cosine similarity scoring. The informative inlier sampling (IIS) strategy and dual OOD stabilization (DOS) mechanism are novel contributions that address the cold-start problem and score instability during early testing, while the theoretical equivalence between cosine similarity and normalized Euclidean distance provides a principled justification for the 3× speedup over standard KNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 26% FPR95 reduction on CIFAR-100 Far OOD (58.17% → 24.74%) and the 3× speedup over KNN make this a practically attractive option for deployment in open-world settings where OOD distributions shift over time, as validated by the temporal drift experiments. A key limitation is that the OOD dictionary stores latent features, providing limited benefit to logits-based methods (MaxLogits, Energy), suggesting the dictionary design needs extension to capture score-space information. Open questions include how the method behaves when training ID features are unavailable at deployment, whether the priority queue size and K parameters transfer across architectures, and whether the approach generalizes to non-image modalities where feature geometry may differ.
