## Problem

Existing neural codecs for LiDAR point cloud compression achieve strong rate-distortion performance but remain too slow for real-time use, with state-of-the-art methods like Unicorn still requiring ~2 seconds per 14-bit frame. The paper identifies two specific bottlenecks: time-consuming octree construction for context modeling and multi-stage inference over upsampled voxels that inflates computational cost. RENO targets both to achieve 10 fps encoding/decoding on a single GPU.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw 3D LiDAR point cloud geometry (occupied voxel coordinates only, no color or intensity attributes) quantized to 12-bit (±3.2 cm) or 14-bit (±8 mm) precision, and produces a lossless compressed bitstream via arithmetic coding of sparse occupancy codes. The model is trained on 16-bit quantized samples and operates on the KITTI (Velodyne HDL-64E) and Ford datasets. The reconstructed output is the exact original geometry, with the entire pipeline (preprocessing, network inference, arithmetic coding) completing in ~0.095 s for a 14-bit frame.

## Method

RENO formulates LiDAR geometry compression as the sequential compression of sparse occupancy codes across a multiscale sparse tensor hierarchy, eliminating octree construction entirely. Occupancy codes (integers 1–255 indicating which of 8 child sub-voxels are occupied) are derived by a fixed-weight sparse convolution (kernel 2, stride 2, weights [1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128]) called FOG, and inverted by FCG via binary expansion and pruning. A Target Occupancy Predictor (TOP) network compresses each scale's codes in one shot by conditioning on lower-scale coordinates and occupancy codes, transferring features via direct replication plus octant-based relative position embedding rather than expensive target convolution. The 8-bit occupancy code is split into two 4-bit sub-codes encoded sequentially, reducing GPU-to-CPU memory bandwidth by ~8× during arithmetic coding. The model (1 MB, c32/k3 ResNet + MLP) is trained with cross-entropy loss for 100k steps using TorchSparse++ on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

RENO achieves 12.26% and 12.23% BD-BR savings (D1/D2) over the G-PCCv23 standard on KITTI, and 12.50% and 12.69% on Ford, while operating at 10 fps for 14-bit LiDAR scans (0.095 s encode, 0.090 s decode on a single RTX 3090). Compared to Draco, RENO delivers 48.34% (D1) and 48.30% (D2) bitrate reductions on KITTI. The model size is only 1 MB, and the full pipeline (preprocessing + network inference + arithmetic coding) runs in 0.095 s per 14-bit frame, making it the first real-time neural LiDAR point cloud codec.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RENO outperforms all four baselines on both rate-distortion and speed. Against the G-PCCv23 standard (the strongest compression baseline), RENO saves 12.26% (D1) / 12.23% (D2) bitrate on KITTI and 12.50% / 12.69% on Ford, while encoding 10× faster (0.095 s vs. 0.973 s at 14-bit). Against Draco, RENO achieves roughly 48% BD-BR improvement on KITTI (−48.34% D1) and 45% on Ford (−45.01% D1), at a comparable encoding speed (0.095 s vs. 0.075 s at 14-bit). The range-image-based methods RTST and R-PCC are dominated by wide margins (−90.66% and −84.80% D1 on KITTI, respectively), and they cannot even produce valid 14-bit reconstructions, so their 14-bit runtimes are not reported. In the downstream 3D object detection evaluation (CenterPoint on KITTI), RENO at 12-bit achieves 65.64 mAP, nearly matching the raw-data baseline of 65.97 mAP, while Draco and range-image methods show noticeably lower and less stable detection accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

RENO builds directly on the multiscale sparse tensor representation introduced by SparsePCGC [34] and refined by Unicorn [36], but departs from both by eliminating the multi-stage upsampling-and-inference pipeline that causes their runtime bottleneck. It also departs from the octree-based lineage (G-PCC, OctSqueeze, OctAttention) by replacing tree construction with a fixed-weight sparse convolution (FOG) that produces sparse occupancy codes in one shot, effectively decoupling the occupancy-symbol concept from the tree structure that traditionally generates it. The Target Occupancy Predictor (TOP) with its target-embedding mechanism (feature replication + octant embedding) is a novel cross-scale context module that replaces the more expensive target convolution used in prior sparse-tensor codecs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RENO is the first neural codec to achieve real-time (10 fps) LiDAR geometry compression at 14-bit precision with a 1 MB model, making learned LPCC practically deployable on autonomous-driving hardware for the first time. The 12.25% bitrate savings over the G-PCCv23 standard at comparable quality, combined with 10× lower latency, suggests that the field's assumption that competitive compression requires either complex rule-based entropy coding or multi-stage neural inference may be revisable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to attribute (color/reflectivity) compression, multi-frame temporal coding, and higher-resolution sensors (e.g., 16-bit or beyond), and whether the bitwise two-stage coding strategy generalizes to other occupancy-code sizes.
