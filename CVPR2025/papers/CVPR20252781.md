## Problem

Data-driven video deraining methods trained on synthetic paired data struggle to generalize to real-world rain streaks due to the domain gap between synthetic and authentic rain effects. Additionally, the potential of video deraining to improve downstream vision tasks such as object detection and tracking in real-world rainy conditions remains largely unexplored, with no dedicated real-world benchmark available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252781/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of N rainy frames (cropped to 256×256 patches with a 7-frame sequence length) and outputs the corresponding derained frames. A key input detail is the use of degradation-free optical flow: a multi-frame flow estimator is trained via knowledge distillation to match flows computed by LiteFlowNet3 on clean ground-truth pairs, enabling reliable frame warping without being corrupted by rain. The spatial-only sub-model (VDMamba-S) can also operate on a single rainy image for single-image deraining.

## Method

VDMamba is a dual-branch U-Net where a spatial branch uses Spatial State-Space Model Layers (S3ML) with Mamba's selective scan along the channel dimension for single-frame feature extraction, and a temporal branch uses Temporal State-Space Model Layers (TSML) with SSM along the temporal dimension for multi-frame fusion. The central novelty is the Dynamic Stacking Filter (DSF), which adaptively approximates mean, median, max, and min statistics at each pixel via differentiable softmax-based formulations with learnable per-pixel parameters a and b, enabling pixel-wise selection of the optimal fusion strategy. For semi-supervised training on unpaired real data, a median stacking loss generates pseudo-clean patches from temporally aligned frames, with a sparsity-based masking mechanism (threshold θ=80%) that discards misaligned or moving-object patches to prevent learning from corrupted supervision. The optical flow estimator is trained with a transfer loss to replicate clean-frame flows in a single forward pass, reducing both latency and rain-induced flow errors.

## Results

On the three synthetic video deraining benchmarks, VDMamba achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all datasets: 37.53 dB / 0.9812 on RainSynLight25, 32.89 dB / 0.9425 on RainSynComplex25, and 39.74 dB / 0.9791 on NTURain. On the proposed RVDT real-world benchmark for downstream tasks, VDMamba yields the highest mAP for both YOLOv3 (42.51) and MEGA (46.27) detection, as well as the best tracking scores (IDF1 68.3, MOTA 31.8, MOTP 21.2). The method also achieves real-time inference speed with a compact model size, as illustrated in the complexity comparison (Fig. 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDMamba outperforms all nine baselines on every synthetic deraining benchmark. Against the most recent state-space model baseline RainMamba [54], VDMamba gains +0.79 dB PSNR on RainSynLight25 (37.53 vs 36.74), +0.24 dB on RainSynComplex25 (32.89 vs 32.65), and a substantial +1.87 dB on NTURain (39.74 vs 37.87). The largest margin is over ESTINet [69] on RainSynComplex25, where VDMamba leads by +4.41 dB PSNR (32.89 vs 28.48), indicating superior handling of complex multi-layer rain. Among CNN-based methods, MFGAN [61] is the closest competitor on NTURain (38.92 vs 39.74 PSNR), while DRSformer [8] (a single-image Transformer) trails by 0.81–2.81 dB across the three datasets. On the RVDT downstream benchmark, VDMamba improves mAP1 (YOLOv3) by +0.92 over SLDNet [60] and mAP2 (MEGA) by +2.05 over RainMamba, while also achieving the best tracking MOTA of 31.8 versus 30.7 for SLDNet, confirming that better deraining directly translates to improved detection and tracking in real-world rainy scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDMamba extends the line of state-space model (Mamba) applications to video deraining, most directly building on RainMamba [54], but departs by introducing a dual-branch architecture that separates spatial feature extraction (S3ML) from temporal feature fusion (TSML) rather than applying a single SSM along one axis. The dynamic stacking filter is conceptually rooted in classical stacking/median filtering techniques long used in model-based video deraining (e.g., FastDeRain [28], tensor factorization methods), but the paper's key contribution is deriving differentiable approximations to min, max, and median that unify all four statistical filters under a single learnable parameterization. The semi-supervised median stacking loss draws on the sparsity prior of rain and the self-supervised video deraining literature (SLDNet [60], S2VD [66]), but applies it specifically to generate pseudo-clean patches from unpaired real-world data via masked temporal median.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically critical gap: synthetic-to-real generalization in video deraining, which limits deployment in autonomous driving and surveillance. The RVDT benchmark is the first to systematically evaluate how deraining quality translates to downstream object detection and tracking performance on real-world rainy videos, providing a much-needed evaluation axis beyond PSNR/SSIM. However, the semi-supervised component still requires an initial paired-data training phase, and the median-stacking pseudo-label strategy may struggle in scenes with heavy motion or dense rain. Open questions include whether the SSM-based architecture truly scales advantageously over attention-based methods at higher resolutions, and whether the dynamic stacking filter's differentiable approximations remain stable at extreme temperature values during training.
