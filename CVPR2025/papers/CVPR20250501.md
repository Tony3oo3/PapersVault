## Problem

One-shot codebook-based dehazing methods (e.g., RIDCP) perform a single nearest-neighbor code matching pass, which fails to exploit the spatial variation in haze degradation: thin-haze regions contain more recoverable information than dense-haze regions, yet a single mapping cannot progressively leverage already-restored clear areas to guide restoration of harder regions. This leads to over-recovery in thin haze and under-recovery in dense haze, particularly in inhomogeneous real-world scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single real-world hazy RGB image of arbitrary resolution as input and produces a dehazed RGB image. During training, paired hazy–clean images are synthesized following the RIDCP multi-factor degradation pipeline, with the VQGAN codebook pretrained on 512×512 patches from DIV2K and Flickr2K. At inference, the hazy image is encoded into latent tokens, iteratively decoded over T steps (default T=8), and the final code sequence is decoded back to pixel space by the VQGAN decoder.

## Method

IPC-Dehaze replaces one-shot nearest-neighbor code matching with an iterative Predictor-Critic loop over a pretrained VQGAN codebook. The Code-Predictor (a 4× RSTB transformer) takes fused tokens—hazy tokens at masked positions and clean tokens at unmasked positions—and predicts a probability distribution over all codebook entries; it is trained with cross-entropy against ground-truth codes. The Code-Critic (a 2× RSTB transformer) evaluates inter-code dependencies in the predicted sequence and outputs a per-token rejection probability, trained with binary cross-entropy against a label indicating whether each sampled code matches the ground truth. At each inference iteration, the Code-Critic's mask determines which codes are finalized and which are resampled, with the mask ratio following a cosine schedule so that easy (thin-haze) regions are locked in early and guide subsequent predictions for harder (dense-haze) regions. A Spatial Feature Transform (SFT) module aligns the hazy-domain encoder features with the clean-domain codebook space.

## Results

IPC-Dehaze achieves the best score on 14 out of 16 evaluated metrics across three real-world datasets (RTTS, Fattal, URHI). On RTTS, it reaches MUSIQ 59.60, TOPIQ 0.500, and CLIPIQA 0.44, surpassing the next-best method RIDCP by 4.37, 0.088, and 0.14 respectively. On Fattal, it attains MUSIQ 66.22 and TOPIQ 0.63, and on URHI it scores MUSIQ 62.5 and Q-Align 3.70. The only metrics where it does not rank first are PI on Fattal (2.41 vs. RIDCP's 2.37) and PI on URHI (3.08 vs. RIDCP's 2.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPC-Dehaze outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of metrics across all three datasets. The closest competitor is RIDCP [38], the prior codebook-based one-shot method; IPC-Dehaze beats it by 4.37 MUSIQ points on RTTS (59.60 vs. 55.23) and by 0.088 TOPIQ (0.500 vs. 0.412), but RIDCP retains a slight edge on the perceptual PI metric for Fattal (2.37 vs. 2.41) and URHI (2.87 vs. 3.08). KA-Net [15] is the second-closest baseline on several metrics (e.g., Fattal Q-Align 3.982 vs. 4.234, URHI MUSIQ 58.57 vs. 62.5) but is consistently outperformed. GAN-based methods (DAD, D4) and direct-restoration methods (MSBDN, Dehamer, DEA-Net) trail significantly, with D4 scoring the lowest on most metrics (e.g., RTTS MUSIQ 49.33, Fattal TOPIQ 0.41), confirming the domain-gap limitation of synthetic-data-trained approaches. PSD [10] shows moderate performance but suffers from overexposure and color shifting noted qualitatively. Overall, the iterative Predictor-Critic mechanism yields consistent gains of 1–5 points over the best one-shot baseline (RIDCP) across perceptual, semantic, and LLM-based IQA metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the codebook-prior paradigm for real-world dehazing introduced by RIDCP, which performs a single-shot code prediction from a pre-trained VQGAN. The key departure is the adoption of an iterative masked-token decoding framework inspired by MaskGIT, adapted from image generation to the LQ-to-HQ restoration setting, where low-quality features condition the prediction. The Code-Critic module draws on the token-critic idea from Lezama et al. to evaluate inter-token dependencies rather than treating each code independently, addressing a limitation the authors identify in both MaskGIT's independent sampling and RIDCP's one-shot matching. The SFT module is repurposed from face restoration and super-resolution literature to align features between hazy and clean domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iterative easy-to-hard decoding strategy directly addresses a practical limitation of one-shot dehazing methods: inhomogeneous haze where thin and dense regions coexist, causing over- or under-recovery. The predictor-critic mechanism is a generalizable design pattern that could transfer to other VQGAN-based restoration tasks (super-resolution, face restoration) where iterative refinement is desirable. However, the evaluation is limited to no-reference IQA metrics due to the absence of real-world ground truth, making it difficult to assess perceptual fidelity beyond learned scores. Open questions include whether the fixed number of iterations (T=8) can be made adaptive per image, and whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or video dehazing without prohibitive computational cost.
