## Problem

Existing fine-tuning-based concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models degrade the fidelity of remaining concepts when the target concept appears in a localized region of an image, because they modify the entire image rather than only the affected area. This reduces overall generation capability, especially when target and remaining concepts coexist in a single prompt. The paper introduces the framework of localized concept erasure—erasing only the specific image region containing the target while preserving surrounding regions—and provides a training-free solution.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained diffusion model (SD v1.4 or SD v3) and requires only a small number of inference-time image generations (no gradient updates) to compute the low-rank matrices and gate parameters for each target concept. At generation time, the modified model takes a standard text prompt and produces an image where the target concept's localized region is replaced by a mapping concept (e.g., 'George Clooney' → 'a person') while all other regions remain faithful to the prompt. The module is injected at the output of selected layers (e.g., Up-Block layers) and modifies image token embeddings of dimension 640 (SD v1.4) during the DDIM denoising process.

## Method

GLoCE injects a lightweight module at layer outputs consisting of a closed-form low-rank projection and a sigmoid gate, with all parameters determined by inference-only adaptation from a few generated images. The projection P* = η·V̂^map(V̂^map)^T(I − V̂^tar(V̂^tar)^T) first removes the principal components of the target concept (via V̂^tar from PCA of target embeddings) and then maps the residual onto the subspace of a related mapping concept (via V̂^map), with a bias b* shifting the result toward the mapping concept mean. The gate s(X) = σ(α*(‖V*(X−β*)‖²−γ*)) selectively activates only for target-concept tokens by using a low-rank orthonormal basis V* derived from residual embeddings (target minus a surrogate mean), with the threshold γ* set relative to anchor concepts to preserve remaining concepts. The max-over-tokens formulation p(X) handles the localized nature of the target, and all hyperparameters (ranks, η, τ₁) are fixed per task, making the entire procedure training-free and scalable to mass concept erasure.

## Results

On 50-celebrity erasure, GLoCE achieves 0.95% target detection accuracy (vs. 3.29% for MACE and 91.35% for the unmodified SD v1.4) while preserving 88.38% remaining-celebrity accuracy and 31.33 CLIP score on COCO-30K, closely matching the original model's 90.86% and 31.34. For explicit content erasure, GLoCE reduces NudeNet detections from 743 (SD v1.4) to just 8, outperforming the next-best method RECE (89) by over 10×. Against adversarial attacks, GLoCE achieves 0.00% attack success rate on Ring-A-Bell and 39.44% on UnlearnDiff, compared to 4.26% and 66.90% for MACE respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLoCE outperforms all five baselines on both efficacy and specificity simultaneously. Against MACE (the strongest prior method), GLoCE reduces target detection from 3.29% to 0.95% while improving remaining-celebrity accuracy from 84.64% to 88.38% and KID from 0.23 to 0.08. UCE achieves the lowest target detection (0.09%) but catastrophically degrades remaining concepts (34.42% Acc_r, 97.09 FID on COCO-30K), while ESD variants and FMN show poor preservation across all domains. On explicit content erasure, GLoCE detects only 8 explicit items versus 111 for MACE and 89 for RECE, a >10× improvement. For adversarial robustness, GLoCE achieves 0.00% ASR against Ring-A-Bell (vs. 4.26% for MACE) and 39.44% against UnlearnDiff (vs. 66.90% for MACE and 65.46% for RECE), making it the most robust method tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLoCE builds directly on LEACE's linear guardedness framework for closed-form concept erasure, but replaces the full-rank projection with low-rank PCA-based matrices to make the approach computationally efficient and amenable to inference-only parameter determination. It departs from the fine-tuning-based concept erasure line (ESD, UCE, MACE, RECE) by requiring no gradient updates whatsoever—parameters are set from a handful of forward passes. The paper also introduces a gate mechanism that selectively activates the erasure projection only on target-concept tokens, a design not present in prior erasure methods that apply transformations uniformly across all embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of GLoCE makes concept erasure dramatically more accessible: practitioners can erase concepts from a pre-trained diffusion model without any fine-tuning infrastructure, and the method generalizes across architectures (U-Net SD v1.4 and DiT-based SD v3). The localized erasure formulation addresses a practical gap—prior specificity evaluations only tested prompts without target concepts, masking fidelity degradation when target and remaining concepts coexist. Open questions include whether the low-rank covariance assumption holds for highly abstract or compositional concepts, whether the 39% UnlearnDiff attack success rate can be further reduced, and how the approach scales to erasing thousands of concepts simultaneously without parameter interference.
