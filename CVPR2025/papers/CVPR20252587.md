## Problem

Existing multi-view tensor clustering methods apply the tensor rotation trick before t-SVD-based regularization, which inadvertently encodes the sequential (label) information of sorted data samples via FFT along the sample dimension, violating the unsupervised learning setting. Additionally, large-scale multi-view clustering approaches based on matrix factorization or anchor techniques fail to capture pairwise similarities among all data samples, limiting further performance gains.

## Input / Output

The method takes V multi-view data matrices {X_v ∈ R^{D_v × N}} and a target cluster number K as input. Crucially, the pairwise linear kernel matrices K_v = X_v^T X_v are never materialized explicitly to avoid O(N²) storage; instead, the product X_v^T X_v H_v^T H_v is computed on the fly. The output is a set of cluster assignments obtained by applying k-means to the vertical concatenation of the learned K×N latent representations across all views.

## Method

The core insight is to remove the tensor rotation trick so that FFT in t-SVD operates along the view dimension (size V) rather than the sample dimension (size N), eliminating inadvertent label leakage while reducing t-TNN computation from O(VN log N) to O(V log V · N). The method formulates an objective that maximizes alignment between each view's implicit linear kernel and its latent representation H_v (with orthogonal rows on the Stiefel manifold), while minimizing the t-TNN of the stacked tensor H ∈ R^{K×N×V} to capture cross-view low-rank structure. Optimization proceeds via Augmented Lagrange Multipliers with three alternating subproblems: (1) H_v update via SVD-based Stiefel manifold projection, (2) auxiliary tensor G update via t-SVD soft-thresholding in the Fourier domain, and (3) Lagrange multiplier update. The overall algorithm achieves O(tN) linear complexity in the number of samples, making it scalable to datasets with over 100,000 samples.

## Results

The proposed LMTC method achieves the best ACC on 6 out of 7 datasets, with headline values of 82.97% on ORL, 94.00% on HW, 69.07% on ALOI, and 74.80% on DryBean. It also attains the highest NMI across all seven benchmarks (e.g., 91.60 on ORL, 79.79 on ALOI, 22.26 on YtVideo) and the best Purity on all datasets. The only notable exception is a 1.35% ACC gap to EOMSC on YtVideo (25.31 vs. 26.66), where EOMSC's NMI is infeasible (0.24). LMTC requires only 1 hyperparameter (λ) versus 2–6 for most competing methods, and runs in linear time (e.g., 3383 s on 101K YtVideo samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMTC outperforms all four tensor-based baselines (ASR-ETR, S²MVTC, TBGL, Orth-NTF) by large margins on every dataset where they complete; for example, on ORL it gains +0.22% ACC over ASR-ETR (82.97 vs. 82.75) and +43.27% over Orth-NTF (82.97 vs. 39.70), while TBGL and Orth-NTF fail (OT/Error) on most large-scale datasets. Against the matrix-factorization and anchor-based methods (RMKC, BMVC, LMSC, OPMC, EOMSC, MCHBG), LMTC leads on ACC for 6/7 datasets, with the sole exception being YtVideo where EOMSC edges it by 1.35% (26.66 vs. 25.31) but produces an infeasible NMI of 0.24. OPMC is the only baseline that beats LMTC on any metric—achieving 35.37 NMI vs. 32.84 on BDGP—but at 24× the runtime (95.27 s vs. 3.90 s). In terms of efficiency, LMTC is competitive with the fastest methods (e.g., 2.91 s on HW vs. 1.44 s for EOMSC) while substantially outperforming them in accuracy, and it avoids the OT/OM failures that plague TBGL, MCHBG, and Orth-NTF on datasets with >10K samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the t-SVD/tensor nuclear norm framework (Kilmer et al., 2013) and the pioneer multi-view tensor clustering paradigm of Xie et al. (2018), which stacks view-specific representations into a unified tensor and regularizes with t-TNN. It departs from this lineage by identifying that the ubiquitous tensor rotation trick (shiftdim) combined with FFT-based t-SVD inadvertently encodes sequential/label information, violating the unsupervised setting. For the large-scale setting, it moves away from the dominant matrix factorization and anchor-based approaches (e.g., OPMC, LMSC) toward implicit linear kernels that capture all pairwise similarities without explicit N×N matrix computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inadvertent label use finding is significant because it implies that a large body of published multi-view tensor clustering results may be inflated, and the field should re-evaluate reported performances on shuffled data. The proposed LMTC method provides a clean, truly unsupervised alternative with linear complexity and only one hyperparameter, making it practical for large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the sequential-information leakage generalizes to other FFT-based tensor operations beyond multi-view clustering, and whether the performance gap between sorted and shuffled settings can be further closed with alternative tensor regularizers that avoid FFT on the sample dimension.
