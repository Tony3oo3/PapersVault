## Problem

Existing text-to-4D generation methods rely on neural deformation fields that predict displacements at every query point in space-time, introducing a very large number of degrees of freedom that makes optimization difficult and produces shape-inconsistent, physically implausible motions (e.g., foot-skating, ground penetration, blurry limbs). Meanwhile, skeleton-based animation offers low-DoF control and physical grounding but requires extensive manual authoring. The paper asks whether the motion knowledge in large video diffusion models can be distilled into a skeleton-based representation that preserves structural integrity while enabling physically plausible articulated motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a rigged 3D asset (a mesh with a manually embedded articulated skeleton, typically generated by an off-the-shelf text-to-3D system like TetSplatting) and a text prompt describing the desired motion. The asset is converted to a dual mesh–3DGS representation where the mesh provides skinning weights and the 3DGS supports differentiable deformation and rendering. The output is a per-frame sequence of 3-DoF compound spherical joint angles for each bone plus a 6-DoF root-bone rigid transformation over F frames; optionally, this trajectory is further projected onto a physics-grounded solution via a PD-controlled rigid-body simulator, yielding a physically plausible tracked motion.

## Method

AKD parameterizes motion as a sequence of joint angles in an articulated skeleton rather than per-point deformations, reducing the optimization space from all query points in space-time to a handful of joints. The 3DGS asset is deformed via Linear Blend Skinning (with weights transferred to Gaussian kernels by barycentric interpolation), passed through differentiable Forward Kinematics, and rasterized into a video. Score Distillation Sampling is adapted for the v-prediction formulation of CogVideoX-5B, with the SDS gradient computed in latent space and backpropagated through the VAE encoder (using gradient checkpointing to manage memory) into the pixel space and then to joint angles. The total loss combines SDS with a temporal smoothness penalty (MAE of the Laplacian of joint angles) and a ground-penetration penalty on bone-cuboid vertices; a checkerboard ground rendered via ray casting provides the video model with cues about character–ground interaction. For physical grounding, the distilled joint trajectory is tracked in a semi-implicit articulated rigid-body simulator (Warp) using a PD controller, with fine-grained gradient clipping applied every few tens of backward steps to prevent gradient explosion over the long unrolled simulation.

## Results

On the VideoPhy automated evaluation, AKD achieves a Semantic Adherence (SA) score of 0.81 ± 0.26 and a Physical Commonsense (PC) score of 0.39 ± 0.17, substantially outperforming TC4D (SA 0.40 ± 0.34, PC 0.31 ± 0.15). In a user study with 20 evaluators comparing 29 asset pairs, AKD was preferred in 51% of comparisons for Motion Amount, 53% for Physical Plausibility, and 53% for Text Alignment. Each asset requires approximately 25 hours of SDS optimization (10,000 iterations) on a single NVIDIA A100 40GB GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline, TC4D [2], the state-of-the-art open-source text-to-4D method capable of synthesizing long-trajectory motions. AKD wins decisively on both automated VideoPhy metrics: SA more than doubles (0.81 vs. 0.40) and PC improves by 0.08 (0.39 vs. 0.31). In the user study, AKD is preferred in a majority of comparisons across all three subjective criteria (Motion Amount 51%, Physical Plausibility 53%, Text Alignment 53%), though the margins are modest. Qualitatively, TC4D frequently fails to produce alternating leg movements and exhibits blurry artifacts where limbs converge, while AKD's low-DoF skeleton parameterization preserves geometric structure of each body part. The comparison is conducted without physics-based tracking for fairness, meaning AKD's full pipeline (with physics grounding) would be expected to perform even better on physical plausibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

AKD positions itself at the intersection of text-to-4D generation (TC4D, 4D-Fy) and traditional skeleton-based animation pipelines. Where prior text-to-4D methods rely on high-DoF neural deformation fields to warp 3D shapes, AKD replaces this with a low-DoF articulated skeleton using Linear Blend Skinning on 3D Gaussian Splatting, drawing on the deformation convention from PhysGaussian. The distillation mechanism adapts Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion) to the v-prediction formulation of CogVideoX-5B, and the physics-based tracking stage borrows from differentiable rigid-body simulation (Warp) in the spirit of PhysGaussian and PhysDreamer, but applied to articulated rather than volumetric dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By constraining the optimization to joint angles rather than per-point displacements, AKD addresses a core failure mode of text-to-4D methods (shape inconsistency, blurry limbs) while retaining the motion diversity of large video diffusion priors. The physics-based tracking post-processing offers a path toward physically grounded animation without requiring the video model to encode fine-grained contact dynamics. Key limitations include the manual rigging step, dependence on asset visual quality, and restriction to articulated (not soft-body) motion; scaling to diverse character types will require automatic rigging (e.g., RigNet) and more efficient video-model sampling. The authors also note potential for generating labeled data for robotics, suggesting a broader impact beyond graphics.
