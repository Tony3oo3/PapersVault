## Problem

GANs are the dominant fast-inference architecture for image generation but have fallen out of favor for text-to-image synthesis due to difficulty scaling up model capacity, since the feedforward generator must fit within limited GPU memory. No open-sourced large-scale GAN-based T2I model exists in the community, leaving the potential of GANs for this task underexplored and hindering downstream research on latent-space manipulation and 3D-aware synthesis.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language text prompt (encoded by OpenCLIP ConvNeXt-XXLarge into a global token and a per-word token sequence) and a 128-dimensional latent code z. The base generator produces a 64×64 RGB image, which is then upscaled to 512×512 by a separate text-conditioned super-resolution network. The text condition is consumed at two levels: the global token is concatenated with z through a mapping MLP to produce a disentangled code w that modulates all convolution and self-attention layers, while the token sequence drives cross-attention for fine-grained text-image alignment.

## Method

Aurora replaces the single FFN in each attention block of a StyleGAN-style generator with a pool of N expert FFNs and a sparse router that selects exactly one expert per spatial feature point. Unlike standard sparse MoE routers that condition only on the input feature, Aurora's router also receives the text-integrated latent code w, enabling routing decisions that account for both the text condition and sampling stochasticity. The two-stage pipeline first trains a 986M-parameter base generator at 64×64 (using adversarial, matching-aware, multi-level CLIP, and MoE balance losses on 120M public text-image pairs), then trains a U-Net upsampler with a single expert to refine outputs to 512×512, fine-tuning it on synthesized images to close the real-to-generated gap. The architecture uses modulated transformation modules (MTM) for convolution, ℓ2-distance attention for stability, and output skip connections at every resolution for multi-scale supervision.

## Results

At 64×64 resolution, Aurora achieves a zero-shot FID₃₀ₖ of 6.20 on MS COCO, outperforming all compared methods including diffusion models (eDiff-I: 7.60, Stable Diffusion: 8.40) and prior GANs (StyleGAN-T: 7.30, GigaGAN: 9.18 FID₁₀ₖ). At 256×256 resolution, Aurora achieves a zero-shot FID₃₀ₖ of 8.74, the best among GAN-based methods (next best: GigaGAN at 9.03), though it trails the strongest diffusion and autoregressive models (eDiff-I: 6.95, Parti-20B: 7.23, Imagen: 7.27). Inference speed is 0.16 s per image, substantially faster than diffusion-based baselines (2.9–32 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 64×64, Aurora (FID₃₀ₖ = 6.20) beats every baseline: it surpasses the best diffusion model eDiff-I (7.60) by 1.4 FID, Stable Diffusion (8.40) by 2.2 FID, and the best prior GAN StyleGAN-T (7.30) by 1.1 FID. At 256×256, Aurora (8.74) is the top GAN, edging out GigaGAN (9.03) by 0.29 FID and substantially outperforming StyleGAN-T (13.90), GALIP (12.54), and LAFITE (26.94). However, Aurora trails the strongest diffusion and autoregressive models at 256×256: eDiff-I (6.95), Parti-20B (7.23), and Imagen (7.27) all achieve lower FID, with eDiff-I leading by 1.79 FID. Notably, Aurora achieves its 256×256 result with only 0.12B training images versus 3–15B for the diffusion baselines, and at 0.16 s inference it is roughly 18× faster than Stable Diffusion (2.9 s) and 200× faster than eDiff-I (32 s). The paper attributes the remaining gap to diffusion/AR models as needing more powerful super-resolution or larger model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

Aurora extends the StyleGAN/GigaGAN lineage of text-conditioned GANs by transplanting the sparse mixture-of-experts mechanism from Switch Transformers into the generator's feedforward layers, replacing the standard FFN with a pool of N experts selected per feature point. The key architectural departure from prior MoE work is that the router conditions on both the local feature and the text-integrated global latent code w, rather than routing solely on the input representation, making the expert selection text-aware. The two-stage pipeline (64×64 base generator + 512×512 U-Net upsampler) follows the GigaGAN/Imagen recipe, while the convolution block inherits MTM and modulated convolution from StyleGAN2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Aurora is the first publicly released large-scale GAN for open-vocabulary T2I, providing the community with a fast-inference (0.16 s) alternative to diffusion models that maintains competitive FID (8.74 at 512×512) and a well-structured latent space amenable to editing and 3D-aware extensions. The paper's empirical finding that text conditioning largely eliminates the training instability historically associated with GANs challenges a long-standing assumption in the field. Open questions remain: the model still lags behind the best diffusion and autoregressive systems at 256×512, struggles with multi-object and fine-grained scenes, and it is unclear whether the discriminator (borrowed unchanged from GigaGAN) is the bottleneck or whether further MoE scaling of the generator alone can close the remaining gap.
