## Problem

Existing indoor scene texture synthesis methods face a fundamental trade-off: per-view inpainting approaches (e.g., Text2Tex) produce cross-view inconsistencies and visible seams, while optimization-based methods (e.g., SceneTex) achieve consistency at the cost of extreme computational overhead (over 43 hours per scene) and training instability. No prior method simultaneously delivers high-fidelity, globally consistent texture for entire indoor scenes with practical generation times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250880/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D indoor scene mesh (with UV atlas generated via Xatlas) and a holistic text prompt describing the desired room style, and produces a complete UV-space texture map for the entire scene. Internally, N predefined cameras are placed around the room center, and depth maps are rendered per view to condition a depth-aware SDXL model via ControlNet. The two-stage pipeline first generates a global texture map, then refines it per-instance to fill occluded regions, with all rendering and projection handled by PyTorch3D.

## Method

RoomPainter is a zero-shot, training-free framework that adapts a standard 2D text-to-image diffusion model (SDXL) for 3D-consistent indoor texturing through a two-stage strategy. Stage 1 (MVIS) performs multi-view integrated sampling: at each DDPM denoising step, all N view latents are decoded, back-projected into UV space, and dynamically merged into a single global texture map using view-weighted blending (with time-varying sharpness), then re-rendered and re-encoded to replace the per-view clean estimates, enforcing cross-view consistency throughout sampling. Stage 2 (MVRS) applies a per-instance repaint variant that combines already-painted regions with the ongoing MVIS sampling via an inpainting mask, filling occlusion holes while preserving the global style. A Related View-based Attention mechanism modifies the U-Net self-attention to concatenate keys and values from adjacent views (room-scale) or all views (instance-scale), enabling information sharing across viewpoints without any additional training.

## Results

On 10 scenes from 3D-FRONT (5 bedrooms, 5 living rooms) across 5 text styles, RoomPainter achieves the highest CLIP Score (23.47) and Aesthetic Score (5.03), outperforming all baselines on both quality metrics. In terms of efficiency, RoomPainter completes texture generation in 46 minutes, which is approximately 57× faster than SceneTex (2614.50 min) and 1.5× faster than Text2Tex-C (70.75 min), though slower than the per-view Text2Tex-H (8.50 min) which suffers from severe occlusion artifacts. Ablation results confirm that removing MVRS drops the Aesthetic Score from 5.03 to 4.40, highlighting its critical role in handling occluded regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoomPainter outperforms both Text2Tex variants and SceneTex on both quality metrics. Against Text2Tex-H (holistic prompt, 12 views), RoomPainter gains +1.89 CLIP and +0.69 AS, while also resolving the large occluded black regions and visible seams that plague Text2Tex-H. Against Text2Tex-C (per-instance prompts), RoomPainter improves CLIP by +1.54 and AS by +0.18, and is 1.5× faster (46 min vs. 70.75 min), since Text2Tex-C requires separate viewpoint selection and texture optimization per instance. Against SceneTex, RoomPainter achieves +1.60 CLIP and +0.28 AS while being roughly 57× faster (46 min vs. 2614.50 min); SceneTex's optimization-based VSD approach and LoRA training account for its extreme runtime, and its cross-attention mechanism for occluded regions still produces noticeable blurriness. The ablation rows further show that the Related View-based Attention and MVIS contribute modestly (~0.15 CLIP each), while MVRS is the most impactful component, with its removal causing a 0.63 drop in AS due to unresolved occlusion artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoomPainter sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: per-view inpainting methods (Text2Tex, TEXTure) that are fast but suffer from cross-view inconsistencies and seams, and optimization-based methods (SceneTex, SDS/VSD variants) that enforce global consistency at the cost of hours of training. The paper's core technical contribution is a zero-shot, training-free adaptation of a 2D diffusion model to 3D-consistent texture synthesis, drawing on the dynamic merge formulation from Meta 3D TextureGen and synchronized multi-view diffusion, and the Repaint inpainting mechanism, but reorganizing them into a two-stage pipeline (MVIS for global texturing, MVRS for per-instance refinement) with a Related View-based Attention module borrowed from style-aligned generation. Unlike prior work that either treats the room as a single rotating object or optimizes an implicit texture field, RoomPainter explicitly decouples global style consistency from local occlusion handling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 57× speedup over SceneTex (46 min vs. 2614 min) while simultaneously improving CLIP and Aesthetic scores makes high-quality indoor texturing practically accessible without GPU-cluster-scale optimization, which is significant for VR, digital media, and creative-arts pipelines. The zero-shot design (no LoRA training, no per-scene optimization) lowers the barrier to adoption, though the method remains tied to a specific 2D diffusion backbone (SDXL + ControlNet) and a fixed set of predefined cameras. Open questions include whether the two-stage MVIS/MVRS strategy generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, how it scales with the number of instances and camera views, and whether the Related View-based Attention can be replaced by learned multi-view consistency modules without sacrificing the training-free property.
