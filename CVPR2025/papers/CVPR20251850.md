## Problem

Hybrid neural fields (e.g., Instant NGP) trained to represent 3D shapes as signed distance fields produce extremely noisy derivatives when computed via automatic differentiation, because their high-resolution spatial grids capture high-frequency noise that differentiation amplifies proportionally to frequency. This causes visible grainy artifacts in rendering, incorrect trajectories in collision simulation, and diverging solutions in PDE solvers, limiting the practical utility of hybrid neural fields in derivative-dependent downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained hybrid neural field (Instant NGP, Dense Grid, or Tri-plane) and a query point in continuous 3D space. At each query, it samples k=256 points from a Gaussian neighborhood N(q, σ) centered at the query, queries the field at those points, and solves a closed-form least-squares polynomial fit. The output is an accurate gradient vector (surface normal) or Hessian/Laplacian (curvature) at the query point. The fine-tuning variant additionally outputs a modified neural field whose own autodiff gradients are accurate, eliminating the need for the external operator in downstream pipelines.

## Method

The core operator replaces direct autodiff derivatives with derivatives of a local low-degree polynomial approximation: for first-order derivatives, it fits a linear model y ≈ gᵀx + b via least squares over k Gaussian-sampled neighbors, yielding the gradient as g; for second-order derivatives, it fits a quadratic model with a symmetric Hessian parameterized by its lower triangle, yielding the Laplacian as the trace. The smoothing scale σ and neighbor count k are the only hyperparameters, trading off fidelity against noise robustness. To avoid requiring downstream pipelines to adopt the new operator, the paper proposes a self-supervised fine-tuning loss that combines a consistency term (preserving the pre-trained field's zeroth-order output) with a gradient-alignment term that penalizes the L2 difference between the fine-tuned field's autodiff gradients and the polynomial-fit gradients of the frozen pre-trained field. This approach is architecture-agnostic, operates at arbitrary continuous-space points (unlike grid-based numerical differentiation), and solves the polynomial fit in closed form without iterative optimization.

## Results

On the FamousShape dataset, the polynomial-fitting operator achieves a 4× reduction in angular error for surface normals compared to autodiff (2.80° vs 12.40° for Instant NGP) and a 4× reduction in mean curvature error (RRE 0.89 vs 3.67) compared to finite differences. For the fine-tuning approach, autodiff gradients improve from 12.40° to 3.19° angular error on Instant NGP while preserving the zero-level set (Chamfer Distance changes by <0.01×10⁻⁴). The method generalizes across three hybrid architectures (Instant NGP, Dense Grid, Tri-plane) with consistent improvements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GAD baseline (Fischer & Ritschel [19], Gaussian averaging of autodiff derivatives), the polynomial-fitting operator achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy: on Instant NGP, Ours reaches 2.80° angular error vs GAD's 2.99°, and 42.92% AA@1 vs 38.35%. Against the stochastic finite differences baselines (Deliot et al. [16]), the proposed method is dramatically superior: SFD alone produces 57.67° angular error (worse than raw autodiff), and even SFD₂₅₆ with 256 Monte Carlo samples only reaches 6.30°, more than 2× worse than Ours. In the fine-tuning comparison (Table 2), the proposed method outperforms eikonal regularization (Atzmon & Lipman [2], Gropp et al. [22]) by roughly 2× in angular error (3.19° vs 6.51° on Instant NGP) and substantially outperforms the finite-differences eikonal variant (Li et al. [28], FD-Eikonal) which barely improves over no regularization (12.46° vs 12.40° baseline). The fine-tuning also preserves geometry fidelity, with Chamfer Distance and F-Score remaining nearly unchanged from the pre-trained field.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on classical signal processing (smoothing before differentiation) and Moving Least Squares polynomial fitting from shape analysis, repurposing them for the specific setting of hybrid neural fields where high-resolution spatial grids introduce high-frequency noise that autodiff amplifies. It departs from eikonal regularization (Atzmon & Lipman, Gropp et al.) and Neuralangelo's finite-differences regularizer (Li et al.), which target training dynamics or smoothness constraints but do not address the high-frequency grid noise inherent to hybrid representations. The differentiable rasterization literature (Deliot et al., Fischer & Ritschel) tackles noisy derivative estimation via Monte Carlo or Gaussian smoothing, but is limited to 2D rendering contexts; this paper generalizes the idea to arbitrary 3D (or higher-dimensional) query points on pre-trained hybrid fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As hybrid neural fields (Instant NGP, Tri-plane) become the dominant representation for large-scale 3D scenes, the finding that their autodiff derivatives are fundamentally unreliable threatens every downstream pipeline that depends on gradients—rendering, physics simulation, PDE solving. The post-hoc polynomial-fitting operator is architecture-agnostic and requires no retraining, while the fine-tuning approach makes standard autodiff work correctly without modifying downstream code. Key limitations include the need to tune the neighborhood scale σ per application, the cost of multiple forward passes per query, and the fact that the method only addresses high-frequency noise, leaving lower-frequency approximation errors in non-hybrid architectures (e.g., SIREN) unaddressed. Open questions include adaptive σ selection, sample sharing across neighboring queries, and whether the fine-tuning approach transfers to other modalities beyond SDFs.
