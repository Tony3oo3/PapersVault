## Problem

Diffusion paths in generative models are manually predetermined (e.g., √α̅ₜ and √(1−α̅ₜ) for DDIM, 1−t and t for Flow Matching) and have never been optimized, despite directly influencing generation quality. Schrödinger Bridge methods can theoretically find optimal transport paths but are computationally prohibitive in high-dimensional image spaces because they train complex networks to predict full image states globally. This paper asks whether one can find a *local* Schrödinger bridge within the low-dimensional subspace of path weights, making the optimization tractable without retraining the denoising network.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained denoising network (DDIM or Flow Matching) and Gaussian noise as input, and produces both optimized per-step path weights A(i), B(i) ∈ (0,1) and the final generated images. The KAN that predicts these weights is under 0.1 MB (e.g., 0.040 MB for 5-step paths, 0.091 MB for 20-step paths), with architecture [1,4,16,4,1] and spline grid size set to 2× the total steps. Experiments are conducted at 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 64×64 (CelebA), and 256×256 (CelebA-HQ) resolutions, evaluating at 5, 10, and 20 NFE.

## Method

LDSB reparameterizes the diffusion path x_t = f_A(t)x_0 + f_B(t)ε into per-step multiplicative weights A(i) and B(i) (so f_A(t)=∏A(i), f_B(t)=∏B(i)), defining a path subspace P_{A,B} that captures the monotonic structure and inter-step correlations of all standard diffusion schedules. Within this subspace, it applies an IPF-style alternating optimization: the reverse path π^{2n+1} is fitted to the forward path π^{2n} by minimizing per-step L2 distance using the pre-trained denoiser's noise prediction, and vice versa for π^{2n+2}, with closed-form targets for each A(t) and B(t) at every iteration. A Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) with learnable spline activations predicts the path weights, chosen over MLP for its local plasticity (preventing catastrophic forgetting during step-by-step optimization) and inherently continuous output (matching the smooth transition required by diffusion paths). The entire optimization runs for L=3 IPF iterations with the denoising network frozen, reducing FID by up to 48.5% (10-step DDIM on CIFAR-10) while the KAN adds less than 0.1 MB of parameters.

## Results

LDSB reduces FID by 25–48% for DDIM and 15–39% for Flow Matching across 5, 10, and 20 NFE settings, using only a <0.1 MB KAN to optimize diffusion path weights while keeping the pre-trained denoising network frozen. The headline result is on CIFAR-10 with DDIM: 10-step LDSB achieves FID 9.619, beating the original 20-step DDIM (FID 11.080) by 13.2%. On CelebA-HQ with Flow Matching, 5-step LDSB cuts FID from 115.177 to 70.106 (39.13% reduction). The KAN-based optimization outperforms an MLP baseline (e.g., 26.929 vs. 66.884 FID at 5 NFE on CIFAR-10), confirming the importance of KAN's resistance to catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDIM (Song et al., 2020), LDSB consistently wins on FID across all datasets and NFE settings, with the largest gain of 48.50% (18.678→9.619) at 10 NFE on CIFAR-10; notably, 10-step DDIM+LDSB (FID 9.619) surpasses 20-step DDIM (FID 11.080), demonstrating that path optimization can compensate for fewer sampling steps. Against Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023), LDSB also wins uniformly, with the strongest improvement of 39.13% (115.177→70.106) at 5 NFE on CelebA-HQ, where the original FM produces heavily blurred, gender-ambiguous images that LDSB corrects. Against DSB (De Bortoli et al., 2021), the comparison is limited to a 2D checkerboard toy experiment at 20 NFE, where both DDIM+LDSB and FM+LDSB produce more accurate checkerboard patterns with fewer error points than DSB. The KAN architecture is validated against an MLP baseline (Table 2), where KAN achieves substantially lower FID (e.g., 26.929 vs. 66.884 at 5 NFE on CIFAR-10), confirming that KAN's local plasticity and continuous output are critical for iterative path optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions the Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (DSB) framework by restricting the path search from the full high-dimensional image space to a low-dimensional subspace parameterized by the diffusion path weights f_A(t) and f_B(t), which encompasses DDIM, Flow Matching, and other standard diffusion formulations. Rather than training two complex image-to-image networks as DSB and S-DSB do, LDSB uses a sub-0.1MB Kolmogorov-Arnold Network to predict the per-step weights A(i) and B(i), leveraging KAN's local plasticity (resistance to catastrophic forgetting during step-by-step IPF optimization) and continuous spline outputs (matching the smoothness requirement of diffusion trajectories). The approach keeps the pre-trained denoising network entirely frozen, making it a plug-and-play path optimizer rather than a new generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: a negligible 0.1MB add-on that reduces FID by 25–48% at low NFE without retraining the denoiser could be adopted broadly across existing diffusion pipelines. However, the evidence base is narrow—only three small-to-medium datasets (32×32 to 256×256), two frameworks (DDIM and FM), and just three IPF iterations—leaving open whether the gains persist at ImageNet scale, in text-to-image settings, or with more aggressive path deviations. A key open question is how far the path can be pushed before the frozen denoiser's training distribution mismatch degrades quality, and whether the reparameterized product form (∏A(i), ∏B(i)) is the right inductive bias or merely a convenient one.
