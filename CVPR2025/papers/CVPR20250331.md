## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods are largely confined to image classification, optimizing only class-level information and using global, single-class image generation. This leaves two critical gaps: (i) Task Optimization Heterogeneity, where detection and segmentation require bounding-box and pixel-level supervision that classification-focused methods cannot provide, and (ii) Inflexible Image Generation, where global updates fail to produce localized, multi-object structures needed for complex scenes. No prior framework unifies distillation across all three vision tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes random Gaussian noise, GPT-4o-generated task-aligned text prompts (describing object classes, spatial relationships, and background interactions), and task-specific target labels (class IDs, bounding box coordinates, or pixel-level masks). It produces synthetic RGB images at 256×256 for classification and 512×512 for detection/segmentation, which are then scored, filtered (discarding ~5–10% below a quality threshold τ), and relabeled by the T_SP model to yield a compact synthetic dataset with corrected annotations.

## Method

UniDD operates in two stages. First, Universal Task Knowledge Mining trains a lightweight Task-Specific Proxy (T_SP) model—ResNet-18, Faster R-CNN, or LR-ASPP depending on the task—on the real dataset to encode task-relevant information in its parameters, while GPT-4o generates contextual prompts aligned to each task's label structure. Second, Universal Task-Driven Diffusion guides Stable Diffusion v1.5's denoising process: at each sampling step, the DDIM-predicted clean image ẑ₀ is fed through the T_SP model to compute a task-specific loss, whose gradient with respect to the noisy sample z_t is added to the noise prediction with a time-dependent strength s(t) = λ_s·√(1−α_t). This classifier-guidance-style mechanism steers generation toward the target label without any fine-tuning of the diffusion model on the target domain. A final Proxy-Driven High-Realism Refinement step scores, filters, and relabels generated images to ensure annotation accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with IPC-10, UniDD achieves 50.4% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best diffusion-based method MiniMax Diffusion (44.3%) by 6.1% and the optimization-based RDED (42.0%) by 8.4%. At IPC-50, UniDD reaches 62.8%, outperforming MiMxDiff (58.6%) by 4.2%. On the more challenging ImageNet-Subset (10-class, IPC-10), UniDD leads by 8.2% on ImageWoof (46.8% vs. 38.6%), 9.1% on ImageFruit (51.6% vs. 42.5%), and 6.6% on ImageMeow (49.5% vs. 42.9%). For detection on Pascal VOC at 1% compression ratio, UniDD achieves 16.8% mAP and 38.9% AP50, far exceeding the best coreset method (Uniform: 5.7% mAP, 17.7% AP50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K (IPC-10), UniDD decisively outperforms all five baselines: it beats the strongest prior diffusion method MiMxDiff by 6.1% (50.4% vs. 44.3%), the decoupled method RDED by 8.4%, D⁴M by 22.5%, SRe²L by 29.1%, and TESLA by 42.7%. On ImageNet-Subset (IPC-10), UniDD surpasses the second-best method HaBa by 8.2% on ImageWoof, 9.1% on ImageFruit, and 6.6% on ImageMeow, and outperforms the diffusion-based MiMxDiff by 9.8% on ImageWoof. For object detection, UniDD dominates all four coreset baselines (Random, Uniform, K-Center, Herding) across every compression ratio on both Pascal VOC and MS COCO; at 0.5% ratio on VOC it achieves 8.5% mAP versus 0.9% for the best coreset (Uniform), and at 1% on COCO it reaches 7.1% mAP versus 3.7% for Random. For semantic segmentation on VOC, UniDD leads at all three compression ratios (e.g., 10.3% mIoU vs. 6.7% for Random at 3.5%). The paper does not report any setting where UniDD underperforms a baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled dataset distillation paradigm (where a trained network encodes dataset information, as in RDED and Fetch-and-Forge) into a generative setting by using a task-specific proxy model to guide Stable Diffusion's denoising process, adapting the classifier-guidance mechanism of Dhariwal & Nichol. Unlike prior diffusion-based DD methods (D4M, MiMxDiff) that require fine-tuning the diffusion model on the target dataset, UniDD avoids this costly step by leveraging the proxy model's gradients at each sampling step. The framework also departs from the classification-only focus of nearly all prior DD work by incorporating bounding-box and pixel-mask supervision into the guidance signal, and introduces GPT-4o-generated contextual prompts to enrich the text conditioning for multi-object scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniDD establishes the first dataset distillation benchmarks for object detection (Pascal VOC, MS COCO) and semantic segmentation (Pascal VOC), filling a notable gap in the DD literature. However, the detection and segmentation comparisons are limited to coreset selection baselines (Random, K-Center, Herding, Uniform) rather than other DD methods, making it difficult to assess true competitive standing in those tasks. Open questions include whether the proxy-guided approach scales to even larger or more diverse datasets, how it handles tasks with richer label structures (e.g., instance segmentation, keypoint detection), and whether the 5–10% data discard rate from the refinement stage becomes prohibitive at very low compression ratios. The cross-architecture generalizability of the synthetic data is a practical advantage over optimization-based DD, but the reliance on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion checkpoint limits applicability to domains where SD has not been trained.
