## Problem

Existing 3D LLMs convert 3D scenes into point clouds or voxel representations for fine-tuning, creating a disconnect with the 2D visual knowledge embedded in pre-trained MLLMs and limiting their effectiveness due to scarce labeled 3D data. Meanwhile, Video LLMs possess rich spatiotemporal priors from large-scale video pre-training but lack explicit 3D spatial information, preventing them from handling tasks that require precise spatial reasoning in 3D environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252519/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames (sampled at 3 FPS from ScanNet scans) paired with per-pixel 3D global coordinate maps obtained by back-projecting depth images through camera intrinsics and extrinsics, along with a text instruction. For grounding tasks, it additionally receives a list of object proposals (from Mask3D at inference or ground-truth boxes at training) and selects the target via proposal classification. Outputs include a selected 3D bounding box, a dense text caption conditioned on a target object's center coordinate, or a free-form text answer.

## Method

Video-3D LLM treats 3D scenes as video sequences and injects 3D position encoding directly into ViT visual embeddings: each patch's 3D coordinates are averaged, encoded via sinusoidal position encoding (split across x, y, z axes), and added to the corresponding visual embedding to form position-aware video representations. A maximum coverage sampling strategy formulates frame selection as a greedy maximum coverage problem over voxel grids, selecting the fewest frames that cover ≥95% of scene voxels (averaging ~18 frames vs. 32 for uniform sampling). Built on LLaVA-Video 7B (QWen2-7B backbone), the model is trained in a multi-task manner using cross-entropy loss for QA and captioning, and InfoNCE loss for 3D visual grounding where object features (RGB patch features + 3D center coordinate encoding) are matched against a <ground> token's hidden state. This approach preserves both temporal and spatial context from the video modality, avoiding the representational gap introduced by converting frames into point clouds or voxels.

## Results

Video-3D LLM (Uniform, 32 frames) achieves state-of-the-art results across all five 3D scene understanding benchmarks: 58.1% Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer, 58.0% F1@0.25 on Multi3DRefer, 83.8 CIDEr@0.5IoU on Scan2Cap, 30.1% EM on ScanQA, and 58.6% EM on SQA3D. The maximum-coverage variant (MC, ~18 frames average) delivers near-identical performance (57.9% Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer, 57.7% EM on SQA3D) at half the inference time (527 ms vs. 1050 ms). Notably, these results are achieved using only 26% of the 3D training data (223k samples) compared to the previous SOTA LLaVA-3D (859k samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the previous SOTA LLaVA-3D [48], Video-3D LLM improves ScanRefer Acc@0.25 by 4.0 points (58.1 vs. 54.1), Acc@0.5 by 9.3 points (51.7 vs. 42.4), Scan2Cap CIDEr@0.5 by 4.6 points (83.8 vs. 79.2), ScanQA EM by 3.1 points (30.1 vs. 27.0), and SQA3D EM by 3.0 points (58.6 vs. 55.6), while using only 26% of the 3D training data. Compared to ChatScene [15], which uses the same Mask3D object proposals, Video-3D LLM gains 2.6 points on ScanRefer Acc@0.25 (58.1 vs. 55.5) and 0.9 points on Multi3DRefer F1@0.25 (58.0 vs. 57.1). Against PQ3D [50], the closest competitor on ScanRefer, the improvement is 1.1 points on Acc@0.25 (58.1 vs. 57.0) with a large 1.5-point gain on Acc@0.5 (51.7 vs. 51.2). On Scan2Cap, Video-3D LLM substantially outperforms LEO [16] by 11.4 CIDEr points (83.8 vs. 72.4) and 3D-VisTA [49] by 16.9 points (83.8 vs. 66.9). The zero-shot LLaVA-Video [45] backbone already achieves 48.5% EM on SQA3D, suggesting the 3D QA benchmarks may not fully stress-test spatial reasoning, yet fine-tuning with 3D-PE still yields a 10.1-point gain (58.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D LLMs (3D-LLM, LL3DA, LLaVA-3D, ChatScene) that converts 3D scenes into point cloud or voxel representations, which creates a representational gap with 2D-pretrained MLLMs. Instead, it builds on the LLaVA-Video backbone and treats 3D scenes as video sequences, injecting 3D global coordinates via sinusoidal position encoding directly into video patch features. It also extends the line of Video LLMs applied to 3D tasks (LLaVA-OneVision, Oryx) by adding explicit 3D spatial information that those models lack, and introduces a maximum coverage sampling strategy for efficient frame selection that has no direct analogue in prior 3D LLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical and data-efficient paradigm for 3D scene understanding: leveraging the spatiotemporal priors of video LLMs with minimal 3D-specific adaptation (position encoding) rather than learning entirely new 3D representations. Achieving SOTA with only 26% of LLaVA-3D's 3D training data suggests the video representation is a more natural bridge to 3D than point clouds or voxels. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to large-scale outdoor scenes, dynamic 3D environments, or tasks requiring fine-grained geometric reasoning beyond what patch-level coordinate averaging can capture, and whether the reliance on a separate 3D detector (Mask3D) for grounding proposals limits end-to-end applicability.
