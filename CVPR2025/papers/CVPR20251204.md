## Problem

Existing video personalization methods are restricted to closed-set categories (e.g., human faces only), support at most a single subject, or require expensive per-subject test-time optimization. Moreover, no benchmark exists that can accurately measure per-subject fidelity in multi-entity video generation, making it impossible to fairly compare open-set personalization approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251204/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt, one or more segmented reference images per subject (foreground objects or people), and optionally a clean background reference image, and generates a 512×288 px personalized video. Reference images are cropped object/face segments (not full frames) extracted via GroundingDINO + SAM, and each is paired with its corresponding entity word from the prompt to enable multi-subject binding. The model requires no per-subject fine-tuning at inference time.

## Method

Video Alchemist is a latent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) trained with rectified flow, where each DiT block contains self-attention plus two separate cross-attention layers—one for the global text prompt and one for personalization embeddings. The key architectural contribution is a subject-level binding mechanism: each reference image is encoded by a frozen DINOv2 encoder into l tokens, the corresponding entity word's text embeddings are flattened, replicated l times, and channel-concatenated with the image tokens before a linear projection and residual connection, producing a fused embedding f_n per subject. To combat the copy-and-paste overfitting effect (where the model replicates reference pose, lighting, and framing), the authors apply aggressive data augmentation to reference images (downscaling, Gaussian blur, color jitter, brightness shift, horizontal flip, shearing, rotation) during training. Training proceeds in two stages: first with text-only cross-attention, then introducing the personalization cross-attention with warmup fine-tuning of the full model.

## Results

On MSRVTT-Personalization (subject mode, single reference image), Video Alchemist achieves 0.617 subject similarity, 0.732 video similarity, 0.269 text similarity, and 0.466 dynamic degree, outperforming all baselines on every metric. In face mode (single reference image), it reaches 0.382 face similarity, surpassing the best face-specific baseline IP-Adapter (0.269) by 11.3%. In the user study, Video Alchemist receives 96.5% quality preference and 98.1% fidelity preference in subject mode, and 68.4% fidelity preference in face mode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Video Alchemist decisively outperforms all six baselines on subject similarity in subject mode: 0.617 vs. VideoBooth (0.395, +23.2%), DreamVideo (0.310, +99%), and ELITE (0.359, +72%). In face mode, it surpasses the strongest face-specific baseline IP-Adapter by 11.3% (0.382 vs. 0.269), and substantially outperforms PhotoMaker (0.189) and Magic-Me (0.135). Video Alchemist also leads on video similarity (0.732 vs. 0.612 for VideoBooth) and dynamic degree (0.466 vs. 0.448 for VideoBooth), while maintaining the best text alignment (0.269). The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is text similarity in face mode, where PhotoMaker scores 0.278 vs. Video Alchemist's 0.273, though PhotoMaker produces static images with no temporal dynamics. In the user study, Video Alchemist dominates with 96.5% quality and 98.1% fidelity preference in subject mode, and 68.4% fidelity preference in face mode, confirming the quantitative gaps are perceptually meaningful.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture and rectified flow training objective, adapting them for multi-subject video personalization. It departs from prior personalization methods (DreamBooth, IP-Adapter, VideoBooth, DreamVideo) in three key ways: (1) it supports multiple open-set subjects simultaneously rather than a single subject or closed-set categories, (2) it extends personalization to background conditioning, and (3) it eliminates test-time optimization by using a frozen image encoder (DINOv2) with a novel word-image binding mechanism that concatenates entity-word tokens with image tokens before cross-attention. The separate cross-attention layers for text and personalization conditioning represent a deliberate departure from IP-Adapter's shared decoupled attention design, motivated by the longer token sequences introduced by multi-image conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MSRVTT-Personalization provides the field with the first comprehensive benchmark for multi-subject video personalization, supporting face, single-subject, multi-subject, and background conditioning modes with subject-level (rather than image-level) fidelity metrics. The identification and mitigation of the copy-and-paste effect through simple data augmentations (downscaling, blurring, color jittering) is a broadly applicable insight for any reference-image-conditioned generative model. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos, higher resolutions, and more than a handful of subjects, and whether the binding mechanism generalizes to more complex compositional prompts. The current 512×288 resolution and reliance on MSRVTT-derived test data limit the benchmark's coverage of diverse real-world scenarios.
