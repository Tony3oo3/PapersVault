## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering but remains bottlenecked by high parameter counts and algorithmic inefficiencies in its tile-based rendering pipeline. Specifically, the standard tile intersection computation grossly overestimates each Gaussian's image-space extent, and existing pruning methods like PUP 3D-GS require storage proportional to N×36, making them impractical to integrate into training. Few prior approaches directly target rendering speed while simultaneously reducing model size and training time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same inputs as standard 3D-GS: a set of RGB training images with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and a sparse SfM point cloud for initialization. It outputs a compressed 3D Gaussian model (10.6× fewer Gaussians on average) that renders novel views at 6.71× the baseline FPS. The SnugBox and AccuTile tile-intersection methods are lossless—they change no rendered pixels—while the pruning stages are integrated directly into the training loop rather than applied post-hoc.

## Method

Speedy-Splat combines two complementary strategies. First, SnugBox replaces the conservative circle-based tile allocation with a tight axis-aligned bounding box derived from the Gaussian's actual elliptical extent (determined by the 1/255 alpha threshold), and AccuTile further refines this to exact tile intersections by iterating over the shorter side of the tile extent and computing only two ellipse-boundary points per row or column. Second, an efficient pruning score—computed as the squared gradient of the 2D projected Gaussian value g_i(p), which is already available in the backward pass—reduces per-Gaussian storage by 36× compared to PUP 3D-GS's Hessian-based score. This score drives Soft Pruning (80% removal at opacity-reset intervals during densification) and Hard Pruning (30% removal every 3000 iterations post-densification), collectively reducing the model by 10.6× while the tile methods add no quality loss.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, Speedy-Splat's full pipeline achieves a 10.6× reduction in Gaussian count (from 2.93M to 0.28M), a 6.51× rendering speedup (898 FPS vs. 134 FPS baseline), and a 1.45× training speedup, while maintaining a PSNR of 26.94 (vs. 27.55 for baseline 3D-GS). The lossless SnugBox and AccuTile methods preserve image quality (PSNR 27.55–27.57) while delivering 1.81× and 1.99× rendering speedups respectively. Across all three datasets (Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, Deep Blending), the average rendering speedup reaches 6.71× with a 10.6× model size reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Speedy-Splat's full pipeline dominates all baselines on rendering speed, achieving 6.51× FPS speedup versus PUP 3D-GS (2.55×), Mini-Splatting (3.20×), ELMGS (2.69×), LightGaussian (1.76×), and EAGLES (1.51×), while also delivering the highest compression ratio (10.6× vs. 8.65× for PUP and 6.84× for Mini-Splatting). In terms of image quality, Speedy-Splat's PSNR of 26.94 is competitive: it slightly trails Mini-Splatting (27.34) and LightGaussian (27.28) but matches EAGLES (26.94) and exceeds PUP 3D-GS (26.83). The lossless SnugBox and AccuTile methods match or marginally exceed the 3D-GS baseline on PSNR (27.55–27.57 vs. 27.55) while providing 1.81× and 1.99× speedups, making them strictly superior to the baseline. Notably, Speedy-Splat also outperforms PUP 3D-GS on PSNR (26.94 vs. 26.83) in the direct pruning-score comparison (Table 4) at the same 88.44% pruning ratio, while achieving comparable FPS (345 vs. 379). The main trade-off is that Speedy-Splat's LPIPS (0.296) is slightly worse than Mini-Splatting (0.217) and the 3D-GS baseline (0.222), reflecting the expected quality cost at very high compression ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) and extends the authors' own prior work, PUP 3D-GS, by reparameterizing its Hessian-based pruning score to reduce memory from O(N×36) to O(N), making it viable for integration into the training pipeline rather than only post-hoc compression. On the rendering side, it addresses the same tile-intersection problem tackled by StopThePop and FlashGS but with a tighter elliptical formulation (SnugBox/AccuTile) that the authors show is faster. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant post-hoc pruning paradigm by weaving pruning into the densification and fine-tuning stages of training, and from the conservative circle-based tile allocation in vanilla 3D-GS by exploiting the actual alpha-threshold extent of each Gaussian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.71× rendering speedup and 10.6× model-size reduction are practically significant for deploying 3D-GS on resource-constrained platforms (mobile, VR, networked streaming) where real-time performance has not yet been achieved. The lossless nature of SnugBox and AccuTile—zero quality degradation from purely tighter tile allocation—makes them immediately adoptable drop-in replacements. The main open question is whether the efficient pruning score can be further refined to close the small but noticeable PSNR gap with the full PUP 3D-GS Hessian, and whether the aggressive 80% Soft Pruning ratio generalizes beyond the tested scene set.
