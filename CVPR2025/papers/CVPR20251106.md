## Problem

State-of-the-art semi-dense and dense feature matchers rely on Transformers with quadratic spatial complexity to model long-range dependencies, making them impractically expensive at high resolutions. While Mamba offers linear O(N) complexity, it was designed for single-image sequence modeling and lacks the mutual cross-view interaction, omnidirectionality, and global receptive field that feature matching requires.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images resized and padded to 832×832 and produces sub-pixel feature correspondences through a three-stage pipeline: many-to-one coarse matches at 1/8 resolution, one-to-one fine matches via mutual nearest neighbor at 1/2 resolution, and sub-pixel offsets in [-1,1]² added to fine match locations. The entire model contains only 5.7M parameters and runs in ~60 ms on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU.

## Method

JamMa replaces the Transformer interaction layer with a Joint Mamba module built on a JEGO scan-merge strategy. The JEGO scan jointly scans the two images' coarse features in an alternating (interleaved) pattern to enable high-frequency mutual interaction, then performs four-directional skip scans (step p=2) so that the total sequence length remains N rather than 4N as in VMamba. Four independent Mamba blocks process the right, left, up, and down sequences to establish long-range dependencies. The JEGO merge restores sequences to 2D, sums horizontal and vertical contributions, and applies a gated 3×3 convolutional aggregator to consolidate omnidirectional and global information at the micro level. A lightweight ConvNeXt V2 encoder (0.65M params) extracts coarse (C=256, 1/8 res) and fine (C=64, 1/2 res) features, and a coarse-to-fine matching module (adapted from XoFTR) with MLP-Mixer fine matching and Tanh-based sub-pixel refinement produces final correspondences.

## Results

On MegaDepth, JamMa achieves the best average rank (3 out of 14 methods) across six efficiency and performance metrics, with 5.7M parameters, 202.9G FLOPs, and 59.9 ms runtime while attaining AUC@5°=64.1, AUC@10°=77.4, and AUC@20°=86.5 for relative pose estimation. These pose-estimation scores are the best among all sparse and semi-dense matchers, surpassed only by the much heavier dense methods DKM (67.3°) and RoMa (68.5°). On HPatches homography estimation, JamMa reaches 68.1/77.0/85.4 AUC at 3/5/10 px, matching or exceeding ASpanFormer and LoFTR. On ScanNet (zero-shot), JamMa achieves 18.5/35.6/51.3 AUC at 5°/10°/20° with half the parameters and roughly half the runtime of LoFTR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JamMa outperforms all attention-based semi-dense matchers (LoFTR, ASpanFormer, ELoFTR, MatchFormer) on both performance and efficiency simultaneously: it achieves higher AUC at all three thresholds (e.g., 64.1 vs. 62.1 for LoFTR at 5°) while using less than half the parameters (5.7M vs. 11.6M) and roughly one-quarter the FLOPs (202.9G vs. 815.4G). Against the dense matchers DKM and RoMa, JamMa trades 3–4 points of AUC@5° for a 12–19× reduction in FLOPs and a 9–14× speedup. On HPatches, JamMa edges out ASpanFormer at the 3 px threshold (68.1 vs. 67.4) and 5 px (77.0 vs. 76.9) but trails by 0.2% at 10 px (85.4 vs. 85.6), while using only 36% of ASpanFormer's parameters. The only method that beats JamMa on pure efficiency is XFeat (0.7M params, 14.2 ms), but XFeat's pose-estimation AUC@5° is 44.2, nearly 20 points below JamMa. On the zero-shot ScanNet benchmark, JamMa (18.5 AUC@5°, 5.7M params, 26.6 ms) underperforms LoFTR (20.7, 11.6M, 49.2 ms) by about 2 points, which the authors attribute to its smaller capacity, yet it still maintains a competitive efficiency–performance balance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Mamba selective state space model—originally designed for 1D NLP sequences—to the 2D two-image feature matching task, departing from the dominant Transformer-based paradigm (LoFTR, ASpanFormer, MatchFormer) that incurs quadratic spatial complexity. It builds directly on EVMamba's skip-scan for sequence-length reduction and XoFTR's coarse-to-fine matching pipeline, but introduces the JEGO strategy (Joint scan, Efficient skip, Global receptive field, Omnidirectional scan) to address three gaps in existing visual Mamba models: lack of cross-view mutual interaction, unidirectionality, and causal (local) receptive fields. Unlike Vim, VMamba, and EVMamba which target single-image classification, JamMa is the first to jointly scan two images with alternating high-frequency mutual interaction, positioning it as a new architectural direction within the semi-dense matching family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JamMa demonstrates that a 5.7M-parameter Mamba-based matcher can match or exceed attention-based semi-dense methods (LoFTR, ASpanFormer) in pose estimation while using less than 50% of the parameters and FLOPs, and can be trained on a single 4090 GPU in ~50 hours—making high-quality feature matching accessible to resource-constrained labs. The finding that a simple 3×3 gated convolution aggregator on a balanced receptive field is critical for global omnidirectionality (removing it costs ~2.2% AUC) offers a practical design principle for future visual SSM architectures. However, zero-shot transfer to indoor scenes (ScanNet) degrades noticeably, suggesting the ultra-lightweight parameter budget may limit generalizability, and the coarse-matching Softmax has emerged as the new efficiency bottleneck, pointing to further optimization opportunities.
