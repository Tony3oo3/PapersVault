## Problem

Multimodal 3D brain tumor segmentation relies on heavy 3D convolutional operations that demand substantial computational resources, making deployment in resource-constrained clinical settings difficult. Recent Transformer- and Mamba-based methods improve accuracy but introduce quadratic complexity or large parameter counts, failing to meet the efficiency requirements of real-world medical applications. The paper tackles the gap of achieving state-of-the-art segmentation accuracy with an extreme reduction in parameters, FLOPs, and memory access.

## Input / Output

The network consumes four 3D MRI volumes per patient (T1, T1ce, T2, FLAIR), each originally 240×240×155 voxels, cropped to 128×128×128 patches during training. It produces a dense voxel-wise segmentation mask with three sub-regions: enhancing tumor (ET), tumor core (TC, including ET and necrotic tissue), and whole tumor (WT, including TC and peritumoral edema). The multimodal input is processed as a 4-channel 3D volume through a fully convolutional stem that expands to 24 channels before entering the encoder.

## Method

SuperLightNet is an encoder-decoder architecture with 2.97M total parameters. The Random Multiview Drop (RMD) encoder replaces full 3D convolutions with a random axis-selection mechanism that projects the 3D volume onto one of three anatomical planes (coronal, axial, sagittal) and applies 2D Group Hadamard Product Attention (G_hpa) on both the forward and inverted views, reducing 3D computation to 2D operations; a drop-residual (dropRes) split routes half the channels through this attention while the other half bypasses directly, preventing overfitting and halving effective computation. The Learnable Residual Skip (LRS) decoder eliminates transposed convolutions in favor of non-trainable linear upsampling combined with learnable per-group skip weights and scale residual weights, and uses a fixed group-12 depthwise MLP instead of full convolutions, yielding only 0.26M decoder parameters. Trained with AdamW, cosine annealing, and binary cross-entropy + Dice loss for ~40k iterations on 4×A100 GPUs, the network achieves 82.30% DSC on BraTS2019 and 87.45% DSC on BraTS2021 while requiring only 0.282 TFLOPs and 45.8G MACs.

## Results

On BraTS2019 (5-fold CV), SuperLightNet achieves 82.30% DSC and 73.23% SDC with only 2.97M parameters, outperforming the previous SOTA SegMamba (82.04% DSC, 72.95% SDC) by 0.26% and 0.28% respectively while using 95.59% fewer parameters. On BraTS2021 (TVE), it reaches 87.45% DSC and 82.99% SDC, surpassing SwinUNETR by 0.06% DSC and 3.37% SDC. In terms of efficiency, the network requires only 0.282 Tflops (fwd+bwd) and 45.8G MACs, representing a 96.78% reduction in computational cost and 96.86% reduction in memory access compared to SegMamba.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperLightNet outperforms all five baselines on both datasets in DSC. Against SegMamba (67.42M params), it gains +0.26% DSC and +0.28% SDC on BraTS2019 while reducing parameters by 95.59% and computational cost by 96.78%. Against LightM-UNet (5.02M params, the closest lightweight competitor), it achieves +0.81% DSC and +1.81% SDC on BraTS2019 with 40.83% fewer parameters and 45.97% lower FLOPs. Against SwinUNETR (62.19M params), it surpasses by +0.71% DSC on BraTS2019 and +0.06% DSC / +3.37% SDC on BraTS2021. Against nnU-Net (31.19M params), it leads by +0.89% DSC on BraTS2019 and +0.12% DSC on BraTS2021, though nnU-Net edges it by 0.22% SDC on BraTS2021. Against TransBTS (32.99M params), the margin is +1.54% DSC on BraTS2019 and +3.27% DSC on BraTS2021.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the recent trend of increasingly complex architectures (Transformers, Mamba/SSMs) for 3D medical segmentation, arguing that their quadratic or high computational costs are incompatible with lightweight deployment. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes concepts from 2D lightweight networks (e.g., EGE-Unet's channel-splitting strategies) and introduces a novel Random Tri-view Hadamard Drop encoder that randomly selects one of three anatomical views for feature extraction, combined with a Learnable Residual Skip decoder that replaces heavy transposed convolutions with linear upsampling and learnable group-weighted skip connections. The encoder-decoder framework follows the standard UNet lineage, but the internal operators (Hadamard Product Attention, drop residual, grouped MLP) are redesigned for extreme parameter efficiency in the multimodal 3D setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95.59% parameter reduction (to 2.97M) while matching or slightly exceeding SOTA DSC on BraTS2019/2021 makes this practically relevant for resource-constrained clinical and mobile healthcare settings where heavy Transformer or Mamba models cannot be deployed. However, the average performance gain over prior SOTA is only 0.21%, and the evaluation is limited to two BraTS datasets with a single task (brain tumor subregion segmentation), leaving open whether the random multiview drop strategy generalizes to other 3D volumetric segmentation problems or higher-resolution data. The paper acknowledges that further accuracy improvements are needed, and the modest SOTA margin suggests the primary value is in efficiency rather than a fundamental accuracy breakthrough.
