## Problem

State-of-the-art video diffusion models use bidirectional attention, requiring the entire sequence to be processed before any frame can be produced, which introduces prohibitive latency (219 s for 128 frames) and prevents interactive or streaming use. Autoregressive alternatives reduce latency but suffer from severe error accumulation over long sequences and remain far too slow for real-time frame rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252057/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (and optionally an initial RGB image for image-to-video, or a noisy video chunk for streaming video-to-video) and produces a sequence of video frames at 352×640 resolution and 12 FPS. Generation proceeds in chunks of 5 latent frames (decoded from 16 pixel frames by a 3D VAE), with each chunk requiring only 4 denoising steps. After a 1.3 s initial latency, frames stream out at approximately 9.4 FPS on a single H100 GPU, and the video length is unbounded via sliding-window inference.

## Method

CausVid adapts a pretrained bidirectional DiT into a causal autoregressive generator using blockwise causal attention: bidirectional self-attention within each chunk of 5 latent frames, and causal (unidirectional) attention across chunks. Training is two-stage: first, the student is initialized by regression on ODE solution pairs generated by the bidirectional teacher; second, it is distilled via an asymmetric distribution matching distillation (DMD) loss where the bidirectional teacher provides score supervision to the causal student. This asymmetric setup—distilling a stronger bidirectional teacher into a weaker causal student—significantly mitigates error accumulation compared to distilling from a causal teacher. At inference, KV caching over previously generated chunks enables efficient streaming, and the blockwise causal mask is replaced by a fast bidirectional attention kernel since only past context is needed.

## Results

On VBench text-to-short-video generation (10s, 352×640), CausVid achieves the highest scores across all three metrics: temporal quality 94.7, frame quality 64.4, and text alignment 30.1, outperforming MovieGen (91.5/61.1/28.8), CogVideoX-5B (89.9/59.8/29.1), and Pyramid Flow (89.6/55.9/27.1). On the VBench-Long benchmark, CausVid secures first place with a total score of 84.27. In terms of efficiency, CausVid achieves an initial latency of 1.3 seconds and a sustained throughput of 9.4 FPS on a single H100 GPU, representing a 160× latency reduction and 16× throughput improvement over the similarly scaled CogVideoX-5B (208.6s / 0.6 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausVid outperforms all four short-video baselines (CogVideoX-5B, OpenSORA, Pyramid Flow, MovieGen) on every VBench metric, with the largest margins over OpenSORA (+6.3 temporal, +12.4 frame) and Pyramid Flow (+5.1 temporal, +8.5 frame). For long-video generation (~30s), CausVid beats Gen-L-Video, FreeNoise, StreamingT2V, and Pyramid Flow on temporal and frame quality, though FIFO-Diffusion edges it slightly on text alignment (29.9 vs 28.9) while CausVid leads on temporal quality (94.9 vs 93.1) and frame quality (63.4 vs 57.9). In streaming video-to-video translation, CausVid surpasses StreamV2V on all three metrics (93.2/61.7/27.7 vs 92.5/59.3/26.9). For image-to-video, CausVid achieves the best temporal quality (92.0) and frame quality (65.0) versus CogVideoX-5B (87.0/64.9) and Pyramid Flow (88.4/60.3). The efficiency gap is dramatic: CausVid's 1.3s initial latency and 9.4 FPS streaming rate dwarf CogVideoX-5B (208.6s / 0.6 FPS) and even the autoregressive Pyramid Flow (6.7s / 2.5 FPS), making it the first autoregressive video model competitive with bidirectional diffusion in quality while enabling interactive use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD/DMD2), originally developed for few-step image generation, to the video domain with a key architectural twist: an asymmetric distillation strategy where a bidirectional teacher supervises a causal (autoregressive) student. The autoregressive training formulation draws on Diffusion Forcing's per-chunk noise-level scheduling, while the backbone follows the DiT architecture used by CogVideoX. The departure from prior video distillation work (which distills non-causal teachers into non-causal students) is the central conceptual contribution, combined with an ODE-trajectory initialization scheme to stabilize training across the architectural mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CausVid is the first autoregressive video generator to match or exceed state-of-the-art bidirectional diffusion models in quality while achieving 160× lower latency and 9.4 FPS streaming on a single GPU, unlocking interactive applications (dynamic prompting, streaming video-to-video translation, real-time game engine enhancement) that were previously infeasible. The finding that a bidirectional teacher outperforms a causal teacher for distilling a causal student is a non-obvious result that may reshape how future few-step video generators are trained. Open questions include whether the quality gap in temporal flickering and output diversity relative to the teacher can be closed, how the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer contexts, and whether the asymmetric distillation principle generalizes to other modalities or architectures.
