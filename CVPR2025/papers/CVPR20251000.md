## Problem

Existing satellite-to-ground synthesis methods (e.g., Sat2Ground, GVG) produce photorealistic single ground views but fail to maintain appearance consistency across neighboring views, as the inherent randomness of generative models causes facade details and scene layouts to shift between independently generated images. This inconsistency renders the outputs impractical for applications requiring coherent multi-view or video sequences, such as immersive 3D gaming and large-scale urban modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251000/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view satellite imagery (reconstructed into a textured 3D mesh via multi-view stereo) and predefined ground camera poses as input. The satellite mesh is projected onto each ground camera to produce a 'ground-view satellite appearance' image, which serves as the conditioning signal rather than the raw top-down satellite image. The output is a sequence of perspective ground-view images (each 512×512 in latent space) separated by 3–10 meter camera displacements, with the full dataset providing 25,000+ panoramic and 100,000+ perspective satellite-ground pairs.

## Method

The approach builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion v2-1 LDM and introduces two trainable conditioning modules. First, a ControlNet-like UNet (E_sat) extracts high-level layout features from the ground-projected satellite appearance and injects them as residuals at every U-Net layer, guiding the denoising process to preserve scene structure (satellite-guided denoising). Second, for multi-view consistency, the LDM is extended to a temporal-spatial architecture by inserting a temporal convolution/attention layer after each spatial layer; the initial ground view's latent is duplicated T times and channel-concatenated with the noisy latent (doubling the channel dimension), while a ResNet (E_phi) encodes camera-motion features from the satellite conditions as additional per-layer residuals (satellite-temporal denoising). Training uses the standard DDPM noise-prediction MSE loss at a learning rate of 1×10⁻⁵ on a single 48 GB GPU, with the LDM backbone parameters held fixed throughout, so only the two conditioning modules are learned.

## Results

On the Sat2GroundScape dataset, Sat2GroundScape achieves the best perceptual quality with LPIPS 0.542, FID 159.6, and DreamSIM 0.531, outperforming all baselines. For temporal consistency, it achieves the lowest FVD scores (16.83 and 16.88 for the two FVD variants), substantially better than GVG (19.34/19.39), SceneScape (19.47/19.53), and Sat2Ground (22.21/22.27). The method also attains the highest SSIM (0.231), though it trails SceneScape slightly on PSNR (15.86 vs. 16.32). On the HoliCity dataset, qualitative comparison confirms superior multi-view consistency over GVG across different positions and view angles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sat2GroundScape outperforms all three baselines on every perceptual metric (LPIPS, FID, DreamSIM, SSIM) and both temporal FVD metrics. Against GVG [39], the closest single-view diffusion baseline, the improvement is most pronounced on FID (159.6 vs. 175.1) and FVD (16.83 vs. 19.34), reflecting the benefit of the satellite-temporal denoising for multi-view coherence. Against SceneScape [5], the autoregressive video-generation baseline, Sat2GroundScape wins on all metrics except PSNR (15.86 vs. 16.32), with a notable FVD gap (16.83 vs. 19.47) that the authors attribute to SceneScape's sequential warping accumulating distortions over iterations. Against Sat2Ground [23], the GAN-based baseline, the advantage is the largest across the board, with FID dropping from 276.8 to 159.6 and FVD from 22.21 to 16.83. On the HoliCity generalization test, Sat2GroundScape produces more spatially and angularly consistent views than GVG, which exhibits variable building appearances across views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the satellite-to-ground synthesis line (Sat2Ground, GVG) from single-view to multi-view consistent generation by combining two conditioning strategies within a frozen Latent Diffusion Model: a ControlNet-style satellite-guided denoising module for layout preservation, and a temporal-spatial extension (inspired by Stable Video Diffusion and ControlVideo) that enforces inter-view consistency. It departs from autoregressive multi-view approaches (SceneScape, Streetscapes) that warp and inpaint sequentially—accumulating errors—by generating all T views in a single joint denoising pass conditioned on satellite camera-motion features. The frozen-LDM-plus-trainable-conditioning-modules design follows the ControlNet paradigm, and the satellite appearance projection pipeline builds directly on GVG's geometry-aware texture mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100K+ perspective (and 25K+ panoramic) satellite-ground pairs in Sat2GroundScape substantially outscale prior datasets (GVG: ~7K pairs), enabling training for large-scale ground video generation that was previously infeasible. The joint temporal-spatial denoising avoids the error accumulation inherent in autoregressive warping, which is a practical advantage for long sequences. However, the approach is evaluated on a single urban area (Jacksonville, FL) and a fixed number of views per pass, leaving open questions about scalability to very long video sequences, non-urban or rural environments, and whether the ResNet-based camera-motion encoder generalizes to arbitrary trajectory shapes. The frozen-backbone design also constrains the model's capacity to learn satellite-ground domain mappings beyond what the conditioning residuals can convey.
