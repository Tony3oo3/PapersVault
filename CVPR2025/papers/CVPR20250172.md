## Problem

Medical VQA annotation requires specialized expertise, leading to semantically coherent label noise from inter-observer variability that existing random-noise models fail to capture. No prior work has systematically studied Med-VQA under noisy labels, and uni-modal label-denoising methods (NTM, co-teaching) are ill-suited to the multi-modal, high-class-count setting of medical VQA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250172/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a medical image (H×W×C, encoded by Swin Transformer) and a text question (encoded by BERT) as input, and outputs a discrete answer class label selected from a predefined set of L answer classes via argmax over the predicted probability distribution. During training, only semantically noisy labels are available as supervision; clean labels are used solely for evaluation. The answer space is fixed (classification-based), distinguishing this from generation-based VQA that produces free-form text.

## Method

DiN comprises three modules: (1) an Answer Condition Generator (ACG) that uses a transformer decoder with L learnable candidate answer embeddings as queries, cross-attending with fused image-question features to produce answer-aware conditions; (2) a Noisy Label Refinement (NLR) module that trains an auxiliary classifier with a Robust Focal Loss (symmetric cross-entropy + focal term) and generates pseudo-labels via a dynamic Answer Adjustment strategy that blends proto-answers with noisy labels using an EMA-tracked confidence weight; (3) an Answer Diffuser (AD) that performs a conditional diffusion process where the forward step interpolates between the refined pseudo-label and the ACG condition, and the reverse step progressively denoises a random Gaussian distribution into the final answer probability distribution. The key insight is treating answer classification as a generative denoising task, enabling coarse-to-fine refinement of answer candidates and bridging the gap between rigid classification and unreliable free-form generation.

## Results

On VQA-RAD with 10% semantic noise, DiN achieves 74.24% overall accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline SNLC (69.65%) by 4.59 points. Under the more challenging 20% semantic noise setting, DiN reaches 62.28% overall accuracy versus 58.88% for SNLC. On PathVQA, DiN similarly leads across all noise conditions, achieving 66.67% (10% semantic), 58.14% (20% semantic), and 60.84% (20% random) overall accuracy. The method consistently surpasses all five baselines on both open-end and close-end question types across every noise configuration tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiN outperforms all five baselines across every noise condition on both VQA-RAD and PathVQA. The closest competitor is SNLC [47], the only method designed for noisy-label VQA, but DiN still leads by 4.59 points (74.24% vs. 69.65%) on VQA-RAD with 10% semantic noise and by 3.40 points (62.28% vs. 58.88%) at 20% semantic noise. The two Med-VQA-specific methods, MMBERT [19] and Q2ATransformer [27], were not designed for noisy labels and show the largest degradation under semantic noise (e.g., MMBERT drops to 52.68% open-end accuracy at 20% semantic noise on VQA-RAD). Among the uni-modal label-denoising methods, CoDis [44] (co-teaching) slightly outperforms SimT [11] (NTM-based), but both remain well below DiN, with SimT actually underperforming the unmodified baseline at 20% semantic noise on VQA-RAD (55.82% vs. 56.01%). On PathVQA, the same ordering holds: DiN leads SNLC by 2.49 points at 10% semantic noise and by 1.42 points at 20% semantic noise, while MMBERT and Q2ATransformer trail by 4–8 points overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the conditional label-diffusion framework of CARD (Han et al., NeurIPS 2022) to the multi-modal Med-VQA setting, where the forward/reverse diffusion process operates over answer probability distributions rather than images. It departs from existing Med-VQA methods (MMBERT, Q2ATransformer) that assume clean annotations, and from uni-modal noisy-label techniques (NTM-based SimT, co-teaching CoDis) that are ill-suited to multi-modal medical tasks. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of semantically structured noise—where incorrect labels remain medically plausible (e.g., 'hydropneumothorax' for 'pneumothorax')—rather than the random or rule-based flips used in prior noisy-label literature, positioning the work at the intersection of diffusion-based classification, robust loss design, and medical annotation realism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of noisy labels in Med-VQA, providing both a semantically realistic noise benchmark and a diffusion-based solution that consistently outperforms classification, generation, and existing denoising baselines. The practical relevance is clear: medical annotation inherently involves inter-observer variability and domain-specific errors that random noise models fail to capture. Limitations include evaluation on only two relatively small datasets (VQA-RAD: 315 images; PathVQA: ~5,000 images), the added training overhead of the diffusion process, and the fact that the approach remains bounded to a fixed answer vocabulary. Open questions include whether the semantic-noise construction generalizes to larger, more diverse medical VQA corpora, and whether the diffusion-based answer refinement transfers to other noisy-label multimodal tasks beyond VQA.
