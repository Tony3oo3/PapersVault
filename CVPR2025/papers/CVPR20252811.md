## Problem

3D scene understanding with Gaussian Splatting suffers from an inherent imbalance where many Gaussians are needed for fine-grained appearance but only a single semantic attribute per region, leading to redundant semantic representation. Existing approaches either decouple appearance and semantic learning (causing boundary inconsistencies) or rely on top-down, category-prior-based instance counting (causing over- or under-segmentation in complex scenes).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252811/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images and SAM-generated 2D segmentation masks during training, and accepts free-form text prompts at inference for open-vocabulary queries. It outputs per-Gaussian 6-dimensional instance features that yield 3D point-level instance labels, category-agnostic instance segmentation, and open-vocabulary semantic segmentation; selected 3D Gaussians can also be rasterized into multi-view 2D renderings for object-level evaluation. The instance features are 6-dim vectors shared across 5 child Gaussians per anchor, and the final instance count is determined adaptively rather than fixed a priori.

## Method

InstanceGaussian introduces a Semantic-Scaffold-GS representation where each anchor point spawns 5 child Gaussians with distinct appearance attributes (decoded via MLPs) but a single shared 6-dim instance feature, balancing the appearance-semantic capacity mismatch. Training follows a progressive schedule: appearance-only (0–10k steps), independent appearance-semantic (10k–20k), then joint (20k–30k), with a modified contrastive loss that truncates the inter-mask penalty via an indicator threshold (τ=0.4) to stabilize joint optimization. For instantiation, the method performs bottom-up, category-agnostic aggregation: FPS-based k-means over-segments the scene into ~1000 sub-objects using concatenated positional encoding and instance features, then a sparse graph (edges weighted by L2 feature distance gated by voxel adjacency) is aggregated via connected-component analysis (threshold γ=0.1) to adaptively recover complete objects without any prior on instance count.

## Results

On the ScanNet dataset for category-agnostic 3D instance segmentation, InstanceGaussian achieves mIoU 50.27 and mAcc 80.22, substantially outperforming OpenGaussian (27.32/52.44) and GaussianGrouping (22.55/30.54), while approaching the depth-assisted MaskClustering (54.17/80.95). For open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (19 classes), InstanceGaussian reaches mIoU 40.66 and mAcc 54.01, surpassing MaskClustering (34.59/54.32) and OpenGaussian (24.73/41.54). On the LeRF dataset for 3D object selection and rendering, InstanceGaussian achieves mIoU 45.30 and mAcc 58.44, outperforming OpenGaussian (38.36/51.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstanceGaussian decisively outperforms all Gaussian-based baselines across all three tasks. Against OpenGaussian, it gains +22.95 mIoU on instance segmentation (50.27 vs 27.32), +15.93 mIoU on 19-class open-vocabulary segmentation (40.66 vs 24.73), and +6.94 mIoU on LeRF rendering (45.30 vs 38.36). Against GaussianGrouping, the margin is even larger (+27.72 mIoU on instance segmentation), attributed to GaussianGrouping's reliance on tracking-based cross-view association that fails under ScanNet's viewpoint changes and occlusions. Against LangSplat and LEGaussians, InstanceGaussian leads by over 36 mIoU on open-vocabulary segmentation, as those methods suffer from ambiguous feature learning and detrimental language distillation. The one comparison where InstanceGaussian trails is against depth-assisted MaskClustering on instance segmentation (50.27 vs 54.17 mIoU), but it reverses the lead on open-vocabulary segmentation (40.66 vs 34.59 mIoU), which the authors attribute to MaskClustering's heavy dependence on high-quality SAM masks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation by combining Scaffold-GS's anchor-based hierarchical structure with OpenGaussian's instance-feature learning, but departs from both in critical ways. Where OpenGaussian and LangSplat freeze appearance attributes before learning semantics (a decoupled paradigm), InstanceGaussian introduces a progressive joint training strategy that co-optimizes appearance and semantics to maintain consistency. It also replaces the top-down, category-count-dependent instance assignment used by GaussianGrouping, FastLGS, and OpenGaussian with a bottom-up FPS-based over-segmentation followed by graph-connectivity aggregation, making the method category-agnostic and adaptive to scene complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-doubling of mIoU over OpenGaussian (50.27 vs. 27.32) and the ability to surpass a depth-assisted method (MaskClustering) in open-vocabulary tasks without depth input suggest that the appearance-semantic imbalance and top-down counting assumptions were genuine bottlenecks in 3DGS-based perception. However, evaluation is limited to 10 ScanNet scenes, and the authors acknowledge that performance degrades when SAM produces poor masks and that MLP-based appearance decoding slows rendering. Open questions include whether the progressive training schedule generalizes to dynamic or outdoor scenes, whether the fixed n=5n' child-to-anchor ratio is optimal, and how the method scales to large-scale outdoor environments like autonomous driving.
