## Problem

Event-based deblurring methods achieve superior quality by leveraging motion information from event cameras, but they require ground-truth event data as inference input, making them impractical for consumer photos from standard cameras. Image-only deblurring methods, while practical, struggle to recover sharp details under complex motion blur due to the absence of explicit motion guidance. This paper asks whether a diffusion model can synthesize the necessary motion information from a single blurry image to close this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252860/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single blurry RGB image as input and produces a sharp deblurred image of the same resolution, requiring no event data or auxiliary sensor input. Internally, the diffusion model also computes a detail guidance signal P = B − T(B) (the residual after downsample-then-upsample) to condition generation. During training, synthetic events (generated via ESIM on GoPro or captured on REBlur) are used to supervise the motion prior features, but are entirely absent at test time.

## Method

EGDeblurring comprises two modules: a Motion Prior Generation Diffusion Model (MPG-Diff) and a Regression Deblurring Network (RDNet). Rather than generating sparse raw events, MPG-Diff is a conditional DDIM-based diffusion model that generates dense motion prior features M′ conditioned on the blurry image and a detail guidance signal, since diffusion models are better suited to dense representations. RDNet is a U-Net-style encoder-decoder that fuses multi-scale motion prior features with image features via a proposed Dual-Attention Channel Fusion Block (DACFB), which uses two value tensors to compute cross-channel attention maps that modulate image features. Training follows a three-stage strategy: (1) jointly train a Feature Extractor and RDNet with ground-truth events, (2) train MPG-Diff to denoise motion prior features conditioned on the blurry image, and (3) jointly fine-tune MPG-Diff and RDNet with an L1 loss on the deblurred output plus a feature-matching term between generated and extracted motion priors.

## Results

On the synthetic GoPro dataset, EGDeblurring (image-only inference) achieves SSIM 0.973 and PSNR 34.62, outperforming the best image-only baseline AdaRevD (SSIM 0.972, PSNR 34.60) and HI-Diff (SSIM 0.964, PSNR 33.33). When trained with events (Stage 1), the method reaches SSIM 0.974 and PSNR 35.50, surpassing the strongest event-based baseline EFNet (SSIM 0.972, PSNR 35.46). On the real-world REBlur dataset, the image-only variant achieves SSIM 0.969 and PSNR 36.21 (vs. HI-Diff at 0.963/35.91), while the Stage 1 variant reaches SSIM 0.977 and PSNR 38.51, exceeding REFID (0.975/38.34) and EFNet (0.975/38.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against image-only methods on GoPro, EGDeblurring (80.3 M params) beats the closest competitor AdaRevD (220.8 M params) by 0.001 SSIM and 0.02 dB PSNR, and outperforms the diffusion-based HI-Diff by 0.009 SSIM and 1.29 dB PSNR. On REBlur, the margin over the best image-only method HI-Diff widens to 0.006 SSIM and 0.30 dB PSNR. In the event-based comparison (Stage 1, using GT events), the method surpasses EFNet by 0.002 SSIM and 0.04 dB PSNR on GoPro, and beats REFID by 0.002 SSIM and 0.17 dB PSNR on REBlur. Notably, the image-only variant (which requires no event sensor at inference) already matches or exceeds most event-based methods, demonstrating that the diffusion-generated motion prior effectively substitutes for ground-truth event input. The ablation confirms that removing the diffusion model (replacing with a CNN) drops PSNR by 1.64 dB, and removing the detail guidance costs 0.78 dB, validating both design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of event-based deblurring and diffusion models, building directly on the DDPM/DDIM framework (Ho et al., Song et al.) for the generative component. It departs from prior event-based methods (EFNet, EDI, REFID) that require ground-truth event streams at inference by instead using a diffusion model to synthesize dense motion prior features from a single blurry image, and from prior diffusion-based deblurring (HI-Diff, DvSR, DiffIR) that generate the output image or latent features directly, by generating an intermediate guidance representation that feeds a separate regression network. The dual-attention channel fusion block (DACFB) and the multi-stage training strategy are novel architectural and training contributions that bridge the two modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that event-based deblurring quality becomes accessible to consumer cameras without event sensors, removing a major hardware barrier. The design choice to generate dense motion prior features rather than raw sparse events for the diffusion model is a useful insight, as the ablation shows a 1.64 dB drop when generating raw events directly. Open questions include how well the method generalizes when trained on synthetic events (ESIM) to real-world blur patterns, whether the 5-step DDIM inference cost is acceptable for real-time applications, and whether the approach extends naturally to video deblurring or other motion-related restoration tasks.
