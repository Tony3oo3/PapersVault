## Problem

Human pose forecasting is inherently multimodal, yet the task is ill-posed because an unbounded set of futures exists for any observation. State-of-the-art methods handle this by oversampling large numbers of predictions from generative models, which is sample-inefficient, provides no per-mode confidence, and cannot distinguish likely from rare futures. The paper asks whether multimodality can be captured with fewer, more informative predictions and how to rank them by likelihood.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T_o past 3D poses (each J×3) as input; the MotionMap predictor specifically consumes only the last three frames via a GRU encoder. It outputs a 128×128 binary heatmap (the MotionMap) whose local maxima correspond to distinct future motion modes, a variable number of decoded future pose sequences (T_f frames each), per-mode confidence scores derived from heatmap peak intensities, and per-joint heteroscedastic uncertainty estimates. A codebook of size ≈64 MB (for 128×128 with 128-dim embeddings) maps each heatmap cell back to a mean latent embedding for decoding.

## Method

MotionMap is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 trains an autoencoder (GRU encoders for observed and future poses, an MLP uncertainty module, and a GRUCell decoder) to reconstruct concatenated input+future sequences under a heteroscedastic negative log-likelihood. Stage 2 trains a lightweight heatmap predictor H (GRU encoder → FC → 1×1 convolutions) with pixel-wise binary cross-entropy to output a 2D heatmap where each local maximum encodes one future motion mode. The key mechanism is a precomputed codebook: all future-pose embeddings are projected to 2D via t-SNE, quantized to integer grid positions, and each cell stores the mean latent of all futures mapped there. At inference, local maxima of the predicted heatmap are extracted deterministically (no stochastic sampling), indexed into the codebook to recover latents, and fed to the decoder—yielding a variable number of ranked forecasts with explicit confidence and per-joint uncertainty, while preserving rare modes that averaging-based methods would suppress.

## Results

On both Human3.6M and AMASS, MotionMap achieves the lowest MMADE and MMFDE among all compared methods, demonstrating superior sample efficiency when constrained to the same number of predictions (7) as baselines. The paper reports that MotionMap outperforms BelFusion [1]—which shares the identical GRU encoder and GRUCell decoder—by replacing diffusion-based latent sampling with heatmap-and-codebook decoding, yielding significant gains in multimodal accuracy. MotionMap also outperforms anchor-based methods (DLow, DivSamp) that, while diverse, produce forecasts that do not accurately correspond to the observed motion transitions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BelFusion [1], which shares the same GRU encoder, MLP uncertainty module, and GRUCell decoder, MotionMap provides significant gains purely by replacing diffusion-based repeated sampling of z_y with deterministic heatmap local-maxima extraction and codebook lookup, confirming that the representation—not the backbone—drives the improvement. Against DLow [3] and DivSamp [4], MotionMap wins on accuracy (lower MMADE/MMFDE) because anchor-based and Gumbel-Softmax sampling methods generate diverse but often implausible transitions that do not match the observed motion, whereas MotionMap's learned spatial prior over the motion space selects only the likeliest modes. The paper also notes that DLow and DivSamp tend to be more diverse than the ground truth itself, indicating over-generation of unlikely futures. On the secondary evaluation protocol (Tables 2–3, restricting multimodal ground truths to the test split), MotionMap remains the most accurate at recalling transitions from the test set without sacrificing generalization to unseen motions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionMap builds directly on the BelFusion autoencoder architecture (GRU encoders, MLP uncertainty module, GRUCell decoder) and its heteroscedastic regression loss, but replaces the diffusion-based latent sampling with a deterministic heatmap-plus-codebook mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant generative-model paradigm for multimodality (GANs, VAEs, diffusion, Gumbel-Softmax, anchor-based sampling) by treating the space of future motions as a 2D spatial distribution where local maxima are identified deterministically rather than sampled stochastically. The paper also revises the standard multimodal ground-truth construction (last-frame similarity) to use the last three frames with skeleton scaling via Cartesian-to-spherical coordinate transforms, addressing size and motion-information gaps in prior evaluation protocols.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing pose forecasting as learning and translating a bounded set of transitions from the training set, the paper offers a principled way to make an otherwise ill-posed problem well-posed, and the heatmap representation provides sample-efficient mode coverage without repeated stochastic sampling. The decomposition of uncertainty into mode confidence and per-mode prediction uncertainty is a practical advance for safety-critical applications. However, the method cannot capture fine-grained variations within a single mode (e.g., subtle walking-style differences), and the reliance on a fixed t-SNE projection and a global codebook raises open questions about scalability to larger, more diverse motion corpora and about whether the 2D embedding preserves all relevant structure.
