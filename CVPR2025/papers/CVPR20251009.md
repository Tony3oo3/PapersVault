## Problem

Class incremental segmentation requires simultaneously retaining old-class knowledge and acquiring new classes, but existing methods impose contradictory optimization directions on the same queries and logits, creating an inherent conflict that cannot be resolved by merely tuning a balance point. The paper argues that this conflict must be structurally deconstructed rather than balanced, so that learning and distillation operate on separated targets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 640×640 RGB images and, at each incremental step, receives ground-truth annotations only for the newly introduced classes while old-class labels are replaced by pseudo-labels generated from the previous model's predictions. Output is structured as N query-based mask proposals, each carrying a class embedding (over all classes seen so far plus a no-object class) and a binary mask, which jointly yield per-pixel class labels (semantic) or instance-level masks with category assignments (panoptic).

## Method

CoMBO introduces a branched optimization structure on top of Mask2Former with three coupled mechanisms. First, a Query Conflict Reduction (QCR) module applies a low-rank (rank-16) adapter to penultimate-layer queries that predict new classes, creating a parallel branch: adapted queries learn new-class features while the original queries remain frozen for distillation. Second, the Half-Distillation-Half-Learning (HDHL) strategy assigns cross-entropy loss to queries matched to current or old-class ground truth, and a shared KL-divergence loss to all unmatched queries that simultaneously preserves old-class probability distributions and suppresses new-class logits on negative regions. Third, Importance-Based Knowledge Distillation (IKD) computes per-query importance from the bipartite-matching cost matrix accumulated over the training set, weighting the L2 distillation on query features so that queries critical for old classes are strongly anchored while less critical ones are free to adapt.

## Results

On the ADE20K dataset under Class Incremental Panoptic Segmentation (CIPS), CoMBO achieves 35.6% PQ on the challenging 100-10 (6-step) scenario, surpassing the previous best (BalConpas, 34.7%) by 0.9% overall and 2.4% on incremental classes. Under Class Incremental Semantic Segmentation (CISS), CoMBO reaches 41.1% mIoU on 100-10, outperforming BalConpas (38.6%) by 2.5% overall and 3.5% on new classes. In the most difficult 100-5 (11-step) CISS setting, CoMBO attains 37.3% mIoU overall with 22.6% on new classes, a 5.4% gain over the prior state-of-the-art on incremental classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMBO outperforms all six baselines on the 100-10 CIPS scenario (35.6% vs. BalConpas 34.7%, ECLIPSE 33.9%, CoMFormer 29.7%), with the largest margin on incremental classes (25.2% vs. BalConpas 22.8%, a 2.4% gap). On CISS, CoMBO leads across all four scenarios, most notably on 100-10 where it reaches 41.1% mIoU vs. BalConpas 38.6% and ECLIPSE 34.6%, and on 100-5 where it achieves 37.3% vs. ECLIPSE 34.2%. The only scenario where CoMBO trails a baseline is 100-5 CIPS, where ECLIPSE's parameter-freezing strategy yields 32.9% overall PQ vs. CoMBO's 30.9%, though CoMBO still leads on new classes (20.5% vs. 16.6%). Against the earliest baselines (MiB, PLOP), CoMBO gains 6–14% PQ on CIPS and 11–16% mIoU on CISS in the 100-10 setting, reflecting the substantial progress from pixel-wise to mask-classification-based incremental segmentation. The Joint upper bound remains 3–4% above CoMBO, indicating remaining headroom but confirming that CoMBO closes most of the gap between incremental and joint training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMBO builds directly on the Mask2Former query-based segmentation architecture and the pseudo-labeling pipeline established by CoMFormer for class incremental segmentation. Its key conceptual departure from prior methods (ECLIPSE, BalConpas, CoMasTRe) is reframing the stability-plasticity tradeoff: rather than tuning a single loss to find a balance point on shared queries, CoMBO architecturally branches the optimization via low-rank class-specific adapters (QCR) and splits supervision between matched and unmatched queries (HDHL). This 'decompose the conflict' philosophy contrasts with the prevailing 'balance the conflict' approach in the continual segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The branched-optimization principle—separating learning and distillation onto different query subsets rather than competing on the same parameters—offers a general recipe that could transfer to other query-based or set-prediction architectures beyond segmentation. Limitations include evaluation only on ADE20K in an exemplar-free setting, and the per-class adapter accumulation (though small at ~2% of total parameters) raises questions about very long incremental sequences. Open questions include whether the importance-weighted distillation (IKD) generalizes to architectures without explicit query structures, and whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or few-shot incremental settings.
