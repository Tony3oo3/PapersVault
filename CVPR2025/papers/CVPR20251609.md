## Problem

Slot-attention methods for object-centric learning suffer from the part-whole hierarchy ambiguity on real-world scenes, producing over-segmented or under-segmented slots with imprecise boundaries. Moreover, existing OCL methods that scale to complex imagery (e.g., StableLSD, SPOT) are restricted to object discovery and cannot perform conditional reconstruction or compositional generation of realistic scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251609/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, which is first processed by BLIP-2 to generate a caption; nouns are extracted via POS tagging and filtered to COCO class labels to form a diffusion prompt. The real image is only used for prompt generation—slot attention is trained entirely on images generated by Stable Diffusion conditioned on that prompt. Outputs include per-slot latent embeddings, binary object masks derived from slot attention weights, a reconstructed image via the diffusion decoder, and compositional scenes produced by adding or removing individual slots.

## Method

GLASS trains a slot-attention module on DINOv2 encoder features with a pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net as the image decoder and a small MLP as a feature decoder. The key insight is to learn in the space of diffusion-generated images, exploiting the U-Net's cross-attention layers to extract pseudo-semantic masks (refined with self-attention maps following Dataset Diffusion) that serve as a semantic guidance signal. Slots are matched to these pseudo-masks via Hungarian matching, and a binary cross-entropy loss enforces boundary alignment. A complementary instance guidance loss (MSE between input and MLP-reconstructed DINOv2 features) counteracts semantic drift, binding slots to individual instances rather than class regions. Training proceeds in two phases: first the slot attention and MLP decoder are trained, then the diffusion decoder is fine-tuned with a higher learning rate while the slot modules are nearly frozen.

## Results

On instance-aware object discovery, GLASS achieves 38.9% mIoU_i on COCO and 58.1% mIoU_i on VOC, surpassing the previous SotA (SPOT) by +4.9 and +9.3 points respectively. For conditional image generation, GLASS achieves a FID of 71.30 on COCO, nearly halving the FID of StableLSD (140.62). In zero-shot object discovery, GLASS reaches 46.1% mIoU_i on CLEVRTex and 18.6% mIoU_i on Object365, outperforming all prior OCL methods. For semantic-level object discovery, GLASS-Sem attains 46.7% mIoU_c on COCO, exceeding all prior methods including language-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLASS outperforms all six OCL baselines (Slot Attention, SLATE, DINOSAUR, SPOT, SlotDiffusion, StableLSD) across all three discovery metrics on both COCO and VOC, with the largest gains over SPOT (+4.9 mIoU_i on COCO, +9.3 on VOC) and StableLSD (+14.2 mIoU_i on COCO). For conditional generation, GLASS nearly halves StableLSD's FID (71.30 vs. 140.62) while matching or exceeding PSNR/SSIM. On zero-shot object discovery (CLEVRTex, Object365), GLASS beats SPOT by +6.6 and +0.6 mIoU_i respectively. For semantic-level object discovery, GLASS-Sem surpasses all 14 non-OCL baselines on COCO (46.7% mIoU_c vs. 34.6% for OVDiff) but trails ToCo by 0.9 points on VOC (68.9% vs. 69.8%). Against weakly-supervised variants of StableLSD (BBox, Dynamic), GLASS wins by a wide margin (58.1% vs. 31.5% mIoU_i on VOC) despite using a weaker supervision signal (generated captions vs. bounding boxes or object counts).

## Relation to Prior Work

GLASS extends the slot-attention lineage (Locatello et al., 2020) by learning in the space of diffusion-generated images rather than raw pixels, building directly on StableLSD's (Jiang et al., 2023) architecture of slot attention over DINOv2 features with a Stable Diffusion decoder. Its key departure is the dual-guidance mechanism: a semantic guidance loss derived from the diffusion decoder's cross-attention maps (following Dataset Diffusion, Nguyen et al., 2023) combined with an instance guidance loss via feature reconstruction (inspired by DINOSAUR, Seitzer et al., 2023). This combination is novel in that neither guidance alone suffices—semantic guidance alone causes under-segmentation while instance guidance alone causes over-segmentation—yet together they resolve the part-whole hierarchy ambiguity that has limited prior OCL methods on real-world scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GLASS establishes the first slot-attention method capable of compositional generation of complex real-world scenes, opening a path toward object-centric generative modeling beyond synthetic benchmarks. The finding that very weak supervision (auto-generated captions) outperforms stronger signals (bounding boxes, object counts) suggests that the quality of the learned representation, not the amount of supervision, is the critical factor. Limitations remain: sensitivity to the number of slots, occasional semantic drift, and reconstruction quality still below SotA generative models. Open questions include whether the dual-guidance principle generalizes to video or 3D settings, and whether the pseudo-ground-truth generation can be made more robust without relying on COCO class labels.
