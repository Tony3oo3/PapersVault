## Problem

Self-supervised learning (SSL) models are predominantly evaluated on high-level vision tasks such as image classification and object detection, leaving their mid-level vision capabilities—generic object segmentation, monocular depth and surface normal estimation, geometric correspondence, and mid-level image similarity—largely unexamined. This paper asks whether strong high-level performance implies strong mid-level performance and what factors (training objectives, architectures, model capacity) drive mid-level vision capabilities in SSL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251960/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark probes frozen SSL backbones (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16) pretrained exclusively on ImageNet1K. For dense prediction tasks (depth, surface normal, segmentation), multi-scale features are extracted from the backbone—last four ResNet blocks in FPN style, or ViT layers 3/6/9/12—and fed into a trainable DPT decoder. For geometric correspondence and mid-level image similarity, frozen features are used directly without any additional training, producing pixel-level correspondences or cosine-distance-based similarity votes on the NIGHTS dataset.

## Method

The paper introduces a controlled benchmark suite of 8 mid-level vision tasks (spanning 2D and 3D) evaluated across 22 SSL models from diverse categories (pretext, contrastive, clustering, self-distillation, generative, and mixture objectives). All models use publicly available ImageNet1K-pretrained checkpoints with standard architectures, enabling fair comparison of training objectives and architectures as the primary variables. Dense prediction tasks use a DPT decoder trained with task-specific losses (AdaBins for depth, angular loss for normals, BCE for segmentation), while correspondence and similarity tasks are training-free. The study reveals a positive but weaker correlation between mid-level and high-level performance compared to correlations within high-level tasks, identifies generative methods (iBOT, MaskFeat) as top mid-level performers, and shows that ViT architectures and larger model capacities consistently improve mid-level vision capabilities.

## Results

iBOT (ViT-B/16) leads in most mid-level vision tasks, achieving 75.74 mIoU on object segmentation, 50.02 ε₁ on object depth, 81.32 ε₁ on scene depth, and 89.27% on mid-level image similarity. MaskFeat excels in scene-level 3D tasks (53.63 ε₁ scene surface normal, 27.94% scene correspondence recall), while DINO (RN-50) is best on object correspondence (3.16% recall). MAE is a clear outlier, scoring only 26.78 ε₁ on object depth and 66.17 ε₁ on scene depth—far below iBOT and MaskFeat—despite competitive ImageNet linear probing accuracy. The correlation between mid-level and high-level performance is positive but weaker than among high-level tasks (R²=0.70 for object segmentation vs. much lower for 3D geometric tasks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

iBOT (ViT-B/16) dominates most mid-level tasks, outperforming MAE by 23.24 points on object depth (50.02 vs. 26.78 ε₁) and 15.15 points on scene depth (81.32 vs. 66.17 ε₁), while also leading in object segmentation (75.74 vs. 65.77 mIoU) and image similarity (89.27% vs. 82.91%). MaskFeat, despite modest ImageNet linear probing accuracy, surpasses MAE by 22.72 points on object depth (49.50 vs. 26.78) and leads all models on scene surface normal (53.63 ε₁) and scene correspondence (27.94% recall). Among contrastive methods, DINO (RN-50) edges out MoCo-v3 (RN-50) on most tasks (e.g., 71.95 vs. 71.48 mIoU on object segmentation; 47.64 vs. 43.03 ε₁ on scene surface normal), while SimCLR, BYOL, and Barlow Twins show a negative correlation between ImageNet accuracy and mid-level performance. Pretext-task models (Jigsaw, RotNet) rank last across all mid-level and high-level tasks. At the architecture level, ViT-B/16 consistently outperforms ResNet-50 (e.g., MoCo-v3: 72.45 vs. 71.48 mIoU; DINO: 74.00 vs. 71.95 mIoU), and ViT-L/16 further improves over ViT-B/16 (iBOT: 52.56 vs. 50.02 ε₁ object depth; 85.14 vs. 81.32 ε₁ scene depth). Among visual foundation models, DINOv2 leads both mid-level and high-level tasks, while CLIP performs notably worse than most ImageNet-pretrained SSLs on mid-level 3D tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probing methodology of Probe3D [17] from visual foundation models (trained under diverse supervision signals and dataset scales) to a controlled study of 22 SSL methods pretrained exclusively on ImageNet1K, enabling isolation of factors like training objective and architecture. It builds on the broader SSL benchmarking literature (Goyal et al. [26], Ericsson et al. [18], Marks et al. [40]) but deliberately shifts focus from high-level recognition tasks to mid-level vision capabilities (depth, normals, correspondence, generic segmentation) grounded in Marr's hierarchical framework. The key conceptual departure is treating mid-level vision as a distinct evaluation axis rather than a byproduct of high-level performance, and using a unified DPT-based probing protocol across all tasks for fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that mid-level and high-level vision performance are only weakly correlated (and that contrastive methods show a negative correlation within their family) challenges the common assumption that strong ImageNet classification accuracy implies strong geometric understanding, directly impacting how practitioners select SSL backbones for 3D-aware applications. The identification of specific imbalanced models (MAE weak in mid-level, MaskFeat strong) and the mechanistic explanation via HOG features provide actionable guidance for future SSL design. Limitations include the restriction to ImageNet1K-pretrained checkpoints and the absence of end-to-end tasks like navigation or robotic manipulation, leaving open whether mid-level capabilities transfer to closed-loop embodied settings.
