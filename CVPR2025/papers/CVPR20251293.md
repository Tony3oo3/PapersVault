## Problem

Existing deep watermarking schemes require the entire watermarked image to extract embedded bits, making them ineffective against partial image theft, where a malicious user steals an arbitrary-shaped portion, applies geometric transformations, and fuses it onto an unknown background. No prior method simultaneously addresses the three resulting challenges: content loss, unknown background interference, and geometric displacement of watermark information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251293/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 200×200 RGB image and a 30-bit watermark as input, producing a watermarked image of the same size. Critically, extraction operates on a *fused* image in which only an irregularly shaped portion (1%–95% area) of the watermarked image is present, composited onto an arbitrary background, and potentially shifted, rotated (±45°), or scaled (±25%). The watermark is embedded once into the full image but must be recoverable from any such partial-theft composite without knowing the mask shape, background content, or transformation parameters.

## Method

WOFA introduces a multi-level architecture that decouples watermark-to-noise-pattern mapping (lightweight encoder/decoder with FC and conv layers) from image-level embedding/extraction (two depth-5 U-Nets), so the extractor outputs a noise pattern rather than raw bits. A two-stage training strategy first trains the encoder-decoder pair against a progressive distortion layer N1 (gradually intensifying partial masking, geometric transforms, and Gaussian noise), then freezes those weights and jointly trains the embedder-extractor against a second distortion layer N2 that simulates the full partial-theft pipeline (masking + transformation + background fusing) plus JPEG compression and resizing. The progressive distortion schedule—starting with mild, regular masks and small transforms and ramping to irregular masks covering 1–95% of the image and maximum geometric distortion by 50% of training—enables stable convergence that end-to-end training fails to achieve. The key design insight, validated by ablation, is that positional disruption (block shuffling) degrades extraction far more than content loss (block erasing), motivating the emphasis on geometric-robust distortion during training.

## Results

On the OPA test set, WOFA achieves 98.39% bit accuracy (BAR) on clean images and maintains 95.92% under partial masking alone. Under the full partial image theft pipeline with geometric transformations, WOFA sustains 87.93% (50% translation), 90.63% (45° rotation), 95.02% (±25% scaling), and 87.06% (mixed 3 transformations), consistently outperforming all baselines by 30–45 percentage points. On generalization tests, WOFA retains 85–95% BAR across matteImageNet and SOIM datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WOFA decisively outperforms all six baselines in every partial image theft scenario. MBRS is the closest competitor under partial masking alone (94.74% vs. WOFA's 95.92%), but collapses to ~50% (chance level) once any geometric transformation is applied, revealing its inability to handle positional shifts. HiDDeN, PIMoG, and StegaStamp all degrade to 50–75% under partial masking and fall to near-chance (~50%) with transformations. DWSF and MuST perform poorly even under partial masking alone (55.84% and 52.77% respectively), dropping to chance level with any additional distortion. WOFA's advantage is most dramatic under mixed transformations, where it maintains 87.06% while all baselines cluster around 49–53%, a gap of over 35 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

WOFA extends the HiDDeN embedder-distortion layer-extractor paradigm by introducing a multi-level architecture that decouples watermark-to-noise-pattern mapping (encoder/decoder) from image-level embedding/extraction (U-Net embedder/extractor), trained in two stages. It draws on MBRS's diffusion-block idea for content-loss resilience and MuST's compositing-aware design for background interference, but departs from all prior work by defining partial image theft as a unified threat model (partial masking + geometric transformation + background fusing) and constructing a progressive distortion layer that simulates this full pipeline during training. The two-stage training strategy is motivated by the observation that end-to-end training of the full multi-level network collapses, a practical departure from the single-stage training used in HiDDeN and most successors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Partial image theft is a common real-world attack that existing watermarking schemes are essentially blind to, and this paper provides the first systematic formulation, dataset, and robust solution for it. The finding that even a modest 10% translation causes a far larger accuracy drop than losing 80% of image content reframes where robustness effort should be directed in watermarking design. Limitations include evaluation at a single resolution (200×200), a fixed 30-bit capacity, and distortion ranges that may not cover all real-world manipulations; open questions include scalability to higher-resolution images, adversarial backgrounds, and whether the progressive distortion strategy generalizes to other threat models beyond partial theft.
