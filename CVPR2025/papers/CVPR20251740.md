## Problem

Existing audio-visual sound source localization methods rely on simple audio-visual correspondence, which cannot distinguish sound-making objects from visually similar silent objects in complex scenes (e.g., a non-playing guitar alongside a playing one). This oversimplification leads to mislocalization when multiple objects share the same category but differ in whether they are actively producing sound.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251740/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a center-frame RGB image (224×224) and a 3-second log-scale audio spectrogram (16 kHz) as input, producing a spatial sound-associated heatmap. For multi-source evaluation, two video frames are concatenated into a 448×224 image with mixed audio waveforms, and the model outputs per-source heatmaps via K iterative identification passes. During training only, an MLLM (InternVL 2.0-8B) additionally consumes the image and audio class label to generate foreground and background captions that are encoded by BERT into reference features; at inference, no MLLM or text is required.

## Method

The framework uses a two-stream ResNet-18 (or AudioCLIP) encoder to extract visual and audio features, then computes cosine similarity to produce a sound-associated map. The key innovation is leveraging an MLLM during training to generate K foreground captions (one per sound source, describing the active sound-making action) and one background caption (describing silent objects), which are encoded into reference features via BERT. Two novel losses guide fine-grained alignment: (1) OCA loss performs contrastive alignment between foreground/background visual features and the MLLM-derived reference features, with a similarity threshold τ to handle false negatives among similar objects in the batch; (2) ORI loss applies first-order Wasserstein distance (computed via the Sinkhorn algorithm) between reference-associated spatial maps to enforce non-overlapping regions for distinct sound sources and background. The total training objective is λ₁·L_oca + λ₂·L_ori (λ₁=1, λ₂=0.1), and multi-source localization is handled through an iterative object identification scheme over K passes.

## Results

On the multi-source MUSIC-Duet test set (ResNet-18 backbone), the proposed method achieves 61.4% CAP, 45.9% CIoU@0.3, and 36.1% AUC, outperforming the previous best (NoPrior, CVPR'24) by 9.3%, 7.3%, and 6.0% respectively. On VGGSound-Duet, it reaches 45.9% CAP, 55.2% CIoU@0.3, and 44.8% AUC, surpassing NoPrior by 13.4%, 8.3%, and 15.6%. For single-source localization on MUSIC-Solo, the method attains 79.8% AP and 71.1% IoU@0.5, improving over NoPrior by 2.4% and 9.0%. With the stronger AudioCLIP backbone, the method further extends its lead, reaching 47.1% CAP and 57.7% CIoU@0.3 on VGGSound-Duet versus 35.7% and 40.1% for T-VSL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric on both multi-source and single-source benchmarks. Against the strongest ResNet-18 baseline NoPrior (CVPR'24), it gains +9.3% CAP and +15.6% AUC on VGGSound-Duet, and +9.0% IoU@0.5 on MUSIC-Solo. Against the strongest AudioCLIP-backbone baseline T-VSL (CVPR'24), the advantage is even larger on VGGSound-Duet (+11.4% CAP, +17.6% CIoU@0.3, +9.5% AUC), while on MUSIC-Duet the margin is more modest (+1.2% CAP, +3.7% CIoU@0.3). Earlier methods such as AVGN (CVPR'23) and Mix-and-Localize (CVPR'22) trail by 10–25 points in CAP on VGGSound-Duet, and single-source baselines like LVS (CVPR'21) and EZ-VSL (ECCV'22) are surpassed by 8–17 points in IoU@0.5 on MUSIC-Solo. The consistent wins across both backbone choices and both datasets indicate that the OCA and ORI losses, combined with MLLM-generated scene context, provide a general improvement rather than a dataset-specific gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stream contrastive localization framework of Chen et al. (CVPR 2021, LVS) and the iterative multi-source identification scheme of Kim et al. (CVPR 2024, NoPrior) by introducing a training-time MLLM (InternVL 2.0-8B) that generates fine-grained scene captions distinguishing sound-making from silent objects. Rather than relying solely on audio-visual cosine similarity to identify sounding regions, the method encodes these MLLM-generated captions as reference anchor features and introduces two new losses—Object-aware Contrastive Alignment (OCA) with false-negative-aware batch contrastive learning, and Object Region Isolation (ORI) using Wasserstein distance via Sinkhorn—to enforce both semantic disambiguation and spatial separation. The key conceptual departure from prior work is shifting from 'which object matches the sound?' to 'which object is actively producing the sound in this scene?', leveraging external linguistic knowledge that is unavailable at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and recurring failure mode in sound source localization—mislocalization when visually similar silent objects coexist with sounding ones—and demonstrates that MLLM-generated scene context can substantially close this gap without increasing inference cost. The consistent 9–13% improvements over the previous SOTA (NoPrior) across both datasets, both backbones, and both single/multi-source settings, supported by clean ablations isolating each loss's contribution, make the approach practically relevant. Open questions include sensitivity to MLLM caption quality, scalability beyond three sources, and whether the training-time MLLM dependency limits reproducibility or introduces dataset-specific biases in the generated captions.
