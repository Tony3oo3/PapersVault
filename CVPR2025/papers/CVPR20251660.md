## Problem

Existing visual grounding methods either rely on two-stage pipelines that cannot recover from poor region proposals or fuse text and visual features within a detector without leveraging the deep semantic understanding of large language models. While recent work has begun integrating LLMs into grounding, these approaches typically use LLMs only as a feature integrator or direct predictor, leaving unexplored the potential of LLMs to produce task-aware priors that actively guide cross-modal feature refinement and alignment.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image resized to 448×448 and a text description (max 40 tokens, encoded by T5) as input. It simultaneously produces three outputs: a bounding box (x, y, w, h) for the referred object, a pixel-wise segmentation mask of the same spatial resolution as the input, and a per-word phrase prediction indicating which tokens in the query refer to the target object. The visual backbone is a Swin-B pre-trained with Mask-RCNN on MSCOCO, and the LLM component is a frozen LLaMA-3.1-8B.

## Method

TCRT introduces an LLM adaptation module that bridges the text-vision domain gap via two mechanisms: (1) conditional normalization, where text-conditioned scaling and shifting parameters modulate visual features at every transformer block, and (2) object-text semantic matching regularization, a binary classification loss that forces visual region tokens to align with their corresponding textual tokens. A 3-layer MLP visual adapter projects the text-aware visual features into the LLM's embedding space; the frozen LLaMA-3.1-8B then processes the concatenated text and visual tokens, and its last output token is decomposed into three task-specific embeddings (box, segmentation, phrase) via linear projections. A task-aware cross-modal feature fusion module then performs bidirectional multi-head attention between task-relevant text features and visual features, applies an LLM-guided refinement prompt (derived from the LLM prior) to compute a spatial weight matrix that suppresses irrelevant image regions, and executes cross-task interaction between the box and segmentation tokens to exploit their complementary localization signals. Training jointly optimizes GIoU+L1 detection loss, focal+DICE segmentation loss, cross-entropy phrase loss, and the matching loss, with the LLM kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On the REC task, TCRT achieves 91.07% (val), 92.85% (testA), and 86.24% (testB) on RefCOCO; 86.81% (val) and 87.13% (test) on RefCOCOg; 85.75% (val), 90.76% (testA), and 77.59% (testB) on RefCOCO+; and 79.09% on ReferItGame, setting new state-of-the-art across all benchmarks. On the RES task, TCRT reaches 77.12% mIoU (val), 78.12% (testA), and 75.67% (testB) on RefCOCO; 70.84% (val) and 72.08% (test) on RefCOCOg; and 72.79% (val), 74.96% (testA), and 66.53% (testB) on RefCOCO+. Notably, TCRT uses a Swin-B visual backbone, which is smaller than the ViT-L backbones employed by Vista-7B and Ferret-7B, yet still outperforms them by 1.3–3.2 percentage points on REC and 1.0–2.6 percentage points on RES.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCRT outperforms all ten baselines on every reported benchmark for both REC and RES tasks. Against the closest LLM-based competitor Vista-7B (ViT-L backbone), TCRT gains +2.97/+1.35/+3.24 pp on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, +0.81/+0.73 pp on RefCOCOg, and +1.65/+0.46/+1.79 pp on RefCOCO+ for REC, while using a smaller Swin-B backbone. On RES, TCRT surpasses Vista-7B by +2.62/+2.12/+1.77 pp on RefCOCO and +1.04/+1.18 pp on RefCOCOg. Compared to the non-multi-task Ferret-7B (ViT-L), TCRT leads by +3.58/+1.50/+3.79 pp on RefCOCO and +2.88/+2.37 pp on RefCOCOg for REC. Against traditional end-to-end methods, TCRT improves over VG-LAW (ViT-B) by +4.45/+3.53/+3.08 pp on RefCOCO and over PVD (Swin-B, same backbone) by +6.08/+4.83/+6.21 pp on RefCOCO, demonstrating that the LLM-guided feature refinement and cross-modal fusion contribute substantially beyond backbone capacity alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCRT extends the multi-task visual grounding framework established by RefTR [22] (joint REC + RES) by inserting a frozen LLM (LLaMA-3.1-8B) as a prior generator rather than a direct predictor. Unlike prior LLM-based grounding methods (MiniGPT-2, Vista-7B, Ferret-7B) that project visual features into the LLM's input space and let the LLM produce box coordinates, TCRT uses the LLM's output embedding to condition a separate cross-modal fusion module that refines visual features. The key architectural departure is the text-conditioned normalization layer inside the visual backbone, which modulates visual feature statistics before they reach the LLM, addressing the modality gap more explicitly than the dimension-projection-only approach used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen LLM can serve as a task-aware prior generator to guide feature refinement, offering a middle ground between lightweight grounding networks and full MLLM-based predictors. However, the practical cost of running an 8B-parameter LLM per grounding query is not discussed, and the LLM is entirely frozen, limiting how much of the gain is attributable to genuine LLM reasoning versus the adaptation and fusion modules. Open questions include whether the conditional normalization strategy transfers to other cross-modal tasks, whether a smaller or distilled LLM could achieve comparable priors, and how the method scales to video grounding or open-vocabulary settings.
