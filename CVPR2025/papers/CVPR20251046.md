## Problem

MLLMs excel at general visual tasks like object detection and localization but struggle with aesthetic visual understanding—perceiving color harmony, lighting quality, compositional balance, and photographic technique. Existing aesthetic datasets (Q-Instruct, AesExpert) are limited to ~20K images with annotations from small fixed annotator groups lacking professional photography expertise, and standard CLIP vision encoders are quantitatively insensitive to low-level aesthetic features such as overexposure or color cast.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with an aesthetics-related text instruction (e.g., an open-ended critique request, a photography technique question, or a multiple-choice prompt) and produces a detailed aesthetic critique, Q&A response, or MCQ answer. The image is processed through four complementary vision encoders (CLIP-ViT-L/14, DINOv2-giant, CoDETR-ViT-L, SAM-ViT-H) whose features are fused into a single representation before projection into the Vicuna-v1.5-7B LLM. Training data spans 450K images with 2.63M instruction-tuning pairs covering 70+ photographic categories, and evaluation uses PhotoBench's 284 photography sub-topics alongside Q-Bench.

## Method

PhotoEye introduces a language-guided multi-view vision fusor with L stacked fusion blocks that integrates features from four pre-trained vision encoders into a single instruction-conditioned representation. At the first layer, a language-guided query generator uses a BERT [CLS] embedding of the instruction to dynamically select a query from M learnable queries via cross-attention, enabling instruction-specific feature extraction tailored to the aesthetic concept in question. Each fusion block then performs cross-attention between the query and each encoder's interpolated features, followed by a multimodal gating network (an MLP conditioned on the language embedding and pooled visual features) that assigns per-encoder importance weights to produce the fused output. The model is trained in two stages—pretraining on LLaVA-Pretrain followed by instruction finetuning on PhotoCritique mixed with LLaVA-665K—using a batch size of 128 and learning rates of 1e-3 (pretrain) and 2e-5 (finetune) on 8 A100 GPUs. The key design insight is that different aesthetic concepts (composition, lighting, color, storytelling) require different visual features, and language conditioning at both the query generation and gating stages allows the model to selectively emphasize the most relevant encoder at each layer, unlike prior multi-encoder approaches that use instruction-agnostic fusion.

## Results

On the paper's own PhotoBench benchmark, PhotoEye achieves an overall score of 73.92, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (64.12), AesExpert (60.01), Q-Instruct (43.86), and UNIAA (36.87). On the established Q-Bench (LLVisionQA-dev), PhotoEye scores 74.50 overall, surpassing GPT-4o (73.04) and Qwen-VL-Max (73.63), and clearly beating aesthetics-specific models like Q-Instruct (67.09) and AesExpert (64.15). The largest gains on PhotoBench appear in Post-Processing (80.95 vs. 58.57 for GPT-4o) and Color & Tone (76.00 vs. 63.97), reflecting the dataset's emphasis on professional photography knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhotoEye (73.92) outperforms GPT-4o (64.12) by 9.8 points on PhotoBench, with the largest margins in Post-Processing (+22.4) and Color & Tone (+12.0), indicating that professional photography knowledge from the PhotoCritique dataset provides capabilities beyond what a general-purpose proprietary model offers. Against aesthetics-specific open-source models, PhotoEye leads AesExpert (60.01) by 13.9 points and Q-Instruct (43.86) by 30.1 points, demonstrating that scale and expertise of training data matter far more than architectural tweaks alone. On Q-Bench, PhotoEye (74.50) edges out GPT-4o (73.04) and Qwen-VL-Max (73.63) by 1.5 and 0.9 points respectively, while maintaining a 7.4-point lead over Q-Instruct and 10.4-point lead over AesExpert. The 34B-parameter LLaVA-v1.6 (55.68) is outperformed by PhotoEye's 7B model by 18.2 points, confirming that domain-specific data and multi-view vision fusion are more effective than simply scaling the LLM backbone. UNIAA (36.87) and Q-Instruct (43.86) show particularly weak performance on Composition and Exposure sub-topics, highlighting their limited coverage of professional photography concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhotoEye extends the multi-encoder vision fusion line initiated by Cambrian-1 (query-based attention over heterogeneous visual features) and MoVA (mixture-of-vision-experts), but departs from both by conditioning the entire fusion pipeline—feature extraction and gating—on the language instruction via a learnable query generator, rather than using instruction-agnostic or CLIP-anchored fusion. On the data side, the paper moves beyond the small-scale, fixed-annotator aesthetic datasets (Q-Instruct ~18K images, AesExpert ~21K images) by mining 450K images and 2.63M instruction pairs from 107K+ photography enthusiasts on online forums, reframing aesthetic understanding as a domain-expertise problem rather than a basic low-level perception problem. The benchmark PhotoBench similarly extends Q-Bench and AesBench from general low-level quality questions to 284 photography-specific sub-topics including camera settings, post-processing, and compositional technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and practically important gap: current MLLMs can identify objects but fail at the nuanced aesthetic judgments (lighting balance, compositional tension, post-processing intent) that photographers and editors need, and the 20× scale-up in training data plus the language-conditioned multi-view fusion provide a clear path to closing that gap. However, several open questions remain: the benchmark is self-created and MCQ-based, limiting its ability to capture open-ended aesthetic reasoning; the critiques are LLM-summarized from noisy forum comments, introducing potential bias; and it is unclear whether the multi-view fusion architecture generalizes beyond photography to other aesthetic domains (fine art, graphic design, video). The reliance on a 7B Vicuna backbone also raises questions about whether the gains are architectural or primarily data-driven.
