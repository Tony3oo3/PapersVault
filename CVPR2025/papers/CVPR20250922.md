## Problem

Existing video outpainting methods that rely solely on video diffusion models (e.g., M3DDM, MOTIA) produce temporally inconsistent results with duplications, distortions, and objects appearing or disappearing, while also being constrained to low resolutions and small field-of-view extensions due to memory limitations. This paper addresses both the quality and scalability gaps by introducing geometric 3D support for static regions and a guided denoising strategy for dynamic content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250922/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (e.g., 480×480 at 31° FoV) and produces a temporally coherent outpainted video sequence at a substantially expanded field of view (e.g., 2560×720 at 120° FoV), all within 16 GB GPU memory. The pipeline decomposes each frame into static background and tracked dynamic objects, outpainting the background via 3D-consistent rendering and inpainting object regions that fall outside the original frame, before a final video diffusion pass unifies temporal coherence across all frames.

## Method

The method decomposes video outpainting into three stages. First, a 3D Gaussian Splatting model is trained on the static background, then iteratively updated: key frames are outpainted with SDXL, depth is predicted, and new Gaussians are added with a combined reconstruction (L1+SSIM) and dual-level depth correlation loss (full-map and super-pixel) to prevent discontinuities. Second, a LoRA-fine-tuned SDXL model (following ReaFill) inpaints missing portions of tracked dynamic objects independently per frame. Third, a pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion model performs guided denoising where a mask-based blending scheme (Eq. 8–9) preserves known static latents while allowing the video model to correct temporal inconsistencies in dynamic regions, effectively using the video diffusion model as a temporal filter without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On DAVIS, the method achieves FVD of 232.5 and E_warp of 0.015, outperforming the prior best (MOTIA paper: FVD 286.3) by 18.8% and (M3DDM: E_warp 0.017) by 11.7%. On YouTube-VOS, it reaches FVD 114.2 and E_warp 0.016, improving over MOTIA's public model by 26.9% and 33.3% respectively. The method also attains the highest PSNR (22.62 on DAVIS, 19.88 on YouTube-VOS) and SSIM (0.806, 0.832) among all compared approaches. A user study with 619 votes from 37 participants shows the method is preferred in over 80% of votes across realism, temporal consistency, and overall visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Dehan et al. [13] (optical-flow warping + image completion), M3DDM [14] (3D masked diffusion trained on large-scale video data), and MOTIA [48] (per-sample LoRA fine-tuning of a video diffusion model). The proposed method wins on all primary metrics (FVD, E_warp, PSNR, SSIM) across both datasets. Against MOTIA's public model, the gains are most dramatic on YouTube-VOS: FVD drops from 156.4 to 114.2 (−26.9%) and E_warp from 0.027 to 0.016 (−33.3%). Against M3DDM on DAVIS, FVD improves from 300.0 to 232.5 (−22.5%) and E_warp from 0.017 to 0.015. The only metric where the method does not lead is LPIPS on DAVIS, where MOTIA's paper-reported value (0.160) is lower than the proposed 0.200, though the authors note their result surpasses MOTIA's publicly available implementation (0.282). Qualitatively, M3DDM suffers from generation failures and blurring, while MOTIA produces repetitive objects and background patterns; the proposed method avoids both issues thanks to the 3D Gaussian Splatting support for static regions and the guided video diffusion for dynamic objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in video outpainting—relying entirely on (video) diffusion models for generation—as exemplified by M3DDM's 3D masked diffusion and MOTIA's per-sample fine-tuned video diffusion. Instead, it decomposes the problem into a 3D-supported static outpainting stage (iterating between SDXL image outpainting and 3D Gaussian Splatting updates, building on Kerbl et al. and the FSGS optimization) and a dynamic object inpainting stage (following ReaFill's reference-driven fine-tuning), then uses a pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion model purely as a temporal consistency filter via a Repaint-style latent blending scheme. The key conceptual shift is treating the video diffusion model as a post-hoc temporal smoother rather than the primary generative engine, which sidesteps the memory and resolution limitations of fine-tuning video diffusion on each test clip.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the method extends a 480×480 (31° FoV) input to 2560×720 (120° FoV) with under 16 GB GPU memory, a capability no prior diffusion-only method achieves, making large-FoV video outpainting viable for VR and legacy content reformatting. The ablation confirms that the 3D GS component is essential—video diffusion alone cannot maintain coherence between outpainted and original regions—validating the hybrid 3D+2D architecture. Open limitations include handling objects that move in and out of the original frame (relying on bounding-box extrapolation) and multiple occluded dynamic objects (processed independently and blended by depth), pointing toward 4D dynamic Gaussian representations as a natural next step.
