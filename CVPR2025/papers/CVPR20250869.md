## Problem

Reconstructing 3D geometry and view-dependent appearance from images captured under extreme illumination variation remains unsolved. Per-image appearance embeddings tend to absorb specular effects into diffuse color, while physically-based inverse rendering suffers from material/lighting ambiguities, and single-image relighting diffusion models produce mutually inconsistent results across views. No prior method can faithfully recover high-frequency specular highlights from such unconstrained, differently-lit image collections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250869/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N posed RGB images (512×512×3) of a single object, each captured under a different illumination, along with known camera poses and object masks; no reference environment map or consistently-lit subset is required. It outputs a NeRF-Casting-based radiance field parameterized with per-image shading embeddings, from which novel views can be rendered under the illumination of any chosen reference image in the input set. At inference the diffusion model simultaneously relights up to N=64 views, and the 3D reconstruction is optimized per scene in roughly 30 minutes on 16 A100 GPUs.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a multiview latent diffusion model (initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5) jointly denoises N latent codes, one per input image, using 3D self-attention across views and cross-attention with camera poses encoded as raymaps; the clean latent and pose of each image are fed to its own denoising branch, while a reference map (single-channel ones/zeros) designates which image's lighting to match, and classifier-free guidance (CFG=3) is applied. Second, a NeRF-Casting-based radiance field is fit to the relit images, but with a novel per-image 'shading embedding' vector that warps surface normals through a 3-layer MLP before they enter the shading network, allowing the model to absorb small residual inconsistencies in specular highlight placement without degrading geometry. The relighting model is trained entirely on synthetic data (~300K objects rendered under ~700 Poly Haven environment maps, augmented with perfect-mirror material copies) in a staged curriculum from 8 to 64 simultaneous views, and generalizes to real photographs without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the synthetic Objaverse dataset, the proposed method achieves PSNR 31.34 / SSIM 0.966 / LPIPS 0.053 on standard assets and PSNR 26.54 / SSIM 0.911 / LPIPS 0.090 on shiny (perfectly reflective) assets, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On real-world NAVI photographs, the method reaches PSNR 25.55 / SSIM 0.929 / LPIPS 0.060, again the best among all compared approaches. Notably, the method outperforms IllumiNeRF even when the latter is given the ground-truth environment map as input, a significant advantage over the proposed method which only receives a single reference image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins on every metric across both datasets against all three baselines. Against NeROIC (inverse rendering with spherical harmonics), the gap is largest on shiny assets: +4.40 PSNR on Objaverse and +1.54 PSNR on NAVI, reflecting NeROIC's inability to model high-frequency specular effects. Against NeRF-Casting + AE (appearance embeddings), the advantage is +3.81 PSNR on standard Objaverse assets and +4.74 PSNR on shiny assets, showing that per-image appearance codes fail to capture view-dependent reflections under extreme illumination variation. Against IllumiNeRF (single-image diffusion relighting), the method gains +2.12 PSNR on standard and +3.08 PSNR on shiny Objaverse assets, despite IllumiNeRF being given the full ground-truth environment map rather than just a reference image. On real NAVI photos, the method leads NeRF-Casting + AE by +1.96 PSNR and NeROIC by +1.54 PSNR, confirming that the multiview relighting + shading-embedding pipeline generalizes to in-the-wild captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on NeRF-Casting for the 3D reconstruction backbone and on CAT3D's multiview diffusion architecture for the relighting component, but departs fundamentally from prior approaches to illumination variation. Where NeRF-W and NeROIC handle lighting changes via per-image appearance embeddings or explicit inverse rendering (decomposing materials and illumination), this work instead uses a multiview diffusion model to jointly relight all input images to a reference illumination before reconstruction, drawing an analogy to photometric stereo. The key architectural departure from single-image relighting methods like IllumiNeRF and Neural Gaffer is the joint multiview denoising, which the authors argue is essential for producing mutually consistent relit samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that strong generative priors (diffusion models) can effectively resolve the material/lighting ambiguity that has plagued inverse rendering, offering a practical path to reconstructing specular objects from unconstrained photo collections. The shading embedding—a simple per-image normal perturbation—is a lightweight but effective mechanism for absorbing residual diffusion-model errors, and its superiority over standard appearance embeddings (which actively degrade quality) is a useful negative result for the community. Limitations include the requirement for accurate camera poses and object masks, and the approach has not been tested on scenes with moving objects or dynamic lighting. An open question is whether the multiview relighting step can be made pose-free or integrated end-to-end with pose estimation.
