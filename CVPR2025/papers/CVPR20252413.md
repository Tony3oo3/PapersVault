## Problem

Existing inverse rendering methods that reconstruct surfaces, materials, and illumination from multi-view images struggle with glossy objects: specular highlights are either absorbed into geometric artifacts during sequential surface-then-material reconstruction, or the diffuse/specular decomposition leaks specular energy into the diffuse albedo. Most prior work relies heavily on synthetic data with complex physically-based renderers, limiting robustness on real-world captures where ground-truth materials and lighting are unavailable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252413/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only posed multi-view RGB images as input, requiring no additional data such as sparse point clouds or known lighting. It produces a complete scene factorization: an SDF for geometry, per-point diffuse albedo and a learnable specular albedo, a Ward BRDF parameterized by Spherical Gaussians (encoding roughness), a direct illumination map (128 SGs), an indirect illumination field (24 SGs), and a continuous light visibility function. All outputs are represented as neural networks or parametric SG fields, enabling continuous evaluation at arbitrary viewpoints and relighting.

## Method

Factored-NeuS is a three-stage progressive inverse rendering pipeline. Stage 1 jointly optimizes an SDF network with both volume rendering (overall appearance) and surface rendering (decomposed into diffuse and specular radiance), where the specular component is conditioned on both the reflection direction and the surface normal—unlike Ref-NeRF which uses only the reflection direction. This dual-rendering loss, combined with an Eikonal regularizer, prevents specular highlights from being encoded as geometric concavities. Stage 2 distills continuous light visibility (supervised by integrating SDF opacity weights along light rays) and indirect illumination (24 SGs) from the frozen SDF and radiance field. Stage 3 recovers materials and direct illumination using a Ward BRDF represented as Spherical Gaussians, augmented with a novel per-point specular albedo network that modulates the BRDF amplitude, enabling more accurate separation of diffuse and specular contributions during relighting.

## Results

On the SK3D glossy scenes, Factored-NeuS achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 1.55, outperforming NeuS (2.31), IndiSG (3.44), NeRO (3.65), and PhySG (7.73), and beating Geo-NeuS (1.81) which uses extra point-cloud supervision. On DTU glossy scenes, the method achieves 0.87 mean Chamfer distance, second only to Geo-NeuS (0.73) which benefits from SfM point clouds. For material and illumination reconstruction on the IndiSG dataset, Factored-NeuS achieves 26.28 dB mean albedo PSNR and 35.41 dB rendering PSNR, substantially outperforming IndiSG (23.63 / 26.51), NVDiffRec (20.41 / 29.51), and PhySG (15.39 / 27.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Factored-NeuS outperforms all baselines on SK3D glossy scenes in Chamfer distance (1.55 vs. 1.81 for Geo-NeuS, 2.31 for NeuS, 3.44 for IndiSG, 3.65 for NeRO, 7.73 for PhySG), with the largest margin over PhySG. On DTU, Geo-NeuS (0.73) retains a slight edge due to its SfM point-cloud supervision, but Factored-NeuS (0.87) beats NeuS (0.98), NeRO (1.12), IndiSG (1.61), and PhySG (3.53) without any extra geometry. For material reconstruction on IndiSG, the method achieves the best albedo PSNR (26.28 dB) and rendering PSNR (35.41 dB), surpassing IndiSG (23.63 / 26.51), NVDiffRec (20.41 / 29.51), and PhySG (15.39 / 27.37), though it trails IndiSG slightly on illumination PSNR (21.21 vs. 22.47). Against Ref-NeuS, Factored-NeuS achieves lower Chamfer distance on the Pot scene (0.85 vs. 1.02), and qualitatively produces cleaner surfaces on the Shiny dataset where Ref-NeRF's geometry exhibits strong noise. NeRO, which targets reflective objects, is outperformed on both SK3D and DTU glossy scenes and produces comparable but not superior results on the Glossy dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeuS line of SDF-based surface reconstruction by jointly training volume rendering and a decomposed surface rendering (diffuse + specular) during the geometry optimization stage, rather than the standard sequential pipeline of reconstructing surfaces first and then recovering materials. It adapts Ref-NeRF's diffuse/specular radiance split to the SDF setting by additionally conditioning specular radiance on the surface normal, and borrows the Spherical Gaussian BRDF representation from PhySG and the SG-based indirect illumination from IndiSG. The key departure from prior inverse rendering work (PhySG, IndiSG, NVDiffRec) is the explicit treatment of glossy highlights as a first-class signal during surface reconstruction, combined with a learnable specular albedo in the Ward BRDF and continuous SDF-based light visibility inspired by NeRFactor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Glossy and reflective objects are common in real-world scenes yet remain a persistent failure mode for implicit surface reconstruction, where highlights are typically absorbed as geometric artifacts; this paper provides a principled and practical solution that works without additional data (e.g., SfM point clouds). The ablation revealing a trade-off—specular albedo greatly improves roughness estimation (18.8→23.1 PSNR) but degrades illumination quality (23.5→20.2 PSNR)—highlights that material-lighting decomposition for glossy surfaces is still an open balancing problem. The authors note that most prior inverse rendering work is developed primarily on synthetic data and may not transfer well to real captures, positioning this paper as a step toward closing that gap, though fine-structure material decomposition remains unresolved.
