## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) are trained on large natural image datasets, creating a significant distribution shift when fine-tuned for medical imaging where data is scarce due to privacy, cost, and disease rarity. Existing fine-tuning methods (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, Textual Inversion) fail to close this gap, particularly for counterfactual medical image generation tasks such as aging, disease addition/removal, and gender alteration, where structural constraints (e.g., skull shape preservation) conflict with the diversity-oriented training objective of general-purpose models.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model takes a text prompt (optionally enriched with patient information like age, sex, and diagnosis) and produces a synthetic 2D brain MRI slice or chest X-ray. For image-to-image manipulation, it takes a source medical image plus an editing prompt (e.g., 'Age this CN 70 years old female brain MRI into a 77 brain MRI with MCI') and outputs the counterfactual image. Unusually, a signed scalar latent drift parameter δ (typically in [-0.1, 0.1]) is injected into the diffusion process as an additional conditioning factor, acting as a distribution-matching hyperparameter that can be tuned at both training and inference time.

## Method

Latent Drifting (LD) introduces a signed scalar δ into the mean of the Gaussian transition kernel in the diffusion reverse process (Eq. 4: μ_θ(x_t, t) + δ), effectively shifting the learned latent distribution toward the target medical data distribution. The approach is framed as a counterfactual explanation min-max optimization: minimizing similarity between original and counterfactual instances (ℓ_in) while maximizing desired outcome fidelity (ℓ_o), with a weighting factor λ controlling the trade-off. The optimal δ is found via grid search minimizing an L1 distance between Monte Carlo-sampled generated images and the target dataset. LD is agnostic to the fine-tuning scheme and can be combined with Custom Diffusion, DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, or basic U-Net fine-tuning; during fine-tuning it is applied to both forward and reverse processes, while at inference it can be applied to the reverse process alone for style control. The key insight is that the final latent variable z_T must be treated as part of the conditioning, and δ modifies the mean to ensure the model's learned representations accurately reflect the target medical distribution rather than the natural image prior.

## Results

On brain MRI (ADNI-1/OASIS3), Stable Diffusion + Basic FT + LD achieves the best FID of 49.68 (vs. 313.61 without LD) and KID of 0.035, with a disease-classification AUC of 0.724 (vs. 0.414 without LD). On CheXpert, the same configuration reduces FID from 331 to 84 and raises AUC from 0.426 to 0.746. Notably, a 50/50 mix of real and LD-generated synthetic data yields a classification AUC of 0.883 on brain MRI and 0.892 on CheXpert, both exceeding the real-data-only baselines (0.870 and 0.880), indicating that LD-generated samples carry useful diagnostic signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LD is applied as an add-on to four fine-tuning strategies (Custom Diffusion, DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, and Basic FT of the U-Net) and consistently reduces FID and KID across all of them. The largest absolute gains appear with Basic FT: brain-MRI FID drops from 313.61 to 49.68 and AUC jumps from 0.414 to 0.724; CheXpert FID falls from 331 to 84 and AUC rises from 0.426 to 0.746. Textual Inversion + LD also improves AUC (0.600→0.670 brain, 0.600→0.640 chest) while halving FID. DreamBooth + LD yields modest AUC gains (0.500→0.512 brain, 0.567→0.582 chest) and a ~30% FID reduction. The one exception is Custom Diffusion, where LD dramatically improves FID (129→64) but slightly degrades brain-MRI AUC (0.609→0.544), suggesting the cross-attention fine-tuning already captures some class-discriminative signal that the latent shift partially disrupts. Overall, SD + Basic FT + LD is the strongest configuration, and its synthetic samples, when mixed 50/50 with real data, push classification AUC above the real-data-only ceiling (0.883 vs. 0.870 brain; 0.892 vs. 0.880 chest).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion / Latent Diffusion Model architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and reframes the fine-tuning problem through the lens of counterfactual explanation optimization (Wachter et al., 2017). It departs from prior medical image generation work—both GAN-based approaches trained from scratch and LDMs trained on large medical datasets—by showing that a single scalar offset (δ) added to the diffusion process mean can bridge the natural-to-medical distribution gap without retraining the full model. The method is explicitly agnostic to the fine-tuning scheme (Custom Diffusion, DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, or basic U-Net fine-tuning), positioning LD as a composable add-on rather than a standalone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for medical imaging, where data scarcity (privacy, cost, disease rarity) makes large-scale training infeasible and domain shift from natural-image pretraining is a persistent bottleneck. The simplicity of the intervention—one grid-searched scalar—makes it easy to adopt, and the consistent FID improvements across four fine-tuning methods and three datasets suggest broad applicability. However, open questions remain: δ is found via exhaustive grid search rather than learned, all evaluations are on 2D slices (not 3D volumes), and the method has not been tested on modalities beyond brain MRI and chest X-ray. Whether the counterfactual framing (min-max objective) provides genuine theoretical grounding beyond the empirical δ offset is also unclear.
