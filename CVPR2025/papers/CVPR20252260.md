## Problem

Existing motion-understanding LLMs (e.g., MotionGPT, MotionLLM) encode skeletal motion into discrete codebook tokens or translate it into language-like representations, which obscures fine-grained 3D spatial-temporal dynamics critical for accurate behavior interpretation. Additionally, these methods treat video and motion as isolated modalities, missing the complementary environmental context that video provides for interpreting complex human actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252260/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LLaMo accepts skeletal motion sequences (3D joint keypoints over T frames), optional video frames, and a text prompt as input; a motion estimator can derive motion from video when skeletal data is unavailable, enabling both motion-only and video-only operation. The output is an autoregressive natural-language description or Q&A response generated token-by-token by the LLM, with no intermediate motion-to-text conversion step. The model is designed to handle variable-length motion sequences and supports interactive multi-turn dialogue about observed behaviors.

## Method

LLaMo retains motion as a native modality rather than tokenizing it, processing it through a dedicated motion encoder in parallel with a video encoder. A feature enhancement module uses cross-attention (motion as queries, video as keys/values) to enrich motion features with visual context. The Cross Talker module then performs language-guided frame selection—using text embeddings as queries to identify K most relevant motion frames from T total—reducing self-attention complexity from O((L_T+T)²) to O((L_T+K)²), followed by adaptive local-window and segment-level global attention to build enriched per-frame representations. Bidirectional cross-attention fuses the selected motion features with text embeddings before feeding them to a frozen-encoder LLM, which is fine-tuned via supervised negative log-likelihood to generate behavior descriptions.

## Results

On MoVid-Bench-Motion, LLaMo achieves 55.32% overall accuracy and 3.67 score, outperforming MotionLLM (49.50% / 3.49), MotionGPT (36.86% / 3.11), and GPT-3.5 (31.33% / 2.31). On MoVid-Bench-Video, LLaMo reaches 52.33% overall accuracy, surpassing MotionLLM (49.00%) and Video-LLAVA (42.53%). On BABEL-QA, LLaMo attains the highest overall score of 0.458 (vs. MotionLLM 0.436, MotionCLIP-M 0.430). On the profession-swing dataset, LLaMo achieves 24.80% overall accuracy, nearly doubling MotionLLM (16.53%) and substantially exceeding MotionGPT (14.35%) and GPT-3.5 (3.42%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaMo outperforms all baselines on MoVid-Bench-Motion across every sub-metric (body-part, sequential, direction, reasoning, hallucination), with the largest gains over MotionGPT (+18.5% overall accuracy) and GPT-3.5 (+24.0%). On MoVid-Bench-Video, LLaMo edges out MotionLLM by 3.33% overall accuracy and leads in reasoning (67.59% vs. 63.20%) and hallucination (72.81% vs. 70.59%), though it slightly trails MotionLLM on body-part awareness (33.83% vs. 34.13%). On BABEL-QA, LLaMo achieves the best overall score (0.458) and leads in Action (0.525), Direction (0.398), and temporal reasoning (Before 0.443, After 0.392), while MotionLLM retains a small edge in the 'Other' category (0.529 vs. 0.518). On profession-swing, LLaMo dominates all four indicators (reasonableness, coherence, pertinence, adaptability), achieving 24.80% overall accuracy versus 16.53% for MotionLLM and 14.35% for MotionGPT. On Mo-RepCount, LLaMo (OBO 0.389, MAE 0.324, OBZ 0.222, RMSE 6.15) outperforms TransRAC and RepNet but trails the specialized counters EScounts (OBO 0.397, MAE 0.291) and PoseRAC (OBO 0.382, MAE 0.312) on most metrics, though it achieves the best OBZ score (0.222 vs. 0.198 and 0.204).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in motion-language models (MotionGPT, MotionLLM, MotionCLIP) that encode skeletal motion into discrete tokens or textual descriptions before feeding them to an LLM. Instead, LLaMo retains motion as a native, continuous modality and introduces a Cross Talker module that performs text-guided frame selection and bidirectional cross-modal fusion, drawing conceptually from cross-attention mechanisms in video-language models like Video-LLaVA. The architecture also extends prior work by jointly processing video and motion as complementary inputs rather than treating them as isolated modalities, and adds a motion estimator to handle video-only scenarios. The training paradigm (frozen encoders, supervised fine-tuning of the LLM) follows standard multimodal instruction-tuning practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Preserving motion in its native spatial-temporal form rather than lossy tokenization is a principled architectural choice that could influence how future motion-language systems are designed, particularly for fine-grained behavioral analysis in sports, healthcare, and digital humans. However, the absolute performance remains modest (e.g., ~55% overall accuracy on MoVid-Bench-Motion, ~25% on professional sports), and the model does not outperform specialized repetition-counting models on most metrics, suggesting the unified framework has not yet fully realized its potential. Open questions include whether the Cross Talker's frame-selection strategy scales to very long sequences, how well the motion estimator generalizes to unseen domains, and whether the approach can achieve real-time inference for practical deployment.
