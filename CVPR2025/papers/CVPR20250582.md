## Problem

Visual narrative generation models that map textual narratives directly to image sequences fail to explicitly model the commonsense knowledge needed to manifest under-specified narrative content and the discourse constraints that maintain character, location, and style consistency across scenes. This leads to generated images that are unfaithful to the input text and visually inconsistent across the narrative, with no systematic benchmark or metrics to measure these failures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250582/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generation pipeline takes a textual narrative (a sequence of scene descriptions) as primary input, optionally augmented with serialized narrative constraints: an image caption with commonsense entity links merged inline (e.g., 'A woman (Samantha) wearing a green shirt...') followed by bracketed discourse features ([Characters], [Time of Day], [Location]). The LLM constraint generator (Llama3.1-70B with LoRA) produces these constraints from the text alone, while the vision model (ARLDM, StoryGen, or MM-Interleaved) then generates one RGB image per scene. The benchmark itself comprises ~25K visual-textual narrative pairs from VWP, Storyboard20K, and StorySalon, each annotated with dense captions, entity links, and global/scene-specific discourse features.

## Method

VinaBench constructs two types of constraints via a hybrid VLM+LLM pipeline: (1) commonsense links that ground visual entities (extracted from Mantis-Idefics2 dense captions via Llama3.1) to their corresponding textual narrative phrases, bridging the manifestation gap; and (2) discourse features comprising global character profiles and appearance style, plus per-scene presented characters, time of day, and location (annotated by LLaVA-OneVision-72B and MiniCPM-V-2.6). For evaluation, the paper introduces ranking-based alignment metrics (CLIP-T-MRR, VQA-MRR) that rank a generated image against a pool of 100 top-aligned candidates rather than comparing to a single reference, plus five VQA-based fine-grained alignment metrics and three consistency metrics (style, character, location) scored as VLM Yes-probabilities. The key experimental finding is that inserting LLM-generated constraints as an intermediate planning step before image generation significantly improves both faithfulness and cross-scene consistency across all three tested vision models, with ablations confirming that both commonsense links and discourse features contribute independently.

## Results

On VWP narratives, augmenting all three generative models with LLM-generated narrative constraints yields substantial gains: ARLDM improves FID from 42.6 to 37.6 and CLIP-T-MRR from 0.110 to 0.151; StoryGen improves FID from 78.6 to 52.1 and CLIP-T-MRR from 0.100 to 0.106; MM-Interleaved improves FID from 48.3 to 42.2 and CLIP-T-MRR from 0.066 to 0.111. Gold constraints provide a further marginal boost (e.g., ARLDM FID 35.3, CLIP-T-MRR 0.155). However, all models remain well below gold references (CLIP-T-MRR 0.159, non-character alignment 0.776, style consistency 0.971), indicating significant room for improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three visual narrative generation baselines—ARLDM (WACV 2024), StoryGen (CVPR 2024), and MM-Interleaved—under three settings: no constraints, LLM-generated constraints, and gold (oracle) constraints. All three models show consistent and substantial improvements when augmented with LLM-generated constraints: ARLDM gains +5.0 FID and +0.041 CLIP-T-MRR; StoryGen gains +26.5 FID and +0.006 CLIP-T-MRR; MM-Interleaved gains +6.1 FID and +0.045 CLIP-T-MRR. StoryGen benefits the most in absolute FID terms (78.6→52.1) but the least in CLIP-T-MRR, suggesting its base model is weakest at narrative alignment. Gold constraints yield only marginal additional gains over LLM constraints (e.g., ARLDM FID 37.6→35.3), indicating that the LLM constraint generator captures most of the benefit. Across all models and settings, generated narratives still trail gold references by a wide margin (e.g., non-character alignment 0.674 vs. 0.776 for ARLDM w/ LLM Cons.), confirming that current visual narrative generation remains far from human-level faithfulness and consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior visual narrative generation methods (ARLDM, StoryGen, MM-Interleaved) that learn a direct text-to-image-sequence mapping, by introducing explicit intermediate constraint planning (commonsense entity links and discourse features) as a scaffold between input narrative and generated images. It extends the visual-linguistic alignment literature, which has focused on explicit token-region or scene-graph matching, toward implicit commonsense alignment where visual manifestations (e.g., a sad face for 'bad news') are not lexically present in the text. The evaluation framework builds on VQAScore-style VLM-based scoring but repurposes it for ranking-based alignment and multi-image consistency assessment, drawing on Cohn's visual narrative discourse structure (constituency phases, scene dynamics) to define what consistency means across a narrative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VinaBench fills a critical gap by providing the first systematic benchmark with annotated commonsense and discourse constraints for visual narrative generation, along with reference-free and consistency-aware metrics that address known biases of FID and fixed-range CLIP scoring. The consistent improvement from constraint-based planning across three architecturally distinct models validates the intermediate-planning paradigm, yet all models remain substantially below gold references on every metric, indicating that faithful and self-consistent visual storytelling is far from solved. Open questions include whether the constraint-planning approach generalizes to longer narratives, whether the LLM-generated constraints can be replaced by learned representations, and how to close the remaining gap to human-level visual coherence.
