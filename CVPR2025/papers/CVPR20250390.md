## Problem

Existing Earth observation foundation models are trained on single datasets with fixed input configurations (specific resolution, scale, and modality combinations), making them inapplicable to new data types without full retraining. This defeats the purpose of foundation models, which should seamlessly integrate diverse sensors and scales. No prior approach simultaneously handles the full diversity of EO data in terms of spatial resolution (0.2–250 m), tile size, channel count, and temporal extent within a single unified architecture.

## Input / Output

The model consumes spatially aligned multimodal tiles of arbitrary size (0.36–164 hectares) and resolution (0.2 m to 250 m per pixel), with 3–11 channels per modality and temporal extents ranging from single-date images to multi-week time series. Patches of varying physical size are processed without rescaling; only the number of sub-patch tokens changes. For segmentation, predictions are produced at sub-patch resolution (finer than the patch grid) by concatenating sub-patch embeddings with multimodal patch embeddings, yielding pixel-level masks. For classification, a [CLS] token in the combiner's cross-attention module produces a single tile-level embedding mapped to label logits.

## Method

AnySat combines a scale-adaptive patch encoder with a JEPA-based self-supervised training objective. The patch encoder subdivides each spatial patch into fixed-size sub-patches, projects them via modality-specific MLPs (with a lightweight temporal attention encoder for time series), and aggregates them through a shared transformer, so that changing resolution only alters the token count rather than the network structure. Training uses a student-teacher JEPA framework where the student applies rectangular patch dropping plus random modality and temporal masking, then predicts the teacher's (EMA) embeddings of dropped patches via an L2 loss, while a modified InfoNCE contrastive loss enforces cross-modal consistency at each spatial location. A cross-attention modality combiner (from OmniSat) fuses per-modality embeddings into a unified representation. Over 75% of parameters are shared across all 11 modalities and resolutions, and the model is trained simultaneously on five heterogeneous datasets (GeoPlex) by randomly sampling a dataset, patch size, and batch each iteration.

## Results

AnySat achieves state-of-the-art results across 9 downstream tasks on 10 datasets. Headline improvements include +0.9 weighted F1 on TreeSatAI-TS, +2.8 mIoU on PASTIS-HD classification, +3.6 mIoU on SICKLE, +10.2 mIoU on BraDD-S1TS change detection, and +11.0 OA on TimeSen2Crop. On Sen1Floods11, a linearly probed AnySat outperforms a fine-tuned Prithvi-EO-2.0 with 10⁵ fewer free parameters. Inference speed is 3 ms per tile, faster than Scale-MAE (10 ms) and comparable to DOFA (3 ms) and OmniSat (2 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnySat outperforms Prithvi [29] on HLS Burn Scar despite GeoPlex containing no HLS data (249K km² vs. Prithvi's 252M km² of HLS training data). A linearly probed AnySat beats fine-tuned Prithvi-EO-2.0 [47] on Sen1Floods11 while using 10⁵ fewer trainable parameters. On PLANTED, AnySat reaches near state-of-the-art performance against ViViT [2] models that were trained on a withheld dataset with ~80% more data of the same type. On FLAIR, AnySat performs close to the UT&T [20] state-of-the-art despite accessing only 6.5% of the Sentinel-2 tile extent used by that method. Against Scale-MAE [42], SatMAE [14], and CROMA [18], AnySat requires only input-layer modifications to match target spectral bands and consistently outperforms them across classification and segmentation tasks. Inference speed comparisons show AnySat (3 ms) is 3.3× faster than Scale-MAE (10 ms) and on par with DOFA [54] (3 ms) and OmniSat [4] (2 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

AnySat extends the JEPA self-supervised framework (Assran et al., CVPR 2023) from natural images to multimodal Earth Observation, eliminating the modality-specific decoders that plague MAE-based EO models like SatMAE and CROMA. It builds directly on the authors' prior OmniSat work (ECCV 2024) for cross-attention modality fusion and contrastive alignment, and incorporates Scale-MAE's ground-sampling-distance positional encodings to handle variable resolutions. The key departure from prior EO foundation models (Prithvi, ScaleMAE, SatMAE) is the first simultaneous self-supervised training on a curated collection of heterogeneous datasets spanning 11 sensors, resolutions from 0.2 m to 250 m, and both single-date and time-series observations, rather than a single fixed-format dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental practical limitation of EO foundation models: the inability to handle new sensor configurations or spatial scales without retraining. By sharing over 75% of parameters across all modalities and resolutions, AnySat demonstrates that a single model can serve as a general-purpose EO backbone, reducing the need for task-specific architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly global, multi-continental training data, how it handles temporal misalignment across sensors, and whether the JEPA objective can be further extended to incorporate geographic or metadata-based supervision. The finding that 249K km² of curated multi-sensor data outperforms 252M km² of single-sensor data suggests data diversity may matter more than sheer volume for EO representation learning.
