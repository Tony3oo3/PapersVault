## Problem

Purely SSM-based vision models suffer from two critical issues: training instability when scaled to large parameter counts (e.g., SiMBA-L with an MLP mixer collapses to 49% accuracy), and computational inefficiency because the VSS block's input/output projections and depth-wise convolutions scale linearly with the number of input channels. This paper addresses both by proposing a parameter-efficient grouped scanning architecture and a distillation-based training objective that stabilizes large-model convergence.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, 1280×800 for detection, 512×512 for segmentation) and produces task-specific outputs: 1000-class labels for ImageNet-1K, bounding boxes and instance masks for MS-COCO, and per-pixel class maps for ADE20K. Internally, the input feature map is split into four channel groups of C/4 channels each, and each group is independently scanned in a distinct spatial direction before being concatenated back to full channel dimension.

## Method

The core contribution is the Modulated Group Mamba layer, which divides input channels into four groups and applies an independent VSSS block (Mamba selective scan + FFN) to each group, with each group scanning in one of four directions (left-to-right, right-to-left, top-to-bottom, bottom-to-top) to achieve comprehensive spatial coverage at reduced per-group channel cost. A Channel Affinity Modulation (CAM) operator, computed via global average pooling and a two-layer MLP with sigmoid gating, recalibrates the concatenated group outputs to restore cross-channel communication lost by the grouping. To stabilize training of larger variants, a distillation loss is added to the standard cross-entropy objective, using RegNetY-16G as a fixed teacher with a weighted combination (α·L_CE + (1−α)·L_CE_teacher). The full model uses a four-stage hierarchical Swin-style architecture with patch embedding via two 3×3 stride-2 convolutions, producing feature maps at H/4, H/8, H/16, and H/32 resolutions.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, GroupMamba-T achieves 83.3% top-1 accuracy with only 23M parameters and 4.5 GFLOPs, while GroupMamba-B reaches 84.5% top-1 with 57M parameters and 14 GFLOPs. On MS-COCO, GroupMamba-T attains 47.6 box AP and 42.9 mask AP with Mask R-CNN. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, GroupMamba-T achieves 48.6 mIoU (single-scale) and 49.2 mIoU (multi-scale) with UperNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroupMamba-T (23M params) outperforms VMamba-T (31M) by 0.8% top-1 accuracy while using 26% fewer parameters, and beats LocalVMamba-T (26M) by 0.6%. Against non-SSM baselines, GroupMamba-T surpasses ConvNeXt-T by 1.2% and Swin-T by 2.0% with fewer parameters. At the larger scale, GroupMamba-B (57M) exceeds VMamba-B (89M) by 0.6% top-1 while reducing parameters by 36%, and GroupMamba-S (34M) edges out LocalVMamba-S (50M) by 0.2% with 32% fewer parameters. On MS-COCO detection, GroupMamba-T achieves 47.6 box AP, marginally surpassing VMamba-T (47.4) and LocalVMamba-T (46.7) with 20% fewer parameters than VMamba-T. On ADE20K segmentation, GroupMamba-T leads all compared backbones with 48.6 mIoU (SS), exceeding VMamba-T (48.3) and LocalVMamba-T (47.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba/VMamba line of visual state-space models by introducing a grouped variant of the VSS block, directly inspired by group convolutions in CNNs. Rather than making the selective scan more complex (as in LocalVMamba's windowed scanning or EfficientVMamba's atrous scanning), it reduces per-channel compute by splitting channels into four groups, each scanned in a single spatial direction, and compensates for limited cross-group interaction with a Channel Affinity Modulation operator reminiscent of SE blocks. The distillation-based training objective, borrowed from DeiT-style knowledge distillation, is repurposed here specifically to stabilize large SSM training, addressing a known failure mode (SiMBA-L's 49% accuracy) that prior visual Mamba papers had not explicitly tackled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a 26–36% parameter reduction over comparable VMamba models is achievable without sacrificing accuracy, which matters for deploying SSM-based backbones in resource-constrained settings. The distillation trick for stabilizing large SSM training is a simple, broadly applicable recipe that other SSM-based vision architectures could adopt. Open questions include whether the four-group, four-direction strategy generalizes to video understanding and time-series tasks (acknowledged as future work), and whether the grouping factor of four is optimal or whether other group sizes/direction assignments could yield further gains.
