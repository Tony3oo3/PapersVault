## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generators either require hours of per-object optimization or depend on small, low-quality 3D datasets for feed-forward training, limiting both speed and generalization to complex prompts. Adapting Stable Diffusion directly into a native 3D generator is blocked by its training paradigm, which requires 3D ground-truth latents to supervise denoising — precisely the data that is scarce. This paper eliminates the 3D data dependency entirely while preserving SD's generative prior.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt and produces a textured 3D mesh in 1.2 seconds via a Triplane representation: a geometry Triplane (SDF + deformation values) and a texture Triplane (RGB), each at 256×256 resolution with 32 channels, decoded by a two-layer MLP per spatial point. The VAE decoder outputs six 256×256×32 feature planes (6×32×32×4 in latent space after 8× compression), which are mesh-extracted via SDF-based volumetric rendering at 64×64 and mesh rasterization at 512×512 during training.

## Method

Progressive Rendering Distillation (PRD) replaces the standard denoising objective with a multi-step progressive scheme: starting from random noise, the adapted U-Net denoises the latent over K=4 timesteps, and at each step the intermediate latent is decoded to a Triplane, rendered from four sampled camera views, and supervised by three multi-view teachers (SD for text fidelity, MVDream for multi-view consistency, RichDreamer for geometry via normal/depth) using Asynchronous Score Distillation. Gradients are detached between steps to reduce memory and prevent explosion. Parameter-Efficient Triplane Adaptation (PETA) freezes SD and adds only 2.5% trainable parameters via LoRA on convolution and cross-attention layers, plus plane-specific LoRA layers in self-attention that allow each of the six feature planes to maintain its unique distribution while enabling cross-plane interaction. This combination yields TriplaneTurbo, a four-step native 3D generator that outperforms prior methods in both CLIP score (68.2 vs. 67.4 best baseline) and latency (1.2s vs. 3.0s best baseline).

## Results

On the DreamFusion gallery test set, TriplaneTurbo achieves a CLIP Score of 68.2 and CLIP R@1 of 32.3, outperforming all seven compared text-to-mesh generators. It also delivers the fastest inference at 1.23 seconds per mesh, roughly 2.4× faster than the next-best method (PI3D at 3.00 s) and 45× faster than LGM (56.1 s). When the training text corpus is scaled from 360K to 1.6M prompts, CLIP Score improves to 75.1 and R@1 to 46.0, demonstrating the data-scaling advantage of the 3D-data-free training scheme.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriplaneTurbo wins on both quality metrics (CLIP Score and R@1) and speed against all seven baselines. The closest quality competitor is LGM (67.4 C.S., 28.3 R@1), but LGM requires 56.1 s per mesh—45× slower than TriplaneTurbo's 1.23 s. PI3D is the nearest speed competitor (3.00 s) yet trails by 2.3 CLIP Score points and 7.1 R@1 points. Direct3D and GVGEN, despite being recent feed-forward methods, show particularly weak R@1 scores (4.33 and 2.44 respectively), indicating poor pose consistency. The ablation with K=1 (single-step, no progressive denoising) drops to 50.9 C.S. and 14.4 R@1, confirming that the progressive multi-step distillation is essential for quality. Scaling text data from 360K to 1.6M prompts yields a 7-point CLIP Score gain (68.2→75.1) and a 14-point R@1 gain (32.3→46.0), a benefit unavailable to data-driven baselines constrained by limited 3D datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) adapting pretrained 2D diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) for native 3D generation, as attempted by PI3D and HexaGen, and (2) data-free multi-view score distillation for training 3D generators, as explored by ATT3D and Scale-Dreamer. The key departure is that prior SD-adaptation methods still require 3D ground-truth latents to supervise denoising, while prior data-free distillation methods train generators from scratch without leveraging SD's generative prior. PRD bridges this gap by using progressive denoising from random noise (rather than ground-truth latents) as the training signal, with multi-view teachers (MVDream, RichDreamer) providing supervision through rendered views, and PETA introduces plane-specific LoRA in self-attention as a parameter-efficient adaptation strategy distinct from full fine-tuning or vanilla LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for 3D ground-truth data, PRD removes the fundamental bottleneck that has limited text-to-3D generation quality and generalizability, enabling straightforward data scaling (360K → 1.6M text prompts yields ~7% CLIP improvement). The 1.2-second inference time via 4-step generation is a practical milestone for interactive 3D content creation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex 3D representations (beyond Triplane), multi-object scenes with precise counts, and whether the progressive denoising paradigm transfers to other pretrained backbones like DiT. The authors note limitations in full-body human generation (facial/hand details) and precise multi-object placement.
