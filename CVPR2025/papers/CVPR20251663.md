## Problem

RGB-based 3D reconstruction fails in low-texture, low-light, and low-albedo scenes, while conventional sparse LiDAR (projecting individual dots) provides poor scene coverage when input views are limited, leaving large depth gaps. This paper asks whether an alternative 'diffuse' LiDAR that emits a wide flash and captures spatially blurred time-of-flight measurements can, counterintuitively, outperform sparse LiDAR for robust handheld scanning in these challenging conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251663/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 10–90 multi-view RGB images (from a RealSense D435i) and co-located diffuse LiDAR transient histograms (from an AMS TMF8828 SPAD sensor with 18×12 pixels aggregated into 8×8 measurement zones, each with ~4.9° IFOV, 40 ps bin resolution, and 1.5 m max range), plus COLMAP-estimated camera poses. Unlike sparse LiDAR that yields per-pixel point depths, the diffuse LiDAR input is a 3D array of time-resolved photon counts per pixel, where each pixel integrates light from a wide cone of scene points. The output is a 3D Gaussian surfel scene from which color-3D meshes, dense depth maps, and surface normals are extracted.

## Method

The reconstruction uses a Gaussian surfel representation (flattened 2D Gaussians with position, quaternion orientation, anisotropic scale, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color) rendered via differentiable rasterization. For the LiDAR signal, rays are sampled within each pixel's wide IFOV cone, and surfel contributions are assigned to temporal histogram bins via soft histogramming (distributing each surfel's opacity across two adjacent bins) to enable gradient flow. The key novelty is a scene-adaptive loss that computes per-patch RGB 'usefulness' from texture variance and SNR, feeds it through a sigmoid to produce a spatially varying weight, and uses that weight to balance an RGB loss (patch-weighted L1 + SSIM) against a LiDAR loss (KL divergence between rendered and ground-truth histograms), so the optimizer automatically prioritizes LiDAR in low-texture/low-SNR regions and RGB elsewhere. A depth-normal consistency regularization from the surfel framework completes the objective.

## Results

On the most challenging No-Texture scenes (10 input views), the proposed method achieves depth MAE of 0.041–0.045 m and normal MAE of 13.5–16.4° across four scenes, substantially outperforming sparse LiDAR (0.106–0.125 m depth MAE, 37–44° normal MAE) and monocular depth (0.716–0.793 m depth MAE). In Textured-Object scenes, depth MAE drops to 0.024–0.033 m (vs. 0.080–0.115 m for sparse LiDAR), and in Full-Texture scenes the method still leads with 0.016–0.025 m depth MAE. In the LiDAR-only ablation (Table 2), diffuse LiDAR achieves 0.036–0.041 m depth MAE, roughly 3–7× better than TransientNeRF with sparse histograms (0.221–0.281 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Surfels RGB Only [7], the proposed method wins decisively on depth and normal accuracy in every texture setting; in No-Texture scenes the gap is extreme (e.g., Blender: 0.045 m vs. 0.081 m depth MAE, 15.68° vs. 36.74° normal MAE), and RGB-only fails entirely on some scenes. Against Surfels RGB w/ Mono.Depth [50], the method dominates in low-texture settings (Blender No-Texture: 0.045 m vs. 0.716 m depth MAE) but is slightly behind in Full-Texture PSNR (30.67 vs. 27.77 on Blender), indicating the adaptive loss correctly prioritizes RGB when texture is abundant. Against Surfels RGB w/ Sparse LiDAR [8], the method consistently outperforms on both depth and normal MAE across all four texture levels and all four scenes; the advantage is largest in No-Texture (≈2.5–3× lower depth MAE) and Textured-Object (≈3–4× lower depth MAE) settings. In the LiDAR-only ablation (Table 2), diffuse LiDAR also outperforms TransientNeRF [27] with sparse histograms by 5–7× in depth MAE (e.g., Blender: 0.041 m vs. 0.281 m), confirming that the spatial coverage of diffuse illumination is the key factor rather than the rendering framework alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Gaussian Surfels [7] for scene representation and 3D Gaussian Splatting [18] for the underlying primitive, extending them with a differentiable transient-histogram rendering pass inspired by TransientNeRF [27]. The key departure from prior LiDAR+RGB fusion work (e.g., depth-supervised NeRF [8]) is the replacement of sparse point-depth supervision with diffuse (blurred) LiDAR histograms, which necessitates a new scene-adaptive loss that dynamically reweights RGB and LiDAR signals per patch based on local texture and SNR. A linear recoverability analysis (rank of the measurement matrix) provides theoretical grounding for why diffuse LiDAR's broader spatial coverage can outperform sparse LiDAR's per-point precision under limited views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for handheld and mobile 3D scanning in environments where RGB cues are unreliable (textureless walls, low albedo, low light), using only low-cost, commercially available sensors (AMS TMF8828 SPAD array + RealSense D435i). However, the current scope is narrow: static, non-specular objects, 8×8 pixel LiDAR arrays, and COLMAP-estimated poses, with real-world validation limited to qualitative comparisons on a small number of scenes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, specular materials, higher-resolution SPAD arrays, and whether joint pose estimation with diffuse LiDAR can remove the COLMAP dependency.
