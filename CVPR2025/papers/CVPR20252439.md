## Problem

Gaussian splatting and single-view depth estimation are typically studied in isolation, each with complementary weaknesses: multi-view feature matching fails on occlusions, texture-less regions, and reflective surfaces, while monocular depth lacks cross-view scale consistency needed for 3D reconstruction. The paper asks whether connecting these two tasks can yield mutual improvements in both depth quality and novel view synthesis fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252439/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N multi-view RGB images (2 to 12 views) with known camera projection matrices as input, operating at resolutions from 256×256 (RealEstate10K) up to 512×960 (high-res DL3DV). It produces per-pixel depth maps for each input view and, for the Gaussian splatting task, per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters (position, opacity, covariance, color) that enable feed-forward novel view rendering. The full pipeline processes 12 input views at 512×960 in 0.6 seconds on a single GPU.

## Method

DepthSplat uses a two-branch architecture: a multi-view branch (weight-sharing ResNet + Swin Transformer with cross-attention over top-2 nearest views) constructs plane-sweep cost volumes via dot-product feature matching, and a single-view branch extracts features from a frozen pre-trained Depth Anything V2 ViT backbone. The cost volumes and monocular features are simply concatenated in the channel dimension and fed to a 2D U-Net that regresses depth over D candidates with a softmax-weighted average, followed by a 2-scale hierarchical refinement at 2× resolution. For Gaussian splatting, depth maps are unprojected to 3D as Gaussian centers, and a DPT head predicts the remaining parameters (opacity, covariance, color) from concatenated image, depth, and feature inputs. The full model is trained with a photometric rendering loss (MSE + LPIPS), which also serves as an unsupervised pre-training objective for the depth model on large-scale posed multi-view datasets like RealEstate10K, eliminating the need for ground-truth depth supervision during pre-training.

## Results

On the RealEstate10K two-view novel view synthesis benchmark, DepthSplat achieves PSNR 27.47, SSIM 0.889, and LPIPS 0.114, outperforming the previous best (TranSplat) by +0.78 dB PSNR. On ScanNet two-view depth estimation, DepthSplat with Gaussian splatting pre-training achieves Abs Rel 3.8 and RMSE 0.114, beating the prior best (NeuralRecon, Abs Rel 4.7) by a clear margin. On the more challenging DL3DV dataset with 6 input views, DepthSplat reaches PSNR 24.19 vs. MVSplat's 22.93, and generalizes zero-shot to DL3DV with PSNR 27.66 (vs. MVSplat's 24.14). The model processes 12 input views at 512×960 in 0.6 seconds on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DepthSplat wins against all 13 baselines on every reported metric. In depth estimation on ScanNet (Table 5), it outperforms UniMatch (Abs Rel 5.9→3.8), NeuralRecon (4.7→3.8), DRO (5.3→3.8), DeepV2D (5.7→3.8), BA-Net (16.1→3.8), and DeMoN (23.1→3.8), with the largest gains over older methods. In novel view synthesis on RealEstate10K (Table 6), it beats the closest competitor TranSplat by +0.78 dB PSNR and MVSplat by +1.08 dB, while older methods like pixelNeRF lag by over 7 dB. On DL3DV (Table 7), DepthSplat consistently surpasses MVSplat across 2, 4, and 6 input views (e.g., +1.77 dB PSNR at 6 views), and the gap widens with fewer views, indicating superior robustness to sparse inputs. In cross-dataset generalization (Table 8), zero-shot transfer from RealEstate10K to DL3DV yields PSNR 27.66 vs. MVSplat's 24.14 (+3.52 dB), while on the easier ACID dataset the margin is smaller (+0.22 dB). The paper also notes that DepthSplat scales more efficiently to additional views (0.132 s at 6 views vs. MVSplat's 0.263 s) due to its lightweight local matching strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DepthSplat extends the feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting line (MVSplat, pixelSplat) by augmenting the multi-view cost volume branch with pre-trained monocular depth features from Depth Anything V2, addressing the well-known failure modes of feature matching in texture-less, occluded, and reflective regions. It departs from prior monocular–multi-view fusion approaches (AFNet, TranSplat) by using a simple channel-wise concatenation of off-the-shelf monocular features with cost volumes rather than attention-based or scale-alignment mechanisms, and by combining both cues early to avoid error propagation. The multi-view feature extraction follows UniMatch's Swin Transformer and plane-sweep stereo formulation, while the Gaussian parameter prediction and rendering follow the 3DGS framework of Kerbl et al. A key conceptual departure is using the fully differentiable Gaussian splatting rendering loss as an unsupervised pre-training objective for the depth model on large-scale posed multi-view datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical and computationally cheap recipe for pre-training depth models on large-scale multi-view posed datasets (e.g., RealEstate10K with ~67K videos) without any ground-truth depth, which is increasingly relevant as new multi-view datasets and generative models proliferate. The finding that simple concatenation of pre-trained monocular features with cost volumes outperforms attention-based fusion and explicit scale alignment suggests that the quality of the pre-trained monocular prior matters more than the sophistication of the fusion mechanism. Limitations include the requirement for known camera poses (challenging for very sparse views) and the pixel-aligned Gaussian representation, which scales poorly with large numbers of input views. Open questions include whether pose-free variants can retain the cross-task benefits and whether the unsupervised pre-training signal generalizes to even larger or more diverse multi-view corpora.
