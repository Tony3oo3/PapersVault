## Problem

Existing dense 3D reconstruction systems from monocular RGB video face a fundamental trade-off: offline SfM+MVS pipelines produce high-quality results but cannot run in real time, while online dense SLAM methods either sacrifice accuracy/completeness or run well below real-time speeds (e.g., NICER-SLAM at <1 FPS). Recent end-to-end approaches like DUSt3R are fast for two-view prediction but require expensive global optimization for multi-view scenes, and Spann3R's incremental pipeline accumulates noticeable drift. The paper addresses how to achieve real-time, high-quality, and complete dense reconstruction without explicitly solving any camera parameters.

## Input / Output

The system takes a monocular RGB video as input, which is converted into overlapping clips via a sliding window mechanism (initial window length L=5, then L=11 for incremental frames). All images are center-cropped to 224×224 pixels before processing. The output is a globally consistent dense 3D point cloud in a unified world coordinate system, along with per-frame 3D pointmaps and confidence maps. The entire pipeline runs at approximately 24–25 FPS on a single NVIDIA 4090D GPU, and camera poses can be optionally derived post-hoc via PnP-RANSAC from the predicted pointmaps.

## Method

SLAM3R employs a two-hierarchy feed-forward framework that eliminates explicit camera parameter estimation. The Image-to-Points (I2P) module uses a multi-branch ViT with a shared image encoder, a keyframe decoder featuring a novel multi-view cross-attention (queries from the keyframe attend independently to each supporting frame's tokens, then aggregate via max-pooling), and a supporting decoder; it directly regresses dense 3D pointmaps and confidence maps for all frames in a window relative to a selected keyframe. The Local-to-World (L2W) module incrementally registers new local pointmaps into a global coordinate system by encoding 3D points via patch embedding, adding them to visual tokens, and processing them through registration and scene decoders that share the keyframe decoder architecture. A reservoir-sampling buffering set maintains a bounded collection of scene frames, and a lightweight retrieval module selects the top-K most relevant historical frames as global reference for each new keyframe, performing implicit re-localization. Both networks are initialized from DUSt3R pre-trained weights and trained end-to-end with confidence-aware L1 losses on a mixture of ScanNet++, Aria Synthetic Environments, and CO3D-v2 (~850K clips).

## Results

On the 7 Scenes dataset, SLAM3R achieves an average reconstruction accuracy of 2.13 cm and completeness of 2.34 cm at ~25 FPS, outperforming all real-time baselines in accuracy while matching or exceeding them in completeness. On the Replica dataset, SLAM3R reaches 3.57 cm accuracy and 2.62 cm completeness at ~24 FPS, surpassing all SLAM-based methods (NICER-SLAM, DROID-SLAM, DIM-SLAM, GO-SLAM) in both metrics. The method also derives camera poses via PnP-RANSAC from predicted point clouds, achieving 8.41 cm ATE-RMSE on 7 Scenes and 6.61 cm on Replica, competitive with optimization-based methods like DUSt3R (8.02 / 4.76 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DUSt3R (offline, <1 FPS), SLAM3R achieves comparable accuracy (2.13 vs 2.19 cm on 7 Scenes) while dramatically improving completeness (2.34 vs 3.24 cm) and running 25× faster; on Replica, SLAM3R also edges out DUSt3R in both accuracy (3.57 vs 3.49 cm) and completeness (2.62 vs 2.48 cm). Against Spann3R (>50 FPS), SLAM3R substantially reduces drift, achieving 38% lower average accuracy (2.13 vs 3.42 cm on 7 Scenes) and 72% lower accuracy on Replica (3.57 vs 10.32 cm), at the cost of roughly 2× lower throughput. Against MASt3R (≤1 FPS), SLAM3R wins on both accuracy and completeness across both datasets while being orders of magnitude faster. Among SLAM baselines on Replica, SLAM3R outperforms NICER-SLAM (3.57 vs 3.65 cm accuracy, 2.62 vs 4.16 cm completeness), DROID-SLAM (3.57 vs 5.50 cm accuracy, 2.62 vs 12.29 cm completeness), DIM-SLAM (3.57 vs 11.60 cm accuracy), and GO-SLAM (3.57 vs 3.81 cm accuracy, 2.62 vs 4.79 cm completeness), all at equal or higher FPS. For camera pose estimation (ATE-RMSE), SLAM3R is competitive with DUSt3R on 7 Scenes (8.41 vs 8.02 cm) but trails specialized pose methods like DROID-SLAM (0.33 cm) and GO-SLAM (0.39 cm) on Replica, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation of the pose-free design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLAM3R extends DUSt3R's end-to-end point-map regression from two-view stereo to multi-view video via a sliding-window mechanism and a novel multi-view cross-attention decoder, while departing from Spann3R's frame-by-frame incremental pipeline (which accumulates drift) by processing multiple frames jointly at each hierarchy. The two-hierarchy design—Image-to-Points for local reconstruction and Local-to-World for global registration—replaces the explicit camera-pose-then-triangulate paradigm of traditional SLAM (DROID-SLAM, NICER-SLAM) and the global optimization step of DUSt3R/MASt3R with purely feed-forward neural networks. A reservoir-sampling retrieval module for selecting long-range reference frames is a practical addition not present in prior DUSt3R extensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLAM3R demonstrates that high-quality dense 3D reconstruction at 20+ FPS is achievable without ever explicitly solving camera parameters, challenging the long-standing assumption that pose estimation is a prerequisite for accurate scene mapping. The practical impact is significant: it bridges the quality-efficiency gap that has separated offline optimization methods (DUSt3R, NICER-SLAM) from real-time SLAM systems. Open questions include whether the absence of global bundle adjustment limits scalability to very long sequences, how the system handles dynamic or partially observed scenes, and whether the derived camera poses can be improved to match dedicated localization systems.
