## Problem

Extending diffusion-based detectors like DiffusionDet to oriented object detection faces three unresolved challenges: the model lacks rotation-equivariance for arbitrarily oriented objects, random initial boxes fail to cover objects with extreme aspect ratios and dense arrangements, and oriented boxes are geometrically misaligned with axis-aligned image feature maps. No prior diffusion-based detector addresses all three issues simultaneously for oriented detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251908/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (800×800 for DIOR-R, 1024×1024 patches for DOTA) and produces oriented bounding boxes parameterized as (cx, cy, w, h, angle) with class labels. During inference, the model starts from standard normal noise and iteratively denoises via DDIM sampling over a configurable number of steps (default 1) and boxes (default 300), with a learned conditional encoder providing initial box locations rather than purely random ones. The box renewal strategy from DiffusionDet is retained to revive high-scoring predictions across iterations.

## Method

ReDiffDet represents each oriented box as a 2D Gaussian distribution (mean = center, covariance = R·Λ·Rᵀ where R is the rotation matrix and Λ encodes width/height scaled by factor m), and formulates detection as a denoising diffusion process from standard normal to this Gaussian. The paper proves that the reverse process is rotation-equivariant under SO(2) when the network maintains equivariance, which is achieved by borrowing ReResNet + ReFPN + RiRoI Align from ReDet. A conditional encoder (box subnet + score subnet) generates top-scoring conditional boxes that anchor the denoising process, preventing boxes from being randomly scattered across the image. The aligned decoder stacks RiRoI Align, self-attention, and instance interaction layers to warp region features according to the current box orientation, resolving the misalignment between rotated boxes and axis-aligned feature maps. Training uses set prediction loss on the predicted boxes and labels, with noise added to sampled points of ground-truth box distributions at random timesteps.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, ReDiffDet achieves 75.45% AP50 with ReResNet50 and 80.70% AP50 under multi-scale training/testing, outperforming the diffusion-based baseline DiffusionDet-O (60.50%) by 14.95 points. On DOTA-v1.5 and DOTA-v2.0, ReDiffDet reaches 68.00% and 55.76% AP50 respectively, surpassing all compared methods including DCFL (67.37%) and OrientedFormer (67.06%) on v1.5. On DIOR-R, ReDiffDet achieves 68.05% AP50 with ReResNet50, outperforming OrientedFormer (67.28%) and DCFL (66.80%). On COCO, the conditional encoder improves DiffusionDet's mAP from 43.2 to 45.0 (+1.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDiffDet dramatically outperforms the primary diffusion-based baseline DiffusionDet-O by 14.95 AP50 points on DOTA-v1.0 (75.45% vs 60.50%) and 18.14 points on DIOR-R (68.05% vs 49.91%), validating the rotation-equivariant Gaussian representation, conditional encoder, and aligned decoder. Among traditional detectors, ReDiffDet (ReR50, 75.45%) is competitive with Oriented R-CNN (75.87%) and SCRDet++* (76.20%) on DOTA-v1.0, trailing by less than 1 point, while surpassing OrientedFormer (75.37%) and FRED (75.56%) is marginal. On DOTA-v1.5, ReDiffDet (68.00%) leads all compared methods including DCFL (67.37%), OrientedFormer (67.06%), and ReDet (66.86%). On DOTA-v2.0, ReDiffDet (55.76%) outperforms OrientedFormer (54.27%) and DCFL (51.57%). On DIOR-R small objects, ReDiffDet with ReR50 (68.05%) surpasses OrientedFormer (67.28%), DCFL (66.80%), and Oriented RePoints (66.71%), with the largest gap over DiffusionDet-O (49.91%). The conditioning strategy alone adds +1.8 mAP on COCO horizontal detection, confirming its generalizability beyond oriented boxes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReDiffDet extends DiffusionDet (ICCV 2023), which first cast horizontal object detection as a denoising diffusion process over box parameters, to the oriented object detection setting. The key conceptual departure is representing each oriented box as a 2D Gaussian distribution rather than DiffusionDet's collection of independent 1D Gaussians, which enables a provable rotation-equivariance property in the reverse process. For the equivariant feature extraction, the paper borrows ReDet's (CVPR 2021) ReResNet, ReFPN, and RiRoI Align components, positioning itself at the intersection of rotation-equivariant CNNs and diffusion-based generation. Additional departures include a conditional encoder that replaces DiffusionDet's random box initialization and an aligned decoder that warps features to match oriented box geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally prove rotation-equivariance within a diffusion-based detection framework, providing a theoretical guarantee that the generated box distribution is consistent under input rotation. The 2D Gaussian representation offers a geometrically natural parameterization for oriented boxes that could extend to other shapes (3D boxes, ellipses, spherical boxes) as the authors note. However, the equivariance proof only covers the DDIM sampling strategy, and the method's practical gains over strong non-diffusion baselines (e.g., Oriented R-CNN at 75.87% vs. ReDiffDet at 75.45% on DOTA-v1.0) are modest, leaving open whether the diffusion formulation offers advantages beyond theoretical elegance. The reliance on ReDet's backbone for equivariance also limits the generality of the claim.
