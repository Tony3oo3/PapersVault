## Problem

Most existing anomaly detection methods are designed for a single domain (industrial, medical, or video) and generalize poorly across domains due to inherent feature discrepancies and reliance on pre-trained networks with source-domain biases. Even domain-adaptive methods like ReContrast struggle to capture target-domain-relevant features and to effectively discriminate between normal and anomalous samples, particularly in supervised settings with limited anomalous data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250796/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

UniNet takes 256×256 RGB images (or video frames for Ped2) as input and produces three outputs: an image-level anomaly score computed via a dynamic weighted top-k mechanism, a pixel-level anomaly map formed by accumulating upsampled per-layer similarity maps, and a segmentation mask for localization. For the MVTec 3D-AD dataset, only RGB projections are used. The weighted decision mechanism adaptively selects the number of top anomaly-map values (v_w) per sample rather than using a fixed percentage, making the score computation sample-dependent.

## Method

UniNet builds on ReContrast's student-teacher-bottleneck architecture with WideResNet50 backbones and introduces four key components. A Multi-Scale Embedding Module (MEM) in the bottleneck splits channels into two paths with 3×3 and 7×7 kernels (reparameterized to 3×3 at inference) to capture multi-scale contextual relationships. Domain-Related Feature Selection (DFS) generates softmax weights from teacher features, fuses them with global spatial information via a learnable parameter θ, and gates student features to focus on target-domain-relevant representations. A similarity-contrastive loss (L_SC) maximizes diagonal similarity between student-teacher feature pairs for normal samples, while a margin loss (L_M) enforces a lower bound on normal-feature similarity and an upper bound on anomalous-feature similarity to sharpen class boundaries. At inference, a weighted decision mechanism transforms per-layer anomaly maps into a probability distribution via Softmax and dynamically determines the number of top values to average for the final image-level score.

## Results

On the standard MVTec AD benchmark, UniNet achieves 99.90% image-level AUROC and 96.00% PRO, outperforming the strongest competitor RealNet (99.65% I-AUROC, 93.07% PRO) and the ReContrast baseline (99.46% I-AUROC, 95.20% PRO). On the more challenging MVTec 3D-AD dataset, UniNet reaches 95.76% I-AUROC, surpassing the previous best (ReContrast, 88.63%) by 7.13%. In the medical domain, UniNet attains 100.0% I-AUROC on all three unsupervised datasets (APTOS, OCT2017, ISIC2018), and on the supervised VAD industrial benchmark it achieves 99.95% I-AUROC, exceeding all competing methods (including those augmented with SegAD) by at least 8.20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniNet outperforms all 29 compared baselines on the majority of metrics across 12 datasets. On MVTec AD, it leads in I-AUROC (99.90%) and PRO (96.00%) but trails RealNet by 0.22% on P-AUROC (98.81% vs. 99.03%). On BTAD, UniNet surpasses RealNet and ReConPatch by 1.66% and 1.93% in I-AUROC, while matching RealNet's P-AUROC (97.70% vs. 97.90%). The largest margin appears on MVTec 3D-AD, where UniNet beats ReContrast by 7.13% I-AUROC (95.76% vs. 88.63%) using only RGB input. In the medical domain, UniNet achieves a perfect 100.0% I-AUROC on APTOS, OCT2017, and ISIC2018, exceeding ReContrast by up to 9.85% (ISIC2018). On the supervised VAD benchmark, UniNet (99.95% I-AUROC) outperforms even methods equipped with the SegAD segmentation module by 8.20%. The only notable loss is a 0.6% mIoU deficit on the Kvasir polyp segmentation dataset compared to MEGANet, while UniNet still leads on DSC and all other medical/supervised metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniNet is a direct extension of ReContrast [17], inheriting its student-teacher-bottleneck architecture and contrastive learning paradigm for domain-adaptive anomaly detection. It departs from ReContrast in three key ways: introducing a Multi-Scale Embedding Module and Domain-Related Feature Selection in the bottleneck to capture target-domain-relevant features, adding a similarity-contrastive loss and margin loss to explicitly enforce class boundaries between normal and anomalous features, and proposing a weighted decision mechanism for dynamic anomaly scoring. Unlike ReContrast, which targets unsupervised AD, UniNet extends the framework to supervised settings by incorporating classification or segmentation losses, positioning itself as a truly unified solution across both paradigms and multiple application domains (industrial, medical, video).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-domain generalization problem is a genuine bottleneck in practical AD deployment, where organizations face diverse anomaly types (defects, polyps, behavioral anomalies) and cannot afford separate models per domain. UniNet's demonstration of a single framework achieving competitive or superior results across 12 datasets from three distinct domains is practically valuable. However, the 100% AUROC on all three medical datasets raises questions about dataset size and evaluation robustness, and the method still depends on a pre-trained WideResNet50 backbone, which the paper itself acknowledges may carry source-domain biases. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to truly unseen domains without any target-domain training data, how it scales to higher-dimensional inputs (volumetric 3D, long video sequences), and whether the modest per-component gains in ablation (each adding ~0.1–0.5%) would hold under different backbones or larger-scale training.
