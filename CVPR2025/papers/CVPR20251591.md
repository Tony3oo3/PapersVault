## Problem

State-of-the-art large video encoders reduce variable-duration videos to a single embedding by mean-pooling logits from multiple temporal clips, discarding chronological order, variable duration, and temporal variance in feature significance. Existing temporal modeling methods (e.g., ILA, ATM, SlowFast) require invasive architectural modifications and costly retraining, making them impractical for off-the-shelf frozen encoders. DejaVid addresses this gap with an encoder-agnostic, lightweight temporal matching layer that improves classification without any encoder retraining or architectural changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a variable-duration video by applying a temporal sliding window (e.g., 33 windows for Kinetics-400/HMDB51, 4|vid| windows for Something-Something V2) over a frozen VideoMAE V2-g encoder, producing a temporal sequence of embeddings (TSE) of shape T×N_f where T varies per video and N_f = 1408 + N_c. The output is a class label obtained by applying softmin over the time-weighted DTW distances between the input TSE and one learned centroid TSE per class, followed by cross-entropy training. Only the centroid TSEs (N_c × T_c × N_f) and per-timestep per-feature weight tensors (N_c × T_c × N_f) are learned, adding fewer than 1.8% parameters to the frozen encoder.

## Method

DejaVid reformulates Dynamic Time Warping as a differentiable neural network architecture: each diagonal of the DTW grid becomes a min-pooling layer (kernel 2, stride 1) followed by an additive skip-connection layer, reducing the critical path from O(nm) to O(n+m) and enabling parallel execution within each diagonal. The pointwise distance is a per-timestep, per-feature weighted Manhattan distance dist_w(u_i, a_i, b_j) = Σ_k u_{i,k}|a_{i,k} − b_{j,k}|, where the positive weight tensor U captures temporal variance in feature importance. Two key modifications to standard DTW improve stability: diagonal transitions are removed (fixing the warping path length to n+m−1), and frozen copies of centroids/weights are used for warping-path determination while gradients flow through the live parameters. Centroid TSEs are initialized via DBA (Dynamic Time Warping Barycenter Averaging) over training samples per class, and a custom CUDA kernel computes the weighted DTW distance and its gradients 161×–750× faster than a naive implementation by exploiting the slope/intercept decomposition of the weighted Manhattan terms.

## Results

DejaVid achieves Top-1 accuracy of 77.2% on Something-Something V2, 89.1% on Kinetics-400, and 88.6% on HMDB51, improving over the VideoMAE V2-g baseline (76.7%, 88.4%, 88.1%) by +0.5%, +0.7%, and +0.5% respectively. These gains are achieved with fewer than 1.8% additional learnable parameters (5.8M–17.6M on top of the 1013M encoder) and less than 3 hours of training time. On Something-Something V2, DejaVid also achieves the highest reported Top-5 accuracy of 96.3%, surpassing the baseline's 95.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline VideoMAE V2-g (1013M params), DejaVid wins on all three datasets: +0.5% Top-1 on Something-Something V2 (77.2 vs 76.7), +0.7% on Kinetics-400 (89.1 vs 88.4), and +0.5% on HMDB51 (88.6 vs 88.1), while adding only 5.8M–17.6M parameters. Among temporal-focused methods, DejaVid substantially outperforms ILA (70.2%), TAM (66.0%), SlowFast (61.7%), and MTV (68.5%) on Something-Something V2, and beats ILA (88.7%) and MTV (89.1%, tie) on Kinetics-400, though it trails ATM (89.4%) by 0.3% on K400. Against the largest SOTA models, DejaVid's 89.1% on K400 remains behind InternVideo2-6B (92.1%) and TubeViT (90.9%), but it matches or exceeds them on Something-Something V2 (77.2% vs 77.5% for InternVideo2-6B, 76.1% for TubeViT) while using an order of magnitude fewer additional parameters. The paper also shows that temporal supersampling alone (feeding 336 clips and averaging) does not improve over the standard 5×3 protocol, confirming that the gain comes from the learned temporal alignment rather than simply more encoder calls.

## Relation to Prior Work

DejaVid repurposes the classic Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) algorithm as a lightweight, encoder-agnostic classification head, departing from prior temporal-focused methods (ILA, ATM, TAM, SlowFast, MTV) that require invasive architectural modifications and costly retraining of the backbone. It extends DTW in three ways: adding learned per-timestep, per-feature weights to handle temporal variance in feature importance, removing diagonal transitions for stability, and reformulating the dynamic programming recurrence as a serial stack of min-pooling layers with additive skip-connections to enable backpropagation and parallelization. The centroid initialization via Dynamic Time Warping Barycenter Averaging (DBA) draws from the time-series clustering literature, and the softmin-based loss connects to differentiable DTW work (SoftDTW, DTWnet), though DejaVid's formulation is distinct in its weight learning and neural-network reinterpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the encoder-agnostic, no-retraining design: practitioners with a frozen billion-parameter video encoder can gain 0.5–0.7% top-1 accuracy with under 1.8% additional parameters and under 3 hours of training, a stark contrast to the field's trend of adding billions of parameters for sub-1% gains. However, the method is validated only on a single backbone (VideoMAE V2-g), the temporal weight component shows inconsistent benefits across datasets (helpful on Something-Something V2 and HMDB51 but not Kinetics-400, where overfitting is suspected), and the absolute improvements, while SOTA, are modest. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other encoder families, whether regularization can unlock the weight component's potential on data-rich benchmarks, and whether the TSE + DTW paradigm transfers to downstream tasks like action segmentation or video captioning as the authors speculate.
