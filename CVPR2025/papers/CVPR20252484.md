## Problem

SAM and its variants fail to produce high-quality segmentation masks for remote sensing video moving objects due to three compounding issues: (1) a domain gap where SAM cannot distinguish semantically similar objects (e.g., airplanes vs. boarding bridges, ships vs. waves) or handle ambiguous orientations, (2) rough and fragmented mask boundaries because the decoder lacks access to fine-grained edge features, and (3) the fixed 1024×1024 input resolution that causes small objects to vanish entirely during downsampling. No existing method effectively converts bounding-box prompts from remote sensing tracking datasets into pixel-accurate segmentation masks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a remote sensing video frame (typically large spatial dimensions) together with a bounding-box prompt indicating the target object's location, and produces a pixel-level segmentation mask. During inference, the pipeline crops N×512×512 patches centered on the prompted object, upsamples them to N×1024×1024 via bicubic interpolation, runs each patch through the model, and stitches the results back into a full-resolution mask. During training, inputs are augmented with large-scale jittering (object scales from 0.1× to 4.0×) and random rotations to expose the model to the wide scale and orientation variability inherent in satellite imagery.

## Method

ROS-SAM fine-tunes SAM's ViT-L image encoder by injecting LoRA low-rank decomposition matrices into the Query and Value projections of every self-attention layer while simultaneously unfreezing the last Transformer block to improve feature discriminability in complex scenes. The mask decoder is extended with the HQ-SAM architecture, which fuses early-stage texture features with final-stage global context to restore fine boundary details, and both the original SAM decoder and the HQ decoder are updated via alternating optimization to avoid catastrophic forgetting while refining pixel-level predictions. A redesigned inference pipeline replaces direct 1024×1024 resizing with prompt-guided center cropping of 512×512 patches followed by 2× bicubic upsampling, ensuring small objects retain sufficient resolution. Training uses BCE + Dice loss over 48 epochs at lr 1e-3, with only the LoRA parameters, last encoder block, and both decoders being learnable while the rest of SAM remains frozen.

## Results

On the SAT-MTB remote sensing video segmentation benchmark, ROS-SAM achieves an IoU of 50.54 and BIoU of 50.36, surpassing SAM (43.41 IoU), SAM2 (41.75 IoU), and HQ-SAM (47.15 IoU). On static remote sensing datasets, ROS-SAM further demonstrates strong generalization, reaching 73.22 IoU on iSAID and 87.46 IoU on NPWS VHR-10, outperforming all baselines by 9–12 points. The inference pipeline alone contributes a +6% IoU gain over the original SAM configuration, while the full ROS-SAM model adds a further +13% over the unmodified SAM baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROS-SAM outperforms all three baselines on the primary SAT-MTB benchmark. Against SAM, it gains +7.13 IoU (50.54 vs. 43.41); against SAM2, +8.79 IoU (50.54 vs. 41.75); and against the strongest baseline HQ-SAM, +3.39 IoU (50.54 vs. 47.15). The advantage is consistent on static datasets: on iSAID, ROS-SAM leads HQ-SAM by 9.26 IoU (73.22 vs. 63.96), and on NPWS VHR-10 by 9.02 IoU (87.46 vs. 78.44). The paper also reports that other fine-tuned SAM variants (SAM-Adapter, CAD, SAM2-UNet) all produced IoU scores below the original SAM, making them unsuitable for further comparison. Qualitatively, ROS-SAM uniquely distinguishes semantically similar objects (e.g., airplane vs. boarding bridge) where HQ-SAM produces refined but semantically confused masks, and where SAM/SAM2 produce fuzzy, fragmented boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROS-SAM extends the SAM family (SAM, SAM2, HQ-SAM) by combining LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning of the ViT image encoder with an HQ-SAM-style multi-stage mask decoder, specifically tailored to remote sensing video moving objects. Unlike HQ-SAM, which freezes the original mask decoder, ROS-SAM alternately updates both the original and HQ decoders, and unlike prior SAM-for-remote-sensing work (e.g., SAM-RSIS, RingMo-SAM), it redesigns the entire training and inference pipeline (LSJ augmentation, random rotation, prompt-based center-cropping with bicubic upsampling) to handle the unique scale and orientation challenges of satellite imagery. The paper positions itself within the growing body of SAM adaptation literature but distinguishes itself by targeting the underexploited task of converting existing remote sensing tracking datasets into segmentation data via interactive prompting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of ROS-SAM lies in its ability to transform existing remote sensing object tracking datasets (SatSOT, VISO, OOTB) into high-quality segmentation data with minimal additional annotation cost, addressing a critical data bottleneck in the field. However, the training dataset is small (249 videos, ~50K frames after subsampling), and the zero-shot evaluation on tracking datasets is purely qualitative, leaving open questions about robustness across sensors, resolutions, and object types beyond the four tested. The finding that the standard SAM inference pipeline (resize to 1024×1024) is fundamentally inadequate for remote sensing—yielding a 6% IoU drop before any training—highlights a broader gap in how foundation models are deployed on high-resolution, small-object imagery. Future work should validate these findings on larger, more diverse remote sensing video corpora and explore whether the alternating decoder update strategy generalizes to other domain-adaptation settings.
