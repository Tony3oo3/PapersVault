## Problem

Standard diffusion models are designed to generate images from pure noise, making them ill-suited for selectively correcting anomalous regions while preserving normal content in multi-class unsupervised anomaly detection. Applying forward-then-backward diffusion to the entire image degrades normal regions (causing false positives) or, with fewer steps, triggers the 'identity shortcut' where anomalies are simply preserved. The paper addresses the lack of an explicit mechanism for region-selective reconstruction that leverages surrounding normal context to repair only abnormal areas.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (industrial textures/objects from MVTec-AD at ~1024×1024, or VisA at similar resolution) and projects them into a latent space via a frozen pre-trained perceptual compression VAE with 8× spatial downsampling. At inference, it produces a reconstructed 'normal' image, a pixel-level anomaly map computed as the geometric mean of clipped latent-space and pixel-space reconstruction discrepancies (with thresholds γ_l and γ_p to prevent over-weighting extreme deviations), and an image-level anomaly score derived from the aggregated map. Training uses only normal images with random masking ratios sampled from U[0, 0.7] and latent patch sizes of {1, 2, 4, 8} (corresponding to 8×8 through 64×64 pixel patches).

## Method

DeCo-Diff reformulates the DDPM forward process by decomposing the noisy latent as z_t = z_0 + √(1−α̅_t)·η, where η is a 'Direction of Deviation' (DoD) that is zero for normal patches and non-zero for anomalous ones. During training, a random mask is applied so that only unmasked patches undergo forward diffusion while masked patches remain clean, forcing the attention-UNet predictor η_θ to learn when to output zero (preserve) versus a correction vector (repair). A patch-shuffling strategy (ratio r_shuffle ∈ U[0, 0.3]) replaces some patches with same-batch counterparts to expose the model to structured anomalies. At inference, a reformulated DDIM sampling (Eqs. 13–14) provides a deterministic, noise-free reverse trajectory over T=10 steps, progressively correcting deviations while leaving normal regions untouched. The final anomaly score combines latent-level and pixel-level discrepancies via a geometric mean to capture both subtle color shifts and fine-grained structural changes.

## Results

On MVTec-AD, DeCo-Diff achieves 99.3% image-level AUROC and 74.9% pixel-level AUPRC, outperforming the strongest baseline (MambaAD) by 0.7% and 14.1% respectively. On VisA, it reaches 96.4% image-level AUROC and 51.3% pixel-level AUPRC, improving over MambaAD by 2.1% and 11.7%. The pixel-level AUPRC gains of 11–14% over state-of-the-art methods represent the most substantial improvements, while image-level metrics show more modest but consistent advantages across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCo-Diff outperforms all eight baselines on every metric for both MVTec-AD and VisA. The closest competitor is MambaAD (NeurIPS'24), which DeCo-Diff surpasses by 0.7% image-level AUROC and 14.1% pixel-level AUPRC on MVTec-AD, and by 2.1% image-level AUROC and 11.7% pixel-level AUPRC on VisA. Among diffusion-based baselines, GLAD (ECCV'24) is the strongest on pixel-level AUPRC (60.8% on MVTec-AD) but still trails DeCo-Diff by 14.1 points; DiAD (AAAI'24) and MoEAD (ECCV'24) are further behind at 52.6% and 49.8% respectively. Earlier reconstruction-based methods (RD4AD, UniAD, SimpleNet, DeSTSeg) are substantially outperformed, with pixel-level AUPRC gaps exceeding 20 points on MVTec-AD. Notably, DeCo-Diff's advantage is most pronounced in pixel-level AUPRC and f1max, indicating that the selective deviation-correction mechanism yields significantly better anomaly localization than indiscriminate denoising.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the DDPM/DDIM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020) by replacing the standard denoising objective with a 'direction of deviation' prediction, shifting the model's role from generating samples from pure noise to selectively correcting anomalous regions while preserving normal ones. It operates in the latent space of a pre-trained perceptual compression VAE (Rombach et al., 2022), aligning with the latent diffusion paradigm, and introduces a random masking strategy during the forward process that has no direct analogue in prior diffusion-based anomaly detection methods (DiAD, GLAD). The work sits squarely within the reconstruction-based multi-class UAD lineage (UniAD, RD4AD, MambaAD) but departs from the indiscriminate whole-image noise application that characterizes existing diffusion-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The selective correction formulation addresses a fundamental tension in applying diffusion models to anomaly detection: the need to alter abnormal regions without degrading normal ones, which prior methods handle only implicitly. The 11–14% pixel-level AUPRC gains over strong baselines like GLAD and MambaAD suggest that the masking-plus-deviation-correction recipe is a meaningful architectural improvement rather than a marginal tuning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D medical imaging, how sensitive performance is to the masking ratio and patch-size hyperparameters in unseen domains, and whether the reliance on a frozen VAE bottleneck limits the method's ability to capture very fine-grained anomalies that the 8× downsampling may obscure.
