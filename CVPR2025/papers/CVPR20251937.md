## Problem

Personalized neural head avatars face a fundamental trade-off: lightweight models sacrifice detail and realism, while high-quality animatable avatars rely on compute-heavy CNN architectures with tens of millions of parameters and checkpoints exceeding 500 MB, making them impractical for commodity devices. This paper asks whether the photorealistic, expression-dependent appearance of CNN-based Gaussian avatars can be captured in a compact, network-free linear representation that still supports real-time animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251937/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training requires a multi-view video (16 cameras at 802×550 from NeRSemble) with FLAME-based mesh tracking to produce per-frame Gaussian primitives in canonical UV space (128², 256², or 512² maps). At inference, a single RGB image or monocular video frame is consumed by a small regressor that outputs a low-dimensional coefficient vector (20–50 values per modality), which is combined with the stored eigenbases via a single dot product to yield the full set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, scale, rotation, opacity) plus a global color texture, rendered via 3D Gaussian Splatting. Notably, no parametric 3D face model (FLAME) is required at test time.

## Method

GEM is constructed in two stages. First, an adapted Animatable Gaussians CNN (merged Style-U-Nets operating in FLAME UV space with deformation gradients) is trained on multi-view video to produce per-frame Gaussian primitive maps in canonical space. Second, PCA is applied independently to each Gaussian modality (position, scale, rotation, opacity) across all training frames, yielding an ensemble of orthonormal eigenbases; color is kept as a fixed global texture to preserve the semantic identity of each Gaussian across deformation states. The bases are then refined for ~30k iterations with photometric losses (L1 + D-SSIM + VGG) while periodically re-orthogonalizing via QR decomposition, improving training PSNR from 34.75 dB to 36.68 dB. For animation, intermediate features from a modified EMOCA network (two ResNets producing 2048-d expression and shape vectors) are projected onto a 50-component PCA manifold and passed through a 3-layer MLP with scaled-tanh output to predict the GEM coefficients, enabling real-time cross-person reenactment at ~200 FPS without any mesh-based tracking.

## Results

On NeRSemble with novel expressions and views (Table 2), GEM achieves PSNR 32.68 dB, LPIPS 0.0675, SSIM 0.9633, and L1 0.0069, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., +3.67 dB PSNR over Animatable Gaussians). In cross-person reenactment (Table 3), GEM attains the highest rendering speed at 201.70 FPS while maintaining competitive emotion-feature cosine similarity (0.9381) and FID (0.4286). The distilled model with only 10 PCA components weighs just 7 MB and still achieves 31.81 dB PSNR, compared to 487 MB for Animatable Gaussians.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM decisively outperforms all three baselines on the novel expression + novel view evaluation: it gains +3.67 dB PSNR over Animatable Gaussians (32.68 vs. 29.01), +4.37 dB over Gaussian Avatars (28.31), and +4.76 dB over INSTA (27.92), while also achieving the best LPIPS, SSIM, and L1 scores. Against Animatable Gaussians, GEM also wins on rendering speed (201.70 FPS vs. 16.51 FPS in cross-reenactment) and model size (7–138 MB vs. 487–636 MB). In cross-person reenactment, GEM's emotion-feature cosine similarity (0.9381) is on par with Animatable Gaussians (0.9396) and slightly below the CNN-based Ours Net (0.9440), but its FID (0.4286) is better than Gaussian Avatars (0.5593) and INSTA (0.5299). The key advantage is that GEM requires no FLAME model at inference (saving 90 MB) and no heavy CNN forward pass, enabling real-time animation from a single webcam image.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM transplants the 3DMM philosophy of PCA-based linear eigenbases from mesh geometry to 3D Gaussian primitive parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity), creating a mesh-free linear appearance model. It departs from the prevailing trend of increasingly heavy CNN architectures for dynamic 3DGS avatars (Animatable Gaussians, Gaussian Head Avatars) by distilling a StyleUNet-based teacher into a compact ensemble of eigenbases, requiring only a single dot product at inference. The image-based animation pipeline repurposes EMOCA's ResNet features as a generalizable regressor for the GEM coefficients, eliminating the need for FLAME-based tracking at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7–138 MB model sizes and 200+ FPS rendering make personalized photorealistic head avatars viable on commodity hardware, addressing a key deployment bottleneck that CNN-based methods (487–636 MB, 13–36 FPS) cannot. The distillation framework is general and could be applied to other 3DGS avatar methods to reduce their footprint. Open questions remain: the global PCA basis limits local expression control, side-view generalization is unstable, and a new multi-view capture is still required per subject—preventing a truly generalizable cross-subject model. Future work on localized PCA and cross-subject statistical models could unlock broader expressiveness and personalization from fewer captures.
