## Problem

Event-based NeRF methods achieve high-quality novel view synthesis from event cameras but are too slow for real-time rendering due to expensive volumetric sampling. Conversely, 3D Gaussian Splatting enables real-time rendering but requires RGB supervision, making it inapplicable when only event data is available. This paper bridges that gap by adapting 3DGS to be directly supervised by event data, enabling real-time novel view synthesis in scenarios where RGB cameras fail (fast motion, extreme dynamic range).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250724/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an asynchronous stream of events (timestamp, pixel coordinates, polarity) from a moving event camera, along with sparse camera poses sampled at a fixed tracking rate and camera intrinsics with lens distortion parameters. It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation (a set of ellipsoids with spherical-harmonic-encoded colors) that rasterizes to novel-view RGB or grayscale images in real time. Because events encode only relative log-intensity changes, the output requires a linear color transformation at inference to calibrate to absolute intensity values.

## Method

The core idea is to supervise 3DGS with log-intensity differences rather than absolute RGB values, matching the event camera's native signal. During training, random sub-trajectories (1–10% of total events) are accumulated into a target image D of summed quantized log-intensity changes; the corresponding prediction is obtained by rasterizing two views from the current Gaussian scene, remosaicing them (Hadamard-multiplying each 2×2 block with channel-specific matrices to reintroduce the Bayer pattern), taking the log, and computing the difference. The loss combines L1 and SSIM terms applied only where events were actually accumulated. For initialization, an event-to-video model generates pseudo-frames that feed into SfM to produce a point cloud prior, and cubic spline interpolation assigns dense camera poses to events between sparse tracking samples, improving reconstruction quality under fast camera motion.

## Results

On real-world EDS sequences, EventSplat achieves a mean PSNR of 18.86 dB, SSIM of 0.792, and LPIPS of 0.363, outperforming both baselines across all three metrics. On synthetic ESIM scenes, it reaches a mean PSNR of 28.14 dB, SSIM of 0.953, and LPIPS of 0.051, matching or exceeding Robust-e-NeRF on perceptual quality. Critically, EventSplat renders an order of magnitude faster than Robust-e-NeRF (which uses InstantNGP), achieving real-time frame rates comparable to E2VID+3DGS while delivering substantially higher reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Robust-e-NeRF, EventSplat wins decisively on real scenes: mean PSNR improves from 16.25 to 18.86 dB (+2.61), SSIM from 0.739 to 0.792, and LPIPS from 0.543 to 0.363, while also rendering an order of magnitude faster due to 3DGS rasterization versus InstantNGP-based NeRF. On synthetic scenes the PSNR is essentially tied (28.14 vs 28.19), but EventSplat edges ahead on SSIM (0.953 vs 0.945) and LPIPS (0.051 vs 0.057), and again renders far faster. Against E2VID+3DGS, EventSplat dominates on every metric in both settings: on real scenes it gains +3.35 dB PSNR (18.86 vs 15.51), +0.100 SSIM, and −0.012 LPIPS; on synthetic scenes it gains +8.85 dB PSNR (28.14 vs 19.29), +0.036 SSIM, and −0.067 LPIPS. The E2VID+3DGS baseline matches EventSplat's rendering speed (both use 3DGS rasterization) but suffers from the quality degradation introduced by the event-to-video conversion step, confirming that direct event supervision is superior to indirect RGB supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), which originally requires RGB image supervision, to be trained on asynchronous event camera data by reformulating the loss around accumulated log-intensity changes rather than absolute pixel values. It departs from prior event-based NeRF methods (Robust-e-NeRF, E-NeRF, EventNeRF) that rely on expensive volumetric rendering, replacing them with the fast rasterization pipeline of 3DGS. For initialization, the paper repurposes the E2VID event-to-video model (Rebecq et al., 2019) not as a rendering target but as a source of geometric priors via Structure from Motion, a use distinct from its original video-generation purpose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving real-time rendering speeds (an order of magnitude faster than event-based NeRF) while maintaining competitive visual quality, this work makes novel view synthesis from event cameras practically deployable in scenarios where RGB cameras fail due to motion blur or extreme dynamic range. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to static scenes, cannot recover absolute intensity without a post-hoc linear calibration, and exhibits border artifacts from the narrow event-camera field of view. Open questions include extending the approach to dynamic scenes, reducing dependence on the event-to-video initialization, and whether the log-intensity formulation generalizes to other sparse or asynchronous sensing modalities.
