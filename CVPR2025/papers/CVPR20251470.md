## Problem

Existing robot pose estimation methods either assume known joint angles or fail to fully exploit the rich physical structure information visible in robot images, resulting in degraded performance under occlusions and truncations. The paper addresses how to teach a vision encoder to internalize the robot's physical model so it can robustly infer missing joint and pose information from partial visual context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251470/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB image, which is first cropped to the robot's region of interest via GroundingDINO and resized to 224×224 pixels, along with known camera intrinsics and robot forward kinematics. It outputs the robot-to-camera SE(3) transformation, n joint angles in radians, and 224×224×k keypoint heatmaps (k = n+1, covering all joints plus the base). During inference, a confidence threshold filters out keypoints whose peak heatmap values fall below ε, and the surviving 2D-3D correspondences (minimum four) are fed to EPnP for the final pose solve.

## Method

RoboPEPP pre-trains a ViT encoder–predictor pair using a JEPA-style embedding-predictive objective, but with a critical modification: masks are placed specifically over four randomly selected robot joint regions (covering 15–20% of the image) rather than random patches, forcing the encoder to infer joint embeddings from surrounding structural context and thereby encoding the robot's physical model. After pre-training, the encoder and predictor are fine-tuned end-to-end with two downstream heads: a Joint Net that iteratively refines angle predictions through four shared MLP steps, and a Keypoint Net that upsamples patch embeddings through four transpose-convolutional layers to produce per-keypoint heatmaps. Random masking (up to 20%) is applied during fine-tuning to further boost occlusion robustness, and a curriculum-based RoI expansion (0→120 px) reduces dependence on precise bounding boxes. At evaluation, confidence-based keypoint filtering removes unreliable detections before EPnP pose estimation, and an optional 10-epoch sim-to-real self-supervised fine-tuning stage using a differentiable PnP loss bridges the domain gap.

## Results

On the DREAM dataset, RoboPEPP achieves the highest AUC on the ADD (average distance) metric across most test sequences among methods that do not assume known joint angles or bounding boxes: 84.1 on Panda Photo, 83.0 on Panda DR, and 76.2 on Kuka DR. For joint angle prediction, RoboPEPP attains a mean absolute error of 3.8° on average across Panda and Kuka test sets, compared to 4.5° for HPE (with known bounding boxes) and 6.0° for RoboPose. Under 40% occlusion, RoboPEPP retains an AUC of 35.1 versus 28.2 for HPE and 14.5 for RoboPose, and completes inference in 23 ms—the fastest among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HPE [4] (ECCV 2024), RoboPEPP outperforms on 8 of 9 sequences even when HPE is given ground-truth bounding boxes (e.g., 84.1 vs 82.0 on Panda Photo, 80.5 vs 75.2 on Panda RS). When HPE is evaluated without known bounding boxes (HPE*), its performance collapses dramatically (e.g., 40.7 on Panda Photo, 9.8 on Baxter DR), while RoboPEPP remains stable due to its RoI training curriculum. Against RoboPose [15] (CVPR 2021), RoboPEPP wins on all Panda real-world sequences (e.g., 75.3 vs 70.4 on AK, 80.5 vs 74.3 on RS) and Kuka Photo (76.1 vs 73.2), though RoboPose retains an edge on Kuka DR (80.2 vs 76.2). Against DREAM [17] (ICRA 2020), which assumes known joint angles, RoboPEPP still surpasses all three DREAM variants on real-world Panda sequences (e.g., 75.3 vs 68.9/52.4/60.5 on AK) and achieves competitive scores on synthetic sets, demonstrating that the unknown-joint formulation does not sacrifice accuracy. On joint angle prediction, RoboPEPP's 3.8° average MAE beats HPE (4.5°) by ~15% and RoboPose (6.0°) by ~37% across all four test sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboPEPP adapts the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) of Assran et al. [3] to the robotics domain by replacing random masking with joint-specific masking, so the encoder learns to infer occluded joint embeddings from surrounding context. For the downstream joint angle prediction, it directly adopts the iterative MLP refinement scheme from HPE [4], and for pose estimation it follows the standard 2D-3D correspondence + EPnP pipeline established by DREAM [17] and RoboPose [15]. The key conceptual departure from all prior robot pose estimation methods is the insertion of a self-supervised pre-training stage that explicitly encodes the robot's physical structure into the encoder before any supervised fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that domain-specific self-supervised pre-training (joint masking) yields meaningful gains in occlusion robustness and overall accuracy over both no pre-training and random-masking baselines, suggesting that embedding physical priors into vision encoders is a practical strategy for structured-object tasks. However, evaluation is limited to three robot types on the DREAM dataset, and the pipeline's dependence on an external detector (GroundingDINO) for region-of-interest cropping introduces a practical dependency that may not transfer cleanly to all deployment settings. Open questions include whether the joint-masking pre-training generalizes to more complex or non-rigid articulated systems, and whether the approach can be extended to multi-robot or video-based settings without the current single-image assumption.
