## Problem

Existing face forgery video detection methods rely on isolated spatial features or coarse spatiotemporal fusion (e.g., 3D CNNs, frame concatenation), which fail to capture the subtle temporal forgery cues—flickering, discontinuities, and inter-frame inconsistencies—that are obscured by natural facial motion and expression changes. This results in significant inter-frame prediction fluctuations and poor generalization to unseen manipulation methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251370/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes face video clips (12 clips × 4 consecutive frames, each face cropped and resized to 224×224 via RetinaFace) along with 5-point facial landmarks extracted by Dlib as positional prompts. It produces frame-wise real/fake discrimination probabilities per frame, from which video-wise predictions are derived by averaging within each video. During training, CCM uses variable-length consecutive frames (1–8) with random sampling, while FGM operates on fixed 4-frame clips.

## Method

TFCU progressively unravels temporal forgery cues at three levels via three modules. The Consecutive Correlate Module (CCM) uses a real-face pre-trained ViT as image encoder and a Transformer with cross-attention, a unit frame-wise lower triangular mask, and random masking to capture momentary anomaly cues from consecutive frames. The Future Guide Module (FGM) iteratively aggregates anomaly cues from historical clips into a forgery cue bank and propagates them to future frames via cross-attention, using inter-clip facial landmark shifts as prompts to stabilize propagation against natural motion. The Historical Review Module (HRM) is an inference-time post-processing step that accumulates distortion cues from future clips onto earlier frames using an inverse linear decay momentum operation. Training is staged: ViT+CCM are first trained with cross-entropy loss, then frozen while FGM is trained; HRM is applied only at inference.

## Results

On cross-dataset evaluation (trained on FaceForensics++ c23), TFCU achieves frame-wise AUC of 91.38% on Celeb-DF, 85.43% on DFDC, and 90.21% on FFIW, outperforming the best prior methods by 4.68%, 2.98%, and 3.15% respectively. A key advantage is the small video-to-frame AUC gap (1.8% on Celeb-DF vs. >5% for all baselines), indicating stable per-frame predictions. On cross-manipulation evaluation (7 methods from DF40), TFCU achieves ≥90% frame-wise AUC on 5 of 7 manipulation types, with a 4.50% gain over the best method on SadTalker and 1.62% on FOMM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFCU outperforms all 12 baselines on frame-wise AUC across all three cross-datasets. Against the strongest spatial-only baselines, TFCU beats LSDA (86.70% → 91.38% on Celeb-DF frame, +4.68%) and FFDBackbone (82.45% → 85.43% on DFDC frame, +2.98%; 87.06% → 90.21% on FFIW frame, +3.15%). Among video-based methods, TFCU surpasses FTCN by 10.74% on Celeb-DF frame and 18.85% on DFDC frame, and outperforms AltFreezing by 18.80% on Celeb-DF frame. On cross-manipulation (Table 2), TFCU leads on 5 of 7 manipulation types in frame-wise AUC, with the largest gains on SadTalker (+4.50% over FFDBackbone) and FOMM (+1.62% over SBI). The only notable exception is video-wise AUC on face-swapping methods (SimSwap, InSwapper), where SBI and AltFreezing slightly edge out TFCU due to their data augmentation strategies specifically simulating swap artifacts, though TFCU still leads in frame-wise AUC on those datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFCU departs from prior video-based FFVD methods (FTCN, AltFreezing, TALL, NACO) that rely on coarse spatiotemporal fusion via 3D CNN kernel adjustments or frame concatenation, arguing these fail to capture subtle temporal forgery cues obscured by natural facial motion. It also builds on frame-based FFVD work (SBI, FFDBackbone) by adopting a real-face pre-trained ViT as the spatial backbone, but adds a dedicated temporal unraveling pipeline on top. Conceptually, the three-level decomposition (momentary anomaly → gradual inconsistency → cumulative distortion) draws on human change-detection psychology (Rensink) and borrows streaming-memory ideas from SAM 2, repurposing them for forgery-specific cue propagation rather than general video understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: temporal forgery cues (flickering, inter-frame discontinuities) have been underexploited relative to spatial features, and the progressive unraveling framework offers a principled way to leverage them without overfitting to specific manipulation types. The notably smaller video-to-frame AUC gap (1.8% vs. >5% for prior methods) suggests more stable per-frame discrimination, which is practically important for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the three-level decomposition is truly necessary versus a simpler bidirectional temporal model, how the approach scales to longer videos or higher resolutions, and whether the momentum-accumulation post-processing generalizes beyond the specific decay functions tested.
