## Problem

Existing diffusion feature extraction methods require adding noise to input images before passing them through the model, which destroys perceptual information and forces task-specific timestep tuning. The paper demonstrates that the added noise is directly encoded in the extracted features (even at low timesteps like t=261), and that ensembling over multiple noise samples cannot remedy this fundamental information loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250814/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single clean RGB image at the diffusion model's native resolution (512×512 for SD 1.5, 768×768 for SD 2.1) with no noise or timestep conditioning. It produces K=11 feature maps extracted from the U-Net's middle block and decoder blocks (excluding the two final blocks), yielding spatial resolutions ranging from 48×48 upward. At inference, a single forward pass suffices—no noise sampling, no DDIM inversion, and no multi-timestep ensembling—making it 8× faster than DIFT and 50× faster than diffusion hyperfeatures.

## Method

CleanDIFT initializes a trainable copy of a pre-trained diffusion U-Net and fine-tunes it on clean images with no timestep input, while a frozen copy of the original model processes the corresponding noisy image at a sampled timestep. Pointwise timestep-conditioned projection heads (three stacked zero-initialized FFNs per feature map, 45M extra parameters for SD 2.1) map the clean model's features onto the frozen model's timestep-dependent features, and the loss is the negative cosine similarity across all K feature maps. During training, I=3 stratified timesteps are sampled per image to cover the full noise spectrum, forcing the clean model to consolidate information from all T feature extractors into one representation. After only 400 steps (~30 min on one A100) on a COYO-700M subset, the projection heads are discarded at inference and the clean model's internal activations serve directly as noise-free, timestep-independent features.

## Results

On SPair-71k zero-shot semantic correspondence, CleanDIFT features improve DIFT by +1.79 PCK_img (66.53→68.32) and +1.86 PCK_bbox (59.57→61.43), and when combined with Telling Left from Right achieve a new state-of-the-art of 78.40 PCK_img / 69.99 PCK_bbox. On NYUv2 monocular depth estimation, CleanDIFT features reduce RMSE from 0.469 to 0.444 (−0.025) over standard diffusion features. The method requires only a single forward pass at inference, yielding an 8× speedup over DIFT's 8-sample noise ensembling and a 50× speedup over Luo et al.'s DDIM-inversion-based approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CleanDIFT features consistently outperform standard noisy diffusion features across all three correspondence methods: DIFT gains +1.79 PCK_img, A Tale of Two Features gains +1.04, and Telling Left from Right gains +1.33, with the combined Telling Left from Right + CleanDIFT setting a new SOTA for unsupervised zero-shot semantic correspondence (78.40 PCK_img). On depth estimation (NYUv2), CleanDIFT features (RMSE 0.444) beat DIFT-like features (0.469) and all non-diffusion self-supervised baselines except DINOv2 (0.344), which remains the strongest overall. In the supervised correspondence setting, CleanDIFT slightly trails Luo et al. (72.48 vs. 72.75 PCK_img) but does so at 50× lower inference cost by eliminating DDIM inversion. Against DINOv2+NN (55.6 PCK_bbox), the diffusion-based methods with CleanDIFT features lead by a wide margin (61.43–69.99 PCK_bbox), confirming that noise-free diffusion features complement and surpass purely discriminative self-supervised features for dense spatial tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DIFT paradigm (Tang et al., NeurIPS 2023) of extracting semantic features from Stable Diffusion's U-Net decoder, but departs from it by eliminating the noise-adding step that DIFT and subsequent methods (A Tale of Two Features, Telling Left from Right) all require. Conceptually, it reframes the diffusion model as a family of T timestep-conditioned feature extractors and consolidates them into a single clean-image feature extractor via a lightweight alignment objective inspired by knowledge distillation. The approach also positions itself as complementary to DINOv2-style discriminative features, showing that the gains transfer when diffusion features are fused with DINOv2 features in existing correspondence pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CleanDIFT removes two practical pain points from diffusion feature extraction: the information loss from adding noise to input images and the need to tune a timestep per downstream task, while delivering an 8× speedup over DIFT's noise ensembling and a 50× speedup over DDIM-inversion-based methods. The 30-minute single-GPU fine-tuning makes the technique accessible and likely to be adopted as a drop-in replacement in existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to DiT-based backbones and video diffusion models, whether the stratified timestep sampling strategy is optimal, and whether the gains persist when the diffusion backbone is itself fine-tuned for a specific domain.
