## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods bake illumination into radiance, producing non-relightable assets, while optimization-based inverse rendering methods that recover PBR materials and illumination are slow (hours per scene) and require handcrafted priors. Additionally, high-quality PBR training data is scarce in existing synthetic datasets, and the synthetic-to-real domain gap prevents direct transfer to real-world digital twinning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252668/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes exactly four posed RGB images along with their foreground masks and camera poses (Plücker embeddings) as input, processed by a DINOv2 ViT image tokenizer. It outputs a 3D voxel grid encoding opacity, albedo, metalness, roughness, and surface normals, plus a six-face cubemap representing the scene's environment illumination. The tricolumn representation encodes the voxel grid using only 3R² transformer tokens (quadratic in grid resolution) rather than R³, enabling large-resolution grids within feasible memory. Inference completes in approximately 0.006 hours (a few seconds) per object.

## Method

Twinner introduces a tricolumn representation where each feature vector in a triplane is itself a column of R voxel cells, reducing token count from cubic to quadratic while preserving explicit per-voxel encoding; three such planes are summed and passed through an MLP to decode the full volumetric feature. A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) module predicts the tricolumn 3D representation, while a second DiT predicts the environment map as a cubemap. Training uses a two-stage strategy: first on a procedurally generated synthetic dataset (10k objects from MatSynth materials lit by ~1k PolyHaven environment maps) with direct PBR and illumination supervision, then fine-tuned on CO3Dv2 real captures using a differentiable Disney microfacet PBR shader that renders the predicted geometry, materials, and illumination to produce photometric losses without requiring ground-truth materials or lighting. The background visible in real input images provides additional direct supervision for the predicted environment map.

## Results

On StanfordORB, Twinner achieves 24.55 dB PSNR-H and 0.038 LPIPS for novel-scene relighting, and 21.70 dB PSNR-H and 0.037 LPIPS for novel-view synthesis, outperforming the main feed-forward baseline MetalLRM (21.50 dB / 0.052 LPIPS) by 27% in LPIPS. For illumination prediction, Twinner achieves 8.09° angular error, a 28% improvement over the best single-scene optimizer (NMF at 11.24°). Inference takes only 0.006 hours (~4 seconds) per object, compared to 1–20+ hours for optimization-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Twinner substantially outperforms MetalLRM [45], the primary feed-forward PBR reconstructor, achieving 27% lower LPIPS on both relighting (0.038 vs 0.052) and novel-view synthesis (0.037 vs 0.051), while also improving shape error from 1.26 to 0.37. Against single-scene optimization methods, Twinner matches or exceeds NVDiffRec [34], NeRD [8], NeRFactor [59], and InvRender [69] on relighting quality, though it trails the strongest optimizer NVDiffRecMC [17] (24.43 vs 24.55 PSNR-H is comparable, but 0.036 vs 0.038 LPIPS) and Neural-PBIR [48] (26.01 PSNR-H, 0.023 LPIPS) on absolute quality. The critical advantage is speed: Twinner runs in ~4 seconds versus 1–20+ hours for optimization-based methods. For illumination prediction specifically (Table 2), Twinner achieves 8.09° angular error, beating all single-scene optimizers (best: NMF at 11.24°) and single-view predictors (best: DiffusionLight at 11.35°), while running in 0.006 hr versus 0.3–2 hr for baselines. The two ablations confirm that both the procedural Zeroverse training data and the real-data shading loss contribute meaningfully, with removing either degrading relighting LPIPS to 0.048 and 0.042 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

Twinner extends the Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) family—specifically LightplaneLRM—by adding predictors for PBR material properties (albedo, metalness, roughness) and scene illumination, coupled with a differentiable physically-based renderer for photometric supervision. Architecturally, it replaces the triplane representation used in LRM and MetalLRM with a novel tricolumn representation that retains explicit per-voxel encoding while keeping token count quadratic rather than cubic. The training strategy departs from MetalLRM's reliance on high-quality artist-created PBR assets by combining a procedurally generated synthetic dataset (Zeroverse with environment maps) with real CO3Dv2 captures supervised through a shading loss, thereby bridging the synthetic-to-real gap without requiring ground-truth materials or illumination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing relightable PBR assets in seconds rather than the hours required by per-scene optimization (NeRFactor, NVDiffRec, NeRD), Twinner makes inverse rendering practical for interactive applications like AR/VR and game asset creation. The tricolumn representation and the synthetic-plus-real training recipe are transferable components likely to influence subsequent LRM work. Limitations include the assumption of spatially constant illumination (no self-shadowing), evaluation on a small object-centric benchmark (14 objects), and reliance on only four input views; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to complex scenes with inter-object shadows, dynamic lighting, or larger object categories.
