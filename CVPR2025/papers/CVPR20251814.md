## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) generation and editing methods are specialized and task-focused, lacking a unified framework. Generation methods are largely restricted to object-level synthetic datasets with controlled viewpoints, while real-world scenes present challenges of varying scales and complex camera trajectories. Editing methods require complex multi-stage pipelines to lift 2D diffusion priors into 3D-consistent modifications, making them brittle and difficult to generalize across tasks.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces K=8 multi-view latents (image, depth, and Plücker ray coordinates) in the SD3 latent space, which are decoded into pixel-aligned 3DGS (per-pixel position, opacity, covariance, and color). For editing, it accepts an existing multi-view latent representation plus a target text prompt and a binary mask. For inpainting-based tasks (pose estimation, novel view synthesis), known views or camera parameters serve as constraints while unknown components are predicted. The Plücker ray representation encodes camera poses as 6-channel spatial maps matching the latent resolution, enabling joint channel-wise concatenation with image and depth latents.

## Method

SplatFlow comprises a multi-view Rectified Flow model fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion 3 and a feed-forward Gaussian Splatting Decoder (GSDecoder). The RF model jointly samples from the distribution of multi-view image latents, depth latents (from DepthAnythingV2), and Plücker ray coordinates conditioned on text, trained with conditional flow matching loss and cross-view attention. The GSDecoder, initialized from the SD3 decoder with increased channel dimensions, decodes pixel-aligned 3DGS from the latent inputs, enhanced by depth latent integration and a delayed adversarial (vision-aided) loss for perceptual quality. A key design choice is freezing and sharing the SD3 encoder, enabling the RF model to operate in the same latent space as SD3 for cross-model compatibility. Training-free editing is achieved by modifying SDEdit inversion for rectified flows, while RePaint-style inpainting reformulates pose estimation and novel view synthesis as partial-data completion problems over the jointly modeled latent space.

## Results

On text-to-3DGS generation, SplatFlow achieves FID 34.85 and CLIP Score 31.43 on MVImgNet, outperforming Director3D (FID 39.55, CLIP 30.48) despite training on a substantially smaller dataset. On DL3DV, SplatFlow reaches FID 79.91 vs. Director3D's 88.44 with comparable CLIP scores (30.06 vs. 30.04). For 3D object editing, SplatFlow+SDS++ attains CLIP Score 31.30 and CLIP D-sim 0.224, roughly doubling DGE's D-sim (0.102). In camera pose estimation, SplatFlow (without depth) achieves 28.8% rotation accuracy @5° and 99.7% camera center accuracy @0.2, surpassing Ray Diffusion (17.5% and 87.6%) and RelPose++ (19.4% and 55.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Director3D (the closest prior work for text-to-3DGS scene generation), SplatFlow wins on FID by 4.7 points on MVImgNet (34.85 vs. 39.55) and 8.5 points on DL3DV (79.91 vs. 88.44), while also improving CLIP Score on MVImgNet (31.43 vs. 30.48); with SDS++ refinement, SplatFlow further extends the CLIP advantage to 32.30 vs. 31.00. In 3D object editing, SplatFlow+SDS++ substantially outperforms DGE on both CLIP Score (31.30 vs. 27.43) and CLIP D-sim (0.224 vs. 0.102), and qualitatively matches MVInpainter, a specialized multi-view inpainting method. For camera pose estimation, SplatFlow (without depth) dominates all baselines: it achieves 28.8% rotation accuracy @5° versus Ray Diffusion's 17.5% and RelPose++'s 19.4%, and 99.7% camera center accuracy @0.2 versus 87.6% and 55.0% respectively. Qualitative comparisons against LucidDreamer and DreamScene show SplatFlow produces sharper, more coherent scenes with better handling of diverse camera trajectories, whereas SDS-based methods yield blurry or texture-poor outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatFlow builds directly on Stable Diffusion 3's rectified flow architecture, fine-tuning it with adjusted channel dimensions and cross-view attention to jointly model multi-view images, depths, and Plücker ray coordinates in a shared latent space. The GSDecoder draws on feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction methods (PixelSplat, MVSplat) but adds depth latent integration and delayed adversarial loss to compensate for information lost in the frozen SD3 encoder. The most direct prior work is Director3D, which also generates camera poses and multi-view images from text; SplatFlow departs by jointly learning the pose-image distribution rather than generating poses first, and by extending the framework to training-free editing via adapted SDEdit inversion and RePaint inpainting—eliminating the complex multi-stage pipelines (attention propagation, autoregressive view updates, texture refinement) that prior 3DGS editing methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified generation-and-editing framework is practically significant: a single trained model handles text-to-3DGS synthesis, object replacement, camera pose estimation, and novel view synthesis without task-specific fine-tuning or auxiliary modules, lowering the barrier to 3D content manipulation. However, several open questions remain: novel view synthesis quality (PSNR ~14–18) is moderate and may limit use in high-fidelity applications; the counterintuitive finding that depth latents *hurt* camera pose accuracy suggests the joint modeling trade-offs are not yet well understood; and scalability beyond 8 views or to very large scenes is untested. The reliance on a frozen SD3 encoder for compatibility also constrains the model's capacity to learn scene-specific geometric priors.
