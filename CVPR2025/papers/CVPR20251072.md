## Problem

Point cloud completion from single-view partial scans faces a vast solution space due to self-occlusion, causing existing geometry-only methods to fail on local thin structures and global symmetric parts. While image-guided approaches improve completeness, they require paired image-point cloud data that is impractical to obtain. This paper exploits the view-consistent multi-view diffusion priors within large generative models to produce novel views of the desired shape for free, eliminating the need for paired data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251072/cropped_page0_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view partial point cloud of 2,048 points (generated by back-projection from a fixed-viewpoint depth image along the positive z-axis) and produces a dense, uniform complete point cloud of 16,384 points (8× upsampling). The fixed viewpoint constraint is imposed because the underlying diffusion models (Wonder3D, SVD) require a specific input camera configuration. Intermediate multi-view depth images (6 views) are generated on-the-fly and consumed by the fusion module rather than being part of the user-facing I/O.

## Method

PCDreamer comprises three modules: (1) a multi-view image generation pipeline that chains ControlNet (depth→RGB), a large diffusion model (Wonder3D or Stable Video Diffusion) to produce 6 posed views, and DepthAnything to extract per-view depth maps; (2) a multi-modality shape fusion module where a patch-based Transformer encoder (DGCNN patches + sinusoidal positional encoding) processes the partial point cloud and a ResNet-based encoder with MLP-encoded camera poses processes the multi-view depth images, then cross-attention (point cloud feature as query, image feature as key/value) fuses them into a coarse complete shape; (3) a confidence-guided shape consolidator that scores each coarse point via self-attention against the multi-view feature, retains the top 75% high-confidence points, and upsamples via FPS + MLP offsets to yield the final dense output. The network is trained end-to-end with a hyperbolic Chamfer distance loss applied to both the coarse and final point clouds.

## Results

On the PCN dataset, PCDreamer (Wonder3D) achieves the best average Chamfer Distance of 6.49×10⁻³, DCD of 0.518, and F1-Score of 0.859, outperforming the strongest baseline CRA-PCN (6.56, 0.537, 0.846) across all three metrics. On ShapeNet-55, PCDreamer (Wonder3D) attains CD-Avg of 0.895×10⁻³ and DCD of 0.569, beating SVDFormer (0.938, 0.621) on both distance metrics, though SVDFormer retains a slight F1 advantage (0.447 vs. 0.418). On the ShapeNet-34 generalization test, PCDreamer achieves the lowest CD on both seen (0.758) and unseen (1.094) categories, confirming strong cross-category transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCDreamer outperforms all 10 point-cloud-completion baselines on the PCN dataset in average CD, DCD, and F1, with the closest competitor being CRA-PCN (CD 6.56 vs. 6.49; F1 0.846 vs. 0.859). On ShapeNet-55, PCDreamer (Wonder3D) achieves the best CD (0.895) and DCD (0.569), surpassing SVDFormer (0.938, 0.621) and CRA-PCN (1.300, 0.617), though SVDFormer retains a marginal F1 edge (0.447 vs. 0.418). On the ShapeNet-34 generalization benchmark, PCDreamer leads on CD for both seen (0.758 vs. 0.839 for SVDFormer) and unseen (1.094 vs. 1.129) categories. The two 3D-generation baselines (One-2-3-45++ and Craftsman) are compared qualitatively: both produce globally regular shapes but fail to recover fine local structures (e.g., chair backrest, airplane engines), whereas PCDreamer faithfully completes these details. The only per-category loss on PCN is Lamp, where CRA-PCN scores 5.46 vs. PCDreamer's 5.64, attributed to multi-view inconsistency on slender folding structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCDreamer extends the transformer-based point cloud completion line (CRA-PCN, SVDFormer, AnchorFormer) by introducing multi-view diffusion priors as a free source of shape guidance, eliminating the need for paired image-point cloud data that prior image-guided methods (ViPC, Cross-PCC) require. The multi-view generation pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf components—ControlNet for depth-to-RGB translation, Wonder3D or Stable Video Diffusion for multi-view synthesis, and DepthAnything for depth estimation—into a unified conditioning signal. The confidence-based filtering module is a novel architectural addition specifically designed to mitigate the inherent cross-view inconsistencies of diffusion-generated images, a problem not addressed by prior image-guided completion methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that large-scale multi-view diffusion priors can be harnessed 'for free' to improve point cloud completion without requiring expensive paired supervision, opening a path toward leveraging ever-improving generative models for 3D geometry tasks. However, the approach is limited by the consistency of diffusion-generated views: slender or highly curved structures (e.g., earphone stems) produce unreliable multi-view cues, leading to failure. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to real-world LiDAR scans with sensor noise and non-ideal viewpoints, and whether the modest gains over strong transformer baselines (≈0.05–0.1 CD) will widen as diffusion models improve in cross-view consistency.
