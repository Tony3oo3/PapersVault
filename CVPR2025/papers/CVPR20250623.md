## Problem

Existing open-set supervised anomaly detection methods rely on generating pseudo anomalies to compensate for scarce anomaly samples, but this approach overlooks the critical priors of diverse normal samples and inherits in-distribution biases, resulting in imprecise discriminative boundaries. The paper asks: instead of generating uncertain pseudo anomalies, how can one accurately characterize compact distribution boundaries around diverse normal samples to achieve robust generalization to unknown out-of-distribution anomalies?

## Input / Output

The method takes 448×448×3 RGB images as input and produces both image-level and pixel-level anomaly scores. Training uses weakly-supervised image-level labels with only M=1 or M=10 anomaly examples per class, augmented via CutMix for known anomalies. At inference, the model identifies the most similar Gaussian prototype via the Schrödinger bridge, then combines scores from three MIL-based heads (anomaly, normal, and residual) to yield the final anomaly score.

## Method

DPDL jointly learns C=32 Gaussian distribution prototypes (initialized via vector quantization) and a Schrödinger bridge that diffuses normal sample features into the prototype space while repelling anomaly features away. The bridge is formulated with a Wiener prior and solved using the LightSB Gaussian-mixture parameterization of Schrödinger potentials, yielding a tractable conditional transport plan with per-prototype adjusted means and covariances. A Dispersion Feature Learning module maps unit-norm intermediate features to a hyperspherical space and optimizes a vMF-based dispersion loss to maximize angular separation between all samples, improving out-of-distribution generalization. Three MIL-based scoring heads (top-K anomaly, mean-pooled normal, and residual-to-prototype) are trained jointly with the SB transform and dispersion loss under a single objective, using a ResNet-18 backbone for feature extraction.

## Results

DPDL achieves state-of-the-art AUC on 7 out of 9 datasets under the general setting with 10 training anomaly examples, with the largest gains on AITEX (0.975 vs. 0.925 for AHL), ELPV (0.937 vs. 0.850), and Mastcam (0.934 vs. 0.855). In the more challenging single-anomaly setting, DPDL wins on all 9 datasets, improving over the closest baseline AHL by up to 8.3% AUC on AITEX, ELPV, and Mastcam. Under the hard setting (single anomaly class), DPDL maintains the highest AUC across all six evaluated datasets in both the 10-anomaly and 1-anomaly configurations, with improvements of 0.6–7.9% and 0.5–8.9% over AHL, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPDL is compared against six baselines: DevNet, FLOS, SAOE, MLEP, DRA, and AHL. The closest competitor is AHL (CVPR 2024), which simulates heterogeneous anomaly distributions; DPDL outperforms AHL on 7 of 9 datasets in the 10-anomaly general setting (e.g., +5.0% on AITEX, +8.7% on ELPV, +7.9% on Mastcam) but trails on BrainMRI (0.969 vs. 0.977) and HeadCT (0.981 vs. 0.999). In the 1-anomaly setting, DPDL wins on all 9 datasets, with the largest margin of +10.4% AUC on ELPV (0.897 vs. 0.828). DRA (CVPR 2022) is consistently outperformed by 3–15% AUC across datasets, with the gap widening in the single-anomaly regime. The older baselines—DevNet, FLOS, SAOE, and MLEP—are substantially outperformed, with FLOS and MLEP often falling below 0.60 AUC on challenging datasets like AITEX and ELPV in the 1-anomaly setting, while DPDL maintains 0.84–0.90 AUC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Schrödinger bridge framework from generative modeling (LightSB, Korotin et al. 2023; Gushchin et al. ICML 2024) for a fundamentally different purpose: instead of generating samples, it uses the SB to map normal sample features onto learnable Gaussian prototypes, creating a compact distribution boundary. It departs from the dominant OSAD paradigm (DRA, AHL) that generates pseudo-anomalies to compensate for scarce anomaly data, arguing instead that the critical bottleneck is characterizing the normal distribution boundary. The anomaly scoring module directly adapts the MIL-based top-K mechanism from DevNet (Pang et al. 2021), while the hyperspherical dispersion loss draws on von Mises-Fisher directional statistics as a regularization for inter-sample separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing from 'generate uncertain pseudo-anomalies' to 'enclose normals in a tight prototype space' addresses a genuine limitation of prior OSAD methods where simulated anomalies inherit in-distribution biases and fail on out-of-distribution anomalies. The consistent 5–9% AUC gains across nine diverse datasets (industrial and medical) under both general and hard settings, with thorough ablations and sensitivity analyses, make this a credible and practically useful contribution for few-shot industrial inspection. Open questions include whether the SB-based prototype alignment scales to video or 3D anomaly detection, whether the ResNet-18 backbone is a bottleneck for fine-grained localization, and whether the approach extends to settings with zero anomaly examples (purely unsupervised).
