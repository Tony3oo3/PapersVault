## Problem

Stable Diffusion-based models produce high-fidelity images but fail to generate genuinely creative outputs when prompted with 'creative'; existing novelty-enhancing methods (ProCreate, ConceptLab, Inspiration Tree) require additional optimization steps, reference images, or training, making them computationally expensive and impractical for scalable use.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., 'a creative [obj]') and produces a single creative RGB image through the existing Stable Diffusion inference pipeline, with no additional inputs, reference images, or training required. It operates as a drop-in modification to the U-Net denoising process, compatible with SDXL, SDXL-Turbo, and SDXL-Lightning (1-step and 4-step), and can be combined with ControlNet or classifier-free guidance without conflict.

## Method

C3 (Creative Concept Catalyst) is a training-free, inference-time technique that selectively amplifies low-frequency components of intermediate feature maps in the shallow U-Net blocks (three down blocks and one middle block). At each target block, the feature map is transformed via FFT, a binarized low-frequency mask isolates the low-frequency band, and only those components are scaled by a block-specific amplification factor λ before inverse FFT returns the modified features to the spatial domain. The key insight is that shallow blocks govern color and structure (creativity-relevant) while deeper up blocks control noise and blur, and that amplifying high-frequency components introduces mosaic artifacts. Amplification factors are automatically selected per block by maximizing λ (proxy for novelty) subject to a usability constraint defined as aesthetic score plus CLIP score, with a bumper parameter ε controlling the novelty–quality trade-off. When multiple blocks are amplified simultaneously, the factors are scaled so their sum is preserved (1.0 for Turbo, 0.6 for others) to maintain image quality.

## Results

Across five object categories (chair, building, garment, car, teddy bear) with 100 images each, C3 consistently improves novelty and diversity over original Stable Diffusion-based models while maintaining usability. For example, on SDXL, FID* (higher = more novel) increases from 128.20 to 157.93, Precision* (lower = more novel) drops from 0.79 to 0.66, and Vendi score rises from 6.52 to 7.32, while CLIP score stays at 0.27 and BLIP drops only from 0.95 to 0.86. In the user study, novelty scores jump substantially (e.g., Lightning 1-step: 2.65 → 4.12 on a 5-point scale) with only modest usability losses (4.62 → 4.19). C3 achieves these gains with zero additional training or optimization steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ConceptLab [19], C3 wins decisively on usability: BLIP scores are 0.86–0.95 for C3 versus 0.32 for ConceptLab, and user-study usability is 4.14–4.19 versus 2.97 (5-point scale). C3 also surpasses ConceptLab on user-study novelty (4.12 vs. 3.65 for Lightning 1-step; 3.79 vs. 3.65 for Turbo). ConceptLab achieves a higher FID* (251.27 vs. 157–164) and Vendi score (8.41 vs. 7.32), but the authors note its FID exceeds the reference-object threshold, meaning it often generates unrecognizable or different objects rather than creative variants of the target. Against FreeU [23], C3 produces cleaner and more creative outputs; FreeU introduces persistent noise when its boost parameter b > 1 regardless of the scale parameter s, whereas C3's frequency-selective amplification avoids this artifact. Overall, C3 trades a small usability decrease (≈0.3–0.4 on the 5-point scale) for a large novelty gain (≈0.7–1.5), a more favorable trade-off than ConceptLab's substantial usability collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the feature-manipulation paradigm established in GAN dissection and cross-attention editing (Prompt-to-Prompt, FreeU) for a new objective: enhancing creativity in diffusion models without training. It departs from prior creative-generation methods (ProCreate, Inspiration Tree, ConceptLab) that require reference images, energy-based optimization, or VLM-guided training, by instead directly amplifying low-frequency features in shallow U-Net blocks during denoising. The Fourier-domain selective amplification draws on the low/high-frequency decomposition used in FreeU for fidelity, but redirects it toward novelty rather than sharpness, and the automatic per-block amplification factor selection via a usability constraint is a new practical mechanism not present in prior feature-editing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C3 offers the first training-free, zero-extra-compute way to push Stable Diffusion models toward more creative outputs, making it immediately deployable for designers and artists who find the default 'creative' prompt ineffective. However, the method's effectiveness is bounded by the pre-trained model's own concept of creativity for a given object, and its transfer to transformer-based architectures (e.g., HunYuan-DiT) remains preliminary. Open questions include whether the shallow-block/low-frequency recipe generalizes beyond U-Net topologies, how it interacts with more complex compositional prompts, and whether the automatic amplification search can be made fully closed-form rather than requiring a small grid search per generation.
