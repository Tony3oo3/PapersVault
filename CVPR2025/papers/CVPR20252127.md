## Problem

Current multimodal LLMs struggle with medium-to-long video understanding due to frame and context length limitations, forcing reliance on frame sampling that risks missing critical temporal information. Existing approaches also lack task-specific relevance, processing all frames uniformly without prioritizing information pertinent to the given prompt, leading to inefficient and incomplete video comprehension.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252127/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video as a sequence of T frames processed sequentially (autoregressively, one frame at a time) along with a text prompt, avoiding the need to feed all frames simultaneously into the LLM. Each frame is encoded by a frozen ViT-G/14 (EVA-CLIP) visual encoder into N×D features. The output is a text response (classification label, QA answer, or caption) generated by a frozen Vicuna-7B LLM, with the visual context compressed to just N tokens per frame regardless of video length, reducing LLM input from N×T to N tokens.

## Method

HierarQ introduces a two-stream language-guided feature modulator that applies cross-attention between frame features and text embeddings: an entity-guided stream (using BERT embeddings of extracted nouns) focuses on frame-level objects, while a prompt-guided stream (using full BERT prompt embeddings) captures broader scene-level interactions. These modulated features feed into a hierarchical Q-Former where an entity-level Q-Former with a short-term memory bank (FIFO queue) retains frame-specific object details, and a scene-level Q-Former with a long-term memory bank (Memory Bank Compression via cosine-similarity-based token merging) captures evolving scene context. The scene-level Q-Former additionally cross-attends to the entity-level Q-Former's learned queries, hierarchically integrating short-term entity insights into long-term scene understanding. The final learned queries are projected to LLM dimension and combined with the prompt for text generation, with training using cross-entropy loss and LoRA (rank 32) fine-tuning of the LLM.

## Results

On the LVU medium-to-long video understanding benchmark, HierarQ achieves 67.9% average top-1 accuracy (69.4% Relation, 49.3% Speak, 85.1% Scene), surpassing the previous best MA-LMM by 6.8%. On MovieChat-1k long video QA, it reaches 87.5% Global accuracy and 76.4% Breakpoint accuracy, outperforming FVS+S3 by 3.5% and 2.9% respectively. On short video QA, HierarQ leads by 3.7% (MSRVTT-QA: 54.1%), 5.6% (MSVD-QA: 66.2%), and 6.0% (ActivityNet-QA: 57.1%) over prior SOTA. For video captioning, it achieves 80.5, 183.1, and 136.1 CIDEr on MSRVTT, MSVD, and YouCook2 respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on all three.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HierarQ outperforms all 30 compared baselines across the 10 evaluated benchmarks. On LVU, it surpasses the strongest prior method MA-LMM [23] by 6.8% average accuracy and also beats state-space models (S5: 60.9%, VideoMamba: 57.8%) and trajectory-attention methods (Orthoformer: 51.5%). On MovieChat-1k, it exceeds FVS+S3 [74] (the previous SOTA) by 3.5% in Global accuracy and 2.9% in Breakpoint accuracy, while also substantially outperforming MovieChat [60] and Video-ChatGPT [45]. On short video QA, it beats Mirasol3B [48] by 3.7% on MSRVTT-QA and 6.0% on ActivityNet-QA, and MA-LMM [23] by 5.6% on MSVD-QA. For video captioning, it edges out mPLUG-2 [80] on MSRVTT (80.5 vs 80.3 CIDEr) and GIT [70] on MSVD (183.1 vs 180.2), while leading MA-LMM by 4.9 CIDEr on YouCook2. Notably, HierarQ achieves these gains without large-scale video-text pre-training, relying only on image-text pre-training (InstructBLIP) plus video-text fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

HierarQ extends the Q-Former architecture from BLIP-2/InstructBLIP by introducing a hierarchical, two-stream design with dedicated short-term and long-term memory banks, building directly on the memory-augmented paradigm of MA-LMM. It departs from prior long-video approaches that rely on frame sampling, spatio-temporal pooling, or token compression by processing every frame autoregressively while using a lightweight two-stream language-guided feature modulator (entity-level and scene-level) to inject task awareness without increasing LLM context length. The memory bank compression (MBC) technique is borrowed from MA-LMM, but the combination of FIFO for short-term entity memory and MBC for long-term scene memory, coupled with the hierarchical cross-attention between entity and scene Q-Formers, is a novel architectural contribution not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work offers a practical, scalable solution to the context-length bottleneck that plagues MLLMs on medium-to-long videos, demonstrating that task-aware, frame-by-frame processing with hierarchical memory can outperform sampling-based methods across 10 diverse benchmarks. The ablation revealing that entity-focused modulation alone *degrades* performance (without scene context) and that short-term memory beyond 10 frames becomes counterproductive provides useful design guidance for future memory-augmented video models. Open questions include how the approach scales to hour-long videos, whether the BERT-based noun extraction for entity identification generalizes to more complex or multilingual prompts, and whether the frozen ViT-G/14 encoder limits ceiling performance on fine-grained temporal tasks.
