## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting's EWA splatting formulation relies on first-order linearization of the projection function, which introduces approximation errors under camera distortion, requires a dedicated Jacobian per camera model, and cannot represent time-dependent effects like rolling shutter. Additionally, rasterization cannot simulate secondary rays needed for reflections and refractions, while ray-tracing-based alternatives are 3–4× slower. The paper asks whether these limitations can be overcome while retaining rasterization-level rendering speed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and intrinsics, where intrinsics may encode arbitrary nonlinear distortion (e.g., equidistant fisheye) and time-dependent sensor effects (e.g., rolling shutter). It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation (positions, covariances, opacities, spherical-harmonic appearance) that can be rendered in real-time at >200 FPS. Notably, the same trained representation can be rendered under different camera models than those used during training, and supports hybrid rendering where primary rays are splatted and secondary rays are traced for lighting effects.

## Method

The core substitution replaces EWA splatting's Jacobian-based linearization with the Unscented Transform: each 3D Gaussian is approximated by 7 sigma points (from the covariance's Cholesky-like factorization), each point is projected exactly through an arbitrary nonlinear camera projection function, and the 2D image-plane covariance is re-estimated as the weighted sample covariance of the projected points. This is derivative-free, requires no per-camera Jacobian derivation, and naturally handles rolling shutter by applying a different extrinsic to each sigma point. To enable secondary rays, the paper aligns particle evaluation with 3DGRT by computing the response in 3D at the point of maximum Gaussian density along each ray (Eq. 11) rather than in 2D after projection, and adopts multi-layer alpha blending for per-ray sorting. This unified formulation allows the same scene to be rendered via rasterization for primary rays and ray tracing for secondary rays, unlocking reflections and refractions at near-rasterization speed.

## Results

On MipNeRF360, 3DGUT (Ours) achieves 27.26 PSNR, 0.810 SSIM, and 0.218 LPIPS at 265 FPS, matching 3DGS in PSNR while improving SSIM and LPIPS, and the sorted variant (Ours sorted) reaches 27.26 PSNR, 0.812 SSIM, 0.215 LPIPS at 200 FPS. On Tanks & Temples, Ours achieves 23.21 PSNR and 0.178 LPIPS at 277 FPS. On the fisheye-camera Scannet++ dataset, Ours (sorted) achieves 29.11 PSNR and 0.910 SSIM with only 0.38M Gaussians, substantially outperforming the dedicated FisheyeGS method (28.15 PSNR, 0.901 SSIM, 1.07M Gaussians). Rendering speed remains in the 200–277 FPS range, far exceeding tracing-based methods (3DGRT at 52 FPS, EVER at 36 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS [18], 3DGUT matches PSNR on MipNeRF360 (27.26 vs 27.26) while improving SSIM (0.810 vs 0.803) and LPIPS (0.218 vs 0.240), at a modest speed cost (265 vs 347 FPS); on Tanks & Temples it trades a small PSNR drop (23.21 vs 23.64) for better SSIM and LPIPS. Against StopThePop [37], the sorted variant matches or exceeds PSNR (27.26 vs 27.14) with better LPIPS (0.215 vs 0.235) but at lower FPS (200 vs 340). Against the tracing baselines 3DGRT [34] and EVER [30], 3DGUT (sorted) is 4–5× faster (200 vs 52 FPS; 200 vs 36 FPS) while achieving comparable or better LPIPS (0.215 vs 0.248 and 0.233), though EVER edges ahead on PSNR (27.51 vs 27.26) and SSIM (0.825 vs 0.812) on MipNeRF360. On the fisheye-camera Scannet++ dataset, 3DGUT (sorted) clearly outperforms the dedicated FisheyeGS [25] method by ~1 dB PSNR (29.11 vs 28.15) and ~0.01 SSIM while using less than half the Gaussians (0.38M vs 1.07M), demonstrating that the general UT formulation surpasses a hand-derived Jacobian for a specific camera model. ZipNeRF [2] achieves the highest PSNR on MipNeRF360 (28.54) but at 0.2 FPS, making it impractical for real-time use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by replacing the EWA splatting projection with the Unscented Transform (UT) from the Kalman filtering literature, eliminating the need for per-camera-model Jacobians and enabling exact projection under arbitrary nonlinear distortions. It simultaneously aligns the rasterization formulation with 3DGRT's ray-tracing formulation (3D particle evaluation, per-ray sorting via MLAB), creating a unified representation that supports both splatting and tracing. This departs from prior approaches that either derive dedicated Jacobians for specific camera models (FisheyeGS) or abandon rasterization entirely for ray tracing (3DGRT, EVER) at a 3–5× speed cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UT-based projection is a clean, general solution that makes distorted cameras and rolling shutter trivially supported within the rasterization framework, which is practically critical for autonomous driving and robotics applications. The hybrid rendering capability (splatting primary rays, tracing secondary rays) unlocks reflections and refractions at near-rasterization speed, a capability previously exclusive to slower tracing pipelines. Limitations include a ~25% speed penalty over vanilla 3DGS, degraded particle-to-pixel assignment under extreme distortion (projected shape deviates from 2D Gaussian), and inability to accurately render overlapping Gaussians due to single-point evaluation. Open directions include multi-sample evaluation (à la EVER), inverse rendering, and relighting enabled by the 3DGRT alignment.
