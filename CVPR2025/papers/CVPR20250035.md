## Problem

Data-driven human trajectory predictors are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations and imperfect observations from upstream perception modules, yet existing robustness defenses are heuristic and offer no formal guarantees. This paper is the first to introduce certification—providing guaranteed output bounds under bounded input noise—to the trajectory prediction task, addressing the unique challenges of unbounded regression outputs, multi-modality, and the performance cost of smoothing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250035/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D observation sequence of T_obs=9 past xy-coordinates (18-dim vector) and produces a predicted trajectory of T_pred=12 future xy-coordinates (24-dim vector) together with certified lower and upper bounds per coordinate. At inference, n=100 Monte-Carlo Gaussian perturbations (σ²I) are added to the input, each passed through a diffusion denoiser and then the predictor, with outputs aggregated via median (preferred) or mean. For multi-modal predictors (e.g., k=20 modes), all modes are certified independently and the best mode is selected by minimum Certified-FDE.

## Method

The core contribution adapts randomized smoothing certification to trajectory prediction by combining three components: (1) an aggregation-based smoothed predictor f(X)=A(g(h(X+ε))) where g is a standard trajectory predictor, h is a denoiser, and A is median or mean aggregation; (2) an unconditional diffusion-based denoiser (an autoencoder trained via multi-step denoising diffusion) that suppresses input noise before the predictor, yielding tighter certified bounds at comparable accuracy; and (3) an adaptive clamping strategy that computes per-coordinate min/max over the training set and clamps outputs to establish the bounded range required by mean-smoothing certification equations. Median smoothing is shown to produce substantially tighter bounds than mean smoothing because trajectory predictors are sensitive to input noise, making mean aggregation vulnerable to outliers. The paper also introduces certified evaluation metrics (Certified-ADE/FDE, Certified-Col, FBD) that measure worst-case performance within the certified bounds rather than assuming clean inputs.

## Results

On the Trajnet++ benchmark (ETH, UCY, WildTrack), the smoothed EqMotion predictor achieves FDE=1.14 m with a Certified-FDE of 2.07 m, while the smoothed D-Pool achieves the lowest Certified-FDE of 2.0 m at FDE=1.23 m. The diffusion denoiser reduces remaining noise to 0.24 m at noise level 0.40, outperforming the Wiener filter (0.26 m), Moving Average (0.29 m), and polynomial fitting (0.36 m). In the multi-modal setting (k=20 modes), the smoothed EqMotion improves to FDE=0.39 m and Certified-FDE=1.38 m, substantially tighter than the single-modal Certified-FDE of 2.07 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four trajectory predictors, EqMotion [59] achieves the lowest clean FDE (1.12 m) but is not the most robust: smoothed D-Pool [34] attains the tightest Certified-FDE (2.0 m vs. 2.07 m for EqMotion), demonstrating that the most accurate model is not necessarily the most robust. Smoothed Social-Force [27] has the largest certified bounds (Certified-FDE=2.27 m, Certified-Col=46%), reflecting its high sensitivity to input perturbations despite its low clean collision rate (7.4%). Smoothed AutoBot [24] sits between D-Pool and EqMotion with Certified-FDE=2.05 m. For denoising, the proposed unconditional diffusion denoiser outperforms all three classical baselines at every noise level: at noise=0.40 it leaves 0.24 m residual noise versus 0.26 m (Wiener filter [57]), 0.29 m (Moving Average [46]), and 0.36 m (polynomial fitting [6]). In the multi-modal setting, the 20-mode EqMotion reduces Certified-FDE from 2.07 m (single-modal) to 1.38 m, a 33% tightening, because each mode concentrates on a specific behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts randomized smoothing certification, previously established for image classification (mean smoothing) and object detection (median smoothing), to the multi-output regression task of human trajectory prediction. It integrates a denoising diffusion model as a pre-processor to mitigate the accuracy drop typically associated with smoothing, and introduces adaptive clamping to handle the unbounded nature of trajectory outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides the first guaranteed robustness framework for trajectory prediction, addressing a critical safety gap in autonomous driving systems where heuristic defenses are insufficient. A key finding is that the most accurate models are not necessarily the most robust, highlighting the need for certified metrics in safety-critical applications. Open questions include the computational cost of Monte-Carlo sampling and the generalization of these bounds to more complex, multi-agent interaction scenarios.
