## Problem

Pre-trained video diffusion models frequently produce outputs that deviate from user preferences in both visual quality (blur, temporal inconsistency) and text-video semantic alignment, yet Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has not been adapted to the video domain. Existing reward models for alignment target only a single aspect—either visual quality or semantic fidelity—leaving the other dimension unaddressed, and human annotation of video preference pairs is prohibitively expensive at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250954/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The alignment pipeline consumes a text prompt and generates N candidate videos (N=4 in experiments) from a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model (VideoCrafter2, T2V-Turbo, or CogVideo). Each candidate is scored by OmniScore conditioned on the prompt, and the highest- and lowest-scoring videos form a preference pair. Training uses 10,000 human-written prompts from VidProm, and the aligned model outputs a single generated video per prompt at inference time, with no change to the generation interface.

## Method

VideoDPO adapts the DiffusionDPO loss to video by introducing three key components. First, OmniScore is a multi-dimensional scoring function that jointly evaluates intra-frame quality (image fidelity and aesthetic appeal), inter-frame quality (subject/background consistency, temporal flickering, motion smoothness, and dynamic degree), and CLIP-based text-video semantic alignment, producing a single scalar per video. Second, an automated pipeline generates preference pairs by sampling N videos per prompt and selecting the best-vs-worst pair by OmniScore, eliminating human annotation. Third, an OmniScore-based re-weighting scheme computes a frequency histogram over all generated video scores and assigns each pair a weight w_pair = (β / √(p(s^W)·p(s^L)))^α, so that distinctive pairs (those with rarer score combinations) receive higher gradient contribution during DPO training, with α=1 and β=0.72 in the main experiments.

## Results

VideoDPO consistently improves VBench Total scores across all three tested models: VC2 from 80.44% to 81.93%, T2V-Turbo from 80.95% to 81.80%, and CogVideo from 79.30% to 79.80%. The most notable gain is in semantic alignment for VC2, which jumps from 73.42% to 77.38% (+3.96 points), while visual quality also improves across all models (e.g., VC2 quality from 82.20% to 83.07%). Human preference metrics show modest but consistent gains, with HPS(V) rising from 0.258 to 0.261 and PickScore from 20.65 to 20.65 for VC2, and PickScore from 21.15 to 21.18 for Turbo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VC2 baseline, VideoDPO achieves a +1.49 point gain in VBench Total (81.93 vs. 80.44) and a substantial +3.96 point improvement in semantic alignment (77.38 vs. 73.42), while also outperforming the VADER reward-gradient method by +1.34 points on VBench Total (81.93 vs. 80.59) and +4.29 points on semantics (77.38 vs. 73.09). Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) actually degrades VC2 performance (78.78 vs. 80.44 baseline), confirming that learning from negative samples in DPO is critical. For T2V-Turbo, VideoDPO improves VBench Total by +0.85 points (81.80 vs. 80.95) and quality by +1.09 points (83.80 vs. 82.71), though semantic alignment remains nearly unchanged (73.81 vs. 73.93). For CogVideo, VideoDPO yields a smaller but consistent +0.50 point VBench Total gain (79.80 vs. 79.30) and +0.65 point quality improvement, while semantic alignment slightly decreases (66.99 vs. 67.10), likely due to the model's inherently lower generation quality limiting the alignment signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework from Rafailov et al. (NeurIPS 2023) and its diffusion-model adaptation by Wallace et al. (DiffusionDPO, 2023) from text-to-image to text-to-video generation. It departs from prior video alignment methods such as VADER (reward-gradient fine-tuning) and T2V-Turbo (distillation with mixed rewards) by using a preference-pair formulation rather than a single differentiable reward, and introduces OmniScore—a multi-dimensional scoring system inspired by VBench—as the basis for constructing those pairs. The data re-weighting strategy, which up-weights rare high-contrast pairs based on score-frequency histograms, is a novel training-time mechanism not present in prior DPO or RLHF-for-diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VideoDPO provides a practical, automated pipeline for aligning video diffusion models without human annotation, which is important as video generation models scale and user expectations for quality and prompt fidelity grow. However, the improvements are modest (roughly 1–2 percentage points on VBench total), and the approach incurs significant data-generation cost (N videos per prompt, 10K prompts). Open questions include whether the method scales to longer or higher-resolution videos, whether the best-vs-worst pairing strategy remains optimal at larger N, and whether the re-weighting mechanism generalizes beyond the specific OmniScore formulation. The paper also does not address potential reward hacking or distribution shift when the aligned model generates samples outside the training prompt distribution.
