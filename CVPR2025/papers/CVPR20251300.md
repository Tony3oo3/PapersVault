## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D reconstruction methods produce dense, photorealistic representations that are over-parameterized, sensitive to appearance variations, and difficult to interpret. No prior work abstracts 3D object movements from video into sparse, view-consistent 3D sketches that capture essential motion structure while remaining robust to environmental factors such as lighting changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251300/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB video frames captured from a non-stationary camera along with per-frame camera extrinsics, intrinsics, and timesteps; the synthetic dataset is re-rendered from D-NeRF scenes with a consistent camera trajectory on an upper hemisphere (100 frames per scene). The output is a dynamic 3D sketch consisting of n fully opaque cubic Bezier curves whose motion is parameterized by per-stroke rigid transforms (quaternion rotation + translation) and per-control-point displacements, plus an intermediate 3D motion guidance represented as a deformed point cloud. Strokes are rendered to 2D via an orthographic projection approximation and a differentiable vector-graphics rasterizer.

## Method

Liv3Stroke operates in two stages. First, a 3D motion guidance is learned by deforming a 10k-point cloud with an MLP that takes positional and temporal encodings; the deformed points are rasterized as black Gaussian units and optimized against RGB frames using a robust LPIPS loss (not pixel-wise), regularized by velocity-continuity and rigid-transformation terms. Second, dynamic strokes are drawn by initializing Bezier control points via FPS sampling from the guidance point cloud, then learning per-stroke rotations and translations (via MLPs M_R and M_T) and per-control-point displacements (via M_L) in a coarse-to-fine schedule. The sketch rendering loss combines LPIPS and CLIP cosine distance, augmented with temporal smoothness and structure-stability regularizers, and a sigmoid correction function ξ suppresses spurious micro-movements during static periods. The key design choice is decomposing stroke motion into a global rigid component and a local shape-deformation component, enabling the method to preserve core object shape while capturing diverse deformable and articulated movements.

## Results

On the sketch synthesis task (Table 1a), Liv3Stroke achieves structural alignment scores of 0.693 (novel views) and 0.683 (fixed views) with the smallest standard deviations among all methods (±0.096 and ±0.108), indicating the most consistent structure preservation across the sequence. For motion prompt similarity, Liv3Stroke scores 0.656 in both novel and fixed view settings with the lowest variance (±0.006 and ±0.008). On the 3D motion guidance task (Table 1b), Liv3Stroke achieves a per-frame Chamfer distance of 0.252±0.049 and motion velocity distance of 4.16×10⁻³±2.34, outperforming Deformable 3DGS (0.302, 5.05) and SC-GS (0.294, 4.21) while remaining competitive with DG-Mesh (0.277, 4.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For sketch synthesis, Liv3Stroke does not achieve the highest mean structural alignment (Suggestive Contours leads at 0.784 novel / 0.750 fixed) or motion prompt similarity (CLIPasso leads at 0.659/0.664), but it achieves the smallest standard deviations across all four metrics, demonstrating the most temporally consistent output. Sketch Video Synthesis performs worst on structural alignment (0.663/0.657) due to its 2D-only formulation and reliance on layered neural atlases that fail under large camera motion. Suggestive Contours achieves the highest structural scores but requires pre-extracted 3D meshes and tends toward detailed outlines rather than abstraction. For 3D motion guidance, Liv3Stroke outperforms Deformable 3DGS (0.302→0.252 Chamfer) and SC-GS (0.294→0.252) on per-frame accuracy, and beats 4DGS (4.60→4.16) and Deformable 3DGS (5.05→4.16) on motion velocity distance, while remaining within 0.06 Chamfer and 0.06 velocity distance of DG-Mesh despite using a simpler point-cloud repositioning approach rather than full mesh reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: dynamic 3D scene reconstruction (D-NeRF, 4DGS, DG-Mesh), stroke-based sketch synthesis (CLIPasso, Sketch Video Synthesis), and 3D curve-based shape representation (3Doodle, EMAP). It departs from dense photorealistic reconstruction by replacing the full radiance field or Gaussian splatting with a sparse set of deformable 3D Bezier curves, and extends 2D sketch synthesis into a view-consistent 3D formulation with temporal dynamics. The MLP-based deformation network for motion guidance borrows directly from D-NeRF's canonical-to-deformed warping, while the perceptual loss strategy (LPIPS + CLIP) follows the sketch-generation literature's insight that latent-space alignment outperforms pixel-wise matching for abstract representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Liv3Stroke demonstrates that a handful of parametric 3D curves can capture the essential structure and motion of diverse objects from monocular video, offering a compact, interpretable, and editable alternative to dense dynamic scene representations. Its robustness to lighting changes and camera motion makes it practical for real-world footage where photorealistic methods degrade. Key limitations include the inability to render view-dependent features (e.g., silhouettes of rounded objects) and the reliance on a two-stage pipeline (guidance then sketch fitting) that adds optimization complexity. Open questions include whether stroke-based representations can support physical simulation or interactive editing of motion, and whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes with occlusion.
