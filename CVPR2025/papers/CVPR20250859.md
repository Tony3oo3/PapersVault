## Problem

GPS tags in photo metadata have been widely used for classification, retrieval, and geolocation prediction, but have never been exploited as a conditioning signal for generative models. This paper asks whether GPS coordinates can serve as a fine-grained spatial control signal that captures how images vary across a city—information that text prompts alone cannot convey—and whether such GPS-conditioned models can also enable 3D reconstruction without explicit camera pose estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250859/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes raw GPS coordinates (longitude, latitude) normalized to [-1,1] and a free-form text prompt as input, producing 512×512 RGB images. For the 3D reconstruction variant, GPS is converted to an azimuth angle relative to a landmark's center, discretized into 10° bins, and the output is a NeRF parameterized by a multiresolution hash encoding. A third output mode produces 'average images' by averaging noise estimates across many GPS locations within a user-specified region, yielding a single representative image of a concept over that area.

## Method

The approach fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v1.4 by encoding GPS coordinates via sinusoidal positional encoding (frequency 10) and a two-layer MLP into a 2×D embedding, which is appended to CLIP text tokens as additional conditioning tokens—preserving the pretrained model's input format. Training uses the standard DDPM denoising loss with both text and GPS as conditions, and inference employs a multi-condition classifier-free guidance formulation (adapted from InstructPix2Pix) with separate guidance weights for text and GPS. For 3D reconstruction, a landmark-specific angle-to-image diffusion model is trained by replacing the 2D GPS embedding with a 1D azimuth-angle embedding, regularized with a DreamBooth-style prior preservation loss to maintain generative diversity. A NeRF is then optimized via score distillation sampling, where each rendered viewpoint is scored by the angle-conditioned diffusion model, eliminating the need for SfM or explicit camera pose estimation.

## Results

On the GPS-to-image generation task (Manhattan dataset), the proposed method achieves the best FID of 6.99, substantially outperforming the strongest text-only fine-tuned baseline (11.26) and the text+address baseline (17.94). It also achieves the highest GPS score (17.41 vs. 11.53 for the best baseline) and the lowest OSV5M geolocation error (MAE 23.15, RMSE 45.75), confirming strong spatial conditioning. For 3D landmark reconstruction, the method achieves CLIP Score 31.87, Perceptual Quality 3.31/5, and Tourist Score 3.45/5, outperforming DreamFusion (29.49, 2.21, 2.09) and Nerfacto (20.57, 1.32, 1.36) on all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three image-generation baselines: (1) Stable Diffusion v1.4 with text+address concatenation, (2) Stable Diffusion v1.4 with text only, and (3) Stable Diffusion fine-tuned on the same city data with text only. The proposed GPS+text method achieves the best FID (6.99 vs. 11.26 for the fine-tuned text-only baseline, a 38% improvement) and the best GPS score (17.41 vs. 11.53), demonstrating that explicit GPS conditioning captures spatial information that text or address names cannot. It also achieves the lowest geolocation error (OSV5M MAE 23.15 vs. 26.83), though it trails slightly on CLIP Score (31.23 vs. 32.17) and VQAScore (82.66 vs. 82.97), suggesting a minor trade-off in text alignment. For 3D reconstruction, the method outperforms DreamFusion by 2.4 CLIP Score points and by 1.1 and 1.36 on the 5-point Perceptual Quality and Tourist Score scales, respectively, while the SfM+NeRF baseline (COLMAP + Nerfacto) catastrophically fails on 5 of 6 landmarks, whereas the GPS-guided approach succeeds on all 6 without requiring camera pose estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion model framework (Stable Diffusion) by introducing GPS coordinates as a novel conditioning signal, building on the multi-condition classifier-free guidance formulation from InstructPix2Pix and the prior preservation loss from DreamBooth. For 3D reconstruction, it adapts DreamFusion's score distillation sampling by replacing text-based view prompts with GPS-derived azimuth angles, providing a stronger geometric prior than language. The work departs from prior GPS-related research (geolocation prediction, image classification with location context, satellite image generation) by treating GPS as a generative control signal rather than a classification or retrieval feature, and from prior 3D reconstruction pipelines by eliminating the need for explicit SfM-based camera pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that GPS metadata—a free, abundantly available signal in EXIF data—can serve as a meaningful conditioning signal for fine-grained spatial image generation, opening a new axis of control complementary to text. The 3D reconstruction pipeline that bypasses SfM is practically significant because traditional structure-from-motion is brittle and fails entirely on a substantial fraction of unconstrained photo collections (3 of 6 landmarks in their evaluation). Key limitations include dependence on densely GPS-tagged tourist photos, saturation artifacts from SDS, and the difficulty of disentangling semantic content (e.g., a museum's interior) from purely spatial information in GPS tags. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to sparsely photographed regions, non-tourist scenes, and whether GPS conditioning can be combined with other spatial signals (elevation, street-level geometry) for richer control.
