## Problem

Generating production-ready 3D plant models from single-view images remains unsolved: diffusion-based 3D reconstruction methods produce noisy, structurally inseparable meshes for plants' intricate branching and foliage, while existing plant-specific methods either require user interaction, rely on expensive multi-view/point-cloud data, or are limited to outdoor trees. No prior approach offers a fully automatic, generic framework that handles both houseplants (requiring individual leaf-level detail) and outdoor trees (requiring efficient sub-tree abstraction) from a single photograph.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB photograph of a plant (houseplant or outdoor tree) as input, first generating a segmentation mask via UPerNet+Swin-Transformer to isolate the plant and provide semantic cues. The output is a structured 3D plant model with fine geometric detail, mediated by an intermediate hierarchical box representation: an n-ary tree of oriented bounding boxes, each annotated with a semantic label (branch, leaf, flower, sub-tree, trunk) and up to 12 botanical parameters (e.g., bending angles, twig density, leaf size). For trees, terminal boxes represent sub-tree volumes; for houseplants, they represent individual leaves, flowers, or short stems.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical box representation that encodes plant structure as an n-ary tree of oriented bounding boxes, learned end-to-end via a VAE-style training scheme. A ResNet-50 image feature network maps the photograph and its segmentation mask to a 256-dim latent vector, which a hierarchical graph decoder (built on MolGAN) recursively expands into child boxes from root to leaves. The decoder comprises two sub-modules: Dec_g predicts the graph topology (child feature vectors, labels, existence probabilities, and edge connections), while Dec_box (an MLP) recovers each box's spatial parameters (center, quaternion rotation, scale) and botanical parameters. Training uses a mirrored hierarchical graph encoder to form a VAE, with losses for geometric reconstruction (Chamfer distance on box surfaces), semantic consistency, and existence accuracy. Geometry synthesis then differs by category: trees use MST-based trunk extraction followed by L-system growth constrained within sub-tree boxes, while houseplants use parametric deformable surfaces for individual leaves shaped by the predicted botanical parameters.

## Results

The paper reports quantitative evaluation of the hierarchical box reconstruction quality using Chamfer distance and Hausdorff distance (with plant heights normalized to 1.0). On the test set, houseplants achieve a Chamfer distance of 0.065 (±0.013) and Hausdorff distance of 0.092 (±0.034), while outdoor trees achieve 0.082 (±0.025) and 0.136 (±0.069), respectively. The average across both categories is 0.073 (±0.019) Chamfer and 0.113 (±0.051) Hausdorff. No quantitative comparison against baselines is provided; all baseline comparisons are qualitative and visual.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All comparisons in this paper are qualitative (visual), with no quantitative head-to-head metrics reported. Against diffusion-based single-image-to-3D methods (Wonder3D [26] and One-2-3-45 [16]), the proposed method clearly outperforms: those methods produce noisy, watertight volumes that lack fine branch and leaf details for trees and yield structurally inseparable meshes, whereas the proposed method generates editable, hierarchically structured 3D plant assets with fine geometric detail. Against the RBV-based tree reconstruction method of Li et al. [11], the proposed method wins on expressiveness and generality: the RBV representation cannot capture challenging tree shapes with holes or long branches, and the method is entirely inapplicable to houseplants, while the proposed hierarchical box representation handles both categories through adaptive levels of detail. The paper positions itself as the first comprehensive single-view solution covering both outdoor trees and houseplants, a capability none of the compared baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hierarchical graph generation paradigm from StructureNet [29] (VAE-style training of a graph encoder-decoder pair) and the MolGAN [2] architecture for graph structure prediction, repurposing both for botanical plant decomposition. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based single-image-to-3D pipeline (Wonder3D, One-2-3-45) by arguing that watertight NeRF-like surfaces are fundamentally unsuitable for plants with intricate branching and self-occlusion, instead proposing a lightweight hierarchical oriented-bounding-box representation as an intermediate. It also extends prior single-image tree reconstruction work (Li et al. [11] with Radial Bounding Volumes, Liu et al. [22] with adversarial networks) by generalizing from trees-only to a unified framework covering both outdoor trees and houseplants through adaptive levels of detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce structured, editable 3D plant assets (not just meshes) from a single photograph for both major plant categories, which is directly useful for game/VR/landscape-design pipelines where artists need separable components. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic training data (2k plants per species), per-species training rather than a single general model, and a limited species catalog (12 trees, 9 houseplants). Open questions include whether the hierarchical box representation generalizes to unseen species without retraining, whether richer botanical priors can be learned end-to-end from images, and whether the AI-CG synergy workflow extends to other structured object categories like buildings as the authors speculate.
