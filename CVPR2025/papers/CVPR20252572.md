## Problem

Current video-based skill assessment methods only produce scores or compare demonstrations, leaving the learner to independently identify and correct their own mistakes. No prior method provides the full coaching loop—identifying the specific error, explaining the correction in natural language, and showing a visual demonstration of the correct execution—tailored to the individual learner's demonstration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252572/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single learner demonstration consisting of an ego-exo RGB video pair (first-person close-up plus third-person full-body context) and a 3D body pose sequence of 17 MS-COCO joints per frame. It produces three complementary outputs: a free-form expert commentary sentence identifying the mistake and prescribing the fix, a retrieved expert demonstration video from a candidate set, and a generated 3D pose sequence that visually corrects the error. At inference, only the learner's video and pose are provided; the model must infer the error and all corrections without access to the expert demonstration.

## Method

ExpertAF encodes video (via frozen InternVideo2 + trainable visual mapper), 3D pose (via PCT adapted to 3D + pose mapper), and text (standard tokenization) into a shared multimodal token space, then uses Llama 3-8B as an autoregressive sequence predictor to jointly learn commentary generation, demonstration retrieval, and pose generation. The training dataset is weakly supervised: Llama3-70B classifies Ego-Exo4D expert commentary into correct/incorrect labels with body-region localization, and incorrect novice clips are paired with correct expert clips via Procrustes-aligned PA-MPJPE temporal alignment. At inference, the model first generates the expert commentary from the learner input alone, then conditions both retrieval (minimizing the autoregressive loss over candidate expert poses) and pose generation (predicting pose tokens decoded back to 3D joints) on that predicted commentary, unifying all three outputs under a single autoregressive framework.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D-based test set (1,272 samples across soccer, basketball, and rock climbing), ExpertAF achieves 44.9 BLEU-4, 49.6 METEOR, and 54.6 ROUGE-L F1 for expert commentary generation; 19.1 recall@50 and 158 median rank for expert demonstration retrieval; and 135 mm PA-MPJPE for expert pose generation. These are the best scores across all three output tasks, with gains of up to 3% over the strongest fine-tuned baseline (LLaVA-FT w/ pose) on commentary metrics, a 14-position improvement in median rank for retrieval, and a 15 mm reduction in pose error. Human evaluation confirms the advantage, with ExpertAF rated up to 0.7 points higher (on a 1–4 Likert scale) and up to 3× preferred over off-the-shelf video models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExpertAF outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three tasks. Against the strongest fine-tuned baseline LLaVA-FT w/ pose (43.6 BLEU-4, 18.0 recall@50, 150 PA-MPJPE), ExpertAF gains +1.3 BLEU-4, +1.1 recall@50, and −15 mm PA-MPJPE. Off-the-shelf captioning models (LLaVA, VideoChat2) perform poorly on commentary (28.5 and 27.8 BLEU-4 respectively) because they describe the activity rather than critique it. PoseScript/Fix, designed for pose-to-text and text-to-pose reasoning, scores lowest on commentary (24.1 BLEU-4) and worst on pose generation (182 PA-MPJPE), confirming that generic pose descriptions are inadequate for actionable coaching feedback. The InternVideo2 retrieval baselines (NN and FT) achieve the lowest recall@50 (13.5 and 14.1) and highest median ranks (198 and 190), showing that pure visual similarity is insufficient for finding the correct expert demonstration. The privileged-input variant (ExpertAF w/ full-sup) further improves all metrics, demonstrating the value of jointly conditioning on both learner and expert demonstrations when available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior skill-assessment work (which reduces coaching to score prediction or fixed error taxonomies) by formulating feedback as free-form, actionable output in both language and visual form. Methodologically, it builds on the vision-language model paradigm (LLaVA-style autoregressive generation) and the PCT pose-tokenization framework, but introduces a novel multimodal tokenization pipeline that unifies ego-exo video, 3D pose sequences, and text within a single LLM (Llama 3-8B). The dataset construction repurposes Ego-Exo4D's expert commentary through LLM-based (Llama3-70B) pseudo-labeling and Procrustes-aligned temporal matching, creating a weakly-supervised training set that no prior work has provided for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark and method for video-based coaching with actionable feedback, establishing a new evaluation paradigm that goes beyond scoring. Key limitations include reliance on auto-generated (noisy) 3D poses, restriction to three physical scenarios, and the absence of expert video generation (only retrieval and pose generation). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to procedural tasks where feedback is not visually groundable, how to handle multiple simultaneous errors in a single demonstration, and whether the weakly-supervised LLM-annotated training data introduces systematic biases that the gold-standard test set only partially mitigates.
