## Problem

Existing concept removal benchmarks for text-to-image diffusion models suffer from three critical gaps: they evaluate only limited concept categories without consistent cross-category comparisons, they include large portions of ineffective prompts (especially for general concepts like harm and nudity) that make evaluation inefficient and create unfair comparisons with specific concepts, and they entirely overlook in-prompt retainability—whether the benign semantics within a prompt are preserved after the unwanted concept is removed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251124/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark operates on Stable Diffusion v1.4 (and other T2I diffusion models) and takes as input a trained model, a text prompt containing one or more unwanted concepts, and the target concept(s) to remove. The Six-CD dataset provides 6,326 harm prompts, 4,518 nudity prompts, 94 celebrity identities, 100 copyrighted characters, 10 objects, and 10 art styles, with high-effectiveness subsets (harm-EF: 991 prompts, nudity-EF: 1,539 prompts) for more efficient evaluation. The Dual-Version Dataset pairs each prompt with a malicious version (containing the unwanted concept) and a benign version (concept removed, rest identical), enabling the in-prompt CLIP score to measure how well the generated image retains the benign semantics.

## Method

The paper introduces Six-CD, a comprehensive dataset covering six categories of unwanted concepts divided into general (harm, nudity) and specific (celebrity, copyrighted characters, objects, art styles) groups, with prompts sourced from NSFW resources and annotated using NudeNet and Q16 classifiers for fine-grained harm/nudity labeling. For specific concepts, prompts are generated from templates applied to collected concept lists. The key evaluation innovation is the in-prompt CLIP score: images are generated from the malicious-version prompt using a concept-removed model, and their CLIP embedding similarity is measured against the corresponding benign-version prompt, directly quantifying whether benign semantics survive removal. The benchmark systematically evaluates 10 methods (NEG, ESD, SPM, SDD, FMN, UCE, MACE, EMCID, SLD, SEGA) on single-concept removal, multi-concept removal (up to 100+ concepts), in-prompt and out-prompt retainability, FID, and training/inference time costs, revealing that general concepts are harder to remove, inference-time methods fail at scale, and closed-form linear-component modifications perform best for multi-concept scenarios.

## Results

On the Six-CD benchmark, removal performance varies dramatically by concept category: SPM and EMCID achieve ~98.9% removal on harm-EF, while SLD and SEGA remove only 27.3% and 23.7% respectively. For nudity-EF, EMCID leads at 97.2% removal, whereas SDD and SLD remove only 4.2% and 16.3%. No single method achieves strong removal on both general categories simultaneously, and in-prompt CLIP scores are consistently lower than out-prompt CLIP scores across all 10 methods, indicating that benign semantics within malicious prompts are disproportionately damaged by concept removal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 10 concept removal methods on Six-CD. For general concepts, SPM (98.9% harm, 81.0% nudity) and EMCID (98.9% harm, 98.3% nudity) are the strongest, while inference-time methods SLD (27.3% harm, 14.6% nudity) and NEG (40.9% harm, 22.3% nudity) are weakest. For specific concepts (celebrity, character, object, art style), ESD, UCE, and SLD achieve near-complete removal, but no method simultaneously excels on both general and specific categories. When scaling to multiple concepts (>50), inference-time methods SLD and NEG degrade to <20% reduction in detection rate, while closed-form methods UCE and MACE maintain strong performance. On retainability, MACE shows the largest drop in both in-prompt and out-prompt CLIP scores due to its reliance on GroundedSAM segmentation, which over-optimizes the target region at the expense of surrounding benign content. The paper's key contribution is showing that in-prompt retainability (measured by the new in-prompt CLIP score) is systematically worse than out-prompt retainability for every method, a gap previously unmeasured in the literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new concept removal method but instead carves new ground as a unified benchmark for the existing landscape of 10 methods spanning fine-tuning (ESD, SPM, SDD, FMN), closed-form linear-component editing (UCE, MACE, EMCID), and inference-time approaches (SLD, SEGA, NEG). It departs from prior evaluations (notably I2P-based studies) by identifying and addressing three structural gaps: inconsistent category coverage across papers, the prevalence of ineffective prompts in general-concept datasets, and the complete absence of in-prompt retainability evaluation. The in-prompt CLIP score metric and the Dual-Version Dataset are novel contributions that repurpose CLIP similarity in a new evaluation context rather than introducing a new model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Six-CD and the in-prompt CLIP score provide the first systematic, category-stratified evaluation framework for concept removal, which is likely to become a standard reference for future method development. The finding that in-prompt retainability is consistently worse than out-prompt retainability across all methods reveals a practical blind spot: methods that successfully erase unwanted concepts may simultaneously destroy benign semantics in the same prompt, degrading user experience in ways prior metrics never captured. Open questions include whether the general-vs-specific difficulty gap persists in newer architectures (e.g., diffusion transformers), whether the art-style detection metric (normalized CLIP score) can be replaced by a dedicated classifier, and how these findings transfer to video or 3D generation models.
