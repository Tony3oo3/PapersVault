## Problem

Diffusion models are conventionally trained with uniform timestep sampling, yet the stochastic gradient variance varies by up to a hundredfold across timesteps, making early timesteps a convergence bottleneck. Existing non-uniform heuristics (P2, Min-SNR, Log-Normal, SpeeD) improve training speed but lack a direct connection to the variational objective and show inconsistent robustness across noise schedules, datasets, and architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252165/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB training images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CelebA-HQ and ImageNet at 256×256), a chosen noise schedule (linear, cosine, or quadratic), and a diffusion backbone (DDPM, LDM, or U-ViT). It produces a trained diffusion model whose generated images are evaluated via FID. Unusually, the timestep sampler is a small neural network trained online alongside the diffusion model, updated only every f_S gradient steps using a queue of per-timestep loss differences computed on a single sampled image, keeping the overhead minimal.

## Method

The paper introduces an adaptive timestep sampler π_φ, parameterized as a neural network that outputs two positive scalars (a, b) defining a Beta distribution over timesteps, ensuring smooth sampling across correlated neighboring timesteps. The sampler is trained via a policy-gradient (likelihood-ratio) update to maximize the expected reduction in the variational lower bound (VLB) after each diffusion-model gradient step, rather than relying on hand-designed weights. Because computing the full VLB change Δ_k^t is infeasible, the paper approximates it using only |S|=3 timesteps selected by an off-the-shelf feature-selection algorithm applied to a fixed-size queue of per-timestep loss differences. A key empirical finding is that while gradient variance is highest at early timesteps, simply sampling proportional to variance performs poorly due to strong interdependence among timestep subproblems; the learned sampler instead discovers which timesteps most effectively reduce the global objective. The approach is compatible with existing heuristic weightings (e.g., Min-SNR), which can be subsumed into the schedule-dependent constant c_t and absorbed by the sampler.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with DDPM, the proposed method achieves FID 2.94 at 1.0M steps under a linear schedule, outperforming DDPM (3.19), Min-SNR (3.16), SpeeD (3.57), Log-normal (3.60), and P2 (4.70). On CelebA-HQ 256×256 with LDM at 300 epochs, it reaches FID 6.62, beating Min-SNR (6.83), P2 (6.88), Log-normal (7.28), SpeeD (7.98), and the LDM baseline (8.72). On ImageNet 256×256 with U-ViT, combining Min-SNR weighting with the proposed adaptive sampler reduces FID from 4.54 to 4.33 at 1.0M steps. The method also demonstrates robustness across linear, cosine, and quadratic noise schedules, where heuristic baselines like Min-SNR degrade substantially (e.g., 4.17 FID on quadratic vs. 3.07 on cosine).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five heuristic baselines (DDPM uniform sampling, P2, Min-SNR, Log-normal, SpeeD) across every noise schedule and training duration on CIFAR-10, with the largest margins against P2 (e.g., 2.94 vs. 4.70 FID at 1.0M linear) and Log-normal (2.94 vs. 3.60). Min-SNR is the strongest heuristic on linear and cosine schedules (3.16 and 3.07 FID at convergence) but degrades sharply on the quadratic schedule (4.17 FID), whereas the proposed method remains stable at 2.95. On CelebA-HQ, the method initially lags behind heuristics at 100 epochs (10.94 vs. 10.69 for P2) but surpasses all baselines by 300 epochs (6.62 vs. 6.83 for Min-SNR). A critical negative result in Table 4 shows that naively sampling timesteps proportional to gradient variance yields FID 11.3 at 1.0M steps—far worse than the proposed method's 2.94—confirming that interdependence across timesteps makes variance-proportional sampling insufficient. Finally, the method is compatible with heuristic weighting: combining Min-SNR weights with the adaptive sampler on ImageNet 256×256 improves FID from 4.54 to 4.33 at 1.0M steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on non-uniform timestep training for diffusion models (P2, Min-SNR, Log-Normal, SpeeD) by replacing hand-designed heuristics with a learned adaptive sampler that directly optimizes the variational lower bound. It builds on DDPM's training objective and forward/reverse process formulation, and draws on the Learning-to-Optimize literature for its policy-gradient-style sampler update, while departing from L2O by operating entirely online without a meta-training set. The gradient variance analysis connecting to Garrigos and Gower's SGD convergence theorems provides a theoretical motivation that prior heuristic methods lacked, though the authors show this motivation alone is insufficient due to inter-timestep gradient interdependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, adaptive alternative to the growing zoo of timestep weighting heuristics, demonstrating robustness across noise schedules, datasets, and architectures where individual heuristics (e.g., Min-SNR degrading on quadratic schedules) fail. The finding that gradient-variance-proportional sampling performs poorly despite the clear variance imbalance is an important negative result that cautions against oversimplifying the optimization landscape. However, the method incurs higher computational overhead than heuristics, has not been extended to score-based diffusion frameworks, and the initial convergence is slower due to its learning-based nature—limiting its practical adoption in compute-constrained settings.
