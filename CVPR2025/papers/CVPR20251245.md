## Problem

The prevailing two-stage PAA paradigm (GAA pre-training + user fine-tuning) suffers from three structural flaws: GAA's collective preferences violate transitivity (voting paradox), personalized surveys are static and ignore object-specific preferences, and multimodal user feedback accumulated during usage is discarded. The paper proposes a three-stage PAA+ paradigm (pre-training, fine-tuning, continual learning) and validates it through the underexplored task of physique aesthetics assessment, for which no public dataset or learning-based model previously existed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB physique image plus a user's personalized survey profile (MBTI type, age, gender, physique-style preferences) and, in the continual-learning stage, multimodal feedback (revised scores, free-text comments, reference images, and short videos). The image is consumed by two parallel 3D-aware branches (mesh and skeleton), while preference data is consumed by an LLM-based module. Output is a triplet of continuous regression scores—appearance, health, and posture—each on a 1–5 scale, rather than a single holistic aesthetic score, reflecting the paper's decomposition of physique aesthetics into expressive, well-being, and structural dimensions.

## Method

PhysiqueFrame pairs a Physique Analysis Network (PANet) with a Preference Extraction Network (PENet). PANet's dual-branch design extracts 3D physique features: a Mesh Perceiving Module normalizes SMPL-X mesh vertices via an affine-geometry local-point transformation and decodes them with an MLP, while a Posture Analyzing Module encodes skeleton keypoints with Swin Transformer V2 + ROI Pooling and feeds them into a GCN over the skeletal adjacency matrix. PENet, built on LLaVA++, applies a Chain-of-Thought prompt to decompose heterogeneous multimodal feedback (text, images, video, scores) into five factor-level probability scores and a binary like/dislike verdict, which are then aggregated into a preference feature vector. The three-stage PAA+ training replaces the single GAA backbone with 16 MBTI-conditioned pre-trained models, fine-tunes the best-matching model on user-specific data, and then iteratively updates the user model with accumulated feedback using a learnable per-dimension weighted loss (Eq. 1) that dynamically balances the three regression targets.

## Results

On the User-ISFJ dataset (Stage 2), PhysiqueFrame achieves SRCC of 0.699 (Appearance), 0.625 (Health), and 0.664 (Posture), with corresponding LCC values of 0.724, 0.657, and 0.703, and ACC of 0.759, 0.761, and 0.764 respectively. These results represent state-of-the-art performance across all nine metrics, outperforming the strongest baseline (EAT) by 7.1 SRCC points on Appearance and 5.2 on Health. In the continual learning stage (Stage 3), user satisfaction reaches 84.5% for User-ISFJ, 81.0% for User-ESFJ, and 80.9% for User-ISTJ, consistently exceeding the GAA-pretrained counterparts (75.3%, 70.6%, 68.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysiqueFrame outperforms all eight baselines across every metric on the User-ISFJ dataset. The closest competitor is EAT (SRCC 0.628 Appearance, 0.573 Health, 0.592 Posture), which PhysiqueFrame surpasses by 7.1, 5.2, and 7.2 SRCC points respectively. Q-Align is the second-closest on Posture (SRCC 0.609 vs. 0.664) and Appearance (0.619 vs. 0.699). Among CNN-based models, NIMA leads (SRCC 0.598/0.559/0.581) but still trails PhysiqueFrame by 10+ SRCC points on Appearance. The weakest baselines are HGCN (SRCC 0.419 Appearance) and TCFormer (SRCC 0.452 Appearance), which struggle significantly with physique-specific assessment. Notably, the gap between PhysiqueFrame and the best baseline widens on the Health dimension (5.2 SRCC over EAT), suggesting that the 3D mesh and posture modules contribute most where generic image aesthetics models are least effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Personalized Aesthetics Assessment (PAA) paradigm introduced by Ren et al. and subsequent works (Wang et al., Li et al., Yang et al.) by proposing a three-stage PAA+ framework that replaces the conventional GAA pre-training with personality-specific pre-training models, adds a continual learning stage for dynamic user feedback, and incorporates multimodal preference data via an LLM-based PENet (built on LLaVA++). It departs from prior PAA work conceptually by arguing, via the voting paradox, that collective GAA preferences violate transitivity and thus introduce conflicting priors into personalized models. Technically, it introduces 3D physique analysis (SMPL-X mesh + skeleton keypoints via GCN) as a new feature extraction pathway, building on human-centric visual analysis (TANet) and aesthetic assessment architectures (NIMA, MaxViT, Q-Align, EAT) as baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale PhysiqueAA dataset (50K images) and benchmark, opening a new sub-domain at the intersection of aesthetics, body perception, and health. The PAA+ paradigm's continual learning stage and multimodal feedback integration address a genuine gap in how personalized models adapt over time, though the validation is limited to three users and 400 feedback images, leaving scalability and generalizability open. The voting-paradox argument against GAA pre-training is conceptually compelling but empirically supported only by modest margins on a small user set. Future work must validate the paradigm across larger user populations, diverse domains beyond physique, and longer feedback horizons to establish whether the three-stage structure yields consistent gains.
