## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing datasets rely on synthetically generated target images (via Prompt-to-Prompt, DALL-E 2, etc.), which introduces artifacts and limits models to high-level edits like style transfer or object replacement. This makes it difficult for trained models to perform complex, structure-altering edits such as non-rigid pose changes, viewpoint adjustments, and element rearrangements while preserving the identity and appearance of the source image.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 512×512 source image, a natural-language editing instruction (e.g., 'Change the view to the side'), and optionally a binary mask or a ControlNet spatial map (skeleton pose, sketch) as input, producing a single edited image at the same resolution. The source image is encoded to a VAE latent and concatenated with the noisy target latent along the width dimension (doubling the spatial width), rather than along the channel dimension as in prior work. Inference uses DDIM sampling with 50 steps on the fine-tuned Stable Diffusion V1.5 U-Net.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part pipeline: (1) a dataset construction method that samples 6M real frame pairs from internet videos (filtered by RAFT optical flow for moderate motion and background consistency) and uses MLLMs (GPT-4o, Pixtral-12B) to generate precise editing instructions by directly comparing source and target frames; and (2) a Spatial Conditioning strategy that concatenates the source image latent with the noisy target latent along the width dimension instead of the channel dimension, allowing the denoising U-Net to attend to any region of the source via cross-attention at every layer. This avoids the spatial alignment constraint of channel conditioning, enabling structural changes (pose, viewpoint, repositioning) while the network retains full access to source features for identity preservation. The approach requires no architectural modifications to the pretrained T2I model, so it seamlessly integrates with mask-based inpainting and ControlNet/T2I-Adapter for precise localized edits.

## Results

On the InstructMove benchmark (50 images with human-curated non-rigid editing instructions), InstructMove achieves the highest instruction-alignment scores: CLIP-D of 0.1361 and CLIP-Inst of 0.8724, outperforming the next-best methods (MagicBrush at 0.0972 and 0.8648 respectively). Content preservation (CLIP-I) is competitive at 0.9275, slightly below InstructPix2Pix (0.9380) and MagicBrush (0.9318), which the authors attribute to those methods failing to actually perform non-rigid edits and leaving the source image largely unchanged. In a human preference study with 40 participants, InstructMove is selected as the best result in 87.62% of cases, compared to 5.0% for Imagic, 4.13% for MagicBrush, and 3.25% for InstructPix2Pix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructMove outperforms all six baselines on both instruction-alignment metrics (CLIP-D and CLIP-Inst). Against the zero-shot description-based methods (NullTextInversion, MasaCtrl, Imagic), the gains are substantial: CLIP-D improves from 0.0436–0.0660 to 0.1361, and CLIP-Inst from 0.7648–0.8645 to 0.8724. Against the instruction-based methods (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, UltraEdit), InstructMove leads on CLIP-D by 0.039–0.054 and on CLIP-Inst by 0.008–0.015. The only metric where InstructMove trails is CLIP-I (content preservation): InstructPix2Pix scores 0.9380 and MagicBrush 0.9318 versus 0.9275 for InstructMove, but the authors argue this is misleading because those methods largely fail to execute non-rigid edits and thus leave the source image nearly unchanged. The human preference study confirms the quantitative picture: InstructMove is preferred in 87.62% of comparisons, while the four competing methods collectively account for only 12.38%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-based image editing line initiated by InstructPix2Pix, which fine-tuned Stable Diffusion on synthetically generated source-target pairs created via Prompt-to-Prompt. The key departure is replacing synthetic targets with real video frame pairs and using multimodal LLMs (GPT-4o, Pixtral-12B) to generate instructions directly from observed frame differences, rather than relying on text-only LLMs to imagine edits. Architecturally, the paper introduces Spatial Conditioning—concatenating the reference latent along the width dimension instead of the channel dimension—which is a minimal change that decouples spatial alignment between source and target, enabling structural edits while preserving identity. This design also preserves compatibility with ControlNet and T2I-Adapter, unlike prior instruction-based models that modify the input channel layout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental data bottleneck in instruction-based editing: prior large-scale datasets (InstructPix2Pix, EmuEdit, UltraEdit) all use synthetically generated targets, which limits models to high-level edits and causes identity drift on complex structural changes. By sourcing 6M real frame pairs from video, the approach naturally captures non-rigid motion, pose changes, and viewpoint shifts that are difficult to synthesize convincingly. However, the method is limited to realistic transformations and cannot handle artistic edits (style transfer, object replacement), and its quality is bounded by MLLM instruction accuracy and the filtering pipeline. An open question is whether combining this dataset with existing synthetic-editing datasets can yield a truly general-purpose editor, and whether the spatial conditioning trick transfers to newer diffusion backbones beyond SD 1.5.
