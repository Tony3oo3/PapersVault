## Problem

Existing real-time semantic segmentation models face two limitations: multi-path blocks (residual connections, Conv-Former blocks) increase memory access frequency and degrade inference speed, and some architectures (e.g., SCTNet) depend on high-performance external teacher models during training, raising costs and requiring pre-trained weights on specific datasets. No prior work simultaneously addresses both the speed bottleneck of multi-path inference and the training dependency on external teachers within a unified framework.

## Input / Output

GCNet consumes standard RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (1024×2048 for Cityscapes, 720×960 for CamVid, 512×2048 for Pascal VOC 2012) and outputs a per-pixel class label map (19 classes for Cityscapes, 12 for CamVid, 21 for VOC). A notable operational detail is that the model exists in two forms: an enlarged multi-convolution, multi-path version used during training and a contracted single-3×3-convolution version used at inference, with identical output quality in both. Training on Cityscapes is performed from scratch without ImageNet pre-training, while CamVid and VOC use fine-tuning from Cityscapes weights.

## Method

The core unit is the GCBlock, which during training comprises parallel paths—N copies of Path_{3×3,1×1}, one Path_{1×1,1×1}, and a Path_residual—stacked vertically with multiple convolutions per path. At inference, all paths are reparameterized into a single 3×3 convolution by merging Conv-BN layers, fusing sequential 3×3 and 1×1 convolutions via matrix multiplication, converting 1×1 convolutions to 3×3 (zero-padding surrounding positions), and summing the resulting parallel weights. The full GCNet is a two-branch architecture (semantic branch for deep features, detail branch for spatial information) with bilateral fusion via channel compression/expansion and bilinear interpolation, plus a pyramid pooling module before the segmentation head. The key insight is that the self-enlarged training form acts as its own 'teacher' while the self-contracted inference form acts as its own 'student', eliminating the need for any external teacher model. Deep supervision with an auxiliary head (weight α=0.4) and OHEM cross-entropy loss are used during training.

## Results

On the Cityscapes validation set (1024×2048, A100 GPU, no ImageNet pre-training), GCNet-S achieves 77.3% mIoU at 193.3 FPS with 9.2M parameters, while GCNet-M reaches 79.0% mIoU at 105.0 FPS and GCNet-L attains 79.6% mIoU at 88.0 FPS. On CamVid (720×960), GCNet-L achieves 79.1% mIoU at 170.4 FPS, and on Pascal VOC 2012 (512×2048), GCNet-L reaches 58.8% mIoU at 145.1 FPS. Notably, GCNet-S outperforms PIDNet-S by 0.9% mIoU while running 64.6 FPS faster on Cityscapes, and GCNet-M surpasses both PIDNet-M and PIDNet-L in both mIoU and speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCNet-S (77.3% mIoU, 193.3 FPS) outperforms PIDNet-S (76.4%, 128.7 FPS) by 0.9% mIoU and 64.6 FPS on Cityscapes, and beats DDRNet-23-Slim (76.3%, 166.4 FPS) on both metrics. GCNet-M (79.0%, 105.0 FPS) surpasses PIDNet-M (78.2%, 78.2 FPS) and PIDNet-L (78.8%, 64.2 FPS) in both mIoU and speed, though it is slightly slower than SCTNet-B-Seg100 (79.0%, 117.0 FPS) at equal mIoU. GCNet-L (79.6%, 88.0 FPS) achieves the highest mIoU among all compared models on Cityscapes, exceeding SCTNet-B-Seg100 by 0.6% mIoU. On CamVid, GCNet-L (79.1%, 170.4 FPS) outperforms PIDNet-L (78.7%, 108.4 FPS) on both metrics, while GCNet-S trails PIDNet-S by 0.6% mIoU but leads by 67.8 FPS. On Pascal VOC 2012, GCNet-L (58.8%, 145.1 FPS) edges out PIDNet-L (58.2%, 104.0 FPS) and matches PIDNet-M (57.5%, 123.1 FPS) with a clear speed advantage. The paper notes that despite higher parameter counts and GFLOPs than some competitors, GCNet's single-path inference structure reduces memory access frequency, yielding faster actual throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCNet adapts the reparameterization paradigm from RepVGG and related classification work (Ding et al., 2019–2021) to the semantic segmentation domain for the first time, converting multi-convolution, multi-path training blocks into a single 3×3 convolution at inference. Architecturally, it builds on the two-branch (semantic + detail) design popularized by DDRNet and BiSeNet, but replaces their residual or Conv-Former blocks with the proposed GCBlock to eliminate the memory-access overhead of multi-path inference. A key departure from prior real-time segmentation models like SCTNet is the elimination of any external teacher model: GCNet's self-enlarged training configuration serves as its own teacher, removing the dependency on pre-trained transformer weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a competitive speed–accuracy trade-off (77.3% mIoU at 193.3 FPS on Cityscapes without ImageNet pre-training) that avoids the training-cost overhead of distillation-based approaches. However, the paper acknowledges that GCNet's parameter count and GFLOPs remain higher than lighter competitors, limiting deployment on storage-constrained edge devices. An open question is whether more parameter-efficient reparameterizable structures can close this gap while preserving the single-path inference advantage. The observation that memory-access frequency, rather than raw FLOPs, is the dominant speed bottleneck for multi-path blocks offers a useful design principle for future real-time architectures.
