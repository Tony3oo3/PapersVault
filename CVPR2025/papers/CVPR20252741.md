## Problem

State-of-the-art semantic occupancy ground truth for autonomous driving is generated from LiDAR using simple heuristics that cannot represent uncertainty from unobserved spaces or conflicting measurements, and prior evidential mapping work (Kälble et al., CVPR 2024) was limited to binary occupancy without semantic classes. This paper addresses both gaps by extending evidence theory to multi-class semantic occupancy and showing that the resulting higher-quality supervision substantially improves downstream camera-based occupancy prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252741/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For ground-truth generation, the method consumes multi-frame LiDAR point clouds with partially annotated semantic labels (17 classes on nuScenes) and produces a Cartesian voxel grid where each voxel carries a belief mass vector over C+3 hypotheses: free, each of the C semantic classes, a class-agnostic 'occupied' set, and total uncertainty Ω. For prediction, the model takes surround-view RGB camera images as input and outputs the same per-voxel BBA structure; a discrete class label is recovered only at evaluation time via a relative-entropy decision rule. The voxel resolutions tested are 0.4 m and 0.2 m.

## Method

The framework has two stages. In the GT stage, per-class spherical reflection maps and a single transmission map are built from LiDAR, normalized by voxel volume, and aggregated across frames with motion compensation. A novel BBA formulation computes each hypothesis's belief as an exponential function of its supporting and conflicting measurements (scaled by sensor-dependent hyperparameters α_t and α_r), then inverts a subset-incidence matrix to recover belief masses. In the prediction stage, BEVDet4D-Occ-R50 is used unchanged architecturally, but its final softmax layer is reinterpreted as a BBA and trained with a differentiable BBA cross-entropy loss (Dezert & Dambreville, 2023) augmented by per-voxel inverse-frequency weights to counteract the extreme imbalance toward the total-uncertainty hypothesis. Training starts from an Occ3D-pretrained checkpoint and continues for 48 epochs with the new evidential supervision, requiring no depth auxiliary loss.

## Results

On nuScenes, EvOcc's evidential semantic occupancy GT achieves 90.1% δ<1.25 depth accuracy and 67.0% ray-based mIoU at 0.4 m voxel resolution, outperforming Occ3D (81.5% / 51.2%) by 8.6 and 15.8 percentage points respectively. For the trained BEVDet4D-Occ model, EvOcc supervision yields 88.6% δ<1.25 and 48.6% mIoU versus 58.8% / 43.1% when trained on Occ3D data — a 29.8-point gain in depth accuracy and 5.5 points in semantic mIoU. At 0.2 m resolution, EvOcc GT reaches 93.8% δ<1.25 and 74.4% mIoU, again surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvOcc outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against Surround Occ [35] and Scene as Occupancy [32] (both at 0.5 m), EvOcc improves δ<1.25 by 33.9 and 43.4 points respectively, and mIoU by 25.3 and 24.0 points. Against Occ3D [31] at 0.4 m, EvOcc GT gains 8.6 points in δ<1.25 and 15.8 points in mIoU; the trained model gains 29.8 points in δ<1.25 and 5.5 points in mIoU over the Occ3D-supervised baseline. Against Kälble et al. [15] (binary occupancy only, no semantic metrics), EvOcc improves δ<1.25 from 87.9% to 90.1% and RMSE from 3.42 m to 2.98 m at 0.4 m. At 0.2 m, EvOcc beats OpenOccupancy [34] by 23.2 points in δ<1.25 and 20.3 points in mIoU, and also surpasses Kälble et al. [15] by 1.2 points in δ<1.25.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the evidential occupancy mapping framework of Kälble et al. (CVPR 2024), which modeled only binary free/occupied states from LiDAR, to the multi-class semantic setting by introducing a novel BBA formulation that handles partially annotated LiDAR measurements. For the prediction stage, it repurposes the off-the-shelf BEVDet4D-Occ architecture without any structural changes, instead adopting the differentiable BBA cross-entropy loss from Dezert and Dambreville (FUSION 2023) as a direct supervision signal. This positions the work as a data-quality and loss-design contribution rather than an architectural one, departing from the dominant trend in the occupancy prediction literature (Occ3D, SurroundOcc, Scene as Occupancy) that focuses on network design or heuristic GT refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical finding—that swapping only the ground-truth generation pipeline and loss while keeping the architecture fixed yields a 5.5% mIoU gain and 15.8% GT improvement—suggests that training-data quality has been an underappreciated bottleneck in occupancy prediction. The uncertainty-aware BBA representation also provides a principled mechanism for handling the inherent noise and partial annotation in real LiDAR data, which heuristic ray-casting pipelines cannot capture. Open questions include whether the approach scales to datasets with many more classes (the power-set approximation becomes tighter), whether the predicted uncertainties are well-calibrated for downstream safety-critical decision-making, and whether the method can be adapted to settings where no LiDAR is available for GT generation.
