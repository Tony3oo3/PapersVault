## Problem

Existing light-field microscopy (LFM) reconstruction methods either average sensor noise across all views (producing artifacts under low-light conditions) or require clean ground-truth 3D volumes for supervised training that are impractical to obtain in live-cell imaging. No prior framework simultaneously denoises and reconstructs 3D volumes from noisy light field images in an unsupervised manner without sacrificing temporal or spatial resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250171/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 13 noisy light field images (each 512×512) captured by a 2pSAM angular-scanning system and produces a 312×512×39 3D intensity volume. The 13 views are partitioned into two non-overlapping subsets per iteration, with one subset serving as network input and the other as self-supervision target. No clean reference volume is required at training time; the only external signal is the physical PSF model of the imaging system.

## Method

V2V3D splits all U views into two disjoint subsets and runs two weight-shared U-Net branches, each reconstructing a 3D volume from its subset and forward-projecting (via the wave-optics PSF model) to simulate the other subset's views, creating coordinate-matched, noise-independent pairs that satisfy Noise2Noise assumptions. A novel wave-optics-based feature alignment module back-projects per-view feature maps into a unified 3D space using PSF-derived convolution kernels (centroids set to 1, all else 0) to warp spatially misaligned features into alignment without the blurring that a full PSF inverse would introduce. Training minimizes a composite loss of MSE, FFT loss (to preserve high-frequency details in the frequency domain), and a de-crosstalk loss that penalizes reconstructed intensities falling below the sensor background noise level. The final 3D volume is the average of the two branch outputs, and the entire pipeline runs in ~0.4 s on an A100 GPU.

## Results

On the synthetic LFM dataset (11 test scenes, 13 views each), V2V3D achieves an average PSNR of 39.05 dB and SSIM of 0.898, outperforming all baselines. In terms of efficiency, V2V3D reconstructs a volume in 0.413 s on an A100 GPU, which is ~18× faster than RLD (7.34 s) and ~149× faster than DINER (61.4 s), though ~9× slower than VCDNet (0.047 s). The method also generalizes to real-world low-SNR data (brain-slice and live neutrophil samples) and to macro-scale light-field scenes (LLFF Fern), where it outperforms both pre-trained and fine-tuned IBRNet under severe Gaussian noise (σ=50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V2V3D outperforms all five baselines on average PSNR and SSIM across the 11-scene synthetic benchmark. Against the supervised VCDNet (PSNR 37.71, SSIM 0.792), V2V3D gains +1.34 dB PSNR and +0.106 SSIM while operating without any clean ground-truth supervision, though VCDNet is ~9× faster (0.047 s vs 0.413 s) and uses fewer parameters (88 M vs 211 M). Against the unsupervised RLD (32.19 dB) and DINER (29.60 dB), V2V3D achieves a substantial margin of +6.86 dB and +9.45 dB respectively, and is 18× and 149× faster. The pre-denoised pipelines (DeepCAD+RLD: 35.41 dB; DeepCAD+DINER: 35.63 dB) close the gap but still trail V2V3D by 3.6–3.7 dB, and the paper notes that N2N-based pre-denoising introduces signal crosstalk artifacts in the final volumes. On the macro-scale LLFF Fern scene under severe noise (σ=50), V2V3D (adapted with IBRNet as the view-synthesis backbone) outperforms both the pre-trained IBRNet and an IBRNet fine-tuned on the Fern scene, demonstrating the generality of the view-to-view denoising strategy beyond microscopy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of light-field microscopy (LFM) 3D reconstruction and self-supervised denoising. It departs from prior LFM reconstruction methods (RLD, VCDNet, DINER) that either iteratively average noise across views or require clean ground-truth supervision, and from prior Noise2Noise (N2N) denoising methods that rely on coordinate-matched pixel pairs (adjacent frames or pixels) at the cost of temporal or spatial resolution. The key conceptual leap is repurposing the N2N principle for view-to-view (pixel-unmatched) pairs by using the physical PSF forward model to generate coordinate-matched supervision views from a disjoint subset, thereby unifying denoising and 3D reconstruction in a single unsupervised architecture. The wave-optics-based feature alignment module further builds on the PSF modeling tradition from Broxton et al. and Lu et al., but repurposes the PSF as a learned convolution kernel for warping misaligned features into a shared 3D space rather than for deconvolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

V2V3D addresses a practical bottleneck in live-cell LFM imaging: the need for high SNR under low-light conditions without sacrificing temporal resolution or requiring clean training data. The view2view framework offers a generalizable paradigm for joint denoising and reconstruction in any multi-view imaging modality where views are pixel-unmatched but share a common 3D source, and the released dataset of 1618 focal stacks provides a useful benchmark for the community. However, the method's dependence on an accurate PSF model limits its robustness to uncalibrated systems, and the simple averaging fusion strategy between the two branches is acknowledged as suboptimal. Open questions include whether PSF can be jointly optimized during training to reduce calibration sensitivity, and whether the view2view N2N principle transfers cleanly to other snapshot 3D imaging setups beyond LFM.
