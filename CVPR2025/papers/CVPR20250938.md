## Problem

Existing human-human motion generation methods either concatenate two individuals into a single sequence (single-person-based) or model them independently with cross-attention (separate modeling-based), both of which neglect the temporal and causal structure of interactions. This inadequate temporal modeling leads to sub-optimal generation quality for long-sequence motions and redundant model parameters, as the interaction mixing module must compensate for missing temporal context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250938/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text description of a two-person interaction, encoded by a frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14 model, and produces a two-person 3D motion sequence in a non-canonical representation comprising global joint positions, velocities, 6D local rotations, and binary foot-ground contact (512-dim motion embedding). Generation uses DDIM sampling with 50 timesteps and classifier-free guidance (coefficient 3.5), and the output is structured as two separate per-person motion sequences of length L frames each.

## Method

TIMotion introduces a temporal modeling stage that precedes the interaction mixing module, consisting of three components: (1) Causal Interactive Injection interleaves the two single-person sequences into a single alternating causal sequence, exploiting the fact that each person's current motion depends on both persons' prior motions; (2) Role-Evolving Scanning constructs a symmetric counterpart sequence with roles swapped, concatenates both along the channel dimension, and after the interaction mixing module splits and merges the outputs to handle dynamic active/passive role transitions without text preprocessing; (3) Localized Pattern Amplification applies 1D convolutions (kernel sizes 3 and 1) with AdaLN to capture short-term per-person motion patterns, reducing high-frequency components for smoother output. The temporal modeling output is fed into a pluggable interaction mixing backbone (Transformer, Mamba, or RWKV), and the entire pipeline is trained as a diffusion denoiser with a composite loss including diffusion, foot contact, joint velocity, bone length, joint distance map, and relative orientation terms.

## Results

On the InterHuman test set, TIMotion+RWKV achieves a new state-of-the-art FID of 4.702 and Top-1 R-Precision of 0.501, outperforming the previous best method InterGen (FID 5.918, Top-1 R-Precision 0.371). TIMotion+Transformer also surpasses InterGen with FID 5.433 and Top-1 R-Precision 0.491, while using only 65M parameters versus InterGen's 182M and reducing inference time from 1.991s to 0.632s per sample. On the motion in-betweening editing task, TIMotion achieves FID 3.590 and Top-1 R-Precision 0.516, again outperforming InterGen (FID 4.700, Top-1 R-Precision 0.461).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIMotion decisively outperforms all six baselines on the InterHuman benchmark. Against the closest competitor InterGen (the previous SOTA), TIMotion+RWKV improves FID from 5.918 to 4.702 (−20.5%) and Top-1 R-Precision from 0.371 to 0.501 (+35%), while also reducing MM Dist from 5.108 to 3.769. The gap over RIG is even larger: FID drops from 6.775 to 4.702 and Top-1 R-Precision rises from 0.285 to 0.501. Single-person baselines (TEMOS, T2M, MDM) and the few-shot ComMDM are far behind, with FIDs ranging from 7.069 to 17.375 and Top-1 R-Precision below 0.24. Notably, TIMotion+Transformer uses only 65M parameters and 40.4 GFLOPs versus InterGen's 182M and 80.5 GFLOPs, achieving better quality at roughly 3× lower inference cost (0.632s vs 1.991s per sample). TIMotion also generalizes across interaction-mixing backbones (Transformer, Mamba, RWKV), consistently outperforming both single-person extension and separate-modeling baselines on each architecture (Table 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

TIMotion builds directly on InterGen's diffusion-based multi-human motion generation pipeline (same non-canonical motion representation, same loss suite, same CLIP text encoder) and on MotionDiffuse's AdaLN conditioning mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from the field's focus on interaction mixing (self-attention/cross-attention between two separately modeled individuals) by introducing a dedicated temporal modeling phase—Causal Interactive Injection—that interleaves two single-person sequences into a unified causal sequence before any interaction mixing occurs. The MetaMotion framework it proposes formalizes this two-phase decomposition, positioning prior methods as either single-person extensions (concatenation) or separate-modeling approaches that skip explicit temporal structure, and showing that the latter's interaction-mixing module alone is insufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: TIMotion achieves SOTA on InterHuman (FID 4.702, R-Precision Top-1 0.501) while using roughly 3× fewer parameters and 3× faster inference than InterGen, and it generalizes across Transformer, Mamba, and RWKV backbones, suggesting the temporal modeling insight is architecture-agnostic. The MetaMotion abstraction provides a useful diagnostic lens for the field, highlighting that temporal structure was being under-modeled relative to interaction mixing. Open questions include whether the causal interleaving strategy scales beyond two individuals, whether the approach transfers to real-time or streaming generation, and whether the gains persist on more diverse or longer interaction sequences beyond the current benchmarks.
