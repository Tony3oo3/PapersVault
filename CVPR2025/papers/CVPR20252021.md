## Problem

Existing 3D editing methods are either too slow for interactive use (SDS-based optimization takes minutes to hours), lack precise local control (text prompts alone cannot localize which regions to edit), or fail to preserve unedited regions identically. A fundamental challenge is that projecting a 3D object to 2D views makes the intended editing region ambiguous: masks are either too coarse (altering unintended areas) or too fine-grained (preventing reasonable editing), especially under occlusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252021/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh, a text prompt describing the desired edit, and rough 2D masks (one per view, 4 orthogonal views at 256×256) as input. The masks only need to approximately indicate target regions and do not require pixel-level precision. The output is a textured 3D mesh where only the semantically intended regions are modified while all other geometry and texture remain bit-identical to the input. The entire pipeline runs in ~74 seconds on a single GPU (24 s editing + 50 s merging), with the 3D reconstruction operating at 256³ voxel resolution with 40-dimensional features.

## Method

PrEditor3D is a training-free, three-stage pipeline. First, it performs synchronized multi-view editing by inverting 4 rendered views via DDPM inversion, then applying Prompt-to-Prompt on MVDream while blending edited and original latents with user masks at each denoising step (Eq. 1). Second, it resolves the 2D projection ambiguity by using Grounding DINO to localize the changing concept (identified by diffing original and edited prompts) within the user mask regions, followed by SAM 2 to produce precise 2D segmentations of the intended editing region in both original and edited views. Third, it lifts these segmentations to 3D by color-coding them (e.g., green) and reconstructing via GTR's triplane model, yielding 3D voxel masks; it then merges by nullifying the original region, pasting the edited voxel features, and applying linear interpolation (θ=0.5) in a dilated XOR boundary band to eliminate seams, producing a seamless edited mesh from the blended voxel features.

## Results

On directional CLIP metrics (scaled ×100), PrEditor3D achieves CLIPdir of 1.782, CLIPdir-cos of 7.679, and CLIPdir-avg-cos of 11.554, outperforming all three baselines across every variant. For shape preservation, CLIPdiff-noedit (lower is better) drops to 2.636 vs. 5.619 (Tailor3D), 4.118 (MVEdit), and 3.432 (Vox-E), confirming that unedited regions remain most consistent with the input. Total runtime is 74 seconds (24 s editing + 50 s merging) on a single GPU, compared to 6 min for MVEdit and 75 min for Vox-E. In a 3600-response user study, PrEditor3D is preferred over all baselines on prompt alignment, visual quality, and shape preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Tailor3D [32], PrEditor3D wins decisively on GPTEval3D (98–99% preference) and user study (96–99%), and leads on all directional CLIP metrics (e.g., CLIPdir-avg-cos 11.554 vs. 4.593); however, Tailor3D is faster at 26 s total runtime. Against MVEdit [8], PrEditor3D achieves clear wins on directional CLIP (CLIPdir 1.782 vs. 0.782; CLIPdiff-noedit 2.636 vs. 4.118) and user study (68–97% preference), while also being ~5× faster (74 s vs. 6 min). Against Vox-E [37], PrEditor3D leads on every directional CLIP metric (e.g., CLIPdir-cos 7.679 vs. 6.178; CLIPdiff-edit 8.812 vs. 10.528) and user study (78–89% preference), with a dramatic runtime advantage of 74 s versus 75 min. The GPTEval3D margins over MVEdit and Vox-E are narrower (53–68%) because that metric does not account for consistency with the input shape, which is precisely where PrEditor3D's 3D segmentation-and-merging pipeline provides its largest gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes three existing building blocks—Prompt-to-Prompt attention editing, DDPM inversion, and GTR's feed-forward multi-view-to-3D reconstruction—into a training-free 3D editing pipeline, departing from the dominant SDS-optimization paradigm (DreamFusion, Vox-E, ShapeEditor) that requires per-scene gradient descent. Its key conceptual departure is recognizing that 2D user masks are fundamentally ambiguous under 3D-to-2D projection (occlusion, depth), and addressing this with a Grounding DINO + SAM 2 segmentation stage whose results are lifted to 3D via a color-coding trick in GTR's triplane space, followed by a voxel-level average-blending merge. This positions the work as a practical, speed-oriented alternative to prior 3D editing methods that either lack precise local control (text-only prompts) or are too slow for interactive use (SDS loops).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~74-second end-to-end runtime (vs. ~75 minutes for Vox-E) makes 3D shape editing viable in iterative artistic workflows, and the 3D segmentation + merging strategy offers a reusable recipe for preserving unedited geometry in any multi-view-editing-to-3D pipeline. However, the method is constrained to object-centric assets renderable from four inward-facing views at 256×256 resolution, leaving large-scale scenes and high-fidelity geometry as open problems. A broader open question is whether the color-coding segmentation trick generalizes to more complex multi-region or hierarchical edits without manual prompt engineering for each concept.
