## Problem

Mask-based self-supervised video-language pretraining has shown that reconstructing image features outperforms video feature reconstruction, contradicting the expectation that temporal information should aid video understanding. The paper identifies that this gap stems from an inconsistency between masking strategies (designed for static images) and the spatio-temporal nature of the reconstruction target, causing the model's attention to misalign with the features it is asked to reconstruct.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251067/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video-text pairs where videos are tokenized into patch sequences via ViT-B/16 (T frames, each split into P1×P2 spatial tokens) and text is tokenized via BERT-base. A 30% masking ratio is applied to both video and image tokens, with Gumbel-Softmax enabling gradient flow through the discrete masking selection. The output is a pretrained video-language encoder-decoder pair, evaluated zero-shot and fine-tuned on retrieval, QA, and captioning benchmarks.

## Method

STM computes a spatial contribution score from the teacher model's [cls]-token attention over each frame's patches, and a temporal contribution score by feeding concatenated adjacent-frame tokens into a lightweight predictor that outputs 2D temporal point coordinates, from which distance-weighted heatmaps are generated. The final mask selects the top-r tokens by the sum of both scores, ensuring masked regions carry rich global spatio-temporal information. A text-guided spatio-temporal decoder re-runs the temporal predictor on partially visible tokens to produce an enhanced heatmap, multiplies it with visible tokens, and concatenates the result with text features plus invisible tokens before an MLP reconstructs the full video feature. Training combines four losses—video-text contrastive, video-text matching, masked visual modeling (L2 to ActionCLIP teacher features), and masked language modeling—enforcing consistency between what is masked and what is reconstructed.

## Results

On fine-tuned text-to-video retrieval, STM (5M pretraining pairs) achieves R@1 of 48.5% on MSRVTT, 56.9% on DiDeMo, and 53.6% on ActivityNet, outperforming the strongest 5M-pair baseline UMT by +2.2%, +2.1%, and +1.5% respectively. On video QA, STM reaches 96.9% on TGIF-Action, 99.0% on TGIF-Transition, 73.0% on TGIF-Frame, and 45.9% on MSRVTT-QA. For video captioning, STM attains CIDEr scores of 63.9 on MSRVTT and 145.4 on MSVD, setting new state-of-the-art among 5M-pair methods. In zero-shot retrieval, STM achieves 29.8% R@1 on MSRVTT and 38.7% on MSVD, surpassing UMT by 0.2% and 2.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 5M-pair methods, STM consistently outperforms all baselines on fine-tuned retrieval: it beats UMT (the strongest prior 5M method) by +2.2% R@1 on MSRVTT, +2.1% on DiDeMo, and +1.5% on ActivityNet, and surpasses HiTeA by +4.1% on MSRVTT R@1. On video QA, STM achieves the best TGIF-Action (96.9%) and TGIF-Frame (73.0%) scores among 5M-pair methods, and leads on MSRVTT-QA (45.9%) and ANet-QA (44.9%), though UMT-L (25M pairs) still dominates with 47.1% and 55.2% respectively. For captioning, STM's CIDEr of 63.9 on MSRVTT and 145.4 on MSVD edges out HiTeA (62.5/145.1) and LAVENDER (58.0/142.9). Against larger-data models, STM remains competitive: its 48.5% MSRVTT R@1 approaches UMT-L's 58.8% (25M pairs) and VAST's 63.9% (152M pairs) while using 5–30× less pretraining data. The paper also shows that the consistency between masking and reconstruction targets is critical: applying spatio-temporal masking with spatial-only reconstruction degrades performance (36.7% vs. 39.7% R@1), confirming that the joint design of STM masking and spatio-temporal decoder is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder paradigm for video-language pretraining established by VIOLETv2 (Fu et al., CVPR 2023) and UMT (Li et al., ICCV 2023), which introduced the four-objective training recipe (VTC, VTM, MVM, MLM) and unmasked teacher initialization, respectively. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that VIOLETv2's counterintuitive finding—that image feature reconstruction outperforms video feature reconstruction—stems from an inconsistency between spatially-oriented masking strategies and the spatio-temporal nature of the reconstruction target. By introducing a spatio-temporal masking strategy that explicitly aligns masked tokens with the video feature reconstruction objective, and a text-guided decoder that leverages adjacent-frame relationships, the paper positions itself as a targeted fix to a specific design flaw in prior mask-based video-language pretraining rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The masking-reconstruction consistency principle offers a useful diagnostic for practitioners designing mask-based pretraining objectives: the choice of what to mask should be coherent with what the model is asked to reconstruct. However, the practical gains are modest (1–2% over UMT on retrieval, ~1% on QA), the method depends on a frozen teacher model (ActionCLIP) for both masking scores and reconstruction targets, and all experiments are conducted at ViT-B/16 scale with 5M pairs. Open questions include whether the consistency principle holds at larger scales (ViT-L, 100M+ pairs), whether the lightweight predictor network for temporal point localization generalizes to longer or higher-resolution videos, and whether the approach transfers to audio-visual or 3D settings.
