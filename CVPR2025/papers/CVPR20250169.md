## Problem

Attention-based arbitrary style transfer methods suffer from three interrelated limitations: pointwise feature matching neglects holistic style representations, the softmax attention can over-concentrate on salient local style regions producing disharmonious repeated patterns, and the quadratic computational complexity of matrix multiplication makes high-resolution (≥1K) inference infeasible on consumer GPUs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250169/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image and a style image (both RGB) and produces a single stylized image. A frozen VGG-19 encoder (up to relu_4_1) extracts features, and a mirror decoder reconstructs the stylized output. Unlike attention-based baselines that hit GPU memory limits at 1024×1024 or 2048×2048, HSI processes images up to 2048×2048 within ~8 GB on a 24 GB 4090Ti, enabling real-time inference (~50 fps at 1K resolution).

## Method

HSI replaces the self-attention module with a structurally similar but linear-complexity transformation built on element-wise multiplication rather than matrix multiplication. It extracts four channel-wise global style statistics (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis) from the style feature and uses a dynamic filter network to convert them into per-statistic weights and biases for weighted summation. A dual-relation mechanism simultaneously computes local-content-to-global-style and global-content-to-global-style interactions, with a cosine-similarity-derived coefficient λ_g that dynamically up-weights the global relation when content and style are semantically aligned. The full network is trained end-to-end with a combination of VGG-based style loss, content loss (including a grayscale term), and a multiscale adversarial loss, achieving the best style loss, LPIPS, and FID among compared methods while maintaining competitive content preservation.

## Results

On the main quantitative evaluation (400 stylized images from 20 content × 20 style pairs), HSI achieves the best style loss (0.95), LPIPS (0.46), and FID (18.46) among all seven compared methods, while its content loss (0.62) is competitive—only slightly higher than StyA2K (0.59). In terms of efficiency, HSI is the only method besides AdaIN that successfully processes 2048×2048 images on a 24 GB GPU (8.12 GB memory, 0.15 s inference), whereas all attention-based baselines (SANet, AdaAttN, StyTr², StyA2K, AesPA-Net) run out of memory at that resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSI outperforms all six baselines on style loss, LPIPS, and FID simultaneously. Against the pure attention-based methods SANet and AdaAttN, HSI wins on all four metrics with substantial margins (e.g., style loss 0.95 vs. 1.26/1.52; FID 18.46 vs. 18.74/19.34). Compared to the transformer-based StyTr², HSI improves style loss by 0.39 (0.95 vs. 1.34) and FID by 0.51 (18.46 vs. 18.97), while StyTr² retains a slight content-loss edge (0.69 vs. 0.62). Against the most recent attention variant StyA2K, HSI achieves better style loss (0.95 vs. 1.21), LPIPS (0.46 vs. 0.49), and FID (18.46 vs. 19.85), though StyA2K has a marginally lower content loss (0.59 vs. 0.62). Versus AesPA-Net, HSI leads on every metric including content loss (0.62 vs. 0.63). In the efficiency comparison (Table 2), HSI is the only method that processes 2048×2044 images without OOM on a 24 GB 4090Ti, using 8.12 GB memory and 0.15 s inference, while SANet, AdaAttN, StyTr², StyA2K, and AesPA-Net all fail at that resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-based arbitrary style transfer lineage (SANet, AdaAttN, StyTr2, StyA2K) by identifying three specific failure modes of self-attention in this setting: local pattern repetition from point-wise matching, softmax-induced style bias, and quadratic memory scaling. It departs from this line by replacing the core matrix multiplication with element-wise multiplication and by injecting four global style statistics (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis) via a dynamic-filter-network-style weight/bias generator, drawing on the dynamic filter network literature. The dual-relation mechanism (local-content-to-global-style and global-content-to-global-style) is a novel architectural choice not present in prior AST modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: HSI enables real-time arbitrary style transfer at 2K resolution on a 24 GB consumer GPU, a regime where all attention-based baselines (SANet, AdaAttN, StyTr2, StyA2K, AesPA-Net) run out of memory. The plug-and-play nature of the module (demonstrated by swapping it into SANet) suggests broad applicability to other attention-based pipelines. Open questions include whether the global-statistics approach generalizes to video or 3D style transfer, whether the VGG-19 encoder bottleneck limits fidelity at very high resolutions, and whether the modest quality gains over the best baselines (e.g., FID 18.46 vs. 18.74) persist under more diverse style-content pairs or adversarial evaluation.
