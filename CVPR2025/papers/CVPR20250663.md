## Problem

Existing embodied multi-agent collaboration frameworks either lack long-term strategic planning (CoELA, where each agent decides independently) or rely on a single linear dialogue chain (RoCo, CaPo) that is vulnerable to LLM stochasticity and can produce suboptimal or conflicting plans. The paper addresses how to enable embodied agents to collaboratively explore multiple planning paths, evaluate them with meaningful rewards, and adapt plans during execution without disrupting ongoing agent actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250663/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives RGB-D frames processed by Mask-RCNN into 3D point clouds and object detections, combined with a fixed spatial layout (room graph with distances) and a natural-language task description specifying target objects and destinations. The system outputs a natural-language collaborative plan stored in a medium-term 'extended memory,' decomposed into per-agent executable actions (grasp, navigate, place), and a scalar plan-evaluation reward (1-5) that gates whether the plan is retained or re-searched. Plans are not regenerated at every step; they persist until the evaluation module signals the current plan is no longer viable.

## Method

CoTS replaces the single-dialogue planning chain with a modified Monte Carlo Tree Search in which one agent (planner) proposes collaborative plans and the other (critic) scores them using a non-simulated LLM-based reward function combining an allocation-coherence score and a spatial-movement-cost score, each on a 1-5 scale. The tree is traversed via UCT selection, expanded by sampling n new discussion paths per node, evaluated by the critic's reward, and updated through backpropagation of rewards along the root-to-leaf path. A separate plan evaluation module continuously monitors execution progress and assigns a viability score; only when this score falls below a threshold does the system trigger a new tree search, preventing the frequent plan revisions that disrupt agent actions in RoCo. Collaboration plans are cached in an 'extended memory' that bridges short-term and long-term storage, and the perception module augments CoELA's original design with explicit inter-room distance information to inform the cost component of the reward.

## Results

On TDW-MAT with GPT-4, CoTS achieves a 93% average transport rate with Oracle Perception (vs. 85% for CoELA, 87% for RoCo, 89% for CaPo) and 83% without Oracle Perception (vs. 71%, 72%, 74% respectively), representing relative improvements of 9.41% and 16.90% over CoELA. On C-WAH (GPT-4), CoTS completes tasks in 81 steps (Visual Obs) and 49 steps (Symbolic Obs), outperforming CoELA (92 and 57 steps) by 11.96% and 14.04% respectively. CoTS also outperforms the RL-based MAT baseline (15% average transport rate) and heuristic base agents (RHP: 50%, CoRHP: 75%) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTS outperforms all LLM-based baselines (CoELA, RoCo, CaPo) on both TDW-MAT settings when driven by GPT-4, with the largest gap appearing without Oracle Perception (83% vs. 71–74% average transport rate). Against the RL-based MAT baseline, CoTS achieves a 78-point absolute improvement in average transport rate (93% vs. 15%), reflecting MAT's difficulty with long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks. On C-WAH, CoTS reduces average steps by 11–14% over CoELA and by 9–10% over RoCo, confirming gains in execution efficiency. The heuristic base agents (RHP, CoRHP, MHP, CoMHP) are substantially outperformed, with CoTS achieving 93% vs. 50–75% on TDW-MAT and 49–81 steps vs. 75–141 on C-WAH. Notably, with GPT-3.5, CaPo slightly edges CoTS on some TDW-MAT metrics (e.g., 84% vs. 82% average with Oracle), attributed to GPT-3.5's weaker evaluation and scoring capabilities; however, with GPT-4, CoTS clearly surpasses CaPo across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoTS builds directly on CoELA's modular architecture (Perception, Memory, Communication, Planning, Execution) for LLM-driven embodied multi-agent collaboration, replacing CoELA's independent per-agent planning with a shared collaborative planning module. It departs from RoCo and CaPo, which generate multi-agent plans through single-chain dialogues, by introducing a modified Monte Carlo Tree Search that explores multiple planning paths with LLM-based (non-simulated) reward functions. The tree-search mechanism draws on multi-branch reasoning in LLMs (e.g., VermCTS, Graph CoT) but is the first to integrate MCTS with embodied agent collaboration, where physical movement costs make traditional simulation-based rewards impractical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plan evaluation module addresses a practical and underexplored problem in multi-agent systems: determining when a collaborative plan should be updated versus retained, avoiding both stale plans and disruptive over-revision. However, the framework is validated only in two simulated household environments with two agents, and its performance is tightly coupled to LLM reasoning quality (GPT-4 substantially outperforms GPT-3.5, and CaPo even edges out CoTS under GPT-3.5). Open questions include scaling to more than two agents, real-world robotic deployment, and the computational cost of iterative tree search in time-constrained settings.
