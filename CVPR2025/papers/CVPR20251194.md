## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks predominantly target event-level or story-level comprehension, leaving the foundational motion-level perception capability largely unexplored and under-evaluated. Current VLMs also face a fundamental tension: fine-grained motion understanding requires high frame rates, but LLM sequence length constraints force models to sample at extremely low rates (e.g., 0.2 fps for a 5-minute video), and existing video feature compression methods rely on shallow fusion that fails to capture higher-level inter-frame redundancies.

## Input / Output

MotionBench presents 5,385 video clips (primarily under 10 seconds, sourced from web videos, public datasets, and Unity-rendered synthetic scenes) paired with 8,052 four-option multiple-choice questions across six motion categories, yielding a single-letter answer. The TE Fusion model consumes 224×224-pixel frames (16 input frames) and produces a compressed token sequence of length N_input×l/k for the LLM decoder, where k is the compression ratio. The benchmark's annotation density reaches 68.4 words per second—roughly double that of existing video benchmarks—reflecting the fine-grained nature of motion descriptions.

## Method

The paper proposes Through-Encoder (TE) Fusion, a video feature compression paradigm that groups adjacent frames in sets of k and applies group-level self-attention throughout the entire visual encoder (ViT), enabling deep temporal dependency modeling before a final spatial-temporal compression operator is applied. This contrasts with existing Pre-Encoder and Post-Encoder fusion methods, which treat each frame independently during encoding and only fuse within a small compression module, limiting their ability to capture higher-level redundancies (e.g., consistent person identity across changing poses and camera angles). TE Fusion is implemented on the GLM-4V-9B backbone and evaluated at compression ratios k=2,4,8,16, showing near-zero performance degradation up to k=4 and under 4% average drop even at k=16. The paper also introduces MotionBench, the largest motion-level video benchmark, with a curation pipeline that uses GPT-4o-assisted QA generation for complex interaction videos, direct annotation for domain-specific videos, and automatic construction for synthetic Unity renderings, followed by filtering of questions answerable from a single frame.

## Results

On MotionBench, TE Fusion (GLM-4V-9B backbone, 16 frames, compression ratio 4) achieves 0.58 average accuracy on both the dev (4,020 QAs) and test (4,034 QAs) splits, matching the best existing VLM (Qwen2VL-72B at 0.57/0.58) while using a 9B LLM instead of 72B. All evaluated video VLMs score below 0.60, far above the random baseline of 0.25 but well below practical thresholds. In the compression ablation (Table 4), TE Fusion at k=4 achieves 51.0 on MotionBench, 72.1 on MVBench, and 61.0/47.3/42.1 on VideoMME (short/medium/long), outperforming the uncompressed 4-frame baseline (47.6/64.5/51.4/41.0/38.3) by up to 18.7% on VideoMME-short.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TE Fusion (9B backbone) achieves 0.58 Dev AVG, edging out Qwen2VL-72B (0.57) and InternVL-40B (0.55) while using a substantially smaller LLM. On individual categories, TE Fusion leads in Motion Recognition (0.64 vs. 0.58 for Qwen2VL-72B) and Repetition Count (0.39 vs. 0.31), but trails Qwen2VL-72B on Camera Motion (0.51 vs. 0.63) and Motion-related Objects (0.69 vs. 0.72). In the controlled compression ablation (Table 4, same GLM-4V-9B backbone), TE Fusion at k=4 outperforms PLLaVA (51.0 vs. 50.5 on MotionBench; 72.1 vs. 70.2 on MVBench), Kangaroo (72.1 vs. 69.8 on MVBench), Qwen2-VL (72.1 vs. 65.6 on MVBench), and QFormer (72.1 vs. 63.8 on MVBench), winning 9 of 10 metrics. QFormer is the weakest compression method, occasionally falling below the uncompressed baseline, while Qwen2-VL's pre-encoder fusion shows diminishing returns at k=4. The text-only GPT-4o baseline (0.33) confirms that even strong LLMs cannot infer fine-grained motion from question text alone, and all video VLMs improve by only 0.05–0.25 over this text-only floor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of prior work. On the benchmark side, it extends the video understanding evaluation landscape (MVBench, VideoMME, LVBench) by identifying that motion-level perception—a foundational granularity—has been systematically underrepresented, with existing benchmarks dedicating less than 30% of questions to motion content. On the architecture side, it builds on the established taxonomy of video feature compression (Pre-Encoder Fusion à la Qwen2-VL, Post-Encoder Fusion via QFormer/PLLaVA/Kangaroo) but departs by identifying their shared limitation of 'shallow fusion' and proposing Through-Encoder Fusion, which introduces group-level self-attention across adjacent frames within the visual encoder before applying the compression operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest SOTA video VLMs (Qwen2-VL-72B) score below 60% on motion-level tasks, exposing a critical gap between current capabilities and practical requirements for applications like anomaly detection and action analysis. The finding that TE Fusion at compression ratio k=4 outperforms k=2 in 3 of 4 datasets challenges the implicit assumption that more tokens in the LLM decoder are always beneficial, suggesting that shifting computational budget toward the visual encoder may be a productive direction. However, even with TE Fusion, substantial room for improvement remains, and the fundamental limitation of motion comprehension in VLMs is not resolved—only partially mitigated—leaving open the question of whether architectural changes alone suffice or whether training data and pretraining objectives must also be rethought.
