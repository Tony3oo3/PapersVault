## Problem

Generating diverse posterior samples for inverse problems using pre-trained generative models is computationally expensive because existing methods like DPS require restarting the full denoising chain for every new sample. This paper addresses the gap by enabling progressive, incremental exploration of the posterior without re-running the entire generative process, drastically reducing the per-sample computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252737/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y (produced by a forward operator A applied to a clean image plus additive Gaussian noise), the operator A itself (linear: mask inpainting, 4×/8× super-resolution, Gaussian deblurring; or nonlinear: neural deblurring, phase retrieval, HDR reconstruction), and a pre-trained consistency model providing a deterministic one-to-one noise-to-data mapping Φ. It outputs N diverse posterior image samples (e.g., 256×256 on LSUN-Bedroom or 64×64 on ImageNet) generated progressively via Euler-Maruyama steps, where each step yields a new sample at a cost of only η NFEs (η=1 or 2 for one- or two-step CMs) rather than a full denoising run.

## Method

The core idea is to simulate Langevin dynamics directly in the noise space of a pre-trained consistency model rather than in data space. The SDE dx₁ = −(x₁ + ∇_{x₁} L_y(Φ(x₁)))dt + √2 dW_t has the model posterior in noise space as its equilibrium distribution, and is discretized with Euler-Maruyama to produce a sequence of posterior samples. A warm-start phase (K Adam steps minimizing L_y with respect to the noise variable) initializes the SDE near the posterior mode. Theoretically, the TV distance between the true and model posteriors is bounded by 2κ_y ε, where ε is the prior approximation error and κ_y is a problem-dependent condition number. Because each EM step advances the noise variable incrementally, generating N samples costs only η·(K+N) NFEs total, with per-sample cost asymptotically approaching η, in contrast to DPS which requires a full ODE solve per sample.

## Results

On LSUN-Bedroom (256×256) linear tasks, the 1-step method achieves PSNR of 20.4 (8× SR), 22.4 (Gaussian Deblur), and 23.8 (10% Inpainting), matching or exceeding DPS-DM and LGD-DM on most fidelity metrics while using only 1–2 NFEs per sample. Against CM-based baselines (DPS-CM, LGD-CM), the proposed method improves PSNR by roughly 10 dB (e.g., 20.4 vs. 10.7 on 8× SR). On diversity, the 1-step method achieves a Diversity Score of 3.01–3.26 across tasks, substantially outperforming DPS-DM (2.10–2.42) and LGD-DM (2.11–2.36), confirming that noise-space Langevin dynamics yields semantically varied posterior samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest DM baselines (DPS-DM, LGD-DM, MPGD-DM), the proposed 1-step method is competitive on reconstruction fidelity: it matches DPS-DM on 8× SR PSNR (20.4 vs. 20.4) and slightly exceeds it on Gaussian Deblur PSNR (22.4 vs. 22.1), while LGD-DM retains an edge on 10% Inpainting (24.7 vs. 23.8 PSNR) and HDR reconstruction. Against CM-based baselines (DPS-CM, LGD-CM, CMEdit), the gap is dramatic—PSNR improves by ~10 dB and FID drops by 200+ points, confirming that the noise-space Langevin framework is far more effective than naively porting DM guidance to a CM backbone. On the paper's headline diversity metric (Diversity Score), the 1-step method leads all baselines on every task (e.g., 3.26 vs. 2.10 for DPS-DM on Gaussian Deblur; 3.15 vs. 2.33 for DPS-DM on 10% Inpainting), while CLIP Cosine Similarity is comparable or slightly lower, indicating genuinely different semantic content rather than mere pixel-level variation. The 2-step variant trades a small amount of fidelity for slightly lower diversity, suggesting the 1-step setting is the sweet spot for diverse posterior exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the deterministic noise-to-data mapping provided by consistency models (CMs) [51] and repurposes Langevin dynamics—previously explored in noise spaces for energy-based models [56, 57] and HMC [25, 40, 55]—for posterior sampling in inverse problems. It departs from diffusion-based posterior samplers (DPS [12], LGD [49], MPGD [21]) that require a full denoising chain restart for each new sample, instead enabling progressive accumulation of diverse posterior samples through a single ongoing SDE simulation in noise space. The theoretical framework connects to flow-based generative model guarantees [7, 8, 33] by bounding posterior TV error via prior approximation error, while the algorithmic design draws on noise-space optimization strategies from GAN and flow literature [1, 2, 16, 41].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that generating N diverse posterior samples costs only O(N) additional NFEs (asymptotically ~1 per sample with 1-step CMs) rather than O(N × full-chain-length) as in DPS, making diverse posterior exploration feasible at scale. The TV guarantee (Theorem 4.1) with its condition number κ_y provides a principled understanding of when posterior sampling is intrinsically hard, independent of the specific generative model. A primary limitation is that CMs approximate the data prior less faithfully than full diffusion models, resulting in lower per-sample fidelity; closing this gap while retaining the efficiency and diversity advantages remains an open question, potentially addressable through better prior models or adaptive SDE simulation.
