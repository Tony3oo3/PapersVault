## Problem

Existing gaze-informed human motion prediction methods (e.g., SIF3D) treat gaze purely as a spatial directional cue indicating where a person will move, but overlook the semantic content of gaze—specifically the affordances of the object being observed. This omission leads to physically implausible predictions where human-object interactions lack semantic consistency, such as generating generic poses near a chair without producing a sitting or reaching motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250444/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs: an observed motion sequence of L frames (each containing global translation in R³, orientation in SO(3), and a 32-dim SMPL-X body pose embedding), a 3D scene point cloud (downsampled to 8192 points from ~500K vertices per scene), and a gaze sequence of L 3D intersection coordinates where the gaze ray meets the scene. The output is a future motion sequence of ΔL frames with per-frame translation, orientation, and body pose, reconstructed through SMPL-X and refined by VPoser into a continuous mesh-level motion.

## Method

GAP3DS introduces three coupled components. First, a Gaze-guided Affordance Learner (GAL) computes a gaze-to-scene distance matrix, aggregates it via a 1D CNN into an interaction map, parses the scene into objects with SoftGroup, selects the highest-scoring object as the gaze target, and extracts visual and textual affordance features via AffordanceNet, yielding an affordance triplet (spatial location, visual shape, textual semantics). Second, an Affordance-aware Pose Generator (APG) uses a motion diffusion model with a dual-layer transformer decoder, conditioned on the visual and textual affordance features, to iteratively denoise and generate w interaction poses near the prediction horizon. Third, a Dual-Prompted Motion Decoder (DPM) feeds the spatial location component into a trajectory decoder for global translation/orientation and the generated interaction poses into a pose refiner, then assembles the final motion through SMPL-X and VPoser, ensuring both trajectory coherence and interaction-specific pose accuracy.

## Results

On the GIMO dataset, GAP3DS achieves the best performance across all metrics: 575 mm trajectory deviation (path), 623 mm (interaction), 141.2 mm MPJPE (path), 171.4 mm MPJPE (interaction), and 0.95% penetration error. Compared to the state-of-the-art SIF3D, GAP3DS reduces trajectory deviation by 15 mm (path) and 43 mm (interaction), lowers MPJPE by 15.4 mm (path) and 24.3 mm (interaction), and cuts penetration error from 4.59% to 0.95%. The gains are most pronounced in interaction-specific metrics, confirming that gaze-informed affordance learning primarily improves human-object interaction accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAP3DS outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against the strongest baseline SIF3D (CVPR 2024), GAP3DS reduces trajectory deviation by 15 mm (path) and 43 mm (interaction), lowers MPJPE by 15.4 mm (path) and 24.3 mm (interaction), and dramatically cuts penetration error from 4.59% to 0.95%—a nearly 5× reduction in physical collisions. The scene-independent AuxFormer is the weakest baseline, with 113 mm higher trajectory deviation and 32.3 mm higher MPJPE (path) than GAP3DS, confirming the importance of scene context. BiFU and MDP occupy the middle range, with MDP slightly ahead of BiFU on trajectory but behind on penetration error. Notably, adding GAL and APG to SIF3D (SIF3D + GAL+APG) already yields substantial gains (e.g., penetration drops from 4.59% to 3.24%), but GAP3DS's full architecture with its dual-prompted decoder delivers further improvements, particularly in interaction-specific pose accuracy (171.4 vs. 186.1 mm MPJPE-interact).

## Relation to Prior Work

GAP3DS extends the SIF3D (CVPR 2024) framework, which already integrates 3D scene point clouds and human gaze for motion prediction, by reinterpreting gaze from a mere directional cue into a semantic signal that identifies target objects and their affordances. The pose generation component draws on the motion diffusion model paradigm (Tevet et al., 2022), while the scene parsing and affordance extraction rely on off-the-shelf tools (SoftGroup, AffordanceNet) from the 3D scene understanding and affordance detection literatures. Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously separate threads—gaze-informed trajectory prediction and affordance-guided interaction synthesis—into a unified prediction pipeline, departing from prior scene-aware HMP methods (BiFU, MDP) that model human-scene spatial relationships without capturing the specific interaction a person intends.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete failure mode in gaze-based HMP: predicted poses that are trajectory-accurate but semantically incoherent with the object a person is looking at, leading to physically implausible interactions. The substantial reduction in penetration error (from ~4.5% to 0.95%) and the consistent gains when GAL/APG are grafted onto diverse baselines suggest that affordance conditioning is a broadly useful inductive bias for scene-aware motion prediction. Limitations include dependence on pre-segmented 3D scenes and off-the-shelf affordance networks, evaluation on only two datasets (GIMO, GTA-IM), and the assumption that gaze reliably indicates interaction intent rather than passive observation. Open questions include generalization to outdoor or dynamic scenes, handling of gaze drift over longer horizons, and whether learned affordance representations could replace the fixed AffordanceNet pipeline.
