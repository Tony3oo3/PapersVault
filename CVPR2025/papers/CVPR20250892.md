## Problem

Object detection models are vulnerable to diverse backdoor attacks (ghost objects, vanishing objects, global misclassification) that existing test-time detection methods cannot handle. The only prior black-box TTSD for OD (Detector Cleanse) requires full knowledge of the attack, while classification-oriented methods fail due to OD's multi-object output and dual-branch architecture. This paper asks whether semantic-aware transformations (distinguishing foreground from background) can expose poisoned samples more effectively than indiscriminate pixel-level modifications.

## Input / Output

TRACE takes a single test image, a set of publicly available background images (e.g., from the Background Challenge dataset), and a small foreground NBO patch (e.g., a stop sign) as auxiliary inputs. It produces a continuous anomaly score (the TRACE value) and a binary poisoned/clean decision via a threshold γ. The method is strictly black-box: it only queries the backdoored model's final predictions and requires no access to model weights, training data, or attack specifications.

## Method

TRACE exploits two anomalous consistency properties of backdoored detectors. First, Contextual Transformation Consistency (CTC) blends diverse backgrounds onto the test image at low opacity and measures the variance of detection confidences; trigger objects exhibit anomalously low variance because shortcut learning makes their recognition context-independent, while clean objects fluctuate. Second, Focal Transformation Consistency (FTC) slides a position-invariant NBO (e.g., stop sign) across the image via Monte Carlo sampling and measures confidence oscillation plus newly-emerged detections; FN-inducing triggers create an 'Island Effect' where the NBO's confidence drops sharply near the trigger. An SSIM-based post-processing filter removes Natural Backdoor Objects (uniform benign objects like stop signs that also show low CTC). The final score combines both signals as Trace(x) = σ(Δ_F^Var) − σ(Δ_B^Var), mapping inputs to a linearly separable space where a single threshold separates poisoned from clean samples.

## Results

Across 42 backdoored models (3 datasets × 3 detectors × 7 attacks), TRACE achieves an average F1 of 0.880±0.040 and AUROC of 0.897±0.041, demonstrating strong consistency. On MS-COCO with YOLOv5, TRACE reaches 0.877 F1 / 0.900 AUROC on average, compared to 0.665/0.657 for the prior SOTA Detector Cleanse. The best individual results include 0.937 F1 / 0.913 AUROC (OGA, YOLO/MS-COCO) and 0.968 AUROC (DC, YOLO/MS-COCO). Against adaptive attacks with full knowledge of TRACE, detection degrades but attack success rate drops sharply (e.g., OGA ASR falls from ~0.97 to 0.73 at λ=5×10⁻⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACE outperforms all five baselines across every dataset, model, and attack configuration. Against Detector Cleanse (the only prior black-box TTSD for OD), TRACE improves average F1 by approximately 30% (e.g., 0.877 vs. 0.665 on YOLO/MS-COCO) and AUROC by a similar margin (0.900 vs. 0.657). The classification-oriented methods TeCo, Strip, and SCALE-UP perform near chance level (F1 ≈ 0.45–0.58), confirming that uniform pixel-level transformations fail to exploit the semantic structure of object detection. FreqDetector, which relies on frequency-domain trigger signatures, is entirely unavailable for the CIB attack (which uses benign features as triggers) and otherwise trails TRACE by 20–35 points in F1. Notably, even in the worst-case adaptive attack setting where the adversary has full knowledge of TRACE's loss, TRACE retains competitive detection (F1 ≥ 0.58) while the attack success rate drops substantially, confirming the fundamental tension between evading detection and maintaining backdoor efficacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACE repurposes the transformation-consistency paradigm from classification TTSD methods (TeCo, SCALE-UP) but fundamentally rethinks what to transform: instead of uniform pixel-level perturbations, it applies semantically distinct foreground and background transformations, exploiting the fact that OD outputs are spatially structured. It departs from Detector Cleanse—the only prior black-box TTSD for OD—by eliminating the need for attack-specific knowledge, and from white-box trigger-reverse-engineering methods (ODSCAN, Django) by operating purely on model outputs. The use of Natural Backdoor Objects (e.g., stop signs) as position-invariant probes for FN-inducing triggers is a novel repurposing of the very property (overconfident, context-independent recognition) that makes such objects hard to distinguish from triggers in the first place.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRACE is the first universal, attack-agnostic, black-box TTSD for object detection, making it directly deployable in MLaaS settings where defenders have no training data or attack knowledge. The adaptive-attack analysis reveals a fundamental tension: an adversary cannot simultaneously maintain high backdoor success rate and evade TRACE, suggesting the detection signal is structurally tied to the backdoor mechanism rather than a superficial artifact. Key limitations include the computational overhead of multiple background/foreground queries per sample and reliance on publicly available background and reference images, leaving efficient zero-shot detection as an open problem.
