## Problem

Training-free caching methods like Tip-Adapter have become popular for few-shot CLIP adaptation, yet no theoretical framework explains why they work or what their limitations are. These local nonparametric regressors lack any global regularization from the zero-shot predictor, which constrains their generalization ability. The paper asks how to incorporate global regularization into few-shot adapters while preserving the training-free, closed-form nature of caching methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252677/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query image encoded by CLIP's visual encoder (L2-normalized D-dimensional feature vector) and a few-shot support set of NK labeled images (K shots per class across N classes, with one-hot label vectors). It outputs an N-dimensional logit vector for class prediction. The entire pipeline is training-free: no gradient updates are required, and the only per-task computation is solving a single linear system of size NK×NK at inference time. When Random Fourier Features are used, the method avoids storing support samples entirely, reducing memory to N×(D+R).

## Method

The paper first shows that Tip-Adapter's adaptation term is a Nadaraya-Watson kernel estimator with an RBF kernel, exposing its well-known boundary bias. Building on this, ProKeR constrains the hypothesis space to a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) for vector-valued functions and introduces a proximal regularization term ‖φ − f_clip‖²_H that enforces pointwise closeness to the zero-shot CLIP predictor globally. By the representer theorem, the solution takes the closed form φ = f_clip + Σᵢ k_β(Sᵢ,·) γᵢ, where γ is obtained by inverting a single (I + (1/λ)K) matrix, making the method fully training-free. The kernel is a separable vector-valued kernel (scalar RBF × identity output matrix), and a Mercer/Bochner decomposition with Orthogonal Fourier Features enables memory-efficient inference without storing support samples. A trained variant (ProKeR+CLAP) jointly optimizes a linear base learner and the KRR solution iteratively, further improving performance.

## Results

On the standard 11-dataset few-shot classification benchmark (validation-free setting, hyperparameters transferred from ImageNet), ProKeR with an RBF kernel achieves 69.07% average top-1 accuracy across 1–16 shots, outperforming the second-best training-free method GDA (67.03%) by 2.04 points and Tip-Adapter (61.68%) by 7.39 points. In the CoOp benchmark (per-dataset hyperparameter tuning), ProKeR reaches 70.46% average accuracy, surpassing GDA (69.49%) and APE (68.62%). The jointly trained variant ProKeR+CLAP further improves to 70.73% average, exceeding all training-based baselines including CLAP (68.92%) and TaskRes (68.22%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProKeR (RBF) outperforms Tip-Adapter by 7.39 points on average (69.07% vs. 61.68%), with the gap widening at higher shot counts (10.51 points at 16 shots). Against APE, ProKeR leads by 3.95 points on average, though APE slightly edges ProKeR at 8 shots in the CoOp benchmark (70.75% vs. 73.85% — actually ProKeR wins here too). GDA is the closest competitor, achieving 67.03% average in the validation-free setting; ProKeR surpasses it by 2.04 points, though GDA narrowly wins at 8 shots (72.46% vs. 72.15%). In the CoOp benchmark, ProKeR beats GDA by 0.97 points on average (70.46% vs. 69.49%). Against training-based methods, training-free ProKeR already exceeds CLAP (68.92%) and TaskRes (68.22%) on average, while ProKeR+CLAP achieves 70.73%, surpassing all training-based baselines. On out-of-distribution generalization (ImageNet-V2, -Sketch, -A, -R), ProKeR achieves 48.10% average versus 47.06% for Tip-Adapter and 47.15% for APE, confirming robustness gains. Zero-shot CLIP (60.33% on ImageNet) is outperformed by all few-shot methods, with ProKeR reaching 64.45% at 16 shots.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes Tip-Adapter, a practical caching-based few-shot adaptation method, as a Nadaraya-Watson estimator from classical nonparametric statistics, thereby connecting a 2021 engineering solution to a 1964 kernel regression framework. From this vantage point, the authors systematically apply known remedies for NW bias (locally linear regression, Mahalanobis distance) and then depart from the purely local paradigm by introducing a proximal global regularization in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), yielding ProKeR. The training-based variant (ProKeR+CLAP) builds on the CLAP loss from Silva-Rodriguez et al. (2023), jointly optimizing a linear base learner with the kernel ridge regression solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework explaining why and how caching-based few-shot adapters work, which had been treated as a black-box engineering trick. The practical impact is significant: a training-free method that outperforms training-based baselines on 8 of 11 datasets while running in under 5 seconds, making it attractive for resource-constrained settings. Open questions remain around bandwidth selection, the choice of global metric beyond Mahalanobis in very low-shot regimes, and whether the local-global combination can be made more principled. The approach is limited to classification and relies on CLIP's feature space, leaving open whether the kernel perspective generalizes to other VLM architectures or downstream tasks like segmentation.
