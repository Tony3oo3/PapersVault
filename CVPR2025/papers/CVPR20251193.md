## Problem

In federated learning with feature distribution skew, clients collect data from different devices or environments (e.g., different MRI scanners, different phone camera styles), causing inconsistent feature distributions that degrade global model performance. Prior solutions address this at the model level (personalized batch normalization, feature-space augmentation, adversarial training) but overlook the data itself as the root cause of feature shift. No plug-and-play input-level data augmentation technique existed that could be seamlessly integrated into the standard data augmentation pipeline to mitigate feature distribution skew.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224 for Office-Caltech-10 and DomainNet classification) or single-channel MRI slices (for ProstateMRI segmentation) and produces class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks, respectively. The key additional input is a set of C-dimensional channel-wise mean and standard deviation vectors (e.g., R³ for RGB) computed per client from their local dataset before training begins. During training, each sample is normalized using a randomly sampled statistic pair from the entire federation rather than its own client's fixed statistics; at test time, only the local client's statistics are applied to maintain train-test consistency.

## Method

FedRDN extends standard data normalization to the federated setting by treating each client's dataset-level channel-wise statistics (μᵏ, σᵏ) as a proxy for its local data distribution. Before training, every client computes its per-channel mean and standard deviation, uploads them to the server, and the server broadcasts the full set {(μᵏ, σᵏ)}ₖ₌₁ᴷ to all clients. During each training epoch, every image xᵢᵏ is normalized as x̂ᵢᵏ = (xᵢᵏ − μʲ)/σʲ where (μʲ, σʲ) is randomly resampled from the federation-wide statistic pool, effectively exposing each local model to the distributional characteristics of all clients. This is non-parametric, requires no architectural modification, adds negligible communication cost (only C floats per client), and integrates into the existing transforms.Compose() pipeline with a few lines of code. At inference, the model reverts to using only its own client's statistics, ensuring consistency with the local test distribution.

## Results

On Office-Caltech-10, FedRDN yields the largest improvement for Scaffold (+11.21%, from 57.43% to 68.64% top-1 accuracy) and FedAvgM (+9.70%, from 59.83% to 69.53%), while the state-of-the-art FedFA still gains +3.27% (70.11% → 73.38%). On DomainNet, improvements range from +0.54% (FedFA) to +3.26% (FedProto). On ProstateMRI (Dice score), FedRDN improves FedAvg by +2.30% (90.02% → 92.32%) and FedFA by +1.24% (91.90% → 93.14%). Across all 8 FL baselines and 3 datasets, FedRDN achieves consistent gains without any degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRDN consistently outperforms all 8 FL baselines it is integrated with, with the largest gains on weaker methods: Scaffold improves by +11.21% on Office-Caltech-10 and +3.09% on DomainNet, while FedAvgM gains +9.70% and +2.88% respectively. Against the feature-distribution-skew-specific baselines, FedRDN still improves FedBN by +0.49%/+0.98% (smaller due to FedBN's personalized batch normalization already partially addressing shift) and FedFA by +3.27%/+0.54%, demonstrating that input-level and feature-level augmentation are complementary. Compared to the other input-level augmentation FedMix, FedRDN dominates across nearly all method–dataset combinations; FedMix even degrades FedFA by −5.54% on DomainNet while FedRDN improves it by +0.54%. Conventional data normalization (+norm) is inconsistent, causing drops of up to −3.54% (FedProto on OC-10) and −1.27% (FedProx on DomainNet), whereas FedRDN never degrades performance. On ProstateMRI segmentation, FedRDN improves all five tested FL methods by +0.50% to +2.30% Dice, with FedFA+RDN reaching the best overall score of 93.14%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on feature distribution skew in FL (FedBN, FedFA, HarmoFL, ADCOL), which addresses feature shift at the model-architecture or feature-space level, by instead targeting the input-level data augmentation pipeline. It repurposes the standard data normalization operation—typically a fixed, dataset-specific preprocessing step—into a federated augmentation mechanism that randomly selects channel-wise statistics from any client in the federation. Conceptually, it extends the data augmentation literature (Mixup, CutMix, FedMix) into FL while avoiding FedMix's privacy cost of sharing actual pixel data, and it complements rather than competes with feature-level methods like FedFA since the two operate in different spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedRDN offers a practically attractive solution: a few lines of code, negligible communication overhead (R^3 per client for RGB), no architectural changes, and consistent gains across eight FL baselines and three datasets. This makes it a low-friction add-on that practitioners can adopt immediately alongside existing FL methods. However, the paper acknowledges the lack of theoretical analysis and restricts validation to visual tasks where pixel statistics are privacy-agnostic; extending the approach to tabular or text data, and providing formal guarantees on how random statistic injection affects the learned feature geometry, remain open. The modest gains on ProstateMRI (≤1.37%) also suggest the method's benefit may be more pronounced in classification than segmentation settings.
