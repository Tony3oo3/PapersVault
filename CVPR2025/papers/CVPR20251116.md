## Problem

Generating high-quality, wide-scope 3D scenes from a single image remains challenging because existing methods either require dense multi-view inputs, rely on time-consuming per-scene optimization, or produce distorted geometry and blurry backgrounds in occluded regions. No prior approach combines precise camera control, feed-forward reconstruction, and wide-scope coverage in a single efficient pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251116/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 480×720 RGB image and a user-specified camera trajectory (49 frame poses) as input. It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene via a two-stage pipeline: first a video diffusion model generates 49 frames compressed into 13×60×90 latents by a 3D-VAE, then a transformer-based reconstruction model regresses pixel-aligned 3DGS parameters (position, covariance, color, opacity, ray distance) from those latents. The entire pipeline runs in approximately 5 minutes on a single A100 GPU, compared to 16 minutes for Cat3D and 3 hours for ZeroNVS.

## Method

Wonderland introduces a dual-branch camera conditioning mechanism (a ControlNet-style trainable copy of 21 transformer blocks plus a LoRA branch with rank-256 adapters) that injects pixel-level Plücker embeddings into a CogVideoX-5B video diffusion model, enabling precise pose control while preserving generation quality. The generated video latents are then lifted to 3D by the Latent Large Reconstruction Model (LaLRM), a 24-block transformer (hidden dim 1024) that operates directly on the compressed latent space rather than decoded RGB, achieving a 256× spatiotemporal reduction that makes wide-scope reconstruction feasible under memory constraints. A progressive training strategy first trains at low resolution (49×240×360) on benchmark datasets with known poses, then scales to full resolution (49×480×720) incorporating 20K out-of-domain videos generated by the camera-guided diffusion model itself, improving generalization to in-the-wild scenes.

## Results

On 3D scene novel view synthesis (Table 2), Wonderland achieves LPIPS 0.292 / PSNR 17.15 / SSIM 0.550 on RealEstate10K, 0.325 / 16.64 / 0.574 on DL3DV, and 0.344 / 15.90 / 0.510 on Tanks-and-Temples, outperforming all baselines by a large margin. For camera-guided video generation (Table 1), Wonderland attains the best FID (16.16 on RE10K), lowest rotation error (R_err 0.046), and lowest translation error (T_err 0.093) across all three datasets. End-to-end latency is approximately 5 minutes on a single A100 GPU, yielding a 3.2× speedup over Cat3D and 36× over ZeroNVS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wonderland consistently outperforms all five baselines across every metric and dataset. In 3D scene generation (Table 2), it beats ZeroNVS [75] by a wide margin (e.g., LPIPS 0.292 vs. 0.448 on RE10K, PSNR 17.15 vs. 13.01) and ViewCrafter [111] by a clear but smaller gap (LPIPS 0.292 vs. 0.341, PSNR 17.15 vs. 16.84 on RE10K). In camera-guided video generation (Table 1), Wonderland surpasses MotionCtrl [95] in both visual quality (FID 16.16 vs. 22.58) and pose precision (R_err 0.046 vs. 0.231, T_err 0.093 vs. 0.794 on RE10K), and edges out VD3D [4] and ViewCrafter [111] on all metrics including FVD (153.48 vs. 187.55 / 203.71) and translation error (0.093 vs. 0.126 / 0.152). WonderJourney [109] is compared only qualitatively (Figure 5); Wonderland produces sharper, artifact-free wide-scope scenes whereas WonderJourney's zoom-out views are blurry with visible artifacts. The ablation in Table 1 further confirms that the dual-branch (ControlNet + LoRA) conditioning is necessary: removing either branch degrades both pose accuracy and generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of camera-controlled video diffusion (building on CogVideoX-5B, ControlNet, and LoRA) and feed-forward 3D reconstruction (building on 3D Gaussian Splatting and transformer-based reconstruction models like LRM and pixelSplat). The key departure from prior work is the use of video diffusion model latents as an intermediate representation that bridges 2D generation and 3D reconstruction, rather than operating in decoded pixel space or requiring per-scene optimization. The dual-branch camera conditioning (ControlNet-style trainable blocks plus LoRA fine-tuning) is a novel combination that achieves precise pose control without degrading generation quality, addressing a limitation of prior camera-conditioned video models like VD3D and MotionCtrl.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a 3D reconstruction model can be effectively built upon the latent space of a video diffusion model, establishing a new architectural paradigm where the 256× spatiotemporal compression of video latents enables wide-scope scene reconstruction under practical memory constraints. The feed-forward pipeline (~5 min end-to-end) eliminates the hours-long per-scene optimization required by methods like ZeroNVS, making single-image 3D scene generation significantly more practical. Open questions include whether this latent-space reconstruction approach generalizes to other video model architectures, whether the 5B-parameter video diffusion model can be replaced by smaller models without quality loss, and how the method handles truly dynamic scenes versus the static scenes it is designed for.
