## Problem

CLIP-style vision pre-training (e.g., SigLIP) is confined to low resolutions (~378×378) because the computational cost of processing larger images grows quadratically for CNNs and quartically for ViTs, making 4K-resolution pre-training infeasible. This limits the model's ability to perceive fine visual details essential for real-world tasks such as reading small text or spotting distant objects, and existing training-free upscaling approaches (AnyRes, S²) cannot leverage large-scale pre-training to learn high-quality high-resolution representations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-resolution RGB image (up to 3780×3780, i.e., 4K) and an optional text prompt (user question or local caption) as input. It produces three types of output: low-resolution global visual features (27×27 tokens from a 378×378 encoding), high-resolution local features from a variable number of selected multi-scale patches (up to 2560+ tokens at scales of 756, 1512, or 3780), and per-position patch selection scores. In the VILA-HD MLLM setting, the final output is a generated text response, with the LLM's last-token embedding serving as the top-down selection prompt to determine which high-res regions to encode.

## Method

PS3 (Pre-training with Scale-Selective Scaling) replaces global image-caption contrastive learning with localized contrastive learning between small image regions and their detailed captions, decoupling computational cost from global image resolution and enabling near-constant-cost scaling to 4K. The architecture has three stages: (1) a SigLIP-SO400M ViT encodes the full image at 378×378 for global features; (2) patch selection computes per-position scores via cosine similarity between low-res features and a text/saliency embedding, augmented by a lightweight 3-block ConvNeXt at 1512 resolution for finer localization; (3) top-k patches at multiple scales (756, 1512, 3780) are processed by the same ViT with KV cache from Stage 1 providing global context. Pre-training jointly optimizes a sigmoid contrastive loss between local high-res features and local caption embeddings (with teacher-forced ground-truth box selection and box-constrained attention pooling) and a segmentation loss (cross-entropy + DICE) supervising the patch selection scores from curated bounding boxes. The training data comprises 75M high-res images with 282M region-caption-box pairs auto-curated via SAM segmentation and MLLM captioning.

## Results

On the proposed 4KPro benchmark, VILA-HD-4K achieves 74.2% accuracy at 35% patch selection (3780 resolution), outperforming Qwen2-VL (71.0%), GPT-4o (59.7%), and Gemini-1.5-Pro (59.7%) while running 2.96× faster than Qwen2-VL (1.22s vs 3.61s latency at 18% selection). Across seven resolution-sensitive benchmarks (TextVQA, ChartQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, OCRBench, V*Bench, RealWorldQA), PS3 at 1512 resolution improves 14.2% over the SigLIP baseline, 6.9% over AnyRes, and 2.4% over S². VILA-HD achieves the best result in 6 out of 11 evaluated benchmarks, with VILA-HD-4K at only 18% patch selection (4036 tokens) matching or exceeding Qwen2-VL (16384 tokens) on V*Bench (71.2 vs 71.0) and RealWorldQA (70.3 vs 70.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the low-resolution SigLIP baseline, PS3 at 1512 resolution yields a 14.2% average improvement across seven benchmarks, confirming that high-resolution pre-training (not just inference-time tiling) is essential. Compared to AnyRes (the tiling approach used by LLaVA-OneVision and others), PS3 improves 6.9% at 1512 resolution while using 5× fewer tokens, and 3.2% at 4K resolution with 2.5× fewer tokens. Against S², PS3 gains 2.4% at 1512 and 3.1% at 4K resolution with fewer tokens, showing that learned patch selection outperforms uniform tiling. On the new 4KPro benchmark, VILA-HD-4K (74.2%) surpasses Qwen2-VL (71.0%) by 3.2% with 2.96× lower latency, and beats GPT-4o (59.7%) by 14.5%. VILA-HD also outperforms NVILA (same LLM backbone) on V*Bench (71.2 vs 67.2), RealWorldQA (70.3 vs 66.4), and 4KPro (72.6 vs 58.1) despite using less training data, isolating the contribution of PS3 pre-training. On benchmarks with lower MRR (MathVista, MMBench, MMMU-Pro), VILA-HD-1.5K is competitive but does not always lead, suggesting PS3's advantage is most pronounced when fine-grained visual detail is required.

## Relation to Prior Work

PS3 extends the CLIP/SigLIP contrastive pre-training paradigm by replacing global image-caption contrast with local region-caption contrast, drawing on the top-down/bottom-up attention framework from Shi et al. (CVPR 2023) for patch selection. The resulting MLLM, VILA-HD, is built directly on NVILA with the vision encoder swapped to PS3, while the high-res feature extraction reuses the same ViT backbone via a KV-cache mechanism borrowed from LLM inference. The paper deliberately departs from training-free high-resolution approaches (AnyRes, S²) that simply tile images at inference time, arguing that without high-res pre-training the model cannot learn quality fine-grained representations. The 4KPro benchmark is introduced as a departure from existing VQA benchmarks, which the authors show do not actually require 4K-resolution perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 79× pre-training cost reduction and the constant-cost scaling property (higher resolution improves accuracy even with a fixed number of selected patches) make 4K-resolution vision pre-training practically feasible for the first time, potentially reshaping how MLLMs handle high-resolution inputs. The finding that mainstream benchmarks (DocVQA, V*Bench, etc.) have a minimum recognizable resolution well below 4K is an important diagnostic for the field, suggesting that current evaluation may be masking the true capability gap in fine-grained perception. Open questions include whether the local-contrastive formulation generalizes to video or 3D, whether the 75M-image dataset is sufficient for full 4K coverage, and whether the approach can be applied to smaller or non-ViT backbones without the ConvNeXt auxiliary encoder.
