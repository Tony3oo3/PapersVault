## Problem

Few-Shot Class Incremental Learning (FSCIL) for 3D point cloud segmentation remains largely unexplored, combining the data scarcity of few-shot learning with the catastrophic forgetting risk of incremental learning. Existing FSCIL methods rely on Euclidean embeddings that lack the capacity to model the hierarchical structures inherent in point cloud data, leading to compaction, class overlap, and severe forgetting as new classes are introduced under limited supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250078/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB point clouds of indoor scenes (S3DIS, ScanNetv2) where the base session provides abundant labeled examples per class, while each incremental session supplies only N-way K-shot labeled samples for novel classes (e.g., 5-shot). The backbone (PointTransformer or DGCNN) extracts per-point features, which are projected into the Poincaré ball and classified by a hyperbolic classifier head producing per-point semantic labels. At test time, only the point-level classification branch is active, segmenting all classes encountered up to the current session without access to prior-session data.

## Method

HIPO embeds point cloud features in the Poincaré Hyperbolic Sphere, where Ideal Prototypes are pre-assigned at the ball boundary (points at infinity) and positioned mutually orthogonally using CLIP text embeddings and a RankNet-inspired triplet loss (L_HIP) to maximize semantic separation. Class-mean features are computed in Euclidean space and projected into hyperbolic space via the exponential map and a Möbius layer, avoiding costly Fréchet mean computation. Training uses a modified Busemann loss combining a prototype-alignment term (Busemann function measuring infinite geodesic distance to the Ideal Prototype) with an uncertainty-aware regularization term (hyperbolic distance from the origin) that penalizes overconfident predictions and prevents vanishing gradients near the ball boundary. This alignment loss is combined with standard cross-entropy for point-level segmentation, and the entire system is optimized with Riemannian SGD (geoopt), enabling rehearsal-free incremental learning that preserves prior class representations while integrating new ones.

## Results

On S3DIS (5-shot, PointTransformer backbone), HIPO achieves an average Last mIoU of 31.13%, AIA of 40.30%, and forgetting rate of 14.16%, outperforming the prior SOTA 3DPC-CISS (26.50% Last, 37.73% AIA, 17.46% forgetting) by +4.63% Last and +2.57% AIA. On ScanNetv2, HIPO reaches 11.68% Last and 22.27% AIA, surpassing 3DPC-CISS (8.83% Last, 20.12% AIA). In the cross-dataset ScanNetv2→S3DIS setting, HIPO attains 20.60% Last and 29.81% AIA with a forgetting rate of 16.05%, maintaining competitive performance despite domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIPO outperforms all six baselines (LwF, C-FSCIL, LGKD, SDCOT, 3DPC-CISS) on S3DIS across every incremental setting, with the largest margin over 3DPC-CISS (the prior SOTA): +4.63% Last and +2.57% AIA on average, and a 3.3-point reduction in forgetting (14.16% vs. 17.46%). The gap is most pronounced in the 7-3T setting where HIPO achieves 29.40% Last vs. 24.20% for 3DPC-CISS, and in forgetting where HIPO drops to 11.10% vs. 15.45%. On ScanNetv2, HIPO leads 3DPC-CISS by +2.85% Last and +2.15% AIA, while SDCOT and LwF collapse to near-zero Last accuracy (0.73% and 2.60% respectively), indicating severe forgetting. In the cross-dataset ScanNetv2→S3DIS transfer, HIPO edges out 3DPC-CISS by +0.65% Last and +0.52% AIA with comparable forgetting (16.05% vs. 16.01%), showing robustness to domain shift. With the DGCNN backbone (Table 5), HIPO beats BiDist (the strongest DGCNN-based baseline) by +2.20% Last, +0.72% AIA, and reduces forgetting by 1.98 points (13.87% vs. 15.85%), confirming architecture-agnostic effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIPO directly extends the hyperbolic Busemann learning framework of Ghadimi Atigh et al. (NeurIPS 2021), which introduced Ideal Prototypes at the Poincaré ball boundary and the Busemann function for measuring infinite geodesic distances, by adapting it to the FSCIL setting for 3D point cloud segmentation. The prototype positioning loss (L_HIP) is adapted from Mettes et al.'s Hyperspherical Prototype Networks (NeurIPS 2019) and the triplet ranking structure from RankNet (ICML 2005), with CLIP text embeddings providing semantic priors for prototype placement. The key departure from prior FSCIL work (CEC, FACT, ALICE, CLOM) is the shift from Euclidean to hyperbolic embedding space, motivated by the hierarchical structure of 3D point cloud data, and the addition of an uncertainty-aware regularization term to the Busemann loss to prevent feature collapse and vanishing gradients near the Poincaré ball boundary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper establishes FSCIL for 3D point cloud segmentation as a formal problem and provides the first dedicated solution, filling a gap where prior FSCIL methods targeted images and prior 3D incremental methods targeted classification. The consistent gains across two backbones (PointTransformer, DGCNN) and two datasets (S3DIS, ScanNetv2), including cross-dataset transfer, suggest the hyperbolic embedding approach is not architecture-specific. However, absolute mIoU values remain modest (30–40% range), the evaluation is limited to indoor scenes, and the authors acknowledge that handling outliers without prior reference across sessions remains open. The framework's reliance on CLIP text embeddings for prototype positioning also raises questions about scalability to domains where semantic class names are unavailable or ambiguous.
