## Problem

Existing audio-driven talking head methods can produce lip-synced avatars but fail to generate natural, diverse emotional expressions. Two fundamental gaps limit progress: (1) no framework exists for modeling individual basic emotions in a way that composes into compound emotions, and (2) no sufficiently large, high-resolution dataset covers both basic and compound emotional expressions with fine-grained annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252718/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image (any style: photo, painting, generative-model output), a sequence of driving audio clips, and a flexible emotion condition that can be a text prompt, an audio signal with emotional cues, or fine-grained Action Unit (AU) labels. It outputs a talking head video at 512×512 resolution (latent space 64×64) where the portrait speaks the audio with the specified emotion. For long-form generation, the last 2 frames of each clip seed the next clip for temporal continuity.

## Method

MoEE builds on a Stable Video Diffusion denoising U-Net with a Reference Net (from Animate Anyone/Hallo) for identity preservation, a wav2vec audio encoder, and AnimateDiff-style temporal attention. The key architectural contribution is a Mixture of Emotion Experts module: six cross-attention experts, each trained exclusively on one basic emotion (happiness, sadness, anger, disgust, fear, surprise), combined via a soft gating mechanism that applies both global (softmax over pooled features) and local (per-token sigmoid) assignment weights, enabling compound emotions to be synthesized as weighted blends of experts. An Emotion-to-Latents module unifies multimodal control signals (text via T5, audio via emotion2vec, AU labels via a small MLP) into a shared latent space through adaptive attention, so any combination of modalities can drive the experts. Training is two-stage: first fine-tuning the backbone on the full emotion dataset, then freezing all non-MoE modules and training only the expert and gating networks. A Masked Noisy Emotion Sampling strategy mixes in other-emotion or neutral frames (with the mouth region masked via MediaPipe) to prevent the model from overfitting to mouth shape rather than expression, and a timestep-aware spatial L1+perceptual loss in pixel space recovers fine facial details lost at the low latent resolution.

## Results

On the DH-FaceEmoVid-150 dataset (the paper's own emotion-rich benchmark), MoEE achieves FID 39.62, FVD 402.80, LPIPS 0.182, AED 0.153, and AKD 4.03, outperforming all five baselines on every metric. On the standard HDTF benchmark, MoEE attains FID 28.83, FVD 322.63, and LPIPS 0.152, matching or exceeding the best baseline (Hallo) on FVD and LPIPS while showing a marginal gap on FID (28.83 vs. 28.61) and lip-sync metrics (Sync-C 6.11 vs. 6.25, Sync-D 9.10 vs. 8.74), which the authors attribute to the Chinese-language audio in HDTF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoEE dominates all five baselines on the DH-FaceEmoVid-150 dataset across every metric, with the largest margins on FID (39.62 vs. 48.01 for the next-best EAT, a 17% improvement) and AKD (4.03 vs. 14.11 for EAT, a 3.5× reduction in keypoint distance). On the standard HDTF benchmark, MoEE is competitive with Hallo (the strongest non-emotion baseline): it wins on FVD (322.63 vs. 343.02) and LPIPS (0.152 vs. 0.167) but trails slightly on FID (28.83 vs. 28.61) and lip-sync (Sync-C 6.11 vs. 6.25; Sync-D 9.10 vs. 8.74), which the authors attribute to HDTF's Chinese audio. Among emotion-specific baselines, EAT and StyleTalk produce the most competitive results on HDTF FID (81.25 and 76.56 respectively) but are far behind MoEE on the emotion-rich DH-FaceEmoVid-150 set, where their FID scores (48.01 and 51.04) are 8–12 points worse. AniPortrait, Hallo, and EchoMimic, which lack explicit emotion control, show the weakest performance on emotion metrics (AED 0.174–0.225, AKD 8.33–20.65), confirming that general-purpose talking-head models struggle with expressive facial animation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoEE builds directly on the diffusion-based talking head generation pipeline established by Stable Video Diffusion, Animate Anyone, and Hallo, adopting their denoising U-Net, Reference Net, and Temporal Attention components as the generative backbone. Its key architectural departure is the application of the Soft Mixture of Experts paradigm (from Puigcerver et al.) to emotion modeling, where six single-emotion cross-attention experts are combined via global and local soft gating to synthesize compound expressions—a decomposition strategy absent from prior emotion-control methods like EAT, StyleTalk, and EAMM, which rely on single reference videos or limited emotion taxonomies. The Emotion-to-Latents module further extends the control interface by unifying audio (emotion2vec), text (T5), and AU-label signals into a shared latent space, going beyond the single-modality control of InstructAvatar or the video-reference requirement of EAMM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released DH-FaceEmoVid-150 dataset (150 hours, 1080p, 80 actors, six basic + four compound emotions, with AU labels and GPT-4V-generated text descriptions) addresses a genuine data bottleneck in emotion-driven talking head research, where prior resources like MEAD cover only basic emotions at smaller scale. The MoEE decomposition offers a clean, interpretable mechanism for compound emotion synthesis that could generalize to richer emotion taxonomies. However, the paper acknowledges that only four compound emotions are represented, and fine-grained single-AU control is limited by inter-AU correlations in the training data—open questions include whether the expert decomposition scales to dozens of compound combinations and whether disentangled AU control is achievable without explicit disentanglement objectives.
