## Problem

Encoder-based ID-preserving generation methods (InstantID, IP-Adapter, PhotoMaker) can produce realistic portraits from a single photo in one inference pass, creating a new threat vector that existing defenses like AntiDreamBooth do not address. No prior work provides a universal, efficient, and robust protection mechanism against these encoder-based approaches, which simultaneously exploit both CLIP and ArcFace feature spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251322/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary-sized RGB portrait image, resizes it to 224×224, and concatenates a fourth-channel face localization mask (from InsightFace landmarks) as input to the noise encoder. It outputs a 3-channel adversarial perturbation bounded to ±ε (ε=0.035, i.e., 9/255), which is resized back to the original image dimensions and added pixel-wise to produce the protected image. The entire protection pipeline completes in ~0.173 seconds per image via a single forward pass.

## Method

IDProtector uses a ViT-S/8 encoder to predict adversarial noise in a single forward pass, trained with a composite loss that simultaneously minimizes cosine similarity between clean and protected embeddings across four target models: ArcFace (for InstantID), CLIP output (for IP-Adapter and PhotoMaker), and CLIP patch features (for IP-Adapter-Plus). The attack targets are selected to block all information pathways while keeping backpropagation paths short and semantically dense. An L1 regularization term plus an ε-ball penalty enforces imperceptibility. For robustness, Gaussian noise (σ=0.003) is injected into the precomputed affine alignment matrix during training, simulating the misalignment that protected images cause in the face-alignment step of InstantID. Training follows a three-stage curriculum that progressively shrinks ε from 0.05 to 0.035 and rebalances the per-model loss weights, trained on 190K CelebA images for 10 days on four H100 GPUs.

## Results

On CelebA, IDProtector (ViT) reduces InstantID's ISM from 0.775 (no protection) to 0.231, a reduction of 0.544, while the best baseline (SimAC) only reduces it to 0.752 (reduction of 0.023). For IP-Adapter, IDProtector lowers ISM to 0.060 versus 0.152–0.162 for baselines. On the unseen VGG Face dataset, the same pattern holds: InstantID ISM drops to 0.232 (from 0.764) with IDProtector, compared to 0.691–0.707 for baselines. Protection time is 0.173 s per image, less than 1% of the fastest baseline (PhotoGuard at 23.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDProtector (ViT) decisively outperforms all five baselines on the primary ISM metric across every generation method and both datasets. The gap is most dramatic on InstantID: IDProtector reduces ISM by ~0.54 (0.775→0.231 on CelebA), whereas the strongest baseline (SimAC) achieves only a 0.023 reduction (0.775→0.752). On IP-Adapter, IDProtector cuts ISM to 0.060 on CelebA, roughly 2.5× lower than the best baseline (PhotoGuard at 0.152). All five baselines are essentially ineffective against PhotoMaker and InstantID, with ISM reductions below 0.06, while IDProtector achieves reductions of 0.14 and 0.54 respectively. The PGD variant of IDProtector is even stronger (e.g., ISM −0.166 on InstantID/CelebA) but requires 4089 s per image versus 0.173 s for the ViT feed-forward model, making the ViT version the practical choice. Generalization to unseen models (Table 5) further confirms the advantage: IDProtector achieves an average ISM reduction of 0.31 across eight additional generators, including proprietary models like Midjourney (0.101 reduction) and Jing Gou (0.541 reduction).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial defense literature for diffusion models (Anti-DreamBooth, AdvDM, PhotoGuard, SimAC, ACE) from tuning-based customization methods (DreamBooth, LoRA, Textual Inversion) to the newer class of encoder-based ID-preserving methods (InstantID, IP-Adapter, PhotoMaker). The key architectural departure is replacing per-image PGD optimization with a feed-forward ViT-S/8 noise encoder, and the key conceptual departure is designing a composite loss that simultaneously targets both CLIP and ArcFace feature spaces used across different encoder-based pipelines. The affine-noise augmentation strategy repurposes the face alignment step inherent to InstantID's preprocessing as a training-time data augmentation, rather than relying on post-hoc differentiable transforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IDProtector makes adversarial face protection practically deployable at 0.173s per image (vs. 23–323s for iterative baselines), which is essential for real-world social media use. Its demonstrated generalization to closed-source proprietary models (Midjourney, Jing Gou) and unseen datasets (VGG Face) suggests the protection is not overfit to specific open-source architectures. The main open question is the imperceptibility trade-off: the authors acknowledge the noise is not fully invisible, and closing this gap while preserving robustness against affine transforms and JPEG compression remains unresolved. Broader implications include whether similar feed-forward adversarial encoders can protect other biometric or identity signals (voice, gait) against analogous encoder-based reconstruction pipelines.
