## Problem

Existing LiDAR 3D detection backbones face a fundamental trade-off: SpCNN-based methods are constrained by small receptive fields, Transformer-based methods suffer from quadratic complexity and limited receptive fields due to window grouping, and direct Mamba adaptation loses spatial voxel locality during 1D serialization. The paper addresses how to simultaneously capture local structural details and global contextual dependencies from sparse 3D voxels in a computationally efficient, group-friendly manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251305/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds that are voxelized into 3D grids with dataset-specific resolutions (0.3×0.3×0.25 m for nuScenes, 0.32×0.32×0.1875 m for Waymo, 0.4×0.4×0.25 m for Argoverse 2) and 128 feature channels per voxel. The encoder-decoder backbone with downsampling strides {1,2,2} produces multi-scale voxel features that are projected into a BEV representation and passed to a detection head, yielding 3D bounding boxes with class labels across 10 (nuScenes), 3 (Waymo), or 26 (Argoverse 2) object categories.

## Method

UniMamba integrates 3D submanifold convolution and bidirectional Mamba (selective SSM) in a unified block with three key components. First, a Spatial Locality Modeling (SLM) module applies 3D submanifold convolution before serialization to recover dynamic spatial position embeddings lost during 1D flattening. Second, a Complementary Z-order serialization converts 3D voxels into 1D sequences using both X-axis-primary and Y-axis-primary bit-interleaving to preserve spatial proximity along two orthogonal directions at negligible cost (0.9 ms vs. 15.8 ms for Hilbert). Third, the Local-Global Sequential Aggregator (LGSA) splits the channel dimension into M=4 groups: J=2 groups are processed by a Global Sequential Encoder (GSE) that treats all voxels as a single 1D sequence through two cascaded bidirectional Mamba layers, while the remaining 2 groups are processed by a Local Sequential Encoder (LSE) that applies window-based grouping with Z-order traversal within each window. This channel-grouping strategy enables parallel local-global aggregation without sequential overhead, and stacked UniMamba blocks in an encoder-decoder architecture provide hierarchical multi-scale feature extraction.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, UniMamba achieves 70.2 mAP and 74.0 NDS, becoming the first LiDAR-based method to exceed 70 mAP. On the Waymo Open validation set, it reaches 76.13/74.11 ALL(L2) mAP/mAPH, outperforming the previous best SpCNN-based method HEDNet by 0.83 in L2 mAP. On Argoverse 2, UniMamba achieves 42.0 mAP, surpassing the prior SOTA LION-Mamba by 0.5 mAP. In terms of efficiency, UniMamba operates at 61.9 GFlops with 1.6M backbone parameters, roughly half the computational cost of DSVT (110.2 GFlops) while delivering 1.4 higher L2 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes test, UniMamba outperforms the strongest SpCNN-based method SAFDNet by +1.9 mAP and +1.7 NDS, and the Transformer-based DSVT by +1.8 mAP and +1.3 NDS, with particularly large gains on Truck (+3.9 mAP over DSVT) and Pedestrian (+1.9 mAP over DSVT). On Waymo, UniMamba surpasses HEDNet (SpCNN SOTA) by 0.83 in ALL(L2) mAP and DSVT by 2.13 in ALL(L2) mAP, with notable advantages on small objects (+2.56 L2 mAP for Pedestrian, +2.86 for Cyclist over DSVT). On Argoverse 2, UniMamba edges out LION-Mamba by 0.5 mAP (42.0 vs. 41.5) simply by replacing its 3D backbone. Compared to the other Mamba-based method VoxelMamba on Waymo, UniMamba gains 0.8 L2 mAP (76.13 vs. 75.33). In efficiency, UniMamba requires only 61.9 GFlops and 1.6M backbone parameters versus DSVT's 110.2 GFlops and 2.7M parameters, achieving higher accuracy at roughly half the compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMamba sits at the intersection of two lines of work in LiDAR 3D detection: sparse convolutional backbones (SpCNN, HEDNet) that excel at local structure but have limited receptive fields, and sequential/attention-based backbones (Transformer, Mamba) that capture long-range dependencies but suffer from quadratic cost or locality loss during serialization. It builds directly on LION's Mamba-based detector framework and HEDNet's encoder-decoder stacking, while borrowing FlatFormer's window grouping strategy for the local encoder. The key departure is the unified multi-head design that interleaves 3D submanifold convolution (for spatial locality) with bidirectional SSM (for global context) via a channel-grouping mechanism, rather than treating convolution and sequential modeling as separate stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniMamba is the first LiDAR-based method to surpass 70 mAP on the nuScenes test set and achieves SOTA on all three major benchmarks (nuScenes, Waymo, Argoverse 2) while operating at roughly half the FLOPs of the prior Transformer-based SOTA (DSVT). The channel-grouping strategy for parallel local-global aggregation is a clean, reusable design pattern that avoids the latency penalty of sequential or naive parallel fusion. Open questions include whether the complementary Z-order serialization is truly optimal versus learned permutation strategies, whether the fixed 2-GSE/2-LSE split generalizes across scenes with varying sparsity, and whether the encoder-decoder stacking depth can be further reduced without performance loss for real-time deployment.
