## Problem

Mamba's state space model (SSM) token mixer has been widely adopted for vision tasks, yet visual Mamba models consistently underperform state-of-the-art convolutional and attention-based backbones. The paper asks whether SSM is genuinely necessary for vision by analyzing the inherent properties of SSM (causal, lossy fixed-size memory) against the characteristics of common visual tasks, and empirically tests this by removing SSM from Mamba blocks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250067/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For ImageNet classification, the model consumes 224×224 RGB images producing 196 tokens (patch size 16×16) and outputs a 1000-way class label. For COCO detection/segmentation, inputs are resized to 800×1280 (~4K tokens) and the model outputs bounding boxes plus instance masks via Mask R-CNN. For ADE20K semantic segmentation, inputs are 512×2048 (~4K tokens) and the model outputs pixel-wise class maps via UperNet. The token count difference (196 vs. ~4K) is central to the paper's argument about when SSM becomes beneficial.

## Method

MambaOut is constructed by taking the Mamba block and removing its core SSM token mixer, leaving only the Gated CNN component: a depthwise 7×7 convolution (following ConvNeXt) applied to partial channels (following InceptionNeXt) for efficiency, wrapped in the standard gated meta-architecture with normalization and residual connection. The paper's conceptual argument is that SSM's recurrent mechanism provides a fixed-size lossy memory that operates in causal mode, making it ideal for long-sequence autoregressive tasks but unnecessary for short-sequence understanding tasks like ImageNet classification (196 tokens ≪ 6D threshold). Empirically, MambaOut-Femto/Tiny/Small/Base (7M–85M params) outperform all visual Mamba models (Vim, VMamba, LocalMamba, PlainMamba, EfficientVMamba) on ImageNet by up to 0.4% at matched compute, while on COCO and ADE20K (~4K tokens) MambaOut falls short of SOTA Mamba models, confirming SSM's value for long-sequence visual tasks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), MambaOut-Small achieves 84.1% top-1 accuracy with 48M params and 9.0G MACs, outperforming all visual Mamba models at comparable scale (VMamba-S: 83.5%, LocalVMamba-S: 83.7%, VMambaV3-S: 83.6%). MambaOut-Base reaches 84.2% top-1 accuracy, surpassing VMamba-B (83.7%) and VMambaV3-B (83.9%). On COCO detection (Mask R-CNN, 1× schedule), MambaOut-Tiny achieves 45.1 AP^b, trailing VMamba-T (46.5) and VMambaV3-T (47.4) by 1.4 and 2.3 AP^b respectively. On ADE20K segmentation (UperNet), MambaOut-Small achieves 49.5 mIoU (SS), below LocalVMamba-S (50.0) and VMambaV3-S (50.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet, MambaOut decisively wins against all visual Mamba baselines: MambaOut-Small (84.1%) beats LocalVMamba-S (83.7%) by 0.4% while using only 79% of the MACs, and MambaOut-Base (84.2%) surpasses VMamba-B (83.7%) by 0.5%. This validates Hypothesis 1 that SSM is unnecessary for ImageNet classification. However, on COCO detection, MambaOut consistently loses to SOTA visual Mamba models: MambaOut-Tiny (45.1 AP^b) trails VMamba-T (46.5) by 1.4 AP^b and VMambaV3-T (47.4) by 2.3 AP^b, and MambaOut-Base (47.4 AP^b) falls short of VMambaV3-B (49.2) by 1.8 AP^b. A similar pattern holds on ADE20K segmentation, where MambaOut-Small (49.5 mIoU SS) is below LocalVMamba-S (50.0) and VMambaV3-S (50.6), confirming Hypothesis 2 that SSM retains value for long-sequence dense prediction tasks. Notably, even the best visual Mamba models (e.g., VMambaV3-B at 83.9%) remain well behind SOTA convolution-attention hybrids like CAFormer-M36 (85.2%) and TransNeXt-Base (84.8%) on ImageNet, indicating that visual Mamba as a whole has yet to demonstrate clear superiority over established architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a conceptual and empirical counterpoint to the wave of visual Mamba models (Vision Mamba, VMamba, LocalMamba, PlainMamba) that introduced SSM-based token mixers into vision backbones. Architecturally, MambaOut is derived directly from the Gated CNN block (Dauphin et al., 2017) with depthwise convolution design following ConvNeXt and partial-channel convolution following InceptionNeXt, effectively removing the SSM component that distinguishes Mamba from Gated CNN. Rather than proposing a new visual Mamba variant, the paper uses this ablation to test whether SSM is actually necessary, framing the analysis around two task characteristics (long-sequence and autoregressive) that determine where RNN-like memory mechanisms provide genuine advantage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, Occam's-razor baseline that future visual Mamba research must beat, and its conceptual framework (long-sequence + autoregressive as the two conditions for SSM benefit) offers a useful lens for deciding where to invest in SSM-based architectures. The finding that MambaOut outperforms all visual Mamba models on ImageNet while underperforming them on detection/segmentation gives a clear, actionable signal about where SSM adds value. Limitations include testing only three common vision tasks, no exploration of domain-specific applications (medical, remote sensing), and no large-scale MambaOut variant, leaving open whether the ImageNet result generalizes to higher-resolution or more complex classification settings.
