## Problem

Satellite videos suffer from a combined double-degradation (haze plus low spatial resolution) that existing single-degradation VSR or dehazing methods cannot address in a unified framework. Two-stage cascading approaches accumulate estimation errors across stages, and most satellite VSR methods are restricted to fixed integer upsampling factors, limiting practical deployment where continuous zoom levels are common.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a group of hazy LR satellite video frames (2N=4 supporting frames plus one reference frame, cropped at 64×64 during training) and a continuous scale factor r (e.g., ×2.1, ×3.6, ×8) as input, and produces a single dehazed, super-resolved frame at the requested arbitrary resolution. The degradation is modeled as haze (atmospheric scattering with airlight A∈[0.7,1] and transmission t∈[0.35,0.6]) applied first, followed by bicubic downsampling and noise, reflecting the real acquisition order of Jilin-1 satellite video at ~1 m ground resolution.

## Method

ODPNet is an end-to-end CNN-Transformer hybrid that jointly learns dehazing and continuous-scale SR. A prior-based feature soft dehazing module (PFSDM) inverts the atmospheric scattering model at the feature level by predicting per-pixel gain K and bias B via a U-shaped encoder with DehazeFormer blocks, yielding haze-free features without explicit pixel-level dehazing. A spatiotemporal self-attention (SSA) module first computes temporal attention along the channel dimension (where aligned multi-frame features are concatenated) to capture inter-frame dependencies, then applies spatial attention with a rotation trick to model intra-frame long-range dependencies. A tri-branch cross-aggregation block (TCB) enriches weak textures through parallel spatial and channel self-attention branches plus an interaction-aggregation branch. Finally, a cross-scale feature Top-k selection Transformer (CFTST) generates multi-scale feature maps via PACconv, interpolates latent codes at query coordinates, and uses an adaptive Top-k attention mask (k∈[50%,80%]) to select the most relevant cross-scale latent codes before an MLP maps them to pixel values. Training uses a joint loss combining L1 SR supervision (with random continuous scale sampling) and a dehazing supervision term (β=0.1) to stabilize feature-level dehazing.

## Results

On the Jilin-1 satellite video test set (10 clips, ×4 scale), ODPNet (7.3M params) achieves the best PSNR/SSIM on every clip, ranging from 24.22/0.9360 (Clip003) to 37.28/0.9750 (Clip005). The average PSNR gain over the strongest TSP baseline (TSP-MC, 7.4+10.6M params) is approximately 3–11 dB depending on the clip, while ODPNet uses roughly half the total parameters. On continuous-scale evaluation (×2.1 to ×8), ODPNet similarly outperforms all nine TSP combinations, e.g., 33.65/0.9916 vs. 31.72/0.9728 (best TSP) at ×2.1 on Clip001.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODPNet outperforms all six TSP baselines on every one of the 10 test clips at ×4 scale, with PSNR gains ranging from +0.53 dB (Clip003, vs. TSP-DC) to +10.81 dB (Clip005, vs. TSP-FL). The strongest TSP baseline is TSP-MC (MB-TaylorFormer + CSVSR, 18.0M total params), which ODPNet (7.3M params) surpasses by 3.4–9.2 dB PSNR across clips. Among dehazing components, MB-TaylorFormer-based TSPs (TSP-ML, TSP-MC) consistently outperform FFANet-based (TSP-FM, TSP-FL) and DehazeFormer-based (TSP-DM, TSP-DC) variants, suggesting stronger dehazing helps the cascade. Among VSR components, CSVSR (continuous-scale) generally edges out MSDTGP and LGTD in the TSP setting, but all are substantially outperformed by ODPNet's end-to-end approach. On continuous-scale evaluation (Table 1), ODPNet similarly dominates all nine TSP combinations at every tested scale (×2.1 through ×8), with the largest margins at intermediate scales (×2.7–×3.6) where TSP methods degrade most sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODPNet unifies two previously separate sub-tasks—remote sensing image dehazing and satellite video super-resolution—into a single end-to-end framework, departing from the standard two-stage cascade (TSP) that chains a dehazing network (e.g., DehazeFormer, FFANet) with a VSR network (e.g., EDVR, MSDTGP). It builds directly on the authors' prior RTDRAM alignment module [23] for feature registration, incorporates DehazeFormer blocks [30] into a U-shaped encoder for feature-level dehazing guided by the atmospheric scattering model, and adapts the Top-k selection Transformer [33] and PACconv [45] for continuous-scale upsampling. The key conceptual departure is performing dehazing at the feature level (predicting K and B factors on shallow features) rather than at the pixel level, and integrating temporal and spatial self-attention in a sequential channel-then-spatial fashion to capture long-range spatiotemporal dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The end-to-end formulation avoids the error accumulation inherent in cascaded dehazing-then-SR pipelines, which is particularly important for satellite videos where weak textures are further degraded by haze. The continuous-scale capability (arbitrary scale factors rather than fixed integers) addresses a practical need in satellite video applications where continuous zooming is common. However, the evaluation is limited to a single satellite platform (Jilin-1) with only nine scenes, and all baselines are TSP cascades rather than true end-to-end hazy VSR methods, making it difficult to isolate how much of the gain comes from the unified architecture versus simply having more parameters or better training. Open questions include generalization to other satellite sensors, computational cost of the multi-branch attention at video scale, and whether the feature-level dehazing prior transfers to other atmospheric degradations (e.g., fog, rain).
