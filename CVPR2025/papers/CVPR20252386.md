## Problem

Existing pseudo-label guided multi-view clustering methods implicitly assume both that generated pseudo-labels are correct and that cross-view sample correspondences are perfectly aligned. In practice, unsupervised models inevitably produce noisy pseudo-labels (NPP), and real-world multi-view data suffers from imperfect cross-view alignment (NCP), causing the model to overfit false supervision and false positive pairs, which severely degrades clustering performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252386/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-extracted multi-view feature vectors of varying dimensions per view (e.g., 20-dim GIST and 59-dim PHOG for Scene15, 4096-dim VGGNet image features and 300-dim sentence CNN text features for NUS-WIDE) as unlabeled input. It outputs cluster assignments obtained by applying K-means to the concatenated view-specific representations after semantic-level alignment, evaluated with ACC, NMI, and ARI metrics.

## Method

ROLL operates in two stages: a Pseudo-Label Learning (PLL) warm-up that trains view-specific autoencoder encoders with reconstruction loss and vanilla InfoNCE contrastive loss, then extracts pseudo-labels via K-means on shared cluster centers; and a Learning from Noisy Pseudo-Labels (LNPL) fine-tuning stage that introduces two key losses. The noise-tolerance pseudo-label learning (NPL) loss weights the cross-entropy by a cross-view correspondence coefficient (the dot product of predicted probability distributions across views), down-weighting samples whose pseudo-labels are inconsistent across views. The robust multi-view contrastive learning (RCL) loss generalizes between InfoNCE (r→0) and MAE (r→1) via a tunable exponent r, reducing the model's attention to hard/noisy pairs while retaining focus on informative ones, thereby mitigating overfitting to false cross-view correspondences.

## Results

On the five multi-view datasets, ROLL achieves the best clustering performance across nearly all settings. At 0% noise, ROLL reaches 82.90% ACC / 83.16% NMI / 74.79% ARI on CUB, 47.14% ACC on Scene-15, and 71.06% ACC on NUS-WIDE. Under 50% noisy correspondence, ROLL maintains 77.63% ACC on CUB and 45.00% ACC on Scene-15, while most baselines drop by 10–30 points. Even at the extreme 80% noise rate, ROLL retains 78.40% ACC on CUB and 81.87% ACC on XMediaNet, substantially outperforming all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROLL outperforms all 11 baselines on the majority of dataset–noise-rate combinations. The closest competitor is RMCNC (the SOTA method designed specifically for noisy correspondence), which ROLL surpasses by 7.6–10.5 ACC points on CUB at 50–80% noise (77.63 vs. 69.97 at 50%; 78.40 vs. 67.73 at 80%) and by 3.4–7.4 points on Scene-15. On XMediaNet at 0% noise, SCM-RE (80.33) and MVCAN (79.62) are within 1–2 points of ROLL (80.79), but at 50% noise ROLL pulls ahead to 82.24 while RMCNC drops to 45.44. Most non-robust baselines (MvCLN, MFLVC, DCP, DealMVC, CVCL, MSESC) suffer catastrophic degradation at 50–80% noise, with ACC values falling below 35% on Scene-15 and below 50% on CUB, whereas ROLL degrades gracefully. ICMVC is particularly fragile, dropping to near-chance levels (16.27% ACC on CUB at 50% noise) and running out of memory on XMediaNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROLL extends the pseudo-label guided multi-view clustering line (PGCL, DuaLGR, PE-MV-DCFCM) by explicitly addressing the assumption that pseudo-labels are correct, and simultaneously extends RMCNC's noisy-correspondence framework by incorporating pseudo-label noise into the learning objective. The robust multi-view contrastive loss (Eq. 11) is a principled generalization of RMCNC's noise-tolerance contrastive loss, interpolating between InfoNCE (r→0) and MAE (r→1) to balance informativeness and robustness. The key conceptual departure is the joint treatment of two noise sources—noisy pseudo-labels and noisy cross-view correspondence—that prior methods have addressed in isolation or not at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies and formalizes a practically critical gap: real-world multi-view data almost always suffers from both imperfect pseudo-label predictions and imperfect cross-view alignment, yet prior methods assumed at least one of these was clean. The consistent performance advantage across five datasets and four noise levels (including 80%) demonstrates that the two-stage PLL→LNPL pipeline with robust losses is a viable recipe for noisy multi-view settings. Open questions include whether the two-stage training can be unified into a single end-to-end objective, how the method scales to very large or incomplete multi-view datasets, and whether the cross-view correspondence weight in NPL can be learned adaptively rather than computed from probability distributions.
