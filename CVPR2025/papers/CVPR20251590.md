## Problem

Scaling multi-concept customized text-to-image generation faces a fundamental trade-off: existing methods either incur exponential fine-tuning complexity as the number of concepts grows, or compromise on generation quality through concept omission, blending, and layout degradation. The root cause is denoising deviation—customized concepts, trained on only 3–5 single-object reference images, pull the diffusion process away from its normal trajectory, and this deviation compounds as more concepts are introduced due to spatial competition and low co-occurrence probability.

## Input / Output

At fine-tuning, each concept is individually customized from 3–5 reference images and stored as a compact latent textual feature vector (the K and V projections of two token positions) in a concept bank. At inference, the user provides a single text prompt naming multiple concepts (e.g., 'V1* dog sitting next to V2* castle with V3* sunglasses'), and the system retrieves the corresponding stored features, blends them into a base latent textual encoding, and produces a single image containing all specified subjects in a coherent layout. The method also accepts optional spatial layout conditions for layout-to-image generation.

## Method

LATEXBLEND operates in a 'Latent Textual space'—the feature space immediately after the text encoder and linear projection matrices but before cross-attention—identified as a pivotal point that carries sufficient concept information without being too deep to require costly model merging. During single-concept fine-tuning, a dual-flow design is used: a learnable concept flow F_c (with tunable projection matrices) and a frozen base flow F_b (with pre-trained projections and a generic article replacing the identifier token). The concept flow's output h_c is blended into the base flow's output by replacing the latent features at the concept token positions, forcing all concept information to concentrate in h_c while gradients flow only through h_c. A pool of 7 prompt templates of varying lengths is sampled during training to ensure position invariance of h_c. At multi-concept inference, stored h_c vectors are simply spliced into the base latent textual features at the corresponding token positions—no retraining or model merging is needed—so fine-tuning cost scales linearly and inference cost is constant regardless of concept count. An optional blending guidance term adjusts cross-attention maps during sampling to maximize identity binding within each concept and minimize spatial overlap between different concepts, further reducing identity mixing as concept count increases.

## Results

In the user study (20 cases, 25 participants, 0–5 scale), LATEXBLEND achieves the highest scores across all three criteria: Concept Alignment 4.33, Prompt Alignment 4.16, and Overall Quality 4.76, compared to the next-best MuDI (3.24 / 2.83 / 3.54). On quantitative image-similarity metrics, LATEXBLEND attains S_CLIP^I = 0.8052 and S_DINO = 0.6564, substantially outperforming all baselines in concept fidelity. Computationally, fine-tuning cost grows linearly with the number of concepts, and no additional inference cost is incurred as concepts are added, unlike OMG whose inference cost scales proportionally.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LATEXBLEND outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. In the user study, it leads MuDI (the closest competitor) by +1.09 on Concept Alignment, +1.33 on Prompt Alignment, and +1.22 on Overall Quality. Cones 2 achieves the best Prompt Alignment among baselines (3.26) but the worst Concept Alignment (1.92), reflecting its tendency to sacrifice subject fidelity for scene coherence. OMG scores well on Prompt Alignment (3.66) but collapses on Overall Quality (1.53) due to its heavy reliance on a segmentation model and high inference cost that scales with the number of concepts. Mix-of-Show and MuDI both suffer from structural degradation (single-object-centric layouts) as noted in qualitative comparisons. On computational efficiency, LATEXBLEND's linear fine-tuning cost contrasts sharply with the exponential growth of Custom Diffusion, CutMix, and MuDI (which require joint training per concept combination), and its zero additional inference cost contrasts with OMG's proportional inference overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

LATEXBLEND builds directly on the Custom Diffusion paradigm [25], which showed that fine-tuning only the identifier token embedding and cross-attention projection matrices suffices for single-concept implantation. The key departure is identifying the latent textual space (post text-encoder, post linear projection) as the correct level of abstraction for multi-concept integration, rather than merging full diffusion branches (OMG, Mix-of-Show) or performing joint multi-concept training (Custom Diffusion, CutMix, MuDI). The auxiliary base flow that rectifies gradient propagation to consolidate concept information into a compact token-level representation is a novel training strategy not present in prior customization work, and the blending guidance mechanism extends attention-map alignment ideas from [7, 35] to the multi-concept setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in personalized image generation: existing methods either scale exponentially in fine-tuning cost or incur prohibitive inference overhead as concept count grows, while LATEXBLEND achieves linear fine-tuning complexity and zero additional inference cost. The diagnosis of 'denoising deviation' as the root cause of structural degradation in customized generation provides a useful conceptual framework for understanding why multi-concept synthesis is harder than single-concept. However, the approach remains bounded by the pre-trained model's capacity to handle complex multi-object prompts, and the method has been validated primarily on 30 subjects with up to ~5 concepts, leaving open whether the compact latent textual representation remains sufficient at much larger scales or for highly dissimilar concept categories.
