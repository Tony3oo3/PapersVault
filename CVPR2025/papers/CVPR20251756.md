## Problem

Egocentric and exocentric videos of the same activity exhibit large visual discrepancies—first-person views capture only local hand-object interactions while third-person views show global context, and certain actions (laughing, sneezing) produce no distinct visual cues in the ego view. Existing Ego-Exo alignment methods rely on visual matching or text anchors, which fail when visual content does not overlap across views. The paper identifies that audio signals remain consistent across views but are insufficiently discriminative on their own, motivating a sound-bridged alignment approach with contextual grounding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired egocentric and exocentric video clips with their corresponding audio tracks, plus a semantic text description (shooting script) per pair. A frozen BEATs audio encoder and a CLIP-initialized ViT-B/16 video encoder produce fixed-length features, while Llama3-8B generates closed-caption-style sound descriptions (e.g., "[Eating the snack] (munching)") from the text, with frequent concepts filtered out. The 256-dimensional sound-aware visual and text representations are used for retrieval ranking (Ego-to-Exo, Exo-to-Ego, text-to-video) and, via a linear probe, for scene classification (ego) and action classification (exo). For tightly synchronized pairs (CharadesEgo), Dynamic Time Warping aligns the two audio sequences before cross-attention.

## Method

SoundBridge introduces two context-aware sound modules that share a multi-head cross-attention architecture but operate on different modalities. The sound-vision module uses audio features as Query and video features as Key/Value, producing a sound-aware visual representation that encodes what is happening acoustically in the context of what is seen. The sound-text module prompts an LLM to generate closed-caption sound descriptions from the semantic text, filters out dataset-frequent concepts, then cross-attends the sound-caption features (Query) with the semantic-text features (Key/Value) to yield a sound-aware text anchor. Training uses two InfoNCE-style contrastive losses (ego-to-text and exo-to-text) that pull each view's sound-aware visual feature toward its paired sound-aware text anchor while pushing it away from other anchors in the batch. The key design choice is using the sound-aware text representation—rather than raw audio or raw text—as the alignment anchor, which the ablation shows outperforms all alternatives by providing both semantic context and discriminative acoustic detail.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo video retrieval task, SoundBridge achieves 50.50% average top-1 accuracy on EgoExoLearn (50.27% Eg2Ex, 50.73% Ex2Eg) and 74.65% on CharadesEgo (77.66% Eg2Ex, 71.63% Ex2Eg), becoming the first method to exceed 50% on EgoExoLearn. On text-video retrieval, it reaches 72.28% average on EgoExoLearn and 64.25% on CharadesEgo. For downstream tasks, the learned sound-aware features yield 51.06% top-1 on egocentric scene recognition (CharadesEgo) and 56.25% on exocentric action recognition (EgoExoLearn).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoundBridge outperforms the strongest Ego-Exo baselines EgoExo [12] and EgoInstructor [34] by 8.13% and 15.93% on Ego-to-Exo retrieval, and by 13.71% and 18.88% on Exo-to-Ego retrieval on EgoExoLearn, respectively. On CharadesEgo, the gains over EgoExo are 9.34% (Eg2Ex) and 9.57% (Ex2Eg). For text-video retrieval, SoundBridge surpasses EgoExo by ~30.5% and EgoInstructor by ~23.5% in overall accuracy across both datasets, with the largest gains on queries where multiple nearest-neighbor videos share similar visual content but differ in audio. On downstream scene recognition, SoundBridge (51.06%) beats EgoInstructor (46.51%) and LaViLa (41.37%), but on exocentric action recognition it trails the task-specific VideoMAEv2 (62.50%) by 6.25%, as the latter is trained on a much larger action dataset. Pre-trained exocentric models (FrozenInTime, InternVideo) and egocentric models (LaViLa, EgoVLP) are consistently outperformed across all retrieval and recognition tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ego-Exo video alignment paradigm established by EgoExo (implicit view-dependent text alignment) and EgoInstructor (explicit mutual text anchor alignment) by introducing audio as a bridging modality. The sound-vision module follows the cross-attention pattern from audio-enhanced retrieval work (Ibrahimi et al., Eclipse), while the sound-text module is novel in using an LLM to generate closed-caption-style sound descriptions that condition the textual anchor. The key conceptual departure from prior Ego-Exo methods is the observation that audio signals remain consistent across views even when visual content diverges significantly, making sound a more reliable alignment signal than vision alone for activities with weak egocentric visual cues (e.g., sneezing, laughing).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of visual-only Ego-Exo alignment: activities that produce minimal first-person visual cues (sneezing, laughing, subtle hand-object interactions) are poorly aligned by existing methods. The sound-aware representation space also transfers to downstream tasks (scene recognition, action recognition), suggesting the learned features capture complementary cross-view information beyond retrieval. Limitations include dependence on LLM quality for sound caption generation, the need for DTW-based audio synchronization for loosely paired videos, and evaluation on only two datasets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer, more asynchronous video pairs, real-time robotic applications, and whether the LLM-generated captions could be replaced by learned audio-to-text modules to reduce inference cost.
