## Problem

Evaluating Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) currently relies on human-annotated QA benchmarks that are expensive and limited in scale, or on MLLM-as-a-Judge approaches that suffer from verbosity and self-preference biases. No existing framework can evaluate MLLMs in a fully unsupervised manner—using only raw images without any human-designed questions or answers—while simultaneously mitigating these inherent evaluation biases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250950/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a set of unlabeled images (25 per evaluation run) and a pool of 6 MLLMs (5 closed-source: GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Claude-3-Haiku, Gemini-1.5-Pro; 1 open-source: Llama-3.2-11B-Vision) as input. It produces a learned score list ranking all models, which is validated against human-annotated benchmark scores (MMStar, ScienceQA) via Pearson/Spearman correlation and Permutation Entropy. Each model alternates roles as candidate and reviewer across iterations, and no model judges its own outputs.

## Method

UPME operates through three coupled components. First, a peer review mechanism randomly selects two candidate models and one review model per iteration; the review model auto-generates a question from an image, the candidates answer, and the reviewer scores both responses. Second, a Vision-Language Scoring System evaluates each answer along three axes: pairwise Response Correctness (binary/tie scoring), Visual Understanding and Reasoning (a four-dimensional assessment of captioning, reasoning, grounding, and object relationships), and Image-Text Correlation (CLIP similarity between the image and the textual response), combined via weighted sum. Third, a Dynamic Weight Optimization loop assigns each model an initial confidence weight and iteratively minimizes MSE between the weighted review scores and the model's own weight, so that better-performing models receive greater influence in the final score aggregation. This iterative refinement converges within ~30 epochs and produces a bias-reduced ranking that aligns closely with human judgments.

## Results

On MMStar, UPME achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.944 and Spearman correlation of 0.972 with human-annotated scores, substantially outperforming the best individual model judge (GPT-4o at 0.878/0.875) and the strongest method baseline (PRD at 0.838/0.864). On ScienceQA, UPME reaches Pearson 0.814 and Spearman 0.886, again surpassing all baselines including GPT-4o (0.617/0.625) and PRD (0.636/0.694). Permutation Entropy, measuring score-list unpredictability, drops to 0.141 on MMStar and 0.422 on ScienceQA for UPME, indicating the most stable and human-aligned rankings among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPME outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest method baseline PRD, UPME gains +0.106 Pearson and +0.108 Spearman on MMStar (0.944 vs. 0.838; 0.972 vs. 0.864) and +0.178 Pearson and +0.192 Spearman on ScienceQA (0.814 vs. 0.636; 0.886 vs. 0.694). Compared to the best individual model judge GPT-4o, UPME improves Pearson by +0.066 on MMStar and +0.197 on ScienceQA, while also reducing Permutation Entropy from 0.159 to 0.141 on MMStar. The original Peer Review mechanism (without weight optimization or visual scoring) lags UPME by a wide margin—0.725 vs. 0.944 Pearson on MMStar—confirming that the dynamic weight optimization and vision-language scoring system are critical. Classical aggregation methods (Majority Vote, Rating Vote) perform worst among method baselines, with Majority Vote showing the highest Permutation Entropy (1.299 on MMStar), indicating the least stable rankings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-as-a-Judge paradigm (itself an extension of LLM-as-a-Judge) by removing the dependency on human-designed QA pairs, which all prior automated evaluation methods still require. It builds on peer review mechanisms for LLM evaluation (PRE, PRD, Majority/Rating Vote) and incorporates vision-specific scoring via CLIP and multi-dimensional visual reasoning assessment, which prior text-only judge frameworks lack. The dynamic weight optimization via MSE-based consistency is borrowed from iterative rating methods but applied here to align confidence weights with estimated scores across a multimodal model pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UPME offers a practical path toward scalable MLLM evaluation without the bottleneck of human-annotated QA pairs, and its demonstrated mitigation of verbosity and self-preference biases (p-values shifting from <0.001 to >0.05) addresses a well-known reliability concern in LLM-as-a-Judge systems. However, the evaluation is limited to six models and two datasets with only 25 images per run, leaving open whether the framework generalizes to larger model pools, video/audio modalities, or weaker model ensembles where the reviewer itself may lack sufficient visual grounding. The notably lower ScienceQA correlation (0.814 vs. 0.944 on MMStar) also suggests the method's effectiveness is tied to visual dependency, raising questions about its applicability to more text-heavy reasoning tasks.
