## Problem

Despite the strong performance of auto-regressive multimodal large language models (MLLMs) on vision-language tasks, the internal mechanism by which visual and linguistic information are integrated within these models remains largely unexplored. Specifically, it is unknown where in the transformer layers and through what attention pathways the two modalities are combined to produce the final answer prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252841/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image encoded by CLIP-ViT-L-336px into a fixed set of visual patch tokens, concatenated before the tokenized question text in a single input sequence. The output is a single-word or short-phrase answer generated at the last position of the sequence. The analysis itself produces layer-wise maps of cross-modal information flow, quantified as the relative change in answer probability (p_c%) when attention edges between specific position sets are blocked within a 9-layer window.

## Method

The paper applies an attention knockout technique (extending Geva et al.'s method for LLMs) to MLLMs: at each transformer layer, specific attention edges between position sets (all image patches, question-relevant image patches, other image patches, question tokens, and the last position) are set to −∞ in the causal mask, and the resulting drop in answer probability reveals functional information flow. Image patches are partitioned into 'related' (containing bounding boxes of question-relevant objects) and 'other' patches to distinguish general from targeted visual integration. A logit-lens analysis additionally projects hidden states at the last position through the unembedding matrix at every layer to track when the answer becomes predictable. Experiments span four LLaVA-series models (7B/13B/8B with Vicuna or Llama3 backbones) across six GQA question types, revealing a two-stage integration: general whole-image features flow into question positions in the lowest layers (~0–4), then question-specific object features flow in around layer 10, after which the fused multimodal representation propagates to the last position in middle layers for final prediction.

## Results

Using attention knockout on LLaVA-1.5-13b across six GQA question types, the paper identifies a two-stage cross-modal integration: blocking image→question attention in layers ~0–4 reduces answer probability by ~60% on average (first stage, general visual features), while a second drop of ~21% around layer 10 corresponds to targeted object-relevant visual information. Blocking question→last-position attention in middle layers reduces prediction probability by up to ~30%, confirming that the integrated multimodal representation flows to the final position for prediction. Logit-lens analysis shows the correct (lowercase) answer probability rises sharply from near 0 to 20–70% in middle layers, then the capitalized form takes over in higher layers, indicating semantic inference precedes syntactic refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability study rather than a method-comparison paper, so there are no traditional baselines to beat or match. Instead, the authors validate their findings for consistency across four MLLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5-7b, LLaVA-1.5-13b, LLaVA-v1.6-Vicuna-7b, and Llama3-LLaVA-NEXT-8b) and six diverse GQA question types spanning perception, spatial reasoning, and logical inference. The two-stage integration pattern (general→specific visual information transfer) and the middle-layer propagation to the last position are observed consistently across all models and tasks. A notable cross-model difference is that the smaller LLaVA-1.5-7b exhibits less information flow from non-object image patches to question positions in the first stage compared to LLaVA-1.5-13b, suggesting model size modulates the breadth of initial visual integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention knockout method from Geva et al. [20], which was designed to trace factual recall in single-modality LLMs, to the multimodal setting by blocking cross-modal attention edges between image and question positions. It also adapts the logit lens technique [1] to monitor answer emergence across layers in MLLMs. Prior interpretability work on multimodal models has examined isolated phenomena (safety mechanisms, redundant visual tokens, object localization) or treated the model as a black box, but no prior study has systematically traced the full spatial and temporal information flow between modalities within auto-regressive MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage integration finding—general visual features first, then question-specific object regions—provides a concrete mechanistic account that can guide targeted interventions such as multimodal information editing, token pruning, or architecture design. However, the analysis is limited to single-word VQA answers on the GQA dataset and the LLaVA model family, leaving open whether the same two-stage pattern holds for open-ended generation, longer-form answers, or fundamentally different multimodal architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder or diffusion-based). The attention knockout methodology itself is a reusable tool that could be applied to other multimodal tasks and model families.
