## Problem

Existing RGB-NIR imaging systems use separate cameras at different poses, causing pixel misalignment that forces depth-dependent registration and creates a chicken-and-egg problem between depth estimation and spectral alignment. No large-scale dataset provides pixel-aligned RGB-NIR stereo pairs with synchronized LiDAR ground truth across diverse lighting conditions, limiting the practical utility of cross-spectral vision for robotics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250901/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system captures pixel-aligned RGB and NIR stereo image pairs at 720×480 resolution (5–10 Hz) using dichroic-prism cameras with independent auto-exposure per spectral band, plus sparse LiDAR point clouds (OUSTER OS1) as ground-truth depth. The image-fusion method outputs a standard 3-channel RGB image directly consumable by pretrained networks without finetuning; the feature-fusion method outputs dense disparity maps from a modified RAFT-Stereo network. Temporal synchronization between stereo cameras is maintained below 1 µs via PTP.

## Method

Two complementary fusion strategies are proposed. The image-fusion method converts RGB to HSV, encodes both RGB and NIR images into 256-channel ResNet features, computes spatially-varying blending weights α and β via an attention-based MLP decoder, and combines the brightness channel with NIR before guided filtering—requiring no downstream model finetuning and running at 100 fps. The feature-fusion method modifies RAFT-Stereo by inserting an attentional feature-fusion module (Dai et al.) between RGB and NIR feature extractors, then alternates between fused-feature and NIR-only correlation volumes at each GRU iteration, inspired by cross-spectral time-of-flight imaging. This alternating cost-volume strategy, validated by ablation, outperforms using either volume alone and leverages NIR's robustness to lighting while retaining RGB's chromatic detail.

## Results

On the real-world pixel-aligned RGB-NIR stereo dataset, the proposed image fusion method achieves a depth MAE of 4.034 m with pretrained RAFT-Stereo, outperforming single-modality RGB (5.311 m) and NIR (4.240 m) inputs, as well as the HSV-based baseline (4.161 m). For object detection with pretrained YOLO, the fused image yields 0.809 mAP, surpassing RGB-only (0.756) and NIR-only (0.703) predictions. The feature fusion variant further reduces depth MAE to 3.619 m (RAFT-Stereo) and 2.446 m (IGEV++), demonstrating consistent gains across three stereo architectures and six lighting conditions (Table 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed image fusion method outperforms all six baselines on both depth estimation and object detection. The closest depth-estimation competitor is the HSV channel-blending baseline [15] (4.161 m vs. 4.034 m MAE), while the closest detection competitor is the adaptive NIR-visible fusion of Awad et al. [1] (0.773 vs. 0.809 mAP). The YCrCb fusion [20] and Bayesian fusion [62] perform substantially worse, with depth MAE of 5.209 m and 7.409 m respectively, and mAP of 0.571 and 0.745. DarkVision [25] and VGG-NIR [30], both learning-based fusion approaches, also trail the proposed method by 0.16–2.16 m in depth MAE and 0.047–0.083 in mAP. Notably, the feature fusion variant (which requires fine-tuning) further narrows the gap to 3.619 m MAE with RAFT-Stereo, confirming that the pixel-aligned property of the dataset is the key enabler for these gains over methods designed for misaligned RGB-NIR pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of RGB-NIR imaging systems (Kinect, RealSense, multi-view setups) that place RGB and NIR sensors at different poses, creating a 'chicken-and-egg' problem where depth estimation requires spectral alignment and spectral alignment requires depth. By using dichroic-prism-based cameras that capture pixel-aligned RGB and NIR on separate CMOS sensors, the authors eliminate this circular dependency at the hardware level. Methodologically, the image fusion approach extends the HSV-channel blending baseline [15] with learned spatially-varying weights via an attention-based MLP, while the feature fusion method modifies RAFT-Stereo [36] by inserting attentional feature fusion [13] and an alternating correlation-volume strategy inspired by cross-spectral time-of-flight imaging [5].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary contribution is the first large-scale pixel-aligned RGB-NIR stereo dataset (90K real frames across 82 scenes plus 2,238 synthetic sequences) with LiDAR ground truth, which removes the alignment burden from downstream research and enables direct comparison of fusion strategies. The practical finding that a lightweight image-fusion module (no downstream finetuning) already outperforms all prior RGB-NIR fusion methods on depth estimation and object detection makes the approach immediately deployable with existing pretrained models. Open questions include whether the benefits generalize beyond the specific JAI camera hardware, whether the sparse LiDAR supervision is sufficient for dense 3D tasks, and how the approach scales to higher resolutions or longer-range scenes where active NIR illumination becomes less effective.
