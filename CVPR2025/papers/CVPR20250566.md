## Problem

Pre-trained vision models (PVMs) are central to robot learning, yet the optimal combination of pre-training method and data source remains unclear. The paper reveals that while DINO and iBOT outperform MAE across visuomotor and perception tasks, they degrade substantially on non-(single-)object-centric (NOC) data due to a diminished ability to learn object-centric representations—a capability strongly correlated with manipulation success. No existing method effectively induces objectness from diverse NOC data sources such as scene-centric, web-crawled, or ego-centric datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224, processed by a ViT-B/16 backbone) from any data source—object-centric (ImageNet), scene-centric (COCO, Open Images), web-crawled (CC12M), or ego-centric video frames (Ego4D). It produces two levels of output: patch-level visual features enriched with semantic objectness, and object-level slot representations formed by attentive pooling of patches assigned to the same prototype. Downstream tasks consume these features via trainable attentive pooling (cross-attention over all patch tokens) rather than the [CLS] token, ensuring fair comparison across methods whose [CLS] tokens may lack training signal.

## Method

SlotMIM extends iBOT's masked-image-modeling self-distillation with three key modifications to induce object-centric representations from NOC data. First, it reduces the number of prototypes (e.g., 512 instead of 8192) to create a semantic bottleneck that forces patch tokens to form compositional object concepts rather than fine-grained patterns. Second, it introduces a cross-view consistency loss that matches overlapping patches between two augmented views via ROIAlign and enforces identical prototype assignments, providing explicit view-invariance guidance absent in iBOT's within-view objective. Third, it groups patches into object-level slots using prototypes as queries in an attentive pooling operation (analogous to slot attention), then applies MoCo-style contrastive learning on slots with matching cluster assignments to improve discriminativeness. The final loss symmetrizes the sum of within-view patch self-distillation (iBOT loss), cross-view patch consistency, and slot-level contrastive learning, with weights λ₁=0.5 and λ₂=1.

## Results

At 241K pre-training scale, DINO and iBOT lead across all six evaluation tasks (Franka Kitchen, Meta-World, Pascal VOC, ADE20K, COCO detection/segmentation), while MAE achieves only moderate manipulation scores and poor ADE20K performance. SlotMIM consistently outperforms all prior methods regardless of data type, and at 1.28M ego-centric scale it already surpasses MVP (4.6M) and VC-1 (5.6M) on manipulation success rates. On navigation, SlotMIM trained on 4M DetSoup achieves 62.0% ObjectNav and 69.8% ImageNav, beating MVP (51.2%/64.7%) and VC-1 (55.4%/67.9%) by 6.6% and 1.9% respectively. On ADE20K and COCO, SlotMIM surpasses iBOT pre-trained on 12M ImageNet-21K using only 3× less data, demonstrating superior data efficiency and continued improvement with scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlotMIM outperforms MAE [29] across all tasks; MAE shows only moderate manipulation success and poor ADE20K segmentation, and while it avoids the over-compression issue at scale, it never matches SlotMIM's peak performance. Against DINO [13] and iBOT [80], SlotMIM wins decisively on NOC data (scene-centric, web-crawled, ego-centric) where DINO/iBOT suffer substantial degradation due to loss of object-centric representations; on object-centric ImageNet the gap narrows but SlotMIM still leads. Compared to BEiT [4] and SplitMask [22], SlotMIM achieves higher scores on both manipulation and perception tasks at the 241K scale. Against the strongest robot-learning baselines MVP [55] and VC-1 [45], SlotMIM pre-trained on just 241K ego-centric samples already exceeds their performance with 4.6M and 5.6M samples respectively, and at 1.28M it achieves the highest manipulation success rates among all compared methods. On navigation, SlotMIM (DetSoup, 4M) surpasses both MVP and VC-1 by 6.6% on ObjectNav and 1.9% on ImageNav, and even rivals their larger ViT-L variants. On ADE20K and COCO detection/segmentation, SlotMIM trained on 4M DetSoup surpasses iBOT trained on 12M ImageNet-21K, confirming that NOC data leveraged with the right method is more scalable than previously assumed.

## Relation to Prior Work

SlotMIM directly extends iBOT's within-view patch-level self-distillation by repurposing it for unsupervised object discovery, adding a cross-view consistency objective for semantic alignment, and introducing object-level contrastive learning over slots (inspired by slot attention). Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant robot-learning pre-training paradigm (MAE on ego-centric data, as in MVP and VC-1) by demonstrating that DINO/iBOT-style methods are superior but fragile on non-object-centric data, and by showing that an explicit objectness bottleneck restores their robustness. The empirical benchmark also extends prior robot-learning evaluations (which focused on manipulation) to include navigation and perception tasks, providing a more complete picture of PVM transferability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges two widely-held assumptions in robot learning—that MAE is the optimal pre-training method and that ego-centric data is the best choice—offering a unified framework (SlotMIM) that works across object-centric, scene-centric, web-crawled, and ego-centric data. The inverse-scaling observation for DINO/iBOT (performance degrades with more data due to over-compression) is a practical warning for the field's push toward ever-larger pre-training corpora. Open questions include whether the object-centric bottleneck is the right abstraction for all embodied tasks (e.g., deformable objects, fluid dynamics), whether the approach extends to 3D or multi-modal settings, and whether the data-type-to-task alignment principle (ego-centric for manipulation, scene-centric for navigation) generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks.
