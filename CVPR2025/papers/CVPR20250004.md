## Problem

Existing data generation methods for embodied AI produce either static scenes or robot actions in isolation, requiring manual pairing to form coherent manipulation episodes. No prior work jointly generates a physically grounded static environment and its corresponding robot action sequence from a single text instruction, leaving a critical gap in scalable, automated dataset creation for robotic manipulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language manipulation instruction (e.g., 'close the cabinet to 30 percent') and produces a dynamic scene: a static configuration (object position in R³, quaternion orientation in R⁴, class, size, 32-dim shape embedding, initial/goal state, robot base and end-effector poses, gripper state, room layout) paired with a K-step action sequence. Actions are expressed as residual deltas (Δp, Δq) between consecutive end-effector poses rather than absolute coordinates, making them transferable across different static scenes. All generated scenes are verified in Isaac Sim and only successful episodes are retained.

## Method

DynScene decomposes generation into two sequential diffusion phases: a text-conditioned U-Net (with BERT instruction embeddings in attention layers) generates the static scene, and a scene-conditioned diffusion model then generates the action trajectory. Three physics-aware refinements ensure plausibility: layout sampling selects collision-free room configurations from a pre-built pool by minimizing position distance to the predicted layout, quaternion quantization rounds object orientations to fixed angular intervals to prevent gravity-induced collapse, and residual action representation decouples actions from absolute scene geometry. This decoupling enables action augmentation—multiple diverse trajectories can be generated per static scene, and actions learned at one state (e.g., 10 cm) can be applied to unseen states (e.g., 20 cm) by merging residual sequences. Generated episodes are filtered through Isaac Sim physics simulation, and agents (PerAct, PerAct-PSA) are trained exclusively on verified successful scenes.

## Results

DynScene generates dynamic manipulation scenes in 2.52 s on average (26.8× faster than the 67.50 s human collection time in ARNOLD) with a 69.50% task success rate (1.84× higher than ARNOLD's 37.50%). In terms of action diversity, DynScene outperforms ARNOLD on all three metrics: Fréchet Distance (30.32 vs 28.39), DTW (55.25 vs 47.58), and Spatial Coverage (3.62 vs 2.83). When used to augment training data, DynScene boosts PerAct's average success rate from 29.14% to 34.79% (+19.4%) and PerAct-PSA from 41.46% to 45.62% (+10.0%) on the ARNOLD benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ARNOLD (human-collected data), DynScene wins decisively on generation efficiency (2.52 s vs 67.50 s per scene, 26.8× faster) and scene success rate (69.50% vs 37.50%, 1.84× higher), while also producing more diverse actions across all three diversity metrics (FD, DTW, SC). Against DiffuScene (static scene generation paired with ARNOLD actions), DynScene achieves ~10× faster generation (2.95 s vs 30.24 s) and 3.5× higher success rate (69.50% vs 20.04%), though DiffuScene marginally edges out on object entropy (0.94 vs 0.93) and scene entropy (0.93 vs 0.91). For downstream agent training, DynScene-augmented data consistently improves all agent variants: PerAct gains +5.65 points (29.14→34.79) and PerAct-PSA gains +4.16 points (41.46→45.62) in average success rate, with the largest improvements on challenging tasks like Open Cabinet (+25.00 for PerAct-PSA) and Transfer Water (+7.38 for PerAct-PSA). The one area where DynScene does not dominate is novel-state generalization, where PerAct(DS+AR) scores 3.30% vs 4.58% for PerAct(AR), reflecting out-of-distribution limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynScene sits at the intersection of two previously independent research threads: diffusion-based static scene generation (DiffuScene, PhyScene) and diffusion-based robot action synthesis (SynthER, MTDIFF). The key departure is the joint formulation—generating a static scene and a physically consistent action sequence as a single 'dynamic scene'—where the action diffusion model is conditioned on the generated scene rather than operating in isolation. The residual action representation (deltas in end-effector position and quaternion) is a novel formulation borrowed from the idea of relative coordinates, enabling the same action model to be reused across different static configurations for augmentation. For agent training, the paper builds on PerAct and introduces PerAct-PSA, a phase-split variant that trains grasping and manipulation with separate networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 26.8× speedup over human data collection and a 1.84× higher scene success rate than human experts directly address the scalability bottleneck in robotic manipulation dataset creation. However, the evaluation is confined to the ARNOLD benchmark (8 tasks, one simulator), and generalization to novel states remains weak (3.30% vs. 4.58% for PerAct), suggesting the framework has not yet been stress-tested beyond its training distribution. Open questions include whether the joint generation approach transfers to other simulators, more complex multi-object interactions, or real-robot deployment, and whether the physics-verification filtering step scales as task complexity grows.
