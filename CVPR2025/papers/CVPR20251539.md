## Problem

Existing Concept-based XAI methods face a fundamental trade-off: methods like ACE produce human-understandable concepts but sacrifice faithfulness through noisy inpainting and heuristic filtering, while MCD provides completeness-guaranteed importance scores but yields grid-aligned, block-like explanations that are difficult for humans to interpret. No prior framework simultaneously achieves both semantically meaningful concept discovery and faithful, completeness-aware importance attribution.

## Input / Output

HU-MCD takes RGB images (224×224, ImageNet-1k) and a pre-trained ResNet-50 as input, using SAM (ViT-H encoder) to generate instance segmentation masks over 400 images per class. The method produces multi-dimensional concept subspaces in the CNN's penultimate hidden layer, per-segment concept activation maps, local concept relevance scores that sum exactly to the model's logits per image, global concept relevance scores that sum to the classifier weight vector per class, and a completeness score quantifying residual unexplained variance in the orthogonal complement subspace.

## Method

HU-MCD discovers concepts by segmenting class images with SAM, extracting the most granular decomposition (masks covering ≥1% of the image), and clustering segment embeddings via Sparse Subspace Clustering in the CNN's hidden feature space, with cluster count determined by the average number of SAM segments per image. To avoid noise from inpainting or rescaling, it employs a CNN-specific input masking scheme that propagates both the image and its mask through each convolutional layer, discarding activations dependent solely on masked regions; the authors modify this by passing the unmasked image to the first convolutional layer (7×7 kernel in ResNet-50) before applying masking, preserving shape context near object boundaries. Concept importance is then computed by adapting the MCD framework: PCA on each cluster's hidden representations yields a subspace basis, and the final hidden layer is decomposed into contributions from each concept subspace plus an orthogonal complement, enabling local relevance (per-image logit decomposition) and global relevance (per-class weight projection) scores that satisfy an exact completeness relation.

## Results

In the human-subject study on ImageNet-1k (10 CIFAR-10-like classes, ResNet-50), HU-MCD achieves 70.24% prediction accuracy, substantially outperforming ACE (42.93%) and MCD (31.22%). HU-MCD also attains the highest percentage of recognizable concepts (67.12%), the highest inner-concept description similarity (0.49, indicating consistent participant descriptions for the same concept), and the lowest intra-concept description similarity (0.28, indicating distinct descriptions across different concepts). All HU-MCD advantages are statistically significant (p < 0.001 for ANOVA; p < 0.05 for pairwise t-tests). On the faithfulness benchmarks (C-Deletion and C-Insertion), HU-MCD also outperforms both baselines, with the sharpest accuracy drop/rise as concepts are removed/inserted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HU-MCD is compared against two baselines: ACE (Ghorbani et al., NeurIPS 2019) and MCD (Vielhaben et al., 2024). On prediction accuracy, HU-MCD (70.24%) beats ACE (42.93%) by ~27 points and MCD (31.22%) by ~39 points, with all pairwise differences statistically significant (p < 0.001). On recognizable concepts, HU-MCD (67.12%) leads ACE (45.66%) and MCD (50.34%), though the gap vs. MCD is smaller (p = 0.001). The description-similarity metrics further confirm HU-MCD's advantage: it achieves the highest inner-concept similarity (0.49 vs. 0.39/0.41) and the lowest intra-concept similarity (0.28 vs. 0.29/0.38), meaning participants both agree on what each concept means and distinguish different concepts more clearly. MCD's notably low prediction accuracy despite moderate recognizability is attributed to its concepts being highly similar to each other, making them hard to tell apart. On the faithfulness benchmarks (C-Deletion and C-Insertion), HU-MCD outperforms both ACE and MCD, showing the steepest accuracy change as concepts are flipped, confirming that its importance scores most faithfully reflect the model's reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

HU-MCD sits at the intersection of two C-XAI lineages: the automatic concept discovery methods (ACE, ICE, CRAFT) that prioritize human-understandable segments, and MCD's multi-dimensional subspace decomposition with completeness guarantees. It departs from ACE by replacing superpixel segmentation with SAM's foundation-model-based instance segmentation, and from MCD by operating on semantically meaningful image regions rather than grid-aligned feature vectors. The CNN-specific input masking scheme from Balasubramanian and Feizi is adapted with a pragmatic modification—propagating the unmasked image through the first convolutional layer before applying the mask—to preserve shape information that SAM's tight boundaries would otherwise lose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete tension in C-XAI: methods that produce understandable concepts (ACE) sacrifice faithfulness through inpainting noise and heuristic filtering, while methods that guarantee completeness (MCD) yield grid-aligned, block-like explanations that are hard for humans to interpret. By combining SAM's interpretable segments with MCD's completeness relation, HU-MCD offers a practical path toward the transparency requirements of the EU AI Act, particularly in high-stakes domains like medical imaging. Key limitations remain: evaluation is restricted to 10 ImageNet classes, concept relevance scoring requires layers followed only by linear operations, and the masking strategy is a heuristic compromise rather than a principled solution. Extending to other modalities, finer-grained taxonomies, and deeper non-linear layers are natural next steps.
