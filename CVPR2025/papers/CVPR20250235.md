## Problem

Existing diffusion-based posterior sampling methods (e.g., DPS) solve the reverse-time SDE with small step sizes, forcing consecutive samples to remain close and limiting each step to correcting only local errors. This causes failures on complex nonlinear inverse problems like phase retrieval, where the sampler converges to low-probability regions of the prior that are consistent with the measurement but visually poor. The paper asks whether decoupling consecutive sampling steps can enable the sampler to explore a larger solution space and correct global errors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement y = A(x_0) + n (with isotropic Gaussian noise, β_y = 0.05) from a known forward model A, a pre-trained diffusion model (pixel-space EDM or latent-space LDM-VQ4), and optionally a text prompt for text-conditioned Stable Diffusion v2.1. It outputs posterior samples x_0 | y at 256×256 (FFHQ, ImageNet), 768×768 (text-conditioned LDM), or MRI k-space data. For phase retrieval, it uses an oversampling rate of 2.0 and reports the best of four independent samples. The method also extends to discrete diffusion on quantized MNIST using Metropolis-Hastings in place of Langevin dynamics.

## Method

DAPS replaces the coupled reverse SDE with a decoupled noise annealing process that recursively samples from the measurement-conditioned time-marginal p(x_t | y). At each step, it (1) solves the unconditional probability flow ODE from x_t to obtain x̂_0(x_t), (2) runs Langevin dynamics to sample x_0|y ~ p(x_0 | x_t, y) using the Gaussian approximation p(x_0|x_t) ≈ N(x̂_0(x_t), r_t²I) plus the measurement likelihood, and (3) re-noises via the forward diffusion to produce x_t ~ N(x_0|y, σ_t²I). Proposition 1 proves this two-step procedure yields valid time-marginals, so as σ_t anneals to zero the samples converge to the true posterior. The key advantage is that consecutive samples x_t and x_{t+Δt} are conditionally independent given x_0, enabling large non-local corrections that the coupled SDE approach cannot make. The method extends naturally to latent space (LatentDAPS) by applying Langevin dynamics in the VAE latent code with the measurement evaluated through the decoder.

## Results

On FFHQ 256, DAPS achieves 30.63 dB PSNR on phase retrieval (vs. 24.52 dB for DPS) and 28.29 dB on HDR reconstruction (vs. 22.16 dB for DPS), representing improvements of ~6 dB and ~6 dB over the strongest diffusion-based baselines respectively. On linear tasks, DAPS leads with 31.12 dB PSNR on Gaussian deblurring and 29.07 dB on 4× super-resolution. On compressed sensing MRI (×4 subsampling), DAPS reaches 31.48 dB PSNR, outperforming the best prior method CSGM (28.78 dB) by 2.70 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPS substantially outperforms DPS [13] across nearly all tasks, with the largest gains on nonlinear problems: +6.11 dB PSNR on phase retrieval (30.63 vs. 24.52) and +6.13 dB on HDR (28.29 vs. 22.16). Among linear-task baselines, DAPS beats DCDP [31] by 0.41 dB on super-resolution and 0.43 dB on Gaussian deblurring, and surpasses DDNM [54] by 1.16 dB on motion deblurring. DAPS is slightly outperformed by FPS-SMC [18] on random-mask inpainting (24.07 vs. 24.86 PSNR) and by RED-diff [35] on nonlinear deblurring (28.29 vs. 30.86 PSNR), though DAPS achieves lower LPIPS (0.155 vs. 0.160) on the latter. In latent space, LatentDAPS is competitive with ReSample [45] and PSLD [42], winning on phase retrieval (27.00 vs. 27.41/22.31 PSNR) and Gaussian deblurring (30.71 vs. 29.61/30.31). On compressed sensing MRI, DAPS leads all baselines by a wide margin, achieving 31.48 dB PSNR at ×4 subsampling versus 28.78 dB for CSGM [23] and 26.13 dB for DPS [13].

## Relation to Prior Work

DAPS departs from the dominant paradigm of diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DPS, DDRM, DDNM, FPS-SMC) that solve a coupled reverse-time SDE, where small step sizes force consecutive samples x_t and x_{t+Δt} to remain close, limiting error correction. Instead, DAPS recursively samples from the measurement-conditioned time-marginal p(x_t|y) at each noise level, decoupling consecutive samples and using Langevin dynamics to explore the solution space more broadly. The method also builds on latent diffusion models (Rombach et al.) to derive LatentDAPS, and connects conceptually to optimization-based solvers like ReSample and DCDP, though DAPS performs full posterior sampling rather than MAP estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling insight addresses a fundamental limitation of SDE-based posterior sampling: the inability to correct global errors accumulated in early denoising steps, which is especially critical for nonlinear inverse problems like phase retrieval and HDR reconstruction where prior methods suffer high failure rates. The practical finding that a simple Gaussian approximation to p(x_0|x_t) achieves near-equivalent quality to the more principled diffusion-score estimation at 6–7× lower cost makes the method broadly deployable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D inverse problems, how it scales to very high-resolution text-to-image restoration with complex conditioning, and whether the number of Langevin steps can be further reduced without quality loss.
