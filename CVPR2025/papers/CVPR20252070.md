## Problem

Existing training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods built on CLIP lack object-level context: they fail to group spatially separated components of the same object (e.g., a truck's wheels and cargo area, or a sheep's body and legs) into a single unified mask. This stems from CLIP's training objective, which emphasizes global image-text alignment over dense, patch-level object structure, making it insufficient for coherent object segmentation under arbitrary user prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252070/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of arbitrary text prompts (class names) as input. Images are processed via sliding-window inference with 224×224 windows and stride 112 (shorter side resized to 336 px, or 560 px for Cityscapes), and per-window patch-text similarity maps are stitched into a full-resolution pixel-level segmentation mask. Both the CLIP ViT-B/16 vision-language model and the DINO ViT-B/8 vision foundation model remain fully frozen throughout inference.

## Method

CASS introduces two complementary mechanisms to inject object-level context into CLIP without any training. First, it treats the DINO attention adjacency matrix as a graph, performs eigendecomposition on each head, and uses Wasserstein distance over sorted eigenvalue distributions plus Hungarian matching to pair structurally complementary VFM and CLIP heads; the matched VFM graph is then reduced to its low-rank components via an energy-based eigenvalue selection and a dynamic eigenscaling function that amplifies dominant eigenvalues while suppressing noise, and the result is aggregated into CLIP's attention with a Wasserstein-distance-weighted coefficient. Second, it exploits CLIP's zero-shot object classification score as an object presence prior to (a) hierarchically cluster text embeddings and nudge the most likely class embedding toward the mean of top-n visually similar patch features, and (b) blend the full-image object presence likelihood into the patch-text similarity logits, ensuring the final mask reflects an object-centered rather than patch-centered perspective.

## Results

CASS achieves a state-of-the-art average mIoU of 44.4 across eight datasets (V21, PC60, C-Obj, V20, PC59, C-Stf, City, ADE), outperforming the best fair training-free baseline ProxyCLIP (41.4) by 3.0 mIoU points. On the most challenging V20 split, CASS gains 5.3 mIoU over the second-best method. In pixel accuracy, CASS reaches 68.9 average pAcc, exceeding ProxyCLIP (66.7) by 2.2 points and CLIPtrase (59.1) by 9.8 points. Notably, CASS also surpasses CLIP-DINOiser (40.3 mIoU), which relies on additional training data and fine-tuning, by 4.1 mIoU points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASS outperforms all 18 compared baselines on every dataset where results are reported. Among fair training-free methods (no extra data, no training), ProxyCLIP is the strongest baseline at 41.4 average mIoU; CASS exceeds it by 3.0 points, with the largest margin on V20 (+5.3 mIoU) and City (+1.3 mIoU). NA-CLIP (39.4) and SCLIP (38.2) trail further behind, while the vanilla CLIP baseline (13.8) is far below all enhanced methods. CASS also surpasses CLIP-DINOiser (40.3 mIoU), which requires ImageNet-1k training data and fine-tuning, by 4.1 mIoU points, demonstrating that the spectral distillation approach is more effective than supervised adaptation. In pixel accuracy (Table 2), CASS leads ProxyCLIP by 2.2 points (68.9 vs. 66.7) and CLIPtrase by 9.8 points (68.9 vs. 59.1), confirming consistent gains across both evaluation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

CASS extends the training-free OVSS line (SCLIP, NA-CLIP, ProxyCLIP, CLIPtrase) by identifying a specific gap—lack of object-level context in CLIP's patch-level attention—and addressing it through spectral graph distillation from a VFM (DINO). The spectral decomposition of VFM attention graphs draws directly from EAGLE and Deep Spectral Methods, which showed that eigenvector-based clustering can recover object-level structure from patch-level features; CASS repurposes this by distilling low-rank spectral components into CLIP's attention rather than using them for direct clustering. The complementary head-matching strategy (pairing structurally contrasting VFM and CLIP heads via Wasserstein distance and Hungarian assignment) departs from the straightforward sequential head alignment used in prior distillation work, and the object presence prior-driven text embedding adjustment builds on CLIP's zero-shot classification capability in a way not previously exploited for dense segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that object-level context can be injected into CLIP's attention in a fully training-free manner, CASS offers a practical, deployable solution for OVSS that avoids the scalability and retraining costs of supervised approaches. The 3.0 mIoU average gain over the prior best training-free method (ProxyCLIP) and consistent improvements across eight datasets validate the object-level context hypothesis. However, the method still depends on CLIP's text encoder quality for arbitrary prompts, incurs additional computational overhead from sliding-window inference and per-head eigendecomposition, and has not been tested on very large-scale or video segmentation settings. Open questions include whether the spectral distillation generalizes to non-ViT backbones or to panoptic/instance segmentation, and whether the complementary matching principle transfers to other cross-model knowledge distillation scenarios.
