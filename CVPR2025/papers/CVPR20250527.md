## Problem

Current linear-complexity visual Transformers remain unsuitable for mobile deployment because they either yield minimal efficiency gains or suffer significant accuracy drops. The stacked local enhancement strategy (e.g., MLLA) that alternates convolutions and linear attention is inflexible and computationally wasteful, as all input features must pass through every local and global processor, and the rigid integration prevents targeted fusion design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250527/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 for ImageNet-1K classification and 512×512 for COCO detection/segmentation and ADE20K semantic segmentation. For classification it outputs a single class label; for COCO it produces bounding boxes with class labels and instance masks via a Mask R-CNN head; for ADE20K it outputs pixel-wise segmentation masks via a Semantic FPN head. Latency is explicitly measured on iPhone 13 (A15) and iPad Pro (M2) to validate mobile-friendliness.

## Method

CARE introduces an asymmetrical feature decoupling strategy that splits input features along the channel dimension into a smaller part (d₁) processed by linear attention and a larger part (d₂) processed by depth-wise convolutions for local inductive bias, with d₁ < d₂ to reduce the quadratic channel overhead of linear attention to O(hwd₁²). A dynamic memory unit stores and replays features from previous stages to preserve critical cross-layer information. A dual interaction module then explicitly facilitates (i) information exchange between the local and global feature branches and (ii) interaction between current-layer features and the stored memory, each implemented as a 1×1 → 3×3 depthwise → 1×1 convolution sequence. This divide-and-conquer learning followed by targeted interaction replaces the rigid stacked alternation, achieving both higher accuracy and lower computational cost simultaneously.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, CARE-S0 achieves 78.4% top-1 accuracy at only 0.7 GMACs (1.1 ms on iPhone 13), outperforming MobileViG-T (75.7%) and FastViT-T8 (76.7%) at the same compute budget. CARE-S1 reaches 80.1% at 1.0 GMACs, and CARE-S2 achieves 82.1% at 1.9 GMACs (2.0 ms on iPhone 13), matching ConvNeXt-T and FLatten-Swin-T (both 82.1%) while using roughly half their GMACs. On COCO object detection, CARE-S2 attains 43.1 AP^b at 10.1 GMACs, and on ADE20K semantic segmentation it reaches 43.5 mIoU, both with substantially lower latency than Swin-T and MLLA-T counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 0.7 GMACs tier, CARE-S0 (78.4%) surpasses MobileViG-T (75.7%) by 2.7%, FastViT-T8 (76.7%) by 1.7%, and MobileViGv2-T (77.7%) by 0.7%, while maintaining comparable or lower latency on iPhone 13. At ~1.0 GMACs, CARE-S1 (80.1%) edges out MobileViG-S (78.2%) and MobileViGv2-S (79.8%) but trails FastViT-T12 (80.3%) by 0.2% at a slightly higher GMACs budget (1.4 vs 1.0). At 1.9 GMACs, CARE-S2 (82.1%) beats EdgeViT-S (81.0%) by 1.1%, MobileOne-S3 (78.1%) by 4.0%, and FastViT-SA12 (81.9%) by 0.2%, while matching ConvNeXt-T and FLatten-Swin-T (both 82.1%) at roughly half their compute. Against the closest linear-attention baseline MLLA-T (83.5% at 4.2 GMACs), CARE-S2 sacrifices only 1.4% accuracy but halves the GMACs and runs 2.5× faster on iPhone 13 (2.0 ms vs 5.1 ms). On COCO detection, CARE-S2 (43.1 AP^b at 10.1 GMACs) outperforms FLatten-PVT-T (38.2), SLAB-PVT-T (36.5), and PoolFormer-S12 (37.3) in both accuracy and efficiency, though MLLA-T (46.8 AP^b at 22.0 GMACs) still leads by 3.7 AP^b at more than double the compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MLLA [16] (NeurIPS 2024), which demonstrated that combining local inductive bias (depth-wise convolutions) with linear attention in a stacked, alternating manner improves upon pure linear attention. CARE departs from this stacking paradigm by arguing it is both inflexible and computationally wasteful, instead proposing an asymmetrical channel-wise decoupling where local and global features are learned in parallel on disjoint channel subsets (with d₁ < d₂ to exploit the quadratic-in-d complexity of linear attention). The broader lineage traces through the linear attention family (Performer, SOFT, FLatten, SLAB, Agent Attention) and the local attention family (Swin, CSWin, NAT), positioning CARE as a more efficient fusion strategy for the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for mobile deployment: CARE-S2 matches MLLA-T's accuracy (82.1% vs. 83.5%) at roughly half the GMACs and 2.5× lower latency on iPhone 13, making linear-attention Transformers genuinely viable on resource-constrained hardware. The asymmetrical decoupling insight—that splitting channels with d₁ < d₂ provably reduces the quadratic channel overhead of linear attention—offers a reusable design principle for future efficient architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger model sizes (the authors acknowledge this limitation due to GPU constraints) and whether NAS could automatically discover optimal decoupling ratios and interaction depths.
