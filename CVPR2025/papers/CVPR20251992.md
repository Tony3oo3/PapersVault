## Problem

All-in-one adverse weather removal models assume all degradation-clean mappings are available simultaneously, which is unrealistic as weather data arrives incrementally in the real world. Training incrementally introduces catastrophic forgetting, and existing incremental learning strategies (regularization, sample replay, parameter expansion) suffer from poor scalability, high memory overhead, or large cache requirements. The paper also identifies a lack of degradation awareness in prior methods, limiting their ability to distinguish and respond to specific weather types.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single degraded RGB image (rain, snow, haze, raindrop, or low-light) at 128×128 patch resolution and outputs a restored clean image of the same size. Training proceeds in T sequential sessions, each containing n_t degraded-clean pairs for one weather condition; critically, data from prior sessions is unavailable during training on session t, and the final model must generalize across all T degradation types at test time. The architecture is a U-Net-based restoration network f = P∘ψ with approximately 4.95M parameters.

## Method

ILAWR introduces Degradation-Aware Multilateral Distillation (DAMD) with two key modules. The Degradation-Aware Module (DAM) leverages Fourier priors: it computes channel-wise mean features, extracts amplitude spectra via FFT, isolates channel-specific spectral differences as degradation cues, and generates a modulation weight tensor through dilated residual blocks to separate degradation information from background content in U-Net's deep layers. The Importance-Guided Aggregation Module (IGAM) measures per-channel KL divergence between student outputs and each teacher's guidance, applies softmax to produce importance weights, and aggregates multi-teacher signals accordingly. Two teacher groups operate in parallel: Teacher Group 1 (DAM outputs from all prior sessions) distills degradation-specific knowledge via KL divergence, while Teacher Group 2 (full restoration outputs) constrains background reconstruction via Charbonnier + contrast loss. In each session, the DAM and restoration network are trained alternately with the other frozen, and the total loss balances the base reconstruction objective with both distillation terms weighted by λ and ζ.

## Results

On the primary synthetic benchmark (Setting 1: Haze→Rain→Snow), ILAWR achieves an average PSNR of 31.32 dB and SSIM of 0.9380, approaching the Individual Training upper bound (31.97 dB) while substantially outperforming the best incremental baseline CLAIO (30.70 dB) by 0.62 dB. Extending to four degradation types (Setting 2: +Raindrop), ILAWR reaches 30.13 dB average PSNR, a 0.85 dB gain over CLAIO. On the first real-world incremental benchmark (Setting 3: Haze→Rain→Snow→Low-Light), ILAWR attains 26.78 dB average PSNR, surpassing CLAIO by 1.16 dB and the best all-in-one method WGWS by 1.10 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ILAWR outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every dataset in all three settings. Against the strongest incremental baseline CLAIO [8], ILAWR gains 0.62 dB (Setting 1), 0.85 dB (Setting 2), and 1.16 dB (Setting 3) in average PSNR, with the gap widening as more degradation types are added. Compared to the best all-in-one methods (RAM [35] at 30.35 dB and WGWS [61] at 30.31 dB in Setting 1), ILAWR leads by roughly 1 dB while using fewer parameters (4.95M vs. 25.31M and 5.97M respectively) and achieving faster inference (0.032 s vs. 0.067 s and 0.030 s on 256×256 images). The older regularization-based incremental methods (EWC, LwF, MAS, POD, PIGWM) all suffer severe forgetting on Rain100H (PSNR 13–17 dB), whereas ILAWR maintains 28.91 dB. AFC [18] and TransWeather [42] represent intermediate performance, with ILAWR exceeding them by 5.05 dB and 2.76 dB respectively in Setting 1. Notably, ILAWR's 31.32 dB average PSNR is within 0.65 dB of the Individual Training upper bound (31.97 dB), indicating near-optimal knowledge retention across sessions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incremental learning line for image restoration (PIGWM's regularization, CLAIO's knowledge replay) by repurposing multi-teacher distillation—previously applied in classification—to low-level vision. The Degradation-Aware Module (DAM) draws on Fourier-domain analysis of degradation, building on frequency-domain insights from prior restoration work, but is the first to use amplitude-spectrum channel-wise differences as a distillation signal for isolating degradation-specific features. The Importance-Guided Aggregation Module (IGAM) adapts the multi-teacher ensemble idea from classification to dynamically weight teacher guidance via per-channel KL divergence, avoiding the replay caches and parameter expansion that limit prior incremental restoration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first incremental adverse weather removal benchmark on real-world datasets (SPA+, REVIDE, RealSnow, LOL-v2), filling a gap where prior work was confined to synthetic data. This is practically important for deployment scenarios where weather data arrives in non-stationary streams and full retraining is infeasible. However, the method is tied to a U-Net backbone and four specific degradation types; whether the Fourier amplitude prior generalizes to more complex or compound degradations (e.g., rain + haze simultaneously) remains open. The efficiency gains over replay-based methods are meaningful but modest in absolute terms, and the approach has not been validated on larger-scale or video restoration settings.
