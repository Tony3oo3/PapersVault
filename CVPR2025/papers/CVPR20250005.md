## Problem

Generating detailed 3D hair from a single image is hindered by the scarcity of diverse paired image-to-3D hair data, forcing prior methods to rely on low-dimensional intermediate representations (guide strands, scalp-level VAE embeddings) that limit spatial detail and fail on curly or balding hairstyles. The core gap is both a data bottleneck and the architectural simplifications that data scarcity necessitates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (arbitrary resolution, resized for DINOv2 encoding) and produces approximately 100K individual 3D hair strands, each represented as 256 3D points, along with a 256×256 scalp density map. The intermediate scalp texture is a 256×256×64 tensor where each texel stores a latent code for one strand, decoded directly via a SIREN-based decoder without any upsampling or post-processing. The resulting strand geometry can be exported as an Alembic file and rendered in real-time (60 FPS) in Unreal Engine.

## Method

DiffLocks first constructs a 40K-sample synthetic dataset by randomizing 75 manually created base hairstyles through a 58-node Blender geometry pipeline (110 parameters governing clumping, curling, physics, and material), rendering path-traced 768×768 images with randomized HDRIs and camera parameters. A strand VAE compresses each 256-point strand into a 64-dim latent code using a novel curvature loss that prioritizes matching local direction changes over per-point positions, which is critical for tightly coiled hair. The diffusion model is an Hourglass Diffusion Transformer (HDiT) operating in pixel space on the concatenated scalp texture and density map, conditioned on DINOv2-large features (55×55×1024 local map + 1024-dim CLS token) injected via cross-attention at each downsampling stage. A key training innovation is per-channel loss weighting: each latent dimension is weighted by its measured perceptual impact on decoded strand shape, combined with EDM2-style log-variance noise-level weighting. Classifier-free guidance (10% dropout) enables both conditional and unconditional generation from the same model.

## Results

On the external Hair Meshes benchmark (straight and curly hairstyles), DiffLocks achieves an F-score of 76.20 at the 4 mm/40° threshold, outperforming NeuralHDHair (68.73) and HairStep (57.20), with the largest gains in Recall (71.79 vs. 64.63 and 53.15). On the authors' own 10-hairstyle evaluation set, the advantage is more pronounced: DiffLocks reaches 91.17 F-score at 4/40 versus 49.30 (NeuralHDHair) and 45.48 (HairStep). In terms of runtime, DiffLocks completes the full pipeline in 2.33 s on an H100 GPU, compared to 18.32 s for NeuralHDHair and 53.40 s for HairStep, primarily because it avoids head fitting, hair segmentation, and strand growth/refinement steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuralHDHair [48], DiffLocks wins on Recall and F-score at every threshold on the Hair Meshes benchmark (e.g., F-score 76.20 vs. 68.73 at 4/40), though NeuralHDHair retains a slight Precision edge at the tightest 2/20 threshold (47.91 vs. 43.60). Against HairStep [55], DiffLocks dominates across all metrics and thresholds on both evaluation datasets, with the gap widening substantially on the authors' own 10-hairstyle set (F-score 91.17 vs. 45.48 at 4/40). HAAR [42] is compared only qualitatively; since HAAR is designed for text-to-hair generation, the authors use LLaVA to convert images to text prompts before applying HAAR's pipeline, and observe that HAAR frequently fails to match the hairstyle in the input image. The paper also notes that DiffLocks reconstructs the backside of the hair more faithfully than both baselines, attributing this to the stronger prior learned from the larger 40K-sample training set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the strand-based hair reconstruction lineage from Neural Strands [36], reusing their VAE for compressing individual strands into 64-dim latent codes but adding a curvature loss for coiled hair. It departs sharply from prior single-view methods (HairStep, NeuralHDHair, HAAR) that rely on Gabor-filter orientation maps, guide strands, and low-dimensional scalp embeddings requiring post-processing upsampling. Instead, DiffLocks uses a 256×256 scalp texture where each texel stores a full strand latent code, conditioned by DINOv2 features through an Hourglass Diffusion Transformer, eliminating the intermediate representations that prior methods needed to cope with limited data. The key conceptual departure is that a sufficiently large synthetic dataset (40K samples) makes those simplifying assumptions unnecessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40K paired image-3D-strand dataset is the largest public synthetic hair resource to date and directly addresses the data bottleneck that has constrained the field since USC-HairSalon (343 samples). The method's ability to reconstruct afro-like and balding hairstyles—previously intractable for single-view approaches—opens up a meaningful range of underrepresented demographics in digital humans. Limitations include the absence of braids, buns, ponytails, and accessories, and a stated trade-off favoring local strand detail over perfect image alignment. An open question is whether the synthetic-to-real generalization (achieved here via DINOv2 features and realistic path-traced rendering) holds for more extreme domain gaps or requires fine-tuning on real captures.
