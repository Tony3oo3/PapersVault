## Problem

No existing dataset simultaneously provides demographic annotations (skin tone, gender, age) and diverse generative methods (deepfake videos, GANs, diffusion models) at million scale, which blocks the development of fair AI face detectors. Additionally, no comprehensive benchmark evaluates both general fairness and fairness reliability (robustness to post-processing, cross-domain generalization) of recent detectors, leaving the fairness landscape of AI face detection poorly understood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250052/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises 1,646,545 face images (400,885 real + 1,245,660 fake) cropped via RetinaFace from 28 subsets spanning 37 generation methods. Each image receives three demographic labels: skin tone on the 10-shade Monk Skin Tone scale, binary gender, and one of five age groups (Child, Youth, Adult, Middle-age Adult, Senior). The benchmark takes a trained detector's real/fake classification scores and produces per-group and intersectional fairness scores (F_DP, F_MEO, F_EO, F_OAE, F_IND) alongside utility metrics (AUC, ACC, AP, EER, FPR), with intersectional subgroups defined as gender × skin-tone-band (Light/Medium/Dark).

## Method

The dataset is built by integrating public real and AI-generated face images, then annotated with a two-part pipeline: skin tone is estimated by combining Mediapipe FaceMesh landmark detection with K-Means (K=3) color clustering mapped to the MST scale, while gender and age are predicted by a lightweight CLIP ViT-L/14-based annotator (frozen backbone + 3-layer MLP head) trained on IMDB-WIKI. The annotator's training objective combines a Vector Scaling imbalance loss with a Sinkhorn-distance fairness loss that minimizes distributional disparity between feature embeddings and their conditional distributions per demographic group, optimized via Sharpness-Aware Minimization for better generalization. The fairness benchmark evaluates 12 detectors across four architectural families (Naive, Frequency-based, Spatial-based, Fairness-enhanced) under default settings, six image post-processing perturbations, and three external datasets (CCv2, DF-Platter, GenData) to assess fairness robustness and generalization.

## Results

On the AI-Face test set (1.2M fake + 400K real images), 10 out of 12 detectors achieve AUC > 98%, confirming the dataset's utility for training. PG-FDD attains the best overall utility (AUC 99.17%, AP 99.69%, FPR 10.97%) and the best gender fairness (F_MEO 3.19%, F_EO 3.70%) and intersectional F_EO (18.35%). UnivFD (CLIP-based) achieves the best skin-tone fairness (F_MEO 2.58%, F_EO 5.54%) and age fairness (F_MEO 5.44%, F_EO 14.15%), as well as the best intersectional F_MEO (6.12%) and F_OAE (2.46%). No single detector dominates all fairness and utility metrics simultaneously, and all detectors suffer significant degradation under post-processing and cross-domain evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PG-FDD [30] achieves the best gender fairness (F_MEO 3.19%, F_EO 3.70%) and intersectional F_EO (18.35%), outperforming UnivFD [88] (F_EO Gender 5.41%, F_EO Intersection 20.26%) and all other baselines, while also leading in AUC (99.17%) and FPR (10.97%). UnivFD [88] dominates skin-tone and age fairness (F_MEO SkinTone 2.58% vs. 6.47% for PG-FDD; F_EO Age 14.15% vs. 29.59%), making it the strongest choice when demographic fairness across all attributes matters. SPSL [86] achieves the lowest EER (4.37%) and SRM [87] the highest ACC (96.35%), but both lag significantly in fairness (F_EO Intersection 21.68% and 30.11% respectively). Naive detectors (Xception [82], EfficientB4 [83], ViT-B/16 [84]) are competitive in utility (AUC 98.6–98.7%) but show the worst intersectional fairness (F_EO 24–27%), with ViT-B/16 uniquely achieving near-zero individual fairness (F_IND 0.022%). Fairness-enhanced DAW-FDD and DAG-FDD [29] do not clearly outperform spatial or frequency baselines on most fairness metrics, suggesting their gains are limited to specific demographic slices. Overall, no single detector wins across all 10 fairness and 5 utility metrics, highlighting the open challenge of jointly optimizing fairness and robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fairness-in-deepfake-detection line (DAW-FDD, DAG-FDD, PG-FDD) by addressing a critical gap: prior fairness methods and datasets were limited to deepfake video faces, ignoring GAN- and diffusion-model-generated faces entirely. For dataset construction, it departs from prior demographically annotated datasets (A-FF++, A-DFD, DF-Platter) by being the first to unify all three generation categories at million scale with full demographic labels, and it replaces off-the-shelf face analysis tools with a CLIP-based annotator trained using Vector Scaling and Sinkhorn-distance fairness losses. The benchmark itself extends prior fairness evaluations (Loc et al., CCv1) by adding reliability assessments (post-processing robustness, cross-domain generalization) that no prior benchmark covered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AI-Face dataset and benchmark fill a critical infrastructure gap: no prior resource simultaneously covered deepfake videos, GANs, and diffusion models with demographic annotations at this scale, making fair detector development across all generation types previously infeasible. Key open questions remain: no detector maintains fairness under real-world post-processing or cross-domain shifts, fairness-enhanced methods still produce higher error rates for minority subgroups (lighter skin tones, females), and increasing training data can paradoxically worsen fairness for some architectures. The finding that foundation-model-based detectors (UnivFD/CLIP) show the most stable fairness across training sizes suggests a promising but underexplored direction for integrating pretrained representations into fair detection.
