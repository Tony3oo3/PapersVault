## Problem

Inverse rendering from a single RGB image is inherently ill-posed, meaning many valid combinations of geometry, material, and lighting can explain the same observation. Existing deterministic methods cannot capture this solution-space diversity, while recent diffusion-based approaches either ignore the accuracy–diversity trade-off, treat modalities independently, or rely on pretrained LDM autoencoders that cannot handle high-dimensional per-pixel lighting data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252353/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (240×320) and produces a normal map, depth map, diffuse albedo map, roughness map, and a per-pixel environment map with angular resolution C=128. The diffusion model operates at a reduced 60×80 resolution to manage the high dimensionality of per-pixel lighting, and a UNet-based RGB-guided super-resolution model upsamples all outputs to full resolution. The environment map is compressed into a 96-dimensional neural feature vector (ILR) via an MLP autoencoder with log1p/expm1 transforms to handle HDR radiance ranges.

## Method

The core contribution is a channel-wise noise scheduling strategy that assigns different noise levels to geometry, material, and lighting channels at each diffusion timestep, reflecting their differing uncertainty levels (geometry first, then material, then lighting). Two models are trained from the same architecture: a Progressive Diffusion Model (PDM) with T=1000 and τ=(0.9, 1.2, 1.5) that generates diverse samples, and a Switchable Diffusion Model (SDM) with T=4 using a discrete switchable scheduler that cycles which modality receives pure noise at each step, enabling iterative refinement for accuracy. Both models use velocity prediction with the input RGB concatenated to the noisy latent and CLIP embeddings as cross-attention conditioning. The ILR is redesigned as an MLP autoencoder (rather than the CNN-based version in MAIR++) that directly encodes the environment map, with batch normalization and log-space transforms to stabilize training on HDR values.

## Results

On the OpenRooms FF synthetic test set, SDM achieves the best single-view performance on normal (1.502), depth (0.039), albedo (0.349), environment map (13.35), and re-rendering (0.156) MSE (×10⁻²), outperforming both single-view baselines Li et al. and Zhu et al. on all metrics except roughness (4.969 vs. 4.208). PDM (mean of 10 samples) is competitive, with re-rendering MSE of 0.150. On the MAW intrinsic decomposition benchmark, SDM achieves the best shading MSE (1.181) and re-rendering MSE (0.006) among all methods, while RGB↔X leads on albedo MSE (0.331 best) but suffers in shading fidelity (1.196). In the AMT user study for object insertion, SDM is selected 32.2% of the time, nearly matching the ground truth (34.8%) and far exceeding Li et al. (17.8%) and Zhu et al. (15.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two single-view deterministic baselines, SDM dominates: it reduces normal MSE by 60% over Li et al. (1.502 vs. 3.782) and by 14% over Zhu et al. (1.502 vs. 1.738), while cutting re-rendering MSE by 68% (0.156 vs. 0.491) and 96% (0.156 vs. 3.565) respectively. The only metric where SDM trails is roughness (4.969 vs. 4.208 for Zhu et al.), though PDM narrows this gap (4.819 mean / 4.644 best). Compared to the multi-view MAIR++ [5], SDM is competitive on albedo (0.349 vs. 0.478) but lags on normal, depth, roughness, and re-rendering, reflecting the inherent advantage of multi-view input. On the MAW intrinsic decomposition task, RGB↔X [43] achieves the best albedo MSE (0.331) and hue (3.091) thanks to its strong pretrained LDM prior, but its diffusion-based rendering degrades shading (1.196) and re-rendering (0.052) fidelity, whereas SDM trained solely on OpenRooms FF achieves the best shading (1.181) and re-rendering (0.006). In the object-insertion AMT user study, SDM is preferred nearly as often as the ground truth (32.2% vs. 34.8%), substantially outperforming both deterministic baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based inverse rendering (Kocsis et al. [14], RGB↔X [43], Luo et al. [22]) by departing from the reliance on pretrained LDMs, which are limited to three-channel image data, and instead operating in pixel space with a cascaded architecture to handle high-dimensional per-pixel environment maps. It builds on the implicit lighting representation (ILR) from MAIR++ [5] but redesigns it as an MLP-based autoencoder that takes the environment map as input, enabling more faithful HDR reconstruction. The key methodological departure is the channel-wise noise scheduling, which applies different noise levels to geometry, material, and lighting channels within a single diffusion model, and the dual-model strategy (PDM with large T for diversity, SDM with small T for accuracy) that explicitly addresses the diversity-accuracy trade-off absent in prior deterministic or single-model probabilistic approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The channel-wise noise scheduling is a general, lightweight technique that could transfer to other multi-modality diffusion tasks beyond inverse rendering, and the dual-model framework provides a practical way to handle the inherent ill-posedness of single-image decomposition. However, the requirement of two separate models limits usability, and the paper acknowledges that uncertainty estimation via PDM variance is imprecise. An open question is whether the two models can be unified into a single architecture, and whether pretrained LDM priors could be adapted to handle per-pixel environment maps, which would broaden the applicability of the approach to more complex outdoor or multi-view settings.
