## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models are too large (billions of parameters) and slow to deploy on mobile devices, and prior efficient models are constrained to low-resolution (512²) output or require laptop GPUs. A critical unexplored question is how to train a T2I model from scratch that generates high-resolution (1024²) images directly on mobile hardware within a few seconds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252431/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt, which is encoded by three text encoders (CLIP-L, CLIP-G, and Gemma2-2b) combined into a unified embedding following the SD3 strategy, with per-encoder dropout enabling flexible subsets at inference. It produces a 1024² px RGB image in 4–8 denoising steps (or 28 steps for the non-distilled variant), operating in a 16-channel latent space at 1/8 spatial resolution. The full pipeline (UNet + 1.38M-param decoder) runs in ~1.4 s on an iPhone 16 Pro-Max.

## Method

SnapGen combines three key innovations. First, an efficient UNet backbone (379M params) derived from SDXL by removing self-attention from high-resolution stages, replacing convolutions with expanded separable convolutions (UIB-style), trimming FFN expansion, substituting MQA for MHSA, injecting cross-attention from the first stage, and adding QK RMSNorm with 2D RoPE. Second, a multi-level knowledge distillation scheme that jointly optimizes a flow-matching task loss, an output-level KD loss, and a cross-architecture feature KD loss (DiT teacher → UNet student via a two-conv projector), all weighted by a timestep-aware scaling function that allocates more teacher supervision where prediction difficulty is high (t near 0 or 1) and more real-data supervision at intermediate timesteps. Third, an adversarial step distillation stage using a LADD-style diffusion-GAN objective with SD3.5-Large-Turbo as a frozen feature-extractor teacher, enabling high-quality generation in only 4 or 8 steps.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K class-conditional generation, SnapGen (372M params, 38G FLOPs) achieves FID 2.06 at 256² px, matching SiT-XL (675M params, 119G FLOPs) with 45% fewer parameters and one-third the compute. On T2I benchmarks, the 0.38B SnapGen achieves GenEval 0.66, DPG-Bench 81.1, CLIP score 0.332, and Image Reward 1.32, surpassing models 7×–14× larger (SDXL, Playgroundv2, IF-XL). The tiny decoder (1.38M params) achieves PSNR 27.85 on COCO, within 0.07 dB of SD3's decoder (49.55M params) while being 36× smaller and 54× faster on mobile. For the first time, 1024² px T2I generation runs in ~1.4 s on an iPhone 16 Pro-Max (4–8 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SnapGen (0.38B) decisively outperforms SDXL (2.6B) on every T2I metric: GenEval 0.66 vs 0.55, DPG 81.1 vs 74.7, CLIP 0.332 vs 0.301, and Image Reward 1.32 vs 0.99, while being 7× smaller and 5.8× faster in throughput. It similarly surpasses Playgroundv2 (2.6B) and IF-XL (5.5B) on GenEval, DPG, and CLIP, and beats IF-XL by a wide margin on Image Reward (1.32 vs 0.65). Against the closest competitor SANA (1.6B), SnapGen ties on GenEval (0.66) and wins on CLIP (0.332 vs 0.327) and Image Reward (1.32 vs 1.25), but trails on DPG (81.1 vs 84.8). On ImageNet-1K, SnapGen matches SiT-XL's FID of 2.06 with 45% fewer parameters and 3× fewer FLOPs, and outperforms LDM-4, UViT-L, UViT-H, and DiT-XL. In human evaluation, SnapGen beats SDXL on all three attributes (aesthetics, alignment, realism), beats SD3-M on alignment and realism, and remains competitive with its 8.1B-parameter teacher SD3.5-Large, trailing only slightly on text-image alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of compressing pre-trained diffusion models (pruning, quantization, step distillation on existing architectures) by designing a compact UNet backbone and autoencoder from scratch, using SDXL's UNet and SD3's AE as starting points but making systematic architectural modifications (separable convolutions, MQA, removing self-attention from high-resolution stages, trimmed FFNs). For training, it extends flow-matching-based knowledge distillation by introducing cross-architecture distillation (DiT teacher → UNet student) with a novel timestep-aware scaling mechanism that adapts loss weighting to prediction difficulty, and combines this with LADD-style adversarial step distillation. The work also builds on MobileDiffusion's mobile deployment insights and SANA/LinFusion's efficiency goals, but targets a broader and more constrained setting: 1024² generation directly on phone hardware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SnapGen is the first T2I model to generate 1024² images on a mobile device in ~1.4 seconds, opening a practical path for on-device image generation with privacy, latency, and cost advantages over cloud deployment. The systematic ablation of architectural choices (e.g., removing self-attention from high-res stages, separable convolutions with expansion) provides a reusable design recipe for future compact diffusion models. Open questions include whether the cross-architecture distillation recipe generalizes to other teacher-student pairs or modalities (e.g., video), whether the timestep-aware scaling extends to other generative objectives, and how the model performs under extreme resource constraints (e.g., 512² on lower-end phones). The reliance on a specific large teacher (SD3.5-Large) for distillation also raises questions about reproducibility and licensing.
