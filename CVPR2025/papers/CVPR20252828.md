## Problem

Existing attacks on Non-Transferable Learning (NTL) models, such as TransNTL, require white-box access to modify model weights, making them inapplicable when NTL models are deployed as black-box APIs. This paper asks whether the non-transferable barrier remains secure under black-box deployment, where an attacker can only query the model's output logits without any knowledge of its structure or parameters.

## Input / Output

The attacker provides unlabeled unauthorized-domain test images (e.g., STL-10 at 96×96) along with a small labeled subset (1%) of authorized-domain data (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32) and black-box access to the NTL model's logits. The method produces disguised images that, when fed to the NTL model, yield correct class predictions on the unauthorized domain. Because the NTL model is black-box, gradients are approximated via zero-order finite-difference estimation rather than backpropagation through the model.

## Method

JailNTL trains a bidirectional disguising network (inspired by CycleGAN and feedback adversarial learning) that maps unauthorized-domain images to a disguised domain indistinguishable from the authorized domain, while a feedback loop enforces per-element content preservation. The training objective combines adversarial domain-discrimination losses in both forward and reverse directions with L1 cycle-consistency terms. On top of this data-intrinsic disguising, a model-guided component minimizes two output-level statistics differences measured from the black-box NTL model: prediction confidence (softmax entropy) and class-balance (distribution entropy) between authorized and disguised batches. At test time, the trained disguising network transforms each unauthorized image, and the NTL model's prediction on the disguised image serves as the final output, effectively bypassing the non-transferable barrier without any weight modification.

## Results

Using only 1% authorized domain data, JailNTL (a black-box attack) recovers unauthorized-domain top-1 accuracy by up to +55.7% (CIFAR-10→STL-10, CUTI) and +51.6% (CIFAR-10→STL-10, NTL), while maintaining authorized-domain accuracy within 1.1% of the pre-trained model. On the VisDA-T→VisDA-V task, JailNTL still achieves +15.0% (NTL) and +15.4% (CUTI) unauthorized accuracy gains. When integrated with the white-box TransNTL attack, JailNTL further boosts unauthorized accuracy by up to +6.5% over TransNTL alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the basic fine-tuning baselines RTAL and FTAL [1], JailNTL is overwhelmingly superior: RTAL and FTAL fail to meaningfully improve unauthorized-domain accuracy (gains of 0.0–3.3%), whereas JailNTL achieves gains of 15.0–55.7% across all six domain/NTL-method combinations. Against the SOTA white-box attack TransNTL [17], JailNTL (operating in the strictly harder black-box setting) wins on 4 of 6 configurations: CIFAR-10→STL-10 NTL (61.4% vs. 27.2% unauthorized accuracy, a +34.2% margin), CIFAR-10→STL-10 CUTI (64.7% vs. 60.3%), STL-10→CIFAR-10 NTL (39.8% vs. 37.7%), and VisDA-T→VisDA-V NTL (21.7% vs. 11.1%). TransNTL retains an edge on STL-10→CIFAR-10 CUTI (55.9% vs. 43.5%) and VisDA-T→VisDA-V CUTI (33.5% vs. 25.4%), but these are white-box attacks that modify model weights, whereas JailNTL requires only query access to model logits. Additionally, when JailNTL is plugged into TransNTL as a data-augmentation module (white-box), the combined method outperforms TransNTL alone by up to 6.5% on unauthorized accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTL attack line initiated by TransNTL (Hong et al., CVPR 2024), which demonstrated that the non-transferable barrier can be broken via white-box fine-tuning on perturbed authorized samples. The key departure is shifting from weight modification to input-level data disguising, repurposing the bidirectional GAN structure from CycleGAN (Zhu et al., ICCV 2017) and the feedback mechanism from Huh et al. (CVPR 2019) for domain translation. Model-guided losses (confidence and class-balance matching) are novel additions that leverage the black-box NTL model's output statistics, with zero-order gradient estimation (Kariyappa et al., CVPR 2021) enabling backpropagation through the opaque model. The paper thus carves out a new attack axis—test-time data manipulation—complementary to the weight-modification axis explored by prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that NTL models deployed as black-box APIs are not inherently secure, directly challenging the assumption that privatizing model weights eliminates attack risk. The practical implication is significant: organizations relying on NTL for IP protection in MLaaS settings must now consider input-level defenses, not just weight-level ones. Open questions include whether NTL training objectives can be redesigned to resist domain-disguising attacks, whether the attack generalizes to non-visual modalities (e.g., NLP-based NTL like UNTL), and how to detect disguised inputs at inference time. The weaker results on VisDA (larger domain gap) suggest the approach has limits when domain discrepancy is extreme.
