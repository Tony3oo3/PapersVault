## Problem

Diffusion-based generative models consistently fail to produce geometrically accurate mirror reflections, even when prompted explicitly. Prior synthetic-dataset approaches like MirrorFusion lack diversity in object positioning, rotation, and multi-object occlusion, causing poor generalization to real-world scenes with varied poses and complex spatial arrangements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251012/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 512×512 RGB image, a binary mask delineating the mirror region, a text prompt (e.g., 'A perfect plane mirror reflection of [object]'), and a depth map. At inference, the depth map is estimated on-the-fly via DepthPro from the masked image, and the text prompt is randomly dropped 20% during training to reduce prompt dependence. The output is a full-resolution inpainted image where the masked mirror region contains a physically consistent reflection of the scene.

## Method

MirrorFusion 2.0 uses a BrushNet-style dual-branch U-Net where the conditioning branch receives the noisy latent, masked image, inpainting mask, and depth map concatenated, and injects features into the generation branch via zero-convolutions (scale w=1.0). The key dataset contribution is SynMirrorV2 (207K images), which introduces random object positioning within the mirror-camera frustum intersection, randomized y-axis rotations, ground-level anchoring, and semantically paired multi-object scenes (3,140 scenes) to handle occlusions. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) 40K iterations on single-object SynMirrorV2 with both branches unfrozen from SD v1.5, (2) 10K iterations on multi-object scenes to learn occlusion, and (3) 10K iterations on real-world MSD data with monocular depth estimates to bridge the synthetic-to-real gap. Inference uses CFG=7.5 with the UniPC scheduler over 50 steps.

## Results

On the single-object split of MirrorBenchV2, MirrorFusion 2.0 (Ours40k) achieves PSNR 18.79, SSIM 0.77, and LPIPS 0.108, outperforming the MirrorFusion baseline (PSNR 18.31, SSIM 0.76, LPIPS 0.122) on all reflection quality metrics while maintaining comparable text alignment (CLIP Sim 25.96 vs 26.00). On the multiple-object split, further fine-tuning on multi-object scenes (Ours50k) improves PSNR from 17.77 to 18.00 and LPIPS from 0.126 to 0.119 over the single-object-only model. A user study with 40 samples across single, multiple, GSO, and real-world scenes shows 84% of users prefer MirrorFusion 2.0 generations over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is MirrorFusion [12] (the prior state-of-the-art for diffusion-based mirror reflection generation). MirrorFusion 2.0 wins on all three reflection quality metrics for single objects: PSNR improves by 0.48 dB (18.79 vs 18.31), SSIM by 0.01 (0.77 vs 0.76), and LPIPS drops by 0.014 (0.108 vs 0.122), while CLIP text alignment is essentially tied (25.96 vs 26.00). Qualitatively, the baseline fails on rotated single objects (misrepresenting chair orientation) and on multi-object scenes (floating objects, incorrect reflections), whereas MirrorFusion 2.0 handles both correctly. The paper also qualitatively outperforms general-purpose inpainting models (HD-Painter) and text-to-image models (SD 3.5, FLUX) on reflection generation, though no quantitative comparison is provided for those. The multi-object ablation (Table 3) shows that adding multi-object training further improves PSNR by 0.23 dB and LPIPS by 0.007, confirming the benefit of the paired-object pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work MirrorFusion [12], which first framed mirror reflection generation as a diffusion inpainting task trained on a synthetic dataset (SynMirror). The key architectural lineage comes from BrushNet [20], whose dual-branch zero-convolution conditioning is adopted and augmented with depth-map inputs. The principal departure is in the data pipeline: SynMirrorV2 introduces random object positioning, rotation, grounding, and semantically-paired multi-object scenes, addressing the limited diversity of SynMirror. The three-stage curriculum (single-object synthetic → multi-object synthetic → real-world MSD) draws on stage-wise training strategies from image editing [29] and multilingual T2I generation [23,50,53].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mirror reflection generation is a physically grounded stress test for whether diffusion models truly understand geometry and optics rather than relying on learned correlations; this paper shows that targeted data diversity and curriculum training can meaningfully close the synthetic-to-real gap for this task. The SynMirrorV2 dataset (207K images with multi-object scenes) is a reusable resource for the broader mirror-related task family (segmentation, depth, detection). However, the method still struggles with scenes containing more than two objects and cluttered real-world settings, and the quantitative gains over the baseline are modest (e.g., PSNR 18.31→18.79). Open questions include whether the curriculum approach generalizes to other physically-constrained effects (refraction, caustics) and whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be eliminated without real-world fine-tuning.
