## Problem

Contrastive geo-embedding methods like SatCLIP and GeoCLIP align locations with co-located satellite images via infoNCE, but this training objective only preserves the shared (low-resolution) information between the two modalities, discarding unique high-resolution visual features that are critical for downstream geospatial tasks. The paper formalizes this as a multi-view non-redundancy problem and demonstrates empirically that adding image features to SatCLIP embeddings yields up to 12.5% gains, confirming the lost information is task-relevant.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single geolocation (lat/lon) as query and produces a multi-resolution geo-embedding vector formed by concatenating an approximate high-resolution visual feature (retrieved from a database of ~82k SatMAE embeddings) with the original low-resolution SatCLIP location embedding. A temperature parameter τ controls the sharpness of the softmax-weighted retrieval, and a β parameter in RANGE+ blends semantic and geodesic retrieval to tune spatial smoothness, enabling generation of embeddings at multiple frequency scales. The retrieval database is compact (as small as 85 MB at 10% sampling) and robust to size changes.

## Method

RANGE builds on a pretrained SatCLIP contrastive model that learns a shared embedding space between geolocations and satellite images. A retrieval database is constructed by computing, for each of 82k global locations, a low-resolution key (projection-layer embedding) and a high-resolution value (SatMAE embedding). At inference, the query location embedding is matched against all low-resolution keys via cosine similarity, and a softmax-weighted average over all high-resolution values (controlled by temperature τ) yields an approximate visual feature for that location. This approximate visual feature is concatenated with the original location embedding to form the final RANGE embedding. The RANGE+ variant adds a second retrieval channel based on geodesic angular distance in 3D Cartesian coordinates, with a β parameter blending semantic and spatial retrieval to enforce spatial smoothness and enable multi-frequency embedding generation.

## Results

On classification tasks, RANGE achieves 83.3% accuracy on Biome (+13.1% over the best baseline GeoCLIP at 70.2%), 75.7% on Ecoregion (+4.1% over GeoCLIP at 71.6%), and 94.7% on Country classification with RANGE+ (+6.4% over SINR at 88.3%). On regression tasks, RANGE+ achieves R² of 0.851 on Elevation (+0.185 over SatCLIP at 0.666) and 0.811 on Population Density (+0.085 over SINR at 0.726), while narrowly trailing SINR (0.942) on Air Temperature (0.931). The method underperforms on California Housing prediction (R² 0.336 vs. GeoCLIP's 0.708), which the authors attribute to temporal mismatch between 1990 census data and 2020 satellite imagery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RANGE and RANGE+ outperform all contrastive-learning baselines (SatCLIP, GeoCLIP, CSP) by large margins on classification: Biome accuracy jumps from 68.9–70.2% to 83.3%, and Ecoregion from 69.3–71.6% to 75.7%. Against the strongest supervised baseline SINR, RANGE+ wins on Elevation (0.851 vs. 0.644 R²) and Population Density (0.811 vs. 0.726 R²) but loses on Air Temperature (0.931 vs. 0.942 R²). Non-parametric baselines (Wrap, Theory, SphereM/C variants) are consistently far behind, with Biome accuracy below 62% and Elevation R² below 0.40. The sole exception where RANGE underperforms is California Housing, where GeoCLIP achieves 0.708 R² versus RANGE+'s 0.336, likely due to the 30-year gap between the 1990 census labels and 2020 Sentinel imagery used for retrieval. On the iNaturalist fine-grained species classification task (Table 4), RANGE achieves competitive top-10 accuracy of 95.5%, marginally outperforming GeoCLIP (95.2%) and CSP (94.8%), confirming that the retrieval-augmented embeddings serve as effective geo-priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the contrastive geolocation-embedding line of work (SatCLIP, GeoCLIP, CSP) but departs from it by arguing that the infoNCE objective inherently discards modality-specific visual information under a multi-view non-redundancy setting. It repurposes the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) paradigm from NLP—originally designed for text generation—into a geospatial embedding context, using a compact database of image features as an external knowledge source. The theoretical grounding draws on factorized contrastive learning and multi-view redundancy theory to formalize why contrastive alignment is sub-optimal here, while the practical mechanism (softmax-weighted retrieval over a frozen feature database) is a novel application of neural field concepts to geographic coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, information-theoretic explanation for why contrastive geo-embeddings underperform and a simple, storage-efficient remedy (an 85 MB database at 10% sampling) that yields large, consistent gains across classification and regression tasks. The β parameter enabling multi-resolution embeddings at inference time is a practical feature for tasks requiring different spatial granularities. A notable limitation is the temporal mismatch on housing price prediction (1990 census vs. 2020 satellite data), suggesting the approach may struggle with rapidly changing land-use semantics. Open questions include whether the retrieval strategy generalizes to ground-level imagery (GeoCLIP setting) and whether the low-variance assumption for satellite semantics holds in all biomes.
