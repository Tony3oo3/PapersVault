## Problem

Monocular dynamic 3D reconstruction is highly ill-posed due to the absence of multi-view consistency. Existing deformation representations for 3D Gaussians face a fundamental trade-off: global motion bases (e.g., SoM) enforce smoothness but cannot capture fine-grained motion details, while large numbers of independent motion nodes (e.g., MoSca) provide expressiveness but overfit to the single training view. The paper asks how to structure motion representation so that both coarse temporal smoothness and fine spatial detail are captured simultaneously from a single video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video with calibrated camera parameters and leverages pretrained models (TAPIR for 2D point tracking, monocular depth estimation, and segmentation) both for initialization and as auxiliary supervision during optimization. It produces a dynamic 3D Gaussian representation consisting of canonical Gaussians and a two-layer hierarchical motion tree (50 first-level nodes sharing 10 SE(3) motion bases, each spawning 10 child nodes sharing 5 bases), which can be rendered at arbitrary novel viewpoints and timesteps in real time via differentiable rasterization.

## Method

HiMoR represents motion as a tree of SE(3) transformations where each node encodes relative motion to its parent, enabling a coarse-to-fine decomposition: shallow nodes capture smooth global motion while deep nodes add fine detail. Within each parent-child group, a small set of shared motion bases is weighted by per-node coefficients (a low-rank factorization), reducing degrees of freedom while preserving expressiveness. Each Gaussian's deformation is interpolated from its K-nearest leaf nodes using Gaussian-kernel skinning weights and dual-quaternion blending, making the deformation field spatially smooth and the optimization more stable than per-Gaussian deformation. A progressive node densification strategy adds new nodes where the curve distance between a Gaussian's trajectory and its nearest node trajectories exceeds a threshold, ensuring coverage of initially invisible regions. The training loss combines photometric, mask, depth, tracking, and a hierarchical rigidity term that applies stronger constraints to shallow nodes and weaker ones to deep nodes, reinforcing the coarse-to-fine motion decomposition.

## Results

On the iPhone dataset (5 scenes), HiMoR achieves the best mean CLIP-I (0.8907), CLIP-T (0.9750), LPIPS (0.4639), and PCK-T (0.8962), outperforming the strongest baseline SoM (0.8603, 0.9712, 0.4827, 0.8464) across all four metrics. On the Nvidia dataset, HiMoR achieves the best PSNR (23.400), SSIM (0.7621), and LPIPS (0.09370), marginally surpassing SoM (23.290, 0.7565, 0.09374). The method also leads in tracking accuracy (PCK-T) over all 3DGS-based baselines, with a notable gap over Deformable 3DGS (0.8962 vs. 0.2001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiMoR outperforms all five baselines on the iPhone dataset across every metric. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor SoM, HiMoR gains +0.030 in CLIP-I, +0.004 in CLIP-T, and −0.019 in LPIPS (mean), while also improving PCK-T from 0.8464 to 0.8962. The NeRF-based methods (T-NeRF, HyperNeRF) achieve competitive CLIP-I but suffer from higher LPIPS (blurrier results) and, in the case of T-NeRF, no tracking capability. Deformable 3DGS is the weakest baseline overall, with substantially worse LPIPS (0.7163) and PCK-T (0.2001), indicating severe overfitting to training views. On the Nvidia dataset, where training and test views are more similar, HiMoR leads by a smaller margin (+0.11 PSNR over SoM), suggesting the hierarchical motion representation's advantage is most pronounced under the challenging monocular setting with large viewpoint changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads in dynamic 3DGS reconstruction: the low-rank motion bases approach (SoM, DynMF) that constrains deformation with a small set of shared bases, and the spatial node-based approach (MoSca, Marbles) that deforms Gaussians via interpolation from nearby motion nodes. HiMoR unifies these by organizing motion nodes in a hierarchical tree where each level shares a small set of SE(3) bases, enabling coarse-to-fine motion decomposition analogous to kinematic chains. The paper also departs from prior evaluation practice by arguing that pixel-level metrics (PSNR) are unreliable for monocular dynamic reconstruction due to depth ambiguity and proposing CLIP-based perceptual metrics as a more faithful alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical motion representation offers a principled way to resolve the long-standing tension in monocular dynamic reconstruction between capturing fine-grained motion details and maintaining spatio-temporal smoothness, and the coarse-to-fine decomposition is validated both quantitatively and visually. The evaluation metric contribution (CLIP-I, CLIP-T) is practically important for the field, as it addresses a real concern that pixel-level scores can be misleading when reconstruction is inherently ill-posed. A key open limitation is that the method cannot represent objects or scene parts absent from the canonical frame, pointing toward future work on adaptive canonical spaces or separate branches for newly appearing content.
