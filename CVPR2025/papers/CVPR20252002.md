## Problem

Previous spike-camera 3D reconstruction methods follow a three-stage cascading pipeline (spike-to-image reconstruction → pose estimation → novel view synthesis), where errors at each stage compound and degrade the final 3D fidelity. In particular, noisy reconstructed images lead to inaccurate poses, which in turn limit the quality of the 3D Gaussian or NeRF representation. This paper eliminates the error propagation by unifying all three stages into a single end-to-end optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252002/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw spike streams (K×H×W binary tensors at 40 kHz) from V viewpoints along with initial camera poses (typically from COLMAP, potentially inaccurate). The spike input is consumed in a complementary long-short format: a voxelized long stream (K/4×H×W, accumulating every 4 frames) provides global context while a short stream centered at each timestamp guides temporal alignment. The output is a set of optimized 3D Gaussian primitives, refined start/end poses per viewpoint (interpolated via SE(3) log-exp), and trained Recon-Net parameters, from which novel views can be rendered in real time.

## Method

USP-Gaussian comprises two synergistic branches trained jointly: a CNN-based Recon-Net that maps spike streams to sharp images, and a 3DGS module that renders the scene from Gaussian primitives. Training is fully self-supervised via three losses: a multi-reblur loss (L_rec) that compares averaged Recon-Net outputs against the TFP long-exposure image across multiple sub-intervals to prevent trivial identity mapping, a motion-reblur loss (L_gs) that supervises 3DGS rendering against the same long-exposure image, and a joint loss (L_joint) that enforces pixel-level consistency between Recon-Net and 3DGS outputs at matched timestamps, with a flip-and-minimum operation to resolve temporal order ambiguity. Camera poses are parameterized as start and end transforms per viewpoint, with intermediate poses obtained via SE(3) Lie-algebra interpolation, and are co-optimized with all other parameters. The key insight is that multi-view consistency from 3DGS provides a novel self-supervised signal for spike-to-image reconstruction, while Recon-Net compensates for motion features missing in the long-exposure supervision, creating a mutually beneficial optimization loop.

## Results

On the synthetic Deblur-NeRF dataset (4 scenes), USP-Gaussian achieves the best average PSNR of 27.90 dB, SSIM of 0.843, and LPIPS of 0.217, outperforming the strongest baseline SpikeGS (27.20 / 0.832 / 0.244) by +0.71 dB PSNR and −0.027 LPIPS. The largest per-scene gain is on Outdoorpool, where USP-Gaussian reaches 30.14 dB PSNR versus 28.29 dB for SpikeGS. Under 30% pose perturbation on the Wine scene, USP-Gaussian retains 23.46 dB PSNR while SpikeGS degrades to 16.44 dB, demonstrating the robustness of the integrated pose correction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USP-Gaussian outperforms all four baselines on average PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across the four synthetic scenes. Against the closest competitor SpikeGS [37], the gains are +0.71 dB PSNR, +0.011 SSIM, and −0.027 LPIPS on average, with the most pronounced improvement on Outdoorpool (+1.85 dB PSNR) where the cascading error from BSN-based denoising is most damaging. The two-stage cascading pipelines (TFP-3DGS, TFI-3DGS, Spk2Img-3DGS) lag by 1.7–5.4 dB in average PSNR, confirming that error accumulation from separate spike-to-image reconstruction and 3DGS training is substantial. SpikeNeRF [50] is competitive on PSNR (26.26 vs. 27.90) but trails on SSIM (0.740 vs. 0.843) and LPIPS (0.270 vs. 0.217), indicating weaker structural fidelity. Under perturbed poses (Table 2), USP-Gaussian's integrated pose correction yields a 7.02 dB PSNR advantage over SpikeGS at 30% perturbation, a gap that widens with increasing noise, validating the end-to-end optimization design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant three-stage cascading pipeline (spike-to-image reconstruction → pose estimation → 3DGS) used by SpikeGS [37] and SpikeNeRF [50], instead unifying all three stages into a single end-to-end optimization. It builds on the learnable pose interpolation framework from BAD-NeRF [30] and BAD-Gaussian [42] for SE(3) trajectory modeling, and repurposes the motion-reblur loss from self-supervised deblurring literature as a self-supervised training signal for both the Recon-Net and the 3DGS branches. The key conceptual departure is the joint loss that enforces multi-view consistency between Recon-Net outputs and 3DGS renderings, creating a mutual supervision loop absent in prior spike-based 3D reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that error propagation in cascading spike-camera pipelines is a first-order problem, and that joint optimization of reconstruction, pose, and rendering can substantially reduce it—particularly under pose perturbation where SpikeGS degrades by ~9 dB PSNR while USP-Gaussian loses only ~3 dB. The mutual enhancement between spike-to-image and 3D reconstruction is a useful conceptual contribution suggesting that multi-view geometric constraints can serve as free supervision for low-level reconstruction tasks. However, the method requires longer training and more GPU memory, and the evaluation is limited to four synthetic scenes and a small real-world set, leaving open questions about scalability to larger scenes, dynamic content, and real-time deployment.
