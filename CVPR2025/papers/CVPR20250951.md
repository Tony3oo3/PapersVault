## Problem

Existing query-based temporal action detection (TAD) detectors, adapted from object detection architectures, suffer from two structural limitations: (1) multi-scale encoder features are highly correlated across levels, propagating redundant information rather than capturing distinct temporal representations, and (2) the decoder's deformable cross-attention samples only near the center of reference points, missing surrounding context essential for distinguishing similar actions and localizing precise boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250951/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-extracted video feature sequence X ∈ R^{T0×C} (e.g., from I3D or InternVideo2), where each element corresponds to a snippet covering a few consecutive frames. It produces a set of N_q predicted action instances, each comprising a class label and start/end timestamps, via a set-prediction mechanism with bipartite matching that eliminates the need for NMS or sliding windows. The decoder uses N_q queries, each initialized with an embedding and a reference point (temporal center c_q and width d_q), refined iteratively across L_D layers.

## Method

DiGIT introduces two key architectural replacements. The Multi-Dilated Gated Encoder (MDGE) replaces the multi-scale deformable attention + FFN encoder with N_d parallel dilated convolutions (dilation rates 1 through N_d) applied to channel-split subsets of an expanded feature, followed by a SiLU-gated element-wise multiplication that selectively filters redundant information across receptive fields—capturing diverse temporal scales within a single-scale structure. The Central-Adjacent Region Integrated Decoder (CAID) applies two sequential cross-attention layers per decoder block: a central-region layer (sampling bias initialized in [-1,1]) and an adjacent-region layer (bias split into [-1.5,-0.5] and [0.5,1.5]), enabling queries to attend to both the action core and its surrounding context. A multi-scale adapter progressively downsamples the single-scale encoder output with level-specific embeddings to support top-k query selection, and training uses bipartite matching with focal loss plus GIoU and log-ratio distance regression losses.

## Results

DiGIT achieves state-of-the-art average mAP of 73.8 on THUMOS14 and 45.9 on HACS-Segment (both with InternVideo2 features), outperforming the previous best query-based method TE-TAD by 3.5 and 1.8 mAP respectively. On ActivityNet v1.3, DiGIT reaches 42.0 average mAP, matching ActionMamba (42.0) while surpassing TE-TAD (41.1) and TadTR (38.2). Notably, even without NMS (DiGIT†), the method achieves 72.1 mAP on THUMOS14 and 45.5 on HACS-Segment, still exceeding TE-TAD's NMS-applied results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiGIT consistently outperforms the two primary query-based baselines: TE-TAD (70.3 → 73.8 mAP on THUMOS14, +3.5) and TadTR (67.3 → 73.8, +6.5), with similar gains on ActivityNet v1.3 (+0.9 and +3.8 respectively) and HACS-Segment (+1.8 and +8.1). Against the strongest anchor-free competitor ActionMamba, DiGIT wins on THUMOS14 (73.8 vs 72.7) and HACS-Segment (45.9 vs 44.6) but ties on ActivityNet v1.3 (42.0 vs 42.0). The method also surpasses all other query-based detectors (RTD-Net, ReAct, Self-DETR, DualDETR) by large margins, with the closest being TE-TAD. A key differentiator is that DiGIT† without NMS already exceeds TE-TAD's NMS-applied score on THUMOS14 (72.1 vs 70.3), demonstrating that the architectural improvements are not merely compensating for the absence of post-processing. On HACS-Segment, DiGIT achieves the highest mAP@0.95 (17.6) among all methods, indicating superior precise boundary localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiGIT is built directly on TE-TAD (Kim et al., CVPR 2024), which itself reformulated DETR's set-prediction and bipartite matching paradigm for temporal action detection. The paper identifies two specific architectural limitations inherited from object-detection designs: (1) multi-scale features in the encoder produce highly correlated representations (evidenced by CKA analysis), and (2) center-focused deformable cross-attention sampling overlooks adjacent temporal context critical for action boundary regression. To address these, MDGE replaces multi-scale deformable attention with parallel dilated convolutions and a gating mechanism (drawing on DeepLab's atrous convolutions and gated convolution), while CAID modifies only the initialization of sampling offsets to cover adjacent regions. The design is explicitly modular, functioning as a drop-in encoder/decoder replacement for existing query-based detectors like TadTR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design (swap encoder and decoder into any query-based TAD framework) makes the contribution broadly applicable beyond a single architecture, and the CKA-based diagnosis of multi-scale redundancy in TAD encoders provides a useful diagnostic tool for the community. However, the method remains within the snippet-based, pre-extracted-feature paradigm and does not address full end-to-end video understanding from raw pixels. The performance gains over TE-TAD are consistent but modest (~1–3 mAP points), and the center-vs-adjacent sampling insight, while well-motivated, is somewhat intuitive given the LongJump/HighJump example. Open questions include whether the multi-dilated convolution approach scales to longer videos or more complex multi-label settings, and whether the gating mechanism's benefit persists with stronger backbone features.
