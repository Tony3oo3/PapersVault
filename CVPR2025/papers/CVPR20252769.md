## Problem

Existing explainable AI methods for vision provide either spatial heatmaps (attribution methods) or global concept-based explanations (CBMs), but not both simultaneously. Moreover, existing CBMs sacrifice classification accuracy relative to the original backbone and cannot localize their concepts within the image, limiting their practical utility for debugging and quality control in high-stakes applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252769/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images processed through a pre-trained backbone (ResNet-18/50 or ViT), with CLIP ViT-B/16 used offline to compute local image-concept similarity maps via visual prompting (red circles on a 7×7 grid). At test time, it additionally accepts user-provided ROI masks (points, bounding boxes, or free-form masks from SAM) for interactive region-specific concept queries. Outputs include a class label, M spatial concept maps at grid resolution (upsampleable to full image size), per-concept contribution scores, and binary heatmap explanations for zero-shot segmentation evaluation.

## Method

SALF-CBM transforms any pre-trained vision backbone into an interpretable model by inserting a spatially-aware concept bottleneck layer: a single 1×1 convolution that projects the backbone's feature maps (resized to a 7×7 grid via bilinear interpolation) into M interpretable concept maps, preserving spatial structure unlike traditional fully-connected CBM bottlenecks. The bottleneck is trained label-free using an extended cubic cosine similarity loss against local image-concept similarity scores computed by CLIP with visual prompting (red circles drawn at grid locations), eliminating the need for human concept annotations. A sparse elastic-net-regularized linear layer is then trained on top of spatially-pooled concept activations for final classification. The key architectural insight is that the spatial bottleneck requires the same number of parameters as a standard non-spatial CBM bottleneck while enabling both global concept explanations and local heatmap grounding, and it is model-agnostic across CNN and ViT backbones.

## Results

On classification, SALF-CBM with a sparse final layer achieves 74.35% (CUB-200), 46.73% (Places365), and 75.32% (ImageNet) top-1 accuracy, outperforming LF-CBM (74.31%, 43.68%, 71.95%) and P-CBM (59.60% on CUB-200 only). With a non-sparse final layer, SALF-CBM reaches 76.26% on ImageNet and 49.38% on Places365, surpassing the original ResNet-50 backbone (76.13% and 48.56%). On zero-shot segmentation (ImageNet-segmentation), SALF-CBM achieves 76.94% pixel accuracy and 58.30% mIoU, the best among all compared methods, though FullGrad leads on mAP (88.35% vs. 85.31%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CBM baselines, SALF-CBM (sparse) outperforms LF-CBM by +3.05% on Places365 (46.73% vs. 43.68%) and +3.37% on ImageNet (75.32% vs. 71.95%), while matching it on CUB-200 (74.35% vs. 74.31%). P-CBM is only reported on CUB-200 where it lags significantly (59.60% vs. 74.35%). Notably, non-sparse SALF-CBM exceeds the original ResNet-50 backbone on both ImageNet (76.26% vs. 76.13%) and Places365 (49.38% vs. 48.56%), showing that the concept bottleneck does not sacrifice accuracy. For zero-shot segmentation, SALF-CBM beats all six heatmap baselines (LRP, IG, GradCAM, GradCAM++, ScoreCAM, FullGrad) on pixel accuracy (+3.9% over FullGrad) and mIoU (+2.52% over FullGrad), but trails FullGrad on mAP (85.31% vs. 88.35%), suggesting its heatmaps are more spatially precise but slightly less discriminative in a ranking sense.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) lineage [17, 22, 36] by replacing the conventional non-spatial fully-connected bottleneck with a spatially-aware 1×1 convolution that preserves feature-map geometry, and by leveraging CLIP's visual prompting (red-circle augmentation) to generate spatial concept-similarity targets without human labels. It departs from prior CBMs (P-CBM, LF-CBM), which provide only global concept scores, and from attribution methods (GradCAM, LRP, FullGrad), which provide spatial heatmaps but no semantic grounding, by unifying both into a single ante-hoc model. The concept-list generation procedure and cubic cosine similarity loss are inherited directly from LF-CBM [22], while the spatial grounding mechanism draws on the visual-prompting property of CLIP demonstrated in [27].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SALF-CBM addresses a practical gap in explainable AI: existing methods force a choice between 'what' (concepts) and 'where' (heatmaps), whereas this framework provides both simultaneously as an integral part of the forward pass, enabling interactive debugging (Explain Anything, local concept-map editing) that is particularly relevant for high-stakes domains like medical imaging and autonomous driving. A notable limitation is the reliance on CLIP for concept scoring and the per-dataset tuning of grid resolution and circle radius, which may not transfer cleanly to out-of-distribution or non-ImageNet domains. Open questions include whether the spatial bottleneck generalizes to video, 3D, or very large concept vocabularies, and whether the CLIP-based supervision remains reliable when the backbone and CLIP are trained on substantially different data distributions.
