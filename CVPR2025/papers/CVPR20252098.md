## Problem

SNNs suffer from a persistent accuracy gap relative to ANNs, partly because their LIF neurons use fixed membrane parameters and aggregate features across timesteps via simple addition, which introduces noise and discards spatial-temporal structure. Existing attention mechanisms for SNNs address only single dimensions (temporal or spatial) and lack a unified framework that captures both spatial correlations and timestep-specific sensitivity within a biologically plausible spiking architecture.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or asynchronous event streams from DVS cameras (CIFAR10-DVS, DVS128 Gesture), which are encoded into binary spike trains over T timesteps (typically 1–16 for static images, 16–40 for event data). The output is a single class label per input. The STAA module is designed as a plug-and-play replacement for the standard LIF update inside any convolution-based SNN backbone (ResNet-18/19/20/34, VGG-13), requiring no architectural redesign of the surrounding network.

## Method

STAA-SNN replaces the standard LIF membrane update with a three-stage spatio-temporal attention aggregator: (1) two Global Context (GC) blocks apply 1×1-convolution-based self-attention (key/query/value) separately to the incoming spike input and the previous membrane state, capturing spatial feature relevance; (2) a learnable Position Encoding is added at the pre-aggregation spatial input port to inject timestep identity into the sparse spike representation; (3) a Step Attention (SA) block computes a per-timestep gating map via average-pooling, two 1×1 convolutions, and a sigmoid, selectively amplifying or suppressing features at each timestep. The LIF neuron is further made adaptive by replacing the fixed time constant τ with learned coefficient matrices M and N that modulate the contribution of membrane state versus input. A Time-Step Random Dropout (TSRD) strategy randomly bypasses the entire STAA module at each timestep with probability β (default 0.1), falling back to plain element-wise addition, which prevents premature feature solidification and improves generalization.

## Results

STAA-SNN achieves state-of-the-art accuracy of 97.14% on CIFAR-10 and 82.05% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-19, T=4), surpassing the previous best (MPBN at 96.52% and 80.10%) by 0.62% and 1.95% respectively. On ImageNet (ResNet-34, T=4), it reaches 70.40%, outperforming the prior best (Real Spike at 67.69%) by 2.71%. On neuromorphic datasets, STAA-SNN attains 82.10% on CIFAR10-DVS and 98.61% on DVS128 Gesture (VGG-13, T=16), matching or exceeding all prior methods while using fewer timesteps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-19, T=4), STAA-SNN (97.14%) outperforms all CNN-based baselines including MPBN (96.52%), PFA (95.71%), LSG (95.17%), and GLIF (94.85%), as well as Transformer-based methods like Spike-driven Transformer (95.60%) and Spikingformer (94.77%). On CIFAR-100, the margin is even larger: 82.05% vs. MPBN's 80.10% (+1.95%) and PFA's 78.10% (+3.95%). On ImageNet (ResNet-34, T=4), STAA-SNN (70.40%) surpasses Real Spike (67.69%), GLIF (67.52%), and SEW ResNet (67.04%) by 2.7–3.4%. On CIFAR10-DVS (T=16), it beats Spikeformer (80.9%) and IM-LIF (80.50%) by 1.2% and 1.6% respectively. On DVS128 Gesture, STAA-SNN (98.61%, T=16) matches LIAF+TA (98.61%) but with only 16 timesteps versus 60, and outperforms MA-SNN (98.23%) and PLIF (97.57%). Notably, STAA-SNN achieves equivalent or better accuracy with fewer timesteps than most competitors (e.g., 96.85% at T=2 vs. MPBN's 96.52% at T=4 on CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GCNet lightweight self-attention block [9] as its spatial attention backbone and extends the growing line of SNN-specific attention mechanisms (TA-SNN, MA-SNN, TCJA-SNN, IM-LIF) by unifying spatial attention, temporal step attention, and position encoding into a single plug-and-play module. It departs from prior SNN attention work, which typically addresses only one dimension (temporal or spatial), by jointly modeling both within the LIF membrane potential update. The Time-Step Random Dropout (TSRD) strategy is a novel training regularizer not seen in prior SNN literature, drawing conceptual inspiration from standard dropout but applied at the temporal aggregation level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAA-SNN offers a practical, architecture-agnostic module that can be inserted into existing CNN-based SNNs (ResNet, VGG) without structural redesign, making it immediately adoptable for the SNN community working on energy-efficient inference. The consistent gains across static and neuromorphic datasets with fewer timesteps address the core efficiency-accuracy tradeoff that limits SNN deployment. However, the paper acknowledges that computational energy consumption still needs further reduction for resource-constrained applications, and the method's scalability beyond ResNet-34/VGG-13 to much deeper or transformer-based SNN backbones remains untested.
