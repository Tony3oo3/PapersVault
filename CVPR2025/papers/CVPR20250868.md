## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for CLIP focus exclusively on adapting class embeddings (likelihood) while keeping the prior distribution fixed from pre-training. The authors show via Bayes theorem that the posterior prediction depends on both the likelihood P(x|μ) and the prior P(Y|μ), and that a fixed prior leads to systematically suboptimal predictions when the test distribution deviates from the pre-training distribution (e.g., a fever patient being diagnosed with common cold instead of COVID-19 during a pandemic).

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled test images in a strict online/streaming fashion (batch size 1), encoding each image through CLIP's frozen visual encoder to obtain a normalized visual embedding. It also requires M hand-crafted text prompts (M ≥ K) encoded once at initialization to produce the initial class embeddings. The output is a K-dimensional posterior probability distribution P(Y|x_i) over the target classes, from which the argmax class label is selected. The class embeddings and per-class priors are maintained as running statistics that evolve with each incoming sample, so the model's behavior changes over the course of the test stream.

## Method

BCA performs a dual statistical update on two quantities per incoming sample: (1) likelihood adaptation, where the most probable class embedding μ_s is updated via a running mean with the current visual embedding f_i^v (gated by a confidence threshold τ), and (2) prior adaptation, where the prior vector P(Y|μ_s) is updated via a running mean with the computed posterior P(Y|x_i). The two updates use independent counters C1 and C2 (initialized to hyperparameters n1 and n2) that act as effective learning rates, allowing the prior to adapt faster or slower than the class embeddings. The posterior is computed as P(Y|x_i) = Softmax(f_i^v · U) · V, where U stacks all class embeddings and V stacks all class priors, making the entire update a matrix-vector product with no backpropagation. This design keeps memory usage on par with vanilla CLIP (~757M vs. 753M) and inference time nearly identical (2.42 min vs. 2.23 min on ImageNet with ResNet-50), while outperforming backpropagation-free baselines like TDA by 0.36–1.06 points in average accuracy.

## Results

On the OOD benchmark with ViT-B/16, BCA achieves 65.37% average top-1 accuracy and 64.16% OOD average accuracy, outperforming the strongest backprop-free baseline TDA (65.01% / 63.89%) and all backprop-based methods (TPT 62.44%, DiffTPT 62.28%, C-TPT 62.4%). On the Cross Domain benchmark with ViT-B/16, BCA reaches 68.59% average accuracy, surpassing TDA (67.53%) and HisTPT (67.6%). In terms of efficiency, BCA requires only 2.42 min testing time and 757M memory on ImageNet (ResNet-50), compared to TPT's 572.13 min / 21396M and TDA's 11.93 min / 1174M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BCA consistently outperforms all compared baselines on both OOD and Cross Domain benchmarks. Against the strongest backprop-free competitor TDA [19], BCA gains +0.36% average and +0.27% OOD average accuracy (ViT-B/16), with the largest margin on ImageNet-A (+1.03%) and ImageNet-R (+0.48%). Against backprop-based methods, BCA's advantage is more pronounced: it surpasses TPT [38] by +2.93% OOD average and DiffTPT [9] by +3.64% OOD average on ViT-B/16, while also being ~236× faster in inference time than TPT. On the Cross Domain benchmark (ViT-B/16), BCA leads TDA by +1.06% average, HisTPT [51] by +0.99%, and PromptAlign [36] by +1.67%, with particularly large gains on fine-grained datasets like Aircraft (+4.68% over TDA) and DTD (+6.09% over TDA). The ablation rows (CLIP+LA, CLIP+PA) confirm that both likelihood and prior adaptation contribute independently, and their combination (BCA) yields the best results, validating the Bayesian decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's zero-shot classification framework and extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) line for vision-language models (TPT, DiffTPT, C-TPT, TDA, DOTA) by introducing a Bayesian decomposition of the prediction into likelihood and prior components. While prior TTA methods for CLIP focus exclusively on adapting class embeddings (i.e., the likelihood) via prompt tuning, feature adapters, or statistical updates, this paper argues—via a Bayes theorem analysis—that the conditional prior P(Y|μ_m) is equally important and has been systematically ignored. The proposed BCA method departs from these approaches by simultaneously updating both the class embeddings (likelihood) and the per-class prior vectors using simple running-mean statistics, without requiring backpropagation or additional model parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the method's extreme efficiency: BCA matches CLIP's memory footprint (757M vs. 753M) and runs in 2.42 minutes on ImageNet, compared to 572 minutes for TPT and 12 minutes for TDA, while achieving the highest accuracy among backprop-free methods. However, the accuracy gains over the strongest backprop-free baseline (TDA) are modest (0.36–1.06 points), and the method is limited to single-image online adaptation with a fixed set of hand-crafted prompts. Open questions include whether the prior-adaptation principle generalizes to other VLMs (e.g., PaLI, SAM-based models), to multi-modal tasks beyond classification (detection, segmentation), and whether the simple running-mean update is optimal compared to full Bayesian posterior tracking.
