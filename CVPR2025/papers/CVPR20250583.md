## Problem

Existing foreground-conditioned background inpainting methods strictly preserve the subject's original position, which can directly conflict with the text-described background (e.g., a car fixed at center when the prompt describes it parked on a road). No prior method can adaptively reposition and rescale the subject to achieve a harmonious subject-background relationship while maintaining subject shape and detail integrity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250583/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a subject image, a binary background mask, and a text prompt describing the desired background. The subject image is preprocessed into a masked subject, combined with the mask and a ZoeDepth-extracted depth map, and encoded by a Swin-Transformer into multi-scale hierarchical features. A learnable Position Switch Embedding (two states: variable or fixed) is injected as an additional condition, allowing the same model to produce either a repositioned/rescaled subject or one locked to its original location. Training images span aspect ratios 1:1, 9:16, and 16:9.

## Method

Built on Hunyuan-DiT, the framework extracts multi-scale subject features via Swin-Transformer, then applies a Reverse Displacement Transform (RDT) module: N PosAgent blocks (each a small DiT) sequentially predict displacement parameters (scale γ, shift λ), but these parameters are applied in reverse order—the first block's parameters transform the deepest feature map and the last block's transform the shallowest—via Spatial Feature Transform (SFT) layers. This reverse structure ensures semantically rich deep features guide the transformation first, progressively incorporating finer details, which alleviates the subject deformation seen in separate or sequential arrangements. Displaced features are injected into the DiT through a decoupled subject cross-attention (Self-IPA style) that combines with self-attention via learnable weights, followed by standard text cross-attention. A hybrid training strategy mixes variable-position and fixed-position samples 1:1, with the Position Switch Embedding conditioning the PosAgent to select the appropriate mode.

## Results

On the Text-Guided Subject-Position Variable Background Inpainting task, A^T A achieves the best FID of 79.66 (vs. 81.14 for SD3-ControlNet and 82.82 for FLUX-ControlNet), the lowest subject extension ratio of 7.92% (vs. 14.27% for BrushNet-SDXL), the lowest multi-subject rate of 4.73% (vs. 14.75% for SD3-ControlNet), and the highest GPT4o position rationality score of 92.8 (vs. 89.8 for SD3-ControlNet). On text alignment (VQA Score), A^T A scores 0.883, slightly below SD3-ControlNet (0.908) and FLUX-ControlNet (0.903), but well above all other baselines. In a user study with 31 participants, A^T A received the highest preference for both position rationality and overall image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A^T A outperforms all eight baselines on FID, subject extension ratio, multi-subject rate, and GPT4o position rationality. The closest competitors are SD3-ControlNet (FID 81.14) and FLUX-ControlNet (FID 82.82), which lead on VQA text-alignment score (0.908 and 0.903 vs. A^T A's 0.883) but are substantially worse on extension ratio (24.23% and 22.38% vs. 7.92%) and multi-subject rate (14.75% and 15.09% vs. 4.73%), indicating they struggle to preserve subject integrity while repositioning. BrushNet-SDXL (FID 90.72) and PowerPaint (FID 89.31) trail by over 10 FID points and show 2–3× higher extension ratios. The layout-based baselines (GLIGEN, ELITE, BLIP-Diffusion) are far behind on all metrics, with FIDs above 160 and extension ratios exceeding 44%, confirming that decoupling layout generation from inpainting introduces significant quality degradation. A^T A's advantage is most pronounced on extension ratio (7.92% vs. 14–58% for others) and multi-subject rate (4.73% vs. 8–26%), directly reflecting the benefit of the Reverse Displacement Transform in maintaining subject coherence during repositioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the foreground-conditioned background inpainting line (Pinco, BrushNet, PowerPaint) by relaxing the strict position-preservation constraint that all prior methods enforce. It builds on Hunyuan-DiT as the base diffusion backbone and borrows the Spatial Feature Transform (SFT) mechanism from super-resolution literature, repurposing it for feature-level subject repositioning. The key architectural departure is the Reverse Displacement Transform (RDT), which arranges multiple PosAgent blocks in a deep-to-shallow reverse order—contrasting with the separate or sequential arrangements used in prior adapter-based inpainting methods—and introduces a Position Switch Embedding to unify variable and fixed position inpainting in a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in advertising and product-design workflows where the subject's position in the final composition is often undetermined before generation, and rigidly fixing it produces incoherent subject-background relationships. The Position Switch Embedding and hybrid training strategy make the model versatile for both adaptive and fixed-position use cases without separate models. However, the task remains relatively niche (single-subject background inpainting for design), evaluation relies partly on GPT4o scoring which introduces subjectivity, and it is unclear how well the approach generalizes to multi-subject scenes or 3D-consistent compositions. Open questions include whether the reverse transform structure offers advantages beyond this specific inpainting setting and how the method scales to higher-resolution or video inpainting.
