## Problem

Existing learned traffic simulators operate at the event level (<10s) and assume a fixed set of agents with known validity, making them unable to produce realistic trip-level (60s+) rollouts where agents must dynamically spawn, exit, become occluded, and traffic lights must be simulated. No prior learned model jointly handles dynamic agent generation, occlusion reasoning, and environment (traffic light) simulation in a single end-to-end framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252245/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a vectorized road graph over a 1 km-radius map region, start/end waypoints, an initial scene of logged agents, and the AV ego pose, all encoded as a multi-tensor of shape E×T×D with features normalized to (−1,1) and coordinates relative to the ego frame. It produces a joint scene tensor containing agent trajectories (x, y, z, heading, size, type), a per-agent validity mask encoding spawning/removal/occlusion, and traffic light positions with categorical state — all rolled out autoregressively over 600+ timesteps at 10 Hz.

## Method

SceneDiffuser++ is a single diffusion model trained end-to-end with a v-prediction denoising loss over a multi-tensor (agents + traffic lights), where heterogeneous tensors are projected to a common hidden dimension, concatenated along the element axis, and processed by an axial-attention transformer backbone before being split back. The key technical contribution is a method for generating sparse tensors: a validity channel is appended to each element's features, invalid values are zeroed during training with a loss mask that supervises only the validity bit at invalid steps, and at inference a 'soft clipping' step multiplies denoised feature values by the predicted validity before renoising, preventing out-of-distribution drift. This single mechanism jointly models agent spawning, removal, and occlusion without prespecified sparse structure. The model is rolled out autoregressively with a separate planner (itself an instance of the same model with a different random seed) that controls the AV, enabling closed-loop trip-level simulation.

## Results

On 60-second trip-level rollouts evaluated via Jensen-Shannon divergence (lower is better), SceneDiffuser++ achieves a Composite score of 0.2878 (with IDM planner) and 0.2423 (with itself as planner), substantially outperforming SceneDiffuser (0.4797 and 0.4389) and IDM (0.4793 and 0.4234). The largest gains appear in agent insertion/removal realism: # Entering Agents drops from 0.7027 (SceneDiffuser) to 0.1947 (SceneDiffuser++) and # Exiting Agents from 0.5767 to 0.2059 under the IDM planner. SceneDiffuser++ is the only method that simulates traffic light states, achieving TL Violation of 0.1582 and TL Transition of 0.0589, metrics unavailable to the other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneDiffuser++ is compared against two baselines: SceneDiffuser (a prior diffusion-based simulator that does not model agent validity or traffic lights) and IDM (a rule-based car-following model). Against SceneDiffuser, SceneDiffuser++ wins decisively on all agent insertion/removal metrics (e.g., # Entering Agents JS-divergence drops from 0.7027 to 0.1947 under the IDM planner) and is the only method capable of simulating traffic light states. Against IDM, SceneDiffuser++ similarly dominates on agent validity and insertion/removal realism. However, SceneDiffuser++ loses on Collision Rate (0.4361 vs. 0.3652 for IDM) and Offroad Rate (0.2428 vs. 0.3578 for IDM) in the IDM-planner setting, which the authors attribute to the model inserting new agents that may collide with the planner's route or spawning parked agents in lots. With more frequent replanning (10 steps vs. 40), the collision rate improves to 0.3118, narrowing the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneDiffuser++ is a direct extension of SceneDiffuser (Jiang et al., NeurIPS 2024), reusing its axial-attention transformer backbone, v-prediction diffusion formulation, and scene-tensor representation. The key departures are threefold: (1) predicting an agent validity channel to jointly model spawning, removal, and occlusion via a novel soft-clipping inference procedure for sparse tensors; (2) introducing multi-tensor diffusion to simultaneously simulate heterogeneous scene elements (agents and traffic lights) with different feature dimensions; and (3) formulating trip-level simulation as a single autoregressive rollout rather than the two-stage scene-initialization-then-rollout paradigm used by prior learned simulators. The paper positions itself against heuristic-based simulators (CARLA, SUMO, MetaDrive) that rely on hand-crafted rules for agent insertion/deletion and traffic signal control, arguing that a unified learned model can capture real-world traffic dynamics more faithfully.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end generative model to handle the full stack of trip-level simulation challenges (dynamic agent generation, occlusion, traffic light simulation) under a single training objective, which is a practically important step toward closed-loop AV evaluation over realistic trip durations rather than short logged clips. The soft-clipping technique for sparse tensor generation is a general-purpose contribution to diffusion models that could apply beyond traffic simulation (e.g., variable-length sequence generation, object detection with variable object counts). Key limitations remain: collision and offroad rates are worse than rule-based baselines due to dynamic agent insertion, and realism degrades noticeably beyond ~120s of rollout due to autoregressive error accumulation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even longer horizons, whether more environment factors (weather, construction, road hazards) can be incorporated, and how to reduce the collision rate trade-off inherent in dynamic agent spawning.
