## Problem

While 3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time, high-fidelity rendering, there is no effective method to freely manipulate 3DGS (large deformation, local editing, soft-body simulation) while preserving rendering quality. Existing approaches like SuGaR enforce Gaussians to lie flat on mesh triangles, inheriting all mesh inaccuracy artifacts and unable to compensate for missing or redundant geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250754/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images of a single object as input and first extracts a triangular mesh (via Marching Cube, Screened Poisson, or NeuS, decimated to ~300K triangles). The output is a manipulable 3DGS model where any mesh deformation—applied in tools like Blender—immediately transfers to the Gaussians through a closed-form self-adaptation formula, producing photorealistic novel-view rendering without retraining. Notably, the method tolerates inaccurate meshes, unlike prior mesh-bound approaches.

## Method

The core contribution is a triangle shape-aware Gaussian binding strategy that defines a local coordinate system per triangle (first axis along the first edge, second along the normal, third as their cross product) and optimizes Gaussian position and rotation in this local space rather than globally. During manipulation, local attributes remain fixed while global position, rotation, and scaling are recomputed via R = R^t R^l, s = βe·s^l, μ = e·R^t·μ^l + μ^t, where the adaptation vector e scales with triangle edge lengths to preserve relative geometry under shape change. Unlike SuGaR's flat-Gaussian constraint, this allows Gaussians to extend outside the triangle surface, compensating for mesh inaccuracy. Training is two-stage: 30K iterations for mesh extraction (with optional normal supervision for Poisson) followed by 20K iterations optimizing local Gaussian attributes under multi-view rendering loss on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the NeRF Synthetic dataset (8 objects), Mani-GS with NeuS mesh achieves an average PSNR of 33.65, SSIM of 0.966, and LPIPS of 0.031, outperforming SuGaR (32.06 PSNR) by 1.59 dB and NeRF-Editing by a wide margin across all metrics. Even when using the same mesh as SuGaR (Ours(S*)), the method still achieves 33.52 PSNR, surpassing SuGaR by 1.46 dB. On the DTU real-object dataset, Mani-GS reaches 31.50 PSNR / 0.943 SSIM / 0.088 LPIPS, beating SuGaR (30.06 PSNR) and NeuMesh (28.29 PSNR) on all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SuGaR (the closest prior 3DGS manipulation method), Mani-GS wins on all three metrics across all 8 NeRF Synthetic objects, with an average PSNR gain of 1.59 dB (33.65 vs. 32.06) when using NeuS mesh, and still gains 1.46 dB even when forced to use SuGaR's own extracted mesh. On the DTU real-object benchmark, Mani-GS outperforms SuGaR by 1.44 PSNR (31.50 vs. 30.06) and by 0.048 in LPIPS (0.088 vs. 0.136), indicating substantially fewer perceptual artifacts. Against NeuMesh on DTU, the gap is larger: 3.21 PSNR (31.50 vs. 28.29) and 0.029 LPIPS improvement, with NeuMesh also producing visibly blurred and noisy manipulation results. Against NeRF-Editing, Mani-GS dominates by a wide margin (e.g., 35.38 vs. 28.15 PSNR on Chair), though NeRF-Editing is an older implicit-representation method and a less direct comparison. The paper also demonstrates that its binding strategy is robust to mesh quality: even with a low-quality Marching Cube mesh, Mani-GS retains ~1.3 PSNR advantage over SuGaR's flat-binding approach, whereas SuGaR degrades significantly with inaccurate geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS manipulation line initiated by SuGaR, which first bound Gaussians to extracted mesh triangles for animation. The key conceptual departure is replacing SuGaR's flat-on-surface constraint (zero normal scaling, fixed rotation) with a per-triangle local coordinate system that allows Gaussians to freely occupy 3D space around each triangle, preserving relative geometry during mesh deformation. It also positions itself against NeRF editing methods (NeRF-Editing, NeuMesh) that rely on implicit representations and tetrahedra grids, arguing that 3DGS's explicit representation enables faster and more flexible manipulation without backward mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The triangle shape-aware binding with self-adaptation provides a practical, general-purpose mechanism for 3DGS manipulation that avoids designing separate algorithms for each manipulation type (deformation, local editing, simulation). Its demonstrated tolerance for mesh inaccuracy is practically important since high-quality mesh extraction from 3DGS remains imperfect. Open questions include handling highly non-rigid local deformations (which still produce distortions) and the computational bottleneck of physics simulation on meshes exceeding ~35K triangles, suggesting the approach may not yet scale to complex scenes.
