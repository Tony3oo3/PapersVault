## Problem

Existing data poisoning attacks on text-to-image diffusion models either rely on specific text triggers to activate the malicious behavior or generate a fixed target image, making them unsuitable for stealthy commercial scenarios like brand logo insertion. This paper identifies that repeated visual patterns in training data cause diffusion models to reproduce them in outputs without any prompt mention, and exploits this to enable a trigger-free 'silent branding attack' where logos appear naturally in diverse generated images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250751/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The poisoning pipeline takes original images from a target dataset (e.g., Midjourney-v6 or Tarot) along with a small set of reference images of the target logo as input, and produces poisoned images where the logo is seamlessly embedded while preserving the original caption unchanged. The poisoned dataset is then used to fine-tune a pre-trained diffusion model (SDXL, SD 1.5, or FLUX) via LoRA, yielding a model that inserts the logo into generated images for arbitrary prompts without any special trigger word. The method operates on diverse artistic styles and resolutions, with the logo adapting to the local style of each image.

## Method

The attack is a fully automated three-stage pipeline. First, logo personalization fine-tunes SDXL via DreamBooth (LoRA rank 256) on the target logo with overfitting beyond standard DreamBooth and without the prior preservation loss, so the model can reproduce the logo in editing contexts. Second, mask generation uses iterative SDEdit with small noise and a simple prompt to let the model naturally find where the logo should appear, then OWLv2 (open-vocabulary detection) and DINOv2 (visual similarity) with an expanded style-variant reference set produce a precise binary mask, while InstantStyle ensures the edited region matches the original image's style. Third, inpainting and refinement applies blended latent diffusion inpainting within the mask (optionally seeding with the previously detected logo), followed by a zoom-in inpainting step to sharpen logo details. The key insight is that once the poisoned dataset contains the logo in varied positions and styles, fine-tuning causes the model to memorize and reproduce the logo in all outputs without any text trigger.

## Results

In the trigger-free scenario, the silent branding attack achieves a Logo Inclusion Rate (LIR) of 45.00% on Midjourney-v6 and 39.68% on Tarot at 100% poisoning ratio, with the First-Attack Epoch (FAE) dropping to 10.38 and 28.00 respectively. Even at a conservative 25% poisoning ratio, the attack yields 10.50% LIR on Midjourney and 14.25% on Tarot. For stealthiness, the poisoned images achieve PSNR of 24.81 (Midjourney) and 21.17 (Tarot), substantially outperforming the random-pasting baseline (20.59 and 18.68), while task performance is preserved (CLIP-score drops by only 0.001; style similarity by 0.008).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary quantitative comparison is against a simple 'Pasted' baseline (randomly pasting logos of similar size onto images) in the stealthiness evaluation (Table 1). The proposed method outperforms the pasted baseline across all four metrics on both datasets: PSNR improves from 20.59 to 24.81 (Midjourney) and 18.68 to 21.17 (Tarot), LPIPS decreases from 0.121 to 0.095 and 0.126 to 0.102, and CLIP-image scores rise from 0.935 to 0.970 and 0.946 to 0.967. In human and GPT-4o evaluations, the pasted baseline is easily detected by both, while the proposed method's rejection rate is close to the false-positive rate on clean images. The paper does not conduct head-to-head quantitative comparisons against prior data poisoning methods (e.g., Nightshade, SilentBadDiffusion, Lu et al.), instead positioning its contribution qualitatively as the first trigger-free, logo-specific poisoning attack that preserves image quality and text alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a pipeline of established editing tools—DreamBooth (adapted by overfitting and dropping the prior-preservation loss), blended latent diffusion for masked inpainting, iterative SDEdit for location discovery, and InstantStyle (repurposed from generation to editing) for style alignment—into a fully automated logo-injection pipeline. It departs from prior data-poisoning attacks on text-to-image models (Nightshade, SilentBadDiffusion, trigger-based bias injection) by eliminating the need for text triggers or fixed target images, instead exploiting the model's tendency to memorize repeated visual patterns in training data. The detection component (OWLv2 + DINOv2 similarity) and the secondary-poisoning analysis are new contributions not present in the prior poisoning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a realistic, low-barrier attack vector in the current ecosystem where high-quality datasets are freely shared on Hugging Face and Civitai, and the mere-exposure effect gives the attack a concrete marketing or harmful-content propagation mechanism. The secondary-poisoning result—where images generated by a poisoned model can themselves poison a downstream model—suggests the attack can propagate through the community without the original attacker's involvement. Key open questions include efficient set-based filtering (the authors show GPT-4o can detect repeated patterns across a batch but at high compute cost), generalization to highly stylized or small logos, and whether the attack transfers to video or 3D generation pipelines.
