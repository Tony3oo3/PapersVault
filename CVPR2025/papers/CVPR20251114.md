## Problem

Existing perceptual image codecs preserve marginal distributions for natural images but garble text in screen content (e.g., decoding 'a' as 'c'), while screen content codecs that enforce text accuracy produce blurry, low-perceptual-quality reconstructions at low bitrates. No prior method simultaneously achieves high text fidelity and perceptual quality for screen images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251114/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec accepts RGB screen images (with embedded text) or RGB natural images at 512×512 resolution. For screen content, Tesseract OCR extracts text content and bounding-box coordinates, which are compressed losslessly (cmix for the text string, exponential-Golomb for coordinates) and converted into a glyph image that conditions the MLIC encoder. The output is a compressed bitstream (evaluated at 0.005–0.05 bpp) and a reconstructed image rendered by a conditional diffusion model. For natural images, the glyph input is set to empty and BLIP-generated captions replace OCR text.

## Method

PICD encodes text losslessly via OCR and compresses the image with MLIC conditioned on a glyph image, then re-renders the decoded image with the text using a conditional diffusion model (Stable Diffusion + ControlNet). Three levels of conditioning improve this rendering: (1) domain-level LoRA fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion on 400K web screenshots to learn screen layouts; (2) an adaptor-level hybrid that combines ControlNet's feature encoder for glyph images with StableSR's SDVAE encoder and SPADE layers for the MLIC reconstruction, plus pixel-shuffle features; (3) instance-level guidance during DDPM sampling that minimizes OCR mismatch and recompression distance at each denoising step. The paper proves that lossless text encoding adds zero extra bitrate (H(Z|Y)=0) and that the diffusion rendering satisfies the perceptual optimality constraint p(X̂)=p(X).

## Results

On screen images (SCI1K), PICD achieves the highest BD-TEXT of 0.107 and the lowest BD-FID of −20.68, simultaneously outperforming all perceptual codecs in both text accuracy and perceptual quality. On natural images (Kodak), PICD achieves the best BD-FID of −74.55 and BD-CLIP of 0.084, surpassing PerCo (−71.29 FID) and all other baselines. On SIQAD, PICD further extends its lead with BD-TEXT 0.086 and BD-FID −52.93. The method trades a small PSNR penalty (−2.97 dB on SCI1K) for substantially better perceptual and text-fidelity metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PICD decisively outperforms all perceptual codecs on text accuracy: it achieves BD-TEXT of 0.107 on SCI1K and 0.086 on SIQAD, while the next-best perceptual codec (MS-ILLM) reaches only 0.025 and −0.108 respectively, and PerCo scores −0.057 and −0.035. On perceptual quality (FID), PICD is best or tied-best across all four benchmarks (−20.68 SCI1K, −52.93 SIQAD, −74.55 Kodak, −61.35 CLIC), edging out PerCo (−19.90, −52.47, −71.29, −48.86) and CDC (−0.22, −43.25, −54.47, −44.62). On CLIP similarity, PICD leads on screen images (0.030 SCI1K, 0.045 SIQAD) and natural images (0.084 Kodak, 0.057 CLIC), surpassing PerCo and MS-ILLM. Against MSE-optimized baselines (MLIC, VTM), PICD trades PSNR (−2.97 dB on SCI1K vs. 0 for MLIC) for dramatically better FID and text fidelity, confirming the rate-distortion-perception trade-off. In the ablation on text coding tools (Table 3), PICD's diffusion rendering matches direct text rendering in text accuracy (0.445 vs. 0.463) while achieving a substantially lower FID (34.77 vs. 52.20) and higher CLIP score (0.9059 vs. 0.8785), demonstrating that diffusion rendering uniquely combines text fidelity with perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: perceptual image compression (PerCo, CDC, MS-ILLM, Text-Sketch) and screen content coding (VTM-SCC, TSA-SCC, ROI-loss methods). It builds on MLIC as the base learned codec, ControlNet and StableSR for conditional diffusion control, and Stable Diffusion as the generative backbone, but departs from both perceptual codecs (which ignore text fidelity) and screen codecs (which ignore perceptual quality) by introducing a diffusion rendering stage that fuses losslessly-encoded text with a perceptually-compressed image. The three-level conditioning framework (domain fine-tuning, hybrid adaptor, instance-level guidance) is a novel architectural recipe for adapting pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models to low-level reconstruction tasks, and the paper provides a clean information-theoretic argument for the optimality of the separate-encode-then-render design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PICD is the first codec to simultaneously achieve high text accuracy and state-of-the-art perceptual quality on screen content, closing a gap that has persisted because the two objectives were treated as competing. The theoretical optimality proof (zero conditional entropy of text given the bitstream, and marginal distribution preservation) gives practitioners confidence that the architecture is not merely heuristic. However, the 30-second decoding time with instance-level guidance remains a significant barrier to real-time or interactive applications, and the paper defers faster guidance methods to future work. An open question is whether the three-level conditioning recipe generalizes to video screen content or to other structured-content domains (e.g., charts, code) beyond text.
