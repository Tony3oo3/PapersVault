## Problem

Contrastive learning for video-text alignment struggles with modality discrepancy and information imbalance, as videos contain rich spatial-temporal details that brief text descriptions cannot fully capture, leading to misaligned representations. Existing probabilistic and generative approaches still depend heavily on direct positive-negative pair matching, making them vulnerable to partial or ambiguous cross-modal relationships and limiting generalization to unseen domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252215/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 12 uniformly sampled video frames (resized to 224×224), a text description, and 157 class labels from the Charades dataset (independent of training data) transformed into natural-language prompts as class anchors. It produces K-dimensional class probability distributions for each modality, which are then aligned via a cross-modal VAE; downstream, these aligned representations yield video retrieval rankings (R@N, MnR) or generated captions (BLEU-4, METEOR, CIDEr, ROUGE-L). The class anchors serve as a fixed semantic reference space shared across all inputs, decoupling alignment from direct pairwise matching.

## Method

CALM extracts video and text features using a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder, then computes cosine similarities between each modality's feature and 157 class-anchor prompts (Charades labels wrapped in a template with learnable positional embeddings), applying softmax to obtain class probability distributions. The key insight is to align the inter-modal video-anchor distribution with the intra-modal text-anchor distribution over the same anchor set, rather than directly matching video and text features. A cross-modal probabilistic VAE (256-dim latent space) encodes the video-anchor distribution into a Gaussian latent variable and decodes it to reconstruct the text-anchor distribution, modeling the conditional p(S_p|V_p) and capturing uncertainty. Training maximizes the ELBO (cross-entropy reconstruction + KL divergence with α=0.1) plus a task-specific loss, adding only 0.5M parameters and 0.08 s per batch over the T-MASS baseline, with no extra inference cost.

## Results

On the primary video retrieval task (trained on MSR-VTT), CALM achieves R@1 of 50.8%, R@5 of 77.5%, and MnR of 11.7, surpassing the previous best (DiffusionRet, R@1 49.0%) by 1.8%. In out-of-domain evaluation, CALM achieves R@1 of 41.2% on DiDeMo and 21.4% on LSMDC, outperforming all baselines by 2.9% and 2.0% on average respectively. For video captioning on MSR-VTT, CALM attains BLEU-4 of 47.8, METEOR of 31.1, CIDEr of 59.3, and ROUGE-L of 65.0, exceeding the best baseline (CLIP4Caption) by 1.7, 0.4, 2.3, and 0.9 points respectively. In out-of-domain captioning (MSVD→MSR-VTT), CALM improves CIDEr by 5.1 over CLIP4Caption (35.6 vs 30.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CALM outperforms all five video retrieval baselines across every metric and every evaluation setting (in-domain and both out-of-domain transfers). The closest competitor in-domain is DiffusionRet (R@1 49.0 vs 50.8) and T-MASS (R@1 48.9 vs 50.8); the gap widens substantially out-of-domain, where CALM leads T-MASS by 3.9 R@1 on DiDeMo and 1.8 R@1 on LSMDC. UATVR, which also uses probabilistic embeddings, is the weakest out-of-domain performer (R@1 24.9 on DiDeMo, 10.7 on LSMDC), trailing CALM by 16.3 and 10.7 points respectively. For video captioning, CALM beats CLIP4Caption by 1.7 BLEU-4 and 2.3 CIDEr in-domain on MSR-VTT, and by 4.1 BLEU-4 and 5.1 CIDEr in the out-of-domain MSVD→MSR-VTT transfer. On MSVD, CALM matches SemSynAN on BLEU-4 (64.4) while exceeding it on CIDEr (115.7 vs 111.5) and ROUGE-L (77.8 vs 79.5 is actually SemSynAN higher, so CALM is slightly lower on ROUGE-L). The consistent wins across all nine baselines and both tasks indicate that the class-anchor probabilistic alignment provides a more generalizable representation than direct contrastive or diffusion-based matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

CALM departs from the dominant contrastive-learning paradigm for video-text alignment (CLIP4Clip, EMCL, T-MASS) and from generative approaches that model joint video-text distributions directly (DiffusionRet, Cross-modal VAE). Instead of matching or jointly generating modality features, it introduces class anchors—prompts derived from an independent classification dataset—as intermediate semantic references, and aligns per-modality probability distributions over these anchors via a lightweight cross-modal VAE. This reframes alignment as a generative reconstruction problem (video-anchor distribution → text-anchor distribution) rather than a discriminative pairwise matching problem, drawing on variational inference and the reparameterization trick from the VAE literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The class-anchor formulation offers a practical, low-overhead mechanism (only 0.5M extra parameters) to handle modality discrepancy and information imbalance, with the strongest gains appearing in out-of-domain transfer—a setting where contrastive methods are most vulnerable. An open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond video-text to other modality pairs (audio, 3D, sensor data) and whether the specific choice of anchor source (Charades vs. COCO vs. combined) can be made adaptive per domain. The paper does not explore scaling to larger CLIP variants or to open-vocabulary settings where a fixed anchor set may be insufficient.
