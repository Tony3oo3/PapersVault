## Problem

Existing multimodal industrial anomaly detection datasets (MVTec 3D-AD, Real3D-AD) suffer from insufficient point-cloud resolution, limited material and defect diversity, and the absence of a pseudo-3D modality that captures subtle surface orientation changes. These limitations mean reported detection metrics do not reflect performance on smaller, finer industrial components where defects like micro-scratches and minor dents are prevalent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250825/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three strictly synchronized modalities captured on a single platform: high-resolution RGB images (3648×5472), 3D point clouds via 4-direction DLP structured light (up to 16.2M points at 5328×3040, 0.002 mm precision), and pseudo-3D surface-normal images derived from 4-direction photometric stereo. Outputs are an image-level anomaly score and a pixel-level segmentation map, both produced by comparing test features against normal-feature memory banks via one-class SVM classifiers.

## Method

The D³M framework extracts 2D features with DINO (ViT-B/8) and 3D geometric features with PointMAE, then introduces a Channel-Spatial Swapping (CSS) module that exchanges 10% of channel and spatial information between the RGB and photometric-stereo feature maps to create enriched pseudo-3D representations. An unsupervised contrastive loss (inspired by M3DM) aligns the swapped 2D and 3D patch features to learn a shared fused representation while preserving modality-specific details. At inference, three memory banks (M_2D, M_PS, M_D) store normal features from each modality, and one-class SVM classifiers over these banks produce the final anomaly score and segmentation map. The key insight is that photometric-stereo pseudo-3D data captures surface orientation cues invisible to both RGB and raw point clouds, and the CSS mechanism explicitly fuses these cues into the 2D feature space before contrastive alignment with 3D.

## Results

On the Real-IAD D³ dataset (20 categories, 8,450 samples), the proposed D³M framework achieves an average I-AUROC of 0.890 and P-AUROC of 0.937 in the full D³ (2D+Pseudo-3D+3D) setting. This outperforms the strongest 2D+3D baseline M3DM (I-AUROC 0.841, P-AUROC 0.922) by +4.9% and +1.5% respectively, and surpasses single-modality RGB methods (Cflow: 0.645, SimpleNet: 0.659) by over 23% in I-AUROC. In the modality ablation (Table 3), adding pseudo-3D to 2D alone (PatchCore 2D+Pseudo-3D: 0.839/0.867) already yields substantial gains over 2D-only (0.747/0.875), and the full three-modality fusion further pushes P-AUROC to 0.937.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D³M consistently outperforms all four baselines across both I-AUROC and P-AUROC on the Real-IAD D³ dataset. Against the closest competitor M3DM (2D+3D fusion), D³M gains +4.9% I-AUROC (0.890 vs. 0.841) and +1.5% P-AUROC (0.937 vs. 0.922), with the largest per-category improvements on objects with subtle surface defects (e.g., audio_jack_socket P-AUROC 0.757 vs. 0.699, crimp_st_cable_mount_box 0.969 vs. 0.933). Over PointMAE+PatchCore (2D+3D), the gains are larger: +7.8% I-AUROC and +3.2% P-AUROC. Single-modality baselines are substantially weaker: Cflow (RGB) scores only 0.645/0.808, SimpleNet (RGB) 0.659/0.843, and PointMAE (3D) 0.644/0.554, confirming that neither RGB nor point cloud alone suffices for fine-grained industrial defects. The modality ablation (Table 3) further shows that pseudo-3D alone (PatchCore Pseudo-3D: 0.715/0.681) underperforms 2D alone (0.747/0.875), but the 2D+Pseudo-3D combination (0.839/0.867) already surpasses M3DM's 2D+3D (0.752/0.687), highlighting the complementary value of photometric stereo depth for surface-level anomaly localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal industrial anomaly detection line initiated by M3DM (CVPR 2023), which fused 2D RGB and 3D point cloud features via contrastive learning and memory banks, by introducing a third modality—pseudo-3D surface normals derived from photometric stereo. The dataset itself builds on MVTec 3D-AD and Real3D-AD but addresses their key limitations: insufficient point-cloud resolution for small components (0.002 mm vs. 0.11 mm and 0.011–0.015 mm respectively), limited category diversity, and lack of synchronized multi-modal capture. The D³M benchmark reuses M3DM's DINO + PointMAE backbone and one-class SVM decision layer, adding a Channel-Spatial Swapping module to interleave RGB and photometric-stereo features before contrastive fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a practical gap: existing 3D IAD benchmarks use components roughly ten times larger than those in Real-IAD D³, so their resolution is inadequate for the small electronic connectors and sensors common in real production lines. The photometric-stereo pseudo-3D modality offers a low-cost, camera-based alternative to dense depth sensing that captures surface orientation information complementary to both RGB texture and absolute 3D geometry. However, the method-level gains over M3DM are modest (≈5 points I-AUROC, ≈1.5 points P-AUROC), and the dataset covers only 20 categories, leaving open whether the pseudo-3D advantage generalizes to larger, more diverse industrial settings or to different acquisition hardware.
