## Problem

Existing caption-based text-video retrieval methods either rely on a single video-level caption that fails to capture temporal changes across the video, or use frame-level captions without query-aware selective usage. Additionally, hallucinated or incorrect information from generative caption models can degrade retrieval accuracy. The paper addresses how to comprehensively and selectively leverage frame-level captions (narration) throughout all stages of text-video retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250025/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and a video clip (encoded via CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-B/32), along with pre-computed frame-level captions generated by an LMM (LLaVA 1.5 7B by default) for each frame in the video. At inference, the output is a fused similarity score per candidate video, computed by standardizing and summing the query-video and query-narration similarity matrices to ensure balanced contribution from both views. The narration is generated offline, so the retrieval pipeline only requires the pre-computed caption sequence alongside the video frames.

## Method

NarVid generates a sequence of frame-level captions (narration) via an LMM and exploits them through four integrated modules: (1) cross-modal co-attention between each video frame and its paired caption, followed by temporal transformer blocks, to produce enhanced sequential features for both modalities; (2) query-aware adaptive filtering using nucleus sampling (threshold p) to dynamically select the most query-relevant frames and captions, suppressing irrelevant or hallucinated content; (3) multi-granularity matching that computes both query-video and query-narration similarity at coarse (weighted-pooled) and fine (max word-frame similarity) levels, fused via standardization at inference; and (4) a cross-view hard negative loss that identifies hard negatives from both inter-modality (query-video) and intra-modality (query-narration) similarity matrices and applies a hinge-based rank loss over their union. Training combines a symmetric InfoNCE loss over both similarity matrices with the cross-view hard negative loss weighted by hyperparameter α.

## Results

On MSR-VTT (ViT-B/16), NarVid achieves R@1=52.7%, R@5=77.7%, R@10=85.6% for text-to-video retrieval and R@1=51.1%, R@5=76.8%, R@10=85.2% for video-to-text retrieval. Across the other three benchmarks, NarVid reports R@1 of 53.1% (MSVD), 68.4% (VATEX), and 53.4% (DiDeMo), establishing state-of-the-art R@1 on all four datasets. The method outperforms the CLIP4Clip baseline by 3.4 points on MSR-VTT R@1 and by 12.5 points on VATEX R@1, demonstrating the substantial benefit of frame-level narration over no auxiliary captions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest prior caption-based method Cap4Video [40], NarVid wins on R@1 (52.7 vs 51.4) and R@5 (77.7 vs 75.7) for text-to-video on MSR-VTT, and also leads on R@1 for MSVD (53.1 vs 51.8), VATEX (68.4 vs 66.6), and DiDeMo (53.4 vs 52.0). Compared to the most recent SOTA T-MASS [35], NarVid ties on MSR-VTT R@1 (52.7) and R@10 (85.6) but edges ahead on R@5 (77.7 vs 77.1); however, T-MASS retains a clear advantage on video-to-text R@5 (80.2 vs 76.8) and MnR (7.4 vs 9.0), which the authors attribute to T-MASS's stochastic text embedding augmenting matched pairs. On DiDeMo, NarVid narrowly beats T-MASS on R@1 (53.4 vs 53.3) but trails on R@5 (79.1 vs 80.1) and R@10 (86.3 vs 87.7), a gap the authors link to DiDeMo's static video content limiting the benefit of temporal narration. Against the no-caption baseline CLIP4Clip [25], NarVid gains 3.4 points on MSR-VTT R@1 and 12.5 points on VATEX R@1, confirming that frame-level narration provides substantial complementary signal. Overall, NarVid achieves the best R@1 across all four benchmarks, while T-MASS remains competitive on higher-rank recall metrics (R@5, R@10) in the video-to-text direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

NarVid extends the caption-augmented text-video retrieval line initiated by Cap4Video (video-level captions) and EA-VTR (frame-level captions for event detection), but departs from both by treating frame-level captions as a temporal 'narration' and exploiting them across all retrieval stages rather than a single one. It builds on CLIP-based retrieval backbones (CLIP4Clip, X-CLIP) for feature extraction, co-attention (ViLBERT) for cross-modal interaction, and nucleus sampling (originally from NLP) for adaptive frame selection. The key conceptual departure is the comprehensive, multi-stage utilization of narration—feature enhancement, query-aware filtering, dual-modal matching, and cross-view hard-negative loss—rather than using generated captions as a single auxiliary signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that frame-level captions from LMMs carry temporal and local detail that video-level captions miss, and that a systematic strategy for filtering and integrating them (nucleus sampling, cross-view hard negatives) is essential to avoid the noise from hallucinated or query-irrelevant content. A notable practical limitation is the precomputation and storage burden of generating narration for every video, which constrains real-world deployment. Open questions include whether stochastic embedding strategies (as in T-MASS, which NarVid trails on R@5/R@10) can be integrated, and whether prompt engineering or more targeted caption generation can reduce the query-misalignment problem observed with powerful VLMs.
