## Problem

Existing 3D assembly benchmarks and methods focus on reassembling geometric fractures or factory parts, leaving a gap for everyday pairwise object assembly tasks (e.g., plugging into sockets, inserting bread into toasters) that require both geometric alignment and functional/spatial reasoning. No large-scale dataset or method specifically targets this multi-task, multi-category pairwise assembly setting with pose and symmetry annotations for real-world robot manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251254/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two point clouds of 1024×3 points each (object A as the fitting component, object B as the base/receiving component), randomly augmented with SO(3) rotation and centered at their centroid. It outputs two SE(3) poses—each comprising a translation vector in R³ and a rotation matrix in R³ˣ³—that place both objects into a predefined canonical pose (object resting stably on the XY plane with lowest point at Z=0). Evaluation accounts for per-category symmetry annotations (e.g., rotational symmetry along Z for bottles, mirror symmetry along X for bread) when computing rotation error.

## Method

The architecture is a two-step pairwise network with two branches that mimics human assembly logic: Branch B first predicts the canonical pose of the base object P_B using a two-scale Vector Neuron DGCNN encoder (dual KNN values for multi-scale geometric features) that extracts SE(3) equivariant features, followed by separate MLP heads for translation and rotation. Branch A then receives the transformed P_B and the initial P_A, extracts both SE(3) equivariant and SO(3) invariant features, and fuses them via element-wise point-wise multiplication so each point in P_A carries geometric context from both objects while preserving rotation equivariance. Training is decoupled—Branch A is trained with ground-truth P_B pose to avoid error propagation, while evaluation is sequential (predict B first, then A). The loss combines L1 translation error with geodesic distance on the rotation manifold, weighted by λ_rot and λ_trans.

## Results

On the 2BY2 dataset, the proposed two-step pairwise network achieves an average improvement of 0.046 in translation RMSE and 8.97° in rotation RMSE over the best baseline across all 18 fine-grained assembly tasks. On the comprehensive ALL task (all 18 tasks jointly), the method reaches 0.110 translation RMSE and 41.44° rotation RMSE, compared to 0.233 and 52.34° for the strongest baseline (SE(3)-Assembly). In real-world robot experiments on four tasks (Cup, Flower, Bread, Plug), the method achieves a 77.5% overall success rate versus 22.5% for SE(3)-Assembly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Jigsaw [32], Puzzlefusion++ [50], Neural Shape Mating (NSM) [5], and SE(3)-Assembly [53]. The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every one of the 18 fine-grained tasks and on the three cross-category summaries. SE(3)-Assembly [53] is the strongest baseline, achieving 0.233 translation RMSE and 52.34° rotation RMSE on the ALL task; the proposed method improves this to 0.110 and 41.44° (a reduction of 0.123 and 10.90° respectively). NSM [5] is the weakest on rotation (70.30° on ALL), while Jigsaw [32] and Puzzlefusion++ [50] cluster in the middle with translation RMSE around 0.34–0.36. Notably, on the challenging Plug task, the method achieves 9.74° rotation RMSE versus 18.58° for SE(3)-Assembly, and on Key it reaches 16.32° versus 17.28°, demonstrating particular strength in high-precision insertion scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vector Neuron DGCNN framework [9] with a two-scale architecture and applies it to a new domain: daily pairwise object assembly, as opposed to the geometric fracture reassembly that dominates prior benchmarks (Breaking Bad, Neural Shape Mating, Jigsaw, Puzzlefusion++). The two-step sequential prediction design departs from the joint-pose-prediction paradigm used by SE(3)-Assembly and NSM, instead mimicking human assembly logic (position the base object first, then the fitting object). The method also connects to the broader equivariant-networks-for-robot-manipulation line (RIEMANN, OrbitGrasp, USEEK) by leveraging SE(3) equivariance for sample efficiency in 6D pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2BY2 dataset fills a genuine gap: prior assembly benchmarks focus on reassembling fractured geometric fragments, while real-world robot manipulation requires aligning functionally distinct everyday objects (plugs into sockets, flowers into vases) with symmetry and semantic constraints. The robot experiments (77.5% vs. 22.5% success rate) provide practical validation, though the dataset's scale (517 pairs, 18 tasks) limits its breadth compared to fracture datasets with 10k+ instances. Open questions include whether the two-step strategy generalizes to multi-object (beyond pairwise) assembly, whether the equivariant encoder's advantage persists at larger dataset scales, and how the method handles occlusion and partial observability in cluttered real-world scenes.
