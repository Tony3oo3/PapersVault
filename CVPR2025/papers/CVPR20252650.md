## Problem

Prior zero-shot Lidar methods (e.g., SAL) can only segment individual 3D scans, while supervised 4D-LPS methods are confined to predefined class vocabularies and require expensive manual annotations. No existing approach can jointly segment, track, and recognize arbitrary objects in 4D Lidar sequences without labeled training data, leaving a critical gap for embodied navigation and open-vocabulary scene understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252650/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes a superimposed sequence of K Lidar point clouds (each with 3D coordinates + intensity) within a fixed temporal window, plus optional free-form text prompts specifying target semantic classes. It outputs per-point spatio-temporal segmentation masks, persistent instance IDs for tracking across the sequence, per-track d-dimensional CLIP tokens for zero-shot recognition, and objectness scores. The pseudo-labeling engine additionally requires synchronized multi-camera RGB video and calibrated camera-to-Lidar extrinsics, but the trained model operates purely on Lidar without image features.

## Method

SAL-4D consists of a pseudo-label engine and a distillation model. The engine slides a window of K frames over the sequence, uses SAM grid-prompting to detect objects in the first video frame, propagates masks via SAM 2 to obtain masklets, computes per-mask CLIP features via relative mask attention, lifts masks to 4D Lidar through calibrated projection and DBSCAN refinement, flattens overlapping masklets by spatio-temporal volume, and associates instances across overlapping windows via linear assignment on 3D-IoU. The SAL-4D model uses a Minkowski UNet backbone with Fourier positional embeddings encoding 3D spatial and temporal coordinates, followed by a Transformer decoder with learnable queries that predict spatio-temporal masks, objectness scores, and CLIP tokens. Training minimizes a combined loss (objectness cross-entropy + segmentation BCE/Dice + CLIP token cosine distance) via bipartite matching against pseudo-labels, with ego-motion compensation applied in 90% of training samples to encourage robustness to non-aligned point clouds.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI, SAL-4D achieves 42.2 LSTQ in zero-shot 4D-LPS, outperforming the best zero-shot baseline (SAL+SW, 32.7 LSTQ) by 9.5 points and reaching 59.9% of the top supervised method (Mask4D, 71.4 LSTQ). On Panoptic nuScenes, SAL-4D reaches 45.0 LSTQ, surpassing the best zero-shot baseline (SAL+MinVIS, 33.2 LSTQ) by 11.8 points and achieving 72.6% of the best supervised method (EfficientLPS+KF, 62.0 LSTQ). For single-scan 3D-LPS, SAL-4D improves over the SAL baseline by +5.1 PQ within-frustum (38.2 vs. 33.1) and +5.5 PQ on full point clouds (30.8 vs. 25.3), with particularly strong gains on thing classes (25.5 vs. 18.3 PQ_th).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAL-4D decisively outperforms all zero-shot 4D-LPS baselines on both datasets: on SemanticKITTI it beats SAL+SW (32.7 LSTQ) by +9.5, SAL+MOT (30.9) by +11.3, and SAL+MinVIS (24.7) by +17.5; on Panoptic nuScenes it beats SAL+MinVIS (33.2) by +11.8, SAL+MOT (32.8) by +12.2, and SAL+SW (30.3) by +14.7. The gains are driven by both association (S_assoc: 51.1 vs. 38.5 on SemanticKITTI) and zero-shot recognition (S_cls: 34.9 vs. 27.7), confirming that temporal coherence from 4D pseudo-labeling improves both tracking and semantics. Against supervised 4D-LPS methods, SAL-4D still trails significantly (42.2 vs. 71.4 LSTQ on SemanticKITTI; 45.0 vs. 62.0 on nuScenes), with the gap concentrated in S_cls (34.9 vs. 67.5–68.0), indicating that zero-shot CLIP-based recognition remains the primary bottleneck. For single-scan 3D-LPS (Table 4), SAL-4D outperforms the prior zero-shot state-of-the-art SAL [62] by +5.1 PQ within-frustum and +5.5 PQ full-point-cloud, while supervised methods (GP-S3Net 63.3 PQ, MaskPLS 59.8 PQ) remain substantially ahead. The SW baseline outperforms MOT on SemanticKITTI (static-dominated) but underperforms on nuScenes (more dynamic), validating that the choice of association strategy matters for scene dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SAL [62], which distills vision foundation models into a single-scan zero-shot Lidar panoptic segmentation model, into the spatio-temporal (4D) domain. The pseudo-labeling pipeline repurposes SAM 2 [78] for short-horizon video object segmentation and CLIP [75] for sequence-level semantic features, lifting these video-domain annotations into 4D Lidar via calibrated multi-modal sensors. The segmentation model itself follows the MaskFormer/Mask4Former [12, 14, 105] query-based decoder design, adapted with Fourier positional embeddings for spatio-temporal reasoning. The key conceptual departure from prior 4D-LPS work [4, 56, 105] is replacing manual supervision with self-generated pseudo-labels, and from prior zero-shot Lidar work [62, 67, 99] is operating on sequences rather than individual scans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAL-4D establishes the first zero-shot 4D-LPS baseline, demonstrating that video foundation models can be distilled into a Lidar-only model that segments, tracks, and recognizes arbitrary objects without any labeled data. However, a substantial gap to supervised methods persists (42.2 vs. 71.4 LSTQ on SemanticKITTI), with semantic recognition (S_cls: 34.9 vs. 68.0) identified as the primary bottleneck. Open questions include whether the pseudo-labeling recipe scales to longer sequences, whether the thing/stuff imbalance can be addressed without manual labels, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two driving datasets evaluated. The framework also provides a reusable blueprint for transferring video foundation model capabilities to other sensor modalities.
