## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) suffer from 'cognitive misalignment': the vision encoder's visual representations do not align with the LLM's interpretive framework, causing models like GPT-4o and Qwen2-VL to fail at recognizing landmarks from images despite accurately describing them from text-only prompts. The paper identifies that VE-Unknown data (images with ambiguous, low-discriminative visual features) exacerbates this misalignment, while VE-Known data (rich, distinctive features) mitigates it, and that data quality matters more than volume.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at both high resolution, split into four sub-images, and low resolution via bilinear interpolation) along with a text question about a landmark, and produces a text response identifying the landmark with descriptive and location-specific details. Training additionally uses hierarchical category labels (e.g., 'church', 'tower') and fine-grained entity annotations (e.g., 'iron lattice structure') per image to supervise the visual token representations.

## Method

EECA (Entity-Enhanced Cognitive Alignment) employs a dual-branch visual architecture where a shared CLIP vision encoder processes both high-resolution (four sub-images) and low-resolution inputs, with an MLP adapter for the LR branch and a Perceiver Resampler for the HR branch. The key training innovation is an Entity-Aware Contrastive Loss that constructs entity-grouped visual embeddings as sparsified, similarity-weighted combinations of HR tokens, then aligns them bidirectionally with entity token embeddings in the LLM's embedding space via a symmetric InfoNCE objective. A Hierarchical Classification Loss is added on pooled LR+HR tokens to enforce inter-category discrimination at a coarser granularity. The total objective combines standard language modeling loss with these two auxiliary losses, and the entire system (LLM + both adapters) is jointly optimized on the Multi-granularity Landmark Dataset (MGLD), a ~200k-sample dataset built from GLDv2 with CLIP-similarity-based image selection, GPT-4o-generated QA pairs, and multi-granularity entity annotations.

## Results

On the MGLD landmark recognition benchmark, the LLaVA-1.5 baseline achieves 8.68% accuracy (4.12% Strongly Known + 4.56% Known). EECA with 50k HDS data raises accuracy to 15.52% (8.52% Strongly Known + 7.0% Known), a 7.84-point absolute gain. Notably, EECA with only 25k data matches the performance of a 125k reference dataset, and HDS-50k training yields a 64.1% relative accuracy increase over the non-extra-data baseline, compared to just 23.0% for VE-Unknown (LCS) data at the same size.

|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (LLaVA-1.5) | 4.12 | 4.56 | 8.68 |

| Entity Prompt (Training + Inference) | 19.52 | 9.32 | 28.84 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external LVLM baselines (e.g., GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL are only mentioned qualitatively in Figure 1 as failing landmark recognition). The quantitative comparisons are internal: (1) EECA (15.52% accuracy) substantially outperforms the LLaVA-1.5 baseline (8.68%) by 7.84 points, while Entity Prompt at inference-only (7.80%) actually degrades performance, confirming that entity information must be integrated during training. (2) Entity Prompt with both training and inference (28.84%) achieves the highest raw accuracy but relies on explicit entity prompts at test time, whereas EECA achieves strong alignment without such prompts, indicating the visual tokens themselves have internalized entity knowledge. (3) Across data selection strategies (Table 1), VE-Known data (HDS-50k: 64.1% relative increase) consistently outperforms VE-Unknown data (LCS-50k: 28.1%), with HDS-25k (49.3%) already surpassing BRS-50k (56.2% is BRS, but HDS-25k at 49.3% is close), demonstrating that data quality outweighs volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaVA-1.5 as its base LVLM framework and adapts the dual-branch high-resolution architecture from MiniGemini, while its Entity-Aware Contrastive Loss is directly derived from the token-level alignment formulation in SEA [44]. Conceptually, it departs from prior LVLM work by reframing the vision-language integration problem as a 'cognitive misalignment' between the VE's representational space and the LLM's interpretive framework, introducing a VE-Known/VE-Unknown taxonomy grounded in CLIP similarity metrics. The multi-granularity supervision (entity-aware contrastive + hierarchical classification) is a novel combination, but each individual component—contrastive alignment, hierarchical labels, dual-branch resampling—has precedent in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VE-Unknown data actively degrades LVLM performance (rather than merely failing to help) and that data quality outweighs volume beyond a threshold offers a practical lens for curating training data in multimodal systems. However, the evaluation is confined to landmark recognition on a single base model (LLaVA-1.5), and the method's reliance on entity-level annotations limits immediate applicability to open-ended VQA. Open questions include whether the cognitive alignment framework generalizes to non-entity tasks (spatial reasoning, counting), whether pseudo-labeling strategies can reduce the annotation burden, and whether the VE-Known/VE-Unknown distinction holds across different vision encoders beyond CLIP.
