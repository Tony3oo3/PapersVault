## Problem

Long-tailed object detection struggles with rare classes that have as few as a single training exemplar. Existing approaches either depend on large external labeled databases (e.g., ImageNet-21K) to augment tail instances, or require complex multi-stage pipelines with knowledge distillation and meta-learning. The paper asks whether unlabeled images—easy to collect without human annotation—can replace this dependency while still achieving state-of-the-art performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252855/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long-tailed labeled dataset (split into head classes appearing in >M images and tail classes in ≤M images) plus an optional pool of unlabeled images with unknown class distribution. It produces standard object detection outputs—bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores—evaluated via mAP_box and per-frequency AP (rare, common, frequent) on LVIS v1. The framework is architecture-agnostic, supporting both two-stage CNN detectors (Faster R-CNN with FPN) and end-to-end transformer detectors (Deformable DETR, DINO) with ResNet or Swin backbones.

## Method

SimLTD decomposes long-tailed detection into three sequential stages: (1) pre-train a detector on head classes, optionally in a semi-supervised manner via pseudo-labeling (SoftER Teacher, MixTeacher, or MixPL); (2) copy the head model's parameters and fine-tune only the classifier/regressor on tail classes while freezing the backbone; (3) merge the head and tail detector heads into a unified model and fine-tune on a small balanced sample (k shots per class) of both head and tail instances to prevent catastrophic forgetting. A key mechanism is a copy-paste augmentation unique to the tail transfer stage: rare instances from the labeled set are pasted onto unlabeled images so the teacher model can generate reliable pseudo-labels for tail classes that would otherwise be nearly absent in unlabeled scenes. The approach avoids knowledge distillation, meta-learning, and external image-level labels, relying instead on Repeat Factor Sampling and Simple Copy-Paste to mitigate residual class imbalance within each stage.

## Results

On LVIS v1 validation, SimLTD establishes new state-of-the-art results in both supervised and semi-supervised settings. The best supervised configuration (DINO + Swin-B) achieves 47.2 mAP_box and 42.7 AP_r, while the best semi-supervised configuration (MixPL + DINO + Swin-B with COCO-unlabeled2017) reaches 49.0 mAP_box and 43.4 AP_r. The supervised Faster R-CNN R50-FPN baseline (29.0 mAP_box, 20.9 AP_r) already outperforms all prior methods using similar detectors by up to +3.9 mAP_box and +5.6 AP_r. On Objects365, SimLTD outperforms the existing NorCal baseline across all class-frequency bins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the fully supervised setting (no external data), SimLTD with Faster R-CNN R101-FPN outperforms all prior methods using similar two-stage detectors by up to +3.9 mAP_box (vs. Seesaw Loss) and +5.6 AP_r (vs. Seesaw Loss). Against the multi-stage method of Dong et al. [12] using the same Deformable DETR R50 architecture, SimLTD achieves +6.3 mAP_box and +10.2 AP_r, attributed to the decoupled head-to-tail transfer strategy. In the external-data setting, SimLTD with MixPL and DINO Swin-B (49.0 mAP_box) surpasses RichSem (48.2 mAP_box) by +0.8 mAP_box while using only unlabeled COCO images instead of ~1.5M ImageNet-21K labeled images plus CLIP; however, RichSem retains a +3.1 AP_r advantage on rare classes with the Swin-B backbone. In the fully supervised comparison at the same backbone level, SimLTD beats RichSem by +6.0 AP_r (42.7 vs. 36.7), indicating RichSem's gains are heavily dependent on CLIP and ImageNet supervision. Against CascadeMatch [44] under the AP^Fixed protocol, SimLTD with MixTeacher and Faster R-CNN R101-FPN achieves 34.4 AP^Fixed_box vs. 32.9, and 26.1 AP^Fixed_r vs. 26.5, winning on overall mAP while being competitive on rare classes despite using a simpler base detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimLTD builds on the head-to-tail model transfer paradigm introduced by Van Horn & Perona and Wang et al., but departs from prior multi-stage methods (LST, Dong et al.) by eliminating knowledge distillation and meta-learning in favor of a simpler three-stage recipe: pre-train on head classes, copy representations to tail classes, and fine-tune on a balanced sample. It also extends the semi-supervised detection literature (SoftER Teacher, MixTeacher, MixPL) by integrating unlabeled images into both the representation and transfer learning stages, and introduces a novel copy-paste augmentation of rare instances onto unlabeled scenes to combat pseudo-label imbalance. Unlike methods such as Detic, RichSem, and MosaicOS that require ImageNet-21K image-level labels, SimLTD relies only on unlabeled images, removing a hard dependency on a large auxiliary labeled database.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for a large labeled auxiliary dataset, SimLTD makes long-tailed detection applicable to bespoke domains (aerial imagery, industrial inspection) where no comparable object-centric labeled database exists. The framework's simplicity and compatibility with both CNN and transformer backbones make it a strong, reproducible baseline for the community. Open questions include whether the approach extends to genuinely novel categories (beyond rare-but-seen classes), how it scales to even more extreme tail distributions, and whether the copy-paste augmentation generalizes to domains where pasting objects onto backgrounds is less physically plausible.
