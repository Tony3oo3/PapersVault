## Problem

Existing active open-set annotation methods either query examples with low epistemic uncertainty (ensuring known-class membership but yielding limited information) or those with high aleatoric uncertainty (capturing ambiguity but producing meaningless scores for open-set examples). No prior method effectively combines both uncertainty types, leading to suboptimal test accuracy despite high query precision in some baselines.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small initial labeled set of known-class RGB images (1–8% of the dataset) and a large unlabeled pool containing a mixture of known and unknown class images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet). Each AL round outputs a query set of b=1,500 images for Oracle labeling, produced via a two-stage selection: first kb candidates with the lowest epistemic uncertainty scores, then the top b by aleatoric uncertainty within that candidate set. The framework simultaneously maintains a (C+1)-class detector and a C-class target classifier, both based on ResNet-18.

## Method

EAOA defines epistemic uncertainty as the free energy score on known classes minus that on the unknown class (Remark 1), computed from both a learning-based perspective (detector logits) and a data-driven perspective (KNN arrow counts from labeled to unlabeled examples), with both scores converted to probabilities via two-component GMMs and combined element-wise. Aleatoric uncertainty is defined as the free energy on all classes minus that on secondary classes (Remark 2), capturing proximity to decision boundaries in the target classifier. A margin-based energy loss is introduced for the detector to explicitly push known-class free energy above a margin and unknown-class free energy below one. The target-driven adaptive sampling strategy adjusts the candidate set multiplier k each round based on the gap between realized and target query precision, ensuring the closed-set condition holds while maximizing the pool from which high-AU examples are drawn.

## Results

EAOA achieves the best test accuracy across all three datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) at every mismatch ratio tested (20%/30%/40% for CIFAR-10/100; 10%/15%/20% for Tiny-ImageNet) and in most of the 10 AL rounds, with its accuracy curve entirely surpassing all baselines. It also attains the highest mean query precision in most scenarios, particularly on the more challenging Tiny-ImageNet. Among all AOSA methods (those using a (C+1)-class detector), EAOA has the shortest runtime while maintaining the highest test accuracy and a very high recognition rate. The paper does not report a single consolidated numerical table; headline results are presented as line charts (Figure 3) and scatter plots (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAOA outperforms all eight baselines on both test accuracy and query precision across every dataset and mismatch ratio. Against the two current SOTA AOSA methods, EOAL [31] (which queries low-EU examples only) and BUAL [50] (which queries high-AU examples only), EAOA shows significant test accuracy gains, validating that combining low EU with high AU is superior to either alone. The other AOSA methods, CCAL [6] and MQNet [28], also underperform EAOA because they fail to effectively balance sample purity and informativeness and do not fully leverage labeled unknown-class examples. Traditional closed-set AL baselines—Uncertainty (entropy) [10], Certainty [17], Coreset [33], and BADGE [1]—are substantially outperformed, as open-set examples receive low-confidence predictions and diverse features that mislead their selection criteria. Notably, Certainty achieves recognition performance similar to Random, confirming that pure aleatoric-uncertainty (entropy) metrics are meaningless in open-set settings. EAOA also has the shortest runtime among all methods that achieve the top test accuracy, owing to its efficient two-stage coarse-to-fine sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the active open-set annotation (AOSA) framework introduced by LfOSA [26], which established the (C+1)-class detector for rejecting open-set examples, and the energy-based free energy formulation from OOD detection [18, 39]. It departs from prior AOSA methods (LfOSA, EOAL, BUAL) that each rely on a single uncertainty type—either low epistemic uncertainty to ensure closed-set purity or high aleatoric uncertainty to find informative examples—by arguing that both are necessary and must be applied sequentially: first filter by low EU to guarantee closed-set conditions, then select by high AU within that filtered set. The energy-based definitions of both EU (free energy on known minus unknown classes) and AU (free energy on all minus secondary classes), along with the margin-based energy loss and target-driven adaptive sampling, represent the methodological novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean conceptual resolution to a practical dilemma in open-world active learning: aleatoric uncertainty metrics are only meaningful for closed-set examples, so blindly querying high-AU examples in an open-set pool is counterproductive. The sequential EU-then-AU strategy with adaptive candidate set sizing offers a principled way to combine the two uncertainty types, and the consistent improvements over SOTA methods (EOAL, BUAL) across three datasets validate this. Limitations include evaluation only on image classification with ResNet-18, a fixed 10-round AL budget, and the overhead of maintaining two networks. Open questions include whether the energy-based uncertainty decomposition generalizes to other modalities (text, audio), whether the GMM-based score normalization is necessary or could be replaced by simpler calibration, and how the method behaves under extreme mismatch ratios or very limited initial labeled data.
