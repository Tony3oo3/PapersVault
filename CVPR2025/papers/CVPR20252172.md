## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods either operate offline with global alignment (DUSt3R, MASt3R, MonST3R), are restricted to static scenes, or require posed inputs, leaving no unified framework that can process an arbitrary-length image stream online, handle both static and dynamic content, and infer unobserved scene regions from a persistent learned prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252172/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a stream of RGB images (video frames or unordered photo collections) with no camera calibration, processed at 224×224 or up to 512 max-side resolution. A secondary input mode accepts a 6-channel raymap (ray origins + directions per pixel) as a virtual camera query to probe the state for unobserved views. Outputs at each step include metric-scale pointmaps in both the camera's own frame and a shared world frame (anchored to the first image), per-pixel confidence maps, and a 6-DoF camera-to-world transformation; raymap queries additionally yield a predicted color image for the virtual view.

## Method

CUT3R maintains a persistent state of 768 learnable tokens that is updated incrementally with each new image. Each image is encoded by a ViT-Large encoder (initialized from DUSt3R weights) into patch tokens, which then interact with the state through two interconnected ViT-Base decoders that simultaneously perform state-update (writing new scene information into the state) and state-readout (retrieving accumulated context for prediction). From the enriched tokens, DPT heads decode pointmaps in self and world coordinate frames plus confidence maps, while an MLP head on a dedicated pose token yields the 6-DoF camera pose; all outputs are in metric scale. For querying unseen views, a lightweight 2-block encoder processes the raymap, which passes through the same decoders in read-only mode (no state update) to produce a pointmap and color for the virtual camera. Training follows a four-stage curriculum across 32 datasets: 4-view static sequences at 224×224, then dynamic scenes and partial-annotation data, then higher resolution (512 max side) with varied aspect ratios, and finally frozen-encoder training on 4–64 view sequences to strengthen long-context reasoning.

## Results

On 3D reconstruction (the primary task), CUT3R achieves mean accuracy of 0.126 m and completion of 0.154 m on 7-Scenes, and 0.099 m / 0.076 m on NRGBD, outperforming all baselines while operating fully online at 17 FPS (vs. 0.68 FPS for DUSt3R-GA). In metric-scale video depth estimation on KITTI, CUT3R achieves Abs Rel of 0.122, substantially outperforming MASt3R-GA (0.467). For camera pose estimation, CUT3R is the best online method on all three benchmarks (Sintel, TUM-dynamics, ScanNet), e.g., achieving ATE of 0.046 m and RPE rot of 0.473° on TUM-dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUT3R outperforms DUSt3R-GA on 7-Scenes accuracy (0.126 vs. 0.146 m) and completion (0.154 vs. 0.181 m) while running 25× faster (17 vs. 0.68 FPS), though DUSt3R-GA retains a slight edge on normal consistency (0.736 vs. 0.727). Against MASt3R-GA, CUT3R wins on all 7-Scenes metrics (Acc 0.126 vs. 0.185, Comp 0.154 vs. 0.180, NC 0.727 vs. 0.701) and on NRGBD accuracy/completion, while MASt3R-GA leads on NRGBD NC (0.794 vs. 0.837 — actually CUT3R wins here too). CUT3R decisively beats MonST3R-GA on both datasets (e.g., 7-Scenes Acc 0.126 vs. 0.248) and Spann3R (the only other online method) on every metric (e.g., NRGBD Acc 0.099 vs. 0.416). In video depth estimation, CUT3R matches or exceeds MonST3R-GA on KITTI (Abs Rel 0.118 vs. 0.168) while being ~50× faster and fully online, and significantly outperforms Spann3R (0.118 vs. 0.198 Abs Rel on KITTI). For camera pose estimation, CUT3R is the best online method on all three benchmarks, with the closest gap to optimization-based methods (e.g., ATE 0.213 vs. 0.111 for MonST3R-GA on Sintel) but without requiring test-time optimization or optical flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

CUT3R extends DUSt3R's two-decoder pointmap architecture and MASt3R's confidence-aware metric loss by replacing the pairwise, offline formulation with a persistent recurrent state (768 tokens) that is updated and read from at each frame. This departs from the 'tabula rasa' paradigm of SfM/SLAM/NeRF by integrating learned scene priors into a continuous online process, and from concurrent Spann3R by using a compressed latent state that supports inference of unobserved geometry via raymap queries, rather than a spatial memory that merely caches observed content. The method also subsumes MonST3R's dynamic-scene handling without requiring per-video optimization or global alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying depth estimation, camera pose estimation, dense 3D reconstruction, and metric-scale inference of unseen regions under a single online model, CUT3R offers a practical drop-in replacement for multi-stage pipelines (pairwise prediction + global alignment) at roughly 50× the speed of MonST3R. Key open questions remain: drift over very long sequences due to the absence of global alignment, blurry extrapolations from the deterministic (non-generative) structure prediction, and the high training cost of recurrent unrolling. The raymap-querying mechanism opens a path toward geometry-centric scene completion that could complement generative 3D approaches.
