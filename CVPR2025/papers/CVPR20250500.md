## Problem

Existing proactive defenses against LDM-based generative models add adversarial perturbations to images but fail in audio-driven talking-head generation: they cannot prevent a portrait from being animated by an arbitrary audio signal, and diffusion-based purification techniques (e.g., DiffPure, GrIDPure) can strip away the protective noise, rendering the privacy safeguard ineffective. The paper asks how to craft perturbations that both nullify audio-visual synchronization and resist purification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait (resized to 512×512) and a driving audio clip (16 kHz sample rate, 25 FPS) as input. It outputs a perturbed portrait image (ℓ∞ budget 16/255) that, when fed into a pre-trained LDM talking-head model (Hallo) with any audio, produces a video where the mouth remains essentially closed and lip-sync confidence drops sharply. The perturbation is applied to the portrait itself, not to the audio or the video, so protection persists across different driving signals.

## Method

Silencer is a two-stage adversarial optimization pipeline. Stage I introduces a nullifying loss that replaces the synchronized talking frame with the reference portrait as the denoising target, thereby forcing the LDM to ignore the audio condition; PGD with gradient descent (not ascent) over timesteps [200, 300] yields an adversarial portrait that renders the face 'silent.' Stage II performs anti-purification by inverting the protected portrait into the LDM's latent space via DDIM and optimizing the latent code with a combined objective that balances the nullifying loss against a texture constraint tying the VAE encoding to the Stage I adversarial example; a facial mask is applied after the first s iterations to prevent identity distortion while preserving robustness against purification attacks.

## Results

On the primary privacy-protection metric (SyncNet confidence, lower = more 'silent'), Silencer Stage II achieves 3.97 on CelebA-HQ and 2.00 on TalkingHead-1KH, substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline: Mist at 5.91 and 3.06 respectively). Silencer Stage I already achieves strong protection (4.06 / 1.80) while preserving the highest image quality (I-PSNR 31.36 / 32.34). In anti-purification tests, Silencer Stage II retains the highest FID after GrIDPure purification (144.89 vs. 38–50 for baselines), confirming that latent-space-optimized perturbations resist removal far better than image-space PGD perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Silencer decisively outperforms all four baseline families on the key privacy metric (SyncNet confidence). On CelebA-HQ, Silencer Stage II reduces sync to 3.97, versus 5.62 (AdvDM+), 5.89 (PhotoGuard), 5.91 (Mist), and 6.40 (SDTS−); on TalkingHead-1KH the gap widens to 2.00 vs. 3.06–5.24 for baselines. On video FID, Silencer is competitive but not uniformly best: Mist achieves a higher FID on TalkingHead-1KH (221.58 vs. 185.87), while Silencer leads on CelebA-HQ (156.99 vs. 134.44 for Mist). In terms of image-quality preservation (I-PSNR, Table 2), Silencer Stage I achieves the highest score (31.36 on CelebA-HQ, 32.34 on TalkingHead-1KH), meaning it adds the least visible noise while still silencing the portrait; Stage II trades ~4 dB of I-PSNR for substantially stronger anti-purification robustness. Against purification attacks (Table 3), Silencer Stage II retains the highest post-purification FID (87.22 after DiffPure, 144.89 after GrIDPure) compared to 25–50 for all baselines, confirming that latent-space-optimized perturbations are fundamentally harder to strip than image-space PGD perturbations used by AdvDM, PhotoGuard, Mist, and SDS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of proactive adversarial defense against diffusion-based generative models (AdvDM, PhotoGuard, Mist, SDS) from the copyright/mimicry setting to the audio-driven talking-head generation domain. The key conceptual departure is the nullifying loss, which reformulates the talking-head training objective by treating the static reference portrait as ground truth, thereby eliminating the need for synchronized video frames that are unavailable for arbitrary portraits. For the anti-purification stage, the paper builds on ACA's latent-space optimization via DDIM inversion but introduces a VAE-feature constraint from Stage I adversarial examples to prevent the identity-destroying distortions that pure latent optimization produces, along with a facial mask to preserve recognizable features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a timely and practically important ethical gap: existing adversarial protections for diffusion models do not prevent unauthorized audio-driven animation of a person's portrait, and purification techniques can strip away naive perturbations. The nullifying loss is a clean reformulation that avoids the paradox of needing to generate fake ground truth to protect against it. However, the anti-purification guarantee is incomplete—GrIDPure still partially restores the portrait's animatability—and the method is validated only on a single talking-head model (Hallo), leaving open whether the approach generalizes across architectures. A fully robust perturbation that guarantees a 'silent' portrait even after purification remains an open challenge.
