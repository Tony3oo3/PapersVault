## Problem

Unsupervised 3D object detection methods rely on clustering-based label fitting from single-view LiDAR scans, but data sparsity and limited viewpoints produce incomplete foreground point distributions—especially for moving objects—leading to low-quality pseudo-labels. Multi-agent collaborative datasets offer complementary observations that could complete these distributions, but no prior method exploits them for unsupervised detection without manual labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250014/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes synchronized LiDAR point clouds from multiple agents (processed individually as intermediate-fusion inputs rather than early-fused) along with each agent's internally shared ego-pose and ego-shape (x, y, z, h, w, l, θ), which are available at no annotation cost in cooperative driving systems. It produces two outputs: a set of quality-discriminated 3D bounding box pseudo-labels (high-quality and low-quality) and a trained PointPillars-based detector. The detector is trained with a combined loss of smooth L1 regression, focal classification, and an InfoNCE-based contrastive term, with the contrastive module active only during training.

## Method

DOtA operates in three stages. First, an initial detector is trained solely on the shared ego-pose/ego-shape of the collaborating agents as supervision, then run at a low confidence threshold (δ=0.01) to generate high-recall preliminary labels. Second, a Multi-scale Bounding-box Encoding (MBE) module filters these labels by creating enlarged and shrunk versions of each box and computing three per-agent signals: Collision Probability Encoding (ratio of new points in the expanded region), Boundary Alignment Encoding (convex-hull occupancy in the shrunk region via Qhull), and Information Confidence Encoding (inverse-distance weighting of each agent's contribution); a weighted discriminator across all agents classifies each label as high- or low-quality. Third, a Label-Internal Contrastive Learning (LICL) module extracts local features at the spatial positions of high-quality (positive) and low-quality (negative) labels on the feature map and applies an InfoNCE loss to pull positive features together while pushing them away from negative features, correcting erroneous feature learning from prior iterations. The key distinction from prior unsupervised methods is that DOtA replaces cluster-based fitting with neural-network generalization from shared agent geometry, and uses multi-view complementary observations to validate label physical plausibility rather than relying on single-view point density.

## Results

On the real-world V2V4Real dataset, DOtA achieves AP@0.3/0.5/0.7 of 54.60/48.84/22.41, surpassing the previous best unsupervised method CPD (40.67/30.27/4.73) by 16.83% average AP. On the simulation OPV2V dataset, DOtA reaches AP@0.3/0.5/0.7 of 66.14/52.37/24.57, outperforming CPD at IoU 0.3 and 0.5 but trailing slightly at IoU 0.7 (24.57 vs. 27.72). On the multi-class V2X-Real dataset, DOtA achieves the best AP across Car, Pedestrian, and Truck categories. When integrated into three collaborative perception frameworks (AttFuse, V2X-ViT, Where2Comm), DOtA yields average improvements of 32.61% and 35.35% over shared-info-only baselines on V2V4Real and OPV2V respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOtA decisively outperforms all clustering-based unsupervised baselines (DBSCAN, MODEST, OYSTER) on both datasets, with the largest gap on V2V4Real where real-world noise severely degrades cluster-fitting methods (e.g., DBSCAN drops to AP@0.7 of 0.88 even with shared-info, vs. DOtA's 22.41). Against the strongest baseline CPD (CVPR'24), DOtA gains +13.93 AP@0.3 and +18.57 AP@0.5 on V2V4Real, but on OPV2V it trails CPD by 3.15 points at IoU 0.7 (24.57 vs. 27.72), which the authors attribute to the simulation dataset's lower noise making cluster-based fitting more effective. When plugged into collaborative perception frameworks, DOtA-based AttFuse, V2X-ViT, and Where2Comm each improve over their shared-info-only counterparts by 30+ AP points (e.g., AttFuse: 11.15→54.60 AP@0.3 on V2V4Real), though they still fall short of fully supervised versions (71.35 AP@0.3). On the multi-class V2X-Real benchmark, DOtA surpasses both CPD and LISO across all three object classes (Car, Pedestrian, Truck) at both IoU thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOtA sits at the intersection of unsupervised 3D object detection (MODEST, OYSTER, CPD) and multi-agent collaborative perception (AttFuse, V2X-ViT, Where2Comm). It departs from the clustering-based pseudo-label generation paradigm of prior unsupervised methods by instead bootstrapping a detector from the agents' own internally shared ego-pose and ego-shape, then leveraging multi-view complementary observations for label refinement. The multi-scale bounding-box encoding module and label-internal contrastive learning are novel contributions that repurpose geometric reasoning (Qhull convex hulls, collision probability) and InfoNCE contrastive losses in a collaborative detection context, building on the AttFuse fusion pipeline as the underlying backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical bottleneck: annotation cost in collaborative perception datasets scales linearly with the number of agents, severely limiting real-world deployment. By showing that multi-agent shared information alone can bootstrap high-quality pseudo-labels without any manual annotation, DOtA opens a path toward self-annotating collaborative fleets. The robustness to localization noise (a key real-world challenge) is particularly valuable, as clustering-based methods degrade sharply under such perturbations. Open questions include scalability to larger agent counts, handling of asynchronous or partially overlapping observations, and whether the approach extends to other sensor modalities (camera, radar) or more diverse object categories beyond vehicles.
