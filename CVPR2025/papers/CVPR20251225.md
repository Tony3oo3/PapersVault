## Problem

Single-image 3D human reconstruction methods largely produce static assets that cannot be animated, despite animation being the core requirement for human avatars. Two obstacles persist: a single image provides incomplete viewpoint and motion information, and diffusion-based 3D priors (e.g., SDS) introduce viewpoint inconsistencies that degrade reconstruction quality. Existing approaches either hallucinate missing views in camera space (which lacks motion information) or rely on costly multi-view capture data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a human (resized to 768×512) and produces a compact animatable 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar of approximately 110.6K Gaussians, skinned via SMPL/LBS for real-time animation. The KHD module internally generates 128 pose-driven frames at 768×512 as an intermediate representation. The final avatar renders at up to 70 FPS using only 3.8 GB of GPU memory on a single NVIDIA 4090, making it suitable for AR/VR deployment.

## Method

SinGS comprises two components. First, Kinematic Human Diffusion (KHD) reframes the underdetermined 3D reconstruction as a generative task by sampling in pose space rather than camera space: it uses SMPL poses (refined by ScoreHMR) rendered as RGB images through a 3D Pose Encoder, concatenated with a CLIP-encoded reference image, and fed into a 3D UNet to generate a temporally consistent sequence of 128 pose-driven frames. KHD is trained on the RotationHuman dataset (10K+ videos emphasizing turning motions) with a 64-frame context window. Second, Geometry-Preserving 3D Gaussian Splatting (GPGS) distills the pose sequence into a compact avatar by initializing Gaussians on a loop-subdivided SMPL mesh with triplane features, then applying Semantic Laplacian Regularization (which adjusts smoothing strength across 15 semantic body parts to balance hair flexibility against facial tightness) and a Geometry-Preserving Density Control strategy that manages Gaussian density via mesh edge subdivision and collapse to prevent holes and floating artifacts under imperfect priors.

## Results

On the UBC Fashion dataset (100 test videos), SinGS achieves 31.33 PSNR, 0.240 LPIPS, 156.3 FVD, 1.847 Contextual Distance, and 88.2% CLIP Score at 70 FPS inference speed with only 3.8 GB GPU memory. The KHD component alone (without 3D distillation) achieves the best animation quality among all methods: 32.81 PSNR, 0.221 LPIPS, 134.4 FVD, and 92.3% CLIP Score, surpassing UniAnimate (32.31 PSNR, 87.1% CLIP) and Animate Anyone (31.33 PSNR, 86.6% CLIP). SinGS's 70 FPS rendering speed is an order of magnitude faster than diffusion-based animation methods (1–2 FPS) and significantly faster than other 3D reconstruction baselines (1–50 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion-based animation methods, KHD outperforms UniAnimate on PSNR (32.81 vs. 32.31), FVD (134.4 vs. 141.3), Contextual Distance (1.768 vs. 1.811), and CLIP Score (92.3% vs. 87.1%), though UniAnimate retains a slight edge on LPIPS (0.211 vs. 0.221). KHD also clearly surpasses Animate Anyone (31.33 PSNR, 86.6% CLIP) and Champ (31.21 PSNR, 84.3% CLIP) across all quality metrics. Against single-image 3D reconstruction baselines, SinGS achieves the best CLIP Score (88.2%) and Contextual Distance (1.847) among all methods that report these metrics, outperforming TeCH (79.3%, 2.331), HumanRef (81.5%, 2.167), and SHERT (78.8%, 2.319). The most striking advantage is inference speed: SinGS renders at 70 FPS with 3.8 GB memory, compared to 1–2 FPS and >12 GB for UniAnimate/Animate Anyone/Champ, and 6 FPS for TeCH. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that Semantic Laplacian Regularization improves PSNR from 30.34 to 31.33 and GPDC improves PSNR from 30.22 to 31.01 over vanilla density control, validating both design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

SinGS builds architecturally on UniAnimate's 3D UNet design (removing ReferenceNet and injecting the reference image directly) and on vanilla 3D Gaussian Splatting for the rendering backbone. Its key conceptual departure is from prior single-image human reconstruction methods (SiTH, HumanRef, SIFU, TeCH) that complete missing information by sampling in camera space via SDS or diffusion-based multi-view synthesis; SinGS instead samples in pose/kinematic space, arguing this simultaneously provides viewpoint and motion priors without the instability of SDS. On the 3DGS side, it extends mesh-initialized human splatting (HUGS, HAHA) with semantic-part-aware Laplacian regularization and a mesh-edge-based density control strategy to handle the imperfect priors that diffusion-based pose sampling inevitably introduces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70 FPS / 3.8 GB inference profile makes animatable single-image 3D avatars practical for AR/VR and real-time applications where diffusion-based animation methods (Animate Anyone, UniAnimate) are infeasible due to >12 GB memory and sub-2 FPS speeds. The pose-space sampling paradigm and the RotationHuman dataset (10K+ turning videos) offer a more cost-effective training recipe than multi-view light-stage capture, potentially lowering the barrier for future work. Open questions include whether the two-stage KHD→GPGS pipeline can be unified end-to-end, how well the approach generalizes beyond the UBC Fashion evaluation set to more diverse appearances and clothing, and whether the SMPL-based driving signal remains a bottleneck for highly articulated or non-standard body shapes.
