## Problem

Diffusion-based 3D generation methods produce over-tessellated, unstructured meshes that lack the compact, sharp-edged, and semantically structured representations of human-designed CAD models. Existing image-to-CAD approaches either rely on retrieval-and-assembly pipelines requiring large CAD databases, or are limited to simple sketch-extrude primitives (planes and cylinders), failing to handle diverse geometric primitives like spheres, tori, and cones. The core challenge is generating watertight B-rep models with correct topological and geometric relationships from a single image, where reconstruction noise easily disrupts primitive intersections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250229/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image as input, first converting it to a normal map via a pre-trained normal predictor (Era3D) to strip away texture and shading interference. The output is a complete watertight B-rep CAD model defined by CAD vertices, edges, and faces derived from intersections of up to six primitive types (planes, cylinders, cones, spheres, tori, and boundary feature lines). Intermediate outputs include a 3D triangular mesh with per-face primitive labels and estimated primitive parameters (axis, position, radius, semi-angle, etc.).

## Method

CADDreamer is a two-module framework. Module 1 fine-tunes Wonder3D's cross-domain diffusion model with separate VAE decoders to jointly generate multi-view normal maps and semantic primitive maps, where primitive types are encoded as distinct colors to exploit pre-trained diffusion priors; NeuS then reconstructs a 3D mesh from the normal maps, and a Graph Cut process on back-projected semantic patches segments the mesh into primitive regions. Module 2 performs geometric optimization via L-BFGS on a 'primitive stitching' objective that minimizes projection distances of boundary vertices onto neighboring primitive surfaces while enforcing parallelism, perpendicularity, and collinearity constraints detected within a 0.05 tolerance. A topology-preserving extraction step then uses the half-edge structure of the segmented mesh to guide intersection computations between adjacent primitives, selecting the closest intersection curve/vertex when multiple exist, ultimately producing a watertight B-rep with correct CAD topology.

## Results

On the synthetic test set (1,000 samples from ABC/DeepCAD), CADDreamer achieves a Chamfer Distance of 1.27×10⁻² for mesh reconstruction, substantially lower than all baselines (3.97–5.49×10⁻²), and a Normal Consistency of 92.6% versus 48.9–64.4% for competitors. For primitive segmentation, CADDreamer reaches 95.7% vertex-level accuracy (SEG(V)) and 97.9% primitive-count accuracy (SEG(P)), compared to 29.8–40.2% and 33.2–49.3% respectively. On the final B-rep output, CADDreamer reduces hanging faces to 2.4% (vs. 35.2–58.5%) and achieves a B-rep Chamfer Distance of 1.36×10⁻² (vs. 9.74–15.4×10⁻²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADDreamer outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric, with particularly large margins on segmentation and B-rep quality. Against CRM (the strongest baseline), CADDreamer reduces mesh CD from 3.97 to 1.27 (68% improvement) and raises SEG(P) from 49.3% to 97.9%, while cutting B-rep hanging faces from 35.2% to 2.4%. LRM and InstantMesh show similar patterns: CADDreamer roughly doubles or triples their segmentation accuracy (e.g., SEG(V) 95.7% vs. 38.4% for LRM, 35.1% for InstantMesh) and reduces B-rep CD by 8–10×. SyncDreamer, the weakest baseline, is outperformed by the largest margins (e.g., NC 92.6% vs. 48.9%, B-rep CD 1.36 vs. 15.4). The consistent dominance across all metrics is attributed to CADDreamer's joint normal-and-primitive-map generation and its topology-preserving geometric optimization, which the baselines lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

CADDreamer builds directly on Wonder3D's cross-domain multi-view diffusion framework and NeuS's neural implicit surface reconstruction, but repurposes them for a fundamentally different output: structured B-rep CAD models rather than dense textured meshes. It departs from prior image-to-CAD work (IM2CAD, ROCA, Mask2CAD, DiffCAD) that relies on retrieval-and-assembly or sketch-extrude commands limited to planes and cylinders, by directly generating B-reps with diverse primitives (spheres, tori, cones) through a semantic-enhanced diffusion pipeline. The geometric optimization and topology-preserving extraction module extends RANSAC-based primitive fitting (as in Point2CAD) with explicit relationship constraints (parallelism, perpendicularity, collinearity) and a mesh-derived topological guide, addressing a gap that purely fitting-based methods leave open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a critical gap between the dense, unstructured meshes produced by current 3D generative models and the compact, watertight B-reps required in manufacturing, gaming, and product design. The geometric optimization and topology-preserving extraction pipeline is a reusable component that could benefit other CAD reconstruction or reverse-engineering pipelines. Key limitations remain: performance is constrained by input image resolution and viewpoint, fine geometric features can be missed, and complex occlusions still cause failures. Future work on multi-view input with enhanced resolution and more robust occlusion handling would be needed to make this approach broadly deployable in industrial settings.
