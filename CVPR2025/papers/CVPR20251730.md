## Problem

Existing ego-centric HOI video datasets like Ego4D suffer from inconsistent camera perspectives and misaligned action-language pairs, making them unsuitable for generating precise imitation learning demonstrations. Additionally, even powerful general-purpose video diffusion models fail to produce high-quality task-oriented hand-object interaction videos with consistent grasping poses, limiting their utility for generalizable robotic manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251730/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB environment image and a natural-language task instruction (e.g., 'use a spatula to scoop the tomato onto a plate') as input, and produces a 1080p ego-centric HOI video of under 8 seconds capturing one complete manipulation action, along with a refined hand key-point pose sequence (normalized 2D coordinates over L_p frames × N_h key-points). The generated video is designed to serve directly as a demonstration for the Im2Flow2Act policy model in a Mujoco simulation environment for robotic manipulation.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage pose-refinement pipeline built on a new 100,856-clip ego-centric dataset (TASTE-Rob) with static cameras and precise action-language alignment. Stage I fine-tunes DynamiCrafter's image context projector and spatial U-Net layers on TASTE-Rob to generate a coarse HOI video with accurate task understanding but inconsistent grasping poses. Stage II extends MDM with a CLIP image-conditioning branch to refine the hand pose sequence extracted from the coarse video, initializing denoising with the extracted poses (rather than Gaussian noise) and running only N_rv=10 refinement steps to preserve spatial awareness while correcting motion infeasibility. Stage III trains a frame-independent pose encoder (inspired by ToonCrafter) as a ControlNet-style adapter on the frozen Stage I model, injecting visualized hand pose images as per-frame conditioning to regenerate the final video with physically consistent grasping postures.

## Results

On TASTE-Rob-Test, TASTE-Rob achieves a KVD of 0.03, FVD of 9.43, and PIC of 0.90, substantially outperforming all four general-purpose I2V baselines. The FVD improvement is particularly dramatic: TASTE-Rob reduces FVD from 48.72 (CogVideoX, the best baseline) to 9.43, a 5.2× reduction. On the Mujoco simulation dataset, the full pipeline achieves a 96% robot manipulation success rate versus 84% for the coarse-only model, demonstrating that the pose-refinement pipeline translates directly into more precise physical manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TASTE-Rob dominates all four baselines across every video generation metric. Against CogVideoX (the strongest baseline), TASTE-Rob reduces KVD from 0.16 to 0.03 (5.3× lower) and FVD from 48.72 to 9.43 (5.2× lower), while improving PIC from 0.85 to 0.90. DynamiCrafter, the base architecture fine-tuned on TASTE-Rob, improves dramatically over its zero-shot performance (FVD 62.36 → 9.43), confirming that the curated dataset is the primary driver of quality gains. consistI2V and Open-Sora Plan perform comparably to each other but remain far behind TASTE-Rob on all metrics. The paper notes that all four general-purpose VDMs fail to produce coherent task-oriented hand-object interactions, making them unsuitable for generating HOI video demonstrations for robot imitation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DynamiCrafter I2V latent diffusion model as its video generation backbone and extends the Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) framework by adding an image-conditioning branch for environment-aware pose refinement. It departs from prior ego-centric HOI datasets (notably Ego4D) by introducing a purpose-built collection with static camera viewpoints and strict single-action-language alignment, addressing two specific limitations that degrade video generation quality for imitation learning. The three-stage pipeline (coarse VDM → MDM pose refinement → pose-conditioned regeneration) is a novel architectural combination, drawing on ToonCrafter's frame-wise pose adapter and ControlNet-style injection to bridge the gap between spatially accurate but pose-inconsistent coarse videos and physically plausible but spatially naive MDM outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released 100K-video TASTE-Rob dataset fills a concrete gap in the HOI video generation literature by providing the first large-scale, task-aligned, fixed-viewpoint ego-centric collection, making it a practical resource for both video generation research and robotic imitation learning. The pose-refinement pipeline addresses a real and previously underexplored failure mode—temporal inconsistency in grasping postures—that directly impacts downstream robot manipulation success. However, the authors acknowledge that generation quality degrades when objects undergo significant transformations (rotation, opening), leaving a clear open question about handling articulated or deformable objects. Broader impact depends on whether the hand-to-robot transfer via Im2Flow2Act generalizes beyond the tested Mujoco tasks to more complex real-world manipulation.
