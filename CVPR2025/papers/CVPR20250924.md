## Problem

Existing sharpness-aware methods (e.g., SAM) for domain generalization seek flat minima in the training loss landscape but ignore that domain shifts cause loss landscapes to differ drastically across domains. As a result, the flat minima found in training domains may not be flat in unseen test domains, degrading out-of-domain performance. No prior method explicitly enforces consistency of loss landscapes across domains while seeking flat minima.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250924/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images from multiple training domains (e.g., PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet) and outputs class labels via a softmax over N categories. A landscape refiner network (sharing the same architecture as the main classifier) generates per-sample soft label vectors that replace one-hot targets during training. The model is trained on p source domains and evaluated on q held-out target domains following the DomainBed protocol, with 20% of training samples reserved for validation-based model selection.

## Method

SFT is an iterative two-phase framework that alternates between a feedback phase and a refinement phase. In the feedback phase, two training domains are randomly selected; the model is updated on one domain using SAM with soft labels from a landscape refiner, and the sharpness difference between the two domains serves as a feedback signal quantifying landscape inconsistency. In the refinement phase, the refiner is updated with a composite loss combining a Projection Cross-Entropy (PCE) term (which constrains soft labels to a correctness-preserving region via KL-divergence projection onto a per-class label space), a sharpness penalty, and the sharpness-difference feedback term. The key mechanism is that the refiner dynamically generates soft labels that reshape loss landscape geometry toward greater cross-domain consistency and flatness, avoiding the overconfidence of one-hot labels while remaining more informative than uniform label smoothing. Theoretical analysis shows that the training-domain split scheme bounds test-domain sharpness by training-domain sharpness, ensuring transferability of landscape consistency to unseen domains.

## Results

With ResNet-50, SFT achieves 67.1% average out-of-domain accuracy across five DG benchmarks (VLCS, PACS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet), surpassing SAM by 2.6% (64.5%) and ERM by 3.8% (63.3%). With ViT-B/16 (visual prompt tuning), SFT reaches 77.8% average, improving over SAM by 1.5% (76.3%). With the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, SFT achieves 81.2% average versus 80.3% for SAM and 79.7% for ERM. Against the strongest sharpness-aware variant SAGM, SFT gains 1.0% (67.1% vs 66.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFT outperforms all 26 baselines on the five-dataset average with ResNet-50. The most direct comparison is against SAM [16], which SFT surpasses by 2.6% (67.1% vs 64.5%), confirming that landscape consistency beyond mere flatness matters. SFT also beats the strongest non-sharpness baselines: MIRO [10] by 1.2% (67.1% vs 65.9%) and mDSDI [8] by 2.0% (67.1% vs 65.1%). Among sharpness-aware variants (Table 3), SFT leads SAGM [61] by 1.0%, FAD [67] by 1.8%, GSAM [72] by 2.0%, and GAM [68] by 2.7%. With ViT-B/16, SFT gains 1.5% over SAM (77.8% vs 76.3%) and 2.5% over ERM (75.3%); with ViT-L/14, the gap over SAM narrows to 0.9% (81.2% vs 80.3%), suggesting diminishing returns at larger model scale. Notably, SFT's largest per-dataset gains appear on PACS (+2.5% over SAM) and TerraIncognita (+7.4% over SAM), indicating the consistency mechanism is most beneficial when domain shifts are severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of sharpness-aware optimization for domain generalization (SAM, SWAD, GAM, FAD, SAGM) by identifying and addressing a gap those methods overlook: the inconsistency of loss landscapes across training domains. While ANDMask [48] also targets cross-domain consistency, it does so through gradient masking on a static landscape; here, the authors instead dynamically reshape the landscape itself via a learnable refiner that generates soft labels, drawing conceptual inspiration from self-refinement in large language models. The projection cross-entropy (PCE) loss subsumes label smoothing as a special case, positioning the work at the intersection of label-based regularization and loss-geometry optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that landscape consistency across domains is a distinct and actionable objective beyond merely seeking flat minima, and that it can be achieved through a surprisingly simple mechanism—soft labels from a lightweight refiner—without architectural changes or novel loss designs. This opens a new axis of DG research (label-based landscape refinement) and suggests that the interplay between label design and loss geometry is underexplored. Limitations include reliance on basic SAM (not advanced variants like SAGM), the refiner sharing the full model architecture (doubling parameters), and theoretical guarantees being deferred to supplementary material. Whether the approach composes well with more sophisticated flatness objectives or extends to non-image modalities remains open.
