## Problem

Existing Gaussian blendshape methods for head avatars rely on predefined 3DMM blendshape bases (e.g., FLAME, FaceWarehouse), requiring 50+ bases that scale linearly in parameter count and slow down both training and rendering. No prior method achieves on-the-fly reconstruction from a streaming monocular video, as the sequential data arrival and catastrophic forgetting make real-time optimization intractable with existing training pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250670/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular RGB video (512×512, ~2 minutes / 2000–5000 frames) with per-frame FLAME parameters (100 expression coefficients + joint poses) extracted by an offline tracker, and a background mask from Robust Video Matting. It produces a base Gaussian model plus K=20 learnable Gaussian blendshape bases (each with position, rotation, scale, opacity, and color per Gaussian), which are linearly combined at runtime to render the animated head at ~400 FPS. In online mode, frames arrive sequentially and the model is updated continuously with a local-global sampling buffer.

## Method

A shallow MLP maps the full FLAME parameter vector to a reduced set of K=20 blendshape weights, which linearly combine learnable Gaussian blendshape bases (G^ψ = G₀ + Σ ψₖ ΔGₖ), replacing the fixed 3DMM base composition with a subject-adaptive one. Gaussians are parameterized in the tangent space of the FLAME mesh (positions sampled from the UV map) and transformed to world space via per-triangle TBN matrices at render time. Training is accelerated by (1) a one-time color initialization that computes each Gaussian's color via 2D convolution over its projected splatting footprint, and (2) a batch-parallel rasterization that splits rendering into preprocess and rasterize stages, assigns batch samples to separate CUDA streams, and performs only one GPU–CPU synchronization per step, achieving 100% SM utilization and 630 img/s throughput. For online reconstruction, a local-global sampling strategy maintains a 150-frame local pool (FIFO) and a 1000-frame global pool (reservoir sampling), splitting each batch 70/30 between them to balance rapid adaptation to new frames against catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On 12 test videos across the INSTA and GaussianBlendshapes datasets, RGBAvatar achieves the best PSNR (27.72–35.77 dB) and SSIM (0.939–0.972) in the majority of cases, outperforming the strongest baseline GaussianBlendshapes by 0.5–2.5 dB PSNR on most sequences. Training throughput reaches 630 images/s with 100% GPU utilization, reconstructing a full head avatar in ~81 seconds—roughly 10× faster than GaussianAvatars (14 min) and 400× faster than MonoGaussianAvatar (9 h). At runtime, the method renders at 398 FPS on an RTX 3090, the highest among all compared methods (next best: FlashAvatar at 304 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGBAvatar outperforms all five baselines on PSNR and SSIM across nearly all 12 test videos. Against the closest competitor GaussianBlendshapes (50 blendshape bases), RGBAvatar (20 bases) gains 0.5–2.5 dB PSNR per video, with the largest margin on nf_03 (+2.21 dB) and subject3 (+2.17 dB). On LPIPS, MonoGaussianAvatar achieves slightly lower (better) values because it explicitly optimizes an LPIPS loss; the authors show that adding a small LPIPS term (weight 0.05) to RGBAvatar closes this gap and yields the best LPIPS scores (e.g., 0.0330 on biden vs. 0.0370 for MonoGaussianAvatar). In terms of efficiency, RGBAvatar's 81-second training time is an order of magnitude faster than mesh-embedded methods (GaussianAvatars 14 min, SplattingAvatar 21 min) and ~400× faster than MonoGaussianAvatar (9 h), while its 398 FPS rendering exceeds FlashAvatar (304 FPS) and GaussianBlendshapes (267 FPS). The online variant (local-global sampling) retains most of the offline quality, losing only ~0.5–1.0 dB PSNR relative to the offline setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

RGBAvatar extends the Gaussian Blendshapes line of work [33] by replacing the fixed 3DMM-derived blendshape bases with a compact, subject-adaptive set learned via a lightweight MLP that maps full FLAME parameters to reduced weights. It inherits the tangent-space Gaussian parametrization and mesh-deformation rendering pipeline from FlashAvatar [46], and builds on the core 3DGS rendering framework [27]. The key conceptual departure is that the blendshape bases are no longer semantically aligned with 3DMM expressions; instead, the model discovers its own compact basis composition during training, trading the interpretability of 3DMM for a smaller, more expressive representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80-second reconstruction time (vs. 14–20 minutes for prior Gaussian methods and 9 hours for MonoGaussianAvatar) makes head avatar creation feasible in interactive and streaming settings for the first time, with the local-global sampling strategy enabling true on-the-fly reconstruction at quality close to offline. The batch-parallel rasterization technique (achieving 100% GPU utilization vs. <60% in prior 3DGS training) is broadly applicable to any 3DGS-based method with small scene complexity. Open questions include whether the reduced blendshape approach generalizes to full-body avatars, how quality degrades over very long streaming sessions, and whether the large-novel-pose artifacts (acknowledged in the paper) can be addressed without sacrificing the compact representation.
