## Problem

Existing traffic anomaly understanding methods rely exclusively on visual inputs, overlooking audio cues such as crashes, braking, and honking that frequently occur beyond the camera's field of view. Moreover, prior datasets lack both audio data and the comprehensive textual annotations needed for causal reasoning and practical response generation, leaving the question of how to effectively integrate audio-visual signals for traffic anomaly understanding unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes traffic anomaly videos with synchronized audio (sampled at 44.1 kHz) and a text question specifying which subtask to answer. Audio is segmented into 2-second windows converted to 128-bin mel-spectrograms, while video frames are selected via spectral flux peaks rather than uniform sampling. Outputs are free-form natural language responses structured across five subtasks: anomaly description, causal explanation, temporal window (start/end timestamps in seconds), prevention strategy, and post-event response recommendation.

## Method

EchoTraffic integrates three key components: an Audio-Insight Frame Selector that computes spectral flux over the audio STFT to identify T critical frames (replacing uniform sampling), separate visual (EVA-CLIP ViT-g/14 with image-level Q-Former) and audio (ViT on mel-spectrograms) encoders producing per-frame and per-segment token sequences, and an Audio-Visual Dynamic Connector that projects both token sets into the LLaMA-2 embedding space via modality-specific Q-formers and applies a learned self-attention weighting to prioritize informative tokens before feeding them to the LLM decoder. Training proceeds in two phases: first, multi-modal alignment on InternVid-10M (10M video-audio-text samples) with frozen encoders and LLM, training only the dynamic connector; second, instruction-tuning on the 29,865-video AV-TAU dataset with LoRA (r=64, α=128) applied to the LLM while keeping encoders frozen. The audio encoder is initialized from ImageBind pre-trained weights to facilitate cross-modal fusion.

## Results

On the AV-TAU test set (3,000 videos, 15,000 Q&A pairs), EchoTraffic achieves Overall GPT-Eval of 0.8103, BLEU of 0.2520, ROUGE of 0.3994, MoverScore of 0.6178, and BERTScore of 0.9041, setting new SOTA across all five metrics. The largest margin is on GPT-Eval, where EchoTraffic (0.8103) outperforms the next-best Holmes-VAD (0.6384) by 17.2 points, and on BLEU where it leads VideoLLaMA2 (0.0996) by over 150%. EchoTraffic also dominates on the Causation subtask (GPT-Eval 0.8301 vs. A-Guardian's 0.7389) and the Response subtask (GPT-Eval 0.8678 vs. Holmes-VAD's 0.6942).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoTraffic outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all four subtasks (Description, Causation, Prevention, Response). The two strongest competitors are VideoLLaMA2 (which also uses audio) and Holmes-VAD (a recent MLLM-based anomaly detector). On GPT-Eval, EchoTraffic leads Holmes-VAD by 17.2 points (0.8103 vs. 0.6384) and VideoLLaMA2 by 18.6 points (0.8103 vs. 0.6243) overall. The gap is especially large on Causation (0.8301 vs. A-Guardian's 0.7389, the next-best) and Response (0.8678 vs. Holmes-VAD's 0.6942), suggesting that audio-visual integration is most beneficial for reasoning about causes and appropriate post-incident actions. On traditional n-gram metrics like BLEU, EchoTraffic's advantage is even more pronounced (0.2520 vs. 0.0996 for VideoLLaMA2), indicating substantially more precise and detailed textual output. General video understanding models (Video-LLaVA, TimeChat, VideoChat2, InternVideo2) trail significantly, with GPT-Eval scores below 0.55, confirming that domain-specific audio-visual training is critical for traffic anomaly understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of MLLM-based video anomaly understanding (Holmes-VAD, HAWK, A-Guardian) and traffic accident detection, but departs from both by introducing audio as a first-class modality for anomaly comprehension. Architecturally, it repurposes the BLIP-2 Q-Former pattern to handle both visual and audio token streams, and borrows spectral flux from music information retrieval as a lightweight frame-selection signal. The two-phase training (modality alignment on InternVid-10M, then instruction-tuning on AV-TAU) follows the standard MLLM recipe, while the dataset itself extends SUTD-TrafficQA and MM-AU by adding synchronized audio tracks and substantially more Q&A pairs covering causal reasoning, prevention, and response.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical insight—that traffic anomalies like collisions and honking frequently occur outside the camera's field of view and are only detectable through audio—opens a genuinely new axis for traffic safety systems that were previously vision-only. The AV-TAU dataset (29,865 videos, 149,325 Q&A pairs) is the first to pair audio with comprehensive textual annotations across five subtasks, providing a benchmark the field lacked. Key limitations include evaluation solely on the authors' own dataset, the absence of the 'When' (temporal localization) subtask from the main quantitative table, and the authors' own acknowledgment that long-video understanding remains an open challenge. The method's components (spectral flux, Q-Former, LoRA) are individually well-known, so the contribution is primarily in their audio-visual integration and the dataset rather than in any single architectural novelty.
