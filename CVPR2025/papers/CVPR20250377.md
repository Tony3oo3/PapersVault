## Problem

Existing unsupervised test-time optimization methods for LiDAR scene flow face a stark trade-off: MLP-based methods (NSFP, FNSF) converge slowly and produce characteristic artifacts (windmill patterns in empty regions, homogeneous motion bias in occluded shadows, false nearest-point associations), while the higher-quality EulerFlow requires roughly a day of computation per sequence. No prior method achieves both competitive accuracy and practical runtime for large-scale autonomous driving point clouds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a window of 2m+1 consecutive ego-motion-corrected LiDAR scans (default m=2, i.e., 5 scans) and outputs a dense per-point 3D motion vector field for the central scan. Flow is parameterized on a 3D voxel grid with 0.5 m cell size; each grid corner stores a 3-velocity vector, and the flow at any query point is obtained by trilinear interpolation from the eight surrounding corners. Precomputed 3D distance transforms for each support frame are used to evaluate the loss without recomputing nearest neighbors at every optimizer step.

## Method

Floxels replaces the coordinate MLP used in NSFP/FNSF with an explicit 3D voxel grid whose corners are trainable 3D flow vectors, yielding local gradient influence (only neighboring voxels are affected by a single point's loss) and eliminating the non-local windmill artifacts of MLPs. The loss combines three terms: (1) a multi-scan distance-transform loss that propagates the target point cloud via Euler integration to ±m adjacent time steps and measures mean distance to precomputed DTs, with weights decaying as 1/t²; (2) a DBSCAN-based cluster-consistency loss that penalizes deviation of each point's flow from its cluster mean, preventing false cross-object associations; and (3) a small L2 flow regularizer suppressing spurious motion in static regions. The grid is optimized with a fixed learning rate of 0.05 for up to 500 epochs with early stopping, requiring no time-conditioned representation and no training on labeled data.

## Results

On the Argoverse 1 test set (dynamic points), Floxels achieves an EPE of 0.104, Acc5 of 0.537, and Acc10 of 0.755, substantially outperforming NSFP-8 (EPE 0.200, Acc5 0.288) and FNSF-8 (EPE 0.282, Acc5 0.281) while running in 4.38 s per frame versus 63.01 s (NSFP) and 20.66 s (FNSF). On the Argoverse 2 CVPR 2024 Scene Flow Challenge, Floxels is the second-best unsupervised method (behind only EulerFlow) and outperforms the supervised methods Khatri et al. [8] and DeFlow [32], performing nearly on par with the best supervised method Flow4D. Floxels achieves a 60–140× speedup over EulerFlow (reducing per-sequence runtime from ~24 h to ~10 min) and a ~14× speedup over NSFP on large point clouds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Floxels decisively outperforms both MLP-based test-time optimization baselines: on Argoverse 1 it halves the EPE of NSFP-8 (0.104 vs. 0.200) and nearly triples its Acc5 (0.537 vs. 0.288), while also being ~14× faster; against FNSF-8 the EPE gap is even larger (0.104 vs. 0.282) with a ~4.7× runtime advantage. Against the concurrent EulerFlow [24], Floxels is slightly worse in overall mdnEPE on Argoverse 2 but achieves a 60–140× speedup (10 min vs. ~24 h per sequence), and for the Pedestrian class Floxels performs on par with EulerFlow. Among supervised methods, Floxels outperforms Khatri et al. [8] and DeFlow [32] on Argoverse 2, and beats Flow4D [9] specifically for Pedestrians and Wheeled VRU classes. The supervised method DifFlow3D [15] is competitive on small nuScenes point clouds but cannot be evaluated on Argoverse 1 due to >16 GB GPU memory requirements, whereas Floxels scales gracefully to large point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

Floxels directly extends the test-time optimization lineage of NSFP and FNSF by replacing their MLP-based implicit flow representation with an explicit 3D voxel grid (inspired by Plenoxels), while retaining FNSF's distance-transform loss for efficiency. It departs from the single-pair formulation of NSFP/FNSF by extending optimization to multi-scan sequences with Euler integration, and introduces a DBSCAN-based cluster consistency loss to prevent cross-object misassociation. The paper explicitly challenges the NSFP claim that MLPs provide beneficial regularization, arguing instead that the grid's local gradient structure and the multi-scan/cluster losses together yield both faster convergence and higher accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60–140× runtime reduction over EulerFlow (from ~24 h to ~10 min per sequence) makes high-quality unsupervised scene flow practically deployable for applications like sensor synchronization, automatic labeling, and point cloud densification, where test-time optimization was previously infeasible. The finding that a parameter-free voxel grid outperforms a trained MLP prior on this task suggests that the 'neural prior' assumption in coordinate-network-based scene flow may be unnecessary, opening the door to simpler, more interpretable alternatives. Open questions include whether the voxel grid approach scales to very long sequences or non-rigid motion, and whether the multi-scan formulation can be further accelerated for real-time use.
