## Problem

Existing text-to-vision evaluation datasets either fail to disentangle visual quality from alignment, merge both into a single ambiguous score, or remain too small to effectively train LMM-based evaluators. No prior benchmark simultaneously provides large-scale, decoupled MOS annotations for both dimensions across both images and videos, limiting the generalization of automated evaluation models in real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252589/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an AI-generated image or a sequence of video frames (sampled at 1 fps) together with the original text prompt used for generation. It outputs two independent continuous scores in [0, 5]: a visual quality score and an alignment score, each computed as a probability-weighted average over five discrete adjective tokens (Excellent, Good, Fair, Poor, Bad). For prompts exceeding 25 words, an additional LLM decomposes the prompt into one vague summary and up to three specific sub-prompts, each scored separately before weighted combination.

## Method

Q-Eval-Score fine-tunes Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct via supervised fine-tuning on a mixed SFT dataset built from Q-Eval-100K, where MOS values are mapped to five adjective ratings and embedded in structured Context Prompts that guide the LMM through a multi-step reasoning chain (identify distortions/appeals → assess severity → balance judgment for quality; overall concept → detail check → conclusion for alignment). Training uses a combined cross-entropy and MSE loss to simultaneously learn the Q&A format and refine score accuracy. At inference, the final score is the weighted average of the output probabilities over the five rating tokens. For long-prompt alignment, a Vague-to-Specific strategy uses QwenLM to split the prompt into a concise vague version (scored normally) and specific sub-prompts (scored via VQAScore-style 'Does the image show…?' logit probability), then combines them with equal 0.5/0.5 weighting to mitigate the LMM's tendency to over-penalize minor discrepancies in detailed prompts.

## Results

On Q-Eval-100K, Q-Eval-Score achieves 0.732 SRCC / 0.731 PLCC for image visual quality (instance-level) and 0.822 SRCC / 0.802 PLCC for image alignment (instance-level), outperforming all baselines. At the model-level, it reaches 0.943 SRCC for image quality and 0.964 SRCC for image alignment, closely matching human judgments. For video, it attains 0.601 SRCC (quality) and 0.607 SRCC (alignment) at instance-level. Cross-dataset validation on GenAI-Bench confirms generalization, with Q-Eval-Score scoring 0.757 SRCC (image) and 0.717 SRCC (video) for alignment, surpassing the best baseline (BLIP2Score) by 7% and 4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-Eval-Score outperforms all eight baselines across both visual quality and alignment tasks. For visual quality (Table 2), it leads Q-Align (0.587 SRCC) by 14.5% on image instance-level and IPCE (0.550) by 33% on image instance-level; on video, it surpasses Q-Align (0.500) by 10% in instance-level SRCC. For alignment (Table 3), Q-Eval-Score (0.822 SRCC) beats the strongest trained baseline CLIPScore (0.768) by 5.4% on image instance-level and the best video baseline BLIP2Score (0.483) by 12.4% on video instance-level. Zero-shot baselines (CLIPScore*, BLIP2Score*, VQAScore*) perform substantially worse, confirming that task-specific training on Q-Eval-100K is critical. On the cross-dataset GenAI-Bench validation (Table 6), Q-Eval-Score (0.757 image / 0.717 video SRCC) exceeds BLIP2Score (0.687 / 0.679) and ImageReward (0.664 / 0.663), demonstrating strong generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LMM-based evaluation work (Q-Align, VQAScore) by unifying visual quality and alignment scoring into a single framework trained on a substantially larger MOS-annotated dataset. Prior benchmarks either conflated quality and alignment into a single score, focused on one dimension exclusively, or relied on pairwise (SBS) annotations rather than absolute MOS ratings; Q-Eval-100K explicitly disentangles the two dimensions and covers both images and videos at 10× the scale of the next-largest MOS dataset. The Vague-to-Specific strategy for long-prompt alignment draws on the VQAScore decomposition idea but applies it to a generative-evaluation setting where prior methods simply failed on prompts exceeding ~25 words.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the largest MOS-based text-to-vision evaluation dataset (100K instances, 960K annotations), Q-Eval-100K provides the scale needed to train LMM-based evaluators that generalize across models and benchmarks, as demonstrated by the cross-dataset transfer to GenAI-Bench. A consistent and quantified finding is that visual quality prediction lags well behind alignment prediction across all models, suggesting the field's current emphasis on alignment optimization leaves a substantial gap in perceptual quality assessment that remains open. Limitations include the 1 fps video sampling that degrades temporal sensitivity, and the reliance on a single LMM backbone (Qwen2-VL-7B), leaving open whether the context-prompt and Vague-to-Specific strategies transfer to other architectures or benefit from further scaling.
