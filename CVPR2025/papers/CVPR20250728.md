## Problem

Large VLMs suffer from high inference cost due to processing thousands of visual tokens, and existing training-free pruning methods (e.g., FastV) rely on single-layer attention maps that fail to accurately identify essential tokens at low retention ratios. While aggregating attention across all layers yields near-oracle pruning quality, obtaining such a map requires a full inference pass of the large model, making it impractical. The paper asks: can a small VLM's global attention map serve as a precise, low-cost proxy for guiding token pruning in a large VLM?

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an RGB image and a text prompt (question) as input. The small VLM (e.g., InternVL-2B) first processes the full image and prompt to produce both a text answer and an aggregated attention map over visual tokens. If the early-exiting decision score is below a threshold, the large VLM (e.g., InternVL-26B/40B/76B) then processes the same image but with most visual tokens pruned based on the small VLM's ranking, producing the final text answer. The method is fully training-free and requires no modification to either model's weights.

## Method

SGL comprises two training-free techniques. First, Small VLM-Guided Visual Token Pruning (SGP) runs a compact VLM on the input and aggregates attention scores from all layers and heads across both the pre-filling stage (prompt tokens attending to visual tokens) and the decoding stage (generated tokens attending to visual tokens), producing a comprehensive importance score per visual token. These scores rank tokens, and only the top R% are retained in the large VLM at an early layer (e.g., layer 2 of 48), enabling up to 91% token reduction. Second, Small VLM Early Exiting (SEE) computes a decision score that averages a length-normalized sequence confidence and a consistency score (the probability of reproducing the same answer when the small VLM is re-run with 95% of visual tokens pruned, computed in a single parallel forward pass). If this score exceeds a threshold, the large VLM is skipped entirely, making the small VLM's computation fully productive rather than pure overhead.

## Results

On InternVL-26B, SGP achieves a score ratio of 89.58% of the full model's performance while retaining only 9% of visual tokens (pruning 91%), compared to 46.99% for FastV and 54.28% for ToMe at the same retention ratio. At 35% retention, SGP maintains 98.36% score ratio versus 88.28% (FastV) and 85.20% (ToMe). The method generalizes across architectures: Qwen2-VL-72B retains 96.94% and LLaVa-OV-72B retains 95.81% of original performance at 9% token retention. With the SEE mechanism added, SGL further reduces average inference time by skipping the large VLM entirely for 'easy' questions at 20–60% early-exiting ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FastV (single-layer attention pruning), SGP achieves a 42.6-point score ratio advantage at 9% retention (89.58% vs. 46.99%), with the gap widening on OCR-heavy tasks like DocVQA (87.26 vs. 26.81) and visual grounding RC (80.36 vs. 19.65). Against ToMe (token merging in the vision encoder), SGP leads by 35.3 points at 9% retention (89.58% vs. 54.28%), again most pronounced on detail-sensitive benchmarks. At 64% retention, all three methods perform comparably (~94–100% score ratio), confirming significant visual token redundancy. The full InternVL-26B model serves as the upper bound; SGP at 9% retains 89.58% of its performance, while the small InternVL-2B alone only reaches 87.25%. Cross-architecture generalization shows SGP applied to Qwen2-VL-72B retains 96.94% and to LLaVa-OV-72B retains 95.81% at 9% retention, outperforming what the respective small models (2B and 0.5B) achieve standalone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual token pruning line initiated by FastV (Chen et al., 2024), which uses a single-layer attention map from the large VLM's LM to rank and discard unimportant visual tokens. The key conceptual departure is replacing that single-layer proxy with the globally aggregated attention map from a much smaller VLM (e.g., 2B guiding 26B), which the authors show more faithfully identifies essential tokens, especially at aggressive pruning ratios. The method also incorporates model cascading via an early-exiting mechanism (SEE) that leverages the small VLM's confidence and a novel consistency score to skip the large VLM entirely on easy queries, building on prior work in confidence estimation and LLM cascades. Unlike token-merging approaches (ToMe) that operate in the visual encoder and may miss vision-language interactions, SGL operates at the LM level using cross-modal attention signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of SGL makes it immediately applicable to a wide range of existing VLMs (demonstrated on InternVL, Qwen2-VL, and LLaVa-OV at 72B scale), offering up to 91% visual token reduction with only ~8% performance loss — a practically significant efficiency gain for deployment. The finding that a 2B model's global attention pattern closely mirrors that of a 26B model challenges the assumption that attention structure scales with model capacity, and opens the door to using even smaller (1B) or architecturally different models as guides. Limitations include validation primarily on understanding tasks (not generation), the requirement that small and large VLMs share the same vision encoder architecture, and the added latency of the small VLM pass that only pays off at larger model scales or with the early-exiting mechanism.
