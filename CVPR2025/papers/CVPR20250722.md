## Problem

Existing audio-driven video generation methods either restrict control to facial regions (producing incoherent body dynamics) or rely on 3D intermediate representations from single-view reconstruction that accumulate errors and limit generalization. The paper tackles the underexplored task of directly generating holistic, realistic human videos with both accurate lip-sync and natural co-speech gestures from audio, without depending on explicit 3D motion pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250722/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image (providing subject appearance) and a driving audio clip (3–10 seconds) as input, and produces a temporally coherent human video in which the subject's lip movements, facial expressions, and full-body/hand gestures are synchronized to the audio. Generation operates in the latent space of a pretrained 3D Causal VAE (from CogVideoX-2B), and the evaluation protocol is strictly zero-shot: test subjects, reference frames, and audio are entirely unseen during training.

## Method

AudCast employs a two-stage cascaded Diffusion Transformer. The first stage, H²-DiT, is built on the CogVideoX-2B backbone with MM-DiT-style transformer layers modified to accept audio tokens (from wav2vec 2.0), motion tokens from prior frames (with 0.5 dropout for robustness), a Head Position Encoder for weak spatial control, and two adapters (Appearance and Identity) injected into self-attention to preserve the reference subject's look. An additional audio cross-attention layer, masked to the head region, enforces precise lip-to-speech alignment. The second stage, R²-DiT, takes the H²-DiT output and performs conditional inpainting on face and hand regions using 3D mesh reconstructions (from established face/hand fitting methods) as structural priors, removing the audio conditioning since motion is already determined. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end on ~300 hours of centrally-framed speaking-human video with 8 A100 GPUs for 14 days, initialized from CogVideoX-2B pretrained weights.

## Results

On the combined test set (50 clips from 10 subjects + 20 clips from PATs), AudCast achieves SSIM 0.8275, LPIPS 0.1368, FID 45.41, FVD 527.24, and CosSim 0.7454, substantially outperforming all four combined baselines (e.g., FID 45.41 vs. 74.20 for Prob-CNxt, the strongest baseline). On the Vlogger demo set, AudCast achieves Hand-C 0.8522 and CosSim 0.7302, surpassing Vlogger's 0.8407 and 0.6484 respectively. On the Oliver subject (zero-shot), AudCast attains FID 42.63 and FVD 504.75, beating the subject-specific S2D method (FID 67.00, FVD 743.93), though S2D retains an edge in hand diversity (Hand-V 0.6065 vs. 0.2859) and identity fidelity (CosSim 0.8325 vs. 0.7527).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four combined baselines (Talk-Mimic, Talk-CNxt, Prob-Mimic, Prob-CNxt) on the mixed test set, AudCast wins decisively on all visual quality metrics (FID 45.41 vs. 74.20–136.82; FVD 527.24 vs. 1289–1649) and identity fidelity (CosSim 0.7454 vs. 0.33–0.62), as well as on Body-C and Hand-C. The only metric where baselines lead is Hand-V (hand gesture diversity): Prob-CNxt reaches 0.4642 and Prob-Mimic 0.4365, both above AudCast's 0.3401, reflecting that the two-stage gesture-generation pipelines produce more varied hand motions at the cost of visual quality and identity consistency. On the Vlogger demo set, AudCast surpasses Vlogger in Hand-C (0.8522 vs. 0.8407) and CosSim (0.7302 vs. 0.6484) but trails slightly in Body-C (0.9059 vs. 0.9231). On the Oliver subject, the zero-shot AudCast outperforms the subject-specific S2D on all visual quality metrics (FID 42.63 vs. 67.00; FVD 504.75 vs. 743.93) and Hand-C (0.8493 vs. 0.7989), but S2D retains advantages in Hand-V (0.6065 vs. 0.2859) and CosSim (0.8325 vs. 0.7527) due to its person-specific training on Oliver's highly dynamic style.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CogVideoX text-to-video DiT backbone (initializing from its pretrained weights and 3D Causal VAE) and adapts the MM-DiT self-attention architecture by replacing text tokens with audio tokens and injecting appearance/identity information through lightweight adapters. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (audio→3D skeleton→video reenactment) exemplified by Vlogger and the combined TALKSHOW/ProbTalk + ControlNext/MimicMotion baselines, arguing that intermediate 3D representations introduce accumulative error and constrain natural dynamics. The key conceptual departure is using 3D structural priors (face/hand meshes) solely for localized refinement in a second DiT stage rather than as global motion guidance, inverting the typical role of 3D representations in human animation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first frameworks to produce holistic, zero-shot audio-driven human videos with fine facial and hand details without requiring subject-specific training or explicit pose input, addressing a practical gap between lip-sync-only methods and full-body animation. The cascaded DiT design (holistic generation + regional 3D-guided inpainting) offers a reusable recipe for enhancing fine-grained details in any video diffusion pipeline. Limitations include the added pipeline complexity of 3D face/hand reconstruction, the relatively small training set (~300 hours), and the fact that identity fidelity (CosSim 0.745) still lags behind subject-specific methods like S2D (0.833), suggesting a trade-off between generalization and per-subject accuracy. Open questions include whether the regional refinement can be extended to other body parts, whether the approach scales to longer sequences without quality degradation, and whether the 3D priors can be replaced by learned structural features to simplify the pipeline.
