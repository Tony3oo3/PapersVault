## Problem

Multimodal motion prediction is hampered by the absence of multimodal ground truth, forcing most methods to rely on winner-take-all (WTA) training that causes mode collapse and indistinguishable confidence scores. Dense mode prediction approaches mitigate this by generating excessive trajectory candidates but require heuristic post-processing (e.g., NMS) that lacks universal principles and sacrifices trajectory accuracy. The paper identifies that all existing predictors decode modes in parallel (one shot), neglecting inter-mode correlations and making sparse, diverse output inherently difficult.

## Input / Output

The model consumes M map polygon instances (contour line segments with semantic traffic-rule tags) and T-step historical trajectories of A agents represented as 3D bounding boxes with type labels. It outputs K=6 trajectory modes per agent of interest, each comprising T̂ waypoints and a confidence score whose values need not sum to one (only relative ranking matters). A distinctive property is that the number of decoded modes is decoupled from training: the same model can produce 3, 6, or 24 modes at inference simply by changing the number of sequential decoding steps, enabling mode extrapolation under high uncertainty.

## Method

ModeSeq replaces parallel one-shot decoding with sequential mode modeling: at each of K decoding steps, a Memory Transformer performs cross-attention from the current mode query over all previously decoded mode embeddings, and a Context Transformer enriches the result with scene context via decomposed mode-time, mode-map, and mode-agent cross-attention. Training uses the Early-Match-Take-All (EMTA) strategy, which designates the earliest (not best) trajectory matching ground truth as the sole positive sample, driving later matches away to cover distinct futures while falling back to WTA when no match exists. Six ModeSeq layers are stacked for iterative refinement, with mode rearrangement (sorting embeddings by predicted confidence) between layers to enforce monotonically decreasing confidence and prioritize refinement of high-probability trajectories. The encoder is QCNet, and the full pipeline uses a hidden size of 128 with 6 layers × 6 decoding steps.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (validation, Lidar-free), ModeSeq achieves Soft mAP6 of 0.4562, mAP6 of 0.4507, and MR6 of 0.1206, outperforming QCNet (0.4508/0.4452/0.1254) on all three scoring and coverage metrics while incurring only a small degradation in minADE6 (0.5237 vs. 0.5122) and minFDE6 (1.0681 vs. 1.0225). On Argoverse 2, ModeSeq surpasses all baselines on every metric, reaching b-minFDE6 of 1.87, MR6 of 0.14, minADE6 of 0.63, and minFDE6 of 1.26. The ensemble version of ModeSeq ranked first among Lidar-free approaches in the 2024 Waymo Open Motion Prediction Challenge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QCNet [51] on Waymo (Lidar-free), ModeSeq improves Soft mAP6 by +0.0054, mAP6 by +0.0055, and MR6 by −0.0048, at the cost of +0.0115 m in minADE6 and +0.0456 m in minFDE6, confirming better mode coverage and confidence calibration with only marginal trajectory accuracy loss. On Argoverse 2, ModeSeq dominates QCNet on all four metrics (b-minFDE6 1.87 vs. 1.91, minADE6 0.63 vs. 0.65, minFDE6 1.26 vs. 1.29, MR6 tied at 0.14). Compared to the dense-prediction MTR++ [38], ModeSeq achieves substantially better MR6 (0.1206 vs. 0.1337 on Waymo) and minADE6 (0.5237 vs. 0.6031), demonstrating that sparse sequential decoding avoids the accuracy degradation typical of dense mode prediction with NMS post-processing. Against MTR v3 [36] (which uses raw LiDAR input), ModeSeq trails slightly on mAP6 (0.4507 vs. 0.4593) but remains competitive given it operates without LiDAR. The ensemble ModeSeq ranked first among Lidar-free methods in the 2024 Waymo challenge test set, with mAP6 of 0.4665 and MR6 of 0.1204.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant parallel mode modeling paradigm (used by DETR-like decoders such as QCNet and MTR) by recasting multimodal trajectory decoding as a sequential, autoregressive process over the mode dimension. It repurposes the iterative refinement and cross-attention machinery from DETR-style object detection, but applies it to a chain of mode embeddings rather than an unordered set, and introduces the EMTA training strategy that exploits the sequential ordering for label assignment. The scene encoder is borrowed from QCNet, and the approach is positioned as an alternative to both WTA training and dense-prediction-plus-NMS pipelines (e.g., MTR, MultiPath++), while drawing conceptual inspiration from sequential modeling in the time and agent dimensions (MotionLM, FJMP).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ModeSeq offers a principled, end-to-end path to sparse multimodal prediction that avoids the hyperparameter-sensitive post-processing (NMS thresholds) required by dense-prediction methods, and the natural mode extrapolation capability (decoding 24 modes from a 6-mode-trained model) is a practically useful property for handling variable uncertainty. However, the inference latency is roughly 2× that of QCNet for 6 modes, and the gains over strong baselines are modest (a few points on mAP/MR), so the practical adoption case depends on whether downstream planners can tolerate the extra latency or benefit from the extrapolation. Open questions include whether sequential mode modeling extends cleanly to joint multi-agent prediction, whether the EMTA strategy generalizes to other encoder architectures, and whether the mode rearrangement mechanism is truly necessary or merely a training stabilizer.
