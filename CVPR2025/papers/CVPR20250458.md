## Problem

Current vision-language-action (VLA) models map directly from observations to actions without explicit intermediate reasoning steps, limiting their temporal planning capability for complex manipulation tasks. Additionally, existing VLAs are trained exclusively on action-annotated robot demonstrations, leaving the vast pool of action-less video data untapped for improving visual reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250458/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 RGB observation and a natural language instruction as input. It first autoregressively generates a subgoal image (encoded as 16×16×4 discrete tokens via residual quantization) representing the desired state n frames ahead, then produces a chunk of m=10 actions, each represented as 7 discrete tokens (one per 7-DoF dimension, binned into 256 levels). The system operates in closed-loop: after executing the full action chunk, a new observation is captured and the reasoning cycle repeats.

## Method

CoT-VLA builds on the 7B VILA-U unified multimodal model and introduces a two-phase visual chain-of-thought: (1) causal-attention next-token prediction generates a subgoal image conditioned on the current observation and instruction, and (2) full-attention prediction generates the entire action chunk simultaneously, allowing all action tokens to interact. This hybrid attention design—causal for pixel/text generation, full for action prediction—is a key architectural choice that improves over purely autoregressive action decoding. The model is pretrained jointly on robot demonstrations (Open X-Embodiment) and action-less videos (EPIC-KITCHENS, Something-Something V2), where the subgoal-generation objective is trained on both data types while the action objective uses only robot data, thereby leveraging abundant unlabeled video to strengthen visual reasoning. Action dimensions are discretized into 256 bins using the 1st–99th percentile range, and the depth transformer autoregressively predicts residual visual tokens at each spatial position.

## Results

On the LIBERO simulation benchmark, CoT-VLA-7B achieves 81.13% average success rate across four task suites (Spatial 87.5%, Object 91.6%, Goal 87.6%, Long 69.0%), outperforming OpenVLA (76.5%) by 4.6 points and Diffusion Policy (72.4%) by 8.7 points. On Bridge-V2 real-robot evaluation, CoT-VLA leads on Motion generalization (60% vs. 45% for OpenVLA) and Semantic generalization (50% vs. 40%), while trailing OpenVLA on Visual (65% vs. 75%) and Language grounding (70% vs. 75%). On Franka-Tabletop with only 10–150 demonstrations per task, CoT-VLA achieves the highest cross-task average success rate among all methods, with a 46.7% relative improvement (53.7% → 78.8%) from pretraining on OpenX plus action-less video data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion Policy (trained from scratch per task), CoT-VLA wins on every LIBERO suite, with the largest gap on Long-horizon tasks (69.0% vs. 50.5%, +18.5 pts) and Goal tasks (87.6% vs. 68.3%, +19.3 pts), while Diffusion Policy retains a narrow edge on Object tasks (92.5% vs. 91.6%). Against Octo (a generalist policy without VLM initialization), CoT-VLA dominates across all four LIBERO suites and all four Bridge-V2 categories, with the most dramatic win on Semantic generalization (50% vs. 0%). Against OpenVLA (the closest VLA baseline), CoT-VLA wins on LIBERO overall (+4.6 pts avg) and on Bridge-V2 Motion (+15 pts) and Semantic (+10 pts) generalization, but loses on Bridge-V2 Visual (−10 pts) and Language grounding (−5 pts), which the authors attribute to grasping failures from action chunking rather than visual reasoning errors. Against SUSIE (a two-stage image-editing + goal-conditioned policy), CoT-VLA substantially outperforms on all Bridge-V2 categories (e.g., 60% vs. 10% on Motion, 70% vs. 40% on Language), despite SUSIE producing higher-quality goal images via its diffusion prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoT-VLA builds on VILA-U, a unified autoregressive multimodal model that can both understand and generate image and text tokens, and adopts OpenVLA's action tokenization and pre-processing pipeline. It departs from prior VLAs (OpenVLA, Octo, RT-2) that perform direct observation-to-action mapping by inserting an explicit visual reasoning step: the model first autoregressively generates a subgoal image, then predicts an action sequence conditioned on both the current observation and that subgoal. This extends the chain-of-thought paradigm from NLP (Wei et al.) and from prior embodied CoT work that used text plans, bounding boxes, or keypoints, by using raw pixel-space subgoal images that are naturally available in demonstration videos and can be trained on abundant action-less video data (EPIC-KITCHENS, Something-Something V2) that prior VLAs could not leverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is showing that action-less video data can meaningfully improve VLA performance (46.7% relative gain from pretraining), unlocking a much larger and cheaper data source for robot learning. The 40% success-rate gap between generated and ground-truth goal images on out-of-distribution tasks clearly identifies visual reasoning quality as the current bottleneck, pointing toward future gains from scaling video/image generation models. However, the 7× inference slowdown from autoregressive image generation and the lower visual fidelity compared to diffusion-based goal generators (e.g., SUSIE) are significant practical limitations that constrain real-time deployment. Open questions include whether faster image generation or world-model approaches can close both the speed and quality gaps without sacrificing the interpretability benefits of explicit visual reasoning.
