## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D semantic segmentation methods optimize holistic metrics (mIoU, mAcc, oAcc) that obscure fine-grained failure modes in challenging boundary regions. The paper identifies four distinct error types—Region Classification Error, Displacement, Merging Error, and False Response—that are systematically underexplored, and shows that boundary-related errors (the latter three) are tightly coupled to the lack of explicit boundary feature modeling in existing architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw 3D point cloud of N points with 9-dimensional features (xyz coordinates, RGB color, and surface normal vector), which is converted into an octree of depth 11 for hierarchical processing. It produces two outputs simultaneously: per-point semantic class predictions (200 classes on ScanNet200, 20 on ScanNetv2) and per-point binary boundary predictions. Boundary pseudo-labels are not provided by the datasets and are computed on-the-fly during training via a CUDA-parallel algorithm (PBPLC) that labels a point as boundary if any neighbor within a 6 cm radius has a different semantic label.

## Method

BFANet uses OctFormer as a backbone to extract multi-level features from the octree, then introduces a Boundary-Semantic Block that decouples these features into semantic and boundary streams via two independent MLP branches (Mb1/Ms1 for direct classification constraints, Mb2/Ms2 for attention query-key-value generation). The key mechanism is a cross-attention fusion where the boundary query Qb is concatenated with the semantic query Qs, passed through a fusion MLP, and used to attend over semantic keys and values, thereby injecting boundary awareness into the semantic feature. Training uses a combined loss of cross-entropy plus Dice for semantic segmentation and binary cross-entropy plus Dice for boundary prediction, with the boundary pseudo-labels computed in real-time by PBPLC (reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(n) via CUDA parallelism, 3.9× faster than CBL). The paper also introduces four error-specific evaluation metrics (RErr, FErr, MErr, DErr) that decompose segmentation quality beyond standard mIoU.

## Results

On ScanNet200 validation, BFANet achieves 37.3 mIoU, 46.7 mAcc, and 84.3 oAcc, surpassing the previous best (PTv3) by 2.1% mIoU and 2.3% mAcc. On the ScanNet200 hidden test set (official benchmark), BFANet scores 36.0 mIoU, ranking 2nd overall and 1st among methods without large-scale auxiliary data. On ScanNetv2, BFANet reaches 78.0 mIoU, a 0.5% improvement over PTv3 (77.5). On the proposed boundary-specific metrics, BFANet reduces FErr to 31.3 (vs. 33.8 for PTv3), MErr to 35.9 (vs. 41.4), and DErr₅₀ to 18.6 (vs. 29.4), though it trails PTv3 on RErr₅₀ (18.1 vs. 15.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BFANet outperforms its backbone OctFormer [48] by 4.7% mIoU on ScanNet200 validation (37.3 vs. 32.6) and by 2.3% on ScanNetv2 (78.0 vs. 75.7), confirming the boundary-semantic block's contribution. Against the strongest point-based competitor PTv3 [57], BFANet gains 2.1% mIoU on ScanNet200 validation and 0.5% on ScanNetv2, while also substantially reducing boundary-related errors (FErr −2.5, MErr −5.5, DErr₅₀ −10.8). On the official ScanNet200 hidden test set, BFANet (36.0 mIoU) beats CeCo [71] by 2.0% and OA-CNN [39] by 2.7%, but trails PTv3+PPT [57,58] (39.2) which relies on large-scale multi-dataset pre-training. On the proposed RErr₅₀ metric, BFANet (18.1) is outperformed by PTv3 (15.6), indicating that region-level misclassification remains a weaker point. The boundary pseudo-label computation (PBPLC) is 3.9× faster than CBL [44] (46.3 ms vs. 179.2 ms per scene), enabling real-time use during training with data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

BFANet builds directly on OctFormer's octree-based transformer backbone and HRNet-style multi-layer feature interaction, extending them with a boundary-semantic block that decouples features into semantic and boundary streams and fuses their attention query queues. Conceptually, it departs from prior boundary-aware 3D segmentation methods (JSENet, BAGE, CBL, CBFLNet) that typically concatenate boundary and semantic features, instead using a cross-attention mechanism where boundary queries modulate semantic keys and values. The paper also adapts the 2D error-frequency analysis from FreqMix to 3D, introducing a new 'Region Classification Error' category and refining displacement/merging/false-response metrics for the unordered, sparse nature of point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The four error-specific metrics (FErr, MErr, RErr, DErr) offer the community a more granular diagnostic lens beyond mIoU, potentially guiding future research toward specific failure modes. The 3.9× faster real-time boundary pseudo-label computation (PBPLC) removes a practical bottleneck that previously prevented boundary-aware training from using standard augmentations like mixup and cropping. However, the method's gains on RErr are modest, and all experiments are confined to indoor ScanNet scenes; the authors themselves flag outdoor and urban point clouds as unexplored territory. Whether the boundary-semantic attention mechanism generalizes beyond octree-based backbones remains an open question.
