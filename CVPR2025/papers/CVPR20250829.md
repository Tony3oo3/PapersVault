## Problem

Existing Large Language Vision Models (LLVMs) trained on web videos struggle with Activities of Daily Living (ADL) because web videos follow strict temporal sequences in controlled settings, whereas ADL videos feature concurrent, unstructured actions, fine-grained human-object interactions, and multi-view exo-centric perspectives. This gap is compounded by the absence of specialized ADL instruction-tuning datasets and the lack of explicit 3D skeleton and HOI cues in current LLVM architectures, which are critical for view-invariant and fine-grained action understanding.

## Input / Output

During training, LLAVIDAL consumes 100-frame RGB videos at 224×224 resolution, 3D skeleton sequences, and tracked object features (from OWLv2), each projected into the Vicuna LLM's 4096-dimensional embedding space via modality-specific linear connectors (356 visual tokens, 256 skeleton tokens). At inference, only the RGB video cue is required, eliminating the need for person-centric cropping or auxiliary skeleton/object modalities. Outputs are free-form text responses covering multiple-choice action recognition, temporal action prediction, and dense video descriptions scored on five dimensions (correctness, detail, context, temporal understanding, consistency).

## Method

LLAVIDAL extends the VideoChatGPT architecture (CLIP-L/14 encoder → linear connector → Vicuna LLM) by adding two additional modality-specific connectors for 3D skeleton features (from SkeletonCLIP) and HOI features (from action-conditioned OWLv2 object tracking), all projected into the shared LLM embedding space. The key training innovation is the Multimodal Progressive (MMPro) strategy, a three-stage curriculum that first aligns each modality independently, then jointly aligns video and skeleton, and finally integrates all three modalities—ordered by increasing alignment difficulty as measured by stage-1 autoregressive loss. The ADL-X training dataset (100K video-instruction pairs) is curated from NTU RGB+D 120 using three techniques: Person Augmented Generation (skeleton-based cropping to focus on human regions), Temporal Stitching (concatenating short clips into untrimmed multi-action sequences), and Weakly Supervised descriptions (GPT-3.5-guided caption synthesis to reduce hallucination). This progressive, curriculum-based integration avoids the conflicting gradients that arise from joint multi-modal alignment, yielding state-of-the-art ADL performance with only 100K training pairs.

## Results

LLAVIDAL, trained on only 100K QA pairs from ADL-X, achieves 55.2% on ADL MCQ-AR (Charades) and 48.1% on Smarthome, outperforming all baselines including models trained on 1.5B images or 3M QA pairs. On temporal understanding (ADL MCQ-TC), LLAVIDAL scores 38.2% on TSU, surpassing the next-best baseline (ChatUniVi at 36.4%) and VideoChatGPT (20.9%) by a wide margin. For video description, LLAVIDAL achieves an average score of 70.8 on TSU and 48.6 on Charades, leading all compared methods across all five evaluation dimensions (Correctness, Detail Orientation, Contextual Understanding, Temporal Understanding, Consistency).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLAVIDAL outperforms VideoChatGPT (the closest same-scale baseline at 100K QA pairs) by +8.2% on Charades MCQ-AR, +21.4% on Smarthome MCQ-AR, and up to +82.7% on TSU MCQ-TC, demonstrating the value of multimodal integration (skeletons + HOI) over video-only training. Against ChatUniVi (3M QA pairs, 30× more data), LLAVIDAL wins on MCQ-AR Charades (55.2 vs 53.1), MCQ-TC LEM (34.3 vs 32.3), MCQ-TC TSU (38.2 vs 36.4), and both description averages (48.6 vs 41.1; 70.8 vs 61.9), while tying on Smarthome MCQ-AR (48.1). The image-captioner pipelines (CogVLM+GPT, BLIP2+GPT) trained on 1.5B images are consistently outperformed by LLAVIDAL on all MCQ tasks and description scores, confirming that domain-specific ADL instruction tuning with multimodal cues is more effective than scale alone. VideoLlama and VideoLLaVA, despite larger training sets (2.6M and 1.2M QA pairs), trail LLAVIDAL by 10–15 points on MCQ-AR and 15–20 points on description quality, highlighting the gap between general web-video training and ADL-specific understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLAVIDAL extends the VideoChatGPT architecture (VLM encoder → linear connector → LLM) by adding modality-specific connectors for 3D skeletons (via SkeletonCLIP) and human-object interactions (via OWLv2), which prior LLVMs like VideoLlama, VideoLLaVA, and ChatUniVi do not incorporate. The MMPro training strategy departs from the joint multi-modal alignment approach of X-InstructBLIP by adopting a curriculum-based progressive integration, motivated by conflicting gradients across modalities. The ADL-X dataset curation framework introduces domain-specific techniques (Person Augmented Generation, Temporal Stitching, Weakly Supervised descriptions) that address the temporal unstructuredness and human-centric focus of ADL videos, distinguishing it from general web-video instruction tuning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first dedicated LLVM for ADL understanding, along with the first multiview RGBS instruction-tuning dataset and ADL-specific benchmarks, filling a clear gap between general-purpose video LLMs and specialized ADL architectures. The finding that 100K domain-specific QA pairs outperform models trained on 2.6M–3M general-purpose pairs underscores the value of targeted instruction tuning for narrow domains. Open questions include whether the MMPro curriculum generalizes to other multimodal settings beyond ADL, whether the specific modality integration order (skeleton before HOI) is optimal, and whether the approach scales to longer, more complex real-world ADL sequences beyond the 100-frame limit.
