## Problem

Current zero-shot motion transfer methods rely on holistic motion representations (global feature differences or temporal attention maps) that entangle motions from multiple subjects and the camera, making them unable to transfer individual subject motions in multi-subject videos. Additionally, the rigid coupling between extracted motion cues and the original subject's geometry limits flexibility when the target subject has a significantly different shape or semantic category.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251138/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference video, a text prompt describing the target scene, and per-subject masks obtained via SAM2. During DDIM inversion of the reference video, intermediate latent features are extracted at each denoising timestep and masked by subject regions to compute motion cues. The output is a generated video whose subjects follow the reference video's motion trajectories while matching the text prompt, with additional controllability over subject size, position, presence, and camera trajectory through simple mask manipulation or guidance weight adjustment.

## Method

ConMo operates in two stages. First, during DDIM inversion of the reference video, it computes Local Spatial Marginal Means (LSMM) of intermediate features within each subject's mask region, using set-difference operations to exclude overlapping areas with other subjects, thereby isolating per-subject motion representations as pair-frame feature differences. Background motion is extracted analogously and empirically approximates camera trajectory. Second, during target video denoising, a guidance loss aligns the target's pair-frame motion differences with the reference's, and a soft guidance strategy blends each subject's motion with background (camera) motion via a weighted average controlled by a hyperparameter w_c. This dilution reduces shape-specific constraints in the motion signal, enabling the diffusion model to adapt motion to substantially different subject geometries and semantic categories without additional training.

## Results

On a custom evaluation set of 26 videos and 56 text-video pairs (sourced from DAVIS, TGVE, and the Web), ConMo achieves the best Motion Fidelity score of 0.8931 and the highest user-study ratings across all three subjective metrics: Motion Preservation (4.40/5), Motion Quality (4.11/5), and Text Alignment (4.30/5). In automated metrics, ConMo scores 31.96 on CLIP-based Text Alignment, slightly below VMC's 32.56 but above DMT (31.46) and MotionClone (31.00). The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the refined motion disentanglement (Eq.3) contributes the largest gain in Motion Fidelity (0.8675 → 0.8931 over DMT's global guidance).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConMo outperforms all four baselines on Motion Fidelity (0.8931 vs. DMT 0.8815, MotionClone 0.8876, VMC 0.7979, CAV 0.7661) and on every user-study metric. Against DMT [34], the closest automated competitor, ConMo gains +0.0116 in Motion Fidelity and +0.50 in CLIP Text Alignment, while also leading in all three subjective scores (e.g., Motion Quality 4.11 vs. 3.70). VMC [12] achieves the highest automated CLIP Text Alignment (32.56) but trails ConMo by a wide margin in Motion Fidelity (0.7979 vs. 0.8931) and user-rated Motion Preservation (2.45 vs. 4.40), indicating it preserves coarse trajectories but loses fine-grained motion details. MotionClone [16] scores competitively on Motion Fidelity (0.8876) yet lags in Text Alignment (31.00) and user-rated Text Alignment (3.01 vs. 4.30), reflecting its tendency to retain the original video's structure rather than following the target prompt. Control-A-Video [2] is the weakest baseline across nearly all metrics, with the lowest Motion Fidelity (0.7661) and user-rated Text Alignment (1.42), consistent with its reliance on structural control signals that constrain shape changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConMo builds directly on DMT (Yatim et al., CVPR 2024), which extracts holistic motion cues via global feature differences between frames, by replacing this with per-subject Local Spatial Marginal Mean (LSMM) computed over mask-defined regions. It departs from the broader zero-shot motion transfer line (VMC, MotionClone, CAV) that treats motion as a single undifferentiated signal, introducing explicit disentanglement of subject-specific and background/camera motions using SAM2 masks and DDIM inversion. The soft guidance mechanism—blending subject motion with background motion via a weighted average to trade off shape fidelity for semantic flexibility—is a novel recomposition strategy not present in prior motion transfer methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling per-subject motion control in multi-subject videos, ConMo addresses a practical gap that prior holistic methods simply cannot fill, and the recomposition framework unlocks editing applications (size, position, removal, camera simulation) that were previously intractable in a zero-shot setting. However, the approach depends on accurate SAM2 masks and the quality of DDIM inversion, and the quantitative gains over strong baselines like MotionClone and DMT are modest (e.g., Motion Fidelity 0.8931 vs. 0.8876). Open questions include scalability to scenes with many overlapping subjects, long video sequences, and whether the disentanglement degrades when subject trajectories heavily interleave.
