## Problem

Novel view synthesis from sparse camera views in 3D/4D Gaussian Splatting is fundamentally ill-posed: insufficient view constraints cause severe ambiguities in geometry and appearance, producing artifacts like floating structures, incorrect depth, and color inconsistencies. Existing regularization-based approaches are insufficient for this underconstrained setting, while purely generative methods (e.g., diffusion-based 3D generation) fail to maintain geometric consistency across views, making them unreliable for accurate scene reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a small set of RGB images (as few as 3 views) with known camera extrinsics, plus pseudo-novel views interpolated from the original poses. It produces an optimized 3D Gaussian Splatting (static) or SpacetimeGS (dynamic) scene representation that renders high-fidelity novel views. Depth maps estimated by a pre-trained DPT model serve as intermediate conditioning signals for both the diffusion-based hallucination pipeline and the depth regularization loss, bridging the gap between sparse observations and dense geometric constraints.

## Method

GS-GS formulates a bi-level optimization that jointly trains a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (via LoRA adapters and a depth T2I-adapter) and the 3D/4DGS scene representation in an alternating loop: the GS model renders images at training and pseudo views, which fine-tune the LoRA module; the fine-tuned diffusion model then hallucinates depth-conditioned images at pseudo views, which further optimize the GS model. A key geometric consistency mechanism warps rendered pseudo-view images back to known training views using camera extrinsics and enforces an L1 loss on the corresponding diffusion features (inspired by DIFT), ensuring the generative model produces view-consistent content. Additionally, a depth regularization term using multi-scale SSIM between the rasterized Gaussian depth and DPT-estimated monocular depth injects richer geometric detail into the splatting optimization, addressing residual blurring from imperfectly aligned hallucinated images.

## Results

On the Blender dataset (8 training views), GS-GS achieves PSNR 28.57, SSIM 0.923, and LPIPS 0.055, surpassing the second-best method FSGS by over 3 dB in PSNR. On LLFF (3 training views), it reaches PSNR 24.82, SSIM 0.737, and LPIPS 0.105, a ~4.5 dB PSNR improvement over FSGS. On Mip-NeRF360 (24 training views), it attains PSNR 25.87, SSIM 0.745, and LPIPS 0.182. For dynamic scenes on Neural 3D Video with only 3 views, GS-GS achieves PSNR 27.13 versus SpacetimeGS's 14.98, a gap of over 12 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-GS outperforms all nine baselines across every metric on all four datasets. The closest competitor is FSGS [51], which GS-GS surpasses by 3.93 dB PSNR on Blender (28.57 vs. 24.64), 4.51 dB on LLFF (24.82 vs. 20.31), and 2.17 dB on Mip-NeRF360 (25.87 vs. 23.70). Among NeRF-based methods, FreeNeRF [44] and SparseNeRF [39] are the strongest, but GS-GS still leads by 4.31 dB (Blender) and 5.19 dB (LLFF) over FreeNeRF. DNGaussian [18], the strongest depth-regularized 3DGS variant, trails GS-GS by 4.26 dB on Blender and 5.70 dB on LLFF. For dynamic scenes, GS-GS (built on SpacetimeGS [20]) achieves a 12.15 dB PSNR gain over the vanilla SpacetimeGS baseline with 3 views (27.13 vs. 14.98), and even with 3 views it outperforms SpacetimeGS with 9 views (27.13 vs. 26.72 PSNR). No baseline matches or exceeds GS-GS on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D/4D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS, SpacetimeGS) as the scene representation and on pre-trained Stable Diffusion with LoRA and a depth T2I-Adapter as the generative prior, combining them in a novel alternating optimization loop. It departs from purely generative 3D/4D methods (DreamFusion, DreamGaussian) that sacrifice geometric precision, and from prior sparse-view GS approaches (DNGaussian, FSGS) that rely solely on regularization without generative hallucination. The geometry-aware fine-tuning strategy draws on DIFT's observation that diffusion intermediate features encode semantic correspondences, repurposing this insight to enforce cross-view consistency during LoRA adaptation rather than for correspondence estimation per se.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GS-GS offers a practical, general pipeline that plugs into any vanilla 3D/4DGS base model, making it broadly applicable to sparse-view reconstruction without requiring large multi-view training datasets or feed-forward architectures that degrade out-of-distribution. The alternating co-optimization of diffusion and GS models is a reusable paradigm that could extend to other underconstrained reconstruction settings. Limitations include dependence on monocular depth estimation quality, added computational overhead from iterative diffusion sampling, and evaluation limited to relatively controlled scenes; open questions include scalability to single-view inputs, highly dynamic or unbounded outdoor scenes, and whether the geometry-aware feature loss generalizes beyond the specific DIFT-style correspondence mechanism.
