## Problem

Existing low-light image enhancement (LLIE) and joint LLIE-deblurring models are constrained by predefined degradation categories, making them poorly suited to the dynamically coupled degradations (noise, blur, color distortion) encountered in real-world low-light scenarios. Unified restoration models, while task-agnostic, lack adaptive mechanisms for complex illumination conditions and suffer from parameter inefficiency, often amplifying degradation or introducing artifacts under low-light settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250276/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts low-light RGB images of arbitrary spatial dimensions directly at inference time, without requiring cropping or resizing to a fixed resolution. It produces a single enhanced RGB image that simultaneously addresses brightness, noise, blur, and color distortions. Training uses paired low-light/normal-light image datasets with a unified loss (L1 + SSIM + Perceptual), and the same trained model is evaluated across eight distinct benchmarks spanning different degradation profiles.

## Method

URWKV extends the RWKV architecture within a three-stage encoder-decoder framework by introducing a multi-state perspective at both intra-stage and inter-stage levels. Luminance-adaptive Normalization (LAN) replaces fixed LayerNorm by extracting 1D luminance vectors from the current and all prior stage outputs, aggregating them via multi-kernel 1D convolutions, and predicting a dynamic scaling offset Δγ through an MLP with tanh activation, enabling scene-aware luminance modulation inspired by the human pupil mechanism. Multi-state Quad-directional Token Shift (SQ-Shift) aggregates the current state with all preceding intra-stage states via exponential moving average before applying the original Q-shift, preserving long-range degradation context that a single-state mechanism would lose. A State-aware Selective Fusion (SSF) module replaces naive skip connections by computing channel-wise means of all three encoder outputs, adaptively aligning them to the decoder's spatial resolution, and predicting a spatial guiding weight through an inception block that selectively fuses encoder features into the decoder, mitigating noise propagation and semantic misalignment. The full model uses only 2.25M parameters and 18.34G FLOPs, substantially fewer than comparable unified models.

## Results

URWKV achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all six main benchmarks: 23.11 dB on LOL-v2-real, 26.36 dB on LOL-v2-syn, 23.11 dB on SID, 29.44 dB on SMID, 31.24 dB on SDSD-indoor, and 29.99 dB on SDSD-outdoor, while using only 2.25M parameters and 18.34G FLOPs. On the coupled low-light + blur dataset LOL-blur, URWKV reaches 27.27 dB PSNR / 0.890 SSIM, outperforming the best dedicated LLIE-deblur model (PDHAT) by 0.56 dB. On MIT-Adobe FiveK, URWKV attains 26.08 dB PSNR, surpassing the next-best method (Retinexformer) by 1.50 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

URWKV outperforms all 18 baselines on every dataset. Among LLIE models, it beats Retinexformer (the strongest LLIE baseline) by 0.32 dB PSNR on LOL-v2-real and 0.48 dB on LOL-v2-syn, while using fewer parameters (2.25M vs. 1.61M) and comparable FLOPs (18.34G vs. 15.57G). Against dedicated LLIE-deblur models, URWKV surpasses PDHAT by 0.56 dB on LOL-blur and by 0.25 dB on SMID, while PDHAT requires 208.19G FLOPs (11× more). Among unified models, the gap is largest: URWKV exceeds Restormer by 2.75 dB on SDSD-indoor and 2.00 dB on SDSD-outdoor, and outperforms MambaIR by 2.66 dB on LOL-v2-real, all with fewer parameters (2.25M vs. 26.11M and 4.30M respectively). On FiveK, URWKV leads Retinexformer by 1.50 dB and Restormer by 0.60 dB in PSNR. Notably, URWKV is the only method that simultaneously achieves top PSNR and SSIM across all eight benchmarks, demonstrating consistent superiority in both fidelity and structural preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RWKV architecture (originally designed for efficient sequence modeling) into the low-light image restoration domain, building directly on Vision-RWKV's quad-directional token shift mechanism. It departs from prior unified restoration models (Restormer, Uformer, MambaIR) by introducing a multi-state perspective—aggregating both intra-stage states via exponential moving average and inter-stage states via luminance-adaptive normalization—rather than relying on a single recurrent state. The SSF module also represents a conceptual departure from conventional skip connections, replacing naive concatenation/addition with a learned spatial gating mechanism that selectively fuses multi-state encoder features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 2.25M-parameter model can outperform much larger unified restoration models (Restormer at 26M, MIRNet at 32M) by 2–3 dB on challenging low-light benchmarks, suggesting that architectural inductive bias (multi-state awareness, luminance-adaptive normalization) can substitute for raw model capacity in low-light restoration. The multi-state perspective is a generalizable design principle that could transfer to other restoration tasks beyond low-light. Open questions include whether the EMA-based state aggregation scales to longer sequences or higher resolutions, and whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-frame settings where temporal coupling of degradations is more complex.
