## Problem

Caricature generation faces a dual challenge: high-quality caricature training data is scarce, and existing methods lack intuitive mechanisms for users to precisely control the exaggeration process while preserving identity. Prior GAN-based approaches require paired caricature data and offer limited creative freedom, while diffusion-based methods like DemoCaricature need expensive per-identity fine-tuning and lack fine-grained spatial control over deformation. No prior system simultaneously achieves ID preservation, interactive creative control, and practical deployment without caricature-specific data.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single reference face image, a text prompt (e.g., 'a playful and highly exaggerated caricature of a man/woman'), and interactive Bezier curve edits where users drag control points to deform facial features. The 68 detected facial landmarks are fitted with region-specific Bezier curves (e.g., a 7th-order curve for the jawline, two 4th-order curves per eye), and the resulting deformed landmark lines are thinned to unitary-thickness skeletons before serving as spatial conditioning. Output is a high-resolution caricature image generated in under 10 seconds on a single GPU, with the system supporting diverse output styles (watercolor, ink, sketch) via prompt changes.

## Method

The core insight is reformulating caricature generation as a controllable TPS deformation problem guided by identity-preserved diffusion priors, eliminating the need for real caricature data. For training, the authors synthesize exaggerated face images by applying TPS warping to 68 landmarks on FFHQ images, then fine-tune only the IdentityNet (a ControlNet-style zero-convolution residual module) on SDXL while freezing the diffusion backbone, text encoder, and ArcFace face encoder. A key architectural contribution is extending InstantID's five-point landmark condition to a line-based representation: Bezier curves fitted to facial landmark polylines encode shape information rather than mere positions, enabling the model to learn distortion geometry. At inference, users drag Bezier control points to deform landmarks, the deformed lines drive TPS transformations, and the resulting spatial condition is combined with ArcFace ID embeddings (injected via IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention) to condition the fine-tuned diffusion model, producing identity-consistent caricatures with user-controlled exaggeration.

## Results

On the 20-identity test set with exaggerated landmark conditions, CaricatureBooth achieves the highest scores across all five metrics: CLIP-T 31.19, ImageReward 0.57, PickScore 21.74, human-rated ID consistency 0.52, and overall user preference 0.87. The ID and Overall scores come from a human study, as automated face-recognition features cannot reliably extract identity from heavily distorted caricatures. Inference runs in under 10 seconds per image, over 5× faster than DemoCaricature's per-identity tuning phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaricatureBooth outperforms all four GAN-based baselines (StyleCariGAN, CariGANs, WarpGAN) and the diffusion-based DemoCaricature on every reported metric. The largest margin is in human-rated ID consistency (0.52 vs. 0.32 for StyleCariGAN, 0.11 for DemoCaricature) and overall user preference (0.87 vs. 0.07 for DemoCaricature), reflecting that prior methods either lose identity under strong distortion or produce artifacts. Against the two diffusion-based personalisation baselines (vanilla InstantID and InstantID + MultiControl), the comparison is qualitative: under exaggerated line-based landmark conditions, both InstantID variants fail to follow the requested shape deformation and instead generate near-standard faces, whereas CaricatureBooth faithfully renders the distorted geometry. In terms of speed, CaricatureBooth generates a caricature in under 10 seconds, while DemoCaricature requires over a minute of per-identity fine-tuning before any generation can occur.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on InstantID's identity-preserving generation pipeline (IP-Adapter + IdentityNet) and extends it with TPS-based spatial deformation and Bezier-curve-based interactive control. It departs from prior caricature work (CariGANs, StyleCariGAN, WarpGAN, DemoCaricature) by eliminating the need for paired caricature-photo training data entirely, instead synthesising training pairs via TPS warping of standard FFHQ faces. The shift from point-based to line-based landmark conditioning (Bezier curves) is a deliberate departure from InstantID's five-keypoint representation, enabling shape-level rather than position-level control over facial features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment angle (sub-10-second generation, no per-identity tuning, interactive dragging) makes caricature generation viable for real-world photo booth use, a setting prior methods could not support. However, the distortion flexibility is bounded by the TPS preprocessing, which may not capture the full range of artistic morphing effects a human caricaturist employs, and the ArcFace-based identity extraction limits attribute-level flexibility. Open questions include whether more expressive deformation priors (beyond TPS) or decoupled facial attribute controls could further expand the creative envelope without sacrificing identity.
