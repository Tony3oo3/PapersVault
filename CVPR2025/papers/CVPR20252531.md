## Problem

The simplicity bias induced by ReLU activations is widely assumed to be universally beneficial for neural network generalization, yet no systematic study has identified where it is suboptimal or what alternative inductive biases would be preferable. This paper asks whether there exist practical ML tasks where the simplicity bias is actively detrimental, and what the optimal inductive biases look like in those cases.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset (images, tabular features, or algorithmic task definitions) and an untrained MLP, and produces a reusable activation function parametrized as a piecewise-linear spline with ~50 control points over [-5, +5]. Unlike prior trainable-activation work that continuously updates the activation during a single training run, the output here is a fixed function intended to be dropped into fresh training runs. For tabular data, the method additionally produces dimension-specific Input Activation Functions (IAFs) applied directly to each input feature before the MLP, subsuming gated inputs and Fourier embeddings.

## Method

The core contribution is a bi-level meta-learning loop (Algorithm 1) that learns dataset-specific activation functions optimized for generalization rather than training fit. The inner loop trains a randomly-initialized MLP with a fixed spline activation on a training subset; the outer loop updates the spline parameters to maximize validation performance, with each outer step constituting a new episode (fresh weights, new data split) to prevent overfitting to a particular initialization. The spline parametrization is deliberately free of smoothness or monotonicity constraints, enabling discovery of sharp transitions, periodic functions, and wavelets that a ReLU-MLP surrogate could never represent. The paper further introduces Input Activation Functions for tabular data—per-dimension splines applied before the network—that induce axis-aligned decision boundaries mimicking tree-based models. Complexity of learned functions is quantified via total variation over random 1D input-space paths, providing a practical alternative to Fourier-based measures.

## Results

On image classification (MNIST, FASHION-MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10), learned activations provide only marginal improvements over ReLUs, with the best learned functions converging to GeLU-like shapes, confirming ReLUs are near-optimal for these tasks. On tabular data (16 datasets), vanilla ReLU MLPs underperform boosted decision trees, but learned input activation functions (IAFs) close or even surpass the tree baseline. On algorithmic grokking tasks (22 modular arithmetic problems), a single shared learned activation achieves 61.5% average accuracy versus 19.9% for ReLUs and 54.0% for task-specific activations, while on modular addition (mod 27) the learned-activation model converges approximately 10× faster than ReLUs, effectively eliminating the grokking delay.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline throughout is a standard ReLU MLP, which the learned-activation approach consistently matches or exceeds. On image classification, the gap is negligible, with learned activations only slightly improving SVHN and CIFAR-10 accuracy, and the learned functions closely resembling GeLUs—suggesting the community has already converged on a local optimum. On tabular data, the comparison with boosted decision trees from Grinsztajn et al. [35] is the most direct: vanilla ReLU MLPs are clearly inferior to trees, but MLPs with learned activation functions eliminate this gap, and IAF-equipped MLPs sometimes surpass the trees, replicating their axis-aligned decision boundaries. On regression tasks (predicting class IDs with MSE loss), learned activations from a zero initialization yield clear accuracy gains over ReLUs, with the improvement correlating with higher function complexity (measured via total variation). On shortcut learning (MNIST/CIFAR collages), the ReLU baseline relies exclusively on the simpler MNIST tile (CIFAR test accuracy at chance level ~10%), while learned activations validated on either tile successfully steer the model's feature preference. On grokking tasks, ReLU networks either never generalize or require thousands of extra steps, whereas learned activations converge ~10× faster, supporting the hypothesis that grokking stems from an inductive-bias mismatch rather than an inherent SGD property.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Teney et al. (CVPR 2024), which established that ReLU activations are the primary source of the simplicity bias in neural networks, and extends it by proposing a method to learn alternative activations that modulate this bias. It departs from prior activation-learning work (Alexandridis et al., Apicella et al., Scardapane et al.) by replacing constrained parametrizations (smoothness, monotonicity, or embedded ReLU MLPs) with an unbiased spline representation and a bi-level episodic meta-learning loop that optimizes for generalization rather than training fit. The empirical analysis draws on the shortcut learning literature (Shah et al., Geirhos et al.), the grokking literature (Power et al., Liu et al.), and the tabular-data benchmark of Grinsztajn et al., unifying them under a common inductive-bias explanation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying explanation for four historically underperforming domains (tabular data, regression, shortcut learning, grokking) by attributing their difficulties to a mismatch between the ReLU simplicity bias and the target function's complexity. This suggests that domain-tailored architectures or activation functions may still offer meaningful gains beyond scaling, a point increasingly questioned in the current landscape. Key open questions include how to characterize inductive biases beyond the single 'complexity' dimension measured here, whether the findings transfer to deep or transformer architectures (the paper uses only 1–4 layer MLPs), and whether learned activations can be predicted ex ante for a given dataset. The practical impact is currently limited by the simplicity of the test architectures, but the spline + bi-level optimization recipe is a reusable tool for future architecture design.
