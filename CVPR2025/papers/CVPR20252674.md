## Problem

Existing subject-driven text-to-image generation methods (e.g., Textual Inversion, DreamBooth) assume high-quality, artifact-free training images, but real-world image collection inevitably yields inputs blemished by visible artifacts (watermarks, stickers, glass effects) or invisible ones (adversarial noise). These methods lack the discriminative power to separate subject features from artifact interference, leading to catastrophic degradation in generation quality. This paper formalizes the novel task of blemished subject-driven generation and proposes the first solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252674/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of blemished subject images (e.g., 4–8 images per subject with watermarks, stickers, or adversarial perturbations) and a free-form text prompt as input. The blemished images are first converted into a blemished textual embedding via standard Textual Inversion (or a LoRA adapter for DreamBooth), which is then fed into the fine-tuned diffusion model alongside the prompt. The output is a high-resolution (512×512) artifact-free image that preserves the subject's identity while faithfully following the text prompt, with no residual watermark, sticker, or noise patterns in the generated scene.

## Method

ArtiFade constructs a paired training dataset by augmenting unblemished subject images with multiple artifact types (e.g., 10 watermark variants) and learning a blemished textual embedding for each augmented subset via Textual Inversion. The core training procedure—called Artifact Rectification Training—fine-tunes only the key and value weights in all cross-attention and self-attention layers of a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model, while simultaneously optimizing an additional artifact-free embedding ⟨Φ⟩ in the textual space. The loss reconstructs the unblemished image conditioned on the blemished embedding, forcing the model to learn the implicit mapping from artifact-contaminated representations back to clean subject features. At inference, a new blemished test set is encoded via Textual Inversion, and the resulting embedding is simply passed to the fine-tuned model with the prompt 'a ⟨Φ⟩ photo of [V_test]', requiring no architectural changes at test time. This partial fine-tuning strategy preserves the base model's generative capacity while generalizing to out-of-distribution artifact types unseen during training.

## Results

On the in-distribution watermark test (WM-ID-test), ArtiFade (TI-based) achieves I^DINO=0.337, R^DINO=1.300, I^CLIP=0.649, R^CLIP=1.020, and T^CLIP=0.282, recovering most of the gap to the unblemished upper bound (I^DINO=0.488, R^DINO=1.349) while substantially outperforming vanilla TI with blemished embeddings (I^DINO=0.217, R^DINO=0.852). On the out-of-distribution test (WM-OOD-test), ArtiFade maintains strong performance (I^DINO=0.356, R^DINO=1.237, I^CLIP=0.654, R^CLIP=1.079, T^CLIP=0.282), demonstrating generalizability to unseen watermark types. The R^DINO and R^CLIP ratios exceeding 1.0 confirm that generated images resemble unblemished subjects more than their blemished counterparts, indicating effective artifact removal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Textual Inversion (TI) with blemished embeddings, which suffers a severe performance drop: I^DINO falls from 0.488 (unblemished) to 0.217 (blemished) on ID tests, and R^DINO drops below 1.0 (0.852), indicating generated images are more similar to blemished inputs than clean ones. ArtiFade recovers approximately 55% of the I^DINO gap (0.337 vs. 0.217) and pushes R^DINO back above 1.0 (1.300), showing effective artifact suppression. On OOD tests, the same pattern holds: ArtiFade improves I^DINO from 0.229 to 0.356 and R^DINO from 0.858 to 1.237, confirming generalization to unseen watermark types. For the DreamBooth-based comparison (Table 3), the paper reports that ArtiFade with DreamBooth+LoRA yields the highest scores across all metrics, maintaining DreamBooth's advantage in subject fidelity and text prompting over the TI-based variant. The unblemished TI reference (I^DINO=0.488) remains the upper bound that ArtiFade does not fully reach, reflecting the inherent information loss in blemished inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends subject-driven text-to-image generation (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth) by addressing a practical failure mode: input images contaminated with visible artifacts (watermarks, stickers) or invisible ones (adversarial noise). Rather than treating artifact removal as a preprocessing step (as in the watermark/shadow removal literature), ArtiFade fine-tunes the diffusion model's cross-attention key/value weights to learn an implicit mapping from blemished textual embeddings back to unblemished image generation. The approach also introduces an artifact-free embedding in the textual space, drawing on the observation that partial parameter optimization in the text-conditioning pathway is more effective for artifact suppression than image-feature parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real-world subject images scraped from the web are routinely blemished, and current subject-driven methods degrade catastrophically on such inputs. The OOD generalization (trained on watermarks, applied to stickers and glass effects) suggests the model learns a general 'artifact vs. subject' discrimination rather than memorizing specific patterns. However, the method still requires paired blemished/unblemished training data, and the quantitative gap to the unblemished baseline remains substantial (e.g., I^DINO 0.337 vs. 0.488), indicating that artifact removal is not yet a solved problem. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex degradations (compression, low resolution, occlusion) and whether the paired-data requirement can be relaxed.
