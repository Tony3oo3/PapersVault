## Problem

Event-based star tracking has been evaluated exclusively in simulation with simplified signal models, and no real-world dataset or open-source tracker exists to benchmark methods. Existing EBS centroiding techniques ignore the low-light circuit behavior of event pixels, leading to magnitude-dependent centroid offsets that cause suboptimal attitude estimation and drift in real night-sky conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251141/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of positive events (pixel coordinates and microsecond timestamps) from a Prophesee EVK4-HD sensor, processed asynchronously one event at a time, along with a star catalog for association. It outputs a 3D rotation quaternion and 3D angular velocity vector at up to 1 KHz update rate. Only a single absolute initialization via Astrometry.net is required; subsequent updates are purely event-driven with no periodic re-anchoring, unlike prior methods that need absolute measurements every 5–30 seconds.

## Method

The key insight is that in low-light conditions, the EBS pixel's bandwidth is proportional to photocurrent, causing the pixel voltage to behave as a first-order low-pass filter with an intensity-dependent cutoff frequency. This produces a magnitude-dependent spatial offset between the centroid of positive events and the star's true position, which the paper models via a continuous-time differential equation (Eq. 4) and approximates with an isotropic Gaussian whose mean is shifted by a function z(m_s) of the star's apparent magnitude. This likelihood is embedded in an asynchronous 3D extended Kalman filter (EBS-EKF) that estimates full 3D rotation and angular velocity, as opposed to the 2D position/velocity models in prior work. The filter updates on each individual positive event: it predicts the state forward to the event timestamp, associates the event with the nearest catalog star, applies the intensity-dependent offset correction, and performs a standard EKF update. This combination of physically grounded centroiding and 3D state estimation yields ~0.4 pixel centroiding accuracy and keeps attitude error within ~100 arcseconds of a space-ready APS tracker, an order of magnitude better than prior EBS methods.

## Results

On 13 real night-sky tracks, EBS-EKF (with intensity-dependent offset) achieves mean squared differences of ~18–71 arcseconds across and ~53–83 arcseconds about relative to the Rocket Lab APS reference tracker, with one outlier track (Velocity Sweep 7) at 170/139 arcseconds. The closest baseline, 2D-KF, ranges from 62–846 arcseconds across and 814–16,000 arcseconds about, while ICP and Hough deviate by thousands to tens of thousands of arcseconds. On a 7.5 deg/sec high-velocity track where the APS tracker fails, EBS-EKF maintains 80.4 arcseconds total difference versus 774.3 arcseconds for 2D-KF, an order-of-magnitude improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBS-EKF outperforms all three prior EBS baselines by one to three orders of magnitude on both across and about metrics. Against 2D-KF [Ng et al., 2022; Latif et al., 2023], the closest competitor, EBS-EKF reduces across error from 62–846" to 18–71" and about error from 814–16,000" to 53–83"; 2D-KF's 2D state model fails to capture 3D roll dynamics, causing periodic drift that requires frequent absolute re-initialization. Against ICP [Chin et al., 2019], EBS-EKF reduces across error by roughly 100× (e.g., 25.8" vs. 3300" on VS1) and about error by 5–100×. Against Hough [Bagchi et al., 2020], the gap is even larger, with Hough reaching 76,000–87,000 arcseconds on some tracks versus EBS-EKF's sub-100 arcseconds. The intensity-dependent offset correction provides an additional 10–20 arcsecond improvement, most visible when bright stars (magnitude < 3) enter the field of view. Critically, EBS-EKF operates above the 3 deg/sec velocity limit of the APS tracker, where it achieves 80.4 arcseconds total difference versus 774.3 for 2D-KF on a 7.5 deg/sec track.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based star tracking line initiated by Chin et al. (ICP, 2019), Bagchi et al. (Hough, 2020), and Ng et al. (2D-KF, 2022), all of which were evaluated exclusively in simulation on LCD-displayed star fields. The key conceptual departure is a low-light EBS circuit model showing that the event likelihood centroid is offset from the true star position in a magnitude-dependent way, rather than being a simple Gaussian centered on the star as prior works assumed. The state estimation is also upgraded from 2D linear Kalman filtering to a 3D extended Kalman filter that captures roll dynamics, and for the first time the method is validated against a space-ready APS tracker on real night-sky data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of the first synchronized EBS/APS real-star dataset and open-source code provides a much-needed benchmark for a field that had been evaluated only in simulation, enabling meaningful comparison of future EBS star trackers. The demonstration that EBS can operate above the 3 deg/sec APS cutoff and achieve comparable accuracy at 1 KHz update rates suggests a practical path toward replacing APS trackers for high-rate attitude determination. Open questions include whether the low-light circuit model generalizes across EBS hardware generations, how atmospheric effects (scintillation, seeing) impact the centroiding model in space versus ground, and whether the 3D EKF advantage persists under the sparser star fields encountered in certain orbital regimes.
