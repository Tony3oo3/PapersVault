## Problem

Existing CLIP adaptation methods for few-shot learning treat synthetic images merely as complements to scarce real images during fine-tuning, rather than as standalone knowledge repositories from distinct foundation models. No prior work has explored self-supervised pretraining on a purely synthetic base set as a dedicated stage before few-shot fine-tuning, leaving the transferability of synthetic-to-real representations underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252456/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 512×512 resolution (synthetic) and standard benchmark resolutions (real), processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 image encoder with a 4-block learnable adapter. Output is a class label obtained by averaging logits from a vision classifier and a vision-language classifier with a learned fusion weight. For zero-shot recognition, fine-tuning uses only synthetic images with no real data, and the VL-classifier is initialized via prompt ensembling of CLIP hand-crafted templates and CuPL prompts.

## Method

ImagineFSL is a two-stage CLIP adaptation: (1) self-supervised pretraining on iBase, a large-scale purely synthetic dataset (SyntIN1K/1.5K), using HoM-DINO — a self-distillation method where the student-teacher pair represents images via concatenated [CLS] token plus first-, second-, and third-order moments of patch tokens (with square/cubic root normalization), constructs positive views via SyntAug (two T2I-generated images from the same caption), and adds a masked image modeling loss for patch-level discrimination; (2) fine-tuning on K-shot real images plus task-specific synthetic images, tuning the adapter, a vision classifier, and a vision-language classifier (optionally with LoRA on the image encoder). The key insight is that self-supervised pretraining on synthetic images as an independent knowledge source yields more transferable representations than supervised pretraining or no pretraining, and that higher-order moment statistics of patch tokens capture richer local feature distributions critical for few-shot discrimination.

## Results

On the 11-dataset few-shot benchmark, ImagineFSL achieves 79.9% average accuracy (1-shot: 76.1%, 16-shot: 84.4%), outperforming the best prompt-tuning method PromptSRC (77.9%) by 2.0% and the best adapter method TIP-Adapter(f) (75.8%) by 4.1%. With LoRA encoder tuning, ImagineFSL_LoRA reaches 82.1% average accuracy (1-shot: 77.6%, 16-shot: 87.6%), surpassing the strongest hybrid method GalLoP (79.0%) by 3.1%. On domain generalization (16-shot ImageNet→4 targets), ImagineFSL_LoRA achieves 63.2% average, setting new SOTA on ImageNet-Sketch (53.8%) and ImageNet-R (80.0%). For zero-shot recognition, ImagineFSL_LoRA attains 73.0% average accuracy, exceeding the prior best DMN (70.5%) by 2.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompt-tuning baselines (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, ProGrad, PLOT++, PromptSRC), ImagineFSL wins across all K-shot settings, with the largest gap in 1-shot (76.1% vs. PromptSRC's 72.3%, +3.8%). Against adapter-tuning methods (CLIP-Adapter, TIP-Adapter(f), TaskRes), ImagineFSL leads by 4.1–10.5% in average accuracy, confirming that self-supervised pretraining on synthetic iBase substantially boosts adapter quality. Against encoder-tuning and hybrid methods (CLIP-LoRA, LLaMP, GalLoP), ImagineFSL_LoRA achieves the best results in every shot setting, beating GalLoP by 3.1% on average and by 5.1% in 1-shot (77.6% vs. 72.8%). Among synthetic-image-based methods (Table 1), ImagineFSL_LoRA outperforms DataDream (87.0% 16-shot avg) by 0.5% and DISEF (85.1%) by 2.4%, while ImagineFSL (without LoRA) already exceeds the reproduced IsSynth and CaFo by 2.0% in both 1-shot and 16-shot. On domain generalization, ImagineFSL_LoRA surpasses GalLoP by 1.9% and CoCoLe by 1.3% in average accuracy, with new SOTA on ImageNet-Sketch and ImageNet-R. For zero-shot recognition, both variants beat the prior best DMN by 1.8–2.5% average, with ImagineFSL_LoRA ranking first on 7 of 11 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINO self-distillation framework (student-teacher with EMA and KL loss) and iBOT's blockwise masked image modeling into a new self-supervised method (HoM-DINO) that represents images via higher-order moments of patch tokens rather than a single [CLS] token. It departs fundamentally from prior CLIP-adaptation-for-FSL methods (IsSynth, CaFo, DISEF, DataDream) that treat T2I-generated images as one-stage augmentations to scarce real data, instead proposing a two-stage paradigm where synthetic images form an independent 'imagined base set' for pretraining before fine-tuning. The synthetic augmentation (SyntAug) strategy—constructing positive pairs from different T2I outputs of the same caption—replaces hand-crafted geometric/color augmentations, and the CoT + ICL caption generation pipeline distinguishes itself from prior synthetic-data approaches that rely on simpler prompting or fine-tuned T2I models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that self-supervised pretraining on purely synthetic images outperforms supervised pretraining on the same data (by 2.3%/1.1% in 1-shot/16-shot) challenges the default assumption that labeled synthetic data should be used with supervised objectives, and reframes T2I outputs as transferable knowledge repositories rather than mere data augmentation. The systematic CoT+ICL pipeline for scalable caption and image synthesis is practically reusable beyond FSL. Key limitations include the non-trivial pretraining cost (~9 hours on 4×RTX 4090 for ViT-B/16), apparent performance saturation when expanding from SyntIN1K to SyntIN1.5K with a frozen encoder, and underutilization of the rich synthetic captions during fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach benefits more substantially from next-generation T2I models, whether unfreezing the encoder (beyond LoRA) further closes the gap, and whether the distribution-based representation (HoM) generalizes to video or 3D recognition.
