## Problem

When 3D objects are sliced or cut in interactive applications, exposed internal regions often reveal unrealistic or missing textures because existing 3DGS editing frameworks focus exclusively on external appearance. Prior internal-filling approaches like PhysGaussian assume interior textures resemble surface textures, which fails for objects like watermelons or apples where the interior is fundamentally different. No prior method generates realistic, spatially consistent internal textures for 3DGS objects that support real-time rendering under arbitrary geometric transformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained 3DGS surface model, 1–4 real cross-sectional reference images (captured online), angle-specific text prompts (e.g., 'the horizontal cross-sectional view of a watermelon'), and user-defined cutting planes expressed as ax+by+cz+d=0. The output is a single 3DGS model with both surface and interior textures baked in, which can be sliced at any arbitrary angle and rendered in real-time without further optimization. Cross-sectional views are conditioned on estimated depth maps from a pre-trained monocular depth estimator, and the diffusion model is optionally fine-tuned via DreamBooth on the small set of reference images.

## Method

FruitNinja first fills the object's interior with raw Gaussian primitives identified via a discretized opacity field (following PhysGaussian), then synthesizes cross-sectional textures through a two-stage SDS optimization: an initial stage independently refines each rendered cross-section with depth-conditioned Stable Diffusion, followed by a joint reconstruction stage that updates 3DGS parameters against the optimized references while simultaneously training on 10–20 surface views to preserve external appearance. To resolve spatial conflicts between independently generated cross-sections, an iterative refinement loop re-renders and re-optimizes each slice with SDS until convergence, while voxel-grid-based smoothing propagates colors to untrained Gaussians via inverse-distance weighting. The OpaqueAtomGS strategy addresses two 3DGS limitations: atomic clipping caps each Gaussian's scale at ~1/3000 of the object size to prevent oversized particles that destabilize training and obscure fine textures, and uniform opacification assigns full opacity to all Gaussians so that front-most particles dominate rendering, enabling sharp color transitions (e.g., white flesh adjacent to green rind) that standard alpha-blending cannot represent.

## Results

On the 6-object FruitNinja dataset, FruitNinja achieves the highest CLIP score of 33.1 on canonical cross-sections, outperforming PhysGaussian (24.6) and 2D inpainting (25.1). Its FID of 209.2 is ~60% better than PhysGaussian (520.1) but slightly worse than fine-tuned 2D inpainting (176.2). For consistency across 120 arbitrary cut angles, FruitNinja attains the best CLIP score (29.1) and pairwise cosine similarity (0.96), compared to PhysGaussian (23.9 / 0.89) and 2D inpainting (27.8 / 0.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: PhysGaussian's internal-filling mechanism and a 2D inpainting approach using depth-conditioned Stable Diffusion (with and without DreamBooth fine-tuning). FruitNinja wins on CLIP score in both evaluation settings (33.1 vs. 24.6 for PhysGaussian and 25.1 for 2D inpainting on canonical slices; 29.1 vs. 23.9 and 27.8 on arbitrary angles), indicating stronger semantic alignment with cross-sectional prompts. On FID, FruitNinja (209.2) substantially outperforms PhysGaussian (520.1) but trails fine-tuned 2D inpainting (176.2) by roughly 19%, suggesting the diffusion-based 2D approach still produces slightly sharper per-view textures. However, FruitNinja achieves the highest pairwise cosine similarity (0.96 vs. 0.89 and 0.87), demonstrating superior spatial consistency across arbitrary cut angles. Critically, FruitNinja renders in real time after training, whereas the 2D inpainting baseline requires ~30 s of DDIM sampling per view, making it impractical for interactive slicing.

## Relation to Prior Work

FruitNinja builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its base representation, adopts the internal-filling mechanism from PhysGaussian to populate hollow interiors, and borrows AtomGS's atomic clipping to constrain Gaussian scale for fine-grained texture fidelity. It departs from prior 3DGS editing work (which targets only external appearance) and from PhysGaussian's assumption that interior textures mirror surface textures, instead using SDS-guided diffusion (à la DreamFusion) with depth-conditioned Stable Diffusion to synthesize cross-sectional views from just 1–4 reference images. The OpaqueAtomGS strategy—combining atomic clipping with uniform full opacity—is a novel modification of the standard 3DGS rendering pipeline specifically designed to handle sharp interior color transitions and stable slicing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to enable real-time rendering of object interiors under arbitrary geometric cuts without per-edit re-optimization, which is practically important for interactive AR/VR, gaming, and advertising applications where users slice or deform 3D objects. Key limitations include reliance on SDS (which introduces high-frequency artifacts), evaluation on only six objects with relatively simple internal structures, and the requirement for at least one or two canonical cross-sectional reference images. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to highly asymmetric or heterogeneous interiors (e.g., complex food structures, medical tissue), whether the voxel-smoothing resolution can be made adaptive, and whether the method can be extended to objects without exploitable cross-sectional symmetry.
