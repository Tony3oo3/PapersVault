## Problem

B/W manga images exhibit a significant domain gap from natural photographs due to screentones, exaggerated artistic styles, and the absence of color, causing most existing segmentation models to fail. The most comprehensive publicly available manga dataset, Manga109, provides only bounding box annotations that are too coarse for pixel-level segmentation tasks. This paper addresses the absence of a large-scale, pixel-level segmentation annotation for B/W manga.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes B/W manga page images (10,130 double-sided pages from 109 volumes) and produces instance-level segmentation masks for six categories: frames/panels, text/dialog, onomatopoeia, character body, character face, and speech balloons. Unlike the original Manga109 bounding boxes, each annotation is a pixel-precise mask capturing irregular panel shapes, thin onomatopoeia regions, and fine character boundaries such as hair strands. The dataset contains over 700,000 such annotations, with the balloon category being newly introduced beyond the original five Manga109 categories.

## Method

The annotation pipeline operates in two stages. First, coarse annotations are collected by running category-specific detectors (panel splitting, balloon extraction, text detection, body segmentation) and, for faces, a novel colorization step that uses ControlNet with structural lines to colorize only the body regions before feeding the result to GroundedSAM with a 'human face' prompt. Second, an iterative human-in-the-loop refinement loop alternates between manual pixel-level correction by 10 annotators and fine-tuning LoRA-SAM (SAM extended with additional instance tokens) on the corrected annotations, repeating until convergence. Quality control flags masks with IoU < 0.9 against the original bounding boxes for re-annotation. The final fine-tuned SAM achieves over 60% AP on all manga components compared to GroundedSAM, demonstrating the value of the augmented annotation.

## Results

On the MangaSeg test set (927 manga pages), the finetuned SAM achieves mAP@0.5 of 0.977 (MAP) for frame/panel segmentation, 0.956 for balloon, 0.688 for text/dialog, 0.884 for character body, and 0.852 for character face. The model outperforms GroundedSAM by over 60% AP across all manga components. The weakest category is onomatopoeia (MAP 0.272), attributed to its scarcity in manga images causing many false positives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The finetuned SAM with MangaSeg annotations dominates on frame/panel (BAP 0.954 vs. 0.424 for [33], a ~50% improvement) and balloon (BAP 0.914 vs. 0.683 for [29], ~25% improvement), and vastly outperforms GroundedSAM on every category (e.g., balloon BAP 0.914 vs. 0.158; text BAP 0.645 vs. 0.077). On text/dialog, the method wins on BAP (0.645 vs. 0.574 for [19]) but loses on MAP (0.688 vs. 0.754) because [19] uses a UNet with finer upsampling. For character body, the method loses to [25] (BAP 0.860 vs. 0.948) since [25] was trained on the entire Manga109 dataset including the test volumes. For character face, DASSDet+SAM edges out the proposed method (BAP 0.820 vs. 0.781), though the gap narrows on MAP (0.852 vs. 0.824). Onomatopoeia remains the hardest category, where the proposed method is the only approach achieving non-zero scores (BAP 0.373), while GroundedSAM scores 0.000.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Manga109 dataset from bounding-box annotations to pixel-level instance segmentation masks, filling a gap that prior manga/comic datasets (eBDtheque, COMICS, DCM772, PopManga) all left unaddressed. Methodologically, it builds on SAM/LoRA fine-tuning for iterative annotation refinement and repurposes Stable Diffusion + ControlNet colorization as a domain-bridging trick to enable face segmentation on B/W manga where standard detectors fail. The benchmark compares against both handcrafted manga-specific methods (panel splitting, balloon extraction) and general-purpose models (GroundedSAM, DASSDet+SAM, Magi+SAM), positioning the contribution as a dataset and evaluation resource rather than a new segmentation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 700K+ pixel-level annotations across six manga component categories (frames, balloons, text, onomatopoeia, faces, bodies) enable downstream tasks previously infeasible with bounding boxes alone, including pose estimation, instance-level colorization, and precise manga inpainting. Open questions remain around occlusion handling (balloons overlapping panel boundaries), ambiguous hair-region boundaries, and the inner-vs-outer boundary choice for thought balloons. The dataset also exposes a fundamental limitation: general-purpose open-vocabulary segmenters like GroundedSAM achieve near-zero performance on B/W manga (e.g., 0.008 MAP on text), underscoring that screentone patterns and exaggerated B/W styles constitute a domain gap that cannot be bridged by prompt engineering alone.
