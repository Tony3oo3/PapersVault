## Problem

Building wireframe reconstruction from airborne LiDAR point clouds is hampered by sparse, unevenly distributed points, noise from trees and adjacent structures, and a large 3D search space for corner detection. Existing deep learning methods (Point2Roof, Building3D) miss corners and edges under these conditions, while the most recent SOTA (PBWR) relies on non-end-to-end post-processing. Additionally, the field lacks large labeled datasets, limiting model training.

## Input / Output

The method takes an airborne LiDAR point cloud (z-axis aligned to gravity, normalized to [-1, 1]) and projects it onto the xy-plane to produce a 256×256 2D height map where each pixel stores the mean z-value of projected points (empty pixels set to 0). The output is a 3D building wireframe graph: up to 150 2D corner positions each expanded to 2 height levels (NH=300 3D corner queries) and a set of binary-classified edges connecting valid corner pairs, forming the final skeletal roof structure.

## Method

BWFormer employs a 2D-to-3D corner detection strategy: a dilated residual network predicts a per-pixel corner heat map from ResNet-50 multi-scale features, NMS selects the top 150 2D positions, and a 6-layer Transformer decoder with deformable cross-attention refines 3D corner queries initialized at those 2D positions with random height embeddings, reducing the search space. For edge classification, a proposed edge attention mechanism uniformly samples M=5 reference points along each candidate edge, replicates the edge content query at each point, and max-pools the multi-head deformable attention outputs to capture both holistic edge context and local details. A conditional latent diffusion model conditioned on the building footprint simulates realistic LiDAR sampling locations to generate synthetic height maps, which are mixed 3:1 with real data for training. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with bipartite matching for corner assignment and cross-entropy losses for 2D corners, 3D corners, and edges.

## Results

On the Building3D dataset, BWFormer achieves a Wireframe Edit Distance (WED) of 0.238 and Average Corner Offset (ACO) of 0.204, both the best among all compared methods. It attains a Corner F1 of 88.4% and Edge F1 of 79.4%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art PBWR by +7.4% and +2.2% respectively. Notably, BWFormer achieves the highest Corner Recall (82.7%) and Edge Recall (74.1%), demonstrating superior reconstruction completeness on sparse LiDAR point clouds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor PBWR [9] (CVPR 2024), BWFormer wins on WED (0.238 vs. 0.271), ACO (0.204 vs. 0.222), Corner Recall (82.7% vs. 68.8%), Corner F1 (88.4% vs. 81.0%), Edge Recall (74.1% vs. 65.4%), and Edge F1 (79.4% vs. 77.2%), but trails on Corner Precision (94.9% vs. 98.5%) and Edge Precision (85.5% vs. 94.3%), indicating a recall-over-precision trade-off that favors completeness. Compared to Point2Roof [11] and its Building3D variants (supervised and self-supervised [28]), BWFormer dominates across all metrics, with CF1 improving from 41–66% to 88.4%. PC2WF [14] performs poorly on this task (CF1 = 28%, EF1 = 1%), highlighting the difficulty of applying general-purpose wireframe methods to sparse airborne LiDAR. In qualitative comparisons, traditional mesh-based methods (2.5D dual contouring [37], City3D/PolyFit [8]) are sensitive to noise and miss small structures like chimneys, whereas BWFormer produces more complete and detailed wireframes.

## Relation to Prior Work

BWFormer builds on the Deformable DETR framework for end-to-end detection and on HEAT's image feature fusion for edge representation, but departs from prior building wireframe reconstruction methods (Point2Roof, Building3D, PBWR) in a key conceptual way: it exploits the 2.5D nature of airborne LiDAR to project point clouds onto a 2D height map, then performs a two-stage 2D-to-3D corner detection that dramatically reduces the 3D search space. The edge attention mechanism extends vanilla deformable attention by sampling multiple reference points along each candidate edge rather than only at midpoints, and a conditional latent diffusion model is repurposed from image synthesis to simulate realistic LiDAR sampling patterns for data augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2D-to-3D corner detection strategy and edge attention offer practical, well-motivated solutions to the sparsity and incompleteness that plague airborne LiDAR building reconstruction, yielding meaningful gains in recall over prior methods. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (Building3D, Tallinn city), and failure cases with extremely sparse clouds or redundant predictions remain unresolved. Open questions include whether the height-map projection generalizes to non-2.5D scenarios (e.g., multi-return or oblique LiDAR), whether the LDM-based augmentation transfers to other sensor modalities, and whether the approach scales to denser urban scenes with more complex roof geometries.
