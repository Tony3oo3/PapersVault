## Problem

Existing visual emotion recognition (VER) models are trained and evaluated within a single domain (e.g., realistic photos or stickers), and fail to generalize across domains due to severe emotional expression variability and affective distribution shift. The paper introduces the Unsupervised Cross-Domain VER (UCDVER) task, where a model trained on labeled source-domain images must recognize emotions in an unlabeled, visually dissimilar target domain, and shows that even powerful VLMs like LLaVa and InstructBLIP underperform in this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250899/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image from any emotion domain (realistic photo, sticker, abstract painting, art photo, or mixed-style image) and produces a discrete emotion class label. During training, the model consumes both labeled source-domain images and unlabeled target-domain images; for the target domain, pseudo-labels are generated on-the-fly by the CLIEA module using cosine similarity between mapped visual features and emotion-specific prompt embeddings. The textual conditioning pipeline (InstructBLIP captions + AllenNLP SRL knowledge triples) is computed per image and fed as cross-attention context to the denoising network.

## Method

KCDP comprises two modules. KADAP extracts domain-agnostic knowledge by generating image captions with InstructBLIP and parsing subject-verb-object triples via AllenNLP SRL; these are encoded by CLIP and injected into a frozen LDM denoising UNet through a knowledge-guided cross-attention (KGCA) layer, where only low-rank LoRA matrices are trained to preserve pre-trained visual priors. Multi-level UNet features are fused via an FPN, and a Mixture-of-Experts predictor (router + N specialized experts) integrates the enhanced visual features with the knowledge embedding to output the emotion class. CLIEA constructs a structured causal graph over image, prompt, knowledge, visual, and alignment variables, then uses counterfactual prompts (e.g., swapping the emotion word) to compute a Total Indirect Effect; a Counterfactual Contrastive Learning objective with MHA and an MLP mapping network pushes factual-prompt similarities up and counterfactual-prompt similarities down, yielding high-quality pseudo-labels for the unlabeled target domain. The total loss combines source-domain cross-entropy, target-domain pseudo-label cross-entropy, and the CCL contrastive term.

## Results

In the Domain Adaptation (DA) setting, KCDP achieves 46.16% average emotion classification accuracy across six transfer directions, outperforming the best DA baseline UniMoS (34.08%) by 12.08% and the best VER baseline TGCA-PVT (34.00%) by 12.16%. In the Universal Cross-domain (UC) setting, KCDP reaches 57.71% on SER30K and 47.84% on Emo8, surpassing TGCA-PVT (37.01% and 34.77%) by 20.70% and 11.39% respectively. On the source domain EmoSet, KCDP scores 83.38%, slightly below EmoVIT (84.02%) but well above all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KCDP decisively outperforms all four DA baselines (PADCLIP, AD-CLIP, DAMP, UniMoS) in the DA setting, with the largest gap over UniMoS (46.16% vs. 34.08% average, +12.08%). Among VER baselines, KCDP surpasses TGCA-PVT by 12.16% on average, LoRA-V by 13.37%, and MDAN by 14.45%, with the most dramatic gain on E→S (62.78% vs. 37.01% for TGCA-PVT). In the UC setting, KCDP beats all VLM baselines (BLIP-2, LLaVa, InstructBLIP) by 15–30+ points on most target domains, and outperforms EmoVIT on SER30K (57.71% vs. 45.66%) and Emo8 (47.84% vs. 36.89%), though EmoVIT retains a marginal 0.64% edge on the source EmoSet (84.02% vs. 83.38%). The paper attributes the large margins to KCDP's knowledge-guided diffusion perception and counterfactual pseudo-labeling, which address the emotional expression variability and affective distribution shift that conventional DA and VLM approaches cannot handle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of visual emotion recognition (VER), unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA), and diffusion-based perception. It builds on LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of cross-attention layers within a Latent Diffusion Model denoising UNet, repurposing the diffusion architecture not for generation but as a knowledge-conditioned feature extractor. Conceptually, it departs from prior VER work (which operates on single domains) and standard UDA (which handles simpler distribution shifts in classification or detection) by introducing the UCDVER task, where emotional expression variability and affective distribution shift create a more severe cross-domain gap. The use of structured knowledge triples (subject-verb-object) as domain-agnostic conditioning signals for the diffusion process, combined with a counterfactual causal-graph approach to pseudo-label generation, distinguishes it from prior diffusion-for-perception methods that rely on raw text prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of UCDVER as a formal benchmark task fills a genuine gap: prior VER models cannot transfer from realistic images to stickers or abstract art, and standard UDA methods are ill-suited to the abstract, multi-element nature of emotional expression across domains. The finding that large VLMs (BLIP-2, LLaVa, InstructBLIP) trained on diverse multi-domain data still underperform significantly on cross-domain emotion recognition suggests that emotional semantics are not well-captured by general-purpose vision-language pretraining, pointing to a need for emotion-specific alignment. Open questions include whether the knowledge-triple extraction generalizes to domains without clear linguistic structure (e.g., pure abstract paintings), whether the approach scales to more than two source-target domains simultaneously, and how sensitive performance is to the quality of the VLM-generated captions.
