## Problem

MLLMs provide broad visual perception and reasoning but fail at fine-grained tasks like spatial grounding, temporal localization, and tracking. Existing remedies—autoregressively predicting coordinates as text or bolting on task heads—improve specific tasks at the cost of overall multimodal dialogue performance, a conflict the authors attribute to the mismatch between discrete token prediction and dense visual supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250009/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16 video frames (or images), a natural-language query, and optionally initial object coordinates for tracking. Depending on the detected task type, it outputs either a free-form text response or a structured visual prediction: a 4-tuple bounding box (region head), start/end timestamps plus a highlight score (temporal head), or a per-frame binary mask sequence (mask head built on SAM2). Task tokens are dynamically selected by the LLM from the user instruction, so a single model handles all output modalities without architectural switching.

## Method

TPO appends three differentiable task heads—region (2-layer MLP regressing box coordinates), temporal (CG-DETR-based moment retrieval/highlight detection), and mask (SAM2 encoder-decoder with an MLP adapter replacing the prompt encoder)—to the MLLM via learnable task tokens whose embeddings are extracted from the LLM's last hidden layer. Training follows a 3-stage local-to-global schedule: (1) LoRA-tune the LLM to recognize task-type tokens from dialogue templates, (2) train each task token and its head on dense visual annotations while the LLM is LoRA-updated, and (3) jointly fine-tune the vision encoder, connector, task tokens, all heads, and LoRA-adapted LLM on a mixed corpus of multimodal conversations and task data. The composite loss sums the standard MLLM cross-entropy, a task-assignment cross-entropy, and per-task regression/classification losses, allowing gradients from dense visual supervision to flow back into the language model and improve its internal spatiotemporal representations without degrading dialogue quality.

## Results

VideoChat-TPO (7B LLM, 16 frames) achieves 66.8 on MVBench (+6.4 over VideoChat2 baseline), 48.8 on VideoMME without subtitles (+9.3), and 54.7 M-AVG on MLVU (+10.2), representing an overall 14.6% average improvement across multimodal benchmarks. On fine-grained visual tasks, TPO achieves 40.2 R@0.5 zero-shot moment retrieval on Charades-STA (surpassing SOTA MLLM ChatVTG at 33.0), 69.4 LaSOT Success in zero-shot tracking (vs. 51.5 for fine-tuned ATOM), and 63.9 J&F zero-shot referring segmentation on Ref-YouTube-VOS (comparable to fine-tuned ReferFormer at 62.9). Applied to LLaVA-OneVision, TPO yields an 8.1-point MVBench improvement (56.7→64.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VideoChat2 baseline, TPO delivers consistent gains: +6.4 on MVBench, +9.3 on VideoMME (w/o subtitles), and +10.2 on MLVU, all with the same 16-frame input and 7B LLM. Compared to ST-LLM (64 frames), VideoChat-TPO achieves 66.8 vs. 54.9 on MVBench while using 4× fewer frames. In zero-shot moment retrieval on Charades-STA, TPO reaches 40.2 R@0.5, surpassing both the SOTA MLLM ChatVTG (33.0) and the expert model UniVTG (25.2). For spatial grounding on RefCOCO, TPO (85.9/90.8/81.3) slightly trails the expert G-DINO-L (90.6/93.2/88.2) but outperforms pixel-to-embedding MLLMs like NExT-Chat (85.5/90.0/77.9). In zero-shot tracking, TPO achieves 69.4 LaSOT Success and 79.8 GOT-10k SR@0.5, substantially exceeding fine-tuned expert trackers SiamFC (33.6) and ATOM (51.5/63.4). On multi-image understanding (MMIU), TPO scores 40.2 vs. 35.0 for VideoChat2 and 32.4 for LLaVA-Interleave, while on SEED-Bench2 it improves average performance by 41.7% over the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two dominant paradigms for equipping MLLMs with fine-grained visual capabilities: pixel-to-sequence (P2S) methods like Shikra and TimeChat that autoregressively predict coordinates or timestamps as text, and pixel-to-embedding (P2E) methods like LISA and NExT-Chat that route visual features to external decoders. TPO departs from both by introducing learnable task tokens that serve as differentiable bridges between the MLLM and task-specific heads (region, temporal, mask), drawing conceptual inspiration from DPO's preference-optimization framing but applying it to dense visual supervision rather than binary response preferences. The architecture composes existing expert components—SAM2 for segmentation, CG-DETR for temporal grounding—into a unified MLLM via a three-stage local-to-global training scheme, while the multi-task co-training design echoes findings from vision foundation model literature on synergistic task learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TPO offers a practical, scalable recipe for integrating fine-grained visual task supervision into MLLMs without degrading general multimodal dialogue, which prior P2S/P2E approaches often sacrifice. The demonstrated synergy from multi-task co-training (Table 12) suggests that adding more task heads and data yields compounding gains, pointing toward a path for progressively enriching MLLMs with the full spectrum of discriminative vision capabilities. However, the framework is limited to supervised discriminative tasks, excludes generative tasks, and relies on human annotations—leaving open whether self-supervised or contrastive signals could extend TPO's scalability. A key open question is whether the task-token mechanism generalizes to tasks with fundamentally different output structures (e.g., 3D reconstruction, video generation) or to larger model scales where the current LoRA-based fine-tuning may become insufficient.
