## Problem

Existing temporal trackers for visual object tracking only transmit previous observations (e.g., updated templates or historical trajectories) without learning temporal dynamics from them, resulting in inferior generalization when the target undergoes appearance deformation or background variation in long-term tracking. The paper identifies that merely passing historical information forward is insufficient for understanding the tracking scenario and adapting to novel situations in new frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250419/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a template image (128×128 or 192×192) from the first frame and a cropped search region (256×256 or 384×384) from the current frame as input. It outputs a target bounding box trajectory spanning the current frame and the next two frames, with each timestamp producing three candidate trajectories (multimodal prediction) along with their probabilities; the highest-probability candidate is selected as the final output. During training, ground-truth search regions from the next two frames are additionally required for self-supervision of the future dreaming module.

## Method

DreamTrack employs a dream-encoder-decoder architecture that reformulates temporal tracking as a History-to-Future process. A Future Dreaming module uses three trainable target queries updated via GRU to cache temporal dynamics, then generates future observation features through cross-attention, supervised by KL divergence and MSE reconstruction losses against ground-truth future frames. A ViT encoder (Base or Large) processes the current and dreamed future features jointly, with a Temporal Self-Attention module inserted after each of four encoder stages to refine temporal dynamics with target-aware information. The decoder applies Temporal Cross-Attention between target queries and search-region features, followed by a Multimodal Prediction head that forecasts three possible future trajectories with associated probabilities per timestamp, trained with GIoU, L1, and cross-entropy losses.

## Results

DreamTrack-L384 achieves SUC scores of 76.6% on LaSOT and 87.9% on TrackingNet, surpassing all recent SOTA trackers. On GOT-10k (strict one-shot protocol), it reaches 79.9% AO and 80.3% SR0.75, and on TNL2K it obtains 62.9% SUC / 67.4% Precision. The compact DreamTrack256 variant already outperforms the larger ARTrack-L384 on LaSOT (73.8% vs. 73.1% SUC) while running at 139 FPS, demonstrating strong efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamTrack-L384 outperforms the strongest recent baselines across all five primary benchmarks: it leads ARTrackV2-L384 by 3.0% SUC on LaSOT (76.6% vs. 73.6%), 1.8% on TrackingNet (87.9% vs. 86.1%), and 0.4% AO on GOT-10k (79.9% vs. 79.5%). Against LoRAT-L378, the gap is 1.5% SUC on LaSOT (76.6% vs. 75.1%) and 0.6% on TNL2K (62.9% vs. 62.3%). Notably, the smaller DreamTrack256 (ViT-Base, 256×256 search) already surpasses ARTrack-L384 (ViT-Large, 384×384) on LaSOT by 0.7% SUC while running at 139 FPS versus 5 FPS, highlighting the efficiency advantage of the one-stream architecture with sparse multimodal prediction. On OTB100, NFS, and UAV123 (Table 2), DreamTrack-L384 also achieves the best SUC scores (72.4%, 67.4%, 73.1%), confirming consistent gains over LoRAT-L378 and ARTrackV2-L384 on these additional benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamTrack departs from the dominant Siamese template-matching paradigm (one-shot detection) and from temporal trackers that merely transmit historical observations (SeqTrack, ARTrack, ARTrackV2) by reformulating temporal learning as a History-to-Future process. The core mechanism is borrowed from the world-model literature in reinforcement learning (Dreamer/DreamerV2/V3), where a latent dynamics model predicts future states from past experience; here, a GRU-cached set of target queries plays the role of the latent dynamics, and a cross-attention 'dreaming' step generates future observation features. The multimodal prediction head is inspired by motion prediction in autonomous driving, where multiple plausible future trajectories are scored and the most probable one is selected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DreamTrack demonstrates that explicitly modeling future environmental variation (rather than only past observations) yields consistent SOTA gains across eight tracking benchmarks while maintaining real-time speed (139 FPS for the 256-px variant), suggesting that 'dreaming' future states is a viable and efficient inductive bias for long-term tracking. However, the future-prediction quality degrades noticeably with horizon (Table 6: SUC drops from 73.8 to 68.5 over two frames), and the dreamed future is used only as a training-time supervision signal rather than at inference, leaving open whether the learned dynamics could be exploited for downstream tasks like video generation or annotation as the authors speculate. The approach also remains single-object and single-template, so generalization to multi-object or class-conditional tracking is unexplored.
