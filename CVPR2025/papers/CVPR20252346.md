## Problem

MLLMs frequently generate hallucinated content that contradicts visual input, and prior decoding strategies (contrastive decoding, self-calibrating attention) fail to address the root cause in the token interaction process. The paper identifies two specific mechanisms—attention collapse, where low-information outlier tokens receive disproportionate softmax attention, and positional information decay, where RoPE's long-term attenuation progressively starves later text tokens of visual context—that together drive both initial and snowball hallucinations, particularly in video tasks.

## Input / Output

FarSight is a plug-and-play decoding module that wraps around any existing MLLM's transformer decoder at inference time, requiring no retraining, additional data, or auxiliary networks. It takes the standard multimodal input (image or video frames encoded as vision tokens concatenated with text prompt tokens) and produces the same autoregressive text output (VQA answers, captions, or free-form responses) as the base model, but with a modified causal mask applied at every attention layer during generation. The only hyperparameter is a decay rate σ derived from the sequence length (default 256) and a maximum token limit (1024).

## Method

FarSight modifies the standard causal mask operation in two ways. First, it introduces an upper-triangular attention register matrix P with linearly decaying entries P_{i,j} = −σ(j−i) for j > i, which is added to the attention scores before softmax and then zeroed out after softmax (W̃ = SoftMax(ω·C + P)·C), effectively absorbing excess attention that would otherwise concentrate on low-information outlier tokens while preserving causal decoding. Second, it implements a progressively diminishing masking rate so that the sum of actual (lower-triangular) attention scores is no longer forced to equal 1; instead, the cumulative attention over valid tokens monotonically increases with row index, encoding absolute positional information that counteracts RoPE's relative-distance decay and maintains attention on early visual tokens throughout long generation sequences. The entire method is training-free and operates purely by optimizing the causal mask structure within the existing softmax attention computation.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5, FarSight achieves CHAIR_S of 41.6 (↓6.4 over baseline), POPE-P of 86.1 (↑3.3), and MMBench of 66.0 (↑1.7), outperforming all competing decoding strategies (ICD, VCD, OPERA) across every image benchmark. On video tasks, FarSight yields an average accuracy gain of +3% on MSRVTT-QA (peak 68.9%) and consistent improvements on MSVD-QA (+1.4–2.9%) and ActivityNet-QA (+0.5–1.2%) across four video MLLMs. The method also preserves text quality, with GPT-4o-rated grammar, fluency, and naturalness scores remaining comparable to baselines on MSCOCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FarSight outperforms all three competing decoding strategies (ICD, VCD, OPERA) on LLaVA-1.5 across every image benchmark. The largest gains are on hallucination metrics: CHAIR_S drops from 45.2 (OPERA) / 46.8 (VCD) / 47.7 (ICD) to 41.6, and POPE-P rises from 82.8 (OPERA) / 83.5 (VCD) / 84.0 (ICD) to 86.1. The only metric where a competitor edges ahead is CHAIR_I, where OPERA achieves 12.7 vs. FarSight's 13.2, though both are close. On the positional-encoding ablation (Table 1), FarSight also surpasses EDVT and FixVPE by 4.6–5.4 points on CHAIR_S and 0.7–3.5 points on POPE-P. Across video benchmarks (Table 3), FarSight consistently improves accuracy by +0.5 to +2.9 points over the base models (Chat-UniVi, Video-LLaVA, VILA, Video-LLaMA2) without any competing decoding method included for direct video comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free decoding strategy line (ICD, VCD, OPERA) by shifting the intervention point from logit manipulation or attention re-weighting to the causal mask itself. It builds on OPERA's identification of 'summary token' attention collapse and EDVT/FixVPE's findings on RoPE's insufficiency for multimodal token propagation, but unifies both issues under a single mechanism: attention registers in the upper triangular mask plus a diminishing masking rate. The key conceptual departure is framing hallucination mitigation as a token-propagation problem solvable by mask optimization rather than as a contrastive or penalty-based decoding problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FarSight offers a genuinely plug-and-play, training-free solution that requires no architectural changes, additional data, or external knowledge retrieval, making it immediately deployable across existing MLLMs. The simultaneous addressing of both initial and snowball hallucinations (which prior decoding methods largely failed to reduce) is practically significant for video captioning and long-form generation. Open questions include whether the fixed decay factor σ = log_α(seq) generalizes to much longer sequences or different tokenization schemes, and whether the approach transfers to audio or 3D modalities where positional decay may behave differently.
