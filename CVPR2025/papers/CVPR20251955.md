## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for 3D intraoral scan tooth segmentation achieve high crown accuracy but perform poorly at the crown-gingiva boundary. This stems from two limitations: uniform mesh downsampling (e.g., QEM) erases geometric details at tooth boundaries, and point cloud features (coordinates + normals) alone are insufficient to capture discriminative boundary information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251955/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D intraoral scan mesh downsampled to 16,000 points (each with 6D features: 3D coordinates + 3D normals) alongside 96 rendered images at 1024×1024 resolution, captured from cameras placed on the upper hemisphere with vertical downward parallel lighting to maximize gum-crown contrast. It produces a 17-class semantic segmentation mask (16 teeth labeled T1–T16 plus gingiva/background T0) and a binary tooth-gingiva boundary mask, with the boundary defined as points having more than half of their 8 nearest neighbors in a different class.

## Method

CrossTooth introduces a curvature-weighted selective downsampling that modifies QEM by assigning a collapse cost multiplier of k=10 to negative-curvature edges (tooth boundaries) and k=1 to positive-curvature edges, retaining 10–15% more boundary vertices than standard QEM. A dual-branch architecture processes the point cloud through a 5-stage Point Transformer encoder-decoder (channels 32–512, kNN fusion) and the rendered images through PSPNet, with image pixel features projected back onto the point cloud via camera intrinsics and fused via MLP after one-hot encoding. Training combines cross-entropy loss on both image and point segmentation with a Contrastive Boundary Learning (CBL) loss applied only at the final decoder layer, which pushes apart features of neighboring points from different classes to sharpen boundary discrimination.

## Results

On the 3DTeethSeg'22 dataset (1,800 intraoral scan models, 17-class segmentation), CrossTooth achieves 95.86% mIoU and 82.06% boundary IoU, outperforming the strongest baseline ToothGroupNet (93.55% mIoU, 76.39% boundary IoU) by 2.3% and 5.7% respectively. The method also leads on per-tooth IoU across all 14 evaluated tooth pairs (T1/T9 through T7/T15), with the largest gains on posterior teeth (e.g., T7/T15: 68.06% vs. 65.13% for ToothGroupNet). On the 3D-IOSSeg dataset, CrossTooth achieves 88.79% mIoU with 128 rendered images, surpassing TSGCNet (85.94%) and HiCANet (88.05%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossTooth outperforms all nine baselines on both mIoU and boundary IoU across both datasets. The closest competitor, ToothGroupNet [1] (the MICCAI 2022 challenge winner), trails by 2.3% mIoU and 5.7% boundary IoU, with the gap widening on posterior teeth (T7/T15: 68.06 vs. 65.13). DilatedSegNet [11] is second-best in mIoU (91.44%) but lags CrossTooth by 4.4% and 7.2% on boundary IoU. SimpSegNet [10] achieves 88.45% mIoU but shows notable weakness on molars (59.95% on T7/T15). TeethGNN [47] (74.63% mIoU) and UpToothSeg [8] (83.95%) are substantially behind, while MeshSegNet [17] and TSegNet [4] fail to produce meaningful boundary segmentation (0.90% and 0.00% boundary IoU respectively). On the 3D-IOSSeg dataset, CrossTooth (88.79% mIoU with 128 images) beats TSGCNet [43] (85.94%) and HiCANet [13] (88.05%), and selective downsampling further improves all three methods by 1–7% boundary IoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossTooth builds on the Point Transformer encoder-decoder architecture and the QEM mesh simplification framework, but departs from both in targeted ways: it replaces uniform QEM with a curvature-weighted selective downsampling that preferentially retains vertices at high-curvature (tooth-gingiva) boundaries, and it introduces a cross-modal stream where multi-view rendered images processed by PSPNet provide dense boundary cues projected back onto the sparse point cloud. This positions the paper at the intersection of mesh simplification, point cloud segmentation, and 2D-to-3D feature transfer, extending the contrastive boundary learning (CBL) loss from Tang et al. into a dental-specific setting. Unlike prior dual-branch methods (TSGCNet, SGTNet) that split coordinates and normals, CrossTooth splits the modality itself—geometry vs. rendered appearance—as the two complementary streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate crown-gingiva boundary delineation is a prerequisite for downstream clinical workflows (crown design, implant planning), and the 5.7% boundary IoU gain over the prior best method is clinically meaningful. However, the paper acknowledges that the simple MLP fusion at the final stage is not fine-grained enough, that cases with severe tooth loss or atypical wisdom teeth remain problematic, and that the image-feature contribution is modest (0.7% mIoU in ablation), suggesting the fusion strategy is the current bottleneck. Open questions include whether more sophisticated cross-modal fusion (e.g., attention-based or multi-level) could close the remaining gap, and whether the curvature-weighted downsampling generalizes to other boundary-critical 3D segmentation tasks beyond dentistry.
