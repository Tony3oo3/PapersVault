## Problem

Existing Gaussian Splatting methods model view-dependent effects by querying per-primitive spherical harmonics with the viewing direction, creating a strong ambiguity between primitive orientation and viewing angle that degrades both rendering quality and geometry accuracy on reflective or refractive surfaces. Prior attempts to use reflection direction as the color query in Gaussian Splatting fail because each primitive independently inherits its own orientation and SH coefficients, so the directional transform can be absorbed by parameter updates, yielding noisy reconstructions.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view posed RGB images as input and produces photorealistic novel view renderings at 800×800 resolution with real-time speed (>45 FPS), along with recovered surface normals, a 3D mesh, and a per-pixel roughness map. Internally, the pipeline generates a G-buffer containing blended diffuse color, a D-dimensional spatial feature, roughness, and normal maps, which are then consumed by a lighting pass that queries a 512×1024×16 spherical mipmap grid (9 levels) via trilinear interpolation in the fragment shader.

## Method

Ref-GS introduces a deferred Gaussian splatting pipeline that first alpha-blends per-primitive attributes (diffuse color, spatial feature, roughness, normal) along each ray to form a G-buffer, and only then evaluates view-dependent color on the integrated surface, eliminating the orientation-viewing ambiguity inherent in per-primitive directional queries. For far-field lighting, a Sph-Mip grid—a multi-level spherical feature parameterized by longitude-latitude coordinates and roughness—provides roughness-aware directional features via trilinear interpolation. A geometry-lighting factorization connects the spatial feature K and directional feature S through a per-pixel vector outer product (K⊗S), which is flattened and fed to a lightweight single-hidden-layer MLP to predict the final specular color. This factorization explicitly decouples geometry from lighting while capturing global illumination, shadows, and self-occlusion, and reduces per-primitive feature channels to lower rendering overhead. Training is self-supervised via multi-view photometric loss over 30,000 Adam iterations on a single V100 GPU.

## Results

On the Shiny Blender dataset, Ref-GS achieves 34.38 average PSNR, competitive with 3DGS-DR (34.61) and surpassing 3iGS (32.64) and 3DGS (32.32). On the Shiny Real dataset, Ref-GS attains the best PSNR (24.44), SSIM (0.682), and LPIPS (0.224) among all compared methods. For geometry recovery, Ref-GS achieves the lowest normal MAE of 2.21° on Shiny Blender, outperforming 3DGS-DR (2.43°) and ENVIDR (4.61°). On Glossy Synthetic, Ref-GS leads with 30.59 PSNR, ahead of 3DGS-DR (29.78) and ENVIDR (29.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ref-GS outperforms all Gaussian-based baselines (3DGS, GaussianShader, 3iGS) on Shiny Real by 0.34–0.98 dB PSNR and by 0.022–0.035 SSIM, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.224 vs. 0.230–0.257). Against the strongest deferred-rendering competitor 3DGS-DR, Ref-GS wins on Shiny Real PSNR (24.44 vs. 23.80), SSIM (0.682 vs. 0.659), and LPIPS (0.224 vs. 0.236), but trails slightly on Shiny Blender average PSNR (34.38 vs. 34.61). On the Garden scene, Ref-GS leads all methods with 34.80 PSNR, a 0.86 dB margin over 3DGS-DR. For geometry, Ref-GS achieves the best normal MAE (2.21°) on Shiny Blender, beating 3DGS-DR (2.43°) and ENVIDR (4.61°), confirming that the deferred directional factorization improves surface accuracy. On NeRF Synthetic, Ref-GS (33.20 PSNR) is marginally below 3iGS (33.60) and 3DGS (33.30), indicating that the advantage is most pronounced on reflective and real-world scenes rather than simple synthetic objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ref-GS extends 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) by introducing a deferred rendering pipeline that decouples geometry blending from view-dependent shading, directly addressing the orientation-viewing ambiguity that plagues per-primitive directional queries in forward-rendered Gaussian methods like GaussianShader, 3iGS, and 3DGS-DR. The directional factorization via vector outer product draws conceptual inspiration from TensoRF's tensorial decomposition, while the Sph-Mip grid adapts mipmap-style multi-resolution encoding to the spherical domain for roughness-aware far-field lighting. Unlike NeRF-based reflective methods (Ref-NeRF, NeRO, ENVIDR) that rely on heavy MLPs for geometry, Ref-GS retains the explicit, rasterization-based efficiency of Gaussian representations while achieving comparable or superior reflective rendering quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the geometry-quality degradation in reflective Gaussian splatting stems from a fundamental representational ambiguity (per-primitive SH coefficients can absorb orientation changes), and that deferred shading is a principled fix—this insight is likely to influence how future Gaussian-based rendering pipelines are structured. However, the method remains 2.6× slower to train and 0.37× slower to render than vanilla 3DGS, and near-field inter-reflections (spatially varying environment maps) are still not fully modeled. Open questions include scalability to very large open-world scenes, extension to dynamic or animated content, and whether the deferred approach can accommodate physically-based material models (e.g., full BRDFs) without further overhead.
