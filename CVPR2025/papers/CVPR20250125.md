## Problem

Conventional ellipsometry requires capturing 20+ images while mechanically rotating polarizing optics, making it impractical for dynamic scenes. Single-shot Mueller-matrix methods exist but sacrifice spatial resolution or are restricted to planar targets at fixed distances. The paper addresses how to acquire full Mueller-matrix video of arbitrary dynamic scenes at video rates without these compromises.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250125/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes asynchronous event streams (timestamp, polarity, pixel location) from a Prophesee EVK4 event camera and synchronized QWP angle measurements from custom microsecond-resolution angle encoders, with one-time calibration of the per-pixel contrast threshold C and QWP fast-axis offsets i₁, i₂. The output is a normalized Mueller matrix (M/M₀₀) video at 30 fps, where each 33 ms frame corresponds to one π rotation of the light-source QWP; normalization is inherent because event cameras detect only intensity changes, not absolute intensity. The system operates in both reflection and transmission modes at 500×500 resolution with an average event rate of 153 MEv/s.

## Method

The core mechanism places fast-rotating quarter-wave plates (QWPs) in front of both an LED source and an event camera in a dual-rotating-retarder configuration with a 5:1 speed ratio, so that continuous polarization modulation produces intensity changes captured as asynchronous events. The paper derives an ellipsometric-event image formation model relating each event's time difference Δt_k to the Mueller matrix via the differential of log intensity, yielding a per-event linear constraint B_tk·M̂ = 0. Reconstruction proceeds in two stages: (1) per-pixel weighted least-squares solved by SVD, iteratively refined with Cloude physical-validity filtering and outlier-aware weight recomputation (up to 5 iterations); and (2) spatio-temporal propagation and refinement inspired by PatchMatchStereo, where neighboring pixels in space and time propagate lower-error estimates and random multiplicative perturbations (σ=0.01) with Cloude filtering refine each pixel over 10 iterations. This physics-based forward model combined with optimization-based reconstruction avoids any learned components and handles sensor noise and scene motion through the physical validity constraints.

## Results

Event Ellipsometer achieves Mueller-matrix video imaging at 30 fps with a per-frame acquisition time of 33 ms (one π rotation of the light-source QWP). On synthetic data with known polarimetric BRDFs, the full two-stage reconstruction reduces the mean absolute error from 0.11 (SVD initialization alone) to 0.04. On real-world samples with known Mueller matrices (air, linear polarizers, quarter-wave plates), the method achieves a mean-squared error of 0.045. Reconstruction of a 500×500, 30-frame sequence at 153 MEv/s takes 62 s (per-pixel) + 68 s (propagation/refinement) on an 8-core CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a formal head-to-head quantitative comparison against other Mueller-matrix imaging methods; instead, it validates against ground-truth references. Against conventional frame-based ellipsometers, Event Ellipsometer reduces per-frame acquisition from several minutes (requiring >20 rotation angles with an intensity sensor) to 33 ms, enabling 30 fps dynamic capture. Compared to single-shot snapshot polarimeters [12, 33, 49], Event Ellipsometer does not sacrifice spatial resolution, does not require planar scenes at fixed distances, and natively handles HDR without multi-exposure capture. The synthetic-data ablation (SVD-only vs. full pipeline) shows that the spatio-temporal propagation and refinement stage nearly halves the reconstruction error (0.11 → 0.04), confirming the benefit of the two-stage design over a per-pixel-only approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the dual-rotating-retarder ellipsometry architecture (Azzam [3], Smith [44]) by replacing the conventional frame-based intensity sensor with an event camera, creating a fundamentally different signal acquisition paradigm. The 5:1 QWP rotation ratio follows Smith's optimization, and Cloude's physical-validity filtering [16] serves as a core regularization step in both reconstruction stages. The key departure from prior event-based vision work (Hawks [25], Muglikar [37]) is that those methods only capture partial polarization (e.g., linear polarization via a single rotating LP), whereas this paper reconstructs the full 4×4 Mueller matrix by modulating both illumination and detection with synchronized rotating QWPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work extends ellipsometry from a minutes-long static-scene measurement to a 33 ms per-frame video-rate technique, opening applications in dynamic photoelasticity, real-time human capture, and industrial inspection (e.g., tape detection on moving boxes) that were previously infeasible. Key limitations include: the reconstructed Mueller matrix is normalized (divided by M₀₀) because event cameras measure only intensity changes, not absolute intensity; reconstruction is CPU-only and takes ~130 s for 30 frames, precluding real-time use; and the system requires custom hardware (brushed DC motors, 3D-printed angle encoders). Open questions include whether full (unnormalized) Mueller matrix recovery is possible with event-intensity hybrid sensors, and whether GPU-accelerated or learned reconstruction could close the gap to real-time operation.
