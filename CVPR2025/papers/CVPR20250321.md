## Problem

Existing pillar-based 3D object detectors suffer from two critical issues in their Pillar Feature Encoding (PFE) stage: (1) information loss along the height dimension caused by the absence of explicit height-aware encoding and the use of max-pooling, which discards fine-grained geometric details; and (2) large numerical distribution disparities across PFE input channels (coordinates spanning [-51.2, 51.2], intensity in [0, 255], offsets in [-0.2, 0.2]), which makes INT8 quantization highly unstable with 7+ NDS drops.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR points (x, y, z, intensity) that are pillarized along the XY plane only (pillar size 0.2 m, no vertical division), and produces a fixed-length D-dimensional feature vector per non-empty pillar. Unlike standard PFE that requires sampling/padding to a fixed point count per pillar and applies point-wise MLPs, PillarHist operates directly on the variable-length point set within each pillar via histogram binning, so no padding is needed. The linear projection input is a compact (2B+2)-dimensional vector (point-count histogram, intensity-weighted histogram, pillar center x/y) rather than N_v × 10 point features.

## Method

PillarHist replaces the conventional point-wise MLP + max-pooling PFE with a height-aware histogram encoder: the pillar height H is divided into B bins, and for each bin it counts the number of points (H^p) and accumulates their LiDAR reflection intensities to compute a per-bin mean intensity (H^i). These two B-dimensional histograms are concatenated with the pillar center coordinates (x_center, y_center) to form a (2B+2)-dimensional vector, which is projected to D channels by a single linear layer applied at the pillar level rather than per-point. This design eliminates max-pooling (preserving full height distribution), removes the need for point sampling/padding, reduces FLOPs by operating on one vector per pillar instead of N_v point vectors, and constrains all input values to bounded ranges (counts ≤ N_v, intensities in [0,255], coordinates in fixed pillar grid), making the module inherently quantization-friendly. PillarHist is designed as a drop-in plugin that can replace the PFE in any existing pillar-based detector (PointPillars, CenterPoint-Pillar, PillarNet, FastPillars) without modifying downstream BEV or head modules.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, PillarHist integrated into PointPillars achieves 47.0 NDS / 32.4 mAP at 25 ms latency, improving over the original PointPillars (45.3 NDS / 30.5 mAP at 31 ms) by +1.7 NDS and reducing latency by 6 ms. On the nuScenes val set under INT8 post-training quantization, PillarHist reduces the quantization drop from 7.3 NDS (PointPillars) to only 1.6 NDS (PH-PointPillars), and from 6.8 NDS (PillarNet) to 1.4 NDS (PH-PillarNet). On KITTI val, PillarHist achieves 81.42 mAP (Car 3D_R40) and 51.07 mAP (Ped 3D_R40) with only 0.065 GFLOPs for PFE, outperforming all compared PFE modules while using fewer FLOPs than the original PointPillars PFE (0.152 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PillarHist consistently outperforms the original PFE in every pillar-based backbone it is plugged into: +1.7 NDS over PointPillars, +1.1 NDS over CenterPoint-Pillar, and +0.7/+0.6 NDS over PillarNet-18/34 on nuScenes test, while also reducing inference latency by 4–9 ms. On KITTI val (Table 5), PillarHist achieves the highest Car 3D_R40 mAP (81.42) and Ped 3D_R40 mAP (51.07) among all compared PFE modules (PFE, PAA, TA, DynPFE, DynPFE*, FG-PFE, MAPE), using only 0.065 GFLOPs—less than half the cost of the original PointPillars PFE (0.152 GFLOPs) and far below TA (7.061 GFLOPs) or PAA (3.391 GFLOPs). Under INT8 quantization on nuScenes val, PillarHist reduces the NDS drop from 6.8–7.8 (original PFE) to 1.4–1.6, making the quantized model nearly match its FP32 counterpart (e.g., PH-PillarNet 8/8: 61.6 NDS vs. FP32: 63.0 NDS). On Waymo val, PH-CP-Pillar gains +1.4 mAP and +0.7 mAPH over CP-Pillar, with the largest pedestrian improvement of +2.0 mAP / +2.5 mAPH. Against non-pillar baselines on nuScenes (Focal Conv, VoxelNeXt, FG-PFE, FastPillars), PillarHist-integrated methods remain competitive in latency (25–92 ms) while the highest-performing non-pillar methods (FastPillars at 71.8 NDS) still lead in absolute accuracy, confirming PillarHist's role as a drop-in PFE improvement rather than a full detector redesign.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PointPillars PFE paradigm (point-wise MLP followed by max-pooling) and positions itself against a lineage of PFE refinements including TANet's triple attention, PiFeNet's pillar-aware attention, PillarNet's dynamic PFE, and FastPillars' MAPE module. The key conceptual departure is replacing point-wise feature extraction and max-pooling with a height-aware histogram that operates at the pillar level, eliminating both the sampling requirement and the information loss from pooling. It also connects to the LiDAR quantization literature (LiDAR-PTQ, PD-Quant) but takes an orthogonal approach: rather than improving quantization algorithms, it redesigns the PFE input distribution to be inherently quantization-friendly by constraining activations to bounded integer counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in PillarHist's plugin architecture: it can be dropped into any existing pillar-based detector (PointPillars, CenterPoint-Pillar, PillarNet, FastPillars) with ~1 NDS improvement and 4–9 ms latency reduction, while simultaneously reducing INT8 quantization degradation from ~7–8 NDS to ~1.5 NDS across all tested baselines. This makes it particularly relevant for edge deployment where both accuracy and quantization robustness are critical. Limitations include its scope being confined to the PFE stage (the rest of the network remains unchanged), the histogram bin count being a fixed hyperparameter, and the quantization experiments using only a simple RTN PTQ setting without exploring more advanced calibration strategies. Open questions include whether the quantization-friendliness generalizes to lower bit-widths (INT4) or to temporal/multi-frame pillar encodings.
