## Problem

Existing captioning paradigms leave a critical gap: image captioning produces isolated per-frame descriptions with no temporal context, while video captioning collapses an entire clip into a single narrative. Neither captures the fine-grained, progressive dynamics of how an action unfolds across frames. Moreover, leading VLMs exhibit two specific failures on this task—overly generic captions that fail to distinguish adjacent frames (lack of temporal granularity) and captions that invent progression not visible in the visuals (temporal hallucination).

## Input / Output

The model accepts a video frame sequence of 2 to T frames sampled at 1 FPS, allowing users to control temporal context (frame pairs for local changes, full sequences for global progression). It outputs a corresponding sequence of per-frame captions, each of which must satisfy three constraints: faithful accuracy to the specific frame, temporal specificity (not generic enough to apply to multiple frames), and progressive coherence (captions build successively on earlier ones to reflect action evolution). The FrameCapEval benchmark comprises 684 manually verified videos from four datasets to assess these properties.

## Method

ProgressCaptioner is a 7B VLM (SigLIP encoder + Qwen2 LLM) initialized from LLAVA-OV-7B and trained in two stages, each combining supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with direct preference optimization (DPO). The key design insight is using frame pairs as a stepping stone: Stage I trains on 2-frame pairs, while Stage II extends to full T-frame sequences via a two-frame sliding window for pseudo-labeling. Preference data for DPO is constructed automatically through two evaluation tasks—progression detection (an LLM checks whether caption pairs correctly reflect visible physical change between frames) and caption matching (a VLM performs a multiple-choice task matching each caption to its correct frame, with 'unsure' as a hard-negative option)—eliminating the need for human annotation. Pseudo-labels are generated by an ensemble of VLMs and filtered through these evaluators, with rejected captions serving as negative examples for preference learning.

## Results

On the FrameCapEval benchmark, ProgressCaptioner (7B) achieves caption matching (Cap) and progression detection (Prog) scores of 37.3/73.6 on HowToChange, 32.3/66.1 on COIN, and 31.3/63.7 on Penn Action & Kinetics, outperforming all open-source VLMs by 1.8×–2.7× and surpassing the much larger proprietary GPT-4o (32.4/64.2, 21.3/58.4, 18.2/63.2) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (31.4/63.8, 25.0/63.8, 17.6/60.3). In user studies, ProgressCaptioner achieves a 31.6% best-caption selection rate, 2×–3.6× higher than comparably sized open-source models. On NExT-QA (ATP-Hard) video QA, ProgressCaptioner+GPT-4o reaches 61.8% overall accuracy, outperforming VideoAgent (58.4%) by +3.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProgressCaptioner (7B) decisively outperforms all open-source VLMs on caption matching: it scores 37.3 on HTC vs. Qwen2-VL's 13.7, LLAVA-OV's 7.8, and Idefics2's 2.0, representing a 1.8×–2.7× gap. Against the proprietary GPT-4o, ProgressCaptioner wins on Cap across all three dataset splits (37.3 vs. 32.4, 32.3 vs. 21.3, 31.3 vs. 18.2) and on Prog for HTC and COIN (73.6 vs. 64.2, 66.1 vs. 58.4), though GPT-4o edges ahead on Penn&K Prog (63.2 vs. 63.7 is actually a narrow win for ours). Against Gemini-1.5-Pro, ProgressCaptioner leads on every metric across all splits. The one notable exception is Qwen2-VL's progression detection on COIN (70.8 vs. 66.1), where Qwen2-VL's conservative captions reduce hallucination at the cost of detail. On NExT-QA video QA, ProgressCaptioner+GPT-4o beats VideoAgent by +3.4% overall (61.8 vs. 58.4) and +6.6% on the causal subset (64.4 vs. 57.8), but trails slightly on the temporal subset (58.1 vs. 58.8). In user studies, ProgressCaptioner's 31.6% top-1 selection rate is 2×–3.6× that of LLAVA-OV and Qwen2-VL, and surpasses GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro on top-1 preference, though GPT-4o leads on the more forgiving top-2 metric (50.3% vs. 47.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a middle ground between image captioning (isolated per-frame descriptions) and video captioning (single narrative for the whole clip), framing progress-aware frame-level captioning as a distinct task that neither paradigm addresses. Methodologically, it builds on the LLAVA-OV-7B architecture (SigLIP + Qwen2) and adapts DPO preference learning, but departs from standard DPO by generating preference pairs automatically via two novel evaluation tasks (progression detection and caption matching) rather than relying on human annotations. The closest prior work is OSCaR for object state change captioning, but the authors distinguish their approach by operating on full action sequences (not just 3-frame OSC clips), using a scalable pseudo-labeling pipeline, and introducing automatic evaluation metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FrameCapEval benchmark (684 videos across four datasets) and the FrameCap training data provide the community with the first dedicated resources for evaluating temporally fine-grained captioning, a capability that underpins keyframe selection, video QA, and potential AR/VR or robotics applications. A key open question is how well the pseudo-labeling pipeline scales beyond the two source datasets (HowToChange, COIN) and whether the VLM-based evaluation metrics (themselves relying on Gemini-1.5-Pro and Llama-3.1-70B) introduce circularity that inflates apparent gains. The identification of 'temporal hallucination'—where models advance action stages based on statistical priors rather than visual evidence—opens a concrete failure mode for future work to target, but the 7B model size and single-benchmark evaluation leave questions about robustness across action domains and video lengths.
