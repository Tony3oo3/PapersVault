## Problem

Current VLMs are pre-trained on web-scale corpora (e.g., LAION-400M) that are heavily skewed toward concrete adjectives, while real-world fashion descriptions are naturally abstract-oriented with abstract adjectives as frequent as or more frequent than concrete ones. This mismatch causes VLMs to under-represent abstract language, leading to significantly degraded text-to-image retrieval when users query with abstract terms like 'sporty' or 'airy'. The paper further shows that abstract attributes are actually more discriminative than concrete ones for retrieval, making this gap both measurable and practically important.

## Input / Output

At inference, ACT takes a single abstract-oriented text query (e.g., 'a sporty top with a fresh collar') and produces a shifted text embedding in the VLM latent space, which is then matched against pre-computed image embeddings via cosine similarity to retrieve top-K images. The preparation phase consumes a paired Abstract-Concrete database built from an existing fashion dataset (DeepFashion or FACAD) where original seller descriptions serve as the abstract side and VLM-generated captions (Qwen2-VL) serve as the concrete side. No training data or gradient updates are required at inference time; only the pre-computed PCA projector W (600 components) and shift statistics (μ_Δ, σ_Δ) are needed.

## Method

ACT is a training-free, model-agnostic two-phase method. In the preparation phase, it constructs an Abstract-Concrete (A-C) database by pairing original abstract fashion descriptions with concrete captions generated by a frozen VLM (Qwen2-VL), then encodes both sides with a VLM text encoder and computes the element-wise difference Δ = H^C − H^A. PCA is applied to the standardized difference to extract the top k=600 principal components W, capturing the dominant directions along which abstract representations deviate from concrete ones. At inference, a frozen LLM (Llama-3.1-8B) first rephrases the abstract query toward a more concrete version, and the resulting text embedding is then augmented by projecting it onto the PCA subspace, rescaling with the A-C shift statistics, and adding the shift back to the original embedding. This two-step augmentation (language rewriting + representation shift) is plug-and-play across different VLM backbones (SigLIP, CLIP, O-CLIP, EVA-CLIP) and model sizes, consistently outperforming both zero-shot and fine-tuned baselines without any parameter updates.

## Results

On DeepFashion text-to-image retrieval, ACT (training-free) achieves H@1 of 43.7% and R@1 of 8.9% in the same-dataset setting, outperforming the best zero-shot model (SigLIP, H@1 31.1%) by +12.6% and the best fine-tuned model (SigLIP-ft-df, H@1 41.7%) by +2.0%. In the cross-dataset setting (FACAD→DeepFashion), ACT-facad maintains H@1 of 42.8%, surpassing the strongest fine-tuned baseline (SigLIP-ft-facad, H@1 35.2%) by +7.6%. ACT also consistently improves across all VLM backbones and scales, with an average +4.9% H@1 gain over zero-shot baselines in the model-scaling study (Table 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACT outperforms all six baseline families in both same-dataset and cross-dataset settings. Against the strongest zero-shot baseline SigLIP (H@1 31.1%), ACT-df gains +12.6% H@1; against the best fine-tuned model SigLIP-ft-df (H@1 41.7%), ACT still leads by +2.0% H@1 without any training. Fashion-specialized models F-CLIP (H@1 29.7% zero-shot, 36.3% fine-tuned) and OF-CLIP (H@1 26.8% zero-shot, 35.8% fine-tuned) are both surpassed by a wide margin. In the cross-dataset setting, ACT-facad (H@1 42.8%) beats the best fine-tuned model SigLIP-ft-facad (H@1 35.2%) by +7.6%, while fine-tuned baselines show a consistent 5–8% drop relative to their same-dataset counterparts, confirming that fine-tuning on limited fashion data harms generalization. The model-scaling study (Table 4) further shows ACT provides a consistent +4.9% average H@1 boost across SigLIP, O-CLIP, and EVA-CLIP at multiple model sizes (ViT-B through ViT-H/EVA-E), indicating the representation shift is a persistent issue regardless of model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of fashion-domain VLM retrieval (F-CLIP, OF-CLIP) and the psycholinguistic distinction between abstract and concrete language (Brysbaert et al. concreteness ratings). While prior fashion retrieval work has focused on fine-grained concrete attributes (colors, fabrics, silhouettes), this paper identifies and quantifies a previously overlooked failure mode: the systematic underrepresentation of abstract-oriented descriptions in web-scale pre-training corpora like LAION-400M. The proposed ACT method repurposes two existing tools—LLM-based text rephrasing and PCA-based dimensionality reduction—into a training-free, model-agnostic pipeline that shifts abstract representations toward the concrete manifold, departing from the standard fine-tuning paradigm used by F-CLIP and OF-CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that abstract adjectives rival or exceed concrete ones in real fashion e-commerce language, yet are poorly encoded by all major VLMs, reframes a practical bottleneck for fashion search and e-commerce applications. The training-free, plug-and-play nature of ACT makes it immediately deployable across VLM backbones without domain-specific fine-tuning, which is valuable given the data scarcity of abstract-oriented multimodal corpora. However, the analysis is limited to adjectives in a single domain (fashion), and the +2% H@1 gain over the best fine-tuned model, while consistent, is modest. Open questions include whether the representation-shift framework generalizes to other abstract-heavy domains (art, film criticism), to non-adjectival abstract expressions, and whether the PCA-based shift captures the full complexity of the abstract-concrete gap.
