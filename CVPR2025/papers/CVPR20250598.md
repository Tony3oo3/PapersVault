## Problem

Current text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion cannot handle fine-grained part-level attributes in prompts: adding part-specific details (e.g., 'pelican beak' to a flamingo prompt) either causes the model to ignore the instructions or generate an entirely different image. No existing training-free, zero-shot method provides controllable generation at the semantic part level, leaving a gap between object-level control and the part-level compositional workflows that artists actually use.

## Input / Output

The method takes a base text prompt, a user-selected object token, and a Rich-Text specification where each part is described with up to four attributes: a part description (footnote, e.g., 'a crown of a peacock'), an exact RGB color value, an artistic style (e.g., 'of Van Gogh'), and a relative font size controlling part prominence. It produces a final image where only the specified part regions are modified while the rest of the scene is preserved, along with intermediate part segmentation masks extracted at 32×32 resolution from attention maps and upscaled to full image size.

## Method

PartComposer is a training-free two-stage pipeline built entirely on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net. In the Part Localization stage, a parallel 'Part Diffusion' process denoises only the masked object region using part-conditioned prompts alongside the base prompt, forcing the part U-Net to encode spatial part locations; part masks are then extracted by combining spectral clustering of self-attention maps with a dot-product assignment protocol against cross-attention scores, gated by a max-value localization criterion to avoid spurious assignments. In the Part Generation stage, parallel region-specific diffusion processes run for each part (and background), with their noise predictions combined via masked summation at every timestep, gradient guidance enforces exact RGB color values, and Self-Injection from Plug-and-Play preserves the background structure from the base generation trajectory.

## Results

On the zero-shot unsupervised part segmentation task, PartComposer achieves FG-NMI of 24.7 and FG-ARI of 18.0 on DeepFashion, and FG-NMI of 20.5 and FG-ARI of 9.2 on CUB200, outperforming the StableDiffusion baseline (FG-NMI 12.0/8.0) and Rich-Text baseline (FG-NMI 16.0/3.1) by at least 5 points across most metrics. For part-based image generation, PartComposer achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.168) indicating the most localized modifications, the highest CLIP text-consistency score (0.201), and a competitive aesthetic score (5.66) comparable to the base StableDiffusion model (5.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartComposer significantly outperforms both zero-shot baselines in part segmentation: on DeepFashion it improves FG-NMI by 8.7 points over Rich-Text (24.7 vs. 16.0) and by 12.7 points over StableDiffusion (24.7 vs. 12.0); on CUB200 the gains are even larger (FG-NMI 20.5 vs. 3.1 for Rich-Text and 8.0 for StableDiffusion). Against the supervised/unsupervised learning baselines (SCOPS, Unsup-Parts, DFF), PartComposer remains competitive on CUB200 (FG-NMI 20.5 vs. 39.1 for SCOPS and 46.0 for Unsup-Parts) while operating in a strictly training-free zero-shot setting. For image generation, PartComposer achieves the best LPIPS score (0.168) indicating the most localized edits, beating InstructPix2Pix (0.189), Rich-Text (0.243), and StableDiffusion (0.467), while also achieving the highest CLIP text-consistency (0.201 vs. 0.183–0.193 for baselines). The aesthetic quality is preserved (5.66) and comparable to all baselines (5.63–5.68), confirming that part-level control does not degrade overall image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartComposer extends the controllable text-to-image generation line (ControlNet, GLIGEN, Attend-and-Excite) from object-level to part-level control, building directly on Rich-Text Generation [18] for the expressive attribute interface and region-diffusion composition, Multi-Diffusion [5] for fusing parallel diffusion paths, and Plug-and-Play [47] for background preservation via self-injection. The key conceptual departure is the novel Part-Diffusion localization process: by denoising only the object region with a part-conditioned U-Net in parallel with the base prompt, the model's cross-attention maps are coaxed into revealing part-level spatial structure, enabling zero-shot part mask extraction without any segmentation training. This contrasts with prior part discovery methods (SCOPS, Unsup-Parts) that require self-supervised training, and with prior controllable generation that operates exclusively at the object or instance granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PartComposer demonstrates that pre-trained diffusion models encode non-trivial part-level spatial information accessible through attention maps, opening a training-free pathway to fine-grained compositional control that is directly useful for creative workflows (e.g., swapping a bird's beak, restyling a character's clothing). The main limitation acknowledged by the authors is that part generation quality is bottlenecked by localization accuracy, and the zero-shot segmentation scores (FG-NMI ~25 on DeepFashion) remain well below supervised or self-supervised learning baselines (~45–68), suggesting the attention-based localization is still imprecise. Open questions include whether part-level understanding in diffusion models can be improved through architectural changes or better conditioning, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-part compositions beyond the 4-part evaluations shown.
