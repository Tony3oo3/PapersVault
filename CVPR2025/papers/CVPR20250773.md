## Problem

Existing PBR material generation methods either rely on 2D diffusion models that cannot comprehend global 3D geometry (causing texture-geometry misalignment and illumination-baked artifacts) or train 3D networks on sparse point clouds that produce blurry outputs. No prior work generates full PBR materials (albedo, roughness, metallic) directly in 3D space via a single feed-forward pass, leaving a gap between 3D global consistency and high-fidelity material synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250773/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untextured 3D mesh (100,000 surface points sampled to build a depth-8 octree at 256³ resolution) and an optional text prompt (encoded by CLIP) as input. It outputs PBR material maps (albedo, roughness, metallic) baked into the mesh's UV space at 1024² resolution via differentiable rendering (Nvdiffrec). The entire inference is a single feed-forward pass taking ~20 seconds on one A100 GPU (~1 s for Gaussian parameter prediction plus baking), compared to 20–48 minutes for SDS-based baselines.

## Method

TexGaussian places one 3D Gaussian at the center of each finest leaf node of an octree built from the mesh surface, extending the standard 3DGS parameterization with two additional channels for roughness and metallic (13 channels total: opacity, 3 scale, 4 rotation, 3 albedo, 1 roughness, 1 metallic). An octree-based 3D U-Net (O-CNN) with 5 down/up-sampling blocks predicts these parameters from geometry features (normal + local displacement) at each leaf node, with CLIP text features injected via multi-head cross-attention for text-conditioned generation. Training uses a regression objective (not diffusion denoising) combining 2D rasterized image losses (MSE + LPIPS on albedo, roughness, metallic, and alpha) with a 3D parameter MSE loss against pre-fitted Gaussians, supervised by 64 rendered views at 512². At inference, the predicted Gaussians are rasterized from multiple views and baked to UV space via Nvdiffrec optimization, yielding a relightable PBR material compatible with modern graphics engines.

## Results

On the Objaverse PBR material generation benchmark (200 test objects), TexGaussian achieves an albedo FID of 123.72 and KID of 0.20, and a PBR-rendering FID of 129.52 and KID of 0.02, outperforming the best baseline DreamMat (albedo FID 152.63, KID 1.09) by ~29 FID points and ~5× on KID. Inference takes only 21 seconds versus 20–48 minutes for all SDS-based baselines (~60× speedup). On ShapeNet unconditional texture generation, TexGaussian attains an average FID of 49.76 and KID of 2.07 across four categories, beating the closest 3D-diffusion baseline TexOct (FID 59.45, KID 2.60) while running in 11 s versus 17 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TexGaussian outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against the 3D-diffusion baselines on ShapeNet, it beats TexOct by 9.69 FID and 0.53 KID on average, and Point-UV (2-stage) by 11.73 FID and 0.60 KID, while also being 1.6× faster than TexOct and 4.5× faster than Point-UV. Against 2D-diffusion multi-view methods (TEXTure, Text2Tex, Paint3D), TexGaussian achieves substantially lower FID (100.97 vs. 135–170) and KID (1.88 vs. 4.7–7.9) and runs 12–90× faster. On the PBR material task, TexGaussian surpasses DreamMat (the strongest SDS-based baseline) by 28.9 FID on albedo and 16.0 FID on PBR rendering, with KID reduced from 1.09 to 0.20 (albedo) and 0.19 to 0.02 (PBR). The speed advantage is dramatic: 21 s versus 20–48 minutes for Fantasia3D, FlashTex, and DreamMat respectively. Make-it-Real, a multimodal-LLM-based method, is compared qualitatively only; TexGaussian produces spatially coherent PBR maps whereas Make-it-Real tends to predict single-value maps, yielding irregular rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

TexGaussian sits at the intersection of three prior threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting as a rendering representation, octree-based convolutional networks (O-CNN) as a sparse 3D backbone, and the LGM paradigm of treating a U-Net's per-voxel output as Gaussian parameters. It departs from the dominant 2D-diffusion-based texture synthesis line (TEXTure, Text2Tex, TexFusion, Paint3D) by operating entirely in 3D space, and from optimization-based PBR methods (DreamMat, Fantasia3D, FlashTex) by replacing iterative score distillation with a single feed-forward regression. The PBR channel extension follows GaussianShader, while the octree-aligned placement of Gaussians (rather than free-floating or full-voxel) is the paper's key architectural departure from both 3DGS and TexOct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60× speedup over SDS-based PBR methods (≈20 s vs. 20–48 min) makes high-quality, relightable material generation practical for interactive or batch 3D content pipelines, and the direct 3D-space formulation eliminates the multi-view inconsistency and Janus artifacts that plague 2D-diffusion approaches. The main open question is generalization: the authors acknowledge that performance on complex, out-of-distribution geometries is limited by the 29K-model training set, and scaling to Objaverse-XL-scale data with a larger model is the natural next step. Whether the regression paradigm can match the diversity ceiling of diffusion-based methods at scale remains to be seen.
