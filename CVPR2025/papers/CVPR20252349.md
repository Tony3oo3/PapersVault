## Problem

Foundation models adapted to semi-supervised learning exhibit class biases that produce imbalanced pseudo-labels, and existing logit-adjustment debiasing methods suffer from three gaps: they treat the estimated logit adjustment offset as equally reliable throughout training, they ignore linguistic knowledge from pre-trained language models that could capture model biases, and they discard low-confidence unlabeled samples rather than exploiting them.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled images (1–100 per class depending on dataset), a large pool of unlabeled images, and C class names formatted into M handcrafted prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a clean {class name}') that are fed through a frozen BERT-Large to produce per-class text embeddings. The vision backbone (ViT-VPT, EfficientNet-B2, or ViT-FFT) processes weakly and strongly augmented images, and the final output is a C-class probability vector used for classification. The text embeddings and image representations are aligned via a feature alignment loss on labeled data.

## Method

LADaS introduces two components. First, Language-Assisted Pseudo-Label Debiasing (LPLD) computes a self-adaptive logit adjustment offset that multiplies the class learning pace by a reliability score g_t (an EMA of mean max-confidence over unlabeled samples, starting near 1/C and growing with training), then further modulates it by a language-oriented preference derived from min-max-normalized language-guided class frequencies (EMA of how often unlabeled samples' image features are closest to each PLM-derived class center under cosine similarity). The product of these two terms replaces the raw logit adjustment in the consistency regularization loss. Second, Language-Aware Pseudo-Label Smoothing (LPLS) rescues low-confidence samples (max probability below threshold τ) by constructing a soft pseudo-label where the predicted class receives probability 1−ε and the remaining ε is distributed across other classes proportionally to their cosine similarity scores with the sample's image representation, then supervises the strongly-augmented prediction with a cross-entropy loss against this refined label.

## Results

Using the ViT-VPT backbone, LADaS achieves the lowest error rate across all settings in Table 1: 10.85% on FOOD-101 (N2), 3.15% on CIFAR-10 (N1), and 18.75% on CIFAR-100 (N4), outperforming the closest competitor FINESSL by 2.11, 0.70, and 0.81 percentage points respectively. With the ViT-FFT backbone (Table 4), LADaS reaches 1.17% error on CIFAR-10 (N4) and 15.04% on CIFAR-100 (N4), surpassing InterLUDE+ by 0.38 and 1.28 points. On CIFAR-100 with EfficientNet-B2 (Table 3), LADaS achieves 41.27% error at N4, beating BorLan (44.82%) by 3.55 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LADaS achieves the best error rate in all 9 settings of Table 1 (ViT-VPT), consistently outperforming the closest debiasing-based competitors FINESSL and DebiasPL by 0.5–2.1 points, and surpassing classical SSL methods (FixMatch, FlexMatch, FreeMatch, SoftMatch) by 5–36 points depending on the setting. The gap is most pronounced in extreme low-data regimes: on CIFAR-10 N1, LADaS (3.15%) beats FINESSL (3.85%) by 0.70 points while DebiasPL (21.7%) and FixMatch (39.73%) are far behind. On CIFAR-100 N4, LADaS (18.75%) leads FINESSL (19.56%) and DebiasPL (20.43%) by 0.81 and 1.68 points respectively. With the ViT-FFT backbone (Table 4), LADaS wins on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 across all settings but is slightly outperformed by InterLUDE on STL-10 at N4 (3.91% vs 3.14%) and N10 (2.69% vs 2.66%). On CIFAR-100 with EfficientNet-B2 (Table 3), LADaS (41.27% at N4) beats BorLan (44.82%) and Self-Tuning (47.17%), confirming the benefit of combining linguistic knowledge with debiasing over either alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logit-adjustment-based debiasing line initiated by DebiasPL and FINESSL, which introduced class-frequency-based offsets into the consistency regularization loss to counteract foundation model biases in SSL. It departs from these methods in two ways: (1) it modulates the logit adjustment with a dynamically updated reliability score (inspired by FreeMatch's EMA-based confidence tracking) to avoid injecting noisy offsets during early training, and (2) it incorporates linguistic knowledge from a frozen PLM (following BorLan's paradigm) to estimate model bias via language-guided class frequencies and to construct soft pseudo-labels for low-confidence samples. The overall pipeline remains rooted in the FixMatch pseudo-labeling + consistency regularization framework, with the addition of a feature alignment loss borrowed from BorLan.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly relevant problem: foundation models carry class-frequency biases from pre-training that degrade SSL performance, and prior debiasing methods either ignore the reliability of their own estimates or discard low-confidence unlabeled samples entirely. The linguistic-knowledge-guided smoothing for low-confidence samples is a reasonable way to recover data utilization without introducing noise, and the reliability score is a simple but effective safeguard. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, STL-10, FOOD-101, Semi-Aves) and a frozen BERT-Large, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger benchmarks or benefits from a more powerful PLM. The added computational overhead of maintaining text embeddings and computing cosine similarities per batch is not thoroughly analyzed.
