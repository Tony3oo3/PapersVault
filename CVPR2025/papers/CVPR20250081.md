## Problem

Existing shape correspondence methods either rely on small category-specific datasets with lightweight descriptor-based architectures or use large-scale deformation-based models that struggle to generalize across diverse shape types. There is no unified approach that leverages the compact spectral representation of functional maps while benefiting from the strong generalization of large-scale generative models trained on diverse synthetic data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250081/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D triangular meshes and produces a point-wise correspondence map, but internally operates on template-wise functional maps (n×n matrices, n ∈ {32, 64, 96}) that are composed pairwise via matrix multiplication. The diffusion model is conditioned on a vertex-count-independent matrix formed by projecting learned per-vertex features onto sign-corrected Laplacian eigenvectors, which has the same dimensions as the functional map itself. At inference, multiple denoised samples are generated and the best is selected by Dirichlet energy of the resulting point-wise map before ZoomOut spectral upsampling and nearest-neighbor conversion.

## Method

DenoisFM uses a 2D U-Net as a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) to directly predict functional maps, treating each n×n map as a single-channel image. Two key design choices reduce complexity: (1) maps are defined relative to a single template shape rather than all shape pairs, reducing training data from m² to m functional maps; (2) an unsupervised sign-correction network (a DiffusionNet-based per-vertex feature extractor) resolves the 2ⁿ sign ambiguity of Laplacian eigenvectors by projecting learned features onto eigenvectors and enforcing positive projections, trained via a self-consistency objective that requires two random-sign eigendecompositions of the same mesh to yield identical corrected bases. The model is trained on 230,000 remeshed SURREAL human meshes with anisotropic connectivity augmentation, and at inference the probabilistic nature of DDPMs is exploited by generating multiple candidate maps and selecting the smoothest via Dirichlet energy.

## Results

On the remeshed FAUST, SCAPE, and SHREC'19 benchmarks, DenoisFM (64×64) achieves mean geodesic errors of 1.8, 2.3, and 3.5 (×100), while the 96×96 variant reaches 1.7, 2.1, and 3.9. The 64×64 model attains the best SHREC'19 score (3.5) and the 96×64 model the best SCAPE score (2.1), both outperforming all descriptor-based and large-scale baselines on those datasets. On FAUST, the method is competitive (1.7–1.8) but slightly behind the best descriptor-based result (SmS at 1.4). On anisotropically remeshed FAUST/SCAPE (Table 2), DenoisFM-96×96 achieves 2.0/2.2, outperforming all baselines and showing robustness to connectivity changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against large-scale template-based methods (3D-CODED, TransMatch), DenoisFM dramatically outperforms on SCAPE (2.1–2.3 vs. 15.3–16.1) and SHREC'19 (3.5–3.9 vs. 17.3–21.0), while matching TransMatch on FAUST (1.7 vs. 1.7). Among descriptor-based methods, DenoisFM-64×64 achieves the best SHREC'19 score (3.5) by a clear margin over ConsistentFMaps (3.8) and SSL (4.0), and DenoisFM-96×96 wins on SCAPE (2.1 vs. ULRSSM's 2.2). On FAUST, the method is competitive but trails SmS (1.4) and ULRSSM (1.6) by 0.1–0.3. On anisotropic connectivity (Table 2), DenoisFM-96×96 (2.0/2.2) outperforms all baselines, with the closest competitor ULRSSM at 1.9/2.4. In the zero-shot DT4D setting (Table 3), DenoisFM achieves the best intra-class scores for most humanoid categories (e.g., 1.0 for mannequin, 1.1 for zlorp/crypto/prisoner) and competitive inter-class results, though it underperforms on a few categories (ortiz 9.3, mousey 10.2, skeletonz 16.3) where descriptor-based methods excel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: the functional maps framework for shape correspondence (Ovsjanikov et al., 2012) and denoising diffusion probabilistic models (Ho et al., 2020). Prior functional map methods relied on small category-specific datasets and lightweight descriptor networks, while large-scale template-based approaches (3D-CODED, TransMatch) learned deformations directly from SURREAL. DenoisFM departs from both by using a DDPM to directly predict the functional map matrix, treating it as a small single-channel image amenable to a standard 2D U-Net, and by introducing an unsupervised sign-correction network to resolve Laplacian eigenvector ambiguity — a problem previously handled only in graph-learning contexts or via sign-equivariant pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a unified model that generalizes zero-shot to non-isometric humanoid shapes (DT4D) and animal shapes (SMAL) without category-specific retraining, bridging the gap between data-hungry large-scale methods and small-data descriptor methods. However, inference remains slow (up to 27 minutes per shape at 96×96 resolution), and the template-wise formulation, while reducing training complexity, may limit expressiveness for highly non-rigid or topologically diverse shape pairs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly heterogeneous shape categories (e.g., objects, hands, faces) and whether the probabilistic sampling + Dirichlet-energy selection can be made more efficient.
