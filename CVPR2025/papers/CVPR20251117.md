## Problem

Standard deep learning training assumes IID batches, but learning from a continuous video stream in temporal order produces highly correlated consecutive gradients that cause severe performance degradation or outright model collapse. This gap between how video is naturally consumed (sequentially) and how models are trained (shuffled clips) limits the development of streaming agents that learn on-the-fly without storing or shuffling data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips in strict temporal order without shuffling, using short temporal windows: 8 consecutive frames at 1 fps for DoRA, 16 frames at 224×224 for VideoMAE, and 4 frames for future prediction. Two batching strategies are explored for multi-video datasets: 'batch-along-time' (clips from one video per batch) and 'batch-along-video' (one clip per video per batch). Outputs are either frozen visual representations evaluated via linear/attn-probe on downstream tasks, or predicted future pixel frames at a 0.64 s displacement.

## Method

The core contribution is a geometric modification to standard optimizers (SGD, AdamW) that replaces the raw gradient g_t with its orthogonal component u_t = g_t − proj_{c_{t−1}}(g_t), where c_{t−1} is an exponential moving average (β=0.9) of past raw gradients. This projection removes the component of the current gradient that is redundant with recent history, leaving only the novel signal for the parameter update. The orthogonal gradient is then fed into the optimizer's first/second moment estimates as usual. The design is self-adaptive: under near-IID conditions (cos ≈ 0) the orthogonal gradient approximates the original, preserving standard training behavior, while under high sequential correlation (cos → 1) it shrinks to a small residual, preventing over-optimization along a single direction. The modification is optimizer-agnostic and requires only a cosine-distance and vector-norm computation per step.

## Results

On DoRA pretraining from a single 1h50m video (WT_venice), Orthogonal-AdamW with DINO initialization achieves 64.5% ImageNet linear-probe top-1, compared to 6.1% for standard AdamW which effectively fails to train. On VideoMAE pretraining over SSV2 with sequential batch-along-time loading, Orthogonal-AdamW reaches 18.4% linear-probe and 48.0% attn-probe top-1 versus 16.4% and 46.1% for AdamW. On future frame prediction (0.64s displacement) over Ego4D-Stream, Orthogonal-AdamW achieves 0.031 pixel MSE / 16.4 PSNR in-stream, outperforming AdamW (0.034 / 15.9) and the reproduced BabyLearning baseline (0.032 / 15.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard AdamW (the primary optimizer baseline), Orthogonal-AdamW wins decisively on all three scenarios: it rescues DoRA training from near-total collapse (6.1% → 64.5% linear probe with DINO init), improves VideoMAE sequential pretraining by +2.0 pts linear-probe and +1.9 pts attn-probe on SSV2, and reduces future-prediction pixel MSE on Ego4D-Stream from 0.034 to 0.031 in-stream. Against the official VideoMAE checkpoints (Tong et al., NeurIPS 2022), the sequential Orthogonal-AdamW still trails the shuffled IID baseline (18.4% vs 23.2% linear-probe on SSV2), but the gap narrows substantially compared to sequential AdamW (16.4%), and Orthogonal-AdamW even slightly exceeds shuffled AdamW (21.0% vs 19.0%), suggesting residual inter-batch correlation benefits from decorrelation. Against BabyLearning (Carreira et al., CVPR 2024) on future prediction, Orthogonal-AdamW outperforms both the originally reported results (0.059 MSE in-stream on Ego4D) and the authors' own reproduction (0.032 MSE), achieving 0.031 MSE / 16.4 PSNR in-stream and 0.023 / 17.6 out-of-stream, indicating the orthogonal gradient update also benefits test-time adaptation beyond representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the orthogonal gradient projection introduced by Farajtabar et al. [14] for continual image classification to the fundamentally different setting of sequential video learning, where the 'tasks' are not discrete but form a continuous temporal stream. It departs from the Baby Learning framework [7], which demonstrated the performance drop from shuffled to sequential video training but did not address the underlying gradient correlation, and from DoRA [43], which learned from a single video but required random access and shuffling. Rather than using replay buffers, architectural modifications, or data filtering to mitigate temporal redundancy, the paper modifies the optimizer itself with a geometric projection, making the fix a drop-in replacement applicable to any standard optimizer (SGD, AdamW).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orthogonal optimizer offers a simple, computationally cheap mechanism to enable self-supervised video learning from continuous streams without storing or shuffling entire datasets, which has implications for privacy-preserving and on-the-fly learning agents. However, the practical gains on standard multi-video benchmarks (VideoMAE on SSV2/K400) are modest (1–2% on attn-probe), and the dramatic recovery on DoRA (6.1% → 64.5% linear probe) is demonstrated on a single 110-minute video with a single training run, limiting generalizability claims. Open questions include whether projecting against a richer history of past gradients (beyond a single EMA) would further improve decorrelation, how the method interacts with other regularization or data-selection strategies, and whether the approach scales to truly massive streaming corpora where the single-EMA approximation may become insufficient.
