## Problem

Direct imaging exoplanet detection is limited by structured speckle noise that closely mimics point-source signals, and existing methods either lack statistically rigorous detection scores, fail to capture spatial and spectral correlations in the nuisance, or suffer from self-subtraction that degrades sensitivity near the host star. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a multi-scale statistical model of speckles that leverages rotational symmetries and joint spectral structure, integrated into an end-to-end learnable framework that provides interpretable, uncertainty-quantified detection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4-D ASDI observations (C=2 spectral channels × T temporal frames × 1024×1024 pixels, analyzed in a 256×256 star-centered region) along with the measured off-axis PSF, parallactic angles, and channel wavelengths. It produces a calibrated detection score map where each pixel value follows N(0,1) under the null hypothesis (no planet), enabling direct mapping to false-alarm probability, plus per-pixel Gaussian flux estimates N(α̃, σ̃) and sub-pixel positions obtained via iterative Newton-Raphson optimization of the joint likelihood.

## Method

The core model represents local speckle patches as multivariate Gaussians in a learned lower-dimensional feature space (via projection matrix A), extended across multiple spatial scales (8×8 to 64×64) and N-fold rotational symmetries (N=1,2,4) to mitigate self-subtraction. In ASDI mode, patches from all spectral channels are jointly modeled after homothety alignment, reducing ambiguity between speckles and exoplanet signals. Detection is computed via a generalized likelihood ratio test yielding a statistically controlled score, which is then denoised by a UNet (f_ν) trained end-to-end with a negative log-likelihood loss on synthetic exoplanet injections. The final output combines the statistical model's uncertainty (σ̂) with the network's refined flux estimate, and multiple trained models are ensembled by averaging precision-weighted terms for improved robustness.

## Results

In ASDI mode, the proposed method achieves an average AUC of 0.761±0.004 across five test datasets, outperforming PACO (0.693±0.005) by 6.8 percentage points and KLIP/PCA (0.515±0.005) by 24.6 points. In ADI mode, it matches MODEL&CO (0.645±0.005) while surpassing PACO (0.520±0.006) by 12.5 points. For flux estimation, the proposed method reduces the absolute relative error from 0.56 (PACO) to 0.51 and the sub-pixel position RMSE from 0.21 to 0.11 pixels. On real HR 8799 data, the method detects all three known exoplanets with no false alarms across three observations spanning 2016–2021.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In ADI mode, the proposed method ties MODEL&CO (0.645 vs. 0.645 average AUC) but wins on three of five individual datasets (HD 159911, HD 216803, HD 206860), while MODEL&CO edges ahead on the two lowest-parallactic-rotation datasets (HD 188228, HD 102647). Both substantially outperform PACO (0.520) and KLIP/PCA (0.478), with cADI (0.369) being the weakest baseline. In ASDI mode, where MODEL&CO is unavailable, the proposed method clearly leads: it beats PACO by 6.8 AUC points on average (0.761 vs. 0.693) and by a wide margin on the most challenging low-rotation dataset HD 102647 (0.744 vs. 0.589). For flux and position estimation (Table B), the proposed method improves on PACO in both metrics: ARE drops from 0.56 to 0.51 and position RMSE from 0.21 to 0.11 pixels, indicating roughly 48% reduction in localization error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PACO family of patch-based Gaussian statistical models for speckle nuisance (Flasseur et al. 2018, 2020) by introducing multi-scale patch collections, N-fold rotational symmetry mixtures, and joint multi-spectral homothety-based distributions, all projected into a learnable lower-dimensional feature space. It also builds on the observation-independent learning paradigm of MODEL&CO (Bodrito et al. 2024), which first combined a statistically-modeled nuisance with a deep denoiser, but departs from it by grounding the statistical model in physical symmetries (rotational invariance of the speckle pattern, spectral homothety) rather than purely data-driven nuisance learning. The end-to-end trainable architecture with a UNet denoiser on the GLRT detection map follows the hybrid statistical+deep-learning template established by Deep PACO and MODEL&CO, but the multi-scale, symmetry-aware covariance structure is the key methodological departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach provides statistically calibrated detection scores with native uncertainty quantification (via GLRT and Cholesky-based precision matrices), which is essential for credible astrophysical claims and distinguishes it from purely heuristic or black-box detection pipelines. The substantial ASDI gains—particularly on low-parallactic-rotation datasets (2°) where prior methods degrade severely—suggest the method could unlock otherwise unusable observations in large surveys like SHINE. Open questions include whether the framework scales to higher spectral resolution (beyond the two-channel SPHERE ASDI data used here), whether the rotational symmetry assumption holds for non-circular pupils or extreme adaptive optics residuals, and how the model performs on extended sources like circumstellar disks, which the authors note as a future direction.
