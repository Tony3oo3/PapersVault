## Problem

Existing polarized image demosaicing (PID) methods cannot enhance resolution, while polarized image super-resolution (PISR) methods assume artifact-free inputs and thus retain or amplify demosaicing errors in DoP and AoP. The straightforward sequential PID→PISR pipeline accumulates errors across stages, producing degraded polarization parameters. No joint framework exists to directly obtain high-quality HR polarized images with accurate DoP and AoP from a single CPFA raw image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251651/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single CPFA raw image (one channel, h×w) as input and simultaneously outputs both demosaiced full-color polarized images (four 3-channel images at h×w) and HR polarized images (four 3-channel images at kh×kw), where the SR scale k=2^n is controlled by the number of recurrent iterations n. Unlike existing PISR methods that require pre-demosaiced images as input, PIDSR consumes the raw CPFA directly, and the DoP/AoP maps are derived from the Stokes parameters computed on the outputs rather than being a separate prediction head.

## Method

PIDSR formulates complementary demosaicing and SR as a two-stage recurrent pipeline: a spatial-physical coherence reconstructor (f) handles intra-resolution reconstruction (alleviating spatial discontinuities and restoring physical correlation among polarized images), while a polarization-aware resolution enhancer (g) handles cross-resolution reconstruction (SR with polarization preservation). The network backbone is a modified U-Net where convolution blocks are replaced by Stokes Feature Injection (SFI) blocks containing MDTA and GDFN modules (from Restormer) to explicitly inject Stokes-domain physical clues and bridge the domain gap between low-frequency image features and high-frequency Stokes features. The recurrent structure iterates f and g n times, with each round doubling the resolution, avoiding the error accumulation of a naive sequential cascade. Training uses a composite loss combining image-domain L1 and gradient losses, Stokes-domain continuity losses, and polarization losses on DoP and AoP, with the physical constraint I_α1+I_α3=I_α2+I_α4 enforced explicitly.

## Results

On synthetic data, PIDSR achieves the best demosaicing performance with S0 PSNR of 40.24 dB (SSIM 0.9780), DoP PSNR of 33.33 dB (SSIM 0.8447), and AoP MAE of 12.24°, outperforming the strongest PID baseline TCPDNet by 1.59 dB on S0 and 0.95 dB on DoP. For 2× super-resolution, PIDSR reaches S0^HR PSNR of 37.44 dB and AoP MAE of 12.35°, surpassing PSRNet (36.46 dB, 13.19°) and CPSRNet (33.60 dB, 15.58°). At 4× SR, PIDSR maintains a clear lead with S0^HR PSNR of 36.31 dB and AoP MAE of 13.05°, while CPSRNet degrades substantially to 31.16 dB and 16.55°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIDSR outperforms all five baselines across every metric in both demosaicing and SR tasks. In demosaicing, it beats the strongest PID method TCPDNet [23] by 1.59 dB on S0 PSNR, 1.07 dB on DoP PSNR, and 0.95° on AoP MAE, with larger margins over IGRI2 [22] (~4.7 dB on S0) and Polanalyser [20] (~7.0 dB on S0). For 2× SR, PIDSR surpasses PSRNet [8] by 0.98 dB on S0^HR and 0.84° on AoP MAE, and dominates CPSRNet [37] by 3.84 dB on S0^HR and 3.23° on AoP MAE. The gap widens at 4× SR, where CPSRNet degrades severely (S0^HR drops to 31.16 dB, AoP MAE rises to 16.55°) while PIDSR retains 36.31 dB and 13.05°, indicating that CPSRNet's reliance on clean demosaicing inputs makes it particularly vulnerable to demosaicing artifacts. Notably, PIDSR's advantage is most pronounced on polarization-specific metrics (DoP, AoP) rather than raw intensity (S0), confirming that the complementary design specifically targets the nonlinear error amplification in polarization parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate task families—polarized image demosaicing (PID) and polarized image super-resolution (PISR)—into a single joint framework, departing from the standard sequential pipeline (PID → PISR) that prior work assumed. Conceptually, it builds on the observation that CPFA raw images can be approximately decomposed into half-resolution full-color polarized images, enabling a recurrent two-stage pipeline that alternates intra-resolution coherence reconstruction (drawing on Restormer-style MDTA/GDFN modules and a U-Net backbone) with cross-resolution enhancement. The Stokes-parameter-guided loss and feature injection extend the physics-aware losses used in PSRNet and CPSRNet but apply them at both resolution levels within a single recurrent loop rather than as a post-hoc refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Polarization cameras are increasingly used in reflection removal, shape-from-polarization, and dehazing, yet their CPFA raw output has historically required a lossy demosaicing step before any downstream processing; PIDSR shows that jointly handling demosaicing and SR from the raw image yields measurably more accurate DoP and AoP, which directly benefits these applications. The paper's limitation to single-frame processing and its inability to handle standard CFA raw images (which lack Stokes information) leave open the question of whether the recurrent complementary formulation extends to video or to sensors without explicit polarization channels. A broader open question is whether the complementarity between resolution and artifact severity generalizes to other multi-channel imaging modalities (e.g., spectral or thermal mosaics).
