## Problem

Single-source domain generalization methods rely heavily on data augmentations to synthesize domain diversity, but they fail under semantic shifts (background, viewpoint, modality changes) because they learn global class features rather than local, domain-invariant concepts. Additionally, no existing DG dataset provides per-class concept annotations with spatial localization, making it impossible to explicitly train models to attend to the stable features that define each class.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252783/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a class label, per-concept GradCAM saliency maps, and (at test time) a potentially corrected class prediction. Concept-level ground-truth saliency maps are generated offline via a pipeline that uses GPT-3.5 to identify key concepts per class, Stable Diffusion v2.1 to synthesize exemplar images and extract cross-attention maps, and DIFT to transfer these maps onto real training images across domains. The ResNet-18 backbone is trained on one source domain with these pre-computed GT-maps and evaluated on three unseen target domains.

## Method

TIDE introduces a two-part training scheme built on a ResNet-18 backbone: a concept saliency alignment (CSA) loss that minimizes L2 distance between predicted concept GradCAM maps and ground-truth concept maps, forcing spatial attention to the correct regions; and a local concept contrastive (LCC) loss that applies a triplet objective on concept-specific feature vectors (computed by element-wise multiplication of GT-maps with backbone features) to cluster same-concept features across augmentations while separating different concepts. At test time, an iterative correction algorithm compares extracted concept features against stored prototypical concept signatures; when misalignment exceeds a threshold, the model progressively masks regions corresponding to the current (incorrect) class prediction and re-predicts until concept features align with their signatures or a maximum iteration count is reached. The annotation pipeline itself is a key contribution, automating concept discovery and spatial grounding without manual labeling.

## Results

TIDE achieves average SSDG classification accuracy of 80.02% on PACS, 77.08% on VLCS, 74.01% on Office-Home, and 82.14% on DomainNet (all with ResNet-18 backbone). This represents an average improvement of 12% over the strongest prior method across the four benchmarks, with the largest gains on VLCS (+13.37% over DSU-MAD) and Office-Home (+16.16% over NJPP). The ablation shows that the Local Concept Contrastive loss (LCC) contributes the largest single jump in accuracy (from 49.93% to 73.67% on PACS), while test-time correction adds a further ~6.35% on top.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIDE outperforms all 21 compared baselines across every dataset and every source-domain split. On PACS, TIDE (80.02%) surpasses the prior best PromptD (71.87%) by 8.15 points and Meta-Casual (69.86%) by 10.16 points; augmentation-heavy methods like AugMix (57.12%) and MixStyle (52.00%) lag further behind. On VLCS, where shifts are driven by background and viewpoint rather than style, TIDE (77.08%) leads DSU-MAD (63.71%) by 13.37 points, and the gap widens to 25.34 points when Caltech101 is the source. On Office-Home (65 classes), TIDE (74.01%) beats NJPP (57.85%) by 16.16 points, the largest margin in the paper. On DomainNet, TIDE (82.14%) exceeds NJPP (73.30%) by 8.84 points. Notably, even before test-time correction, TIDE's training-only accuracy (73.67% on PACS) already exceeds all prior methods, indicating that the interpretability-oriented losses (CSA + LCC) are the primary drivers of the gain rather than the correction step alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIDE departs from the dominant augmentation-based paradigm in single-source domain generalization (SSDG), where methods like ABA, MixStyle, and NJPP synthesize diverse domains through data transformations. Instead, it draws on the concept bottleneck model (CBM) literature for learning interpretable local concepts, but critically extends CBMs by grounding each concept to spatial image regions via saliency maps—a capability prior CBM variants lack. The annotation pipeline repurposes diffusion models (Stable Diffusion cross-attention) and LLMs (GPT-3.5) not for augmentation but for generating concept-level ground-truth saliency maps, and leverages DIFT [47] to transfer these maps from synthetic exemplars to real images across domains. The test-time correction mechanism, which iteratively masks misaligned concept regions using stored prototypical signatures, has no direct precedent in the SSDG literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12% average improvement over SOTA across four standard benchmarks, combined with visually interpretable concept saliency maps, addresses two long-standing pain points in DG: robustness to semantic shifts (background, viewpoint) and model explainability. The test-time correction strategy opens a practical avenue for deploying DG models in safety-critical settings where a verification-and-refine loop is acceptable. Limitations include reliance on a fixed ResNet-18 backbone, the annotation pipeline's dependence on GPT-3.5's concept identification quality, and the added inference cost of iterative correction. Open questions raised by the authors include extending TIDE to fine-grained classification and generating calibrated confidence scores grounded in the concept verification mechanism.
