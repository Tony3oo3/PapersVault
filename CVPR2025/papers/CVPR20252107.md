## Problem

Existing bi-encoder text-to-video retrieval methods either collapse video content into a single vector (losing fine-grained detail) or apply tokenwise interaction to only one level of visual representation. Prior tokenwise approaches for video retrieval employ overly complicated interaction mechanisms or operate solely on temporally modeled features, failing to exploit the complementary information available in both static spatial and spatio-temporal representations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252107/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query (tokenized and padded to 32 tokens for short-caption datasets or 64 tokens for paragraph-level datasets) and a video clip (12 uniformly sampled 224×224 frames for MSR-VTT/MSVD/VATEX, 64 frames for DiDeMo/ActivityNet). Each frame is independently encoded by a ViT image encoder to produce a [CLS] token, which is then processed by a 4-layer temporal transformer to yield temporally contextualized video features. The output is a scalar relevance score per query-video pair, computed as the sum of two MeanMaxSim terms (frame-level and video-level), which is used to rank the full video collection.

## Method

Video-ColBERT is a multi-vector bi-encoder that performs one-directional tokenwise late interaction at two complementary levels: MMS_F matches each contextualized query token against static frame [CLS] features, while MMS_V matches the same query tokens against temporally contextualized video features produced by a 4-layer transformer. The MeanMaxSim operator replaces ColBERT's summation with a mean over query tokens to handle variable-length queries and control score magnitude. Training uses a dual sigmoid loss that computes separate binary-classification objectives on the MMS_F and MMS_V similarity matrices (weighted by λ_F and λ_V), encouraging strong yet compatible independent representations at each level. Soft query augmentation via padding tokens and two visual expansion tokens fed into the temporal transformer further enrich the interaction. The entire system is initialized from CLIP or SigLIP ViT-B/16 or B/32 and fine-tuned with differential learning rates (1e-7 for pre-trained encoders, 1e-4 for the temporal transformer).

## Results

On sentence-to-video retrieval, Video-COLBERT with SigLIP-B/16 backbone achieves R@1 of 51.5 on MSR-VTT, 55.2 on MSVD, and 68.0 on VATEX, setting new state-of-the-art among bi-encoder methods of comparable size. With CLIP-B/16, it reaches R@1 of 51.0 (MSR-VTT), 50.2 (MSVD), and 66.8 (VATEX), outperforming the strongest tokenwise-interaction baseline DRL by 0.8–1.1 R@1 points. On paragraph-to-video retrieval (DiDeMo), Video-COLBERT (CLIP-B/16) achieves 51.9 R@1, surpassing DRL (49.0) and X-CLIP (47.8) by 2.9 and 4.1 points respectively. The method also reports nDCG@10, reaching 0.833 on VATEX and 0.724 on MSVD with SigLIP-B/16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among tokenwise-interaction bi-encoder baselines, Video-COLBERT (SigLIP-B/16) outperforms DRL [50] by 1.3 R@1 on MSR-VTT (51.5 vs 50.2), 5.2 R@1 on MSVD (55.2 vs 50.0), and 2.3 R@1 on VATEX (68.0 vs 65.7). It also beats X-CLIP [36] by 2.2 R@1 on MSR-VTT (51.5 vs 49.3) and 4.8 R@1 on MSVD (55.2 vs 50.4). Against the upgraded SigLIP4CLIP-seqTransf baseline, Video-COLBERT (SigLIP-B/16) gains 5.8 R@1 on MSR-VTT and 7.8 R@1 on MSVD, confirming that the two-level MMS interaction—not just the backbone—drives the improvement. On DiDeMo (paragraph retrieval), Video-COLBERT (CLIP-B/16) leads DRL by 2.9 R@1 (51.9 vs 49.0) and X-CLIP by 4.1 R@1 (51.9 vs 47.8). The only method that surpasses Video-COLBERT across all datasets is InternVideo2-6B [54], a 6-billion-parameter model that is roughly 15× larger, confirming Video-COLBERT's efficiency advantage at comparable model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

Video-ColBERT adapts ColBERT's contextualized late-interaction paradigm (token-wise MaxSim) from text-document retrieval to the video domain, building on CLIP/SigLIP dual encoders as the backbone. It departs from prior token-wise video retrieval methods (X-CLIP, DRL, UATVR) by performing interaction simultaneously on both static frame features and temporally contextualized video features via a dual MeanMaxSim (MMS) operation, rather than relying on a single representation level. The method also introduces the first sigmoid-based contrastive loss in T2VR, drawing on SigLIP's demonstration that sigmoid losses outperform softmax InfoNCE in image-text pre-training, and extends ColBERT's soft query augmentation (padding-token participation) to the video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video-ColBERT provides a clean, efficient bi-encoder recipe for T2VR that achieves competitive or state-of-the-art results at the ViT-B/16 scale without requiring cross-encoders or large-scale video pre-training, making it practical for real-world deployment. The finding that simple summation of frame-level and video-level MMS scores outperforms reciprocal rank fusion (RRF) is a useful negative result suggesting that score magnitudes carry meaningful information that rank-based fusion discards, pointing toward future work on better score alignment. Limitations include continued underperformance relative to large foundation models (e.g., InternVideo2-6B) and the SigLIP variant's degradation on long-caption datasets like ActivityNet, highlighting that the approach's effectiveness is sensitive to backbone pre-training characteristics and query length.
