## Problem

State-of-the-art ViT-based segmentation models rely on multiple task-specific components (ViT-Adapter, pixel decoder, Transformer decoder) to achieve strong performance, but it is unclear whether these components are truly necessary or whether their inductive biases can be absorbed by the ViT itself given sufficient scale and pre-training. This paper investigates whether removing all such components degrades segmentation accuracy meaningfully, and finds that with large DINOv2-pretrained ViTs, the performance gap shrinks to under 1 PQ while inference speed improves by over 4×.

## Input / Output

EoMT takes a single RGB image (typically 640×640 or 1280×1280) and processes it through a plain ViT with 16×16 patches, producing a single-scale feature map at 1/16 resolution. K learnable queries are concatenated to the patch tokens and jointly processed by the final L2 ViT blocks; each query outputs a class logit vector and a mask logit map at 1/4 resolution (obtained via dot product between a mask embedding and the upscaled ViT features). No multi-scale feature pyramid, no separate decoder, and no masked attention are required at inference time.

## Method

EoMT repurposes the plain ViT encoder for segmentation by concatenating K learnable object queries to the patch tokens after the first L1 encoder blocks, so that the remaining L2 blocks perform both query-to-patch information transfer and query-to-query coordination in a single self-attention operation, eliminating the need for a separate Transformer decoder. A lightweight mask module (linear layer for class logits, three-layer MLP followed by dot product with upscaled features for mask logits) is applied to each query. The key training innovation is mask annealing: during early training, intermediate mask predictions are used to mask the query-to-patch portion of self-attention (mimicking M2F's masked cross-attention), but the masking probability is polynomially decayed to zero over training, enabling fully unmasked and highly efficient inference. The paper validates this design through a stepwise ablation removing the ViT-Adapter, pixel decoder, multi-scale features, and Transformer decoder, showing that each removal costs <0.5 PQ with DINOv2-L while substantially increasing FPS, and that the gap to the full ViT-Adapter+M2F baseline narrows from 5.8 PQ (ViT-S) to 0.7 PQ (ViT-g) as model size scales.

## Results

On COCO val2017 panoptic segmentation, EoMT with DINOv2-pretrained ViT-L at 640² input achieves PQ 56.0 at 128 FPS, compared to ViT-Adapter + Mask2Former (same backbone) at PQ 57.1 and 29 FPS — a 4.4× speedup for only 1.1 PQ loss. At 1280² input, EoMT (ViT-L) reaches PQ 58.3 at 30 FPS, matching MaskDINO's PQ 58.3 while being 2.1× faster. For semantic segmentation on Cityscapes, EoMT achieves 84.2 mIoU at 25 FPS versus 84.5 mIoU at 7 FPS for the ViT-Adapter + M2F baseline (3.6× faster). On COCO instance segmentation at 1280², EoMT attains 48.8 AP at 30 FPS, outperforming the same-size M2F baseline (47.6 AP at 29 FPS) in both accuracy and speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest same-architecture baseline (ViT-Adapter + Mask2Former with DINOv2), EoMT trades a small accuracy drop for a large speed gain: at 640², PQ drops from 57.1 to 56.0 (−1.1) while FPS jumps from 29 to 128 (4.4×); at 1280², PQ drops from 59.7 to 58.3 (−1.4) while FPS improves from 10 to 30 (3×). Against non-ViT SOTA methods, EoMT (ViT-L, 1280²) matches MaskDINO's PQ of 58.3 at 2.1× the speed (30 vs 14 FPS) and exceeds OneFormer (58.0 PQ at 20 FPS) in both accuracy and speed. On semantic segmentation (Cityscapes), EoMT achieves 84.2 mIoU at 25 FPS, within 0.3 mIoU of ViT-Adapter + M2F (84.5 at 7 FPS) and competitive with OneFormer (83.1 at 14 FPS) and kMaX-DeepLab (83.5). On COCO instance segmentation at 1280², EoMT (48.8 AP, 30 FPS) beats the same-size M2F baseline (47.6 AP, 29 FPS) in both AP and speed, though it remains below MaskDINO (52.3 AP, 14 FPS) and M2F at 1280² (51.4 AP, 10 FPS). Overall, EoMT consistently wins on the speed–accuracy trade-off, delivering near-SOTA accuracy at 2–4× the inference speed of comparable-accuracy baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant ViT-Adapter + Mask2Former paradigm (and its variants like OneFormer, MaskDINO, kMaX-DeepLab) by systematically removing each task-specific component—adapter, pixel decoder, multi-scale features, Transformer decoder, and masked attention—to arrive at an encoder-only architecture (EoMT) that repurposes plain ViT blocks for segmentation. It builds on DINOv2/EVA-02 vision foundation models for pre-training and on the Mask Transformer query-based prediction framework, but eliminates the separate decoder by concatenating learnable queries directly into the final ViT encoder blocks. The mask annealing strategy is a novel training technique that bridges the gap between masked-attention training and unmasked inference, a problem specific to this encoder-only design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all task-specific components become nearly redundant with sufficient ViT scale and large-scale pre-training (only 1.1 PQ loss for ViT-L with DINOv2, while gaining 4.4× speed) provides a clear practical guideline: invest compute in scaling the backbone rather than adding architectural complexity. This has broad implications for real-time segmentation deployment and for leveraging future ViT/foundation-model advances (FlashAttention, token merging, specialized hardware) without being bottlenecked by non-Transformer modules. Open questions include whether the remaining ~1 PQ gap can be closed with further scaling, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D segmentation, and how it performs under extreme efficiency constraints where even ViT-L is too large.
