## Problem

AI-based chest X-ray diagnosis models deployed in mHealth settings face three under-studied failure modes when images are transmitted through compression-heavy messaging apps like WhatsApp: prediction instability (different diagnoses on original vs. compressed images), out-of-lung saliency (explanations driven by non-lung regions), and localization instability (bounding boxes shifting or disappearing). No publicly available dataset of paired original and WhatsApp-compressed CXR images existed to systematically quantify these effects or to train models robust to such perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252391/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 141,804 pairs of original (1024×1024, ~668 KB) and WhatsApp-compressed (1024×1024, ~134 KB, ~66% size reduction) CXR images. Classification models consume a single CXR image and output multi-label pathologies (14 classes from NIH) or binary pneumonia/normal (RSNA); object detection models output bounding boxes for lung abnormalities. Evaluation produces three scalar scores per model: PI Score (fraction of pairs with differing predictions), OLS Score (fraction of images whose saliency falls outside the U-Net-segmented lung region, threshold η=0.4), and LI Score (fraction of pairs with differing ground-truth-agreeing localizations at IoU threshold t).

## Method

CheXwhatsApp is constructed by automatically sending all NIH and RSNA images through the WhatsApp Business API to a fixed recipient, with image filenames embedded in a 15-pixel white border and recovered via Tesseract OCR to handle WhatsApp's out-of-order delivery and random renaming. The paper defines three quantitative metrics—PI Score, OLS Score (via Intersection-Over-Lung-Region computed from GradCAM/GradCAM++/EigenCAM heatmaps and U-Net lung masks), and LI Score (IoU-based localization agreement)—to measure the three instability problems. SOTA CNN classifiers (DenseNet, ResNet-50, ResNeXt-50, Inception) and object detectors (Faster-RCNN, YOLOv5s, YOLOv8s) are trained under identical protocols on either the original dataset or the paired original+compressed dataset, then evaluated on both original and WhatsApp test images. The key finding is that training on the paired CheXwhatsApp data reduces PI, OLS, and LI scores by up to 80% compared to training on original images alone, demonstrating that exposure to compression artifacts during training is sufficient to improve robustness without architectural changes.

## Results

Training on the proposed paired dataset (NIH-WA) reduces the Prediction Instability Score (PI Score) by up to 79% compared to training on the original NIH dataset alone (e.g., DenseNet drops from 15.74 to 3.31). For object detection, training on RSNA-WA reduces the Localization Instability Score (LI Score) by up to 81% (YOLOv8s: 4.02 → 0.78 at t=0.7). The Out-of-Lung Saliency (OLS) Score also improves substantially, with reductions of 33–82% depending on the model and attribution method (GradCAM, GradCAM++, EigenCAM), and the OLS problem is further exacerbated on WhatsApp-compressed test images for models trained only on originals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare different architectures against each other as competing methods; instead, it compares the same architectures trained on original-only datasets (NIH, RSNA) versus the proposed paired datasets that include WhatsApp-compressed counterparts (NIH-WA, RSNA-WA). Across all four classification backbones (DenseNet, Inception, ResNet-50, ResNeXt-50), training on NIH-WA consistently reduces PI Score by 77–79%, with InceptionNet achieving the lowest absolute score of 3.14. For object detection, YOLOv8s trained on RSNA-WA shows the largest improvement (81% reduction in LI Score), while Faster-RCNN sees a more modest 13% reduction, suggesting that YOLO-family detectors benefit more from exposure to compression artifacts. On OLS, the gains are model- and method-dependent: ResNeXt with GradCAM++ shows the largest reduction (82%), while Inception and ResNet with EigenCAM show minimal improvement (~1–3%), indicating that some architectures remain prone to out-of-lung saliency regardless of training data augmentation with compressed images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of mHealth image transmission and CXR AI diagnosis. While prior work has documented the use of WhatsApp for sharing medical images in clinical settings and identified qualitative concerns about compression effects, no prior dataset or quantitative framework existed to systematically study how messaging-app compression alters model predictions, saliency, and localizations. The paper repurposes two well-established CXR datasets (NIH and RSNA) by creating their WhatsApp-compressed counterparts, and introduces three formal metrics (PI Score, OLS Score, LI Score) to quantify prediction instability, out-of-lung saliency, and localization instability. The benchmarking uses standard CNN classifiers and object detectors without architectural novelty, positioning the contribution squarely in the dataset-and-evaluation space rather than model design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CheXwhatsApp is the first large-scale paired dataset (141,804 pairs) enabling systematic study of how real-world messaging-app compression affects CXR AI, which is critical for mHealth deployment in LMICs where WhatsApp is the dominant communication channel and radiologist access is severely limited. The finding that training on the paired dataset reduces PIP, OLS, and LIP by up to 80% provides a practical recipe for building more robust diagnostic models. However, the dataset is derived from existing sources rather than newly collected, the study does not demonstrate integration into an actual mHealth pipeline, and generalizability to other compression schemes (e.g., other messaging platforms, different JPEG quality settings) remains partially open. The very high OLS scores observed with EigenCAM (95–99% for several architectures) raise important questions about whether current SOTA CXR models are genuinely attending to lung pathology or exploiting spurious non-lung features.
