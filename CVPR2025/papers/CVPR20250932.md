## Problem

Existing 3D vessel segmentation methods rely on discrete per-pixel mask classification, which inherently suffers from fractures in thin and fragile vessel regions, poor robustness to local noise (e.g., tissue density variations), and the absence of an explicit mechanism for feature aggregation along tubular curves. The paper addresses this by proposing a continuous, graph-based centerline representation that models vessels holistically rather than pixel-by-pixel.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250932/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D medical image volume (CTA or MR) as input, with all centerline coordinates normalized to [0,1]. It produces two outputs: a refined vessel segmentation mask and a set of continuous centerline points organized as a graph (N vertices connected by edges representing spatial adjacency). The centerline graph is directly usable as a directional centerline for downstream clinical tasks like curved planar reformation, eliminating the need for post-hoc thinning-based extraction from the segmentation mask.

## Method

DeformCL initializes a tubular template per vessel category by thinning a coarse segmentation, reconstructing a minimum spanning tree, selecting k=4 control points, and applying curvilinear interpolation to generate N_c=100 initial points. A cascaded deformation process (L=4 stages) then iteratively refines these points: trilinear interpolation extracts hierarchical features from a 3D UNet backbone, a Transformer aggregates features along the tubular curve using positional encodings of absolute and relative 3D positions, and a Tanh-bounded MLP predicts per-point offsets. After each deformation step, unpooling inserts new points between adjacent vertices to increase density. Final segmentation is obtained by computing a distance map from the predicted centerline points and fusing it with the coarse segmentation via a convolutional layer. Training uses a combination of local chamfer distance (computed over FPS-sampled patches to avoid global fluctuation), SDF loss for centrality, edge-length regularization, and Dice loss on both coarse and refined masks.

## Results

On the four 3D vessel segmentation datasets, DeformCL as an additional segmentation head consistently improves all baselines. The largest gains appear on HaN-Seg, where Ours(softDice) achieves 79.58% Dice (+3.28 over softDice) and 88.86% clDice (+2.90), with HD95 reduced from 4.905 to 4.113. Topology metrics (Betti errors) also improve substantially, e.g., β₀ drops from 3.316 to 2.123 on HaN-Seg. On the already high-performing ImageCAS dataset, gains are smaller but still consistent (Dice +0.74–1.19, clDice +0.33–1.83 across baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeformCL is evaluated as a plug-in segmentation head on three baselines (softDice, clDice, DSCNet) and compared against DDT, across four datasets. It wins on Dice, clDice, and HD95 in virtually all settings: the largest Dice gain is +3.28% on HaN-Seg (softDice→Ours), and the largest clDice gain is +2.90% on the same dataset. Topology errors (β₀, β₁, Euler) are reduced by 20–50% relative to the corresponding baselines, confirming the continuous centerline representation's benefit for structural completeness. On the high-performing ImageCAS dataset, gains shrink (Dice +0.07–1.19%) but remain positive, and HD95 improves by 1.0–1.4 mm. The method does not lose to any baseline on any dataset for the primary volumetric or distance metrics; the only near-tie is clDice on ImageCAS where Ours(clDice) achieves 96.01% vs. the clDice baseline's 96.00%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeformCL repurposes the iterative deformation paradigm from 3D mesh generation (Pixel2Mesh, Voxel2Mesh) to the problem of tubular structure extraction, replacing the per-pixel discrete mask representation that dominates prior vessel segmentation work (clDice, DSCNet, DDT). While point-set representations like PointScatter already moved away from fixed grids, DeformCL adds explicit graph connectivity and a cascaded unpooling strategy to enforce structural continuity. The paper positions its contribution as addressing the root cause of segmentation fractures and false positives—the discrete representation itself—rather than patching symptoms with topology-preserving losses or tubular priors in the network architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous centerline output is directly usable for downstream clinical tasks (curved planar reformation, computational hemodynamics) without the fragile and time-consuming post-processing of extracting centerlines from discrete masks, which is a practical bottleneck in radiology workflows. The method's plug-in nature (applicable to softDice, clDice, DSCNet) makes it broadly adoptable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex vascular networks with dense branching, whether the adaptive template generation scales to very long or highly tortuous vessels, and whether the 4-stage cascaded deformation can be made more efficient for real-time clinical deployment.
