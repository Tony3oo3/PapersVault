## Problem

The standard reflow training pipeline for rectified flow models is suboptimal in two ways: directly enforcing velocity consistency across all timesteps simultaneously is difficult because pretrained diffusion models exhibit large velocity discrepancies between distant timesteps, and the conventional MSE loss treats velocity direction and magnitude equally despite direction being far more critical for trajectory straightness. This paper identifies both gaps and proposes targeted training techniques to address them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250082/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated RGB image in latent space (512×512 for SDv1.5, 1024×1024 for SDXL), decoded by a frozen VAE. During training, a teacher diffusion model (32 DDIM steps) generates endpoint targets from noisy real images, and the student flow model is trained to predict a consistent velocity field that maps noise to image in as few as 2 or 4 integration steps. The trained model replaces the iterative denoising loop with a single ODE integration of the learned velocity.

## Method

ProReflow introduces two complementary training techniques. First, progressive reflow decomposes the reflow objective into a curriculum: it starts with 8 time windows (each approximated by a local linear segment), then applies cross-windows reflow to merge adjacent windows into 4, and finally into 2, where each merge aligns the optimization direction across the combined interval so the trajectory progresses from the outer endpoints rather than the intermediate one. Second, aligned v-prediction replaces the pure MSE velocity loss with a weighted combination L = (1−α)·MSE(v, pred) + α·(1−cos(v, pred)), explicitly penalizing directional misalignment via cosine similarity; the paper shows via a law-of-cosines analysis that directional errors cause roughly |v|² times larger degradation than equivalent magnitude errors, justifying α = 0.1. The two techniques are orthogonal and combine additively, yielding state-of-the-art few-step FID on both SDv1.5 and SDXL.

## Results

On COCO-2014 validation set, ProReflow-II achieves an FID of 10.70 with only 4 sampling steps (0.21s), approaching the 32-step DDIM teacher (FID 10.05). On COCO-2017, ProReflow-II reduces FID from 32.97 (2-ReFlow) to 22.03 at 4 steps, and from 49.32 to 27.59 at 2 steps. On SDXL (COCO2014-10k), ProReflow-SDXL achieves FID 19.10 at 4 steps, outperforming DMDv2 (19.32), Rectified Diffusion (19.71), and Perflow (20.99). The ablation confirms both progressive reflow and aligned v-prediction contribute: removing both yields FID 23.81 vs. 22.97 for the full ProReflow-I model on COCO-2017.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProReflow-II (4 steps, FID 10.70) outperforms all flow-based baselines on COCO-2014: PeRFlow (12.01), 2-ReFlow (15.32), and InstaFlow-0.9B (44.01), with the largest margin over InstaFlow. It also beats distillation-based methods PCM (11.70) and Flash Diffusion (12.41) at the same 4-step budget, while remaining competitive with the 32-step DDIM teacher (10.05). On COCO-2017, ProReflow-II (22.03) improves over PeRFlow (23.81) and 2-ReFlow (32.97) by 1.78 and 10.94 FID points respectively. On SDXL (COCO2014-10k), ProReflow-SDXL (19.10) edges out DMDv2 (19.32), Rectified Diffusion (19.71), Perflow (20.99), and LCM (22.16), establishing state-of-the-art among 4-step methods. The only method achieving lower FID is the 25-step DPMSolver (9.78), which requires ~4× more inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rectified flow / reflow training pipeline (Liu et al., ICLR 2022; InstaFlow, ICLR 2023) by addressing two specific weaknesses in how the velocity-matching objective is optimized. Temporally, it generalizes PeRFlow's fixed 4-window piecewise straightening into a multi-stage curriculum (8→4→2 windows), grounded in a knowledge-distillation / privileged-information framework (Lopez-Paz et al., 2015). Spatially, it introduces a velocity decomposition that separates direction from magnitude, arguing that the standard MSE loss treats them symmetrically when direction is empirically far more critical for trajectory straightness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The velocity-decomposition insight (directional errors degrade FID far more than magnitude errors of comparable size) offers a principled, low-cost loss modification that could be adopted across flow-matching training pipelines beyond this specific architecture. The progressive reflow curriculum is a practical recipe that reduces optimization difficulty without additional inference cost, and the step-scalability observation (a model trained with 2 windows outperforms one trained with 4 windows at 4-step inference) suggests that over-constraining the trajectory during training can hurt generalization. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to one-step generation (the authors could not train a single-window model to convergence) and whether the direction-over-magnitude priority holds for video or higher-dimensional latent spaces.
