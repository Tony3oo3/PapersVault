## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration methods rely on either implicit priors (learnable prompts, task-specific sub-networks) that scale poorly to unseen degradations, or explicit text-based instructions that are inherently ambiguous and weakly aligned with low-level visual artifacts. No prior mechanism directly and faithfully encodes the visual patterns of degradations (e.g., rain streaks, noise dots, haze veils) in a form that a restoration model can exploit for precise, composable guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252772/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded RGB image (256×256) and a visual instruction — a degraded TE42 chart patch (concentric/random/natural textures + color swatches corrupted by the same degradation process) — as dual inputs. The visual instruction is discretized by a VQ-VAE encoder into a sequence of degradation tokens, and the clean (undegraded) visual ground embedding is subtracted to form a residual signal. The output is a single restored high-quality image, produced in 4 DDIM sampling steps, with the model operating directly in pixel space rather than a VAE latent space.

## Method

Defusion formulates restoration as denoising in a degradation space: the diffusion target is the difference vector y₀ = x_LQ − T_v(x_LQ) (the degradation to be removed), not the HQ image itself, which yields a more stable and semantically aligned training distribution. Visual instructions are constructed by applying the target degradation to standardized TE42 chart elements (visual grounds), then tokenized via a quantization-regularized autoencoder (Degradation Tokenizer) into discrete codes. A Visual Instruction Adapter (VIA), built by copying cross-attention layers from a pretrained text-to-image U-Net (IP-Adapter style), injects these degradation tokens as key/value, while an Image Restoration Bridge (IRB) injects LQ image features at multiple resolutions via AdaLN-Zero. The score model is a U-Net trained with denoising score matching on the conditioned degradation distribution, and the framework can be initialized from any pretrained diffusion model without architectural modification to the noise schedule.

## Results

Defusion achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all 8 restoration tasks in Table 1, outperforming both task-specific and all-in-one baselines. Headline numbers include 34.53 PSNR / 0.966 SSIM on GoPro motion deblurring (vs. 33.20 for the best task-specific DiffIR), 29.68 / 0.922 on DPDD defocus deblurring (vs. 26.18 for FocalNet), 34.09 / 0.954 on Rain1400 deraining (vs. 33.68 for Restormer), and 40.89 / 0.954 on SIDD real denoising (vs. 40.02 for Restormer). On real-world datasets (Table 2), Defusion leads on all four benchmarks, e.g., 32.98 PSNR on LHP deraining and 32.39 on RealBlur motion deblurring. On the mixed-distortion WED dataset (Table 3), Defusion achieves 25.05–27.01 PSNR across six distortion orderings, surpassing the next-best Restormer* by 1.2–2.5 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Defusion outperforms all 35 compared baselines on every task in Table 1. Against the strongest all-in-one competitors, Defusion leads MPerciver [4] by 2.04 dB on GoPro deblurring, 3.80 dB on DPDD defocus deblurring, and 0.69 dB on Rain1400 deraining; it surpasses PromptIR [86] by 3.51 dB on GoPro and 0.83 dB on Rain1400. Against task-specific SOTA, Defusion exceeds DiffIR [125] by 1.33 dB on GoPro, FocalNet [21] by 3.50 dB on DPDD, and Restormer [134] by 0.41 dB on Rain1400. The only near-tie is on SIDD real denoising, where Defusion (40.89) edges out Restormer* (40.60) by 0.29 dB while Restormer task-specific (40.02) trails by 0.87 dB. On the mixed-distortion WED benchmark (Table 3), Defusion gains 1.2–2.5 dB over Restormer* and 10+ dB over PromptIR and WeatherDiff, confirming superior handling of composite degradations. On underwater datasets (Table 4), Defusion achieves 21.23 PSNR on EUVP and 18.26 on TURBID, outperforming PromptIR by 1.19 and 2.94 dB respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

Defusion builds on the score-based SDE diffusion framework (Song et al., 2020) and the IP-Adapter cross-attention mechanism (Ye et al., 2023), but departs from prior diffusion-based restoration work (DiffIR, DiffBIR, SUPIR) by formulating the denoising process in degradation space rather than image space, and from text-instruction-based methods (InstructIR, AutoDIR, DA-CLIP) by replacing ambiguous language priors with explicit visual instructions constructed from degraded TE42 charts. The VQ-VAE-based degradation tokenizer draws on LDM and VQ-VAE, while the overall all-in-one positioning extends the line of work from AirNet, PromptIR, and MPerciver that use implicit learnable prompts or task embeddings. The key conceptual departure is the 'let visual speak for visual' principle: grounding degradations on standardized visual elements to produce instructions that are inherently aligned with low-level pixel distortions, avoiding the lossy language-vision bridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Defusion addresses a practical gap in real-world image restoration where degradations are mixed, unknown, or compound, and demonstrates that a single diffusion model guided by visual instructions can match or exceed task-specific SOTA across 8+ restoration tasks. The degradation-space formulation offers a clean theoretical justification for why visual instructions (which describe distortions, not content) are the right conditioning signal, and the ablation confirms that removing either the visual grounding or the degradation-space formulation causes significant performance drops. Open questions include whether the TE42-based visual grounds generalize to truly unseen degradation types beyond those in training, the computational cost of diffusion sampling at inference (even with 4 DDIM steps), and whether the approach extends naturally to video restoration or higher-resolution settings. The framework also raises the broader question of whether other low-level vision tasks (super-resolution, enhancement) benefit similarly from visual-instruction conditioning in a residual space.
