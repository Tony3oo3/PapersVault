## Problem

RGB-T trackers suffer from distribution shift during long-term tracking across varying scenes, yet existing test-time adaptation methods either cause catastrophic parameter drift through repeated entropy minimization or impose prohibitive computational costs via gradient-based consistency regularization. No TTA solution tailored to RGB-T tracking has been proposed that simultaneously achieves fast adaptation and parameter stability without gradient computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250846/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a pair of co-registered RGB and thermal frames per timestep along with a target template, processing them through a one-stream ViT-B backbone that concatenates token sequences from both modalities for joint feature extraction. A convolutional head produces bounding box predictions, while a score prediction head generates confidence scores used to dynamically update the template. Only the BatchNorm affine parameters in the prediction head are modified during adaptation, keeping the backbone frozen and enabling 42 FPS inference.

## Method

PURA operates in two alternating phases. In the fast parameter update phase, BatchNorm mean and variance are recalibrated per frame via exponential moving average of test-sample statistics, requiring no gradient computation. In the parameter decomposition recovery phase (triggered every K=3 frames), the accumulated parameter deltas are treated as pseudo-gradients and subjected to SVD; the principal eigenvectors identify the dominant update direction, and parameters are partially recovered along that direction using a similarity-gated, eigenvalue-weighted aggregation to retain critical knowledge. An adaptive momentum scaling mechanism monitors the consistency of principal eigenvalues over time and dynamically adjusts the EMA momentum, preventing both overly aggressive and overly conservative updates.

## Results

On RGBT234, PURA achieves 93.3% MPR and 70.3% MSR, outperforming the closest competitor MambaVT (90.7% MPR, 67.5% MSR) by 2.6 and 2.8 points respectively. On RGBT210, PURA reaches 90.3% PR and 66.8% SR, surpassing MambaVT by 1.8 and 2.4 points. On GTOT, PURA attains 95.7% MPR and 78.6% MSR. PURA also achieves the highest processing speed among all compared methods at 42 FPS, compared to 31.1 FPS for MambaVT and 36.2 FPS for TBSI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PURA outperforms the strongest RGB-T tracker MambaVT on all three benchmarks: +2.6 MPR and +2.8 MSR on RGBT234, +1.8 PR and +2.4 SR on RGBT210, and +0.5 MPR on GTOT (tied on MSR at 78.6%), while also running 35% faster (42 vs 31.1 FPS). Against the TTA baselines in the LasHeR→RGBT234 domain-shift setting, PURA beats Tent (93.3 vs 91.8 MPR), ETA (93.3 vs 92.3 MPR), AdaBN (93.3 vs 91.6 MPR), and CoTTA (93.3 vs 91.2 MPR), while being 2–15× faster than gradient-based methods (42 FPS vs 18.5 for Tent, 2.8 for CoTTA). In the LasHeR→GTOT scenario, Tent narrowly edges PURA by 0.3 MPR (96.0 vs 95.7), but PURA is 2.3× faster. PURA also demonstrates tracker-agnostic applicability: when plugged into TBSI it raises MPR from 85.3 to 86.3, and into ViPT from 83.5 to 84.7, consistently outperforming Tent, ETA, and AdaBN as TTA add-ons on both backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to introduce test-time adaptation (TTA) into RGB-T tracking, a domain where prior methods (TBSI, ViPT, MambaVT) rely on fixed parameters at inference. Conceptually, it repurposes the gradient-free BatchNorm calibration idea from AdaBN and DELTA but augments it with an SVD-based parameter decomposition recovery mechanism inspired by PCA and the fast-slow gradient decomposition in FSTTA. It deliberately departs from gradient-based TTA methods (Tent, CoTTA, ETA) that are computationally prohibitive for real-time tracking, instead using EMA-driven statistical updates on a small set of BatchNorm parameters in the prediction head. The baseline tracker itself is a standard one-stream ViT-B with template updating, so the contribution is orthogonal to the backbone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a lightweight, gradient-free TTA strategy can push RGB-T trackers to SOTA while maintaining 42 FPS, this work opens a practical path for deploying adaptive trackers in real-world scenarios with shifting lighting, weather, and backgrounds. The SVD-based recovery mechanism addresses a known failure mode of long-term online adaptation (parameter drift and knowledge forgetting) in a way that is specific to the small parameter space of BatchNorm layers, raising the question of whether similar decomposition-recovery strategies generalize to larger parameter subsets or other modalities. Limitations include the method's dependence on a specific tracker architecture (ViT-B with BatchNorm in the head), the modest 1–2% gains over strong baselines, and evaluation only on three RGB-T benchmarks without cross-domain generalization tests beyond LasHeR→target splits.
