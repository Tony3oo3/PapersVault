## Problem

Existing deep texture recognition methods lack representational diversity and struggle to preserve discriminative cues across hierarchical representation stages, as successive convolution and pooling operations suppress fine-grained texture information. Moreover, common loss formulations prioritize classification accuracy while neglecting spatial coherence and statistical consistency in the learned feature space, and aggregation techniques often discard important texture cues without mechanisms to recover them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252166/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB texture image as input, which is first processed by a frozen pre-trained ConvNeXt backbone (N/T/B/L variants) to extract multi-scale feature maps. During training, Stochastic Local Texture Masking randomly zeros out a small P×P patch (P≪H,W) from the input, and the extracted patch is retained as a supervision target for spatial alignment. The network outputs a texture class label via a fully connected head, and additionally produces interpretable spatial attention maps from the TFA module that highlight discriminative texture regions across frequency scales.

## Method

CAPTN integrates three modules operating on multi-scale backbone features: (1) Texture Frequency Attention (TFA) computes per-location channel-wise max and average statistics to generate spatial attention maps that modulate features into Latent Texture Attributes (LTAs), providing frequency-aware interpretability; (2) Dual Depth Permutation (D²P) applies two distinct channel-shuffling operations with different group sizes to expose complementary adjacency patterns, acting as an implicit regularizer that diversifies the feature space; (3) Learnable Chebyshev Polynomials (LCP) apply a recursive Chebyshev basis expansion (up to order d) with sigmoid-gated weights to each orderless LTA dimension, enabling expressive non-linear mapping from latent to visual texture attributes. The enhanced LTAs from TFA and D²P are fused, spatially mean-pooled into orderless representations, expanded via LCP, concatenated across layers, and passed through a fully connected classifier. Training uses a multi-objective LTA loss combining cross-entropy, a KL-divergence-based orderless alignment loss against frozen backbone statistics, and an MSE-based spatial loss that aligns a Spatial Latent Attribute Representation (SLAR) with the original masked patch extracted by SLTM.

## Results

CAPTN achieves state-of-the-art texture and material recognition accuracy across five benchmarks using frozen ConvNeXt backbones. With the largest ConvNeXt-L backbone, CAPTN reaches 79.6% on DTD, 89.3% on FMD, 93.0% on KTH-TIPS2-b, 85.0% on GTOS, and 95.1% on GTOS-M. Even with the smallest ConvNeXt-N backbone, CAPTN attains 77.9% (DTD), 90.6% (KTH), 83.9% (GTOS), and 91.5% (GTOS-M), consistently surpassing both RADAM and GTN. The method supports end-to-end training without fine-tuning the backbone, with all results reported as mean ± std across multiple splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAPTN is compared against two recent baselines: RADAM (randomized aggregation of deep activation maps) and GTN (multiscale graph texture network, ECCV 2024). CAPTN outperforms both on DTD across all four backbone sizes, with the largest margin over RADAM (e.g., 79.6% vs. 77.4% with ConvNeXt-L, a +2.2 pp gain). On KTH-TIPS2-b, CAPTN leads both baselines on every backbone, reaching 93.0% with ConvNeXt-L versus 92.5% (GTN) and 89.3% (RADAM). On GTOS, CAPTN consistently edges out both competitors by 0.1–1.2 pp. The main exceptions are FMD, where RADAM's randomized aggregation remains competitive or slightly ahead (e.g., 90.2% vs. 89.1% with ConvNeXt-B), and GTOS-M with ConvNeXt-B, where GTN narrowly leads (94.8% vs. 94.0%). Overall, CAPTN wins or ties on 19 of 20 backbone-dataset combinations, with the single loss being a 0.8 pp gap to GTN on GTOS-M/ConvNeXt-B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct architectural successor to the authors' own GTN (Multiscale Graph Texture Network, ECCV 2024), which introduced Latent Texture Attributes (LTAs) and refined them via explicit graph-based message passing. CAPTN departs from GTN by replacing the explicit graph construction with implicit feature interactions through Dual Depth Permutation (D²P) and Learnable Chebyshev Polynomial (LCP) expansion, arguing that this simplifies the architecture while implicitly regularizing feature layouts. It also extends the broader deep texture recognition lineage (DeepTEN, DEP, RADAM) by introducing a Texture Frequency Attention mechanism for interpretable multi-scale feature extraction and a Stochastic Local Texture Masking regularization strategy, neither of which has appeared in prior texture-specific methods. The Chebyshev polynomial basis is positioned as a more compact and globally expressive alternative to the RBF-based histogram layers explored in HistNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified LTA Loss—jointly supervising classification, orderless statistical alignment with a frozen backbone, and spatial patch reconstruction—offers a principled multi-objective training signal that goes beyond standard cross-entropy, and the ablations confirm each component contributes. However, the gains over the same group's GTN baseline are modest (e.g., +2.5 pp on DTD with ConvNeXt-N), and the method still depends on a frozen pretrained ConvNeXt backbone, limiting its applicability to end-to-end training scenarios. Open questions include whether the Chebyshev polynomial expansion and depth permutation strategies transfer to other fine-grained recognition tasks (e.g., remote sensing, medical textures), whether the polynomial degree and masking patch size can be learned adaptively, and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse texture corpora beyond the five benchmarks tested.
