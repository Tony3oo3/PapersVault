## Problem

Local motion deblurring requires restoring only the spatially varying blurry regions while preserving sharp background, but existing methods depend on coarse manually annotated blur masks and ignore the underlying motion kernel characteristics (direction, amplitude, trajectory). This lack of kernel-aware, fine-grained blur localization limits restoration quality and introduces artifacts in sharp areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252009/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurry RGB image (256×256 patches during training) and produces a deblurred image at the same resolution. As intermediate outputs, it estimates a per-pixel 3-channel parameter map (kernel length, angle, curvature) at full spatial resolution, from which a fine-grained binary blur mask is derived by thresholding the length channel against a fixed value σ. The kernel parameters are used directly as conditioning signals for the deblurring network rather than being converted into explicit kernel images.

## Method

The approach has three components. First, a parameterized motion kernel model represents each blur kernel as a parabolic trajectory (x = center + t, y = center + curvature·t²) rotated by an angle and normalized, reducing the kernel to three interpretable scalars. Second, a Motion Prior Estimation Network (MPENet) uses a CNN encoder-decoder with a shared self-supervised memory bank (updated via cosine-similarity retrieval and exponential moving average) to predict per-pixel kernel parameters; an auxiliary reblurring network shares the same memory bank to enforce consistent motion representation, and the whole system is trained with an L1 reblurring loss. Third, a dual-branch deblurring network consumes the estimated parameters and mask: a global branch applies deformable convolution (conditioned on kernel parameters) followed by a U-shape Mamba (selective state space model) for long-range dependencies, while a local branch uses a lightweight mask-guided CNN to refine only the blurry regions, with the inverted mask recombined to preserve the sharp background. Training is two-stage: pre-train MPENet, then fine-tune the deblurring network with L1 loss.

## Results

On the ReLoBlur test set, PGDN achieves 36.53 dB PSNR and 0.9340 SSIM, surpassing the previous best (Uformer-B at 35.03 dB) by 1.5 dB. When trained on ReLoBlur+GoPro, PGDN reaches 36.64 dB PSNR and 0.9351 SSIM, exceeding the SOTA LMD-ViT (35.42 dB) by over 1.2 dB. In terms of weighted metrics on blurry regions, PGDN attains 30.87 dB PSNRw and 0.8991 SSIMw on ReLoBlur+GoPro. The method also achieves the lowest computational cost among all compared methods, with only 29.21M parameters and 0.613T FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGDN outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both ReLoBlur evaluation settings. On ReLoBlur+GoPro, PGDN (36.64 dB) exceeds the strongest prior method LMD-ViT (35.42 dB) by 1.22 dB in PSNR and 0.0066 in SSIM, while also improving weighted PSNRw by 0.62 dB. Against CNN-based methods like NAFNet (34.67 dB) and Restormer (34.85 dB), the gap is even larger at roughly 2 dB. In terms of efficiency, PGDN uses only 0.613T FLOPs compared to 4.375T for Uformer-B and 3.129T for NAFNet, while maintaining fewer parameters (29.21M) than most competitors. On the GoPro global deblurring benchmark (Table 6), PGDN achieves 34.17 dB PSNR, slightly trailing FFTformer (34.21 dB) and NAFNet (33.71 dB in PSNR but 0.9668 SSIM vs. PGDN's 0.9501), though PGDN remains the most efficient at 0.613T FLOPs versus 10.95T for FFTformer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local motion deblurring (LMD) line initiated by ReLoBlur/LBAG [17] and LMD-ViT [18], but departs from their reliance on manually annotated or coarsely generated blur masks by introducing a parameterized kernel model (length, angle, curvature) that yields a fine-grained, pixel-level blur mask as a byproduct of kernel estimation. The global deblurring branch repurposes Mamba's selective state-space mechanism [7] for image restoration, while the memory bank for storing motion degradation priors is adapted from the self-supervised update strategy in Huang et al. [11] (originally for deraining). Conceptually, the key departure is treating blur kernel estimation as a low-dimensional parameter regression problem rather than a full kernel regression, which shrinks the solution space and makes the estimation task more tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameterized kernel formulation offers a practical and interpretable way to handle spatially varying blur without expensive manual annotation, and the derived fine-grained mask outperforms the coarse masks used in prior LMD methods. The efficiency advantage (0.613T FLOPs vs. 1.485T for LMD-ViT at higher PSNR) makes the approach attractive for deployment. However, the GoPro global-deblurring results show the method is competitive but not dominant in SSIM (0.9501 vs. 0.9692 for FFTformer), suggesting the parameterization may be less expressive for uniform global blur. The authors note future work on extending to video by exploiting temporal redundancy of local motion, which remains an open direction.
