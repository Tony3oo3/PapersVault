## Problem

Cross-modal knowledge distillation from LiDAR-based teachers to camera-based students for monocular 3D object detection is hampered by a large feature representation gap between modalities, making direct BEV feature alignment ineffective. Existing approaches rely on complex adaptation modules or regularization to bridge this gap, but these remain insufficient for effective knowledge transfer, leaving camera-based students unable to fully capture LiDAR-exclusive 3D spatial cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252682/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the student model consumes a single monocular RGB image and produces 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation) with class labels via a BEV detector. During training, the TA model additionally ingests ground-truth depth maps (discretized into D depth bins) to construct optimal BEV features, while the LiDAR teacher processes synchronized point clouds voxelized into 3D grids. The student's dual-branch architecture generates two separate BEV feature maps (visual and spatial) that are fused before detection.

## Method

MonoTAKD introduces a camera-based Teaching Assistant (TA) model that uses GT depth to produce optimal BEV features, acting as a same-modality intermediary between the LiDAR teacher and camera student. Intra-modal distillation (IMD) minimizes MSE between the student's visual branch and the TA's BEV features, transferring 3D visual knowledge across a smaller representation gap. Cross-modal residual distillation (CMRD) computes the element-wise difference (binarized by threshold) between the teacher and TA BEV features as residual features encoding LiDAR-exclusive 3D spatial cues, which the student's spatial branch learns via MSE. A Spatial Alignment Module (ASPP + deformable convolutions + SENet) refines the spatial branch to capture global context and correct depth-induced misalignment, and a Feature Fusion Module integrates both branches before the BEV detector. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a combined loss of IMD, CMRD, and logit distillation (quality focal loss + Smooth L1).

## Results

On the KITTI 3D test set (Car category), MonoTAKD achieves AP3D of 27.91 / 19.43 / 16.51 (Easy/Mod./Hard) and APBEV of 38.75 / 27.76 / 24.14, outperforming the second-best method (OccupancyM3D) by +2.30, +2.06, and +1.21 in AP3D and by +1.88, +3.34, and +2.26 in APBEV across difficulty levels. On nuScenes val, MonoTAKD as a plug-in distillation module improves BEVFormer (R101) from NDS 0.445 to 0.558 and BEVDepth (R101) from NDS 0.535 to 0.564, consistently surpassing prior cross-modal distillation methods (BEVDistill, DistillBEV, STXD, LabelDistill). The model uses only 47.8M parameters and 44.90G FLOPs, comparable to the lighter CMKD (45.1M, 41.32G) while delivering substantially higher AP3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoTAKD decisively outperforms the strongest prior KD-based method CMKD [12] by +2.82, +2.44, and +1.21 in AP3D (Easy/Mod./Hard) and by +5.06, +4.66, and +3.47 in APBEV, while using a comparable parameter count (47.8M vs. 45.1M). Against the top depth-guided methods that require LiDAR auxiliary supervision, MonoTAKD surpasses MonoPGC [41] by +3.23 AP3D (Easy) and MonoNeRD [43] by +5.16 AP3D (Easy), all without any extra training data. Compared to the strongest no-extra-data baselines, MonoTAKD leads MonoDETR [49] by +2.91 AP3D (Easy) and +2.93 AP3D (Hard), and MonoATT [51] by +3.19 AP3D (Easy) and +1.51 AP3D (Hard). On nuScenes, MonoTAKD as a distillation plug-in consistently beats BEVDistill [6], DistillBEV [39], STXD [16], and LabelDistill [19] across both BEVFormer and BEVDepth backbones at both R50 and R101 scales, e.g., improving BEVFormer-R101 NDS from 0.545 (DistillBEV) to 0.558. The efficiency comparison (Table 6) shows MonoTAKD-Lite (without SAM/FFM) matches CMKD's 45.1M/41.32G budget yet achieves +7.83 AP3D (Easy) over CMKD, confirming the gains come from the distillation strategy rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modal BEV distillation paradigm established by CMKD and BEVDistill, where a LiDAR-based teacher transfers 3D knowledge to a camera-based student. The key departure is the introduction of a camera-based Teaching Assistant (TA) model—conceptually inspired by the teacher-assistant distillation framework of Mirzadeh et al.—that uses ground-truth depth to produce '3D visual knowledge' via intra-modal distillation, while the LiDAR-exclusive spatial cues are extracted as residual features (teacher BEV minus TA BEV) and distilled via a novel cross-modal residual distillation loss. The student architecture builds directly on CaDDN's depth-distribution-to-BEV projection, and the LiDAR teacher uses SECOND's sparse convolution pipeline, making the contribution primarily a new distillation strategy layered onto existing detection backbones rather than a new detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MonoTAKD sets a clear new SOTA on KITTI 3D (Car) with margins of +2.3 to +3.3 AP over prior methods, and the residual-distillation idea—learning the LiDAR-camera difference rather than the full LiDAR target—offers a principled way to decompose cross-modal knowledge transfer into a tractable intra-modal component and a focused cross-modal component. The generalization to multi-view nuScenes (BEVFormer, BEVDepth) and the low compute footprint (47.8M params, 44.9 GFLOPs) make the approach practically relevant for deployment-oriented teams. However, the TA model's reliance on ground-truth depth during training limits applicability to settings where depth annotations are unavailable, and the method is tightly coupled to BEV-based detection architectures, leaving open whether the residual-distillation principle transfers to non-BEV or end-to-end transformer detectors.
