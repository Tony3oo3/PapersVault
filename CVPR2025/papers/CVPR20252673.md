## Problem

Existing vision-language models are constrained by frame limitations and cannot perform temporal grounding in hour-long videos, losing essential temporal details needed for accurate event localization. Even on short (2-minute) localization tasks, VLM-based approaches underperform non-LLM methods, and no VLM had previously demonstrated the ability to ground events in videos spanning hours.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252673/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video of arbitrary length (minutes to 12+ hours) and a free-form text query describing a target event, and outputs precise start and end frame indices in the format 'From s to e' or 'Not Present' if the event is absent. Video frames are encoded via CLIP ViT-L/14 into per-frame CLS tokens, which are then partitioned into sliding windows and projected into dense (full-resolution) or sparse (condensed) temporal features depending on the hierarchy level. The LLM processes only 57% of input frames on average compared to non-recursive baselines, making it substantially more efficient.

## Method

ReVisionLLM introduces a recursive hierarchical architecture with three components: a CLIP ViT-L/14 multimodal encoder, a Hierarchical Adapter that produces both dense temporal features (via linear projection) and sparse text-aligned features (via cross-attention with the query followed by self-attention condensation), and a Vicuna 7B LLM decoder. The model processes the video top-down across L hierarchies: the top level uses sparse features to identify broad segments of interest, and each subsequent level refines the focus using progressively denser representations until the bottom hierarchy pinpoints exact boundaries. Training is progressive—Stage 1 fine-tunes the LLM (via LoRA) on short segments with contrastive negative clips to calibrate confidence, then Stage 2 fine-tunes the Hierarchical Adapter on hour-long videos using sparse features. At inference, predictions are ranked by the LLM's internal entropy-based confidence score rather than CLIP similarity, reducing false positives in long videos.

## Results

On the MAD dataset, ReVisionLLM achieves R1@0.1 of 15.0%, R1@0.3 of 11.0%, and R1@0.5 of 5.8%, surpassing the previous best method RGNet (12.4% R1@0.1) by +2.6%. On VidChapters-7M, it reaches R1@0.3 of 33.8%, R1@0.7 of 21.8%, and R1@0.9 of 15.2%, outperforming M-DETR by +4.2% at R1@0.7 and +8.8% at R1@0.9. The overall average score across both datasets is 19.5%, the highest among all compared methods. The ReVisionLLM-I variant (processing all frames) further improves MAD R1@0.1 to 17.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReVisionLLM outperforms all non-LLM long-video grounding baselines on MAD: RGNet (12.4% R1@.1), SOONet (11.3%), SnAG (10.3%), M-Guide (9.3%), and CONE (8.9%), with the largest margin over RGNet at +2.6% R1@.1. On VidChapters-7M, ReVisionLLM surpasses M-DETR at stricter IoU thresholds (R1@.7: 21.8% vs 17.6%; R1@.9: 15.2% vs 6.4%) but trails slightly at R1@.3 (33.8% vs 37.4%), indicating superior precision for tight boundary localization. Against VLM baselines, the gap is dramatic: VTimeLLM+CONE scores only 1.4% R1@.1 on MAD and 4.1% average on VidChapters-7M, while CLIP achieves 6.6% R1@.1 on MAD. On the MSRVTT text-to-video retrieval generalization task, ReVisionLLM achieves R@5 of 77.5% and R@10 of 85.7%, beating T-MASS (75.3%, 85.1%) and CLIP-ViP (74.8%, 84.6%) at those ranks, though it trails T-MASS slightly on R@1 (49.1% vs 50.2%). The ReVisionLLM-I variant, which processes all frames in reverse hierarchical order, extends the MAD lead over RGNet to +4.9% R1@.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VTimeLLM architecture (video encoder + adapter + LLM) by introducing a Hierarchical Adapter that produces both dense and sparse temporal features, enabling recursive multi-scale processing of hour-long videos. It departs from prior VLMs (VTimeLLM, TimeChat, LITA, Hawkeye) that are confined to short clips due to context-length limits, and from non-LLM long-video grounding methods (CONE, RGNet, SnAG) that lack instruction-following and rely on CLIP-similarity ranking. The recursive, coarse-to-fine search strategy is inspired by cognitive models of human visual search, and the progressive training with contrastive segments adapts a confidence-calibration idea from the text domain to the video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReVisionLLM is the first VLM to perform temporal grounding on hour-long videos, opening the door to instruction-following video search at scales previously accessible only to specialized non-LLM pipelines. The 43% frame reduction relative to prior VLMs while achieving higher accuracy suggests the recursive hierarchy is a practical path to scaling VLMs to very long content. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-day videos, whether integrating audio would further improve event comprehension, and whether the recursive structure generalizes to other long-sequence multimodal tasks beyond grounding.
