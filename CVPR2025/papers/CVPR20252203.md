## Problem

Creating photorealistic, drivable 3D head avatars from only a few input images remains challenging because existing monocular methods overfit to their training viewpoint distribution and fail to generalize to novel expressions and views, while multi-identity priors trained on real data are expensive to capture and subject to strict privacy regulations (e.g., GDPR). The paper addresses this by asking whether a generative prior trained exclusively on synthetic data can be adapted to real subjects with minimal input, bridging the synthetic-to-real domain gap without requiring large-scale multi-view capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252203/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3–20 in-the-wild RGB images of a subject, from which a 3DMM regressor extracts a UV texture map, a rasterized neutral-mesh vertex position map, and PCA expression deltas as network inputs. The output is a fully drivable 3D Gaussian head avatar whose primitives are parameterized in UV texture space and can be re-rendered from arbitrary camera viewpoints and driven with unseen FLAME expression codes. Optimization for up to 20 input views completes in under 10 minutes on a single Nvidia H100 GPU.

## Method

SynShot trains a VQ-VAE with disentangled identity and expression latent spaces on ~2,000 synthetic identities (14M images rendered in Blender Cycles), where the decoder outputs a high-dimensional feature map, identity maps (texture + vertex positions), and expression deltas. Per-part (face vs. hair) bilinear sampling of the feature map at variable UV resolutions acts as adaptive primitive densification, and lightweight per-region regressors predict spherical-harmonic color coefficients and additive offsets for Gaussian position, rotation, scale, and opacity. For few-shot inversion, a two-stage pivotal tuning strategy first optimizes only the identity encoder to recover the subject's latent code, then fine-tunes all decoders and regressors against the real images using a combined photometric (L1+SSIM+LPIPS), VGG perceptual, ArcFace, and identity-preservation loss, effectively bridging the synthetic-to-real domain gap while preserving the prior's generalization to unseen views and expressions.

## Results

On GAN-based self-reenactment, SynShot achieves an LPIPS of 0.0236, substantially outperforming InvertAvatar (0.0962), Portrait4D (0.0843), and Next3D (0.2274) using only 3 input images. In the monocular comparison (Figure 7), SynShot with as few as 3–13 training frames matches or exceeds the LPIPS and SSIM of INSTA, Flash Avatar, and Splatting Avatar trained on ~3000 frames, demonstrating that the synthetic prior compensates for the three-orders-of-magnitude data gap. The VQ-VAE ablation (Table 1) shows the best configuration (F=128) yields LPIPS 0.2686, SSIM 0.8189, and PSNR 20.15 on 50 held-out synthetic identities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three monocular baselines (INSTA [79], Flash Avatar [65], Splatting Avatar [54]), each trained on ~3000 frames, SynShot with only 3 input images produces cleaner novel-view and novel-expression synthesis, as the monocular methods overfit to their training viewpoint distribution and exhibit artifacts under cross-reenactment (Figure 5). Among GAN-based inversion methods, SynShot's LPIPS of 0.0236 is roughly 3.6× lower than InvertAvatar (0.0962), 3.6× lower than Portrait4D (0.0843), and nearly 10× lower than Next3D (0.2274) on self-reenactment. In cross-reenactment (Figure 9), SynShot best preserves the target subject's identity while faithfully transferring the source subject's pose and expression, whereas InvertAvatar, Portrait4D, and Next3D all introduce identity drift and side-view artifacts. The paper notes that monocular methods can appear competitive on self-reenactment with limited head motion (Figure 7), but their advantage vanishes under the more challenging cross-reenactment protocol where out-of-distribution expressions and views are tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynShot sits at the intersection of three established lines: (1) 3D Gaussian Splatting-based avatar representations (Kerbl et al., Saito et al., Qian et al.), (2) VQ-VAE generative modeling with transformer-based latent spaces (Van Den Oord et al., Esser et al.), and (3) pivotal tuning for domain adaptation (Roich et al., Lattas et al.). It departs from prior few-shot avatar methods (Preface, Cafca, GPHM, HeadGAP) by training the prior exclusively on synthetic data rendered via Blender Cycles, rather than on expensive real multi-view captures, and by explicitly disentangling identity and expression into separate quantized latent spaces. The part-based densification mechanism for Gaussian primitives extends the UV-space regression approach of Animatable Gaussians and GaussianAvatars with adaptive per-region primitive counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for VR/MR applications and privacy-regulated settings (GDPR), where capturing large real datasets is infeasible or legally constrained; a synthetic-only prior eliminates these barriers while still producing avatars that generalize to novel views and expressions from just three images. Key limitations remain: the synthetic dataset uses a single environment map (limiting lighting generalization), shares identical teeth geometry across all identities, and lacks diverse expression-dependent wrinkles. Open questions include whether the domain gap can be further narrowed with more diverse synthetic rendering (varied lighting, materials, expression-dependent skin deformation) and whether the pivotal tuning strategy generalizes to body avatars or other anatomical structures.
