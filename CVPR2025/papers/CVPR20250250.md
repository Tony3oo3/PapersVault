## Problem

Most optical flow methods are designed for ~1K resolution inputs and fail to generalize to high-resolution (4K–8K) images, relying on downscaling or input tiling that loses detail and global context. Additionally, no quantitative benchmark exists for systematically evaluating optical flow generalization across resolutions, as prior work only provided qualitative comparisons on a few hand-picked samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250250/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DPFlow takes a pair of RGB images at arbitrary spatial resolutions (1K through 8K, defined as 960n × 540n) and outputs a dense per-pixel optical flow field. The number of pyramid levels N is automatically determined at inference from the input diagonal size, so the same trained model handles all resolutions without retraining. Training uses only standard ~1K resolution samples from FlyingChairs, FlyingThings3D, KITTI 2015, HD1K, Sintel, and Spring.

## Method

DPFlow introduces a dual-pyramid bidirectional recurrent encoder that fuses an image pyramid (preserving direct access to input information at every level) with a feature pyramid (propagating multi-scale context across levels) via ConvGRU cells running in both forward and backward directions. The core building block is a Cross-Gated Unit (CGU), a pure convolutional module with self- and cross-gates, GELU non-linearity, and Layer Scale, which replaces attention mechanisms to avoid quadratic cost and resolution-dependent positional encoding. The decoder follows RAPIDFlow's iterative refinement structure, using a GRU with CGU as the refinement block. Training employs a multi-scale Mixture-of-Laplace loss over 3 scales and 4 refinement iterations per scale, and the pyramid depth is adaptively adjusted at inference to match the input resolution.

## Results

On the proposed Kubric-NK benchmark, DPFlow achieves EPE of 0.50 (1K), 0.85 (2K), 1.77 (4K), and 4.11 (8K), outperforming the next best method RAPIDFlow by 30% at 8K (4.11 vs. 5.96) and FlowFormer++ by 83% (4.11 vs. 24.4). On the Spring benchmark, DPFlow achieves 3.44 (1px) in the fine-tuning setting and 4.28 zero-shot, beating SEA-RAFT by 6.5% and RPKNet by 10.8%. On Middlebury-ST (3K), DPFlow reduces error by 21% over RAPIDFlow (5.03 vs. 6.41 EPE) and 66% over RPKNet (5.03 vs. 15.0). On standard-resolution benchmarks, DPFlow achieves the best average rank (2.7) across MPI-Sintel and KITTI 2015 public test sets, with 1.04 EPE on Sintel clean and 3.56 Fl-All on KITTI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPFlow outperforms all 26 compared baselines on the Kubric-NK 8K evaluation, beating RAPIDFlow (the closest adaptive-pyramid competitor) by 30% (4.11 vs. 5.96 EPE) and FlowFormer++ by 83% (4.11 vs. 24.4). On Spring 4K, DPFlow (6.03 1px) surpasses RAPIDFlow by 13% and SEA-RAFT by 27%, while on Middlebury-ST 3K it reduces error by 21% over RAPIDFlow and 66% over RPKNet. On standard-resolution MPI-Sintel and KITTI 2015 public benchmarks, DPFlow achieves the best average rank (2.7) among two-frame methods, edging out FlowDiffuser (3.3) and CCMR+ (5.0). In zero-shot generalization (Table 5), DPFlow leads on KITTI (3.37 EPE, 11.1 Fl-All) but trails FlowDiffuser by 3% on Sintel final pass (2.26 vs. 2.19). Notably, many top-performing transformer-based methods (FlowFormer++, MatchFlow, CroCo-Flow) either run out of memory or degrade severely at 4K/8K due to their reliance on input tiling, while fixed-pyramid methods like SEA-RAFT show exponential error growth (0.53→51.8 EPE from 1K to 8K). DPFlow's dual-pyramid design maintains near-linear error scaling with resolution, making it the only method to produce reliable predictions across all four Kubric-NK resolutions without tiling.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPFlow extends the recurrent encoder-decoder lineage established by RAPIDFlow (ICRA 2024) and RPKNet (AAAI 2024), both from the same group, by introducing a dual-pyramid encoder that unifies image and feature pyramids in a bidirectional ConvGRU structure. It deliberately departs from the dominant RAFT paradigm (single-scale features + iterative decoding) and from transformer-based methods (FlowFormer, FlowFormer++) that rely on input tiling for high-resolution inputs, instead using a pure convolutional Cross-Gated Unit to avoid the quadratic cost and resolution-sensitivity of attention. The CGU design draws on Gated-CNNs but adapts them with cross-gating for paired-image optical flow, positioning the method as a CNN-based alternative to the increasingly transformer-heavy field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Kubric-NK benchmark is the first to provide quantitative, densely-annotated evaluation of optical flow methods across 1K–8K resolutions, exposing that most existing methods (especially RAFT variants and tiling-based transformers) degrade exponentially with resolution — a gap previously visible only through a few qualitative examples. The finding that adaptive recurrent pyramids are the key architectural ingredient for resolution generalization, and that a pure CNN can outperform transformer-based methods at 8K, offers a clear design principle for future high-resolution flow work. Open questions remain: the benchmark is synthetic and does not test adverse conditions (fog, lighting, fine texture) at high resolution, and the method still underperforms on MPI-Sintel's clean pass, suggesting room for improvement in standard-resolution accuracy.
