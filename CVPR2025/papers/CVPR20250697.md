## Problem

Anomaly segmentation in autonomous driving has been extensively studied in 2D, but 3D LiDAR-based anomaly segmentation remains underexplored. Existing 3D anomaly benchmarks either use low-resolution LiDAR (16-beam), rely on validation sets where anomalous objects may overlap with training data, or are proprietary and inaccessible. No publicly available dataset combines high-resolution 128-beam LiDAR with synchronized surround-view cameras, dense point-level annotations, and a strict separation between in-distribution training and out-of-distribution test objects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-scan 128-beam LiDAR point clouds (~100,000 points per scan) from a vehicle-mounted sensor, with 8 synchronized surround-view RGB cameras available for multimodal extensions. Outputs include point-level anomaly scores (evaluated via AUROC, FPR@95, AP), instance-level segmentation masks for each anomaly object, and semantic class labels for inlier objects. Evaluation is constrained to a 50-meter radius (matching SemanticKITTI) and requires a minimum of 5 LiDAR points per anomaly instance, reflecting the radial resolution degradation of LiDAR at range.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the STU dataset (70 annotated sequences: 51 test, 19 validation, plus 2 inlier sequences), collected with a 128-beam LiDAR and 8 cameras in both naturalistic and controlled settings, with point-level semantic and instance labels for anomalies. For baseline evaluation, five 2D anomaly segmentation methods are adapted to 3D LiDAR using Mask4Former-3D as the shared backbone: Deep Ensembles (3 independently trained models), MC Dropout (50 stochastic forward passes with dropout in the transformer decoder), MaxLogit (single forward pass using maximum logit as anomaly score), Void Classifier (treating unlabeled regions as an additional training class), and RbA (exploiting the query deactivation mechanism of MaskFormer-style models to score regions with no predicted mask). All models are jointly trained on SemanticKITTI, Panoptic-CUDAL, and STU-inlier sequences. A key empirical finding is that all baselines confidently misclassify anomalies as inlier classes (e.g., a chair predicted as 'other-vehicle'), revealing a substantial performance gap between 2D and 3D anomaly segmentation that motivates future research.

## Results

On the STU dataset, Deep Ensemble achieves the best anomaly segmentation performance with AUROC 86.74, FPR@95 58.05, and AP 5.17 at the point level, and PQ 8.81 at the object level. All baselines struggle severely with OOD detection, with AP values ranging from 0.11 (MC Dropout) to 5.17 (Deep Ensemble), indicating that 3D LiDAR anomaly segmentation remains an open challenge. For inlier performance, jointly trained Mask4Former-3D achieves PQ 52.7 on STU-inlier (vs. 42.8 when trained on SemanticKITTI alone), confirming a small domain gap between the datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deep Ensemble [34] dominates all other baselines on the STU dataset, achieving the highest AUROC (86.74 vs. 61.51–85.99), the lowest FPR@95 (58.05 vs. 78.60–100.0), the highest AP (5.17 vs. 0.11–3.92), and the best object-level PQ (8.81 vs. 1.77–6.91). Void Classifier [4] is the second-best method (AUROC 85.99, AP 3.92, PQ 6.91) and is the only baseline that uses OOD data during training. Max Logit [24] achieves the highest RecallQ (26.14) and UQ (21.71), indicating it detects the most anomaly objects, but at the cost of very high false positives (FPR@95 81.49, PQ only 1.77). MC Dropout [50] and RbA [41] perform worst overall, with AUROC below 67 and AP below 1, confirming that uncertainty-based and mask-rejection strategies transfer poorly from 2D to 3D LiDAR. The paper highlights that unlike in 2D where Max Logit outperforms Deep Ensembles, the 3D setting reverses this ranking, and all methods exhibit high confidence in misclassifying anomalies as inliers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D anomaly segmentation line of work (Fishyscapes, CODA, Lost and Found) into the 3D LiDAR domain, adapting MaskFormer-style architectures (Mask4Former-3D, MaskPLS) and uncertainty-based scoring methods (Deep Ensembles, MC Dropout, MaxLogit, RbA) from image segmentation to point clouds. It departs from prior 3D anomaly efforts (SOD, TOR4D, Rare4D) by providing the first publicly accessible dataset with high-resolution 128-beam LiDAR, dense per-point semantic and instance annotations, and a surround-view camera setup, rather than relying on validation sets of existing datasets or proprietary data. The authors explicitly contrast their anomaly segmentation formulation with open-set segmentation, arguing that open-set methods' reliance on unknown instances during training makes them unsuitable for the strict ID/OOD split required here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap: no prior public benchmark offered dense 3D LiDAR anomaly segmentation with modern AV-grade sensors, making it a necessary resource for evaluating and comparing future 3D anomaly detection methods. The key empirical finding—that all adapted 2D baselines fail to detect OOD objects (AP < 6, FPR@95 > 58) and confidently misclassify anomalies as inliers—highlights a substantial unsolved problem and motivates genuinely new 3D-specific approaches. Open questions include whether temporal LiDAR fusion, multimodal camera-LiDAR integration, or 3D-native uncertainty estimation can close this gap, and whether the staged-data protocol generalizes to truly naturalistic anomaly distributions.
