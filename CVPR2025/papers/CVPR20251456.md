## Problem

Estimating hand pressure and pose from egocentric cameras is critical for AR/VR and robotics, yet no dataset provides both high-resolution pressure annotations and accurate 3D hand meshes from a first-person perspective. Prior pressure datasets (PressureVision, PressureVision++) rely solely on exocentric static cameras and lack hand pose/mesh labels, while existing hand-object interaction datasets infer contact from pose without measuring actual pressure forces.

## Input / Output

The benchmark models take a single RGB image (1920×1080 egocentric or 2560×1440 exocentric) as input, optionally augmented with 21 2.5D hand keypoints (3D joints projected to the image plane with z-depth scaled to millimeters). The image-projected baselines output a 2D pressure map on the touchpad surface, while PressureFormer outputs a quantized UV pressure map (C force classes) on the MANO hand mesh, which can be rendered back to the image plane or interpreted as 3D pressure on the hand surface. The dataset itself provides synchronized RGB-D frames at 30 fps from 7 static + 1 head-mounted Azure Kinect cameras plus ground-truth pressure from a Sensel Morph touchpad.

## Method

The annotation pipeline uses a two-stage multi-view optimization over 7 static cameras: (1) POSE OPTIMIZATION refines MANO pose θ and translation t via DIB-R differentiable rendering loss plus a mesh intersection loss for occluded fingers, and (2) SHAPE REFINEMENT adds per-vertex displacement along normals and optimizes a pressure UV texture map T_P using a virtual orthogonal camera placed under the touchpad, which enforces both pressure alignment (MSE between rendered and ground-truth pressure) and physical contact (z-depth matching at contact vertices). For the PressureFormer model, HaMeR's ViT extracts image feature tokens and hand vertices V_hand; a transformer decoder cross-attends V_hand tokens to image features, producing per-vertex features that are mapped to UV coordinates and interpolated via two convolutional layers into C force-class channels. Training combines a coarse UV-pressure cross-entropy loss with a rendered image-plane pressure loss (computed by inverting face normals to identify occluded/contact faces), enabling joint estimation of hand pose and pressure distribution in 3D.

## Results

On EgoPressure (all views), PressureFormer achieves 43.04% Image Contact IoU and 33.12% UV Pressure Contact IoU, outperforming PressureVisionNet (40.71% and 21.53%) and the HaMeR-augmented baseline (42.52% and 24.10%). On the UV pressure map, PressureFormer also leads in Volumetric IoU (24.54% vs. 16.41% and 17.36%). For cross-dataset generalization to PressureVision, PressureFormer dramatically outperforms PressureVisionNet, achieving 29.03% Contact IoU vs. 7.54% and 21.71% Vol. IoU vs. 7.11%. However, PressureFormer lags on Image Volumetric IoU (31.57% vs. 35.40% for the HaMeR-augmented baseline) and MAE (71 kPa vs. 44 kPa), indicating the UV-space model trades some image-plane fidelity for 3D consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is PressureVisionNet [19], an encoder-decoder segmentation model that predicts 2D pressure maps from RGB images alone. PressureFormer clearly wins on UV-space metrics: it achieves 33.12% UV Contact IoU vs. 21.53% for PressureVisionNet (a 11.6-point gain) and 24.54% UV Vol. IoU vs. 16.41% (an 8.1-point gain), confirming that predicting pressure directly on the hand mesh yields substantially better 3D pressure localization. On image-plane Contact IoU, PressureFormer also leads (43.04% vs. 40.71%), but it loses on Image Volumetric IoU (31.57% vs. 32.11%) and MAE (71 vs. 44 kPa), suggesting the UV-space formulation sacrifices some pixel-level pressure magnitude accuracy. The HaMeR-augmented variant of PressureVisionNet [19] narrows the gap on image-plane metrics (35.40% Vol. IoU, 49 kPa MAE) but still trails PressureFormer on UV-space scores. Most strikingly, on the held-out PressureVision dataset, PressureFormer generalizes far better (29.03% Contact IoU, 79% Temporal Accuracy) compared to PressureVisionNet (7.54%, 55%), demonstrating that the UV-map representation transfers across capture setups more effectively than image-plane pressure prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PressureVision/PressureVision++ line of work, which estimated 2D pressure maps from exocentric RGB images, by shifting to an egocentric perspective and representing pressure as a 3D UV texture on a MANO hand mesh rather than on the image plane. For hand pose annotation, it follows the two-stage optimization paradigm of HARP (pose optimization then shape refinement) with differentiable rendering (DIB-R), and uses HaMeR as the backbone for initial pose estimation and feature extraction in the PressureFormer model. The key conceptual departure is the virtual orthogonal camera placed beneath the touchpad, which enables pressure to be optimized directly as a mesh texture feature, grounding the pressure annotation in 3D hand geometry rather than 2D pixel space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoPressure is the first bare-handed egocentric dataset providing synchronized pressure maps and 3D hand meshes, filling a critical gap for AR/VR and robotic manipulation research where understanding touch from the user's own viewpoint is essential. The demonstrated benefit of incorporating hand pose as input (5–7% volumetric IoU improvement) and the PressureFormer's strong generalization to the PressureVision test set (~4× Contact IoU over the original method) suggest that 3D hand-centric pressure representation is a more robust formulation than image-plane prediction. However, the dataset is limited to flat surfaces, single-hand interactions, and indoor settings, leaving open the much harder problem of pressure estimation on arbitrary 3D objects and in dual-hand scenarios.
