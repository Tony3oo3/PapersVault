## Problem

Existing composed image retrieval methods struggle with complex, multi-attribute text modifications because global contrastive pretraining (e.g., CLIP) does not ensure fine-grained alignment between individual text concepts and their corresponding image regions. When a query text specifies multiple changes (e.g., adding an object while removing background elements), current models retrieve results correlated with only some of the specified concepts rather than capturing the full image-text relationship.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251541/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a query image and a free-form relative text modification (which can be multi-attribute and arbitrarily long) and produces a single vision-language embedding by cross-attending text tokens to image tokens followed by Set Transformer attention pooling. Gallery images are pre-indexed by encoding them with an empty text string, so retrieval reduces to cosine similarity between the query embedding and pre-computed gallery embeddings. Noun phrases are extracted from the text via Stanza constituency parsing (up to 10 per sentence, prioritizing branch-level over leaf-level NPs) and are used only during training for the CC loss, not at inference time.

## Method

ConText-CIR pairs CLIP image and text encoders with a cross-attention fusion layer and Set Transformer attention pooling, trained with two losses: a standard contrastive loss (with the query image encoded under empty text as a hard negative, following MagicLens) and a novel Text Concept-Consistency (CC) loss. The CC loss extracts noun phrases from the modification text, computes the cross-attention map of each NP in the context of the full sentence versus in isolation, and applies a ReLU penalty (with slack ε) to the difference, forcing in-context attention to match isolated concept attention. This prevents contextual interference where one concept's tokens suppress or confuse another's grounding. The paper also introduces a GPT-4o-based synthetic data pipeline that decomposes triplet generation into object detection, descriptor matching, difference captioning, and multi-attribute concatenation steps, producing richer training data from existing image pairs or new domains.

## Results

On the CIRR test set (supervised), ConText-CIR with a ViT-H backbone achieves R@1=55.24 and R@1_subset=82.96, outperforming the previous best (CoVR-2, ViT-G) by 4.81 and 6.21 points respectively, while using a smaller backbone (ViT-H vs. ViT-G) and no 4.9M-sample pretraining. In the zero-shot CIRR setting, ConText-CIR improves R@1 by 4.78 (ViT-B), 5.38 (ViT-L), and 12.88 (ViT-H) over prior state-of-the-art. On the zero-shot CIRCO benchmark, ConText-CIR achieves mAP@5=31.74 (ViT-H) and mAP@5=30.05 (ViT-L), surpassing all baselines including those using the much larger ViT-G backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConText-CIR outperforms all 16 baselines across every backbone size and evaluation setting. On supervised CIRR (Table 1), it beats the strongest ViT-G baseline CoVR-2 [58] by 4.81 R@1 (55.24 vs. 50.43) and 6.21 R@1_subset (82.96 vs. 76.75) using a smaller ViT-H backbone without 4.9M-sample pretraining; at ViT-L it surpasses CoVR-BLIP [59] by 2.96 R@1 (52.65 vs. 49.69). In the zero-shot CIRR setting (Table 2), gains are even larger: 4.78 R@1 over CASE [30] at ViT-B (40.18 vs. 35.40), 5.38 over CoVR-BLIP [59] at ViT-L (43.86 vs. 38.48), and 12.88 over CoVR-2 [58] at ViT-H (46.71 vs. 43.74). On zero-shot CIRCO (Table 3), ConText-CIR leads by 2.14 mAP@5 over MagicLens [72] at ViT-H (31.74 vs. 29.6) and by 6.70 mAP@5 over LDRE [69] at ViT-L (30.05 vs. 23.35). Notably, training-free methods (CIReVL [26], LDRE [69], TFCIR [57]) and textual-inversion methods (SEARLE [4], Pic2Word [47], LinCIR [16]) are all substantially outperformed, with gaps of 10–20+ R@1 points on CIRR, indicating that the Text CC loss and synthetic data pipeline provide a clear advantage over parameter-free or inversion-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConText-CIR builds directly on the CLIP/OpenCLIP encoder stack and the cross-attention fusion paradigm established by methods like MagicLens (from which it adopts the query-image-as-hard-negative strategy) and the broader CIR literature (CIRPLANT, CLIP4CIR, CompoDiff, CoVR-2). Its key conceptual departure is the Text Concept-Consistency loss, which uses linguistically-motivated noun-phrase extraction (via Stanza) to enforce that in-context cross-attention for each concept matches its isolated cross-attention — a structured, fine-grained grounding signal that prior contrastive or interpolation-based CIR methods lack. The paper also introduces a GPT-4o-based synthetic data pipeline (good4cir) that departs from both manual annotation (CIRR) and diffusion-model-based generation (CompoDiff/ST18M) by decomposing triplet generation into targeted sub-tasks to reduce hallucination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CC loss provides a simple, interpretable mechanism for improving concept-level visual grounding in CIR without increasing inference-time complexity, and the ablation (55.24 → 48.92 R@1) confirms it is the primary driver of the SOTA gains. The synthetic data pipeline is practically reusable for generating multi-attribute CIR annotations in new domains, as demonstrated by the Hotel-CIR dataset. Open questions include whether the CC loss generalizes beyond CLIP-family backbones, whether it transfers to composed video retrieval, and whether the reliance on constituency parsing (Stanza) limits applicability to non-English or highly informal queries. The zero-shot setting results suggest the learned grounding is robust, but the evaluation is still confined to relatively small-scale benchmarks (CIRR, CIRCO) and has not been stress-tested on truly large-scale retrieval.
