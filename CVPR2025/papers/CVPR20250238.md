## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models encode powerful image priors but require hundreds to thousands of reverse diffusion steps for reconstruction, performing poorly at low compressive sensing ratios. Deep Unfolding Networks (DUNs) reduce iteration counts but rely on learned priors that are weaker than those in diffusion models. The paper asks whether the strong prior of a pre-trained diffusion model can be embedded directly into a DUN framework to achieve high-quality CS reconstruction in as few as 2 steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250238/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on block-based CS: the image is divided into non-overlapping 64×64 blocks (N=4096 per block), each flattened to a 1D vector, with M=⌊δN⌋ Gaussian measurements per block at CS ratios δ∈{1%,4%,10%,25%,50%}. The input is the measurement vector y and sensing matrix Φ; the output is the reconstructed Y-channel image. The pre-trained diffusion model operates in RGB space, so a tail ResBlock bridges the RGB intermediate representation back to the single-channel Y output. Training uses 64×64 patches from COCO2017 in YCbCr space (Y-channel only), with the diffusion model pre-trained on 256×256 ImageNet images.

## Method

The core contribution is the Diffusion Message Passing (DMP) algorithm, which modifies AMP by replacing the denoiser η_t with one step of the reverse diffusion process p_θ(x_{t-1}|r_t), yielding an iterative formula where each step performs a gradient-descent update, a Gaussian-filter/Onsager correction, and a diffusion denoising step. DMP-DUN deeply unfolds DMP into a network where lightweight ResBlocks replace both the Gaussian filter D_t and the Monte-Carlo SURE divergence computation (saving ~512G FLOPs), a head ResBlock maps Φ^T y directly to the diffusion state at time K=100 (skipping the initial T−K forward diffusion steps), and a learnable λ_t substitutes for √ᾱ_t to increase fault tolerance. The state evolution equation (Eq. 6) proves that DMP preserves data manifolds throughout reconstruction, explaining its quality advantage. Training is end-to-end with MSE loss; DMP-DUN freezes the diffusion model while DMP-DUN⁺ unfreezes it for further quality gains at no additional inference cost.

## Results

On Set11, DMP-DUN⁺(4-step) achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 33.26/0.8713 across CS ratios of 1%–50%, outperforming the best non-diffusion baseline PRL-RND⁺ (31.87/0.8603) by 1.39 dB while using 258.8 G fewer FLOPs (303.4 G vs. 562.2 G). The 2-step variant DMP-DUN⁺(2-step) still reaches 32.74/0.8667, surpassing CPP-Net (31.69/0.8613) by 1.05 dB at comparable FLOPs (157.0 G vs. 153.5 G). On Urban100, DMP-DUN⁺(4-step) gains 2.15 dB over PRL-RND⁺. Compared to diffusion-based baselines, DMP-DUN⁺(4-step) improves over DDNM⁺(1000-step) by 8.64 dB average PSNR while requiring only 4 steps instead of 1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMP-DUN⁺(4-step) achieves the best average PSNR across all 12 baselines on Set11, beating the strongest non-diffusion DUN baseline PRL-RND⁺ by 1.39 dB (33.26 vs. 31.87) while using 46% fewer FLOPs (303.4 G vs. 562.2 G). Against CPP-Net (CVPR'24), the closest FLOPs-matched competitor, DMP-DUN⁺(4-step) gains 1.57 dB at 2× the FLOPs, while DMP-DUN⁺(2-step) gains 1.05 dB at nearly identical FLOPs (157.0 G vs. 153.5 G). The diffusion-based baseline DDNM⁺(1000-step) trails by 8.64 dB in average PSNR and requires 221× more FLOPs (67039.1 G vs. 303.4 G); DDNM(10-step) is even worse at 16.04 dB average. On Urban100, DMP-DUN⁺(4-step) outperforms PRL-RND⁺ by 2.15 dB and CPP-Net by 2.11 dB. Even without pre-trained diffusion weights, DMP-DUN⁺(4-step) retains a 0.42 dB advantage over PRL-RND⁺, confirming the algorithmic contribution is independent of the prior. The 2-step variant demonstrates that the DMP unfolding can compress the reverse diffusion process from 1000 steps to just 2 iterations with minimal quality loss (0.52 dB gap to the 4-step version).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established research threads: Deep Unfolding Networks (DUNs) that unfold iterative CS algorithms like AMP into trainable networks, and pre-trained diffusion models that encode powerful image priors but require hundreds to thousands of reverse-diffusion steps for inverse problems. The key conceptual move is deriving a new iterative algorithm (DMP) by replacing the denoiser in AMP with a single reverse-diffusion step, then unfolding DMP into a network where lightweight ResBlocks approximate the Onsager term and Gaussian filter that would otherwise require Monte-Carlo SURE. This departs from prior diffusion-based CS methods (DDNM, DPS, MPGD) that treat the diffusion model as a black-box sampler requiring many steps, and from prior DUNs that learn their own priors end-to-end without leveraging pre-trained generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DMP-DUN+ achieves SOTA reconstruction with only 2–4 DMP steps (vs. 1000+ for DDNM+), and the ResBlock replacement of Monte-Carlo SURE saves ~512G FLOPs per 4-step run. The state evolution analysis showing manifold preservation throughout reconstruction provides a theoretical justification absent in prior diffusion-based CS work. Limitations include the block-based (64×64) formulation, reliance on a specific ImageNet-pretrained model, and evaluation only on the Y-channel; open questions include whether the approach extends to newer diffusion architectures (e.g., latent diffusion, flow matching), larger image sizes without blocking, and other linear inverse problems beyond CS.
