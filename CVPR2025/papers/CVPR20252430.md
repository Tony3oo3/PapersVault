## Problem

Directly applying CLIP's contrastive learning to chest X-ray (CXR) data faces two medical-specific challenges largely unaddressed by prior adaptations: (1) severe data imbalance from template-based duplicate reports and the predominance of normal cases, which creates semantic overlap within batches that conflicts with the contrastive objective, and (2) frequent negations in radiology reports (e.g., 'No pneumothorax') that CLIP's bag-of-words tendency fails to interpret correctly. This paper is the first to tackle both issues simultaneously at a global-feature scale within a CLIP training pipeline.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 chest X-ray images and free-text radiology reports, with a third modality derived by extracting RadGraph entity-relation graphs from each report. The image encoder is Swin-Tiny, the text encoder is BioClinical-BERT, and a 2-layer GCNN (with ClinicalBERT node embeddings) produces graph embeddings. Outputs are aligned embeddings across all three modalities, used downstream for zero-shot/fine-tuned entity classification, report retrieval (Top-5 with CheXbert F1), adversarial prediction, normal-case detection, and the novel CXR-Align benchmark where the model must discriminate between an original report, a negated variant, and an entity-removed variant.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces CLIP's hard one-hot InfoNCE targets with dynamically generated soft labels: intra-modal self-similarity matrices (text-text, CheXbert clinical-label, and graph-graph) are computed within each batch, thresholded to suppress noise from minimally similar pairs, row-normalized, and used as KL-divergence targets against the cross-modal logit distributions. To handle negations, the pipeline synthesizes hard-negative reports by removing a randomly selected positive entity and/or inserting a predefined negation, then concatenates these with original embeddings to form 2B-sized batches so that near-duplicate and negated reports are explicitly contrasted. Graph embeddings from RadGraph + GCNN capture relational structure (location, severity, entity co-occurrence) that CheXbert's 14 binary labels cannot, providing a third soft-label stream. The final loss sums weighted KL-divergence terms over all cross-modal pairs (image↔text, image↔graph, text↔graph), each conditioned on its respective soft-label distribution, yielding a unified contrastive objective that is robust to batch-level semantic overlap while sharpening negation discrimination.

## Results

The full model (CLIP^{N,G}-D_{t+c+g}) achieves 86.6% zero-shot AUC on RSNA-Pneumonia and 91.1% at FT100, surpassing all baselines in fine-tuned settings. On SIIM Pneumothorax, it reaches 87.2% ZS and 90.2% FT100 AUC, the best among all compared methods. For report retrieval on MIMIC, it attains 38.8% Top-5 accuracy and 50.6% CheXbert F1, outperforming the CLIP baseline (35.5% / 44.8%) and BIOVIL (10.9% / 36.4%). On the novel CXR-Align benchmark, the model scores 96.5% (negation task) and 80.1% (entity-removal task) on MIMIC, substantially exceeding CXR-CLIP (78.3% / 73.6%) and CLIP (75.4% / 72.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest baseline BIOVIL-T [3], the proposed model loses slightly on RSNA zero-shot AUC (86.6 vs. 87.8) but clearly wins on fine-tuned RSNA (91.1 vs. 89.2) and dominates on SIIM (87.2 vs. 74.9 ZS; 90.2 vs. 87.9 FT100), indicating richer transferable representations. Compared to CXR-CLIP [38], the method gains +5.2 points on RSNA ZS (86.6 vs. 81.4) and +1.5 on SIIM FT100 (90.2 vs. 88.7). Over the plain CLIP baseline [26], improvements are consistent across all datasets, with the largest gap on SIIM ZS (+12.9) and CXR14 ZS (+6.3). SoftCLIP [12] and ConVIRT [40] are substantially outperformed on every metric, with SoftCLIP trailing by 10+ points on RSNA ZS. On the CXR-Align negation benchmark, the full model (96.5% / 80.1% on MIMIC) surpasses CXR-CLIP (78.3% / 73.6%) and CLIP (75.4% / 72.4%) by a wide margin, and on adversarial prediction it reaches 34.4% accuracy versus 23.3% for BIOVIL and 12.0% for GLORIA, the next-best baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical CLIP lineage (ConViRT, BioViL, CXR-CLIP, GLORIA, MedKlip, MLIP) by targeting two underexplored failure modes of contrastive learning in radiology: semantic overlap from templated/normal reports and negation comprehension. It departs from SoftCLIP and Pyramid-CLIP, which relax contrastive loss via intra-modal text similarity alone, by incorporating CheXbert clinical labels and RadGraph graph embeddings as additional soft-label sources, and from CoNCLIP/SUGARCREPE (general-domain negation work) by generating negation-based hard negatives specifically within the medical contrastive training loop. The CXR-Align benchmark is positioned as the first evaluation suite dedicated to negation and clinical-entity alignment in CXR reports, filling a gap left by existing retrieval and classification benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that medical contrastive training can be made more stable and clinically faithful by combining thresholded multi-source soft labels with negation hard negatives—a recipe that is straightforward to bolt onto existing CLIP pipelines. The CXR-Align benchmark and the adversarial-prediction task (where all prior SOTA models score below chance) provide the community with sharper diagnostic tools for clinical language understanding. Open questions remain: the method is limited to single-image settings (no temporal/interval-change reasoning), CheXbert's 14 labels do not cover all clinical entities, and the authors themselves flag that near-perfect negation-detection scores (≈97%) may reflect shortcut learning rather than genuine comprehension. Refining RadGraph to encode location, severity, and size, and exploring compositional text encoders, are identified as natural next steps.
