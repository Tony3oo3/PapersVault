## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inpainting methods either add conditioning modules or hijack the reverse process with elaborate nested loops (e.g., RePaint's repeated resampling), resulting in significantly extended inference times and added architectural complexity. No prior approach reformulates the diffusion framework itself to natively support localized, asynchronous generation of image regions without extra components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250768/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

RAD takes a 256×256 RGB image with a binary inpainting mask (box, extreme, or wide configurations) and produces a fully inpainted 256×256 image using 100 reverse diffusion steps. During training, masks are not provided externally but are automatically generated from thresholded Perlin noise at varying spatial scales, covering diverse naturalistic inpainting patterns. The known (non-masked) region is kept clean throughout the reverse process while only the masked region undergoes denoising.

## Method

RAD reformulates DDPM by assigning each pixel its own noise schedule b_{t,i}, so that different spatial regions can be at different noise levels simultaneously, enabling asynchronous generation. The forward process is an element-wise Gaussian with pixel-specific variance, and the reverse denoiser ε_θ(x_t, t) takes the full image as input to capture global context. Two key design choices make this work: (1) a two-phase noise schedule where Phase 1 adds noise only to the mask region and Phase 2 to the rest, with masks generated from Perlin noise for training diversity; and (2) a spatial noise embedding that replaces the standard scalar timestep embedding with pixel-wise b̄_t values (converting FC layers to 1×1 convolutions), directly informing the network of local noise intensity. Training is performed via LoRA fine-tuning on pretrained ADM models with rank-16 adapters, reducing computational cost while the inverse-mapping of b̄_t to DDPM-compatible timesteps keeps the modification mild enough for low-rank adaptation.

## Results

RAD achieves the best FID and LPIPS scores across all 9 evaluation settings (3 datasets × 3 mask types). On FFHQ, RAD obtains FID 22.1 (Box), 33.4 (Extreme), and 21.5 (Wide) with LPIPS 0.074, 0.317, and 0.078 respectively. On LSUN Bedroom, RAD reaches FID 19.2 (Box), 21.6 (Extreme), and 20.8 (Wide). On ImageNet, RAD achieves FID 47.0 (Box), 57.8 (Extreme), and 56.7 (Wide). Inference time is 8.44 s on a single TITAN RTX, approximately 100× faster than RePaint (837 s) and 15× faster than MCG (128 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAD outperforms all seven baselines on FID across every dataset and mask configuration. The closest competitor is MCG, which trails RAD by 1.4–1.6 FID points on FFHQ and by 0.7–1.1 on LSUN Bedroom and ImageNet; RePaint is a further 3–4 FID points behind RAD on FFHQ. On LPIPS, RAD also achieves the best score in 8 of 9 settings (tied with MCG on LSUN Bedroom Box at 0.131). DDNM and DeqIR, though faster than RAD in inference (6.6 s and 26.4 s vs. 8.4 s), produce substantially worse FID (e.g., 87.7 vs. 33.4 on FFHQ Extreme), as they are designed for image restoration rather than large-area inpainting. In terms of speed, RAD is ~100× faster than RePaint (8.4 s vs. 837 s) and ~15× faster than MCG (8.4 s vs. 128 s) while delivering superior quality, making it the only method that combines state-of-the-art inpainting quality with a plain single-pass reverse process.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAD repositions diffusion-based inpainting by reformulating the forward process rather than manipulating the reverse process, departing from the dominant paradigm of methods like RePaint, MCG, and DDRM that 'hijack' pretrained reverse steps with resampling or nested loops. Conceptually, it builds directly on DDPM's element-wise Gaussian transitions and ADM's architecture, but replaces the uniform noise assumption with spatially variant per-pixel noise schedules, making asynchronous regional generation an inherent property of the diffusion process. The use of LoRA for efficient fine-tuning on pretrained ADMs borrows from parameter-efficient adaptation in NLP, and Perlin noise for mask generation draws from procedural texture synthesis. The spatial noise embedding (replacing the scalar timestep embedding with a pixel-wise noise intensity map) is a minimal architectural change—converting FC layers to 1×1 convolutions—that the authors show is critical for the reformulation to work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAD's 100× inference speedup over RePaint and 15× over MCG while matching or exceeding their quality makes high-quality diffusion-based inpainting practically deployable in settings where iterative resampling is prohibitive. The framework's generality—applicable to DDIM, iDDPM, ADM, and score-based models—suggests spatially variant noise schedules could become a standard tool for any task requiring asynchronous or region-selective generation. Key limitations remain: the method still requires training (even with LoRA), performance depends on the mask distribution seen during training, and the authors acknowledge that in extreme inpainting scenarios, slower methods like RePaint may still outperform. Extending RAD to text-conditioned editing and other spatially variant generation tasks is identified as a natural next step.
