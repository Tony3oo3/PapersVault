## Problem

Generating diverse Implicit Neural Representation (INR) weights from only a few examples is hindered by two compounding issues: the curse of dimensionality in high-dimensional weight space, and the fact that functionally equivalent INRs can differ drastically in individual weight values due to neuron permutations, violating the element-wise similarity assumption underlying standard few-shot transfer. Existing weight-generation methods either struggle with dimensionality directly or use autoencoders that ignore the inherent permutation symmetry of weight space, leading to poor generalization and mode collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small support set of k (1–10) INR weight checkpoints from an unseen target category, plus a larger source set from seen categories used for pre-training. INRs are 3-layer MLPs (SIREN with widths 32/64 for 2D images, standard ReLU MLP with width 128 and positional encoding for 3D shapes). The output is a set of generated INR weight vectors that, when evaluated, produce diverse yet class-consistent 2D images or 3D isosurfaces (extracted via Marching Cubes). The generated weights are full flattened parameter vectors of the same architecture as the input INRs.

## Method

EQUIGEN is a three-stage framework built around the principle that permuting neurons within a layer (and reversing in the next) preserves network function, forming equivariance groups. Stage 1 pre-trains an equivariant encoder (four equivariant layers, 128-dim output) via SimCLR-style contrastive learning, where positive pairs are created by smooth augmentation—solving a multi-objective Shortest Hamiltonian Path problem per weight-matrix clique to find permutations that minimize total variation—plus stochastic INR-based augmentations (rotation, translation, scaling, color jitter, bias perturbation). Stage 2 trains a transformer-based diffusion model (DDIM, 1000 steps, squared-cosine schedule) that denoises flattened weight vectors conditioned on equivariant features via cross-attention, optimized with an MSE reconstruction loss plus an equivariance regularization term (L2 distance between equivariant features of generated and target weights, weighted by λ=0.1). Stage 3 performs few-shot adaptation by fine-tuning the diffusion model on the k support samples and, at generation time, adding controlled Gaussian noise (γ=0.3) to the equivariant features of each support sample to perturb the conditioning, thereby sampling diverse points within the equivariance group while preserving functional consistency.

## Results

On 2D image INR generation (10-shot), EQUIGEN achieves FID 121.24 and LPIPS 0.4133 on MNIST-INRs, and FID 164.14 and LPIPS 0.4926 on CIFAR-10-INRs, outperforming all baselines on both quality and diversity metrics. On 3D shape INR generation (10-shot, ShapeNet), EQUIGEN achieves the best MMD (3.4/3.5/4.2), COV (35%/31%/41%), and 1-NNA (73.0%/76.5%/67.1%) across the airplane, car, and chair categories respectively, surpassing all competing methods on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EQUIGEN consistently outperforms all seven baselines across both 2D and 3D evaluation scenarios. On 2D images, it reduces FID by 26–61 points over the best competing method (e.g., 121.24 vs. 149.77 for DAGAN on MNIST; 164.14 vs. 186.58 for HyperDiffusion on CIFAR-10) while simultaneously achieving the highest LPIPS diversity (0.4133 vs. 0.2727 on MNIST; 0.4926 vs. 0.3104 on CIFAR-10). On 3D shapes, EQUIGEN achieves the lowest MMD across all three ShapeNet categories (3.4 airplane, 3.5 car, 4.2 chair), beating the next-best HyperDiffusion by 1.6–3.0 points, and the highest COV (35%/31%/41% vs. 33%/22%/28% for HyperDiffusion). The modality-based baselines (PVD, DPC for 3D; FIGR, DAGAN, DAWSON for 2D) are substantially outperformed, confirming that operating directly in INR weight space with equivariance-aware modeling is more effective than generating in the original signal domain. Among INR-based methods, HyperDiffusion is the strongest competitor but lacks equivariance structure, while INR2Vec's autoencoder-based approach fails to capture weight-space symmetries, resulting in lower diversity (COV) and higher MMD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the equivariant architecture for deep weight spaces (Navon et al., ICML 2023) as its encoder backbone, adapts SimCLR-style contrastive learning (Chen et al., ICML 2020) to the weight domain, and adopts the weight-space diffusion paradigm from HyperDiffusion (Erkoç et al., CVPR 2023) and Peebles et al. (2022) for the generative stage. Its key departure is the systematic exploitation of permutation equivariance as the organizing principle for few-shot generation: rather than treating INR weights as generic high-dimensional vectors (as prior autoencoder or direct-diffusion approaches do), EQUIGEN projects them into an equivariant latent space where functionally equivalent weights cluster together, then performs controlled perturbations within that subspace. The smooth augmentation via TSP-based permutation optimization is a novel preprocessing step not present in prior weight-space generative work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in INR research: collecting large-scale trained INR checkpoints is expensive, and few-shot weight generation could unlock weight-space learning from limited data. The conceptual contribution—showing that equivariance (a structural symmetry already known in the weight-space literature) is the missing ingredient for effective few-shot generation rather than simply more data or better generative models—is a useful reframing. However, the evaluation is limited to 3-layer MLPs with modest widths (32–128) and small datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, three ShapeNet categories), leaving open whether the approach scales to deeper or wider INRs, to non-MLP architectures, or to the 1–10 shot regime where the equivariant subspace may be underdetermined. The reliance on a pre-trained source distribution also constrains applicability to truly zero-shot or cross-domain settings.
