## Problem

Mirror detection models fail to generalize across diverse real-world scenes because annotated datasets are small (~13K images total), and existing semi-supervised methods that exploit unlabeled data ignore the reliability of teacher-generated pseudo-labels, injecting noisy supervision into the student model. Additionally, no large-scale unlabeled mirror dataset existed to bridge this data gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252401/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a pixel-level binary mirror segmentation mask. During the iterative data engine, the detection model generates pseudo-labels for 406,817 unlabeled images, which are then scored by two models that each consume an image–pseudo-label pair; the semantic scoring stage additionally feeds a custom text prompt to three multi-modal LLM agents (MiniCPM-V-2.6, Qwen2-VL, InternVL-2) whose outputs are aggregated by a GLM4-flash LLM before being distilled into a lightweight scoring network.

## Method

The core contribution is an iterative data engine that cycles through four steps: training a mirror detection model (DINOv2-small encoder + DPT decoder), predicting pseudo-labels on the unlabeled set, scoring each pseudo-label via a dual guidance strategy, and retaining only samples that exceed a threshold T on both scores. Geometric scoring uses a ResNet50 trained with L2 loss to predict an aggregated segmentation metric (mean of IoU, F_β, Accuracy) from the image–pseudo-label pair, while semantic scoring distills the judgments of three MLLMs plus a text LLM into a compact model that captures whether the predicted region is semantically plausible as a mirror. The reliability-enhanced training strategy builds on Mean Teacher, adding a confidence matrix that gates the consistency loss to only pixels where the teacher's prediction probability exceeds 0.9, and applies cross-entropy on both labeled data and the filtered reliable pseudo-labels with equal weighting (λ₁=λ₂=λ₃=1).

## Results

DAM achieves state-of-the-art performance on all three mirror detection benchmarks, reaching IoU of 0.934 (MSD), 0.734 (PMD), and 0.808 (RGBD-Mirror*), while also attaining the best scores on Accuracy, Fβ, MAE, and BER across all datasets. On the MSD dataset, DAM reduces BER to 2.32, a 42% improvement over the next-best semi-supervised method (DepthAnything, BER 3.98). On two completely unseen video mirror detection datasets (VMD-D and MMD), DAM generalizes without any retraining, achieving IoU of 0.755 and 0.759 respectively, surpassing all methods that were explicitly trained on those datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAM outperforms all 11 compared methods on every metric across all three labeled datasets. Among semi-supervised baselines, DepthAnything is the closest competitor, but DAM still leads by +0.019 IoU on MSD (0.934 vs 0.915), +0.023 on PMD (0.734 vs 0.711), and +0.009 on RGBD-Mirror* (0.808 vs 0.799), with the largest margin in BER (2.32 vs 3.98 on MSD, a 42% reduction). Among supervised methods, HetNet is the strongest, yet DAM surpasses it by +0.046 IoU on MSD and +0.033 on RGBD-Mirror*. On the two unseen video datasets (VMD-D and MMD), DAM—trained only on the three labeled datasets plus unlabeled data—beats all 14 and 11 baselines respectively that were explicitly trained on those datasets, achieving IoU 0.755 on VMD-D (vs. 0.723 for DepthAnything) and 0.759 on MMD (vs. 0.732 for DepthAnything). UniMatch, another semi-supervised method, underperforms even some supervised baselines on PMD (IoU 0.698 vs. HetNet's 0.702), highlighting the importance of the dual guidance scoring and reliability-enhanced training that DAM introduces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean Teacher semi-supervised framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) and the large-scale unlabeled data paradigm popularized by DepthAnything (2024) and UniMatch (2024), but departs from them by introducing an explicit reliability assessment of the teacher's pseudo-labels before they are used for training. Where prior methods focus on challenging the student model or enforcing output consistency, DAM adds a dual guidance scoring stage (geometric segmentation-metric scoring distilled into a ResNet-50, plus MLLM-driven semantic scoring distilled into a lightweight model) to filter unreliable pseudo-labels iteratively. The paper also contributes a 406K-image unlabeled mirror dataset, addressing a data scarcity problem that prior mirror detection methods (MirrorNet, PMDNet, VCNet) could not overcome.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iterative data engine with dual scoring offers a generalizable recipe for improving pseudo-label reliability in any semi-supervised segmentation task with limited annotations, and the 406K-image dataset fills a critical data gap for mirror detection. The strong zero-shot generalization to unseen video mirror detection datasets (VMD-D, MMD) without retraining suggests the learned representations are genuinely robust rather than overfit to the training distribution. Open questions include whether the MLLM-based semantic scoring transfers to other object categories, whether the iterative process has diminishing returns beyond a few iterations, and whether the geometric scoring model (trained on only ~13K labeled pairs) remains reliable as the pseudo-label distribution shifts across iterations.
