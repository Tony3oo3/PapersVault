## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods fail on objects with complex multi-part attributes because (1) the CLIP text encoder cannot reliably align long descriptions with correct spatial regions, causing wrong attribute binding in cross-attention, and (2) occlusion relationships between parts demand a principled generation order that prior automated methods lack. Approaches like Progressive3D address this but require user-defined bounding boxes and generation order, making results unstable and non-automated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250600/cropped_page0_idx10.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single long natural-language prompt describing a 3D object with multiple parts and distinct attributes (e.g., 'A man in black coat, yellow shirt, pink trousers, blue shoes and green hat is waving') and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting asset where each part is correctly bound to its specified attribute. GPT-4o decomposes the prompt into hierarchical blocks with simplified coarse texts and fine-grained attribute texts; SAM/LangSAM segment rendered views for part localization; and the final 3DGS is optimized with MVDream and ControlNet SDS losses under spherical camera sampling (±45° vertical, 360° horizontal).

## Method

HCoG generates 3D assets in 3D Gaussian Splatting representation through three coupled mechanisms. First, an LLM decomposes the long prompt into hierarchical blocks ordered inner-to-outer by occlusion, and within each block a coarse-to-fine paradigm first generates all parts with an attribute-agnostic text, then refines each part's attributes serially. Second, Part-optimization uses LangSAM to train a per-kernel binary label p_seg (200 iterations, lr 0.05) that segments the target part in 3D, after which only those kernels receive gradients under a combined ControlNet-SDS + MVDream-SDS loss for shape-consistent, multi-view attribute binding. Third, between blocks, Gaussian Extension densifies the scene by sampling new kernel positions from N(x_origin, s_origin) plus small perturbation while freezing original kernels, and Label Elimination then uses SAM to re-assign semantic labels and remove redundant extended kernels that do not belong to the new part, preserving previously optimized geometry and appearance without any user-provided bounding boxes.

## Results

On the complex-attributes text-to-3D benchmark, HCoG achieves a BLIP-VQA score of 0.7295 and CLIP-Score of 31.998, outperforming all automatic baselines. When upgraded to Stable Diffusion v3 as the 2D backbone (Ours+SD3), the scores further improve to 0.8055 (BLIP-VQA) and 33.189 (CLIP-Score). The method surpasses the closest competitor Progressive3D by 7.42% on BLIP-VQA and 1.547 on CLIP-Score, while requiring no manual bounding boxes or generation-order specification.

**Table 1: Quantitative comparison on complex-attributes text-to-3D generation.**

| Method | BLIP-VQA ↑ | CLIP-Score ↑ |
|---|---|---|
| GaussianDreamer [35] | 0.4919 | 30.709 |
| MVDream [27] | 0.5519 | 31.132 |
| Progressive3D* [5] | 0.6553 | 30.451 |
| **HCoG (Ours)** | **0.7295** | **31.998** |
| HCoG + SD3 (Ours+SD3) | **0.8055** | **33.189** |

*Progressive3D requires user-defined bounding boxes and generation order.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCoG outperforms all four baselines on both BLIP-VQA and CLIP-Score. Against GaussianDreamer [35], HCoG gains +0.2376 BLIP-VQA and +1.289 CLIP-Score, reflecting substantially better attribute binding. Against MVDream [27], the improvement is +0.1776 BLIP-VQA and +0.866 CLIP-Score. The most direct comparison is with Progressive3D [5], which also targets complex-attribute generation but relies on manual bounding boxes and generation order; HCoG leads by +0.0742 BLIP-VQA (7.42%) and +1.547 CLIP-Score while being fully automated. LucidDreamer [15] is compared only qualitatively in Figure 3, where the paper notes it frequently misbinds attributes (e.g., placing a star on a magic stick instead of a shirt) or omits parts entirely. The ablation in Table 2 further confirms that the in-n-out generation order is critical: using inverse or random order drops BLIP-VQA to 0.5023 and 0.5961 respectively, versus 0.7295 for the proposed order.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDS-based text-to-3D generation pipeline (DreamFusion, MVDream, GaussianDreamer) by introducing a hierarchical decomposition strategy for complex multi-part objects. It departs from Progressive3D, which also targets complex attribute binding but requires user-defined bounding boxes and generation order, by using an LLM (GPT-4o) to automatically decompose long prompts into occlusion-ordered blocks. The 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and ControlNet-based shape conditioning are repurposed from their original editing/generation contexts to enable per-part fine-grained optimization and inter-block Gaussian Extension with Label Elimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fully automated pipeline removes the manual bottleneck (bounding boxes, generation order) that limited Progressive3D's practicality, making complex-attribute 3D generation more accessible. The plug-and-play design across backbones (SD v2.1, SD v3) and the in-n-out occlusion ordering principle offer a reusable paradigm for sequential 3D part generation. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o for decomposition (cost, potential errors on unusual objects), evaluation on a small set of human-like examples, and the SDS-based optimization inheriting known artifacts. Open questions include scalability to scenes with many interacting objects and whether the hierarchical approach generalizes to non-anthropomorphic complex structures.
