## Problem

Current multi-exposure correction methods separate features based on physical properties (frequency, contrast, color) but fail to learn optimal exposure-centric representations, producing artifacts in sky regions and inaccurate sunset corrections. No prior work has leveraged unsupervised slot-based clustering to partition image regions by exposure level, nor has it applied region-aware prompts to guide exposure-specific feature enhancement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252122/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single poorly exposed RGB image (under- or over-exposed) and outputs a corrected RGB image of the same spatial dimensions. Training uses 256×256 patches with a batch size of 16; at inference the model processes full-resolution images (e.g., 844×1500) in under 0.07 s on an RTX 4090. A slot reconstruction decoder (Dec_slot) produces a pseudo-corrected image only during training to supervise slot learning and is discarded at inference, keeping the model lightweight at 1.229M parameters.

## Method

Exposure-slot is a U-shaped residual network where a Slot-Prompt Interaction Module (SPIM) bridges the encoder and decoder. The core mechanism is the Slot-in-Slot Attention Block (SSAB), which hierarchically applies Slot Attention in two levels: main-slots (K=3) coarsely partition regions by exposure, then sub-slots (K=7) refine the partitioning over T=3 iterations, with each level's attention maps modulating the key/value representations passed to the next. Learnable exposure-specific prompts are element-wise multiplied by the slot attention maps to produce region-aware prompt features, which condition a cross-attention step that refines the encoder features before decoding. During training, a separate slot reconstruction decoder maps the final slot features to a pseudo-corrected image, and an L1 loss on this reconstruction (combined with the standard L1 enhancement loss) encourages each slot to capture distinct exposure-centric information without requiring region annotations.

## Results

Exposure-slot achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three multi-exposure benchmarks. On SICE, it reaches 22.81 dB PSNR / 0.7239 SSIM (average), surpassing the previous best CSEC (20.41 / 0.7062) by 2.4 dB in PSNR. On MSEC, it scores 23.18 / 0.8697, and on LCDP it achieves 24.03 / 0.8592, outperforming CSEC by 0.4 dB. In color fidelity metrics on SICE, Exposure-slot reduces ΔE2000 to 6.94 and ΔEab to 8.89, improving over CSEC by 1.78 and 2.13 respectively, while using fewer parameters (1.23M vs. 1.36M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Exposure-slot outperforms all 14 baselines on average PSNR and SSIM across SICE, MSEC, and LCDP. Against the previous state-of-the-art CSEC, it gains 2.4 dB PSNR on SICE (22.81 vs. 20.41), 0.45 dB on MSEC (23.18 vs. 22.73), and 0.4 dB on LCDP (24.03 vs. 23.63), while using fewer parameters (1.23M vs. 1.36M). Compared to the lightweight FECNet (0.15M params), Exposure-slot improves SICE PSNR by 1.85 dB and MSEC PSNR by 0.06 dB. PromptIR, despite having 28× more parameters (34.16M), underperforms Exposure-slot on SICE (20.90 vs. 22.81) and MSEC (16.36 vs. 23.18), suggesting that its generic prompt design is less effective for exposure-specific correction. In color fidelity (ΔE2000, ΔEab on SICE), Exposure-slot leads all methods by a clear margin (6.94/8.89 vs. CSEC's 8.72/11.39), confirming superior color preservation. The only near-tie is on SICE under-exposed SSIM, where DRBN-ENC (0.7071) slightly edges out Exposure-slot (0.7092 is actually higher, so Exposure-slot wins here too); the paper notes one SSIM value where it ranks second.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Slot Attention (Locatello et al., NeurIPS 2020) from its original object-centric learning setting to exposure correction by introducing a hierarchical 'slot-in-slot' structure that progressively partitions exposure regions from coarse to fine. It departs from prior exposure correction methods (ECLNet, FECNet, CSEC) that separate features based on fixed physical properties (frequency, contrast, color) by instead learning exposure-centric clusters in an unsupervised manner through iterative slot updates. The learnable prompt mechanism draws from the prompt-based learning literature (CoOp, PromptIR) but is specifically conditioned on slot attention maps to produce region-aware guidance, a combination not previously explored in low-level vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The slot-in-slot attention mechanism offers a principled, annotation-free way to discover exposure regions, which could generalize to other low-level vision tasks requiring spatially heterogeneous processing (e.g., dehazing, denoising with spatially varying noise). However, the approach is limited to three benchmark datasets, and the 3-level ablation reveals that additional hierarchy yields diminishing returns with exponential time complexity, suggesting practical limits to the hierarchical design. An open question is whether the learned slot partitions remain meaningful under domain shift (e.g., real-world photos with mixed exposure errors not present in training data), and whether the prompt mechanism can be decoupled from the specific slot architecture for broader reuse.
