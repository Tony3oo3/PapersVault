## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods compare test images against normal references learned from a training set, but appearance and positioning variations cause misalignment between these pre-stored prototypes and the test image, degrading detection accuracy—especially in few-shot and multi-class settings. The paper observes that most anomalies are local, meaning the anomalous test image itself still contains abundant normal information that is inherently better aligned with the anomaly than any external reference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251702/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 448², center-cropped to 392²) and processes it through a frozen ViT-Base/14 encoder with DINOv2-R weights. It produces both an image-level anomaly score and a pixel-level anomaly map derived from reconstruction errors between encoder and decoder features at multiple scales. Notably, only six intrinsic normal prototypes (INPs) are needed to represent the normality of an entire image, keeping inference lightweight.

## Method

INP-Former dynamically extracts Intrinsic Normal Prototypes (INPs) from the test image itself via an INP Extractor that uses cross-attention with six learnable tokens to linearly combine encoder features into compact normal representations. An INP Coherence Loss minimizes the cosine distance between each normal feature and its nearest INP, ensuring the extracted prototypes faithfully capture normality without absorbing anomalous regions. The INP-Guided Decoder then uses these INPs as key-value pairs in attention (replacing self-attention), so every decoded token is a linear combination of normal INPs, inherently suppressing reconstruction of anomalous queries and eliminating the identical-mapping shortcut. A Soft Mining Loss, inspired by Focal Loss, upweights regions with high reconstruction error relative to the batch mean, directing optimization toward hard-to-reconstruct normal areas. The overall training objective is L_sm + λL_c, and the decoder's reduced attention complexity (O(NMC) vs. O(N²C) for self-attention, with M=6 ≪ N) yields substantial computational savings.

## Results

On the multi-class AD benchmark, INP-Former achieves SOTA image-level AUROC of 99.7 on MVTec-AD, 98.9 on VisA, and 90.5 on Real-IAD, with pixel-level AUPRO of 94.9, 94.4, and 95.0 respectively. On Real-IAD, it improves over the second-best method (Dinomaly) by 1.2 points in I-AUROC and 4.7 points in P-AP. In the few-shot (4-shot) setting, INP-Former reaches 97.6 I-AUROC on MVTec-AD and 76.7 on Real-IAD, outperforming the previous best by 1.0 and 3.7 points respectively. The method also achieves SOTA single-class results on MVTec-AD (99.7 I-AUROC, 70.2 P-AP) and Real-IAD (92.1 I-AUROC, 48.1 P-AP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INP-Former outperforms all 12 baselines on MVTec-AD and Real-IAD across multi-class, few-shot, and single-class settings. The closest competitor is Dinomaly [14], which INP-Former surpasses by 0.1 I-AUROC and 1.7 P-AP on MVTec-AD, and by 1.2 I-AUROC and 4.7 P-AP on Real-IAD in the multi-class setting. On VisA, Dinomaly retains a slight edge in pixel-level P-AP (53.2 vs 51.2) and P-F1_max (55.7 vs 54.7), while INP-Former leads in I-AUROC (98.9 vs 98.7) and P-AUROC (98.9 vs 98.7). Among reconstruction-based baselines, MambaAD [15] is the strongest, but INP-Former improves I-AUROC by 1.1 on MVTec-AD and 4.2 on Real-IAD. In the few-shot setting, INP-Former beats WinCLIP [21] by 2.4 I-AUROC on MVTec-AD and 3.7 on Real-IAD, and outperforms PatchCore [34] by 8.8 I-AUROC on Real-IAD, demonstrating that dynamically extracted INPs are far more effective than pre-stored prototypes when normal samples are scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in anomaly detection where normality is modeled from external training data (pre-stored prototypes in PatchCore, learned embeddings in RD4AD/UniAD, or class-specific models). Instead, it repurposes the cross-attention mechanism—commonly used for learnable queries in detection/segmentation—to extract a small set of Intrinsic Normal Prototypes (INPs) directly from the test image, then feeds these INPs as key-value pairs into a reconstruction decoder. The INP Coherence Loss and Soft Mining Loss are new training objectives, but the architectural building blocks (ViT encoder, cross-attention, FFN decoder) are standard. The paper acknowledges a prior zero-shot texture AD work [1] that used handcrafted aggregated features as prototypes, positioning INP-Former as a learnable, generalizable extension of that idea.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INP-Former provides a single framework that achieves SOTA or near-SOTA across single-class, multi-class, and few-shot anomaly detection on three major benchmarks, reducing the need for task-specific model architectures. The computational efficiency gain (O(NMC) vs O(N²C) attention with only 6 INPs) makes it attractive for deployment. A notable limitation is failure on logical anomalies that closely resemble background (e.g., misplaced transistors), where the INP Extractor incorrectly treats the anomaly as normal; the authors suggest combining INPs with pre-stored prototypes as a future direction. The zero-shot transfer (trained on Real-IAD, tested on MVTec-AD) hints at broader generalization but remains preliminary and unexplored in depth.
