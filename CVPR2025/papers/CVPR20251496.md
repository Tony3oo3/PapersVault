## Problem

In Generalized Category Discovery (GCD), prior work consistently treats scene information as noise to be minimized, yet the paper identifies an 'Ambiguity Challenge'—where base objects in novel scenes are misclassified as novel, and novel objects in base scenes are misclassified as base—as the root cause of this misinterpretation. Once this ambiguity is addressed, scene information becomes a strong prior for inferring novel classes rather than a source of spurious correlation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251496/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two inputs: the original RGB image and a segmented object image produced by the zero-shot IS-Net saliency model, where scene pixels are replaced with the image's mean pixel value. Both images are processed through a shared ViT-B/16 backbone at standard fine-grained resolutions (e.g., 224×224 for CUB, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft). During evaluation, only the object-branch output is used for classification, while the original-image branch serves solely as a scene feature provider during training.

## Method

MOS employs a dual-branch architecture with a shared ViT-B/16 backbone: one branch processes the segmented object image, the other processes the original image. Scene features are approximated by the original image features (justified by the fact that the original image contains only scene and object components), and a fixed teacher network provides stable scene feature targets to prevent over-optimization. The key component is a lightweight scene-awareness module that concatenates and L2-normalizes the object (or original) feature with the scene feature, then passes the result through a single MLP to produce the classification embedding. Training combines supervised and unsupervised contrastive losses with classification losses across both branches, using Hungarian matching for final label assignment. The entire framework adds only one trainable MLP on top of the SimGCD baseline, yet achieves a 9% average accuracy improvement on the Semantic Shift Benchmark.

## Results

On the three Semantic Shift Benchmark (SSB) fine-grained datasets, MOS achieves an average clustering accuracy of 65.1% (CUB 69.6%, Stanford Cars 64.6%, FGVC-Aircraft 61.1%), surpassing the SimGCD baseline by 9.0% on average. On Oxford-IIIT Pet, MOS reaches 93.2% overall accuracy, outperforming the previous best (InfoSieve, 91.8%) by 1.4%. The method also shows strong novel-class performance, achieving 68.2% on CUB-novel and 56.7% on Stanford Cars-novel, indicating that scene-aware modeling particularly benefits the harder novel-class discovery setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOS outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every dataset and every split (All/Base/Novel). Against the direct baseline SimGCD, MOS gains +9.3% on CUB, +10.8% on Stanford Cars, and +6.9% on FGVC-Aircraft (9.0% average on SSB). Against the previous state-of-the-art SPTNet (ICLR 2024), MOS improves by +3.8% on CUB, +5.6% on Stanford Cars, and +1.8% on FGVC-Aircraft. On Oxford-IIIT Pet, MOS (93.2%) surpasses InfoSieve (91.8%) by 1.4% and SimGCD (88.8%) by 4.4%. The largest margins appear on Stanford Cars, where MOS leads the runner-up SPTNet by 5.6% and μGCD by 8.1%, suggesting scene-aware modeling is especially beneficial when object appearance alone is insufficient for fine-grained discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SimGCD (Zhao et al., ICCV 2023), adopting its parametric classification framework, loss formulation, and ViT-B/16 backbone, while introducing a dual-branch architecture with a lightweight MLP-based scene-awareness module. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant GCD literature (GCD, DCCL, PromptCAL, SPTNet, etc.) that treats scene information as spurious correlation or noise to be minimized, instead reframing scene as a useful prior for novel-class inference. The key conceptual contribution is the 'Ambiguity Challenge' — the observation that scene removal appears beneficial only when base-novel object-scene relationships conflict, not because scene is inherently uninformative. For object-scene decoupling, it repurposes IS-Net (a zero-shot saliency segmentation model) without any task-specific training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a long-standing assumption in GCD that scene information degrades generalization, showing that the apparent benefit of scene removal is an artifact of the ambiguity between base/novel objects and their scenes. The 9% average improvement over SimGCD on the Semantic Shift Benchmark and the annotated CUB scene dataset provide concrete evidence and a resource for future scene-aware GCD research. However, the approach is limited to fine-grained settings (the authors note it struggles on CIFAR-10/100), and the observation experiment is conducted only on CUB, leaving open whether the ambiguity-driven scene benefit generalizes to coarser-grained or more diverse domains. The simplicity of the scene-awareness module (a single MLP) makes the technique easy to adopt, but whether it transfers to other backbone architectures or multi-modal GCD settings remains unexplored.
