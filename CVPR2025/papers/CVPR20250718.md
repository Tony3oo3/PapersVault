## Problem

Generative models such as Stable Diffusion are confined to 8-bit low dynamic range (LDR) output, clipping bright highlights and deep shadows, while existing LDR-to-HDR conversion methods fail to produce photorealistic details in those clipped regions. No prior generative model can produce high-bit, high-dynamic-range content in a generalizable way that preserves the full scene dynamic range.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250718/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a single LDR image (encoded into the pre-trained VAE latent space) or a text prompt as input, and produces a linear HDR image in log space as output, which can be tone-mapped for display on HDR monitors. For panorama and video applications, the same pipeline is applied to multi-view or sequential latent codes, yielding HDR panoramas suitable for image-based lighting and HDR video from a single LDR frame. The output retains full scene dynamic range (over 10 stops) rather than the ~4–5 stops of standard LDR.

## Method

LEDiff preserves the pre-trained Stable Diffusion latent space and introduces three components trained on ~36K HDR images: (1) a highlight denoiser ε_θ− fine-tuned to generate a lower-exposure latent code from an over-exposed one (and vice versa for C₀ from C₊), (2) a shadow denoiser ε_θ+ that performs the analogous operation for under-exposed regions, and (3) a lightweight learnable fusion module F (depth-wise convolution + softmax) that merges the three exposure-bracketed latent codes into a single clipping-free code. The key insight is that clipping in image space is mirrored in latent space, so exposure fusion can be performed directly on latent codes without estimating camera response curves or exposure parameters. A fine-tuned VAE decoder then linearizes and expands the dynamic range of the merged latent code, outputting HDR in log space. Conditioning is achieved by channel-concatenating the reference latent with the noisy target before the denoiser, with only the first layer modified to handle the extra channels.

## Results

On the SI-HDR test set, LEDiff achieves the best no-reference PU21-PIQE scores for both highlights (48.46) and shadows (43.37), and the lowest FID scores across all three tone-mapping operators (Reinhard, Durand, Liang) for both regions, indicating the closest distributional match to ground-truth HDR. For shadow hallucination, LEDiff also attains the highest full-reference HDR-VDP3 (6.98), outperforming all baselines. In a 1200-pair user study on an HDR display, LEDiff is preferred over every competing method with margins of 84–95%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEDiff outperforms all five ITM baselines (HDRCNN, MaskHDR, SingleHDR, ExpandNet, GlowGAN) on the no-reference PU21-PIQE metric for both highlights and shadows, and achieves the lowest FID scores under all three tone-mapping operators, confirming superior perceptual fidelity and distributional alignment with ground-truth HDR. On the full-reference HDR-VDP3 metric, LEDiff leads in shadow reconstruction (6.98 vs. 6.56 for HDRCNN and 5.40 for ExpandNet) but is slightly behind HDRCNN (6.90) and MaskHDR (6.47) for highlights, which the authors attribute to the inherent deviation of hallucinated content from the original scene. In the user study, LEDiff is preferred 84.19% of the time over HDRCNN, 88.22% over MaskHDR, 89.40% over SingleHDR, and 94.52% over ExpandNet (all p < 0.01). For text-to-HDR generation, LEDiff produces clipping-free output with wider dynamic range compared to vanilla Stable Diffusion, and for panorama generation it yields higher-contrast, more realistic image-based lighting results than MVDiffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEDiff builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture, preserving its pretrained VAE encoder and denoiser while fine-tuning only the VAE decoder and adding conditional denoisers for exposure bracket generation. Conceptually, it transplants the classical image-space exposure fusion paradigm (Debevec, Mertens) into the latent space, motivated by the observation that clipping in image space mirrors clipping in latent space. It departs from prior inverse tone mapping methods (HDRCNN, MaskHDR, SingleHDR, ExpandNet) that operate entirely in pixel space, and from concurrent diffusion-based HDR work (Bemana et al., Goswami et al.) that still performs merging in image space, by performing both hallucination and fusion within the latent code. The conditioning strategy for generating exposure brackets is inspired by Ke et al.'s approach of repurposing diffusion generators for conditional tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEDiff provides a plug-and-play mechanism to unlock HDR capabilities in any pretrained latent diffusion model, enabling HDR text-to-image, HDR panorama generation for image-based lighting, and HDR video—applications that were previously inaccessible to generative models. The use of only ~36K HDR images for fine-tuning makes the approach practical, and the method naturally handles both generated content and real LDR photos. Key limitations include dependence on Stable Diffusion's generative quality (weaker with SVD for video), inability to handle compression artifacts or sensor noise in inputs, and the fact that full-reference metrics (HDR-VDP3) show only competitive rather than dominant performance, suggesting hallucinated content still deviates from ground truth. Open questions include whether the latent-space fusion principle generalizes to other diffusion architectures and whether robustness to real-world degradations can be achieved without substantially larger datasets.
