## Problem

Latent diffusion models have become the default for high-resolution image synthesis, but they require a separately trained autoencoder that is difficult to tune and can produce artifacts on out-of-distribution latents. Pixel-space diffusion models, which avoid this bottleneck entirely, have historically underperformed at high resolutions (best prior result: 2.65 FID on ImageNet512). This paper asks whether pixel-space diffusion can match or exceed latent models with the right loss weighting, architecture, and scaling strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250815/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates directly on raw 3-channel RGB pixels without any autoencoder, using 2×2 or 4×4 patching to form token sequences for the U-ViT backbone. For unconditional image generation it takes Gaussian noise and produces images at 128×128 through 1024×1024; for text-to-image it conditions on a text prompt; for video it takes 5 conditioning frames and generates 11 subsequent frames at 128×128. The moment-matching distilled variant reduces sampling from 512 NFEs to 16 while maintaining or improving FID.

## Method

SiD2 combines three key design choices: (1) a sigmoid loss weighting w(λ) = σ(λ − b) that is monotonically increasing in x-mse (unlike EDM-monotonic), with the bias b shifted more negative as resolution increases (b ≈ −3 for 512×512, b ≈ −5 for 1024×1024); (2) flop-heavy scaling, which increases FLOPS by reducing patch size (2×2 instead of 4×4) rather than widening channels, acting as implicit regularization that avoids overfitting on limited datasets like ImageNet; and (3) Residual U-ViTs, which replace blockwise skip-connections with a single skip-connection per downsampling level, guaranteeing the network is an identity function at initialization and reducing memory at evaluation. The model is trained with a variance-preserving schedule and guidance intervals, and optionally distilled via moment matching for few-step sampling.

## Results

SiD2 (Flop Heavy) achieves 1.26 FID on ImageNet-128, 1.38 FID on ImageNet-256, 1.48 FID on ImageNet-512, and 1.75 FID on ImageNet-1024, setting new state-of-the-art for pixel-space diffusion on all four resolutions. The 16-step distilled variant maintains 1.50 FID on ImageNet-512 with only 16 NFEs. For text-to-image generation on MSCOCO (512×512), the distilled model achieves 6.7 FID₃₀ₖ, and for video prediction on Kinetics600, SiD2 achieves 2.3 FVD₅₀ₖ, both outperforming prior latent-space approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-128 and ImageNet-256, SiD2 Flop Heavy achieves clear state-of-the-art, beating the previous best pixel-space model VDM++ (1.75→1.26 on 128; 2.12→1.38 on 256) and the one-step generator PaGoDA (1.48→1.26; 1.56→1.38). On ImageNet-512, SiD2 (1.48) dramatically outperforms all prior pixel-space models (VDM++ 2.65, DiT-XL/2 3.04, SD U-ViT-L 3.02) and is competitive with the best latent model EDM2-XXL (1.40), trailing by only 0.08 FID. On ImageNet-1024, SiD2 (1.75) improves over RIN (8.72) by a factor of ~5. For text-to-image on MSCOCO-512, the 16-step distilled SiD2 (6.7 FID₃₀ₖ) outperforms DMD2 (8.3) and simple diffusion (9.6), matching DeepFloyd (6.7). For video prediction on Kinetics600, SiD2 (2.3 FVD₅₀ₖ) beats the best latent method W.A.L.T (3.3) and MAGVITv2 (4.3), though the authors note latent methods use substantially less training compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the U-ViT architecture from Simple Diffusion [14] and the sigmoid loss weighting from VDM++ [25], but repositions both: it re-derives the sigmoid loss in x-mse form to argue it should be preferred over the EDM-monotonic weighting that VDM++ ultimately adopted, and it strips the U-ViT of its blockwise skip-connections in favor of a single residual connection per level. The broader positioning challenges the dominance of latent diffusion (Stable Diffusion, EDM2) by showing that a pixel-space model with a simple recipe—sigmoid loss with resolution-dependent bias, flop-heavy token scaling, and Residual U-ViTs—can close most of the quality gap at 512×512 and surpass all prior pixel-space results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners no longer need to train and maintain a separate autoencoder to achieve near-SOTA image quality, and the recipe is simple enough to adopt in existing pipelines. The paper acknowledges that latent diffusion (EDM2) still scales slightly better on a compute-quality curve, so the gap is narrowed but not eliminated. Open questions include whether the flop-heavy scaling advantage persists at much larger scales (beyond 1024×1024), whether the Residual U-ViT simplification transfers to other architectures, and whether the sigmoid loss bias heuristic (≈ −1.5 per 2× resolution) generalizes to non-square or video data without re-tuning.
