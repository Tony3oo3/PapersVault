## Problem

Existing Gaussian splatting-based surface reconstruction methods parameterize density or opacity fields rather than explicit geometry fields, so the recovered surface is only approximately defined and the volume splatting algorithm relies on Taylor-series and no-self-occlusion approximations that cap reconstruction quality. Moreover, when surfels cluster tightly around a surface (as required for deterministic geometry), their per-ray ordering becomes unstable, producing a discontinuous loss landscape that destabilizes optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of calibrated RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics and produces a 3D surface mesh extracted via TSDF fusion from rendered depth maps, along with per-view depth maps used for training losses. The surfels are initialized and densified following the 2DGS pipeline, and the final mesh is a post-hoc extraction rather than a direct field-to-mesh conversion, which allows the method to handle open-boundary objects that SDF-based approaches struggle with.

## Method

The paper parameterizes a stochastic geometry field F(x) with 2D Gaussian surfels, converts it to a density field via the Miller et al. stochastic geometry formulation, and renders it with a refined volume splatting algorithm that removes the Taylor-series expansion and self-occlusion assumption, yielding an almost-exact differentiable rendering (the only remaining approximation is global sorting in place of per-ray sorting). To fix the discontinuous loss landscape caused by surfel reordering during clustering, the method blends each surfel's color with spatially nearby surfels using an exponential distance kernel, guaranteeing that the rendered color is a continuous function of surfel properties (Theorem 2). For specular surfaces, a 32-dimensional latent code per surfel is fed into a shallow MLP together with spherical-harmonics-encoded view and reflection directions, replacing the standard SH-encoded color representation. Training uses RGB, depth-distortion, and depth-normal consistency losses, with the depth-normal term computed from the expected depth rather than median depth.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, the proposed method with latent color representation (Ours(Latent)) achieves the best average Chamfer Distance of 0.58, outperforming the closest competitor F.D.S. (0.62), 2DGS (0.72), and RaDe-GS (0.67). On BlendedMVS scene-level cases, Ours(SH) achieves 1.52 average CD (×10⁻¹), beating F.D.S. (1.62) and GOF (1.65); on object-centric cases, Ours(Latent) achieves 0.66 (×10⁻²), again best. Training time is approximately 10–11 minutes per scene, significantly faster than Neuralangelo (>12 h), O.V. (>6 h), and F.D.S. (1 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 baselines on average Chamfer Distance across both DTU and BlendedMVS. Against the closest neural-splatting competitor F.D.S. [5], Ours(Latent) improves DTU average CD from 0.62 to 0.58 and BlendedMVS object-centric CD from 0.70 to 0.66, with the gap widening on specular scenes (e.g., scan110: 0.67 vs 0.57; Dog: 0.25 vs 0.21). Against 2DGS [20], the improvement is 0.72→0.58 on DTU and 0.89→0.66 on BlendedMVS object-centric, attributed to the geometry-field-based density conversion and color blending that eliminates loss-landscape discontinuities. RaDe-GS [60] (0.67) and GOF [59] (0.73) are also clearly beaten on DTU. All neural implicit methods (NeuS2, Neuralangelo, O.V., NeUDF) are outperformed by a large margin (0.79–0.95 vs 0.58) while being 6–70× slower. The latent color representation specifically recovers specular surfaces (scan97, scan110, Dog, Clock) where the SH variant shows visible holes, confirming the benefit of encoding reflection vectors rather than raw colors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate threads: the stochastic geometry field formulation of Miller et al. [31], which provides a theoretically well-defined surface for opaque solids, and the efficient Gaussian splatting rendering pipeline popularized by 3DGS [23] and 2DGS [20]. Rather than using 2D Gaussian surfels to parameterize a density or opacity field (as 2DGS does), the authors parameterize the geometry field F(x) directly and convert it to a density via the stochastic geometry-to-density mapping, yielding a rendering that is 'almost exact' after removing the Taylor series and self-occlusion approximations inherited from EWA volume splatting [64]. The color-blending mechanism to fix the discontinuous loss landscape when surfels cluster is a novel practical contribution not present in prior splatting-based reconstruction methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical justification for why Gaussian surfels should parameterize a geometry field rather than a raw density, giving practitioners a principled surface definition that prior splatting methods (2DGS, SuGaR, GOF) lack. The 'almost exact' rendering and the color-blending fix for loss-landscape discontinuities are practical techniques likely to be adopted by subsequent splatting-based reconstruction work. However, the method remains limited to opaque objects, relies on a global-sorting approximation, and the quantitative gains over strong baselines like 2DGS and RaDe-GS are moderate (e.g., 0.63 vs. 0.72 Chamfer Distance on DTU), so the broader impact depends on whether the theoretical grounding translates to advantages in more challenging settings (dynamic scenes, unbounded environments, transparent objects).
