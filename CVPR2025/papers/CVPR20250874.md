## Problem

Existing generative retrieval methods are task-specific—most target only text retrieval, with a few addressing single-modality image retrieval—and they underperform embedding-based retrieval in cross-modal settings. No prior framework can handle diverse retrieval tasks across multiple modalities (image, text, image-text pairs) within a single generative model, leaving a gap for universal multimodal search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250874/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multimodal query (an image, a text string, or an interleaved image-text pair) concatenated with a free-form text instruction that specifies the desired target modality and domain. It outputs a discrete ID—a sequence of M codes (default 9 levels, codebook size 4096 except the first level of size 3)—where the first code explicitly encodes the target modality and subsequent codes encode semantic content in coarse-to-fine granularity. At inference, Trie-constrained beam search generates candidate IDs, optionally followed by embedding-based re-ranking over the small candidate set to recover accuracy lost to discrete representation.

## Method

GENIUS introduces modality-decoupled semantic quantization: a residual quantization scheme where the first codebook (size 3) is trained to separate modality (image, text, image-text pair) while subsequent levels progressively encode semantics, enabled by contrastive learning with modality-specifying instructions that naturally cluster data by modality in the embedding space. A lightweight fusion module (weighted sum + MLP over concatenated features) combines frozen CLIP image and text encoder outputs into a unified embedding for quantization. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) contrastive pretraining of CLIP encoders following UniIR, (2) joint training of the fusion module and codebooks with a combined loss (contrastive + residual quantization + MSE in code space), and (3) autoregressive T5-small decoder training with cross-entropy over target IDs. A query augmentation strategy linearly interpolates between query and target embeddings (with a Beta-distributed coefficient) to generate diverse training queries, improving the decoder's generalization to varied query formulations. At inference, a Trie over all valid candidate IDs constrains beam search to O(M) per step, and optional embedding-based re-ranking over the top-B candidates narrows the performance gap to embedding-based retrieval.

## Results

On the M-BEIR task-specific benchmark, GENIUS achieves 68.1 R@5 on COCO text-to-image retrieval, outperforming the best prior generative method (IRGen, 50.7) by 28.6 points and GRACE (39.5) by 28.6 points. With embedding-based re-ranking (GENIUS^R), performance rises to 78.0 R@5 on COCO T→I, approaching the embedding-based CLIP-SF baseline (81.1). On the standard generative retrieval benchmark (Flickr30K), GENIUS achieves 84.0 R@5 vs. IRGen (68.9) and GRACE (59.5), and with re-ranking reaches 92.0 R@5. Across all 16 task-dataset combinations in the universal retrieval setting, GENIUS^R averages 38.3 R@5 vs. 28.8 without re-ranking, compared to 48.9 for CLIP-SF and 45.5 for BLIP-FF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prior generative retrieval methods, GENIUS achieves decisive wins: on COCO text-to-image it surpasses IRGen (50.7) by 17.4 points and GRACE (39.5) by 28.6 points in R@5, and on Flickr30K it leads IRGen by 15.1 points (84.0 vs. 68.9 R@5). Against embedding-based baselines (CLIP-SF and BLIP-FF from UniIR), GENIUS without re-ranking trails by a wide margin (e.g., 68.1 vs. 81.1 on COCO T→I), but with embedding-based re-ranking (GENIUS^𝓡) it narrows the gap substantially—reaching 78.0 vs. 81.1 on COCO T→I and 91.1 vs. 92.3 on COCO I→T. On composed image retrieval (CIRR), GENIUS^𝓡 (39.5) still lags BLIP-FF (52.2) but exceeds CLIP-SF (44.6) is not achieved; it remains below both. On knowledge-intensive tasks like WebQA (T→T), the gap to embedding-based methods remains large (44.6 vs. 84.7 for CLIP-SF), reflecting the inherent capacity limitation of discrete IDs. Overall, GENIUS is the first generative method to support all 8 multimodal task types simultaneously, whereas GRACE and IRGen are restricted to single-modality or single-task settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

GENIUS extends the generative retrieval paradigm—originally developed for text document retrieval (DSI, NCI) and later adapted to single-modality image retrieval (IRGen) and cross-modal retrieval (GRACE)—into a universal, instruction-guided framework spanning images, text, and image-text pairs. It builds directly on UniIR's CLIP-based encoder pretraining and instruction-following formulation for the multimodal search task, but departs from prior generative methods by introducing modality-decoupled residual quantization (where the first code level explicitly encodes modality) and a query-target interpolation augmentation strategy. Unlike GRACE and IRGen, which are task-specific and modality-restricted, GENIUS uses a single T5-small decoder with a Trie-constrained beam search to generate IDs across all modalities from a unified candidate pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GENIUS demonstrates that generative retrieval can be made universal across modalities while maintaining near-constant inference speed regardless of database size—a practical advantage over embedding-based methods whose nearest-neighbor search cost grows with corpus size. However, a significant performance gap to embedding-based retrieval persists, particularly on knowledge-intensive tasks (e.g., WebQA, InfoSeek), suggesting that discrete ID representations still lack the expressive capacity of dense embeddings for complex reasoning. Open questions include whether larger codebooks, deeper quantization hierarchies, or hybrid discrete-dense representations can close this gap, and whether the framework scales to additional modalities (video, audio) or to truly open-world candidate sets without pre-constructed Tries.
