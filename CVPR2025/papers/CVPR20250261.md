## Problem

Existing training-free diffusion-based image editing methods rely on heuristics or intuition without a clear theoretical foundation, making it difficult to justify their effectiveness or generalize to complex scenarios. In particular, combining text-guided editing with other editing objectives (e.g., reward-model-based identity transfer or style matching) remains largely unaddressed, as most prior work focuses on improving reconstruction fidelity while leaving the editing mechanism unchanged.

## Input / Output

The method operates in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion v1.4 model (50 sampling steps) and takes as input an original RGB image, an original text prompt, and an edited text prompt. For reward-model-based editing (face swapping), it additionally accepts a reference face image and uses an ArcFace cosine similarity score as the reward; for combined style editing, it accepts a style reference image and uses negative L2 distance between CLIP Gram matrices as the style reward. The output is a single edited RGB image that modifies the specified attributes while preserving non-edited regions.

## Method

h-Edit formulates image editing as a reverse-time bridge modeling problem: it modifies the backward transition p(x_{t-1}|x_t) of a pretrained diffusion model via Doob's h-transform to obtain p^h(x_{t-1}|x_t) = p(x_{t-1}|x_t)·h(x_{t-1},t-1)/h(x_t,t), where h encodes the target editing property. Because p^h is generally non-Gaussian, sampling is performed via Langevin Monte Carlo, which decomposes each update into a 'reconstruction' term x_{t-1}^{base} (computed by existing inversion techniques like Edit Friendly or PnP) and an 'editing' term ∇log h (the score of the h-function). This decomposition is the key design insight: the reconstruction term handles faithfulness independently of the editing term, which can be freely customized per task (classifier-free guidance for text editing, ArcFace gradient for face swapping, Gram-matrix gradient for style). Multiple h-functions are combined additively in log-space ('product of h-experts'), enabling simultaneous text-guided and reward-model-based editing. Both an explicit form (editing at time t) and an implicit form (editing at time t-1, interpretable as gradient ascent on log h) are provided, with the implicit form supporting multiple optimization steps for harder edits.

## Results

On PIE-Bench text-guided editing, h-Edit-D (deterministic inversion + P2P) achieves the best scores across all six metrics: CLIP similarity 0.253, Local CLIP 0.147, DINO distance 1.17×10⁻², LPIPS 4.85×10⁻², SSIM 8.54×10⁻¹, and PSNR 27.87 dB. With random inversion, h-Edit-R reaches CLIP 0.256 and Local CLIP 0.159 (with P2P), outperforming EF and LEDITS++ on every metric. For face swapping on CelebA-HQ, h-Edit-R attains the highest identity similarity (0.80) and lowest LPIPS (0.04), while h-Edit-R with 3 optimization steps pushes identity to 0.84.

## Baselines & Comparisons

h-Edit-D dominates all five deterministic-inversion baselines (NP, NT, StyleD, NMG, PnP Inv) on every metric; the largest gaps are in LPIPS (4.85 vs. 6.90 for NP, a 1.22×10⁻² improvement) and Local CLIP (0.147 vs. 0.085–0.140). Among random-inversion methods, h-Edit-R beats EF by 0.001 CLIP and 0.022–0.033 Local CLIP, and substantially outperforms LEDITS++ (LPIPS 5.55 vs. 8.88, PSNR 26.43 vs. 23.36). For face swapping, h-Edit-R achieves the best identity score (0.80 vs. 0.74 for EF, 0.70 for FaceShifter) and lowest LPIPS (0.04 vs. 0.06 for EF), but trails FaceShifter (FID 10.16) and DiffFace (FID 11.89) on perceptual quality, which the authors attribute to those methods being trained on larger face datasets. The paper notes that PnP Inv and NMG sometimes reconstruct the original image unchanged in hard cases, inflating their faithfulness scores without performing meaningful edits, whereas h-Edit consistently makes the intended change while preserving non-edited regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Doob's h-transform, a classical tool from stochastic process theory, to provide a principled theoretical foundation for diffusion-based image editing that prior training-free methods (NTI, P2P, NMG, PnP, EF, LEDITS++) lacked. While those methods focused on improving inversion/reconstruction quality through heuristics, h-Edit reframes editing as a reverse-time bridge modeling problem, decomposing each update into a reconstruction term (compatible with existing inversion techniques like EF's residual) and an editing term derived from the h-function's score. The product-of-h-experts mechanism for compositional editing is a natural consequence of this decomposition, with no direct analogue in prior training-free editing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical grounding via bridge modeling offers a principled path to extending training-free editing beyond text prompts to arbitrary reward functions and compositional objectives, which was previously ad hoc. The demonstrated ability to simultaneously perform text-guided and reward-model-based editing (e.g., face swapping with ArcFace, style transfer with Gram-matrix loss) opens a practical avenue for multi-objective editing without fine-tuning. Open questions include automated adaptation of guidance weights for difficult editing cases, restricting the reconstruction term to non-edited regions only, and whether the framework extends naturally to video or 3D editing.
