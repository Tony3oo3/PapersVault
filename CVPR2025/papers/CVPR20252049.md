## Problem

Existing thermal object detection methods that leverage prior thermal-to-visible (T2V) translation rely on simple attention-based cross-modality aggregation, which fails to capture fine-grained relationships between pseudo-visible and thermal features at multiple granularities. The generated visible images are inherently unreliable and introduce noise, yet prior fusion strategies lack a mechanism to selectively filter redundant information while preserving complementary visible knowledge across different feature levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252049/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes a single thermal infrared image (H×W×1) at 640×640 resolution; at training time, paired thermal-visible images are used to train the Pearl-GAN T2V translator. The T2V model produces a pseudo-visible image (H×W×3) whose latent code serves as the generative feature, while the CSPDarknet backbone processes the channel-concatenated thermal and pseudo-visible pair to yield multi-level discriminative features. The final output is a set of bounding boxes with class labels (person, car, bicycle, etc.) produced by a YOLOX decoupled detection head.

## Method

PFGF constructs a multi-subgraph structure where nodes are generated via pyramid pooling of multi-level backbone features (f³, f⁴, f⁵) and the Pearl-GAN latent code z, with intra-subgraph edges computed from feature differences. An Inter-Mamba block performs cross-modality fusion between generative and low-level discriminative nodes by projecting both into hidden state space via VMamba's selective SSM, then applying a gating operation that lets the thermal feature selectively modulate the fused representation. A Cascade Knowledge Integration (CKI) strategy then propagates fused information upward: GRU-based node updates aggregate neighborhood messages, VMamba blocks refine node features, and leader nodes (computed by concatenating and convolving all refined nodes at a scale) gate the next higher-level subgraph via global-average-pooling attention. This cascade from f³ to f⁵ avoids expensive inter-subgraph edge computation while ensuring low-level spatial details inform high-level semantics, and Mamba's input-dependent selection mechanism suppresses noise from the unreliable pseudo-visible features throughout the graph.

## Results

On the FLIR dataset, PFGF achieves 47.1 mAP, 84.8 mAP50, and 44.6 mAP75, outperforming the best mono-modality thermal baseline IDA (46.3 mAP) by 0.8% and TIRDet (44.3 mAP) by 2.8%. On LLVIP, PFGF* reaches 67.3 mAP and 78.4 mAP75, surpassing TIRDet* (65.2 mAP) by 2.1%. On the more complex Autonomous Vehicles dataset, PFGF attains 45.9 mAP and 78.8 mAP50, exceeding IDA (43.1 mAP) by 2.8% and 5.7% respectively. PFGF also outperforms most multispectral (V+T) methods that require visible images at test time, achieving the highest mAP and mAP75 on FLIR and LLVIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFGF outperforms all mono-modality thermal baselines across all three datasets. On FLIR, it beats the strongest thermal-only competitor IDA [28] by 0.8 mAP (47.1 vs 46.3) and 3.7 mAP50 (84.8 vs 81.1), and surpasses TIRDet [44] by 2.8 mAP. On LLVIP, PFGF* (using a Pearl-GAN trained on LLVIP) improves over TIRDet* by 2.1 mAP (67.3 vs 65.2) and 3.7 mAP75 (78.4 vs 74.7); the original PFGF also beats TIRDet by 2.6 mAP. On the AV dataset, PFGF leads IDA [28] by 2.8 mAP and 5.7 mAP50. Against multispectral (V+T) methods that require visible images at inference, PFGF achieves the highest mAP on FLIR (47.1 vs Fusion-Mamba's 47.0) and LLVIP (67.3 vs Fusion-Mamba's 64.3), while trailing Fusion-Mamba by only 0.1 on FLIR mAP50 (84.8 vs 84.9) and 0.1 on LLVIP mAP50 (96.9 vs 97.0). Among the fusion-strategy ablations (Table 5), PFGF's GMF module outperforms CMA [44] by 2.8 mAP, MMFT [45] by 2.5 mAP, GIM [24] by 1.1 mAP, and FMB [10] by 1.4 mAP on FLIR, confirming the advantage of the graph-Mamba architecture over attention-based, transformer-based, and pure-Mamba fusion alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the 'visible knowledge assistance' lineage for thermal object detection, specifically the image-domain transformation route pioneered by TIRDet, which uses Pearl-GAN to synthesize pseudo-visible images from thermal input. The key architectural departure is in the fusion mechanism: rather than the attention-based CMA module used in TIRDet or simple concatenation, PFGF introduces a Graph-Mamba Fusion module that adapts the graph-based cross-modality interaction structure from Li et al. (ACM MM 2023) and augments it with Mamba's selective state-space mechanism for efficient long-range dependency modeling. The Inter-Mamba block and Cascade Knowledge Integration strategy represent novel components that explicitly model multi-granularity relationships between generative (pseudo-visible) and discriminative (thermal backbone) features, going beyond the flat attention fusion of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA thermal detection performance (47.1 mAP on FLIR, 67.3 on LLVIP) using only thermal images at test time while still benefiting from visible-spectrum knowledge learned during training, reducing hardware requirements compared to dual-sensor systems. The efficiency comparison showing 32% FLOPs reduction over the Transformer-based variant (GMTF) while simultaneously improving mAP by 0.9% validates Mamba as a viable replacement for attention in cross-modal fusion pipelines. However, the method still depends on paired visible-thermal data for training and on the reliability of Pearl-GAN's generated images, which the authors acknowledge introduces noise; whether the graph-Mamba fusion paradigm generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., LiDAR + RGB) or to detection tasks without a generative prior remains an open question.
