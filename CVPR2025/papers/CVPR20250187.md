## Problem

Automatically creating semantically meaningful, editable rigs (sparse skeletons with skinning weights) for arbitrary articulated objects from monocular videos remains unsolved. Existing approaches either depend on category-specific template priors (SMPL, MANO), require predefined skeleton topologies, rely on high-quality 3D reconstruction for skeleton extraction, or produce dense non-semantic control points that are difficult to drive with plausible new motions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250187/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of a continuously moving articulated object (no camera calibration beyond per-frame poses) and produces a sparse 3D skeleton tree with per-Gaussian skinning weights bound to a canonical 3D Gaussian representation. The 2D skeleton supervision is derived from per-frame silhouettes via morphological thinning, and DINOv2 features provide semantic labels for symmetry-aware joint selection. The resulting model supports real-time novel view rendering, interactive pose editing via a GUI, motion interpolation, and cross-object motion transfer.

## Method

RigGS operates in three stages. First, a canonical 3D Gaussian shape is deformed over time by a skeleton-aware node-controlled field where an MLP predicts per-node rotations and translations; a 2D Chamfer-distance projection loss against thinned silhouettes anchors nodes to the object's central axis. Second, a heuristic coarse-to-fine algorithm extracts a sparse skeleton: farthest-point sampling subsamples nodes, a motion-weighted minimum spanning tree establishes connectivity, and geometric turning-point detection combined with DINOv2 semantic symmetry pruning yields the final joint set with a BFS-determined root. Third, a skeleton-driven deformation model combines LBS-based coarse deformation (with learnable skinning weights computed from distance-to-bone, learnable radii, and MLP-predicted scaling factors) with a pose-dependent detail MLP that takes all joint quaternions as input and outputs per-Gaussian position offsets, making the entire deformation pose-driven rather than time-driven so that arbitrary new motions can be generated by simply changing joint rotations.

## Results

On the D-NeRF dataset, RigGS achieves 40.82 PSNR / 0.996 SSIM / 0.0112 LPIPS, second only to SC-GS (43.04 / 0.998 / 0.0066) and substantially ahead of all other baselines. On DG-Mesh, RigGS scores 37.65 PSNR / 0.991 SSIM / 0.0169 LPIPS, again trailing SC-GS (38.96 / 0.993 / 0.0136) by a narrow margin. On the real-captured ZJU-MoCap dataset, RigGS dramatically outperforms the only other skeleton-based method AP-NeRF, improving PSNR from 25.62 to 33.54 (+7.9 dB) and LPIPS from 0.0934 to 0.0327.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RigGS is compared against five baselines: D-NeRF, TiNeuVox, 4D-GS, SC-GS, and AP-NeRF. Against the non-skeleton methods (D-NeRF, TiNeuVox, 4D-GS), RigGS wins decisively on all metrics across both synthetic datasets, e.g. 40.82 vs. 33.25 PSNR over 4D-GS on D-NeRF. Against SC-GS, RigGS trails by ~2.2 dB PSNR on D-NeRF and ~1.3 dB on DG-Mesh; the authors attribute this gap to SC-GS's use of 512 control points and temporally-varying Gaussian scales/rotations, which are incompatible with the pose-driven editing requirement. Against AP-NeRF (the only other template-free skeleton method), RigGS wins on every metric: on D-NeRF it gains +9.9 dB PSNR (40.82 vs. 30.94), and on the real ZJU-MoCap dataset the advantage widens to +7.9 dB (33.54 vs. 25.62). Qualitatively, RigGS produces smoother skinning weights without joint discontinuities and more semantically plausible skeletons than AP-NeRF, as shown in Figure 7.

## Relation to Prior Work

RigGS builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its canonical shape representation and adopts the LBS-based coarse deformation formulation from AP-NeRF and MagicPony for skeleton-driven articulation. Its key departure is a fully template-free pipeline: rather than relying on category-specific priors (SMPL, MANO, SMAL) or predefined skeleton trees, it introduces a skeleton-aware node-controlled deformation field that simultaneously reconstructs the dynamic object and yields candidate skeleton points, which are then simplified via a heuristic combining geometric, semantic (DINOv2), and motion information. This contrasts with neural-bone approaches (BANMo, BAGS, DreaMo) that lack explicit semantic skeleton structure, and with methods like AP-NeRF that depend on 3D mesh reconstruction quality for skeleton extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RigGS provides a practical, template-free path to animatable 3D models from casual monocular video, enabling pose editing, motion interpolation, and motion transfer with real-time rendering—capabilities that are valuable for games, AR/VR, and film production. The paper acknowledges limitations with sparse viewpoints, inaccurate camera poses, excessive motion, and the absence of pose-dependent appearance modeling. An open direction the authors highlight is leveraging richer input signals (text, images) for semantic skeleton pose editing, which could bridge the gap between low-level kinematic control and high-level intent-driven animation.
