## Problem

Existing 3D watermarking methods are poorly suited to the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering pipeline: per-scene decoder training is prohibitively expensive, pre-trained 2D watermarking decoders suffer from an inherent fidelity-capacity trade-off, and directly optimizing all Gaussian attributes degrades 3D structure. No prior method simultaneously satisfies the four key requirements of security, invisibility, large message capacity, and fast optimization for 3DGS assets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250400/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS model (with per-Gaussian position, covariance, opacity, and SH coefficients) and a binary message of 16, 32, or 48 bits, and produces a watermarked 3DGS model where each Gaussian carries an additional 48-dimensional learnable SH offset. For extraction, rendered views from arbitrary viewpoints are fed through CLIP's frozen visual encoder and the pre-trained 3-layer MLP decoder to recover the binary message. The SH offset size matches the original SH coefficient dimensionality, and the entire watermarking step requires only 10 minutes on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

GuardSplat decouples message decoder training from 3D model optimization via a CLIP-guided pipeline: binary messages are tokenized into CLIP text tokens, encoded by CLIP's frozen textual encoder into 512-dim features, and a lightweight 3-layer MLP decoder is trained solely on the message loss (5 minutes), avoiding the fidelity-capacity trade-off of joint encoder-decoder 2D watermarking. For embedding, all Gaussian attributes are frozen and a learnable SH offset is added to each Gaussian's spherical harmonic coefficients, preserving the original 3D structure while an L2 offset loss prevents excessive perturbation. During watermarking optimization, a differentiable distortion layer randomly applies cropping, scaling, rotation, JPEG compression, and brightness jittering to rendered views, training the SH offsets to be robust against these distortions. The full objective combines RGB+LPIPS reconstruction loss, message extraction loss, and the offset regularization, with the CLIP visual encoder and message decoder both frozen during the 10-minute watermarking phase.

## Results

On the Blender and LLFF datasets with 32-bit messages, GuardSplat achieves 99.04% bit accuracy with PSNR 39.40, SSIM 0.9939, and LPIPS 0.0022, outperforming all baselines on both extraction accuracy and reconstruction quality. At 16 bits, bit accuracy reaches 99.64% (PSNR 41.55), and at 48 bits it maintains 98.29% (PSNR 38.90). Training the message decoder takes only 5 minutes and watermarking a 3DGS asset takes 10 minutes on a single RTX 3090 GPU, compared to 12–84 hours for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GuardSplat outperforms all 8 baseline categories on both bit accuracy and reconstruction quality at 32 bits. Against NeRF-based methods, GuardSplat leads CopyRNeRF by 21.0% in bit accuracy (99.04% vs. 78.08%) and WateRF by 10.5% (99.04% vs. 88.58%), while also achieving substantially higher PSNR (39.40 vs. 26.13 and 31.19 dB). Among 3DGS-based approaches, GuardSplat surpasses GaussianMarker (the strongest 3DGS baseline) by 0.19% in bit accuracy and 5.42 dB in PSNR (39.40 vs. 33.98), and beats 3DGS w/ WateRF by 8.89% in bit accuracy. The 3DGS-trained-on-watermarked-images baselines (3DGS + CIN/SSL/HiDDeN/StegaStamp) all fall below 83% bit accuracy, far behind GuardSplat's 99.04%. Notably, GuardSplat also outperforms its own variants using pre-trained 2D watermarking decoders (e.g., GuardSplat + SSL at 98.60%), confirming that the CLIP-guided decoder is better suited for large-capacity messages than repurposed 2D decoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

GuardSplat extends the 3D watermarking line initiated by CopyRNeRF and WateRF (which target NeRF) and GaussianMarker/GS-Hider (which target 3DGS) by introducing a fundamentally different optimization strategy: instead of jointly training a per-scene decoder or repurposing a 2D encoder-decoder pair, it uses CLIP's pre-trained text-image alignment to train a standalone message decoder on textual features, then applies that decoder to CLIP visual features of rendered 3DGS views. The SH-aware embedding module departs from prior 3DGS watermarking (GaussianMarker, 3D-GSW) that perturbs all Gaussian attributes, restricting modifications to spherical-harmonic coefficients only to preserve 3D geometry. This positions the work at the intersection of CLIP-based representation learning and differentiable 3D rendering, carving out a niche where the decoder is trained once and reused across scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~100× speedup over prior 3D watermarking methods (5+10 minutes vs. 13–84 hours) makes practical deployment of 3DGS copyright protection feasible for content creators, which was previously prohibitive. The SH-only embedding strategy is a clean, generalizable insight for any 3DGS-based pipeline: it guarantees geometric fidelity while still providing sufficient capacity, and it inherently prevents file-level watermark removal. However, the method remains vulnerable to generative-removal attacks (VAE attack drops accuracy to 82%), requires a pre-trained 3DGS model as input, and has not been validated on very large-scale scenes or against geometric (non-photometric) distortions. Open questions include whether the CLIP-bridge approach transfers to other 3D representations (NeRF, mesh) and whether the decoder's capacity scales beyond 48 bits without additional architectural changes.
