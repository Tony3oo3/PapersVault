## Problem

ANN-based offline RL methods (e.g., Decision Transformer) achieve strong performance but incur high computational and energy costs, making them unsuitable for energy-constrained embodied AI. While spiking neural networks offer event-driven, low-power computation, no spike-driven transformer has been designed for offline RL sequence modeling—existing SNN transformers target spatial dependencies in vision or rely on floating-point softmax and LayerNorm incompatible with SNN spiking dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252295/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decision step l, the model receives a context window of N concatenated (action, return-to-go, state) tuples, where return-to-go is the suffix sum of future rewards rather than per-step rewards. Each tuple is embedded into a D-dimensional vector, the resulting N×D matrix is repeated T times along the SNN internal timestep axis, and the model autoregressively predicts the next action. The output is a single continuous action vector per step, with T=4 SNN timesteps used across all tasks.

## Method

DSFormer replaces standard transformer self-attention with two spike-driven mechanisms: Temporal Spiking Self-Attention (TSSA) concatenates Q, K, V across all T SNN timesteps before computing masked attention, enabling global temporal credit assignment (proven to reduce input entropy via Theorem 1), while Positional Spiking Self-Attention (PSSA) uses element-wise multiplication with a learnable pairwise positional bias restricted to a local window S=8, reducing complexity from O(TDN²) to O(TDN). To handle normalization, Progressive Threshold-dependent Batch Normalization (PTBN) linearly interpolates between threshold-dependent LayerNorm (preserving per-sequence temporal dependencies) and threshold-dependent BatchNorm (mergeable with linear layers at inference) over training, eliminating floating-point operations in the final SNN. The architecture uses LIF neurons with causal masking, Membrane Shortcut connections, and is trained via supervised sequence prediction on D4RL trajectories without any value-based regularization.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo benchmark, DSFormer with PSSA achieves a mean normalized score of 78.8, surpassing DT (74.7), FCNet (75.1), and CQL (63.9), while TSSA reaches 75.7. On the Adroit benchmark, PSSA and TSSA achieve mean scores of 48.6 and 47.9 respectively, substantially outperforming DT (27.8) and FCNet (41.0). In terms of energy, PSSA consumes only 88.8 μJ per inference versus 410.5 μJ for DT, yielding a 78.4% energy reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary ANN transformer baseline DT, PSSA wins on both MuJoCo (78.8 vs 74.7) and Adroit (48.6 vs 27.8) mean scores, with the Adroit gap being particularly large (+20.8 points); TSSA also outperforms DT on both domains. Against FCNet, PSSA leads on MuJoCo (78.8 vs 75.1) and Adroit (48.6 vs 41.0), while TSSA edges out FCNet on Adroit (47.9 vs 41.0) and matches it on MuJoCo (75.7 vs 75.1). Against CQL, both DSFormer variants dominate on MuJoCo (75.7/78.8 vs 63.9) and Adroit (47.9/48.6 vs 40.3). Against the SNN baselines, DSFormer wins by wide margins: PSSA scores 78.8 vs SpikeGPT's 26.4 and SpikeBERT's 39.9 on MuJoCo, and 48.6 vs 16.3 and 21.1 on Adroit, indicating that existing SNN architectures designed for NLP or vision are poorly suited to offline RL. In energy, PSSA (88.8 μJ) is 4.6× cheaper than DT (410.5 μJ) and 11.5× cheaper than FCNet (1022.0 μJ), while SpikeGPT (27.8 μJ) is cheaper but achieves far lower performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: the Decision Transformer (DT) paradigm for offline RL as conditional sequence modeling, and the growing family of spike-driven transformers (Spikformer, SpikeFormer, SpikeGPT, SpikeBERT). Prior spike-driven transformers were designed for spatial dependencies in vision or element-wise temporal operations in NLP; none addressed the Markovian, autoregressive structure of RL trajectories. DSFormer departs from this lineage by introducing TSSA (temporal concatenation before attention) and PSSA (learnable pairwise positional bias with a local window) specifically for RL's temporal and local dependencies, and by proposing PTBN to resolve the fundamental incompatibility between LayerNorm (needed for per-sequence normalization) and the fixed-weight requirement of SNN inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSFormer demonstrates that SNN-based sequence modeling can match or exceed ANN transformer performance in offline RL while consuming roughly 78% less energy, a meaningful step toward energy-constrained embodied AI on neuromorphic hardware. The PTBN progressive normalization is a practical, reusable technique for any SNN sequence model that needs per-sample statistics without breaking spike compatibility at inference. Key open questions remain: the work is validated only on D4RL simulation benchmarks with T=4 timesteps, no neuromorphic chip deployment has been performed, and it is unclear whether the energy savings and performance parity hold at larger scales, longer contexts, or in real-world robotic settings. The paper also does not explore whether the SNN advantage persists when combined with value-based regularization (e.g., Q-value-augmented transformers).
