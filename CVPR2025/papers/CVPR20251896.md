## Problem

Echocardiographic examination requires segmentation across multiple cardiac planes (2CH, 3CH, 4CH, PSAX), but existing models must be tailored per plane due to dramatic structural differences, and combining planes into a single model causes significant performance degradation. SAM, while a promising foundation, fails on echocardiographic images due to the large semantic gap between natural and medical imaging. No uniform model has previously solved the multi-plane segmentation (MPS) problem for echocardiography in a single architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251896/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 256×256 2D echocardiographic image as input without requiring any plane-identity label, and produces a unified multi-class segmentation mask (background, left ventricle, LV cavity, myocardium) covering all cardiac structures visible in that view. A key design constraint is that the PC-Mask prompt generator must operate without knowing which plane the input belongs to, enabling a single model to handle all six echocardiographic planes. Annotations from heterogeneous datasets are remapped to this common four-class protocol before training.

## Method

EchoONE builds on SAM's ViT-B image encoder and mask decoder, augmented with two novel modules. The PC-Mask module clusters training images in a ResNet34 latent space, computes cosine-similarity weights between each input and cluster prototypes, and composes a multi-channel prior embedding from weighted center masks; a lightweight U-Net then refines this into a semantic-aware dense prompt that guides SAM without explicit plane information. The LFFA module introduces a learnable CNN branch (Ladder Side-Tuning style) whose four-layer local features are concatenated with the corresponding mask-decoder transformer outputs and fused via 1×1 convolutions, with three additional transformer blocks added to the decoder to accommodate this integration. Training uses a combined objective of dice loss and binary cross-entropy (weighted 0.8/0.2) for the final segmentation plus a separate dice+BCE loss for PC-Mask learning (λ=0.5), initialized from SAM's SA-1B pretrained weights and trained for 100 epochs on a single A6000 GPU.

## Results

On internal evaluation across four echocardiographic planes (2CH, 3CH, 4CH, PSAX), EchoONE achieves mDice of 89.67%, 87.27%, 90.28%, and 88.26% respectively, with mIoU of 81.87%, 78.01%, 82.67%, and 79.68%, outperforming all 10 baselines on every plane. On external evaluation without any training on those datasets, EchoONE achieves mDice of 73.94% on HMC_QU (noisy, low-quality images) and 88.36% on EchoNet-Dynamic, again the best among all methods. The largest margin over the closest competitor (SAMUS) appears on PSAX, where EchoONE gains +2.34 mDice and +3.16 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoONE outperforms all 10 baselines on every plane and every metric. Among traditional segmentation models, H2Former is the strongest (e.g., 88.01 mDice on 2CH) but still trails EchoONE by 1.66 mDice; U-Net, DeepLabV3+, and SwinUNet lag further behind. Zero-shot SAM is catastrophically ineffective on echocardiography (mDice 25–35%, HD95 >55), confirming the large domain gap. Among retrained SAM-based methods, SAMUS is the closest competitor (87.51 mDice on 2CH vs. EchoONE's 89.67), while MedSAM, SAMed, SAM-Med2D, and MSA all fall 2–13 mDice below EchoONE depending on the plane. The advantage is most pronounced on PSAX, where EchoONE gains +2.34 mDice over SAMUS and +13.16 over SAMed, attributed to the semantic-aware PC-Mask dense prompt handling the structurally distinct short-axis view. On external datasets, EchoONE leads by +2.45 mDice over SAMUS on HMC_QU and +0.80 mDice on EchoNet-Dynamic, demonstrating generalization without any training on those data.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoONE builds directly on the SAM architecture (ViT-B image encoder + mask decoder) and inherits the Ladder Side-Tuning (LST) paradigm from SAM-LST and SAMUS for introducing a learnable CNN branch. It departs from prior SAM-based medical imaging methods (MedSAM, SAMed, SAM-Med2D, MSA, SAMUS), which each adapt SAM to a single imaging scenario, by introducing a PC-Mask module that generates semantic-aware dense prompts from clustered structural priors without requiring plane identity at inference. It also departs from multi-branch multi-plane segmentation approaches (TransFusion, MVFusFra, cross-view attention networks) that treat each plane in a separate branch and fuse results, instead proposing a single uniform model that handles all planes simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstrated solution to the multi-plane segmentation problem in echocardiography using a single model, which directly addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: clinicians and hospitals would no longer need to maintain and update separate segmentation models for each cardiac view. The PC-Mask design—composing prior structural knowledge via cosine-similarity-weighted cluster centers—offers a generalizable mechanism for injecting domain priors without explicit plane labels, potentially transferable to other multi-view medical imaging tasks. However, the approach is validated only on 2D echocardiography with a fixed set of six planes, and the clustering-based prior requires a pre-trained ResNet34 for plane classification, raising open questions about robustness to truly novel planes or modalities. The modest margins over the strongest baseline (SAMUS) on most planes suggest the gains are real but not transformative.
