## Problem

While prior work demonstrated that VLM text embeddings organize into linear compositional structures, it remained unclear whether analogous compositional patterns emerge in the visual embedding space. Visual embeddings face two challenges absent in text: noise from extraneous visual cues (background, context) and sparsity, where certain attribute-object combinations simply do not appear in real image collections. This paper investigates whether VLMs develop human-like compositional reasoning in the visual domain and how to properly analyze it despite these difficulties.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled image collection where each image is tagged with a composite concept (e.g., an attribute-object pair like 'red car'), and produces geodesically decomposable embeddings: a set of tangent-space vectors representing each primitive concept (attribute directions and object directions) that can be additively combined to reconstruct or synthesize embeddings for any composite, including unseen combinations. The noise distribution weighting each image's contribution can be uniform or derived from CLIP image-to-text similarity scores with a tunable temperature. The decomposed embeddings serve as classifiers (cosine similarity against test image embeddings) or as conditioning vectors for diffusion-based image generation via unCLIP.

## Method

GDE formalizes compositionality on the Riemannian manifold of the CLIP unit sphere, where linear decomposition is insufficient due to the curved geometry. The procedure computes a weighted intrinsic mean μ of all input embeddings, maps them to the tangent space at μ via the logarithmic map, and then computes denoised tangent representations as weighted means within each composite concept (using CLIP image-to-text probabilities to down-weight noisy images). Primitive directions are obtained by averaging the denoised representations across all compositions containing that primitive, with a centering constraint (sum of vectors per dimension equals zero) guaranteeing uniqueness. The final decomposable approximation is mapped back to the manifold via the exponential map. Sparsity is handled by computing means only over available elements, requiring each primitive to appear in at least one observed tuple, which allows the framework to produce embeddings for unseen combinations.

## Results

On the group robustness benchmark, GDE(IMAGE) achieves a worst-group (WG) accuracy of 86.4% on Waterbirds and 87.5% on CelebA, with a group accuracy gap (GAP) of 5.0 and 0.4 respectively, outperforming all task-specific debiasing methods. On compositional classification (closed-world, CLIP ViT-L/14), GDE(IMAGE) reaches an AUC of 13.9 on UT-Zappos (317.9% relative to zero-shot CLIP) and 8.6 on MIT-states, substantially exceeding the linear decomposition baseline LDE(IMAGE) which scores only 0.9 and 2.0 AUC. GDE also demonstrates strong data efficiency, losing less than 1% WG accuracy when using only 25% of training samples on both group robustness datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDE(IMAGE) decisively outperforms the zero-shot CLIP baseline [54] on all group robustness metrics, improving WG accuracy by 42 points on Waterbirds (86.4 vs. 44.4) and 13.1 points on CelebA (87.5 vs. 74.4). Against the linear decomposition method LDE [67], GDE gains 21.8 WG points on Waterbirds and 3.6 on CelebA, confirming that spherical geometry is essential for visual embeddings. Among task-specific debiasing methods, GDE surpasses FairerCLIP [10] (the closest training-free competitor) by 0.4 WG on Waterbirds and 2.3 WG on CelebA, while also achieving a smaller GAP (5.0 vs. 6.1; 0.4 vs. 2.5). GDE also beats the supervised methods ERM Adapter [16] (76.1 WG) and ERM Linear Probe [32] (65.4 WG) on Waterbirds, and ERM Linear Probe (30.4 WG) and ERM Adapter (40.0 WG) on CelebA, despite requiring no labeled training. Compared to Contrastive Adapters [73] (85.3 WG Waterbirds, 83.9 WG CelebA) and DFR [30] (66.5 WG Waterbirds, 83.9 WG CelebA), GDE achieves the best WG accuracy and smallest GAP on both datasets. On compositional classification, GDE(IMAGE) dramatically outperforms LDE(IMAGE) (AUC 13.9 vs. 0.9 on UT-Zappos; 8.6 vs. 2.0 on MIT-states), while slightly underperforming the full-state CLIP zero-shot baseline on MIT-states (AUC 8.6 vs. 11.1) but exceeding it on UT-Zappos (13.9 vs. 4.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional structure analysis of Trager et al. (ICCV 2023), who demonstrated linear decomposability in CLIP's *text* embedding space, to the *visual* embedding space. The key departure is the finding that linear geometry is insufficient for visual embeddings, motivating a Riemannian-geometric framework (Geodesically Decomposable Embeddings) grounded in Principal Geodesic Analysis. The authors also address two challenges absent in the text setting—noise from extraneous visual cues and sparsity of composite concepts in image collections—by introducing weighted denoising and partial-observation handling. The paper positions itself as the first to systematically investigate whether compositional regularities emerge in the visual latent space of pretrained VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that VLMs spontaneously develop a geometry-aware compositional structure in their visual embeddings offers a new lens for interpreting and controlling these models, with practical payoffs in compositional zero-shot classification and group-robust debiasing (where a training-free method outperforms labeled-data baselines). However, the analysis is limited to attribute–object compositions on relatively small datasets, and it remains open whether the geodesic structure generalizes to richer compositional grammars (spatial relations, multi-object scenes) or to next-generation VLMs with different training objectives. The finding that linear decomposition fails for visual data while succeeding for text also raises broader questions about how modality-specific geometry should inform downstream representation engineering.
