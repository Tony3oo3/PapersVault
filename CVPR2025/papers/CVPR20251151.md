## Problem

Semi-supervised learning for image-text sentiment recognition is undermined by sentiment inconsistency between modalities: images and text frequently convey different emotions, causing standard SSL methods that align or fuse modalities into a shared space to degrade on inconsistent samples. Additionally, large-scale labeled sentiment data is scarce due to annotation cost and inherent subjectivity, making it critical to exploit abundant unlabeled image-text pairs without requiring per-modality labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-text pairs where images are resized to 256×256 and center-cropped to 224×224, and text is tokenized for BERT (English or Chinese variants). In the semi-supervised setting, a small set of labeled pairs (as few as 30–1500 depending on dataset) is combined with 4× more unlabeled pairs. The output is a sentiment class label: binary positive/negative for MVSA, Twitter, and Tourism, or one of seven fine-grained emotion categories (Angry, Bored, Calm, Fear, Happy, Love, Sad) for TumEmo.

## Method

SCRD decouples each modality's features into common and private components via an auto-regressive encoder-decoder with reconstruction, cycle-consistency, margin, and orthogonality losses. Private features train separate unimodal classifiers using a noisy-label-inspired strategy: the multimodal ground-truth (or pseudo-)label is applied to each unimodal classifier, and only samples whose loss falls below a batch-mean-scaled threshold are retained as reliable unimodal supervision, eliminating the need for per-modality annotations. A Modal Selection based Attention (MSeA) module, inspired by mixture-of-experts, dynamically identifies the dominant sentiment modality per sample via a feedforward expert and uses its classifier output as the cross-attention query to fuse with the other two modalities' features. A secondary Pseudo Label Filtering (PLF) step then discards multimodal pseudo-labels whose predicted class mismatches the dominant modality's private-feature prediction, preventing over-reliance on common features under multimodal label guidance.

## Results

SCRD achieves the best accuracy across all five datasets and all label budgets. On the largest dataset TumEmo (14,000 labels), SCRD reaches 78.37% accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline FixMatchLS (76.01%) by 2.36 points and the best fully supervised method DMD (70.75%) by 7.62 points. On the small-scale Tourism dataset (150 labels), SCRD attains 53.45%, outperforming FixMatchLS (48.02%) by 5.43 points. On MVSA-Single (600 labels), SCRD scores 66.60% versus 63.59% for FixMatchLS and 62.33% for the best supervised method CLMLF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCRD outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset and label budget. Among the eight fully supervised methods (MGNNS, Hgraph-CL, INIT, DPRN, CLMLF, MVCN, CMGCN, DMD), the best is DMD at 63.77% on MVSA-M (1500 labels) and 70.75% on TumEmo (14000 labels); SCRD exceeds these by 3.99 and 7.62 points respectively, confirming that SSL with unlabeled data substantially surpasses supervised training. Among the four unimodal SSL extensions (S2-VER, CHMatch, MixText, SAT), CHMatch is the strongest at 65.88% on MVSA-M and 69.73% on TumEmo, still trailing SCRD by 1.88 and 8.64 points. The closest competitor overall is FixMatchLS (the only other multimodal SSL method designed for image-text pairs), which SCRD beats by 1.48–5.43 points depending on dataset and label count; the gap is largest on Tourism (+5.43 at 150 labels) and TumEmo (+6.60 at 700 labels). The two other multimodal SSL baselines, CMML and TCGM, are consistently 2–5 points below SCRD, indicating that their feature-correlation or distribution-matching strategies do not adequately handle modality sentiment inconsistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FixMatch self-training framework (Sohn et al., NeurIPS 2020) and its multimodal extension FixMatchLS (Sirbu et al., COLING 2022), but departs from them by explicitly modeling modality inconsistency—a phenomenon specific to sentiment where image and text can convey opposing emotions. The feature decoupling mechanism is adapted from DMD (Li et al., CVPR 2023), which originally addressed incomplete modalities in emotion recognition, here repurposed to separate shared sentiment from modality-specific sentiment. The noisy-label learning paradigm (Arpit et al., 2017; Han et al., 2018) is repurposed not for label noise but for generating reliable unimodal pseudo-labels from multimodal annotations, a novel application that sidesteps the need for per-modality ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underexplored failure mode: standard multimodal SSL methods degrade specifically on sentiment-inconsistent samples because they assume cross-modal alignment. This insight is practically important for social media applications where sarcasm and irony are common. However, the approach relies on relatively small datasets (largest: TumEmo with ~195K pairs) and fixed ResNet/BERT backbones, leaving open whether the decoupling strategy scales to larger models or more fine-grained emotion taxonomies. The relabeled MVSA dataset, while useful, introduces a small-scale evaluation that may not generalize to the full distribution of real-world sentiment inconsistency.
