## Problem

Discriminative metric depth models (e.g., UniDepth, Metric3D) achieve accurate absolute depth but produce over-smoothed maps lacking fine boundary detail, while generative diffusion-based depth models (e.g., Marigold, Lotus) yield sharp edges but only provide affine-invariant (relative) depth with significant domain gaps on real images. No existing zero-shot method simultaneously delivers metric accuracy and high-frequency detail, leaving a gap for applications requiring both precise scale and crisp object boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251447/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a sharpened metric depth map. Internally, it consumes two pre-computed depth predictions: a metric depth map from UniDepth (normalized to [-1,1] via min-max before VAE encoding) and an affine-invariant depth map from Lotus (scale-shifted to match the metric range). The final output is rescaled to metric depth via least-squares alignment with the initial UniDepth prediction, restricted to pixels where the difference map is below 0.5 to avoid propagating errors in uncertain regions.

## Method

SharpDepth initializes a diffusion-based sharpener from Lotus's architecture and weights, then refines UniDepth's metric predictions using a Noise-aware Gating mechanism: a difference map between the two depth estimates controls per-pixel noise intensity, so high-discrepancy regions receive strong Gaussian noise (directing the model to reconstruct detail) while low-discrepancy regions remain nearly clean. Training is ground-truth-free, relying on two losses: a modified Score Distillation Sampling loss (adapted from ε-prediction to x0-prediction) that distills fine-grained detail from the frozen Lotus teacher, and a Noise-aware Reconstruction loss that weights the L1 distance to the UniDepth output by the difference map, anchoring metric accuracy in uncertain regions. The teacher is progressively replaced by an EMA of the training model, enabling iterative self-improvement. The entire pipeline trains on ~90,000 unlabeled real images across six datasets—100–150× less data than discriminative baselines—using only the two pre-trained depth models as supervision signals.

## Results

On six real-world zero-shot depth estimation benchmarks, SharpDepth achieves an average rank of 3.5 in metric accuracy (δ₁, A.Rel), competitive with UniDepth (rank 2.0) and Metric3Dv2 (rank 1.5). On KITTI it reaches δ₁=97.3% and A.Rel=0.06, while on NuScenes it attains δ₁=78.5% and A.Rel=0.18. Crucially, on depth-detail metrics (PDBE/DBE), SharpDepth achieves the best average rank of 2.0, substantially outperforming UniDepth (rank 7.0) and PatchRefiner (rank 7.2) in boundary completion — e.g., on Sintel, PDBE completion drops from 113.3 (UniDepth) to 36.2 (SharpDepth).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest metric-depth baselines, SharpDepth trades a small accuracy margin for substantially better detail: UniDepth (Avg Rank 2.0) and Metric3Dv2 (Avg Rank 1.5) edge out SharpDepth (Avg Rank 3.5) on δ₁/A.Rel, but SharpDepth's PDBE completion on Sintel is 36.2 vs. 113.3 (UniDepth) and 63.8 (Metric3Dv2), a 3× improvement. Against the naive UniDepth-aligned Lotus baseline, SharpDepth wins on every accuracy metric (e.g., KITTI δ₁ 97.3 vs. 83.6; NuScenes A.Rel 0.18 vs. 0.43), confirming that per-pixel difference-map-guided alignment is far superior to global scale-shift. PatchRefiner, despite similar training data volume, collapses on indoor datasets (NYUv2 δ₁=1.0, Booster δ₁=1.0) while SharpDepth maintains 96.9 and 27.7 respectively. DAv2-Metric variants show competitive detail scores but poor cross-domain accuracy (e.g., DAv2-M Indoor on KITTI δ₁=1.0; DAv2-M Outdoor on NYUv2 δ₁=1.0). On the detail-specific iBims benchmark, SharpDepth achieves the best PDBE accuracy (1.80) and competitive completion (13.1), outperforming UniDepth (2.00 / 30.0) and PatchRefiner (2.49 / 38.3). Marigold, Lotus, and BetterDepth are relative-depth methods requiring GT alignment at test time, so they are reported for reference only and are not directly comparable in the zero-shot metric setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

SharpDepth sits at the intersection of two established lines of zero-shot monocular depth estimation: discriminative metric models (UniDepth, Metric3Dv2, ZoeDepth) that achieve accurate scale but produce over-smoothed boundaries, and diffusion-based generative models (Marigold, Lotus) that yield sharp edges but only relative depth. The paper departs from prior refinement approaches (BetterDepth, PatchRefiner) that relied on synthetic data with dense ground truth by introducing a ground-truth-free training protocol that uses only real images and two pretrained depth models as mutual teachers. The core architectural contribution is the Noise-aware Gating mechanism, which selectively injects noise into a latent depth map based on a difference map between the two estimators, combined with an adapted SDS loss (for detail distillation) and a Noise-aware Reconstruction loss (for metric anchoring) that together enable training without any depth annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 100–150× reduction in training data (≈90K images vs. millions for discriminative baselines) while maintaining competitive metric accuracy and substantially improving boundary sharpness, making high-quality depth estimation more accessible. A key open question is how well the difference-map gating generalizes when both input models fail in correlated ways (e.g., both misestimate depth in the same region), since the mechanism assumes disagreement signals uncertainty. The approach also raises the broader question of whether this 'sharpen a metric prediction with a diffusion prior' paradigm can be extended to other dense prediction tasks (normals, segmentation) where a similar accuracy-vs-detail tradeoff exists.
