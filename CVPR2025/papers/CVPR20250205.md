## Problem

Prompt learning for vision-language models (e.g., CoOp on CLIP) remains vulnerable to overfitting under noisy labels when trained with cross-entropy loss, despite being more robust than other parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods. The paper identifies that mean absolute error (MAE) loss—long known for robustness but rarely used in noisy-label learning due to slow convergence—actually performs well specifically in the prompt learning setting, and seeks to explain and exploit this phenomenon.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed by CLIP's image encoder, ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) and a learnable text prompt consisting of 16 shared context tokens plus a class-specific token (processed by CLIP's 63M-parameter text transformer) as input. It produces a class label via cosine similarity between the image feature and per-class text features, and an intermediate OT transportation matrix (C×N) that partitions the training set into clean and noisy subsets. Training uses 16-shot samples per class with 200 epochs under SGD with cosine annealing.

## Method

NLPrompt combines two components: (1) PromptMAE, which replaces cross-entropy with MAE loss for prompt learning; the paper proves via feature learning theory that MAE suppresses the influence of noisy samples by preserving task-relevant feature coefficients while limiting degradation from label-flipped samples, yielding lower test loss than CE under noise. (2) PromptOT, which uses the VLM's text features (generated by the pre-trained text encoder from class prompts) as prototypes in a Sinkhorn-regularized optimal transport problem, computing a transportation matrix whose column argmax yields pseudo-labels for partitioning data into clean and noisy subsets. The final training loss applies CE to the clean subset (where CE outperforms MAE) and MAE to the noisy subset (where MAE is more robust), harmonizing the strengths of both losses under the OT-based purification.

## Results

NLPrompt achieves state-of-the-art test accuracy across 7 synthetic noisy datasets and 1 real-world noisy dataset (Food101N) under both symmetric and asymmetric noise at rates from 12.5% to 75%. On Flowers102 at 50% symmetric noise, NLPrompt reaches 89.90% accuracy versus 70.10% (CoOp), 84.07% (GCE), and 70.23% (JoAPR). On the real-world Food101N dataset, NLPrompt attains 76.46% accuracy, outperforming CoOp (69.50%), GCE (71.32%), and JoAPR (72.57%). At the most extreme 75% noise level, NLPrompt maintains 76.80% on Flowers102 and 49.27% on UCF101, while CoOp drops to 37.17% and 26.30% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NLPrompt consistently outperforms all six baselines across nearly every dataset and noise level. Against CoOp [60], the gap widens dramatically with noise: at 75% symmetric noise on Flowers102, NLPrompt leads by 39.6 points (76.80 vs. 37.17), and on StanfordCars by 35.4 points (58.30 vs. 22.90). Against GCE (CoOp+GCE) [53], which is the strongest single-loss baseline, NLPrompt still leads by 5.8 points on Flowers102 at 50% symmetric noise (89.90 vs. 84.07) and by 7.4 points on StanfordCars at 75% (58.30 vs. 50.87). Against JoAPR [19], the most competitive noisy-label prompt method, NLPrompt wins by 12.9 points on Flowers102 at 50% symmetric noise (89.90 vs. 70.23) and by 15.7 points on EuroSAT at 75% (43.80 vs. 27.33); the closest margin is on DTD at 75% asymmetric noise where NLPrompt edges out JoAPR by only 0.11 points (28.37 vs. 28.26). In the generalization study (Table 3), NLPrompt's MAE+OT strategy transfers to VPT, MaPLe, and PromptSRC, improving each by 6–14 points at 50% noise (e.g., VPT+Ours 86.93 vs. VPT 61.37; MaPLe+Ours 76.43 vs. MaPLe 55.40). On the real-world Food101N dataset, NLPrompt achieves 76.46% accuracy, surpassing JoAPR (72.57%) by 3.89 points and CoOp (69.50%) by 6.96 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CoOp prompt learning framework (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022) by addressing the underexplored problem of noisy labels in vision-language prompt tuning. It departs from prior noisy-label prompt learning methods (GCE by Wu et al., ICCV 2023; JoAPR by Guo et al., CVPR 2024) by showing that the simple MAE loss—long considered unsuitable for noisy-label classification due to slow convergence—becomes effective specifically in the prompt learning regime, and by replacing the randomly initialized prototypes in OT-based purification (Feng et al., CVPR 2023) with semantically meaningful text features from the VLM's text encoder. The theoretical grounding draws on feature learning theory (Pan et al., 2024) to explain why MAE suppresses noisy-sample influence on task-relevant prompt coefficients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a traditionally 'bad' loss (MAE) becomes robust in prompt learning provides a useful conceptual bridge between loss-function robustness literature and the prompt-tuning community, and the OT purification using VLM text features is a natural, low-cost addition that practitioners can adopt immediately. However, the evaluation is limited to seven small-scale datasets (16-shot) with only symmetric and asymmetric noise, and the single real-world noisy dataset (Food101N) provides limited external validation. Open questions include whether the MAE advantage persists under instance-dependent or class-dependent noise, whether it extends to larger-scale or unbalanced settings (acknowledged as future work), and whether the theoretical guarantees hold beyond the binary-classification simplification used in the analysis.
