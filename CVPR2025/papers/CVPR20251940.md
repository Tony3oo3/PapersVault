## Problem

Reconstructing 4D scenes from a single monocular video is inherently under-constrained, and existing methods that incorporate data-driven priors (depth, segmentation, optical flow) require hours of gradient-based optimization to align splatted 2D feature maps of explicit Gaussians with these priors. This computational bottleneck limits practical deployment for large-scale or real-time applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251940/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a casually captured monocular video (hundreds of frames at 960×720) along with per-frame camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and internally generates depth maps via DepthCrafter, foreground segmentation via Track Anything, dense 2D tracks via TAPIR, and camera parameters via Droid-SLAM. It produces a global 4D scene map of dynamic and static isotropic 3D Gaussian primitives with per-timestep position and color offsets, supporting real-time novel view rendering (~160 FPS) at any queried timestep as well as 3D and 2D point tracking. A 100-frame sequence is reconstructed in approximately 5.3 minutes.

## Method

The key insight is to exploit the explicit spatial definition of 3D Gaussian primitives to directly apply data priors (depth, 2D tracks) rather than relying on slow gradient-based alignment. The framework operates in a streaming manner, processing each new frame through three stages: (1) anchor-based propagation, where 2D tracks are lifted into 3D anchor points and existing dynamic Gaussians are moved via KNN-weighted local rigidity; (2) scene expansion via a Canonical Gaussian Map (CGM) that establishes a pixel-to-Gaussian one-to-one mapping with an alignment loss, adding new Gaussians only in unconstructed regions identified by rendered density and depth discrepancies; and (3) a fast component-specific optimization (200 iterations total per frame) that refines position/color offsets with rigidity and color-consistency regularization while keeping canonical states fixed. This streaming design avoids the global optimization bottleneck, achieving a 20× speedup over Shape of Motion and 35× over Dynamic Gaussian Marbles while matching or exceeding their reconstruction quality.

## Results

On the DyCheck iPhone dataset, 4D-Fly achieves mPSNR 17.03, mSSIM 0.60, and mLPIPS 0.37 while processing 100 frames in just 5.3 minutes (163 FPS at test time). On the NVIDIA Dynamic Scenes dataset, it reaches mPSNR 22.52, mSSIM 0.69, and mLPIPS 0.14 in 5.1 minutes. This represents a 35× speed-up over Dynamic Gaussian Marbles (187.6 min) and a 20× speed-up over Shape of Motion (109.4 min) while matching or exceeding their reconstruction quality. For point tracking, 4D-Fly achieves 3D EPE of 0.08 and Occlusion Accuracy of 85.4%, tying Shape of Motion on EPE while surpassing it on OA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4D-Fly decisively outperforms all NeRF-based baselines (T-NeRF, HyperNeRF) on both quality and speed, achieving mPSNR 17.03 vs. 15.72/15.83 on DyCheck iPhone while being >200× faster. Against the multi-view Gaussian method 4D Gaussians, 4D-Fly improves mPSNR by 3.92 dB (17.03 vs. 13.11) and is 4× faster. Among prior-guided monocular methods, 4D-Fly surpasses Dynamic Gaussian Marbles on all three NVS metrics (mPSNR 17.03 vs. 15.79, mSSIM 0.60 vs. 0.59, mLPIPS 0.37 vs. 0.44) while being 35× faster. Compared to Shape of Motion, 4D-Fly achieves higher mPSNR (17.03 vs. 16.42) and lower mLPIPS (0.37 vs. 0.43) on DyCheck iPhone, and higher mPSNR (22.52 vs. 22.07) on NVIDIA Dynamic Scenes, all at 20× less training time. For point tracking, 4D-Fly ties Shape of Motion on 3D EPE (0.08) and slightly trails on δ3^5 (40.1 vs. 43.2) and δ3^10 (70.3 vs. 72.3), but wins on Occlusion Accuracy (85.4 vs. 82.7) and surpasses TAPIR+DC on all 3D tracking metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the trajectory-based 4D Gaussian representation introduced by Dynamic Gaussian Marbles [35] and the broader line of monocular 4D reconstruction work that incorporates data-driven priors (depth, segmentation, optical flow) to regularize optimization. It departs fundamentally from prior methods by replacing the expensive global or per-frame optimization loop—where 2D-splatted Gaussian features must be aligned to priors through gradient descent—with a streaming paradigm that directly lifts 2D tracking and depth priors onto explicit 3D Gaussian primitives via an anchor-based propagation strategy. The Canonical Gaussian Map (CGM) further distinguishes the approach by providing a well-defined canonical state for newly added dynamic Gaussians, addressing a gap in unprojection-based initialization for dynamic scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× speedup (from hours to ~5 minutes for 100 frames) makes monocular 4D reconstruction practically viable for applications like AR/VR content creation and autonomous navigation where real-time or near-real-time processing is needed. The streaming paradigm—propagate, expand, optimize per frame—offers a reusable architectural pattern for other sequential 3D reconstruction tasks. However, the method's reliance on external foundation models (DepthCrafter, TAPIR, Track Anything) introduces error propagation, and the authors acknowledge limitations with complex illumination changes and non-rigid deformations. A key open question is whether feed-forward or learned approaches can replace the per-frame optimization step entirely, and whether the rigidity assumption generalizes to highly deformable objects.
