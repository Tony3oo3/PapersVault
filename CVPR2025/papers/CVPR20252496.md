## Problem

Existing view-conditioned diffusion models for novel view synthesis (e.g., Zero-1-to-3) are trained on single-object datasets and, when applied to multi-object indoor scenes, produce objects that disappear, distort, or are incorrectly placed under novel views due to a lack of structural awareness of object composition. Additionally, there is no systematic evaluation framework for cross-view structural consistency in multi-object NVS beyond standard image-level metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252496/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an input RGB image, relative camera pose (R, T), a monocular depth map, and a continuous object mask image (rendered with instance IDs and normalized) as conditions. At inference, depth and mask are obtained via off-the-shelf predictors (Marigold and SAM). The depth and mask are each replicated to three channels to simulate RGB, then encoded through a VAE before being concatenated with the noisy target latent. The model outputs a synthesized novel-view RGB image and, as an auxiliary output, a predicted novel-view object mask image.

## Method

MOVIS extends the Zero-1-to-3 view-conditioned diffusion paradigm with three structural-awareness mechanisms. First, structure-aware features (depth and object mask latents) are concatenated with the noisy target image as additional U-Net inputs, while a DINOv2 image encoder provides semantic features via cross-attention alongside the camera pose. Second, an auxiliary head predicts the target-view object mask conditioned on the noisy image and structure-aware features, jointly trained with the diffusion loss at weight γ=0.1 to enforce correct object placement and instance differentiation. Third, a structure-guided timestep sampling scheduler replaces uniform sampling with a Gaussian whose mean linearly decays from μ_global=1000 to μ_local=500 over training (with a 4000-step warmup at μ_global), prioritizing global placement learning at large timesteps and fine-grained geometry recovery at smaller timesteps. The model is trained on C3DFS, a 100k-composite synthetic dataset built from 3D-FUTURE furniture assets.

## Results

On the primary C3DFS test set, MOVIS achieves PSNR 17.43, SSIM 0.825, LPIPS 0.171, object-placement IoU 58.1, cross-view Hit Rate 19.3, and matching Dist 44.9, outperforming all baselines on every metric. Generalization to unseen datasets is strong: on Objaverse, MOVIS reaches PSNR 17.75 and IoU 51.3 (vs. 15.98/35.6 for the best baseline Free3D), and on Room-Texture it attains IoU 24.2 and Hit Rate 4.4 (vs. 19.7/2.1 for Free3D). The largest gains appear in cross-view consistency metrics (Hit Rate and Dist), where MOVIS improves by roughly 3–4× over the strongest single-object baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOVIS outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all three evaluation datasets. On C3DFS, the gains over the strongest single-object baseline (Zero-1-to-3) are substantial: +3.2 dB PSNR, +24.4 IoU points, and a 4.4× improvement in Hit Rate (19.3 vs. 4.4), indicating far more accurate object placement and cross-view structural consistency. Free3D is the closest competitor on Objaverse (PSNR 15.98 vs. 17.75; IoU 35.6 vs. 51.3), but MOVIS still leads by ~1.8 dB and ~16 IoU points. ZeroNVS, trained on mixed real-world scenes, is consistently the weakest method, trailing MOVIS by 6–7 dB in PSNR and 30–40 IoU points, suggesting that scene-level training without explicit multi-object structural cues is insufficient. Notably, retraining Zero-1-to-3 on C3DFS (Zero-1-to-3†) does not close the gap and actually degrades Hit Rate (1.3 on C3DFS, 1.2 on Objaverse), confirming that the architectural and training-strategy changes in MOVIS—rather than simply more multi-object data—are the critical factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOVIS extends the view-conditioned diffusion paradigm established by Zero-1-to-3 from single-object to multi-object novel view synthesis, addressing the structural inconsistencies (object disappearance, misplacement, distortion) that arise when single-object models are naively applied to compositional scenes. It departs from the prevailing divide-and-conquer pipeline (segment → amodal completion → per-object 3D lifting → spatial compositing) by proposing an end-to-end image-conditioned model that directly handles multi-object complexity. The structure-guided timestep sampling scheduler draws on prior observations about noise scheduling importance [4, 28] but applies a principled linear decay motivated by an analysis of what the denoising process learns at different timesteps in the multi-object setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-object NVS is a natural and practically critical extension for applications in interior design, robotics, and embodied AI, yet prior scene-level NVS methods (ZeroNVS, MegaScenes) focus on small view changes and simple object counts. The proposed cross-view consistency metrics (Hit Rate, matching distance via MASt3R) fill a gap in evaluation, revealing structural failures invisible to standard PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS. Limitations include the focus on foreground objects with background left for future work, and the primary evaluation on synthetic composites (C3DFS) with real-world generalization (SUNRGB-D) shown only qualitatively. Open questions include whether the structure-guided scheduler generalizes to outdoor or dynamic multi-object scenes and whether the auxiliary mask task can be replaced by learned representations at scale.
