## Problem

Indoor scene relighting from a single image remains challenging due to multiple overlapping light sources, complex material interactions, and cluttered geometry. Existing generative relighting methods either require dense 3D scene capture or rely on latent-space/reference-image control that only induces coarse global lighting changes, leaving no mechanism for the user to specify local, fine-grained lighting adjustments such as turning a specific lamp on or adding a cast shadow in a particular region.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB indoor photograph, a binary scribble map where 1 indicates brightening, 0 indicates darkening, and 0.5 (unlabeled) leaves the region to the model's prior, plus a surface normal map (estimated via DSINE) and a text prompt (generated by BLIP-2). The output is a relit RGB image of the same scene with modified illumination while preserving the original albedo. Scribbles are intentionally sparse and high-level rather than pixel-precise shading specifications, and the model is expected to infer physically plausible lighting effects (soft highlights, inter-reflections, gradual falloff) in unlabeled regions.

## Method

ScribbleLight uses a two-stage training pipeline built on Stable Diffusion v2. In Stage 1, the U-Net is extended with an albedo latent channel (zero-initialized) so the diffusion process is conditioned on the scene's intrinsic color and texture; a fixed noise level (T=200) is added to the albedo latent to tolerate imperfect IID predictions and to allow the model to generate diverse lighting rather than simply copying the albedo. In Stage 2, a ControlNet is trained jointly with a control encoder-decoder: the encoder maps the concatenated scribble and normal maps into a lighting feature vector, while the decoder regularizes this latent by reconstructing the target monochromatic shading and normals, ensuring the latent retains sufficient geometric and shading information. The ControlNet is initialized from the original (non-albedo-conditioned) Stable Diffusion weights and trained with a combined denoising loss and decoder reconstruction loss. Training uses 100K LSUN Bedrooms images with auto-generated scribbles derived from thresholded IID shading maps, followed by morphological dilation/erosion to simulate hand-drawn imprecision.

## Results

On the BigTime test set (206 image pairs), ScribbleLight achieves RMSE 0.206, PSNR 14.29, SSIM 0.436, and LPIPS 0.394 (mean over 5 diffusion seeds), outperforming both baselines across all four metrics. Against the closest baseline RGB↔X, ScribbleLight reduces RMSE by 23% (0.206 vs. 0.269) and improves PSNR by 1.8 dB (14.29 vs. 12.47). In a user study with 30 participants, 95.6% preferred ScribbleLight's relit results over RGB↔X.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScribbleLight outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against LightIt* (retrained on LSUN Bedrooms with scribble input), ScribbleLight reduces RMSE from 0.341 to 0.206 and improves PSNR from 9.61 to 14.29; LightIt* fails to preserve the albedo of the source image, producing lighting that diverges significantly from the target. Against RGB↔X (used without retraining, with scribbles replacing the shading map), ScribbleLight achieves lower RMSE (0.206 vs. 0.269) and lower LPIPS (0.394 vs. 0.439); RGB↔X unrealistically overlays the coarse scribble annotation rather than interpreting it as a lighting intent. Against IC-Light (compared on 50 test images with carefully crafted prompts), ScribbleLight again wins on all metrics (RMSE 0.210 vs. 0.278, PSNR 14.21 vs. 11.46), while IC-Light often fails to adhere to turn-on/off prompts and alters background geometry and texture. The user study further confirms the advantage: 95.6% of 30 participants preferred ScribbleLight over RGB↔X when asked to select the best relit image conforming to the scribble while preserving geometry and texture.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScribbleLight extends the ControlNet architecture (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) and Stable Diffusion v2 (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) into a two-stage pipeline: an albedo-conditioned diffusion model that preserves intrinsic color/texture, followed by a scribble-and-normal-guided ControlNet for local lighting control. It departs from prior indoor relighting methods that rely on explicit lighting representations (shadow maps, irradiance fields) or implicit latent-space navigation, which only offer coarse global adjustments. The scribble-based control paradigm draws from interactive image manipulation work (segmentation, inpainting, portrait relighting via LightPainter), but is here applied to the substantially more complex setting of multi-source indoor scenes with cluttered geometry and varied materials.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ScribbleLight is the first method to enable fine-grained, local lighting control in single-image indoor relighting, opening a practical workflow for real estate, virtual staging, and interior design where users can iteratively refine lighting without 3D reconstruction or dense capture. Key limitations include the inability to handle colored lighting adjustments and the generation of physically implausible results when scribbles contradict scene geometry. Open questions include whether colored scribbles or richer annotation modalities can improve user precision, and whether the approach generalizes beyond bedroom-scale scenes to larger or more complex indoor environments.
