## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods rely on COLMAP for camera parameters, which frequently fails on in-the-wild monocular videos, and their deformation models (MLPs, grids, fixed-degree polynomials) either lack flexibility for complex motions or incur high computational cost. Additionally, no prior dynamic 3DGS method has demonstrated reliable novel spatio-temporal view synthesis at unseen intermediate timestamps, leaving a gap in continuous trajectory modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251076/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence along with pre-computed metric depth maps (from UniDepth) and binary motion masks (from Track Anything), plus 2D point tracks from CoTracker for control-point initialization. It outputs rendered novel spatio-temporal views at arbitrary camera poses and time indices, jointly with estimated per-frame camera extrinsics and a shared intrinsic matrix. Notably, no COLMAP or any external SfM preprocessing is required; camera parameters are learned end-to-end via a shallow MLP with positional encoding of the time index.

## Method

SplineGS models each dynamic 3D Gaussian's trajectory with a cubic Hermite spline defined by a small set of learnable 3D control points, where tangents are approximated from neighboring points. A Motion-Adaptive Control Points Pruning (MACP) mechanism iteratively removes one control point after each densification step if the projected position error stays below a threshold, so simple motions use fewer points while complex motions retain more. Camera parameters are predicted by a shallow MLP and jointly optimized with 3D Gaussian attributes using photometric consistency (warping reference-frame colors via metric depth), geometric consistency (3D unprojection alignment), and a binary dice loss on rendered vs. ground-truth motion masks. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up phase that optimizes only camera parameters, followed by a main phase that jointly refines Gaussians, splines, and cameras with RGB, depth, and consistency losses.

## Results

On the NVIDIA dataset, SplineGS (SfM-free) achieves the best average PSNR of 27.21 dB and lowest LPIPS of 0.053, outperforming the best SfM-based method (MoSca w/ COLMAP: 26.55/0.070) and the best SfM-free method (MoSca: 26.61/0.069). For novel view and time synthesis (unseen intermediate timestamps), SplineGS achieves 25.92 PSNR / 0.098 LPIPS / 0.703 tOF, substantially surpassing the next best method DynNeRF (23.36/0.219/0.921). SplineGS renders at 400 FPS, which is 890× faster than RoDynRF (0.45 FPS) and 8,000× faster than DynNeRF (0.05 FPS), with a training time of only 1.5 hours (SfM-free) versus >24 hours for NeRF-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplineGS outperforms all 17 baselines on both PSNR and LPIPS in the SfM-free setting, with the closest competitor being MoSca (26.61/0.069 vs. SplineGS 27.21/0.053, a +0.60 dB PSNR and −0.016 LPIPS margin). Among SfM-based methods, SplineGS w/ COLMAP (26.90/0.060) also leads, beating MoSca w/ COLMAP (26.55/0.070) and RoDynRF w/ COLMAP (25.89/0.067). In the more challenging novel view and time synthesis task (unseen intermediate timestamps), SplineGS (25.92 PSNR) surpasses DynNeRF (23.36) by 2.56 dB and RoDynRF (21.58) by 4.34 dB, while 3DGS-based baselines degrade severely (STGS: 15.72, 4DGS: 17.07, D3DGS: 19.63). On rendering speed, SplineGS at 400 FPS is 10× faster than STGS (900 FPS is the only faster 3DGS method but with much lower quality at 22.17 PSNR) and 890× faster than RoDynRF. Training time is 1.5 h for SplineGS SfM-free versus >24 h for RoDynRF and DynNeRF, and 0.5 h with COLMAP versus 7.0 h for Kinematic Fields.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplineGS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [20] to dynamic scenes by replacing the MLP-based (D3DGS, SC-GS), grid-based (4DGS), and polynomial-trajectory (STGS) deformation models with cubic Hermite splines, drawing on classic curve modeling from computer graphics. It also joins the COLMAP-free neural rendering line (RoDynRF, MoSca, BARF, NeRF-) but is the first to combine spline-based dynamic 3DGS with joint camera estimation via photometric and geometric consistency in a two-stage optimization. The Motion-Adaptive Control Points Pruning (MACP) mechanism is a novel contribution that adaptively allocates spline complexity per Gaussian based on motion difficulty, a degree of adaptivity absent in prior fixed-degree or fixed-resolution approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The COLMAP-free capability is practically significant because SfM pipelines like COLMAP frequently fail on in-the-wild monocular videos (as demonstrated on DAVIS), and SplineGS matches or exceeds COLMAP-assisted baselines without that preprocessing. The 400 FPS rendering speed makes real-time dynamic NVS from casual video feasible. However, the method still relies on external priors (UniDepth for metric depth, CoTracker for 2D tracking, SAM-based motion masks), is per-scene optimized, and is validated only on relatively short sequences (NVIDIA ~100 frames, DAVIS ~70 frames); scalability to longer videos, more complex multi-object interactions, and fully autonomous operation without depth/tracking priors remain open questions.
