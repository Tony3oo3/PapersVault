## Problem

Aligning text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences via direct preference optimization (DPO) suffers from low training efficiency and subpar generation quality because existing methods distribute sparse rewards across all denoising steps in the long Markov chain. This paper tackles the specific challenge of how to allocate implicit rewards to latent variables at any timestep without requiring the full multi-step denoising trajectory, enabling efficient single-step fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252527/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired human preference data (winner/loser image pairs with a shared text prompt) from the Pick-a-Pic v2 dataset (851,293 pairs, 58,960 prompts) and fine-tunes a pretrained latent diffusion model (SD1.5 or SDXL-base-1.0). At inference, the aligned model takes a text prompt and produces a 512×512 or 1024×1024 RGB image, and the same fine-tuned weights transfer directly to conditional generation tasks (depth-map, canny-edge, inpainting) via ControlNet without additional training.

## Method

DDIM-InPO reparameterizes the DDIM denoising step with an 'initial' variable x₀(t) = x̄_t − σ_t·ε_θ^t(x_t, c), treating the diffusion model as a timestep-aware single-step generator p_θ^c(x₀|x_t). It then uses DDIM inversion to solve for the noise δ_t(x₀(t)) that maps a clean image back to its latent variable x_t at any timestep, so that x_t can regenerate x₀ in one step. The DPO objective is reformulated over the joint distribution p_θ^c(x₀, x_t), and the resulting loss (Eq. 18) replaces the random noise used by Diffusion-DPO with the inversion-derived δ_t, concentrating gradient updates on latent variables directly correlated with the target image. This yields a 18.4× speedup over Diffusion-KTO and 3.6× over Diffusion-DPO while achieving state-of-the-art win-rates in just 400 fine-tuning steps.

## Results

DDIM-InPO achieves state-of-the-art preference alignment with only 400 fine-tuning steps. On the HPDv2 test set, InPO-SDXL attains a 65.81% HPSv2 win-rate over DPO-SDXL and 85.38% over Base-SDXL, while InPO-SD1.5 reaches 84.06% HPSv2 win-rate over DPO-SD1.5 and 90.22% over Base-SD1.5. Training efficiency is dramatically improved: DDIM-InPO is 18.4× faster than Diffusion-KTO and 3.6× faster than Diffusion-DPO while producing higher-quality images. In user studies, human evaluators consistently prefer DDIM-InPO outputs over all baselines across overall preference, visual attractiveness, and text alignment criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDIM-InPO wins against all four baselines on nearly every evaluator and test set. Against Base-SDXL and Base-SD1.5, the gains are largest (HPSv2 win-rates of 85.38% and 90.22% on HPDv2), confirming that preference alignment substantially improves over unaligned generation. Against DPO-SDXL (Diffusion-DPO [63]), InPO-SDXL achieves a 65.81% HPSv2 win-rate on HPDv2 and 60.11% on Parti-Prompts, while InPO-SD1.5 beats DPO-SD1.5 by 84.06% HPSv2 on HPDv2. Against KTO-SD1.5 (Diffusion-KTO [32]), InPO-SD1.5 leads with 60.94% HPSv2 win-rate on HPDv2. The one notable exception is CLIP score on Parti-Prompts, where InPO-SDXL vs. Base-SDXL (50.55%) and vs. DPO-SDXL (42.28%) fall below 50%, suggesting a slight trade-off in text-image alignment on that specific prompt set. In terms of training efficiency, DDIM-InPO requires only 57.6 GPU-hours versus 204.8 for DPO-SD1.5 (3.6× faster) and far less than KTO (18.4× faster), as shown in Figure 4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al.) to diffusion models by departing from the multi-step trajectory formulation of Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024). The key conceptual shift is reparameterizing DDIM (Song et al.) so that the diffusion model is treated as a timestep-aware single-step generative model, then using DDIM inversion (inspired by Mokady et al. and Lukoianov et al.) to identify only the latent variables strongly correlated with the target image for optimization. Rather than distributing reward across all denoising steps (as Diffusion-DPO and Diffusion-KTO do), DDIM-InPO restricts fine-tuning to a small set of relevant latents, directly addressing the sparse-reward problem inherent in long Markov chains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.6× speedup over Diffusion-DPO and 18.4× over Diffusion-KTO while matching or exceeding quality makes preference alignment of large T2I models substantially more tractable, potentially enabling more frequent re-alignment as preference data evolves. The single-step reframing suggests a broader principle: multi-step generative processes may not require trajectory-wide optimization for preference alignment, which could inform future work on video diffusion or other iterative generative models. Limitations include sensitivity to offline dataset quality (risk of inappropriate outputs), and the authors themselves flag online training as a promising but unexplored direction.
