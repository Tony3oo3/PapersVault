## Problem

Generalizable human-to-mobile-robot (H2MR) handover requires a mobile robot to receive objects across a large workspace while coordinating base and arm movements, yet existing H2R handover methods are limited to fixed-base arms and lack full-body human motion modeling. Real-world H2R training is non-scalable due to safety risks, and prior synthetic pipelines (e.g., GenH2R) produce only partial hand motions without the diversity, interactivity, and mobile-base coordination needed for realistic mobile handover scenarios.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes segmented point clouds of the human body, hand, and object from both a head camera and a wrist camera, all aligned to the robot's end-effector frame; the wrist camera supplements the head camera to overcome arm occlusion at close range. These point clouds are enriched with 4D flow information computed via ICP registration between consecutive frames. The output is a 9D egocentric action vector: 6D for the robotic arm (3D gripper translation + 3D rotation) and 3D for the mobile base (2D translation + 1D rotation), decoded simultaneously from a shared global feature.

## Method

MobileH2R comprises three components. First, MobileH2R-Sim generates over 100K diverse interactive handover scenes via a two-stage motion pipeline: Guided Motion Diffusion (trained on AMASS) produces task-independent full-body pre-handover motions, while a task-specific kinematic synthesizer generates arm-only handover motions, with an interactive human agent that transitions phases based on robot proximity. Second, an automatic demonstration pipeline uses CHOMP with future obstacle avoidance (exploiting oracle knowledge of future human and robot positions over a time window), face-to-face final pose constraints, and a differentiable vision neural loss—implemented via a pose prediction network and a vision-state recovery estimator—to ensure generated trajectories are both collision-free and vision-action learnable. Third, a 4D imitation policy uses a modified PointNet++ with separate set abstraction layers at different sampling radii for the human body and hand-object point clouds (addressing their scale mismatch), concatenates the resulting features, and decodes coordinated base-arm actions through a single MLP, supervised with joint base/arm losses plus an auxiliary object-pose prediction loss.

## Results

On the three test sets (m0: simple handover, n0: complex full-body motion, s0: real mocap data), MobileH2R achieves 63.8%, 53.4%, and 77.8% success rates respectively, with Average Success (AS) scores of 34.81, 28.68, and 50.65. In sim-to-real transfer on the Galbot G1 robot, the method reaches 80.0% success in the simple setting and 63.3% in the complex setting, compared to 40.0% and 30.0% for the GenH2R(reprod.) baseline. The policy requires only ~0.003s inference time per step, versus >0.1s for motion-planning-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileH2R outperforms all baselines across every test set. Against the non-end-to-end 'Grasp Selection + Trajectory Planning' baseline, it achieves +23.6%, +18.6%, and +36.8% higher success rates on m0, n0, and s0 respectively, while reducing execution time by ~5s on average. Against the original GenH2R (fixed-base, no human body modeling), the improvement is dramatic: from 4.80% to 63.80% on m0 and from 3.10% to 53.40% on n0, as the original model lacks awareness of full-body human motion and mobile-base coordination. Against the retrained GenH2R(reprod.) which incorporates collision-free demonstrations and inverse-kinematics-based base control, MobileH2R still gains +17.0%, +20.5%, and +16.7% success rate on m0, n0, and s0, attributed to its explicit human-body point cloud input and learned 9D coordinated base-arm actions. In the real-world sim-to-real evaluation (Table 5), MobileH2R achieves 80.0% vs. 40.0% (m0) and 63.3% vs. 30.0% (n0) success over GenH2R(reprod.), confirming the advantage transfers beyond simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GenH2R's fixed-base human-to-robot handover framework to the mobile-base setting, adding coordinated base-arm action output and full-body human motion modeling. It departs from prior H2R work (HandoverSim, GenH2R) that relies on mocap data or simple randomized hand trajectories by introducing a two-stage synthetic motion pipeline (Guided Motion Diffusion for full-body pre-handover motion plus task-specific arm synthesis for the handover phase). The demonstration generation strategy builds on CHOMP-based TAMP but introduces a novel vision neural loss that makes demonstrations imitation-friendly by ensuring the rendered vision can recover object pose, and a future obstacle avoidance loss for proactive collision prevention in the mobile setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that generalizable mobile-robot handover skills can be learned entirely from scalable synthetic data without any real-world robot demonstrations or human mocap, which is significant for the safety and cost constraints of HRI training. The 100K+ scene pipeline and the finding that asset diversity (complex full-body motions) matters as much as demonstration count provide a practical recipe for scaling HRI learning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-robot or multi-human scenarios, how well it transfers across different mobile robot morphologies beyond the single Galbot G1 platform tested, and whether the sim-to-real gap widens with more complex real-world clutter and lighting.
