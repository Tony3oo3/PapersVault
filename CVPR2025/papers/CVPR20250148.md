## Problem

Diffusion models for multi-contrast MR image synthesis struggle to preserve anatomical consistency across modalities due to the stochastic nature of the denoising process, and they fail to effectively exploit the complementary structural information present in multiple contrast inputs. Existing methods either lack explicit mechanisms to enforce cross-contrast anatomical alignment or initialize generation from pure noise, discarding the rich structural priors available from the available contrasts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250148/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes C−1 available 2D contrast MR slices (256×256 pixels; e.g., 3 of 4 contrasts on BraTS 2021, 2 of 3 on ADNI) and a precomputed prior codebook containing per-contrast index mappings, Bezier curve transformation functions, and intensity scaling weights. It outputs the single missing contrast image at the same resolution. The codebook is built offline by computing VLM attention maps across all training image pairs and generating Bezier-augmented variants, so at inference the model retrieves a structurally similar reference and applies the stored transformation and scaling to produce an anatomically informed initialization.

## Method

APT first extracts per-contrast features with a frozen BiomedCLIP vision encoder and fuses them through a mutual information fusion module (self-attention + FFN) that produces a subject-level representation z_s^all; a contrastive anatomical consistency loss then aligns this full-contrast representation with representations from random C−1 subsets. The fused vector conditions a U-Net-based conditional diffusion model via cross-attention at every denoising step. At inference, a two-stage strategy is used: Stage 1 retrieves a prior-informed guidance image from the codebook (via similarity-matched index, Bezier transformation, and intensity scaling) and starts the reverse diffusion from an intermediate step M rather than pure noise; Stage 2 performs N iterative unrolling steps near t=0, updating the predicted clean image through weighted averaging between successive predictions to refine fine anatomical details while preserving global structure.

## Results

On BraTS 2021, APT achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all four contrasts: FLAIR (25.52 dB / 0.920), T1-w (25.88 dB / 0.942), T1Gd (24.54 dB / 0.903), and T2-w (27.44 dB / 0.951). On ADNI, APT leads on T1-w (20.82 dB / 0.804) and T2-w (24.56 dB / 0.854), and achieves the highest SSIM on PD (0.849) despite SynDiff having a marginally higher PSNR (24.75 vs. 24.48 dB). Overall, APT improves PSNR by 1–2 dB and SSIM by 0.01–0.04 over the strongest baseline on most contrasts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APT outperforms all six baselines on PSNR and SSIM for every BraTS 2021 contrast, with the largest gains on T2-w (+1.14 dB PSNR over ResViT) and FLAIR (+1.16 dB over HiNET). On ADNI, APT leads on T1-w and T2-w across both metrics, but on PD-weighted images SynDiff achieves a slightly higher PSNR (24.75 vs. 24.48 dB) while APT still wins on SSIM (0.849 vs. 0.821). Among GAN-based baselines, MM-GAN and HiNET are the strongest, yet APT surpasses them by 0.4–1.2 dB PSNR on BraTS. Among diffusion-based baselines, SynDiff and M2DN are the closest competitors; APT improves over SynDiff by 3.54 dB on BraTS FLAIR and over M2DN by 1.69 dB on BraTS T2-w. The consistent advantage across both datasets and all contrasts (except the narrow PD-PSNR gap on ADNI) indicates that the anatomical consistency loss and prior-informed initialization provide meaningful structural benefits over unconditional or cycle-consistent diffusion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the latent diffusion model (LDM) framework, adopting its U-Net backbone with cross-attention conditioning, and the DDPM forward/reverse diffusion process as the generative engine. Its key conceptual departure is the integration of a pretrained biomedical vision-language model (BiomedCLIP) as a frozen feature extractor, whose outputs are fused via a mutual information module and enforced through a contrastive anatomical consistency loss—neither of which appears in prior diffusion-based MR synthesis work (SynDiff, M2DN). The prior codebook with Bezier-curve-augmented similarity mappings and the two-stage inference (prior-informed initialization followed by iterative unrolling) represent a practical departure from the standard 'start from pure noise' paradigm used in most diffusion-based medical image translation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-contrast MR synthesis directly addresses a significant clinical bottleneck—long scan times, patient discomfort, and motion artifacts—so a method that reliably preserves anatomical structure across contrasts has clear translational value. The paper's ablations convincingly isolate the contributions of the prior codebook, anatomical consistency loss, and iterative unrolling, but the evaluation is limited to 2D brain slices on two datasets, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumes, other anatomical regions, or rarer contrast combinations. A notable open question is whether the BiomedCLIP-based anatomical features remain informative when the target pathology (e.g., tumor vs. neurodegeneration) shifts the appearance distribution substantially beyond the VLM's training distribution.
