## Problem

Vision-based RL agents rely on self-supervised auxiliary tasks to learn state representations, but these tasks provide no task-relevant semantic constraints and are limited by the small amount of data collected through self-exploration. Existing VLM-enhanced VRL methods either use VLM outputs directly for policy fine-tuning (producing infeasible decisions in specific contexts) or use VLM features for representation fine-tuning without reasoning-based prompts, both of which underperform on complex tasks like open-world driving. The paper addresses how to distill VLM semantic knowledge into a compact VRL visual encoder while keeping policy optimization fully delegated to VRL.

## Input / Output

The compact VRL agent takes three consecutive RGB frames (stacked as a single observation) and outputs continuous driving actions (steering, throttle, brake) for the Carla simulator. During training, the VLM pipeline additionally consumes the two most recent historical actions as textual input to the reasoning model. The visual encoder produces a 6×6×256 saliency feature map and a 512-dimensional latent vector; the reasoning VLM outputs a free-text semantic category (e.g., 'pedestrian on the right side'), and CRIS converts this into a convolutional kernel and a pixel-level saliency map used as distillation targets.

## Method

DGC distills VLM knowledge into a compact 4-layer CNN visual encoder via a two-stage prompting-reasoning pipeline: first, Qwen-VL-Chat uses chain-of-thought prompts (task definition, driving safety rules, step-by-step scene reasoning) to infer a semantic category of the key area of interest from three frames and two historical actions; second, CRIS converts that category into a convolutional kernel and applies it to the current frame to yield a pixel-level saliency feature map. Two distillation losses align the RL encoder's per-channel convolutional weights with the CRIS kernel (L_k) and its feature maps with the CRIS saliency map (L_f). Two action-conditioned SSL heads (transition prediction and reward prediction) refine the distilled latent representation by filtering task-irrelevant noise. The policy decoder is then trained with SAC's TD loss on the distilled 512-dim latent vector, and during inference the VLMs are discarded, leaving only the lightweight CNN encoder and policy network.

## Results

On the Carla driving benchmark (100K training steps, 20 episodes × 3 seeds), DGC achieves the best Episode Return (ER) and Driving Distance (D) across all three scenarios: #JW 214±77 ER / 195±31 D, #HB 189±79 ER / 141±63 D, and #HW 287±47 ER / 263±89 D. The most challenging Highway scenario shows the largest absolute gains, with DGC improving ER by 31 points over the strongest SSL baseline (MLR, 256±51) and by 145 points over the SAC baseline (142±71). DGC also demonstrates the best sample efficiency during training, with consistently rapid episode-return growth compared to VLM-based PFT and RFT methods whose performance plateaus after initial gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGC outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three Carla scenarios. Against the strongest SSL-based baseline MLR, DGC gains +31 ER and +25 D on #HW (287 vs 256, 263 vs 238), +62 ER and +54 D on #HB (189 vs 127, 141 vs 87), and +20 ER and +18 D on #JW (214 vs 194, 195 vs 177). The data-augmentation baseline DrQ and the contrastive method CURL are further behind, with DGC achieving +109 ER on #HW over DrQ (287 vs 178) and +73 ER over CURL (287 vs 214). DBC performs worst among all baselines (95±64 ER on #HW), suggesting its bisimulation-based invariance is poorly suited to the complex multi-agent driving setting. DGC also surpasses the VLM-based PFT and RFT methods (which directly fine-tune the policy or representation with VLM outputs/features), confirming that distilling VLM semantic knowledge into a compact encoder while delegating policy optimization to VRL yields superior results over end-to-end VLM-guided fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGC extends the VRL-with-SSL line of work (DeepMDP, CURL, DBC, MLR) by adding a semantic supervision channel that SSL alone cannot provide, while departing from prior VLM-enhanced VRL approaches (PFT, RFT, Executor) that either use VLM outputs as policy supervision or VLM features as frozen representation priors. The key conceptual departure is a prompting-reasoning pipeline that converts VLM chain-of-thought outputs into pixel-level distillation targets (convolutional kernels and saliency feature maps) for a compact CNN encoder, rather than feeding VLM features directly into the agent. Policy learning is then fully delegated to SAC's TD loss on the distilled latent, cleanly decoupling representation learning from decision-making.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of VLM-guided representation learning from VRL policy optimization offers a practical recipe for improving sample efficiency in visually complex RL without requiring VLMs at inference time. However, the approach is validated only on the Carla driving benchmark (three scenarios), and the prompting-reasoning pipeline is hand-crafted for driving, raising open questions about generalizability to other domains and the trade-off between discriminative and generalizable representations that the authors flag for future work. The reliance on a specific VLM (Qwen-VL-Chat) and referring model (CRIS) during training also limits reproducibility and raises cost considerations for broader adoption.
