## Problem

Scaling laws have been well-established in NLP and vision but remain unexplored in text-driven motion generation, largely due to three barriers: limited motion data scale and quality, codebook collapse when scaling vector-quantization tokenizers, and the inability of prior architectures (LLM-based or CLIP-embedding-based) to independently scale model size and vocabulary. This paper asks what specifically hinders the verification of scaling laws in motion generation and provides a scalable system to answer it.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text description as input, which is encoded at the word level by a frozen T5-XL encoder into a sequence of text embeddings. These text tokens are prefixed to the autoregressive motion generation stream, where the model outputs a sequence of discrete motion tokens (from an FSQ codebook of size up to 2^16) that are decoded into a 3D SMPL skeleton motion sequence. The text tokens attend bidirectionally among themselves, while motion tokens attend causally to all preceding motion tokens and all text tokens.

## Method

ScaMo comprises two components: a Motion FSQ-VAE tokenizer and a text-prefix autoregressive transformer. The FSQ-VAE replaces the argmin-based vector quantization with a rounding-based finite scalar quantizer (each channel rounded to one of L integers), which eliminates codebook collapse and enables stable scaling of vocabulary size from 2^8 to 2^16 using only a reconstruction loss. The autoregressive transformer uses a frozen T5-XL encoder to produce word-level text embeddings that are prefixed to the motion token sequence, with bidirectional attention over text tokens and causal attention over motion tokens, trained solely with cross-entropy on motion tokens. The paper then conducts a comprehensive scaling study across model sizes (44M–3B) and vocabulary sizes (2^8–2^16) on the newly constructed MotionUnion dataset (~150k sequences, 30M frames), discovering a logarithmic law between FLOPs and normalized test loss, and power-law relationships governing optimal vocabulary size, non-vocabulary parameters, and data tokens as functions of compute budget.

## Results

The paper establishes the first scaling laws for autoregressive motion generation. The normalized test loss follows a logarithmic law with FLOPs: L_u = -1.062 × log₁₀(C) + 13.839, while vocabulary parameters, non-vocabulary parameters, and data tokens follow power laws with exponents 0.75, 0.57, and 0.43 respectively. The scaling law is validated by training a 3B-parameter model with a 2^16 codebook at ~1×10^18 FLOPs, whose test loss precisely matches the predicted value. In the architecture ablation on HumanML3D, the T5-XL text-prefix design achieves FID 0.104, Matching Score 3.021, and Top-1 R-Precision 0.510, substantially outperforming the CLIP-based variant (FID 0.226, Matching Score 3.422, Top-1 R-P 0.402).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the proposed FSQ tokenizer and the vanilla VQ-VAE tokenizer (from T2M-GPT [68]) across codebook sizes from 2^8 to 2^16. FSQ consistently outperforms VQ on reconstruction loss, MPJPE, codebook utilization, and entropy, with the gap widening at larger codebook sizes where VQ suffers from codebook collapse. In the architecture comparison, the T5-XL text-prefix autoregressive model (343M params) reduces FID from 0.226 to 0.104 and improves Top-1 R-Precision from 0.402 to 0.510 over the CLIP-based variant without prefix, demonstrating that word-level text embeddings with bidirectional attention are critical for cross-modality alignment. The scaling law prediction is validated against the actual 3B model trained at 1×10^18 FLOPs, where the predicted normalized loss aligns precisely with the observed value, confirming the logarithmic scaling relationship. The paper does not directly compare against other end-to-end motion generation methods (e.g., diffusion-based approaches), focusing instead on internal ablations and scaling behavior within the autoregressive framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive motion generation paradigm introduced by T2M-GPT [68] by replacing its vanilla VQ-VAE tokenizer with a Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) approach that avoids codebook collapse at large codebook sizes, and by substituting sentence-level CLIP embeddings with word-level T5-XL prefixes under a bidirectional-causal attention scheme. The scaling law methodology directly follows Chinchilla [23] and Tao et al. [50], who established power-law relationships for LLMs and vocabulary-aware scaling respectively, but this is the first application of that framework to the motion generation domain. The MotionUnion dataset (~150k sequences) addresses the data-scale limitation that prior works like Motion-X [31] and the concurrent 1M-sequence dataset [58] could not overcome due to static-motion contamination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By confirming that scaling laws hold in motion generation, this work provides the field with a principled framework for allocating compute, data, and model capacity—something previously unavailable and forcing practitioners to rely on ad-hoc choices. The practical finding that vocabulary parameters should scale faster than non-vocabulary parameters (γ≈1.46) and that a logarithmic (rather than pure power-law) relationship governs test loss vs. FLOPs offers concrete guidance for future large-scale motion model training. Open questions remain: whether the logarithmic law persists beyond 1e18 FLOPs, whether these findings transfer to diffusion-based motion generators, and whether the MotionUnion dataset (still ~150k sequences) is sufficient to fully characterize the scaling regime, given that NLP scaling laws were validated over orders of magnitude larger data ranges.
