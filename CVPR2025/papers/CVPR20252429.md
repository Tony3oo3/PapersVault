## Problem

Contrastive learning for LiDAR point clouds enforces feature invariance to rotation, translation, and scaling, causing learned representations to lose object pose and size information that is critical for geometry-sensitive downstream tasks like 3D semantic segmentation and object detection. Additionally, existing SSL methods rely on random point dropping and cuboid cropping as augmentations, which do not represent real LiDAR beam pattern variations, limiting cross-sensor generalizability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (3D coordinates plus intensity) that are preprocessed via Patchwork++ ground-plane fitting and HDBSCAN clustering to identify object clusters, with upright bounding boxes fitted around each cluster as regression targets. During pretraining, two augmented views of each scan are generated (one optionally transformed to a different LiDAR beam pattern via spherical projection), and the model outputs per-point features from a MinkUNet backbone. At fine-tuning, a segmentation head produces per-point class labels or a PartA2 detection head produces 3D bounding boxes with class scores.

## Method

PSA-SSL extends contrastive-based SSL (SegContrast or DepthContrast) with two lightweight additions: (1) a self-supervised bounding box regression pretext task, where a two-layer FC head predicts per-cluster bounding box offsets from a fixed-size anchor using smooth L1 loss on the concatenated query and momentum encoder outputs, and (2) LiDAR beam pattern augmentation (LPA) that randomly re-renders one view through a different cylindrical LiDAR configuration (Velodyne-32/64 or Ouster-64) via spherical projection and inverse mapping. The total pretraining loss is a weighted sum of the contrastive (InfoNCE) and regression losses (β₁=1, β₂=0.5), trained with the MoCo momentum-encoder framework. The regression head counteracts the geometric invariance imposed by contrastive loss by forcing features to retain pose and size, while LPA makes the learned representations invariant to LiDAR sparsity patterns, enabling a single pretrained model to transfer across sensors. Ablations show that Single Pattern LPA outperforms PolarMix mixing, and combining both components yields the best results across Waymo, nuScenes, and SemanticKITTI.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI (pretrained on SemanticKITTI), PSA-SC achieves 65.1 mIoU at 100% labels, outperforming BEVContrast (64.1), MAELi (64.2), and TARL (63.7). At the more challenging 1% label regime, PSA-SC reaches 55.3 mIoU vs. BEVContrast's 53.8 and TARL's 52.5. For 3D object detection on full KITTI, PSA-SC achieves 74.08 mAP, surpassing TARL (73.86) and SegContrast (73.52). Cross-LiDAR transfer from SemanticKITTI to nuScenes yields 71.82 mIoU, a +3.56 improvement over TARL (68.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSA-SC outperforms all seven SOTA SSL baselines on SemanticKITTI at every label percentage from 1% to 100%, with the largest margin over BEVContrast (+1.5 mIoU at 1%, +2.8 at 10%) and TARL (+2.8 at 1%, +1.4 at 10%). The only exception is at 0.1% labels, where BEVContrast (39.7) edges out PSA-SC (37.7). Against the direct baselines it extends, PSA-SC improves over SegContrast by +2.39 mIoU on 1% SemanticKITTI and +0.86 on 1% Waymo, while PSA-DC improves over DepthContrast by +2.66 mIoU on 1% Waymo. The two related geometric-pretext baselines (ProposalContrast's attention encoder and Shi & Rajkumar's scale/rotation regression) show only marginal or inconsistent gains over plain SegContrast, whereas PSA-SC consistently outperforms both. On 3D object detection (full KITTI), PSA-SC (74.08 mAP) beats TARL (73.86) and SegContrast (73.52), and on cross-LiDAR transfer (SemanticKITTI→nuScenes) it surpasses TARL by +3.56 mIoU, confirming superior sensor generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends contrastive-based SSL for LiDAR point clouds (SegContrast, DepthContrast, BEVContrast) by identifying that InfoNCE loss's enforcement of invariance to rotation, translation, and scaling actively discards object pose and size information critical for downstream geometry-sensitive tasks. It introduces bounding box regression as a self-supervised pretext task—a repurposing of a standard supervised detection component into the SSL pretraining loop—and incorporates LiDAR beam pattern augmentation (LiDomAug) from the domain adaptation literature to learn sensor-agnostic features. The approach is model-agnostic and composable with any existing contrastive SSL baseline, positioning it as a lightweight add-on rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single pretrained model can transfer across Waymo, nuScenes, and SemanticKITTI with competitive or superior performance, reducing the need for per-sensor pretraining in autonomous driving pipelines. However, the method underperforms on small object classes (pedestrians, cyclists) in detection, attributed to LPA sparsifying small clusters and imperfect self-supervised box targets; the authors propose multi-frame aggregation and small-cluster augmentation as future fixes. The 33% pretraining speedup from LPA-induced sparsification is a practical bonus but the overall performance gains (+1–3 mIoU) are incremental rather than transformative, and the approach remains specific to LiDAR point clouds with upright box fitting.
