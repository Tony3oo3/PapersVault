## Problem

In Cross-Domain Few-Shot Segmentation, segmentation performance on distant target domains peaks at very early training epochs and then declines sharply as source-domain training proceeds, yet this phenomenon has been under-explored. The paper identifies the root cause: low-level features are vulnerable to domain shifts, causing the loss landscape to become progressively sharper during training, which makes the model increasingly sensitive to domain gaps and produces fundamentally wrong predictions on target domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252222/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in an episodic meta-learning paradigm: given K support images with pixel masks and one query image (all RGB, resized to 480×480 for the ViT variant), it produces a pixel-level binary segmentation mask for the query. Both CNN (ResNet-50) and ViT (ViT-B/16) backbones are supported, with the last backbone stage and final ReLU discarded following SSP. The two proposed modules are plug-and-play and require no additional training parameters beyond the random convolution filter (3×3, σ=0.1) and a few LCM hyperparameters (K=3, w=0.6, β=0.7).

## Method

The method introduces two plug-and-play modules targeting the vulnerability of low-level features to domain shifts. During source-domain training, the Low-level Enhancing Module (LEM) acts as a novel sharpness-aware minimization: it applies a random convolution to early-layer support features to perturb their domain, then uses FFT to recombine the phase spectrum of the original feature with the amplitude spectrum of the perturbed feature, preserving shape/boundary information while transforming texture/domain characteristics. During target-domain testing, the Low-level Calibration Module (LCM) directly supplements collapsed low-level target-domain information by computing a confidence map from the coarse score map, selecting Top-K high-confidence patches, and updating foreground similarity via cosine similarity with the low-level query feature map. Together, LEM flattens the loss landscape to prevent overfitting to source-domain specifics, while LCM corrects fundamental errors caused by already-collapsed low-level features at test time.

## Results

On the four CDFSS benchmarks (FSS-1000, Deepglobe, ISIC, Chest X-ray), the proposed method with ViT-B/16 backbone achieves 65.01% average MIoU in the 1-shot setting and 70.43% in the 5-shot setting, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art APSeg (61.30% / 65.09%) by 3.71% and 5.34% respectively. With the ResNet-50 backbone, the method reaches 60.46% (1-shot) and 65.01% (5-shot) average MIoU. The ablation confirms that the Low-level Enhancing Module (LEM) contributes +1.14% (CNN) / +0.89% (ViT) and the Low-level Calibration Module (LCM) adds a further +2.11% (CNN) / +1.95% (ViT) in the 1-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (ViT-base) achieves the highest average MIoU across all four target domains in both 1-shot (65.01%) and 5-shot (70.43%) settings, outperforming the previous SOTA APSeg [16] by 3.71% and 5.34% respectively. Against the strongest CNN-based CDFSS baselines, the ViT variant leads DRA [33] by 4.15% (1-shot) and 5.01% (5-shot), ABCDFSS [17] by 4.34% and 5.46%, and APM [35] by 4.98% and 5.25%. Compared to the FSS-only baselines, the method surpasses FPTrans [41] (62.17%/69.06%) by 2.84% and 1.37%, and SSP [12] (57.21%/62.35%) by 7.80% and 8.08%. The ResNet-50 variant (60.46%/65.01%) is competitive with APSeg's ViT-base (61.30%/65.09) in 5-shot but trails by 0.84% in 1-shot, indicating the ViT backbone benefits more from the proposed modules. Notably, the method achieves the best per-domain scores on Deepglobe (42.12%/51.48%) and ISIC (52.91%/62.43%), the two most domain-distant targets, confirming the low-level feature interpretation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) framework [13, 46], repurposing its perturbation-based loss-landscape flattening idea to target specifically the low-level features in a cross-domain few-shot segmentation setting. It also draws on random convolution as a shape-preserving perturbation [39] and FFT amplitude/phase decomposition [28] to construct domain-oriented perturbations, and on the self-support principle from SSP [12] for the test-time calibration module. Conceptually, it departs from prior CDFSS methods (PATNet, ABCDFSS, DR-Adapter, APSeg) that address domain shift through feature transformation, adapters, or prompt tuning, by instead identifying and mitigating the root cause—shallow-layer overfitting to source-domain statistics—through a targeted SAM variant and a direct low-level feature calibration at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that low-level features—long assumed to be the most domain-invariant and transferable—are actually the primary bottleneck for cross-domain generalization challenges a working assumption in the feature-transfer literature and redirects attention to shallow-layer training dynamics. The plug-and-play design (no architectural changes, no target-domain training) makes the approach broadly applicable to existing CDFSS pipelines on both CNN and ViT backbones. Open questions include whether the insight generalizes to other few-shot tasks (classification, detection) and whether the specific random-convolution-plus-FFT perturbation is optimal compared to other domain-shift simulation strategies. The LCM's reliance on target-domain query features at test time, while natural in the few-shot setting, limits applicability to fully zero-shot scenarios.
