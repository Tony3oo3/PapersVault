## Problem

Existing Grouped Kernel Pruning (GKP) methods introduce dynamic operations—such as per-layer clustering scheme selection (TMI-GKP) or variable group sizes and pruning ratios (DSP)—in ways that create expensive computational overhead, require custom architecture support, or depend on training snapshots of the original model. The paper identifies that the optimal locus for such dynamic operations in GKP is the group count parameter of grouped convolutions, which is natively supported by standard ML infrastructure and avoids these complications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252155/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the weights of a pre-trained CNN (no pruning data required, making it data-agnostic) and produces a pruned model expressed as standard grouped convolutions (Conv2d with a per-layer groups parameter), compatible with existing PyTorch/TensorFlow operators without custom kernels. The user specifies a target pruning rate, and the resulting model's MACs and parameter count are predictable within ~1% of the assigned rate, unlike many competing methods where hyperparameter search is needed to hit a target size. Fine-tuning after pruning uses standard SGD on any available dataset.

## Method

LeanFlex-GKP is a one-shot, post-train, data-agnostic four-stage procedure whose central mechanism is flexible per-layer group counts selected via integral optimization. Stage 1 (filter grouping) uses k-Means++ to determine cluster centers, then greedily assigns the m nearest filters to each center in a cycle, with multiple restarts to select the grouping minimizing intra-group distance. Stage 2 (grouped kernel pruning) retains kernels with large L2 norms and small distances to the geometric median of their group, replacing TMI-GKP's expensive multi-restart graph search. Stage 3 (post-prune group count evaluation) scores each candidate group count by the normalized difference between inter-group and intra-group geometric-median distances on the already-pruned layer, coupling the grouping and pruning stages into a single integral optimization. Stage 4 reconstructs the pruned layer as a standard grouped convolution. The entire pipeline requires no training snapshots, no iterative train-prune cycles, and minimal hyperparameters.

## Results

Across 21 model-dataset-pruning-rate settings, LeanFlex-GKP achieves positive accuracy gain (ΔAcc > 0) in 18 cases, with the largest improvements on ResNet56/CIFAR-100 (+0.58%) and ResNet110/CIFAR-100 (+0.43%). On ResNet50/ImageNet-1K at ~33% MACs reduction, it reaches 75.62% top-1 accuracy (ΔAcc = −0.51%), outperforming TMI-GKP (75.53%), KPGP (75.50%), and OTOv2 (75.38%). The method is data-agnostic and non-iterative (one-shot post-training), and its actual MACs/Params reduction stays within 1% of the target pruning rate across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LeanFlex-GKP outperforms TMI-GKP [60] on all five shared settings, with the largest margin on ResNet56/CIFAR-100 (72.11% vs 71.11%, a 1.0-point gap) and ResNet110/CIFAR-100 (73.63% vs 72.79%). Against CC [30], it wins on ResNet32/CIFAR-10 (92.40 vs 92.39), ResNet110/CIFAR-10 (94.35 vs 94.29), ResNet56/CIFAR-100 (72.11 vs 71.43), and ResNet110/CIFAR-100 (73.63 vs 73.21). On ResNet50/ImageNet-1K, it beats OTOv2 [3] (75.62 vs 75.38), FPGM [20] (75.62 vs 75.04), KPGP [57] (75.62 vs 75.50), and ThiNet [37] (75.62 vs 72.04) at comparable ~33% MACs reduction. The only notable loss is to LRF [23] on ResNet56/Tiny-ImageNet (55.67 vs 55.95, a 0.28-point gap) and to KPGP [57] on VGG16/CIFAR-10 (94.15 vs 94.17, a 0.02-point gap). Critically, LeanFlex-GKP is the only method that is simultaneously data-agnostic, non-iterative, and achieves positive ΔAcc on the harder CIFAR-100 and ImageNet settings, whereas most competitors require training data or iterative retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Grouped Kernel Pruning (GKP) framework introduced by Zhong et al. (TMI-GKP, ICLR 2022), which first demonstrated that grouped convolutions enable finer-than-filter pruning while preserving dense structure. It departs from TMI-GKP's dynamic clustering-scheme selection (which requires expensive eigen-decompositions and training snapshots) and from DSP's variable group sizes and per-group pruning ratios (which necessitate custom architecture support), arguing instead that the optimal locus for dynamic operations is the Conv2d(groups) parameter itself. By anchoring flexibility there, the authors can replace TMI-GKP's multi-scheme clustering with a single k-Means++ pass and swap the graph-search kernel selection for a lightweight L2-norm-plus-geometric-median heuristic, yielding a one-shot, data-agnostic, post-training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the most comprehensive empirical evaluation of GKP to date (21 model-dataset-pruning-rate settings across ResNet, VGG, DenseNet, MobileNetV2, WideResNet, and SDXL-UNet), offering practitioners a clear performance map for this underdeveloped pruning granularity. Its practical value lies in the 'hassle-free' property: predictable model-size reduction, no hyperparameter search, and no dependency on training checkpoints—traits that many competing methods lack. Limitations include restriction to CNNs, the need for fine-tuning data (so not truly zero-data), and the fact that accuracy gains over strong baselines like TMI-GKP are modest (often <0.5%). Open questions include whether the flexible-group-count principle transfers to non-convolutional architectures (transformers, Mamba) and whether the geometric-median evaluation criterion generalizes beyond the tested pruning ratios.
