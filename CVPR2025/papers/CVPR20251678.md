## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for urban scenes are trained on either aerial or street views exclusively, producing disjointed experiences that cannot support seamless free-view exploration across large vertical and horizontal view changes. Jointly training both view types introduces two fundamental conflicts: opposing gradient signals during Gaussian densification (street views prune occluded Gaussians while aerial views promote their regrowth) and an imbalanced camera distribution that biases reconstruction toward street-level detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251678/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated aerial and street view RGB images (1920×1080 synthetic; 1600×1066 aerial and 1600×900 street for real data), SfM camera poses, pseudo-ground-truth depth maps (from rendering engines or Depth-Anything-V2 with scale alignment), and an initial point cloud. It outputs a unified LOD-structured 3D Gaussian scene that renders novel views at ~51 FPS, and can additionally produce depth maps, surface normal maps, and a TSDF-fused 3D mesh by switching between 3D and 2D Gaussian primitives decoded from the same anchor structure.

## Method

Horizon-GS builds on Scaffold-GS anchors and introduces a two-stage coarse-to-fine training strategy: stage 1 trains primarily on aerial views (R=2 sampling ratio) to establish coarse geometry across K_aerial LOD layers, while stage 2 freezes the anchor MLP weights, activates all K LOD layers, and refines with street views using a modified densification policy that selects significant Gaussians by max-gradient × projected-radius × opacity^τ rather than averaging screen-space gradients. A balanced camera distribution mechanism (inspired by NeRF Director) randomly samples aerial vs. street images at a controlled ratio to prevent overfitting to the more numerous street views. For large-scale scenes, the scene is partitioned into m×n overlapping chunks based on ground-projected camera positions, each trained independently and merged by discarding out-of-boundary Gaussians and concatenating attributes, with MLPs replaced by explicit SH coefficients for real-time rendering.

## Results

On small-scale scenes (Table 1), Horizon-GS achieves 30.59 PSNR / 0.913 SSIM / 0.094 LPIPS on Block_Small aerial views and 31.60 PSNR / 0.938 SSIM / 0.101 LPIPS on the Synthetic aerial scene, outperforming the strongest baseline (Scaffold-GS) by over 2 dB in aerial PSNR. On real-world aerial views, Horizon-GS reaches 23.23 PSNR, surpassing the next best (Hier-GS at 21.43) by 1.8 dB. On large-scale scenes (Table 2), the chunked version (Ours*) achieves 28.89 PSNR on Block_A aerial and 31.09 PSNR on Hillside_Morning aerial, while maintaining real-time rendering at 51.5 FPS. On the UC-GS benchmark (Table 3), Horizon-GS (100k iterations) slightly trails UC-GS (900k iterations) on held-out PSNR (25.35 vs 25.95) but achieves better LPIPS (0.280 vs 0.291) and clearly wins on shifted/rotated views (25.46 vs 23.52 PSNR at +1m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Horizon-GS outperforms 3D-GS [8] and 2D-GS [7] by a wide margin across all settings (e.g., +5.15 dB aerial PSNR over 3D-GS on Block_Small). Against Scaffold-GS [17], Horizon-GS gains ~2.1 dB on aerial views (30.59 vs 28.44 on Block_Small) while being competitive on street views (23.80 vs 23.84). Hierarchical-3DGS [9] is the closest competitor on street views, actually edging out Horizon-GS on the Real Street benchmark (22.53 vs 22.04 PSNR), but Horizon-GS dominates on all aerial views by 1.8–3.4 dB. On large-scale scenes, VastGaussian [12] and CityGaussian [16] trail Horizon-GS by 2–3 dB in aerial PSNR (e.g., 28.89 vs 25.85/26.86 on Block_A). On the UC-GS benchmark [43], Horizon-GS trained for only 100k iterations slightly underperforms UC-GS (900k iterations) on held-out PSNR (25.35 vs 25.95) but achieves better perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.280 vs 0.291) and clearly surpasses UC-GS on extrapolated views with camera shifts and rotations (25.46 vs 23.52 PSNR at +1m offset).

## Relation to Prior Work

Horizon-GS builds directly on Scaffold-GS's anchor-based neural Gaussian representation and 2D-GS's surface-aligned primitives, extending both to handle the joint aerial-street setting. It draws on BungeeNeRF's progressive multi-scale training philosophy and Octree-GS's LOD hierarchy, but departs from prior large-scale GS methods (VastGaussian, CityGaussian, Hierarchical-3DGS) that each target only one view domain. The closest prior work, UC-GS, attempts cross-view reconstruction via uncertainty weighting but does not address the fundamental gradient-conflict and camera-imbalance issues that Horizon-GS identifies and resolves through its two-stage densification and balanced sampling strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to deliver a single unified Gaussian model that simultaneously supports high-fidelity aerial and street-view rendering with real-time speeds, which is critical for digital twins, autonomous driving simulation, and immersive VR/AR where users need seamless transitions between altitude levels. The curated dataset (5 synthetic + 2 real calibrated scenes) addresses a genuine gap in the field, as no prior dataset provided both view types with full calibration. Limitations include the Manhattan-world assumption, dependence on calibrated SfM poses, and noticeable quality degradation on real data due to pose estimation and sensor noise; the authors note future directions toward pretrained 3D scene foundation models and distributional training to reduce input dependency.
