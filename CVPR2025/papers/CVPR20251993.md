## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models effectively remove target concepts but suffer from 'adjacency' — they inadvertently degrade the model's ability to generate semantically related classes (e.g., erasing one dog breed impairs generation of other breeds). No prior method provides fine-grained control to precisely excise a target concept while preserving the fidelity of its semantic neighborhood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251993/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 model and takes a target concept (e.g., 'Golden Retriever') as input. It constructs an adjacency set by generating images for all candidate classes, encoding them with a CLIP image encoder, and selecting the top-K classes by cosine similarity of mean feature vectors. The output is a LoRA-adapted diffusion model that no longer generates the target concept while maintaining generation quality for adjacent and unrelated classes, evaluated via a pre-trained ResNet-50 classifier and NudeNet for nudity detection.

## Method

FADE comprises two components: (1) Concept Neighborhood, which identifies the adjacency set by computing CLIP embedding cosine similarities between the target concept and all other classes, selecting the top-K most similar; and (2) Mesh Modules, a LoRA-based parameter-efficient adapter trained with a composite loss of three terms — an Erasing Loss that enforces a margin separating the target's noise prediction from adjacent concepts' predictions, a Guidance Loss that steers the target's noise toward a null/averaged concept embedding, and an Adjacency Loss that penalizes deviations in noise predictions for adjacent classes. The total loss is a weighted sum (λ_er·L_er + λ_adj·L_adj + λ_guid·L_guid), and the theoretical justification links the k-NN-based adjacency identification to Naive Bayes optimality in latent feature space. This structured loss design creates a targeted 'hole' in the semantic manifold for the erased concept while preserving the local geometry of neighboring classes.

## Results

On fine-grained unlearning (FG-Un) across Stanford Dogs, Oxford Flowers, and CUB, FADE achieves ERB scores of 94.60–98.62 with near-perfect erasure accuracy (A_er = 99.60–100.00%) and high adjacency retention (A_adj = 89.76–97.28%), outperforming all baselines by at least 15% in ERB. On ImageNet-1k (Table 3), FADE achieves ERB scores of 87.08–97.93, exceeding the next-best method SPM (63.64–86.02) by 12–34 points. On coarse-grained unlearning (Imagenette, Table 2), FADE attains the best balance of target erasure (A_tar ≈ 0.00–0.72%) and retention on other classes (A_others = 83.75–89.30%), and on I2P nudity erasure it achieves an 87.88% erasure ratio change, the highest among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FADE outperforms all six baselines across every target class and dataset. The closest competitor is SPM, which reaches ERB up to 82.73 (Blue Jay) but drops to 49.66 (Pomeranian); FADE exceeds SPM by 12–48 ERB points depending on the class. CA and UCE achieve moderate ERB (40–75) but suffer from incomplete erasure (A_er as low as 27–68%) or poor adjacency retention. ESD achieves perfect erasure (A_er = 100%) but destroys adjacency knowledge (A_adj as low as 0.52–48%), yielding ERB scores of only 7.69–64.86. FMN and Receler are the weakest: FMN's ERB rarely exceeds 13.84, and Receler's ERB is below 12 in all settings, indicating catastrophic collateral forgetting of adjacent concepts. On the ImageNet-1k adjacency inflection analysis (Figure 3), FADE maintains high adjacency accuracy even at >90% CLIP structural similarity, whereas SPM degrades beyond 90% and ESD/FMN/Receler fail at 78–80% similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-erasure line of work for text-to-image diffusion models (ESD, Concept Ablation, FMN, SPM, Receler, UCE), which has focused on preserving generation quality for *unrelated* classes (locality). FADE departs by introducing an explicit adjacency constraint: preserving semantically *related* classes (e.g., other retriever breeds when erasing 'Golden Retriever') that prior methods inadvertently destroy. Methodologically, it combines a CLIP-embedding-based Concept Neighborhood for identifying adjacent classes with a LoRA-based Mesh Module that jointly optimizes three loss terms (Erasing, Adjacency, Guidance), building on the PEFT paradigm established by SPM and Receler but adding the adjacency regularization that those methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adjacency problem is practically critical in fine-grained domains (breed-level dog/bird/flower classification, medical imaging subtypes) where erasing one concept without collateral damage to its neighbors is a hard requirement. The paper's finding that state-of-the-art erasure methods (Receler, FMN) reduce adjacent-class accuracy to near-random levels (0.1–7%) while reporting strong unrelated-class retention reveals a significant blind spot in current evaluation protocols. Open questions include whether the CLIP-embedding-based Concept Neighborhood generalizes to domains without good visual proxies, how the approach scales to multi-concept or sequential erasure, and whether the three-loss formulation is optimal or whether alternative manifold-preserving regularizers could further improve retention.
