## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations in zero-shot classification, yet existing defenses (e.g., PMG-AFT, TeCoA) require adversarial fine-tuning on additional datasets such as TinyImageNet, which contradicts the zero-shot premise and risks overfitting. This paper asks whether zero-shot adversarial robustness can be improved without any training data or model modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250798/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 224×224×3) and produces a class label via cosine similarity between the manipulated image feature and hand-crafted text prompt features (e.g., 'a photo of [CLASS]'). Crucially, the manipulation occurs in CLIP's image-encoder feature space rather than in pixel space: Gaussian noise is added to the input image only to construct the anchor, while the final 'movement' is a linear interpolation between two encoded feature vectors. The method is applied identically to both adversarial and clean inputs since no distinction is made at inference time.

## Method

AOM (Anchor-guided One-step linear Movement) consists of three training-free steps performed entirely in CLIP's embedding space. First, the input image is encoded to obtain the source feature. Second, an anchor is constructed by adding Gaussian noise (σ=0.18) to the input n=10 times, encoding each noisy variant, and averaging the resulting features—this leverages the random-smoothing insight that Gaussian noise weakens adversarial perturbations. Third, a one-step linear interpolation f_moved = (1−α)·f_source + α·f_anchor with α=1.2 moves the source feature along the line toward and slightly beyond the anchor, effectively performing a single gradient-descent step toward the clean feature distribution. The approach requires no additional datasets, no model fine-tuning, and no generative model, distinguishing it from both adversarial training methods and diffusion-based purification.

## Results

Under PGD-10 (ε=1/255) zero-shot adversarial evaluation across 16 datasets, AOM achieves 41.72% average robust accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA PMG-AFT (31.95%) by 9.77% and the unmodified CLIP baseline (17.38%) by 24.34%. On clean samples, AOM attains 62.24% average accuracy, only 2.31% below the original CLIP (64.55%) while outperforming PMG-AFT (55.71%) by 6.53%. Under the stronger AutoAttack (ε=1/255), AOM reaches 44.79% average robust accuracy versus 17.91% for PMG-AFT (+26.88%). Against non-zero-shot methods under PGD-100 (ε=4/255), AOM achieves 38.1% robust accuracy, exceeding APT (27.0%) by 11.1% without any training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AOM outperforms all zero-shot adversarial robustness baselines: it beats PMG-AFT [10] by 9.77% (41.72% vs. 31.95%) and TeCoA [9] by 14.76% (41.72% vs. 26.96%) on average PGD-10 robust accuracy, and surpasses PMG-AFT by 26.88% under AutoAttack (44.79% vs. 17.91%). Against non-zero-shot adversarial fine-tuning methods under PGD-100 (ε=4/255), AOM exceeds APT [11] by 11.1% (38.1% vs. 27.0%), PAFT [48] by 10.6% (38.1% vs. 27.5%), AVP [47] by 25.0% (38.1% vs. 13.1%), and HEP [46] by 27.8% (38.1% vs. 10.3%). On clean-sample accuracy, AOM (62.24%) slightly trails the unmodified CLIP (64.55%) by 2.31% but substantially outperforms PMG-AFT (55.71%) and TeCoA (46.99%), confirming that the training-free approach preserves generalization. The only setting where AOM does not lead is clean accuracy under PGD-100 (ε=4/255), where APT achieves 59.4% versus AOM's 57.4%, which is expected since APT leverages full training-set adversarial fine-tuning. Notably, AOM wins on the majority of individual datasets (e.g., Caltech-101: 74.65% vs. 24.02% for PMG-AFT; ImageNet: 33.71% vs. 17.26%) while losing on a few (e.g., CIFAR-10: 63.72% vs. 66.99% for PMG-AFT; DTD: 23.89% vs. 34.38%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of improving VLM adversarial robustness via adversarial fine-tuning (TeCoA, PMG-AFT) or generative-model-based purification (DiffPure, MimicDiffusion), arguing that both violate the zero-shot setting or introduce overfitting risk. Instead, it repurposes the random smoothing observation—that Gaussian noise diminishes adversarial perturbations—into a feature-space manipulation: the noise-perturbed sample serves as an anchor, and one-step linear interpolation in the embedding space moves adversarial features toward the clean domain. The method thus sits at the intersection of certified-robustness theory (random smoothing) and feature-space editing, without requiring any training data or auxiliary generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AOM provides a genuinely training-free, zero-shot defense that outperforms methods requiring adversarial fine-tuning on TinyImageNet, making it attractive for practitioners who cannot or should not retrain VLMs. The modest clean-accuracy drop (2.31% vs. original CLIP) suggests the approach is deployable without sacrificing generalization. However, the method is validated only on CLIP ViT-B/32, and its reliance on a fixed σ and α (tuned on TinyImageNet/ImageNet) raises open questions about transferability to other VLM backbones, larger perturbation budgets, or adaptive per-sample noise levels. Whether the linear-interpolation step is truly necessary beyond the Gaussian-noise anchor alone remains an interesting ablation question the paper only partially addresses.
