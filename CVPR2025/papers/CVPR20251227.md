## Problem

Existing methods that incorporate monocular depth priors into relative pose estimation assume depth maps from different views are related by a single scale factor, ignoring the shift component of the affine transformation. This assumption is fundamentally misaligned with how state-of-the-art MDE models are trained (affine-invariant loss), and the paper shows that even metric depth models exhibit non-negligible shifts in practice, leading to degraded pose accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251227/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images, their feature correspondences (sparse via SuperPoint+LightGlue or dense via RoMa/MASt3R), and monocular depth maps from any off-the-shelf MDE model as input. It outputs the relative rotation R and translation t, the depth affine corrections (relative scale α and two shifts β₁, β₂), and optionally the focal lengths for uncalibrated settings. The solvers are minimal (3 points calibrated, 4 points uncalibrated), and the pipeline is agnostic to the specific matcher or depth model used, operating on images resized to 640×480 (ScanNet) or 720×480 (ETH3D).

## Method

The core contribution is three minimal solvers (calibrated 3-point, shared-focal 4-point, two-focal 4-point) that explicitly estimate the affine corrections (scale α, shifts β₁, β₂) alongside the relative pose by exploiting the length-preserving property of rigid transforms: pairwise distances among back-projected 3D points must be equal across both views, yielding polynomial equations in the affine parameters after eliminating R and t. These polynomial systems are solved via Gröbner basis solvers (4 solutions for calibrated, 8 for shared-focal, 4 for two-focal). The solvers are integrated into a hybrid LO-MSAC pipeline that randomly alternates between depth-aware and classic point-based solvers, scores candidates using a combined depth-induced reprojection error and Sampson error, and performs local optimization jointly over all parameters (pose, affine corrections, focal lengths) with a weighted cost function balancing the two error types.

## Results

On ScanNet-1500 (calibrated, indoor), the proposed method with SP+LG matches and MoGe depth priors achieves a median rotation error of 1.57° and translation error of 4.77°, with pose error AUC of 23.36% @5°, 43.39% @10°, and 61.08% @20°, outperforming the PoseLib-5pt baseline (55.60% @20°) by 5.5 points. On the most challenging two-focal uncalibrated setting (Stanford 2D-3D-S), the method with MASt3R matches and depth achieves 1.33° median rotation error and 76.55% AUC @20°, more than doubling the AUC of the 7-point fundamental matrix baseline (59.85%). The method also improves over DUSt3R and MASt3R's direct two-view outputs when using their matches and depth as priors, demonstrating orthogonal gains from the affine correction framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all six baselines across every tested setting. Against PoseLib point-based solvers (5pt/6pt/7pt), the method improves AUC@20° by 5.5 points on calibrated ScanNet-1500 (61.08% vs 55.60%) and by 16.7 points on the two-focal Stanford 2D-3D-S setting (76.55% vs 59.85%). The PnP baseline [24,41], which directly applies PnP-RANSAC on monocular depths, is outperformed even when using the same metric depth model (e.g., 61.08% vs 58.73% AUC@20° with MoGe on ScanNet-1500), confirming that affine shift modeling is critical. Barath et al.'s 2PT+D solver [4] is also surpassed (61.08% vs 54.98% AUC@20°), partly because their 2-point solver is degenerate for rigid alignment. Ding et al.'s 3p3d and 4p4d solvers [17], which model only scale invariance without shift, fail to improve over the point-based baselines in most settings (e.g., 4p4d achieves 30.22% vs 30.71% AUC@20° on two-focal Stanford 2D-3D-S), whereas the proposed method reaches 43.26% with the same matcher. Finally, the method outperforms both DUSt3R (58.36%) and MASt3R (71.91%) direct two-view outputs on the two-focal setting when using MASt3R's matches and depth as priors (76.55%), showing that the affine correction framework provides orthogonal gains over these 3D foundation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimal-solver framework for relative pose estimation by incorporating explicit affine (scale and shift) corrections on monocular depth priors, building on the Gröbner-basis solver generation methodology of Larsson et al. [34] and the hybrid LO-MSAC robust estimation pipeline of Camposeco et al. [12]. It departs from prior depth-based pose solvers (Barath et al. [4], Ding et al. [17]) that model only scale invariance, arguing that the shift component is critical in practice. The hybrid design also positions the method as complementary to recent 3D foundation models like DUSt3R and MASt3R, which the paper shows can be further improved by the affine correction framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even explicitly metric-trained depth models (e.g., Depth Anything) still benefit from affine correction challenges the assumption that metric depth eliminates the need for per-view normalization, with practical implications for any pipeline integrating MDE priors into geometric estimation. The modular design—compatible with arbitrary feature matchers and MDE models—makes the contribution easy to adopt in existing SfM/VO pipelines. Open questions include whether the affine model is sufficient for more complex depth ambiguities (e.g., non-linear distortions in very large scenes), how the approach extends to multi-view or sequential settings, and whether the gains persist as MDE models become truly metric-consistent across views.
