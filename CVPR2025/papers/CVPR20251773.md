## Problem

In conditional coding-based neural video codecs, the inherent reference feature propagation mechanism carries irrelevant information that accumulates errors over long prediction chains. While DCVC-FM mitigates this by periodically switching between reference frames and propagated features, this manual fixed-period switching does not sufficiently exploit reference information in both pixel and feature domains, leaving room for better temporal context modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251773/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec takes the current frame x_t, the previous decoded reference frame x̂_{t-1}, and the propagated reference feature F_{t-1} as inputs, and produces a compressed bitstream (motion bits + frame bits) and a reconstructed frame x̂_t whose feature F_t is propagated to the next frame. Training uses 7-frame Vimeo-90k clips cropped to 256×256 and 32-frame cascaded sequences at 256×384; evaluation is on 96-frame sequences under low-delay settings with intra-period 32 or −1, measured by BD-rate in RGB-PSNR.

## Method

DCMVC introduces a two-step context modulation on top of the DCVC-DC baseline. First, flow orientation warps the reference frame with the decoded flow to form a prediction frame, then uses Spynet as a pyramid inter-frame correlation extractor to estimate an oriented flow between the reference and prediction frames; this oriented flow aligns the reference frame to produce an additional oriented temporal context C̃_t^0 that captures inter-frame correlation lost under the rate-distortion constraint on motion bits. Second, context compensation fuses the oriented context with the propagated context C_t^0 via a global-local network: shared mutual extractors produce shallow features, private global and local extractors (the local branch using invertible neural networks with affine decoupling layers) capture complementary information, and corresponding fusion networks recombine them into the final compensated context C̄_t^0. A decoupling loss L_decouple = (Cor(L̃,L))²/(Cor(G̃,G))²+δ) supervises training to make global features (structure, background) more correlated while local features (texture, edges) less correlated across the two contexts, thereby eliminating irrelevant propagated information and alleviating error propagation without extra signal overhead.

## Results

Under the challenging intra-period=-1 setting (96 frames, RGB-PSNR), DCMVC achieves an average BD-rate of −22.7% relative to H.266/VVC (VTM-13.2), representing a 22.7% bitrate saving. Compared to the previous state-of-the-art neural video codec DCVC-FM (−12.6% average), DCMVC delivers an additional 10.1% bitrate reduction. Under the standard intra-period=32 setting, DCMVC achieves −19.4% average BD-rate vs. VTM and outperforms DCVC-FM (−9.9%) by roughly 9.5 percentage points. The method also demonstrates superior error-propagation resistance, maintaining quality across long prediction chains with lower bitrate cost than DCVC-FM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCMVC outperforms all seven baselines on every tested dataset under both intra-period settings. Against the traditional codec H.266/VVC (VTM-13.2), DCMVC achieves 22.7% average bitrate saving (intra-period=−1) and 19.4% (intra-period=32). Versus the previous SOTA neural codec DCVC-FM, DCMVC improves by 10.1% average BD-rate (−22.7% vs. −12.6%), with the largest per-dataset gains on HEVC E (−36.5% vs. −26.6%) and UVG (−35.6% vs. −24.3%). Compared to DCVC-DC (the direct baseline architecture), DCMVC improves by 13.4 percentage points in average BD-rate. The earlier DCVC-series methods (DCVC, DCVC-TCM, DCVC-HEM) and SDD are all substantially outperformed, with DCVC-HEM and SDD still showing positive (worse-than-VTM) average BD-rates on several datasets while DCMVC is negative across all except USTC-TD. Notably, DCMVC's advantage over DCVC-FM is most pronounced on high-motion or high-resolution content (HEVC D, HEVC E, UVG), suggesting the context modulation is particularly effective where error propagation is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DCVC lineage of conditional coding-based neural video codecs, building directly on DCVC-DC's diverse context generation and DCVC-FM's periodic reference-frame refreshing to mitigate error propagation. Its key departure is replacing DCVC-FM's binary switching between propagated features and reference frames with a continuous two-step modulation: flow orientation (using Spynet to extract oriented inter-frame correlation from the reference frame) generates an additional oriented context, and context compensation (a global-local fusion network with invertible affine decoupling layers) modulates the propagated context with the oriented one. A decoupling loss that encourages global-feature correlation while suppressing local-feature correlation between the two contexts is a novel training constraint not seen in prior DCVC variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.1% BD-rate improvement over DCVC-FM and 22.7% over H.266/VVC are meaningful in a field where recent gains have been narrowing, and the error-propagation mitigation in long prediction chains (intra-period −1) is practically important for real-world streaming. However, the complexity cost is non-trivial (4131G MACs vs. 2334G for DCVC-FM), and the paper acknowledges that rigid warping and indirect MV constraint via RD loss remain suboptimal. Open questions include whether learnable warping or explicit motion-pattern priors could close the remaining gap without further complexity growth, and whether the decoupling-loss strategy generalizes beyond the specific global/local feature decomposition used here.
