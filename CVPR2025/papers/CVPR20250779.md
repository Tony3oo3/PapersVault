## Problem

Existing methods for removing sensitive concepts from diffusion models—dataset-level filtering, post-generation NSFW classifiers, and generation-time guidance (SLD, ESD)—either destroy user experience by replacing entire images, incur prohibitive retraining costs, or modify unrelated regions of the output, degrading overall image quality. No prior approach can simultaneously pinpoint the spatial location of a target concept during generation and replace only that region while leaving the rest of the image consistent with the original model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250779/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During each denoising step, the method consumes three text inputs (a location prompt identifying the target concept, the original generation prompt, and a replacing prompt specifying the substitute concept) alongside the current latent representation. The concept localizer outputs a spatial mask over the latent, and the Dual Prompts Cross-Attention module produces a modified latent in which only the masked region is conditioned on the replacing prompt. The final output is a generated RGB image where the target concept has been swapped, with non-target pixels remaining pixel-consistent with the unmodified Stable Diffusion output.

## Method

The Concept Localizer fine-tunes only the W_k, W_v projection matrices in all self- and cross-attention layers plus word embeddings of a pretrained Stable Diffusion U-Net using 1- or 10-shot cross-entropy and MSE losses. A key design choice is to partition cross-attention scores by spatial resolution: lower-resolution scores (better semantic disentanglement) are used to gate and refine higher-resolution scores (richer high-frequency detail) via A_cross = A_cross^l + A_cross^l · A_cross^h, and the result is element-wise multiplied with the self-attention map to yield the final mask. The Dual Prompts Cross-Attention module is entirely training-free: it computes two separate attention outputs—one with the replacing prompt's keys/values masked by M, the other with the original prompt's keys/values masked by (1−M)—and sums them, so that only the localized region is reconditioned on the new concept while all other regions follow the original prompt exactly.

## Results

On CelebA-Mask-HQ (10-shot), the concept localizer achieves 78.1 average mIOU, outperforming SLiMe (75.7) and SegDDPM (78.0), and substantially exceeding ReGAN (69.9). On Pascal-Car (10-shot), it reaches 69.29 average mIOU versus SLiMe's 68.3. For non-target region preservation during nudity replacement, the method achieves MSE 45.86, PSNR 34.18, and SSIM 0.828, dramatically outperforming SLD (MSE 104.81, SSIM 0.346) and ESD-u (MSE 99.71, SSIM 0.482). On COCO 30K, image fidelity (FID 15.15) and text alignment (CLIP 30.67) remain close to the original Stable Diffusion (FID 14.50, CLIP 31.32), confirming minimal collateral damage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SLiMe (the closest few-shot diffusion-based segmentation baseline), the proposed localizer wins on average mIOU in both 10-shot (78.1 vs 75.7) and 1-shot (70.2 vs 69.6) settings on CelebA-Mask-HQ, and in both settings on Pascal-Car (69.29 vs 68.3; 64.3 vs 61.6). Against SegDDPM, the method edges ahead in 10-shot (78.1 vs 78.0) but the gap is narrow; in 1-shot it wins more clearly (70.2 vs 60.1). ReGAN and SegGPT are substantially outperformed across all settings. For the replacement task, the key differentiator is non-target region preservation: the method achieves SSIM 0.828 and PSNR 34.18 versus SLD's 0.346/27.93 and ESD-u's 0.482/28.15, indicating far less disruption to surrounding content. On COCO image quality, all methods (including the original SD) produce similar FID and CLIP scores, confirming that the replacement does not degrade general generation capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-removal line of work (SLD, ESD, Selective Amnesia, MACE) by introducing a spatially localized replacement mechanism rather than the global guidance or weight modification used by prior methods. The concept localizer draws directly from the attention-based segmentation literature (SLiMe, DiffSegmenter), repurposing U-Net self- and cross-attention scores for in-generation localization rather than post-hoc segmentation of existing images. The Dual Prompts Cross-Attention module is a targeted modification of the standard Transformer cross-attention formula, splitting the value pathway by a binary mask so that two different text embeddings condition different spatial regions simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that targeted concept replacement can preserve non-target regions far better than global guidance methods (SSIM 0.83 vs. 0.35–0.48), which addresses a major user-experience limitation of current safety filters. However, the evaluation is limited to a single target concept (nudity) on Stable Diffusion v1.4, and the concept localizer still requires few-shot fine-tuning per concept, raising open questions about scalability to multiple simultaneous concepts, zero-shot generalization, and applicability to more abstract or non-spatially-localized concepts. Whether the dual-prompt masking strategy transfers to other diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models) remains untested.
