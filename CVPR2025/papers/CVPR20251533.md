## Problem

Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V) generation models struggle to follow text prompts that specify motion, largely due to conditional image leakage where the model over-relies on the image condition. The standard L2 diffusion loss treats all pixels equally, so the small fraction of pixels (~3%) carrying meaningful motion is effectively drowned out by the static majority, causing the model to learn to produce near-static outputs. Additionally, no existing benchmark adequately evaluates text-driven motion in TI2V generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (serving as the first frame) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired motion, and produces a 16-frame video at 320×512 resolution. The conditioning image is concatenated channel-wise with the noised video latents (x-cat) rather than injected via cross-attention, and the text is encoded with a CLIP text encoder for cross-attention conditioning. During inference, DDIM sampling with 50 steps and a guidance scale of 7.5 is used, with no additional motion inputs required at test time.

## Method

The core contribution is the Motion Focal Loss (MotiF), which re-weights the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss using a per-pixel motion heatmap derived from RAFT optical flow between consecutive frames. The optical flow magnitude is normalized to [0,1] via a steep sigmoid-like function σ(x) = 1/(1+e^{100(0.05−x)}) to produce a polarized heatmap, then downsampled to match the latent spatial resolution. The weighted loss L_motif = E‖m′·(ε − ε_θ(z_t, c, t))‖² is added to the unweighted diffusion loss with a coefficient λ=1, explicitly forcing the model to reduce error in high-motion regions. The model is built on a pretrained VideoCrafter2 T2V diffusion model (spacetime-factorized U-Net with VAE encoder/decoder) and trained for 32K steps on 1M video-text pairs in v-prediction mode. The authors also introduce TI2V-Bench, a 320-pair evaluation set spanning 22 scenarios with synthetic images from meta.ai, and a JUICE-inspired human A-B evaluation protocol that yields a single TI2V score plus per-axis justifications.

## Results

On the proposed TI2V-Bench, MotiF achieves a 72% average human preference (TI2V score) over nine open-sourced TI2V models, with the largest gains in text alignment and object motion justifications. On Animate Bench automatic metrics, MotiF scores 92.68 on Image Alignment and 67.73 on Text Alignment, placing it in the middle of the compared methods. Notably, a trivial static-video baseline (repeating the first frame) achieves the highest Image Alignment (99.29) and a reasonable Text Alignment (66.24), underscoring the limitations of these automatic metrics for TI2V evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotiF is compared against nine open-sourced TI2V methods (VideoCrafter1, VideoCrafter2, SEINE, AnimateAnything, Cinemo, TI2V-Zero, ConsistI2V, DynamiCrafter, I2VGen-XL, and Cond-leak) on both TI2V-Bench (human evaluation) and Animate Bench (automatic metrics). In the primary human evaluation on TI2V-Bench, MotiF wins with a 72% average preference, with the dominant justifications being superior text alignment and object motion—directly validating the motion focal loss design. On Animate Bench automatic metrics, MotiF (92.68 / 67.73) is competitive but not the best on either axis: TI2V-Zero leads Text Alignment (68.89) while the static baseline dominates Image Alignment (99.29), and methods like AnimateAnything (96.78) and Cond-leak (94.05) score higher on Image Alignment. The paper argues these automatic metrics are unreliable for TI2V (a static video scores best on Image Alignment), which is why human A-B evaluation is treated as the primary evidence. Among the compared methods, those relying solely on cross-attention for image conditioning (I2VGen-XL, VideoCrafter) produce visibly worse image alignment, while MotiF's concatenation-based conditioning (x-cat) combined with the motion focal loss yields the strongest overall human preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VideoCrafter2 as a pretrained T2V diffusion backbone and positions itself as an orthogonal complement to the two dominant strategies in TI2V generation: (1) novel image-conditioning architectures (cross-attention, concatenation) and (2) additional motion priors fed as model inputs (motion scores, optical flow masks, motion residuals). Rather than adding another input signal, MotiF modifies the training objective by reweighting the diffusion loss with an optical-flow-derived motion heatmap, drawing a direct analogy to focal loss in dense detection. The TI2V-Bench benchmark extends prior evaluation efforts (AnimateBench, AIGCBench) by requiring text to specify motion rather than merely describe the starting image, and by covering challenging multi-object and novel-object-introduction scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical and underappreciated failure mode—conditional image leakage—where models over-rely on the input image and produce near-static videos, and shows that a simple loss reweighting can substantially improve text-driven motion without any inference-time overhead. The finding that standard automatic metrics (image alignment, text alignment on Animate Bench) are unreliable for TI2V evaluation, with a static-video baseline scoring best on image alignment, is an important caution for the community. However, the method still struggles with fine-grained multi-object control and novel object introduction, and the evaluation relies on a single 1M-pair internal dataset and one base architecture, leaving open whether the gains transfer to DiT-based backbones or longer videos.
