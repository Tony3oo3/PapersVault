## Problem

Existing temporal fusion in vision-based 3D semantic occupancy prediction (VisionOcc) is limited to voxel-level feature stacking, overlooking three other exploitable temporal cues: scene-level consistency, motion calibration, and geometric complementation. Moreover, multi-frame stacking approaches (e.g., SOLOFusion) incur linearly growing memory costs as the number of stored historical frames increases, making them impractical for resource-constrained deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251935/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of N-camera RGB images (256×704 or 512×1408) over T timesteps along with camera pose transformations, and outputs a 3D semantic occupancy map at 0.4 m voxel resolution spanning −40 m to 40 m in X/Y and −1 m to 5.4 m in Z, with 18 semantic classes. All temporal information—volume features, scene-adaptive parameters, motion offsets, and depth probability distributions—is compressed into a single-frame-sized hidden state, so memory remains constant regardless of sequence length.

## Method

GDFusion reinterprets the vanilla RNN update h^t = Ah^{t−1} + Bx^t as a gradient descent step minimizing an MSE loss between current and historical features (Proposition 1), providing a unified theoretical framework for fusing heterogeneous temporal representations. Four distinct loss functions are defined—one per temporal cue (voxel, scene, motion, geometry)—and their gradients serve as temporal residuals that update each representation via a single-frame hidden state. Scene-level fusion encodes global environmental priors into learnable network parameters (γ, β, W, b) updated by a self-supervised augmentation-prediction task; motion fusion refines per-voxel alignment offsets using a Jacobian-aware gradient; geometry fusion blends depth probability distributions with a GRU-style adaptive gate; and voxel-level fusion applies the standard RNN recurrence on warped volume features. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play, adding less than 6% runtime per fusion module while reducing inference memory by 30–72% relative to 16-frame SOLOFusion stacking.

## Results

On the Occ3D benchmark (nuScenes), GDFusion applied to BEVDetOcc achieves 43.6 mIoU (+1.7 over the SOLOFusion-based baseline of 41.9), 77.8 IoU (+2.7), and reduces inference memory from 10,717 MB to 3,017 MB (−72%). Applied to ALOcc, it reaches 46.5 mIoU (+1.0) and 77.4 IoU (+2.1) with 60% memory reduction. In the scaled-up Swin-Base setting (Table 2), ALOcc-GF attains the best overall mIoU of 51.8 (+1.2) and 79.9 IoU (+1.8) while cutting memory from 24,072 MB to 8,312 MB (−65%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDFusion is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on three baselines (BEVDetOcc, FB-Occ, ALOcc) and compared against 12 prior methods. Against the SOLOFusion-based BEVDetOcc-SF, GDFusion gains +1.7 mIoU and +2.7 IoU while using 72% less memory (3,017 vs. 10,717 MB). Against FB-Occ, it improves mIoU by +1.9 and IoU by +3.3 with 30% memory savings. Against ALOcc, it adds +1.0 mIoU and +2.1 IoU with 60% memory reduction. In the scaled-up Swin-Base setting, ALOcc-GF (51.8 mIoU) surpasses all other methods including COTR (46.2) and BEVDetOcc-SF (47.1), while BEVDetOcc-GF (48.5) also exceeds COTR. The memory advantage is consistent: GDFusion's single-frame RNN hidden state avoids the linear memory growth of SOLOFusion's multi-frame stacking, maintaining near-constant memory regardless of temporal history length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VisionOcc pipeline established by BEVDet4D and its successors (FBOcc, ALOcc, COTR), which all perform only voxel-level temporal fusion via multi-frame feature stacking (as in SOLOFusion). It departs from this lineage by identifying three additional temporal cues—scene-level consistency, motion calibration, and geometric complementation—and unifying their fusion through a gradient-descent reinterpretation of vanilla RNNs. The theoretical framing connects to optimization-inspired RNNs (LISTA, CRFasRNN, TTT, Titans), while the individual mechanisms draw from test-time adaptation (scene-level), deformable convolutions (motion), and GRU gating (geometry), repurposing each within a single RNN-style update rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30–72% inference memory reduction while matching or exceeding 16-frame SOLOFusion performance is practically significant for real-time autonomous driving deployment, where memory budgets are tight. The gradient-descent unification provides a principled, extensible template for integrating heterogeneous temporal signals (parameters, flow fields, probability distributions, volume features) that could transfer to other sequential perception tasks. Open questions include whether the single-frame hidden state degrades over very long sequences, whether the scene-level parameter approach generalizes beyond short driving clips, and whether the framework scales to higher-resolution or larger-scene settings without the current per-voxel gradient computations becoming prohibitive.
