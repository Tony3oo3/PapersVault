## Problem

Embodied AI requires diverse, realistic, and interactable 3D scenes, but current datasets rely on labor-intensive artist-driven design or procedurally generated environments that lack real-world diversity and small-item detail. Converting real-world 3D scans into simulatable replicas is particularly challenging because available synthetic assets lack direct equivalents for scanned objects, and no principled method exists for selecting the optimal replacement asset from diverse candidates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SCAN2SIM takes a 2D object image (selected for minimal occlusion via SAM segmentation), a GPT-4V-generated text description, and a set of L candidate 3D point clouds (L≥6 per object, up to 10 in evaluation) as input, and outputs the optimal asset selection via a one-hot ranking followed by heuristic pose alignment (translation to object center, bounding-box scaling, and 30-degree-interval rotation). The full pipeline produces a simulatable scene replica with per-object physical properties (mass, material, bounciness) and 21 spatial relationship types, built from 15,366 objects across 831 fine-grained categories in 706 scenes.

## Method

SCAN2SIM is a multi-modal contrastive model that aligns frozen image and text encoders (from ULIP-2) with a learnable 3D point cloud encoder to score each candidate against the scanned object. Training uses human ranking annotations from METASCENES, where annotators ranked all candidates by geometric and visual similarity; the primary loss (Eq. 2) supervises selection among in-scene candidates, while an auxiliary loss (Eq. 3) enforces cross-scene alignment by mixing the true best candidate with distractors from other scenes. The dataset construction pipeline gathers candidates via three complementary routes—text-to-3D generation (Shape-E), image-to-3D generation (TripoSR, InstantMesh, Michelangelo), and text-to-3D retrieval (Uni3D, ULIP)—then applies texture optimization (Paint3D) and physics-based MCMC optimization guided by a hierarchical scene graph to ensure collision-free, physically plausible placements.

## Results

On the METASCENES test set (2497 objects, 10 candidates each), SCAN2SIM (I+T→P) achieves 28.4% Top-1 and 76.0% Top-5 accuracy for optimal asset selection, with the best similarity scores (CD 0.17, ECD 0.23, IoU 0.60, Color Hist. 24.65). For object pose alignment, SCAN2SIM outperforms ACDC on both METASCENES (IoU 0.35 vs. 0.29; Size Err. 0.26 vs. 0.34) and ScanNet++ (IoU 0.40 vs. 0.24; Size Err. 0.36 vs. 0.55). In cross-domain VLN, agents trained on METASCENES achieve 50.67% SR on held-out ScanNet++ scenes, a 5.34% absolute improvement over ProcTHOR-trained agents (45.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAN2SIM (I+T→P) achieves 28.4% Top-1 accuracy, more than doubling the best single-modality baseline GPT-4V (T→I, 16.5%) and ULIP-2 (T→P, 14.3%), and more than tripling the I→I baselines SSIM (6.3%) and LPIPS (5.9%). Among multi-modal baselines, ULIP-2 (I+T→P, 13.1%) and ACDC (I+T→I, 12.3%) both fall well short, confirming that task-specific training on METASCENES ranking annotations is critical. For pose alignment (Table 3), SCAN2SIM reduces Size Error from 0.55 to 0.36 m³ and improves IoU from 0.24 to 0.40 over ACDC on ScanNet++, attributed to using scanned point clouds rather than render-and-compare. In the Micro-Scene Synthesis benchmark (Table 4), PhyScene achieves the lowest collision rates (Col_obj 0.395, Col_scene 0.45) while ATISS has the best SCA (0.631) and R_out (0.015), showing complementary strengths. For cross-domain VLN (Table 5), METASCENES-trained agents outperform ProcTHOR-trained agents by 5.34% SR on held-out ScanNet++ domains, with lower curvature (0.25 vs. 0.38) indicating smoother trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the real-to-sim 3D scene conversion line (Scan2CAD, ACDC, R3DS) by replacing the reliance on a fixed asset library (e.g., ShapeNet) with a diverse candidate pool assembled from retrieval (Objaverse via Uni3D/ULIP), text-to-3D generation (Shape-E), and image-to-3D generation (TripoSR, InstantMesh, Michelangelo). The key conceptual departure is introducing human-annotated ranking data over multiple candidates per object, which enables training a task-specific multi-modal alignment model (SCAN2SIM) rather than relying on general-purpose encoders or manual curation. For the downstream benchmarks, it builds on scene synthesis (ATISS, DiffuScene, PhyScene) and VLN (SPOC, ProcTHOR) but reframes them around small-object layouts and cross-domain transfer from real-scan-derived scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

METASCENES provides a scalable, annotation-driven path to creating simulatable 3D scenes without the artist labor bottleneck that limits existing EAI datasets, and the ranking annotations create a reusable training signal for future automated replica pipelines. However, the automated pipeline's 28.4% Top-1 asset selection accuracy reveals that fully autonomous replica creation remains far from solved, and the 706-scene scale, while larger than some prior efforts, is modest relative to the thousands of scenes in procedural datasets. Open questions include whether the ranking-based training signal transfers to unseen object categories, whether the sim-to-real gap truly narrows for manipulation (not just navigation), and whether the Micro-Scene Synthesis benchmark will drive progress on small-object layout generation that current methods handle poorly.
