## Problem

Video Scene Graph Generation suffers from two distinct biases that existing debiasing methods fail to address jointly: visual bias, where blurring and occlusion in video frames degrade object feature representations and cause the model to favor frequent entities, and semantic bias, where predicting predicates from visual features alone forces the model to over-rely on imbalanced prior distributions in the training data. Prior approaches either amplify high-frequency class biases through learnable attention or apply generic debiasing strategies that neglect the intrinsic visual-semantic duality of scene graphs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames processed by a Faster R-CNN (ResNet-101) detector with RoI-Align feature extraction, along with GloVe word embeddings for semantic context and bounding-box coordinates for spatial relationships. It supports both bounding-box grounding (4D boxes) and panoptic segmentation (n boundary points) for object anchors. The output is a set of scene graph triplets with predicate predictions across attention, spatial, and contacting categories, produced through an iterative refinement process where the Hierarchical Semantic Extractor is inactive during the first pass and activates from iteration N≥1 onward.

## Method

VISA comprises two coupled modules: a Memory Guided Sequence Modeling (MGSM) component that maintains an exponentially-decayed memory representation M_i^t (updated with rate λ) and fuses it with current-frame features via dual attention, theoretically reducing feature variance from Σ to approximately λΣ/2 to mitigate visual bias; and an Iterative Relation Generator (IRG) that constructs composite object features from visual, union-box, spatial, and GloVe embeddings, performs initial predicate prediction, then iteratively refines predictions through a Hierarchical Semantic Extractor using stride-two downsampling and cross-attention to capture multi-level context. The semantic debiasing is grounded in information theory: incorporating contextual information S reduces conditional entropy H(r_ij|v_i,v_j,S), which increases the KL divergence between the posterior and the biased prior, thereby reducing reliance on imbalanced training distributions. Training uses a combined loss of predicate cross-entropy, entity cross-entropy, and contrastive loss, with λ tuned per task (0.04 for SGCLS, 0.06 for SGDET) and one iteration (N=1) as the default configuration.

## Results

On the Action Genome dataset, VISA achieves state-of-the-art unbiased VidSGG performance across all three tasks (PREDCLS, SGCLS, SGDET) and all three constraint settings. The headline result is a +13.1% improvement in mR@20 and mR@50 for SGCLS under Semi Constraint (52.6 vs. TEMPURA's 39.5), the most realistic evaluation setting. Under No Constraint, VISA reaches 65.8 mR@10 (PREDCLS), 66.3 mR@20 (SGCLS), and 50.4 mR@50 (SGDET), surpassing the prior best (FloCoDe) by +2.6, +2.5, and +3.2 points respectively. On tail-class predicates (fewer than 8,000 training samples), VISA achieves a notable +11.0% mR@10 improvement over TEMPURA for SGDET under No Constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISA outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated setting. The most direct comparison is against TEMPURA [23] (CVPR 2023), the prior state-of-the-art unbiased VidSGG method: VISA gains +13.1% mR@20/mR@50 on SGCLS under Semi Constraint (52.6 vs. 39.5) and +9.9% mR@10 on SGDET under No Constraint (30.7 vs. 24.7). Against FloCoDe [12] (CVPRW 2024), the closest competitor, VISA leads by +2.1 to +3.2 points across tasks under No Constraint and by +3.3 to +3.8 points under With Constraint. Earlier baselines—STTran [5], STTran-TPI [33], TRACE [31], RelDN [40], and ISGG [13]—are surpassed by margins of 10–40+ points depending on the constraint setting, reflecting their lack of explicit debiasing mechanisms. On the extended PVSG and 4DPVSG tasks, VISA (as MGSM+IRG) outperforms VPS [4,17], IPS+T [4,34], and both PSG4DFormer variants [37] by +7.3 to +7.5 points in mR@20, confirming generalizability beyond standard VidSGG.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISA builds directly on TEMPURA [23] for its Transformer encoder pipeline and on STTran [5] for its Spatial Encoder and Temporal Decoder prediction modules, but departs conceptually by explicitly decomposing scene graph bias into two distinct sources: visual bias (high feature variance from blurring/occlusion) and semantic bias (over-reliance on imbalanced predicate priors due to insufficient context). Prior unbiased SGG methods (FloCoDe, DHL, LSKD) applied generic debiasing techniques—attention reweighting, dual-branch architectures, knowledge distillation—without distinguishing these two failure modes. VISA's MGSM module targets visual bias through memory-enhanced temporal integration that provably reduces feature variance, while its IRG with Hierarchical Semantic Extractor targets semantic bias by iteratively increasing KL divergence between the posterior and the biased prior, a framing grounded in information-theoretic analysis rather than purely empirical heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of visual and semantic bias provides a diagnostic framework that prior unbiased SGG work lacked, and the +13.1% mR@20/50 gain on SGCLS under Semi Constraint (with +11% on tail classes) demonstrates that addressing both bias sources simultaneously yields substantially better performance than either alone. However, the primary evaluation is confined to the Action Genome dataset, and the PVSG/4DPVSG extensions, while showing large gains, compare against limited baselines. Open questions include whether the memory-based variance reduction generalizes to longer video sequences or higher-resolution inputs, whether the iterative semantic fusion (N>1) offers diminishing returns that justify its computational cost, and whether the visual-semantic bias decomposition transfers to other structured prediction tasks like VQA or video captioning as the authors suggest.
