## Problem

Existing layout generation methods reduce design to predicting numerical bounding-box coordinates via cross-entropy or diffusion objectives, which penalizes aesthetically valid alternative layouts and ignores the rendered visual output entirely. This reductionist approach fails to capture contextual aesthetic requirements, hierarchical relationships, and the subjective quality that human designers naturally consider when arranging elements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252162/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a verbalized prompt where each design element is encoded as either image tokens (for images, shapes, backgrounds) processed by a vision encoder or text tokens (for text elements) processed by a text encoder, along with canvas width and height. The output is a sequence of discretized position tokens (K=224 bins per axis) representing the center coordinates, width, and height of each element's bounding box, which are mapped back to continuous spatial coordinates via the inverse binning function.

## Method

AesthetiQ extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) into a layout-specific Aesthetic-Aware Preference Alignment (AAPA) loss. During training, the MLLM (InternVL backbone) first undergoes position-aware instruction tuning with cross-entropy loss, then samples multiple layout candidates per input; a separate judge MLLM (VILA-7B) performs pairwise aesthetic comparisons on rendered layouts to select preferred vs. rejected pairs, and AAPA fine-tunes the layout model to increase the probability gap between preferred and rejected outputs. A quality-filtering protocol based on alignment and overlap heuristics ensures only well-formed layouts enter the preference training set, and an 80k-template pretraining phase provides foundational design priors before fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Crello dataset, AesthetiQ-8B achieves 42.83% Mean IoU (All setting), 52.67% (Single), and 40.64% (Multiple), along with a 17.19% Judge Win Rate, outperforming the strongest baseline LayoutNUWA (25.74% All IoU, 5.58% Win Rate) by approximately 17 percentage points in IoU. On WebUI, AesthetiQ-8B reaches 48.29% Mean IoU and 24.48% Judge Win Rate, surpassing LayoutNUWA (32.16% IoU, 15.28% Win Rate) by roughly 16 percentage points. Scaling from 1B to 8B parameters boosts the Judge Win Rate from 2.43% to 17.19% on Crello, confirming that larger MLLMs capture aesthetic nuances more effectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AesthetiQ-8B decisively outperforms all seven baselines on both Crello and WebUI across every reported metric. On Crello, it leads LayoutNUWA (the strongest baseline) by 17.09 pp in All IoU (42.83% vs 25.74%) and by 11.61 pp in Judge Win Rate (17.19% vs 5.58%); the gap over LACE is even wider at 19.65 pp IoU and 13.68 pp Win Rate. On WebUI, AesthetiQ-8B surpasses LayoutNUWA by 16.13 pp in Mean IoU (48.29% vs 32.16%) and by 9.20 pp in Win Rate (24.48% vs 15.28%), while the diffusion-based LACE and Desigen lag far behind at 17.88% and 15.36% IoU respectively. Notably, even the smallest AesthetiQ-1B variant (38.47% IoU, 19.29% Win Rate on WebUI) exceeds every baseline, suggesting the AAPA training paradigm is effective regardless of model scale. The paper also reports that human preferences correlate 78% with the VILA judge and 88% with GPT-4o, validating the MLLM-based evaluation protocol used throughout the comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from Rafailov et al. [40]—originally designed for aligning language models with human preferences—to the domain of graphic layout generation, where it becomes Aesthetic-Aware Preference Alignment (AAPA). It departs from the dominant paradigm in MLLM-based layout generation (LayoutNUWA, PosterLLaVa) and diffusion-based methods (LACE, LayoutDM), all of which rely on cross-entropy or denoising losses over discretized position tokens, by instead training on pairwise aesthetic preferences judged by a separate MLLM. The conceptual lineage also draws on the broader RLHF/preference-optimization literature, repurposing the 'judge model + preference pair' framework from text alignment to visual design quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that preference-based training can meaningfully improve layout aesthetics beyond what supervised position matching achieves, and introduces a win-rate evaluation metric that more directly captures design quality than IoU/BDE. However, absolute win rates remain low (17–24%), indicating that MLLMs still struggle to match human-designed layouts aesthetically, and the approach inherits the judge model's own aesthetic biases. Open questions include whether AAPA generalizes to more complex multi-page or interactive design tasks, whether the quality-filtering heuristics transfer across design domains, and whether the 78–88% human-judge correlation is sufficient for production use. The reliance on a separate judge MLLM also raises practical concerns about inference cost and circularity in evaluation.
