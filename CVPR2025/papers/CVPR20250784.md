## Problem

Existing sketch-to-image methods focus on simple, abstract hand-drawn sketches and struggle with professional design drawings that contain multi-level structural details (contours, local features, implicit gradients). Text-prompt approaches cannot accurately capture complex material and texture requirements, while image-translation methods suffer from appearance-structure conflicts and lack fine-grained control. Additionally, high-quality paired training data for design drawings is scarce, making training-based approaches impractical.

## Input / Output

The method takes an object-centered design drawing (line art with contours and fine structural details) and a reference photo providing the target appearance/material as input. The drawing is decomposed into three edge levels (single lines, double lines, soft edges) and fed into ControlNet, while the reference photo is background-removed, patch-reassembled into a texture map, and split into a Retinex illumination layer and a texture embedding. The output is a photorealistic rendered image that preserves the original drawing's structural fidelity while transferring the reference's lighting, material, and color properties.

## Method

LineArt is a training-free framework built on ControlNet (inpainting mode), IP-Adapter, and Stable Diffusion that simulates hierarchical visual cognition and classical oil-painting (Imprimatura) to guide generation. A multi-frequency lines fusion module decomposes the input drawing via morphological operations (dilation/erosion) into single lines for region boundaries, double lines for local detail emphasis, and soft edges extracted via Haar wavelet from a depth-estimated initial generation, all fused as geometry conditions for ControlNet. The appearance transfer is split into two stages: Base Layer Shaping uses a multi-scale Retinex decomposition of the reference to extract illumination statistics (mean, variance) that shift the initial noise distribution in latent space, establishing a soft link between reference lighting and generated shading; Surface Layer Coloring injects the patch-reassembled texture embedding into specific cross-attention layers of the U-Net to simulate translucent glazing, decoupling color/texture from the structural and lighting channels.

## Results

On the four ProLines sub-datasets (Bronze, DifferSketching, ImageNet-Sketch, DeepPatent), LineArt achieves the best FID on three of four (86.31 on Bronze, 100.71 on ImageNet-Sketch, 107.23 on DeepPatent) and the best LPIPS on three (0.25, 0.23, 0.20). The method dominates on texture fidelity (GLCM: 1.24–3.42 vs. 1.61–37.48 for baselines) and edge preservation (CD: 2.38–8.12 vs. 6.32–263.97), while also achieving the highest SSIM (0.83–0.96) and CLIPi (0.83–0.86) across most datasets. On DeepPatent, LineArt reaches a PSNR of 28.86 dB, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline (21.39 dB for CIA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LineArt outperforms the ControlNet+IP-Adapter baseline on every metric across all four datasets, with the largest gaps in GLCM (e.g., 2.42 vs. 29.23 on Bronze) and CD (2.38 vs. 2.72 on Bronze; 8.12 vs. 14.49 on DeepPatent), confirming that the multi-frequency line fusion and Imprimatura-inspired two-stage process add substantial value over a straightforward adapter combination. Against Cross-Image Attention (CIA), LineArt wins decisively on edge fidelity (CD 2.38–8.12 vs. 224.91–263.97) and FID, while CIA's texture metric (GLCM) is competitive but its structural collapse makes it unsuitable for design drawings. ZeST is the closest competitor on PSNR (25.87 vs. 25.20 on Bronze) but trails LineArt on FID (113.79 vs. 86.31), GLCM (5.71 vs. 2.42), and CD (6.32 vs. 2.38) on Bronze, and the gap widens on DeepPatent (FID 182.03 vs. 107.23; CD 27.56 vs. 8.12). DreamBooth achieves a marginally lower FID on DifferSketching (202.52 vs. 211.37) but is far behind on GLCM (37.48 vs. 1.24) and CD (30.93 vs. 2.48), indicating it captures overall realism at the cost of texture and structural accuracy. InstructPix2Pix'+IP-Adapter consistently ranks last or near-last on FID and LPIPS across all datasets. StyleID and AesPA-Net, evaluated qualitatively in Figure 13, lose light/shadow realism and fine structural detail during transfer, whereas LineArt preserves both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ControlNet + IP-Adapter + Stable Diffusion pipeline for conditional image generation, but departs from prior sketch-to-image work by targeting professional design drawings with complex multi-level structural details rather than simple hand-drawn sketches. Conceptually, it repurposes two domains of human knowledge—Marr's hierarchical visual cognition (decomposing edges into single lines, double lines, and soft edges) and the classical Imprimatura painting technique (splitting generation into underpainting and glazing stages)—to structure the diffusion denoising process in a training-free manner. This positions it as a knowledge-guided alternative to both text-conditioned generation (which struggles with complex appearance) and paired-data image translation methods (which suffer from identity inconsistency and attribute conflicts).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in design workflows where professional line drawings with fine structural details need photorealistic rendering without expensive 3D modeling or paired training data. The introduction of the ProLines dataset (5,101 curated design drawings with complexity-based filtering) provides a much-needed benchmark for this underexplored subdomain. However, the method still struggles with drawings containing excessive shadows or complex textures and with tasks requiring precise semantic correspondence, leaving open the question of whether knowledge-guided decomposition can scale to arbitrarily complex scenes. The training-free design also raises questions about how much of the performance gain comes from the specific painting-inspired decomposition versus the underlying ControlNet/IP-Adapter architecture.
