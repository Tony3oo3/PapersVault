## Problem

Existing certified defenses against graph poisoning attacks suffer from three critical limitations: they restrict the attacker to a single perturbation type (edges, nodes, or node features), are tailored to a specific GNN architecture or task, and provide only probabilistic rather than deterministic robustness guarantees. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously by developing the first certified defense that handles arbitrary combined perturbations on both node and graph classification with 100% accurate guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251518/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a training graph set (each graph comprising nodes, edges, and node feature vectors) and a test graph, and produces a class label for a target node or entire graph along with a certified perturbation size P. The certified size P is the maximum number of arbitrarily manipulated nodes, edges, and node-feature-modified nodes the model can tolerate while guaranteeing an unchanged prediction. The graph is internally partitioned into S subgraphs (default S=50 for node classification, S=60 for graph classification) via a cryptographic hash function, which determines the granularity of the robustness certificate.

## Method

PGNNCert divides each training graph into S subgraphs using a hash function—either edge-centric (hashing concatenated node indices of each edge) or node-centric (hashing the source node index for all outgoing edges)—then trains S independent GNN classifiers (GCN, GraphSAGE, or GAT) on the resulting subgraph sets. At inference, the test graph is divided identically, and a majority-voting ensemble aggregates the S sub-classifier predictions. The key theoretical contribution is a deterministic robustness guarantee: the certified perturbation size P = ⌊n_{y_a} − n_{y_b} − I(y_a > y_b)⌋ / 2, where n_{y_a} and n_{y_b} are the vote counts for the top two classes, is derived by upper-bounding how many sub-classifiers can change weights under any perturbation of size p (Theorems 2 and 4). The node-centric strategy is particularly powerful because all edges incident to a manipulated node are mapped to a single subgraph, meaning an injected node with arbitrarily many edges affects only one classifier, yielding stronger guarantees against node and feature perturbations.

## Results

PGNNCert achieves certified perturbation sizes up to 25 for node classification and 30 for graph classification, meaning it provably defends against that many arbitrary edge/node/feature perturbations. Normal (p=0) accuracy of PGNNCert is within ~5% of standard GNN training (e.g., GCN on Cora-ML: 0.73 standard vs. 0.68 PGNNCert-E/N; on PubMed: 0.86 vs. 0.83/0.85). On graph classification against edge perturbations, PGNNCert-E achieves 0.518 certified accuracy at 5 perturbed edges on PROTEINS (vs. 0.205 for Bagging) and 0.578 on DD (vs. 0.311 for Bagging). Against the optimization-based Metattack on Cora-ML, PGNNCert-N maintains 0.661 accuracy even when 40 edges/nodes are perturbed (exceeding its certified bound of 25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against node injection poisoning on node classification (Cora-ML, Citeseer, PubMed), PGNNCert-N outperforms all three baselines (RS, Bagging, Bi-RS-Include, and Bi-RS-Exclude), with the key advantage that each bounded node can have arbitrarily many edges. PGNNCert-E, however, is weaker than Bi-RS-Exclude on node injection due to its edge-centric counting, though it still beats RS and Bagging. On graph classification against edge perturbations, PGNNCert-E substantially outperforms Bagging: at 5 perturbed edges, PGNNCert-E achieves 0.518 vs. 0.205 on PROTEINS and 0.578 vs. 0.311 on DD; at 10 edges, PGNNCert-E still retains 0.260/0.330 while Bagging drops to 0.000 on both datasets. PGNNCert-N is competitive with PGNNCert-E on PROTEINS (0.474 vs. 0.518 at 5 edges) but weaker on DD, reflecting the different sensitivity of the two division strategies to edge-only attacks. Overall, PGNNCert dominates the baselines in certified robustness at non-trivial perturbation sizes, while sacrificing only ~1-3% clean accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGNNCert extends the deterministic certification framework of AGNNCert [24] (which certified GNNs against test-time evasion perturbations) to the training-time poisoning setting, and generalizes the hash-based subgraph partitioning and majority-voting mechanism from [24, 54] to handle arbitrary combinations of edge, node, and node-feature manipulations simultaneously. Prior certified defenses against poisoning—Bi-RS [22], Bagging [17], and PORE [18]—each restrict the adversary to a single perturbation type, target a specific GNN architecture or task, and provide only probabilistic guarantees. PGNNCert unifies these as special cases under a single deterministic guarantee that covers all three perturbation types jointly, while introducing two tailored division strategies (edge-centric and node-centric) that exploit GNN message-passing structure to bound the number of affected sub-classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first certified defense that simultaneously handles arbitrary edge, node, and node-feature poisoning with a 100%-accurate (deterministic) guarantee across both node and graph classification, filling a clear gap where prior methods could only certify against one perturbation type at a time. The practical limitation is the S× training and certification overhead (S sub-classifiers), which becomes prohibitive for large-scale GNNs, and the ~5% normal-accuracy drop from subgraph-based training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to federated or causal-explainable GNN settings (noted by the authors), whether the certified perturbation sizes (25–30) are sufficient for real-world threat models, and whether the S× overhead can be reduced via parallelism or approximate certification.
