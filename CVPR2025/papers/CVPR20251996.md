## Problem

Upcycled Mixture-of-Experts models, which convert pre-trained dense FFN layers into multi-expert MoE layers, introduce massive parameter overhead (e.g., 3.4B of 5B parameters in MoE-LLaVA-Phi) because each expert is a full copy of the original FFN. The paper identifies that these experts share the same initialization and exhibit >0.999 cosine similarity after training, meaning the expert-specific adjustments are minor and highly redundant, yet no prior work exploits this structural redundancy for parameter efficiency.

## Input / Output

For DeRS compression, the input is a fully trained upcycled MoE model and the output is a compressed weight representation consisting of one shared base matrix plus N compact delta matrices (stored as sparse index-value pairs or k-bit quantized tensors), which are synthesized on-the-fly during inference. For DeRS upcycling, the input is a pre-trained dense model (e.g., StableLM-1.6B, Phi-2-2.7B, DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B) and the output is a trainable MoE model where experts are parameterized as a shared FFN plus lightweight sparse or low-rank increments. Downstream, the models consume image+text questions or text prompts and produce text answers or generated code.

## Method

The DeRS paradigm decomposes N trained expert weights {W_1,...,W_N} into one expert-shared base weight W_base and N expert-specific delta weights {Δ_1,...,Δ_N}, then replaces each Δ_i with a lightweight representation F(Δ_i), and synthesizes the full expert weight as W_base + F(Δ_i) at inference. Two concrete applications are proposed: (1) DeRS Compression applies post-training sparsification (Bernoulli masking with drop rate p, compactly stored as index+value vectors) or k-bit quantization to the delta weights of already-trained experts, reducing a 4-expert MoE layer from 4×d×d_h parameters to (1+4(1-p))×d×d_h; (2) DeRS Upcycling avoids creating N full FFN copies during training by initializing one trainable shared FFN from the original dense weights and N zero-initialized sparse matrices (DeRS-SM) or low-rank factorizations A_i·B_i with rank r≪min(d,d_h) (DeRS-LM), reducing trainable expert parameters from N·d·d_h to (1+N(1-p))·d·d_h or d·d_h+N·r·(d+d_h) respectively. The shared base FFN must remain trainable (freezing it causes 1.3–1.6% performance drops), and the router is randomly initialized as in standard upcycling.

## Results

On the general multi-modal task (MoE-LLaVA-Phi), DeRS-SM upcycling achieves 61.1 overall score with only 1.11M added parameters versus 2.52B for vanilla upcycling (a 2270× reduction), while DeRS-LM reaches 61.0 with 2.42M added parameters (1041× reduction). On the code generation task (Coder-MoE), DeRS-SM improves overall performance from 60.2 to 61.0 (+0.8%) while reducing added parameters from 2.43B to 325M, and DeRS-LM achieves 60.9 with just 9.09M added parameters. DeRS compression (sparsification at 90% drop rate or 2-bit quantization) reduces MoE layer parameter count by ~65% with no measurable performance degradation across all three task domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DeRS upcycling against vanilla upcycling (the standard approach from Komatsuzaki et al. [21]) across three MoE-LLaVA architectures, two Med-MoE architectures, and one Coder-MoE architecture. On MoE-LLaVA-Phi, DeRS-SM wins on overall score (61.1 vs 60.8) and on 6 of 8 individual benchmarks, while reducing added parameters by 2270× (1.11M vs 2.52B). On MoE-LLaVA-StableLM, DeRS-SM also wins (57.7 vs 57.4) with a 4770× parameter reduction. On MoE-LLaVA-Qwen, both DeRS variants slightly underperform vanilla (56.2/56.3 vs 56.5), a rare loss of 0.2–0.3%. On the medical task (Med-MoE-Phi), DeRS-SM ties or marginally wins (73.0 vs 72.9) with a 2270× reduction, and on the code generation task (Coder-MoE), DeRS-SM clearly wins (61.0 vs 60.2, +0.8%) with a 7.5× parameter reduction, while DeRS-LM achieves 60.9 with a 267× reduction. Overall, DeRS upcycling matches or beats vanilla upcycling in 8 of 9 model-task combinations while reducing expert parameters by 2–4 orders of magnitude.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the upcycling paradigm (Komatsuzaki et al., 2022), which converts dense models into MoE by duplicating FFN layers as experts, and on prior MoE compression work (expert pruning, EEP, MC-SMoE) that reduces deployment cost by removing or merging entire experts. The key departure is exploiting a redundancy specific to upcycled models: since all experts initialize from the same base weight, their trained weights differ only by small deltas, enabling compression of the *differences* rather than the experts themselves. The parameter-efficient representations (sparse matrices, low-rank factorization) draw on LoRA-style and structured sparsity ideas, but are applied to the expert-delta decomposition rather than to the full weight matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying upcycled MoE models: a 2270× reduction in additional parameters with equal or better performance, and 43.8% inference memory savings, makes these models far more deployable on resource-constrained hardware. However, the extreme compression results (99% sparsity, 1-bit quantization) are partly enabled by the base models having been pre-fine-tuned on relevant data, and all experiments use relatively small backbones (1.6B–2.7B); whether the same degree of delta redundancy persists in larger models or under higher training budgets where experts diverge more substantially remains an open question. The paper also leaves open whether the DeRS paradigm extends to MoE models trained from scratch, where no shared initialization exists.
