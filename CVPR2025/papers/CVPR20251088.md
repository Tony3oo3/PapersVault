## Problem

Direct 3D Gaussian Splatting editing under diffusion-based generative guidance produces unstable geometry, blurred renderings, and needle-like artifacts because the discrete Gaussian structure restricts gradient propagation and its densification is highly sensitive to fluctuant generative gradients. This is especially problematic for 360° full-body avatars where maintaining 3D consistency across large viewpoint variations while enabling localized geometric changes remains unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251088/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 360° monocular video (1080×1920, 40–50 s, 250 frames) captured with a portable camera around a static subject, along with either a text prompt (generated via GPT-4) or a reference garment image from e-commerce. Camera poses are estimated with COLMAP. The output is a locally editable 3D avatar in TetGS form—tetrahedral grids with embedded Gaussian kernels—supporting photorealistic multi-view rendering and region-specific geometric modification while preserving all non-editing content exactly as reconstructed.

## Method

The core contribution is TetGS, a hybrid representation that embeds Gaussian kernels inside DMTet-style tetrahedral grids, where each Gaussian's position is determined by the SDF values of its father tetrahedron's vertices, enabling structured spatial guidance for kernel redistribution. The editing pipeline decouples into three stages: (1) high-fidelity 3D avatar instantiation via SDF reconstruction with NeuDA deformable anchors and normal regularization; (2) localized spatial adaptation that explicitly partitions tetrahedrons into frozen (preserved) and editable subsets, optimizing the editing SDF under a dual SDS constraint—global for overall harmony and local for precise geometric change—plus a surface-aware regularization to prevent occlusion of preserved Gaussians; (3) coarse-to-fine texture generation that first learns appearance on restricted 2D-surfel Gaussians via iterative normal-based inpainting from few sampled views, then activates full 3D attributes (position, covariance, opacity, view-dependent SH) under I2I-augmented multi-view supervision to recover high-frequency details.

## Results

On the 360° Full-Body Human Dataset, TetGS achieves a FID of 115.95, substantially outperforming GaussianEditor (194.54), DGE (201.82), and TIP-Editor (258.27) in rendering fidelity. For text-prompt alignment, TetGS scores 26.28 CLIP similarity, exceeding all baselines (22.14–23.05). For image-guided editing, TetGS attains 0.752 DINO similarity versus 0.726 for TIP-Editor. All metrics are computed on 60 rendered images captured evenly around the 3D avatar.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TetGS decisively outperforms all three baselines across every reported metric. Against GaussianEditor [60] and DGE [11] (text-guided 3DGS editing), TetGS reduces FID by roughly 40–43% (115.95 vs. 194.54 and 201.82) while improving CLIP text-image similarity by 4.1 and 3.2 points respectively, indicating both sharper renderings and better prompt alignment. Against TIP-Editor [77] (image-guided editing), the FID gap is even larger (115.95 vs. 258.27, a 55% reduction), and DINO reference-image similarity improves from 0.726 to 0.752. Qualitatively, the baselines exhibit needle-like artifacts, blurred renderings, color leakage into irrelevant regions, and minimal geometric changes, whereas TetGS produces coherent 360° full-body edits with clear geometric boundaries and photorealistic texture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for high-fidelity rendering and DMTet's tetrahedral grid structure for explicit geometric control, combining them into a novel hybrid representation (TetGS) where Gaussian kernels are embedded within and constrained by tetrahedra. It departs from prior 3DGS editing methods (GaussianEditor, DGE, TIP-Editor) that directly optimize Gaussians under diffusion-based generative guidance, which the authors argue causes unstable joint geometry-texture optimization. The key conceptual departure is the decoupling of editing into a structured spatial adaptation phase (guided by partitioned tetrahedra and dual SDS losses) followed by a separate coarse-to-fine appearance generation phase, rather than optimizing all Gaussian attributes simultaneously under generative supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled editing paradigm—first adapting geometry via structured tetrahedral constraints, then learning appearance with few-shot inpainting and attribute activation—offers a practical recipe for stable 3D editing that could generalize beyond avatars to other 3DGS-based editing tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to 10 static full-body subjects in a single self-created dataset, and the method requires 360° monocular video capture, restricting its applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to animated avatars, multi-subject scenes, or real-time interactive editing, and whether the tetrahedral constraint overhead is justified for simpler editing tasks where direct 3DGS optimization might suffice.
