## Problem

Progress toward generalist biomedical vision-language models is bottlenecked by the absence of large-scale, publicly accessible, annotated datasets spanning the full breadth of biomedical research. Existing literature-based datasets (ROCO, MEDICAT, PMC-CLIP, BiomedCLIP) are pre-filtered to narrow imaging modalities like radiology and pathology, overlooking complementary domains such as cell biology, molecular biology, genetics, and pharmacogenomics that encode critical biological mechanisms underlying health outcomes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB biomedical images (uniformly resized to ViT-L-14 input dimensions, with original resolutions ranging from thumbnails to 52,490×65,081 pixels) paired with text captions (median 64 tokens, max 12,389 tokens) or free-form text prompts. Outputs include image-text similarity scores for retrieval (reported as Recall@1/10/100), zero-shot class predictions via prompt matching, and ranked lists of images or captions. The underlying dataset provides 24M image-caption pairs with 27 metadata fields per entry, including expert-curated global and local concept labels, panel type, license, and article-level provenance.

## Method

BIOMEDICA is an ETL pipeline that extracts the full PMC-OA repository (6M articles, 24M image-caption pairs) and serializes it into WebDataset format for high-throughput streaming. For concept annotation, images are embedded with DINOv2 (ViT-L-14 distilled), reduced via PCA to 25 components, over-clustered with k-means (K=2000), and then annotated by seven domain experts using a hierarchical taxonomy derived from biomedical ontologies (Uberon, NCBI Taxon, Cell Line Ontology); labels are propagated to individual images via cluster membership. BMC-CLIP is produced by continually pretraining OpenCLIP (ViT-L-14) with the InfoNCE contrastive loss at an effective batch size of 8192 (4× H100 80GB, 32-bit FP, LR 1e-6, 1K warmup), exploring three data-mixture strategies: full 24M dataset, concept-balanced 8M subset (dropping over-represented topics like plots), and concept-filtered 6M subset (retaining only clinical/scientific imaging, immunoassays, diagrams, chemical structures, maps, and tools). A WiSE-FT model-merging step (convex combination of base and adapted weights) is applied post-training to recover performance on specific task subsets without additional training.

## Results

The best model, BMC-CLIP_CF (concept-filtered continual pretraining on 6M pairs), achieves 47.72% average zero-shot accuracy across 35 biomedical classification tasks, outperforming BioMedCLIP (41.16%) by 6.56% and OpenCLIP (41.18%) by 6.54%. The largest domain-specific gains are in dermatology (65.81% vs. 36.01% for BioMedCLIP, +29.8%) and ophthalmology (43.62% vs. 26.12%, +17.5%). On microscopy composition identification (4 tasks), BMC-CLIP_CF/WiSE-FT reaches 58.53% average, surpassing BioMedCLIP (49.72%) by 8.81%. In retrieval on BioMed-Flickr, BMC-CLIP_CF achieves Recall@100 of 38.30 (image→text) and 36.10 (text→image), exceeding BioMedCLIP by +2.03 and +0.47 respectively, while using 10× less compute and 2.5× less data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMC-CLIP_CF decisively outperforms PMC-CLIP across all 35 tasks, with a +24.67% average improvement (minimum gap +5.13% in breast ultrasound, maximum +53.22% in dermatology). Against BioMedCLIP, BMC-CLIP_CF wins in 8 of 10 domain subsets, achieving a +6.56% average gain, with the largest margins in dermatology (+29.8%), ophthalmology (+17.5%), breast ultrasound (+8.01%), and non-neo histopathology (+6.98%); it trails only in cell profiling (−2.18%) and structure profiling (−4.37%). Compared to OpenCLIP, BMC-CLIP_CF improves the overall average by +6.54%, with particularly large gains in ophthalmology (+19.87%) and dermatology (+45.44%), though OpenCLIP retains an edge in M.I. surgery (67.51 vs. 55.27). CoCa is the weakest general-purpose baseline (31.12% average), losing to BMC-CLIP_CF by +16.60% overall. Notably, BMC-CLIP_CF achieves these results using 10× less compute and 2.5× less training data than BioMedCLIP, and WiSE-FT model merging further extends the lead in x-ray radiology (+15.46% over BioMedCLIP) and ultrasound (+11.22%) without additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of literature-based biomedical image-text pretraining (ROCO, MEDICAT, PMC-CLIP, BiomedCLIP) by adopting a domain-agnostic approach: rather than filtering to radiology or pathology via supervised classifiers, it serializes the full PMC-OA corpus and provides 27 metadata fields plus expert-curated concept labels for on-demand filtering. Methodologically, it departs from prior work's train-from-scratch paradigm by continually pretraining OpenCLIP (ViT-L-14) with InfoNCE loss, and introduces streaming via WebDataset to eliminate the need for 27 TB of local storage. The concept labeling pipeline (DINOv2 embeddings → PCA → k-means → expert annotation → propagation) replaces the supervised CNN classifiers used in ROCO/MEDICAT, and WiSE-FT model merging is applied as a post-training refinement step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BIOMEDICA is the largest open-source biomedical image-text dataset to date (24M pairs, 6M articles) and the first to support streaming, making it accessible to labs without multi-TB storage. The 40-task benchmark spanning pathology, radiology, ophthalmology, dermatology, surgery, and molecular biology addresses a real gap: prior evaluations were confined to radiology and pathology. Key limitations include the 77-token CLIP context window (truncating longer captions) and uniform resizing of highly variable image resolutions, both of which likely cap performance. Open questions include whether longer-context encoders, resolution-aware preprocessing, or scaling beyond ViT-L-14 would close the remaining gap to human-level zero-shot performance across the full biomedical domain.
