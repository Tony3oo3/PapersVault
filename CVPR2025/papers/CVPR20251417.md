## Problem

While the <SEG> token is widely used as a text prompt to bridge LMMs (e.g., LLaVA) with mask decoders (e.g., SAM) in reasoning segmentation, its underlying mechanism—how it maps language vocabulary embeddings into visual space—remains unexplored. This paper investigates what the <SEG> token actually contributes and leverages the finding that it encodes semantic similarity akin to direct text mentions to guide where the model should attend.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251417/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 336×336 RGB image (processed into CLIP patches) and a natural-language query that may be explicit (e.g., "antler") or implicit (e.g., "part of the deer's body used for defense and attracting mates"). It produces a binary segmentation mask of the same spatial resolution as the input image, along with a free-form text reasoning response. Unusually, the intermediate similarity map between the <SEG> token embedding and image token embeddings is explicitly computed and converted into spatial point coordinates that serve as additional prompts to the SAM decoder.

## Method

READ consists of three modules: a frozen LLaVA encoder (fine-tuned via LoRA), a Similarity as Points (SasP) module, and a SAM ViT-H decoder. The key mechanism is that the last hidden-layer embedding of the <SEG> token is dot-producted with all image token embeddings to produce a similarity score vector; points exceeding a mean-plus-standard-deviation threshold become positive prompts, those below a mean-minus-standard-deviation threshold become negative prompts, and the rest are neutral. Because sorting and thresholding make these points discrete and non-differentiable, a Discrete-to-Continuous (DtoC) step applies a Gaussian-like weighted average interpolation over the spatial grid, yielding continuously differentiable coordinates whose gradients propagate back into the LMM. These continuous points, together with the <SEG> token embedding, are fed into SAM's prompt encoder and TwoWayAttention decoder to produce the final mask. The entire SasP module is parameter-free and can be inserted into any existing <SEG>-like pipeline in a plug-and-play fashion.

## Results

On the ReasonSeg test set, READ-7B-LLaVA1.5(ft) achieves 58.5 gIoU and 58.6 cIoU overall, outperforming LISA-7B-LLaVA1.5(ft) by 2.9% gIoU and 1.7% cIoU, with a 4.7% cIoU gain on the validation set (67.6 vs. 62.9). On RefCOCO(+/g), READ achieves 78.1 cIoU on refCOCO val (+3.2% over LISA-7B) and 70.1 cIoU on refCOCOg val(U) (+2.2%). On the false-premise FP-RefCOCO(+/g) benchmark, READ surpasses SESAME by up to 3.73% cIoU on the segmentation task and 3.51% on the detection ('see') task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

READ consistently outperforms LISA-7B-LLaVA1.5(ft) across all ReasonSeg splits, with the largest margin on validation cIoU (+4.7%) and test long-query gIoU (+2.5%), confirming that the SasP point-prompt mechanism improves fine-grained grounding. On RefCOCO(+/g), READ beats LISA-7B by 3.2% cIoU on refCOCO val, 3.3% on refCOCO+ val, and 2.2% on refCOCOg val(U), while also surpassing earlier non-LMM baselines (MCN, VLT, CRIS, LAVT, ReLA) by 4–16% cIoU. On the false-premise FP-RefCOCO(+/g) benchmark, READ exceeds SESAME by 3.03–3.51% on the 'see' (detection) task and 2.33–3.73% on the 'segment' task, indicating that the similarity-as-points guidance helps the model both detect absent objects and produce more accurate masks under false premises. READ also outperforms non-reasoning baselines (X-Decoder, Grounded-SAM, SEEM, OVSeg, GRES) by 20–40+ cIoU on ReasonSeg, highlighting the advantage of LMM-based reasoning over purely visual or detection-based pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the <SEG>-token paradigm established by LISA and SESAME, which jointly train an LLaVA-based LMM with a SAM decoder for reasoning segmentation. Rather than treating the <SEG> token as a black-box prompt, the authors conduct an empirical investigation (via similarity maps) revealing that the token acquires semantics analogous to direct text mentions, and then exploit this by converting high-activation regions into differentiable point prompts (SasP + DtoC) fed alongside the token embedding into SAM. The method departs from prior work by adding a spatially-informative, parameter-free guidance signal derived from the LLM's own hidden states, while remaining a drop-in module for existing <SEG>-like pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single placeholder token learns to encode object-level semantics comparable to explicit text queries offers a useful interpretability lens for how LMMs ground language into visual space, with implications beyond segmentation. The plug-and-play SasP module is lightweight and broadly applicable to any <SEG>-style architecture, though the authors acknowledge the vanilla dot-product similarity is a starting point and leave more expressive cross-attention strategies for future work. Open questions include whether the similarity-as-points signal generalizes to multi-object or video settings, and whether the moderate gains (≤4.7% cIoU) persist at larger model scales or with richer similarity computations.
