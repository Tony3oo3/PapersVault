## Problem

MLLM-based automatic captioning for text-to-image person ReID produces captions with limited style diversity because existing methods rely on a small number of manually designed templates (46–456) that capture sentence patterns but ignore wording-level style variations. No prior approach learns and mimics the rich, heterogeneous description styles of thousands of human annotators to generate diverse captions at scale, which limits the generalization of ReID models trained on such annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250805/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For ReID inference, the model takes a free-form text description of a person (max 77 tokens) and retrieves a ranked list of pedestrian images from a gallery; images are resized to 384×128 pixels. For the annotation pipeline, the MLLM takes a pedestrian image plus a learned style prompt and produces a natural-language caption; the resulting HAM-PEDES dataset contains 1.0M images each annotated with 2 captions, used to pre-train the ReID backbone (CLIP-ViT-B/16 image encoder + 12-layer transformer text encoder) with the SDM loss.

## Method

HAM extracts a style feature from each human caption by using an LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) to replace identity-specific attribute words with generic placeholders, then encoding the anonymized text with CLIP's text encoder; KMeans clustering groups captions into K₁ style clusters, each represented by a learnable prompt (M=10 tokens) that is concatenated with image and text tokens at the MLLM input. To complement KMeans' density-biased clustering, UPS defines a style feature space bounded by [μ−βσ, μ+βσ] and uniformly samples K₂ additional cluster centers, assigning Q=200 nearest samples to each, thereby capturing styles in sparse regions. The MLLM (LLaVA-1.6-7B or Qwen-VL-Chat-7B) is fine-tuned with only the K₁+K₂ style prompts and the vision-language adaptor updated (all LLM and vision-encoder parameters frozen) using an autoregressive loss, so each prompt steers the MLLM toward a distinct annotator style. The combined 3,000 prompts are used to annotate 1.0M SYNTH-PEDES images, producing the HAM-PEDES pre-training dataset that yields state-of-the-art ReID performance under both direct-transfer and fine-tuning settings.

## Results

Under the traditional pre-training + fine-tuning protocol, HAM-PEDES (1.0M) pre-trained models achieve new state-of-the-art results on all three benchmarks. With the RDE fine-tuning method, the model reaches R1=77.99 / mAP=69.72 on CUHK-PEDES, R1=69.95 / mAP=42.72 on ICFG-PEDES, and R1=72.50 / mAP=55.47 on RSTPReid. Under the direct transfer setting (no fine-tuning), HAM-PEDES (1.0M) achieves R1=70.15 on CUHK-PEDES, R1=59.63 on ICFG-PEDES, and R1=58.85 on RSTPReid, surpassing all other pre-training datasets by 2.5–9.1% in R1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAM-PEDES consistently outperforms all 18 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the closest pre-training dataset competitor LuPerson-MLLM (Tan et al., CVPR 2024), HAM-PEDES+IRRA gains +3.19% R1 and +1.99% mAP on RSTPReid (71.69 vs 68.50) while using only half the captions per image. Against SYNTH-PEDES (Zuo et al., NeurIPS 2024), the improvement is even larger: +7.34% R1 on RSTPReid (71.69 vs 64.35). The HAM-PEDES+RDE combination surpasses the standalone RDE method (Qin et al., CVPR 2024) by +7.15% R1 on RSTPReid (72.50 vs 65.35) and +2.05% on CUHK-PEDES (77.99 vs 75.94). On CUHK-PEDES, HAM-PEDES+IRRA (77.71 R1) edges out AUL (Li et al., AAAI 2024) at 77.23 R1, and HAM-PEDES+RDE (77.99) sets a new record. The method also outperforms IRRA (Jiang et al., CVPR 2023) by +4.33% R1 on CUHK-PEDES (77.71 vs 73.38) and +11.49% on RSTPReid (71.69 vs 60.20), demonstrating that the pre-training data quality from HAM-PEDES is the primary driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based annotation framework of Tan et al. (CVPR 2024), which used 46 manually designed templates to diversify captions for ReID pre-training, by replacing heuristic templates with learned style prompts that model the description preferences of thousands of human annotators. The style feature space definition follows Tan et al. (TMM 2023), who first formalized style distributions for person ReID. The key conceptual departure is shifting from template-based sentence-pattern diversity to prompt-learning-based wording-style diversity, where each learnable prompt captures the full stylistic fingerprint (including attribute phrasing granularity) of a cluster of human descriptions rather than a fixed sentence skeleton.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HAM-PEDES dataset (1M images, 2 captions each) provides a practical, high-quality pre-training resource that achieves SOTA across CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES, and RSTPReid, and the HAM technique is generalizable to any MLLM annotation task where style diversity matters. The finding that 68K extracted templates yield only marginal gains over 46 templates, while learned style prompts yield 10–15% improvements, reframes the diversity bottleneck from template coverage to style learning. Open questions include whether the approach handles MLLM hallucination (acknowledged limitation), whether the style prompts transfer across MLLM architectures, and whether the gains persist when the ReID model architecture changes substantially.
