## Problem

Existing generative anamorphosis methods are constrained to pixel-space diffusion models and orthogonal pixel transformations (flips, rotations), making them incapable of producing the complex spatial distortions required for anamorphic illusions involving curved mirrors or refractive lenses. Additionally, naively extending these synchronization techniques to latent diffusion or flow models introduces visible VAE artifacts because the encoder-decoder is not equivariant to arbitrary warping. The paper addresses both limitations to enable high-quality, feed-forward generation of anamorphic images with arbitrary 3D optical devices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of text prompts (one per view, e.g., 'a turtle' for the hidden view and 'an Earth' for the main view) and a view transformation defined as a 2-channel UV coordinate map produced by ray-tracing through the target optical device (conic mirror, cylindrical mirror, Nicéron's lens, or 2D transforms). It outputs a single anamorphic RGB image at 1K resolution that reads as one scene when viewed directly and reveals a different scene when observed through the specified device. The UV map encodes, for each output pixel, where to sample in the canonical view, enabling arbitrary non-analytical projections.

## Method

The core pipeline operates on a pretrained latent rectified flow model (Stable Diffusion 3.5) in a training-free manner. At each denoising step, the predicted clean image is obtained via a single Euler step (Tweedie's formula), decoded to pixel space by the VAE, warped to a canonical view using Laplacian Pyramid Warping, averaged across views via Laplacian pyramid blending, warped back, and re-encoded to latent space with a residual correction term to counteract VAE reconstruction drift. Laplacian Pyramid Warping is the key technical contribution: forward warping builds a Gaussian pyramid and samples at a per-pixel LOD level computed from UV-map derivatives (analogous to MIP-mapping), while inverse warping transports pixels into the pyramid via gradient-based back-projection, preserving high-frequency detail under extreme stretching. Additional design choices include time-traveling (repeating intermediate denoising segments for better view consistency) and view prioritization (allocating final timesteps exclusively to one view so its high-frequency details remain coherent while the hidden view stays concealed).

## Results

On the cylindrical mirror anamorphosis task, LookingGlass achieves the best CLIP alignment (A=0.272, A0.9=0.318), concealment (C=0.698, C0.9=0.810), FID (130.27), and KID (0.070) among all compared methods. For the 135° rotation task, it similarly leads on every metric (A=0.295, FID=129.74, KID=0.055), substantially outperforming Geng et al. (FID=293.00) and Tancik (FID=439.35). On the simpler vertical flip task, LookingGlass achieves the lowest FID (124.67) and KID (0.049) but is slightly behind SyncTweedies on concealment (C=0.680 vs. 0.707). Generation takes approximately 80 seconds per image pair on an RTX 4090 with 30 inference steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LookingGlass dominates on the two complex transformation tasks (135° rotation and cylindrical mirror), achieving the best scores on all six metrics simultaneously. On the cylindrical mirror, it improves FID by 8.4 over SyncTweedies (130.27 vs. 138.69) and by 99.4 over Geng et al. (130.27 vs. 285.23), while also leading on CLIP alignment (0.272 vs. 0.261 for Burgert et al.) and concealment (C0.9=0.810 vs. 0.795). On the 135° rotation, the gap is even wider: FID of 129.74 versus 293.00 (Geng et al.) and 439.35 (Tancik), with KID of 0.055 versus 0.254 and 0.545 respectively. On the simpler vertical flip task, LookingGlass achieves the best FID (124.67) and ties Tancik on KID (0.049), but trails SyncTweedies slightly on concealment (C=0.680 vs. 0.707) and Geng et al. on alignment (A=0.297 vs. 0.306), indicating that the advantage of Laplacian Pyramid Warping is most pronounced under non-orthogonal, highly distorting transforms. A 27-participant user study further confirms that LookingGlass is consistently preferred over all four baselines in prompt fidelity, style adherence, and overall visual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Visual Anagrams (Geng et al., 2024), which synchronized diffusion paths across multiple views but was restricted to pixel-space models and orthogonal pixel permutations. It incorporates the SyncTweedies insight of averaging predicted clean images rather than noisy samples, and repurposes classic computer-graphics MIP-mapping / Laplacian pyramid techniques into a novel image-warping operator (LPW) that handles arbitrary non-orthogonal projections. The VAE encode-decode-encode loop with a first-order residual correction is a new contribution addressing the non-equivariance of latent-space VAEs, a problem also observed in Tancik's earlier Stable Diffusion illusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LookingGlass is the first method to generate high-quality anamorphoses (conic mirrors, cylindrical mirrors, Nicéron's lens) in a single feed-forward pass with off-the-shelf latent rectified-flow models, making these historically niche optical illusions accessible to a broad generative-AI audience. The Laplacian Pyramid Warping component is a general-purpose frequency-aware warping primitive with stated potential applications in generative mesh texturing and panorama synthesis beyond illusions. Key open questions remain: prompt selection is still largely trial-and-error, the VAE round-trip adds significant compute overhead relative to Geng et al., and the method has not been validated on 3D-consistent anamorphoses or integrated with conditioning frameworks like ControlNet.
