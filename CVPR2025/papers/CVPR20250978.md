## Problem

In standard diffusion model training, a single parameter-shared denoiser is optimized across all timesteps simultaneously, but gradients at non-adjacent timesteps exhibit low cosine similarity and conflict, causing negative interference that degrades generation quality. Existing remedies—timestep-wise model ensembles incur prohibitive storage overhead (e.g., 6× for OMS-DPM), and loss reweighting strategies only partially alleviate rather than eliminate the gradient conflicts.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a pretrained diffusion model (DDPM U-Net for unconditional generation or Stable Diffusion for text-to-image). During finetuning, each of the N model copies receives noisy latents x_t sampled only from its assigned timestep sub-range (with a small probability p of sampling from the full range). At inference, the final merged model accepts the same inputs as the original—random Gaussian noise (plus a text prompt for text-to-image)—and produces a single generated image with identical computational and memory costs to the unmodified pretrained model.

## Method

DeMe divides the full timestep range [0, T) into N contiguous, non-overlapping sub-ranges and finetunes N independent copies of a pretrained diffusion model, each restricted to its own sub-range, thereby eliminating cross-timestep gradient accumulation. Three auxiliary techniques preserve knowledge sharing during finetuning: a consistency loss that penalizes deviation from the pretrained model's outputs, a probabilistic sampling strategy that occasionally draws timesteps outside the assigned range, and a lightweight channel-wise projection layer (a learnable C×C matrix, ~1% of model parameters) that explicitly captures channel-dimension feature shifts. After finetuning, all N models are merged into a single model via task arithmetic: the weighted sum of task vectors (θ_i − θ) is added to the pretrained parameters, yielding one unified model that retains the specialized knowledge of each timestep range without any additional inference cost.

## Results

On unconditional generation with DDPM, DeMe (After Merge) achieves FID of 3.51 on CIFAR-10, 7.27 on LSUN-Church, and 5.84 on LSUN-Bedroom, reducing FID by 0.91, 3.42, and 0.62 respectively over the pretrained baseline. On text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion, DeMe (After Merge) improves FID by 0.36 on MS-COCO (13.06 vs 13.42) and 0.39 on ImageNet (27.23 vs 27.62), while increasing CLIP score by 0.23 on MS-COCO and 0.20 on PartiPrompts. All experiments use only 20K×4 finetuning iterations (80K total), matching the iteration budget of baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeMe decisively outperforms all four loss-reweighting baselines (SNR+1, Trun-SNR, Min-SNR-γ, P2 Weighting) on every unconditional benchmark; in fact, these methods degrade FID relative to the pretrained model (e.g., Min-SNR-γ worsens CIFAR-10 from 4.42 to 5.77). The MTL-based ANT methods (NashMTL and UW) achieve modest improvements (FID 4.21–4.24 on CIFAR-10) but are still 0.27–0.30 FID worse than DeMe (After Merge) at 3.51. On text-to-image generation, DeMe (After Merge) surpasses all baselines on CLIP score for MS-COCO (+0.23) and PartiPrompts (+0.20), while the best baseline (ANT-UW) only reaches CLIP 29.94 on MS-COCO. The model merging step provides an additional 0.28 FID gain on CIFAR-10 and a dramatic 2.30 FID gain on LSUN-Church over the ensemble (Before Merge) variant, confirming that parameter-space merging captures complementary knowledge across timestep ranges without extra inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeMe sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the multi-task learning perspective on diffusion training, where prior work (ANT [10], DTR [34], Switch-DiT [35]) has identified negative transfer across timesteps and proposed loss reweighting or architectural routing to mitigate it, and (2) parameter-space model merging (task arithmetic [17], model soups [54]), which has been applied to combine models finetuned on different datasets or tasks but not previously to diffusion models across timesteps. The key departure from prior work is the decouple-then-merge pipeline: rather than reweighting a shared loss or routing to expert sub-networks, DeMe finetunes separate models on non-overlapping timestep ranges and then linearly combines their task vectors, inheriting the zero-overhead inference property of merging while avoiding the storage cost of timestep-wise ensembles (EDM [1], OMS-DPM [28]).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: the merged model incurs no additional computation, storage, or memory cost at inference, making it a drop-in replacement for any pretrained diffusion model. The 3.42 FID reduction on LSUN-Church and consistent gains across six benchmarks demonstrate that gradient conflicts across timesteps are a real and exploitable bottleneck. However, the text-to-image improvements are more modest (0.36 FID on COCO after merge), the merging weights require grid search rather than being learned, and the experiments use relatively short finetuning budgets (20K × 4 iterations). Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models (e.g., SDXL, DiT-based architectures), whether the merging weights can be optimized end-to-end, and whether the decouple-then-merge paradigm generalizes to other multi-task settings beyond diffusion timesteps.
