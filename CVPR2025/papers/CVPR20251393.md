## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) for 3D generation requires thousands of diffusion model inferences across diverse camera angles and denoising timesteps, resulting in optimization times of hours per object. While feature redundancy across nearby timesteps has been exploited in 2D diffusion acceleration, the spatial redundancy across nearby camera poses in 3D generation remains unexploited, and existing 2D caching methods cannot handle the unordered, stochastic sampling process inherent to SDS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251393/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Hash3D is a plug-and-play acceleration layer that wraps around existing SDS-based pipelines. It takes the same inputs as the underlying method (a text prompt for text-to-3D or a single 256×256 reference image for image-to-3D) and produces the same 3D outputs (Gaussian splatting or NeRF representations). Internally, it maintains a hash table storing intermediate U-Net features (the input to the last up-sampling layer), noisy latent inputs, camera poses, and timesteps, with a configurable retrieval probability η controlling when to reuse versus recompute.

## Method

Hash3D exploits the observation that diffusion features from nearby camera poses and timesteps are highly similar (cosine similarity >0.8 within ±10°). It maps each (θ, φ, ρ, t) key to a grid cell via floor division by adaptive grid sizes, then hashes the cell indices into a table using large prime multipliers. On retrieval, features from the matched bucket are combined via distance-weighted averaging in latent space; on update, the extracted U-Net feature is appended to the bucket's queue (max length 3, oldest evicted). An adaptive grid mechanism maintains M=3 candidate grid sizes in parallel, tracking running cosine similarity per bucket, and selects the grid with highest average similarity for each query. This design requires no model training, uses constant memory, and is orthogonal to the specific 3D representation or diffusion backbone.

## Results

Hash3D achieves 1.5×–4× speedup across all tested 3D generation methods while maintaining or slightly improving visual quality. On text-to-3D (Table 2), speedups range from 1.5× (DreamFusion: 60m→40m, GaussianDreamer: 15m→10m) to 1.9× (SDS+GS: 78m→40m), with CLIP-G/14 scores remaining within ±0.01 of baselines. On image-to-3D (Table 1), DreamGaussian+Hash3D achieves a 4× speedup (reducing generation to ~2 min) and Zero-123(NeRF) achieves 3× (20 min), while Magic123+Hash3D reaches 1.6× (120m→74m). The user study confirms Hash3D is preferred over baselines with scores of 52.33/100 (vs. Zero-123 NeRF) and 56.29/100 (vs. GaussianDreamer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hash3D consistently wins on speed across all seven baselines, with the largest gain being 4× on DreamGaussian (image-to-3D) and 1.9× on SDS+GS (text-to-3D). On quality metrics, Hash3D slightly improves CLIP-G/14 for DreamFusion (0.407→0.411) and GaussianDreamer (0.412→0.416), ties Latent-NeRF (0.406→0.406), and shows minor drops for Magic3D (0.399→0.393) and SDS+GS (0.413→0.402). For image-to-3D, Zero-123(NeRF)+Hash3D achieves 3× speedup with PSNR 17.773 and CLIP-G/14 0.662, while Magic123+Hash3D reaches 1.6× speedup with the best PSNR (18.718) and CLIP-G/14 (0.718) among image-to-3D methods. The user study (44 participants) confirms Hash3D is marginally preferred over both Zero-123(NeRF) and GaussianDreamer, supporting the claim that feature sharing improves cross-view consistency. Overall, Hash3D trades negligible quality loss (≤0.01 CLIP) for substantial speed gains, with the strongest results on 3D Gaussian Splatting-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hash3D sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) feature-reuse acceleration for 2D diffusion models (DeepCache, Learning-to-Cache, Faster Diffusion), which assume an ordered denoising schedule, and (2) grid-based spatial hashing from 3D representation work (Instant-NGP, voxel hashing). The key conceptual departure is adapting ordered feature caching to the inherently unordered, stochastic sampling process of Score Distillation Sampling, where camera poses and timesteps are sampled randomly across thousands of iterations. By employing a hash table with adaptive grid sizing, Hash3D bridges these two ideas into a training-free, plug-and-play module that can be dropped into any diffusion-based 3D generation pipeline without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing text-to-3D generation to ~10 minutes and image-to-3D to ~30 seconds (with Gaussian Splatting) makes diffusion-based 3D creation feasible for interactive or near-real-time workflows. The finding that feature sharing across nearby views *improves* smoothness and view consistency—rather than merely preserving quality at lower cost—suggests a useful regularization effect that could inform future multi-view consistency strategies. Open questions include whether the approach extends to newer diffusion backbones (e.g., DiT-based models), whether the brute-force adaptive grid selection scales to more complex camera parameterizations, and how Hash3D interacts with other acceleration techniques like distillation or step reduction.
