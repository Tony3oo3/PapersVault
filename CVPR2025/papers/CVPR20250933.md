## Problem

Reconstruction-based street scene methods (3DGS, NeRF) produce severe artifacts when viewpoints deviate from the training trajectory due to insufficient observation in under-observed regions. Meanwhile, video diffusion models can generate photorealistic novel views but rely on high-level text prompts that lack the fine-grained camera controllability required for autonomous driving simulation. StreetCrafter bridges this gap by providing pixel-level geometric control to a video diffusion model for precise, controllable street view synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250933/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated RGB images (1066×1600 on Waymo, 900×1600 on PandaSet), synchronized LiDAR point clouds, and object tracklets from a single driving trajectory, along with a user-specified novel camera trajectory. LiDAR points are colorized from adjacent frames, separated into background and per-object clouds via tracklets, aggregated over a ±1 s temporal window, and rendered with a fixed NDC-space radius to produce pixel-level condition images. Outputs include 25-frame photorealistic video clips at 576×1024 (Ours-V, ~0.16 FPS), a distilled dynamic 3DGS representation enabling real-time rendering at ~113 FPS (Ours-G), and edited scenes (object removal, replacement, translation) obtained by modifying LiDAR point attributes without per-scene optimization.

## Method

StreetCrafter initializes from Vista (a driving world model finetuned from Stable Video Diffusion) and injects colorized LiDAR renderings as pixel-level conditions into the first U-Net layer via a trainable zero convolutional layer, so the network only needs to recover clean images from noisy geometric guidance rather than learn camera-to-video mapping from scratch. A reference image CLIP embedding provides appearance conditioning, and the model is trained with 15% random dropout of either the reference or LiDAR condition. For real-time rendering, StreetCrafter is distilled into a dynamic 3DGS (following the Street Gaussians scene-graph decomposition) by generating novel-view supervision from noisy rendered latents via DDIM sampling rather than pure noise, with a progressive noise-scale schedule that shifts reliance from the diffusion prior to 3D structure over training. The distillation loss combines L1, SSIM, and LPIPS for input views with LPIPS-only for novel views, plus LiDAR depth, sky mask, and object regularization terms, achieving state-of-the-art FID on lane-shift extrapolation while maintaining real-time throughput.

## Results

On Waymo, Ours-G (the distilled 3DGS) achieves the best LPIPS (0.054) and FID for lane-shift extrapolation (58.17 @2m, 71.40 @3m) while running at 113 FPS, compared to Ours-V (the raw diffusion model) at 0.16 FPS. On PandaSet, Ours-G similarly leads in LPIPS (0.062) and FID (62.15 @2m, 78.88 @3m) at 80.6 FPS. On interpolation PSNR, Street Gaussians slightly edges out Ours-G on Waymo (30.95 vs. 30.05), but Ours-G surpasses all baselines on perceptual and distributional metrics for view extrapolation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Street Gaussians (the strongest baseline), Ours-G wins on LPIPS (0.054 vs. 0.130 on Waymo; 0.062 vs. 0.109 on PandaSet) and FID at both 2 m and 3 m shifts, while Street Gaussians retains a small PSNR advantage on Waymo interpolation (30.95 vs. 30.05). Against 3DGS, Ours-G dominates on all quality metrics (e.g., FID@2m 58.17 vs. 100.50 on Waymo) and is competitive on speed (113 vs. 213 FPS). Against EmerNeRF, UniSim, and NeuRAD, Ours-G achieves the best LPIPS and FID on both datasets while running 10–13× faster (e.g., 80.6 FPS vs. 6.0–6.4 FPS for UniSim/NeuRAD on PandaSet). The only metric where Ours-G trails a baseline is interpolation PSNR on Waymo, where Street Gaussians leads by ~0.9 dB; on PandaSet, NeuRAD edges out Ours-G on PSNR by 0.32 dB. Overall, the distilled 3DGS representation combines the generative quality of the diffusion prior with real-time rendering, outperforming all reconstruction-only baselines on extrapolation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreetCrafter builds directly on Vista (a driving world model finetuned from Stable Video Diffusion) as its backbone, and adopts the zero-convolutional-layer conditioning mechanism from ControlNet to inject LiDAR renderings into the U-Net. It departs from prior camera-controlled video diffusion methods (which use Plücker embeddings or pose vectors) by replacing these high-level pose signals with pixel-level LiDAR point cloud renderings, providing geometrically grounded guidance that is better suited to complex multi-object street scenes. The distillation stage extends the Street Gaussians dynamic 3DGS formulation by using diffusion-generated novel views as additional supervision, a combination not previously explored for driving scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical pipeline for generating corner-case driving data with precise camera control and real-time rendering, which is critical for closed-loop autonomous driving evaluation. The ability to perform scene editing (object removal, replacement, translation) without per-scene optimization by simply manipulating LiDAR points is a notable practical advantage over reconstruction-based methods. Key limitations include the dependency on LiDAR and object tracklets (costly to collect), the slow inference of StreetCrafter itself (~0.2 fps), and the fact that the distillation only handles a fixed lateral shift trajectory rather than arbitrary novel paths. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to sensor suites without LiDAR and whether faster diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based) can close the real-time gap.
