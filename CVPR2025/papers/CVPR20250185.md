## Problem

Estimating 3D gaze direction in unconstrained settings—where the subject is distant, facing away, or captured under diverse camera poses—remains challenging because prior methods either rely solely on 2D appearance or incorporate 3D spatial cues only through non-learnable post-processing. The spatial relationships between the subject, scene objects, and gaze direction are not modeled end-to-end, leaving a gap in how 3D context is learned for gaze estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a depth map (from a sensor or zero-shot estimator like Zoedepth), known camera intrinsics, and 2D bounding boxes for the head and full-body as input, and outputs a 3D gaze direction as a unit vector on S² in camera space. Pre-trained frozen modules (3D pose estimator, view direction estimator, MobileSAM) extract intermediate 3D representations from the raw inputs, so the trainable network operates on 3D keypoints and object positions rather than raw pixels.

## Method

The core contribution is GA3CE, which encodes 3D context (body pose keypoints and object positions) in a gaze-aware manner through two mechanisms. First, an egocentric transformation normalizes all 3D positions relative to the subject's head and applies a cyclotorsion rotation that aligns the subject's view direction with a fixed z-axis while keeping the image horizon horizontal, reducing camera-pose variation into a unified subject-centric space. Second, a D³ (direction-distance-decomposed) positional encoding represents each 3D point as the concatenation of sinusoidal encodings of its unit direction and its distance from the head, explicitly capturing directional and radial similarities. A transformer encoder processes object embeddings while a decoder uses the subject embedding (view direction + pose) as query to attend over object features, producing a residual gaze direction in egocentric space that is rotated back to camera space. The network is trained end-to-end with an angular error loss, with all upstream 3D perception modules kept frozen.

## Results

On the GFIE dataset, the proposed method achieves 11.1° 3D MAE without 2D gaze-following, a 37% improvement over the leading baseline GFIE (17.7°). On the GAFA dataset, it achieves 19.9° 3D MAE and 20.6° 2D MAE in the single-frame setting, outperforming GAFA (22.9°/22.1°) by 13% and 7% respectively. On the CAD-120 generalization test, the method reduces 3D MAE from 27.3° (GFIE_head) to 25.2° without GFM, and from 19.8° to 15.8° with GFM. When combined with the gaze-following modules (Ours+GFM), it further improves to 10.6° 3D MAE and 0.260 3D distance on GFIE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 7+ baselines across three datasets. On GFIE, the method (11.1° 3D MAE) outperforms the primary baseline GFIE [15] (17.7°) by 37%, and also beats head-appearance methods Gaze360 [17] (19.8°) and Rt-Gene [9] (21.0°) by large margins, as well as 2D gaze-following methods Chong [5] (20.8°) and Lian [21] (26.7°). Notably, GFIE+3D (16.4°), which adds the same 3D pose and object inputs to GFIE without GA3CE, still trails the proposed method by 5.3°, confirming that the encoding strategy—not just the 3D inputs—drives the gain. On GAFA, the method (19.9°) beats the temporal baseline GAFA_temporal [26] (21.7°) and single-frame GAFA [26] (22.9°), as well as XGaze [41] (29.2°) and Gaze360 [17] (30.4°). On CAD-120 (generalization), the method outperforms GFIE_head [15] (27.3° → 25.2°) and GFIE+GFM [15] (19.8° → 15.8° with GFM). The method does not report AUC/L2/3D distance for the no-GFM setting, so direct comparison on those metrics is only available for Ours+GFM, where it leads on AUC (0.987) and 3D distance (0.260) but is slightly behind GFIE+3D on L2 distance (0.067 vs 0.062).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in unconstrained 3D gaze estimation, where prior methods (GFIE [15], GAFA [26]) estimate gaze from 2D appearance cues and incorporate 3D spatial information only through non-learnable post-processing (e.g., depth-based directional constraints). Rather than extending these pipelines, the authors introduce a fundamentally different formulation: representing the subject and scene as explicit 3D context (pose keypoints + object positions) and learning spatial relationships in 3D space via a transformer. The egocentric normalization and D³ positional encoding are novel encoding strategies inspired by human vision literature, repurposing the transformer architecture for a task where it had not previously been applied in this manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that learnable 3D spatial reasoning substantially outperforms the analytical post-processing approach that has been the standard in unconstrained 3D gaze estimation, with the ablation showing that the encoding design (GA3CE) matters far more than simply feeding 3D data into the network. Limitations include reliance on frozen pre-trained components (3D pose estimator, MobileSAM) without object semantic features, single-frame operation, and evaluation limited to indoor scenes. Open questions include whether incorporating category-agnostic semantic features (e.g., CLIP) would further improve performance, and whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or highly dynamic scenes where object positions are less stable.
