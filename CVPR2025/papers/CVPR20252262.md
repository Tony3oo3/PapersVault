## Problem

Existing collaborative perception research and evaluations assume ideal conditions—clear weather and fully functional sensors—yet real-world deployment inevitably encounters adverse weather, sensor failures, and temporal misalignments. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates how collaborative detection models perform under diverse camera corruptions, nor does it isolate whether collaboration helps or hurts when only the ego vehicle or only the collaborating vehicles are affected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252262/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes multi-camera RGB images from the ego vehicle and one or more collaborating CAVs, with 14 corruption types (5 weather, 3 noise, 3 blur, 1 crash, 1 quantization, 1 temporal misalignment) applied at 5 severity levels, yielding 70 unique conditions per dataset. Evaluation is structured around three interference scenarios—Global (both ego and CAVs corrupted), Ego (only ego corrupted), and CAV (only CAVs corrupted)—and outputs 3D object detection AP at IoU 0.3/0.5/0.7, supplemented by four new robustness metrics (AP_cor, mRCE, mPosC, mNegC) that quantify absolute robustness, relative degradation, collaborative compensation, and collaborative disruption respectively.

## Method

RCP-Bench introduces three corrupted datasets (OPV2V-C, V2XSet-C, DAIR-V2X-C) and evaluates 10 leading collaborative perception models across six cases including single/multiple/heterogeneous corruptions and unseen scenes. To improve robustness, the paper proposes two training-time strategies: RCP-Drop, which randomly discards features from selected CAVs during training using a dynamic dropout schedule p_drop = 1.0 − (t/T)² to prevent over-reliance on collaborative data, and RCP-Mix, which probabilistically blends feature map statistics (mean and standard deviation) between the ego vehicle and CAVs via a Beta-distributed weight, inspired by MixStyle but adapted to the multi-agent setting. The paper further identifies that backbone architecture (EfficientNet-b0 over ResNet101), multiscale feature fusion, camera count, and number of collaborating vehicles are critical factors governing robustness, and shows that simple Max fusion outperforms attention-based fusion under many corruption types.

## Results

On OPV2V-C (Global Interference, AP@0.5), the best-performing method Late achieves AP_cor of 26.62, followed by V2X-ViT (22.21) and Max (19.40), while the NoFusion baseline drops to 12.44. Under Ego Interference, Late achieves the highest collaborative compensation (mPosC = 50.64) and the lowest collaborative disruption (mNegC = 95.22), substantially outperforming learned fusion methods like AttFuse (mPosC = 16.97, mNegC = 121.59). The proposed RCP-Drop and RCP-Mix strategies boost CoBEVT's AP_cor from 15.91 to 31.83 (+BN+RCP-Mix) and AttFuse's from 15.99 to 28.85 (+BN+RCP-Mix), roughly doubling robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six learned collaborative perception models, V2X-ViT achieves the highest AP_cor (22.21) and the lowest mNegC (101.27) among learned methods, making it the most robust learned model under Global and CAV Interference. However, the simple Late fusion baseline (AP_cor = 26.62, mPosC = 50.64, mNegC = 95.22) outperforms all learned methods on every robustness metric, indicating that complex intermediate feature fusion (attention-based AttFuse, graph-based DiscoNet/V2VNet) is less resilient than fusing final predictions. The Max fusion baseline also surpasses several learned models (e.g., AttFuse, F-Cooper, V2VNet) in AP_cor, suggesting that model complexity does not translate to corruption robustness. AttFuse and F-Cooper are the most vulnerable, with mNegC values exceeding 118%, meaning corrupted CAV data degrades ego performance by more than the clean baseline gap. The proposed RCP-Mix strategy provides the largest robustness gains, improving CoBEVT's AP_cor by +15.92 points and AttFuse's by +12.86 points over their respective BN-adapted baselines, while also reducing mNegC by 2–7 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the corruption-robustness benchmarking lineage (ImageNet-C, RoboBEV, RoboDepth) from single-vehicle perception to the multi-agent collaborative setting, where corruptions can propagate across interconnected vehicles. It builds directly on the collaborative perception frameworks (AttFuse, V2X-ViT, CoBEVT, V2VNet, DiscoNet) and their underlying datasets (OPV2V, V2XSet, DAIR-V2X), but departs from prior robustness-in-collaboration work that focused narrowly on communication-layer issues (delays, interruptions, pose errors) by systematically addressing sensor-level and environmental corruptions. The RCP-Mix strategy adapts MixStyle's feature-statistic mixing from domain generalization to the ego-CAV feature interaction, while RCP-Drop repurposes dropout from intra-network regularization to inter-vehicle data dropping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first comprehensive benchmark for collaborative perception robustness, RCP-Bench provides the field with a standardized evaluation protocol (14 corruption types × 5 severities × 3 interference scenarios) that was previously absent, making it likely to become a default stress-test for new collaborative methods. The finding that simple Max/Late fusion outperforms complex attention- and graph-based fusion under corruption challenges the prevailing assumption that more sophisticated fusion mechanisms are universally superior, suggesting a trade-off between clean-condition performance and corruption resilience. Open questions include whether the camera-only corruption scope generalizes to LiDAR or multi-modal settings, whether RCP-Drop/RCP-Mix transfer to other collaborative tasks (segmentation, occupancy), and how these strategies interact with communication-bandwidth constraints in real deployments.
