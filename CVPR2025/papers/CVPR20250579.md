## Problem

Determining globally consistent normal orientation for 3D point clouds remains challenging because initial normal estimates are inherently local and unoriented, while existing propagation-based methods accumulate errors, volume-based methods are computationally expensive and fail on thin structures, and learning-based methods require GPU resources and extensive training data. The paper addresses the gap of providing a single global, non-iterative algorithm that is simultaneously robust to noise, outliers, holes, and complex topology (high genus, thin structures) without requiring learned components.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense 3D point cloud (1k–300k points) with associated unoriented normals as input, assuming the underlying surface is closed so that space is partitioned into inner and outer regions. It produces globally consistent oriented normals (flipped or not per point) as output. The method is entirely non-learning-based and runs on CPU, with a single user-specified ratio α (< 1) controlling the level-set thickness; no training data, GPU, or iterative refinement is required.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) estimating an extended point-based shape diameter via cone-cast ray queries along both normal directions to capture local thickness and separation; (2) constructing a Delaunay graph by triangulating the level set of a Gaussian-weighted unsigned implicit function at a level set 0.5α·δ_min, where δ_min is the minimum shape diameter, yielding inner and outer shells that separate thin structures; (3) solving a relaxed sign field on the Delaunay graph by formulating the binary orientation as a least-squares problem with edge constraints derived from surface-intersection indicators, constrained to +1 on convex-hull vertices, and solved via KKT conditions as a sparse linear system. The gradient of the resulting piecewise-linear sign field (evaluated via barycentric interpolation within each tetrahedral cell) is then used to flip each unoriented normal so it points from inside to outside. This formulation avoids the greediness of propagation, the density of volumetric solvers, and the data/GPU requirements of learning-based approaches.

## Results

The method is evaluated using NION (Number of Incorrect Oriented Normals, lower is better) across 15 shapes (1k–300k points) under clean, 0.25% Gaussian noise, and 5% outlier conditions. On clean data, the method achieves 0 NION on 8 of 15 shapes and single-digit NION on most others (e.g., 2 on fandisk, 7 on feline, 11 on metatron), while GCNO exceeds 12 hours on all shapes above 25k points. Under 5% random outliers, the method maintains 0–861 NION across all shapes, whereas MST degrades to 547–119k and GCNO still exceeds 12 hours on large shapes. Running time ranges from 0.4 s (torus) to 436.6 s (dragon), compared to GCNO's >12 h timeout on 9 of 15 shapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against propagation-based baselines, MST and SNO are fast but fail catastrophically on holes (bird: 2.1k/3.6k NION vs. Ours 3), thin structures (bull: 989/1.8k vs. 15), and complex topology (honeycomb: 58k/519 vs. 61); they also cannot distinguish inward vs. outward orientation. GCNO achieves near-zero NION on small shapes but times out (>12 h) on all shapes above 25k points, making it impractical for large-scale use. Among learning-based methods, Dipole is robust on simple shapes but degrades severely on complex geometry (honeycomb: 62k, filigree: 108k NION) and holes (bird: 2.1k). Supervised methods PCPNet, DPGO, and SHSNet struggle with complex structures (metatron: 24k/25k/14k) and holes (bird: 326/399/252), while unsupervised NGL and NeuralGF (which require per-shape training) achieve competitive results on simple shapes but still lag on complex ones (honeycomb: 4482/4136 vs. Ours 61). The proposed method consistently achieves the lowest or near-lowest NION across all 15 shapes in under 437 s, without requiring GPU resources or per-shape training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established threads: shape diameter estimation (Rolland-Neviere 2013, Shapira 2008, and the extended point-based variant from Fu et al. 2024), Delaunay-based implicit surface meshing (CGALMesh), and least-squares sign-field formulation. Conceptually, it departs from propagation-based methods (MST, SNO) that suffer from cumulative greedy errors, and from volumetric iterative solvers (GCNO, iPSR) that are computationally expensive on large or thin-structured inputs. The key reframe is treating the binary inside/outside sign assignment as a relaxed sparse linear system on a shape-diameter-guided Delaunay graph, rather than as a graph propagation or a PDE solve, thereby inheriting the topological awareness of volume-based methods with the speed of a single linear solve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, GPU-free pipeline that orients normals in seconds (vs. hours for GCNO on large shapes) while remaining robust to noise, outliers, holes, and thin structures—conditions where both propagation and learning-based baselines degrade sharply. This makes it attractive for industrial scanning pipelines and real-time applications where iterative volumetric solvers or trained networks are impractical. Limitations include the assumption of a closed manifold surface, the need for pre-estimated unoriented normals, and a user-tunable ratio α that controls shell thickness; open questions include extension to open surfaces, very sparse point clouds, and whether the shape-diameter prior can be replaced by learned thickness estimates to reduce sensitivity to local noise.
