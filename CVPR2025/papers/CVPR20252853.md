## Problem

Current lightweight Mamba-based visual models report low FLOPs but exhibit suboptimal GPU throughput, and FLOPs alone do not predict actual inference speed. CNN-based models are limited by local receptive fields, while Transformer-based models suffer from quadratic complexity at high resolutions. The paper addresses the gap of designing a Mamba-based lightweight network that simultaneously achieves high accuracy, fast real-world throughput, and strong performance on high-resolution downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at resolutions scaled to model size: 192×192 for tiny variants (T2, T4), 224×224 for small (S6), 256×256 for base (B1), and 384×384 or 512×512 for larger base variants (B2, B4). For classification it outputs a single ImageNet-1K class label; for downstream tasks it produces bounding boxes (SSDLite, RetinaNet on COCO), instance segmentation masks (Mask R-CNN), and pixel-wise semantic segmentation maps (DeepLabv3, PSPNet, Semantic FPN on ADE20K at 512×512). The resolution-scaling strategy is a deliberate design choice: smaller models use lower input resolution to reduce FLOPs while maintaining throughput, and larger models increase resolution rather than depth/width to improve accuracy.

## Method

MobileMamba employs a three-stage network (instead of the conventional four-stage) to reduce feature map sizes and boost throughput while maintaining accuracy. The core building block is the Multi-Receptive Field Feature Interaction (MRFFI) module, which splits input channels into three parts: (1) a Long-Range Wavelet Transform-Enhanced Mamba (WTE-Mamba) branch that performs bidirectional selective scan for global modeling while a parallel Haar wavelet transform + convolution + inverse wavelet path extracts high-frequency edge details at an effectively doubled receptive field; (2) a Multi-Kernel Depthwise Convolution (MK-DeConv) branch capturing multi-scale local receptive fields; and (3) an Identity mapping on remaining channels to eliminate redundancy in high-dimensional space. Training strategies include Knowledge Distillation from a TResNet-L teacher and extended training to 1000 epochs, while a Normalization Layer Fusion strategy at test time further reduces inference latency. The Mamba component uses bidirectional scanning with expanding ratio 2 and d_state=1, chosen via ablation to maximize throughput without sacrificing accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MobileMamba achieves Top-1 accuracies of 76.9/78.9/80.7/82.2/83.3/83.6% across six model scales (255M–4313M FLOPs) with training strategies, surpassing all CNN, Transformer, and Mamba-based baselines at comparable FLOPs. On downstream tasks, MobileMamba-B1 achieves 40.6 mAP^b and 37.4 mAP^m with Mask R-CNN on COCO (+1.3/+1.0 over EMO-5M), 39.6 mAP^b with RetinaNet (+2.1 over EfficientVMamba-T), and 29.5 mAP with SSDLite at 512×512. For semantic segmentation on ADE20K at 512×512, MobileMamba-B4 reaches 42.5 mIoU with Semantic FPN using only 22% of EMO-5M's FLOPs, and 36.9 mIoU with PSPNet using only 11% of MobileViTv2-1.0's FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileMamba dominates across all FLOPs ranges on ImageNet-1K: at ~255M FLOPs, MobileMamba-T2† (76.9%) outperforms SHViT-S1 (72.8%) by +4.1 and EMO-1M (71.5%) by +5.4; at ~652M FLOPs, MobileMamba-S6† (80.7%) beats EfficientVMamba-T (76.5%) by +4.2 and EfficientViT-M5 (77.1%) by +3.6; at ~1080M FLOPs, MobileMamba-B1† (82.2%) surpasses EMO-6M (79.0%) by +3.2 and SHViT-S4 (79.4%) by +2.8. In terms of speed, MobileMamba-S6 achieves 11000 img/s GPU throughput vs. 6285 for EfficientVMamba-T (×1.75 faster) and 1003 for LocalVim-T (×11 faster), while also improving Top-1 by +4.2 and +4.5 respectively. On downstream tasks, MobileMamba-B1 with Mask R-CNN achieves 40.6 mAP^b vs. EMO-5M's 39.3 (+1.3) and SHViT-S4's 39.0 (+1.6), with 57% higher throughput than EMO-5M. For semantic segmentation, MobileMamba-B4 matches PVTv2-B1's 42.5 mIoU on Semantic FPN while using only 16% of its FLOPs (5.6G vs. 34.2G), and outperforms MobileViTv2-1.0 by +0.4 mIoU on PSPNet with only 11% of its FLOPs. The paper acknowledges that on CPU and mobile latency, MobileMamba still lags behind Transformer-based models due to immature Mamba engineering implementations, though it remains 15–42% faster than EfficientVMamba on CPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

MobileMamba extends the line of lightweight Mamba-based visual models (Vim, VMamba, EfficientVMamba, LocalVim) by addressing their poor GPU throughput despite low FLOPs, and derives its three-stage backbone from EfficientViT's cascaded group attention architecture. The key architectural departure is the MRFFI module, which splits channels into three paths—wavelet-transform-enhanced bidirectional Mamba for global and high-frequency features, multi-kernel depthwise convolution for multi-scale local receptive fields, and identity mapping to reduce redundancy—rather than relying on a single scanning strategy. The paper also positions itself against the broader CNN-based (MobileNet, EdgeNeXt, FasterNet) and Transformer-based (SHViT, MobileViTv3) lightweight model families, arguing that state-space models can finally match or exceed them in both accuracy and actual inference speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that FLOPs alone are a misleading efficiency metric for Mamba-based models, and that architectural choices (three-stage vs. four-stage, scanning direction, d_state) can yield order-of-magnitude throughput differences at similar FLOPs—a practical insight for anyone deploying SSM-based backbones. The ×21 speedup over LocalVim and consistent gains across detection, segmentation, and classification make MobileMamba a strong candidate for mobile and edge deployment. However, the authors acknowledge that CPU and mobile-device latency still lag behind Transformer-based models due to immature Mamba engineering, leaving open the question of whether SSM-based backbones can truly compete on resource-constrained hardware without further systems-level optimization.
