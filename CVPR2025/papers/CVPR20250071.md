## Problem

Existing image deblurring methods process rotational and translational motion components jointly, typically within Cartesian-coordinate attention windows that model motion along orthogonal directions. Since real-world motion blur is often irregular and rotational motion is inherently non-linear, this unified treatment leads to linearization errors and insufficient capture of rotational blur patterns, particularly in complex motion scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250071/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurry RGB image of resolution H×W×3 and produces a deblurred sharp image via residual learning, where a 3×3 convolution generates a residual R added to the input (X' = X + R). Training uses 128×128 patches with a 3-level encoder-decoder architecture; the model is trained on GoPro, REDS, or RSBlur and evaluated zero-shot or fine-tuned on six datasets including real-world benchmarks (RealBlur, RSBlur, RWBI) where no ground truth is available for RWBI.

## Method

MDT decomposes motion blur into translational and rotational components using a dual-branch Motion Decomposition Module (MDM) that applies deformable convolution (DCNv2) with Cartesian offsets in one branch and polar-coordinate offsets (derived via arctan of the spatial grid) in the other, summing the two feature maps. The decoder employs a Radial Stripe Attention Solver (RSAS) that performs self-attention within radial stripe windows (M×M along angles, 1 along radius) in polar coordinates, paired with an Angular Relative Positional Encoding (ARPE) that parameterizes token relationships via relative angle Δφ and relative distance Δr rather than Cartesian Δx/Δy. The overall architecture is a U-shaped encoder-decoder with 3 levels (6, 6, 12 Transformer blocks), using FFTformer's FFN in the encoder and RSAS in the decoder, with azimuth patch merging for downsampling. This design avoids the information loss from radial partitioning and sampling used in prior polar-coordinate methods, preserving fine motion details needed for deblurring while keeping computational cost (14.3M params, 112.61 GMACs) lower than dual-coordinate alternatives.

## Results

On GoPro, MDT achieves 34.26 dB PSNR and 0.969 SSIM, outperforming the previous best FFTformer (34.21 dB) and Stripformer (33.08 dB) by 0.05 dB and 1.18 dB respectively, while using only 14.3M parameters. On HIDE, MDT reaches 31.84 dB PSNR and 0.948 SSIM, surpassing UFPNet (31.74 dB) and FFTformer (31.62 dB). Across RealBlur, RSBlur, REDS-val-300, and RWBI, MDT consistently achieves the best or near-best results, with a 0.40 dB PSNR gain over Uformer on RSBlur and the lowest NIQE score (4.929) on the no-reference RWBI benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDT outperforms all 14 compared methods on both GoPro and HIDE. Against the closest competitor FFTformer (34.21 dB on GoPro), MDT gains 0.05 dB PSNR while using 2.3M fewer parameters (14.3M vs. 16.6M). The gain over Stripformer is more substantial: +1.18 dB on GoPro and +0.81 dB on HIDE, which the authors attribute to the polar-coordinate design better capturing rotational blur that Cartesian strip windows miss. On RealBlur, MDT trained solely on GoPro surpasses all methods on average (30.36 dB), demonstrating strong generalization. On RSBlur, MDT leads Uformer by 0.40 dB PSNR with 36.6M fewer parameters (14.3M vs. 50.9M). On REDS-val-300, MDT achieves 29.25 dB, beating NAFNet (29.09 dB) and HINet (28.83 dB) with the fewest parameters (14.3M vs. 67.9M and 88.7M). On the no-reference RWBI benchmark, MDT attains the lowest NIQE score (4.929) versus FFTformer (5.188) and FPro (5.154), confirming superior perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based image deblurring lineage (SwinIR, Uformer, Stripformer, FFTformer) by replacing the Cartesian coordinate system with a polar one, motivated by the observation that motion blur decomposes naturally into translational and rotational components. The Motion Decomposition Module repurposes DCNv2 deformable convolution in two coordinate systems (Cartesian for translation, polar for rotation), while the Radial Stripe Attention Solver adapts the stripe-window concept from Stripformer into angular-radial windows with a novel angular relative positional encoding. The FFN and training protocol are inherited from FFTformer, and the bias matrix parameterization follows Swin Transformer, but the core architectural departure is the polar-coordinate attention mechanism designed specifically to model rotational blur trajectories that Cartesian windows cannot capture efficiently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in deblurring research: existing methods treat rotational and translational motion jointly in Cartesian space, leading to linearization errors for inherently non-linear rotational blur. By validating the approach across 6 datasets (including real-world RSBlur and RWBI) with fewer parameters (14.3M) than most competitors, MDT offers a practical, efficient architecture for motion deblurring. However, the authors explicitly acknowledge a limitation: the method is designed around motion vector decomposition and performs poorly on defocus blur, which lacks motion structure. An open question is whether the polar-coordinate attention paradigm can be extended to handle non-motion degradations or combined with defocus-specific modules.
