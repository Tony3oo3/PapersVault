## Problem

Existing prompt-driven single-domain generalized object detection methods rely on a single textual prompt (one-step prompting) to simulate target-domain styles, which produces overly simplistic style effects when scenes involve combinations of multiple styles (e.g., rainy + night). This limitation arises because a single prompt cannot effectively synthesize the combined information spanning diverse style dimensions, resulting in poor generalization to complex unseen domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251424/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image from an unseen target domain and outputs bounding boxes with class labels (7 driving classes or 20 Pascal VOC classes) evaluated at IoU 0.5. During training, the model consumes source-domain images (daytime sunny driving scenes or Pascal VOC trainval) alongside progressively constructed text prompts generated by ChatGPT from five vocabularies (weather, time, style, action, detail). The detector is a Faster R-CNN initialized with CLIP weights, with backbone layers 1–3 frozen, and style evolution parameters (μ_t, σ_t, each 256 channels) are trained in a dedicated phase and cycled through training.

## Method

The core mechanism is a three-level Chain-of-Thought style evolution: text prompts progress from individual words (randomly sampled across five vocabularies) to composed phrases to full sentences, with CLIP text features accumulated additively at each level (F_t^1 → F_t^2 → F_t^3). These refined text features supervise two learnable AdaIN parameters (μ_t, σ_t) via a cosine-similarity consistency loss, which are then applied to normalize and re-scale source-domain visual features to simulate target-domain styles. To prevent semantic loss during style transfer, a Style Disentangled Module splits first-layer features into style and content streams using two extractors, supervised by contrastive loss, text–style consistency loss, and content–prototype consistency loss. A Class-Specific Prototype Clustering Module (soft-assignment over K class-matched prototypes with residual weighting) generates prototype-enhanced features that both enrich content semantics and provide a supervisory signal for the disentanglement. Only the style stream undergoes AdaIN-based evolution; the enhanced content stream is fused with the evolved style stream before feeding into backbone layers 2–4 and the RPN.

## Results

On the S-DGOD driving benchmark (trained on Day Clear, tested on 4 unseen weather domains), the method with Swin Transformer backbone achieves mAP of 64.4 (Day Clear), 52.7 (Night Sunny), 49.5 (Dusk Rainy), 33.7 (Night Rainy), and 44.9 (Day Foggy), setting new state-of-the-art on all five domains. With the same ResNet-101 backbone as most baselines, it achieves 55.4, 42.0, 39.2, 24.5, and 40.6 mAP, outperforming the strongest Res101 baselines on 4 of 5 target domains. On the Real-to-Art benchmark, the Swin variant reaches 87.6 (VOC), 36.9 (Comic), 60.7 (Watercolor), and 42.5 (Clipart) mAP, improving over the best prior method (DIV) by 3.7%, 3.7%, 3.3%, and 3.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest prompt-driven baseline C-Gap (Res101), the proposed method (Res101) improves mAP by 4.1, 5.1, 6.9, 5.8, and 2.1 points on Day Clear, Night Sunny, Dusk Rainy, Night Rainy, and Day Foggy respectively, with the largest gains in the most challenging combined-style scenarios (Dusk Rainy, Night Rainy). Compared to UFR (Res101), the method wins on 4 of 5 domains (Night Sunny +1.2, Dusk Rainy +6.0, Night Rainy +5.3, Day Foggy +1.0) but trails on Day Clear by 3.2 points. Against DIV (Res101), which is the strongest on Night Sunny (42.5 vs 42.0), the method still leads on Dusk Rainy (+1.1), Night Rainy (+0.4), and Day Foggy (+3.4). On the Real-to-Art benchmark, the method (Res101) outperforms DIV by 2.8, 1.6, 0.1, and 1.3 mAP on VOC, Comic, Watercolor, and Clipart, and surpasses C-Gap by 2.4, 5.4, 6.8, and 3.5 mAP on the same domains. Traditional normalization baselines (SW, IBN-Net, IterNorm, ISW) are consistently outperformed by 5–15 mAP across all target domains, confirming the advantage of prompt-driven style simulation over purely architectural regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-driven single-domain generalization line initiated by C-GAP and PODA, which use a single text prompt to simulate target-domain styles via CLIP. The key departure is replacing the one-step prompt with a progressive, chain-of-thought-guided style evolution that accumulates style information from individual words to phrases to full sentences, combined with AdaIN-based style transfer on disentangled features. The feature disentanglement and class-specific prototype clustering modules draw on contrastive learning and prototype-based representation learning, but are specifically designed to preserve class semantics during style manipulation in an object detection pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation of one-step prompting in complex multi-style scenarios (e.g., rainy + night), showing that progressive style accumulation yields meaningfully better generalization across weather and artistic domain shifts. However, the method's reliance on ChatGPT for prompt generation introduces a practical dependency and reproducibility concern, and the optimal 3-level CoT hierarchy suggests the approach may not scale gracefully to more complex or open-ended style spaces. Open questions include whether the progressive style evolution principle transfers to other detection architectures or tasks beyond S-DGOD, and whether the gains persist under more extreme or compositional domain shifts not covered by the current benchmarks.
