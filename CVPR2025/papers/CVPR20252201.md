## Problem

Existing diffusion models are trained at fixed resolutions (e.g., 1024² for SDXL) and produce structural distortions or content repetition when scaled beyond this range. Reference-based methods that upsample a low-resolution guide to steer higher-resolution generation face a critical underexplored issue: latent-space upsampling causes manifold deviation (artifacts), while RGB-space upsampling yields overly smooth outputs lacking fine detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252201/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces RGB images at resolutions up to 4096×4096 (16× the native 1024² SDXL resolution), supporting arbitrary scaling factors and aspect ratios (e.g., 2048×4096). Internally, the LSR module operates on SDXL's 8×-compressed latent space, mapping a low-resolution latent (h×w×C) to a higher-resolution latent (H×W×C) via continuous coordinate queries. The framework is plug-and-play: it replaces the reference upsampling step in existing pipelines (DemoFusion, Pixelsmith) while retaining their denoising strategies, and reduces required denoising steps from 50 to 30.

## Method

LSRNA combines two modules to improve reference-based higher-resolution generation. The Latent Space Super-Resolution (LSR) module consists of a lightweight SR backbone (e.g., SwinIR) followed by a LIIF-based continuous upsampler, trained with L1 loss on 4.7M LR-HR latent pairs constructed by encoding degraded and original OpenImages images; it learns to map low-resolution latents directly onto the high-resolution manifold, eliminating the need for progressive upscaling. The Region-wise Noise Addition (RNA) module decodes the reference latent, applies Canny edge detection to identify high-frequency regions, and adaptively modulates Gaussian noise by the normalized edge map before adding it to the LSR-upsampled latent, compensating for the smoothing bias of L1 training. Together, LSR aligns the guidance with the target manifold (enabling fewer denoising steps) while RNA injects region-specific stochasticity to recover fine textures, yielding sharper and more detailed outputs than both latent-only and RGB-only upsampling baselines.

## Results

At 4096×4096 resolution, LSRNA-DemoFusion achieves a pFID of 29.12 and FID of 85.03, while LSRNA-Pixelsmith achieves the best reference-based FID of 84.19 with the fastest inference time of 313 s among reference-based methods. Compared to their base methods, LSRNA-DemoFusion reduces inference time from 1507 s to 506 s (66% speedup) and LSRNA-Pixelsmith from 505 s to 313 s (38% speedup) at 4K, while improving pFID by 3.77 and 2.72 points respectively. At 2048×4096, LSRNA-Pixelsmith achieves the best overall FID (78.47) and pFID (32.96) among all methods, with inference time of only 157 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DemoFusion (latent upsampling), LSRNA-DemoFusion consistently wins on pFID (29.12 vs 32.89 at 4K; 33.31 vs 38.64 at 2048×4096) and pKID, while reducing inference time by 66% at 4K (506 s vs 1507 s) and 66% at 2048×4096 (223 s vs 648 s). Against Pixelsmith (RGB upsampling), LSRNA-Pixelsmith achieves lower FID (84.19 vs 84.75 at 4K; 78.47 vs 81.21 at 2048×4096) and substantially lower pFID (29.62 vs 32.34 at 4K), confirming that latent-space guidance preserves more detail than RGB-space guidance. Against Self-Cascade, LSRNA-Pixelsmith matches or beats it on FID at 2048×4096 (78.47 vs 78.34, a near-tie) and clearly wins on pFID (32.96 vs 34.40) and inference speed (157 s vs 210 s); at 4K, LSRNA-Pixelsmith dominates with FID 84.19 vs 90.94 and pFID 29.62 vs 43.91. Against non-reference-based methods (SDXL+BSRGAN, HiDiffusion, ScaleCrafter, FouriScale), LSRNA methods achieve substantially better quality (e.g., pFID 29.12 vs 37.04 for SDXL+BSRGAN at 4K) but at higher inference cost (313–506 s vs 17–240 s). The only case where a baseline edges out LSRNA is Self-Cascade's FID at 2048×4096 (78.34 vs 78.47), a negligible 0.13-point difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reference-based higher-resolution generation paradigm established by DemoFusion (latent upsampling) and Pixelsmith (RGB upsampling), identifying a fundamental tension: naive latent upsampling causes manifold deviation while RGB upsampling sacrifices high-frequency detail. It builds on LIIF's continuous implicit upsampling to learn a lightweight latent-space super-resolution module that maps low-resolution latents onto the high-resolution manifold, and draws on SDEdit's noise-injection principle to design region-adaptive noise guided by Canny edges. The key departure from prior work is eliminating progressive upscaling entirely—a single learned LSR step replaces the multi-stage cascade—while also demonstrating that latent-space guidance is inherently superior to RGB-space guidance for detail preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LSRNA reduces 4K generation time from 1507s to 506s (DemoFusion) and 505s to 313s (Pixelsmith) while matching or exceeding quality, making high-resolution diffusion generation substantially more accessible. The conceptual finding that a lightweight learned upsampler in latent space can replace progressive upscaling challenges the assumption that multi-stage cascades are necessary for manifold alignment. However, the paper acknowledges that RNA strength must be tuned per method and noise schedule, and that generation quality remains bounded by the pretrained LDM's capacity—open questions include whether the LSR module generalizes across different base models (beyond SDXL) and whether the Canny-edge heuristic for region selection can be replaced by learned frequency-aware mechanisms.
