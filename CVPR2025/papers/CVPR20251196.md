## Problem

Existing multi-layer transparent image generation methods rely on semantic layouts that require per-region text prompts, making annotation labor-intensive and unscalable to the tens or hundreds of layers common in graphic design. Additionally, prior approaches are limited to a small fixed number of layers at fixed resolutions and generate layers sequentially, leading to inter-layer incoherence. This paper asks whether a generative model can autonomously determine what content belongs in each spatial region from a single global prompt alone, eliminating the need for region-wise annotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single global text prompt describing the full scene and an anonymous region layout consisting of K bounding boxes (center coordinates, height, width) with no semantic labels, either user-specified or predicted by a fine-tuned LLaMA-3.1-8B planner. It simultaneously outputs a global reference composed image, an RGB background layer, and K RGBA transparent foreground layers, each cropped to its tight bounding box (ceiling-aligned to 16 px) so that layer resolutions vary. Transparency is encoded by pre-multiplying the alpha channel into RGB before VAE encoding, and the decoder reconstructs all layers in a single ViT forward pass.

## Method

ART is built on the FLUX.1[dev] MMDiT backbone and introduces three core mechanisms. First, a layout-conditional multi-layer 3D RoPE encodes width, height, and layer indices into rotary position embeddings, allowing the transformer to distinguish tokens from different transparent layers while handling variable sequence lengths. Second, a Regional Full Attention scheme applies ceiling-aligned tight crops per layer so that each layer's visual tokens attend only within their own spatial region, reducing attention cost from quadratic to near-constant as the number of layers grows (12× faster than full attention at 50+ layers). Third, a novel multi-layer transparent autoencoder uses a ViT-Base decoder with 3D RoPE to jointly decode a variable number of RGBA layers at different resolutions from a single concatenated latent sequence. The model is trained via LoRA fine-tuning (rank 64, Prodigy optimizer) on 800K samples from the newly collected MLTD dataset of multi-layer graphic designs, and all layers plus the reference image are denoised simultaneously rather than sequentially.

## Results

On the DESIGN-MULTI-LAYER-BENCH, ART with the Anonymous Region Layout Planner achieves a FID_merged of 19.90, outperforming GPT-4o (20.72) and LayoutGPT (20.92) as layout planners, while running 3× faster (5.68 s vs. 19.19 s for the semantic layout planner). The multi-layer transparency decoder reaches PSNR of 30.48 dB and FID_merged of 2.84, substantially exceeding LayerDiffuse's decoder (26.51 dB, FID 4.27). In user studies, ART significantly outperforms LayerDiffuse in the photorealistic domain and COLE in the graphic-design domain across harmonization, aesthetics, and prompt following. The Regional Full Attention design maintains near-constant computational cost for 10–50 layers, achieving >12× speedup over full attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **LayerDiffuse** [83] (photorealistic domain), ART wins the user study on all three evaluated dimensions—harmonization, aesthetics, and prompt following—and also surpasses it in transparency decoder quality (PSNR 30.48 vs. 26.51 dB; FID_merged 2.84 vs. 4.27 in Table 10). Against **COLE** [37] (graphic-design domain), ART achieves significantly better scores in layout organization, visual aesthetics, and inter-layer harmonization in the user study, but concedes the typography dimension because COLE renders text with a dedicated typography renderer. Against **LayoutGPT** [17] as a layout planner, ART's Anonymous Region Layout Planner attains a lower FID_merged (19.90 vs. 20.92) and runs more than 3× faster (5.68 s vs. the semantic planner's 19.19 s), while GPT-4o achieves a marginally higher PSNR (22.80 vs. 22.70) but lacks a reported inference speed. Overall, ART dominates on efficiency and layer coherence, with the only notable loss being typography rendering versus COLE.

## Relation to Prior Work

ART builds directly on the FLUX.1/MMDiT diffusion transformer backbone and extends the DiT architecture with a novel layout-conditional 3D RoPE that encodes spatial (x, y) and layer (l) positions simultaneously. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant semantic layout paradigm (GLIGEN, ReCo, LayerDiff) by removing region-wise text prompts entirely, instead relying on the model's learned attention to autonomously assign semantics to anonymous bounding boxes—an idea grounded in Schema Theory from cognitive science. Unlike sequential layer-generation methods (LayerDiffuse, COLE) that cascade diffusion models, ART generates all transparent layers and a reference composed image simultaneously in a single end-to-end pass, and unlike prior multi-layer approaches limited to 2–4 fixed-resolution layers, it supports variable numbers of layers at variable resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12× speedup over full attention and the ability to generate 50+ coherent layers open a practical path toward interactive, layer-by-layer content creation in graphic design and digital art, where creators currently rely on manual compositing. The finding that removing region-wise prompts *improves* inter-layer coherence (PSNR 17.71 → 22.90) challenges the intuitive assumption that more specific per-region instructions yield better results, suggesting that over-constraining attention creates a semantic gap between global and local tokens. Open questions include whether the anonymous layout generalizes to all possible compositions (the authors note repeated and combined layer generation remain weak), whether the approach transfers to video or 3D layer generation, and whether the 8K-sample data efficiency holds at higher resolutions or more diverse domains beyond graphic design.
