## Problem

Existing conditional DDPMs for 3D tasks use a Noise-Conditional Framework where the Noise Network dominates and must fit scores from complex scene distributions, requiring dozens to thousands of training and inference iterations that are impractical for real-time 3D understanding. Meanwhile, standard point cloud segmentation methods remain sensitive to the noise and sparsity inherent in raw 3D sensor data, degrading accuracy on object boundaries and small objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251064/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes voxelized 3D point clouds (0.02 m resolution for indoor ScanNet/ScanNet200, 0.05 m for outdoor nuScenes) and produces per-point semantic class labels. During training, the input point cloud is additionally perturbed with Gaussian noise at multiple diffusion levels (t sampled uniformly from 1–1000) to generate augmented features, while during inference a single maximum-noise sample (c_T ~ N(0,I)) is fed through the auxiliary branch, making the forward pass equivalent to a standard non-DDPM segmentation network.

## Method

CDSegNet introduces a Conditional-Noise Framework (CNF) that inverts the standard DDPM hierarchy: the Conditional Network (CN), a 4-stage Transformer-U-Net, becomes the dominant segmentation backbone, while the Noise Network (NN), a lightweight 2-stage Transformer-U-Net, serves only as an auxiliary noise-feature generator operating on the conditional point cloud rather than the target labels. A Feature Fusion Module (FFM) at the bottleneck uses cross-attention to filter and inject NN's noise features into CN, providing controlled multi-level feature perturbations that act as learnable data augmentation. Training is multi-task: NN is optimized with the standard DDPM noise-prediction loss (Eq. 7) while CN is optimized with cross-entropy plus Lovász-softmax loss (Eq. 10), balanced via a Geometric Loss Strategy to prevent the slower-converging noise branch from destabilizing the segmentation branch. At inference, CDSegNet requires only a single forward pass—CN processes the clean point cloud alongside one filtered noise perturbation from NN—eliminating the iterative denoising loop entirely while retaining the noise- and sparsity-robustness benefits of the DDPM training paradigm.

## Results

On ScanNet, CDSegNet achieves 77.9 mIoU / 85.2 mAcc / 92.2 allAcc, outperforming PTv3 (77.6/85.0/92.0) and PTv2 (75.5/82.9/91.2). On ScanNet200, it reaches 36.3 mIoU / 45.9 mAcc / 83.9 allAcc, surpassing PTv3 (35.3/46.0/83.3) and OctFormer (32.9/42.4/81.2). On the outdoor nuScenes benchmark, CDSegNet attains 81.2 mIoU, exceeding PTv3 (80.3), SphereFormer (79.5), and RPVNet (77.6) by 0.9, 1.7, and 3.6 mIoU respectively. The method also demonstrates strong noise robustness, retaining 57.0 mIoU under Gaussian noise (τ=0.5) on ScanNet versus 45.8 for PTv3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDSegNet consistently outperforms all compared methods across both indoor benchmarks. On ScanNet, it edges out PTv3 by 0.3 mIoU (77.9 vs. 77.6) and PTv2 by 2.4 mIoU, while on ScanNet200 the gap widens to 1.0 mIoU over PTv3 (36.3 vs. 35.3) and 3.4 mIoU over OctFormer. On the sparser outdoor nuScenes benchmark, the advantage is most pronounced: CDSegNet leads PTv3 by 0.9 mIoU (81.2 vs. 80.3), SphereFormer by 1.7 mIoU, and RangeNet++ by 15.7 mIoU, with particularly large gains on the 'vehicle' class (62.3 vs. 54.0 for SphereFormer). The method also surpasses its own baseline (which removes diffusion modeling from the Noise Network) by 0.4 mIoU on ScanNet and 0.8 mIoU on nuScenes, confirming the contribution of the Conditional-Noise Framework. In the noise robustness experiments (Table 3), CDSegNet retains 57.0 mIoU under Gaussian noise at τ=0.5 on ScanNet, compared to 45.8 for PTv3 and 8.7 for PTv2, demonstrating substantially better degradation under perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper restructures the conditional DDPM paradigm by inverting the dominant/auxiliary roles of the Noise Network and Conditional Network, moving from the standard Noise-Conditional Framework (NCF) to a proposed Conditional-Noise Framework (CNF). It builds directly on the DDPM formulation of Ho et al. [16] and the authors' own prior work on conditional DDPMs for point cloud upsampling [39], extending the noise-system robustness insight to semantic segmentation. Conceptually, it departs from prior DDPM-for-3D work (which typically uses two-stage pre-training or dozens of inference steps) by showing that the noise construction system can be retained in an auxiliary branch while the segmentation backbone operates as a standard single-step classifier at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CNF framework offers a practical path to integrating diffusion-model robustness into real-time 3D perception pipelines (autonomous driving, robotics) without the multi-step inference cost that has previously made DDPMs impractical for such tasks. The demonstrated generalizability across backbones (MinkUNet, PTv3, PointNet/++) and tasks (segmentation, classification) suggests CNF could become a reusable module rather than a one-off architecture. Open questions include whether the noise-fitting objective in NN is truly the source of robustness (the authors acknowledge this needs further validation), whether the modest absolute mIoU gains over strong baselines like PTv3 will hold as backbone architectures continue to improve, and whether CNF extends to other generative 3D tasks beyond segmentation and classification.
