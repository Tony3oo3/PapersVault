## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for diffusion models operate in image space (e.g., given an image, is it OOD?), but the uncertainty of a text-to-image generation with respect to its conditioning prompt remains unexplored. No prior work has defined or quantified how uncertain a T2I model is about a given text prompt, despite this being a critical gap for trustworthy generative AI applications such as bias detection, deepfake prevention, and copyright protection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251715/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt as input and produces a scalar uncertainty score computed in text space, along with decomposed aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty components. The prompt is first fed to a T2I diffusion model to generate an image, which is then captioned by an LVLM; the final uncertainty is derived from the text-level similarity between the original prompt and the LVLM-generated caption. Unlike image-space baselines that require multiple generations or access to intermediate denoising steps, PUNC only needs one image generation plus one LVLM inference call.

## Method

PUNC (Prompt-based UNCertainty Estimation for T2I Generation) bypasses direct image comparison by leveraging a Large Vision-Language Model to extract semantics from the generated image and evaluate alignment in the more semantically meaningful text space. The pipeline has three steps: (1) sample an image from the T2I model given the prompt, (2) use an LVLM (e.g., Molmo) to produce a descriptive caption of the generated image, and (3) compute a text similarity score (ROUGE or BERTScore) between the original prompt and the caption, where lower similarity indicates higher uncertainty. A key differentiator is the disentanglement of uncertainty types: epistemic uncertainty is measured as 1 − recall (concepts in the prompt missing from the caption, reflecting the model's lack of knowledge), while aleatoric uncertainty is measured as 1 − precision (extra concepts in the caption not in the prompt, reflecting prompt ambiguity). This precision/recall decomposition is impossible with image-space similarity metrics, which conflate pixel-level variation with semantic divergence.

## Results

On OOD (epistemic) detection, PUNC with BERTScore recall achieves 89.28% average AUROC on microscopic and remote sensing datasets, substantially outperforming DDPM-OOD (66.17% and 85.39%) and LMD (22.79% and 40.63%). On the texture dataset, PUNC ROUGE (L_recall) reaches 89.04% AUROC versus 41.93% for DDPM-OOD and 32.11% for LMD. For aleatoric uncertainty, PUNC ROUGE (1_precision) achieves near-perfect 99.99% AUROC on Vague prompts, compared to 40.59% (DDPM-OOD) and 57.83% (2XDM). On adversarial and corrupted prompts, PUNC BERT (precision) attains 72.69–76.04% AUROC, roughly doubling the performance of all image-space baselines (38.97–60.24%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUNC consistently outperforms both DDPM-OOD and LMD across all three OOD datasets. On microscopic images, PUNC BERT (recall) achieves 89.28% AUROC versus 66.17% for DDPM-OOD and 22.79% for LMD — a 23-point and 67-point margin respectively. On remote sensing, PUNC narrows the gap with DDPM-OOD (89.28% vs. 85.39%) but still leads by ~4 points, while LMD lags far behind at 40.63%. On texture, PUNC ROUGE (L_recall) dominates with 89.04% AUROC, nearly doubling DDPM-OOD (41.93%) and LMD (32.11%). For aleatoric uncertainty, the advantage is even more pronounced: on Vague prompts, PUNC ROUGE reaches ~100% AUROC while the best baseline (2XDM) manages only 57.83%; on adversarial and corrupted prompts, PUNC BERT (precision) scores 72–76% AUROC versus 39–60% for all image-space methods. The 2XDM baseline is the strongest competitor on remote sensing (81.51%) but is outperformed by PUNC on every other setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by shifting the uncertainty quantification paradigm from image-space (given an image, is it OOD?) to prompt-space (given a text prompt, how uncertain is the generation?). It adapts existing image-space OOD detection methods (DDPM-OOD, LMD) and test-time ensembling (2XDM) as baselines, but departs fundamentally by arguing that semantic alignment between prompt and output—not pixel-level reconstruction fidelity—is the correct axis for measuring uncertainty in T2I generation. The PUNC method repurposes Large Vision-Language Models (originally designed for image captioning and VQA) as semantic extractors, then applies standard text-similarity metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore) to score prompt-caption alignment, a pipeline that has no direct precedent in the diffusion-model uncertainty literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of prompt-conditioned uncertainty in T2I models, and the disentangling of aleatoric (precision) from epistemic (recall) uncertainty via text-space metrics is something image-space approaches structurally cannot achieve. The practical applications demonstrated—detecting whether models can generate recognizable politicians, copyrighted characters, or biased demographic outputs—give the method immediate relevance for AI safety and regulatory compliance. Key limitations include heavy reliance on LVLM interpretive quality (which may inherit training-data biases), English-only evaluation, and mixed results on texture datasets where training-data coverage is ambiguous. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multilingual prompts, video generation, or models without explicit text conditioning.
