## Problem

Existing deep learning-based LiDAR odometry methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: they lack semantic scene understanding, making them vulnerable to dynamic objects, repetitive patterns, and low-texture environments, and they operate as deterministic predictors that cannot model uncertainty or adapt to novel environmental changes. No prior work has combined semantic awareness with probabilistic refinement in the odometry pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two consecutive Velodyne LiDAR point clouds, each randomly subsampled to 8,192 points (the two frames need not have equal point counts), and outputs a relative 6-DoF pose as a unit quaternion and a 3D translation vector. The pose is produced hierarchically: a coarse estimate at the sparsest level is progressively refined through multiple diffusion denoising steps at increasingly dense levels, with the final output being the composition of all residual corrections.

## Method

DiffLO combines three key components: (1) a Semantic Perception Module that distills per-point features from a frozen Spherical Transformer segmentation teacher into a lightweight student MLP within the odometry network, so that at inference only the student remains and provides semantic conditioning without extra cost; (2) a semantic-aware pose estimator (an extension of PWCLO-Net's embedding-mask formulation) that concatenates the distilled semantic features into both the embedding and mask computation at every pyramid level; and (3) a diffusion-based refinement module that treats the coarse-to-fine pose residual as a diffusion latent variable, applying a forward Gaussian noising schedule and a reverse denoising process where a GRU-based network predicts the clean residual pose conditioned on geometric features, cross-frame cost volume, residual pose embeddings, and the semantic feature. The denoising network is trained with a simple L2 loss on the predicted signal (rather than noise), and the full pipeline is supervised with a multi-scale pose loss plus the distillation loss, with learnable scale parameters balancing translation and quaternion terms.

## Results

On the KITTI odometry dataset (sequences 07–10 as test), DiffLO achieves a mean translational RMSE (t_rel) of 0.708% and rotational RMSE (r_rel) of 0.328°/100m. The best per-sequence results are on sequence 01 (t_rel = 0.36%, r_rel = 0.15°/100m) and sequence 04 (t_rel = 0.30%, r_rel = 0.17°/100m). The method achieves 76.9 ms inference time, satisfying the real-time requirement for 10 Hz LiDAR. Ablation shows the diffusion refinement reduces translation error by 88.2% and rotation error by 90.0% compared to the non-diffusion variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffLO outperforms all learning-based baselines on mean rotational error (r_rel = 0.328°/100m vs. PWCLO-Net 0.490, TransLO 0.500, DELO 0.630, LO-Net 0.93) and on mean translational error vs. PWCLO-Net (0.708 vs. 1.085), TransLO (0.708 vs. 0.993), DELO (0.708 vs. 1.175), and LO-Net (0.708 vs. 1.80). Against the classic Full A-LOAM (with mapping optimization), DiffLO achieves lower t_rel (0.708 vs. 0.810) but a marginally higher r_rel (0.328 vs. 0.323), winning on 9 of 11 individual sequences. Compared to EfficientLO, DiffLO achieves a 16.9% improvement in rotation accuracy (0.328 vs. 0.395) but a higher mean translation error (0.708 vs. 0.46), with the gap driven primarily by sequence 08 (1.12 vs. 0.38). Against traditional ICP-based methods (ICP-po2po, ICP-po2pl, GICP, CLS), DiffLO achieves 2–11× lower errors on both metrics. In the alternative split (00–06 + 09–10 train, 07–08 test), DiffLO (t_rel 0.745, r_rel 0.355) substantially outperforms LodoNet (1.950, 1.305), and in the 00–08 train / 09–10 test split it beats both Nubert et al. (1.66, 0.685) and the multi-modal H-VLO (1.64, 0.430) on both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffLO extends the PWCLO-Net hierarchical embedding-mask framework by injecting semantic understanding via knowledge distillation from a frozen Spherical Transformer segmentation model, and replaces the deterministic MLP-based refinement with a DDPM-style diffusion process conditioned on geometric, semantic, and cross-frame cost-volume features. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two recent threads: (1) the growing use of diffusion models for 3D geometric tasks (SE(3) registration, scene flow, LiDAR localization) and (2) the integration of semantic cues into odometry, which prior learning-based methods (LO-Net, TransLO, EfficientLO) addressed only through learned masks rather than explicit scene understanding. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to apply a diffusion probabilistic model to the LiDAR odometry task, departing from the deterministic prediction paradigm that characterizes all prior deep odometry networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a diffusion-based iterative refinement can substantially outperform deterministic MLP refinement (88% reduction in translation error in ablation) while maintaining real-time inference (76.9 ms), the paper opens a path toward uncertainty-aware, probabilistic odometry that could be critical for safety-critical autonomous driving. However, evaluation is limited to the KITTI dataset (11 sequences), and the semantic distillation pipeline, while dropped at inference, adds training complexity and depends on a pretrained segmentation model whose quality may not transfer to unseen environments. Open questions include whether the diffusion refinement generalizes to higher-frequency or multi-sensor LiDAR, whether the semantic conditioning provides robust gains in truly dynamic or degraded-sensor scenarios beyond KITTI's urban driving, and how the method scales to longer-range odometry where cumulative drift becomes dominant.
