## Problem

Existing active learning methods address either out-of-distribution (OOD) filtering or category discovery in isolation, yet real-world open-world scenarios (autonomous driving, robotics) require both simultaneously. Prior open-set AL approaches rely on auxiliary models and access to the unlabeled pool for OOD detection, which is impractical when the pool composition is unknown. No prior work jointly handles the three-way separation of known classes, novel discoverable classes, and irrelevant OOD data within a single active learning framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled pool (initially containing only known-class images, later augmented with accidentally selected OOD samples) and an unlabeled pool containing a mixture of known-class, novel discoverable-class, and OOD images. It operates on standard RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 64×64 (TinyImageNet) using a ResNet-18/50 backbone. The output is a filtered subset of unlabeled samples deemed InD or discoverable (via an energy-score threshold), from which q samples are selected for annotation each cycle; the method requires no access to the unlabeled pool during training, only during the filtering and selection phases.

## Method

Joda is a single-model, three-phase active learning algorithm: (I) Training combines Cross-Entropy loss on known-class data with an Outlier Exposure loss on accidentally labeled OOD data (weighted by λ_OE), shaping a feature space that clusters InD and discoverable classes together while pushing OOD samples toward a uniform logit distribution; (II) Filtering computes an energy score per unlabeled sample and applies a threshold derived from Youden's J statistic on an ROC curve over the labeled pool, separating OOD from InD/discoverable candidates without any auxiliary classifier; (III) Selection applies the SISOMe metric (a self-balancing combination of inner/outer class distance ratio and energy score) augmented with a class-balancing factor that up-weights underrepresented pseudo-classes to counteract the imbalance introduced when novel categories are first discovered. The key architectural insight is that the outlier-exposure training, energy-based filtering, and SISOMe selection are deeply entangled—removing any component degrades all three metrics (accuracy, class discovery, selection precision)—and the entire pipeline requires zero auxiliary models and zero unlabeled-pool access during training.

## Results

Across 18 experimental configurations (3 InD datasets × 3 OOD sources) evaluated on accuracy, class discovery, and selection precision, Joda achieves the highest accuracy in 17 out of 18 settings, ranking second only for TinyImageNet with random noise OOD. Joda attains a selection precision of nearly 1.0 in 8 out of 10 combinations and demonstrates the fastest class discovery in all settings. For the most realistic OOD datasets (Places365 and ImageNetC-800), Joda consistently reports the best accuracy across all three InD datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet). The method requires zero auxiliary models and only accesses the labeled pool, compared to 1–2 auxiliary models and unlabeled pool access for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Joda outperforms all eight baselines on the combined accuracy–precision–discovery trade-off. LfOSA [39] achieves high selection precision via its auxiliary classifier but consistently lags in class discovery, failing to unveil all unknown categories. CCAL [11] and MQNet [40] (both contrastive-learning-based open-set methods) show inconsistent performance across OOD sources: CCAL handles MNIST and Places365 well but struggles with random noise, while MQNet has difficulties with random noise and MNIST and suffers in selection precision on Places365. Pal [66] maintains a reasonable exploration–precision balance but does not surpass Joda or LfOSA on precision. Classic AL methods (Ent [17], LLoss [68], Badge [2]) perform adequately for easy OOD (random noise, MNIST) but degrade sharply on realistic OOD (Places365, ImageNetC-800); LLoss is the strongest classic baseline, maintaining performance on TinyImageNet. EIOAL [56], an ensemble-based semi-supervised method, outperforms LfOSA on TinyImageNet but is not applicable to the supervised OSDAL setup and contradicts the goal of computational cost reduction. Joda's key advantage is its single-model design: it eliminates the 1–2 auxiliary models and unlabeled-pool access required by all open-set AL baselines while simultaneously achieving superior OOD filtering, class discovery, and accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—active learning, out-of-distribution detection, and category discovery—into a single framework (OSDAL) that had not been jointly addressed before. Methodologically, it builds directly on SISOMe (Schmidt et al., 2024) for sample selection and on the outlier-exposure loss (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2016) for OOD-aware training, but departs from all prior open-set AL methods (LfOSA, CCAL, MQNet, Pal, EOAL) by eliminating auxiliary models and any access to the unlabeled pool. The key conceptual departure is that OOD data accidentally collected into the labeled pool is repurposed as a training signal (via outlier exposure) to shape the feature space, rather than being treated as a nuisance to be handled by a separate detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical simplification—requiring only a single model trained on labeled data alone—makes open-set AL substantially more deployable in streaming or resource-constrained settings where unlabeled pool composition is unknown. The 18-configuration benchmark across three InD datasets and four OOD sources is the largest to date for open-set AL, providing a useful reference for future work. However, the evaluation is limited to image classification with ResNet backbones; the authors themselves note that extension to semantic segmentation, object detection, and more complex novel-class behaviors remains open. Whether the energy-score + outlier-exposure combination generalizes to non-CNN architectures or higher-dimensional feature spaces is untested.
