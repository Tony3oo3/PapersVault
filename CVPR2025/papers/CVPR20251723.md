## Problem

Existing energy-based OOD detection methods like EBO require empirical estimation of energy thresholds from a pre-trained model for both ID and OOD data, making them dataset- and architecture-dependent and limiting scalability. Additionally, standard K-class classifiers inherently overconfidently assign OOD inputs to one of the predefined ID classes, degrading detection reliability in open-world settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251723/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-200) through a ResNet-18 backbone, producing a 2K-dimensional dual-classifier output where the first K logits correspond to ID classes and the last K to negative (OOD) classes. At inference, a weighted score S_dec combining the maximum conditional probability and the negative marginal energy is compared against a threshold α to yield a binary ID/OOD decision, while the argmax over the first K logits gives the class label.

## Method

DEBO introduces a dual classifier that maps each input to ℝ^{2K}, partitioning the output space into K positive (ID) and K negative (OOD) classes, and formulates a unified energy-based loss that simultaneously minimizes the KL divergence between data and model distributions for both the marginal and joint cases. The key insight is that OOD samples are treated as negative classes in the dual classifier, so the loss naturally drives low energy and high confidence for ID data while inducing high energy and low confidence for OOD data—eliminating the need for empirical energy thresholds. A practical non-saturating modification (analogous to GAN training) replaces log(1−P) terms with −log P to avoid vanishing gradients early in training. For OOD detection, the score S_dec = (1−γ)·max_y+ log p(y+|x) + γ·(−E_θ(x)) flexibly balances conditional probability and marginal energy via a tunable hyperparameter γ, and an entropy-minimization term C_en is added to sharpen OOD predictions over the K negative classes without requiring OOD labels.

## Results

DEBO achieves state-of-the-art OOD detection across all three benchmark datasets using a ResNet-18 backbone. On CIFAR-10, it attains FPR95 of 14.41% and AUROC of 96.67%, while on CIFAR-100 it reaches FPR95 of 33.73% and AUROC of 89.76%—substantially outperforming the second-best method EBO (w. D_aux) by 16.64 and 5.45 points respectively on FPR95 and AUROC. On ImageNet-200, DEBO achieves the best AUROC (88.10%) and AUPR-In (83.24%), while maintaining competitive ID classification accuracy (86.26%) comparable to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEBO achieves the best FPR95 and AUROC on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, beating the strongest competitor EBO (w. D_aux) by 2.58 FPR95 points (14.41 vs. 16.99) and 2.93 AUROC points (96.67 vs. 93.74) on CIFAR-10, and by 16.64 FPR95 points (33.73 vs. 50.37) and 5.45 AUROC points (89.76 vs. 84.31) on CIFAR-100. On ImageNet-200, DEBO leads in AUROC (88.10 vs. 87.15 for MSP) and AUPR-In (83.24 vs. 82.32 for MCD), but trails MCD on FPR95 (41.12 vs. 40.07) and EBO on AUPR-Out (87.03 vs. 87.70). Notably, DEBO avoids the need for empirical energy threshold estimation required by EBO (w. D_aux), which also suffers a significant ID-ACC drop (90.40% on CIFAR-10, 72.12% on CIFAR-100) compared to DEBO's 94.86% and 76.26%. Among methods using auxiliary OOD data, DEBO consistently outperforms OE, UDG, MCD, and MIXOE on the primary OOD metrics across all three datasets, with the largest margins observed on CIFAR-100 where the gap over the second-best method exceeds 16 FPR95 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEBO extends the energy-based OOD detection line initiated by JEM (Duvenaud et al., ICLR 2020) and EBO (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2020), which recast standard classifiers as energy-based models and use energy regularization to separate ID from OOD samples. The key departure is architectural: rather than a single K-way classifier with empirically estimated energy thresholds (as in EBO), DEBO introduces a 2K-way dual classifier that explicitly partitions the output space into K positive (ID) and K negative (OOD) classes, eliminating the need for dataset- and architecture-dependent energy value estimation. The GAN-inspired non-saturating loss modification and the entropy-based unsupervised OOD term further distinguish the training objective from prior energy-regularized approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the empirical energy threshold estimation step, DEBO offers a more scalable and architecture-agnostic OOD detection pipeline that aligns naturally with standard cross-entropy training, which is practically valuable for practitioners deploying models in open-world settings. The theoretical guarantee that the loss minimizes an upper bound on the KL divergence between data and model distributions (both marginal and joint) provides a principled foundation absent in many heuristic OOD methods. However, the method is validated only on ResNet-18 across vision benchmarks, and the reliance on SGLD-sampled generated data during training introduces additional computational overhead that limits immediate applicability to very large-scale or non-vision tasks. Open questions include whether the dual classifier generalizes to transformer backbones, whether the SGLD sampling can be replaced by cheaper alternatives, and how the method performs in truly open-world (unbounded OOD) settings rather than fixed auxiliary OOD pools.
