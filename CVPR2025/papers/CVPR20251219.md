## Problem

Existing controllable I2V methods rely on large Gaussian kernels to extend sparse trajectories, which blurs fine-grained motion details and limits precise control. Additionally, using trajectory alone as the sole condition fails to disambiguate between object motion and camera motion, causing motion misinterpretation in generated videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the user provides a reference image (320×512), draws one or two sparse trajectory points indicating desired motion direction, and brushes a binary motion region mask. The sparse trajectory is padded into 8×8 local regions to match the training paradigm, and the brushed mask serves directly as the motion mask. The output is a 16-frame video at 8 fps with motion aligned to the specified trajectory and category (object vs. camera).

## Method

MotionPro replaces Gaussian-filtered trajectories with region-wise trajectories: optical flow maps (estimated by DOT) are split into 8×8 local regions, and trajectories are sampled directly within randomly selected regions (mask ratio r_min=0.95) to preserve fine-grained motion details. A binary motion mask is simultaneously derived by thresholding the average flow magnitude (>1) to specify the target motion category. A lightweight motion encoder processes the concatenated trajectory and mask into multi-scale features, which modulate video latent features in the SVD 3D-UNet via adaptive feature modulation (predicting per-scale γ and β with zero-initialized temporal convolutions). LoRA (rank 32) is applied to all attention modules in spatial-temporal transformer blocks, while the base 3D-UNet remains frozen; only the motion encoder and LoRA parameters are trained with AdamW at lr 1e-5.

## Results

On WebVid-10M (fine-grained motion control), MotionPro achieves FVD of 59.88 and FID of 10.40, outperforming MOFA-Video (FVD 87.70, FID 12.18) and DragNUWA (FVD 96.65, FID 13.19). On the paper's own MC-Bench for fine-grained control, MotionPro attains MD-Img of 10.56 and MD-Vid of 8.34, beating MOFA-Video (13.94 / 10.50) and DragDiffusion (14.70 / 13.84). For object-level motion control on MC-Bench, MotionPro scores MD-Img 10.48 and MD-Vid 8.59, surpassing DragAnything (12.30 / 11.37) and MOFA-Video (15.56 / 12.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionPro consistently outperforms all four baselines on the trajectory-alignment metrics (MD-Img and MD-Vid) across both fine-grained and object-level settings. Against MOFA-Video (the strongest competitor, appearing in all three tables), MotionPro reduces MD-Vid by 2.16 (fine-grained) and 2.78 (object-level), while also improving MD-Img by 3.38 and 5.08 respectively; however, MOFA-Video achieves slightly higher Frame Consistency (~0.001) due to its two-stage densification producing less motion dynamics (average flow magnitude 4.95 vs. MotionPro's 8.95). On WebVid-10M, MotionPro beats DragNUWA by 36.77 in FVD and 2.79 in FID, confirming that region-wise trajectories preserve fine-grained motion details lost by Gaussian filtering. DragDiffusion (an image-editing method repurposed for video) is the weakest baseline on MC-Bench fine-grained, trailing MotionPro by 4.14 in MD-Img and 5.50 in MD-Vid. DragAnything, while competitive on object-level MD-Img (12.30 vs. 10.48), still misinterprets trajectories as camera motion in several cases, a failure mode MotionPro avoids through its motion mask conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionPro builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its base I2V architecture, extending the trajectory-based motion control paradigm established by DragNUWA and DragAnything. The key conceptual departure is replacing the large-kernel Gaussian filtering of point-wise trajectories with region-wise trajectories sampled from local patches of optical flow maps, preserving fine-grained motion details that Gaussian extension inevitably blurs. Additionally, while MOFA-Video introduced a movement region mask, it was used only as a post-processing flow-masking step; MotionPro integrates the motion mask as a first-class conditional input alongside the trajectory through adaptive feature modulation, enabling the model to explicitly disambiguate object motion from camera motion during denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete failure modes of prior trajectory-based I2V control—loss of fine-grained motion detail and motion-category misinterpretation—that practitioners encounter when deploying such systems. The introduction of MC-Bench (1.1K user-annotated image-trajectory pairs) fills a practical evaluation gap, since prior benchmarks like WebVid-10M rely on automatically sampled trajectories that do not reflect real user interaction patterns. Limitations include evaluation at only 320×512 resolution with 16-frame clips, a single base architecture (SVD), and the reliance on an off-the-shelf tracking model (DOT) whose errors propagate into the control signals; whether the region-wise trajectory formulation scales to longer, higher-resolution, or multi-object scenes remains open.
