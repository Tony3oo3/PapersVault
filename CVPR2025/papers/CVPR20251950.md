## Problem

Existing methods for adapting pre-trained video-language models to action recognition introduce large numbers of new parameters (temporal modules, full fine-tuning) that cause catastrophic forgetting of generalizable knowledge, degrading performance on unseen categories. Additionally, no effective parameter-efficient transfer learning method had been designed for ViCLIP, which replaces CLIP's visual attention with spatiotemporal attention, making standard CLIP-based adapters inapplicable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251950/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips (T×H×W×3) processed through ViCLIP's spatiotemporal visual encoder and text inputs in the language branch. The trainable branch receives simple template prompts ('a video of {cls}'), while the frozen pre-trained branch receives LLM-generated spatio-temporal descriptions for the consistency constraint. Inference uses 8-frame single-view for zero-shot, multi-view for few-shot/base-to-novel, and 4 clips × 3 crops (8 frames each) for fully-supervised evaluation, producing action class labels via cosine similarity in the shared vision-language embedding space.

## Method

MSTA inserts lightweight adapters selectively into higher transformer layers (≥k) of both the video and text encoders, with a shared projection layer that receives gradients from both modalities to optimize cross-modal alignment. The video branch additionally employs separate spatial (linear) and temporal (3D convolution) up-projection layers to capture spatiotemporal structure. A spatio-temporal description-guided consistency constraint is imposed by feeding LLM-generated spatio-temporal descriptions to the frozen pre-trained text encoder and template prompts to the trainable branch, then minimizing cosine distance between their embeddings to prevent overfitting. The final loss combines cross-entropy with a weighted consistency term (α·L_CC), and only 2–7% of total parameters are trainable, with the scaling factor λ controlling the contribution of adapter outputs to the residual stream.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization task, MSTA+LCC achieves harmonic mean (HM) scores of 72.0 (K-400), 66.3 (HMDB-51), 82.9 (UCF-101), and 18.9 (SSv2), setting new state-of-the-art on all four datasets while training only 8.7M parameters (2–7% of the full model). In fully-supervised Kinetics-400, MSTA+LCC reaches 82.2% Top-1 and 96.2% Top-5 accuracy, outperforming full fine-tuning of ViCLIP (79.9%/95.1%) with 14× fewer tunable parameters. For zero-shot transfer, the method attains 55.8% (HMDB-51), 78.7% (UCF-101), and 74.5% (K-600) Top-1 accuracy, surpassing OST by 0.9–3.0 points across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSTA+LCC outperforms full fine-tuning of ViCLIP on all four base-to-novel datasets (e.g., HMDB-51 HM 66.3 vs. 62.7; SSv2 HM 18.9 vs. 17.0) while using only 8.7M vs. 124.3M tunable parameters. Against OST (CVPR'24), the gains are most dramatic on HMDB-51 (HM 66.3 vs. 47.2, a +19.1 improvement) and SSv2 (HM 18.9 vs. 13.1, +37% relative), indicating that the consistency constraint effectively prevents the catastrophic forgetting that plagues full fine-tuning approaches. Compared to classic PEFT baselines AdaptFormer and LoRA applied to ViCLIP, MSTA+LCC consistently leads by 0.6–2.9 HM points, with the largest margin on SSv2 (18.9 vs. 17.0 and 16.0), confirming that the shared cross-modal projection and spatio-temporal up-projection layers capture video-specific structure that uni-modal adapters miss. In fully-supervised Kinetics-400, MSTA+LCC (82.2% Top-1) matches XCLIP (82.3%) and surpasses OST (82.0%) and ViCLIP (79.9%) with 14–17× fewer parameters. For few-shot learning, MSTA+LCC achieves 9 out of 12 best results across K=2/4/8/16 on three datasets, using only 10% of MoTE's parameter count (8.7M vs. 88M). In zero-shot transfer, the method leads all baselines on all three test datasets (HMDB-51, UCF-101, K-600), with the largest margin over ViCLIP on K-600 (74.5 vs. 71.5, +3.0 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ViCLIP (itself an extension of CLIP with spatiotemporal attention in the visual encoder) and departs from two prior lines of work. First, it moves beyond uni-modal PEFT methods (LoRA, AdaptFormer) by introducing a shared cross-modal projection layer that receives gradients from both branches, addressing the inter-modal alignment gap that independent adapters ignore. Second, it contrasts with video-specific fine-tuning approaches (XCLIP, ActionCLIP, ViFi-CLIP, OST, MoTE) that add large numbers of temporal modules and suffer catastrophic forgetting; MSTA instead uses only 2–7% trainable parameters with a spatio-temporal description-guided consistency constraint that distills knowledge from the frozen pre-trained encoder into the learnable adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides the first parameter-efficient transfer learning recipe specifically designed for ViCLIP, a model that prior CLIP-based methods cannot directly apply to. The consistency constraint leveraging LLM-generated spatio-temporal descriptions as a regularization signal is a practical technique that could transfer to other multi-modal foundation models. However, the gains over ViCLIP + AdaptFormer/LoRA are modest (e.g., +0.6 HM on base-to-novel), and the method has only been validated on action recognition tasks with ViTB/16; whether the shared-projection design and LLM-guided constraint generalize to other video understanding tasks (VQA, segmentation) or to larger model scales remains open.
