## Problem

Existing physics-based 3DGS methods (e.g., PhysGaussian, VR-GS) only support passive dynamics where objects respond to externally applied forces, leaving 3DGS scenes populated by lifeless, static objects. Active locomotion—where an object self-drives its motion through internal muscle contractions interacting with frictional contacts—remains unexplored for 3DGS, and the underlying inverse problem of computing muscle activations from a desired trajectory is an NP-hard contact/friction optimization.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a trained static 3DGS scene, a user-specified high-level motion trajectory (e.g., COM path or local point positions over time), and a segmentation plane (estimated from the scene via depth-map fitting) to extract the object of interest. It produces a physics-aware 4D animation of the object walking, jumping, or twisting, the per-frame muscle activation vector driving the motion, and an inpainted background where the removed object left a hole. The trajectory is intentionally low-dimensional (a few 3D points per frame) rather than full joint angles, and the segmentation plane serves dual roles as a cutting tool and a collision surface.

## Method

EnliveningGS introduces a hybrid mesh-Gaussian representation where a tetrahedral mesh provides global displacement via barycentric interpolation while bidirectional local embeddings (ellipsoid endpoints per Gaussian) capture per-kernel rotation and scaling, enabling accurate deformation and collision detection. Muscle fibers are embedded as piecewise-linear springs along the medial axis, and their activations are the unknowns to be solved. The core solver is a two-stage procedure: Stage 1 substitutes penalty-method spring forces for contact forces and integrates the dynamics with the previous frame's muscle activations to predict contact states; Stage 2 formulates a QP with LCP complementarity constraints (non-penetration, Coulomb friction) to find the optimal activation vector given those predicted states, using a pivoting scheme to handle mixed contact modes. A self-splitting mechanism divides Gaussian kernels when the angle between forward and reverse embedding rotations exceeds a threshold, preventing spiky artifacts under large deformation. Background inpainting leverages the segmentation plane's depth map as a geometric prior, reducing fine-tuning from ~10,000 iterations to ~100.

## Results

On the NeRF Synthetic dataset, EnliveningGS achieves the best rendering quality across all three deformation types (bending, twisting, falling), reaching PSNR of 28.79, 26.89, and 25.67 respectively, with corresponding SSIM of 0.923, 0.910, and 0.951 and LPIPS of 0.031, 0.041, and 0.032. The self-splitting mechanism provides consistent gains over the no-split variant (e.g., +0.17 PSNR on bending, +0.42 PSNR on twisting). For the locomotion solver, the two-stage LCP-penalty approach reduces average QP iterations from 46.19 (QPCC) to 17.01 while also lowering the average objective value from 1.29 to 0.83.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnliveningGS outperforms all three physics-aware GS baselines (SuGaR, PhysGaussian, VR-GS) on every metric across all three deformation types in Table 1. The closest competitor is VR-GS, which EnliveningGS beats by 0.16 PSNR on bending, 0.36 PSNR on twisting, and 0.36 PSNR on falling, with the largest gap in LPIPS (0.031 vs 0.038 on bending). SuGaR and PhysGaussian lag further behind, particularly on twisting where SuGaR's LPIPS of 0.101 is more than double EnliveningGS's 0.041. For the locomotion solver (Table 2), the two-stage LCP-penalty method reduces average iterations by 63% compared to QPCC (17.01 vs 46.19) and achieves a 36% lower average objective value (0.83 vs 1.29). For inpainting (Figure 7), EnliveningGS completes large-hole filling in ~20 seconds versus ~20 minutes for Gaussian Grouping, which still fails to fill the region after 10,000 fine-tuning iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in physics-aware 3DGS (PhysGaussian, VR-GS, GASP), where external forces passively drive deformation, by formulating an inverse problem: given a user-specified trajectory, compute the muscle activations that produce that motion. It builds on the 3DGS representation (Kerbl et al.) for scene encoding, the classical LCP formulation of Stewart and Trinkle for frictional contact, and InFusion for depth-based inpainting, but introduces a hybrid LCP-penalty solver and a bidirectional local embedding for Gaussian deformation that are absent from prior 3DGS physics work. The authors explicitly note that 'existing methods consider the simulation process as a forward process, leaving the inverse solving and control of physical parameters of objects on 3D GS as an unexplored area.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EnliveningGS is the first pipeline to enable active, muscle-driven locomotion in 3DGS scenes, opening a path toward interactive 3D content where objects behave as living creatures rather than passive deformable bodies. The hybrid LCP-penalty contact solver offers a practical route around the NP-hard combinatorial explosion of frictional contacts, and the plane-prior inpainting achieves a ~60× speedup over prior 3DGS inpainting for large occlusions. However, the method is currently limited to plane-approximated environments and simple rigid-like objects (stool, chess piece, Peashooter); extending to complex curved geometries, multi-object interactions, and real-time performance remain significant open questions.
