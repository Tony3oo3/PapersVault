## Problem

Existing white balance methods either perform global illuminant correction before the camera ISP pipeline (raw-WB) or apply end-to-end DNN correction directly on sRGB images without explicitly modeling global color distribution. This leaves a gap: sRGB-WB methods lack access to scene-level color temperature information that would guide more accurate, globally consistent correction, particularly under strong or mixed color casts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single sRGB image (H×W×3) as input, internally converting it to CIELab and extracting 256-bin per-channel histograms in both color spaces as auxiliary inputs. The primary output is a WB-corrected sRGB image of the same spatial dimensions, while the two auxiliary models simultaneously produce corrected sRGB and CIELab histograms (256×3 each). During training, 128×128 patches are randomly cropped from training images with geometric augmentation.

## Method

ABC-Former comprises three U-shaped transformers: two 1D auxiliary PDFformers that learn to predict corrected sRGB and CIELab histograms, and a 2D target sRGBformer that produces the final WB-corrected image. The key mechanism is the Interactive Channel Attention (ICA) module inserted in every sRGBformer block, which extracts channel-wise weighting vectors from the target image features and from the corresponding auxiliary PDFformer blocks, then combines them via learnable re-parameterization scalars (λ^sRGB, λ^Lab) to produce a fused reweighting vector applied to the image features. This allows the target model to adaptively incorporate calibrated global color knowledge from both histogram modalities at every encoder/decoder level. Training is multi-task: auxiliary models are supervised with L2 loss on predicted vs. ground-truth histograms, while the target model is trained with L1 pixel loss, all optimized jointly over 350 epochs.

## Results

On the Rendered WB Set1-Test (21,046 images), ABC-Former achieves a mean MSE of 20.47, MAE of 1.99°, and ΔE2000 of 2.18, substantially outperforming the next-best method WBFlow (MSE 78.89, MAE 2.67°, ΔE2000 3.13) by roughly 4× in MSE. On the more challenging Set2 (2,881 images), ABC-Former records MSE 104.31, MAE 3.39°, and ΔE2000 4.56, again leading all baselines. On the Rendered Cube+ dataset (10,242 images), ABC-Former achieves MSE 60.60, MAE 2.99°, and ΔE2000 3.95, with a model size of only 20.2 MB—comparable to KNN (21.8 MB) and far smaller than SWBNet (258.8 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABC-Former outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The most dramatic margin is against WBFlow [18], the strongest prior method: on Set1-Test, ABC-Former reduces MSE from 78.89 to 20.47 (≈74% reduction) and ΔE2000 from 3.13 to 2.18. Against KNN [3] and Deep-WB [2], ABC-Former also leads by large margins (e.g., MSE 20.47 vs. 77.49 and 82.55 on Set1-Test). Mixed-WB [5] and SWBNet [19] are the weakest baselines on Set1-Test (MSE 142.25 and 111.62 respectively), though SWBNet is competitive on Cube+ (MSE 74.35 vs. ABC-Former's 60.60). Notably, ABC-Former achieves these gains with a 20.2 MB model, smaller than WBFlow (30.2 MB) and far smaller than SWBNet (258.8 MB), while only slightly larger than KNN (21.8 MB) and Deep-WB (16.7 MB).

## Relation to Prior Work

ABC-Former extends the sRGB-WB line of work (Deep-WB, WBFlow, SWBNet, KNN, Mixed-WB) by introducing auxiliary histogram-based models that provide global color context absent from prior methods, which operate solely on sRGB pixel data. Conceptually, it adapts the cross-modal re-parameterization mechanism from M2PT [26], but departs from M2PT's use of irrelevant multimodal data by leveraging strongly correlated modalities (sRGB and CIELab histograms derived from the same input image). The U-shaped transformer backbone follows UFormer [25], and the Interactive Channel Attention module is a novel fusion mechanism that has no direct precedent in the WB literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The large performance gap over the strongest prior method (WBFlow) on rendered benchmarks suggests that global color distribution information, when properly integrated, is a significant missing ingredient in sRGB-WB correction. However, all evaluation is on rendered datasets with controlled color casts, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to real-world images with complex mixed lighting or sensor-specific artifacts. The authors note that extending to 2D histograms is a promising future direction, and the reliance on ground-truth WB images for supervision limits applicability to unsupervised or in-the-wild settings.
