## Problem

Existing human motion recovery methods are designed for ground-based activities (walking, running, dancing) and fail to capture the unique challenges of rock climbing: extreme off-ground poses, complex human-wall interactions, and the need for accurate global localization as climbers ascend. The field also lacks a large-scale, challenging 3D climbing dataset with global trajectories, as the only prior datasets (SPEED21, CIMI4D) are small and contain trivial motions from casual climbers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized RGB video (1080P, 20 FPS) and LiDAR point clouds (Ouster OS1, 128 beams, 20 FPS); the point cloud is first transformed from world to camera coordinates via an extrinsic matrix before feature extraction. It outputs SMPL pose and shape parameters in camera coordinates, global translation parameters in world coordinates, and 3D keypoints. The AscendMotion dataset additionally provides IMU MoCap ground truth (Xsens MVN, 17 sensors, 60 FPS) and high-resolution scene meshes (~80M points per scene) for evaluation.

## Method

ClimbingCap employs a three-stage pipeline. First, Separate Coordinate Decoding (SCD) extracts features via ViT (RGB) and PointNet++ (point cloud), then uses two iterative decoders to independently refine SMPL parameters in camera coordinates and global translation in world coordinates. Second, a post-processing stage applies three losses—limb weight differentiation (LWD), velocity direction smoothing (SDS), and velocity limb refinement (VLR)—to optimize poses using the rigid transform between camera and world coordinates enabled by LiDAR's 3D information. Third, a teacher-student semi-supervised framework leverages the large pool of unlabeled climbing data: the teacher (trained on labeled sequences) generates pseudo-labels to further train the student model. The key insight is that RGB and LiDAR modalities naturally decompose the problem into camera-space pose and world-space trajectory, which are then jointly optimized rather than estimated in a single coordinate frame.

## Results

On AscendMotion (horizontal/vertical scenes), ClimbingCap achieves MPJPE of 75.45/88.92 mm, WA-MPJPE of 62.95/85.26 mm, W-MPJPE of 78.99/106.95 mm, and RTE of 1.57/3.12 m, substantially outperforming all 9 baselines. The advantage is most dramatic in vertical scenes, where W-MPJPE is 106.95 mm versus 1442.50 mm for GVHMR (the best RGB global HMR method) and 1435.92 mm for LEIR (the best LiDAR+RGB method). On the external CIMI4D dataset, ClimbingCap also achieves the best MPJPE (84.03 mm), W-MPJPE (261.06 mm), and RTE (8.17 m), demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ClimbingCap outperforms all 9 baselines on nearly every metric in both horizontal and vertical scenes of AscendMotion. Among RGB-only global HMR methods, GVHMR is the closest competitor in camera-space metrics (MPJPE 107.09 vs. 75.45 in horizontal scenes) but degrades catastrophically in vertical scenes (W-MPJPE 1442.50 vs. 106.95), because GVHMR estimates movement direction via horizontal velocity, which is irrelevant for upward climbing. WHAM and SLAHMR show moderate camera-space accuracy but poor world-coordinate consistency (W-MPJPE > 600 mm in vertical scenes). LiDAR-only methods (LiDARCapV2, LiveHPS) and LiDAR+RGB fusion methods (ImmFusion, FusionPose, LEIR) all perform worse than ClimbingCap, with LEIR being the strongest fusion baseline (W-MPJPE 282.31/1435.92) yet still 3–13× worse than ClimbingCap in world coordinates. On the external CIMI4D dataset, ClimbingCap again leads on MPJPE (84.03 vs. 152.56 for GVHMR), W-MPJPE (261.06 vs. 476.90), and RTE (8.17 vs. 21.54), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

ClimbingCap extends the global HMR line of work (WHAM, GVHMR, TRACE, SLAHMR) from ground-based motions to the off-ground domain of rock climbing, where existing methods fail due to their reliance on horizontal-velocity assumptions and lack of scene-aware constraints. Architecturally, it repurposes the two-stage camera-then-global paradigm but introduces a key departure: RGB and LiDAR are assigned to separate coordinate decoders (camera vs. global) rather than being fused into a single pipeline, and a post-processing stage with scene-touch and velocity-smoothing losses enforces physical plausibility against the rock wall. The semi-supervised teacher-student training borrows from object detection literature to exploit the large pool of unlabeled climbing data in AscendMotion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AscendMotion fills a critical data gap: prior climbing datasets (CIMI4D, SPEED21) are small, feature casual climbers, and lack multi-modal coverage, leaving the community without a benchmark for challenging, skilled climbing motions. The separate-coordinate-decoding insight and scene-aware post-processing losses offer a practical recipe for any off-ground HMR task where the body interacts with a known 3D surface. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other off-ground activities (parkour, gymnastics), whether real-time inference is feasible with the LiDAR+RGB dual-branch architecture, and how performance scales with more diverse wall geometries and climbing styles beyond the 12 scenes captured.
