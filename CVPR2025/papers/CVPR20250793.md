## Problem

Existing palmprint generation methods (PCE-Palm, RPG-Palm) rely on Bezier curves for crease representation and conditional GANs, producing synthetic data that requires real-data fine-tuning to achieve competitive recognition performance. This gap stems from two issues: Bezier curves poorly match the statistical distribution of real palm creases, and simple random noise injection in GANs fails to balance intra-class variation with identity consistency.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 4-channel input to the UNet: the palm crease image (synthesized from polynomial curves sampled from estimated multivariate Gaussian distributions) concatenated channel-wise with the noisy palmprint image at each diffusion step. The output is a 112×112 synthetic palmprint image; by varying the K parameter in the noise-sharing sampling, the method produces datasets with tunable intra-class variation while maintaining a fixed identity condition per class. Default generation produces 2k identities × 20 samples per identity.

## Method

The method replaces Bezier curves with 4th-order polynomial creases (5 coefficients per line), whose distributions are estimated as multivariate Gaussians from 1000 manually labeled real palmprints, enabling realistic crease synthesis via Gaussian sampling. A conditional diffusion model (UNet with channel concatenation of the crease image) is trained with the standard denoising objective to generate palmprints conditioned on these polynomial creases. The key innovation is K-step noise-sharing sampling: during the reverse diffusion process, the same random noise vector is shared across all samples of the same identity for the last K of T=1000 steps, rather than using independent noise per sample. This exploits the observation that style information is established early in sampling while crease texture details are restored later, so sharing noise in the final K steps preserves identity-consistent crease structure while permitting stylistic diversity. Adjusting K (and a variance-scaling factor γ on the crease distribution) provides a continuous control over the intra-class variation/identity consistency trade-off.

## Results

Using TAR@FAR=1e-6 as the metric, Diff-Palm (2k IDs, 20 samples/ID, no fine-tuning) achieves an average of 0.7729 across five public datasets (CASIA, PolyU, Tongji, MPD, XJTU-UP), surpassing the real-data baseline (0.7546) for the first time in palmprint generation. With fine-tuning on real data, Diff-Palm reaches 0.8760 average, also exceeding PCE-Palm's fine-tuned result (0.8642). Performance scales with identity count, rising from 0.7729 (2k IDs) to 0.9026 (60k IDs) without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-Palm substantially outperforms PCE-Palm [20], the prior SOTA palmprint generator, by +21.7 points in average TAR@FAR=1e-6 without fine-tuning (0.7729 vs. 0.5561), and still leads by +1.2 points with fine-tuning (0.8760 vs. 0.8642). Against the face-generation baselines IDiff-Face [2] (0.5265) and Vec2Face [37] (0.5932), Diff-Palm gains +24.6 and +18.0 points respectively, confirming that palm-crease-conditioned diffusion is far more effective than identity-embedding or vector-conditioned approaches for this domain. Most notably, Diff-Palm trained purely on synthetic data (0.7729) exceeds the real-data-trained model (0.7546) by +1.8 points on average, a first for palmprint generation, with the largest margin on PolyU (+4.1 points) and Tongji (+2.5 points). The advantage over real data narrows on MPD (0.4643 vs. 0.3877, +7.7 points) where both methods struggle, suggesting MPD's domain shift remains challenging even for synthetic-trained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the palmprint generation line initiated by BezierPalm [44], RPG-Palm [33], and PCE-Palm [20], which all use Bézier curves as identity conditions fed into conditional GANs. It departs from this lineage in two key ways: replacing second-order Bézier curves with fourth-order polynomial curves whose coefficients are sampled from a statistically estimated multivariate Gaussian (grounded in manual annotation of 1000 real palmprints), and replacing the GAN backbone with a channel-concatenated conditional diffusion model. The K-step noise-sharing sampling mechanism is a novel addition not present in prior palmprint or face-generation diffusion work, though it draws on the general observation that per-step noise in diffusion sampling controls sample diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates for the first time that a palmprint recognition model trained exclusively on synthetic data (without any real-data fine-tuning) can match or exceed one trained on real data, closing a gap that prior GAN-based methods (PCE-Palm, RPG-Palm) could not bridge. The K-step noise-sharing sampling is framed as a plug-and-play module applicable to other conditional diffusion models, and the polynomial crease representation offers a reusable, statistically grounded alternative to hand-designed curve parameterizations. Limitations include reliance on a non-public 48k-image training set, evaluation confined to the palmprint domain, and modest absolute margins over real data on some datasets (e.g., MPD). Whether the noise-sharing strategy transfers effectively to higher-complexity modalities (faces, full-body biometrics) remains an open question.
