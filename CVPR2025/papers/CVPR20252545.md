## Problem

Existing learned image compression entropy models rely exclusively on hyper-prior and autoregressive architectures to capture internal dependencies within the latent representation, while neglecting the rich prior information about typical natural-image structures that is already present in the training data. This paper asks whether directly extracting and reusing such external priors can yield more accurate distribution estimation for entropy coding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252545/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a compressed bitstream (via entropy coding of quantized latents) plus a reconstructed image. Training uses 256×256 patches from OpenImages (300k images, short edge ≥256 px); evaluation is on Kodak (768×512), Tecnick, and CLIC Professional Validation sets. A key design constraint is that the learnable dictionary is shared between encoder and decoder, so it incurs zero additional transmission bitrate.

## Method

The core contribution is the Dictionary-based Cross Attention Entropy model (DCAE), which augments the standard hyper-prior + channel-wise autoregressive entropy model with an external prior. A learnable dictionary of 128 entries (640-dim) is trained to summarize typical natural-image textures and is shared between encoder and decoder. A Multi-Scale Features Aggregation module stacks efficient convolutions (1×1 linear + 3×3 depthwise) to build features with progressively larger receptive fields, then applies spatial attention to weight positions. These multi-scale features serve as query tokens in a cross-attention operation where the dictionary provides keys and values, yielding a dictionary feature that is concatenated with the hyper-prior and autoregressive features before the entropy module estimates (μ, σ). The encoder/decoder uses Swin transformer blocks with ResScale depth scaling and ConvGLU FFNs, with channel counts increasing across stages (96→144→256) to shift computation to lower resolutions for faster inference.

## Results

The proposed DCAE model achieves BD-rate improvements of -17.0% (Kodak), -21.1% (Tecnick), and -19.7% (CLIC) over the VVC anchor, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmark datasets. In terms of efficiency, the model runs at 193 ms total latency (93 ms encoding, 100 ms decoding) with 252/305 GFLOPs for encoder/decoder, substantially faster than the previous best BD-rate method MLIC++ (772 ms) while delivering 1.9–4.2 percentage points better compression. The model uses 119.2M parameters, comparable to other top-performing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCAE outperforms all nine compared baselines on all three datasets in BD-rate. Against the previous best BD-rate method MLIC++ (ICML'23), DCAE improves by 1.9 pp on Kodak (-17.0% vs -15.1%), 2.5 pp on Tecnick (-21.1% vs -18.6%), and 2.8 pp on CLIC (-19.7% vs -16.9%), while reducing latency from 772 ms to 193 ms (≈4× faster). Compared to the most recent NeurIPS'24 method CCA, DCAE gains 3.3 pp on Kodak, 5.8 pp on Tecnick, and 5.2 pp on CLIC at a similar latency (193 ms vs 223 ms). Against the CVPR'23 TCM baseline, DCAE improves BD-rate by 5.2 pp on Kodak and 9.1 pp on Tecnick/CLIC while being 100 ms faster. The method also surpasses the ICLR'24 FTIC by 2.4 pp on Kodak and 6.0 pp on Tecnick, and the ECCV'24 WeConvene by 8.5 pp on Kodak, confirming consistent superiority across the full range of recent learned image compression approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learned image compression (LIC) framework by introducing an external prior into the entropy model, departing from the dominant paradigm of modeling only internal dependencies among latent representations. It builds directly on the hyper-prior architecture (Ballé et al.), the autoregressive context model (Minnen et al.), and the channel-wise autoregressive formulation with latent residual prediction (He et al.), augmenting all three with a shared learnable dictionary queried via cross-attention. Conceptually, it repurposes dictionary learning—well established in image restoration and generation—as a mechanism for entropy estimation, distinguishing itself from the global-token approach (Kim et al.) by using a larger, shared dictionary that requires no transmission overhead. The encoder/decoder backbone follows recent Swin Transformer–based LIC designs, incorporating ResScale and Convolutional Gated Linear Unit for enhanced nonlinear transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly extracting structural priors from training data via a shared dictionary can meaningfully complement internal dependency modeling in entropy estimation, yielding SOTA BD-rate (−17.0% on Kodak) at roughly 4× lower latency than the previous best (MLIC++). The shared-dictionary design is practically attractive because it adds no transmission cost, making it a drop-in enhancement for existing LIC pipelines. Open questions include whether the dictionary generalizes well to specialized or out-of-distribution image domains (it is trained on OpenImages), whether the approach extends naturally to video compression where temporal priors dominate, and whether the 128-entry dictionary size is near-optimal or whether larger dictionaries with sparser attention could capture rarer structures.
