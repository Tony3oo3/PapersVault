## Problem

Existing single-image-to-3D mesh pipelines suffer from three coupled defects: pixel misalignment across diffusion-generated multiview images causes ghosting, the reconstructed mesh often deviates in shape from the input image, and the final mesh coloration is blurry because LRM-based reconstruction does not effectively transfer image colors to mesh vertices. Prior consistency fixes (cross-view attention, sampling schedules) are indirect and uncontrollable due to the black-box nature of diffusion models, leaving residual inconsistency that propagates into the mesh.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (1024×1024 in experiments, rendered at elevation 0° and azimuth −30° to provide depth cues) and produces a colored 3D mesh normalized to the [−1, 1]³ bounding box. The full pipeline runs in approximately 62 seconds on an NVIDIA A100 GPU with ~21 GB peak memory, broken down as 6 s multiview diffusion, 4 s LRM, 27 s geometry refinement, 10 s appearance enhancement, and 15 s fidelity enhancement. Both enhancement modules are plug-and-play at inference time and can be attached to any existing single-image-to-3D backbone without retraining.

## Method

Fancy123 is a three-step inference-time pipeline: (1) a multiview diffusion model (Zero123++-based) generates multiview images, which feed an LRM (InstantMesh) to produce an initial mesh refined with normal-map optimization; (2) an appearance enhancement module optimizes per-view 2D grid-based deformation fields (G×G offsets) so that deformed multiview images, when unprojected onto the mesh and re-rendered, match the deformed images—eliminating ghosting from multiview inconsistency; (3) a fidelity enhancement module estimates the input image's camera pose via coarse-to-fine LPIPS search, then optimizes a per-face Jacobian field (initialized from the mesh's own gradients and solved via a Poisson equation) to deform the mesh so its rendering matches the input, after which the input image is unprojected onto the deformed mesh for lossless color transfer. The key insight is that explicit unprojection of clear source images onto the mesh—enabled by the two deformation steps—replaces LRM's blurry predicted colors and is the primary driver of visual sharpness.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects dataset (100 objects), Fancy123 achieves the best score on 6 out of 7 metrics: FID 37.99, LPIPS 0.330, PSNR 14.37, SSIM 0.651, CLIP-Similarity 0.835, and F-Score 0.9064 (tied with CRM). The only metric where it does not lead is Chamfer Distance (0.0151), where SF3D is slightly better (0.0142). The authors note that these quantitative metrics do not reliably reflect human perceptual quality and emphasize visual comparisons as the primary evaluation criterion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fancy123 outperforms all six baselines on all five appearance metrics (FID, LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM, CLIP-Similarity), with the largest margins over LGM and CRM in FID (37.99 vs. 86.24/87.74) and over Unique3D in PSNR (14.37 vs. 12.74). On geometry, Fancy123 ties CRM on F-Score (0.9064) and is second only to SF3D on Chamfer Distance (0.0151 vs. 0.0142), while substantially outperforming Unique3D (CD 0.0266, F-Score 0.8966). Compared to its closest backbone InstantMesh, Fancy123 improves FID by 17.6% (46.16→37.99), LPIPS by 5.4% (0.349→0.330), and CLIP-Similarity by 4.4% (0.800→0.835), while maintaining comparable geometry (CD 0.0151 vs. 0.0153). The authors caution that these metrics poorly correlate with human perception and that visual inspection (Fig. 4) is more informative: only Fancy123 and Unique3D produce sharp, non-blurry coloration, but Unique3D exhibits artifacts on several samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fancy123 sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) single-image-to-3D pipelines built on multiview diffusion + LRM (InstantMesh, Zero123++), (2) post-hoc multiview consistency correction, and (3) Jacobian-field-based mesh deformation from the shape-editing literature (APAP, Neural Jacobian Fields). The key conceptual departure is from prior multiview-consistency work (MVDream, SyncDreamer, ConsistNet, Cycle3D), which attempts to enforce consistency *inside* the diffusion model via architecture or training; Fancy123 instead treats the generated multiview images as fixed and corrects misalignment *after* generation via explicit 2D grid deformation, making the fix plug-and-play at inference time. The unprojection-based color mapping builds directly on Unique3D's observation, but the paper argues this operation was underappreciated and that the deformation steps are what make unprojection safe (avoiding ghosting and mismatch artifacts).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, inference-time nature of both enhancement modules means they can be dropped into any existing single-image-to-3D backbone (demonstrated with TripoSR, Unique3D, and LGM), which has broad practical value for practitioners who want quality improvements without retraining. The paper also raises a legitimate concern that standard appearance metrics (PSNR, SSIM) poorly reflect human perception for this task due to the inherent ambiguity of unseen views, suggesting the field needs better evaluation protocols. Limitations include dependence on the underlying multiview diffusion model's quality and occasional artifacts when semantically distinct parts share similar colors; the authors note semantic guidance and large-scale scene extension as open directions.
