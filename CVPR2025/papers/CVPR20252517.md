## Problem

Existing STEM image enhancement datasets use random noise parameters that do not match real STEM images, particularly failing to capture scan noise that scales with image gradients. These datasets also cover only regular atomic patterns in HAADF mode, excluding defects, embeddings, interfaces, and BF mode. Furthermore, prior enhancement networks operate solely in the spatial domain, ignoring the strong periodicity prior available in the frequency domain of atomically arranged structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252517/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 noisy grayscale STEM images (HAADF or BF mode) and produces a denoised, enhanced version of the same size. The noise model decomposes into three calibrated components: smooth background (Perlin noise), gradient-dependent scan noise, and pixel-independent pointwise noise (Tukey's lambda distribution). The same trained network can be repurposed for atom detection (outputting position labels) and atom super-resolution (reducing apparent atomic radius) by changing the training target.

## Method

The paper first calibrates noise parameters from 40 real STEM images containing atoms by statistically fitting histograms: scan noise standard deviation is linearly regressed against local gradient amplitude, and pointwise noise is modeled with a Tukey's lambda distribution whose standard deviation scales with pixel brightness. Using these calibrated parameters, a general dataset is synthesized with 15 material structures, random atomic deletions/additions/positions, and both HAADF and BF modes. The SFIN network splits shallow features into parallel spatial and frequency branches; each Spatial-Frequency Interactive Block applies a standard convolution and a Frequency Convolution Module (FFT → conv → BN → ReLU → IFFT) respectively, then exchanges information between branches via two additional convolutions before summing. This design lets the network exploit periodicity in the frequency domain while retaining spatial flexibility for irregular features like defects and interfaces.

## Results

On the TEMImageNet HAADF benchmark, SFIN achieves 31.76 dB PSNR and 0.9543 SSIM, surpassing the previous best FCN (26.18 dB / 0.8842) by 5.6 dB. On the authors' own dataset (HAADF mode), SFIN reaches 38.48 dB / 0.9582 SSIM, and on BF mode it reaches 33.24 dB / 0.9885 SSIM, both outperforming FCN by ~1–2 dB. In the secondary atom-detection task, SFIN scores 28.67 dB / 0.9642 SSIM (HAADF), and in atom super-resolution it achieves 41.74 dB / 0.9933 SSIM, substantially ahead of FCN (32.11 dB / 0.8943).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFIN outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the two traditional methods (bilateral filtering / ABSF and Wiener filter), SFIN gains roughly 15–23 dB in PSNR, confirming that classical filters cannot handle the structured scan noise in STEM images. Against the two deep-learning baselines, SFIN beats FCN [7] by 5.6 dB on TEMImageNet (31.76 vs 26.18 PSNR), by 2.0 dB on the authors' HAADF dataset (38.48 vs 36.50), and by 1.0 dB on BF mode (33.24 vs 32.22). The gap over AtomSegNet [9] is even larger: 9.4 dB on TEMImageNet, 4.2 dB on HAADF, and 3.0 dB on BF. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that removing the frequency-domain branch drops PSNR from 38.48 to 34.61, and removing the spatial–frequency interaction drops it to 38.38, confirming that both the frequency convolution and cross-domain information exchange contribute to the gains over purely spatial CNNs like FCN and AtomSegNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the physics-based noise calibration framework from natural image denoising (Wei et al., 2020/2021) to STEM imaging, with a key modification: because STEM scan noise amplitude is proportional to local image gradient, calibration must be performed on images containing atomic features rather than flat-field frames. For data synthesis, it builds on the abTEM rendering library and extends the TEMImageNet dataset by adding BF mode images and randomized atomic arrangements (defects, embeddings, interfaces). The enhancement network departs from purely spatial-domain CNNs (AtomSegNet, FCN) by introducing frequency-domain convolutions with cross-domain interaction, motivated by the strong periodicity of atomic lattices in the Fourier domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The calibrated dataset addresses a practical gap: prior synthetic STEM datasets used random noise parameters that did not match real instrument behavior, limiting the transferability of trained models to actual microscopy data. The inclusion of BF mode and random atomic arrangements broadens applicability to light-atom observation and defect/interface analysis. However, calibration relies on only 40 real images at 256×256 resolution, and the frequency-domain benefit may diminish for highly disordered or amorphous samples where periodicity is absent. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other electron microscopy modalities (e.g., EELS, EBSD) and whether larger, more diverse calibration sets would further close the synthetic-to-real gap.
