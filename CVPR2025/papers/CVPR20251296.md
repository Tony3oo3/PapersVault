## Problem

Existing Transformer-based HSI restoration methods rely on uni-dimensional self-attention (spatial or channel) that compresses the 3D hyperspectral cube into a 2D similarity matrix, losing the joint spatial-spectral interaction. This limits the model's ability to capture comprehensive correlations across all three dimensions of the image cube, particularly given the hundreds or thousands of spectral channels in HSI data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251296/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded HSI of size h×w×D (low-resolution or noisy) and an auxiliary RGB image of size h×w×3, which is spectrally aligned to D bands via a data alignment operation before joint training. For restoration tasks, it outputs a restored HSI at the target resolution H×W×D; for classification, it outputs per-pixel class labels. The model is trained simultaneously on both HSI and RGBI datasets (e.g., CAVE/Pavia/Chikusei with DIV2K, or ICVL with RENOIR) to leverage the larger RGBI data as an auxiliary task.

## Method

VolFormer's core contribution is Volumetric Self-Attention (VolSA), which implicitly models 3D cube attention by projecting the input into two 2D similarity matrices—a spectral-wise projection (C×C) and a spatial-wise projection (HW×HW)—then computing their cross-covariance to obtain the full volumetric attention map. This decomposition reduces complexity from cubic O(H²W²C²) to quadratic O(H²W²+C²). A spectrum location prior, implemented as a bidirectional exponential decay matrix D_nm = γ^|n−m|, is element-wise multiplied into the spectral attention to encode wavelength proximity, so closer spectral bands receive higher correlation weights. The architecture follows a residual-in-residual design with multiple Transformer blocks (each containing VolSA + FFN with depth-wise 3×3 convolutions), trained end-to-end with L1 and spatial-spectral total variation losses. VolSA is applied with shifted-window operations and multi-head attention (g heads) to balance local and global context.

## Results

On the CAVE dataset for ×4 super-resolution, VolFormer achieves MPSNR 40.205 dB, MSSIM 0.9663, SAM 3.083°, and ERGAS 2.856, outperforming the previous best (DSTrans) by 0.13 dB in MPSNR and 0.086° in SAM. On Pavia ×4, it reaches MPSNR 32.925 dB and SAM 6.398°, surpassing ESSA by 0.12 dB and 0.78° respectively. For denoising on ICVL at σ=30, VolFormer attains MPSNR 43.551 dB and MSSIM 0.9938, leading SST by 0.50 dB. On the HU2013 classification benchmark, VolFormer achieves 97.35% overall accuracy, exceeding HybridFormer (96.40%) by nearly 1 percentage point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For super-resolution, VolFormer outperforms all seven SR baselines (GDRRN, MCNet, ERCSR, SSPSR, HSISR, DSTrans, ESSA) across all six metrics on both CAVE and Pavia at both ×4 and ×8 scales; the closest competitor is DSTrans [56], which VolFormer surpasses by 0.13 dB MPSNR on CAVE ×4 and 0.17 dB on CAVE ×8. For denoising, VolFormer beats all eight denoising baselines (BM4D, KBR, WLRTR, NGmeet, HSID-CNN, QRNN3D, DPPR, SST) on both synthetic (ICVL) and real-world (HSIDwRD) noise; the nearest competitor SST [24] is outperformed by 0.50 dB MPSNR at σ=30 and by 0.28 dB on HSIDwRD. Traditional methods (BM4D, KBR, WLRTR, NGmeet) lag far behind deep learning approaches, with BM4D achieving only 18.59 dB vs. 43.55 dB for VolFormer at σ=30. For classification on HU2013, VolFormer achieves 97.35% OA, surpassing HybridFormer [36] (96.40%) by 0.95 points and all other baselines (SVM, DBMA, DBDA, SSRN, SSFTT, BS2T, CS2DT) by 1–10 points; the largest gap is over SVM (87.47%) at nearly 10 points. Notably, VolFormer also maintains lower FLOPs (90.15G) and fewer parameters (5.10M) than the combined SSA+CSA baseline (93.60G, 5.24M) while delivering higher performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

VolFormer extends the Transformer-based HSI restoration line initiated by DSTrans [56] and ESSA [62], which employ uni-dimensional self-attention (spatial or channel) to capture long-range dependencies. The key conceptual departure is the argument that mapping 3D cube interactions onto 2D similarity matrices inherently compresses spatial-spectral joint information, and the proposed volumetric self-attention (VolSA) addresses this by decomposing full 3D attention into two 2D projections (spectral-wise and spatial-wise) whose cross-covariance recovers cube-level interactions at quadratic rather than cubic cost. The spectrum location prior is adapted from the retention mechanism [41] in sequence modeling, repurposed to encode the physical property that adjacent spectral bands in a continuous narrowband HSI are more correlated than distant ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cubic-to-quadratic complexity reduction makes volumetric (3D) self-attention practically feasible for HSI with hundreds or thousands of spectral bands, removing a key barrier to applying full cube-level attention in HSI restoration. The consistent but modest gains (0.1–0.2 dB over ESSA/DSTrans) across SR, denoising, and classification suggest the 3D interaction modeling captures genuinely useful spatial-spectral correlations that 2D attention misses. However, the improvements are incremental, the classification results show notable per-class inconsistencies (e.g., class 6 accuracy drops from 88.70% to 97.07%), and the method still relies on shifted-window partitioning, leaving open whether the volumetric formulation scales to very large spatial extents or benefits from global (non-windowed) attention.
