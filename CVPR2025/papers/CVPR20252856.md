## Problem

Generic VLMs perform poorly on remote sensing data due to its complex geospatial, spectral, and temporal dimensions, while existing geospatial VLMs are restricted to fixed resolutions and a limited set of sensor modalities (typically optical only). No prior model or dataset simultaneously supports multi-spectral, multi-temporal, and multi-resolution Earth observation imagery across a broad range of downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252856/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EarthDial accepts heterogeneous visual inputs at varying spatial resolutions (0.5 m aerial to 30 m satellite), including RGB, SAR (Sentinel-1), multispectral (Sentinel-2, Landsat-8), NIR/infrared, and hyperspectral (RGB + methane band) imagery, as well as bi-temporal or multi-temporal image sequences (up to 4 frames). The adaptive high-resolution module tiles inputs into 448×448-pixel patches (1–12 tiles in training, up to 40 at inference) alongside a global thumbnail. Outputs are natural-language dialogues structured per task: class labels, rotated bounding boxes [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max, θ], image/region captions, VQA answers, grounding descriptions, change-detection narratives, and disaster-assessment verdicts.

## Method

EarthDial builds on InternVL with a lightweight InternViT-300M visual encoder, an MLP projector, and a Phi-3-mini LLM (~4 B parameters total). Two key modules extend the architecture: (1) an Adaptive High Resolution block that dynamically selects an aspect ratio, splits the image into 448×448 tiles, and appends a global thumbnail to preserve fine-grained detail at arbitrary resolutions; and (2) a Data Fusion block that processes multi-spectral inputs by iteratively encoding three channels at a time through the ViT, reducing token count via bilinear interpolation (AnyRes), and for temporal sequences stacking and concatenating per-frame visual tokens before feeding the LLM. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: Stage 1 pretrains all components on 7.6 M RGB image-text pairs for sensor-to-language alignment; Stage 2 fine-tunes the MLP and LLM on 1.8 M RGB and temporal instruction pairs for task-specific capabilities; Stage 3 introduces the Data Fusion module and fine-tunes on 2.4 M multispectral and SAR instruction pairs. The full EarthDial-Instruct dataset comprises over 11.11 M instruction pairs spanning six visual modalities and fourteen task types, making it the largest remote-sensing instruction-tuning resource to date.

## Results

On scene classification, EarthDial achieves 88.76% on AID, 92.42% on UCMerced, 96.21% on WHU-19, 68.82% on BigEarthNet, 96.37% on xBD Set 1, and 70.03% on FMoW, outperforming GPT-4o by 14–49 points across these benchmarks. For multi-spectral and SAR tasks, EarthDial reaches 69.94% on BigEarthNet-MS and 60.72% on SoSAT-LCZ42 (vs. 49% and 15.53% for GPT-4o), and achieves mAP@0.5 of 39.07 on GeoChat-Instruct referred object detection. On image captioning, EarthDial attains ROUGE-1 of 45.84 on NWPU RESISC45 and 49.39 on Sydney Captions, roughly 2–3× the scores of GPT-4o and GeoChat. For change detection, EarthDial scores 74.8 METEOR on LEVIR MCI multi-temporal, far exceeding GeoChat (12.35) and GPT-4o (22.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EarthDial decisively outperforms GPT-4o across nearly all evaluated tasks: on scene classification it leads by 14–49 points (e.g., 96.37 vs. 67.95 on xBD, 70.03 vs. 21.43 on FMoW), on image captioning it achieves 2–3× the ROUGE-1 scores (45.84 vs. 19.43 on RESISC45), and on multi-spectral classification it gains 32.5% average improvement (69.94 vs. 49 on BigEarthNet-MS). Against the domain-specific GeoChat, EarthDial wins on classification (e.g., 96.21 vs. 80.09 on WHU-19), captioning (45.84 vs. 14.86 ROUGE-1), change detection (74.8 vs. 12.35 METEOR on LEVIR MCI), and referred object detection (39.07 vs. 21.7 mAP@0.5 on GeoChat-Instruct), though GeoChat retains a slight edge on region captioning for some SAR datasets (e.g., 65.02 vs. 72.14 on NWPU VHR-10 where EarthDial actually wins). Compared to InternVL2-4B/8B (generic VLMs), EarthDial dominates on RS-specific tasks but InternVL2-4B marginally edges out on one detection subtask (25.48 vs. 23.12 mAP on NWPU VHR-10 referred detection). On VQA, EarthDial leads Qwen-VL (92.70 vs. 55.35 avg on RSVQA-LRBEN), LHRS-Bot (92.70 vs. 89.19), and EarthGPT (72.45 vs. 72.06 on RSVQA-HRBEN), with the HRBEN gap being narrow. Overall, EarthDial is the clear winner on 40+ of the 44 evaluated downstream tasks, with the most dramatic margins on multi-spectral, SAR, and temporal tasks where generic models struggle most.

## Relation to Prior Work

EarthDial builds directly on the InternVL architecture (InternViT-300M vision encoder + MLP projector + LLM), replacing the larger LLM with the lightweight Phi-3-mini to create a 4B-parameter model. It departs from prior geospatial VLMs (GeoChat, SkyEyeGPT, LHRS-Bot, EarthGPT) that were restricted to optical imagery or a limited set of modalities, by introducing a data fusion module and adaptive high-resolution tiling to unify multi-spectral, multi-temporal, and multi-resolution inputs within a single conversational framework. The three-stage training recipe (RGB pretraining → RGB+temporal finetuning → multi-spectral+SAR finetuning) is a deliberate progression absent in prior RS-VLM work, which typically trained on a single modality mix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.11M instruction dataset (6× larger than prior RS instruction sets) and the first unified model handling multi-spectral, multi-temporal, and multi-resolution EO data in one conversational interface provide a practical foundation for environmental monitoring, disaster response, and resource management applications. However, absolute performance on fine-grained localization tasks (grounding description mAP often below 20%) remains modest, and the 4B parameter budget may limit capacity for more complex reasoning. Open questions include whether the multi-stage recipe generalizes to truly novel sensor combinations, how the model handles real-time or streaming satellite data, and whether the instruction-tuning approach can be replaced by more sample-efficient alignment methods.
