## Problem

Pixel-based Gaussian representations for feed-forward sparse-view reconstruction require large cross-view overlap for accurate depth estimation and fail under object occlusions and frustum truncations. Ego-centric driving scenarios, where cameras are rigidly mounted around a vehicle with minimal overlap (<15%) and frequent occlusions, expose these limitations, yet no prior method addresses this setting. Volume-based representations mitigate occlusion and truncation issues but are spatially bounded and lack fine detail, creating a complementary gap that neither representation alone can fill.

## Input / Output

The method takes K=6 surrounding RGB images at 224×400 resolution captured in a single frame from cameras mounted around a vehicle, with only adjacent cameras sharing small overlap regions. It outputs a combined set of Omni-Gaussians (volume-based Gaussians anchored to a 192×192×16 voxel grid spanning [-50m, -50m, -3m, 50m, 50m, 12m] around the vehicle, plus pixel-based Gaussians unprojected along rays to arbitrary distances), from which novel-view images and depth maps are rendered via rasterization. Optionally, a 2D diffusion model (MagicDrive) can synthesize the six input images from text descriptions and 3D layouts, enabling text-to-3D scene generation.

## Method

Omni-Scene introduces an Omni-Gaussian representation that fuses two complementary branches: a Volume Builder that lifts 2D features into 3D via a Triplane Transformer (using cross-image and cross-plane deformable attentions to avoid dependence on cross-view overlap) and a Volume Decoder that predicts voxel-anchored Gaussians, and a Pixel Decorator that uses a Multi-View U-Net with patchified cross-view attentions and Plücker ray embeddings to predict per-pixel Gaussians along camera rays. Two collaboration mechanisms enable the branches to complement each other: Projection-Based Feature Fusion projects pixel-Gaussian features onto the triplane so the Volume Builder is aware of areas already covered, and Depth-Guided Training Decomposition applies masked photometric and depth losses so the volume-based Gaussians focus on the bounded volume range while pixel-based Gaussians handle distant elements. The Pixel Decoder initializes per-pixel depths using Metric3D v2 predictions rather than learning from scratch, and the overall loss combines full-Gaussian photometric terms (MSE + LPIPS) with volume-specific masked terms including a scale-alignment L1 depth loss between the two branches.

## Results

On the ego-centric nuScenes task, Omni-Scene achieves 24.01 PSNR, 0.733 SSIM, 0.238 LPIPS, and 0.804 PCC, substantially outperforming pixelSplat (21.51 PSNR, 0.001 PCC) and MVSplat (21.61 PSNR, 0.181 PCC) by 2.4 dB PSNR and a large margin in geometric quality. On the scene-centric RealEstate10K task, Omni-Scene reaches 26.19 PSNR, 0.865 SSIM, 0.131 LPIPS, and 0.368 PCC, matching or exceeding all prior methods in geometric quality (PCC) while remaining competitive in visual quality. The PCC metric (Pearson Correlation Coefficient against Depth Anything V2 predictions) is the most discriminative, showing that pixel-based methods nearly fail on ego-centric scenes due to lack of cross-view overlap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the ego-centric nuScenes task, Omni-Scene decisively beats both pixel-based baselines: it gains +2.40 dB PSNR over pixelSplat and +2.40 dB over MVSplat, while PCC jumps from 0.001 (pixelSplat) and 0.181 (MVSplat) to 0.804, indicating that pixel-based methods essentially fail to recover geometry when cross-view overlap is minimal. On the scene-centric RealEstate10K task, Omni-Scene is competitive with the strongest baseline MVSplat (26.39 PSNR, 0.128 LPIPS) at 26.19 PSNR and 0.131 LPIPS, a small visual-quality gap, but achieves the best PCC (0.368 vs. 0.363 for MVSplat and 0.344 for MuRF). Against the light-field method AttnRend, Omni-Scene surpasses it by 1.41 dB PSNR and 0.045 SSIM while also providing interpretable 3D geometry (PCC) that AttnRend cannot produce. Overall, the paper's method wins all four comparisons on the ego-centric task and ties or wins on the scene-centric task, with the largest margins appearing in geometric accuracy (PCC) rather than photometric quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its core rendering representation but departs fundamentally from prior feed-forward reconstruction methods (pixelSplat, MVSplat) that rely exclusively on pixel-based Gaussians requiring large cross-view overlap for depth estimation. The key architectural departure is borrowing volume-based representations (triplane + deformable attention) from the 3D perception literature (BEVFormer, OccFormer, TPV) and fusing them with the pixel-based path to handle occlusions and frustum truncations that are inherent to ego-centric camera rigs. The method also integrates DINO-pretrained ResNet50 features, Plücker ray embeddings for camera conditioning, and a 2D diffusion model (MagicDrive) for multi-modal scene generation, positioning itself at the intersection of feed-forward 3D reconstruction and generative scene synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes ego-centric sparse-view reconstruction as a practical task for autonomous driving, where the minimal cross-view overlap (<15%) and frequent occlusions/truncations make prior scene-centric methods fundamentally inapplicable. The dramatic PCC gap (0.804 vs. 0.181 for MVSplat) underscores that the failure of pixel-based methods in this regime is not a tuning issue but a representational limitation. Open questions include handling dynamic scenes (the method is single-frame), scaling the bounded volume to larger environments, and whether the triplane-based volume encoding can be replaced by more efficient implicit representations. The integration with 2D diffusion models for text-to-3D driving scene generation opens a path toward controllable synthetic data generation for autonomous driving.
