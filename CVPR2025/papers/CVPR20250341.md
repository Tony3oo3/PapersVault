## Problem

Existing Scene Text Retrieval (STR) methods depend on explicit OCR pipelines (text spotting or localization), which introduce accumulated errors, complex architectures, and require large-scale synthetic pre-training plus full fine-tuning of tens of millions of parameters. While CLIP models have demonstrated the ability to perceive scene text, adapting them to STR at the higher input resolutions needed for small text, and achieving fine-grained cross-modal alignment without additional learnable layers, remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query string (wrapped in a quotation-mark prompt template) and a gallery of scene images as input. Images are resized to expanded resolutions beyond CLIP's native size (e.g., 512×512 for RN50, 640×640 for RN50x16) via position embedding interpolation, and ViT-based models additionally use 2×2 image splitting. The output is a two-stage result: a ranked list of all gallery images by contrastive similarity (retrieval), followed by refined binary match/non-match scores for the top-K (K=32) images produced by a linear classifier (reranking).

## Method

CAYN adapts frozen CLIP encoders (ResNet or ViT) to STR with at most 0.50M trainable parameters. A Visual Position Adapter (VPA) is inserted after convolutional features are flattened into tokens but before attention layers, using a bottleneck MLP with a learnable scale-factor projection and residual connection to compensate for positional information lost during embedding interpolation. For reranking, a Visual Context Dropout (VCD) technique removes attention in the final encoder layers, degenerating them to a value-projection linear transform that yields locally aligned visual token features. A Parameter-Free Cross-Attention (PFCA) mechanism then aggregates these local tokens using the global text feature as query (and symmetrically, local text tokens using the global image feature), transferring the pre-trained contrastive alignment to the token level without any additional parameters. A single linear classifier on the concatenated enhanced features predicts the image-text matching score, and the retrieval and reranking losses are jointly optimized on the MLT5k dataset.

## Results

CAYN-RN50x16 achieves state-of-the-art mAP of 92.46% on SVT, 89.49% on IIIT-STR, and 85.98% on TTR, while running at 38.79 FPS. The method requires only 0.45M additional trainable parameters (vs. 20M+ for the closest CLIP-based competitor FDP) and needs only transcription-level supervision (no localization labels). The lighter CAYN-RN50 variant runs at 80.13 FPS with 85.05%/82.88%/74.04% mAP, and CAYN-RN50x4 offers a balanced 88.94%/85.77%/81.90% mAP at 53.22 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAYN-RN50x16 outperforms the strongest prior CLIP-based method FDP-L [49] by 2.83% mAP on SVT (92.46 vs. 89.63) and 6.80% on TTR (85.98 vs. 79.18), while running 3× faster (38.79 vs. 11.82 FPS) and using 73× fewer trainable parameters (0.45M vs. 33.45M). Against the best localization-based method Wen et al. [41], CAYN-RN50x16 leads by 1.51% on SVT, 12.09% on IIIT-STR, and 5.89% on TTR. CAYN also surpasses TDSL [39] by 3.08%/12.40%/11.23% mAP on SVT/IIIT-STR/TTR, and outperforms all spotting-based baselines (Mask TextSpotter v3 [20], ABCNet [21], Jaderberg et al. [12]) by large margins on every dataset where they report results. Notably, CAYN requires only transcription-level supervision (T) whereas all competing methods require both localization and transcription labels (L+T), and the zero-shot EXCLIP baseline (no training) already reaches 81.60% on SVT, confirming CLIP's inherent scene-text perception capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP contrastive pretraining paradigm and the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) literature (adapters, LoRA, prompt learning), but departs sharply from prior scene text retrieval (STR) methods that rely on explicit localization or OCR pipelines. The closest prior work, FDP [49], also leverages CLIP for STR but retains a segmentation head for text localization and introduces over 20M trainable parameters; CAYN eliminates both, using a 0.20–0.45M visual position adapter and a parameter-free cross-attention mechanism instead. The paper also extends the empirical study of CLIP's zero-shot scene text perception (position embedding interpolation, prompt design) into a systematic baseline before introducing its architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that CLIP's pre-trained contrastive alignment is sufficient for fine-grained token-level cross-modal interaction without any learned attention parameters, the paper challenges the assumption that explicit localization or parameterized cross-attention layers are necessary for accurate scene text retrieval. The practical impact is significant: a strictly OCR-free pipeline with under 0.5M trainable parameters and 3× faster inference than the prior CLIP-based SOTA removes a major engineering burden for deployment. Open questions remain around ViT-based CLIP backbones, which still underperform ResNet variants even with splitting strategies, and whether the parameter-free cross-attention insight generalizes to other fine-grained vision-language tasks beyond STR.
