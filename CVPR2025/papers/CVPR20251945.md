## Problem

Existing dual-stream ultra-high resolution (UHR) segmentation methods pursue one of three objectives—dual-stream ensemble, selective zoom, or complementary learning—through separate complex pipelines, leading to computational redundancy and limited joint performance in accuracy and inference cost. The paper identifies that all three objectives are fundamentally driven by the same signal: resolution-biased uncertainty in the low-resolution stream, which has been overlooked by prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251945/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single ultra-high resolution RGB image (sizes ranging from 2448×2448 on DeepGlobe to 6748×4499 on ISIC) and produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask at full resolution. The input is consumed in two forms: a downsampled version fed to the deep low-resolution stream for global context, and original-resolution patches selected by an uncertainty-aware selector fed to the shallow UHR stream for spatial detail refinement. A tunable selection ratio K controls the fraction of patches processed by the UHR stream, trading a small accuracy drop for substantial speed and memory gains.

## Method

The framework uses ResNet18+DAPPM as the deep low-resolution stream and the first four stages of STDC as the shallow UHR stream. A resolution-biased uncertainty estimator combines two signals: (1) an entropy map computed from the coarse prediction of the deep stream, and (2) a resolution bias map derived from a Laplacian-pyramid-like high-frequency residual between the first two stages of the deep network, fused via a learnable 1×1 convolution and a bias factor α. The resulting pixel-wise uncertainty U1 drives an uncertainty-aware ensembler that performs weighted fusion between deep context features and shallow spatial details, and an uncertainty-based complementer that applies pixel-wise weighting to the cross-entropy loss to force the shallow network to learn complementary details in resolution-susceptible regions. A patch-level uncertainty U2 (obtained by interpolating U1 to a fixed grid) drives an uncertainty-aware selector that routes only the top-K uncertain patches to the UHR stream, reducing inference cost without proportional accuracy loss.

## Results

On DeepGlobe, the proposed method (K=100%) achieves 75.1% mIoU and 89.4% accuracy with only 1638 MB memory and 44.38 FPS, outperforming WSDNet (74.1% mIoU, 30.30 FPS) and ISDNet (73.3% mIoU, 27.70 FPS) in both accuracy and speed. On Inria Aerial, it reaches 79.0% mIoU and 96.8% accuracy at 10.89 FPS, surpassing SGHRQ (77.3%) and GPW-Former (76.5%). On the medical datasets ISIC and CRAG, it achieves 83.4% and 90.3% mIoU respectively, leading all compared methods. The uncertainty-aware selector further reduces memory to 1576 MB and boosts speed to 56.92 FPS at K=50% while retaining 73.7% mIoU on DeepGlobe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all UHR baselines with comparable backbones (R18+STDC*) across all five datasets: on DeepGlobe it leads WSDNet by 1.0% mIoU and ISDNet by 1.8%, while on Inria Aerial it surpasses SGHRQ by 1.7% and WSDNet by 3.8%. The only method achieving higher mIoU on DeepGlobe is GPW-Former (75.8% vs. 75.1%), but GPW-Former uses a heavier Wave-ViT backbone and does not report FPS. The headline advantage is inference speed: at 44.38 FPS on DeepGlobe, the method is 55× faster than MagNet (0.80 FPS), 261× faster than CascadePSP (0.11 FPS), and 1.5× faster than WSDNet (30.30 FPS), while also using less memory (1638 MB vs. 1876 MB). On the medical benchmarks, the method leads GPW-Former by 2.7% mIoU on ISIC (83.4% vs. 80.7%) and by 0.4% on CRAG (90.3% vs. 89.9%), confirming generalization beyond aerial imagery. The uncertainty-aware selector (K=50%) retains 73.7% mIoU on DeepGlobe—still above ISDNet and SGHRQ—while nearly doubling speed to 56.92 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground by unifying three previously separate improvement objectives in dual-stream UHR segmentation—ensemble fusion, selective zoom, and complementary learning—under a single resolution-biased uncertainty signal. Rather than building on any one prior architecture, it repurposes well-known components (entropy maps for predictive uncertainty, Laplacian-pyramid-style high-frequency residuals for resolution bias, patch selection à la PPN/PointRend, and uncertainty-weighted loss à la U-CE) into a coherent framework where one estimator drives the selector, ensembler, and complementer simultaneously. The dual-stream backbone (ResNet18+DAPPM for context, STDC for spatial details) follows the ISDNet/WSDNet lineage, but the paper's contribution is the unifying uncertainty principle rather than the network design itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by processing all UHR patches at once through a shallow network guided by uncertainty, the method achieves 44–57 FPS with under 1.7 GB memory on 2448×2448 images, making UHR segmentation viable in near-real-time settings where iterative patch-based methods (0.03–0.17 FPS) are infeasible. The unification also suggests a general design principle for other dual-stream dense prediction tasks (salient object detection, depth estimation) where a single reliability signal could coordinate multiple sub-operations. Open questions include whether the binary pruning strategy (top-K patches) would benefit from the hierarchical multi-group processing the authors mention but do not implement, and whether the resolution-biased uncertainty formulation transfers cleanly to tasks where 'resolution loss' is less clearly defined (e.g., medical histopathology with variable magnification).
