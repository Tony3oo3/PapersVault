## Problem

Existing modality-agnostic brain analysis methods (e.g., SynthSR, Brain-ID) are trained exclusively on healthy anatomy and degrade significantly when pathology is present. The only pathology-compatible contrast-agnostic method, PEPSI, requires paired pathology segmentation annotations during training, access to pretrained pathology segmenters, and additional fine-tuning for anomaly detection. This paper addresses the gap of reconstructing healthy brain anatomy from images with arbitrary pathology in a modality-agnostic manner, without paired pathology labels or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250272/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 3D brain volume (CT or MRI in T1w, T2w, or FLAIR contrast, at any resolution) as input, paired with its pre-registered contralateral-mirrored counterpart to exploit hemispheric symmetry. It outputs a reconstructed healthy brain anatomy in a T1w-like representation at 160³ resolution. For anomaly detection, the voxel-wise absolute difference between the input and the reconstruction yields a normalized anomaly map without any additional training.

## Method

UNA's core novelty is a fluid-driven anomaly randomization module that generates unlimited diverse pathology profiles by solving a forward advection-diffusion PDE, where the velocity field is parameterized as the curl of a random potential (ensuring incompressibility) and the diffusion coefficient as the square of another potential (ensuring non-negativity), with zero-Neumann boundary conditions enforcing anatomical constraints. These synthetic pathologies are encoded onto randomly modulated healthy anatomy (via label-conditioned intensity sampling) to create training data without requiring paired pathology annotations. The reconstruction network is a 3D UNet trained jointly on synthetic and real data using a weighted L1+gradient reconstruction loss (with pathology-aware masking for real images) plus an intra-subject self-contrastive loss that pulls the reconstruction toward the contralateral healthy hemisphere while pushing it away from the original pathological region, leveraging brain symmetry as a free anatomical prior.

## Results

On simulated pathology images with ground truth healthy anatomy, UNA achieves the best reconstruction across all four modalities (T1w, T2w, FLAIR, CT) and all three metrics (L1, PSNR, SSIM). For T1w MRI, UNA reaches PSNR 31.98 dB and SSIM 0.981 on the full brain, outperforming the next-best method Brain-ID (22.86 dB, 0.859) by ~9 dB in PSNR. In the diseased region specifically, UNA achieves PSNR 45.61 dB (T1w) and SSIM 0.998, while PEPSI does not report scores in pathology regions at all. For direct anomaly detection (no fine-tuning), UNA attains Dice scores of 0.31–0.37 across six datasets, surpassing the best competing method (Brain-ID at 0.24–0.28) by roughly 10–15% relative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNA outperforms all five baselines across every modality and metric in the main reconstruction benchmark (Table 1). Against Brain-ID [33] (the strongest healthy-anatomy baseline), UNA improves full-brain PSNR by 8–11 dB (e.g., 31.98 vs. 22.86 on T1w) and SSIM by 0.08–0.14, with the largest gains in the diseased region (PSNR 45.61 vs. 40.22 on T1w). PEPSI [34], the only prior method designed for pathology, does not synthesize healthy tissue within lesion regions and therefore has no scores in the diseased column; where it does report (full brain), UNA still leads by 5–9 dB in PSNR. SynthSR [23] is the weakest baseline, failing to capture anatomy on noisy CT (PSNR 13.97) and producing unrealistic patterns around pathology. For the downstream anomaly detection task (Table 3), UNA's zero-shot Dice of 0.31–0.37 beats Brain-ID (0.24–0.28), VAE [4] (0.11–0.18), and LDM [18] (0.20–0.24) by 10–15% relative, confirming that the reconstruction quality translates directly into detection performance without any fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrast-agnostic brain imaging line (SynthSR, Brain-ID) by addressing pathology, which prior methods either ignore or handle only with paired annotations (PEPSI). The key methodological departure is repurposing advection-diffusion PDEs from fluid dynamics (Liu et al. [35]) as a data generation mechanism: a small set of real pathology segmentations is advected and diffused through learned potential fields to produce unlimited, physically plausible anomaly profiles. Combined with Brain-ID's domain-randomization strategy for modality synthesis and a novel contralateral self-contrastive loss, this eliminates the need for paired pathology labels during training—a requirement that limited PEPSI and most prior pathology-aware models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By generating unlimited pathology profiles from a handful of real annotations via PDE transport, UNA removes the primary practical barrier (expensive paired pathology segmentation) that has constrained pathology-aware brain imaging to small, curated datasets. This opens the door to applying general-purpose analysis tools (segmentation, registration, parcellation) directly to uncurated clinical scans with pathology, without fine-tuning. The acknowledged limitation—'over-correction' when pathology heavily occludes underlying anatomy—remains an open problem, and the authors note broader applications (pathology-aware registration, treatment outcome prediction) as future directions.
