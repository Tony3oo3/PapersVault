## Problem

In complex scenes, a single object can assume multiple roles (subject or object) across overlapping visual relation tuples, causing feature overlap that impairs predicate recognition. Prior triplet-feature methods still struggle to distinguish these multi-role objects and largely overlook the inherent connectivity between aligned triplet-level and component-level representations for mutual enhancement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250941/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and produces a scene graph of quadruples: a binary triplet segmentation mask (with predicate class label) covering the subject-object pair region, plus subject and object bounding boxes with class labels, and a predicate class label whose bounding box is derived from the center point of the corresponding subject and object. The triplet mask is a novel output beyond standard SGG, generated via masked attention over multi-scale image features, and serves as a proposal to constrain the predicate search space.

## Method

The architecture is a hybrid reciprocal transformer with four 6-layer decoder branches (triplet mask with 300 queries; subject, object, predicate each with 600 queries) operating over ResNet-101 + transformer encoder features. Two interaction paradigms link the branches: (1) triplet-guided positional embedding, where component-level decoder position embeddings are updated via multi-head attention on the current-layer triplet query outputs, and (2) bidirectional cross-conditioning, where each layer's queries are refined by attending to the other branch's previous-layer outputs, enabling reciprocal refinement across layers. A triplet alignment loss computes a predicted similarity matrix between a pseudo-triplet representation (sum of matched component features) and the triplet query, then applies binary cross-entropy against a one-hot GT matching matrix, forcing multi-role objects to acquire distinct role-specific features. Training is end-to-end with Hungarian matching for triplets and groupwise multi-assignment for components, summing losses across all decoder layers.

## Results

On Visual Genome (ResNet-101 backbone), the method achieves R@50=34.1, R@100=38.3, mR@50=16.0, mR@100=20.5, and F@50=21.8, setting a new state-of-the-art among one-stage methods. Compared to the strongest one-stage baseline SpeaQ^ISG (R@50=32.1, mR@50=15.1), it gains +2.0 on R@50 and +2.9 on mR@100. On Action Genome, it achieves R@20/50=32.0/40.2, mR@20/50=21.3/23.6, and F@20/50=25.5/29.7, outperforming all prior methods on every reported metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest one-stage ResNet-101 baseline SpeaQ^ISG [18], the method wins on all metrics: +2.0 R@50 (34.1 vs 32.1), +2.8 R@100, +0.9 mR@50, +2.9 mR@100, +1.3 F@50, and +3.2 F@100. Compared to the best two-stage method DRM [22] (X101-FPN), it matches R@50 (34.1 vs 34.0) but trails slightly on R@100 (38.3 vs 38.9), while substantially outperforming on mR@50 (+7.0) and F@50 (+7.6), reflecting the advantage of joint one-stage optimization. Against ISG [16], the gains are larger: +6.9 R@50 and +3.9 mR@100. On Action Genome, the method surpasses the best prior baseline SpeaQ^ISG† [18] by +0.2 R@20, +0.8 R@50, +0.4 mR@20, +0.5 mR@50, and +0.6 F@50, confirming consistent improvements across datasets. The method also outperforms the ResNet-50-based Hydra-SGG [4] by +5.7 R@50 and +0.8 mR@100 despite using a larger backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-stage scene graph generation line initiated by ISG (Khandelwal & Sigal, NeurIPS 2022) and SpeaQ (Kim et al., CVPR 2024), adopting their iterative decoder structure and groupwise multi-assignment matching, while introducing triplet-level segmentation masks (via Mask2Former-style masked attention) as an additional representation level. It departs from prior triplet-augmented methods (DRM, PE-Net, Hydra-SGG) that treat triplet features as static prototypes or operate in a two-stage pipeline, by making the triplet-component relationship bidirectional and jointly optimized end-to-end within a single transformer. The key architectural departure is the reciprocal refinement module that allows triplet and component features to condition each other across layers, rather than the unidirectional triplet-to-component guidance used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The triplet alignment loss offers a principled mechanism for disambiguating multi-role objects in overlapping relation tuples, a persistent weakness in scene graph generation that prior methods address only implicitly. The bidirectional refinement paradigm could inform other tasks where multiple granularities of representation (e.g., region-level and object-level) need to be jointly optimized. However, the performance gains are modest (+1.2 R@50 on VG), the triplet mask decoder adds significant computational overhead (300 additional queries with segmentation heads), and it remains unclear whether the alignment loss generalizes to datasets with far more predicate classes or to video-based scene graphs beyond Action Genome. The paper does not ablate the contribution of the triplet mask itself versus the alignment loss in isolation, leaving open whether the mask is truly necessary or whether a lighter-weight triplet representation would suffice.
