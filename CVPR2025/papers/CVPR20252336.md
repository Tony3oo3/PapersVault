## Problem

Current audio-driven talking face generation methods rely on auxiliary visual signals (motion frames, landmarks, overlapping latents) to stabilize temporal coherence, which limits motion diversity and disrupts audio-visual synchronization. The paper argues that audio is the ideal and unique prior for driving facial animation, yet existing audio learning paradigms fail to capture the global, long-range temporal knowledge (tone, rhythm, speech speed) that implicitly encodes expression and head-movement priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252336/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single portrait reference image (supporting real human, animation, and 3D styles) and an audio clip of arbitrary length (demonstrated from 20 seconds up to 10 minutes) as input, and produces a temporally coherent talking face animation video. Audio features are extracted via Whisper-Tiny with 0.2-second windows per video frame, and a dynamic scale parameter β (0.5/1.0/2.0) allows users to control motion intensity from mild to intense. The framework is one-stage, initialized from Stable Video Diffusion, with multi-condition classifier-free guidance (image CFG=2.0, audio CFG=7.5).

## Method

Sonic decomposes global audio perception into intra-clip and inter-clip components. For intra-clip perception, context-enhanced audio learning injects Whisper-Tiny features via spatial cross-attention (masked to the face region) for lip sync and via temporal cross-attention (using average-pooled audio embeddings) to capture long-range speech priors for expressions; a motion-decoupled controller then disentangles head translation and expression amplitude into independent motion-bucket parameters predicted from audio and CLIP image embeddings. For inter-clip perception, time-aware position shift fusion bridges consecutive clips by progressively shifting the sliding window start position by a small offset α (e.g., 7 frames) at each denoising timestep, so that the model integrates long-range context across the full audio sequence without overlapping frames or motion-frame reference networks, incurring no additional training or inference cost.

## Results

On HDTF, Sonic achieves the best FID (29.10) and FVD (301.17), approximately 8% lower than the second-best (Hallo: FID 30.18, Hallo2: FVD 328.54), along with the highest Sync-C (4.197) and Smoothness (0.9970). On CelebV-HQ, Sonic attains the best FID (43.14), Sync-D (10.19), E-FID (1.78, 16.5% lower than second-best), F-SIM (0.9624), and Smoothness (0.9972), while maintaining a competitive FVD (483.11) close to Hallo2 (481.34). Sonic runs at 17.04 s per clip, substantially faster than Hallo (74.65 s), AniPortrait (44.03 s), and V-Express (39.04 s), though slower than EchoMimic (5.45 s) and SadTalker (3.75 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sonic outperforms all six baselines on FID and FVD on HDTF, with an 8% margin over the second-best in both metrics. On CelebV-HQ, Sonic leads in FID (43.14 vs. 47.40 for Hallo), E-FID (1.78 vs. 2.14 for EchoMimic, a 16.5% improvement), F-SIM, and Smoothness, while its FVD (483.11) is marginally behind Hallo2 (481.34). In the user study (Table 2), Sonic scores 4.58/5 on lip sync (45% above the best baseline Hallo2 at 3.15), 4.55/5 on motion diversity (72% above AniPortrait at 1.62), 4.29/5 on identity consistency, and 4.66/5 on smoothness. Sonic's main trade-off is runtime: at 17.04 s it is 4.4× slower than EchoMimic (5.45 s) and 4.5× slower than SadTalker (3.75 s), but 4.4× faster than Hallo (74.65 s) and 2.6× faster than AniPortrait (44.03 s). EchoMimic achieves the best E-FID on HDTF (1.486 vs. Sonic's 1.745), indicating slightly more diverse expressions in that setting, though Sonic recovers the lead on CelebV-HQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sonic builds on Stable Video Diffusion as its latent diffusion backbone and departs from the dominant audio-driven talking-face paradigm (EMO, Hallo, Loopy, SadTalker, AniPortrait) that relies on auxiliary visual motion signals—motion frames, 3D mesh coefficients, or landmark priors—to enforce temporal coherence. Its core conceptual departure is treating audio as the sole and sufficient driving signal for both intra-clip dynamics (lip sync, expression, head movement) and inter-clip stability, replacing the motion-frame/overlap strategies with a time-aware position shift fusion that bridges clips along the denoising timestep axis. The use of Whisper-Tiny (rather than Wav2Vec) for lightweight audio encoding and the motion-decoupled controller that derives head/expression amplitudes directly from audio and a CLIP image embedding further distinguish it from prior methods that inject spatial or 3D geometric priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that global audio perception alone can match or exceed methods that depend on visual motion frames for temporal stability, Sonic challenges a working assumption in the field and offers a computationally cheaper long-video inference strategy (no extra training cost, no overlapping frames). The 72% user-study gain in motion diversity and the ablation showing the motion controller is critical for dynamic degree (0.22 → 0.86) suggest that audio carries more motion information than prior methods exploited. Open questions include whether the audio-only paradigm generalizes to multi-person or occluded scenes, whether the SVD backbone limits ceiling quality, and whether the time-aware shift fusion extends to other conditional video generation tasks beyond talking heads.
