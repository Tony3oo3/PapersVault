## Problem

Annotated thermal facial landmark datasets remain extremely small (tens to hundreds of subjects) compared to RGB counterparts that have reached millions of faces, and no dense thermal landmarker or multimodal RGB+Thermal landmarker exists. The thermal modality is critical for medical screening, biometrics, and affective analysis, yet its higher intra-individual variability (temperature shifts, sweating, vascular changes) makes direct transfer of RGB landmarkers unreliable on challenging thermal images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251108/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The thermalization stage takes 256×256 RGB images from the synthetic FAKE dataset and paired 256×256 RGB+Thermal images from the SEJONG lab dataset, producing 512×512 thermalized images (200k total across 'cold' and 'warm' conditions). The landmark prediction stage consumes 224×224 images (RGB or thermal) and outputs per-landmark 2D Gaussian parameters (mean and variance) for 70 sparse 3DA-2D or 478 dense 2D points, from which face detection is derived by thresholding the average uncertainty across sliding windows. A separate 5-layer MLP label adapter maps between different landmark conventions (e.g., 70→72 or 70→68 points) using only 2D coordinate vectors as input.

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-supervised RGB2Thermal loss for a U-Net thermalizer with three terms: (i) an L2 reconstruction loss on paired SEJONG data, (ii) an entropy-regularized Wasserstein-2 distance (computed via the Sinkhorn algorithm) between subsampled patch distributions of thermalized FAKE images and real thermal images, applied at multiple scales, and (iii) a segmentation-based regularizer that penalizes deviation of mean pixel intensities in 18 semantic facial regions from hardcoded clinical reference temperatures. The thermalizer is trained twice with different reference temperature sets to simulate 'cold' and 'warm' environments, then applied to the full FAKE dataset to generate T-FAKE. Landmark prediction uses a MobileNet V2 trained with a Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss (GLL+SW), where the predicted variance per landmark enables integrated face detection via uncertainty thresholding, and a small MLP label adapter unifies evaluation across landmark conventions.

## Results

On the CHARLOTTE thermal landmarking benchmark, the GLL+SW Sparse model trained on FAKE+T-FAKE achieves a NME W/H of 0.0815 (Full) with a 20% failure rate at the strictest confidence threshold (σ̄ < 2.2×10⁻⁴), outperforming all thermal-specific baselines (best thermal: YOLO5Face at 0.1012) and RGB methods with pre-processing (FA* at 0.1593). At the more permissive threshold (σ̄ < 6×10⁻⁴), the model achieves 0.0889 NME with only 5.79% failure rate, surpassing Star (0.0868, but without face detection) on failure rate while matching its NME. On 300W RGB evaluation, the multimodal model achieves 0.0522 NME IO (Full), competitive with FA (0.0435) and Mediapipe (0.0562), confirming that thermal training does not degrade RGB performance. For thermalization quality, the model achieves the best temperature prediction MSE on both TUFTS (0.0353) and DRIVE-IN (0.0580), with a FID of 0.1598.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's GLL+SW Sparse model trained on FAKE+T-FAKE outperforms all thermal-specific baselines on CHARLOTTE: it beats YOLO5Face/TFW (0.1012 Full NME) by ~20%, DAN/AACHEN (0.2054) by ~60%, and U-Net+Wing/SF-TL54 (0.2829) by ~71%. Against RGB methods with pre-processing, it substantially outperforms FA* (0.1593) and Mediapipe* (0.2023), while Star (0.0965) achieves a slightly lower NME but does not perform face detection (uses GT bounding boxes), making the comparison not directly equivalent. On 300W RGB, the multimodal model (0.0522) is outperformed by Star (0.0287, trained directly on 300W) and FA (0.0435), but outperforms Mediapipe (0.0562), confirming no RGB degradation from thermal training. For thermalization quality (Table 3), the proposed RGB2Thermal loss achieves the best temperature prediction MSE on both TUFTS (0.0353) and DRIVE-IN (0.0580), outperforming Pix2Pix (0.0421/0.1453), CycleGAN (0.0521/0.0730), CUT (0.0468/0.1370), and QS-Attn (0.0409/0.1303), though QS-Attn achieves a lower FID (0.1173 vs. 0.1598) at the cost of poor temperature accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the FAKE synthetic dataset success story (Wood et al., 2021/2022) from the RGB domain to thermal imaging, applying the same probabilistic landmark prediction framework (GLL+SW) and label adaptation paradigm. For thermalization, it builds on prior RGB2Thermal translation work (Mallat et al., Kniaz et al.) but departs by introducing a semi-supervised loss combining a Wasserstein patch-distribution term and a segmentation-based temperature prior, enabling domain adaptation beyond lab-condition training data. The paper positions itself as the first to provide dense (478-point) thermal landmarks and the first multimodal RGB+Thermal landmarker, filling a gap where existing thermal datasets contain only tens to hundreds of subjects with sparse annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The T-FAKE dataset (200k images, ≥2k subjects, both sparse and dense landmarks) addresses a critical data scarcity problem in thermal facial analysis, where the largest public datasets contain only ~10k frames from 10 subjects. The finding that purely synthetic thermal training data yields state-of-the-art landmarking while preserving RGB performance suggests a scalable path for other underrepresented modalities. Open questions include whether the synthetic thermal appearance generalizes to truly diverse real-world conditions (e.g., extreme temperatures, varied sensor types), and whether the approach extends to other thermal applications beyond landmarking such as temperature-based health screening.
