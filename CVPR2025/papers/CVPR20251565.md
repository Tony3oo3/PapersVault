## Problem

Current text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models struggle to simultaneously achieve high imaging quality and effective motion representation. While T2I models can refine individual frames, applying them frame-independently introduces flickering, and existing temporal consistency methods (optical flow, NNF) designed for real videos fail on model-generated videos with inherent inconsistencies. The paper addresses how to compose T2I and T2V models to leverage each one's strengths without the quality degradation that sequential pipelines suffer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video latent generated by a T2V model (VideoCrafter-2.0 at 320×512 or AnimateDiff-V2 at 512×512, both upscaled 2×) along with a text prompt, and produces an enhanced video with improved imaging fidelity and temporal motion consistency. The pipeline is entirely training-free, composing a pre-trained T2I model (epiCRealism, T_I=50 steps) with a T2V model (AnimateDiff-Lightning, T_V=8 steps) without any additional training. The T2V block is applied only once during the T2I denoising process, yielding a 1.6×–4.5× speedup over sequential T2I+T2V pipelines.

## Method

EVS introduces an Encapsulated Composition pipeline structured as T2I+[T2V]+T2I, where the T2V stabilization block is inserted at an intermediate timestep t_T2V during T2I denoising. The key bridging mechanism uses the predicted clean latent z_{t→0} at t_T2V as a shortcut that aligns the two models' different latent distributions, avoiding the need for full denoising before switching models. To prevent T2V from degrading the imaging quality already achieved by T2I, the paper proposes Selective Feature Injection (SFI): during DDIM inversion of the T2I-refined latent, attention features (Q_inv, K_inv, V_inv) are collected and selectively injected at predefined U-Net layers during T2V denoising, with K_inv and V_inv fully injected to preserve spatial details while Q is blended at rate γ to allow temporal smoothing. This decouples spatial and temporal information in an explainable manner, enabling the T2V model to contribute only its temporal prior without overwriting the T2I imaging distribution.

## Results

On VBench's Overall Consistency subset, EVS achieves the highest Overall score of 0.8545 on VideoCrafter-2.0 (VC2) videos and 0.8243 on AnimateDiff-V2 (AD2) videos, outperforming all baselines. EVS also attains the best Subject Consistency (SC: 0.9808 on VC2, 0.9530 on AD2) and Aesthetic Predictor (AP: 5.46 on VC2, 5.42 on AD2) scores. In terms of efficiency, EVS runs in 120.86 s (VC2) and 302.82 s (AD2), yielding a 1.6×–4.5× speedup over the slowest baselines (Rerender-A-Video at 532–1174 s, TokenFlow at 547–1092 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVS outperforms all five baselines on the Overall score for both VC2 (0.8545 vs. best baseline Rerender-A-Video at 0.8385) and AD2 (0.8243 vs. Rerender-A-Video at 0.8197). Against Rerender-A-Video and TokenFlow, which rely on optical flow or NNF estimation, EVS avoids the local inconsistencies (blurring, flickering) that arise from inaccurate correspondence on model-generated videos, achieving higher MS on AD2 (0.9825 vs. 0.9750/0.9630) and comparable MS on VC2. BIVDiff, the closest T2V-based competitor, achieves slightly higher MS on VC2 (0.9885 vs. 0.9881) but lags significantly on imaging quality (DOVER 64.62 vs. 73.20) and is nearly 3× slower (352.70 s vs. 120.86 s). AnyV2V struggles to propagate first-frame enhancements to subsequent frames, yielding the lowest SC on AD2 (0.9270) and a lower Overall (0.5998). FRESCO shows the weakest performance overall, with the lowest MS on AD2 (0.9667) and a 4× longer runtime than EVS. The key differentiator is that EVS is the only method that simultaneously improves both imaging quality and temporal consistency while being the fastest, thanks to its encapsulated T2I+[T2V]+T2I pipeline that avoids redundant denoising steps and explicit per-frame correspondence computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVS builds on the SDEdit noising-denoising paradigm for frame refinement and DDIM inversion for deterministic latent encoding, but departs from prior T2I+T2V composition work (BIVDiff, VideoElevator) by introducing an encapsulated T2I+[T2V]+T2I pipeline that uses intermediate predicted clean latents as distribution bridges between the two models. The Selective Feature Injection strategy adapts the attention-feature collection technique from style-injection work [4] to selectively preserve T2I imaging information while injecting only T2V temporal prior, decoupling spatial and temporal components that SDEdit mixes implicitly in noisy latent space. This positions EVS as a training-free orchestration layer over existing T2I and T2V diffusion backbones rather than a new generative architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVS offers a practical, training-free recipe for practitioners who already have access to strong T2I and T2V models but cannot retrain them, and the 1.6x–4.5x inference speedup over explicit-correspondence baselines (Rerender-A-Video, TokenFlow, FRESCO) makes it attractive for deployment. However, the approach is sensitive to hyperparameters (t_I, t_V, t_T2V, n_V) and the SFI mechanism is only needed for out-of-domain styles, limiting its generality. Open questions include whether the encapsulated composition generalizes to transformer-based T2V backbones (e.g., HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX) that do not share a U-Net architecture with T2I models, and whether the single [T2V] block insertion suffices for longer or more dynamically complex videos.
