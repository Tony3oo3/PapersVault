## Problem

Pre-trained self-supervised audio encoders (Wav2Vec 2.0, HuBERT) model features at the phoneme level, causing near-homophonic syllables with distinct lip shapes (e.g., /i:/ in 'sheep' vs /ɪ/ in 'ship') to become tightly coupled in the feature space. This coupling produces an averaging effect in downstream lip motion generation, leading to significant lip-syncing errors. The paper addresses the absence of sentence-level semantic representation in these encoders, which prevents them from disambiguating contextually different but acoustically similar syllables.

## Input / Output

Wav2Sem takes a raw audio waveform as input and outputs a single global semantic vector (1×C) that is fused with the frame-level phoneme features (N'×C) from an existing self-supervised encoder via two linear projections and element-wise addition, yielding a decoupled feature (N'×C). The module operates at 49 Hz temporal resolution with ~20 ms stride between samples, and the fused features are then consumed by any downstream speech-driven facial animation model to produce 3D facial vertex positions. No additional text input is required at inference time, making it a drop-in replacement for the audio encoder's output.

## Method

Wav2Sem is a two-stage encoder: a 7-layer Temporal Convolutional Network (512 channels, 49 Hz output) extracts local acoustic features, followed by a 12-layer Transformer encoder (8 attention heads, 3072-dim MLP) that aggregates long-range dependencies into a single sentence-level semantic vector via mean pooling. The module is pre-trained on LibriSpeech 960h with an L1 loss aligning the audio semantic vector to BERT's sentence representation (either the CLS token or the mean of all token embeddings), effectively learning a mapping from audio to the BERT semantic space. After pre-training, Wav2Sem parameters are frozen and the module is plugged into existing self-supervised encoders (Wav2Vec 2.0, HuBERT, DeepSpeech) with a simple fusion: the semantic vector is projected through two linear layers and added to the phoneme features before being passed to the downstream animation model. This design requires no architectural modification to the host model and no additional text input at test time.

## Results

On VOCASET, integrating Wav2Sem into UniTalker reduces LVE from 3.5416 to 3.1476 (×10⁻⁵), the largest relative improvement among all frameworks (~11%). On BIWI, FaceFormer+ Wav2Semc achieves the best FDD of 4.9157 (×10⁻⁵) versus 5.0972 without Wav2Sem, and CodeTalker+ Wav2Semc drops FDD from 4.1170 to 3.9181. For phoneme recognition on TIMIT, Wav2Semc reduces test PER from 8.3 to 8.0 for Wav2Vec 2.0 and from 8.0 to 7.8 for HuBERT. Word-level feature decoupling (L2 distance between near-homophonic pairs) nearly doubles for Wav2Vec 2.0 (0.0397→0.0701) and increases for HuBERT (0.2689→0.2909) after Wav2Sem integration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wav2Sem consistently improves all six facial animation baselines (VOCA, FaceFormer, CodeTalker, UniTalker, FaceDiffuse, LG-LDM) across MVE, LVE, and FDD on both VOCASET and BIWI, confirming its plug-and-play nature. The most dramatic gain is UniTalker's LVE on VOCASET (3.5416→3.1476, ~11% reduction), while FaceFormer shows the largest FDD improvement on BIWI (5.0972→4.9157). For phoneme recognition on TIMIT, Wav2Semc reduces test PER by 0.3 for Wav2Vec 2.0 (8.3→8.0) and by 0.2 for HuBERT (8.0→7.8). In the ablation against BERT as a direct semantic source (Table 3), Wav2Sem outperforms BERT on 5 of 6 frameworks for LVE on VOCASET (e.g., FaceFormer: 3.9891 vs. 4.0241 for BERTc), attributed to Wav2Sem's audio-native training on LibriSpeech reducing domain gap. The word-level decoupling metric (Table 2) shows Wav2Sem nearly doubles the L2 separation of near-homophonic pairs in Wav2Vec 2.0 space (0.0397→0.0701), providing direct evidence of feature-space decoupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of self-supervised audio encoding (Wav2Vec 2.0, HuBERT) and speech-driven 3D facial animation (VOCA, FaceFormer, CodeTalker, UniTalker, FaceDiffuse, LG-LDM). It departs from prior multimodal approaches (EMAGE, Yu et al.) that inject external text features to supplement audio semantics, instead arguing that semantic information is already present in the audio signal itself and can be extracted without additional text input. The semantic alignment target is borrowed from BERT's sentence-level representations, but the key architectural contribution is a TCN + Transformer encoder trained to map raw audio into that BERT semantic space, then fused via two linear projections into any existing self-supervised audio encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (two linear layers + addition) makes Wav2Sem broadly applicable across heterogeneous facial animation architectures without retraining the base model, which is practically valuable for practitioners who have already invested in a specific pipeline. However, the quantitative gains are modest (typically 1–5% relative improvement in LVE/MVE/FDD), and the evaluation is limited to English datasets (BIWI, VOCASET) with small subject counts. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to languages with denser homophone inventories (e.g., Mandarin), whether the sentence-level semantic bottleneck is sufficient for longer utterances, and whether the decoupling effect persists under cross-identity or cross-domain transfer settings.
