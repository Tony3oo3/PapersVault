## Problem

Existing cross-domain few-shot action recognition (CDFSAR) methods rely on joint training of source and target data, which requires retraining the model for every new target domain and incurs heavy computation cost. Additionally, these methods apply pre-trained models to the target domain in a straightforward manner (e.g., a simple nearest-neighbor classifier), failing to fully exploit the potential of the frozen backbone for dynamic adaptation to target data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250568/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16-frame video clips at 112×112 (ViT-S) or 224×224 (ViT-B) resolution, which are reduced to 8 frames during patch embedding before entering the transformer backbone. For few-shot inference, each episode provides a set of query videos and an N-way K-shot annotated support set (typically 5-way 1-shot or 5-way 5-shot), and the model outputs a class label per query by comparing its representation against support-set prototypes via Euclidean distance. Evaluation averages over 10,000 randomly sampled episodes per setting.

## Method

TAMT introduces a decoupled two-phase paradigm: the VideoMAE backbone is first pre-trained on source data using reconstruction-based SSL followed by supervised learning, then fine-tuned on target data with a lightweight Hierarchical Temporal Tuning Network (HTTN) while keeping the backbone frozen. HTTN comprises (1) local Temporal-Aware Adapters (TAA) inserted into the last L transformer blocks, which recalibrate intermediate features via temporal-convolution-generated scale and bias parameters (with dimensionality reduction ratio ρ=4 and kernel k_t=3), and (2) a Global Temporal-aware Moment Tuning (GTMT) module at the output that fuses first-order (GAP) and second-order (Efficient Long-Short Temporal Covariance, ELSTC) moments to produce a powerful video representation. ELSTC splits the temporal sequence into G groups and computes long-short temporal covariance within each group, reducing the (TC)² computation of full temporal covariance by a factor of G while capturing correlations from single-frame appearance to multi-frame motion. This design requires only ~2.8M trainable parameters and 1.9G GPU memory versus 29.9M and 17.5G for full fine-tuning, while outperforming joint-training baselines by 13%–31%.

## Results

On the CDFSAR task (5-way 5-shot accuracy), TAMT achieves average scores of 68.37% (K-400 source), 57.91% (K-100 source), 57.02% (SSV2 source), 45.92% (Diving source), and 39.93% (UCF source) across five target domains. These represent improvements of 13%–31% over the strongest prior CDFSAR method (CDFSL-V), with the largest gap of 31.15% on K-100→target transfers. TAMT also requires only ~0.1× GPU memory (1.9G vs. 17.5G), 0.1× learnable parameters (2.8M vs. 29.9M), and 0.7× training time compared to full fine-tuning, while using a decoupled paradigm that pre-trains once per source rather than retraining per target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAMT outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every source–target configuration. Against the strongest dedicated CDFSAR methods, TAMT beats CDFSL-V [38] by 21.13% (K-400 source), 31.15% (K-100), 28.02% (SSV2), 17.92% (Diving), and 13.11% (UCF) in average 5-way 5-shot accuracy; it surpasses SEEN [49] by up to 27.18% and DMSD [18] by up to 26.28% on K-100→target transfers. Among general FSAR baselines (STARTUP++ [34], DD++ [22], STRM [42], HYRSM [45]), TAMT leads by 15–60+ points depending on the setting, confirming that the decoupled pre-training + temporal tuning paradigm is substantially more effective than joint-training or simple nearest-neighbor approaches. In the adapter ablation (Table 4b), TAMT's TAA module outperforms spatial-only Adapter [21] by 4.17%, SSF [25] by 7.69%, TAM [28] by 2.97%, and ST-Adapter [32] by 2.58% in average accuracy, validating the benefit of temporal-aware recalibration over purely spatial or 3D-convolution-based adapters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the parameter-efficient adapter paradigm (Houlsby et al., ICML 2019; Lian et al., NeurIPS 2022) by extending scale-and-bias recalibration with temporal convolution to handle video features, and on moment-based representation learning (Gao et al., ICCV 2023) by incorporating first- and second-order temporal statistics into few-shot video matching. It departs from prior CDFSAR methods (SEEN, CDFSL-V, DMSD) that rely on joint source-target training, instead proposing a decoupled pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm that avoids repeated retraining when multiple target domains share a single source. The VideoMAE self-supervised pre-training backbone and prototypical-network-style metric matching are carried over from the broader FSAR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled paradigm offers a practical efficiency win: one pre-training run serves all target domains, reducing training cost by roughly 5× compared to joint-training baselines, which is significant for large source datasets. The 13–31% accuracy gains over existing CDFSAR methods, achieved with only 2.8M trainable parameters (vs. 29.9M for full fine-tuning), suggest that lightweight temporal-aware tuning can unlock substantial latent capacity in frozen video backbones. Open questions include whether the moment-based representation (GTMT) remains beneficial at larger scales or with stronger backbones, and whether the decoupled paradigm generalizes to more extreme domain shifts or multi-source settings. The limited number of CDFSAR-specific baselines (only 2–3 dedicated methods) means the competitive landscape is still thin, leaving room for further methodological development.
