## Problem

Alpha matte extraction from video against a known background remains ill-posed when relying on a single cue: color-based chroma keying is underconstrained and struggles with hair, motion blur, and transparent objects, while polarization-based methods (PTM, PolarMatte) fail when foreground specular reflections align with the background's polarization angle. No prior single-shot, per-pixel method jointly exploits both color and polarization cues with a closed-form theoretical guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method consumes four polarization images at 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135° from a single-shot polarization camera (e.g., Blackfly S USB3), a known fully-polarized background (e.g., an LCD monitor), and a calibrated illumination color (via color checker). It produces a per-pixel alpha matte in [0,1] and the foreground color, operating at any resolution without spatial context or multi-view captures. The specular reflection color must be known (assumed white for natural illumination), and the background must be fully polarized with no unpolarized component.

## Method

The core contribution is a diffuse-specular polarimetric compositing equation that models the foreground as a sum of diffuse and specular reflections, each decomposed into unpolarized and polarized terms with distinct phase angles (θ_d = θ_s ± π/2 under unpolarized illumination). From this equation, two closed-form matting methods are derived: pCSM-S (assuming zero polarized diffuse, solvable whenever background color ≠ specular color) and pCSM-DS (full model with solvability conditions expressed as strong/weak color and polarization constraints). The non-linear pCSM-DS reduces to finding roots of a fourth-degree polynomial in a scalar parameter t, with boundary checks at α=0 and α=1. A physics-motivated polarized diffuse regularization (ridge regression on the polarized diffuse coefficients) is added to both variants, granting robustness to sensor noise and applicability to arbitrary scenes regardless of color/polarization conditions.

## Results

On the PolarMatte static scene test data (12 scenes, green polarized background), the regularized non-linear pCSM-DS achieves the best performance across all three metrics: MAE 7.48, MSE 0.26, and GRAD 1.68. The faster pCSM-S variant achieves MAE 7.57, MSE 0.29, and GRAD 1.92, already outperforming the closest polarization-based competitor PolarMatte (MAE 10.35) by ~27%. On synthetic datasets, the unregularized pCSM-DS methods achieve zero error on solvable conditions (datasets A and B), validating the theoretical closed-form solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Keylight (commercial chroma keying), BGMv2 (learning-based background matting), PTM (polarimetric triangulation matting), and PolarMatte (polarization-only matting). Both pCSM variants decisively outperform all baselines on every metric. Against the strongest competitor PolarMatte, pCSM-S reduces MAE from 10.35 to 7.57 (−27%) and MSE from 0.84 to 0.29 (−65%), while pCSM-DS further improves GRAD from 1.97 to 1.68 (−15%). The color-based methods (Keylight, BGMv2) are substantially worse, with BGMv2 achieving MAE 85.20—over 11× worse than pCSM-DS—highlighting the ill-posedness of color-only matting. PTM, which relies solely on polarization, achieves MAE 11.26 but degrades on specular highlights where polarized foreground reflections align with the background, a failure mode that pCSM's joint color-polarization formulation explicitly addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the polarimetric screen matting lineage (PTM [22], PolarMatte [11]) by jointly incorporating color cues alongside polarization, departing from the prior work's exclusive reliance on a single modality. The key theoretical departure is the diffuse-specular polarimetric compositing equation, which models foreground appearance as a sum of diffuse and specular reflections within the Stokes-parameter framework, enabling closed-form solvability analysis that was intractable for polarization-only formulations. It also builds on the dichromatic reflectance model [26] and the diffuse-specular separation literature [16] from polarimetric vision, repurposing them for the compositing/opacity estimation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing ill-posedness in screen matting by showing that color and polarization together provide sufficient constraints for a unique per-pixel solution, with explicit solvability conditions that tell practitioners when each method variant applies. Practical impact is tempered by hardware requirements (a polarization camera and a calibrated LCD background), and the non-linear pCSM-DS is too slow for real-time use (~70 s per 2K frame), though pCSM-S runs in ~2 ms on GPU. Open questions include generalization to more complex reflectance models (e.g., multi-lobe BRDFs), handling of partially polarized backgrounds, and whether the polarized-diffuse regularization can be replaced by learned priors to reduce the need for manual hyperparameter tuning.
