## Problem

Point-based crowd counters trained with point-to-point (P2P) matching cannot be adapted to semi-supervised pseudo-labeling because P2P only assigns confidence to matched foreground pixels, leaving background pixels without any supervision signal. This makes the unlabeled-data loss ill-posed (the background term is identically zero), causing the model to over-activate neighborhoods of foreground pixels and ultimately break down during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252283/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB crowd image and produces a per-pixel foreground probability map at the same spatial resolution as the input. Pedestrian locations are extracted as pixels with scores above 0.5, and the crowd count is simply the number of such pixels. The model is a simplified encoder-decoder (no offset regression branch), so localization is purely coordinate-based from the point map rather than from regressed offsets.

## Method

The paper proposes a point-to-region (P2R) matching strategy that replaces P2P in both supervised and semi-supervised training. Instead of matching one predicted pixel to one GT point via the Hungarian algorithm, P2R assigns each GT/pseudo-point a local 'dominated zone' (a region of radius μ) and selects a single representative foreground pixel within that zone using a cost matrix, while all other pixels in the zone are supervised as background. This allows the confidence score of each pseudo-point to propagate to its entire neighborhood, fixing the ill-posed background loss. To diagnose the P2P failure, the authors introduce Point-Specific Activation Map (PSAM), an efficient gradient-based visualization showing that P2P training over-activates and enlarges the receptive regions of foreground pixels. The overall framework uses a mean-teacher pseudo-labeling pipeline (FixMatch-style confidence thresholding) where the teacher generates pseudo-points from weakly augmented images and the student is trained on strongly augmented images with the P2R loss. P2R also eliminates the Hungarian algorithm entirely, yielding a ~68× speedup in loss computation.

## Results

On four crowd counting benchmarks (ShTech A, ShTech B, UCF-QNRF, JHU++), P2R achieves the best MAE across all semi-supervised protocols. With only 5% labeled data, P2R reaches MAE 69.9 / 9.1 / 100.1 / 77.8 on the four datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline OT-M (83.7 / 12.6 / 118.4 / 82.7) by 13.8 / 3.5 / 18.3 / 4.9 MAE respectively. At 10% labels, P2R further improves to MAE 65.2 / 8.4 / 94.9 / 68.7, and at 40% labels to 55.6 / 6.8 / 86.0 / 63.3. Under fully supervised (100%) training, P2R also surpasses the original P2PNet by 1.7 / 0.08 / 2.06 / 2.42 MAE on the four datasets while being ~68× faster in loss computation (0.0064 s vs 0.4307 s per image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2R outperforms all 16 baselines on nearly every dataset and label protocol. At 5% labels, the gap over the strongest semi-supervised baselines (OT-M and DAC) is largest: 13.8 MAE on ShTech A and 18.3 on UCF-QNRF, indicating that the P2R confidence-propagation mechanism is most beneficial when labeled data is scarce. At 10% labels, P2R beats the next-best method CU [18] by 5.5 MAE on ShTech A and 5.1 on UCF-QNRF; notably, MT [59] and L2R [32] degrade severely (MAE > 90 on ShTech A), confirming that naive pseudo-labeling without proper background supervision fails. At 40% labels, P2R still leads DAC [23] by 11.9 MAE on ShTech A and 5.1 on UCF-QNRF. Under fully supervised training (100% labels), P2R surpasses the original P2PNet [57] on all four datasets (e.g., 51.02 vs 52.74 MAE on ShTech A), demonstrating that the region-based matching is also beneficial in the supervised setting. In the UDA experiments (Table 2), P2R with domain adaptation achieves the best MAE across all four adaptation directions (e.g., 10.6 on A→B, 89.3 on B→A), outperforming dedicated UDA methods like FSIM [80] (11.1 / 120.3) and C2MoT [70] (12.4 / 120.7), while P2R without DA (25.6 / 130.2) confirms the domain gap is non-trivial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the point-based crowd counting framework of P2PNet (Song et al., CVPR 2021) into the semi-supervised regime by adopting the pseudo-labeling pipeline of FixMatch and mean-teacher. All prior semi-supervised counting methods (OT-M, DAC, L2R, GP, IRAST, SUA) operate on density regression, making this the first work to apply point detection under pseudo-labeling. The key conceptual departure is replacing the Hungarian-algorithm-based P2P matching with a P2R strategy that assigns each pseudo-point a local dominated region, and the PSAM diagnostic tool is inspired by Grad-CAM/ODAM but adapted to pixel-level point detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental ill-posedness in applying P2P matching to semi-supervised learning: background pixels receive no confidence signal, causing the model to over-activate foreground neighborhoods and eventually break down. The PSAM diagnostic provides a reusable tool for understanding pixel-level activation in point detectors, and the 68× speedup from eliminating the Hungarian algorithm makes point-based counting substantially more practical. Limitations include evaluation on only four crowd datasets and applicability restricted to point-based (not density-regression) counters; open questions include whether P2R generalizes to other dense-detection tasks and whether the PSAM over-activation phenomenon appears in other pixel-level architectures.
