## Problem

Existing lifelong robot learning methods (experience replay, regularization, task-specific LoRA) fail to exploit shared motion primitives that underlie semantically distinct skills, limiting effective knowledge transfer across diverse manipulation tasks. The paper asks whether reusable, extensible primitive representations can be learned and transferred to accelerate new skill acquisition without access to prior data.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes RGB images, proprioceptive state, and a language instruction, and outputs a continuous action sequence via a diffusion transformer. Unusually, the input is also processed through RAFT to compute optical flow between consecutive frames, and the instruction is embedded via CLIP; these two signals form a joint text-flow query that indexes into a bank of learned primitive prompts rather than being fed directly into the policy backbone.

## Method

PPL is a two-stage prompt-learning framework on a diffusion transformer policy. In multi-skill pre-training, a set of primitive prompts (split into key and value prefixes) is prepended to every multi-head self-attention layer; a Motion-Aware Prompting (MAP) module computes a weighted-sum prompt by combining CLIP text embeddings with RAFT optical-flow features, enabling the model to capture both semantic and kinematic shared structure across skills. In the lifelong stage, pretrained prompts are frozen and new task-specific prompt components are appended; a cosine-similarity weighting between the MAP query and each component's key vector determines the activation weight, so only the new task's prompts are updated while all prior prompts remain fixed. This design allows the policy to selectively retrieve relevant primitives from old skills when learning a new one, without replay or per-task adapter parameters.

## Results

On the MimicGen lifelong learning benchmark, PPL achieves an average FWT of 0.83±0.03, outperforming LoRA/TAIL (0.78±0.09), Sequential learning (0.65±0.06), and Experience Replay (0.61±0.09). On real-world Franka Panda tasks, PPL reaches 0.84±0.05 average success rate in multi-skill pre-training (vs. 0.73 for MoE and 0.42 for Diffusion Transformer) and 0.68±0.05 in lifelong learning (vs. 0.55 for Sequential and 0.58 for ER). PPL also maintains positive BWT (−0.56 for Sequential, −0.46 for ER) without requiring replay data, demonstrating effective knowledge retention across 7 sequential tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA/TAIL [31], PPL achieves a 5-point FWT improvement (0.83 vs. 0.78) on the MimicGen lifelong benchmark, while also matching LoRA's training speed advantage over MoE. Against the MoE-based sparse diffusion policy [54], PPL outperforms by 11 points in real-world multi-skill pre-training (0.84 vs. 0.73 average success rate) and by 1 point in simulation pre-training (0.84 vs. 0.82), while training approximately twice as fast as MoE due to the absence of gating and multiple expert networks. PPL also surpasses both Sequential and Experience Replay baselines by 12–22 points in FWT on MimicGen, and by 10–13 points in real-world lifelong learning, all without requiring access to previously collected replay data.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPL builds directly on prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021) for the mechanism of prepending learnable prompts to attention keys and values in a diffusion transformer policy, and on the broader parameter-efficient continual learning literature (Dual-Prompt, Coda-Prompt, TAIL/LoRA) for the two-stage frozen-plus-adaptive prompt architecture. Its key departure from prior prompt-based lifelong learning methods is the introduction of motion-aware prompting: rather than relying solely on task identifiers or language instructions to select and weight prompts, PPL queries prompts via a combined CLIP semantic embedding and RAFT optical flow representation, explicitly targeting shared motion primitives that span semantically distinct skills. This positions the work at the intersection of robotic primitive learning (movement primitives, hierarchical imitation learning) and modern prompt-based adaptation, repurposing the primitive concept as a latent prompt component rather than an explicit subtask decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPL offers a practical middle ground between memory-heavy experience replay and parameter-isolated adapters (LoRA), enabling knowledge transfer across skills without storing or replaying old demonstrations — a meaningful constraint for real-world deployment. The motion-aware prompting insight that optical flow can bridge semantically unrelated but kinematically similar tasks is a useful inductive bias for robot manipulation, though its fragility under extreme lighting variation (Table 3) highlights that 2D flow remains an incomplete motion representation. Open questions include whether 3D scene flow or depth-augmented queries would resolve the lighting sensitivity, how the approach scales to hundreds of skills with growing prompt sets, and whether the pre-training stage can be replaced by a single large-scale multi-skill dataset to reduce the two-stage overhead.
