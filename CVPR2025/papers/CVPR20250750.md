## Problem

3D reconstruction has traditionally depended on iterative optimization (Bundle Adjustment, global alignment) as a costly post-processing step, and existing feed-forward methods like DUSt3R and MASt3R are limited to pairwise image processing. The paper asks whether a single large feed-forward network can directly predict all key 3D scene attributes—cameras, depth, point maps, and tracks—from one to hundreds of views in a single forward pass, without any geometry optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250750/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an unordered sequence of N RGB images (N ranges from 1 to hundreds), where the first image is designated as the reference frame and all 3D predictions are expressed in its coordinate system. Outputs are over-complete: camera parameters (rotation quaternion, translation, and field of view), per-pixel depth maps, per-pixel 3D point maps, and dense per-pixel tracking features. Notably, the point maps are viewpoint-invariant (defined in the first camera's frame), and at inference the paper finds that unprojecting the independently predicted depth maps with the predicted cameras yields more accurate 3D points than using the dedicated point map head directly.

## Method

VGGT is a large transformer with minimal 3D inductive biases: input images are patchified by DINO, augmented with per-frame camera tokens and register tokens (with distinct learnable tokens for the first frame to establish the reference coordinate system), and processed through 24 layers of Alternating-Attention that alternate between frame-wise self-attention (within each image) and global self-attention (across all images), with no cross-attention layers. A camera head (4 self-attention layers + linear projection) predicts intrinsics and extrinsics from the camera tokens, while a DPT head converts image tokens to dense feature maps that feed 3×3 convolutions for depth, point maps, and tracking features, plus aleatoric uncertainty maps. Tracking is delegated to a CoTracker2 module that correlates the dense tracking features across frames. The network is trained end-to-end with multi-task supervised losses on 3D-annotated datasets, and ablations show that the alternating-attention design and joint prediction of interrelated quantities (camera, depth, point map, tracks) each provide substantial accuracy gains over simpler alternatives.

## Results

On camera pose estimation (Table 1), VGGT in feed-forward mode achieves AUC@30 of 85.3 on RealEstate10K and 88.2 on CO3Dv2 in ~0.2s, outperforming all baselines including optimization-based methods like VGGSfMv2 (78.9/83.4, ~10s) and MASt3R (76.4/81.8, ~9s). With optional Bundle Adjustment post-processing, VGGT reaches 93.5/91.8 AUC@30 in ~1.8s. On dense MVS (DTU), VGGT reduces the Overall Chamfer distance from 1.741 (DUSt3R) to 0.382 without ground-truth cameras, approaching GT-camera methods like GeoMVSNet (0.295). On ETH3D point map estimation, VGGT (Depth+Cam) achieves Overall 0.677 vs. MASt3R's 0.826 and DUSt3R's 1.005, all in 0.2s vs. ~7-9s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGGT decisively outperforms all camera pose estimation baselines: it beats the strongest optimization-based method VGGSfMv2 by 6.4 AUC@30 on Re10K (85.3 vs. 78.9) and 4.8 on CO3Dv2 (88.2 vs. 83.4) while being 50× faster (0.2s vs. ~10s). Among concurrent feed-forward works, VGGT surpasses FLARE (78.8/83.4) and CUT3R (75.3/82.8) by 6.5 and 10.0 AUC@30 on Re10K respectively. On dense MVS (DTU), VGGT dramatically outperforms DUSt3R (Overall 0.382 vs. 1.741) and approaches GT-camera methods like GeoMVSNet (0.295) without requiring ground-truth poses. On ETH3D point maps, VGGT (Depth+Cam, 0.677) beats both MASt3R (0.826) and DUSt3R (1.005) in a single 0.2s forward pass versus their ~7-9s global alignment. For two-view matching on ScanNet, VGGT's tracking head (AUC@5=33.9) outperforms the dedicated matcher Roma (31.8) and all other baselines (LoFTR 22.1, DKM 29.4, SuperGlue 16.2). In novel view synthesis on GSO, VGGT (PSNR 30.41) is slightly below LVSM (31.71) but operates without known input camera parameters and uses only 20% of the training data. For dynamic point tracking, replacing CoTracker's backbone with VGGT features improves δ_vis_avg from 78.9 to 84.0 on TAP-Vid RGB-S, surpassing TAPTR (76.2), LocoTrack (83.2), and BootsTAPIR (83.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

VGGT extends the feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm established by DUSt3R (2-view) and MASt3R (2-view with matching) to an arbitrary number of input views, while simultaneously predicting camera parameters, depth maps, point maps, and tracking features in a single forward pass. It departs from VGGSfM and other differentiable-SfM methods by eliminating the need for iterative bundle adjustment or global alignment post-processing, relying instead on a standard large transformer with Alternating-Attention (frame-wise and global self-attention) and DINO patchification. The tracking module reuses the CoTracker2 architecture, and the dense prediction heads follow the DPT design, making the overall architecture deliberately free of specialized 3D inductive biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VGGT demonstrates that a single feed-forward transformer can replace an entire multi-stage 3D vision pipeline (SfM + MVS + tracking) while being 30–50× faster, which has immediate practical implications for real-time applications and mobile deployment. The finding that VGGT's pretrained features substantially improve downstream tasks (dynamic point tracking, novel view synthesis) positions it as a potential universal 3D backbone analogous to DINO or CLIP in 2D vision. Open questions include whether the over-complete multi-task supervision is truly necessary at inference (the paper shows depth+camera outperforms the dedicated point map head), how the model scales beyond hundreds of views, and whether the reliance on 3D-annotated training data limits generalization to truly novel scene structures.
