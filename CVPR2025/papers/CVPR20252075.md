## Problem

Existing point cloud completion methods trained on synthetic datasets generalize poorly to out-of-distribution real-world scans, while prior zero-shot approaches (e.g., SDS-Complete) rely on iterative SDS optimization from 2D diffusion priors, making them slow and limited in geometric fidelity. The paper asks whether the explicit 3D geometric priors embedded in feed-forward image-to-3D generative models can be directly leveraged for zero-shot completion, achieving both faster inference and finer structural detail.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial point cloud P_in ∈ R^{N×3} (ranging from dense multi-frame scans in Redwood to sparse LiDAR sweeps in KITTI) and a free-form text prompt T_in as input, and produces a complete point cloud P_out that preserves the original input geometry. No training on completion data is required; the pipeline is entirely zero-shot, relying on pre-trained depth inpainting, depth-conditioned image generation (ControlNet), image-to-3D reconstruction (LGM/InstantMesh), and a 2D diffusion prior (Zero123) for optional refinement.

## Method

GenPC bridges the modality gap between point clouds and image-to-3D models via a Depth Prompting module that estimates the original scanning viewpoint using a convex-hull-based hidden-point-removal strategy, projects the partial cloud to a coarse depth map, inpaints sparse regions with a 2D diffusion model, and conditions a depth-aware ControlNet to synthesize an RGB image. This image is fed to a feed-forward image-to-3D model (e.g., LGM) to produce a complete 3D shape in seconds. A Geometric Preserving Fusion module then aligns the generated shape with the input via Dynamic Scale Adaptation—searching over scale factors in [0.8, 1.2] with ICP registration scored by a combined XYZ and RGB Chamfer Distance—to retain both geometric and semantic consistency. An optional Refining stage re-initializes the merged point cloud as 3D Gaussians with frozen parameters for the partial region and optimized coordinates for the missing region, guided by an SDS loss from Zero123 plus a preservation loss that locks the partial geometry, thereby reducing multi-stage error accumulation.

## Results

On the Redwood dataset, GenPC (with Refining) achieves an average CD of 1.74 and EMD of 2.88 (×10²), outperforming all baselines across 10 object categories. Even without the optional SDS Refining step (Ours*), GenPC reaches 1.98 CD and 3.16 EMD, still surpassing the best prior zero-shot method SDS-Complete (2.72 CD / 4.06 EMD) by 36% in CD and 29% in EMD. On ScanNet, GenPC achieves 1.62 CD and 3.55 EMD, beating SnowflakeNet (1.74/4.27) and AdaPoinTr (2.21/2.38). Qualitative results on KITTI show GenPC maintains scale consistency with the input, unlike learning-based methods that produce undersized completions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenPC outperforms all four baselines on both CD and EMD across the Redwood dataset. Against the learning-based methods, GenPC achieves substantially lower errors: CD of 1.74 vs. 2.89 (PoinTr), 2.96 (SnowflakeNet), and 4.45 (AdaPoinTr), with EMD of 2.88 vs. 5.24, 5.64, and 6.19 respectively — indicating that training-based approaches suffer significant domain-gap degradation on real-world scans. Against the only prior zero-shot method SDS-Complete, GenPC reduces average CD by 36% (1.74 vs. 2.72) and EMD by 29% (2.88 vs. 4.06), while also producing finer geometric details due to explicit 3D priors rather than implicit 2D diffusion guidance. On ScanNet, GenPC (1.62 CD / 3.55 EMD) beats SnowflakeNet (1.74/4.27) on both metrics and AdaPoinTr (2.21 CD) on CD, though AdaPoinTr reports a lower average EMD (2.38) on that dataset. On KITTI, GenPC qualitatively produces scale-consistent completions while AdaPoinTr outputs undersized shapes, highlighting the benefit of the Dynamic Scale Adaptation module.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenPC sits at the intersection of two recent trends: zero-shot point cloud completion via 2D diffusion priors (SDS-Complete, Huang et al.) and feed-forward image-to-3D generative models (LRM, InstantMesh, LGM). It departs from the SDS-based line by replacing implicit 2D priors extracted through per-instance optimization with explicit 3D geometric priors from a pre-trained image-to-3D model, eliminating the need to train a radiance field from scratch. The Depth Prompting module borrows the convex-hull viewpoint estimation from Katz et al. [20] and repurposes depth inpainting and ControlNet as a bridge between the point cloud modality and the image-conditioned generative model, while the Geometric Preserving Fusion module introduces a combined geometric-semantic ICP alignment (XYZ + RGB Chamfer Distance) not previously applied in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By leveraging explicit 3D generative priors, GenPC achieves zero-shot completion with significantly reduced inference time compared to SDS-based methods while producing finer geometric details, making real-world scan completion more practical. The ablation showing that the optional SDS Refining step yields only marginal gains (CD 1.98→1.74) suggests the 3D generative model does most of the heavy lifting, which is both a strength (speed) and a limitation (upper bound set by the generative model's quality). Open questions include how the pipeline handles extremely sparse or heavily occluded scans beyond the tested datasets, whether the text prompt requirement can be eliminated, and whether the approach extends to multi-object or scene-level completion.
