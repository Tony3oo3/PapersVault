## Problem

Conformal classifiers produce prediction sets with valid coverage guarantees, but these sets are often uninformatively large and highly sensitive to small input perturbations (e.g., a horizontal flip changes set sizes for 75% of ImageNet examples at 99% coverage). While test-time augmentation (TTA) is known to improve accuracy, robustness, and calibration of standard classifiers, its utility within the conformal prediction pipeline had not been explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251498/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed as a 256×256 crop from a 4-pixel-padded original) and produces a prediction set of classes with a user-specified marginal coverage guarantee (1−α). The image is passed through the base classifier multiple times under different augmentations (2 for the simple policy, 12 for the expanded policy), and the resulting logits are aggregated before conformal scoring. The output is not a single class but a variable-size subset of the K possible classes, with the set size determined by the conformal threshold.

## Method

The approach inserts a learned TTA aggregation step between the base classifier and the conformal scoring function. Concretely, the image is forwarded through the classifier under m augmentations, producing an m×K logit matrix; a learned weight vector θ (1×m) is applied to this matrix, followed by softmax, to yield a single probability distribution over classes. The weights θ are trained by minimizing cross-entropy on a dedicated subset D^(TTA) of the validation set, kept distinct from the calibration set D^(cal) to preserve the exchangeability assumption required for valid conformal coverage. The conformal score (RAPS or APS) is then computed from the TTA-transformed probabilities rather than the raw classifier output. The key mechanism by which this reduces set sizes is that TTA promotes the true class's rank among predicted probabilities even when it is initially unlikely (e.g., from 200th to 100th most probable), thereby reducing the number of incorrect classes the conformal procedure must include to satisfy the coverage guarantee.

## Results

Using the expanded augmentation policy (12 augmentations) with ResNet-50, RAPS+TTA-Learned reduces average prediction set size by 10–17% across ImageNet, iNaturalist, and CUB-Birds at α=0.01 (e.g., 37.75→31.25 on ImageNet, 61.44→53.20 on iNaturalist). At the more practical α=0.05, reductions are 13–16% on ImageNet and iNaturalist (5.64→4.89 and 7.99→6.68 respectively). Under distribution shift (ImageNet-C corruptions), TTA-Learned achieves a 14% average reduction in set size with no loss of coverage. The method also bridges model-size gaps: ResNet-101+TTA-Learned (26.5 classes at α=0.01) outperforms ResNet-152 alone (29.6 classes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is RAPS (Angelopoulos et al., 2022), the state-of-the-art conformal score that penalizes inclusion of low-probability classes. RAPS+TTA-Learned significantly outperforms RAPS in 16 of 18 dataset/α combinations (expanded policy), with the largest gains at α=0.01 (up to 17% reduction on ImageNet). The secondary baseline, APS (Romano et al., 2020), also benefits from TTA, and in fact shows larger absolute improvements than RAPS because TTA tempers overconfident probabilities that APS does not explicitly penalize. A simple average aggregation (TTA-Avg) serves as an ablation baseline; TTA-Learned matches or beats it in all comparisons, confirming that learned augmentation weights (assigning zero weight to unhelpful transforms like blur and shear) provide a consistent but modest edge over uniform averaging. Notably, TTA does not improve CUB-Birds as reliably, likely due to the much smaller validation set (2,827 vs. 25,000–50,000 images), suggesting the method's effectiveness scales with available calibration data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: split conformal prediction (building on the APS score from Romano et al. and the RAPS score from Angelopoulos et al.) and test-time augmentation (building on learned aggregation from Shanmugam et al.). It departs from prior conformal ensemble methods (cross-conformal, bootstrap, aggregated conformal) by generating an ensemble from a single model via input perturbations rather than training multiple base classifiers. It also departs from the TTA literature, which typically focuses on improving the maximum predicted probability or top-1 accuracy, by showing that TTA's value in conformal prediction comes from a different mechanism: promoting the true class's rank among all predicted probabilities even when it is far from the top.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a simple, retraining-free post-hoc step (learned TTA) can reduce conformal prediction set sizes by 10–14% and even bridge the gap between model sizes (ResNet-50+TTA outperforms ResNet-101 alone), which is useful for practitioners who cannot retrain or deploy larger models. However, the approach is limited to image classification, incurs additional inference cost proportional to the number of augmentations, and shows no improvement on CUB-Birds (likely due to small validation set size). Open questions include generalization to non-vision modalities, whether the rank-promotion mechanism holds for other conformal scores or regression settings, and how to select augmentations without labeled validation data.
