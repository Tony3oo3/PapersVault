## Problem

High-resolution 3D reconstruction from monocular video via 3D CNNs requires dense sub-voxel sampling to capture fine surface detail, but this incurs prohibitive compute cost (e.g., FineRecon needs >24 s for surface extraction and produces ~55 MB meshes). The key insight is that the ideal sub-sample rate is both adaptive (proportional to local surface complexity) and anisotropic (varying across axes), yet determining the correct rate a priori is a chicken-and-egg problem: you need the surface structure to set the resolution, but need the resolution to reconstruct the surface.

## Input / Output

The system takes a set of posed RGB images with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a 3D mesh via non-uniform dual marching cubes, along with an underlying continuous TSDF field and occupancy volume. The base voxel grid is fixed at 4 cm; the adaptive sub-sampling operates within each central cube (CC) with per-axis sample rates clamped between 1 and R_max=4 (i.e., finest 1 cm), and a user-specified aliasing threshold Δd=1 cm controls the trade-off between fidelity and sample count. Occupancy filtering prunes voxels with probability <0.5 before sub-sampling.

## Method

The core architectural change re-interprets each voxel's feature vector as a weight vector for a linear combination of 64 learned local 3D basis functions (implemented as a coordinate MLP), rather than as a direct TSDF value. This re-formulation enables an analytical, per-CC, per-axis upper bound on the local TSDF gradient (φ_x(s)) computed from pre-computed basis maxima and the predicted weights, without any sampling. The local sample spacing is then set as Δx = Δd/φ_x(s), guaranteeing no aliasing beyond the user-specified threshold Δd. Mesh extraction uses a modified dual marching cubes that builds the dual grid iteratively from the non-uniform sample rates. Training is fully supervised with a combined loss including gradient supervision (inspired by Neural Poisson), surface-magnitude enforcement, smooth-L1 TSDF distance, occupancy BCE, and a novel basis-gradient loss that penalizes high-gradient basis combinations in flat regions to prevent over-sampling.

## Results

On ScanNet (100 test scenes), AniGrad achieves an F-Score of 74.0 and Chamfer distance of 5.29 cm at 1 cm adaptive resolution, matching FineRecon's F-Score (74.1) while reducing surface extraction latency (SEL) from 24.81 s to 1.11 s (~22×) and mesh size from 54.85 MB to 5.47 MB (~10×). Per-frame feature extraction time is 10.2 ms, faster than FineRecon's 37.8 ms. The method also generalizes to ScanNet++ with no parameter changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FineRecon (the closest competitor at 1 cm resolution), AniGrad ties on F-Score (74.0 vs. 74.1) but loses on Chamfer distance (5.29 vs. 4.39 cm), which the authors attribute to not using depth guidance. However, AniGrad dramatically outperforms FineRecon on efficiency: SEL drops from 24.81 s to 1.11 s (22.3×) and mesh size from 54.85 MB to 5.47 MB (10×). Against the 4 cm uniform baselines (Atlas, NeuralRecon, VoRTX), AniGrad wins decisively on F-Score (74.0 vs. 65.4/60.4/69.5) and is competitive on Chamfer distance (5.29 cm vs. 6.13/7.75/5.59 cm). VoRTX is the strongest 4 cm baseline, achieving the best Chamfer among the coarse methods (5.59 cm) and the lowest mesh size (2.13 MB), but its F-Score is 4.5 points lower than AniGrad's. NeuralRecon and Atlas are clearly outperformed on all accuracy metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D CNN TSDF reconstruction lineage initiated by Atlas [22] and refined by FineRecon [34], which introduced a two-level resolution hierarchy (4 cm voxels with uniform 1 cm sub-sampling). The key departure is re-formulating the network's final layers to output weights over learned local 3D basis functions rather than relying on trilinear interpolation, which enables analytical gradient bounds and thus adaptive, anisotropic sub-sampling. The authors also adapt dual marching cubes [29] to operate on the resulting non-uniform grid. This is, to their knowledge, the first work to study adaptive resolution specifically in the context of image-based TSDF reconstruction with 3D CNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22× reduction in surface extraction latency (24.8 s → 1.1 s) and 10× reduction in mesh size while maintaining state-of-the-art F-score makes high-quality 3D reconstruction feasible on compute-limited hardware such as mobile AR devices and robots. Open questions include whether the resolution can be made coarser than the voxel size for large flat surfaces, whether non-axis-aligned sampling patterns could improve efficiency for arbitrarily oriented geometry, and whether the fixed set of 64 basis functions limits representational capacity. The axis-aligned sampling assumption also means the method is most effective on scenes with predominantly axis-aligned surfaces.
