## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation ignores individual identity, while motion style transfer (MST) relies on a single source motion, limiting the diversity of generated actions. No prior dataset or method supports generating a broad range of text-driven motions that consistently reflect a person's unique style (persona) across different action types. This paper introduces the Motion Personalization task and the PerMo benchmark to fill this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251142/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple atomic motion sequences (e.g., jumping, walking, punching) in HumanML3D format (f×263 vectors encoding joint rotations, positions, velocities, and foot contact) along with a free-form text prompt. It produces a personalized 3D human motion that simultaneously follows the prompt's action content and reflects the persona embedded in the input motions. During inference, Context-Aware Fusion selects the top-k most contextually relevant motions from the input set rather than averaging all, preventing unnatural blending of dissimilar actions.

## Method

PersonaBooth finetunes a pretrained 50-step MDM diffusion model via two parallel adaptation paths: a visual path that injects a persona feature V* through a self-attention adaptive layer in each transformer block, and a textual path where a learnable persona token P* is inserted into the prompt and processed by a frozen CLIP text encoder with zero-gated merging. A supervised contrastive persona cohesion loss (L_pc) trains the Persona Extractor (built on TMR + CLIP) to cluster same-persona features across different action contents, disentangling style from content. For multiple inputs, Context-Aware Fusion computes prompt-motion similarity scores to assign softmax weights over the top-k motions, replacing naive averaging. Training uses classifier-free guidance with separate guidance scales for text and visual modalities, and the base diffusion model and CLIP encoder remain frozen throughout finetuning.

## Results

On the PerMo dataset (single-input setting), PersonaBooth achieves a FID of 3.18, substantially outperforming MoMo (13.91) and MCM-LDM (9.16), while also leading in R-Precision Top-1 (0.15 vs. 0.05 and 0.13), PRA (18.05% vs. 13.47% and 17.00%), and Diversity (7.74 vs. 6.74 and 6.70). With multiple inputs and Context-Aware Fusion, FID further drops to 2.95 and Diversity rises to 8.12. On the 100Style dataset, PersonaBooth (SI) achieves FID 3.27, R-Precision Top-1 0.20, SRA 64.53%, and Diversity 7.90, again surpassing both baselines across all metrics. Notably, PersonaBooth uses only a 50-step diffusion model, whereas MoMo uses 100 steps and MCM-LDM uses 1000 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PersonaBooth decisively outperforms both baselines on all four metrics (FID, R-Precision, PRA/SRA, Diversity) on both PerMo and 100Style. Against MoMo [25], PersonaBooth (SI) reduces FID from 13.91 to 3.18 on PerMo (a 77% reduction) and from 5.97 to 3.27 on 100Style, while improving R-Precision Top-1 from 0.05 to 0.15 and PRA from 13.47% to 18.05%. Against MCM-LDM [32], PersonaBooth cuts FID from 9.16 to 3.18 on PerMo and from 7.20 to 3.27 on 100Style, with gains in R-Precision (0.15 vs. 0.13) and PRA (18.05% vs. 17.00%). The finetuned MCM-LDM* variant narrows the gap slightly on PRA (16.76% vs. 18.05%) but still trails PersonaBooth by a wide margin on FID (9.70 vs. 3.18). Qualitatively, MoMo produces sliding motions that ignore the prompt, and MCM-LDM generates sideward movements, whereas PersonaBooth accurately reflects both the persona style and the text-described action.

## Relation to Prior Work

PersonaBooth adapts the multi-modal finetuning paradigm from image personalization (InstantBooth's adaptive layers and persona tokens) to the motion domain, building on a pretrained MDM diffusion model and a TMR-based motion encoder. It departs from existing Motion Style Transfer methods (MoMo, MCM-LDM) in two key ways: it accepts multiple atomic motions as persona references rather than a single source, and it introduces a textual adaptation path via a learnable persona token that existing MST methods lack. The persona cohesion loss draws on supervised contrastive learning to disentangle persona from action content, addressing a limitation explicitly acknowledged by prior MST work (MoST, MCM-LDM) but previously unsolved in the diffusion-based text-to-motion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PerMo dataset is the first multi-actor, multi-style motion capture collection with mesh, marker, and text annotations, filling a gap that limits reproducibility in motion personalization research. The finding that a 50-step diffusion model with targeted multi-modal finetuning substantially outperforms 100- and 1000-step baselines suggests that careful adaptation of pretrained models is more effective than simply scaling diffusion steps for persona-specific tasks. Open questions include handling longer motion sequences (current PerMo clips are short, causing stationary end poses), applying CAF to sequential multi-action prompts, and whether the persona token approach generalizes to other modalities beyond 3D motion.
