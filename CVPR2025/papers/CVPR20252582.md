## Problem

While proprietary LMMs like GPT-4V/4o have demonstrated strong capability as generalist evaluators for vision-language tasks, no open-source LMM has been specifically trained to serve this role. This creates a dependency on costly commercial APIs for model evaluation and reward signal generation in preference learning, limiting scalability and accessibility for resource-constrained researchers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252582/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a structured sequence: an image, a question, one or more candidate model responses, an optional reference answer, and a task-specific evaluation prompt that defines scoring criteria and output format. It produces either a pointwise numerical score (e.g., 0–100) followed by a multi-line justification, or a pairwise preference judgment (Response A/B is better, or Tie) with image-grounded reasoning. The evaluation prompt varies across 7+ benchmark-specific templates and 30 pairwise ranking templates, requiring the model to adapt its scoring rubric per instruction.

## Method

The paper curates a 113k-sample critic instruction-following dataset (46k images) spanning pointwise scoring (72,782 samples from 7 benchmark prompt templates) and pairwise ranking (40,1k samples from VLFeedback, LLaVA-RLHF, and RLHF-V), where GPT-4o generates high-quality judgment scores and justifications as training targets. LLaVA-OneVision (7B/72B) is fine-tuned for 1 epoch with standard cross-entropy loss applied to both the judgment tokens and the justification text, treating evaluation as an additional instruction-following capability layered on top of existing visual understanding. For preference learning, an iterative DPO pipeline generates K=5 candidate responses per prompt, scores all K×(K−1) ordered pairs via LLaVA-Critic to compute aggregate reward scores, and selects the best/worst response pairs for DPO training over M=3 rounds, progressively improving the base LMM using AI-generated feedback rather than human annotations.

## Results

On the out-of-domain MLLM-as-a-Judge benchmark, LLaVA-Critic-72B achieves a Pearson score correlation of 0.393 with human annotators, surpassing LLaVA-OV-72B (0.287) and all other open-source models, while approaching GPT-4V (0.424 in the authors' re-evaluation). In in-domain pointwise scoring across 7 benchmarks, LLaVA-Critic-72B reaches an average Pearson-r of 0.754 versus 0.634 for the LLaVA-OV-72B baseline. In preference learning, LLaVA-Critic-72B as a reward model outperforms the human-feedback-based LLaVA-RLHF reward model on all 6 evaluation benchmarks (e.g., +9.2 on WildVision-Bench, +2.3 on LiveBench for the 72B base), and LLaVA-Critic-7B similarly surpasses LLaVA-RLHF on 5 of 6 benchmarks for the 7B base.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against commercial models, LLaVA-Critic-72B narrows the gap substantially: it reaches 0.393 score correlation vs. GPT-4V's 0.424 (authors' eval) and 0.490 (original paper), and 0.715 pairwise accuracy (w/o tie) vs. GPT-4V's 0.717, effectively matching GPT-4V on pairwise ranking while trailing on pointwise scoring. Against the strongest open-source baseline LLaVA-OV-72B, LLaVA-Critic-72B improves score correlation from 0.287 to 0.393 (+37% relative) and pairwise accuracy (w/o tie) from 0.701 to 0.715. The 7B variant (0.314 score, 0.689 pair w/o tie) already surpasses Qwen2-VL-7B (0.253, 0.645) and LLaMA3.2-V-11B (0.237, 0.658) by large margins. In preference learning, LLaVA-Critic-72B outperforms the LLaVA-RLHF human-feedback reward model on all 6 benchmarks for the 72B base (e.g., WildVision-Bench 70.0 vs. 65.2), while LLaVA-Critic-7B wins on 5 of 6 for the 7B base (LLaVA-W 100.3 vs. 97.5, WildVision-Bench 67.3 vs. 64.1), though LLaVA-RLHF retains a slight edge on MMHal-Bench (4.01 vs. 3.91). Compared to other self-rewarding methods (SIMA, CSR, RLAIF-V) on LLaVA-v1.5-7B, LLaVA-Critic achieves the largest gains on LLaVA-W (+10.1), LLaVA-Wilder (+3.0), and WildVision-Bench (+8.8) while preserving overall capabilities on MME, MMB-en, and MMStar.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LMM-as-a-Judge paradigm, previously dominated by proprietary models (GPT-4V/4o) and narrow open-source efforts like Prometheus-Vision (trained for specific user-defined rubrics), by training the first open-source *generalist* evaluator via supervised fine-tuning on a curated 113k critic instruction dataset. It builds directly on LLaVA-OneVision as the base architecture and incorporates DPO (Rafailov et al.) as the preference optimization algorithm in its second scenario. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating evaluation as a learnable instruction-following skill rather than an emergent capability of sufficiently large proprietary models, and curating training data that spans both pointwise scoring and pairwise ranking across diverse multimodal benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a free, open-source alternative to GPT-4V/4o for model evaluation and reward signal generation, this work removes a significant cost barrier for resource-constrained research groups and industry teams that need scalable LMM assessment. The finding that a 7B model retains near-72B evaluation quality suggests that evaluation ability is relatively compact and transferable, potentially enabling on-premise evaluation pipelines. Open questions remain: whether iterative DPO with self-generated critic feedback leads to reward hacking or distributional drift over many rounds, whether the evaluator generalizes to truly novel task types outside its training distribution, and whether the approach scales to video and multi-image evaluation with the same fidelity.
