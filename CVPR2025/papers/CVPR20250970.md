## Problem

Existing model poisoning attacks to federated learning suffer from self-cancellation across training rounds because they only enforce consistency within a single round, causing attack effects to reverse and diminish over time. Additionally, most effective attacks require knowledge of genuine clients' model updates or local training data, which is impractical in realistic threat models. No prior attack simultaneously enforces multi-round consistency and operates under minimal attacker knowledge (no access to genuine data, models, or the deployed defense).

## Input / Output

The attacker's inputs are the global models broadcast by the server each round (from which the previous round's aggregated update g^{t-1} = w^{t-1} - w^{t-2} is computed), the attacker's own previous-round malicious updates, and a fixed random sign vector s of dimension d (the model dimensionality). The output is a set of malicious model updates g_i^t = k^t ⊙ s sent by each fake client, where k^t is a dynamically computed non-negative magnitude vector. The attacker needs no access to genuine local data, local models, or the server's aggregation rule/defense, making the attack executable from injected fake clients with only the publicly available global models.

## Method

PoisonedFL enforces multi-round consistency by fixing a random sign vector s at the start and expressing every malicious update as a dimension-wise product g_i^t = k^t ⊙ s, so that aggregated updates across rounds accumulate along the same direction rather than self-cancelling. The magnitude vector is decomposed as k^t = λ^t · v^t: the unit vector v^t is estimated by subtracting the normalized known malicious contribution from the previous round's aggregated update (g^{t-1}), and the scaling factor λ^t = c^t · ‖w^{t-1} - w^{t-2}‖ adapts to the observed update magnitude. The coefficient c^t is dynamically adjusted via a binomial hypothesis test over the past e rounds: if the fraction of dimensions in the cumulative update matching s is not significantly above 0.5 (p-value 0.01), the attack is deemed filtered by the defense and c^t is multiplied by β < 1 to reduce stealthiness. This closed-loop mechanism allows the attack to maintain large cumulative displacement along s while evading unknown Byzantine-robust aggregation rules, provably robust defenses, and client-detection methods.

## Results

PoisonedFL increases the testing error rate of the global model by 15.84 to 90.67 percentage points across five datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, Purchase, CIFAR-10, FEMNIST) and nine FL methods (FedAvg plus eight defenses). In 29 out of 45 dataset-defense combinations, PoisonedFL drives the global model to near-random-guessing or worse (≥50% error). Under the non-robust FedAvg baseline, PoisonedFL achieves 90–99% testing error on all five datasets, matching or exceeding the best existing attacks. Under defenses, PoisonedFL achieves 70–99% error in most cases, while existing attacks typically remain below 30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoisonedFL is compared against seven existing attacks: five that require genuine clients' model updates (Fang, Opt. Fang, LIE, Min-Max, Min-Sum) and two that do not (Random, MPAF). Under defenses, PoisonedFL outperforms all seven attacks in 32 out of 40 cases, with gaps ranging from 2.11 to 84.33 percentage points in testing error rate. Against the strongest information-privileged baseline (Opt. Fang), PoisonedFL achieves dramatically higher error under defenses: e.g., 75.28% vs. 6.80% on MNIST+Multi-Krum, 90.00% vs. 45.02% on CIFAR-10+Multi-Krum, and 95.18% vs. 32.25% on FEMNIST+Multi-Krum. Against MPAF (the closest comparable attack under the same no-knowledge threat model), PoisonedFL achieves 59–86 percentage points higher error on MNIST defenses and 55–60 points on CIFAR-10 defenses. The only cases where PoisonedFL does not strictly dominate are two instances where both it and an existing attack already reach ~90% error (e.g., CIFAR-10+Norm Bound where MPAF also reaches 90.02%), and Purchase+FLAME where PoisonedFL (28.09%) is lower than Opt. Fang (11.89%)—though even there the absolute error remains modest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the entire lineage of model poisoning attacks to FL (Fang et al., LIE, Opt. Fang, Min-Max, Min-Sum, MPAF, Random) by identifying a fundamental flaw they all share: their malicious model updates are inconsistent across training rounds, causing the attack effect to self-cancel. Rather than building on any single prior attack, PoisonedFL introduces a new formulation—enforcing multi-round consistency via a fixed random sign vector combined with a dynamically adjusted magnitude vector—that is orthogonal to the direction-matching or magnitude-matching strategies used by prior work. The paper also explicitly positions itself in the minimal-knowledge threat model (fake clients only, no genuine data, no defense knowledge), which aligns with MPAF but extends it with the multi-round consistency mechanism and hypothesis-testing-based stealth adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that eight state-of-the-art FL defenses (Multi-Krum, Median, Trimmed Mean, Norm Bound, FLTrust, FLAME, FLCert, FLDetector) are all broken by a single attack requiring only 20% fake clients and no knowledge of defenses or genuine data fundamentally undermines the security guarantees these defenses were designed to provide. The self-cancellation observation also reframes how the community should evaluate attack effectiveness: prior attacks may have been underperforming not because defenses were strong, but because the attacks were self-defeating. Open questions include whether truly robust defenses exist that can handle consistent-direction attacks without sacrificing utility, and how these findings translate to cross-silo FL settings with fewer clients and stricter access controls, which the paper acknowledges as a limitation.
