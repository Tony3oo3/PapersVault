## Problem

Accurate anthropometric measurement extraction from images or point clouds remains challenging because existing two-stage methods depend heavily on the reconstruction quality of parametric body models, which degrades under varying camera angles, distances, and postures. No ViT-based two-stage pipeline exists for body measurement estimation, and no public dataset simultaneously provides RGB images, point clouds, meshes, and ground-truth measurement values for training and evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251575/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a single RGB image (cropped to a whole-body bounding box and resized to 512×384, then patchified into 32×24 tokens of size 16) or point clouds derived from Mojave Flash LiDAR amplitude and distance files. It outputs SMPL pose θ, shape β, and camera translation π, from which a 3D body mesh is generated; anthropometric measurements (chest, waist, hip, wrist circumferences and shoulder width) are then computed as sums of Manhattan distances between adjacent vertices on predefined ISO 8559 measurement planes intersecting the SMPL template surface.

## Method

The architecture freezes the SMPLer-X ViT-H/16 backbone and attaches a lightweight plug-and-play Bypass Network (CNN + ResNet blocks with SirenReLU activation) that extracts high-frequency guidance features via residual connections, preventing loss of raw image information during the ViT tokenization pipeline. A Dynamic Loss (L_DL) introduces a time-varying weight α that progressively shifts optimization emphasis from global body keypoints toward local measurement-part vertices, so the network learns to reconstruct anthropometric regions with higher fidelity. The bypass network is trained for only 20 epochs (~20 min) on 8 RTX 4090 GPUs while the backbone is fine-tuned for 10 epochs with Adam and cosine annealing (lr 1e-5). Measurements are extracted by intersecting k user-defined planes with the deformed SMPL template and summing Manhattan distances between consecutive intersection vertices, yielding the final circumference or width values.

## Results

On the Body Measurements Dataset, Our-BMViT achieves MAE of 3.29 cm (chest), 6.73 cm (waist), 8.27 cm (hip), and 2.29 cm (shoulder width), outperforming all baselines. On the proposed Fashion-body Dataset, the method achieves MAE of 3.07 cm (chest), 3.91 cm (waist), 6.95 cm (hip), 0.41 cm (wrist), and 2.03 cm (shoulder width). On LiDAR point cloud data, Our-BMViT further reduces errors to 3.07 cm (chest), 3.41 cm (waist), 5.71 cm (hip), 0.41 cm (wrist), and 1.53 cm (shoulder width). Against commercial apps, the method achieves 3.32 cm chest MAE vs. 5.21 cm (3DLook) and 3.63 cm (Verifyt).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Linear Regression [Bartol et al., 2022], NeuralAnthro [González Tejeda & Mayer, 2021], and two self-defined transformer-based baselines (HMR-BMViT and 4D-BMViT). Our-BMViT wins on every measurement across both datasets. On the Body Measurements Dataset, it reduces chest MAE from 5.19 cm (4D-BMViT, best baseline) to 3.29 cm and hip MAE from 9.29 cm (HMR-BMViT) to 8.27 cm. On Fashion-body, the gap widens: waist MAE drops from 4.88 cm (HMR-BMViT) to 3.91 cm, and shoulder width from 3.88 cm to 2.03 cm. NeuralAnthro is the weakest baseline, with hip MAE reaching 17.70 cm on the Body Measurements Dataset and 20.04 cm on Fashion-body. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that both the bypass network and the dynamic loss contribute: removing the dynamic loss increases chest MAE from 3.07 to 5.61 cm, and removing the bypass network increases it to 4.73 cm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage template-based measurement pipeline (reconstruct SMPL/SMPL-X parameters, then compute measurements on the deformed mesh) by introducing a ViT-H/16 backbone via SMPLer-X and a lightweight CNN/ResNet bypass network that provides measurement-part-specific supervision to the frozen backbone. It departs from prior ViT-based human mesh recovery work (4D-Humans, HMR) by treating the backbone as a frozen foundation model and adding a plug-and-play focusing module rather than end-to-end training. The dynamic loss function, which re-weights vertex-level reconstruction error between measurement and non-measurement regions, is a novel training signal not present in prior SMPLer-X or HMR formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Fashion-body dataset is the first public benchmark combining depth, point clouds, meshes, and ground-truth anthropometric measurements, addressing a noted gap in the literature, though its small scale (30 subjects, ages 18–24, A-pose only) limits generalizability claims. The practical relevance to mass-customized apparel and the demonstrated parity with commercial scanning apps (Verifyt, 3DLook) on a small 6-subject test suggest a viable path toward privacy-preserving measurement via LiDAR. Open questions include whether the bypass-network-plus-dynamic-loss recipe transfers to a broader set of ISO 8559 measurements, non-A-pose scenarios, and diverse populations, and whether the frozen-backbone assumption holds as the underlying foundation model is updated.
