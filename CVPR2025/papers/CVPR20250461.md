## Problem

Most class incremental learning (CIL) methods rely on linear classifiers that activate all weights equally across tasks, causing irrelevant weights to drift during new-task training and exacerbating catastrophic forgetting. While Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN) showed natural locality benefits in simple 1D continual regression, their B-spline basis functions suffer from the curse of dimensionality on high-dimensional feature embeddings, forcing excessive backbone updates that destroy the feature space. No prior work had identified this limitation or developed a KAN-based classifier specifically suited to high-dimensional CIL.

## Input / Output

KAC operates as a drop-in replacement for the linear classifier in existing prompt-based CIL pipelines. It consumes 768-dimensional feature embeddings from a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone (after layer normalization) and produces classification logits over all classes encountered so far. The classifier uses N=4 Gaussian RBF centers evenly distributed in [-2, 2] with σ=1, and expands its weight matrix W ∈ R^{C×(N×n)} by concatenation when new classes arrive, adding only ~0.23M parameters for 100 classes.

## Method

KAC replaces the conventional linear classifier with a single KAN layer whose basis functions are Gaussian Radial Basis Functions rather than B-splines. The key insight is that B-splines cannot effectively model compositional structure in high-dimensional data, so the model compensates by over-updating the backbone, destroying the feature space; Gaussian RBFs instead induce a per-channel Gaussian mixture distribution that provides strong long-range structure (addressing the curse of dimensionality) while preserving the Gaussian classification-space assumption exploited by many CIL methods. Concretely, each input channel x'_p is mapped through N Gaussian RBFs centered at c_i, producing an N×n activation matrix that is then weighted by W_q (per-channel, per-class univariate activations) and W_C (class-specific output weights) to yield the final logits. This learnable per-channel activation allows the model to selectively update only the relevant portions of each channel for new classes while leaving deactivated regions of old tasks untouched, directly leveraging KAN's locality to mitigate forgetting.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (200 classes), replacing the linear classifier with KAC yields consistent gains across all four prompt-based baselines, with average incremental accuracy improvements of 0.7–2.7 points at 20 steps and 1.9–3.0 points at 40 steps (e.g., CODA-Prompt: 80.92→83.59 Avg, 74.40→77.94 Last at 20 steps). On the fine-grained CUB200 dataset, improvements are dramatically larger, reaching 19–21 points in average accuracy and 25–27 points in last-task accuracy at 40 steps (e.g., DualPrompt: 50.61→71.31 Avg, 37.55→64.69 Last). On DomainNet (Domain Incremental Learning, 345 classes, 6 domains), KAC provides a more modest but consistent ~1 point improvement in average incremental accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAC outperforms the linear-classifier version of every baseline across all three datasets and all sequence lengths, with no comparison where KAC degrades performance. On ImageNet-R, the largest gains appear for DualPrompt (+4.00 Last at 20 steps, +4.93 Last at 40 steps) and CODA-Prompt (+3.54 Last at 20 steps, +4.97 Last at 40 steps), while CPrompt sees the smallest improvement (+1.41 Last at 20 steps). On CUB200, the advantage is far more pronounced: DualPrompt gains +27.14 points in last-task accuracy at 40 steps, L2P gains +25.02, and CODA-Prompt gains +26.75, whereas CPrompt (already the strongest baseline) improves by +11.71. On DomainNet, all four methods improve by roughly 0.4–2.0 points, confirming robustness beyond CIL. Notably, the paper observes that CODA-Prompt surpasses CPrompt when both use KAC (83.59 vs. 82.56 Avg at 20 steps on ImageNet-R), whereas with linear classifiers they are nearly tied, suggesting KAC's Gaussian RBF structure interacts differently with each method's prompt design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) architecture [41] and the FastKAN result [37] showing that B-spline basis functions can be replaced by Radial Basis Functions. It departs from the dominant CIL paradigm where nearly all prompt-based and pre-trained-model methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, CPrompt) rely on linear or nearest-class-mean classifiers, by introducing a non-linear KAN-based classifier with learnable per-channel activations. The key conceptual departure is identifying that KAN's B-spline locality—beneficial in 1D continual regression—actually harms high-dimensional CIL due to the curse of dimensionality, and that RBFs restore the locality advantage by naturally inducing a Gaussian mixture structure aligned with the Gaussian-space assumptions already exploited by methods like RanPAC and GKEAL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KAC offers a genuinely plug-and-play classifier that requires no architectural changes, hyperparameter re-tuning, or additional training tricks, making it easy to adopt across existing CIL pipelines. The CUB200 results (10–25 point gains in long-sequence settings) suggest the Gaussian-mixture inductive bias is particularly valuable for fine-grained classification, though the ImageNet-R gains are more modest (1–5 points). Limitations include evaluation on only four prompt-based methods with a single ViT-B/16 backbone, non-exemplar settings only, and 200-class datasets; whether KAC transfers to larger-scale benchmarks (full ImageNet), other backbones, or exemplar-based methods remains open. The paper also leaves open whether the RBF choice is truly optimal or whether other basis functions with similar compositional structure could match or exceed it.
