## Problem

No existing multi-view dataset simultaneously satisfies all four requirements for immersive volumetric video: full 360° foreground-and-background capture, high-quality 6-DoF interaction space, multimodal (visual + auditory) content, and high-resolution/long-duration dynamic sequences. Prior datasets are either static camera arrays with limited frontal views, monocular handheld captures with small FOV, or lack synchronized audio entirely, leaving a critical gap for research on complete immersive VR experiences.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 39–46 synchronized GoPro video streams at 5312×2988 resolution and 60 FPS (with co-located audio) captured from a hemispherical mobile rig, along with COLMAP-calibrated camera poses; sequences range from 1 to 5 minutes per scene. The light field output is a real-time-renderable dynamic 3DGS representation (60+ FPS on a single GPU), while the sound field output is binaural spatial audio computed per-frame via HRTF lookup and distance scaling in the STFT domain. Together they produce a 6-DoF multimodal VR experience where both visual and auditory feedback change with user position and orientation.

## Method

The primary contribution is the ImViD dataset captured by a 46-camera hemispherical rig mounted on a remotely controlled mobile cart, employing two strategies: fixed-point shooting for dynamic detail and slow-moving shooting for larger exploratory volume (~0.10 m³/s spatiotemporal capture density). For the light field baseline, the authors adopt STG (Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting) and improve it to STG++ by introducing a global, time-invariant color mapping during training to eliminate the flickering and floaters caused by inter-camera color inconsistencies during viewpoint transitions. For the sound field, they propose a training-free auralization pipeline that decomposes binaural synthesis into direction mapping (via arccos of relative vectors), distance scaling (inverse-distance ratio), and STFT-domain filtering using HRTFs from the SADIE II database, requiring only a single recording microphone as reference. The full pipeline is benchmarked against 4DGS and 4DrotorGS on three scenes, with STG++ achieving the best PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS trade-off and highest rendering speed (~110–138 FPS on A100).

## Results

On the ImViD dataset, the proposed STG++ (improved STG with global color mapping) achieves the best reconstruction quality across all three tested scenes: PSNR 31.24 / SSIM 0.799 / LPIPS 0.277 on Scene1 (Opera), PSNR 27.58 / SSIM 0.916 / LPIPS 0.107 on Scene2 (Laboratory), and PSNR 20.53 / SSIM 0.597 / LPIPS 0.202 on Scene6 (Puppy). STG++ also delivers the highest rendering speed among quality-competitive methods (108–138 FPS on a single A100 GPU) with moderate model size (329–1276 MB). In the sound field user study (21 experts), 61.9% rated auditory spatial perception as 'excellent' and 90.5% found the reconstructed binaural audio immersive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STG++ consistently outperforms all three baselines (4DGS, 4DRotor, and original STG) on every metric across all three scenes. Against 4DGS, STG++ gains the largest margin on the texture-rich outdoor Scene6 (Puppy), where 4DGS drops to PSNR 18.12 and SSIM 0.222 due to its reliance on a single-frame sparse point cloud initialization. Against 4DRotor, STG++ achieves comparable or better quality while rendering 100–200× faster (108–138 FPS vs. 0.56–1.00 FPS) and using roughly 5–17× less memory. Against the original STG, STG++ improves PSNR by 2.8–1.2 dB across scenes, primarily by eliminating color flickering and floaters during viewpoint transitions through a global time-invariant color mapping. Notably, 4DRotor achieves the best SSIM on Scene2 (0.917) and Scene6 (0.298 vs. STG++'s 0.597 is actually higher), but its extremely low rendering speed makes it impractical for real-time VR use.

## Relation to Prior Work

ImViD positions itself at the intersection of dynamic novel view synthesis datasets and immersive VR media, departing from all prior multi-view datasets (Panoptic Sports, ZJU-Mocap, Immersive Light Field, Replay, 360+X) by introducing a moving capture rig that simultaneously provides 360° foreground-background coverage, 6-DoF interaction space, synchronized audio, and 5K@60FPS long-duration content. The reconstruction pipeline builds directly on the 3DGS-based dynamic scene paradigm (STG, 4DGS, 4DrotorGS), with the STG++ variant adding a global time-invariant color mapping to address inter-view color inconsistency. The sound field reconstruction is training-free, leveraging HRTF lookup (SADIE II) and a simplified room-impulse-response scaling model, contrasting with learned approaches like AV-NeRF, NeRAF, and AV-GS that require extensive multi-location audio training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first dataset explicitly targeting all four criteria of immersive volumetric video (full 360° FG+BG, 6-DoF, multimodal, high-res/long-duration), ImViD provides a much-needed benchmark that exposes the limitations of current dynamic 3DGS methods on texture-rich outdoor scenes and long sequences. The moving-rig capture data remains uncalibrated with no existing reconstruction solution, representing a significant open problem for the field. The training-free sound field approach, while effective for single-source scenarios, does not yet handle multi-source or complex acoustic environments, and the overall pipeline's 60 FPS real-time rendering on a single 3090 GPU sets a practical bar for future immersive VR applications.
