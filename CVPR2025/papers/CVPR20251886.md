## Problem

Existing training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) methods designed for natural images produce distorted target shapes and ill-fitting boundaries on remote sensing images, where spatial detail is critical and object scales span from meters to kilometers. The root causes are CLIP's excessively low-resolution features (1/16th of input) and the global bias embedded in patch tokens from contrastive pretraining, both of which are more detrimental in the remote sensing context than in natural image segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a remote sensing RGB image (satellite or UAV) resized to a long side of 448 px (or 896 px for building extraction) and a text prompt in the OpenAI ImageNet template format (e.g., 'a photo of a {class name}'). Inference uses slide windows of 224×224 with 112 px stride to handle full-resolution output. The output is a full-resolution pixel-level segmentation mask; SimFeatUp is pre-trained once on 16k unlabeled images from Million-AID and then frozen, making the segmentation pipeline itself training-free.

## Method

SegEarth-OV combines two key components. First, SimFeatUp is a lightweight feature upsampler that applies a single parameterized Joint Bilateral Upsampling (JBU) module with an 11×11 kernel, repeated 4 times for 16× upsampling, to CLIP features taken from the input of the last Transformer block (projected to the multimodal space) rather than the final output, ensuring consistency with the modulated self-self attention used at inference. It is trained with a combined loss of feature reconstruction and an image reconstruction loss through a two-layer Content Retention Network (CRN) to preserve spatial content. Second, a simple subtraction of the [CLS] token (scaled by λ=0.3) from all patch tokens alleviates the global bias that contaminates local features during dense prediction. The baseline additionally replaces the last self-attention with a modulated self-self attention (sum of q-q, k-k, v-v attention weighted on v) and removes the FFN and residual connection of the final block.

## Results

On 8 remote sensing semantic segmentation datasets, SegEarth-OV achieves an average mIoU of 39.2%, surpassing the previous best (ClearCLIP, 33.4%) by 5.8 points. On single-class extraction tasks, SegEarth-OV delivers 8.2% average IoU improvement on building extraction (e.g., 49.2% on WHU Aerial vs. 36.6% for ClearCLIP), 4.0% on road extraction (35.4% on CHN6-CUG), and 15.3% on flood detection (60.2% on WBS-SI vs. 44.9%). The method also generalizes to natural images, where SimFeatUp as a plug-and-play module improves MaskCLIP's average mIoU by 5.7 points on PASCAL Context59, COCO-Stuff, and Cityscapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegEarth-OV outperforms all five training-free OVSS baselines (CLIP, MaskCLIP, SCLIP, GEM, ClearCLIP) on every one of the 8 semantic segmentation datasets, with the largest margins on UDD5 (+9.0 over ClearCLIP) and OpenEarthMap (+9.3). On building extraction, SegEarth-OV leads by 12.6 IoU on WHU Aerial (49.2 vs. 36.6 ClearCLIP) and 5.6 on Inria (44.6 vs. 39.0). On flood detection (WBS-SI), the gap is the most dramatic: 60.2% IoU vs. 44.9% for ClearCLIP, a 15.3-point gain. On road extraction, all methods perform poorly (best IoU only 35.4%), but SegEarth-OV still leads on all four road datasets, with the largest advantage on CHN6-CUG (35.4 vs. 25.5). The plug-and-play ablation (Table 4) confirms that SimFeatUp + global bias alleviation consistently improves stronger baselines more: adding the module to ClearCLIP yields +8.1 mIoU on OpenEarthMap and +14.5 on WHU Aerial, versus only +3.3 and +5.6 when added to the weaker MaskCLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based training-free OVSS paradigm (MaskCLIP, SCLIP, GEM, ClearCLIP) from natural images to remote sensing, identifying that the low-resolution feature maps (1/16 downsampled) and global bias in patch tokens are particularly detrimental in the remote sensing context where objects span a logarithmic scale from meters to kilometers. The upsampling component builds directly on FeatUp's JBU-based framework but reconfigures it for the training-free setting: upsampling earlier-stage features to avoid train/inference mismatch, adding an image reconstruction loss via a lightweight CRN, and simplifying to a single JBU module. The global bias alleviation departs from prior work that modifies attention mechanisms (self-self attention, proxy attention) by instead applying a trivial subtraction of the [CLS] token from patch tokens, motivated by the observation that CLIP's contrastive training attaches global properties to local tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first training-free OVSS method for remote sensing, this work demonstrates that pixel-level open-vocabulary perception is feasible for Earth observation even when VLMs are pretrained exclusively on natural images, opening a path toward scalable, annotation-free land cover mapping. The plug-and-play nature of SimFeatUp and the global bias subtraction means these components can be attached to future stronger remote sensing VLMs as they emerge. However, road extraction remains uniformly poor (best IoU 35.4%) across all methods, and fine-grained datasets like iSAID (21.7% mIoU) reveal that training-free OVSS still struggles with subtle category distinctions. An open question is whether domain-specific remote sensing VLMs (GeoRSCLIP, SkyCLIP) would benefit more substantially from these components, since the paper shows they currently underperform or only marginally outperform vanilla CLIP on OVSS.
