## Problem

Fine-tuning step-distilled (≤2-step) diffusion models with arbitrary reward signals via RL is fundamentally broken: policy-based objectives degenerate because the second sampling step is deterministic, the noisy-to-clean mapping is highly non-smooth (high Lipschitz constant) making gradient estimation unstable, and limited stochasticity creates a hard exploration problem. Existing methods like DDPO, Diffusion-DPO, and PSO either fail to train stably or produce blurred images on two-step models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252408/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and Gaussian noise as input and produces a generated image at 1024² resolution (SSD-1B-LCM) or 512² (SDXL-Turbo) in at most 2 sampling steps. The target reward signal can be an arbitrary black-box function (e.g., Image Reward, Attribute Binding Score, Text Alignment Score) that need not be differentiable. The surrogate reward model operates entirely in the SDXL latent space, avoiding back-propagation through the VAE decoder during optimization.

## Method

LaSRO learns a differentiable surrogate reward in the latent space using the UNet encoder of a pretrained latent DM (SDXL) as backbone with a CNN prediction head, trained via Bradley-Terry loss on winner/loser pairs selected by the target reward. The fine-tuning stage alternates between (a) optimizing the two-step DM with normalized, clipped surrogate reward scores applied to both the 1st-step and 2nd-step latent outputs plus an LCM consistency regularization loss, and (b) online adapting the surrogate to track distribution drift of the fine-tuned model. This avoids policy gradient estimation entirely by providing direct reward gradient guidance, and enables efficient off-policy exploration by sampling multiple initial noise vectors rather than relying on the limited stochasticity of the two-step sampler. The paper further establishes a formal connection to value-based RL, showing the surrogate reward is functionally equivalent to a learned value function Q, explaining why off-policy exploration and gradient-based guidance outperform on-policy policy-gradient methods for short-horizon MDPs.

## Results

On the primary test bed (SSD-1B-LCM at 1024² resolution), LaSRO achieves an Image Reward of 0.998 for 2-step generation and 0.946 for 1-step generation on the out-of-distribution MJHQ-30K prompts, substantially outperforming the strongest RL baselines (RLCM/DDPO at 0.830, GORS-LCM at 0.836, PSO/Diff-DPO at 0.821) and the un-fine-tuned LCM baseline (0.781 for 2-step). LaSRO also generalizes across architectures: applied to SDXL-Turbo at 512², it lifts Image Reward from 0.839 (2-step) to 0.957, and from 0.817 (1-step) to 0.904. The method further improves non-differentiable rewards (Attribute Binding Score and Text Alignment Score) where other RL baselines fail due to training instability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaSRO decisively outperforms all three RL baselines on the primary 2-step 1024² Image Reward metric: it reaches 0.998 versus RLCM/DDPO (0.830, +0.168), GORS-LCM (0.836, +0.162), and PSO/Diff-DPO (0.821, +0.177). The gap is even more pronounced for 1-step generation, where LaSRO achieves 0.946 while the un-fine-tuned LCM baseline sits at 0.609 and no RL baseline is reported for 1-step. On the secondary non-differentiable rewards (Attribute Binding Score and Text Alignment Score), LaSRO is the only method that trains stably and improves over the LCM baseline; RLCM and PSO fail due to training instability, leaving GORS-LCM as the only viable comparison, which LaSRO also surpasses. Cross-architecture results confirm LaSRO's generality: on SDXL-Turbo (512²) it gains +0.065 (1-step) and +0.118 (2-step) over the base model, demonstrating that the method is agnostic to the specific step-distillation technique used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward fine-tuning of diffusion models (established by DDPO, Diffusion-DPO, and reward-weighted regression methods) to the challenging regime of ≤2-step step-distilled models, where the authors show that policy-based RL objectives degenerate due to the deterministic second sampling step, non-smooth mappings, and limited stochasticity. Rather than adapting policy gradient or preference-optimization methods, LaSRO departs from this lineage by learning a differentiable surrogate reward in the latent space of a pre-trained latent DM (using its UNet encoder as backbone), effectively converting arbitrary rewards—including non-differentiable ones—into gradient signals. The approach is positioned as a value-based RL method, drawing a theoretical connection to Q-learning that prior DM fine-tuning work (which has been almost exclusively policy-based) had not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaSRO makes reward fine-tuning of ultra-fast (≤2-step) image generation practically feasible, a setting where all prior RL methods (DDPO, Diffusion-DPO, PPO variants) fail or produce degraded outputs. The latent-space surrogate reward is a general-purpose mechanism that works with any reward signal (differentiable or not), opening the door to flexible model alignment for few-step generators without requiring differentiable reward functions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to other step-distillation techniques beyond LCMs and SDXL-Turbo, how the surrogate reward handles distribution shift in long training runs, and whether the value-based RL connection can be formalized more rigorously to guide further algorithmic design.
