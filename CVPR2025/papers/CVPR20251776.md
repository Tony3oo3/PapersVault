## Problem

Existing interleaved image-text datasets (MMC4, OBELICS) suffer from poor narrative coherence, inconsistent entity and visual style across images, and weak semantic alignment between images and text, which limits MLLMs' ability to generate coherent interleaved content. The paper asks how to construct a high-quality interleaved image-text dataset that simultaneously ensures developed text structure, consistent visual style, and strong image-text alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251776/cropped_page1_idx26.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises 227K interleaved image-text documents (2.28M images) sourced from instructional websites (e.g., WikiHow) and visual storytelling pages, with a median of 4 images per document. The four benchmark tasks consume different input formats: sequential images (Task 1), sequential text descriptions (Task 2), partial interleaved content of k steps (Task 3), or a single text prompt/question (Task 4), and produce corresponding text sequences, image sequences, or full interleaved image-text documents. A preference dataset variant pairs each positive document with four types of shuffled negatives (text-only, image-only, both, or step-order shuffles) for DPO training.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-perspective quality filter applied to raw interleaved documents. The Text Sequence Filter uses LLMs (Llama3) to score contextual development and coherence, discarding incoherent sequences. The Image Sequence Filter computes a custom metric F(·) via CLIP's visual encoder that balances average consecutive-image similarity (promoting smooth transitions) against average all-pair similarity (ensuring visual diversity and development). The Image-Text Alignment Filter combines CLIP cosine scores (threshold 0.1) with MLLM-based (GPT-4o/Llama3) context-aware assessment to handle the gap between caption-style alignment and step-level narrative alignment. After filtering, negative samples are generated by four shuffling strategies to form a preference dataset for DPO post-training, and four new benchmark tasks with a GPT-4o-based evaluation framework (IRS, style/entity/trend consistency, completeness, image quality) are introduced for comprehensive assessment.

## Results

On the few-shot in-context understanding benchmark (Table 2), CoMM-trained Open-Flamingo 9B achieves 100.3 CIDEr on COCO (0-shot) and 111.9 (32-shot), outperforming MMC4 (97.1 / 94.9) and OBELICS (83.5 / 96.5). On TextVQA, CoMM reaches 33.5% VQA accuracy at 0-shot versus 23.4% (MMC4) and 25.6% (OBELICS), and 34.9% at 32-shot versus 20.0% and 26.8% respectively. In dataset quality evaluation (Table 1), CoMM scores 7.46/8.91 on Image-Text Alignment (Llama3/GPT-4o) compared to 6.53/4.66 (MMC4) and 5.00/3.81 (OBELICS), with an Image Sequence metric of 4.27 versus 0.21 and 1.00.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MMC4 [45], CoMM consistently outperforms across nearly all tasks and shot settings: at 0-shot, CoMM gains +3.2 CIDEr on COCO (100.3 vs 97.1), +10.1 on TextVQA (33.5 vs 23.4), and +11.5 on OKVQA (41.3 vs 29.8); at 32-shot, the gap widens to +17.0 on COCO (111.9 vs 94.9) and +14.9 on TextVQA (34.9 vs 20.0). Against OBELICS [19], CoMM leads on COCO (+15.4 at 32-shot), Flickr30k (+17.6 at 32-shot), TextVQA (+8.1 at 32-shot), and OKVQA (+3.0 at 32-shot), though OBELICS slightly edges CoMM on VQAv2 at 8-shot (44.5 vs 44.5, tied) and HatefulMemes at 16-shot (57.8 vs 59.9, CoMM wins). Among the model baselines in Table 3, Emu2 (33B) achieves the strongest continuation and question-based generation scores (e.g., Style 8.22, Entity 7.99 on Task 3), while SEED-Llama (14B) leads on IRS for I2T generation (8.13) and T2I generation (6.24). MiniGPT-5 (7B) achieves the best METEOR/ROUGE on I2T generation but lags on IRS (7.39) and all image-generation metrics. After DPO preference training on CoMM, SEED-Llama (14B) improves from 8.13 to 8.18 IRS and from 8.17 to 10.30 ROUGE_L (whole document) on I2T generation, confirming the preference dataset's utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself squarely in the data-centric line of work on interleaved image-text generation, building on the task formulations from OpenLEAF, Emu, and DreamLLM while departing from the large-scale web-scraping approach of MMC4 and OBELICS. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it argues that the bottleneck for coherent interleaved generation is training data quality, and addresses this through a multi-perspective filtering pipeline (LLM-based text coherence, CLIP-based image sequence consistency, and MLLM-based image-text alignment) applied to curated instructional and storytelling sources. The preference dataset construction via shuffled sequences for DPO training extends the standard SFT-only paradigm used in prior interleaved generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that coherent, filtered data at 227K documents outperforms billion-scale unfiltered corpora for interleaved generation, which has practical implications for resource-constrained groups building MLLMs. The four new benchmark tasks and the Illustration Relevance Score metric fill a gap in how the community evaluates open-ended multimodal generation beyond captioning-style metrics. However, the dataset's focus on instructional content (WikiHow-style) and visual stories may limit generalization to more diverse domains, and the heavy reliance on GPT-4o as an evaluation judge introduces a circularity concern. Open questions include whether the filtering strategy transfers to non-instructional content and whether the preference dataset approach scales to larger model families.
