## Problem

Polynomial networks like MONet are the only architecture family that produces identical outputs on plaintext and LFHE-encrypted data, making them essential for security-preserving inference. However, like ViTs and CNNs, MONet suffers from catastrophic forgetting when learning sequential tasks, and no prior method enables incremental learning under the strict LFHE constraint that only addition and multiplication are permitted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250995/cropped_page4_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts standard RGB images (or their LFHE-encrypted equivalents) and outputs a single class label, with the critical guarantee that decrypted outputs from encrypted inputs match plaintext results exactly. In the class-incremental, exemplar-free, task-agnostic setting, the model must distinguish all previously seen classes at inference without being given a task ID and without access to any data from prior tasks during training of subsequent tasks.

## Method

The paper introduces PoLoRA, a low-rank adaptation (W₀ + BA) tailored to MONet's channel projections (PoLoRA-C) or entire Mu-Layers (PoLoRA-M), where the update operates on pixel-wise feature vectors and uses only multiplication and addition. The key insight (Proposition 1) is that fine-tuning the up-projection B_t is equivalent to fine-tuning the pre-trained weights within the subspace spanned by the rows of the down-projection A_t. To prevent interference with old tasks, A_t is initialized via Gradient Projection Memory so its rows lie in the intersection of the new task's gradient space and the orthogonal complement of all prior tasks' gradient spaces, then frozen; only B_t is trained with a local cross-entropy loss. All PoLoRA branches are concatenated at inference for task-agnostic prediction, and a GPM scaling factor α_GPM downscales the down-projection to control the magnitude of the low-rank update.

## Results

On the standard 10-task splits, IPoLoRA-C achieves 62.85% average accuracy on ImageNet-R and 70.70% on CIFAR-100, while IPoLoRA-M reaches 60.09% and 69.21% respectively. Both IPoLoRA variants outperform the strongest baseline (Classifier FT) by at least 17 percentage points across all dataset/task configurations. On the more challenging 20-task ImageNet-R setting, IPoLoRA-C still attains 57.78% versus 40.25% for Classifier FT, and on PACS (4 domain tasks) it reaches 77.56% versus 54.90%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Full Fine-tuning (all parameters updated), Classifier Fine-tuning (only the final FC layer updated), and two ablated versions of the proposed method without GPM (PoLoRA-M and PoLoRA-C). IPoLoRA-C dominates all baselines on every dataset and task split, with the largest margin over Classifier FT being ~22 points on ImageNet-R (10 tasks) and ~23 points on CIFAR-100 (5 tasks). Full Fine-tuning suffers severe catastrophic forgetting, dropping to 8.75% on ImageNet-R (20 tasks) and 11.35% on CIFAR-100 (10 tasks), far below even Classifier FT. PoLoRA-C (LoRA without GPM) provides a moderate 6–14 point gain over Classifier FT, while PoLoRA-M without GPM actually underperforms Classifier FT, confirming that the GPM initialization is critical for the Mu-Layer-integrated variant. Adding GPM yields an additional ~10 points for IPoLoRA-C and ~30 points for IPoLoRA-M over their non-GPM counterparts, demonstrating that orthogonal gradient projection is the key mechanism preventing interference between tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: polynomial networks for homomorphic encryption (MONet, Cheng et al. 2024), parameter-efficient fine-tuning via low-rank adaptation (LoRA, Hu et al. 2021), and gradient-based continual learning (GPM, Saha et al. 2021). It departs from prior PEFT-for-continual-learning work (InFLORA, adapter-based methods) by restricting the backbone to polynomial-only operations compatible with Leveled FHE, a constraint that eliminates all non-linear activations and rules out standard ViT/CNN backbones. The authors position PoLoRA as the first low-rank adaptation designed specifically for MONet's Mu-Layer and channel-projection structure, and IPoLoRA as the first framework combining PEFT with GPM under LFHE constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstrated pipeline for class-incremental learning that operates directly on LFHE-encrypted data, which is practically important for security-sensitive domains (healthcare, finance) where decryption is unacceptable. However, the evaluation is limited to three relatively small datasets and a single backbone size (MONet-S, 32.9M params), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger networks, deeper FHE circuits, or longer task sequences. The high sensitivity of the GPM scaling factor (especially for IPoLoRA-M, which requires α_GPM ≈ 200–300) suggests the method may be fragile in practice and warrants further study on adaptive or learned scaling. A key open question is how the method behaves when the number of tasks grows substantially, since the concatenated PoLoRA branches accumulate parameters linearly with task count.
