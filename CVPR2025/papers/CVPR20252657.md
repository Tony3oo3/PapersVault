## Problem

Existing MLLM-based approaches to Referring Video Object Segmentation suffer from three interrelated failures: they cannot accurately regress numerical spatiotemporal values through standard text tokenization, fixed sentence templates cause repetitive degradation when a single query references multiple targets, and unguided video sampling omits critical visual cues. No prior MLLM method methodologically addresses the one-to-many multi-reference problem in RVOS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video and a free-form natural language query as input. The video is encoded into a multimodal sequence that interleaves original-resolution frames (for spatial detail) with image-grid-compressed groups of adjacent frames (for temporal coverage), each unit prefixed with a numerical frame index. A single query can reference multiple spatiotemporal targets; the output is a set of video-level segmentation masks (one per target) generated via SAM2 propagation from detected bounding boxes, along with the temporal clip boundaries identified by the sampler.

## Method

DTOS is a two-stage MLLM framework built on VILA1.5-LLaMA3-8B with LoRA adapters. Stage 1 (TCS) regresses temporal clip boundaries [t_start, t_end] by decoding dedicated <reg> special tokens through an MLP head, using bipartite matching to assign unique labels to multiple candidate segments and a hybrid multimodal input (original frames + image-grid compressed frames) to balance spatial fidelity with temporal resolution. Stage 2 (TCD) regresses bounding box coordinates [x1,y1,x2,y2] via <seg> special tokens within a multi-turn dialogue that queries each frame individually, then feeds the optimal box to SAM2 for mask generation and cross-frame propagation. The key design choice is replacing text-based numerical output with regression-based decoding of special tokens, which avoids LLM tokenization bias for numbers and converts sentence-level repetition into manageable word-level redundancy. Training uses separate loss functions per stage (cross-entropy + gIoU/L1/label for TCS; cross-entropy + bbox regression + SAM segmentation losses for TCD), with TCS additionally trained on augmented MR data (90K expressions from 33K videos) to strengthen temporal boundary regression.

## Results

On the primary RVOS task, DTOS-9B achieves J&F scores of 48.86 on MeViS, 74.88 on Ref-DAVIS17, and 68.52 on Ref-YT-VOS, improving over the previous SOTA (VISA-13B) by +4.36, +4.48, and +3.02 respectively. A zero-shot evaluation (trained only on MeViS and Ref-DAVIS17) still yields 60.89 J&F on Ref-YT-VOS, demonstrating strong generalization. On Moment Retrieval, the TCS module achieves 81.29 R1@0.3 and 43.2 R1@0.7 on Charades-STA, and 60.96 R1@0.3 on ActivityNet Captions, substantially outperforming prior LLM-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTOS-9B outperforms the strongest prior LLM-based method, VISA-13B, by +4.36 J&F on MeViS (48.86 vs. 44.5), +4.48 on Ref-DAVIS17 (74.88 vs. 70.4), and +3.02 on Ref-YT-VOS (68.52 vs. 63.0). Among non-LLM baselines, the best performer is DsHmp (46.4 J&F on MeViS, 67.1 on Ref-YT-VOS), which DTOS surpasses by 2.46 and 1.42 J&F respectively. On Moment Retrieval, DTOS-TCS achieves 43.2 R1@0.7 on Charades-STA, exceeding the previous best LLM-based method HawkEye (28.8) by +14.4, and 25.16 R1@0.7 on ActivityNet Captions versus HawkEye's 17.9 (+7.26). On QVHighlights, DTOS-TCS attains the highest R1@0.5 (72.24) among all compared methods, surpassing LLMEPET (66.73) and LMR (64.4), though its mAP.75 (41.78) is slightly below LLMEPET (43.91). The zero-shot variant DTOS-9B† (trained without Ref-YT-VOS data) still reaches 60.89 J&F, exceeding all non-LLM baselines on that benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTOS extends the VISA [60] line of work on LLM-based video object segmentation by replacing its Text-guided Frame Sampler (TFS) with a clip-level Text-guided Clip Sampler (TCS) that maintains consistent temporal resolution. The special-token regression design builds directly on LISA [23] and Next-Chat [63], which validated <seg> tokens for bounding-box regression, but generalizes from single-reference to multi-reference outputs using stacked tokens and bipartite matching (borrowed from QD-DETR [25]). The image-grid compression for video frames is adopted from Kim et al. [20], and the base architecture is VILA-1.5-LLaMA3-8B [29] with SAM2 [44] for mask propagation. The key conceptual departure is treating multi-reference RVOS as a first-class problem rather than an afterthought, which prior MLLM-based methods largely ignored due to data scarcity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DTOS fills a genuine gap: no prior MLLM framework methodologically handled multiple spatiotemporal references in a single query, and the special-token regression approach offers a practical workaround for LLMs' well-known weakness in numerical tokenization. The TCS module's dual applicability to both RVOS and Moment Retrieval suggests a reusable component for temporal video understanding more broadly. However, the method remains heavily dependent on SAM2 for mask quality, and the temporal propagation step (selecting one optimal frame and tracking forward) is acknowledged as a simplification that discards inter-frame context. Open questions include whether the multi-turn training format generalizes to longer videos, whether the image-grid compression strategy scales to higher resolutions, and whether the special-token approach can be extended to more fine-grained spatial outputs beyond bounding boxes.
