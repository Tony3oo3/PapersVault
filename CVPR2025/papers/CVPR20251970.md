## Problem

Existing object detection methods operate on 8-bit sRGB images that lose high-bit-depth information from RAW sensor data, particularly under adverse light and weather conditions. The field lacks a large-scale, diverse RAW dataset for object detection—prior datasets cover only 3–8 categories, 1–2 conditions, or driving-only scenes—and sRGB pre-training creates a domain gap that degrades RAW fine-tuning performance. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a comprehensive RAW dataset and a pre-training strategy that eliminates the need for neural ISP adapters.

## Input / Output

The detector consumes 16-bit RAW images originally stored in bayer pattern (1×H×W), which are demosaiced to 3×H×W and gamma-corrected before feeding into standard CNN or transformer backbones. The original 6000×4000 resolution is either downsampled to 2000×1333 or sliced into 1280×1280 patches (300-pixel overlap, IoU>0.4 filter) to support tiny-object detection. Outputs are bounding boxes with 62 class labels, evaluated via AP decomposed by condition (normal, low-light, rain, fog) and object scale (small/medium/large). The AODRaw dataset also ships paired sRGB images, enabling direct sRGB-vs-RAW comparison under identical annotations.

## Method

The core contribution is RAW-domain pre-training with cross-domain knowledge distillation. The authors synthesize a 16-bit ImageNet-RAW dataset from ImageNet-1K using the unprocessing pipeline (Brooks et al.), inserting random brightness shifts and shot-noise augmentation at each iteration to build condition invariance. An off-the-shelf sRGB-pretrained model of identical architecture serves as a teacher, providing both logit-level (KL divergence) and feature-level (L1) distillation targets to the RAW-pretrained student, compensating for the harder optimization landscape of noisy, high-dynamic-range inputs. Gamma correction is applied during pre-training to mitigate HDR training instability. This yields a backbone that, when fine-tuned on AODRaw with Cascade R-CNN + ConvNeXt-T, reaches 34.8% AP—outperforming both sRGB pre-training (34.0%) and neural-ISP-based adaptation (34.4%)—without any additional pre-processing module, with the largest gains in rain (+4.8% AP) and low-light (+0.8% AP) conditions.

## Results

On AODRaw (down-sampled 2000×1333), Cascade RCNN with ConvNeXt-T backbone achieves 34.8% AP with the proposed RAW pre-training + cross-domain distillation, compared to 34.0% AP with sRGB pre-training + sRGB fine-tuning and 33.7% AP with sRGB pre-training + RAW fine-tuning. The gains are most pronounced in adverse conditions: +4.8% AP_rain, +1.2% AP_fog, and +0.8% AP_low versus only +0.9% AP_normal over the sRGB-pretrained baseline. On the synthetic ImageNet-RAW validation set, the pre-trained ConvNeXt-T reaches 81.8% Top-1 accuracy (vs. 82.1% for sRGB pre-training), and distillation improves robustness to brightness and noise perturbations (e.g., 4.6% vs. 5.0% Top-1 drop when brightness is reduced from 791 to 80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed RAW pre-training with cross-domain distillation (Cascade RCNN + ConvNeXt-T, 34.8% AP) outperforms the sRGB-pretrained + sRGB-finetuned baseline (34.0% AP) by 0.8% AP and the sRGB-pretrained + RAW-finetuned setting (33.7% AP) by 1.1% AP, confirming that the sRGB-to-RAW domain gap degrades performance when no domain-specific pre-training is used. Against neural-ISP-based RAW detection methods, the proposed approach (no ISP module) beats RAOD [42] by 0.4% AP (34.8 vs. 34.4) and RAW-Adapter [8] by 2.4% AP with ResNet-18 (22.3 vs. 19.9), while eliminating the extra computational cost of a trainable ISP. The domain-gap analysis (Table 4) shows a model trained on sRGB drops from 34.0% to 28.0% AP when evaluated on RAW, and a RAW-trained model drops to 21.2% AP on sRGB, underscoring why direct RAW pre-training is necessary. Among real-time detectors, YOLO-MS-XS [6] achieves 24.7% AP at 113 FPS, while YOLOv8-n [24] reaches 18.9% AP at 188 FPS; adding the trainable ISP from [42] to YOLOv8-n improves AP_rain from 16.8% to 18.9% but reduces FPS from 188 to 58, sacrificing real-time capability. Across all detector families (multi-stage, one-stage, transformer-based), RAW pre-training consistently improves over sRGB pre-training, with the largest gains in rain (+4.8% AP_rain for Cascade RCNN) and fog (+1.2% AP_fog) conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in RAW object detection, which adapts sRGB-pretrained detectors to RAW inputs via trainable neural ISP modules (RAOD, RAW-Adapter). Instead, it directly pre-trains backbones on synthetic RAW images (ImageNet-RAW) with cross-domain knowledge distillation from an sRGB-pretrained teacher, eliminating the need for ISP adapters. The AODRaw dataset extends prior RAW detection benchmarks (LOD, RAOD, PASCALRAW), which were limited to 2–3 light conditions and 3–8 categories, to 9 combined light/weather conditions and 62 categories, filling a gap in dataset diversity for adverse-condition detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AODRaw provides the first comprehensive benchmark for RAW object detection across diverse adverse conditions, enabling fair comparison of methods that were previously evaluated on narrow, condition-specific datasets. The finding that sRGB pre-training actively degrades RAW detection (33.7% vs. 34.0% sRGB baseline) challenges the assumption that large-scale sRGB pre-training is universally beneficial and motivates domain-specific pre-training. Open questions include whether RAW pre-training scales to larger datasets and architectures, whether the approach transfers to other perception tasks (segmentation, tracking), and how to close the remaining gap between RAW and sRGB pre-training Top-1 accuracy (81.8% vs. 82.1%) without further distillation overhead.
