## Problem

Existing layer-based video decomposition models rely on implicit neural representations trained independently for each video, requiring tens of minutes of optimization even for a single 480p clip. This per-video training paradigm prevents generalization across videos and makes the workflow impractical for real-time or batch video editing applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251165/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short video clip (16 frames at 768×432) and object masks (ground-truth or MaskRCNN-predicted) as input. The video is first compressed by a frozen VideoMAE encoder into a single dense embedding of shape (768,1), which conditions the hypernet. The NVD model outputs per-frame foreground and background texture layers (rendered on a 1000×1000 grid), a soft opacity map for alpha blending, and per-frame residual coefficient maps that capture transient effects like reflections and shadows.

## Method

HyperNVD introduces a hypernetwork that, given a VideoMAE-derived video embedding, generates the full set of parameters for a compact INR-based decomposition model, including the multi-resolution hash encoding tables and all MLP weights. The target NVD model decomposes each frame into foreground and background layers via mapping, texture, and residual sub-modules (with MRHE in the latter two), combined by an alpha-blending module. Training is end-to-end and self-supervised using reconstruction, optical-flow consistency, sparsity, residual consistency, rigidity, and alpha-bootstrapping losses, with only the hypernet weights being optimized while the NVD model acts as a differentiable parameterized layer. A pre-training step aligns the mapping network to produce rectangular textures before joint optimization. For unseen videos, the hypernet-generated weights serve as initialization for fine-tuning the NVD model, reducing convergence time by over 30 minutes and improving PSNR by ~0.8 dB compared to training from scratch.

## Results

On the DAVIS dataset (768×432, 16 frames), HyperNVD achieves PSNR of 30.06, 29.53, 34.94, 31.58, and 30.30 on the five test videos (hike, blackswan, car-turn, bear, lucia), matching or exceeding the Hashing-nvd baseline (its single-video architecture) on 3 of 5 videos while remaining within ~0.5 dB on the others. The key meta-learning benefit is a consistent +0.8 dB PSNR improvement when fine-tuning on unseen videos from the 15-video metamodel versus training from scratch (Table 3), and reaching the same PSNR more than 30 minutes faster (Figure 1). When trained on multiple videos (up to 30), reconstruction quality drops by only ~3 dB from single-video training, confirming stable generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Deformable Sprites [38], HyperNVD wins decisively on all five videos, gaining 3.6–4.8 dB in PSNR (e.g., 30.06 vs 25.30 on hike, 31.58 vs 26.34 on bear), confirming that INR-based decomposition with hash encoding substantially outperforms the earlier sprite-based approach. Against Layered Neural Atlases [17], HyperNVD wins on PSNR for hike (+0.04), bear (+1.96), and lucia (+0.38), but trails on blackswan (−0.43) and car-turn SSIM (0.909 vs 0.929), indicating LNA still captures some high-frequency texture better in certain scenes. Against its own architectural baseline Hashing-nvd [4], HyperNVD is essentially matched: it gains +0.94 dB on hike and +1.01 dB on lucia, but loses −0.77 dB on blackswan and −0.49 dB on car-turn, which the authors attribute to the hypernet's slight capacity trade-off. The paper's central claim is that this near-parity in reconstruction quality comes with the added benefit of meta-learning: fine-tuning from the 15-video metamodel consistently improves PSNR by 0.25–1.65 dB over training from scratch on ten unseen videos (Table 3), and reduces convergence time by over 30 minutes to reach the same PSNR (Figure 1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Hashing-NVD architecture (Chan et al., ICCV 2023) for its NVD model—mapping module, texture module, residual module, and multi-resolution hash encoding—and on Layered Neural Atlases (Kasten et al., ACM TOG 2021) for the layered decomposition framework and loss design. The key departure is wrapping this single-video INR pipeline in a hypernetwork meta-learning strategy: rather than training a fresh INR per video (40+ minutes), a hypernet conditioned on a frozen VideoMAE embedding generates initial NVD parameters, enabling faster fine-tuning on unseen videos. The closest prior work, HyP-NeRF (Sen et al., NeurIPS 2024), applies hypernets to NeRF for simple 3D objects, whereas here the target is a more complex, multi-object, motion-rich video decomposition task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a ~30-minute reduction in per-video training time and a consistent ~0.8 dB PSNR gain when fine-tuning from the metamodel versus from scratch, which is meaningful for iterative video editing workflows. However, reconstruction quality drops roughly 3 dB as training scales from 1 to 30 videos, and the evaluation is limited to 16-frame clips at 768×432 on DAVIS, leaving open whether the approach scales to longer, higher-resolution, or more diverse video content. An open question is whether the quality plateau observed with more training videos reflects a fundamental limitation of the hypernet's parameter-generation capacity or simply insufficient training budget per video.
