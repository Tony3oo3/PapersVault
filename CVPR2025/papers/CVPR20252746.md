## Problem

Prior pose-free reconstruction methods like DUSt3R and MASt3R process only two views at a time, requiring a combinatorial number of pairwise inferences followed by expensive global optimization to align local reconstructions. This two-stage pipeline is slow (tens of seconds for large scenes) and often fails to rectify erroneous pairwise matches, producing misaligned pointmaps especially on large multi-room scenes with sparse input views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252746/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse RGB images (4–24 views) at 224×224 resolution with completely unknown camera intrinsics and poses, and produces per-view pixel-aligned 3D pointmaps all expressed in a single shared reference camera coordinate system, eliminating the need for any post-hoc global alignment. Optionally, per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters (scale, rotation quaternion, opacity) are predicted for differentiable splatting-based novel view rendering. The entire pipeline runs in a single GPU forward pass, completing 24-view scene reconstruction in under 2 seconds.

## Method

MV-DUSt3R replaces DUSt3R's pairwise decoder with multi-view decoder blocks: a shared ViT encoder produces tokens for all N views, then D stacked decoder blocks apply self-attention within each view's tokens and cross-attention against tokens from all other views simultaneously, so every view's representation is updated using the full multi-view context in one pass. MV-DUSt3R+ extends this to a multi-path architecture where M reference views are selected and each path independently runs the decoder; Cross-Reference-View blocks inserted after every decoder layer fuse the per-view tokens computed under different reference choices, enabling long-range information propagation that a single reference view cannot provide. For novel view synthesis, lightweight Gaussian heads predict per-pixel scale, rotation, and opacity, jointly trained with a confidence-aware pointmap regression loss and a differentiable splatting rendering loss (L2 + LPIPS). The network is initialized from pre-trained DUSt3R weights and trained for 100 epochs on 150K trajectories per epoch across ScanNet, ScanNet++, HM3D, and Gibson, with a two-stage curriculum that first trains on 8 views then fine-tunes on mixed 4–12 view inputs.

## Results

On HM3D (multi-room scenes) with 24 input views, MV-DUSt3R+ achieves ND=2.1, DAc=64.5%, and CD=3.9, compared to DUSt3R's ND=6.8, DAc=7.3%, and CD=32.4 — a 3.2× reduction in ND and 8.8× improvement in DAc. MV-DUSt3R runs 48–78× faster than DUSt3R (0.05s vs. 2.42s for 4 views; 0.35s vs. 27.21s for 24 views), while MV-DUSt3R+ runs 8–14× faster (0.29s vs. 2.42s; 1.97s vs. 27.21s). On the zero-shot MP3D benchmark with 24 views, MV-DUSt3R+ achieves ND=4.3 and DAc=26.7%, substantially outperforming DUSt3R (ND=11.4, DAc=2.5%). The method reconstructs 24-view scenes of average size 17.9 m² (HM3D) and 37.3 m² (MP3D) in under 2 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DUSt3R (the primary baseline), MV-DUSt3R consistently wins on all three metrics (ND, DAc, CD) across all three datasets and all view counts (4, 12, 24). The advantage grows with scene size and view count: on HM3D with 24 views, MV-DUSt3R reduces ND from 6.8 to 3.4 (2×) and increases DAc from 7.3% to 36.7% (5×), while MV-DUSt3R+ further narrows ND to 2.1 and lifts DAc to 64.5%. On the zero-shot MP3D benchmark, DUSt3R degrades severely (DAc drops to 2.5% at 24 views), whereas MV-DUSt3R+ maintains DAc of 26.7%. In terms of speed, MV-DUSt3R is 48–78× faster than DUSt3R (eliminating the expensive global optimization stage), and MV-DUSt3R+ is still 8–14× faster. Against Spann3R, both proposed methods dominate across all settings; Spann3R frequently fails entirely on sparse multi-view input (DAc ≈ 0% on HM3D and MP3D), confirming that its spatial-memory approach does not generalize beyond sequential video frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DUSt3R's pose-free pairwise pointmap prediction to a single-stage multi-view setting, replacing DUSt3R's combinatorial pairwise inference plus global optimization with a joint feed-forward pass over all input views. The multi-view decoder blocks (self-attention within a view, cross-attention across views) are a natural architectural generalization of DUSt3R's two-view decoder, and the model is initialized from pre-trained DUSt3R weights. MV-DUSt3R+ further departs from the single-reference-view paradigm by introducing cross-reference-view attention blocks that fuse information across multiple reference-view paths, addressing the spatial quality degradation observed when one reference view has large viewpoint changes to certain source views. The Gaussian splatting heads for novel view synthesis follow the trend of attaching lightweight prediction heads to reconstruction backbones (as in PixelSplat, MVSplat), but are jointly trained with the pointmap regression loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 48–78× speedup over DUSt3R while simultaneously improving reconstruction quality (2.8× lower Chamfer distance on HM3D with 4 views) is a substantial practical win that removes an entire pipeline stage (global optimization) and makes large-scene reconstruction from sparse views feasible in under 2 seconds. The finding that a single-stage multi-view approach outperforms the two-stage pairwise-plus-optimization paradigm on large multi-room scenes challenges the assumption that global alignment is necessary to resolve pairwise conflicts. Open questions include how the method scales beyond 24 views, whether the oracle-to-non-oracle gap (still visible in Tables 2–4) can be closed with better reference selection strategies, and whether incorporating diffusion-based image priors (explicitly deferred to future work) could help with unseen regions. Zero-shot performance on MP3D remains notably weaker than supervised results, suggesting domain gap issues that are not fully resolved.
