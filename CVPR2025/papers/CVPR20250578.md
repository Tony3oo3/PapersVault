## Problem

Existing stateful defenses for detecting black-box adversarial attacks in the making (e.g., Blacklight, PIHA) monitor input-space patterns and are vulnerable to adaptive attacks like OARS that craft queries to evade detection while still progressing toward adversarial examples. These defenses also exhibit high false positive rates on high-similarity image sequences. The paper addresses the gap of finding a detection signal that is robust to adaptive evasion and less sensitive to input-space similarity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming sequence of image queries (the actual inputs sent to a victim model) and, for each new query, computes the Delta Similarity (DS) between the current and previous query-update vectors. It maintains a sliding window of the last 256 DS values, binning them into a 200-bin histogram over [-1, 1] plus one dedicated bin at DS = 1.0, then applies min-max normalization to produce a 1×201 feature vector. The output is a multi-class prediction (benign or one of six attack types) from a Log-Softmax layer, from which a binary attack-detection flag is derived.

## Method

The core insight is that query-based black-box attacks rely on zeroth-order gradient estimation, where consecutive input updates are differences of i.i.d. random vectors that are approximately orthogonal in high-dimensional space (concentration of measure), producing distinctive DS patterns (e.g., clusters at -0.7071 for NES, -0.5 and ±1.0 for HSJA) that are absent in benign query sequences. GWAD extracts these patterns by computing DS for each triplet of consecutive queries, building a Histogram of DS (HoDS) over a 256-query window, and feeding the normalized 1×201 vector into a lightweight 6-layer fully-connected MLP (ReLU activations, Log-Softmax output) trained with SGD for 100 epochs at batch size 128. Because the detection operates in the update-similarity space rather than the input space, it is agnostic to the specific input distribution and model architecture, enabling cross-dataset and cross-model generalization. An optional Screener pre-processing step (Canny edge detection + bitwise XOR similarity over a FIFO of 100 prior queries) can be prepended to filter benign injections, yielding the enhanced GWAD+ variant.

## Results

GWAD achieves near-perfect attack detection (>99.42%) across six SOTA query-based black-box attacks (HSJA, NES, SimBA, Sign-Opt, Sign-Flip, BA) on both CIFAR-10/MobileNet-V2 and ImageNet/EfficientNet. Under the OARS adaptive attack, GWAD maintains 100% detection rate at all tested σ² values, while Blacklight drops to 0% and PIHA to 31.7% at σ²=0.05. False positive rates are negligible: 0% on CIFAR-10, Tiny-ImageNet, and FLIR ADAS; 0.03% on ImageNet; and 1.29% on BIRDSAI. GWAD generalizes across datasets, with GWAD-CIFAR10 achieving 97.32% multi-class accuracy on ImageNet and GWAD-ImageNet achieving 86.36% on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GWAD decisively outperforms both stateful baselines on the critical adaptive-attack benchmark: under OARS-NES at σ²=0.05, Blacklight is fully evaded (97.5% ASR, 0% detection) and PIHA is largely evaded (84.5% ASR, 31.7% detection), while GWAD maintains 0% ASR and 100% detection. On standard (non-adaptive) detection, GWAD achieves 100% detection for all six attacks, compared to Blacklight's 23.96% for BA and PIHA's 38.08% for BA (Table 4). On false positive rate, GWAD is superior in high-similarity settings: on Hollywood Heads (high similarity), GWAD achieves 17.99% FPR versus 25.47% (Blacklight) and 26.19% (PIHA); on FLIR ADAS, GWAD achieves 0% FPR versus 2.17% (Blacklight) and 1.43% (PIHA). The key structural advantage is that GWAD operates in the update-similarity space rather than the input space, making it inherently resistant to OARS-style evasion that manipulates input-space patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of stateful defenses (Stateful Detection, Blacklight, PIHA) that detect adversarial attacks by monitoring similarity patterns in the input space. Rather than building on any single prior method, it repurposes the general idea of sequence monitoring by shifting the analysis to the input update space, introducing Delta Similarity (DS) as a new feature derived from consecutive query differences. The theoretical grounding draws on the concentration of measure phenomenon in high-dimensional spaces, which predicts that random vectors used in zeroth-order gradient estimation will be near-orthogonal, producing characteristic DS signatures absent in benign queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the demonstrated robustness against OARS, an adaptive attack specifically engineered to bypass stateful input-space defenses, where GWAD maintains 100% detection while Blacklight and PIHA are largely evaded. The model- and dataset-agnostic generalization (training on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 to defend ImageNet/EfficientNet) reduces deployment cost for MLaaS providers. Key limitations include vulnerability to benign example injection (mitigated but not eliminated by the GWAD+ screener) and evaluation restricted to image classification with zeroth-order-based attacks; whether the approach extends to non-image modalities or gradient-free attacks that do not use structured random perturbations remains open.
