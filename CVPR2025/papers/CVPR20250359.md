## Problem

Existing approaches to generating synchronized video-audio pairs from a static image either produce the two modalities independently (risking semantic and temporal inconsistency), chain them sequentially (suffering error propagation), or rely on coarse-grained contrastive alignment (insufficient for fine-grained audiovisual synchronization). No unified model has demonstrated the ability to enable fine-grained bidirectional cross-modal interaction while preserving the generation quality of strong pretrained single-modality diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250359/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static RGB image as input, which serves simultaneously as the conditional signal and the first frame of the generated video. The image is consumed differently per modality: for video it is VAE-encoded, duplicated to match latent frame length, channel-concatenated with noisy video latents, and supplemented with CLIP embeddings for cross-attention; for audio it is CLIP-encoded and added to timestep embeddings. The output is a temporally and semantically synchronized video-audio pair (video latents from SVD's VAE, audio latents from AudioLDM2's VAE), with no text conditioning required.

## Method

JointDiT decomposes a pretrained video diffusion model (SVD) and a pretrained audio diffusion model (AudioLDM2) into Input, Expert, and Output submodules, then interleaves them into a unified DiT where each Joint Block contains modality-specific expert layers, a Perceiver Joint Attention layer, and modality-specific feedforward layers. The Perceiver Joint Attention uses modality-specific Q, K, and V projections to handle the large channel-dimension disparity between video and audio tokens, enabling effective cross-modal interaction without forcing both modalities into a shared representation space. Training unifies the DDPM (audio) and EDM (video) timestep schedules through a shared t_uni ∈ (0,1) coordinate, preserving both pretrained objectives under a single L2 loss. At inference, JointCFG extends classifier-free guidance by subtracting modality-isolated (block-diagonal attention-masked) predictions from the joint prediction, and JointCFG* further replaces the negative term with a degraded model (full-attention layers removed) inspired by autoguidance, simultaneously enforcing condition adherence and cross-modal consistency.

## Results

On AVSync15, JointDiT achieves FVD 326, KVD 15.2, FAD 23.9, KL 1.36, IB-VA 37.5, IB-IV 87.7, and IB-IA 36.3, outperforming all baselines on every metric except AV-Align (1.296 vs. 1.285 for AudioLDM-v+AVSyncD). On GreatestHits, JointDiT reduces FVD from 287 (best baseline) to 173 and FAD from 24.86 to 1.08. On Landscape, it achieves FVD 262 (vs. 402 for all I→V-based baselines) and AV-Align 1.178 (best). In user studies, JointDiT scores 1.52/5 overall versus 1.28 for the next-best method, leading on all five subjective dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JointDiT outperforms all independent-parallel baselines (SVD+IM2Wav, SVD+AudioLDM-v) by large margins: FVD drops from 444 to 326, FAD from 30.3–34.9 to 23.9, and IB-VA jumps from 12.8–24.9 to 37.5, confirming that joint modeling captures cross-modal interactions that sequential or parallel pipelines miss. Against sequential V→A baselines (Diff-Foley, FoleyCrafter, TiVA, SeeingHearing), JointDiT improves FAD by 7–12 points and IB-VA by 8–20 points, while matching their FVD (all inherit SVD's 444). The I→A+A†→V pipeline (AudioLDM-v+AVSyncD) is the only method that beats JointDiT on AV-Align (1.285 vs. 1.296), but JointDiT dominates it on FVD (326 vs. 610), FAD (23.9 vs. 30.3), and IB-VA (37.5 vs. 25.5). The text-mediated pipeline (Qwen+CogVideoX/Hunyuan+SDA) suffers severe information loss from image-to-caption conversion, yielding FVD >1094 and IB-IV <71, far below JointDiT's 326 and 87.7. The direct I2SV baseline CoDi is the weakest overall (FVD 1873, IB-VA 5.3), highlighting the inadequacy of coarse-grained contrastive alignment for fine-grained audiovisual synchronization. On GreatestHits, JointDiT's FAD of 1.08 is an order of magnitude better than the next-best (24.86 for SeeingHearing), though CoDi achieves a lower AV-Align (1.143 vs. 1.462), suggesting JointDiT's temporal sync metric may be less sensitive in this narrow domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT architecture and the observation that pretrained unimodal diffusion models (SVD for video, AudioLDM2 for audio) contain reusable submodules, but departs from prior joint-generation work (AV-DiT, MM-Diffusion) that trains a joint model from scratch, and from sequential pipelines (I→V→A, I→A→V) that suffer error propagation. The key conceptual move is decomposing pretrained denoisers into Input/Expert/Output submodules and reassembling them around a shared Joint Block with Perceiver-style modality-specific attention, inspired by Perceiver IO's heterogeneous QKV design. The inference-time contribution extends classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans) and autoguidance (Karras et al.) into a JointCFG/JointCFG* scheme that explicitly penalizes the absence of cross-modal interaction, not just the absence of conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular decomposition strategy offers a practical recipe for combining any two pretrained diffusion models into a joint generator without retraining from scratch, a pattern potentially transferable to other modality pairs (e.g., text-video-audio). However, the evaluation is limited to three small datasets (1,350–977 videos each) and short clips (2–10 s), leaving open whether the approach scales to longer, higher-resolution, or more diverse content. The authors themselves note that objective metrics (AV-Align) do not always agree with human judgments, and the task remains restricted to image conditioning without text, limiting its applicability to open-domain prompting. Future work on extending JointDiT to text modality and larger-scale training is needed to assess whether the fine-grained cross-modal interaction advantage persists at scale.
