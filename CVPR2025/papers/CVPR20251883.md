## Problem

Existing visual in-context learning (VICL) models like Painter exhibit poor generalizability under distribution shifts, with performance degrading significantly on corrupted test inputs. Surprisingly, even providing task prompts from the same corrupted distribution (one-shot) fails to recover performance and often makes it worse. This paper presents the first systematic study of VICL robustness under distribution shifts and proposes a test-time tuning method to address this gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a task prompt (an input-output image pair, e.g., a photo and its segmentation mask) and a single test input image, arranging them in a 4-cell grid canvas where each subimage is resized to 448×448. The model predicts the missing cell (the test output) via masked image inpainting. During test-time tuning, only the encoder weights of the ViT-Large-based Painter model are optimized, while the decoder remains frozen, and weights are reset to the pre-trained θ₀ for each new test sample to treat inputs independently.

## Method

VICT introduces a cycle consistency loss for self-supervised test-time tuning of VICL models. Given task prompts (x, y) and test input x_t, the model first predicts the test output ŷ_t from the grid (x, y, x_t, ∅), then flips the roles by constructing a new grid (x, ∅, x_t, ŷ_t) and predicting the task prompt output ŷ. The model is optimized by minimizing a smooth-L1 regression loss between ŷ and the ground-truth task prompt output y, requiring no additional labels. The key insight is that successful recovery of the original task prompts conditioned on the model's own in-context prediction indicates the model has adapted to the new test distribution. Optimization runs for 60 steps with AdamW (lr=1e-6, batch size 1) per test sample, and the tuned model then produces the final prediction on the original grid.

## Results

On the averaged results across 15 corruptions (level 5), VICT consistently outperforms frozen Painter in both zero-shot and one-shot settings across all six tasks. In the one-shot setting, the gains are most dramatic: VICT reduces depth estimation error on NYUv2-C from 0.537 to 0.205 A.Rel (−62%), improves semantic segmentation on ADE20K-C from 25.8 to 29.3 mIoU (+3.5), and boosts deraining PSNR on Rain-C from 17.77 to 19.92 (+2.15). In the zero-shot setting, improvements are more modest but still consistent, e.g., +1.2 mIoU on ADE20K-C and +0.5 PQ on COCO-C. Notably, zero-shot VICT can even outperform one-shot Painter on several tasks (e.g., NYUv2-C: 0.365 vs. 0.537 A.Rel), and VICT with a single test sample matches or exceeds Painter fine-tuned with up to 64 corrupted training examples on high-level tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is frozen Painter [Wang et al., CVPR 2023], compared in both zero-shot (clean prompts) and one-shot (corrupted prompts) settings. VICT wins on every task in both settings, with the largest margins in the one-shot configuration: depth estimation improves by 0.332 A.Rel (0.537→0.205), deraining by 2.15 PSNR, and semantic segmentation by 3.5 mIoU. A striking finding is that one-shot Painter actually performs *worse* than zero-shot Painter on most tasks (e.g., ADE20K-C: 25.8 vs. 27.0 mIoU; NYUv2-C: 0.537 vs. 0.392 A.Rel), indicating that providing corrupted prompts without adaptation is counterproductive. In contrast, VICT consistently benefits from the one-shot setting over zero-shot, and zero-shot VICT already surpasses one-shot Painter on depth estimation (0.365 vs. 0.537 A.Rel) and semantic segmentation (28.2 vs. 25.8 mIoU). Additionally, VICT with a single test sample outperforms Painter fine-tuned with up to 64 corrupted training examples on high-level tasks like ADE20K-C and COCO-C, demonstrating that the cycle-consistency test-time signal is more effective than simply adding more in-context examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of visual in-context learning (VICL) and test-time training (TTT). It builds directly on Painter [52], which formulates VICL as grid-based image inpainting, and on the TTT paradigm of Sun et al. [41], which unfreezes model parameters at test time via self-supervised pretext tasks. The key departure is that instead of relying on external pretext tasks (rotation prediction, MAE, etc.), VICT exploits the inherent structure of VICL itself—flipping the roles between task prompts and test samples—to construct a cycle consistency loss that is task-agnostic and requires no additional data. This makes it the first work to apply test-time training specifically to VICL models, where prior self-supervised pretext tasks are inapplicable due to their single-image context constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that VICL models, despite their promise as generalist vision systems, are fragile under even common corruptions, and that the intuitive fix of providing corrupted in-context examples actually makes things worse. The cycle consistency approach is appealing because it is fully self-supervised, task-agnostic, and requires only a single test sample, making it practical for real-world deployment where the test distribution is unknown. Limitations include the per-sample optimization cost (~0.4s/step on A100), evaluation restricted to a single VICL model (Painter) and corruption-based shifts, and the acknowledgment that cycle consistency may not produce useful gradients for every distribution. Open questions include extending VICT to multi-modal in-context learning and to more diverse shift types beyond corruptions.
