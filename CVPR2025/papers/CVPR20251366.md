## Problem

Existing individual adversarial attacks (e.g., PGD) are significantly weaker than ensemble methods like AutoAttack, yet they are essential for large-scale evaluation due to their efficiency. Additionally, prior robustness evaluations have been confined to small-scale test sets (e.g., ImageNet-1k's 50K images), leaving it unclear whether adversarially trained models retain their robustness on diverse, million-scale data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251366/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes an RGB image, its class label (or the model's own predicted label for unlabeled data), and a target model, and produces an adversarial example constrained within an L∞ ball (ε=8/255 for CIFAR-10/100, ε=4/255 for ImageNet-1k). For the million-scale CC1M evaluation, the paper introduces a 'relative robustness' metric where attack success is defined as the model's prediction changing from its clean-image prediction, since no ground-truth labels exist. The final output is a robust accuracy score per model, enabling comparison across 29 adversarially trained models on three datasets.

## Method

The core contribution is the Probability Margin Loss (PM loss), which defines the adversarial margin as p_max − p_y in probability space rather than the logit-space margin z_max − z_y. The key theoretical insight is that the PM loss gradient decomposes into a weighted combination of the untargeted and targeted cross-entropy gradients (weighted by p_y and p_max respectively), inheriting the multi-directional exploration of untargeted CE and the focused optimization of targeted CE in a single formula. The PMA attack follows the two-stage Margin Decomposition pipeline: Stage 1 alternates between maximizing p_max and minimizing p_y across multiple restarts to explore diverse adversarial directions, while Stage 2 attacks the full PM loss to refine the perturbation. Building on PMA, the paper proposes a PMA+ ensemble (PMA + multi-targeted APGD) that outperforms AutoAttack in robustness reduction while running at only 25% of AA's time. Finally, the authors construct CC1M, a 1-million-image dataset derived from CC3M by filtering unavailable, noisy, and LID-outlier images, enabling the first million-scale white-box robustness evaluation that reveals a dramatic drop in relative robustness (from >59% to <19%) compared to ImageNet-1k test-set results.

## Results

PMA achieves the lowest robust accuracy (strongest attack) across all 29 adversarially trained models on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1k, reducing robustness by up to 0.22% compared to the best individual baseline (MD attack). On ImageNet-1k, the PM loss alone reduces robustness by an average of 1.22% over the best existing loss function. The PMA+ ensemble (PMA + APGDT) outperforms AutoAttack (AA) on every model while using only 25% of AA's running time. On the million-scale CC1M dataset, relative robustness of top-5 ImageNet models drops from above 59% to below 19%, revealing a large gap versus ImageNet-1k test-set evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMA outperforms all individual baselines across every model and dataset. Compared to the MD attack (Ma et al., 2023), which shares the same two-stage pipeline, PMA reduces robustness by up to 0.55% (e.g., 66.24% vs. 66.68% on WRN-70-16 [32] CIFAR-10). Against PGD with CE loss (Madry et al., 2017), the PM loss alone lowers robustness by 0.03–1.56%, with the largest gaps on ImageNet-1k (avg. 1.22%). PMA also surpasses PGD with alternating objectives (Antoniou et al., 2022) and MIFPE loss (Yu et al., 2023) by 0.03–0.22% on CIFAR-10. The PMA+ ensemble (PMA + APGDT) beats AutoAttack (Croce & Hein, 2020) on all 29 models—e.g., 67.91% vs. 68.06% on Mixing CIFAR-10—while running in only 25% of AA's time. FAB (Croce & Hein, 2020) is consistently weaker than PMA on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 72.31% vs. 71.10% on RWRN-70-16), and margin loss (Carlini & Wagner, 2017) is outperformed by PM loss on every model tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Margin Decomposition (MD) attack pipeline of Ma et al. (2023), which already integrates multi-targeted optimization, objective alternation, and random restarts into a unified two-stage framework. The key conceptual departure is replacing the logit-space margin loss with a probability-space margin loss, whose gradient is shown to be a weighted combination of targeted and untargeted cross-entropy gradients — unifying two previously separate attack directions in a single formula. The paper also extends the evaluation scope beyond standard benchmarks by constructing CC1M, a million-scale unlabeled dataset filtered via Local Intrinsic Dimensionality, enabling the first million-scale white-box robustness evaluation of adversarially trained ImageNet models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in bridging the effectiveness-efficiency gap in robustness evaluation: PMA+ (PMA + APGDT) matches or exceeds AutoAttack's strength at roughly 25% of its runtime, making large-scale evaluation feasible. The million-scale evaluation on CC1M reveals a dramatic robustness gap — relative robustness drops from above 59% on ImageNet-1k to below 19% on CC1M — suggesting that standard benchmark evaluation substantially overestimates real-world robustness of adversarially trained models. Open questions include whether the PM loss advantage persists in black-box settings, whether the CC1M robustness gap generalizes to other out-of-distribution distributions, and whether the scale effect is driven by distribution shift or by the sheer diversity of natural images.
