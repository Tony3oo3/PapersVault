## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks either rely on metrics and embeddings (FID, CLIP scores) that significantly misalign with human preferences, or use LLMs that struggle with cross-modal video-text comparison and ambiguous quality scoring criteria. No prior benchmark systematically leverages multimodal LLMs across all evaluation dimensions relevant to video generation assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250554/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a generated video clip, its originating text prompt, and human-written evaluation guidelines for a specific dimension. For video-condition alignment, it outputs scores on a 3-point scale (object class, action, color, scene consistency) or 5-point scale (video-text consistency); for video quality, all four dimensions (imaging, aesthetic, temporal, motion) use a 5-point scale. In few-shot scoring, multiple videos generated from the same prompt are batched together so each video is scored relative to its peers rather than in isolation.

## Method

The core framework uses GPT-4o as the multimodal evaluator and GPT-4o-mini as a text-only query generator, with two key mechanisms. For video-condition alignment, a chain-of-query protocol avoids direct cross-modal comparison: the MLLM first produces a full video description, the LLM generates N dimension-specific probing questions from that description and the prompt, the MLLM then revisits the video to answer each query iteratively, and finally scores using the accumulated multi-turn conversation. For video quality, a few-shot scoring strategy batches multiple videos from the same prompt so that when scoring any one video, all other videos in the batch and their prior scores serve as implicit calibration references, resolving the ambiguity that causes MLLMs to default to average ratings when scoring in isolation.

## Results

Video-Bench achieves the highest Spearman rank correlation with human ratings across all 9 evaluation dimensions, with scores ranging from 0.402 (Temporal Consistency) to 0.750 (Color Consistency). The strongest LLM-based baseline, CompBench, reaches at most 0.696 (Color Consistency), while individual metrics (MUSIQ, CLIP, RAFT, etc.) peak at 0.545 (GRiT on Color Consistency). In terms of inter-rater agreement (Krippendorff's α), Video-Bench's MLLM evaluations achieve 0.50 average agreement with human raters, nearly matching the human-human agreement of 0.52, and the average absolute mean difference between MLLM and human scores is only 0.18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Video-Bench outperforms all 10 baselines across every single evaluation dimension. Against the strongest LLM-based competitor, CompBench, Video-Bench improves Spearman correlation by 0.099 on Video-text Consistency (0.732 vs. 0.633), 0.124 on Object-class Consistency (0.735 vs. 0.611), 0.054 on Color Consistency (0.750 vs. 0.696), 0.085 on Action Consistency (0.718 vs. 0.633), and 0.102 on Scene Consistency (0.733 vs. 0.631). Against individual metrics, the gains are even larger: e.g., 0.733 vs. 0.363 for MUSIQ on Imaging Quality, 0.702 vs. 0.446 for LAION on Aesthetic Quality, and 0.514 vs. 0.329 for RAFT/AMT on Motion Effects. The paper attributes the improvement over CompBench primarily to the chain-of-query mechanism (multi-round iterative questioning vs. single-round), which adds ~0.093 on alignment dimensions, and to few-shot scoring for quality dimensions, which adds ~10.33% average correlation. Notably, Video-Bench's Temporal Consistency score (0.402) remains the weakest dimension, though it still exceeds CLIP's 0.260 by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two existing lines of video generation evaluation: metric/embedding-based benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter) that compute FID, FVD, CLIP scores, and LLM-based benchmarks (CompBench, T2VScore) that use language models for instruction alignment but still rely on traditional metrics for perceptual quality. Video-Bench departs from both by being the first to systematically deploy MLLMs across all evaluation dimensions—both video-condition alignment and video quality—rather than a hybrid approach. Its two methodological contributions, chain-of-query (iterative multi-turn questioning to resolve cross-modal comparison) and few-shot scoring (batch-relative calibration to disambiguate quality levels), are novel procedural designs that address specific failure modes of prior LLM-based evaluators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that MLLM-based evaluation achieves inter-rater agreement (Krippendorff's α ≈ 0.50) on par with human-human agreement (0.52), the paper suggests automated evaluation could substantially reduce the cost and subjectivity of video generation benchmarking. However, temporal consistency remains the weakest dimension (Spearman 0.402), and the entire pipeline depends on proprietary GPT-4o, raising questions about reproducibility and whether open-source MLLMs (Gemini 1.5 Pro, Qwen2-VL) can close the gap. An open question is whether the chain-of-query and few-shot scoring techniques transfer to longer-form or more complex video generation tasks beyond the 70–90 prompts per dimension tested here.
