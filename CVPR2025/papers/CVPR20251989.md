## Problem

Existing 3D inpainting methods rely on Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) or Iterative Dataset Update optimization, which are extremely slow (hours per edit) and produce low-quality, fuzzy results due to the inherent mode-seeking behavior of SDS. This paper asks whether 3D editing can be reformulated as a multiview image inpainting problem solvable in a single diffusion forward pass, combined with fast feedforward 3D reconstruction, to achieve interactive-speed editing with higher fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object (mesh, NeRF, or Gaussian Splat), a user-drawn 3D mask (composed of coarse primitives like ellipsoids or cylinders), and a text prompt. The object and mask are rendered from four canonical viewpoints (azimuths 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° at 45° elevation) into a 2×2 grid at 512² per view (1024² total). The output is a multiview-consistent inpainted 2×2 image grid, which is then passed to an off-the-shelf Large Reconstruction Model (NeRF-LRM, GS-LRM, or MeshLRM) to produce the edited 3D asset in the same representation as the input. Total pipeline time is approximately 3 seconds for inpainting plus 0.7–3 seconds for reconstruction.

## Method

The core contribution is a fine-tuning strategy that converts a pretrained single-view inpainting diffusion model (SDXL-inpainting) into a multiview-consistent inpainter by training it on 2×2 grids of 3D renderings with 3D-aware binary masks. The inpainting model receives a 9-channel latent input (VQ-VAE-encoded base image, downsampled mask, and noisy latents) conditioned on text, and is trained with a standard denoising objective where the mask is randomly dropped 10% of the time to retain multiview generation capability. A key design choice is the construction of three types of 3D-consistent masks—coarse convex-hull edits, tight surface selections, and local surface patches—rendered with proper occlusion against the base shape, which the paper shows is critical for performance over random 2D masks. The training dataset comprises ~150K samples from 5K curated Objaverse objects, each with 30 masks across the three types. The paper empirically demonstrates that fine-tuning an inpainting model for multiview consistency outperforms the reverse strategy (fine-tuning a multiview generator for inpainting), and that the resulting model generalizes to unseen camera azimuths and fields of view.

## Results

On the multiview inpainting benchmark, Instant3dit (fine-tuned from SDXL-inpainting) achieves the best prompt adherence with ClipL 29.01 and ClipG 43.48, and the best visual quality with FID 118.4. For multiview consistency, it achieves SSIM 0.894, LPIPS 0.045, and DreamSim 0.100, closely matching the Instant3D-backbone variant (LPIPS 0.043, DreamSim 0.097). In a 15-user preference study against NeRFiller, users preferred Instant3dit in 180 out of 208 pairs (86%), and the method runs in ~3 seconds per edit versus hours for SDS-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Instant3dit outperforms all three Table 1 baselines on prompt adherence: it beats SDXL (ClipL 29.01 vs 28.63), SDXL-inpainting (29.01 vs 27.57), and Instant3D (29.01 vs 28.78). On visual quality (FID), Instant3dit achieves 118.4, improving over Instant3D (120.0), SDXL (127.0), and SDXL-inpainting (159.5). For multiview consistency, Instant3dit (SSIM 0.894, LPIPS 0.045, DreamSim 0.100) slightly edges out the Instant3D baseline (0.892, 0.044, 0.102) and substantially outperforms both SDXL variants (SSIM ≤ 0.874, DreamSim ≥ 0.134). Against NeRFiller in the NeRF editing application, Instant3dit wins the user preference study 86% to 14% and is orders of magnitude faster (~3 s vs ~30 min for IDU optimization). Vox-E and MVEdit do not accept user-provided masks, instead inferring edit regions from prompt attention weights, which can cause unwanted changes elsewhere on the object. Progressive3D, like NeRFiller, relies on 2D diffusion with SDS/IDU and struggles with multiview consistency, producing incorrectly positioned edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the fast 3D generation paradigm established by Instant3D (Li et al., 2024), which showed that generating a 2×2 multiview image grid with a fine-tuned diffusion model followed by a feedforward Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) can replace slow SDS/IDU optimization. The key departure is reframing 3D inpainting as multiview 2D inpainting: rather than optimizing a 3D representation against a 2D diffusion prior (as in NeRFiller, Vox-E, Progressive3D), the authors fine-tune a pretrained single-view inpainter (SDXL-inpainting) to produce multiview-consistent fills in a single inference pass. The concurrent MVInpainter also identifies SDS/IDU instability but uses video priors via LoRA, whereas this paper's empirical study shows that starting from an inpainting model and teaching it multiview consistency outperforms the reverse direction (teaching a multiview generator to inpaint).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orders-of-magnitude speedup (hours → ~3 seconds for inpainting, under 1 second for reconstruction) makes 3D generative editing viable in interactive production pipelines for the first time, and the representation-agnostic design (NeRF, Gaussian Splat, mesh) broadens applicability across the 3D content creation stack. Limitations include sensitivity to thin masks, a white-background bias when the unmasked region provides little inductive bias, and the 2×2 grid resolution cap imposed by SDXL. Open questions include whether scaling to 3×3 or more views with multiview-aware super-resolution can improve coverage, whether distillation (e.g., DMD) can push inference to sub-second, and whether video diffusion models can further improve 3D consistency at the cost of speed.
