## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for autonomous driving either operate at the task-output level without capturing epistemic uncertainty at the multimodal feature fusion stage, or rely on Bayesian approximations (deep ensembles, MC dropout) that impose prohibitive training and inference costs for real-time deployment. No prior method efficiently quantifies per-modality, feature-level epistemic uncertainty to enable uncertainty-aware weighting before cross-modal fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251876/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained modality-specific latent features (C×H×W tensors) extracted from camera, LiDAR, radar, event, and depth sensors, inserting the UQ module at the pre-fusion point of architectures like BEVFusion and CMNeXt. It produces per-channel and per-patch epistemic uncertainty values (cosine similarity distances to context-labeled prototypes) that drive a learnable reweighting of each modality's features before fusion. Downstream, the reweighted fused features yield 3D bounding boxes with class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks, with all layers prior to the UQ module kept frozen during fine-tuning.

## Method

HyperDUM projects latent features into a hyperdimensional space via a learned projection matrix, then forms context-labeled prototypes (e.g., by weather scenario) through element-wise bundling of hypervectors. Two key modifications distinguish it from standard VSA: Channel-wise Projection & Bundling (CPB) retains the channel dimension during projection (using Einstein summation rather than collapsing channels), yielding per-channel uncertainty, while Patch-wise Projection & Bundling (PPB) slices spatial dimensions into P patches and projects/bundles each independently to capture fine-grained spatial uncertainty without infeasible per-pixel computation. Uncertainty for a new sample is the set of cosine similarity distances between its hypervector and each prototype, producing C×L channel-wise and P×L patch-wise uncertainty values. A learnable uncertainty weighting module then scales each modality's features inversely to its uncertainty before the fusion blocks, and post-fusion layers are fine-tuned end-to-end. The entire pipeline is deterministic (single forward pass), requiring up to 2.36× fewer FLOPs and 38.30× fewer trainable parameters than the SOTA deterministic method (LDU).

## Results

On the aiMotive 3D object detection benchmark, HyperDUM achieves a mean all-point/11-point AP of 66.70/66.00 across diverse driving scenes (Highway, Urban, Night, Rain), outperforming the state-of-the-art deterministic method LDU (64.69/64.73) by 2.01%/1.27%. On the DeLiVER semantic segmentation benchmark, HyperDUM attains a mean mIoU of 67.59, improving over the CMNeXt baseline by 1.29% and over GeminiFusion by 0.69%. In terms of efficiency, HyperDUM requires 2.36× fewer FLOPs and up to 38.30× fewer trainable parameters than LDU, while also achieving the lowest Expected Calibration Error (1.20E-02 mean ECE vs. 1.53E-02 for the baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperDUM outperforms all five UQ baselines on the aiMotive 3D object detection task. Against the strongest deterministic baseline LDU, HyperDUM gains 2.01%/1.27% mean AP, with the largest margin in the Rain scenario (+4.29%/+4.24% all-point/11-point). Bayesian approximation methods (InfMCD, InfNoise) and PostNet all degrade significantly under the Rain and Urban scenes (e.g., InfMCD drops to 35.58 AP in Rain vs. 44.78 for HyperDUM), while HyperDUM maintains robustness. On DeLiVER semantic segmentation, HyperDUM (67.59 mIoU) surpasses GeminiFusion (66.90), InfMCD (66.90), and LDU (66.60), with the largest gains in the most challenging scenarios: Under-Exposure (+1.20), Night (+1.75), and Motion Blur (+1.37) over the CMNeXt baseline. In terms of calibration, HyperDUM achieves the lowest ECE (1.20E-02) across all scenarios, a 0.33 absolute reduction over the baseline, outperforming PostNet (1.34E-02) and InfMCD (1.35E-02). Computationally, HyperDUM uses 2.36× fewer FLOPs and 38.30× fewer parameters than LDU on aiMotive, making it substantially more practical for real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperDUM extends the hyperdimensional computing (HDC) / Vector Symbolic Architecture (VSA) framework from Ni et al. [37], which applied HDC prototypes to aleatoric uncertainty in regression, to the problem of feature-level epistemic uncertainty in multimodal fusion. It also builds on the prototype-learning paradigm of Latent Deterministic Uncertainty (LDU) [10], replacing LDU's learnable prototypes with hyperdimensional prototypes formed via bundling, and introduces two novel modifications—Channel-wise and Patch-wise Projection & Bundling (CPB/PPB)—that preserve per-channel and per-patch spatial structure rather than collapsing all dimensions into a single hypervector. In the multimodal fusion literature, prior uncertainty-aware fusion methods (e.g., [14], [30]) rely on Bayesian approximations or output-level calibration; HyperDUM is positioned as the first deterministic, single-forward-pass UQ method applied at the pre-fusion feature level in an autonomous driving setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 38× reduction in trainable parameters and 2.36× reduction in FLOPs relative to the SOTA deterministic method (LDU) makes feature-level epistemic UQ practically deployable in real-time autonomous driving stacks, where Bayesian approximations are often infeasible. A key limitation is the reliance on labeled data for supervised prototype bundling; the authors acknowledge that semi-supervised and unsupervised HDC variants exist but lack the theoretical guarantees of standard bundling, leaving open whether label-free prototype formation can match the calibration and robustness gains shown here. Future work could explore whether the massively parallel nature of HDC operations (element-wise addition, cosine similarity) enables further hardware-level speedups on embedded accelerators, and whether the channel/patch decomposition generalizes to other fusion architectures beyond BEV-space and pyramidal designs.
