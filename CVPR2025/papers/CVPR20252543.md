## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods fail to transfer knowledge effectively from large vision foundation models (LVFMs) with large backbones (e.g., ViT-L) to lightweight edge models (e.g., MobileNetV3) due to substantial model discrepancies in parameter count and heterogeneous architectures (ViT vs. CNN). While scaling up the teacher backbone improves the teacher's own downstream performance, the student model's performance gain plateaus, leaving the full potential of LVFMs unrealized for edge deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252543/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from both a small labeled set D_L and a larger unlabeled set D_U (target domain in UDA, unlabeled pool in SSL). The student is a pretrained off-the-shelf edge model (MobileNetV3, WideResNet28-2, ResNet18/50) that outputs class labels; the teacher is a frozen LVFM encoder (DINOv2, OpenCLIP, EVA02, ConvNeXt) providing intermediate features. No architectural modifications or additional inference-time computation are introduced to the student model.

## Method

CustomKD alternates two stages every epoch. In the feature customization stage, the frozen teacher encoder's output f_t is passed through a learnable projection layer θ_t^h and then through the student's frozen head classifier θ_s^c, with θ_t^h trained via cross-entropy on labeled data to produce a customized feature f̃_t aligned to the student's representation space. In the knowledge distillation stage, the student encoder is trained to match both the original teacher feature f_t (via a student-side projection θ_s^h, loss L_ft) and the customized feature f̃_t (directly, since dimensions already match, loss L_f̃t), alongside standard cross-entropy on labeled data and entropy minimization on unlabeled data. The key insight is that using the student's own head classifier to shape the teacher's features makes the knowledge more comprehensible to the student, bridging the architectural and capacity gap without modifying the student's architecture or inference pipeline.

## Results

On OfficeHome UDA (MobileNetV3 student, DINOv2 ViT-L teacher), CustomKD achieves 69.36% average accuracy as a feature-level KD method, and DKD+CustomKD reaches 74.63% as a prediction-level method, compared to the source MobileNetV3 at 46.39% (a ~23% gain). On DomainNet, DKD+CustomKD achieves 41.10% average accuracy vs. 34.10% for the source model. On CIFAR-100 SSL with 400 labeled samples (WideResNet28-2 student), CustomKD achieves 32.51% error rate, substantially outperforming the best KD baseline DKD (45.18%) and the best SSL method SimMatch (48.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CustomKD outperforms all UDA baselines (DAN 44.39%, DANN 46.11%, DSAN 47.52%) by a large margin, achieving 69.36% average accuracy on OfficeHome. Among feature-level KD methods, CustomKD surpasses FitNet (65.81%), RKD (54.97%), and CC (48.80%), with the largest gap over FitNet (+3.55%). When combined with prediction-level KD, DKD+CustomKD (74.63%) beats standalone DKD (69.48%), Logits (69.52%), and Soft Target (60.75%). On CIFAR-100 SSL (400 labels), CustomKD (32.51% error) dramatically outperforms all KD baselines (DKD 45.18%, FitNet 48.58%, Soft Target 48.71%) and all SSL methods (SimMatch 48.82%, FixMatch 53.37%, AdaMatch 52.07%). CustomKD is also composable with recent KD methods: applying it on top of TfFD, NORM, and DisWot yields consistent improvements (e.g., NORM+CustomKD 66.65% vs. NORM 59.20% on OfficeHome clipart→art/product/real-world).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends feature-level knowledge distillation (building on FitNet's projection-based feature matching) to the specific and challenging setting of distilling from large ViT-based vision foundation models (DINOv2, OpenCLIP) into small CNN-based edge models (MobileNetV3, WideResNet). Prior student-friendly KD methods addressed teacher-student discrepancy but were limited to similar architectures or small teacher backbones (e.g., ViT-S); this paper identifies that the discrepancy becomes prohibitive with large backbones (ViT-L) and proposes an alternating two-stage process—feature customization via the student's head classifier followed by dual-feature distillation—that bridges the gap. The approach is deliberately architecture-agnostic and composable, demonstrated by its plug-and-play integration with existing KD methods like DKD, TfFD, and NORM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: a 1.48M-parameter WideResNet28-2 can outperform a 21.52M-parameter DINOv2 (ViT-S) after CustomKD, without any architectural modification or inference-time overhead. The empirical finding that scaling teacher backbones yields diminishing returns for students under existing KD methods is a useful caution for practitioners assuming 'bigger teacher = better student.' Open questions include extension to dense prediction tasks (semantic segmentation), other modalities, and whether the alternating schedule generalizes to iterative or online settings. The method's simplicity (a single projection layer trained with cross-entropy) makes it easy to adopt, but its reliance on a pretrained student head classifier limits applicability to cold-start scenarios.
