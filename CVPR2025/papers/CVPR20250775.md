## Problem

Existing cross-modal retrieval methods are deterministic, relying solely on similarity scores and unable to capture the epistemic uncertainty inherent in retrieval outputs, which leads to unreliable results in high-stakes applications. While Evidential Deep Learning offers a low-cost alternative for uncertainty estimation, it produces counter-intuitive uncertainty values—underestimating uncertainty for conflicting evidence and overestimating it for confident predictions—because it depends only on total evidence and category count rather than how evidence is distributed across categories.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted VGGNet image features and word2vec text features, which are projected into a shared latent space whose dimensionality equals the number of categories. Beyond standard similarity-based retrieval, FUME outputs a per-result cross-modal uncertainty score in [0,1] computed by merging per-modality decision uncertainties; results exceeding a user-defined threshold (e.g., 0.5) are filtered out, effectively trading recall for precision to yield trusted retrieval.

## Method

FUME reframes classifier outputs as fuzzy membership degrees and defines a category credibility metric that averages the possibility measure (membership degree) and necessity measure (1 minus the maximum membership of all other categories). A novel fuzzy multimodal learning loss uses label information to guide necessity optimization in the correct direction—preventing the over-optimization of necessity for unmatched categories that would arise from naively aligning credibility with one-hot labels. Decision uncertainty is then computed as the normalized Shannon entropy of the category credibility vector, which avoids EDL's counter-intuitive behavior. A merging function g(u¹,u²)=1−(1−u¹)(1−u²) fuses the two per-modality decision uncertainties into a single cross-modal uncertainty that satisfies four axiomatic properties (boundedness, monotonicity, and boundary conditions). The full training objective combines this fuzzy multimodal loss with a multimodal contrastive (consistency) loss to simultaneously learn discriminative shared-space representations and reduce cross-modal discrepancy.

## Results

FUME achieves the best mAP@all across all five benchmark datasets without uncertainty filtering: 0.723 (Pascal Sentence), 0.530 (Wikipedia), 0.598 (NUS-WIDE-10K), 0.577 (INRIA-Websearch), and 0.651 (XMediaNet), outperforming the strongest baseline (RONO at 0.706 on Pascal Sentence, MARS at 0.579 on XMediaNet). When the proposed cross-modal uncertainty threshold (u=0.5) is applied to filter unreliable retrievals, mAP@all improves dramatically to 0.892, 0.743, 0.778, 0.904, and 0.864 respectively — gains of 16.9%–32.7% over the unfiltered FUME. The largest relative improvement from uncertainty filtering occurs on INRIA-Websearch (+32.7%), where FUME_u=0.5 reaches 0.904 mAP@all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUME outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset in both retrieval directions. On Pascal Sentence, FUME (0.723 avg) beats the closest competitor RONO (0.706) by 1.7 points; on XMediaNet, the gap is largest at 7.2 points over MARS (0.579). On NUS-WIDE-10K, FUME (0.598) edges out GNN4CMR (0.593) and SDML (0.583), while on INRIA-Websearch it leads MARS (0.555) by 2.2 points. The semi-supervised baselines SCL and HOPE, evaluated in their supervised variants, trail FUME by 3–10 points across datasets. The most striking result is that applying the uncertainty threshold (u=0.5) lifts FUME above 0.89 mAP@all on four of five datasets, a regime no baseline approaches — e.g., 0.904 on INRIA-Websearch versus 0.555 for the best baseline MARS, demonstrating that the fuzzy-set-based uncertainty quantification filters out unreliable retrievals far more effectively than similarity-score-based filtering or EDL-based uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUME sits at the intersection of cross-modal retrieval (CMR) and uncertainty quantification, repurposing Fuzzy Set Theory concepts (membership degrees, possibility and necessity measures) within a deep learning pipeline. It departs from Evidential Deep Learning (EDL), which the authors show produces counter-intuitive uncertainty estimates in the CMR setting (conflicting evidence yielding low uncertainty, confident predictions yielding high uncertainty), and from the earlier DECL framework that applied Dempster-Shafer theory to CMR but lacked fine-grained per-result uncertainty. The consistency learning component draws on standard contrastive learning (CLIP/SimCLR-style), while the fuzzy credibility formulation and label-guided necessity optimization constitute the paper's core methodological departure from prior CMR and uncertainty-estimation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: most CMR methods produce similarity scores without any indication of reliability, which is problematic in high-stakes applications (healthcare, autonomous driving). The large mAP gains from uncertainty-based filtering (up to 32.7%) demonstrate that a significant fraction of retrieved results are indeed unreliable, and that the proposed cross-modal uncertainty can identify them. However, the approach is limited to two modalities (image-text) in a supervised setting, the 0.5 uncertainty threshold is somewhat arbitrary, and it remains open whether the fuzzy credibility framework scales to more than two modalities or to truly open-world retrieval scenarios. The counter-intuitive EDL analysis, while well-documented, is specific to the CMR context and may not generalize to all classification tasks.
