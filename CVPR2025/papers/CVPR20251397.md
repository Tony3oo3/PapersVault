## Problem

Learning-based multi-view stereo methods rely on 3D cost volumes for cross-view feature matching, leading to high GPU memory consumption, failure under sparse viewpoints or weak textures, and limited generalization due to dependence on scarce 3D ground-truth supervision. The paper asks whether multi-view geometry can be reconstructed by instead conditioning a monocular depth estimator on explicit multi-view structure, thereby bypassing the expensive and fragile matching step entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251397/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images and a sparse SfM point cloud (from Detector-free SfM or COLMAP) as input. The point cloud is projected per-view into sparse depth maps, densified via k-nearest-neighbors interpolation (k=3), and paired with a Euclidean distance map that encodes confidence. The output is a per-view metric depth map at the same scale as the SfM reconstruction, which is then fused via TSDF or point-cloud fusion into a dense 3D scene mesh. Training resolution is 384×512; evaluation is performed at scene-native resolutions (e.g., 680×1200 on Replica).

## Method

Murre fine-tunes the U-Net of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion V2 model (VAE frozen) to perform latent-space depth denoising conditioned on three inputs: the RGB image, a KNN-densified SfM depth map, and a distance-to-nearest-valued-pixel map. The key design insight is using the SfM point cloud as an explicit intermediate representation that condenses multi-view information into a per-view sparse depth signal, making it naturally compatible with image-space diffusion conditioning and eliminating the need for 3D cost volumes. SfM depth is normalized per-view (top/bottom 2% filtered, range expanded by 0.8×/1.2×) so the model learns to predict at the SfM metric scale. Training uses an MSE loss on the predicted noise in latent space, supervised by GT depth from synthetic Hypersim and 3D Ken Burns scenes. At inference, a 5-sample ensemble (pixel-wise median) and an optional RANSAC-based scale/shift alignment between predicted and SfM depth further improve multi-view consistency before TSDF fusion.

## Results

On the DTU benchmark (15 scenes, 3-view reconstruction), Murre achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 1.42 mm, outperforming the next-best method MonoSDF (1.86 mm) by 24% and DUSt3R (2.81 mm) by nearly 2×. Murre uses only 86.4K training frames, far less than DUSt3R (8.5M), Metric3D (8M), or Depth-Anything (1.5M). On Replica (indoor), Murre achieves an F-score of 0.853 with DF-SfM(LoFTR) guidance, and the method generalizes to streetscapes (Waymo) and aerial scenes (UrbanScene3D) without additional training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Murre outperforms all nine baselines on DTU Chamfer Distance (average 1.42 mm). The closest competitor is MonoSDF (1.86 mm), a neural implicit surface method that Murre beats by 0.44 mm on average; Murre also wins on 14 of 15 individual scenes. Among MVS-based methods, MVNet (2.38 mm) and IGEV-MVS (3.50 mm) are both substantially outperformed, confirming that bypassing multi-view matching via SfM-guided diffusion is advantageous even in the 3-view setting. Monocular depth estimators without multi-view guidance—Marigold (5.46 mm), Depth-Anything (3.09 mm), Depth-Anythingv2 (3.69 mm), and Metric3D (5.01 mm)—produce visually plausible but metrically inconsistent depths, leading to poor fused reconstructions. Traditional SfM (COLMAP, 2.56 mm) and commercial software (RealityCapture, 2.84 mm) are also surpassed, as are optimization-based neural methods like VolSDF (4.21 mm). Notably, Murre achieves these results with only 86.4K training frames, compared to 8.5M for DUSt3R and 8M for Metric3D, highlighting the efficiency of leveraging 2D foundation model priors via fine-tuning on synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Murre sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-based monocular depth estimation (building on Marigold [Ke et al., CVPR 2024] and Stable Diffusion [Rombach et al., CVPR 2022]) and classical multi-view stereo reconstruction. It departs from the dominant MVS paradigm (MVSNet, IGEV-MVS, MVSFormer) that aggregates features in 3D cost volumes, and from pure monocular depth estimators (Depth-Anything, Metric3D) that lack multi-view consistency. The key conceptual move is using SfM point clouds as an explicit intermediate representation to condition the diffusion model, effectively converting a multi-view problem into a per-view conditional generation problem with sparse geometric guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bypassing multi-view matching entirely, Murre addresses two persistent pain points in learning-based MVS: prohibitive GPU memory for high-resolution reconstruction and failure under sparse viewpoints. The ability to fine-tune on only ~86K synthetic frames yet generalize across object, indoor, street, and aerial scenes suggests that 2D foundation model priors can substitute for large-scale 3D supervision. However, the method still requires a working SfM solution (infeasible with only two overlapping views) and handles only static scenes, leaving dynamic and extremely sparse-view settings open. Future work on integrating DUSt3R-style pairwise matching for pose estimation and tracking for dynamic elements is identified as a natural next step.
