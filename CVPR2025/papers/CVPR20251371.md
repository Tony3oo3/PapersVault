## Problem

Unsupervised video object-centric learning methods based on recurrent slot processing lack long-term temporal consistency, meaning the same object can be assigned to different slots across frames. This instability is particularly severe on real-world videos with occlusions, reappearances, and complex multi-object interactions, undermining downstream tasks like multi-object tracking and dynamic scene modeling. Existing approaches enforce consistency only implicitly through recurrence or on toy datasets, leaving a gap for scalable, explicitly consistency-enforcing objectives on unconstrained video.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips as sequences of RGB frames, which are encoded by a frozen DINOv2 ViT (ViT-S/14 at 336×336 for MOVi-C yielding 576 patch tokens; ViT-B/14 at 518×518 for MOVi-E and YouTube-VIS yielding 1369 tokens). Outputs are K object-centric slot vectors per frame (64-dim for MOVi-C/YTVIS21, 128-dim for MOVi-E) that serve as stable object identifiers over time, decodable via an MLP into reconstructed DINOv2 features or object segmentation masks. For the dynamics prediction task, a separately trained SlotFormer autoregressively predicts future slots from a burn-in sequence of inferred slots.

## Method

SLOT CONTRAST builds on a Recurrent Slot Attention encoder-decoder (grouping module + temporal predictor) over frozen DINOv2 features, but introduces two key changes. First, a slot-slot contrastive loss (InfoNCE with cosine similarity) enforces temporal consistency by treating same-slot representations from consecutive frames as positives and all other slots across the entire batch of videos as negatives, preventing degenerate solutions that rely solely on initialization uniqueness. Second, a learned fixed initialization replaces random Gaussian sampling, producing distinct initial queries that consistently attend to different objects. The final training objective combines a feature reconstruction loss (decoding slots back to DINOv2 features) with the contrastive loss weighted by a hyperparameter α, and the batch-level contrast set is what drives the large gains over intra-video-only contrast.

## Results

On the real-world YouTube-VIS 2021 dataset, SLOT CONTRAST achieves 38.0 FG-ARI and 33.7 mBO, surpassing VideoSAURv2 (31.2 FG-ARI, 29.7 mBO) by +6.8 and +4.0 points respectively. On synthetic MOVi-E, it reaches 82.9 FG-ARI (vs. 77.1 for VideoSAURv2) but trails on mBO (29.2 vs. 34.4). For per-frame object discovery on MOVi-E, SLOT CONTRAST attains 84.8 image FG-ARI, outperforming even weakly-supervised methods that use motion segmentation masks (DIOD: 82.2, MoToK: 66.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLOT CONTRAST decisively outperforms the older baselines SAVi (22.2→69.3 FG-ARI on MOVi-C) and STEVE (36.1→69.3), confirming that the slot-slot contrastive loss plus learned initialization is far more effective than pixel-reconstruction objectives alone. Against the strongest prior method VideoSAUR, SLOT CONTRAST wins on video FG-ARI across all three datasets (69.3 vs. 64.8 on MOVi-C; 82.9 vs. 73.9 on MOVi-E; 38.0 vs. 28.9 on YouTube-VIS) but concedes on mBO for the synthetic benchmarks (32.7 vs. 38.9 on MOVi-C; 29.2 vs. 35.6 on MOVi-E), indicating slightly less sharp masks in controlled settings. On the most challenging real-world YouTube-VIS, SLOT CONTRAST beats both VideoSAUR and its DINOv2-upgraded variant VideoSAURv2 on both metrics (38.0/33.7 vs. 31.2/29.7). For per-frame object discovery on MOVi-E, SLOT CONTRAST (84.8 image FG-ARI) surpasses weakly-supervised methods that exploit motion segmentation masks—DIOD (82.2) and MoToK (66.7)—as well as SOLV (80.8) and VideoSAUR (78.4), demonstrating that the contrastive temporal objective alone is sufficient to match or exceed motion-cue-based approaches. The one consistent trade-off is mBO on synthetic data, where VideoSAUR's temporal-similarity prediction yields sharper masks, suggesting the contrastive loss prioritizes identity consistency over pixel-level mask precision in controlled scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recurrent Slot Attention framework for video (SAVi, Kipf et al. 2022) and the DINOv2-based feature reconstruction pipeline (DINOSAUR, Seitzer et al. 2023; VideoSAUR, Zadaianchuk et al. 2023) by introducing an explicit slot-level temporal contrastive loss (InfoNCE) that pulls same-object slots across consecutive frames together while pushing apart all other slots in the batch. The key conceptual departure from prior video OCL methods is that temporal consistency is enforced through a dedicated contrastive objective rather than emerging implicitly from reconstruction or feature-similarity prediction. Additionally, the learned slot initialization (replacing random Gaussian sampling) is shown to be synergistic with the contrastive loss, a combination not explored in prior slot-based video models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal consistency of object slots is a prerequisite for downstream tasks like unsupervised tracking, dynamics prediction, and structured world modeling, yet prior methods struggled to maintain it on long, complex real-world videos. SLOT CONTRAST demonstrates that a simple contrastive objective can simultaneously improve both temporal consistency and per-frame object discovery (outperforming motion-supervised baselines), suggesting that contrastive structure in slot space is a useful inductive bias broadly applicable to OCL architectures. Open questions include whether the fixed slot count and inability to control segmentation granularity limit applicability, whether the contrastive loss composes well with other OCL inductive biases (e.g., SlotDiffusion), and how the approach scales to compositional world modeling from robotics data.
