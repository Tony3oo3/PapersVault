## Problem

Existing image cropping methods require training dedicated neural networks on specific datasets, and each cropping variant (free-form, subject-aware, aspect ratio-aware) demands a separate architecture and retraining. While large vision-language models (VLMs) support in-context learning without parameter updates, this capability has not been explored for image cropping, and key challenges—automated selection of relevant in-context examples and incorporating aesthetic judgment—remain unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252463/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB query image (optionally accompanied by a binary subject mask or a target aspect ratio string) and retrieves top-S visually similar images from a labeled training set as in-context demonstrations. The VLM outputs R crop candidates per iteration, each encoded as a 5-tuple (MOS score, x1, y1, x2, y2) with spatial coordinates normalized to a 1–1000 grid. After L refinement rounds, the final output is a single crop region (coordinates plus optional MOS) that can be applied to produce the cropped image.

## Method

Cropper is a training-free, two-stage pipeline built on a pretrained VLM (Gemini 1.5 Pro or GPT-4o). Stage 1 (visual prompt retrieval) uses CLIP ViT-B/32 cosine similarity to select the top-S most relevant images from a labeled dataset, then applies a task-specific criterion to pick the most informative ground-truth crop for each: highest MOS for free-form, nearest mask-center L2 distance for subject-aware, and closest aspect-ratio match for aspect-ratio-aware cropping. Stage 2 (iterative crop refinement) prompts the VLM to generate R candidate crops, scores each with VILA-R (aesthetics), CLIP embedding similarity (content preservation), and normalized area size, then feeds the scored crops back into the VLM prompt for L refinement iterations (default L=2, R=6, S=30, temperature 0.05). The final crop is selected from the last iteration for free-form and subject-aware tasks, or from the highest-scoring candidate across all iterations for aspect-ratio-aware cropping.

## Results

On the GAICD free-form cropping benchmark, Cropper achieves Acc₁/₅ of 88.9%, Acc₁₀ of 96.5%, and SRCC of 0.904, substantially outperforming the best training-based baseline (Chao et al., 70.0% Acc₁/₅, 83.3% Acc₁₀, 0.872 SRCC) by ~19 points on Acc₁/₅. On SACD subject-aware cropping, Cropper reaches IoU 0.769 and Disp 0.0372, surpassing the best baseline SAC-Net (IoU 0.767, Disp 0.0491). On FCDB aspect-ratio-aware cropping, Cropper attains IoU 0.756 and Disp 0.053, outperforming Mars (IoU 0.735, Disp 0.062). All results are training-free, using only 30 in-context examples and 2 refinement iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cropper outperforms all 10 baselines across the three cropping tasks. On GAICD free-form cropping, Cropper leads the strongest baseline Chao et al. [30] by 18.9 points on Acc₁/₅ (88.9 vs 70.0) and 13.2 points on Acc₁₀ (96.5 vs 83.3), though it trails slightly on PCC (0.860 vs 0.893). TransView [23] and GAIC [37] are the next closest competitors at 69.0% and 68.2% Acc₁/₅ respectively, both well below Cropper. On SACD subject-aware cropping, Cropper edges out SAC-Net [35] by 0.002 IoU (0.769 vs 0.767) while substantially reducing Disp (0.0372 vs 0.0491), and dominates earlier methods like VPN [32] (IoU 0.704) and A2RL [16] (IoU 0.667). On FCDB aspect-ratio cropping, Cropper beats Mars [17] by 0.021 IoU (0.756 vs 0.735) and 0.009 Disp (0.053 vs 0.062). The user study further confirms Cropper's superiority, with human raters preferring Cropper over A2RL [16], GAIC [37], and CGS [18] by margins of 21–27 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the in-context learning paradigm from NLP and early visual ICL work (e.g., image inpainting, segmentation) to the image cropping domain, where no prior ICL-based approach existed. It draws on prompt retrieval literature (CLIP-based similarity, supervised retrievers) to automate example selection, and on LLM-as-optimizer work (Yang et al., 2024) to motivate iterative refinement with external scorer feedback. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of training task-specific cropping networks (GAIC, VPN, A2RL, etc.) by showing that a frozen VLM with a handful of retrieved examples can match or exceed them, unifying free-form, subject-aware, and aspect-ratio-aware cropping under a single prompt-based framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of Cropper is practically significant: it eliminates the need to retrain a new network for each cropping variant or dataset shift, and the unified prompt design means a single VLM call handles all three task types. The main limitation is inference latency, which is bottlenecked by the retrieval step over a large candidate pool and multiple VLM API calls per image. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to cropping tasks beyond the three studied (e.g., multi-subject, video), whether smaller open-source VLMs can close the gap with proprietary ones, and how retrieval quality scales with dataset size. The reliance on proprietary VLMs (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o) also raises reproducibility and cost concerns for broader adoption.
