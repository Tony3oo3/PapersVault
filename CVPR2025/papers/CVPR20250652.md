## Problem

Existing diffusion-based face rigging methods trained on single-image-per-individual datasets (e.g., FFHQ) are forced into a reconstruction setup where the model either trivially copies the reference image (ignoring the target control) or must compress the reference into a single vector, losing fine-grained identity and semantic details like hairstyle. This necessitates cumbersome individual-specific fine-tuning and limits practical usability for real-world face editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250652/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB reference face image and a 9-channel target control (surface normal, albedo, and Lambertian rendering extracted via DECA from FLAME 3DMM parameters) as input, along with a corresponding 9-channel reference control extracted from the reference image itself. It produces a 256×256 rigged face image that matches the target pose, expression, and lighting while preserving the reference identity and semantic details. Training uses paired quadruplets {X_R, X_T, D_R, D_T} sampled from two random frames of the same video in CelebV-HQ, avoiding the reconstruction formulation entirely.

## Method

ControlFace employs a dual-branch U-Net architecture (both initialized from Stable Diffusion v1.5): a FaceNet branch encodes the reference image for identity and fine details, while a denoising U-Net handles generation, with the two integrated via augmented self-attention that concatenates keys and values from both networks. A lightweight Face Controller (~1M params) encodes the target 3DMM control directly into the denoising U-Net, and a Control Mixer Module (CMM) with shared-weight cross-attention layers produces correlated embeddings between the target and reference controls at four resolutions, which are injected into the augmented attention to guide where the model should attend. At inference, a novel Reference Control Guidance (RCG) mechanism replaces the standard null condition in Classifier-Free Guidance with the reference control D_R, providing a face-aligned grounding signal that yields cleaner guidance deltas across all timesteps. The model is trained on CelebV-HQ video pairs, which avoids the reconstruction trap and fully leverages FaceNet's rich representations without the model ignoring the target control.

## Results

On FFHQ, ControlFace achieves the best average DECA re-inference error (4.85) for control adherence, outperforming DiffusionRig (5.06), GIF (8.91), CapHuman (10.40), and Arc2Face (10.77). For identity and image quality, ControlFace attains an ID score of 0.7586, FID of 15.50, and LPIPS of 0.1429, achieving the lowest FID and LPIPS among all methods while maintaining near-best identity preservation. In the user study, ControlFace scores 0.8750 on semantic consistency and 0.8605 on perceptual quality, roughly doubling the scores of CapHuman (0.4086 SC, 0.7019 PQ) and DiffusionRig (0.3533 SC, 0.5072 PQ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ControlFace outperforms all four baselines on the majority of metrics. Against DiffusionRig (the closest competitor), ControlFace achieves a lower average DECA re-inference error (4.85 vs. 5.06), a substantially higher ID score (0.7586 vs. 0.2042), a lower FID (15.50 vs. 23.05), and a much lower LPIPS (0.1429 vs. 0.3758), indicating superior identity preservation and semantic consistency without requiring per-identity fine-tuning. Against CapHuman, ControlFace wins on every reported metric: ID (0.7586 vs. 0.4356), FID (15.50 vs. 59.78), LPIPS (0.1429 vs. 0.4556), and average control adherence (4.85 vs. 10.40). Against Arc2Face, ControlFace trades a small ID margin (0.7586 vs. 0.7825) for dramatically better LPIPS (0.1429 vs. 0.5253) and FID (15.50 vs. 17.82), reflecting Arc2Face's failure to preserve background and semantic details. GIF, which does not accept a reference image, is excluded from ID and LPIPS evaluation and shows the worst lighting control adherence (17.04 vs. 3.75 for ControlFace).

## Relation to Prior Work

ControlFace builds directly on the latent diffusion model architecture (Stable Diffusion v1.5) and the 3DMM-rendering conditioning paradigm established by prior face rigging methods (GIF, CapHuman, Arc2Face, DiffusionRig). Its key departure is the dual-branch U-Net design—pairing a dedicated FaceNet for rich identity/detail encoding with a denoising U-Net—replacing the single-vector identity compression (e.g., ArcFace embeddings) used by most prior diffusion-based methods. The paper also introduces two novel control mechanisms: a Control Mixer Module that cross-attends between target and reference 3DMM renderings, and Reference Control Guidance, which substitutes the reference control for the null condition in CFG. Training on a facial video dataset (CelebV-HQ) rather than single-image-per-identity datasets (FFHQ) is a deliberate shift away from reconstruction-based training, which the authors show causes models to ignore target controls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for individual-specific fine-tuning while simultaneously preserving fine-grained identity and semantic details (hairstyle, background), ControlFace addresses the two most cited practical barriers to deploying face rigging in real-world applications. The RCG mechanism—a simple but effective re-framing of the null condition in guidance—offers a transferable idea for other conditional generation tasks where a 'before' state provides better grounding than an empty label. Open questions remain: the method's reliance on DECA for 3DMM extraction limits geometric fidelity, training on a single video dataset constrains diversity, and whether the dual-branch approach scales to higher resolutions or more complex multi-person scenes is unexplored.
