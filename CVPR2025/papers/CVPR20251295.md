## Problem

Existing video retrieval datasets are limited in scale (typically under 10K videos), clip duration (often under 30 seconds), and linguistic diversity (predominantly English), and they reward models for matching vague descriptive captions rather than specific event-centric queries. This creates a stark gap relative to text retrieval benchmarks such as HC4 (6M+ documents) and fails to reflect real-world multilingual, multimodal retrieval where queries target specific information scattered across visual content, audio, embedded text, and metadata.

## Input / Output

The system takes a manually-written English text query targeting a specific world event and retrieves relevant videos from a collection of 218,000+ multilingual news videos (Arabic, Chinese, English, Korean, Russian, Spanish) spanning professional broadcasts to raw cell-phone footage, with clips up to 5 minutes long. Queries are deliberately designed to target information in a specific modality—visual content, spoken audio, embedded on-screen text, or human-written metadata—forcing systems to integrate across modalities and languages. Two evaluation variants are provided: MULTIVENT TEST-DESC (permitting human-written video descriptions) and MULTIVENT TEST-NODESC (disallowing them), reflecting the reality that raw user-generated content often lacks clean metadata.

## Method

The dataset is constructed by merging the original MultiVENT 1.0 collection (2,400 event-aligned videos) with a filtered subset of InternVid (~40,000 videos per language, excluding clips longer than 5 minutes), yielding 218K+ videos split into train (108,500) and test (109,800) sets. Queries are created through a two-pronged annotation process by professional linguists: for MultiVENT 1.0 events, annotators write base event queries plus specific queries leveraging fine-grained MULTIVENT GROUNDED annotations (frame-level bounding boxes, time intervals, and IE-based questions); for InternVid videos, annotators write base queries plus up to three modality-specific queries (Description, Speech via Whisper transcripts, Embedded Text via multilingual OCR). Relevance is graded on a 0-1-3 scale across 16,116 judged pairs, with silver judgments propagated from base to associated specific queries to ensure consistent credit. Evaluation compares full VLMs (mCLIP, VAST, LanguageBind, InternVideo2.0) against single-modality pipelines (vision, OCR, speech, description) using R@10, R@100, MRR, mAP, and nDCG@10, with detailed breakdowns by language, video type, query type, and event type to diagnose where each modality succeeds or fails.

## Results

On MultiVENT 2.0, the best full vision-language model (LanguageBind) achieves R@10=0.355, R@100=0.620, MRR=0.443, mAP=0.283, and nDCG@10=0.324. The vision-only mCLIP pipeline is nearly as strong (R@10=0.333, nDCG@10=0.303), while InternVideo2.0 collapses to R@10=0.004 and VAST to R@10=0.118. In stark contrast, all models score R@10 > 0.73 on the standard MSR-VTT benchmark, highlighting the dramatic difficulty increase from event-centric multilingual retrieval over 218K videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LanguageBind is the strongest full VLM on MultiVENT 2.0 (R@10=0.355, nDCG@10=0.324), but it is closely matched by the simple vision-only mCLIP pipeline (R@10=0.333, nDCG@10=0.303), suggesting that multimodal fusion does not yet provide a clear advantage over single-modality retrieval in this setting. InternVideo2.0, despite being state-of-the-art on MSR-VTT (R@10=0.851), fails almost entirely on MultiVENT 2.0 (R@10=0.004), likely due to its training on short clips and simple visual concepts. VAST, which integrates vision, audio, and subtitle modalities, also underperforms (R@10=0.118), indicating that its omni-modality pretraining does not transfer well to event-centric multilingual retrieval. Among the single-modality pipelines, the Description→mCLIP text baseline achieves the highest MRR (0.445) and R@100 (0.491), while Whisper→mCLIP excels specifically on professional news broadcasts (R@10=0.318) but is nearly useless on raw footage (R@10=0.021). The OCR pipeline (ICDAR→mCLIP) is the weakest overall (R@10=0.227) but shows relative strength on embedded-text queries (0.245) compared to other modalities. Overall, no single system adequately addresses the full range of query types, video types, and languages in MultiVENT 2.0, underscoring the benchmark's difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MultiVENT 1.0 (Sanders et al., 2023), a 2,400-video multilingual event-centric collection, by augmenting it with ~215,000 additional videos drawn from InternVid (Wang et al., 2024) and by replacing the original caption-as-query paradigm with manually crafted event-specific queries that target distinct modalities (visual, audio, embedded text, metadata). It also leverages fine-grained event annotations from MultiVENT Grounded (Sanders et al., 2024) to generate aspect-specific queries. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant video retrieval literature (MSR-VTT, VATEX, ActivityNet) where short descriptive captions serve as proxy queries over small English-only collections, reframing the task as large-scale, multilingual, event-centric retrieval requiring cross-modal and cross-lingual reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark gap between current VLM capabilities and the demands of realistic event-centric video retrieval: models that achieve R@10 > 0.8 on MSR-VTT drop to near-zero on MultiVENT 2.0, and even the best system (LanguageBind) reaches only R@10 ≈ 0.36. Single-modality pipelines (speech for news broadcasts, descriptions for raw footage) show complementary strengths but none suffices alone, leaving open the question of how to robustly fuse heterogeneous modalities under multilingual, long-video conditions. The proposed dual evaluation protocol (with/without descriptions) highlights a practical concern for real-time event scenarios where clean metadata is unavailable, and the 218K-video scale makes this a more realistic stress test than prior collections.
