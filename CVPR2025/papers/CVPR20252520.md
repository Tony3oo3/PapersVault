## Problem

In Federated Graph Learning, clients hold local graphs with different homophily levels (the proportion of edges connecting same-class nodes), a phenomenon the paper terms homophily heterogeneity. This creates two unaddressed challenges: (1) homophily conflict, where GNNs overfit to their local homophily level and feature propagation scheme, degrading global generalizability during collaboration; and (2) homophily bias, where clients with different homophily levels contribute unequally to the global model, yet standard node-count-based aggregation treats them equally. Existing FGL methods focus on structural non-IID but ignore this homophily-level discrepancy.

## Input / Output

Each client provides a local graph with node features, an adjacency matrix, and partially visible node labels (some labels are invisible, making edge relationships unknown). The method produces a globally generalizable GNN (ACM-GCN backbone, 2 layers, hidden dim 128) for node classification. A key constraint is that the overall local homophily level is inaccessible due to partially visible labels, which motivates the use of spectral high-frequency area as a proxy for quantifying homophily bias during aggregation.

## Method

FedSPA comprises two hyperparameter-free components. First, Subgraph Feature Propagation Decoupling (SFPD) splits feature propagation into three paths: a homophilic channel (edges where both endpoints share a label, homophily=1), a heterophilic channel (edges where endpoints differ, homophily=0), and an unknown channel (edges with at least one invisible label) whose parameters are a learned convex combination of the other two via a harmonizer. This ensures each channel is trained on a unified homophily level across all clients, directly mitigating homophily conflict. Second, Homophily Bias-Driven Aggregation (HBDA) reweights client contributions: for the harmonizer, it computes a prior distribution bias using the graph spectral high-frequency area (proven to be negatively correlated with homophily level) and applies negative-exponential weighting; for the two decoupled channels, it computes a posterior parameter bias via the Fisher Information Matrix to capture parameter sensitivity distribution disparity. Both biases are normalized via min-max scaling before the exponential weighting, so clients closer to the global optimization direction receive higher aggregation weights.

## Results

FedSPA achieves the best node classification accuracy on 6 out of 7 datasets, with the largest gains on heterophilic graphs: +4.7% on Cornell (77.0% vs. 72.3% FedAvg), +4.6% on Wisconsin (90.3% vs. 85.7%), and +2.5% on Arxiv-Year (38.1% vs. 35.6%). On homophilic datasets, improvements are more modest but consistent: +1.7% on Cora (82.5%), +2.6% on Coauthor-CS (94.4%), and +1.0% on Coauthor-Physics (95.3%). The only dataset where FedSPA is not the top performer is Cora, where FGSSL edges it out by 0.3% (82.8% vs. 82.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSPA outperforms all 11 baselines on 6 of 7 datasets, with the sole exception being Cora where FGSSL achieves 82.8% vs. FedSPA's 82.5% (a 0.3% margin within one standard deviation). Against the strongest FGL-specific baselines, FedSPA leads by +1.3% over FGGP on CS (94.4% vs. 92.8%), +2.1% over FGGP on Minesweeper (83.2% vs. 81.9%), and +1.0% over FedGTA on Arxiv-Year (38.1% vs. 37.1%). The advantage is most pronounced on heterophilic graphs: FedSPA surpasses the best baseline (AdaFGL) by +2.1% on Cornell (77.0% vs. 74.9%) and +2.3% on Wisconsin (90.3% vs. 88.0%), confirming that homophily heterogeneity is the dominant challenge in low-homophily settings. Traditional FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova) and the personalized FL method FedFa trail FedSPA by 1.7–4.7% across all datasets, underscoring that generic heterogeneity handling is insufficient for graph-structured data with varying homophily levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of federated graph learning and the homophily/heterophily GNN literature. While prior FGL methods (FedStar, FedPub, FedGTA, AdaFGL, FGGP) address structural or distributional heterogeneity, none explicitly model the impact of inconsistent homophily levels across clients. The decoupling of homophilic and heterophilic feature propagation draws conceptually from heterophilic GNN designs (H2GNN, CPGNN, ACM-GCN), but is repurposed here to enforce unified homophily levels per channel in a federated setting rather than to improve single-graph performance. The aggregation strategy repurposes spectral graph theory (high-frequency area) and the Fisher Information Matrix—tools from information geometry and graph signal processing—for client reweighting, a novel application in the FGL context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying homophily heterogeneity as a distinct and previously unaddressed source of degradation in FGL, this paper opens a new axis of analysis for federated graph methods beyond the standard label/feature non-IID framing. The hyperparameter-free design (SFPD + HBDA) is practical and could be integrated into existing FGL pipelines. Limitations include reliance on a single GNN backbone (ACM-GCN), restriction to inter-graph FGL, and the need for partially visible labels to define homophilic/heterophilic/unknown edge sets—conditions that may not hold in all real-world deployments. Open questions include whether the spectral bias measure scales efficiently to very large graphs and whether the approach extends to intra-graph FGL or other downstream tasks like link prediction.
