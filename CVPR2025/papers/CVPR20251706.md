## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks for MLLMs suffer from limited video type diversity, insufficient temporal coverage (mostly short clips), and a narrow focus on a single modality (video frames only). This makes it impossible to holistically assess whether MLLMs can handle the full spectrum of real-world video content spanning multiple domains, durations from seconds to hours, and complementary modalities like subtitles and audio.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251706/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance consists of a video (11 s to 1 h) sampled into frames at a model-specific rate, a multiple-choice question with four options, and optionally the corresponding subtitle transcript and audio track. The model outputs a single letter (A–D), scored by exact string match against the ground-truth answer. Subtitles are aligned to the sampled frame timestamps, and audio is provided as a separate channel; the benchmark reports accuracy under three modality settings (frames only, frames+subtitles, frames+audio) to isolate each modality's contribution.

## Method

Video-MME is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) collecting 900 YouTube videos across 6 domains and 30 fine-grained subcategories, stratified into short (<2 min), medium (4–15 min), and long (30–60 min) duration tiers; (2) expert annotators watch each full video and author 3 multiple-choice questions (2,700 total) spanning 12 task types from perception to multi-step reasoning; (3) a rigorous quality review verifies linguistic clarity, logical consistency of options, and—critically—filters out any question answerable from text alone (Gemini 1.5 Pro achieves <15% accuracy on the text-only subset, confirming video content is necessary). Temporal difficulty is quantified via certificate length (the minimum sub-clip set sufficient to justify the answer), with medians of 26 s, 165 s, and 891 s for short/medium/long subsets, making it the most temporally demanding video QA dataset to date. Evaluation covers 16+ models (commercial and open-source) under matched protocols, with ablations over modality and duration to diagnose specific failure modes.

## Results

On Video-MME, Gemini 1.5 Pro achieves the highest overall accuracy of 75.0% (frames only) and 81.3% (with subtitles), outperforming GPT-4o (71.9%/77.2%) and GPT-4V (59.9%/63.3%). Among open-source models, VILA-1.5 (34B) leads with 59.0% overall accuracy, followed by VITA-1.5 (7B) at 56.1% and LLaVA-NeXT-Video (34B) at 52.0%. Image-based models (Qwen-VL-Max at 51.3%, InternVL-Chat-V1.5 at 50.7%) perform comparably to the video-specific LLaVA-NeXT-Video, confirming the benchmark's universality across image and video MLLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 1.5 Pro leads all evaluated models by a clear margin, surpassing GPT-4o by 3.1% (75.0% vs. 71.9%) and GPT-4V by 15.1% on frames-only overall accuracy. Among open-source video MLLMs, VILA-1.5 (34B) is the strongest at 59.0%, but still trails Gemini 1.5 Pro by 16.0%, with the gap widening on long videos (50.8% vs. 67.4%). VITA-1.5 (7B) is the best small open-source model at 56.1%, outperforming LLaVA-NeXT-Video (34B) by 4.1% despite having roughly 5× fewer parameters. Image-based models Qwen-VL-Max (51.3%) and InternVL-Chat-V1.5 (50.7%) achieve accuracy close to the video-specific LLaVA-NeXT-Video (52.0%), suggesting that strong image understanding transfers well to video tasks. Adding subtitles consistently improves all models, with the largest gains on long videos (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro: +10.1% on long vs. +2.8% on short), and audio provides additional but smaller improvements (+4.3% overall for Gemini 1.5 Pro).

## Relation to Prior Work

Video-MME extends the line of video QA benchmarks for MLLMs (MVBench, EgoSchema, TempCompass, NExT-QA, Video-Bench) by being the first to simultaneously cover the full temporal spectrum (11 s to 1 hr), six visual domains with 30 subfields, and multi-modal inputs (subtitles and audio) under a single unified evaluation. It inherits the certificate-length analysis from EgoSchema to quantify temporal difficulty and adopts the multiple-choice QA format common to prior video benchmarks, but departs from them by requiring expert manual annotation with a text-only filtering step (using Gemini 1.5 Pro) to ensure questions cannot be answered without video content. The evaluation protocol also bridges image and video MLLMs by allowing frame-based image models to be assessed alongside native video models, a practice not standard in prior video-specific benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial gap between the best commercial model (Gemini 1.5 Pro at 75%) and the best open-source model (VILA-1.5 at 59%), and quantifies a consistent performance decline as video duration increases, identifying long-context modeling as a critical bottleneck for the field. The finding that image MLLMs (Qwen-VL-Max, InternVL-Chat-V1.5) perform comparably to video-specific models (~50%) suggests that video understanding is largely grounded in image understanding, reframing where architectural investment should focus. Open questions remain around how to build training data for complex temporal reasoning in long videos and how to architecturally extend context windows without prohibitive compute, both of which the paper identifies as key directions for next-generation MLLMs.
