## Problem

Most video-text retrieval methods assume accurate visual-text correspondences during training, but real datasets inevitably contain semantically mismatched pairs (noisy correspondences) that mislead the model. Existing noise-rectify approaches rely on confident similarity predictions to partition clean/noisy subsets, creating a self-perpetuating error loop under high noise ratios. This paper asks whether more reliable supervision can be constructed from representative reference features and inter-pair structural relations rather than from individual pairwise comparisons.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips (12 or 64 frames resized to 224×224) and text descriptions (32 or 64 tokens) encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone, producing 512-dim frame and word embeddings. Within each batch of 128 pairs, the top-K=10 cleanest pairs are selected as reference agents, and the top-4 agents per sample are used to compute permutation-based ranking distributions. The final output is a video-text similarity score (via purified max-correspondence interaction) and a retrieval ranking, trained with an InfoNCE contrastive loss plus a KL-divergence ranking consistency regularizer (λ=0.2).

## Method

The RPC network has two procedures. First, representative agents are constructed with three mechanisms: a semantic purification mechanism that replaces simple dot-product similarity with a thresholded max-correspondence interaction (selecting only frame-word pairs exceeding a data-driven relevance threshold) and a bidirectional evaluation to pick the top-K most reliable pairs; a cross-aggregation step where agents attend to all batch features via cross-attention to absorb diverse semantics; and a self-aggregation step where concatenated video and text agent features are refined via multi-head self-attention to bridge the modality gap. Second, relation-aware ranking distribution alignment treats the ordering of agents as a stochastic event: for each video/text, the probability of every permutation of the top-4 agents is computed from the softmax agent-correlation vector, yielding a ranking distribution over |P| permutations. Consistency between the video and text ranking distributions is enforced via KL divergence against the purified pairwise similarity matrix, providing a higher-order structural regularization that is more robust to noisy correspondences than individual pairwise comparisons.

## Results

On MSR-VTT 1k-A at 50% noise, the proposed RPC achieves T2V R@1 of 44.6 and V2T R@1 of 44.3, surpassing the strongest baseline RVTR (36.2 / 36.0) by 8.4 and 8.3 points respectively. Even in the noise-free setting, RPC leads with T2V R@1 of 47.6 and V2T R@1 of 46.3, outperforming RVTR by 1.8 and 2.4 points. Across all five datasets (MSR-VTT, MSVD, LSMDC, ActivityNet, DiDeMo) at 50% noise, RPC consistently achieves the best R@1 in both retrieval directions, with gains of 7.8–9.8 points over RVTR on T2V R@1. The method exhibits remarkable noise resilience: as noise increases from 20% to 50%, RPC's T2V R@1 on 1k-A drops only 1.5 points, whereas RVTR drops 6.1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPC outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and noise level. Against the closest competitor RVTR [52] (a noise-rectify method), RPC gains 8.4 T2V R@1 points on MSR-VTT 1k-A at 50% noise (44.6 vs. 36.2) and 9.8 points on MSVD (38.5 vs. 28.7). CLIP4Clip [27], the strongest non-noise-aware baseline, is surpassed by 20.2 T2V R@1 points on MSR-VTT 1k-A at 50% noise (44.6 vs. 24.4), highlighting the severe degradation of standard contrastive methods under noise. Older baselines CE [24], MMT [8], TT-CE+ [6], and T2VLAD [45] all suffer catastrophic performance collapse at 50% noise (T2V R@1 dropping below 15 on MSR-VTT 1k-A), while RPC retains over 44. Notably, even in the noise-free (0%) setting, RPC exceeds RVTR by 1.8 T2V R@1 points, indicating that the relation-aware ranking distribution alignment provides genuine representational benefits beyond noise robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant noise-rectify paradigm for noisy correspondence (NC) learning, which partitions training data into clean/noisy subsets via the DNN memory effect and corrects labels. Instead, it introduces a fundamentally different mechanism: constructing a small set of representative reference 'agents' from the batch and enforcing consistency through a ranking distribution over agent permutations, rather than through pairwise label correction. The approach builds on CLIP-initialized encoders and InfoNCE contrastive loss (standard in modern VTR), but repurposes cross-attention and self-attention not for feature fusion per se, but for enriching and harmonizing the agent representations. It also extends the NC learning literature (originally developed for image-text matching and person re-identification) into the video-text retrieval domain, where prior work (e.g., RVTR) has relied on the same noise-rectify strategy the authors argue is fragile under high noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively lightweight mechanism (K=10 agents, top-4 permutation ranking) can substantially improve robustness to noisy correspondences in VTR, with the most pronounced gains at 50% noise where prior methods degrade severely. The ranking-distribution framing—treating agent ordering as a stochastic event to capture inter-agent structural relations—offers a conceptually distinct supervision signal that may transfer to other cross-modal retrieval tasks. However, all noise is synthetic (random caption shuffling), so it remains open whether the approach generalizes to realistic annotation errors (e.g., partially relevant captions, temporal misalignment). The method is also tied to CLIP ViT-B/32 and a specific batch-level agent selection strategy, leaving questions about scalability to larger backbones, longer videos, or streaming settings.
