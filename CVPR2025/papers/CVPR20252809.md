## Problem

Existing 3D PEFT methods are predominantly additive (adapters or prompts), which introduce inference overhead and are tightly coupled to Transformer architectures, limiting generalization to Mamba or hierarchical backbones. Reparameterization-based alternatives like LoRA avoid these issues but rely on low intrinsic rank, capturing only global information while failing to model the local geometric structure essential to irregular point clouds. This paper addresses the gap by introducing the first reparameterization-based 3D PEFT method that explicitly captures local point cloud features without adding inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds (xyz coordinates, optionally with RGB) organized into patches, where each patch's center point and its KNN geometric neighbors define local neighborhoods used by the K-Rectify mechanism. Outputs are task-dependent: class labels for object/scene classification, per-point part masks for ShapeNetPart, or per-point semantic labels for S3DIS scene segmentation. During inference, the sparse Point Monarch matrices merge into the frozen pretrained weights, so the forward pass is architecturally identical to the original backbone with zero additional latency.

## Method

MoST reparameterizes dense update weight matrices ΔW as Point Monarch (KLRK), a structured matrix defined as K·P·L·P^T·R·K, where L and R are block-diagonal sparse matrices (b blocks, typically b≈√d) and K is a KNN-wise local rectification operator that interpolates new center features via inverse distance weighting (IDW) over geometric neighbors and adds them back with a learnable residual λ. This design smooths local geometric features while preserving the block-diagonal Monarch structure for efficient batch matrix multiplication, reducing parameters from d² to 2d²/b. During training only the Point Monarch parameters and task header are updated; at inference the matrices merge into pretrained weights for zero overhead. A parameter-free multi-layer feature fusion strategy (weighted sum of MixPooled features from selected backbone layers) further improves backbone-to-header alignment without additional learnable parameters.

## Results

MoST achieves state-of-the-art 3D PEFT results across five backbones. With PointGPT and b=8, MoST reaches 97.50% accuracy on ScanObjectNN PB_50_RS and 96.23% on ModelNet40, surpassing full fine-tuning (93.4%/94.7%) by 4.1% and 1.5% respectively while tuning only 8.0M (2.2%) parameters. Even with the most compact setting (b=32, 0.8M params / 3.6%), MoST on Point-MAE achieves 91.95% on PB_50_RS, exceeding full fine-tuning (85.18%) by 6.77%. On S3DIS scene segmentation, MoST attains 58.9% mIoU, outperforming the best 3D PEFT baseline PointGST (57.9%) and approaching full fine-tuning (60.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoST consistently outperforms all additive 3D PEFT baselines (IDPT, DAPT, PPT, PointGST) across every backbone and dataset. For example, on Point-MAE/PB_50_RS, MoST (b=8) achieves 92.92% versus 89.3% (PointGST), 89.00% (PPT), 88.34% (IDPT), and 88.27% (DAPT). Against reparameterization-based baselines, MoST dominates: LoRA scores only 82.76% on Point-MAE/PB_50_RS while MoST (b=32) reaches 91.95%, a 9.19-point gap. MoST also surpasses full fine-tuning in most settings—e.g., 92.92% vs. 85.18% on Point-MAE/PB_50_RS and 97.50% vs. 93.4% on PointGPT/PB_50_RS—while tuning only 3.6%–2.2% of parameters. On S3DIS scene segmentation, MoST (58.9% mIoU) beats PointGST (57.9%), DAPT (56.3%), and PPT (54.8%), though it remains 1.9 points below full fine-tuning (60.8%). On ShapeNetPart segmentation, MoST achieves 84.42% mIoU_C, edging out PointGST (83.98%) and PPT (84.23%) while matching full fine-tuning (84.52%). The key differentiator is that MoST is the only method combining 3D-specific design, zero inference overhead, and cross-backbone generalizability (Transformer, Mamba, hierarchical U-Net).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoST extends the Monarch structured matrix family (Dao et al., ICML 2022) to 3D point clouds by introducing K-Rectify, a KNN-based local feature smoothing operation that addresses the key weakness of existing reparameterization methods like LoRA: their inability to capture local geometric structure in irregular point clouds. While prior 3D PEFT work (IDPT, DAPT, PPT, PointGST) relies on additive adapters or prompts designed specifically for Transformers, MoST departs from this paradigm by reparameterizing dense weight matrices directly, inheriting LoRA's zero-inference-overhead property while adding point-cloud-specific local awareness. The multi-layer feature fusion strategy is a lightweight, parameter-free addition that complements the structured matrix approach without introducing new learnable components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3D representation learning increasingly relies on large pretrained backbones (PointGPT at 360M, ReCon++ at 657M), the need for efficient fine-tuning that generalizes across architectures (Transformer, Mamba, hierarchical) becomes critical. MoST's reparameterization approach avoids the inference overhead and architecture lock-in of adapter/prompt methods, making it a practical default for 3D PEFT. Open questions include whether the K-Rectify local smoothing principle transfers to other structured data (e.g., meshes, graphs), whether the method scales to even larger 3D foundation models, and whether the block-diagonal structure can be further optimized for hardware-specific sparsity patterns.
