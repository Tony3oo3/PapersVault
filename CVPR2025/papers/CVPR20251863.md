## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting models contain millions of Gaussians, creating prohibitive storage and memory demands for resource-constrained devices. Existing post-hoc pruning methods rely on heuristic significance scores that degrade visual fidelity and lose foreground details at high compression ratios. This paper asks whether a mathematically principled sensitivity measure can guide pruning to preserve quality at substantially higher compression ratios (90%) than prior heuristics-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251863/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a fully converged pretrained 3D-GS model (N Gaussians each with position, scaling, rotation, color, spherical harmonics, and opacity) and the training camera poses — notably, the Fisher computation depends only on poses, not on the ground-truth training images. It outputs a pruned 3D-GS model with 90% fewer Gaussians (e.g., 2.65M → 0.265M) that renders novel views at 3.56× the original speed. Pruning scores are computed over 4×4 image patches rendered at reduced resolution, and the entire score computation across all training views takes seconds on a single GPU.

## Method

PUP 3D-GS derives a per-Gaussian sensitivity pruning score from the Fisher Information Matrix, which approximates the Hessian of the L2 reconstruction error on a converged model (where the residual term vanishes). The full Hessian is intractable, so they restrict to the block-diagonal (per-Gaussian) sub-matrices and further limit parameters to only the spatial mean and scaling (yielding 6×6 matrices), capturing the projective geometric invariances that make Gaussians interchangeable in pixel space. The score is the log-determinant of each block, measuring the relative volume of the error basin around each Gaussian's parameters. A multi-round prune-refine pipeline (prune 80%, fine-tune 5K steps, prune 50%, fine-tune 5K steps) outperforms equivalent single-round pruning across all quality and speed metrics, and the approach is fully post-hoc — requiring no modification to the original 3D-GS training pipeline.

## Results

After pruning 90% of Gaussians via a two-round prune-refine pipeline (80% then 50%), PUP 3D-GS achieves a 3.56× average rendering speed increase over the unpruned 3D-GS baseline while retaining high image quality. On Mip-NeRF 360, the pruned model reaches 26.67 PSNR, 0.7862 SSIM, and 0.2719 LPIPS at 204.81 FPS (vs. 27.47 PSNR at 64.07 FPS for the full model). On Deep Blending, it achieves 28.85 PSNR and 0.8810 SSIM at 301.43 FPS, and on Tanks & Temples it reaches 22.72 PSNR at 391.10 FPS. Final model sizes are reduced by ~10× (e.g., 74.65 MB vs. 746.46 MB on Mip-NeRF 360).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unpruned 3D-GS baseline, PUP 3D-GS trades a small quality drop (e.g., −0.80 PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360, −0.13 PSNR on Deep Blending) for a 3.56× average FPS increase and ~10× size reduction, which is the expected trade-off at 90% pruning. Against LightGaussian (the primary competing pruning method), PUP 3D-GS wins on SSIM, LPIPS, and FPS across all three datasets. The sole exception is PSNR on Tanks & Temples, where LightGaussian leads by 0.36 points (23.08 vs. 22.72), while PUP 3D-GS still wins on SSIM (+0.0063), LPIPS (−0.0193), and FPS (+62.07). On Mip-NeRF 360, PUP 3D-GS outperforms LightGaussian by +0.39 PSNR, +0.024 SSIM, −0.034 LPIPS, and +42.69 FPS. On Deep Blending, the margins are +0.34 PSNR, +0.0135 SSIM, −0.0277 LPIPS, and +67.33 FPS. The authors attribute LightGaussian's relative weakness to its visibility-based heuristic being biased toward retaining larger Gaussians, whereas the Fisher-based sensitivity score preferentially preserves smaller, detail-carrying Gaussians.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) representation and the prune-refine framework introduced by LightGaussian, but replaces LightGaussian's heuristic global significance score with a mathematically principled sensitivity score derived from the Fisher Information Matrix (a second-order approximation of the reconstruction error Hessian). Conceptually, it bridges the uncertainty-estimation literature for radiance fields (BayesRays, FisherRF) with model compression, repurposing Fisher information not for active view selection or uncertainty visualization but as a per-Gaussian pruning criterion. The key departure from prior pruning methods (Compact-3DGS, EAGLES, LightGaussian) is the restriction to spatial mean and scaling parameters only, motivated by the projective geometric invariances inherent in multi-view reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 90% Gaussian reduction while maintaining or exceeding LightGaussian's image quality at a significantly higher compression ratio is practically important for deploying 3D-GS on resource-constrained devices. The multi-round prune-refine strategy and the finding that spatial parameters alone suffice (while adding rotation or switching to RGB actually degrades quality) provide actionable design guidance for future compression pipelines. Open questions include whether the Fisher approximation remains valid for non-converged or partially trained models, whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes or larger-scale environments, and whether the N×36 memory overhead becomes prohibitive at extreme scales beyond the tested scenes.
