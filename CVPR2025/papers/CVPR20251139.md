## Problem

Existing video customization methods can only personalize a single concept—either subject identity or motion pattern—leaving joint multi-subject and interactive motion customization unsolved. Motion LoRAs trained with standard diffusion losses suffer from severe appearance leakage, entangling subject appearance with motion dynamics, and no prior work provides a mechanism to compose multiple customized subjects with a desired interactive motion pattern.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251139/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N subject identities (3–5 RGB images per subject, e.g., animals, robots, toys), a single reference video depicting the desired interactive motion, and a free-form text prompt containing special tokens (e.g., "A <toy> is riding a <dog>"). It produces a 24-frame video at 8 fps and 320×576 resolution using a 50-step DDIM sampler with guidance scale 9.0 on the ZeroScope base model. The text prompt's special tokens are replaced by superclass names in the motion branch to decouple identity from motion conditioning.

## Method

VideoMage learns a subject LoRA on spatial UNet layers (with auxiliary video regularization to preserve motion prior) and a motion LoRA on temporal layers using a novel appearance-agnostic objective that applies negative classifier-free guidance conditioned on subject appearance tokens, effectively erasing visual identity from the learned motion. Multiple subject LoRAs are merged into a single fused LoRA via gradient-based noise matching plus a CutMix-style spatial attention regularization that enforces correct subject-region attention. At inference, a Spatial-Temporal Collaborative Sampling (SCS) scheme runs subject and motion branches in parallel, mutually aligning their spatial cross-attention maps (for correct subject placement) and temporal self-attention maps (for correct motion) through gradient updates on the input latents over the first τ=15 denoising steps, then combines the two noise predictions with equal weights.

## Results

On the multi-subject and motion customization benchmark (72 subject-motion-background combinations, 10 videos each), VideoMage achieves the best CLIP-T (0.662), CLIP-I (0.670), and DINO-I (0.407) scores, outperforming MotionDirector by 5.7% on CLIP-I and 10% on DINO-I. Temporal Consistency is comparable to MotionDirector (0.983 vs. 0.987). In a human preference study with 25 participants evaluating 360 videos, VideoMage was preferred across all four criteria: Text Alignment, Subject Fidelity, Motion Fidelity, and Video Quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoMage is compared against two baselines: DreamVideo [44], which uses separate ID and motion adapters for single-subject customization, and MotionDirector [51], which fine-tunes a LoRA on temporal attention layers for motion transfer. VideoMage outperforms MotionDirector on all identity and text-alignment metrics (CLIP-I: 0.670 vs. 0.634; DINO-I: 0.407 vs. 0.370; CLIP-T: 0.662 vs. 0.656) while being marginally lower on Temporal Consistency (0.983 vs. 0.987). Against DreamVideo, VideoMage leads substantially on every metric, with the largest gap on DINO-I (0.407 vs. 0.197, more than 2×). Qualitatively, both baselines suffer from appearance leakage and attribute mixing when handling multiple subjects, whereas VideoMage preserves subject identities and correctly arranges interactions. The human preference study confirms VideoMage is preferred over both baselines across all four evaluation criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoMage sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of video customization research: subject-identity personalization (AnimateDiff, VideoBooth, CustomVideo) and motion-pattern transfer (Customize-A-Video, MotionDirector, DreamVideo). It builds directly on LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning, gradient-based LoRA fusion from Mix-of-Show, and attention-map alignment from Attend-and-Excite, but departs from prior work by introducing negative classifier-free guidance (inspired by concept-erasing methods) to learn appearance-agnostic motion, and by proposing a spatial-temporal collaborative sampling scheme that jointly aligns subject and motion branches at test time. The key conceptual departure is treating multi-subject and motion customization as a unified problem rather than composing single-concept modules post hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly customize multiple subject identities and their interactive motion in a single video, addressing a gap the authors explicitly identify as unsolved. The appearance-agnostic motion learning via negative CFG is a practical recipe for preventing the appearance leakage that plagues prior motion-transfer methods. However, evaluation is limited to 6 motion videos, 24-frame clips at 320×576, and only two baselines, leaving open questions about scalability to longer videos, more subjects, and more complex multi-agent interactions. The reliance on a single base model (ZeroScope) and the need for per-subject LoRA training (300 iterations each) also constrain practical deployment.
