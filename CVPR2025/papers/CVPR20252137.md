## Problem

Existing uncertainty-aware semi-supervised segmentation methods discard high-uncertainty voxels through thresholding or dual-decoder disagreement, losing global imaging context needed for scattered or irregular lesions. Meanwhile, contrastive learning approaches in medical segmentation treat all sample pairs equally, failing to prioritize hard-to-distinguish pairs under the extreme class imbalance typical of lesion voxels versus background. No prior method dynamically integrates uncertainty awareness into both global consistency regularization and local contrastive feature learning simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D MRI volumes (DWI for ISLES'22, T2-FLAIR for BraTS'19) or 3D CT volumes (Pancreas-NIH) as two augmented views per sample, cropped into patches of 96×96×64 (ISLES), 96×96×96 (BraTS, Pancreas), or 112×112×80 (LA). It outputs a binary voxel-wise segmentation mask from the student network. An ASPP-based projection head extracts multi-scale patch embeddings (256-dim) from the student encoder for the contrastive loss, while the teacher provides inter-network hard negatives via its own feature space.

## Method

DyCON augments a Mean-Teacher 3D UNet framework with two complementary losses. UnCL computes a voxel-wise consistency loss (MSE between teacher and student predictions) inversely weighted by the sum of exponentiated entropies from both networks, so high-uncertainty voxels receive lower penalty early in training; an adaptive β(t) = max(β_min, β_max·exp(−λ·t/T)) decays over epochs to shift focus from exploration to refinement, and an entropy regularization term prevents premature confidence collapse. FeCL reformulates supervised contrastive learning over k×k×k patch embeddings with three mechanisms: dual focal weights that up-weight hard positives (low-similarity same-class pairs via (1−S)^γ) and hard negatives (high-similarity different-class pairs via S^γ), a Gambling Softmax entropy term that further amplifies uncertain positive pairs, and inter-network top-k hard negative mining that selects the K most similar teacher patches of the opposite class to sharpen the denominator. The total loss combines supervised Dice + CE on labeled data with η·(UnCL + FeCL) on unlabeled data, trained end-to-end for 6000 iterations.

## Results

On ISLES'22 with 10% labeled data, DyCON achieves 65.71% Dice, 51.09% IoU, 13.35 HD95, and 0.71 ASD, outperforming the next-best method GALoss (60.13% Dice) by over 5.5 Dice points. On BraTS'19 with 10% labels, DyCON reaches 87.05% Dice and 7.41 HD95, surpassing CML (85.26% Dice) and AC-MT (83.77% Dice). On the LA dataset with only 5% labels, DyCON attains 90.96% Dice (3D-UNet) and 91.18% Dice (VNet), approaching the fully supervised 3D-UNet baseline of 91.51%. On Pancreas-CT with 20% labels, DyCON achieves 84.81% Dice, exceeding BCP (82.91%) and DTC (78.27%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyCON consistently outperforms all 15 compared baselines across all four datasets and all label proportions, with no losses reported. On ISLES'22 (the most challenging dataset with scattered stroke lesions), DyCON leads the nearest competitor GALoss by 5.6 Dice points at 10% labels (65.71% vs. 60.13%) and by 8.8 points at 5% labels (61.48% vs. 53.53% BCP). On BraTS'19, DyCON surpasses CML by 1.79 Dice points at 10% labels (87.05% vs. 85.26%) and by 2.12 points at 20% labels (88.75% vs. 86.63%), while also achieving substantially lower HD95 (7.41 vs. 9.08). On the LA dataset, DyCON with 3D-UNet backbone reaches 92.77% Dice at 10% labels, exceeding CML (90.36%) and AC-MT (90.31%), and with VNet backbone achieves 91.18% at 5% labels, surpassing AC-MT (89.12%). On Pancreas-CT, DyCON leads BCP by 1.9 Dice points (84.81% vs. 82.91%) and DTC by 6.54 points (84.81% vs. 78.27%). The supervised baselines (W-Net, PAMSNet, 3D-UNet, V-Net) remain above DyCON as expected, but DyCON with only 5–20% labels closes much of the gap, e.g., reaching 69.11% Dice on ISLES'22 with 20% labels versus 87.37% for fully supervised PAMSNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyCON builds on the Mean Teacher (MT) consistency framework and supervised contrastive learning (SupCon), but departs from prior uncertainty-aware SSL methods that filter or discard high-uncertainty voxels via MC dropout, deep ensembles, or dual-decoder disagreement. Instead, UnCL preserves all voxels by dynamically reweighting their contribution through an entropy-scaled denominator with an adaptive β schedule, shifting from exploration of uncertain regions to refinement of confident ones. On the contrastive side, FeCL extends SupCon with dual focal weighting (emphasizing hard positives/negatives) and Gambling Softmax entropy to focus on ambiguous boundaries, addressing class imbalance in a way prior contrastive SSL methods (which treat all pairs equally) do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (compatible with MT, CT, and other SSL pipelines) and consistently strong gains across four diverse 3D datasets (stroke, tumor, atrium, pancreas) make DyCON a practical tool for reducing annotation burden in clinical segmentation. However, the method is validated only for binary segmentation (C=2) with 3D UNet/VNet backbones, leaving open whether the entropy-weighted consistency and focal contrastive mechanisms transfer to multi-class or transformer-based architectures. The heuristic exponential decay of β and the fixed patch size k=16 across all datasets suggest room for more principled scheduling and adaptive patch selection. A key open question is whether the global (UnCL) and local (FeCL) losses can be further decoupled or made dataset-agnostic without per-dataset hyperparameter tuning.
