## Problem

Current video captioning methods for text-to-video generation suffer from a fundamental trade-off: short captions lack sufficient detail, dense captions introduce hallucinations and redundancy, and coarse structured captions (e.g., MiraData) still fail to capture instance-level fidelity. No existing approach achieves instance-level, fine-grained video descriptions that simultaneously preserve high caption-video fidelity and minimize hallucinated or irrelevant content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250721/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For captioning, the method takes a video clip (uniformly sampled frames with temporal metadata) and produces a structured caption organized into three blocks: a global description with background and camera movement, followed by per-instance entries each specifying class, appearance, actions/motion, and position. At inference, InstanceEnhancer accepts a short user prompt and expands it into the same structured format via a two-stage pipeline (prompt expansion, then instance-level segmentation and enhancement), ensuring the inference-time caption distribution matches the training distribution used to fine-tune the T2V model.

## Method

InstanceCap decomposes the captioning problem into two stages. First, an Auxiliary Models Cluster (AMC) comprising object detection, SAM2 video instance segmentation, and blur-background masking isolates each instance from the global video, preventing MLLMs from conflating attributes across objects. Second, an improved Chain-of-Thought pipeline feeds each isolated instance (with blurred surroundings) plus the global description into an MLLM, guided by human-designed class-specific hints for ~80 categories and a curated positive/negative lexicon, producing concise structured phrases rather than dense free-form text. Camera movement is annotated by combining OpenSora's basic motion labels with MLLM-inferred subtle attributes (intensity, speed). The resulting captions form the 22K-sample InstanceVid dataset, used to fine-tune Open-Sora, while InstanceEnhancer bridges the gap between short user prompts and the structured training format at inference time.

## Results

On the Inseval benchmark, Open-Sora finetuned with InstanceCap + InstanceEnhancer achieves 45.00% average instance-level accuracy, outperforming the base Open-Sora (28.63%), CogVideoX-5B (43.00%), and Pyramid-Flow-2B (28.38%). On reconstruction-via-recaption (Table 1), InstanceCap attains the best 3DVAE score (143.00, lower is better) and VQAScore (0.916) among all captioning methods, with a moderate caption length of 165 words. On EvalCrafter, InstanceCap improves Action Score to 72.04 and Flow Score to 0.68 over the base Open-Sora (67.87 and 0.65 respectively), while maintaining competitive CLIP and SD scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstanceCap + InstanceEnhancer (45.00% avg) surpasses all captioning-method baselines finetuned on Open-Sora: Panda-captioner (27.00%), ShareGPT4Video (24.63%), MiraData (30.63%), and LLaVA (29.50%), with the largest margin over ShareGPT4Video (+20.37 pp). Against larger T2V models, InstanceCap outperforms Pyramid-Flow-2B (28.38%) and Open-Sora Plan (33.38%) by 16.62 and 11.62 pp respectively, and edges out CogVideoX-5B (43.00%) by 2 pp while using a 1.1B-parameter base model versus CogVideoX's 5B. On the reconstruction-via-recaption task (Table 1), InstanceCap achieves the lowest 3DVAE score (143.00 vs. MiraData's 144.75 and ShareGPT4Video's 147.00) and highest VQAScore (0.916 vs. 0.905 and 0.866), confirming superior caption-video fidelity. On EvalCrafter (Table 3), InstanceCap leads in Action Score (72.04 vs. 68.55 for ShareGPT4Video) and Flow Score (0.68 vs. 0.67 for Panda), though it trades a small amount of temporal consistency (56.57 vs. 60.67 for ShareGPT4Video). The ablation in Table 1 further shows that removing camera hints or human-designed class hints degrades 3DVAE by 1.38 and 0.87 points respectively, validating both components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video captioning and T2V training data construction. Prior work falls into three camps—short captions (Panda-70M), dense captions (ShareGPT4Video), and coarse structured captions (MiraData)—each with known failure modes (low fidelity, hallucination, or redundancy). InstanceCap departs from all three by introducing an instance-level structured format that decomposes video into individual objects with class, appearance, motion, and position fields, leveraging an auxiliary models cluster (SAM2 segmentation, object detection, camera motion estimation) to isolate instances before MLLM annotation. The approach repurposes existing components (CoT prompting, SAM2, OpenSora camera labels) into a novel pipeline architecture rather than building on a single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental data-quality bottleneck in T2V training: caption-video misalignment that degrades generation fidelity. The 22K InstanceVid dataset and Inseval benchmark provide practical resources for the community, and the finding that instance-level structured captions outperform both shorter and denser alternatives offers a clear design principle for future T2V data pipelines. However, the 22K scale limits pre-training applicability, the method's effectiveness depends on upstream detection quality (requiring domain-specific fine-tuning), and benefits diminish in instance-free scenes—leaving open the question of how to scale this approach to diverse, large-scale video corpora.
