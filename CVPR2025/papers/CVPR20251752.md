## Problem

Existing zero-shot metric depth foundation models (e.g., Metric3Dv2, UniDepth) are trained exclusively on perspective images and suffer severe performance degradation on large field-of-view cameras such as fisheye and 360° sensors. No prior framework enables a single model trained on standard perspective datasets to generalize zero-shot to arbitrary camera FoVs without requiring dedicated large-FoV training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251752/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image from any camera type (perspective, fisheye, or 360°/ERP) along with its camera intrinsic parameters (focal length, distortion coefficients, and pitch angle), and produces a metric depth map expressed as Euclidean distance from the camera center rather than Z-buffer depth, which is necessary for compatibility with spherical projections. All inputs are converted into a canonical ERP space of height 1400 px; testing resolutions are aligned to the training patch size (500×700) while preserving aspect ratio, with evaluation at 512×1024 for 360° datasets, 500×750 for ScanNet++, and 700×700 for KITTI360.

## Method

DAC unifies all camera types into a shared Equi-Rectangular Projection (ERP) space and trains exclusively on perspective images converted into ERP patches. The core mechanism is a pitch-aware Image-to-ERP conversion that uses grid sampling combined with gnomonic projection geometry to map each ERP grid point back to the input image, where the tangent-plane latitude is set by the camera's pitch angle—projecting perspective data into high-distortion ERP regions that simulate observations unique to large-FoV cameras. A FoV alignment step applies a per-image scaling augmentation so that all training samples, regardless of their native FoV, fill a single predefined ERP patch size (500×700), maximizing content while minimizing background padding. Multi-resolution training feeds the network three resolutions (1.0×, 0.7×, 0.4×) per batch to build scale-equivariant features. The framework is architecture-agnostic (tested with iDisc + ResNet101, Swin-L, DINOv2) and trained with SIlog loss on 670K indoor or 130K outdoor perspective images, achieving up to 50% improvement in δ₁ over prior metric depth models on fisheye and 360° test sets.

## Results

On indoor 360° datasets, DAC (ResNet101, 670K training images) achieves δ1 of 0.7727 on Matterport3D and 0.8115 on Pano3D-GV2, improving over the next-best method (iDisc with Metric3Dv2 pipeline) by ~46% and ~44% respectively. On the 150° fisheye ScanNet++ dataset, DAC reaches δ1 of 0.8517 and AbsRel of 0.1323, outperforming Metric3Dv2 (DINOv2, 16M images) by 31% in δ1. On the outdoor 180° fisheye KITTI360 dataset, gains are more marginal (δ1 0.7858 vs. 0.7833 for iDisc), attributed to limited pitch variance in outdoor training data and LiDAR concentration in less-distorted regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAC substantially outperforms all three baselines on indoor large-FoV datasets. Against Metric3Dv2 (DINOv2, 16M images), DAC (ResNet101, 670K images) improves δ1 by 79% on Matterport3D (0.7727 vs. 0.4381) and 101% on Pano3D-GV2 (0.8115 vs. 0.4040), despite using a much smaller backbone and 24× less training data. Against UniDepth (ViT-L, 3M images), the gap is even wider, with DAC achieving 3× higher δ1 on both 360° datasets. Against iDisc trained with the Metric3Dv2 pipeline (same ResNet101 backbone, same 670K data), DAC improves δ1 by 46% on Matterport3D, 44% on Pano3D-GV2, and 39% on ScanNet++. On the outdoor KITTI360 dataset, DAC's advantage narrows to a marginal 0.3% δ1 improvement over iDisc (0.7858 vs. 0.7833), which the authors attribute to limited pitch variance in outdoor training data and LiDAR ground-truth concentration in less-distorted ERP regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot metric depth estimation line (Metric3Dv2, UniDepth) by addressing a critical gap: prior methods handle only perspective cameras and degrade sharply on fisheye and 360° inputs. Rather than learning a network-based camera conversion (as UniDepth does), DAC adopts a geometry-based pipeline that converts all images into a canonical Equi-Rectangular Projection (ERP) space using gnomonic projection, then applies pitch-aware augmentation to simulate high-distortion regions unique to large-FoV cameras. The depth scaling operations from Metric3D are retained and integrated into the ERP framework, while the network backbone (iDisc) is treated as interchangeable—the contribution lies in the training pipeline (pitch-aware ERP conversion, FoV alignment, multi-resolution training) rather than the architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAC's practical impact is significant: it ensures that all previously collected 3D data (overwhelmingly from perspective cameras) remains valuable for downstream applications involving large-FoV sensors, which are common in autonomous driving, AR/VR, and robotics. The 50% δ1 improvement on indoor datasets demonstrates that geometry-based simulation of large-FoV observations can substitute for domain-specific training data, challenging the assumption that large-FoV depth estimation requires large-FoV supervision. However, outdoor gains are marginal (limited pitch variance in training data), and the approach's dependence on accurate camera intrinsics and pitch estimation remains a practical constraint. Open questions include whether the ERP-based framework extends to even more exotic projections (e.g., cylindrical, stereographic) and whether the multi-resolution strategy is sufficient for extreme resolution disparities on edge devices.
