## Problem

LLMs have not been effectively leveraged for generating parametric CAD construction sequences because these models lack exposure to CAD data during pretraining and have no inherent understanding of 3D geometry. Prior encoder-decoder approaches for text-to-CAD suffer from weak generalization and cannot produce complex models from detailed text instructions, while earlier LLM-based attempts only generate simple CAD primitives.

## Input / Output

The method accepts text descriptions (ranging from abstract one-liners to detailed multi-component specifications), raw CAD code paired with natural-language editing instructions, or partial SPCC sequences for completion. Outputs are parametric CAD models expressed as Structured Parametric CAD Code (SPCC): a Python-like format where sketches are lists of loops calling Line/Arc/Circle methods, coordinates are 8-bit quantized and recentered to origin, and angles are discrete 0–360° values. Each SPCC document is prefixed with hierarchical annotations (global abstract + detailed descriptions, then per-component short names and descriptions) that encode both shape semantics and spatial assembly relationships.

## Method

CAD-Llama adapts LLaMA3-8B through two stages: (1) SPCC-adaptive pretraining, where the SPCC corpus is organized into contexts by grouping visually similar CAD models (via CLIP image embeddings and cosine similarity) so the model learns fine-grained differences between near-identical designs, and (2) LoRA-based instruction tuning on a 48K-entry multitask dataset covering text-to-CAD, captioning, completion, addition, and deletion. The key architectural insight is a two-stage hierarchical annotation pipeline using GPT-4o: first, per-component 3D renderings and 2D sketches are described in detail; second, outline-highlighted images plus component descriptions are used to generate global spatial-relationship descriptions and short component names. This hierarchical text is embedded directly into the code-like SPCC representation, exploiting LLMs' strong code-generation priors while grounding them with structured 3D semantic annotations.

## Results

On the primary text-to-CAD task (DeepCAD test set), CAD-Llama-INS achieves 84.72% overall accuracy (ACC_T), 10.53 MCD, 1.54 MMD, and 3.59 JSD, surpassing the best transformer-based baseline CAD-Translator (70.36% ACC_T) by ~14 percentage points and GPT-4 (20.03% ACC_T) by ~65 points. For unconditional generation, CAD-Llama attains a 99.90% success ratio (vs. 79.11% for HNC-CAD) with the best MMD (0.96) and JSD (0.66). Across five CAD-related downstream tasks (captioning, completion, addition, deletion), CAD-Llama-INS achieves a 63.58% average score, outperforming GPT-4 (47.88%) by 15.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD-Llama-INS decisively outperforms all general-purpose LLM baselines (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, LLaMA3-8B, Mistral-7B) on text-to-CAD by 64–72 percentage points in ACC_T, indicating that zero-shot or few-shot prompting of off-the-shelf LLMs is insufficient for parametric CAD generation. Against the two dedicated transformer-based text-to-CAD methods (CAD-Translator at 70.36% and Text2CAD at 69.91% ACC_T), CAD-Llama-INS gains ~14–15 points in accuracy and roughly halves the geometric error metrics (MCD 10.53 vs. ~21, JSD 3.59 vs. ~10). On unconditional generation, CAD-Llama matches HNC-CAD on coverage (79.93 vs. 80.46) but substantially exceeds it in success ratio (99.90% vs. 79.11%) and distributional fidelity (JSD 0.66 vs. 0.74); it slightly trails SkexGen on novelty (97.1 vs. 99.1) but dominates on all other metrics. On the multi-task CAD editing benchmark, CAD-Llama-INS wins on captioning, completion, and addition tasks, but GPT-4 retains an edge on the Deletion* task (90.41% EM vs. 39.81%), where GPT-4's natural-language reasoning for identifying removable components proves advantageous.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-CAD generation line (Text2CAD, CAD Translator) by replacing their encoder-decoder transformers with a domain-adapted LLM backbone, and builds on early LLM-for-CAD attempts (CAD-LLM, LLM4CAD, OpenECAD) that demonstrated basic feasibility but produced only simple models. The key conceptual departure is treating parametric CAD sequences as code and leveraging the hierarchical annotation pipeline to inject rich 3D geometric semantics into the training corpus, mirroring how code comments and docstrings make LLM code generation effective. The adaptive pretraining strategy (grouping similar CAD models via CLIP embeddings, inspired by in-context pretraining) further distinguishes it from standard LLM fine-tuning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a fine-tuned open-source LLM can generate complex, multi-component parametric CAD models from both abstract and detailed text instructions, with a 99.9% success ratio that far exceeds prior autoregressive methods. The SPCC format and hierarchical annotation pipeline offer a reusable recipe for bridging the semantic gap between natural language and parametric design data, potentially applicable beyond sketch-extrude CAD. Open questions include generalization to richer CAD operations (boolean, fillet, sweep), multi-modal conditioning (image, point cloud), and whether the approach scales to industrial CAD complexity with hundreds of components. The evaluation is confined to the DeepCAD dataset and a single 8B backbone, leaving scalability and robustness untested.
