## Problem

Multi-modal trajectory planning methods in end-to-end autonomous driving (e.g., VADv2, SparseDrive) are one-shot and rely solely on the current perception frame, making them vulnerable to occlusions and producing temporally inconsistent trajectories that cause vehicle trembling and oscillations. Existing approaches address temporal instance characteristics but entirely overlook temporal planning consistency across consecutive frames, leaving a gap in stable, coherent long-horizon trajectory generation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surround-view camera images (360° horizontal FOV) at 2 Hz, encoded via a ResNet-50 backbone into sparse instance features for road agents and map elements. It outputs K=18 multi-modal ego trajectories (6 proposals × 3 commands: left/right/straight) with waypoints at 0.5 s intervals over a 3 s or 6 s horizon, alongside 3D detection, tracking, online mapping, and motion prediction. A novel Trajectory Prediction Consistency (TPC) metric is introduced to quantify frame-to-frame trajectory stability, complementing standard L2 and collision-rate metrics.

## Method

MomAD introduces two momentum-aware planning modules on top of SparseDrive's sparse perception backbone. Topological Trajectory Matching (TTM) transforms current and historical trajectories into a common ego coordinate frame and selects the candidate trajectory minimizing the Hausdorff Distance to the prior frame's path, enforcing temporal coherence without gradient-based selection. The Momentum Planning Interactor (MPI) then cross-attends the selected planning query against LSTM-processed historical planning queries (gated by sigmoid-weighted scores) to inject long-horizon ego-agent and ego-map context, after which a planning head regenerates refined multi-modal trajectories. A Robust Instance Denoising module applies controlled Gaussian noise (σ=0.1) to instance features during training through a lightweight encoder-decoder transformer, teaching the planner to ignore perception fluctuations and misdetections at inference time.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set (VAD protocol), MomAD achieves an average L2 error of 0.60 m, a collision rate of 0.09%, and a TPC of 0.54 m, outperforming SparseDrive (0.61 m / 0.08% / 0.57 m) and VAD (0.72 m / 0.21% / 0.64 m). On the curated Turning-nuScenes set, MomAD reduces the 6-second collision rate by 26% (2.31% vs. 2.57%) and TPC by 0.97 m (32.45%) relative to SparseDrive. In closed-loop Bench2Drive evaluation, MomAD improves the success rate by 16.3% over VAD's multi-modal variant and by 8.4% over SparseDrive, while also boosting the comfort score by 7.2% and 5.3% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SparseDrive (the primary SOTA baseline), MomAD achieves a lower average L2 (0.60 m vs. 0.61 m) and TPC (0.54 m vs. 0.57 m) on nuScenes, though SparseDrive retains a marginal edge in collision rate (0.08% vs. 0.09%). The gap widens substantially on the Turning-nuScenes set, where MomAD reduces L2 by 0.10 m, collision rate by 0.08%, and TPC by 0.16 m over SparseDrive, and at the 6-second horizon the TPC advantage grows to 0.97 m (32.45%). Against VAD, MomAD dominates across all three planning metrics (L2 0.60 vs. 0.72 m, Col. 0.09% vs. 0.21%, TPC 0.54 vs. 0.64 m), and similarly outperforms UniAD (L2 0.60 vs. 0.73 m, Col. 0.09% vs. 0.61%, TPC 0.54 vs. 0.68 m). In closed-loop Bench2Drive, MomAD surpasses SparseDrive by 1.4 percentage points in success rate (18.11% vs. 16.71%) and by 2.57 points in comfort score, while improving over VAD's multi-modal variant by 2.2 percentage points in success rate. The only metric where MomAD does not lead is raw FPS (7.8 vs. 9.0 for SparseDrive), reflecting the additional cost of the momentum planning modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

MomAD builds directly on SparseDrive's sparse scene representation for end-to-end driving, extending its multi-modal trajectory planning with a temporal momentum mechanism. It departs from the deterministic planning paradigm of UniAD and VAD, and from the one-shot multi-modal selection of VADv2 and SparseDrive, by explicitly incorporating historical trajectory and perception context through Hausdorff-distance-based trajectory matching (TTM) and cross-attention with prior planning queries (MPI). The conceptual framing borrows the physics notion of momentum to formalize what prior methods addressed only implicitly through temporal instance features, shifting the focus from temporal perception consistency to temporal *planning* consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the TPC metric and the Turning-nuScenes benchmark addresses a genuine gap: mainstream planning evaluations on nuScenes mask temporal instability because most scenes involve straight driving, and no standard metric quantifies frame-to-frame trajectory coherence. The 26% collision reduction and 33% TPC improvement on turning scenarios suggest that temporal planning consistency is a meaningful and previously under-addressed failure mode. However, the Turning-nuScenes set is small (680 samples, 17 scenes), the FPS is slightly lower than SparseDrive, and the authors acknowledge that teacher-forcing induces mode collapse limiting trajectory diversity—open questions include whether momentum-aware planning scales to longer horizons (>6s) and whether diffusion-based or speculative-decoding approaches can recover the lost diversity while preserving temporal coherence.
