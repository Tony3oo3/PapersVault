## Problem

In federated semi-supervised learning, data heterogeneity causes local models to become overconfident while global models grow conservative, widening the confidence gap between their pseudo-labels and degrading both accuracy and convergence. Existing FSSL methods rely on hard pseudo-labels from either local or global models without accounting for this discrepancy, so incorrect labels propagate aggressively under non-IID conditions. The paper asks whether the confidence discrepancy itself can be exploited to flexibly correct pseudo-labels and restore consensus between local and global models.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a small labeled set (10% or 20% per class) and a large unlabeled set of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 32×32 for SVHN, 32×32 for CINIC-10), both drawn from a Dirichlet(α) distribution to simulate inter- and intra-client imbalance. Both the local and global models produce class probability vectors on weakly augmented unlabeled samples; the final pseudo-label is a soft one-hot interpolation rather than a single hard class, and the unsupervised loss is a KL divergence between the local model's strongly augmented prediction and this corrected soft label. The output is a standard class label over the dataset's class set.

## Method

SAGE introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of a FixMatch-style weak/strong augmentation pipeline with ResNet-8 and FedAvg aggregation. First, Collaborative Pseudo-Label Generation (CPG) assigns a pseudo-label from the local model when its max confidence exceeds a threshold τ, and falls back to the global model's prediction otherwise, thereby compensating for the scarcity of pseudo-labels in local minority classes. Second, Confidence-Driven Soft Correction (CDSC) computes the absolute confidence difference ΔC between local and global predictions, maps it to a dynamic coefficient λ = exp(−κ·ΔC), and linearly interpolates the one-hot local and global pseudo-labels as ỹ = λ·δ_l + (1−λ)·δ_g, so that larger discrepancies shift weight toward the more conservative global model. The corrected soft label is then used in a KL-divergence unsupervised loss against the local model's strongly augmented output, combined with a standard cross-entropy supervised loss. This design lets SAGE act as a plug-in that replaces the hard pseudo-label step in any existing FSSL method.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 10% labels, SAGE achieves 54.18% (α=0.1), 55.82% (α=0.5), and 56.06% (α=1) top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best FSSL baseline (FedLabel at 50.88–52.38%) by 3.3–3.7 percentage points. On CIFAR-10, SAGE reaches 87.05–89.08%, surpassing FedDure (84.60–87.34%) by up to 2.45 points. On SVHN, SAGE attains 93.85–94.65%, matching the fully-labeled FedAvg-SL upper bound (94.11–94.40%). Convergence is also significantly faster: SAGE reaches 30% accuracy in 56 rounds vs. 91–118 rounds for baselines (2.11× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE outperforms all five FSSL baselines (FedMatch, FedLabel, FedLoke, FedDure, FedDB) across every dataset and heterogeneity level. The largest gains appear on CIFAR-100 under high heterogeneity (α=0.1), where SAGE leads FedLabel by 3.30 points and FedDure by 5.91 points, indicating that hard pseudo-label strategies degrade most severely under non-IID conditions. Against the vanilla SSL baselines, SAGE surpasses FixMatch-LPL by 4.86 points on CIFAR-100 (α=0.1) and FixMatch-GPL by 5.22 points, confirming that neither local-only nor global-only pseudo-labeling is sufficient. On SVHN, SAGE (93.85–94.65%) matches or exceeds the fully-labeled FedAvg-SL upper bound (94.11–94.40%), suggesting the semi-supervised signal fully compensates for the 90% missing labels. As a plug-in, SAGE also boosts FlexMatch on SVHN by 33–41 points (from ~52–60% to ~93–94%), demonstrating that the confidence-driven correction mechanism is broadly applicable beyond its native FixMatch-based pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the FixMatch paradigm for semi-supervised learning and the FedAvg aggregation framework, positioning itself within the Label-at-All-Client FSSL setting that prior methods (FedMatch, FedLabel, FedLoke, FedDure, FedDB) have explored. Its key departure is identifying that data heterogeneity systematically widens the confidence gap between local and global models, and using this gap as a dynamic signal to soft-correct hard pseudo-labels via linear interpolation. Unlike prior FSSL methods that treat pseudo-labels as fixed binary decisions, SAGE introduces a continuous correction mechanism (CDSC) and a collaborative generation strategy (CPG) that leverages the complementary strengths of local (high utilization) and global (distribution-aware) pseudo-labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The confidence-discrepancy diagnostic offers a practical, lightweight signal for pseudo-label reliability that can be plugged into existing FSSL pipelines without architectural changes, as demonstrated by the FlexMatch+SVHN rescue. However, the evaluation is limited to ResNet-8 on four small-scale datasets with 20 clients, and the method is only validated in the Label-at-All-Client setting; extending to Label-at-Partial-Client and Label-at-Server scenarios, as the authors note, remains open. The approach also assumes the global model is a meaningful conservative reference, which may not hold in highly asymmetric client populations or with very few communication rounds.
