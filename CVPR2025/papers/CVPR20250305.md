## Problem

Diffusion-based imitation learning policies for robotic manipulation struggle to generalize beyond the same object category with similar appearances. While recent works improve visual feature encoding (3D point clouds, equivariant networks, semantic fields), they remain limited to intra-category generalization. The paper identifies that transferable manipulation priors—affordances encoding 'where' and 'how' to interact with an object—can bridge the gap to entirely unseen instances and categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250305/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a cropped and downsampled scene point cloud (from RGB-D), robot proprioception (joint positions), and a transferred 3D affordance consisting of a static contact point c ∈ R³ and a dynamic post-contact trajectory τ ∈ R^(3×N). The affordance is not directly observed but computed at inference time by retrieving a similar source object from an affordance memory (indexed by CLIP features) and applying a 6D transformation estimated via SD-DINOv2 semantic correspondence and ICP point cloud registration. The output is a sequence of robot joint actions generated through iterative DDIM denoising with an adaptive affordance-guidance correction step.

## Method

AffordDP conditions a DDIM-based diffusion policy on 3D affordance (encoded via MLP for the contact point and a transformer with [CLS] token for the trajectory), scene point cloud, and proprioception. The key transfer mechanism independently estimates translation (difference of 3D contact points) and rotation (ICP on Point-SAM-segmented manipulable parts) to form a 6D transformation that maps source-object affordance to the target object, avoiding unreliable full-object registration. During sampling, an adaptive affordance guidance term—defined as the L2 distance between the forward-kinematics end-effector position and the static contact point, activated only within a threshold radius—is injected as a normalized gradient correction (inspired by DSG) at each DDIM step, steering the generated action toward the desired manipulation while preserving it on the learned action manifold. Training uses the standard noise-prediction MSE loss on expert demonstrations collected via CuRoBo (simulation) or teleoperation (real-world).

## Results

In real-world experiments across three tasks (Pull Drawer, Open Door, Pick & Place), AffordDP achieves 67.5–80.0% success on seen instances, 45.0–65.0% on unseen instances, and 40.0–50.0% on unseen categories, while Diffusion Policy (DP) fails entirely (0.0% across all settings) and DP3 reaches at most 40.0% on seen instances. In simulation under unified policy training, AffordDP reaches 90.0% on seen instances and 73.3% on unseen categories for Pull Drawer, compared to 20.7% and 3.3% for DP and 41.3% and 3.3% for DP3. The method's advantage grows with increasing generalization difficulty, with the largest gaps appearing on unseen categories where baselines drop below 10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two diffusion-based baselines: Diffusion Policy (DP) [Chi et al., RSS 2023], which uses 2D image observations, and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) [Ze et al., RSS 2024], which replaces images with point clouds. AffordDP decisively outperforms both across all real-world and simulation settings. DP achieves 0.0% success rate in every real-world task and generalization condition, likely due to insufficient training data (the paper notes DP typically requires >200 demonstrations). DP3 shows limited capability—reaching 40.0% on seen Pick & Place instances but dropping to 0.0% on unseen instances for Pull Drawer and Open Door, and at most 30.0% on unseen categories. AffordDP's largest margins appear on unseen categories: 40.0% vs. 10.0% (DP3) on Pull Drawer, 50.0% vs. 30.0% on Open Door, and 50.0% vs. 15.0% on Pick & Place. In simulation, the gap is even more pronounced, with AffordDP reaching 73.3% on unseen-category Pull Drawer versus 3.3% for both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

AffordDP extends the Diffusion Policy lineage (Chi et al., RSS 2023) by introducing transferable 3D affordances—contact points plus post-contact trajectories—as an additional conditioning signal, building on DDIM sampling and the DSG guidance mechanism (Yang et al., ICML 2024) for constrained generation. It departs from concurrent generalization efforts (3D Diffusion Policy, EquiBot, GenDP) that improve visual feature encoding but remain limited to same-category transfer, instead leveraging foundation vision models (SD-DINOv2, CLIP, GroundedSAM, Point-SAM) to perform training-free affordance transfer across categories via 6D transformation estimation. The paper also contrasts with prior affordance-based manipulation work (Robo-ABC, RAM) that relies on separate grasp generators and motion planners, integrating affordance directly into the diffusion policy's action generation loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AffordDP demonstrates that manipulation priors encoded as 3D contact points and trajectories can bridge the generalization gap to entirely unseen object categories—a setting where prior diffusion policies fail completely in real-world deployment. The training-free affordance transfer pipeline (foundation models + ICP) is practically attractive for rapid adaptation to new objects without retraining. However, the approach inherits limitations from foundation models' spatial understanding, struggles when affordance extraction is ambiguous, and is validated only on relatively simple single-object tasks (pull drawer, open door, pick-and-place); scaling to multi-step, multi-object, or highly articulated manipulation remains an open question.
