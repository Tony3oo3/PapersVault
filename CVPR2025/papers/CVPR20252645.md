## Problem

Existing image-based human relighting methods are restricted to narrow scenarios (face-only or static full-body) due to the scarcity of large-scale relighting datasets, and most can only accept a single lighting control (either a background image or spherical harmonics parameters, but not both). No prior approach jointly models relighting and background harmonization in a generalizable way that handles arbitrary body parts, poses, and multi-person scenes while maintaining temporal coherence in video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252645/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image or video of humans (any body part from portrait to multi-person), spherical harmonics coefficients (up to 2nd order, 25 parameters), and optionally a target background image. The input image is consumed as a composite of the foreground subject and the conditioning background, following prior harmonization practice. Output is a relit and harmonized image or video where the human's lighting matches the target conditions and is visually consistent with the background; a guided refinement step preserves high-frequency details from the original input.

## Method

The framework repurposes a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) in a coarse-to-fine scheme: a pixel-aligned coarse shading map is computed analytically from spherical harmonics coefficients and detected surface normals (no learned network), and a Light ControlNet conditions the diffusion model to learn only the residual fine-grained lighting (e.g., self-occluded shadows) plus background harmonization. A separate Motion ControlNet is trained unsupervised via lighting cycle consistency on real videos—predicting future-frame lighting from past frames without ground-truth relighting data—and is plugged in at inference without retraining the diffusion model. At test time, features from the Light and Motion ControlNets are blended spatially (0.85:0.15) and temporally (0.5:0.5 with optical-flow alignment) in a recurrent pipeline to enforce temporal coherence. A lightweight residual network performs guided refinement to recover high-frequency details lost during denoising.

## Results

On the static synthetic test set (Table 2), Comprehensive Relighting achieves 23.04 PSNR / 0.90 SSIM on portrait, 30.81 / 0.97 on full-body, and 31.49 / 0.96 on multi-person, outperforming the strongest baseline GFR by 5.3 dB on portrait and 1.3 dB on full-body. On the video test set (Table 1), the method attains the best fidelity (PSNR 25.95, 33.58, 26.61) and best temporal consistency (tPSNR 33.58, 35.18, 38.32) across all three scenarios (static lighting + moving human, dynamic lighting + static human, dynamic lighting + moving human), with the largest margin over GFR in Scenario 1 (25.95 vs. 25.59 PSNR) and Scenario 3 (26.61 vs. 25.49 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Comprehensive Relighting outperforms all four baselines on the majority of metrics. Against DPR [68] and RHW [47], the gains are substantial: on portrait relighting, Ours achieves 23.04 PSNR versus 17.74 (DPR) and 15.75 (RHW), a 5–7 dB improvement, reflecting the limited generalizability of these portrait-focused methods to full-body and multi-person settings. Against GFR [22], the closest competitor, Ours leads by 5.3 dB on portrait (23.04 vs. 17.71) and 1.3 dB on full-body (30.81 vs. 29.51), while GFR marginally edges Ours on multi-person SSIM (0.97 vs. 0.96) but trails by 2.1 dB in PSNR (29.35 vs. 31.49). Against LPBR [38], a harmonization-only method without explicit lighting control, Ours dominates across all three video scenarios in both fidelity and temporal consistency (e.g., 25.95 vs. 18.19 PSNR in Scenario 1), and qualitatively avoids the full-body distortions that LPBR exhibits. The temporal module further differentiates Ours: in Scenario 2 (dynamic lighting, static human), Ours achieves 35.18 tPSNR versus GFR's 32.36, confirming that the unsupervised cycle-consistency training yields measurably smoother lighting transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) as a general image prior and attaches two ControlNet-based modules — a Light ControlNet for coarse shading/background conditioning and a Motion ControlNet for temporal coherence — to jointly perform human relighting and background harmonization. It departs from prior relighting methods (DPR, RHW, GFR) that are restricted to specific body parts and single lighting controls, and from harmonization methods (LPBR) that lack explicit lighting parameters, by unifying both tasks under a coarse-to-fine framework where spherical-harmonics-based shading serves as a generalizable control prior and the diffusion model learns only the residual fine-grained details. The unsupervised temporal module, trained via cycle consistency on real videos without ground-truth relighting data, is a novel addition that has no direct analogue in prior image-based relighting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first approach to jointly handle relighting and background harmonization for arbitrary human body parts (portrait through multi-person) in a single model, addressing a key gap that has limited production-level deployment of image-based relighting. The coarse-to-fine decomposition — where the diffusion model learns only the residual over a physically-motivated coarse shading estimate — offers a practical recipe for generalizing diffusion-based editing to new domains with limited paired data. However, the method remains computationally expensive (diffusion-based generation per frame), and temporal coherence is sensitive to detection noise in masks and surface normals, leaving real-time and robustness questions open for production use.
