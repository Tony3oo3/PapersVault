## Problem

Whether a deep generative model can acquire complex knowledge—rules, reasoning, and planning—solely from unlabeled visual input remains unexplored, as prior work either relies on text-based LLMs or requires language instructions and action labels for video-based learning. This paper investigates if pure video observation, processed through next-token prediction, is sufficient for knowledge acquisition in tasks demanding long-horizon reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251427/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw video frames (256×256 pixel images) as observation history, with each frame compressed to a 4×4 grid of discrete tokens via a VQ-VAE; for robotics tasks, a text instruction label additionally conditions generation. At inference, the autoregressive transformer produces both the next frame tokens and H latent dynamics codes per step, from which a separately trained Inverse Dynamics Model (MLP) infers the concrete action (Go move coordinates or robotic joint commands). The dual output of frames plus latent codes is the key structural distinction from standard video generation.

## Method

VideoWorld combines a MAGVIT-v2-style VQ-VAE (with FSQ quantization) and a Llama-architecture autoregressive transformer trained under next-token prediction on discretized video. The central contribution is the Latent Dynamics Model (LDM), which uses a causal codec (without temporal downsampling) to extract frame features, then applies H learnable query embeddings through cross-attention to compress multi-step visual changes into H compact latent codes, quantized via FSQ as an information bottleneck to prevent trivial copying. These LDM codes are concatenated with frame tokens into a unified vocabulary for the transformer, enabling joint prediction of visual content and temporal dynamics. During inference, the model generates latent codes and the next frame, and a lightweight IDM maps these to task actions. The key insight is that a compact representation of visual change—rather than raw pixel-level redundancy—is what enables efficient knowledge acquisition, with the LDM codes encoding forward-planning information that the model leverages for strategic decision-making.

## Results

On Video-GoBench, VideoWorld 300M achieves a tournament Elo of 2317±25, surpassing the RL-based KataGO-5d (Elo 2253±20) and reaching a 5-dan professional Go level with only 300M parameters and no search or reward mechanisms. Its action-value score of 83.7% and best-action accuracy of 88.1% are competitive with the state-based Transformer 300M (79.7% / 87.2%) while using only raw video input. On CALVIN, VideoWorld (trained purely on video without action labels) achieves 56.2%/75.4%/72.1% task success on Push/Open-Close/Turn On-Off, dramatically outperforming the label-free Transformer (17.3%/24.1%/19.2%) and approaching oracle performance (75.4%/95.3%/96.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KataGo (the RL-based Go engine), VideoWorld 300M outperforms KataGO-5d by 64 Elo points (2317 vs. 2253) and KataGO-1d by 274 points, all without search or reward mechanisms. On CALVIN, VideoWorld (video-only, no action labels) substantially outperforms the label-free Transformer baseline by 38.9/51.3/52.9 points on Push/Open-Close/Turn On-Off respectively. However, VideoWorld still trails the action-labeled baselines: LCD (71.3/90.2/92.1%), SPIL (68.4/87.9/85.3%), HULC (65.8/80.9/85.3%), and MCIL (33.0/38.7/41.2%) on the three CALVIN tasks. The gap narrows considerably with additional data: VideoWorld with +30k trajectories reaches 72.7/91.0/93.8%, matching or exceeding LCD on all tasks. The key distinction is that all labeled baselines require per-frame action annotations, whereas VideoWorld learns purely from unlabeled video, making it more scalable to real-world settings where such labels are unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MAGVIT-v2 tokenizer architecture for video discretization and the Inverse Dynamics Model approach from UniPi for converting generated frames into actions, but departs fundamentally from both by eliminating language instructions and action labels entirely. Unlike prior video-generation-for-control work (UniPi, Genie, LAPA) that conditions on text or learns single-step latent actions, VideoWorld introduces a multi-step Latent Dynamics Model that compresses future visual changes into compact latent codes, enabling the autoregressive transformer to capture long-horizon temporal dynamics. The paper also contrasts with state-based game learning (ChessGPT, Othello-GPT) and RL-based Go engines (KataGo), positioning itself as the first to demonstrate complex rule/reasoning/planning acquisition purely from unlabeled video via next-token prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 300M-parameter video generation model can reach 5-dan Go strength without RL, search, or action labels challenges the prevailing assumption that complex strategic reasoning requires reward-based training, opening a path toward knowledge acquisition from the vast corpus of unlabeled video. The cross-environment generalization in robotics (CALVIN → RLBench) without domain-specific retraining addresses a key limitation of RL-based methods. However, the work is limited to simplified visual environments (9×9 Go, simulation robotics), and the authors acknowledge that scaling to larger models, higher-fidelity visual synthesis, and real-world deployment remain open challenges that will determine whether this paradigm generalizes beyond controlled benchmarks.
