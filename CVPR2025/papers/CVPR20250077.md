## Problem

Multi-level emotion recognition (head, body, context) inherently produces conflicting predictions that degrade final accuracy, yet existing methods lack a principled mechanism to resolve these inconsistencies. Prior work relies on knowledge graphs or simple feature concatenation, which do not directly address the challenge of hierarchically misaligned emotion outputs interfering with the decision-making process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250077/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, crops it into three windows (head, body, context) via ArcFace object detection, and resizes each to a common (H, W) resolution. It additionally consumes k text prompts of the form 'This photo shows {emotion} emotion' through a frozen CLIP text encoder. Output is a set of k emotion category scores (26 multi-label on EMOTIC, 7 single-label on CAER-S), produced by concatenating refined per-level features with the vocabulary-informed feature and passing through a final feed-forward network.

## Method

CocoER comprises three modules: (1) Hierarchical Feature Extraction applies cross-level attention (e.g., head attends to context) with linear projections to enrich local representations before per-level classification; (2) Vocabulary-Informed Alignment uses a frozen CLIP image encoder plus prototype learning (a trained linear projection ω minimizing distance to text-space emotion prototypes) to generate a pseudo-label y_t that guides refinement; (3) The Workspace module performs an iterative competition process that identifies and eliminates the most inconsistent level (via a differentiable max-loss formulation reduced to a quadratic program) and a coordination process that accumulates the resulting descent directions, updates the shared linear-mapping parameters at inference time, and re-projects all remaining level features through the updated mappings. This inference-stage parameter update is regularized by an L2 norm on the accumulated descent direction to prevent over-reliance on the pseudo-label.

## Results

On the EMOTIC test split, CocoER achieves a state-of-the-art mAP of 37.32%, surpassing the previous best (Mittal et al. [24], 35.48%) by 1.84% while using only RGB images (no depth modality). On CAER-S, the method reaches 91.86% accuracy, outperforming the prior best (Wu et al. [36], 90.83%) by 1.03%. In terms of F1 scores on EMOTIC, CocoER attains 36.77% C-F1 and 57.23% O-F1, both exceeding the runner-up Wu et al. [36] (34.24% / 54.78%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CocoER outperforms all 11 baselines on both datasets. On EMOTIC mAP, it beats Mittal et al. [24] (35.48%) by 1.84% despite Mittal using an additional depth modality, and surpasses Hoang et al. [9] (35.16%) by 2.16% and Li et al. [19] (32.41%) by 4.91%. The gap over Wu et al. [36] (26.68%) is particularly large at 10.64% mAP, highlighting the advantage of the competition-coordination mechanism over scene-graph-based reasoning. All three VLMs (BLIP2: 25.96%, LLaVa: 27.99%, GPT-4o: 23.80%) are substantially outperformed, with GPT-4o trailing by 13.52% mAP. On CAER-S, CocoER (91.86%) edges out Wu et al. [36] (90.83%) by 1.03% and MA-Net (88.42%) by 3.44%, while VLMs remain far behind (best: GPT-4o at 29.33%). On EMOTIC F1 scores, CocoER leads Wu et al. [36] by 2.53% C-F1 and 2.45% O-F1, and Kosti et al. [14] by 7.44% C-F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of context-aware emotion recognition that uses hierarchical visual features (head, body, context) by explicitly addressing the problem of *conflicting* predictions across levels, rather than simply fusing them. It builds on CLIP and prototype learning (Fu et al., 2020) to generate vocabulary-informed pseudo labels that guide feature refinement, and borrows the min-max QP formulation from distributionally robust optimization (Shen et al., 2022) to make the iterative elimination of inconsistent levels differentiable. The competition-coordination mechanism is conceptually inspired by Workspace Theory from cognitive science, distinguishing it from prior approaches that rely on knowledge graphs or causal disentanglement to handle multi-level inconsistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-level prediction inconsistency is a real bottleneck even for large vision-language models (GPT-4o achieves only 23.80% mAP on EMOTIC), and that a lightweight inference-stage parameter update can improve VLM performance by 1–3% mAP without retraining. The interpretability of the competition process (explicitly showing which level is eliminated and why) is a practical advantage over black-box fusion. Open questions include whether the mechanism scales beyond three levels, how sensitive it is to the quality of the object-detection-based cropping, and whether the inference-stage gradient step introduces instability at scale.
