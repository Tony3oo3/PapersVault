## Problem

Long-form video understanding with LLMs is hampered by information overload: naively captioning densely sampled frames floods the context window with redundant content, while existing structured representations (search trees, memory banks) lack the spatial topology needed for grounded reasoning. The paper identifies an underexplored property of long videos—critical moments are temporally dispersed yet concentrated in a small set of spatial zones—and argues that no prior representation explicitly exploits this spatio-temporal structure for LLM-based reasoning.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes untrimmed long-form egocentric video (RGB frames), estimated camera poses (x, y, z per frame), and a natural-language question. It first constructs a three-layered hierarchical semantic graph serialized as JSON—where nodes are spatial concepts (objects, activity zones, rooms) and edges carry typed attributes (interaction type, temporal window, layout relation, relative distance)—which is then passed as text to a text-only LLM (GPT-4) to produce a free-form or multiple-choice answer. No pixel-level features are fed to the LLM; all visual information is distilled into the graph's textual description.

## Method

VideoMindPalace builds a three-layer hierarchical graph in a training-free pipeline. Layer 1 (Human & Object) uses RT-DETR + ByteTrack for multi-object tracking and AMEGO's hand-object interaction tracker to create nodes for hands/objects and edges labeled with interaction type, spatial relation, and temporal window. Layer 2 (Activity Zones) discovers spatial zones by incrementally clustering frames using CLIP visual-feature similarity combined with 3D camera-pose proximity, then assigns each zone a semantic label via a captioner; edges between zones encode relative distance and layout relations (e.g., 'left of', 'in front of'). Layer 3 (Scene Layout) groups zones into rooms and connects them with layout-distance edges. The resulting graph is serialized to JSON and supplied to GPT-4, which performs spatial, temporal, and layout reasoning over the structured text without any fine-tuning, avoiding the exhaustive frame search required by tree- or memory-bank-based alternatives.

## Results

On standard video QA benchmarks, VideoMindPalace achieves 75.8% on NExT-QA, 70.1% on EgoSchema, and 70.1% on IntentQA, outperforming all training-free and fine-tuned baselines. On the proposed VMB benchmark, it leads in Spatial (51.2 avg), Temporal (56.3 avg), and Layout (34.5 avg) multiple-choice accuracy, as well as Open-Ended quality (3.8/5). On the Active Memories Benchmark, it attains the highest total accuracy of 37.8%, with particularly strong Temporal Grounding (57.4% on Q8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoMindPalace outperforms all seven baselines across the three standard benchmarks. Against the strongest training-free competitor VideoTree [41], it gains +2.3 pp on NExT-QA (75.8 vs 73.5), +3.9 pp on EgoSchema (70.1 vs 66.2), and +3.2 pp on IntentQA (70.1 vs 66.9). Against the memory-augmented VideoAgent [11], gains are +4.5 pp on NExT-QA and +9.9 pp on EgoSchema. On the VMB benchmark, the Layout category shows the largest margin: 34.5 vs 24.5 (VideoAgent) and 22.9 (VideoTree), reflecting the graph's explicit 3D spatial encoding. On the Active Memories Benchmark, VideoMindPalace edges out AMEGO [12] by 1.5 pp total (37.8 vs 36.3) and leads in Temporal Grounding (57.4 vs 48.9 on Q8), though it trails AMEGO on some spatial grounding sub-questions due to errors in converting visual queries to text. Fine-tuned models like SF-LLaVA-34B [45] and Video-LLaMA2 [7] are consistently outperformed, confirming the advantage of the structured graph representation over end-to-end training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free long-video QA paradigm (LLoVi, VideoTree, VideoAgent) by replacing temporal sequences or query-specific trees with a holistic, three-layer topological graph that encodes spatial layout, activity zones, and human-object interactions. It draws on perception models (RT-DETR, ByteTrack, CLIP, EgoSTARK) as modular tools, similar to VideoAgent's tool-use philosophy, but departs by constructing a single environment-grounded representation rather than per-query retrieval. The 'Mind Palace' memorization metaphor provides the conceptual framing: associating temporally dispersed events with spatially fixed zones, which prior structured-video works (robotics scene graphs, egocentric action graphs) have not explicitly exploited for LLM-parseable video understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VMB benchmark fills a genuine gap: existing video QA benchmarks (EgoSchema, NExT-QA, AMB) test temporal and causal reasoning but largely ignore layout-aware spatial navigation, where all prior methods score near random. The finding that a topologically structured graph outperforms flat or tree-based representations specifically on layout questions (34.5% vs. ~22–25%) suggests that explicit 3D spatial encoding is a missing ingredient in current long-video pipelines. Key limitations remain: perception-model errors propagate through the graph, low-level visual attributes (color, texture) are absent, and absolute accuracy on layout tasks is still modest, leaving open whether richer 3D reconstruction or learned graph construction could close the gap.
