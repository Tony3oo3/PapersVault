## Problem

Visual aliasing (doppelgangers) causes spurious feature matches between visually similar but distinct 3D surfaces, corrupting SfM reconstructions. Prior learning-based classifiers (Cai et al., 2023) were trained solely on landmark photos with CNN features, making them brittle on everyday scenes, sensitive to decision thresholds, and lacking a principled way to evaluate reconstruction correctness automatically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252559/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images and produces a single doppelganger probability score via a voting mechanism over four sub-scores (two Transformer heads × two input orderings). This score is applied as a threshold filter on the SfM scene graph edges, removing pairs below τ to yield a pruned match graph that feeds into COLMAP or MASt3R-SfM for corrected 3D reconstruction. The evaluation protocol additionally consumes geo-tagged Mapillary images registered to the model, producing a RANSAC inlier ratio as an automated correctness metric.

## Method

Doppelgangers++ repurposes the frozen MASt3R model as a 3D-aware feature extractor: for each image pair, it concatenates the encoder features with all B decoder-block outputs from both intertwined branches to form multi-level feature tensors F¹ and F². Two independent 3-layer Transformer classification heads (768-dim, 8 attention heads) process F¹ and F² separately, respecting MASt3R's asymmetric reference-frame design, and the image pair is evaluated in both orderings (I_p,I_q) and (I_q,I_p) to yield four scores. A majority-voting rule combines these into a final score (max if majority positive, min if majority negative, mean otherwise). Training is supervised with cross-entropy on 120K pairs from the original Doppelganger dataset plus a new VisymScenes dataset (47K pairs from 26 geo-tagged everyday-scene sites), and only the two classification heads are trained (5 epochs, Adam, lr 1e-4) while MASt3R weights remain frozen.

## Results

On the in-domain Doppelganger test set (trained on DG + VisymScenes), Doppelgangers++ achieves AP 0.981 and ROC AUC 0.981, versus 0.956 and 0.947 for DG-OG. On the out-of-domain Mapillary test set, the gap widens dramatically: AP 0.968 and ROC AUC 0.958 for Doppelgangers++ versus 0.692 and 0.701 for DG-OG. On VisymScenes, Doppelgangers++ reaches near-perfect AP 0.991 and ROC AUC 0.990. For SfM reconstruction (Table 2), the method uses a single consistent threshold (τ=0.8) across all 20 scenes while DG-OG requires scene-specific tuning, and achieves higher geo-alignment inlier ratios in every scene (e.g., 0.962 vs 0.881 on Church on Spilled Blood, 0.874 vs 0.451 on Belvedere where DG-OG fails entirely).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DG-OG (Cai et al., ICCV 2023), the original CNN-based doppelganger classifier. Doppelgangers++ outperforms DG-OG on every metric across all three test sets. The advantage is most pronounced on the out-of-domain Mapillary set, where Doppelgangers++ achieves AP 0.968 versus DG-OG's 0.692 (a 40% absolute gap), and Recall@Prec=0.99 of 0.736 versus 0.000, indicating DG-OG is essentially non-functional in that domain. On the in-domain DG set, the improvement is more modest (AP 0.981 vs 0.956) but still consistent. The second baseline is vanilla COLMAP SfM; in Table 2, Doppelgangers++ integrated into COLMAP achieves higher geo-alignment inlier ratios than both vanilla COLMAP and DG-OG in all 20 scenes, while also registering more images (better completeness). Notably, DG-OG requires scene-specific threshold tuning (e.g., τ=0.97 for Church on Spilled Blood, τ=0.6 for Ponte di Rialto) and fails entirely on Belvedere (Vienna), whereas Doppelgangers++ uses a single τ=0.8 across all scenes and succeeds on Belvedere with inlier ratio 0.874.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Cai et al.'s (ICCV 2023) formulation of doppelganger detection as a binary image-pair classification task, replacing their CNN classifier trained on landmark photos with a Transformer-based head on frozen MASt3R features. The key conceptual departure is repurposing MASt3R's internal decoder features—designed for dense correspondence and known to produce incorrect point clouds on doppelganger pairs—as a rich geometric representation for classification, rather than fine-tuning the full model. The paper also introduces the VisymScenes dataset to diversify training beyond the original landmark-centric Doppelganger dataset, and proposes an automated geotag-based evaluation protocol to replace the manual model inspection used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Doppelganger-induced errors remain a practical bottleneck for SfM in real-world scenes with repeated or symmetric structures, and this paper provides a drop-in classifier that integrates into both COLMAP and MASt3R-SfM pipelines without scene-specific threshold tuning. The geotag-based inlier-ratio evaluation is a useful methodological contribution that could be applied to other SfM benchmarks (e.g., MegaScenes) to quantify reconstruction correctness automatically. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SfM to other 3D tasks (e.g., NeRF/3DGS reconstruction), whether lighter-weight feature extractors could replace MASt3R for the classification head, and how the method performs in fully unstructured captures without the semi-structured assumptions used in VisymScenes data mining.
