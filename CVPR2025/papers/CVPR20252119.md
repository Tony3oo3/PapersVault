## Problem

State-of-the-art memory-based online action detection methods suffer from a training-inference discrepancy: during training, all short-term frames are used with varying context exposure under a causal mask, but inference relies solely on the latest frame with full context. This creates two learning biases—imbalanced immediate context across short-term frames and non-causal leakage when anticipated futures (derived from the full short-term memory) are fed back to enrich earlier frames—ultimately degrading the representation quality of the frame that matters at inference time.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted frame-wise features (e.g., two-stream ResNet-50/BN-Inception at 4 FPS for THUMOS'14, I3D+audio VGG at 1 FPS for CrossTask) from a streaming video, partitioned into long-term (128–256 s), short-term (4–10 s), near-past, and near-future contexts. At inference, a sliding window with stride 1 simulates online streaming, and the model simultaneously outputs per-frame action probability distributions for both detection (last frame of short-term) and anticipation (a future frame at a configurable interval τ, ranging 0.25–2.0 s).

## Method

CMeRT is an encoder-decoder transformer built from Transformer Decoder Units (TDU). The Context-Enhanced Encoder appends a near-past context window to the short-term memory before masked self-attention, giving earlier short-term frames additional immediate context to counteract the causal-mask imbalance. The Memory-Refined Decoder generates near-future features exclusively from the compressed long-term memory (not from short-term), then uses them via cross-attention to refine the encoded short-term representations—this design eliminates the non-causal leakage that in prior work (MAT) allowed earlier frames to indirectly access their own future short-term frames. A weight-shared classifier scores both the encoder and decoder outputs, and training combines cross-entropy losses on short-term, anticipation, and generated near-future predictions with balancing coefficients.

## Results

CMeRT achieves state-of-the-art online action detection on all three datasets: 73.2 mAP on THUMOS'14, 35.9 mAP on CrossTask, and 47.1/48.3/27.6 Top-5 Recall (Verb/Noun/Action) on EK100. For action anticipation, CMeRT reaches 59.5 average mAP on THUMOS'14 (outperforming MAT by 1.3%) and 35.1/39.7/19.8 Top-5 Recall on EK100. The model also achieves 126.6 FPS with 94.5M parameters, outperforming MAT (102.0 FPS, 107.4M params) in both speed and accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMeRT outperforms all memory-based baselines across every dataset. On THUMOS'14, it beats LSTR by 3.7 mAP, GateHub by 2.5 mAP, Testra by 2.0 mAP, MAT by 1.6 mAP, and the most recent JOAAD by 0.6 mAP. On CrossTask, CMeRT improves over MAT by 2.0 mAP (35.9 vs 33.9) and over LSTR by 2.9 mAP. On EK100, CMeRT leads MAT by 2.6 points in Verb recall (47.1 vs 44.5) and by 1.3 points in Action recall (27.6 vs 26.3), while matching MAT on Noun (48.3). For anticipation on THUMOS'14, CMeRT surpasses MAT (59.5 vs 58.2 avg mAP), Testra (56.8), and LSTR (52.6); on EK100 anticipation it edges out MAT by 0.1/0.9/0.3 in Verb/Noun/Action Top-5 Recall. The naive baselines MAT-rw (reweighting) and MAT-stream (single-frame training) confirm the training-inference gap: reweighting yields only marginal gains while streaming training drops performance by 13.5 mAP on THUMOS'14.

## Relation to Prior Work

CMeRT builds directly on the long-short-term memory formulation introduced by LSTR [49] and TeSTra [53], maintaining their three-partition context structure (long-term, short-term, anticipation) while introducing two new context partitions: near-past and near-future. Its key conceptual departure from MAT [45] is identifying that MAT's Conditional Circular Interaction creates non-causal leakage by allowing short-term frames to indirectly access future frames through anticipation generated from the full short-term memory; CMeRT instead generates near-future exclusively from compressed long-term memory to preserve causality. The paper also positions itself as advancing the OAD evaluation landscape by introducing DinoV2 features, event-based metrics (P-F1, S-F1, Edit score), and the CrossTask dataset, arguing that the field has been constrained by outdated features and frame-only evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis of a systematic training-inference discrepancy in all memory-based OAD methods is a useful conceptual contribution that explains why prior approaches underperform on the latest frame at inference. The finding that naively matching training to inference (MAT-stream) causes a dramatic 13.5-point drop reveals that the multi-frame training strategy, despite its biases, is essential for batch diversity and training stability. Open questions include the inherent trade-off between detection and anticipation in the unified framework, whether the near-past/near-future formulation generalizes to longer-horizon or multi-modal settings, and whether the identified biases persist when using stronger foundation-model features. The new benchmark with event-wise metrics and DinoV2 features provides a more realistic evaluation protocol that the community can adopt.
