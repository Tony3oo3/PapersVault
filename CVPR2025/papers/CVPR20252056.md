## Problem

In Consistency Distillation, the knowledge discrepancy between student and teacher outputs is highly imbalanced across timesteps: it is large at low noise (t→0, KDC≈60) causing unstable convergence, and small at high noise (t→1, KDC<40) causing inefficient learning. Existing remedies like iCT and ECM reduce the distillation step over training, which further shrinks the discrepancy and worsens the problem. The paper asks whether one can maintain a stable, balanced curriculum difficulty across all timesteps to enable effective single-step generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252056/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model receives a single random noise vector x₀ ~ N(0,I) (and a text prompt for text-to-image variants) and produces a complete synthetic image in one forward pass (NFE=1). Experiments span 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 64×64 (ImageNet), and 1024×1024 (SDXL/SD3 via LoRA on CC3M). For text-to-image, the text prompt is encoded by the base model's text encoder and conditioned on the denoising process; the output is a single RGB image with no intermediate steps exposed to the user.

## Method

CCM introduces a Knowledge Discrepancy of the Curriculum (KDC) metric, computed as 100 − PSNR between the student's one-step prediction and the teacher's output over the interval (t, u). Instead of fixing u or shrinking the distillation step l = u − t, CCM dynamically extends u forward from t in small increments of size s, iterating the frozen teacher model step-by-step until KDC exceeds a threshold T_KDC (typically 60). This 'see further when clear' strategy ensures the student always faces a curriculum of comparable difficulty regardless of the current noise level, and the multi-step teacher iteration prevents quality degradation when l becomes large. The final training objective combines this KDC-adjusted consistency distillation loss with an adversarial GAN loss, and the framework is unified across both diffusion models (parameterized by noise-to-signal ratio σ) and flow matching models (parameterized by continuous t ∈ [0,1)).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CCM (flow matching, OT-CFM base) achieves a one-step FID of 1.64, surpassing the teacher OT-CFM (FID 4.49 at NFE=100) and all consistency distillation baselines including PCM (1.94), iCT (2.83), and CD (3.55). On ImageNet 64×64, CCM reaches FID 2.18 at NFE=1, a 2.4× improvement over its teacher OT-CFM (5.36). On COCO-2014-30K, CCM on SDXL achieves FID 20.47 and CLIP score 31.73, outperforming PCM (FID 21.15) and Hyper-SD (FID 30.87), while CCM on SD3 achieves FID 21.49 and CLIP 31.41, beating LCM (FID 25.44) and PCM (FID 28.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10 with flow matching, CCM (FID 1.64) dramatically outperforms its teacher OT-CFM (4.49 at NFE=100) and all CD-based baselines: PCM (1.94), CD w/GAN (4.08), and vanilla CD (10.5). Among diffusion-model baselines on CIFAR-10, CCM w/o GAN (2.79) beats iCT (2.83), ECM (3.60), and CD (3.55), but trails CTM (1.98) and SiD (1.92), which use stronger teacher models (EDM). On ImageNet 64×64, CCM (2.18) substantially improves over OT-CFM (5.36) but remains behind the strongest non-CM distillation methods: PaGoDA (1.21), DMD2 (1.28), and SiD (1.52). On COCO-2014-30K, CCM consistently wins: on SD3 it beats LCM by 3.95 FID points (21.49 vs. 25.44) and PCM by 7.03 points; on SDXL it beats PCM by 0.68 points (20.47 vs. 21.15) and Hyper-SD by 10.4 points. On T2I-CompBench, CCM-4Step outperforms both LCM and PCM across all six compositional metrics (color, shape, texture, spatial, non-spatial, complex) for both SD3 and SDXL, approaching the quality of the original 28/40-step models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency distillation framework introduced by Song et al. (ICML 2023) and the local consistency formulation from Consistency Trajectory Models (Kim et al., NeurIPS 2023) by reframing the distillation interval (t, u) as a curriculum whose difficulty should be balanced across timesteps. It departs from iCT and ECM, which progressively reduce the distillation step to combat error accumulation, by arguing that smaller steps actually shrink the knowledge discrepancy and make learning less efficient. The method also unifies treatment of diffusion models and flow matching models under a single KDC-adjusted loss, and incorporates adversarial training following the CTM recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCM provides a simple, model-agnostic scheduling mechanism (PSNR-based KDC threshold) that practitioners can plug into existing consistency distillation pipelines for both diffusion and flow matching architectures, with demonstrated gains on SDXL and SD3 for high-resolution text-to-image generation. The counterintuitive finding that reducing distillation steps (as in iCT/ECM) worsens learning efficiency challenges a common heuristic in the field. Open questions include the computational overhead of multi-step teacher iteration at scale, sensitivity to the T_KDC threshold in practice, and whether the curriculum-consistency principle transfers to video or 3D generation where timestep structure differs.
