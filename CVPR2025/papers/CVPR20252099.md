## Problem

Current text-guided image editing methods perform a single-state transition from noise to an edited image, which forces the model to simultaneously satisfy source fidelity and complex edit instructions in one denoising pass. This often results in either inaccurate edits or loss of key source content (e.g., text on clothing, background details). The paper asks whether reformulating editing as a continuous temporal process—traversing the natural image manifold through physically plausible intermediate states—can resolve this tension.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1:1 aspect-ratio source image and a free-form text editing prompt. The source image is resized to 480×480 and horizontally padded with black pixels to 720×480 to match CogVideoX's fixed resolution; after video generation, the central 480×480 region of the selected frame is cropped and upscaled to 512×512 for evaluation. The output is a single edited image (one frame from the generated 49-frame video), not the full video sequence.

## Method

Frame2Frame (F2F) is a training-free, three-stage inference pipeline that repurposes a pretrained image-to-video diffusion model (CogVideoX I2V-5B) as an image editor. First, a VLM (ChatGPT-4o) converts the static edit prompt into a Temporal Editing Caption describing how the change should unfold over time, using in-context learning with nine exemplar pairs. Second, the video model generates a 49-frame sequence starting from the source image, guided by the temporal caption, producing a smooth path along the image manifold. Third, a second VLM call (GPT-4o) selects the earliest frame that fully realizes the edit by examining a collage of every fourth frame alongside the source. The key insight is that temporal coherence in video models naturally enforces incremental, physically plausible changes, preserving source attributes that single-shot diffusion editing tends to discard.

## Results

On TEdBench, Frame2Frame (F2F) achieves 0.22 LPIPS, 0.89 CLIP-I (source preservation), and 0.63 CLIP score (edit accuracy), matching or exceeding all baselines across all three metrics. On the newly introduced PosEdit benchmark for human pose editing, F2F substantially outperforms the strongest baseline LEDITS++ with 0.14 vs. 0.26 source LPIPS and 0.82 vs. 0.65 source CLIP-I, while also achieving 0.15 vs. 0.28 target LPIPS against ground-truth pose images. In a 59-participant human survey, F2F receives 65.6% global preference for edit quality (preservation) versus 34.4% for LEDITS++, and 54.1% vs. 45.9% for edit accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F2F outperforms SDEdit and Pix2Pix-ZERO on all three TEdBench metrics, with particularly large margins on LPIPS (0.22 vs. 0.30 and 0.29). Against Imagic, F2F achieves a dramatic LPIPS improvement (0.22 vs. 0.52) while matching its CLIP score of 0.63. Compared to the closest competitor LEDITS++, F2F edges ahead on source preservation (0.22 vs. 0.23 LPIPS, 0.89 vs. 0.87 CLIP-I) while tying on edit accuracy (0.63 CLIP). FlowEdit matches F2F on LPIPS and CLIP-I but trails on CLIP score (0.61 vs. 0.63). On PosEdit, F2F's advantage is far more pronounced: it nearly halves the source LPIPS gap (0.14 vs. 0.26 for LEDITS++) and improves target-image CLIP-I by 0.15 points (0.84 vs. 0.69), reflecting superior identity preservation during pose changes. The human survey confirms these quantitative gains, with F2F winning edit-quality preference by a 31-point margin over LEDITS++ (65.6% vs. 34.4%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of text-based image editing (SDEdit, Imagic, LEDITS++, FlowEdit), which treats editing as a single-step latent-space projection from source to target, by reframing it as a continuous temporal traversal along the natural image manifold using pretrained video diffusion models (CogVideoX). While prior work at the image-video intersection (AnyDoor, MagicFixup, drag-based editing) repurposed video frames for dataset construction, Frame2Frame is the first to use the video generation process itself as the editing mechanism, guided by VLM-generated temporal captions. The manifold-pathway interpretation provides a geometric unification: conventional methods jump to a target point, whereas this method walks a smooth path, preserving intermediate structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PosEdit benchmark fills a gap in pose-editing evaluation by providing ground-truth target images from the same subject, enabling direct identity-preservation metrics that TEdBench lacks. The approach's generalization to denoising, deblurring, and relighting suggests video models' learned temporal dynamics can serve as a general-purpose image transformation engine without task-specific training. Key open questions include reducing the computational overhead of full 49-frame video generation, suppressing unwanted camera motion, and whether fine-tuning video generators specifically for editing (e.g., enforcing static cameras) can close the remaining gap to dedicated image-editing methods in speed and controllability.
