## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video frame interpolation methods struggle to generate sharp, temporally consistent frames under large or nonlinear motion, producing motion blur and artifacts. The authors further reveal that in current diffusion-based VFI pipelines, the diffusion process itself contributes surprisingly little to output quality—decoding random noise yields perceptually similar results to decoding denoised latents—indicating that the latent representation and conditioning mechanisms are the true bottlenecks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB frames (starting and ending) at arbitrary resolutions and produces a single intermediate frame. During training, frames are cropped to 256×448 patches; at inference, the model handles diverse resolutions (e.g., 480×854 on DAVIS, 544×1280 on DAIN-HD544p) via interpolated position embeddings. Notably, only 2 denoising steps are required at inference, yielding a running time of ~0.13–0.25 s per frame on a V100 GPU—comparable to or faster than many optical-flow-based methods.

## Method

EDEN amplifies the diffusion process's impact along three axes: (1) a transformer-based tokenizer (inspired by TiTok) compresses intermediate frames into compact 1D latent tokens using pyramid feature fusion (concatenating large-scale and small-scale tokens before self-attention) and temporal attention that aligns tokens with start/end frame context; (2) a DiT backbone replaces the conventional U-Net, with a dual-stream context integration mechanism that injects start/end frame information via temporal attention after each self-attention layer and a difference embedding (normalized cosine similarity of the frame pair) fed through adaLN to condition on motion magnitude; (3) a rectified-flow training objective (velocity-field prediction) combined with multi-resolution and multi-frame-interval fine-tuning to generalize across resolutions and motion scales. The tokenizer is trained with L1 + perceptual + adversarial + KL losses, while the diffusion model is trained with the standard flow-matching L2 loss.

## Results

EDEN achieves state-of-the-art LPIPS of 0.0874 on DAVIS (a ~9.2% reduction over the previous best diffusion method LBBDM at 0.0963) and 0.1321 on the more dynamic DAIN-HD544p (an ~8% improvement over SGM-VFI and VFIMamba). On SNU-FILM Hard, EDEN reaches LPIPS 0.0986, outperforming all baselines by over 10%. Notably, EDEN requires only 2 denoising steps, yielding a runtime of 0.130s per frame on DAVIS—comparable to optical-flow methods and far faster than LDMVFI (7.0s) or LBBDM (1.7s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDEN outperforms all seven baselines on DAVIS (LPIPS 0.0874 vs. 0.0963 for LBBDM, the prior best diffusion method) and DAIN-HD544p (LPIPS 0.1321 vs. 0.1423 for SGM-VFI and 0.1426 for VFIMamba, both ~8% improvements). On SNU-FILM Hard, EDEN achieves the best LPIPS (0.0986) and FloLPIPS (0.1653), beating LBBDM (0.1101 / 0.1696) by over 10%. The only loss is on SNU-FILM Easy, where LBBDM edges out EDEN (0.0120 vs. 0.0129 LPIPS), likely because the easy subset involves minimal motion where the Brownian bridge formulation has a slight advantage. In terms of speed, EDEN (0.130s on DAVIS) is dramatically faster than LDMVFI (7.0s) and LBBDM (1.7s), and competitive with the fastest optical-flow methods like AMT (0.122s), thanks to requiring only 2 denoising steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDEN builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and the rectified-flow training formulation (Esser et al., ICML 2024), but departs from prior diffusion-based VFI methods (LDMVFI, LBBDM) in three key ways: replacing the standard 2D-convolutional VAE with a transformer-based tokenizer inspired by TiTok, introducing temporal attention throughout both the tokenizer and diffusion transformer, and adding a start-end frame difference embedding as an explicit conditioning signal. The paper's motivating observation—that in existing diffusion-based VFI methods, decoding random noise yields frames nearly indistinguishable from those decoded after full denoising—positions EDEN as a corrective that amplifies the diffusion process's actual contribution rather than treating it as a nominal component. On the non-diffusion side, EDEN competes with and surpasses recent optical-flow and state-space-model approaches (SGM-VFI, VFIMamba) that were specifically designed for large-motion scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that prior diffusion-based VFI methods derive almost no benefit from the denoising process is a useful diagnostic for the field, suggesting that simply applying a diffusion backbone to a VFI pipeline is insufficient without architectural changes that make the latent space and conditioning genuinely informative. EDEN's practical advantage—achieving SOTA perceptual quality with only 2 denoising steps and competitive inference speed—makes it a strong candidate for real-time applications where prior diffusion methods were too slow. The acknowledged limitation on fine-detail blurring (e.g., text) and the reliance on a fixed latent dimension (16) that trades reconstruction quality for generative tractability point to open questions about whether richer latent spaces or higher-fidelity decoders can close the remaining gap without sacrificing the efficiency gains.
