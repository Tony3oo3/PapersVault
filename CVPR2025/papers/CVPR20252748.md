## Problem

Single-image 3D face reconstruction under occlusion is inherently multi-hypothesis: occluded regions can correspond to many equally valid face shapes, yet most existing methods are deterministic and output a single solution. The few approaches that attempt diverse sampling (e.g., Diverse3D with Determinantal Point Processes) produce unrealistic and exaggerated reconstructions because they do not model the underlying data distribution. Additionally, no dedicated evaluation protocol existed for quantitatively assessing expressive face reconstruction under occlusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252748/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image of an occluded face (no specific resolution constraint stated; evaluated on frontal views from CoMA and in-the-wild images) and produces N=100 3D face meshes, each combining a single selected FLAME shape (300-dim coefficients) with a distinct FLAME expression (50-dim coefficients). The input image is consumed through two frozen-then-fine-tuned face recognition encoders (ArcFace for 512-dim identity features and FaRL for 1024-dim expression features), and the output is structured as a set of diverse expression variants applied to one consistent identity shape.

## Method

Ofer employs three components: (1) IdGen, a 1D U-Net DDPM conditioned on the ArcFace embedding that denoises 300-dim Gaussian noise into FLAME shape coefficients, generating N=100 plausible neutral shapes; (2) IdRank, a 5-layer MLP that scores each shape candidate by comparing zero-centered residual frontal meshes against the set mean, trained with a cross-entropy loss between predicted softmax scores and softmax-transformed ground-truth L1 reconstruction errors, selecting the top-ranked shape for identity consistency; and (3) ExpGen, a second 1D U-Net DDPM conditioned on concatenated ArcFace+FaRL features that generates diverse 50-dim FLAME expression coefficients. The key insight is that diffusion models naturally capture the multi-modal solution distribution, while the ranking mechanism—novel in the context of diffusion model output selection—resolves the identity ambiguity by filtering to the most image-consistent shape before applying expression diversity.

## Results

On the NoW validation benchmark, OFER-rank achieves a median MSE of 0.98 (mean 1.21) on the full set, outperforming FOCUS (1.05/1.31), Diverse3D (1.41/1.78), and DECA (1.17/1.46). The best single sample from OFER's 100 generated shapes (OFER-min) reaches 0.81/1.05, competitive with TokenFace (0.79/0.99) and MICA-8DS (0.90/1.11). On the CO-545 expression reconstruction benchmark, OFER achieves a CSE of 0.17 and ODE of 0.01, substantially outperforming Diverse3D (CSE 0.30, ODE 0.4). On the Erakiotan hard-occlusion dataset, OFER's ODE scores (0.001–0.003) are two orders of magnitude lower than Diverse3D's (0.34–0.95), confirming far more plausible expression generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against occlusion-specific baselines, OFER-rank clearly dominates: it reduces median MSE from 1.41 (Diverse3D) to 0.98 and from 1.05 (FOCUS) to 0.98 on the full NoW set, with a 45% reduction in standard deviation over Diverse3D (1.01 vs 1.52). On the CO-545 expression benchmark, OFER halves the CSE (0.17 vs 0.30) and reduces ODE by 40× (0.01 vs 0.4) compared to Diverse3D, indicating far more plausible and accurate diverse reconstructions. Against standard (non-occlusion) methods, OFER-rank is slightly behind TokenFace (0.98 vs 0.79 median MSE) and MICA-8DS (0.98 vs 0.90), but OFER-min (0.81) is competitive with both, and these baselines do not handle occlusion or generate diverse expressions. On the Erakiotan hard-occlusion dataset, OFER's ODE scores (0.001–0.003) are roughly 100× lower than Diverse3D's (0.34–0.95), though Diverse3D achieves higher STD-S diversity (11.81 vs 34.04 for masks), suggesting OFER trades some raw diversity for much greater plausibility. EMOCA is compared qualitatively only; the paper notes it produces degenerate faces under hard occlusion, whereas OFER maintains coherent geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

OFER builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) as its generative backbone and follows MICA's (Zielonka et al., 2022) training strategy of using a small paired 2D-3D dataset with a frozen-then-fine-tuned ArcFace encoder. It departs from deterministic occlusion-aware methods (FOCUS, Egger et al.) that produce a single reconstruction, and from Diverse3D (Dey et al., 2022) which used Determinantal Point Processes for diversity—arguing that DPP does not capture the underlying facial geometry distribution. The key conceptual departure is introducing a learning-to-rank selection step over diffusion model outputs, a mechanism the authors note has not been explored in the diffusion literature before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: prior occlusion-aware methods either ignore the multi-hypothesis nature of the problem or produce implausible diverse samples, and no standard benchmark existed for evaluating expressive faces under occlusion. The CO-545 dataset and the ranking-as-selection paradigm for diffusion models are both reusable contributions. However, the evaluation is limited in scale (545 validation images, one primary baseline), the ranking network is a simple MLP with softmax that the authors themselves acknowledge is suboptimal for large candidate sets, and the training data is a small subset of available 3D supervision—leaving open whether the approach scales to richer expression spaces or more diverse occlusion types.
