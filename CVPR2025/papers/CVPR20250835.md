## Problem

Sparsely-supervised 3D object detectors (e.g., CoIn, SS3D) degrade sharply when annotation rates drop below ~2%, because the initial detector lacks sufficient feature discrimination to learn from so few labeled instances. Existing label-efficient strategies do not provide a mechanism to bootstrap the detector's representational capacity before fine-tuning on the scarce ground-truth labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250835/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB images, LiDAR point clouds, category-text prompts (e.g., 'car', 'pedestrian', 'cyclist'), and camera intrinsic/extrinsic calibration matrices. It first produces 3D bounding box pseudo-labels (center, dimensions, heading) via 2D-to-3D semantic mask transfer and dynamic clustering, which are then used to train a standard 3D detector (VoxelRCNN, CenterPoint, or CasA). The final output is a fine-tuned 3D detector that emits 3D bounding box detections at inference time; pseudo-label generation is confined to an 8-meter radius around semantically relevant points to limit computation.

## Method

SP3D is a two-stage boosting strategy. In stage one, a Confident Points Semantic Transfer (CPST) module runs FastSAM for class-agnostic segmentation and SemanticSAM for per-mask text descriptions, filters foreground masks by cosine similarity against category-text prompts, then applies a boundary-constrained mask shrink (factor γ=0.3) to discard edge-ambiguous pixels before projecting the shrunk masks onto LiDAR points via camera calibration, yielding clean semantic seed points. A Dynamic Cluster Pseudo-label Generation (DCPG) module then iterates over each seed point with a linearly increasing DBSCAN radius (Eq. 4) to produce multi-scale foreground clusters, fitting a bounding box to each. A Distribution Shape score (DS score) combines a Gaussian-fit distribution constraint on interior-point-to-boundary distances with a KL-divergence meta-shape constraint against per-category size templates, and is used as the confidence score in NMS to suppress low-quality proposals. The surviving pseudo-labels train the detector via CoIn's contrastive instance feature mining; in stage two, the detector is fine-tuned on the sparse ground-truth split (2% or less of instances).

## Results

On KITTI val split at 2% annotation cost, SP3D-initialized CoIn++ achieves 91.3/80.5/74.0 AP (Easy/Mod/Hard) for Car, 67.4/58.7/50.9 for Pedestrian, and 92.5/73.1/68.3 for Cyclist, substantially closing the gap to the fully-supervised VoxelRCNN (92.3/84.9/82.6 Car). On Waymo Open Dataset at 1% annotation cost, SP3D boosts CoIn by +7.1 mAP and +7.0 mAPH for Vehicle Level-1, and +6.2/+6.3 for Level-2. In the zero-shot setting on KITTI, SP3D reaches 69.71 Car-3D AP@IoU 0.7 (Easy), far exceeding VS3D (9.09) and WS3DPR (60.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CoIn++ (the strongest sparsely-supervised baseline), SP3D delivers dramatic gains on Pedestrian (+20.7 Easy, +22.6 Mod, +19.7 Hard AP) and Cyclist (+10.5 Easy, +14.7 Mod, +13.7 Hard AP), while Car performance remains competitive (−0.7 Easy, +1.0 Mod, +2.5 Hard). Compared to CoIn without the ++ enhancement, SP3D improves Car Hard AP by +18.4 and Ped Easy by +16.6. On Waymo, SP3D outperforms CoIn by +7.1 mAP (Vehicle L1) and +6.2 mAP (Vehicle L2) at 1% annotation cost. In the zero-shot setting, SP3D surpasses VS3D by +60.6 Car-3D AP@IoU 0.7 (Easy) and WS3DPR by +9.7 on KITTI, and beats SAM3D by +15.9 and CM3D by +11.3 Vehicle mAP on WOD. At extremely low annotation rates (0.5%), SP3D still provides +10.6 average AP over CoIn, confirming robustness as labels become scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

SP3D builds directly on CoIn's contrastive instance feature mining framework for sparsely-supervised 3D detection, extending it with a two-stage training strategy that pre-trains the detector on LMM-generated pseudo-labels before fine-tuning on sparse ground truth. It departs from prior cross-modal transfer methods (PointPainting, MixSup, SAL) by explicitly transferring boundary-constrained semantic masks onto point clouds rather than implicitly embedding 2D features into 3D representations, thereby avoiding semantic confusion at instance edges. The DCPG module adapts DBSCAN-based clustering from unsupervised detection (CPD) with a dynamic radius schedule, and the DS score introduces a novel GT-free quality metric combining Gaussian distribution priors and meta-shape KL divergence for pseudo-label filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SP3D addresses a practical bottleneck in label-efficient 3D detection: the initial detector's weak feature discrimination under extremely sparse annotations, which cascades into poor downstream performance. The modular design (CPST → DCPG → DS score) can be plugged into diverse architectures (CenterPoint, VoxelRCNN, CasA) and supervision paradigms (semi-supervised, sparsely-supervised, unsupervised), making it broadly applicable. The zero-shot results substantially outperform SAM3D and CM3D, suggesting that careful geometric filtering of LMM outputs is more effective than direct mask-to-box fitting. A key open question is the performance cliff below 0.1% annotation rate, where the authors acknowledge significant degradation, and whether more robust fine-tuning strategies can close this gap.
