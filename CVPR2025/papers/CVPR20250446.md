## Problem

Existing text-driven human-object interaction (HOI) generation methods embed full-body poses as tokens and process them with Transformers, modeling skeletal joints implicitly in latent pose space. This implicit formulation obscures the geometric and semantic relationships between individual joints and fails to capture the biomechanical coordination required for realistic interactions, such as the coordinated motion of shoulder-elbow-wrist during grasping. The paper argues that explicitly modeling interactions at both the joint level and the kinetic chain level is more natural and effective for generating physically plausible HOIs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text description (encoded by a frozen CLIP model) and a static object geometry represented as a point cloud (encoded by PointNet) as inputs. The output is a 3D HOI sequence of length L in a joint-level representation m ∈ R^{L×(J+2)×12}, where each frame encodes local joint positions, velocities, and 6D rotations in root space, root angular/linear velocity and height, binary foot-ground contact features, and the object's 6-DoF pose (global rotation and translation). The representation is designed to be convertible to the HOI-Diff format for fair comparison.

## Method

ChainHOI is a diffusion-based generator with N identical blocks, each containing a Generative Spatiotemporal GCN (GST-GCN) and a Kinematics-based Interaction Module (KIM). The GST-GCN operates on a novel HOI joint graph that adds an object node connected only to eight potential interaction joints (pelvis, neck, feet, shoulders, hands) plus a foot-contact node; it combines ST-GCN for short-term spatiotemporal modeling with a Semantic-consistent Module (a Transformer decoder using text and object geometry as keys/values) for long-term semantic coherence. The KIM introduces six learnable kinetic chain tokens (five internal body chains plus one human-object interaction chain) that first pass through a Context-aware Decoder to encode text and object geometry, then through a Kinematic-aware Decoder where a dedicated attention mask restricts each token to attend only to joints within its own chain, enabling explicit intra- and inter-chain coordination. Training minimizes a composite loss combining the standard diffusion denoising loss, a contact loss penalizing the minimum distance between predicted interaction joints and the GT object mesh (weighted by GT contact labels), and an L2 loss on the object's 6-DoF pose.

## Results

On the BEHAVE dataset, ChainHOI achieves a FID of 0.095, R-Precision Top-1 of 0.435, OCD of 0.091, and FSR of 0.063, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On OMOMO, ChainHOI reaches FID 0.112, R-Top1 0.264, OCD 0.263, and FSR 0.089, again leading all compared methods. Inference time is 0.61 s per sample (vs. 3.61 s for HOI-Diff and 5.80 s for CHOIS), making it the fastest method evaluated. With the AIC post-processing, OCD further drops to 0.072 (BEHAVE) and 0.120 (OMOMO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChainHOI dominates all five baselines on both datasets across every reported metric. On BEHAVE, it reduces FID from 0.157 (CHOIS*, the next best) to 0.095 and from 0.437 (HOI-Diff+AIC) to 0.095, while improving R-Top1 from 0.312 to 0.435 and OCD from 0.101 to 0.091. On OMOMO, the FID gap is even larger: 0.112 vs. 0.169 (MDM*) and 0.245 (HOI-Diff+AIC), and R-Top1 jumps from 0.140 (HOI-Diff+AIC) to 0.264. The text-driven motion baselines (MDM*, PriorMDM*) perform well on human motion quality but are far worse on interaction metrics (OCD 0.458–0.588 vs. 0.091 for ChainHOI on BEHAVE). HOI-Diff, the closest HOI-specific competitor, is outperformed on FID by a factor of ~4.8× (0.457 vs. 0.095) and on R-Top1 by 0.14, while ChainHOI is ~6× faster in inference (0.61 s vs. 3.61 s). The only metric where a baseline is competitive is PS on OMOMO (InterDiff 0.015 vs. ChainHOI 0.019), but ChainHOI still leads on all other interaction and motion-quality measures.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChainHOI departs from the dominant paradigm in text-driven HOI generation (HOI-Diff, InterDiff, CHOIS, OMOMO), which embeds full-body poses as flat tokens processed by Transformers, implicitly modeling joint interactions in latent pose space. Instead, it explicitly represents the human skeleton as a joint graph with a dedicated object node and selective edges to eight potential interaction joints, processed by a Generative Spatiotemporal GCN (GST-GCN) built on ST-GCN. A second, novel contribution is the Kinematics-based Interaction Module (KIM), which uses learnable kinetic-chain tokens with DETR-style Transformer decoders and attention masks to model intra- and inter-chain coordination, drawing on the object-query formulation from DETR but repurposing it for kinematic chains rather than detection boxes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit joint- and kinetic-chain-level modeling offers a more interpretable and biomechanically grounded alternative to implicit pose-space modeling, and the 0.61 s inference time (vs. 3.61 s for HOI-Diff and 16.5 s for InterDiff) makes the approach practical for interactive applications. However, evaluation is limited to two datasets (BEHAVE, OMOMO), the contact loss is a simplified joint-to-mesh distance rather than a true SDF, and the OCD metric relies on ChatGPT for ground-truth contact labeling, introducing potential bias. Open questions include whether the kinetic-chain decomposition generalizes to multi-object or multi-person interactions, and whether the explicit graph structure can be scaled to higher-fidelity body models.
