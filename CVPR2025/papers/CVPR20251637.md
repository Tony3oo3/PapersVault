## Problem

Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models incur high computational cost due to their iterative denoising process, and existing cache-based acceleration methods reuse intermediate features from prior timesteps without any correction, risking quality degradation. No prior work has addressed how to calibrate cached values in a training-free manner to recover accuracy lost by skipping computations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251637/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained DiT-XL/2 (class-conditional, 256×256 ImageNet) and PixArt-α (text-to-image, MSCOCO-2014), taking a class label or text prompt plus Gaussian noise as input and producing a generated image. It is entirely training-free: calibration parameters are extracted from the pre-trained model itself using only 256 calibration images in a few minutes on a single GPU, and the method is compatible with any existing caching pattern (e.g., FORA's fixed-period gather/scatter) and sampler (DDIM, DPM-Solver).

## Method

Increment-calibrated caching (ICC) corrects cached layer outputs by adding a low-rank increment term: y ≈ C(y) + W_r^a W_r^b (x_m − C(x)), where W_r^a and W_r^b are rank-r SVD factors of each linear layer's weight matrix, reducing per-layer complexity from O(NC_iC_o) to O(N(C_i+C_o)r). Rather than using SVD to compress the model (which degrades quality), the SVD factors serve as calibration parameters that guide cached values toward accurate results. To handle outlier activations in DiT, the paper introduces channel-aware SVD (CA-SVD and CD-SVD), which scales the weight matrix by per-channel activation magnitudes or inter-timestep deltas before performing SVD, so that sensitive channels receive higher reconstruction priority. The calibration parameters are derived in a training-free manner from the pre-trained model, and the method is orthogonal to the choice of caching schedule.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-XL/2, ICC(CD-SVD, r=192, p=2) achieves IS=253.0 and FID=2.28 at ~7.0T MACs, outperforming naive caching (IS=240.0, FID=2.48) and 30-step DDIM (IS=235.0, FID=2.64) at the same compute budget. Under the tightest budget (~2.37T MACs), ICC(CD-SVD) reaches IS=217.6 and FID=5.05, surpassing naive caching by 22 IS points and 1.68 FID. On MSCOCO-2014 with PixArt-α, ICC(CA-SVD, r=64, p=2) reduces FID-30k from 25.47 to 13.94 at ~2.78T MACs while improving Clip Score from 29.68 to 30.29. Overall, the method eliminates >45% computation relative to 35-step DDIM while improving IS by 12 at <0.06 FID cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FORA (naive caching with fixed period p), ICC consistently wins across all compute budgets: at ~7.1T MACs, ICC(CD-SVD) gains +13 IS and −0.20 FID over p=2 naive caching; at ~2.37T MACs the gap widens to +22 IS and −1.68 FID. Against L2C (learning-based fine-grained caching), ICC(CD-SVD, r=192, p=2) matches FID (2.28 vs. 2.27) while using 23% fewer MACs (6.98T vs. 9.04T) and achieving +8.9 IS (253.0 vs. 244.1), all without any training. Compared to 35-step DDIM, ICC with 30 steps delivers +12 IS at <0.06 FID cost and 45% fewer MACs, yielding a theoretical 1.8× speedup. For text-to-image generation against DPM-Solver, ICC(CA-SVD) at 18 steps reduces FID-30k from 25.47 (naive caching at 20 steps) to 13.94 while improving Clip Score from 29.68 to 30.29 at the same ~2.78T MAC budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of DiT acceleration research: cache-based methods (FORA, L2C, DeepCache, TokenCache) that exploit temporal similarity between denoising steps, and low-rank approximation via SVD (FWSVD, ASVD, SVD-LLM) that compresses weight matrices. The key conceptual departure is repurposing SVD: rather than using truncated singular values to replace the full model (which the paper shows catastrophically degrades DiT generation), the low-rank factors serve as lightweight calibration parameters that correct cached activations toward their true values. The channel-aware SVD variant extends ASVD's activation-magnitude scaling by additionally incorporating inter-timestep activation differences (CD-SVD), specifically targeting the outlier channels observed in DiT's FFN and attention projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature and minimal deployment overhead (calibration parameters obtained in minutes on a single GPU) make this practically attractive for DiT deployment, where retraining is often infeasible. The 45% computation reduction with <0.06 FID increase over 35-step DDIM is a meaningful efficiency gain, though it operates within the already-crowded caching-acceleration space. Open questions include whether the non-monotonic rank behavior (quality degrading at higher ranks) reflects a fundamental limitation of the calibration formulation, whether the approach generalizes to video DiTs or larger models, and whether combining with fine-grained caching patterns (beyond FORA's coarse period-based scheme) would yield further gains. The paper does not explore interaction with distillation or fast-sampler methods, leaving the optimal combination strategy unclear.
