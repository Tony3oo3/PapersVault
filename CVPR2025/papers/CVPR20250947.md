## Problem

Person ReID models can track specific individuals across cameras, creating serious privacy risks, yet no method exists to selectively unlearn specific persons from a ReID model while preserving its ability to re-identify others. Unlike face recognition where machine unlearning has been explored, the ReID setting is uniquely challenging because the model must destroy cross-view matching for target persons without collapsing the identity-discriminative feature space that serves all other persons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250947/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT-B ReID model, a labeled image set of M_T unlearned persons (with identity annotations), and an unlabeled image set of accessible persons, all at 256×128 resolution. It produces a fine-tuned ReID model whose feature embeddings place unlearned persons in a variation-sensitive region of the feature space (so cross-view matching fails) while keeping accessible persons in a view-invariant region. Evaluation uses Rank-1 accuracy separately on unlearned and accessible persons, plus a combined H-Mean metric that balances both.

## Method

The core mechanism is variation-guided identity shift (VIS), an asymmetric contrastive objective built on RandAugment-style augmentations. For unlearned persons, the model is trained to push an image and its augmented version apart in feature space, biasing the encoder toward environmental/style cues (brightness, color tone) that differ across views, thereby shifting those persons out of the view-invariant subspace. For accessible persons, the model pulls an image and its augmented version together, reinforcing view-invariant identity features. Two relation regularizers complement VIS: a triplet constraint (TRC) that ensures the nearest accessible-person neighbors surround each unlearned-person image, and a relation-consistent regularization (RCR) that preserves the pairwise similarity structure of accessible persons relative to the original pre-trained model, preventing feature-space collapse during fine-tuning.

## Results

On MSMT17 (M_T=25), the proposed method achieves an H-Mean of 76.3% with R-1_T of 4.6% (unlearned persons) and R-1_O of 77.0% (accessible persons), substantially outperforming all baselines. On Market-1501 (M_T=25), H-Mean reaches 84.4% with R-1_T of 10.7% and R-1_O of 91.1%. The method also benefits from Re-Ranking (k-reciprocal encoding), improving H-Mean to 77.3% on MSMT17 and 87.4% on Market-1501, whereas most baselines degrade after re-ranking, indicating residual re-identifiability of unlearned persons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on H-Mean across every dataset and M_T setting. LIRF [53] is the closest competitor (66.2% H-Mean at M_T=25 on MSMT17) but sacrifices accessible-person ReID accuracy (R-1_O drops to 62.5% vs. 77.0%), because its attention-map constraint damages identity-discriminative features. SCRUB [24] fails to effectively unlearn (R-1_T remains at 43.8%), yielding only 49.1% H-Mean. GS-LoRA [58] preserves ReID well (R-1_O=77.5%) but barely reduces unlearned-person retrievability (R-1_T=56.9%), resulting in 35.9% H-Mean. Boundary Shrink [8] shifts the classification decision boundary but leaves the feature representation largely intact, achieving the worst H-Mean (27.3%). Notably, existing baselines degrade after Re-Ranking (e.g., SCRUB's R-1_T rises from 43.8% to 52.3%), revealing residual re-identifiability, while the proposed method improves with Re-Ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a genuinely new problem space at the intersection of person ReID and machine unlearning, explicitly positioning itself as the first work to formalize 'De-ReID' — unlearning specific persons from a ReID model while preserving matching ability for others. It departs from prior privacy-preserving ReID work (which anonymizes images or uses alternative sensors like event cameras) by targeting the model's feature space directly rather than the input data. Conceptually, it repurposes the machine unlearning literature (SCRUB, LIRF, GS-LoRA, Boundary Shrink) but argues these classification-oriented methods are fundamentally mismatched to ReID's metric-learning objective, motivating the variation-guided identity shift mechanism that treats cross-view augmentation asymmetrically per person group.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically urgent gap: surveillance deployments (community cameras, workplace monitoring, smart homes) need models that can be scoped to exclude specific individuals, a requirement implied by GDPR but previously unaddressed for ReID. The H-Mean evaluation metric and the new benchmark protocol (splitting unlearned persons across training/testing cameras) provide a reusable framework for future work. Open questions include scalability to thousands of unlearned persons, extension to video ReID and other matching modalities (gait, face), and whether the view-sensitivity shift can be certified or verified post-hoc. A limitation is that the method requires labeled images of unlearned persons in the fine-tuning set, which may not always be available in practice.
