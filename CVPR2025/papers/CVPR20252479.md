## Problem

Diffusion-based shadow generation methods such as SGDiffusion produce realistic shadow appearance (intensity, boundary transitions) but fail to generate geometrically plausible shadows—correct location, scale, and shape—especially in complex scenes with diverse poses or illumination directions. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly injecting geometry priors into the diffusion process to constrain shadow placement and morphology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252479/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a composite RGB image (foreground object already inserted into a background) and a binary foreground object mask as input, concatenated channel-wise. The geometry encoder operates at 256×256 resolution while the diffusion model runs at 512×512. The output is a full composite image with a realistic foreground shadow rendered in the appropriate region; a post-processing network (reused from SGDiffusion) preserves background information in the final result.

## Method

GPSDiffusion is a two-stage pipeline. In stage 1, a multi-task geometry encoder predicts (a) a rotated bounding box for the foreground shadow via regression coefficients from the object's rotated box (supervised with KFIoU loss) and (b) matched shadow shapes by retrieving top-K=64 prototypical embeddings from N=256 K-means clusters learned by a shape auto-encoder trained on 64×64 shadow crops. In stage 2, the rotated bounding box is converted to a binary mask and concatenated with the composite image and foreground mask as ControlNet encoder input, while the retrieved shape embeddings are injected into the denoising U-Net through additional cross-attention layers (initialized from the text-embedding cross-attention weights, following IP-Adapter). The geometry encoder is trained first and then frozen; the control encoder and cross-attention layers are jointly fine-tuned for 80 epochs at 512×512 with a standard diffusion noise-prediction loss conditioned on the geometry priors.

## Results

On the DESOBAv2 dataset, GPSDiffusion achieves the best Global RMSE (5.896 vs. 6.098 for SGDiffusion), Local RMSE (46.713 vs. 53.611), Global BER (0.213 vs. 0.245), and Local BER (0.423 vs. 0.487) on BOS test images. The advantage is even more pronounced on BOS-free images, where GPSDiffusion leads on all six metrics, including Global RMSE (13.809 vs. 15.110), Local SSIM (0.166 vs. 0.117), and Local BER (0.384 vs. 0.452) compared to SGDiffusion. The only metric where GPSDiffusion does not lead is Global SSIM on BOS images (0.966 vs. 0.971 for SGDiffusion), a negligible difference. A user study with 50 raters using B-T scores confirms GPSDiffusion produces the most visually plausible shadows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPSDiffusion outperforms the closest diffusion-based baseline SGDiffusion [20] on 11 out of 12 metrics, with the sole exception being Global SSIM on BOS images (0.966 vs. 0.971). The most substantial gains over SGDiffusion appear in shadow mask accuracy: Local BER drops from 0.487 to 0.423 (BOS) and from 0.452 to 0.384 (BOS-free), and Local SSIM improves from 0.370 to 0.374 (BOS) and from 0.117 to 0.166 (BOS-free), confirming better geometric alignment. Against GAN-based baselines, GPSDiffusion dominates across the board: compared to SGRNet [11], it reduces Local RMSE from 68.255 to 46.713 (BOS) and from 60.350 to 55.616 (BOS-free), and reduces Local BER from 0.596 to 0.423 (BOS). DMASNet [37], which also predicts bounding boxes, is outperformed on all metrics; GPSDiffusion further surpasses DMASNet in rotated bounding box regression (KFIoU 0.225 vs. 0.203). On real composite images (DESOBA backgrounds), GPSDiffusion trained solely on DESOBAv2 without fine-tuning still produces the most realistic shadows in a 50-rater user study, outperforming all baselines including SGDiffusion which was fine-tuned on DESOBA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SGDiffusion (Liu et al., CVPR 2024), which first adapted ControlNet to shadow generation, by injecting explicit geometry priors—rotated bounding box regression and discretized shape embeddings—into the diffusion pipeline. The rotated bounding box formulation departs from DMASNet's axis-aligned box prediction, and the shape retrieval via K-means clustering of auto-encoder bottleneck features replaces direct shape regression. The cross-attention mechanism for injecting shape embeddings follows the IP-Adapter paradigm, repurposing text-conditioning layers for geometric conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that diffusion-based shadow generation, while superior in appearance realism, can be meaningfully improved in geometric accuracy through structured priors, addressing a clear gap identified in SGDiffusion. The two-stage design (geometry encoder + guided diffusion) is modular and could potentially transfer to other composition sub-tasks like reflection or contact shadow generation. Limitations include reliance on paired training data, the discretization of continuous shapes into 256 clusters (which may lose fine-grained shape variation), and evaluation limited to a single dataset (DESOBAv2) plus a small set of real composites. Whether the geometry-prior paradigm generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes or arbitrary camera/lighting configurations remains open.
