## Problem

The Segment Anything Model (SAM) has revolutionized visual segmentation but remains unexplored for referring audio-visual segmentation (Ref-AVS), where multimodal cues embedded in text expressions must guide segmentation without explicit user-interactive prompts. SAM's single-frame image encoder also lacks temporal awareness, limiting its ability to capture the spatio-temporal interactions essential for multi-frame audio-visual scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250296/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TSAM consumes n video frames sampled at 1-second intervals (1024×1024 RGB), a temporally aligned audio clip encoded offline via VGGish into n×128 representations, and a reference text expression encoded via RoBERTa into l×768 tokens. Both audio and text are projected into SAM's 256-dimensional visual embedding space and temporally expanded along n frames before fusion. The output is a binary segmentation mask per frame, with the model also required to produce empty masks on Null test cases where the referenced object is absent.

## Method

TSAM augments SAM's frozen ViT-B image encoder with a trainable temporal modeling branch of M=4 blocks appended at the encoder's final layers, where each block integrates a cached memory module (tracking evolving audio-visual context) and a bottleneck adapter module (injecting text-refined audio cues) to enable spatio-temporal multimodal fusion. A dual-pathway prompting framework replaces SAM's user-interactive prompts: the Sparse Prompting Module selects the top-k=5 most relevant audio queries via language-guided selection (max inner product with text cues) and fuses them with visual cues through cross-attention, while the Dense Prompting Module sequentially applies audio and text cross-attention layers to produce spatially comprehensive embeddings. Both sparse and dense prompts feed SAM's mask decoder, which remains largely unchanged. Training is supervised with a combined BCE + IoU loss (λ=1.0) over 15 epochs, preserving SAM's pre-trained segmentation knowledge while adapting the temporal branch and prompting modules for Ref-AVS.

## Results

On the Ref-AVS dataset, TSAM achieves J=43.43% and F=0.568 on the Seen test set, and J=54.58% and F=0.664 on the Unseen test set, outperforming the strongest baseline EEMC by 9.23 percentage points in J and 5.5 percentage points in F on Seen, and by 5.04 and 1.6 percentage points respectively on Unseen. TSAM also achieves the lowest Null S score (0.017) among SAM-based methods, though slightly behind EEMC (0.007). The method demonstrates strong generalization, with the Unseen J score (54.58%) exceeding the Seen J score (43.43%), indicating robustness to novel object categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSAM outperforms all seven baselines on both Seen and Unseen J and F metrics. Against the primary Ref-AVS baseline EEMC [36], TSAM gains 9.23% in J and 5.5% in F on Seen, and 5.04% in J and 1.6% in F on Unseen, though it trails slightly on the Null set (S=0.017 vs. 0.007), attributed to SAM's tendency to always produce a mask. Among SAM-based AVS methods, SAMA [24] (J=39.22% Seen) and GAVS [35] (J=28.93% Seen) both underperform TSAM substantially, with the paper attributing SAMA's weakness to superficial modality integration and GAVS's to insufficient multimodal alignment. Non-SAM methods (AVSBench, AVSegFormer, ReferFormer, R2-VOS) lag further behind, with the best non-SAM method (AVSegFormer) achieving only J=33.47% on Seen versus TSAM's 43.43%. The results confirm that SAM's pre-trained segmentation knowledge, combined with TSAM's temporal branch and dual-pathway multimodal prompting, provides a clear advantage over both prior Ref-AVS and single-modality approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSAM extends the Segment Anything Model (SAM) to the Ref-AVS task by adding a trainable temporal modeling branch atop SAM's frozen ViT image encoder and replacing SAM's user-interactive prompts with data-driven sparse and dense multimodal prompts derived from audio and text. It departs from prior SAM-based AVS methods (AV-SAM, SAMA, GAVS) that fuse modalities only in the spatial domain, and from Ref-VOS methods that lack audio cues, by jointly modeling temporal dynamics and cross-modal interactions. The primary baseline, EEMC [36], uses a Mask2Former decoder without leveraging SAM's pre-trained segmentation knowledge or its promptable architecture, which TSAM explicitly addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSAM demonstrates that SAM's pre-trained segmentation knowledge can be preserved and extended to video via a lightweight temporal branch, offering a template for adapting frozen foundation models to temporally dynamic multimodal tasks. The ablation showing a 7.24% Jaccard drop without the temporal branch underscores that spatial-only multimodal fusion is insufficient for video segmentation. A notable limitation is that SAM's architecture always produces a mask, slightly hurting Null-set performance compared to EEMC, and the authors acknowledge that comparison with SAM2 (which has native temporal memory) remains open. The approach could inform future work on extending other frozen foundation models to video or multi-modal settings.
