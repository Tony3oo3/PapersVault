## Problem

Transformer-based super-resolution methods typically restrict attention to content-agnostic local windows or axial stripes, preventing the capture of long-range dependencies that are critical for reconstructing repetitive textures. Clustering-based alternatives like SPIN address this by grouping similar tokens, but they propagate information only through sparse cluster centers (yielding coarse approximations) and require iterative clustering at inference, severely limiting speed. The paper asks how to perform content-aware long-range token aggregation that is both fine-grained and fast enough for lightweight deployment.

## Input / Output

The network accepts a single low-resolution RGB image and outputs a super-resolved high-resolution image at scale factors ×2, ×3, or ×4. The LR image is first mapped to a high-dimensional feature space via a 3×3 convolution, processed through K residual groups, and the final SR image is produced by adding a learned global residual (from a 3×3 conv + pixel-shuffle reconstruction head) to the bicubically upsampled LR input. The L-variant model contains only 477K–550K parameters and runs in ~86 ms on an RTX 4090 for a 256×256 input.

## Method

CATANet's core is the Token-Aggregation Block (TAB), which combines three mechanisms. First, the Content-Aware Token Aggregation (CATA) module maintains M shared token centers (initialized by average pooling over regular regions) that are updated exclusively during training via Exponential Moving Average (λ=0.999); at inference, each image token is simply assigned to its nearest center by cosine similarity, eliminating iterative clustering cost. The resulting variable-size groups are split into fixed-size subgroups to improve GPU parallelism. Second, Intra-Group Self-Attention (IASA) performs multi-head self-attention within each subgroup, with each subgroup's Q attending to the K and V of two consecutive subgroups to recover tokens split across boundaries. Third, Inter-Group Cross-Attention (IRCA) applies cross-attention between each subgroup and the M token centers to inject global prior information at negligible cost (M≪N). Each residual group also includes a Local-Region Self-Attention (LRSA) module using overlapping patches for fine local detail, followed by a 3×3 convolution for positional embedding and feature refinement.

## Results

CATANet achieves state-of-the-art performance among lightweight SR methods across all five benchmark datasets (Set5, Set14, B100, Urban100, Manga109) at scales ×2, ×3, and ×4. At ×4, CATANet reaches 32.58 dB on Set5, 28.90 dB on Set14, 27.75 dB on B100, 26.87 dB on Urban100, and 31.31 dB on Manga109, outperforming the closest competitor SPIN by up to 0.33 dB (0.60 dB with self-ensemble). The model uses only 535K parameters and 46.8G Multi-Adds, and runs in 86 ms on an RTX 4090—approximately 5× faster than SPIN (435 ms) and 2× faster than SwinIR-light (158 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CATANet outperforms all 19 compared baselines on every dataset and scale. Against the primary cluster-based competitor SPIN [56], CATANet gains 0.33 dB on Urban100 (×4: 26.87 vs 26.55) and 0.33 dB on Manga109 (31.31 vs 30.98) without self-ensemble, and up to 0.60 dB with self-ensemble, while using 20K fewer parameters and running ~5× faster (86 ms vs 435 ms). Among CNN-based methods, CATANet surpasses the strongest CNN baseline OSFFNet [48] by 0.19 dB on Set5 and 0.47 dB on Urban100 at ×4. Among transformer-based methods, CATANet beats SwinIR-light [24] by 0.14 dB on Set5 and 0.40 dB on Urban100, and ELAN-light [59] by 0.15 dB on Set5 and 0.33 dB on Urban100. In the model-size-matched comparison (Table 7, ×2), CATANet-L (477K) outperforms ATD-L (484K) by 0.05–0.14 dB, CATANet-M (306K) beats DITN-Tiny (367K) by 0.17–0.59 dB with fewer parameters, and CATANet-S (230K) surpasses SeemoRe-T (220K, trained on DF2K) by up to 0.28 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

CATANet sits at the intersection of token clustering for efficient transformers and lightweight image super-resolution. It departs from SPIN's approach of using cluster centers as sparse proxies in attention by instead forming content-aware token groups and performing full self-attention within each group, enabling finer-grained long-range interaction. The shared token centers with EMA updates draw directly from the Routing Transformer paradigm, while the Local-Region Self-Attention module adapts the overlapping-patch design from HPINet. A key architectural departure is that token centers are frozen at inference (updated only during training), eliminating the iterative clustering cost that slows down SPIN and BOAT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving both better reconstruction quality and substantially faster inference (5× over SPIN) for lightweight SR, making content-aware long-range attention viable for deployment on resource-constrained devices. The sub-grouping strategy for parallelism and the adjacent-subgroup attention trick are practical design choices that other sparse-attention SR methods could adopt. Open questions include whether the shared token centers generalize well to highly diverse or out-of-distribution content, whether the approach maintains its advantage at much higher resolutions where the number of tokens grows, and whether the content-aware grouping provides consistent gains over simpler spatial partitioning strategies as model scale increases.
