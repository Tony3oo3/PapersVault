## Problem

Existing NeRF-based SLAM systems are confined to single-agent settings and suffer from accumulated pose drift in large-scale indoor scenes, while prior multi-agent SLAM frameworks (e.g., CP-SLAM) require centralized raw-data exchange that violates communication bandwidth constraints. Additionally, no real-world dataset provides both time-continuous camera trajectory and high-accuracy 3D mesh ground truth suitable for evaluating neural SLAM systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251918/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent ingests a live RGB-D video stream with known camera intrinsics and operates independently, building a local keyframe graph and scene representation. The system outputs per-agent and global camera poses, an implicit TSDF scene representation, and rendered color/depth images. Critically, inter-agent communication is restricted to exchanging network parameters and high-dimensional RAFT features (not raw frames), reducing communication by 7× compared to CP-SLAM.

## Method

MNE-SLAM employs a joint scene representation combining two-scale tri-plane encoding (coarse + fine feature planes) with one-blob MLP coordinate encoding, producing SDF and RGB outputs. Camera tracking uses a RAFT-based recurrent optical flow operator for keyframe selection, followed by differentiable Dense Bundle Adjustment to refine poses. A novel intra-to-inter loop closure mechanism first corrects single-agent drift via local and global covisibility-based keyframe graph optimization, then registers multi-agent submaps by minimizing a photometric consistency loss on rendered images from both agents. Submap fusion is achieved through peer-to-peer knowledge distillation: overlapping-region keyframes are rendered from both agents' networks, and a distillation loss on rendered RGB, depth, and SDF values aligns the network parameters into a globally consistent implicit map.

## Results

On the Replica dataset (Table 1), MNE-SLAM achieves the best global reconstruction with 1.681 cm accuracy, 1.509 cm completeness, and 96.96% completion ratio, outperforming the best single-agent method (PLGSLAM: 1.884/1.543/96.33%) and the only other multi-agent baseline (CP-SLAM: 1.794/2.455/90.39%). On ScanNet (Table 3), MNE-SLAM achieves the lowest average ATE RMSE of 6.6 cm, beating GO-SLAM (6.9 cm) and CP-SLAM (9.7 cm). On the large-scale INS dataset (Table 4), MNE-SLAM achieves 0.38 m mean tracking error and 64.0% completion ratio, while most competing methods either fail entirely or degrade severely (e.g., ESLAM: 1.10 m mean, 58.5% completion). Communication cost is reduced by 7× compared to CP-SLAM (60.58 MB vs. 429.78 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MNE-SLAM outperforms all single-agent NeRF-based SLAM methods (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, ESLAM, Co-SLAM, GO-SLAM, Point-SLAM, PLGSLAM, Loopy-SLAM) on global reconstruction accuracy and completion ratio on Replica, achieving 1.681 cm accuracy vs. the next-best PLGSLAM (1.884 cm) and ESLAM (2.082 cm). Against the only other multi-agent neural SLAM baseline, CP-SLAM, MNE-SLAM improves global reconstruction accuracy by 6.3% (1.681 vs. 1.794 cm) and completeness by 38.5% (1.509 vs. 2.455 cm), while reducing communication by 7× (60.58 MB vs. 429.78 MB). On ScanNet tracking, MNE-SLAM achieves 6.6 cm average ATE RMSE, beating GO-SLAM (6.9 cm), Loopy-SLAM (7.7 cm), and CP-SLAM (9.7 cm). On the large-scale INS dataset, MNE-SLAM is the only method that simultaneously achieves sub-0.5 m tracking error (0.38 m mean) and >60% completion ratio, whereas ESLAM and PLG-SLAM degrade to ~1.0–1.1 m mean error and Photo-SLAM, despite lower accuracy (60.61 cm), suffers 139.89 cm completeness due to severe holes. Loopy-SLAM marginally beats MNE-SLAM on Replica global localization (0.369 vs. 0.398 m) but trails significantly on reconstruction completeness (2.645 vs. 1.509 cm), and MNE-SLAM's advantage grows on larger, real-world scenes where single-agent loop closure is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent neural implicit SLAM line (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, ESLAM, Co-SLAM, Loopy-SLAM) into a fully distributed multi-agent setting, departing from CP-SLAM's distributed-to-centralized paradigm that requires aggregating all keyframe data at a central node. The scene representation builds directly on Co-SLAM's one-blob coordinate encoding combined with a two-scale tri-plane architecture, while the tracking pipeline reuses RAFT-based optical flow and differentiable dense bundle adjustment. The key conceptual departure is the intra-to-inter loop closure mechanism, which unifies single-agent drift correction with cross-agent submap registration, and a peer-to-peer knowledge distillation strategy for fusing implicit neural submaps without transmitting raw observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The INS dataset addresses a critical gap: no prior real-world dataset provides both time-continuous trajectory ground truth and high-accuracy 3D mesh ground truth simultaneously, making it a likely standard benchmark for NeRF-based and 3DGS-based SLAM evaluation. The 7× communication efficiency gain over CP-SLAM makes multi-agent neural SLAM practically deployable under bandwidth constraints. Open questions include scalability beyond two agents, handling dynamic or outdoor environments, and whether the distillation-based submap fusion generalizes to highly heterogeneous agent observations. The framework's reliance on RGB-D sensors and indoor scenes limits immediate applicability to broader robotics settings.
