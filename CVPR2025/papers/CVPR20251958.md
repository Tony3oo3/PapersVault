## Problem

No existing 3D medical imaging model simultaneously provides highly accurate automatic segmentation across many classes, interactive human-in-the-loop correction, zero-shot segmentation of novel structures, and few-shot transfer learning. 2D foundation models (SAM, SAM2) fail on complex 3D anatomical structures, while established 3D foundation models like TotalSegmentator lack interactive editing and zero-shot capability, and in-context learning methods fall far short of expert 3D models in accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251958/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D CT volumes processed as 128-voxel cubic patches during training and via sliding-window inference at test time. The automatic branch accepts a single integer class index (from a global list of 127 supported classes) as its prompt, while the interactive branch accepts 3D point coordinates with positive/negative polarity labels. Output is a 3D binary segmentation mask; when both branches are active, a connected-component-based merging algorithm (Alg. 1) restricts modifications to only the regions containing click points, preventing destruction of correctly segmented areas.

## Method

VISTA3D uses a shared SegResNet encoder with two decoders: an automatic head that maps a learnable class embedding through an MLP and dot-products with decoder features via sigmoid (avoiding softmax over all classes), and an interactive head adapted from SAM's point prompt encoder with 3D cross-attention, local-patch inference for speed, and special embeddings for ambiguous classes and zero-shot generalization. The key novelty is a 3D supervoxel generation pipeline (Alg. 2) that runs SAM's ViT-H encoder slice-by-slice across all three anatomical views, sums the upsampled feature maps into a 3D tensor, and applies SLIC superpixel segmentation to produce class-agnostic supervoxels that distill SAM's zero-shot image understanding into 3D training signal. Training follows a four-stage recipe: (1) interactive branch training on 11,454 CT scans using manual labels, TotalSegmentator pseudo-labels, and SAM-derived supervoxels with iterative point sampling; (2) interactive finetuning with class oversampling; (3) automatic branch training with the encoder frozen; and (4) automatic finetuning augmented with MAISI synthetic data for under-represented classes.

## Results

On the primary benchmark of 14 datasets covering 127 classes, VISTA3D achieves an average Dice of 0.711 for out-of-the-box automatic segmentation (comparable to nnU-Net's 0.718 and Auto3DSeg's 0.706), and 0.792 with a single corrective click per sliding-window patch (VISTA3D auto+point), a substantial improvement over all baselines. For zero-shot interactive segmentation on 4 external datasets with novel classes, VISTA3D achieves state-of-the-art Dice scores with fewer clicks than MedSAM and SegVol, boosted by 50% from the SAM-distilled supervoxel training. In few-shot finetuning (1–10 cases), VISTA3D substantially outperforms nnU-Net and Auto3DSeg trained from scratch (e.g., 0.926 vs. 0.820/0.759 on Micro-CT Mouse with 1 case). Inference is also ~4× faster than TotalSegmentator (e.g., 2m09s vs. 12m01s on a 512³ volume).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against nnU-Net and Auto3DSeg (dataset-specific expert models), VISTA3D's automatic branch (0.711 avg Dice) is competitive but slightly below nnU-Net (0.718); however, with just one corrective click per patch, VISTA3D auto+point jumps to 0.792, surpassing both by 7–8 points. Against TotalSegmentator (the leading 3D foundation model), VISTA3D auto is comparable on most datasets and superior on several (e.g., BTCV-Cervix 0.672 vs. 0.611, Bone Lesion 0.491 vs. –), while also running ~4× faster (2m09s vs. 12m01s on 512³). In zero-shot interactive segmentation, VISTA3D clearly outperforms both MedSAM (2D slice-by-slice) and SegVol (3D bounding-box + text prompt) across all four external novel-class datasets, achieving higher Dice with fewer clicks; the SAM-distilled supervoxel training contributes a 50% Dice improvement over VISTA3D without supervoxels. In few-shot finetuning (1–10 cases), VISTA3D substantially beats nnU-Net and Auto3DSeg trained from scratch (e.g., 0.926 vs. 0.759 on Micro-CT Mouse with 1 case), and matches or exceeds TotalSegmentator finetuning on WORD.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISTA3D departs from the dominant approach of adapting SAM's 2D ViT backbone to 3D (as in MedSAM, SAM adapters, and SegVol) by instead using a 3D CNN (SegResNet) as the shared encoder and distilling SAM's zero-shot knowledge through a novel 3D supervoxel generation pipeline. The interactive branch borrows the point-prompt cross-attention design from SAM and the local-refinement merging strategy from FocalClick, while the automatic branch draws on the well-established 3D sliding-window inference paradigm from nnU-Net and Auto3DSeg. Unlike TotalSegmentator (automatic only) or SegVol (interactive only), VISTA3D unifies both paradigms in a single model with a shared encoder, positioning itself as the first 3D foundation model covering the full human-in-the-loop annotation workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practically deployable unified model that reduces the need to train separate expert models per organ or pathology, and the supervoxel distillation technique offers a general recipe for transferring 2D foundation model knowledge to 3D without inheriting the 2D backbone's architectural constraints. Key limitations include CT-only support (no MRI/PET), a fixed 127-class vocabulary for automatic mode, and zero-shot performance that still requires at least one click per region. Open questions include whether the supervoxel distillation approach generalizes to other modalities, whether the CNN-over-transformer finding for 3D medical imaging holds at larger scales, and whether the workflow translates to real clinical validation with diverse acquisition protocols.
