## Problem

Despite the shift toward sequential generation paradigms in visual tracking, widely adopted image-level pretrained backbones (e.g., MAE) inherently lack temporal modeling capacity, failing to capture the continuous appearance deformation and trajectory evolution that define tracking. Existing video-level attempts like DropMAE only model discrete two-frame interactions, which are insufficient for the long-range successive temporal dependencies spanning thousands of frames in real tracking scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250661/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes continuous video clips (up to 7 frames, sampled with max interval 50) with a randomly initialized target bounding box and appearance from the first frame; the ViT encoder (256×256 or 384×384 template/search regions) processes historical appearance embeddings and current search images. The model outputs predicted next-frame target appearances (via conditional diffusion) and autoregressively generated bounding box coordinate sequences (via backtracking), with the final tracked position in the first frame trained to match the initialized state. At inference, ARPTrack produces per-frame bounding box coordinates as an autoregressive trajectory sequence.

## Method

ARP is an unsupervised spatio-temporal pretraining framework built on a shared ViT backbone that performs two coupled tasks: (1) appearance prediction, where a conditional diffusion model—unconventionally using search-image embeddings as the noise source rather than Gaussian noise—autoregressively generates the target's next-frame appearance conditioned on historical appearance embeddings; and (2) trajectory verification (backtracking), where the same ViT uses the predicted appearances as templates to autoregressively generate coordinate sequences in reverse temporal order, enforcing motion consistency by requiring the backtracked position to match the initialized target. Training employs a progressive clip-length schedule (2→7 frames over 600 epochs), a hybrid loss combining MSE for appearance, cross-entropy for coordinate generation, and SIoU for geometric alignment, plus spatial perturbations (center/scale jitter) to prevent center-bias. The pretrained encoder is then transferred into ARTrackV2 to form ARPTrack, achieving 81.5% AO on GOT-10k—the first tracker to surpass 80%—while retaining ARTrackV2's computational efficiency (51G FLOPs, 91 FPS).

## Results

ARPTrack-L384 achieves 81.5% AO on GOT-10k, becoming the first tracker to surpass the 80% AO threshold, outperforming the previous SOTA ARTrackV2-L384 (79.5%) by 2.0% in AO and 2.8% in SR0.5. On LaSOT, it reaches 74.2% AUC and 83.4% PNorm; on TrackingNet, 86.6% AUC and 91.1% PNorm. The smaller ARPTrack256 variant (77.7% AO on GOT-10k) also outperforms all same-resolution baselines while maintaining 91 FPS and 51G FLOPs, identical to ARTrackV2256.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARPTrack-L384 outperforms its direct predecessor ARTrackV2-L384 by 2.0% AO (81.5 vs. 79.5) and 2.8% SR0.5 (90.6 vs. 87.8) on GOT-10k, and by 0.6% AUC on LaSOT (74.2 vs. 73.6) and 0.5% AUC on TrackingNet (86.6 vs. 86.1). Against other autoregressive trackers, it leads ARTrack-L384 by 3.0% AO and HIPTrack384 by 4.1% AO on GOT-10k. Among sequential-generation methods, it surpasses SeqTrack-L384 by 6.7% AO and DiffusionTrack-L256 by 6.8% AO. Against per-frame template-matching trackers, the margin is even larger: +7.8% AO over OSTrack384 and +11.4% over MixFormer-L320. The smaller ARPTrack256 also beats all same-resolution baselines (e.g., +1.8% AO over ARTrackV2256, +3.0% over ARTrack256) while matching ARTrackV2256's computational cost (51G FLOPs, 91 FPS). Notably, ARPTrack-L384 achieves the best score on every metric across all four benchmarks simultaneously, with no trade-offs observed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive tracking lineage (ARTrack → ARTrackV2) by addressing a pretraining gap: all prior trackers rely on image-level (MAE) or frame-pair-level (DropMAE) pretrained backbones that cannot model long-range temporal dependencies. ARP introduces a video-level unsupervised pretraining objective that combines conditional diffusion-based future-frame appearance generation with reverse-autoregressive trajectory backtracking, both operating on a shared ViT encoder. The approach borrows the autoregressive coordinate-sequence formulation from ARTrackV2 and the conditional diffusion framework from Ho et al., but repurposes them as a pretraining signal rather than a tracking mechanism, and departs from MAE-style masked reconstruction by explicitly modeling temporal evolution rather than spatial completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that video-level autoregressive pretraining consistently outperforms image-level and pairs-level alternatives across multiple tracker architectures (including the per-frame OSTrack), the paper establishes a principled argument that temporal pretraining is the correct inductive bias for tracking, not merely a marginal trick. The first 80%+ AO on GOT-10k (81.5%) is a meaningful milestone, though the 2-point gain over ARTrackV2-L384 is within the range of typical SOTA increments in this field. Open questions include whether the approach scales to web-scale video datasets and ViT-G backbones, whether it transfers to other temporal video tasks (e.g., segmentation) beyond bounding-box tracking, and whether the diffusion-based appearance prediction can be replaced by lighter alternatives without losing the temporal modeling benefit.
