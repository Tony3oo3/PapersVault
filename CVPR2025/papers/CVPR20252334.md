## Problem

Existing spatial transcriptomics gene prediction models rely on a single type of pathology image feature (either deep or handcrafted) and treat each gene's prediction independently, ignoring both the complementary information across feature modalities and the functional similarities among genes that could improve individual gene predictions. Additionally, GNN-based approaches suffer from over-smoothing that erodes the topological distinctions between spatially distinct spots.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252334/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each spot in a tissue slide, the method consumes a 224×224 H&E image patch (yielding ResNet-101 deep features, 14 per-nucleus morphology features via HoVer-Net, and 3 Delaunay-triangulation topology features) along with the spot's spatial coordinates used to build a K=6 nearest-neighbor graph. The output is predicted expression values for the top 50 highly variable genes and top 50 highly expressed genes per spot, evaluated via Pearson correlation and MSE against ground-truth spatial transcriptomics measurements.

## Method

M2TGLGO fuses three feature modalities (deep, morphology, topology) through a Multi-modal Graph Embedding module that applies GATs for within-modal neighborhood aggregation and a trace-based softmax similarity for inter-modal interaction, blending the two with a tunable parameter γ. A Spatial-Oriented Neighborhood Ranking Module then enforces that representation similarity decays with hop distance (1-hop > 2-hop > 3-hop) via quartile-based hinge losses, counteracting GNN over-smoothing. For gene prediction, a Gene Ontology DAG is used to compute pairwise gene similarity, which defines a gene graph processed by a GAT layer initialized with Gene2Vec embeddings to produce inter-dependent gene predictors. The total training objective combines MSE gene prediction loss, the SONRM ranking loss, and an adjacency-matrix reconstruction loss, all optimized jointly with AdamW.

## Results

M2TGLGO achieves the highest average Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC) across all three spatial transcriptomics datasets for both highly variable genes (HVG) and highly expressed genes (HEG). Specifically, it reaches PCC of 0.291 (HVG) and 0.217 (HEG) on DLPFC, 0.296 (HVG) and 0.256 (HEG) on ccRCC, and 0.308 (HVG) and 0.269 (HEG) on BC, outperforming the strongest baseline BLEEP by 3.9–8.1 PCC points. The method also achieves lower MSE than all competitors, and its predicted gene-gene correlations more faithfully recover the ground-truth association structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M2TGLGO outperforms all seven ST-based gene prediction baselines on every dataset and gene category. The closest competitor is BLEEP [39] (bimodal contrastive learning), which trails by 3.9 PCC on DLPFC-HVG (0.252 vs 0.291) and by 4.6 PCC on BC-HEG (0.223 vs 0.269); TG-GATEs [17] and TRIPLEX [5] follow at 0.241 and 0.236 on DLPFC-HVG respectively. Among the earlier methods, ST-Net [14] (the first deep-learning ST gene predictor) and THItoGene [18] show substantially lower PCC (≤0.219 and ≤0.191), while Hist2ST [42] and HisToGene [27] are the weakest at ≤0.116. Against the six pathology image representation methods (IGI-DL, SI-MIL, TMEGL, MGNN, GECMC, TSIEN) evaluated in Figure 3, M2TGLGO also achieves the highest PCC on all three datasets, attributed to its multi-modal feature fusion and spatial-oriented neighborhood ranking. The ablation confirms that removing the GO prior knowledge causes the largest single drop (e.g., DLPFC-HVG falls from 0.291 to 0.163), highlighting the critical role of gene-gene functional similarity in the prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate threads in spatial transcriptomics: (1) multi-modal pathology image representation (combining handcrafted morphology/topology features with deep ResNet-101 features), (2) graph-based spatial modeling of spots, and (3) biological prior knowledge for gene-gene relationships. It departs from prior ST gene prediction methods (ST-Net, BLEEP, TRIPLEX, Hist2ST) that rely on a single feature type and treat each gene independently, by introducing a multi-modal graph embedding with within/inter-modal interactions, a spatial-oriented ranking module to counter GNN over-smoothing, and—most distinctively—a Gene Ontology-guided GNN that models functional gene associations during prediction. The GO-guided multi-gene prediction is the paper's clearest conceptual departure, as no prior ST gene prediction method incorporates structured biological knowledge about gene-gene relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical recipe for integrating heterogeneous pathology image features with spatial topology and biological priors, which could inform future ST imputation pipelines. However, several limitations temper its impact: evaluation is restricted to three datasets with only 12 slides each under leave-one-out, the GO similarity is a static prior that may not capture tissue-specific regulatory context, and the 3000-gene subset limits generalizability. An open question is whether the substantial performance gain from GO prior knowledge (ablation shows DLPFC HVG PCC dropping from 0.291 to 0.163 without it) reflects genuine biological signal or an artifact of the small evaluation set. Scaling to full transcriptomes and diverse tissue types remains to be demonstrated.
