## Problem

Existing universal image restoration methods rely exclusively on degraded images as the sole feature source, neglecting the prior distribution of clean images, which limits structural reconstruction and realistic texture restoration. While generative priors from GANs and diffusion models have been explored for restoration, the VAR paradigm—a next-scale prediction autoregressive model that surpasses diffusion models in generation quality—has never been investigated for this purpose. The paper asks whether VAR's endogenous multi-scale representations encode an adaptive distribution alignment capability that can be directly leveraged as a restoration prior.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single degraded RGB image (randomly cropped to 256×256 patches during training) and outputs a restored high-quality image of the same resolution. A single unified model handles six degradation types simultaneously—deraining, dehazing, low-light enhancement, motion deblurring, Gaussian denoising, and real denoising—without task-specific conditioning. The degraded image is first encoded by VAR's VQVAE encoder into multi-scale token maps, and the final output is decoded back to pixel space through VAR's decoder.

## Method

VarFormer operates in two training stages. In Stage 1, VAR is frozen and an Adapter module (self-attention blocks) is inserted after the VQVAE encoder to bridge the domain gap between clean pre-training data and degraded inputs, trained with a Feature Matching Loss that reduces discrepancies between degraded and clean multi-scale latent representations. In Stage 2, the Degradation-Aware Enhancement (DAE) module uses encoder layer outputs to predict per-scale weights for re-weighting VAR's autoregressive priors, then applies region-specific softmax fusion weights to selectively inject high-quality priors only where degradation is present. The Adaptive Feature Transformation (AFT) module introduces a low-dimensional mediator feature for cross-attention between degraded and prior-enhanced features, avoiding the structural warping that standard cross-attention can cause. An adaptive mix-up skipping mechanism with learnable sigmoid coefficients blends encoder features into the decoder to preserve fine details lost during downsampling.

## Results

On the all-in-one benchmark covering six restoration tasks, VarFormer achieves 31.33 PSNR / 0.913 SSIM on deraining (5 test sets), 30.99 / 0.956 on motion deblurring, 25.13 / 0.917 on low-light enhancement, and 32.96 / 0.956 on dehazing, outperforming all universal baselines (e.g., DiffUIR, Prompt-IR, IDR) across every task. Notably, on low-light enhancement and dehazing, VarFormer's all-in-one results surpass dedicated task-specific models such as MIRNet (24.74 PSNR on enhancement) and Restormer (30.87 PSNR on dehazing). On zero-shot under-display camera restoration (TOLED/POLED), VarFormer reaches 30.61 PSNR / 0.275 LPIPS on TOLED, leading all compared methods including DiffUIR (29.55 / 0.281).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among universal methods, VarFormer consistently outperforms the strongest competitor DiffUIR [65] on all four tasks in Table 1 (e.g., +1.11 PSNR on deblurring, +0.22 on enhancement, +0.22 on dehazing) and also leads on zero-shot UDC restoration (TOLED: +1.06 PSNR, −0.006 LPIPS). Against task-specific models, VarFormer's all-in-one training surpasses dedicated methods on low-light enhancement (25.13 vs. MIRNet's 24.74 PSNR) and dehazing (32.96 vs. Restormer's 30.87 PSNR), though it trails Restormer on deraining (31.33 vs. 33.96) and deblurring (30.99 vs. 32.92). On Gaussian denoising (Table 2), VarFormer achieves the best or near-best PSNR across BSD68, Urban100, and Kodak24 at all noise levels, edging out Restormer and MPRNet. On real-image denoising (SIDD, Table 3), VarFormer marginally beats DiffUIR (40.13 vs. 40.11 PSNR; 0.978 vs. 0.976 SSIM). On zero-shot POLED, VarFormer (16.63 PSNR / 0.605 LPIPS) slightly trails IDR on PSNR (16.71) but substantially improves LPIPS (0.605 vs. 0.716), indicating better perceptual quality on the harder dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on generative priors for image restoration—previously dominated by GAN inversion and diffusion-based approaches—by repurposing VAR's next-scale autoregressive predictions as a multi-scale restoration prior. It builds directly on VAR [40], whose encoder, decoder, and scale-prediction Transformer are frozen and reused, and on the multi-scale restoration principle long recognized in the IR community (MPRNet, Restormer). The key departure from prior universal restoration methods (AirNet, IDR, DiffUIR) is the explicit exploitation of VAR's endogenous distribution-alignment property: the autoregressive scale predictions progressively project degraded features into the clean-image distribution, a behavior the authors formalize and leverage through their DAE and AFT modules rather than treating the generative model as a black-box prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VarFormer demonstrates that a single model trained on six degradation types can match or exceed task-specific methods on several benchmarks and generalize zero-shot to an unseen under-display-camera task, suggesting that VAR's multi-scale alignment prior is a broadly useful inductive bias for restoration. The observation that lower autoregressive scales correct global degradations (haze, low-light) while higher scales address local ones (noise, rain) provides an interpretable mechanism that could inform future multi-scale restoration architectures. Open questions include whether the frozen-VAR constraint limits adaptability to truly novel degradations, whether the approach scales to video or 3D restoration, and whether the two-stage training (reconstruction pre-training followed by restoration fine-tuning) can be simplified or made end-to-end.
