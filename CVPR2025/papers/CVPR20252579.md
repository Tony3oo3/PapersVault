## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from slow inference (thousands of denoising steps) and large model sizes (billions of parameters), but existing acceleration methods target either the temporal level (caching, fast solvers) or the structural level (quantization, pruning) in isolation. The paper identifies that these two optimization axes are not orthogonal: independently optimizing each and then combining them causes coupled and accumulated errors that severely degrade generation quality, making it impossible to push both acceleration dimensions simultaneously without a joint optimization strategy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained diffusion models (UNet-based DDPM/LDM/Stable Diffusion and DiT-XL/2) and accepts either text prompts (MS-COCO 512×512, PartiPrompt) or class labels (ImageNet 256×256, LSUN 256×256, CIFAR-10 32×32) plus random noise as input, producing synthesized images. It is entirely training-free: the user specifies a cache frequency N and bit-width (e.g., W8A8 or W4A8), and the method computes the optimal cache schedule and correction parameters on the fly, requiring only ~8 minutes of calibration for a 250-step model. The quantization correction is absorbed into the weight representation, so inference adds only one extra matrix multiplication and addition per corrected block.

## Method

CacheQuant jointly optimizes model caching and quantization through two components. First, Dynamic Programming Schedule (DPS) formulates cache-schedule selection as a grouping problem over T denoising steps, where the intra-group error is the L1 distance between the cached feature and all features it replaces; a DP recurrence with practical group-length bounds (N/2 to 2N) reduces the search from hours to minutes while minimizing combined caching and quantization error. Second, Decoupled Error Correction (DEC) splits the total output error into a cache error (difference between ground-truth and cached activations) and a quantization error (difference between full-precision and quantized outputs), then applies independent channel-wise linear corrections (solved via least squares) to each component at every step. The quantization-side correction is folded into the quantized weights, so the runtime overhead is a single additional matmul-add per corrected block, and the entire pipeline remains training-free.

## Results

On ImageNet (LDM-4, 250 DDIM steps), CacheQuant with N=5 cache frequency and W8A8 quantization achieves FID 4.03 with a 7.87× speedup and 4× model compression (457.1 MB vs. 1824.6 MB), compared to the full-precision baseline FID of 3.37. At higher compression (N=20, W8A8), it reaches 18.06× speedup with FID 7.21. On Stable Diffusion (MS-COCO), the method achieves a 5.18× speedup and 4× compression with only a 0.02 CLIP score drop (26.87 vs. 26.89). On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet), CacheQuant N=2 W8A8 improves FID from 9.06 (Δ-DiT) to 7.86 while doubling the speedup to 2.72×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepCache (caching baseline), CacheQuant achieves comparable or better FID at higher cache frequencies (N=20: FID 7.21 vs. 8.08) while adding 4× model compression and substantially greater speedup (18.06× vs. 10.54× at N=20); at N=5, DeepCache has a slightly lower FID (3.79 vs. 4.03) but no compression and only 4.12× speedup. Against EDA-DM (quantization baseline), CacheQuant wins on both FID (4.03 vs. 4.13 at W8A8) and speedup (7.87× vs. 1.91×), and the gap widens dramatically at lower bit-widths (FID 12.65 vs. 44.12 at W4A4-equivalent compression). Against Diff-Pruning, CacheQuant dominates on all axes: FID 4.03 vs. 9.27, speedup 7.87× vs. 1.51×, and compression 4× vs. 2.4×. On the DiT framework, CacheQuant outperforms Δ-DiT at both N=2 (FID 7.86 vs. 9.06) and N=3 (FID 12.42 vs. 13.75) with roughly 2× the speedup. On Stable Diffusion (MS-COCO), CacheQuant (5.18× speedup, CLIP 26.87) surpasses all distillation-based methods (Small SD: 2.93×, CLIP 26.02; BK-SDM-Base: 2.79×, CLIP 26.21) and the PLMS-20 solver (2.43×, CLIP 26.74) in both speed and quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of diffusion model acceleration: temporal-level model caching (DeepCache for UNet, Delta-DiT for DiT) and structural-level post-training quantization (EDA-DM, DilateQuant). Rather than treating these as independent optimizations, the authors identify that their errors are non-orthogonal and couple iteratively, causing severe degradation when naively combined (FID 11.99 vs. ~5.5 if errors were additive). Their contribution is a joint optimization framework—Dynamic Programming Schedule for cache placement and Decoupled Error Correction for per-step error mitigation—that explicitly accounts for the interaction between the two error sources, positioning this as the first work to co-optimize temporal and structural acceleration simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 5.18× speedup combined with 4× model compression at only 0.02 CLIP score loss on Stable Diffusion, all in a training-free manner, makes real-time or near-real-time diffusion deployment substantially more feasible across GPU, CPU, and ARM platforms. The non-orthogonality finding cautions against the common practice of independently tuning each acceleration knob and then combining them, which could affect how future multi-technique acceleration pipelines are designed. Open questions include whether the dynamic programming schedule generalizes to video diffusion or 3D generation with much longer denoising trajectories, whether the channel-wise linear correction in DEC remains effective at very low bit-widths (e.g., 2-bit), and how the approach interacts with emerging architectural changes like MMDiT or flow-matching formulations.
