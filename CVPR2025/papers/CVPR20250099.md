## Problem

Existing virtual-point-based LiDAR-camera fusion methods for 3D object detection suffer from high computational overhead due to processing all dense virtual points from the entire image, noise from inaccurate depth completion that distorts object dimensions and locations, and underutilization of the rich semantic information available in RGB images. The paper asks whether a much smaller, semantically selected subset of virtual points can achieve equivalent or better detection performance at substantially lower cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250099/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (processed through a pre-trained depth completion model PENet and a BiSeNet V2 segmentation model) and a LiDAR point cloud. Rather than converting all image pixels into virtual points, only pixels belonging to segmented key instances are projected into 3D space and augmented with per-class semantic scores, yielding a compact set of virtual key instances (VKIs). The network outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels and orientation estimates, evaluated via 3D AP (R40), BEV AP, 2D AP, and AOS on KITTI, JRDB, and nuScenes.

## Method

ViKIENet fuses VKIs with LiDAR points through three novel modules. SKIS selects semantically enriched virtual key instances from the image using a segmentation mask and depth map, reducing the virtual point count to roughly 10% of full-image methods. VIFF performs multi-staged cross-modal fusion: in the BEV stage it applies channel attention followed by VKI spatial attention on concatenated features, and in the RoI stage it uses bi-directional cross-attention between LiDAR and VKI RoI features so each modality learns contextual or semantic information from the other. VIRA mitigates depth-completion noise by projecting both modalities' voxel features onto a shared 2D image domain and applying cross-attention where LiDAR features serve as queries to refine VKI features with precise depth. The extended ViKIENet-R variant applies 2N rotational transformations and a transformation-equivariant sparse convolutional encoder only to VKIs, achieving near-SOTA equivariant detection at 15 FPS versus ~10.7 FPS for full-virtual-point equivariant baselines.

## Results

On the KITTI test set for car 3D detection, ViKIENet achieves 91.79/84.96/80.20 AP (R40) on Easy/Moderate/Hard at 22.7 FPS, while ViKIENet-R achieves 91.20/86.04/81.18 at 15.0 FPS. On the KITTI validation set, ViKIENet reaches 95.58/88.49/86.07 3D AP, surpassing the LiDAR-only Voxel-RCNN baseline (92.38/85.29/82.86) by over 3% on all difficulty levels. On JRDB, ViKIENet improves 3D mAP by 5.49% over Voxel-RCNN and 2.13% over VirConv-L while running at 19.2 FPS versus 8.6 FPS for VirConv-L. On nuScenes, ViKIENet+MVP achieves 68.72 mAP versus 66.73 for VirConv+MVP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViKIENet outperforms all LiDAR-only baselines (PV-RCNN, Voxel-RCNN, BtcDet, TSSTDet) by 0.9–4.4% on Moderate/Hard 3D AP while matching or exceeding their speed. Against multi-modal non-equivariant methods, ViKIENet beats SFD by 0.06/0.20/2.28% and VPFNet by 0.77/1.75/2.00% on Easy/Mod/Hard, and roughly matches LoGoNet (91.79 vs 91.80 Easy) while running at 22.7 FPS versus 10.7 FPS. Compared to VirConv-L (the closest non-equivariant multi-modal baseline), ViKIENet achieves comparable accuracy (within 0.1–0.3%) at 22.7 FPS vs 17.9 FPS. ViKIENet-R trades a small accuracy drop versus VirConv-T (91.20 vs 92.54 Easy; 86.04 vs 86.25 Mod; 81.18 vs 81.24 Hard) for a 41% speedup (15.0 vs 10.7 FPS), making rotation-equivariant detection practical in resource-constrained settings. On JRDB, ViKIENet surpasses VirConv-L by 2.13% in 3D mAP and 2.68% in BEV mAP at more than 2× the inference speed, and on nuScenes it improves mAP by 1.99 points over VirConv+MVP.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViKIENet extends the virtual-point-based LiDAR-camera fusion lineage (PointPainting → SFD → VirConv) by replacing the full set of depth-completed virtual points with a sparse, semantically-enriched subset called Virtual Key Instances (VKIs), selected via image segmentation. It builds on Voxel-RCNN's voxel-based detection backbone and VirConv's sparse convolutional encoding, but departs from prior work's symmetric grid-level fusion by introducing an asymmetric, cross-modal VIFF module that treats VKI and LiDAR features as fundamentally different in nature. The ViKIENet-R variant further adapts TED's rotation-equivariant encoder to operate only on VKIs rather than the entire virtual point cloud, substantially reducing the computational cost of equivariant feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a 2×+ inference speedup (22.7 FPS vs. ~10.7 FPS for VirConv-T) at comparable or slightly better accuracy, making rotation-equivariant multimodal detection feasible on resource-constrained platforms. The conceptual insight that a semantically-guided ~10% subset of virtual points suffices challenges the field's trajectory toward ever-denser virtual point representations. Open questions include: dependence on depth completion quality (the paper acknowledges noise from inaccurate depth), generalizability to datasets without depth labels (nuScenes results rely on MVP rather than learned depth), and whether the approach degrades when the segmentation model produces false negatives (mitigated by concatenation with LiDAR features but not fully resolved).
