## Problem

Estimating canopy height at meter resolution from satellite imagery is critical for forest management and climate policy, yet no open-access benchmark exists at very high resolution (≤1.5 m). Existing studies rely on coarse GEDI footprints (25 m) or commercial/closed data sources, and none provide a reproducible evaluation for detecting tree-level canopy height reduction between consecutive satellite acquisitions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250223/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The primary task takes a single SPOT 6-7 VHR satellite image with four pansharpened spectral bands (RGB + NIR) at 1.5 m resolution and produces a per-pixel continuous canopy height map in meters, evaluated only within a vegetation mask and for ground-truth heights below 60 m. The secondary task (Open-Canopy-Δ) takes two such images from different years, generates two height maps, and outputs a binary mask of significant canopy height reduction (≥15 m loss) after morphological regularization and removal of components smaller than 200 m². Ground truth for both tasks is derived from aerial LiDAR (LiDAR-HD) at ≥10 points/m², rasterized to 1.5 m.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a country-scale open-access benchmark (87,000 km² of France) paired with a systematic evaluation of 12 model configurations spanning UNet, DeepLabv3, ViT, HViT, SWIN, PCPVT, and PVTv2, each with different pretraining (ImageNet, DINOv2, CLIP, ScaleMAE, Satlas, Tolan). Models are adapted from RGB segmentation to 4-channel dense regression by initializing the NIR input channel with N(0, 0.01) and replacing the classification head with a transposed-convolution decoder trained with L1 loss on 224×224 tiles. Key findings: hierarchical ViTs (SWIN, PVTv2) achieve the best MAE (~2.5 m) and tree-cover IoU (>90%), outperforming both pure CNNs and non-hierarchical ViTs; ImageNet pretraining beats large-scale foundation models (DINOv2, CLIP) due to viewpoint and spectral-band mismatch; and a proposed gradual NIR-channel initialization further improves over random or LoRA adaptation. For Open-Canopy-Δ, the PVTv2 model achieves 54% F1 on canopy loss detection, substantially outperforming Sentinel-based (6% F1) and Global Forest Change (1.7% F1) baselines.

## Results

On the Open-Canopy test set (13,675 km²), the best-performing model (PVTv2, ImageNet-1k pretrained) achieves a MAE of 2.52 m, nMAE of 22.9%, RMSE of 4.02 m, and tree-cover IoU of 90.5% at 1.5 m resolution. Hierarchical ViTs (SWIN, PCPVT, PVTv2) outperform both standard UNet (MAE 2.67 m) and plain ViT-B (MAE 4.26 m), while foundation-model pretraining (DINOv2, CLIP, ScaleMAE) does not improve over ImageNet pretraining. On the Open-Canopy-Δ change-detection task, PVTv2 achieves 54.1% F1 and 37.0% IoU for canopy height reduction detection, substantially outperforming Global Forest Change (1.7% F1) and Schwartz et al.'s Sentinel-based maps (6.0% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Open-Canopy PVTv2 model (MAE 2.52 m, nMAE 22.9%) outperforms all six existing canopy height products by a wide margin: it beats the closest competitor Liu et al. [37] (MAE 4.83 m, 3 m resolution) by 2.31 m in MAE, Tolan et al. [67] (MAE 5.07 m, 1 m resolution) by 2.55 m, Schwartz et al. [61] (MAE 5.17 m, 10 m) by 2.65 m, Potapov et al. [50] (MAE 6.27 m, 30 m) by 3.75 m, Pauls et al. [47] (MAE 6.70 m, 10 m) by 4.18 m, and Lang et al. [35] (MAE 9.22 m, 10 m) by 6.70 m. The tree-cover IoU gap is equally pronounced: 90.5% vs. 76.8–84.1% for all baselines. For the canopy height reduction detection task (Open-Canopy-Δ), PVTv2 achieves 54.1% F1 and 37.0% IoU, dramatically outperforming Global Forest Change [24] (F1 1.7%, IoU 0.8%) and Schwartz et al.'s Sentinel-derived maps (F1 6.0%, IoU 3.1%), though absolute performance remains modest, indicating the task is still far from solved. In the out-of-domain evaluation on Utah (NEON ground truth), the Open-Canopy-trained PVTv2 with SPOT input (MAE 2.08 m) matches Tolan et al.'s MAXAR-based map (MAE 2.02 m), confirming cross-geographic generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by making VHR canopy height estimation a fully reproducible, open-access problem. Prior VHR canopy height work (Tolan et al., Wagner et al., Liu et al.) relied on commercial satellite imagery or restricted data, while GEDI-based approaches (Potapov, Schwartz, Lang, Pauls) operated at 10–30 m resolution with sparse 25 m footprint supervision. Open-Canopy departs from both lines by pairing 1.5 m SPOT 6-7 panchromatic imagery with dense ALS ground truth (>10 pts/m²) across 87,000 km², all under an open license with ready-to-use splits and loaders. Methodologically, it repurposes standard dense-prediction architectures (UNet, hierarchical ViTs) and tests whether large-scale foundation-model pretraining (DINOv2, CLIP, ScaleMAE) transfers to this satellite-viewpoint, four-band setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a critical reproducibility gap: for the first time, the CV community can train and evaluate canopy height models at meter resolution without commercial data access, enabling fair comparison of architectures and pretraining strategies. The finding that ImageNet-pretrained models outperform DINOv2 and CLIP foundation models challenges the assumption that larger, more diverse self-supervised pretraining universally benefits downstream tasks, and points to the need for remote-sensing-specific pretraining that incorporates NIR channels. The Open-Canopy-Δ task (tree-level canopy height reduction detection) remains very hard—best F1 is only 54%—highlighting a significant open problem for deforestation monitoring. Key limitations include geographic restriction to France (no tropical forests), sensor-specificity to SPOT 6-7, and the inherent noise in ALS-derived ground truth.
