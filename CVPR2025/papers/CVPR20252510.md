## Problem

Training-free conditional diffusion methods steer generation by approximating the conditional score ∇_{z_t} log p_t(y|z_t), which requires modifying the sampling algorithm itself (e.g., altering DDPM or DDIM update rules). This coupling between the guidance approximation and the specific sampler reduces flexibility, disrupts sampling stability, and demands extensive per-algorithm tuning. No prior method achieves conditional generation by leaving the underlying sampling algorithm entirely unchanged.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt plus a condition image (depth map, Canny/HED/MLSD edge, segmentation, normal map, skeleton, location, sketch, or style reference) and outputs a 512×512 RGB image. It operates as a drop-in noise replacement within any existing diffusion sampler (DDPM, DDIM, LCM, DPM-Solver, Euler, SA-Solver) and is compatible with both standalone models (Stable Diffusion v1.5) and adapter-based CDMs (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, IP-Adapter), where it adds an additional condition on top of the adapter's own guidance.

## Method

NoiseCtrl replaces the isotropic Gaussian noise ε_t in the reverse diffusion step z_{t-1} = μ_t + σ_t ε_t with a conditional noise ε'_t = r·u, where r follows the chi distribution (preserving the radial scale) and u is drawn from a von Mises-Fisher distribution centered on a direction vector d. The direction d is estimated by minimizing an energy function E(ẑ_{0|t}, y) that measures the mismatch between the Tweedie-estimated clean image and the condition, using either a slow full optimization over polar angles or a fast closed-form solution d = −∇L_y'(z')/‖∇L_y'(z')‖₂ computed at a random Gaussian perturbation of the current mean. Because only the noise distribution is altered while μ_t and σ_t remain untouched, the method is sampling-algorithm-agnostic and integrates with any backward process without code changes. The concentration parameter κ of the von Mises-Fisher distribution controls the trade-off between controllability (large κ, tight angular focus) and diversity (small κ, more random directions).

## Results

On COCO2017 (512×512, 5000 images), NoiseCtrl variants consistently outperform the three training-free baselines (UGD, FreeDom, DSG) on FID across all three conditions (Depth, Canny, Style), with the best FID of 21.25 (DDIM fast,2 on Canny) versus 26.84 (DSG) and 27.11 (FreeDom). CLIP text-image scores also favor NoiseCtrl on Depth (0.2961 best, Euler fast,2) and Canny (0.3083 best, SA fast,2) over all baselines, though DSG leads on Style (0.3449 vs. 0.3271). For controllability, NoiseCtrl achieves lower MSE on Depth (86.36–87.94 vs. 95.17–97.02) but higher MSE on Canny (0.48–0.51 vs. 0.35–0.38), indicating a trade-off between generation quality and edge-condition fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UGD [1], FreeDom [34], and DSG [32], NoiseCtrl variants achieve substantially lower FID on Depth (22.76–24.82 vs. 26.11–27.34) and Canny (21.25–22.97 vs. 26.37–27.11), confirming superior generation quality. On CLIP text-image scores, NoiseCtrl leads on Depth and Canny by 0.03–0.05 points, but DSG outperforms all NoiseCtrl variants on Style (0.3449 vs. best 0.3271). For controllability, NoiseCtrl clearly wins on Depth MSE (86.36–87.94 vs. 95.17–97.02) but loses on Canny MSE (0.48–0.51 vs. 0.35–0.38), suggesting the conditional noise introduces slightly more deviation from edge maps. On Style loss, UGD is marginally best (6.75e-5) with NoiseCtrl variants (6.85e-5–7.32e-5) within a narrow range. In efficiency (Table 2), the fast NoiseCtrl variants add only 10–16 seconds over the base sampling algorithm (e.g., DDPM 6 s → DDPM fast,2 16 s), far less than UGD (4507 s) while remaining competitive with FreeDom (116 s) and DSG (121 s). Training-based CDMs (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, IP-Adapter) are excluded from the quality comparison but serve as efficiency references at 4–6 seconds.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoiseCtrl builds on the training-free conditional diffusion model lineage initiated by Dhariwal et al. and extended by UGD, FreeDom, and DSG, all of which approximate the conditional score ∇log p_t(y|z_t) via differentiable loss functions and modify the sampling algorithm accordingly. The key departure is that NoiseCtrl avoids approximating p_t(z_t|y) entirely; instead, it reinterprets the isotropic Gaussian noise in the reverse process as a mixture of directionally-biased components and replaces it with a von Mises-Fisher-distributed conditional noise, leaving the sampling algorithm untouched. This makes it sampling-algorithm-agnostic in a way that DSG (which shares the same closed-form gradient solution but is coupled to a specific sampler) is not, and it contrasts with classifier-guided or classifier-free approaches that require additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can add conditional control to any existing diffusion sampler (DDPM, DDIM, LCM, DPM-Solver, Euler, SA-Solver) with a single noise-substitution line, lowering the barrier to controlled generation. However, the controllability results are mixed: NoiseCtrl achieves the best MSE for depth conditions but the worst for Canny edges among compared methods, and the efficiency gain over the base sampler is modest (e.g., 16 s vs. 6 s for DDPM fast,2). Open questions include whether the energy-function requirement per condition type limits scalability to arbitrary or compositional conditions, how the method behaves at higher resolutions or with video diffusion, and whether the κ=10⁴ setting generalizes across architectures or requires per-task tuning.
