## Problem

Gradient-based algorithms for the Gromov-Wasserstein problem (CG and PG) operate over the full m×n coupling space even when input data exhibits cyclic symmetry, wasting computation on redundant symmetric copies. No prior work had exploited symmetry as a structural property to accelerate GW, leaving a gap between the symmetry-aware solvers already available for optimal transport and the non-convex GW setting where such exploitation is non-trivial.

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete probability vectors a∈Δ^m and b∈Δ^n, two non-negative metric matrices C∈R^{m×m} and D∈R^{n×n}, and a known symmetry order K that divides both m and n. The inputs must satisfy K-order cyclic symmetry: a and b are K-fold concatenations of smaller vectors, and C and D are block circulant matrices. The output is a coupling matrix T restricted to the (m′,n′,K)-block circulant form (K blocks of size m′×n′ instead of a full m×n matrix) and the corresponding GWD. For real-world data with only approximate symmetry, a symmetric alignment preprocessing step enforces strict symmetry before the algorithm runs.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that if the initial coupling T⁽⁰⁾ is initialized as a block circulant matrix, then every intermediate gradient G⁽ᵗ⁾, subproblem solution S⁽ᵗ⁾, and updated coupling T⁽ᵗ⁺¹⁾ in both CG and PG remain block circulant until convergence, allowing the entire solution space to be restricted to K blocks of size m′×n′. This restriction propagates into the inner subproblems: the OT solved at each CG iteration reduces to a small m′×n′ problem (Proposition 1), and the EOT dual solved at each PG iteration reduces to an m′+n′ variable problem (Proposition 2), both solvable via network simplex and Sinkhorn-Knopp respectively. The resulting C-CG and C-PG algorithms update only K small matrices per iteration rather than the full m×n matrix, yielding time complexity improvements from O(mn(m+n)) to O(mn(m′+n′)) for gradient computation and correspondingly smaller subproblem solves. Experiments on synthetic 10-fold symmetric point sets and real-world 2D/3D shapes with approximate mirror symmetry (K=2) confirm that C-CG and C-PG match or improve GWD quality while achieving 2–26× speedups over their non-symmetric counterparts.

## Results

On synthetic 2D point sets with 10-order rotational symmetry (m=n=2000), C-CG with K=10 achieves a GWD of 3.75×10⁻³ (identical to CG's 3.75×10⁻³) while reducing computation time from 16.82 s to 0.64 s (~26× speedup). Similarly, C-PG with K=10 matches PG's GWD (6.89×10⁻³ vs. 6.88×10⁻³) while cutting time from 6.73 s to 0.94 s (~7× speedup). On real-world 2D and 3D shape datasets with approximate mirror symmetry (K=2), C-CG and C-PG (combined with symmetric alignment) produce larger inter-class GWDs, confusion matrices closer to the identity, and the fastest total computation among all four algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two proposed algorithms (C-CG, C-PG) against their non-symmetric counterparts (CG [41], PG [28]) on both synthetic and real-world data. On synthetic data, C-CG and C-PG match the GWD of CG and PG respectively (within ±0.01×10⁻³) while achieving 4.8×–26× and 1.9×–7.1× speedups depending on the symmetry order K used. On real-world 2D shapes (2D Shape Structure Dataset) and 3D shapes (ShapeNet), C-CG and C-PG with symmetric alignment not only compute faster but also produce *better* GWDs: larger inter-class distances, confusion matrices closer to the identity (i.e., higher 1-NN classification accuracy), and the fastest total runtime among all four algorithms. The authors attribute this quality improvement to the smaller solution space of the cyclic-symmetric algorithms, which avoids the poor local minima that CG and PG frequently reach due to their larger unconstrained search space. In the sensitivity experiment (Sec. 6.2), C-CG and C-PG maintain GWDs compatible with CG and PG across 50 random initializations, confirming that the block-circulant constraint on the initial solution does not systematically degrade solution quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work exploiting special input structures to accelerate Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) solving, joining prior efforts that leverage low-rank, hierarchical, 1D, or tree-metric structures. Its core contribution is introducing cyclic symmetry (block circulant structure) as a new exploitable property, building directly on the authors' earlier work on optimal transport with cyclic symmetry (Takeda et al., AAAI 2024) by showing the analogous preservation property holds for the non-convex GW setting. The algorithms C-CG and C-PG are fast variants of the standard Conditional Gradient (Vayer et al., 2019) and Projected Gradient (Peyré et al., 2016) methods, with the key theoretical result (Theorem 1) that initializing the coupling as block circulant guarantees all intermediate matrices retain that structure throughout the iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to introduce symmetry as a structural assumption for accelerating GW, opening a new axis of algorithmic design beyond rank or hierarchy. The practical speedup (up to ~26× on synthetic data) is substantial for applications involving symmetric objects (image registration, point cloud matching, stereo). However, the approach is limited to cyclic symmetry with a known order K, requires a symmetric alignment preprocessing step for approximate symmetry, and the restricted solution space may miss the global optimum in some instances. Generalizing to other symmetry groups, combining with other structures (low-rank, hierarchical), and providing global optimality guarantees remain open.
