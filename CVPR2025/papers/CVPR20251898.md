## Problem

Single-view hand-object interaction (HOI) reconstruction is ill-posed due to mutual occlusions, depth/scale ambiguity, and the vast diversity of hand poses and object shapes. Existing methods either assume known 3D object templates (limiting generalization) or train end-to-end on small, low-quality HOI datasets, resulting in poor robustness on in-the-wild images. The paper's key insight is that off-the-shelf large models for segmentation, inpainting, and 3D reconstruction provide strong visual and geometric priors that can be combined with a prior-guided optimization to handle these ambiguities without task-specific training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image depicting a hand-object interaction as input and produces a watertight 3D object mesh, a MANO-parameterized 3D hand mesh (global 6D pose + 45-DoF articulation), camera intrinsics/extrinsics in the object coordinate frame, and the resulting hand-object relative pose. No training data or 3D annotations are required; all reconstruction is performed at test time using a pipeline of pretrained models followed by gradient-based optimization. The hand is represented by the MANO parametric model, and the object is a free-form mesh with no category constraint.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, initial reconstruction: LISA segments hand and object regions, a diffusion model inpaints the occluded object to recover its complete appearance, SAM segments the full object, InstantMesh reconstructs the object mesh, and HaMeR predicts initial MANO hand parameters. Second, a three-step prior-guided optimization refines the result: (1) Camera System Setup aligns the object mesh to the image via differentiable rendering with a combined soft-IoU and Sinkhorn optimal-transport loss on the inpainted object mask; (2) HOI Contact Alignment converts 2D contact regions (from mutual occlusion) into 3D contact points via ray-casting and applies ICP to register hand and object; (3) Hand Parameter Refinement jointly optimizes global pose and articulation using a multi-loss objective comprising hand-mask IoU, signed-distance penetration loss, ObMan contact-zone proximity loss, and L1 regularization toward the initial HaMeR estimate. The entire optimization is test-time, requiring no learned HOI-specific parameters.

## Results

On all three datasets (Arctic, OakInk, DexYCB), EasyHOI achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (1.089, 1.035, 1.628 cm), the highest F-scores (e.g., F10 = 0.272 on Arctic, 0.445 on OakInk), the smallest Simulation Displacement (2.25, 3.08, 2.43 cm), and the lowest Intersection Volume (4.67, 4.11, 4.52 cm³) among all baselines. With ground-truth object geometry, the method also improves hand pose accuracy over HaMeR, reducing MPJPE from 1.05 to 0.95 cm on Arctic and from 1.03 to 0.77 cm on OakInk. The ablation confirms that each of the three optimization stages (Camera Setup, HOI Contact Alignment, Hand Parameter Refinement) contributes progressively, with the full pipeline reducing Simulation Displacement from 5.79 to 2.25 cm on Arctic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EasyHOI outperforms all four HOI reconstruction baselines (IHOI, AlignSDF, gSDF, MOHO) on every reported metric across all three datasets. On Chamfer Distance, EasyHOI achieves 1.089 cm on Arctic vs. 1.247 (gSDF, best baseline) and 1.375 (IHOI), and 1.035 cm on OakInk vs. 1.813 (AlignSDF) and 1.992 (gSDF); MOHO is an outlier at 12.878 cm on Arctic. For grasp stability (Simulation Displacement), EasyHOI records 2.25 cm on Arctic, beating gSDF (2.31) and IHOI (2.79), and 2.43 cm on DexYCB, beating MOHO (2.62) and IHOI (2.59). Intersection Volume is also lowest for EasyHOI on all datasets (e.g., 4.11 vs. 4.16 for gSDF on OakInk). On hand pose accuracy (Table 2), EasyHOI with predicted objects is slightly worse than HaMeR alone (MPJPE 1.36 vs. 1.05 on Arctic), but with ground-truth object geometry it surpasses HaMeR (0.95 vs. 1.05 MPJPE on Arctic; 0.77 vs. 1.03 on OakInk), demonstrating that the HOI optimization refines hand pose when object geometry is reliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training dedicated end-to-end HOI models (ObMan, IHOI, AlignSDF, gSDF, MOHO) by instead composing off-the-shelf large models—LISA for segmentation, a diffusion model for inpainting, InstantMesh for object reconstruction, and HaMeR for hand pose—followed by a three-stage prior-guided optimization. The optimization framework itself (differentiable-rendering camera alignment with Sinkhorn distance, ICP-based contact registration, and multi-loss hand refinement) is the methodological core, borrowing ICP from classical registration and contact zones from ObMan. The key conceptual shift is treating HOI reconstruction as a test-time composition and alignment problem rather than a learned regression task, leveraging the strong generalization of each individual large model to avoid the data-hunger of supervised HOI training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that zero-shot composition of large models plus lightweight optimization can match or exceed dedicated HOI methods across three diverse datasets, this work suggests a scalable path for HOI reconstruction that automatically benefits from future improvements in any constituent model (e.g., swapping InstantMesh for Tripo3D). The main limitation acknowledged is fragility: if any upstream model fails (poor segmentation, bad inpainting, inaccurate object geometry), the entire pipeline degrades, and the authors note that imperfect object geometry from LRM inevitably compromises hand accuracy. Open questions include whether the optimization can be made more efficient (currently iterative), whether end-to-end fine-tuning of the composed pipeline could close the remaining gap to GT-object performance, and how to handle multi-hand or multi-object interactions beyond the single-pair setting.
