## Problem

While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) perform well on vision-centric benchmarks, their behavior when textual context contradicts or is irrelevant to visual evidence remains largely unexplored. This paper investigates modality preference biases in VLMs and discovers a 'blind faith in text' phenomenon where models disproportionately trust textual data over visual data when inconsistencies arise, causing significant performance drops under corrupted text and raising safety concerns in applications like multi-modal RAG and phishing detection.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample pairs an image and a question with a text context of one of three types: matched (supports the correct answer), corrupted (supports a different answer), or irrelevant (unrelated to both image and question). The model is queried under three conditions—image-only, text-only, and both modalities—enabling a three-way comparison where the mixed-modality answer is categorized as aligning with the image answer, the text answer, or neither. The primary output metric is the Text Preference Ratio (TPR), measuring the fraction of inconsistent cases where the model follows text over image, reported alongside accuracy, normalized accuracy, and macro accuracy across all text variation conditions.

## Method

The paper constructs a 16,500-sample benchmark across four vision-centric tasks by generating matched and corrupted text via GPT-4o and sampling irrelevant passages from WikiText, then evaluates ten VLMs (proprietary and open-source) under all three text conditions. It systematically analyzes five factors influencing text bias: instruction prompts, language model size (7B→34B in LLaVA-NeXT), text relevance via BM25 retrieval, token ordering (text-before-image vs. image-before-text), and uni-modal generation certainty. For mitigation, supervised fine-tuning with LoRA on 1,000 text-augmented samples (spanning text-only, original VQA, and all three text variation types) substantially reduces text bias, raising corruption accuracy from 28.7% to 71.3% for LLaVA-NeXT-7B. A theoretical analysis further shows the blind faith in text phenomenon arises from the imbalance between pure-text training data (N) and multi-modal data (M) in VLM pre-training, where the cross-modal error term is weighted by M/(N+M) for text loss and N/(N+M) for multi-modal loss, systematically favoring text when N≫M.

## Results

Under text corruption, open-source VLMs suffer severe performance drops: LLaVA-NeXT-7B accuracy on VQAv2 falls from 79.45% to 28.69% (Norm 36.10%), and Qwen2-VL-7B drops from 85.51% to 50.79% (Norm 59.41%). Text Preference Ratios (TPR) confirm strong text bias, with LLaVA-NeXT-7B at 85.52% and Molmo-7B-D at 59.40% on VQAv2, while Claude Sonnet shows the lowest TPR at 9.58%. Supervised fine-tuning with text augmentation substantially mitigates this: LLaVA-NeXT-7B corruption accuracy improves from 28.69% to 71.25% and Qwen2-VL-7B from 50.79% to 82.72%, with macro accuracy gains of ~12 and ~9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 10 VLMs (4 proprietary, 6 open-source) under matched, corrupted, and irrelevant text conditions. Proprietary models are substantially more robust to text corruption: Claude Sonnet achieves the lowest TPR (9.58% on VQAv2, 3.21% on DocVQA) and highest corruption accuracy (68.17% on VQAv2, 84.60% on DocVQA), while GPT-4o follows with TPR of 27.09% and 17.96%. Open-source models exhibit dramatically higher text bias: LLaVA-NeXT-7B reaches 85.52% TPR on VQAv2 and 87.77% on DocVQA, with corruption accuracy collapsing to 28.69% and 10.00% respectively. Scaling within the LLaVA-NeXT family (7B→13B→34B) reduces TPR from 85.52% to 67.56% on VQAv2 but does not eliminate the bias. Qwen2-VL-7B is the most robust open model (TPR 29.22% on VQAv2), and Molmo-7B-D shows moderate bias (TPR 59.40%). After SFT with text augmentation, LLaVA-NeXT-7B's corruption accuracy jumps from 28.69% to 71.25% on VQAv2, surpassing even GPT-4o mini (51.55%), and Qwen2-VL-7B reaches 82.72%, approaching Claude Sonnet's 68.17%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-perturbation evaluation paradigm from NLP (Shi et al., 2023, who showed LLMs are distracted by irrelevant context) to the vision-language setting, where the critical question becomes which modality the model trusts. It repurposes standard VQA benchmarks (VQAv2, DocVQA, MathVista) by injecting controlled text variations (match, corruption, irrelevance) to isolate modality preference, a dimension absent from prior VLM evaluation protocols. The theoretical analysis draws on generalization bounds for ERM to formalize how the N≫M imbalance between pure-text and multi-modal training data induces text bias, connecting the empirical observation to a principled explanation rooted in the LLM backbone's pre-training distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are increasingly deployed in RAG pipelines, multi-modal agents, and safety-critical applications (e.g., phishing detection), the finding that models systematically over-trust text—even corrupted text—poses a concrete reliability and safety risk that standard benchmarks do not capture. The observation that open-source models exhibit stronger text bias than proprietary ones, despite comparable VQA accuracy, is particularly concerning for the open-model ecosystem. The SFT-with-text-augmentation remedy is effective but requires curated data and does not fully eliminate the bias, leaving open whether architectural changes or balanced multi-modal pre-training can resolve the issue more fundamentally.
