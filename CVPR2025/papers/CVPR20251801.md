## Problem

Spike cameras capture dynamic scenes with extremely high temporal resolution (40 kHz) but at low spatial resolution, making super-resolution reconstruction essential. However, spike streams suffer from inherent fluctuations caused by Poisson photon arrival, readout quantization, and thermal circuit noise, which render extracted features noisy and temporally incoherent. Existing spike camera super-resolution methods do not explicitly model or mitigate these fluctuations, leading to degraded reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

The network takes a low-resolution binary spike stream of size H×W×T (e.g., 360×640×101 for ×2, 240×426×101 for ×3, 180×320×101 for ×4) and produces a single high-resolution RGB image (720×1280 for ×2/×4, 720×1278 for ×3). The binary spikes are first converted to intensity frames via spike-interval-based reconstruction (SIR) to bridge the domain gap, then partitioned into K=9 overlapping short-term blocks of temporal length L=21 centered around the target reconstruction moment. The output is a single HR frame at the key time t₀, not a video sequence.

## Method

Spk2SRImgNet is an end-to-end CNN whose core contribution is the Motion Aligned Collaborative Filtering (MACF) module, inspired by the non-local similarity priors in BM3D, BAS, LSSC, and WNNM but adapted to exploit temporal rather than spatial similarity. MACF operates in six steps: (1) estimating forward/backward motion offsets via convolutional blocks, (2) channel rearrangement to group same-channel features across K moments, (3) deformable-convolution feature warping to align neighboring features to the key frame, (4) transform-domain stacked feature filtering (TDF) that applies a learned transform, estimates noise via temporal averaging residuals, filters through a U-Net, and inverse-transforms to produce per-moment refinement features, (5) inverse warping to reposition refinements back to original locations and aggregate with initial features, and (6) inverse channel rearrangement. The refined multi-moment features are then fed into a motion-aligned super-resolution reconstruction module that performs deformable-convolution alignment, multi-scale fusion, and pixel-shuffle upsampling. The entire network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss for 800 epochs using Adam.

## Results

On the REDS-based SCSR evaluation dataset, Spk2SRImgNet achieves PSNR of 34.50 dB (×2), 31.41 dB (×3), and 29.50 dB (×4), with corresponding SSIM of 0.9377, 0.8850, and 0.8369. On the Adobe240-based dataset, it reaches 37.22 dB (×2), 34.11 dB (×3), and 31.49 dB (×4) PSNR. The method also achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.1403, 0.2231, 0.2816 on REDS; 0.0816, 0.1327, 0.1822 on Adobe240) and the fastest runtime (0.81 s, 0.37 s, 0.21 s) among all compared methods, with only 3.72–3.90 M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spk2SRImgNet outperforms all eight baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three SR scales (×2, ×3, ×4) on both REDS and Adobe240 datasets. The closest dedicated SCSR competitor is SpikeSR-Net [50], which Spk2SRImgNet surpasses by 0.21 dB PSNR at ×2, 0.32 dB at ×3, and 0.28 dB at ×4 on REDS, while also achieving a 2.6× speedup (0.81 s vs. 2.14 s at ×2). VidarSR [42], the other dedicated SCSR method, trails by 2.53 dB (×2), 1.86 dB (×3), and 1.20 dB (×4) PSNR on REDS. The combined reconstruction+SR baselines (TFP+DAT, Spk2ImgNet+DAT, WGSE+DAT, BSF+DAT, and their BasicVSR++ variants [4,5]) are substantially weaker, with the best combined method (BSF+DAT) still 2.58 dB below Spk2SRImgNet at ×2 on REDS. Notably, Spk2SRImgNet uses fewer parameters (3.72–3.90 M) than all combined baselines (7.32–18.78 M) while delivering the best quality and fastest inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spike camera super resolution (SCSR) line initiated by VidarSR and SpikeSR-Net, which both learn to map low-resolution spike streams to high-resolution images. Its key conceptual departure is repurposing the non-local collaborative filtering paradigm from classic image restoration (BM3D, BAS, WNNM) into a deep learning module (MACF) that exploits temporal similarity across spike frames rather than spatial patch similarity. For motion alignment, it follows the deformable-convolution approach established in video SR (EDVR, BasicVSR++), and for the initial spike-to-intensity conversion it relies on the spike-interval reconstruction (SIR) formulation from Zhu et al. The MACF module's transform-domain filtering with a U-Net and noise-estimation branch is the novel architectural contribution, bridging classical signal-processing priors with end-to-end trainable networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spike cameras offer 40 kHz temporal resolution but at low spatial resolution, making SR a critical enabling step for high-speed vision applications in autonomous driving and robotics. The MACF module addresses a practical and previously under-addressed problem—spike fluctuations from Poisson noise, quantization, and thermal effects—that degrades both feature quality and motion-alignment accuracy. Limitations include reliance on synthesized training data (real-world evaluation is qualitative only), evaluation on a single SR scale per dataset, and the absence of spatial non-local similarity (explicitly deferred to future work). Open questions include whether the collaborative filtering idea generalizes to color spike cameras, longer temporal sequences, or real-time deployment on embedded neuromorphic hardware.
