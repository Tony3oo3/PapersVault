## Problem

Temporal Δ-Σ DNNs exploit inter-frame redundancy to drastically reduce computation in video processing, but their neuron states (storing convolution outputs for inter-frame differencing) consume memory that exceeds the on-chip SRAM of embedded event-driven platforms. Directly compressing or skipping these states breaks the linearity of Δ-Σ modulation, causing error accumulation over long video sequences, while off-chip DRAM storage incurs ~100× the energy cost of on-chip access, negating the energy savings from sparse computation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential video frames (20–30 frame clips at resolutions ranging from 256×256 to 512×512 depending on the task) and produces task-specific outputs: 2D pose keypoints (MPII), object bounding boxes (UA-DETRAC), or pixel-level segmentation masks (Cityscapes). Uniquely, each frame's inference depends on the stored neuron state from the previous frame via Δ-Σ accumulation, so the model is inherently stateful across the video sequence and must maintain error-free long-term processing without periodic keyframe resets.

## Method

MEET reframes the state-memory bottleneck as a weight-compression problem by trading fewer activations for more weights (A→W tradeoff), implemented through a Compute- and State Memory-aware Neural Architecture Search (CSM-NAS) that uses NSGA-III to jointly optimize accuracy, static computation, and state memory footprint over a search space of FuseV2 MB blocks (fusing depthwise and projection convolutions to reduce activation count) with varying kernel sizes and expansion ratios. The framework then co-designs the network with a mixed spatial-temporal suppression strategy: spatial suppression (e.g., ReLU-based) in early stages where large feature maps dominate state memory, and temporal Δ-Σ suppression (using EvNet with power-of-2 thresholds) in deeper stages where computation intensifies and the A→W tradeoff is most effective. Finally, well-established weight compression (pruning, quantization) reduces the inflated weight count, yielding a model that fits within on-chip SRAM while preserving both computation efficiency and error-free long-term Δ-Σ accuracy.

## Results

On MPII pose estimation (256×256, 20 frames), MEET-Mix@16× achieves 86.47% PCK@0.5 with 54M dynamic cycles, 6.8 MB state memory, and 12.8 MB total memory, fitting within the 36 MB SRAM of the GrAI-VIP platform and delivering 2.7× energy savings over the ReLU-sparse baseline. On UA-DETRAC object detection (384×384, 30 frames), MEET-Full reaches 51.50% mAP@0.5 with 5M dynamic cycles and 26.7 MB total memory, outperforming MobileNetV2 (50.15%, 7M cycles, 86.4 MB). On Cityscapes semantic segmentation (256×512, 30 frames), MEET-Mix@16× attains 50.93% mIoU with 60M dynamic cycles and 17 MB total memory, compared to MobileNetV2's 50.24%, 64M cycles, and 87 MB. Overall, MEET reduces total memory by 5.1–13.3× relative to the most computation-efficient temporal DNNs while preserving accuracy within 1.0% tolerance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEET-Mix matches EvNet [18] on dynamic cycles (54M vs 53M) and accuracy (86.47% vs 86.40% PCK) but reduces state memory from 66.2 MB to 6.8 MB, fitting within the 36 MB SRAM budget where EvNet requires off-chip DRAM access (7.4× energy penalty). Against the closest mixed-execution competitor ELSE [69], MEET-Mix achieves 23M fewer dynamic cycles (54M vs 77M), 3.2 MB less total memory (12.8 vs 16.0 MB), and 0.23 lower normalized energy (0.37 vs 0.60). Temporal methods that skip or compress states (TempDiff [14], RRM [49]) either suffer large accuracy drops (−3.61% for TempDiff) or fail to fit in SRAM (RRM: 37.9 MB total), whereas MEET preserves accuracy within 0.5% and stays on-chip. In object detection, MEET-Full outperforms MobileNetV2 [51] by 1.35% mAP (51.50% vs 50.15%) while cutting total memory from 86.4 MB to 26.7 MB and dynamic cycles from 7M to 5M; it also beats GhostNet [28] (48.94% mAP, 80.7 MB) and ResNet18 [30] (51.13% mAP, 28.4 MB) on both accuracy and memory. In semantic segmentation, MEET-Mix@16× surpasses MobileNetV2 [55] on mIoU (50.93% vs 50.24%) and reduces total memory from 87 MB to 17 MB, while also outperforming MEET-Full in dynamic cycles (60M vs 66M) due to camera motion limiting temporal suppression in early layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEET builds on the temporal Δ-Σ convolution line (Skip-convolutions, EvNet, DeltaCNN) that exploits inter-frame redundancy for video inference, and on event-driven/neuromorphic hardware (GrAI-VIP, NeuronFlow) that exploits activation sparsity for energy savings. It departs from prior temporal methods (RRM, TempDiff, DAL, ELSE) that either compress/skip neuron states (causing error accumulation) or selectively apply temporal execution, by reframing the state memory bottleneck as a weight compression problem via an A→W tradeoff. The CSM-NAS component draws on multi-objective evolutionary NAS (NSGA-III) and the FuseV2 block design extends the inverted bottleneck architecture of MobileNetV2 with layer fusion specifically for temporal sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MEET enables on-chip deployment of temporal Δ-Σ DNNs on embedded event-driven platforms, a practical gap that prior work overlooked by underestimating state memory. The A→W tradeoff insight—that deliberately increasing static computations is viable when temporal sparsity will eliminate them—reframes state compression as the well-studied weight compression problem, opening the door to mature pruning/quantization tools. Limitations include evaluation on a single hardware platform (GrAI-VIP with 36 MB SRAM) and three vision tasks; generalizability to other neuromorphic architectures, larger models, or non-video modalities remains open. The mixed spatial-temporal design also requires per-application tuning of the downscale factor λ, suggesting the co-design space is not yet fully automated.
