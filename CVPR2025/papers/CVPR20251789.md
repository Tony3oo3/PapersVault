## Problem

Autoregressive models have demonstrated strong scalability and zero-shot generalization across many tasks, but monocular depth estimation lacks an obvious sequential structure that AR prediction requires. This paper asks whether the inherent ordering properties of depth maps—resolution (low-to-high) and value granularity (coarse-to-fine)—can be reformulated as autoregressive objectives to unlock the scalability and generalization benefits of AR architectures for MDE.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251789/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (H×W×3) and produces a per-pixel depth map, but internally it generates K=5 progressively higher-resolution depth maps, each conditioned on all previous ones. The image is encoded by a ViT-L encoder into 1536-channel tokens at 1/8 resolution, which serve as cross-attention context throughout all AR steps. Depth values are discretized into N=16 bins per step, with the full range set to [0.1, 10] m for NYU Depth V2 and [0.1, 80] m for KITTI; the final depth is a linear combination of bin centers and softmax probabilities.

## Method

DAR couples two autoregressive objectives: (1) a resolution AR objective where a decoder-only Transformer with a patch-wise causal mask predicts each next-resolution token map conditioned on all prefix token maps and global image features, and (2) a granularity AR objective via Multiway Tree Bins (MTBin), which recursively subdivides the depth range around the previous step's prediction (expanded by one adjacent bin for error tolerance) into N finer sub-bins. A Bins Injection module projects the new bin candidates through 3×3 convolutions and feeds them as context into a ConvGRU that refines the Transformer's output logits, thereby linking the two AR processes. The model is trained end-to-end with a scale-invariant loss summed over all K steps, and scales from 440M to 2.0B parameters by increasing Transformer depth and width.

## Results

On KITTI, DAR-Large (2.0B) achieves RMSE 1.799, Abs Rel 0.044, and δ₁ 0.986, improving RMSE by 5% over the previous SOTA Depth Anything (1.896). On NYU Depth V2, DAR-Large attains RMSE 0.205 and δ₁ 0.982, outperforming Depth Anything (RMSE 0.235, δ₁ 0.975) by a clear margin. Even the smallest variant DAR-Small (440M) surpasses Depth Anything (343M) on both datasets, achieving RMSE 1.839 on KITTI and 0.217 on NYU. In zero-shot transfer to SUN RGB-D, DAR (trained only on NYU) achieves RMSE 0.319 and δ₁ 0.885, beating Depth Anything (RMSE 0.346, δ₁ 0.864) which was pretrained on 61M images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAR outperforms the previous SOTA Depth Anything [63] on every metric on both KITTI and NYU Depth V2: on KITTI, DAR-Small (440M) already beats Depth Anything (343M) in RMSE (1.839 vs. 1.896) and δ₁ (0.984 vs. 0.982), while DAR-Large extends the gap to 5% in RMSE (1.799 vs. 1.896). Against the best supervised baselines (EcoDepth, IEBins, DCDepth, WorDepth), DAR-Small matches or exceeds them in RMSE with a smaller model (440M vs. 259–954M), and DAR-Large dominates across all metrics. On NYU Depth V2, DAR-Large improves RMSE from 0.235 (Depth Anything) to 0.205 and δ₁ from 0.975 to 0.982, surpassing all prior methods including VPD (0.254) and EcoDepth (0.218). In zero-shot transfer to SUN RGB-D, DAR trained solely on NYU Depth V2 achieves RMSE 0.319 and δ₁ 0.885, outperforming Depth Anything (0.346, 0.864) which relied on 61M pretraining images, as well as VPD (0.355, 0.861) and IEBins (0.405, 0.822). The paper does not report any metric where DAR underperforms the compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAR sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: ordinal-regression depth estimation (DORN, AdaBins, BinsFormer, IEBins) and autoregressive visual generation (VAR, VQGAN, RQ-Transformer). It departs from the standard encoder-decoder MDE paradigm by reframing low-to-high resolution feature fusion as a next-resolution autoregressive prediction with a patch-wise causal mask, and by extending fixed-bin ordinal regression into a recursive, prediction-conditioned bin refinement (MTBin). The Bins Injection module via ConvGRU is a novel coupling mechanism that threads the granularity objective into the latent token space, distinguishing DAR from prior ordinal-regression methods that use static or adaptively-sized bins without progressive autoregressive refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAR is the first demonstration that autoregressive modeling with scaling laws applies to dense depth prediction, opening a path to integrating depth estimation into autoregressive foundation models (e.g., GPT-4o-style MLLMs). The zero-shot generalization to SUN RGB-D without the 61M-image pretraining that Depth Anything requires is practically significant, suggesting the AR inductive bias may be more data-efficient. However, the multi-step progressive paradigm risks blurring sharp boundaries, and the high parameter count (up to 2B) limits deployment; distillation and lightweight AR design remain open. Whether the scaling trend continues beyond 2B and whether the approach transfers to other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, flow) are natural next questions.
