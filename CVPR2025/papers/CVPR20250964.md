## Problem

CLIP's contrastive pre-training is computationally expensive, and existing patch-masking strategies to reduce cost either remove critical semantic content (random masking in FLIP), add heavy attention-based modules (A-CLIP), or lack the granularity to selectively preserve primary object regions (cluster-based E-CLIP). The paper asks how to mask image patches for training efficiency while deliberately retaining the semantically important regions that drive vision-language alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250964/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images (split into 196 patches of 16×16 by ViT-B/16) and 77-token text sequences from a 49K vocabulary. Before the image encoder, a dynamic subset of patches (30–50% depending on the variant) is masked out, so the encoder processes only the retained patches. The output is a pair of L2-normalized embeddings in a shared 512-dim space, used directly for zero-shot classification via text-prompt matching, bidirectional retrieval, and linear probing.

## Method

CLIP-PGS replaces FLIP's one-shot random 50% masking with a gradual generation-to-selection pipeline. First, a small set of candidate patches (5% initial ratio) is randomly pre-selected as potential mask regions. Second, Sobel edge detection is applied to the full image to produce an edge map; patches near strong edges (object boundaries) are assigned higher retention priority. Third, cosine similarities between all patches are computed as a weighted combination of feature-space and pixel-space affinities (S = αS_x + (1−α)S_I), and the Sinkhorn algorithm is applied to normalize this matrix into a doubly stochastic form (optimal transport normalization), ensuring balanced similarity distribution. Finally, patches are ranked by their refined similarity scores and progressively masked until the target ratio (fixed 0.5 or dynamic 0.3–0.5) is reached, with the edge map acting as a hard constraint that prevents masking of high-edge-score patches. The total overhead of edge detection and Sinkhorn normalization is under 3% of training time, yet the structured selection preserves semantically critical regions that random or cluster-based masking would discard.

## Results

CLIP-PGS_0.3 achieves a 39.5% average zero-shot classification accuracy across 17 datasets, surpassing the strongest baseline E-CLIP (36.9%) by 2.6% and the original CLIP (35.1%) by 4.4%, while training at only 0.6× the time of CLIP. On linear probing, CLIP-PGS_0.3 reaches 90.0% on CIFAR-10, 72.3% on CIFAR-100, and 64.4% on ImageNet-1K, outperforming E-CLIP by 1.0%, 2.6%, and 1.7% respectively. For robustness, CLIP-PGS_0.3 attains 32.9% in-distribution and 31.8% out-of-distribution average accuracy on ImageNet variants, the best among all compared methods. In zero-shot retrieval, CLIP-PGS_0.3 achieves 36.0% R@1 on MS-COCO text retrieval and 25.1% R@1 on MS-COCO image retrieval, leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (the unmasked baseline), CLIP-PGS_0.3 improves average zero-shot accuracy by 4.4% (39.5% vs. 35.1%) while reducing training time to 0.6×, and CLIP-PGS_0.5 gains 2.5% at 0.5× training time. Compared to FLIP (random masking), CLIP-PGS_0.3 achieves a 6.5% average gain (39.5% vs. 33.0%) at nearly the same 0.5×–0.6× training cost, demonstrating that structured masking preserves far more semantic content than random removal. Versus A-CLIP (attention-based masking), CLIP-PGS_0.3 outperforms by 3.6% on average (39.5% vs. 35.9%) while training 45% faster (0.6× vs. 1.1×), showing that the Sobel + OTN pipeline avoids the extra computational modules of attention scoring. Against E-CLIP (cluster-based masking), the closest competitor at the same 0.6× training time, CLIP-PGS_0.3 leads by 2.6% on average (39.5% vs. 36.9%) and wins on 12 of 17 individual datasets, with particularly large margins on CIFAR-10 (+2.8%), CIFAR-100 (+5.3%), and VOC2007 (+11.5%). On robustness (ImageNet variants) and language compositionality (SugarCrepe), CLIP-PGS_0.3 also achieves the best ID/OOD averages (32.9%/31.8%) and the highest overall Recall@1 (67.9%), confirming consistent gains beyond standard classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masking-for-CLIP-efficiency line initiated by FLIP (random patch masking) and refined by A-CLIP (attention-based token retention) and E-CLIP (cluster-based masking). Its key departure is replacing these selection heuristics with a two-stage 'generation-to-selection' pipeline: Sobel edge detection to protect object boundaries, followed by Sinkhorn optimal transport normalization on patch similarity scores to balance which regions to mask. Conceptually, it repurposes a classical image-processing tool (Sobel) and a mathematical optimization technique (optimal transport) within the established CLIP dual-encoder training loop, rather than introducing new architectural components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLIP-PGS demonstrates that a simple, low-overhead masking strategy (<3% extra compute) can simultaneously halve training time and improve zero-shot accuracy, robustness, and compositionality over both the unmasked CLIP baseline and more complex masking alternatives. This is practically significant for practitioners who need efficient vision-language pre-training without large GPU clusters. However, the evaluation is limited to CC12M and ViT-B/16, leaving open whether the edge-detection + OTN combination generalizes to larger datasets (e.g., LAION-400M), deeper backbones, or non-transformer architectures. The authors also note the approach has not yet been tested in self-supervised settings like MAE, a natural but unexplored extension.
