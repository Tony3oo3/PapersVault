## Problem

Existing fine-tuning methods for VLMs in multi-step reasoning—SFT and PPO-based RL—suffer from limited solution diversity and poor generalization: SFT maximizes likelihood over a fixed dataset, while PPO converges to a single reward-maximizing policy. Prior GFlowNet-based reasoning approaches (e.g., FoR) operate only on text and assume Markovian structure, leaving the multimodal, long-horizon setting of embodied AI unaddressed. GFlowVLM is the first to fuse GFlowNets directly with a VLM under a non-Markovian formulation for structured sequential decision-making.

## Input / Output

At each step t, the model receives the current RGB observation image o_t and a structured text prompt p_t that concatenates the goal description, the full history of environment states s_{0:t} and actions a_{0:t}, and the set of admissible actions A_t for the current observation. The output is a JSON-formatted response containing a free-text chain-of-thought field (image description plus intermediate reasoning) and an action field selecting one admissible action; a special [DONE] token signals task completion. The non-Markovian design means the prompt grows with episode length, explicitly conditioning on all prior transitions rather than only the current state.

## Method

GFlowVLM fine-tunes LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B as the forward policy P_F of a GFlowNet, where each trajectory is a sequence of (observation, CoT, action) transitions ending in a terminal state. The log forward policy at step t is a weighted sum of the action token log-probabilities and the CoT token log-probabilities (weight λ=0.4), so the GFlowNet training signal shapes both the reasoning text and the chosen action simultaneously. Three GFlowNet objectives are used—Variance Trajectory-Balanced (Var-TB), Subtrajectory-Balanced (SubTB), and Detailed-Balanced (DB)—with SubTB and DB requiring an SFT warm-start on correctly completed trajectories to stabilize estimation of the termination probability P_F(⊤|·). The framework supports off-policy training by replaying transitions from a buffer across multiple trajectories, and applies reward shaping (e.g., R(x)=100/(|c−y_t|+1) for NumberLine) to convert originally negative environment rewards into the non-negative form GFlowNets require.

## Results

On NumberLine, GFlowVLM achieves 100% in-distribution success rate (vs. 89.4% for RL4VLM) and 9.1% OOD success rate (vs. 3.1% for RL4VLM). On BlackJack, GFlowVLM with DB loss reaches 42.2% success rate, marginally outperforming RL4VLM (40.2%). On ALFWorld, GFlowVLM with SubTB achieves 26.1% average success rate (vs. 22.1% for RL4VLM non-Markovian), 12.3% OOD success rate (vs. 6.1%), and Div@16 of 1.40 (vs. 1.11), representing 29%, 100%, and 27% improvements respectively. GFlowVLM also converges ~10,000 environment steps faster than RL4VLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is RL4VLM [41], which fine-tunes the same LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B using PPO in a Markovian setting. GFlowVLM wins decisively on NumberLine in-distribution (100% vs. 89.4%) and OOD (9.1% vs. 3.1%, a 322% relative improvement), and on ALFWorld across all metrics (26.1% vs. 22.1% average, 12.3% vs. 6.1% OOD, 1.40 vs. 1.11 Div@16). On BlackJack, the gap is smaller (42.2% vs. 40.2%), likely due to the stochastic nature of the game limiting the benefit of diverse trajectory sampling. Against SFT baselines, GFlowVLM achieves roughly 4× higher success rates on both card games and a 29% absolute gain on ALFWorld. The paper also shows that even when RL4VLM is given the same non-Markovian prompt and revised reward (RL4VLM†), GFlowVLM still leads on OOD generalization, indicating the advantage stems from the GFlowNet training objective rather than prompt design alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet fine-tuning paradigm from text-only LLMs (as in FoR [40]) to multimodal Vision-Language Models, and introduces non-Markovian trajectory modeling where the full history of states and actions is retained in the prompt. It builds directly on the three canonical GFlowNet training objectives—Trajectory Balance [23], Subtrajectory Balance [22], and Detailed Balance [2]—adapting them to a VLM forward policy that jointly generates chain-of-thought reasoning and actions. The key departure from prior VLM fine-tuning work (SFT and PPO-based RL4VLM [41]) is the shift from reward maximization to reward-proportional sampling, which the authors argue preserves solution diversity and long-range dependencies that Markovian, single-mode policies discard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first direct fusion of GFlowNets with VLMs opens a path toward structured, diverse reasoning in embodied and interactive settings where PPO's single-policy optimization and SFT's IID assumption are known to underperform. The large OOD generalization gaps (322% on NumberLine, 156% on ALFWorld) suggest that reward-proportional sampling genuinely captures broader reasoning structures rather than overfitting to a narrow set of trajectories. However, the evaluation is limited to a 7B model, a single-agent setting, and relatively constrained tasks (card games, ALFWorld), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger VLMs, multi-agent scenarios, or more complex embodied environments. The critical dependence on SFT initialization for SubTB and DB losses (performance collapses without it) also raises practical questions about the robustness of the pipeline.
