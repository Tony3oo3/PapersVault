## Problem

Weakly supervised referring expression comprehension (WREC) and segmentation (WRES) have long been treated as separate single-task problems, despite their natural complementarity. Joint multi-task learning under weak supervision is non-trivial due to conflicting training objectives (contrastive vs. pseudo-label learning), divergent visual feature requirements, and asymmetric task difficulty, making naive combination of the two tasks ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250590/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes an RGB image resized to 416×416 and a referring expression (max 15 words for RefCOCO/RefCOCO+, 20 for RefCOCOg) as input, and simultaneously outputs a bounding box (via anchor-based detection with cosine similarity matching) and a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask (via an ASPP-based decoder with sigmoid thresholding at 0.5). The WREC branch operates on the lowest-resolution feature map (1/8 scale) for anchor generation, while the WRES branch fuses multi-scale features for pixel-level prediction.

## Method

WeakMCN employs a dual-branch architecture where the WREC branch performs anchor-text contrastive learning and simultaneously acts as a teacher, feeding its predicted bounding box into SAM to generate pseudo-masks that supervise the WRES branch. Two key modules enable effective collaboration: Dynamic Visual Feature Enhancement (DVFE) maintains a visual bank of features from DarkNet, SAM, and DINOv2, then computes task-specific softmax weights to adaptively combine these features for each branch; and the Collaborative Consistency Module (CCM) enforces cross-task alignment via a Spatial Consistency Loss (projecting 2D masks onto 1D axes and computing Dice loss against the WREC-predicted box) and an Inconsistency Suppression Loss (gating the segmentation loss by an IoU threshold between the predicted mask and bounding box mask to filter unreliable pseudo-labels). The overall loss combines the contrastive anchor-text loss, binary cross-entropy segmentation loss, and both CCM losses with learned balancing coefficients.

## Results

On WREC, WeakMCN (ViT-tiny) achieves 68.55% IoU@0.5 on RefCOCO val, 51.48% on RefCOCO+ val, and 53.44% on RefCOCOg val-g, outperforming the previous best WREC method APL by +3.91%, +9.00%, and +4.40% respectively. On WRES, WeakMCN (ViT-tiny) achieves 58.15% mIoU on RefCOCO val, 41.48% on RefCOCO+ val, and 44.83% on RefCOCOg val-g, surpassing the best prior WRES method PPT by +13.11% on RefCOCO and +8.93% on RefCOCOg. In semi-supervised settings with only 1% labeled data, WeakMCN outperforms RefTeacher by over 10% on Semi-REC and SemiRes by over 8% on Semi-RES across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For WREC, WeakMCN decisively outperforms the strongest single-task baseline APL (ECCV'24) by +3.91% on RefCOCO val, +9.00% on RefCOCO+ val, and +4.40% on RefCOCOg val-g, and also surpasses RefCLIP (CVPR'23) by +8.19% on RefCOCO val. For WRES, WeakMCN achieves the largest gains over PPT (CVPR'24), the previous best WRES method, with +13.11% mIoU on RefCOCO val and +8.93% on RefCOCOg val-g, while on RefCOCO+ it trails PPT by −2.76% (41.48 vs. 45.34), the only dataset where a baseline retains an edge. In the semi-supervised setting (1% labeled data), WeakMCN outperforms RefTeacher (CVPR'23) by +10.01% on RefCOCO val and +12.67% on RefCOCO+ val for Semi-REC, and beats SemiRes (ICML'24) by +8.21% on RefCOCO val and +6.51% on RefCOCO+ val for Semi-RES. Notably, all WRES baselines in Table 1 require additional image-text pairs for training, whereas WeakMCN uses only the standard image-text pairs, making its WRES gains particularly significant. The multi-task formulation also yields a consistent WREC improvement over the single-task RefCLIP baseline (63.52 → 68.55 on RefCOCO val), confirming that joint learning with DVFE and CCM benefits both tasks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: weakly supervised REC (WREC), which uses anchor-text contrastive learning (RefCLIP, APL), and weakly supervised RES (WRES), which relies on pseudo-label generation (TRIS, PPT). While fully supervised multi-task REC+RES networks exist (MCN, OneRef, C³VG), the weakly supervised joint formulation had not been explored. WeakMCN inherits the contrastive anchor-matching paradigm from RefCLIP for the WREC branch, the multi-modal fusion segmentation head from MCN for the WRES branch, and SAM for pseudo-mask generation, then introduces DVFE (combining DarkNet, DINOv2, and SAM features) and CCM (spatial consistency + inconsistency suppression losses) to resolve the inherent multi-task conflict between contrastive and pseudo-label learning objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-task collaboration under weak supervision can simultaneously improve both WREC and WRES over their single-task counterparts, challenging the assumption that weakly supervised tasks with different learning paradigms (contrastive vs. pseudo-label) cannot benefit from joint training. The large WRES gains (+13.11% on RefCOCO) without requiring extra image-text pairs are practically significant, as prior WRES methods needed additional data. However, the WRES performance on RefCOCO+ actually decreases slightly (−2.76%), and the method's reliance on SAM for pseudo-labels introduces a quality ceiling that limits gains in complex scenes. Open questions include whether the multi-task benefit generalizes to other weak supervision signals (e.g., point annotations) and whether the DVFE visual bank approach scales to more diverse foundation models.
