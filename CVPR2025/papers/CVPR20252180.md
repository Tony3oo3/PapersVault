## Problem

Traditional incremental SfM pipelines rely on low-dimensional parametric camera models (3–12 parameter polynomials) that fail to self-calibrate when images exhibit extreme or unknown distortion patterns, such as fisheye or catadioptric optics. No existing pipeline can jointly reconstruct 3D structure and estimate a flexible, non-parametric intrinsic calibration from scratch without prior knowledge of the camera model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252180/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an unordered collection of RGB images from arbitrary central cameras (pinhole through catadioptric) along with 2D-2D feature matches, requiring no prior intrinsics beyond an assumed principal point at the image center. It outputs a 3D point cloud, full 6-DoF camera poses, and a per-camera non-parametric calibration map represented as a cubic spline from opening angle to image radius. The calibration map is inherently partial: only angular intervals with sufficient point constraints are calibrated, and the calibrated region grows incrementally during reconstruction.

## Method

GenSfM represents camera intrinsics as an adaptive spline-based mapping M[θ]→r (opening angle to image radius) with a configurable number of control points (default 10), enabling smooth, invertible modeling of arbitrary radial distortions. The pipeline initializes with a radial quadrifocal tensor (13-point solver in RANSAC from [8]), registers new images using an implicit distortion pose estimator [18], and progressively calibrates each camera via a two-step adaptive scheme: (1) identifying a calibrated angular interval by clustering angle-radius pairs by local density, and (2) robustly fitting spline control points via RANSAC within that interval. A mixed triangulation strategy combines full 2D reprojection constraints for points inside the calibrated region with distortion-invariant 1D radial constraints for points outside, and bundle adjustment minimizes the corresponding full or radial reprojection errors while jointly refining the spline radii, 3D points, and camera poses.

## Results

On the ETH3D benchmark (raw DSLR images), GenSfM achieves an average AUC@5° of 71.8 for camera pose estimation, substantially outperforming COLMAP with pinhole (22.8), radial (50.0), and thin-prism (61.5) models, as well as the non-parametric RadialSfM+implicit baseline (27.3). On artificially distorted ETH3D images, the advantage is even more dramatic: GenSfM scores 43.2 AUC@5° versus 2.4, 1.9, and 0.3 for the three COLMAP variants and 14.5 for the non-parametric baseline. On BabelCalib, GenSfM with optimized principal points achieves reprojection errors comparable to the ground-truth BabelCalib calibration (e.g., 0.89 vs. 1.52 on OV corner) and significantly lower than the implicit distortion model [18] (2.09).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against COLMAP [24] with three parametric camera models (pinhole, radial, thin-prism), GenSfM wins on average AUC@5° (71.8 vs. 22.8/50.0/61.5) on raw ETH3D and by a large margin on distorted ETH3D (43.2 vs. 2.4/1.9/0.3), where all COLMAP variants fail to initialize. Against the non-parametric RadialSfM+implicit baseline [13]+[18], GenSfM achieves 71.8 vs. 27.3 AUC@5° on raw ETH3D and 43.2 vs. 14.5 on distorted ETH3D, with the gap widening under distortion due to the calibrated region providing stronger regularization. On BabelCalib calibration, GenSfM with optimized principal points matches the ground-truth BabelCalib [16] reprojection error (e.g., 0.89 vs. 1.52 on OV corner) and substantially outperforms the implicit distortion model [18] (0.89 vs. 2.09). The paper notes that COLMAP's performance is highly sensitive to the chosen parametric model (e.g., thin-prism scores 95.5 on terrace but 0.1 on terrains), whereas GenSfM's non-parametric spline adapts automatically, making it more robust across diverse scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenSfM sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: calibration-free SfM based on the radial alignment constraint (Larsson et al. [13], Hruby et al. [8]) and implicit distortion modeling for pose estimation (Pan et al. [18]). The key departure is replacing the fixed-form polynomial distortion functions used in all standard parametric pipelines (COLMAP, Brown-Conrady, Kannala-Brandt) with an adaptive spline-based non-parametric representation that is self-calibrated during reconstruction. The incremental pipeline structure follows COLMAP [24] and RadialSfM [13], but the mixed triangulation (2D reprojection in calibrated regions, 1D radial constraints elsewhere) and the progressive calibration scheme are new contributions that bridge the gap between fully uncalibrated radial SfM and fully calibrated parametric SfM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical path to incorporating arbitrary optical systems (fisheye, catadioptric, unknown in-the-wild lenses) into SfM pipelines without requiring the user to select or know the correct parametric distortion model in advance. The adaptive calibration mechanism—only fitting the spline where constraints are sufficient—addresses the overfitting risk of high-DoF non-parametric models, which is a genuine practical concern. Limitations include the assumption of radial symmetry and a known principal point (image center), and the single-segment calibration design, which may underutilize constraints in images with complex multi-region distortion. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-radially symmetric distortions, how it scales to very large image collections, and whether the spline representation can be replaced by learned (e.g., neural) distortion maps for even greater generality.
