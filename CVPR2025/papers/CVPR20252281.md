## Problem

Traditional hyperspectral image (HSI) calibration relies on physical white references that occlude the scene, require manual double-shot capture, and restrict camera mobility. No learning-based method existed to automatically estimate and remove global illumination from a single uncalibrated HSI, making it impossible to obtain physically accurate reflectance in dynamic natural scenes without a reference panel.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single uncalibrated hyperspectral image (204 bands spanning 400–1000 nm at 512×512 spatial resolution, 12-bit linear, or a 31-channel 400–700 nm subset) and produces the corresponding calibrated reflectance as if the scene were lit by an ideal white source. No reference measurement, second capture, or external illumination estimate is required at inference time; the network infers the illumination purely from the image content.

## Method

SIT is a U-shaped transformer (encoder–decoder with skip connections) built on the HCANet spectral-attention backbone, augmented with a novel Illumination Attention (IA) module inspired by the Gray-World assumption. In each SIT-U layer, the IA branch progressively downscales features via strided convolutions and average pooling to extract a per-channel global illumination descriptor, which is linearly projected into query/key vectors and used to compute an illumination attention matrix A_I. This matrix is element-wise multiplied with the spectral attention matrix A_S and softmax-normalized to form the final spectral-illumination attention, which modulates the attention value before the feed-forward output. The model is trained end-to-end with L1 pixel-wise loss and Adam (lr = 1e-4) for 500 epochs on 256×256 crops, learning to map uncalibrated intensity directly to reflectance without any explicit illumination parameterization.

## Results

On the full-spectrum BJTU-UVA test set (204 channels, 400–1000 nm), SIT achieves PSNR 26.3, SAM 3.1°, RMSE 5.8%, and ERGAS 9.6%, outperforming the strongest baseline HCANet (PSNR 25.7, SAM 3.6°, RMSE 6.4%, ERGAS 9.7%). On the expanded BJTU-UVA-E test set (7,650 pairs with 10 diverse illuminations), SIT reaches PSNR 39.1, SAM 2.0°, RMSE 1.3%, and ERGAS 1.9%, again leading all baselines. On the 31-channel subset, SIT similarly attains the best scores (e.g., PSNR 41.1, SAM 1.9° on BJTU-UVA-E test). The ablation confirms that combining Spectral Attention (SA) and Illumination Attention (IA) yields the best SAM (2.9° vs. 4.1° for IA alone and 4.4° for SA alone on BJTU-UVA validation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIT outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against HCANet (the closest deep-learning competitor), SIT gains +0.6 dB PSNR and −0.5° SAM on BJTU-UVA, and +1.8 dB PSNR and −0.5° SAM on BJTU-UVA-E. SERT, a rectangle self-attention denoising model, lags SIT by 3.5 dB PSNR and 8.0° SAM on BJTU-UVA, indicating that denoising architectures do not transfer well to illumination calibration. DivIll, an indoor spectral restoration method, is competitive on PSNR (24.1 vs. 26.3) but suffers a much higher SAM (6.1° vs. 3.1°), showing it preserves spectral shape less faithfully. Notably, the classic Gray-World heuristic achieves a SAM (3.1°) comparable to SIT on the original BJTU-UVA dataset with natural illuminations, but degrades sharply on BJTU-UVA-E (SAM 2.8° vs. 2.0°) where non-natural and color-filtered illuminations are present, confirming that learning-based methods generalize better to unseen illumination types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the U-shaped transformer architecture from HCANet [13] (originally designed for HSI denoising) by introducing an illumination attention module inspired by the classic Gray-World white-balancing algorithm [5], repurposing a 40-year-old perceptual heuristic as a learnable spectral attention mechanism. It deliberately departs from the physical-reference paradigm (synchronous/asynchronous calibration) that dominates remote sensing and laboratory HSI work, and distinguishes itself from RGB white-balancing research by targeting narrow-band spectral accuracy rather than wide-band perceptual color correction. The paper positions SIT as the first learning-based method for this task, with no prior deep-learning baseline available for direct comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing the first large-scale HSI auto-calibration dataset (765 real pairs, expanded to 7,650) and establishing a baseline method with detailed benchmarks, this work opens a previously unaddressed research direction that could eliminate the occlusion, manual operation, and camera-stability constraints of physical calibration in natural scenes. A notable open question is the persistent degradation in low-light and infrared bands, which the authors flag as future work. The finding that the simple Gray-World heuristic remains competitive with transformers on natural illuminations suggests that the learning-based advantage may be most pronounced under non-natural or synthetic illuminations, a nuance worth further investigation.
