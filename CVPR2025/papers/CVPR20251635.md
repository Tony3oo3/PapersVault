## Problem

Existing change detection methods for 3D scenes either require precisely aligned viewpoints, supervised training on labeled change pairs, or operate only on single-object scenes. This paper tackles the underexplored problem of label-free, pose-agnostic change localization in complex multi-object scenes, where pre- and post-change images are captured from random, independent trajectories with no pose correspondence and no annotated training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251635/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images of a pre-change (reference) scene and a post-change (inference) scene, with camera poses recovered via COLMAP SfM registration to a shared reference frame; as few as 5 inference images suffice. It produces binary change segmentation masks (thresholded at 0.5) for every inference view, a persistent change-aware 3DGS representation, and—uniquely—change masks for entirely unseen query poses by rendering from the learned 3D change channels. Unseen scene regions are excluded via alpha-channel binarization to prevent false positives from background pixels.

## Method

The approach first builds a standard 3DGS of the reference scene, then renders reference images at inference poses to compute per-view change masks by combining DINOv2 feature differences (feature-aware) with SSIM binarization (structure-aware) via element-wise multiplication. These masks supervise the learning of a new inference-scene 3DGS augmented with two per-Gaussian channels—a change magnitude and a change opacity—optimized with additional L1 and D-SSIM losses alongside the standard RGB reconstruction. Critically, the change magnitude is modeled with degree-0 spherical harmonics (view-independent), which the ablations confirm suppresses view-dependent false positives (reflections, shadows) by ~70% compared to degree-3. A data augmentation step reverses the comparison by rendering the inference 3DGS at reference poses to generate additional training masks, and the final 3DGS can be queried at any pose to produce multi-view-consistent change masks, including for viewpoints never observed in either scene.

## Results

On the novel PASLCD dataset (10 real-world multi-object scenes), the method achieves 0.461 mIoU and 0.612 F1, representing a 1.7× improvement in mIoU and 1.5× in F1 over the best baseline (SplatPose: 0.173 mIoU, 0.281 F1). On MAD-Real (single-object LEGO scenes), it reaches 0.132 mIoU and 0.210 F1, outperforming SplatPose (0.077 mIoU) by approximately 1.7×. On ChangeSim (simulated industrial scenes), it achieves 0.407 mIoU on the Change class, surpassing CSCDNet (0.229) by 1.8×. Notably, even with only 5 inference views, the method still outperforms the single-view Feature Difference baseline by ~1.8× in mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines across all three datasets. On PASLCD, it beats the closest competitor SplatPose [16] (0.173 mIoU) by 2.7× in mIoU and OmniPoseAD [49] (0.168 mIoU) by 2.7×, while supervised methods CYWS-2D [33] (0.273 mIoU) and CSCDNet [36] (0.125 mIoU) also fall well short. On MAD-Real, the method achieves 0.132 mIoU vs. SplatPose's 0.077 and OmniPoseAD's 0.064, with a 1.7× mIoU gain over the nearest competitor. On ChangeSim, it surpasses CSCDNet [36] (0.229) and ChangeNet [40] (0.176) by 1.8× and 2.3× respectively on the Change class. The supervised pairwise SCD methods (CYWS-3D [32] at 0.201 mIoU, CYWS-2D [33] at 0.273 mIoU) underperform the unsupervised Feature Difference baseline (0.264 mIoU) on PASLCD, highlighting distribution-shift sensitivity. The paper further demonstrates that Change-3DGS can boost any existing pairwise method (e.g., CYWS-2D gains 44% mIoU when combined with their multi-view framework).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of pose-agnostic 3D anomaly/change detection initiated by OmniPoseAD (NeRF-based) and SplatPose (3DGS-based), which both operate on a single per-view basis. The key architectural departure is embedding two additional per-Gaussian channels (change magnitude and change opacity) directly into a 3DGS representation, enabling multi-view consistency to be learned across the scene rather than computed independently per image. It also departs from supervised pairwise scene change detection (CYWS-2D/3D, CSCDNet, ChangeNet) by requiring no labeled training data, and from prior label-free approaches that rely solely on feature-level comparison by incorporating a complementary structure-aware signal (SSIM) and fusing both through the 3D representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to render change masks for entirely unseen viewpoints is a new capability not available in prior single-view methods, with direct implications for autonomous agents that need to verify changes from arbitrary inspection angles. The Change-3DGS framework is also positioned as a general multi-view extension applicable to any existing change mask generator (demonstrated with a 44% mIoU boost for CYWS-2D), suggesting broad utility. Key limitations remain: the method struggles with surface-level color changes (where DINOv2 features fail to register differences), very small changes in large-scale scenes, and requires COLMAP registration to succeed (failing under extreme appearance shifts). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large outdoor scenes, how it handles temporal sequences of changes, and whether the fixed 0.5 thresholding can be replaced by learned or adaptive binarization.
