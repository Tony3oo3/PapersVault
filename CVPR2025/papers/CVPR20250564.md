## Problem

Transferring semantic textures between 3D meshes while faithfully preserving the source appearance remains challenging. Existing diffusion-based texturing methods either require per-object optimization (slow), rely on large category-specific datasets, or fail to maintain the visual identity of the source texture during transfer. TriTex addresses this by learning a feed-forward texture transfer function from a single textured mesh that generalizes to novel shapes in the same category.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250564/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a target mesh whose vertices carry pre-extracted Diff3F semantic features (32×32 per view, aggregated from 6 orthographic projections) and outputs per-vertex RGB colors that texture the target surface. The source texture is encoded entirely during training from a single textured mesh and is not available at test time. The triplane operates at 32×32 spatial resolution with concatenated opposite-direction projections (2D channels per plane), and positional encoding is added to the input to compensate for the low resolution and enable fine detail generation.

## Method

TriTex projects Diff3F semantic features onto an axis-aligned triplane via orthographic projection, then processes the triplane with triplane-aware convolutional 2D blocks (from Sin3DM) that aggregate features across the three planes rather than processing them independently. A lightweight coloring MLP samples the processed triplane at query 3D points and maps the concatenated feature vector to RGB. Training uses a single textured mesh with a rendering-based reconstruction loss combining pixel-wise MSE and a perceptual loss (Tumanyan et al.), plus two levels of augmentation (preprocessing 3D transformations and training-time geometric perturbations) to improve generalization. The key insight is that the triplane representation preserves spatial proximity and connectivity of 3D semantic features, enabling efficient 2D convolutional processing that generalizes from one example to diverse shapes within a category.

## Results

On the TriTex texture transfer benchmark (52 meshes, 9 categories, 256 source-target pairs from Objaverse), TriTex achieves the best SIFID of 0.22 and CLIP similarity of 0.87, outperforming all three baselines on both metrics. Inference time is 1 minute per mesh, matching MVEdit and being 5× faster than TEXTure (5 min) and 15× faster than EASI-TEX (15 min). A user study with 15 raters over ~750 2AFC comparisons confirms that TriTex is strongly preferred over all competing methods for texture preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriTex outperforms all three baselines on both SIFID and CLIP similarity. Against TEXTure [40], TriTex reduces SIFID from 0.34 to 0.22 (a 35% relative improvement) and improves CLIP similarity from 0.84 to 0.87, while also being 5× faster (1 min vs. 5 min). Against MVEdit [9], TriTex achieves a substantially lower SIFID (0.22 vs. 0.38) and higher CLIP score (0.87 vs. 0.84) at the same 1-minute inference time; qualitatively, MVEdit tends to deviate significantly from the source texture, taking only vague inspiration from the reference. Against EASI-TEX [37], TriTex improves SIFID from 0.29 to 0.22 and CLIP from 0.85 to 0.87, while being 15× faster (1 min vs. 15 min); EASI-TEX produces visible artifacts from its iterative painting approach. The user study further confirms these quantitative advantages, with raters strongly favoring TriTex over each baseline in a 2AFC setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TriTex sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the triplane representation and triplane-aware convolution blocks introduced by EG3D and Sin3DM for efficient 3D feature processing, (2) pre-trained 3D semantic feature extraction via Diff3F (which itself builds on DINO and DIFT features), and (3) the long history of texture transfer methods ranging from classical sampling to recent diffusion-based approaches. The key departure is from optimization-based texturing (SDS loss, iterative multi-view inpainting) and from methods requiring large category-specific datasets (AUV-Net, Texturify): TriTex trains a feed-forward network on a single textured mesh, using semantic features as the bridge between source and target geometry, and processes them through 2D convolutions on a triplane rather than 3D volumetric operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 1-minute feed-forward texture transfer that outperforms iterative baselines (5–15 min) in both SIFID and CLIP similarity, making it viable for stylizing large scenes in game development or simulation. However, the method lacks generative capacity—it cannot synthesize texture details absent from the source (e.g., a tongue on a dog that had none)—and requires matching orientation between source and target. Open questions include whether cross-attention with reference images or integration of generative priors (as the authors propose) can close the detail gap, and whether the approach extends to cross-category transfer where semantic correspondence is weak.
