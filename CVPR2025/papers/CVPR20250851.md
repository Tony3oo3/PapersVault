## Problem

Existing driving simulation approaches either reduce the problem to agent behavior (bypassing perception), rely on reconstructive methods that cannot flexibly change weather or lighting, or generate entirely new scenes not grounded in real driving logs. Multi-view driving scene editing—simultaneously editing 8 camera views while preserving 3D geometric consistency—remains largely unexplored, with specific challenges in learning 'empty street' priors from occluded data and generating geometrically consistent paired training tuples for both local (object) and global (weather, time-of-day) edits.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 surrounding camera views at 512×512 resolution along with multi-modal control signals: weather (CLIP-encoded text), time of day (sun-angle positional encoding), up to 256 agent 3D bounding boxes (8-dim: center, dimensions, yaw, type), HD map (up to 4,096 lane segments via Perceiver IO), and camera poses (raymaps). Source images are concatenated channel-wise with noisy latents rather than fed through cross-attention. Output is 8 geometrically consistent edited views; agent insertion/removal is controlled by 3D box conditions (not 2D masks), enabling precise edits on small objects.

## Method

SceneCrafter uses a two-stage teacher-student pipeline built on a pre-trained multi-view latent diffusion model (CAT3D). For synthetic paired data generation, two teacher models are trained: a global-edit teacher extends Prompt-to-Prompt by replacing image-to-image self-attention weights (instead of cross-attention) to preserve pixel-level geometry while changing weather/time-of-day tokens, and conditions on additional scene signals (agent boxes, HD maps) for tighter geometric consistency; a local-edit teacher uses masked training (zero noise on foreground, standard noise on background, loss computed only on background pixels) to learn 'empty street' priors, followed by a multi-view RePaint algorithm for view-consistent inpainting and alpha blending to synthesize arbitrary agent configurations. The unified student editing model is initialized from the global teacher, trained on 1M synthetic pairs, and conditions on source images via channel concatenation with noisy latents (outperforming cross-attention by 13.1 FID), jointly handling local object edits and global condition changes in a single forward pass.

## Results

On global editing (time of day), SceneCrafter achieves FID 37.2 and 83.6% user preference, outperforming SDEdit (FID 60.4, 2.7%) and P2P* (FID 46.8, 13.6%). On weather editing, SceneCrafter reaches FID 38.9 with 85.5% user preference versus SDEdit (FID 78.3, 1.8%) and P2P* (FID 55.4, 12.7%). For local editing, SceneCrafter attains FID 23.5 (removal) and 21.7 (insertion), beating 2D-RePaint (30.6/31.9) and MV-RePaint (26.0/28.5). On the generation task, SceneCrafter(full) achieves 3D LPIPS 0.187, matching real log data (0.186) and far surpassing CAT3D (0.249).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneCrafter decisively outperforms SDEdit on all global editing metrics, reducing FID by 23.2 (time) and 39.4 (weather) points while achieving 83.6% and 85.5% user preference versus SDEdit's 2.7% and 1.8%. Against P2P*, SceneCrafter wins on FID (37.2 vs 46.8 for time; 38.9 vs 55.4 for weather) and user study by a wide margin (83.6% vs 13.6%; 85.5% vs 12.7%), though P2P* edges out slightly on CLIP Score for time-of-day editing (0.223 vs 0.220). For local editing, SceneCrafter's box-based conditioning yields FID 23.5/21.7 for removal/insertion, beating mask-based 2D-RePaint (30.6/31.9) and the multi-view MV-RePaint variant (26.0/28.5). On the generation task, SceneCrafter(full) achieves FID 36.2 and 3D LPIPS 0.187, substantially outperforming CAT3D (FID 121.3, 3D LPIPS 0.249) and nearly matching real driving log data (FID 11.5, 3D LPIPS 0.186).

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneCrafter builds on the CAT3D multi-view latent diffusion architecture as its base model, adapting Prompt-to-Prompt for synthetic paired-data generation, and extending RePaint into a multi-view repaint algorithm for local edits. The key conceptual departure from Prompt-to-Prompt is the shift from cross-attention to self-attention weight manipulation, motivated by the observation that pixel-level geometric consistency is better preserved when intervening in self-attention layers. The teacher-student data generation pipeline (masked training + alpha blending for local edits; modified P2P for global edits) is a novel synthesis of these components, but each individual technique has clear antecedents in the diffusion editing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in AV simulation: editing real driving footage to vary traffic agents and operating conditions while preserving 3D consistency across cameras, rather than generating entirely synthetic scenes that lack grounding in real-world plausibility. The 3D LPIPS metric and the demonstration that conditioned generation can match real-data 3D consistency (0.187 vs 0.186) are useful contributions to the evaluation methodology for multi-view generation. Limitations include dependence on a single proprietary dataset (Waymo logs with 8 cameras), the need for pre-computed HD maps and 3D agent boxes, and the fact that the editing is limited to the specific modalities (weather, time of day, agent boxes) available in the driving log — more open-ended semantic edits remain unaddressed.
