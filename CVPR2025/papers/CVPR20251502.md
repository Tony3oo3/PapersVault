## Problem

Existing Visual In-Context Learning (VICL) methods treat prompt selection as a competitive process, assuming a single ideal prompt exists among candidates. In practice, multiple suitable prompts often exist but individually fall short, forcing selection methods to discard useful context and limiting generalizability. The paper asks whether multiple prompts can instead collaborate to form a better composite prompt without sacrificing input resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251502/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image (112×112×3) and K retrieved candidate prompt image-label pairs (each 112×112×3) from a task-specific database. These are arranged on a 224×224×3 inpainting canvas (prompt in top half, query in bottom-left), and the CONDENSER operates on 14×14 grids of D-dimensional patch tokens extracted via the MAE-VQGAN patch embedding. The output is a single condensed prompt (image + label features) that, combined with the query, feeds the frozen backbone to predict VQGAN tokens for the masked query label region, which are decoded into pixel-level outputs (segmentation masks, bounding boxes, or colored images).

## Method

CONDENSER is a lightweight external plugin to the frozen MAE-VQGAN backbone that adaptively fuses K candidate prompts into one. It first applies shared self-attention within each prompt image and its label to preserve spatial correspondence, then performs Patch-wise Cross-Attention (PCA) where each query patch attends only to patches at the identical spatial position across all K prompts, producing a single condensed prompt feature. This spatial restriction (vs. full cross-attention) preserves local consistency and is validated as critical in ablations. Training is end-to-end with two objectives: a token prediction loss that supervises the backbone's VQGAN token predictions for the query label using ground-truth tokens from each candidate arrangement, and a pre-alignment loss that maximizes cosine similarity between condensed prompt features and query features as a regularizer. Only CONDENSER parameters are optimized while the backbone remains frozen, making it a plug-and-play module compatible with any VICL task.

## Results

On Pascal-5^i foreground segmentation, CONDENSER with a single prompt (K=1) achieves 44.14 mean mIoU, surpassing the strongest baseline InMeMo (43.14) by 8.09% relatively, and with 16 prompts (K=16) reaches 46.63 mean mIoU. On Pascal VOC 2012 single-object detection, CONDENSER K=16 achieves 44.64 mIoU versus InMeMo's 43.21. On ImageNet colorization, CONDENSER K=1 attains 0.56 MSE, outperforming Partial2Global (0.58) by 6.9% relatively. Inference time remains modest at 59.17 ms/query (K=1) and 66.61 ms/query (K=16), far below Prompt-SelF's 989.62 ms at K=16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONDENSER outperforms all seven baselines across every task. Against the strongest single-prompt selector InMeMo [36], CONDENSER K=1 gains +1.0 mIoU on segmentation (44.14 vs 43.14) and matches detection (43.22 vs 43.21), while K=16 extends the lead to +3.49 mIoU on segmentation and +1.43 on detection. Compared to Partial2Global [34], the best single-prompt ranker, CONDENSER K=1 improves segmentation by +5.74 mIoU (44.14 vs 38.40) and colorization MSE from 0.58 to 0.56. Against Prompt-SelF [24], CONDENSER K=1 surpasses its single-prompt result by +8.35 mIoU on segmentation, and even at K=16 (46.63) it exceeds Prompt-SelF's voting variant (41.02) by +5.61 mIoU while running ~15× faster (66.61 ms vs 989.62 ms per query). The PEFT ablations (LoRA, P-Tuning V2) confirm that parameter-matched adapter methods (39.49 and 42.24 mIoU) trail CONDENSER K=1 (44.14) and cannot benefit from additional prompts, whereas CONDENSER scales monotonically with K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MAE-VQGAN inpainting framework [2] and the broader VICL line of work (SegGPT, PIT, VPR, Prompt-SelF, Partial2Global, InMeMo), which has predominantly treated prompt selection as a competitive, single-prompt retrieval problem. It departs from this paradigm by proposing 'collaborative condensation'—fusing multiple candidate prompts into one via a lightweight patch-wise cross-attention plugin (CONDENSER) trained end-to-end with a frozen backbone. The design draws on standard attention mechanisms but constrains cross-attention to spatially aligned patches, a deliberate departure from full cross-attention that the ablations validate as critical for preserving local structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in VICL: the single-prompt assumption discards useful context and limits compositional generalization, while existing multi-prompt alternatives (downsampling, output ensemble) sacrifice resolution or scale poorly. CONDENSER's ~15× inference-time advantage over Prompt-SelF at K=16 makes multi-prompt VICL practically deployable, and the cross-dataset transfer results suggest the collaboration mechanism improves robustness to domain shift. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the MAE-VQGAN inpainting formulation to other backbone architectures (e.g., diffusion-based or LLM-based VICL), whether the patch-wise spatial constraint is optimal for tasks without well-defined spatial correspondence, and whether the method extends to open-vocabulary or multi-category settings.
