## Problem

Existing Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) methods rely on intuitive motivations—data generation, self-training, or self-supervised learning—without a unified theoretical framework explaining what controls target domain risk. This paper derives a general upper bound on the target empirical risk and identifies three previously unstructured factors (representativeness, generalization, and variety) that jointly govern adaptation performance, then translates these insights into a practical algorithm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the weights of a source-domain pre-trained model (ResNet-50 for Office-Home/DomainNet, ResNet-101 for VisDA-C, each with a replaced bottleneck FC layer followed by BatchNorm) and a set of unlabeled target RGB images; no source data is accessed at any point. During adaptation, the model is updated over E epochs with sample re-selection triggered every Q epochs, and the final output is the adapted classifier producing per-class softmax predictions. The progressive schedule means the labeled subset D_{t,l} grows and shifts across rounds, so the effective training set changes over time.

## Method

The core algorithm is guided by a derived risk bound (Theorem 1) decomposing target empirical risk into representativeness, HΔH intra-domain divergence, and a VC-dimension variety term. For representativeness, an intersection-based sampling (I-sampling) strategy selects target samples that are simultaneously top-k by classifier confidence (C-sampling) and top-k by cosine similarity to estimated target class centers (T-sampling), yielding higher pseudo-label purity than either alone. For generalization, a certainty-weighted semantic alignment loss pushes data-augmented unlabeled samples toward learned class centers, where pseudo-labels are refined via a memory-bank nearest-neighbor logit aggregation and gated by a harmonic certainty metric (combining entropy and max-probability) to filter noisy labels. For variety, a progressive optimization framework re-runs I-sampling every Q epochs, iteratively expanding the labeled subset and balancing label quality against coverage. The total training objective is L_CE (on selected data) + L_SA (on remaining data) + ρ·L_IM (information maximization), with ρ annealed over training.

## Results

On Office-Home (ResNet-50), the proposed method achieves 74.1% average top-1 accuracy across 12 domain tasks, surpassing the best SFDA baseline C-SFDA (73.5%) and the representative SHOT (71.8%) by 0.6% and 2.3% respectively. On DomainNet-126 (ResNet-50), it reaches 73.2% average accuracy, outperforming the second-best GPUE (69.6%) by 3.6% and SHOT (67.1%) by 6.1%. On VisDA-C (ResNet-101), the method attains 89.1% average accuracy, exceeding I-SFDA (88.4%) and AaD (88.0%). Overall, the paper reports relative improvements of 3.2%, 9.1%, and 7.5% over SHOT on the three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 17 baselines on the average metric across all three benchmarks. On Office-Home, it beats the strongest SFDA baselines C-SFDA (73.5%) and I-SFDA (73.4%) by 0.6% and 0.7% respectively, and surpasses the UDA method CST (73.0%) by 1.1% despite not using any source data. On DomainNet-126, the margin is substantially larger: 73.2% vs. GPUE (69.6%), AdaCon (67.8%), and SHOT (67.1%), with the method achieving the highest accuracy on all 7 individual domain tasks. On VisDA-C, it leads I-SFDA (88.4%) by 0.7% and AaD (88.0%) by 1.1%. The method wins on 8 out of 12 Office-Home domain tasks and all 7 DomainNet tasks, while the UDA baselines (CDAN, CST) are only competitive on Office-Home but are not applicable to DomainNet-126 in the SFDA setting. The ERM baseline (no adaptation) serves as a clear lower bound, confirming that the adaptation gains are substantial (e.g., 46.1% → 74.1% on Office-Home).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical domain adaptation risk bound framework of Ben-David et al. [1] to the source-free setting, but with a key conceptual departure: rather than bounding source-target divergence (which is intractable without source data), it decomposes the target empirical risk into representativeness, generalization (target intra-domain divergence), and variety terms. It builds directly on SHOT [15] for the pretraining protocol and information maximization regularization, while departing from the prior SFDA theoretical analysis of Mitsuzumi et al. [21], which focused on training-objective decomposition rather than the general empirical risk. The practical algorithm synthesizes ideas from pseudo-label self-training, nearest-neighbor label propagation, and progressive curriculum learning into a unified framework guided by the derived bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical grounding for SFDA from the perspective of general target-domain empirical risk, offering a structured three-factor framework (representativeness, generalization, variety) that can guide future method design beyond ad-hoc heuristics. The shift from source-target to target intra-domain divergence as the alignment objective is a useful reframing that sidesteps the fundamental inaccessibility of source data. However, the analysis is limited to image classification with ResNet backbones, the VC-dimension-based bound is likely loose in practice, and it remains open whether the progressive intersection-sampling strategy and certainty-weighted alignment generalize to denser prediction tasks (segmentation, detection) or to settings with open-set target domains.
