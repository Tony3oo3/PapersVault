## Problem

Existing T2I diffusion model control methods offer either fine-grained continuous attribute modulation (e.g., Concept Sliders) or subject-specific localization (e.g., Prompt-to-Prompt), but no approach provides both simultaneously. This gap prevents users from precisely tuning how strongly a specific attribute is expressed on a particular subject in a multi-subject scene.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and per-attribute scalar modulation parameters (λ_i) targeting specific subject tokens, then produces a generated image with controlled attribute expression at no additional inference cost. For real image editing, it additionally accepts a real image with a matching caption, inverts it via ReNoise into the noise latent, and regenerates with modulations applied. The modulation is applied only to the single subject-noun token embedding and is typically delayed until after the first 20% of denoising steps to improve disentanglement.

## Method

The key insight is that diffusion models interpret local deviations in the tokenwise CLIP embedding space in semantically meaningful, subject-specific ways: perturbing one subject's token embedding primarily affects only that subject in the output. Two methods identify attribute-specific direction vectors Δe_Ai in this space: (1) an optimization-free approach that computes the difference between subject token embeddings of a neutral prompt and an adjective-prefixed prompt, averaged over many prompt pairs; and (2) a learning-based approach that backpropagates through the diffusion model's noise predictions, optimizing Δe_Ai via a reconstruction loss that matches the noise-space difference between neutral and adjective-modified prompts across diverse images, timesteps, and modulation magnitudes. At inference, the direction is simply added to the target subject's token embedding scaled by λ_i, enabling continuous, subject-specific, and composable attribute control with zero additional sampling cost.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion XL, the proposed method achieves a subject-specificity ratio of 3.35 (comparable to adjectives at 4.14 and P2P at 3.93) while being the only method alongside Concept Sliders to offer fully continuous control. It attains the best LPIPS (0.10, tied with AdapEdit) and second-best identity preservation (ΔId = 0.40) among all standalone methods, indicating highly disentangled attribute modulation. Inference time is 12.0s (4.17 it/s), matching the cost of standard adjective-based prompting and far faster than Concept Sliders (33.8s) or MasaCtrl generation (153.0s). When combined with AdapEdit, the method achieves the best overall scores: subject-specificity 6.46, ΔId 0.19, and LPIPS 0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Concept Sliders [14], the proposed method wins decisively on subject-specificity (3.35 vs. not applicable, as Concept Sliders performs only global modulation) and inference speed (12.0s vs. 33.8s), while Concept Sliders has a slight edge on LPIPS (0.20 vs. 0.10) — though both are competitive. Against Prompt-to-Prompt [17], the method wins on continuous control (full ✓ vs. partial ~✗), LPIPS (0.10 vs. 0.29), and ΔId (0.40 vs. 0.60), while P2P has a marginal subject-specificity advantage (3.93 vs. 3.35). Against AdapEdit [32], the method wins on continuous control (✓ vs. ✗) and matches on LPIPS (0.10), but AdapEdit has higher subject-specificity (6.92 vs. 3.35) and lower ΔId (0.24 vs. 0.40) in standalone use; however, combining the two yields the best overall results (6.46 subject-specificity, 0.19 ΔId, 0.05 LPIPS). Against MasaCtrl [6], the method wins on all metrics: subject-specificity (3.35 vs. 2.48/1.93), ΔId (0.40 vs. 0.66/0.61), LPIPS (0.10 vs. 0.28/0.43), and generation speed (12.0s vs. 153.0s for generation mode). The key differentiator is that the proposed method is the only approach that simultaneously provides both continuous fine-grained control and subject-specific localization without requiring manual masks or reference images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new middle ground between two existing lines of work on T2I control: global fine-grained attribute modulation (Concept Sliders, which trains LoRA adaptors per attribute) and subject-specific but discrete text-based editing (Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl, AdapEdit, which modify prompts or attention maps to target individual subjects). The key conceptual departure is the observation that tokenwise CLIP embeddings—as opposed to the widely-studied pooled embedding space—exhibit local smoothness that diffusion models interpret in semantically meaningful, subject-specific ways, enabling continuous modulation via a single vector addition to one token. This repurposes the direction-based control paradigm well-established in GAN editing (InterFaceGAN, StyleCLIP) but applies it to the text-embedding input of diffusion models rather than the latent image space, and exploits the causal structure of the CLIP tokenizer to achieve subject-specificity without masks or attention manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete and previously unaddressed gap: no prior method simultaneously offers continuous (fine-grained) and subject-specific attribute control in T2I generation, and this method does so at zero additional inference cost (a single vector addition) while remaining composable with existing editing pipelines (P2P, AdapEdit, ReNoise). The zero-shot generalization across architectures—including non-diffusion models like aMUSEd and T5-based PixArt-α—suggests the identified semantic directions are a property of the text encoder rather than the specific diffusion backbone, which broadens practical applicability. Open questions include whether nonlinear modulations (analogous to REDS in GANs) could further improve disentanglement, and how to address the inherited limitation of diffusion models mixing attributes across subjects in multi-subject scenes.
