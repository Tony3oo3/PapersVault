## Problem

Large-scale 3D mapping from point clouds requires a representation that is both highly expressive for complex geometry and extremely memory-efficient, yet prior methods consistently trade one for the other: traditional grid-based approaches demand large node allocations, while neural implicit methods rely on numerous high-dimensional latent features that inflate memory. This paper tackles the gap by asking whether a feature-free, point-anchored neural representation can match or exceed state-of-the-art reconstruction quality at a fraction of the memory cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a scene point cloud accumulated from posed point cloud frames (LiDAR or RGB-D), which is voxel-downsampled to yield support points and constrained to a near-surface spatial hash grid. The output is a continuous, differentiable global truncated SDF parameterized entirely by per-point geometric parameters (position, rotation, scaling) and a single tiny shared MLP, from which meshes are extracted via marching cubes. Notably, the representation is unordered and contains no per-point latent features, making its memory footprint scale only linearly with the number of support points.

## Method

3D-SLNR defines a global SDF as a weighted average of band-limited local SDFs, each anchored at a support point and shaped by three learnable geometric parameters (position, rotation, scaling) applied to a single tiny MLP that serves as a data-driven basis SDF. A novel CUDA-parallel algorithm, inspired by 3DGS rasterization but adapted to unordered 3D points, efficiently detects which local SDFs influence each sampled point by building a grid index over oriented bounding boxes of candidates, enabling real-time geometric parameter updates during training. A prune-and-expand strategy periodically removes outlier support points (whose SDF values exceed a threshold) and clones or splits local SDFs in under-reconstructed regions identified via gradient analysis on planes orthogonal to surface normals, functioning as parameter regularization. Training uses a standard TSDF loss with Eikonal regularization, sampling points along rays within the hash grid, and the entire pipeline achieves state-of-the-art F-scores with less than 1/5 the memory of prior advanced methods.

## Results

On the Matterport3D indoor dataset, 3D-SLNR achieves the best F-score of 94.4%, best accuracy of 4.3 cm, and best precision of 92.3%, while using only 13.7 MB of map representation memory—less than 1/6 of the next best method (SPSR at 80.5 MB). On the Newer College outdoor dataset, the method achieves the highest F-scores of 92.7% (Quad) and 85.3% (Math Institute) with memory footprints of just 3.3 MB and 8.0 MB respectively, compared to 15.8–408.7 MB for competing methods. The parallel local SDF detection algorithm runs in 8 ms for 100k sampled points, and the CUDA-implemented backward pass is 2× faster than native PyTorch, enabling real-time updates during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-SLNR outperforms all five baselines on F-score, accuracy, and precision across both indoor (Matterport3D) and outdoor (Newer College) scenes. Against NKSR (the strongest feature-based neural kernel method), 3D-SLNR improves F-score from 94.2% to 94.4% and accuracy from 4.7 cm to 4.3 cm on Matterport3D, while reducing memory from 128.9 MB to 13.7 MB (≈93% reduction); on Newer College the memory gap is even more dramatic (3.3/8.0 MB vs. 368.5/408.7 MB). Compared to SHINE-Mapping, 3D-SLNR gains 1.6 F-score points on Matterport3D (94.4 vs. 92.8) and 1.5 points on Newer College Quad (92.7 vs. 91.2), with memory reduced from 167.4 MB to 13.7 MB. Against SPSR, 3D-SLNR achieves 0.6 F-score improvement on Matterport3D (94.4 vs. 93.8) and 0.9 on Newer College Quad (92.7 vs. 91.8), with memory cut from 80.5 MB to 13.7 MB. PIN-SLAM, the only other point-based method tested, is clearly outperformed on all metrics in outdoor scenes (e.g., F-score 92.7 vs. 88.3 on Quad; 85.3 vs. 75.4 on Math Institute), with 3D-SLNR also producing smoother, less noisy meshes.

## Relation to Prior Work

3D-SLNR sits at the intersection of three research threads: traditional basis-function reconstruction (Poisson, TSDF fusion), neural implicit representations with sparse feature grids (SHINE-Mapping, NKSR), and point-based neural methods (PIN-SLAM, Point-SLAM). It departs from the dominant paradigm of encoding per-point high-dimensional latent features by instead parameterizing each local SDF with only a tiny shared MLP (the learnable basis SDF) plus three geometric properties (position, rotation, scaling), drawing a conceptual parallel to how 3D Gaussian Splatting uses geometric parameters rather than learned features. The spatial hash grid construction and ray-based sampling are inherited from the authors' prior work [37], while the parallel local SDF detection algorithm is inspired by the rasterization mechanism in 3DGS but adapted to unordered 3D point queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–6× memory reduction while matching or exceeding SOTA F-scores across indoor, outdoor, and kilometer-scale scenes makes large-scale 3D mapping substantially more deployable on resource-constrained platforms (robotics, autonomous driving). The parallel detection algorithm and prune-and-expand strategy together address the key practical bottleneck of unordered point-based representations—efficient real-time updates—opening the door to online SLAM integration, which the authors identify as the primary future direction. An open question is whether the geometric-parameter-only formulation generalizes to tasks requiring richer per-point semantics (e.g., appearance, material), and whether the method's reliance on posed point cloud frames limits its applicability compared to purely image-based pipelines.
