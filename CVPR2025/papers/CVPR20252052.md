## Problem

Existing 360-degree head synthesis methods are either restricted to photorealistic human heads (GAN-based approaches like PanoHead and SphereHead) or limited to frontal views with noticeable inter-angle inconsistencies (diffusion-based methods like DiffPortrait3D). No prior method can generate globally consistent, full 360-degree views from a single portrait that generalizes across diverse styles including stylized, anthropomorphic, and accessory-laden subjects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252052/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB portrait image of arbitrary style (human, cartoon, anthropomorphic, with accessories like glasses or hats) and produces a full 360-degree sequence of head views, each computed offline in approximately 5.6 seconds. Internally, a ControlNet generates a back-view reference image from the input, which is then used alongside the original front image as dual appearance conditions. The generated views can be fitted into a NeRF for real-time free-viewpoint rendering.

## Method

DiffPortrait360 builds on the DiffPortrait3D latent diffusion framework with three key innovations. First, a custom ControlNet is trained on curated front-back pairs (including stylized data from Flux AI and Unique3D) to synthesize a plausible back-of-head image from the front-facing input, providing appearance information for previously unobserved regions. Second, a dual appearance module (ReferenceNet) extracts features from both the front reference and the generated back reference, injecting them into the Stable Diffusion UNet via attention layers so the network can autonomously select relevant appearance cues per viewpoint, preventing facial information leakage onto the back of the head. Third, the view-consistency temporal transformer is trained on continuous 8-view sequences (rather than randomly shuffled views) from PanoHead and SphereHead, leveraging the motion prior to enforce locally smooth transitions. The denoising process uses 3D-aware noise initialized through a fine-tuned PanoHead inversion, and the full system is trained on a hybrid dataset combining RenderMe360 captures with synthetic 360-degree head data.

## Results

On RenderMe360, DiffPortrait360 achieves the best scores across all metrics for both frontal and back views. For frontal views, it reaches PSNR 29.44, SSIM 0.578, LPIPS 0.384, CSIM 0.746, and FID 35.34. For back views, the advantage is even more pronounced: PSNR 30.92, SSIM 0.648, LPIPS 0.313, and FID 39.40, substantially outperforming the next-best method (DiffPortrait3D* at FID 91.37 for back views). The method also demonstrates superior qualitative generalization on stylized portraits where all GAN-based and general-object diffusion baselines fail to produce coherent back-of-head content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffPortrait360 outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric for both frontal and back views. Against the closest competitor, DiffPortrait3D* (fine-tuned on full-head data), the gap is most dramatic in back-view FID (39.40 vs. 91.37) and back-view SSIM (0.648 vs. 0.373), reflecting the paper's core contribution of consistent back-of-head synthesis. The GAN-based methods PanoHead+PTI and SphereHead+PTI trail by 1–2.5 dB in PSNR and produce noticeably lower CSIM scores (0.471 and 0.556 vs. 0.746), indicating poor identity preservation, especially at the back. General-object diffusion models Zero123 and Unique3D report only CSIM and FID (no per-pixel metrics), and their FID scores (152–286) are 4–7× worse than the proposed method, confirming they cannot handle head-specific appearance. On stylized portraits (Fig. 3), all baselines—including Rome and Itseez3D—fail to generate plausible back-view textures or maintain style consistency, whereas DiffPortrait360 produces coherent full-head views across diverse artistic styles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DiffPortrait3D (CVPR 2024), which achieved state-of-the-art frontal portrait view synthesis via a latent diffusion model with 3D-aware noise, to the full 360-degree range by introducing a dual appearance module (front + back reference), a custom ControlNet for back-view generation, and a sequential view-consistency training strategy. It also repurposes PanoHead's 3D-aware GAN as the camera-control backbone and AnimateDiff's temporal transformer for inter-view consistency, but departs from both by training on continuous view sequences rather than randomly shuffled views. The work positions itself against GAN-based 3D head synthesis (PanoHead, SphereHead), which achieves 360-degree coverage but is restricted to realistic human heads, and general diffusion-based 3D generation (Zero-1-to-3, Unique3D), which handles diverse objects but produces severe artifacts on domain-specific head appearances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce globally consistent 360-degree head views from a single image across photorealistic, stylized, and anthropomorphic subjects, enabling downstream NeRF reconstruction for real-time free-viewpoint rendering—a capability previously unavailable for non-photorealistic avatars. The practical impact is significant for immersive telepresence, game/animation pipelines, and personalized avatar creation where users provide a single photo or drawing. Limitations include the 5.6-second-per-view inference cost (offline only), dependence on a 3D-aware GAN for camera conditioning, and evaluation on a single dataset (RenderMe360). Open questions include whether the dual-appearance and sequential-training strategies generalize to full-body or multi-subject synthesis, and whether the back-view ControlNet can be replaced by a more principled 3D prior.
