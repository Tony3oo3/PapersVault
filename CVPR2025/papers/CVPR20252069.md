## Problem

Prior gaze target estimation methods rely on complex multi-branch architectures that fuse features from separate head encoders, scene encoders, and auxiliary models for depth and pose, all trained end-to-end on small-scale datasets. A natural question is whether frozen foundation model features (e.g., DINOv2) can replace this pipeline, but the paper shows that naively substituting DINOv2 into existing gaze architectures actually degrades performance, revealing a need for a purpose-built decoder that adapts general-purpose features to the gaze task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252069/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image at 448×448 resolution and a 4-value head bounding box as input, with no auxiliary modalities (depth, pose, object detections). The bounding box is downsampled to a binarized mask at the feature map resolution (32×32 for ViT-B) rather than being used to crop a high-resolution head region. Output is a 64×64 gaze target heatmap where each pixel encodes the probability of being a gaze target, plus an optional scalar in/out-of-frame probability for benchmarks that require it.

## Method

Gaze-LLE uses a frozen DINOv2 encoder to extract scene features, projects them to a 256-dim space, and then performs 'head prompting' by adding a learned position embedding to the scene tokens at the head's location—integrating head information after the encoder rather than as an extra input channel. A compact 3-layer transformer decoder (8 attention heads, MLP dim 1024) with absolute 2D sinusoidal position embeddings updates the head-conditioned scene tokens via self-attention, and a 2-layer convolutional head upsamples the result to the 64×64 heatmap. An optional learnable task token prepended to the token list enables joint in/out-of-frame classification. The entire trainable module contains only ~2.8M parameters (vs. 50–135M in prior multi-branch methods), is trained with pixel-wise binary cross-entropy on Gaussian-smoothed ground truth (σ=3), and converges to state-of-the-art in under 1.5 GPU-hours on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

Gaze-LLE (ViT-B, 2.8M learnable params) achieves state-of-the-art on GazeFollow with AUC 0.956, Avg L2 0.104, and Min L2 0.045, and on VideoAttentionTarget with AUC 0.933, L2 0.107, and AP 0.897. The ViT-L variant (2.9M params) further improves to AUC 0.958 / 0.937 on the two benchmarks. On ChildPlay, Gaze-LLE (ViT-B) reaches AUC 0.949, L2 0.106, and AP 0.994, outperforming all prior methods. Notably, Gaze-LLE uses only an RGB image input and a frozen DINOv2 backbone, requiring ~5% of the trainable parameters of prior multi-branch methods (29M–135M+) and converging in under 1.5 GPU-hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gaze-LLE (ViT-B) outperforms the strongest prior image-only method, Tafasca et al. [61] (Sharingan, >135M params), by +1.2 AUC (0.956 vs 0.944) and −0.009 Avg L2 on GazeFollow, while using only ~2% of the learnable parameters. It also surpasses depth+pose methods like Gupta et al. [23] (AUC 0.943, 35M params) and Miao et al. [43] (AUC 0.934, 61M params) on GazeFollow, and beats Miao et al. [43] on VideoAttentionTarget (AUC 0.933 vs 0.917). On ChildPlay, Gaze-LLE (ViT-B) achieves the best AUC (0.949 vs 0.935 for Tafasca et al. [60]) and best AP (0.994 vs 0.990). In cross-dataset transfer without fine-tuning, Gaze-LLE (ViT-B) attains the best GOO-Real AUC (0.901) versus Tonini et al. [64] with UDA (0.840) and Miao et al. [43] (0.869), and the best ChildPlay AUC (0.946 vs 0.933 for Miao et al.). The only metric where Gaze-LLE does not lead is VideoAttentionTarget in/out AP, where Miao et al. [43] scores 0.908 vs Gaze-LLE's 0.897 (ViT-B) / 0.903 (ViT-L), though Gaze-LLE still leads on all other VAT metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-branch fusion paradigm for gaze target estimation (separate head encoder, scene encoder, and auxiliary depth/pose models) by demonstrating that a single frozen DINOv2 encoder with a lightweight learned decoder suffices. It builds on the broader trend of leveraging self-supervised foundation models for dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth estimation via linear probes), but is the first to show this works for gaze estimation. A key conceptual departure is the 'head prompting' mechanism—injecting a learned positional embedding at the head location into scene tokens—replacing the need for a dedicated head branch entirely. The paper also establishes a negative result: naively swapping DINOv2 into existing multi-branch architectures degrades performance, motivating the novel decoder design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing learnable parameters from 50–135M to ~2.8M and training time to under 1.5 GPU-hours, Gaze-LLE makes gaze estimation dramatically more accessible and positions it to automatically benefit from future foundation model improvements. The strong cross-dataset generalization (without fine-tuning) suggests the frozen encoder captures genuinely transferable gaze-relevant features rather than dataset-specific patterns. Limitations include dependence on encoder quality for dense tasks and the computational cost of the large backbone for embedded deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based gaze tracking, 3D gaze estimation, or multi-person social gaze analysis at scale.
