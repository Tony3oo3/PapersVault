## Problem

Deep neural networks produce overly confident predictions on out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, a reliability issue that existing approaches address only superficially through output calibration or training on limited OOD subsets. This paper attributes the root cause to inherent model over-complexity—specifically excessive capacity and nonlinearity—and investigates whether simplifying the model itself can fundamentally mitigate OOD overconfidence without requiring auxiliary OOD data.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) and produces the same class label and confidence score as the original classifier, but with systematically lower maximum confidence on OOD inputs. The intervention is applied at training time to the model architecture rather than at inference time to the scoring function, so the deployed model is a smaller, less nonlinear network that inherently assigns lower confidence to OOD samples.

## Method

The approach combines two simplification strategies applied during a single knowledge-distillation training run. First, a large teacher (e.g., ResNet-50 or ResNet-101) is distilled into a moderately-sized student (e.g., ResNet-18 or WRN-16) using standard KD loss, reducing model capacity. Second, during distillation, a fraction of ReLU activations (2% for CIFAR-10, 5% for CIFAR-100/ImageNet) are selectively identified via the Negative Response Ratio (NRO)—which measures how often each ReLU truncates negative responses—and replaced with Leaky ReLU whose negative slope is linearly ramped from 0 to 1 starting at epoch 40 of 100, effectively converting those ReLUs into identity functions. This dual reduction in capacity and nonlinearity yields lower mean maximum confidence on OOD sets while preserving or improving ID/OOD discrimination metrics (AUROC, FPR95), and the simplified model can be paired with any post-hoc OOD detector (MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, ReAct, FeatureNorm) for further gains.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-50, the proposed method (Ours, WRN-16 student) reduces mean maximum confidence (MMC) from 72.12 (baseline) to 51.43, outperforming CEDA (56.99), ACET (62.79), and CCUs (56.09). On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-101, MMC drops from 73.84 to 54.50 (Ours, WRN-28), again beating all baselines. For OOD detection, combining the simplified model with MSP on CIFAR-100/ResNet-101 improves FPR95 from 84.41 to 71.42 and AUROC from 73.03 to 81.81. On ImageNet-1k, ReAct+Ours(WRN-50) achieves FPR95 of 47.74 and AUROC of 88.56, surpassing the ReAct baseline (54.43 / 86.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For OOD overconfidence mitigation (Table 3), the proposed simplification approach outperforms all three confidence-suppression baselines—CEDA, ACET, and CCUs (all from Hein et al. / Meinke & Hein)—on CIFAR-100, where the gap is largest (e.g., 51.43 vs. 56.99 MMC for ResNet-50). On CIFAR-10, the standalone method is competitive but the combination with CCUs yields the best result (63.38 MMC), confirming complementarity. For OOD detection (Tables 4–5), the simplified model consistently improves every post-hoc scorer: MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, ReAct, and FeatureNorm all show FPR95 reductions of 5–15 points when paired with the distilled student (e.g., FeatureNorm on CIFAR-10 drops from 54.71 to 20.09 FPR95 with Ours(ResNet-18)). On ImageNet-1k, gains are more modest but consistent (e.g., ReAct FPR95 improves from 54.43 to 47.74 with WRN-50 student). In the ablation comparing capacity-reduction strategies (Table 7), knowledge distillation outperforms network pruning (Han et al.) on both MMC and FPR95, e.g., KD(WRN-28) achieves 77.01 MMC vs. 78.20 for Prune on ResNet-101/CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of mitigating OOD overconfidence through confidence suppression losses (ACET, CEDA, CCUs) or auxiliary OOD data, instead attributing the problem to inherent model over-complexity. It repurposes knowledge distillation—not as an efficiency tool but as a capacity-reduction mechanism to curb overconfidence—and adapts the ReLU-to-Leaky-ReLU transition strategy from VanillaNet [4] to selectively reduce nonlinearity. The paper positions itself as a data-independent, training-integrated alternative that complements rather than replaces post-hoc OOD detection scores (MSP, Energy, ReAct, FeatureNorm), demonstrating that simplification and detection can be stacked for additive gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe (distill to a moderately-sized student + remove a small fraction of low-NRO ReLUs) is simple to implement and requires no auxiliary OOD data, making it attractive for safety-critical deployments where OOD diversity is unbounded. However, the paper remains purely empirical with no theoretical account of *why* reduced capacity or nonlinearity suppresses overconfidence, and the optimal removal ratio and student size appear dataset-dependent (2% for CIFAR-10, 5% for CIFAR-100), limiting generalizability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CNN architectures (transformers, diffusion models), whether the benefits persist under distribution shift at test time, and what the fundamental information-theoretic link is between model complexity and OOD confidence.
