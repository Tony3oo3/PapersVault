## Problem

Diffusion-based face restoration methods require 50–1000 iterative denoising steps, making them impractical for real-time applications, while existing one-step diffusion (OSD) models are designed for general image restoration and lack face-specific priors. This results in restored faces with unharmonious details, unrealistic hair/backgrounds, and inconsistent identity. No prior work has combined one-step diffusion with dedicated face prior extraction for high-fidelity face restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250286/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB low-quality face image as input and produces a 512×512 high-quality restored face in one forward pass (~0.1 s on an A6000 GPU). Unlike text-to-image diffusion models, the conditioning signal is a visual prompt derived directly from the LQ input via the VRE, eliminating the need for text encoders or external reference images. The model is evaluated on both synthetic degradations (CelebA-Test) and real-world datasets (Wider-Test, LFW-Test, WebPhoto-Test) with diverse degradation severities.

## Method

OSDFace operates in two stages. Stage 1 trains a Visual Representation Embedder (VRE): a VQ-VAE whose encoder tokenizes the LQ face into K discrete tokens, which are then looked up in a learnable LQ VQ dictionary (1024 codes of dimension 512) to produce a visual prompt embedding; an HQ–LQ feature association loss (cross-entropy on a similarity matrix, inspired by CLIP) aligns the LQ dictionary with an HQ dictionary so the encoder attends to meaningful facial categories rather than degradation artifacts. Stage 2 fine-tunes the UNet of Stable Diffusion 2.1-base via LoRA (rank 16) to perform a single denoising step at a predefined timestep T_L, conditioned on the VRE prompt; the generator is trained with a composite loss combining ArcFace-based facial identity loss (cosine similarity in embedding space), edge-aware DISTS perceptual loss (DISTS on both the image and its Sobel-filtered version), and a latent-space GAN adversarial loss that encourages distribution alignment with ground-truth faces.

## Results

On the synthetic CelebA-Test benchmark, OSDFace achieves the best DISTS (0.1773), NIQE (3.8840), Deg. (60.07), and FID(HQ) (17.06) among all compared methods, with FID(HQ) surpassing the next-best DifFace (22.24) by over 5 points. On real-world datasets (Wider-Test, LFW-Test, WebPhoto-Test), OSDFace attains the lowest FID scores (34.65, 44.63, 84.60) and top MUSIQ scores, while completing inference in just 0.10 s per 512×512 image with 2,132 G MACs—roughly 70× faster than PGDiff (85.81 s) and 23% faster than OSEDiff (0.13 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step diffusion competitor OSEDiff [59], OSDFace wins decisively on DISTS (0.177 vs 0.217), NIQE (3.88 vs 5.12), Deg. (60.07 vs 67.39), FID(FFHQ) (45.42 vs 73.48), and FID(HQ) (17.06 vs 37.21), while narrowly losing on LPIPS (0.337 vs 0.331). Versus multi-step diffusion methods, OSDFace outperforms PGDiff [64] on DISTS, NIQE, Deg., and FID(HQ) but trails slightly on FID(FFHQ) (45.42 vs 44.63); it beats DifFace [70] on DISTS, NIQE, Deg., and FID(HQ) by wide margins, and surpasses DiffBIR [32] on DISTS, NIQE, Deg., and FID(HQ) while DiffBIR edges ahead on LMD (5.10 vs 5.29). Among non-diffusion baselines, OSDFace dominates on DISTS, NIQE, Deg., and FID(HQ) over CodeFormer [74], DAEFR [51], VQFR [16], and RestoreFormer++ [58], though CodeFormer achieves a marginally higher MUSIQ (75.94 vs 75.64). On real-world datasets, OSDFace achieves the best FID on all three benchmarks (Wider-Test 34.65, LFW-Test 44.63, WebPhoto-Test 84.60) and the best MUSIQ on Wider-Test and WebPhoto-Test, confirming consistent gains over both diffusion and non-diffusion baselines in the wild.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSDFace sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) one-step diffusion for image restoration (D3SR, OSEDiff), (2) VQ-based face restoration (CodeFormer, DAEFR, VQFR), and (3) multi-step diffusion face restoration (PGDiff, DifFace, DiffBIR). It departs from OSEDiff by replacing text-based prompt extraction (DAPE) with a face-specific Visual Representation Embedder that uses VQ tokenization as visual prompts, and from VQ-based methods by using the codebook as a prompt generator rather than a direct image synthesizer. The one-step denoising framework follows D3SR's formulation, while the identity and adversarial losses draw on face recognition (ArcFace) and GAN literature respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving competitive face restoration in ~0.1s (one diffusion step) makes high-quality face restoration viable for real-time or edge deployment, a regime previously dominated by slower multi-step methods or lower-fidelity VQ approaches. The VRE paradigm—using VQ tokenization to produce visual prompts in O(1) time rather than relying on text encoders—offers a reusable pattern for incorporating domain-specific priors into one-step diffusion models beyond faces. Open questions include whether the two-stage training (VRE then diffusion) can be unified, whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or video, and whether the VRE's reliance on a fixed-size codebook (1024 items) limits expressiveness for highly diverse or out-of-distribution faces.
