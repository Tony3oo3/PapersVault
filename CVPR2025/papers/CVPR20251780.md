## Problem

Text-to-motion models struggle to faithfully generate motions matching complex, multi-event textual descriptions involving temporal ordering and specified frequency, yet existing alignment approaches rely on costly human annotation or coarse-grained AI feedback that treats text and motion as undifferentiated wholes. No prior work leverages Vision-Language Large Models to provide fine-grained, event-level alignment scoring across multiple dimensions simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251780/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes free-form text prompts describing one to five sequential motion events (with temporal conjunctions like 'then' or frequency qualifiers like 'two times') and produces a 3D human motion sequence via MotionGPT's VQ-VAE discrete token representation. For reward evaluation, generated motions are rendered into video and sampled at 8-frame intervals, producing a sequence of RGB frames that are injected frame-by-frame into GPT-4V alongside the prompt and task-specific scoring instructions.

## Method

AToM operates in three stages: (1) GPT-4 constructs 5.3K event-level prompts from meta-motion labels in HumanML3D, and MotionGPT generates 6–10 motions per prompt, yielding 47.1K motion samples; (2) rendered motion frames are scored by GPT-4V under task-specific rubrics for three alignment dimensions—integrity (all events present), temporal order (correct sequence), and frequency (correct repetition count)—producing the 80K-pair MotionPrefer dataset; (3) MotionGPT is fine-tuned with LoRA (R=8, α=16) using the IPO objective on preference pairs whose score gap exceeds a threshold δ, requiring only ~12 GB on a single RTX 4090. The key distinction from prior RLHF/RLAIF work is the use of a VLM as a structured, multi-dimensional reward model operating on rendered visual frames rather than learned motion encoders, enabling fine-grained event-level alignment without human annotation.

## Results

On the General task, AToM♠ achieves the lowest MM Dist (3.943), highest Top-1 retrieval (0.410), and best FID (0.177), outperforming both MotionGPT (FID 0.188) and InstructMotion (FID 0.260). On the Temporal sub-task, AToM reduces FID by 6.4% (0.613 vs. 0.655) and improves Top-1 from 0.193 to 0.199 over MotionGPT. On the Frequency sub-task, FID drops from 1.409 to 1.165 (17.3% improvement). In the user study, AToM achieves win rates of 74.4% (temporal), 70.0% (frequency), and 84.4% (integrity) against MotionGPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MotionGPT (the base model), AToM wins on all three sub-tasks: it reduces MM Dist and FID while improving Top-1 and Top-3 retrieval on Temporal, Frequency, and Integrity tasks, with the largest FID gain on Frequency (1.409→1.165, −17.3%). On the General task, AToM♠ (matched to InstructMotion's ~3.5K pair budget) beats both MotionGPT and InstructMotion on MM Dist (3.943 vs. 3.998 and 3.947), Top-1 (0.410 vs. 0.407 and 0.402), and FID (0.177 vs. 0.188 and 0.260), showing that GPT-4V-based AI feedback outperforms human-annotated RLHF at equal data scale. The only metric where AToM slightly trails MotionGPT is MModality on Temporal (3.495 vs. 3.846), attributed to fine-tuning concentrating on high-quality aligned samples. In the user study, AToM is preferred over MotionGPT in 74.4% (temporal), 70.0% (frequency), and 84.4% (integrity) of pairwise comparisons by 50 human evaluators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF/RLAIF paradigm for text-to-motion alignment (InstructMotion, MoDiPO) by replacing human annotators or contrastive text-motion encoders with GPT-4Vision as the reward model, and by shifting from holistic alignment to event-level scoring across integrity, temporal order, and frequency. It builds directly on MotionGPT as the base generator and adopts IPO as the preference optimization objective, but the core departure is the reward paradigm design—how to render motion into frame sequences, structure task-specific instructions, and define granular scoring rubrics for a VLM. Unlike prior AI-feedback approaches that rely on pretrained motion encoders trained on standard datasets, AToM leverages a general-purpose VLM's multimodal reasoning to evaluate out-of-distribution text-motion pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a general-purpose VLM can serve as a scalable, fine-grained reward signal for motion generation alignment, reducing dependence on costly human annotation and enabling event-level evaluation that prior holistic approaches overlooked. However, the quantitative improvements over MotionGPT are modest (e.g., Top-1 retrieval gains of ~0.006–0.01, FID reductions of 6–17%), and the MModality drops suggest a diversity-alignment trade-off that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond MotionGPT to diffusion-based generators, whether GPT-4V's scoring is sufficiently reliable for high-stakes applications, and whether the event-level decomposition can be extended to more complex multi-agent or long-horizon motion scenarios.
