## Problem

Temporal consistency in video prediction remains challenging: 3D convolutions assume local spatiotemporal correlations and space-time attention struggles with large object displacements, both leading to flickering and artifacts. Meanwhile, modern point trackers (e.g., CoTracker3) can reliably track quasi-dense point correspondences across long sequences, but this capability has not been exploited to improve general video analysis tasks. The paper asks whether the motion knowledge encoded in point tracks can be injected into arbitrary video transformers to achieve precise temporal alignment regardless of object motion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip (8–16 frames per training batch) along with 576 point tracks extracted by CoTracker3, where each track is a sequence of 2D locations across all frames (T×M×2 tensor). Tracks are initialized by uniformly sampling query points from the full spatio-temporal volume and tracking them both forward and backward, ensuring good coverage despite camera and object motion. For depth estimation, the output is a per-frame depth map up to a shared affine calibration across the video; for colorization, the output is a colorized frame sequence. The module adds only ~17M parameters to the base model and runs in ~0.9 s per sequence (including 0.4 s for tracking).

## Method

Tracktention is a plug-and-play layer with three stages: (1) Attentional Sampling uses cross-attention with a Gaussian spatial bias (σ=1/2) to pool video features into track tokens, augmented with RoPE for relative spatial encoding and QK-normalization for training stability; (2) a 2-layer Track Transformer processes each track's temporal sequence independently (treating M tracks as a batch dimension), propagating information along tracks without cross-track attention; (3) Attentional Splatting reverses the query/key roles to redistribute updated track information back onto the feature grid. The layer is inserted after selected transformer or convolutional blocks with a residual connection and a zero-initialized output projection, preserving the pretrained image model at training onset. For video depth, six Tracktention layers are added after the last six blocks of Depth Anything's DINO ViT backbone, fine-tuned with the backbone frozen using a scale-and-shift-invariant loss with shared per-video calibration. This explicit tracking-based correspondence mechanism outperforms implicit alternatives (3D convolutions, time attention) by directly aligning features at tracked locations, handling occlusions and large displacements limited only by the tracker's accuracy.

## Results

On the DepthCrafter video depth benchmark (Table 1), Tracktention achieves an average AbsRel of 0.138 and δ₁.₂₅ of 0.862, outperforming the strongest video baseline DepthCrafter (AbsRel 0.163, δ₁.₂₅ 0.843) by 15.3% and 2.3% respectively, while using only 140M parameters versus DepthCrafter's 1.5B. On the RobustMVD multi-view depth benchmark (Table 2), the method achieves the lowest average relative error (6.2) and highest inlier ratio (41.1%), with a runtime of 0.9 s per sequence. For video colorization (Table 3), augmenting DDColor with Tracktention reduces Color Distribution Consistency error by 46.5% on DAVIS and 45.7% on Videvo, and augmenting ColorFormer reduces it by 41.3% and 28.8% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tracktention decisively outperforms all video-native depth baselines: it beats DepthCrafter (1521M params) by 15.3% on average AbsRel and 2.3% on δ₁.₂₅, and surpasses DUSt3R (578M) by 59.5% on AbsRel (0.138 vs 0.341) and NVDS (430M) by 45.7%. It also outperforms the image-based baselines it augments—DepthAnything (AbsRel 0.169) and DepthAnything-V2 (0.187)—by 18.3% and 26.2% respectively, confirming that the Tracktention layer adds substantial temporal value. On RobustMVD, the method achieves the best average relative error (6.2) and inlier ratio (41.1%), beating DUSt3R (7.6 / 37.7%) and DepthCrafter (9.9 / 27.4%) while running 42× faster than DepthCrafter (0.9 s vs 38.1 s). For colorization, Tracktention consistently improves every image-based model it is plugged into (CIC, IDC, ColorFormer, DDColor) on the CDC metric, with the largest gains on DDColor (−46.5% DAVIS, −45.7% Videvo) and ColorFormer (−41.3%, −28.8%), and also outperforms the dedicated video colorization baselines TCVC and FAVC on colorfulness and consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tracktention carves new ground by repurposing modern point trackers (CoTracker3, TAPIR, PIPs) — previously confined to 3D reconstruction and object tracking — as a general motion prior for video analysis. It departs from implicit temporal modeling (3D convolutions, space-time attention) by making motion correspondence explicit at the resolution of tracked points, and from optical-flow-based consistency methods by leveraging the superior robustness of learned trackers to occlusions and large displacements. The architectural contribution is a lightweight cross-attention module (Attentional Sampling → Track Transformer → Attentional Splatting) that slots into any ViT or ConvNet backbone, requiring no changes to the base model's architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means any strong image model can be upgraded to a competitive video model with ~17M additional parameters and a frozen backbone, dramatically lowering the barrier to temporally consistent video prediction. The key limitation is dependence on tracker quality: if tracks fail (e.g., prolonged off-camera objects), the temporal alignment degrades, and the tracker adds ~0.4s overhead per sequence. Open questions include generalization to tasks where motion is less structured (e.g., video generation, action recognition), whether the approach benefits from future tracker improvements, and whether the explicit tracking prior can be replaced by a learned lightweight motion estimator to eliminate the external dependency.
