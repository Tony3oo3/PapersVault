## Problem

CLIP-based unsupervised multi-label classification (e.g., CDUL) suffers from two reliability issues that limit performance: CLIP predictions are highly view-dependent, so small changes in crop position or size cause large probability shifts, and CLIP exhibits inherent class-wise prediction bias due to polysemy in text embeddings, systematically under-predicting certain classes. These issues propagate into pseudo-labels and degrade the trained classifier, yet prior methods did not explicitly address them.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB images (global views at 640×640; local views are CAM-guided crops resized to 640px on the longer side) and produces per-class binary predictions for multi-label classification. No ground-truth annotations are used during training; the only external signal is CLIP (ResNet50×64) zero-shot inference with class-name prompts. The classifier backbone is an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet101, and evaluation uses mAP on held-out test sets.

## Method

CCD operates in two phases. First, a warm-up phase trains the classifier on debiased global CLIP pseudo-labels. Second, a classifier-guided label update uses Class Activation Mapping (CAM) from the trained classifier to locate object regions, generates bounding boxes with ±80px perturbation, and feeds these local views to CLIP for additional pseudo-labels; the number of local views is adaptively increased for complex images where multiple classes exceed a probability threshold. A key debiasing step computes the class-wise average top-1 CLIP probability (restricted to low-entropy samples) as a bias vector and multiplies all pseudo-labels by its inverse, correcting systematic under-prediction. The final pseudo-label is a weighted sum (α=0.4) of global and local labels, and training uses binary cross-entropy plus a consistency loss between weakly and strongly augmented views to suppress noise amplified by debiasing.

## Results

CCD achieves state-of-the-art unsupervised multi-label classification with mAP of 90.1% on PASCAL VOC 2012, 91.0% on PASCAL VOC 2007, 70.3% on MS COCO, and 44.5% on NUSWIDE. Notably, CCD matches the fully supervised BCE baseline (90.1%) on VOC 2012 and comes within 0.3%p on VOC 2007, all without any human annotations. Compared to the previous unsupervised SOTA (CDUL), CCD improves by 1.5%p on VOC 2012, 2.0%p on VOC 2007, and 1.1%p on COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest unsupervised competitor CDUL [2], CCD wins on all four datasets: +1.5%p on VOC 2012 (90.1 vs 88.6), +2.0%p on VOC 2007 (91.0 vs 89.0), +1.1%p on COCO (70.3 vs 69.2), and +0.5%p on NUSWIDE (44.5 vs 44.5). CCD also surpasses all other unsupervised baselines (Naive AN, Szegedy et al., Aodha et al., Durand et al., ROLE) by 2.5–9.8%p across datasets. Remarkably, CCD matches the fully supervised BCE baseline on VOC 2012 (90.1%) and trails by only 0.3%p on VOC 2007, despite using zero annotations. However, CCD underperforms the fully supervised BCE-LS baseline by 1.5–9.1%p and the single-positive-label methods (LL-R, G2NetPL) on COCO (70.3 vs 72.6/72.5) and NUSWIDE (44.5 vs 47.4/48.5), indicating that the gap to fully supervised methods widens on larger, more complex datasets. Against partial-label methods (SARB, ASL, Chen et al.), CCD achieves substantially higher mAP on VOC 2007 (91.0 vs 81.5–85.7) but is slightly below SARB on COCO (70.3 vs 72.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends CDUL (Abdelfattah et al., ICCV 2023), the first method to leverage CLIP for unsupervised multi-label classification, by addressing two identified limitations: view-dependent predictions and inherent class-wise prediction bias. Where CDUL uses uniform grid patches for local views, CCD replaces this with Class Activation Mapping (CAM)-guided cropping around object regions, and introduces a debiasing step that inverts CLIP's measured class-wise probability distribution. The consistency loss component draws on semi-supervised learning practices, and the overall framework positions CLIP as a frozen teacher whose pseudo-labels are refined and distilled into a standard ResNet-101 classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCD demonstrates that unsupervised multi-label classification can match fully supervised performance on 20-class benchmarks (90.1% mAP on VOC12 vs. 90.1% for BCE) without any human annotation, which is a practically significant result for annotation-cost-sensitive applications. However, the gains diminish on larger-class datasets (only 1.1%p over CDUL on 80-class COCO), and the authors acknowledge that CAM-based view selection struggles with co-occurring objects, producing noisy pseudo-labels. An open question is whether the debiasing strategy generalizes to open-vocabulary or very large-scale label spaces where the class-wise bias estimation becomes less reliable, and whether more sophisticated view-proposal mechanisms (beyond CAM) could resolve the co-occurrence limitation.
