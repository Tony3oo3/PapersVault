## Problem

Existing 2D slice-based angiography synthesis methods produce discontinuous blood vessels in 3D volumes and are restricted to single modality pairs. The paper addresses how to synthesize anatomically continuous 3D vasculature from non-angiographic inputs across multiple modalities (MRI, CT) and anatomical regions, reducing the need for nephrotoxic contrast agents and ionizing radiation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250713/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D non-angiographic volumes (brain MRI sequences: T1-Flash, T2, T1-MPRAGE; lung CT) as input and produces 3D synthesized angiography volumes (MRA or CTA) as output. Processing occurs in a VAE latent space where the 3D volume is decomposed into cross-sectional slices; the vision embedder generates per-slice mask and token embeddings that condition the diffusion process, and the final angiography is reconstructed by the VAE decoder back to volumetric form.

## Method

VasTSD combines a pre-trained vision embedder (trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss to extract modality-invariant vascular state representations) with a diffusion model whose reverse denoising step uses a novel vascular tree-state space module. The key architectural innovation is a dynamic tree scanning algorithm: given per-slice vessel features, it constructs a 4-connected graph weighted by cosine distance, prunes it to a Minimum Spanning Tree via Kruskal's algorithm, and performs bidirectional (top-down and bottom-up) state propagation along the tree to aggregate long-range vascular dependencies with linear complexity. A cross-slice attention mechanism, guided by mask-weighted cosine similarity between slice feature vectors, enforces 3D geometric continuity across the volume. The overall training objective combines the InfoNCE embedding loss, the standard denoising MSE loss, and a tree-scan gradient loss, with the tree topology adapting to each input's vascular geometry rather than using fixed serialization orders.

## Results

VasTSD achieves the highest PSNR across all five evaluation settings, reaching 29.92 dB on ITK-TubeTK T1-MPRAGE and 29.48 dB on ISICDM 2020 CT. On SSIM, VasTSD leads on T1-Flash (0.9478), CT (0.9562), and CTA (0.9372), though SynDiff slightly edges it on T2 (0.9482 vs. 0.9384) and T1-MPRAGE (0.9516 vs. 0.9454). In 3D volume evaluation, VasTSD also outperforms baselines on Dice, Jaccard, and Connectivity scores, demonstrating superior geometric continuity of synthesized vessels across slices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VasTSD outperforms all five baselines on PSNR across every dataset, with the largest margins over Fast-DDPM (up to +2.94 dB on CTA) and SAGAN (up to +2.04 dB on T1-MPRAGE). Against SynDiff, the strongest 2D diffusion baseline, VasTSD gains 0.74–1.23 dB in PSNR but concedes a small SSIM gap on T2 (−0.0098) and T1-MPRAGE (−0.0062), which the authors attribute to SynDiff's 2D-only design versus VasTSD's 3D connectivity focus. DiffMa achieves competitive PSNR on MRI sequences but suffers severely degraded SSIM (0.69–0.73) on all non-CT settings, indicating poor structural fidelity; VasTSD recovers SSIM to 0.94–0.96 on those same settings. On the ISICDM 2020 CT→CTA task, VasTSD leads both PSNR (29.48 vs. 28.94 for DiffMa) and SSIM (0.9562 vs. 0.9428), confirming its advantage in the clinically most relevant contrast-enhanced synthesis scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

VasTSD sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-based medical image synthesis (building on DDPM [13] and extending methods like SynDiff [27] and DiffMa [37]) and state space models for sequence modeling (building on Mamba [8]). The key departure is from prior angiography synthesis work, which operates on 2D slices with fixed serialization patterns, to a 3D-aware framework that dynamically constructs a minimum spanning tree over vascular features to respect anatomical topology. The pre-trained vision embedder with contrastive learning (InfoNCE) provides a modality-agnostic conditioning signal, distinguishing it from single-modality GAN approaches (SAGAN, cGAN) and from DiffMa's Mamba-based CT-to-MRI conversion, which does not model vascular geometry explicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By synthesizing angiography without contrast agents, this work addresses a direct clinical safety concern (nephrotoxicity and radiation exposure) while enabling multi-modal vascular imaging from routine scans. The 3D continuity guarantee is practically important for surgical planning and disease diagnosis, where disconnected vessels in synthesized volumes would be clinically misleading. However, the 3D evaluation relies on stacking 2D slices from prior methods as the comparison baseline rather than true 3D generative models, leaving open whether the advantage persists against dedicated 3D diffusion approaches. The authors also note that anisotropy in medical acquisition introduces orientation-dependent quality variation that remains unaddressed.
