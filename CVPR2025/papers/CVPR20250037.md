## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for VLMs fail to simultaneously satisfy three practical requirements: explicit modeling of the target distribution, availability (no access to source data or storage of historical test samples), and training-free efficiency. Prior approaches either treat each test sample independently, require costly gradient-based prompt optimization, or cache subsets of test features, limiting their applicability in real-world sequential online settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250037/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single unlabeled RGB test image at a time (batch size 1, processed by a frozen CLIP image encoder) along with pre-defined class text prompts (processed by the frozen CLIP text encoder). It outputs a final class label derived from a weighted combination of CLIP zero-shot logits and Gaussian discriminant analysis logits, along with per-class posterior probabilities. Crucially, the method operates strictly online and sequentially—each sample is predicted without access to or storage of any previously seen test samples, and the only persistent state is a compact set of per-class mean vectors, a shared covariance matrix, and class priors.

## Method

FreeTTA models the test distribution as a Gaussian mixture and adapts it via an online EM algorithm. In the E-step, it computes the posterior probability of the current sample belonging to each class using the current GMM parameters; in the M-step, it updates class-specific mean vectors, a shared covariance matrix, and class priors in a single-sample online fashion. To stabilize early-stage adaptation, the method weights each sample's contribution by an exponential decay of its CLIP zero-shot prediction entropy (w(h)=e^{-βh}), reducing the influence of high-uncertainty samples. The final prediction fuses CLIP zero-shot logits with GDA-derived linear logits (w_y^T F + b_y) via a fixed mixing coefficient α. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no gradient computation, and stores only O(K·d) parameters rather than any test samples.

## Results

On the cross-domain benchmark (10 datasets), FreeTTA with ViT-B/16 achieves 68.42% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest training-free and available baselines MTA (64.63%) and ZERO (64.66%) by 3.79% and 3.76% respectively. On the OOD benchmark (ImageNet + 4 variants), FreeTTA with ViT-B/16 reaches 65.58% average accuracy and 64.42% OOD average, surpassing MTA (63.16%/61.63%) and ZERO (64.02%/62.76%). With ResNet-50, FreeTTA achieves 61.33% cross-domain average, a 3.67% gain over TPT (57.66%). The method is the only one satisfying all three criteria: target distribution modeling, availability (no stored data), and training-free operation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeTTA outperforms all 9 baselines on average across both benchmarks. Against the closest training-free and available competitors MTA [53] and ZERO [11], FreeTTA gains 3.79% and 3.76% on cross-domain (ViT-B/16) and 2.42% and 1.56% on OOD, respectively, attributed to explicit target distribution modeling via online EM. Against TDA [21], which requires caching test features, FreeTTA wins on 9/10 cross-domain datasets (+0.89% avg) while eliminating the storage requirement. Against prompt-learning methods TPT [39] and DiffTPT [13], FreeTTA achieves +3.32% and +2.95% on cross-domain (ViT-B/16) and +3.14% and +3.30% on OOD, while being training-free (no backpropagation). Against PromptAlign [1], which requires source-domain statistics, FreeTTA gains 1.5% on cross-domain without any source data access. FreeTTA is the only method satisfying all three criteria (target distribution modeling, availability, training-free), and it leads on 8/10 cross-domain datasets and all 5 OOD datasets with ViT-B/16.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical EM algorithm for Gaussian Mixture Models (Moon, 1996) and Gaussian Discriminant Analysis (Bishop, 2006) into an online, sequential variant for test-time adaptation of VLMs. It departs from the dominant TTA paradigm of prompt optimization (TPT, DiffTPT) and feature shifting (MTA, ZERO) by instead explicitly modeling the target test distribution as a GMM whose parameters are updated sample-by-sample via online EM, using CLIP's zero-shot predictions as soft priors. Unlike TDA, which caches historical test features as instance-level references, FreeTTA compresses all historical information into a small set of distribution parameters (means, shared covariance, priors), eliminating the need to store any past samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeTTA is the first TTA method to simultaneously satisfy target distribution modeling, full availability (no source data, no stored test samples, no parameter modification), and training-free operation, making it directly applicable to API-based VLMs where gradient access and data retention are restricted. The shared-covariance Gaussian assumption and the ad-hoc exponential entropy weighting (w(h)=e^{-βh}) are simplifications that may limit performance on highly anisotropic or multimodal class distributions, and the method has not been tested on very large class spaces or non-i.i.d. test streams. Open questions include whether per-class covariances or non-Gaussian mixture components would yield further gains, and how the approach extends to dense prediction tasks where the per-sample independence assumption is less natural.
