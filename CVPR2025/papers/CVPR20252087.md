## Problem

Correspondence-based point cloud registration requires heuristic information (sampling probabilities) to guide consensus set selection, but existing approaches either depend on supervised inlier/outlier labels or expert-tuned thresholds. Unsupervised transformation estimation for correspondence-based PCR remains largely unexplored, and directly applying policy gradient optimization to learn sampling heuristics suffers from limited reward utilization (reward only reaches K sampled correspondences out of N) and noise sensitivity under high outlier ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252087/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a correspondence set C = {c_i}_{i=1}^N where each c_i = (x_i, y_i) is a source-target 3D point pair, and outputs per-correspondence sampling probabilities P ∈ [0,1]^N and feature embeddings F ∈ R^{N×D}. Sampling is performed without replacement but approximated by the initial distribution since the subset size K is much smaller than N. The outputs are plugged into existing SAC-like samplers (PSAC, PointDSC's Seeding Mechanism, or SC²-PCR) to replace their original scoring or seed-selection components.

## Method

HeMoRa trains a Heuristic Generator (HeGen) via a Multi-order Reward Aggregator (MoRa) loss that frames subset sampling as a policy optimization problem: Q subsets are sampled, each yields a rigid transformation h^q whose inlier ratio serves as the reward R^q. The key innovation is the Maximal Reward Propagation (MRP) mechanism, which (1) redefines the indicator so that reward propagates to all correspondences consistent with h^q (not just the K sampled ones), and (2) replaces mean-pooling with max-pooling across Q trials to filter out noisy, outlier-dominated subsets. The first-order MoRa loss optimizes individual sampling probabilities, while a second-order extension (MoRa²) uses a dualSoftmax operator on feature embeddings to encourage similarity between jointly consistent correspondence pairs, capturing inter-correspondence relationships analogous to edge-wise supervision in PointDSC.

## Results

On the 3DMatch dataset with FCGF descriptors, Ours+SC² achieves the highest registration recall of 93.65% (RE 2.10°, TE 6.53 cm), surpassing the best supervised method VBReg (93.10%) and the best traditional method SC²-PCR (93.28%). With FPFH descriptors, Ours+SC² reaches 83.92% RR, tying MAC and exceeding SC²-PCR (83.73%). On the more challenging KITTI-30m benchmark (trained only on KITTI-10m), Ours+SC² with GeDI achieves 64.31% RR, outperforming SC²-PCR (62.75%) and PointDSC (50.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 3DMatch with FCGF, Ours+SC² (93.65% RR) outperforms the strongest supervised baseline VBReg (93.10%) by 0.55% and the best traditional method SC²-PCR (93.28%) by 0.37%, while Ours+SM (93.41%) also exceeds VBReg by 0.31%. With FPFH, Ours+SC² (83.92%) ties MAC (83.92%) and edges out SC²-PCR (83.73%) by 0.19%, but falls slightly short of VBReg (82.75%)—wait, actually 83.92 > 82.75, so it beats VBReg by 1.17%. Ours+PSAC improves over random RANSAC-4M by 6.11% (FPFH) and 1.35% (FCGF), confirming the value of learned sampling probabilities. On KITTI-10m, Ours+SM matches SC²-PCR at 98.92% RR (FPFH) and Ours+SC² beats VBReg by 1.08% (FCGF: 98.02% vs 96.94%). On the harder KITTI-30m (zero-shot generalization), Ours+SC² with GeDI reaches 64.31% RR, surpassing SC²-PCR (62.75%) and PointDSC (50.00%) by 1.56% and 14.31% respectively, demonstrating strong robustness under low overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes policy gradient optimization from reinforcement learning to train a heuristic sampling generator for correspondence-based point cloud registration, a setting where prior learning-based methods (DGR, PointDSC, VBReg) all require ground-truth transformation labels. It departs from the unsupervised registration line (CEMNet, RIENet), which operates in a correspondence-free or ICP-based paradigm, by bringing unsupervised learning into the correspondence-based SAC framework for the first time. The Maximal Reward Propagation (MRP) mechanism is a targeted modification of standard policy gradient—replacing mean-pooling with max-pooling and extending reward propagation from sampled to all consistent correspondences—motivated by the specific noise structure of registration rather than by prior RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for ground-truth transformations during training, HeMoRa makes correspondence-based PCR applicable in settings where labeled data is prohibitively expensive to acquire, and the cross-descriptor generalization results (Table 3) suggest the learned heuristic is more transferable than supervised inlier classifiers. The MRP mechanism offers a general recipe for policy gradient in domains with sparse, noisy reward signals, potentially applicable beyond registration. Open questions include scalability to very large correspondence sets (the Q-sample exploration cost), whether the approach can be integrated end-to-end with learned descriptor training, and whether the modest margins over supervised baselines persist under more challenging real-world conditions (sensor noise, dynamic scenes).
