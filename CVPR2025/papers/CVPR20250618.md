## Problem

Generating 3D hand-object interaction (HOI) motion that generalizes to unseen object geometry remains largely unexplored. Existing diffusion-based HOI methods assume seen objects, and directly modeling the joint hand-object distribution overfits on the small available datasets, producing penetration errors, lost tracking, and physically implausible grasps on novel shapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250618/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a canonical object point cloud (encoded via Basis Point Set) and a free-text motion description (encoded via CLIP) as conditions. It outputs a sequence of N frames (up to 160 at 20 fps for bimanual, 96 for single-hand) containing MANO hand poses (1 global + 15 local 6D joint rotations per hand) and rigid object 6D poses. Hand root translations are expressed as object-centric offsets rather than world coordinates, and rotations use the 6D representation (first two rows of the rotation matrix) for continuity.

## Method

LatentHOI decouples high-level temporal motion from fine-grained spatial grasping via a two-module pipeline. A single-frame GraspVAE (16-dim latent) encodes articulated hand poses conditioned on hand root position and object pose, trained with ELBO plus a MANO mesh reconstruction loss; left and right hands share the same VAE backbone. A latent diffusion model then generates the triplet (Z^{1:N}, O^{1:N}, H_γ^{1:N}) conditioned on text and object BPS, operating in the regularized latent space rather than the full 96-dim pose space. Training is two-staged: GraspVAE is first trained on individual frames, then its frozen encoder produces latent codes for diffusion training. A shared reparameterization noise η across all temporal frames promotes smoother latent transitions, and classifier-free guidance is used at sampling time.

## Results

On the GRAB unseen-object split, LatentHOI achieves the lowest interpenetration volume (IV: 6.38 cm³ right, 1.66 cm³ left) and interpenetration volume per contact unit (IVU: 0.10), along with the highest physical plausibility score (Phy: 96.16%), outperforming MDM (IV: 9.12/2.61, IVU: 0.19, Phy: 89.81%) and MLD (IV: 9.62/3.14, IVU: 0.24, Phy: 85.68%). On the more challenging OakInk out-of-distribution split, LatentHOI achieves the best IVU (0.14) and Phy (71.24%), substantially reducing penetration compared to MDM (IVU: 0.20, Phy: 60.89%) and MLD (IVU: 0.29, Phy: 46.41%). On DexYCB single-hand grasping, LatentHOI achieves a higher contact ratio (11.98% vs. 8.87% for MDM) and lower IVU (0.13 vs. 0.18), though gains are more modest due to the small training set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LatentHOI consistently outperforms MDM [38] (the primary diffusion baseline) across all physics-based metrics on both GRAB and OakInk: on GRAB it reduces right-hand IV by 30% (6.38 vs. 9.12) and left-hand IV by 36% (1.66 vs. 2.61), while on OakInk it cuts IVU from 0.20 to 0.14 and improves Phy from 60.89 to 71.24. Against MLD [5], the advantage is even larger on OakInk, where MLD's Phy collapses to 46.41% versus LatentHOI's 71.24%, and IVU is 0.29 vs. 0.14. IMoS [11] is only evaluated on GRAB (it requires leading frames unavailable for OakInk) and shows the worst IVU (0.53) and Phy (84.88%), with zero sample diversity. Text2HOI [2] is only tested on OakInk; while it achieves a higher Phy (82.58) than LatentHOI (71.24), it suffers from severe penetration (IV: 15.19/11.54 vs. 7.22/3.11) and higher IVU (0.26 vs. 0.14), indicating its high contact comes at the cost of unrealistic interpenetration. On DexYCB, LatentHOI narrowly beats MDM on all metrics (IV 7.70 vs. 7.78, CR 11.98 vs. 8.87, IVU 0.13 vs. 0.18, Phy 88.52 vs. 86.22), though the margin is smaller due to the limited 16-object training set.

## Relation to Prior Work

LatentHOI builds on the latent diffusion paradigm established by MLD (Chen et al., CVPR 2023) and the diffusion backbone of MDM (Tevet et al., ICLR 2023), but repurposes the latent space not for generation speed (as in MLD) but as a regularization mechanism for hand-object interaction generalization. The GraspVAE component draws on single-frame grasp generation literature (GraspTTA, Grasping Field) but integrates it as a conditional decoder within a temporal generation pipeline, eliminating the need for post-processing contact consistency modules. The key departure from prior HOI diffusion works (Text2HOI, InterHandGen, GeneoH Diffusion) is the explicit decoupling of high-level temporal trajectories from fine-grained spatial grasping, which the authors argue is necessary to avoid overfitting on the small-scale HOI datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Generalization to unseen object geometry is a critical bottleneck for deploying HOI motion synthesis in robotics and interactive applications, yet prior methods largely evaluate only on seen objects. The paper demonstrates that a principled architectural decomposition (temporal diffusion + spatial VAE) can substantially reduce penetration and improve physical plausibility on completely novel shapes (OakInk), offering a practical recipe for small-data HOI generation. However, the method remains constrained by training data diversity—it cannot handle objects with significantly different sizes or geometries, nor unseen motion intents—leaving open the question of how to scale beyond the ~50-object training regime, potentially through 2D video reconstruction or physics-based augmentation.
