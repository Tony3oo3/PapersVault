## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting-based generation methods suffer from three interrelated limitations: they lack part-level descriptions of Gaussian primitives, require time-consuming preprocessing (e.g., GaussianCube's O(N³) optimal transport) or complex training to handle the disordered spatial structure of Gaussians, and offer limited controllability over the generation process. These constraints prevent efficient, fine-grained control over generated 3D content from text or image prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251258/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts, single reference images, or class labels as conditioning signals and produces a fixed set of 36,864 3D Gaussian primitives (structured as 64×64×9) that can be rendered at 512×512 resolution or extracted into meshes. A key structural detail is that the output Gaussians are organized into a hierarchical tree of part-level latent representations, enabling selective modification of individual object parts during generation or editing.

## Method

The framework combines three components: (1) HGMM Splatting, which builds a hierarchical Gaussian Mixture Model tree over a fixed set of 3DGS primitives with jointly optimized explicit (tree-structured) and implicit (decoder-generated latent) branches, distributing multiscale detail across levels while establishing part-level representations; (2) a Diffusion Mamba with tree topology that constructs a minimum spanning tree over disordered Gaussians via the Contractive Boruvka algorithm and performs state propagation along tree paths, achieving linear complexity without handcrafted scanning or O(N³) preprocessing; (3) a cascaded diffusion pipeline that first hierarchically generates part-level implicit latents conditioned on the input, then uses the Diffusion Mamba to denoise explicit Gaussian primitives conditioned on those latents, with an image-level VGG perceptual loss and alpha-channel MSE loss for rendering fidelity.

## Results

On unconditional generation, the method achieves FID-50K of 11.03 and KID-50K of 7.16 on ShapeNet Car, and 12.74/8.61 on ShapeNet Chair, outperforming GaussianCube (13.01/8.46 and 15.99/9.95) and all GAN/diffusion baselines. On class-conditioned generation over OmniObject3D, it reaches FID-50K 10.57 and KID-50K 2.02, again beating GaussianCube (11.62/2.78). For text-to-3D, the method attains a CLIP score of 30.61 with 5-second inference, marginally surpassing LGM (30.06, 4 s) and Shape-E (30.52, 14 s) while being 46× faster than DreamGaussian (~230 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms GaussianCube [57] on every reported metric: FID drops from 13.01→11.03 (Car) and 11.62→10.57 (OmniObject3D), KID from 8.46→7.16 and 2.78→2.02, fitting speed improves from 3.33× to 9.32×, and mesh extraction FPD halves from 3.5 to 1.7. Against the 3D-aware GANs EG3D [5] and GET3D [15], the gains are substantial (FID 30.48→11.03 and 17.15→11.03 on Car), reflecting the advantage of explicit Gaussian primitives over triplane/latent representations. DiffTF [4] is far behind on all FID/KID scores (e.g., 51.88 vs 11.03 on Car), confirming that triplane diffusion struggles with fine geometry. In text-to-3D, the method edges out LGM [43] on CLIP (30.61 vs 30.06) at a negligible 1-second cost, and dramatically outperforms DreamGaussian [41] (CLIP 30.61 vs 26.38; 5 s vs ~230 s). For image-to-3D on 3DBiCar, PSNR improves from 24.78 to 27.63 and FID-5K from 7.35 to 4.99 over GaussianCube. The only minor trade-off is inference speed vs LGM (5 s vs 4 s), but the CLIP score and multi-view consistency favor the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 3D Gaussian Splatting as a generative representation, building directly on 3DGS [27] and competing with GaussianCube [57] and LGM [43]; (2) hierarchical Gaussian Mixture Models originally developed for point cloud registration and generation [10, 11, 21], here repurposed to distribute multi-scale detail within a fixed set of Gaussian primitives and to create an implicit part-level latent space; and (3) Mamba-based state space models [16] with tree-topology scanning borrowed from GrootVL [52], adapted to handle the disordered spatial structure of Gaussian primitives without the O(N³) optimal-transport preprocessing that GaussianCube requires. The key departure from prior work is the joint explicit-implicit hierarchical representation that simultaneously fixes the primitive count, encodes part-level semantics, and serves as a conditioning signal for a cascaded diffusion generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The part-level controllability demonstrated here (12.73% → 87.27% prompt alignment with cascaded diffusion) addresses a practical gap in current 3DGS generation where users cannot selectively edit or specify individual object components. The linear-complexity tree scanning eliminates the expensive preprocessing bottleneck of GaussianCube, making the approach more scalable. However, several open questions remain: the hierarchical GMM fitting still requires 30K iterations per object, the tree construction (Boruvka + BFS) adds non-trivial overhead, and all experiments are limited to single-object datasets (ShapeNet, OmniObject3D) with no evaluation on scenes or articulated objects. Whether the fixed 36,864-primitive budget generalizes to highly complex or multi-part objects, and whether the implicit latent hierarchy scales to larger scene-level generation, are important directions for future work.
