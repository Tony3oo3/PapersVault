## Problem

Pre-trained SSL vision encoders (e.g., CLIP, EVA) are widely shared and frozen inside many LVLMs, yet no prior work has shown that compromising just the encoder alone can induce significant visual hallucinations in downstream LVLMs. Existing SSL backdoor attacks target classification and cannot precisely manipulate high-dimensional visual features, while LVLM-specific backdoor attacks lack transferability, require expensive full-model fine-tuning, and are limited to predefined text outputs rather than free-form misleading narratives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained SSL vision encoder (CLIP ViT-L-336px or EVA ViT-G/14) and fine-tunes it on a 5K-image shadow dataset (PASCAL VOC, out-of-distribution from pre-training). The trigger is a universal, imperceptible adversarial perturbation with L∞ bound of 8/255, stamped onto any input image. The backdoored encoder produces visual features closely matching a single attacker-chosen target image, causing the downstream LVLM (LLaVA-1.5 or MiniGPT-4) to generate coherent but hallucinated captions and VQA answers describing the target rather than the actual input. The attack is transferable: a backdoor trained on LLaVA-7B transfers directly to LLaVA-13B without retraining.

## Method

BADVISION formulates backdoor implantation as a bilevel optimization: in the first stage, the trigger Δ* is optimized (via PGD) against the frozen clean encoder to pull its output embeddings toward the target image embedding, minimizing the parameter deviation needed later. In the second stage, the encoder is fine-tuned with three loss terms — an effectiveness loss aligning triggered features to the target, a utility loss preserving clean-input feature fidelity, and a trigger-focus loss that trains the encoder to ignore an 'invalid trigger' δ* (optimized to cluster features) by producing clean-like outputs under δ*. This trigger-focusing mechanism, inspired by adversarial training, prevents the backdoored encoder from exhibiting the feature-concentration sensitivity that detection methods like DECREE exploit, allowing the backdoor to evade SoTA detection while maintaining >99% attack success rate and near-zero benign performance degradation.

## Results

BADVISION achieves 100% attack success rate (ASR) on EVA ViT-G/14 and 98–99% ASR on CLIP ViT-L-336px across five datasets (COCO, Flickr30k, VizWiz, GQA, VQAv2), while maintaining benign embedding similarity (Sim-B) of 0.95 and 0.88 respectively. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, BADVISION induces a 77.6% average relative error in visual understanding across eight benchmarks when the trigger is active, with only a 1.4-point drop in clean performance. The attack also transfers directly to LLaVA-1.5-13B without retraining, retaining 100% ASR and 0% false activation rate, and evades the SoTA detector DECREE (Pℓ¹ = 0.220 for CLIP, 0.498 for EVA, well above the 0.1 detection threshold).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the universal adversarial attack (Adv., adapted from [59]), BADVISION dominates on both encoders: on EVA, ASR jumps from 25% to 100%, and on CLIP from 2% to 99%, while also achieving far higher target-embedding similarity (Sim-T 0.85 vs. 0.56 on EVA). Against BadEncoder [22], BADVISION is decisive on EVA (100% vs. 2.2% ASR) and slightly ahead on CLIP (98.8% vs. 99.4% ASR with higher Sim-T of 0.76 vs. 0.72), but critically BADVISION evades DECREE detection (Pℓ¹ = 0.220/0.498) while BadEncoder is flagged (Pℓ¹ < 0.1). Against Shadowcast [52], BADVISION achieves 100% vs. 3.4% ASR on random images (86% only when restricted to a single concept), uses less GPU memory (27.2 vs. 37.8 GB), and transfers to LLaVA-13B without retraining. Against ImgTroj [45], BADVISION matches or exceeds ASR (100% vs. 86.3% on 7B; 100% vs. 65.7% on 13B) with zero false activations and full cross-scale transferability, whereas ImgTroj drops to 65.7% on 13B and cannot transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of SSL backdoor attacks and LVLM security. Prior SSL backdoor work (BadEncoder, BadCLIP, Carlini et al.) targets classification tasks and cannot precisely control high-dimensional visual features needed for generative models, while prior LVLM backdoor attacks (Shadowcast, ImgTroj, VLTrojan) modify the LLM or projection layer, are non-transferable, and are limited to predefined text outputs. BADVISION departs from both lines by targeting the frozen vision encoder itself, using bilevel trigger-encoder co-optimization and a trigger-focusing mechanism to achieve free-form, transferable hallucinations that evade SoTA detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single compromised pre-trained encoder (e.g., CLIP ViT-L-336px) can backdoor every downstream LVLM that freezes it—without any modification to the LVLM itself—exposes a critical supply-chain vulnerability in the LVLM ecosystem, with direct implications for safety-critical deployments in autonomous driving and embodied AI. The successful evasion of DECREE, the only applicable SoTA detector, means current defenses are insufficient. Open questions include whether trigger-focusing generalizes to other encoder architectures, whether physical-world triggers (e.g., printed stickers) can activate the backdoor, and what practical defenses (encoder provenance verification, feature-space monitoring) can mitigate this class of attack.
