## Problem

Existing SSDA methods for semantic segmentation suffer from three intertwined failures: misclassification among visually similar classes under limited target supervision, over-reliance on source-domain features that produces noisy pseudo-labels, and class imbalance that suppresses tail-class learning. No prior work has leveraged the rich semantic relationships encoded in vision-language models to bridge these gaps in the SSDA setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250732/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes three data streams: fully labeled source images (GTA5 or SYNTHIA, resized to 760×1280), a small set of labeled target images (0–2975 from Cityscapes, resized to 512×1024), and abundant unlabeled target images. An off-the-shelf BLIP-2 captioning model generates text descriptions for every input image, which are then encoded by a frozen CLIP language encoder to produce dense language embeddings. The output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask at 512×512 resolution, evaluated via mIoU on the Cityscapes validation set (19 or 16 classes) or DSC/ASD on the Synapse medical dataset (13 organs).

## Method

SemiDAViL initializes both student and teacher segmentation encoders with CLIP's ViT-B/16 vision encoder rather than an ImageNet backbone, transferring rich class-agnostic semantic priors. The key architectural novelty is the Dense Language Guidance (DLG) module, which projects both vision and language features into symmetric key-value pairs and computes a cross-attention matrix A = (1/√c) F_V^K · (F_L^K)^T, then applies SoftMax-normalized attention over both modalities to produce a true multimodal feature F_M = F_V^A · (F_L^A)^T, avoiding the information loss of standard single-modality attention. Training uses a student-teacher consistency framework with EMA updates and thresholded pseudo-labels for unlabeled data, combined with a supervised loss on labeled source and target samples. The proposed DyCE loss dynamically reweights gradients by mining the h% hardest instances per batch, assigning class weights 1/f_c^(1−ω) inversely proportional to mined class frequency, and scaling by a volume factor 1/f_H^ω to resolve gradient vanishing in large imbalanced batches.

## Results

On GTA5→Cityscapes (19-class mIoU), SemiDAViL with DyCE achieves 71.1% with 100 labeled target samples, outperforming the previous best SSDA method IIDM (69.5%) by 1.6 points, and 75.2% with full supervision (2975 samples) vs. IIDM's 73.3%. In the fully unsupervised setting (0 labels), it reaches 67.7% mIoU, a 5-point improvement over the best UDA method DIGA (62.7%). On Synthia→Cityscapes (16-class mIoU), the method achieves 76.9% at 100 labels and 80.5% at full supervision, surpassing IIDM (74.2% and 79.2%) and ALFSA (68.9% and 79.9%). On the severely imbalanced Synapse medical dataset, DyCE as a plug-in loss boosts the weakest SSL baseline (UA-MT) from 20.3% to 48.1% mean DSC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiDAViL consistently outperforms all compared SSDA methods across every label budget. Against the strongest SSDA baseline IIDM [23], it gains +1.6 mIoU at 100 labels (71.1 vs. 69.5) and +1.9 at full supervision (75.2 vs. 73.3) on GTA5→Cityscapes; on Synthia→Cityscapes the margin widens to +2.7 at 100 labels (76.9 vs. 74.2). In the unsupervised regime (0 labels), the language-guided joint embedding yields a substantial +5.0 mIoU over the best UDA method DIGA (67.7 vs. 62.7), attributed to more robust semantic alignment than divergence-based approaches like DaFormer. The DyCE loss contributes a consistent +0.8 mIoU over the no-DyCE variant across all label splits, with the largest relative gains on tail classes (e.g., Fence: 49.2→51.5, Pole: 58.3→60.2, Wall: 50.2→51.9 on GTA5→Cityscapes at 100 labels). On the Synapse medical dataset, DyCE as a plug-in loss improves every tested SSL method: UA-MT jumps from 20.3% to 48.1% mean DSC, DePL from 36.3% to 51.8%, and the prior best A&D from 60.9% to 65.5%, confirming its generality beyond the SSDA setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SemiVL line of work (VL-guided semi-supervised segmentation) into the semi-supervised domain adaptation (SSDA) setting, which prior SSDA methods (IIDM, S-Depth, DSTC, DECOTA) addressed without language priors. Architecturally, it departs from standard cross-attention fusion by proposing a symmetric Dense Language Guidance (DLG) module that treats vision and language features as equal key-query pairs, and introduces a Dynamic Cross-Entropy (DyCE) loss that addresses gradient vanishing in imbalanced dense prediction — a problem that static reweighting schemes (focal loss, class-weighted CE) leave unresolved. The student-teacher consistency training framework follows Tarvainen & Valpola, while the CLIP-initialized ViT-B/16 backbone and BLIP-2 captioning pipeline follow SemiVL and Kalluri et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DyCE loss is validated as a plug-in across five unrelated SSL methods on a severely imbalanced medical dataset (Synapse), suggesting broad applicability beyond the SSDA setting; however, the largest gains (+27.9% DSC) come from the weakest baseline (UA-MT), and improvements over the strongest class-balanced SSL (A&D) are modest (+4.6%). The framework's reliance on an external captioning model (BLIP-2) for every input image adds inference overhead and a potential failure mode not fully analyzed. Open questions include whether the DLG module's symmetric attention design generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the gains persist under larger domain shifts (e.g., cross-weather, cross-sensor) beyond the GTA5/SYNTHIA→Cityscapes benchmarks.
