## Problem

Direct generation of complex B-rep CAD models (beyond ~50 faces) remains unsolved: prior methods like BrepGen rely on 2D tree embeddings and multiple diffusion/VAE modules, requiring 2× RTX4090 GPUs and limiting complexity to 50 faces. There is no lightweight approach that can generate high-quality, watertight B-rep models with over 100 faces using minimal GPU resources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250615/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes random noise as input (unconditional generation) and produces a complete B-rep CAD model consisting of parametric B-spline surfaces, B-spline edge curves, and a valid topological structure (faces, edges, vertices) assembled into a watertight solid via OpenCascade. Internally, the B-rep is represented as a 3D graph where each node is a face centroid in 3D space carrying a 256-dim feature vector (from 32×32 UV-domain sampling processed by 2D CNN plus type/area/degree/index properties), and each edge carries a 256-dim feature vector (from 1D CNN on sampled edge points plus type/length/convexity/adjacency properties). The model supports generation of models up to 120 faces.

## Method

BrepGiff encodes B-rep models into a centered 3D graph (nodes = face centroids, edges = face adjacencies simplified via Minimum Spanning Tree) and applies a degree-guided discrete diffusion process. In the forward pass, edges are selectively removed based on node degrees (inspired by EDGE) while Gaussian noise is added to node and edge features; nodes whose degree changes are tracked as an 'active node' set. The reverse denoising uses a Graph Attention Network with a novel cross-attention mechanism that bidirectionally fuses 256-dim node (face) and edge features, enforcing topology-geometry consistency. A local-to-global strategy predicts edges only for active nodes at each step, guided by the initial degree sequence, which drastically reduces computation. The final denoised graph is decoded into a valid B-rep via B-spline surface/curve fitting, topology-aware trimming, and watertight assembly. With only 7.80×10⁷ parameters (vs. BrepGen's 4.56×10⁸), BrepGiff trains on a single RTX4090 and generates complex models (>100 faces) with 67.7% validity at 50 faces and 62.9% at 100 faces.

## Results

On the ABC dataset (≤50 faces), BrepGiff achieves state-of-the-art performance on 4 of 6 metrics: Coverage 75.52%, MMD 0.98%, JSD 1.17%, and Validity 67.7%, all surpassing BrepGen (73.87%, 1.04%, 1.28%, 62.9%). The model is notably lightweight with only 7.80×10⁷ parameters (vs. 4.56×10⁸ for BrepGen), trains in 4 days on a single RTX 4090, and uniquely supports generation of complex B-rep models up to 100 faces (62.9% valid) where BrepGen cannot train beyond 50 faces on the same hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepCAD, BrepGiff dominates on all six metrics, with the largest gap in Validity (67.7% vs. 46.1%) and Coverage (75.52% vs. 65.46%), reflecting DeepCAD's command-sequence approach lacking explicit B-rep topology. Against SolidGen, BrepGiff again wins on all metrics, improving Validity by 7.4 points (67.7% vs. 60.3%) and COV by 4.5 points, while requiring only 1 RTX 4090 versus SolidGen's 8 A100 GPUs. Against the closest competitor BrepGen, BrepGiff achieves better Coverage (+1.65%), lower MMD (0.98 vs. 1.04), lower JSD (1.17 vs. 1.28), and higher Validity (+4.8 points), but trails slightly on Novelty (98.9% vs. 99.8%) and Uniqueness (98.6% vs. 99.7%). Critically, BrepGiff is the only method that can generate B-rep models with >100 faces on 2 RTX 4090s (62.9% valid at 100 faces), a capability BrepGen lacks entirely on the same hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

BrepGiff builds directly on the degree-guided discrete diffusion framework of EDGE [5] and the DDPM formulation [13], adapting their edge-removal/addition mechanism to B-rep topology. It departs from BrepGen's 2D tree embedding and SolidGen's autoregressive Transformer by representing B-rep as a 3D graph (face centroids as nodes, topological adjacencies as edges) simplified via MST, and by replacing generic denoisers with a cross-attention GAT that jointly refines face and edge geometry. The UV-domain CNN feature extraction for faces and edges follows the sampling paradigm of UVNet and BRepNet but repurposes it as graph node/edge attributes rather than a standalone classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BrepGiff demonstrates that complex B-rep generation (>100 faces) is feasible on consumer-grade hardware (2× RTX 4090), a significant practical step toward democratizing direct CAD synthesis that was previously locked to multi-A100 clusters. However, validity degrades sharply with complexity (67.7% at 50 faces → 48.3% at 120 faces), and failure cases reveal missing or broken faces, indicating the method has not yet solved the fundamental challenge of maintaining geometric consistency at scale. Open questions include whether the MST-based graph simplification loses critical topological information for very complex assemblies, whether conditional generation (e.g., from point clouds or images) is tractable within this framework, and whether the degree-guided diffusion can be extended to handle non-manifold or multi-body B-rep structures.
