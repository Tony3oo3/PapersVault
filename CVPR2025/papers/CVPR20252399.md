## Problem

Existing LVQ-based neural image compression methods suffer from two limitations: they require separate trained models for each rate-distortion trade-off (no multirate mode), and they use a fixed lattice basis that cannot adapt to latent feature distributions from domains different from the training data. This paper is the first to address both rate adaptation and domain adaptation simultaneously through quantizer design alone, without modifying the underlying network architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (256×256 patches during training) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. A single trained network handles multiple target bitrates by adjusting a scalar scaling factor on the lattice basis, and domain adaptation to a new content type (e.g., screen content or cartoons) requires learning only an invertible linear transform comprising roughly 0.06% of total network parameters. The method is architecture-agnostic and can replace the scalar quantizer in any existing autoencoder-based compression model.

## Method

The core idea is to express the adaptive lattice basis as B = AG, where G is a fixed diamond lattice basis with coset representation and A is a learned invertible linear transform. For rate adaptation, A is a diagonal scaling matrix diag(a,a,…,a) that adjusts lattice point density; equivalently, latent features are scaled by 1/a before quantization with the fixed lattice and rescaled by a after dequantization. For domain adaptation, A is parameterized via eigen-decomposition A = VMVᵀ, where V is a rotation matrix obtained from the matrix exponential of a skew-symmetric matrix, ensuring invertibility and differentiability. The key equivalence is that quantizing y with basis B reduces to quantizing A⁻¹y with the fixed basis G, preserving O(1) encoding complexity via the coset representation while avoiding the train/inference mismatch that would arise from freely optimizing B and applying lattice basis reduction at inference. Training uses the straight-through estimator for the non-differentiable arg-min in the quantizer, and the entropy model is a product of convolved Gaussian-uniform distributions whose parameters are predicted by a context model.

## Results

The proposed LVQ-based variable-rate model (LVQ-m) achieves BD-Rate savings of up to −17.39% (Cheng2020, Tecnick) and −15.63% (Cheng2020, CLIC) over the corresponding SQ-based variable-rate baseline, while using a single shared network. Across all three architectures and three natural-image test sets, LVQ-m preserves approximately 80% of the bitrate or PSNR gains of the best-performing LVQ-based fixed-rate models (LVQ-s), reaching up to 90% in some cases. For domain adaptation, adapting the lattice basis yields BD-Rate improvements of −0.99% on SCI1K screen content and −0.47% to −1.59% across PACS cartoon categories, using only ~0.06% of total network parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three learned image compression architectures (Cheng2020, Cheng2020 checkerboard, MBT2018 mean) in three quantization configurations: SQ-based fixed-rate (SQ-s), LVQ-based fixed-rate (LVQ-s), and the proposed LVQ-based variable-rate (LVQ-m). The proposed LVQ-m consistently outperforms or matches SQ-s across all nine architecture–dataset combinations, e.g., on Cheng2020/Tecnick it achieves −17.39% BD-Rate versus −22.60% for SQ-s (where SQ-s actually wins in this one case due to the fixed-rate advantage). Against the strongest baseline LVQ-s, LVQ-m preserves roughly 80% of the bitrate savings in most settings (e.g., −6.82% vs −9.56% on Cheng2020/Kodak; −8.44% vs −12.55% on MBT2018/Tecnick), with the gap narrowing to ~1–2 percentage points. The key practical advantage is that LVQ-m uses a single network for all rate points, whereas LVQ-s and SQ-s each require a separately trained model per λ, substantially reducing memory footprint. For domain adaptation, the adapted lattice basis improves BD-Rate by 0.47–1.59% on PACS cartoon and 0.99% on SCI1K screen content over the unadapted natural-image-trained model, with no changes to the network weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LVQ-based learned image compression line initiated by LVQAC [47], which introduced the diamond lattice with coset representation for O(1) encoding complexity. It departs from all prior LVQ methods (LVQAC, Kudo et al. [27], Lei et al. [29], ERLVQ [11]) by replacing their fixed, predefined lattice basis with an adaptive one: a diagonal scaling for rate control and a learned invertible linear transform for domain adaptation. The underlying neural compression architectures (Cheng2020, checkerboard, MBT2018) are treated as black boxes whose scalar quantizer is swapped for the adaptive LVQ, making the contribution orthogonal to and composable with any existing entropy model or transform design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a single network that operates across multiple bitrates without retraining, reducing memory footprint and deployment complexity for real-world codecs. The domain adaptation result—achieving 0.5–1.6% BD-rate savings by updating only ~0.06% of parameters—is modest in absolute terms but notable for its extreme parameter efficiency and the fact that it requires no architectural changes or risk of catastrophic forgetting. The authors acknowledge the domain adaptation gains are limited and plan to explore combining lattice basis optimization with fine-tuning a few network layers; whether this hybrid approach can close the gap to full retraining remains an open question. The unification of rate and domain adaptation through a single quantizer-design mechanism is conceptually clean and could inspire similar 'adapt the quantizer, not the network' strategies for other structured quantization schemes.
