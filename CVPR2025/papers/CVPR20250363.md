## Problem

Existing UDA-SS methods assume the category overlap between source and target domains is known in advance, which is unrealistic when target labels are unavailable. When source-private classes are present, their feature similarity to common classes reduces pseudo-label confidence, causing common classes to be misassigned as unknown and degrading adaptation performance. This paper introduces the UniDA-SS scenario, where no prior knowledge of category configuration is given, and target-private regions must be classified as 'unknown'.

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled source RGB images (512×512 random crops with pixel-wise annotations) and unlabeled target RGB images of the same resolution. The segmentation head outputs C_s+1 channels per pixel, where the (C_s+1)-th channel corresponds to an 'unknown' class for target-private regions. A teacher network (EMA-updated) generates target pseudo-labels, and pixels whose confidence falls below a threshold τ_p are assigned to the unknown class rather than a known source class.

## Method

UniMAP introduces two components built on a SegFormer MiT-B5 backbone with self-training. Domain-Specific Prototype-based Distinction (DSPD) assigns two non-learnable prototypes per class (one source, one target) in a fixed ETF space, trained via cross-entropy, pixel-prototype contrastive, and pixel-prototype distance losses; a pixel-wise weight w computed from the relative cosine similarities to both prototypes up-weights common-class pixels in the target loss and pseudo-label thresholding. Target-based Image Matching (TIM) computes a softmax-weighted class distribution from target pseudo-labels (prioritizing rare classes) and selects the source image with the highest pixel-level overlap in common classes to pair with each target image in a batch, ensuring domain-invariant learning focuses on shared categories. Training combines supervised source segmentation loss, DACS domain-mixed target pseudo-label loss weighted by w and image-level reliability, and the prototype loss, all optimized with AdamW over 40k iterations.

## Results

On the Pascal-Context → Cityscapes OPDA-SS benchmark, UniMAP achieves a Common mIoU of 60.94, Private IoU of 31.27, and H-Score of 41.33, outperforming the strongest baseline BUS (57.64 / 20.38 / 30.11) by +3.3, +10.89, and +11.22 respectively. On the GTA5 → IDD benchmark, UniMAP gains approximately +6.25 Common, +10.3 Private, and +9.69 H-Score over prior methods, surpassing BUS in Private performance by ~5.08 despite a slight Common deficit. Across all four domain adaptation settings (Closed, Open, Partial, Open Partial), UniMAP achieves the highest Common Average (60.86) and H-Score Average (37.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMAP outperforms all seven baselines on both the Common and H-Score metrics in the Pascal-Context → Cityscapes OPDA-SS setting. The largest gap is against BUS (the state-of-the-art ODA-SS method), where UniMAP improves Private IoU from 20.38 to 31.27 (+10.89) and H-Score from 30.11 to 41.33 (+11.22), while also gaining +3.3 on Common mIoU. CDA-SS methods (DAFormer, HRDA, MIC) suffer severe degradation in the OPDA setting—DAFormer drops to 4.43 Private IoU and HRDA to 8.55—because they lack any mechanism to handle source-private classes. UniDA classification methods (UAN, UniOT, MLNet) perform worst overall (H-Score 7–11), confirming that pixel-level adaptation is critical for segmentation. On GTA5 → IDD, UniMAP trails BUS slightly on Common (due to BUS's stronger sidewalk prediction at 74.34 vs. 62.50) but surpasses it on Private by ~5.08, yielding a superior H-Score. Across all four category settings (Closed, Open, Partial, Open Partial), UniMAP achieves the best average H-Score (37.90) and Common (60.86), demonstrating robustness to unknown category configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) concept, previously studied only in classification (You et al., 2019), to the pixel-level setting of semantic segmentation, where the absence of prior category knowledge is more consequential due to spatially structured confusion between classes. Methodologically, it builds directly on BUS (Choe et al., CVPR 2024) for the open-set self-training backbone and on ProtoSeg (Zhou et al., CVPR 2022) for prototype-based pixel clustering, repurposing the latter's non-learnable prototypes into a domain-specific (source vs. target) pair per class within a fixed ETF geometry. The key departure from prior UDA-SS work is the removal of the assumption that source-target class overlap is known, unifying closed-set, open-set, and partial-set adaptation into a single OPDA-SS formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the UniDA-SS/OPDA-SS task and two new benchmarks (Pascal-Context→Cityscapes, GTA5→IDD), the paper fills a practical gap: real-world deployment rarely provides the category configuration that existing UDA-SS methods require. The DSPD and TIM components offer reusable techniques—domain-specific prototypes for confidence calibration and target-guided source sampling—that could transfer to other open-set or partial adaptation settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more extreme category shifts (e.g., very few common classes), whether the fixed ETF structure is optimal compared to learned prototype geometries, and how the method performs when the target domain contains many more private classes than the two benchmarks test.
