## Problem

State-of-the-art unsupervised interactive segmentation methods rely on self-supervised backbones to generate pseudo-labels, which are then used to train a prompt-based segmentation model. This paper asks whether competitive point-prompt interactive segmentation can be achieved entirely training-free, using only the self-attention maps of a pre-trained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2) without any additional training or pseudo-label generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251448/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of foreground/background point prompts as input. The image undergoes a single denoising step through SD2's U-Net with no added noise and an empty text prompt, extracting 128×128 self-attention tensors from the highest-resolution transformer blocks. The output is a full-resolution binary segmentation mask, recomputed after each new click using all accumulated prompts. At 854×480 resolution on an RTX 4090, the system achieves ~0.6 seconds per click with cached attention tensors, enabling near real-time interaction.

## Method

M2N2 interprets the aggregated multi-head self-attention tensor of Stable Diffusion 2 as a right-stochastic Markov transition matrix. After temperature scaling and iterative proportional fitting (IPF) to make the matrix doubly stochastic, a Markov chain is run from each prompt point's one-hot state; the number of iterations required for each pixel to reach a relative probability threshold τ yields a Markov-map that is less noisy, has sharper semantic boundaries, and more uniform intra-region values than raw attention. A modified flood fill suppresses local minima to enable instance-level separation. Finally, a truncated nearest-neighbor classifier assigns each query pixel to its nearest prompt point using the Markov-map value divided by an adaptively chosen threshold λ_i (determined by a product of prior, edge-gradient, positive-consistency, and negative-consistency score functions), with any distance exceeding 1 defaulting to background.

## Results

M2N2 with SD2 backbone achieves NoC85 of 1.62 (GrabCut), 2.45 (Berkeley), 7.72 (SBD), and 4.60 (DAVIS), with corresponding NoC90 values of 1.90, 3.88, 10.94, and 6.72. Among unsupervised methods, M2N2 is the only training-free approach and outperforms the best training-based unsupervised method (MIS) on three of four datasets, with the largest margin on DAVIS (NoC85: 4.60 vs 6.33, a reduction of 1.73 clicks). The method runs at ~0.6 seconds per click on an RTX 4090 at 854×480 resolution, enabling near real-time interaction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unsupervised training-based method MIS, M2N2 (SD2) wins on GrabCut (NoC85: 1.62 vs 1.94), Berkeley (2.45 vs 3.09), and DAVIS (4.60 vs 6.33), but loses on SBD (7.72 vs 6.91), which the authors attribute to SBD training data overlap with other deep-learning baselines. M2N2 substantially outperforms all classical training-free methods (GraphCut, Random Walk, Geodesic Matting, GSC, ESC) by 3–13 clicks on NoC85 across datasets. Compared to supervised methods, M2N2 remains competitive on GrabCut (NoC85: 1.62 vs SimpleClick ViT-B's 1.40) but trails on DAVIS (4.60 vs SimpleClick ViT-B's 4.10) and SBD (7.72 vs SimpleClick ViT-H's 2.51), reflecting the inherent advantage of pixel-level supervision. Among the paper's own ablation baselines, the progression from Attention-NN (NoC85=9.66 on DAVIS) → KL-NN (7.60) → M2N2 w/o flood fill (5.50) → full M2N2 (4.60) demonstrates the incremental contribution of Markov-maps and flood fill. On medical datasets (BraTS, OAI), M2N2 shows a domain bias, achieving 70% and 66% IoU at 10 clicks versus SimpleClick's 87% and 76%, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Stable Diffusion 2's self-attention maps—previously explored for unsupervised segmentation by DiffSeg [45]—through a fundamentally different lens: interpreting the aggregated attention tensor as a Markov transition operator and extracting per-pixel convergence times (Markov-maps) as semantic distance signals. It departs from the dominant unsupervised interactive segmentation paradigm (pseudo-label generation followed by training, as in MIS [21] or FreeSOLO [48]) by eliminating training entirely, and from classical Markov-chain segmentation methods (Random Walk [8], GraphCut [38]) by sourcing the transition matrix from a learned diffusion model rather than hand-crafted pixel affinities. The truncated nearest neighbor with adaptive thresholding and the modified flood fill for instance awareness are lightweight algorithmic additions that connect these two threads into a practical interactive framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a completely training-free method outperforms pseudo-label-trained unsupervised baselines (MIS) on three of four datasets challenges the working assumption that some form of task-specific training is necessary for competitive interactive segmentation, suggesting that pre-trained diffusion models encode richer spatial-semantic structure than previously exploited. However, the method exhibits clear domain bias (significant degradation on medical imaging), struggles with fine structures due to limited attention resolution, and still lags substantially behind supervised methods like SimpleClick. Open questions include whether the Markov-map formulation can be adapted to higher-resolution attention or other foundation models, and whether the domain gap can be closed without any fine-tuning.
