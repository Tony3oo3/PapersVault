## Problem

Existing PTM-based continual learning methods either restrict adaptation to a fixed pool of shared adapters/prompts (limiting plasticity) or add task-specific modules that grow linearly with the number of tasks (impairing knowledge reuse). There is no mechanism to automatically determine when and where in the network to expand capacity based on actual distribution shifts, leaving the stability-plasticity trade-off poorly controlled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250063/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224 for ViT-B/16) in a class-incremental streaming setting where only the current task's training data is available and no rehearsal buffer is used. The frozen ViT backbone processes each image, and the model outputs a class label over the growing label space. Adapters are inserted as side branches of the MLP in the last few transformer layers (default: last 3), so the input representation at each of those layers is augmented by a weighted mixture of learned adapter outputs before being passed to the classification head.

## Method

SEMA introduces modular adapters, each pairing a lightweight functional adapter (bottleneck with down-projection, ReLU, up-projection) with a representation descriptor (autoencoder) trained to model the local feature distribution. During training on a new task, the RDs compute z-scores of reconstruction errors; expansion is triggered at a layer only if all existing RDs flag the input as novel, at which point one new modular adapter is added and trained while all previously learned adapters remain frozen. An expandable weighting router (linear map + softmax) is jointly learned to produce a soft weighted mixture of all adapters at each layer, with only the newly added column of the router being trained to limit forgetting. This on-demand, layer-wise expansion yields sub-linear parameter growth and enables effective reuse of adapters across tasks with similar feature distributions.

## Results

SEMA achieves the best final accuracy (A_N) across all six benchmarks: 86.98 on CIFAR-100, 79.78 on 5-Task IN-R, 78.00 on 10-Task IN-R, 74.53 on 20-Task IN-R, 53.32 on ImageNet-A, and 89.64 on VTAB. The average incremental accuracy (Ā) is also top or near-top on every dataset, with the largest margins on ImageNet-A (64.53, +4.38 over the next best ADAM) and VTAB (91.26, +2.36 over InfLoRA). On CIFAR-100, CODA-P slightly leads on Ā (91.55 vs. 91.37), but SEMA still wins on A_N (86.98 vs. 86.11). The method operates without memory rehearsal and uses only sub-linear parameter growth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEMA outperforms all six baselines on A_N (final accuracy) across every benchmark, with the most substantial gains on ImageNet-A (+4.08 over ADAM, +7.11 over InfLoRA) and VTAB (+2.01 over InfLoRA, +8.06 over CODA-P). Among prompt-based methods, CODA-P is the strongest competitor on CIFAR-100 (Ā 91.55 vs. SEMA's 91.37) but SEMA still leads on A_N (86.98 vs. 86.11); on the more challenging ImageNet-A, CODA-P drops sharply to 35.02 A_N while SEMA reaches 53.32. Among adapter/LoRA-based methods, InfLoRA is the closest rival on VTAB (87.63 vs. 89.64 A_N) and 10-Task IN-R (75.32 vs. 78.00 A_N), but SEMA consistently leads by 2–4 points on A_N. The gap widens with increasing task count and distribution shift: on 20-Task IN-R, SEMA leads ADAM by 5.43 points on A_N, and on ImageNet-A (natural adversarial examples) it leads the runner-up by over 4 points, confirming the advantage of the self-expansion mechanism under larger distribution shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEMA sits at the intersection of PTM-based continual learning (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P, InfLoRA) and modular/mixture-of-experts architectures (LoRAMoE, modular networks). It departs from the two dominant paradigms in PTM-based CL: fixed-size prompt/adapter pools shared across all tasks (L2P, DualPrompt) and per-task linear expansion (InfLoRA, Expandable Subspace Ensemble). The key conceptual departure is the on-demand self-expansion mechanism, where autoencoder-based representation descriptors act as local novelty detectors to decide *when* and *where* to add new adapters, rather than expanding unconditionally per task. The expandable weighting router for soft mixture composition draws directly on the LoRAMoE routing design [16], while the RD-based expansion signal borrows from anomaly detection via reconstruction-error z-scores [20].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEMA offers a practical, rehearsal-free solution to the stability-plasticity tradeoff in PTM-based CL with sub-linear parameter growth, making it attractive for deployment scenarios where model size must remain bounded as tasks accumulate. The finding that the method is robust to the expansion threshold (thanks to z-score normalization) lowers the practical barrier to adoption. However, the task-oriented restriction (at most one adapter per layer per task) limits applicability to intra-task diversity and fully online settings, and the authors acknowledge that elevating RD optimization to a meta-level with a closed loop could further improve expansion decisions. Open questions include whether the self-expansion mechanism generalizes beyond CIL to domain-incremental or task-incremental settings, and whether the autoencoder-based RDs can be replaced by lighter-weight or learned novelty detectors without sacrificing reliability.
