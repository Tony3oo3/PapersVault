## Problem

Existing controllable video generation methods face a fundamental dilemma: training-based approaches require large-scale data and compute yet can only control trajectories (not spatial layouts), while training-free methods that manipulate attention maps achieve simultaneous layout and trajectory control but suffer from inaccurate instance positioning, unnatural motion, and low video quality. No prior method simultaneously achieves high-quality video generation with precise control over both instance layouts and movement trajectories in a zero-shot manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatial layout signals (bounding boxes or instance masks) paired with movement trajectory segments, a text prompt, and optionally a reference video for motion transfer. Layout signals are augmented along trajectory endpoints to form a sequence of per-frame layout conditions, which are fed into a grounded T2I model to produce keyframe candidates. The output is a temporally consistent video where instances follow the specified spatial path with natural motion, supporting subpart-level control (e.g., head turning) and mask-specified instance shapes beyond simple bounding boxes.

## Method

IM-Zero is a two-stage zero-shot pipeline that decouples spatial location control from motion control. In the Motion Generation Stage, a grounded T2I model (InstanceDiffusion) is inflated with cross-frame attention to generate texture-consistent keyframe candidates at user-specified locations; Canny edges extracted from these candidates drive ControlNet (SparseCtrl) for motion guidance, while an IP-Adapter and additional CFA provide textural guidance to generate a coarse motion video. In the Video Refinement Stage, the coarse video is encoded into latents and progressively denoised; at each step, motion injection denoises M additional steps with a T2V model (AnimateDiff) and fuses the result to improve temporal smoothness, followed by detail injection that transforms latents into T2I noise space via DDIM inversion, denoises D steps with an inflated T2I model (Stable Diffusion V1.5), transforms back, and fuses to enrich visual details. This architecture avoids the attention-map manipulation bottleneck by leveraging instance-level priors from grounded T2I models for positioning and motion/detail priors from pre-trained T2V and T2I models for quality.

## Results

On DAVIS-17, IM-Zero achieves FVD 4070.01, KID 25.75, CLIPSim 98.91, mIoU 0.27, and CD 0.26, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On GOT10k, it reaches FVD 4187.27, KID 15.92, CLIPSim 99.04, mIoU 0.21, and CD 0.27. The most notable gains are in control accuracy: mIoU improves by 28.6% over the best prior method (TrailBlazer, 0.21) on DAVIS-17 and by 61.5% on GOT10k (0.21 vs. 0.13), while CD is reduced by 10.4% and 15.6% respectively. For motion transfer, IM-Zero achieves the best Imaging Quality (0.73) but trails MotionDirector (75.5) and MotionClone (74.5) on Motion Fidelity (63.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IM-Zero outperforms all four main controllable video generation baselines (T2V-Zero, Peekaboo, TrailBlazer, MotionBooth) on every metric across both DAVIS-17 and GOT10k. The largest relative gains are in mIoU (control accuracy): 28.6% over TrailBlazer on DAVIS-17 and 61.5% on GOT10k, confirming that attention-map manipulation methods (Peekaboo, TrailBlazer) suffer from imprecise instance placement. In video quality (FVD, KID), IM-Zero leads by 3–19% over the closest competitor, with the gap most pronounced against T2V-Zero which provides only rough translation-vector guidance. For motion transfer, IM-Zero achieves the best Imaging Quality (0.73) but trails MotionDirector (75.5) and MotionClone (74.5) on Motion Fidelity (63.7), though it substantially outperforms MOFT (51.2). In pure T2V generation, IM-Zero improves over the AnimateDiff baseline on both Imaging Quality (0.74 vs. 0.67) and CLIPSim (99.73 vs. 99.05), demonstrating that the video refinement stage adds value even without layout/trajectory inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

IM-Zero departs from the dominant paradigm of attention map manipulation (Peekaboo, TrailBlazer, FreeTraj) for zero-shot controllable video generation, arguing that such manipulation is a 'compromise solution' that inherently sacrifices location precision and disrupts T2V motion priors. Instead, it decouples spatial placement (delegated to grounded T2I models like InstanceDiffusion) from motion synthesis (delegated to T2V models like AnimateDiff), bridging two previously separate lines of work. The pipeline also repurposes ControlNet/SparseCtrl for Canny-edge-based motion guidance, IP-Adapter for cross-frame textural consistency, and DDIM inversion for noise-space transformation between T2I and T2V models, all in a training-free composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IM-Zero is the first zero-shot method to simultaneously control instance layout and movement trajectories, a capability that training-based methods (DragNUWA, MotionCtrl, etc.) achieve only for trajectory alone at the cost of large-scale data and compute. Its versatility—subpart motion control, mask-based shape specification, motion transfer from reference videos, and high-quality T2V generation—makes it a practical plug-and-play tool. However, the authors acknowledge that zero-shot quality is inherently bounded by the underlying pre-trained models and that temporal consistency remains weaker than training-based approaches, leaving open whether incorporating lightweight tuning could close this gap without sacrificing the zero-shot property.
