## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3DGS methods for urban view synthesis predict per-pixel Gaussians in local frustums, which produces multi-view inconsistencies and ghost artifacts in driving scenes characterized by small parallax angles and texture-less regions. These methods also consume excessive memory due to redundant primitives in overlapping regions, and per-scene optimization approaches (e.g., 3DGS, NeRF) require roughly one hour of training per scene, precluding practical deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of sparse posed RGB images (e.g., 30 stereo frames per KITTI-360 sequence, with a 50% drop rate applied) along with calibrated camera intrinsics and extrinsics. It additionally consumes off-the-shelf metric depth predictions (from Metric3D or UniDepth) as geometric priors. The output is a set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, scale, rotation, opacity, and view-dependent color via degree-1 spherical harmonics) organized in a 128×320×640 voxel grid at 0.1 m resolution, enabling real-time rendering at ~84 FPS with ~10.4 GB GPU memory on a full-resolution 376×1408 image.

## Method

EVolSplat replaces per-pixel Gaussian prediction with a global volume-based approach: monocular depth maps are unprojected and accumulated into a noisy global point cloud, which is voxelized and processed by a sparse U-Net 3D CNN to produce a world-aligned feature volume. Geometry attributes (position offset, opacity, covariance) are decoded from this 3D context via MLP heads, with a recursive position-offset update rule that iteratively refines Gaussian centers toward object surfaces. To recover high-frequency appearance lost through downsampling, an occlusion-aware image-based rendering module projects each Gaussian onto K nearby reference views, samples a local color window, and uses monocular depth to compute per-pixel visibility maps that suppress occluded colors before an aggregation MLP predicts the final spherical-harmonic color. A lightweight hemisphere Gaussian model with fixed geometry and learned color handles the unbounded background (sky and distant scenery), and the two components are composited for full-image rendering.

## Results

On KITTI-360 (50% drop rate, no fine-tuning), EVolSplat achieves 23.26 dB PSNR, 0.797 SSIM, and 0.179 LPIPS, outperforming the best NeRF-based baseline MuRF (22.77 dB) and the best feed-forward 3DGS baseline MVSplat (21.22 dB). On the unseen Waymo Open Dataset, it reaches 23.43 dB PSNR and 0.786 SSIM, again surpassing all baselines. The method renders at 83.81 FPS with only 10.41 GB inference memory, making it the fastest 3DGS-based method while using ~35% less memory than MVSplat (16.14 GB) and ~61% less than PixelSplat (26.75 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVolSplat outperforms all five feed-forward baselines on PSNR and SSIM across both KITTI-360 and Waymo. On KITTI-360, it beats the strongest NeRF baseline MuRF by +0.49 dB PSNR and +0.017 SSIM, and the strongest 3DGS baseline MVSplat by +2.04 dB PSNR. On Waymo (zero-shot), it edges out MuRF by +0.10 dB PSNR and +0.016 SSIM, while MVSplat trails by a full 2.10 dB. On LPIPS, EDUS achieves slightly lower values (0.178 on KITTI, 0.164 on Waymo) than EVolSplat (0.179, 0.202), though EDUS renders at only 0.14 FPS versus EVolSplat's 83.81 FPS. In terms of efficiency, EVolSplat is the fastest 3DGS method (83.81 FPS vs. MVSplat's 81.58) and uses substantially less memory than all other 3DGS baselines (10.41 GB vs. 16.14–26.75 GB), while MuRF and PixelSplat cannot even run at full resolution on a 24 GB consumer GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVolSplat builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its core scene representation but departs fundamentally from the dominant feed-forward 3DGS paradigm (MVSplat, PixelSplat, MVS-Gaussian) that predicts per-pixel aligned Gaussians via 2D CNN/transformer pipelines. Instead, it shifts the prediction to a global 3D volume processed by a sparse 3D CNN, using monocular depth priors (Metric3D, UniDepth) to initialize a noisy point cloud that is then refined in 3D space. The occlusion-aware IBR module for color prediction draws on image-based rendering concepts (IbrNet), while the hemisphere background model addresses the unbounded-scene problem that pixel-aligned methods cannot handle. This volume-based formulation is the key architectural departure, motivated by the observation that driving scenes with small parallax and texture-less regions make per-pixel feature matching unreliable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVolSplat demonstrates that feed-forward urban scene reconstruction can simultaneously achieve real-time rendering (83.81 FPS), low memory (10.41 GB), and zero-shot cross-dataset generalization (KITTI-360 → Waymo), a combination that prior per-scene 3DGS methods (requiring ~1 hour training) and pixel-aligned feed-forward methods (suffering from ghosting artifacts) could not match. The practical implications for autonomous driving simulation and sensor augmentation are significant, as the method removes the per-scene optimization bottleneck. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges that dynamic agents (other vehicles, pedestrians) remain unhandled, and the volume-based approach requires a predefined bounding box that may not generalize to all driving geometries without reconfiguration.
