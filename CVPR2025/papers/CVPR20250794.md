## Problem

Despite strong per-domain fine-tuning results, existing stereo matching networks fail to achieve the zero-shot cross-domain generalization that foundation models have demonstrated in other vision tasks. This gap stems from two limitations: network architectures that cannot effectively gather non-local context to scale with large datasets, and training data that is too small or insufficiently diverse to bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Input / Output

The model takes a left-right RGB stereo image pair and outputs a dense disparity map at full image resolution. During training, images are randomly cropped to 320×736; at inference the model supports a maximum disparity of 416 pixels with 32 GRU refinement iterations. The initial disparity is predicted at 1/4 resolution via soft-argmin on the filtered cost volume, then progressively refined and upsampled to full resolution through convex sampling.

## Method

FoundationStereo combines three key innovations. First, a Side-Tuning Adapter (STA) freezes a DepthAnythingV2 ViT backbone and adapts its 1/4-scale latent features (via a 4×4 stride-4 convolution before the DPT head) to stereo matching by concatenating them with EdgeNeXt-S CNN features, injecting internet-scale monocular geometric priors without corrupting the pretrained weights. Second, an Attentive Hybrid Cost Filtering (AHCF) module filters the 4D cost volume using an hourglass of Axial-Planar Convolutions (decoupling 3D convolutions into spatial Ks×Ks×1 and disparity 1×1×Kd kernels for larger effective receptive fields) in parallel with a Disparity Transformer that performs FlashAttention self-attention over the disparity dimension for long-range global reasoning. Third, a 1M-pair synthetic dataset (FSD) rendered in NVIDIA Omniverse with domain-randomized camera parameters, lighting, and object configurations is iteratively self-curated by pruning ambiguous samples (BP-2 > 60%) and regenerating them. The filtered cost volume initializes a multi-level coarse-to-fine GRU refinement loop that iteratively updates disparity using looked-up volume features and context features from the STA-adapted backbone.

## Results

In the primary zero-shot generalization evaluation (Table 2), FoundationStereo achieves 1.1 BP-2 on Middlebury, 0.5 BP-1 on ETH3D, 2.3 D1 on KITTI-12, and 2.8 D1 on KITTI-15, dramatically outperforming the best prior zero-shot method (Selective-IGEV* at 7.5/3.4/3.2/4.5) by 4–8× on each metric. Even when trained solely on Scene Flow, FoundationStereo (5.5/1.8/3.2/4.9) surpasses all Scene-Flow-only baselines. On the ETH3D leaderboard, the zero-shot model achieves EPE 0.13, matching or exceeding methods that fine-tune on ETH3D training data, while the fine-tuned variant ranks 1st with EPE 0.09.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoundationStereo's zero-shot model dominates all 19 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. On Middlebury, it reduces BP-2 from 7.5 (Selective-IGEV*, the best prior with multi-dataset training) to 1.1, a 6.8× improvement; on ETH3D, BP-1 drops from 3.0 (HVT-RAFT) to 0.5. On KITTI-15, D1 improves from 4.5 (Selective-IGEV*) to 2.8. Notably, even the Scene-Flow-only variant (5.5/1.8/3.2/4.9) outperforms all methods trained exclusively on Scene Flow, demonstrating the architectural contribution independent of data scale. On the in-domain Scene Flow benchmark (Table 3), FoundationStereo achieves EPE 0.34, beating the previous best MoCha-Stereo (0.41) by 17%. On the ETH3D leaderboard (Table 4), the zero-shot model (EPE 0.13) matches or exceeds fine-tuned methods like CREStereo (0.13) and Selective-IGEV (0.12), while the fine-tuned variant (EPE 0.09) ranks 1st, halving the error of the prior best (Selective-IGEV at 0.12).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate threads in stereo matching: (1) the cost-volume-plus-iterative-refinement paradigm from IGEV/RAFT-Stereo, (2) the side-tuning paradigm for adapting pretrained vision foundation models, and (3) the scaling-law-driven foundation model approach that has transformed other CV tasks. The key architectural departure is the Attentive Hybrid Cost Filtering module, which introduces Axial-Planar Convolution (decoupling spatial and disparity convolutions) and a Disparity Transformer that performs self-attention over the disparity dimension of the 4D cost volume—components absent from prior cost-filtering networks. The Side-Tuning Adapter repurposes DepthAnythingV2's frozen ViT features as geometric priors for stereo, a use of monocular depth foundation models that goes beyond the correlation-volume approach of concurrent work [3].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FoundationStereo establishes that stereo matching can achieve foundation-model-level zero-shot generalization, closing the gap with per-domain fine-tuning to near-parity on ETH3D (0.5 vs. 3.0–5.7 BP-1) and Middlebury (1.1 vs. 7.5–14.8 BP-2). The 1M-pair FSD dataset with iterative self-curation is a substantial new training resource that may benefit the broader stereo community. Open questions include efficiency (0.7 s per frame on A100 limits real-time deployment), robustness to fully transparent objects, and whether the approach extends to multi-view or video stereo settings. The finding that a simple 4×4 convolution + concatenation side-tuning design outperforms more elaborate ViT-Adapter-style feature exchange suggests the field may be over-engineering the CNN-ViT interface for dense prediction tasks.
