## Problem

Existing 3D inpainting methods rely on per-view 2D inpainting masks that may include background regions visible from other camera views, causing the inpainted content in those shared regions to be inconsistent across views. This inconsistency propagates into the reconstructed 3D scene, producing artifacts and reducing fidelity. The paper addresses how to automatically refine inpainting masks using depth information and enforce cross-view consistency during 3DGS optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250372/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS model, K multi-view images at 1008×567 resolution with known camera poses, and binary 2D object masks (e.g., from SAM) indicating the object to remove. It renders depth maps from the pre-trained 3DGS to guide mask refinement, then produces an optimized 3DGS model (G′) that renders the scene without the target object from arbitrary novel camera poses. The reference view for 2D inpainting is automatically selected as the one with the largest refined mask to maximize 3D coverage.

## Method

3DGIC operates in two stages. First, Inferring Depth-Guided Inpainting Masks projects background pixels from each other view into the current view using rendered depth maps and camera poses, then removes those visible-background pixels from the original object mask, yielding a refined mask that only covers regions truly invisible from all training views. Second, Inpainting-guided 3DGS Refinement removes object-labeled Gaussians and replaces them with randomly initialized ones, applies 2D inpainting (LAMA or LDM) on the reference view's rendered image and depth, then enforces cross-view consistency by projecting the inpainted reference view as a colored point cloud into all other views and computing LPIPS loss against the rendered results. The total loss combines RGB L1+SSIM and depth L1 rendering losses at the reference view with summed LPIPS cross-view losses, jointly optimizing the new Gaussians for both fidelity and multi-view consistency.

## Results

On the SPIn-NeRF benchmark, 3DGIC with LDM as the 2D inpainter achieves the best scores across all four metrics: FID 36.4, m-FID 96.3, LPIPS 0.26, and m-LPIPS 0.028. Even with the weaker non-diffusion LAMA inpainter, 3DGIC (FID 41.7, m-FID 102.4) outperforms MVIP-NeRF and MALD-NeRF, both of which use the stronger LDM inpainter, indicating the gains come from the 3D inpainting framework rather than the 2D inpainter alone. Qualitative evaluations on 360° scenes (InNeRF360, Mip-NeRF360, LeRF) further confirm superior cross-view consistency and background preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DGIC (LDM) outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against the strongest baseline GScream (also 3DGS + LDM), 3DGIC improves FID by 2.2 (36.4 vs 38.6), m-FID by 5.3 (96.3 vs 101.6), LPIPS by 0.02 (0.26 vs 0.28), and m-LPIPS by 0.005 (0.028 vs 0.033). The gap is largest against NeRF-based methods: SPIn-NeRF and MVIP-NeRF score 49.6 and 50.5 FID respectively, roughly 13–14 points worse than 3DGIC. Notably, 3DGIC with the simpler LAMA inpainter (FID 41.7) still beats MVIP-NeRF (50.5) and MALD-NeRF (44.9), both of which use LDM, demonstrating that the depth-guided mask refinement and cross-view consistency loss contribute independently of the 2D inpainter quality. Gaussian Grouping, the other 3DGS-based method, is outperformed by 3DGIC (LDM) by 8.3 FID and 36.2 m-FID, with the m-FID gap reflecting better inpainting fidelity within the masked region.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as the scene representation and on Gaussian Grouping's technique of removing semantically-labeled Gaussians and replacing them with randomly initialized ones. It departs from the prior 3D inpainting lineage (SPIn-NeRF → SAM-based methods like OR-NeRF and Gaussian Grouping → cross-view consistency methods like MALD-NeRF and GScream) by introducing a deterministic depth-guided mask refinement step: rather than treating each view's inpainting mask independently, it projects visible background pixels across all training views to shrink the mask to only truly occluded regions. This addresses a specific failure mode identified in Gaussian Grouping where per-view masks include areas visible from other views, causing inpainted content to conflict with original scene geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The depth-guided mask refinement is a simple, deterministic, and computationally cheap step that meaningfully reduces the inpainting region and improves cross-view consistency without requiring additional learned components. However, the method still depends on a 2D inpainter (LAMA or LDM) for the reference view and enforces cross-view consistency through geometric projection rather than learned feature alignment, which may limit its applicability to scenes with significant parallax or dynamic content. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond object removal to more complex 3D editing tasks, how it handles scenes with large occlusion changes between views, and whether the heuristic reference-view selection (largest refined mask) is optimal for all scene geometries.
