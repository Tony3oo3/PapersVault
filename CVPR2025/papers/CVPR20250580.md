## Problem

Existing temporal 3D occupancy prediction methods follow a perception-transformation-fusion pipeline that independently encodes each frame and then fuses multi-frame representations, ignoring the strong prior that driving scenes evolve continuously and primarily through dynamic object motion. This design introduces unnecessary computational overhead and fails to exploit the close correlation between adjacent frames, limiting both efficiency and accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250580/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the current frame's 6-camera RGB images (900×1600) and the previous frame's 25,600 3D Gaussians (each carrying position, scale, rotation, semantic probability, and a temporal feature), along with the ego trajectory as an affine transformation matrix. It outputs a 200×200×16 semantic occupancy grid with 18 classes and the refined 3D Gaussians, which are passed directly to the next frame for continuous streaming inference. Notably, only one historical frame's Gaussians are required (not multiple), and the model achieves streaming prediction with nearly identical latency and memory to its single-frame counterpart.

## Method

GaussianWorld reformulates 3D occupancy prediction as 4D occupancy forecasting conditioned on the current sensor input, decomposing scene evolution into three factors: ego-motion alignment of static scenes (via a global affine transform on Gaussian positions), local movements of dynamic objects (updating only positions of Gaussians classified as dynamic), and completion of newly-observed areas (sampling random Gaussians and predicting all their attributes from the current image). A unified refinement block—comprising self-encoding and cross-attention between Gaussians and multi-scale image features—serves both the motion and perception roles, allowing historical and new Gaussians to be processed in parallel with shared parameters across 4 evolution layers plus additional refinement layers. Training proceeds in two stages: single-frame pretraining (replicating GaussianFormer) followed by streaming finetuning with gradually increasing sequence lengths and probabilistic dropout of the previous frame's Gaussians to stabilize early training. This design achieves over 2% mIoU improvement over the single-frame baseline without any additional inference computation.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, GaussianWorld achieves 32.75 IoU and 21.78 mIoU for 3D semantic occupancy prediction, outperforming the single-frame GaussianFormer-B (30.68 IoU, 19.73 mIoU) by +2.07 IoU and +2.05 mIoU. It also surpasses the temporal-fusion variant GaussianFormer-T (31.34 IoU, 20.42 mIoU) by +1.41 IoU and +1.36 mIoU, while using only 1 historical frame versus 3 for fusion-based methods. Notably, GaussianWorld achieves this with nearly identical inference latency (228 ms vs. 225 ms) and memory (7030 MB vs. 6958 MB) to the single-frame model, whereas fusion methods require ~380 ms and ~10 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianWorld outperforms all seven compared baselines on both IoU and mIoU. Against the strongest single-frame baseline SurroundOcc (31.49 IoU, 20.59 mIoU), it gains +1.26 IoU and +1.19 mIoU. Against the temporal-fusion variant GaussianFormer-T (31.34 IoU, 20.42 mIoU), it gains +1.41 IoU and +1.36 mIoU while using only 1 historical frame instead of 3, with ~40% lower latency (228 ms vs. 382 ms) and ~30% less memory (7030 MB vs. 10019 MB). The largest gains appear on dynamic-object classes: car improves from 10.70 (SurroundOcc) to 13.70, and pedestrian from 12.06 to 11.61 (slight drop), while static classes like drive.surf. improve from 24.49 to 24.30 (nearly matched) and other flat from 24.49 to 28.35. The paper also shows that conventional temporal fusion in either 3D Gaussian space or perspective view space (GaussianFormer-T variants) underperforms the world-model approach, confirming that explicit scene-evolution modeling is more effective than feature-level fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GaussianFormer, which introduced explicit 3D Gaussians as a scene representation for single-frame 3D occupancy prediction. It departs from the dominant temporal modeling paradigm (perception → transformation → fusion, as in BEVFormer, CVT-Occ, and GaussianFormer-T) by reframing the problem through a world-model lens: rather than fusing multi-frame features, it predicts the current scene from the previous frame's Gaussians plus the current observation, explicitly decomposing scene evolution into ego-motion alignment, dynamic object movement, and new-area completion. Conceptually, it repurposes the world-model literature (OccWorld, DriveWorld) by conditioning on current sensor input rather than ego actions, and by using explicit Gaussians instead of implicit latent representations to make the evolution dynamics tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that a structured world-model approach using only one historical frame outperforms three-frame temporal fusion methods while matching single-frame inference latency (~228 ms), suggesting that explicit scene-evolution priors are more sample-efficient than brute-force multi-frame aggregation. This is relevant for real-time autonomous driving where compute budgets are tight. The acknowledged limitation—imperfect dynamic/static disentanglement preventing full cross-frame consistency for static elements—points to an open problem in structured scene evolution modeling. The authors note the approach could extend to other perception tasks, but no such experiments are provided.
