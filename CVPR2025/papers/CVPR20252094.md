## Problem

Existing neural implicit surface methods (NeuS, Neuralangelo) assume bounded, high-overlap, object-centric scenes and initialize the SDF as a sphere, making them unsuitable for large, unbounded urban driving sequences with limited image overlap. Driving-specific methods like StreetSurf rely on strong topological assumptions (e.g., flat road-surface initialization) or LiDAR supervision, limiting their adaptability to diverse driving geometries. ViiNeuS addresses this gap by enabling accurate SDF-based reconstruction of complex urban scenes from limited-overlap street views without any geometric prior on scene topology.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB street-view images from a moving ego-vehicle (with dynamic objects masked where applicable) and produces a watertight 3D mesh via Marching Cubes on the learned SDF, which can subsequently be textured using standard MVS tools. Positions are encoded with a 3D hierarchical hash grid (InstantNGP) and viewing directions with spherical harmonics; no LiDAR, depth maps, or explicit geometric priors are required as input. The final mesh represents the static background geometry of the driving scene at a scale spanning both close-range and far-range structures.

## Method

ViiNeuS employs a hybrid architecture with two MLPs: a geometry network F_Θ^h that outputs volumetric density σ, SDF value f(x), and a latent vector h, and a color network F_Θ^c that predicts RGB from h, the viewing direction, and the SDF-gradient normal. The core innovation is a progressive SDF initialization strategy that transitions through three stages—pure volumetric (α computed from σ), hybrid (a mix of density-based α^v and SDF-based α^f), and pure surface (α computed from f(x))—exploiting the fact that density fields converge faster than SDF fields under limited overlap. A self-supervised probabilistic density estimator (based on Mip-NeRF 360 proposal networks) guides targeted sampling near surfaces, while a probability-based attribution rule assigns SDF alpha values to the highest-density samples to constrain the SDF learning range. Additional regularizations include SDF-gradient normalization during the hybrid stage, an eikonal loss, a soft penalty on the NeuS sigmoid parameter s, and monocular normal supervision applied only to the transmittance-saturated (closest) sample rather than the full accumulated normal.

## Results

ViiNeuS achieves a mean Point-to-Mesh (P→M) distance of 0.24 m across four driving datasets (KITTI-360, Pandaset, Waymo, nuScenes), roughly half the 0.47 m mean of StreetSurf, with a mean precision of 0.46 vs. 0.42 for StreetSurf. On KITTI-360 (straight, open roads), ViiNeuS matches StreetSurf at ~0.11–0.14 m P→M, but on more complex scenes (curvy roads, varied elevations) it consistently outperforms StreetSurf by 30–80%. ViiNeuS also outperforms explicit MVS baselines (COLMAP, OpenMVS) on P→M in complex environments, while MVS methods retain an edge in precision on KITTI-360 due to denser point-cloud initialization. Training takes 20 min on a single GPU (vs. 40 min for StreetSurf) with 8 GB memory and 27 MiB parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StreetSurf [11] (the closest implicit SDF baseline), ViiNeuS ties on KITTI-360 (mean P→M ≈ 0.11 m for both) but wins decisively on Pandaset (0.36 m vs. 0.86 m mean P→M), Waymo (0.19 m vs. 0.26 m), and nuScenes (0.31 m vs. 0.65 m), with the largest gap on Pandaset Seq. 23 (0.49 m vs. 2.33 m). Against explicit MVS methods (COLMAP and OpenMVS), ViiNeuS achieves lower P→M on complex scenes (e.g., Pandaset, nuScenes) but trails in precision on KITTI-360 where MVS benefits from high-overlap, high-FOV imagery and dense point-cloud initialization. GOF [46] (Gaussian Opacity Fields) performs poorly on driving scenes: the sparse variant fails on 2 of 4 KITTI-360 sequences and yields P→M > 1.5 m on Waymo and nuScenes, while even the dense variant lags ViiNeuS by 3–10× in P→M on Waymo and nuScenes. Overall, ViiNeuS is the best implicit SDF method across all four datasets and the most robust to scene topology (curvy roads, elevation changes) where StreetSurf's road-surface initialization assumption breaks down.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViiNeuS builds directly on NeuS's SDF-based volume rendering formulation and NeRF's volumetric density representation, combining them into a single hybrid architecture. It departs from StreetSurf (the closest prior work) by eliminating the ego-pose-based road-surface initialization and the close/far-range scene decomposition, instead using a progressive transition from density to SDF guided by self-supervised probabilistic density estimation (borrowed from Mip-NeRF 360's proposal networks). The monocular normal supervision follows MonoSDF and StreetSurf, while the hash-encoding backbone follows Instant-NGP. Unlike Neuralangelo and NeuS, which initialize the SDF as a sphere assuming a centered, bounded scene, ViiNeuS is explicitly designed for unbounded, linear-trajectory driving sequences with limited image overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViiNeuS addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving: accurate 3D surface reconstruction of large urban scenes from RGB alone, without LiDAR supervision or strong geometric priors, at half the training time of the prior SoTA (StreetSurf). The progressive density-to-SDF initialization strategy is a transferable idea that could benefit other limited-overlap reconstruction settings beyond driving. However, the method still fails on very wide and open sequences, and its reliance on monocular normal cues and a fixed 14k-iteration budget limits its applicability to extremely large or highly dynamic scenes. An open question is whether the hybrid two-field architecture can be extended to handle dynamic objects or to scale to city-level reconstructions without per-scene training.
