## Problem

Existing RVOS methods follow a 'fuse then select' paradigm that extracts visual and textual features from independent encoders, creating a semantic gap between language descriptors and video content. Additionally, they perform frame-level query interaction without modeling the global trajectory structure, leading to inconsistent segmentation under occlusion, motion, and similar-object interference in real-world scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250477/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of T frames and a referring text expression of N words (which may contain motion descriptions or multi-target references) and produces a binary segmentation mask of size H×W for each frame. For single-object scenarios, the mask is selected via argmax over classification scores; for multi-instance scenarios, a threshold operation is applied. The model operates on the MeViS, Ref-Youtube-VOS, and Ref-DAVIS17 benchmarks, handling both static and motion-guided expressions.

## Method

SSA introduces two alignment modules built on a frozen ConvNeXt-based CLIP backbone. The Semantic Feature Alignment module inserts a lightweight adapter at each CLIP stage that performs two-granularity cross-attention (word-level and sentence-level textual features against visual features) alongside a residual convolution path, with an MLP gating mechanism to dynamically weight the three streams. The Sequential Object Alignment module first generates per-frame instance queries via bidirectional cross-attention between visual and textual features, then uses Hungarian matching to link queries into trajectories across frames, applies 1D convolution and temporal self-attention for short- and long-range trajectory enhancement, and finally ensembles all trajectories into a global video query via temporal weighted fusion. This video query, together with the enhanced frame queries and language features, feeds a Mask2Former-style decoder that produces classification scores and mask embeddings. The approach reduces trainable parameters by ~46M compared to DsHmp while achieving 2.2% higher J&F on MeViS.

## Results

On the challenging MeViS benchmark, SSA achieves 48.6% J&F on Val and 56.9% J&F on Valu, surpassing the previous best method DsHmp by 2.2% and 1.6% respectively. With joint co-training on MeViS and Ref-Youtube-VOS, SSA further improves to 48.9% J&F (Val) and 57.8% J&F (Valu). On Ref-Youtube-VOS, SSA reaches 64.3% J&F (+0.7% over DsHmp), and on Ref-DAVIS17 it achieves 67.3% J&F (+3.3% over DsHmp). Notably, SSA outperforms large VLM-based methods (VideoLISA-3.8B, VISA-7B) by 4.5–5.4% J&F on MeViS while using approximately 46M fewer trainable parameters than DsHmp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSA outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against the strongest expert method DsHmp [13], SSA gains +2.2% J&F on MeViS Val, +1.6% on Valu, +0.7% on Ref-Youtube-VOS, and +3.3% on Ref-DAVIS17. Against the other 'fuse then select' method LMPM [7], SSA improves by a substantial 11.4% J&F on MeViS Val (48.6 vs. 37.2). Against large VLM-based approaches, SSA surpasses VideoLISA-3.8B [1] by 4.5% J&F and VISA-7B [55] by 5.4% J&F on MeViS Val, despite using far fewer parameters. Against SAM2-based pipelines, SSA leads AL-Ref-SAM2 [15] by 5.8% J&F and Grounded-SAM2 [44] by 9.7% J&F on MeViS Val. On Ref-Youtube-VOS and Ref-DAVIS17, SSA also beats the next-best methods (LoSh [60] at 63.7% and DsHmp [13] at 63.6%/64.0%) by 0.6–3.3% J&F, confirming consistent gains across both single-target and multi-target settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'fuse then select' paradigm in RVOS (exemplified by LMPM and DsHmp), which relies on independent Swin/RoBERTa encoders trained from scratch to model visual-text relationships. Instead, SSA repurposes a frozen CLIP VLM as the feature backbone, inserting a lightweight multi-granularity cross-attention adapter to refine semantic alignment without full fine-tuning. The sequential alignment module, which uses Hungarian matching to build instance trajectories and then ensembles them into a video-level query, is a novel combination of tracking-style association and temporal attention that prior RVOS methods (which either segment per-frame or initialize video queries solely from text) do not employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a frozen VLM with a small adapter can outperform both expert-designed RVOS pipelines and large LVLM-based approaches (VideoLISA-3.8B, VISA-7B) while using ~46M fewer trainable parameters, the paper offers a practical and efficient recipe for RVOS that practitioners can adopt without massive compute budgets. The trajectory-to-instance alignment addresses a real failure mode (temporal inconsistency under occlusion and similar-object interference) that is particularly relevant for the motion-heavy MeViS benchmark. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to very long videos where Hungarian matching cost becomes prohibitive, and whether the frozen-CLIP assumption remains valid for domain-specific or low-resource video settings.
