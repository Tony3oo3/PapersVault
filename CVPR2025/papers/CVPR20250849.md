## Problem

MLLMs lack reliable pixel-level understanding, and existing evaluation tasks (VQA, visual grounding) are too coarse to measure fine-grained pixel comprehension. Current MLLM-based segmentation methods rely on implicit tokens decoded by external pixel decoders, which disrupt the model's text output space, limit extensibility, and obscure the model's intrinsic pixel understanding. The paper asks whether MLLMs can perform segmentation by imitating human annotators who use interactive segmentation tools with iterative positive/negative clicks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250849/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step, the MLLM receives a composite image (the original RGB image with the current mask overlaid as a semi-transparent green layer) and a text prompt containing task instructions and the target object description. The model outputs a single text-formatted action such as 'Positive point: (175,483)' with coordinates normalized to [0,1]², which is parsed and fed to an interactive segmentation model (SAM or SimpleClick) to produce the next mask. The process repeats for up to T steps (7 for RES, 11 for HRES), and the final mask is the one with the highest predicted IoU.

## Method

SegAgent models segmentation as a multi-step Markov Decision Process (HLMAT) where the MLLM policy π_θ(a_t | M_t, I, P) outputs text-based click coordinates at each step, requiring no architectural changes or implicit tokens. An automated simulation function F_sim generates human-like annotation trajectories from existing segmentation datasets by iteratively placing clicks at the center of false-positive/false-negative regions between the current mask and ground truth, with noise-mitigation steps (trajectory length caps, reward-threshold termination, low-impact action discarding). The MLLM (LLaVA-v1.5-7B or Qwen-VL-7B) is fine-tuned on these trajectories via instruction tuning. For policy improvement, the authors adapt StaR into StaR+, which computes per-action reward changes and replaces reward-decreasing actions with F_sim corrections rather than filtering entire trajectories. At inference, a process reward model (PRM) predicts per-step mIoU to provide a stop signal and guide a heuristic greedy tree search over K candidate actions per step, improving robustness on complex scenes.

## Results

On the RES benchmark (Table 1), SegAgent-Qwen+SClick achieves the best overall performance with cumulative IoU of 79.69/81.35/76.57 on refCOCO val/testA/testB, 72.49/75.80/66.89 on refCOCO+, and 75.11/75.20 on refCOCOg val/test, surpassing all LLM-based baselines including SAM4MLLM (77.1/80.9/72.5 on refCOCO) and GSVA (77.2/78.9/73.5). On the more complex HRES dataset, StaR+ policy improvement boosts ThinObject5K IoU from 71.45 (SFT baseline) to 86.57, and adding PRM-guided tree search (K=3) yields a further +10.59 IoU gain on ThinObject5K over PRM alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegAgent outperforms all LLM-based baselines across every RES subset. On refCOCO val, SegAgent-LLaVA+SAM (79.20) beats SAM4MLLM (77.1), GSVA (77.2), and LISA (74.9) by 2.1, 2.0, and 4.3 IoU points respectively. On refCOCO+ val, SegAgent-Qwen+SClick (72.49) leads SAM4MLLM (71.5) and PolyFormer-L (72.15), while on refCOCOg test(U), SegAgent-Qwen+SClick (75.20) surpasses SAM4MLLM (75.2) by a narrow margin and GSVA (73.3) by 1.9 points. Against traditional methods, SegAgent exceeds the best traditional baseline PolyFormer-L by 2.75 IoU on refCOCO val and 1.34 on refCOCO+ val. The paper also notes that LLaVA outperforms Qwen in mask refinement (with SAM), attributed to Q-former's loss of spatial locality, while Qwen dominates when using SimpleClick due to its stronger initial grounding capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MLLM-segmentation paradigm (LISA, PixelLM, GSVA, SAM4MLLM) that relies on implicit tokens decoded by external pixel decoders, arguing that such designs disrupt the MLLM's native text output space and obscure intrinsic pixel understanding. Instead, it repurposes interactive segmentation tools (SAM, SimpleClick) as the environment in a multi-step Markov Decision Process, where the MLLM outputs only text-based click coordinates—mimicking how human annotators iteratively refine masks. For policy improvement, it adapts StaR (Zelikman et al.) into StaR+ by computing per-action reward deltas rather than filtering whole trajectories, and borrows the Process Reward Model concept (Lightman et al.) to guide a heuristic greedy tree search at inference time, extending these text-domain RL techniques into a vision-centered setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HLMAT offers a principled, architecture-agnostic protocol for probing fine-grained pixel understanding in MLLMs, filling a gap left by coarse VQA and grounding benchmarks. The HRES dataset (DIS5K, ThinObject5K) addresses the well-known quality and complexity limitations of RefCOCO, enabling meaningful multi-step evaluation. However, the approach still delegates actual mask generation to an external interactive segmentation model, so the MLLM's pixel understanding is only partially exercised; a fully self-contained MLLM that both decides clicks and produces masks remains open. The observation that LLaVA (linear projector) outperforms Qwen-VL (Q-former) in mask refinement, despite Qwen's broader VQA superiority, raises important questions about whether Q-former-style token compression inherently sacrifices spatial fidelity.
