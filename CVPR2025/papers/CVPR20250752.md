## Problem

Existing spatiotemporal predictive models rely on task-specific architectures (e.g., ConvLSTM for precipitation, EarthFormer for earth systems, TAU for general video), making them unable to generalize across disciplines and suffering significant performance degradation when jointly trained on more than 3 tasks. The paper asks whether a single, standard Transformer-based framework can unify 10 spatiotemporal tasks across 4 disciplines (traffic, trajectory/robotics, driving, weather) without task-specific architectural design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250752/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a historical frame sequence of shape (B×T_i) × C_i × H_i × W_i, where channel count, spatial resolution, and temporal length vary per task (e.g., 13 input frames at 384×384 for SEVIR weather radar vs. 2 frames at 64×64 for BAIR robot planning). It outputs the next T'_i predicted frames at the same spatial resolution. A 2D convolutional encoder downsamples spatial dimensions before the Transformer, and a transposed-convolutional decoder upsamples back, so the Transformer operates on a fixed token count N=H'×W' with feature dimension L=T_i×C' per token regardless of the original task geometry.

## Method

UniSTD uses a standard ViT-base backbone (12 layers, 768-dim, 12 heads) initialized from CLIP pretrained weights, followed by joint multi-task fine-tuning with parameter-efficient LoRA adapters. The key architectural innovation is a rank-adaptive Mixture-of-Experts mechanism: each attention projection (Q, K, V, Proj) hosts 6 low-rank experts whose ranks are dynamically optimized via a continuous relaxation—fractional interpolation between floor and ceiling rank solutions using a modified identity matrix—making the discrete rank selection differentiable and enabling per-task rank allocation without combinatorial search. An L1 penalty on the summed expert ranks controls total trainable parameter budget. A lightweight temporal attention module (average-pooled sequence → FFN with zero-initialized second MLP + 1D rank-adaptive MoE → sigmoid gating) is inserted after self-attention to explicitly model temporal dynamics without modifying the pretrained FFN layers. Sinusoidal position encoding replaces learnable embeddings to handle variable spatial token counts across tasks.

## Results

UniSTD, trained jointly on all 10 tasks across 4 disciplines, achieves 39.6 PSNR / 0.98 SSIM on TaxiBJ, 27.4 PSNR / 0.89 SSIM on Cityscapes, 33.2 PSNR / 0.98 SSIM on Human3.1M, and 0.41 CSI on SEVIR, consistently outperforming baselines trained on only 3–5 tasks. On KITTI it reaches 17.2 PSNR (vs. 10.9 for the best Earthformer variant), and on ENSO it attains 0.72 NINO. The headline claim is lossless scaling: while baselines degrade sharply beyond 3 jointly-trained tasks (e.g., SimVPv2 drops from 24.6 to 20.4 PSNR on TaxiBJ when going from 3 to 5 tasks), UniSTD maintains or improves performance at 10 tasks, with gains of up to ~16 PSNR over the weakest multi-task baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniSTD is compared against five families of spatiotemporal predictors (SimVP, SimVPv2, TAU, Earthformer, MCVD), each of which is trained on only 3–5 tasks, whereas UniSTD jointly trains on all 10. On most tasks UniSTD wins decisively: on TaxiBJ it reaches 39.6 PSNR vs. 30.5 for the best TAU 3-task variant (+9.1) and 23.3 for Earthformer (+16.3); on Cityscapes it achieves 27.4 PSNR vs. 19.6 for TAU (+7.8) and 10.9 for Earthformer (+16.5); on SEVIR it scores 0.41 CSI versus ~0.30 for baselines. The one notable exception is BAIR, where TAU 3Tasks (22.4 PSNR) edges out UniSTD (20.3 PSNR) by 2.1 dB, suggesting that the trajectory-prediction task benefits from a dedicated architecture. Against single-task baselines (Table 3), UniSTD still leads on TaxiBJ (39.6 vs. 39.3 TAU, 36.4 MCVD) and Cityscapes (27.4 vs. 26.7 SimVPv2, 19.1 MCVD), confirming that multi-task joint training does not sacrifice per-task quality. Overall, the comparison demonstrates that a single unified Transformer with rank-adaptive MoE can match or exceed task-specific models while simultaneously handling 10 diverse tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm by introducing a rank-adaptive Mixture-of-Experts mechanism that dynamically adjusts adapter ranks per task, departing from the fixed-rank assumption in standard LoRA. Conceptually, it transplants the two-stage pretraining-then-adoption paradigm from LLMs and VLMs into spatiotemporal prediction, using a standard ViT backbone initialized with task-agnostic CLIP/ImageNet weights rather than the task-specific architectures (ConvLSTM, Inception-UNet, cuboid attention) that dominate the spatiotemporal literature (TAU, SimVP, Earthformer). The lightweight temporal attention module with zero-initialization is positioned as a more stable alternative to prior temporal modules (SE, TAU) that use random initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single Transformer can handle 10 spatiotemporal tasks across 4 disciplines without the performance collapse seen in existing methods at just 3 tasks is practically significant for multi-domain deployment. However, the comparison is somewhat asymmetric (UniSTD at 10 tasks vs. baselines at 3 tasks), and single-task comparisons show only modest gains over the best dedicated architectures (e.g., 39.6 vs. 39.3 PSNR on TaxiBJ). Open questions include whether the rank-adaptive MoE scales beyond 10 tasks, whether the approach extends to generative spatiotemporal tasks, and whether the 10-epoch rank optimization window is sufficient for more heterogeneous task collections.
