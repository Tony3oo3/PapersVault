## Problem

Self-supervised pre-training for 3D medical image segmentation has been hampered by three recurring pitfalls: pre-training on small datasets (<10K volumes), reliance on non-state-of-the-art transformer backbones, and insufficient evaluation against strong from-scratch baselines. This paper asks whether a properly configured Masked Auto Encoder on a state-of-the-art CNN backbone, trained on a sufficiently large dataset and rigorously evaluated, can consistently outperform the dynamically configured nnU-Net baseline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D brain MRI volumes (T1, T2, T1 FLAIR, T2 FLAIR) resampled to 1×1×1 mm isotropic spacing and z-score normalized, processed as 160×160×160 patches. During pre-training, 60–90% of the input is randomly masked at the CNN bottleneck and the network reconstructs only the occluded regions via L2 loss in voxel space. During fine-tuning, the model produces per-voxel segmentation masks for diverse brain structures (lesions, organs, vessels, aneurysms) across 13 downstream datasets.

## Method

Spark3D (S3D) pre-trains a Residual Encoder U-Net (ResEnc U-Net) using a masked autoencoder objective with three sparsification adaptations: sparse convolutions with masked-aware batch normalization, learnable mask tokens to densify encoder features before the decoder, and a 3×3×3 densification convolution at each resolution level. Masking is applied dynamically at a 60–90% ratio by sampling random regions in the 5×5×5 bottleneck and up-sampling to input resolution. Pre-training runs for 250k steps on 39,168 brain MRI volumes from 44+ centers. Fine-tuning transfers both encoder and decoder weights, applies a 12.5k-step warm-up to each, reduces the peak learning rate to 1e-3, and unfreezes the encoder throughout, yielding a consistent ~3 DSC improvement over the nnU-Net baseline across 11 test datasets.

## Results

Across 11 diverse brain MRI segmentation datasets, S3D achieves an average DSC of 72.37 and average NSD of 85.58, outperforming the dynamic nnU-Net from-scratch baseline (No Dyn.) by approximately +3 DSC points (72.37 vs. 69.40) and the fixed-configuration from-scratch baseline (No Fix.) by +2 DSC points (72.37 vs. 70.40). S3D also surpasses the strongest SSL baseline, Models Genesis (MG), by +0.54 DSC points (72.37 vs. 71.83) and +0.63 NSD points (85.58 vs. 84.95). In the low-data regime with only 40 labeled images, the S3D fine-tuned model nearly matches the from-scratch model trained on the full dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S3D wins against all four baselines on average DSC: it beats the dynamic nnU-Net from-scratch baseline (No Dyn., 69.40) by +2.97 DSC, the fixed-configuration from-scratch baseline (No Fix., 70.40) by +1.97 DSC, Volume Fusion/VF (70.94) by +1.43 DSC, and Models Genesis/MG (71.83) by +0.54 DSC. On NSD, S3D (85.58) similarly leads all baselines, with the largest margin over VoCo (82.39, +3.19 NSD). S3D achieves the best average rank (2.00) across all 11 datasets, compared to MG (3.18), VF (4.36), No Fix. (4.55), No Dyn. (4.64), and VoCo (6.27). Notably, S3D wins on 10 out of 11 datasets versus the No Fix. baseline, with the sole exception being Brain Mets (D2) where MG edges ahead (65.39 vs. 65.24). The paper attributes MG's competitive performance to its use of intensity augmentations during pre-training, which aids generalization across MRI sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) paradigm for 3D medical image segmentation, building directly on the original MAE formulation (He et al., CVPR 2022) and its CNN adaptations via FCMAE (Woo et al., CVPR 2023) and sparse/hierarchical masked modeling (Tian et al., 2023). It departs from prior 3D medical SSL work (Swin UNETR, 3DMAE, GL-MAE, VoCo, VolumeFusion) by arguing that those methods suffered from three systemic pitfalls: insufficient pre-training data, reliance on transformer backbones that underperform CNNs in 3D medical segmentation, and inadequate evaluation protocols. The key conceptual move is to pair the 'simple' MAE objective with the state-of-the-art ResEnc U-Net CNN backbone from nnU-Net Revisited, rather than introducing a novel architecture or SSL objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that SSL pre-training can consistently outperform the dynamically-configured nnU-Net baseline (~3 DSC points across 11 diverse brain MRI datasets), a result that challenges the field's default of training from scratch. The finding that the 2019 Models Genesis method outperforms 2023–2024 methods (VoCo, VF) when given the same CNN backbone suggests the MAE paradigm is fundamentally stronger than contrastive or pseudo-segmentation objectives for 3D CNNs, redirecting attention from novel SSL objectives to proper configuration. Limitations include restriction to head/neck MRI, reliance on a proprietary 39k-volume pre-training dataset, and unexplored scaling questions (whole-body, multi-modality, larger models). Open questions include whether intensity-augmentation-based objectives (as in Models Genesis) can be integrated into MAEs to improve cross-modality generalization, and whether data curation strategies can further enhance pre-training quality.
