## Problem

Cross-modal image matching (e.g., RGB-Infrared, RGB-Depth, RGB-Event) suffers from a severe data gap: existing multimodal datasets contain only 2.5K–408K pairs with limited scene coverage and imbalanced modality types, making it impossible to train a single unified model that generalizes across multiple cross-modal cases. Prior approaches either train modality-specific models on tiny datasets or augment aligned pairs with synthetic homographies that do not reflect real viewpoint changes, resulting in poor zero-shot generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250692/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of images from any two modalities (RGB, Infrared, Depth, Event, Normal, Sketch, or Paint) resized to 640px long side, and produces pixel-wise correspondences in the form of sparse keypoint matches, semi-dense match maps, or dense match maps depending on the backbone. A key property is that all training data is entirely synthetic—generated from MegaDepth RGB pairs—yet the model is evaluated on real cross-modal pairs (METU-VisTIR, DIODE, DSEC, MMIM) where ground-truth labels are camera poses or homography matrices recovered via RANSAC from the predicted matches.

## Method

The core contribution is a data engine that generates six pseudo modalities from 40M MegaDepth RGB pairs using generative models: StyleBooth (fine-tuned with LoRA on LLVIP/M3FD) for Infrared, DepthAnything V2 for depth, a log-photocurrent simulation for event cameras, DSINE for surface normals, and Paint Transformer/Anime2Sketch for artistic styles, yielding 480M cross-modal pairs (MD-syn) with inherited depth and pose labels. Matching models (LightGlue, LoFTR, RoMa) are first pre-trained on RGB data, then fine-tuned on randomly selected modality pairs from MD-syn at a small learning rate, producing three unified models (MINIMA_LG, MINIMA_LoFTR, MINIMA_RoMa). The key insight is that scaling up from cheap, accurately-labeled RGB data via generative models provides the scene diversity and label precision that real multimodal capture cannot, enabling a single model to handle 19 cross-modal cases including zero-shot transfer to unseen modalities like medical and remote-sensing pairs.

## Results

On the real RGB-IR dataset METU-VisTIR, MINIMA_LG achieves AUC@10° of 37.17%, a >400% improvement over LightGlue (5.37%) and surpassing the SOTA semi-dense method XoFTR (34.64%). MINIMA_RoMa reaches AUC@10° of 60.70%, outperforming RoMa (48.12%) by 12.6 points. On real RGB-Depth (DIODE), MINIMA_RoMa achieves AUC@5px of 50.88% vs. RoMa's 24.64%, and MINIMA_LG reaches 26.80% vs. LightGlue's 3.30%. Zero-shot on unseen modalities (Medical, Remote Sensing, RGB-Event), MINIMA_RoMa leads with AUC@10px of 57.55% (Medical), 64.38% (Remote Sensing), and 17.07% (RGB-Event).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINIMA dominates all sparse matching baselines on METU-VisTIR: MINIMA_LG (37.17% @10°) outperforms LightGlue (5.37%) by 6.9×, SuperGlue (9.26%) by 4.0×, ReDFeat (4.57%) by 8.1×, GIM_LG (5.85%) by 6.4×, and OmniGlue (4.13%) by 9.0×, while also beating the handcrafted methods RIFT/SRIT/LNIFT by orders of magnitude. Against the strongest semi-dense baseline XoFTR (34.64% @10°), MINIMA_LG wins (37.17%) but MINIMA_LoFTR (30.84%) falls slightly behind, which the authors attribute to LoFTR being an older architecture without the tailored designs XoFTR incorporates. In dense matching, MINIMA_RoMa (60.70% @10°) surpasses RoMa (48.12%) by 12.6 points and DKM (13.69%) by 4.4×. On DIODE (RGB-Depth), MINIMA_RoMa (50.88% @5px) nearly doubles RoMa (24.64%), and MINIMA_LG (26.80%) approaches XoFTR (27.24%) while far exceeding LightGlue (3.30%). For zero-shot tasks, MINIMA_RoMa leads all baselines on Remote Sensing (64.38% vs. RoMa 57.84% @10px) and RGB-Event (17.07% vs. RoMa 10.71%), while on Medical the gap is smaller (57.55% vs. RoMa 53.75%) due to the dataset being either too easy or too hard for most methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the model-centric tradition of cross-modal matching (ReDFeat, XoFTR) by taking a data-centric approach: rather than designing modality-specific architectures, it uses generative models (StyleBooth for IR, DepthAnything V2 for depth, DSINE for normals) to synthesize a 480M-pair multimodal dataset (MD-syn) from cheap MegaDepth RGB images, then fine-tunes existing matching backbones (LightGlue, LoFTR, RoMa) on randomly sampled modality pairs. The key conceptual shift is that the modality gap is addressed through data diversity and scale rather than through tailored feature extraction or matching rules. This positions the work at the intersection of generative modeling and geometric matching, inheriting the pre-trained matching priors from the RGB-only literature while filling the data gap that has limited prior cross-modal methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MD-syn dataset (480M pairs, 7 modalities, 196 scenes) is orders of magnitude larger than existing multimodal matching datasets (which range from 1.3K to 408K pairs) and provides precise depth/pose labels that were previously only available through expensive manual annotation or camera calibration. The finding that simple fine-tuning on synthetic multimodal data yields zero-shot generalization to entirely unseen domains (medical imaging, remote sensing) suggests that data scaling may be the primary bottleneck in cross-modal matching, not architectural design. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real gap degrades performance for modalities with fundamentally different information content (e.g., SAR vs. optical), whether the approach extends to indoor scenes, and whether the generative models introduce systematic biases that limit robustness in safety-critical applications.
