## Problem

Inferring signed distance functions from sparse point clouds remains difficult because sparse inputs lack the geometric detail needed to learn a continuous implicit field. Existing approaches either require dense supervision or shape priors that fail to generalize to complex real-world geometries, and self-supervised occupancy methods produce coarse approximations that cannot handle extremely sparse or topologically diverse inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252233/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single sparse point cloud as input (as few as 300 points for shape reconstruction, 1024 points for scanned objects, or 100 points/m² for scenes) with no ground-truth SDFs, normals, or surface priors required. It outputs a continuous signed distance field from which a watertight 3D surface is extracted via Deep Marching Tetrahedra. The entire pipeline is self-supervised, using only the input points themselves as supervision through Chamfer Distance losses.

## Method

The method has two coupled components trained end-to-end. First, Bijective Surface Parameterization (BSP) learns a canonical mapping Φ (PointTransformer-based) that encodes each sparse point onto a unit sphere parametric domain, then samples local patches around each point's code; a transformer decoder Ξ maps these densified patches back to 3D, integrating them into a global surface S supervised by Chamfer Distance to the input. Second, Grid Deformation Optimization (GDO) drives a deformable tetrahedral grid toward S using a neural network g that predicts SDFs, but instead of learning raw offsets, it constrains deformation direction to the gradient ∇g(V) to ensure consistent movement, with stride magnitude given by g(V). The total loss combines the parameterization Chamfer loss, a zero-level-set regularization, and a deformation Chamfer loss between the DMT-extracted surface and S, enabling progressive coarse-to-fine SDF learning without any external supervision.

## Results

On ShapeNet with 300 input points, the method achieves a mean CD_L1 of 0.099 (×10), CD_L2 of 0.073 (×100), and NC of 0.909, outperforming the closest competitor NTPS++ (0.137, 0.089, 0.905) by 28%, 18%, and 0.4% respectively. On D-FAUST, it achieves the best 5% CD_L2 of 0.007 (×100) and NC of 0.914. On the real-scanned SRB dataset, it attains CD_L1 of 0.41 (×100) and Hausdorff Distance of 5.66, surpassing SparseOcc (0.49, 6.04) and NTPS (0.73, 7.78).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is NTPS++ [9], which the method beats by 28% on mean CD_L1 (0.099 vs. 0.137) and 18% on CD_L2 (0.073 vs. 0.089) on ShapeNet, while also achieving higher NC (0.909 vs. 0.905). NTPS [8] is the next closest, with the method improving CD_L1 by 30% (0.099 vs. 0.141). Prior-based methods Onsurf [3] and SparseOcc [43] show moderate performance (CD_L1 of 0.214 and 0.187) but struggle with complex geometries under extreme sparsity. NDrop [5] performs worst overall (CD_L1 of 0.392), failing to constrain sample points accurately. On D-FAUST, the method achieves the best 5% CD_L2 (0.007) and NC (0.914), surpassing NTPS (0.012, 0.909) and OnSurf (0.015, 0.908). On real-scanned SRB, it reduces Hausdorff Distance to 5.66 versus 6.04 for SparseOcc and 7.78 for NTPS, while PSR [26] and IMLS [54] are significantly worse (HD of 21.1 and visual-only comparison, respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parametric surface learning line initiated by TPS (Chen et al., CVPR 2023), which learns a single global parametric mapping from sparse points, by introducing a bijective local-to-global parameterization that learns multiple local patches on a canonical sphere and integrates them into a continuous surface. It also adapts the deformable grid framework from Deep Marching Tetrahedra (Shen et al., NeurIPS 2021) to the sparse reconstruction setting, replacing direct offset prediction with a gradient-consistent deformation strategy (GDO) that constrains movement along the SDF normal. The combination of these two components into an end-to-end self-supervised pipeline—without pretrained shape priors or dense supervision—distinguishes it from prior-based methods (Onsurf) and occupancy-learning approaches (NDrop, SparseOcc) that struggle with complex geometries under extreme sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sparse point cloud reconstruction is critical for real-world applications (autonomous driving, robotics, VR) where only limited sensor data is available, and this paper demonstrates that a purely self-supervised, prior-free pipeline can match or exceed methods that rely on large-scale pretrained priors. The bijective local-patch parameterization offers a generalizable mechanism for densifying sparse geometry that could transfer to other reconstruction or upsampling tasks. Open questions include scalability to very large scenes beyond the tested 3DScene/KITTI settings, handling of non-manifold or open surfaces, and whether the end-to-end training cost is prohibitive for real-time deployment.
