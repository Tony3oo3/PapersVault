## Problem

Large-scale 3D scene generation remains underexplored compared to single-object synthesis, and existing scene generation methods lack the localized editability essential for iterative content creation workflows. Users cannot modify specific objects or regions of a generated 3D scene without re-synthesizing the entire output, making practical interactive design infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250764/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text captions (one per scene chunk) as generation input and produces a full 3D scene as a truncated unsigned distance field (UDF) at 4.2 cm voxel resolution, synthesized chunk-by-chunk (2.7 m or 5.4 m cubic chunks) via sliding-window outpainting. For editing, the user specifies two opposite corners of a bounding box in the proxy semantic map (10-channel one-hot grid: free space, wall/floor, 8 object categories), and only the corresponding geometric region is re-synthesized while the rest of the scene remains unchanged.

## Method

SceneFactor factors 3D scene generation into two conditional latent diffusion stages: a text-to-semantic model that produces a coarse 3D semantic box layout, and a semantic-to-geometry model that synthesizes high-fidelity UDF geometry conditioned on that layout. Both stages operate in compressed 3D latent spaces learned via VQ-VAEs (semantic: 8×4×8, geometric: 32×16×32), enabling spatially-structured representations that support localized editing. The geometric diffusion model uses convolutional cross-attention (window size 3) rather than standard linear projections to capture local neighborhood correlations between the semantic condition and the latent grid, which the ablations show is critical for avoiding over-smoothed geometry. Text conditioning is injected via BERT-encoded tokens used as keys/values in cross-attention, and large scenes are assembled by RePaint-style inpainting of overlapping chunks, with the final geometric latent decoded as a single grid to avoid seams.

## Results

On independent scene chunks, SceneFactor achieves the best geometric quality across all metrics: MMD (CD) 0.019, MMD (EMD) 0.140, COV (CD) 0.421, and 1-NNA (CD) 0.738 (closest to the optimal 0.5). For outpainted scene chunks, SceneFactor substantially outperforms SDFusion (MMD CD 0.512 vs. 0.890) and BlockFusion (MMD CD 0.512 vs. 0.986), while NFD and PVD fail to produce coherent multi-chunk scenes. In text-consistency evaluation (neural listener), SceneFactor is preferred over SDFusion (58% vs. 42%) and Text2Room (65% vs. 35%), and its gap to ground truth (16%) is smaller than SDFusion's (34%) and Text2Room's (24%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneFactor outperforms all four geometric baselines on every reported metric for both independent and outpainted scene chunks. Against SDFusion [13], the closest comparable text-conditioned method, SceneFactor reduces MMD (CD) from 0.034 to 0.019 on independent chunks and from 0.890 to 0.512 on scene chunks, while improving COV (CD) from 0.291 to 0.421 and 1-NNA from 0.847 to 0.738. Against BlockFusion [73] (evaluated unconditionally per the authors' protocol), SceneFactor achieves roughly 2.5× lower MMD (CD) on scene chunks (0.512 vs. 0.986) and 2.4× higher COV (CD) (0.410 vs. 0.330). NFD [58] and PVD [86] produce competitive single-chunk geometry but fail to generate coherent multi-chunk scenes (reported as dashes), highlighting the advantage of SceneFactor's chunk-based outpainting strategy. In the text-consistency neural listener evaluation (Table 2), SceneFactor is preferred over SDFusion (58% vs. 42%) and Text2Room [29] (65% vs. 35%), and its 16% gap to ground truth is substantially smaller than SDFusion's 34% gap, indicating better semantic alignment between generated geometry and text prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneFactor builds on the DDPM diffusion framework and VQ-VAE latent compression, adapting the OpenAI LDM architecture to a 3D setting with BERT-based text conditioning. It departs from prior 3D scene generation methods (BlockFusion, XCube, SDFusion) that perform direct, monolithic scene synthesis by introducing a factored two-stage pipeline: a semantic box layout diffusion model followed by a geometry diffusion model conditioned on that layout via spatial cross-attention. The RePaint-style inpainting strategy is repurposed for chunk-based 3D outpainting, and the use of 3D VQ-VAE latent grids (rather than 1D or triplane representations) for both semantics and geometry is a key architectural choice that preserves spatial locality for editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factored semantic-then-geometric formulation provides a practical editing interface—users can add, remove, move, resize, or replace objects by manipulating 2D box corners in a semantic map without re-synthesizing the full scene, which is a meaningful step toward artist-driven 3D content creation. However, the approach is limited to a closed vocabulary of 8 object categories trained on indoor scenes, and room boundaries remain difficult to control via text alone. Open questions include whether the factored approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale architectural scenes, whether the semantic box proxy can be replaced by richer intermediate representations, and whether the chunk-based outpainting strategy scales beyond the tested scene sizes without accumulating boundary artifacts.
