## Problem

Existing SDF-based neural surface reconstruction methods co-locate spatial and angular appearance features in the same voxel representation, forcing large MLPs and long training times to decode the full 5D light field. The paper questions whether this co-location is necessary, arguing that angular components (lighting, environment) are locally almost invariant to parallax and can be encoded at a much lower spatial resolution than the high-frequency spatial texture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250544/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses as input, supporting resolutions from 1600×1200 (DTU) up to 4088×2990 (ActorsHQ), with optional foreground masks and per-camera bias vectors for scenes with inconsistent exposure. It produces a sparse voxel SDF grid (15–232 MB depending on resolution) that supports real-time rendering at 200–400 Hz, along with novel view renderings. The model requires as few as 4 spatial and 4 angular parameters per voxel, with spherical harmonic order up to l=4.

## Method

ProbeSDF decouples the radiance model into two decorrelated volumetric grids: a high-resolution sparse voxel grid storing SDF values and planar-factorized spatial features (F_x ⊗ F_y ⊗ F_z per 16³ tile), and a coarser grid at 1/16th resolution storing spherical harmonic coefficients as light field probes. Angular features are obtained by tri-linearly interpolating the 8 nearest probes and evaluating the SH basis with the reflected vector r, while a polynomial approximation of the Fresnel term ((1−n·v)^0…^5) is appended to handle grazing-angle reflectivity. A tiny 2-layer MLP (32 neurons) fuses these inputs into the outgoing color, and the entire computation is fused into a single CUDA kernel. Training follows a coarse-to-fine strategy in both spatial resolution and SH order, minimizing a weighted sum of photometric, Eikonal, normal-smoothness, feature-smoothness, and probe-smoothness losses with Adam.

## Results

On MVMannequins, ProbeSDF achieves the best Chamfer distance (1.04 mm) and PSNR (36.81 dB) among all baselines while training in ~1 minute, roughly 3× faster than Millimetric Humans and 15× faster than Voxurf. On DTU, it reaches 0.68 mm Chamfer and 37.03 dB PSNR (150 s training), outperforming Voxurf (0.73 mm, 37.08 dB, 15 min) and NeuS2 (0.80 mm, 36.00 dB, 5 min). On ActorsHQ at full resolution, ProbeSDF attains 37.48 dB PSNR in 250 s, surpassing Voxurf (36.56 dB, 1 h) and NeuS2 (34.53 dB, 250 s). On BlendedMVS, the (8,8,4) configuration yields 2.22 mm Chamfer and 35.89 dB PSNR in 120 s, beating Voxurf (2.64 mm, 35.11 dB, 15 min) and NeuS2 (2.93 mm, 33.62 dB, 5 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProbeSDF outperforms all five baselines on both Chamfer distance and PSNR across the four datasets. Against Millimetric Humans [27] (the closest competitor on MVMannequins), it improves Chamfer by 0.10 mm and PSNR by 0.48 dB while training ~3× faster. Against Voxurf [33], the gains are larger: 0.55 mm Chamfer and 1.30 dB PSNR on MVMannequins, with a 15× training speedup; on DTU, Voxurf's PSNR (37.08) marginally exceeds ProbeSDF's (4,4,4) config (37.03), but the (8,8,4) config closes this gap to 37.74. Against NeuS2 [31], ProbeSDF wins by 0.12–1.09 mm Chamfer and 1.0–2.6 dB PSNR depending on dataset, while training 2–5× faster. Against COLMAP [26] and 2DGS [6], the advantages are most pronounced: 2.4–2.5 mm lower Chamfer and 1.9–2.0 dB higher PSNR on MVMannequins, with training times of ~1 min versus over an hour.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDF-based neural surface reconstruction lineage (NeuS → Voxurf → NeuS2 → Millimetric Humans) by replacing the standard co-located spatial+angular appearance model with a decoupled two-grid architecture: a high-resolution sparse voxel grid with planar factorization for spatial features (following TensorF) and a coarser 1/16-resolution grid of light field probes encoding angular features via spherical harmonics. The light field probe concept is borrowed directly from real-time rendering, where angular/environment components are precomputed at sparse 3D locations and interpolated, but here the probe parameters are jointly optimized with the SDF. The key departure from prior SDF methods is that angular features are no longer stored per-voxel at full resolution but at a much lower spatial frequency, justified by the observation that lighting varies more slowly across the surface than intrinsic texture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SOTA on both Chamfer distance and PSNR across four diverse datasets (objects and humans) while training in 1–4 minutes versus 15 minutes to over an hour for baselines, and enabling real-time rendering at 200–400 Hz. The model's interpretability (spatial features = texture, angular features = shading, Fresnel term = grazing reflections) makes it a natural drop-in replacement for existing SDF pipelines. Open questions include whether the local lighting encoding limits generalization to novel viewpoints (noted on ActorsHQ legs), whether explicit light transport could resolve shape-radiance ambiguities, and whether the decoupled representation extends to temporal or relighting applications.
