## Problem

Existing diffusion-based human image animation methods, whether using ReferenceNet-style parallel UNets or SVD fine-tuning, impose strong appearance constraints on spatial attentions that suppress dynamic details, resulting in static backgrounds and rigid human motions. This limits the ability to generate lifelike dynamics such as flowing hair, garments, and environmental effects (waterfalls, rain, fireworks) while simultaneously preserving identity and transferring pose and expression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251709/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference human image (cropped to 896×512) and a driving video from a different person, from which 2D skeletal pose sequences and local face patches are extracted per frame. It produces a 16-frame animated video where the subject follows the driving video's body pose and facial expression while the background exhibits natural dynamics consistent with the reference image context. During training, face patches are processed through a portrait reenactment network (FaceVid2Vid) to create identity-swapped conditioning, but at inference this step is bypassed and raw cropped face patches from the driving video are fed directly into the S-Face ControlNet.

## Method

The core contribution is the Dynamics-Adapter, a lightweight cross-frame attention module that injects reference appearance into the diffusion UNet's spatial self-attention layers via a shared-weight parallel branch: it computes attention between a trainable query (projected from the noised generation frame) and frozen key/value (from the denoised reference image), with a zero-initialized output projection so the backbone's dynamics synthesis capability remains intact at initialization. A second plug-in, S-Face ControlNet, learns identity-disentangled facial expression control by training on cross-identity face patches synthesized via a portrait reenactment network, avoiding the identity leakage inherent in explicit landmark-based face control. The model is trained using Harmonic Data Fusion Training, which simultaneously feeds human motion videos (900 hours, 107,546 clips) and natural scene videos (Skyscape, 3000 time-lapse clips) with blank pose/face conditions for scene-only data, enabling the backbone and temporal modules to learn both human dynamics and environmental effects in a single pass. The full pipeline builds on frozen SD 1.5 weights with AnimateDiff temporal modules and a Pose ControlNet, trained in two stages: first the Dynamics-Adapter, Pose ControlNet, and motion module are trained jointly, then the S-Face ControlNet is fine-tuned on human video data alone.

## Results

On the dynamics texture generation benchmark (Table 1), X-Dyna achieves a DTFVD of 1.518, substantially outperforming all baselines: MagicAnimate (2.601), Animate-Anyone (2.310), MagicPose (2.412), and MimicMotion (3.590). On the human video animation benchmark (Table 2), X-Dyna attains the best L1 (7.15e-05), PSNR (17.201), Face-Cosine similarity (0.497), Face-Detection rate (94.8%), and background FID (25.59). In the user study, X-Dyna receives an overall rating of 4.09/5, compared to 2.48–2.89 for all baselines, with the largest gap in background dynamics (4.26 vs. 2.18–2.78).

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Dyna decisively outperforms all four baselines on the paper's primary metric, DTFVD, reducing the overall score from 2.310–3.590 to 1.518 (a 34–58% improvement). On the broader human animation benchmark (Table 2), X-Dyna wins on L1, PSNR, Face-Cosine, Face-Detection rate, and background FID, but trails MagicPose on foreground FID (22.56 vs. 18.52) and MimicMotion on foreground cd-FVD (325.35 vs. 150.01), suggesting its dynamics focus comes at a slight cost to foreground temporal consistency. The ReferenceNet-based methods (MagicAnimate, Animate-Anyone, MagicPose) all produce near-static backgrounds, while the SVD-based MimicMotion shows the weakest dynamics overall. In the user study, X-Dyna leads every criterion—foreground dynamics (3.87), background dynamics (4.26), and identity preservation (4.14)—by at least 1.0 point over the strongest baseline, confirming that the Dynamics-Adapter and Harmonic Data Fusion Training yield perceptually superior animations.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-Dyna builds directly on the diffusion-based human image animation pipeline established by ReferenceNet-based methods (Animate-Anyone, MagicPose, MagicAnimate) and SVD-based approaches (MimicMotion), all of which use ControlNet for pose and AnimateDiff for temporal consistency. The key architectural departure is replacing the heavy ReferenceNet (a full trainable parallel UNet) with a lightweight Dynamics-Adapter that injects reference appearance via a single cross-frame attention term with only trainable query and output projectors, inspired by the I2V-Adapter's attention mechanism. The paper also extends the task beyond prior work by introducing Harmonic Data Fusion Training (co-training on human and natural scene videos) and an S-Face ControlNet that uses cross-identity face patches for identity-disentangled expression control, drawing on portrait reenactment networks like FaceVid2Vid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete, widely-observed limitation: ReferenceNet-based appearance control produces static backgrounds and rigid human dynamics, which undermines the lifelike quality of generated animations. The Dynamics-Adapter is a simple, plug-and-play module that could be retrofitted onto other diffusion backbones (SVD, SDXL, SD3, Flux), making it practically useful beyond this specific pipeline. Open questions remain around extreme pose deviations, hand pose generation, and whether the Harmonic Data Fusion strategy generalizes to more complex scene interactions. The authors note that the approach is currently limited to SD 1.5 and 896×512 resolution, leaving room for scaling to higher-fidelity models.
