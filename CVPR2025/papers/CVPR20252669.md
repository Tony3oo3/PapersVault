## Problem

Index-wise discrete tokenizers in autoregressive image models are bottlenecked by limited vocabulary sizes, causing quantization errors, fuzzy supervision signals, and cumulative train-test discrepancies from teacher-forcing that degrade high-resolution generation. Directly enlarging the vocabulary to compensate is computationally infeasible with conventional classifiers, creating a fundamental barrier to scaling AR models to match diffusion-model quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252669/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language text prompt (encoded by Flan-T5 into cross-attention embeddings) and produces a photorealistic RGB image at up to 1024×1024 resolution with arbitrary aspect ratios. Generation proceeds coarse-to-fine across K residual scales, and the 2B-parameter model synthesizes a 1024×1024 image in 0.8 s without additional optimization, roughly 2.6× faster than SD3-Medium.

## Method

Infinity replaces index-wise tokens with bitwise tokens throughout the pipeline via three coupled components. First, a multi-scale residual quantizer using Binary Spherical Quantization (BSQ) expands the effective vocabulary to 2^64 while keeping memory constant at O(d) per scale. Second, an Infinite-Vocabulary Classifier (IVC) predicts d independent binary bits per spatial position instead of a single index over V_d classes, reducing parameters by 99.95% and providing per-dimension supervision that avoids the near-zero ambiguity of index labels. Third, bitwise self-correction randomly flips a fraction of predicted bits during training and re-quantizes the residual, forcing the transformer to learn error recovery and eliminating the train-test gap inherent to teacher-forcing. The overall architecture follows VAR's next-scale prediction with a RoPE2D-equipped transformer, cross-attention to text embeddings, and progressive training from 256→512→1024 resolution.

## Results

On GenEval, Infinity (2B) achieves the best overall score of 0.73 (with prompt rewriting), surpassing SD3-8B (0.71), DALL-E 3 (0.67), and SDXL (0.55), while also reaching the highest position reasoning score of 0.49 and color attribute score of 0.57. On DPG, Infinity scores 83.5 overall with the best relation score of 90.76 among all open-source T2I models, matching DALL-E 3 (83.5) and slightly trailing SD3-2B (84.1) and FLUX-schnell (84.8). On human preference benchmarks, Infinity achieves the highest ImageReward (0.953) and HPSv2.1 (32.05) scores. Inference speed is 0.8s for a 1024×1024 image, 2.6× faster than SD3-Medium and 7× faster than SD3.5-8B and FLUX-Dev (12B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Infinity (2B) decisively outperforms all prior autoregressive T2I models: it surpasses Emu3 (8.5B) by +0.07 on GenEval Overall (0.73 vs 0.66) and +1.9 on DPG Overall (83.5 vs 81.6), and leads LlamaGen (0.8B) by a wide margin (0.73 vs 0.32 GenEval). Against diffusion models, Infinity beats SDXL (2.6B) by +0.18 on GenEval Overall and +8.8 on DPG Overall, and outperforms PixArt-Sigma (0.6B) by +0.18 on GenEval and +3.0 on DPG. Infinity matches DALL-E 3 on DPG Overall (83.5) and exceeds it on GenEval (0.73 vs 0.67), while slightly trailing SD3-2B on DPG Overall (83.5 vs 84.1) and FLUX-schnell (83.5 vs 84.8). On human preference, Infinity achieves the top ImageReward (0.953 vs 0.871 for SD3-Medium) and HPSv2.1 (32.05 vs 30.91) scores, though SD3-Medium edges it on VQAScore (0.906 vs 0.894). Notably, Infinity achieves these results with only 2B parameters and 0.8s inference time, compared to 12B parameters for FLUX models, making it the fastest T2I model evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

Infinity directly extends VAR's next-scale prediction framework by replacing its index-wise discrete tokenizer with a bitwise multi-scale residual quantizer (BSQ), enabling a vocabulary size of 2^64 that was previously infeasible. The key architectural departure is the Infinite-Vocabulary Classifier, which decomposes the single large-integer prediction into d parallel binary classifiers, and the bitwise self-correction mechanism that addresses the train-test discrepancy inherent in teacher-forced AR training. This positions the work at the intersection of the AR-vs-diffusion debate and the scaling-law literature, showing that discrete tokenization need not be a fundamental bottleneck if the vocabulary is scaled sufficiently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching or surpassing leading diffusion models (SD3, DALL-E 3, SDXL) on GenEval and DPG while generating 1024×1024 images in 0.8s (2.6×–7× faster), Infinity challenges the assumption that iterative denoising is necessary for high-fidelity T2I synthesis. The near-linear scaling relationship (Pearson r ≈ −0.98) between validation loss and downstream metrics across 125M–4.7B parameters suggests continued gains from further scaling, though the paper does not explore video generation or multimodal understanding. Open questions include whether the bitwise self-correction mechanism generalizes to other AR settings, whether the 2^64 vocabulary advantage persists at even larger scales, and how the approach handles the data-efficiency gap relative to diffusion models trained on similar corpora.
