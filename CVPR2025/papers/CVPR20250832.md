## Problem

Fusing independently trained subject and style LoRAs for diffusion models remains challenging: element-level weight merging smooths style textures and distorts object identity, while existing alternatives (ZipLoRA, B-LoRA) require additional training or manual hyperparameter tuning to balance contributions. The paper identifies that the intrinsic sparsity structure of LoRA matrices and the distinct roles of early vs. late diffusion steps can be exploited to achieve training-free fusion without these compromises.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250832/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two pre-trained LoRA weight sets (a content/subject LoRA and a style LoRA) of arbitrary rank, along with a standard text prompt, and operates at inference time on SDXL v1.0 or FLUX base models. No retraining, fine-tuning, or manual coefficient tuning is required; the LoRAs can come from different sources (locally trained or community-downloaded) with potentially very different element magnitudes. The output is a single generated image that simultaneously preserves the subject's structural identity and the style's color, texture, and pattern attributes.

## Method

K-LoRA performs per-attention-layer LoRA selection during each denoising step. For each layer, it computes the absolute values of both LoRA weight matrices, extracts the Top-K largest elements (K = r_c · r_s, the product of the two LoRA ranks), and sums them to obtain importance scores S_c and S_s. A timestep-dependent scaling factor S = α·(t_now/t_all) + β is applied to the style score, biasing content selection in early steps and style selection in later steps, consistent with the empirical finding that early diffusion reconstructs objects while later steps refine style details. A normalization factor γ, computed as the ratio of total absolute element sums across all layers, compensates for numerical scale differences between LoRAs from different sources. The layer with the higher adjusted score is selected wholesale, producing a seamless object-to-style transition across the 50 diffusion steps without modifying any original LoRA weights.

## Results

On 18 object-style combinations (10 images each), K-LoRA achieves the highest subject similarity scores: 69.4% CLIP Score and 46.9% DINO Score, surpassing all baselines. Style similarity (CLIP-based) is competitive at 58.7%, slightly below ZipLoRA (60.4%) and Joint training (68.2%), but the Joint method sacrifices subject fidelity dramatically (17.4% DINO). In a user study over 22 result sets, K-LoRA is preferred by 52.7% of users and receives 83.3% positive GPT-4o feedback, far exceeding ZipLoRA (29.2% / 5.6%) and B-LoRA (18.1% / 11.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

K-LoRA outperforms all three baselines on subject fidelity metrics: it leads ZipLoRA by +5.0 points on CLIP Score (69.4% vs 64.4%) and +11.2 points on DINO Score (46.9% vs 35.7%), and beats B-LoRA by +5.6 and +16.3 points respectively. Against direct arithmetic merging (MergingLoRA), K-LoRA gains +2.8 CLIP and +3.9 DINO while also improving style similarity by +9.8 points. The one metric where K-LoRA trails is style similarity versus ZipLoRA (58.7% vs 60.4%), a narrow 1.7-point gap, and versus Joint training (68.2%), though Joint collapses on subject preservation (17.4% DINO). In the user study, K-LoRA dominates with 52.7% preference and 83.3% GPT-4o positive feedback, roughly doubling ZipLoRA's user preference and outscoring it by 77.7 points on GPT-4o feedback, confirming that the quantitative subject-fidelity gains translate into perceptually superior fused images.

## Relation to Prior Work

K-LoRA sits at the intersection of LoRA merging and timestep-aware diffusion scheduling. It builds directly on ZipLoRA's observation that a small subset of dominant LoRA elements suffices for generation, and on B-LoRA's identification of distinct attention-module roles in the generative process, but departs from both by avoiding any additional training or weight modification. Where ZipLoRA learns a per-layer fusion ratio vector and B-LoRA fine-tunes two specific attention modules, K-LoRA performs a training-free, per-layer binary selection (content vs. style) driven by Top-K element sums, modulated by a linear timestep-dependent scaling factor. The approach also draws on Multi-LoRA Composition's cyclic LoRA scheduling, but replaces the fixed cycle with an adaptive, data-driven selection criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of K-LoRA makes it immediately applicable to any pair of existing LoRAs without retraining, which is practically valuable for the large community of LoRA users on platforms like Hugging Face. The empirical finding that content LoRAs are most effective in early diffusion steps while style LoRAs dominate in later steps provides a useful inductive bias for future fusion methods. However, the binary per-layer selection (all content or all style, no blending) limits expressiveness, and the method has only been validated on two base models (SDXL, FLUX) with two LoRAs; generalization to multi-LoRA compositions, other diffusion architectures, or non-style/content LoRA pairs remains open. The scaling factor introduces three hyperparameters (α, β, γ) whose sensitivity is not thoroughly characterized.
