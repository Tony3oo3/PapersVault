## Problem

Existing federated unlearning methods suffer from three interrelated deficiencies: they indiscriminately erase shared knowledge across clients when overlapping data exists, they are irreversible so that forgotten knowledge cannot be recovered if a client retracts its unlearning request, and retraining-based approaches incur prohibitive computational and communication costs. No prior method simultaneously addresses all three challenges while maintaining unlearning quality comparable to full retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252226/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully trained federated model (e.g., ResNet-18, LeNet, or SimpleViT), a specification of which clients/classes/samples to forget, and the local data of remaining clients. It produces a set of sparse unlearning adapters (binary-masked parameter matrices with only a small fraction of parameters active) that are added element-wise to the frozen original model at inference time. Because the adapters are independent and sparse, the unlearned model can be reverted to its original state by simply discarding the adapters, and the communication payload per client drops from tens of megabytes to roughly 1–11 KB.

## Method

FUSED operates in two stages. First, Critical Layer Identification (CLI) runs one federated round, computes the Manhattan distance between each client's locally trained layer parameters and the server's distributed model, and ranks layers by average sensitivity; the top-K most sensitive layers are selected for unlearning. Second, for each selected layer a sparse adapter is constructed by randomly retaining a small fraction of parameters, and only these adapters are trained via FedAvg on the remaining (non-forgotten) data while the original model stays frozen. The key mechanism is knowledge overwriting: the theoretical analysis shows that when the cosine similarity between the gradients of the forgotten task and the remaining task is negative, training on remaining data in the sensitive layers progressively erases the target knowledge without disturbing other layers. This design yields reversibility (removing adapters restores the original model), reduced communication (only sparse adapters are transmitted), and minimal interference with overlapping knowledge shared across clients.

## Results

On the client unlearning scenario, FUSED achieves RA of 0.99/0.67/0.41/0.36 and FA of 0.00/0.05/0.07/0.01 on FashionMNIST-LeNet, Cifar10-ResNet18, Cifar10-Transformer, and Cifar100-ResNet18 respectively, matching or exceeding the Retraining upper bound in most settings (e.g., RA 0.41 vs. 0.33 on Cifar10-Transformer). Communication cost is reduced by ~43× (0.98M vs. 42.73M on Cifar10-ResNet18) and computation time by ~1.7× (262s vs. 434s) compared to Retraining. In the knowledge interference experiment, FUSED achieves 0.37 C-Acc on overlapping knowledge vs. 0.38 for Retraining, while all other baselines drop to 0.06–0.14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSED matches or exceeds the Retraining upper bound on remaining accuracy (RA) in 3 of 4 settings (0.41 vs. 0.33 on Cifar10-Transformer; 0.67 vs. 0.71 on Cifar10-ResNet18 is a 4-point gap) while cutting communication by ~43× and computation by 1.6–10.7×. Against Federaser, FUSED wins decisively on RA (0.41 vs. 0.21 on Cifar10-Transformer; 0.36 vs. 0.19 on Cifar100-ResNet18) and computation (262s vs. 991s on Cifar10-ResNet18). Against Exact-Fun, FUSED achieves higher RA on Cifar10-Transformer (0.41 vs. 0.33) and Cifar100-ResNet18 (0.36 vs. 0.25) with roughly 5–6× lower computation. Against EraseClient, FUSED dominates on RA (0.99 vs. 0.09 on FashionMNIST; 0.67 vs. 0.64 on Cifar10-ResNet18) and reduces communication from 42.73M to 0.98M. The one area where FUSED trails is MIA on Cifar100-ResNet18 (0.48 vs. 0.22 for Retraining), suggesting slightly higher privacy leakage in that specific setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSED repurposes the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) paradigm—specifically sparse adapter modules—toward the federated unlearning problem, a setting where prior work has relied on full retraining (Federaser, Exact-Fun) or direct parameter manipulation (EraseClient, class-discriminative pruning). The Critical Layer Identification stage introduces a layer-sensitivity analysis that has no direct analogue in existing unlearning literature, while the 'knowledge overwriting' mechanism draws on the linearized multi-task interference theory of Lee et al. [22] to justify why training on remaining data can selectively erase target knowledge. The paper thus carves new ground by combining a diagnostic (CLI) with a PEFT-style training procedure, rather than extending any single prior unlearning method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reversibility property (simply removing the adapter restores the original model) and the ~16× communication reduction address two practical pain points that prior FU methods leave unresolved, making FUSED a more deployable option for real-world FL systems where unlearning requests may be dynamic. However, the method still requires access to the full remaining dataset for adapter training, and the theoretical analysis relies on a first-order Taylor approximation in a linear regime that may not hold for deep, non-convex networks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large language models or vision transformers with billions of parameters, how to handle adversarial unlearning requests, and whether data compression or replay can reduce the remaining-data dependency.
