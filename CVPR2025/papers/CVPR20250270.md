## Problem

Existing 3D scene manipulation methods based on NeRF or 3DGS struggle to precisely control the manipulation region, often affecting irrelevant areas and producing unexpected results. While interactive 3D segmentation could address this, current frameworks require a scene-specific training step (feature learning or mask optimization) as preprocessing, which limits the efficiency and flexibility needed for practical manipulation workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250270/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The user provides 2D clicks (positive or negative) from any arbitrary viewpoint on a pre-trained 3DGS scene, optionally accompanied by a text instruction for semantic editing. The system outputs a set of extracted 3D Gaussians defining the target region, which can then be manipulated via the toolbox (semantic editing, colorization, scaling, copy & paste, combination, or removal). Notably, no scene-specific training or feature extraction is required at any stage; the only external model calls are SAM for 2D masking and DINO for feature extraction, both used in a frozen, inference-only manner.

## Method

iSegMan combines two training-free algorithms for region control. First, Epipolar-guided Interaction Propagation (EIP) propagates a 2D click to all other views by computing the epipolar line in each view (via camera pose geometry) and then matching DINO features only along that line, drastically reducing the search space and improving robustness over unconstrained feature matching. Second, Visibility-based Gaussian Voting (VGV) models 3D region extraction as a voting game: SAM produces 2D binary masks per view, and each 2D pixel 'votes' for every 3D Gaussian with a weight equal to its alpha-blending visibility (Eq. 6), so that Gaussians accumulating votes above a threshold are selected. An Iterative Inspection Mechanism (IIM) further filters erroneous SAM predictions by checking whether the predicted mask intersects the rendered mask of the currently selected Gaussians, excluding views where the target is occluded or out-of-frame. The resulting region is then passed to a Manipulation Toolbox that implements semantic editing (via InstructPix2Pix with iterative 3DGS parameter updates), colorization, scaling, copy & paste, combination, and removal.

## Results

On SPIn-NeRF, iSegMan achieves 92.4% mIoU and 99.1% mAcc for interactive 3D segmentation without any scene-specific training, with a feature extraction time of 52 s and 6 s per interaction. On NVOS, it reaches 92.0% mIoU and 98.4% mAcc (30 s feature, 4 s per interaction). For semantic editing, iSegMan scores 4.52 ± 0.20 in user study and 0.2189 CLIP directional similarity, outperforming both Instruct-GS2GS (2.10, 0.1647) and GaussianEditor (3.32, 0.2071).

## Baselines & Comparisons

iSegMan outperforms all six baselines on SPIn-NeRF in both mIoU and mAcc while requiring no scene-specific training. Against the closest competitor SA3D, iSegMan gains +0.5 mIoU (92.4 vs 91.9) and +0.3 mAcc (99.1 vs 98.8), while reducing feature extraction from 5 min to 52 s and per-interaction segmentation from 30 s to 6 s. Compared to SAGA, iSegMan improves mIoU by 4.4 points (92.4 vs 88.0) and cuts feature time from ~1.5 h to 52 s. The gap over LangSplat (69.5 mIoU) and ISRF (71.5 mIoU) is even larger, exceeding 20 mIoU points. For semantic editing, iSegMan leads GaussianEditor by 1.20 in user study (4.52 vs 3.32) and by 0.0118 in CLIP_dir (0.2189 vs 0.2071), and substantially outperforms Instruct-GS2GS (2.10 user study, 0.1647 CLIP_dir), which cannot explicitly control the editing region.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation and SAM for 2D mask generation, but departs fundamentally from prior interactive 3D segmentation methods (SA3D, SAGA, LangSplat, ISRF) that all require scene-specific feature or mask training as a preprocessing step. Instead, iSegMan replaces learned 3D features with two training-free mechanisms: epipolar-guided interaction propagation (using multi-view geometry to constrain cross-view click matching) and visibility-based Gaussian voting (modeling region extraction as a voting game weighted by 3DGS alpha-blending visibility). For manipulation, it extends the Instruct-N2N/GaussianEditor line of text-driven 3D editing by adding precise interactive region control, addressing the core limitation that prior methods cannot reliably confine edits to user-specified regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the scene-specific training bottleneck, iSegMan makes interactive 3D segmentation immediately applicable to any 3DGS scene without a multi-hour preprocessing pipeline, which is critical for practical 3D content creation workflows. The manipulation toolbox (semantic editing, colorization, scaling, copy-paste, combination, removal) positions this as a practical editing tool rather than a pure segmentation benchmark. Key limitations remain: semantic editing quality is bounded by the underlying 2D image editor (InstructPix2Pix), and gradient-based 3D Gaussian updates prevent real-time interaction. An open question is whether the visibility-voting framework can generalize to more complex manipulation tasks (e.g., shape deformation) or to representations beyond 3DGS.
