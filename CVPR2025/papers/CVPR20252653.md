## Problem

Black-box Domain Adaptation (BDA) assumes identical label sets across source and target domains, which fails in practice when the target contains categories absent from the source. The only prior work addressing this (UB²DA) suffers from two limitations: noisy pseudo-labels from the source model accumulate prediction errors during knowledge distillation, and a manually preset threshold for detecting 'unknown' categories cannot adapt to varying degrees of category shift across domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252653/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target-domain RGB images and queries a black-box source predictor strictly as an API, receiving only per-class probability vectors (no access to source data, model weights, or internal representations). The output is a per-sample class assignment: either one of the known source-domain labels or a single 'unknown' label, with the decision governed by an adaptive entropy threshold computed as the mean entropy of the target model's predictions over the entire target set.

## Method

ADU combines two loss modules. Selective Amplification Knowledge Distillation (SAKD) modifies the standard KD loss by raising the confidence-weighted term (1 + p_s^ĉ) to a power θ ≥ 1, which via the generalized binomial expansion introduces an additional positive term proportional to p_s^ĉ that amplifies learning from high-confidence pseudo-labels while suppressing noisy ones. Entropy-Driven Label Differentiation (EDLD) splits pseudo-labels into high-quality (entropy below the adaptive mean-entropy threshold) and low-quality groups: HQ samples receive entropy minimization plus a negative-learning loss that pushes complementary-class probabilities toward zero to refine labels, while LQ samples receive entropy maximization to widen the gap between known and unknown categories. An exponential moving average (EMA) of target-model predictions iteratively refines the source pseudo-labels, balancing consistency with adaptability across training iterations.

## Results

ADU achieves state-of-the-art H-scores across all four benchmarks in both OPBDA and OSBDA settings. In the OPBDA scenario, ADU reaches 88.1% on Office31, 72.2% on OfficeHome, 48.7% on VisDA, and 51.0% on DomainNet (averaged over 6 directions), surpassing the previous best method UB²DA by 1.2%, 1.8%, 3.5%, and 1.7% respectively. In OSBDA, ADU scores 88.1% on Office31, 71.1% on OfficeHome, and 50.8% on VisDA. All BDA baselines (DINE, BETA, SEAL) fall below the 'No Adapt.' baseline on Office31 and OfficeHome, confirming they are ill-suited for open-set scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADU outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and both OPBDA/OSBDA settings. The three BDA methods—DINE (55.9% Office31 avg), BETA (55.0%), and SEAL (59.1%)—all score below the 'No Adapt.' baseline (82.0%) on Office31 and OfficeHome, indicating that standard knowledge-distillation BDA without open-set handling actively degrades performance when unknown classes are present. The only directly comparable open-set black-box method, UB²DA [9], is the closest competitor: ADU improves over it by 1.2% on Office31, 1.8% on OfficeHome, 3.5% on VisDA, and 1.7% on DomainNet in OPBDA, and by 1.0%, 2.0%, and 2.7% on Office31, OfficeHome, and VisDA in OSBDA. The largest single-task gain is +20.4% H-score on OfficeHome Pr→Re in OSBDA, where ADU reaches 76.1% versus UB²DA's 75.0% and No Adapt.'s 55.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the black-box domain adaptation (BDA) line of work—where only a source model's API is accessible—into the open-set and open-partial settings (OSBDA/OPBDA), a formulation first introduced by UB²DA [9]. Methodologically, it builds directly on standard knowledge distillation (Hinton et al. [17]) by introducing a confidence-amplifying exponent (SAKD) and on negative learning (Kim et al. [21]) for pseudo-label refinement, but departs from prior BDA methods by replacing their fixed-threshold unknown-class detection with an adaptive entropy-based criterion (EDLD). The EMA-based pseudo-label refinement connects to the noisy-label learning literature, though it avoids the ensembling overhead typical of that line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world deployment rarely guarantees identical label sets across domains, yet all prior BDA methods assumed this. The adaptive entropy threshold eliminates per-dataset hyperparameter tuning, and the SAKD loss (a single parameter θ) offers a lightweight, drop-in modification to any KD-based BDA pipeline. Limitations include evaluation only on image classification with ResNet-50 across four datasets, and the gains over the sole OPBDA baseline (UB²DA) are modest (1–3.5% H-score). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other modalities (video, point clouds), scales to very large label spaces, or remains effective under extreme category shifts where the source model's predictions are near-random.
