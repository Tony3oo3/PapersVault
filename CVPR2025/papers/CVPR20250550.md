## Problem

Affective Image Manipulation (AIM) requires simultaneously evoking a target emotion and preserving the original image composition, but existing methods rely solely on color and style adjustments, which fail to produce precise, profound emotional shifts. Psychological research shows that semantic content (objects, scenes, actions, facial expressions) is a critical driver of emotional perception, yet no prior AIM method performs content-level modifications guided by emotion-specific semantics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single user-provided RGB image and a target emotion word drawn from eight discrete categories (amusement, awe, contentment, excitement, anger, disgust, fear, sadness) as the sole prompt—no detailed textual description of the desired edit is required. It outputs a modified image that introduces emotion-appropriate semantic elements (e.g., adding a butterfly for contentment, a graveyard for sadness) while maintaining the original spatial layout. An image guidance scale parameter lets users trade off between emotion intensity and structural fidelity at inference time.

## Method

EmoEdit introduces an Emotion Adapter built on the Q-Former architecture, where learned queries act as an emotion dictionary; self-attention selects relevant semantic entries conditioned on the target emotion embedding, and cross-attention then fuses this selection with the input image embedding to produce a context-appropriate emotion conditioning vector c_e. The adapter is trained on the newly constructed EmoEditSet (40,120 source–emotion–target triplets) with IP2P parameters frozen, optimizing a combined objective of standard diffusion loss (for pixel-level structure preservation) and a novel instruction loss that aligns c_e with the text-encoded content instruction for each pair, thereby capturing semantic variation across different realizations of the same emotion. The dataset is built in two stages: emotion attribution (CLIP-based clustering of EmoSet followed by GPT-4V semantic summaries organized into factor trees) and data construction (IP2P generation with emotion-factor instructions, filtered by CLIP image/text similarity, emotion score, aesthetic score, and manual review). The resulting adapter is plug-and-play, transferable to other diffusion-based editing and generation models without retraining the base model.

## Results

On the 405-image evaluation set (3,240 pairs across 8 emotions), EmoEdit achieves the best scores on 5 of 6 metrics: PSNR 16.62, SSIM 0.571, LPIPS 0.289, Emo-A 50.09%, and Emo-S 0.335. The largest margin is on emotion accuracy (Emo-A), where EmoEdit leads the next-best method (SDEdit, 38.21%) by over 12 percentage points. On CLIP-I (semantic similarity), EmoEdit (0.828) is slightly behind PnP (0.851), which the authors attribute to content-level modifications inherently reducing pixel-level semantic overlap. In the user study, EmoEdit receives 89.12% preference for the best balance between structure and emotion, far exceeding SDEdit (5.07%), P2P-Zero (0.94%), and BlipDiff (4.88%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmoEdit outperforms all eight baselines on the two emotion-specific metrics (Emo-A and Emo-S), with the closest competitor being SDEdit (38.21% vs. 50.09% Emo-A; 0.221 vs. 0.335 Emo-S). Among global editing methods, SDEdit and PnP are the strongest on structure metrics, but both lag significantly on emotion: PnP achieves the highest CLIP-I (0.851) yet only 23.83% Emo-A. Local editing methods (InsDiff, P2P-Zero, ControlNet, BlipDiff) perform poorly on emotion metrics (Emo-A ranging from 18.06% to 36.33%) and also score lower on structure metrics than EmoEdit. Style-based AIM methods (CLVA, AIF) preserve structure reasonably well (AIF: SSIM 0.537) but are the weakest on emotion, with Emo-A of only 12.74–14.04% and near-zero Emo-S, confirming that color/style adjustments alone cannot evoke precise emotional shifts. The only metric where EmoEdit does not lead is CLIP-I, where PnP edges ahead by 0.023, a gap the authors explain as an inherent trade-off of content-level modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Affective Image Manipulation (AIM) line of work, which has historically been limited to color and style adjustments (CLVA, AIF), by introducing content-level semantic modifications as the primary mechanism for evoking emotions. Architecturally, it builds on InstructPix2Pix as a frozen base editor and adapts the Q-Former structure from BLIP-2 into a lightweight Emotion adapter that fuses target emotion and input image via self- and cross-attention. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes EmoSet for emotion attribution and GPT-4V for semantic factor summarization, while the instruction loss is a novel addition to the standard diffusion training objective to capture per-pair semantic variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EmoEditSet (40,120 pairs) is the first large-scale benchmark for content-aware AIM, and the plug-and-play Emotion adapter demonstrates that emotion knowledge can be injected into diverse diffusion-based editing and generation models without retraining them. However, the method is constrained to eight emotion categories inherited from EmoSet, and the evaluation metrics (Emo-A, Emo-S) rely on a classifier trained on the same source data, raising circularity concerns. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to finer-grained or culturally specific emotions, how to incorporate interactive human feedback during editing, and whether the content-modification paradigm scales beyond the curated emotion factor trees.
