## Problem

Video event localization research has almost exclusively relied on natural language queries, overlooking the practical need for multimodal queries (MQs) that combine reference images with text. This gap is significant because users often struggle to articulate unfamiliar, abstract, or non-verbal concepts in words, and images can convey richer semantic intent. No prior benchmark or systematic study has evaluated how existing NLQ-based localization models perform when queries are expressed as composed image-text pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250812/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video and a multimodal query consisting of a reference image (generated in one of four styles: scribble, cartoon, cinematic, or realistic) paired with an optional refinement text that provides complementary or corrective semantic details. The output is a set of temporal segment boundaries [τ_start, τ_end] localizing all relevant moments in the video. Evaluation follows standard video moment retrieval metrics: Recall@1 at IoU 0.5 and 0.7, and mAP over IoU thresholds [0.5:0.05:0.95], with the testbed ICQ-Highlight built on the QVHighlights validation set using human-curated queries and synthetic reference images from DALL-E-2 and Stable Diffusion.

## Method

The paper proposes a Multimodal Query Adaptation (MQA) framework with two strategies: Language-Space MQA (converting MQs to NLQs via MLLM captioning/summarization) and Embedding-Space MQA (directly encoding reference images as query embeddings via CLIP's visual encoder). Three training-free methods are introduced: MQ-Cap (MLLM captions the image, LLM integrates refinement text), MQ-Sum (MLLM summarizes image and text in one step), and VQ-Enc (image embedding replaces text embedding). To address the distribution shift between regenerated NLQs and original training queries, the paper introduces SUIT (Surrogate Fine-tuning on Pseudo-MQs), which automatically generates pseudo-MQs from Flickr30K/COCO captions using GPT-3.5, then fine-tunes LLaVA-mistral-1.6 with LoRA (rank 32) to produce task-specific, formatted summaries. The framework is evaluated across 12 backbone models spanning specialized DETR-based localizers, unified multi-task frameworks, and Video LLMs, establishing a comprehensive SOTA baseline for MQ-based video event localization.

## Results

On the ICQ-Highlight benchmark, the best-performing specialized models (UVCOM, TR-DETR) achieve R1@0.5 scores in the 52–56% range and R1@0.7 scores around 37–42% across the four reference image styles (scribble, cartoon, cinematic, realistic) using the MQ-Sum(+SUIT) adaptation. Among training-free methods, MQ-Cap is the most robust, outperforming MQ-Sum by an average margin of 3.6% across all styles. The SUIT fine-tuning strategy yields a non-marginal improvement of 4.3%–9.7% over the training-free MQ-Sum baseline, with more stable performance (lower standard deviation) across backbone models. LLM-based models (SeViLA, TimeChat, VTimeLLM) perform substantially worse, with SeViLA dropping to ~13% R1@0.5, far below the ~50%+ achieved by specialized DETR-based models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 12 backbone models evaluated, UVCOM and TR-DETR consistently achieve the highest R1@0.5 scores (~52–56%) across all four reference image styles and all adaptation methods, making them the strongest baselines on ICQ. CG-DETR and QD-DETR are competitive, reaching ~50–51% R1@0.5, while EaTR performs similarly on scribble but drops more on other styles. The older Moment-DETR (NeurIPS 2021) and the unified frameworks UMT and UniVTG trail by 3–6 points, confirming that more recent specialized architectures better handle the semantic shift introduced by MQs. LLM-based models (SeViLA, TimeChat, VTimeLLM) are dramatically outperformed by all specialized models—SeViLA scores only ~13% R1@0.5 versus ~52% for UVCOM—consistent with their already subpar performance on NLQ-based benchmarks. The paper also shows that model rankings on ICQ highly correlate with rankings on the original QVHighlights NLQ benchmark, validating that the MQ adaptation preserves relative model quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing video event localization with multimodal queries (MQs) — composed reference images plus optional refinement texts — as a distinct task from the well-established NLQ-based video moment retrieval and temporal grounding literature. It repurposes existing NLQ-based backbones (DETR-family models, unified frameworks, Video LLMs) through proposed Multimodal Query Adaptation (MQA) methods rather than training new architectures from scratch, and draws conceptual inspiration from composed image retrieval (Pic2Word, TIR) while explicitly distinguishing its temporal localization objective from instance-level similarity matching. The SUIT strategy for pseudo-MQ fine-tuning borrows from PEFT/LoRA techniques applied to MLLMs, bridging the data-scarcity gap without requiring large-scale MQ-annotated training sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a practically motivated direction — enabling video search and event localization through images (including scribbles) for users who struggle with precise textual descriptions, are cross-lingual, or need to express abstract/visual concepts — and provides the first systematic benchmark (ICQ) with 12 backbone models to anchor future research. Key limitations include the persistent performance gap versus the original NLQ benchmark (indicating semantic distortion across modalities), reliance on a single evaluation dataset (ICQ-Highlight built on QVHighlights), and synthetic reference images whose artifacts, while shown to be manageable, may not capture the full diversity of real user-drawn queries. Open questions include whether purpose-built MQ-aware architectures can close the NLQ gap, whether the findings generalize beyond highlight detection to other localization tasks, and how to scale training data for MQ-specific fine-tuning beyond the surrogate approach.
