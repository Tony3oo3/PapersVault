## Problem

Quad-pixel (QP) sensors, which capture four sub-aperture views per microlens compared to two in dual-pixel sensors, offer richer disparity information for defocus deblurring, yet research on QP-based deblurring is nearly absent due to the lack of a dedicated dataset. Existing DP-based methods cannot exploit the additional top/bottom views, and the fixed-aperture constraint of most QP sensors makes the standard DPDD acquisition protocol inapplicable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251261/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For DP data, the network takes left (L) and right (R) sub-aperture views as two separate encoder inputs; for QP data, it takes concatenated L+R and T+B pairs as the two branch inputs. A center (C) view is added as a skip connection to the final output to produce the all-in-focus image. The QPDD dataset provides both RAW (linear-light) and sRGB formats at resolutions of 2160×1280, 1280×1792, or 1080×1280, with 4,935 defocus/all-in-focus pairs across 200 indoor scenes.

## Method

LMNet is a three-level encoder-decoder with a two-branch encoder that separately extracts features from different sub-aperture views, fused at each level by a Simple Fusion Module (SFM) using 7×7 depth-wise convolution and 1×1 point-wise convolution. The core building block is the Local-gate assisted Mamba Block (LAMB), which runs a Vision State Space Module (VSSM, bidirectional Mamba for global dependencies) in parallel with a Local Gate Module (LGM, combining channel and spatial attention via Hadamard gating for local dependencies), then adaptively integrates them with learnable per-channel scale factors α and β. This design mitigates Mamba's local pixel forgetting and channel redundancy while preserving linear-complexity long-range modeling. Training uses a combined L1 and Fourier-domain loss with AdamW, cosine annealing, and progressive patch-size scheduling from 128 to 384 pixels.

## Results

On the DPDD dataset (combined indoor+outdoor), LMNet achieves 27.25 dB PSNR, 0.845 SSIM, and 0.033 MAE with 18.18M parameters, outperforming the closest competitor LDP (27.24 dB) and K3DN (27.06 dB). On the QPDD dataset, the four-view QP input (Ours_Q) reaches 36.75 dB PSNR and 0.928 SSIM in RAW format, surpassing the best two-view baseline LaKDNet_D (34.64 dB) by 2.11 dB. The four-view QP input also improves over the two-view DP input (Ours_D) by 0.27 dB in RAW and 0.39 dB in sRGB, confirming the advantage of additional sub-aperture views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMNet wins on PSNR and SSIM across all 11 baselines on the DPDD combined set. Against the strongest prior methods, it edges out LDP [47] by 0.01 dB PSNR and 0.005 SSIM while using fewer parameters (18.18M vs. 19.60M + 88M CLIP), and beats K3DN [48] by 0.19 dB PSNR. The gap widens against general restoration backbones: +0.59 dB over Restormer [52] and +0.36 dB over LaKDNet [32], while using only 69.6% and 47.3% of their parameters respectively. On the QPDD dataset, the four-view QP input (Ours_Q) achieves 36.75 dB PSNR, surpassing LaKDNet_D (34.64 dB) by 2.11 dB and Restormer_D (34.36 dB) by 2.39 dB, demonstrating the clear advantage of quad-pixel over dual-pixel inputs. In the generalization test (trained on DPDD, tested on QPDD), LMNet achieves 30.37 dB PSNR vs. 29.53 dB for both Restormer and LaKDNet, confirming superior cross-dataset transfer. The only metric where a baseline leads is MAE on DPDD, where LDP achieves 0.028 vs. LMNet's 0.033, though LMNet still leads on PSNR and SSIM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established dual-pixel (DP) defocus deblurring line of work (DPDNet, BaMBNet, K3DN, LDP) to the richer quad-pixel (QP) sensor setting, where four sub-aperture views replace two. Architecturally, it departs from the CNN- and Transformer-dominated prior methods by adopting a Mamba-based state space model (VSSM, following Vision Mamba [55] and Mamba [13]) as the global-dependency backbone, while addressing Mamba's known local pixel forgetting through a novel Local Gate Module that fuses channel and spatial attention via gating. The two-branch encoder with Simple Fusion Module is a lightweight design choice specifically motivated by the multi-view input structure of QP/DP data, distinguishing it from single-encoder restoration networks like Restormer or LaKDNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The QPDD dataset is the first open benchmark for QP-based defocus deblurring, filling a gap that has limited research in this area despite QP sensors being widely deployed in smartphones. The modest but consistent gains from four-view over two-view inputs (0.27–0.39 dB) validate that additional sub-aperture views carry useful disparity information beyond what DP sensors provide. However, the dataset is restricted to indoor planar scenes, and the paper itself acknowledges the need for more diverse DoF scenarios and lighter models for on-device deployment. Open questions include whether the QP advantage persists in complex multi-depth outdoor scenes and whether the Mamba backbone's linear-complexity benefit translates to practical speedups on mobile hardware.
