## Problem

Existing GAN-based super-resolution methods perform coarse-grained discrimination directly on images (image-wise, patch-wise, or pixel-wise), ignoring the semantic content of the images. This semantic-unaware discrimination makes it difficult for SR networks to reconstruct fine-grained, semantically consistent textures, often producing artificial artifacts. The paper also notes that discriminator-based opinion-unaware IQA methods rely on semantic-unaware discriminators, limiting their assessment accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252067/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For super-resolution, the method takes a low-resolution RGB image and produces a ×4 super-resolved image, using a frozen CLIP image encoder as a fixed feature extractor alongside a trainable SRN backbone (RRDB or SwinIR). For the IQA extension (SFD-IQA), a single image is fed through the same frozen CLIP encoder and the already-trained Feat-D and learnable prompt pairs to produce a scalar quality score, requiring no additional task-specific training on any IQA dataset. The text modality enters through learnable prompt pairs (positive/negative) processed by the CLIP text encoder in the TG-D component.

## Method

SFD performs adversarial discrimination on CLIP's semantic features rather than on raw images, via two complementary mechanisms. Feat-D is a VGG-style discriminator that operates on CLIP's three multi-scale pixel-wise middle features, learning to distinguish SR from HR feature distributions at the pixel level. TG-D introduces learnable prompt pairs (LPP) that are adversarially trained to become quality-sensitive, then discriminates CLIP's abstract final output feature by computing softmax-normalized cosine similarity scores between the image feature and the positive/negative text features. The SRN is optimized with a combined loss of L1, VGG perceptual, and two adversarial terms (one from Feat-D, one from TG-D). For SFD-IQA, the trained Feat-D produces a spatial score matrix and the LPP plus original CLIP-IQA prompts produce a global similarity score; the final quality score is their product, all without any additional IQA-specific training.

## Results

On real-world SISR (×4), SFD achieves the best SSIM across all three benchmarks (0.606 on RealD2K, 0.772 on RealSR, 0.815 on DRealSR) and the best LPIPS on RealD2K (0.358) and RealSR (0.256), while also attaining the highest PSNR on DRealSR (29.20 dB). On classical SISR, SFD reports the best PSNR and SSIM on Set5, Set14, Urban100, and DIV2K with competitive LPIPS/DISTS. For OU NR-IQA, SFD-IQA achieves the best PLCC on all four datasets: 0.4252 (PIPAL), 0.7574 (KonIQ-10k), 0.8012 (Ma's dataset), and 0.7111 (LIVE-itW), without any additional task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On real-world SISR, SFD outperforms BSRGAN, RealESRGAN, LDL, and RealSwinIR on SSIM across all three benchmarks and on LPIPS for RealD2K and RealSR, while trailing BSRGAN on NIQE (e.g., 8.600 vs. 7.537 on RealD2K) and on PSNR for RealD2K/RealSR. On classical SISR, SFD achieves the best PSNR and SSIM on Set5, Set14, Urban100, and DIV2K with competitive LPIPS/DISTS, surpassing SRGAN, SED, LDL, DualFormer, ESRGAN, and RankSRGAN. For OU NR-IQA, SFD-IQA dominates all 13 compared methods (including CLIP-IQA, MDFS, RecycleD, DualFormer, and ContentSep) on PLCC, SRCC, and KRCC across PIPAL, KonIQ-10k, Ma's dataset, and LIVE-itW, with the largest margin on PIPAL (PLCC 0.4252 vs. 0.3698 for RankIQA, the next best). On image deraining (Rain100L) and deblurring (GoPro), SFD improves LPIPS and DISTS over both the NAFNet fidelity baseline and the vanilla-discriminator variant while maintaining comparable PSNR, confirming generalizability beyond SISR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GAN-based perceptual SR line (SRGAN, ESRGAN, SED) by shifting the discrimination target from raw images or pixel-level features to CLIP's semantic feature space. It builds directly on CLIP-IQA's cosine-similarity scoring with text prompts and RecycleD's discriminator-recycling paradigm for IQA, but departs from both by introducing a dedicated feature discriminator (Feat-D) on CLIP's multi-scale middle features and a text-guided adversarial mechanism (TG-D) with learnable prompt pairs on the global output feature. The closest prior work, SED (CVPR 2024), also incorporates semantic features into a discriminator, but SFD performs discrimination entirely within CLIP's feature space rather than augmenting a vanilla image discriminator, and adds the text-guided global discrimination that SED lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that the trained Feat-D and LPP can be repurposed for opinion-unaware NR-IQA without any task-specific IQA training, yielding state-of-the-art results across SR and authentically distorted benchmarks — a convenient and well-motivated byproduct of adversarial semantic discrimination. However, the method's reliance on CLIP as a frozen feature extractor raises open questions about generalizability to other foundation models or to domains where CLIP's semantic priors are weak (e.g., medical or satellite imagery). The SR fidelity gains are modest and the t-SNE evidence is limited to 8 categories × 50 images, so broader validation of the semantic-awareness claim would strengthen the contribution.
