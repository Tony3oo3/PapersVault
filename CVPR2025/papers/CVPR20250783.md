## Problem

Existing deep multi-view clustering methods produce biased results where sensitive attributes (gender, marital status, etc.) disproportionately drive cluster assignments. Prior fair MVC approaches either enforce a uniform sensitive-attribute distribution per cluster—creating an artificial performance-fairness trade-off—or rely on CNN/MLP backbones that cannot efficiently approximate the complex nonlinear inter-view relationships needed for both accuracy and fairness simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes V tabular data matrices X^v ∈ R^{N×d_v} (views generated by applying distinct nonlinear transforms to a single-view source) together with a sensitive attribute matrix S ∈ R^{N×d_s}. It outputs per-view soft cluster probability matrices H^v ∈ R^{N×K}, a unified latent embedding Z ∈ R^{N×d_z}, and final hard labels obtained by argmax over the view-averaged assignment probabilities. The fair clustering module additionally produces a subgroup-normalized target distribution P that guides the soft assignments Q toward equitable sensitive-attribute representation.

## Method

DFMVC-AKAN comprises three modules trained jointly under L_total = L_r + λL_c + γL_f. The Attention KAN Learning Module uses view-specific encoder-decoder pairs in which a hybrid attention mechanism (Squeeze-and-Excitation channel recalibration fused with multi-head attention via a learnable scalar α) feeds into KAN layers that decompose multivariate nonlinearities into learnable univariate functions per the Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem, enabling efficient approximation of complex inter-feature relationships. The View-Contrastive Module maps each view's embedding through linear layers and softmax to yield per-view cluster probabilities, then enforces cross-view semantic consistency via a contrastive loss over positive pairs (same sample, different views) and negative pairs, augmented by an entropy regularizer to prevent cluster collapse. The Fair Clustering Module fuses view embeddings with learned importance weights, computes soft assignments via a Student's t-distribution, and defines a target distribution P that squares Q and normalizes within each sensitive-attribute subgroup, then minimizes KL(P‖Q) to align assignments with a subgroup-balanced target without forcing uniformity.

## Results

DFMVC-AKAN achieves the highest NMI on Bank Marketing (80.46%) and Zafar (99.98%), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline DFMVC (54.62% and 93.93% respectively). On Credit Card and Law School, the method attains the best BAL scores (39.98% and 46.01%) while trailing DFMVC by a narrow margin on NMI (34.84% vs. 35.13% and 23.64% vs. 24.24%). Across all four datasets, DFMVC-AKAN consistently achieves the top or near-top fairness (BAL) scores, confirming that the distribution-alignment module preserves clustering accuracy while improving equitable representation of sensitive attributes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFMVC-AKAN decisively outperforms all nine baselines on Bank Marketing (NMI 80.46 vs. DFMVC's 54.62, a 25.8-point gain) and Zafar (NMI 99.98 vs. 93.93), while also achieving the best BAL on both. On Credit Card and Law School, the method trades a small NMI deficit (≈0.3 and 0.6 points below DFMVC) for the highest BAL scores (39.98% and 46.01%), indicating a favorable fairness–accuracy trade-off. Among non-fair baselines, MFLVC is the strongest on Zafar NMI (90.52) but lags DFMVC-AKAN by nearly 10 points; DCP and APADC sacrifice fairness (BAL drops to 24–28%) to gain modest NMI on some datasets. The closest overall competitor is DFMVC [46], which uses a similar fairness constraint but relies on CNN/MLP encoders; the attention-KAN architecture and distribution-alignment module yield consistent gains across all four datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fair multi-view clustering line (DFMVC [46], Fair-MVC [48]) by replacing the standard MLP/CNN encoders with Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN) [22], motivated by KAN's theoretical guarantee of approximating arbitrary multivariate continuous functions via univariate components. It further departs from prior fair MVC methods that enforce a uniform distribution of sensitive attributes within each cluster by instead aligning soft cluster assignments to a group-normalized target distribution via KL divergence, which the authors argue avoids the performance-fairness trade-off. The hybrid attention mechanism (Squeeze-and-Excitation combined with multi-head attention) is a novel architectural choice applied to KAN layers, though each component is individually well-established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fair multi-view clustering is increasingly relevant in sensitive domains (healthcare, finance, education) where clustering outcomes can perpetuate bias, and this paper offers a principled alternative to the common 'equalize sensitive attributes per cluster' strategy. However, the experimental scope is narrow: all four datasets are tabular, views are synthetically generated by applying nonlinear functions to a single source, and only two views are used, limiting confidence that the KAN advantage generalizes to genuinely heterogeneous multi-view data (e.g., imaging modalities). Open questions include whether the distribution-alignment fairness mechanism scales to multiple sensitive attributes simultaneously, whether KAN's expressiveness advantage over MLPs persists at larger scale, and whether the 99.98% NMI on Zafar reflects genuine clustering or near-trivial separability.
