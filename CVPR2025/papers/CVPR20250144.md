## Problem

Existing virtual dressing methods are limited to single-garment conditioning and struggle to preserve fine-grained garment textures while remaining faithful to text prompts. When naively extended to multiple garments, they suffer from severe garment confusion and background contamination. This paper introduces the Multi-Garment Virtual Dressing task, where a character must be dressed in any arbitrary combination of garments according to a customized text prompt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250144/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N reference garment images (laid-out or segmented), a free-form text prompt, and optional control signals (ControlNet, IP-Adapter-FaceID, LoRA) as input, and produces a 768×576 RGB image of a character wearing all specified garments in the prompted scene. The GFE module scales to any number of garments by adding only small LoRA matrices per garment, and the method operates as a plug-in compatible with community extensions for Stable Diffusion v1.5.

## Method

AnyDressing comprises two networks: GarmentsNet and DressingNet. GarmentsNet uses a Garment-Specific Feature Extractor (GFE) that runs parallelized self-attention layers (each with garment-specific LoRA on Q/K/V projections) within a shared U-Net, individually encoding each garment's texture without cross-garment blending. DressingNet injects these features via a Dressing-Attention module (frozen self-attention plus learnable cross-attention) and an Instance-Level Garment Localization (IGL) loss that constrains each garment's attention map to its segmentation mask, preventing leakage into irrelevant regions. A Garment-Enhanced Texture Learning (GTL) strategy adds DISTS perceptual loss and Canny-based high-frequency loss at low-noise timesteps to sharpen fine patterns. The full system is trained end-to-end on 768×576 triplets (upper garment, lower garment, person image) initialized from Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net weights.

## Results

On the multi-garment Dressing-Pair benchmark, AnyDressing achieves CLIP-I* of 0.734 and DINO* of 0.674, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (IMAGDressing: 0.614/0.378; StableGarment: 0.556/0.412). On single-garment VITON-HD, it attains the best scores across all four metrics: CLIP-T 0.289, CLIP-I 0.741, CLIP-AS 5.881, and DINO 0.571. In a 40-participant user study, AnyDressing is preferred 93.80% of the time for texture consistency, 77.00% for prompt alignment, 71.80% for image quality, and 90.30% in comprehensive evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyDressing outperforms all four baselines (IP-Adapter, StableGarment, MagicClothing, IMAGDressing) on every metric across all three evaluation settings. The most dramatic gains appear in multi-garment dressing: DINO* improves from 0.412 (StableGarment) and 0.378 (IMAGDressing) to 0.674, a relative increase of ~63–82%, and CLIP-I* rises from 0.614 (IMAGDressing) to 0.734. In single-garment VITON-HD, the margins are smaller but consistent—AnyDressing leads on CLIP-I (0.741 vs. 0.734 for IMAGDressing), DINO (0.571 vs. 0.553), and CLIP-AS (5.881 vs. 5.781 for StableGarment). MagicClothing achieves the highest CLIP-T on the proprietary single-garment set (0.298 vs. 0.296), but trails significantly on all other metrics. The user study confirms these quantitative advantages, with AnyDressing preferred by 90.30% of participants in comprehensive evaluation versus at most 3.75% for MagicClothing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the virtual dressing (VD) line of work (StableGarment, IMAGDressing, MagicClothing, DreamFit), which has so far been restricted to single-garment conditioning, by formulating and solving the multi-garment variant. Architecturally, it departs from the full-U-Net ReferenceNet paradigm used by prior VD methods and instead draws on the LoRA-based lightweight encoder idea from DreamFit, but generalizes it via parallelized garment-specific self-attention layers within a shared U-Net (GFE). The Dressing-Attention mechanism is inspired by IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention, while the Instance-Level Garment Localization loss and Garment-Enhanced Texture Learning (DISTS + Canny) are novel additions not present in prior VD or subject-driven generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-garment virtual dressing is a natural and practically important extension for e-commerce and creative design, and AnyDressing's plug-in compatibility with ControlNet, IP-Adapter, and community LoRAs makes it broadly integrable into existing Stable Diffusion pipelines without retraining. A key limitation is the reliance on segmentation masks during training (for IGL and GTL losses) and the use of a proprietary dataset, which limits full reproducibility. Open questions include scalability to very large garment counts, extension to video or 3D-consistent generation, and whether the IGL localization strategy generalizes to occluded or heavily overlapping garments.
