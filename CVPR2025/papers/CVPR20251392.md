## Problem

Current T2I foundation models are designed exclusively for generation and require task-specific plugins (ControlNet, IP-Adapter) or input-channel expansion for editing, fragmenting deployment and preventing joint multi-task training. Additionally, creating accurate, high-quality paired data for instruction-based and drag-based editing is extremely inefficient—existing pipelines like InstructPix2Pix achieve under 15% success rate with frequent artifacts—making large-scale editing data infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251392/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous inputs through a single Qwen2-VL 7B encoder: plain text prompts for T2I, source image + text instruction for editing, source image + binary mask for inpainting/outpainting, source image + normalized drag-point tuples (x, y, dx, dy) for drag editing, and reference/condition images (canny, depth, segmentation maps) + text for conditioned generation. Outputs are images at 256×256 to 1024×1024 across 31 aspect-ratio buckets (4:1 to 1:4), plus segmentation masks and bounding boxes for the detection task. For consistency-critical tasks (instruction editing, drag editing), the VAE encoding of the source image is additionally fed into the DIT to preserve non-edited regions.

## Method

DreamOmni is a 2.5B-parameter DIT where Qwen2-VL features are concatenated with the noisy latent and processed through joint multi-head self-attention, followed by separate FeedForward modules for the VLM and latent streams. The architecture concentrates DIT block computation at 2× downsampled latent (rather than 4× as in UNet), adds residual Conv blocks for 1× latent detail refinement, and incorporates UNet-style skip connections that accelerate convergence ~4× over SD3-Medium. Training uses Rectified Flow with the FLUX-schnell VAE (more latent channels for finer detail) in three stages (256→512→1024 resolution). The key data contribution is a synthetic collage pipeline that composites sticker-like objects onto canvases to generate precise, diverse editing pairs—addition/removal/replacement for instruction editing, translation/scaling/rotation for drag editing, random masks for inpainting, canny/depth/segmentation maps for conditioned generation, and sticker canvases for subject-driven generation—enabling ~60M synthetic images alongside 125M T2I images for native multi-task training.

## Results

On GenEval, DreamOmni achieves an overall score of 0.70, matching SD3-Medium (0.70) while surpassing SDXL (0.55), SD 1.5 (0.43), and PixArt-alpha (0.48). DreamOmni leads on Position (0.34 vs. 0.28 for SD3-Medium) and Counting (0.65 vs. 0.63), though it trails slightly on Two-object (0.81 vs. 0.84) and Color Attribution (0.54 vs. 0.55). For inpainting, DreamOmni achieves FID 0.84 and LPIPS 0.12, substantially outperforming SD-inpainting (FID 1.35, LPIPS 0.16) and ControlNet-inpainting (FID 1.84, LPIPS 0.16); for outpainting, it reaches FID 1.69 and LPIPS 0.20 versus 2.92/0.25 and 4.23/0.25 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, DreamOmni ties SD3-Medium (0.70 overall) but wins on Position (0.34 vs. 0.28) and Counting (0.65 vs. 0.63), while losing narrowly on Two-object (0.81 vs. 0.84) and Color Attribution (0.54 vs. 0.55); it clearly outperforms SDXL (0.55), SD 1.5 (0.43), and PixArt-alpha (0.48). For inpainting and outpainting, DreamOmni achieves FID 0.84/1.69, beating SD-inpainting (1.35/2.92) by ~38%/42% and ControlNet-inpainting (1.84/4.23) by ~54%/60% on FID. On subject-driven generation, DreamOmni qualitatively outperforms both BLIP-Diffusion and IP-Adapter in prompt adherence and subject preservation across photo and anime domains. For instruction-based editing, DreamOmni produces more precise additions, removals, and replacements with better non-edited-region consistency compared to MGIE and InstructP2P. For image-conditioned generation (canny, depth, segmentation), DreamOmni surpasses ControlNet in both condition fidelity and visual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamOmni builds on the DIT (Diffusion Transformer) architecture and Rectified Flow training objective, but departs from the fragmented ecosystem of image editing where each task (inpainting, instruction editing, drag editing, reference generation) requires a separate plugin (ControlNet, IP-Adapter) or channel extension (InstructP2P, SD-inpainting). The key architectural departure is replacing CLIP/T5 text encoders with a VLM (Qwen2-VL) that jointly encodes image and text prompts, concatenated with the noisy latent for unified self-attention. The synthetic collage data pipeline represents a conceptual shift from prior editing data creation (e.g., InstructP2P's GPT-3 + Prompt-to-Prompt with <15% success rate, or MagicBrush's manual annotation) toward programmatically generated, precisely paired editing data at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of T2I generation with six editing tasks in a single 2.5B-parameter model directly addresses the deployment complexity that plagues current pipelines requiring multiple specialized plugins. The synthetic collage pipeline offers a practical, cost-efficient path to scaling editing data that was previously bottlenecked by manual annotation or low-success-rate generation. Open questions include whether the sticker-based synthetic data generalizes to more complex, semantically rich editing scenarios beyond geometric operations, and whether the 2.5B parameter scale is sufficient for competitive performance on all tasks simultaneously as model sizes grow. The paper also notes that large-scale rotation in drag editing still causes object deformation, indicating limits of the current approach.
