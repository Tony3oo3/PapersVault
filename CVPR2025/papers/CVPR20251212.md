## Problem

Video diffusion models show promise for generating 3D-consistent scenes from sparse views, but suffer from two critical limitations: (1) they require 50+ denoising steps, making inference far slower than feed-forward 3D methods, and (2) they are trained on 2D video data without explicit 3D geometric constraints, producing structural inconsistencies and unrealistic motion that hinder downstream 3D reconstruction. No existing approach efficiently bridges the gap between video generation and reliable 3D scene synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251212/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two input RGB images (720×480) with corresponding camera poses (estimable via COLMAP or DUSt3R) and produces a 49-frame novel-view video along an interpolated camera trajectory between the two views. The output is a 3D-consistent video suitable for downstream 3D reconstruction, generated in a single forward pass (~2.5s for video generation plus ~0.5s for the initial 3DGS rendering). The two-view input is chosen because monocular reconstruction is inherently ill-posed, while two-view geometry enables triangulation and serves as the fundamental building block for multi-view extension.

## Method

VideoScene introduces a 3D-aware leap flow distillation strategy that modifies consistency distillation by starting from a 3D-rendered video (produced by MVSplat from the two input views) rather than pure noise, effectively leaping over the low-information early denoising steps near T. The student model is trained to map from a noised version of this 3D-rendered video (at an intermediate timestep t < T) directly to the clean target video in one step, using a modified consistency loss where the teacher estimates the intermediate state via an ODE solver. A dynamic denoising policy network (DDPNet), trained with contextual bandit policy gradient optimization, adaptively selects the optimal leap timestep at inference based on the quality of the rendered input—adding less noise for high-quality renders and more for artifact-laden ones. The backbone is CogVideoX-5B-I2V with only attention layers fine-tuned on RealEstate10K, and the entire pipeline (3DGS rendering + one-step video generation) completes in under 3 seconds.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, VideoScene with a single denoising step achieves an FVD of 103.42, dramatically outperforming all baselines at the same 1-step setting (CogVideoX-5B: 753.02, DynamiCrafter: 846.85, Stable Video Diffusion: 1220.80) and even surpassing their 50-step results (424.68–521.04). Its 1-step Aesthetic Quality (0.5416), Subject Consistency (0.9259), and Background Consistency (0.9461) are also the best among all 1-step entries. Inference takes under 3 seconds total (~0.5 s for 3DGS rendering + ~2.5 s for one-step video generation), compared to over 2 minutes for the 50-step CogVideoX baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoScene decisively outperforms all three baselines at every step count. At 1 step, its FVD of 103.42 is roughly 7× lower than CogVideoX-5B (753.02), 8× lower than DynamiCrafter (846.85), and 12× lower than Stable Video Diffusion (1220.80); it also beats each baseline's own 50-step FVD (424.68, 458.27, 521.04 respectively). On Aesthetic Quality at 1 step, VideoScene scores 0.5416 versus 0.3987 (CogVideoX), 0.3737 (DynamiCrafter), and 0.2981 (SVD), a gap of 0.14–0.24 points. In the cross-dataset generalization test (trained on RealEstate10K, evaluated on ACID), VideoScene's 1-step FVD of 121.93 again far exceeds the fine-tuned DynamiCrafter (595.87) and CogVideoX (464.87) at 1 step, and is comparable to their 50-step results (96.10 and 114.04). For geometric consistency, VideoScene produces the highest number of correctly matched epipolar points in the structure-matching evaluation (Fig. 5), confirming superior 3D coherence over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoScene extends the consistency distillation framework (Song et al., 2023; Luo et al., 2023) by replacing the standard pure-noise starting point with a 3D-rendered video from a feed-forward 3DGS model (MVSplat), effectively 'leaping' over the low-information early denoising steps. It further departs from prior video-for-3D work (ReconX, ViewCrafter, DimensionX) that relies on multi-step video diffusion by distilling the process into a single forward pass, and introduces a contextual-bandit policy network (DDPNet) to adaptively select the noise level—a reinforcement-learning component not present in prior consistency or distillation methods. The approach sits at the intersection of latent consistency models, 3D Gaussian splatting, and video diffusion, but the key conceptual departure is using 3D geometric structure as the distillation anchor rather than ground-truth video frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50× speedup (2 minutes → ~3 seconds) while maintaining near-50-step quality (FVD 103 vs. 99) makes video-diffusion-based 3D scene generation practically viable for interactive applications, a regime previously inaccessible. The ablation showing a 5.6× FVD degradation without the 3D-aware leap flow (97.53 → 543.53) strongly validates that geometric priors are the critical ingredient, not merely the distillation itself. However, the method is currently limited to two input views and indoor scenes (RealEstate10K), and its quality is bounded by the coarse MVSplat reconstruction; open questions include scaling to multi-view inputs, outdoor/unbounded scenes, and whether the leap-flow strategy transfers to newer, stronger video backbones as the authors themselves note.
