## Problem

Existing multi-agent SLAM systems with novel view synthesis capabilities are limited to two agents, computationally slow, and unable to accurately render real-world data due to the use of neural scene representations that inherently lack support for rigid body transformations. This makes efficient map correction and merging across agents impractical, while tracking accuracy remains limited by trajectory drift and discrepancies across agents' observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250862/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes single-camera RGBD video streams from an arbitrary number of simultaneously operating agents, processing each stream in fixed-size sub-maps (θ_submap frames). Only Gaussians with zero rendered opacity in the current camera frustum are transmitted to the centralized server, significantly reducing disk storage. The output consists of per-agent camera trajectories, a globally consistent 3D Gaussian map, and photorealistic novel view renderings with corresponding depth maps from previously unseen viewpoints.

## Method

MAGiC-SLAM uses 3D Gaussians as the scene representation, which natively supports rigid body transformations for efficient map correction and merging. Tracking employs a hybrid implicit approach: a deterministic frame-to-frame colored ICP registration provides robust pose initialization, followed by frame-to-model re-rendering optimization with soft alpha and error masking for refinement. Loop closure leverages a DINOv2 foundational vision model for feature extraction and loop detection (replacing NetVLAD for better generalization), FPFH+RANSAC+ICP for loop constraint estimation, and pose graph optimization to correct accumulated drift across all agents. A coarse-to-fine global map merging strategy appends sub-maps and then refines them with color/depth rendering losses to eliminate visual and geometric artifacts at sub-map boundaries.

## Results

On the ReplicaMultiagent dataset (2 agents), MAGiC-SLAM achieves an average ATE RMSE of 0.25 cm, outperforming CP-SLAM (1.23 cm avg), Swarm-SLAM (3.60 cm avg), and CCM-SLAM (which fails on multiple sequences). On the real-world AriaMultiagent dataset (3 agents), MAGiC-SLAM reaches 0.90 cm average ATE RMSE versus 2.95 cm for CP-SLAM and 5.62 cm for Swarm-SLAM. For rendering, MAGiC-SLAM achieves 34.26 dB PSNR and 0.97 SSIM on ReplicaMultiagent training views (vs. 22.71 dB / 0.69 for CP-SLAM) and 22.61 dB PSNR on AriaMultiagent novel views (vs. 9.06 dB for CP-SLAM). The system is also dramatically faster: 0.69 s/frame tracking and 1.12 GiB peak GPU vs. 3.36 s/frame and 7.70 GiB for CP-SLAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGiC-SLAM outperforms all multi-agent baselines on both datasets. Against CP-SLAM (the only prior multi-agent NVS-capable SLAM), MAGiC-SLAM reduces ATE RMSE by roughly 3–4× on ReplicaMultiagent (0.25 vs. 1.23 cm avg) and by ~3× on AriaMultiagent (0.90 vs. 2.95 cm), while also achieving far superior rendering (34.26 vs. 22.71 dB PSNR on training views; 22.61 vs. 9.06 dB on novel views). Against Swarm-SLAM, the gap is even larger (0.25 vs. 3.60 cm on Replica; 0.90 vs. 5.62 cm on Aria), and CCM-SLAM fails entirely on AriaMultiagent and on the Apt-0 Replica sequence. Compared to single-agent systems, MAGiC-SLAM beats MonoGS (1.15 cm avg on Replica) and ORB-SLAM3 (1.99 cm) by a wide margin, and avoids the catastrophic failures of Gaussian-SLAM (38.21 cm avg on Replica due to trajectory divergence). The loop closure module provides an additional ~2× improvement over the no-loop-closure variant (0.25 vs. 0.50 cm on Replica; 0.90 vs. 1.69 cm on Aria), confirming that multi-agent information sharing is critical for global consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGiC-SLAM extends the single-agent 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM line (Gaussian-SLAM, SplatAM, MonoGS) into a multi-agent setting by introducing sub-map-based mapping, a centralized server for loop closure, and a coarse-to-fine global map merging strategy. It departs from the only prior multi-agent NVS-capable SLAM (CP-SLAM) by replacing the distributed neural scene representation with 3D Gaussians, which natively support rigid body transformations and enable efficient map correction and merging. For tracking, it combines a deterministic colored point-cloud registration (Park et al.) for pose initialization with implicit re-rendering refinement, and for loop detection it substitutes NetVLAD descriptors with DINOv2 features for better generalization to unseen environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first multi-agent NVS-capable SLAM system that renders real-world data at high fidelity and scales beyond two agents, addressing a clear gap left by CP-SLAM. The 24× speedup in mapping and 8.7× reduction in peak VRAM over the prior state of the art make multi-agent Gaussian SLAM practically deployable. However, the system still operates at only ~1 FPS due to the iterative implicit tracking, and the authors note that faster convergence without accuracy loss remains an open problem. Broader questions include how the approach handles dynamic scenes, larger agent counts under bandwidth constraints, and whether the centralized server architecture can be made more distributed.
