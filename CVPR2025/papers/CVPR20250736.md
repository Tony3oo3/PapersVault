## Problem

Neural 3D representations like NeRF and 3DGS can accurately reconstruct visible external geometry from multiview images but cannot identify invisible internal structures hidden behind the surface. Two objects with different internal cavities appear identical in static images, making standard reconstruction methods unable to distinguish them. This paper introduces Structure from Collision (SfC), a new task that exploits the fact that collision-induced deformation reveals internal structure, and proposes SfC-NeRF to solve this ill-posed inverse problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250736/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multiview video sequence (11 viewpoints on an upper hemisphere) of a deformable object falling and colliding with a known ground plane, along with known material properties (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, density) and object mass. Camera intrinsics/extrinsics are assumed known, and video matting isolates the object from the background. The output is a 3D particle set at the initial time t₀ representing both the external surface and internal cavities, where each particle carries a position and an alpha (opacity) value; evaluation is via chamfer distance between predicted and ground-truth particles.

## Method

SfC-NeRF builds on PAC-NeRF's pipeline (continuum NeRF + DiffMPM + particle-grid interconversion) but shifts the optimization target from physical properties to the initial volume density field σ^{G′}(t₀), which encodes the internal structure. A two-step procedure first learns the filled external structure from the first frame, then optimizes the internal structure across the full video under four constraints: (1) physical constraints via DiffMPM with known material properties plus a logarithmic mass loss, (2) appearance-preserving constraints combining pixel and depth losses on the first frame with periodic re-optimization of that frame, (3) a keyframe loss that upweights the frame immediately after collision where shape changes are most informative, and (4) volume annealing, which alternates volume reduction with volume expansion (via successive G2P/P2G conversions) to escape local optima in the ill-posed solution space.

## Results

On the SfC dataset (115 objects, 5 external shapes), SfC-NeRF achieves an average chamfer distance (CD ×10³) of 0.165 across cavity sizes, substantially outperforming the best baseline LPO (0.656) and GO (0.730). For cavity location estimation, SfC-NeRF achieves an average CD of 0.281 (with ACD of 0.416 confirming correct positional bias), compared to 0.779 for LPO and 0.869 for GO. In the future-prediction task, SfC-NeRF achieves PSNR 26.60 and SSIM 0.981, surpassing PAC-NeRF (PSNR 23.44, SSIM 0.975) which assumes a filled interior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SfC-NeRF consistently outperforms both adapted baselines (GO and LPO from [31]) across all cavity sizes and locations. On the primary cavity-size experiment, SfC-NeRF achieves an average CD of 0.165, a 4.0× improvement over LPO (0.656) and a 4.4× improvement over GO (0.730); the gap widens dramatically for larger cavities (e.g., at s_c=(3/4)³, SfC-NeRF scores 0.262 vs. 1.406 for LPO). Adding mass loss to the baselines (GO_mass, LPO_mass) does not help and sometimes hurts, confirming that appearance-preserving mechanisms are essential. In the future-prediction experiment, SfC-NeRF (which optimizes internal structure with fixed physical properties) beats PAC-NeRF [35] (which optimizes physical properties with a fixed filled interior) by 3.16 dB in PSNR (26.60 vs. 23.44) and 0.006 in SSIM (0.981 vs. 0.975), demonstrating that recovering the true internal structure is more beneficial for downstream prediction than recovering physical parameters on a filled object.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly inverts the optimization target of PAC-NeRF (Li et al., ICLR 2023): where PAC-NeRF recovers physical properties assuming a filled interior, SfC-NeRF recovers the internal cavity structure assuming known physical properties. It reuses PAC-NeRF's continuum NeRF + DiffMPM + particle-grid interconverter pipeline and the voxel-based NeRF (Sun et al., CVPR 2022) for Eulerian rendering, but introduces four new components (mass loss, appearance-preserving losses/training, keyframe loss, and volume annealing) to handle the ill-posedness of recovering hidden geometry. The paper positions itself against prior reverse-engineering methods (NeuroFluid, ParticleNeRF, etc.) that either assume translucent objects or filled interiors, explicitly targeting the unexplored case of nontransparent objects with internal cavities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SfC opens a genuinely new direction in neural 3D representation learning: recovering invisible internal structure from dynamic observations, with direct implications for AR/VR object simulation, robotic force control, and physics-based interaction. However, the method is validated only on 115 simulated objects, assumes known material properties, and requires objects to deform during collision (ruling out rigid metals). The ill-posed nature means perfect recovery is unattainable—the best results capture only the 'bias in the location of the holes'—and the practical gap to real-world deployment (sensor noise, unknown materials, multi-object scenes) remains substantial. Future work on real-data validation, material-specific strategies, and handling non-deforming objects are needed before this becomes practically useful.
