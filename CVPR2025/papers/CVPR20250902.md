## Problem

The ability of both specialized composition models and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to embed and understand photographic composition remains underexplored, primarily due to the absence of suitable evaluation datasets. Existing composition datasets suffer from limited category coverage, noisy labels, small scale, and insufficient scene diversity, making it impossible to reliably assess whether models truly capture compositional structure or merely exploit semantic shortcuts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250902/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For specialized models, pre-trained networks (CNNs, Transformers, GNNs) process photographic RGB images to produce composition embedding vectors, which are then evaluated via triplet distance comparisons, retrieval against a gallery of 36,857 images, and clustering quality metrics (Silhouette Score, Davies-Bouldin Index). For MLLMs, the input is one or three RGB images paired with a text prompt (e.g., "Which of these three images has a composition that differs from the other two?"), and the model outputs a selection, an element count, or an arrangement label from a fixed vocabulary of six predefined arrangements. The underlying label system is structured along two axes—element type (point, line, shape) and spatial arrangement (12 patterns)—yielding 24 optimized composition categories.

## Method

The paper introduces PICD, a 36,857-image dataset spanning 24 composition categories and 355 scenes, constructed via a multi-stage pipeline: script-assisted candidate filtering using object detection (YOLOv8) and semantic line detection to identify point/line/shape elements, keyword-based search for hard-to-capture categories (perspective, radial, geometric), and five-expert voting requiring at least three consensus annotations per image, with a Places365-trained scene-classification network actively balancing scene diversity during annotation. On top of PICD, the authors design a four-task benchmark for each model type: composition triplet distinction (CDA metric), robustness to semantic interference (where the negative sample shares object semantics with a positive), composition retrieval (mAP@k), and feature distribution analysis (Silhouette/DBI) for specialized models, plus element counting and arrangement recognition for MLLMs. The key metric, Composition Discrimination Accuracy (CDA), is a lightweight three-choice accuracy that the authors validate as a proxy for retrieval performance via a Spearman correlation of 0.758 (p=0.011) with mAP@10, making it practical for MLLMs where composition retrieval is infeasible.

## Results

On the PICD benchmark, all ten specialized models achieve avg-CDA₁ below 0.5 (best: MUSIQ at 0.488, GANC at 0.478), indicating limited composition discrimination ability. All MLLMs score near random chance (1/3) on composition triplet distinction, with Qwen-VL-max (0.462) and Idefics2 (0.458) performing best. Semantic interference consistently degrades performance: avg-CDA₂ drops below avg-CDA₁ for every model in both categories. Feature distribution analysis confirms poor embedding quality, with all specialized models showing negative Silhouette scores (range: −0.234 to −0.010) and DBI values exceeding 10, while the CDA metric correlates strongly with mAP@10 (Spearman ρ = 0.758, p = 0.011).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among specialized models, aesthetic-evaluation methods (MUSIQ, EAT) and cropping-based methods (GANC, S2C) outperform composition-classification and composition-assessment methods on avg-CDA₁, with MUSIQ (0.488) and GANC (0.478) leading, likely due to larger training data and more effective supervision. GANC also achieves the best robustness to semantic interference (avg-CDA₂ = 0.445) and the lowest DBI (6.493), suggesting its graph-based architecture captures composition more cleanly. Among MLLMs, Qwen-VL-max (0.462) and Idefics2 (0.458) lead on composition triplet distinction, while MiniCPM (0.421) and Qwen-VL-max (0.422) perform best on arrangement recognition (Acc_t). However, all MLLMs remain near or below random chance on element counting (Acc_n), with the best being XComposer2.5 at 0.504. The paper's central finding is that no model—specialized or MLLM—achieves reliable composition understanding, and all are susceptible to semantic interference, with avg-CDA₂ consistently lower than avg-CDA₁ across every evaluated model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first comprehensive dataset (PICD) and unified benchmark specifically targeting composition understanding as a distinct capability, separate from the broader aesthetic assessment task. It departs from prior composition datasets (KUPCP, CADB, LODB) by addressing their simultaneous limitations in scale, category coverage, label quality, and scene diversity, and extends the evaluation scope from specialized embedding models to MLLMs—a model class previously unevaluated on composition. The CDA metric repurposes the triplet-discrimination paradigm from metric learning into a lightweight proxy for retrieval performance, bridging the evaluation gap between the two model families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that both specialized models and MLLMs perform near-randomly on composition discrimination, confirming that composition has been an undertrained and under-evaluated dimension in visual AI. The semantic interference finding (CDA2 consistently below CDA1) highlights a concrete failure mode—tight coupling of semantic and compositional information in learned embeddings—that motivates explicit compositional element modeling. Open questions include whether knowledge distillation from specialized composition models can improve MLLM composition understanding, and whether explicit element-and-arrangement architectures aligned with human cognition can break the current performance ceiling.
