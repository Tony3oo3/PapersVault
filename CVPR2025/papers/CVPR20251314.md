## Problem

Existing omnidirectional depth estimation methods from fisheye cameras face a fundamental trade-off: spherical sweeping approaches (OmniMVS+, CrownConv360) achieve high accuracy but are too slow for real-time use, while faster methods (FastOmniMVS, Sphere-Stereo) sacrifice accuracy in complex scenes. Epipolarity-based methods like MODE improve efficiency but overlook projection-mapping errors and depth discontinuities during multi-view fusion, and all prior approaches struggle to generalize to unseen environments without prior depth information.

## Input / Output

The method takes four fisheye images (forming four stereo pairs in different directions) as input, which are first rectified into Cassini projection images to establish horizontal epipolar alignment. The output is a complete omnidirectional depth map in equirectangular projection (ERP) domain, evaluated at resolutions up to 1920×960, along with per-pixel confidence maps used internally for fusion. The system is designed to run in real-time, achieving 35 FPS on TITAN RTX and 12.3 FPS on Jetson AGX Orin at 640×320.

## Method

OmniStereo is a four-stage pipeline: (1) Cassini projection rectifies fisheye images to restore horizontal epipolar lines, simplifying photometric matching; (2) an ultra-lightweight 1.8M-parameter stereo matching network (U-Net inspired) constructs a compact attention volume by filtering a concatenation volume with GwcNet-derived attention weights, then upsamples via CUBG bilateral grid to produce coarse depth and confidence maps; (3) a confidence-guided fusion module combines multi-view depth maps in ERP domain using softmax-weighted confidence scores to mitigate discontinuities, followed by an encoder-decoder network (without normalization, using ReLU) to fill invalid pixels from neighborhood information; (4) a refinement network samples a small set of depth hypotheses around each pixel, warps input image features onto the sphere via the fisheye camera model to build a compact 4D cost volume, and applies softargmin to recover fine details lost during projection and fusion. The entire network is trained end-to-end with Smooth L1 losses at each stage using dynamically weighted coefficients.

## Results

On the Urban dataset, OmniStereo achieves MAE 6.57 and RMSE 60.26, surpassing the second-best method (OmniMVS+, MAE 12.14) by 46% in MAE and 35% in RMSE, while running 16.6× faster (28.5 ms vs. 472.2 ms). On OmniHouse, OmniStereo reaches MAE 1.76 and RMSE 21.66 (vs. OmniMVS+ at 2.59/27.54), and on OmniThings it achieves MAE 31.66 and RMSE 137.10 (vs. Fast-OmniMVS at 83.10/217.54). The method runs at 35 FPS on TITAN RTX and 12.3 FPS on Jetson AGX Orin at 640×320 resolution, confirming real-time capability on both high-end and embedded hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniStereo outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three primary datasets. Against the most accurate prior methods (OmniMVS+ and MODE), OmniStereo reduces MAE by 46% on Urban (6.57 vs. 12.14) and by 32% on OmniThings (31.66 vs. 99.52 for MODE), while being 12–16× faster. Against the fastest prior method (Fast-OmniMVS, 13–14 ms), OmniStereo is roughly 2× slower (28 ms) but achieves 61% lower MAE on Urban (6.57 vs. 23.29) and 62% lower MAE on OmniThings (31.66 vs. 83.10). Spherical-sweeping methods (CrownConv360, Sphere-Stereo, OmniMVS+Tiny) show the largest accuracy gaps, with Sphere-Stereo degrading most severely on OmniThings (MAE 111.26) and OmniTown (MAE 171.62), confirming their reliance on prior depth ranges. On the unseen OmniTown dataset, OmniStereo (MAE 13.28) still beats the second-best MODE (MAE 21.80) by 39%, while all spherical-sweeping baselines degrade to MAE > 140, highlighting OmniStereo's superior cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniStereo builds on the epipolarity-based reconstruction line initiated by MODE [15], which first demonstrated that Cassini projection can reestablish horizontal epipolar lines in fisheye stereo pairs, and on OmniVidar [30], which reformulated fisheye depth as a classic stereo task. However, it departs sharply from MODE's heavy two-stage network by borrowing cost-volume construction ideas from perspective stereo matching (ACVNet's attention-filtered concatenation volume, GwcNet's group-wise correlation, CUBG's bilateral-grid upsampling) to build a 1.8M-parameter stereo matcher. The paper also explicitly positions against spherical sweeping methods (OmniMVS+, CrownConv360, FastOmniMVS), arguing their reliance on prior depth ranges and limited candidate counts fundamentally constrain accuracy in unfamiliar scenes, and introduces confidence-guided fusion and a neighborhood-sampling refinement module that prior epipolarity methods (MODE, OmniVidar) lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of SOTA accuracy (32%+ MAE improvement) with 12.3 FPS on Jetson AGX Orin makes omnidirectional depth estimation viable on embedded platforms for autonomous driving and drone navigation, where LiDAR is expensive or unavailable. The strong generalization to the unseen OmniTown dataset validates the conceptual choice of epipolarity-based reconstruction over spherical sweeping, suggesting that methods not anchored to prior depth ranges will be more robust in deployment. Open questions include whether the limited neighborhood sampling in the refinement module scales to very large depth discontinuities (e.g., indoor-outdoor transitions), how the method handles dynamic objects, and whether the four-camera configuration can be extended to denser arrays without proportional cost increases.
