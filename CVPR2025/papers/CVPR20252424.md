## Problem

Current sparse-point monocular 3D lane detection methods generate training ground truth by truncating the original lane at preset point boundaries, causing up to 20 m of unrecoverable distance loss that makes predictions inherently incomplete. Additionally, existing architectures underutilize the geometric structure of lanes—adjacent-point smoothness, inter-lane relationships, and same-depth correlations—relying on insufficient constraints like simple parallelism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252424/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image (1280×1920 from the OpenLane/Waymo benchmark) and outputs M uniformly spaced 3D lane points per lane, each with (x, y, z) coordinates, a binary visibility flag, and a lane category label. The key structural addition is a pair of 3D patching distance vectors (start and end) per preset point, which at inference are added to the first and last valid points to extend the predicted lane back to its true original extent, recovering the truncated distance.

## Method

The paper introduces two plug-in modules. First, the EP-head is a lightweight MLP that predicts, for every preset point, the 3D displacement to the true start and end of the original ground-truth lane; at inference these displacements are applied only to the first and last valid points, yielding a complete lane even with as few as 10 preset points. Second, PL-attention restructures self-attention into three geometrically motivated sub-operations: Point-Point attention models smoothness within a single lane, Lane-Lane attention (operating on per-lane [CLS] tokens) captures inter-lane context, and Point-Y attention groups points at the same y-coordinate across lanes to exploit shared road geometry. The combined complexity is O(NM² + N² + MN²), lower than full self-attention over all points. Both modules are drop-in replacements that integrate into existing backbones (LATR, PersFormer, Anchor3DLane) without architectural overhaul, improving F1 by 2.8–4.4 points across baselines.

## Results

On the OpenLane validation set, the proposed EP-head + PL-attention applied to LATR achieves a new state-of-the-art F1-score of 64.7 (vs. 61.9 baseline) and 92.8% accuracy, with X-error of 0.205 m (near) / 0.255 m (far) and Z-error of 0.074 m (near) / 0.105 m (far). Applied to Persformer, the method improves F1 from 50.5 to 54.9 (+4.4) and reduces X-error from 0.485 to 0.311 m (near). Applied to Anchor3DLane, F1 improves from 53.1 to 56.3 (+3.2) with X-error dropping from 0.300 to 0.257 m (near).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper applies its EP-head + PL-attention modules on top of three strong baselines (PersFormer, Anchor3DLane, LATR) and consistently improves all of them: PersFormer gains +4.4 F1 (50.5→54.9), Anchor3DLane gains +3.2 F1 (53.1→56.3), and LATR gains +2.8 F1 (61.9→64.7), while also reducing X and Z positional errors across near and far ranges. Against the remaining six baselines listed in Table 3 (3D-LaneNet, Gen-LaneNet, CurveFormer, BEV-LaneDet, GroupLane, PVALane), the enhanced LATR achieves the highest F1-score (64.7) and lowest X-error (0.205 m near), surpassing PVALane (F1=62.7) by 2.0 points. In scenario-specific evaluation (Table 4), the largest gains appear in challenging conditions: curves (+3.3 for LATR), night (+3.1), intersections (+3.2), and merge/split (+4.0), confirming that the geometric priors in PL-attention and the completeness from EP-head are most beneficial in complex lane geometries. The method also shows stronger recall on short lanes (0–40 m), improving from 55.4% to 58.6% with EP-head alone, directly addressing the truncation flaw identified in the theoretical analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper identifies a fundamental representation flaw in the sparse-point lane detection paradigm shared by anchor-based methods (Anchor3DLane, 3D-LaneNet) and query-based methods (Persformer, LATR): the standard training ground truth generation truncates lanes at preset-point boundaries, losing up to 20 m of lane extent. While LATR (ICCV 2023) is used as the primary experimental framework and its self-attention is directly replaced by the proposed PL-attention, the conceptual contribution is broader—it critiques the GT generation pipeline common to all sparse-point methods and proposes a patching strategy with an EP-head as a drop-in fix. The PL-attention module extends the line of geometric-prior work (parallelism in Anchor3DLane, 3D positional embeddings in LATR) by structuring attention along three complementary axes: within-lane point interactions, cross-lane relationships, and same-y-coordinate groupings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the endpoint truncation flaw is practically significant for safety: a 20 m prediction error in lane extent could mislead trajectory planning or HD map construction. The patching strategy and EP-head offer a simple, architecture-agnostic fix that enables fewer preset points without accuracy loss, pointing toward more efficient future models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 2D lane detection and online HD map construction (as the authors suggest), whether the single-valid-point case can be handled gracefully, and whether the geometric priors in PL-attention generalize to non-standard road geometries (e.g., roundabouts, construction zones) not well represented in OpenLane.
