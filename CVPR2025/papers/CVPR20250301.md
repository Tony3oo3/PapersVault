## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on self-supervised learning either rely on visible triggers (e.g., colored patches) that are easily detected by human inspection, or use invisible triggers designed for supervised learning that achieve poor attack success rates in SSL. The paper identifies the root cause: the backdoor trigger transformation is distributionally entangled with the inherent augmentations (e.g., RandomGrayscale, ColorJitter) used in contrastive learning, causing the SSL encoder to treat backdoored samples as merely augmented versions of clean images.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained clean SSL encoder (ResNet18 by default), a shadow dataset of unlabeled images, a small set of reference images from the target downstream class, and the specific augmentation transforms used during SSL pre-training. It outputs a backdoored encoder and a U-Net-based trigger injector that produces sample-specific, imperceptible perturbations in HSV/HSL color space. Unlike fixed-patch or filter-based triggers, the injector generates a unique transformation per input image, and the downstream classifier (trained separately on the backdoored encoder) outputs the attacker-chosen target label for triggered inputs.

## Method

INACTIVE formulates backdoor injection as a joint optimization over a U-Net trigger injector and the encoder, guided by three loss terms. The key novelty is L_disentangle, which maximizes the ℓ₂ distance between backdoored and augmented samples in both HSV and HSL color spaces, explicitly pushing the trigger outside the learned augmentation space S_A' that the SSL encoder has internalized. L_alignment pulls feature embeddings of backdoored shadow images toward those of reference images from the target class (following BadEncoder), while L_stealthy constrains the trigger's imperceptibility via SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS, and Wasserstein distance between backdoored and clean images. A pre-training phase (Algorithm 2) initializes the injector using only L_disentangle and L_stealthy before the main alternating optimization (Algorithm 1) jointly updates injector and encoder. Theoretically, Theorem 3.1 proves that a perfectly-trained encoder cannot be backdoored with any trigger within its predefined augmentation space, motivating the need to escape this space.

## Results

INACTIVE achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) of 99.09% across six pre-training/downstream dataset pairs (STL10→CIFAR10, GTSRB, SVHN; CIFAR10→STL10, GTSRB, SVHN), while maintaining a benign accuracy (BA) of 73.10% versus a clean accuracy (CA) of 72.96%. On stealthiness, it attains 0.9633 SSIM, 35.86 dB PSNR, 0.00896 LPIPS, 0.969 FSIM, and 16.32 FID on CIFAR-10. On ImageNet, INACTIVE reaches 98.66% average ASR with 83.91% BA, far surpassing ISSBA (11.03% ASR). The method also achieves near-100% ASR (99.99–100.00%) under BYOL and SimSiam when pre-training and downstream datasets match.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INACTIVE outperforms all invisible-trigger baselines by a wide margin: WaNet achieves only 10.93% average ASR, CTRL 29.32%, Ins-Kelvin 25.90%, and Ins-Xpro2 21.46%, versus INACTIVE's 99.09%. The strongest visible-trigger baseline, DRUPE (patch trigger), reaches 96.89% ASR but has far worse stealthiness (SSIM 0.8355, PSNR 14.11) compared to INACTIVE (SSIM 0.9633, PSNR 35.86). Among data-poisoning methods (Table 2), INACTIVE achieves 100.00% ASR under SimCLR, 99.99% under BYOL and SimSiam, beating BLTO (91.27/94.78/84.63%), CTRL (85.30/61.90/74.90%), and SSLBKD (33.20/46.20/53.10%). On ImageNet, INACTIVE (98.66% ASR, 83.91% BA) dominates ISSBA (11.03% ASR, 75.51% BA) by over 87 percentage points in ASR. INACTIVE also evades all tested defenses: DECREE (PL¹-Norm > 0.1 threshold), Beatrix (<50% detection accuracy, near random), and ASSET (7.14% TPR), whereas BadEncoder is detected by Beatrix with >93% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BadEncoder threat model for training-time backdoor injection in SSL and extends the line of invisible-trigger research (WaNet, ISSBA, filter attacks) from supervised learning to self-supervised settings. Its key departure is identifying *why* these triggers fail in SSL: the learned augmentation space of contrastive learning absorbs small pixel-level perturbations, making backdoor samples indistinguishable from augmented positives. The proposed INACTIVE method introduces a disentangling loss in HSV/HSL color space to push the trigger outside this augmentation manifold, combined with a U-Net injector and multi-objective optimization (alignment, stealthiness, distributional gap) that has no direct analogue in prior SSL backdoor work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SSL pre-trained encoders become the backbone of foundation models, demonstrating that imperceptible backdoors can achieve ~99% ASR while evading all current SSL-specific defenses (DECREE, Beatrix, ASSET) raises the urgency of developing detection methods that do not rely on visible trigger anomalies. The conceptual finding that SSL augmentations create a 'safe harbor' for small perturbations is a useful diagnostic for understanding vulnerability boundaries in contrastive learning. Open questions include whether the attack transfers to non-contrastive SSL paradigms (e.g., masked image modeling), whether the U-Net injector's compute cost limits real-world deployment, and whether defenses can be designed around the feature-space distributional signature rather than pixel-space visibility.
