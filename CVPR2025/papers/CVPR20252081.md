## Problem

Existing multi-agent trajectory methods are limited to fixed-horizon forecasting and do not handle the broader trajectory completion task (imputation, inferring fully unobserved agents). They also provide no state-wise uncertainty estimates, and multi-modal sampling approaches lack per-mode error probability scores, making it impossible to rank generated scenes by reliability at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252081/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial trajectory tensor of shape T x N x 2 (x,y coordinates for N agents over T timesteps) together with a T x N binary mask marking observed vs. unobserved states, and produces K complete trajectory scenes, each annotated with a per-state variance map. A post-processing RankNN then assigns a softmax-normalized error probability to each of the K modes. The architecture is dimension-agnostic: T, N, and K are not fixed, allowing the same model to handle forecasting, in-between imputation, and full-agent inference under a single framework.

## Method

U2Diff is a DDPM-based generative model whose denoising backbone decouples temporal processing (bidirectional MambaSSM, termed Temporal Mamba) from social processing (Transformer Encoder, termed Social Transformer) within two residual blocks, enabling variable-length and variable-agent inputs. The key training innovation augments the standard epsilon-prediction MSE loss with a negative log-likelihood term on the predicted noise distribution (mean and standard deviation), with a stop-gradient on the mean so the NLL exclusively trains the variance head. During DDIM sampling, the learned noise variance is propagated to the real (x,y) state space via a closed-form variance rule that is activated only from denoising step s-hat=30 onward, mitigating unreliable early-step estimates. A separate supervised RankNN consumes the K generated modes, their state-wise variances, and the mask, processes them through Temporal Mamba, Social Transformer, and a Multi-scene Transformer over the K scenes, and outputs error probabilities trained to maximize Spearman correlation with the scene-level SADE, achieving median rho values of 0.58 to 0.78 across four sports datasets.

## Results

On trajectory completion, U2Diff achieves minADE20 of 4.65 (Basketball-U), 2.42 (Football-U), and 53.93 (Soccer-U), outperforming the strongest baseline UniTraj by 31% and 42% on Football-U and Soccer-U respectively. On scene-level minSADE20, U2Diff scores 3.13, 2.35, and 51.14 across the three datasets, a 27% improvement over UniTraj on Basketball-U. For NBA trajectory forecasting, U2Diff achieves state-of-the-art scene-level metrics (minSADE20 = 1.48, minSFDE20 = 2.68), surpassing the diffusion-based LED by over 9%, while ranking second on agent-wise minADE20 (0.85 vs. LED's 0.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

U2Diff outperforms all 14 baselines on trajectory completion across Basketball-U, Football-U, and Soccer-U. The closest competitor, UniTraj (ICLR 2025), is beaten by 31% on Football-U minADE20 (2.42 vs. 3.55) and 42% on Soccer-U (53.93 vs. 94.59), with a 27% gap on Basketball-U minSADE20 (3.13 vs. 4.29). Earlier methods (LSTM, Transformer, MAT, Naomi, INAM, SSSD, GC-VRNN) are all surpassed by large margins, with GC-VRNN being the strongest non-uniTraj baseline at 5.81/4.95/105.87. On NBA forecasting, U2Diff achieves the best scene-level metrics (minSADE20 = 1.48, minSFDE20 = 2.68), beating LED (1.63/2.99) by 9% and AutoBots (1.75/2.73) by 15%, though LED edges it slightly on agent-wise minADE20 (0.81 vs. 0.85). GroupNet and MID are outperformed on both agent-wise and scene-level metrics, while MemoNet and NPSN (which lack scene-level evaluation) are beaten on agent-wise scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

U2Diff adapts the CSDI diffusion framework for time-series imputation to the 2D multi-agent trajectory domain, replacing the Transformer encoder with a bidirectional MambaSSM for temporal processing and a Social Transformer for inter-agent interactions. The uncertainty estimation mechanism draws on Nichol et al.'s variance-learning in diffusion and BayesDiff's pixel-wise uncertainty propagation, but extends both to a state-wise, multi-agent setting via a novel NLL loss on the predicted noise. The RankNN post-processing module is a new contribution that departs from prior scene-level probability estimation (e.g., AutoBots' fixed-mode latent variables) by supporting a variable number of sampled modes under a shared prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap in multi-agent trajectory modeling by providing state-wise uncertainty and per-mode error probabilities, which are essential for downstream decision-making (e.g., selecting the most reliable generated scene). However, the uncertainty correlation with ground-truth error remains moderate (Spearman ρ medians of 0.25–0.35 for AvgUcty, 0.58–0.78 for RankNN), and RankNN requires supervised training on ground-truth SADE, limiting its use in truly unsupervised deployment. Evaluation is confined to four sports datasets, leaving open whether the unified completion framework and uncertainty propagation generalize to other multi-agent domains such as autonomous driving or robotics.
