## Problem

Existing video-text compositionality benchmarks focus on static image-text pairs or isolated single-event clips, leaving a gap in evaluating fine-grained temporal alignment across continuous, multi-event video sequences. Models are not rigorously tested on their ability to detect subtle compositional disruptions—such as temporal reordering, action word replacement, or segment-level mismatches—within longer, cohesive video-text narratives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16 uniformly sampled video frames at 256×256 resolution alongside text captions of up to 160 tokens (extended from CLIP's 64). Evaluation uses triplets of (video, positive caption, disrupted caption), where the model must assign a higher cosine similarity to the positive pair; retrieval uses only (video, positive caption) pairs for Recall@1. The disrupted captions are generated by LLM (Gemini-1.5-Pro) with automatic validation enforcing ≥80% word-level precision and recall against the original text.

## Method

The architecture is a dual-encoder CLIP (ViT-Large vision encoder + 12-layer Transformer text encoder) with temporal positional encoding and two attention pooling layers (196 queries → 1 query) for video representation. The key training innovation is a hierarchical pairwise preference loss (CompLoss) that, alongside standard InfoNCE, enforces a strict similarity ranking: positive pairs score highest, then progressively less-disrupted negatives (temporal reorder < action replace < segment mismatch < multi-disruption), using hinge-style penalties between adjacent disruption levels. To address the scarcity of densely captioned multi-event video data, CompPretrain concatenates 4 short video-caption pairs from VideoCC3M into pseudo-long-form sequences and applies the same preference loss, enabling compositional learning at scale before fine-tuning on the smaller ActivityNet-Comp/YouCook2-Comp benchmarks.

## Results

On ActivityNet-Comp, the full VidCLIP-final model (CompPretrain + CompLoss, ~500M params) achieves 68.2% / 75.4% / 68.0% binary classification accuracy on temporal reordering / action replacement / segment mismatch, with a comprehensive (multiplicative) score of 35.0%, up from 18.9% for the zero-shot VidCLIP-base. On YouCook2-Comp, the same pipeline reaches 58.2% / 70.3% / 69.5% (comprehensive 28.4%) versus 21.4% zero-shot. In zero-shot retrieval on MSR-VTT, VidCLIP-base achieves 41.8% T2V / 67.0% V2T Recall@1, competitive with InternVid (42.4/41.3) and VideoCoCa (34.3/64.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidCLIP-final substantially outperforms all dual-encoder video-text baselines: it beats CLIP4Clip by 17.0 points on comprehensive score (35.0 vs 17.0), VideoCoCa by 14.9 points (35.0 vs 20.1), and VideoPrism by 12.8 points (35.0 vs 22.2), with consistent gains across all three disruption types. Against NegCLIP's negative-augmented contrastive loss (Table 4), CompLoss improves the comprehensive score from 27.3% to 31.2%, with the largest gain on temporal reordering (65.4% vs 60.0%). Compared to Gemini-1.5-Flash-8B, VidCLIP-final is competitive on temporal reordering (68.2% vs 67.1%) and segment mismatch (68.0% vs 67.3%) but trails on action replacement (75.4% vs 79.6%) and comprehensive score (35.0% vs 35.9%), reflecting LMMs' stronger action-level semantics. Against the larger Gemini-1.5-Flash and Pro models, VidCLIP-final lags by 7.5 and 8.9 points on comprehensive score respectively, with the gap widest on action replacement (75.4% vs 83.3%/84.2%). The paper notes that the 8B Gemini model already struggles on temporally sensitive tasks (67.1% reorder, 67.3% seg-mismatch), suggesting that compositionality-aware training in a compact dual-encoder can match or exceed much larger LMMs on fine-grained temporal alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style dual-encoder video-text alignment paradigm (VideoCLIP, CLIP4Clip, VideoCoCa) by introducing a hierarchical pairwise preference loss (CompLoss) that generalizes NegCLIP's negative-augmented contrastive approach from flat positive/negative distinctions to a graded hierarchy of disruption severity. It also extends the line of video compositionality benchmarks (PerceptionTest, VITATECS, ViLMA, ICSVR, TempCompass), which have focused on isolated single-event clips, to continuous multi-event video sequences with fine-grained temporal disruptions. The CompPretrain strategy of concatenating short video-caption pairs to simulate dense multi-event sequences is a novel data-efficient pretraining approach not seen in prior video-text alignment work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even state-of-the-art LMMs like Gemini-1.5-Pro achieve only ~44% comprehensive accuracy across all disruption types, exposing a significant gap in fine-grained temporal compositionality that current models have not addressed. The CompPretrain strategy offers a practical solution to the scarcity of densely annotated multi-event video data, making compositional training more accessible. Open questions include whether the dual-encoder architecture fundamentally limits fine-grained temporal alignment compared to cross-attention models, whether the LLM-generated negative samples fully capture the diversity of real-world disruptions, and whether the 16-frame sampling is sufficient for truly long-form video understanding.
