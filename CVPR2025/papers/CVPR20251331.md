## Problem

Current VLMs that leverage multiple vision encoders (e.g., CLIP, EVA-02, ConvNeXt) to capture diverse visual capabilities suffer from substantial increases in computational cost and model complexity. The paper asks whether the unique proficiencies of multiple pre-trained visual encoders can be distilled into a single efficient encoder without the knowledge conflicts that arise when learning from heterogeneous teachers simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251331/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard RGB image and a text question, producing a text response through a single student vision encoder (CLIP ViT-L) connected to an LLM via MLP projection, identical in architecture to vanilla LLaVA. During training, the same image is additionally processed by multiple frozen teacher encoders (CLIP, EVA-02, ConvNeXt, and optionally SAM-L) whose outputs are projected into a unified token space via per-teacher two-layer MLP adapters. LLaVA-NeXT variants use adaptive image cropping to handle variable resolutions before encoding.

## Method

MoVE-KD distills multiple teacher encoders into a single student encoder using two key mechanisms. First, a Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts (MoLE) module is inserted into each FFN layer of the student encoder: a linear router selects one of three LoRA experts (rank 32) per input token, allowing the student to dynamically specialize to different teacher knowledge without catastrophic forgetting or the parameter overhead of full FFN replicas. Second, an attention-guided distillation loss regularizes the transfer by computing token-level weights from CLIP's [CLS] cross-attention (emphasizing semantically rich, non-repetitive visual tokens) and teacher-level weights from the mean [CLS] attention over each teacher's tokens (up-weighting teachers that respond more strongly to the current image). The total training objective combines the standard VLM text log-likelihood loss with this weighted MSE distillation loss (λ_kd = 0.5), with CLIP given a fixed high weight of 0.8 to preserve the student's initialization knowledge.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 7B, MoVE-KD-v1.0 achieves 79.5 on VQA^V2, 63.2 on GQA, 58.3 on TextVQA, 52.3 on VizWiz, 86.9 on POPE, 69.3 on SQA, 1524.5 on MME, and 66.3 on MMBench, outperforming the LLaVA-1.5 baseline across nearly all benchmarks. On LLaVA-NeXT 7B, MoVE-KD-v1.0 further improves MME to 1537.2 (vs. 1519.0 baseline) and SQA to 70.7 (vs. 70.1). At 13B scale, MoVE-KD-v1.0 on LLaVA-NeXT reaches 1579.3 on MME and 70.6 on MMBench, surpassing the LLaVA-NeXT 13B baseline. The method also demonstrates scalability: adding SAM-L as a fourth teacher (v1.1) further boosts TextVQA to 59.6 on 7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-1.5 7B, MoVE-KD-v1.0 improves on all reported benchmarks: VQA^V2 by +1.0 (79.5 vs 78.5), GQA by +1.2, VizWiz by +2.3, POPE by +1.0, SQA by +2.5, MME by +13.8, and MMBench by +2.0. Compared to RADIO (the only other multi-encoder distillation baseline), MoVE-KD-v1.0 substantially outperforms on VQA^V2 (79.5 vs 76.3) and TextVQA (58.3 vs 56.3), where RADIO exhibits knowledge forgetting, while also matching or exceeding RADIO on GQA and POPE. Against LLaVA-NeXT 7B, MoVE-KD applied to LLaVA-1.5 still surpasses it on MME (1524.5 vs 1519.0), though LLaVA-NeXT retains advantages on VQA^V2, TextVQA, and VizWiz due to its higher-resolution input strategy. At 13B scale, MoVE-KD-v1.0 on LLaVA-NeXT improves MME by +4.3 (1579.3 vs 1575.0) and MMBench by +0.6 over the LLaVA-NeXT baseline, while also beating LLaVA-1.5 13B on MME by +37.0 (1568.1 vs 1531.3). MoVE-KD-v1.1 (with SAM-L as an additional teacher) further improves TextVQA to 59.6 on 7B, demonstrating that performance scales with the number of teacher encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-encoder VLMs (Eagle, Mini-Gemini, S²) and knowledge distillation, but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than running multiple encoders at inference time or distilling a standalone encoder (as RADIO/AM-RADIO does), it integrates multi-teacher distillation directly into VLM training. The MoLE architecture repurposes LoRA as lightweight expert modules within a standard MoE routing scheme, and the attention-guided KD loss borrows CLIP's [CLS] cross-attention as a fixed, learnable-free regularizer for token and teacher weighting — a departure from prior multi-teacher KD that relies on learned or uniform weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a single-encoder VLM that captures multi-encoder strengths at only 0.3% parameter overhead, avoiding the inference-time cost of parallel encoders. The paper's observation that KD gains diminish as the LLM backbone scales (7B→13B) and its suggestion that the projector, not the vision encoder, may be the true bottleneck for large VLMs, opens an important direction for future work. Open questions include optimal teacher weight allocation strategies, whether the approach extends to video or 3D encoders, and how to address the projector bottleneck the authors identify.
