## Problem

Single domain generalization (SDG) requires a model trained on one source domain to perform well on multiple unseen target domains, but existing data augmentation strategies struggle to generate domains with extreme distributional shifts. While text-to-image (T2I) foundation models offer a promising path for generating diverse training images, manually crafting textual prompts for all possible domains is impractical, and many domain characteristics are too abstract to describe in natural language.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250492/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single labeled source domain (e.g., one of PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, DomainNet, or TerraIncognita) as training input and produces class labels for test images from unseen target domains. During training, the pre-trained latent diffusion model generates synthetic images conditioned on learned abstract prompts, which are then combined with source domain images to train a ResNet-18 or ResNet-50 classifier. The generated images inherit the category label from the corresponding category prompt, so no manual annotation is needed for the synthetic data.

## Method

PAPT operates on a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model and learns two sets of abstract prompts: category prompts (E_k^c) that capture domain-invariant class semantics, and domain prompts (E_i^d) that encode diverse domain styles. The adversarial domain prompt tuning module minimizes a softmax-based style similarity loss between the generated latent z_0 and all previously learned domain prompts stored in a memory bank P, while a category consistency loss maximizes semantic alignment between z_0 and the corresponding category embedding. A Progressive Training Scheme iteratively adds each newly learned domain prompt to P, forcing subsequent prompts to be distributionally distinct from all prior ones. Gradients are back-propagated through a one-step approximation of z_0 from an intermediate noisy state z_t, avoiding the intractable full sampling chain. The final loss L_all = L_class + λ·L_domain (with λ=10) balances diversity in domain styles against preservation of categorical features.

## Results

On the PACS single-domain generalization benchmark, PAPT achieves 72.72% average accuracy, outperforming the previous best method Prompt-Driven (71.12%) by 1.60%. On VLCS, PAPT reaches 73.37% average accuracy, a 9.55% improvement over the prior best MAD (63.82%). On OfficeHome, PAPT attains 62.03% average accuracy, surpassing UDIM (58.30%) by 3.73%. In the multi-source DG setting across five datasets, PAPT achieves 69.64% average accuracy, exceeding SMOS (68.30%) by 1.34%, with a particularly large 4.88% gain on DomainNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAPT outperforms all 34 compared baselines across every dataset and setting. On PACS (SDG), it beats the strongest prior method Prompt-Driven [34] by 1.60% (72.72% vs. 71.12%) and Meta-Casual [6] by 2.86%, with the largest per-domain gain on Sketch (80.34% vs. 62.94% for Prompt-Driven, a 17.40% improvement). On VLCS, the advantage is most dramatic: PAPT (73.37%) surpasses MAD [49] (63.82%) by 9.55%, demonstrating that learnable domain prompts capture viewpoint/background shifts that textual descriptions cannot. On OfficeHome, PAPT (62.03%) leads UDIM [58] (58.30%) by 3.73% and SAGM [67] (56.30%) by 5.73%. In multi-source DG, PAPT (69.64%) edges out SMOS [40] (68.30%) by 1.34% overall but gains 4.88% on DomainNet (50.18% vs. 45.30%), where large style discrepancies are most challenging. Notably, traditional data augmentation methods like Mixstyle [78] and EFDMix [75] show limited or even negative gains on VLCS and TerraIncognita, where domain shift manifests as viewpoint/background changes rather than texture/style, whereas PAPT's diffusion-based generation handles these shifts effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) as the generative backbone and on the textual inversion / prompt-tuning paradigm (optimizing conditional input embeddings rather than model parameters) to avoid retraining the diffusion model. It departs from prior SDG methods—which rely on hand-crafted data augmentation (MixStyle, EFDMix), adversarial domain augmentation, or adaptive normalization—by replacing static textual domain descriptions with two sets of learnable abstract prompts (category and domain) and introducing an adversarial progressive training loop with a memory bank to enforce inter-domain diversity. The work thus sits at the intersection of prompt-tuning for diffusion models and single-domain generalization, a combination the authors claim is entirely new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a pre-trained T2I foundation model can be steered via learned prompts to synthesize training images spanning a wide range of unseen domain styles, the paper opens a practical route to SDG that does not require manual domain curation or access to target-domain data. The notably large gain on VLCS (≈9.5 pp) suggests the approach is especially effective when domain shift manifests as background/viewpoint changes rather than texture, a regime where prior augmentation methods struggle. Open questions include whether the one-step backpropagation approximation through the diffusion sampler limits fidelity at scale, whether the method extends beyond image classification (e.g., detection, segmentation), and how computational cost of iterative image generation compares to lightweight augmentation baselines in deployment settings.
