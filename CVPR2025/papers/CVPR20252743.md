## Problem

Existing continual segmentation methods are forced to balance stability (preserving old knowledge) against plasticity (learning new knowledge) because high-level semantic understanding and low-level boundary prediction are implicitly entangled in a single encoder-decoder pipeline. This paper reveals that these two types of knowledge have fundamentally different sensitivities to forgetting and convergence speed, and that no single regularization can simultaneously satisfy both. Additionally, prior approaches are specialist to specific segmentation tasks and architectures, limiting their generality.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed at 518×518 crop for the understanding stage and 1024×1024 for SAM refinement) and, for language-guided tasks, a text prompt encoded by BERT-base. The understanding stage operates on single-scale, low-resolution embeddings (1/16 image size) to produce class-aware positional proposals (masks at reduced resolution plus bounding boxes for things), which are then converted to point prompts for SAM. Final outputs are full-resolution segmentation masks with assigned class labels, plus bounding boxes for thing categories, unified across semantic, instance, panoptic, and language-guided segmentation.

## Method

CUE decouples segmentation into an understanding-then-refinement pipeline: a DETR-like semantic understanding module (ViT-B/DINOv2 backbone, 3-layer enhancer, 2-layer decoders) generates class-aware positional proposals, and a frozen SAM refines each proposal into a pixel-accurate mask via point-prompt conditioning. The understanding module uses separate decoders for things (MaskDINO-style with detection head) and stuff (Mask2Former-style mask-only), semantic-aware static query initialization from category prototypes with Gaussian noise, and grouped bipartite matching restricted to queries and categories from the same learning step. During continual updating, the understanding part is trained without knowledge distillation (only a small memory buffer as weak constraint plus pseudo-labeling for old classes), while SAM is completely frozen, exploiting the transferability of low-level visual cues to novel categories. This design simultaneously achieves high plasticity for new categories and high stability for old ones by applying appropriately differentiated constraints to each knowledge level.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012 semantic segmentation (15-1, 6 steps), CUE-M achieves 84.0 mIoU (all classes), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline AMS-M (71.9 mIoU) by 12.1 points, while closing the gap to the Joint upper bound (87.8) to just 3.8 points. On ADE20K semantic segmentation (100-50, 2 steps), CUE-M reaches 54.6 mIoU versus 38.4 for CoMFormer and 37.7 for AMS-M. On ADE20K panoptic segmentation (100-50, 2 steps), CUE achieves 46.2 PQ, surpassing ECLIPSE (35.6 PQ) by 10.6 points. The method also demonstrates superior efficiency with 147G FLOPs and 63.5 FPS versus 586G and 4.0 FPS for CoMFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUE-M outperforms all nine baselines across every setup and dataset. Against the strongest CNN-based baselines (MiB, PLOP, RCIL), CUE-M leads by 20–50 mIoU points on the 15-1 VOC setup, reflecting the advantage of the decoupled understanding-then-refinement pipeline over monolithic encoder-decoder designs. Among memory-based methods, CUE-M surpasses SSUL-M (71.3→84.0 all mIoU) and RECALL-M (64.8→84.0) by 12–19 points, while using the same memory budget (100 samples). Against the strongest Transformer-based baselines, CUE-M beats CoMFormer (38.4→54.6 mIoU on ADE20K 100-50) and ECLIPSE (35.6→46.2 PQ on ADE20K panoptic) by 16 and 10.6 points respectively. The most notable margin is on new-class performance: CUE-M achieves 77.9 mIoU on new VOC classes versus 50.8 for AMS-M and 49.0 for SSUL-M, demonstrating that the decoupled design resolves the stability-plasticity trade-off rather than merely balancing it.

## Relation to Prior Work

CUE departs from the dominant continual segmentation paradigm (MiB, PLOP, RCIL, CoMFormer, ECLIPSE) that treats stability and plasticity as a single trade-off managed through knowledge distillation or replay. Instead, it reformulates segmentation as an understanding-then-refinement pipeline, drawing on DETR/MaskDINO for the semantic understanding stage and SAM for the visual refinement stage, with Mask2Former-style decoders for stuff categories. This lineage is distinct from prior universal segmentation work (MaskFormer, Mask2Former, OneFormer) in that it explicitly decouples high-level and low-level knowledge to address continual updating, and extends beyond generic-vision tasks to language-guided segmentation via BERT text encoders and bi-directional cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that high-level semantic understanding and low-level boundary prediction exhibit fundamentally different sensitivity to catastrophic forgetting—provides a principled rationale for why prior single-constraint approaches (KD, replay) can only balance rather than simultaneously satisfy stability and plasticity. The pipeline's unification across semantic, instance, panoptic, and language-guided segmentation under one framework reduces research fragmentation and offers a reusable template. The authors acknowledge the work is preliminary: the frozen SAM component limits adaptability to truly novel visual domains, and scaling to larger networks/datasets remains untested. Open questions include whether the decoupling principle generalizes beyond segmentation (e.g., detection, tracking) and whether the efficiency gains persist at scale.
