## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for spatial transcriptomics prediction operate only on 2D single-layer WSI sections, ignoring the rich 3D structural information of tissues and risking diagnostic errors due to cross-section heterogeneity. Full 3D ST acquisition is prohibitively expensive (an order of magnitude costlier than 2D ST), and current 2D-to-3D prediction approaches yield unsatisfying accuracy. This paper introduces a novel problem setting: imputing a full 3D ST volume from 3D WSI histology sections combined with a single 2D ST slide, balancing precision and cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-layer WSI histology sections (e.g., 3–6 layers per sample) and a single 2D ST slide providing ground-truth gene expression for one layer (20% of total labels). Patches are extracted at three resolutions—224×224 (spot-level), 512×512 (region-level), and 1024×1024 (global-level)—and the model predicts the top 250 most highly expressed genes per spot across all layers. The output is a complete 3D ST volume with spot-level gene expression predictions for every layer, not just the one with known labels.

## Method

ASIGN constructs a 3D graph by aligning WSI layers via XFeat features and ANTs registration, then connecting spots across layers using IoU of spot circles plus cosine similarity of extracted features, and within layers using Euclidean distance, with Top-k neighbor selection. The MSAGNet module fuses multi-resolution features through cross-attention (treating spot-level features as queries against region- and global-level features), processes 3D spatial structure via multi-head GAT blocks over the constructed graph, and aggregates information through a Transformer layer before a regression head produces predictions at each resolution. A Cross-layer Imputation (CLI) block then propagates known labels to unknown spots via iterative label diffusion with learnable node weights and edge weights from the 3D graph, and the final prediction is an adaptive weighted combination of the MSAGNet output and the CLI imputation. Training uses a hybrid loss combining MSE and PCC at the spot level, PCC at region/global levels, and a cross-resolution consistency term, with sample-level cross-validation where one layer serves as the known-label training layer.

## Results

ASIGN 3D achieves the best performance across all three datasets in the 3D sample-wise setting: PCC of 0.693 on HER2, 0.773 on DLPFC, and 0.739 on ST-Data, with corresponding MSE values of 0.363, 0.353, and 0.332. In the 2D WSI-wise setting, ASIGN 2D also leads or matches the best baselines, achieving PCC of 0.476 (HER2), 0.766 (DLPFC), and 0.552 (ST-Data). The 3D formulation yields substantial gains over 2D, e.g., PCC improves from 0.476 to 0.693 on HER2 and from 0.552 to 0.739 on ST-Data, demonstrating the value of cross-layer spatial information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASIGN 3D outperforms all five 2D baselines (ST-Net, EGN, HisToGene, BLEEP, His2ST) and two 3D imputation baselines (Overlap, Similarity) on every dataset and metric. On HER2, ASIGN 3D achieves PCC 0.693, surpassing the best 2D method EGN (0.442) by 25.1 points and the best 3D baseline Similarity (0.649) by 4.4 points. On DLPFC, ASIGN 3D reaches PCC 0.773, edging out HisToGene (0.753) and Similarity (0.762) by 2.0 and 1.1 points respectively. On ST-Data, the gap is largest: ASIGN 3D (PCC 0.739) beats HisToGene (0.552) by 18.7 points and Similarity (0.696) by 4.3 points. In the 2D-only setting, ASIGN 2D also leads on HER2 (PCC 0.476 vs. EGN's 0.442) and DLPFC (PCC 0.766 vs. HisToGene's 0.753), while matching HisToGene on ST-Data (both 0.552). BLEEP consistently underperforms, particularly on HER2 where its PCC drops to 0.340, suggesting its query-reference imputation strategy struggles with cross-sample heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D WSI-to-ST prediction line (ST-Net, EGN, BLEEP, HisToGene, His2ST, TRIPLEX) by introducing a 3D partially-known imputation setting that combines 3D WSI sections with a single 2D ST slide, rather than predicting each 2D layer independently or requiring full 3D ST acquisition. Methodologically, it draws on GAT-based spatial modeling (His2ST, HisToGene), multi-resolution feature fusion (TRIPLEX), and label propagation (BLEEP's query-reference imputation), but reorganizes these into a cross-layer graph constructed via WSI-level registration (XFeat + ANTs) rather than spot-level alignment (PASTE, Stitch3D). The key conceptual departure is shifting from an entirely unknown 2D prediction task to a partially known 3D imputation task, where one layer's ground-truth labels are propagated to unknown layers through the constructed 3D graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical cost argument is compelling: a full 3D ST volume costs an order of magnitude more than a single 2D ST slide, and this approach reduces that to one ST acquisition per sample while maintaining competitive accuracy. However, the evidence base is limited to three small public datasets (13K–42K spots total), and the 20% known-label ratio, while showing diminishing returns, has not been validated on larger or more heterogeneous clinical cohorts. Open questions include whether the WSI-level registration approach scales to highly deformed or irregularly sliced tissues, whether the method generalizes to other spatial transcriptomics platforms (e.g., Visium HD, MERFISH), and whether the single-seed, small-sample evaluation supports the claimed generalizability gains in real clinical deployment.
