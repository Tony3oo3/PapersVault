## Problem

Existing shadow-related methods handle detection, removal, and synthesis as isolated tasks without sharing shadow knowledge, and most lack object-centered formulations required for practical image editing (object removal, relocation, insertion). This fragmentation leads to inconsistent shadow shapes, colors, and intensities when multiple methods are chained in an editing pipeline, and no prior framework jointly addresses all three tasks in a mutually beneficial, knowledge-sharing manner.

## Input / Output

The Shadow Analyzer takes an RGB image at 512x512 plus an object mask (which may be empty for general shadow detection) and produces both a shadow mask and a shadow-free image. The Shadow Synthesizer operates at 128x128, consuming the object mask, the image containing the moved/inserted object, and an optional reference shadow mask, then outputs a composited image with a synthesized shadow. A key design choice is that the Synthesizer receives multi-scale intermediate features (32x32, 1344 channels) extracted from the frozen Shadow Analyzer as a conditioning signal rather than relying on text or CLIP embeddings.

## Method

MetaShadow couples two components: a Shadow Analyzer built on CM-GAN with an encoder, two parallel cascaded decoders (global and spatial modulation), and an integrated shadow detector that upsamples multi-scale features to a 256x256 mask; and a Shadow Synthesizer adapted from a DDPM inpainting diffusion model that predicts a shadow mask at every denoising step. The core novelty is shadow knowledge transfer: multi-scale features from the Analyzer's spatial decoder are passed through a 2D-then-1D convolutional adaptor and an MLP (expanding 1344 to 2048 dims) to form a shadow embedding injected into the diffusion U-Net via cross-attention, replacing the standard text-embedding pathway. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I trains the Analyzer for 100 epochs at 512x512 with adversarial, perceptual, masked-R1, L1, and dice losses; Stage II freezes the Analyzer and fine-tunes the diffusion U-Net and adaptor for 400 epochs at 128x128 with a decaying learning rate for the U-Net and a constant rate for the adaptor. Multi-source training across DESOBA, MOS, ISTD+, SRD, and Shadow-AR, combined with shadow-specific augmentations (intensity, color grading, random dropping), and the use of only 4 diffusion steps (vs. 30-50 for baselines) further distinguish the approach.

## Results

On the SOBA test set, MetaShadow achieves 71.0 mIoU for object-centered shadow detection, surpassing SSISv2 (55.8) by 15.2 points. For shadow removal on DESOBA, it attains 32.97 Bbox PSNR and 96.49 Bbox SSIM, outperforming the best baseline (ShadowDiffusion with GT mask at 24.28 Bbox PSNR) by 8.7 dB. For shadow synthesis, MetaShadow reduces Local RMSE from 56.44 (SGRNet) to 36.84 on DESOBA and from 51.73 to 36.54 on Video DESOBA, while also achieving the best Global RMSE (3.12 and 8.07 respectively) and Bbox PSNR (29.16 and 23.14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaShadow outperforms all eight baselines across every task and dataset. For shadow detection on SOBA, it beats SSIS (51.6 mIoU) and SSISv2 (55.8 mIoU) by 19.4 and 15.2 points respectively, with the MOS dataset contributing an additional 3.8 mIoU gain. For shadow removal on DESOBA, MetaShadow (32.97 Bbox PSNR, 21.32 Masked MAE) surpasses ShadowDiffusion with GT mask (24.28 Bbox PSNR, 35.45 Masked MAE) by 8.7 dB and 14.1 MAE, and also outperforms the fine-tuned cascade of ShadowDiffusion + SSISv2 (23.41 Bbox PSNR). On ISTD+ general removal, MetaShadow without shadow masks (31.38 PSNR) beats ShadowDiffusion (29.86) and HomoFormer (30.63) but trails RASM (34.66), which uses shadow masks. For shadow synthesis, MetaShadow dominates SGRNet (Local RMSE 36.84 vs 56.44 on DESOBA), SGDiffusion (36.84 vs 64.90), and Libcom (36.84 vs 67.21) on all four metrics, with the advantage being particularly pronounced on the more challenging Video DESOBA set where SGRNet's Local RMSE is 51.73 versus MetaShadow's 36.54.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaShadow builds on CM-GAN for its Shadow Analyzer (object-centered shadow detection and removal) and on DDPM-based inpainting diffusion for its Shadow Synthesizer, but departs from prior work by unifying all three shadow tasks—detection, removal, and synthesis—into a single object-centered framework with shared knowledge. Prior methods handle at most two of these tasks (e.g., DHAN for detection+removal, SGRNet for reference-based synthesis), and none transfer learned shadow representations across tasks. The key architectural departure is the shadow knowledge transfer mechanism, which injects multi-scale intermediate features from the GAN-based Analyzer into the diffusion-based Synthesizer via cross-attention, replacing the text or CLIP image embeddings typically used to condition diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly perform object-centered shadow detection, removal, and synthesis, directly enabling realistic object relocation, removal, and insertion in image editing pipelines where shadows are currently ignored. The introduction of the MOS synthetic training set and two real-world evaluation sets (Moving DESOBA, Video DESOBA) addresses a critical data scarcity problem in shadow research. Limitations include reliance on synthetic training data for the MOS set, fixed input resolutions (512×512 for Analyzer, 128×128 for Synthesizer), and the requirement of an object mask as input, which limits fully autonomous operation. Open questions include whether the GAN-to-diffusion feature transfer generalizes to other degradation types beyond shadows and whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and more complex multi-object scenes.
