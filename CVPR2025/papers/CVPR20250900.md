## Problem

End-to-end vision-based autonomous driving planners are efficient but struggle with rare long-tail scenarios due to limited training data, while LLM-based planners leverage world knowledge for better generalization but incur prohibitive computational overhead at inference. The core question is how to transfer an MLLM's world knowledge into a lightweight vision-based planner so that the LLM can be discarded at test time without sacrificing robustness to unseen or rare driving events.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250900/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a multi-view camera image sequence (6 cameras from nuScenes) and an optional text question prompt. The scene encoder produces structured BEAM token embeddings (Bird's-eye-view, Ego, Agent, Map) that serve as the shared representation between the planning transformer and the MLLM. At inference, the vision-based planner outputs 6 future waypoints (3 seconds at 2 Hz) without any LLM involvement; optionally, the MLLM branch can additionally produce text responses for visual question answering or a hybrid DiMA-Dual prediction that max-pools features from both branches.

## Method

DiMA jointly trains a vision-based planner (scene encoder + planning transformer, based on VAD or UniAD) with an MLLM (component-specific Q-former adapters + LLaVA-7B + task-specific decoder heads) under a shared scene encoder that acts as a trainable tokenizer, providing structured BEAM token embeddings rather than dense unstructured image tokens. The MLLM is supervised on VQA, trajectory estimation, KL-divergence distillation from its ego-token hidden features to the planning transformer, and three surrogate tasks—masked BEV token reconstruction, future BEV prediction, and scene editing (adding/removing agents with corresponding QA pairs)—all designed to enrich the shared scene representations with planning-relevant spatio-temporal and linguistic grounding. Training is two-stage: 60 epochs of vision-only pre-training followed by 30 epochs of joint training with LoRA fine-tuning of the LLM. At inference the MLLM is entirely discarded, so the system runs at the speed of the underlying vision planner (e.g., 16.8 FPS for VAD-Tiny) while retaining the improved representations learned through distillation.

## Results

Under standardized evaluation on the full nuScenes validation set, DiMA+(VAD-Base) achieves 0.56 m average L2 trajectory error (t=1,2,3s) and 0.07% collision rate, outperforming the VAD-Base baseline (0.91 m, 0.37%) by 37% in L2 error and 80% in collision rate. On the targeted (turning) validation split, DiMA+(VAD-Base) reaches 0.79 m L2 error and 0.06% collision, beating PARA-Drive+ (0.83 m, 0.10%) and VAD-Base (1.27 m, 0.39%). In long-tail scenarios (3-point turn, resume from stop, overtake), DiMA(VAD-Base) achieves 44% lower L2 error than VAD-Base and consistently outperforms both PARA-Drive and TOKEN, including on the zero-shot 3-point turn case (1.30 m vs. 1.43 m for TOKEN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiMA outperforms all nine compared baselines on both L2 trajectory error and collision rate. Against the vision-based planners, DiMA(VAD-Base) reduces L2 error by 37% and collision rate by 80% over VAD-Base (0.47 vs. 0.78 m; 0.06% vs. 0.30%), and by 44% and 85% over UniAD (0.47 vs. 0.83 m; 0.06% vs. 0.40%). Against the strongest vision-based competitor PARA-Drive, DiMA+(VAD-Base) achieves 0.46 m L2 and 0.06% collision versus PARA-Drive's 0.56 m and 0.17% (without ego status) or PARA-Drive+'s 0.49 m and 0.13% (with ego status). Against the LLM-based planner TOKEN, DiMA(VAD-Base) improves L2 error from 0.68 m to 0.47 m (31% reduction) and collision rate from 0.15% to 0.06% (60% reduction), while not requiring an LLM at inference. In long-tail scenarios, DiMA(VAD-Base) achieves the lowest L2 error on the zero-shot 3-point turn (1.30 m vs. TOKEN's 1.43 m and PARA-Drive's 1.55 m) and on overtake (0.82 m vs. 0.90 m and 1.03 m). Under VAD evaluation (Table 2), DiMA(VAD-Tiny) at 0.38 m L2 and 16.8 FPS outperforms VAD-Base (0.72 m, 4.5 FPS) by 47% in error while being 4× faster, and surpasses MLLM planners DriveVLM (0.40 m) and OmniDrive (0.84 m) without any LLM at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiMA sits at the intersection of two lines of work: end-to-end vision-based planners (VAD, UniAD) that are efficient but struggle with long-tail scenarios, and MLLM-based planners (TOKEN, DriveVLM, OmniDrive) that leverage world knowledge but incur heavy inference cost. The key departure from TOKEN [38] is that the scene encoder is jointly trained with the MLLM rather than frozen, and the MLLM receives structured BEAM token embeddings rather than dense unstructured visual tokens. The surrogate tasks (masked token reconstruction, future BEV prediction, scene editing) and the KL-divergence distillation between the MLLM and planning transformer penultimate layers are novel training objectives not present in prior end-to-end AD frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiMA offers a practical path to LLM-level robustness in autonomous driving without the computational overhead of running a 7B-parameter LLM at inference, which is critical for real-time deployment. The 80% collision rate reduction and 44% long-tail trajectory error improvement over the vision-based baseline are substantial, and the zero-shot 3-point-turn result suggests genuine generalization beyond training data. However, evaluation remains open-loop on nuScenes, and the paper does not address closed-loop performance, multi-dataset generalization, or whether the gains persist under distribution shift beyond the curated long-tail splits. The optional MLLM branch (DiMA-Dual) adds inference complexity that partially undermines the efficiency claim.
