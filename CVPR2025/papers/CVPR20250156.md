## Problem

Existing Offline Handwritten Text Generation models suffer from three key limitations: they fail to generalize to handwriting styles significantly different from training data, they impose technical constraints such as fixed output length or normalized character widths, and they entangle text style with background, producing unwanted artifacts in generated images. No prior autoregressive model has been proposed for this task, and no existing approach is specifically designed for zero-shot generalization to unseen styles.

## Input / Output

Emuru takes three inputs at inference: a reference style image (a single text line, fixed height H=64 with width proportional to text length), the text string contained in that reference image, and the desired output text string. The model produces a background-free styled text image of arbitrary length, with the VAE operating on a single-channel latent space (h=8) where each vector encodes a vertical slice of the text line. Generation terminates autonomously when the model emits 10 consecutive padding embeddings, removing any fixed-length constraint.

## Method

Emuru combines a β-VAE with a T5-Large autoregressive Transformer Encoder-Decoder, both trained on a 2.2M-sample synthetic dataset of English text rendered in over 100,000 fonts. The VAE uses a single latent channel and is trained with reconstruction loss plus auxiliary writer-identification and HTR CTC losses, which enforce style fidelity, readability, and background removal simultaneously. The Transformer Decoder receives noisy VAE embeddings of the style image (noise added to reduce exposure bias) and cross-attends to text token embeddings from the Encoder, autoregressively predicting VAE-compatible vectors via MSE loss. Training follows a two-stage curriculum (4–7 words, then 1–32 words) with noisy teacher-forcing in the first stage. The key architectural insight is using continuous (unquantized) latents and letting the model learn an implicit end-of-sequence signal through padding embeddings, enabling variable-length generation without fixed canvas constraints.

## Results

On the Karaoke typewritten benchmark, Emuru achieves FID 9.85, BFID 4.33, KID 5.60, and HWD 1.28, dramatically outperforming the best baseline (VATr++ at FID 76.03, BFID 41.69). On Karaoke calligraphy, Emuru reaches FID 13.87 and HWD 2.24 versus the next-best DiffPen (FID 34.19, HWD 4.18). On IAM Lines (zero-shot), Emuru attains the best BFID (6.19) and HWD (1.87), while on CVL Lines it leads on FID (14.39), BFID (10.77), KID (12.34), and HWD (1.82). The VAE reconstruction quality (Table 1) shows Emuru's text-specific VAE outperforms SD1.5 and SD3 VAEs on FID (19.22 vs 29.39/21.90) and BFID (1.62 vs 7.36/3.61).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Emuru is evaluated zero-shot (trained only on a synthetic dataset) against seven HTG baselines (TS-GAN, HiGAN+, HWT, VATr, VATr++, One-DM, DiffPen) that were all trained on IAM. On IAM Lines, Emuru matches or beats DiffPen (the strongest baseline) on BFID (6.19 vs 6.87) and HWD (1.87 vs 2.13), though DiffPen retains a slight edge on FID (12.89 vs 13.89) and KID (9.73 vs 11.30). On IAM Words, DiffPen clearly leads (FID 15.54 vs 63.61), reflecting Emuru's design focus on full-line generation. The decisive advantage appears on out-of-distribution datasets: on CVL, RIMES, and Karaoke, Emuru wins on FID, BFID, KID, and HWD by large margins (e.g., FID 14.39 vs 40.40 for DiffPen on CVL; FID 9.85 vs 70.75 for One-DM on Karaoke typewritten), while all baselines degrade severely. The only metric where baselines sometimes match or beat Emuru is ΔCER (readability gap), since Emuru lacks an explicit text-correctness mechanism. Overall, Emuru is the only model maintaining a consistent score range across all four datasets, whereas GAN-based methods (TS-GAN, HiGAN+) and even DiffPen collapse on typewritten styles.

## Relation to Prior Work

Emuru departs from the dominant GAN-based (HiGAN+, TS-GAN, HWT, VATr) and Diffusion Model-based (DiffPen, One-DM) paradigms for Offline styled HTG by introducing the first autoregressive Transformer approach to the task. Conceptually, it repurposes the continuous-latent autoregressive generation idea from natural image synthesis (GIVT, LlamaGen) and the β-VAE representation from Stable Diffusion, but adapts them to the variable-length, style-conditioned text image domain. The synthetic pretraining strategy draws on prior work in handwriting processing [38, 39], while the curriculum learning schedule for handling variable-length outputs is adapted from [24]. The key architectural departure is letting the model autonomously decide when to stop generating (via padding embeddings), removing the fixed-length constraint inherent to GAN and diffusion approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Emuru addresses three persistent limitations of prior HTG methods—poor generalization to unseen styles, fixed output length, and background artifact entanglement—simultaneously, which no single prior approach achieved. The introduction of the Karaoke dataset (100 fonts, multiple languages, both calligraphy and typewritten) and the standardized evaluation protocol with new metrics (BFID, ΔCER) provide the community with tools to assess generalization more rigorously. However, the model lacks a mechanism to guarantee text correctness (visible in the ΔCER gap on Karaoke), and word-level generation on IAM underperforms DiffPen significantly, suggesting the autoregressive approach may struggle with short, high-fidelity outputs. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be further closed, whether the approach extends to non-Latin scripts, and whether the background-free property can be leveraged for more complex document editing pipelines.
