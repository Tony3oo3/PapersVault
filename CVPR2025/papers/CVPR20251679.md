## Problem

Existing plug-and-play KV-injection methods for visual characteristics transfer in diffusion models suffer from three fundamental limitations: a domain gap that makes target-query/source-key similarity unreliable, error accumulation across the Markov chain of denoising steps, and an architectural constraint where self-attention operates within the residual branch, bounding the influence of injected features on the target latent. As a result, these methods produce insufficient stylization and fail to faithfully reproduce fine texture and style details from the reference image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251679/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion UNet (512×512 base resolution), requiring no fine-tuning of the diffusion model. The reference image is encoded once to extract KV features at each timestep; optionally, a content image provides query features for structure preservation, a text prompt conditions generation, and segmentation maps or layout images guide controlled texture synthesis. For texture expansion, the method extends to arbitrary resolutions (demonstrated up to 512×1536) via MultiDiffusion, and an optional VAE fine-tuning step on the reference image improves high-frequency reconstruction quality.

## Method

The core contribution is the Attention Distillation (AD) loss, defined as the L1 distance between the ideal stylization Self-Attn(Q, K_s, V_s) (reaggregating reference KV under target queries) and the current stylization Self-Attn(Q, K, V) (the target's own attention output), computed over the last 6 self-attention layers of the UNet. By optimizing a random latent via backpropagation through this loss, information flows through both the residual self-attention path and the identity connection, progressively narrowing the Q–K_s gap and enabling accurate feature aggregation. A complementary content loss (L1 between target Q and reference Q_c) preserves semantic structure for style/appearance transfer. For faster synthesis, the AD loss is integrated into DDIM sampling as an improved Classifier Guidance, where an Adam optimizer automatically manages the guidance strength at each timestep, eliminating the need for manual hyperparameter tuning. This guided sampling is compatible with ControlNet conditioning and MultiDiffusion for multi-resolution generation.

## Results

This paper is primarily qualitative, relying on visual comparisons across four tasks (style transfer, appearance transfer, style-specific text-to-image generation, and controlled texture synthesis) rather than standard quantitative metrics. The only quantitative evaluation is a user preference study with 1500 responses from 50 participants across 30 questions (5 per competitor × 6 competitors), where the authors report that their method 'consistently outperforms the alternatives by significant margins' (Fig. 13), though specific preference percentages are deferred to the supplementary. No FID, LPIPS, or other numerical benchmarks are reported in the main text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 13 baselines across four tasks, all evaluated qualitatively. For style transfer (Fig. 6), the method outperforms CSGO [63], StyleShot [33], StyleID [10], StyTR² [12], and NST [22], with the authors noting that baselines exhibit 'notable style discrepancies' while their approach captures coherent style while preserving content structure. For appearance transfer (Fig. 6), it beats Cross-Image Attention [2] (which suffers from color oversaturation) and SpliceViT [57]. For style-specific text-to-image generation (Fig. 7), it matches InstantStyle [59], Visual Style Prompting [29], and RB-Modulation [49] on text alignment but achieves 'notably better style coherence' with the reference. For controlled texture synthesis (Fig. 9), it outperforms Self-Rectification [70] in texture fidelity (though Self-Rectification has smoother transitions) and GCD [69] in edge quality (GCD shows color aliasing artifacts). For texture expansion to 512×1536 (Fig. 10), it shows 'significant advantages' over both GCD [69] and GPDM [16]. The user preference study (Fig. 13) confirms consistent wins over all six tested competitors (including CIA [2] and VSP [29]) across the three transfer tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the KV-injection paradigm (Cross-Image Attention, StyleID, Visual Style Prompting) that treats self-attention keys and values as plug-and-play appearance attributes, identifying three concrete limitations: domain gap between target queries and source keys, error accumulation across layers, and the bounded influence of features injected only into the residual branch. Rather than swapping KV features during sampling, the authors propose an Attention Distillation (AD) loss that computes the L1 difference between the 'ideal' attention output (target Q with source KV) and the 'current' output (target Q with its own KV), then backpropagates through the full UNet to update the latent. The AD loss is further integrated into DDIM sampling as an improved Classifier Guidance (building on Dhariwal & Nichol's formulation), enabling text-conditioned generation and compatibility with ControlNet without any model fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AD loss provides a single, training-free mechanism that unifies style transfer, appearance transfer, controlled texture synthesis, and style-specific text-to-image generation under one framework, reducing the need for task-specific architectures or fine-tuning. The finding that a 512×512-trained Stable Diffusion model can synthesize coherent 512×1536 textures via MultiDiffusion + AD guidance is practically significant for texture artists. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models (SDXL, video diffusion), how it behaves with non-UNet architectures (e.g., DiT), and whether the 100-step optimization-based variant can be further accelerated to match pure sampling speed while retaining quality.
