## Problem

Existing category-level articulated part pose estimation methods (e.g., GAPartNet) rely on multi-stage pipelines that first segment parts from point clouds and then estimate poses independently, causing error accumulation and loss of global context needed to resolve visual ambiguities (e.g., front vs. back of a laptop lid). These approaches also depend primarily on geometric cues while neglecting dense RGB semantic information, and the synthetic datasets they use lack photorealistic rendering and realistic sensor noise, creating a significant sim-to-real gap for robotic deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB-D image patch (640×640) of a target object, requiring only an object-level mask rather than part-level annotations. The depth map is back-projected into a 24,576-point cloud that is fused point-wise with RGB features. The network outputs, per articulated part instance: a 6D pose (rotation ∈ SO(3) and translation ∈ ℝ³), a 3D size vector, along with intermediate per-point predictions of semantic class labels, centroid offsets, and discrete NPCS coordinates (32 bins per axis).

## Method

CAP-Net is a single-stage, end-to-end network that jointly predicts semantic labels, centroid offsets, and NPCS maps in one forward pass over the whole object. It extracts dense semantic features using pretrained SAM2 (96-dim) and FeatUp/DINOv2 (384-dim) encoders, concatenates them point-wise with the 3D point cloud, and processes the enriched features through a PointNet++ backbone. Three parallel heads then predict: (1) per-point part class via Focal Loss, (2) per-point centroid offset via L1 loss (enabling DBSCAN-based instance clustering), and (3) per-point NPCS via 32-bin-per-axis classification with cross-entropy. Final pose and size are recovered by RANSAC outlier removal followed by Umeyama alignment between predicted NPCS and observed points. The key insight is that processing the complete object (rather than isolated segmented parts) preserves global context to resolve pose ambiguities, and RGB semantic cues are essential for segmenting small parts that geometric features alone cannot distinguish.

## Results

On the RGBD-Art dataset, CAP-Net achieves a rotation error of 10.39° and 3D mIoU of 56.23%, dramatically outperforming the prior state-of-the-art GAPartNet (83.3° rotation error, 39.53% mIoU). The accuracy at 5°/5cm threshold (A5) jumps from 0.71% (GAPartNet) to 33.91%, and A10 from 1.40% to 58.44%. For part segmentation, CAP-Net reaches 53.58% AP50 on seen categories and 19.38% on unseen categories, versus 11.35% and 6.59% for GAPartNet. In real-world robot manipulation, CAP-Net achieves a 28/30 success rate compared to GAPartNet's 5/30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAP-Net decisively outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the primary SOTA baseline GAPartNet [10], the improvement is most dramatic on rotation error (10.39° vs. 83.3°, an ~8× reduction) and on the A5/A10 accuracy thresholds (33.91%/58.44% vs. 0.71%/1.40%), which the paper attributes to GAPartNet's two-stage pipeline losing global context and its reliance on 180° mirror-symmetry tolerance for hinged parts. On part segmentation (Table 2), CAP-Net achieves 53.58% AP50 on seen categories versus 11.35% for GAPartNet, 6.22% for AutoGPart [20], and 4.44% for SoftGroup [32], with a similar gap on unseen categories (19.38% vs. 6.59%). PointGroup [13] and AutoGPart [20], adapted for part-based pose estimation, perform comparably to each other but remain far below CAP-Net on all pose metrics (e.g., Re of 89.30° and 99.40° respectively vs. 10.39°). In the real-world robot manipulation experiment, CAP-Net achieves 28/30 task successes (hinge handle, drawer, hinge lid) while GAPartNet manages only 5/30, confirming the practical advantage of the single-shot, RGB-informed approach over the point-cloud-only two-stage pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the category-level articulated object pose estimation line initiated by CArt (ANCSH) and GAPartNet, but departs fundamentally from their two-stage 'segment-then-pose' pipelines by unifying segmentation, instance clustering, and NPCS prediction into a single end-to-end network. The key conceptual shift is the incorporation of dense RGB semantic features (via SAM2 and FeatUp/DINOv2) fused point-wise with the depth-derived point cloud, whereas prior methods relied almost exclusively on geometric cues from point clouds. The NPCS representation itself is inherited from NOCS, discretized into 32 bins per axis, and the final pose recovery uses the standard RANSAC + Umeyama alignment pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RGB semantic cues are not merely complementary but essential for accurate articulated part perception, particularly for small components (buttons, knobs) where noisy consumer-grade depth alone is insufficient. The RGBD-Art dataset, with photorealistic rendering and simulated sensor noise, addresses a critical sim-to-real gap that has limited prior synthetic datasets, and the 93% robot manipulation success rate (vs. 17% for GAPartNet) validates practical utility with affordable hardware. A key limitation is the reliance on depth for pose fitting—missing surface points degrade accuracy—and the authors acknowledge depth completion as future work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-joint articulations and whether the heavy pretrained backbones (SAM2, FeatUp) can be replaced by lighter alternatives for real-time deployment.
