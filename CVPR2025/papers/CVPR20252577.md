## Problem

Score-based generative models for human motion synthesis suffer from curved SDE trajectories that cause unstable training and slow inference, while flow-matching alternatives that straighten trajectories sacrifice output diversity. The paper addresses how to simultaneously achieve straight (easy-to-train) generation paths and high diversity in synthesized 3D human motions without the computational overhead of iterative denoising or additional score-estimation networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252577/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a single sample from a standard Gaussian distribution; for conditional (Action-to-Motion) generation, it additionally conditions on a discrete action label. The output is a full 3D human motion sequence of V joints × 3 coordinates over T timesteps, produced by first generating a discrete latent code in a 512×512 VQVAE codebook and then decoding it through a Transformer+GRU decoder. The diversity strength η (set to 0.1) and the number of DivSDE sampling steps (100) control the trade-off between diversity and fidelity at inference time.

## Method

DSDFM operates in two stages. Stage 1 uses a VQVAE (Transformer encoder + GRU, 4 layers, 8 heads) to map motion sequences into a discrete latent space via a 512×512 codebook, learning a well-structured representation. Stage 2 builds a deterministic mapping (DerODE) between a standard Gaussian and the latent distribution using optimal-transport coupling: the drift is the constant vector z₁−z₀ along straight-line interpolations, trained with a drift-estimate loss plus a drift-consistent loss that enforces the same drift output across different time interpolations. At sampling time, a stochastic diverse output generation procedure (DivSDE) is applied on top of the DerODE result, injecting controlled noise via a reverse-time SDE whose score function is computed analytically from the DerODE output—eliminating the need for a separate score network or any additional training. This separation of deterministic mapping (training) from stochastic diversification (inference-only) is the key architectural distinction from prior diffusion or flow-matching approaches.

## Results

On the HumanAct12 unconditional generation task, DSDFM achieves FID 12.86, KID 0.10, Precision 0.75, Recall 0.85, and Diversity 18.41 with only 15M parameters, outperforming the strongest baseline Modi (FID 13.03, KID 0.12, Precision 0.71, Recall 0.81, Diversity 17.57, 23M params). On the Action-to-Motion task (HumanAct12), DSDFM achieves FID 0.068 and Accuracy 0.994, marginally surpassing MotionDiffuse (FID 0.070, Accuracy 0.992) while using 15M vs. 25M parameters. Training time is reduced to 25.33 min vs. 42.93 min (VPSDE) and 40.57 min (VESDE) over 500 epochs, and inference at 500 steps takes 5.03 s vs. 9.93 s (VPSDE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSDFM outperforms all seven baselines on the unconditional HumanAct12 task across every metric. The closest competitor, Modi (CVPR'23), is beaten by 1.31% on FID (12.86 vs. 13.03), 4.17% on Precision (0.75 vs. 0.71), 4.93% on Recall (0.85 vs. 0.81), and 4.78% on Diversity (18.41 vs. 17.57), while using 35% fewer parameters (15M vs. 23M). MLD (CVPR'23) and MDM (ICLR'23) show substantially larger gaps, with MDM's FID of 31.92 being more than 2.4× worse than DSDFM's. On the conditional Action-to-Motion task, DSDFM achieves the best FID (0.068) and Accuracy (0.994), narrowly edging out MotionDiffuse (FID 0.070, Accuracy 0.992) and MLD (FID 0.077, Accuracy 0.964), though it trails MotionDiffuse by 0.01% on Diversity. Older baselines (VPoser, Action2Motion, ACTOR, INR) are clearly outperformed, with FID gaps exceeding 0.05 on the conditional task and exceeding 35 on the unconditional task. Notably, DSDFM uses the fewest parameters (15M) among all compared methods while achieving the best or near-best results on both tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: VQVAE-based latent representation learning for motion, flow matching / optimal transport for distribution coupling, and score-based SDEs for generative sampling. It departs from standard diffusion models (DDPM, VPSDE/VESDE) and Flow Matching by replacing their curved, multi-step denoising trajectories with a straight deterministic ODE (DerODE) trained via OT-coupled pairs, and then augments inference with a lightweight stochastic SDE (DivSDE) that injects diversity without any additional training. The key architectural departure is the clean separation: the expensive learning happens entirely in the deterministic stage, while diversity is achieved purely at sampling time through a closed-form score derived from the DerODE output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in training efficiency (roughly 40% faster than VPSDE/VESDE baselines) and parameter reduction (15M vs. 20–27M), which makes high-quality motion synthesis more accessible. However, the performance gains over the strongest baselines (Modi, MLD, MotionDiffuse) are modest—single-digit percentage improvements on FID and diversity—and the conditional Action-to-Motion results show DSDFM is essentially on par with MotionDiffuse rather than clearly superior. Open questions include whether the OT coupling strategy generalizes to richer conditioning signals (text, music, audio) beyond action labels, whether the fixed η diversity knob can be made adaptive per sample, and whether the VQVAE discretization bottleneck limits expressibility for longer or more complex motions.
