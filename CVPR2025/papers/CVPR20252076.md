## Problem

Despite advances in diffusion-based image generation, realistic hand synthesis remains a persistent challenge due to complex finger articulation, diverse viewpoints, and frequent occlusions. Existing approaches either lack sufficient hand-specific training data or rely on 3D hand templates (e.g., MANO) that are expensive to obtain and often inaccurate, leaving no scalable, controllable solution for generating single or dual hand images with precise pose and viewpoint control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252076/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a reference hand image (encoded to a 4×32×32 latent via a pretrained VAE), 42-channel Gaussian keypoint heatmaps (21 per hand, zero-padded for absent hands), and a 1-channel hand segmentation mask as spatially-aligned inputs at 256×256 resolution. It produces a denoised target hand image conditioned on target keypoint heatmaps, and through stochastic reference switching during denoising, extends to novel-view sequences and temporally coherent video frames without explicit 3D or camera parameter inputs.

## Method

FoundHand formulates hand generation as a 2D-pose-conditioned image-to-image diffusion task, training on frame pairs from hand videos (for pose transfer) and synchronized multi-view frames (for viewpoint transfer) using a binary flag to distinguish the two. The architecture builds on a DiT-XL backbone (28 blocks, 0.6B parameters) with 3D self-attention across reference and target tokens, and a shared-weight embedder that fuses spatially-aligned image latents, keypoint heatmaps, and segmentation masks into coherent feature patches. Key design choices include encoding 2D keypoints as multi-channel Gaussian heatmaps to disentangle overlapping fingers, leveraging REPA alignment with DINOv2 features for improved training efficiency, and applying condition dropout to make reference and mask inputs optional at inference. For novel view synthesis and video generation, the model employs stochastic conditioning that randomly samples the reference from previously generated frames at each denoising step, providing both long-term scene consistency and short-term temporal coherence without any video-specific training.

## Results

On gesture transfer (Table 1), FoundHand achieves PSNR 30.96, SSIM 0.82, LPIPS 0.20, and FID 2.58, substantially outperforming the closest baseline CosHand (PSNR 26.21, FID 3.60). On novel view synthesis over InterHand2.6M (Table 3a), FoundHand reaches PSNR 27.72 and LPIPS 0.10, a 39% PSNR improvement over the best baseline ImageDream (PSNR 19.97). On zero-shot video synthesis (Table 3b), FoundHand attains PSNR 24.08 and LPIPS 0.17, surpassing ControlNeXt (PSNR 17.64) and AnimateAnyone (PSNR 15.76) despite being trained only on image pairs rather than video sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoundHand outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. In gesture transfer, the closest competitor CosHand [94] achieves PSNR 26.21 and FID 3.60, while FoundHand leads by 4.75 dB in PSNR and 1.02 in FID; the general-purpose control models Uni-ControlNet [71] and AnyControl [96] trail by over 20 dB in PSNR, and the task-specific GestureGAN [99] shows severe overfitting (PSNR 11.18). For novel view synthesis on InterHand2.6M, FoundHand (PSNR 27.72) beats ImageDream [103] (19.97) by 7.75 dB and ZeroNVS [84] (19.21) by 8.51 dB, while Zero123 [44] (11.37) is far behind; notably, FoundHand achieves this without explicit 3D supervision or costly test-time SDS optimization. In zero-shot video synthesis, FoundHand (PSNR 24.08, LPIPS 0.17) surpasses ControlNeXt [67] (17.64, 0.29) by 6.44 dB and AnimateAnyone [31] (15.76, 0.35) by 8.32 dB, despite not being explicitly trained on video sequences. The paper also demonstrates that domain-transferred data from FoundHand improves downstream 3D hand estimation (HaMeR [65]), reducing PA-MPJPE from 14.41 to 13.68 and F@5 from 0.30 to 0.46 on EPIC-KITCHENS.

## Relation to Prior Work

FoundHand departs from prior hand generation methods (GestureGAN, HandDiffuser, CosHand) that rely on 3D MANO templates or task-specific conditioning by adopting 2D keypoint heatmaps as a universal control signal that simultaneously encodes articulation and camera viewpoint. Architecturally, it builds on the DiT-XL latent diffusion transformer backbone and incorporates REPA alignment with DINOv2 features for training efficiency, while borrowing the 3D self-attention mechanism from video diffusion models and repurposing it for a two-frame image-to-image translation setting. The hand-fixing application adapts the Repaint inpainting strategy, and the overall formulation as pose-conditioned image-to-image translation (rather than text-to-image) is inspired by video diffusion pipelines but avoids their computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of FoundHand-10M — the first large-scale, unified hand dataset with consistent 2D keypoint and segmentation annotations across 12 source datasets — addresses a fundamental data bottleneck that has limited hand generation research. The emergent zero-shot capabilities (HOI video synthesis without object supervision, video generation without video training) suggest that domain-specific scaling can yield implicit physical understanding, though these claims rest on limited quantitative evidence (12 video clips, qualitative HOI demos). The 256×256 resolution constraint from the 32×32 latent space is a significant practical limitation, and open questions include whether super-resolution can close this gap and whether the 2D-keypoint formulation generalizes to higher-fidelity or real-time XR avatar applications.
