## Problem

Deep stereo matching suffers from limited generalization due to constrained training data and fails on critical conditions—textureless regions, occlusions, and non-Lambertian surfaces (mirrors, glass)—that violate the geometric assumptions linking pixel correspondences to 3D geometry. Meanwhile, monocular depth Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) trained on millions of samples handle these conditions well but are vulnerable to perspective illusions and scale ambiguity. The paper addresses the gap by combining stereo geometric constraints with monocular VFM priors in a single architecture that is robust where either modality alone fails.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a rectified stereo image pair (I_L, I_R ∈ R^{3×H×W}) and additionally feeds both images through a frozen monocular depth VFM (Depth Anything v2, Large) to obtain auxiliary depth maps M_L, M_R. The output is a full-resolution disparity map D, upsampled from 1/4 resolution via convex upsampling. The VFM remains frozen throughout training; only the stereo network parameters are optimized, making the approach a transfer-learning setup where the VFM acts as a fixed feature provider.

## Method

Stereo Anywhere is a dual-branch iterative architecture built on RAFT-Stereo. The stereo branch constructs a standard correlation volume from learned image features, while the monocular branch computes surface normals from VFM depth maps to build a second correlation volume, segmented by relative depth masks and aggregated through a 3D CNN with adapted CoEx excitation. A differentiable weighted least-squares module jointly estimates scale and shift to align monocular depth to metric disparity, guided by confidence maps derived from correlation-curve entropy. Three volume augmentations (rolling, noising, zeroing) plus a hand-crafted volume truncation for mirror surfaces teach the network when to suppress unreliable stereo costs. The iterative refinement stage extends the Multi-GRU update with a second lookup operator that retrieves features from the monocular volume, allowing the network to iteratively fuse stereo and mono evidence at each of 12 (training) or 32 (inference) iterations.

## Results

On zero-shot generalization (trained only on SceneFlow), Stereo Anywhere achieves 6.96% bad>2 (All) on Middlebury 2014, 3.90% bad>3 (All) on KITTI 2012, and 3.93% bad>3 (All) on KITTI 2015, consistently outperforming all 10 compared baselines across all five standard benchmarks. On non-Lambertian surfaces, it reduces the bad>2 error on Booster from 17.84% (RAFT-Stereo) to 9.01% and the average pixel error from 3.59 to 1.21 px, while on LayeredFlow it cuts the average error from 19.27 to 11.20 px. On the novel MonoTrap dataset of optical illusions, Stereo Anywhere achieves 3.50% AbsRel and 80.27% δ<1.05, outperforming its RAFT-Stereo baseline (5.01%, 77.05%) while monocular VFMs fail catastrophically (Depth Anything v2: 53.46% AbsRel).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stereo Anywhere wins against all 10 compared stereo baselines on every standard zero-shot benchmark. On Middlebury 2014, it achieves 6.96% bad>2 (All), beating the second-best DLNR (9.46%) by 2.5 points and RAFT-Stereo (11.15%) by 4.2 points; on KITTI 2012 and 2015 it breaks below 4% bad>3 (All), outperforming NMRF (4.62% / 5.24%) and RAFT-Stereo (4.80% / 5.44%). On occluded pixels (Occ metric), the margin is even larger: ~6% lower than the next best on Middlebury 2014 and KITTI 2012. On non-Lambertian surfaces (Booster, LayeredFlow), Stereo Anywhere is the clear winner, halving the bad>2 error versus RAFT-Stereo (9.01% vs 17.84%) and reducing average pixel error by ~67% on LayeredFlow (11.20 vs 19.27 px); even the fine-tuned DKT-RAFT (10.32% bad>2 on Booster) is outperformed. On MonoTrap, Stereo Anywhere slightly outperforms its own RAFT-Stereo baseline (3.50% vs 5.01% AbsRel), confirming it can ignore unreliable monocular priors, while monocular VFMs (Depth Anything v2: 53.46% AbsRel; DepthPro: 47.77%) fail catastrophically on optical illusions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAFT-Stereo iterative architecture [55] by introducing a dual-branch design that fuses a standard stereo correlation volume with a second correlation volume derived from monocular depth VFM predictions (Depth Anything v2). While prior work has loosely combined stereo and monocular cues in self-supervised settings [1, 12, 112], this is the first to tightly integrate a frozen VFM's depth priors into the cost volume construction, iterative GRU refinement, and a differentiable scaling module within a supervised stereo pipeline. The 3D convolutional regularization module follows IGEV-Stereo [116], and the correlation volume excitation adapts CoEx [4]. The MonoTrap dataset is entirely novel, filling a gap in evaluation for optical-illusion robustness that no prior stereo or monocular benchmark addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the data scarcity problem in stereo matching—particularly for non-Lambertian surfaces—can be circumvented by borrowing priors from monocular VFMs trained on millions of samples, without collecting new stereo data. This opens a practical path for deploying robust stereo in domains (mirrors, glass, specular materials) where annotated training data is nearly impossible to obtain. Limitations include dependence on VFM quality (a poor VFM degrades performance), the small size of MonoTrap (26 scenes), and the hand-crafted volume truncation for mirrors which may not generalize to all specular geometries. An open question is whether this VFM-in-the-loop paradigm extends to multi-view stereo or video stereo, where temporal consistency of the monocular branch becomes critical.
