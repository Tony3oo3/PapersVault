## Problem

Latent Diffusion Models produce drastically different outputs under even tiny input perturbations (e.g., 1/8-pixel shifts), undermining their use in tasks requiring spatial or temporal consistency. While anti-aliasing modules from StyleGAN3 partially address this in GANs, they are insufficient for LDMs because aliasing is amplified during VAE training and accumulates across multiple U-Net denoising steps, and self-attention inherently breaks shift-equivariance under cropped shifts.

## Input / Output

The model operates on 256×256 images compressed to 32×32×4 latents (8× downsampling). The core pipeline takes a noisy latent and a text prompt, producing a generated image; the equivariance property guarantees that a fractional shift Δ in the latent yields a k·Δ shift in the output image. For downstream applications, AF-SD accepts video frames with a new text prompt to produce temporally consistent edited videos, latent I²SB maps low-resolution latents to high-resolution ones for 4× super-resolution, and AF-YOSO takes an RGB image to produce a normal map, all while maintaining shift-equivariance.

## Method

AF-LDM combines three mechanisms: (1) anti-aliasing modules (FFT-based ideal resampling and filtered nonlinearities) in both the VAE and U-Net, (2) an equivariance loss L = ||f(T_Δ(x)) − T_{kΔ}(f(x))||² applied separately to the VAE encoder, VAE decoder, and U-Net, which forces the latent to behave as a band-limited continuous signal by penalizing high-frequency leakage, and (3) Equivariant Attention (EA), which fixes keys and values to a reference frame while queries shift with the input, making self-attention equivariant under arbitrary relative deformations. Critically, EA (equivalent in form to Cross-Frame Attention) must be active during both training and inference—using it only at test time, as in prior video editing work, is insufficient to suppress aliasing. The equivariance loss and EA are complementary: without EA, the loss would focus on correcting attention rather than aliasing, and without the loss, EA alone cannot prevent aliasing amplification during training.

## Results

On the Sintel warping-equivariance benchmark, AF-SD achieves a Generation Warping PSNR of 26.10, substantially outperforming SD (21.95) by over 4 dB, and an Inversion Warping PSNR of 19.68 vs. SD's 17.53. In the VAE ablation on ImageNet, AF-VAE with equivariance loss reaches an encoder SPSNR of 45.10 and decoder SPSNR of 33.88, compared to 25.07 and 22.31 for the original SD VAE. For the full LDM pipeline on FFHQ, AF-LDM attains a latent SPSNR of 40.94 and image SPSNR of 28.06, versus 27.47 and 12.38 for the standard LDM with SD VAE, while maintaining competitive FID (19.05 vs. 14.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original Stable Diffusion (SD) [Rombach et al., CVPR 2022], AF-SD achieves a 4.15 dB improvement in Generation Warping PSNR (26.10 vs. 21.95) and a 2.15 dB gain in Inversion Warping PSNR (19.68 vs. 17.53) on Sintel, demonstrating superior preservation of deformation information through the diffusion pipeline. For 4× super-resolution via latent I²SB [Liu et al., 2023], the standard latent I²SB exhibits severe flickering and quality degradation under input shifts, while the AF-LDM variant produces consistent, shift-equivariant outputs (Fig. 9). For normal estimation via YOSO [Xu et al., 2024], vanilla YOSO shows residual flickering around high-frequency textures, whereas AF-YOSO delivers fully consistent results across the entire image (Fig. 10). In all three downstream comparisons, AF-LDM/AF-SD wins decisively on shift-equivariance and temporal consistency, at the cost of a modest FID increase (19.05 vs. 14.91) in the unconditional generation setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the alias-free neural network line of work (StyleGAN3, Alias-Free Convnets) from single-stage GAN synthesis to the two-stage LDM pipeline, where equivariance must hold jointly across the VAE encoder, iterative U-Net denoising, and VAE decoder. It departs from prior anti-aliasing approaches by showing that architectural modifications alone are insufficient in LDMs—aliasing actually amplifies during training—necessitating a new equivariance loss as a training objective. The equivariant attention mechanism repurposes Cross-Frame Attention (CFA) from video editing literature, reframing it not as an inference-time consistency trick but as a fundamental requirement for shift-equivariance that must be active during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since LDMs underpin virtually all modern image and video generation, establishing a principled path to shift-equivariance has broad practical impact for video editing, super-resolution, and any task requiring spatial consistency. The finding that anti-aliasing modules degrade over training (rather than merely failing to help) challenges the assumption that architectural band-limiting is a one-time fix, suggesting that equivariance must be actively enforced throughout learning. Open questions include whether the equivariance loss scales to larger resolutions and model sizes without further FID degradation (the full AF-LDM shows FID 19.05 vs. 14.91 for the baseline), and whether the approach extends to 3D/video diffusion or transformer-based backbones like DiT.
