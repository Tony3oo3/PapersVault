## Problem

Existing 4D reconstruction methods are either restricted to specific object categories (humans, pets) or rely on per-scene optimization that takes minutes to hours. The paper asks whether a feed-forward, category-agnostic approach—analogous to LRM for static 3D—can reconstruct a tracked, continuously deforming 3D asset from video in seconds, while producing a mesh with fixed topology and shared UV texture suitable for production pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251385/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LIM consumes multi-view RGB images (4 views) at two discrete keyframe timesteps k and k+1, along with a continuous interpolation parameter α ∈ [0,1], and outputs a triplane at the intermediate timestep k+α. For monocular video, a pretrained diffusion model first synthesizes 3 additional views per frame to convert the problem into the multi-view setting. Beyond the implicit triplane, the system outputs a time-deforming mesh extracted via Marching Cubes with time-invariant faces and a shared UV texture map, plus per-vertex canonical surface coordinates that enable cross-timestep correspondence.

## Method

LIM is a 6-block transformer that takes intermediate features from the last 6 blocks of a frozen 12-block multi-view LRM (conditioned on keyframe k), concatenates a positional encoding of α, and refines them via cross-attention with DinoV2 image tokens from keyframe k+1 to predict the interpolated triplane. The key training innovation is a causal consistency loss: it enforces that a triplane interpolated directly from k to k+δ must match one obtained by first interpolating to a random intermediate time k+α_rand and then further interpolating to k+δ, where the second pass feeds LIM's own intermediate features back as input. This self-supervised constraint exposes the model to arbitrary continuous α values during training, enabling smooth temporal interpolation. For mesh tracking, a parallel overline-LRM/overline-LIM pair predicts and interpolates canonical-coordinate triplanes; Marching Cubes extracts meshes at each timestep, and vertices are matched across consecutive frames via nearest-neighbor search in the canonical coordinate space, yielding a consistently textured deformable mesh.

## Results

On the triplane interpolation benchmark (256 held-out animated scenes), LIM achieves PSNR 23.11, PSNR_FG 16.12, and LPIPS 0.075, outperforming linear triplane interpolation (PSNR 20.96) and the FiLM image-based interpolator (PSNR 22.05), while approaching the Oracle upper bound (PSNR 24.43). For full monocular 4D reconstruction, LIM (combined with a diffusion-based multiview generator) achieves LPIPS 0.142 and FVD 811.1 in ~3 minutes, substantially outperforming Consistent4D (LPIPS 0.429, FVD 1136.3, ~1.5 hours) and per-frame TripoSR (LPIPS 0.504, FVD 1427.2, ~30 s). Ablating the causal consistency loss degrades PSNR from 23.11 to 22.2 and LPIPS from 0.075 to 0.084, confirming its importance for temporal smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIM outperforms the FiLM image-based interpolator [41] on all three metrics (PSNR 23.11 vs. 22.05, PSNR_FG 16.12 vs. 14.98, LPIPS 0.075 vs. 0.082), with the gap attributed to FiLM's view-inconsistencies producing ghosting artifacts around dynamic parts. Against Consistent4D [18], LIM achieves a 3× lower LPIPS (0.142 vs. 0.429) and 29% lower FVD (811.1 vs. 1136.3) while running ~30× faster (~3 min vs. ~1.5 hours), since LIM is feed-forward whereas Consistent4D requires per-scene optimization. Compared to per-frame TripoSR [50], LIM reduces LPIPS by 72% (0.142 vs. 0.504) and FVD by 43% (811.1 vs. 1427.2), though TripoSR is faster per individual frame (~30 s vs. ~3 min for the full sequence); the large quality gap stems from TripoSR's single-view conditioning lacking temporal coherence. In the mesh-tracking evaluation, LIM's canonical-coordinate interpolation also beats a nearest-neighbor tracing baseline (PSNR 21.56 vs. 20.33, LPIPS 0.096 vs. 0.122), demonstrating that dense implicit interpolation avoids the correspondence problem inherent in keyframe-to-keyframe matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIM is built directly on the multi-view Large Reconstruction Model (LRM), reusing its pretrained 12-layer transformer and triplane output as the foundation for a 6-block interpolation module. It departs from optimization-based 4D methods (Consistent4D, DreamGaussian4D, 4Dify) by eliminating per-scene optimization entirely, and from the feed-forward L4GM by adding continuous-time interpolation and explicit mesh tracking via canonical surface coordinates. The novel causal consistency loss is a self-supervised mechanism with no direct precedent in the 4D reconstruction literature, enforcing that multi-step interpolation is equivalent to direct interpolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LIM is the first feed-forward system to produce production-ready, time-deforming mesh sequences with fixed topology and shared UV texture, which is directly applicable in gaming and animation pipelines where Gaussian splat outputs are unusable. The ~3-minute inference time versus ~1.5 hours for optimization-based baselines makes 4D asset creation practically accessible. Key limitations include training exclusively on synthetic artist-created meshes (raising open questions about real-world generalization) and the restriction to interpolation rather than extrapolation, both of which the authors flag as important future directions.
