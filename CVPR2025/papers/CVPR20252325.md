## Problem

Existing perception and prediction benchmarks (KITTI, nuScenes, Waymo) are designed for autonomous driving on structured roads with sensors mounted on vehicles, leaving a critical gap for social mobile robots that navigate crowded, unstructured environments (parks, campuses, sidewalks) at low speeds (<1 m/s). In these scenarios, surrounding obstacles are much closer to the ego-agent (distribution mode at ~5 m), causing frequent truncation and occlusion that degrade near-field perception, yet no dataset or evaluation protocol specifically targets this regime.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 133K+ synchronized frames at 10 FPS from 4 pinhole cameras (1920×1080, 25 Hz), 4 fisheye cameras (1280×720, 25 Hz), 5 LiDARs spliced into full 360° point clouds, and GPS/IMU, with annotations at 1 Hz keyframe frequency. A key design choice is the novel Closest Collision-Point Distance Proportion (CCDP) matching criterion, which thresholds true positives by a relative proportion (5%, 10%, 20%) of the ground-truth closest collision-point distance from the ego-vehicle rather than an absolute center distance or IoU, reflecting the safety-critical importance of near-obstacle localization. Outputs span 3D bounding boxes (x,y,z,w,l,h,θ,cls) with persistent track IDs forming 216K trajectories, dense voxel occupancy labels (occupied/free/unknown with 3-class semantics), and K future trajectory hypotheses over 3 s horizons.

## Method

The core contribution is a large-scale egocentric multimodal dataset (RoboSense) collected over 42 hours across 22 locations in 6 scene categories using a robosweeper platform, combined with a three-stage auto-labelling pipeline that leverages the top-mounted Hesai Pandar40M LiDAR to generate initial 3D box annotations for spliced multi-LiDAR point clouds and multi-view images. The paper formulates six standardized benchmarks (multi-view 3D detection, LiDAR 3D detection, multi-modal 3D detection, 3D MOT, motion prediction, occupancy prediction) with a unified CCDP-based evaluation protocol, and provides baselines by adapting existing architectures: PointPillar/SECOND/PV-RCNN/Transfusion-L for LiDAR detection, BEVDet/BEVDet4D/BEVDepth/BEVFormer for vision-based detection, AB3DMOT for tracking, and ViP3D/PnPNet for end-to-end motion prediction. Occupancy labels are generated by voxelizing 3D space and performing temporal segmentation of dynamic vs. static content using annotated boxes and trajectories over a ±10-frame window. The dataset is partitioned 50/40/10 (train/test/val) with one scene (S-6, predominantly night) reserved exclusively for testing, and all data undergoes face/plate/sign masking for privacy.

## Results

On the RoboSense validation set, Transfusion-L achieves the best LiDAR 3D detection with Vehicle 3D AP of 83.6% (CP) / 65.1% (CCP), Cyclist 59.7% / 47.0%, and Pedestrian 72.3% / 42.8%. Among multi-view image-based detectors, BEVFormer leads with Vehicle 3D AP of 78.2% / 32.0%, Cyclist 44.1% / 27.6%, and Pedestrian 50.2% / 32.3%. The CCP matching criterion reveals a substantial performance drop (e.g., 18.5% AP loss for Transfusion-L on Vehicle) compared to center-point matching, highlighting the difficulty of near-field localization. For motion prediction, PnPNet achieves the best minADE of 0.89 m and EPA of 0.313, while occupancy prediction with 4F sensors yields mIoU-3D of 24.6% overall and 39.6% within 2 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LiDAR detectors, Transfusion-L [1] clearly outperforms PointPillar [15] (+11.1% Vehicle CP AP), SECOND [39] (+4.8%), and PV-RCNN [29] (+9.0%), confirming the advantage of transformer architectures for 3D detection. In the multi-view image track, BEVFormer [17] and BEVDepth [16] both surpass the original BEVDet [12] by ~2% CP AP, with BEVFormer edging ahead on Pedestrian (50.2% vs. 50.1%). LiDAR-based methods substantially outperform vision-based methods under CCP matching (e.g., Transfusion-L 65.1% vs. BEVFormer 32.0% for Vehicle), but the gap narrows under CP matching. For motion prediction, PnPNet [18] (LiDAR-based) beats ViP3D [9] (vision-based) on all metrics (minADE 0.89 vs. 1.31 m; EPA 0.313 vs. 0.283), and both significantly outperform constant-velocity baselines. The multi-modal late-fusion setup (BEVDepth + PointPillar, 8V+4L) boosts CCP-based 3D AP within 5 m from 20.5% to 36.9%, yet remains far below LiDAR-only performance at farther ranges, underscoring the persistent challenge of near-field perception in crowded scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-sensor perception dataset lineage (KITTI, nuScenes, Waymo) by targeting a fundamentally different operational domain: social mobile robots navigating crowded, unstructured environments rather than cars on structured roads. The sensor suite (4 pinhole cameras + 4 fisheye cameras + 4 LiDARs) and the novel Closest-Collision-Point-Distance-Proportion (CCDP) matching criterion represent deliberate departures from prior conventions that use center-distance or IoU thresholds. For occupancy prediction, the authors repurpose BEVDepth's BEV feature encoders with new reconstruction heads, and for tracking they adopt the AB3DMOT baseline, positioning RoboSense as a natural extension of existing task formulations into the near-field egocentric setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoboSense is the first large-scale dataset purpose-built for egocentric robot perception in crowded social environments, with 270× and 18× more near-range (≤5 m) annotations than KITTI and nuScenes respectively, filling a critical gap for social robotics research. The CCP matching criterion exposes a substantial and previously unquantified performance gap: even the best multi-modal detector achieves only 36.9% 3D AP within 5 m under CCP, revealing that current methods struggle with the truncation and occlusion inherent to near-field perception. Open questions include whether joint multi-task optimization (detection + tracking + prediction + occupancy) can close this gap, and whether the low occupancy prediction scores (~25% mIoU-3D) reflect a fundamental difficulty in dense near-field voxel reconstruction from sparse camera views.
