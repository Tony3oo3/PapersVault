## Problem

Self-supervised low-light image enhancement methods either rely on hand-crafted priors (smoothness, brightness) that lack semantic awareness, or incorporate a single high-level model for guidance without exploiting the complementary strengths of multiple vision foundation models. The paper asks how to collaboratively leverage diverse foundation models (CLIP, SAM, ResNet) to simultaneously improve visual quality and downstream task performance in unsupervised LIE.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251333/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The enhancer G takes a low-light RGB image (resized to 448×448) and produces a well-lit enhanced RGB image. Training is fully self-supervised: low-light images are sampled from ExDark and normal-light images from COCO, with no paired data required. During training, frozen foundation models (CLIP, SAM, ResNet) consume the enhanced outputs as feature extractors and task evaluators, while the enhancement model itself is trained with Adam at lr=1e-4, batch size 32, over 50 epochs.

## Method

FoCo introduces two complementary alignment strategies on top of a baseline prior-based enhancer. FoFA (Foundation Feature-space Alignment) projects features from CLIP, SAM, and ResNet into a unified space via convolutional projectors, then trains a discriminator with cross-attention (spatial and channel) between image features and the unified semantic features; the discriminator simultaneously aligns its internal features with foundation model outputs and distinguishes enhanced from high-quality images. GaTA (Gap-driven Task-based Alignment) exploits the robustness gap between CLIP (robust) and ResNet (degradation-sensitive): the robust model generates pseudo-labels on enhanced images, corrected via a memory-bank voting mechanism over T iterations, and the sensitive model's task loss on those pseudo-labels penalizes residual degradation. Both foundation models remain frozen throughout, and the overall objective combines the baseline prior loss, the FoFA adversarial loss, and the GaTA task loss.

## Results

On the FiveK dataset, FoCo achieves PSNR 19.58, SSIM 0.83, and LPIPS 0.13, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline CoLIE (18.71/0.82/0.16) and approaching supervised Retinexformer (23.28/0.84/0.24). On Huawei, FoCo reaches SSIM 0.72 (vs. 0.61 for SKF) and PSNR 18.61, while on Nikon it attains PSNR 17.76 and SSIM 0.67. For downstream tasks, FoCo achieves 74.2% top-1 accuracy on CODaN (EfficientNet), 0.683 AP on DarkFace (S3FD), and 0.281 mAP_seg on LIS (Mask R-CNN), all leading among unsupervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unsupervised methods, FoCo achieves the best SSIM on all three enhancement datasets (0.72/0.67/0.83) and the best LPIPS on FiveK (0.13 vs. 0.16 for CoLIE) and Nikon (0.37 vs. 0.38 for SCI). The only unsupervised method that beats FoCo on some enhancement metrics is SKF, which achieves higher PSNR (19.31 vs. 18.61) and lower LPIPS (0.32 vs. 0.35) on Huawei, though FoCo substantially outperforms SKF on SSIM (0.72 vs. 0.61). On high-level tasks, FoCo leads all unsupervised baselines: it surpasses CoLIE by 1.5% on CODaN ResNet18 accuracy (0.588 vs. 0.573), SGZ by 6.6% on CODaN EfficientNet (0.742 vs. 0.676), SGZ by 1.4% on DarkFace S3FD AP (0.683 vs. 0.669), and SCL-LLE by 1.4% on LIS mAP_box (0.331 vs. 0.317). Against supervised methods, FoCo remains competitive on SSIM and LPIPS but trails on PSNR (e.g., 19.58 vs. 23.28 on FiveK vs. Retinexformer), which is expected given the absence of paired training data. The largest gains over prior unsupervised work appear on downstream tasks, where the multi-VFM collaboration strategy yields consistent 1–7% improvements across classification, detection, and segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised low-light enhancement (LIE) line of work by departing from both hand-crafted prior-based methods (ZeroDCE, SCI) and single-model high-level guidance approaches (SGZ, SKF, CLIPLIT). It builds on the adversarial training framework of SCI and the cross-attention mechanism of SKF, but introduces a multi-foundation-model collaboration paradigm where CLIP, SAM, and ResNet jointly supervise the enhancer at both feature and task levels. The key conceptual departure is treating the robustness gap between a strong model (CLIP) and a weaker one (ResNet) as an exploitable signal for degradation detection, rather than relying on a single semantic teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that combining complementary foundation models—where each contributes different types of information (CLIP for object-level semantics, SAM for fine-grained structure, ResNet for degradation sensitivity)—yields consistent gains across enhancement quality and diverse downstream tasks (classification, face detection, instance segmentation). A practical limitation is the computational overhead of maintaining multiple frozen foundation models and a memory bank during training, and the approach's dependence on the specific robustness ordering between CLIP and ResNet may not transfer to other model pairs or degradation types. Open questions include whether the collaboration strategy generalizes to video enhancement, other low-level tasks (denoising, deblurring), or to newer foundation models like DINOv2 or MAE.
