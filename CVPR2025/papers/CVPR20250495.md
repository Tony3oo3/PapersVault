## Problem

LLM-generated class-specific descriptions for VLMs suffer from hallucination, producing inaccurate, non-visual, or non-discriminative text that degrades fine-grained classification. While automatic prompt optimization (APO) methods exist, they target only task-specific templates; extending optimization to class-specific descriptions causes an explosion in the search space, leading to prohibitive LLM querying costs, long iteration times, and severe overfitting where multiple prompts achieve identical training accuracy but divergent test performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and one labeled sample per class (one-shot supervision) to evaluate candidate prompts during optimization. It produces a set of optimized natural-language prompts (ensembled templates plus class-specific visual descriptions) that are plugged into a frozen CLIP model for inference. The optimized prompts are discrete text strings, making them directly transferable across different VLM backbones (ResNet-50, ResNet-101, ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16) without retraining, unlike continuous prompt-tuning vectors.

## Method

ProAPO iteratively refines prompts in two stages—first optimizing task-specific templates, then class-specific descriptions—using an evolution-based search over a pre-built prompt library generated by a single LLM query. Each iteration applies edit-based operators (add, remove, replace elements from the library) and evolution-based operators (crossover of two high-scoring candidates, mutation via random library insertion) to generate diverse candidates without additional LLM calls. Candidates are scored by a fitness function combining classification accuracy with an entropy constraint that penalizes low-confidence correct predictions, directly mitigating the overfitting observed when accuracy alone is used. Two sampling strategies reduce cost: prompt sampling selects the highest-scoring initial description ensemble per class, and group sampling restricts iterative optimization to the most confused or most gainable category groups rather than all classes. After T iterations (default 4), the top-scoring prompt ensemble is retained for classification.

## Results

On the ViT-B/32 backbone, ProAPO achieves 65.0% average top-1 accuracy across 13 datasets (67.9% on 11 datasets excluding CUB and Places), improving vanilla CLIP by 5.7% (59.3%→65.0%) and the strongest description-based baseline CuPL by 2.7% (62.3%→65.0%). On the ResNet-50 backbone, ProAPO reaches 61.8% average (64.6% on 11 datasets), surpassing the best prompt-tuning method ProGrad (62.7% on 11) and the automatic prompt optimization method PN (61.1% on 11). Gains are most pronounced on fine-grained datasets: ProAPO improves DCLIP by 5.4% on Flowers102 and by 21% on EuroSAT, while achieving 55.4% on CUB-200-2011 versus 53.3% for CuPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProAPO consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across both backbones. Against prompt-tuning methods (CoOp, PLOT, ProGrad), ProAPO wins by at least 1.9% average on 11 datasets while being training-free, demonstrating that discrete natural-language prompt search is more effective than gradient-based continuous prompt learning in one-shot settings. Compared to the closest automatic prompt optimization baseline PN (which only optimizes task-specific templates), ProAPO gains 3.5% on 11 datasets (64.6% vs. 61.1%) on ResNet-50, with the largest margins on fine-grained datasets like CUB (+50.7 vs. –) and DTD (+52.3 vs. 44.8). Among description-based methods, ProAPO surpasses the strongest baseline CuPL by 2.7% average (65.0% vs. 62.3% on ViT-B/32), with particularly large gains on EuroSAT (+12.5%) and Flowers102 (+4.8%). ProAPO also outperforms GPT4Vis (61.7%) and AdaptCLIP (61.7%) by 3.3% on average, confirming that iterative evolution-based refinement of class-specific descriptions is more effective than one-shot LLM generation. On coarse-grained datasets (ImageNet, Caltech, Places, SUN), gains over the best baselines are smaller (1–2%), which the authors attribute to less inter-class confusion in those categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automatic prompt optimization (APO) line initiated by PN (Liu et al., CVPR 2024), which used LLMs to iteratively refine task-specific templates, by progressing the optimization to class-specific descriptions where the search space explodes. It also builds on LLM-generated description methods (CuPL, DCLIP) that produce one-shot visual descriptions per category, but departs from them by treating those descriptions as an initial population for iterative evolutionary refinement rather than a fixed output. The key conceptual departure is the use of edit-based and evolution-based operators (add/delete/replace, crossover, mutation) over a pre-built prompt library to avoid repeated LLM queries, combined with an entropy-constrained fitness score to counter overfitting in the expanded class-specific search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProAPO provides a practical, training-free recipe for improving VLM classification in low-shot settings, with the notable advantage that optimized discrete prompts transfer across backbones (ResNet-50 → ViT-B/16) while continuous prompt-tuning methods (CoOp) collapse. However, gains on coarse-grained datasets (ImageNet, Caltech) are modest, and scene-recognition datasets (Places, SUN) remain limited by visual confusion that textual optimization alone cannot resolve. Open questions include whether the evolutionary search scales to very large category spaces (thousands of classes), whether the approach generalizes to other VLM architectures beyond CLIP, and whether the one-shot supervision requirement can be further relaxed.
