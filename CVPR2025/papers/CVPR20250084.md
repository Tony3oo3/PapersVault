## Problem

Existing self-attention modification methods for image prompting in diffusion models suffer from two issues: (1) they apply the image prompt as both positive and negative conditions during classifier-free guidance, creating conflicting signals that cause the model to avoid reflecting the image prompt, and (2) the two common self-attention modifications (KV replacement and KV concatenation) represent a trade-off between image realism and alignment with the image prompt, with neither achieving both simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250084/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image prompt, a positive and negative text prompt, and optionally a structural condition (depth map or soft edge map) fed through ControlNet. The image prompt is first processed via DDIM inversion to produce a chain of noised latents, and AdaIN is applied to the initial Gaussian noise using the initial inverted latent to transfer color tone. The output is a generated image that faithfully reflects fine details (textures, colors, patterns) of the image prompt while remaining diverse in structure. The method is entirely training-free and operates on pre-trained Stable Diffusion 1.5, 2.1, and SDXL models.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary components. First, conflict-free guidance corrects classifier-free guidance by using the image prompt exclusively as a positive condition (omitting it from the negative-guidance branch), eliminating the conflicting signal that pushes the model away from the image prompt. Second, Stratified Attention replaces KV concatenation by computing attention scores separately for the generated image features and the image prompt features, then combining them via a weighted sum (λ_G · M_t^G · V_t^G + λ_P · M_t^P · V_t^P), which avoids the attention bias in KV concatenation where queries derived from generated features inherently favor those same features. Stratified Attention is used within an Attention Fusion framework across all diffusion time steps, replacing the selective KV replacement/KV concatenation schedule used by prior methods like RIVAL, ensuring balanced incorporation of both generated and image prompt features throughout generation.

## Results

On the image variation task across three datasets, the proposed method achieves the highest image-prompt alignment (CLIP-I) in all settings: 0.878 on CelebAHQ, 0.883 on ImageNet-R-TI2I, and 0.873 on COCO validation, outperforming the next-best baselines by 0.028–0.044. It also attains the best structural diversity (DINO-G) on ImageNet-R-TI2I (0.142) and COCO (0.093), while remaining competitive on text alignment (CLIP-T) and realism (FID 14.557 on COCO vs. 14.016 for RIVAL). In structure-guided generation (Table 2), the method leads on CLIP-I for both depth (0.861) and soft-edge (0.844) conditions while matching baselines on structural faithfulness (DINO-C).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines: IP-Adapter and SSR-Encoder (training-based cross-attention adapters) and RIVAL and StyleAligned (training-free self-attention modifications). The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on image-prompt alignment (CLIP-I), gaining +0.028 over IP-Adapter and +0.044 over RIVAL on COCO, and +0.047 over RIVAL on CelebAHQ. On structural diversity (DINO-G), it matches or exceeds RIVAL (0.142 vs. 0.140 on ImageNet-R-TI2I; 0.093 vs. 0.089 on COCO) while the training-based baselines lag significantly (0.073–0.078 on CelebAHQ). On text alignment (CLIP-T), the method is competitive but slightly behind RIVAL on CelebAHQ (0.274 vs. 0.289) and ImageNet-R-TI2I (0.311 vs. 0.317), though it leads on COCO (0.326 vs. 0.325). For realism (FID on COCO), RIVAL achieves the best score (14.016) versus the proposed method's 14.557, while IP-Adapter and SSR-Encoder are worse (15.485 and 15.730). In the cross-prompt generation task (Fig. 10), the method achieves the highest simultaneous CLIP-I and CLIP-T scores, whereas RIVAL and StyleAligned sacrifice text alignment for image alignment, and IP-Adapter/SSR-Encoder underperform on both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-attention modification framework for image prompting introduced by RIVAL (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2024), which established KV replacement and KV concatenation as training-free mechanisms to inject image prompt features into diffusion models. It also builds on StyleID (Chung et al., CVPR 2024) for AdaIN-based color tone transfer. The key departure is identifying that prior self-attention modification methods incorrectly apply image prompts to both the positive and negative branches of classifier-free guidance, creating conflicting signals that actively suppress image prompt alignment. The proposed Stratified Attention further departs from the KV replacement/concatenation tradeoff by computing attention scores independently for each source before aggregation, avoiding the attention bias inherent in concatenated KV.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conflict-free guidance correction is a practical and somewhat counterintuitive fix: practitioners using self-attention modification for image prompting were unknowingly applying the image prompt as a negative condition, undermining their own goal. This insight is broadly applicable to any method that modifies self-attention with external conditions. However, the method remains training-free and thus inherits all quality limitations of the base diffusion model (e.g., hand artifacts), and the quantitative gains over strong baselines like RIVAL are moderate rather than transformative. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video generation, multi-image conditioning, and whether the Stratified Attention weighting (λ_G, λ_P) can be learned or adapted per-layer.
