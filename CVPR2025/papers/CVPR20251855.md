## Problem

Existing NIG methods either compress 3D scene features onto 2D BEV planes (losing height-aware spatial structure) or feed textual map descriptions into LLMs (requiring extensive annotation and failing to capture fine-grained landmarks in zero/few-shot settings). This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a framework that preserves full 3D voxel geometry locally and encodes topological trajectory maps as latent prompt features globally.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251855/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes T steps of panorama observations, each comprising K discrete RGB views (encoded by frozen CLIP at 14×14 spatial resolution) plus discrete navigation actions, and produces a natural-language instruction of L tokens generated autoregressively. Internally, the pipeline first outputs M landmark sequences (M=3 at inference) for uncertainty scoring before committing to the final instruction. The 3D scene representation operates at 7×7×7 voxel resolution per viewpoint.

## Method

MAPINSTRUCTOR comprises three modules: (i) CrossView Attention projects K egocentric 2D features into a unified 7×7×7 3D voxel grid via deformable attention with a multi-scale cascaded structure, preserving height-aware geometry that BEV compression discards; (ii) a topological graph is built over trajectory nodes (augmented with 2D/3D Euclidean distance encodings), processed by GCN message passing to aggregate spatial context, then compressed by a lightweight transformer decoder into fixed-length prompt tokens fed to a parameter-efficiently fine-tuned LLaMA-7B; (iii) a landmark uncertainty assessment stage generates M landmark sequences, clusters them via DeBERTa-large semantic similarity, and computes semantic entropy to filter hallucinated landmarks before the instruction completion stage. Training is two-stage (landmark prediction with GPT-4-extracted labels, then instruction completion) under supervised learning with parameter-efficient fine-tuning of the LLM.

## Results

On R2R val unseen, MAPINSTRUCTOR achieves a CIDEr of 0.490, surpassing the strongest LLM-based competitor C-INSTRUCTOR (0.450) by 4.0% and BEVInstructor (0.449) by 4.1%. On R2R val seen, it reaches CIDEr 0.576 vs. 0.534 for C-INSTRUCTOR (2.7% gain). On the more challenging RxR val unseen, MAPINSTRUCTOR leads all baselines by 4.5%/3.5%/0.4%/1.0%/2.2% across BLEU-1/BLEU-4/CIDEr/METEOR/ROUGE, and on REVERIE val unseen it gains 3.8% CIDEr over BEVInstructor (0.562 vs. 0.489).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPINSTRUCTOR outperforms all seven baselines on R2R across most metrics. Against the two strongest LLM-based competitors, it gains 2.7% CIDEr over C-INSTRUCTOR (0.576 vs. 0.534) and 2.7% over BEVInstructor (0.576 vs. 0.549) on val seen, with larger margins of 4.0% and 4.1% respectively on val unseen. On REVERIE val unseen, the CIDEr gap widens to 3.8% over BEVInstructor (0.562 vs. 0.489) and 3.8% over C-INSTRUCTOR (0.562 vs. 0.524), though it trails BEVInstructor slightly on SPICE (0.158 vs. 0.159) and METEOR (0.271 vs. 0.267). On RxR val unseen, MAPINSTRUCTOR achieves the best score on every metric, leading C-INSTRUCTOR by 0.057 vs. 0.053 CIDEr and 0.411 vs. 0.366 BLEU-1. The older non-LLM baselines (BT-speaker, EDrop-speaker, CCC-speaker, Lana, Lana+) are consistently outperformed by 8–10+ CIDEr points on R2R val unseen, confirming the advantage of LLM-based generation combined with 3D scene understanding and topological map prompting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAPINSTRUCTOR extends the LLM-based NIG line initiated by C-INSTRUCTOR and BEVInstructor, both of which use Llama-7B with prompt tuning but rely on BEV (2D bird's-eye-view) scene representations and text-based map descriptions. The paper departs from this lineage in two key ways: replacing BEV with 3D voxel scene representations via CrossView Attention (borrowing deformable attention from BEVFormer), and encoding topological trajectory maps as latent graph features (via GCN message passing) rather than textual prompts. The landmark uncertainty assessment pipeline additionally repurposes semantic entropy from NLP hallucination detection (Kuhn et al., 2023) into a two-stage generation process for NIG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that integrating global topological context as structured latent prompts into LLMs can meaningfully improve instruction quality over purely local or text-described approaches, offering a practical recipe for spatially-grounded LLM prompting in embodied settings. However, the method remains tied to discrete panorama viewpoints and indoor environments (R2R, REVERIE, RxR), and the 3D voxel resolution (7×7×7) is coarse; whether this approach transfers to continuous or outdoor navigation is unaddressed. The landmark uncertainty assessment adds inference cost (multiple generation rounds plus DeBERTa-based clustering), and the gap to human-annotated instruction quality in path-guiding (24.12% vs. 30.20% SPL on HAMT) remains substantial, suggesting that spatial reasoning in generated instructions is still far from human-level.
