## Problem

Spatio-temporal scene graph (STSG) generation and anticipation models suffer from long-tailed predicate distributions, causing them to over-predict frequent head relationships and under-predict rare but critical tail relationships. Additionally, no prior work systematically evaluates the robustness of STSG models under realistic input corruptions such as noise, fog, frost, brightness shifts, and sun glare.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252365/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frame sequences (with optional bounding boxes and object category labels depending on the evaluation mode: SGDET/SGCLS/PREDCLS for VidSGG, AGS/PGAGS/GAGS for SGA) and outputs per-frame scene graphs as (object_i, predicate, object_j) triplets with normalized probability distributions over all predicate classes. For SGA, it additionally produces anticipated scene graphs for H future frames. Three graph-building strategies (with-constraint, no-constraint, semi-constraint) control whether multiple predicates per object pair are retained based on confidence thresholds.

## Method

IMPARTAIL is a training framework that de-biases any object-centric VidSGG/SGA architecture without adding new modules. Its core mechanism is masked loss with partial gradients: at each epoch, a curriculum-guided mask generator selectively zeros out the loss contribution of head (frequent) predicate classes, forcing gradient updates to focus on underrepresented tail relationships. The masking ratio increases linearly per epoch (R_m = e × R_s), and the set of unmasked predicates is sampled via a multinomial distribution with uniform weights, progressively shifting the effective training distribution from long-tailed toward uniform. The framework wraps existing pipelines (STTran, DSGDetr, SceneSayer) by replacing their standard cross-entropy predicate loss with the masked variant while keeping all architectural components (ORPU, STPU, decoders) unchanged. The paper also introduces two new robustness evaluation tasks that apply 16 standard image corruptions to test inputs and measure performance degradation.

## Results

On the Action Genome dataset, IMPARTAIL consistently improves mean recall across all VidSGG evaluation modes. In the PREDCLS setting (most informative), STTran+IMPARTAIL achieves mR@10 of 44.0 (+44.3% over STTran's 30.5) and mR@50 of 52.9 (+52.0%), while DSGDetr+IMPARTAIL reaches mR@10 of 41.0 (+30.2%) and mR@50 of 48.2 (+33.1%). For SGA, improvements are more modest but consistent: SceneSayerSDE+IMPARTAIL gains ~10 points in mR@50 (18.3→27.7) under the AGS setting at F=0.5, and STTran+++IMPARTAIL improves mR@10 from 7.9 to 9.3. Under robustness evaluation with input corruptions, IMPARTAIL-trained models exhibit smaller performance drops (e.g., STTran PREDCLS mR@10 drops from 65.5 to 57.5 under Gaussian Noise vs. 45.7 to 31.4 for the baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMPARTAIL is applied as a training framework on top of existing object-centric VidSGG/SGA methods, so all comparisons are between a baseline and its IMPARTAIL-adapted version. For VidSGG, STTran+IMPARTAIL outperforms STTran by 17.5–44.3% in mR@10 across SGDET, SGCLS, and PREDCLS modes respectively, with the largest gains in PREDCLS (44.0 vs. 30.5 mR@10). DSGDetr+IMPARTAIL similarly beats DSGDetr by 11.9–30.2% in mR@10, confirming the framework's method-agnostic benefit. For SGA, IMPARTAIL consistently improves all six baselines (STTran+, STTran++, DSGDetr+, DSGDetr++, SceneSayerODE, SceneSayerSDE) in mR@10 under the AGS setting at F=0.5, with the largest relative gain on SceneSayerSDE (mR@50: 18.3→27.7, +51.4%). The authors note that IMPARTAIL's PREDCLS results are on par with or occasionally surpass the SOTA unbiased method TEMPURA [26], without introducing any new architectural components. Under robustness evaluation (16 corruption types), IMPARTAIL-trained models show smaller performance degradation than their unmodified counterparts, e.g., STTran PREDCLS mR@10 drops only 8 points (65.5→57.5) under Gaussian Noise versus a 14-point drop (45.7→31.4) for the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

IMPARTAIL positions itself as a training-only intervention for existing object-centric VidSGG/SGA architectures (STTran, DSGDetr, SceneSayer), deliberately avoiding the architectural modifications adopted by prior unbiased learning methods like TEMPURA and FloCoDe. It draws on the curriculum learning literature for its progressive mask scheduling and on the broader long-tail learning toolbox (cost-sensitive learning, resampling) for conceptual grounding, but its specific mechanism—selectively voiding loss contributions from head-class predicates via a curriculum-guided mask—is novel to the STSG setting. The robustness evaluation tasks (Robust VidSGG, Robust SGA) extend the existing VidSGG/SGA evaluation protocol by introducing systematic input corruptions, filling a gap where prior STSG work had no standardized robustness benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture-agnostic nature of IMPARTAIL makes it a practical plug-in for any object-centric STSG method, lowering the barrier to unbiased scene graph generation. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (Action Genome), and the SGA results are mixed—some configurations (e.g., STTran++ at F=0.7) actually degrade with IMPARTAIL, suggesting the masking strategy may not transfer uniformly across anticipation horizons. The finding that a fixed masking ratio of 0.9 matched the curriculum strategy somewhat undermines the theoretical motivation for the curriculum component. Open questions include generalization to other STSG datasets, interaction with other debiasing techniques, and whether the robustness gains observed under synthetic corruptions hold under real-world distribution shifts.
