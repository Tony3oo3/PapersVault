## Problem

Sign language translation (SLT) is fundamentally difficult because sign languages rely heavily on contextual discourse—spatial indexing, topic-comment structure, and homonyms—yet prior methods translate from the signing video alone, ignoring the surrounding context that human interpreters naturally exploit. Additionally, large-scale parallel sign-language data is scarce, making it hard to train robust translation systems. This paper investigates whether incorporating automatically extracted contextual cues (background scene descriptions and previous sentence translations) alongside the signing video can significantly improve open-vocabulary SLT.

## Input / Output

The model consumes four input streams: (i) visual features extracted as 768-dim embeddings from 16-frame sliding-window clips of the signing video via a pre-trained Video-Swin ISLR encoder, (ii) noisy pseudo-glosses obtained by running the same ISLR classifier in a sliding-window fashion (typically ~22 glosses per sentence vs. ~56 visual features), (iii) a keyword list derived from BLIP-2 captions of the background scene sampled at 1 fps with stopwords removed, and (iv) the previous sentence translation, used auto-regressively at inference. The output is a free-form English sentence generated token-by-token by the LLM until an end-of-sentence token is reached.

## Method

The framework fine-tunes Llama3-8B with LoRA (rank 4, alpha 16) to generate spoken-language translations from a single concatenated input sequence containing all four cues, separated by descriptive prompts. A 2-layer MLP with GELU activation projects the 768-dim visual features into the 4,096-dim LLM embedding space; all other inputs are already text. Training uses standard cross-entropy over the 128k-token vocabulary with masked self-attention, and applies three augmentations to improve robustness: random word dropping (0–50%) within each textual cue, random full-cue omission (50% probability per cue), and random sampling between ground-truth and predicted previous sentences (50/50) to bridge the train-test gap. The key design insight is that the LLM's pre-trained language understanding, combined with LoRA's parameter-efficient adaptation, allows the model to learn which cues are relevant and to suppress noisy pseudo-glosses (e.g., homonym confusions) while leveraging context to resolve ambiguities in pronouns, tenses, and referents.

## Results

On BOBSL SENT-TEST, the full model (Vid+PrevPred+PG+BG) achieves 40.3 BLEURT, 14.8 IoU, and 1.20 LLM score, surpassing the strongest prior method Sign2GPT (w/PGP) by +5.1 BLEURT and +6.1 IoU. Even with video-only input, the method reaches 37.8 BLEURT, already exceeding Sign2GPT (35.2) and GFSLT (27.7). On How2Sign, the best variant (Vid+PG+PrevPred) attains 45.3 BLEURT and 32.5 ROUGE-L, outperforming VAP (27.8 R-L) by ~5 points and matching its BLEU-4 (12.7 vs 12.9). In the oracle setting with ground-truth previous sentences and spotings, the method reaches 47.1 BLEURT and 26.5 IoU, substantially outperforming the Sincan oracle (37.0 BLEURT, 12.5 IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BOBSL, the proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines. Against GFSLT [84], the full model gains +12.6 BLEURT (40.3 vs 27.7) and +9.6 IoU (14.8 vs 5.2); against Sign2GPT [75] (with pre-training), it leads by +5.1 BLEURT and +6.1 IoU. The video-only variant already surpasses both, confirming the LLM-based architecture's inherent advantage. In the oracle setting, the method with ground-truth previous sentences and spotings (45.9 BLEURT, 25.5 IoU) substantially outperforms Sincan's oracle (37.0 BLEURT, 12.5 IoU), and adding background descriptions further lifts it to 47.1 BLEURT. On How2Sign, the method (Vid+PG+PrevPred) achieves 45.3 BLEURT and 32.5 ROUGE-L, beating VAP [35] by ~5 ROUGE-L points and matching its BLEU-4 (12.7 vs 12.9), while trailing the pre-trained SSLT [82]† (55.7 BLEURT) and SSVP-SLT [60]† (49.6 BLEURT) which leverage an additional 984-hour YouTube-ASL corpus. Against non-pre-trained How2Sign baselines (SSLT 34.0, SSVP-SLT 39.3, Fla-LLM, VAP), the method leads on all reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-for-SLT line of work (Sign2GPT, SignLLM, VAP) by integrating multiple contextual cues—background scene descriptions, autoregressive previous-sentence predictions, and noisy pseudo-glosses—alongside visual features into a single Llama3-8B decoder fine-tuned with LoRA. The closest prior work is Sincan et al. [64], which also leverages discourse context, but that method requires ground-truth previous sentences and sign spottings derived from ground-truth subtitles, whereas this paper is fully automatic. The visual backbone (Video-Swin ISLR from Raude et al. [59]) and the pseudo-gloss extraction strategy are directly inherited, but here pseudo-glosses serve as an additional input cue rather than as supervision, and a BLIP-2 captioner provides background context that no prior SLT system has exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that contextual information—background descriptions and prior discourse—provides substantial, measurable gains for open-vocabulary SLT, validating a core insight from sign language linguistics that a third of sentences require discourse context for full translation. However, several limitations remain open: autoregressive previous-sentence predictions can accumulate errors, background descriptions occasionally introduce conflicting or irrelevant information, and the model still struggles with negations, question vs. statement disambiguation, and pointing/fingerspelling. The LLM-based evaluation metric (GPT-4o-mini scoring) is a useful complement to BLEU/BLEURT but adds a dependency on a proprietary API and introduces its own biases. Broader impact hinges on whether this multi-cue LLM paradigm generalises beyond the two datasets tested and can be deployed in real-time interpretation settings.
