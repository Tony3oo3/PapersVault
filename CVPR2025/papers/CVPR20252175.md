## Problem

Unconditional 4D generative modeling of deforming shapes remains challenging because existing neural field representations must simultaneously capture high-fidelity shape detail, maintain temporal coherence across frames, and preserve a contiguous weight space suitable for diffusion-based generation. Single global latent vectors lose fine local detail, while per-shape optimized MLPs lack shared structure, making generative modeling in their weight space ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes random Gaussian noise as input and produces a 16-frame 4D deforming shape sequence decoded via a shared dictionary into a neural SDF field (shape) and a flow field (motion). For conditional motion generation on unseen species, a mesh of the new shape identity is provided, from which a shape code and coefficient vector list are optimized against the learned shape dictionary. Training uses 200,000 surface-sampled points per frame, and the diffusion model operates on (L+1)-length token sequences (one latent code plus L per-layer coefficient vectors) rather than raw geometry.

## Method

DNF first pre-trains disentangled shape (SDF) and motion (flow) MLPs with per-instance latent codes following NPMs, then decomposes each MLP's weight matrices via layer-wise SVD to form a shared dictionary of singular vector outer products. The dictionary is compressed by retaining only the top-k singular values and extended with low-rank residual matrices (orthogonalized via an explicit loss), after which only the singular-value coefficient vectors are fine-tuned per instance while all singular vectors remain frozen. A transformer-based diffusion model is then trained in this weight space: shape diffusion treats the (L+1)-length vector list as tokens with self-attention, while motion diffusion models windowed t-frame sequences using cross-attention for shape conditioning plus an additional temporal self-attention layer to enforce frame-order coherence. Sequences longer than the training window are generated via iterative diffusion outpainting, using the last k frames as context to in-paint the next (t−k) frames.

## Results

On the DeformingThings4D dataset for unconditional 4D motion generation (16-frame animal sequences), DNF achieves MMD 15.3, Coverage 54.1%, and 1-NNA 58.2%, outperforming both baselines on all three Chamfer-based metrics. In the shape reconstruction ablation, DNF reduces the average Chamfer Distance to 0.067 (×10³, 10,000 points) compared to NPMs' 0.128, demonstrating substantially improved fidelity from dictionary-based fine-tuning.

|---|---|---|---|
| HyperDiffusion [9] | 16.0 | 45.9 | 63.5 |
| Motion2VecSets [4] | 18.7 | 48.1 | 68.2 |
| **Ours (DNF)** | **15.3** | **54.1** | **58.2** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNF outperforms both baselines on all three evaluation metrics. Against HyperDiffusion [9], DNF improves MMD from 16.0 to 15.3 (−4.4%), increases Coverage from 45.9% to 54.1% (+8.2 pp), and reduces 1-NNA from 63.5% to 58.2% (−5.3 pp); the paper attributes HyperDiffusion's weaker shape quality to the lack of continuity in its weight space, leading to broken or missing legs during motion. Against Motion2VecSets [4], DNF improves MMD from 18.7 to 15.3 (−18.2%), increases Coverage from 48.1% to 54.1% (+6.0 pp), and reduces 1-NNA from 68.2% to 58.2% (−10.0 pp); Motion2VecSets preserves finer per-frame shape details but struggles with temporal coherence in unconditional generation. In the reconstruction ablation (Table 2), DNF's decoupled shape-and-motion dictionary representation achieves CD 0.067, nearly halving the error of NPMs (0.128) and substantially outperforming the shape-code-only fine-tuning variant (0.096), confirming that the SVD-based dictionary with residual expansion is critical for capturing local detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NPMs, which disentangle 4D deforming shapes into separate shape and motion MLPs with per-instance latent codes, and on weight-space diffusion approaches like HyperDiffusion that model neural field parameters directly. The key departure is introducing SVD-based dictionary learning into the neural field fine-tuning process: rather than optimizing full per-instance MLP weights (which destroys weight-space continuity) or relying solely on a global latent (which loses local detail), the authors decompose pre-trained MLPs into a shared singular-vector dictionary plus per-instance singular-value coefficient vectors, with compression and low-rank residual extension. This positions DNF between the global-latent paradigm of NPMs and the full-weight-space paradigm of HyperDiffusion, adding a structured intermediate representation that preserves both fidelity and contiguity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dictionary-based representation offers a practical recipe for balancing fidelity, contiguity, and compression in neural field generative modeling—a tension that plagues both global-latent and full-weight-space approaches. The demonstrated generalization to unseen animal species (by fitting a new shape into the learned dictionary and conditioning motion diffusion on it) suggests the representation space is meaningfully structured rather than merely interpolating training data. However, the approach is limited to the DeformingThings4D animal dataset, imposes no physical constraints (allowing volume distortion), and the diffusion model operates on per-instance encodings without awareness of the decoded surface, leaving open whether the technique scales to more diverse categories or benefits from surface-aware generative objectives.
