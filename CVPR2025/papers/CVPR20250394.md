## Problem

Depth from defocus (DfD) estimates object depth from spatial image derivatives as a proxy for defocus level, making it fundamentally sensitive to photon-limited noise. Existing DfD algorithms assume relatively low noise levels (≤4 LSB SD), yet practical applications in AR/VR, smartphones, and drones inevitably operate in dark environments. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a DfD method robust to 4× higher noise levels than prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250394/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 147×147 RGB images of a static scene captured with a deformable lens at two different optical powers (ρ−=10.0 m⁻¹, ρ+=10.2 m⁻¹), corrupted by Poisson-Gaussian noise at photon-limited levels (α∈[180,200], σ=2). It outputs a sparse depth map localized along defocused boundaries, a dense depth map (via wedge assignment or U-Net post-processing), a boundary center map, a noiseless color map, and a confidence map for filtering unreliable estimates. Larger images are processed by tiling into 147×147 blocks.

## Method

The core contribution is the Blurry-Edges representation, which models each 21×21 image patch as a stack of l=2 partially occluded wedges parameterized by vertex position, angular extent, RGB color, and boundary smoothness η, rendered via α-clipping with a Gaussian error function. A two-stage architecture predicts these parameters: a local CNN with residual blocks estimates per-patch geometry and smoothness (colors recovered via ridge regression), then a global Transformer Encoder refines all patches to enforce defocus consistency (shared wedge positions/colors across the image pair) and spatial consistency of boundaries, colors, and derivatives among neighbors. Depth is computed in closed form from the smoothness pair (η+, η−) of corresponding boundaries using a derived DfD equation that eliminates the unknown intrinsic texture sharpness ξ. The model is trained modularly on synthetic scenes with basic geometries and Poisson-Gaussian noise, yet generalizes to real-world textured objects without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the synthetic photon-limited testing set (noise SD 18.21–19.22 LSB, ~4× higher than prior DfD work), the proposed sparse depth map (Ours) achieves δ1=0.720, RMSE=5.281 cm, and AbsRel=3.295 cm, outperforming Focal Track (RMSE 6.308 cm) and Tang et al. (RMSE 6.737 cm) on all metrics. For dense depth maps, the post-processed variant (Ours-PP) achieves δ1=0.806, RMSE=3.992 cm, and AbsRel=2.691 cm, surpassing the best dense baseline DefocusNet (RMSE 6.092 cm) by ~34% in RMSE. The method also simultaneously produces a boundary center map, a noiseless color map, and a confidence map as auxiliary outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six baselines are retrained on the same noisy synthetic data to ensure a fair comparison under photon-limited conditions. In the sparse setting, Ours outperforms Focal Track [8] by 1.03 cm in RMSE (5.281 vs. 6.308) and 1.35 cm in AbsRel (3.295 vs. 4.640), and beats Tang et al. [38] by 1.46 cm in RMSE (5.281 vs. 6.737). In the dense setting, Ours-PP dominates all four dense baselines: it reduces RMSE from 6.092 cm (DefocusNet [23], the best dense baseline) to 3.992 cm (−34%), and from 9.883 cm (PhaseCam3D [44]) to 3.992 cm (−60%). Notably, Ours-PP also uses only 2 input images versus 5 for DefocusNet, DFV-DFF, and DERE, while achieving superior accuracy. The direct Blurry-Edges dense output (Ours-W) is competitive with DefocusNet on RMSE (6.297 vs. 6.092 cm) but lags slightly, confirming that the U-Net post-processing step provides a meaningful densification gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the depth-from-defocus (DfD) literature by introducing the Blurry-Edges patch representation, which generalizes the earlier Field-of-Junction (FoJ) model by explicitly parameterizing boundary smoothness alongside position and color. It departs from both analytical DfD methods (Focal Track, Tang et al.) and learning-based DfD methods (DefocusNet, DERE, PhaseCam3D) by targeting the photon-limited regime where all prior work assumes relatively low noise. The closed-form depth equation derived from a pair of differently defocused images follows the differential-defocus lineage (Guo et al., Focal Flow) but operates on estimated boundary smoothness rather than raw image gradients, and the CNN-plus-Transformer architecture borrows structural ideas from recent boundary-detection work (CT-Bound, Boundary Attention) while applying them to a depth-estimation objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: DfD is the go-to passive depth cue for compact platforms (smartphones, AR/VR, drones), yet all prior methods degrade sharply in the low-light conditions these devices routinely encounter. By handling roughly 4× the noise level of previous DfD algorithms and generalizing from basic-geometry synthetic training to textured MS-COCO objects without fine-tuning, the work opens a path toward reliable passive depth in twilight and indoor settings. Key limitations remain: depth is estimated only along boundaries (sparse), the method requires a deformable lens to capture the image pair, and the synthetic-to-real gap, while small in experiments, has not been stress-tested across diverse real-world scenes. Open questions include whether the Blurry-Edges representation can be extended to handle more complex occlusion patterns, whether the approach can be adapted to single-image or coded-aperture DfD, and how it scales to high-resolution, large-scene capture.
