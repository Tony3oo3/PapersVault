## Problem

Nighttime smartphone photography suffers from simultaneous degradations—low illumination, sensor noise, and motion blur—yet most restoration methods address these tasks individually or use computationally heavy transformer architectures. Existing joint methods like LEDNet achieve reasonable results but at high parameter and FLOP costs, limiting deployment on resource-constrained mobile devices. The paper asks whether a lightweight CNN can match or exceed transformer-based approaches on multi-task low-light restoration while being substantially more efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252474/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single low-light, blurry, noisy RGB image (as captured by a handheld smartphone at night) and produces a fully restored RGB image with corrected illumination, reduced blur, and suppressed noise. During training, inputs are 256×256 or 384×384 crops from paired datasets (LOLBlur, LOLv2, LSRW). Internally, the encoder produces an intermediate 1/8-resolution illumination-corrected estimate (x̂↓8) that serves as an auxiliary supervision signal, while the final full-resolution output is generated by the decoder.

## Method

DarkIR is an asymmetric encoder-decoder CNN built on the Metaformer block structure (attention + FFN with residual connections), but with task-specific blocks for each stage. The encoder (EBlock) performs low-light enhancement in the Fourier domain: it applies FFT, modifies only the amplitude via a frequency-domain MLP (FreMLP), and applies IFFT, operating at progressively lower resolutions via strided convolutions. The decoder (DBlock) handles deblurring in the spatial domain using a Dilated-Spatial Attention Module (Di-SpAM) that combines three dilated depth-wise convolutions (dilation factors 1, 4, 9) for large receptive fields, followed by simplified channel attention and a gated MLP. Both blocks replace standard activations with simple gates and use simplified channel attention (SCA) instead of full channel attention, reducing parameters. Training uses a composite loss combining L1 pixel loss, LPIPS perceptual loss, gradient edge loss, and an architecture-guiding loss (L_lol) that penalizes the upsampled encoder output against the ground truth, explicitly forcing the encoder to focus on illumination correction rather than spatial detail.

## Results

On the LOLBlur dataset, DarkIR-m achieves 27.00 dB PSNR and 0.162 LPIPS, improving over the previous state-of-the-art LEDNet (25.74 dB, 0.224 LPIPS) by +1.26 dB PSNR while using 55% fewer parameters (3.31M vs. 7.4M) and 81% fewer MACs (7.25G vs. 38.65G). The larger DarkIR-l variant reaches 27.30 dB PSNR and 0.137 LPIPS. On LOLv2-Real, the multi-task DarkIR-mt achieves 23.87 dB PSNR and 0.880 SSIM, outperforming RetinexFormer (22.80 dB, 0.840 SSIM) and FourLLIE (21.60 dB, 0.847 SSIM) with only 7.25G MACs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DarkIR-m outperforms the previous joint low-light deblurring SOTA, LEDNet [65], by +1.26 dB PSNR (27.00 vs. 25.74) and halves the LPIPS (0.162 vs. 0.224), while using 55% fewer parameters and 81% fewer MACs. Against the general-purpose restoration model Restormer [57], DarkIR-m achieves higher PSNR (27.00 vs. 26.72) with 88% fewer parameters (3.31M vs. 26.13M) and 20× fewer MACs (7.25G vs. 144.25G), though Restormer retains a slight edge in SSIM (0.902 vs. 0.883) and LPIPS (0.133 vs. 0.162). On LOLv2-Real, DarkIR-mt surpasses RetinexFormer [3] by +1.07 dB PSNR (23.87 vs. 22.80) and FourLLIE [44] by +2.27 dB (23.87 vs. 21.60), confirming the benefit of joint deblurring. On the unpaired Real-LOLBlur benchmark (blind metrics), DarkIR-m is competitive but does not dominate: NAFNet [9] leads on MUSIQ (50.22 vs. 48.36) and NIQE (5.123 vs. 4.998), while LEDNet [65] leads on NRQM (5.643 vs. 4.983), suggesting that dedicated deblurring or deblurring-first pipelines retain an edge on purely real-world blur. In the 2-step pipeline comparison (Table 2), DarkIR-m's end-to-end PSNR of 27.00 dB dwarfs the best pipeline (DeblurGAN-v2 → Zero-DCE at 18.33 dB), a gap of nearly 9 dB, underscoring the advantage of joint training over sequential task composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

DarkIR builds on the Metaformer block structure (attention + FFN with residual connections) and adapts NAFNet's simplified channel attention and gating mechanisms, while following LEDNet's asymmetric encoder-decoder strategy that splits low-light correction (encoder) from deblurring (decoder). Its key departure from the prevailing Transformer-based trend (RetinexFormer, Restormer) is the use of task-specific CNN blocks: a Fourier-domain MLP (FreMLP) in the encoder to enhance illumination via amplitude manipulation, and a dilated spatial attention module (Di-SpAM) in the decoder for large-receptive-field deblurring. This positions DarkIR as an efficiency-focused alternative that argues well-designed CNN attention can match or exceed Transformer-based restoration at a fraction of the cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 55% parameter reduction over LEDNet and 88% over Restormer, combined with a +1 dB PSNR gain, makes joint low-light restoration practically deployable on memory-constrained mobile devices—a setting where most prior SOTA methods are infeasible. The multi-task variant (DarkIR-mt) also generalizes to real-world datasets (LOLv2-Real, LSRW) without task-specific fine-tuning, suggesting a path toward unified night-photography pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that depth-wise convolutions, while reducing parameter count, may not translate to proportionally faster inference on all GPU architectures due to low arithmetic intensity, leaving open the question of how to combine low FLOPs with genuinely fast wall-clock speed.
