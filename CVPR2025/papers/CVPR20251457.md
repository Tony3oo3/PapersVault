## Problem

Standard IID sampling from flow-based generative models is sample-inefficient for achieving diverse mode coverage: independently drawn source samples can converge to the same target mode due to the non-linear transport map, requiring repeated sampling to discover all relevant modes. The paper asks how to generate a diverse set of target samples under a fixed, limited sampling budget without retraining the flow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251457/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Given K Gaussian source samples and a conditioning signal (text prompt, masked image, or class label), DiverseFlow produces a set of K diverse images from the target distribution in a single ODE integration pass. Distances for the diversity kernel are computed in a ViT feature space rather than raw pixel space to remain meaningful in high dimensions. The method is entirely training-free and operates at inference time, modifying the ODE trajectory of an already-trained flow matching model.

## Method

DiverseFlow couples K otherwise independent flow ODE trajectories by adding a DPP-based diversity gradient to each particle's velocity field. At each timestep, the target sample is estimated via a large Euler step (x̂₁ = x_t + v_θ(x_t,t)(1−t)), and a determinantal point process kernel L is constructed from pairwise distances in a ViT feature space; the log-determinant of L serves as a volume-based diversity score. The gradient of this log-likelihood with respect to each particle's position is subtracted from the flow velocity (scaled by a time-varying γ(t)), turning K independent ODEs into a system of coupled non-linear ODEs that repel similar trajectories. A quality constraint modulates the DPP kernel by penalizing samples whose estimated source position deviates beyond a percentile-radius ρ of the Gaussian prior, preventing the diversity force from degrading sample fidelity.

## Results

On ImageNet-256 class-conditional synthesis with LFM, DiverseFlow improves recall while retaining similar precision: at CFG=2, recall increases from 0.41 to 0.46, and at CFG=4, recall jumps from 0.26 to 0.38, while precision stays within 0.01 of the baseline. In a synthetic 10-mode 2D experiment with K=10 samples, DiverseFlow discovers 7.11 modes on average versus 5.64 for MB-OT alone. In a symmetric 10-mode Gaussian mixture, DiverseFlow recovers all 10 modes on average (over 100 trials), compared to ~6.5 for IID sampling and ~8.8 for Particle Guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Particle Guidance (Corso et al., ICLR 2023), DiverseFlow wins on both diversity and quality: in the 10-mode toy experiment, DiverseFlow discovers all 10 modes on average versus ~8.8 for Particle Guidance, and in the polysemous text-to-image evaluation (30 prompts × 10 seeds), DiverseFlow achieves comparable in-batch similarity (diversity) to Particle Guidance while maintaining a substantially higher aesthetic score, since Particle Guidance suffers from visible image artifacts. Against the LFM baseline (Dao et al., 2023), DiverseFlow consistently improves recall at every CFG level above 1 (e.g., +0.05 at CFG=2, +0.12 at CFG=4) while keeping precision within 0.01, indicating that the DPP coupling recovers additional modes without degrading sample fidelity. The paper also notes that MB-OT and SB-CFM formulations benefit most from DiverseFlow, likely because their optimal-path structure yields more accurate target estimates for the diversity gradient.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiverseFlow sits at the intersection of flow matching (Lipman et al. 2022) and determinantal point processes (Kulesza & Taskar 2012), repurposing the DPP's volume-based repulsion as a gradient signal that couples otherwise independent ODE trajectories at inference time. The closest prior work is Particle Guidance (Corso et al. 2023), which also performs non-IID diverse sampling for diffusion models, but DiverseFlow differs in using a DPP determinant (which assigns zero likelihood to duplicates) rather than an SVGD-inspired row-sum kernel, and in operating on flow ODEs rather than diffusion SDEs. The paper also draws on the diverse M-best literature (Batra et al. 2012) for motivation and on Manifold Constrained Gradients (Chung et al. 2022) for the inpainting application, but the core contribution is the coupled-ODE formulation driven by the DPP log-likelihood gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is training-free and model-agnostic, meaning it can be dropped into any pre-trained flow or diffusion model without retraining, which makes it practically attractive for practitioners who need diverse outputs under a fixed compute budget. However, the approach requires a domain-specific feature extractor (e.g., ViT) to compute meaningful distances in high-dimensional spaces, and the quality constraint (Equation 9) is a heuristic that may not generalize cleanly to non-Gaussian source distributions. Open questions include how the coupled ODE behaves at very large sampling budgets (K >> number of modes), whether the method extends naturally to stochastic ODEs or multi-step denoising schedules, and how it interacts with other inference-time interventions like classifier-free guidance at extreme strengths.
