## Problem

Multi-species animal pose and shape estimation from images remains underexplored because prior methods rely on small-capacity CNN backbones and scarce multi-species 3D-annotated datasets, limiting generalization beyond well-studied species like horses and dogs. The successful paradigm from human mesh recovery—combining a high-capacity Transformer backbone with a large aggregated dataset—has not been tested in the animal domain, and the limited 3D data space for animals (compared to humans) demands different training strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252312/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (512×512) and a learnable class token as input, producing SMAL shape parameters β ∈ ℝ⁴¹, pose parameters θ ∈ ℝ^(35×3) in axis-angle form, and a weak-perspective camera translation T ∈ ℝ³ (with fixed focal length f=1000). Unlike HMR2.0's residual decoding relative to a population mean, AniMer directly regresses the final SMAL parameters due to the limited pose prior available for animals. The class token additionally encodes animal family identity for supervised contrastive learning, and 3D keypoints are derived from the predicted mesh via a linear mapping matrix.

## Method

AniMer couples a pretrained ViT encoder with a Transformer-based decoder and independent MLP regression heads for shape, pose, and camera parameters. The key architectural innovation is a family-aware supervised contrastive loss applied to a learnable class token, which pulls features of same-family animals together and pushes different families apart in embedding space, capturing the two-level shape hierarchy (inter-family and intra-family) unique to quadrupeds. Training uses a two-stage schedule—first on 3D-annotated data only, then on the full 3D+2D dataset—to handle the imbalanced data scale. The total loss combines direct 3D parameter supervision, 2D keypoint reprojection, a Mahalanobis prior on shape/pose, an adversarial discriminator, and the contrastive family loss. To address 3D data scarcity, the authors introduce CtrlAni3D (~10k images), a synthetic dataset generated by conditioning ControlNet on rendered SMAL mask/depth maps and ChatGPT-generated text prompts, with SAM2-based cycle-consistency filtering to ensure alignment.

## Results

AniMer achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three evaluation benchmarks. On Animal3D, it reaches 88.9 AUC and 89.5 PCK@HTH, with PA-MPJPE of 80.4 mm and PA-MPVPE of 85.7 mm. On the out-of-distribution Animal Kingdom dataset, AniMer attains 82.9 AUC, 34.9 PCK@0.1, and 54.7 PCK@0.15, substantially outperforming all baselines. On CtrlAni3D, it achieves 93.8 AUC and 95.4 PCK@HTH with PA-MPJPE of 44.1 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AniMer outperforms all baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Compared to HMR (CNN-based, CVPR 2018), AniMer improves PA-MPJPE from 123.5 to 80.4 mm (−35%) and PA-MPVPE from 133.9 to 85.7 mm (−36%) on Animal3D, and boosts PCK@0.1 from 12.8% to 34.9% on Animal Kingdom. Against WLDO (ECCV 2020), AniMer reduces PA-MPJPE from 112.3 to 80.4 mm on Animal3D and PCK@0.15 from 27.6% to 54.7% on Animal Kingdom. The closest competitor is HMR2.0 (ICCV 2023), which also uses a ViT backbone; AniMer still leads by 2.2 AUC points and 13.7 mm PA-MPJPE on Animal3D, and by 5.6 AUC points and 12.2 PCK@0.1 points on Animal Kingdom, demonstrating the benefit of the family-aware contrastive learning and direct parameter decoding over HMR2.0's residual decoding and single-stage training.

## Relation to Prior Work

AniMer directly adapts the HMR2.0 architecture (ViT encoder + Transformer decoder for parametric mesh recovery) from the human domain to quadrupedal animals, with two key departures: direct SMAL parameter decoding instead of residual regression (due to limited animal pose priors), and a two-stage training protocol that prioritizes 3D data before introducing 2D data. The family-aware supervised contrastive learning on a learnable class token extends the supervised contrastive framework of Khosla et al. to encode inter-family shape differences in the animal domain. For synthetic data generation, the paper extends the 3D-conditioned diffusion pipeline of Ma et al. from static CAD models to dynamic SMAL animations, using ControlNet with rendered mask/depth conditions and ChatGPT-generated text prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the 'scale the backbone, scale the data' paradigm proven effective for human mesh recovery transfers to the animal domain, and that diffusion-based synthetic data (CtrlAni3D) outperforms traditional CG rendering for pretraining—practical findings for a field constrained by scarce 3D-annotated animal data. The family-aware contrastive loss provides a lightweight mechanism to handle multi-species shape variation without species-specific heads. However, the method remains bounded by SMAL's linear shape space, limiting applicability to species far from the 41-scan basis (birds, goats), and motion blur or severe occlusion remain unaddressed. Open questions include whether richer parametric models or spatio-temporal extensions can close the gap for non-quadrupedal or highly occluded scenarios.
