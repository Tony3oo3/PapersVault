## Problem

Generating fine-grained human action videos (e.g., gymnastics routines like 'split leap with 1 turn') remains intractable for current diffusion-based video generators, which produce temporally inconsistent, anatomically distorted, and physically implausible results. The core gap is the absence of explicit physical law enforcement in the generation pipeline: existing methods rely on purely data-driven pose estimation and text conditioning, ignoring kinematic constraints such as Newton's laws and joint acceleration dynamics.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes 16-frame video clips at 384×384 resolution from FineGym subsets (FX-JUMP, FX-TURN, FX-SALTO) along with GPT-4-enhanced textual descriptions of fine-grained action labels. During inference, Gaussian noise is fed into the trained framework conditioned on text and online-estimated 2D skeletal guidance (17 joints × T frames), producing fine-grained human action videos. The 2D poses are extracted in an online manner from input videos rather than provided as ground truth, making the system robust to noisy pose detection in complex gymnastics scenarios.

## Method

FinePhys builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with AnimateDiff motion modules and LoRA adapters, but its core contribution is a physics-informed skeletal guidance pipeline. Online 2D poses are lifted to 3D via an in-context learning transformer (using mean 3D skeletons from Human3.6M/AMASS as pseudo-3D references), then a PhysNet module explicitly estimates the inertia matrix M⁻¹, generalized forces J, and joint constraints C in the Euler-Lagrange equation, computing bidirectional joint accelerations via second-order central differences to produce physically refined 3D poses. The data-driven and physically predicted 3D skeletons are fused, projected back to 2D, and encoded as multi-scale heatmaps injected into the 3D-UNet denoising process. Physics is incorporated through three complementary biases: observational (ICL for dimension lifting), inductive (fully differentiable PhysNet modules parameterizing EL-Eq.), and learning (re-projection losses and noise-constraint penalties). Training proceeds in three stages: pre-training the 2D-to-3D and PhysNet modules on Human3.6M/AMASS with 3D ground truth, fine-tuning on FineGym 2D skeletons, and end-to-end generation tuning with spatial, temporal, and appearance-debiased temporal losses.

## Results

On the FineGym fine-grained action subsets (FX-JUMP and FX-TURN), FinePhys achieves the best User Study scores of 4.13 (Text), 3.86 (Domain), and 4.03 (Smooth) on a 1–5 scale, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline AnimateDiff (T+I, finetuned) at 3.20/3.20/3.17. On the improved CLIP-SIM* metrics, FinePhys scores 0.826 (Domain) and 0.833 (Smooth) versus 0.769/0.793 for AnimateDiff, and achieves the lowest FVD of 484.49 (vs. 529.38 for AnimateDiff). The paper also demonstrates that the original CLIP-SIM metric is unreliable for fine-grained action evaluation (e.g., T2I-Zero produces irrelevant outputs yet scores highest on original CLIP-SIM Smooth), motivating their improved CLIP-SIM* formulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FinePhys outperforms all seven baselines across every reliable metric (User Study, CLIP-SIM*, FVD) when finetuned on FineGym. The closest competitor is AnimateDiff with an initial-frame condition (T+I), which scores 3.20/3.20/3.17 on User Study and 0.769/0.793 on CLIP-SIM*; FinePhys leads by roughly 0.9 points on User Study and 0.06 on CLIP-SIM* Domain. Among text-only baselines, VideoCrafter2 achieves the highest PickScore (20.023) but lags significantly on all other metrics (FVD 697.73, User Text 2.23), indicating that high text-image alignment does not translate to plausible motion. Pose-conditioned baselines (Follow-Your-Pose, Text2Video-Zero T+P) perform poorly on fine-grained gymnastics actions, with User Smooth scores as low as 1.67, confirming that raw 2D skeleton guidance without physical modeling is insufficient for complex deformations. The paper also notes that the original CLIP-SIM metric is misleading for this task (e.g., T2I-Zero scores 0.978 on CLIP-SIM Smooth despite generating irrelevant content), which is why the authors introduce CLIP-SIM* as a more faithful evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FinePhys builds on the latent diffusion video generation stack (Stable Diffusion v1.5 + AnimateDiff motion modules) and extends pose-guided video generation (Follow-Your-Pose, Control-A-Video) by introducing an explicit physics-informed skeletal guidance pipeline. Its 2D-to-3D lifting module follows the in-context learning paradigm of Skeleton-in-context [69] and MotionBERT [87], while the PhysNet module departs from prior physics-informed motion work (PhysDiff, PhysPT, PhysMoP) by explicitly instantiating Euler-Lagrange equations through differentiable neural network modules rather than relying on physics simulators or Lagrangian losses. The paper positions itself at the intersection of controllable video generation and physics-based human dynamics, arguing that fine-grained gymnastics actions require all three forms of physics incorporation (observational, inductive, learning bias) simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit physical law enforcement (via bidirectional Euler-Lagrange re-estimation) can meaningfully improve skeletal guidance for the hardest sub-category of human action video generation, where data-driven pose estimation alone fails. The improved CLIP-SIM* metric addresses a real evaluation gap for fine-grained motion, and the three-pronged physics incorporation framework provides a reusable template for other physics-constrained generation tasks. However, the authors acknowledge that salto actions (simultaneous aerial rotations) remain intractable, and the narrow evaluation scope (35 classes, 350 videos from FineGym Floor Exercise) limits generalizability claims. Whether explicit EL-Eq. parameterization scales advantageously over learned implicit physics representations in broader action domains remains an open question.
