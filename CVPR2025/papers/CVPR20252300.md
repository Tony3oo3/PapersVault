## Problem

Existing multimodal emotion recognition methods apply a unified fusion paradigm to all samples, ignoring that the relative importance of language, visual, and acoustic modalities varies substantially across individuals and utterances. Additionally, mainstream modality decoupling approaches preserve modality-specific representations but discard the predictive capability of unimodal data, causing modality specialization to disappear during fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252300/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three unaligned low-level feature sequences per utterance: language (300-d GloVe or 768-d BERT hidden states), visual (35-d Facet facial action units at 15 Hz), and acoustic (74-d COVAREP at 12.5–20 Hz), each with its own temporal length. For MER tasks the output is a continuous sentiment intensity score in [−3, 3]; for the MIR extension on MIntRec the output is one of 11 discrete intent class labels.

## Method

EMOE introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, a Mixture of Modality Experts (MoME) module uses a router network (linear layers with batch norm and activation, plus a temperature-scaled softmax) to produce a per-sample weight vector over the three modality encoders, which are then fused via weighted summation of transformer-extracted high-level features; a balancing loss combining router entropy and a weight-to-predictive-importance similarity term prevents the dominant modality from monopolizing the weights. Second, Unimodal Distillation with Router Selection adds single-modality classification heads whose prediction losses keep each modality individually informative, then performs one-way knowledge distillation from the multimodal logits to a router-weighted combination of unimodal logits, so that modality-specific predictive signals are injected back into the fused representation. The total objective is the sum of the multimodal and unimodal task losses, the MoME balance loss, and the distillation loss, each scaled by a hyperparameter.

## Results

On CMU-MOSI (aligned), EMOE achieves 47.7% ACC7, 85.4% ACC2, 85.4% F1, and 0.710 MAE, outperforming the strongest baseline DMD (46.2% ACC7, 0.721 MAE) by 1.5% in ACC7. On CMU-MOSEI (unaligned), EMOE reaches 53.9% ACC7, 85.5% F1, and 0.530 MAE, surpassing DMD (53.1% ACC7, 0.536 MAE) and Self-MM (52.9% ACC7, 0.535 MAE). On MIntRec for intent recognition, EMOE attains 72.58% ACC and 70.73% F1, matching MulT on accuracy while improving F1 by 1.37 points and precision by 1.35 points over MISA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMOE outperforms all 11 baselines across both MER datasets. On CMU-MOSI, the largest margin is over DMD [29] (47.7% vs. 46.2% ACC7, aligned) and Self-MM [60] (85.4% vs. 84.9% F1); over older methods like MulT [44] and TFN [63], the gap exceeds 12% in ACC7. On CMU-MOSEI, EMOE leads DMD [29] by 0.8% ACC7 (53.9% vs. 53.1%, unaligned) and Self-MM [60] by 1.0% ACC7 (53.9% vs. 52.9%), while also achieving the lowest MAE (0.530). On MIntRec, EMOE ties MulT [44] on accuracy (72.58%) but surpasses it by 1.37 points in F1 (70.73% vs. 69.36%) and by 1.35 points in precision over MISA [14] (72.08% vs. 71.24%). EMOE does not lose to any baseline on any reported metric across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMOE repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm by treating each modality's encoder as an expert rather than duplicating a single network, with a router network learning per-sample importance weights. It departs from prior multimodal fusion methods (MulT, MISA, DMD, TFN) that apply a unified fusion strategy to all samples, and from modality-balance approaches (gradient modulation, re-learning) that operate at the training level rather than the sample level. The Unimodal Distillation component extends knowledge distillation into the multimodal fusion setting, using router-selected unimodal logits to guide multimodal features and counteract the modality specialization disappearance observed in decoupling-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sample-level dynamic fusion addresses a practical limitation of current MER systems: a single fusion paradigm cannot accommodate the fact that different individuals express emotions through different modalities. The extension to Multimodal Intent Recognition (MIntRec) suggests the routing-and-distillation recipe generalizes beyond sentiment, though the paper does not explore more than three modalities or missing-modality scenarios. Open questions include whether the router entropy loss is essential or merely a useful regularizer, how the approach scales to higher-modality settings, and whether the modest 1–2% gains over strong baselines like DMD persist under different feature extractors or larger model capacities.
