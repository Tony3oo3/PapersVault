## Problem

Sparse view synthesis methods built on 3D Gaussian Splatting suffer from region-wise imbalanced supervision: high-covisibility (multi-view) regions receive strong multiview constraints while mono-view (high-uncertainty) regions are under-supervised, leading to missing details in sparsely captured areas. Additionally, COLMAP-derived initial point clouds lack geometric detail when training views are limited, further degrading reconstruction in underrepresented regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250194/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse training view images (3/6/9 views at 8× downsampled for LLFF; 12/24 views at 4× downsampled for Mip-NeRF 360), COLMAP camera poses and initial point cloud, monocular depth estimates from Metric3D v2, and dense correspondences from MASt3R. It produces rendered novel views via a 3D Gaussian representation, per-image pixel-wise covisibility maps (integer counts from 0 to n−1), and an enhanced initial point cloud that covers both multi-view and mono-view regions in a unified coordinate system.

## Method

CoMapGS generates per-pixel covisibility maps by accumulating dense correspondence counts from MASt3R across all training view pairs, then uses these maps to drive two key mechanisms. First, it enhances initial point clouds by triangulating dense correspondences for multi-view regions and unprojecting monocular depth for mono-view regions, aligning the latter to the former via a learned anisotropic scaling transformation. Second, it introduces a covisibility-weighted proximity loss: a 3-layer MLP classifier predicts how close each Gaussian is to the scene geometry, and the loss is weighted inversely to the local covisibility count (stronger supervision for mono-view pixels) within the frustum, while Gaussians outside the frustum receive a scene-level weight scaled by the average covisibility score. This proximity loss is added to the standard 3DGS objective (L1 + D-SSIM), making the approach a drop-in extension of existing methods like CoR-GS and FSGS.

## Results

On LLFF (6 views), CoMapGS applied to CoR-GS achieves PSNR 25.204, SSIM 0.854, and LPIPS 0.108, outperforming the CoR-GS baseline (24.777 / 0.844 / 0.116) and all other 3DGS-based methods. On Mip-NeRF 360 (12 views), CoMapGS+CoR-GS reaches PSNR 19.680 and LPIPS 0.394, improving over CoR-GS (19.162 / 0.414) by +0.52 dB and −0.020 respectively. The method also extends to FSGS, yielding PSNR 24.808 (6-view LLFF) and 19.680→23.462 (24-view Mip-NeRF 360), consistently ranking first or second across all metrics and view counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMapGS consistently outperforms its two direct 3DGS baselines (CoR-GS and FSGS) across all metrics and view counts on both LLFF and Mip-NeRF 360. On LLFF 6-view, CoMapGS+CoR-GS gains +0.43 dB PSNR, +0.010 SSIM, and −0.008 LPIPS over CoR-GS, and +0.51 dB PSNR over FSGS. On Mip-NeRF 360 12-view, the improvement over CoR-GS is +0.52 dB PSNR and −0.020 LPIPS, with the LPIPS gap widening to −0.007 at 24 views. The only method that beats CoMapGS on PSNR is ReconFusion (21.34 vs 21.105 on LLFF 3-view), but CoMapGS substantially outperforms it on SSIM (0.747 vs 0.724) and LPIPS (0.182 vs 0.203), indicating better structural and perceptual fidelity. Among NeRF-based methods, CoMapGS surpasses all of them by a wide margin (e.g., +1.47 dB PSNR over SparseNeRF on LLFF 3-view). On Mip-NeRF 360, CoMapGS also outperforms vanilla 3DGS by +1.16 dB PSNR (12-view), confirming gains over the unmodified baseline as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMapGS builds directly on CoR-GS and FSGS, the two most recent 3DGS-based sparse view synthesis methods, by adding a covisibility map (pixel-wise correspondence counts from MASt3R) and a proximity MLP classifier as new supervision components. Conceptually, it departs from prior 3DGS sparse-view methods that primarily improve multiview regions through densification or depth constraints, instead explicitly targeting high-uncertainty mono-view regions that prior work either ignores or penalizes. The covisibility map idea extends DyCheck's binary visibility mask to a graded, pixel-level count, and the weighted supervision strategy is distinct from NeRFVS (dense-view, video-based) and InfoNorm (structured indoor scenes), which use covisibility in narrower settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoMapGS is the first method to explicitly recover high-uncertainty mono-view regions in sparse 3DGS, and its reframing of 'sparsity' as a region-wise covisibility problem rather than a global view-count problem is a useful conceptual shift for the field. The modular design (covisibility map + enhanced point cloud + proximity loss) can be plugged into other 3DGS sparse-view pipelines, making it practically relevant. Limitations include dependence on multiple external models (MASt3R, monocular depth, COLMAP) and evaluation limited to two datasets; open questions include scalability to very large scenes, dynamic content, and whether the covisibility-aware weighting generalizes beyond the tested view counts.
