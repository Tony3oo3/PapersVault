## Problem

3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) require dense multi-view coverage and produce severe artifacts in under-observed regions, while generative models can synthesize content from few or no views but lack 3D geometric consistency. This conditioning gap stems from a fundamental misalignment between 3D reconstruction constraints and 2D generative priors, limiting both fields' practical applicability to real-world video captures that are dense along a trajectory but sparse in angular coverage.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse video captures (downsampled to 1/2 or 1/4 of original frames) with calibrated camera poses as input, where 75% of pixels are masked per scene to simulate limited view coverage. It produces an optimized 2D Gaussian Splatting scene that renders artifact-free novel views as RGB-D video at 960×540 resolution, supporting both view interpolation between input poses and extrapolation to far-field trajectories. The video diffusion component specifically consumes artifact-prone RGB-D renderings (with black backgrounds in unobserved regions) and outputs clean RGB-D video sequences of 16 frames.

## Method

GenFusion fine-tunes DynamiCrafter as a reconstruction-driven video diffusion model by replacing its RGB VAE with a frozen RGB-D VAE from LDM3D, enabling depth-aware generation without architectural changes. Training data is generated via masked 3D reconstruction: one quadrant of pixels is removed per scene, 2DGS reconstructs the partial scene, and full-frame rendering along the original trajectory produces artifact-prone videos paired with ground-truth captures. At inference, a cyclic fusion loop iteratively renders the current 3DGS from novel trajectories, feeds renderings to the diffusion model to generate clean RGB-D video, and adds these generated frames as additional photometric supervision for 3DGS optimization. Unreliable regions (low cumulative opacity or depth mismatch) trigger adaptive Gaussian insertion by back-projecting generated RGB-D points, enabling content expansion into previously invisible areas. The generation loss weight follows a sinusoidal warm-up schedule to stabilize optimization.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360 sparse-view reconstruction (3/6/9 input views), GenFusion achieves the best average PSNR of 16.93 dB and SSIM of 0.437, surpassing ReconFusion (16.87 dB, 0.397) and FSGS (16.08 dB, 0.408). On the more challenging view extrapolation task (masked input on DL3DV and TnT), GenFusion achieves 20.47 dB PSNR and 0.788 SSIM on DL3DV at 1/2 fps, outperforming FSGS (18.25 dB, 0.722) and 3DGS (17.22 dB, 0.740) by a significant margin. Notably, GenFusion is the first Gaussian Splatting method to match state-of-the-art NeRF performance on Mip-NeRF360 in sparse-view settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenFusion outperforms all NeRF-based baselines (Zip-NeRF, DiffusionNeRF, FreeNeRF, SimpleNeRF, ZeroNVS) by a large margin on Mip-NeRF360, with PSNR gains of 2–4.6 dB over the best NeRF method (ZeroNVS at 15.31 dB). Against the closest competitor ReconFusion [48], GenFusion edges ahead on PSNR (16.93 vs. 16.87) and substantially on SSIM (0.437 vs. 0.397), while ReconFusion retains a slight LPIPS advantage (0.547 vs. 0.517). Among Gaussian Splatting methods, GenFusion surpasses 3DGS by 2.0 dB PSNR and 0.086 SSIM, and 2DGS by 2.0 dB PSNR and 0.102 SSIM, though 3DGS achieves the best LPIPS (0.509 vs. 0.517). On the view extrapolation benchmark (Table 3), GenFusion dominates all baselines across both DL3DV and TnT at both 1/2 and 1/4 fps, with the largest gap over FSGS reaching 2.2 dB PSNR on DL3DV 1/2 fps. The paper highlights that Gaussian Splatting is typically harder to train than NeRF in sparse settings, and GenFusion closes this gap for the first time on Mip-NeRF360.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenFusion extends the ReconFusion line of work, which regularizes NeRF optimization with image diffusion priors, by shifting to video diffusion (DynamiCrafter) and 2D Gaussian Splatting, and by introducing a cyclic fusion loop that iteratively feeds generated frames back into the reconstruction training set. The key conceptual departure is treating reconstruction and generation as a bidirectional feedback process rather than a one-shot regularization step, and using masked 3D reconstruction as a scalable data-generation strategy to produce artifact-GT video pairs without requiring separate far-field captures. The RGB-D VAE (from LDM3D) replaces the standard RGB VAE to inject geometric constraints into the diffusion backbone without architectural changes, a practical adaptation not seen in prior view-conditioned generation work like ZeroNVS or CAT3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: video captures are easy to obtain but produce 3D reconstructions with severe far-field artifacts, and existing feedforward methods saturate beyond 4–8 views. The cyclic fusion mechanism and masked-reconstruction evaluation protocol offer a reusable framework for closing the reconstruction–generation gap at scene scale. However, the method adds ~40 minutes of training per scene, and large invisible regions still suffer from blurriness due to inconsistency between independently generated video fragments—addressing this temporal coherence problem is identified as the key next step. The approach's reliance on a frozen, pre-trained video diffusion model also raises questions about how well it generalizes to scene types outside the DL3DV-10K training distribution.
