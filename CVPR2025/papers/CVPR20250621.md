## Problem

Automatic video chaptering—partitioning long videos into semantic segments with descriptive titles—remains underexplored, and the prior state of the art (Vid2Seq) relies on fixed equidistant frame sampling and direct visual-to-textual mapping, which is both computationally expensive and suboptimal for hour-long content. The paper tackles how to efficiently process hour-long videos by operating entirely in the text domain with a large-context LLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250621/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two text streams: (1) ASR speech transcripts (Whisper-Large-V2) with start timestamps, and (2) image captions (MiniCPM-V) for a sparse set of speech-selected keyframes, each prepended with an HH:MM:SS timestamp and a modality prefix. The LLM outputs a single autoregressive token sequence where each chapter is a timestamp followed by a free-form title, with chapter end times implicitly defined by the next chapter's start. For videos exceeding the LLM context window (~25k tokens at inference), an iterative chunked prediction procedure sequentially processes the timeline and merges results.

## Method

Chapter-Llama finetunes Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct via LoRA to jointly predict chapter boundaries and titles as plain text tokens, treating the entire video as a long multi-modal text document. The key efficiency mechanism is a speech-based frame selection strategy: a speech-only variant of the LLM first predicts approximate chapter boundaries from ASR alone, and only those ~10 timestamp locations are captioned by MiniCPM-V, reducing visual processing from hundreds of frames to ~10 per video. Both the speech-only selector and the full chaptering model share the same Llama backbone but use separate LoRA adapters (13 MB each). For videos exceeding the context window, an iterative prediction scheme processes sequential chunks (each starting where the previous ended) and merges predictions, improving F1 from 13.1 to 25.3 on the >35k-token subset. The approach achieves 45.3 F1 on VidChapters-7M, substantially outperforming Vid2Seq (26.7 F1) while using only 2.5% of the available training data.

## Results

On the full VidChapters-7M test set, Chapter-Llama (Llama-3.1-8B finetuned with LoRA) achieves 45.3 F1, 71.8 tIoU, 19.3 SODA, and 100.9 CIDEr, substantially outperforming the prior state of the art Vid2Seq (26.7 F1, 58.6 tIoU, 11.6 SODA, 55.8 CIDEr). The improvement is most pronounced on medium (46.7 vs 19.0 F1) and long (41.3 vs 16.7 F1) videos. Even in zero-shot mode without any chapter-annotation finetuning, Chapter-Llama reaches 29.5 F1, far exceeding Vid2Seq's zero-shot score of 3.0 F1. The model was trained on only 20k videos (2.5% of the available training data) yet surpasses Vid2Seq which was trained on HowTo100M plus the full VidChapters-7M training set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chapter-Llama decisively outperforms the prior state of the art Vid2Seq [110] across all metrics: 45.3 vs 26.7 F1, 71.8 vs 58.6 tIoU, 19.3 vs 11.6 SODA, and 100.9 vs 55.8 CIDEr, despite using only 2.5% of the training data. Against proprietary LLMs evaluated zero-shot with the same speech-based frame selection and captioning pipeline, Chapter-Llama still leads: 45.3 vs 42.2 F1 (Gemini-1.5-Pro), 45.3 vs 37.6 F1 (GPT-4o), and 45.3 vs 40.2 F1 (Gemini-2.0-Flash). In the zero-shot setting (no finetuning on chapter annotations), Chapter-Llama achieves 29.5 F1 versus Vid2Seq's 3.0 F1, demonstrating that the LLM backbone and text-based formulation provide strong inductive bias even without task-specific training. For frame selection, the speech-based strategy (10.3 frames avg.) outperforms 100 equidistant frames (F1: 42.6 vs 39.2 in the combined modality setting) and shot-boundary sampling (42.6 vs 40.6), while using roughly 10× fewer frames. The off-the-shelf shot detector Pyscenedetect [11] achieves only 6.2 F1 as a standalone segmentation method, far below any LLM-based approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper addresses video chaptering, a task just introduced by Yang et al. [109] alongside the VidChapters-7M dataset, where the prior baseline (Vid2Seq [110]) processes 100 equidistant video frames through a multimodal transformer. Chapter-Llama departs from this by converting all inputs to text—ASR transcripts plus MiniCPM-V captions of speech-selected keyframes—and fine-tuning a pure-text LLM (Llama-3.1-8B with LoRA) to predict boundaries and titles. The speech-guided frame selection strategy is conceptually related to SCSampler [43], which uses audio to pick salient clips, but here it is bootstrapped by a speech-only LLM variant to predict chapter boundaries before captioning. The broader LLM-for-video trend (VideoTree, VideoAgent, [121]) of captioning frames before feeding an LLM is extended here to the hour-long regime via iterative chunked prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video chaptering is a practically important but underexplored task for navigating long-form content, and this work establishes a strong, efficient baseline that substantially outperforms the prior state of the art (45.3 vs 26.7 F1) while requiring only 10k training videos and ~10 captioned frames per video. The finding that a purely text-based pipeline can beat a multimodal model that directly ingests visual features suggests that the bottleneck in long-video understanding may be context management rather than modality fusion. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond YouTube-style content, how it handles videos with minimal speech, and whether hierarchical or multi-granularity chaptering is feasible. The reliance on ASR and captioner quality, and potential biases inherited from web-scale pretraining, remain practical limitations.
