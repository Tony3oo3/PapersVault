## Problem

Diffusion-based scene text editing methods that rely on pretrained OCR features for glyph guidance fail to capture the hierarchical structure of text—from individual strokes to stroke interactions to overall character structure—resulting in distorted or unrecognizable characters, especially for complex logographic systems like Chinese. Existing approaches treat OCR features as a flat conditioning signal without explicitly modeling the cross-level relationships between character-level and line-level representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251540/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a scene image, a binary mask delineating the text region to be edited, and a target text string. The text string is rendered into two forms: individual single-character glyph images (local-level, N images of size H_l × W_l) and a unified text-line glyph image (global-level, H_g × W_g), both processed by a pretrained OCR backbone. The output is a full-resolution edited scene image where the masked region contains the new text while preserving surrounding context; training operates at 512×512 resolution with DDIM sampling (20 steps) at inference.

## Method

GlyphMastero is a trainable glyph encoder that extracts dual-stream features from a pretrained PaddleOCR-v4 model: a local-level stream from individual character glyph images and a global-level stream from a unified text-line image, where the global stream additionally fuses five hierarchical backbone feature maps through a feature pyramid network. Two glyph attention modules then perform multi-head cross-attention (with rotary positional embeddings) between local and global features at both the neck and backbone levels, explicitly capturing character-to-line interactions. The resulting cross-level, multi-scale features are concatenated and projected into a condition embedding that guides a Stable Diffusion 2.1 inpainting UNet via cross-attention. The entire glyph encoder is trained jointly with the diffusion model using the standard denoising objective, optimizing glyph representations directly through generation-based supervision rather than a separate pretraining stage.

## Results

On the multi-lingual AnyText-Eval benchmark (4,181 English and 2,092 Chinese text-position pairs), GlyphMastero achieves 81.70% English and 73.01% Chinese sentence accuracy, surpassing the prior SOTA AnyText (60.67% / 58.01%) by 18.02% in average sentence accuracy. Text-region FID drops to 4.61 (English) and 11.89 (Chinese), a 53.28% reduction over AnyText on average. In the English-only comparison against seven baselines, GlyphMastero attains the best sentence accuracy (81.70%) and CER (7.41%), while TextCtrl edges it out slightly on FID (4.15 vs. 4.61) and LPIPS (0.0425 vs. 0.0545).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two multi-lingual baselines, GlyphMastero dominates across every metric: it outperforms DiffUTE by 48.14% in average sentence accuracy and by 57.95% in FID, and outperforms AnyText by 18.02% in sentence accuracy and 53.28% in FID. In the English-only comparison (Table 2), GlyphMastero achieves the highest sentence accuracy (81.70%) and lowest CER (7.41%), beating SRNet (39.94%), MOSTEL (57.81%), DiffSTE (51.20%), TextDiffuser (52.60%), DiffUTE (33.19%), and AnyText (60.67%) on accuracy. The closest competitor is TextCtrl (NeurIPS 2024), which achieves 76.54% sentence accuracy and 9.40% CER; GlyphMastero wins on both accuracy metrics but TextCtrl retains a marginal advantage in style similarity (FID 4.15 vs. 4.61, LPIPS 0.0425 vs. 0.0545). The authors note that TextCtrl's style advantage is small and that GlyphMastero's multi-lingual capability (Chinese support) is absent in TextCtrl.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DiffUTE's inpainting-based diffusion formulation and TextDiffuser's region-specific granularity for scene text editing, but departs from both by replacing their direct use of pretrained OCR features with a trainable, hierarchical glyph encoder. Where DiffUTE uses fixed-length TrOCR features and AnyText concatenates OCR neck features into the latent space, GlyphMastero introduces a dual-stream architecture (local character-level and global line-level) with cross-level glyph attention modules and an FPN for multi-scale backbone fusion, explicitly modeling the stroke-to-character-to-line hierarchy that prior methods treat as a flat feature vector. The cross-attention conditioning strategy (rather than ControlNet-style latent concatenation) is chosen to preserve style coherence, a design decision motivated by observed limitations of latent-space guidance methods in maintaining scene context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18% sentence accuracy gain and 53% FID reduction over the prior SOTA (AnyText) are substantial for a task where complex logographic systems like Chinese have been a persistent failure mode, making this practically relevant for multilingual content editing. However, the method is tied to a specific OCR backbone (PaddleOCR-v4) and 512×512 training resolution, and the authors acknowledge that long-text accuracy still lags behind shorter sequences. Open questions include whether the hierarchical glyph attention generalizes to other writing systems (e.g., Arabic, Devanagari), whether the FPN design transfers to other OCR backbones, and whether higher-resolution training or stronger base diffusion models would close the remaining long-text gap.
