## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation under diverse adverse conditions (nighttime, rain, fog) suffers from two fundamental gaps: heterogeneous degradation types make it hard to learn a unified representation, and the absence of labeled real-world adverse data limits direct learning. Existing methods either train solely on synthetic adverse data with pseudo-depth labels or apply daytime photometric losses to real adverse images, both of which fail to bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap and produce suboptimal robustness.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a single RGB frame or a consecutive frame pair (I_{t-1}, I_t) and outputs a per-pixel depth map, operating in both single-frame and multi-frame inference modes. Depth is predicted over a fixed range of 0.1–80 m (nuScenes, DrivingStereo) or 0.1–50 m (Robotcar) using adaptive depth bins. The backbone is a shared ResNet encoder, and the model is evaluated zero-shot on unseen conditions (fog on DrivingStereo) without any condition-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. In Synthetic Adaptation, a frozen Manydepth daytime model generates pseudo-depth labels on GAN/diffusion-augmented adverse images, and the key innovation is distilling knowledge through cost volumes (constructed via feature dot-product across warped frames) rather than raw features, which naturally filters redundant information and retains explicit motion-structure cues. In Real Adaptation, two strategies address the synthetic-real gap: (1) a consistency-reweighting loss that compares daytime and synthetic model predictions on real images to produce an exponential confidence map, up-weighting reliable pseudo-label regions; and (2) a structure prior constraint that computes differentiable depth histograms from the daytime model over the entire training set and minimizes KL divergence between the adverse-condition distribution and the daytime reference distribution, anchoring far-plane predictions that are most affected by degradation.

## Results

On nuScenes (daytime, nighttime, rain), the multi-frame model achieves AbsRel of 0.1170, 0.1713, and 0.1266 respectively, outperforming the best prior single-frame methods (md4all-DD, DM-MDE) by an average of 4.8% in AbsRel and 4.2% in RMSE. On Robotcar (daytime, nighttime), the single-frame model achieves AbsRel of 0.1063 and 0.1103, improving by 7.8% in AbsRel over recent methods. In zero-shot evaluation on DrivingStereo (rain, fog), the method surpasses state-of-the-art by 3.0% in AbsRel and 2.8% in RMSE, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all single-frame baselines (md4all [8], robust-depth [22], DM-MDE [25], Monodepth2 [10], RNW [30]) across all three nuScenes conditions in both AbsRel and RMSE. In the multi-frame setting, Ours-Multi surpasses the self-designed baseline Manydepth+[8] (which uses only synthetic pseudo-labels) by 2.5% in AbsRel on nighttime and 3.4% on rain, confirming that the gains come from the training strategy rather than the multi-frame backbone. Notably, Ours-Multi also beats the radar-based R4Dyn [7] on nighttime (0.1713 vs. 0.2194 AbsRel) and rain (0.1266 vs. 0.1337 AbsRel) without using any additional sensor. On Robotcar, Ours-Single achieves the best AbsRel in both daytime (0.1063) and nighttime (0.1103), beating md4all [8] (0.1128/0.1219) and DM-MDE [25] (0.1190/0.1290). In zero-shot DrivingStereo evaluation, the method leads on rain (0.1707 AbsRel vs. 0.1822 for md4all) and fog (0.1281 vs. 0.1259 for md4all, where it is slightly behind on AbsRel but ahead on SqRel and RMSE), showing stronger generalization to unseen weather conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Manydepth (CVPR 2021) as its multi-frame backbone, repurposing the cost volume component not merely as a geometric guidance mechanism but as an auxiliary distillation space for transferring motion-structure knowledge from a frozen daytime model to adverse-condition models. It departs from prior robust depth estimation methods (md4all, robust-depth, DM-MDE) that operate exclusively on synthetic adverse data with pseudo-labels or GAN-based photometric losses, by introducing a two-stage synthetic-to-real pipeline that explicitly addresses the synthetic-real domain gap. The consistency-reweighting strategy and differentiable histogram KL-divergence constraint represent new loss formulations that go beyond the pseudo-label distillation paradigm used by all prior multi-condition methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a critical and previously underappreciated gap: models trained on synthetic adverse data suffer a 70% AbsRel degradation when evaluated on real adverse data, motivating the real adaptation stage. The synthetic-to-real pipeline is practically important because it enables a single model to handle multiple real-world conditions (daytime, night, rain, fog) without condition-specific architectures, which is essential for autonomous driving deployment. Limitations include the multi-stage training complexity (three sequential phases), dependence on a strong frozen daytime model and pretrained augmentation models, and evaluation limited to driving scenarios. Open questions include whether the structure prior (daytime depth distribution) remains valid for fundamentally different scenes (e.g., urban vs. rural) and whether the pipeline can be simplified into a single end-to-end training procedure.
