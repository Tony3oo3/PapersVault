## Problem

Existing event-based 3D reconstruction methods (E-NeRF, EventNeRF, Robust e-NeRF) all require ground-truth camera poses, limiting their use in real-world robotic scenarios where poses are unknown. Moreover, they rely on implicit NeRF representations, which are less efficient for rendering and optimization than explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting. No prior method performs pose-free, incremental dense 3D reconstruction from a single event camera.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single asynchronous event camera stream (no poses), chunked into fixed 50 ms windows; within each chunk, two close random timestamps are sampled to compute a measured brightness change E(x). The output is a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (positions, covariances, opacities, colors), a per-chunk SE(3) camera trajectory, and rendered novel-view brightness images. On synthetic data (Replica, 768×480) events are generated from 1000 Hz RGB renders with contrast threshold 0.1; on real data (TUM-VIE, 1280×720) the left Prophesee Gen4 HD camera is used with threshold 0.2.

## Method

IncEventGS adopts a tracking-and-mapping SLAM paradigm over 3D Gaussian Splatting. Each event chunk is associated with a continuous SE(3) trajectory parameterized by start and end poses, connected by geodesic linear interpolation; random close timestamps within the chunk yield a photometric loss between the rendered brightness change (log-ratio of two 3D-GS renders) and the measured event brightness change. Tracking optimizes only the latest chunk's trajectory, while mapping performs sliding-window bundle adjustment (n_w=20 chunks) jointly refining both 3D-GS attributes and all trajectories in the window, minimizing a weighted L2 + SSIM loss. A key initialization strategy bootstraps the system by first optimizing random Gaussians and near-identity poses, then re-projecting a monocular depth estimate (from a pre-trained diffusion-based network) to re-seed Gaussian centers, which reduces ATE from 1.534 cm to 0.064 cm. New Gaussians are incrementally added in previously unmapped regions guided by a rendered alpha-visibility mask.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, IncEventGS achieves average PSNR of 23.32 dB across five scenes (ranging from 21.18 to 25.64 dB), substantially outperforming all NeRF-based baselines that use ground-truth poses (best: Robust e-NeRF at 17.73 dB) and the two-stage E2VID+COLMAP+3DGS pipeline (15.28 dB). For camera pose estimation, IncEventGS achieves ATE of 0.045–0.067 cm on Replica sequences, compared to 0.138–0.289 cm for DEVO and 17.93–105.19 cm for E2VID+COLMAP. On the real TUM-VIE dataset, IncEventGS attains ATE of 0.115–0.298 cm, outperforming both DEVO (0.147–2.93 cm) and ESVO2 (0.337–2.506 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IncEventGS outperforms all three NeRF-based baselines (E-NeRF, EventNeRF, Robust e-NeRF) by 5.6–8.2 dB in average PSNR, despite those methods relying on ground-truth camera poses while IncEventGS estimates poses entirely from the event stream. Against the two-stage E2VID+COLMAP+3DGS pipeline (which also uses 3D-GS), IncEventGS gains ~8 dB in PSNR, attributed to its joint event-based bundle adjustment that avoids error accumulation from the separate pose-estimation stage. For pose estimation, IncEventGS reduces ATE by 3–6× compared to DEVO on Replica (e.g., 0.046 cm vs. 0.289 cm on room0) and by 2–10× on TUM-VIE sequences (e.g., 0.251 cm vs. 2.93 cm on 6dof). Compared to the stereo ESVO2, IncEventGS achieves lower ATE on all TUM-VIE sequences where both methods report results (e.g., 0.115 cm vs. 0.337 cm on 1d, 0.298 cm vs. 1.066 cm on 3d). The E2VID+COLMAP baseline is orders of magnitude worse in ATE (17.93–105.19 cm), confirming that converting events to video and running standard SfM is far less accurate than the proposed direct event-based tracking and mapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

IncEventGS sits at the intersection of event-based 3D reconstruction and 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM. It departs from prior event-based NeRF methods (E-NeRF, EventNeRF, Robust e-NeRF) and concurrent 3D-GS event works (EvGGS, Event3DGS, E2GS) by eliminating the ground-truth pose requirement, instead adopting a tracking-and-mapping SLAM paradigm where the 3D-GS scene itself serves as the prior for motion estimation. The SE(3) continuous trajectory parameterization is directly inherited from BAD-Gaussians and BADNeRF, which modeled intra-exposure motion for blurry images; here it is repurposed to handle the asynchronous, high-rate nature of event streams. The method also borrows the monocular depth estimation bootstrapping idea from diffusion-based depth models, a component absent from all prior event-based reconstruction pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pose-free dense 3D reconstruction from a single event camera, which is practically important for robotic scenarios where no external pose reference is available and frame-based cameras suffer from motion blur or low-light failure. The ablation showing that depth bootstrapping reduces ATE by a factor of ~24 (1.534 cm → 0.064 cm) highlights a critical open question: can this dependency on a pre-trained depth model be removed or made more robust for unseen environments? Limitations include evaluation only on indoor scenes (Replica, TUM-VIE), relatively low NVS metrics due to the absence of absolute brightness supervision, and reliance on a sliding-window BA that may struggle with long trajectories or large-scale outdoor scenes.
