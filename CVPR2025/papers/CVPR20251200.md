## Problem

As pretrained vision models grow in scale, fine-tuning them on low-resource devices (no GPU, limited memory, constrained bandwidth) becomes infeasible. Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods require the full backbone on-device, while side-tuning methods that split computation between a cloud server and an edge device still transfer large intermediate feature sets (e.g., 7.5 MB per image for LST), making communication a critical bottleneck that prior work has largely ignored.

## Input / Output

The raw input is a standard RGB image (224×224), but the edge device never sees it directly. Instead, the cloud-hosted ViT-B/16 extracts intermediate feature maps from the first k Transformer blocks, and only their element-wise sum (a single 768-dim × 197-token tensor, ≈0.58 MB) is transferred to the edge device. The edge network then maps this gathered feature to a class label, so the effective input to the trainable module is a compressed, task-agnostic representation rather than pixels or full-layer activations.

## Method

MIST introduces a 'gather function' that replaces the full stack of intermediate features with their element-wise sum over the first k blocks (Gather*(B,x,k) = Σ_{l=0}^{k} z_l), reducing communication overhead to 1/(N+1) of the side-tuning baseline. This design is derived analytically from the skip-connection structure of ladder side-tuning: the summed feature is the only term in the side-block input that depends solely on the frozen backbone, making it the natural minimal external signal. The gathered feature is fed into a lightweight low-rank attention edge network (LAE) of L=4 blocks, each comprising layer norm, self-attention with QKV hidden dimension r=32 (vs. 768 feature dimension), and a residual connection, totaling only 0.38 M trainable parameters. A hyperparameter k ∈ {N, 3N/4, N/2} is selected via validation to control how many early-layer features are included, enabling domain migration for tasks with large distribution shifts. The entire fine-tuning loop (forward, backward, parameter update) runs exclusively on the edge device, requiring ~663 MB peak memory on a Raspberry Pi 4B with 2 GB SDRAM.

## Results

On VTAB-1K with ViT-B/16, MIST achieves 76.7% group-wise average accuracy, the highest among all compared methods, while requiring only 0.58 MB/image transfer overhead (vs. 4.04 MB for LAST and 7.50 MB for LST) and just 0.38M trainable parameters. On domain generalization (16-shot ImageNet-1K), MIST reaches 76.5% on ImageNet and 37.5% on ImageNet-R, outperforming the previous best (NOAH) by 5.0 and 9.0 points respectively. On fine-grained few-shot learning, MIST consistently outperforms all baselines across 1–16 shots, with a 3.2% average improvement over FacT at 16-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among separated-tuning-compatible methods, MIST (76.7% avg) beats LAST (76.5%) by 0.2% while reducing transfer overhead from 4.04 MB to 0.58 MB per image (7× less) and edge GPU memory from 0.77 GB to 0.45 GB. MIST also outperforms LST (74.3%) by 2.4% with 13× less transfer overhead and 6× fewer trainable parameters. Against non-separated PEFT methods, MIST matches or exceeds SPT-LoRA (76.4%) and FacT (75.6%) in accuracy while requiring ~1% of their edge MACs and ~10% of their edge GPU memory. On domain generalization, MIST surpasses NOAH (previous SOTA) by 5.0–13.1 points across ImageNet, Sketch, V2, A, and R, and beats LoRA, VPT, and Adapter by 5–21 points on the hardest shifts (ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R). On fine-grained few-shot, MIST leads all five baselines at every shot count, with the largest gap (3.2%) over FacT at 16-shot.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIST builds directly on the side-tuning lineage (LST, LAST), which decouples a trainable side network from a frozen pretrained backbone to save compute and memory during fine-tuning. The key departure is treating communication between the backbone and the side network as a first-class constraint: the paper derives a 'gather function' (sum of intermediate features) from the skip-connection algebra of side-tuning architectures, reducing the transferred payload from stacking all N+1 feature maps to a single summed tensor. The low-rank attention edge network (LAE) is adopted from LAST, while the broader PEFT landscape (LoRA, AdaptFormer, FacT, SPT-LoRA) serves as the accuracy benchmark that MIST must match despite operating under the additional communication constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper elevates communication overhead to a primary design criterion for PEFT, a factor largely ignored in prior work that assumes the backbone and adapter co-reside on the same device. The practical validation on a 2GB Raspberry Pi (663 MB peak training memory, 255 MB inference) demonstrates that the separated-tuning paradigm is deployable on genuinely resource-constrained hardware, not just simulated. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (ViT-B/16), a small candidate set for the hyperparameter k, and focus on recognition tasks; the authors note adaptation to segmentation and potential extension to generation tasks as future directions. An open question is whether more sophisticated gather functions (beyond summation) could further improve the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff.
