## Problem

Existing multi-modal generative models for nanoparticle distribution prediction neglect the inherent divergence between TME components (vessels and nuclei), which can cause the best uni-modal model to outperform the joint multi-modal model. No prior diffusion-based approach adaptively decides whether multi-modal fusion is beneficial or whether the uni-modal prediction should be preferred for a given input pair.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252239/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired 2D tumor vessel images (CD31-stained) and cell nuclei images (DAPI-stained) as conditioning inputs, which are concatenated channel-wise with the noisy latent at each reverse diffusion step. The diffusion process operates in the latent space of a VAE/VQGAN encoder for efficiency, and the output is a pixel-level nanoparticles distribution map (20-nm PEGylated CdSe/ZnS quantum dots) at the same spatial resolution as the inputs. The model is validated on 25,097 patches (internal, T1 breast cancer) and 3,800 patches (external, B16 tumor), as well as the BRATS brain MRI dataset for generalization.

## Method

DAMM-Diffusion performs both uni-modal and multi-modal reverse diffusion steps within a single unified U-Net network (UMM-Network), where the multi-modal branch additionally incorporates a Multi-Modal Fusion Module (MMFM) combining spatial and channel attention, and an Uncertainty-Aware Fusion Module (UAFM) that learns a per-node uncertainty map to modulate cross-attention weights. A Divergence-Aware Multi-Modal Predictor (DAMMP) computes a divergence score d (the mean of the uncertainty map) and selects the final noise prediction from either the multi-modal or uni-modal branch based on a threshold γ. A Divergence Feedback Loss (DFL) adaptively adjusts d by comparing L1 distances of both branches against ground truth, making the model less sensitive to the fixed threshold. Training minimizes standard MSE noise-prediction losses for both branches, with the multi-modal loss only active when d ≤ γ, and the total objective includes the DFL weighted by λ.

## Results

On the internal validation set (25,097 patches, 6-fold CV), DAMM-Diffusion achieves 96.54% SSIM and 47.93 dB PSNR for nanoparticle distribution prediction, outperforming the best uni-modal baseline (BBDM: 93.01% SSIM, 43.96 dB PSNR) by 3.53% SSIM and 3.97 dB PSNR, and the best multi-modal baseline (CoLa-Diff: 94.36% SSIM, 45.02 dB PSNR) by 2.18% SSIM and 2.91 dB PSNR. On the external validation set (3,800 patches from a different B16 tumor model), the method maintains its lead with 88.79% SSIM and 41.95 dB PSNR versus CoLa-Diff's 86.72% SSIM and 40.28 dB PSNR. On the BRATS brain image synthesis task, DAMM-Diffusion also achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all three many-to-one modality generation tasks (e.g., 25.61 dB PSNR / 92.68% SSIM for T2,FLAIR→T1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAMM-Diffusion outperforms all 12 baselines on both internal and external validation sets. Among uni-modal methods, the diffusion-based BBDM (93.01% SSIM / 43.96 dB PSNR) and LDM (92.97% / 43.72) are the strongest, yet both are surpassed by DAMM-Diffusion by over 3.5% SSIM and 4 dB PSNR. Among multi-modal methods, CoLa-Diff (94.36% / 45.02) and DiffDP (94.21% / 44.86) are the closest competitors, with DAMM-Diffusion leading by ~2.2% SSIM and ~2.9 dB PSNR. Notably, the paper highlights that several multi-modal methods (DiamondGAN, GANDA) actually underperform the best uni-modal baselines (LDM, BBDM), which the authors attribute to unaddressed inter-modality divergence. On the external B16 tumor validation set, the same ranking holds with DAMM-Diffusion achieving 88.79% SSIM / 41.95 dB PSNR versus CoLa-Diff's 86.72% / 40.28. On the BRATS brain MRI synthesis task, DAMM-Diffusion also leads all baselines across all three modality-pair generation tasks, confirming generalizability beyond the nanoparticle domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion framework of LDM [29] and the DDPM formulation [15] to perform image-to-image translation in the VAE/VQGAN latent space. It departs from prior multi-modal generative models (GANDA, HRPN, DiffDP, CoLa-Diff) by explicitly modeling the divergence between input modalities: rather than always fusing vessel and nuclei features, DAMM-Diffusion runs parallel uni-modal and multi-modal branches within a single U-Net and uses a Divergence-Aware Multi-Modal Predictor (DAMMP) to select the more reliable output at each denoising step. The MMFM (spatial + channel attention fusion) and UAFM (uncertainty-weighted cross-attention) modules extend standard multi-modal fusion by incorporating a learnable uncertainty map that quantifies per-node reliability during attention computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a practical failure mode in multi-modal medical image generation—namely, that inconsistent auxiliary modalities can degrade performance below the best single-modality baseline—which is a concern broadly applicable to any multi-modal fusion setting. The divergence-aware switching mechanism and DFL are general-purpose components that could be transplanted to other multi-modal diffusion tasks beyond NPs distribution prediction, as partially validated on BRATS brain MRI synthesis. Open questions include whether the binary uni/multi-modal switch is optimal compared to continuous soft blending, how the approach scales to three or more modalities, and whether the divergence signal (mean uncertainty) is sufficient or whether spatially-varying divergence maps would yield further gains.
