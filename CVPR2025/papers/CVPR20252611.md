## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods face a fundamental efficiency-accuracy tradeoff: two-stage pipelines are too slow (<3 FPS) due to redundant feature extraction from separate detection and grounding models, while single-stage methods remain far from real-time (<6 FPS) because they rely on point-based backbones with expensive operations (FPS, kNN) and aggressive downsampling that degrades small-object geometry. No prior work has explored sparse convolutional architectures for 3DVG, leaving a gap in efficient single-stage frameworks that can handle the large voxel counts required for deep text-voxel interaction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud with 6-dimensional features (xyz position + RGB) and a free-form natural language query. The point cloud is voxelized at 1 cm initial resolution and processed through three levels of sparse convolution at progressively coarser resolutions (8 cm, 16 cm, 32 cm). The text is encoded by a pre-trained RoBERTa into token embeddings. The output is a single 3D bounding box (center, dimensions, orientation) for the referred object, selected by highest objectness score from an anchor-free convolutional head.

## Method

TSP3D builds a single-stage 3DVG pipeline on a three-level multi-level sparse convolutional backbone (MinkResBlocks) with an anchor-free head, inspired by FCAF3D. The core innovation is Text-guided Pruning (TGP), which progressively sparsifies voxel features under textual guidance: a scene-level TGP (level 3→2) removes background voxels, and a target-level TGP (level 2→1) removes irrelevant objects, each using cross-attention to make voxels text-aware, an MLP to predict a binary pruning mask, and then self/cross-attention on the reduced feature set for multi-modal fusion. To counteract over-pruning of small or narrow targets, Completion-based Addition (CBA) adaptively queries high-resolution backbone features via a text-guided relevance mask and interpolates missing voxels into the pruned upsampled features with negligible overhead. The simplified TGP variant merges the pruning-guidance and feature-enhancement interactions into a single cross-attention step before pruning, reducing the voxel count to ~7% of its unpruned size while maintaining accuracy.

## Results

On ScanRefer, TSP3D achieves Acc@0.25 = 56.45 and Acc@0.5 = 46.71, surpassing the best two-stage method G³-LQ (45.58 Acc@0.5) by +1.13, while running at 12.43 FPS — more than 2× faster than the previous fastest single-stage method EDA (5.98 FPS). On ReferIt3D, TSP3D in single-stage mode reaches 48.7% on NR3D and 57.1% on SR3D, outperforming the best two-stage (detected-box) pipeline MCLN (46.1% / 53.9%) by +2.6% and +3.2% respectively. The method is the first to achieve real-time 3D visual grounding (>10 FPS) while maintaining state-of-the-art accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSP3D outperforms all single-stage baselines on ScanRefer in both accuracy and speed: it leads EDA (41.70 Acc@0.5, 5.98 FPS) by +5.01 Acc@0.5 and 2.1× speed, and G³-LQ single-stage (44.72 Acc@0.5) by +1.99. Against two-stage methods, TSP3D surpasses G³-LQ (45.58) and MCLN (45.53) on Acc@0.5 while being 4–6× faster than the fastest two-stage method (MCLN at 3.17 FPS). On ReferIt3D, TSP3D's single-stage results (48.7% NR3D, 57.1% SR3D) exceed the best two-stage (detected-box) pipeline MCLN (46.1% / 53.9%) by +2.6% and +3.2%, and also surpass EDA two-stage (det) (40.7% / 49.9%) by a wide margin. The only method with slightly higher Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer is MCLN two-stage (57.17 vs 56.45), but TSP3D still leads on Acc@0.5 and is 4× faster. Overall, TSP3D is the first method to simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art accuracy and real-time inference speed for 3D visual grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSP3D transfers the multi-level sparse convolutional architecture that has proven successful in 3D object detection (FCAF3D, GSDN) to the 3D visual grounding task, where it had not previously been explored. The key departure from prior 3DVG work is architectural: existing single-stage methods rely on point-based backbones (PointNet++) with expensive FPS/kNN operations, while two-stage methods redundantly run separate detection and grounding pipelines. The paper's central conceptual contribution is addressing the incompatibility between sparse voxel representations (which produce large feature counts) and the deep text-3D cross-attention that grounding requires, solved through the text-guided pruning and completion-based addition mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3DVG method to simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art accuracy and real-time inference speed (12.43 FPS), making 3D visual grounding practically deployable in resource-constrained settings like embodied robotics and AR/VR where previous methods (<6 FPS single-stage, <3 FPS two-stage) were infeasible. The text-guided pruning strategy—using language to reduce the 3D feature space before expensive attention—offers a general recipe for efficient multi-modal interaction that could transfer to other 3D perception tasks. Open questions include whether the pruning supervision (which relies on ground-truth object centers during training) limits generalization to unseen object categories, and whether the approach scales to multi-object or video-based grounding scenarios.
