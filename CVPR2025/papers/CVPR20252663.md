## Problem

Existing visual instruction generation methods either produce single-step images, generate sequences without conditioning on the user's specific environment, or rely on iterative generation that accumulates errors. No prior approach can generate a full multi-step image sequence that is simultaneously faithful to each textual instruction and grounded in the user's actual scene (same tools, workspace, ingredients). This paper tackles the joint challenge of step faithfulness, scene consistency, and task-level coherence in a single generation pass.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single user-provided RGB image (e.g., a photo of ingredients or tools on a countertop) and a variable-length sequence of 1–15 free-form textual instructions as input. It outputs a corresponding sequence of n generated images, one per instruction step, all at once rather than iteratively. The input image is projected into the VAE latent space and concatenated to each frame's noise tensor, while each frame receives its own dedicated text prompt through cross-attention, enabling per-step conditioning without a fixed sequence length constraint.

## Method

ShowHowTo is a latent video diffusion model built on a U-Net with interleaving spatial and temporal attention layers, initialized from the DynamicRafter checkpoint pretrained on WebVid10M and fine-tuned on 578K automatically extracted instruction sequences from HowTo100M. The key architectural choices are: (1) per-frame text conditioning, where each frame i in the sequence attends to its own instruction prompt τ_i via cross-attention (ablations show this substantially outperforms a single concatenated or summarized prompt); (2) dual input-image conditioning, where the user's image I_0 is both concatenated to each frame's latent noise and fed as a cross-attention feature to the U-Net; and (3) variable-length training, where sequence lengths are sampled per batch to support arbitrary step counts at inference. The model generates the entire image sequence in one forward pass, with temporal attention across all frames ensuring scene and object consistency, rather than chaining single-step edits.

## Results

On the ShowHowTo test set, ShowHowTo achieves 0.52 Step Faithfulness, 0.34 Scene Consistency, and 0.42 Task Faithfulness, outperforming all visual instruction generation baselines on all three metrics simultaneously. On the WikiHow-VGSI zero-shot test set, it reaches 0.72 Step Faithfulness, again the best among instruction-sequence methods. In a user study with 9 participants, ShowHowTo is preferred over all baselines with win rates of 84–97% on step faithfulness, 68–91% on scene consistency, and 65–96% on task faithfulness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShowHowTo outperforms all five visual instruction generation baselines on the combined step-and-scene metrics. Against image-to-image methods (InstructPix2Pix, AURORA, GenHowTo), it achieves substantially higher Step Faithfulness (0.52 vs. 0.25–0.49) while maintaining competitive Scene Consistency (0.34 vs. 0.13–0.33), indicating that iterative single-step editing cannot capture the full instruction. Against sequence-generation methods (Phung et al., StackedDiffusion), ShowHowTo dominates on Scene Consistency (0.34 vs. 0.02–0.03) because those methods lack input-image conditioning, and it also leads on Step Faithfulness (0.52 vs. 0.36–0.43). The only method that exceeds ShowHowTo on Step Faithfulness is unconditioned Stable Diffusion (0.70 vs. 0.52), but it scores near-zero Scene Consistency (0.03), confirming that text-only generation cannot ground outputs in the user's environment. In the user study, ShowHowTo is preferred over source video sequences in 42% of cases for both step and scene metrics, suggesting the generated instructions can sometimes be clearer than the original footage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on latent video diffusion models (specifically DynamicRafter) as its generative backbone, repurposing a video animation architecture for the distinct task of multi-step visual instruction generation. It departs from prior visual instruction generation work (StackedDiffusion, GenHowTo, Phung et al.) in two key ways: (1) it conditions generation on a user-provided input image to ensure scene consistency, whereas prior sequence methods generate in arbitrary settings, and (2) it generates the full sequence simultaneously with cross-step attention rather than iteratively. The data pipeline extends LLM-based step extraction from narrations (HowToStep, HowToCaption) but adds cross-modal frame alignment via DFN-CLIP to produce individual instruction frames rather than temporal intervals, and scales to 25K tasks beyond cooking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 578K-sequence ShowHowTo dataset is a significant open resource that removes the manual annotation bottleneck for visual instruction research, and the scene-conditioned formulation directly addresses the practical need for personalized visual assistants and robot planning that must respect a user's specific environment. Key limitations remain: the model struggles with object state tracking across many frames (e.g., generating raw meat after a cooking step) and can produce physically impossible configurations for rare objects. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences (>15 steps), whether it generalizes to egocentric or multi-agent scenarios, and how to close the gap with source video quality on task faithfulness.
