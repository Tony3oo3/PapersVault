## Problem

Natural Language-based Egocentric Task Verification (NLETV) requires determining whether procedural tasks in egocentric videos follow natural language instructions, but faces two core challenges: cross-modal heterogeneity (rules are in text while evidence is in video) and hierarchical misalignment (text encodes topological logic while video presents linear, detail-heavy operation flows). Prior verification methods relied on video-based references rather than language, limiting flexibility and generalizability, and no realistic physical-world benchmark existed for evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric video split into 20-frame clips and a natural language description of a multi-step procedural task (e.g., ~46 words on average in CSV-NL). The video clips are encoded via CLIP image encoder with transformer contextualization, while the text is first decomposed by a pre-trained T5 model into objective state and operational sub-descriptions before CLIP encoding; all features are projected into a shared 512-dimensional space. The output is a single binary label indicating whether the video's operation flow matches the language rule, with training supervised only at the pair level (no fine-grained temporal annotations).

## Method

PHGC formulates verification as a procedural heterogeneous graph completion process with three modules. (1) Heterogeneous Graph Construction (HGC) initializes a directed acyclic graph with five vertex types (objective states, operational descriptions, rule description, visual actions, visual progress) plus an implicit discriminative token, connected by temporal, topological, and rule-task matching edges. (2) Cross-Modal Path Finding (CMPF) discovers implicit semantic matching edges between fine-grained text vertices and visual action vertices by computing pairwise similarities and applying a dynamic-programming edge-cutting strategy that selects the highest-total-similarity subset under a one-to-one matching constraint. (3) Discriminative Entity Representation (DER) performs graph attention message passing over the completed procedural heterogeneous graph, aggregating hierarchical and cross-modal cues into the ending discriminative vertex for final classification. Training uses a combined objective: binary cross-entropy on the pair-level prediction plus an auxiliary maximum-likelihood loss on the selected semantic matching edges, enabling joint optimization at both coarse and fine-grained levels.

## Results

On the EgoTV benchmark, PHGC achieves an average F1 score of 87.4%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art NSG (80.0%) by 7.4 points, with the largest gain on Novel Steps (82.4% vs. 64.7%). On the newly introduced CSV-NL dataset, PHGC reaches 68.49% average F1, outperforming NSG (61.04%) by 7.45 points. PHGC approaches the Oracle Model (96.6% avg on EgoTV) which uses manual temporal annotations, while all MLLM baselines (PandaGPT, GoldFish, Otter) remain far below, with averages between 19.7% and 60.0% on EgoTV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHGC outperforms all 10 compared baselines on both EgoTV and CSV-NL across every evaluation scenario. The closest competitor, NSG [5] (the original NLETV method), is surpassed by 7.4 points in average F1 on EgoTV (87.4 vs. 80.0) and 7.45 points on CSV-NL (68.49 vs. 61.04), with the most dramatic gap on Novel Steps (82.4 vs. 64.7 on EgoTV; 67.59 vs. 60.35 on CSV-NL), indicating that the Cross-Modal Path Finding module is critical for handling shuffled or omitted operations. Among MLLM baselines, Otter [51] is the strongest (60.0% avg on EgoTV, 48.99% on CSV-NL), yet still trails PHGC by 27.4 and 19.5 points respectively, confirming that general-purpose multimodal LLMs struggle with fine-grained procedural verification. Foundation-model baselines (CLIP, CoCa, VideoCLIP) cluster around 49–60% on EgoTV, while video-specific methods like VIOLIN [52] (64.0%) and CLIP4Clip [56] (55.4%) are intermediate. On the realistic CSV-NL dataset, MLLMs maintain relatively stronger performance (PandaGPT 57.41%, GoldFish 58.92%) compared to their EgoTV results, but PHGC still leads all methods, suggesting that heterogeneous graph completion provides robustness to real-world variability that pre-trained MLLMs partially capture but do not fully exploit for structured procedural reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NLETV task and EgoTV benchmark introduced by Rishi et al. (2023), which first formulated natural language-based procedural task verification for egocentric videos. It departs from the video-reference paradigm of earlier procedural verification works (SVIP, D3TW, collaborative weakly supervised learning) by representing both the textual rules and visual operation flows as a heterogeneous graph, then framing verification as a graph completion problem. The heterogeneous graph modeling draws on established techniques in multimodal video understanding (temporal action detection, video QA, moment retrieval), but the specific combination of hierarchical vertex types, dynamic-programming-based cross-modal edge selection, and graph-attention-based discriminative entity aggregation is novel to this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PHGC approach provides a structured, interpretable framework for cross-modal procedural verification that outperforms both prior NLETV methods and multimodal LLMs on both synthetic and realistic benchmarks, and the CSV-NL dataset fills a critical gap by enabling evaluation on physical-world egocentric videos. However, the performance gap between synthetic (EgoTV) and realistic (CSV-NL) settings remains substantial, and the method's reliance on CLIP features and a one-to-one text-to-video matching assumption limits its applicability to longer or more complex procedural tasks. Open questions include whether the graph completion paradigm scales to multi-minute videos, whether the dynamic programming edge-cutting generalizes beyond the at-most-one-match assumption, and how the approach performs in real-time agent deployment scenarios.
