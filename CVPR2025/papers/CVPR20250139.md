## Problem

Text prompts in video generation cannot precisely control spatial layout and geometric details, while image-based conditioning is cumbersome for interactive editing and motion control. Using sparse keyframe sketches (one or two frames at arbitrary time points) as a natural interaction interface introduces unique challenges: propagating temporally sparse conditions across all frames, maintaining memory efficiency in DiT-based architectures where copying half the base model as a control encoder causes out-of-memory errors, and training with limited paired video-sketch data. For video editing, existing methods preserve original temporal motion and thus fail at shape manipulation and dynamic object insertion, while also struggling to accurately preserve unedited regions.

## Input / Output

For generation, the method takes a text prompt and one or two hand-drawn keyframe sketches at user-specified arbitrary time points (not restricted to first/last frames) and produces a ~6-second video clip. For editing, it additionally accepts an original video and per-frame binary masks delineating regions to modify, producing a locally edited video where unedited regions are preserved. The VAE performs 8×8 spatial and 4× temporal downsampling, and sketches are encoded through the same pretrained VAE before patchifying and time-aware positional embedding.

## Method

Built on CogVideoX-2b's 30-block DiT, the method introduces a memory-efficient sketch condition network with only 5 control blocks in a skip residual structure, predicting residual features for blocks 0, 6, 12, 18, and 24 rather than copying half the base model. A key innovation is the inter-frame attention mechanism that propagates sparse sketch conditions: Q is computed from all frames' noisy hidden features, K from the control keyframe hidden features, and V from the sketch-derived features, leveraging inter-frame similarity to spatially and temporally distribute sketch information without white placeholders. For editing, a video insertion module processes the masked original video through a trainable DiT copy, and its output is concatenated with the sketch branch output (each multiplied by their respective masks) before a feed-forward layer. At inference, latent fusion via DDIM inversion at steps 25 and 49 replaces unedited-region latents with inversion codes to preserve fine details. Training uses a two-stage hybrid strategy (image+video, then video-only) for generation, and the editing network is fine-tuned from the pretrained generation model with self-supervised inpainting using random masks.

## Results

On sketch-based video generation (200 examples from OpenVid-1M), SketchVideo achieves the lowest LPIPS (27.56) and highest CLIP similarity (98.31), outperforming AMT (29.17 / 96.12), SparseCtrl (44.85 / 96.48), and Ctrl-CogVideo (32.23 / 98.04). In the user study, SketchVideo ranks first in Fidelity (1.21), Consistency (1.08), and Realism (1.11), compared to the next-best Ctrl-CogVideo (2.86 / 2.47 / 2.50). For sketch-based video editing, SketchVideo achieves LPIPS 9.74, CLIP 98.34, and PSNR 36.48, substantially surpassing InsV2V (13.61 / 95.39 / 16.84) and AnyV2V (11.92 / 93.47 / 13.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SketchVideo outperforms all four baselines across every metric in both generation and editing. Against the interpolation baseline AMT [37] (ControlNet sketch-to-image + frame interpolation), SketchVideo reduces LPIPS from 29.17 to 27.56 and improves CLIP from 96.12 to 98.31, while the user study shows a large gap in realism (1.11 vs 3.57). Against SparseCtrl [22], which fills missing sketch frames with white placeholders, SketchVideo achieves a dramatically lower LPIPS (27.56 vs 44.85), indicating far better sketch fidelity; the paper attributes SparseCtrl's weakness to temporal flickering and the network processing both sketches and white images simultaneously. The closest competitor, Ctrl-CogVideo (SparseCtrl extended to CogVideoX-2b with a PIXART-δ-style 5-block encoder), still trails SketchVideo on LPIPS (32.23 vs 27.56) and all three user-study criteria, with the gap attributed to CogVideoX's pretrained self-attention being designed for dense rather than sparse inputs. For editing, SketchVideo dominates InsV2V [17] (text-based) and AnyV2V [34] (first-frame propagation) on LPIPS (9.74 vs 13.61 / 11.92), CLIP (98.34 vs 95.39 / 93.47), and PSNR (36.48 vs 16.84 / 13.68), with the PSNR gap reflecting superior preservation of unedited regions via latent fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT-based video generation paradigm (CogVideoX-2b) and the residual-feature control strategy from ControlNet and PIXART-δ, but departs from both in key ways. Rather than replicating half the base model as a condition encoder (as in ControlNet/PIXART-δ) or filling missing frames with white placeholders (as in SparseCtrl), it introduces a 5-block skip residual structure distributed across the 30-block DiT and an inter-frame attention mechanism that derives Q and K from the noisy video latents while sourcing V from sketch features. The editing component further extends the generation framework with a video insertion module and DDIM-inversion-based latent fusion, moving beyond prior text- or first-frame-based editing methods that cannot handle shape manipulation or object insertion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SketchVideo provides a unified, memory-efficient framework for both sketch-conditioned video generation and fine-grained video editing, addressing a practical gap where text prompts alone cannot control geometry and image-based editing cannot introduce new content with coherent motion. The skip residual structure (5 blocks vs. 15) and inter-frame attention offer a template for adding sparse spatial conditions to DiT-based video models without prohibitive memory cost. However, the method remains bounded by the pretrained CogVideoX-2b quality, is limited to ~6-second clips, and struggles with complex scenarios like human hands and multi-object interaction; incorporating 3D priors and scaling to longer videos are natural next steps.
