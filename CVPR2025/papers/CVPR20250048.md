## Problem

Vanilla visual networks exhibit an implicit bias toward learning texture and appearance cues, causing them to fail at localizing novel objects with unseen textures in open-world instance segmentation. Existing open-world methods (e.g., SWORD, LDET) do not explicitly enforce appearance-invariant representation learning, leaving models overfit to the limited visual appearance of known training classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single natural RGB image and produces class-agnostic bounding boxes and instance segmentation masks for all detected objects, without predicting any class label. During training, each sample is randomly presented as one of three views—natural image, colorized depth map, or an auxiliary view (stylized or edge map)—with random cropping and patch integration to further destroy appearance. The model is built on DINO-DETR or Deformable-DETR with dynamic convolution heads, using 1000–1500 decoder queries to predict per-instance prototypes that are matched against pyramid encoder features to yield segmentation masks.

## Method

v-CLR introduces a two-branch view-consistent training framework: a natural-image branch (updated via EMA as a teacher) and a transformed-image branch (student) that randomly receives one of the appearance-altered views. Off-the-shelf CutLER object proposals serve as a matching anchor, ensuring that the queries being aligned across views are genuinely object-oriented rather than capturing spurious shortcuts. A cosine-similarity loss (L_sim) enforces feature consistency between matched query pairs from the two branches, while a standard segmentation loss (L_obj) supervised by the proposals and a ground-truth loss (L_gt) jointly train the student. The key insight is that by destroying texture through depth/stylization/edge transformations while preserving geometric structure, and by constraining consistency only at object-proposal locations, the model is forced to learn transferable, appearance-invariant object representations that generalize to unseen classes.

## Results

In the primary VOC → Non-VOC setting, v-CLR(DINO) achieves AR₁₀₀ᵇ = 40.9 and AR₁₀₀ᵐ = 34.1, outperforming the DINO-DETR baseline (31.1 / 22.0) by 9.8 and 12.1 points respectively. Across cross-dataset settings, v-CLR consistently leads: VOC → UVO (AR₁₀₀ᵇ = 47.2 vs. SWORD's 43.1), COCO → LVIS (AR₁₀₀ᵇ = 28.4 vs. SWORD's 23.5), and COCO → Objects365 (AR₁₀₀ᵇ = 47.9 vs. SWORD's 43.9). The method also improves over the strongest prior open-world method SWORD by 3.2–5.6 points in AR₁₀₀ᵇ across all four evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

v-CLR outperforms all baselines across every reported metric in the VOC → Non-VOC setting. Against the strongest prior open-world method SWORD [61], v-CLR(DINO) gains +5.6 in AR₁₀₀ᵇ (40.9 vs. 35.3) and +3.9 in AR₁₀₀ᵐ (34.1 vs. 30.2). Over its own DINO-DETR backbone baseline, the improvement is +9.8 in AR₁₀₀ᵇ and +12.1 in AR₁₀₀ᵐ, demonstrating the value of the view-consistent training objective. Compared to the unsupervised CutLER detector [57] (AR₁₀₀ᵇ = 34.5), v-CLR achieves +6.4 in AR₁₀₀ᵇ while also producing segmentation masks. In the cross-dataset VOC → UVO setting, v-CLR(DINO) surpasses SWORD by +4.1 in AR₁₀₀ᵇ (47.2 vs. 43.1) and +1.0 in AR₁₀₀ᵐ. On COCO → LVIS, v-CLR(DINO) leads SWORD by +4.9 in AR₁₀₀ᵇ (28.4 vs. 23.5) and +3.2 in AR₁₀₀ᵐ. The only notable weakness is on small objects in COCO → Objects365, where v-CLR(DINO) scores ARₛᵇ = 26.2 versus DINO-DETR's 28.8, attributed to CutLER's inherent bias toward larger objects in its proposals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DETR-based instance segmentation architectures (DINO-DETR, Deformable-DETR) and the MaskDINO segmentation head, but departs from prior open-world instance segmentation methods (OLN, LDET, SWORD, GGN, UDOS) that focus on discovery mechanisms like localization-aware scores, copy-paste synthesis, or geometric cues. Instead, v-CLR identifies the root cause of poor open-world generalization as the well-documented texture bias in neural networks and addresses it through a self-supervised-style view-consistency objective, borrowing the EMA teacher-student paradigm from contrastive learning. The use of CutLER's unsupervised object proposals as a matching anchor is a key design choice that distinguishes this work from naive cross-view consistency, ensuring the learned representations remain object-oriented rather than collapsing to trivial solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing open-world instance segmentation as an appearance-invariance problem rather than a discovery problem, this paper offers a modular training framework that can be plugged into any DETR-based architecture, potentially benefiting a wide range of downstream detectors. The consistent 2–4% AR improvements across four cross-category and cross-dataset benchmarks validate the approach, though the reliance on an off-the-shelf unsupervised detector (CutLER) for proposals introduces a dependency that limits full end-to-end training. A notable limitation is the unstable performance on small objects, attributed to CutLER's inherent size bias, suggesting that the object-proposal component needs refinement for fine-grained scenes. Open questions include whether the method extends to video or 3D settings, whether the specific transformation choices (depth vs. stylized vs. edge) can be learned rather than hand-picked, and whether the EMA consistency objective can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives.
