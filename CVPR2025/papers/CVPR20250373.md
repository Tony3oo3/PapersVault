## Problem

Current LVLM-based visual grounding methods require explicit fine-tuning and additional model components (e.g., [SEG] tokens, mask decoders) to generate bounding boxes or segmentation masks, despite LVLMs inherently understanding text-image relationships. The paper asks whether this grounding capability can be directly observed and exploited from frozen LVLMs without any training, by examining how text tokens attend to image regions within the attention mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250373/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a free-form referring expression as input, processed through a frozen LVLM's vision encoder, projector, and LLM backbone. The key internal signal extracted is the text-to-image attention vector from the last text token's query attending to all P² image patch tokens at specific (layer, head) positions. For REC, the output is a bounding box derived from the largest rectangle enclosing the binarized combined attention map; for RES, this bounding box serves as a prompt to SAM to produce a pixel-level segmentation mask.

## Method

The paper identifies a small subset of attention heads—called localization heads—using two criteria: (1) attention sum, which selects heads that dominantly attend to image tokens over text tokens, and (2) spatial entropy, which selects heads whose attention concentrates on spatially coherent clusters rather than being diffuse. Across 1,000 RefCOCO samples, heads are ranked by selection frequency (how often they appear in the top-10 lowest spatial entropy among high-attention-sum heads), and the top-k=3 heads are fixed for inference. At test time, the text-to-image attention maps from these k heads are Gaussian-smoothed, summed element-wise, binarized, and the largest enclosing rectangle yields the bounding box. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring no fine-tuning, no additional parameters, and no task-specific datasets, yet achieves performance comparable to fine-tuned LVLMs like LISA and Ferret across ten different LVLMs ranging from 1.3B to 13B parameters.

## Results

On the REC task (Acc@0.5), the best configuration (LLaVA-1.5-13B, k=3 localization heads) achieves 87.2/90.0/83.3 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 82.7/88.5/74.0 on RefCOCO+, and 84.3/85.5 on RefCOCOg, all without any fine-tuning. On the RES task (cIoU), the same model reaches 76.1/78.9/72.8 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB. On the more challenging ReasonSeg benchmark, LLaVA-1.5-13B achieves 60.5 overall cIoU, matching the fine-tuned LISA-13B (60.3). Only 3 out of thousands of attention heads are needed to achieve these results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The training-free LVLM approach dramatically outperforms all existing training-free baselines: LLaVA-1.5-13B achieves 87.2 on RefCOCO val vs. 65.0 for GroundVLP (best prior training-free), 49.4 for Han et al., and 45.8 for ReCLIP — a margin of over 20 points. Against fine-tuned LVLMs sharing the same LLaVA-1.5 backbone, the method is competitive: LLaVA-1.5-13B (87.2/90.0/83.3) vs. Shikra-13B (87.8/91.1/81.8) and Ferret-13B (89.5/92.4/84.4), trailing by 1–2 points on testA/testB but leading on testB. On the RES task, LLaVA-1.5-13B (76.1/78.9/72.8 cIoU) matches or slightly exceeds fine-tuned LISA-7B (74.1/76.5/71.1) and approaches GSVA-13B (77.7/79.9/74.2). On ReasonSeg, the method (60.5 overall) ties LISA-13B (60.3) despite requiring no fine-tuning. The gap to the strongest fine-tuned non-LVLM methods (ONE-PEACE at 92.6, UNINEXT at 92.6 on RefCOCO val) remains around 5 points, indicating that dedicated grounding architectures still hold an edge at the top end.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of LVLM-based visual grounding, which requires fine-tuning with grounding-specific datasets and adding specialized components (e.g., [SEG] tokens in LISA, mask decoders in GLaMM). It also moves beyond existing training-free approaches that rely on CLIP similarity scores or diffusion model attention residues. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the multi-head self-attention mechanism of frozen LVLMs—drawing on attention interpretability work from ViTs and diffusion models—and introduces two selection criteria (attention sum and spatial entropy) to identify a small subset of 'localization heads' that inherently encode text-to-region correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that only three attention heads out of thousands in a frozen LVLM can achieve grounding performance comparable to fine-tuned methods (e.g., LISA) challenges the assumption that explicit grounding training is necessary, suggesting LVLMs develop innate spatial grounding as a byproduct of text-image alignment. This opens a training-free pathway for grounding that avoids the cost and data requirements of fine-tuning. However, a notable performance gap remains versus the best fine-tuned methods (e.g., 87.2 vs. 92.8 on RefCOCO val REC), and the failure analysis reveals persistent difficulties with ordinal and fine-grained spatial reasoning, leaving open whether localization heads can be further refined or combined with other signals to close this gap.
