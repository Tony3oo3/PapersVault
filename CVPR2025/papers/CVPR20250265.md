## Problem

Directly feeding retrieved external knowledge into MLLMs for knowledge-based VQA introduces noise and redundancy that misdirects reasoning, while relying solely on the MLLM's implicit knowledge fails to retrieve the correct facts during inference. Additionally, MLLMs lack fine-grained visual perception, causing hallucinations on details like traffic light colors or small object attributes. No prior method simultaneously addresses both the knowledge-noise problem and the visual-hallucination problem in a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, a text question, and top-k retrieved knowledge passages (k=5, sourced from Google Search Corpus for OK-VQA or Wikipedia for A-OKVQA) via the PreFLMR retriever. The frozen MLLM (LLaVA-NeXT-8B or Qwen2-VL-7B) receives the original image, a cropped visual-notes image highlighting task-relevant regions, the question, and generated knowledge notes as a single multimodal prompt. Output is a single text answer selected from a set of 3 candidate answers generated in a first pass, with the MLLM performing a second-pass selection.

## Method

NoteMR is a three-step zero-shot pipeline over a frozen MLLM. First, retrieved explicit knowledge passages and the image are jointly fed to the MLLM as a prompt, causing the model to filter noisy passages and activate its own implicit knowledge, producing concise knowledge notes that integrate both sources. Second, multi-head cross-modal attention is computed between tokenized knowledge notes (query) and BLIP-encoded image patches (key/value); attention scores above a threshold (λ=0.6) form a binary mask that crops the original image into visual notes retaining only task-relevant regions. Third, the original image, visual notes, question, and knowledge notes are fed to the MLLM to generate 3 candidate answers, which are then re-presented to the MLLM for final selection. The key insight is using explicit knowledge as a retrieval cue to unlock the correct implicit knowledge rather than as direct input, and using knowledge-guided attention to compensate for the MLLM's coarse visual encoder.

## Results

On OK-VQA, NoteMR with LLaVA-NeXT-8b achieves 70.0% VQA score, surpassing the strongest baseline LLaVA-13B (64.7%) by 5.3 points and the same-size LLaVA-NeXT-8b baseline (62.2%) by 7.8 points. On A-OKVQA, NoteMR reaches 68.7% on Direct Answer (outperforming SKP's 65.3% by 3.4 points) and 88.1% on Multiple Choice (outperforming Prophet's 76.4% by 11.7 points). The method also improves Qwen2-VL-7b from 58.8% to 64.8% on OK-VQA, demonstrating cross-architecture robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoteMR outperforms all 25 baselines on both OK-VQA and A-OKVQA. On OK-VQA, the 7.8-point gain over the LLaVA-NeXT-8b baseline (62.2% → 70.0%) demonstrates that the knowledge-notes and visual-notes pipeline adds substantial value without any fine-tuning. Against the strongest retrieval-based method RA-VQA-v2 (62.1%, T5-XL 3B), NoteMR with a smaller 8B MLLM leads by 7.9 points. Among LLM-based methods, NoteMR surpasses SKP (63.3%) by 6.7 points and LLaVA-13B (64.7%) by 5.3 points, showing that a well-guided smaller model can beat a larger unguided one. On A-OKVQA Direct Answer, NoteMR (68.7%) beats SKP (65.3%) by 3.4 points; on Multiple Choice, it leads Prophet (76.4%) by 11.7 points. The ablation confirms each component contributes: raw retrieved knowledge adds +3.1 points, knowledge notes add another +3.5, visual notes add +0.8, and candidate outputs add +0.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoteMR carves a new middle path between two established lines of KB-VQA work: retrieval-based methods (RA-VQA, TRiG, KAT) that feed explicit knowledge directly into a generator, and LLM-based methods (Prophet, SKP, PromptCap) that rely on the model's implicit knowledge. Rather than treating retrieved passages as direct input or merely re-weighting their tokens (as SKP does), NoteMR uses them as prompts to a frozen MLLM, eliciting summarized 'knowledge notes' that fuse explicit and implicit knowledge. The visual notes component repurposes cross-modal attention (via GradCAM-style scoring between knowledge-note tokens and image patches) as a region-selection mechanism, a technique borrowed from explainability literature but applied here as an inference-time perceptual aid rather than a post-hoc explanation tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a frozen 8B MLLM augmented with notes (70.0% on OK-VQA) surpasses a 13B model used without them (64.7%), suggesting that structured knowledge guidance can be more effective than raw parameter scaling for knowledge-intensive VQA. This is appealing for practitioners constrained by compute, since the approach requires no fine-tuning and only ~43 GB of VRAM. Open questions include whether the fixed attention threshold (λ=0.6) and top-k retrieval depth generalize across domains, whether the candidate-output selection step (which adds inference cost) is necessary at scale, and whether the notes paradigm extends naturally to video, multi-image, or long-document VQA settings where the current patch-level visual notes may be insufficient.
