## Problem

Existing fast feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (e.g., TripoSR, LRM) produce meshes that are not directly usable in graphics pipelines due to four key issues: illumination effects baked into textures, reliance on high-polygon vertex coloring instead of UV-mapped textures, stair-stepping artifacts from Marching Cubes, and absence of material properties. These defects require laborious manual post-processing before the assets can be deployed in games, AR/VR, or e-commerce applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a UV-unwrapped, textured 3D mesh packaged as a GLB file under 1 MB, generated in 0.3 s on an H100 GPU. The output includes an albedo texture, a tangent-space normal map, and homogeneous (non-spatially-varying) metallic and roughness PBR parameters. The mesh is intentionally low-polygon but smooth, with surface detail encoded in the UV textures and learned vertex displacements rather than in high vertex counts.

## Method

SF3D builds on TripoSR's transformer-to-triplane pipeline but introduces five key modules: (1) an enhanced transformer using DINOv2 image tokens and a PointInfinity-inspired architecture that outputs 384×384 triplanes (vs. 64×64) to reduce aliasing; (2) a Material Net (frozen CLIP encoder + MLPs) that predicts homogeneous metallic/roughness via Beta-distribution parameters to avoid training collapse; (3) a Light Net (CNN + MLP) that estimates 24 Spherical Gaussian illumination amplitudes from the triplanes, enabling a differentiable PBR shading model and a demodulation loss for illumination disentanglement; (4) DMTet-based mesh extraction augmented with learned vertex offsets and world-space normals (stabilized via slerp) to eliminate stair-stepping; and (5) a parallelizable cube-projection UV unwrapping pipeline with 2D occlusion resolution that completes in ~10 ms. Training proceeds in two phases—NeRF pre-training followed by mesh-based fine-tuning with SG shading—across three resolution stages (128→256→512) with combined rendering, mesh-regularization, and shading losses.

## Results

SF3D achieves state-of-the-art performance on both GSO and OmniObject3D, attaining the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.096 on GSO, 0.089 on OmniObject3D) and highest F-scores (FS@0.1: 0.710/0.731, FS@0.2: 0.897/0.922, FS@0.5: 0.988/0.990) among all compared methods. On image quality metrics, SF3D leads with PSNR of 17.619 (GSO) and 15.136 (OmniObject3D), SSIM of 0.821/0.785, and LPIPS of 0.152/0.209. Despite using fewer polygons than all baselines, SF3D matches or exceeds their geometric accuracy thanks to learned vertex offsets and high-resolution triplane supervision. The method generates a full UV-unwrapped textured mesh in 0.3 s on an H100 GPU, tying with TripoSR for the fastest inference among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF3D outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric across both GSO and OmniObject3D. The closest competitor is TripoSR (same 0.3 s runtime), which SF3D surpasses by 13.5% in CD on GSO (0.096 vs. 0.111), 10% in FS@0.1 (0.710 vs. 0.645), and 1.17 dB in PSNR (17.619 vs. 16.445). Among slower methods, LGM (64.6 s) and 3DTopia-XL (38.3 s) show the weakest geometric accuracy (CD 0.195–0.201 on GSO), while CRM (10.2 s) and LN3Diff (5.0 s) occupy the middle range. OpenLRM (2.0 s) and InstantMesh (32.4 s) are intermediate in both speed and quality. Notably, SF3D achieves these gains despite using substantially fewer polygons than all baselines, which the authors attribute to learned vertex offsets and high-resolution triplane supervision compensating for the lower mesh complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SF3D builds directly on TripoSR's feed-forward transformer-to-triplane architecture and DMTet for mesh extraction, but departs from the broader feed-forward 3D reconstruction line (LRM, OpenLRM, InstantMesh) by addressing practical usability gaps: it replaces vertex colors with a fast box-projection UV unwrapping pipeline, introduces explicit Spherical Gaussian illumination estimation for delighting, and predicts PBR material properties (metallic, roughness) via a CLIP-conditioned network. The higher-resolution triplane design draws on PointInfinity's linear-complexity attention to avoid quadratic scaling, and the mesh refinement (vertex offsets + learned normals) follows MeshLRM's split-decoder approach. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as an engineering integration of several known components (SG shading, DMTet, probabilistic regression) into a single fast pipeline rather than proposing a fundamentally new representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: producing a production-ready, UV-unwrapped, material-tagged, de-lit GLB mesh in 0.3 s removes the last manual post-processing steps that made prior fast-reconstruction outputs unusable in game engines and AR/VR pipelines. Key limitations remain—materials are homogeneous (single metallic/roughness per object), LDR inputs lose shadow information, and neither material nor illumination estimation has explicit supervision—leaving open the question of whether spatially-varying PBR properties and real-world training data can be incorporated without sacrificing speed. The fast box-projection UV unwrapping (0.5 s vs. 10–30 s for xatlas/geogram) is a useful technique that could be adopted independently by other 3D generation pipelines.
