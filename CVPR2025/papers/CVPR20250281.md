## Problem

Pairwise relative pose estimation from images with little or no visual overlap remains an open challenge, as both classical feature-matching methods and learned pose estimators like DUSt3R and MASt3R rely on identifiable correspondences that become unavailable under extreme viewpoint changes. This paper asks whether the rich spatial priors encoded in large-scale generative video models—trained on orders of magnitude more data than 3D datasets—can be leveraged to bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (I_A, I_B) with 50°–90° yaw separation and no guaranteed overlap, along with GPT-4o-generated text captions describing the shared scene. A video generative model (DynamiCrafter, Runway Gen-3 Alpha Turbo, or Luma Dream Machine) produces intermediate frames at resolutions ranging from 320×512 (DynamiCrafter, 16 frames) to ~1 MP (Runway/Luma, 112–114 frames). The pose estimator (DUSt3R or MASt3R multi-view extension) consumes 5 frames per sample (2 original + 3 generated) and outputs the relative 6-DoF camera pose (rotation and translation) from I_A to I_B.

## Method

InterPose generates n=4 interpolated videos per image pair by combining two GPT-4o captions with both input orderings (A→B and B→A) to counteract a left-to-right panning bias in video models. For each video, m=11 subsets of k=5 frames are sampled (always including both original images plus 3 generated frames), and a black-box pose estimator computes a relative pose for each subset. A self-consistency score based on medoid distance measures how tightly the m pose estimates cluster: a geometrically consistent video yields nearly identical poses regardless of which generated frames are sampled, while an artifact-laden video produces scattered estimates. A bias term penalizes medoids that deviate from the pose estimated from the original pair alone, guarding against degenerate videos that are consistently wrong. The video with the lowest total score is selected, and its medoid pose is output as the final prediction.

## Results

On the challenging outward-facing Cambridge Landmarks dataset (50°–65° yaw change, minimal overlap), InterPose with DUSt3R and Runway reduces mean rotation error from 13.28° to 10.78° and improves Racc@30° from 88.97% to 94.14%. On ScanNet, the same setup (DUSt3R + Dream Machine) reduces MRE from 21.31° to 17.65° and MTE from 24.72° to 15.88°, while raising AUC30° from 60.34% to 63.06%. On center-facing datasets (DL3DV-10K, NAVI) where baselines already perform well, improvements are smaller but consistent: e.g., on NAVI, MASt3R+Runway reduces MRE from 5.59° to 5.28° and MTE from 5.23° to 5.20°. The method generalizes across three video models (DynamiCrafter, Runway Gen-3 Alpha Turbo, Luma Dream Machine) with consistent gains over the pair-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIFT+NN and LOFTR, both feature-matching-based methods, are dramatically outperformed by all deep pose estimators on every dataset, confirming that explicit correspondences fail under large viewpoint changes with little overlap (e.g., SIFT achieves 97.64° MRE on Cambridge Landmarks vs. 10.78° for InterPose+DUSt3R+Runway). DUSt3R is the primary baseline: InterPose consistently reduces MRE by 1–4° and improves Racc@30° by 3–6 points across all four datasets and all three video models, with the largest gains on outward-facing scenes (Cambridge Landmarks, ScanNet) where overlap is minimal. MASt3R outperforms DUSt3R on center-facing datasets (DL3DV-10K, NAVI) due to its local feature matching, but InterPose still improves over MASt3R on outward-facing ScanNet (MRE 24.35°→19.91°, AUC30° 55.10%→58.28% with Dream Machine) and achieves comparable or slightly better results on NAVI (MRE 5.59°→5.28° with Runway). On DL3DV-10K, where MASt3R already achieves near-perfect accuracy (MRE 4.13°), InterPose+MASt3R+Runway is essentially tied (MRE 4.17°), demonstrating that the video frames do not degrade performance when the baseline is already strong.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of generative video models and geometric 3D vision, a combination not previously explored. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes off-the-shelf video interpolation models (DynamiCrafter, Runway, Luma Dream Machine) as implicit world priors that hallucinate intermediate frames to bridge two images with little or no overlap, then feeds those frames into existing pose estimators (DUSt3R, MASt3R). The self-consistency scoring via medoid distance is a new selection mechanism, but the overall pipeline is a straightforward composition of existing components. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first demonstration that a generative video model can serve as a world prior to improve pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that video models trained on web-scale data encode richer scene priors than 3D-trained models, and that these priors can be extracted via hallucinated intermediate frames—opens a potentially broad avenue for leveraging generative models in 3D understanding tasks beyond pose estimation. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the method is expensive (~$5,500 for a single evaluation run), slow, and the improvements over strong baselines like DUSt3R are moderate (e.g., ~2–4° MRE reduction on the hardest datasets). Key open questions include whether better video selection strategies (the oracle gap is large) can close the remaining gap, whether multi-view consistency in generated videos can be improved, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex or dynamic scenes. The authors themselves frame this as a preliminary investigation with significant room for improvement.
