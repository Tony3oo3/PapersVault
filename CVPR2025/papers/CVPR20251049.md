## Problem

Existing text-to-HOI generation methods rely on diffusion models that are limited to short sequences, support only text conditioning, and cannot perform bidirectional tasks like HOI captioning or motion completion. While LLM-based approaches have shown promise for human body motion, HOI involves up to three interacting entities (two hands and an object) with diverse object geometries, making naive single-codebook tokenization combinatorially intractable and physically implausible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251049/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes text prompts, 6DoF object poses (translation, 6D rotation, articulation angle), MANO-parameterized hand poses (global 3D location + 6D rotation), and canonical object point clouds as geometric conditioning. HOI sequences are tokenized in time windows, with each window producing one left-hand token, one right-hand token, and one object token from separate codebooks of size 512. Outputs include full 3D HOI motion sequences, natural language descriptions, and completed/predicted HOI segments, all generated autoregressively from the same Flan-T5 backbone via different instruction prompts.

## Method

HOIGPT introduces an HOI-decomposed VQ-VAE that factorizes the motion codebook into separate hand and object codebooks, avoiding the combinatorial explosion of jointly encoding three semi-independent entities. The encoder uses PointNet for object shape features and independent hand/object motion extractors, while the decoder conditions hand motion on predicted object motion to enforce spatial consistency. Three geometric losses (penetration, contact grasping, and contact region) are integrated into tokenizer training to ensure physical plausibility without post-processing. The Flan-T5 (220M) language model is trained in two stages: masked-token pre-training on paired motion-text data, followed by instruction tuning on multi-task prompts covering generation, captioning, prediction, and completion.

## Results

On the combined ARCTIC+GRAB benchmark for text-to-HOI generation, HOIGPT achieves a FID of 3.29, substantially outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline Text2HOI (5.85) and all LLM-based baselines (8.69–9.51). HOIGPT also leads on R Precision (Top-3) at 74.80% (vs. 72.34% for Text2HOI), MMDist at 4.71 (vs. 5.24), and interpenetration volume (IV) at 12.1 (vs. 12.3). On the HOI-to-text task, HOIGPT attains 48.43% Top-3 R Precision and 7.55 MMDist, surpassing MotionGPT (46.88%, 8.83) and TM2T (45.67%, 9.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOIGPT outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric for text-to-HOI generation. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor, Text2HOI, HOIGPT reduces FID by 2.56 points (3.29 vs. 5.85), improves R Precision by 2.46 points (74.80 vs. 72.34), and lowers MMDist from 5.24 to 4.71, while also achieving far higher MModality (4.75 vs. 1.66), indicating much greater output diversity for the same prompt. Against the LLM-based baselines (T2MGPT, MotionGPT, TM2T), HOIGPT's advantage is even more pronounced: FID drops from 8.69–9.51 to 3.29, and R Precision rises by 5–11 points. On the HOI-to-text task, HOIGPT leads MotionGPT by 1.55 points in Top-3 R Precision (48.43 vs. 46.88) and by 1.28 in MMDist (7.55 vs. 8.83). For HOI prediction and interpolation, HOIGPT halves the FID of MotionGPT (4.08 vs. 8.23 for prediction; 2.91 vs. 6.16 for interpolation) and reduces ADE by roughly 19% and 20% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOIGPT extends the tokenize-then-LLM paradigm established by MotionGPT and T2MGPT from single-entity human body motion to the multi-entity hand-object interaction setting. The key architectural departure is the HOI-decomposed VQ-VAE, which factorizes the codebook into separate hand and object components to avoid the combinatorial explosion that a joint codebook would face, and introduces geometric losses (penetration, contact, contact-region) directly into the tokenizer training rather than relying on post-hoc refinement as diffusion-based HOI methods (Text2HOI, DiffH2O) do. The masking strategy from MAE is repurposed to encourage cross-entity correlation learning within the decomposed latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified bidirectional model for HOI that handles text-to-motion, motion-to-text, prediction, and interpolation within a single autoregressive framework, addressing the long-sequence coherence limitation of diffusion-based HOI generators. However, the evaluation is conducted on a relatively small combined dataset (~5.6k training sequences from ARCTIC and GRAB) with a compact 220M-parameter Flan-T5 Base, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse object vocabularies and longer multi-action sequences. The decomposed codebook design and geometric-aware tokenization are likely transferable to other multi-entity motion domains (e.g., full-body HOI, multi-agent interaction), but this remains untested.
