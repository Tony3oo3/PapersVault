## Problem

Traditional monocular SLAM systems assume static scenes and fail when dynamic objects introduce photometric inconsistencies. Existing dynamic SLAM approaches rely on semantic segmentation, object detection priors, or depth sensors to identify moving regions, limiting generalizability to unknown distractors. No prior method achieves robust monocular SLAM in dynamic environments using purely geometric cues without class-specific priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252754/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB video sequence (downsampled to 360×480 or 360×640 for efficiency) and produces a camera trajectory, a 3D Gaussian map of the static scene, and per-pixel uncertainty maps at 1/14 resolution (from DINOv2 feature maps). Metric depth from Metric3D v2 is used as an auxiliary signal during both tracking and mapping. The 3D Gaussian map supports real-time novel view synthesis rendering of the static scene with dynamic distractors removed.

## Method

WildGS-SLAM builds a 3D Gaussian Splatting map of the static scene while using a shallow MLP, trained online on 3D-aware DINOv2 features, to predict per-pixel uncertainty that identifies dynamic distractors. During tracking, the uncertainty map weights the Mahalanobis distance in a dense bundle adjustment (based on DROID-SLAM), down-weighting pixels associated with moving objects, while a metric depth regularization term stabilizes pose estimation before the uncertainty MLP converges. During mapping, the same uncertainty map weights the color and depth rendering losses in the 3DGS optimization, preventing dynamic content from corrupting the Gaussian map. The uncertainty MLP and the 3D Gaussian map are optimized independently with detached gradient flows, and the uncertainty loss combines a modified SSIM term, an L1 depth loss against Metric3D v2 predictions, and variance/upper-bound regularization terms inherited from NeRF On-the-go.

## Results

On the newly introduced Wild-SLAM MoCap dataset, WildGS-SLAM achieves an average ATE RMSE of 0.46 cm, substantially outperforming the next-best method MegaSaM (2.40 cm) and Splat-SLAM (8.71 cm). On the Bonn RGB-D Dynamic Dataset, it achieves 2.31 cm average ATE RMSE, beating DDN-SLAM (2.91 cm) and MegaSaM (3.51 cm). On TUM RGB-D, it ties MegaSaM at 1.63 cm average. For novel view synthesis on Wild-SLAM MoCap, WildGS-SLAM achieves 20.59 PSNR, 0.783 SSIM, and 0.209 LPIPS, compared to Splat-SLAM's 17.23 PSNR, 0.699 SSIM, and 0.346 LPIPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildGS-SLAM outperforms all 14 baselines on the Wild-SLAM MoCap dataset, achieving 0.46 cm average ATE RMSE versus MegaSaM's 2.40 cm (5.2× improvement) and Splat-SLAM's 8.71 cm (19× improvement). Among RGB-D methods, DynaSLAM (N+G) is the closest competitor at 7.84 cm, while ReFusion and NICE-SLAM fail or produce errors exceeding 37 cm on average. On Bonn RGB-D, WildGS-SLAM (2.31 cm) beats DDN-SLAM (2.91 cm) and MegaSaM (3.51 cm), with DynaMoN (MS&SS) at 4.10 cm and DROID-SLAM at 4.91 cm. On TUM RGB-D, WildGS-SLAM ties MegaSaM at 1.63 cm average, both substantially ahead of DynaMoN (MS&SS) at 1.98 cm and DROID-SLAM at 2.25 cm. The only sequence where WildGS-SLAM is not best is 'Person' on Wild-SLAM MoCap (0.8 cm vs. DynaSLAM RGB's 0.4 cm), where a single person moving in a simple pattern is easily masked by semantic segmentation. For novel view synthesis, WildGS-SLAM achieves 3.36 dB higher PSNR and 0.084 higher SSIM than Splat-SLAM, confirming that uncertainty-aware mapping yields artifact-free renderings in dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

WildGS-SLAM extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM line (MonoGS, Splat-SLAM) by integrating uncertainty-aware dynamic object removal, drawing conceptually from NeRF On-the-Go and WildGaussians, which demonstrated that a learned uncertainty map can suppress distractors in sparse-view rendering. The key departure is moving this purely geometric uncertainty mechanism from offline, pose-known settings into an online sequential SLAM pipeline built on DROID-SLAM's optical-flow-based dense bundle adjustment, eliminating the need for semantic segmentation, object detection, or depth sensors that prior dynamic SLAM methods (DynaSLAM, DDN-SLAM, DynaMoN) rely on. The system also incorporates Metric3D v2 monocular depth as a stabilizing signal during early tracking when the uncertainty MLP has not yet converged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first monocular 3DGS SLAM system that handles dynamic environments without any semantic priors, opening the door to robust real-world deployment where moving objects are unpredictable and depth sensors are unavailable. The newly introduced Wild-SLAM Dataset (MoCap + iPhone subsets) provides a much-needed benchmark for unconstrained, in-the-wild dynamic SLAM evaluation beyond the limited Bonn and TUM sequences. A stated limitation is that the on-the-fly uncertainty predictor struggles when only a few views cover the same regions, and the authors suggest motion priors could further improve robustness—pointing to an open question about how to combine learned uncertainty with explicit motion modeling in sequential SLAM.
