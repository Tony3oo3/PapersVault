## Problem

Existing text-guided mesh deformation methods face a fundamental trade-off: bump-map-style approaches make only superficial surface changes, while Jacobian-field-based methods (e.g., TextDeformer, MeshUp) produce expressive deformations that distort or destroy the identity of the source shape. The paper argues this failure stems from using the wrong deformation representation, and that per-vertex surface normals offer a principled middle ground—expressive enough for detailed stylization yet inherently restrictive enough to preserve shape identity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source triangle mesh (remeshed with isotropic explicit remeshing for cotangent Laplacian stability) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired style. It outputs a deformed triangle mesh with the same topology. The optimization runs for 2500 epochs (8 rendered views per batch, ~2h15min on a single A40 GPU), and the hyperparameter λ controlling normal-matching strength can be re-applied post-optimization to tune stylization intensity without re-running the full pipeline.

## Method

The core contribution is dARAP, a differentiable single-iteration As-Rigid-As-Possible layer that maps target per-vertex normals to deformed vertex positions. The local step solves an orthogonal Procrustes problem per vertex neighborhood (spokes-and-rims) to find a best-fit rotation aligning the original normal to the target normal while preserving edge directions; the global step solves a Poisson equation with the cotangent Laplacian to recover deformed vertices from these rotations. The outer optimization performs gradient descent on the |V|×3 array of target normals, guided by a Cascaded Score Distillation (CSD) loss from the DeepFloyd IF diffusion model applied to multi-view differentiable renders. Unlike NJF-based methods that assume per-face Jacobians and require explicit L2 identity regularization, dARAP's Procrustes formulation inherently discourages scaling and shearing, preserving shape identity by construction. The single local-global pair (vs. classical ARAP's many iterations) suffices because the outer gradient loop iteratively refines the target normals across epochs.

## Results

Using triangle area ratio (deformed/original) as a proxy for identity preservation, Geometry in Style achieves a mean of 1.080 and standard deviation of 0.233 across 20 mesh-prompt pairs, compared to TextDeformer (mean 0.827, std 0.360) and MeshUp (mean 1.288, std 0.363). The ideal identity-preserving values are mean 1.0 with std 0.0, and Geometry in Style is closest on both metrics. The paper also reports that Geometry in Style achieves better CLIP similarity to the text prompt than both baselines (details in supplementary material).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geometry in Style outperforms both baselines on the triangle area preservation metric, achieving a mean ratio of 1.080 (vs. 0.827 for TextDeformer and 1.288 for MeshUp) and a standard deviation of 0.233 (vs. 0.360 and 0.363 respectively), indicating substantially less geometric distortion. Qualitatively, TextDeformer distorts the surface, changes the source pose, and fails to achieve the target style, while MeshUp produces notable artifacts such as a crushed head on the knight example and a Janus effect (duplicate face and chestplate) on the terracotta warrior. Geometry in Style avoids these artifacts while still achieving a prominent desired style. The paper also reports better CLIP similarity to the prompt than both baselines. The key structural advantage is that dARAP's rotation-based formulation inherently regularizes against scaling and shearing, eliminating the need for an extra L2 identity regularization loss that both TextDeformer and MeshUp require to prevent losing shape identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical variational surface deformation (ARAP, Sorkine & Alexa 2007) and modern score-distillation-based 3D editing. It departs from the Jacobian-based deformation representation used by TextDeformer and MeshUp (both built on Neural Jacobian Fields) by instead optimizing per-vertex target normals, drawing on the normal-driven shape analogy framework of Liu and Jacobson (2021). The key architectural contribution is dARAP, which condenses classical ARAP's multi-iteration local-global loop into a single differentiable local step (orthogonal Procrustes) and global step (Poisson solve), making it usable as a layer inside a gradient-based optimization driven by a cascaded T2I diffusion loss (CSD with DeepFloyd IF).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean, principled answer to a practical problem in 3D editing: how to make deformations expressive enough for detailed stylization while preserving the source shape's identity, without ad-hoc identity-preservation losses. The dARAP layer is general and could be reused in other geometry tasks (parameterization, re-posing, collision resolution). Open questions include handling topology changes, mitigating self-intersections more robustly, and whether the cotangent-Laplacian sensitivity to mesh quality limits real-world applicability. The authors also note that localized stylization (via unsupervised segmentation) and part-adding edits are natural next steps.
