## Problem

Visual Autoregressive Modeling (VAR) generates images via next-scale prediction but suffers from three key limitations: (1) parallel generation of all tokens within a scale assumes conditional independence, causing intra-scale inconsistencies and error accumulation; (2) conditioning on all previous scales produces sequences up to 5.84× longer than next-token prediction, making training and inference expensive with quadratic attention cost and large KV-cache memory; and (3) the number of sampling steps is fixed at training time, preventing quality-speed trade-offs at inference without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250306/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single ImageNet class label as input and produces a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image. The image is decomposed into K=10 resolution scales via a pre-trained multi-scale VQ-VAE tokenizer (shared with VAR), where each scale contains a residual token map of increasing spatial resolution. A key distinction from VAR is that the number of masked refinement steps per scale (M_k) is adjustable at inference time without retraining, enabling a quality-speed trade-off: fewer steps at coarse scales improve FID, while more steps at fine scales enhance perceptual detail.

## Method

HMAR reformulates next-scale prediction as a Markovian process: each scale is conditioned only on the running reconstruction of its immediate predecessor (equivalent to all previous scales via the residual accumulation property), replacing VAR's block-causal attention with a sparser block-diagonal pattern. A custom I/O-aware Triton kernel exploits this block-diagonal structure for >10× faster attention computation, yielding 2.5× end-to-end training speedup and 1.75× inference speedup over VAR with 3× lower memory. Within each scale, HMAR replaces VAR's single-step parallel token generation with a MaskGIT-style multi-step masked prediction procedure, where a subset of tokens is iteratively unmasked conditioned on already-generated tokens and the accumulated reconstruction, removing the per-scale conditional independence assumption. Training uses a two-stage procedure (next-scale pretraining followed by masked-prediction finetuning) with a log-normal loss reweighting across scales that aligns with the empirically observed difficulty distribution, prioritizing early scales where errors propagate most severely.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, HMAR-d24 (1.3B params) achieves FID 2.10 and IS 324.3, outperforming the parameter-matched VAR-d24 (FID 2.15, IS 312.4) and the diffusion baseline DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, IS 278.2). At the largest scale, HMAR-d30 (2.4B) matches VAR-d30 in FID (1.95) while improving IS by ~31 points (334.5 vs. 303.6). On ImageNet 512×512, HMAR-d24 (1.3B) achieves FID 2.99 and IS 304.1, matching the IS of the 2×-larger VAR-d36 (2.5B, FID 2.63) with roughly half the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the directly comparable VAR models, HMAR wins on both FID and IS at every matched scale: HMAR-d24 improves FID from 2.15 to 2.10 and IS from 312.4 to 324.3 over VAR-d24, and HMAR-d30 ties VAR-d30 in FID (1.95) while gaining ~31 IS points (334.5 vs. 303.6). Against the diffusion baseline DiT-XL/2, HMAR-d24 achieves a lower FID (2.10 vs. 2.27) and substantially higher IS (324.3 vs. 278.2) with fewer sampling steps (14 vs. 250). HMAR-d30 slightly trails the hybrid-AR model HART in FID (1.95 vs. 1.77) but edges it out in IS (334.5 vs. 330.3). On ImageNet 512×512, HMAR-d24 (1.3B) is competitive with the 2×-larger VAR-d36 (2.5B), matching its IS (304.1 vs. 303.2) at a modest FID gap (2.99 vs. 2.63). HMAR also achieves the best Recall (0.62) among all 256×256 methods, indicating stronger diversity in generated samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

HMAR builds directly on VAR's next-scale prediction formulation for autoregressive image generation, but departs from it in three key ways: (1) a Markovian reformulation that conditions each scale only on the running reconstruction of its immediate predecessor rather than all previous scales, drawing an analogy to Laplacian/Gaussian pyramids; (2) a multi-step masked generation procedure within each scale borrowed from MaskGIT, replacing VAR's single-step parallel sampling; and (3) a custom I/O-aware block-sparse attention kernel (built on Triton, extending FlashAttention) that exploits the resulting block-diagonal pattern. The method also inherits VAR's multi-scale VQ-VAE tokenizer and is positioned against both diffusion models (DiT) and other autoregressive/masked baselines (MaskGIT, MAR, MAGE, LlamaGen).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HMAR addresses a practical bottleneck in autoregressive image generation: VAR's quadratic sequence-length growth and KV-caching overhead make high-resolution training and inference expensive, while its single-step per-scale sampling limits quality. The Markovian reformulation is a clean, well-motivated insight (validated by attention visualizations) that yields 2.5× faster training, 1.75× faster inference, and 3× lower memory without sacrificing FID, and the flexible intra-scale masking allows practitioners to trade speed for quality at inference time without retraining. The zero-shot transfer to editing tasks (inpainting, outpainting, class-conditional editing) broadens the method's utility. Open questions include whether the framework extends to text-to-image synthesis (acknowledged as future work), whether the Markovian assumption holds at even higher resolutions or with different tokenizers, and whether the efficiency gains persist as model scale increases beyond 2.4B parameters.
