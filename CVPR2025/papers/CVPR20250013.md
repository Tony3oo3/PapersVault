## Problem

Existing image copyright protection methods against diffusion models are either adversarial (irreversible, model-specific, and incompatible with authorized use) or passive detection schemes (watermarks/backdoors) that fail when watermarked images constitute only a small fraction of mixed-source training data, or are restricted to specific model types. No prior method provides a model-agnostic, black-box detection mechanism that remains reliable at low watermark injection ratios (e.g., 1–10%) across both unconditional and text-to-image diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250013/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During the protection phase, the method takes clean RGB images and a reference watermark image, embedding the watermark into the DWT high-frequency sub-bands via an invertible network to produce watermarked images for release. During inspection, the protector samples N images (10,000 for unconditional models, 300 for Stable Diffusion with 60 prompts) from a suspicious model in a fully black-box manner, feeds each generated image through the IEM and watermark decoder, and computes cosine similarity against the reference watermark. The final output is a binary infringement verdict (reject/accept null hypothesis), the extracted watermark image, and the watermark detection ratio P_u across all sampled images.

## Method

CoprGuard exploits the empirical finding that diffusion models preserve the frequency-domain statistical properties (DFT, DCT, DWT spectra) of their training data in generated images, unlike GANs which imprint model-specific spectral signatures. The watermark is embedded into the DWT high-frequency components using a customized HiNet invertible network, augmented with a UNet-based Information Enhancement Module (IEM) that compensates for watermark erasure caused by autoencoders such as AutoencoderKL in Stable Diffusion. Detection employs a statistical hypothesis test: N images are sampled from the suspicious model, watermarks are extracted, and the null hypothesis (no watermark present) is rejected at significance level λ=0.05 if the observed watermark detection ratio P_u exceeds a threshold derived from the t-distribution. The approach is model-agnostic (works for DDIM, DDPM, Classifier-Free Guidance, and Stable Diffusion with full-parameter or LoRA fine-tuning) and achieves 100% detection accuracy when watermarked images exceed 5% of training data, with reliable detection even at 1% injection ratio.

## Results

CoprGuard achieves 100% detection accuracy (TP=10, FP=0, FN=0) across all evaluated model–dataset pairs (DDIM/FFHQ, Classifier-Free/ImageNet, SD/Pokemon, SD+LoRA/Pokemon) for watermark injection ratios R from 10% to 100%, and correctly classifies all non-infringing models at R=0. The watermark detection ratio P_u reaches 100% at R=100% and remains non-zero even at R=1% (P_u = 0.4%–1.2% depending on model). Watermarked image quality is preserved with average PSNR of 34.9–39.9 dB and SSIM of 0.963–0.980, and FID degradation is modest (≈4 for DDIM, ≈13–15 for SD/SD+LoRA) as R increases from 0 to 100%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoprGuard is compared against two baselines: Yu et al. [50] (pretrained watermark encoder for unconditional diffusion) and DIAGNOSIS [47] (image-warping-based detection for text-to-image models). Yu et al. achieves only 50% detection accuracy on SD+LoRA/Pokemon at R=25%, missing all 10 infringing models (10 FN), because their method is limited to unconditional diffusion and cannot handle text-to-image fine-tuning. DIAGNOSIS matches CoprGuard's 100% detection accuracy at R=25% and R=100% on SD+LoRA/Pokemon, but suffers from false positives at R=0 (P_u = 3.8%–6.0%), which risks misclassifying clean models as infringing. At lower injection ratios the gap widens: for SD+LoRA/Pokemon at R=20% and R=50%, DIAGNOSIS yields P_u of 14% and 38% respectively, while CoprGuard achieves 28.7% and 59.3%, making CoprGuard substantially more reliable when watermarked images constitute a small fraction of the training set. In terms of image quality, CoprGuard outperforms DIAGNOSIS by an average of ~5 dB in PSNR (e.g., 38.0 vs. 32.45 on FFHQ) and ~0.01 in SSIM, indicating less perceptual distortion from watermark embedding.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoprGuard builds on HiNet's invertible neural network for frequency-domain watermark embedding, extending it with a UNet-based Information Enhancement Module to counteract watermark erasure by AutoencoderKL in text-to-image pipelines. For the detection decision, it adopts the statistical hypothesis-testing framework from Li et al. for black-box dataset ownership verification. Conceptually, the paper departs from both adversarial protection methods (GLAZE, AdvDM, Nightshade), which are model-specific and irreversible, and prior passive detection approaches (DIAGNOSIS, GenWatermark), which struggle with text-to-image models or low watermark ratios, by grounding its design in the newly discovered spectral-preservation property of diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that diffusion models faithfully inherit spectral statistics from training data (cosine similarity >0.95) while GANs do not is a fundamental insight into how diffusion models operate, with potential implications beyond copyright protection for model analysis and interpretability. Practically, the method offers a model-agnostic, black-box detection mechanism that works even when watermarked images constitute only 1% of training data, addressing a critical gap for content owners facing AI-driven infringement. Key limitations include the absence of from-scratch training experiments for text-to-image models (only fine-tuning was tested), residual watermark degradation through AutoencoderKL, and a relatively narrow set of comparison baselines (two methods).
