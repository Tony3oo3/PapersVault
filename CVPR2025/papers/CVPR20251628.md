## Problem

Existing video editing methods that incorporate sketch guidance rely on pre-trained text-to-image models and suffer from temporal inconsistency (flickering) and imprecise alignment with the provided sketch sequence. While VideoComposer addresses temporal modeling, its focus on compositionality limits accurate fine-grained instance-level edits. The paper tackles the challenge of controllable video instance repainting that simultaneously maintains temporal consistency, follows sketch structure faithfully, and respects text-specified appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251628/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 51-frame video clip at 512×512 resolution, a corresponding per-frame sketch sequence (extracted via HED edge detection), a single text description of the target appearance (generated by PLLaVa), and an instance mask sequence indicating the repaint region. The output is a repainted 51-frame video clip at the same resolution where only the masked instance is modified while the background remains unchanged. The sketch encodes structure (pose, movement) and the text encodes appearance (color, style), enabling four distinct editing scenarios: repainting, replacement, generation, and removal.

## Method

VIRES builds on Open-Sora's DiT backbone with a flow-matching objective and introduces three tailored modules for sketch-conditioned generation. A Sequential ControlNet with 3D causal convolutions extracts spatiotemporal structure layouts from the sketch sequence, and a standardized self-scaling operation adaptively amplifies high-contrast sketch edges before injection into the first transformer block. A sketch attention mechanism, implemented as a parallel branch within each spatial self-attention module (except the first), uses a predefined binary correspondence matrix to inject fine-grained sketch semantics into the latent space with a learnable scaling parameter. During decoding, a sketch-aware encoder (sharing the VAE encoder architecture) provides multi-level texture features that are added to the VAE decoder at each level to align output structure with the sketch. Training proceeds in three stages: first the sketch-aware encoder, then the Sequential ControlNet and self-scaling with frozen DiT, and finally the full model with sketch attention, all using flow-matching velocity prediction loss with selective latent masking for instance-level editing.

## Results

On VIRESET, VIRES achieves PSNR 23.87, SSIM 77.87%, WE 5.09, FC 92.23%, and TC 16.19%, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The most dramatic gains are in human preference: VIRES scores 54.40% on Visual Quality Evaluation (VQE) and 43.20% on Text Alignment Evaluation (TAE), compared to the next-best RAVE at 18.00% and 23.60% respectively. On DAVIS, VIRES similarly leads with PSNR 25.36, SSIM 76.74%, and VQE 50.80%, confirming consistent superiority across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIRES outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both VIRESET and DAVIS. RAVE is the closest competitor on reconstruction metrics (PSNR 18.99 vs. 23.87, SSIM 70.81% vs. 77.87% on VIRESET), but VIRES leads by a wide margin in human preference (VQE 54.40% vs. 18.00%, TAE 43.20% vs. 23.60%), indicating RAVE's inter-frame flickering significantly hurts perceived quality. VideoComposer achieves competitive WE (5.15) but lags substantially on PSNR (16.57) and SSIM (53.99%), reflecting its limited fine-grained instance-level control. Text2VideoZero and Rerender produce the worst reconstruction quality (PSNR 10.04 and 11.85 respectively) due to their reliance on pre-trained text-to-image models without proper temporal modeling. VidToMe achieves moderate reconstruction (PSNR 16.40) but the lowest human preference scores (VQE 4.00%, TAE 6.40%), likely due to its cartoonish stylization artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIRES extends the video editing line of work from UNet-based text-to-image models (e.g., VideoComposer, RAVE, VidToMe) to a DiT-based text-to-video backbone (Open-Sora), addressing the temporal consistency failures of zero-shot image-diffusion approaches. It adapts ControlNet's conditional injection mechanism into a Sequential ControlNet with 3D causal convolutions for video, and introduces flow matching as the training objective in place of standard DDPM. The dual sketch-and-text conditioning departs from prior sketch-guided video editing (VideoComposer), which focuses on compositionality, by targeting precise instance-level repainting with structure (sketch) and appearance (text) separated into distinct control channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIRES provides the first DiT-based controllable video instance editing framework and contributes VIRESET (85K training / 1K evaluation clips), a resource that addresses the lack of temporally consistent instance masks in existing datasets. The large human-preference gap (VQE 54.4 vs. 23.2 for the next-best method) suggests that temporal consistency and structural alignment matter far more to perceptual quality than standard metrics capture, a finding relevant to the broader video generation community. Key open questions include reducing the ~323-second inference cost per 51-frame clip, scaling to longer and higher-resolution videos, and whether the sketch-attention mechanism generalizes to other structural conditions (e.g., depth, pose).
