## Problem

When multiple conditional inputs (text prompts, style images, structure maps) are applied simultaneously to diffusion models, they interfere with each other, causing artifacts, style loss, or content misalignment. Existing style-conditioning methods apply conditioning uniformly across all self-attention layers, leading to over-conditioning where one condition overshadows another. The paper identifies this content-style trade-off and asks whether selectively routing conditions to specific layers can restore balance without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252117/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts, a style reference image, and optional structure maps (Canny edges or MiDaS depth maps) as conditional inputs, alongside pre-computed per-layer sensitivity rankings for style and structure. At inference, two interpolation parameters (λ_S for style, λ_T for structure) control what fraction of self-attention layers receive each condition, with default values of 0.43 and 0.15 respectively. The output is a generated image where content and style are more faithfully preserved simultaneously compared to applying all conditions across all 70 self-attention layers.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free layer sensitivity analysis: the authors generate collections of images where one stylistic aspect (e.g., color palette, geometric style) is varied while other aspects are held constant, then extract (μ, σ) statistics from each self-attention layer's Key/Query features at each timestep. They compute a clustering score per layer using Jensen-Shannon Divergence between Gaussian representations of images, taking the ratio of intra-cluster to inter-cluster distances (analogous to the Dunn index) to rank layers by sensitivity to that aspect. At inference, conditioning is applied only to the top-K most sensitive layers for each condition type, rather than all layers, which reduces interference between conditions. This strategy is applied to existing methods (StyleAligned, B-LoRA) by simply restricting which layers receive the style or ControlNet signal, requiring no retraining or architectural modification.

## Results

The paper evaluates on 5,120 images per method across 32 styles, 20 prompts (10 Easy + 10 Complex), and three conditioning types (text-only, Canny, Depth). The primary quantitative evidence comes from a user study (1,134 evaluations by 42 participants) where balanced methods achieve a statistically significant preference: in the multi-choice test, balanced methods receive 386 votes vs. 244 for imbalanced (χ²=35.1, p<0.001); in A/B tests, balanced B-LoRA wins 195 vs. 57 (χ²=30.0) and balanced StyleAligned wins 185 vs. 67 (χ²=21.9). The quantitative content/style similarity plot (Fig. 8) shows balanced methods maintain consistently high scores across all conditioning combinations, while imbalanced methods exhibit large variance—style drops sharply under structure conditioning and content degrades under complex prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its balancing strategy applied to StyleAligned and B-LoRA against four style-based baselines: StyleAligned (unbalanced), B-LoRA (unbalanced), InstantStyle, and Jeong et al. (self-attention swapping). In the user study multi-choice test, the two balanced methods collectively outperform all four imbalanced baselines (386 vs. 244 votes, χ²=35.1, p<0.001). In head-to-head A/B tests, balanced B-LoRA wins 77% of comparisons over unbalanced B-LoRA (195 vs. 57), and balanced StyleAligned wins 73% over unbalanced StyleAligned (185 vs. 67). The quantitative similarity plot (Fig. 8) shows that balanced methods maintain high style and content scores consistently across text-only, Canny, and Depth conditioning, whereas InstantStyle and Jeong et al. show significant style degradation under structure conditioning and B-LoRA (unbalanced) suffers from under-stylization. Two additional methods (Instastyle [8] and RB-Modulation [31]) are compared in the supplemental file. The key differentiator is that the balancing strategy's advantage grows with conditioning complexity—under text-only prompts the gap is modest, but under combined text+image conditioning the balanced methods clearly dominate in both style fidelity and content alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on StyleAligned (Hertz et al., 2023), which established that style information is embedded in self-attention layers and can be injected via AdaIN, and on B-LoRA (Frenkel et al., 2024), which identifies style-sensitive attention blocks for LoRA fine-tuning. The key departure is methodological: rather than applying conditioning to all layers (StyleAligned) or a fixed heuristic subset (B-LoRA), the authors introduce a systematic, training-free sensitivity analysis using Jensen-Shannon Divergence-based clustering scores over attention statistics to rank each layer's responsiveness to a specific artistic aspect at each timestep. This extends the timestep-level analysis of ProSpect (Zhang et al., 2023) into the layer dimension and generalizes beyond text conditioning to image-based style and structure (ControlNet) conditioning, covering architectures like SDXL and SD3.5-Large.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that conditioning only ~43% of layers for style and ~15% for structure yields better-balanced outputs than full-layer conditioning—offers a simple, training-free knob for practitioners dealing with multi-conditional generation artifacts. The layer sensitivity analysis framework is general in principle (applicable to any attention architecture and any conditioning type), though its current validation is limited to SDXL and SD3.5-Large with a specific set of artistic styles. A key limitation is dependence on the base model's prior knowledge: styles unfamiliar to the model cannot be properly generated regardless of layer selection. Open questions include whether the sensitivity rankings transfer across model scales, whether the approach extends to video generation, and whether the clustering-based analysis can be made more principled or connected to theoretical guarantees.
