## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are designed for natural RGB images and degrade significantly in low-light or high-dynamic scenarios where event cameras excel. No MLLM has been developed to understand asynchronous event streams, and the large domain gap between sparse, asynchronous brightness-change events and natural language, combined with the absence of large-scale event-text datasets, has left this cross-modal alignment problem unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250640/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an event stream S = {(x_j, y_j, t_j, p_j)} — a set of asynchronous events with spatial coordinates, timestamps, and polarity — segmented into N_w temporal bins (default 5), along with a free-form text query. For fair comparison with RGB-based MLLMs, the event stream is rendered as an event image at the same spatial resolution. The model produces free-form text responses covering detailed scene captioning, multi-step reasoning, and visual question answering, and can also generate object descriptions that drive downstream detection (GroundingDINO) and segmentation (GroundedSAM) pipelines.

## Method

EventGPT comprises an event encoder (OpenCLIP ViT-L/14-336px), a spatio-temporal aggregator that performs separate temporal and spatial average pooling over bin-encoded features and concatenates them along the feature dimension, a two-layer MLP linear projector, a linear event-language adapter, and a Vicuna-v1.5 LLM. The key training insight is a three-stage progressive alignment: (1) warm up the linear projector on LLaVA-Pre-train image-text pairs since RGB images are closer to language than events; (2) train the spatio-temporal aggregator and event-language adapter on 1M synthetic N-ImageNet-Chat samples to map event features into language space; (3) unfreeze all parameters and fine-tune on 120k real-world Event-Chat instruction samples covering challenging lighting and high-speed motion. This staged curriculum progressively narrows the cross-modal gap, with each stage freezing previously trained modules to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

EventGPT-7B achieves DC/CR/VQA scores of 2.39/2.57/2.23 on N-ImageNet-Chat and 3.52/4.09/4.29 on Event-Chat, outperforming all four RGB-based MLLM baselines on every metric. The 13B variant further improves to 2.41/2.81/2.40 (N-ImageNet-Chat) and 3.40/4.13/4.26 (Event-Chat). The largest margins appear on the synthetic N-ImageNet-Chat benchmark, where EventGPT-7B leads the best baseline (Qwen2-VL-7B) by 0.65 on DC and 1.11 on CR, reflecting the model's dedicated event-language alignment. On the real-world Event-Chat set, EventGPT-7B gains +1.11 DC and +0.58 VQA over the strongest competitor (Intern2VL-8B), while CR is nearly tied with Deepseek-VL-7B (4.09 vs. 4.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventGPT-7B decisively outperforms all four RGB-based MLLM baselines on the synthetic N-ImageNet-Chat benchmark, leading Qwen2-VL-7B (the strongest RGB baseline there) by +0.65 DC, +1.11 CR, and +0.32 VQA, confirming that the three-stage event-language alignment pipeline is critical for event stream understanding. On the real-world Event-Chat benchmark, EventGPT-7B achieves the best DC (3.52 vs. 2.41 for Deepseek-VL-7B) and VQA (4.29 vs. 3.71 for Intern2VL-8B), but its CR score of 4.09 is marginally below Deepseek-VL-7B's 4.10, suggesting that complex multi-step reasoning on real-world event scenes remains a narrow gap. The 13B variant extends the CR lead to 4.13, surpassing all baselines on every metric. Qualitatively, EventGPT correctly identifies vehicles at tunnel exits and captures high-speed object dynamics (e.g., a breaking cup) where LLaVA-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B produce inaccurate or static descriptions, underscoring the practical advantage of event-native spatio-temporal encoding over RGB frame-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

EventGPT extends the LLaVA architecture (vision encoder + linear projector + LLM) to the event-camera domain, where prior work like EventCLIP and EventBind aligned event features with text via CLIP but lacked the generative and reasoning capabilities of an LLM. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a spatio-temporal aggregator and a dedicated event-language adapter, motivated by the asynchronous, sparse nature of event streams that differs fundamentally from frame-based RGB inputs. The three-stage training paradigm—RGB warm-up, synthetic event-language alignment, then real-world instruction tuning—repurposes LLaVA's two-stage recipe by inserting an intermediate event-specific alignment phase to bridge the larger domain gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first MLLM for event streams, EventGPT opens a new application axis for neuromorphic vision, enabling scene summarization, reasoning, and QA in low-light and high-dynamic settings where RGB cameras fail. The two large-scale datasets (N-ImageNet-Chat: ~1M synthetic samples; Event-Chat: ~120K real-world samples) are a significant community resource. However, the evaluation is largely self-referential (trained and tested on the authors' own GPT-generated benchmarks with GPT-scored metrics), and the LLM backbone (Vicuna-v1.5) is not state-of-the-art, leaving open questions about whether the gains persist with stronger LLMs and whether the approach scales to longer, higher-resolution event streams or real-time deployment.
