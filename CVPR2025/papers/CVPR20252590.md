## Problem

Existing coarse-to-fine point cloud registration methods (e.g., GeoTransformer, Predator) rely on dense feature interactions at both the superpoint and point scales, which the authors show through attention visualizations introduces redundant and erroneous information that dilutes feature discriminability and geometric consistency. No prior work systematically addresses how to restrict feature interactions to only relevant points at both scales simultaneously to improve registration robustness under repetitive structures and low overlap.

## Input / Output

The method takes two unaligned 3D point clouds P (N points) and Q (M points) and outputs a rigid transformation T = {R, t} with R ∈ SO(3) and t ∈ R³. Internally, a KPConv-FPN backbone produces superpoints (coarsest downsampled points) for inter-patch matching and dense points at half the original resolution for intra-patch matching. The final transformation is selected from multiple candidates via a local-to-global scheme (weighted SVD per correspondence set, then inlier-count selection), avoiding RANSAC entirely.

## Method

DFAT builds on GeoTransformer and introduces dual-level focus-attention to replace indiscriminate dense interactions. At the coarse scale, a Superpoint Focus-Attention Transformer first runs the geometric transformer for global context, then selects top-k sparse keypoints from each superpoint's neighborhood using a combined criterion of feature similarity (FSSN) and cross-point saliency, and performs focused attention only between superpoints and these keypoints to refine local geometric consistency. A Dual-space Consistency Matching module then filters putative superpoint correspondences by verifying that geometric nearest neighbors of matched pairs also exhibit high feature similarity. At the fine scale, a Point Focus-Attention module applies linear attention (with ELU kernel) exclusively among points belonging to the same superpoint pair, encoding local context before Sinkhorn-based correspondence assignment. The entire pipeline is trained with overlap-aware circle loss at the superpoint level and negative log-likelihood point matching loss at the point level.

## Results

On 3DMatch, DFAT+PEAL achieves 95.8% Registration Recall (RR), matching GeoTrans+PEAL but with fewer iterations (3 vs. 6); without PEAL, DFAT reaches 94.3% RR, outperforming GeoTrans (91.5%) by 2.8 pp. On 3DLoMatch (10–30% overlap), DFAT+PEAL attains 80.5% RR, a 4.8 pp improvement over GeoTrans (74.0%) without PEAL. On KITTI, DFAT reduces Relative Translation Error to 4.4 cm and Relative Rotation Error to 0.19°, both best among all compared methods, while maintaining 99.8% RR. On the multiway registration task (Augmented ICL-NUIM), DFAT achieves a mean ATE of 13.95, slightly outperforming GeoTrans (14.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest RANSAC-based baselines (RoReg, BUFFER at 92.9% RR on 3DMatch), DFAT without PEAL (94.3%) gains 1.4 pp while using a far cheaper local-to-global estimator instead of 50k RANSAC iterations. Compared to its direct predecessor GeoTransformer, DFAT improves 3DMatch RR by 2.8 pp (94.3 vs. 91.5) and 3DLoMatch RR by 4.8 pp (78.8 vs. 74.0) without PEAL, and matches GeoTrans+PEAL (95.8/80.5) with only 3 iterations versus 6. On KITTI, DFAT reduces RTE from 6.8 cm to 4.4 cm and RRE from 0.24° to 0.19° relative to GeoTrans, while tying at 99.8% RR; it also outperforms BUFFER (5.4 cm / 0.22° / 97.7%) on all three metrics. On the multiway registration benchmark (Augmented ICL-NUIM), DFAT achieves a mean ATE of 13.95, narrowly beating GeoTrans (14.12) and substantially outperforming classical methods like RANSAC (40.49) and DGR (17.57). The paper does not report any setting where DFAT underperforms GeoTransformer, though the gains on 3DMatch with PEAL are zero (both at 95.8%), with the advantage being computational (fewer iterations).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GeoTransformer [24], which itself extends Predator [14] and the coarse-to-fine paradigm introduced by CoFiNet [31]. The key conceptual departure is the identification that dense self-attention and cross-attention interactions—used by Predator, GeoTransformer, and subsequent works—introduce redundant and erroneous information that dilutes feature discriminability, particularly in the presence of repetitive structures. Rather than replacing the geometric transformer backbone, DFAT cascades a sparse keypoint-based focus-attention module after the dense geometric transformer at the superpoint level, and adds a linear-attention point focus-attention module at the fine level, introducing 'focused' interactions as a new inductive bias. A dual-space consistency matching module further filters unreliable superpoint correspondences by checking geometric-neighbor consistency in feature space, a constraint not explicitly exploited in prior coarse-to-fine registration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete diagnostic (attention-map visualization) for a failure mode affecting an entire family of transformer-based registration methods, and offers a targeted fix that yields SOTA results on 3DMatch, KITTI, and Augmented ICL-NUIM without RANSAC. The dual-space consistency idea—verifying that geometric neighbors of a correspondence also match in feature space—is a simple but effective filtering principle that could be adopted in other correspondence-based pipelines. Limitations include evaluation only on rigid registration, dependence on the GeoTransformer/KPConv-FPN architecture, and the fact that the strongest comparison (GeoTrans+PEAL) matches DFAT+PEAL exactly, suggesting the gains over the raw baseline are partly attributable to PEAL. The authors note future extension to non-rigid registration as an open direction.
