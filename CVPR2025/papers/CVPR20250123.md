## Problem

Most video diffusion models are trained at fixed resolutions and aspect ratios, making them unable to directly generate the wide or 360° panoramic dynamic scenes required for immersive AR/VR applications. Existing spatial-scaling methods for images either require fine-tuning, are limited to square outputs, or produce seam artifacts when stitching patches, while prior panoramic video methods (e.g., 360DVD, 4K4DGen) suffer from low resolution, restricted motion range, or reliance on optimization procedures that limit efficiency and generalization.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt and/or a reference image as conditioning and produces panoramic videos at arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios, including full 360° equirectangular panoramas (2:1 aspect ratio). A key property is that VRAM consumption remains constant regardless of output resolution, since the underlying diffusion model (VideoCrafter2 for text-to-video, VideoCrafter1 for image-to-video) always operates at its native fixed resolution. The output can be extended to arbitrary temporal length and made seamlessly loopable by treating the frame sequence as a ring.

## Method

DynamicScaler is a training-free framework built on top of a standard video diffusion model. Its core mechanism, the Offset Shifting Denoiser (OSD), divides the target panorama latent into a grid of windows matching the model's native resolution, then shifts these windows by an offset in both horizontal and vertical directions at every denoising step; the resulting cross-step overlap regions synchronize content and motion across the entire panorama, while the horizontal dimension is treated as a ring to ensure seamless left-right boundary continuity. For 360° FoV generation, a Panoramic Projecting Denoiser maps the equirectangular latent into perspective view-port windows for denoising and re-projects back, with view angles also shifted per step and a noise-rebalancing mask handling overlapping high-latitude regions. A Global Motion Guidance (GMG) module provides a two-stage hierarchy: a low-resolution pass captures global motion structure, which is then upsampled and re-noised to initialize the high-resolution pass, preserving both local detail fidelity and global motion coherence. Finally, OSD is extended along the temporal axis to generate long-duration or loopable videos by shifting frame-clip windows across time, overcoming the limited frame capacity of the base model.

## Results

On the primary quantitative comparison (Table 1), DynamicScaler outperforms 360DVD across all eight metrics: CLIP-Score (0.302 vs. 0.293), Image Quality (0.583 vs. 0.436), Dynamic Degree (0.783 vs. 0.412), Motion Smoothness (0.963 vs. 0.917), Temporal Flickering (0.982 vs. 0.964), Scene Richness (0.499 vs. 0.417), Q-Align Image (0.632 vs. 0.485), and Q-Align Video (0.613 vs. 0.532). The most dramatic gains are in Dynamic Degree (+90% relative) and Image Quality (+34% relative), reflecting the method's ability to generate richer motion at higher resolutions. In the user preference study (Table 2), DynamicScaler achieves the highest or near-highest ratings across all five criteria (Graphics Quality, Frame Consistency, End Continuity, Motion Pattern, Scene Richness) compared to 360DVD, 4K4DGen, ScaleCrafter, and VividDream.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynamicScaler is compared against four baselines: 360DVD (CVPR 2024), 4K4DGen (arXiv 2024), ScaleCrafter (ICLR 2023), and VividDream (arXiv 2024). Against 360DVD, the only method that directly generates 360° dynamic panoramas, DynamicScaler wins on every quantitative metric, with the largest margins in Dynamic Degree (0.783 vs. 0.412) and Image Quality (0.583 vs. 0.436), and also leads in the user study on all five criteria (e.g., Graphics Quality 4.6 vs. 3.3, End Continuity 4.8 vs. 3.6). Against 4K4DGen in the random-case user study, DynamicScaler wins on Frame Consistency (3.9 vs. 3.6), End Continuity (4.5 vs. 4.3), and Motion Pattern (4.5 vs. 3.6), while 4K4DGen edges ahead on Graphics Quality (4.5 vs. 4.3) and Scene Richness (4.3 vs. 4.4 is a near-tie). Against ScaleCrafter, DynamicScaler dominates on End Continuity (4.5 vs. 1.9) and Frame Consistency (3.9 vs. 3.7), though ScaleCrafter scores slightly higher on Motion Pattern (4.4 vs. 4.5 is a near-tie). Against VividDream, DynamicScaler wins on Graphics Quality (4.3 vs. 3.6), Frame Consistency (3.9 vs. 3.7), and End Continuity (4.5 vs. 3.8), while VividDream leads slightly on Scene Richness (4.1 vs. 4.4 is actually a DynamicScaler win). Overall, DynamicScaler achieves the best or near-best score in every comparison, with particularly strong advantages in boundary continuity and motion coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynamicScaler builds on the video diffusion model family (VideoCrafter1/2) and the spatial-scaling literature for diffusion models (MultiDiffusion, SyncDiffusion, ScaleCrafter), but departs from them by replacing static overlapping windows with a temporal offset-shifting mechanism that synchronizes denoising across the entire panorama without requiring retraining or optimization. It contrasts with 360DVD, which fine-tunes a video diffusion model on equirectangular data, and 4K4DGen, which relies on per-scene optimization and is limited to image-to-video; DynamicScaler achieves comparable or better quality in a fully training-free, text- or image-conditioned setting. The Global Motion Guidance mechanism extends the hierarchical coarse-to-fine idea from ScaleCrafter to the temporal and panoramic domains, while the spherical projection denoising generalizes the perspective-window approach of 4K4DGen by integrating offset shifting in view-angle space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constant-VRAM property—where output resolution and aspect ratio can be scaled arbitrarily without increasing memory—makes this approach practically attractive for AR/VR content pipelines that must handle diverse display formats. The training-free nature lowers the barrier to adoption, as practitioners can plug the OSD and GMG modules onto any existing video diffusion backbone without collecting panoramic training data or running optimization loops. Open questions include how the method behaves at very high resolutions (beyond what the base model was trained on), whether the two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline can be made more efficient, and whether the shifting mechanism generalizes to other projection types (cylindrical, fisheye) or to 3D-consistent multi-view generation.
