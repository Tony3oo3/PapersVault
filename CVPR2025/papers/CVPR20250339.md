## Problem

Existing polarization-aided 3D reconstruction methods for glossy objects, such as PANDORA, require expensive dedicated polarization cameras or labor-intensive calibration of polarizer angles, making them impractical for widespread use. This paper asks whether a single polarization image per view, captured with an off-the-shelf RGB camera and a linear polarizer at an unknown angle, is sufficient to recover high-fidelity geometry and radiance decomposition of specular surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250339/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes approximately 40 multi-view polarization images (one per view, downsampled by a factor of 4) captured with a SONY A6400 RGB camera and a linear polarizer whose angle is not calibrated, along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses. It outputs a neural implicit surface (SDF) extractable as a 3D mesh, decomposed diffuse and specular radiance maps, per-point Stokes vectors with derived AoP and DoP, the jointly-optimized polarizer angle, and novel view renderings. Notably, the polarizer angle is treated as an unknown to be solved rather than a known input, and only a single polarization image per view is required instead of the four-angle set used by PANDORA.

## Method

The pipeline combines VolSDF for the neural implicit surface and Ref-NeRF-style separate radiance networks for diffuse and specular components, then applies the polarimetric BRDF (pBRDF) model to compute outgoing Stokes vectors from the decomposed radiances, surface normals, and view directions. A fully differentiable polarization rendering layer then modulates these Stokes vectors through a Mueller matrix parameterized by the unknown polarizer angle to produce a rendered polarized image, which is compared against the captured image via L1 loss. The polarizer angle is jointly optimized with all network parameters, eliminating the need for pre-calibration. The total loss also includes a binary cross-entropy mask loss and an eikonal regularization on the SDF. Theoretically, the method shows that with 10 unknowns (normal, diffuse/specular albedo, roughness, polarizer angle) and 3 constraints per view (RGB channels), only four views make the system overdetermined, providing the physical constraints that disambiguate diffuse from specular components.

## Results

On five real-world glossy objects (RedOx, GreenOx, Cat, Horse, Lays), the proposed method achieves the best rendering quality (PSNR 24.83–31.02, SSIM 0.9606–0.9883) and the best geometric accuracy (Chamfer Distance 9.88e-5 to 1.01e-3) across all objects, outperforming all six non-polarization baselines. The geometry improvement is particularly dramatic: on RedOx, CD drops from 0.3005 (NVDiffRec) to 2.23e-4, a ~1350× reduction. On the synthetic Bust model, the method is slightly behind PANDORA (diffuse PSNR 23.29 vs 23.97, normal MAE 4.227° vs 4.096°), which is expected since PANDORA uses full Stokes vectors as supervision while this work uses only one polarization image per view.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against non-polarization baselines, the proposed method wins on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, CD) across all five real-world objects. NVDiffRec achieves competitive rendering quality (e.g., PSNR 30.86 vs 30.88 on RedOx) but produces fundamentally erroneous geometry (CD 0.3005 vs 2.23e-4), confirming the paper's hypothesis that RGB-only methods cannot disentangle diffuse and specular components. PhySG and InvRender show overly smooth geometry and are limited to synthetic scenarios, respectively. NeRO, designed for fully specular objects, struggles with mixed-material scenes (SSIM drops to 0.5972 on GreenOx). NeuralPIL and Ref-NeuS fail to produce valid geometry in several cases, so no CD is reported. Against the polarization baseline PANDORA on the synthetic Bust model, the proposed method is slightly behind (diffuse PSNR 23.29 vs 23.97, normal MAE 4.227° vs 4.096°), but this gap is expected since PANDORA uses full four-angle Stokes vectors as supervision while this work requires only a single polarization image per view with unknown polarizer angle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the polarization-aided NeRF paradigm introduced by PANDORA (Dave et al., ECCV 2022), which first integrated Stokes-vector supervision into neural radiance fields for glossy object reconstruction. The key departure is replacing the expensive full-polarization camera (requiring four images per view or a dedicated sensor like SONY IMX250MZR) with a single linear polarizer on an off-the-shelf RGB camera, capturing one image per view with an unknown polarizer angle that is jointly optimized. The method builds on VolSDF for the neural implicit surface, Ref-NeRF for view-dependent radiance decomposition, and the polarimetric BRDF model of Baek et al. (SIGGRAPH Asia 2018) to compute outgoing Stokes vectors, while introducing a differentiable polarization rendering layer based on Mueller matrices to close the loop between network outputs and the single captured image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the acquisition hardware to a cheap linear polarizer and eliminating calibration, this work makes polarization-based glossy reconstruction accessible to a much broader audience, with the authors explicitly noting potential deployment on smartphones and IoT devices. The theoretical analysis showing that four views yield an overdetermined system (12 equations for 10 unknowns) provides a clean justification for why the single-image-per-view constraint is sufficient. Open questions include scalability to dynamic scenes, handling of complex multi-material objects with strong inter-reflections, and whether the color-bleeding artifacts noted in the discussion can be mitigated. The approach also raises the question of whether even fewer views or partially overlapping polarizer angles could suffice in practice.
