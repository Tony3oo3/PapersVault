## Problem

Existing multi-view representation learning methods predominantly fuse all views in a single parallel step, which is static and cannot resolve conflicts or redundancy among heterogeneous views. Sequential fusion offers a more flexible alternative by integrating views one at a time, but determining the optimal per-sample view sequence without supervised signals remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252145/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V views per sample (e.g., six handcrafted features such as Gabor, LBP, HOG, GIST, CENTRIST, or multi-modal sensor data), each first projected to a shared 256-dimensional latent space via view-specific autoencoders. The output is a single unified representation per sample, from which cluster assignments (via K-means) or class labels (via SVM) are derived for downstream evaluation. Notably, the fusion sequence is sample-specific: the model may fuse views in different orders for different samples and can optionally reuse a view after a cooldown window.

## Method

SeqMvRL models multi-view fusion as a sequential decision-making problem solved with Deep Q-Networks. A Pairwise Integrator (PI), a two-layer MLP trained with the DDC clustering loss, incrementally merges the current fused state with the next selected view; a Next-View Selector (NVS), also a two-layer MLP acting as the DQN agent, chooses which view to fuse at each step from a (V+1)-dimensional action space (V views plus an 'End' action). A novel reward function combines intra-cluster compactness, inter-cluster separation, and pseudo-label assignment consistency to guide the agent toward views that improve discriminability. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) pre-train view-specific autoencoders for reconstruction, (2) train the PI on randomly sampled fusion sequences of varying lengths, and (3) run DQN with experience replay and a target network to learn the optimal per-sample view ordering.

## Results

On the primary clustering task, SeqMvRL achieves the best performance across all four main datasets: 46.23% ACC / 55.19% NMI / 41.02% ARI on Flower17, 79.68% / 87.47% / 74.62% on COIL-20, 58.25% / 56.24% / 44.64% on Caltech-7, and 47.85% / 67.72% / 37.30% on Caltech-20. The largest gains over the previous SOTA appear on Caltech-7 (+6.73% ACC, +6.03% ARI) and Flower17 (+2.11% ACC, +2.38% ARI). On the secondary classification task, SeqMvRL leads in accuracy on all four datasets (e.g., 93.27% on Caltech-7, 90.33% on COIL-20), though it trails PDMF on F-score for COIL-20 (84.51 vs. 86.93). On the multi-modal Oxford-IIIT Pet and CAT datasets, SeqMvRL also outperforms DSMVC by 0.96–2.80% in clustering accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeqMvRL outperforms all eight baselines on clustering accuracy for Flower17, COIL-20, and Caltech-7, with the most notable margin over PDMF on Flower17 (46.23 vs. 44.12 ACC) and over DSMVC on Caltech-7 (58.25 vs. 51.52 ACC, +6.73%). On Caltech-20, SeqMvRL leads in ACC and NMI but trails DSMVC by 0.06% in ARI (37.30 vs. 37.36), the only metric where it does not achieve the top score. Among baselines, DSMVC is the strongest competitor on COIL-20 clustering (77.07 ACC) and Caltech-20 (48.24 ACC), while PDMF leads on Flower17 (44.12 ACC) and RCML on Caltech-20 ACC (45.86). For classification, SeqMvRL achieves the highest accuracy on all four datasets (e.g., 93.27% on Caltech-7 vs. 91.29% for PDMF), but PDMF retains a slight edge on COIL-20 F-score (86.93 vs. 84.51) and Flower17 F-score (36.06 vs. 35.73). On the multi-modal Oxford-IIIT Pet dataset, SeqMvRL improves over DSMVC by 0.96% ACC (clustering) and 0.20% ACC (classification), while on the more challenging CAT dataset the gap widens to +2.80% ACC in clustering and +3.05% in classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant parallel fusion paradigm in multi-view representation learning, where all views are combined simultaneously, by reframing fusion as a sequential decision-making problem solved with reinforcement learning. It repurposes DQN (a standard RL algorithm for discrete action spaces) as the Next-View Selector, while the Pairwise Integrator draws on existing two-view fusion architectures and the DDC clustering loss. The latent feature extraction stage uses view-specific autoencoders, a well-established preprocessing step. Conceptually, the paper carves new ground by being the first to apply RL-based sequential view selection at the sample level in MvRL, though it acknowledges one prior work on orthogonal sequential fusion [15] that remains at a different level of abstraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper validates that view ordering is a non-trivial factor in multi-view fusion quality and that an RL agent can learn sample-specific sequences without supervision, offering a flexible alternative to fixed parallel pipelines. However, the practical impact is tempered by evaluation on small-scale datasets (≤2386 samples, ≤7 views), modest improvements over strong baselines (typically 1–3%), and the added training complexity of a three-stage pipeline with a separate RL phase. Open questions include scalability to dozens of views or truly heterogeneous modalities (e.g., text + image + audio), whether simpler learned heuristics could approximate the RL policy at lower cost, and how the method behaves when views are partially missing or of highly variable quality.
