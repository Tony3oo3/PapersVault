## Problem

Existing AD generation methods rely on adapter-based architectures whose parameter count grows with LLM size, limiting scalability. Additionally, prior approaches treat all characters appearing in a video clip as relevant, ignoring that ADs should focus only on main story-driving characters, and they underutilize rich contextual information (past video and past descriptions) while suffering from repetitive generation when consecutive clips are visually similar.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251916/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of N frames, character exemplar features (averaged CLIP embeddings of 5 frames most similar to each actor's portrait), character/actor names, up to K past ADs as text, and up to K past video clips as frame features. These are assembled into a single interleaved multimodal sequence where visual embeddings and text tokens preserve their positional relationships. The output is a single audio description sentence naming the relevant character(s) and describing their expression, action, or status in the current scene.

## Method

Uni-AD maps all visual inputs (video frames and character portraits) through a lightweight multi-layer transformer encoder with a fixed number of learnable vectors, projecting CLIP features into the frozen LLM's text embedding space without inserting adapters, enabling straightforward scaling to larger LLMs like LLaMA2-7B. A character-refinement module (3 MHCA + FFN layers) performs binary classification over the external character bank to retain only AD-related characters, using character exemplar features as queries and video features as keys/values. Contextual video clips are concatenated with the current clip before passing through the visual mapping network, while past ADs are appended as text tokens in the prompt. A contrastive loss penalizes the model when the likelihood of the previous AD exceeds that of the current ground-truth AD, discouraging repetitive generation. The overall training objective combines standard auto-regressive cross-entropy with this contrastive term, training only the visual mapping network and character-refinement module while keeping the LLM frozen.

## Results

On MAD-eval-Named without context, Uni-AD with LLaMA2-7B and CLIP-L14 achieves 16.8 ROUGE-L, 27.3 CIDEr, and 53.3 R@5/16, outperforming all prior methods. With contextual video features and contrastive loss, Uni-AD reaches 17.1 ROUGE-L, 28.2 CIDEr, and 54.2 R@5/16, setting a new state of the art. On CMDAD, Uni-AD scores 21.8 CIDEr, 41.9 CRITIC, and 2.92 LLM-AD-eval; on TVAD it achieves 26.6 CIDEr, 28.3 CRITIC, and 2.89 LLM-AD-eval, matching or exceeding all baselines on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest prior method without context (AutoAD-III, LLaMA2 + EVA-CLIP), Uni-AD (LLaMA2 + CLIP-L14) improves CIDEr from 24.0 to 27.3 (+3.3) and R@5/16 from 52.8 to 53.3, while also reporting ROUGE-L (16.8 vs. not reported). In the contextual setting, Uni-AD dominates MM-Narrator (GPT-4) by a wide margin: +3.7 ROUGE-L, +14.3 CIDEr, and +5.2 R@5/16, indicating that the interleaved multimodal prompt with contrastive loss is far more effective than GPT-4 prompting with expert tools. On CMDAD, Uni-AD ties AutoAD-III on CIDEr (21.8 vs. 21.7) but substantially outperforms it on CRITIC (41.9 vs. 25.2) and LLM-AD-eval (2.92 vs. 2.85); on TVAD it leads on all three metrics (26.6 vs. 26.1 CIDEr, 28.3 vs. 15.9 CRITIC, 2.89 vs. 2.78 LLM-AD-eval). The largest single-metric gap is on TVAD CRITIC, where Uni-AD nearly doubles AutoAD-III's score, suggesting the character-refinement module and contextual video features are particularly effective for TV-series content with recurring characters.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni-AD extends the AutoAD lineage (AutoAD-I/II/III) by replacing adapter-based or Q-former-based modality bridging with a lightweight visual mapping network that projects CLIP features into the LLM's embedding space, avoiding the parameter-scaling problem of adapters as LLMs grow. The interleaved multimodal sequence formulation draws from web-scale pre-training work (Flamingo, BLIP-2, CM3) but repurposes it for the AD task, where preserving positional relationships between character portraits, video frames, and text queries enables finer-grained cross-modal alignment. The character-refinement module departs from AutoAD-II's character bank by filtering to only AD-relevant characters rather than all visible ones, and the contrastive loss addresses a repetition failure mode not tackled by prior AD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, memory-friendly framework for AD generation that scales to larger open-source LLMs (LLaMA2-7B) without inserting adapters, which is important for accessibility applications where deployment cost matters. However, the character-refinement module's performance (0.41 precision, 0.77 recall) remains far from ground-truth character selection, and the recurrent context AD setting shows a clear training-inference gap that degrades performance—both are open problems. The finding that more visual tokens benefit Uni-AD but not the Flamingo-style AutoAD-II suggests architecture-specific limits on fine-grained alignment that deserve further investigation.
