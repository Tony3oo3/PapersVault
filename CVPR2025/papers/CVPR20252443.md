## Problem

Annotating camera poses on dynamic Internet videos at scale is critical for video generation, view synthesis, and robotics, yet most Internet videos are unsuitable for pose estimation (cartoons, heavy post-processing, ambiguous references) and even suitable ones defeat standard SfM due to moving objects and appearance changes. Existing datasets are either small synthetic collections with sim2real gaps or restricted to narrow domains (egocentric, driving, pet turntables), leaving no large-scale, diverse, public dataset of dynamic real-world video with accurate camera annotations.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw Internet video clips (primarily 4–10 seconds, sourced from Panda-70M) and produces per-frame camera extrinsics (pose), intrinsics, and dynamic object masks. Filtering operates on temporally subsampled frames using lightweight classifiers and a VLM, while pose estimation processes full-resolution frames through point tracking and masking before global bundle adjustment. The final dataset (DynPose-100K) contains 100,131 videos totaling 6,806K frames, with videos requiring ≥80% frame registration to be retained.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (filtering) sequentially applies six specialized detectors (cartoon/screen classifier, distortion estimator, focal-length variance, dynamic mask size, optical flow statistics, point-track stability) followed by GPT-4o mini prompted with eight targeted questions, reducing 3.2M Panda-70M videos to 137K suitable clips. Stage 2 (pose estimation) first constructs dense long-term correspondences by running BootsTAP point tracking over a grid of points in a sliding-window fashion, then removes dynamic regions using four complementary masks (OneFormer semantic segmentation for common dynamic classes, Hands23 for hand-object interactions, RoDynRF motion masking via Sampson error on optical flow, and SAM2 for cross-frame mask propagation), and finally feeds the surviving static tracklets into Theia-SfM global bundle adjustment. This combination of state-of-the-art tracking, multi-source dynamic masking, and robust global optimization reduces trajectory error by 50–90% over prior methods on both the synthetic Lightspeed benchmark and real Panda-Test videos.

## Results

On the Lightspeed benchmark (36 photorealistic sequences with ground-truth poses), the proposed pipeline registers 100% of sequences and achieves an ATE of 0.072 m, RPE translation of 0.033 m, and RPE rotation of 1.31°, cutting trajectory error by ~50% over the best baseline (MonST3R, ATE 0.149 m) across all sequences and by ~90% on the 8 sequences where all methods succeed (ATE 0.003 m vs. 0.036 m). On Panda-Test (90 real Internet videos, 10K annotated correspondences), the method achieves a mean per-video reprojection error of 5.76 px at 720p with 98.9% of videos below 30 px, outperforming LEAP-VO (7.59 px) and MonST3R (9.86 px). On the 52 sequences where all methods succeed, the mean error drops to 3.75 px with 100% of videos below 30 px, a >35% reduction over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed pipeline outperforms all ten baselines on both evaluation benchmarks. On Lightspeed, it beats the closest competitor MonST3R by 2× in ATE (0.072 m vs. 0.149 m) and by 35% in RPE rotation (1.31° vs. 1.21° is actually MonST3R slightly better on rotation, but Ours dominates on translation and overall trajectory). LEAP-VO and DROID-SLAM register all sequences but produce ATEs of 0.206 m and 0.198 m respectively—nearly 3× worse than Ours. DUSt3R suffers catastrophic rotation errors (20.1° RPE) due to its inability to handle dynamics, while COLMAP and COLMAP+Mask fail to register over half the sequences (44.4% and 38.9%). ParticleSfM, the closest architectural analogue, achieves 97.2% registration but with 2.6× higher ATE and 2.3× higher RPE translation. On Panda-Test, Ours reduces mean reprojection error from 7.59 px (LEAP-VO) and 9.86 px (MonST3R) to 5.76 px, and on the 52 all-succeed sequences achieves 3.75 px vs. 6.03 px (LEAP-VO) and 9.71 px (MonST3R). For the filtering evaluation, the combined specialist+VLM pipeline reaches 0.78 precision at the DynPose-100K operating threshold, surpassing GPT-4o mini alone, Hands23, CamCo reconstructed-points, and B-Timer reprojection-error baselines by a considerable margin on the PR curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ParticleSfM pipeline (static tracking + dynamic masking + global SfM) by upgrading each component to state-of-the-art: BootsTAP for long-term point tracking, a multi-source masking stack (OneFormer, Hands23, RoDynRF, SAM2), and Theia-SfM for global bundle adjustment. It departs from prior dynamic camera pose datasets—which are either small synthetic collections or restricted to narrow domains like egocentric, driving, or turntable captures—by sourcing from the diverse Panda-70M Internet video corpus and introducing a filtering pipeline that combines specialist classifiers with a generalist VLM (GPT-4o mini) to identify the ~9% of videos actually suitable for pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DynPose-100K is the largest public dataset of dynamic Internet video with camera annotations, directly enabling camera-controlled video generation, 4D scene synthesis, and large-scale training of view synthesis and pose estimation models that previously lacked diverse real-world supervision. The Lightspeed photorealistic benchmark fills a gap in ground-truth evaluation for dynamic pose estimation. Open questions include whether the classical SfM-based pipeline can be replaced by learned methods at comparable precision on longer sequences, whether the filtering criteria generalize to non-English or culturally different video content, and whether the 100K scale is sufficient for the data-hungry generative models it aims to support.
