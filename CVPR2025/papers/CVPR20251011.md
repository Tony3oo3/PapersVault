## Problem

Existing post-hoc safe generation methods for text-to-image diffusion models—whether unlearning-based or safety-guidance-based—operate in uninterpretable ways and apply global modifications that cause severe mode shift on multi-concept prompts, making it unclear which intermediate variables drive unsafe generation. The paper asks whether an image-centric framework can perform fine-grained, region-specific erasure of unsafe concepts while preserving safe co-existing concepts and generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt as input and produces a safe generated image at the same resolution as the base Stable Diffusion model (512×512). Internally, it also produces pixel-level cross-attention detection maps at each denoising timestep, computed from U-Net hidden states at 16² and 32² spatial resolutions, which highlight the spatial extent and confidence of unsafe (nude human) regions. The only learned artifact is a small set of guideline token embeddings (initialized from 'nude person'), optimized on just 8 annotated images.

## Method

DAG (Detect-and-Guide) has two stages. First, guideline token embeddings are optimized via a pixel-level cross-entropy loss against ground-truth segmentation masks (from GroundedSAM) on a tiny dataset of 5 unsafe and 3 safe images, incorporating background-leakage and negative-sample losses to ensure the resulting cross-attention maps are specific to the (nude, human) attribute-object pair and generalize across styles and scenes. Second, during sampling, these optimized tokens extract detection maps from high-level U-Net cross-attention layers, which serve as a spatial gate for safety guidance: the guidance is restricted to pixels where the CAM exceeds a 0.01 threshold, and the editing strength is adaptively scaled by both the CAM magnitude (rescaled to [1,5]) and the total detected area (Area_{0.5}), eliminating the need for fixed warm-up steps or manually tuned guidance scales. No model weights are modified, preserving generation diversity.

## Results

On I2P-sexual (931 prompts, 9,840 images), DAG detects 86 total unsafe concepts via NudeNet, reducing the count from 1,070 in undefended SDv1.4 by 92%. On COCO-1K, DAG maintains a VQA Score of 0.72 (vs. 0.70 for SDv1.4) and FID_SDv1.4 of 23.67, indicating minimal mode shift. Under adversarial attacks, DAG achieves 88.14% erase rate on Ring-A-Bell prompts and 77.60% on MMA prompts, competitive with fine-tuning-based methods without any weight modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAG outperforms all guidance-based baselines (SLD-weak/medium/strong, SEGA) on both erasure effectiveness and generation quality: it reduces total unsafe detections from 856/660/207/155 to 86 while keeping FID_SDv1.4 at 23.67 (vs. 23.34–41.14 for SLD and 33.47 for SEGA). Against fine-tuning-based methods, DAG trades a small amount of erasure for substantially better generation quality: ESD-u (Total=63), MACE (56), and AdvUnlearn (10) erase more concepts but degrade VQA Score to 0.63/0.59/0.54 and inflate FID_SDv1.4 to 50.55/51.01/44.56, whereas DAG maintains 0.72 and 23.67. SalUn achieves perfect erasure (Total=0) but catastrophically harms generation (VQA 0.42, FID_SDv1.4 87.68). SDID achieves the lowest FID_SDv1.4 (1.52) but erases fewer concepts (181) than DAG (86). On adversarial robustness (Table 3), DAG's 88.14% erase rate on Ring-A-Bell matches ESD-u (87.74%) and SDID (87.68%), while its 77.60% on MMA trails ESD-u (97.80%) and MACE (99.41%) but exceeds all guidance-based methods (43–67%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DAG sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: token-optimized cross-attention maps for semantic segmentation in diffusion models (Marcos-Manchón et al., CVPR 2024; Yang et al., NeurIPS 2023; Jiang et al., CoMAT) and safety guidance methods that adjust estimated noise during sampling (SLD, SEGA). The key departure is repurposing optimized guideline token embeddings not for post-hoc segmentation but as an *online* detection mechanism during the denoising loop, then using the resulting pixel-level confidence maps to restrict and adaptively scale safety guidance to only the detected unsafe region. This avoids the weight modification of unlearning methods (ESD, SA, MACE, AdvUnlearn) while overcoming the global, hyperparameter-sensitive nature of prior safety guidance approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fine-grained, region-restricted safety guidance can achieve a favorable balance between harmfulness mitigation and text-following on multi-concept prompts—something global guidance and weight-erasure methods struggle with—without any model retraining. However, the evaluation is limited to sexual content on SDv1.4/2.1, and the authors acknowledge that memorized unsafe images (where the sampling trajectory collapses within the first few steps) remain unaddressed. Open questions include generalization to other unsafe concept categories, applicability to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and whether the small-dataset token optimization approach scales to more diverse or fine-grained safety targets.
