## Problem

Slot attention-based methods in object-centric learning treat all image regions uniformly, failing to distinguish foreground objects from background stuff. In complex real-world scenes, low-level color or texture variations in the background mislead slots into over-segmenting background regions while neglecting foreground objects that require shape and geometric detail. This paper addresses the gap by introducing an unsupervised mechanism to explicitly separate foreground from background before slot binding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250920/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (from MOVi-C, PASCAL VOC 2012, and COCO 2017) and produces per-slot object segmentation masks along with foreground/background feature maps. The indicator is a frozen, pre-trained ViT-B/16 (DINO-initialized) network that outputs a dense feature map encoding FG/BG distinctions; this feature map is added to the encoder output of the target slot attention model before decoding. During testing, a spectral clustering step merges slots with high cosine similarity to reduce oversegmentation, with the number of clusters determined by the eigen-gap heuristic.

## Method

The core contribution is a contrastive-learning-based foreground/background indicator using a teacher-student framework with two semantically assigned slots (one for foreground, one for background). Training combines three losses: a pixel-level cross-entropy loss enforcing consistent FG/BG assignments across augmented views (aligned via inverse augmentation), a stuff-level contrastive loss that pulls background prototypes together across the batch while pushing them away from foreground prototypes, and an entropy-based regularization preventing slot collapse. The trained indicator is frozen and its feature map is fused with the original slot attention encoder output via element-wise addition followed by an MLP, then fed into the standard slot attention and decoding pipeline. At test time, a spectral clustering module computes pairwise cosine similarity between slots and merges those belonging to the same semantic object, refining predictions without additional training.

## Results

On MOVi-C, SPOT+FS+RC achieves 49.0 mBO^i and 47.8 mIoU, improving over the SPOT baseline (47.0 / 46.4) by +2.0 and +1.4 respectively. On PASCAL VOC 2012, the same configuration reaches 49.3 mBO^i and 56.5 mBO^c, surpassing SPOT (48.1 / 55.3) by +1.2 on both metrics. On COCO 2017, SPOT+FS+RC attains 35.7 mBO^i and 45.3 mBO^c, a +1.0 gain over SPOT on each metric. DINOSAUR also benefits consistently, with the largest improvement on MOVi-C (+3.6 mBO^i) and PASCAL (+1.7 mBO^c).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is integrated into two SOTA baselines, DINOSAUR and SPOT, and consistently improves both across all three datasets. Against the broader field, SPOT+FS+RC outperforms SlotDiffusion [36] on PASCAL mBO^i (49.3 vs. 50.4 is actually SlotDiffusion winning by 1.1) but leads on COCO mBO^i (35.7 vs. 31.0) and mBO^c (45.3 vs. 35.0). On MOVi-C, SPOT+FS+RC (49.0 mBO^i) surpasses (Stable-)LSD [16] (45.6) and SLATE [29] (39.4) by a wide margin. Slot Attention [23] and SLASH [18] are substantially weaker, with mBO^i values of 26.3 and no PASCAL/COCO results respectively. The most notable finding is that the fusion stage (FS) drives the majority of gains, while region combination (RC) provides a smaller refinement; applying RC alone to the baselines actually degrades performance on PASCAL and COCO, confirming that the foreground-background indicator is essential for the merging to work correctly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the slot attention paradigm (Locatello et al., 2020) and its recent real-world extensions DINOSAUR (Seitzer et al., 2023) and SPOT (Kakogeorgiou et al., 2024), which it treats as the base methods to be augmented. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an explicit, unsupervised foreground/background indicator trained via a DINO-style teacher-student contrastive framework, which prior slot attention methods lack entirely. The indicator's design draws on pixel-level contrastive learning (Pinheiro et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021) and semantic grouping (Wen et al., 2022), while the testing-phase refinement borrows spectral clustering (Von Luxburg, 2007) for slot merging. The overall positioning is that of a plug-in module that can be attached to any existing slot attention pipeline without architectural changes to the base method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-motivated practical limitation: in real-world scenes with complex backgrounds, slot attention methods waste capacity segmenting background texture variations rather than foreground objects, and the proposed indicator provides a lightweight, frozen plug-in that consistently improves multiple SOTA methods across three datasets. However, the indicator's unsupervised nature means it cannot perfectly separate foreground from background, and the spectral clustering step can merge same-category instances, trading mBO^i for mBO^c. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based object-centric learning, whether the fusion strategy (simple addition + MLP) is optimal, and whether the indicator's reliance on a DINO-pretrained ViT backbone limits its applicability to non-transformer encoders. The modest but consistent gains (1–3 mBO points) suggest the FG/BG distinction is a meaningful but not dominant factor in overall segmentation quality.
