## Problem

Existing human motion generation models are restricted to a single input modality (speech, text, or motion) and cannot leverage the full diversity of available motion data or follow both verbal and non-verbal cues simultaneously. While multimodal language models have shown promise in vision and audio, they have not been explored for unifying the verbal and non-verbal language of 3D human motion, leaving a gap in generating expressive, semantically grounded body language from arbitrary combinations of inputs.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any combination of speech audio (16 kHz, tokenized via HubERT at 50 fps), text (SentencePiece with 32K WordPiece vocabulary), and 3D motion (compositional VQ-VAE tokens for face, hands, upper-body, and lower-body) as input, all mapped into a single unified token vocabulary with modality-specific boundary tokens. Output is either a full-body SMPL-X motion sequence (55 joint poses, 100 expression parameters, 3D translation per frame) or free-form text such as an emotion label. The encoder-decoder architecture enforces a maximum input length of 512 tokens, and generation is autoregressive over the unified vocabulary.

## Method

The framework builds on a 220M Flan-T5-Base encoder-decoder transformer, extending its text vocabulary with discrete tokens from four body-part-specific VQ-VAEs (trained with geodesic/ℓ₂ reconstruction, velocity, acceleration, and mesh losses on 6D rotation representations) and HubERT audio tokens. Training proceeds in two stages: first, generative pre-training aligns modalities via compositional motion tasks (spatial: predict one body-part combination from another; temporal: predict unmasked frames from masked ones) and audio-to-text translation, all on unpaired data; second, post-training fine-tunes the full model on 1000+ instruction templates covering audio-to-motion, text-to-motion, and emotion-to-motion tasks. The key insight is that language-model pre-training provides strong semantic understanding of speech content, while the compositional pre-training captures universal spatial-temporal motion priors, enabling strong generalization to new speakers with as little as 1/32 of the paired data.

## Results

On the BEATv2 co-speech gesture generation benchmark, the proposed model achieves FGD of 5.301 (×10⁻¹), BC of 7.780 (×10⁻¹), and Diversity of 15.167, outperforming the strongest baseline EMAGE (FGD 5.512, BC 7.724, Diversity 13.06) on realism and diversity while using only audio as input (no text or style signals). In the data-scarcity experiment, the full model with pre-training achieves substantially lower FGD than both the no-pre-training ablation and EMAGE even when trained on only 1/32 of the paired data. For the novel emotion-prediction-from-motion task, the model achieves BLEU@1 of 14.71, Rouge Cider of 26.67, and BERTScore of 16.94, far exceeding MotionGPT (1.68 / 10.67 / 2.31) and a random baseline (2.45 / 4.44 / 0.19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method outperforms all seven baselines on FGD (realism), achieving 5.301 versus EMAGE's 5.512 (the closest competitor) and TalkSHOW's 6.209, while using only audio input—no transcribed text, style vectors, or facial signals that several baselines require. On Diversity, the method leads by a wide margin (15.167 vs. TalkSHOW's 13.47 and EMAGE's 13.06), indicating more varied and expressive gestures. On Beat Correlation (speech-motion sync), the method scores 7.780, slightly below SynTalker (7.971) and EMAGE (7.724 is actually lower), so it is competitive but not the absolute best on this single metric. Notably, the method achieves these results without any hand-crafted audio features (onsets, amplitudes) or text transcriptions, relying instead on the language model's inherent semantic understanding. In the emotion-prediction task, MotionGPT essentially fails (BLEU@1 of 1.68, near random), while the proposed model achieves 14.71 BLEU@1 and 16.94 BERTScore, demonstrating a clear advantage in understanding subtle body-language cues that general motion captioning models miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional body motion tokenization from EMAGE [43] (dividing the body into face, hands, upper-body, and lower-body with 6D rotations) and the motion-as-foreign-language paradigm from MotionGPT [23] (representing motion as discrete tokens within a language model) into a unified multimodal framework. The key departure is that prior co-speech gesture models (EMAGE, SynTalker, TalkSHOW) accept only speech input, while prior text-to-motion models (MotionGPT, T2M-GPT) accept only text; this work combines both under a single Flan-T5-based encoder-decoder with a shared multimodal vocabulary, adding a generative pre-training stage (compositional spatial/temporal motion alignment plus audio-text alignment) that no prior motion generation work employs. The instruction-following post-training, borrowed from the LLM literature, is repurposed here to enable multi-task motion generation and understanding from a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of verbal (speech, text) and non-verbal (motion) modalities in one model is a practical step toward virtual characters that respond to mixed prompts, and the data-efficiency finding (competitive performance with 1/32 of paired data after pre-training on unpaired audio-text and motion data) is directly relevant to the high cost of motion capture. However, the evaluation is limited to a single speaker on BEATv2 for co-speech generation, the emotion prediction task is relatively simple (8-class label from a fixed set), and the authors themselves note that discrete tokenization sometimes produces incoherent motion, pointing to continuous tokenization as an important open direction. Scaling the framework to multi-speaker, multi-scene, and longer-horizon generation remains unaddressed.
