## Problem

Current MLLMs applied to robotics lack three critical capabilities for long-horizon manipulation: decomposing complex instructions into executable sub-tasks (planning), identifying graspable regions on objects (affordance perception), and anticipating complete end-effector paths (trajectory prediction). This gap is compounded by the absence of large-scale, fine-grained datasets that jointly label planning, affordance, and trajectory information for robotic operation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250431/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at up to 384×384×6×6 resolution (yielding up to 729×37 visual tokens), long video clips (≥30 frames), and natural-language instructions. Outputs are structured hierarchically: a text-based task plan decomposed into sub-task descriptions, affordance regions as axis-aligned bounding boxes {l_x, l_y, r_x, r_y}, and trajectory waypoints as sequences of at least three 2D coordinates normalized to [0, 1000). The planning output feeds into the affordance and trajectory modules, creating a pipeline from abstract instruction to concrete spatial action.

## Method

RoboBrain builds on the LLaVA architecture (SigLIP ViT + 2-layer MLP projector + Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and introduces two LoRA adapters—A-LoRA for affordance perception and T-LoRA for trajectory prediction—trained on top of a shared robotic foundation model. Training follows a five-stage curriculum: projector alignment (LCS-558K), general multimodal pretraining (4M image-text pairs), high-resolution/video instruction tuning (4.8M samples), robotic data fine-tuning (1.3M samples from RoboVQA, ScanView, and ShareRobot mixed with 1.7M general data to prevent catastrophic forgetting), and LoRA-specific fine-tuning on affordance (10K) and trajectory (400K) data. The key dataset contribution, ShareRobot, provides 1,027,990 planning QA pairs, 6,522 affordance-annotated images, and 6,870 trajectory-annotated images across 12 embodiments and 107 atomic task types, all derived from Open X-Embodiment with rigorous quality filtering. The design enables a unified model to transition from abstract instruction comprehension to concrete spatial action prediction within a single forward pass.

## Results

RoboBrain achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three planning benchmarks (OpenEQA, ShareRobot, RoboVQA), exceeding the second-place model by 18.75 BLEU-4 on RoboVQA. On affordance prediction (AGD20K test set), RoboBrain reaches 27.1% AP, surpassing Qwen2-VL-7B (12.5%) by 14.6 AP and LLaVA-NeXT-7B (9.8%) by 17.3 AP. For trajectory prediction, the full model (with start points, max points, and special tokens) reduces Discrete Fréchet Distance by 42.9%, Hausdorff Distance by 94.2%, and RMSE by 31.6% relative to the base fine-tuned model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboBrain outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated task. On the three planning benchmarks (OpenEQA, ShareRobot, RoboVQA), RoboBrain surpasses GPT-4V, Claude3, LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-OneVision-7B, Qwen2-VL-7B, and RoboMamba, with the largest margin on RoboVQA where it leads the second-place model by 18.75 BLEU-4. On affordance prediction, RoboBrain (27.1% AP) more than doubles Qwen2-VL-7B (12.5% AP) and nearly triples LLaVA-NeXT-7B (9.8% AP), indicating substantially stronger physical-object grounding. The paper attributes the planning advantage to RoboBrain's support for long-video input and high-resolution images, which the baselines lack or handle less effectively. No baseline is reported to match or exceed RoboBrain on any metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboBrain extends the LLaVA/LLaVA-OneVision architecture and multi-stage training pipeline into the robotic manipulation domain, where prior MLLM-based approaches (PaLME, RT-H, RoboMamba) have focused primarily on planning or action generation without explicitly modeling affordance perception and trajectory prediction as first-class outputs. The ShareRobot dataset is derived from Open X-Embodiment but departs from its high-level task descriptions by decomposing them into frame-level atomic instructions and adding multi-dimensional labels (planning QA pairs, affordance bounding boxes, 2D trajectory waypoints). The use of separate A-LoRA and T-LoRA modules to specialize the shared backbone for concrete spatial outputs is a novel architectural choice not seen in prior unified robot-MLLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ShareRobot dataset (1M+ QA pairs across 12 embodiments and 107 atomic task types) fills a critical gap in fine-grained, multi-dimensional robotic manipulation data that has limited prior MLLM training. However, several open questions remain: the affordance AP of 27.1% is still modest, trajectory prediction operates in 2D visual space rather than 3D, and all evaluation is benchmark-based without demonstration of closed-loop robotic execution. Whether the unified planning-affordance-trajectory pipeline translates to reliable real-world control, and whether the LoRA-based specialization generalizes to new embodiments or task types beyond the training distribution, are important directions for follow-up work.
