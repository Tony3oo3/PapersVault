## Problem

Existing 3D scene representations for embodied agents suffer from a fundamental trade-off: object-centric scene graphs oversimplify spatial relationships into restrictive textual descriptions, losing nuanced spatial context needed for complex queries, while dense representations (point clouds, neural fields) are computationally expensive and lack scalability during lifelong exploration. Moreover, neither paradigm can model unexplored regions, preventing agents from leveraging scene memory to actively expand their knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of egocentric RGB-D observations with camera poses during exploration, along with a text question or navigation instruction. It produces two types of structured image snapshots: Memory Snapshots (each pairing a selected multi-view image with a cluster of co-visible objects) and Frontier Snapshots (each pairing an image of an unexplored region with a navigable 3D location). A prefiltering step reduces the snapshot set to only those containing VLM-ranked relevant object categories (top-K, default K=10) before feeding images directly into the VLM prompt, typically retaining ~3-5 snapshots from an average of 11-17 total.

## Method

3D-Mem constructs scene memory via Co-Visibility Clustering: objects detected across all observations are hierarchically split (using K-Means on 2D horizontal positions when no single frame contains the full cluster) until each cluster is fully visible in at least one frame candidate, which is then selected as the snapshot image based on maximum object count and detection confidence. Frontier Snapshots extend this by capturing images of unexplored regions from the occupancy map, giving the VLM visual glimpses of potential new information alongside known memory. The entire pipeline operates incrementally—at each exploration step, only newly detected objects and their associated prior snapshots are re-clustered, avoiding full recomputation. A Prefiltering retrieval mechanism queries the VLM for relevant object categories given the task, then discards snapshots lacking those categories, keeping the VLM prompt compact. The VLM (GPT-4o or LLaVA-7B) directly interprets the raw image snapshots to decide whether to explore a frontier or answer the question, leveraging its native visual-spatial reasoning rather than requiring specialized 3D model fine-tuning.

## Results

On A-EQA (184-question subset), 3D-Mem achieves 52.6 LLM-Match and 42.0 LLM-Match SPL, outperforming the strongest VLM exploration baseline (CG w/ Frontier Snapshots) by 5.4 and 8.7 points respectively. On EM-EQA, 3D-Mem reaches 57.2 LLM-Match using only 3.1 average frames, surpassing Multi-Frame (48.1) with a comparable frame budget. On GOAT-Bench (278 subtasks), 3D-Mem with GPT-4o achieves 69.1% Success Rate and 48.9 SPL, exceeding the next-best method (CG w/ Frontier Snapshots) by 7.6 and 3.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-Mem consistently outperforms all baselines across all three benchmarks. Against ConceptGraph (CG w/ Frontier Snapshots), 3D-Mem gains +5.4 LLM-Match and +8.7 SPL on A-EQA, and +7.6 Success Rate and +3.6 SPL on GOAT-Bench, demonstrating that multi-view snapshot images capture richer spatial context than object-level image crops. Against Explore-EQA, the advantage is even larger: +5.7 LLM-Match and +18.6 SPL on A-EQA, and +14.1 SR and +11.0 SPL on GOAT-Bench, attributed to 3D-Mem's persistent scene memory versus Explore-EQA's per-task value maps. Against OpenEQA's Multi-Frame baseline (which uses 75 linearly sampled frames), 3D-Mem achieves +10.8 LLM-Match on A-EQA while using only ~3.3 prefiltered snapshots, highlighting the compactness of co-visibility clustering. On EM-EQA, 3D-Mem (57.2) beats Multi-Frame (48.1) with nearly identical frame counts (3.1 vs 3.0), confirming that snapshot selection matters more than raw frame volume. Against GOAT-Bench's original RNN-based baselines (best: SenseAct-NN Skill Chain at 29.5 SR), 3D-Mem achieves 69.1 SR, a 39.6-point gap, though this comparison is less informative due to fundamentally different paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

3D-Mem builds on two distinct lines of work: the object detection and tracking pipeline from ConceptGraph [8] (segmentation, spatial transformation, matching, merging) and the frontier-based exploration framework from Explore-EQA [28]. Its key departure is in the scene representation itself: rather than encoding explored regions as object-centric 3D scene graphs with textual relationship edges, or as dense point clouds/neural fields, 3D-Mem stores raw multi-view snapshot images selected via a novel co-visibility clustering algorithm. This positions the work at the intersection of 3D scene understanding and VLM-based reasoning, arguing that the visual modality is natively more informative for spatial queries than structured graph or numeric representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact set of well-chosen images can serve as a lifelong 3D scene memory that is both more informative than scene-graph captions and more VLM-compatible than dense 3D modalities, with practical implications for embodied agents operating over extended periods. Limitations include reliance on a simple direct-path navigation strategy, evaluation on limited subsets (184/557 A-EQA questions, 1/10 GOAT-Bench), and dependence on the quality of the underlying VLM. Open questions include how the approach scales to very large or dynamically changing scenes, whether the co-visibility clustering heuristic generalizes beyond indoor environments, and whether the prefiltering mechanism degrades gracefully as memory grows over truly long horizons.
