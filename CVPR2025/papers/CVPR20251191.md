## Problem

Existing radiance-field-based 3D talking head methods require minutes of high-quality video and hours of training per new identity, while few-shot alternatives sacrifice personalization and visual fidelity. The paper asks how to learn a faithful, highly personalized 3D talking head from as little as 5 seconds of video while maintaining real-time inference and high lip-synchronization quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251191/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short video clip (as short as 5 seconds, cropped to 512×512) of the target person for adaptation, plus arbitrary audio (extracted via DeepSpeech), an upper-face expression signal, and camera pose [R, t] at inference time. It produces a lightweight person-specific 3DGS synthesizer that renders photorealistic talking head images at 82.5 FPS. Pre-training consumes 5 long speaking videos (800–8000 frames at 25 FPS) to build the universal motion field before any identity-specific adaptation begins.

## Method

InsTaG decouples universal motion learning from personalization into two stages. First, an Identity-Free Pre-training strategy trains a shared Universal Motion Field (UMF) alongside per-video Personalized Fields (each with its own structure and residual motion field), so that identity-specific appearance and motion are isolated while the UMF captures common audio-driven talking dynamics; a Negative Contrast Loss (truncated dot product on personalized Δμ pairs) enforces diversity between identities to prevent universal knowledge from leaking into personalized fields. Second, a Motion-Aligned Adaptation strategy fine-tunes the UMF to a new identity using only 10,000 iterations: a Motion Aligner predicts a primitive-wise coordinate offset and 3D scale factor to correct structural and amplitude mismatches, a Face-Mouth Hook feeds the face branch's max/min lip displacement and inter-lip distance into the inside-mouth motion field to enforce inter-branch alignment, and a geometry prior regularizer (monocular depth and normal from Sapiens) stabilizes unseen-view rendering. The entire synthesizer is built on 3DGS with face-mouth decomposition and tri-plane hash encoders with region attention, enabling end-to-end deformation-based rendering without expensive 2D-to-3D generation modules.

## Results

With only 5 seconds of training data, InsTaG achieves the best rendering quality across all metrics: PSNR 28.86/26.32 (all/face), LPIPS 0.039/0.026, SSIM 0.861, and LMD 3.167, outperforming all person-specific and few-shot baselines. It also attains the best personalized motion fidelity (AUE-L 0.926, AUE-U 0.313) and a Sync-C of 5.318. Critically, InsTaG is the only method combining top-tier quality with real-time inference (82.5 FPS) and fast adaptation (13 min training), whereas adaptation-based baselines require 7–9 hours and run at 0.03–18 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against person-specific methods trained from scratch (ER-NeRF, TalkingGaussian, GaussianTalker, RAD-NeRF), InsTaG wins on every quality metric (PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM, LMD, AUE) while requiring far less training data (5 s vs. minutes of video) and less training time (13 min vs. 31 min–5 h). Against adaptation/few-shot baselines DFRF and GeneFace, InsTaG improves PSNR by 0.4–1.6 dB, LPIPS by 0.013–0.027, and LMD by 0.27–0.34, while reducing training time from 7–9 hours to 13 minutes and achieving real-time inference (82.5 FPS vs. 0.03–18.1 FPS). Against the concurrent MimicTalk, InsTaG substantially outperforms on rendering quality (PSNR 28.86 vs. 24.69, LPIPS 0.039 vs. 0.075) and personalized motion (AUE-L 0.926 vs. 0.958, AUE-U 0.313 vs. 0.775), and is the only real-time method; however, MimicTalk achieves a higher Sync-C (6.926 vs. 5.318), reflecting its stronger lip-sync confidence at the cost of visual fidelity. Against one-shot methods (EAT, Real3DPortrait), InsTaG dominates on all image-quality and motion metrics (e.g., PSNR 28.86 vs. 22.44/23.58) while those methods only lead on Sync-C due to their large pre-trained backbones. In the cross-domain setting (Table 3), InsTaG with 20 s of data exceeds TalkingGaussian trained on ~1 minute of full data on both Sync-E and Sync-C, confirming the advantage of the identity-free pre-training prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

InsTaG builds directly on the 3DGS-based talking head synthesizer from TalkingGaussian [28] (face-mouth decomposition with structure and motion fields) and the tri-plane hash encoder from ER-NeRF [25], but departs fundamentally from both the person-specific-from-scratch paradigm (RAD-NeRF, ER-NeRF, TalkingGaussian) and the generative few-shot paradigm (DFRF, GeneFace, MimicTalk). Its key conceptual departure is enabling pre-training of a person-specific model in an identity-free manner by isolating universal motion into a shared Universal Motion Field while confining identity-specific appearance and motion to per-video Personalized Fields, then adapting via a lightweight Motion Aligner rather than expensive 2D-to-3D generative modules. This positions the work at the intersection of transfer learning and 3DGS-based talking head synthesis, treating the pre-trained motion field as a reusable prior that directly deforms Gaussians at render time rather than guiding a separate image generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing adaptation data from minutes to 5 seconds and training time from hours to 13 minutes while maintaining or exceeding the quality of full-data person-specific baselines opens the door to real-world deployment where only short clips are available. The identity-free pre-training concept—showing that a person-specific architecture can be meaningfully pre-trained across identities by explicitly separating universal from personalized motion—challenges the assumption that person-specific radiance field models must always be trained from scratch. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to highly diverse populations (extreme facial geometry, occlusions, non-frontal views), whether the pre-training corpus size and diversity further affect adaptation quality, and whether the person-specific adaptation step can be eliminated entirely for true one-shot synthesis without quality loss.
