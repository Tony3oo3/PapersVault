## Problem

Road graph extraction from satellite images is constrained by two bottlenecks: (1) existing graph-labeled datasets (City-Scale, SpaceNet) are small, urban-centric, and geographically limited, hindering generalization evaluation; and (2) the state-of-the-art SAM-Road suffers from a training-inference mismatch because its connectivity classifier is trained on ground-truth node positions but must operate on nodes selected from predicted masks at test time, compounded by occlusion from trees and shadows that breaks road visibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251118/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2048×2048 RGB satellite images at 1 m/pixel resolution (processed as 512×512 patches during training) and produces a vector road graph consisting of node coordinates and pairwise edge connectivity probabilities, alongside per-pixel road and key-point segmentation masks. At inference, nodes are extracted via NMS on the predicted masks (with separate thresholds and radii for road vs. key-point masks), and the connectivity classifier outputs a binary road-existence score for each node pair, which is averaged across redundant predictions to yield the final graph.

## Method

SAM-Road++ extends SAM-Road's two-stage pipeline (SAM encoder/decoder for segmentation, then a connectivity classifier) with two key modifications. First, a node-guided resampling strategy replaces direct use of ground-truth nodes during classifier training: for each ground-truth source node, the target node is re-located to the highest-probability pixel within a small radius (r=8 px) in the predicted road mask, so the classifier sees node positions that mimic the NMS-based selection used at inference. Second, an extended-line strategy augments the classifier input by uniformly sampling road-mask values along short extensions (8 px long, 3 px wide) beyond each node and along the segment between the pair (n=15, m=20 samples), providing contextual evidence that helps the model infer connectivity through occluded regions. Training is supervised end-to-end with binary cross-entropy losses on both the segmentation mask and the topology predictions, and the entire system is trained on the newly curated Global-Scale dataset (3,468 images spanning six continents with urban, rural, and mountainous scenes).

## Results

On the newly introduced Global-Scale dataset, SAM-Road++ achieves F1=62.33 and APLS=62.19 on the in-domain test set, and F1=48.34 and APLS=43.17 on the out-of-domain test set, outperforming all baselines on both splits. On established benchmarks, SAM-Road++ reaches F1=80.01 (City-Scale) and F1=81.57 (SpaceNet), with APLS of 68.34 and 73.44 respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on both. Pre-training on Global-Scale further boosts performance (e.g., F1=80.66 on City-Scale, F1=82.07 on SpaceNet), confirming the dataset's utility for transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM-Road++ consistently outperforms the direct predecessor SAM-Road [17] on F1 and APLS across all three datasets (e.g., F1 62.33 vs 59.80 and APLS 62.19 vs 59.08 on Global-Scale in-domain), though it trades a small amount of precision (88.95 vs 91.93) for substantially higher recall (49.27 vs 45.64). Against the iterative baselines RNGDet [49] and RNGDet++ [50], SAM-Road++ leads by 7–10 points in F1 and 10–13 points in APLS on Global-Scale, confirming the advantage of global-based prediction over point-by-point iteration. The segmentation-based SegFormer [47] and graph-tensor Sat2Graph [14] baselines perform markedly worse on Global-Scale (F1 42.49 and 35.53 respectively), likely due to their reliance on fixed post-processing pipelines that generalize poorly across diverse road types. On the out-of-domain test set (unseen cities), SAM-Road++ maintains its lead over all methods, with a 1.7-point F1 advantage over SAM-Road and a 2.6-point APLS advantage, demonstrating stronger generalization to unseen geographic regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct extension of SAM-Road [17], the first method to leverage the Segment Anything Model (SAM) foundation model for end-to-end road graph extraction. It departs from SAM-Road by introducing node-guided resampling to resolve the training-inference mismatch (where the connectivity classifier is trained on ground-truth nodes but must operate on predicted-mask nodes at inference) and an extended-line strategy to handle occlusion. In the broader landscape, it positions itself against iterative methods (RoadTracer, RNGDet, RNGDet++) that suffer from error accumulation, and against other global-based methods (Sat2Graph) that rely on graph-tensor encoding with limited generalization. The Global-Scale dataset extends the geographic and scene diversity beyond the urban-centric City-Scale and SpaceNet benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Global-Scale dataset (~20× larger than prior public road extraction datasets, covering all continents except Antarctica with urban, rural, and mountainous scenes) addresses a critical data bottleneck and introduces an out-of-domain evaluation protocol that better reflects real-world deployment. The node-guided resampling strategy is a principled fix for a mismatch that affects all global-based road graph methods, and the extended-line heuristic offers a simple, interpretable way to handle occlusion. Open questions remain: reliance on OpenStreetMap for ground truth introduces region-dependent annotation quality, the single-overhead-view limitation is only partially mitigated, and whether the extended-line strategy generalizes to more complex occlusion patterns (e.g., dense urban canyons) is untested.
