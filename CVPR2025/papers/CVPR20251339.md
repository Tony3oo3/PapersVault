## Problem

While unified image comprehension and generation in LLMs has advanced rapidly, extending this unification to video remains under-explored. The core challenge is designing a video tokenizer that captures both spatial characteristics and temporal dynamics in continuous representations suitable for LLM comprehension, while also being decodable into realistic video clips for generation. Prior approaches relying on discrete video tokens sacrifice multimodal understanding performance, motivating a continuous-representation alternative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251339/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For comprehension, the model takes a video clip (sampled as 5 sparse frames at 2 fps per 2-second segment, up to 20 segments per video) plus a text query, producing a text response. For generation, it takes a text prompt and produces a 16-frame video at 256×256 resolution. The tokenizer compresses each 2-second clip into 64 continuous video tokens via a Perceiver Resampler, and for storytelling the output is an interleaved sequence of narrative text and corresponding video clips.

## Method

Divot is a video tokenizer (ViT encoder + spatial-temporal transformer + Perceiver Resampler) trained self-supervisedly via a diffusion criterion: a U-Net (adapted from DynamiCrafter) is trained to denoise VAE latents of densely sampled frames conditioned on the tokenizer's features through cross-attention, so the tokenizer must encode sufficient spatiotemporal information for successful denoising. The same U-Net then serves as a de-tokenizer, decoding realistic video clips from the learned representations. Divot-LLM integrates this tokenizer with Mistral-7B, using next-word prediction for comprehension and, for generation, a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to model the distribution of continuous video features—predicting 2kd+k parameters per token via learnable queries with bidirectional attention and NLL loss—rather than deterministic MSE regression, which the authors show produces overly averaged and repetitive outputs.

## Results

On video comprehension, Divot-LLM (7B) achieves 46.5 on EgoSchema, 58.3 on Perception-Test, 52.1 on MVBench, 76.4 on MSVD, and 55.8 on ActivityNet-QA, outperforming all other 7B unified models that also support video generation. On zero-shot text-to-video generation (MSR-VTT), Divot-LLM achieves CLIPSIM 0.2938 and FVD 301.4 using only 4.8M video-text pairs, competitive with dedicated video generation models trained on 10–270M samples. The model uses significantly fewer training pairs (4.8M) than VideoLLaMA2 (100M) while achieving competitive comprehension scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 7B unified models (those that can both comprehend and generate video), Divot-LLM clearly outperforms Video-LaVIT (46.5 vs 37.3 on EgoSchema, 58.3 vs 47.9 on Perception-Test, 76.4 vs 73.2 on MSVD, 55.8 vs 50.1 on ActivityNet) and VILA-U (76.4 vs 75.3 on MSVD, 55.8 vs 52.7 on ActivityNet). Against comprehension-only 7B models, Divot-LLM trades some EgoSchema accuracy (46.5 vs 51.7 for VideoLLaMA2) and MVBench (52.1 vs 54.6) for substantially better MSVD (76.4 vs 70.9) and ActivityNet (55.8 vs 50.2), while using only 4.8M training pairs versus 100M. On text-to-video generation, Divot-LLM achieves FVD 301.4, beating VILA-U (499.06) by a wide margin and Loong (274) slightly, though Video-LaVIT achieves a lower FVD of 188.36 with 10M training samples. On CLIPSIM, Divot-LLM (0.2938) is essentially tied with VILA-U (0.2937) and slightly below Video-LaVIT (0.3012), while matching or exceeding dedicated generators like CogVideo (0.2631) and Loong (0.2903) with far less data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of unified multimodal LLMs (Seed-X, Chameleon, Janus) from images to video, but deliberately departs from the discrete-tokenizer paradigm adopted by LWM, VILAU, Video-LaVIT, and VILA-U, which the authors argue degrades comprehension quality. The core methodological novelty is using a video diffusion model (adapted from DynamiCrafter) as a self-supervised training signal for the tokenizer, drawing on the diffusion-for-representation-learning idea from SODA and Diffusion Feedback but applying it to video for the first time. For the generation side, the GMM-based modeling of continuous video features is inspired by MAR's probabilistic approach to image generation without VQ, but adapted to handle high-level spatiotemporal tokens where the authors find diffusion-based denoising underperforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that continuous video representations learned via a diffusion self-supervised objective can support both strong comprehension and competitive generation within a single 7B LLM, using only 4.8M video-text pairs—far fewer than dedicated video-LLMs. The practical finding that GMM modeling outperforms both MSE regression and diffusion modeling for high-level video features provides actionable guidance for future unified video-LLM designs. Key limitations include generating only 2-second, 16-frame clips at 256×256 resolution, and the reliance on a frozen VAE and pre-trained U-Net that constrain the achievable video quality and length. Open questions include whether the diffusion-tokenizer paradigm scales to longer, higher-resolution videos and whether the GMM approach generalizes beyond the specific Perceiver Resampler architecture.
