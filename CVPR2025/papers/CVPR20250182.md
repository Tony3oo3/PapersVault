## Problem

Existing adversarial patch defenses struggle to generalize to unseen patches, unseen datasets, and unseen natural corruptions simultaneously, and none provide interpretable explanations for their detection decisions. The paper identifies that no prior defense combines high generalizability with explainability, and that aligning machine detection with human visual perception is essential to reduce false rejections of legitimate objects that resemble patches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250182/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (clean or adversarially patched) that are augmented via two sequential TrivialAugment stages—spatial transformations followed by grayscale conversion—before being fed into a pre-trained backbone (ResNet50, ConvNeXt-tiny, MobileNetV2, or ViT) whose final stride is modified to yield finer-grained 28×28, 26×26, or 14×14 feature maps. Beyond the binary real/patched label, the model outputs a D-dimensional prototype presence score vector p ∈ [0,1]^D (obtained by max-pooling each feature map) and, for explainability, identifies which specific prototypes are activated in the image along with per-prototype Grad-CAM heatmaps. The sparse linear layer's unnormalized scores double as interpretable class-evidence metrics.

## Method

AdvPatchXAI introduces a two-phase training procedure built on a prototypical-parts framework. In the self-supervised pretraining phase, a novel patch decorrelation loss (L_PD) diagonalizes the cross-correlation matrix of patch-level features across two augmented views, reducing redundancy and promoting distinctiveness at the spatial patch level rather than the image level; this is combined with a softmax alignment loss (L_A) and a tanh diversity loss (L_T) to prevent trivial solutions. In the supervised phase, a sparse linear layer with strictly non-negative weights maps the D prototype presence scores to class scores, so each class's evidence is a transparent weighted sum of present prototypes, enabling both global interpretability (which prototypes define each class) and local explainability (which prototypes fired for a given image). The architecture is backbone-agnostic, plugging into any CNN or ViT, and the grayscale augmentation strategy suppresses color bias to improve generalization across unseen patch styles and datasets.

## Results

On COCO (seen dataset, unseen patches), AdvPatchXAI-R (ResNet50 backbone) achieves the highest silent detection accuracy, peaking at 99.33% (Patch-9) with a mean of ~91.8% across all 10 unseen patches. Under noise perturbation (Gaussian, Shot, Impulse at severity 2), AdvPatchXAI-ViT is the most resilient, dropping only from 89.6% to 86.02% on COCO and 87.65% to 83.97% on ImageNet, compared to 7.5–20% drops for other backbones. On ImageNet (unseen dataset, unseen patches), AdvPatchXAI-R reaches 98.94% (Patch-9, silent) and AdvPatchXAI-ViT maintains 92.95% (Patch-4, noisy). The authors recommend AdvPatchXAI-ViT with Patch-4 as the best general-purpose configuration for real-world deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ojaswee et al. (ICCV 2023), AdvPatchXAI-R outperforms by 13.35% on COCO and 12.03% on ImageNet in the silent unseen-patch setting; in the noisy setting, AdvPatchXAI-ViT surpasses it by 13.64% (COCO) and 13.33% (ImageNet). Against Kumar & Agarwal (SSRN 2023), the margins are 11.9% (COCO) and 14.01% (ImageNet) for AdvPatchXAI-R in silent, and 12.22% (COCO) and 12.67% (ImageNet) for AdvPatchXAI-ViT in noisy. Against PIP-Net (the closest architectural baseline), AdvPatchXAI improves by 4.73% (ResNet50), 6.9% (MobileNetV2), and 0.28% (ViT) in the silent unseen-patch setting on COCO, with the largest gain coming from the novel patch decorrelation loss and grayscale augmentation. The paper also reports physical-world (printed patch) accuracy of 91.11% (Patch-0) and 88.88% (Patch-4) with the ViT backbone, and robustness against PGD-based adaptive attacks at 98.04% (R) and 95.67% (ViT) in silent, 95.55% and 93.05% in noisy settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PIP-Net [41], the closest architectural predecessor, by introducing a patch-level decorrelation loss (L_PD) that diagonalizes the cross-correlation matrix of backbone feature maps, going beyond PIP-Net's image-level alignment. It also departs from prior adversarial patch detection work (Ojaswee et al. [43], Kumar & Agarwal [31], Pintor et al. [44]) by integrating explainability natively through a sparse non-negative linear layer connecting learned prototypes to classes, rather than treating interpretability as a post-hoc module. The two-stage TrivialAugment strategy (spatial then grayscale color) and the evaluation against natural noise corruptions represent practical extensions not present in the prior benchmarking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first adversarial patch defense to simultaneously provide zero-shot generalization to unseen patches and datasets, robustness to unseen natural noise corruptions, and built-in explainability via interpretable prototypes, addressing a gap the authors explicitly identify in the literature. The finding that grayscale conversion outperforms RGB and YCbCr by over 14% challenges the assumption that color information aids patch detection, suggesting that suppressing non-essential channels improves generalization. Limitations include the binary (patch vs. clean) formulation, evaluation on only 10 patch styles, and the modest ViT improvement over PIP-Net (0.28%), leaving open whether the approach scales to multi-class detection, video streams, or more diverse physical-world perturbations beyond the three noise types tested.
