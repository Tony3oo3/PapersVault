## Problem

Existing mesh tokenization methods (MeshXL, MeshGPT, MeshAnythingV2, EdgeRunner) produce sequences too long to accommodate high-poly meshes beyond ~4k faces, restricting training data to low-detail geometry and limiting both the richness and robustness of generated meshes. No prior tokenization achieves sufficient compression to unlock the large pool of high-poly mesh data available in modern 3D asset libraries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251699/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a 4096-point cloud (randomly sampled from a 50k-point set) or an RGB image (encoded via a pre-trained DINOv2 backbone) as conditioning, and autoregressively generates a 1D token sequence that decodes into a 3D triangle mesh with up to 8k+ faces. Coordinates are discretized at 7-bit resolution (128 levels per axis), and the transformer operates within a 9600-token context window. For image conditioning, an intermediate DiT diffusion model first synthesizes point-cloud latent features from DINO image features before the mesh decoder consumes them.

## Method

BPT compresses mesh sequences via two complementary mechanisms: (1) block-wise indexing partitions the 3D coordinate space into B×B×B blocks, representing each vertex with a block index plus an intra-block offset (at most 2 tokens vs. 3 for raw coordinates), with adjacent same-block vertices sharing a single block index; (2) patchified aggregation groups all faces incident to the highest-degree unvisited vertex into a single patch, eliminating repeated vertex tokens (average 6 occurrences reduced to 1) and using dual block-index vocabularies to mark patch boundaries without special tokens. Together these yield ~75% sequence-length reduction over MeshXL's vanilla tokenization. The generative model is a 24-layer autoregressive Transformer (hidden dim 1024) trained with cross-attention conditioning on point-cloud or image features, first pre-trained on ~1.5M meshes and fine-tuned on 0.3M high-quality meshes, with a 7-day training run on 384 L40 GPUs.

## Results

On point-cloud-to-mesh generation, BPT achieves a Hausdorff Distance of 0.166 and Chamfer Distance of 0.094, substantially outperforming MeshAnything (HD 0.301, CD 0.136) and MeshAnythingV2 (HD 0.265, CD 0.114). In terms of tokenization efficiency, BPT achieves a compression ratio of 0.74, compared to 0.00 for MeshXL/MeshAnything, 0.33 for MeshGPT/PivotMesh, and 0.54 for MeshAnythingV2. The model generates meshes up to 8k faces, roughly double the maximum face count of prior methods (4k), enabling significantly richer geometric detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPT decisively outperforms both MeshAnything and MeshAnythingV2 on point-cloud-to-mesh generation, reducing Hausdorff Distance by 45% (0.301→0.166) and 37% (0.265→0.166) respectively, with corresponding Chamfer Distance improvements of 31% and 18%. Against EdgeRunner, the comparison is qualitative (image-to-mesh), where BPT produces meshes with more intricate details that more faithfully follow the input image, though no quantitative metrics are reported due to EdgeRunner's lack of public code. In the tokenization compression comparison (Table 1), BPT's 0.74 ratio nearly doubles the best prior method (MeshAnythingV2 at 0.54) and is more than twice that of MeshGPT/PivotMesh (0.33). The ablation further confirms that the performance gains stem from the ability to train on high-poly meshes (up to 8k faces) rather than architectural changes, as the same Transformer backbone trained on fewer faces degrades significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive mesh generation line initiated by MeshXL, which tokenizes meshes as sorted coordinate triplets (9 tokens per face). Prior compression efforts addressed either vertex-level redundancy (MeshGPT, PivotMesh via auto-encoders) or face-level redundancy (MeshAnythingV2, EdgeRunner via improved traversal), but BPT is the first to combine both: block-wise indexing compresses vertex coordinates while patch aggregation eliminates repeated vertices shared across adjacent faces. For the conditioning pipeline, it builds on Michelangelo's architecture for the point-cloud encoder and image-to-mesh diffusion alignment, repurposing DINO features as image conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75% sequence compression is practically important because it unlocks training on meshes exceeding 8k faces—previously infeasible for autoregressive transformers with fixed context windows—enabling a qualitative jump in detail fidelity. The controlled scaling experiments (1600→8000 faces) provide clear evidence that high-poly training data is genuinely valuable rather than merely filling context, and the finding that truncated training with sliding-window attention degrades robustness cautions against a common engineering shortcut. Open questions include whether the 500M parameter scale is sufficient to fully exploit BPT's compression, whether topology guarantees can be integrated natively, and whether alternative sequence-modeling architectures (e.g., non-autoregressive) could further leverage BPT's spatial locality.
