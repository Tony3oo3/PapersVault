## Problem

Existing monocular 3D human reconstruction methods rely on SMPL-X priors or generative models that capture only general body geometry, overlooking multi-level structures such as skeleton joints and fine details like finger wrinkles and facial expressions. This oversimplified modeling produces inaccurate skeletal reconstructions, incorrect joint positions, and unclear clothing wrinkles, particularly in occluded or fine-grained regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single front-view RGB image and an estimated SMPL-X body mesh (not ground-truth) as input, and produces a textured 3D human mesh via an intermediate 3D Gaussian representation (14 parameters per Gaussian: 3D position, scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and RGB color). During training, the model is supervised on 8 rendered views (1 input + 7 novel views) against GT scans; during inference, the SMPL-X prior is estimated from the single image, introducing depth ambiguities that the method must correct. The final mesh is refined through a diffusion-based normal-map prediction followed by iterative differentiable remeshing.

## Method

MultiGO builds on the LGM Gaussian reconstruction backbone and introduces three multi-level geometry components. The Skeleton-Level Enhancement (SLE) module projects q-order 3D Fourier features of SMPL-X mesh vertices from three orthogonal camera angles into the 2D image plane, then fuses these projected geometric features with 2D image latents via a multimodal UNet with self-attention to predict per-pixel 3D Gaussians. The Joint-Level Augmentation (JLA) strategy perturbs depth-related SMPL-X parameters with uniform noise during training, forcing the model to learn correction from image cues and become robust to depth estimation errors at inference. The Wrinkle-Level Refinement (WLR) module treats the coarse exported mesh as a 'noisy' version of the GT mesh, conditions a fine-tuned latent diffusion UNet on CLIP-encoded Gaussian-rendered textures, and predicts refined normal maps in k denoising steps; these normal maps then drive continuous differentiable remeshing to recover fine geometric details like wrinkles and facial features.

## Results

On CustomHuman, MultiGO achieves a Chamfer Distance of 1.620/1.782 cm (P-to-S/S-to-P), Normal Consistency of 0.850, and f-score of 42.425, outperforming the second-best method SiTH (1.800/2.188, 0.816, 36.148) by 0.180/0.406 cm on CD, 0.034 on NC, and 6.277 on f-score. On THuman3.0, the gains over SiTH are 0.355/0.369 cm on CD, 0.047 on NC, and 9.861 on f-score. For texture quality, MultiGO achieves PSNR of 22.347/20.849 (F/B) on CustomHuman and 23.794/22.657 (F/B) on THuman3.0, surpassing the best prior method (GTA) by over 3 dB on PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiGO outperforms all eight baselines on every geometry metric (CD, NC, f-score) across both CustomHuman and THuman3.0. The closest competitor is SiTH (CVPR'24), which leverages a 2D diffusion prior; MultiGO improves over SiTH by 0.180 cm on P-to-S CD and 6.277 on f-score (CustomHuman), and by 0.355 cm on P-to-S CD and 9.861 on f-score (THuman3.0). Among earlier methods, ECON (CVPR'23) is the strongest on CustomHuman geometry (CD 2.192/2.342), but MultiGO still leads by 0.572/0.560 cm on CD. On texture metrics (Table 2), MultiGO achieves the best LPIPS (0.0414/0.0643 F/B on CustomHuman), SSIM (0.9603/0.9415), and PSNR (22.347/20.849), beating GTA (NeurIPS'23) by 3.56 dB PSNR on CustomHuman and 2.68 dB on THuman3.0. The paper notes that all comparisons use estimated (not ground-truth) SMPL/SMPL-X priors, making the results more realistic for practical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiGO builds on the LGM large multi-view Gaussian reconstruction model [30] as its base architecture, adapting it from general 3D objects to monocular human reconstruction. It departs from prior monocular human reconstruction methods (PIFu, ICON, ECON, GTA, VS, HiLo, SiTH, SiFU) that treat body geometry as a monolithic problem by introducing a three-level decomposition (skeleton, joint, wrinkle), each addressed by a dedicated module. The SLE module's key architectural choice—projecting 3D SMPL-X Fourier features into 2D image space rather than directly fusing heterogeneous 3D/2D features—contrasts with prior work that concatenates or cross-attends 3D shape encodings with 2D image features. The WLR module repurposes the latent diffusion de-noising paradigm as a fixed-step refinement operator for mesh normals, conditioned on Gaussian-rendered textures, which is a novel application of diffusion theory to geometric detail enhancement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-level geometry decomposition provides a principled framework for diagnosing and addressing different failure modes in monocular human reconstruction (posture errors, joint depth errors, missing fine details), which prior methods addressed only implicitly. The JLA strategy addresses a practical and underexplored problem—depth estimation errors in single-view SMPL-X fitting—through a simple but effective training-time augmentation, making the pipeline more robust to real-world inference conditions. Limitations include continued dependence on SMPL-X estimation quality, the additional computational cost of the WLR diffusion refinement and continuous remeshing, and evaluation on relatively small datasets (CustomHuman, THuman3.0). Open questions include whether the multi-level framework generalizes to other articulated objects (animals, robots) and whether the diffusion-based wrinkle refinement can be made faster or replaced by a single-step predictor.
