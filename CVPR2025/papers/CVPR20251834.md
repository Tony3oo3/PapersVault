## Problem

Vision Graph Neural Networks (ViG) suffer from quadratic computational complexity in KNN graph construction and cannot capture high-order correlations among more than two nodes due to their pairwise graph structure. The follow-up ViHGNN addresses high-order relations via hypergraphs but still relies on Fuzzy C-means for global hypergraph construction (retaining quadratic complexity) and lacks dynamic adaptability between hypergraph structure and convolution during learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251834/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet classification, 1280×800 for COCO detection/segmentation, 2048×512 for ADE20K segmentation), which are split into N patches and linearly projected into D-dimensional vertex embeddings. Depending on the downstream head, it outputs a 1000-way class label, object bounding boxes with class scores, pixel-level instance masks, or dense semantic segmentation maps. The hypergraph is constructed per region (m subregions per stage, analogous to Swin's window partitioning) rather than globally, which bounds per-block complexity to O(NCD/m).

## Method

DVHGNN builds a multi-scale hypergraph per block via two complementary mechanisms: (1) cosine-similarity clustering that assigns each patch vertex to one of C learned centroids, forming C global semantic hyperedges, and (2) Dilated HyperGraph Construction (DHGC) that, for each w×w window center, generates R dilated hyperedges at dilation rates r=1,…,R (e.g., 3×3, 5×5, 7×7 receptive fields) with learnable sparsity-aware weights. A two-stage Dynamic Hypergraph Convolution (DHConv) then performs message passing: Vertex Convolution aggregates vertex features into hyperedge features using cosine-similarity gating (for clustering hyperedges) or the dilation-rate weight (for DHGC hyperedges), and Hyperedge Convolution distributes the resulting hyperedge features back to each vertex via a GIN-style update with a learnable residual coefficient. Multi-head computation (h heads per stage) produces h independent hypergraphs to enrich feature diversity, and a four-stage pyramid with ConvFFN blocks provides hierarchical downsampling while mitigating over-smoothing.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224, 300 epochs), DVHGNN-S achieves 83.1% Top-1 accuracy with 30.2M parameters and 5.2G FLOPs, surpassing ViG-S (82.1%) by +1.0% and PViHGNN-S (82.5%) by +0.6% while using 18% fewer FLOPs than PViHGNN-S. On COCO with RetinaNet, DVHGNN-S reaches 43.3% AP (vs. 41.8% for ViG-S and 42.2% for PViHGNN-S), and with Mask R-CNN achieves 44.8% box AP and 40.2% mask AP. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, DVHGNN-S attains 43.8% mIoU with Semantic FPN and 46.8% mIoU with UperNet, outperforming Swin-T by 2.3% in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among GNN/HGNN-based backbones, DVHGNN-S clearly outperforms both ViG-S (+1.0% Top-1) and PViHGNN-S (+0.6% Top-1) on ImageNet-1K, and extends this lead to COCO detection (+1.5% AP over ViG-S, +1.1% over PViHGNN-S with RetinaNet) and segmentation (+1.7% box AP over PViHGNN-S with Mask R-CNN). Against Transformer-based baselines, DVHGNN-S matches Swin-S (83.1% vs. 83.0%) with fewer FLOPs (5.2G vs. 8.7G), but trails InceptionNeXt-S (83.5%), VMamba-M (83.5%), and ClusterFormer-S (83.4%) by 0.3–0.4%. At the larger B-size, DVHGNN-B (84.2%) surpasses PViHGNN-B (83.9%) and matches ViG-B (83.7%) while using fewer FLOPs (16.8G vs. 18.1G). On ADE20K, DVHGNN-S leads all compared backbones in both S-FPN (43.8% vs. 43.1% InceptionNeXt-T) and UperNet (46.8% vs. 46.1% ConvNeXt-T) settings, with the largest margin over Swin-T (+2.3% mIoU). Overall, DVHGNN is most competitive in the GNN/HGNN family and achieves top-tier results among efficient backbones, though it does not surpass the strongest CNN and SSM baselines at the same parameter scale on ImageNet classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVHGNN extends the Vision GNN (ViG) and Vision HGNN (ViHGNN) line of work, which progressively moved from KNN-based pairwise graphs to Fuzzy C-means-based hypergraphs for image representation. The key departure is replacing both KNN and Fuzzy C-means with a dual-path multi-scale construction: cosine-similarity clustering for global semantic grouping and Dilated HyperGraph Construction (DHGC) for local multi-scale spatial structure, drawing on the dilated convolution concept from CNNs. The Dynamic Hypergraph Convolution adapts the two-stage vertex/hyperedge message passing from Jiang et al. (IJCAI 2019) but introduces learnable cosine-similarity and sparsity-aware weighting to make the hypergraph structure end-to-end trainable, addressing ViHGNN's non-learnable Fuzzy C-means bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practically efficient alternative to quadratic-complexity graph construction in vision backbones, achieving competitive ImageNet accuracy with ~18% fewer FLOPs than ViHGNN-S, which is meaningful for deployment. The dilated hypergraph construction provides a structured, interpretable way to capture multi-scale spatial relationships that neither KNN nor global clustering alone can express. However, the gains over the most direct baselines (ViG, ViHGNN) are modest (0.6–1.5%), and the model does not clearly outperform strong CNN/Transformer backbones like ConvNeXt or Swin at matched scale, leaving open whether the hypergraph formulation offers a fundamental representational advantage or primarily an efficiency trade-off. The authors note future work on scalability to additional vision tasks, but the current evaluation is limited to standard classification, detection, and segmentation benchmarks.
