## Problem

Existing vectorized HD map construction methods achieve strong performance on standard benchmarks but suffer significant degradation on geographically disjoint data splits, as they tend to memorize training scenes rather than learn generalizable road structures. Additionally, the implicit PV-to-BEV transformation inevitably introduces geometric errors and loses texture details, further limiting robustness in unfamiliar driving environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250816/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surrounding-view RGB images (480×800 on nuScenes, 608×608 on Argoverse2) sampled at 2 Hz, along with camera intrinsic matrices and ego-to-camera extrinsic transformations needed for Inverse Perspective Mapping. It outputs vectorized map elements (lane dividers, pedestrian crossings, road boundaries) as ordered point sequences with class labels within a 30 m × 60 m BEV perception range, accompanied by per-point Laplace uncertainty parameters (σ_x, σ_y) that quantify localization confidence. During inference, a lightweight mimic query replaces the full PV detection branch, reducing FPS cost while preserving structural compensation.

## Method

UIGenMap introduces a pre-trained PV detection branch whose outputs are transformed into BEV-space prompts via IPM, then injected into the main BEV decoder through a hybrid mechanism combining prompt-to-BEV cross-attention (enriching spatial features) and prompt-to-query cross-attention (seeding instance queries). Both PV and BEV decoders employ a UA-Decoder that replaces deterministic deformable attention weights with Gaussian-distributed probabilistic weights via the reparameterization trick, and adds a point-level UA-Head predicting Laplace-distributed coordinates with per-point uncertainty. PV uncertainties serve dual roles: as normalized inverse-variance weights for dynamic feature strengthening and as learned embeddings within the prompt representation. For real-time inference, a Mimic Query Distillation module trains lightweight queries to replicate PV prompts via MSE loss, eliminating the need for the PV branch at test time while retaining structural compensation.

## Results

On the geographically disjoint (geo-based) nuScenes splits with ResNet50, UIGenMap achieves 39.8 mAP on the region-based partition (+5.7 over the best prior method) and 23.6 mAP on the city-based partition (+4.3), substantially outperforming all baselines. On Argoverse2, UIGenMap reaches 60.4 mAP (region-based) and 44.8 mAP (city-based), again leading all compared methods. On the original (non-disjoint) partitions, UIGenMap attains 64.8 mAP on nuScenes and 63.8 mAP on Argoverse2, confirming gains beyond the generalization setting. The lightweight distilled variant (UIGenMap-d) retains most of the improvement (39.3 mAP region-based) while running at 12.2 FPS versus 8.2 FPS for the full model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UIGenMap outperforms all seven baselines on both geo-based partitions. Against the strongest competitor StreamMapNet [50], UIGenMap gains +5.7 mAP on the nuScenes region-based split (39.8 vs. 34.1) and +4.3 mAP on the city-based split (23.6 vs. 19.3); on Argoverse2 the margins are +2.1 and +2.5 mAP respectively. MapTRv2 [26], the next-best non-streaming method, trails by 10.9 mAP (region-based) and 1.8 mAP (city-based) on nuScenes. Earlier methods—VectorMapNet [29], MapTR [25], MGMap [28], and MapQR [31]—are outperformed by 15+ mAP on the region-based partition, reflecting their limited robustness under geographic distribution shift. On the original (non-disjoint) partitions (Table 3), UIGenMap still leads SQD-MapNet [43] by +0.9 mAP on nuScenes (64.8 vs. 63.9) and +0.5 mAP on Argoverse2 (63.8 vs. 63.3), confirming that the uncertainty-instructed injection provides gains even when train/test scenes overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BEVFormer-style multi-camera-to-BEV pipeline and the MapTR paradigm of representing map elements as ordered point sequences with a transformer decoder. Its key departure is the systematic introduction of uncertainty-aware components (Gaussian-distributed attention weights and Laplace-distributed point outputs) into both the PV and BEV decoders, combined with explicit 2D structural priors from a pre-trained PV detection branch injected via hybrid cross-attention. The MQ-Distillation module draws on knowledge distillation to replace the PV branch at inference, a technique borrowed from 3D detection (e.g., BEVFormer-v2, SimMoD) but repurposed here for map construction efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that prior SOTA vectorized map methods suffer substantial degradation (up to ~25 mAP) when evaluated on geographically disjoint splits, exposing a previously underappreciated overfitting-to-geography problem. The proposed geo-based evaluation protocol (region-based and city-based) is a useful community benchmark for measuring true generalization. Limitations include reliance on a pre-trained PV detector during training, evaluation on only two datasets, and the added architectural complexity; open questions include whether the uncertainty-instructed injection strategy transfers to other BEV perception tasks and whether larger, more geographically diverse datasets would further validate the approach.
