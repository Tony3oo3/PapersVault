## Problem

Fast 3D clothed human reconstruction from monocular video remains unsolved: pixel-aligned methods are too computationally intensive for video, while video-based methods require minutes-to-hours of per-subject optimization. The specific challenge of learning a consistent canonical shape through sequential frame-by-frame processing—exploiting temporal coherency without global optimization—remains largely unexplored in existing literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251516/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular RGB video frames at 256³ canonical resolution, with SMPL shape and pose parameters regressed per-frame via PyMAF. It outputs a textured 3D mesh in both canonical and posed space, with vertex-level color. The pipeline is plug-and-play: any pre-trained SMPL-guided pixel-aligned backbone (e.g., GTA, SIFU) can be swapped in without retraining, and the framework naturally extends to multi-view by processing orthogonal views (0°, 120°, 240°) independently and merging their canonical representations.

## Method

TemPoFast3D performs shape inference in canonical space rather than posed space, establishing bidirectional coordinate mapping between canonical and posed coordinates via SMPL's Linear Blend Skinning matrices transferred to arbitrary query points through K-nearest-neighbor association. For the first n=5 frames, full coarse-to-fine reconstruction establishes a reliable canonical shape; thereafter, the pipeline bypasses hierarchical inference and instead refines only visible, surface-adjacent regions (occupancy band α=0.4 to β=0.7) using visibility masks propagated from rasterized SMPL meshes. Occluded texture is recovered via neighbor-based color propagation in canonical space, matching current canonical vertices to previous-frame counterparts. Volumetric boundary filtering eliminates irrelevant query points outside a proximal region around the canonical SMPL mesh, and TorchScript compilation yields a 3.06× speedup over the baseline, reaching 12.3 FPS on an RTX 4090.

## Results

On the NeuMan video dataset, TPF3D-GTA achieves 27.60 PSNR, 0.965 SSIM, and 0.022 LPIPS at 8.9 FPS with no per-subject optimization (pretrained weights only), compared to HumanNeRF's 27.06 PSNR at 0.54 FPS after 26.5 hours of training. On single-image benchmarks, the multi-view variant TPF3D-SIFU-3v achieves the best Chamfer distance of 0.4144 on THuman2.0, outperforming re-evaluated SIFU† (0.4409) and GTA† (0.4625). The ablation study shows the full optimization pipeline reaches a maximum of 12.3 FPS (3.06× speedup over the 3.27 FPS baseline) while maintaining PSNR above 30.96.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NeuMan, TPF3D-GTA (27.60 PSNR) slightly outperforms HumanNeRF (27.06 PSNR) while requiring zero per-subject optimization versus 26.5 hours of training, and runs ~16× faster (8.9 vs. 0.54 FPS). However, it trails all other video-based methods on rendering quality: ExAvatar leads with 34.80 PSNR (4h training, 4.0 FPS), followed by Vid2Avatar (30.70, 97h), GaussianAvatar (29.94, 43m, 15.7 FPS), and InstantAvatar (28.47, 26m, 21 FPS). The key differentiator is that TPF3D-GTA's FPS includes both shape and color reconstruction from scratch per frame, whereas GaussianAvatar and InstantAvatar FPS reflect rendering only after their per-subject optimization phase. On single-image THuman2.0, the three-view variants (TPF3D-SIFU-3v: Chamfer 0.4144; TPF3D-GTA-3v: 0.4195) surpass the re-evaluated single-frame baselines SIFU† (0.4409) and GTA† (0.4625), confirming that temporal/multi-view accumulation improves geometry. On CAPE, TPF3D-SIFU-3v achieves the best Chamfer (0.8024 NFP, 0.5794 FP) among all compared methods, though single-frame SIFU* (0.7725 NFP) remains slightly better on NFP poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate paradigms in 3D clothed human reconstruction: pixel-aligned implicit function methods (PIFu, GTA, SIFU) that achieve high per-frame detail but ignore temporal structure, and SMPL-based video reconstruction methods (Vid2Avatar, HumanNeRF, GaussianAvatar) that exploit temporal coherence but require minutes-to-hours of per-video optimization. The key conceptual departure is performing shape inference in a canonical space defined by SMPL's Linear Blend Skinning, then propagating that canonical representation forward through time, rather than re-optimizing a per-video neural field. The plug-and-play design—wrapping pre-trained pixel-aligned backbones without retraining—distinguishes it from both the single-image methods it accelerates and the optimization-heavy video methods it replaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 12 FPS for textured 3D human reconstruction from monocular video without per-subject optimization, this work opens a path toward real-time applications (VR, telepresence) that were previously locked behind hours of offline fitting. The plug-and-play architecture means future improvements to pixel-aligned backbones can be immediately incorporated, and the natural multi-view extension provides a quality ceiling without architectural changes. However, the method's dependence on accurate SMPL parameter regression limits its robustness for loose clothing, accessories, and extreme poses, and the canonical representation may accumulate drift over long sequences—questions the paper does not fully address. The gap in rendering quality versus optimization-based methods (PSNR 27.6 vs. 34.8 for ExAvatar) remains a significant trade-off for applications demanding photorealism.
