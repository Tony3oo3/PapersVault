## Problem

Augmentation-based single source domain generalization methods universally exhibit large fluctuations in target domain accuracy during training (mid-train OOD fluctuation), which complicates model selection and undermines generalization. The paper identifies that this fluctuation stems from the model's inability to accumulate knowledge across diverse augmentations, causing progressive distortion of learned features as training proceeds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251134/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a single source domain in paired form: an original image x and its augmented view x̄ = G(x) generated by a random augmentation policy (e.g., RandAugment). The task model processes x while the proxy model processes x̄, and both produce class label predictions. The final output is the task model's classification, evaluated on unseen target domains (e.g., Art/Cartoon/Sketch for PACS, SVHN/USPS for Digits, Clipart/Product for Office-Home).

## Method

PEER introduces two interacting modules with identical architecture: a frozen task model F and a trainable proxy model P. The proxy model is trained on augmented data with a PEER regularization loss that maximizes mutual information (via Barlow Twins decorrelation or InfoNCE) between the task model's projected features on the original image and the proxy model's projected features on the augmented image, guiding the proxy to preserve domain-invariant structure. Every k epochs, the task model is updated by parameter-averaging all saved snapshots of the proxy model's learning trajectory, progressively accumulating generalization knowledge from diverse augmentation policies. The augmentation function is reinitialized each cycle, so the task model stacks the effects of varying augmentations similar to an ensemble, while the mutual information regularization ensures mode connectivity between snapshots to make parameter-averaging effective.

## Results

On the standard sDG benchmarks, PEER with simple random augmentation achieves 59.42% mean accuracy on PACS and 81.06% on Digits, outperforming all baselines including MetaCNN (57.17% / 78.76%) and AdvST (49.90% / 80.10%). On the more challenging Office-Home and VLCS benchmarks, where prior augmentation-based methods show little or negative gains, PEER achieves 60.63% and 79.10% mean accuracy respectively, surpassing the best prior method PDEN by 6.29% and 1.71%. PEER also reduces mid-train OOD fluctuation (variance of target accuracy) across all four benchmarks, e.g., from 4.02 to 3.59 on PACS and from 1.52 to 1.36 on Digits compared to RandAug alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEER outperforms all 10 baselines on mean target-domain accuracy across all four benchmarks. On PACS, PEER (59.42%) beats the closest same-backbone competitor MetaCNN (57.17%) by 2.25% and PDEN (56.06%) by 3.36%; on Digits, it surpasses MetaCNN (78.76%) by 2.30% and AdvST (80.10%) by 0.96%. The gains over plain RandAug are particularly notable: +3.76% on PACS and +7.08% on Digits, showing that the parameter-space ensemble and entropy regularization add substantial value on top of simple random augmentation. On Office-Home and VLCS, where prior augmentation methods (PDEN, RandAug) yield only marginal or negative improvements over ERM, PEER achieves +10.62% and +6.66% over RandAug respectively, and +6.29% and +1.71% over PDEN. The paper notes that SimDE and AdvST used a stronger backbone (ResNet-18 vs. the standard AlexNet), making their PACS numbers not directly comparable; under the same backbone, PEER is the clear leader. In the model-to-model regularization comparison (Table 5), PEER with parameter averaging (81.06% Digits) outperforms both the frozen teacher (T+RA, 75.33%) and the non-averaged PEER variant (74.46%), confirming that the evolving task model is a more effective regulator than a static pre-trained teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: augmentation-based single source domain generalization (PDEN, L2D, SimDE, AdvST), mode connectivity and parameter-space ensembles (SWA, model soups, diverse weight averaging), and model-to-model regularization via mutual information (Cha et al., Li et al.). The key departure from prior model-to-model regularization work is the elimination of an external pre-trained teacher: instead, a parameter-averaged snapshot of the training model itself serves as the evolving regulator, updated periodically through the proxy model's learning trajectory. The paper also introduces a diagnostic lens—mid-train OOD fluctuation—that prior augmentation-based sDG methods have not explicitly addressed, reframing the problem as one of feature distortion accumulation rather than insufficient augmentation diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of mid-train OOD fluctuation as a systematic, augmentation-induced phenomenon provides a concrete explanation for why model selection in sDG is harder than expected, and the proposed remedy (parameter-space ensemble + MI regularization) is simple enough to be adopted as a drop-in wrapper around existing augmentation pipelines. The fact that plain RandAugment + PEER outperforms methods with elaborate learned augmentation strategies on both standard and challenging benchmarks (Office-Home, VLCS) suggests that the regularization mechanism may be more important than the augmentation complexity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones and higher-resolution datasets, whether mode connectivity assumptions hold for transformer architectures, and whether the periodic reinitialization of the augmentation policy (every k epochs) is truly necessary or an artifact of the small-scale evaluation settings.
