## Problem

Existing 3D generative models synthesize complete shapes but cannot produce small, parameterized geometric stamps (Vector Displacement Maps) that artists use to add details like eyes, ears, or decorations onto existing surfaces. No prior generative model for VDMs exists, no public VDM dataset is available, and standard depth-map or full-object reconstruction approaches fail to capture the high-amplitude overhangs and cavities that VDMs naturally represent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251856/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (optionally generated by a text-to-image model) depicting a part or detail, which is composited onto a gray square background to mimic a VDM applied to a flat tile. The output is a vector displacement map: a 3-channel image over a 2D UV domain where each pixel stores a 3D displacement vector, directly loadable in 3D software (Blender, Maya, ZBrush, Mudbox). An intermediate product is a set of six multi-view normal maps at fixed orthographic camera poses (four azimuth views at 0° elevation plus two elevation views at ±45°).

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, a fine-tuned Zero123++ diffusion model (Stable Diffusion backbone) generates six multi-view normal maps from the input image, using redesigned camera poses that omit back views and employ orthographic projection to reduce distortion. Second, a neural SDF is optimized via differentiable rendering against the normal maps (adapting Wonder3D's formulation but dropping the RGB loss), yielding a mesh with a solid plane base from which the VDM surface is extracted. Third, rather than topology-fixing and Tutte embedding (which introduces severe distortion) or direct mesh optimization (which falls into local minima), a neural deformation field parameterized by an MLP maps a unit square to the reconstructed mesh, optimized with symmetric Chamfer Distance plus a boundary-preservation loss; the MLP's inductive smoothness bias acts as a natural regularizer. Training data consists of 1,200 VDM-like patches cropped from Objaverse via a 3D lasso tool, remeshed with Screened Poisson reconstruction, and stitched to a coplanar square boundary using a Poisson Image Editing-inspired deformation.

## Results

On a 50-image evaluation set of common VDM categories (facial elements, decorations), GenVDM achieves CLIPImg 0.8520, CLIPText 0.2701, and 3D-FID 192.7, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest competitor is Wonder3D (CLIPImg 0.8246, 3D-FID 199.5), followed by Magic123 (CLIPImg 0.8293, 3D-FID 213.2). The scalar displacement map baseline (DepthAnything) scores CLIPText 0.2564 but 3D-FID 213.0, confirming that vector displacements are necessary for capturing overhangs and cavities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenVDM outperforms all four baselines on all three metrics. Against Wonder3D (the closest competitor with a similar multi-view normal + neural SDF pipeline), GenVDM improves CLIPImg by +0.027 and reduces 3D-FID by 6.8 points, attributed to its VDM-specific reconstruction rather than whole-object generation. Magic123 and LRM, which rely heavily on texture hallucination, produce less geometric detail and score 0.023 and 0.038 lower on CLIPImg respectively, with 3D-FID gaps of 20.5 and 47.2. The scalar displacement map baseline (DepthAnything) is competitive on CLIPText (0.2564 vs 0.2701) but fails on side views because a heightfield cannot represent unseen regions or inward displacements, yielding a 3D-FID 20.3 points worse than GenVDM. Overall, the results confirm that generating true vector displacement maps—supporting both outward and inward displacements—is essential for VDM-quality output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multi-view diffusion + neural SDF reconstruction pipeline (from Zero123++ and Wonder3D) for a fundamentally different output: a parametric 2D vector displacement map rather than a complete 3D mesh. It departs from prior image-to-3D work (Magic123, LRM, Wonder3D) that targets whole-object generation, and from scalar displacement map approaches (DepthAnything) that cannot represent overhangs or cavities. For the parameterization step, it replaces traditional mesh-based methods (topology fixing + Tutte embedding, mesh optimization) with a neural deformation field (MLP), drawing conceptual inspiration from AtlasNet and Deep Geometric Prior. The data pipeline borrows from Poisson surface reconstruction and Poisson image editing to convert arbitrary 3D object patches into VDM-like training shapes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method and first public dataset for VDM generation, opening a practical workflow where artists can produce custom geometric stamps from a single image and refine them via 2D image editing—a capability that was previously locked behind expensive third-party stamp libraries. The per-shape optimization (~6 min/shape) is a significant practical limitation compared to feed-forward LRMs, and the authors explicitly flag that a VDM-LRM with limited training data is an open question. Thin-structure failures due to multi-view inconsistency remain unresolved, and the small 1,200-sample dataset, while sufficient for the target domain, limits generalizability to arbitrary shape categories.
