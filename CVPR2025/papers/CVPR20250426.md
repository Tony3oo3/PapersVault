## Problem

Existing single-image-to-3D generation methods produce meshes with baked-in lighting, making them appear incorrect under novel illumination conditions. Reconstructing physically-based rendering (PBR) material properties from a single image is challenging due to the entanglement of geometry, material, and lighting, and the inherent ambiguity of disentangling these factors under unknown illumination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250426/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and first generates six novel-view RGB images via Zero123++. It then predicts per-view G-buffers (XYZ coordinates, surface normals, albedo, roughness, metallic) tiled in a 3×2 grid at 512×512 resolution, which are unprojected through Nvdiffrast to produce a 3D mesh with decomposed PBR material maps. Unlike prior methods that output a single textured mesh, MAGE outputs separate albedo, roughness, and metallic channels that enable physically correct relighting under arbitrary environment maps.

## Method

MAGE replaces the multi-step denoising U-Net of Zero123++ with a deterministic single-step network that takes the VAE-encoded tiled RGB image as its latent input and directly predicts the G-buffer latent in one forward pass, eliminating stochastic noise and enabling task-specific image-space losses during training. A key innovation is the lighting response loss, which uses a differentiable split-sum image-based renderer (microfacet BRDF) to re-render predicted G-buffers under known environment lighting and compares the result to ground-truth RGB; gradients are isolated per G-buffer component by substituting ground-truth values for all other channels, resolving the ambiguity of simultaneous multi-domain prediction. The network is trained on a synthetic dataset of 17k+ Objaverse objects with randomized PBR material assignment and custom Blender shaders that extract all five G-buffer channels, supervised by a combination of L2/L1/LPIPS losses per attribute plus the lighting response loss.

## Results

On the Objaverse evaluation set, MAGE achieves PSNR of 22.243 (albedo), 28.399 (normal), 21.819 (roughness), and 20.278 (metallic), consistently outperforming all baselines across all four G-buffer domains. In SSIM, MAGE reaches 0.8946 (albedo), 0.9387 (normal), 0.9110 (roughness), and 0.9029 (metallic), and in LPIPS achieves 0.0780, 0.0626, 0.0974, and 0.1014 respectively. For normal estimation, MAGE's PSNR of 28.399 is comparable to GeoWizard (26.917), a method dedicated solely to geometry prediction, while substantially surpassing RGB↔X (20.489).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGE outperforms both baselines across all four G-buffer domains and all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). Against IID + GeoWizard, MAGE gains +4.05 PSNR on albedo, +1.48 on normal, +2.48 on roughness, and +4.01 on metallic, with the largest relative improvement on metallic (20.278 vs. 16.264 PSNR). Against RGB↔X, the margins are even larger: +6.76 PSNR on albedo, +7.91 on normal, +1.97 on roughness, and +4.53 on metallic. Notably, MAGE's normal estimation (28.399 PSNR) surpasses GeoWizard (26.917), a method purpose-built for single-image geometry, while also producing accurate material maps simultaneously. Qualitatively, MAGE produces clean, lighting-free albedo and part-aware roughness/metallic maps that enable realistic relighting, whereas RGB↔X fails to capture correct geometry and IID bakes lighting effects into material maps. For full 3D reconstruction, MAGE is compared qualitatively against MeshFormer, which bakes shadows and specular reflections into textures, producing incorrect appearances under novel lighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGE sits at the intersection of single-image-to-3D generation (e.g., MeshFormer, LRM) and intrinsic image decomposition (e.g., IID, RGB↔X), but departs from both by introducing G-buffers from deferred rendering as an explicit intermediate representation. Architecturally, it repurposes the pretrained multi-view diffusion U-Net from Zero123++ by converting it into a deterministic single-step estimator, drawing on the consistency-model line of work. The physically-based lighting response loss borrows the split-sum IBR formulation from Unreal Engine 4's shading model and a differentiable renderer from Munkberg et al., applying them as a training-time consistency constraint rather than an inference-time tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first method to produce physically decomposed PBR materials (albedo, roughness, metallic) alongside geometry from a single wild image, enabling relightable 3D assets without multi-view captures or text prompts. The G-buffer formulation elegantly reduces a high-dimensional 3D problem to structured 2D map prediction, and the released 17k-object synthetic dataset with explicit material supervision is a useful resource for the community. However, the method is limited to standard PBR (no anisotropy, subsurface scattering, or refraction), relies entirely on synthetic training data, and the evaluation lacks a truly comparable baseline since no prior method performs the identical task, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to complex real-world materials and dynamic scenes.
