## Problem

Existing unsupervised outlier detection (UOD) methods exhibit severe performance instability when the contamination factor γ varies: inlier-manifold methods fail at high γ while outlier-based methods fail at low γ, and no single method covers both regimes. Current γ-estimators rely on threshold learning over a given score function, which is ill-posed when the score's discriminative power is unknown or the inlier distribution assumptions are violated. The paper asks: can γ be estimated in a way that is structurally consistent with the outlier scoring mechanism itself?

## Input / Output

FlexUOD consumes pre-extracted feature vectors (ResNet-50, CLIP, ResNet-18, or raw pixels for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST) from an unlabeled, contaminated image or tabular dataset with no train/test split. It outputs a per-sample outlier score, a binary outlier/inlier label derived from a threshold τ_ours = sort(scores)[n·(1−|φ|)], and an approximate contamination factor estimate φ. The method is entirely training-free and operates in a single forward pass over the feature matrix, making it suitable for plug-and-play integration with any existing low-γ detector by simply replacing f_low.

## Method

FlexUOD constructs two structurally consistent score functions: f_low, which scores each sample by its asymmetric Bray-Curtis distance (or Euclidean distance) from the dataset centroid, motivated by the shell-theory property that in high dimensions nearly all volume concentrates on a hypersphere surface; and f_high, which applies Shell Theory's outlier-based normalization to f_low using MAD-filtered outlier candidates. The contamination factor is then estimated via the Spearman rank correlation φ between the f_low and f_high score rankings, which is quasi-linearly correlated with γ because the two scores diverge at low γ but converge at high γ. A simple decision rule selects f_low (φ < 0.1), f_high (φ > 0.3), or their min-max normalized sum (otherwise), and the estimated φ directly yields the classification threshold without any separate threshold-learning procedure.

## Results

On the primary RGB benchmarks (averaged over contamination ratios 0.01–0.5), FlexUOD with CLIP features achieves AUC of 0.988 on STL-10, 0.985 on Internet, 0.957 on CIFAR-10, 0.954 on CIFAR-100, and 0.979 on MIT-Places-Small, surpassing the previous best (Multi-T) by 2.2–8.2 percentage points. For threshold learning (F1-score), FlexUOD reaches 0.687 on STL-10 and 0.653 on CIFAR-100, outperforming the best dedicated threshold estimator (KARCH) by 2.7 and 4.2 points respectively. Inference time is 0.118 s for 1,000 features on CPU, roughly 68× faster than LVAD (8.057 s) and 119× faster than GOAD (14.06 s on GPU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexUOD outperforms all 28 baselines on every evaluated dataset and metric. The closest competitor is Multi-T (ECCV-24), which FlexUOD surpasses by 2.2 pp on STL-10 (0.988 vs 0.966), 4.4 pp on Internet (0.985 vs 0.941), 4.0 pp on CIFAR-10 (0.957 vs 0.917), 4.1 pp on CIFAR-100 (0.954 vs 0.913), and 8.2 pp on MIT-Places-Small (0.979 vs 0.897) using CLIP features. Among inlier-manifold methods, LVAD (ECCV-22) is the strongest single-method baseline but is outperformed by 3.2 pp on STL-10 and 8.0 pp on MIT-Places-Small; Shell-Re (TPAMI-21) shows the largest gap on CIFAR-100 (0.954 vs 0.821). For threshold learning (F1-score), FlexUOD's implicit threshold beats the best dedicated estimator KARCH by 2.7 pp on STL-10 (0.687 vs 0.660) and 4.2 pp on CIFAR-100 (0.653 vs 0.611). On MVTec-AD (industrial AD), FlexUOD achieves 0.834 average AUC with CLIP vs 0.756 for LVAD and 0.723 for Shell-Re, and on medical AD (RESC) it reaches 0.948 vs 0.818 for Shell-Re. Notably, FlexUOD is also the fastest method tested (0.118 s for 1,000 features on CPU), being 68× faster than LVAD and 119× faster than GOAD.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexUOD builds directly on Shell Theory (Lin et al., TPAMI 2021), which established that high-dimensional data concentrates on a hypersphere surface and that outlier-based normalization improves feature discriminativeness. It departs from the dominant 'single method fits all scenarios' paradigm in UOD by explicitly acknowledging that inlier-manifold methods (LVAD, OCSVM, ICL) and outlier-exposure methods (Shell-Re) are each suited to different contamination regimes. The key conceptual departure from prior contamination-factor estimation work (KARCH, REGR, FGD, etc.) is making γ-estimation structurally coupled to the outlier score function itself—comparing two consistent score functions (f_low and f_high) via Spearman rank correlation—rather than treating threshold learning as an independent, ill-posed problem. The use of Bray-Curtis distance, originally from ecological community analysis, as an asymmetric metric for outlier scoring is a repurposing from a different domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of FlexUOD (0.118 s for 1,000 features on CPU) makes it immediately deployable in real-world pipelines where the contamination factor is unknown and compute budgets are tight. Its ability to improve existing methods (OCSVM, LVAD, ICL) by simply wrapping them in the f_low/f_high selection framework suggests a broadly applicable meta-strategy for UOD. The main limitation acknowledged is that the method operates on whole-image features and may not transfer to anomaly segmentation or localization tasks. An open question is whether the structural-consistency principle for γ-estimation generalizes to settings where f_low and f_high cannot be made architecturally related, or to non-image modalities with fundamentally different geometry.
