## Problem

Existing correspondence refinement methods for point cloud registration follow a top-down outlier removal paradigm that can only produce a subset of the initial correspondences. When initial inliers are extremely scarce (e.g., outlier ratios above 99%), even perfect identification of all inliers yields too few correspondences to support robust 6DoF pose estimation, causing registration to fail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250619/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two point clouds P and Q with pre-extracted per-point features F^P and F^Q (compatible with any descriptor, from traditional FPFH to learning-based FCGF/SpinNet/Predator) and produces a rigid transformation T{R,t} estimated via SVD along with a set of refined correspondences. The process is progressive and iterative: the correspondences G^t from iteration t serve as positional priors for iteration t+1, with the local search radius r^t decreasing across iterations to drive convergence. The method is training-free and descriptor-agnostic, operating purely on geometric consistency.

## Method

Regor is a progressive correspondence regenerator that, in each iteration, performs three stages: (1) Prior-guided local grouping samples seed correspondences from the previous iteration and constructs local point cloud pairs via radius nearest-neighbor search, followed by generalized mutual matching (GMM) that relaxes the strict mutual NN constraint by combining NN and multi-NN matching matrices via Hadamard product and element-wise OR; (2) Local correspondence refinement uses a center-aware three-point consistency (CTC) that leverages the high-confidence seed correspondence as a geometric prior to identify inliers, then corrects correspondences by estimating a local pose and snapping source points to their nearest target neighbors; (3) Global correspondence refinement merges local results via a hash table and applies second-order consistency for global correction. The key distinction from prior work is bottom-up regeneration rather than top-down pruning: each iteration generates new dense correspondences (up to 10× more inliers) rather than merely filtering the initial set, enabling robust registration even when initial inliers number fewer than 1.

## Results

On the 3DMatch dataset with FPFH descriptors, Regor achieves 88.48% registration recall (RR), 1.70° rotation error (RE), and 5.94 cm translation error (TE), outperforming the previous best (Hunter at 84.70% RR) by 3.78 pp. The most striking result is the inlier number ratio (INR): Regor generates 1253.74% of the initial inliers (2532 inliers on average), roughly 10× more than outlier-removal baselines (e.g., SC2-PCR at 78.78% INR). On KITTI, Regor achieves 99.82% RR with the lowest RE (0.50°) and TE (5.36 cm), and an INR of 2486.82%. Under extreme outlier ratios (>99%), Regor achieves 26.76% RR versus only 9.15% for SC2-PCR, with an average of 667 inliers versus <1 for SC2-PCR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Regor outperforms all nine baselines on 3DMatch (FPFH) in RR, RE, TE, and IP. Against the strongest geometry-based baseline SC²-PCR [6], Regor gains +5.24 pp in RR (88.48 vs 83.24) and produces ~8.7× more inliers (2532 vs 289). Against the best learning-based baseline Hunter [40], Regor improves RR by +3.78 pp and INR by ~15.8× (1253.74% vs 79.27%). On KITTI, Regor ties Hunter and SC²-PCR at 99.82% RR but achieves the lowest RE (0.50° vs 0.55°/0.61°) and TE (5.36 cm vs 6.29/8.16 cm), while generating 21× more inliers than SC²-PCR (5353 vs 248). On 3DLoMatch with weak FPFH features, Regor achieves 43.96% RR versus 40.88% for MAC [47] and 36.90% for Hunter [40], a gap of 3–7 pp. Under the extreme-outlier 3DMatch-EOR benchmark (>99% outliers), Regor achieves 26.76% RR versus 9.15% for SC²-PCR and 2.56% for PointDSC [3], with 667 average inliers versus <1 for SC²-PCR. The only metric where Regor trails is inlier precision (IP) on KITTI (79.57% vs 93.59% for SC²-PCR), but the vastly larger inlier count more than compensates for pose accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

Regor departs fundamentally from the dominant outlier-removal paradigm in point cloud registration (RANSAC, SC2-PCR, MAC, PointDSC, VBReg, Hunter), which treats correspondence quality as a filtering problem. Instead, it reframes the task as progressive correspondence *generation*, using positional priors from prior iterations to guide local re-matching within shrinking radius neighborhoods. The method borrows specific algorithmic components from prior work—second-order consistency from SC2-PCR for global refinement and TEASER's translation/rotation invariants for the center-aware three-point consistency—but recombines them within a novel iterative regeneration loop that has no direct precedent in the registration literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a training-free regeneration strategy can produce 10× more inliers than the best outlier-removal methods, and that weak traditional descriptors (e.g., RoPS) jump from ~54% to ~91% registration recall when paired with Regor, challenges the field's implicit assumption that correspondence improvement requires either powerful learned features or aggressive filtering. The 3DMatch-EOR benchmark (outlier ratios >99%) exposes a regime where all existing methods fail outright, providing a useful stress test for future work. Open questions include whether the progressive strategy scales to very large outdoor scenes with millions of points, how the method behaves under non-rigid or partial-overlap deformations, and whether the radius-schedule heuristic can be learned rather than hand-tuned.
