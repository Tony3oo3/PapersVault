## Problem

The most performant vision-language models remain proprietary, and existing open-weight VLMs achieve their performance by distilling synthetic data from closed models (e.g., GPT-4V captions), leaving the community without foundational knowledge of how to build competitive VLMs from scratch. This paper addresses that gap by releasing both model weights and fully open, non-distilled training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250282/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input. The image is split into a low-resolution full-image overview plus multiple high-resolution overlapping square crops (e.g., 336px each, 12 at training and up to 36 at test time), which are independently encoded by a ViT. Outputs include free-form text responses, detailed captions averaging ~196 words, 2D point coordinates expressed as plain-text values normalized to 0–100 (ordered top-down, left-to-right), and object counts produced via a chain-of-thought sequence of points.

## Method

Molmo follows a standard ViT encoder + connector + decoder-only LLM architecture but introduces several key design choices: an overlapping multi-crop strategy that preserves border-patch context, multi-headed attention pooling over 2×2 patch windows, and text-only residual dropout during pre-training to encourage reliance on visual tokens. Training is a simplified two-stage pipeline: pre-training on 712k PixMo-Cap images with length-conditioned captioning (no separate connector-tuning stage), followed by fine-tuning on a mixture of PixMo datasets and academic benchmarks. The critical contribution is the PixMo data collection, which avoids all VLM-generated content: captions are gathered via 60–90-second spoken descriptions transcribed and cleaned by a language-only LLM, Q&A pairs are produced through interactive human-LLM editing, and 2.3M 2D pointing annotations enable grounding, counting-by-pointing, and visual explanation. Ablations confirm that the human-collected PixMo-Cap data performs on par with GPT-4o-generated captions on the same images, validating that high-quality open data can replace proprietary distillation.

## Results

Molmo-72B achieves the highest average score (81.2) across 11 academic benchmarks, surpassing GPT-4o (78.5), Gemini 1.5 Pro (78.3), and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (76.7), while ranking second in human-preference Elo (1077 vs. GPT-4o's 1079). On the new PixMo-Count benchmark, Molmo-72B scores 85.2, far ahead of GPT-4o (59.6) and all other models, demonstrating the effectiveness of its pointing-based chain-of-thought counting. The most efficient model, MolmoE-1B (68.6 avg), nearly matches GPT-4V (71.1 avg) and achieves 79.6 on PixMo-Count. Molmo-7B-D (77.3 avg) sits comfortably between GPT-4V and GPT-4o on both benchmarks and Elo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Molmo-72B outperforms all open-weight baselines on average benchmark score: Qwen2-VL-72B (79.4), InternVL2-Llama-3-76B (77.1), Llama-3.2V-90B (74.5), and LLaVA-OneVision-72B (76.6). It also surpasses three proprietary API models—Gemini 1.5 Pro (78.3), Claude 3.5 Sonnet (76.7), and Gemini 1.5 Flash (75.1)—on average score, though GPT-4o (78.5) retains a slight Elo advantage (1079 vs. 1077). On counting, Molmo-72B dominates every baseline by a wide margin: 85.2 on PixMo-Count vs. 64.3 for Gemini 1.5 Pro and 59.6 for GPT-4o, and 91.2 on CountBenchQA vs. 89.7 for Claude 3.5 Sonnet. Molmo-7B-D (77.3 avg) beats GPT-4V (71.1) by 6.2 points and trails GPT-4o by only 1.2 points, while MolmoE-1B (68.6) is within 2.5 points of GPT-4V despite using a 1B-parameter MoE LLM. On OCR-centric benchmarks (ChartQA, DocVQA, TextVQA), Molmo-72B slightly trails Qwen2-VL-72B, and on reasoning tasks (MMMU, MathVista) it lags behind GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet, reflecting the absence of advanced-reasoning data in its training mix.

## Relation to Prior Work

Molmo follows the standard LLaVA-style architecture of connecting a pre-trained ViT vision encoder to a decoder-only LLM, but deliberately departs from the dominant trend in open VLMs of relying on synthetic data distilled from proprietary models (e.g., ShareGPT4V generated by GPT-4V). Instead, the paper's core contribution is the PixMo data collection pipeline—speech-based dense captioning, interactive LLM-assisted Q&A, and 2D pointing annotations—all gathered without any VLM involvement. Architecturally, it introduces an overlapping multi-crop strategy to preserve border context, attention-based 2×2 pooling in the connector, and text-only dropout during pre-training, while simplifying the training pipeline by eliminating the separate connector-tuning stage common in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first complete, fully open recipe (weights, data, code, evaluations) for building a VLM that matches or exceeds top proprietary systems, filling a critical gap in the scientific community's understanding of how to train competitive multimodal models from scratch. The 2D pointing paradigm opens a promising direction for embodied agents (robots, web agents) that act by pointing in their environment, and the PixMo datasets—particularly the 712k dense captions and 2.3M pointing annotations—become reusable resources for the field. Key limitations include weaker performance on advanced reasoning benchmarks (MMMU, MathVista) due to the training mix lacking reasoning-focused data, and the pointing capability not generalizing well across different crop counts without additional high-resolution post-training. An open question is whether the speech-based annotation trick and pointing-based counting can be further scaled or adapted to video and other modalities.
