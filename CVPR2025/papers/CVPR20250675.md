## Problem

Standard semi-supervised learning assumes labeled and unlabeled data share the same distribution, while unsupervised domain adaptation targets only one domain at test time. Neither paradigm handles the realistic scenario where labeled and unlabeled training data come from heterogeneous domains with different label and class-feature distributions, and the test set is drawn from both domains without domain identity. This paper formalizes this setting as Heterogeneous Semi-Supervised Learning (HSSL) and shows that existing SSL and UDA methods fail to generalize across such heterogeneous test data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled RGB images from one domain (with C-class ground-truth labels) and unlabeled RGB images from a second domain that shares the same C semantic categories but has different label and class-feature distributions. The output is a 2C-class fine-grained prediction: the first C logits correspond to the labeled-domain version of each semantic class and the second C logits correspond to the unlabeled-domain version, effectively encoding both domain origin and semantic class in a single classifier head. At test time, samples from either domain are fed without domain labels and the model must assign the correct domain-specific class.

## Method

Uni-HSSL learns a unified 2C-class classifier (shared feature encoder + 2C-class head) by expanding the label space so that each semantic class is represented twice—once per domain. Three components drive training: (1) a weighted moving average (WMA) pseudo-labeling scheme that smoothly updates pseudo-labels for unlabeled instances each iteration, gated by a confidence threshold to filter noisy assignments; (2) a cross-domain contrastive prototype alignment loss that pulls corresponding semantic-class prototypes from the two domains closer in feature space while pushing non-corresponding pairs apart; and (3) a progressive inter-domain mixup that generates synthetic training pairs by linearly interpolating labeled and pseudo-labeled samples, with the mixing coefficient λ drawn from a schedule that shifts from favoring the labeled domain (λ ∈ [0, 0.5)) early in training to full inter-domain mixing (λ ∈ [0, 1]) by the end. The model is initialized via supervised pre-training on the labeled domain, then jointly optimized with a combined loss of supervised cross-entropy, thresholded pseudo-label cross-entropy, prototype alignment, and mixup MSE.

## Results

Uni-HSSL achieves average classification accuracy of 87.5% on Office-31, 74.7% on Office-Home, 93.1% on VisDA, and 75.4% on ISIC-2019, consistently outperforming all baselines across every domain pair. Gains over the supervised-only baseline exceed 9% on Office-31, 5% on Office-Home, 9% on VisDA, and 7.5% on ISIC-2019. On VisDA, per-class improvements over supervised reach up to 22% (e.g., Bicycle: 97.5% vs. 74.1%). The method also achieves 100% accuracy on the D/W and W/D domain pairs of Office-31, indicating near-perfect generalization in those settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-HSSL outperforms all six baseline categories across all four datasets. Against the strongest SSL baselines (SimMatch, FixMatch, FlexMatch), Uni-HSSL gains 3.2–5.8% average accuracy (e.g., 87.5% vs. SimMatch's 84.3% on Office-31; 93.1% vs. FixMatch's 87.3% on VisDA). Against the best UDA baselines (MCC+Sup, CDAN+Sup), gains are 1–6.3% (e.g., 74.7% vs. MCC+Sup's 72.3% on Office-Home; 75.4% vs. CDAN+Sup's 69.1% on ISIC-2019). The most notable margin is over BiAdapt [14], the closest prior work addressing distribution mismatch: Uni-HSSL surpasses it by 5.5% on Office-31, 6.9% on Office-Home, 14% on VisDA, and 4.2% on ISIC-2019, with BiAdapt struggling particularly on VisDA (79.1% vs. 93.1%). On Office-Home, Uni-HSSL wins on all 12 domain pairs, with the largest single-pair gain being P/A (78.7% vs. MCC+Sup's 76.1%). The only near-tie is on VisDA where CDAN+Sup (92.1%) and MCC+Sup (92.0%) are within 1% of Uni-HSSL (93.1%), though Uni-HSSL still leads on 10 of 12 individual classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of semi-supervised learning and unsupervised domain adaptation, introducing HSSL as a distinct problem where labeled and unlabeled data come from different domains and the test set spans both. It builds conceptually on the pseudo-labeling paradigm of SSL methods (FixMatch, FlexMatch, SimMatch) and the feature-alignment strategies of UDA (CDAN, MCC), but departs from both by removing the homogeneity assumption of SSL and the single-target-domain restriction of UDA. The closest prior work, BiAdapt [14], also addresses distribution mismatch but trains separate domain-specific components and evaluates only on the labeled domain; Uni-HSSL instead learns a unified 2C-class model that jointly handles both domains. The progressive inter-domain mixup extends the standard Mixup [40] and MixMatch [1] augmentation strategies with a time-varying mixing schedule tailored to the cross-domain setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HSSL addresses a practically common scenario—labeled data from one source (e.g., research hospitals) and unlabeled data from another (e.g., rural clinics)—that neither standard SSL nor UDA handles natively. The 2C-class fine-grained formulation is an elegant way to encode domain identity without an explicit domain classifier, and the progressive mixup schedule offers a reusable principle for gradual domain bridging. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification with ResNet backbones, the assumption of exactly two domains with shared semantic classes, and the doubling of the output space which may not scale gracefully to many domains. Open questions include whether the framework extends to more than two domains, to non-image modalities, or to settings where the semantic class sets are only partially overlapping.
