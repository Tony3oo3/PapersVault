## Problem

Existing deep learning pansharpening methods process sequential features but overlook two dimensions of feature heterogeneity and redundancy: redundant channels within individual feature maps and overlapping information across different network depths. This unaddressed redundancy limits the quality of the final fused output, as the network cannot selectively emphasize informative channels or balance the contributions of shallow (detail-rich) versus deep (semantic) features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252532/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a high-resolution panchromatic image P ∈ R^{H×W} and a low-resolution multispectral image L ∈ R^{H/4×W/4×c} (4× downsampled, c spectral bands) and produces a high-resolution multispectral image H ∈ R^{H×W×c}. ADWM itself operates on intermediate feature maps F_i ∈ R^{H×W×C} produced by a backbone's sequential feature extraction blocks (e.g., ResBlocks), making it a plug-and-play module that does not alter the raw input/output interface of the host network.

## Method

The core mechanism is Correlation-Aware Covariance Weighting (CACW), which computes a normalized covariance matrix from a set of feature vectors, then passes it through an MLP to generate adaptive weights—inspired by PCA's use of covariance for capturing inter-feature correlations but replacing fixed eigenvector projection with a data-driven nonlinear mapping. CACW is applied at two levels: Intra-Feature Weighting (IFW) reshapes each feature map to HW×C, computes channel-level weights via CACW, and applies them element-wise to suppress redundant channels; Cross-Feature Weighting (CFW) spatially pools all N intermediate features into C×N, computes layer-level weights via CACW, and uses softmax-normalized weights to form a weighted sum of the IFW-adjusted features. Unlike attention mechanisms that compute QKV projections before an MLP, CACW places the MLP after the covariance matrix to perform a data-driven eigenvalue-decomposition-like operation. The entire ADWM module is architecture-agnostic and can be inserted into any network with sequential feature extraction (demonstrated on LAGNet, FusionNet, LGPNet, and U2Net) with only a small parameter overhead.

## Results

On the reduced-resolution datasets, ADWM (integrated into LAGNet) achieves PSNR of 39.170 dB (WV3), 38.466 dB (QB), and 43.884 dB (GF2), outperforming the second-best method (PanMamba) by 0.158 dB, 0.257 dB, and 0.977 dB respectively. It also attains the best SAM (2.913/4.450/0.672), ERGAS (2.145/3.705/0.597), and Q8/Q4 (0.921/0.937/0.985) across all three datasets. In the full-resolution plug-and-play experiment (Table 2), adding ADWM to FusionNet, LGPNet, and U2Net consistently improves HQNR by 0.003–0.016 with only a small parameter increase (e.g., LGPNet: 27.0K → 33.4K params, HQNR 0.940 → 0.950 on WV3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed ADWM (on LAGNet backbone) achieves the best score on every metric across all three datasets, beating all 11 baselines. The closest competitor is PanMamba [19], which the proposed method surpasses by 0.158 dB PSNR on WV3 (39.170 vs 39.012) and 0.977 dB on GF2 (43.884 vs 42.907), while tying on SAM (2.913) for WV3. On QB, the second-best is LAGNet without ADWM (38.209 dB), and the proposed method gains 0.257 dB. Classical methods (MTF-GLP-FS, BDSD-PC, TV) trail by roughly 6–11 dB in PSNR, confirming the gap between traditional and deep-learning approaches. Early CNN baselines (PNN, PanNet, DiCNN) score around 37 dB PSNR on WV3, roughly 2 dB below the proposed method. In the full-resolution plug-and-play setting (Table 2), ADWM improves every backbone it is attached to: FusionNet HQNR rises from 0.940 to 0.946, LGPNet from 0.940 to 0.950, and U2Net from 0.952 to 0.955 on WV3, with analogous gains on QB and GF2, demonstrating consistent benefit without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the covariance matrix from PCA—not for dimensionality reduction but for data-driven weight generation—into a lightweight alternative to attention mechanisms (SENet, SRANet) for channel and layer reweighting. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant attention-based weighting paradigm by arguing that inter-channel and inter-layer correlations captured in a covariance matrix, processed through a small MLP, provide a more direct and parameter-efficient signal than QKV-based attention. The dual-level design (intra-feature + cross-feature) is positioned as a general plug-and-play module that can be inserted into existing sequential-feature-extraction networks (LAGNet, FusionNet, LGPNet, U2Net) without architectural modification, distinguishing it from DenseNet-style concatenation by adaptively weighting rather than uniformly combining layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature and modest parameter overhead (e.g., +6.9K for FusionNet, +6.4K for LGPNet) make ADWM a practical drop-in improvement for a wide range of pansharpening pipelines, and the consistent gains across three satellite datasets and three host architectures strengthen the generality claim. However, the improvements, while consistent, are relatively small (0.16–0.60 dB PSNR), and the evaluation is confined to pansharpening; whether the covariance-based weighting transfers to other fusion or multi-modal tasks remains untested. An open question is how the mechanism interacts with non-CNN backbones (e.g., state-space models like PanMamba, which is only used as a baseline, not a host), and whether the fixed softmax normalization in CFW is optimal compared to learned gating.
