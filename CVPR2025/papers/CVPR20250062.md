## Problem

Existing video object segmentation benchmarks and methods are limited to single-phase objects (typically rigid solids), overlooking the complex morphological and appearance changes that occur during intra-phase and cross-phase transitions (e.g., water freezing, dry ice sublimation, glass shattering). This paper introduces the concept of 'phase' in segmentation and identifies that current appearance-based VOS models show significant performance degradation when tracking objects undergoing such transitions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250062/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task follows the semi-supervised VOS protocol: given a video sequence and a ground-truth mask on the first frame, the model predicts per-frame instance masks for the target object. The M³-VOS dataset provides 471 high-resolution videos (avg. 14.34 s, 30 fps annotation) across 14 scenarios, with bilingual text descriptions, phase labels for first/last frames, and void masks for ambiguous regions (motion blur, unclear boundaries). The ReVOS module additionally consumes the forward-propagated final mask as a boosted re-input and operates on a sliding window of T previous frames during reverse propagation.

## Method

ReVOS is a plug-and-play bidirectional propagation module built on the Cutie-base matching backbone, motivated by the observation that phase transitions increase object disorder (measured via LBP and grayscale entropy), making forward prediction progressively harder while reverse propagation from a well-defined final state is easier. It comprises three components: (1) a Booster that applies a learnable scaling factor α to decoded logits before sigmoid to expand the predicted mask and reduce local information loss during forward propagation; (2) a Reverse Memory that stores high-resolution features exclusively for the current reverse pass (cleared at each reverse start), enabling the model to re-propagate the boosted final mask backward through a sliding window; and (3) a Readout Fusion module that integrates forward and reverse readout features to produce the final mask. Training freezes all Cutie parameters and only optimizes the Readout Fusion Module with AdamW (lr=1e-5, 75k iterations on 4×A100 GPUs), making the approach lightweight and transferable to any mask-propagation backbone.

## Results

On the proposed M³-VOS benchmark (full set), ReVOS achieves J=75.6, J_tr=66.5, J_cc=65.2, outperforming the strongest baseline Cutie-base (J=74.6) by +1.0 J and the next-best RMem+DeAOT (J=73.4) by +2.2 J. On the more challenging M³-VOS core subset, ReVOS reaches J=66.3 vs. Cutie-base's 64.6 and RMem+DeAOT's 56.1, showing a larger margin where phase-transition diversity is balanced. On standard benchmarks, ReVOS matches or slightly exceeds Cutie-base on DAVIS'17 (J=86.0 vs. 85.6) and YouTubeVOS (G=86.8 vs. 86.8), while remaining competitive on VOST (J=41.0 vs. Cutie-base 40.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the new M³-VOS benchmark, ReVOS consistently outperforms all five baselines: it beats Cutie-base by +1.0 J (full) and +1.7 J (core), RMem+DeAOT by +2.2 J (full) and +10.2 J (core), DeAOT by +3.1 J (full), XMem by +5.2 J (full), and SAM2 by +6.1 J (full). On DAVIS'17, ReVOS edges out Cutie-base (86.0 vs. 85.6 J) and SAM2 (86.0 vs. 85.5 J) while leading XMem and DeAOT by ~3 J. On YouTubeVOS, ReVOS ties Cutie-base on the overall G score (86.8) but trails slightly on seen-class J (85.3 vs. 85.9) while leading on unseen-class J (82.1 vs. 81.6). Notably, on VOST ReVOS (J=41.0) underperforms DeAOT (45.6) and SAM2 (44.1), which the authors attribute to VOST's construction from EPIC-kitchens-style data that SAM2 was trained on. The largest relative gains from ReVOS over Cutie-base appear on the M³-VOS core subset and on cross-phase transitions (Table 5: J=78.1 vs. 77.5), confirming that the reverse-propagation mechanism is most beneficial when objects undergo dramatic morphological changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised VOS line (DAVIS, YouTubeVOS, VOST, VSCOS) by introducing the concept of object *phase*—a macroscopic categorization of materials by visual appearance and transformation dynamics—and building the first benchmark (M³-VOS) that systematically covers intra-phase and cross-phase transitions (e.g., liquid flow, gas diffusion, solid-to-liquid melting). Methodologically, it builds directly on Cutie's propagation-based matching backbone, adding a plug-and-play reverse-propagation module (ReVOS) motivated by an entropy argument: forward propagation faces increasing disorder, while reverse propagation benefits from decreasing disorder. The reverse-propagation idea is loosely related to DyeNet's bidirectional re-identification, but ReVOS requires no high-confidence mask and operates as a drop-in module on any mask-propagation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a clear and quantified gap: all evaluated SOTA methods (SAM2, Cutie, DeAOT, XMem, RMem) suffer substantial Jaccard drops on M³-VOS relative to standard benchmarks, and the gap widens on the balanced core subset, revealing scenery and phase biases in current models. The plug-and-play nature of ReVOS makes the reverse-propagation idea broadly applicable, though the absolute gain (~1 J point) is modest, suggesting that truly robust phase-transition understanding likely requires richer physical priors (e.g., from MLLMs) rather than purely appearance-based memory. Open questions include whether the entropy-based reverse refinement generalizes beyond the tested architectures, how annotation quality for ambiguous fluid/gas boundaries affects downstream evaluation, and whether the 30 fps annotation rate is sufficient for very rapid transitions.
