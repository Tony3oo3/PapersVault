## Problem

Existing region-level multimodal methods process referred regions at a fixed resolution, which forces a trade-off between capturing fine-grained details (needed for attribute detection) and preserving surrounding context (needed for captioning). This static treatment fails to match the resolution adaptability of human visual cognition, where foveation and saccadic eye movements dynamically shift focus depending on the task at hand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251422/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a referred region bounding box, then constructs a set of nested views by cropping and resizing at interpolation coefficients t ∈ [0,1] (t=0 is the tight crop of the region, t=1 is the full image), all at 224×224 resolution. At inference, the number and selection of views are determined dynamically: either by task prior (e.g., contextless views for attribute detection, context-rich views for captioning) or by image prior via a greedy pHASH-based search. Outputs are task-specific: region-level captions, multi-label tags, open-vocabulary attribute predictions, or region category labels, all generated from a single unified model.

## Method

DynRefer achieves dynamic resolution through two key procedures. During training (stochastic vision-language alignment), n views are randomly sampled from the nested set to simulate foveation/saccade, encoded by a frozen ViT with RoI-Align, spatially aligned via a dynamic-convolution offset module that warps each view's embedding toward the t=0 view, fused by an MLP, and compressed by a pretrained Q-former into a single region representation. This representation is decoded by three parallel heads—query-based tagging (asymmetric loss), region-text contrastive (sigmoid loss), and a frozen LLM (FlanT5XL) with control embeddings (cross-entropy)—enabling multi-task supervision. During inference (selectively multimodal referring), views are chosen either by task prior (empirically determined optimal t values per task type) or by image prior (greedy search maximizing incremental pHASH information while downweighting overly context-rich views via a 1/t_i penalty), allowing the model to adapt its effective resolution to each specific region and task.

## Results

On region-level captioning, DynRefer (4.2B) achieves 18.1 METEOR / 115.7 CIDEr on RefCOCOg and 21.2 METEOR / 190.9 CIDEr on Visual Genome, outperforming all baselines including larger 7B models. For open-vocabulary attribute detection on OVAD, DynRefer with EVA backbone reaches 29.2% mAP, surpassing the previous best (X-VLM, 28.1%). On open-vocabulary region recognition (COCO), DynRefer achieves 91.8% accuracy and 89.2 mAP, improving over RegionGPT by 8.8% accuracy and 19.2 mAP. For dense captioning with ground-truth localization, DynRefer reaches 47.4% mAP on VG V1.2, exceeding GRiT by 7.1 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynRefer outperforms all 30 compared baselines across every evaluated task. On region-level captioning (RefCOCOg), it beats the closest competitor ControlCap [71] by 1.1 METEOR (18.1 vs. 17.0) and 4.3 CIDEr (115.7 vs. 111.4), while using the same 4.2B model size; against larger 7B models like GLaMM [40] and RegionGPT [15], the margins widen to 1.9 METEOR and 9.7 CIDEr. On open-vocabulary attribute detection (OVAD), DynRefer (EVA) achieves 29.2% mAP, exceeding X-VLM [64] (28.1%) and BLIP-2 [27] (25.5%) by 1.1 and 3.7 mAP respectively, with particularly strong gains on Tail attributes (14.0 vs. 12.9). For region recognition on COCO, DynRefer (EVA) reaches 91.8% accuracy, surpassing RegionGPT [15] (80.6%) by 11.2 points and PVT [5] (64.5%) by 27.3 points. On dense captioning with GT localization, DynRefer (47.4% mAP on VG V1.2) outperforms GRiT [52] (40.3%) by 7.1 mAP and BLIP-2 [27] (37.9%) by 9.5 mAP. Notably, DynRefer achieves these gains with a smaller language model (FlanT5XL-3B vs. Vicuna-7B) and comparable vision FLOPs (~206G vs. 257G for GLaMM), while also running faster (4.81 FPS vs. 0.71 FPS for GLaMM on a single A100).

## Relation to Prior Work

DynRefer builds on the BLIP-2-style architecture (frozen ViT + Q-former + LLM) and the ControlCap framework for controllable region-level captioning, repurposing its query-based recognition decoder and control embedding mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from the fixed-resolution paradigm that dominates prior region-level multimodal methods (RegionGPT, GLaMM, Osprey, GRiT) by introducing stochastic multi-view embedding inspired by foveation and saccadic eye movements in human visual cognition. The selectively multimodal referring procedure—using task priors or a pHASH-based greedy search over nested views—has no direct precedent in the region-level referring literature, where prior work treats all visual regions at a single uniform resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single model with dynamic resolution can simultaneously achieve SOTA on region-level captioning, open-vocabulary attribute detection, region recognition, and dense captioning, with mutual improvement across tasks—a practical unification that reduces the need for task-specific architectures. The finding that increasing views beyond 3 degrades performance (due to combinatorial complexity of the representation manifold) provides a useful practical boundary for multi-view designs. Open questions include whether the pHASH-based view selection generalizes to video or 3D inputs, whether the approach extends to pixel-level (rather than box-level) referring, and whether the stochastic training strategy can be replaced by learned view selection to avoid the combinatorial explosion at higher view counts.
