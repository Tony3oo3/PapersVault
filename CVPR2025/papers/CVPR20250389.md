## Problem

Existing Video Scene Graph Generation methods model only pairwise object relationships within individual frames, making them unable to capture higher-order multi-object interactions or temporal causal dependencies across video sequences. Additionally, prior benchmarks are confined to SGG and SGA tasks, lacking annotations for reasoning tasks such as Video Question Answering, Video Captioning, and Relation Reasoning, which limits evaluation of comprehensive video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250389/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw video frames (encoded by CLIP-ViT-L-336 into 10 tokens per frame via 3×3 average pooling) and a text task query directing the specific task (SGG, SGA, VQA, VC, or RR). Objects are detected externally with Faster R-CNN, and the model outputs structured scene graph triplets (subject-relation-object), anticipated future-frame graphs, free-form video captions, VQA answers, or relation reasoning completions. The unified HyperGraph is serialized into the LLM's token stream as frame tokens interleaved with language tokens, so the LLM reasons directly over the graph structure.

## Method

HyperGLM constructs a unified HyperGraph that merges per-frame entity scene graphs (capturing spatial subject-relation-object triplets) with a procedural graph whose nodes are relationship categories and whose edges encode causal transition probabilities computed from observed frame-to-frame frequencies. A random-walk algorithm (Alg. 1) samples hyperedges connecting multiple nodes across both subgraphs, capturing higher-order interaction chains (e.g., holding → placing → releasing) that pairwise graphs cannot represent. These hyperedges are injected into a Mistral-7B-Instruct LLM via a CLIP-ViT-L-336 image encoder, a two-layer MLP projector, and a temporal aggregator that compresses T×N frame embeddings; the LLM is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 128, scaling 256) over two epochs. The key architectural distinction is that the HyperGraph provides the LLM with explicit multi-node relational structure, enabling it to reason about multi-way interactions and anticipate future relationships through the procedural graph's transition probabilities rather than relying solely on learned pairwise attention.

## Results

On the SGG task, HyperGLM achieves R/mR@20 of 7.5/2.8 on PVSG and 35.8/9.2 on VSGR, outperforming the strongest baseline CYCLO (5.8/2.0 and 29.4/7.1 respectively). On the SGA task, HyperGLM reaches R/mR@10 of 38.8/22.3 on Action Genome and 30.2/18.1 on VSGR, surpassing SceneSayerSDE (37.3/20.8 and 27.5/16.0). For reasoning tasks, HyperGLM attains 45.4% accuracy on VQA, 47.2% on Relation Reasoning, and 64.9% ROUGE-L on Video Captioning, leading all baselines on each task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperGLM consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across the five evaluated tasks. On SGG, it surpasses CYCLO [35] by 6.4 R@20 points on VSGR (35.8 vs 29.4) and 1.7 on PVSG (7.5 vs 5.8), while the earlier HIG [34] and Transformer [52] baselines trail by 12 and 10 R@20 points respectively on VSGR. On SGA, HyperGLM edges out the strongest competitor SceneSayerSDE [36] by 1.5 R@10 on Action Genome (38.8 vs 37.3) and 2.7 on VSGR (30.2 vs 27.5), with a larger mR gap of 2.2 and 2.1 points; the STTran [5] and DSGDeT [11] families lag by 18–20 R@10 points. On VQA, HyperGLM leads Chat-UniVi-7B [20] by 1.1 accuracy points (45.4 vs 44.3) and Video-ChatGPT [32] by 12.2 points. On Video Captioning, HyperGLM wins on ROUGE-L (64.9 vs 62.5 for MV-GPT [38]) and BLEU-4 (48.8 vs 47.2) but trails MV-GPT on CIDEr (54.5 vs 57.1), while decisively beating CoCap [40] and UniVL+MELTR [24] on all four metrics. On Relation Reasoning, HyperGLM achieves 47.2% accuracy, exceeding LLaMA-VID-7B [29] by 3.1 points and MA-LMM [14] by 4.4 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pairwise scene graph paradigm in VidSGG (STTran, DSGDetr, SceneSayer) by replacing binary edges with hyperedges that connect multiple nodes simultaneously, drawing on hypergraph theory previously applied to group activity recognition and video QA but not to scene graph generation. It further introduces a procedural graph—a frequency-based transition model over relationship categories—as a temporal counterpart to per-frame entity scene graphs, a construction not seen in prior VidSGG work. The LLM integration follows the standard CLIP-encoder + MLP-projector + instruction-tuned LLM recipe (Mistral-7B with LoRA), positioning the contribution primarily in the graph representation rather than the language model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VSGR dataset (1.9M frames, 3,748 videos, five tasks including the novel Relation Reasoning task, and three camera viewpoints) is a substantial practical resource that fills a gap left by prior benchmarks limited to SGG/SGA. The HyperGraph formulation offers a principled way to reason about multi-way interactions that pairwise graphs cannot express, and the procedural graph provides a lightweight, interpretable mechanism for temporal anticipation. However, the authors acknowledge that as the HyperGraph grows with more objects, essential relationships may become obscured, and the procedural graph's reliance on simple transition frequencies limits its ability to capture context-dependent or long-range causal dynamics—open questions for future work.
