## Problem

While 2D text-to-image models have achieved sub-second generation through diffusion distillation, text-to-3D generation remains orders of magnitude slower, typically requiring minutes to hours. Existing fast 3D methods either rely on iterative multi-view denoising (10+ steps) or suffer from quality degradation when naively distilled, as the multi-view teacher is already biased toward synthetic Objaverse-style appearance, and further distillation compounds this mode collapse.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single text prompt and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting asset in under 0.35 seconds on one A100 GPU. Internally, the pipeline generates 4 multi-view images at 256×256 resolution in latent space (not pixel space), which are then lifted into 3D Gaussians in a single forward pass. The 4 views are rendered at a fixed 20° elevation with equidistant azimuths, and the final 3DGS asset can be rendered from arbitrary novel viewpoints.

## Method

Turbo3D is a two-stage pipeline: a 4-step, 4-view latent diffusion generator followed by a single-step latent GS-LRM reconstructor. The generator is distilled from a multi-step multi-view DiT teacher using a novel Dual-Teacher DMD loss that combines an MV DMD term (enforcing cross-view consistency) with an SV DMD term from a separate single-view teacher trained on high-quality aesthetic images (enforcing per-view photorealism), directly addressing the compounding mode collapse problem. The reconstructor operates entirely in latent space, consuming the 4 MV latents directly without VAE decoding, which halves the transformer sequence length and eliminates the expensive Conv2D decoding step. The student generator is conditioned on Plücker embeddings for 3D awareness, and the entire system is trained on ~400K Objaverse objects with Cap3D captions across three phases: multi-view diffusion fine-tuning, dual-teacher distillation, and latent GS-LRM training.

## Results

Turbo3D achieves the highest CLIP score (27.61) and VQA score (0.76) among all compared text-to-3D methods, while also being the fastest at 0.35 seconds inference time on a single A100 GPU. This represents a ~43× speedup over Instant3D (15.02s) and a ~19× speedup over LGM (6.56s), while simultaneously improving CLIP score by 1.38 and 2.88 points respectively. In a user study with 56 participants, Turbo3D achieved an 89.8% win rate over LGM and a 74.9% win rate over Instant3D, while matching its own multi-step MV teacher at 50.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Turbo3D outperforms all four baselines on both quality metrics (CLIP and VQA) while being the fastest method by a large margin. Against the closest text-to-3D competitor Instant3D [17], Turbo3D gains +1.38 CLIP and +0.11 VQA while running ~43× faster (0.35s vs 15.02s). Against LGM [47], the advantage is even larger: +2.88 CLIP and +0.18 VQA at ~19× speed. The image-to-3D baselines TripoSR [49] and SV3D [51] are substantially weaker in quality (CLIP 23.85 and 24.92 respectively) though TripoSR is relatively fast at 1.19s. In the user study, Turbo3D was preferred over LGM 89.8% of the time and over Instant3D 74.9% of the time, while achieving parity (50.6%) with its own undistilled multi-step MV teacher, confirming that the dual-teacher distillation preserves generation quality at ~50× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

Turbo3D extends the multi-view generation + reconstruction paradigm (Instant3D, MVDream, LGM) by introducing diffusion distillation to collapse the iterative denoising into a 4-step generator, building directly on DMD/DMD2 for the distillation objective and GS-LRM for the reconstruction stage. The key departure is the dual-teacher distillation strategy: rather than distilling only from a multi-view teacher (which compounds the mode collapse already introduced by synthetic-data finetuning), the student is simultaneously pulled toward photorealism by a single-view teacher. The latent-space GS-LRM further departs from prior pixel-space reconstruction by operating directly on VAE latents, eliminating the decoding step and halving the transformer sequence length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sub-second text-to-3D generation on a single GPU is a practical milestone that could enable interactive 3D content creation workflows previously limited to minutes or hours. The identification of 'compounding mode collapse' as a distinct failure mode in multi-view distillation provides a useful diagnostic for the broader diffusion distillation community. Limitations include reliance on synthetic Objaverse training data (constraining generalization to real-world scenes), a fixed 4-view setup, and evaluation at 256 resolution in the main comparison; open questions include whether the dual-teacher approach scales to more views, higher resolutions, or scene-level generation beyond single objects.
