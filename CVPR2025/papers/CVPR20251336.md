## Problem

CLIP's text encoder is capped at 77 tokens, preventing it from encoding the rich detail in longer captions, and its reliance on global feature alignment limits fine-grained cross-modal understanding. Existing long-caption extensions (Long-CLIP, TULIP) address the token limit but still align only global features, neglecting local image-text correspondences that are critical for detailed retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251336/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FineLIP takes an RGB image (processed into ViT patches) and a long text caption (up to 248 tokens, roughly 4× CLIP's original limit) as input. It outputs a bidirectional image-text similarity score computed via token-level max-pooled cosine similarities in both image-to-text and text-to-image directions. The text encoder can also be swapped into the SDXL pipeline, where it produces conditioning features that guide long-text-to-image generation, evaluated by FID.

## Method

FineLIP extends CLIP in three stages. First, positional embeddings are stretched from 77 to 248 tokens by preserving the well-trained first 20 positions and adaptively interpolating the remainder, preserving pretrained alignment. Second, an Adaptive Token Refinement Module (ATRM) applies a learned, self-attention-inspired aggregation (with GELU nonlinearity and learnable temperature for sparsity) to both image and text tokens, compressing them to 20% of their original count while increasing per-token information density. Third, a Cross-Modal Late Interaction Module (CLIM) computes pairwise cosine similarities between all refined visual and text tokens, pools them via max in each direction, and optimizes with a dual Triplet Marginal Loss (margin 0.2) instead of standard contrastive loss, enabling fine-grained token-to-token alignment while retaining global [CLS]/[EOS] tokens for cross-granular matching.

## Results

On the L/14 backbone, FineLIP achieves 93.2% I2T R@1 and 93.0% T2I R@1 on Urban1k, and 82.2% I2T R@1 and 83.1% T2I R@1 on DOCCI, outperforming all baselines across both datasets and all three CLIP variants (B/16, L/14, bigG/14). The inference-enhanced variant FineLIP* further improves to 94.5% I2T R@1 and 93.9% T2I R@1 on Urban1k (L/14). For long-text-to-image generation via SDXL, FineLIP achieves FID 27.26 on Urban1k and 15.41 on DOCCI, beating the Baseline (29.54 / 16.88) and SPARC (31.60 / 17.24), while matching LAPS (26.74 / 15.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineLIP consistently outperforms all four compared methods across both Urban1k and DOCCI. Against the Baseline (positional-embedding stretching + global contrastive loss), FineLIP gains approximately 4 percentage points on I2T R@1 (0.932 vs. 0.892 on Urban1k, L/14) and 2.3 points on T2I R@1 (0.930 vs. 0.907). Against Long-CLIP, which also extends the token limit but relies solely on global features, FineLIP leads by a substantial margin of 10.5 points on Urban1k I2T R@1 (0.932 vs. 0.827). Against TULIP, which uses relative positional encodings for longer context, FineLIP improves I2T R@1 by 3.1 points (0.932 vs. 0.901). Against LAPS, the closest fine-grained competitor, FineLIP edges ahead by 1.1 points on Urban1k I2T R@1 (0.932 vs. 0.921) and 1.4 points on T2I R@1 (0.930 vs. 0.916). SPARC, designed for short-caption fine-grained alignment, underperforms significantly (0.772 I2T R@1), confirming that its patch-grouping strategy does not transfer well to long-caption settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

FineLIP builds directly on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture and adopts the positional embedding stretching strategy from Long-CLIP to extend the 77-token limit to 248. However, it departs from Long-CLIP and TULIP, which align only global features, by introducing token-level fine-grained cross-modal alignment inspired by FILIP, SPARC, and LAPS — methods previously validated only on short captions. The key architectural novelty is the Adaptive Token Refinement Module (ATRM), which applies learned token aggregation to both visual and textual branches before alignment, a step absent in prior fine-grained CLIP variants that refine only the visual side. The Cross-Modal Late Interaction (CLIM) module with bidirectional max-pooling and triplet marginal loss further distinguishes FineLIP from the contrastive-loss-based approaches in the Long-CLIP/TULIP line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling CLIP-style models to process detailed, long captions while preserving pre-trained alignment, FineLIP addresses a practical bottleneck for applications requiring rich scene descriptions (e.g., retrieval over detailed annotations, long-prompt image generation via SDXL). The paper acknowledges that performance on short-caption retrieval and classification degrades, leaving open the question of a unified model handling both caption lengths. The integration of the FineLIP text encoder into SDXL demonstrates a concrete downstream path, but broader adoption will depend on whether the short-caption regression can be resolved through joint long/short training as the authors suggest.
