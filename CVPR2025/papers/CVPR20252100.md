## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces spurious floaters and view-specific artifacts when training images contain distractors (moving people, vegetation, transient effects) that violate the multi-view photometric consistency assumption. Existing distractor-free methods rely on external pre-trained networks for semantic segmentation or uncertainty prediction, introducing additional computational overhead and leaving open whether the 3DGS rendering framework itself can be modified to directly separate static and distractor content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252100/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (downscaled 8× for RobustNeRF/On-the-go, 4× for Patio) and a COLMAP sparse point cloud for static Gaussian initialization. Per-view distractor Gaussians (K=1000 per view) are initialized on a 2D plane at depth ρ=0.02 in front of each camera, using RGB vectors rather than spherical harmonics since they are view-specific. The output is an explicit decomposition into a global static Gaussian set and per-view distractor Gaussian sets, enabling independent rendering of static and transient components at 3DGS-level speed.

## Method

DeSplat decomposes the 3DGS volume rendering equation into two independently alpha-composited images (static and distractor) that are combined via c_comp = c_d + (1−α_d)·c_s, allowing the photometric loss to jointly supervise both components. Static Gaussians are initialized globally from COLMAP and optimized across all views, while per-view distractor Gaussians are optimized only when their associated camera is selected, with Adaptive Density Control applied after S=10 training passes per image. The loss adds L1 regularizers on the static and distractor alpha maps (λ_s|1−α_s| + λ_d|α_d|) to discourage distractor Gaussians from explaining static content and to prevent holes in the background. Notably, the method requires no external networks, pre-trained models, or pre-processing steps—distractor separation emerges purely from the decomposed splatting formulation and photometric supervision.

## Results

On the RobustNeRF dataset, DeSplat achieves the best PSNR on 4 out of 5 scenes (Statue 23.40, Crab(1) 36.15, Crab(2) 35.23, BabyYoda 35.60) and competitive results on Android (24.80 vs. Splatfacto-W-T's 25.39). On the On-the-go dataset, DeSplat leads on high-occlusion scenes (Patio-High: PSNR 22.59, SSIM 0.84, LPIPS 0.12) and is competitive on medium-occlusion scenes (Patio: PSNR 26.07, SSIM 0.90), but underperforms on low-occlusion outdoor scenes (Mountain: 19.59, Fountain: 20.27) where distractor Gaussians confuse sky regions. The method maintains near-identical rendering speed to vanilla 3DGS while requiring no external pre-trained networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeSplat outperforms the vanilla Splatfacto baseline by 0.65–11.35 dB PSNR across RobustNeRF scenes, with the largest gains on Crab(1) (+11.35 dB) and Crab(2) (+4.03 dB) where distractors are most prevalent. Against Splatfacto-W-T (which uses robust masks for distractor detection), DeSplat wins on Statue (+0.19 dB), Crab(1) (+7.41 dB), Crab(2) (+10.82 dB), and BabyYoda (+4.07 dB), but trails on Android (−0.59 dB). Compared to SpotlessSplats (SLS-mlp), which relies on Stable Diffusion semantic features, DeSplat achieves higher PSNR on Statue (+0.71 dB), Crab(1) (+0.30 dB), Crab(2) (+0.88 dB), and BabyYoda (+2.00 dB), while SLS-mlp leads on Android (+0.35 dB). On the On-the-go dataset, DeSplat beats WildGaussians by 1.46–2.25 dB PSNR on medium/high-occlusion scenes (Corner, Patio, Patio-High) but falls behind SLS-mlp on Mountain (−2.94 dB) and Fountain (−2.54 dB) due to sky misclassification. The key advantage is that DeSplat achieves these results without any pre-trained network, maintaining 3DGS-level rendering speed, whereas SLS-mlp, WildGaussians, and Splatfacto-W-T all require additional neural components.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeSplat builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) by decomposing its volume rendering equation into two independently optimized sets of Gaussians: a global static set and per-view distractor sets initialized on a 2D plane in front of each camera. This departs sharply from the dominant line of distractor-free methods (RobustNeRF, NeRF On-the-go, SpotlessSplats, WildGaussians, Splatfacto-W) that rely on external pre-trained networks (DINOv2, Stable Diffusion, SAM) or learned MLPs to detect or model transient effects. The per-view distractor initialization draws conceptual inspiration from 2D Gaussian Splatting (Zhang et al., 2024), repurposing the idea of view-local Gaussian primitives to absorb multi-view-inconsistent content rather than to represent a single image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that robust 3DGS reconstruction against distractors can be achieved without any external semantic model, pre-processing step, or additional neural network, preserving the original 3DGS rendering speed and pipeline compatibility. The explicit scene separation (static vs. distractor Gaussians) is unique among prior methods, which typically only mask distractor pixels rather than reconstructing them as a separate component. However, the method has clear limitations: it struggles with outdoor scenes where sky is misclassified as a distractor, fails when distractors appear static across most views, and can produce boundary artifacts. An open question is whether the per-view distractor formulation can be extended to handle genuinely dynamic (moving) objects rather than merely transient occluders, and whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale unstructured photo collections beyond the tested benchmarks.
