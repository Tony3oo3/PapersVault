## Problem

Contrastively-trained VLMs like CLIP excel at image-text discrimination but exhibit bag-of-words behavior and limited compositional understanding, while LVLMs like LLaVA offer strong language reasoning yet their autoregressive training makes them poorly suited for discriminative retrieval tasks. The paper tackles how to convert a generative LVLM into a discriminative one that retains its compositional strengths while achieving state-of-the-art retrieval performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250381/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, an RGB image is passed through the full LVLM (vision encoder → projection → LLM) alongside a learned soft prompt, and the hidden state at the final token position serves as the image embedding; a text query is passed through the LLM alone with a separate soft prompt, yielding a text embedding at the same position. The two embeddings are compared via cosine similarity to produce a retrieval score. No tokens are generated at test time—the model operates purely in embedding mode, with the soft prompts (initialized from handcrafted prompts like 'Summarize the provided image in one word') replacing the original hard prompts.

## Method

VladVA introduces a hybrid training framework that jointly optimizes a symmetric contrastive loss on short captions (<30 tokens) and a next-token prediction loss on long captions (30–500 tokens) within a single forward pass per sample. The contrastive loss is applied to the summary-token embeddings of both modalities, while the AR loss is applied over the long caption tokens, addressing the observation that contrastive training collapses on long captions due to task triviality. For parameter-efficient adaptation, the method combines modality-specific soft prompts (learnable vectors replacing handcrafted prompt tokens) with LoRA adapters (rank 16) on the LLM's linear layers, keeping the vision encoder frozen. The key design insight is that short captions teach high-level discrimination while long captions, processed autoregressively, force the model to condense fine-grained visual and compositional information into the summary token, and both signals are necessary for strong performance on both retrieval and compositionality benchmarks.

## Results

VladVA (LLaVA-1.5-7B) achieves 85.0% R@1 on Flickr30k, 59.0% R@1 on COCO, and 72.3% R@1 on nocaps for image retrieval, outperforming the much larger EVA-CLIP (18B) by 1.7, 3.4, and 3.0 points respectively. For text retrieval, it reaches 94.3% R@1 on Flickr30k and 72.9% R@1 on COCO. On the SugarCrepe compositionality benchmark, VladVA achieves 86.8% on relation replacement and 79.0% on object swap, surpassing EVA-CLIP (18B) by 10.7 and 13.7 points respectively, directly addressing the bag-of-words weakness of CLIP-style models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VladVA (7.06B params) outperforms all CLIP-style two-tower baselines by large margins: +17.7 R@1 over CLIP (ViT-L) and +1.7 R@1 over EVA-CLIP (18B) on Flickr30k image retrieval, despite using a model 2.6× smaller than the 18B model. Among LVLM-based methods, VladVA surpasses E5-V (LLaVA-Next-8B, 8.36B) by 5.5 R@1 on Flickr30k and 7.0 R@1 on COCO for image retrieval, and VLM2Vec (Mistral-7B) by 4.9 and 7.0 R@1 respectively. On SugarCrepe compositionality, VladVA wins on all seven sub-tasks against every baseline, with the largest gaps on object swap (+13.7 over EVA-CLIP 18B) and attribute adding (+10.8 over EVA-CLIP 18B), directly targeting the bag-of-words failure mode. The only setting where VladVA does not lead is SugarCrepe++ TOT (text-to-text), where E5-V's text-text training gives it an edge, though the authors note this loss can be incorporated into their framework. Compared to the untrained LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline, VladVA gains +25.4 R@1 on Flickr30k image retrieval, demonstrating the effectiveness of the discriminative fine-tuning framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-1.5-7B architecture and adapts it from a generative to a discriminative model, drawing on soft prompting (prefix-tuning) and LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning. It directly departs from the concurrent E5-V work, which found that contrastive image-text fine-tuning is detrimental to LVLMs and restricted training to text-text contrastive learning; VladVA instead demonstrates that a hybrid contrastive + next-token prediction framework with variable-length captions successfully unlocks discriminative capabilities. It also contrasts with VLM2Vec, which adapts LVLMs for retrieval but omits the generative loss, short-long caption strategy, and soft prompting that this paper shows to be important.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper bridges a key gap between generative LVLMs (strong language understanding and compositionality) and discriminative two-tower VLMs (strong retrieval), offering a practical parameter-efficient recipe that practitioners can apply to existing LVLMs. The finding that contrastive image-text training is not inherently harmful to LVLMs—when paired with the right data granularity and loss combination—challenges a recent concurrent conclusion and opens the door to richer training signals. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger or architecturally different LVLMs, whether incorporating text-text contrastive loss (as in E5-V) further improves performance, and how much additional gain is available from scaling training data beyond the 8.1M samples tested.
