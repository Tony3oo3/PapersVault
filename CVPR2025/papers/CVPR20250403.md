## Problem

Sharing SfM maps across devices requires registering partial reconstructions into a joint frame, but no scalable training datasets or methods exist for SfM point cloud registration. Current SotA registration models trained on RGB-D/LiDAR datasets (e.g., 3DMatch) fail on SfM point clouds due to the large distribution shift in scene scale, point density, and geometric structure. The paper asks whether SfM maps can be merged using only 3D geometry, without visual descriptors, image retrieval, or topological data.

## Input / Output

The method takes two SfM point clouds P=(P,N,X) and Q=(Q,M,Y) consisting of 3D coordinates, estimated normals, and (optionally) features that are always set to constants in this work. Point clouds are normalized by their largest singular value to handle unknown relative scale, and normals are oriented consistently by aligning toward a random observing camera center. The output is a set of point correspondences from which the relative similarity transform (s,R,t) is recovered via RANSAC, with evaluation under both SE(3) (known scale) and Sim(3) (unknown scale) settings.

## Method

RefineRoITr builds on the SE(3)-invariant RoITr architecture, which encodes local geometry via Point Pair Features (PPFs) for rotation invariance, processes them through an encoder and a global Transformer to extract coarse superpoint correspondences, and then refines matches via Sinkhorn optimal transport. The key addition is a local refinement Transformer r_θ that takes the coarse correspondence neighbourhoods and applies interleaved self-attention and cross-attention layers (inspired by LightGlue but restricted to local patches) to produce enriched cross-point-cloud fine features before the final Sinkhorn matching. For training data, the paper introduces a pipeline that generates synthetic partial reconstructions from MegaDepth scenes by sampling camera trajectories (bridging the gap between random image collections and sequential video) and retriangulating with fixed global poses, yielding ~22,000 registration pairs with known ground-truth correspondences. The model is trained with a superpoint circle loss plus a point-level NLL loss on Sinkhorn-transported correspondences, and can be trained from scratch or fine-tuned from 3DMatch pre-training.

## Results

On the MegaDepth SfM registration benchmark (SE(3) setting), RefineRoITr trained from scratch on the proposed dataset achieves 51.0% Inlier Ratio (IR), 96.5% Feature Matching Recall (FMR), and 70.2% Registration Recall (RR), substantially outperforming RoITr trained only on 3DMatch (3.0% IR, 12.6% FMR, 0.0% RR). Under the more challenging Sim(3) setting (unknown scale), the same model achieves 44.6% IR, 92.8% FMR, and 42.7% RR. On Cambridge Landmarks, RefineRoITr(Mega) reaches up to 81.8% IR (St Mary's Church) with 5,167 matches, versus at most 2.8% IR for the best 3DMatch-only baseline. The refinement Transformer adds roughly 3% computational overhead (102.84 ms vs. 99.94 ms median runtime on an RTX 4090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four baselines trained exclusively on 3DMatch (OverlapPredator, GeoTransformer, PareNet, RoITr) fail dramatically on SfM point clouds, achieving at most 7.3% IR and 38.6% FMR on SE(3) MegaDepth, confirming a large distribution shift between RGB-D/LiDAR scans and SfM reconstructions. The dominant factor is training on the proposed MegaDepth-derived dataset: fine-tuning RoITr on it lifts IR from 3.0% to 44.6% and RR from 0.0% to 60.0%. RefineRoITr trained from scratch on the proposed dataset (51.0% IR, 96.5% FMR, 70.2% RR) outperforms the fine-tuned RoITr (44.6/90.9/60.0) by 6.4 IR points and 10.2 RR points, showing the refinement Transformer's contribution. The fine-tuned RefineRoITr (48.7/95.1/67.7) sits between the two, confirming that the refinement stage adds consistent gains regardless of training data. On the low-overlap Quad6k benchmark (16.5% overlap vs. 61.2% on MegaDepth), RefineRoITr(Mega) still achieves 17.2% IR and 67.4% FMR, while all 3DMatch-only baselines remain below 2% IR, demonstrating generalization to challenging registration scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions SfM map merging as a point cloud registration problem, departing from the dominant descriptor-based approaches (3D-3D matching, 2D-3D correspondences) that require storing and matching visual features. Methodologically, it builds directly on RoITr (Yu et al., CVPR 2023) as the SE(3)-invariant baseline and adapts the interleaved self+cross attention pattern from LightGlue (Lindenberger et al., ICCV 2023) into a local refinement transformer. The key conceptual departure from prior registration work (PREDATOR, GeoTransformer, RoITr) is the identification of a distribution shift: methods trained on RGB-D/LiDAR data (3DMatch) fail on SfM point clouds, motivating a dedicated synthetic dataset generation pipeline from random-image SfM reconstructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for visual descriptors, ColabSfM addresses privacy (no feature inversion attacks), scalability (no 2-3 order-of-magnitude map size increase), and interoperability (no shared feature extractor) concerns that plague current collaborative mapping systems. The synthetic dataset generation pipeline is a practical contribution that could enable further research on SfM-specific registration. Limitations include SE(3) invariance struggling with symmetric scenes, evaluation on retriangulated (drift-free) reconstructions, and relatively small scene sizes (~15K points); open questions include how the approach scales to city-level maps with accumulated drift and whether the geometric-only formulation can match descriptor-based accuracy in texture-poor environments.
