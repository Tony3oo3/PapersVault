## Problem

Existing agglomerative vision foundation models that distill from multiple teachers (CLIP, DINOv2, SAM) suffer from a critical 'mode switching' phenomenon where feature distributions shift dramatically with input resolution, causing quality degradation at high resolutions. Additionally, teacher activation magnitudes are imbalanced (SAM dominates CLIP/DINOv2), and high-resolution features produce excessive token counts that are impractical for VLM integration due to quadratic attention complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251438/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at arbitrary resolutions (tested from 256² to 1920²) and produces a summary token plus patch tokens at the corresponding spatial resolution, leveraging Cropped Position Embeddings (CPE) for resolution flexibility. For VLM integration, a bipartite soft-matching token merging step compresses the patch tokens to a fixed count (e.g., 196 or 256) before feeding into the language model, decoupling the vision encoder's native resolution from the LLM's sequence length. The same feature set supports downstream tasks including zero-shot classification, linear-probe semantic segmentation, instance segmentation via a SAM adaptor head, and 3D probing (depth, normals).

## Method

RADIOv2.5 introduces a three-stage multi-resolution training schedule (256² → 432² → 432²+1024²) that trains the ViT student against all teachers simultaneously at each resolution, eliminating mode switching by ensuring the student sees consistent teacher signals across scales. To efficiently train against SAM at sub-1024² resolutions without costly per-image SAM inference, the method uses mosaic augmentation: packing k×k smaller images into a single 1024² canvas, running SAM once, and cropping out individual feature maps. Teacher loss imbalance is addressed via PHI-S (PCA-Hadamard Isotropic Standardization), which rotates and rescales each teacher's activations to unit per-channel variance before distillation. For VLM integration, bipartite soft matching (ToMe) with strided partitioning merges the most similar token pairs at the encoder output, preserving spatial coverage while reducing token count; the paper shows this outperforms pixel unshuffling by an average of 5.9 points on VLM benchmarks and benefits RADIOv2.5 disproportionately compared to SigLIP.

## Results

On VLM benchmarks (Table 5), RADIOv2.5-H (653M params, 768² resolution, 196 tokens/image) achieves an average score of 63.19, outperforming SigLIP-SO400M (60.95), RADIOv2.1-H at 768² (56.66), and OpenAI-CLIP (58.55). On dense tasks (Table 4), RADIOv2.5-H achieves 53.97 mIoU on NYUDv2 semantic segmentation and 83.43 mIoU on Pascal Context parsing, surpassing RADIOv2.1-H (51.36 / 82.78) and UNIT-632M (50.19 / 82.36). The 1.1B RADIOv2.5-g variant reaches 54.56 mIoU on NYUDv2, the best among all compared agglomerative models. Token merging (ToMe) at 196 tokens yields an average +5.9 point improvement over pixel unshuffling on VLM benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SigLIP-SO400M (the strongest non-agglomerative baseline), RADIOv2.5-H wins by +2.24 points on average VLM score, with particularly large gains on ChartQA (+5.9) and DocVQA (+6.3), while SigLIP retains a slight edge on SEED(I) and MMMU. Compared to RADIOv2.1-H at the same 768² resolution, RADIOv2.5-H improves the average by +6.53 points, demonstrating that multi-resolution training and token merging substantially fix the mode-switching degradation that plagues the original AM-RADIO at high resolution. On dense tasks, RADIOv2.5-L (320M) outperforms UNIC-L (307M) by +4.65 mIoU on NYUDv2 semantic segmentation and +1.05 on Pascal Context parsing, while RADIOv2.5-H (653M) beats UNIT-632M by +3.78 mIoU on NYUDv2. RADIOv2.5-B (98M) surpasses Theia (86M) by +13.39 mIoU on NYUDv2 SemSeg and +11.91 on Pascal Context parsing, though Theia retains a small edge on Pascal Context Saliency. SAM-CLIP could not be directly compared due to unavailable model weights, limiting the dense-task comparison to Theia, UNIC, and UNIT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agglomerative multi-teacher distillation paradigm established by AM-RADIO, SAM-CLIP, UNIC, and Theia, which distill knowledge from vision foundation models (CLIP, DINOv2, SAM) into a unified student. Its key departures are methodological: multi-resolution training to eliminate the 'mode switching' artifact identified in AM-RADIO, mosaic augmentation to reduce SAM inference cost, PHI-S standardization for teacher loss balancing, and ToMe-based token compression for VLM integration. Unlike Eagle or Cambrian-1, which aggregate multiple encoders at inference time, RADIOv2.5 produces a single versatile encoder that can be plugged into any downstream pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-resolution training recipe and token merging technique are immediately practical for anyone building or deploying agglomerative vision models, and the released weights at four scales (B, L, H, g) lower the barrier to adoption. The finding that token merging yields larger relative gains on RADIOv2.5 than on SigLIP suggests that feature quality (lower noise) is a prerequisite for effective compression, an insight that could guide future encoder design. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to additional teacher families (e.g., video models, depth estimators), whether the specific three-teacher combination is near-optimal, and whether the VLM integration benefits persist with larger or differently-architected LLMs.
