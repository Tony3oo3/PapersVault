## Problem

Despite the rapid proliferation of diverse AI-generated forgeries, no existing benchmark comprehensively evaluates Large Vision Language Models' forgery detection capabilities across the full spectrum of forgery semantics, modalities, tasks, types, and generating models. Prior benchmarks (FakeBench, MMFakeBench, MFC-Bench) cover only limited forgery categories, leaving LVLMs' true detection boundaries uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251010/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a visual input (RGB image, NIR image, extracted video frames, or image-text composite) with a four-option multi-choice question. For spatial localization tasks, incorrect options are generated by applying random perturbations to the ground-truth segmentation mask or bounding box, ensuring distractors remain visually plausible. The model outputs a single option letter, scored by accuracy; evaluation uses a three-stage extraction pipeline (letter match → content match → ChatGPT-assisted parsing) via the OpenCompass/VLMEvalKit toolkit.

## Method

Forensics-Bench is constructed via a top-down pipeline: co-authors enumerate forgery categories across five orthogonal perspectives (semantics, modality, task, type, model), retrieve matching public datasets, cleanse and standardize them into a unified metadata schema, then generate multi-choice Q&A pairs manually or with ChatGPT. The resulting 63,292 samples span 112 unique forgery detection types, 21 forgery types, 22 generating models, and 4 modalities, with a forgery-to-real ratio following prior dataset conventions. Two supplementary protocols extend evaluation: (1) Robust Forgery Detection applies six perturbation types (saturation, contrast, block distortion, Gaussian noise, blur, JPEG) at five intensity levels to test stability, and (2) Forgery Attribution repurposes binary classification samples into model-identification questions with four randomly sampled model options. The benchmark is used to evaluate 25 LVLMs (22 open-source, 3 proprietary) without any fine-tuning, measuring accuracy per perspective and overall.

## Results

On Forensics-Bench (63,292 multi-choice questions across 112 forgery detection types), the best-performing model LLaVA-NEXT-34B achieves only 66.7% overall accuracy, underscoring the difficulty of comprehensive forgery detection. Among proprietary models, GPT-4o leads at 57.9% overall, still trailing the top open-source model by nearly 9 points. Performance is most uneven across the five perspectives: the forgery-model perspective is easiest (LLaVA-NEXT-34B: 72.7%), while the forgery-task perspective (spatial/temporal localization) is hardest, with even the best model (InternVL-Chat-V1-2) reaching only 43.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 25 evaluated LVLMs are substantially challenged by Forensics-Bench, with the best (LLaVA-NEXT-34B) reaching only 66.7% overall accuracy. Open-source LLaVA-series models dominate the top of the leaderboard: LLaVA-NEXT-34B (66.7%), LLaVA-v1.5-7B-XTuner (65.7%), and LLaVA-v1.5-13B-XTuner (65.2%) all outperform the strongest proprietary model GPT-4o (57.9%) by 8–9 points, which the authors attribute to proprietary models' tendency to give conservative, non-committal answers. Among proprietary models, GPT-4o (57.9%) clearly leads Gemini-1.5-Pro (48.3%) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (33.8%). InternVL-Chat-V1-2 (62.2%) is the strongest non-LLaVA open-source model and achieves the best Task-perspective score (43.4%), while mPLUG-Owl2 (57.8%) and LLaVA-NEXT-13B (58.0%) form a mid-tier cluster. At the bottom, Monkey-Chat (27.2%), Qwen-VL-Chat (34.5%), and InstructBLIP-13B (37.3%) struggle across all five perspectives, with Monkey-Chat scoring below 22% on every individual perspective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LVLM evaluation benchmarks (MM-Bench, MMMU, MMT-Bench) into the forgery detection domain, while also building on prior forgery-specific LVLM benchmarks (FakeBench, MMFakeBench, MFC-Bench) that each covered only a narrow slice of forgery types or modalities. Rather than proposing a new detection method, it repurposes existing public forgery datasets (FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF, DFDC, OpenForensics, ForgeryNIR, etc.) into a unified multi-choice Q&A format spanning five orthogonal perspectives—semantics, modality, task, type, and generating model—creating the first benchmark to simultaneously stress-test all five dimensions. The evaluation protocol leverages the OpenCompass/VLMEvalKit infrastructure, positioning the work squarely within the empirical-study tradition of LVLM capability mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With the best model (LLaVA-NEXT-34B) achieving only 66.7% overall accuracy and large intra-model gaps across forgery types (near 100% for spoofing vs. below 55% for multi-face swap), the results reveal that current LVLMs possess only a partial, biased understanding of synthetic media rather than a generalized forensic capability. The finding that proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) systematically underperform open-source LLaVA variants—attributed to conservative refusal behavior—challenges the assumption that commercial frontier models lead on every multimodal task. Open questions include whether instruction-tuning on forgery-specific data can close the type-level gaps, how performance degrades as new AIGC architectures emerge, and whether the robustness anomalies (e.g., block distortion aiding detection) can be exploited for practical deployment.
