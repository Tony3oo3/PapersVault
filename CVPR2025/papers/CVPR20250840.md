## Problem

Existing federated learning methods with pre-trained models still rely on gradient-based iterative optimization, requiring multi-epoch local training and multi-round aggregation across clients. This introduces vulnerabilities to data heterogeneity (non-IID), performance degradation at large client counts (≥1000), slow convergence, and high communication overhead. No prior FL framework achieves exact equivalence to centralized joint training in a single aggregation round.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250840/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) paired with class labels, which are passed through a frozen pre-trained backbone (ResNet-18, VGG11, or ViT-B16 from ImageNet-1k) to produce fixed-dimension embedding vectors. The only trainable parameter is a linear classification head (embedding dimension × number of classes), solved in closed form per client. The server outputs a single aggregated weight matrix that maps embeddings to class labels, requiring no iterative refinement.

## Method

AFL replaces gradient-based FL with a two-stage analytic pipeline. In the local stage, each client extracts embeddings via the frozen backbone and solves a least-squares problem via the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, yielding a closed-form weight in one epoch. In the aggregation stage, the Absolute Aggregation (AA) law (Theorem 1) provides a closed-form formula—derived from the partitioned MP inverse identity—that combines independently trained client weights into the exact joint-trained weight in a single pairwise aggregation pass, eliminating all iterative rounds. For rank-deficient scenarios (large K where N_k < embedding dimension), a regularization intermediary (RI) process adds a γI term during local solving and removes it post-aggregation via a one-to-one mapping (Theorem 2), restoring optimality without tuning. The resulting framework is provably invariant to data partitioning: the aggregated weight is identical regardless of non-IID degree or client count.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, AFL achieves 80.75% top-1 accuracy across all non-IID settings (α=0.1, α=0.05, s=4, s=2), outperforming the best baseline (FedDyn at 73.50%) by over 7 points. On CIFAR-100, AFL reaches 58.56% consistently regardless of partitioning, while baselines degrade severely under extreme non-IID (e.g., FedAvg drops from 56.62% to 32.99% as α goes from 0.1 to 0.01). On Tiny-ImageNet, AFL attains 54.67% across all settings, surpassing the best baseline (FedDyn at 47.72%) by nearly 7 points. Notably, AFL reports zero standard deviation across repeated runs due to its deterministic closed-form solution, and completes training in ~237s (CIFAR-100) versus 30,000–50,000s for gradient-based FL methods, yielding ~150–200× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFL decisively outperforms all seven baselines on CIFAR-10 (80.75% vs. best 73.50% for FedDyn) and Tiny-ImageNet (54.67% vs. best 47.72% for FedDyn) across every non-IID setting. On CIFAR-100, AFL (58.56%) slightly trails FedDyn (61.09%) and FedGen (60.93%) under the milder NIID-2 (s=10) setting, but dominates under extreme non-IID (α=0.01) where FedDyn drops to 36.12% and FedDisco to 25.72%. The most striking contrast is robustness: while FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, and FedNTD all suffer 20–30 point accuracy drops as heterogeneity increases (e.g., FedAvg on CIFAR-100: 56.62%→32.99%), AFL's accuracy remains perfectly constant at 58.56% regardless of α or s. This invariance extends to client count: scaling from 100 to 1000 clients causes FedAvg to drop from 56.57% to 41.01% on CIFAR-100, while AFL maintains 58.56%. In terms of efficiency, AFL completes a single aggregation round in ~237s versus 30,000–50,000s for multi-round baselines, a 150–200× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the analytic (pseudoinverse) learning paradigm—originally developed for one-epoch training of shallow and deep networks—into the federated learning setting with pre-trained backbones. The key theoretical departure from gradient-based FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, etc.) is the derivation of an Absolute Aggregation Law, which reformulates Cline's 1964 partitioned-matrix inverse identity into a closed-form rule for combining independently trained client weights in a single round. Unlike prior FL-with-pretrained-models work that still relies on iterative local optimization and multi-round server aggregation, AFL replaces both stages with exact least-squares solutions, making the entire pipeline gradient-free and deterministic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-round aggregation eliminates the dominant communication bottleneck in FL (yielding ~150–200× wall-clock speedups) and, more fundamentally, the invariance-to-data-partitioning property means non-IID heterogeneity and client count no longer degrade accuracy—a stark contrast to every gradient-based baseline tested. Open questions remain: the method is restricted to a linear head on a frozen backbone, requires full client participation (no stragglers or partial participation), and the rank-deficiency workaround (RI process) introduces a removable but non-trivial regularization step. Extending the AA law to nonlinear heads, multi-layer analytic training, and asynchronous/partial-participation settings are natural next steps that would broaden practical applicability.
