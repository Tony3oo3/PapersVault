## Problem

Existing multi-modal human motion understanding systems (e.g., MotionLLM) rely on implicit cross-modal alignment only during LLM fine-tuning, leaving encoder features unaligned across video and motion modalities. Additionally, standard masked autoencoding pre-training inherently discards high-frequency temporal information, producing over-smoothed reconstructions that prevent the discovery of fine-grained motion concepts needed for accurate reasoning about human behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251399/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts either a video clip (represented as T keyframes) or a 3D motion sequence (K consecutive pose frames) paired with a free-form text question, and generates a natural-language answer. Video frames are encoded by a pre-trained SigLIP model (patch size 16, hidden dim 256) while motion is encoded by a 4-layer transformer; both are quantized into a shared 512-entry VQ-VAE codebook before being projected into the Llama3.1-8B token space via lightweight translation layers. The final LLM is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 8) on 200K+ generated Q&A pairs.

## Method

HuMoCon pre-trains video and motion encoders under four joint objectives: (1) masked reconstruction of the original input from a VQ-VAE-quantized codebook, (2) velocity reconstruction via two sub-objectives—discriminative informativeness (a hyper-network generates per-code classifiers to sharpen feature separation) and actionable informativeness (a network predicts optical flow or delta-motion from discriminator gradients to recover high-frequency dynamics), (3) explicit cross-modal alignment loss that projects paired video and motion discrete features into a shared space via learned projection layers, and (4) the combined total loss weighted by λ coefficients. After encoder pre-training, frozen encoders feed through trainable modality translation layers into Llama3.1-8B, which is instruction-tuned with LoRA on motion-video Q&A data, enabling the model to answer questions about human motion from either video or pose input.

## Results

On BABEL-QA (motion understanding), HuMoCon achieves 0.711 overall accuracy, outperforming the best baseline MotionLLM (0.436) by a relative margin of ~63%. The largest gains appear on the BodyPart query type (0.623 vs. 0.272 for MotionCLIP-M, a 2.29× improvement) and the Direction query type (0.697 vs. 0.354 for MotionLLM). On ActivityNet-QA (video understanding), HuMoCon reaches 54.2% accuracy and 3.6 LLM-evaluated score, edging out MotionLLM (53.3% / 3.5) and VideoChat2 (49.1% / 3.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HuMoCon outperforms all ten baselines on both benchmarks. On BABEL-QA, the improvement over the strongest baseline MotionLLM is 0.711 vs. 0.436 overall (+27.5 absolute), with particularly large gains on BodyPart queries (0.623 vs. 0.154) attributed to velocity reconstruction capturing high-frequency joint dynamics. Against classification-based baselines 2s-AGCN (0.355–0.357) and MotionCLIP (0.420–0.430), the margin exceeds 28 points, reflecting the advantage of an LLM-based generative approach with explicit cross-modal alignment. On ActivityNet-QA, HuMoCon (54.2% acc / 3.6 score) beats the closest competitor MotionLLM (53.3% / 3.5) by a narrow margin, and surpasses VideoChat2 (49.1% / 3.3) and Video-LLaVA (45.3% / 3.3) by 5.1 and 8.9 points respectively. The largest video-side gains are over older baselines: Video-LLaMA (12.4%), LLaMA-Adapter (34.2%), and FrozenBiLM (24.7%), where the multi-modal encoder pre-training and explicit alignment provide substantial benefits. Notably, the paper does not report results on MotionLLM's own large-scale benchmark, as it was not yet publicly available at submission time.

## Relation to Prior Work

HuMoCon extends MotionLLM's encoder–projector–LLM pipeline for joint video and motion understanding, but departs from it by performing explicit cross-modal feature alignment during encoder pre-training rather than relying on implicit alignment through paired data during LLM fine-tuning. The velocity reconstruction mechanism (discriminative and actionable informativeness) is adapted from InfoCon's concept-discovery framework for robotic manipulation, repurposed here to combat temporal over-smoothing in masked autoencoder pre-training. The overall architecture combines VQ-VAE quantization, SigLIP-based video encoding, and Llama3.1-8B with LoRA, positioning the work at the intersection of self-supervised encoder pre-training and multi-modal LLM instruction tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit encoder-level alignment and velocity reconstruction address two concrete failure modes in multi-modal motion understanding: environmental bias in video features and high-frequency detail loss in masked reconstructions. The ~63% relative improvement over MotionLLM on BABEL-QA is substantial, though the comparison set includes older baselines (2s-AGCN, MotionCLIP) and the ActivityNet-QA gain over MotionLLM is marginal (53.3→54.2 accuracy). Open questions include whether the alignment strategy generalizes beyond paired Motion-X data, how the framework handles human-environment contact and niche motions (acknowledged limitations), and whether the velocity reconstruction idea transfers to other temporal reconstruction settings beyond human motion.
