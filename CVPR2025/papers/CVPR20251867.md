## Problem

CLIP-based prompt learning for UDA relies on zero-shot pseudo-labels that degrade under covariate shift between the target domain and CLIP's pre-training distribution, and existing methods focus on aligning source-target prompts in text space rather than ensuring target text embeddings geometrically represent the visual embedding clusters in the target domain. This leads to target prompts that perform poorly in isolation and fail to capture domain-specific visual structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251867/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled RGB images from one or more source domains and unlabeled RGB images from a target domain, processing them through a frozen CLIP visual encoder to obtain unit-normalized visual embeddings. Prompts (shared domain-invariant tokens plus domain-specific tokens concatenated with class names) are encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder to produce text embeddings. At inference, the method uses only the learned target prompt to classify target images, deliberately excluding source prompts to verify that the target prompt independently captures domain-specific visual structure.

## Method

CRPL introduces two key mechanisms. First, it replaces CLIP zero-shot pseudo-labels with source-enhanced pseudo-labels: for each target image, it computes a weighted combination of source-domain text embeddings (weights derived from softmax of L2 distances between the target visual embedding and per-domain class centroids) and the base prompt embedding, then applies softmax to obtain soft pseudo-labels that guide target prompt training via cross-entropy. Second, it enforces a clustering-preserving constraint by minimizing the Wasserstein distance (with cosine metric) between the discrete distribution of target text prompt embeddings and the empirical distribution of target visual embeddings, which by Lemma 1 drives each text embedding to become the centroid of its corresponding visual cluster. The final objective jointly optimizes source cross-entropy, target cross-entropy with enhanced pseudo-labels, and the Wasserstein clustering term with fixed weights λ_T = λ_W = 0.5.

## Results

On Office-Home (source-combined), CRPL achieves 77.6% average accuracy, surpassing the second-best method PGA (74.5%) by 3.1% and zero-shot CLIP (71.4%) by 6.2%. On ImageCLEF (source-combined), CRPL reaches 90.3% average accuracy, outperforming PGA (88.2%) by 2.1%. In the multi-source setting, M-CRPL achieves 78.9% average on Office-Home and 91.5% on ImageCLEF, beating the SOTA prompt-based method MPGA by 3.4% and 0.7% respectively. On DomainNet, CRPL improves over zero-shot CLIP by approximately 6% in average accuracy (55.8% vs. 50.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRPL consistently outperforms all prompt-based UDA baselines (DAPL, MPA/MPGA, PGA/MPGA) across both source-combined and multi-source settings on all three datasets. The largest gains are on Office-Home's →Cl domain, where CRPL reaches 60.4% (SC) and 63.5% (MS) versus 53.9% (PGA) and 56.0% (MPGA), reflecting the difficulty of adapting to the Clipart domain. Against traditional UDA methods (DANN, D-CORAL, DAN), CRPL improves average accuracy by 5.2% on Office-Home and 2.5% on ImageCLEF in the source-combined setting. On DomainNet, CRPL (55.8% SC) and M-CRPL (56.2% MS) both surpass PGA (55.4%) and MPGA (55.4%), with the most notable improvement on the →Pnt domain (66.7% vs. 60.4% for PGA). In the 12 pairwise Office-Home settings (Table 4), CRPL achieves 74.4% average, beating PGA (73.9%), DAPL (73.3%), and zero-shot CLIP (72.0%), with the largest margin on Cl→Ar (+2.4% over PGA). The ablation in Table 6 further shows that CRPL's target prompt (τ_T) alone outperforms PGA's source prompt (τ_S), whereas PGA's target prompt remains below its source prompt, confirming that source-target alignment alone is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning-for-UDA line initiated by DAPL and MPA, which introduced domain-invariant and domain-specific prompts trained with CLIP zero-shot pseudo-labels. It departs from the dominant mechanism in MPA and PGA—aligning source and target prompts in the text embedding space—by arguing that this alignment has limited effect on target prompt quality, and instead leverages the geometric relationship between visual and text embeddings. The key novelty is a Wasserstein-distance-based clustering loss that forces target text embeddings to act as centroids of target visual embedding clusters, drawing on optimal transport theory in a way not previously applied to prompt learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that source-target prompt alignment (the core mechanism of the two most recent SOTA methods, MPA and PGA) contributes little to target prompt quality challenges a working assumption in the field and redirects attention toward the visual-text embedding geometry. The practical implication is that pseudo-label quality and cluster structure matter more than prompt-space alignment, which could reshape how future prompt-based adaptation methods are designed. Limitations include the dependence on multiple source domains, the vulnerability of the Wasserstein term to collapse under weak initial predictions (as shown in the ablation), and the restriction to CLIP-based architectures. Open questions include whether the clustering assumption generalizes to other VLMs or to domains with highly non-convex class structure.
