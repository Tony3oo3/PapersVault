## Problem

Existing angle resolvers for rotated object detection (PSC, ACM) predict angle encoding states independently without enforcing the unit circle constraint, creating a many-to-one mapping that introduces prediction biases. Additionally, the absence of a large-scale multi-class rotated SAR object detection dataset has stalled progress in rotated SAR detection, as manual annotation is costly and weakly supervised pseudo-labels suffer from inaccurate angle predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251722/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes SAR or optical aerial images (patched to 1024×1024 with 200px overlap for DOTA/RSAR, or scaled to 800×800 for HRSC) and produces rotated bounding boxes parameterized as (cx, cy, w, h, θ) along with class labels. In the weakly supervised setting, only horizontal bounding boxes are provided as supervision while the model predicts full rotated boxes. The angle is internally represented as multi-dimensional encoding states (2D: cos θ, sin θ; or 3D: three phase-shifted cosines) that are decoded back to a scalar angle in the le90 range [−π/2, π/2).

## Method

The paper unifies existing angle resolvers under a dimensional mapping perspective: 1D angle values are mapped onto a unit circle in 2D (ACM's complex exponential form) or a circular curve in 3D (PSC's phase-shifting form). The key insight is that prior methods predict each encoding dimension independently, allowing predictions to deviate from the unit circle and creating a many-to-one ambiguity. The proposed Unit Circle Resolver (UCR) adds a constraint loss L_uc = |n/2 − Σm_i²| + σ(n)|Σm_i| that forces predicted encodings onto the unit circle, where n is the mapping dimension (2 or 3) and σ(n) activates an additional zero-sum constraint only in 3D. An invalid region near the circle's center is defined where only the unit circle loss applies, preventing noisy early predictions from corrupting angle regression. UCR is plugged into H2RBox-v2 (FCOS + ResNet-50 + FPN) with total loss L_cls + λ_reg·L_reg + λ_uc·L_uc, and the resulting pseudo-rotated labels are manually calibrated to construct the RSAR dataset (95,842 images, 183,534 instances, 6 categories).

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, UCR (2D) achieves AP75=42.26 and mAP=42.65 under weak supervision (HBB), surpassing the fully supervised FCOS baseline (AP75=41.76, mAP=41.81) for the first time. On HRSC, UCR (3D) improves AP75 by 3.47% over the best prior resolver (ACM: 72.02 → 75.49) and mAP by 2.79% (58.95 → 61.74). On the newly introduced RSAR dataset, UCR (3D) yields AP75=26.17 and mAP=32.64, outperforming both ACM (23.53/30.64) and PSC (24.07/30.91) resolvers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCR consistently outperforms both prior angle resolvers (ACM and PSC) across all three datasets and all metrics. On DOTA-v1.0, UCR (2D) beats ACM by 2.09% in AP75 (42.26 vs. 40.17) and PSC by 2.77% (42.26 vs. 39.49); UCR (3D) achieves the highest mAP at 43.10. The most striking result is that UCR (2D) under weak supervision (HBB) surpasses the fully supervised FCOS baseline on DOTA-v1.0 in AP75 (42.26 vs. 41.76) and mAP (42.65 vs. 41.81), a first for this benchmark. On HRSC, UCR (3D) improves AP75 by 3.47% over ACM and 10.69% over PSC, while on RSAR the gains are more modest (2.10% AP75 over PSC) due to the inherent difficulty of SAR imagery. The original H2RBox (without any resolver) performs dramatically worse, confirming that angle encoding is critical for weakly supervised rotated detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent angle-resolver approaches—ACM (2D complex-exponential encoding) and PSC (3D phase-shifting encoding)—under a single dimensional-mapping framework, revealing that both share the same critical flaw: they predict encoding states independently without enforcing the unit-circle constraint. The proposed Unit Circle Resolver (UCR) corrects this by adding a constraint loss that restricts predictions to the valid manifold, and is integrated into the H2RBox-v2 weakly supervised detector. The paper also extends the SARDet-100K horizontal-box SAR benchmark into RSAR, a rotated-annotation dataset, by leveraging UCR-augmented pseudo-labels followed by manual calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RSAR fills a clear gap as the largest multi-class rotated SAR object detection dataset (95,842 images, 183,534 instances, 6 categories), enabling systematic benchmarking that was previously impossible with small single-category rotated SAR datasets. The finding that a weakly supervised model (horizontal boxes only) with UCR surpasses fully supervised rotated-box models on DOTA-v1.0 suggests that angle-encoding quality, not label richness, may be the bottleneck in rotated detection. Open questions include whether UCR's gains transfer to fully supervised SAR detectors trained directly on RSAR, and whether the unified dimensional-mapping perspective generalizes to higher-dimensional encodings or other periodic regression tasks.
