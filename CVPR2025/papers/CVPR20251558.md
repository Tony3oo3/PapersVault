## Problem

Existing sequence-based gait recognition methods rely heavily on expert-driven temporal architectures (e.g., multi-scale receptive fields, self-attention, visual prompts) whose feature diversity is constrained by dataset-specific design. The powerful, task-agnostic sequence modeling capabilities of Large Language Models remain entirely unexplored for gait recognition, despite the structural analogy between gait sequences and natural language sentences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251558/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 64×64 gait silhouette sequences (30 consecutive frames randomly sampled per sequence) as input and produces a 256-dimensional identity embedding for recognition. It is gait-encoder-agnostic, accepting features from any off-the-shelf backbone (GaitPart, GaitGL, GaitBase, DeepGaitV2-P3D, DeepGaitV2-3D), and the final embedding is formed by concatenating the LLM-refined features with traditionally pooled gait features along the part dimension before passing through a recognition head.

## Method

GaitLLM introduces a G2L-L2G add-on framework that bridges gait features and a frozen LLM (Qwen-0.5B) with only 4.3% trainable parameters. The G2L-Tokenizer first compresses spatial patches per body part via a transformer block with a learnable [CLS]-like token, then the Temporal Tokenizer uses a Peak Tokens Finder (cosine similarity against a global token + peak detection) to identify informative frames and a Motion Clip Assembler (cross-attention from dropped to retained tokens) to merge redundant frames into semantically meaningful clip-level tokens forming a 'gait sentence.' A Separate Fully-Connected projector maps these tokens into the LLM's embedding space; after the frozen LLM processes the sequence, the L2G module projects the last hidden states back to gait feature space and applies global max pooling to aggregate identity-relevant information. Training is two-stage: the gait encoder is first trained from scratch, then both the encoder and LLM are frozen while only the G2L, L2G, and recognition head are trained with triplet + cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On Gait3D, GaitLLM with the 22-layer DeepGaitV2-P3D encoder achieves 76.5% rank-1 accuracy and 68.3% mAP, surpassing the strongest prior method VPNet-L (75.4% rank-1) and the same-encoder baseline DeepGaitV2-P3D_22 (74.4% rank-1, 65.8% mAP). On GREW, GaitLLM reaches 80.3% rank-1 (3D_22 configuration) and 79.8% rank-1 (P3D_22), outperforming VPNet-L (80.0%) and DeepGaitV2-3D_22 (79.4%). On CCPG, GaitLLM-10 achieves 85.4% mean rank-1 under the gait protocol and 94.7% under the ReID protocol, both exceeding DeepGaitV2-P3D (83.3% and 93.7%). On SUSTech1K, GaitLLM-10 attains 84.5% overall rank-1, a 2.2-point gain over the baseline and an 8.4-point gain over GaitBase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaitLLM consistently outperforms all compared baselines across the four datasets. Against the strongest prior method VPNet-L (CVPR 2024), GaitLLM with P3D_22 improves Gait3D rank-1 by 1.1 points (76.5% vs. 75.4%) and matches GREW rank-1 (79.8% vs. 80.0%), while the 3D_22 configuration edges ahead on GREW (80.3% vs. 80.0%). Compared to the same-encoder baseline DeepGaitV2-P3D_22, GaitLLM gains +2.1% rank-1 and +2.5% mAP on Gait3D, and +2.1% rank-1 on GREW, confirming the LLM add-on's encoder-agnostic benefit. Against older baselines the margins are larger: GaitLLM surpasses QAGait by +9.5% rank-1 on Gait3D and +20.7% on GREW, and GaitBase by +11.9% on Gait3D and +19.7% on GREW. On CCPG, GaitLLM-10 beats DeepGaitV2-P3D by +2.1% mean rank-1 (gait protocol) and +1.0% (ReID protocol), with the largest gain under bag-carrying conditions (+3.0%). On SUSTech1K, GaitLLM-10 improves overall rank-1 by +2.2% over DeepGaitV2-P3D and +8.4% over GaitBase, with the most notable improvement under clothing changes (+6.3% over baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

GaitLLM departs from the dominant paradigm in sequence-based eait recognition, where methods like DANet, DyGait, and GaitFormer rely on expertert-driven temporal architectures (self-attention, multi-scale receptive fields, visual prompts) to capture temporal dependencies. Instead, it repurposes the MLLM connector paradigm (LLaVA, Flamingo, BLIPv2) by treating eait sequences as 'sentences' and inserting a frozen LLM as a task-agnostic sequence modeler between a gait encoder and a recognition head. The G2L-Tokenizer's dynamic motion clip segmentation—using Peak Tokens Finder and Motion Clip Assembler rather than uniform frame-to-token mapping—represents a novel conceptual bridge that prior MLLM-for-vision work has not addressed, since eait silhouettes carry far more spatiotemporal redundancy than RGB frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a small frozen LLM (Qwen-0.5B) can serve as a drop-in sequence refiner for eait recognition, achieving SOTA on four datasets with only 4.3% trainable parameters and no architectural changes to the underlying gait encoder. The gait-encoder-agnostic property (consistent gains across GaitPart, GaitGL, GaitBase, and DeepGaitV2) suggests the LLM captures complementary temporal structure that no single encoder design fully exploits. Key limitations include a 2.4× inference speed reduction (31.5 vs 75.3 FPS), the absence of fine-tuning for both encoder and LLM, and the field's lack of a strong pre-trained eait foundation model—open questions include whether larger LLMs or joint fine-tuning yield further gains, and whether the approach transfers to 3D point-cloud or pose-based eait modalities.
