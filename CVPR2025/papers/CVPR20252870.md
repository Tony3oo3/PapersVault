## Problem

HOI detection performance remains unsatisfactory, and the paper identifies an overlooked bottleneck: the no-interaction category has accuracy roughly 40 percentage points lower than with-interaction categories, largely due to misclassification between no-interaction and both direct (e.g., carry) and indirect (e.g., watch) interactions. The authors show that these confusions can be resolved by explicit human-object relation priors—3D spatial proximity for direct interactions and gaze visibility for indirect interactions—that are not captured by visual features alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252870/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a set of HOI triplets (human bbox, object bbox, interaction category). Internally, it consumes three frozen components: a DETR detector for human/object localization, an off-the-shelf gaze cone predictor for each detected human, and a CLIP ViT-32/B text encoder that converts generated relational text templates into embeddings. The interaction decoder receives multi-modal queries formed by concatenating pairwise visual features with the two text-encoded prior vectors, rather than the standard random or purely visual query initialization.

## Method

HORP is a two-stage pipeline: a frozen DETR localizes humans and objects, then a Transformer-based interaction decoder classifies each pair. The key novelty is the construction of two human-object relation priors expressed as natural-language text and encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. The 3D location prior combines 2D IoU with a rough relative-depth estimate (pixel-area ratio of each box normalized by image size, paired with the object category) to capture whether the person and object are physically close in 3D space. The gaze area prior computes the proportion of the object's bounding box falling within a predicted gaze cone, indicating whether the object is visible to the person. Both priors are filled into fixed text templates, encoded, linearly projected into the visual feature space, and concatenated with pairwise visual features to form multi-modal queries for the interaction decoder, which is trained with Focal Loss to handle category imbalance.

## Results

On HICO-DET (Default Full mAP), HORP with ResNet-50+CLIP achieves 38.61, outperforming all same-backbone methods except SICHOI (41.79, which uses BLIP+GPT). With Swin-L+CLIP, HORP reaches 47.53 mAP, surpassing the previous best PAFR (46.01) by 1.52 pp. On V-COCO, HORP (Swin-L) achieves 68.9 (S1) and 71.1 (S2), setting the best S1 score and second-best S2 score (behind SICHOI's 72.8). The no-interaction category AP improves from 16.32 (baseline) to 23.11 (+6.79 pp) when both priors are used.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With the ResNet-50 backbone, HORP (38.61 mAP) outperforms all same-backbone competitors including ADA-CM (38.40), ViPLO (37.22), AGER (36.75), PVIC (34.69), and GEN-VLKT (33.75), but trails SICHOI (41.79), which leverages BLIP and GPT for textual cue generation. Under the Swin-L backbone, HORP (47.53) surpasses the previous state-of-the-art PAFR (46.01) by 1.52 pp, and also beats PVIC (44.32), DiffHOI (41.50), and RLIPv2 (33.66). On V-COCO, HORP (Swin-L) achieves the best S1 score (68.9) and the second-best S2 score (71.1), narrowly behind SICHOI (72.8) on S2 while beating RLIPv2 (70.8) and all other methods. The Known-Object setting shows similar trends: HORP (Swin-L) reaches 51.24, exceeding PAFR (49.50) and PVIC (47.81). Overall, HORP is the strongest method that does not rely on large generative language models (GPT/BLIP), and its gains are most pronounced on the no-interaction category (+6.79 pp over baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

HORP builds on the two-stage HOI detection paradigm established by UPT and PVIC, which separate object detection from interaction classification to allow flexible feature fusion. Unlike prior VLM-based HOI methods (GEN-VLKT, ADA-CM, ViPLO, AGER) that leverage CLIP for visual feature extraction or knowledge transfer, HORP repurposes the text encoder to encode hand-crafted relational priors (3D location, gaze area) as explicit semantic cues. The key conceptual departure is identifying the no-interaction category as a specific, previously under-addressed failure mode and targeting it with structured spatial/relational knowledge rather than purely learned visual features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that no-interaction accuracy lags with-interaction categories by ~40 percentage points provides a clear target for future HOI research, and the text-prior approach offers a lightweight, interpretable mechanism for injecting world-knowledge into interaction recognition. However, the depth estimation relies on a crude pixel-count heuristic, the gaze prior depends on an external predictor, and the text templates are hand-designed, all of which limit generalizability. Open questions include whether learned depth estimation or more flexible language generation (e.g., LLM-based) could further improve the priors, and whether this relational-prior-as-text strategy transfers to other structured prediction tasks beyond HOI.
