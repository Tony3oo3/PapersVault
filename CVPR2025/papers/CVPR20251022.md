## Problem

Multimodal LLMs rely exclusively on knowledge encoded during training, making them unable to answer questions requiring specific encyclopedic or long-tail facts. Existing retrieval-augmented MLLMs lack the ability to autonomously determine when external knowledge is needed and to assess which retrieved passages are actually relevant, leading to degraded performance when irrelevant content is injected into the generation context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251022/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a query RGB image and a text question as primary input, and optionally a set of retrieved Wikipedia passages (textual sections from top-k documents) as auxiliary context. At inference, the model first emits a single reflective token (<RET> or <NORET>) to decide whether retrieval is needed; if retrieval is triggered, it then emits a relevance token (<REL> or <NOREL>) for each retrieved passage before generating the final text answer. The retrieval backbone uses CLIP ViT-L/14 or EVA-CLIP-8B for image-to-text or image-to-image similarity search over a Wikipedia knowledge base of 2M–6M pages.

## Method

ReflectiVA extends the LLaVA-v1.5 vocabulary (LLaMA-3.1-8B backbone) with four special reflective tokens that enable the model to decide whether to retrieve and to classify each retrieved passage as relevant or irrelevant. Training follows a two-stage, two-model recipe: first, an in-article reflective model is trained to discriminate relevant from irrelevant passages within the same Wikipedia page, using GPT-4-annotated positive/negative pairs; second, predictions from this model are used to automatically label positive, hard-negative (same-page), and soft-negative (cross-page) passages across the full training set, and the final model is trained on a balanced mixture of all four token types plus answer generation. The generation protocol is strictly sequential: the model first constrains its output to {<RET>, <NORET>}, then (if retrieval is triggered) evaluates each passage with {<REL>, <NOREL>}, and finally generates the answer using only passages marked relevant. This design preserves the model's standard MLLM capabilities on non-knowledge tasks while adding explicit, learnable control over the retrieval pipeline.

## Results

On Encyclopedic-VQA (test set), ReflectiVA with EVA-CLIP-8B textual retrieval achieves 29.2 BEM accuracy (All) and 28.0 (Single-Hop), outperforming the best prior retrieval-augmented method EchoSight (21.7 All) by 7.5 points. On InfoSeek (validation set), ReflectiVA reaches 40.1 VQA accuracy (All), surpassing EchoSight (30.4) by 9.7 points and Wiki-LLaVA (27.1) by 13.0 points. With visual retrieval on Encyclopedic-VQA, ReflectiVA achieves 35.5 accuracy, exceeding GPT-4V (28.1) by 7.4 points. On standard MLLM benchmarks (Table 6), ReflectiVA retains performance close to the base LLaVA-v1.5 (e.g., 38.9 vs 39.4 on MMMU, 62.1 vs 63.6 on GQA), confirming that reflective tokens do not degrade general capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReflectiVA outperforms all ten compared baselines on both primary datasets. Against zero-shot LLMs (Vicuna-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, GPT-4), the gap is stark: these models score near 0% on InfoSeek (lacking visual grounding) and at most 23.4 on E-VQA All, versus ReflectiVA's 40.1 and 29.2 respectively. Among zero-shot MLLMs, GPT-4V is the strongest (28.1 E-VQA All, 14.6 InfoSeek All), still trailing ReflectiVA by 1.1 and 25.5 points; open-source MLLMs (BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA-v1.5) score 7.8–16.9 on E-VQA All and 7.8–12.5 on InfoSeek All. Among retrieval-augmented competitors, EchoSight with EVA-CLIP-8B textual retrieval is the closest rival (21.7 E-VQA All, 30.4 InfoSeek All), yet ReflectiVA leads by 7.5 and 9.7 points. Wiki-LLaVA (19.6 E-VQA All, 27.1 InfoSeek All) and RoRA-VLM (20.3 E-VQA All) trail further behind. The advantage is most pronounced on InfoSeek, where the reflective relevance tokens (<REL>/<NOREL>) filter out irrelevant passages and yield a 9.7-point gain over the best prior method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the retrieval-augmented generation paradigm from text-only LLMs (e.g., Self-RAG, which uses special tokens to decide when to retrieve) to multimodal LLMs, building directly on the LLaVA-v1.5 architecture. It departs from prior multimodal RAG approaches like Wiki-LLaVA (hierarchical retrieval pipeline) and EchoSight (Q-Former-based re-ranking) by embedding retrieval and relevance decisions directly into the MLLM's vocabulary as reflective tokens, eliminating the need for separate external re-ranking modules. The two-stage, two-model training recipe—first training an in-article passage discriminator, then using its synthetic annotations to train the full model—is a novel data-construction strategy that addresses the absence of human-labeled passage relevance in existing knowledge-based VQA datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reflective token approach offers a clean, unified mechanism for MLLMs to autonomously decide when external knowledge is needed and which retrieved passages are relevant, without architectural modifications beyond vocabulary extension. This is practically important because it preserves the model's generative capabilities on standard benchmarks (Table 6 shows only minor degradation) while substantially improving knowledge-based VQA. Limitations include reliance on CLIP-based coarse retrieval, which the authors acknowledge has limited performance for fine-grained multimodal retrieval, and evaluation restricted to Wikipedia-derived knowledge bases. Open questions include whether the token-based approach generalizes to non-Wikipedia knowledge sources, video/3D modalities, or larger-scale retrieval settings where the number of candidate passages grows substantially.
