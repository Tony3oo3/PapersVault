## Problem

Existing event-driven autofocus methods (EGS, PBF) still require capturing a complete focus stack—moving past the focus position and back—before the focus point can be determined, resulting in one unavoidable round of 'focus hunting.' No prior method detects the focus position in real time during a single motor pass, making true one-step event-driven AF an open problem, particularly under low-light, high-motion, and large-defocus conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an asynchronous event stream (x, y, t, polarity tuples) accumulated into short event frames (Δt < 1 ms) and a single grayscale image—either a live frame from a DAVIS346 sensor or a defocus HDR frame synthesized via EvTemMap from an event-only EVK4 camera. The grayscale image need not be at the current focus position; any defocus frame suffices because the sign of the ELP is invariant to the defocus kernel. Output is a real-time scalar ELP value whose sign encodes focus direction (positive = approaching, negative = past focus) and whose abrupt sign mutation triggers an immediate motor stop signal, eliminating any post-hoc search.

## Method

The core contribution is the Event Laplacian Product (ELP), defined as −Σ(∇²I(t)·E(t)), which the authors derive theoretically as a discrete approximation of the product between the temporal first-order derivative (events) and the spatial second-order derivative (Laplacian) of the blurred image. A key derivation shows that the sign of this product is governed solely by the rate of change of the Gaussian blur variance (α), so ELP is positive while the lens approaches focus and flips negative once it passes, producing a sharp 'sign mutation' within one depth of focus. An adaptive filter (windowed mean comparison with threshold-gated smoothing) suppresses local noise fluctuations while preserving the steepness of the sign-mutation point. For event-only cameras, EvTemMap converts initial positive event timestamps into an HDR grayscale frame, providing the Laplacian reference without any conventional image sensor. The entire pipeline operates in a single motor pass: the motor moves continuously, ELP is computed in real time, and the motor halts the instant the sign mutation is detected—cutting total focusing time by roughly two-thirds compared to stack-based methods.

## Results

On the DAVIS346 dataset, ELP achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.29 μm, reducing focusing error by 24× compared to the state-of-the-art PBF method (7.02 μm) and by ~98× compared to EGS (28.42 μm). On the EVK4 dataset, ELP achieves 0.57 μm MAE, a 22× improvement over PBF (12.54 μm). Total focusing time is reduced by approximately two-thirds (103.36 ms vs. 302.37 ms on DAVIS; 112.97 ms vs. 302.52 ms on EVK4), and ELP achieves accurate focus (within one depth of focus, 6 μm) in every tested case, whereas PBF succeeds in only ~43% of cases and EGS in none.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELP decisively outperforms both baselines on the primary metric (focusing MAE) across all four scene conditions (bright/dark × dynamic/static) on both datasets. Against PBF [1], the closest competitor, ELP reduces MAE from 7.02 to 0.29 μm on DAVIS (24×) and from 12.54 to 0.57 μm on EVK4 (22×), while also cutting total focusing time by roughly two-thirds due to its one-step detection of the ELP 'sign mutation' rather than requiring a full focus-stack search. Against EGS [10], ELP reduces MAE by ~98× on DAVIS and ~72× on EVK4; EGS fails to achieve accurate focus in any real-world case and its principle (highest event rate = focus) breaks down without optical flow. The one trade-off is algorithm runtime: PBF's O(1/Δt) complexity gives it the fastest per-frame computation (2.37 ms on DAVIS), while ELP's O(k/Δt) complexity (k = non-zero event pixels) yields 12.14 ms on DAVIS and 83.00 ms on EVK4; however, this is more than offset by the elimination of the full focus-stack traversal, making ELP's end-to-end focusing time the shortest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-driven autofocus line initiated by EGS (event rate as focus metric) and PBF (event polarity symmetry), both of which require capturing a complete focus stack before locating the focus position. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical derivation linking the spatial second-order derivative (Laplacian) to the temporal first-order derivative (events) during focusing, which transforms focus search from a 'peaked function' optimization into a real-time 'sign mutation' detection problem. For event-only cameras, the method integrates EvTemMap to recover grayscale information from event timestamps, enabling the Laplacian component without a conventional image sensor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for a complete focus stack, ELP cuts focusing time by two-thirds and reduces error by 22–24× over the prior state-of-the-art, making event-driven autofocus practical for real-time applications. The one-step formulation also removes the ambiguity problem (pre-focus vs. post-focus) that plagues contrast-based and PDAF methods, without requiring dual-pixel hardware. The main open question is robustness under extreme high-speed motion in event-only settings, where the single reference frame may become stale; the authors suggest adaptive sign-mutation detection as a path forward.
