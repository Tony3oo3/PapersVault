## Problem

Example-based modeling from real-world captures requires seamlessly stitching multiple neural 3D objects into a harmonious composite, but the prior SeamlessNeRF approach relies on gradient propagation over an implicit grid representation, which fails on real-world scenes: it cannot perform fine-grained editing, produces artifacts at complex boundaries, and offers no interactive workflow. The paper addresses this by moving to an explicit point-based representation (3DGS) and designing an optimization strategy that works with its discrete, irregular structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple pre-trained 3DGS scenes as input; the user interactively segments source and target parts via bounding boxes and a brush tool, then applies rigid transformations (quaternion rotation, translation, scale) to arrange them into a semantically meaningful composite. The output is a single harmonized 3DGS model where the target object's spherical-harmonic color features have been optimized to match the source's appearance at the intersection boundary and globally. The entire pipeline runs in real time through a custom GUI that previews the optimization iteratively.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-phase optimization that replaces SeamlessNeRF's gradient propagation with a sampling-based cloning strategy suited to 3DGS's irregular point distribution. First, KNN analysis identifies boundary points where the target field meets the source field (using distance threshold β and opacity threshold τ). In the S-phase, each non-boundary target point is assigned a target color by sampling its K nearest 'driven points' in the boundary via a perturbed mapping φ(x)=x+sin(γ·δx) that injects structural randomness; a color loss drives explicit feature propagation inward, while a 2D Sobel gradient loss in screen space preserves the target's original texture content. In the T-phase, a streaming aggregation algorithm clusters the source field's colors into a weighted palette, and a pixel-wise weighted distance loss tunes the target's global brightness, hue, and saturation. Both phases are jointly optimized with the S-phase losses always active and T-phase losses added on top, with empirically set weights λ₁=λ₂=2.0.

## Results

The paper reports a single quantitative metric: VQA (Video Quality Assessment) average score, evaluated via 2D projection of rendered video. The proposed method achieves a VQA score of 0.836, substantially outperforming the SeamlessNeRF baseline at 0.662 (a ~26% relative improvement). Qualitatively, the method produces seamless, artifact-free composites across 17 interactive compositions built from 31 part models sourced from BlendedMVS, Mip360, and SeamlessNeRF datasets, while SeamlessNeRF fails on all three tested real-world cases (clay & bread, bottle & bell, and a third complex-boundary case).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SeamlessNeRF [14], the first work to address seamless stitching of neural fields. Our method decisively outperforms it on the VQA metric (0.836 vs. 0.662) and on all three qualitative comparison cases in Fig. 9, where SeamlessNeRF fails due to its implicit grid-based representation and gradient-propagation strategy producing artifacts, insufficient color propagation, and inability to handle complex boundaries. The second comparison is SNeRF [36], a 2D-guided style-transfer method re-implemented on 3D-GS; the paper demonstrates in Fig. 13 that SNeRF cannot produce seamless stitching, and in Fig. 14 that SNeRF requires over 10 hours of optimization versus the paper's significantly faster convergence. Overall, the proposed sampling-based cloning and clustering-based tuning pipeline wins on both quality and practicality (real-time interactivity) against both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the seamless neural field stitching task introduced by SeamlessNeRF from implicit grid-based NeRFs to explicit point-based 3D Gaussian Splatting, addressing the fundamental incompatibility of gradient propagation with discrete, irregularly distributed Gaussians. It inherits the example-based modeling tradition from classical CG (Funkhouser et al.) but shifts the substrate from hand-modeled meshes to photorealistic captures. The key methodological departure is replacing SeamlessNeRF's gradient-based style transfer with a sampling-based cloning strategy (S-phase) combined with a clustering-based global tuning (T-phase), motivated by the observation that Laplacian-coordinate regularization fails entirely on sparse point sets before propagation even begins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to enable real-time, interactive example-based modeling directly from real-world 3DGS captures, lowering the barrier for artists to compose photorealistic 3D content without manual texturing. The sampling-based cloning strategy with its random-perturbation mapping φ is a practical technique that could generalize to other discrete-representation editing tasks. However, significant limitations remain: no non-rigid deformation support, no handling of specular or translucent materials, and no geometry modification during stitching. Open questions include whether PBR integration and diffusion priors can automate the currently manual boundary identification and composition steps.
