## Problem

Existing personalized scanpath prediction (PSP) models require large per-subject datasets (e.g., 269,760 fixations in COCO-Search18) and overfit severely when only a handful of examples are available for a new subject. This paper introduces the Few-Shot Personalized Scanpath Prediction (FS-PSP) task, which requires predicting an unseen subject's scanpaths from at most 10 support image-scanpath pairs, and shows that prior PSP methods (ISP, EyeFormer) degrade substantially under this constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250437/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a query image, a support set of n (1, 5, or 10) image-scanpath pairs from the unseen subject (each pair containing 2D fixation coordinates, fixation durations in ms, and the corresponding image), and optionally a text task description (e.g., 'search for bowl'). It outputs a predicted scanpath as a sequence of up to 20 (free-viewing) or 10 (search) fixation positions with durations, plus a 384-dimensional subject embedding. Both SE-Net and ISP-SENet are fully frozen at test time; adaptation to a new subject requires only a single forward pass through SE-Net on the support set (~3.6 s), with no fine-tuning.

## Method

The framework decouples subject embedding extraction from scanpath prediction into two independently trained components. SE-Net encodes each image-scanpath pair via a ResNet + Deformable Attention feature extractor (following HAT), then a Context-Scanpath Encoder (CSE) fuses RoBERTa task embeddings, binned duration encodings, and 2D sinusoidal position encodings into the scanpath tokens; critically, context tokens are discarded after self-attention (Eq. 4) to isolate scene-specific content from subject-specific traits. A User-Scanpath Decoder (USD) then uses cross-attention with an initialized subject token to distill a 384-d embedding, trained jointly with a classification head (subject ID) and a triplet contrastive loss that pulls same-subject embeddings together and pushes different-subject embeddings apart. ISP-SENet, built on Gazeformer-ISP, replaces the fixed per-subject embedding lookup with the SE-Net output; at inference, embeddings from all n support scanpaths are averaged (prototypical-network style) to form a single subject embedding that conditions the scanpath predictor, enabling zero-fine-tuning adaptation to unseen subjects.

## Results

ISP-SENet achieves the best performance across all three datasets and all n-shot settings (n=1, 5, 10). On OSIE, it reaches SM=0.375 (10-shot) and SED=7.318, outperforming the second-best method by 5.9% in SM and 2.5% in SED. On COCO-FreeView, it achieves SM=0.367 and SED=11.956, a 7.9% SM improvement over the best baseline. On COCO-Search18, it reaches SM=0.482 and SED=2.359, a 6.0% SM gain. Notably, ISP-SENet requires only 3.62 seconds of adaptation time (embedding extraction) versus 161–267 seconds for fine-tuning-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISP-SENet is compared against four baselines derived from the ISP framework [10]: ChenLSTM-ISP and Gazeformer-ISP (full fine-tuning on the support set) and their partial variants ChenLSTM-ISP-S and Gazeformer-ISP-S (only the subject embedding layer is fine-tuned, all other parameters frozen). ISP-SENet wins on every metric (SM, MM, SED) across all three datasets and all n-shot settings. The full fine-tuning baselines (ChenLSTM-ISP, Gazeformer-ISP) suffer severe overfitting, especially on COCO-FreeView where Gazeformer-ISP drops to SM=0.244 at n=1, far below ISP-SENet's 0.369. The partial fine-tuning variants (ISP-S) perform better than full fine-tuning but still trail ISP-SENet by 1.4–3.6% in SM, and their subject embeddings change minimally from seen-subject embeddings, indicating poor adaptability. Critically, ISP-SENet requires no fine-tuning at test time—adaptation takes only 3.62 s (embedding extraction) versus 161 s (ChenLSTM-ISP) or 267 s (Gazeformer-ISP) for the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the personalized scanpath prediction (PSP) line initiated by Gazeformer-ISP [10] and EyeFormer [20], which both rely on fixed or jointly-learned subject embeddings that require extensive per-subject data. The key conceptual departure is decoupling subject embedding extraction from scanpath prediction: a dedicated SE-Net trained with classification and contrastive (triplet) losses produces subject embeddings, which then condition a frozen scanpath predictor (ISP-SENet, built on Gazeformer-ISP's architecture and HAT's feature extractor). The averaging of support-set embeddings follows the prototypical network paradigm [38], and the visual-task encoder borrows from RoBERTa-based task conditioning, but the overall pipeline of 'learn a subject embedding space first, then condition a predictor on it' is the paper's architectural contribution relative to prior PSP methods that entangle both objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing FS-PSP (≤10 support samples) and demonstrating that a decoupled embedding-then-predict pipeline outperforms joint fine-tuning baselines by 6–8% on ScanMatch across three datasets, the paper provides a practical recipe for personalizing gaze models without hours of per-subject data collection. The 3.62-second adaptation time (vs. 161–267 s for fine-tuning baselines) makes real-time personalization feasible for applications like adaptive AR/VR interfaces and recommendation systems. A stated limitation is that SE-Net may overemphasize idiosyncratic traits at the expense of shared attention patterns, and the approach has only been validated on free-viewing and search tasks; whether the embedding space generalizes to more diverse task types (e.g., visual question answering, object referral) remains open.
