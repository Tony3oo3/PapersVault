## Problem

Current self-supervised pre-training for medical imaging operates on uni-modal data or processes mixed modalities independently, failing to model the strong structural correlations that exist between different MRI sequences acquired from the same patient. This limitation is especially costly for multi-parametric MRI, where complementary modalities (T1, T2, FLAIR, T1CE, etc.) are naturally grouped per study, and where missing modalities in real-world data further reduce the usable pre-training pool.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes single-modality 3D MRI volumes (one channel at a time) rather than fixed multi-modal stacks, so any number of modalities per patient can be included without architectural changes. The pre-trained output consists of encoder weights (UniFormer or UNET3D backbone) plus a set of learnable modality templates of shape S×H×W×D, one per modality. At downstream inference, these templates can substitute for missing modalities in the input, and the encoder produces segmentation masks or class labels as usual.

## Method

BrainMVP defines three jointly optimized proxy tasks on a shared encoder-decoder (UniFormer or UNET3D, no skip connections). (1) Cross-modal reconstruction: a large region of modality m is masked and filled with a spatially aligned patch from modality n of the same patient; the decoder must reconstruct the original modality m from this mixed input, forcing the encoder to learn cross-modal structural mappings. (2) Modality-wise data distillation: the same masking-filling procedure is repeated but the fill source is a learnable per-modality template (initialized to zero) instead of a real image, so the template absorbs patient-agnostic structural priors for that modality. (3) Modality-aware contrastive learning: the encoder embeddings of the cross-modal-masked volume and the template-masked volume are pulled together via a symmetric InfoNCE loss, enforcing feature-level consistency across the two views. The three losses are summed with balancing coefficients, and the contrastive term is activated only after epoch 1000 once templates have visually converged.

## Results

On six segmentation benchmarks, BrainMVP (UniFormer backbone) achieves the best average Dice Score across all datasets: 76.80% on BraTS2023-PED, 73.67% on BraTS-MET, 86.60% on ISLES22, 80.27% on MRBrainS13, 90.01% on UPENN-GBM, and 83.64% on VSseg. On four classification tasks, BrainMVP attains the highest accuracy (0.8596 on BraTS2018, 0.6765 on ADNI, 0.6883 on ADHD-200, 0.6182 on ABIDE-I) and AUC (0.9452, 0.6964, 0.7249, 0.6329 respectively). The paper reports Dice Score improvements of 0.28%–14.47% over state-of-the-art pre-training methods across segmentation benchmarks and accuracy gains of 0.65%–18.07% on classification tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainMVP (UniFormer) outperforms all 14 baselines on every one of the six segmentation benchmarks. Against the strongest medical-domain SSL baseline M³AE (UniFormer), BrainMVP gains +2.66% Dice on BraTS2023-PED (76.80 vs 74.14), +3.28% on BraTS-MET (73.67 vs 70.39), +0.28% on ISLES22 (86.60 vs 86.32), +2.98% on MRBrainS13 (80.27 vs 77.29), +0.38% on UPENN-GBM (90.01 vs 89.63), and +4.33% on VSseg (83.64 vs 79.31). General-domain SSL methods (MAE3D, SimMIM, MoCoV3) lag substantially, with MAE3D achieving only 67.65% on BraTS2023-PED versus BrainMVP's 76.80% (a 9.15% gap), confirming poor cross-domain transfer from natural images. Among CT-pretrained medical SSL methods, MG is the closest competitor (74.36% on BraTS2023-PED) but still trails BrainMVP by 2.44%. On classification, BrainMVP achieves 0.8596 ACC / 0.9452 AUC on BraTS2018, surpassing the next-best M³AE (0.7895 / 0.8659) by 7.01% ACC and 7.93% AUC; on ABIDE-I it improves accuracy by 5.46% over the prior best (0.6182 vs 0.5636). The only near-tie is on UPENN-GBM where M³AE (89.63%) is within 0.38% of BrainMVP (90.01%), and on ABIDE-I F1 where BrainMVP (0.5890) matches MoCoV3 (0.5890).

## Relation to Prior Work

BrainMVP extends the self-supervised pre-training paradigm for medical imaging from uni-modal or mixed-modality settings (M3AE, VoCo, MG) to an explicitly cross-modal formulation where different MRI sequences from the same patient are treated as complementary views of shared anatomy. It repurposes the dataset distillation literature (performance-matching methods) to learn per-modality structural templates, and introduces a novel cross-modal masking strategy that fills masked regions with a different modality's image rather than noise, leveraging the high anatomical similarity across mpMRI sequences. The modality-aware contrastive loss further ties the reconstruction and distillation objectives together at the feature level, a combination not present in prior medical SSL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical clinical bottleneck—missing or mismatched modalities in multi-parametric MRI—by enabling pre-training on arbitrary subsets of modalities and providing distilled templates that bridge pre-training and downstream domains. The label-efficiency finding (40% labeled data matching or exceeding fully supervised baselines) has direct implications for reducing annotation costs in clinical deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond brain MRI to other organ systems or imaging families (CT, ultrasound), whether templates can be incrementally updated for genuinely novel modalities, and whether the late introduction of the contrastive module (epoch 1000) is optimal or an artifact of the training schedule.
