## Problem

3D hand reconstruction from low-resolution images (e.g., 64×64 or 32×32) is severely degraded due to insufficient appearance cues for resolving joint self-similarity and depth ambiguity. Existing temporal methods compress frames into sparse joint features to capture motion context, but this excessively discards the spatial context of visual features, limiting reconstruction accuracy. The paper addresses how to effectively integrate temporal joint information with dense visual features despite their misalignment in both representation form (sparse vs. dense) and semantics (3D geometric structure vs. 2D appearance).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T=6 low-resolution hand crop frames (32×32 or 64×64) at 5-frame intervals, processed by a modified ViTPose-B backbone where the patch embedding is replaced with two convolution layers and the token count is reduced to 64 (rearranged as an 8×8 feature map). The output is a 3D hand mesh (vertices and face normals) and 3D joint coordinates, produced by a regression-based decoder (SAR-style) that predicts 2.5D heatmaps per mesh vertex. Temporal fusion is inserted at multiple intermediate backbone levels rather than only at the final layer.

## Method

The framework first compresses each historical frame into joint features by sampling local visual features at predicted 2D joint locations (guided by finger-semantic pose-aware tokens) and encoding 2D pose coordinates, then models intra- and inter-frame correlations via a spatio-temporal Transformer with alternating spatial and temporal multi-head self-attention. The key architectural contribution is Triplane Feature Encoding: fused joint features are splatted onto three orthogonal planes (x-y, x-z, y-z) using distance-aware heatmaps decoded from 3D pose, converting sparse 3D structural information into dense 2D representations aligned with visual features. A Spatial-Aware Fusion module then compresses the two planes orthogonal to the visual plane (x-z and y-z) into 1D global features via directional summation, which are injected into the x-y plane through cross-attention to provide depth-aware spatial context without redundant overlap. The enhanced visual features are used at multiple intermediate backbone levels to progressively disambiguate local representations before final mesh regression, and training combines vertex/joint L1 loss, mesh smoothing loss, and auxiliary 2D/2.5D pose supervision on tokens and temporal features.

## Results

On DexYCB at 64×64 resolution, the method achieves PA-MPJPE of 5.72 mm and PA-MPVPE of 5.52 mm, matching or exceeding high-resolution (256×256) methods such as HandOccNet (5.80) and H2ONet (5.70 at 128×128). At the even lower 32×32 resolution, it still reaches PA-MPJPE 6.03 mm. On HanCo, the method achieves J-PA of 5.77 mm, outperforming HaMeR (5.99) and approaching MobRecon (5.70) at 256 resolution. On H2O, it achieves PA-MPJPE of 6.11 mm, slightly beating HandOccNet (6.13) at 256 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DexYCB at 64×64, the method (PA-MPJPE 5.72) outperforms HandOccNet (5.80 at 256) by 0.08 mm and the low-resolution baselines HMR+LR-ViT (6.45) and VIBE+LR-ViT (6.22) by 0.73 and 0.50 mm respectively, but trails Deformer (5.22 at 512) and SimpleHand (5.50 at 256). On HanCo, it achieves J-PA 5.77, beating HaMeR (5.99) by 0.22 mm and HMR+LR-ViT (6.88) by 1.11 mm, though it falls slightly short of MobRecon (5.70) by 0.07 mm. On H2O, the method (PA-MPJPE 6.11) marginally beats HandOccNet (6.13) and substantially outperforms HMR+LR-ViT (7.49) and VIBE+LR-ViT (7.05). The key finding is that temporal triplane enhancement at 64×64 recovers most of the accuracy lost to resolution reduction, closing the gap to 256-resolution SOTA to within 0.1–0.3 mm on PA-MPJPE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of temporal hand pose estimation (VIBE, TCMR, Deformer) and low-resolution pose estimation (Low-Res Pose, RSC-Net), but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than treating temporal joint features as sparse tokens to be fused via cross-attention, it repurposes triplane features from 3D GAN literature [4] as a dense intermediate representation that bridges the semantic and form gap between 3D joint features and 2D visual features. The hand mesh regression head follows SAR [53], and the backbone is a modified ViTPose-B adapted for 64×64 inputs. The spatio-temporal Transformer for joint correlation modeling is standard, but the triplane projection of joint features onto three orthogonal planes with spatial-aware compression is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is clear: achieving PA-MPJPE of 5.72 at 64×64 resolution matches or exceeds high-resolution (256×256) single-frame SOTA methods, which is directly relevant for XR, telepresence, and classroom interaction scenarios where subjects are farther from the camera. The triplane encoding as a bridge between sparse 3D structural priors and dense 2D appearance features is a transferable idea that could apply to other low-resolution reconstruction tasks. However, the paper acknowledges it does not model long-term motion consistency, and the temporal window is limited to 6 frames with 5-frame intervals, leaving open whether longer sequences or explicit motion modeling would further improve robustness. The approach also assumes a fixed hand crop, which may not generalize to unconstrained video.
