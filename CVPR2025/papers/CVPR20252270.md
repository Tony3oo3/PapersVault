## Problem

Existing specular highlight removal methods are designed for natural images where highlights are small, sparse, and appear on raised surfaces, but they fail on text images (cards, posters) where flat surfaces reflect light uniformly, producing large-area highlights that cause severe artifacts, color distortion, and detail loss. Additionally, no publicly available dataset contains text images with realistic large-area highlights, limiting both training and evaluation of such methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252270/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB specular highlight image (e.g., a bank card, bus card, identity card, or poster with large-area highlights) at the native resolution of the dataset and produces a corresponding highlight-free RGB image. Training uses precisely aligned image pairs where non-highlight regions maintain consistent tonal values, and the network input size matches the dataset image dimensions. The LSH dataset used for training contains 13.6k pairs and the test set 1.8k pairs, generated via Unity3D PBR rendering with varied lighting conditions.

## Method

HAFNet performs hierarchical adaptive filtering at two levels: at the downsampled deep feature layer, the Adaptive Comprehensive Filtering Module (ACFM) combines a small 3×3 predicted kernel for local detail, a large 15×15 predicted kernel for extended receptive field, and FFT-based frequency-domain processing for a global receptive field, with the Parameter-Optimized Filtering Module (POFM) reducing kernel parameters from ck² to 2ck via outer-product decomposition. At the upsampled image reconstruction layer, the Adaptive Dilated Filtering Module (ADFM) applies three 3×3 predicted kernels with dilation factors 1, 2, and 3 (guided by CBAM attention) to recover multi-scale details and reduce artifacts. Kernels are dynamically predicted by separate networks conditioned on the input image and intermediate features, and training combines L1, relativistic adversarial, VGG-19 perceptual, and Gram-matrix style losses.

## Results

On the proposed LSH dataset, HAFNet achieves PSNR 26.593 and SSIM 0.937, outperforming the strongest baseline TSHR (25.066 / 0.905) by 1.53 dB and 0.032 SSIM respectively. On the real-world RD dataset, HAFNet reaches PSNR 26.217 and SSIM 0.885, surpassing the next-best IDRHR (23.903 / 0.840) by 2.31 dB. On the synthetic SD1 dataset, HAFNet attains PSNR 28.952 and SSIM 0.947, leading TSHR (25.147 / 0.930) by 3.81 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAFNet outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the two traditional methods (Shen, Yang), the gains are dramatic—over 11 dB PSNR on LSH—confirming that hand-crafted filtering cannot handle large-area highlights on text images. Among deep learning baselines, the closest competitor is TSHR [5], which leads on SD1 PSNR (25.147) but trails HAFNet by 3.81 dB; on LSH, TSHR lags by 1.53 dB PSNR and 0.032 SSIM. IDRHR [10] is the strongest on RD SSIM (0.840) yet still falls 0.045 short of HAFNet. TASHR [9] and JSHDR [4] produce visible black patches and illumination residues on large-area highlights, while Wu [40] introduces color distortions; HAFNet avoids these artifacts while recovering fine text detail under strong highlights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Kernel Prediction Filtering (KPF) framework [35], originally developed for denoising, to the specular highlight removal task by applying it hierarchically at both deep feature and image reconstruction layers. It departs from prior highlight removal methods (TSHR, IDRHR, JSHDR) that target small, sparse highlights on natural objects by specifically addressing large-area highlights on flat text surfaces (cards, posters), where existing approaches produce severe artifacts or fail to recover obscured content. The POFM's use of separable kernel estimation to reduce parameter count from ck² to 2ck, combined with FFT for global receptive field and dilated convolutions for multi-scale detail recovery, represents a novel architectural combination not seen in prior highlight removal or kernel prediction filtering work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LSH dataset (13.6k training pairs generated via Unity3D PBR rendering) fills a notable gap in highlight removal benchmarks, as existing datasets (RD, SD1, SD2) either use unrealistic highlight generation or suffer from tone inconsistencies. The paper's limitation—when highlights are extremely large and intense, reliable information for learning is insufficient, leading to blurriness—highlights a fundamental information-theoretic boundary that remains open. Future work could explore whether generative priors (diffusion models) or cross-image context could push beyond this boundary, and whether the hierarchical filtering strategy generalizes to other large-area inpainting-like tasks beyond highlight removal.
