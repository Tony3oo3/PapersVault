## Problem

Unsupervised cross-modal homography estimation lacks direct supervision of the ground-truth homography, and existing approaches rely on content-consistency (intensity-based) losses whose non-convex solution space contains multiple local minima, causing failure under large modality and deformation gaps. The paper addresses this by reformulating the unsupervised problem into two sub-problems that can each be solved with direct supervision, eliminating the reliance on indirect content-matching signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251998/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 128×128 images from two different modalities (e.g., optical and SAR, RGB and NIR, flash and no-flash) and outputs a 3×3 homography matrix representing the global perspective transformation between them. During training, simulated homography deformations with corner perturbations in the range [-32, 32] pixels are applied to generate self-supervised pairs, and the modality transfer network converts one modality into a pseudo version of the other to enable supervised training without ground-truth cross-modal labels.

## Method

SSHNet reformulates unsupervised cross-modal homography estimation into two mutually causal sub-problems: (I) mono-modal homography estimation between a transferred image and the target, and (II) modality transfer supervised by the warped target. A split optimization strategy trains each network separately while freezing the other, with a two-branch self-supervised regularization term that guides early convergence. For correlation-based architectures (IHN, RHWF, SCPNet), an extra homography feature space supervision using a correlation-based loss directly enforces feature consistency between the transferred and target images. The modality transfer network uses a Swin-UNet architecture with perceptual loss to tolerate residual misalignment, and a distillation step transfers knowledge from the full SSHNet into a lighter student (SSHNet-D) that retains only the homography estimation network, reducing parameters while improving cross-domain generalization.

## Results

On the five cross-modal datasets, SSHNet-IHN achieves MACEs of 1.23 (GoogleMap), 1.12 (DPDN), 2.94 (OPT-SAR), 1.08 (Flash/no-flash), and 1.66 (RGB/NIR), outperforming all previous unsupervised methods by a large margin. On the challenging OPT-SAR dataset, SSHNet-IHN achieves 47.4% and 85.8% lower MACE than the supervised approaches MHN (5.59) and LocalTrans (20.68), respectively. The distilled variant SSHNet-IHN-D retains comparable performance (e.g., 3.31 vs. 2.94 on OPT-SAR) while reducing model parameters and improving cross-domain generalization, with up to 92.1% improvement in reliable-estimation ratio when trained on DPDN and tested on Flash/no-flash.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSHNet-IHN decisively outperforms all unsupervised baselines: it reduces MACE from 4.35 to 1.23 on GoogleMap (vs. SCPNet, the previous unsupervised SOTA), and from 24.76 to 2.94 on OPT-SAR (vs. CA-UDHN), with several baselines (UDHN, biHomE, SCPNet) failing to converge on DPDN and OPT-SAR entirely. Against supervised methods, SSHNet-IHN beats MHN by 54.3% on GoogleMap (1.23 vs. 2.69) and 47.4% on OPT-SAR (2.94 vs. 5.59), and beats LocalTrans by 61.8% on GoogleMap (1.23 vs. 3.22) and 85.8% on OPT-SAR (2.94 vs. 20.68). However, SSHNet-IHN still trails the strongest supervised iterative methods IHN (0.92 on GoogleMap, 1.67 on OPT-SAR) and RHWF (0.71 on GoogleMap, 1.48 on OPT-SAR), indicating that direct cross-modal supervision remains advantageous when available. Notably, SSHNet-SCPNet (using the same architecture as the prior SOTA) outperforms SCPNet itself on every dataset (e.g., 3.89 vs. 4.35 on GoogleMap, 2.65 vs. 2.67 on Flash/no-flash), isolating the contribution of the proposed training framework from architectural choices. The distilled SSHNet-IHN-D maintains within ~12% MACE of SSHNet-IHN while substantially improving cross-domain generalization (up to 92.1% gain in reliable-estimation ratio on Flash/no-flash when trained on DPDN).

## Relation to Prior Work

SSHNet departs from the dominant paradigm in unsupervised cross-modal homography estimation, where all prior methods (UDHN, CA-UDHN, biHomE, BasesHomo, SCPNet) rely on content-consistency indirect supervision via intensity or feature similarity losses. Instead, SSHNet reformulates the unsupervised problem into two supervised sub-problems—mono-modal homography estimation and modality transfer—each with direct supervision, and trains them via a split optimization strategy inspired by decomposition methods in convex optimization. The framework is architecture-agnostic and can incorporate iterative homography estimators (IHN, RHWF) that prior unsupervised SOTA (SCPNet) could not stably train, while the modality transfer component draws on image-to-image translation literature (Swin-UNet, perceptual loss).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that SSHNet enables iterative homography architectures to be trained in an unsupervised cross-modal setting for the first time, closing the gap between unsupervised and supervised methods to the point where SSHNet-IHN outperforms supervised baselines on challenging datasets. However, cross-domain generalization remains limited: the distillation variant (SSHNet-IHN-D) shows improved transfer across easy datasets but still struggles across challenging ones (e.g., OPT-SAR to GoogleMap), and the modality transfer network introduces additional computational cost. An open question is whether the split-optimization-for-mutually-dependent-sub-problems strategy generalizes beyond homography estimation to other unsupervised geometric estimation or registration tasks.
