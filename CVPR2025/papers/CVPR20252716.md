## Problem

Existing single-image human mesh recovery methods rely on orthographic projection assumptions or heuristic conversions from 2D affine transforms to perspective parameters, which break down for close-range images with strong perspective distortion. No prior method can accurately recover full perspective projection parameters (focal length and 3D translation) from a single image without such heuristics, and the 2D-vs-3D accuracy tradeoff remains unresolved for near-camera subjects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image along with a bounding-box crop of the person; the crop feeds the pelvis depth estimator while the full uncropped image feeds the pose estimator. Outputs include SMPL-X shape and pose parameters (β, θ), the pelvis Z-translation Tz, focal length f, and XY-translation (Tx, Ty), from which a fully perspective-projected 2D-aligned mesh is rendered. The camera solver additionally requires an off-the-shelf segmentation mask of the person for differentiable rasterization alignment.

## Method

BLADE exploits the key insight that perspective distortion is governed by the subject's Z-translation Tz (not focal length), enabling a 3-step disentangled pipeline. First, a pelvis depth estimator built on a DAv2 backbone with a ConvNet-transformer head predicts Tz from the crop, trained with an inverse-depth-weighted L1 loss to prioritize accuracy at close ranges (<1.2 m). Second, a Tz-aware pose estimator freezes the pretrained AiOS backbone and adds a trainable ControlNet-style copy whose features are conditioned on Tz via MLP-encoded depth features injected through a zero-initialized residual, preserving generalizability while learning close-range distortion. Third, a camera solver initializes f = image height and T = [0, 0, Tz], then optimizes (f, Tx, Ty) via differentiable rasterization to maximize IoU between the rendered SMPL-X mask and the segmentation mask. The authors also contribute BEDLAM-CC, a 2-million-image synthetic dataset with 80% of samples in the 0.3–1.2 m range, sampled uniformly in inverse depth to match the perspective distortion curve.

## Results

On the real-world SPEC-MTP dataset, BLADE (real-world) achieves E_Tz = 0.127 m, E_f = 0.159, PVE = 99.6 mm, and mIoU = 69.5%, representing an 85.9% relative reduction in depth error and 21.4% reduction in PVE over the prior SOTA ZOLLY. On the synthetic BEDLAM-CC dataset, BLADE attains 75.0% mIoU (vs. 51.8% for ZOLLY, a 44.8% relative gain) and 106.8 mm PVE. On PDHUMAN, BLADE achieves the best PVE (80.5 mm) and mIoU (67.3%) while also dramatically outperforming all baselines on camera parameter accuracy (E_Tz = 0.106 m vs. 0.255 m for ZOLLY and >2.2 m for AiOS/TokenHMR/SMPLer-X).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLADE decisively outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three evaluation datasets. Against ZOLLY (the prior perspective-aware SOTA), BLADE reduces E_Tz by 85.9% on SPEC-MTP (0.127 m vs. 0.899 m) and improves mIoU by 44.8% on BEDLAM-CC (75.0% vs. 51.8%), while also achieving lower PVE on SPEC-MTP (99.6 vs. 126.7 mm). The orthographic-based methods (AiOS, TokenHMR, SMPLer-X) use a fixed focal length of 5000 and do not estimate camera parameters, resulting in E_f ≈ 1.12 on SPEC-MTP and E_Tz > 2.2 m on PDHUMAN and BEDLAM-CC—orders of magnitude worse than BLADE's 0.13 m and 0.11 m respectively. Notably, these methods achieve competitive PVE on SPEC-MTP (102.6–124.3 mm) but fail on PDHUMAN (156–183 mm), suggesting their orthographic assumption breaks down under strong perspective distortion. BLADE's 'real-world' variant (trained with more H36M sampling) achieves the best PVE on SPEC-MTP (99.6 mm) and BEDLAM-CC (106.8 mm), while the balanced 'Ours' variant performs best on PDHUMAN (80.5 mm PVE, 67.3% mIoU), confirming that dataset composition matters for synthetic vs. real-world generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLADE builds on AiOS as its pose estimation backbone (frozen, with a ControlNet-style trainable copy conditioned on Tz) and DAv2 as its depth feature extractor, but departs fundamentally from the orthographic camera assumption that underpins most prior HMR methods (SMPLer-X, TokenHMR, AiOS, ZOLLY). Where ZOLLY heuristically converts a 2D affine scale to focal length and 3D translation, BLADE instead directly estimates pelvis depth Tz from the image and then solves for focal length and XY-translation via differentiable rasterization, avoiding all heuristic conversions. The paper also corrects a widespread misconception in the field: focal length acts only as a uniform image scale and does not affect perspective distortion, which is driven solely by Tz.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BLADE resolves a long-standing tradeoff in single-image HMR where methods achieved either accurate 3D pose or good 2D alignment but not both, by properly recovering the full perspective projection model. The BEDLAM-CC dataset (2M close-range images) addresses a critical data gap that limits training of depth-aware pose estimators. Limitations include single-person scope, no video exploitation, sensitivity to segmentation quality, and no handling of lens distortion; future work on multi-person, video, and learnable optimization in place of differentiable rasterization are natural next steps.
