## Problem

Existing SNN object detectors are either ANN-to-SNN conversions whose performance is capped by the baseline ANN and which require hundreds to thousands of time steps, or shallow directly-trained architectures that underutilize multi-scale feature information. A key open question is how to build a directly-trained, fully spiking, multi-scale SNN detector that achieves competitive accuracy with very few time steps while avoiding the energy overhead of non-spiking convolutional operations on shortcut paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250065/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts either static RGB images (resized to 640×640 and duplicated across T=5 time steps) or asynchronous event camera streams (304×240, binned into constant-width time slices and processed as T=5 frames). Outputs are produced by feeding the final membrane potentials of spiking neurons into a Decouple Head that generates class scores and bounding box coordinates via NMS-free post-processing, yielding per-object class labels and 2D box coordinates.

## Method

MSD introduces two core components: (1) the Optic Nerve Nucleus Block (ONNB), which uses a Spiking Convolutional Neuron (SCN) built from LIF dynamics, tdBN normalization, and surrogate-gradient backpropagation to enable direct training of deep SNNs; the ONNB enforces fully spiking residual paths by replacing non-spiking shortcut convolutions with maxpooling followed by SCN, eliminating MAC operations and reducing energy to 6.43 mJ. (2) The Multi-scale Spiking Detection Framework (MSDF) fuses features across depths using SCN-based spiking multi-scale fusion and employs a Decouple Head where SCN spike-driven excitation automatically balances the classification and regression branches. The entire network is trained end-to-end with STBP and tdBN over 300 epochs, requiring only 5 time steps and 7.8 M parameters to reach 62.0% mAP on COCO and 66.3% mAP on Gen1.

## Results

On COCO 2017, MSD achieves 62.0% mAP@0.5 and 45.3% mAP@0.5:0.95 with only 7.8M parameters and 6.4 mJ energy consumption, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art SNN (SpikeYOLO) by +2.8% on both metrics. On the Gen1 neuromorphic dataset, MSD reaches 66.3% mAP@0.5 and 38.9% mAP@0.5:0.95 with 6.51 mJ, surpassing SpikeYOLO by +0.3 and +0.4 respectively while using less than 41% of its parameters and energy. The ablation shows that combining ONNB and MSDF reduces energy consumption by 82.9% and parameter count by 11.2% relative to the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSD outperforms all seven compared SNN baselines on both datasets. On COCO 2017, MSD surpasses the closest competitor SpikeYOLO [34] by +2.8% mAP@0.5 (62.0 vs. 59.2) and +2.8% mAP@0.5:0.95 (45.3 vs. 42.5), while using 41% fewer parameters (7.8M vs. 13.2M) and 72% less energy (6.4 vs. 23.1 mJ). The gap widens substantially against earlier methods: EMS-YOLO [45] achieves only 50.1% mAP@0.5 with 26.9M parameters, and Spiking-YOLO [23] reaches just 25.7% mAP@0.5:0.95. On the Gen1 event-camera dataset, MSD edges out SpikeYOLO [34] by +0.3 mAP@0.5 and +0.4 mAP@0.5:0.95 at the same T×D=5×1 setting, while consuming 40.8% less energy (6.51 vs. 11.0 mJ). Compared to Tr-Spiking-YOLO [51] (45.3% mAP@0.5) and Spiking-YOLO [23] (44.2% mAP@0.5, 102.3 mJ), MSD achieves a 21-point mAP improvement with dramatically lower energy. Meta-SpikeFormer(YOLO) [50] reports 50.3% mAP@0.5 but at 70.7 mJ—over 11× the energy of MSD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant ANN-to-SNN conversion paradigm (Spiking-YOLO, Spike Calibration) that requires hundreds to thousands of time steps, instead adopting the directly-trained SNN lineage established by STBP and tdBN for classification tasks and extended to detection by EMS-YOLO and SpikeYOLO. The Optic Nerve Nucleus Block (ONNB) is a novel architectural contribution that repurposes the LIF neuron and residual learning ideas from MSResNet into a fully spiking module designed to avoid non-spiking MAC operations on shortcut paths. The multi-scale detection framework draws on feature pyramid concepts from ANN detectors (FPN, SPPF) but re-implements them entirely with spiking convolutional neurons, positioning the work at the intersection of biological neural circuit modeling (dendritic heterogeneity, bypass pathways) and practical detection architecture design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 82.9% energy reduction (38.2→6.5 mJ) and 7.8M parameter count make this one of the most efficient SNN detectors reported, with practical implications for deployment on neuromorphic hardware where only spike operations are permitted. However, the paper's limitations are notable: no ablation varying network depth or width, the effect of the membrane potential reset mechanism on convergence is unexplored, and the claimed superiority over ANN detectors is relegated to supplementary material. Open questions include whether the ONNB's advantage over standard residual blocks persists at greater depths, whether the 5-time-step budget generalizes to more complex scenes, and how the approach scales to multi-task or video-based detection settings.
