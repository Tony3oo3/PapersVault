## Problem

Existing Minecraft goal-conditioned policies neglect the causal relationship between observations and actions at each timestep, and rely on implicit goal embeddings (from video encoders or CVAEs) that cannot adequately capture the complex relationship between open-ended language sub-goals and observation-action sequences. Additionally, high-quality aligned goal-observation-action datasets are scarce, with existing datasets either lacking natural language goals or using misaligned pseudo-labels.

## Input / Output

The agent operates at 20 fps in MineRL, consuming a single RGB game-screen observation, a natural-language task instruction, and the full historical observation-action sequence. The MLLM planner outputs a sequence of executable sub-goals as text, which are then fed individually to GOAP; GOAP outputs discrete low-level mouse/keyboard control actions via a VPT-based action head that maps the LLM's output embeddings into the Minecraft action space. The MLLM backbone is initialized from DeepSeek-VL-1.3B, and the visual encoder is a ViT producing patch-level features.

## Method

GOAP consists of two components. The Action-guided Behavior Encoder first uses a Causal Perceiver (cross-attention with action embeddings as keys/values and ViT visual features as queries) to inject causal action information into observation features at each timestep, then a History Aggregator (Q-former with a similarity-based memory bank) dynamically compresses the entire observation-action history into fixed-length behavior tokens. The MLLM backbone (DeepSeek-VL-1.3B) takes the sub-goal text, current visual features, and behavior tokens as input and predicts the next action auto-regressively, with VPT serving as the action head. Training combines behavior cloning loss with a KL-divergence distillation term from the VPT teacher model. The accompanying MGOA dataset (~30M aligned pairs from 25K videos across 8 atomic tasks) is generated automatically by running existing agents with natural-language instructions and filtering for successful, timely completions.

## Results

On atomic tasks, GOAP[VPT] achieves average rewards of 15.0, 8.5, 26.7, and 25.7 on Logs, Seeds, Dirt, and Stone respectively, outperforming the previous SOTA GROOT (14.3, 7.3, 19.7, 19.0) by 5%, 4%, 31%, and 35%. On long-horizon tasks, Optimus-2 achieves success rates of 99%, 93%, 53%, 9%, 13%, 28%, and 21% across seven task groups (Wood through Armor), surpassing Optimus-1 by an average of 10%. On open-ended instruction tasks, GOAP with GPT-4V as planner achieves success rates of 75%, 47%, 13%, 16%, and 17% on Torch, Rail, Golden Shovel, Diamond Pickaxe, and Compass, while all existing policies score 0% on the last three tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Optimus-2 (GOAP policy + GPT-4V planner) outperforms all seven baselines across all three evaluation settings. On atomic tasks, GOAP[VPT] beats GROOT (previous SOTA) by 5–35% in average reward and FSQ GROOT by 39–360%, while also surpassing STEVE-1 and VPT(text) by large margins. On long-horizon tasks, Optimus-2 improves over Optimus-1 (its direct predecessor) by an average of 10% success rate, and over Jarvis-1 by 27% and DEPS by 43%, with the largest gains on the hardest groups (Diamond: 13% vs. 11%; RedStone: 28% vs. 25%). On open-ended instruction tasks, GOAP with GPT-4V achieves 75% on Torch and 47% on Rail, whereas STEVE-1 drops to 66% and 10% respectively, and VPT(text) scores only 11% and 0%. Critically, on Golden Shovel, Diamond Pickaxe, and Compass, all existing policies (VPT, STEVE-1) score 0%, while GOAP achieves 13–17%, demonstrating that the MLLM backbone is essential for open-ended language comprehension. The gap to the human baseline narrows from ~15% (Optimus-1) to ~10% (Optimus-2) on long-horizon tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Minecraft agent lineage from VPT (behavior cloning on unlabeled video) through STEVE-1 (adding MineCLIP as a goal encoder) and GROOT (video-encoder-based goal conditioning) to a fundamentally different architectural choice: using an MLLM (DeepSeek-VL) as the core backbone of the goal-conditioned policy itself, rather than merely as a high-level planner. The Action-guided Behavior Encoder draws on the memory-augmented long-sequence modeling of MA-LMM [14] but adapts it to jointly encode observation-action causal structure via a Causal Perceiver and a History Aggregator with a fixed-length memory bank. The key departure from prior goal-conditioned policies (STEVE-1, GROOT, FSQ GROOT) is the explicit modeling of the causal link between actions and subsequent observations at each timestep, and the replacement of implicit goal embeddings with autoregressive MLLM-based alignment to open-ended language sub-goals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MGOA dataset (25K videos, ~30M aligned goal-observation-action pairs) and its automated generation pipeline address a genuine data bottleneck for Minecraft policy training, offering the community a resource that prior datasets (VPT, MineCLIP, STEVE-1) lacked in terms of goal-action alignment. However, Optimus-2 still trails human performance substantially on harder long-horizon groups (Diamond: 13% vs. 16%; Redstone: 28% vs. 33%), and the MLLM backbone introduces significant computational overhead (8× L40 GPUs for training). Open questions include whether the causal-perceiver + MLLM-policy architecture generalizes beyond Minecraft to other embodied domains, and whether the 30M-pair dataset is sufficient to close the remaining gap to human-level performance or whether further scaling is needed.
