## Problem

Existing any-to-any generative models either stitch together modality-specific encoders and decoders (limiting cross-modal information flow) or require training a single unified model from scratch with massive compute. No open-sourced unified model simultaneously supports text, image, and audio generation, and it remains unclear whether text-to-image flow-matching recipes generalize to audio and text generation.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any subset of {text, image, audio} as conditioning input and generates any subset as output, including joint outputs like image+audio from a text prompt. Inputs are encoded to latents via modality-specific VAEs (audio spectrograms at 16 kHz, text via a text VAE, images via an image VAE); missing modalities are filled with Gaussian noise at timestep 1. A single unified sinusoidal timestep embedding is shared across all modalities, and the model outputs velocity predictions for each active modality simultaneously.

## Method

OmniFlow extends the MMDiT architecture of Stable Diffusion 3 by adding independent audio and text streams alongside the existing image and text streams, where each modality has its own projection and FFN layers but all modalities interact through a parameter-free joint attention at every layer. The training objective is a multi-modal rectified flow formulation: each modality has an independent noise level t_i, and any-to-any tasks are encoded as linear paths through the joint timestep space (e.g., text+audio→image is a path from (0,0,1) to (0,0,0) in image-text-audio coordinates), with the loss summing velocity-matching errors only over active modalities. A multi-modal classifier-free guidance mechanism generalizes standard CFG by defining per-pair influence terms δ_ij, allowing independent control of how strongly each input modality conditions each output modality. The modular design enables a practical training recipe: initialize image+text modules from SD3, pretrain a separate text-to-audio model, merge them (averaging overlapping text-module weights), then fine-tune the combined model on diverse any-to-any tasks—requiring only 30M fine-tuning images versus billions for from-scratch unified models.

## Results

On MSCOCO-30K text-to-image generation, OmniFlow achieves FID 13.40 and CLIP score 31.54, outperforming SDXL (FID 16.49) and SD3-Medium (FID 20.94) while matching SD3-Medium on GenEval (0.62). On AudioCaps text-to-audio generation, OmniFlow achieves FAD 1.75 and CLAP 0.183, surpassing the specialist AudioLDM2 (FAD 1.86, CLAP 0.182) and significantly outperforming the generalist CoDi (FAD 1.80, CLAP 0.053). Notably, OmniFlow achieves these results using only 30M fine-tuning images, compared to billions for specialist models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest any-to-any baselines, OmniFlow wins on CLIP score (+0.61 over UniDiffuser, +0.85 over CoDi) and GenEval (+0.19 over UniDiffuser, +0.24 over CoDi), though it trails UniDiffuser on FID (13.40 vs. 9.71) and CoDi on FID (13.40 vs. 11.26). Compared to text-to-image specialists, OmniFlow beats SDXL on both FID (13.40 vs. 16.49) and CLIP (31.54 vs. 31.36), and matches SD3-Medium on GenEval (0.62) while using only 30M fine-tuning images versus 1B for SD3. On text-to-audio, OmniFlow outperforms the specialist AudioLDM2 on FAD (1.75 vs. 1.86) and ties on CLAP (0.183 vs. 0.182), while dramatically surpassing CoDi on CLAP (0.183 vs. 0.053). Against larger generalist models, OmniFlow (3.4B, 30M images) outperforms Chameleon-7B (GenEval 0.62 vs. 0.39) and is competitive with Transfusion-7B (0.62 vs. 0.63) despite using ~100× fewer training images. The paper notes that FID comparisons are confounded by training data distribution differences, as newer models trained on high-quality data (SDXL, SD3) show inflated FID on COCO despite being perceptually superior.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniFlow extends the MMDiT architecture of Stable Diffusion 3 from a two-stream (text+image) design to a three-stream (text+image+audio) framework, replacing the single joint-attention block with modality-specific projections that share only the attention operation. Conceptually, it departs from prior any-to-any models like CoDi (which naively averages modality embeddings for joint conditioning) and from unified models like UniDiffuser, Chameleon, and Transfusion (which train a single monolithic network from scratch) by introducing a modular design where each modality's parameters are independent and can be pretrained separately before merging. The multi-modal rectified flow formulation generalizes the single-modality rectified flow of Liu et al. by representing each any-to-any task as a path in a multi-dimensional noise space, and the multi-modal guidance mechanism extends classifier-free guidance to independently weight the influence of each input modality on each output modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniFlow is the first open-source unified generative model supporting text, image, and audio simultaneously, and its modular design (pretrain individual modality experts, merge, fine-tune) dramatically reduces the compute required compared to training unified models from scratch—achieving competitive GenEval scores with 30M images versus 3.5B for Chameleon/Transfusion. The systematic ablation of flow-matching formulations (eps/linear, v/cos, v/linear, rf/uniform, rf/lognorm) for audio and text generation fills a gap in the literature where text-to-image findings could not be assumed to transfer. Open questions include whether the modular-merge strategy scales to additional modalities (video, 3D), whether the path-based task formulation generalizes to non-linear or conditional paths, and whether the modest text-generation quality (CLAP ~0.25) can be improved without sacrificing the unified architecture.
