## Problem

Video quality varies across spatiotemporal regions due to motion blur, compression, and texture complexity, yet existing no-reference VQA methods lack robust constraints on local perception because region-wise quality annotations are prohibitively expensive to collect (scaling as O(N³)). Prior approaches either produce quality maps conflated with visual saliency or rely on external saliency models as ground truth, failing to properly decouple the two distinct perceptual factors—global attention allocation and local low-level texture—that jointly determine human-perceived quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (32 frames sampled from 8 uniformly spaced segments, each resized to 448×448) and produces three outputs: a scalar global quality score computed as the weighted average of element-wise saliency×texture products, a spatiotemporal saliency map (T×H×W) reflecting attention allocation, and a local texture map (T×H×W) capturing region-specific distortions independent of global semantics. For local perception validation, the authors introduce the LPVQ dataset where 50 images are treated as 16-frame static videos and divided into 7×7 grids, with 34,300 expert annotations (14 raters, 1–5 scale) providing region-wise ground truth.

## Method

KVQ builds on a Video Swin-T backbone (pretrained on Kinetics-400) and introduces two key modules. First, Fusion-Window Attention (FWA) replaces standard window attention with a combination of Intra-Window Attention and Cross-Window Attention: a Correlated-Window Selection step computes a window-level correlation map via patch-level attention and selects the top-k most semantically related windows, enabling each window to attend to both its own patches and those in correlated distant windows, thereby modeling long-range attention allocation analogous to human visual saliency. Second, a Local Perception Constraint (LPC) enforces consistency by requiring that the texture prediction for a region extracted from the full-video forward pass matches the prediction obtained by feeding that region independently into the same network, minimizing a cosine-distance loss that decouples local texture from inter-regional correlations. A dual-branch head predicts the saliency map (fusing multi-scale correlation maps from N FWA blocks) and the local texture map, which are combined via element-wise multiplication and averaging to yield the final quality score. Training uses a weighted sum of PLCC loss, a rank-based loss for relative quality ordering, and the LPC term.

## Results

On the intra-dataset LSVQtest benchmark, KVQ achieves the best SRCC of 0.896 and PLCC of 0.897, surpassing the previous best (Fast-VQA) by 2.3% on both metrics. On the higher-resolution LSVQ_1080p, KVQ reaches SRCC 0.814 and PLCC 0.846, improving over Fast-VQA by 4.5% and 3.9% respectively. In cross-dataset generalization, KVQ attains SRCC 0.890 / PLCC 0.892 on KoNViD-1k (+3.6%/+4.3% over Fast-VQA) and SRCC 0.820 / PLCC 0.843 on LIVE-VQC. In transfer-learning evaluation (Table 2), KVQ further leads on YouTube-UGC with SRCC 0.903 / PLCC 0.905, a 5.6–6.2% gain over Fast-VQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KVQ outperforms all 14 baselines on LSVQtest, LSVQ_1080p, and KoNViD-1k, with the largest margins over handcrafted-feature methods (BRISQUE, TLVQM, VIDEVAL) exceeding 10–30 SRCC points. Against the closest Transformer-based competitors, KVQ beats Fast-VQA by +2.3% SRCC on LSVQtest, +4.5% on LSVQ_1080p, and +3.6% on KoNViD-1k, while slightly trailing Fast-VQA by 0.4% SRCC on LIVE-VQC. KVQ also surpasses Faster-VQA (which uses a lighter 69.8 GFlops budget) by 2.3–4.5% SRCC across all four benchmarks, and outperforms the heavier Li et al. [21] (112,537 GFlops) by 4.4–7.9% SRCC. In transfer-learning settings (Table 2), KVQ leads Fast-VQA by +1.2–2.0% on LIVE-VQC/KoNViD-1k and by a substantial +5.6–6.2% on YouTube-UGC, while also surpassing StarVQA+ (the strongest multi-pretrain baseline) by 0.2–7.7% SRCC depending on the target dataset. The computational cost of KVQ (353 GFlops) is modestly higher than Fast-VQA (279 GFlops) but far below Li et al. (112,537 GFlops) and PVQ (58,501 GFlops).

## Relation to Prior Work

KVQ builds on the Video Swin Transformer backbone used by Fast-VQA and Faster-VQA, but departs from their static window-attention design by introducing Fusion-Window Attention (FWA), which adaptively selects correlated windows for cross-window attention inspired by BiFormer's bilevel routing. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior saliency-guided VQA methods (SGDNet, TranSLA, MMMNet) that rely on external saliency models as ground truth, instead deriving saliency endogenously from the attention mechanism itself. The Local Perception Constraint (LPC) is a novel self-supervised regularization that enforces region-independent texture perception without requiring any local annotations, addressing a gap left by Fast-VQA and PVQ, which produce local maps but lack explicit constraints decoupling texture from saliency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the LPVQ dataset (34,300 region-wise annotations) is the first benchmark enabling direct evaluation of local quality perception, filling a notable gap in the VQA literature where local maps were produced but never validated against ground truth. The LPC's effectiveness (nearly doubling intra-sample SRCC on LPVQ) suggests that self-supervised constraints can substitute for expensive local annotations, a direction with broader implications for fine-grained quality assessment. However, the LPVQ dataset is limited to 50 static images (treated as single-frame videos), so whether the local perception capability generalizes to genuinely dynamic video content with temporal distortions remains an open question. The modest 2–4% gains on standard VQA benchmarks over Fast-VQA/Faster-VQA, while consistent, do not constitute a paradigm shift in global quality prediction.
