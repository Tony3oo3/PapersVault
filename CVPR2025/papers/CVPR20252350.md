## Problem

Reconstructing high-quality meshes from sparse point clouds is critical for CAD reverse engineering, but sharp edges are rarely captured by sampled points, causing visible artifacts in standard reconstruction methods like Poisson or Voronoi. Existing upsampling and consolidation approaches increase point density near edges but cannot place points accurately on them, and analytic curve-fitting methods require per-object parameter tuning. The paper addresses this gap by learning to jointly predict geometric features that enable precise edge point estimation without requiring normals or dense sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw point cloud of 3D coordinates only (no normals), typically 8,192–10,000 points, normalized to a unit sphere centered at the origin. It produces an edge-preserving 3D mesh as the final output, along with intermediate per-point predictions: surface normals, unit vectors pointing toward the nearest sharp edge, and scalar distances to that edge. The pipeline also generates a refined set of edge points (via projection, interpolation, and optimization) that are used to relocate vertices in the base reconstruction, so the mesh topology is unchanged but vertex positions snap to estimated edge locations.

## Method

EdgeMovingNet is a global encoder-decoder network (N×3 coordinates → N×256 features → three decoders) that jointly regresses point normals, point-to-edge directions, and point-to-edge distances, avoiding patch-based processing to maintain consistent edge priors across the whole shape. Training uses a combined loss of L1 terms for each feature plus two geometric regularizers: a surface loss (projected edge point must lie on the ground-truth surface plane) and a tangent loss (local edge-point displacement must be perpendicular to both the normal and the edge direction), with an edge mask restricting direction-related losses to near-edge points (d_e ≤ 0.05). A post-hoc refinement process projects near-edge points onto their estimated edges, adds midpoint-interpolated points, applies farthest-point sampling for even distribution, and optimizes positions via a least-squares objective using predicted normals (solved with LBFGS). Finally, a relocation strategy runs Poisson reconstruction on the original cloud with predicted normals, then moves mesh vertices within a threshold to their nearest refined edge point, preserving connectivity while snapping geometry to edges.

## Results

On the ABC dataset (8,192 input points, no normals), EdgeMovingNet achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.703×10⁻²), Hausdorff Distance (1.368×10⁻²), Precision (0.993), Recall (0.992), and F-score (0.993) among all compared methods. Normal Consistency is competitive at 0.991, slightly below Voronoi (0.993) and PCP (0.996). The method also generalizes to ModelNet (CD 0.919, F-score 0.982) and ShapeNet (CD 1.186, F-score 0.939) without retraining, and remains robust under 1% and 2% Gaussian noise (HD 1.858 and 1.902 respectively, vs. 3.456 and 4.983 for Voronoi).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EdgeMovingNet outperforms all seven baselines on Chamfer Distance, Hausdorff Distance, and F-score on the ABC dataset. The closest competitor is GeoUDF [39] (CD 0.709, HD 1.444, F-score 0.962), which EdgeMovingNet beats by 0.006 CD, 0.076 HD, and 0.031 F-score. RFEPS [47] (CD 0.755, HD 1.401) and Voronoi [5] (CD 0.776, HD 1.695) are also clearly surpassed on geometric accuracy. NKSR [14] achieves high Precision (0.991) but suffers from a much larger HD (2.331 vs. 1.368), indicating poor worst-case edge alignment. POCO [6] and PCP [29] perform substantially worse (CD 4.388 and 1.573 respectively), struggling with sparse inputs. The only metrics where EdgeMovingNet does not lead are Normal Consistency, where Voronoi (0.993) and PCP (0.996) are marginally higher, though the gap is negligible (≤0.005). On ModelNet and ShapeNet, the same pattern holds: EdgeMovingNet achieves the best CD and F-score across all methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

EdgeMovingNet carves new ground by reframing edge estimation as a joint regression problem over three continuous geometric features (point-to-edge direction, point-to-edge distance, and point normal) rather than the more common binary edge-point classification or parametric curve fitting used by prior methods like PIE-Net or DEF. It departs from patch-based reconstruction approaches by adopting a global encoder-decoder prediction strategy, and it avoids the normal-input requirement of methods like Poisson and RFEPS by predicting normals end-to-end. The refinement pipeline—combining midpoint interpolation, FPS-based densification, and a normal-guided LBFGS optimization—repurposes ideas from RFEPS's point optimization into a learned-feature context, while the final relocation strategy treats Poisson reconstruction as a pluggable base solver rather than the final output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap in CAD reverse engineering: producing sharp, accurate edges from sparse, normal-free point clouds without manual parameter tuning. Its ability to generalize across edge types (lines, circles, B-splines) and to ModelNet/ShapeNet without retraining suggests broad applicability. However, the authors acknowledge limitations with very thin structures and heavy noise, and the modest quantitative margins over strong baselines like GeoUDF and RFEPS (e.g., CD 0.703 vs. 0.709) leave open whether the joint-feature formulation offers a fundamental advantage or primarily a practical convenience. Future work could explore whether the refinement process generalizes to non-CAD organic shapes or whether the global (non-patch) prediction strategy scales to very large scenes.
