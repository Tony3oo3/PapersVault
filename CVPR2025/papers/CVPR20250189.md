## Problem

Current Customized Portrait Generation (CPG) methods produce high-fidelity portraits but offer no protection against malicious face recognition systems tracking and misusing the generated images. Conversely, existing adversarial attack methods for facial privacy only modify the original face and cannot produce personalized, scene-customized portraits. Adv-CPG is the first work to unify facial adversarial attacks with test-time tuning-free CPG, generating portraits that are both visually customized and resistant to FR-based identification.

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a reference facial image (the person whose likeness should be preserved), a free-form text prompt describing the desired scene or action (e.g., 'in the park'), and a target identity image (the identity the adversarial example should be misclassified as). The output is a head-and-shoulders or half-body adversarial portrait at SDXL resolution, generated via a 50-step DDIM sampler with classifier-free guidance scale 5. The portrait appears visually consistent with the reference person to human observers while causing black-box FR systems to match it to the target identity with high confidence.

## Method

Adv-CPG is built on SDXL and comprises three modules operating in a two-stage pipeline. In the first stage (Progressive Facial Privacy Protection), the ID Encryptor (En1) injects a composite target identity feature—combining CLIP image encoder embeddings and white-box FR model embeddings—directly into an identity-dependent cross-attention layer (IP-Adapter style), while the Encryption Enhancer (En2) adds per-step ID gradient guidance to the denoising trajectory. In the second stage (Fine-Grained Customized Portrait Generation), the Multi-Modal Image Customizer (MMIC) uses LLaVA-1.5 to generate a detailed facial description, concatenates it with the original text prompt for an augmented text embedding, and injects both the augmented text and CLIP image features into a decoupled cross-attention layer for fine-grained control. During training, only the ID projector weights in En1 are optimized (all SDXL parameters frozen) using a standard diffusion denoising loss; at inference, a delayed primacy conditioning strategy (replacing the initial text embedding with the augmented one after a specific step) balances identity preservation against scene personalization.

## Results

On the face verification task (Table 2), Adv-CPG achieves an average black-box ASR of 79.65% across FFHQ and CelebA-HQ against four FR models (IR152, IRSE50, FaceNet, MobileFace), surpassing the best noise-based method TIP-IM (51.58%) by 28.1% and the best unconstrained method DiffAM (76.79%) by 2.86%. On face identification (LFW, Table 3), Adv-CPG reaches 34.55% Rank-1-Targeted and 61.35% Rank-5-Targeted ASR, outperforming the prior best GIFT (32.55% / 59.95%). In terms of image quality (Table 4), Adv-CPG attains the highest PSNR (30.00) and competitive SSIM (0.898) and FID (26.08) among methods with strong attack performance, while commercial API confidence scores average ~77.5 (Face++) and ~63.0 (Aliyun).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adv-CPG achieves the highest average ASR (79.65%) among all 17 compared methods, winning on 6 of 8 individual model–dataset combinations. Against the strongest noise-based baseline TIP-IM (51.58%), Adv-CPG gains +28.1% on average, with the largest margin on FaceNet (+4.0% on FFHQ, +24.4% on CelebA-HQ). Against the strongest unconstrained baseline DiffAM (76.79%), the gain is +2.86% on average; Adv-CPG loses narrowly on FFHQ FaceNet (63.84 vs. 64.96) and CelebA-HQ IRSE50 (88.72 vs. 89.66) but wins on the remaining six settings. Compared to the next-best unconstrained methods P3-Mask (71.74%) and GIFT (74.40%), Adv-CPG leads by +7.9% and +5.3% respectively. On face identification (LFW), Adv-CPG outperforms GIFT by +2.0% (R1-T) and +1.4% (R5-T), and surpasses all other unrestricted methods by at least 2.0%. In image quality, Adv-CPG attains the best PSNR (30.00) and competitive FID (26.08) among high-ASR methods, though Adv-Makeup achieves a lower FID (4.23) at the cost of a much weaker ASR (14.15%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: customized portrait generation (CPG) via diffusion models (building on IP-Adapter, InstantID, PuLID, and SDXL) and adversarial attacks on face recognition (spanning noise-based methods like PGD/TIP-IM and unrestricted semantic-space methods like DiffAM, GIFT, and Adv-Diffusion). The ID Encryptor (En1) is architecturally derived from IP-Adapter's cross-attention injection, while the Encryption Enhancer (En2) borrows the gradient-guidance idea from AdvDiffusion and FreeTuner. The key departure is that prior adversarial attack methods only perturb the original face (no scene or style customization), and prior CPG methods generate high-fidelity portraits with no privacy mechanism; Adv-CPG is the first to unify both objectives in a single diffusion-based pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: social-media users increasingly want attractive, scene-customized portraits that simultaneously resist tracking by commercial face recognition systems, a need neither prior CPG nor prior adversarial-attack literature addresses. The progressive two-layer encryption (global ID injection + local gradient guidance) provides a clean architectural recipe that could be transplanted to other identity-preserving generation tasks. Open questions include whether the targeted-attack formulation generalizes to untargeted or multi-target settings, whether the approach extends to video or face-swapping pipelines (as the authors themselves suggest), and how robust the generated adversarial portraits are against emerging FR defenses or re-identification via auxiliary cues (gait, accessories).
