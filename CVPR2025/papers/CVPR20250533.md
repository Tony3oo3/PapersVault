## Problem

Existing head-swapping methods rely on face-centered cropped datasets with primarily frontal-facing views, and their masking strategies are optimized for such crops, failing to produce seamless blends in real-world scenarios involving full upper-body images, diverse head orientations, or features like long hair extending beyond the masked region. No prior method addresses these complexities in a zero-shot setting without requiring additional pasting steps or multi-view inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250533/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two full upper-body RGB images (a body image and a head image, each 512×512 preprocessed from SHHQ's 1024×512 originals and upscaled to 1024×1024 via GFPGAN) along with a text prompt, and produces a single head-swapped image where the entire head—face identity, face shape, hairstyle, and skin tone—from the head image is seamlessly integrated onto the body image. The process is zero-shot: no per-person training or manual mask annotation is required, and the method handles arbitrary head orientations without explicit alignment.

## Method

HID (Head Injection Diffusion) is built on PhotoMaker V2 and introduces two key components. First, IOMask automatically generates a context-aware editing mask by performing DDIM inversion on the body image, then computing the orthogonal projection of the head-conditioned predicted noise onto the body-conditioned predicted noise (Eq. 3), thresholding, and applying a Gaussian filter—yielding a mask that precisely isolates the head region without manual annotation. Second, a Hair Injection Module (a Q-former + MLPs trained with a masked reconstruction loss on SHHQ) fuses hair embeddings from a pretrained Hair Encoder and CLIP image encoder into text embeddings, enabling precise hairstyle transfer that PhotoMaker V2 alone cannot achieve. During denoising, the process starts from the DDIM inversion latent and blends the denoised latent with the inversion latent via the IOMask at each step (Eq. 5), while an open-pose ControlNet provides body alignment, ensuring that only the masked head region is modified and the rest of the body image is preserved exactly.

## Results

On the SHHQ-1.0 evaluation set, HID achieves a FID of 37.19, outperforming REFace (40.72) by approximately 8.7%. For head-region fidelity, HID obtains LPIPS of 0.0721 and CLIP-I of 0.8512, compared to REFace's 0.0770 and 0.7867 respectively, indicating substantially better identity and face-shape preservation. For hair-region fidelity, HID achieves LPIPS of 0.0596 and CLIP-I of 0.8686 versus REFace's 0.0658 and 0.8563, confirming superior hairstyle transfer. HID wins on all five reported metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline, REFace [2], because the authors note that no other zero-shot head-swapping method operates without face-centered cropping or alignment, making direct comparison infeasible. HID outperforms REFace on every metric: FID improves from 40.72 to 37.19 (−8.7%), head CLIP-I rises from 0.7867 to 0.8512 (+8.2%), and hair CLIP-I increases from 0.8563 to 0.8686 (+1.4%). The LPIPS gaps are smaller but consistent (head: 0.0770→0.0721; hair: 0.0658→0.0596). Qualitatively, REFace suffers from hair being cut off at crop boundaries and poor blending in challenging cases with differing head orientations or gender, whereas HID's IOMask and full-body formulation produce seamless integration without requiring a separate paste-back step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the head-swapping task from the face-centered, frontal-view regime used by prior methods (REFace, FaceX, HeSer) to full upper-body images with diverse orientations in a zero-shot setting. Methodologically, it builds on PhotoMaker V2's ID-embedding fusion architecture for identity preservation and adapts DDIM inversion as the backbone for both mask extraction (IOMask) and the denoising initialization. The IOMask mechanism departs from prior noise-difference or attention-based mask strategies by projecting the head-conditioned predicted noise onto the orthogonal complement of the body-conditioned noise, yielding a context-aware editing region without requiring external segmentation. A dedicated Hair Fusion module, trained analogously to PhotoMaker V2's ID Fusion, is introduced to inject hairstyle information that the base identity model does not capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clear practical gap: existing head-swapping pipelines require face-centered crops and frontal views, forcing an additional (often artifact-prone) paste-back step for real-world upper-body images. The IOMask's automatic, context-aware mask generation removes the need for manual or dataset-specific masking, making the pipeline more deployable. However, the evaluation is limited to a single baseline (REFace) on one dataset (SHHQ-1.0), and the authors acknowledge that non-head regions (hands, neck) can suffer unintended modifications. Open questions include whether the orthogonal-projection mask generalizes to more extreme pose differences, whether the approach scales to video, and how to better preserve fine details in adjacent body regions during skin-tone transfer.
