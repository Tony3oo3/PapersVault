## Problem

In immutable heterogeneous collaborative perception, agents have fixed, architecturally different perception networks whose intermediate features are semantically misaligned. Existing interpreters either require training and storing a separate interpreter per new agent type (one-stage), limiting extensibility, or route features through a standardized intermediate semantic space (two-stage), incurring cumulative semantic loss at each stage. The paper addresses the open question of how to achieve both high extensibility to new agent types and low-loss one-stage feature interpretation simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The ego agent (e.g., PointPillar pp8 producing 256×50×176 BEV features) receives heterogeneous neighbor BEV features with varying channel counts, voxel resolutions, and semantic encodings (e.g., VoxelNet vn4 at 0.4m resolution, SECOND sd1 at 0.1m resolution). A shared general prompt G (sized to ego features) and per-neighbor specific prompts S_i (channel dimension matching the neighbor, spatial dimensions matching ego) are added to the reorganized neighbor features. The interpreted features are fused with ego features via F-cooper or CoBEVT, and the frozen ego detection head outputs 3D bounding boxes evaluated over x∈[-140,140]m, y∈[-40,40]m.

## Method

PolyInter is a single polymorphic interpreter on the ego agent comprising a channel selection module (cosine-similarity cross-channel matching between ego and neighbor features, followed by softmax-weighted reorganization) and a spatial attention module (fused axial attention for local and global 3D spatial alignment). Feature semantics are decoupled into shared and agent-specific components via a general prompt G and per-neighbor specific prompts S_i, enforced by an adversarial loss (making G's output indistinguishable from ego features) and a single-agent detection loss plus style loss (making S_i retain agent-specific information). Training proceeds in two phases: Phase I jointly trains the interpreter network, G, and S_i for existing agents; Phase II freezes all interpreter parameters and G, fine-tuning only the new agent's S_i and a 1D-convolution resizer. Specific prompts can be low-rank decomposed (C×H×W → C×R×(H+W)) to reduce trainable parameters to as little as 0.4–1.4% of the full interpreter while still outperforming baselines that train all parameters per agent pair.

## Results

On the OPV2V benchmark, PolyInter achieves AP@0.5/AP@0.7 of 79.4/66.3 (pp8-sd1), 77.2/65.8 (pp8-pp4), and 72.8/55.1 (pp8-vn6) with F-cooper fusion, outperforming the best baseline by up to 11.1% in AP@0.7. With CoBEVT fusion, the best result is 81.7/69.7 on pp8-sd1. Critically, these gains are achieved by training only 1.4% of the interpreter's parameters (specific prompt + resizer) when adapting to a new neighbor agent, versus full retraining required by baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolyInter is compared against two existing interpreters for immutable heterogeneous collaborative perception: PnPDA (ECCV 2025, two-stage via a standard semantic space) and MPDA (ICRA 2023, one-stage direct interpretation). PolyInter wins in all six scenario-fusion combinations. The largest margin is in the pp8-sd1 scenario with F-cooper fusion, where PolyInter achieves 79.4/66.3 versus PnPDA's 68.4/40.3 and MPDA's 59.4/43.4 — a gap of over 20 AP points at IoU 0.7. In the pp8-pp4 scenario (milder heterogeneity), PolyInter's advantage narrows to ~9–15 points at IoU 0.7, with PnPDA nearly matching it at IoU 0.5 (77.1 vs 77.2). Notably, with CoBEVT fusion, MPDA narrows the gap on pp8-sd1 (80.9/71.6 vs PolyInter's 81.7/69.7), but PolyInter still leads at IoU 0.5. The key extensibility advantage is that PolyInter trains only a specific prompt (~1.4% of parameters) per new agent, while PnPDA and MPDA require full retraining of their interpreter and fusion networks for each new neighbor type.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyInter extends the interpreter-based approach to immutable heterogeneous collaborative perception established by MPDA (one-stage, per-pair interpreters) and PnPDA (two-stage, standard semantic space). It departs from both by introducing a polymorphic design inspired by visual prompt tuning: a single shared interpreter network is paired with per-agent learnable prompts, so new agents integrate by fine-tuning only their specific prompt rather than training a new interpreter or routing through an intermediate semantic space. The channel selection module draws on cross-feature attention (going beyond single-feature channel attention in prior work), and the low-rank decomposition of prompts borrows the parameter-efficiency idea from LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important constraint in deployed autonomous driving systems—perception networks are fixed and cannot be retrained—while showing that a single interpreter with tiny per-agent prompts (as few as 0.04M parameters, ~1.4% of the full interpreter) can outperform methods that train entire per-pair adapters. This suggests a scalable path toward open heterogeneous collaboration where the ego agent stores one network plus a growing set of small prompts. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to truly unseen encoder architectures (not just different configurations of known families), whether the adversarial training for shared-semantics extraction remains stable with many agent types, and whether the two-phase protocol can be replaced by a fully online adaptation scheme.
