## Problem

Fully quantizing Winograd convolution for large-scale diffusion models suffers severe quality degradation because the Winograd output transformation exhibits large dynamic range differences across pixels that group-wise quantization cannot handle. Existing remedies require domain-specific training data for QAT or finetuning of Winograd transformation matrices, which is impractical and risks overfitting for foundation models that must generalize across diverse downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252123/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained diffusion model checkpoints (InstaFlow-0.9B, Stable Diffusion V1.5 at 512×512 resolution) and ResNet classifiers, requiring no calibration or training data. For scale learning, the only input is random Gaussian or uniform noise fed through paired standard and Winograd convolution layers; the output is a single shared set of diagonal scaling matrices S_B and S_G applied to all convolution layers. At inference, text prompts produce generated images (evaluated via FID and CLIP scores on MS-COCO 2017), and RGB images produce ImageNet class labels.

## Method

The paper applies group-wise quantization (hardware-vectorization-friendly group sizes) to every stage of the Winograd pipeline—input transform, Hadamard product, and output transform—achieving full 8-bit integer arithmetic. The key insight is that the large dynamic range imbalance in the Winograd output Y stems from the row norms of the Vandermonde-derived transformation matrices, which are controlled by diagonal scaling matrices S_B and S_G (with S_A = (S_B·S_G)^{-1}). Rather than finetuning with domain data, the authors optimize these scale matrices via SGD using only random noise inputs, minimizing the reconstruction error between standard convolution outputs and fully quantized Winograd outputs (Algorithm 1). A single set of learned scales is shared across all convolution layers to preserve generalizability. Highly optimized CPU kernels exploit the group-wise structure for vectorized integer matrix multiplication, yielding a 31.3% speedup over standard convolution layers on Arm Graviton3.

## Results

On ImageNet classification with Winograd F(6,3) and W8A8 quantization, the proposed method achieves 68.51% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-18 and 72.28% on ResNet-34, recovering most of the full-precision gap (69.76% and 73.30% respectively). For text-to-image generation on MS-COCO 2017, the 8-bit fully-quantized Winograd diffusion model with learned scales achieves FID 26.58 and CLIP 29.65 on InstaFlow-0.9B (vs. FP16 FID 23.00, CLIP 30.19), and FID 20.52 / CLIP 31.53 on Stable Diffusion V1.5 (vs. FP16 FID 21.72 / CLIP 31.72), demonstrating near-lossless quality. Optimized group-wise quantized Winograd kernels deliver a 31.3% speedup in convolution layer runtime and 12.8% end-to-end speedup on Arm Graviton3 CPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior quantized Winograd methods: BQW (Channel Balancing, CVPR 2022) and PAW+FSQ (NeurIPS 2023). On the headline Winograd F(6,3) setting, the proposed data-free learned-scales method outperforms PAW+FSQ by 1.62% on ResNet-18 (68.51% vs. 66.89%) and by 2.56% on ResNet-34 (72.28% vs. 69.72%), and also beats BQW by 2.43% and 1.41% respectively. On ResNet-50 F(6,3), the method slightly trails both baselines (75.11% vs. BQW 75.79% and PAW+FSQ 75.36%), suggesting the advantage diminishes for deeper networks. A critical practical distinction is that PAW+FSQ requires domain-specific finetuning data while BQW requires channel balancing calibration; the proposed method needs no training or calibration data at all, making it directly applicable to foundation models where such data is unavailable or would risk overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Winograd fast convolution (Lavin & Gray, 2016), group-wise quantization popularized for LLMs (Dai et al., 2021), and the Vandermonde-based numerical stability analysis of Winograd matrices (Bohan et al., 2017). It departs from prior quantized-Winograd work (BQW, PAW+FSQ) by eliminating the need for domain-specific calibration or finetuning data entirely, instead learning only the diagonal scale factors of the transformation matrices via random noise. The key conceptual departure is reframing the Winograd quantization error problem as a dynamic-range equalization problem solvable by a small set of scale parameters, rather than requiring full matrix retraining or QAT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical path to deploying large diffusion models on CPU-constrained edge devices by combining near-lossless 8-bit quantization with a 31.3% convolution speedup, all without access to training data—a critical requirement for foundation models where overfitting to small calibration sets is a real risk. The data-free guarantee and the use of a single shared scale set across all layers make the method broadly applicable across architectures and downstream tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even larger Winograd tile sizes (F(8,3) and beyond), whether it generalizes to other fast convolution formulations (e.g., FFT-based), and whether the random-noise finetuning objective can be further simplified or made more theoretically grounded.
