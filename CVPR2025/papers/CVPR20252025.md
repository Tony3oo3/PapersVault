## Problem

Existing RoPE methods rely on manually designed 2D rotation matrices that limit expressiveness in high-dimensional spaces and lack trainability. Previous attempts to generalize the rotation group (e.g., LieRE) do not consistently satisfy the relative-position-dependency property, causing performance degradation under coordinate offsets. No prior work formally characterizes the conditions under which trainable rotation matrices preserve the RoPE invariance to position shifts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 224×224 (training) and evaluates at resolutions from 112×112 to 512×512 for ImageNet-1K classification, or variable-resolution images for MS COCO detection. Unlike discrete token positions in NLP, image patch coordinates are treated as continuous values scaled to [0,1] relative to the canvas dimensions, and a Gaussian perturbation is sampled around each patch center during training to improve scale robustness. Outputs are standard class labels (1000-way) or bounding boxes with confidence scores.

## Method

ComRoPE parameterizes the RoPE rotation matrix as R(x; A) = exp(Σ A_i x_i) where {A_i} are trainable skew-symmetric matrices, and proves that pairwise commutativity of these matrices is both necessary and sufficient for the RoPE Equation (i.e., R(x)ᵀR(y) = R(y−x)) to hold. Two sufficient constructions are provided: ComRoPE-AP assigns each spatial axis its own block of trainable parameters within a block-diagonal structure (block size 8), while ComRoPE-LD trains a single base matrix P and scales it per-axis by learned factors θ_i, guaranteeing commutativity via linear dependence. The block-diagonal design extends the rotation from 2D planes to a larger subgroup of SO(d), increasing degrees of freedom. During training, patch coordinates are perturbed with a Gaussian distribution to enhance robustness across scales, and relative coordinate scaling ensures consistent positional relationships across different patch sizes.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 2D classification (ViT-B/16, trained at 224×224), ComRoPE-LD achieves 65.49% top-1 accuracy at training resolution and 55.29% at 512×512, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art LieRE (64.36% and 53.74%) by 1.1% and 1.6% respectively. At the highest evaluation resolution (512×512), ComRoPE-LD outperforms LieRE by 2.9% when comparing the no-perturbation setting (53.64% vs 52.51%). On MS COCO object detection (ViT-S backbone), ComRoPE-LD achieves 44.7 AP, edging out LieRE (44.5 AP) and APE (44.0 AP) while using nearly half the extra parameters of LieRE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComRoPE-LD consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation resolution on ImageNet-1K. Against APE (Vaswani et al., 2017), ComRoPE-LD gains approximately 6.7% at training resolution (65.49% vs 58.76%) and 4.3% at 512×512 (55.29% vs 50.99%), confirming the well-known limitations of absolute positional encoding for resolution extrapolation. Against vanilla RoPE (Su et al., 2024), ComRoPE-LD improves by 2.4% at 224 and 4.2% at 512, demonstrating that trainable angle matrices substantially enhance the expressiveness of the rotation group beyond the fixed 2D rotation design. Against the closest competitor LieRE (Ostmeier et al., 2024), ComRoPE-LD leads by 1.1% at training resolution and 1.6% at 512×512 (perturbation=1), with the gap widening to 2.9% in the no-perturbation setting at 512. The paper attributes this advantage to the enforced pairwise commutativity of angle matrices, which LieRE does not guarantee; an ablation showing that adding a random coordinate offset to LieRE causes significant performance degradation while ComRoPE remains invariant further validates this mechanism. On MS COCO object detection, ComRoPE-LD achieves 44.7 AP vs. 44.5 for LieRE and 44.0 for APE, with faster convergence (29% fewer iterations to match APE's final performance).

## Relation to Prior Work

ComRoPE extends the RoPE lineage initiated by RoFormer [27] and the trainable-matrix generalization of LieRE [20] by identifying a previously unenforced algebraic requirement: pairwise commutativity of the angle matrices. While LieRE directly trains skew-symmetric matrices without this constraint, ComRoPE proves that commutativity is both necessary and sufficient for the rotation matrix to consistently encode relative positions, and provides two constructive families (axial-partition and linearly-dependent) that guarantee it. The framework also subsumes standard RoPE (block size 2) and vanilla attention (zero matrices) as special cases, unifying several existing formulations under a single theoretical umbrella.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal RoPE Equation and the commutativity theorem give the field a clean, verifiable criterion for designing positional encodings that are robust to coordinate offsets — a property that was previously only informally assumed. The 2.9% gap at 512×512 over LieRE, while modest, demonstrates that the theoretical fix translates into measurable scalability gains. Open questions include whether the block-size trade-off (performance vs. O(Lndb²) cost) can be mitigated, whether the framework extends cleanly to very long NLP sequences or 3D+ data, and whether richer sufficient conditions beyond the two presented (zero-matrix or linearly-dependent) could unlock further expressiveness without sacrificing commutativity.
