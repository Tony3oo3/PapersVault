## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image models like FLUX and SD3 can render sentence-level visual text but fail at the far more demanding task of article-level visual text rendering in infographics and slides, where hundreds of characters across dozens of text layers must be spelled accurately within ultra-dense layouts. This failure stems from two compounding challenges: context lengths exceeding 10× those of standard image generation (500+ words vs. <50), and the severe scarcity of high-quality, high-resolution business content training data.

## Input / Output

The system takes an article-level descriptive prompt (typically 500+ words), an ultra-dense layout specifying tens to hundreds of bounding boxes for individual visual elements, and region-wise prompts (shorter, sentence-level descriptions per sub-region) as input, and produces a 2240×896 high-resolution infographic or multi-page slide image. The resolution is nearly twice the conventional 1024×1024 used by existing text-to-image systems, which is critical for rendering small visual text legibly. During inference, the long article-level prompt is decomposed into manageable region-wise prompts, and the layout provides explicit spatial constraints for each element's placement.

## Method

BIZGEN introduces a layout-guided cross-attention mechanism that decomposes the long-context modeling problem by cropping the latent feature map into region-wise groups according to the layout prior, then performing cross-attention only between each group of vision tokens and its corresponding short text prompt (≤100 tokens), reducing effective context length by ~10×. Non-text layers use CLIP text encoders while text layers use Glyph-ByT5, with a lightweight mapper aligning Glyph-ByT5 embeddings to the SDXL latent space. The model is fine-tuned via LoRA on Glyph-SDXLv2 with a hybrid loss that applies a higher weight β to visual text regions. A layout-conditional CFG (LCFG) strategy is applied during inference, using a dense 2D guidance scale map with per-layer CFG values to selectively refine artifacts in specific sub-regions. The training data (INFOGRAPHICS-650K) is generated by a scalable retrieval-augmented pipeline that uses LayerDiffuse to create transparent layers, GPT-4o for layer selection, and CLIP-similarity-based retrieval to swap layers across infographics.

## Results

On BIZEVAL, BIZGEN achieves 92.98% visual text spelling precision on infographics with ≤10 layers, substantially outperforming FLUX (41.33%), SD3 Large (30.92%), and DALL-E3 (16.42%). For the most challenging setting (≥20 layers), BIZGEN reaches 55.48% spelling precision, a >25% absolute gain over FLUX (27.12%). On GPT-4o prompt-following scores for infographics, BIZGEN scores 8.09/10 versus 4.11 for FLUX and 3.88 for SD3 Large, while maintaining competitive aesthetics (7.74 vs. 6.20 for FLUX). In a user study with 10 design-background evaluators, BIZGEN was preferred over FLUX in 84.3% of cases on typography quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIZGEN is compared against three SOTA text-to-image models (FLUX, SD3 Large, DALL-E3) and its own unmodified base model (Glyph-SDXL-v2) on the BIZEVAL benchmark. Against FLUX, BIZGEN wins decisively on visual text spelling precision across all infographic layer counts (e.g., 92.98% vs. 41.33% at ≤10 layers; 55.48% vs. 27.12% at ≥20 layers) and on GPT-4o prompt-following (8.09 vs. 4.11), while also leading on aesthetics (7.74 vs. 6.20). Against SD3 Large, the margin is even larger on spelling (up to 72.46 points at ≤10 layers) and prompt following (8.09 vs. 3.88). Against DALL-E3, BIZGEN dominates on all metrics. The one area where BIZGEN does not clearly lead is slide aesthetics (7.64 vs. 8.07 for DALL-E3), though it still wins on slide prompt following (7.61 vs. 6.80) and style consistency (8.37 vs. 8.23). Compared to its own base Glyph-SDXL-v2, BIZGEN gains 14.8 points on ≤10-layer infographics and 21.0 points on ≥20-layer infographics, confirming the contribution of the layout-guided cross-attention and layout-conditional CFG.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIZGEN builds directly on the Glyph-ByT5/Glyph-SDXL line of work, which introduced a byte-level text encoder and region-wise cross-attention to improve visual text rendering in diffusion models. The key architectural departure is replacing the text-only region-wise cross-attention with a layout-guided cross-attention that decomposes an ultra-dense layout into per-element bounding boxes, routing each region's visual tokens to its corresponding short prompt (via CLIP for non-text layers, Glyph-ByT5 for text layers), thereby reducing effective context length by ~10×. The data engine leverages LayerDiffuse for transparent layer generation and a retrieval-augmented pipeline (GPT-4o layer selection + CLIP similarity search) to synthesize INFOGRAPHICS-650K, addressing the data scarcity that prior layout-guided generation works (GLIGEN, LayoutDiffusion, etc.) sidestepped by annotating existing images. A layout-conditional CFG at inference provides per-layer guidance strength, a finer-grained control mechanism not present in prior CFG formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: current SOTA text-to-image models (FLUX, SD3, DALL-E3) produce unusable infographics and slides due to spelling errors and layout breakdowns at article-level text density. The INFOGRAPHICS-650K dataset (650K high-res multilingual samples with ultra-dense layouts and region-wise captions) and the BIZEVAL benchmark are valuable community resources that were previously unavailable. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to the FLUX backbone (authors speculate this would yield stronger results), whether the layout-guided cross-attention generalizes to other dense-composition tasks beyond infographics/slides, and how the system handles layouts with even more than ~30 layers where performance still degrades notably.
