## Problem

Medical image segmentation models struggle with 'fuzzy regions'—lesion areas with unclear boundaries, irregular shapes, and minimal tissue density contrast—where noisy labels misguide optimization and feature representations become unstable. Existing training paradigms treat all samples equally, allowing noisy labels to accumulate errors and preventing the model from learning discriminative features in these ambiguous regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251297/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard medical images (CT slices at 224×224, dermoscopic images, colonoscopy frames, abdominal CT slices) and produces pixel-level binary or multi-class segmentation masks of identical spatial dimensions. Internally, each image is partitioned into 16×16 patches for the soft-thresholding step, and the method operates as a model-agnostic training wrapper: it modifies how any given segmentation backbone (CNN, Transformer, Mamba, or multimodal) is trained rather than altering its architecture.

## Method

DALE is a data-driven alternating learning paradigm that first trains on a reliable non-fuzzy R-set (identified via average entropy and edge-pixel-ratio soft thresholding) to establish stable feature priors, then trains on a noisy fuzzy R-set using a composite loss. Two key mechanisms operate during fuzzy-set training: (1) a loss-consistency-based bi-level optimization (inspired by meta-learning) learns per-pixel label confidence parameters ω that dynamically down-weight noisy labels, with a theoretical proof showing ω increases for clean samples and decreases for mislabeled ones; (2) an unstable representation calibration step estimates per-class multivariate Gaussian distributions from both non-fuzzy and denoised fuzzy features, then minimizes the Wasserstein distance between them to restore discriminative structure in fuzzy-region representations. The fuzzy loss is L_f = ω*(L + α·L_W), combining the standard segmentation loss with the distribution alignment term, and the entire procedure is model-agnostic, plugging into any segmentation backbone without architectural modification.

## Results

On the ISIC2016&ph2 dataset, DALE (applied to XboundFormer) achieves Dice 0.936, mIoU 0.884, 95HD 3.224, and ASD 0.595, outperforming all competing methods. On MosMedData+, DALE reaches Dice 0.709, mIoU 0.572, 95HD 20.534, and ASD 4.674. Across all model backbones tested, DALE yields an average Dice improvement of 5.33% on MosMedData+, 2.37% on ISIC2016&ph2, and 7.16% on the Polyp benchmark, with the largest gains observed on EGEUNET (+8.58%) and EMCAD (+8.05%) in the polyp task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DALE outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest competitor CIRL [6], DALE improves Dice from 0.928 to 0.936 and mIoU from 0.870 to 0.884 on ISIC2016&ph2, while reducing 95HD by 21.3% (4.097→3.224) and ASD by 20.67% (0.750→0.595), indicating substantially better boundary precision in fuzzy regions. On MosMedData+, DALE beats CIRL by 0.3% Dice and 1.2% mIoU, with 95HD dropping from 22.349 to 20.534. BECO [24] and GCE [41] both underperform the unmodified XboundFormer baseline on ISIC2016&ph2 (Dice 0.881/0.882 vs. 0.917), suggesting their noisy-label strategies are counterproductive for this task. MW-Net [28] achieves competitive Dice (0.919) but lags DALE by 1.7% and has notably worse boundary metrics (95HD 5.470 vs. 3.224). The paper attributes BECO's degradation to its replacement of all fuzzy-region samples with pseudo-labels, which corrupts originally clean annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DALE synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads—noisy-label robust learning (co-teaching, label refurbishing, robust regularization), meta-learning-based bi-level optimization for adaptive sample weighting, and optimal-transport-based distribution alignment—into a unified training paradigm for medical image segmentation. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes the curriculum-learning intuition (learn easy regions before hard ones) by explicitly partitioning data into non-fuzzy and fuzzy region sets via an entropy-and-edge-ratio soft threshold, then alternating optimization between them. The confidence-parameter optimization via loss consistency draws on meta pseudo-labels and meta soft-label correctors, while the Wasserstein-distance-based representation calibration borrows from distribution-shift correction in long-tailed and noisy-label classification, but applies it to the spatial feature maps of a segmentation network rather than to class-level statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic nature of DALE (validated across CNN, CNN-Transformer hybrid, Mamba, and text-image multimodal backbones) makes it a practical drop-in training strategy for any medical segmentation pipeline, and the 7.16% average Dice gain on polyp benchmarks plus 21.3% 95HD reduction over the closest competitor (CIRL) demonstrate tangible benefit for boundary-sensitive clinical tasks. A key limitation is the doubled per-iteration cost from alternating non-fuzzy/fuzzy optimization, though the paper shows total wall-clock time is reduced due to faster convergence. Open questions include whether the AE&ER soft-threshold partitioning generalizes to 3D volumetric data or to tasks where 'fuzziness' is not well captured by pixel entropy and edge ratio, and whether the Gaussian-distribution assumption for feature representations holds under more extreme label noise or domain shift.
