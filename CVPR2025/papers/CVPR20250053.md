## Problem

Existing ANN-to-SNN conversion methods that achieve high accuracy require retraining or fine-tuning the source ANN (e.g., quantized training, calibration), incurring substantial GPU-based computational overhead that scales with inference time-steps. This makes it impractical to rapidly deploy SNNs from open-source pre-trained models, especially for large architectures on resource-constrained or neuromorphic hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250053/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained ANN checkpoint (e.g., ResNet-34, MobileNetV2, RetinaNet, DeepLabV3) and a small calibration subset of the original training data (batch size 100, a few thousand iterations of forward passes only). It produces a fully converted SNN with IF neurons using reset-by-subtraction, where all ReLU activations are replaced by spiking dynamics and thresholds are absorbed into weights. No gradient backpropagation or weight updates occur; the only learned parameters are per-channel (convolutional) or per-element (fully connected) firing thresholds.

## Method

The framework derives a layer-wise upper bound on conversion error (Theorem 1) that decomposes it into intra-layer clipping error and inter-layer propagation error weighted by spectral norms of downstream weights. To minimize this bound, a greedy layer-by-layer local threshold balancing algorithm updates each layer's threshold via a simple Heaviside-gated gradient step on a clipping-function surrogate, requiring only forward passes at inference-scale cost. For convolutional layers, thresholds are shared per channel and absorbed into kernel weights; for fully connected layers, they are per-element. A pre-neuron max pooling layer (justified by Theorem 2, which proves max-pooling and ReLU commute) handles architectures with max pooling without retraining. Finally, a delayed evaluation strategy estimates the first-spike arrival time t̃₀ across layers and averages output spikes only over [t̃₀, T], mitigating the initial firing-rate fluctuation caused by inter-layer propagation delays.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-34 (post-training, no retraining), the converted SNN achieves 71.17% top-1 accuracy at T=64 and 72.46% at T≥512, compared to the ANN's 73.31%. When applied to a QCFS pre-trained model (Ours*), the method reaches 71.94% at just T=32 and 73.54% at T≥512. The converted SNN achieves 622 FPS/W energy efficiency versus 22 FPS/W for the original ANN (28× improvement). On downstream tasks, the method achieves 53.50% mIoU (FCN-34, Pascal VOC, T=64), 27.3% mAP (RetinaNet, MS COCO, T=64), and 61.60% accuracy (MC3-18, Kinetics-400, T=64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the post-training baseline LCP [36], the proposed method (ResNet-34) achieves 71.17% at T=64 versus LCP's 67.56%, a 3.6-point gain, though LCP (using a modified ResNet-34 with extra layers) reaches 75.44% at T≥512 vs. Ours' 72.46%. Compared to the training-involved QCFS [5], the pure post-training Ours method trails slightly at high time-steps (72.46% vs. 73.76% at T≥512), but when the proposed conversion is applied to a QCFS pre-trained model (Ours*), it dramatically outperforms vanilla QCFS at low latency: 71.94% vs. 64.43% at T=32 (7.5-point gain) and 73.06% vs. 71.51% at T=64. Against ACP [37], Ours* achieves 71.94% at T=32 versus ACP's 64.65%, a 7.3-point advantage at the lowest latency, though ACP (with its modified architecture) still leads at T≥512 (75.45% vs. 73.54%). The RMP baseline [21] is substantially outperformed, with Ours reaching 71.17% at T=64 while RMP only reports 55.65% at T=256. Overall, the method's key advantage is achieving near-ANN accuracy at very low time-steps without any GPU-based retraining, making it uniquely suited for on-chip deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN-to-SNN conversion paradigm established by Cao et al. (2015) and refined by Diehl et al. (2015) and Rueckauer et al. (2017), which map pre-trained ANN activations to SNN firing rates. It departs from prior conversion methods (QCFS, LCP, ACP) that require quantized retraining or calibration by deriving a theoretical error bound (Theorem 1) and optimizing it via a greedy, layer-wise local threshold balancing rule that operates at inference-scale computational cost. The delayed evaluation strategy builds on Hwang et al. (2021), and the channel-wise threshold mechanism follows Li et al. (2021), but the key conceptual shift is eliminating all backpropagation-based optimization from the conversion pipeline entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling direct, GPU-free conversion of any open-source pre-trained ANN into a deployable SNN, which is critical for on-chip neuromorphic deployment where retraining infrastructure is unavailable. The reported 28× energy efficiency gain (622 FPS/W vs. 22 FPS/W for the ANN) underscores the low-power advantage for edge and embedded applications. However, the authors acknowledge a small but consistent performance gap versus training-involved methods (e.g., QCFS), and the framework is currently limited to convolutional architectures, leaving extension to transformers and other complex layers as an open question. The plug-and-play compatibility with existing conversion pipelines (LCP, ACP, QCFS) suggests the local threshold balancing component could be adopted broadly as a lightweight add-on.
