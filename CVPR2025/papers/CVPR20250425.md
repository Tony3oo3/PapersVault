## Problem

Current image-to-video generation models struggle to produce consistent, faithful object motion, particularly in multi-object scenarios where interactions and individual trajectories must be captured simultaneously. Existing two-stage approaches that use optical flow as an intermediate representation are overly restrictive at the pixel level, lack semantic information, and propagate prediction errors into the final video. The paper asks whether a more expressive yet compact intermediate representation can better guide multi-object motion generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (first frame) and a free-form text prompt as input. An LLM decomposes the prompt into a global motion-specific prompt and per-object local prompts, while Grounding DINO and SAM2 extract initial bounding boxes and segmentation masks. The output is a photorealistic video of up to 128 frames, with the intermediate product being a time-consistent sequence of per-object semantic masks (the mask-based motion trajectory) that encodes both object identity and spatial displacement over time.

## Method

THROUGH-THE-MASK factorizes I2V into two independently trained latent diffusion stages: an Image-to-Motion model that generates mask-based motion trajectories conditioned on the reference image, its segmentation, and a motion prompt, and a Motion-to-Video model that synthesizes the final video conditioned on the reference image, generated trajectory, and text. The key architectural contribution is a pair of masked attention blocks inserted into the first K transformer blocks: a masked cross-attention that restricts each latent position to attend only to the prompt tokens of the object present at that spatial location, and a masked self-attention that restricts each position to attend only to positions belonging to the same object across frames, enforcing per-object temporal consistency. Both stages are initialized from pre-trained text-to-video models (U-Net from AnimateDiff or DiT from Movie Gen) and fine-tuned with the mask trajectory concatenated along the channel dimension. The mask-based trajectory is preferred over optical flow because it operates at the object level, captures semantics alongside motion, and allows the second stage to autonomously refine fine details rather than being constrained to exact pixel correspondences.

## Results

On the ImageAnimation-Bench, THROUGH-THE-MASK (UNet) achieves the lowest FVD of 196.23 and highest ViCLIP-V of 0.865, outperforming all baselines. On the newly introduced SA-V-128 benchmark, the UNet variant achieves FVD of 925.39 (single-object) and 1089.86 (multi-object), substantially below the best baseline Motion-I2V (1195.08 and 1162.06 respectively). The DiT variant also leads on FVD (192.45 on ImageAnimation-Bench; 1216.83/1134.71 on SA-V-128) and achieves the highest ViCLIP-T scores (0.226 single-object, 0.225 multi-object). Human evaluation confirms preference for THROUGH-THE-MASK over all baselines on motion consistency and quality, with a near-tie on text alignment (50.8% vs 49.2% for TI2V).

## Baselines & Comparisons

THROUGH-THE-MASK outperforms all four external baselines on FVD across both benchmarks: on ImageAnimation-Bench, the UNet variant achieves 196.23 FVD versus 266.83 (VideoCrafter), 217.40 (DynamiCrafter), 286.42 (Motion-I2V), and 283.59 (ConsistI2V). On the SA-V-128 multi-object setting, the gap is most pronounced against VideoCrafter (1089.86 vs 1413.83 FVD) and DynamiCrafter (1089.86 vs 1300.07), while the closest competitor is Motion-I2V (1089.86 vs 1162.06). In terms of image faithfulness (ViCLIP-V), THROUGH-THE-MASK leads all baselines by a wide margin (0.865 vs 0.810 for VideoCrafter, 0.746 for Motion-I2V on ImageAnimation-Bench). The closest competitive comparison is with the internal TI2V baseline (same architecture without masked attention), where THROUGH-THE-MASK wins on FVD and ViCLIP-V but TI2V edges ahead on human-rated text alignment (50.8% vs 49.2%), though automatic ViCLIP-T still favors THROUGH-THE-MASK. Motion-I2V, the most relevant prior two-stage method using optical flow, is consistently outperformed, supporting the paper's argument that mask-based trajectories are a superior intermediate representation over pixel-level flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage I2V paradigm introduced by Motion-I2V (Shi et al., 2024), which uses optical flow as an intermediate representation, by replacing it with mask-based motion trajectories that capture both semantics and object-level motion. The masked cross-attention mechanism is adapted from Nie et al. (2024) for text-to-image generation, and a novel masked self-attention objective is introduced for temporal consistency in video. The denoising backbone follows the LDM formulation (Rombach et al., 2022), with architecture variants based on AnimateDiff's U-Net and Movie Gen's DiT, making the approach architecture-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness in I2V generation—consistent multi-object motion—by decomposing the problem into explicit motion planning and video synthesis, which is a practical and interpretable design choice. The introduction of SA-V-128, a benchmark explicitly separating single- and multi-object scenarios, fills a gap in evaluation that prior benchmarks lacked. However, the pipeline's reliance on multiple external tools (LLM for prompt extraction, Grounding DINO for detection, SAM2 for segmentation) introduces fragility and computational overhead that limits real-time applicability. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to long videos, heavily occluded scenes, and object types not well-represented in the segmentation models used during pre-processing.
