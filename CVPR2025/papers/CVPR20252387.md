## Problem

Previous state-of-the-art 3D plane reconstruction methods were trained and evaluated on a single indoor or outdoor dataset, leaving cross-domain generalization unexplored. No unified framework existed for zero-shot plane detection and reconstruction from a single image across diverse environments, and the scarcity of high-quality outdoor plane annotations further hindered multi-domain training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252387/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces per-plane 3D geometry (unit normal vector and scalar offset from camera), a 2D segmentation mask, and a classification score for each detected plane. The planar depth map is then analytically recovered from the predicted normal, offset, and camera intrinsics. Unlike prior work that operates at 256×192, the authors generate and train on high-resolution (640×480) dense plane annotations, and the model is shown to be adaptable to different input resolutions at inference.

## Method

ZeroPlane is a query-based Transformer detector built on the Mask2Former/PlaneRecTR architecture, using a pretrained DINOv2 ViT encoder with a DPT-style pixel decoder. The key architectural insight is disentangling the coupled n/d plane representation into separate normal and offset heads, each learned via a classification-then-regression scheme: K-Means clustering over the mixed training set yields exemplar centers (7 for normals, 20 for offsets), and the network first classifies a plane into an exemplar bin then regresses a small residual. A pixel-geometry-enhanced embedding module projects auxiliary pixel-level depth and normal predictions into the embedding space and performs cross-attention with plane queries, injecting low-level geometric cues into the instance-wise plane embeddings. The model is trained end-to-end with bipartite matching and a multi-task loss combining classification, mask, geometry, and auxiliary depth/normal terms across 14 datasets and 560K images spanning indoor and outdoor domains.

## Results

On zero-shot evaluation across four unseen datasets, ZeroPlane (DINO-B, mixed-trained) achieves plane recall at 0.1m depth of 17.86% on NYUv2 and 17.19% on 7-Scenes (indoor), and 44.51% at 3m on ParallelDomain and 40.44% at 3m on ApolloScape (outdoor). For normal accuracy at 5°, it reaches 37.29% on NYUv2, 23.96% on 7-Scenes, 53.23% on ParallelDomain, and 27.56% on ApolloScape. On the outdoor ParallelDomain benchmark, ZeroPlane substantially outperforms the mixed-trained PlaneRecTR baseline, improving depth recall at 1m from 19.11% to 25.96% and normal recall at 5° from 24.17% to 53.23%. The DINO-L and Dust3R backbone variants further improve depth recall on NYUv2 to 20.29% and 21.2% at 0.1m respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroPlane (DINO-B, mixed-trained) consistently outperforms the strongest baseline PlaneRecTR (mixed-trained) across all four zero-shot evaluation datasets. On NYUv2, it improves depth recall at 0.1m from 14.29% to 17.86% and normal recall at 5° from 24.97% to 37.29%. On 7-Scenes, the gains are even more pronounced: depth recall at 0.05m jumps from 3.20% to 8.19% and normal recall at 5° from 11.17% to 23.96%. The largest advantage appears on the outdoor ParallelDomain benchmark, where normal recall at 5° more than doubles from 24.17% to 53.23%, and depth recall at 1m improves from 19.11% to 25.96%. On ApolloScape, ZeroPlane wins on normal accuracy (27.56% vs. 22.99% at 5°) and segmentation (VOI 0.30 vs. 0.38), though depth recall at 3m is slightly lower (40.44% vs. 43.06%). Against the single-dataset baselines (PlaneRCNN, PlaneAE, PlaneTR), ZeroPlane achieves substantially higher scores on all metrics, confirming that the mixed-domain training and disentangled classification-then-regression paradigm are the key drivers of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based plane detection lineage (Mask2Former, PlaneTR, PlaneRecTR) by introducing cross-domain, zero-shot 3D plane reconstruction from a single image. It departs from prior single-dataset methods in two key ways: (1) disentangling the coupled normal/offset representation into separate classification-then-regression heads guided by K-Means exemplars, and (2) incorporating pixel-level depth and normal predictions as geometry-enhanced attention inputs to plane queries. The multi-dataset training strategy is motivated by the zero-shot depth estimation literature (MiDaS, Depth Anything, ZeroDepth), which demonstrated that large-scale mixed-source training yields superior generalization, but this principle had not been applied to plane reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 560K-image, 14-dataset planar benchmark is a significant practical resource that was previously absent for outdoor scenes, enabling the first systematic study of cross-domain plane reconstruction. A key limitation is the reliance on synthetic outdoor data (Synthia, Virtual KITTI, Sanpo) for training, since real outdoor dense depth is unavailable, which may cap generalization to truly in-the-wild outdoor scenes. The authors identify leveraging foundation models (SAM, DepthAnything) on unlabeled data as a promising path forward. The finding that mixed-domain training matches or exceeds single-domain performance on both indoor and outdoor test sets suggests the disentangled representation and exemplar-guided learning effectively bridge domain gaps, opening the door to unified geometry models for robotics and AR across environments.
