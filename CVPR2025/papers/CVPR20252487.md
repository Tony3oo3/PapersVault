## Problem

Masked Image Modeling (MIM) faces significant optimization challenges during early training, where models must learn complex mappings from partial observations to the full natural image distribution before developing basic visual processing capabilities. This paper hypothesizes that the order in which training examples are presented is a critical but underexplored factor, and that standard uniform sampling is suboptimal for the early stages of MIM training.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K, which are divided into non-overlapping patches with 75% randomly masked. Prototype identification uses DINO ViT-Base/16 features (or alternatively SIFT, untrained ViT, or MAE features) to compute K-means cluster centroids in a 768-dimensional embedding space. The output is a pre-trained ViT-Base/16 encoder whose weights are evaluated via linear probing, nearest-neighbor classification, and full fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K and eight fine-grained datasets under 5-shot settings.

## Method

The core contribution is a temperature-controlled sampling curriculum for MAE training. First, K-means clustering (with cluster count selected via the Davies-Bouldin index, yielding ~978 clusters) is performed in DINO feature space to identify prototypical images near each centroid. During training, each image is assigned a normalized distance to its nearest centroid, and sampling probabilities follow a softmax over these distances with a temperature parameter τ. The temperature is annealed from 0.07 to 0.6 via a cosine schedule on the effective dataset size |D_τ|, which is solved for τ by binary search. This causes early epochs to concentrate sampling on prototypical (easy-to-reconstruct) examples, gradually expanding to cover the full distribution. The approach requires no changes to the MAE architecture or reconstruction objective—only the data sampling distribution is modified.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ViT-B/16 trained for 800 epochs, the prototype-driven curriculum achieves 47.40% nearest-neighbor (NN) accuracy, 68.84% linear probing (LP), and 83.31% fine-tuning (FT) accuracy, substantially outperforming the standard MAE baseline (28.73% NN, 64.25% LP, 83.08% FT). The efficiency gain is striking: at just 200 epochs, the curriculum method already reaches 34.92% NN and 63.74% LP, surpassing the 800-epoch MAE baseline on NN. In 5-shot linear probing across eight fine-grained datasets, the 400-epoch curriculum model achieves 47.23% average accuracy, exceeding the 1600-epoch baseline (41.77%), demonstrating up to 8× training speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: standard MAE with uniform sampling [17] and a hard-sample curriculum adapted from [40] that prioritizes non-prototypical images distant from cluster centroids. The proposed prototypical curriculum decisively outperforms both on all three metrics. Against the MAE baseline, it gains +18.67 points on NN (47.40 vs 28.73), +4.59 on LP (68.84 vs 64.25), and +0.23 on FT (83.31 vs 83.08). Against the hard-sample curriculum, the advantage is even larger: +22.77 on NN (47.40 vs 24.63), +6.75 on LP (68.84 vs 62.09), and +0.36 on FT (83.31 vs 82.95). Notably, the hard-sample strategy—which preserves non-prototypical examples for supervised dataset distillation [40]—is actively harmful for self-supervised MIM, confirming that early exposure to prototypical (easy-to-reconstruct) examples is critical for stable optimization in the MIM setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Masked Autoencoders (MAE) [17], proposing a curriculum learning strategy that restructures the training data distribution over time rather than modifying the architecture or objective. It departs from the dominant paradigm in supervised data selection, where prototypical (easy) samples are typically down-weighted or removed, by arguing that these same samples are crucial for stabilizing early MIM optimization. The temperature-based sampling mechanism draws on importance sampling and curriculum learning literature, while the prototype identification via K-means clustering in DINO feature space connects to self-supervised representation learning. The work also contrasts with prior MIM improvements that focus on masking strategies or auxiliary objectives, instead targeting the data ordering problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 8× training speedup (matching 800-epoch MAE performance in ~100 epochs) is practically significant as computational costs for pre-training large vision models continue to grow. The conceptual finding that prototypical examples—typically discarded in supervised data pruning—actually accelerate self-supervised MIM training challenges a working assumption in the data selection literature. However, validation is limited to ImageNet-1K with ViT-Base/Small backbones, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger datasets (e.g., JFT-300M), other MIM variants (SimMIM, video MIM), or different masking ratios. The simplicity of the method (K-means + cosine temperature schedule) makes it easy to adopt, but the optimal hyperparameters (cluster count, temperature range) may require dataset-specific tuning.
