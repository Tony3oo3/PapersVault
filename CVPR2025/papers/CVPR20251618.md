## Problem

In 3D Gaussian Splatting, each Gaussian can only represent a single color (modulated by a Gaussian falloff) and a simple ellipsoidal shape, severely limiting the set of appearances and geometric structures a single primitive can model. This causes 3DGS to fail at reconstructing high-frequency textures and complex shapes, especially when the number of Gaussians is constrained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251618/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and produces a 3D scene representation where each Gaussian carries an additional fixed-resolution 2D texture map of size T×T×K (K∈{1,3,4} for alpha, RGB, or RGBA channels) alongside standard 3DGS parameters (position, rotation, scale, opacity, SH coefficients). Rendering outputs novel-view images via differentiable volume compositing, with the texture maps providing spatially varying color and opacity within each Gaussian's extent.

## Method

Each 3D Gaussian is augmented with a per-Gaussian 2D texture map projected onto a plane defined by the Gaussian's two major axes (the plane normal aligns with the smallest-scale axis). During rendering, a ray from the camera to each pixel is intersected with this plane to obtain a 3D point, which is then mapped to UV coordinates on the texture map via bilinear interpolation. The final per-Gaussian color is the sum of a spatially constant SH-based base color and the spatially varying texture color, while the alpha is the product of the texture alpha, the 3D Gaussian value at the intersection point, and the per-Gaussian opacity. Training proceeds in two stages: first optimizing a vanilla 3DGS model for 30k iterations, then jointly optimizing all Gaussian parameters and texture maps for another 30k iterations with adaptive density control disabled. Custom CUDA kernels implement the ray-Gaussian intersection, UV mapping, and alpha compositing for efficient differentiable rendering.

## Results

With the default number of Gaussians, Textured Gaussians (RGBA) achieves 33.24/0.9674/0.0428 (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) on Blender and 27.35/0.8274/0.1858 on Mip-NeRF 360, marginally outperforming 3DGS* (33.08/0.9671/0.0440 and 27.26/0.8318/0.1871). The advantage grows substantially with fewer Gaussians: at 10% of the default count, the method gains ~0.7 dB PSNR on Blender (32.14 vs 31.47) and ~1.6 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (26.32 vs 25.77). At just 1% of Gaussians, the improvement reaches nearly 2 dB on Blender (28.11 vs 26.89) and 1.6 dB on DTU (32.43 vs 30.88), with LPIPS dropping from 0.1165 to 0.0849 on Blender.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS* (the primary baseline), Textured Gaussians with RGBA textures achieves strictly better PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on all five datasets at the default Gaussian count, though margins are small (e.g., +0.16 dB PSNR on Blender). The gap widens dramatically at reduced Gaussian budgets: at 10% Gaussians the method wins on every metric across all datasets (e.g., +0.67 dB PSNR and −0.0105 LPIPS on Blender), and at 1% Gaussians the advantage reaches +1.22 dB PSNR on Blender, +1.55 dB on Mip-NeRF 360, and +1.55 dB on DTU, with LPIPS improvements of 0.03–0.13. Against Mip-NeRF 360 [4], the method outperforms on PSNR for Blender (+2.90 dB), DTU (−0.67 dB, a loss), Tanks & Temples (+2.04 dB), and Deep Blending (−1.07 dB, a loss), while Mip-NeRF 360 retains a small PSNR edge on its own dataset. Against Instant-NGP [39], Textured Gaussians wins on PSNR across all five datasets (e.g., +1.04 dB on Blender, +2.05 dB on Mip-NeRF 360). Against Xu et al. [63] (Texture-GS), the method achieves +4.27 dB PSNR on Blender and +3.58 dB on DTU, though Xu et al. only reported results on these two datasets due to limitations with complex geometry and scene-level captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by Kerbl et al. and the ray-based formulation from Gaussian Opacity Fields by augmenting each Gaussian with a local 2D texture map (alpha, RGB, or RGBA). It departs from prior texture-augmented Gaussian approaches: Huang and Gong's Textured-GS only achieved smooth low-frequency color variation via modified viewing directions, while Xu et al. relied on a global unit-sphere UV mapping that limited them to simple object geometry. The key conceptual departure is using per-Gaussian local texture maps (rather than a shared global map) combined with an alpha channel for shape representation, enabling complex geometry and high-frequency textures without the constraints of prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that alpha-only textures outperform RGB textures at the same model size is practically significant: it suggests that spatially varying opacity (enabling non-ellipsoidal shapes and frequency-selective alpha compositing) is a more efficient use of the model budget than adding color channels. This provides a clear design principle for future Gaussian-based representations. However, the method is limited to 2D diffuse textures on a local plane, does not model spatially varying specular reflections, and has not been extended to dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether 3D volume textures or factorized high-dimensional representations (e.g., triplanes, TensoRF) could capture more complex appearance, and whether the two-stage optimization (3DGS pretraining then texture refinement) is necessary or if end-to-end training with better initialization could match it.
