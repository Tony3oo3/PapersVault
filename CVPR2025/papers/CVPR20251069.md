## Problem

Estimating global 3D motion (joint rotations and root trajectories in world coordinates) from 2D observations typically requires 3D ground truth for training, which is unavailable for out-of-distribution scenarios like complex athletic movements, animal motion, or human-object interactions. Prior methods that avoid 3D supervision, such as MAS, generate 3D poses with a fixed root joint and do not recover realistic root trajectories, leaving a gap in producing full global 3D motion from 2D poses alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251069/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view 2D pose sequence of shape T×J×2 (extracted from monocular videos via ViTPose or UniPose) as input, with no RGB data required. For the interaction task, the input additionally includes 2D object keypoints. The output is a full global 3D motion: SMPL (or SMAL) joint rotations plus a root trajectory in the world coordinate system. As an intermediate, the framework generates multi-view 2D pose sequences across a circular camera arrangement (6 views at 60° intervals for optimization, 4 views at 90° intervals for final generation).

## Method

MVLift is a four-stage progressive framework that builds multi-view consistency without any 3D supervision. Stage 1 trains a transformer-based line-conditioned 2D motion diffusion model to generate poses respecting epipolar line constraints (simulated via random virtual epipoles during training). Stage 2 jointly optimizes 2D sequences across all six views using Score Distillation Sampling from the Stage-1 model plus a pairwise multi-view consistency loss over all 15 view pairs. Stage 3 recovers 3D motion from the roughly consistent multi-view sequences via reprojection optimization and VPoser fitting, then reprojects the fitted 3D motions to create a synthetic dataset of strictly consistent multi-view 2D sequences. Stage 4 trains a multi-view 2D motion diffusion model with cross-view attention layers on this synthetic data, enabling single-pass generation of consistent multi-view 2D sequences that are then lifted to 3D.

## Results

On AIST++ (with 3D ground truth), MVLift achieves T_root=67.6, MPJPE=110.7, PA-MPJPE=79.2, J2D=14.0, and FID=1.9, outperforming all baselines on root trajectory and reprojected 2D metrics while matching or exceeding 3D-supervised methods on joint position errors. On out-of-distribution datasets without 3D ground truth, MVLift achieves J2D=11.7 on Steezy (vs. 22.8 for SMPLify, 60.8 for WHAM) and J2D=26.2 on NicoleMove (vs. 45.1 for SMPLify, 69.4 for WHAM). On the animal dataset CatPlay, MVLift achieves J2D=57.0 and FID=8.5, outperforming SMPLify (J2D=58.8, FID=12.4) and MAS (J2D=180.6, FID=22.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVLift outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across all four evaluated datasets. Against the 3D-supervised methods, MVLift beats MotionBERT on T_root (67.6 vs. 101.6), MPJPE (110.7 vs. 134.0), and J2D (14.0 vs. 59.6) on AIST++, and substantially outperforms WHAM on T_root (67.6 vs. 164.3) and J2D (14.0 vs. 94.3), though WHAM retains a slight edge on MPJPE (104.8 vs. 110.7) and PA-MPJPE (75.1 vs. 79.2). Among 2D-only methods, MVLift dominates: it achieves J2D=11.7 on Steezy versus 22.8 (SMPLify), 60.8 (WHAM), 106.4 (MAS), and 132.7 (ElePose), and J2D=26.2 on NicoleMove versus 45.1 (SMPLify) and 69.4 (WHAM). On the animal dataset CatPlay, MVLift narrowly beats SMPLify on J2D (57.0 vs. 58.8) and J2C2D (39.4 vs. 42.0) while significantly improving FID (8.5 vs. 12.4), and dramatically outperforms MAS (J2D=180.6, FID=22.0). The paper also reports that in a human perceptual study, 39% of participants preferred MVLift's AIST++ results over ground truth, and 11% found no noticeable difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVLift extends the 2D-to-3D motion lifting paradigm by combining two previously separate threads: (1) 2D motion diffusion models (MAS [14], which generates 3D motion from 2D diffusion but ignores root trajectories) and (2) Score Distillation Sampling from DreamFusion [38], adapted here to enforce motion realism during multi-view optimization. The key conceptual departure is reformulating global 3D motion estimation as progressive multi-view 2D motion generation, drawing inspiration from multi-view image synthesis (Zero-1-to-3, SyncDreamer) but operating on pose sequences rather than pixels. Unlike prior lifting methods (MotionBERT, WHAM) that require paired 3D ground truth, or single-frame approaches (ElePose) that lack temporal and translational modeling, MVLift uses a four-stage pipeline to establish multi-view consistency purely from single-view 2D data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for 3D motion capture supervision, MVLift opens the door to 3D motion estimation for domains where mocap is impractical or impossible (animal motion, complex athletic movements, human-object interactions), and it outperforms 3D-supervised baselines on out-of-distribution data. The progressive multi-stage strategy (pairwise epipolar constraints → global SDS optimization → synthetic data distillation → learned multi-view generation) provides a reusable recipe for establishing geometric consistency without ground truth. Open questions include whether the multi-stage pipeline can be collapsed into a single end-to-end model, how the approach scales to longer sequences or more complex scenes with occlusions, and whether the synthetic data bottleneck in Stage 3 limits fidelity for highly dynamic motions.
