## Problem

Existing self-supervised methods for medical imaging rely on contrastive or pixel-level reconstruction objectives that do not explicitly encode the multi-scale anatomical and domain knowledge radiologists use in practice. The world modeling paradigm—predicting unobserved outcomes from observed context—offers a principled way to capture this knowledge, but no prior work has applied it to radiographic representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252758/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 frontal chest X-ray images and produces patch-level feature embeddings from a ViT-Base encoder. During pre-training, the input is consumed in a JEPA-style manner: a masked and augmented crop serves as context, and the model predicts the target's representation in latent space (not pixel space) conditioned on spatial or domain-transition latent variables. Downstream, the frozen or fine-tuned encoder outputs are fed to task-specific heads (linear classifiers or U-Net decoders) for classification, segmentation, or few-shot learning.

## Method

CheXWorld builds on the JEPA architecture (context encoder, EMA target encoder, and a predictor operating in representation space) and unifies three world modeling tasks in a single forward pass. (1) Local anatomical structure modeling masks four random rectangular regions and predicts their latent features from surrounding context; (2) global anatomical layout modeling predicts the features of a spatially displaced crop given only the proportional relative displacement Δ between the two crops; (3) domain variation modeling predicts the un-augmented target's features from an augmented context, conditioned on the augmentation parameter vector a (Gaussian blur, brightness, contrast, gamma) via a lightweight policy network that modulates mask tokens. The unified pipeline samples two target crops, applies distinct augmentation-and-mask configurations to each to form two contexts, and computes four cross-prediction losses (2×2), so each context simultaneously performs local+domain or global+domain modeling. This yields equivariant representations—input transformations produce predictable, structured changes in embedding space—rather than the invariant representations learned by contrastive methods, preserving more information for downstream transfer.

## Results

On five chest X-ray classification benchmarks, CheXWorld (ViT-B, 0.5M X-rays) achieves AUROC/accuracy of 95.24 on VinDr-CXR, 98.88 on ShenZhen, 83.58 on ChestX-ray14, 75.03 on RSNA Pneumonia, and 89.63 on CheXpert, setting new state-of-the-art on all five. On segmentation, it reaches 84.58 Dice on SIIM-ACR pneumothorax and 79.02 on RSNA segmentation. In few-shot learning on MedFMC-ChestDR, CheXWorld attains 64.60 AUROC at 1-shot, 75.19 at 5-shot, and 76.40 at 10-shot, outperforming all baselines in every setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CheXWorld outperforms all directly comparable baselines on every classification benchmark. Against the strongest same-data baseline MAE (0.5M X-rays, ViT-B), CheXWorld gains +2.48 AUROC on VinDr-CXR (95.24 vs 92.76), +1.25 on ShenZhen (98.88 vs 97.63), and +1.28 on RSNA (75.03 vs 73.75). Against SimMIM and Adam-v2, which use 0.9M X-rays plus ImageNet-21k pre-training and different backbones (Swin-B, ConvNeXt-B), CheXWorld still leads by +2.43 AUROC on VinDr-CXR (vs Adam-v2) and +0.54 on ChestX-ray14 (vs SimMIM). The closest competitor, Rad-DINO, requires 20× more GPU-hours and private data; CheXWorld matches or exceeds it on 4 of 5 benchmarks (e.g., +0.68 on RSNA, +0.69 on CheXpert) while using only 0.5M public X-rays. On segmentation, CheXWorld leads Adam-v2 by +0.98 Dice on SIIM-ACR and +0.49 on RSNA. In few-shot learning, CheXWorld's 1-shot advantage over the next-best MAE is +3.29 AUROC (64.60 vs 61.31), highlighting the sample-efficiency benefit of world-modeling pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CheXWorld builds directly on the JEPA (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture) framework of Assran et al. (CVPR 2023), adopting its EMA target encoder, latent-space prediction objective, and ViT-based predictor. It departs from the dominant masked-image-modeling paradigm (MAE, SimMIM) by predicting in an abstract representation space rather than reconstructing pixels, and from contrastive medical SSL methods (Adam, DINO-based approaches) by constructing equivariant rather than invariant representations. The key conceptual departure is framing three distinct medical-knowledge dimensions—local anatomy, global layout, and domain variation—as unified world-modeling tasks within a single forward pass, a formulation not present in prior medical SSL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first application of the world-modeling philosophy to medical imaging, offering a principled way to encode anatomical and domain knowledge without explicit annotations. The data-efficiency result (10% training data outperforming all baselines trained on 100%) is practically significant for settings where labeled radiographs are scarce. Open questions include whether the three-task formulation generalizes to 3D volumetric imaging or other modalities, whether the equivariant representation advantage persists at larger model scales, and whether the domain-variation task can be extended to handle genuinely unseen acquisition protocols rather than simulated augmentations.
