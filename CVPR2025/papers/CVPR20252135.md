## Problem

Tuning-free image editing with flow transformers (e.g., Stable Diffusion 3.5 / MM-DiT) faces two unresolved challenges: the Euler inversion used in rectified flow suffers from accumulated approximation error that degrades editing fidelity far more than DDIM inversion in diffusion models, and existing attention-based invariance control mechanisms cannot simultaneously preserve non-target content and allow rigid/non-rigid edits (layout, pose, quantity) in the MM-DiT architecture, which lacks the cross-attention pathway available in U-Net.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original RGB image, a 'before' text prompt describing the original scene, and an 'after' text prompt describing the desired edit, and produces a single edited RGB image. It is entirely tuning-free (no fine-tuning or additional training), operating on Stable Diffusion 3.5 with 30 inversion steps, classifier-free guidance of 1 during inversion and 2 during editing, and 3 fixed-point iterations per timestep. The only tunable hyperparameter is the AdaLN replacement timestep S, which the authors show is insensitive to the specific editing type.

## Method

The framework has two core components. First, a two-stage flow inversion: Stage I applies fixed-point iteration (Eq. 10) at each reverse ODE step to refine the velocity estimate v_θ(x_t, t) rather than using the approximated v_θ(x_{t+1}, t), with averaging over iterations to stabilize the trajectory near the authentic generation path; Stage II adds a per-timestep velocity compensation ε_t during the forward editing pass so the regenerated image exactly matches the original. Second, an AdaLN-based invariance control: at each MM-DiT block, the text features M^a in the adaptive layer normalization are selectively replaced with the corresponding features M^b from the original prompt for tokens that remain unchanged between before and after prompts, while edited-token features are left intact. This token-aware replacement (Eq. 13) is applied only up to timestep S and directly ties preserved image content to specific text semantics, enabling both rigid edits (object replacement) and non-rigid edits (pose, quantity, expression) without per-type hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On the PIE benchmark (700 images), the proposed method achieves the best CLIP similarity scores for both whole-image (25.74) and edited-region (22.27) alignment, as well as the highest SSIM (91.50) among all methods. P2P leads on several background-preservation metrics (Distance 13.44, LPIPS 60.67, MSE 35.86), but the proposed method is competitive on those (Distance 18.17, LPIPS 80.55, MSE 40.24) while substantially outperforming P2P on editing fidelity (CLIP Edited 22.27 vs. 21.86) and structural similarity (SSIM 91.50 vs. 84.11). The method thus reconciles the typical trade-off between invariance preservation and editing ability that plagues attention-injection baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on CLIP Whole (25.74) and CLIP Edited (22.27), confirming superior text-to-image alignment after editing. Against P2P, which leads on Distance (13.44 vs. 18.17), LPIPS (60.67 vs. 80.55), and MSE (35.86 vs. 40.24), the proposed method trades a modest increase in pixel-level background distance for a large gain in SSIM (91.50 vs. 84.11) and editing fidelity, indicating that P2P's stronger background lock comes at the cost of reduced editability. MasaCtrl and InfEdit, which inject KV attention in later steps to accommodate non-rigid changes, show similar background-preservation scores to PnP but lower CLIP Edited scores (21.35 and 21.86), confirming their difficulty with large geometric edits. The instruction-based InsP2P performs worst on background preservation (Distance 57.91, MSE 227.78) due to imperfect training triplets, while the flow-based RFinv, though architecturally closest, lags significantly on both preservation (SSIM 73.26) and editing (CLIP Edited 21.89). Overall, the proposed two-stage inversion plus AdaLN invariance control achieves the best balance, winning on the editing metrics and SSIM while remaining competitive on pixel-level preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tuning-free image editing paradigm (P2P, PnP, MasaCtrl, InfEdit) from U-Net diffusion models with DDIM sampling to flow transformers (MM-DiT) with Euler-based rectified flow. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it identifies that Euler inversion for rectified flow, despite being structurally analogous to DDIM inversion, suffers significantly more from velocity approximation error, necessitating a two-stage fixed-point + compensation strategy rather than direct porting of diffusion inversion techniques; and (2) it replaces the dominant attention-injection mechanism for invariance control with text-feature replacement within AdaLN, motivated by the architectural absence of cross-attention in MM-DiT. The closest prior work, RFinv [37], also targets flow-based editing but relies on stochastic differential equations and shows limited tradeoffs in reconciling rigid and non-rigid editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As flow transformers (SD3.5, Flux) become the dominant architecture for text-to-image generation, having a principled tuning-free editing pipeline for them is practically important for both research and deployment. The AdaLN-based invariance control offers a unified mechanism that handles diverse editing types (quantity, pose, visual text, expression) without per-type hyperparameter tuning, which is a meaningful simplification over attention-injection methods. However, the approach is tightly coupled to MM-DiT's specific architecture and depends on the inversion trajectory staying close to the model's prior distribution, limiting its applicability to out-of-domain images. Open questions include whether the AdaLN insight generalizes to other transformer-based flow architectures and whether the fixed-point iteration overhead can be further reduced for real-time applications.
