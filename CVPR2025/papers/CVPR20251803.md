## Problem

Existing Video-LMMs align video features with language via single projection layers, enabling basic video comprehension but failing to produce fine-grained pixel-level grounding that ties specific textual phrases to spatio-temporal object regions. This limits their ability to localize objects, track them across frames, or generate visually grounded conversational responses in videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251803/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (T×H×W×C) and a natural language query as input. The video is consumed at two resolutions simultaneously: 336×336 frames for spatial encoding and 224×224 segment-wise clips for temporal encoding. The output is a grounded text response where specific phrases are wrapped in <p>...</p> tags followed by <SEG> tokens, each triggering a corresponding spatio-temporal segmentation mask that is consistent across frames. The pixel decoder receives 8 raw frames for temporal supervision during mask generation.

## Method

VideoGLaMM employs a dual spatio-temporal encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14 for local spatial features and InternVideo2 with segment-wise sampling for global temporal features), each connected to the LLM (Phi-3-Mini-3.8B) via separate V→L adapters (W_g, W_h) that project visual tokens into the LLM's embedding space alongside text tokens. The LLM, fine-tuned with LoRA and an expanded <SEG> vocabulary, generates grounded text; its last-layer embeddings at <SEG> positions are projected back to visual space via an L→V adapter (W_p) and fed as prompts to a SAM2-based pixel decoder (frame encoder + prompt encoder + mask decoder) that produces the final masks. Training is end-to-end in a single stage with a combined cross-entropy (text) and IoU (mask) loss, using a gradual schedule: first on image/video segmentation datasets (epochs 1–20), then on the newly curated GCG dataset of 38k grounded video-QA triplets with 671k masks (epochs 20–30). The key architectural distinction from prior grounded LMMs is the dual-encoder design with separate spatial and temporal alignment paths, enabling the model to capture both frame-level detail and segment-level temporal dynamics for accurate spatio-temporal grounding.

## Results

On the MeViS referring video segmentation benchmark, VideoGLaMM achieves J=42.07, F=48.23, and J&F=45.15, outperforming VideoLISA (J&F=44.40) and GLaMM+SAM2 (J&F=38.66). On the novel Grounded Conversation Generation task, VideoGLaMM reaches mIOU=62.34 and CLAIR=28.2, substantially surpassing PG-Video-LLaVA (mIOU=24.03, CLAIR=15.0) and GLaMM+SAM2 (mIOU=28.60, CLAIR=22.9). On Ref-DAVIS-17, VideoGLaMM attains J&F=69.5, edging out VideoLISA (68.8) and TrackGPT-13B (66.5). On VidSTG visual grounding, VideoGLaMM achieves mIOU=39.66, the highest among all compared models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoGLaMM consistently outperforms all baselines across all three evaluation tasks. Against PG-Video-LLaVA, the gains are most dramatic: mIOU improves from 24.03 to 62.34 on GCG and J&F from 18.87 to 45.15 on MeViS, reflecting the advantage of the dual spatio-temporal encoder and dedicated pixel decoder over a single-adapter design. Compared to GLaMM+SAM2 (which extends the image-based GLaMM with SAM2 for temporal processing), VideoGLaMM achieves roughly 2× mIOU on GCG (62.34 vs. 28.60) and a 6.5-point J&F improvement on MeViS (45.15 vs. 38.66), demonstrating that end-to-end spatio-temporal alignment surpasses modular composition. Against the strongest video-specific baseline VideoLISA, VideoGLaMM wins by a narrow but consistent margin: J&F 45.15 vs. 44.40 on MeViS and 69.5 vs. 68.8 on Ref-DAVIS-17, with the advantage growing after task-specific fine-tuning (epochs 30–40). On VidSTG visual grounding, VideoGLaMM leads GLaMM+SAM2 by 1.03 mIOU (39.66 vs. 38.63) and PG-Video-LLaVA-13B by 4.56 mIOU, confirming superior fine-grained spatial localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoGLaMM extends the grounded LMM line (GLaMM, LISA, Shikra) from static images to video by introducing a dual spatio-temporal encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14 for spatial, InternVideo2 for temporal) and end-to-end trainable V→L and L→V adapters, departing from prior video LMMs (VideoChat, Video-LLaVA, Video-GPT+) that use single projection layers and lack pixel-level grounding. It also builds on PG-Video-LLaVA and VideoLISA for video grounding, but unlike those, it jointly optimizes the LLM (via LoRA) and pixel decoder (SAM2) in a single end-to-end stage rather than combining frozen pre-trained modules. The paper further introduces a new grounded conversation generation (GCG) task and a 38k-triplet benchmark dataset, filling a gap where no prior instruction-tuning dataset associated fine-grained masks with video conversations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GCG benchmark (38k video-QA triplets, 83k objects, 671k masks) is a practical resource for the community studying pixel-level video grounding, and the unified model that handles GCG, visual grounding, and referring video segmentation in one architecture is a useful reference point. However, the authors note limitations: the dataset covers only short-to-medium clips, annotations retain some noise despite manual review, and the model struggles with objects of varying granularities. Open questions include extending to longer videos, improving annotation density, and integrating the grounded masks into downstream video generation/editing pipelines (a direction the authors briefly explore in the supplementary).
