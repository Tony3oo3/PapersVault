## Problem

Pre-trained VLA models exhibit strong generalization across tasks and environments but are restricted to fixed-base manipulation, unable to generate cooperative actions between a mobile base and robot arm. Conventional mobile manipulation methods rely on expensive expert demonstrations or modular navigation-manipulation pipelines, resulting in poor generalization. The paper addresses how to efficiently transfer fixed-base VLA policies to mobile manipulation while preserving their generalization ability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251687/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes RGB-D images, camera poses, proprioceptive states (base position/rotation in world frame, end-effector position/rotation/gripper openness in base frame), and a natural language instruction. The VLA model outputs end-effector waypoints in the base frame, which are transformed to the world frame via the current base pose. The final output is a temporally ordered sequence of whole-body poses—base translation and rotation in the world frame plus end-effector pose in the base frame—forming a physically feasible trajectory between consecutive VLA waypoints, with joint angles recovered via Pinocchio IK at each step.

## Method

MoManipVLA fine-tunes OpenVLA-7B with LoRA on 200 pick-and-place episodes to produce generalized end-effector waypoints, then solves a constrained trajectory optimization between consecutive waypoints using three physical-feasibility costs: reachability (penalizing IK iteration count beyond a budget), smoothness (L2 differences in joint angles and base pose between consecutive steps), and collision (ESDF-based distance to scene surfaces via nvblox). The key architectural contribution is a bi-level trajectory optimization: the upper level searches base poses via Dual Annealing to maximize the quality of the arm's search space (evaluated by expected and top-k objectives over sampled arm poses), while the lower level refines end-effector poses within that fixed base configuration to follow VLA waypoints. SLSQP refines the Dual Annealing solution at each level. This decomposition avoids the intractable 10-DoF joint search and reduces latency compared to direct optimization, requiring only 50 expert episodes for real-world deployment.

## Results

On the OVMM benchmark, MoManipVLA achieves 15.8% overall success rate and 49.4% partial success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline KUZHUM (11.6% overall, 38.2% partial) by 4.2% and 11.2% respectively. The method also leads on all partial success metrics: 66.1% FindObj, 62.6% Pick, and 53.1% FindRex. In real-world deployment, MoManipVLA requires only 50 fine-tuning episodes and achieves 40% success rate on the 'Put in Bowl' task with ~585 ms inference latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoManipVLA outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric in Table 1. Against the strongest competitor KUZHUM (11.6% overall SR), MoManipVLA gains 4.2 percentage points in overall success and 11.2 points in partial success, with particularly large margins on FindRex (53.1% vs. 35.2%) and Pick (62.6% vs. 50.2%). The OVMM baselines (RL and Heuristic) achieve only 1.2% and 0.8% overall success respectively, highlighting the difficulty of the task without VLA-guided waypoints. UniTeam (9.2% overall) and RoboAI (0.0% overall) are also clearly surpassed. The step count of MoManipVLA (1240.5) is slightly higher than the heuristic baseline (1009.8) but comparable to other learned methods, while the ablation in Table 2 shows the bi-level optimization reduces latency from 742.9 ms (direct search) to 693.1 ms, confirming the efficiency of the hierarchical decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: VLA models (OpenVLA, ManipLLM, π0) that achieve strong generalization in fixed-base manipulation, and mobile manipulation frameworks (Home-Robot/OVMM, OK-Robot, M2Diffuser) that require whole-body coordination but suffer from limited generalization due to expensive data collection. Rather than training a new end-to-end mobile manipulation policy, MoManipVLA repurposes a pre-trained fixed-base VLA model as a waypoint generator and couples it with a bi-level trajectory optimization framework (Dual Annealing + SLSQP) to produce physically feasible base-and-arm trajectories. This positions the work as a policy transfer/adaptation approach, drawing on trajectory optimization techniques from VoxPoser and ReKep for scene-aware feasibility constraints, while departing from prior mobile manipulation methods that either jointly learn navigation and manipulation or rely on modular pipelines with compounding errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that a pre-trained VLA model's generalization can be transferred to mobile manipulation with only 50 fine-tuning episodes, dramatically reducing the data collection burden compared to end-to-end imitation learning approaches that require thousands of demonstrations. However, real-world success rates remain modest (10–40%), and the method is constrained by the VLA model's fixed-base assumptions and cannot handle long-horizon tasks without an external task planner. Open questions include how to integrate foundation-model-based task planning, how to extend the framework to non-differential-drive platforms with additional motion constraints, and whether the bi-level decomposition generalizes to higher-DoF embodiments like bimanual or humanoid systems.
