## Problem

All-in-one unified vision-language models face fundamental scalability and performance conflicts: integrating diverse generative and understanding tasks in a single architecture leads to degraded per-task performance, limited support for heterogeneous input/output formats (images, videos, 3D), and prohibitive training costs (e.g., Omni-Gen requires 104×A800 GPUs across five training stages). This paper asks whether a lightweight MLLM can instead act as a universal task router, delegating specialized vision tasks to external expert models while handling comprehension tasks internally, without training heavy generative components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252263/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a natural-language instruction, optionally accompanied by an RGB image or video (encoded via SigLIP-384 at 384×384 resolution). For comprehension tasks (e.g., VQA), the MLLM produces a direct text response. For all other tasks, it outputs structured routing tokens of the form `<task_tag>refined_prompt</task_tag>` (e.g., `<image_gen>a chihuahua in a costume</image_gen>`), which are parsed to invoke the corresponding specialist model. In chain-of-action mode, up to five such token-prompt pairs are emitted sequentially within a single response, enabling multi-step workflows like pose-conditioned generation followed by image editing.

## Method

Olympus initializes from Mipha-3B (SigLIP-384 vision encoder + Phi-2 language model + two-layer MLP projector) and introduces 20 task-specific routing tokens as new vocabulary entries. The key training data is OlympusInstruct (446.3K instruction-response pairs) and OlympusBench (49.6K evaluation pairs), both synthesized by GPT-4o with controlled diversity in language style, tone, and instruction complexity (short/moderate/extended). The model is jointly fine-tuned on LLaVA-Mix665K and OlympusInstruct for 2 epochs using standard next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss, fully updating all components (vision encoder, language encoder, MLP) at a learning rate of 5e-5 on 64 V100 GPUs (~24.8 hours). At inference, the MLLM generates routing tokens that schedule external specialist models; for chain-of-action, multiple routing tokens are emitted in sequence, and the outputs of each specialist are aggregated into a final response. This modular design avoids training generative models entirely, adding only a 23.6% training cost over the base MLLM while supporting 20 tasks across image, video, and 3D domains.

## Results

On the task-routing benchmark (OlympusBench), Olympus achieves 94.75% accuracy and 95.77% F1 in single-task routing, and 91.82% precision with 0.18 edit distance in chain-of-action scenarios, substantially outperforming HuggingGPT with GPT-4o (81.35% accuracy, 75.03% precision). On standard multimodal understanding benchmarks, Olympus (3B params, 384px) achieves 80.5 on VQAv2, 63.9 on GQA, 1520.7 on MME-P, 71.2 on MMBench, and 86.6 on POPE, matching or exceeding Mipha-3B on 4 of 11 benchmarks while uniquely supporting routing to 20 external vision tasks. In human evaluation, Olympus achieves an 86.5% success rate versus 75.2% for HuggingGPT (GPT-4o).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest architectural baseline Mipha-3B (same SigLIP + Phi-2 backbone), Olympus wins on VisWiz (+2.5), MME-P (+31.8), MMBench (+1.5), and MM-Vet (+1.7), while showing slight drops on VQAv2 (−0.8), TextVQA (−3.2), and MME-C (−11.8), with near-ties on GQA, ScienceQA, POPE, and MMMU. Against HuggingGPT (GPT-4o) on the routing benchmark, Olympus achieves a 13.4-point accuracy gain (94.75% vs. 81.35%) and a 16.8-point precision gain in chain-of-action (91.82% vs. 75.03%), with edit distance reduced from 0.35 to 0.18. In human evaluation, Olympus's 86.5% success rate exceeds HuggingGPT (GPT-4o) by 11.3 points. Compared to larger 7B models (LLaVA-1.5, mPLUG-Owl2, Qwen-VL-Chat), Olympus is competitive on most benchmarks despite being roughly 2.5× smaller, and uniquely supports routing to 20 external specialist models. Among 3B-class models, Olympus achieves the best MMBench (71.2) and MME-P (1520.7) scores, trailing only Mipha-3B on VQAv2 (80.5 vs. 81.3) and TextVQA (53.4 vs. 56.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

Olympus is directly inspired by HuggingGPT's paradigm of using an LLM as a controller that delegates tasks to specialist models, but departs from it in three key ways: it trains the router from scratch (rather than relying on prompt engineering), it operates in the multimodal domain (images, video, 3D), and it introduces task-specific routing tokens that enable chain-of-action (sequencing up to five tasks in one instruction). Architecturally, it builds on Mipha-3B (SigLIP-384 + Phi-2 with a two-layer MLP projector) and is positioned as a modular alternative to all-in-one unified models like Emu3, Omni-Gen, and Show-o, which the authors argue suffer from task-objective conflicts and prohibitive training costs. The training data (OlympusInstruct) is generated by GPT-4o, making the approach data-centric rather than architecture-centric in its novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular routing framework offers a practical path to scaling vision-language systems to new tasks without retraining a monolithic model—adding a new specialist only requires new routing tokens and a small amount of instruction data. The 23.6% training cost increase for 20 tasks (vs. the 104×A800 GPUs and five training stages reported for Omni-Gen) makes this approach accessible. However, the reliance on GPT-4o for data generation introduces a circularity concern: biases and coverage gaps in GPT-4o propagate into the router, and the evaluation is conducted on the authors' own GPT-4o-generated benchmark. Open questions include how well the router generalizes to tasks or phrasings outside the GPT-4o distribution, whether the chain-of-action capability degrades beyond five tasks, and how to handle tasks for which no suitable specialist model yet exists.
