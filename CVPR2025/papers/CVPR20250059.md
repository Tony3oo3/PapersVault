## Problem

Video-LLMs trained on question-answer pairs achieve strong benchmark accuracy but lack explainability and spatial-temporal grounding, while agent-based systems that decompose questions into sub-tasks offer interpretability yet are too slow and memory-intensive for practical deployment. No prior work has bridged this gap by transferring the multi-step reasoning of agent pipelines into a single generative video-language model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250059/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip (32 uniformly sampled frames with normalized frame IDs for scale consistency), a natural-language question, and a task-specific suffix prompt that controls output format. Depending on the prompt, the model produces either a concise answer (e.g., a single letter for multiple-choice or a short phrase for open-ended) or a full step-by-step rationale that embeds spatial grounding (bounding boxes) and temporal grounding (frame-level windows) alongside the reasoning chain.

## Method

AoTD constructs high-quality Chain-of-Thoughts via a four-stage pipeline: (1) an LLM agent (DeepSeek-Coder) decomposes each VideoQA question into an executable Python program of sub-tasks; (2) specialized vision models (OWL-ViTv2 for detection, UniVTG for temporal grounding, LLaVA-NeXT-Video-DPO for QA) execute the sub-tasks sequentially, and their intermediate outputs form the raw execution trace; (3) a second LLM (LLaMA-3.1-8B) converts the trace into natural-language CoTs and applies a two-step verification—checking answer correctness and logical coherence—to filter out unreliable chains; (4) the surviving CoTs are distilled into LLaVA-NeXT-Video (7B) via instruction-tuning with a joint cross-entropy loss that equally weights the answer and the rationale (λ=1), enabling the single model to generate both concise answers and comprehensive spatial-temporal rationales without runtime tool calls.

## Results

On MC-VQA benchmarks, LLaVA-NeXT-Video-AoTD achieves 55.6% on MVBench, 45.0% on VideoMME, 74.3% on STAR, 77.6% on NExT-QA, 50.6% on Perception-Test, and 28.8% on VSIBench, outperforming the instruction-tuned baseline (LNV-Instruct) by 2.2, 1.8, 2.1, 0.5, 0.3, and 2.1 points respectively. On OE-VQA, AoTD reaches 53.9% Acc. on ANetQA and 60.9% Acc. on AGQA, surpassing the Instruct variant by 6.8 and 1.6 points. The distilled model also matches or exceeds specialized models on temporal grounding (IoU 21.7% vs. UniVTG's 22.8%) and spatial grounding (IoU 45.2% vs. OWL-ViTv2's 64.7%), confirming successful acquisition of spatial-temporal reasoning through CoT distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base LLaVA-NeXT-Video model, AoTD yields substantial gains: +21.9 points on STAR (74.3 vs. 52.4) and +16.0 on NExT-QA (77.6 vs. 61.6), demonstrating that CoT distillation is far more effective than the base model's zero-shot capability. Compared to VideoLLaMA2 (7B), AoTD wins on MVBench (55.6 vs. 53.4), STAR (74.3 vs. 58.5), NExT-QA (77.6 vs. 62.3), and Perception-Test (50.6 vs. 49.6), but trails slightly on VideoMME (45.0 vs. 45.1). Against VideoChat2 (7B), AoTD dominates across all shared benchmarks, with the largest margin on STAR (+15.3) and NExT-QA (+9.0). On OE-VQA, AoTD outperforms LLaMA-VID, Video-LLaVA, VideoChat2, and VideoLLaMA2 on ANetQA and AGQA, though it does not surpass the base LLaVA-NeXT-Video on ActivityNet-QA (53.2 vs. 54.3 Acc.) or certain Video-ChatGPT sub-scores, which the authors attribute to the base model's strong image-centric training. The method also transfers to LLaVA-OneVision, improving MVBench from 59.2 to 60.5 and STAR from 75.8 to 76.6, confirming cross-model applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual program distillation line (VPD [16], FACT [10]) from the image domain to video, and operationalizes the agent-based video reasoning paradigm (ViperGPT, Visual Programming) as an offline CoT generator rather than an inference-time system. It builds directly on STAR's [42] executable symbolic program framework for decomposing video questions into sub-tasks, but replaces STAR's limited scene-graph-based pipeline with a generalizable agent system that can produce CoTs for any VideoQA dataset. The key conceptual departure from prior agent-based VideoQA work is that the multi-model agent system is used only during data construction, and its reasoning traces are distilled into a single Video-LLM, eliminating the high inference cost and memory overhead of runtime agent orchestration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AoTD offers a practical path to making Video-LLMs interpretable without sacrificing inference speed, which is critical for real-world deployment in embodied AI and autonomous driving. However, the temporal grounding capability distilled into the model (IoU ~22%) remains close to the best specialized model (UniVTG at ~23%), suggesting that spatial-temporal reasoning is still a fundamental bottleneck that CoT distillation alone cannot fully resolve. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos or more deeply nested reasoning chains, and whether the reliance on off-the-shelf vision models (whose quality caps the CoT ceiling) will be a persistent limitation as VideoQA complexity grows.
