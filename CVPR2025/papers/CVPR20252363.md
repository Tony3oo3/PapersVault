## Problem

Semantic correspondence methods built on 2D pre-trained features (Stable Diffusion, DINOv2) lack 3D geometric understanding and degrade sharply under large viewpoint changes. Prior attempts to inject geometric awareness—direction-basis finetuning, spherical map back-projection, or ControlNet on posed videos—either require domain-specific supervision or handle only minor viewpoint shifts, leaving a gap for robust, generalizable correspondence under extreme viewpoint variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252363/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (source and target) containing objects from the same category; at inference, SAM2 segments the object region before processing. Internally, 3D pointmaps are predicted at 224×224 resolution and semantic feature maps (DINOv2/SD) at 840×840, with geometric KNN search (K=300) performed via KD-trees on the 3D pointmaps before upsampling candidates to the higher-resolution feature space for final cosine-similarity matching. The output is a set of pixel-level correspondences between source keypoints and target pixels.

## Method

The core pipeline fine-tunes DUSt3R on 5.1M synthetic cross-instance renderings from ShapeNet so that two distinct objects are reconstructed in a shared, orientation-aligned 3D coordinate system. A deformation head then predicts per-pixel 3D offsets that morph the target object's shape onto the source object's shape, trained with a novel label-free semantic supervision: DINOv2 features from same-view renderings of different objects generate sparse pseudo-correspondences (via local 3D KNN + cosine threshold), combined with dense Chamfer distance for geometric accuracy. At inference, coarse correspondences are found by 3D KNN (KD-tree) on the deformed pointmaps, and a sparse semantic refinement step computes cosine similarity only within the local KNN candidate set rather than over the full dense feature map, yielding up to 40× speedup over dense matching baselines while improving PCK@0.1 by 7.4 points over the prior state of the art.

## Results

On the SPair-71k rigid-body benchmark (PCK@0.1), the full method (Geo + Semantic with DINOv2 + Stable Diffusion) achieves 72.3%, outperforming the strongest baseline Telling Left from Right (64.9%) by 7.4 points. Even the geometry-only variant (69.6%) surpasses all prior methods by 4.7 points while running ~40× faster. Under extreme viewpoint changes (Table 3), the full method reaches 71.9% PCK@0.1, a 19.6-point gain over the best zero-shot baseline. At stricter thresholds, PCK@0.01 improves from 9.5 (DIFT) to 19.1, and PCK@0.05 from 48.4 (TLR) to 59.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the 2D-representation group: DIFT [50] (55.6% PCK@0.1) and SD+DINO [60] (58.0%) are beaten by 16.7 and 14.3 points respectively by the full method, with the geometry-only variant already exceeding them by 11.6 and 11.6 points. Against the geometry-injected group: Telling Left from Right [61] (64.9%) is the strongest zero-shot baseline and is surpassed by 7.4 points; Spherical Maps [34] (57.2%) and its SD variant (63.9%) are beaten by 15.1 and 8.4 points, though Spherical Maps is finetuned on SPair-71k training data while the proposed method is zero-shot. The advantage is most pronounced under extreme viewpoint changes (Table 3), where the full method gains 19.6 points over the best zero-shot baseline and 8.7 points over the in-domain finetuned Spherical Maps+SD. The geometry-only variant also runs ~40× faster than dense feature-matching baselines, making it the most efficient method in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes DUSt3R, originally designed for two-view 3D reconstruction of the same object, into a cross-instance category-level reconstruction framework that aligns distinct objects in a shared 3D coordinate space. It departs from the dominant paradigm of injecting geometric awareness into 2D semantic features (as in Telling Left from Right, Spherical Maps, and 3DiFfection) by inverting the pipeline: explicit 3D geometric reasoning via point-map reconstruction and deformation is performed first, and 2D semantic features (DINOv2, Stable Diffusion) are used only for sparse local refinement within KNN candidates. The deformation head trained with DINOv2-distilled pseudo-labels and Chamfer distance supervision is a novel architectural addition not present in prior DUSt3R-based correspondence work like MASt3R.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× runtime speedup (from dense feature-by-feature matching to sparse 3D KNN + local semantic refinement) makes semantic correspondence practical for real-time applications, and the zero-shot generalization from synthetic ShapeNet renderings to real SPair-71k images demonstrates that explicit 3D geometric reasoning can substitute for in-domain fine-tuning. However, the method is currently limited to rigid-body categories, depends on the quality of available 3D meshes (notably underperforming on buses due to simplistic ShapeNet geometry), and has not been validated on non-rigid objects or broader real-world scenes. Open questions include whether parametric models (SMPL, SMAL) can extend the pipeline to deformable categories and whether the approach generalizes beyond the 11 rigid-body SPair-71k categories to more diverse or articulated objects.
