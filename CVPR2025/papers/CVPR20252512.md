## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-modal brain tumor segmentation methods either project all modalities into a shared latent space without decomposing modality-shared from modality-specific information, or rely on balanced missing-rate assumptions that do not hold in clinical practice. Under imbalanced missing rates, the latent space becomes biased toward modalities with more abundant data, and no prior work constructs explicit bi-directional relationships among modalities to maintain invariance when specific sequences are absent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252512/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D multi-modal brain MRI volumes (T1, T2, T1ce, Flair) at 128×128×128 resolution where any subset of modalities may be missing, and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks for three tumor sub-regions: Whole Tumor (WT), Tumor Core (TC), and Enhancing Tumor (ET). KMD is a plug-and-play module inserted between the encoder and decoder of existing backbones (RFNet, mmFormer, M2FTrans, ShaSpec); it operates on the encoder's feature maps and outputs decomposed shared and specific features that are concatenated back as the decoder input, requiring no architectural modification to the backbone.

## Method

KMD leverages the Koopman Invariant Subspace to decompose each modality's encoded features into modality-shared and modality-specific components. After centralization and L2 spherization of encoder features, three losses are applied across all modality pairs: (1) a Koopman loss L_k that enforces bi-directional linear mappings (A₁x+b₁ and A₂x+b₂) to be mutually consistent via pseudoinverse constraints, (2) a cosine similarity loss L_c that aligns the extracted shared information across modalities, and (3) an orthogonality loss L_o that minimizes similarity between modality-specific residuals. The theoretical contribution (Propositions 2–3) proves that if all M modalities span a Koopman Invariant Subspace, the remaining modalities can reconstruct missing ones through the submatrix of the global Koopman operator, and the operator's effectiveness is preserved under imbalanced missing rates as long as the remaining observables span the same-dimensional subspace.

## Results

On the imbalanced missing rate setting (BraTS2020), KMD consistently outperforms both the raw baselines and the Passion-enhanced baselines across all three backbones (RFNet, mmFormer, M2FTrans) and all six missing-rate configurations. For example, with the (s,m,l) configuration, KMD achieves average Dice of 71.78 (RFNet), 76.59 (mmFormer), and 74.57 (M2FTrans), compared to 65.56/67.66/64.24 for baselines and 68.64/69.31/70.11 for Passion. On the balanced missing rate setting (BraTS2018), the most dramatic gains appear in the ET class: RFNet improves from 54.70 to 67.85 average Dice, mmFormer from 57.89 to 72.29, and M2FTrans from 65.49 to 70.64. In the single-modality comparison (Table 4), KMD with mmFormer achieves 71.74–78.38 Dice across modalities, far exceeding D²-Net (16.0–66.3) and Correlation-Fusion (5.3–56.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KMD outperforms all three backbone baselines (RFNet, mmFormer, M2FTrans) by 3–9 points in average Dice under imbalanced missing rates, with the largest gains in the ET class (e.g., +12.8 for RFNet under (s,m,l): 49.14→61.89). Against Passion, which re-balances gradient magnitudes, KMD still leads by 2–7 points in average Dice across all configurations, indicating that gradient re-weighting alone is insufficient without explicit modality relationship construction. On balanced missing rates (BraTS2018), KMD yields the most striking improvement for ET: RFNet 54.70→67.85, mmFormer 57.89→72.29, M2FTrans 65.49→70.64, while WT scores remain comparable (within ±1 point). In the single-modality stress test (Table 4), KMD with mmFormer achieves 71.74–78.38 Dice, dwarfing D²-Net (16.0–66.3) and Correlation-Fusion (5.3–56.1), confirming that the Koopman-based decomposition generalizes far better when only one modality is available. The one notable exception is the (m,l,s) configuration for mmFormer, where KMD's WT score (79.59) drops below the baseline (84.10), though TC and ET still improve substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Koopman Invariant Subspace framework from dynamical systems theory (Takeishi et al., 2017) and repurposes it for multi-modal medical image feature decomposition. It departs from shared-latent-space approaches (RFNet, mmFormer, M2FTrans) that project all modalities into a common space without separating shared from specific information, and from decomposition methods (RobustMseg, ShaSpec) that require complete modality inputs. Unlike Passion, which re-balances convergence rates for imbalanced missing rates but does not construct inter-modality relationships, KMD uses the Koopman operator's linearity to enforce bi-directional mappings between modalities, yielding a principled decomposition into modality-common and modality-specific components. The module is designed as a plug-and-play add-on to existing backbones rather than a standalone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and theoretical guarantee (Proposition 3) that imbalanced missing rates do not degrade the Koopman representation make KMD broadly applicable to any multi-modal segmentation backbone, addressing a practical clinical need where modality availability is inherently uneven. However, the approach is validated only on brain tumor MRI with four fixed modalities, and the core assumption—that all modalities span a Koopman Invariant Subspace—has not been independently verified in practice. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other imaging tasks with different modality counts, whether the orthogonality constraint on modality-specific features is optimal, and whether the Koopman-based decomposition can be extended to handle more than pairwise modality relationships without combinatorial loss growth.
