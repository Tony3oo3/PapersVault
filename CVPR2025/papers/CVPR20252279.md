## Problem

NeRF-based Earth observation methods like EO-NeRF achieve state-of-the-art digital surface modeling from multi-date satellite images but require day-long optimization, making them impractical for the rapidly growing volume of satellite data. 3D Gaussian Splatting offers 100×+ faster training and rendering with comparable accuracy, but has not been adapted to handle the specific challenges of remote sensing: pushbroom sensor geometry, multi-date radiometric variation, and physically consistent shadow modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes N (10–20) non-orthorectified WorldView-3 image crops (~256×256 m² each at 30–50 cm/pixel) along with their bundled-adjusted RPC coefficients and per-image sun direction, and produces a set of 3D Gaussian primitives from which both a digital surface model (via an elevation render that composites real altitudes in meters) and an albedo map (via a shadow-free color render) can be extracted. Each AOI is normalized into a unit cube in an east-north-up world coordinate system, and the full world-to-pixel transformation is approximated per-scene as an affine map with ~0.012 pixel mean error.

## Method

EOGS adapts 3DGS to satellite photogrammetry through three key mechanisms. First, it replaces the pinhole camera model with a per-scene affine approximation of the full UTM→LLA→RPC→NDC pipeline, which simplifies the splatting Jacobian and eliminates the need for local first-order projection approximations. Second, it introduces a shadow-mapping pipeline: a sun-aligned affine camera renders an elevation map, which is resampled into the satellite view and compared against the satellite elevation to produce a binary shadow mask with exponential attenuation (physically motivated by homogeneous medium absorption). Third, it adds three regularizers absent from standard 3DGS—an L1 opacity sparsity term with thresholding pruning (yielding up to 2× speedup), a local view-consistency loss enforcing color and altitude agreement between a real camera and a randomly perturbed virtual camera, and an entropy penalty on the shadow map that discourages semi-transparent shadows and promotes fully opaque geometry. Training is self-supervised over 5000 iterations, minimizing photometric loss plus these regularizers, with camera-specific affine color correction and ambient light learned per view.

## Results

On the DFC2019 (JAX) and IARPA2016 datasets, EOGS achieves a mean absolute elevation error of 1.46 m (JAX) and 1.62 m (IARPA) without masking, and 1.19 m / 1.37 m with a foliage mask, all in just 3 minutes of training. This is approximately 300× faster than EO-NeRF (15 hours) while remaining Pareto-optimal in the MAE-vs-training-time trade-off. With the foliage mask, EOGS matches or slightly exceeds EO-NeRF's accuracy (1.19 m vs. 1.21 m on JAX), demonstrating that the speedup comes at negligible cost for structural geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EOGS is compared against four baselines: EO-NeRF (NeRF-based, 15 h), SAT-NGP (Neural Graphics Primitives, 25 min), Sat-Mesh (neural implicit surfaces, 8 min), and S2P (stereo pipeline, 20 min). Against EO-NeRF, EOGS is slightly worse without masking (1.46 m vs. 1.35 m on JAX) but matches or edges it out with the foliage mask (1.19 m vs. 1.21 m on JAX), while training 300× faster. Against SAT-NGP, EOGS wins on both datasets in both settings (e.g., 1.46 m vs. 1.72 m on JAX no-mask) and trains 8× faster. Against S2P, EOGS achieves lower MAE on both JAX (1.46 m vs. 1.53 m) and IARPA (1.62 m vs. 1.78 m) while training 6.7× faster. Sat-Mesh is only reported on JAX (1.52 m) and is outperformed by EOGS (1.46 m) with a 2.7× speed advantage. Overall, EOGS is Pareto-optimal: no other method simultaneously achieves lower MAE and shorter training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

EOGS is the first method to adapt 3D Gaussian Splatting to the satellite photogrammetry domain, repurposing a framework originally designed for close-range pinhole-camera scenes to handle pushbroom satellite sensors modeled by RPC coefficients. It inherits the remote-sensing formulation from EO-NeRF (radiometric correction, explicit shadow modeling, multi-date handling) but replaces EO-NeRF's ray-marching-based shadow casting with a shadow-mapping technique borrowed from classic 3D graphics, which is naturally compatible with the splatting rasterization pipeline. Three new regularizers (L1 opacity sparsity, local view consistency, and shadow entropy) address the under-constrained optimization that arises from the sparse, multi-date nature of satellite observations, a problem less acute in the dense-view 3DGS setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~300× speedup (minutes vs. hours) while matching EO-NeRF's accuracy makes high-fidelity digital surface modeling practical for the rapidly growing volume of multi-date satellite imagery, positioning EOGS as a viable near-term tool for large-scale Earth observation. The affine camera approximation (introducing only ~0.012 px mean error) is a clean simplification that eliminates the per-Gaussian Jacobian computation of standard 3DGS, and the shadow-mapping formulation avoids the ray-marching that is incompatible with splatting. Open questions include handling low-visibility regions where EOGS underperforms EO-NeRF, incorporating transient objects, and whether the fixed Lagrangian coefficients generalize beyond the tested WorldView-3 scenes.
