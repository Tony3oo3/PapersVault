## Problem

State-of-the-art rectified flow text-to-image models (SD3, Flux, AuraFlow) frequently produce misspelled, missing, or hallucinated text in generated images, and existing remedies require expensive fine-tuning on curated text data that can degrade overall image quality. This paper asks whether a training-free, sampling-time intervention can correct the compounding discretization errors of the Euler sampler specifically in text regions without additional training or computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250852/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt as input and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image with rendered text, operating entirely at inference time on top of pretrained RF models (SD3-medium, Flux-dev, AuraFlow) without modifying their weights. The attention modulation step requires access to cross-attention scores between text-content tokens in the prompt and image patch tokens (e.g., 64×64 spatial grid), which are extracted from the model's existing attention layers during the forward pass.

## Method

The Overshooting sampler replaces each Euler step with two sub-steps: (1) an ODE overshoot that advances the state by (1+c)ε instead of ε, and (2) a noise compensation step that reverts from the overshoot time back to the target time while preserving the marginal distribution, with coefficients a=s/o and b=√((1-s)²−s²(1-o)²/o²). In the limit of small step sizes, this process is shown to be equivalent to the Euler ODE plus a Langevin dynamics term (α_t∇log ρ_t dt + √(2α_t)dW_t) that corrects compounding discretization errors. The key innovation, AMO, modulates the overshooting strength c per image patch using a mask m computed from cross-attention scores between text-content tokens and image patch tokens, so that text regions receive stronger stochastic correction while non-text regions retain Euler-level fidelity. This avoids the over-smoothing artifacts that uniform overshooting introduces on high-frequency details like feathers or smoke.

## Results

On the Flux model, AMO achieves 82.5% human-evaluated text rendering correction rate (CR) versus 74.0% for the Euler sampler, a 35.9% relative improvement. On SD3, CR increases from 32.5% to 43.0% (32.3% relative gain). AMO also improves FID (e.g., 111.8→108.9 on Flux) and OCR accuracy (0.313→0.381 on Flux) without increasing inference cost, confirming that the gains come from better text rendering rather than overall image quality trade-offs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative baseline is the standard Euler sampler, which AMO outperforms on every metric across all three models (SD3, Flux, AuraFlow), with the largest gains in human-evaluated correction rate (+10.5 pp on SD3, +8.5 pp on Flux) and OCR accuracy. In the human evaluation study (Figure 8, 304 cases, 15 participants), AMO on Flux achieves text rendering quality on par with GlyphControl [22], a model explicitly fine-tuned on ground-truth written text, while delivering superior overall image quality thanks to the Flux backbone. Compared to TextDiffuser [5], another task-specific fine-tuned model, AMO matches or exceeds text rendering accuracy without any retraining, avoiding the risk of degrading general image generation capabilities. The key practical advantage is that AMO is entirely training-free and adds no extra model evaluations per step, making it a drop-in replacement for the Euler sampler in any existing RF-based pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Rectified Flow (RF) framework [Liu 2022], constructing a stochastic sampler by over-simulating the learned RF ODE and compensating with noise to preserve the marginal distribution. The key theoretical link is to the Fokker-Planck-based SDE construction from score-based generative modeling [Song et al. 2020], which the authors use to show that overshooting plus noise compensation is equivalent to injecting a Langevin dynamics term at each Euler step. Unlike prior stochastic sampling methods (Karras et al. 2022, Meng et al. 2023) designed for diffusion models, this work adapts the idea specifically to RF and adds an attention-based spatial modulation to prevent over-smoothing artifacts, positioning itself as a training-free alternative to fine-tuning approaches like TextDiffuser and GlyphControl.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that AMO delivers 32–36% improvements in text rendering accuracy on SD3 and Flux with zero additional training and negligible inference overhead, making it immediately deployable on any existing RF-based text-to-image model. The attention modulation mechanism is a simple but effective solution to the over-smoothing problem that plagues uniform stochastic corrections, and the ablation showing that overshooting without noise compensation yields 0% correct rate underscores the importance of the marginal-preserving construction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond image generation (e.g., video), whether it interacts well with other inference-time techniques like classifier-free guidance, and whether the attention-based spatial control can be made more principled rather than heuristic.
