## Problem

Panoramic 3D scene understanding methods either rely on costly projection-based strategies (cubemap, tangent patches) that introduce boundary discontinuities and computational overhead, or use ERP-specific architectures that do not explicitly exploit the spherical geometry inherent to 360° data. No prior work has leveraged spherical harmonics as implicit structural priors to provide a unified, projection-free framework for holistic panoramic 3D understanding across multiple tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250666/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HUSH takes a single equirectangular panorama RGB image resized to 512×1024 as input and produces pixel-wise depth maps, 3-channel surface normal maps, and room layout line segments as outputs. The framework is multi-task by design: a single shared backbone and SH attention module feed into separate task-specific heads, and the SH basis index module generates per-pixel index maps that highlight which of the 55 SH bases (level 10) are dominant for each task and region. The method can be trained depth-only, depth+normal jointly, or further fine-tuned for layout estimation.

## Method

HUSH's core insight is to treat spherical harmonics (SH) basis functions not merely as approximation tools but as implicit structural priors that serve as queries in a hierarchical cross-attention mechanism. A scene-wise SH coefficient extractor first scales pre-defined SH bases using coefficients predicted from multi-scale image features (extracted by a Swin Transformer with multi-scale deformable attention), producing scene-adaptive bases that maintain orthogonality. These modified SH bases then act as queries in a hierarchical cross-attention module where they alternate roles with multi-scale image features as key-values across multiple cross-attention blocks, yielding a comprehensive scene feature that integrates spherical geometry with image context. A task-wise SH basis index module further refines this by computing per-pixel cosine similarity between task-relevant features and each SH basis, producing an index map that amplifies the most dominant basis for each task and region, thereby reducing ambiguity among the 55 bases. Task-specific heads (depth, normal, layout) then combine the comprehensive scene feature with the task-relevant indexed feature to produce final predictions, trained with BerHu depth loss, cosine normal loss, and spherical gradient loss.

## Results

HUSH achieves state-of-the-art depth estimation across all three benchmark datasets. On Matterport3D, the multi-task variant (D+N) attains Abs Rel 0.0838 and RMSE 0.4164, outperforming the best single-task baseline Elite360D (Abs Rel 0.1115, RMSE 0.4875) by 24.8% and 14.5% respectively. On Stanford2D3D, HUSH (D+N) reaches Abs Rel 0.0782 and RMSE 0.3332, and on Structured3D it achieves Abs Rel 0.0459 and RMSE 0.2577, both substantially ahead of all prior methods. The multi-task (D+N) configuration consistently matches or slightly exceeds the depth-only (D) variant, confirming the benefit of joint surface normal supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUSH outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and every reported metric. Against the strongest single-task baseline Elite360D [1], HUSH (D) improves Abs Rel by 24.1% and RMSE by 14.5% on Matterport3D (0.0845 vs. 0.1115; 0.4167 vs. 0.4875), with similar margins on Stanford2D3D and Structured3D. Against the multi-task baseline Elite360M* [2] (retrained for depth+normal only), HUSH (D+N) achieves 11.4% lower Abs Rel and 10.3% lower RMSE on Matterport3D (0.0838 vs. 0.0946; 0.4164 vs. 0.4642). The projection-based methods UniFuse [12], HRDFuse [3], and EGformer [39] are consistently outperformed by 15–30% in Abs Rel across datasets, with EGformer showing the largest gap (e.g., 0.1473 vs. 0.0838 Abs Rel on Matterport3D). PanoFormer [28], which uses tangent-patch projections, is also surpassed by a wide margin on all three benchmarks. The gap is most dramatic on the synthetic Structured3D dataset, where HUSH (D+N) achieves Abs Rel 0.0459 versus 0.1480 for Elite360D, a 69% relative improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUSH departs from the dominant projection-based paradigm in panoramic depth estimation (cubemap, tangent patches, icosahedron) by working directly in the equirectangular/spherical domain, and from ERP-specific transformer methods (PanoFormer, EGformer) by introducing spherical harmonics as implicit structural priors rather than learning task-specific geometric embeddings. The hierarchical cross-attention design is adapted from Plane2Depth's learnable plane queries, but HUSH replaces those queries with scene-adaptive SH basis functions, repurposing SH from a classical approximation tool into an attention mechanism. The SH basis index module draws conceptual inspiration from HRDFuse's spatial feature alignment, but operates over a fixed orthogonal basis set rather than learned spatial features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HUSH demonstrates that a mathematically grounded, task-agnostic basis (SH) can serve as effective structural guidance for multiple 3D understanding tasks simultaneously, reducing the need for task-specific geometric assumptions (Manhattan, Atlanta world) and enabling straightforward extension to new tasks (layout estimation with only 200 fine-tuning epochs). The finding that low-order SH (5 levels, 15 bases) performs nearly as well as 10 levels (55 bases) suggests the relevant geometric information in indoor scenes is concentrated in low-frequency spherical components, which could inform more efficient representations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or non-Manhattan scenes, whether the Swin Transformer backbone is essential, and whether higher-order SH or learned SH modifications could capture finer geometric details.
