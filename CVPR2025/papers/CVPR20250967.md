## Problem

Existing learned feature detectors and descriptors rely on regular or deformable convolutions that are limited to local windows, failing to capture long-range geometric cues (e.g., vanishing lines) critical for robust matching under large viewpoint, scale, and illumination changes. Additionally, no public benchmark adequately stresses these challenging cross-view and large-baseline scenarios, making it difficult to evaluate true robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250967/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a pair of RGB images (resized to 800 px for training, 1600 px for evaluation), the network produces in a single forward pass: sub-pixel keypoints at full H×W resolution (top 500 via DKD), a dense 256-dim descriptor map at H/4 × W/4 resolution, and a matchability map at the same 1/4 resolution. Sparse matches are obtained by sampling descriptors at detected keypoints and applying dual-softmax MNN; semi-dense matches are produced by selecting top-K locations from the matchability map and refining them via epipolar constraints, yielding sub-pixel correspondences at 1/4 image resolution.

## Method

RDD employs a two-branch architecture that decouples keypoint detection from descriptor extraction. The keypoint branch is a lightweight multi-scale CNN (resolutions 1/1, 1/2, 1/8, 1/32) whose concatenated features feed a scoremap head, with sub-pixel localization via differentiable keypoint detection (DKD). The descriptor branch uses ResNet-50 to extract five-scale feature maps (1/4 through 1/64), which are positionally encoded and processed by a 4-layer multi-scale deformable attention encoder (8 heads, 8 sample points per head) to produce the dense descriptor map; deformable attention selectively attends to key spatial locations across scales, capturing global geometric context at linear rather than quadratic cost. Training is sequential: the descriptor branch is first optimized with focal loss on positive correspondences plus a modified focal matchability loss, then the keypoint branch is trained with reprojection, reliability, and peaky losses while the descriptor weights are frozen. For semi-dense matching, coarse matches from the matchability map are refined by solving per-point offsets that satisfy the epipolar constraint derived from a fundamental matrix estimated via the eight-point method on sparse matches.

## Results

On the standard MegaDepth-1500 benchmark (sparse MNN matching, top 4096 features), RDD achieves 48.2/65.2/78.3 AUC@5°/10°/20°, outperforming the previous best sparse method DeDoDe-V2-G (47.2/63.9/77.5). On the more challenging MegaDepth-View benchmark with large viewpoint and scale changes, RDD achieves 38.3/53.1/65.6 AUC@5°/10°/20°, a substantial margin over DeDoDe-V2-G (33.1/47.6/60.2). On the newly introduced Air-to-Ground benchmark, RDD achieves 41.4/56.0/67.8 AUC@5°/10°/20°, surpassing the runner-up DeDoDe-V2-G (31.5/45.3/58.3) by over 10 points at 5°. In the challenging night setting of Aachen Day-Night visual localization, RDD reaches 86.7/92.9/99.0 AUC, beating DISK (83.7/89.8/99.0) and ALIKED (85.7/91.8/96.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDD outperforms all sparse MNN baselines (SuperPoint, DISK, ALIKED, XFeat, DeDoDe-V2-G) on every benchmark, with the largest margins on the challenging MegaDepth-View (+5.2 AUC@5° over DeDoDe-V2-G) and Air-to-Ground (+9.9 AUC@5° over DeDoDe-V2-G) datasets. With the LightGlue learned matcher, RDD+LG (52.3/68.9/81.8 on MD-1500) surpasses SP+LG (49.9/67.0/80.1) and DeDoDe-V2-G+LG (44.1/62.1/76.5), confirming the descriptor quality advantage. In the semi-dense setting, RDD* (51.3/67.1/79.3 on MD-1500) trails the strongest dense/semi-dense methods—RoMa (62.6/76.7/86.3), ELoFTR (56.4/72.2/83.5), and ASpanFormer (55.3/71.5/83.1)—but narrows the gap considerably on MegaDepth-View where geometric distortion is more severe. On HPatches, RDD matches ALIKED on illumination sequences (93.0 vs 97.0 MHA@3px) but clearly wins on viewpoint changes (86.0 vs 85.0 MHA@5px, 90.0 vs 88.0 MHA@10px). On Aachen Day-Night, RDD leads all baselines in the night setting (86.7/92.9/99.0 vs DISK's 83.7/89.8/99.0 and ALIKED's 85.7/91.8/96.9), while remaining competitive in the day setting (87.0/94.2/97.8 vs SuperPoint's 87.4/93.2/97.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

RDD extends the line of geometric-invariant descriptor learning from deformable convolutions (ALIKED, ASLFeat) to deformable attention, removing the local-window constraint and enabling the network to capture long-range geometric cues such as vanishing lines. Architecturally, it follows the decoupled detection/description paradigm established by DeDoDe and XFeat, assigning a lightweight CNN to sub-pixel keypoint localization (via DKD) and a multi-scale deformable transformer encoder to descriptor extraction. The semi-dense matching refinement module, which uses epipolar constraints from sparse matches to correct coarse correspondences, is a novel addition not present in prior detector/descriptor pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Air-to-Ground dataset and MegaDepth-View benchmark address a real gap: prior evaluations rarely stress-test feature matching under extreme viewpoint and scale changes, yet these conditions are common in aerial-to-ground 3D reconstruction and cross-view localization. The semi-dense matching capability (RDD*) is practically significant because it increases inlier count and matching density without requiring a separate dense matcher, directly benefiting downstream SfM and pose estimation. Limitations include the absence of explicit data augmentation during training and the semi-dense refinement's dependence on confident sparse priors, which can cascade into failure under very large baselines; improving the refinement with visual features and adding augmentation are natural next steps.
