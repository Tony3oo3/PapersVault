## Problem

Auto-regressive image models like iGPT and AIM exhibit desirable scaling laws analogous to NLP, but suffer from severe sample and parameter inefficiency—requiring 150× more training samples and 7–16× more parameters than contrastive or masked image modeling methods for competitive results. This inefficiency stems from predicting individual patches sequentially, which forces the model to expend capacity on high-frequency pixel details rather than low-frequency structural patterns essential for object recognition.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (224×224 or 256×256 pixels) partitioned into 14×14 or 16×16 pixel patches, which are further grouped into spatial blocks of 4×4 patches (56×56 or 64×64 pixels). During pre-training, the output is the reconstructed pixel values of the next block in raster order, computed via an MLP over concatenated token embeddings. During evaluation, the frozen encoder produces visual representations used for linear or attentive probing on 15 downstream image classification benchmarks.

## Method

XTRA introduces Block Causal Masking, which divides the patch sequence into k×k spatial blocks and enforces causality only at the block level using a rasterized pattern, while allowing unrestricted attention among tokens within each block. The architecture is an encoder-decoder ViT where the decoder's per-block token representations are concatenated in raster order and passed through a shared MLP to predict all pixel values of the next block, trained with MSE loss on per-block normalized pixels. This block-wise factorization lets the model capture low-frequency structural dependencies over larger image regions in fewer autoregressive steps, rather than spending capacity on redundant high-frequency detail. Ablations confirm that the raster pattern (vs. random) yields an 11.9% gain, that block size relative to resolution (not absolute size) is the controlling factor, and that matching the decoder width to the encoder width provides a further 2.0% improvement.

## Results

XTRA-H (ViT-H/14, 632M params), pre-trained on only 13.1M ImageNet-21K samples, achieves 76.2% average top-1 accuracy across 15 diverse image recognition benchmarks via attentive probing, outperforming AIM-0.6B trained on 2B samples (74.5%) by 1.7% and AIM-0.6B+ (75.6%) by 0.6%. On ImageNet-1K, XTRA-B (85M params) achieves 70.2% linear probing and 76.8% attentive probing accuracy, surpassing iGPT-L (1.36B params, 65.2% LIN) by 5.0% and AIM-0.6B (0.63B params, 73.5% ATT) by 3.3% while using 7–16× fewer parameters. Among single-view methods on ImageNet-1K, XTRA-B at 800 epochs outperforms MAE (1600 epochs, 67.8% LIN) and Data2Vec (1600 epochs, 68.0% LIN) by 2.2–2.4% in linear probing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary auto-regressive baselines, XTRA-H (13.1M samples) beats AIM-0.6B (2B samples) by 1.7% average accuracy across 15 benchmarks and AIM-0.6B+ (2B+ samples) by 0.6%, while XTRA-B (85M params) surpasses iGPT-L (1.36B params) by 5.0% in linear probing and AIM-0.6B (0.63B params) by 3.3% in attentive probing on ImageNet-1K. Among single-view methods on ImageNet-1K, XTRA-B at 800 epochs outperforms MAE (1600 epochs) by 2.4% LIN and 2.6% ATT, Data2Vec (1600 epochs) by 2.2% LIN, and CAE (800 epochs) by 1.6% LIN and 0.9% ATT. XTRA-B also beats SimMIM (800 epochs) by 13.5% in linear probing. However, XTRA-B trails multi-crop methods (MoCo-v3, DINO, iBOT) by 0.2–3.0% in attentive probing, though these methods use 2–12 augmented views per image, making the comparison less direct. On the 15-benchmark suite, XTRA-H (76.2% avg) surpasses MAE-H (75.3%) and DINO (75.0%) but remains below iBOT (77.6%), which uses multi-crop augmentations and a larger ViT-L/16 backbone trained on the full ImageNet-21K.

## Relation to Prior Work

XTRA extends the auto-regressive image modeling lineage established by iGPT (pixel-level) and AIM (patch-level) by introducing a Block Causal Mask that enforces causality at the level of k×k token blocks rather than individual patches. This departs from AIM's standard causal masking and its encoder-only design, adopting instead an encoder-decoder architecture where the decoder localizes pixel reconstruction so the encoder retains more abstract representations. The paper positions XTRA as a simpler, more efficient alternative to the complex training recipes (multi-crop augmentations, momentum encoders, KoLeo regularization) that contrastive and MIM methods like DINO, iBOT, and MAE require, while preserving the predictable scaling behavior that auto-regressive objectives offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 152× sample-efficiency gain over AIM and 7–16× parameter-efficiency gain demonstrate that auto-regressive vision models need not be prohibitively expensive to be competitive, lowering the barrier to adopting this paradigm in resource-constrained settings. The finding that a single architectural change—grouping tokens into blocks with intra-block free attention—yields such large gains suggests the standard token-level causal mask was a significant bottleneck, opening questions about optimal block granularity, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D data, and how it interacts with further scaling. A limitation is that the sample-efficiency claim is relative to AIM specifically; against the best contrastive/MIM methods (e.g., iBOT, DINOv2), XTRA still trails on several benchmarks, so the practical advantage is strongest within the auto-regressive family.
