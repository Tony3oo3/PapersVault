## Problem

LVLM instruction-tuning data suffers from severe long-tail imbalance, where ~97% of entities correspond to only ~14% of training instances, yet prior long-tail mitigation work has focused almost exclusively on discriminative VLMs (CLIP, ViT) and narrow tasks like image classification. The unique challenges of LT in generative LVLMs—cross-modal co-occurrence patterns, multi-perspective entity distributions, and the gap between training and benchmark distributions—remain largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LVLM instruction-tuning datasets (e.g., InstructMix665K for LLaVA 1.5, 1.2M instances for ShareGPT4V) structured as (image, conversation) pairs, and produces a refined dataset of the same total size: a filtered core set of head instances plus newly synthesized tail instances. Entity extraction requires a POS parser for tokens, GroundingDINO for visual object grounding, an LLM for interrogation-type classification, and WordNet for synonym lookup; visual synthesis uses ControlNet conditioned on the original tail image plus a default preservation prompt, followed by an off-the-shelf vision captioner and LLM-based conversation expansion.

## Method

ADR operates in three stages. First, an Analyzing stage builds reverse-indexed frequency distributions Q_r across four entity perspectives (tokens, objects, co-occurrence pairs, interrogation types), mapping each entity to the number of training instances containing it. Second, the Data Rebalancing (DR) stage computes a per-entity sampling probability P_e = τ/N_e (where τ is a perspective-specific threshold and N_e is the instance count), then retains a data instance only if the total number of its sampled entities exceeds a hyperparameter n_p, effectively decimating redundant head data while preserving diverse coverage. Third, the Data Synthesis (DS) stage addresses tail scarcity via two complementary paths: (a) language synthesis, where WordNet synonyms of head tokens are substituted with tail tokens and an LLM rewrites the conversation accordingly, and (b) diffusion-based visual synthesis, where ControlNet generates style-varied images from tail instances while preserving key objects and co-occurrences, and the resulting images are captioned and expanded into full conversations by an LLM. The synthetic quantity per tail instance is governed by a piecewise function of the maximum entity probability P_d*, capping augmentation at 2 copies for the most scarce cases.

## Results

On LLaVA 1.5, the full ADR framework (+DR+DS) achieves an average absolute improvement of 2.28 points and a relative improvement of 4.36% across 11 benchmarks, without increasing training data volume beyond the original 665K instances. On the most challenging benchmarks (MMStar, SEED Bench2, OK-VQA, Q-Bench-2), the improvement reaches 4.3 points absolute (9.15% relative). On tail-concept evaluation (ScienceQA), ADR improves tail@5 accuracy from 67.9% to 70.1% and overall accuracy from 69.3% to 70.2%, while also boosting head@80 from 74.6% to 78.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA 1.5 (the primary baseline), ADR+DR+DS wins on 9 out of 11 benchmarks, with the largest gains on SEED Bench2 (+8.7 pts), OK-VQA (+4.2 pts), and Q-Bench-2 (+2.3 pts). Against ShareGPT4V, ADR similarly improves all benchmarks, confirming the method is orthogonal to caption-quality improvements. In the direct comparison with existing data-balancing methods (Table 4), ADR-DR dramatically outperforms EL2N, Perplexity, and CLIP Score on SEED (61.0 vs. 53.0–53.6) and SEED v2 (57.2 vs. 47.0–47.4), where those pruning methods cause severe performance collapse. ADR also edges out COINCIDE on GQA (62.8 vs. 59.8), POPE (87.2 vs. 86.1), and MMB (65.5 vs. 63.1). The full ADR (+DR+DS) further improves POPE to 87.4 and SEED to 61.3, achieving the best or tied-best score on every benchmark in Table 4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends long-tail mitigation from traditional VLMs (CLIP-based classification) to generative LVLMs (LLaVA, ShareGPT4V) performing open-ended tasks like VQA and visual reasoning. Prior LT work (MetaCLIP, REAL, GENSYNTH) addressed imbalanced recognition or contrastive pre-training, while data-pruning methods (EL2N, Perplexity, COINCIDE) filtered LVLM instruction data without explicitly targeting distributional imbalance. The ADR framework repurposes ControlNet for conditioned image synthesis and WordNet/LLM rewriting for text augmentation, combining them into a two-stage rebalancing-then-synthesis pipeline that is orthogonal to existing data-selection techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The four-perspective entity analysis (tokens, objects, co-occurrences, interrogations) provides the first systematic diagnostic of LT severity in LVLM training data, revealing that ~97% of entities map to only ~14% of instances. The framework is model-agnostic and data-agnostic, making it a practical toolkit for any open-source LVLM pipeline. However, evaluation is limited to two base models at the instruction-tuning stage only; the authors acknowledge extending to pre-training as future work, and the 4.36% relative gain, while consistent across 11 benchmarks, is modest. Whether the diffusion-based synthesis introduces subtle distributional artifacts at scale remains an open question.
