## Problem

Existing 2D rotation symmetry detection methods based on CNN segmentation detect rotation centers but cannot enforce inherent 3D geometric properties (equal side lengths, interior angles) because real-world annotations are assigned from a 3D perspective while the model operates in 2D image space. Additionally, these methods overlook supporting vertices and symmetry group structure, limiting their ability to analyze individual symmetries. The paper addresses this by formulating rotation symmetry detection as a 3D set-based detection problem with explicit geometric constraints.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (resized to max side 1333 px) as input; since the DENDI dataset lacks camera intrinsics, the focal length is fixed at 1000 and the focal center at the image center. It outputs a set of up to 800 polygon predictions, each consisting of a 3D rotation center, a 3D seed vertex, a 3D rotation axis vector, an angle bias, and a symmetry group label (SO(2), C2–C8), which are then projected to 2D image coordinates via a pinhole camera model. The 3D point cloud range is constrained to [−1, 1] m in x-y and [0, 4] m in z.

## Method

The architecture combines a Swin-T backbone with a 6-layer transformer encoder that uses a novel Camera Cross Attention (CCA) module: learnable camera queries on a 3D grid are projected to 2D reference points at multiple depth levels to sample multi-scale image features via deformable attention, effectively lifting 2D features into 3D camera coordinates. The detection head regresses a 3D center, a single seed vertex, a rotation axis vector, and an angle bias, while classifying the symmetry group. The key innovation is a vertex reconstruction stage that generates all N vertices by rotating the seed point around the axis using Rodrigues' rotation formula, enforcing equal radial distances and uniform angular spacing without per-vertex regression. For C2 (rectangular) objects, an additional angle bias β produces four vertices from one center. Training uses a two-step bipartite matching scheme—first aligning predicted centers to ground-truth centers (Rotation Center Matching), then aligning reconstructed vertex sets within each matched pair (Rotation Vertex Matching)—with L1 regression losses on projected 2D coordinates.

## Results

On the DENDI test set, the proposed model achieves a mean AP of 31.3 for rotation center detection across seven symmetry groups (SO(2), C2–C8) and a mean AP of 30.6 for rotation vertex detection across six discrete groups (C2–C8). In the state-of-the-art comparison using a heatmap-based F1-score protocol, the method reaches a maximum F1-score of 33.2, substantially outperforming the prior best (EquiSym) at 22.5. The ablation confirms that the vertex reconstruction stage with 3D geometric priors lifts vertex mAP from 23.5 (3D baseline) to 30.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single state-of-the-art baseline: EquiSym (Seo et al., CVPR 2022), a segmentation-based equivariant learning method for rotation symmetry detection. Evaluated under the same heatmap-based F1-score protocol (100 thresholds, 5-pixel dilation), the proposed detection model achieves a maximum F1-score of 33.2, outperforming EquiSym's 22.5 by 10.7 points. Notably, this comparison is somewhat asymmetric: EquiSym is a segmentation model while the proposed method is a detection model, yet the authors render predicted points as a score map to enable fair metric comparison. The detection-based approach also offers a practical advantage over EquiSym's heatmap output, as it directly identifies individual symmetry instances without requiring post-processing to separate overlapping detections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the DETR set-based detection paradigm (bipartite matching, object queries) to rotation symmetry detection, replacing the heatmap/segmentation formulation used by prior methods like EquiSym. It borrows the deformable attention mechanism from Deformable DETR and the spatial cross-attention philosophy from BEVFormer to introduce a Camera Cross Attention module that lifts 2D backbone features into 3D camera coordinates. The key conceptual departure from prior rotation symmetry work is the vertex reconstruction stage: rather than regressing individual vertex coordinates, the model predicts a single seed vertex and rotation axis, then generates all vertices via Rodrigues' rotation formula, thereby enforcing 3D geometric priors (equal radial distances, uniform angular spacing) by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a fundamental mismatch in 2D symmetry detection: ground-truth labels encode 3D symmetry perceived by annotators, yet 2D models must infer this from perspective-distorted images. The ablation showing that a naive 3D baseline (23.5 mAP) actually underperforms the 2D baseline (24.7) before geometric priors are added (30.6) underscores that 3D lifting alone is insufficient—explicit structural constraints are what drive the gain. Limitations include the small DENDI dataset (~1.5K training images), the absence of camera intrinsics (focal length hardcoded to 1000), and modest absolute AP scores (~30), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse symmetry benchmarks or benefits from learned intrinsics.
