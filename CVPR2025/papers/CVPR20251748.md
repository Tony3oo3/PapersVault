## Problem

PnP algorithms for single-pixel imaging are flexible across compressive ratios but limited in reconstruction accuracy and inference speed, while unrolling networks achieve SOTA accuracy with fast inference yet require fine-tuning or retraining when the CR changes due to their lack of convergence guarantees and interpretability. The paper addresses how to endow unrolling networks with the flexibility of PnP while retaining their accuracy and speed, by ensuring the learned subnets genuinely approximate proximal operators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251748/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes compressed measurements Y ∈ R^{h×w} and two small measurement matrices H ∈ R^{h×H} and W ∈ R^{w×W} (Kronecker decomposition of the full sensing matrix) to reconstruct an image X ∈ R^{H×W}. A single trained model handles arbitrary CRs from 0.01 to 0.50 without retraining, and the Kronecker formulation reduces space complexity from O(ηH²W²) to O(√η H² + √η W²), making it practical for real SPI cameras where block-wise sampling is infeasible.

## Method

The core contribution is a proximal trajectory (PT) loss that supervises each unrolled iteration to match a closed-form proximal step Prox_g̅ derived from an explicit L2 restoration regularizer, thereby training the deep image restorer (DIR) to approximate a true proximal operator with guaranteed convergence. The DIR is a 4-level asymmetric encoder-decoder CNN-Transformer hybrid where a memory block (channel cross-attention) propagates informative representations across iterations, and each processing stage combines Swin Transformer (shifted-window self-attention for low-frequency modeling) with a gated dynamic CNN using AdaConv (input-dependent convolution kernels for high-frequency detail) in parallel. The full network unrolls 6 iterations of DIR ∘ Prox_f under either HQS or ADMM, with the PT loss replacing the standard MSE objective to enforce step-by-step improvement rather than only a satisfactory final output.

## Results

On Set11, ADMM-ProxUnroll achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 32.59/0.8692 across five CRs {0.01, 0.04, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50}, outperforming the best CR-specific unrolling network HATNet (32.41/0.8640) and the best PnP algorithm PnP-DRUNet (30.85/0.8383). On BSD68, ADMM-ProxUnroll reaches 30.50/0.8046, surpassing SAUNet (30.31/0.7951) and PnP-DRUNet (28.83/0.7677). In terms of efficiency, HQS/ADMM-ProxUnroll requires only 107.73G MACs, 3.90M parameters, and 0.27s runtime on 256×256 images at CR=0.25, compared to 494.42G/31.28M/0.60s for HATNet and 7175.10G/32.64M/0.65s for PnP-DRUNet (50 iterations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CR-specific unrolling networks (HATNet, SAUNet, CPP-Net, DGUNet+, OCTUF+), ProxUnroll achieves higher average PSNR on both Set11 (+0.18 dB over HATNet) and BSD68 (+0.19 dB over SAUNet) while being the only unrolling method that handles arbitrary CRs with a single model. Against PnP algorithms (PnP-DnCNN, PnP-DRUNet), ProxUnroll gains +1.74 dB on Set11 and +1.67 dB on BSD68 over PnP-DRUNet, and runs 2.4× faster (0.27s vs 0.65s) with 18× fewer MACs. Against COAST, the only other multi-CR unrolling network, ProxUnroll outperforms it by +5.61 dB on Set11 at CR=0.01 (where COAST fails catastrophically at 10.45 dB) and maintains flexibility for unseen CRs. In the efficiency comparison (Table 3), ProxUnroll uses 3.90M parameters vs 31.28M for HATNet and 32.64M for PnP-DRUNet, with the lowest runtime at 0.27s. On real captured SPI data, ProxUnroll recovers high-frequency textures and details that PnP-DRUNet smooths out, and matches or exceeds SAUNet/HATNet which require separate models per CR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two competing paradigms for solving SPI inverse problems: plug-and-play (PnP) iterative algorithms, which offer CR flexibility but limited accuracy, and deep-unrolling networks, which achieve SOTA accuracy but require retraining per CR. It builds directly on HQS and ADMM as the underlying proximal algorithms, on Kronecker compressed sensing for efficient 2D matrix-form processing, and on prior unrolling architectures (SAUNet, HATNet) whose convergence limitations motivate the memory block design. The key departure is the proximal trajectory (PT) loss, which trains the unrolled network's deep image restorer to approximate the proximal operator of an explicit restoration regularizer, thereby importing PnP-like flexibility into the unrolling framework without sacrificing accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PT loss provides a general training principle that could extend beyond SPI to other inverse problems where unrolling networks currently lack degradation flexibility, potentially eliminating the need for CR-specific retraining across a wide range of imaging tasks. The memory block's channel cross-attention mechanism for cross-iteration information propagation addresses a recognized gap in unrolling networks (lack of guaranteed progressive refinement) and may be applicable to other multi-stage architectures. Limitations include evaluation only on 256×256 and 512×512 images, reliance on BSD500 for training (400 images), and the fact that the DIR architecture, while efficient, is still a single fixed model rather than adapting its depth to the severity of degradation. Whether the PT loss generalizes to non-convex or non-differentiable data fidelity terms remains an open question.
