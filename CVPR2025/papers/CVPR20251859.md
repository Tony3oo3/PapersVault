## Problem

Previous MLLM-based universal multimodal retrieval models (e.g., E5-V) were fine-tuned exclusively on text data, leaving the multimodal capacity of the backbone underutilized. Moreover, existing multimodal training corpora are severely imbalanced—fused-modal (image-text composed) pairs are scarce, and visual document retrieval is largely unsupported—limiting the universality of the resulting retrievers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251859/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any combination of text, RGB images, image-text pairs, or visual document screenshots, optionally prefixed with a task-specific instruction string (e.g., 'Retrieve a passage that provides an answer to the given query about the image'). Visual inputs are capped at 1,024 tokens per image to bound compute, while text length is limited to 1,800 tokens for image-containing inputs and 512 tokens for text-only inputs. The output is a single dense embedding vector obtained from the final hidden state of the last token, which is then compared via cosine similarity against candidate embeddings for ranking.

## Method

GME builds on the Qwen2-VL MLLM backbone and is trained with an InfoNCE contrastive objective under LoRA (rank 8), where a task-specific instruction is prepended to each query to condition the embedding on the retrieval scenario. The key insight is that a balanced mixture of single-modal (T→T, I→I), cross-modal (T→I, T→VD), and fused-modal (IT→IT) training data—rather than any single modality—yields the strongest universal retriever, as confirmed by controlled ablations. To address the scarcity of fused-modal pairs, the paper introduces a Doc2Query-inspired synthesis pipeline that uses an LLM to generate queries from Wikipedia passages, extracts entities and rewrites queries into image-referencing forms, retrieves or generates (via FLUX) matching images, and filters with CLIP relevance scoring, producing 1.1M high-quality fused-modal pairs. Training follows a two-stage ANCE-style protocol: initial contrastive training with random negatives, followed by hard-negative mining and continued training. The resulting 2B and 7B models achieve state-of-the-art results on the newly compiled UMRB benchmark (47 datasets spanning single-modal, cross-modal, and fused-modal retrieval), including competitive visual document retrieval performance on par with specialized models.

## Results

On the UMRB benchmark (47 datasets), GME-Qwen2-VL-7B achieves an average score of 67.44, while GME-Qwen2-VL-2B reaches 64.45, both substantially outperforming the previous state-of-the-art UMR model VISTA (37.32) and the MLLM-based E5-V (42.52). Notably, GME-2B achieves 87.84 on the T→VD (text-to-visual document) sub-task, surpassing the specialized document retrieval model DSE (78.21) by nearly 10 points. The 7B model further improves fused-modal retrieval (IT→IT: 73.62 vs. 66.47 for 2B), demonstrating consistent scaling benefits across all modality combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GME outperforms all five baselines across nearly every sub-task. Against VISTA (0.2B, text-encoder + visual plugin), GME-2B gains +27.1 points on average, with the largest margins on T→VD (+77.7) and IT→IT (+40.2), where VISTA's fixed text backbone severely limits cross-modal alignment. Against E5-V (8.4B, MLLM trained on text-only data), GME-2B leads by +21.9 points overall; E5-V remains competitive on T→T (52.41 vs. 55.93) but collapses on fused-modal tasks (IT→IT: 7.23 vs. 66.47), confirming that text-only training is insufficient for universal multimodal retrieval. Against the specialized document retrieval model DSE (4.2B), GME-2B surpasses it on T→VD (87.84 vs. 78.21) while also matching or exceeding it on all other sub-tasks, demonstrating that a unified model can rival task-specific approaches. CLIP-SF (score-fusion pipeline) and One-Peace (4B modality-alignment model) trail GME by 20+ points on average, with One-Peace particularly weak on fused-modal content (IT→IT: 23.41 vs. 66.47). The 7B GME model extends these advantages, achieving the best score on every single sub-task in the benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based dense retrieval paradigm introduced by E5-V, which demonstrated that fine-tuning a multimodal LLM on text-only data yields reasonable multimodal embeddings. The key departure is showing that diverse multimodal training data (single-modal, cross-modal, and fused-modal) is essential for truly universal retrieval, rather than relying on text-only supervision. The hard-negative mining strategy follows ANCE, and the fused-modal data synthesis pipeline adapts the Doc2Query idea from text-only query generation to the image-text setting. Unlike prior CLIP/BLIP-based UMR systems (UniVL-DR, UniIR, VISTA, MARVEL) that use separate encoders with fusion modules, GME uses a single MLLM backbone to handle all modalities natively, and is the first to incorporate visual document retrieval into the unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UMRB benchmark (47 datasets across single-modal, cross-modal, and fused-modal settings) fills a critical gap in evaluation, as prior benchmarks did not cover fused-modal or visual document retrieval in a unified framework. The finding that a general-purpose MLLM retriever can match or exceed specialized visual document retrieval models (DSE) suggests that unified architectures are becoming viable for RAG pipelines that need to handle diverse content types. Open questions include whether the linear scaling trend observed in training will continue with longer training, whether the data synthesis pipeline generalizes to other domains beyond Wikipedia, and how the approach performs with even larger MLLM backbones. The practical limitation is that the 8M training dataset and 600 A100 GPU hours for data synthesis still represent significant resource requirements.
