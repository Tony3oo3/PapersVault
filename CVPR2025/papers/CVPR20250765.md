## Problem

Existing zero-shot multi-face portrait generation methods (e.g., PhotoMaker, FlashFace) produce lower-fidelity results because they inject face embeddings through lightweight Adapters with weaker control than ControlNet-style networks, and they lack facial positional guidance during multi-face fusion, leading to misaligned features. Additionally, these methods cannot precisely control facial expressions and poses, relying solely on text prompts for attribute customization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250765/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N (1–4) reference face images of the same person, a target image that supplies expression and pose via 3D reconstruction, and a free-form text prompt for style/background. Reference images are pre-processed through matting (PP-Matting) and face detection (RetinaFace), then encoded at 1024×1024 resolution. The output is a single 1024×1024 portrait that preserves the reference identity while matching the target's expression/pose and the prompt's stylistic attributes, generated in 8 DDIM steps using SDXL-Lightning.

## Method

HiFi-Portrait introduces three coupled modules on top of SDXL: (1) a face refiner that uses a 5-layer transformer decoder with 32 learnable face tokens to fuse local CLIP features and global ArcFace features into 33-token multi-face representations, avoiding the 'blurred face pasting' artifact of raw local features; (2) a landmark generator that extracts 3D ID/expression/pose vectors via DECA, combines the reference ID with the target's expression and pose, renders a new face through 3DMM fitting, and projects it to 2D landmarks; (3) HiFi-Net, a ControlNet-derived network where the landmark latent serves as the cross-attention query and the flattened multi-face features serve as key/value, enabling landmark-guided fusion and spatial alignment of identity features. Only the face refiner and HiFi-Net are trained (all other modules frozen) with a combined diffusion + face-ID loss (α=0.1) on a custom 34k-ID / 960k-image dataset built from VGGFace2-HD and IMDb-Face, where each ID contains ~28 diverse portraits with generated captions.

## Results

On WebFace-100, HiFi-Portrait achieves the best face similarity (Sim_f = 77.7%), CLIP similarity (CLIP_f = 72.4%), expression control (Exp = 14.7 RMSE), pose control (Pose = 5.4 RMSE), and FID (108.5) among all compared methods. On the more challenging InternetFace-300 benchmark, it leads in Sim_f (73.1%), CLIP_f (71.1%), Exp (16.9), and Pose (6.2), though InstantID achieves a slightly lower FID (158.1 vs. 163.4). The largest margin over the strongest baseline (InstantID) is in expression control, where HiFi-Portrait reduces RMSE from 18.8 to 14.7 on WebFace-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiFi-Portrait outperforms all five baselines on face similarity (Sim_f) and CLIP similarity (CLIP_f) across both benchmarks, with the largest gap over PhotoMaker (+31.1 Sim_f on WebFace-100) and a consistent +4.8 to +3.4 advantage over the strongest competitor InstantID. In expression and pose control (Exp, Pose), HiFi-Portrait leads by a wide margin over InstantID (14.7 vs. 18.8 Exp; 5.4 vs. 6.1 Pose on WebFace-100), attributable to its landmark-guided multi-face fusion in HiFi-Net. On FID, HiFi-Portrait achieves the best score on WebFace-100 (108.5) but trails InstantID slightly on InternetFace-300 (163.4 vs. 158.1), suggesting a small quality trade-off on out-of-distribution identities. FlashFace and IP-Adapter, which use multi-face embeddings via Adapter-style cross-attention, are consistently outperformed by 8–13 points in Sim_f, confirming the paper's claim that Adapter-based injection provides weaker ID control than the ControlNet-derived HiFi-Net. ConsistentID, which relies on multimodal fine-grained identity features, lags behind by 16+ points in Sim_f, indicating that its approach is less effective for zero-shot multi-face scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot identity-preserved portrait generation (IPG) line of work, building directly on SDXL's latent diffusion backbone and ControlNet's conditional injection architecture. It departs from InstantID, which uses a single reference image guided by five keypoints, and from multi-face methods (PhotoMaker, FlashFace, IP-Adapter) that inject multiple face embeddings via lightweight Adapter cross-attention without positional guidance. HiFi-Portrait's key conceptual departure is combining a face refiner (merging CLIP local features with ArcFace global features via a transformer decoder) with 3DMM-derived landmarks as ControlNet-style conditions, enabling landmark-guided multi-face fusion that neither prior single-reference nor prior multi-reference approaches achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: multi-reference IPG methods previously produced lower-fidelity results due to weak Adapter-based control and absent facial positional guidance, while single-reference methods (InstantID) left performance on the table. The released 34k-ID / 960k-pair dataset with diverse expressions and poses is a tangible community resource for future multi-face research. Limitations include reliance on the SDXL backbone (not yet validated on newer architectures like FLUX), the added 3DMM fitting step in the landmark generator, and the fact that gains over InstantID, while consistent, are moderate (~5 points Sim_f). Open questions include whether the landmark-guided fusion strategy transfers to video portrait generation or to non-portrait identity tasks.
