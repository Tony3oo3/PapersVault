## Problem

Vision Mamba models exhibit severe feature artifacts—high-norm tokens in low-information background regions—that are more pervasive and extreme than those in Vision Transformers, appearing even in tiny models and intensifying with depth and scale. These artifacts compromise feature map quality and appear to be a key factor limiting Vision Mamba's scalability beyond ~90M parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250632/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (pre-trained at 128×128, fine-tuned at 224×224 or 384×384) which are decomposed into non-overlapping 16×16 patches, then augmented with n input-independent register tokens evenly interleaved among the image tokens. For classification, the output is a 1000-way class label; for segmentation, a 150-class per-pixel mask via an UperNet head at 512×512. Unusually, the final prediction uses a 'register head'—a linearly reduced and concatenated vector of register token outputs—rather than a standard class token or global average pooling over image tokens.

## Method

Mamba-Reg modifies the vanilla Vision Mamba (Vim) backbone with two key changes motivated by Mamba's unidirectional recurrent scanning: (1) n register tokens are evenly inserted throughout the input sequence rather than appended at one end, ensuring registers interact with local tokens at multiple positions to absorb artifact information; (2) a register head is introduced at the output, where a linear layer reduces each register's dimensionality by a factor r and the results are concatenated into a single n×d/r vector used for final prediction, analogous to multi-head attention's concatenation. The bidirectional Mamba scanning (forward + backward, averaged) is retained. Ablations confirm that even distribution outperforms head or middle placement by 0.3–0.8%, and reusing all registers for prediction outperforms using a single register by 1.2%, demonstrating that registers serve both as artifact sinks and as a meaningful global representation in the Mamba setting.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k classification, Mamba®-B achieves 83.0% top-1 accuracy at 224×224 (99M params), outperforming Vim-B's 81.8% by 1.2%, and reaches 84.3% at 384×384. The first successful scaling to 341M parameters (Mamba®-L) attains 83.6% at 224×224 and 84.5% at 384×384, surpassing all prior Mamba variants. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, Mamba®-L achieves 49.1% mIoU, significantly outperforming Vim-S (44.9%) and DeiT-B (45.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct predecessor Vim (pure Mamba), Mamba® consistently wins: +1.3% for Tiny (77.4% vs 76.1%), +0.9% for Small (81.4% vs 80.5%), and enables the first successful Base/Large scaling that Vim could not achieve. Compared to DeiT-B (81.8% at 224²), Mamba®-B gains 1.2% (83.0%), and at 384², Mamba®-B (84.3%) and Mamba®-L (84.5%) both exceed DeiT-B (83.1%). However, Mamba®-B (83.0%) trails the hybrid VMamba-B (83.9%) and ConvNeXt-B (83.8%) by roughly 0.8–0.9% at 224², though Mamba®-L at 384² (84.5%) surpasses both. On ADE20K segmentation, Mamba®-L (49.1% mIoU) outperforms all listed baselines including ResNet-101 (44.9%), DeiT-B (45.5%), and Vim-S (44.9%) by 3.2–4.2% mIoU, demonstrating that the register mechanism transfers effectively to dense prediction tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the register token mechanism introduced for Vision Transformers (Darcet et al., ICLR 2024) to the Vision Mamba (Vim) architecture, which is built on bidirectional selective state space model blocks. Two key departures from the ViT register formulation are motivated by Mamba's unidirectional recurrent scanning: registers are evenly interleaved throughout the token sequence rather than appended at one end, and they are recycled via a 'register head' (dimension-reduced concatenation) for the final classification prediction rather than discarded. The paper also extends the empirical analysis of feature artifacts (high-norm outlier tokens in background regions) from ViTs to Vision Mamba, showing the problem is substantially more severe in the Mamba setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that artifact tokens are more prevalent and higher-norm in Vision Mamba than in ViTs—even in tiny models—provides a concrete explanation for the scaling difficulties previously observed in Mamba-based vision backbones (prior work was limited to ~90M parameters). By demonstrating successful training at 341M parameters with competitive ImageNet accuracy, the paper opens the door to larger Mamba vision models and suggests that register tokens may become a standard component in future SSM-based visual architectures. Open questions include whether the emergent multi-head-like behavior of individual registers (attending to different semantic regions) can be explicitly exploited, and whether the approach generalizes to other SSM variants or video understanding tasks.
