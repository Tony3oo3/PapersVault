## Problem

Prior video generation models typically treat image and video as separate tasks and rely on diffusion-based formulations, limiting unified representation and convergence speed. No existing framework combines rectified flow with a joint image-video architecture at industry scale, and the data curation and training infrastructure required for long-sequence, multi-resolution video generation remain underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251220/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image and text-to-video, the model takes a natural-language prompt encoded by Flan-T5-XXL and produces a generated image or video in a shared latent space (8×8 spatial compression for images, 8×8×4 spatio-temporal compression for videos). For image-to-video, a reference image is broadcast and channel-concatenated with noised video tokens, with a single MLP layer for channel alignment. The Patch n' Pack strategy packs variable-length, variable-aspect-ratio instances into a single sequence, supporting training resolutions from 480p up to 1080p and inference at 128×128 through 256×256 and beyond.

## Method

Goku applies rectified flow (linear interpolation between noise and data, predicting velocity) to a joint image-video Transformer, a formulation previously unexplored for this setting. A 3D joint image-video VAE compresses both modalities into a shared latent space, where a full-attention Transformer (2B or 8B parameters) with 3D RoPE positional embeddings, Q-K RMSNorm for training stability, and adaLN-Zero timestep conditioning models all tokens uniformly. Training follows a multi-stage curriculum—text-semantic pairing, joint image-video at cascaded resolutions, then modality-specific fine-tuning—on ~36M video-text and 160M image-text pairs curated through aesthetic/OCR/motion filtering and MLM-based dense captioning. Infrastructure relies on 3D parallelism (Ulysses sequence parallelism + FSDP hybrid shard), selective activation checkpointing, MegaScale fault tolerance, and ByteCheckpoint for sub-4-second checkpointing across thousands of GPUs.

## Results

On VBench, Goku-T2V achieves a state-of-the-art overall score of 84.85, surpassing CausVid (84.27), Luma (83.61), and HunyuanVideo (83.24). For text-to-image generation, Goku-T2I(2B) scores 0.76 on GenEval (with prompt rewriting) and 83.65 on DPG-Bench, outperforming DALL-E 3 (0.67†, 83.50†) and SD3 (0.74). On UCF-101 zero-shot video generation, Goku-2B achieves FVD of 217.24 at 128×128 resolution, the best among all compared methods. The pilot experiment on ImageNet 256×256 confirms that rectified flow converges faster than DDPM, reaching FID 2.16 at 400k steps versus 2.52 for DDPM at the same step count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VBench (Table 6), Goku-T2V (84.85) outperforms all 12 compared video generation models, with the closest competitor being CausVid (84.27, −0.58) and Luma (83.61, −1.24); Goku leads on Dynamic Degree (76.11 vs. CausVid's 92.69 is an exception where CausVid wins) and Multiple Objects (79.48 vs. Luma's 82.63 is another exception), but dominates on Semantic Score (81.87 vs. Luma's 84.17 is the only loss) and Quality Score (85.60, tied with CausVid). On UCF-101 (Table 5), Goku-2B achieves the lowest FVD at 128×128 (217.24), beating PixelDance (242.82) and SVD (242.02 at 240×360), though SVD retains a slight FVD edge at 240×360 (242.02 vs. 254.47). For text-to-image (Table 4), Goku-T2I(2B)† with prompt rewriting achieves the best GenEval score (0.76), surpassing SD3 (0.74), DALL-E 3 (0.67†), and Emu 3 (0.66†), while also leading on DPG-Bench (83.65 vs. DALL-E 3's 83.50† and Emu 3's 80.60). Among the 25 baselines, Goku wins or ties on all headline metrics across both T2I and T2V benchmarks, with the only losses being CausVid on Dynamic Degree and Luma on Multiple Objects and Semantic Score in VBench sub-metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

Goku builds directly on GenTron's diffusion transformer block design and extends it with full attention (replacing separate temporal/spatial attention), 3D RoPE, and Q-K normalization for training stability. The flow formulation adopts rectified flow (Liu et al., Lipman et al.), which the paper positions as a pioneering application to joint image-and-video generation, while the 3D joint VAE and Patch n' Pack packing strategy are inherited from CogVideoX and NaViT respectively. The key conceptual departure from prior video generation work is the unified full-attention treatment of image and video tokens within a single rectified-flow transformer, rather than the more common factorized temporal-spatial attention or separate modality pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Goku provides a complete, reproducible blueprint for industry-scale joint image-video generation, with detailed data curation (36M video + 160M image pairs), multi-stage training, and infrastructure recipes (FSDP + Ulysses sequence parallelism, ByteCheckpoint, MegaScale fault tolerance) that are broadly applicable beyond this specific model. The VBench SOTA (84.85) and competitive T2I results validate the rectified-flow + full-attention + joint-training recipe, but the paper does not deeply ablate which individual architectural choices (e.g., full vs. factorized attention, specific RoPE configuration) drive the gains, leaving open questions about the minimal sufficient design. For practitioners, the practical value lies in the end-to-end system recipe and the demonstration that ~36M/160M curated pairs suffice for competitive generation quality.
