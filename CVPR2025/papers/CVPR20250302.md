## Problem

Existing speech-driven 3D talking head generation models rely on MSE-based losses and vertex-level metrics (e.g., Lip Vertex Error) that measure geometric differences without capturing the perceptual correspondence between speech characteristics and lip movements. The paper identifies three underexplored criteria—Temporal Synchronization, Lip Readability, and Expressiveness—that jointly determine whether lip movements appear perceptually accurate to human observers, and shows that humans prioritize expressive alignment over strict temporal precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250302/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The speech-mesh representation takes time-sequenced speech spectrograms (patchified into tokens) and 3D face meshes (patchified into mesh tokens) as input, producing mean-pooled embeddings in a shared space that clusters by phoneme, is sensitive to temporal shifts, and exhibits directional progression with speech intensity. As a plug-and-play perceptual loss, it requires only the generated 3D mesh and input speech from any existing talking head model. The evaluation metrics operate on lip vertex displacement sequences (MTM via DDTW), cosine similarity of speech-mesh embeddings (PLRS), and z-normalized RMS speech loudness vs. averaged lip displacement (SLCC).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage transformer-based speech-mesh synchronized representation. Stage 1 learns a rich audio-visual speech representation on LRS3 2D video using a masked autoencoder with cross-modal contrastive learning (InfoNCE), employing modality-specific encoders, a cross-modal fusion encoder, and modality-specific decoders. Stage 2 freezes the speech encoder and trains a 3D mesh encoder via contrastive alignment to the pre-trained speech embeddings, transferring emergent properties (phoneme clustering, temporal sensitivity, intensity directionality) into the 3D mesh space. The resulting representation is deployed as a symmetric InfoNCE perceptual loss that can be plugged into any existing 3D talking head generation model (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, SelfTalk) without architectural modification, consistently improving all three perceptual criteria. The paper also introduces two new benchmark datasets (LRS3-3D via SPECTRE, MEAD-3D via SMIRK) to address the limited intensity range of existing 3D face mesh corpora.

## Results

On the VOCASET test set, the proposed speech-mesh perceptual loss consistently improves all three lip synchronization criteria across three base models. The most notable gain is in Perceptual Lip Readability Score (PLRS): FaceFormer improves from 0.368 to 0.463, CodeTalker from 0.381 to 0.388, and SelfTalk from 0.414 to 0.418. Mean Temporal Misalignment (MTM) also decreases slightly (e.g., FaceFormer 53.6→52.2 ms; SelfTalk 50.1→49.2 ms), and the Speech-Lip Intensity Correlation Coefficient gap (Δ) narrows substantially (e.g., FaceFormer Δ drops from 0.08 to 0.03). On the MEAD-3D expressiveness benchmark, combining MEAD-3D training data with the perceptual loss raises level-wise SLCC from 0.06–0.15 (base models) to 0.27–0.34, approaching the ground-truth range of 0.24–0.42.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three state-of-the-art 3D talking head generators—FaceFormer, CodeTalker, and SelfTalk—as base models and shows that plugging in the proposed speech-mesh perceptual loss improves all three criteria (MTM, PLRS, SLCC/Δ) in every case. The largest absolute gain is on FaceFormer, where PLRS jumps by +0.095 (0.368→0.463) and the SLCC gap to ground truth shrinks from 0.08 to 0.03. Against the 3D SyncNet perceptual loss (Yang et al., CVPR 2024), the proposed representation with 2D prior consistently outperforms: for CodeTalker, 3D SyncNet degrades LVE from 3.700 to 4.319 and drops SLCC from 0.38 to 0.14, whereas the proposed method improves LVE to 3.579 and narrows Δ to 0.01. On the MEAD-3D expressiveness benchmark, base models trained only on VOCASET show near-zero SLCC (0.02–0.17) across intensity levels, while adding MEAD-3D data plus the perceptual loss lifts SLCC to 0.27–0.34, closely matching the ground-truth distribution (0.24–0.42) and outperforming the MEAD-3D-only training variant that degrades LVE and PLRS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-visual representation learning paradigm (masked autoencoder + contrastive learning, as in Gong et al. and Sun et al.) from the 2D lip-video domain to 3D face meshes via a two-stage curriculum that first learns a rich speech-2D lip video representation on LRS3, then aligns a 3D mesh encoder to that frozen speech space. It departs from prior 3D talking head generators (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, SelfTalk) that optimize only vertex-wise MSE/LVE by instead defining three perceptual criteria—Temporal Synchronization, Lip Readability, and Expressiveness—and building a speech-mesh representation that serves as both a plug-and-play perceptual loss and an evaluation metric. The 3D SyncNet extension (Yang et al., CVPR 2024) is the closest prior work in 3D speech-mesh alignment, but it is used only for evaluation, whereas this paper demonstrates the representation's versatility as a training signal across multiple base architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the field with a concrete, multi-faceted definition of 'perceptually accurate' lip synchronization and three corresponding metrics (MTM, PLRS, SLCC) that go beyond the long-dominant LVE, offering a more holistic evaluation framework. The plug-and-play perceptual loss is immediately applicable to any existing 3D talking head generator, and the constructed LRS3-3D and MEAD-3D datasets address a real data-scarcity bottleneck. Open questions include whether the pseudo-GT meshes from SPECTRE/SMIRK introduce systematic biases that limit representation quality, whether the metrics scale to multilingual or highly expressive settings, and whether the expressiveness criterion can be further decomposed (e.g., per-phoneme intensity modulation).
