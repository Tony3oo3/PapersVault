## Problem

Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) methods for outdoor LiDAR localization achieve high accuracy but require approximately 2 days of training per new scene, making them impractical for time-sensitive applications like autonomous driving and robotics. The paper identifies two root causes: large coverage areas containing visually similar regions that complicate regression learning, and the vast volume of training data (e.g., ~150K samples over 2 km²) that imposes heavy computational and storage demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251848/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-frame LiDAR point cloud in the sensor coordinate frame (LiDAR at origin) as input and regresses correspondences in the world coordinate frame, with the target point set downsampled by a factor of 8 (M′ = M/8). RANSAC is then applied to the predicted correspondences to recover the 6-DoF pose. The scene-agnostic backbone is pre-trained on 18 nuScenes scenes (350K frames, 2 days), and for each new scene only the lightweight prediction heads (22M parameters total) are trained, achieving real-time inference at 29 ms (Oxford) and 48 ms (NCLT) with batch size 1.

## Method

LightLoc freezes a scene-agnostic sparse-convolution backbone (SGLoc architecture with reduced dimensionality) trained via parallel regression heads across 18 scenes, then trains only scene-specific prediction heads for new scenes. Two key techniques accelerate this: (1) Sample Classification Guidance (SCG) applies K-Means to cluster training positions into k₁ clusters, trains an MLP classifier in ~5 minutes, and injects the resulting probability distribution (perturbed with Gaussian noise σ=0.1 and normalized to the unit sphere) into the SCR loss to disambiguate visually similar regions; (2) Redundant Sample Downsampling (RSD) hierarchically prunes the training set by computing the variance of per-sample median L1 loss over a sliding window, retaining only high-variance (slowly converging) samples during intermediate epochs before restoring the full set for final convergence. Together these reduce training from ~53 hours to ~1 hour while matching or exceeding prior SCR accuracy. The SCG module is additionally integrated into LOAM via a Kalman filter, using its confidence score to modulate measurement noise and eliminate SLAM drift (reducing mean position error from 87.81 m to 4.48 m).

## Results

On QEOxford, LightLoc achieves a mean position error of 0.83 m and orientation error of 1.12° with only 1 hour of training and 22M parameters, outperforming the previous SCR state-of-the-art LiSA (0.95 m / 1.14°, 53 h, 105M params) by 12.6% in position while being 50× faster to train. On the non-enhanced Oxford dataset, LightLoc achieves 2.67 m / 1.25°, and on NCLT it reaches 1.46 m / 2.80°, consistently the only method completing training within 1 hour. Runtime is 29 ms on Oxford and 48 ms on NCLT, meeting real-time requirements (50 ms and 100 ms respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightLoc outperforms all SCR baselines: it beats SGLoc by 45.8% in position (0.83 m vs 1.53 m) and LiSA by 12.6% (0.83 m vs 0.95 m) on QEOxford, while reducing training time from 50–53 h to 1 h and parameters from 105 M to 22 M. Against the strongest APR method DiffLoc, LightLoc achieves 55.4% lower position error (0.83 m vs 1.86 m) and 145× faster training, though DiffLoc retains a slight orientation advantage (0.87° vs 1.12°). All other APR baselines (PointLoc, PoseSOE, NIDALoc, HypLiLoc, STCLoc) are substantially outperformed in position error, with gaps ranging from 2.5× (HypLiLoc, 3.89 m) to 13× (PointLoc, 10.79 m). On NCLT, LightLoc (1.46 m) is competitive with LiSA (1.47 m) in position but trails DiffLoc (1.06 m), while still being 100× faster to train. The consistent 1-hour training across all three datasets is the key differentiator, as no other method achieves sub-2-hour training at comparable accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightLoc extends the scene-coordinate regression (SCR) paradigm established by SGLoc and LiSA, adopting their backbone architecture and RANSAC-based pose estimation pipeline. It follows the scene-agnostic backbone + scene-specific heads design from ACE (camera localization) and borrows the Gaussian noise augmentation strategy from GLACE. The key departure is that prior fast-training methods (ACE, GLACE) relied on GPU feature buffers that become infeasible for large-scale outdoor scenes with ~150K samples (~150GB storage), so LightLoc introduces sample classification guidance (SCG) and redundant sample downsampling (RSD) as alternatives that avoid expensive feature caching while still achieving 50× faster training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× reduction in training time (1 hour vs. ~2 days) makes regression-based LiDAR localization practical for time-sensitive deployments in autonomous driving, drones, and robotics where scene updates must be rapid. The SCG component (5-minute classification training guiding regression) and RSD (variance-based sample pruning) are lightweight, general-purpose techniques that could transfer to other large-scale regression tasks. Limitations include the dependency on a pre-trained backbone (itself requiring 2 days on nuScenes), and the approach has only been validated on three datasets with a single LiDAR type; generalizability across platforms, sensor configurations, and even larger scenes remains an open question the authors acknowledge for future work.
