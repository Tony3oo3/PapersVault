## Problem

Audio-visual segmentation faces two inherent challenges from audio's nature that prior architecture-focused methods overlook: (1) feature confusion, where overlapping frequency and temporal components of simultaneous sounds prevent reliable isolation of individual source semantics, and (2) audio-visual matching difficulty, where the same object produces highly variable sounds (e.g., different cat vocalizations) that hinder consistent cross-modal alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251269/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a mixed audio signal (encoded by HTSAT or VGGish into a single feature vector F_a) and a visual frame (encoded by PVT-V2-B5 or a transformer backbone into multi-scale spatial feature maps). It outputs either a binary mask identifying sounding regions (AVS-Objects) or a semantic mask with per-class labels (AVS-Semantic, VPO). The audio is derived from 5-second video clips or 3-second audio paired with single images, and the framework operates at 224×224 input resolution with four hierarchical fusion stages.

## Method

DDESeg introduces a Dynamic Derivation Module (DDM) that, instead of separating mixed audio, derives K distinct single-source representations by computing Generalized Laplace Operator-inspired edge features between the input audio and K nearest class centroids from a pre-built semantic memory (constructed via k-means clustering of single-source features), then applies additive compensation to bridge inter-class gaps and multiplicative scaling (element-wise) to refine intra-class discriminability. A Dynamic Elimination Module (DEM) then filters irrelevant audio tokens by soft-clustering visual features with Gumbel-Softmax, performing cross-attention between derived audio and visual centers, and applying sigmoid-scored gating to suppress audio representations lacking visual correspondence. The refined audio representations serve as queries in a multi-stage cross-attention fusion (modified from MiT/SegFormer) with visual features as keys and values, progressively aligning modalities across four hierarchical levels before a segmentation head decodes the final mask.

## Results

On AVS-Objects-S4, DDESeg (Trans. + HTSAT) achieves 94.2% J&F, surpassing the previous best CAVP (90.5%) by 3.7 points. On AVS-Objects-MS3, it reaches 77.9% J&F, a 5.2-point gain over CAVP (72.7%). On AVS-Semantic, DDESeg attains 67.9% J&F, improving over the second-best AVSSone (61.5%) by 6.4 points. On the VPO benchmark, DDESeg leads all methods, achieving 74.38% J&F on VPO-SS, 74.30% on VPO-MS, and 68.39% on VPO-MSMI, outperforming CPM* (evaluated at original resolution) by 0.9, 1.4, and 0.3 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDESeg outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every reported metric on all three benchmark suites. On AVS-Objects-S4, it leads CAVP (90.5→94.2 J&F), COMBO (88.3→94.2), and CATR (87.9→94.2), with the largest margin over TPAVI (83.3→94.2, +10.9). On AVS-Objects-MS3, the closest competitor is BiasAVS (74.0 J&F), which DDESeg surpasses by 3.9 points; CAVP (72.7) and AuTR (72.0) trail by 5.2 and 5.9 points respectively. On AVS-Semantic, the gap is most pronounced: DDESeg (67.9 J&F) exceeds AVSSone (61.5) by 6.4 points, CAVP (55.3) by 12.6 points, and COMBO (44.1) by 23.8 points. On VPO, DDESeg (74.38 J&F on SS) edges out CPM* (73.49, evaluated at original resolution) and substantially surpasses AVSSstone (68.54) by 5.8 points. Notably, DDESeg achieves these gains with a comparable parameter count (~104M) to most competitors, and swapping VGGish for HTSAT as the audio backbone yields an additional ~1.5% J improvement, confirming the benefit of stronger audio representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDESeg builds on the SegFormer/MiT [46] architecture for its hierarchical feature fusion blocks but departs fundamentally from prior AVS methods in how it handles mixed audio. While QD-Former [24] assumes independent sound events and uses quantization-based decomposition, and CPM [6] decodes fused features into separated spectrograms, DDESeg instead 'derives' distinct single-source semantic representations from the mixed signal by exploiting inter-class relationships (inspired by the Generalized Laplace Operator) and then refines them via intra-class discriminative features. The Dynamic Elimination Module further distinguishes this work by explicitly filtering out audio representations that lack visual correspondence (e.g., off-screen sounds), a concern that prior query-based or classification-thresholding approaches [27, 28, 39] address only implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.4% J&Fβ gain on AVS-Semantic and consistent improvements across all three benchmarks demonstrate that explicitly modeling audio's overlapping and intra-class variability—rather than relying on architectural interaction alone—yields meaningful segmentation gains, particularly in multi-source scenarios. The derivation-then-elimination paradigm offers a reusable recipe for any modality-guided segmentation where the guiding signal is inherently mixed and variable. Open questions include whether the semantic memory construction (requiring single-source audio per class) scales to open-vocabulary or long-tail sound categories, and whether the Generalized Laplace Operator-inspired derivation generalizes beyond audio to other overlapping signal modalities.
