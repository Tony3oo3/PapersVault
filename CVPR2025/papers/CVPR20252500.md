## Problem

Depth Anything Models (DAMs) have demonstrated strong zero-shot depth estimation on perspective images, but their performance on panoramic images (180°×360° FoV with spherical distortions) remains unexplored. The paper identifies that DAMs are highly sensitive to spherical spatial transformations (vertical rotation, zoom), that polar-region dominance degrades equator depth accuracy, and that no existing panoramic depth foundation model handles diverse open-world scenes with robustness to such transformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252500/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single equirectangular projection (ERP) panoramic image as input (trained at 504×1008, evaluated at 512×1024) and produces an affine-invariant normalized depth map of the same spatial dimensions, with depth values clipped to [0.01, 1] using 2nd/98th percentile normalization. Sky regions are explicitly masked via SegFormer and assigned the farthest depth value (1.0). The model is designed to remain robust under Möbius spherical transformations (vertical rotation up to ±90°, zoom from 0.4× to 4.0×), which is a key differentiator from standard perspective depth models.

## Method

PanDA employs a two-stage semi-supervised learning pipeline. First, a teacher model is obtained by fine-tuning DAM v2's ViT encoder with LoRA on synthetic indoor (Structured3D) and outdoor (Deep360) panoramic datasets, using a novel Equator-aware Patch Normalization Loss (EPNL) that samples patches via a Gaussian distribution centered at the equator to prevent polar regions from squeezing equatorial structural detail. Second, a student model is trained jointly on labeled data and large-scale unlabeled panoramas (ZInD, 360+x) with pseudo-labels from the teacher, where the key innovation is Möbius Transformation-based Spatial Augmentation (MTSA): unlabeled panoramas are transformed via random vertical rotation (±10°) and zoom (1.0–1.5×), and a SILog consistency loss is enforced between the student's depth prediction on the transformed image and the Möbius-transformed depth prediction on the original image. This forces the student to learn distortion-invariant feature representations, yielding substantial robustness gains under spatial transformations without sacrificing original-image accuracy.

## Results

On Matterport3D, PanDA-L achieves RMSE 0.3305 and AbsRel 0.0717, outperforming the previous best S2Net (RMSE 0.4052) by 18.4% and HRDFuse (RMSE 0.4433) by 25.5%. On Stanford2D3D, PanDA-L reaches RMSE 0.2540 and AbsRel 0.0609, surpassing HRDFuse (RMSE 0.3106) by 18.2%. In zero-shot evaluation (Table 4), PanDA reduces RMSE by 15–47% over DAM v2 under various spatial transformations, with the largest gains (47.13%) at 20° vertical rotation on Matterport3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanDA outperforms all 12 prior panoramic depth estimation methods on both Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D across every reported metric. On Matterport3D, PanDA-L (RMSE 0.3305) beats the strongest prior method S2Net (RMSE 0.4052) by 18.4% and HRDFuse (RMSE 0.4433) by 25.5%; even the smallest PanDA-S (RMSE 0.3950) surpasses S2Net. On Stanford2D3D, PanDA-L (RMSE 0.2540) improves over HRDFuse (0.3106) by 18.2% and S2Net (0.3383) by 24.9%. In the zero-shot setting (Table 4), PanDA consistently outperforms DAM v2 and Marigold, with RMSE reductions of 14–47% depending on the spatial transformation applied; the gap widens dramatically under 20° vertical rotation (47.13% for ViT-L) and zoom 3.0 (36.60%), confirming that MTSA provides substantial robustness gains that prior methods lack. Depth Anywhere [43], the closest prior SSL-based panoramic method, is outperformed by PanDA-L on both AbsRel (0.0717 vs 0.0850) and RMSE (0.3305 vs not reported but implied higher) on Matterport3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised learning paradigm of Depth Anything (DAM v1/v2) from perspective images to panoramic depth estimation, using LoRA fine-tuning to adapt the ViT backbone while preserving its zero-shot capability. It departs from prior panoramic depth methods (BiFuse, UniFuse, HRDFuse, S2Net) that primarily address spherical distortion through architecture design (biprojection, tangent patches, spherical convolutions), instead tackling the problem through data-driven augmentation (Möbius transformation-based spatial augmentation) and a targeted equator-aware normalization loss. The empirical analysis of DAMs across representations, camera positions, and spatial transformations is a new diagnostic contribution that motivates the method design, positioning PanDA as the first panoramic depth foundation model built on the DAM SSL recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Panoramic depth estimation has been constrained to indoor scenes due to the scarcity of large-scale labeled 360° data; PanDA's SSL framework with pseudo-labels from unlabeled panoramas (ZInD, 360+x) offers a practical path toward open-world generalization. The finding that ERP—despite its severe distortion—outperforms distortion-free representations challenges the prevailing assumption in the field that reducing projection distortion is always beneficial, suggesting that global semantic continuity matters more for transformer-based models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to metric (not just relative) depth, whether Möbius augmentation is optimal compared to other spherical transformations, and whether the method generalizes to higher-resolution or non-ERP panoramic formats in deployment.
