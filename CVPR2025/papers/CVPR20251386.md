## Problem

Existing MOT methods either rely on handcrafted heuristic association rules that struggle with complex scenarios (irregular motion, similar appearances) or use end-to-end query-based architectures where joint detection and tracking cause irreconcilable conflicts in the unified decoding process. The paper asks whether object association can be made fully end-to-end trainable while preserving the structural decoupling of detection and association, without resorting to elaborate matching heuristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251386/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of video frames (resized to 800×1440) and maintains a sliding window of T past frames' object-level embeddings (256-dim DETR output vectors) concatenated with 256-dim ID dictionary tokens to form 512-dim tracklets. Output consists of per-frame bounding boxes with detection confidence from DETR, plus a (K+1)-way ID classification per detection from the ID Decoder, where K=50 identity classes plus one special 'newborn' class; at inference, the highest-probability ID above a threshold is assigned, and unassigned high-confidence detections receive a new recycled ID.

## Method

MOTIP reformulates object association as in-context ID prediction: each trajectory is randomly assigned an ID label k_m, and the task becomes predicting that same label for all detections belonging to the trajectory, with the label serving as an in-context prompt rather than a fixed semantic class. The architecture comprises three components: a Deformable DETR (ResNet-50 backbone) that detects objects and provides 256-dim embeddings as tracking cues; a learnable ID dictionary of K+1 embeddings (K identity tokens + 1 special token for newborn objects) that encodes trajectory identity; and a standard transformer decoder (ID Decoder) that cross-attends current-frame tracklets (object feature ⊕ special token) against historical tracklets (object feature ⊕ ID token) to predict the correct ID via cross-entropy loss. Two trajectory augmentation strategies—random token dropout (simulating occlusion) and random ID swapping (simulating assignment errors)—are applied during training to improve robustness. Notably, self-attention layers in the ID Decoder enable implicit global optimal assignment, making the Hungarian algorithm unnecessary, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a unified loss combining focal, L1, GIoU, and ID cross-entropy terms.

## Results

On DanceTrack, MOTIP achieves 69.6 HOTA (80.4 DetA, 60.4 AssA) without extra data, surpassing the previous best CO-MOT by 4.3 HOTA and 6.9 AssA. With extra training data, it reaches 72.0 HOTA and 63.5 AssA, outperforming MOTRv2 (69.9) and CO-MOT (69.4). On SportsMOT, MOTIP scores 72.6 HOTA and 63.2 AssA, leading MeMOTR (68.8) by 3.8 HOTA. On BFT, it achieves 70.5 HOTA and 71.8 AssA, the highest association accuracy among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOTIP wins decisively across all three benchmarks. On DanceTrack, it outperforms the strongest same-detector competitor CO-MOT [61] by 4.3 HOTA (69.6 vs. 65.3) and 6.9 AssA (60.4 vs. 53.5), and also beats methods using extra training data (MOTRv2 [70] at 69.9, CO-MOT at 69.4). On SportsMOT, MOTIP leads MeMOTR [16] (same Deformable DETR backbone) by 3.8 HOTA (72.6 vs. 68.8) and 5.4 AssA (63.2 vs. 57.8), while also surpassing OC-SORT [5] (71.9 with extra data) on AssA. On BFT, MOTIP achieves 70.5 HOTA and 71.8 AssA, beating OC-SORT [5] (66.8 HOTA) by 3.7 HOTA and CenterTrack [72] (65.0) by 5.5 HOTA. Heuristic tracking-by-detection methods (ByteTrack [69], FairMOT [68], SORT [3]) are consistently outperformed by 7–30 HOTA points across all datasets, confirming the advantage of end-to-end learnable association over handcrafted matching rules.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOTIP builds on Deformable DETR as a frozen-architecture detector but departs fundamentally from both major MOT paradigms. Unlike tracking-by-detection methods (SORT, ByteTrack, OC-SORT) that rely on handcrafted heuristics (Kalman filters, Hungarian matching, ReID cosine similarity) for association, and unlike tracking-by-propagation methods (TrackFormer, MOTR, CO-MOT) that extend DETR with track queries and suffer from detection-tracking conflicts, MOTIP maintains strict decoupling of detection and association while making the association step fully end-to-end trainable. The key conceptual departure is reframing object association as an in-context ID prediction problem—where the model predicts a label conditioned on historically assigned ID tokens—rather than as a fixed-label classification or a similarity-matching problem, thereby resolving the generalization dilemma that forces ReID-based methods to switch from classification loss at training to cosine similarity at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The in-context ID prediction framing offers a clean, generalizable formulation of association that avoids both heuristic brittleness and the detection-tracking conflicts plaguing unified query-based trackers, and its simplicity (standard transformer decoder + learnable ID dictionary) leaves substantial room for future enhancements such as richer tracking cues, tailored decoder layers, or more sophisticated trajectory modeling. The paper acknowledges that the ID dictionary capacity K may be insufficient in extremely crowded scenes and that only object-level features are used as tracking cues, leaving motion, depth, and other modalities unexplored. As a deliberately minimal baseline that already achieves SOTA across three challenging benchmarks, it is well-positioned to become a reference architecture for subsequent MOT research.
