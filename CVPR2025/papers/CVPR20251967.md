## Problem

Diffusion models for large-image domains (digital pathology, satellite imagery) are limited to generating small fixed-size patches that lack global context, or produce blurry gigapixel-scale images without fine detail. No existing method can simultaneously synthesize coherent, high-fidelity images across multiple magnification levels while remaining computationally tractable, and the absence of paired annotations in these domains further complicates conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251967/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes variable-size SSL embedding matrices (e.g., 4x4x1024 for a 5x patch, pooled to 8x8 for magnifications at or below 1.25x) extracted by a 20x-specific encoder (UNI), together with a scalar magnification level, and produces fixed 256x256 RGB patches at the target zoom. For unconditional synthesis, a Conditioning Diffusion Model (CDM) replaces real embeddings with sampled latents; for super-resolution, a low-resolution image plus magnification triggers an embedding-inversion step before joint multi-scale sampling. The joint sampling pipeline outputs coherent images up to 4096x4096 pixels by simultaneously denoising patches at four nested scales.

## Method

ZoomLDM introduces a cross-magnification latent space implemented via a 12-layer ViT-Base-style Summarizer transformer that compresses variable-length SSL embedding arrays (paired with a learned magnification embedding) into a fixed-size conditioning vector, which is fed to a VQ-f4 + U-Net latent diffusion model trained jointly across all eight magnifications. Sharing denoiser weights across scales lets data-scarce low-magnification patches (as few as 2,500 training examples) benefit from high-magnification data, yielding large FID improvements at lower scales. A separate Conditioning Diffusion Model (a Diffusion Transformer) learns the distribution of the Summarizer's output, enabling novel image synthesis without access to real embeddings. For large-image and super-resolution generation, the paper proposes a joint multi-scale sampling algorithm that extends constrained diffusion inference: it uses the so-far-generated coarser-scale image as a self-guidance measurement and replaces backpropagation-based error computation with a finite-difference approximation through the VAE encoder/decoder, enabling tractable 4096x4096 synthesis in approximately 8 minutes.

## Results

ZoomLDM achieves state-of-the-art FID across all eight histopathology magnifications, with particularly dramatic gains at low scales: FID 13.42 at 0.15625× vs. 106.14 for single-scale baselines, and 8.74 at 1.25× vs. 39.72. For 1024×1024 large-image generation, ZoomLDM achieves CLIP FID 1.23 (vs. 7.43 for Graikos et al. and 3.74 for ∞-Brush) in 28 seconds, and generates 4096×4096 images in 8 minutes (vs. 4h and 12h for prior methods). In 4× super-resolution, ZoomLDM with inferred embeddings achieves the best LPIPS (0.229) and UNI patch similarity (0.719) on TCGA-BRCA. For MIL, multi-scale ZoomLDM features (20×+5×) reach 94.91 AUC for BRCA subtyping and 88.03 AUC for HRD prediction, surpassing all SoTA SSL encoders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Graikos et al. [17] (SSL-embedding-guided patch diffusion), ZoomLDM wins on FID at every magnification (e.g., 7.98 vs. 9.74 at 5×) and on large-image generation (CLIP FID 1.23 vs. 7.43 at 1024×1024), while also being faster (28s vs. 60s per image). Against ∞-Brush [26] (infinite-dimensional diffusion), ZoomLDM achieves substantially lower CLIP FID at 1024×1024 (1.23 vs. 3.74) and completes 4096×4096 generation in 8 minutes versus 12 hours, though ∞-Brush retains a slight edge on Crop FID at 4096×4096 (14.76 vs. 18.90). Against PathLDM [47] (text-conditioned histopathology LDM), ZoomLDM improves FID at 10× (7.60 vs. 7.64) and dominates at all lower scales. In super-resolution, ZoomLDM with inferred embeddings outperforms both CompVis [38] (LPIPS 0.229 vs. 0.247) and ControlNet [48] (LPIPS 0.229 vs. 0.252) on TCGA-BRCA, and also beats bicubic interpolation on perceptual metrics. For MIL, multi-scale ZoomLDM features (94.91 AUC subtyping, 88.03 AUC HRD) surpass UNI [8] (94.09, 81.79), Phikon [14] (93.81, 76.88), and CTransPath [45] (93.11, 85.37), with even single-scale 20× ZoomLDM features (94.49, 85.25) exceeding all three encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZoomLDM extends the SSL-embedding-conditioned diffusion framework of Graikos et al. [17] (which generated only single-scale patches) into a unified multi-scale model via a novel cross-magnification latent space and a trainable Summarizer module. It builds on the constrained-sampling algorithm of Graikos et al. [16] to perform joint multi-scale generation, and departs from Harb et al. [18] (scale-conditioned but unconditioned) and Le et al. [26] (resolution-free but blurry) by combining scale-aware conditioning with a shared latent space that captures cross-magnification dependencies. The Conditioning Diffusion Model (CDM) further extends the pipeline by learning to sample from the Summarizer's output distribution, enabling novel synthesis without access to reference embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZoomLDM provides the first practical framework for generating 4096×4096 histopathology images with both local detail and global coherence in under 10 minutes, addressing a long-standing gap where prior methods produced either contextually incoherent patches or blurry global structures. The finding that intermediate features from a generative diffusion model outperform dedicated SSL encoders (UNI, Phikon, CTransPath) on MIL tasks opens an interesting question about whether generative pretraining yields more informative representations than contrastive/masked pretraining. Limitations include worse 4096×4096 FID scores than baselines (mitigated by qualitative differences), evaluation primarily on a single pathology dataset, and the CDM's slight quality degradation relative to real-embedding conditioning. Open questions include whether the cross-magnification latent space generalizes to other large-image domains (satellite, microscopy) and whether text or molecular (RNA) conditioning can be integrated through the CDM.
