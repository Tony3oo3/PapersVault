## Problem

Existing 3D human-object interaction (HOI) synthesis methods either require training on curated HOI datasets and thus cannot generalize to open-set objects, or rely on optimization-based approaches that fail to produce precise contact and physically plausible interactions. The core challenge is generating novel, detailed, and geometrically accurate interactions for arbitrary unseen objects from a simple text description in a zero-shot manner, without dataset-specific training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short text prompt describing the desired interaction (e.g., 'A person grasps the chair') and an arbitrary 3D object mesh as input, with no requirement for object category labels or part annotations. It outputs a complete 3D HOI scene consisting of a SMPL-H human mesh (body pose θ_b, MANO hand poses θ_h, shape β, and global translation) and the object mesh with optimized rotation, translation, and scale. The object mesh can originate from any source—curated datasets like BEHAVE or PartNet, or generative models—making the pipeline truly open-set.

## Method

InteractAnything decomposes zero-shot HOI synthesis into four stages. First, an LLM (GPT) infers human-object spatial relations (relative position, orientation, scale, ground state) by selecting from predefined options to minimize hallucination, initializing object properties and identifying interacted body parts. Second, open-set object affordance parsing uses SDXL-based 2D inpainting with LLM-generated adaptive masks (full-body and body-part level) to produce inpainted images, from which a distance-based 2D probability function computes per-vertex contact probabilities aggregated across multiple views to yield a 3D affordance map. Third, initial human pose is synthesized via multi-view SDS loss with the object mesh embedded in NeRF as a spatial constraint to prevent penetration. Fourth, a coarse-to-fine optimization refines the interaction: a global stage updates object pose using weighted chamfer distance, normal alignment, and penetration losses, while a fine-grained stage optimizes SMPL-H parameters using a differentiable force closure loss adapted from MANO to SMPL-H, with sparse contact sampling and separate interacted/non-interacted mesh handling for robustness.

## Results

On the GPT-4V selection evaluation (higher is better), InteractAnything achieves 45.6/100 Overall and 52.1/100 Contact, substantially outperforming DreamHOI (26.0 / 19.5), DreamFusion* (17.3 / 15.2), DreamFusion (6.5 / 4.3), and Magic3D (4.3 / 8.6). On CLIP similarity score, the method reaches 0.2968, compared to 0.2757 for DreamHOI, 0.2437 for DreamFusion, and 0.2416 for Magic3D. The largest margin is on the Contact metric, where InteractAnything nearly triples DreamHOI's score (52.1 vs. 19.5), reflecting the paper's emphasis on fine-grained hand-object contact accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InteractAnything outperforms all four baselines on both Overall and Contact GPT-4V scores. Against the closest competitor DreamHOI [67], the method gains +19.6 points on Overall (45.6 vs. 26.0) and +32.6 points on Contact (52.1 vs. 19.5), indicating a decisive advantage in generating physically plausible contact. The re-implemented DreamFusion* variant (which embeds the object mesh into the diffusion pipeline) scores 17.3 Overall and 15.2 Contact, still well below InteractAnything, confirming that simply adding the object to SDS is insufficient for accurate interaction. Magic3D and vanilla DreamFusion, which lack any explicit HOI mechanism, score near the bottom (≤8.6 on both metrics), underscoring that general-purpose text-to-3D models cannot capture human-object contact. On CLIP score, the margin is smaller (0.2968 vs. 0.2757 for DreamHOI), consistent with the authors' observation that CLIP is less sensitive to fine interaction details than GPT-4V-based evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-3D generation paradigm (DreamFusion's SDS, MVDream's multi-view SDS) to the human-object interaction domain, where prior methods like InterFusion and DreamHOI struggle with precise contact on open-set objects. It departs from data-driven HOI approaches (OMOMO, InterDiff) that require object-specific training by instead distilling interaction knowledge from LLMs and 2D diffusion inpainting models in a zero-shot manner. The force-closure-based fine-grained optimization adapts the differentiable force closure framework from robotic grasping (Liu et al., RA-L 2021) to the SMPL-H body model, and the adaptive inpainting pipeline builds on the dynamic mask approach from Kim et al. (ECCV 2024) but introduces LLM-guided mask generation to improve success rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot HOI synthesis for arbitrary objects is practically critical for AR/VR, animation, and simulation pipelines where lab-captured data is infeasible. The paper's key limitation is its reliance on 2D priors (diffusion inpainting, OpenPose) as the primary geometric guidance, which constrains multi-view consistency and physical plausibility; the authors acknowledge that advances in multi-view or 4D generation would strengthen the approach. Additionally, the SMPL-H representation restricts the framework to human anatomy, leaving non-human interaction agents unaddressed. Open questions include whether the LLM-guided initialization can be replaced by learned priors at scale, and whether the force-closure constraint can be validated against physics simulators for manipulation tasks.
