## Problem

Markush structures in patent documents combine a visual backbone (atoms, bonds, variable regions) with textual definitions of substituents, yet existing methods treat these modalities separately—image-only OCSR models miss frequency and positional variation indicators, while text-only classifiers ignore their spatial context in the backbone. No public benchmark of real-world Markush structures or training dataset exists, leaving the joint multi-modal recognition task largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251751/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a Markush structure image (backbone plus any accompanying text description), externally extracted OCR text cells, and their bounding boxes as three interdependent modalities. It autoregressively generates a single text sequence containing an optimized CXSMILES (with variable groups inlined as special atoms, per-atom indices, and a dedicated atom/bond vocabulary) followed by a free-text substituent table that groups variable groups sharing identical definitions. The optimized format shortens the attention window and explicitly links SMILES atoms to the CXSMILES extension sections for 'm' and 'Sg' indicators.

## Method

MarkushGrapher is a T5-large encoder-decoder (831M parameters) that fuses two parallel encoders: a Vision-Text-Layout (VTL) encoder following the UDOP scheme, which merges image-patch embeddings with text-token embeddings at bounding-box locations, and a frozen OCSR vision encoder (MolScribe) that provides robust chemical-structure features. The two encodings are concatenated via late fusion and fed to a text decoder that autoregressively predicts the optimized CXSMILES and substituent table. Because no real training data exist, the authors build a synthetic pipeline that samples PubChem molecules, augments them into CXSMILES with RDKit, renders diverse images with CDK (random fonts, bond widths, aromaticity styles), extracts OCR cells from the SVG output, and generates chemically valid text descriptions from manually curated patent templates, with 10% paraphrased by Mistral-7B. The model is trained for 10 epochs on 210K synthetic samples, and ablations confirm that the OCSR encoder contributes most on backbone-only images while the VTL encoder enables cross-modal inference such as deducing bond order from variable-group valence constraints.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (MarkushGrapher-Synthetic, 1000 samples), MarkushGrapher achieves 65% CXSMILES exact match, 96% Tanimoto score, 84% table exact match, and 57% overall Markush exact match. On the real-world M2S benchmark (103 samples), it reaches 38% CXSMILES EM, 76% Tanimoto, 29% table EM, and 10% Markush EM. On USPTO-Markush (74 samples), it achieves 32% CXSMILES EM and 74% Tanimoto. The model substantially outperforms all image-only OCSR methods and general-purpose VLMs on CXSMILES exact match across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against image-only OCSR baselines, MarkushGrapher achieves 38% CXSMILES EM on M2S versus 21% for MolScribe and 3% for DECIMER, and 32% versus 7% for both on USPTO-Markush. Notably, MolScribe achieves a higher Tanimoto score on USPTO-Markush (97 vs. 74), reflecting its stronger recognition of standard chemical substructures when Markush-specific features are disregarded. Against general-purpose VLMs (GPT-4o, Pixtral-12B, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision), MarkushGrapher dominates on CXSMILES EM (38% vs. 0–4% on M2S) and table recognition (29% EM / 65% F1 vs. 19% EM / 49% F1 for GPT-4o). Uni-SMART, despite being trained on Markush-related tasks, achieves 0% CXSMILES EM on M2S, confirming it cannot explicitly extract Markush structures. The only metric where a baseline outperforms MarkushGrapher is the USPTO-Markush Tanimoto score, where MolScribe's 97% reflects robustness to standard molecular substructures in images lacking textual descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MarkushGrapher unifies two previously separate lines of work: OCSR models (DECIMER, MolScribe) that recognize molecular structure images, and document understanding models (UDOP, LayoutLMv3) that jointly encode vision, text, and layout. Prior chemistry-specific approaches either handled only the Markush backbone image (recognizing R-groups as abbreviations but ignoring frequency and positional variation indicators) or only the textual variable-group definitions, with toolkits like CliDE and OpenChemIE combining separate models post-hoc. The key departure is the joint encoder-decoder architecture that processes both modalities simultaneously, exploiting cross-modal dependencies (e.g., text definitions informing bond inference in the image) that separate pipelines cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to perform unified multi-modal Markush structure recognition, and the first public benchmark (M2S) and synthetic training pipeline for the task, addressing a critical gap in automated patent analysis and prior-art search where existing databases (CAS MARPAT, WIPO Patentscope) are proprietary and manually curated. The strong synthetic-to-real generalization (trained only on synthetic data, evaluated on real patents) is encouraging, but absolute performance remains modest (38% EM on M2S, 32% on USPTO-Markush), and frequency variation indicators ('Sg' sections) are still poorly recognized (31% on M2S). Open questions include scaling to full patent documents with hundreds of Markush structures, handling the full diversity of real-world annotation conventions, and whether the approach transfers to other structure-template formalisms beyond CXSMILES.
