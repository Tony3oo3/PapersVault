## Problem

Generating animatable 4D head avatars from a single image without large-scale human data remains challenging. While score distillation sampling (SDS) with pre-trained diffusion models enables data-free 3D/4D generation, directly distilling from video diffusion produces over-smooth avatars due to inherent spatial and temporal inconsistencies in the generated video. Image-conditioned generation for realistic, animatable head avatars is also largely underexplored compared to text-conditioned approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251228/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image (supporting realism, comic, and cartoon styles) and produces an animatable 4D head avatar represented as 3D Gaussians rigged to the FLAME mesh, renderable at 512×512 resolution in real-time (~76 FPS on a single GPU). The avatar is driven by FLAME pose and expression parameters or facial landmark maps, enabling arbitrary animation. Training takes approximately 5 hours on one NVIDIA A6000 (48 GB) GPU with 10,000 iterations.

## Method

Zero-1-to-A replaces direct SDS distillation with a reconstruction-based pipeline called SymGEN (Symbiotic Generation), which iteratively synthesizes a spatially and temporally consistent pseudo ground-truth dataset using a pre-trained video diffusion model (Follow-your-emoji) and trains the animatable Gaussian head via supervised L1 + LPIPS + position + scaling losses. The key mechanism is an iterative mutual enhancement loop: the current avatar renders videos, which are refined via DDIM inversion guided by Mediapipe facial landmarks and fed back into the dataset, while the updated dataset in turn improves the avatar—creating a self-reinforcing cycle every 30 iterations. To break the initial chicken-and-egg problem, a Progressive Learning strategy decouples training into two stages: Spatial Consistency Learning (fixed expression, camera sweeping from frontal to side views) followed by Temporal Consistency Learning (fixed camera, expressions progressing from relaxed synthetic to exaggerated real-world sequences), ensuring stable initialization and smooth quality escalation from simple to complex scenarios.

## Results

On the static head avatar generation task, Zero-1-to-A achieves a CLIP-Score of 0.285 (ViT-L/14), 0.320 (ViT-B/16), and 0.322 (ViT-B/32), outperforming the strongest head-specialized baseline HeadStudio (0.275 / 0.322 / 0.317) by 0.010 on ViT-L/14 and 0.005 on ViT-B/32. For dynamic avatars, the method achieves 76 FPS rendering speed versus 48 FPS for HeadStudio on the same hardware, while producing fewer animation artifacts (e.g., mouth expression distortions). The paper does not report a single unified quantitative benchmark for dynamic avatars; dynamic comparisons are primarily qualitative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against seven static avatar generation baselines, Zero-1-to-A wins on ViT-L/14 (0.285 vs. HeadStudio's 0.275, the closest competitor) and ViT-B/32 (0.322 vs. 0.317), while tying ProlificDreamer on ViT-B/16 (both 0.320) and slightly trailing HeadStudio (0.322). The gains over head-specialized methods (HeadSculpt, HeadArtist, HeadStudio) are modest (0.005–0.013) but consistent across all three CLIP variants. For dynamic avatars, the paper qualitatively shows Zero-1-to-A outperforming both TADA and HeadStudio in animation fidelity (fewer mouth/eye artifacts) and rendering speed (76 FPS vs. 48 FPS for HeadStudio on the Geralt example). Compared to portrait video diffusion methods (Follow-your-emoji, AniPortrait), SymGEN produces superior 3D spatial consistency (correct eye positioning in side views) and temporal smoothness (stable adjacent frames under minor expression changes), as shown in Fig. 7. The ablation study confirms that removing progressive learning degrades expression control, and removing either spatial or temporal consistency learning introduces specific failure modes (misaligned eyes or mouth deformation artifacts, respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the authors' prior HeadStudio work for the animatable Gaussian head representation (FLAME + 3DGS) and on recent portrait video diffusion models (Follow-your-emoji, AniPortrait, EchoMimic) as the generative prior. It departs from the dominant SDS-based paradigm (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer, HeadArtist) by replacing score distillation with an iterative reconstruction pipeline: video diffusion synthesizes a pseudo-ground-truth dataset, and the avatar is trained on that dataset via L1/LPIPS losses. The key conceptual departure is the 'symbiotic generation' loop—where the avatar's own renderings feed back into DDIM-inverted video diffusion to refine the dataset—combined with a progressive learning schedule that decouples spatial (front-to-side) and temporal (relaxed-to-exaggerated) consistency, addressing the 'chicken-or-egg' instability of naive iterative updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, data-free recipe for generating animatable 4D head avatars from a single image at real-time rendering speed (76 FPS), which is directly applicable to AR/VR, virtual assistants, and content creation. The progressive learning strategy is a generalizable insight: starting from simple, consistent cases and gradually introducing complexity is a natural way to bootstrap quality when the generative prior is noisy. However, the method is constrained by the FLAME mesh alignment, limiting it to the head region (hair, accessories, and full-body elements are poorly modeled), and the CLIP-score gains over HeadStudio are modest (~0.01), suggesting the fidelity ceiling is still set by the underlying video diffusion prior rather than the reconstruction framework.
