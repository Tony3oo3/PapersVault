## Problem

Existing 3D self-supervised learning approaches for point clouds suffer from a 'geometric shortcut' where representations collapse to trivial low-level spatial cues (surface normals, point height) rather than learning semantic structure, because point cloud operators inherently consume point coordinates that cannot be masked. This results in poor linear probing performance (at most 21.8% mIoU on ScanNet vs. 77.6% from scratch), making current 3D SSL models unreliable for downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes scene-level 3D point clouds (coordinates plus optional features) and produces multi-scale point-level feature embeddings from the PTv3 encoder at progressively coarser spatial resolutions. Features are up-cast back to the original point density via a parameter-free hypercolumn-style concatenation before being fed to a single linear layer (<0.2% of total parameters) for semantic or instance segmentation, or to a lightweight 13%-parameter hierarchical decoder for probing. Training views include 2 global crops (40–100% of scene points), 4 local crops (5–40%), and 2 masked views derived from global views.

## Method

Sonata is a point self-distillation framework built on a decoder-free Point Transformer V3 encoder (108M params), using an EMA teacher-student setup with Sinkhorn-Knopp centering, KoLeo regularization, and clustering assignments (following DINOv2). To combat the geometric shortcut, it applies SSL losses at coarser spatial scales (removing the U-Net decoder), applies stronger Gaussian jitter (σ=0.01) specifically to masked points to disrupt their spatial relationships, and uses a progressive parameter scheduler that gradually increases mask size (10→40 cm), mask ratio (30%→70%), teacher temperature (0.04→0.07), and weight decay (0.04→0.2) over the first 5% of training. A parameter-free feature up-casting step (applied twice) recombines multi-scale encoder features via pooling-layer mapping and concatenation, recovering coarse-to-fine context without reintroducing fine-grained geometric shortcuts. The model is trained on 140k point clouds (86.7× the data of PointContrast) for 200 epochs with AdamW, cosine LR schedule, and layer-wise LR decay of 0.9.

## Results

With linear probing (<0.2% learnable parameters), Sonata achieves 72.5% mIoU on ScanNet semantic segmentation, a 3.3× improvement over the previous SOTA MSC (21.8%), and surpasses DINOv2 image features aggregated onto point clouds (63.1%). Decoder probing (13% parameters) reaches 79.1% on ScanNet and 81.5% on S3DIS 6-fold. Full fine-tuning sets new SOTA across indoor benchmarks (79.4% ScanNet, 82.3% S3DIS 6-fold) and outdoor LiDAR benchmarks (81.7% nuScenes, 72.9% Waymo, 72.6% SemanticKITTI). In extreme data-scarce settings (1% of ScanNet scenes), Sonata's linear probing reaches 43.6% mIoU versus 25.8% from scratch, a 19.5-point gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sonata decisively outperforms all prior point-cloud SSL methods on linear probing: MSC (21.8% → 72.5% on ScanNet, +50.7 pts), CSC (12.6% → 72.5%, +59.9 pts), and PointContrast (5.6% → 72.5%, +66.9 pts), confirming the geometric shortcut hypothesis. Against DINOv2 image features projected onto 3D (63.1% mIoU), Sonata's linear probing gains +9.4 pts, and combining both modalities reaches 76.4%, indicating complementary 3D and 2D information. On full fine-tuning, Sonata surpasses PPT (supervised multi-dataset pretraining) by +0.8 pts on ScanNet and +1.5 pts on S3DIS 6-fold, and outperforms MSC by +1.2 pts on ScanNet. On outdoor LiDAR, Sonata's full fine-tuning beats PPT by +0.5, +0.8, and +0.3 mIoU on nuScenes, Waymo, and SemanticKITTI respectively. The one area where Sonata's representation shows a limitation is high-class-count benchmarks: decoder probing on ScanNet200 (33.5%) and ScanNet++ (40.9%) still trails the full PTv3 fine-tuned baseline (35.3% and 42.1%), suggesting the learned features struggle to discriminate among 100–200 fine-grained classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sonata extends the 3D point cloud self-supervised learning lineage initiated by PointContrast and Masked Scene Contrast (MSC), but fundamentally departs from them by diagnosing a 'geometric shortcut'—a collapse to low-level spatial cues unique to sparse point cloud data—as the root cause of weak representations. Methodologically, it transplants the DINOv2 self-distillation framework (Sinkhorn-Knopp centering, KoLeo regularization, EMA teacher-student) from 2D images to 3D points, while replacing the standard U-Net backbone with a decoder-free Point Transformer V3 encoder. The data scaling strategy builds on PPT's multi-dataset joint training but removes the human-labeling constraint, expanding from ~6.6k to 140k scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3D SSL model whose linear probing performance (72.5% mIoU on ScanNet) approaches full fine-tuning from scratch (77.6%), closing a gap that had persisted since PointContrast. The geometric shortcut diagnosis provides a clear, actionable explanation for why 3D SSL lagged behind 2D SSL, and the fix (coarser-scale losses, masked-point jitter, progressive difficulty) is simple enough to be widely adopted. Open questions remain: performance on high-class-count benchmarks (ScanNet200, ScanNet++) still lags, full fine-tuning is still needed for best results, and the paper identifies but does not yet realize cross-modal distillation with DINOv2 or unified indoor/outdoor training as promising next steps.
