## Problem

Existing 3D character agents are limited to text or voice-only interaction, and modular LLM-Agent pipelines that chain ASR, motion captioning, and text-to-motion introduce both information loss (subtle body-language nuances are lost in text intermediaries) and substantial latency that undermines natural real-time communication. No end-to-end model exists that jointly perceives user speech and body motion to generate coordinated character speech and body motion responses in an immersive setting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes user speech audio (tokenized via SpeechTokenizer into semantic tokens from the first RVQ layer), user body motion captured by a VR headset's full-body tracking system and retargeted to SMPL-X joint rotations (tokenized by separate VQ-VAEs for body, hands, and inter-character relative transform), and a character text description as a system prompt. It produces character speech (decoded via SoundStorm with instance voice cloning from a 4–6 s prompt), character body motion as SMPL-X joint rotation sequences, and facial animation parameters (generated post-hoc by UniTalker from the speech). The interaction is formulated as multi-turn auto-regressive generation where each round's motion and speech tokens are interleaved in a single token sequence.

## Method

SOLAMI is a decoder-only LLM (AnyGPT-base on LLaMA2-7B) that treats speech and motion as new languages appended to the text vocabulary, enabling a single model to perceive and generate both modalities in an end-to-end fashion. Three separate VQ-VAE tokenizers (body, hand, inter-character transform) discretize SMPL-X joint rotations into sequential tokens, while SpeechTokenizer provides semantic speech tokens, reducing inference cost. Training follows a three-stage strategy: (1) tokenizer pre-training with reconstruction/embedding/commitment/velocity losses, (2) multi-task pre-training on 46 K motion-text pairs, 11 K interactive motion pairs, 410 K speech-text pairs, and 100 K speech dialogue pairs to align modalities with language, and (3) supervised instruction tuning on 5.7 K multi-turn multimodal conversation items from the synthesized SynMSI dataset, where only the character's response tokens are supervised. The SynMSI dataset (6.3 K items) is generated by a pipeline that uses GPT-4o to write dialogue scripts conditioned on 5.3 K curated topics, retrieves matching motions from a 46 K motion database via text-embedding similarity, and refines speech to coordinate with retrieved motions before TTS synthesis.

## Results

SOLAMI (full params) achieves the best motion quality across all four motion metrics: FID 3.443, Diversity 8.853, PA-MPJPE 151.5 mm, and Angle Error 0.360°, outperforming the DLP (MotionGPT) baseline (FID 4.254, PA-MPJPE 165.1 mm, Angle Error 0.495°). For speech, SOLAMI attains the highest VC Similarity (0.824) and Context Relevance (3.634/5), while achieving the lowest inference latency among motion-capable methods at 2.639 s (vs. 5.518 s for DLP). In the VR user study (60 participants), SOLAMI scores highest across all four subjective dimensions (Motion Coherence, Motion Interaction, Speech Consistency, Overall Experience).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOLAMI (full params) outperforms the DLP (MotionGPT) LLM-Agent baseline on every motion metric (FID 3.443 vs. 4.254; PA-MPJPE 151.5 vs. 165.1 mm; Angle Error 0.360° vs. 0.495°) and reduces inference latency by more than half (2.639 s vs. 5.518 s), confirming the advantage of end-to-end VLA over modular text-intermediary pipelines. Against the speech-only baselines (LLM+Speech and AnyGPT fine-tune), SOLAMI achieves higher Context Relevance (3.634 vs. 3.527 and 3.502) and VC Similarity (0.824 vs. 0.818 and 0.819), benefiting from the additional motion modality that captures user body language. The sole metric where SOLAMI trails is Character Consistency (3.824 vs. 3.859 for LLM+Speech), which the authors attribute to the multimodal training partially overwriting character-knowledge tokens in the LLM backbone—a degradation pattern also observed in PaliGemma and VILA. The ablation without pre-training (SOLAMI w/o pretrain) degrades substantially on motion (FID 5.052) and character consistency (3.461), underscoring the importance of the multi-task alignment stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOLAMI departs from the dominant LLM-Agent modular architecture for 3D character interaction (exemplified by DLP [13], which chains ASR → LLM → TTS/motion-generation via text instructions) by proposing an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action formulation borrowed from robotics (RT-1, OpenVLA, PaLM-E). It repurposes existing motion tokenizers (VQ-VAE à la MotionGPT/MotionPT) and speech tokenizers (SpeechTokenizer/AnyGPT) as new 'languages' within a single decoder-only LLM, unifying what were previously separate sub-modules. The SynMSI dataset is novel in that no prior work had constructed a large-scale multimodal (speech + body motion) social interaction dataset; the synthesis pipeline leverages existing motion-text corpora (HumanML3D, Inter-X, DLP-MoCap) and GPT-4o to fill this gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that an end-to-end VLA model can handle the full social interaction loop (perceive user speech + body language → generate character speech + body motion) with lower latency than modular pipelines, validating the robotics-inspired analogy that digital avatars are 'virtual humanoid robots.' Key limitations include the small instruction-tuning set (5.7K items), reliance on a single 7B backbone, and a slight degradation in character consistency when multimodal tokens are added. Open questions include whether full-duplex streaming interaction is achievable, how the approach scales to more diverse character behaviors and longer conversations, and whether the data synthesis pipeline can be extended to richer interaction scenarios (e.g., multi-character, spatial reasoning).
