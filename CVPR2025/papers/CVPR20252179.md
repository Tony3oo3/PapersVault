## Problem

Current VLMs for segmentation are unreliable under complex real-world instructions involving multi-object references, reasoning, hallucinated objects, multi-granularity, and part-level references. The authors identify the root cause as a mismatch between the rich, nuanced language humans use to describe scenes and the oversimplified, single-object references in existing grounding training datasets, which limits models' ability to translate complex instructions into precise pixel-level localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252179/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image (sourced from COCO 2017) paired with a free-form text instruction as input and produces one or more pixel-level segmentation masks as output, each linked to a specific object name. For hallucination-mitigation cases, the correct output is an empty mask accompanied by a text explanation of why no segmentation should be produced. Instructions can reference multiple objects simultaneously (averaging ~10 objects per instruction in the multi-object subset), abstract categories (e.g., 'pets'), specific parts (e.g., 'the button on the microwave'), or non-existent objects/attributes/relations.

## Method

The core contribution is an automated, scalable data generation pipeline that uses knowledge distillation from Claude 3 Sonnet to produce 500K instruction-segmentation pairs (Ground-V) across five challenging dimensions: multi-granularity, multi-object, hallucination, part reference, and reasoning. For each dimension, the authors manually craft 3-shot in-context demonstrations, then prompt Claude to generate new instruction-response pairs conditioned on COCO 2017 images, linking generated instructions to existing segmentation masks and bounding boxes. A two-stage validation (Claude 3.5 Sonnet as judge, followed by dual human annotation) ensures test-set quality, removing 23.1% of initially generated test samples. The resulting dataset is then simply appended to the existing training data of LISA and PSALM without any architectural changes, demonstrating that data scaling with richer instruction complexity is sufficient to elicit pixel-level grounding under complex instructions.

## Results

On gRefCOCO, PSALM-G5 achieves 70.6% gIoU and 83.7% N-Acc on average, surpassing the previous SOTA (PSALM at 50.1% gIoU / 24.5% N-Acc) by 20.5% in gIoU and 59.2% in N-Acc. On the standard RefCOCO/+/g benchmarks, PSALM-G5 achieves 77.3% average cIoU, marginally ahead of PSALM (77.1%) and clearly ahead of specialist models like UNINEXT-H (76.6%). On the Ground-V test set, PSALM-G5 gains 10–20% over PSALM across all five challenge dimensions (e.g., +20.8% gIoU on abstract multi-granularity, +19.2% on reasoning). On D³, PSALM-G5 reaches 58.5% gIoU (+9.0%) and 66.0% N-Acc (+23.5%) over PSALM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSALM-G5 decisively outperforms PSALM on gRefCOCO, gaining +20.5% gIoU (70.6 vs 50.1) and +59.2% N-Acc (83.7 vs 24.5), with the N-Acc jump indicating far better hallucination rejection. Against the strongest VLM baseline GSVA, PSALM-G5 leads by +5.7% gIoU and +20.9% N-Acc, and also surpasses specialist GRES models (ReLA: 64.9 gIoU / 57.9 N-Acc; LAVT: 60.0 / 49.0) by a wide margin. On the standard RefCOCO/+/g benchmarks (Table 3), PSALM-G5 (77.3% avg cIoU) edges out PSALM (77.1%) and UNINEXT-H (76.6%), while LISA-G5 (66.6%) improves over LISA (64.4%) but remains below specialist models. On the Ground-V test set, PSALM-G5 beats PSALM by +20.8% gIoU on abstract multi-granularity, +19.2% on reasoning, +13.4% N-Acc on hallucination, and +12.2% on multi-object, confirming gains across all five designed challenge dimensions. Even after fine-tuning on gRefCOCO, PSALM-G5 (ft) maintains its lead over PSALM (ft) with 74.5% gIoU vs 71.8% cIoU and 89.2% vs 77.1% N-Acc.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a data-centric complement to the architecture-centric line of work on VLMs for segmentation (LISA, PSALM, GSVA, GLaMM, PixelLM). Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it argues that the bottleneck for complex-instruction grounding is the oversimplicity of existing training data, and draws an analogy to the data-scaling paradigm that drove progress in NLP instruction tuning (Self-Instruct, scaling instruction-finetuned LMs). The generation pipeline repurposes knowledge distillation from a teacher VLM (Claude 3 Sonnet) to automatically produce instruction-segmentation pairs linked to existing COCO annotations, extending the ReasonSeg and MUSE datasets by systematically covering five real-world challenges (multi-object, multi-granularity, hallucination, reasoning, part reference) that prior datasets addressed only in isolation or not at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ground-V provides a 500K-scale, plug-and-play dataset that practitioners can add to existing VLM segmentation training pipelines without architectural modifications, yielding immediate and substantial gains (up to 20% in N-Acc for hallucination detection on gRefCOCO). The finding that even a generalist VLM like LLaVA benefits from the hallucination subset on standard VQA benchmarks suggests the data generation recipe has broader applicability beyond segmentation. Key open questions include mitigating the acknowledged noise from automatic generation by off-the-shelf models, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D grounding, and whether the gains saturate at larger model scales. The paper's limitation section is brief, leaving the noise-mitigation problem largely unexplored.
