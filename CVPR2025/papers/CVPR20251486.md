## Problem

Existing egocentric VideoQA benchmarks (EgoSchema, EgoTaskQA, EgoThink) contain questions answerable from a single frame or commonsense reasoning alone, with state-of-the-art MLLMs achieving 41–51% accuracy on just one frame. This means these datasets fail to measure genuine temporal understanding across video sequences, leaving a critical gap in evaluating whether MLLMs can actually reason over temporal dynamics in egocentric settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251486/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes egocentric video clips (3–140 s, 45 s average) from Ego4D, sampled at variable frame rates (S = 0.1 to 1.5 FPS, or fixed counts of 1–64 frames), paired with a free-form text question. The model produces a free-form text answer, which is scored by an LLM judge (Gemini 1.5 Pro) that marks each prediction correct or incorrect with reasoning. The 500 test questions span 10 categories (5 action-based, 5 object-based), with answers ranging from 2 words (counting) to 22 words (object sequences).

## Method

EgoTempo is constructed via a semi-automated pipeline: video clips are extracted from Ego4D by grouping consecutive narration segments (up to 120 narrations or 120 s), then Gemini 1.5 Pro generates captions and Q&A pairs in zero-shot across 10 temporal-reasoning categories. A rigorous manual review step discards any pair answerable from a single frame or lacking temporal complexity, yielding 500 validated pairs (50 per category). The evaluation protocol uses LLM-as-judge scoring and benchmarks 13 MLLMs (Gemini, GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-NeXT-Video, VideoLLaVA, InternLM, MiniCPM, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) under three input conditions—text-only, single-frame, and multi-frame—across varying sampling rates and strategies (uniform, random, shuffled). Key findings show that even the best model (GPT-4o at 64 frames) reaches only 44.4% accuracy, and counting/sequence tasks show no improvement with more frames, revealing that expanded context windows alone do not confer true temporal reasoning.

## Results

On EgoTempo, the best-performing model GPT-4o (32 frames) achieves 40.1% average accuracy, while Gemini 1.5 Flash at 1FPS reaches 39.1%. Performance drops dramatically with fewer inputs: single-frame accuracy falls to 9.1% (Gemini Flash) and 12.9% (GPT-4o), and text-only accuracy is 10.0% (Gemini Flash), barely above the 4.9% random-chance baseline. The relative improvement from single-frame to multi-frame input is 4.3× on EgoTempo, compared to only 1.4× on existing egocentric VideoQA benchmarks (EgoSchema, EgoTaskQA), confirming that EgoTempo genuinely requires temporal reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (40.1%) narrowly outperforms Gemini 1.5 Flash (39.1%) on average accuracy with multiple frames, with GPT-4o leading on Action Sequence (39.6 vs 36.0) and Object Counting (51.0 vs 40.0), while Gemini Flash leads on Spatial Relations (64.0 vs 50.0) and Temporal Event Ordering (42.0 vs 38.0). Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL-72B (28.4%) and LLaVA-OneVision-72B (26.5%) are the strongest, but both trail the proprietary models by over 11 percentage points. Smaller open-source models (Qwen2-VL-7B at 26.1%, MiniCPM-V2.6 at 18.6%) and video-specific models (LLaVA-NeXT-Video-34B at 15.7%, VideoLLaVA at 12.7%) perform substantially worse, with VideoLLaVA and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (limited to 8 frames) falling below 14%. A critical finding is that single-frame accuracy across all models (3.2%–15.7%) is far below multi-frame performance, and the 4.3× single-frame-to-video improvement on EgoTempo contrasts sharply with the ≤1.4× improvement on EgoSchema and EgoTaskQA, demonstrating that EgoTempo cannot be shortcut by static or commonsense reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying a critical flaw in existing egocentric VideoQA benchmarks (EgoSchema, EgoTaskQA, EgoThink): their questions can largely be answered from a single frame or commonsense text alone, without genuine temporal reasoning. It departs from prior work by constructing EgoTempo from Ego4D narrations with a semi-automated pipeline (Gemini 1.5 Pro for generation, manual review for quality) that explicitly filters out questions solvable without multi-frame temporal information. Conceptually, it extends the VideoQA evaluation paradigm by introducing a 10-category taxonomy of temporal reasoning capabilities (action sequences, counting, event ordering, future prediction, etc.) that prior egocentric datasets do not systematically cover, and by using OpenQA format to avoid the pitfalls of closed-ended questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art MLLMs achieve only 39% on EgoTempo even with full 1FPS video access, while scoring 51–70% on existing benchmarks with a single frame, exposes a significant gap between apparent and actual temporal understanding in current models. The observation that counting tasks (action counting, object counting) show no improvement with additional frames for any model tested highlights a fundamental architectural limitation that simply scaling context windows cannot resolve. This benchmark is likely to become a standard reference for evaluating temporal reasoning in egocentric settings, and the 4.3× single-frame-to-video improvement ratio (vs. 1.4× on prior datasets) provides a clear diagnostic signal for whether a model genuinely processes temporal information. Open questions include whether explicit temporal modeling architectures (e.g., recurrent or attention-based temporal encoders) can close the gap, and whether the 10-category taxonomy captures the full breadth of temporal reasoning needed for embodied AI.
