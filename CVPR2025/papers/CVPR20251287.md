## Problem

Existing 3D scene reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: neural rendering approaches (NeRF, 3DGS) achieve photorealism but produce static, non-interactive scenes, while interaction-oriented methods (URDF-Former, Digital Cousin) sacrifice visual and geometric fidelity for physical simulability. No prior framework simultaneously delivers high-fidelity rendering, accurate geometry, and physically grounded articulation from a single static video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251287/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single video of a static indoor scene (no interactions required) and extracts posed images for reconstruction. It produces a dual scene representation (neural SDF for geometry + Gaussian splats for appearance) with segmented articulated and rigid objects, estimated articulation types and hinge axes, amodal shape completion for previously hidden interior regions, and PBR material texturing. The output is directly importable into Unreal Engine (via Luma plugin for Gaussian rendering + SDF mesh for collision) and Isaac Sim (for physics-based robot learning), supporting real-time interaction at interactive frame rates.

## Method

DRAWER's core architectural contribution is a dual scene representation that tightly couples a neural SDF (for geometry) with 3D Gaussian splatting (for appearance) by anchoring each Gaussian to a face of the SDF-extracted mesh, constraining its movement to the face plane plus a limited normal-direction offset, and regularizing scale along the normal. A straight-through estimator handles the non-differentiable clipping that enforces this constraint. For articulation, the pipeline segments objects via Grounded SAM + Louvain fusion + GPT-4o filtering, estimates articulation types and axes through an ensemble of 3DOI and GPT-4o with VLM arbitration, then performs amodal shape estimation by optimizing compositional 3D shape-primitive templates against mask, Chamfer distance, and structural adjacency losses via differentiable rendering (PyTorch3D). Hidden interior regions are textured with MatFuse (a conditional diffusion model), and the completed articulated objects are converted back to the dual representation for seamless compositional integration into the scene.

## Results

DRAWER achieves PSNR 27.80, SSIM 0.912, and LPIPS 0.159 on novel view synthesis across six kitchen scenes, while being the only method that simultaneously supports real-time rendering, rigid-body physics, scene decomposition, and articulation. For articulation understanding, DRAWER reaches 97.2% precision and 84.0% recall, substantially outperforming URDF-Former (93.5%/46.4%) and Digital Cousin (46.9%/60.0%). In articulated motion simulation, DRAWER achieves an EMD of 1.41×10⁻⁵, outperforming KlingAI (17.7×10⁻⁵) by over an order of magnitude, and attains an EA-Score of 0.994 versus 3DOI's 0.861 for rotation axis estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pure rendering methods (3DGS, Nerfacto, Video2Game-NeRF), DRAWER trades some visual fidelity (PSNR 27.80 vs. 3DGS's 30.42) for full interactive capability—no other method supports all four interactive features simultaneously. Among geometry-aware baselines, DRAWER significantly outperforms BakedSDF (PSNR 27.80 vs. 21.11) and Video2Game-Mesh (27.80 vs. 22.63) while adding articulation support that neither provides. For articulation understanding, DRAWER nearly doubles the recall of URDF-Former (84.0% vs. 46.4%) while maintaining higher precision (97.2% vs. 93.5%), and dramatically outperforms Digital Cousin on precision (97.2% vs. 46.9%). In rotation axis estimation, DRAWER's EA-Score of 0.994 far exceeds 3DOI's 0.861, demonstrating the benefit of grounding predictions in 3D geometry. For articulated motion simulation, DRAWER's physics-based approach achieves EMD 1.41×10⁻⁵, roughly 12× better than KlingAI's 17.7×10⁻⁵ despite KlingAI receiving manual segmentation and motion prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRAWER builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al.) for photorealistic rendering and BakedSDF (Yariv et al.) for geometric reconstruction, but departs from both by tightly coupling them into a dual scene representation where Gaussians are anchored to SDF surface faces with constrained movement. It extends the authors' own prior work Video2Game (Xia et al., CVPR 2024), which reconstructed interactable scenes from video, by adding articulated object simulation (opening/closing drawers and cabinets) and amodal shape estimation for hidden regions. The articulation module leverages foundation models (3DOI, GPT-4o) in an ensemble, distinguishing it from prior scene-level articulation methods like URDF-Former and Digital Cousin that prioritize semantic functionality over visual and geometric fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRAWER is the first framework to simultaneously achieve photorealistic rendering, fine-grained geometric accuracy, and physically grounded articulation simulation from a single static video, enabling practical applications in game creation (Unreal Engine) and real-to-sim-to-real robot policy transfer without manual articulation specification. The zero-shot transfer of drawer-opening policies from simulation to a Franka Emika Panda robot demonstrates tangible value for reducing real-world data collection burden. Open questions include extending to dynamic scenes, relightable environments (acknowledged as future work), and whether the VLM-dependent pipeline (GPT-4o for mask quality and articulation arbitration) generalizes beyond the six kitchen scenes evaluated.
