## Problem

Existing one-shot controllable video editing (OCVE) methods rely on DDIM inversion to convert source frames into latent noise, but the recursive inversion accumulates approximation errors at each timestep, degrading content consistency and weakening editing fidelity. Additionally, methods that use video diffusion models to enforce temporal consistency are computationally expensive and depend on lower-quality open-source video datasets. This paper eliminates the need for DDIM inversion entirely and addresses both content and temporal consistency through a training-free sampling framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video and a single user-edited first frame (produced by any off-the-shelf image editing tool) as input. Rather than inverting source frames into latent noise, each source frame is encoded directly as a feature representation via the diffusion model's image encoder. The four components (first source frame, first edited frame, current source frame, and a blank region) are arranged in a 2×2 grid as the inpainting input, with a mask designating the blank region for generation. The text prompt is not manually written but computed as a scaled CLIP embedding difference between the edited and source first frames. The output is the full sequence of edited video frames.

## Method

The core insight is recasting OCVE as a visual prompting task: the first source/edited frame pair serves as the example, and each subsequent source frame is the query, all processed by a pre-trained Stable Diffusion Inpainting 1.5 model in a 2×2 grid layout. To ensure content consistency without DDIM inversion, the authors propose Content Consistency Sampling (CCS), which modifies the inpainting model's denoising equations to perform multi-step consistency sampling—starting from the source frame at the first timestep and using the denoising difference between the query and output regions to progressively steer generation along the user's editing direction. To enforce temporal consistency, they introduce Temporal-Content Consistency Sampling (TCS), which treats the N source frames as samples from a distribution and applies Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) to deterministically update the CCS-generated edited frames toward the source distribution over 50 steps. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring only hyperparameter tuning (λ₁=0.7, λ₂=1.2, η=2.0) and operating on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the MagicBrush-derived dataset (10,388 tuples), the proposed method achieves the best performance across all four evaluation perspectives: edit fidelity (CLIP_tgt, TIFA), source faithfulness (CLIP_src, Flow, FVD, SSIM), temporal consistency (CLIP_TC), and efficiency (time per video). The method is significantly more efficient than prior OCVE methods (Videoshop, AnyV2V) because it uses a lightweight image inpainting diffusion model (Stable Diffusion Inpainting 1.5) rather than computationally demanding video diffusion models. Ablation confirms that CCS improves source faithfulness metrics and TCS substantially improves temporal consistency (CLIP_TC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two SOTA OCVE baselines, Videoshop [12] and AnyV2V [36], the proposed method wins on all metrics including edit fidelity, source faithfulness, temporal consistency, and efficiency. The efficiency gain is particularly pronounced because the method uses a single image inpainting diffusion model (Stable Diffusion Inpainting 1.5) with 30 CCS timesteps and 50 TCS steps, whereas Videoshop and AnyV2V rely on heavier video diffusion models. Among the five text-based video editing baselines (Pix2Video, FateZero, Spacetime, RAVE, BDIA), the proposed method also outperforms all of them, demonstrating that the visual-prompting formulation with a single edited first frame provides finer-grained control than text instructions alone. Qualitatively, in the fruit-to-vegetable replacement task, Videoshop struggles to maintain the fruit's appearance across frames, while the proposed method's CCS consistently preserves content consistency. The ablation variants confirm that removing CCS degrades source faithfulness (CLIP_src, SSIM) and removing TCS causes a significant drop in temporal consistency (CLIP_TC), validating each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant DDIM-inversion paradigm in one-shot controllable video editing (Videoshop, AnyV2V) by reframing OCVE as a visual prompting task, where the first source/edited frame pair serves as an example and subsequent source frames serve as queries for an inpainting diffusion model. It builds on the visual prompting formulation of Bar et al. [3] and the multi-step consistency sampling property of consistency models [57], adapting both to the inpainting diffusion setting without additional training. For temporal consistency, it replaces the video-diffusion-model priors used by prior OCVE methods with a lightweight SVGD-based update, drawing on the Bayesian-inference framing of Kim et al. [34].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating DDIM inversion removes a well-documented source of cumulative approximation error that has limited the fidelity and controllability of prior OCVE methods, and the visual-prompting reframing offers a cleaner conceptual foundation that may generalize to other propagation-style editing tasks. The practical efficiency gain from using an image inpainting model rather than a video diffusion model makes the approach more accessible. Open questions include how well the method scales to longer videos, whether the visual-reasoning quality of current inpainting models is sufficient for complex multi-object or structural edits, and whether the SVGD-based temporal alignment degrades when source and edited frames differ substantially in layout.
