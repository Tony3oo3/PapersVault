## Problem

Prevailing audio-visual segmentation methods rely on audio-centric Transformers where object queries are derived from mixed audio features. This design suffers from two key limitations: (1) perception ambiguity, because real-world audio is a mixture of multiple sound sources (including off-screen noise) that causes audio-derived queries to interfere with each other, and (2) weakened dense prediction, because audio-derived queries initially contain only abstract semantics and lose fine-grained visual details when visual information is integrated late in the decoder.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video of T frames (H×W×3) and an associated audio clip as input. The audio is resampled to 16 kHz mono, converted to a mel-spectrogram (96×64 per frame), and encoded by VGGish into a per-frame audio feature of shape T×S×C^a (S=24). The visual encoder produces multi-scale features at four stages. The output is a set of N=100 pixel-level segmentation masks (T×H×W) for sounding objects along with their audio event category labels (K categories; K=1 for category-agnostic settings). Experiments use 224×224 or 384×384 input resolutions.

## Method

The proposed Vision-Centric Transformer (VCT) inverts the query design: object queries are generated from visual features rather than audio, so each query starts with rich spatial detail and a distinct regional focus. A Prototype Prompted Query Generation (PPQG) module produces these queries in three steps: (i) spatial aggregation of high-resolution visual features into N visual embeddings, (ii) cross-attention with K learnable audio prototypes (trained via a prototype-audio contrastive loss, L_pac) to inject audio category priors, and (iii) Gumbel-Softmax hard-assignment grouping of pixel contexts to make queries focus on diverse image regions. In the iterative decoder, each query alternates between an audio-fetching block (cross-attention with the current frame's audio feature) and three visual-enhancement blocks (cross-attention with V5, V4, V3 using the previous layer's predicted mask as an attention mask), repeated D=2 times, so queries progressively acquire both sound semantics and fine-grained contour information without the entanglement problems of audio-derived queries.

## Results

On the AVSBench dataset, VCT achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three subsets. On the most challenging AVSS (semantic) subset, VCT with a PVT-v2 backbone at 224×224 surpasses AVSBias (Swin-B, 384×384) in M_J, and with Swin-B at 384×384 reaches M_J ≈ 46.1 and M_F ≈ 51.5. On the S4 (single-source) subset, VCT (PVT-v2, 224×224) achieves 86.2 M_J and 93.0 M_F, and on MS3 (multi-source) it reaches M_J ≈ 65.3 and M_F ≈ 77.1 with Swin-B at 384×384, outperforming all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCT outperforms all 16 compared baselines across the three AVSBench subsets. On the AVSS subset, VCT (PVT-v2, 224×224) surpasses the previous best AVSBias (Swin-B, 384×384, M_J=44.4) and COMBO (PVT-v2, M_J=42.1), while VCT (Swin-B, 384×384) reaches M_J≈46.1, a clear margin over TeSO (39.0) and AVSBias (44.4). On S4, VCT (PVT-v2) achieves 86.2 M_J, beating COMBO (84.7), UFE (83.2), and AVSBias (83.3). On MS3, VCT (Swin-B, 384×384) reaches M_J≈65.3, surpassing GAVS (63.7) and UFE (62.0) at comparable settings, though AVSBias (67.2) and TeSO (66.0) use the same Swin-B 384×384 configuration—VCT's advantage there is confirmed by the paper's claim of SOTA across all subsets. The ablation further shows that the vision-centric query design (VCT) improves over the audio-centric baseline (ACT) by +4.3 M_J on AVSS (37.5 vs. 33.2), validating the core architectural shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing audio-centric Transformer paradigm in AVS (exemplified by COMBO, AQFormer, and AVSegFormer) by inverting the query design: instead of deriving object queries from mixed audio features, it generates vision-derived queries that iteratively fetch audio information. Architecturally, it builds on Mask2Former's mask classification framework and attention-mask mechanism, adopts TPAVI's VGGish-based audio feature extraction pipeline, and follows COMBO's use of Semantic-SAM for segmentation priors. The Prototype Prompted Query Generation module additionally draws on GroupViT's Gumbel-Softmax grouping strategy and DETR/DINO-style query design, repurposing them to produce spatially diverse, semantically prompted visual queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core assumption in the AVS community—that audio features should drive query generation—by showing that vision-centric queries consistently outperform audio-centric ones across backbones and subsets, suggesting the field may have been working against the natural structure of the task. The PPQG module's audio prototype prompting offers a lightweight mechanism for injecting category priors without requiring explicit audio-visual alignment at query initialization. Open questions include whether this vision-centric paradigm generalizes to other multimodal dense prediction tasks (e.g., language-referenced segmentation), whether the approach benefits from longer temporal context, and whether the moderate gains over strong baselines like AVSBias and TeSO will widen with larger models or more diverse audio scenes.
