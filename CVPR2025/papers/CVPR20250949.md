## Problem

Gradient inversion attacks in federated learning have been demonstrated under full fine-tuning (FFT), where the attacker observes gradients for all model parameters. However, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) freezes the backbone and shares only gradients for lightweight adapter modules (typically r ≤ 64 neurons per layer), leading to the assumption that PEFT inherently limits privacy leakage. This paper asks whether a malicious server can still recover a victim's local training images using only these severely constrained adapter gradients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250949/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker consumes the weight and bias gradients of the adapter down-projection layers (r neurons per adapter, typically r=64) from a single victim user's training round, combined with a one-time poisoned ViT-B/16 pretrained model and a public dataset for estimating patch-statistic distributions. The output is a set of reconstructed image patches (16×16 pixels each) that are then grouped into full images of 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100, 4 patches), 64×64 (TinyImageNet, 16 patches), or 224×224 (ImageNet, 196 patches). Reconstruction quality is measured via MSE, LPIPS, and SSIM between recovered and ground-truth patches.

## Method

PEFTLeak poisons the pretrained ViT so that all intermediate layers (LayerNorm, MSA, MLP) act as identity mappings, ensuring patch embeddings y^(n,m) propagate undistorted to the adapter layers. In each adapter's down-projection, weight vectors are set to the target position's encoding vector E_pos^(t), and biases are designed using quantiles of an estimated Gaussian distribution of patch statistics, so that only patches from the target position pass the activation while others are blocked. Because a single adapter has only r neurons (recovering at most r−1 patches), the attack distributes recovery across multiple adapter layers, each targeting different statistical intervals of the same patch position. The embedding is recovered via the ratio of weight-gradient differences to bias-gradient differences (Eq. 24), and the raw patch is obtained by subtracting the position encoding and applying the pseudoinverse of the projection matrix. For small r, the server tampers with aggregated adapter parameters across multiple training rounds to target different intervals, progressively recovering the full image.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (batch of 32, 4 patches per image), PEFTLeak recovers 110 out of 128 patches (85.9%) with mean SSIM of 0.88 and LPIPS of 0.08. On TinyImageNet (16 patches per image), approximately 81% of patches are recovered with SSIM 0.76. Even at a batch size of 128 on CIFAR-100, the attack recovers 72.6% of patches, and on ImageNet (224×224, 196 patches per image) it recovers 90% of patches at batch size 8, degrading to 62.6% at batch size 64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PEFTLeak against the optimization-based gradient inversion attack of Geiping et al. [20] (NeurIPS 2020), which minimizes the distance between true and estimated gradients to recover images. As shown in Figure 8, the optimization-based baseline produces significantly degraded reconstructions on CIFAR-100 compared to PEFTLeak's near-pixel-perfect recovery (Figure 4). This gap is expected because the optimization-based attack was designed for full fine-tuning (FFT) where the attacker has access to gradients of all model parameters, whereas under PEFT the attacker only observes gradients of the small adapter modules (r ≤ 64 neurons per layer). PEFTLeak overcomes this information bottleneck by poisoning the pretrained model to make intermediate layers act as identity mappings and by distributing the recovery task across multiple adapter layers, enabling high-fidelity reconstruction from the limited adapter gradient signal alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient inversion attack line initiated by Feng & Tramèr (ICML 2024), which poisoned ViT parameters to recover images from full-model gradients under FFT, to the far more constrained PEFT setting where only lightweight adapter gradients (r ≤ 64 neurons) are observable. It also draws on Fowl et al.'s (ICLR 2022) activation-based patch blocking and statistics estimation, and Fowl et al.'s (ICLR 2023) patch-tagging scheme for reassembling recovered patches into full images. The key departure is that prior ViT inversion attacks assumed access to all 3072 MLP neuron gradients, whereas here the attacker must recover the same information through a severely bottlenecked channel by distributing the recovery burden across multiple adapter layers and training rounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption that PEFT inherently improves privacy in federated learning by reducing the number of shared parameters, showing instead that a malicious server controlling the pretrained model can still reconstruct user data with high fidelity. The finding that even a very small adapter dimension (r=8) does not guarantee privacy—because the attack can be extended over multiple rounds—closes an apparent defense avenue. Open questions include whether other PEFT mechanisms (LoRA, prefix-tuning, bias-tuning) are similarly vulnerable, and what certifiable privacy guarantees (e.g., differential privacy) are needed to protect PEFT-based FL systems.
