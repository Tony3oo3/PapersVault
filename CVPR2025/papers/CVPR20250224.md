## Problem

Contrastive decoding methods (e.g., VCD, ICD) mitigate object hallucination in MLLMs by contrasting output distributions from original and perturbed inputs, but they suffer from two critical drawbacks: they bluntly suppress language priors, degrading the coherence and accuracy of generated content (e.g., ~19% CIDEr drop on NoCaps), and they require a second forward pass on contrastive samples, nearly doubling inference time. The paper identifies that hallucination actually stems from insufficient attention to visual information during modality fusion in middle layers, rather than from an overemphasis on language signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250224/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VAF operates as a plug-and-play inference-time modification on standard MLLM backbones (LLaVA-v1.5-7B/13B, Qwen-VL-7B) that take an RGB image and a textual query and autoregressively generate a text response. No additional forward passes, external tools, or retraining are required; the method simply adjusts attention score matrices in layers 8–15 during the existing decoding loop, so the input format and output structure remain identical to vanilla decoding.

## Method

VAF (Visual Amplification Fusion) modifies the attention score matrix Z in the middle layers (8 < l < 15) where visual-textual modality fusion predominantly occurs, by adding an enhancement term α·M_enh∘Z that amplifies attention from instruction tokens to visual tokens and subtracting a suppression term β·M_sup∘Z that reduces attention to system prompts. Saliency analysis (Taylor expansion of attention matrices) and attention-allocation measurements motivate this design: visual-textual information flow peaks in middle layers, yet visual features receive markedly less attention than system prompts and user instructions there. To prevent over-amplification from degrading content quality, VAF restricts the attention redistribution to 'visual perception heads'—those attention heads whose visual attention allocation falls in the top 50% within each layer—leaving all other heads unchanged. With default hyperparameters α=0.15 and β=0.1, VAF achieves ~3% POPE and ~7% MME improvements over baselines while preserving NoCaps CIDEr scores and incurring negligible inference overhead.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark (averaged across MS-COCO, A-OKVQA, and GQA), VAF achieves the highest F1-scores across all three models and all three categories (Random, Popular, Adversarial), with improvements of approximately 1.8–3.2 points over the best baseline (VCD or ICD). On the MME benchmark, VAF yields total scores of 636.67 (LLaVA-7B), 661.67 (LLaVA-13B), and 628.33 (Qwen-VL-7B), representing roughly 7% gains over the regular decoding baseline. Critically, VAF preserves content quality on NoCaps (CIDEr 78.8 for LLaVA-7B vs. 78.7 regular) and ScienceQA (68.5% vs. 68.0%), whereas VCD degrades NoCaps CIDEr by ~19% and ScienceQA accuracy by ~5%. VAF also incurs virtually no inference-speed penalty, in contrast to VCD and ICD which nearly double inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAF consistently outperforms both VCD and ICD on the POPE benchmark across all three models and all three difficulty categories (Random, Popular, Adversarial), with F1-score gains of 1.6–3.2 points over the stronger of the two baselines. On MME, VAF achieves the highest total scores for every model (e.g., 636.67 vs. 607.66 for VCD and 608.32 for ICD on LLaVA-7B), with particularly large gains on object-existence (+10.0) and counting (+11.66) sub-tasks. On content-quality metrics, VAF matches or slightly exceeds regular decoding (NoCaps CIDEr 78.8 vs. 78.7; ScienceQA 68.5% vs. 68.0% for LLaVA-7B), while VCD drops NoCaps CIDEr by 18% and ScienceQA accuracy by 5%, and ICD drops NoCaps CIDEr by 27%. In terms of inference speed, VAF introduces negligible overhead, whereas both VCD and ICD approximately double inference time (e.g., 0.456s → 1.086s per sample on NoCaps for LLaVA-7B with VCD). Overall, VAF dominates both baselines on hallucination reduction without the quality or speed trade-offs that limit contrastive decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct alternative to contrastive decoding strategies (VCD, ICD) for hallucination mitigation in MLLMs, which it identifies as having two critical flaws: content quality degradation and doubled inference time. Rather than processing contrastive inputs at the output distribution level, VAF operates internally by modifying attention weights in the middle transformer layers where modality fusion occurs. The key conceptual departure is the diagnostic finding from saliency analysis: hallucination arises not from overemphasis on language signals but from insufficient attention allocated to visual tokens during fusion, reframing the problem as one of visual neglect rather than language dominance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAF offers a genuinely zero-overhead, plug-and-play hallucination mitigation technique that practitioners can adopt without retraining or architectural changes, which is practically valuable for deployment in latency-sensitive applications like medical imaging or autonomous driving. However, the method is validated only on linear-projection MLLMs (LLaVA, Qwen-VL) with a fixed middle-layer range (8–15), raising open questions about transferability to Q-Former-based architectures, different model scales, or non-English settings. The moderate gains (~3% POPE, ~7% MME) and the reliance on a hand-tuned α parameter (0.15) suggest the technique, while effective, may not be a universal solution and could benefit from adaptive or learned layer selection.
