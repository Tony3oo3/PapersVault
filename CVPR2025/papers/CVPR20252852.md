## Problem

Supervised image deraining methods require large paired clean-rainy datasets that are impractical to collect in real-world scenarios, and both supervised and existing unsupervised methods suffer from poor generalization when applied to unseen real-world rain. Existing unsupervised deraining approaches still lag significantly behind supervised ones in performance and have not adequately addressed how to improve generalization to diverse real-world rain distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252852/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the framework consumes unpaired clean and rainy RGB images (randomly cropped to 256×256 patches) without requiring any paired correspondence. At inference, a single rainy RGB image is fed to the derainer (NAFNet-based) to produce a clean derained image of the same resolution. The generator internally processes both a clean image and a real rainy image as separate inputs to synthesize pseudo rainy images, using a clean feature extraction module (CFEM) and a rain information extraction module (RIEM) that are concatenated before residual processing.

## Method

CSUD is a GAN-based unsupervised framework with a derainer (NAFNet), a rainy-image generator (CFEM + RIEM + 6 residual blocks), and a Patch-GAN discriminator, trained entirely on unpaired data. The key insight is the Channel Consistency Prior (CCP): rain streaks are spectrally consistent across R, G, and B channels, so the cycle subtractions between channels in a rainy image should closely match those in the corresponding clean image; this is enforced via a Channel Consistency Loss (CCLoss) that penalizes L1 differences in channel-pair subtractions between the input clean image and the generated pseudo rainy image. A Self-Reconstruction (SR) strategy further constrains the generator: when the clean image itself (or a derained image) is used as the rain guide, the generator must reconstruct the clean image unchanged, preventing redundant background textures (e.g., hair edges, object outlines) from being mistakenly transferred as rain streaks. The SR loss is applied to both the generator and the derainer, encouraging the derainer to focus on background reconstruction and thereby improving generalization. Training combines four adversarial losses, CCLoss, SR losses, L1, SSIM, and VGG-19 perceptual loss with tuned weight coefficients.

## Results

On the unsupervised deraining benchmark (Table 1), CSUD achieves 33.28 dB PSNR / 0.954 SSIM on Rain100L, 34.56 dB / 0.951 on Rain12, and 32.71 dB / 0.959 on RealRain1K-L, outperforming the best prior unsupervised method DCD-GAN by 1.46 dB, 3.00 dB, and 2.22 dB respectively. In the generalization setting (Table 2), where all models are trained only on Rain100L and tested on unseen real-world data, CSUD reaches 29.21 dB / 0.928 SSIM on RealRain1K-L and 25.45 dB / 0.878 on RealRain1K-H, surpassing all supervised baselines (e.g., NAFNet at 28.11 / 0.915 and Restormer at 26.29 / 0.883) on the first four real-world test sets. The method also maintains competitive performance on the challenging Night-Rain dataset (25.94 dB / 0.805 SSIM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unsupervised baselines, CSUD consistently outperforms DCD-GAN (the strongest prior unsupervised method) by 1.46 dB on Rain100L, 3.00 dB on Rain12, and 2.22 dB on RealRain1K-L in PSNR. The gap over DerainCycleGAN is even larger on real-world data: +4.55 dB on RealRain1K-L and +4.89 dB on RealRain1K-H. NLCL and vanilla CycleGAN are substantially weaker, with CSUD exceeding NLCL by over 11 dB on Rain100L and Rain12. In the generalization setting (trained on Rain100L only), CSUD surpasses all supervised methods including NAFNet, Restormer, PromptIR, and NeRD-Rain-S on RealRain1K-L, RealRain1K-H, and SPA-data, while remaining competitive on RainDS and Night-Rain. Notably, CSUD's unsupervised performance on in-domain datasets (e.g., 33.28 dB on Rain100L) approaches but does not match the best supervised methods (NeRD-Rain-S at 42.00 dB), reflecting the inherent difficulty of the unsupervised setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSUD extends the CycleGAN-based unpaired image-to-image translation framework (Zhu et al., 2017) into the deraining domain, building directly on DerainCycleGAN and DCD-GAN which already adapted adversarial cycle-consistency for rain removal. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a domain-specific physical prior—the Channel Consistency Prior (CCP) of rain streaks—as a structured loss on the generator, which no prior unsupervised deraining method had exploited. The Self-Reconstruction strategy further differentiates CSUD by explicitly penalizing the generator for transferring redundant background edges as rain, addressing a failure mode (redundant information transfer) that prior CycleGAN-based derainers did not formally diagnose or constrain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful finding is that a purely unsupervised framework, trained only on synthetic rain, comprehensively outperforms strong supervised baselines (Restormer, NeRD-Rain-S, NAFNet) on real-world generalization, challenging the assumption that paired supervision is necessary for robust real-world deraining. The CCP is a simple, interpretable physical observation that could potentially transfer to other weather-artifact removal tasks (snow, fog) where similar channel-consistency properties may hold. Limitations include the continued reliance on GAN training stability, weaker performance on the RainDS dataset (which contains raindrops in addition to streaks), and the fact that the nighttime generalization advantage over supervised methods narrows considerably. Open questions include whether the SR strategy generalizes to other unpaired restoration tasks and whether the CCP can be formalized for multi-channel or hyperspectral rain scenarios.
