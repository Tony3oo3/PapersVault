## Problem

Video turbulence mitigation requires a large spatiotemporal receptive field to aggregate stochastic degradation across many frames, but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: CNNs have limited spatial reach, self-attention scales quadratically with frame count, and recurrent models suffer from parallelization and training instability. Additionally, estimating the underlying Zernike-based degradation parameters from a degraded image is highly ill-posed, as infinitely many coefficient fields can produce identical blur patterns, making physics-informed supervision unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a turbulence-degraded RGB video clip of shape T×H×W×3 (trained on 192×192 to 256×256 patches with 18 to 36 frames) and produces both restored video frames and a 4-channel latent phase distortion (LPD) map of shape T×H×W×4, where the first two channels encode the spatial deformation (tilt) field and the last two encode the blur component. The LPD is additionally compressed to T×H/8×W/8×8C to serve as a conditioning signal for the guided Mamba blocks, and the ReBlurNet re-degradation module is pre-trained separately and frozen during the main restoration training.

## Method

MambaTM combines two key ideas: a Learned Latent Phase Distortion (LPD) representation and a Mamba-based restoration backbone. The LPD is learned via a conditional VAE that maps the ill-posed Zernike coefficient random field to a Gaussian latent code (μ, σ) with a one-to-one correspondence to the actual degradation pattern; a lightweight ReBlurNet (based on NAFNet) then uses this latent code to re-degrade images through multi-scale feature modulation following a tilt-then-blur decomposition, running 50× faster than the original Zernike simulator. The restoration backbone uses cascaded bidirectional Mamba blocks with three complementary scan orders (space-first, time-first, and local Hilbert) to achieve a global spatiotemporal receptive field at linear complexity. The LPD explicitly guides the state space model by modulating the SSM's Δ, B, and C parameters as well as the output gating, making the state evolution degradation-aware. Training proceeds in two stages: first the ReBlurNet is trained with a VAE objective (L1 re-degradation loss plus KL divergence), then MambaTM is jointly optimized with a combined restoration loss (Charbonnier + perceptual) and re-degradation loss under progressive resolution and frame-count scheduling.

## Results

On the ATSyn-dynamic benchmark (overall), MambaTM achieves 29.92 PSNR / 0.8843 SSIM / 0.1516 LPIPS, outperforming the previous SOTA DATUM (29.42 / 0.8714 / 0.1919) by ~0.5 dB in PSNR and substantially in LPIPS. On the TMT dynamic dataset, MambaTM reaches 28.90 PSNR at 55.4 FPS, nearly doubling the inference speed of DATUM (32.7 FPS) while also improving quality. On the real-world Turb-Text benchmark, MambaTM achieves 97.80% / 99.35% / 98.15% text recognition rates (CRNN/DAN/ASTER), a new SOTA that exceeds the previous best (DATUM) by 4+ points on CRNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaTM outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated benchmark. The closest competitor is DATUM [92], the previous SOTA using a non-linear recurrent architecture; MambaTM improves over it by 0.50 dB PSNR and 0.040 LPIPS on ATSyn-dynamic (overall), and by 0.30 dB PSNR at 1.7× the inference speed (55.4 vs 32.7 FPS) on the TMT dataset. Among general video restoration methods, RVRT [50] is the strongest baseline (28.93 PSNR), still trailing MambaTM by ~1 dB. The self-attention-based TMT [93] (28.27 PSNR) and the GAN-based TSRWGAN [34] (26.45 PSNR) are further behind, with TSRWGAN showing the largest gap (~3.5 dB). On the static-scene ATSyn-static benchmark, MambaTM leads DATUM by 0.25 dB PSNR, and on the real-world Turb-Text benchmark it achieves over 98% recognition on all three text recognizers versus DATUM's 93.55% on CRNN. The consistent wins across synthetic and real-world settings, combined with the speed advantage, indicate that the linear-complexity SSM backbone and LPD-guided state space construction provide both quality and efficiency gains over recurrent and attention-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) Zernike-based atmospheric turbulence simulation and mitigation, where prior work [12, 56, 92] uses large-kernel depthwise convolutions and Zernike coefficient fields to model spatially varying blur, and (2) selective state space models (Mamba) [23], which have recently shown linear-complexity global receptive fields for vision tasks [25, 84, 98]. The key departure is the reparameterization of the Zernike-based simulator via a conditional VAE into a latent phase distortion (LPD) representation, which the authors argue makes degradation estimation well-posed and 50× faster. Architecturally, MambaTM adopts bidirectional scanning from Vision Mamba [98] and local Hilbert scanning from RainMamba [84], but introduces a novel phase-distortion-guided SSM where LPD features explicitly modulate the state-space transition parameters (Δ, B, C), a mechanism not present in prior Mamba-based restoration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LPD reparameterization addresses a fundamental ill-posedness in turbulence degradation estimation (multiple Zernike fields can produce identical blur patterns), and the 50× simulation speedup makes joint degradation-estimation-and-restoration training practically feasible. Achieving near real-time inference (55.4 FPS on A100) while setting SOTA on both synthetic and real-world benchmarks positions MambaTM as a strong baseline for future video TM research. Open questions include whether the LPD representation generalizes across different turbulence regimes or imaging geometries without retraining, how the method scales to very long video sequences beyond 36 frames, and whether the guided SSM mechanism transfers to other degradation-aware restoration tasks beyond turbulence.
