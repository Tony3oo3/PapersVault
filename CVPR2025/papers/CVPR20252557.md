## Problem

Training diffusion models requires tens of thousands of GPU days, and existing efficiency techniques (pruning, timestep weighting, architectural modifications) still necessitate fully training a dense model before any speedup is realized. This paper investigates whether Early-Bird (EB) tickets—sparse subnetworks that emerge early in training and retain high generation quality—exist in diffusion models, and whether timestep-aware variants that adapt sparsity to the importance of different denoising regions can further reduce training costs without a full dense training run.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252557/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion model takes Gaussian random noise and a scalar timestep as input and produces RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (LSUN, ImageNet-1K). A distinctive aspect of the TA-EB variant is that inference ensembles three separate pruned subnetworks, each responsible for a specific timestep region (0–260, 240–460, 440–1000) with 2% boundary overlap, so the generated image is assembled by routing each denoising step to the subnetwork assigned to that timestep range.

## Method

EB-Diff-Train first identifies EB tickets by training a dense DDPM or LDM briefly, storing epoch-wise magnitude-pruned masks in a FIFO queue, and extracting the subnetwork once pairwise Hamming distances stabilize below a threshold η=0.1. It then extends this to Timestep-Aware EB (TA-EB) tickets by partitioning the 1000-step denoising trajectory into three regions (acceleration, deceleration, convergence) following SpeeD's analysis, assigning progressively higher pruning rates (30%, 60%, 80%) to regions of decreasing importance. The three region-specific subnetworks are trained in parallel from their respective EB masks, and at inference they are ensembled by routing each timestep to its designated subnetwork. This yields 2.9×–5.8× speedups over unpruned dense training and up to 10.3× over standard train-prune-finetune pipelines while maintaining competitive FID scores, and the approach is orthogonal to timestep-resampling methods like SpeeD, with which it composes for additional gains.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (DDPM, 32×32), EB-Diff-Train (EB) at 50% pruning achieves FID 7.32–8.20 with a 2.45× speedup over the unpruned model (FID 5.15), while EB-Diff-Train (TA-EB) at 64% average pruning reaches FID 7.29 with a 5.78× speedup. Across all five datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA, LSUN Church, LSUN Bedroom, ImageNet-1K) and two architectures (DDPM, LDM), the method achieves 2.9×–5.8× speedups over unpruned dense training and up to 10.3× over train-prune-finetune baselines, with FID scores within ~2 points of the unpruned model on CIFAR-10 and CelebA. On CelebA (64×64), TA-EB achieves FID 5.41 with a 4.38× speedup, matching or slightly beating the unpruned FID of 5.03.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EB-Diff-Train (EB) consistently outperforms all train-prune-finetune baselines on CIFAR-10: at 30% pruning, it achieves FID 5.68–6.10 versus 6.32–6.72 for Diff-Pruning, Taylor, Magnitude, and Random; at 50% pruning, it reaches FID 7.32–8.20 versus 10.68–15.23 for the same baselines, while also delivering 2.45× speedup (vs. 0.92–0.95× for baselines). The 'Scratch' baseline (prune-then-retrain from random init) is the strongest competitor, achieving FID 5.45 at 30% and 8.47 at 50%, but EB-Diff-Train (EB) still matches or beats it at 50% (7.32 vs. 8.47) with a 2.45× speedup versus Scratch's 0.71×. EB-Diff-Train (TA-EB) further improves efficiency, reaching FID 7.29 at 64% average pruning with a 5.78× speedup—surpassing all baselines on both quality and speed. On CelebA, EB-Diff-Train (EB) at 50% achieves FID 5.42–5.58 (vs. 6.00–6.86 for baselines) with 1.89× speedup, and TA-EB reaches FID 5.41 with 4.38× speedup. The method is also orthogonal to SpeeD timestep resampling: combining EB-Diff-Train (EB) with SpeeD reduces FID from 9.32 to 8.89, and TA-EB+SpeeD drops from 9.34 to 8.01, both outperforming SpeeD alone (9.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established research threads: the Early-Bird (EB) ticket framework from the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (You et al., ICLR 2020), which identifies high-performing sparse subnetworks early in training, and timestep-aware diffusion training (Wang et al., SpeeD), which shows that different timestep regions contribute variably to the denoising process. The key conceptual move is combining these: rather than applying a single EB ticket uniformly across all timesteps, the authors partition the diffusion trajectory into regions of differing importance and draw region-specific EB tickets with tailored sparsity levels. This is positioned as orthogonal to existing efficient DM training strategies (timestep resampling, architectural modifications, standard pruning pipelines), meaning it can be stacked on top of methods like SpeeD for compounding gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is meaningful: 2.9×–5.8× speedups over dense training and up to 10.3× over train-prune-finetune pipelines without requiring a fully trained dense model as a starting point, which is a significant cost reduction for practitioners training DMs at scale. However, the FID gaps on larger, higher-resolution datasets (LSUN 256×256, ImageNet-1K) are non-trivial (e.g., 54.03 vs. 26.58 FID on LSUN Church for TA-EB), suggesting the approach may not yet be competitive where it matters most for production-quality generation. Open questions include whether the parallel-training-and-ensemble strategy scales to much larger architectures (e.g., Stable Diffusion-scale U-Nets), whether the three-region partitioning is optimal or dataset-dependent, and whether the non-monotonic iteration-FID relationship observed in ablations indicates a fundamental limitation of the parallel training paradigm.
