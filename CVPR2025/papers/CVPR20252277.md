## Problem

Current state-of-the-art visual navigation policies (e.g., NoMaD, GNM) are fixed after training: new constraints like 'no left turns' cannot be easily introduced, and computational effort cannot be dynamically allocated to harder problems. This paper asks whether a learned world model that simulates future observations can replace or augment such policies by enabling flexible, constraint-aware trajectory planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252277/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of m context frames (up to 4) encoded by a Stable Diffusion VAE into latents, a 3D action vector (2D translation u, yaw rotation φ, and time shift k up to ±16 s), and optionally a goal image for planning. Actions are normalized by each agent's average step size to unify the action space across embodiments. The model autoregressively produces future latent frames (decoded to 224×224 RGB for evaluation), and in planning mode outputs an optimized action sequence or a ranking score over candidate trajectories sampled from an external policy.

## Method

The core contribution is the Conditional Diffusion Transformer (CDiT), which replaces the full self-attention over all context tokens in a standard DiT with a cross-attention layer where the target frame's query tokens attend to past-frame key/value tokens, reducing complexity from O(m²n²d) to O(mn²d) in context length. Navigation actions, time shift, and diffusion timestep are each mapped through sine-cosine features and a 2-layer MLP, summed into a single conditioning vector fed to AdaLN blocks that modulate layer normalization and attention outputs. The model is trained with a standard denoising diffusion objective (MSE + VLB) in VAE latent space, scaled to 1B parameters across diverse robotic and human egocentric video. For planning, NWM is used in an MPC loop with the Cross-Entropy Method to minimize an energy function combining LPIPS goal similarity and indicator-based constraint penalties, or to rank trajectories sampled from NoMaD by simulating each and scoring the final frame against the goal.

## Results

On the RECON goal-conditioned navigation benchmark, NWM (planning) achieves the best ATE of 1.13±0.02 and RPE of 0.35±0.01, substantially outperforming GNM (ATE 1.87, RPE 0.73) and NoMaD (ATE 1.93, RPE 0.52). When used to rank NoMaD trajectories (×32 samples), NWM improves ATE to 1.78 and RPE to 0.48. For video synthesis quality on RECON, NWM achieves an FVD of 200.97±5.63, roughly 3.8× lower than DIAMOND's 762.73±3.36, indicating significantly more realistic 16-second generated videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GNM [53], NWM (planning) reduces ATE by 40% (1.13 vs 1.87) and RPE by 52% (0.35 vs 0.73), demonstrating that world-model-based planning with the Cross-Entropy Method substantially outperforms a learned trajectory prediction network. Against NoMaD [55], NWM (planning) achieves 42% lower ATE (1.13 vs 1.93) and 33% lower RPE (0.35 vs 0.52); even in the ranking setup, NWM+NoMaD(×32) improves over standalone NoMaD on both metrics (1.78 vs 1.93 ATE, 0.48 vs 0.52 RPE). Against DIAMOND [1], NWM produces significantly higher-quality video predictions: FVD is 200.97 vs 762.73 (≈3.8× improvement), and LPIPS remains lower than DIAMOND across all prediction horizons up to 16 seconds at both 1 FPS and 4 FPS. NWM also supports constraint-aware planning (e.g., 'forward first', 'left-right first') with only minor performance drops, a capability not available in the supervised baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

NWM builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023), replacing full self-attention over context tokens with a cross-attention mechanism to achieve linear complexity in context length, yielding the CDiT block. Conceptually, it extends the diffusion-based world model line of work from DIAMOND (Atari) and GameNGen (Doom) by training a single 1B-parameter model across diverse environments and embodiments rather than per-task models. It also draws on novel view synthesis methods (NeRF, Zero-1-2-3, GDC) for the idea of predicting future views from actions, but departs by learning temporal dynamics from natural video without explicit 3D priors. For navigation, it positions itself as complementary to supervised policies like NoMaD and GNM, offering a planning layer that can operate independently or rank their outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NWM demonstrates that a single scalable world model can serve as a general-purpose navigation planner, enabling constraint-aware planning (e.g., 'no left turns') that fixed supervised policies cannot easily accommodate, and can generalize to unseen environments using unlabeled egocentric video (Ego4D). Key limitations include mode collapse in out-of-distribution settings, difficulty simulating dynamic agents like pedestrians, and restriction to 3-DoF actions, all of which the authors attribute to insufficient context length and training data. Open questions include what internal representations (e.g., allocentric maps) emerge from egocentric next-frame prediction, whether the approach scales to 6-DoF or manipulation tasks, and whether the CDiT efficiency advantage persists at even larger scales.
