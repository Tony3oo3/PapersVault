## Problem

Foundational monocular depth estimation models (e.g., Metric3D, Depth Anything) achieve state-of-the-art accuracy but are too computationally heavy for real-time deployment on resource-limited edge ASICs. Existing post-training quantization methods fail to handle the persistent extreme outliers in MDE decoder activations, and no prior work co-designs quantization algorithms with a dedicated ASIC accelerator for depth estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252010/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single RGB image (tested at 256×256, 512×512, and 1024×1024 resolutions) and produces a per-pixel metric depth map. Quantization is applied to both weights and activations in W4A8 and W4A4 configurations using per-channel asymmetric quantization, with non-linear operations (softmax, layer norm, SiLU, GeLU) kept in Float32 to preserve accuracy. The system is evaluated on indoor (NYUv2, SUN RGB-D, iBims-1, HyperSim) and outdoor (KITTI, vKITTI, DIODE) benchmarks.

## Method

QuartDepth is a three-stage post-training quantization pipeline: (1) LogNP polishing applies a per-channel log-based transformation Φ(x,α)=sign(x)·[log₂(|x|+α)−log₂(α)] to smooth extreme outliers in decoder activations into a quantization-friendly distribution, with α set to the 95th percentile; (2) activation loss compensation solves a layer-wise least-squares problem to update weights ΔW* = −W(x−x̂)x̂ᵀ(x̂x̂ᵀ)⁻¹, minimizing the output discrepancy caused by activation quantization; (3) weight reconstruction uses a KFAC-approximated Hessian with AdaRound to learn optimal rounding decisions that minimize second-order quantization loss. A custom 28nm ASIC accelerator co-designed with these algorithms features specialized W4A4/W4A8 multiply-accumulate trees, a programmable vector computation array with SFU (polynomial approximation for log₂/exp in 3–5 cycles), kernel fusion to avoid DDR round-trips, and overlapped matrix/vector/load/store execution to hide polishing overhead.

## Results

On NYUv2 with ViT-Large backbone, QuartDepth achieves near-lossless performance under W4A8 quantization (AbsRel 0.071 vs. Float32 0.067; δ1 0.970 vs. 0.972), and retains strong accuracy under W4A4 (AbsRel 0.097, δ1 0.932). On KITTI with the same backbone, W4A8 yields AbsRel 0.055 (vs. Float32 0.054) and W4A4 yields AbsRel 0.070. On the ASIC, W4A4 with ViT-Small at 256×256 resolution reaches 26 FPS, and W4A4 with ViT-Large at 1024×1024 achieves a 4.9× latency speedup and 5.0× energy-efficiency gain over Float32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuartDepth substantially outperforms all conventional PTQ baselines (minmax, EMA, percentile) by a wide margin: on ViT-Large W4A8 NYUv2, AbsRel drops from 0.425–0.671 to 0.071, and on W4A4 it drops from 0.920–2.238 to 0.097. Against the strongest learning-based baselines, QuartDepth edges out BrecQ (0.076→0.071 AbsRel on W4A8 NYUv2) and AdaRound (0.084→0.071), with the gap widening dramatically under W4A4 (BrecQ 0.469, AdaRound 0.501 vs. Ours 0.097). The weight-only OBS method (W4) remains the closest competitor to Float32, but QuartDepth's W4A8 configuration matches or slightly exceeds OBS while also quantizing activations, and its W4A4 configuration far surpasses OBS in model size reduction. On KITTI, the same pattern holds: QuartDepth W4A8 achieves AbsRel 0.055 (vs. BrecQ 0.057, AdaRound 0.057), and W4A4 achieves 0.070 (vs. BrecQ 0.434, AdaRound 0.482), confirming consistent gains across indoor and outdoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) literature—building directly on AdaRound for adaptive rounding and KFAC for Hessian approximation in weight reconstruction—by introducing a domain-specific activation polishing step (LogNP) tailored to the persistent extreme outliers observed in MDE decoder layers. It departs from prior efficient depth estimation work (pruning, distillation, architecture search) that targeted smaller models, instead addressing the newer, larger foundational MDE models (Metric3D, Depth Anything) that prior compression techniques were not designed for. The hardware contribution positions the work alongside ASIC accelerators for vision transformers (e.g., ViTCOD, FLAT) but is the first to co-design a programmable accelerator specifically around a quantized MDE pipeline with kernel fusion for polishing and dequantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that near-lossless W4A8 and substantially degraded-but-usable W4A4 quantization of large foundational MDE models is achievable on a 28nm ASIC at real-time frame rates (up to 26 FPS), which is practically important for robotics, autonomous driving, and AR where power budgets are tight. A key open question is whether the LogNP polishing generalizes beyond MDE decoders to other tasks with similar per-channel outlier structures (e.g., segmentation, detection heads). Limitations include evaluation on only two model families (Metric3D, Depth Anything), a single ASIC implementation at 28nm, and reliance on a 32-sample calibration set whose sensitivity to domain shift is not thoroughly explored.
