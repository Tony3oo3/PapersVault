## Problem

Text-to-3D generation lacks a standardized, human-aligned evaluation metric: similarity-based measures (PSNR, Chamfer Distance) require ground-truth references that do not exist when multiple valid outputs correspond to a single prompt, CLIP scores capture only text fidelity, and FID requires a reference distribution that is impractical to establish. General-purpose vLLMs like GPT-4o are not specifically trained for 3D quality assessment and perform significantly worse than a domain-specific evaluator in aligning with human judgments.

## Input / Output

Gen3DEval consumes up to 8 rendered images per comparison (4 per object, uniformly sampled from a 360° azimuth at fixed elevation), each encoded as 576 visual tokens, in either RGB or surface-normal format, alongside a QA comparison instruction (~250 text tokens). The generation prompt is supplied only for text-fidelity evaluation and omitted for appearance and surface scoring, making those two dimensions prompt-agnostic and applicable to image-to-3D methods. The model outputs a natural-language preference decision that is parsed to yield a pairwise win/loss, which is then aggregated across all prompt-method pairs via the ELO rating system into a final ranking per dimension.

## Method

Gen3DEval is an 8.35B-parameter vLLM built on the LLaVA architecture with a Llama3 backbone and a CLIP image encoder, trained in two stages. Stage 1 (pre-training) freezes the LLM and encoder, updating only the linear projection matrix on 141K artist-created 3D mesh renderings (single- and multi-view RGB and normals) paired with image-summary VQA captions to align visual and language token spaces. Stage 2 (SFT) jointly fine-tunes the projector and LLM on human preference annotations (5K+ pairwise comparisons across 13 generative methods) and a large synthetic dataset created by applying controlled perturbations—Laplacian smoothing, floaters, transparency errors, disconnected components, texture seams, and caption modifications via Llama3.2—to artist meshes, NeRF, and Gaussian splatting assets. The key design choice is injecting 3D aesthetic and quality priors directly into the vision-language space through synthetic artifact simulation rather than relying on similarity to a reference, and the CLIP encoder was selected over DinoV2 and Fit3D based on ablations showing superior out-of-domain generalization across all three evaluation dimensions.

## Results

Gen3DEval (CLIP) achieves 0.90 accuracy on human-annotated appearance evaluation, 0.89 on out-of-domain appearance, 0.99 on synthetic surface quality, and 0.86 on out-of-domain text fidelity, substantially outperforming all baselines. The strongest vLLM baseline, GPT-4o, reaches only 0.69 on OOD appearance and 0.55 on OOD text fidelity. On Gen3DEval-Bench (80 prompts), Trellis ranks first overall (appearance rank 1, surface rank 1, text fidelity rank 1), while DreamFusion ranks last among text-to-3D methods (appearance 8, text fidelity 8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gen3DEval (CLIP) decisively outperforms all classical metrics: CLIP score achieves only 0.17 on OOD appearance vs. Gen3DEval's 0.89, and ImageReward/PickScore trail by 0.23 and 0.55 respectively on OOD appearance. Among vLLM baselines, GPT-4o is the strongest competitor but still lags Gen3DEval by 0.20 on OOD appearance (0.69 vs. 0.89), 0.13 on OOD surface (0.54 vs. 0.67), and 0.31 on OOD text fidelity (0.55 vs. 0.86). Smaller vLLMs (Phi-3.5-Vision, LLaVA variants, PaliGemma) perform near chance or worse, with PaliGemma scoring as low as 0.02 on human appearance. On synthetic surface quality, Gen3DEval achieves 0.99 accuracy, far exceeding GPT-4o (0.54) and all other baselines. The only dimension where a classical metric is competitive is OOD text fidelity, where ImageReward and PickScore reach 0.85 (vs. Gen3DEval's 0.86), but both collapse on appearance and surface evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gen3DEval builds on the LLaVA architecture and Llama3 backbone, adapting the vision-language pre-training and fine-tuning pipeline to the specific domain of 3D object quality assessment. It departs fundamentally from similarity-based metrics (PSNR, Chamfer Distance, FID) that require ground-truth references, and from CLIP scores that capture only text fidelity, by instead injecting 3D aesthetic priors into a fine-tuned vLLM that performs holistic pairwise comparison across appearance, surface quality, and text fidelity. The closest prior work, GPT4VEval, used the task-agnostic GPT-4V model off-the-shelf; Gen3DEval differentiates itself through domain-specific fine-tuning on curated synthetic perturbations and human preference data, and by providing an open-weight 8.35B parameter model rather than a proprietary API.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper addresses a genuine gap in text-to-3D research: the absence of a standardized, ground-truth-free, multi-dimensional evaluation metric that aligns with human judgment. The Gen3DEval-Bench (80 prompts) and the ELO-based ranking pipeline offer a practical, reproducible benchmark that the community can adopt for comparing new generation methods. Key limitations include erratic performance on janus artifacts, limited out-of-domain generalization for surface evaluation due to scarce annotated data, and the confounding influence of text-to-image pipelines on image-to-3D methods. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to emerging 3D representations beyond NeRF and Gaussian splatting, and whether the 80-prompt benchmark is sufficient for robust method comparison as the field scales.
