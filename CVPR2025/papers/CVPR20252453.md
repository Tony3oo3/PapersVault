## Problem

Existing PBR material generation methods for 3D objects are either per-case optimization approaches that lack scalability, multi-model pipelines (e.g., SAM + GPT) that suffer from instability, or methods sensitive to specific lighting conditions. No prior work provides a single feed-forward model that robustly handles the full spectrum of input scenarios—texture-less meshes, albedo-only objects, realistically lit scans, and generated objects with physically inconsistent lighting—within one unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252453/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any 3D mesh in one of four states (texture-less, albedo-only, scanned with realistic lighting, or generated with unrealistic lighting) plus a text prompt, and produces four PBR material channels (albedo, roughness, metallic, bump) as UV-unwrapped texture maps ready for direct use in rendering engines. For texture-less inputs, a preliminary coarse texture is synthesized via a depth-conditioned Stable Diffusion model before material estimation begins. The final output is a set of UV-space material maps with occlusion holes filled and seams smoothed by a dedicated UV-space refiner.

## Method

Material Anything adapts a pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net with a triple-head architecture—three separate output branches for albedo, roughness-metallic, and bump maps sharing middle layers—to decouple material channels while preserving cross-modal consistency. A confidence mask is injected as a conditioning input to dynamically switch the model between material estimation (high confidence, realistic lighting) and material generation (low confidence, absent or synthetic lighting), with adaptive intermediate values for generated objects. Training combines v-prediction loss with an LPIPS-based rendering loss that decodes predicted materials back into images via differentiable rendering under random lighting, bridging the domain gap between natural images and PBR maps. For multi-view consistency, a progressive generation scheme initializes each new view's noise latent from previously estimated materials and uses the confidence mask to guide inpainting of unknown regions. A final UV-space diffusion refiner, conditioned on a canonical coordinate map, fills occlusion holes and smooths baking seams to produce seamless UV-ready material maps.

## Results

On texture-less objects, Material Anything achieves the best FID of 100.63 and CLIP score of 31.06, outperforming all texture-generation baselines (Text2Tex: FID 116.41, SyncMVD: FID 118.46, Paint3D: FID 153.20) and optimization-based methods (NvDiffRec: FID 103.81, DreamMat: FID 113.34). On textured objects, it achieves FID 101.19 and CLIP 89.70, surpassing the retrieval-based Make-it-Real (FID 104.38, CLIP 88.62). The method is a feed-forward model, avoiding the per-object optimization cost of NvDiffRec and DreamMat while still producing superior material quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Material Anything outperforms all texture-generation baselines on both FID and CLIP for texture-less inputs: it beats Text2Tex (FID 116.41→100.63, CLIP 30.33→31.06), SyncMVD (FID 118.46→100.63), and Paint3D (FID 153.20→100.63) by wide margins. Against optimization-based methods, it improves FID over NvDiffRec (103.81→100.63) and DreamMat (113.34→100.63) while being a feed-forward model rather than requiring per-object optimization. For textured inputs, it surpasses the retrieval-based Make-it-Real on both metrics (FID 104.38→101.19, CLIP 88.62→89.70), with qualitative gains in fine detail preservation and shadow removal. The paper also reports comparable visual quality to closed-source systems Rodin Gen-1 and Tripo3D despite using a significantly smaller training dataset (80K objects vs. their undisclosed larger corpora).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) as its generative backbone, adapting them from natural image synthesis to PBR material estimation via a triple-head U-Net architecture inspired by Clay. It departs from prior material generation work in two key ways: (1) unlike optimization-based methods (NvDiffRec, DreamMat) that require per-object fitting, it operates as a feed-forward model; and (2) unlike texture generation methods (Text2Tex, Paint3D) that produce lighting-entangled textures, it explicitly disentangles material properties. The confidence mask mechanism is the paper's central conceptual departure, unifying previously separate scenarios (texture-less, albedo-only, scanned, generated) under a single model rather than treating each as a distinct task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified feed-forward framework addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D content pipelines: the inability to reliably assign physically-based materials to arbitrary meshes without per-object optimization or fragile multi-model pipelines. The confidence mask as a dynamic switch between estimation and generation modes is a clean, generalizable idea that could transfer to other lighting-variant tasks. Limitations include dependence on a separate texture generation step for textureless objects, the two-stage (image-space then UV-space) pipeline adding complexity, and evaluation limited to FID/CLIP scores without user studies or downstream application benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-poly meshes, whether the progressive multi-view strategy can be made real-time, and how well it generalizes to object categories underrepresented in Objaverse.
