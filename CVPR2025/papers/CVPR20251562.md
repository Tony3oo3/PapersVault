## Problem

In-bed human mesh recovery is critical for healthcare monitoring but suffers from severe real-world data scarcity due to privacy and cost constraints. Existing methods (e.g., BodyMAP) rely heavily on real data and exhibit significant performance degradation when the proportion of real samples in training is low, failing to effectively bridge the sim-to-real domain gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251562/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel overhead depth image (captured from a fixed camera above the bed) and a 2-element one-hot gender flag as input. It outputs an 88-dimensional SMPL parameter vector (10 shape coefficients, 69 joint angles, 3 translation, and 6 rotation components) which is fed into the SMPL model to produce a 6,890-vertex 3D human mesh. The depth images may include blanket occlusions (thin sheet, thick blanket, or uncovered), and the model must handle the inherent ambiguity where multiple body configurations can produce similar depth patterns.

## Method

The framework reformulates in-bed mesh recovery as a conditional diffusion process over SMPL parameters rather than images: a U-Net-style network with 6 downsampling residual blocks and 3 attention blocks denoises noisy SMPL parameters conditioned on the depth image via adaptive normalization layers (replacing batch/layer norm) that inject depth features and timestep embeddings as scale/shift parameters. Crucially, the model predicts the clean SMPL parameters x₀ (x₀-prediction) at each denoising step rather than the noise term, ensuring all intermediate estimates correspond to feasible human body configurations. Training follows a two-stage Sim-to-Real strategy: first, the model is trained on 97,495 synthetic depth samples (BodyPressureSD) with a fixed learning rate to establish a strong prior over resting poses, then fine-tuned on limited real SLP data using a linearly adjusted learning rate scheduler that adapts to the available real-data quantity. Inference uses DDIM sampling with only 5 timesteps for efficiency.

## Results

On the SLP dataset, DiSRT-In-Bed achieves the best MPJPE and PVE across all real-data availability settings. With only 11% of real training data (Sim+11%Real), it reaches 74.37 mm MPJPE and 78.03 mm PVE, outperforming BodyMAP (85.79/90.14) and BodyPressure (89.87/104.67). With full real data (Sim+100%Real), it achieves 50.81 mm MPJPE and 61.18 mm PVE, a 11% improvement over BodyMAP (57.06/69.95) and a 30% improvement over BodyPressure (72.93/86.78). Under occlusion (thick blanket, Cover 2), it maintains 52.65 mm MPJPE vs. 59.55 mm for BodyMAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiSRT-In-Bed outperforms all three baselines (HMR2.0, BodyPressure, BodyMAP) on both MPJPE and PVE at every real-data ratio from 11% to 100%. Against BodyMAP, the closest in-bed-specific competitor, the improvement is 11% in MPJPE at full real data (50.81 vs. 57.06 mm) and 13% at 11% real data (74.37 vs. 85.79 mm). Against BodyPressure, the gap widens substantially with more real data: 30% MPJPE improvement at 100% real data (50.81 vs. 72.93 mm), though BodyPressure is slightly better in the sim-only setting (103.47 vs. 109.73 mm) due to its dedicated shape-prediction network. HMR2.0, a general-purpose RGB mesh recovery model adapted to depth input, is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing DiSRT-In-Bed by 26–34 mm in MPJPE across all splits. Under heavy occlusion (thick blanket), DiSRT-In-Bed maintains the smallest performance drop (52.65 mm MPJPE) compared to BodyMAP (59.55 mm) and BodyPressure (75.36 mm), confirming superior robustness to covering conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-bed human mesh recovery line of work (PressureNet, BodyPressure, BodyMAP) by reformulating the task from a direct depth-to-mesh regression into a conditional generative problem over SMPL parameters using a diffusion model. It builds on BodyPressure's physics-based simulation pipeline for synthetic data generation and on DDPM's forward/reverse diffusion formulation, but applies the denoising process to the low-dimensional SMPL parameter vector rather than to images. The conditioning mechanism borrows adaptive layer normalization from DiT, and the overall U-Net backbone follows the latent diffusion architecture, distinguishing it from prior diffusion-based HMR methods (HMDiff, ScoreHMR) that operate on 2D keypoints or full image latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical pathway for deploying in-bed monitoring in clinical settings where labeled real data is scarce due to privacy and cost constraints, and the decoupled synthetic-then-fine-tune training strategy is a reusable recipe for other data-scarce sensing tasks. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (SLP) with one home and one hospital setting, and the hospital evaluation lacks ground truth, leaving open questions about robustness across truly diverse environments, sensor types, and body conditions. Whether the diffusion-based generative formulation generalizes beyond in-bed scenarios to other occluded or data-limited pose estimation settings remains unexplored.
