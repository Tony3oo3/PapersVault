## Problem

Existing dense feature matching methods (DKM, RoMa) are designed for perspective images and degrade significantly on equirectangular projection (ERP) omnidirectional images due to severe geometric distortions, particularly near the poles. While cubemap decomposition avoids distortion, it requires multiple independent inferences per image pair and loses the global context of a single spherical view. No learning-based dense matching method had been designed specifically for omnidirectional images prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251877/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of ERP images resized to 640×320 as input and produces dense correspondences expressed as 3D Cartesian coordinates on the unit sphere (i.e., ray directions of a spherical camera), along with a per-pixel certainty map. During training, ground-truth depth maps and aligned camera poses are used to derive supervision via spherical warping. At evaluation, up to 5,000 matches are randomly sampled after applying a certainty threshold of 0.8, and the relative pose is estimated via a modified 8-point algorithm for spherical cameras.

## Method

EDM reformulates DKM's kernelized matching in 3D Cartesian coordinates on a unit sphere rather than 2D pixel space. The Spherical Spatial Alignment Module (SSAM) performs coarse global matching via Gaussian Process regression, where the feature grid is embedded using 3D Cartesian coordinates obtained by inverse-projecting the 2D ERP grid onto the sphere (π⁻¹), making the kernel distortion-aware. A CNN decoder then produces initial 3D matches and a certainty map. The Geodesic Flow Refinement stage iteratively projects 3D matches into 2D ERP space for linear residual updates (where standard warp refinement works), then back-projects to 3D, enabling multi-scale refinement while respecting spherical geometry. The regression loss uses cosine similarity (angular difference) instead of Euclidean distance to constrain predictions to the sphere surface, and azimuth rotation augmentation during training accounts for the non-uniqueness of ERP representations of a single spherical image.

## Results

On Matterport3D, EDM achieves AUC@5° of 45.15, AUC@10° of 60.99, and AUC@20° of 73.60 in relative pose estimation, improving AUC@5° by +26.72 over the best dense perspective baseline (DKM, 18.43). On Stanford2D3D (zero-shot, no additional training), EDM reaches AUC@5° of 55.08, AUC@10° of 71.65, and AUC@20° of 82.72, a +42.62 improvement over DKM (12.46). EDM also demonstrates robustness to decreasing overlap ratio, maintaining similar performance down to 60% overlap where other methods degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDM substantially outperforms all four baselines on both Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D. Against the sparse spherical methods, SPHORB (AUC@5° = 0.38) and SphereGlue (11.29), EDM's dense ERP approach achieves 45.15 on Matterport3D, a 4× improvement over SphereGlue and a >100× improvement over SPHORB. Against the dense perspective methods, DKM (18.43) and RoMa (12.45), EDM gains +26.72 and +32.70 in AUC@5° respectively on Matterport3D, and +42.62 and +43.60 on Stanford2D3D. The gap widens in low-overlap scenarios: while DKM and RoMa degrade sharply as overlap decreases, EDM maintains comparable performance down to 60% overlap. On Stanford2D3D, where panorama images contain missing upper/lower regions, EDM's spherical positional embedding enables accurate matching that perspective-based methods cannot achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDM directly extends DKM (Edstedt et al., CVPR 2023), which introduced kernelized global matching via Gaussian Process regression and iterative warp refinement for perspective images. The key conceptual departure is reformulating the matching problem from 2D pixel-space displacements to 3D Cartesian coordinates on a unit sphere, thereby making the method inherently distortion-aware for equirectangular projection images. This positions EDM between two prior paradigms: sparse spherical matchers (SPHORB, SphereGlue) that handle ERP geometry but lack dense coverage, and perspective dense matchers (DKM, RoMa) that ignore spherical distortion. The spherical positional embedding and bidirectional Cartesian–spherical coordinate transformation during refinement are the novel architectural contributions that bridge these two lines of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first learning-based dense matching method for omnidirectional images, EDM opens the door to applying the full suite of dense-matching downstream tasks (triangulation, visual localization, mapping) to 360° imagery without the computational overhead and information loss of cubemap decomposition. The substantial AUC gains (+26.72, +42.62 at 5°) over both sparse spherical and perspective dense baselines suggest that explicitly modeling spherical geometry is critical rather than merely beneficial. However, the method is trained exclusively on gravity-aligned indoor panoramas, leaving open questions about robustness to extreme camera tilts, outdoor scenes, and dynamic environments. Future work on diversifying training data and extending to multi-view localization would strengthen the practical impact.
