## Problem

Existing multimodal retrieval systems predominantly support single-modality queries and text-only documents, falling short of real-world scenarios where both queries and documents are composed of interleaved images and text. Moreover, prior feature-fusion approaches (e.g., UniIR, PreFLMR) extract representations only from the final layer of vision-language backbones, discarding low-level visual details (e.g., jersey numbers, fine textures) that shallower layers naturally encode and that may be critical for diverse retrieval queries.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multimodal query (an RGB image paired with an instruction-plus-question text) and a multimodal document (a text passage with an optional accompanying image) as input. Rather than producing a single embedding per item, ReT outputs k=32 latent tokens (projected to 128 dimensions) for both the query and the document, which are then compared via a fine-grained late-interaction score: the sum over query tokens of the maximum dot-product with any document token. This token-level output enables fine-grained local matching that a single global vector cannot capture.

## Method

ReT introduces a Transformer-based recurrent cell that sequentially processes features from every layer of frozen CLIP visual and textual backbones (ViT-B through ViT-G). At each layer l, the cell fuses three inputs—its previous hidden state h_l, the l-th layer visual features E_l^V, and the l-th layer textual features E_l^T—via three parallel branches: a self-attention residual (candidate state) and two cross-attention modules (one per modality). LSTM-inspired sigmoidal gates control information flow: a forget gate determines how much to retain from shallower layers, while separate visual and textual input gates modulate each modality's contribution at the current layer, allowing the model to dynamically emphasize low-level or high-level features. Training uses a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss where the standard cosine similarity is replaced by the fine-grained max-sim late-interaction score, and query and document encoders maintain independent parameters for asymmetric representation.

## Results

On the M2KR benchmark with CLIP ViT-L, ReT achieves 73.4 R@10 on WIT, 81.8 R@1 on IGLUE, 63.5 R@5 on KVQA, 82.0 R@5 on OVEN, and 79.9 R@1 on LLaVA, consistently outperforming all baselines. On the M-BEIR benchmark (multimodal documents), ReT reaches 56.3 R@5 on OVEN and 36.5 R@5 on InfoSeek after fine-tuning. In retrieval-augmented generation on InfoSeek with LLaMA-3.1-8B, ReT achieves 22.1 All-score (top-3), substantially exceeding CLIP (17.6) and Pre-FLMR (17.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReT outperforms Pre-FLMR by large margins on most M2KR datasets with CLIP ViT-L: +12.9 R@10 on WIT, +12.6 R@1 on IGLUE, +19.9 R@5 on KVQA, +22.2 R@5 on OVEN, and +8.1 R@1 on LLaVA. The only losses are on E-VQA PR@5 (57.9 vs. 70.8) and OKVQA PR@5 (66.2 vs. 68.5), which the authors attribute to Pre-FLMR's three-stage training that oversamples these datasets. Against UniIR (Feature Fusion), ReT trained on M2KR already surpasses it on InfoSeek (28.3 vs. 22.4 R@5 on M-BEIR), and after fine-tuning on M-BEIR, ReT wins on both OVEN (56.3 vs. 55.8) and InfoSeek (36.5 vs. 33.0). All CLIP-based baselines (Feature Averaging, Unimodal, Feature Fusion) are substantially outperformed, with the Feature Fusion variant being the closest competitor but still trailing by 5–15 points across datasets. In the RAG setting, ReT improves over CLIP by +4.5 All-score and over Pre-FLMR by +4.6 All-score (top-3, LLaMA-3.1-8B), confirming that multi-layer multimodal retrieval yields more useful passages for downstream generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReT extends the fine-grained late-interaction multimodal retrieval paradigm established by ColBERT, FLMR, and PreFLMR by replacing single-layer feature fusion with a Transformer-based recurrent cell that progressively integrates representations from all layers of both visual and textual backbones. The gating mechanism is directly inspired by LSTM cell dynamics (forget and input gates), but repurposed: rather than managing temporal memory, the gates control how much information from each hierarchical layer and modality to retain. This departs from UniIR and PreFLMR, which fuse only last-layer features, by hypothesizing that shallow-layer representations (capturing low-level visual details like jersey numbers or colors) are essential for diverse multimodal queries. The late-interaction scoring (max-sim over token pairs) follows ColBERT/FLMR, but ReT reduces the token count to 32 learnable vectors, trading representational breadth for computational efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world retrieval increasingly requires both multimodal queries (image + text) and multimodal documents (interleaved text and images), a setting where prior methods like PreFLMR only handle text-only documents. The demonstrated +26.6 R@5 gain over PreFLMR on OVEN and the strong RAG results (21.6 vs 16.9 with LLaMA-3-8B on InfoSeek) show that multi-layer fusion and document images materially improve downstream utility. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to additional modalities (video, audio) as the authors note, and the observation that larger visual encoders (ViT-H) do not consistently outperform smaller ones (ViT-L) suggests the recurrent fusion may be sensitive to backbone quality rather than capacity. The 32-token design also raises questions about the optimal trade-off between token count and retrieval granularity for very large-scale corpora.
