## Problem

Egocentric motion understanding from wearable devices is fundamentally under-constrained: the wearer's body is rarely visible in first-person videos, and sparse motion sensors (head, wrists) cannot determine full-body pose, especially the lower body. No prior framework unifies kinematic motion tracking and semantic motion narration from these complementary sparse modalities within a single model, leaving a gap between precise sensor tracking and contextual video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EgoLM consumes sparse 6-DoF sensor sequences (three-point or one-point poses from glasses/watches), egocentric video frames encoded by CLIP into per-frame vectors, and text instructions, all interleaved in a single token sequence. It produces either full-body 3D motion (279-dim per-frame representation of root velocity, 6D rotations, and 22-joint angles/velocities, decoded from motion tokens via PQ-VAE) or natural language narrations. The auto-regressive formulation supports online inference where each new sensor frame is appended to history for a single forward pass.

## Method

EgoLM is trained in three stages: (1) a Motion PQ-VAE tokenizer replaces vanilla VQ-VAE with product quantization across multiple codebooks (2×8192 entries, dim 64) for higher-fidelity motion tokenization; (2) motion-augmented LM pre-training expands GPT-2's codebook for motion tokens, uses token interleaving to arrange multi-codebook tokens, and adds a motion regression loss (blending predicted logits with codebook entries) to bridge the discrete-token-to-continuous-representation gap; (3) multi-modal multi-task instruction tuning jointly trains four tasks (3pt/1pt motion tracking, motion narration, text-to-motion, motion-to-text) by encoding sensor data with a fully convolutional encoder, video frames with CLIP, and interleaving all features with text instructions in a unified template. This joint training implicitly connects sparse sensor poses to natural language, enabling the novel task of motion narration directly from three-points and egocentric videos without requiring ground-truth motion as intermediate input.

## Results

On the Nymeria dataset, EgoLM achieves 73.38 mm full-body joint position error for three-points + video motion tracking, outperforming the adapted AvatarPoser* (127.08 mm) by over 50 mm and demonstrating that egocentric video context substantially improves tracking. For one-point + video tracking, EgoLM reaches 106.95 mm full-body error, beating EgoEgo (132.16 mm) by 25 mm. For motion narration from three-points + video, the joint-training variant achieves 19.40 BERT score and 45.45 BLEU@1, surpassing MotionGPT (14.09 BERT) and TM2T (11.08 BERT) which use full-body motion as input. The motion PQ-VAE tokenizer reduces reconstruction MPJPE from 51.60 mm (vanilla VQ-VAE) to 34.49 mm, a 33% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For three-points-only motion tracking, EgoLM (83.88 mm full-body error) is competitive with BoDiffusion (79.80 mm) and AvatarPoser (85.89 mm), with a notable advantage in lower-body tracking (148.37 vs. 152.68 and 165.18 mm). When egocentric video is added, EgoLM dramatically outperforms the adapted AvatarPoser* (73.38 vs. 127.08 mm full-body error), showing that the LLM-based framework effectively fuses video context while the task-specific method fails to exploit it. For one-point tracking, EgoLM beats EgoEgo by 25 mm in full-body error (127.45 vs. 132.16 mm) and gains a further 20 mm improvement with video input (106.95 mm). For motion narration, EgoLM's joint-training variant (19.40 BERT, 45.45 BLEU@1) outperforms MotionGPT (14.09 BERT, 42.22 BLEU@1) and TM2T (11.08 BERT, 40.11 BLEU@1) despite not using full-body motion as input, relying instead on sparse three-points and egocentric video. The motion-only variant of EgoLM (M2T&T2M: 15.90 BERT) already surpasses both baselines, and adding video (MV2T&T2M: 20.32 BERT) yields the best overall narration quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoLM extends the LLM-based motion generation paradigm established by MotionGPT and T2M-GPT by replacing the encoder-decoder architecture with a more scalable decoder-only LM (GPT-2) and introducing product quantization (PQ-VAE) in place of vanilla VQ-VAE for motion tokenization. It draws on the multi-modal instruction tuning recipe from LLaVA but generalizes it to handle substantially more modalities (sparse 6-DoF sensor data, egocentric video, motion, and text) and tasks (kinematic tracking alongside semantic narration) with greater inter-modal disparity. Conceptually, it departs from dedicated motion tracking methods (AvatarPoser, BoDiffusion, EgoEgo) by unifying tracking and understanding in a single autoregressive model, and from prior motion narration work (TM2T, MotionGPT) by incorporating environmental context from egocentric video rather than relying solely on kinematic input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoLM demonstrates that a single decoder-only LM can serve as a generalist for both kinematic (motion tracking) and semantic (motion narration) egocentric understanding, enabling novel AR use cases like generating natural language descriptions from sparse wearable sensors and egocentric video alone. The finding that naively concatenating CLIP video features with sensor input (adapted AvatarPoser*) actually degrades performance substantially validates the need for the multi-task instruction tuning strategy to bridge modality gaps. Key limitations include the motion tokenizer bounding tracking accuracy, CLIP's per-frame compression limiting fine-grained object identification, and the well-known hallucination problem in LLMs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger LMs and more complex multi-person or long-horizon scenarios, and whether the Nymeria-specific evaluation generalizes to other egocentric datasets.
