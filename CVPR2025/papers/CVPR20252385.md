## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion struggle to accurately depict human-object interactions because CLIP's strong object bias causes it to overlook semantic distinctions between interaction verbs (e.g., 'walking' vs. 'riding' a bicycle). Prior methods that add bounding boxes or LLM-based layout planning still fail to capture verb-level semantics, as demonstrated by InteractDiffusion producing incorrect interactions even with explicit grounding boxes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252385/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt in the template 'A photo of a {human} {verb} a/an {object}' (e.g., 'A photo of a person holding a backpack') and produces a 512×512 RGB image with no additional conditions such as bounding boxes, keypoints, or layout specifications. Training is performed on HICO-DET's 600 HOI categories (80 objects × 117 verbs), with prompts realigned so each image corresponds to exactly one human-object-interaction triplet.

## Method

VerbDiff fine-tunes only the cross-attention layers of Stable Diffusion v1.4 with two novel guidance losses. First, Relation Disentanglement Guidance (RDG) identifies the most frequent verb for each human-object pair as an anchor, then applies a triplet loss to push generated image features away from the anchor sentence while pulling toward the target interaction sentence, combined with a masked image-alignment loss and an effective-number-of-samples scaling factor to handle the extreme long-tail distribution of verbs. Second, Interaction Direction Guidance (IDG) uses an Interaction Region module that computes centroids from cross-attention maps of the <h, r, o> tokens to localize the interaction area without bounding boxes, then aligns the direction of full-image feature differences with the direction of interaction-region feature differences. The total loss combines the standard diffusion reconstruction loss (masked to the relevant region), RDG, and IDG with fixed weights (λ₁=1.0, λ₂=10, λ₃=0.8).

## Results

On the HICO-DET test set, VerbDiff achieves the highest HOI classification accuracy across all settings: 27.05 (Default/Full) and 28.43 (Known Object/Full) with the SOV-STG-Swin-L detector, and 22.59/24.79 with SOV-STG-S. In similarity evaluation, VerbDiff scores 0.733 CLIP T2T, 0.633 S-BERT T2T, and 0.771 VQA I2T, outperforming all baselines on text-to-text and VQA metrics. The method also achieves the best rare-class HOI accuracy (13.18 KO/Rare with Swin-L), indicating robustness to long-tail interaction verbs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VerbDiff outperforms all three baselines on every HOI accuracy metric. Against Stable Diffusion (SD), VerbDiff gains +6.97 points on Default/Full HOI accuracy (27.05 vs. 20.08) and +6.74 on Known Object/Full (28.43 vs. 21.69) with Swin-L, while also leading on S-BERT T2T (0.633 vs. 0.620) and VQA I2T (0.771 vs. 0.769); the only metric where SD edges out VerbDiff is CLIP T2I (0.251 vs. 0.242). Compared to GLIGEN, which requires explicit bounding boxes, VerbDiff improves Default/Full HOI accuracy by +9.22 points (27.05 vs. 17.83) and S-BERT T2T by 0.079 (0.633 vs. 0.554), demonstrating that text-only conditioning with interaction-aware guidance surpasses grounding-box-based methods. Against InteractDiffusion, the closest competitor that also uses HOI triplets with bounding boxes, VerbDiff leads by +3.52 on Default/Full (27.05 vs. 23.53) and +3.57 on KO/Full (28.43 vs. 24.86), with a particularly large gap on rare classes (+2.33 KO/Rare: 13.18 vs. 11.18), confirming that semantic disentanglement of interaction verbs is more effective than spatial grounding alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

VerbDiff builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture, fine-tuning only the cross-attention layers to capture interaction semantics. It departs from the dominant line of HOI generation work (InteractDiffusion, GLIGEN) that relies on explicit bounding boxes or grounding conditions, instead extracting interaction regions from internal cross-attention maps and using frequency-based anchor texts to disentangle verb semantics. The method also repurposes the centroid extraction mechanism from diffusion self-guidance (Epstein et al.) for localizing interaction regions and adapts the effective-number-of-samples balancing from long-tail classification to handle the extreme class imbalance across 600 HOI categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that text-only diffusion models can match or exceed bounding-box-conditioned methods on HOI accuracy, suggesting that the field may not need expensive spatial annotations to capture interaction semantics. The introduction of S-BERT as a complementary evaluation metric is practically useful, as it reveals semantic gaps in interaction verbs that CLIP's object bias masks. However, the approach is validated only on HICO-DET with a single base model (SD v1.4) and fine-tunes only cross-attention layers, leaving open whether the guidance losses transfer to larger or newer diffusion architectures, whether they generalize beyond the 600 HOI categories, and whether the modest T2I CLIP score drop (0.242 vs. SD's 0.251) indicates a trade-off in overall image quality.
