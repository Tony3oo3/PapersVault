## Problem

Mamba-based visual models suffer from three inherent SSM limitations: causal recurrence cannot access future context in 2D images, recurrent memory decay causes long-range forgetting, and the 2D-to-1D sequence transformation destroys spatial structural dependencies. These constraints prevent vision Mamba from matching the performance of transformer and CNN backbones on classification and dense prediction tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1K, variable resolution for COCO detection, 32×32 for CIFAR-100) and produces class labels, bounding boxes, or segmentation masks depending on the task head. Internally, the 2D feature map is flattened to a 1D sequence for SSM processing, then reshaped back to 2D where the spatial adaptor operates. The method supports three deployment modes: a full backbone trained from scratch, a booster appended to a pretrained VMamba (requiring only 10 extra epochs), and a parallel-branch adaptor for parameter-efficient transfer learning with zero initialization.

## Method

Mamba-Adaptor inserts two lightweight modules into the SSM solving pipeline. Adaptor-T addresses temporal decay by using a learnable linear predictor to dynamically select K most-forgotten hidden state coordinates per timestep, then aggregates them with softmax-weighted coefficients across S bidirectional sequences, effectively providing memory retention without handcrafted patterns. Adaptor-S addresses spatial locality by applying multi-scale dilated depthwise convolutions to the reshaped 2D output, introducing image inductive bias with minimal parameter overhead. Implementation decouples the optimized Mamba kernel by substituting an identity matrix for C and a zero matrix for D, so the CUDA selective-scan operator computes only hidden states; Adaptor-T is then applied, followed by explicit matrix multiplication for output and Adaptor-S for spatial aggregation. Two insertion forms are provided: sequential (for from-scratch training) and parallel with zero initialization and weight-sharing coefficients (for transfer learning and booster settings).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, Mamba-Adaptor-b1 (7.8M params, 1.4G FLOPs) achieves 78.4% top-1 accuracy, surpassing Vim-T by 2.3% and LocalViM-T by 2.2% at similar FLOPs. At the larger scale, Mamba-Adaptor-b2 (32M params, 5.4G FLOPs) reaches 83.0% top-1, exceeding Swin-T by 1.7% and VMamba-T by 0.4%. On COCO object detection with Mask R-CNN (1× schedule), Mamba-Adaptor-b2 achieves 47.3 AP^b and 43.4 AP^m, outperforming VMamba-T by 0.8 AP^b and 1.3 AP^m. For transfer learning, Mamba-Adaptor-T uses only 5.56% of parameters yet retains 90% of full fine-tuning performance on CIFAR-100 (86.82% vs. 87.48%), far exceeding VPT (80.68%) and linear probing (61.23%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vision Mamba baselines, Mamba-Adaptor-b1 outperforms Vim-T (76.1%) and LocalViM-T (76.2%) by 2.3% and 2.2% top-1 on ImageNet-1K at comparable FLOPs, while Mamba-Adaptor-b2 exceeds VMamba-T (82.6%) and LocalVMamba-T (82.7%) by 0.4% and 0.3% respectively. Compared to transformer and CNN backbones, Mamba-Adaptor-b2 (83.0%) surpasses Swin-T (81.3%), ConvNeXt-T (82.1%), and DeiT-S (79.9%), but trails InternImage-T (83.5%) by 0.5%. On COCO detection (1×), Mamba-Adaptor-b2 leads VMamba-T by 0.8 AP^b (47.3 vs. 46.5) and 1.3 AP^m (43.4 vs. 42.1), and outperforms Swin-T by 4.6 AP^b. In transfer learning, Mamba-Adaptor dramatically outperforms VPT (e.g., 86.82% vs. 80.68% on CIFAR-100 for VMamba-T) and linear probing (61.23%), while using only 5.56% of trainable parameters versus 100% for full fine-tuning. The method also generalizes to bi-directional scanning variants (ViM-Adaptor, VMamba-Bi-Adaptor), showing consistent gains of +6 to +9% over VPT on transfer tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mamba/SSM line of work (Gu & Dao, 2023) and its visual adaptation in VMamba (Liu et al., 2024), targeting three specific limitations of sequential SSM processing for 2D images: causal inaccessibility to future states, long-range memory decay, and loss of spatial structure from 2D-to-1D transformation. Rather than modifying the scanning strategy (as ViM's bidirectional scan, VMamba's cross-scan, or LocalMamba's windowed scan do), it intervenes inside the SSM solving equations themselves—decoupling the optimized Mamba kernel by substituting identity/zero matrices for C/D, then inserting a learnable memory-retention module (Adaptor-T) on hidden states and a multi-scale dilated depthwise convolution (Adaptor-S) on the reshaped 2D output. The adapter concept also draws from the visual adapter literature (ViT-Adaptor, AdaptFormer, VPT), but is specifically tailored to the recursive hidden-state computation unique to SSMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design offers a practical, low-overhead way to enhance existing Mamba-based vision backbones across three deployment modes (backbone, booster, transfer-learning adapter), with the transfer-learning setting being the most compelling—achieving ~90% of full fine-tuning accuracy with under 10% trainable parameters. However, the absolute gains are modest (0.1–0.2% for booster, ~2% over ViM-T for backbone), and the paper itself acknowledges that scaling behavior and applicability to other Mamba variants remain open. A key open question is whether the learnable memory-selection mechanism generalizes beyond the small K used here, and whether the depthwise-convolution spatial bias becomes redundant as Mamba architectures evolve to natively handle 2D structure.
