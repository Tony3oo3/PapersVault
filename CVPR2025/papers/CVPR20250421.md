## Problem

Existing zero-shot, training-free text-to-video methods require specific architectural modifications to particular image diffusion models, making them brittle to the rapid evolution of diffusion architectures (e.g., UNet to DiT transitions in SD1.5→SDXL→SD3). This limits both flexibility and scalability, as tools become obsolete with each new model release. The paper asks whether video generation can be achieved purely through latent-space manipulation of any off-the-shelf image diffusion model, without architectural changes or additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt as input and produces a 24-frame video sequence (GIF) as output. Internally, an LLM decomposes the prompt into per-frame conditional prompts constrained to CLIP's 77-token limit (60 tokens for a fixed scene descriptor, 15 for a dynamic component), and the diffusion process operates entirely in the latent space of the chosen base model (SD1.5, SDXL, or SD3 Medium). The entire pipeline runs on a single consumer-grade GPU (24 GB), with no architectural modifications to the underlying diffusion model.

## Method

EIDT-V exploits the uniqueness property of ODE-based diffusion trajectories: by switching the text condition at a chosen timestep t_s, the resulting image is constrained to remain within a bounded distance of the original, with earlier switching yielding more variance and later switching yielding more coherence. To achieve spatially localized control, the image is divided into an n×n grid, and a Switch Time Matrix assigns each cell its own prompt-switching time, producing a binary mask that blends latents from the original and new-prompt trajectories per cell (Eq. 6). Two in-context-trained LLaMA-3 8B modules drive the process: one generates coherent frame-wise prompts, and the other detects semantic differences between consecutive frames. These differences feed a CLIP segmentation model that produces attention maps over the previous frame, which are converted into the STM so that high-motion regions switch earlier (more variance) while static regions switch later (more coherence). The approach is fully model-agnostic, requiring only access to the latent denoising loop of any image diffusion model.

## Results

On the fair-comparison setting (SD1.5 backbone), EIDT-V achieves MS-SSIM 0.63±0.137, LPIPS 0.33±0.1, and Temporal Consistency Loss 0.152±0.062, outperforming T2V-Zero and DirecT2V on all three metrics while matching or slightly trailing Free-Bloom on MS-SSIM (0.63 vs. 0.672). With the IP-Adapter variant on SD1.5, LPIPS improves to 0.316±0.089, the best among SD1.5-based methods. Scaling to SD3 Medium yields the strongest results: MS-SSIM 0.81±0.109, LPIPS 0.184±0.08, and Temporal Consistency 0.087±0.042, demonstrating the model-agnostic pipeline's scalability. In the user study, EIDT-V SD1.5 achieves the best overall ranking (1.9/4) and the best temporal coherence score (1.9/4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against T2V-Zero [24], EIDT-V (SD1.5) wins decisively on all three metrics: MS-SSIM 0.63 vs. 0.428, LPIPS 0.33 vs. 0.404, and Temporal Consistency 0.152 vs. 0.206. Against DirecT2V [20], EIDT-V similarly dominates: MS-SSIM 0.63 vs. 0.492, LPIPS 0.33 vs. 0.445, and Temporal Consistency 0.152 vs. 0.185. The closest competitor is Free-Bloom [21], which edges out EIDT-V on MS-SSIM (0.672 vs. 0.63) and Temporal Consistency (0.159 vs. 0.152) on SD1.5, but EIDT-V achieves a lower LPIPS (0.33 vs. 0.353) and, with the IP-Adapter variant, the best LPIPS (0.316). In the user study, EIDT-V ranks first in temporal coherence (1.9 vs. Free-Bloom's 2.5) and overall satisfaction (1.9 vs. 2.3), while Free-Bloom holds a narrow edge in semantic coherence (2.0 vs. 2.1). A key differentiator is that EIDT-V requires no architectural modification to the base diffusion model (marked ✓ in Table 1), whereas all three baselines require modified architectures, and EIDT-V's performance scales substantially when paired with newer backbones (SDXL, SD3 Medium) without any retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the zero-shot, training-free text-to-video generation space by repurposing a known property of ODE-based diffusion—trajectory uniqueness and intersection—as a mechanism for controlling inter-frame variance, rather than relying on the architectural modifications (cross-attention replacement, temporal shift modules) that prior methods like T2V-Zero, DirecT2V, and Free-Bloom require. It also repurposes in-context LLMs (LLaMA-3 8B) for two distinct roles—frame-wise prompt generation and inter-frame difference detection—whereas prior LLM-based approaches (Free-Bloom, DirecT2V) used manually configured LLMs without standardized frameworks. The grid-based prompt switching with CLIP-segmentation-guided attention masking is a novel spatial control mechanism not present in any of the cited baselines, and the model-agnostic design (operating purely in latent space) deliberately departs from the architecture-specific pipelines that limit prior work's scalability across SD1.5, SDXL, and SD3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a single, unmodified image diffusion model can produce temporally coherent video without any architectural changes or additional training, running on a single 24 GB GPU—this lowers the barrier to video generation substantially. However, the paper acknowledges that using variance as a proxy for motion is an imperfect substitute, leading to occasional visual inconsistencies and artifacts (notably limb elongation, especially with SD3), and that significant late-stage prompt changes can prevent full refinement of fine details. Open questions include whether a hybrid approach combining this training-free pipeline with lightweight training (à la AnimateDiff) could resolve the variance-vs-motion gap, and whether the grid-based switching mechanism generalizes to longer sequences or more complex multi-object motion. The limited evaluation (50 prompts, 8 user-study participants, only three SD1.5 baselines) leaves room for broader validation.
