## Problem

Existing 3D talking head generation models handle either the speaker or the listener in isolation, neglecting the seamless role transitions and bidirectional non-verbal feedback that characterize natural face-to-face conversations. This paper introduces the task of multi-round dual-speaker interaction for 3D talking head generation, requiring a single model to produce both speaking and listening behaviors in continuous dialogue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252197/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes Speaker-A's raw audio waveform, Speaker-A's per-frame blendshape coefficients (56 parameters), and Speaker-B's raw audio waveform as input, and outputs Speaker-B's per-frame blendshape parameters (56 coefficients) driving the 3D facial animation. Audio is encoded via pretrained Wav2Vec 2.0 into 1024-dim features before projection, and the dataset provides dual-channel audio so each speaker's voice can be isolated in multi-speaker recordings. The DualTalk dataset spans ~50 hours across 1,052 identities with an average of 2.5 conversational rounds per clip.

## Method

DualTalk is a four-stage pipeline: (1) a Dual-Speaker Joint Encoder that encodes both speakers' audio through separate Wav2Vec 2.0 encoders and Speaker-A's blendshape through a two-layer FC network, projecting all into a shared d-dimensional space; (2) a Cross-Modal Temporal Enhancer that applies cross-attention (audio as query, blendshape as key/value) followed by a bidirectional LSTM to fuse and temporally align the modalities; (3) a Dual-Speaker Interaction Module combining a Transformer encoder, a Modal Alignment Attention mechanism (adapted from FaceFormer's biased attention) to synchronize temporal focus between speakers, and a Transformer decoder for context-aware response generation; (4) an Expressive Synthesis Module that applies adaptive ReLU-based expression modulation before a final linear layer maps to the 56 blendshape parameters. The model is trained end-to-end in a supervised manner on the newly constructed DualTalk dataset, which is the first 3D facial mesh dataset with dual-channel audio and multi-round face-to-face interactions.

## Results

On the DualTalk test set, DualTalk achieves a Fréchet Distance (FD) of 11.14 for expressions, 3.59×10⁻¹ MSE, 3.48 SID (diversity), and 4.73×10⁻² rPCC (synchronization), outperforming the closest baseline L2L (FD 24.61, MSE 5.68×10⁻¹, SID 2.86, rPCC 8.52×10⁻²) by over 50% in expression accuracy. On the speaker-only VOCASET benchmark, DualTalk attains the lowest Lip Vertex Error (2.79×10⁻⁵ mm) and highest Lip Readability Percentage (96.69%), surpassing SelfTalk by 5% in LRP. On the listener-only ViCo dataset, DualTalk achieves the lowest FD (22.27) and MSE (0.58×10⁻¹) among all listener baselines. The model also runs at 0.03 s per second of generated feedback, supporting real-time deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualTalk outperforms all five speaker/listener baselines (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, EmoTalk, SelfTalk, L2L) on every metric in the DualTalk test and OOD evaluations. The closest competitor, L2L [22], achieves FD-EXP of 24.61 versus DualTalk's 11.14 (a 55% reduction), and its SID-EXP of 2.86 trails DualTalk's 3.48 by 22%. On the speaker-only VOCASET benchmark, DualTalk beats SelfTalk [28] (the strongest speaker baseline) by 13% in LVE (2.79 vs. 3.22 ×10⁻⁵ mm) and 5% in LRP (96.69% vs. 91.37%), while also surpassing FaceFormer [9], CodeTalker [39], VOCA [6], MeshTalk [31], and DiffSpeaker [21]. On the listener-only ViCo dataset, DualTalk edges out DIM [36] (FD-EXP 22.27 vs. 23.88) and L2L [22] (22.27 vs. 33.93), and substantially outperforms RLHG [42] (22.27 vs. 39.02). Notably, CodeTalker [39] and FaceFormer [9] produce zero SID (no diversity) on the DualTalk test set, indicating they collapse to near-constant expressions in the dual-speaker setting, whereas DualTalk maintains rich, varied animations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualTalk carves new ground by unifying two previously separate research threads—speaker-only 3D talking head generation (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, SelfTalk) and listener-only non-verbal feedback modeling (Learning2Listen, DIM)—into a single architecture that handles role transitions. Architecturally, it repurposes Wav2Vec 2.0 audio encoders, cross-attention, bidirectional LSTMs, and Transformer encoder-decoder stacks (with a Modal Alignment Attention inspired by FaceFormer's biased attention) into a pipeline that had not previously been assembled for the dual-speaker setting. The paper does not extend a single prior method but rather synthesizes components from both literatures to address a gap neither line of work tackles: continuous, multi-round interaction where a participant alternates between speaking and listening.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50-hour, 1,000+ identity DualTalk dataset with dual-channel audio and multi-round dialogue is the first benchmark of its kind, providing a foundation the field lacked for evaluating interactive 3D talking heads. A notable limitation is the substantial OOD performance drop (FD nearly doubles from 11.14 to 21.71), suggesting the model may overfit to specific interaction patterns and identities, which limits generalizability. Open questions include scaling beyond two speakers, extending to photorealistic rendering rather than mesh coefficients, and whether the 2.5-round average captures the full complexity of natural conversation. The 0.03 s-per-second-of-feedback runtime is promising for real-time deployment but was not stress-tested under longer sequences.
