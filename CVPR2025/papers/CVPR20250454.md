## Problem

In global SfM pipelines, translation averaging is significantly more sensitive to noise and outliers than rotation averaging, yet effective outlier-edge filtering remains underexplored. Existing methods like 1DSfM project translations into random 1D subspaces and offer only marginal practical improvements, while recent work shifts focus to degenerate configurations rather than outright outliers. This paper tackles the gap by learning to directly identify and remove inaccurate relative translation edges before averaging.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pose graph whose vertices are images with known global orientations (from prior rotation averaging) and whose edges carry relative SE(3) poses (rotation matrix plus unit-length translation vector). Each image also contributes a 384-dimensional DINOv2 embedding, which is concatenated with the shared camera's 3D orientation to form 393-dimensional edge features in the line graph. The output is a binary inlier/outlier label per original edge; edges appearing in multiple clusters receive majority voting, and the surviving edges form the filtered pose graph passed to translation averaging.

## Method

The key reformulation converts the pose graph into its line graph, turning edge classification into vertex classification so that standard vertex-focused GNN architectures can be applied. A three-layer TransformerConv network (35,992 parameters, ReLU, 0.3 dropout, gated residual in the final layer) classifies each line-graph vertex (i.e., original edge) as inlier or outlier, with attention coefficients computed via scaled dot-product over query/key projections enriched by edge attributes. To prevent memory explosion—since line-graph edge count is quadratic in original node degrees—the pose graph is first partitioned into k clusters minimizing inter-cluster cut (weighted by RANSAC inlier counts), each cluster is independently converted to its line graph and processed, and overlapping edges are resolved by majority voting. The model is trained supervised on a single outdoor scene (Piazza San Marco, 249 images) using Binary Cross Entropy Loss with a 20° (Theia) or 10° (GLOMAP) angular threshold for outlier labeling, and generalizes across indoor/outdoor domains without retraining.

## Results

On the 1DSfM dataset with Theia, the proposed method combined with the Triangle filter (Ours+Tri.) achieves a mean position error of 21.15 m and Recall@10m of 44.42%, reducing the error by roughly a factor of 5 compared to the unfiltered baseline (154.29 m) and the 1DSfM filter (154.21 m). On GLOMAP, adding the proposed filter to the default pipeline lowers the median position error from 9.56 m to 8.59 m and improves Recall@10m from 59.39% to 61.68%. On PhotoTourism, Ours+Tri. attains the best mean position error (2.28) and highest Recall@5 (89.72% for standalone Ours). On ScanNet, Ours+Tri. achieves the lowest mean position error (0.62 m) and highest Recall@1m (85.71%) among all non-Oracle methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 1DSfM filter [64], the proposed method (Ours+Tri.) dramatically outperforms on the 1DSfM dataset, reducing mean position error from 154.21 m to 21.15 m (≈7× improvement) and improving Recall@10m from 38.47% to 44.42%; on ScanNet it lowers mean error from 0.83 m to 0.62 m and recall from 72.05% to 85.71%. Against the Triangle degeneracy filter [41], Ours+Tri. achieves lower mean error on 1DSfM (21.15 vs. 22.61 m) and ScanNet (0.62 vs. 0.73 m), confirming the two filters are complementary. The MSAC score baseline [40] performs poorly across all datasets (e.g., 158.83 m on 1DSfM, 1.09 m on ScanNet), indicating it fails to effectively identify incorrect edges. CleanNet [50], the outlier detector from NeuRoRA, provides only marginal gains over no filtering (155.57 m vs. 154.29 m on 1DSfM; 0.78 m vs. 0.78 m on ScanNet), suggesting its rotation-focused design does not transfer well to translation outlier detection. Overall, Ours+Tri. achieves the best non-Oracle results on all three datasets across both mean position error and recall metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the line graph representation—previously used in GNNs for link prediction (Cai et al., 2021)—to reframe pose-graph edge filtering as vertex classification, enabling the use of standard vertex-focused Transformer architectures. It departs from prior learning-based SfM work (NeuRoRA, PoGONet, MSP), which targets rotation averaging, by arguing that rotation estimation is already sufficiently accurate and that translation outlier filtering is the remaining bottleneck. The method also introduces DINOv2 image embeddings as a content-based signal for outlier detection, a signal not previously exploited in pose-graph filtering, and addresses the memory explosion of line graph conversion via a graph clustering strategy with majority voting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Translation averaging remains a key accuracy bottleneck in global SfM, and this work provides a drop-in, learning-based filter that consistently improves camera position accuracy across three large-scale datasets and two SfM frameworks (GLOMAP, Theia). The finding that the filter complements the Triangle degeneracy filter [41]—addressing orthogonal failure modes (outliers vs. quasi-linear motion)—suggests a modular filtering paradigm for SfM pipelines. Limitations include the multi-minute runtime (vs. sub-second for 1DSfM), training on a single scene, and the fact that the method removes cameras from the reconstruction, which may be undesirable in applications requiring full coverage. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to incremental SfM, whether the DINOv2 signal can be replaced by lighter-weight features, and whether the clustering strategy can be made adaptive to graph density.
