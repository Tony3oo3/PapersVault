## Problem

Existing in-context segmentation generalists assume high-quality, uniformly reliable support examples and treat them equally, overlooking the distributional diversity and quality degradation (annotation errors, style shifts, compression artifacts) that arise in real-world use. Prior work addressed example selection only at test time, leaving the training phase unoptimized for handling unreliable inputs. This paper asks how to explicitly model per-example uncertainty during both training and testing to extract more robust in-context representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes K reference image–mask pairs (RGB 3×518×518 with binary 1×518×518 masks) and a target RGB image at 518×518, and outputs a binary segmentation mask for the target. A frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 encoder and frozen SAM ViT-H decoder are used, with only 55M trainable parameters in between. The in-context instruction set comprises four channels—raw visual features, mask-pooled features, discovered part prototypes, and the binary mask label—which are concatenated and projected before feeding a query-based prompt generator.

## Method

UNICL-SAM introduces an Uncertainty Gaussian Graph Network (UGGN) that parameterizes each patch-level node as a Gaussian (mean + variance) over a sparse affinity graph, using the reparameterization trick to obtain differentiable samples whose channel-wise variance yields a per-node uncertainty map. This uncertainty drives two enhancement strategies: (1) uncertainty-guided graph augmentation, where high-uncertainty nodes are preferentially dropped/masked and edges perturbed, with a KL-divergence loss between the augmented and original graph representations enforcing invariance; and (2) uncertainty-guided feature refinement, which multiplies features by (1 − uncertainty) through a learnable gate to suppress unreliable regions. The final UGGN layers are repurposed as a probabilistic clustering module: variance-weighted attention propagates node distributions, and Gumbel-Softmax sampling produces soft cluster assignments whose averaged features form part prototypes capturing fine-grained local semantics. These prototypes, together with the other three instruction types, are projected and passed through a query-based prompt generator that produces in-context prompts for the frozen SAM mask decoder. Training combines BCE + Dice mask loss with KL regularization toward a unit Gaussian, L2 weight decay on the first UGGN layer, a prototype-separation margin loss, and the graph-augmentation KL term.

## Results

On the in-domain COCO-20i benchmark, UNICL-SAM achieves 77.8 MIoU (one-shot) and 78.7 MIoU (few-shot), surpassing the previous best generalist SINE by 13.3 and 12.6 points respectively. On the out-of-domain FSS-1000 benchmark, UNICL-SAM reaches 84.0/86.3 MIoU, approaching specialist-model performance without any FSS-1000 training data. On the challenging LVIS-92i one-shot benchmark, UNICL-SAM attains 34.1 MIoU, outperforming all prior generalists. In robustness simulation tests, UNICL-SAM achieves 79.8 MIoU on clean data with only a 3.1 MIoU average decline across five degradation types, compared to 5.0–5.2 MIoU declines for SINE and SegGPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNICL-SAM decisively outperforms all generalist baselines on COCO-20i: it beats SINE (64.5/66.1) by 13.3/12.6 MIoU, Matcher (52.7/60.7) by 25.1/18.0 MIoU, SegGPT (56.1/67.9) by 21.7/10.8 MIoU, and Painter (33.1/32.6) by over 44 MIoU. On LVIS-92i, UNICL-SAM surpasses SINE (31.2/35.5) by 2.9/1.9 MIoU and Matcher (33.0/40.0) by 1.1 MIoU in one-shot, though Matcher retains a 2.6 MIoU edge in the few-shot setting. On FSS-1000, UNICL-SAM (84.0/86.3) trails Matcher (87.0/89.6) and SegGPT (85.6/89.3) by 1.6–3.3 MIoU, yet remains competitive with in-domain specialists like HSNet (86.5/88.5) despite never seeing FSS-1000 data. In robustness tests (Table 7), UNICL-SAM's average performance decline of 3.1 MIoU (−3.9%) is substantially smaller than SINE (−5.0, −7.1%), SegGPT (−5.2, −8.4%), and Matcher (−4.1, −9.9%), confirming its superior resilience to color shifts, blur, compression, spatial degradation, and domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNICL-SAM builds directly on the in-context segmentation pipeline established by Matcher [29] (dense correspondence between reference and target features) and SEGIC [33] (multi-type visual instructions fed into a query-based prompt generator). Its key departure is the introduction of a Bayesian uncertainty framework—specifically an Uncertainty Gaussian Graph Network (UGGN)—to model the reliability of each in-context example, a dimension that prior generalists (SegGPT, SINE, Matcher, SEGIC) implicitly assume away by treating all support examples as equally trustworthy. The part prototype discovery stage, which clusters local features via a probabilistic graph clustering network with Gumbel-Softmax, extends the instruction set beyond the global-pooled features used in SEGIC, adding fine-grained local semantics as an additional in-context signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world in-context examples suffer from annotation noise, image degradation, and domain shift, yet prior methods offer no mechanism to down-weight unreliable regions. The robustness simulation (Table 7) shows UNICL-SAM degrades far less than SegGPT, Matcher, or SINE under color, blur, compression, spatial, and domain-shift perturbations (avg. −3.1 vs. −4.1 to −5.2 MIoU), suggesting uncertainty-aware refinement is a viable path toward deployment-ready in-context segmentation. Open questions include whether the graph-based uncertainty module scales efficiently to many more examples or higher resolutions, whether the approach transfers to other in-context tasks (detection, tracking), and how much of the large COCO-20i gain (77.8 vs. 64.5 MIoU over SINE) is attributable to the DINOv2 + SAM-ViT-H backbone versus the proposed modules.
