## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from slow generation due to sequential denoising, and while Picard iteration (ParaDiGMS) enables parallel sampling with exact convergence guarantees, it does not ensure fast convergence in practice, often requiring many iterations for large-scale problems. The paper asks whether the convergence speed of Picard iteration can be accelerated while still guaranteeing exact recovery of the original model's output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the full denoising trajectory tensor X ∈ R^{T×n} (all T discretized time steps simultaneously) rather than a single latent vector, which is what enables parallelism. For image generation, input is Gaussian noise (unconditional, CelebA) or noise + text prompt (conditional, Stable Diffusion v1.4), producing a 64×64 or 512×512 image respectively. For robotic control (PushT), input is a 1D state observation and output is an action sequence with horizon 8 over a maximum trajectory length of 200.

## Method

PCM is trained via Picard Consistency Training (PCT): Picard trajectories are collected from the base model, and the network is fine-tuned to predict the fixed-point solution X* directly from any intermediate trajectory point X^k, using a variance-weighted L2 loss (α(k) = 1/√Var(k)) to handle the noise-level gap between early and late iterations. To preserve exact convergence, a model switching mechanism linearly interpolates between PCM and the base model (in feature space) or scales LoRA adapter weights (in parameter space) via a schedule λ(k) that decays from 1 to 0 over K iterations, ensuring the final output matches the original model exactly. EMA updates stabilize training by smoothing the transition point across iterations. The LoRA variant (PCM-LoRA) avoids storing two full models and halves inference cost by adjusting only low-rank adapter scales per Picard step.

## Results

On the PushT robotic control task, PCM-LoRA achieves a reward of 0.83 in just 8 Picard iterations (1.90s), yielding a 2.71× wall-clock speedup over sequential DDIM (25 steps, 5.14s) and a 1.88× speedup over Picard iteration (15 steps, 3.57s). On LDM-CelebA with DDIM (18 sequential steps), PCM-LoRA reaches FID 35.97 in 6 steps (2.11× speedup), matching or slightly beating sequential FID (36.09) while Picard at the same 6 steps gives FID 36.19. On Stable Diffusion v1.4 (25 sequential steps), PCM-LoRA attains CLIP score 26.35 in 8 steps (3.13× theoretical speedup), marginally surpassing both sequential (26.34) and Picard (26.34 at 9 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against sequential DDIM sampling, PCM-LoRA achieves 2.71× speedup on PushT (8 vs. 25 steps), 2.11× on LDM-CelebA (6 vs. 18 steps), and 3.13× on Stable Diffusion (8 vs. 25 steps), while matching or slightly exceeding the quality metrics (FID, CLIP score, reward) of the sequential baseline. Against Picard iteration (ParaDiGMS [38]), PCM-LoRA is consistently faster: on PushT it needs 8 steps versus Picard's 15 (≈1.88× fewer iterations) and achieves a higher reward (0.83 vs. 0.80); on Stable Diffusion it needs 8 steps versus Picard's 9 with a marginally better CLIP score (26.35 vs. 26.34). On LDM-CelebA at matched step counts, PCM-LoRA produces lower FID than Picard (e.g., 67.74 vs. 257.83 at 2 steps; 35.97 vs. 36.19 at 6 steps), confirming that the consistency training yields substantially better intermediate iterates. In the Newton's method comparison (Fig. 9), PCM's convergence speed is nearly on par with the oracle Newton solver, while being more stable under poor initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: ParaDiGMS [38], which introduced Picard iteration as a parallel sampling scheme for diffusion models, and Consistency Models [43], which train a network to jump directly to the final denoising output from any intermediate point. The key conceptual move is recognizing that Picard iteration traces a trajectory in a higher-dimensional tensor space (T×n) rather than a single vector, and applying the consistency-training paradigm to that trajectory. The model-switching mechanism—both in feature space and via LoRA in weight space—borrows from weight-mixing [47] and LoRA [8] to restore exact convergence to the original model's output, addressing the fundamental limitation that any weight modification breaks the fixed-point guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCM offers a practical path to latency reduction in diffusion-based systems (robotic control, real-time image generation) while preserving the exact output distribution of the pretrained model—a property that distillation-based speedups sacrifice. The 2.71× wall-clock speedup on PushT with higher reward than sequential sampling is directly relevant to industrial deployment where iterative denoising is a bottleneck. Open questions include how the method scales to much larger models (e.g., SDXL, video diffusion), whether the Picard trajectory dataset generation cost is prohibitive at scale, and whether the linear model-switching schedule is optimal or whether learned schedules could further reduce the number of Picard iterations needed.
