## Problem

Diffusion models can generate realistic images for data augmentation, but existing pipelines rely on expensive ground-truth annotations (captions, bounding boxes, masks) that limit scalability, and the inherent randomness of generation produces variable-quality samples that can degrade downstream training. Current quality metrics—CLIP score for image-text alignment, detector-based scores, and rule-based layout checks—fail to jointly assess multi-instance scenes with dense annotations, leaving no principled way to cherry-pick the best synthetic samples for perception tasks.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes only simple object category combinations in natural language (e.g., [man, parasail]) sampled from data priors extracted from real datasets, requiring no ground-truth captions or dense annotations. It produces paired synthetic images and detailed scene graphs containing a global caption, per-instance attributes, spatial layouts (bounding boxes), and inter-object relationships. These scene graphs are directly convertible into bounding boxes for detection, segmentation masks via SAM for instance segmentation, or templated Q&A pairs for MLLM instruction tuning, making the output task-agnostic.

## Method

ACP comprises a raw data generator and a data filter. The generator uses LLMs in-context to expand object combinations into full scene graphs (attributes, relations, captions, spatial layouts), then feeds these to an off-the-shelf controllable diffusion model (InstanceDiffusion) to synthesize images with multiple random seeds per scene graph. The filter applies CLIS, a composite metric with two branches: CLIS-L scores layout reasonableness by computing size, distance, and direction similarity between generated layouts and reference layouts from data priors (using a percentile operation for robustness), while CLIS-I scores image quality and alignment by generating global and local captions via a pre-trained captioning model and using an LLM to measure semantic similarity against the scene graph text. The key empirical finding is a positive correlation between CLIS scores and downstream performance gains, validating CLIS as a practical selection criterion that outperforms CLIP-based filtering for training data curation.

## Results

On LVIS long-tailed instance segmentation, ACP with Mask R-CNN (ResNet-50) achieves 14.5 AP_r^mask (+5.2 over baseline) and 22.8 AP^mask (+1.1). With the stronger CenterNet2 (Swin-B) backbone, ACP further improves AP_r^mask to 30.7 (+1.4). For open-vocabulary detection, ACP boosts Grounding DINO by +1.3 AP_novel^box on LVIS and +0.4 on COCO. On multi-modal benchmarks, ACP improves LLaVA-v1.5 (Vicuna-7B) by +80.1 on MME and +0.4 on GQA, surpassing even LLaVA-v1.5 with Vicuna-13B or LLaMA-3-8B backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Mask R-CNN baseline, ACP delivers a substantial +5.2% AP_r^mask gain on LVIS rare categories, while X-Paste [87] provides only +0.1% AP_r^mask and MosaicFusion [80] achieves +1.8% AP_r^mask but sacrifices overall AP^mask (26.7 vs. 28.0 for ACP). With the stronger CenterNet2 + Swin-B backbone, ACP achieves the best AP^mask (35.2) and near-best AP_r^mask (29.6), outperforming MosaicFusion (34.3 AP^mask) and X-Paste (35.1 AP^mask but 26.8 AP_r^mask). For open-vocabulary detection, ACP improves Grounding DINO by +1.3 AP_novel^box on LVIS despite using only 50K synthetic samples versus 61.8M pre-training images. On multi-modal tasks, ACP's +80.1 MME gain with Vicuna-7B exceeds the absolute scores of LLaVA-v1.5 with Vicuna-13B (1438.3) and LLaMA-3-8B (1445.3), demonstrating that high-quality synthetic data can compensate for a weaker language backbone. In generation quality (Table 1), CLIS-filtered InstanceDiffusion achieves the best FID (48.9) and YOLO score (47.9), outperforming unfiltered InstanceDiffusion (53.5 FID, 45.6 YOLO) and all other controllable T2I baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACP extends the LLM-guided diffusion pipeline established by LMD [43] and LayoutGPT [14], but redefines the conditioning signal from detailed image captions or ground-truth annotations to simple object combinations in natural language, drawing spatial and relational priors from real datasets. It departs from copy-paste augmentation methods (X-Paste, MosaicFusion) by generating full coherent scenes with LLM-planned layouts rather than compositing individual instances onto backgrounds. The most significant conceptual departure is the CLIS metric, which unifies layout reasonableness (CLIS-L) and image-scene-graph alignment (CLIS-I) into a single filtering criterion, moving beyond CLIP-based or rule-based quality checks that prior work has shown to be insufficient for dense multi-object scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a well-designed quality metric for synthetic data can predict downstream task performance, offering a practical criterion for data curation in perception and MLLM training pipelines. The finding that CLIS outperforms CLIP score at selecting useful training samples—despite CLIP being the standard generation metric—challenges the assumption that conventional image-text alignment scores are sufficient proxies for training utility. Key limitations include the resource intensity of scaling synthetic data and the current restriction to limited volumes; the authors note that leveraging low-quality data remains an open problem. The framework's reliance on off-the-shelf components (LLMs, InstanceDiffusion, SAM) makes it readily adaptable as these models improve.
