## Problem

Latent Diffusion Models for pose-guided person image synthesis suffer from detail degradation in high-frequency regions (facial features, clothing textures) due to the lossy VAE compression, and this deterioration is further amplified at inference when the generated latent deviates from the compressed real latent. Existing LDM-based methods rely on whole-image conditioning, which fails to provide sufficient focus on these perceptually critical but spatially small regions, leading to identity drift and texture collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250687/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source person image (resized to 512×512) and a target pose represented as a 24-part DensePose map (200×200, warped to a 512×512 SMPL UV texture map) as inputs, and produces a person image in the target pose at 256×176 or 512×352 resolution. The source image is decomposed into three focal conditions: a face crop (detected by SSD), an appearance texture map (warping via SMPL), and the full image, each encoded by different pretrained models. The disentangled conditioning also enables localized editing by selectively modifying the texture map or face embedding without retraining.

## Method

MCLD builds on Stable Diffusion 1.5 with a two-branch conditioning architecture. The first branch uses a ReferenceNet (from Animate Anyone) to extract semantic and low-level features from the SMPL texture map, concatenated with UNet features at each stage. The second branch introduces a Multi-focal Conditions Aggregation (MFCA) module that injects three pose-invariant embeddings—CLIP image embedding, CLIP texture-map embedding, and ArcFace identity embedding—into the UNet via selective cross-attention, where different conditions are routed to different UNet stages (global semantics to the encoder, texture details to the decoder, identity throughout). A lightweight ControlNet-derived pose guider fuses DensePose with the noisy latent. Training uses a standard MSE denoising loss augmented with a face-region-masked MSE loss to explicitly supervise identity preservation, optimized for 60K iterations with Adam (lr=1e-5) on 2×A100 GPUs.

## Results

On DeepFashion at 256×176, MCLD achieves FID 6.693, LPIPS 0.1482, SSIM 0.7511, and PSNR 18.84, outperforming the closest LDM-based competitor CFLD (FID 6.804, LPIPS 0.1519, SSIM 0.7378, PSNR 18.235) on all four metrics. At 512×352, MCLD records FID 7.079, LPIPS 0.1757, SSIM 0.7557, and PSNR 18.211, again surpassing CFLD (FID 7.149, LPIPS 0.1819, SSIM 0.7478, PSNR 17.645). For face identity preservation, MCLD achieves the best face cosine similarity (FS_tgt = 0.413 at 256×176; 0.344 at 512×352) and lowest Euclidean distance among all compared methods, approaching the reference-image upper bound (0.497 / 0.643).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCLD outperforms the strongest LDM-based baseline CFLD on all four image-quality metrics at both resolutions (e.g., SSIM 0.7511 vs 0.7378 and PSNR 18.84 vs 18.235 at 256×176), confirming that multi-focal conditioning recovers details lost to VAE compression. Against the pixel-space diffusion method PIDM, MCLD trades a slightly higher FID (6.693 vs 6.36 at 256×176) for substantially better perceptual and pixel-level fidelity (LPIPS 0.1482 vs 0.1678, SSIM 0.7511 vs 0.7312). MCLD clearly surpasses all GAN-based methods (NTED, CASD, SPGNet, GFLA, CoCosNets) by large margins on every metric. For face identity, MCLD achieves the best FS_tgt (0.413 at 256×176) and lowest dist_tgt (25.02), beating PIDM (0.394 / 25.17) and CFLD (0.363 / 26.11), and approaching the reference-image upper bound of 0.497 / 22.53. The paper notes that PIDM's lower FID does not necessarily indicate superior quality, since the VAE reconstruction FID ceiling in LDM methods is ~7.97, and the other three metrics (LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR) more reliably reflect perceptual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LDM-based PGPIS line initiated by PIDM and refined by PoCoLD and CFLD, which all condition the diffusion UNet on the source image as a whole. The key conceptual departure is identifying that VAE compression in LDMs disproportionately degrades high-frequency regions (faces, garment textures) and addressing this by decomposing the source into pose-invariant focal conditions—face identity via ArcFace, texture via SMPL texture maps, and global semantics via CLIP—fused through a selective cross-attention module (MFCA) inspired by InstantID. The ReferenceNet branch follows Animate Anyone's architecture for structured appearance features, while the pose conditioning leverages DensePose via a ControlNet-derived guider, building on PoCoLD's 3D pose correspondence idea.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-focal conditioning strategy offers a practical remedy for the well-known LDM compression bottleneck in person synthesis, and the condition disentanglement enables mask-free, region-specific editing (identity swap, clothing change) that is more streamlined than latent-space blending. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (DeepFashion) at two resolutions, and failure cases with significant view changes or incomplete texture maps remain unresolved. The authors note that FID can be misleading in LDM-based settings (VAE reconstruction FID ≈ 7.97), suggesting the field needs more robust evaluation protocols. Future work on 3D priors and broader pose/view generalization would strengthen the approach's applicability beyond in-shop fashion imagery.
