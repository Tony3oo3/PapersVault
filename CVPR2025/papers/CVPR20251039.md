## Problem

Existing promptable segmentation models for medical imaging are largely 2D, require multiple prompt types (class labels, text), or are trained on single lesion types, limiting zero-shot generalization across diverse tumors and body regions. Critically, no prior method unifies segmentation with longitudinal tracking in an end-to-end 4D framework, and the scarcity of multi-timepoint annotated datasets remains a major bottleneck for training robust lesion trackers.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D volume (CT, MRI, or PET) together with a spatial prompt: either a point rendered as a 5-pixel-radius ball (to accommodate the limited receptive field of 3x3x3 convolutions) or a bounding box expanded by 0-10 pixels per dimension. For longitudinal tracking, the autoregressive input is the predicted segmentation mask from the prior scan, warped by the learned deformation field, rather than a fresh user prompt. Output is a full 3D binary mask per timepoint; in tracking mode, a sequence of masks is produced from a single initial prompt.

## Method

LesionLocator pairs a densely promptable 3D residual-encoder UNet (prompts fed as extra input channels at full resolution) with a multi-resolution GradICON-based deformation-field network that warps prompts between consecutive scans. The two modules are trained end-to-end with a combined loss (cross-entropy + soft-Dice for segmentation, normalized cross-correlation + GradICON regularizer for propagation), allowing the segmentation head to compensate for propagation errors. Training follows a two-stage protocol: supervised pretraining on 47 multi-modal datasets (18,035 volumes) via the MultiTalent framework, then fine-tuning on 5,227 lesion-annotated images. To overcome longitudinal data scarcity, the authors synthesize follow-up scans by applying anatomy-informed deformations with per-voxel random amplitude modulation (Gaussian-smoothed) to simulate lesion growth/shrinkage, combined with intensity and spatial augmentations, yielding 2,728 synthetic timepoint pairs for pretraining the tracker before fine-tuning on 159 real melanoma scans.

## Results

On six held-out zero-shot lesion segmentation datasets, LesionLocator with box prompts achieves a mean Dice of 83.26, outperforming the next-best method (ULS Model, 74.25) by ~9 points and reaching human inter-rater variability (e.g., 82.51 Dice on lung tumors vs. 81–85 human range). With point prompts, it scores 79.71 mean Dice, still surpassing all baselines. In longitudinal lesion tracking on the melanoma dataset, the full model with synthetic data and mask propagation achieves 68.31 Dice, 85.96% CPM@25, and 3.12 mm MED, outperforming the best prior method (Hering et al.) by ~10 Dice points and ~3 mm in localization error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LesionLocator (box) outperforms all 11 baselines across all six lesion types, with the largest margins over 2D models (SAM: +27.8 Dice, SAM-Med2D: +37.2 Dice) and a consistent 9–12 point lead over the strongest 3D competitors (ULS Model: +9.0, SegVol Box+Text: +12.0). Among 3D methods, ULS Model is the closest competitor at 74.25 mean Dice, but LesionLocator surpasses it on every individual dataset (e.g., +7.0 on melanoma, +6.0 on lung, +10.7 on liver). In tracking (Table 2), LesionLocator with mask propagation achieves 68.31 Dice and 3.12 mm MED, beating Hering et al. (58.66 Dice, 4.45 mm MED) by ~10 Dice points and 1.3 mm in localization, and the ULS+registration baseline (55.69 Dice, 5.81 mm MED) by 12.6 Dice points. The point-only tracker of Yan et al. reports only MED (6.92 mm) without segmentation, making it incomparable on Dice metrics. Notably, 3D spatial context is critical: SAM2 (2D+t) gains 15 points over 2D SAM but still trails LesionLocator by 14 points, confirming that full volumetric processing is essential for generalist lesion segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LesionLocator sits at the intersection of promptable segmentation (SAM, MedSAM, SegVol, VISTA) and longitudinal lesion tracking (Hering et al., Yan et al.), but departs from both in key ways. Rather than adopting the Transformer-based architectures that dominate recent promptable models, it uses a 3D UNet with prompts injected as additional input channels, arguing that spatial prompts align better with convolutional inductive biases. For tracking, it builds on GradICON/UniGradICON for deformation-field estimation and adapts anatomy-informed transformations (Kovacs et al.) to synthesize longitudinal pairs, while the MultiTalent framework provides the multi-dataset pretraining recipe. The autoregressive mask-propagation mechanism—feeding the previous scan's predicted mask as a prompt to the next—is the paper's core architectural novelty, unifying segmentation and tracking into a single end-to-end 4D pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses two critical bottlenecks in clinical AI: the lack of generalizable zero-shot lesion segmentation and the scarcity of longitudinal training data. By releasing both the model and the synthetic 4D dataset, it creates the first open-source benchmark for promptable lesion tracking, enabling the community to build on a previously inaccessible task. Limitations include reliance on a single-institution real longitudinal dataset (60 melanoma patients), difficulty with closely located or merging lesions, and demonstration primarily on CT. Open questions include whether the UNet-over-Transformer advantage persists at larger scale, whether the synthetic data approach generalizes to more aggressive disease progression patterns, and how the method performs when the initial prompt is a manual segmentation rather than a point or box.
