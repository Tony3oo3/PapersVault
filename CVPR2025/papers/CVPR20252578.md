## Problem

Existing novel-view synthesis stylization methods are largely limited to superficial texture and color changes because convincingly editing geometry while preserving multi-view consistency is extremely challenging. This paper addresses the gap by enabling controllable, geometry-altering stylization of 3D scenes that remains consistent across arbitrary camera trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252578/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a depth-regularized 3DGS model, a text prompt, and two scalar strength parameters (one for appearance, one for geometry) as input. It renders RGB and depth images at 512×512 along a smooth representative camera trajectory, then processes frames autoregressively. The intermediate output is a set of consistently stylized RGBD frames, which are used to retrain a final stylized 3DGS model with depth and normal regularization plus TV-L1 loss to suppress floating artifacts.

## Method

The core contribution is an autoregressive stylization pipeline built on three mechanisms. First, a custom RGBD diffusion model (initialized from Stable Diffusion 2.1) denoises color and depth simultaneously but with separate maximum timesteps (T_max^I and T_max^D) and binary masks that gate when each channel is allowed to change, enabling independent control over appearance vs. shape stylization strength. Second, a Warp ControlNet conditioned on forward-warped composites of previously stylized frames (RGBD plus validity mask) guides the diffusion model to correct warping artifacts and inpaint missing regions consistently. Third, depth-informed feature sharing modifies the U-Net's self-attention to concatenate keys/values from reference frames, with attention weights modulated by 4D heatmaps computed from forward-warped reference depths, and additionally injects reference features via argmax selection at intermediate layers. The RGBD model is trained on 500k LAION-2B images with pseudo-depth from three complementary depth estimators, and the ControlNet is trained on 250k generated warp-composite pairs.

## Results

Across 53 stylizations on a range of published and new scenes, Morpheus achieves the best score on all four quantitative metrics: CLIP Direction Similarity (0.175 vs. 0.123 for the next-best GaussCtrl), CLIP Direction Consistency (0.606 vs. 0.590), RMSE (0.0370 vs. 0.0384 for DGE), and LPIPS (0.0378 vs. 0.0403 for Instruct-GS2GS). The method runs in approximately 10 minutes per scene, matching the speed of GaussCtrl and DGE while Instruct-NeRF2NeRF requires hours. A 31-participant A/B user study confirms that participants consistently preferred Morpheus for prompt adherence and found it to match or exceed other methods in aesthetic quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Morpheus outperforms all four quantitative baselines on every metric. Against GaussCtrl [87], the closest competitor, Morpheus improves CLIP Direction Similarity by 42% (0.175 vs. 0.123) and LPIPS by 13% (0.0378 vs. 0.0438), while matching its ~10-minute runtime. Against DGE [13], Morpheus achieves lower RMSE (0.0370 vs. 0.0384) and substantially higher prompt similarity (0.175 vs. 0.113). The instruction-based methods Instruct-NeRF2NeRF [31] and Instruct-GS2GS [81] trail significantly on all metrics and are slower (hours and ~30 minutes respectively). CCEdit [21], a video editing model, is compared in the supplemental material. ConsistDreamer [12] is compared qualitatively only (code unavailable); the paper shows Morpheus produces more dramatic geometry changes beyond local texture edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation for scene rendering and the Stable Diffusion 2.1 / ControlNet architecture for text-conditioned image generation, extending both into a joint RGBD stylization pipeline. It departs from prior NVS stylization methods (Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, GaussCtrl, DGE, ConsistDreamer) that rely on uniform cross-attention, epipolar-line guidance, or score distillation to enforce multi-view consistency, by introducing depth-guided cross-attention and feature injection that respect scene geometry, plus a custom Warp ControlNet conditioned on forward-warped composites. The autoregressive, frame-by-frame design with separate denoising schedules for color and depth channels is a key architectural departure from methods that stylize all frames simultaneously or alternate between editing and NVS optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to independently control the strength of shape versus appearance stylization opens a practical design space for artists and downstream applications (e.g., data augmentation for limited training sets) that prior texture-only methods could not offer. Limitations include inheriting reconstruction errors from the input 3DGS, dependence on trajectory selection, and a ~10-minute runtime that could be reduced with faster diffusion models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether the depth-guided feature sharing scales to longer trajectories without accumulated drift, and whether the method can be adapted to other NVS representations beyond 3DGS.
