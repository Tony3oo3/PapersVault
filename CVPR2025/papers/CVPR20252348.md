## Problem

Fine-tuning Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) for Domain Generalized Semantic Segmentation (DGSS) risks destroying the generalization acquired during large-scale pretraining. Existing adapter-based methods leave the VFM's internal representations largely untouched, while selective parameter fine-tuning methods (e.g., ChildTune, VQT) target only task-sensitive parameters and can actually degrade cross-domain performance. No prior approach identifies which parameters are specifically sensitive to domain shifts versus task shifts, leaving a gap in how to adapt VFMs to unseen domains without sacrificing their pretrained knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images from a single source domain (e.g., GTAV or Cityscapes at 256×256) and produces pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks for unseen target domains. During the DR-FIM estimation phase, the method additionally generates simulated unseen-domain samples by perturbing the feature mean and variance of seen-domain inputs using uncertainty estimates, which are then fed through the frozen VFM to compute domain-shift sensitivity. The final segmentation output is produced by a lightweight mask decoder attached to the VFM backbone, following the Rein architecture.

## Method

FisherTune introduces the Domain-Related Fisher Information Matrix (DR-FIM), which decomposes parameter importance into a task-sensitive component (standard FIM) and a domain-sensitive component (ΔF_θ, measuring FIM change between seen and simulated unseen domains). The simulated unseen domain is constructed by perturbing feature statistics (mean and variance) with noise scaled by uncertainty estimates, inspired by out-of-distribution generalization methods. To stabilize DR-FIM estimation under the large task gap between VFM pretraining and DGSS, the method employs variational inference: parameters are treated as Gaussian random variables with a prior centered on pretrained weights, and the DR-FIM is derived from the posterior covariance matrix Λ via a closed-form relation (F_θ = γΛ − γτ⁻²I), avoiding expensive gradient-based FIM computation. A dynamic training schedule then progressively increases the fraction of fine-tuned parameters (from δ_min% to δ_max%) based on DR-FIM rankings, starting with only the most domain-sensitive parameters and expanding as training stabilizes.

## Results

On the GTAV → Cityscapes + BDD100K + Mapillary benchmark, FisherTune achieves average mIoU of 59.2% (CLIP), 56.1% (MAE), 59.7% (SAM), 64.4% (EVA02), and 66.5% (DINOv2) with only 15.21M trainable parameters. The method outperforms the strongest adapter-based baseline Rein by 4.3% average mIoU across five VFM backbones, and surpasses the selective-parameter method ChildTune by 2.7% on average. On the Cityscapes → ACDC setting, FisherTune reaches 77.5% mIoU with DINOv2, a 5.8% gain over the full fine-tuning baseline. Under adverse weather conditions (Cityscapes → Foggy/Dark/Nighttime/ACDC-Rain/Snow), FisherTune achieves 66.3% average mIoU with DINOv2, outperforming Rein by 2.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FisherTune consistently outperforms all adapter-based methods (LoRA, VPT, Rein, AdaptFormer) across every VFM backbone. Against Rein (the strongest adapter baseline), FisherTune gains +1.8 to +2.2 mIoU per backbone, averaging +4.3% across five models; the gap is largest for MAE (+1.8) and DINOv2 (+2.2). Against the selective-parameter method ChildTune (same 15.21M trainable params), FisherTune wins by +2.7 mIoU on CLIP, +1.4 on MAE, +2.5 on SAM, +3.3 on EVA02, and +3.1 on DINOv2, confirming that DR-FIM-based selection is more effective than ChildTune's Fisher-matrix-only selection. Against VQT (self-focused parameter tuning), FisherTune leads by +3.9 to +3.4 mIoU per backbone. Notably, FisherTune with only 15.21M trainable parameters surpasses full fine-tuning (304M params) on CLIP (+8.1), MAE (+1.9), SAM (+2.8), and DINOv2 (+4.8), while EVA02 full tuning (60.9) is slightly below FisherTune (64.4). Compared to external SOTA methods (tqdm at 66.1% with EVA02, SDT at 65.8% with DINOv2), FisherTune is competitive at 64.4% and 66.5% respectively, using fewer trainable parameters (15.21M vs. 304.2M for tqdm).

## Relation to Prior Work

FisherTune sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for Vision Foundation Models and domain-generalized semantic segmentation (DGSS). It departs from the dominant adapter-based paradigm (Rein, LoRA, AdaptFormer, VPT) that leaves the VFM backbone untouched, and from NLP-inspired selective fine-tuning (ChildTune, Fishermask) that selects parameters based solely on task sensitivity. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Domain-Related FIM (DR-FIM), which augments the standard Fisher Information Matrix with a cross-domain sensitivity term, and the use of variational inference to stabilize DR-FIM estimation by treating parameters as Gaussian-distributed with a pretrained prior. This bridges the gap between task-aware parameter selection and domain-aware generalization in a way neither prior line of work addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naive selective fine-tuning of VFMs can actually underperform adapter-based methods in DGSS (Fig. 2), a finding that challenges the assumption that activating more backbone parameters is always beneficial. FisherTune's DR-FIM provides a principled, data-driven criterion for which parameters to update, and the variational inference formulation offers a computationally efficient path to stable estimation that scales to large ViT backbones. However, the evaluation is confined to driving-scene segmentation benchmarks (Cityscapes, BDD100K, Mapillary, ACDC, GTA), and the domain simulation via feature perturbation is a proxy rather than a true distribution shift. Open questions include whether DR-FIM generalizes to non-driving domains, non-ViT architectures, and whether the approach remains effective when the pretraining task is already closely aligned with the downstream task.
