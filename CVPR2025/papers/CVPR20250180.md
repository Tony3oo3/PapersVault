## Problem

Existing LLM-based listening head generation (LM-listener) relies solely on unimodal speech content, ignoring acoustic prosody and speaker emotion that significantly shape listener responses. Additionally, it entangles listener identity with motion synthesis in the decoder, degrading output fidelity and preventing identity transfer without retraining. This paper addresses both gaps while preserving the strong language priors of LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a speaker video (T frames) from which three token streams are extracted: text transcriptions via Whisper, acoustic tokens from the second quantizer of SpeechTokenizer, and discrete emotion labels from EMOCA's emotion recognition module (downsampled by rate r). The listener's 3DMM shape parameter β^l is provided separately as an identity condition. Output is a full-length listener facial motion sequence of 3DMM expression (ψ) and head pose (θ) parameters per frame, with the identity shape parameter intentionally excluded from the motion representation to enable clean disentanglement.

## Method

The framework has two core components. First, a Multimodal-LM extends a transformer LM (GPT-2 or LLaMA-2) by appending randomly initialized embeddings for acoustic and emotion tokens, organizing the input as interleaved blocks of listener-response, text, acoustics, and emotion tokens, and training in two stages: speaker-understanding pretraining (next-token prediction over all speaker modalities) followed by listener-response fine-tuning (cross-entropy on LR tokens only). Second, an identity-disentangled VQ-VAE (codebook size 256, dim 512, downsampling rate 8) uses instance normalization in the encoder to strip identity-specific variation from the learned codebook, and AdaIN in the decoder to re-inject the listener's β^l as a style condition, so the same LR tokens reconstruct identity-specific expressions and poses without any retraining. The key insight is treating listener identity as a separable 'style' variable rather than a confound in the motion codebook.

## Results

On the L2L-trevor dataset, the proposed method (GPT2) achieves L2=0.2848 and FD=9.8093, improving 38% and 45% over LM-listener (GPT2) respectively, while also reducing P-FD from 19.99 to 11.08 and L2 Affect from 6.49 to 2.66. On the more challenging multi-listener RealTalk dataset, the gains are even larger: L2 drops from 0.2026 to 0.0860 (60% improvement) and FD from 9.60 to 3.39 (65% improvement). Notably, even random sampling from the identity-disentangled VQ-VAE codebook (Ours Random Walk) outperforms the full LM-listener pipeline, indicating the codebook itself encodes high-quality, identity-aware listener motions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is LM-listener [24], the state-of-the-art LLM-based method that uses only speech content and a naive (identity-entangled) VQ-VAE. The proposed method decisively outperforms LM-listener on every metric across both datasets: on L2L-trevor, L2 improves from 0.4485 to 0.2848 (−38%) and FD from 18.52 to 9.81 (−45%); on RealTalk, L2 improves from 0.2026 to 0.0860 (−60%) and FD from 9.60 to 3.39 (−65%). The larger gap on RealTalk (multi-listener) versus L2L-trevor (single-listener) confirms that identity disentanglement is particularly beneficial when generalizing across diverse listener identities. Simpler baselines (Random, NN Facial Motion, NN Speech Content, Naive Random Walk) are all substantially worse, with L2 values ranging from 0.22 to 0.71 on L2L-trevor. A striking finding is that Ours Random Walk—merely sampling random tokens from the identity-disentangled codebook without any language model—already beats the full LM-listener pipeline, demonstrating that the VQ-VAE identity disentanglement alone contributes most of the fidelity gain. A user study with 33 participants corroborates these quantitative results: the proposed method was preferred over LM-listener in 86.4% of trials and even over ground truth in 65.8% of trials.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LM-listener (Ng et al., ICCV 2023), which first demonstrated that LLMs can generate contextually appropriate listener responses from speech text alone, by addressing two limitations: unimodal input and entangled identity handling. Architecturally, it follows the multimodal LLM paradigm (AnyGPT, X-VILA) of converting non-textual modalities into discrete tokens appended to a language model's vocabulary, while repurposing the VQ-VAE framework (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) with instance normalization and AdaIN for identity-agnostic codebook learning and identity-conditional decoding. The broader lineage includes Learning2Listen, DIM, and ELP for listening head generation, and the emotional contagion literature for justifying emotion as an input signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identity disentanglement via IN/AdaIN enables multi-listener generation without retraining, which is practically important for digital human and avatar applications requiring diverse listener personas. The 60% L2 improvement on the multi-listener RealTalk dataset (vs. 38% on single-listener L2L-trevor) suggests the approach scales with identity diversity, though it remains limited to 3DMM parameter space and 8-second segments. Open questions include whether the coarse emotion discretization (one token per r frames) captures rapid emotional shifts, whether the approach generalizes to full-body or pixel-level generation, and whether the reliance on multiple pre-trained components (Whisper, SpeechTokenizer, EMOCA) introduces compounding errors in deployment.
