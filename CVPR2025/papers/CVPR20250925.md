## Problem

Neural rendering methods (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) predominantly rely on RGB images, yet complementary imaging modalities such as near-infrared, polarization, and multispectral data carry additional information that could improve reconstruction quality. This potential has been largely unexplored due to the scarcity of multi-view multimodal datasets and the absence of a flexible framework capable of handling heterogeneous sensors with different intrinsics, RAW mosaicked data, and arbitrary modality combinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250925/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes unaligned, lens-distorted, mosaicked RAW frames from up to five heterogeneous sensors (RGB, monochrome, NIR, polarization, multispectral) with independently calibrated intrinsics and extrinsics, supporting unbalanced frame counts per modality (e.g., 45 RGB views but only 1–10 views of a second modality). It outputs perfectly aligned novel-view renderings for every training modality simultaneously, along with depth maps and surface normals. Polarization data is handled specially: Stokes vectors are estimated in a fixed world reference frame and converted to camera-frame intensities at render time, accounting for sensor rotation.

## Method

MMS-FW decouples geometry from appearance by using a shared SDF-based density module (following NeuS) across all modalities and a shared multi-resolution hash-grid (from Neuralangelo) for radiance estimation, the latter promoting cross-modality information transfer through overlapping spectral bands. A single shallow MLP takes concatenated hash-grid features, 3D coordinates, and viewing direction, producing output channels for every modality's channels; during training, only the channels corresponding to the sampled ray's modality are supervised, while at evaluation any channel of any modality can be rendered from any viewpoint. The framework directly ingests RAW mosaicked and distorted frames (avoiding costly demosaicking and undistortion preprocessing), and its modular pipeline—inspired by NeRFStudio and SDFStudio—allows swapping implicit representations, sampling strategies, losses, and optimizer configurations per modality. Training runs 100k iterations on a single A6000 GPU with 2048 rays per modality per iteration and an AdamW optimizer with a piecewise-decaying learning-rate schedule.

## Results

On the X-NeRF dataset, MMS-FW achieves 33.75 dB PSNR (RGB), 33.33 dB (NIR), and 36.11 dB (MS), outperforming X-NeRF by ~2–3 dB across all modalities. On MMS-DATA (object-centric scenes), the gap widens dramatically: MMS-FW reaches 32.42 dB (RGB), 32.97 dB (NIR), and 29.89 dB (MS) versus X-NeRF's 25.73, 26.97, and 23.63 dB respectively (~6 dB improvement). Internally, 5-modality training on MMS-DATA yields PSNR gains of +4.23 dB (RGB), +3.88 dB (NIR), +3.39 dB (MS), +3.34 dB (Mono), and +2.22 dB (Pol) over single-modality baselines, confirming cross-modality information transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is X-NeRF [Poggi et al., 3DV 2022], the prior state-of-the-art multimodal NeRF method. MMS-FW wins on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) in every configuration tested. On the X-NeRF's own forward-facing dataset, MMS-FW gains ~2 dB PSNR on RGB (33.75 vs 31.77) and ~3 dB on MS (36.11 vs 33.22). On MMS-DATA's object-centric scenes, the advantage expands to ~6–7 dB (e.g., RGB 32.42 vs 25.73; MS 29.89 vs 23.63), which the authors attribute to X-NeRF's Normalized Cross-Device Coordinates (NXDC) being designed exclusively for forward-facing scenes and degrading when disabled for object-centric geometry. MMS-FW also achieves substantially lower LPIPS on RGB (0.06 vs 0.35 on MMS-DATA), indicating better perceptual quality. No comparison is made against single-modality NeRF baselines in the head-to-head table, though internal ablations (Tables 2–5) show consistent 2–4 dB PSNR improvements from adding a second modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMS-FW extends the NeRF volume rendering paradigm (Mildenhall et al.) with the multi-resolution hash-grid from Neuralangelo and SDF-based density estimation from NeuS, while adopting the modular pipeline architecture of NeRFStudio and SDFStudio. The closest prior methods are X-NeRF and NeSpoF, which introduced cross-modality NeRF but were limited to specific modality pairs (RGB+IR+MS or Pol+MS), required balanced frame counts, and could not handle RAW or object-centric scenes. MMS-FW departs from these by supporting an arbitrary number of heterogeneous sensors, unbalanced view counts, RAW mosaicked frames, and polarization via Stokes-vector estimation in a fixed reference frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of MMS-DATA (32 scenes × 5 modalities × 50 views, geometrically calibrated) fills a critical data gap that has constrained multimodal neural rendering research, enabling systematic study of cross-modality information transfer that was previously infeasible. The finding that even 5 frames of a secondary modality improve RGB rendering quality, and that RAW mosaicked frames perform comparably to demosaicked ones, has practical implications for sensor pipeline design. Open questions include whether a pretraining-then-finetuning strategy can resolve the polarization-overfitting issue, whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, and whether geometry reconstruction (currently unevaluable due to lack of 3D ground truth) benefits similarly from multimodal supervision.
