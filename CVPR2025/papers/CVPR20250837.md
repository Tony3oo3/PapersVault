## Problem

Vision-language models are bottlenecked by the limited context window when processing high-resolution images (e.g., 576 vision tokens per image) and long video sequences, making multi-image and video tasks computationally prohibitive. Previous compression approaches rely on external modules like Q-Former or average pooling to reduce token count, but these force the LLM to interpret compressed representations without leveraging its own learned understanding of vision tokens, leading to significant visual information loss at high compression ratios.

## Input / Output

The model takes images encoded into 576 vision tokens (from 336×336 resolution via CLIP-ViT-L) or sequences of video frame tokens, concatenated with a text prompt. During inference, a two-stage forward pass is used: the first pass compresses vision tokens into a small set of VoCo tokens (as low as 1), and the second pass processes VoCo tokens plus text to generate the response. The VoCo token activations are stored as a compact KV cache (0.525 MB vs. 302.4 MB for full caching) that can be reused across different tasks sharing the same visual input.

## Method

VoCo-LLaMA injects special VoCo tokens between the vision and text token sequences and modifies the causal attention mask so that text tokens attend exclusively to VoCo tokens while VoCo tokens attend to vision tokens, creating an exclusive vision→VoCo→text interaction pathway. Training uses KL-divergence distillation: the model is optimized to match the output distribution of an uncompressed reference model (LLaVA) while the text tokens are forced to rely solely on the compressed VoCo representations. For video, each frame is independently compressed into VoCo tokens, the resulting KV caches are concatenated into a time-series sequence, and the model undergoes continued training to capture temporal correlations. This design eliminates all external compression modules and allows the LLM to learn both the compression and the interpretation of compressed tokens simultaneously under standard supervised fine-tuning.

## Results

VoCo-LLaMA achieves a 576× compression rate (576 vision tokens → 1 VoCo token) while retaining 83.7% average performance across seven image understanding benchmarks (GQA, MMBench, MME, POPE, SEED-Bench, ScienceQA, VQA v2). On video QA, with only 2 VoCo tokens per frame, VoCo-LLaMA reaches 72.3% accuracy on MSVD-QA and 61.1% on MSRVTT-QA, outperforming all prior compression-based and non-compression baselines. Inference efficiency gains include 99.8% reduction in KV-cache storage, 94.8% reduction in FLOPs, and 69.6% reduction in CUDA time compared to the uncached baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two primary compression baselines in Table 1, VoCo-LLaMA (1 token) outperforms Avg. Pool + Linear [30] by 19.6% in average compression retention (83.7% vs. 64.1%) and Q-Former [27] by 26.5% (83.7% vs. 57.2%), with consistent gains on every individual benchmark. On video QA (Table 8), VoCo-LLaMA with 2 VoCo tokens per frame surpasses LLaMA-VID [30] (the only other compression-based video method) by 2.6% on MSVD-QA (72.3 vs. 69.7) and 3.4% on MSRVTT-QA (61.1 vs. 57.7), while also beating all non-compression baselines that use 256+ tokens per frame, including Chat-UniVi [25] (69.3% MSVD-QA), Video-ChatGPT [45] (64.9%), and VideoChat [28] (56.3%). On the fine-grained REC/REG tasks (Tables 5–6), VoCo-LLaMA with 1 token retains 79.9% and 81.6% respectively, far exceeding the lower bound. The efficiency comparison (Table 7) shows VoCo-LLaMA reduces KV-cache storage by 99.8% relative to full caching and cuts FLOPs by 94.8% versus the uncached baseline, with 69.6% lower CUDA time.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoCo-LLaMA builds directly on the LLaVA visual instruction tuning paradigm but fundamentally departs from prior vision compression approaches (Q-Former, LLaMA-VID's average pooling) that rely on external modules to reduce token count. Instead of forcing the LLM to interpret externally compressed representations, this paper modifies the attention mask to create an exclusive interaction pathway through VoCo tokens, effectively distilling the LLM's own understanding of vision tokens into a compact representation. The method also extends the compression framework from static images to video by concatenating per-frame VoCo caches and performing continued training for temporal modeling, a direction not explored by prior compression methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 576× compression with 83.7% performance retention and the 99.8% KV-cache storage reduction address a practical bottleneck that limits VLM deployment, particularly for multi-image and video workloads. The KV-cache reuse mechanism (compress once, infer many times) offers a concrete efficiency path for production systems. However, the method requires architecture-specific training and is validated primarily on a single 7B model (Vicuna-7B + CLIP-ViT-L), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger or differently-structured LLMs. An open question is whether the VoCo tokens can be made transferable across different LLM backbones without retraining, which would significantly broaden applicability.
