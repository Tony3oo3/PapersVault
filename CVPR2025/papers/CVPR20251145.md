## Problem

Full attention in vision Transformers incurs quadratic complexity in token length, making it expensive for high-resolution inputs. While state space models like Mamba offer linear-complexity alternatives, recent analyses suggest their core SSM components are partially redundant for vision and still require specialized CUDA kernels for efficient training and inference. The paper asks whether a simpler, purely multiplicative mechanism—element-wise (star) operations augmented with bilinear mapping—can achieve comparable global contextual modeling at linear cost without such overhead.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images, which are split into non-overlapping patches of spatial resolution H/4 × W/4 in the stem, then hierarchically downsampled by a factor of 2 at each of four stages (doubling channel depth). For classification it emits a single class label; for segmentation (via UperNet) it produces per-pixel class maps at 512×2048; for detection (via Mask R-CNN) it outputs bounding boxes and instance masks. All three variants (SBM-T/S/B) share a fixed latent dimension m = 64 for the low-rank spatial projection.

## Method

The SBM module replaces self-attention with a three-branch design: (1) a bilinear mapping branch that projects tokens through a low-rank spatial matrix W_s (decomposed into n×m and m×n factors, m≪n) followed by a channel projection W_c, yielding matrix U; (2) a linear mapping branch that applies a channel-wise projection W_v to the same projected tokens, yielding matrix V; and (3) a mapping control branch G(x) = σ(xW_G^T) that gates the output via element-wise multiplication. The core interaction is the star operation f(x) = σ(U) * V, which fuses the bilinearly-mapped and linearly-mapped feature views at the element level, expanding the implicit feature dimensionality quadratically per layer. Stacked across a Swin-style four-stage hierarchical backbone with conditional positional encoding, layer normalization, and residual connections, the overall per-block complexity is O(nd² + ndm) rather than O(n²d), yielding linear scaling in token count while preserving global receptive field coverage.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, SBM-T achieves 83.2% top-1 accuracy (25M params, 4.5G FLOPs), SBM-S reaches 84.4% (43M, 8.2G), and SBM-B attains 85.1% (93M, 18.2G), all outperforming same-scale Swin, ConvNeXt, and VMamba baselines. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, SBM-B achieves 50.2% mIoU, surpassing RepLKNet-31B (49.9%) and MambaOut-B (49.6%). On COCO 2017 object detection, SBM-B reaches 50.2 AP^b and 44.8 AP^m, exceeding VMamba-B by 1.7 points on both metrics. On CIFAR-100 transfer learning, SBM-B achieves 91.9% fine-tuning and 81.7% linear probing accuracy, outperforming DeiT-B by 1.4% and 1.1% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBM consistently outperforms all compared baselines at every model scale across all four benchmarks. On ImageNet-1K, SBM-T (83.2%) surpasses the strongest T-scale competitors MambaOut-T and CSwin-T (both 82.7%) by 0.5 points, and Swin-T (81.3%) by 1.9 points; SBM-S (84.4%) beats MambaOut-S (84.1%) by 0.3 points and VMamba-S (83.6%) by 0.8 points; SBM-B (85.1%) exceeds CSwin-B and MambaOut-B (both 84.2%) by 0.9 points. On ADE20K, SBM-B (50.2% mIoU) edges out RepLKNet-31B (49.9%) and MambaOut-B (49.6%), while SBM-T (47.2%) surpasses NAT-T (47.1%) and ConvNeXt-T (46.0%). On COCO 2017, SBM-T achieves 47.2 AP^b, beating VMamba-T (46.5) by 0.7 and Swin-T (42.7) by 4.5 points; SBM-B reaches 50.2 AP^b / 44.8 AP^m, outperforming VMamba-B (48.5/43.1) by 1.7 points on both metrics. On CIFAR-100 transfer learning, SBM-B (91.9% fine-tuning, 81.7% linear probing) outperforms DeiT-B (90.5%, 80.6%) and all other listed methods. Notably, SBM achieves these gains with fewer parameters than most competitors (e.g., 25M vs. 29M for Swin-T, 43M vs. 50M for VMamba-S) and lower FLOPs, while also reporting higher throughput (images/sec) than both Transformer and Mamba-based models.

## Relation to Prior Work

SBM builds on the star operation (element-wise multiplication) introduced in 'Rewrite the Stars' (Ma et al., CVPR 2024), which demonstrated that stacked element-wise multiplications can exponentially expand implicit feature dimensionality. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a bilinear mapping module with low-rank decomposition (inspired by LoRA) to give the star operation a global receptive field, which it inherently lacks due to its element-wise nature. The paper positions itself against both full-attention Transformers (quadratic complexity) and Mamba/SSM-based vision models, arguing that the selective state space mechanism is partially redundant for vision and that a simpler multiplicative interaction suffices for global context modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SBM offers a practical alternative to both attention and SSM for linear-complexity global context modeling, with the notable advantage of higher throughput than either family, making it attractive for large-scale deployment. The consistent performance across classification, segmentation, and detection (outperforming Swin and VMamba by 0.5–2 points at comparable FLOPs) suggests the bilinear-mapping-plus-star formulation is a robust inductive bias. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-resolution inputs where token count grows substantially, whether the low-rank spatial projection (m=64) remains sufficient at larger scales, and whether the architecture generalizes to video or multi-modal settings where the 2D spatial structure assumption may break down.
