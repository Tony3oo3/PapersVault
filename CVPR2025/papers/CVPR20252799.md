## Problem

Existing category-level garment pose tracking methods (GarmentNets, GarmentTracking) predict per-vertex canonical coordinates entirely in 3D point cloud space, which limits explicit perception of garment warping and twisting processes and reduces interpretability. Additionally, the natural geometric symmetry of garments (front/rear surfaces) has been overlooked as a structural prior. The paper argues that garment shape deformation is better perceived in 2D space, where 2D neural networks can more efficiently model warping of point sets.

## Input / Output

The method takes partial point clouds from two adjacent frames (k and k+1, 4000 points each), the previous frame's predicted PC NOCS (frame k), and a complete rest-state mesh NOCS (6000 surface points) as input. It outputs the complete garment point cloud (mesh Task) in task space for frame k+1, along with the intermediate NOCS pose. The pipeline is inherently 3D→2D→3D: 3D points are projected onto a learned 2D plane, deformed there, and then depth is regressed per-pixel to recover the full 3D surface.

## Method

GaPT-DAR consists of four modules forming a 3D-2D-3D pipeline. First, a weight-shared ResUNet3D fuses inter-frame features to produce the (k+1)-th frame pose in NOCS. Second, a Voting-based Projection module predicts the optimal 3D-2D projection plane via a per-point offset-voting scheme with a heatmap, regressing both a normal vector and pivot point to maximize orthogonal projection information entropy. Third, a Garment Deformation module applies a 2D Thin Plate Spline (TPS) transformation, where a 2D PointNet extracts features from projected point sets, a correlation layer computes matching scores, and a regressor predicts the TPS warping parameter θ that maps the rest-state 2D point set to the deformed one (preceded by an affine pre-alignment to reduce the domain gap). Fourth, a Depth Reconstruction module regresses per-pixel depth using a convolutional layer trained with a Log-L1 consistency loss and a Sobel-based depth gradient loss to recover fine geometric details like wrinkles and folds.

## Results

On the VR-Folding dataset, GaPT-DAR achieves the best performance across all four garment categories (Shirt, Pants, Top, Skirt) and both manipulation tasks (Folding, Flattening). For example, on Shirt Folding, GaPT-DAR reaches 32.3% A3cm and 0.044 Dnocs, outperforming GarmentTracking (29.8% A3cm, 0.051 Dnocs) by ~2.5% and ~14% respectively, while GarmentNets achieves only 0.8% A3cm and 0.221 Dnocs. On the more challenging Flattening task, GaPT-DAR attains 33.1% A5cm and 0.098 Dnocs for Shirt, compared to 30.7%/0.105 for GarmentTracking and 13.2%/0.135 for GarmentNets. The method also leads on Chamfer Distance (e.g., 1.03 vs 1.16 for Shirt Folding) and Correspondence Distance (3.85 vs 3.88), indicating superior surface reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaPT-DAR consistently outperforms both baselines on every metric across all four garment categories and both tasks. Against GarmentNets [8] (adapted to tracking via frame-wise prediction), the improvements are dramatic: e.g., Dnocs drops from 0.221 to 0.044 on Shirt Folding (an 80% reduction), and A3cm jumps from 0.8% to 32.3%, reflecting that GarmentNets lacks any inter-frame information fusion. Against the stronger GarmentTracking [32] baseline, GaPT-DAR achieves a consistent but more modest margin: roughly 2–4% absolute gain on A3cm/A5cm accuracy metrics and 5–15% relative improvement on Dnocs (e.g., 0.044 vs 0.051 for Shirt Folding; 0.031 vs 0.039 for Pants Folding). The advantage is most pronounced on the Folding task where severe self-occlusion makes reconstruction harder, and on Chamfer Distance where the depth reconstruction module recovers finer geometric details (e.g., 1.03 vs 1.16 for Shirt Folding). In robustness experiments (Table 4), GaPT-DAR also degrades less under noise and frame dropout, with smaller Dnocs increases at 3× noise (0.087 vs 0.093 for Folding) and at 1/8 frame rate (0.078 vs 0.088 for Folding).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the category-level garment pose tracking task established by GarmentTracking [32] (which itself builds on GarmentNets [8] for single-frame estimation) by reformulating the deformation learning from 3D point-wise coordinate prediction into a 3D→2D→3D pipeline. The key conceptual departure is the use of a voting-based projection to find an optimal 2D plane, followed by explicit Thin-Plate Spline (TPS) warping in that plane, and finally per-pixel depth reconstruction to recover the full 3D mesh. This contrasts with prior methods that implicitly learn deformation through 3D GNNs or feature concatenation across frames, which the authors argue limits interpretability and warping perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Garment pose tracking is a critical sub-problem for embodied intelligence applications such as robot-assisted dressing and human-machine collaboration, where accurate non-rigid shape recovery from partial observations is essential. The paper's main limitation is its dependence on high-quality labeled 3D meshes and synthetic-to-real transfer that remains qualitative, leaving the sim-to-real gap and the need for self- or weakly-supervised learning as open challenges. Additionally, performance degrades under severe self-occlusion, and the method has only been validated on four garment categories in a single dataset, limiting claims about generalizability to diverse clothing types or real-world deployment.
