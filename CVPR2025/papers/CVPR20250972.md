## Problem

Diffusion models can memorize training data and generate NSFW content, creating privacy and safety risks that legal frameworks like GDPR mandate be addressed. Existing machine unlearning methods designed for classification tasks are ineffective for generative models, and naive multi-objective optimization of erasure and preservation objectives suffers from conflicting gradients that prevent a balanced trade-off. The paper addresses how to efficiently erase specific data influence from diffusion models while maintaining overall generation quality, without the prohibitive cost of full retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (DDPM on 32×32 CIFAR-10 or Stable Diffusion v1.4 operating in latent space), a forgetting set D_f (e.g., all 'airplane' images, or ~400 generated NSFW images when no training data is available), and a remaining set D_r for preservation. It outputs updated noise-prediction network parameters θ* that fail to generate meaningful images conditioned on the forgotten class or concept while retaining quality on D_r. For concept-level erasure (e.g., 'nudity'), the forgetting data is synthesized via prompts rather than drawn from the original training set, and the replacement target ε_f is set to the model's own prediction under an unrelated class label (e.g., 'a photo of pokemon').

## Method

EraseDiff formulates unlearning as a bi-level optimization—inner loop minimizes a forgetting loss L_f (training the model to predict noise from a mismatched class rather than the true class), outer loop minimizes a preservation loss L_r—then reformulates it via a value function into a single constrained optimization: minimize L_r subject to L_f(θ) − min_φ L_f(φ) ≤ 0. This avoids the nested-loop instability of bilevel optimization and yields a closed-form first-order update (Theorem 3.1): δ* = ∇L_r + λ_t ∇g, where g(θ) = L_f(θ) − L_f(φ^K) and λ_t = max{0, (a_t − ∇gᵀ∇L_r)/‖∇g‖²}. The inner minimization is approximated by K steps of gradient descent on L_f from the current θ, and the scalar a_t (set to η‖∇g‖²) controls how aggressively the update deviates from the pure preservation direction toward erasure. The resulting stationary point is provably Pareto optimal for the joint (L_r, L_f) minimization, and empirically the update vector maintains positive cosine alignment with both the preservation and erasure gradients, unlike naive multi-objective optimization which exhibits alternating sign conflicts.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with DDPM (forgetting 'airplane'), EraseDiff_noise achieves the best FID of 7.61 and EraseDiff_rl achieves 8.66, both outperforming the unscrubbed model (9.63) and all baselines except SA (8.19). On Stable Diffusion v1.4 for nudity erasure, EraseDiff achieves FID 17.01 and CLIP score 30.58, substantially outperforming SA (FID 25.58) and SalUn (FID 25.06) while slightly trailing ESD on FID (15.76). On Imagenette class-wise forgetting, EraseDiff achieves the best average FID (1.11) and UA (99.91%) across 10 classes. Computationally, EraseDiff is ~11× faster than SA (12.7 min vs 140 min) and ~2× faster than SalUn (12.7 min vs 28.17 min) on DDPM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EraseDiff clearly outperforms the traditional unlearning baselines (FT, NG, BlindSpot) on all metrics: FT and BlindSpot suffer from under-forgetting (P_ψ ≈ 0.90–0.96, nearly matching the unscrubbed 0.97), while NG catastrophically over-forgets (FID 76.73). Against SA [28], EraseDiff achieves better FID (7.61 vs 8.19) and is ~11× faster (12.7 min vs 140 min), but SA achieves a lower P_ψ (0.06 vs 0.22–0.24), indicating stronger erasure at the cost of diversity (recall 0.75 vs 0.72–0.77) and computational overhead. Against SalUn [16], EraseDiff wins on FID (7.61/8.66 vs 9.16), is ~2× faster, and achieves better average Imagenette FID (1.11 vs 1.22) and UA (99.91% vs 99.82%), though SalUn achieves a slightly lower P_ψ (0.07 vs 0.22–0.24). On the nudity-erasure task, EraseDiff substantially outperforms SA (FID 17.01 vs 25.58) and SalUn (FID 17.01 vs 25.06) in image quality, while ESD [20] achieves a marginally lower FID (15.76) but EraseDiff edges ahead on CLIP score (30.58 vs 30.33). On Imagenette, EraseDiff dominates FMN [71] (average FID 1.11 vs 1.30, UA 99.91% vs 42.54%) and ESD [20] (FID 1.11 vs 1.49), confirming consistent superiority across class-wise forgetting scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

EraseDiff builds on the emerging line of diffusion-model unlearning methods (ESD, Selective Amnesia, SalUn, FMN) that target erasing specific classes or concepts while preserving generative quality. Its key methodological departure is borrowing the value-function reformulation from bilevel optimization (Liu et al., Outrata, Ye & Zhu) to convert the nested erasure/preservation problem into a single constrained optimization, yielding a closed-form first-order update rule. This contrasts with prior approaches that either scalarize the two objectives with a fixed weight (risking gradient conflict) or rely on expensive auxiliary computations like FIM (SA) or saliency maps (SalUn). The paper also draws on optimization-based meta-learning (Finn et al., Maclaurin et al.) for the iterative inner-outer structure, but the value-function trick is what eliminates the need for the inner loop at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ~11× speedup over Selective Amnesia and ~2× over SalUn while maintaining competitive or better erasure/preservation trade-offs, making diffusion unlearning more accessible for deployment. The Pareto-optimality guarantee (Theorem 3.2) provides a theoretical foundation that prior heuristic methods lack. However, the paper acknowledges incomplete erasures (e.g., residual 'tench'-like features after forgetting), the risk of scrubbed models being misused for selective censorship, and the sensitivity to the choice of replacement noise ε_f. Open questions include whether the K-step value-function approximation remains accurate for larger models or multi-concept erasure, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-10, Imagenette, I2P) to frontier-scale diffusion models.
