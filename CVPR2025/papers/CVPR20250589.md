## Problem

Diffusion models dominate text-to-image generation but have historically failed at visual understanding tasks like captioning and VQA, which are handled by autoregressive vision-language models. Existing multimodal diffusion approaches either rely on a separate autoregressive decoder (e.g., GPT-2) to produce text from denoised latents, or bolt diffusion onto a pretrained LLM, leaving text generation fundamentally autoregressive. This paper asks whether a single, fully end-to-end diffusion model can natively perform both image and text generation without any next-token prediction component.

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input configurations: a text prompt for image generation, an RGB image for captioning, or an RGB image paired with a text question for VQA. Images are processed in a VAE latent space at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution, while text is tokenized with T5 (max 256 tokens). For VQA, question tokens remain unmasked throughout sampling while only the answer span is generated via discrete diffusion, enabling controllable in-filling. Outputs are generated images (via Euler ODE solver, 28 steps, CFG scale 7.0) or text sequences (via ancestral sampling, 16–256 diffusion steps depending on answer length).

## Method

D-DiT is a dual-branch transformer built on the MM-DiT architecture from SD3, where an image branch performs continuous flow matching (predicting velocity v = ε − x) and a text branch performs discrete masked-token diffusion (predicting the clean token x_θ with a log-linear schedule α_t = 1−t). The two branches share cross-attention at every layer, and a bidirectional T5 encoder sits atop the text branch to provide conditioning for image generation. Training uses a simple weighted joint loss L_dual = L_image + λ_text · L_text, where the image loss is standard flow-matching MSE and the text loss is a negative ELBO over masked tokens with antithetic timestep sampling. The model is initialized from a pretrained SD3 checkpoint and trained in three stages (joint pretraining, high-quality continued pretraining, and visual instruction tuning), requiring no autoregressive text decoder at any point. A key design choice is that the conditioning modality is always kept clean (timestep = 0) during training, so image denoising sees clean text and vice versa.

## Results

On visual question answering benchmarks, D-DiT (512×512, 2B params) achieves 60.1% VQAv2 accuracy, 29.9% VizWiz, 59.2% GQA, 84.0% POPE, and 1124.7 MME score, outperforming Show-O (512×512) on MME, GQA, and POPE while remaining competitive with AR-based VLMs like QWEN-VL (57.5% GQA) and BLIP-2 (85.5% POPE). On GenEval text-to-image generation, D-DiT preserves SD3's performance and slightly improves on several metrics (e.g., single-object 0.65 vs 0.62, counting 0.80 vs 0.74, color attribution 0.50 vs 0.36) without catastrophic forgetting. The model is the first diffusion-only multimodal model to support full VQA, captioning, and T2I generation under a single unified architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Show-O (512×512), the closest unified diffusion+AR baseline, D-DiT wins on MME (1124.7 vs 1097.2), GQA (59.2 vs 58.0), and POPE (84.0 vs 80.0), while losing on VQAv2 (60.1 vs 69.4) due to Show-O's larger AR text backbone. Compared to AR-only VLMs, D-DiT (2B) approaches QWEN-VL (7B) on GQA (59.2 vs 57.5) and BLIP-2 (13B) on POPE (84.0 vs 85.5), but trails significantly on VQAv2 (60.1 vs 78.2 for QWEN-VL, 82.8 for LLaVA-Next) and VizWiz (29.9 vs 38.9/60.5). On GenEval T2I generation, D-DiT slightly outperforms its SD3 initialization on single-object (0.65 vs 0.62), counting (0.80 vs 0.74), position (0.76 vs 0.67), and color attribution (0.50 vs 0.36), but underperforms on colors (0.54 vs 0.63) and two-object (0.97 vs 0.98). D-DiT also outperforms SD3 on the GenEval overall trend, confirming that joint diffusion training does not cause catastrophic forgetting of image generation. Against UniDiffuser and Versatile Diffusion, D-DiT is the only model that performs true discrete token-level text generation (enabling VQA and in-filling) rather than relying on a separate AR decoder for text output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MM-DiT architecture from Stable Diffusion 3 (Esser et al., 2024) and the discrete masked diffusion framework of Sahoo et al. (2024), combining them into a single dual-branch transformer that applies flow matching to images and masked token diffusion to text under one joint loss. It departs from prior multimodal diffusion models (UniDiffuser, Versatile Diffusion) that performed text diffusion in a continuous CLIP latent space and required a separate autoregressive decoder (e.g., GPT-2) to produce readable text, and from hybrid models (Show-O, Transfusion) that retained a full AR text branch. The key conceptual departure is making the text branch itself a discrete diffusion process, eliminating any autoregressive component and enabling token-space infilling for VQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully end-to-end diffusion model that handles T2I generation, image captioning, and VQA without any autoregressive text prediction, demonstrating that the bidirectional MM-DiT architecture is a viable alternative to the dominant AR paradigm for unified vision-language modeling. The practical finding that SD3 checkpoints adapt to text generation in under 25B tokens makes the approach accessible to the existing diffusion-model ecosystem. However, the model still trails strong AR VLMs (LLaVA-Next, Qwen-VL) on understanding benchmarks, and the ablation revealing that SD3's internal features are worse than CLIP for text decoding suggests the bidirectional attention mechanism—not the diffusion features per se—is what enables the text branch to work. Open questions include whether modeling marginal (unconditional) distributions for each modality would improve performance, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or additional modalities.
