## Problem

Evaluating VLMs on open-ended VQA questions is unreliable: rule-based metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) show only 0.09 Spearman correlation with true model rankings, while model-based judges (e.g., GPT-4o) shift absolute scores by 6% across model versions, breaking reproducibility. The paper asks how to convert open-ended VQA into a format that enables objective, consistent, and scalable evaluation without the prohibitive cost of hand-crafting multiple-choice questions.

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image, an open-ended question string, and its ground-truth answer, AutoConverter produces a 4-option multiple-choice question (one correct answer plus three generated distractors). The distractors are constrained to be semantically distinct from the correct answer (verified by a correctness evaluator) while simulating specific error categories. The final benchmark (VMCBench) standardizes all 20 source datasets into this unified 4-choice format, with a fixed zero-shot prompt template for evaluation.

## Method

AutoConverter is a GPT-4o-powered multi-agent pipeline with two coupled phases. For difficulty, five specialized proposer agents each generate distractors targeting a distinct error type (concept misunderstanding, visual misinterpretation, reasoning error, data processing error, question bias); a reviewer agent iteratively critiques and refines the pool, and a selector agent picks the three most challenging distractors. For correctness, a dedicated evaluator assigns a 5-point Likert score to each question; if the score falls below 4, a refiner agent adjusts distractors based on the evaluator's feedback, repeating up to three rounds. Ablations show each agent contributes: removing the error-type proposers raises average VLM accuracy by 1.6%, removing the reviewer by 4.9%, and removing the evaluator/refiner drops correctness by 8.7% relative. Applied to 20 existing VQA datasets (12 open-ended converted, 8 multiple-choice refined), the pipeline yields VMCBench with 9,018 validated questions after human verification of all low-scoring items.

## Results

On VMCBench (9,018 multiple-choice questions from 20 datasets), the best-performing model Qwen2-VL-72B achieves 85.0% overall accuracy, surpassing the top private model GPT-4o (80.3%). Performance spans a wide range from 85.0% (Qwen2-VL-72B) down to 36.4% (Chameleon-7B), with OCR tasks being the easiest (up to 96.8%) and reasoning the hardest (up to 72.6%). AutoConverter-generated distractors produce questions on which VLMs score similar or lower than on original human-crafted questions across MMMU, MathVista, and AI2D, with only 3% of top-correctness-scored questions flagged as incorrect by human annotators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare AutoConverter against a single competing method but rather validates it against a naive baseline and human-crafted questions. Against the naive baseline (a single GPT-4o prompt asking for 3 distractors), AutoConverter achieves a 2.2% relative increase in correctness score (4.69 vs. 4.59) and an 11.5% relative decrease in average VLM accuracy (40.7% vs. 46.0% on MMMU), confirming substantially harder and more correct questions. When compared to original human-crafted distractors on MMMU, MathVista, and AI2D, VLMs consistently score similar or lower on AutoConverter-generated questions, indicating comparable or greater difficulty. Ablation shows the refiner agent contributes the largest single gain (removing it drops correctness from 4.69 to 4.28 and raises average VLM accuracy from 40.7% to 37.8%), while the reviewer agent's removal increases average VLM performance by 4.9% relative. On the evaluation methodology side, rule-based metrics (BLEU/ROUGE) show only 0.09 Spearman correlation with true model rankings on VQAv2, while model-based evaluation (GPT-4o) shifts absolute scores by 6% between versions (0513 vs. 0806) on MMVet, motivating the shift to multiple-choice format.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard practice of evaluating VLMs via open-ended VQA by demonstrating that both rule-based metrics (BLEU, exact match) and model-based LLM-as-judge approaches suffer from fundamental limitations—near-random correlation with true model rankings and version-dependent score shifts, respectively. Rather than improving these evaluation metrics, the paper pivots to a format-level solution: converting open-ended questions into multiple-choice format. For distractor generation, it builds on the multi-agent LLM paradigm (role-playing, self-reflection) but applies it to a specific evaluation-engineering problem, going beyond prior distractor-generation work that relied on knowledge graphs, retrieval, or RL and produced simpler distractors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VMCBench (9,018 questions across 20 datasets) provides a practical, reproducible evaluation resource that sidesteps the reproducibility crisis in open-ended VLM scoring, and the AutoConverter pipeline is broadly applicable beyond VLM evaluation (e.g., educational question generation, refining existing MCQs). A key open question is whether the GPT-4o-dependent pipeline introduces its own form of model-version fragility, mirroring the very problem it aims to solve. The finding that 59% of conversion errors trace back to original ground-truth inaccuracies also highlights a broader data-quality issue in existing VQA datasets that the community has underaddressed.
