## Problem

Existing 3D scene inpainting methods struggle with multi-view consistency and geometric accuracy in 360° unbounded scenes, where extreme viewpoint changes expose large unseen regions that forward-facing or small-angle methods cannot handle. Reference-based approaches suffer from inaccurate depth projection causing fine-tuning artifacts, while semantic-tracking methods like Gaussian Grouping frequently misidentify unseen regions. Additionally, no comprehensive dataset with ground truth existed for benchmarking 360° unbounded scene inpainting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (180–200 training views at 960×540 after downscaling from 4K), their camera intrinsics/extrinsics, per-view object masks (generated by SAM2), and a single reference image captured from a fixed tripod position without the object. The output is a 2D Gaussian Splatting scene where only the newly initialized Gaussians in unseen regions are updated during fine-tuning, while existing background Gaussians remain fixed, enabling real-time novel view rendering of the inpainted scene from arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

AuraFusion360 is a three-stage pipeline built on 2D Gaussian Splatting. First, depth-aware unseen mask generation warps removal regions across all views using incomplete depth maps, aggregates them with thresholding, and feeds the resulting bounding boxes to SAM2 for fully automated, per-view unseen region segmentation. Second, Adaptive Guided Depth Diffusion (AGDD) performs zero-shot depth alignment by modifying the Marigold denoising loop: at each timestep, an adaptive loss with a distance-dependent threshold δ downweights errors in far regions to prevent over-alignment, producing metric-aligned depth that is unprojected into 3D points to initialize new Gaussians in unseen regions. Third, instead of injecting random Gaussian noise as in standard SDEdit, the method applies DDIM Inversion to the rendered initial Gaussians to obtain structured noise preserving reference-view geometry, then denoises with a LeftRefill-conditioned diffusion inpainting model to produce multi-view-consistent RGB guidance for Gaussian fine-tuning under L1+SSIM+LPIPS supervision.

## Results

On the 360-USID dataset (7 scenes), AuraFusion360 achieves the best average PSNR of 17.661 and the lowest average LPIPS of 0.388, outperforming all 7 baselines on both metrics. The closest competitor, SPIn-NeRF, reaches 16.734 PSNR / 0.464 LPIPS, a gap of ~0.9 dB in PSNR and ~0.076 in LPIPS. The ablation variant without SDEdit (16.367 PSNR / 0.406 LPIPS) confirms that the SDEdit-based detail enhancement contributes roughly 1.3 dB PSNR improvement. Per-scene, the largest gains appear on Carton (+1.0 dB over SPIn-NeRF) and Skateboard (+1.4 dB over SPIn-NeRF), scenes with complex geometry and large removed objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AuraFusion360 outperforms all seven baselines on both PSNR and LPIPS across the 360-USID dataset. Against the strongest baseline SPIn-NeRF (16.734 PSNR / 0.464 LPIPS), AuraFusion360 gains +0.93 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 0.076, with the largest per-scene margin on Skateboard (+1.36 dB) and Carton (+1.02 dB). Gaussian Grouping (16.074/0.480) and 2DGS+LeftRefill (16.282/0.518) trail by 1.4–1.6 dB in PSNR; the paper attributes Gaussian Grouping's gap to its text-based tracker misidentifying unseen regions, producing floating artifacts. GScream (14.758/0.514) and Infusion (14.416/0.484) lag furthest, with Infusion collapsing to 9.562 PSNR on Newcone due to inaccurate depth projection. The ablation without SDEdit (16.367/0.406) still beats SPIn-NeRF on LPIPS but loses 1.3 dB in PSNR, confirming that the DDIM-Inversion-guided SDEdit step is critical for closing the perceptual-quality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads—explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) for scene representation, diffusion-based depth estimation (Marigold/GDD) for geometric alignment, and SDEdit/DDIM Inversion for detail-preserving inpainting—into a single pipeline tailored to 360° unbounded scenes. It departs from Gaussian Grouping's tracker-based unseen-region detection by replacing it with a geometry-driven depth-warping approach, and refines Guided Depth Diffusion (GDD) into AGDD to fix over-alignment in distant regions. The reference-guided inpainting builds on LeftRefill and Infusion but addresses their failure modes in extreme viewpoint changes by anchoring both depth initialization and RGB guidance to a single reference view.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of 360-USID, the first dataset with ground-truth novel views for 360° unbounded scene inpainting, fills a critical evaluation gap that has limited quantitative comparison in this setting. The method's practical relevance is high for VR/AR and real-estate visualization where objects must be removed from full-sphere captures, yet the approach still requires a manually selected reference view and operates on static scenes, leaving dynamic and language-guided editing as open problems. Computational cost (~1 min per scene on an RTX 4090 for inference, plus 10k fine-tuning iterations) and the dependence on accurate COLMAP poses remain practical constraints for large-scale deployment.
