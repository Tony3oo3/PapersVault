## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) suffer from slow inference due to their iterative denoising process, and existing feature-reuse acceleration methods (e.g., DeepCache, ToMe) apply coarse-grained reuse across all blocks and all denoising stages, inadvertently reusing low-similarity features that cause structural distortions and prompt misalignment. The paper identifies that the most redundant computation is concentrated in shallow and middle transformer blocks during the later stages of denoising, once structural content has stabilized, but no prior method specifically targets these structurally similar spatio-temporal features.

## Input / Output

BlockDance operates as a plug-and-play wrapper around existing DiT models (DiT-XL/2, PixArt-α, Open-Sora), consuming the same inputs as the base model: a text prompt or class label plus a Gaussian noise latent. It produces the same outputs as the unmodified model—512×512 or 1024×1024 images, or 16-frame 512×512 videos—while skipping computation of the first 20 transformer blocks at designated reuse steps. The cached feature size is modest (4.5–72 MB depending on model), and no architectural modification to the base DiT is required.

## Method

BlockDance partitions denoising steps into cache steps (full forward pass, saving features from block 20) and reuse steps (partial forward starting from block 21 using cached features), concentrating reuse in the latter 60% of denoising after structure has stabilized. The key insight is that shallow/middle blocks (0–20) encode low-level structural content that changes minimally across adjacent steps once the global structure is established, while deep blocks (21–27) encode high-frequency textures that still evolve. BlockDance-Ada extends this with a lightweight 0.08B decision network trained via policy gradient reinforcement learning: given an intermediate latent and prompt embedding after the first 40% of steps, it outputs a binary cache/reuse schedule for the remaining steps, optimizing a reward that balances normalized computation savings against ImageReward-based image quality, enabling instance-specific acceleration that adapts to content complexity.

## Results

BlockDance achieves 25.4% speedup on PixArt-α (COCO2017) with FID 30.69 and SSIM 0.89 at N=2, scaling to 39.4% speedup at N=4. On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet), it delivers 37.4% speedup at N=2 (FID 15.70, sFID 22.86) and up to 57.5% at N=4. On Open-Sora (MSR-VTT), BlockDance accelerates text-to-video by 34.8% at N=2 while maintaining FVD 550.22 (vs. 548.72 baseline). BlockDance-Ada further improves the trade-off, achieving 30.6% speedup with FID 30.71, outperforming BlockDance (N=3) in quality at comparable speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMe, BlockDance dominates on every quality metric: FID 30.69 vs. 174.57, IS 38.73 vs. 11.68, and SSIM 0.89 vs. 0.18, confirming that token merging is poorly suited to attention-heavy DiT architectures. Against DeepCache (N=2), BlockDance achieves lower FID (30.69 vs. 31.57), higher IS (38.73 vs. 37.44), higher IR (0.82 vs. 0.76), and substantially better SSIM (0.89 vs. 0.60), attributed to DeepCache's indiscriminate reuse of low-similarity features across all denoising stages. Against TGATE, BlockDance wins on FID (30.69 vs. 30.82), IS (38.73 vs. 38.50), IR (0.82 vs. 0.77), and SSIM (0.89 vs. 0.55) at comparable latency, and additionally supports DiTs without cross-attention (e.g., SD3, Flux). Against PixArt-LCM, BlockDance is slower (2.31 s vs. 0.83 s) but achieves better FID (30.69 vs. 31.67), IS (38.73 vs. 37.83), IR (0.82 vs. 0.58), and SSIM (0.89 vs. 0.41) without any additional training. On DiT-XL/2, BlockDance (N=2) outperforms DeepCache on FID (15.70 vs. 16.11), sFID (22.86 vs. 28.18), and SSIM (0.98 vs. 0.90) at similar speed. On Open-Sora, BlockDance (N=2) maintains FVD 550.22 vs. DeepCache's 942.06, a 42% improvement, while achieving comparable 34.8% speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-reuse paradigm established for U-Net-based diffusion models (DeepCache, ToMe) to the DiT architecture, but with a key departure: rather than reusing features uniformly across all blocks and all denoising stages, BlockDance identifies and targets only the structurally similar spatio-temporal features in shallow and middle blocks during the later 60% of denoising, when structure has stabilized. The BlockDance-Ada variant further departs from fixed-schedule reuse by introducing a reinforcement learning (policy gradient) framework to make instance-specific cache/reuse decisions, repurposing RL techniques previously applied to diffusion model fine-tuning for inference-time scheduling. The approach is positioned as training-free and plug-and-play, contrasting with distillation-based methods (PixArt-LCM, SDXL-Lightning) that require additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25–50% training-free speedup with minimal quality degradation is practically significant for real-time DiT deployment, and the plug-and-play nature means it can be applied to new DiT architectures (SD3, Flux) without retraining. The empirical finding that structural features in blocks 0–20 stabilize early while deep blocks continue to evolve provides a useful architectural insight for future DiT design. However, the paper acknowledges limited benefits at very low step counts (1–4 steps), and the RL-based adaptive variant, while elegant, adds a 0.08B-parameter network that must be trained per model. Open questions include whether the 20-block threshold generalizes across DiT depths and whether the approach composes well with other acceleration techniques like step distillation.
