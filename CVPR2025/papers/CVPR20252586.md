## Problem

State-of-the-art Video Scene Graph Generation (VSGG) methods rely on offline pipelines that require the entire video sequence as input, consuming large GPU memory and precluding real-time processing. Moreover, their temporal reasoning is limited to global aggregation of frame-level predictions via Transformer blocks, failing to model the dynamic evolution of inter-object relationships over time. This paper asks whether VSGG can be reframed as an iterative, online denoising problem that naturally supports sequential temporal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252586/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames sequentially (one at a time in an online stream), with an off-the-shelf detector extracting per-frame features, bounding boxes, and class predictions. These are assembled into a fixed-size N×N adjacency matrix A^t where each entry encodes the subject's instance feature, the union-box feature of the pair, and the subject's box encoding; entries for absent objects are padded with random Gaussian embeddings. Output is a per-frame scene graph read from the denoised adjacency matrix A^{t,0}: predicate labels via a per-pair MLP, and object class plus bounding box via row-wise MLPs that aggregate all pairs sharing the same subject.

## Method

DIFFVSGG treats the adjacency matrix as a latent signal and applies a Latent Diffusion Model (denoising U-Net) to iteratively remove noise, recovering a clean embedding that encodes object identities, locations, and inter-object relationships simultaneously. Spatial reasoning within a frame is achieved by K reverse-diffusion steps on A^t, while temporal reasoning across frames is achieved by conditioning each frame's denoising on the previous frame's fully denoised matrix A^{t-1,0} (Eq. 11), so that historic predictions guide the current reverse-diffusion trajectory. A motion-enhancement module computes pairwise approaching speeds from a memory bank of object positions and injects them additively into the noisy adjacency at every denoising step, providing explicit cues for predicates like 'following' or 'approaching'. Training is two-stage: the U-Net is first pre-trained with ground-truth bounding boxes to learn the denoising objective L_T-VSGG, then frozen while the readout MLPs are fine-tuned with standard classification and Smooth-L1 regression losses. This unified decoding eliminates handcrafted modules (NMS, cross-frame entity matching) and allows all three sub-tasks to be jointly optimized from a single shared embedding.

## Results

On Action Genome under the w/o constraint setting, DIFFVSGG achieves SGDET R@10 of 35.4 and mR@10 of 27.2, surpassing the previous best DSG-DETR (32.1 / 24.7) by 3.3 and 2.5 points respectively. Under the w constraint setting, it reaches SGDET R@10 of 32.8 and mR@10 of 20.9, outperforming DSG-DETR (30.3 / 18.5). On PredCLS, DIFFVSGG attains R@10 of 83.1 (w/o) and 71.9 (w), matching or exceeding all prior methods. The method also uses the fewest trainable parameters (46.7M) among all compared approaches while running at 8.7 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFVSGG outperforms all 13 baselines across every reported metric on both w and w/o constraint settings. Against the strongest offline competitor DSG-DETR [30], it gains +3.3 R@10 on SGDET (35.4 vs 32.1) and +2.5 mR@10 (27.2 vs 24.7) under w/o constraint, while also using fewer parameters (46.7M vs 65.5M) and running faster (8.7 vs 7.3 FPS). Compared to TEMPURA [69], the prior best online-capable method, DIFFVSGG improves SGDET R@10 by +5.6 (35.4 vs 29.8) and mR@10 by +2.5. On PredCLS, DIFFVSGG ties TR2 [94] at 83.1 R@10 (w/o) but leads on SGCLS by +3.3 (60.5 vs 57.2). The mR metrics show the largest relative gains, indicating the diffusion-based denoising effectively mitigates the long-tail predicate distribution bias that plagues earlier methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFFVSGG departs from the dominant offline VSGG paradigm (DSG-DETR, TEMPURA, TR2) that generates per-frame scene graphs and then aggregates them via Transformer blocks over the full temporal context. Instead, it repurposes the step-wise denoising mechanism of Latent Diffusion Models to iteratively refine a unified adjacency-matrix embedding, treating each frame's prediction as a reverse-diffusion step conditioned on the prior frame's denoised output. This places the work at the intersection of two active threads: the growing body of diffusion-model applications beyond image generation (segmentation, detection, tracking) and the line of VSGG research seeking better temporal reasoning beyond simple aggregation. The unified feature embedding that jointly decodes object classification, box regression, and predicate prediction also echoes the end-to-end DETR-style philosophy but applied within a diffusion loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing VSGG as an online, iterative denoising problem, DIFFVSGG addresses two practical bottlenecks of prior SOTA methods: GPU memory constraints on long videos and the inability to process real-time streams. The conditional temporal prompting (using prior-frame predictions as denoising conditions) offers a principled alternative to post-hoc temporal aggregation, and the motion-enhanced denoising explicitly encodes kinematic cues that prior methods leave implicit. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (Action Genome), inference speed (8.7 FPS) is modestly slower than some baselines, and the reliance on an off-the-shelf detector means detection errors propagate into the diffusion loop. Open questions include whether the diffusion framing generalizes to other video understanding tasks, how it scales to much longer sequences, and whether the performance gap over offline methods persists on more diverse benchmarks.
