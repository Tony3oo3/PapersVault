## Problem

Existing neural-rendering reconstruction methods produce assets that only render well from viewpoints close to the original observations and require per-scene optimization, while recent diffusion-based 3D generative models rely on clean synthetic data with full object coverage and fail on in-the-wild driving scenes where actors are captured from sparse viewpoints under partial occlusions, limited resolution, and sensor noise. No prior method can generate complete, high-quality 360° 3D assets directly from the sparse, noisy multi-sensor data captured by a moving autonomous vehicle platform.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250993/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse in-the-wild camera images (from six 1920×1080 surround cameras) and LiDAR point clouds captured over 8-second driving sequences, where each actor is observed from only a few viewpoints with partial occlusions. It produces complete 360° 3D neural assets as triplane feature fields (with SDF geometry and view-dependent appearance), which can be rendered into arbitrary camera or LiDAR observations for sensor simulation. Optional conditioning inputs include actor class labels, time-of-day (day/night), or a single reference image for guided generation.

## Method

GenAssets uses a two-stage 'reconstruct-then-generate' framework. In stage 1, a compositional scene representation decomposes each scene into a static background and dynamic actors, each encoded as triplane feature fields; a shared asset decoder maps per-actor latent codes (plus a class embedding) into triplane representations, and occlusion-aware volume rendering across many scenes jointly optimizes these codes with photometric, perceptual, patchGAN adversarial, LiDAR depth, and KL regularization losses. In stage 2, a U-Net diffusion model is trained in the learned latent space to generate new asset codes, supporting unconditional sampling via DDIM and conditional generation through classifier-free guidance (for class/time-of-day) or rendering-guided denoising that steers the sampling trajectory using gradients of the camera/LiDAR rendering loss with respect to the current latent code (for single-image-to-3D).

## Results

On PandaSet, GenAssets achieves the best reconstruction quality across all three evaluation settings: PSNR 21.34 / SSIM 0.825 / LPIPS 0.113 on sparse view synthesis (10% training frames), PSNR 18.36 / SSIM 0.805 / LPIPS 0.147 on novel camera synthesis, and FID 100.28 on 360° view synthesis. For unconditional 3D generation, it attains FID 59.50 and KID 28.32 (×10³), substantially outperforming the best GAN baseline EG3D (FID 80.56, KID 38.97). Augmenting a BEVFormer 3D detector with GenAssets-generated assets improves mAP from 27.08 to 29.32 and AP@4m from 45.67 to 49.00.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenAssets outperforms all reconstruction baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest per-scene method NeuRAD [74], it gains +0.27 dB PSNR and −0.016 LPIPS on sparse views, a larger +0.87 dB PSNR and +0.082 SSIM on novel camera synthesis, and a 59-point FID reduction (100.28 vs 159.38) on 360° synthesis. Generalizable methods G3R [10] and Street Gaussians [93] trail by 2–3 dB PSNR on sparse views and show severe degradation on novel cameras and 360° rotation. PixelSplat [8], limited to two input views, is the weakest reconstruction baseline (PSNR 17.67). For generation, GenAssets (FID 59.50, KID 28.32) beats EG3D [7] by 21 FID points, DiscoScene [90] by 79 FID points, and SSDNeRF [9] by 132 FID points, with the gap widening on KID. The single-image-to-3D comparison against MeshLRM [83] is qualitative only, where GenAssets produces more consistent and less distorted unobserved views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: per-scene neural rendering for driving (NeuRAD, Street Gaussians, UniSim) and 3D generative models (EG3D, SSDNeRF, DiffRF). It departs from prior 3D generative models, which rely on clean synthetic object-centric data with full 360° coverage, by learning directly from sparse, occluded, in-the-wild LiDAR and camera data via a compositional scene representation. The key architectural departure is the 'reconstruct-then-generate' pipeline: rather than diffusing in high-dimensional triplane space (as in DiffRF or L3DG) or optimizing per-scene representations (as in NeuRAD), it first learns a shared low-dimensional latent code per actor across many scenes using occlusion-aware multi-scene rendering, then applies a U-Net diffusion model in that compact latent space. The triplane representation and volume-rendering pipeline are inherited from EG3D and UniSim/NeuRAD, while the latent diffusion framework follows the LDM paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving simulation: the inability to generate diverse, complete 3D assets from real sensor data at scale, which is essential for testing long-tail scenarios. The demonstrated data augmentation result (improving BEVFormer mAP from 27.08 to 29.32) shows downstream utility beyond visual quality. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (PandaSet, 103 scenes), and the paper explicitly leaves open dynamic lighting, animation, and intrinsic decomposition. The approach's reliance on a pre-trained asset decoder and per-scene background fields also raises scalability questions for very large or highly diverse driving corpora.
