## Problem

Analytical learning-based CIL methods (e.g., ACIL) that freeze the pre-trained feature extractor after first-task fine-tuning achieve strong stability but suffer from limited adaptivity to new classes, particularly on datasets diverging from the pre-training distribution. This paper identifies this overlooked sub-optimality and asks how to continually enhance PTM adaptivity while preserving the recursive, replay-free memorization that makes analytical learning attractive.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images sequentially across T non-overlapping class tasks in a replay-free setting (no stored instances from prior tasks). It outputs a single class label per image via a two-layer analytical classification head (random buffer layer + linear layer). The only persistent state between tasks is the adapter weights, the auto-correlation matrix, and a set of class prototypes—no exemplar storage is required.

## Method

MoAL combines three mechanisms on a ViT-B/16-IN21K backbone with a single trainable adapter. (1) Momentum-based adapter weight interpolation (θ = αθ + (1−α)θ̂) is applied at each epoch end to gradually adapt the PTM to new tasks while retaining pre-trained generalizability, replacing the frozen-extractor constraint of ACIL. (2) Knowledge Memorization recursively updates the analytical head's linear weights and auto-correlation matrix via a closed-form least-squares solution (Theorem 1), requiring only current-task samples. (3) Knowledge Rumination corrects old-class prototypes through a two-layer MLP trained to align consecutive feature spaces, then generates fine-grained features for old classes by translating new-class features via prototype offsets, and selectively reinforces misclassified old knowledge through a masked recursive update (Theorem 2). The momentum-induced forgetting of outdated mappings is thus compensated by rumination, achieving simultaneous gains in plasticity and stability.

## Results

MoAL achieves state-of-the-art average incremental accuracy (Ā) across all four benchmarks: 93.84% on CIFAR-100, 72.26% on ImageNet-A, 84.26% on ImageNet-R, and 84.72% on OmniBenchmark (averaged across task settings). On the most challenging out-of-distribution datasets, MoAL surpasses the best baseline (ACIL) by +2.29% on ImageNet-A and +2.92% on ImageNet-R in Ā. For last-task accuracy (A_T), MoAL reaches 89.90% (CIFAR-100), 61.10% (ImageNet-A), 79.19% (ImageNet-R), and 76.62% (OmniBenchmark), outperforming all PTM-based baselines except ACIL on CIFAR-100 where the gap is negligible (−0.05%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoAL outperforms all nine baselines on average incremental accuracy across all four datasets. The closest competitor is ACIL [59], the original analytic CIL method with a frozen feature extractor; MoAL improves over ACIL by +1.78% (CIFAR-100), +2.29% (ImageNet-A), +2.92% (ImageNet-R), and +8.19% (OmniBenchmark) in Ā, with the largest gains on out-of-distribution data where frozen representations are most limiting. Among PTM-based prompt/adapter methods, EASE [53] is the strongest baseline on CIFAR-100 (91.81% avg Ā) but MoAL leads by +2.03%; on OmniBenchmark, Aper [52] is the best baseline (79.78%) and MoAL surpasses it by +4.94%. Prompt-based methods (L2P [43], DualPrompt [42], CODA-Prompt [34]) and the unified tuning framework LAE [6] trail MoAL by 4–12% on ImageNet-A and ImageNet-R, confirming that adapter-based tuning with analytic heads is more effective than prompt-based approaches for CIL. On last-task accuracy A_T, MoAL matches or exceeds ACIL on CIFAR-100 (89.90% vs 89.95%) while leading by +1.45% on ImageNet-A and +1.91% on ImageNet-R, demonstrating that the knowledge rumination mechanism effectively recovers old-class performance lost through continual adapter adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two CIL lineages: analytical learning-based methods (ACIL, GKEAL, DS-AL) that use recursive least-squares solutions with frozen feature extractors, and PTM-based methods (Aper, EASE, LAE) that leverage pre-trained representations with parameter-efficient tuning. It departs from the standard ACIL formulation by identifying that freezing the PTM after first-task fine-tuning creates a sub-optimality in adaptivity, and replaces the frozen backbone with momentum-based adapter weight interpolation for continual adaptation. The knowledge rumination mechanism, inspired by biological rumination, is a novel addition that uses prototype correction and fine-grained feature generation to selectively reinforce old-class knowledge without replay, going beyond the absolute memorization guarantee of classical analytical CIL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the stability-plasticity tradeoff in PTM-based analytical CIL can be addressed without sacrificing the recursive closed-form solution, offering a practical recipe (momentum interpolation + rumination) that practitioners can adopt. The finding that deliberately disrupting the strict recursive solution through continual adapter updates—thereby 'forgetting' outdated mappings—actually enables better old-class performance via selective reinforcement is a useful conceptual reframing. Open questions include whether the rumination mechanism scales to very long task sequences (beyond 30 tasks), whether the prototype correction network generalizes to non-visual modalities, and whether the approach extends to other analytical learning variants (e.g., kernel-based GKEAL).
