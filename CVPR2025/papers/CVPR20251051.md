## Problem

Current image tokenizers (KL-VAE, VQ-VAE) face two coupled limitations: they require 256–1024 latent tokens for 256×256 or 512×512 images, creating a quadratic computational bottleneck for Transformer-based generative models, and their latent spaces lack discriminative semantic structure due to lossy Gaussian constraints or non-differentiable discrete quantization. This paper asks whether a single tokenizer can simultaneously achieve very high compression (32–64 tokens) and a semantically rich latent space suitable for downstream generation.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes RGB images at 256×256 or 512×512 and produces a sequence of L continuous 1D latent tokens (L=32 or 64) of dimension D, rather than a 2D spatial grid. The encoder is a ViT that patchifies the image and appends L learnable 1D tokens; only the outputs at those token positions are retained as the latent code. The decoder reconstructs the image from N learnable mask tokens concatenated with the L latent tokens, regressing pixel values via a final linear layer. For conditional generation, a class label is additionally provided to the downstream generative model (DiT, SiT, or MAR).

## Method

SoftVQ-VAE replaces VQ-VAE's hard argmin codebook lookup with a soft categorical posterior: q(z|x) = Softmax(−‖ẑ − C‖₂/τ), so each latent token becomes a weighted sum of multiple codewords rather than a single one, substantially increasing per-token representation capacity. The encoder and decoder are ViT blocks operating on 1D learnable tokens, and because the soft posterior is fully differentiable, the codebook is optimized directly from the reconstruction loss without codebook or commit losses. A key enabling consequence is that the latent space can be regularized by a simple cosine-similarity alignment loss against frozen DINOv2 features (analogous to REPA but applied at the tokenizer level), yielding a semantically discriminative latent space. The final training objective combines reconstruction, perceptual, adversarial, alignment, and KL-divergence terms, and the method generalizes from soft K-Means to a GMM variant (GMMVQ-VAE) with data-dependent component weights.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, SiT-XL trained with SoftVQ-L (64 tokens) achieves a gFID of 1.78 (with CFG) and 5.35 (without CFG), outperforming SiT-XL/2 with KL tokenizer (1024 tokens) which scores 2.06 and 8.30 respectively. On 512×512, SiT-XL with SoftVQ-BL (64 tokens) reaches gFID 2.21 with CFG, beating SiT-XL/2 (2.62) and DiT-XL/2 (3.04). Inference throughput improves by up to 18× (256×256) and 55× (512×512) over KL-based counterparts, while training iterations are reduced by 2.3× (2.5M vs 7M steps). Reconstruction rFID remains competitive at 0.61–0.89 (256×256) and 0.64–0.71 (512×512) with only 32–64 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KL-VAE-based baselines (DiT-XL/2, SiT-XL/2 with 1024 tokens), SoftVQ-VAE with only 32–64 tokens achieves substantially lower gFID: SiT-XL + SoftVQ-L (64 tokens) scores 5.35 w/o CFG vs 8.30 for SiT-XL/2, and 1.86 w/ CFG vs 2.06. Even SiT-XL/2 + REPA (1.42 w/ CFG) is outperformed by SoftVQ-L 64 tokens w/o CFG (5.35 vs 5.90), and the gap narrows with CFG (1.86 vs 1.42). Against VQ-VAE-based autoregressive models (MaskGIT, Taming, RQ-Trans, LlamaGen), SoftVQ-VAE achieves far better reconstruction (rFID 0.61–0.65 vs 2.19–7.94) and competitive or better generation FID. Compared to TiTok (128 tokens, rFID 1.61), SoftVQ-S at 64 tokens achieves rFID 1.03 and gFID 11.24 vs TiTok's 19.23, a large margin. Against DC-AE (64 tokens, rFID 0.69), SoftVQ-S is competitive on reconstruction (1.03) but achieves better generation (gFID 11.24 vs 15.36). With MAR-H, SoftVQ-VAE is slightly worse than the KL-256 baseline (gFID 1.93 vs 1.55 w/ CFG), likely due to a lower learning rate, but at 40% lower GFLOPs. On 512×512, SiT-XL + SoftVQ-BL (64 tokens) achieves gFID 2.21 w/ CFG, beating SiT-XL/2 (2.62), DiT-XL/2 (3.04), and approaching StyleGAN-XL (2.41).

## Relation to Prior Work

SoftVQ-VAE is a direct, minimal modification of VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al. 2017; Esser et al. 2021), replacing the hard argmin quantization with a soft categorical posterior over a learnable codebook, effectively generalizing K-Means to Soft K-Means (and further to GMM). It adopts the Transformer auto-encoder architecture with learnable 1D latent tokens from TiTok (Yu et al. 2024) and similar concurrent work, but departs from both VQ-VAE and KL-VAE by being fully differentiable, eliminating the need for straight-through estimation, codebook loss, and commit loss. The representation alignment component adapts the REPA idea (Yu et al. 2024) from the generative model's intermediate layers to the tokenizer's latent space, which the authors note is equivalent to performing REPA at the input of downstream models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18x–55x inference throughput gains with competitive FID scores make high-quality image generation substantially more accessible, and the fully-differentiable design simplifies the training recipe (no codebook/commit loss tuning). A key conceptual finding is that latent space semantic quality (measured by linear probing) predicts downstream generation performance more strongly than tokenizer reconstruction quality, shifting focus from pixel-fidelity to representation structure. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video and audio tokenization, whether even higher compression ratios (e.g., 16 tokens) remain viable, and whether the observed tradeoff between latent dimensionality and generation quality reflects a fundamental limitation of current generative architectures.
