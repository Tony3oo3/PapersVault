## Problem

Existing INR-based continuous super-resolution methods rely on single-scale positional encoding with MLPs, which fails to model the hierarchical neighborhood structure among local sampling points and limits their ability to capture fine multi-scale details. Additionally, these methods do not incorporate non-local information within the implicit decoding function, further constraining reconstruction quality at arbitrary scales.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image of arbitrary size and produces a high-resolution reconstruction at any continuous scale factor (tested from ×2 to ×30). The encoder extracts latent features at the same spatial resolution as the input (no downsampling), and the decoder queries individual pixel coordinates to reconstruct RGB values, with a skip connection adding the bilinearly upsampled LR image to the decoded output. Training uses 48×48 LR patches with random scale factors sampled uniformly from U(1,4).

## Method

HIIF introduces a hierarchical positional encoding that reparameterizes the relative coordinate of each queried pixel into L=6 discrete levels (using a mod factor S=2), so that neighboring sampling points share encoded features at coarser levels but diverge at finer levels, explicitly modeling multi-scale local structure. These hierarchical encodings are progressively injected into successive decoder layers (a multi-scale architecture) rather than all at once, allowing each layer to focus on a specific frequency subband. A multi-head linear attention module (B=2 blocks, N=16 heads) is embedded in the decoder to capture non-local dependencies across the latent feature map while maintaining low computational cost. The framework is encoder-agnostic, validated with EDSR, RDN, and SwinIR backbones, and trained end-to-end with L1 loss and cosine-annealed Adam optimization.

## Results

On the DIV2K validation set with the RDN encoder, HIIF achieves 35.25 dB (×2), 31.56 dB (×3), and 29.56 dB (×4) PSNR for in-distribution scales, and 27.18 dB (×6) and 24.04 dB (×12) for out-of-distribution scales. With the SwinIR encoder, HIIF reaches 35.36 dB (×2) and 31.61 dB (×3), the highest absolute PSNR among all tested configurations. Across all three encoder backbones and five test datasets, HIIF consistently outperforms the second-best method by up to 0.17 dB in PSNR, with the largest margins observed at in-distribution scales using the RDN backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIIF outperforms all six INR-based continuous SR baselines (MetaSR, LIIF, LTE, CLIT, CiaoSR, SRNO) across every encoder backbone, dataset, and scale tested. The most notable gains appear with the RDN encoder on DIV2K: HIIF leads SRNO by 0.09 dB at ×2 (35.25 vs 35.16) and by 0.14 dB at ×3 (31.56 vs 31.42), and surpasses CiaoSR by 0.13 dB at ×4 (29.56 vs 29.43). At out-of-distribution scales (×6–×30), HIIF maintains a consistent 0.02–0.05 dB advantage over the strongest competitors (SRNO, CLIT, CiaoSR). Compared to the encoder-only fixed-scale baselines (RDN only: 34.59 dB at ×2), HIIF with the same RDN backbone gains 0.66 dB while simultaneously supporting arbitrary scales. On the Urban100 dataset with SwinIR, HIIF achieves 33.75 dB (×2) and 29.63 dB (×3), beating CiaoSR (33.65 / 29.52) and CLIT (33.56 / 29.43) by 0.10 and 0.11 dB respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIIF extends the LIIF framework (Chen et al., CVPR 2021) for continuous super-resolution by replacing the single-scale relative coordinate input with a multi-scale hierarchical positional encoding, an idea borrowed from the authors' prior video compression work (HiNeRV, NeurIPS 2023). It also departs from the fixed-weight local ensemble used in LIIF/LTE by introducing a multi-head linear attention block (inspired by XCiT and Efficient Attention) to capture non-local dependencies among the four nearest latent codes. The method is positioned as a drop-in decoder that can be paired with any existing encoder backbone (EDSR, RDN, SwinIR), distinguishing it from end-to-end cascaded approaches like CLIT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent PSNR gains (up to 0.17 dB) across all tested scales (×2 to ×30), three encoder backbones, and five benchmark datasets demonstrate that multi-scale hierarchical encoding is a broadly applicable enhancement for INR-based continuous SR, not an architecture-specific trick. The modular design means practitioners can adopt the decoder without retraining their preferred encoder. However, the absolute improvements remain modest, and the inference cost (35 s on Urban100 with EDSR) is notably higher than LIIF or LTE, leaving open the question of whether the hierarchical encoding can be made more efficient without sacrificing the multi-scale benefit. A key open direction is whether the hierarchical encoding depth (L=6) and mod factor (S=2) can be adapted per-scale or per-content to further close the gap at very large out-of-distribution factors.
