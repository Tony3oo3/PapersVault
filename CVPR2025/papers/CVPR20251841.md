## Problem

Existing conditional image synthesis methods are task-specific, each designed to handle a single condition type (text, layout, or drag) with limited generalizability across diverse conditioning signals. This paper identifies that even within each condition type, current approaches suffer from specific alignment failures: text methods treat descriptions holistically rather than matching fine-grained region-word concepts, layout methods only enforce object placement without preserving relative geometry, and drag methods rely on sparse one-step feature optimization that produces incomplete or unstable motion.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts any flexible combination of three condition units: natural language text descriptions, spatial layout specifications (segmentation masks or bounding boxes with class labels), and drag signals (original-destination point pairs applied to a reference image). These units can be used independently or jointly, and the method is training-free, operating as a guidance layer on top of pre-trained diffusion backbones (Stable Diffusion v1.5/v3.0, DenseDiffusion, A&R, DragDiffusion). The output is a single synthesized RGB image that simultaneously satisfies all active conditions, with guidance applied only during the first 70% of diffusion timesteps.

## Method

The paper proposes three plug-and-play dense alignment guidance modules that modify the predicted noise during diffusion sampling via gradients of energy functions computed on the one-step approximate clean image x̂₀. The Dense Concept Alignment (DCA) module decomposes text into attribute concepts (adjective-noun pairs) and relation concepts via scene graph parsing, extracts corresponding visual regions using Grounding DINO, and aligns them at scene, attribute, and relation levels using CLIP cosine similarity. The Dense Geometry Alignment (DGA) module uses YOLOv7-Seg to obtain predicted masks from x̂₀, then enforces coverage (IoU plus non-overlap penalties), pairwise size-ratio consistency, and pairwise centroid-distance alignment relative to the target layout. The Dense Motion Alignment (DMA) module densifies sparse drag signals using SAM instance masks and radial-basis-function Gaussian displacement fields, then aligns RAFT-estimated optical flow with the reference flow while preserving pixel-level appearance and DINOv2 semantic consistency between adjacent timesteps.

## Results

On the DenseDiffusion benchmark (text + segmentation mask), A&R + DGA + DCA achieves IoU 50.61 and SOA-I 86.33, a 27.1% relative improvement in IoU and 7.55% in SOA-I over the strongest baseline A&R (38.97 / 78.80). On COCO long descriptions (text-only), SD v3.0 + DCA reduces FID from 10.51 to 9.22 (12.3% improvement) and raises CLIP Score from 0.2861 to 0.2946. On DragBench (text + drag), the combined DMA + DCA modules outperform DragDiffusion on both Mean Distance (lower is better) and Image Fidelity (1−LPIPS, higher is better), confirming effective motion alignment with fewer visual artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed framework is evaluated against 13 baselines across three conditioning scenarios. On text-only generation (COCO), SD v3.0 + DCA outperforms all training-based baselines including SDXL (FID 9.22 vs 9.79), PixArt-Σ (10.73), PixArt-α (13.97), ELLA (13.64), and ParaDiffusion (12.96) on both FID and CLIP Score. On layout-guided generation (DenseDiffusion), the modular combination DGA + DCA on top of A&R achieves IoU 50.61, surpassing A&R (38.97), Dense Diffusion (34.99), SD-Pww (23.76), Structure Diffusion, and Composable Diffusion by wide margins; notably, DGA alone already yields a 27.1% IoU gain over A&R, and adding DCA provides a further 1.09 IoU and 1.58 SOA-I improvement. On drag-based editing (DragBench), DMA + DCA beats DragDiffusion on both Mean Distance and Image Fidelity, though DMA alone causes a slight IF drop due to oversmoothing, which DCA partially recovers. The method also demonstrates plug-and-play compatibility, showing consistent gains when applied to both SD v1.5 and SD v3.0 backbones, as well as to the DenseDiffusion and A&R architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free diffusion guidance paradigm (Bansal et al., CVPR 2023) by decomposing conditional synthesis into three independently composable modules—DCA, DGA, and DMA—each targeting a specific condition unit (text, layout, drag). For text alignment, it departs from whole-image CLIP guidance (DOODL) by introducing scene-graph-based decomposition into attribute and relation concepts; for layout, it goes beyond attention-map region constraints (BoxDiff, DenseDiffusion, A&R) by adding explicit pairwise size-ratio and centroid-distance losses; for drag, it replaces single-step feature optimization (DragDiffusion, DragNoise) with dense displacement fields regularized across displacement, appearance, and semantics. The key conceptual contribution is the modular framing: rather than designing a separate pipeline per condition, the paper treats conditions as plug-and-play units that can be freely combined during a single diffusion sampling process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free design means practitioners can adopt individual modules (or combinations) on top of existing diffusion backbones (SD v1.5, v3.0, DenseDiffusion, A&R) without retraining, lowering the barrier to multi-condition control. A notable limitation is the dependence on the foundation model's world knowledge: counterfactual scenes (e.g., a river flowing upward) remain ungeneratable, pointing toward future work on knowledge-aware generative paradigms. Open questions include whether the modular combination scales gracefully to more than three condition types, and whether the reliance on external models (Grounding DINO, SAM, RAFT, DINOv2) introduces compounding error in complex multi-condition scenarios.
