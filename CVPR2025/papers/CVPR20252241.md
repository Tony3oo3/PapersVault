## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) suffer from catastrophic forgetting when trained sequentially on non-stationary VQA data streams, and directly applying existing continual learning methods to MLLMs still yields significant performance drops compared to joint multi-task training. Additionally, prior continual VQA work relies on smaller pre-trained encoders (e.g., T5, CLIP) with limited commonsense knowledge, and no method adequately handles the fact that each task and instance may require a different subset of specialized experts.

## Input / Output

The model receives an instruction template containing a 336×336 RGB image (encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14) and a natural-language question, and generates a text answer autoregressively (typically a single word or phrase). During continual training, the model processes 10 sequential VQA task types (recognition, location, judgment, commonsense, count, action, color, type, subcategory, causal) from VQA v2. At inference, the task identity is unknown and must be recovered by matching the input's CLS-token embedding to pre-computed per-task cluster centers via nearest-neighbor lookup.

## Method

CL-MoE builds a LoRA-based Mixture-of-Experts layer (8 experts, rank 64) on top of LLaVA-7B, replacing the FFN in each transformer block. The Dual-Router MoE (RMoE) uses an instance-level router (softmax over expert scores from the input representation) to select local experts per sample, and a task-level router (mean of instance-level weights over the entire task dataset) to identify the top-K global experts for that task; at inference, the final expert-weighted output blends both with a balancing hyperparameter β. The Dynamic Momentum MoE (MMoE) then updates expert parameters after each task by classifying them as task-shared (appearing in both current and previous top-K sets), task-specific (only current), or irrelevant, and applying a per-expert momentum coefficient λ (γ for shared/irrelevant, 1−γ for specific, with γ=0.7) to interpolate between the previous parameters θ_{t−1} and the newly tuned parameters φ_t, thereby retaining old knowledge while absorbing new task-specific skills.

## Results

On VQA v2 with LLaVA-7B, CL-MOE achieves a Final Average Performance (AP) of 51.34% and Average Forgetting (AF) of −0.02%, outperforming the previous SOTA VQACL (AP 43.49%, AF 9.10%) by 7.85 and 9.12 percentage points respectively. The negative AF value indicates positive backward transfer: the model's average performance on the 9 earlier tasks after learning all 10 tasks is marginally better than the per-task fine-tuning performance. Compared to the Vanilla LLaVA baseline (AP 32.51%, AF 20.69%), CL-MOE improves AP by 18.83 points and reduces forgetting by 20.71 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL-MOE outperforms all six baselines on both AP and AF under the LLaVA-7B setting. Against the strongest baseline VQACL (CVPR 2023), CL-MOE gains +7.85 AP (51.34 vs. 43.49) and reduces AF from 9.10 to −0.02, the only method achieving negative forgetting. Among rehearsal-based methods, ER (AP 41.95, AF 10.20) and DER (AP 41.16, AF 11.28) are the closest competitors but still trail CL-MOE by 9.39 and 10.18 AP points respectively. Regularization-based methods EWC (AP 37.28) and MAS (AP 37.71) lag further behind, confirming that parameter-importance regularization alone is insufficient for MLLM-scale continual VQA. The virtual-sample method VS (AP 39.79, AF 12.70) also underperforms CL-MOE by a wide margin. Notably, CL-MOE's negative AF demonstrates positive backward transfer—something none of the baselines achieve—while its per-task accuracies (e.g., 75.22% on Judgement, 69.54% on Action) approach the Multitask upper bound (80.74%, 75.97%) more closely than any other continual method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual VQA setting established by VQACL (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023) from smaller encoder-decoder models (VL-T5) to instruction-tuned MLLMs (LLaVA-7B), arguing that the rich commonsense knowledge in large LLMs is essential for multimodal reasoning. Architecturally, it builds on LoRA-based Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but introduces two novel components: a Dual-Router MoE (RMoE) that combines instance-level and task-level routing to select experts from local and global perspectives, and a Dynamic Momentum MoE (MMoE) that updates expert parameters via a momentum strategy conditioned on whether each expert is task-shared, task-specific, or irrelevant. This departs from prior CL methods (EWC, MAS, ER, DER) that apply uniform regularization or rehearsal across all parameters, instead exploiting the heterogeneous expert structure to differentially preserve or update knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that MLLMs are both more capable and more vulnerable to catastrophic forgetting than smaller multimodal models, highlighting a tension that the field must address as MLLMs become the default backbone for vision-language tasks. The negative AF (−0.02) showing net backward transfer is encouraging but the gap to the multitask upper bound (~4 AP points) and the sensitivity to task order (57.08 vs 51.34 AP) indicate the problem is not yet solved. Evaluation is limited to a single dataset (VQA v2) and a single task family, leaving open whether the dual-router + momentum strategy generalizes to more diverse continual multimodal settings (e.g., video, audio, or open-ended generation). The approach could serve as a template for parameter-efficient continual learning in other MoE-based LLM architectures.
