## Problem

Existing view-pair contrastive multi-view clustering methods assume uniform view quality and inherent multi-view redundancy, but when these assumptions fail—particularly with non-redundant or noisy views—forced alignment degrades high-quality view representations and amplifies error propagation. The paper identifies that the multi-view redundancy assumption is non-transitive, making indiscriminate pairwise contrastive learning across all views actively harmful rather than beneficial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252647/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts V heterogeneous views of N samples, where each view v has its own feature dimensionality d^v (e.g., Caltech101-20 has 6 views with dimensions 48/40/254/1984/512/928), along with the number of clusters K. During pretraining, k-means anchors are extracted per view to initialize bipartite graphs of shape n×k^(i), which are then refined through an encoder-decoder. The output is a set of cluster assignments plus shared cross-view representations Z^H ∈ R^{N×V×D} produced by a shared MLP that projects view-specific features into a unified space.

## Method

EASEMVC introduces a dual selection mechanism operating at both view and sample levels. At the view level, bipartite graphs are constructed per view using k-means anchors and refined via an encoder-decoder; Optimal Transport distances between these bipartite graphs define a view-graph topology, from which each view is paired only with its nearest neighbor (minimum OT distance) rather than all other views. At the sample level, OT distances between cross-view sample representations form a sample graph, and each sample's weight is inversely proportional to its total distance to all others (softmax-normalized), so more representative samples contribute more to the contrastive loss. The contrastive loss (a weighted InfoNCE variant) is computed only over the selected view pairs with sample-level weighting, and is jointly optimized with a reconstruction loss and a Deep Divergence Clustering (DDC) loss in an end-to-end framework.

## Results

EASEMVC achieves the best ACC on five of six datasets: 96.05% on HandWritten, 93.25% on UCI, 83.81% on ALOI-100, 62.15% on FashionMNIST, and 55.87% on Caltech101-20. On ALOI-100, the largest gap is observed, with EASEMVC surpassing the second-best method (SCMVC, 82.52%) by 1.29 ACC points and 0.81 NMI points (93.17 vs 92.36). On the text dataset BBC, EASEMVC is second-best with 83.94% ACC, trailing SDSNE (85.84%) by 1.9 points. The method also achieves the highest NMI on all image datasets (e.g., 92.67 on HandWritten, 93.17 on ALOI-100) and the best Purity and F-score on HandWritten (96.65%) and ALOI-100 (85.48% / 82.83%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EASEMVC outperforms all 10 baselines on the four image datasets (Caltech101-20, HandWritten, UCI, ALOI-100) and FashionMNIST across ACC, NMI, Purity, and F-score. The closest competitor on image data is SCMVC [44], which trails by 1.29 ACC on ALOI-100 (82.52 vs 83.81) and 0.30 ACC on UCI (92.95 vs 93.25). DSMVC [33] is competitive on HandWritten (95.25 vs 96.05 ACC) but is far surpassed on ALOI-100 (14.89 vs 83.81). On the text dataset BBC, SDSNE [21] is the sole method that beats EASEMVC, achieving 85.84% ACC versus 83.94%, though EASEMVC still leads all other baselines by a wide margin (next best: BMVC at 75.04%). Several baselines (SDSNE, LMVSC, MFLVC) fail with out-of-memory errors on ALOI-100 or FashionMNIST, whereas EASEMVC completes training on all six datasets. The conventional methods LMVSC [15] and BMVC [56] are consistently outperformed by 10–40 ACC points across datasets, and the deep baselines AE2-Nets [53], SDMVC [47], MFLVC [48], and DEMVC [46] show particularly large gaps on ALOI-100 (ranging from 14.89% to 63.54% ACC vs EASEMVC's 83.81%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Dong et al. (ICCV 2023), which introduced optimal transport (OT) distance between bipartite graphs for cross-view topology modeling in deep MVC, and on Kampffmeyer et al. (2019) for the Deep Divergence Clustering (DDC) loss. Its key departure is a theoretical argument that the multi-view redundancy assumption underlying view-pair contrastive learning is non-transitive, meaning that indiscriminate alignment of all view pairs degrades high-quality views when redundancy is absent. Rather than fusing all views or relying on a fixed 'core view' strategy (as in DCP), EASEMVC introduces a dual selection mechanism: a view-level nearest-neighbor selection in OT-graph space and a sample-level weighting based on cross-view sample topology, both applied within a standard InfoNCE-style contrastive objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in the MVC community—that adding more views monotonically improves clustering—by showing empirically and theoretically that non-redundant views actively harm representation quality when forced into pairwise alignment. The dual selection mechanism is modular and could be grafted onto existing view-pair MVC frameworks, offering a practical remedy for the 'view degradation' problem. However, the view selection is limited to a single nearest neighbor per view (rather than top-k), the pre-training phase for bipartite graphs adds computational overhead, and it remains open whether the OT-based view topology generalizes to very large view counts or highly heterogeneous modalities. The method also does not address incomplete views, a common real-world scenario.
