## Problem

Existing non-rigid point cloud matching methods either require triangular meshes during training (limiting applicability to raw scans) or rely on reconstruction-based proxy tasks that suffer from mode collapse, preventing high-quality dense correspondences. The paper identifies two critical design gaps: the need for matching cues beyond spatial proximity that are easy to obtain without meshing, and the need for a proxy task that physically aligns shapes rather than merely reconstructing them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251973/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two unstructured point clouds of arbitrary and potentially different resolutions (S ∈ R^{N×3}, T ∈ R^{M×3}) as input, with no meshing, re-sampling, or correspondence annotation required. It produces dense soft correspondence maps (Π_ST, Π_TS) via KNN in the learned latent space, along with a deformation graph encoding per-node rigid transformations. The visual encoding step internally projects each point cloud onto three orthogonal planes (xy, yz, xz) with depth-aware pixel intensities, but this is an internal representation—the user-facing interface remains purely point-cloud in and out.

## Method

DV-Matcher is a Siamese network with two key innovations. First, a visual encoding module projects each point cloud onto three orthogonal planes, extracts per-pixel features via DINOv2 upscaled by FeatUp, and pulls them back to per-point features, which are fused with positional encodings as input to a dual-pathway LG-Net (global PCT-style attention + local N2P attention with feature-domain neighborhood search). Second, a deformation-based proxy task replaces reconstruction: an MLP predicts rigid transformations at FPS-sampled deformation graph nodes, which are propagated to all points via distance weighting, and the chamfer distance between the deformed source and target serves as the primary loss. This is supplemented by ARAP regularization, a smoothness loss, and a geometric similarity loss that maximizes angular alignment between embedding-space neighbor distances and heat-method geodesic distances. The entire pipeline is trained self-supervised without correspondence labels, and the deformation graph prediction is a single feedforward pass rather than iterative optimization.

## Results

On the standard near-isometric benchmarks (Table 1), DV-Matcher achieves mean geodesic errors of 6.2 (SCAPE_r→SCAPE_r), 5.1 (SCAPE_r→FAUST_r), and 7.2 (SCAPE_r→SHREC'19_r), outperforming the best pure point-cloud baselines DPC (17.3, 11.2, 28.7) and SE-ORNet (24.6, 22.8, 23.6) by 59–75%. On the challenging non-isometric stress tests, DV-Matcher scores 7.7 on SHREC'07-H and 6.9 on DT4D-H, a 42% improvement over the second-best method. On partial matching (Table 2), it achieves 6.2 on SCAPE-PV and 13.0 on SHREC'16-HOLES, beating DPC by 54% and 48% respectively. On medical SSM (Table 5), it reaches 2.3 mm CD on Spleen and 1.9 mm on Pancreas, outperforming Point2SSM by 29% and 30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the primary pure point-cloud competitors, DV-Matcher dominates: it outperforms DPC by 64% (6.2 vs 17.3) and SE-ORNet by 75% (6.2 vs 24.6) on SCAPE_r→SCAPE_r, and the gap widens on cross-dataset transfers (e.g., 7.2 vs 28.7 on SCAPE_r→SHREC'19_r). Against the strongest mesh-trained baseline SSMSM, DV-Matcher wins on all cross-dataset settings (5.1 vs 8.5 on SCAPE_r→FAUST_r; 10.4 vs 11.0 on FAUST_r→SCAPE_r) but trails on the in-distribution SCAPE_r→SCAPE_r case (6.2 vs 4.1). On non-isometric stress tests, DV-Matcher beats NIE (7.7 vs 13.4 on SHREC'07-H) and SSMSM (8.2 vs 42.2 on FAUST_r→SHREC'07-H) by large margins. For partial matching, DV-Matcher surpasses DPFM (6.2 vs 11.5 on SCAPE-PV) and SSMSM (13.0 vs 16.7 on SHREC'16-HOLES). On medical SSM, it outperforms Point2SSM (2.3 vs 3.4 mm CD on Spleen) and ISR (2.3 vs 17.6 mm), while on real noisy scans (Table 4) it achieves 9.40 vs DPC's 32.77 and SE-ORNet's 29.18 on SHREC19-PV — roughly a 3× improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

DV-Matcher sits at the intersection of two recent trends: (1) injecting pre-trained 2D vision model features into 3D shape analysis (following I2P-MAE's projection scheme and FeatUp's resolution upsampling), and (2) deformation-based proxy tasks for correspondence learning (following the deformation graph formulation of RMA-Net and DPFM). The key departure is conceptual: rather than using DINOv2 features as intermediate matching cues or reconstruction targets (as in I2P-MAE or semantic surface maps), the paper treats them as a fixed 'visual encoding' analogous to positional encoding, and lets a learnable dual-pathway attention network (PCT global attention + N2P local attention) refine them under geometric losses. This contrasts with reconstruction-based methods (DPC, SE-ORNet) that the authors show suffer from mode collapse, and with mesh-dependent spectral methods (SSMSM, NIE) that violate the point-cloud-only training requirement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DV-Matcher achieves state-of-the-art non-rigid matching without meshes, correspondence labels, or fixed-resolution resampling — a combination that prior methods could only satisfy partially. The robustness to point density (0.46% degradation from 1024 to ~5000 points vs. 8–15% for baselines) suggests visual encoding effectively compensates for geometric sparsity, which is directly relevant to real-world scans. Open questions remain: the 3.22 s per-pair inference is far from real-time, general orientation invariance is not guaranteed, and the method still requires overlapping-region prediction for raw multi-view scans. The deformation-graph-as-single-feedforward-prediction idea (replacing iterative optimization) is a useful architectural pattern that could transfer to other registration tasks.
