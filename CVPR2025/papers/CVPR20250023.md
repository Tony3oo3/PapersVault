## Problem

Quad Bayer HybridEVS demosaicing requires capturing both long-range and local dependencies to handle the complex CFA pattern and event-pixel color loss, but existing methods relying on Transformers or Mamba incur prohibitive computational costs for edge-device deployment. Binary neural networks offer extreme compression yet remain unexplored for demosaicing, and no prior work has attempted to binarize Mamba's architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel Quad Bayer HybridEVS RAW image where identical-color pixels are grouped in 2×2 blocks and event pixels introduce additional color loss (modeled as an additive term L in the mosaic equation). It outputs a full 3-channel RGB image. Training uses 128×128 random crops with batch size 32, and all computational-load measurements are conducted on 256×256 images; upsampling and downsampling operations remain in full precision to preserve performance.

## Method

BMTNet combines a BBCU-based inpainting subnetwork with a main Binarized Mamba-Transformer (BMT) block that runs Bi-Mamba (global) and Bi-SwinT (local) in parallel. Bi-Mamba binarizes all linear and convolution projections via Sign/RSign with XNOR-based computation and learnable scaling factors, while retaining the core Selective Scan in full precision, achieving 96–97% parameter and OPs reduction on Mamba projections. A frozen binarized visual encoder, pretrained via knowledge distillation from RAM, injects global visual embeddings into the control matrix B of the Selective Scan, enriching the input in an additive manner that stabilizes the latent state. This design reduces total parameters to 1.28M and OPs to 6.56G while matching or exceeding other BNNs and approaching full-precision results.

## Results

On the MIPI dataset, BMTNet achieves 36.95 dB PSNR / 0.975 SSIM with only 1.28M parameters and 6.56G OPs, outperforming the best BNN baseline (ReActNet, 36.47/0.971) by 0.48 dB while using fewer parameters. Across six image datasets, BMTNet attains an average of 35.52 dB PSNR / 0.975 SSIM, surpassing all other BNN methods by at least 0.44 dB and approaching full-precision methods like NAFNet (37.16/0.978) and Restormer (37.45/0.980) at roughly 1/20th the parameter count. On video datasets (REDS, Vid4), BMTNet reaches 37.70/0.985 average, exceeding the best BNN (BBCU, 37.13/0.983) by 0.57 dB and matching the SSIM of the top full-precision method DFormer (39.23/0.985).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMTNet outperforms all five BNN baselines (BNN, ReActNet, BBCU, BTM, BiViT) on every image and video dataset, with the largest margin over BiViT (+2.19 dB average PSNR) and the smallest over ReActNet (+0.44 dB). Among full-precision methods, BMTNet trails DFormer by 2.63 dB and Restormer by 1.93 dB on average PSNR, but achieves comparable SSIM (0.975 vs. 0.978–0.980) while using only 1.28M parameters versus 26–30M. Notably, BMTNet surpasses the lightweight full-precision methods PIPNet (31.97) and CycleISP (31.14) by over 3.5 dB, demonstrating that the binarized Mamba-Transformer hybrid is more effective than larger FP demosaicing networks. On video data (REDS/Vid4), BMTNet (37.70/0.985) exceeds BBCU (37.13/0.983) and ReActNet (37.09/0.983) by ~0.6 dB and matches DFormer's SSIM (0.985) at 1/24th the parameter count. The ablation confirms that replacing Bi-Mamba with full-precision Mamba gains only 0.78 dB at 4.8× the parameters, validating the binarization strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: state space models (Mamba), binary neural networks, and demosaicing for non-standard CFAs. It extends the BNN literature—which has primarily targeted CNNs (BNN, ReActNet, BBCU) and Vision Transformers (BiViT)—by pioneering the binarization of Mamba's projection layers while retaining the core Selective Scan in full precision. The hybrid Mamba-Transformer design draws on both Mamba's linear-complexity long-range modeling and Swin Transformer's local windowed attention, while the global visual encoder borrows the teacher-student distillation paradigm from RAM/SEESR to inject scene-level priors into the SSM's control matrix. The paper departs from prior demosaicing work (which assumes standard Bayer or Quad Bayer without event pixels) by explicitly modeling the additional color loss at event positions in HybridEVS sensors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: HybridEVS sensors (e.g., Alpsentek ALPIX-Eiger) are being integrated into next-generation mobile phones, and demosaicing must run on resource-constrained ISPs where 1.28M parameters and ~6.5G OPs are far more deployable than the 26–30M parameter full-precision alternatives. The design principle of binarizing non-critical projections while preserving the core SSM in full precision offers a general recipe for compressing other SSM-based architectures. However, the ~2 dB PSNR gap to full-precision methods and the reliance on a frozen, pre-trained visual encoder (from RAM) limit generality; it remains open whether this strategy transfers to other SSM variants (e.g., H3, S4) or to demosaicing on other non-Bayer CFA configurations without retraining the encoder.
