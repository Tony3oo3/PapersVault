## Problem

When MLLMs process long videos, the number of visual tokens far exceeds the model's context capacity, forcing frame sampling. Existing approaches like LLaVA-Video rely on uniform sampling, which frequently misses question-relevant moments and produces incorrect answers. The keyframe selection problem—how to choose the most informative subset of frames given a fixed token budget—has not been systematically studied.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (candidate frames sampled at 1 fps) and a text question as input, and outputs M selected keyframes (M = 32 or 64, matching the target MLLM's context capacity) that are fed into the MLLM's visual encoder to produce a text answer. AKS operates as a plug-and-play module inserted before the MLLM, using a separate lightweight VL model (BLIP) to compute prompt-frame relevance scores, so the target MLLM itself remains frozen and unmodified.

## Method

AKS formulates keyframe selection as maximizing a weighted sum of (1) relevance, computed as BLIP image-text matching scores between the prompt and each candidate frame, and (2) coverage, estimated via recursive bin partitioning of the time axis inspired by Ripley's K-function. The proposed ADA (adaptive sampling) algorithm performs a hierarchical judge-and-split optimization: at each recursion level, it compares the average relevance score against the top-M score; if the gap exceeds a threshold s_thr, it greedily selects top-scoring frames (relevance mode), otherwise it splits the bin evenly and recurses (coverage mode). This adaptively interpolates between two extremes—TOP (pure relevance, best for single-moment questions) and BIN (pure coverage, best for multi-moment questions)—without explicitly tuning the balancing hyperparameter λ. The recursion depth L is bounded by ⌈log₂M⌉, keeping computational cost low while ensuring temporal spread of selected keyframes.

## Results

On LongVideoBench, AKS applied to LLaVA-Video-7B achieves 62.7% accuracy, surpassing GPT-4V (61.3%) and Gemini-1.5-Flash (61.6%) while using only 64 input frames versus their 256. On VideoMME, the same configuration reaches 65.3%, a 0.9% gain over the uniform-sampling baseline. Across all three 7B MLLMs tested (Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-OV, LLaVA-Video), AKS yields consistent improvements of 3.8–5.0% on LongVideoBench and 0.9–2.3% on VideoMME without any parameter tuning of the underlying models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AKS consistently outperforms its three host MLLMs under uniform sampling: +5.0% on LVB and +2.3% on V-MME for Qwen2-VL, +4.5%/+1.9% for LLaVA-OV, and +3.8%/+0.9% for LLaVA-Video. Against proprietary models, LLaVA-Video-7B w/AKS (62.7% LVB) exceeds GPT-4V (61.3%) and Gemini-1.5-Flash (61.6%) by 1.4% and 1.1% respectively, while still trailing GPT-4o (66.7%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (64.0%). The paper also reports that LLaVA-Video-7B w/AKS surpasses the 72B variant of LLaVA-Video without AKS by 0.8% on LVB, demonstrating that better keyframe selection can compensate for model scale. Among open-source 7B models, AKS-equipped LLaVA-Video also beats MiniCPM-V 2.6 (8B, 54.9% LVB) and VILA (40B, 60.1% V-MME) on their respective benchmarks. The smaller gain on VideoMME (0.9%) versus LongVideoBench (3.8%) is attributed to VideoMME's multi-moment questions where uniform sampling is already a safer default.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions keyframe selection as an under-studied but critical sub-problem in video-based MLLMs, where prior work (LLaVA-Video, Video-LLaVA) relied on uniform sampling and token-compression methods (MovieChat, LongVLM, CrossGET) reduced token count without guaranteeing preservation of question-relevant information. It departs from language-only keyframe selection (VideoAgent, VideoTree) and dominant-frame selection (Seeling) by formulating the problem as a joint optimization over prompt-frame relevance (via a lightweight VL scorer like BLIP or CLIP) and temporal coverage (via a Ripley K-function-inspired recursive bin partitioning). The resulting AKS algorithm is a plug-and-play module that sits before any frozen MLLM's visual encoder, requiring no retraining of the downstream model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a simple, model-agnostic pre-filtering step can close much of the gap between a 7B video MLLM and proprietary 256-frame systems (e.g., LLaVA-Video-7B + AKS surpasses GPT-4V and Gemini-1.5-Flash on LongVideoBench), suggesting that information quality in the visual context matters as much as model scale. Open questions include whether more efficient pre-filtering (the paper shows 0.1 fps candidate sampling still beats the baseline) can reduce the added VL-scoring cost, whether the relevance-coverage tradeoff generalizes to 4D or streaming video, and whether the adaptive threshold s_thr can be learned rather than hand-tuned per benchmark. A limitation is that the method still requires a separate forward pass through a VL model for every candidate frame, which scales linearly with video length.
