## Problem

Existing novel-view synthesis methods assume input images are mutually consistent in geometry and illumination, but casual real-world captures inevitably contain scene motion, lighting changes, and other inconsistencies. No prior method can synthesize novel views from inputs that are simultaneously sparse (few views) and inconsistent (varying scene states), as dense-capture robustness methods require hundreds of images and sparse-view methods are trained only on consistent data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251062/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes up to 8 sparse posed RGB images that may be mutually inconsistent (different scene states or lighting), a designated reference image specifying the desired scene state to harmonize toward, and camera poses for all views. It produces a set of 7 consistent multiview images (plus the reference, totaling 8) that are mutually 3D-consistent and match the reference state, which are then densified via a finetuned CAT3D model and used to train a Zip-NeRF for final novel-view rendering. At test time the model handles anywhere from 1 to 8 inconsistent conditioning images via uniform dropout during training.

## Method

The core contribution is a generative data augmentation pipeline that uses a pretrained video diffusion model (Lumiere) to simulate realistic world inconsistencies—scene dynamics and lighting changes—on existing consistent multiview datasets, creating paired (inconsistent, consistent) training data at scale (~6M frames for dynamics, ~12M for lighting). Text prompts for the video model are generated by Gemini to describe plausible scene motions, with negative prompts preventing camera movement. The harmonization model is a latent multiview diffusion network finetuned from CAT3D, where VAE-encoded inconsistent image latents are concatenated to the target raymaps and noisy latents as additional conditioning channels, alongside a binary mask identifying the reference image. Training uses the standard diffusion denoising loss with the reference latent and inconsistent latents as fixed conditioning, and the model is evaluated by sampling consistent outputs, densifying them with a 5-frame-conditional CAT3D, and training a Zip-NeRF for final 3D-consistent rendering.

## Results

On the DyCheck dynamics dataset, SimVS achieves 16.73 PSNR, 0.463 SSIM, and 0.413 LPIPS, outperforming CAT3D (all images) by +1.14 PSNR and CAT3D (single image) by +2.12 PSNR. On the custom lighting dataset, SimVS reaches 20.98 PSNR, 0.707 SSIM, and 0.357 LPIPS, surpassing CAT3D (all images) by +2.72 PSNR and WildGaussians by +6.18 PSNR. The method also demonstrates a practical advantage: COLMAP successfully registers test images for all of SimVS's outputs, whereas it fails for at least one baseline in 4 of 28 timesteps on DyCheck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimVS outperforms all baselines on both evaluation tasks. On DyCheck (scene dynamics), it beats CAT3D (all images) by +1.14 PSNR / +0.015 SSIM / −0.049 LPIPS and CAT3D (single image) by +2.12 PSNR, with the qualitative gap being even more pronounced—CAT3D's diffusion samples exhibit high variance and state-switching, while SimVS produces coherent 3D-consistent outputs. On the lighting dataset, the margin widens: SimVS exceeds CAT3D (all images) by +2.72 PSNR and WildGaussians by +6.18 PSNR, with baselines often producing degenerate or 'cloudy' geometry that fails to reconcile inconsistent illumination. Notably, CAT3D (all images) performs substantially better than CAT3D (single image) on both tasks, confirming that multi-view conditioning helps, but neither variant can handle the inconsistencies that SimVS's harmonization stage explicitly resolves. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the video-model-based augmentation is critical: heuristic flow-field warping and animated Objaverse both underperform SimVS by 1–2 PSNR on dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CAT3D, a latent multiview diffusion model, extending it with additional conditioning channels to accept inconsistent input images alongside a reference image. It departs from prior robust view synthesis methods (which require dense captures and explicitly model motion flows or per-image appearance embeddings) and from sparse view synthesis methods (which assume consistent inputs) by addressing the previously unaddressed combination of sparse *and* inconsistent captures. The key conceptual departure is repurposing pretrained video diffusion models (Lumiere) not as the synthesis engine itself, but as a data-generation tool to simulate world inconsistencies for training a harmonization network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to handle both sparse and inconsistent multiview captures, a common real-world scenario that prior approaches cannot address. The demonstration that video diffusion models can serve as 'world simulators' for generating paired training data opens a general recipe for tackling tasks where paired data is expensive to collect. Limitations include the requirement for accurate camera poses (difficult for sparse captures) and degradation under large inconsistencies or minimal view overlap. The authors note the approach should scale with improving video model quality and could be applied to other architectures like DUSt3R-style models or camera-controlled video models.
