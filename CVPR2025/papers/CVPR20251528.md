## Problem

INR-based classification significantly underperforms pixel-based methods like CNNs because the parameters of an INR contain inherent permutation and scale symmetries that make them difficult for downstream classifiers to interpret. Prior work has focused on designing equivariant classifier architectures to handle these symmetries, yet performance still lags. This paper asks whether structuring the INR parameters themselves—rather than making the classifier equivariant—can close the gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251528/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of arbitrary resolution and produces a class label. The image is consumed by fitting a SIREN network to its continuous spatial coordinates (x,y)→(r,g,b) in only k=4–6 gradient steps using a meta-learned initialization; the classifier then operates on the resulting SIREN weight matrix (specifically, the scaled difference between image-specific weights and the shared base initialization, with a learned positional bias). For high-resolution images, only a random subset of pixels (controlled by subsampling rate s) is used per inner-loop step, reducing computational cost without degrading accuracy.

## Method

MWT (Meta Weight Transformer) jointly meta-learns a shared SIREN initialization θ and a per-parameter learning-rate schedule α (Meta-SGD style) so that after k inner-loop gradient steps on a new image, the resulting weights φ are both a good reconstruction and directly interpretable by a classifier. The key mechanism is back-propagating the classification loss through the inner-loop gradient steps into the outer loop, combining it with the reconstruction gradient via a scalar weight w_cls, so the classifier actively shapes the INR parameter structure. The downstream classifier is a standard 10-block Transformer that treats each hidden-layer neuron of the SIREN as a token with its input weights as the feature vector, requiring no explicit permutation or scale equivariance. A subsampling variant fits the SIREN to a random pixel subset per step, enabling high-resolution and even full ImageNet-1K classification with modest compute.

## Results

On the no-augmentation SIREN classification benchmarks, MWT-L achieves 98.80% on MNIST, 90.43% on Fashion-MNIST, and 59.57% on CIFAR-10, substantially outperforming the previous SOTA (ScaleGMN-B) of 96.59%, 80.78%, and 38.82% respectively. With spatial augmentations on CIFAR-10, MWT-L reaches 64.7%, surpassing the prior best of 63.4% (inr2array). The method is the first to establish SIREN classification baselines on high-resolution datasets, achieving 60.8% on Imagenette and 23.63% on full ImageNet-1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MWT-L decisively outperforms all five compared baselines across every dataset. Against the strongest prior method ScaleGMN-B [15], MWT-L gains +2.21 pp on MNIST (98.80 vs 96.59), +9.65 pp on Fashion-MNIST (90.43 vs 80.78), and a dramatic +20.75 pp on CIFAR-10 (59.57 vs 38.82). The equivariant graph-based methods NFN [45] and NG-GNN [17] trail by 19–31 pp on CIFAR-10, while DWS [27] and Inr2Vec [8] are even further behind (34.45% and 23.69% on MNIST respectively). Notably, even the ablated WT variant (which omits end-to-end classifier gradients) already surpasses ScaleGMN-B on Fashion-MNIST (83.97 vs 80.78) and CIFAR-10 (43.78 vs 38.82), indicating that the meta-learned few-step SIREN fitting itself provides a structural advantage. The full MWT with classifier-in-the-loop gradients adds another 12–21 pp over WT, confirming that the end-to-end meta-learning signal is the primary driver of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in INR classification, which designs downstream classifiers to be equivariant to permutation and scale symmetries in the INR weights (DWS-Net, NFN, Graph Metanetworks, ScaleGMN). Instead, it uses MAML-style meta-learning with Meta-SGD to jointly optimize a shared SIREN initialization and a per-parameter learning-rate schedule, so that the resulting image-specific weights are directly interpretable by a plain, non-equivariant Transformer. The end-to-end backpropagation of the classification loss through the inner-loop fitting steps is the key architectural departure from the standard two-step pipeline (fit INR, then train classifier separately), and it builds on the observation from Papa et al. that shared initialization with few update steps aids classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first SIREN classification baselines on high-resolution datasets (Imagenette at 60.8%, ImageNet-1K at 23.6%), a regime where no prior INR classification method had operated. The conceptual finding that parameter structure imposed via meta-learning can substitute for explicit equivariance in the classifier challenges a core assumption in the field and opens a simpler design space. Open questions include whether initialization and learning-rate schedule alone can impose sufficient structure for more complex tasks, whether the approach generalizes beyond SIREN to other INR families, and how to further improve reconstruction quality at high resolution without sacrificing classification accuracy.
