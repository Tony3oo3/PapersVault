## Problem

Existing real-distribution-based LoRA merging methods (e.g., RegMean) suffer from three overlooked limitations: they assume input features for each individual LoRA equal those of the unified adapter (a rough approximation that degrades with model depth), they require massive unlabeled sample sets for robustness, and they produce unbalanced optimization when output feature magnitudes vary across tasks. These issues collectively limit the quality and practicality of merged multi-task adapters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N task-specific LoRA adapters (each fine-tuned on a distinct task), a small set of unlabeled input samples per task (only 1–5% of what prior methods require, typically 50 samples), and a shared pre-trained backbone. It outputs a single unified model with merged adapters that simultaneously handles all N tasks—producing multi-concept images at 512×512, style-controlled captions, or multi-task NLP predictions depending on the domain—without any labeled data or gradient-based training.

## Method

IterIS formulates LoRA merging as an iterative inference-solving loop: in each iteration, it performs forward inference through the current unified model to extract the true input features for each unified adapter (rather than approximating them with the individual LoRA features), then solves a closed-form least-squares objective that aligns unified adapter outputs with each LoRA's outputs. A Frobenius-norm-scaled identity regularization (α‖X̃X̃ᵀ‖_F I) is added to the Gram matrices, ensuring invertibility with very few samples and preventing overfitting. Adaptive per-task weights λ_i = ‖W_i‖²_F / ‖W_i^T X_i‖²_F normalize the contribution of each LoRA to counteract magnitude imbalances. Because the model has a directed acyclic structure, the algorithm provably converges after at most J−1 iterations for a J-layer encoder (capped at 20 in practice), and all updates are layer-wise closed-form solutions requiring no backpropagation.

## Results

On multi-style caption generation (BLIP), IterIS achieves (83.1%, 78.1%) style accuracy for POS/NEG, surpassing RegMean (62.4%, 69.2%) by 20.7% and 8.9% respectively, while also leading in CIDEr (0.794) and BLEU-4 (0.152). On multi-concept customization (Stable Diffusion v1.5), IterIS outperforms linear merging by 0.78%, 1.61%, and 2.53% on image-alignment₁, image-alignment₂, and text-alignment, and slightly edges out Custom Diffusion on all three metrics. On NLP multi-task integration (FLAN-T5, GLUE), IterIS improves over RegMean by 1.7% on MNLI, 1.4% on COLA, and 0.9% on MRPC, while achieving the best average F1 (2.940) across all in-domain emotion task combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IterIS consistently outperforms all six baselines across the three evaluated domains. Against RegMean [15] (the strongest real-distribution-based baseline), IterIS gains 20.7% POS style accuracy and 8.9% NEG style accuracy on caption generation, and improves MNLI/COLA/MRPC by 1.7%/1.4%/0.9% on GLUE multi-task integration. Against linear merging [46] and hyper-linear [46], IterIS wins on every metric in all three domains, with the largest gap in style accuracy (83.1% vs. 52.2% POS). Against Task Arithmetic [13] and Ties Merging [45], IterIS achieves substantially higher style controllability (83.1%/78.1% vs. 52.2%/55.7% and 46.8%/54.3% respectively) while matching or exceeding their CIDEr scores. Against Custom Diffusion [17] on multi-concept customization, IterIS slightly surpasses it on average across image-alignment₁ (0.6889 vs. 0.6881), image-alignment₂ (0.7124 vs. 0.7091), and text-alignment (0.6800 vs. 0.6509), while Textual Inversion [5] is outperformed by IterIS on all three metrics by 3.2%, 2.4%, and 4.4% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

IterIS extends the real-distribution-based LoRA merging line initiated by RegMean [15] and Mix-of-Show [8], which formulate merging as a closed-form optimization aligning unified-adapter outputs with individual LoRA outputs. The key departure is rejecting the 'rough assumption' that input features for the unified adapter equal those of individual LoRAs; instead, IterIS directly extracts unified-adapter features and refines the objective iteratively through an inference-solving loop. It further departs from prior work by adding a Frobenius-norm-scaled regularization (reducing sample needs to 1–5%) and adaptive per-task weights to counteract magnitude imbalance, neither of which appears in RegMean or Mix-of-Show.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic reduction in required unlabeled samples (1–5% of prior methods) makes real-distribution-based merging practically viable in settings where large unlabeled corpora are unavailable, and the layer-wise closed-form updates keep memory and compute costs low. The method's generality across diffusion models, VLMs, and LLMs suggests a reusable recipe for any LoRA-based multi-task composition. Open questions include whether the iterative refinement generalizes to other PEFT paradigms (adapters, prompt tuning), whether the J−1 convergence bound holds for architectures without clean layer-wise DAG structure (e.g., transformers with cross-attention or MoE), and whether the adaptive weighting scheme remains stable as the number of merged tasks grows beyond the 2–4 tested here.
