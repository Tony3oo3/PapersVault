## Problem

Existing fairness-improvement methods that leverage generative models face two critical limitations: synthetic data often suffers from low diversity and quality due to linguistic ambiguity in prompts, and blind fine-tuning on synthetic data introduces a domain shift that degrades model utility. Moreover, many approaches still require demographic group annotations that are costly or unavailable in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251055/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained ResNet-18 classifier (trained on biased real facial data without group labels), balanced synthetic facial images generated by Stable Diffusion v2.1 conditioned on GPT-4 contextual prompts, and a binary parameter selection mask derived from gradient differences across three reference datasets. It outputs a fair facial attribute classifier that predicts binary attributes (e.g., Smiling, Young, Gender) while minimizing equalized odds and maximizing worst-group accuracy across demographic subgroups.

## Method

AIM-Fair comprises three components. First, Contextual Synthetic Data Generation (CSDG) uses GPT-4 to produce rich, multi-attribute prompts (facial features, hair, accessories, head poses) that condition Stable Diffusion, yielding diverse synthetic images without requiring protected-attribute labels on real data. Second, Selective Mask Generation (SMG) computes parameter-wise gradients on three datasets—biased real, biased synthetic, and unbiased synthetic—then ranks parameters by ascending domain-shift sensitivity (|g_R − g_S1|) and descending fairness sensitivity (|g_S1 − g_S2|), taking the top-k intersection to form a binary mask. Third, Selective Fine-Tuning (SFT) applies this mask element-wise to gradients during optimization on balanced synthetic data, updating only parameters that are fairness-sensitive yet domain-insensitive, thereby correcting bias while preserving the utility learned from real data.

## Results

On CelebA (T=Smiling, P=Male), AIM-Fair achieves 89.02% overall accuracy, 84.20% worst-group accuracy, and 6.07 equalized odds, outperforming the strongest baseline DiGA (88.40% ACC, 81.90% WST, 7.40 EO) on all three metrics. Under T=Smiling/P=Young, AIM-Fair reaches 90.21% ACC, 87.78% WST, and 5.44 EO, again leading all baselines. On the harder T=Young/P=Male setting, AIM-Fair attains 54.89% WST and 28.18 EO (best fairness) but trails DiGA slightly on overall accuracy (78.19% vs. 80.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIM-Fair outperforms all seven baselines on worst-group accuracy and equalized odds across all three CelebA attribute settings. Against the strongest competitor DiGA [59], AIM-Fair improves WST by 2.3 points (84.20 vs. 81.90) and reduces EO by 1.33 points (6.07 vs. 7.40) on T:Smiling/P:Male, and by 9.28 WST points and 4.06 EO points on T:Smiling/P:Young. On T:Young/P:Male, DiGA retains a 1.81-point ACC advantage (80.00 vs. 78.19), but AIM-Fair still leads on WST (54.89 vs. 51.30) and EO (28.18 vs. 33.30). The regularization-based methods (CVaR DRO, EIL) and reweighting-based methods (LfF, JTT, MAPLE) all sacrifice 1–3 points of overall accuracy relative to ERM while only modestly improving fairness (EO reductions of 3–8 points), whereas AIM-Fair simultaneously improves both accuracy and fairness. On UTKFace (T:Female, P:White), AIM-Fair achieves 88.30% ACC, 84.26% WST, and 4.81 EO, outperforming full fine-tuning (87.90% ACC, 83.00% WST, 5.40 EO) and all other strategies in Table 3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) fairness-aware training via regularization (DRO, IRM) and reweighting (JTT, MAPLE), which typically require demographic group labels; (2) generative data augmentation for fairness (DiGA, D'Incà et al.), which edits real images or generates counterfactuals but still needs group annotations or inflates training cost; and (3) selective/surgical fine-tuning (Lee et al., Kaplun et al.), which identifies subsets of layers to update under distribution shift. AIM-Fair departs from prior generative-fairness work by generating images entirely from scratch via a T2I diffusion model conditioned on LLM-produced contextual prompts (eliminating the need for protected-attribute labels on real data), and departs from prior selective fine-tuning by operating at the individual parameter level—using gradient differences across biased/unbiased synthetic and real data to construct a per-parameter mask—rather than at the layer or block level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical pathway to debiasing models when demographic annotations are unavailable or prohibitively expensive, a common real-world constraint. However, the evaluation is confined to facial attribute classification on CelebA and UTKFace with ResNet-18, leaving open whether the gradient-difference parameter selection generalizes to other architectures, tasks (e.g., object detection, medical imaging), or more complex protected attributes. The reliance on GPT-4 for prompt generation and Stable Diffusion for image synthesis also introduces cost and reproducibility concerns. A key open question is whether the identified fairness-sensitive vs. domain-sensitive parameter structure is stable across model scales and training regimes, or whether it is an artifact of the specific ResNet-18 + binary-attribute setup.
