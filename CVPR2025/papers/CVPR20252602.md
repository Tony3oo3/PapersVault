## Problem

Weakly supervised histopathological segmentation using image-level labels produces incomplete CAMs that focus only on the most discriminative regions, while recent text-prompted methods (e.g., TPRO, CLIMS) fail in this domain due to inter-class homogeneity (visually similar tissue types) and intra-class heterogeneity (variable staining and texture within a class). No prior WSS method in histopathology leverages image-based prototypes to provide finer-grained visual guidance for CAM refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252602/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes histopathology image patches (H×W×C) with single-class image-level labels, plus a pre-computed image bank of N classes × K clusters × N_K representative images per cluster (built via K-Means on MedCLIP features). The first stage outputs pseudo-segmentation masks at full resolution (H×W×N) by aggregating hierarchical cosine-similarity scores across three feature levels; these masks then serve as training labels for a second-stage DeepLab-v2 model that produces the final pixel-level segmentation masks.

## Method

PBIP comprises a Classification Network (SegFormer backbone) that computes per-pixel cosine similarity against class-specific prototype features to generate initial pseudo-masks, and an Image Feature Matching Network that refines these masks via contrastive learning. The image bank is constructed by clustering single-class training patches into K subcategories per class and selecting the N_K images nearest each cluster center; MedCLIP encodes these into prototype features projected to each hierarchical level via an MLP. An adaptive thresholding module separates foreground and background regions from the pseudo-mask, and a two-way contrastive loss (L_FGS + L_BGS) pulls foreground features toward their own class prototypes while pushing them away from other-class prototypes, and vice versa for background. The total objective combines classification loss (cosine entropy on global-pooled masks) with the similarity loss, jointly optimizing both components; the resulting refined CAMs are then used to train a fully supervised DeepLab-v2 in the second stage.

## Results

On BCSS-WSSS, PBIP achieves 69.54% mIoU and 82.03% Dice, outperforming the previous best weakly supervised method Proto2Seg (67.86% mIoU) by 1.68%. On LUAD-HistoSeg, PBIP reaches 76.44% mIoU and 86.64% Dice, surpassing Proto2Seg (74.91% mIoU) by 1.53%. On GCSS, PBIP attains 55.10% mIoU and 71.05% Dice, exceeding Proto2Seg (53.23% mIoU) by 1.87%. All p-values from t-tests comparing PBIP to the second-best method on BCSS-WSSS and LUAD-HistoSeg are below 0.05, confirming statistical significance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBIP outperforms the closest weakly supervised competitor Proto2Seg [32] on three of four datasets: +1.68% mIoU on BCSS-WSSS (69.54 vs. 67.86), +1.53% on LUAD-HistoSeg (76.44 vs. 74.91), and +1.87% on GCSS (55.10 vs. 53.23), though it trails by a marginal 0.06% on BCSS (55.28 vs. 55.34). Against CAM-based baselines MLPS [17] and TPRO [57], PBIP leads by 4.37–4.42% mIoU on BCSS-WSSS and 2.30–2.32% on LUAD-HistoSeg, with consistent gains across all four datasets. Text-prompted methods CLIMS [50] and QA-CLIMS [9] underperform substantially, achieving only 38.98% and 22.45% mIoU on BCSS-WSSS respectively, confirming that textual descriptions are poorly suited to histopathological tissue segmentation due to inter-class homogeneity and intra-class heterogeneity. Compared to fully/semi-supervised methods (SSPCL, SAM-Path, SAM2-Path), PBIP is competitive on GCSS (55.10% vs. 51.63–58.78%) and BCSS (55.28% vs. 61.57–63.27%), though it remains below the fully supervised upper bound on BCSS. Early CAM-based methods (HistoSegNet, OEEN, TransWS) are consistently outperformed by 9–47% mIoU across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the recent line of text-prompted weakly supervised segmentation methods (CLIMS, QA-CLIMS, TPRO) that leverage CLIP-style vision-language models to bridge image-level labels and pixel-level predictions. Instead of textual descriptions, PBIP constructs visual prototypes from the training set via K-Means clustering and a MedCLIP image encoder, then uses contrastive matching between input features and class-specific prototypes to refine CAMs. The framework builds on the two-stage WSS paradigm (pseudo-mask generation followed by supervised refinement) established by HistoSegNet, MLPS, and OEEN, and repurposes the prototype-learning idea from few-shot segmentation (PANet, Proto2Seg) into a weakly supervised histopathology setting where the prototypes are automatically derived rather than manually curated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot classification experiment (Table 6) provides concrete evidence that text prompts are nearly useless for histopathology (CLIP text F1 ≈ 0.035) while image prototypes are highly effective (PLIP image F1 ≈ 0.874), offering a clear rationale for the image-prompting approach in this domain. However, the method still requires access to the full training image set to build the prototype bank, and all experiments are limited to four-class H&E-stained patch-level segmentation; generalization to more classes, different staining protocols, or whole-slide images remains untested. The modest 1.5–1.9% mIoU gains over Proto2Seg, while statistically significant, suggest that the prototype-matching mechanism captures a real but bounded signal beyond what strong CAM-based baselines already achieve.
