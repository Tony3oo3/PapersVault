## Problem

Directly applying the standard inversion-then-editing paradigm (pivotal inversion + cross-attention control) from T2I models to T2V models produces severe artifacts like color flickering and content distortion, because T2V models tightly couple spatial and temporal information and have a more complex spatial-temporal layout. Existing video editing work therefore relies on T2I models augmented with optical flow or auxiliary temporal layers, sacrificing the native temporal-coherence generation ability of T2V models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 16-frame video clip at 512×512 resolution and a text prompt describing the desired edit as input, and produces a temporally coherent edited video as output. It operates on the AnimateDiff T2V model in latent space, requiring 8.5 minutes for pivotal tuning and 1 minute for editing on a single A100 GPU. SAM2 segmentation masks are used internally to separate foreground and background regions for decoupled guidance and attention masking.

## Method

VideoDirector introduces three key components to enable precise editing directly with a T2V model. First, multi-frame null-text embeddings (F×l×c) replace the shared single null-text to encode per-frame temporal information during pivotal inversion. Second, Spatial-Temporal Decoupled Guidance (STDG) computes gradients from temporal attention maps (L_T) and self-attention keys (L_K) between the DDIM-inversion and denoising paths, separately for foreground and background via SAM2 masks, and adds these as an explicit guidance term alongside CFG to constrain the denoising trajectory. Third, a two-stage self-attention control (SA-I replaces self-attention maps in the first τ_s steps; SA-II concatenates keys/values from reconstruction and editing paths with a foreground mask to compute mutual attention) combined with a cross-attention control (replacing cross-attention maps for shared words in the first τ_c steps) preserves the complex spatial-temporal layout while injecting editing guidance from the prompt.

## Results

On 75 text-video editing pairs (512×512), VideoDirector achieves the best score across all five metrics: 97.68% Motion Smoothness (VBench), 21.64 Pick Score, 21.37 masked PSNR, 0.270 masked LPIPS, and a user-study rank of 1. The largest margins over the strongest baseline (TokenFlow) are in fidelity: masked PSNR improves from 17.94 to 21.37 (+3.43 dB) and masked LPIPS drops from 0.313 to 0.270. Motion Smoothness leads by ~1 percentage point over TokenFlow (96.69%), and the user-study realism rank is 1 versus 2.89 for the next-best method (RAVE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoDirector outperforms all four T2I-based baselines on every metric. Against TokenFlow (the closest competitor), it gains +0.99 pp in Motion Smoothness, +3.43 dB in masked PSNR, and reduces masked LPIPS by 0.043, while Pick Score is nearly tied (21.64 vs 21.44). Against RAVE, the Pick Score margin is minimal (21.64 vs 21.61) but fidelity and realism gaps are large: masked PSNR improves by 3.88 dB and the user-study rank drops from 2.89 to 1. Against Flatten, VideoDirector leads by +1.60 pp in Motion Smoothness and +6.67 dB in masked PSNR, the largest fidelity gap among all comparisons. Video-P2P is the weakest baseline overall (lowest MS at 94.46% and lowest m.P at 17.66), partly because it requires training a video-customized T2S model, increasing editing time. The consistent wins across both automatic metrics and the human user study indicate that leveraging a native T2V model (AnimateDiff) for editing yields superior temporal coherence and realism compared to T2I-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image editing paradigm of Prompt-to-Prompt (cross-attention control) and Null-text Inversion (step-wise null-text optimization) from T2I to T2V models, identifying that the standard inversion-then-editing pipeline fundamentally breaks down in the video setting due to tightly coupled spatial-temporal information. Unlike prior video editing methods (Video-P2P, RAVE, Flatten, TokenFlow) that patch temporal consistency onto T2I models via optical flow, random shuffling, or inter-frame features, VideoDirector directly leverages a T2V model (AnimateDiff) and introduces spatial-temporal decoupled guidance (STDG) plus multi-frame null-text embeddings to fix the inversion, along with a self-attention control strategy to preserve complex spatial-temporal layout during editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear conceptual diagnosis of why T2I-based video editing is inherently limited (lack of temporal-coherence generative ability) and demonstrates that T2V models can be directly controlled for precise editing, opening a path toward more realistic and temporally coherent video editing. However, practical limitations remain significant: the method is constrained to 16 frames at 512×512 resolution, requires 8.5 minutes of per-video pivotal tuning, and depends on SAM2 for segmentation masks. Open questions include whether the STDG and self-attention control strategies generalize to longer videos, higher resolutions, and newer T2V architectures (e.g., latent diffusion transformers), and whether the per-video tuning overhead can be amortized.
