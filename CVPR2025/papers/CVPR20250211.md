## Problem

Most 360° perception methods project omnidirectional images onto 2D grids (equirectangular, cube mapping, or patches), introducing distortions, imbalanced resolution, or limited receptive fields. Spherical-domain alternatives using mesh convolutions have failed to match grid-based state-of-the-art. The paper asks whether a transformer architecture can operate natively on a spherical mesh and outperform projection-based methods.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 360° RGB image discretized onto an icosphere mesh (rank 7, ~164K vertices for 256×512-equivalent resolution, or rank 8, ~655K vertices for 512×1024-equivalent), where each vertex or face carries an RGB value. It produces a per-vertex dense prediction: either a depth value (up to 10 m on Stanford2D3D, 5 m on Structured3D) or a semantic class label (13 or 40 categories). Evaluation projects all predictions uniformly onto the sphere surface to avoid bias toward extreme vertical angles.

## Method

SphereUFormer is a U-shaped encoder-decoder transformer built on the UFormer skeleton but with all spatial operations replaced by spherical-graph operators. The core module is Spherical Local Self-Attention (SLSA), where each node attends only to its K graph neighbors (controlled by a window coefficient C_win), with a learned relative position bias sampled via bilinear interpolation from a 7×7 grid indexed by normalized angular offsets (Δθ, Δφ). A head-dimension coefficient (C_head) up-scales the per-head dimension as a reverse bottleneck, increasing attention capacity without inflating overall parameter count. Up/downsampling exploits the icosphere's subdivision structure: each rank increase splits every triangle into four, so pooling and interpolation are defined by the parent-child face/vertex relationships. Global positional encoding is applied only to the vertical angle φ (sinusoidal + MLP), preserving horizontal rotation equivariance, while horizontal position enters solely through the relative bias in SLSA.

## Results

On Stanford2D3D depth estimation at 256×512 (sphere rank 7), SphereUFormer achieves MAE 0.165, MRE 0.071, RMSE 0.432, and δ₁ 94.0%, outperforming all six baselines on all but one metric (MRE, where Elite360D leads at 0.069). On Structured3D depth, it reaches MAE 0.142 and δ₁ 96.4%. For semantic segmentation, SphereUFormer attains 88.6% accuracy and 72.2% mIoU on Stanford2D3D, and 95.8% accuracy and 53.0% mIoU on Structured3D, setting new state-of-the-art on every segmentation metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SphereUFormer outperforms all six baselines on nearly every metric. Against the strongest ERP-based competitor, Elite360D, it wins on MAE (0.165 vs 0.169), RMSE (0.432 vs 0.448), δ₁ (94.0 vs 93.5), and all segmentation metrics (72.2 vs 71.4 mIoU on Stanford2D3D; 53.0 vs 52.0 mIoU on Structured3D), while trailing only on Stanford2D3D MRE (0.071 vs 0.069). Against the other ERP transformers (PanoFormer, EGFormer, SFSS), the margins are larger: e.g., 0.165 vs 0.174 MAE over PanoFormer and 72.2 vs 60.6 mIoU on Stanford2D3D segmentation. The spherical baselines HexRUnet and HealSWIN are clearly behind, with HexRUnet achieving only 56.1 mIoU and HealSWIN 63.2 mIoU on Stanford2D3D segmentation, and both lacking Structured3D depth results. At higher resolution (512×1024, rank 8), the gap widens further: SphereUFormer reaches 0.147 MAE and 71.5 mIoU on Stanford2D3D, versus 0.167/55.6 for PanoFormer and 0.181/63.3 for Elite360D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the U-shaped transformer architecture of UFormer (Wang et al., CVPR 2022) to the spherical domain by replacing grid-based attention with a novel Spherical Local Self-Attention mechanism that operates on icosphere graph neighborhoods. It departs from the dominant paradigm in 360° perception, where most methods (PanoFormer, EGFormer, SFSS) project omnidirectional images onto equirectangular grids and apply standard 2D operations, and also from prior spherical approaches (SpherePHD, HealSWIN, HexRUnet) that relied on convolution-like operations on icosphere or healpix meshes. The key conceptual departure is treating the icosphere as a fixed graph structure where attention is restricted to local neighborhoods with learned relative positional biases, rather than using global attention or hand-crafted spherical convolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first transformer architecture to operate entirely in the spherical domain without any projection, and it demonstrates that this avoids the distortion and boundary artifacts that plague ERP-based methods, yielding consistent SOTA improvements on both depth estimation and semantic segmentation. The gap widens notably at higher resolution (512×1024), suggesting the spherical representation's advantage grows with input size. However, the ~30% runtime penalty over PanoFormer due to unoptimized graph-gathering operations limits immediate practical adoption, and the authors identify computational efficiency, extension to generative models, and applicability to other spherical domains (medical imaging, geographic data) as key open directions.
