## Problem

Existing learning-based clothed human modeling methods rely on LBS deformation from minimally-clothed templates (e.g., SMPL), which becomes ill-defined for loose clothing far from the body, producing split and fragmented artifacts on skirts and long dresses. No prior method addresses the fundamental challenge of accurately modeling complex clothing in regions where the canonicalization process breaks down.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252805/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a posed SMPL-X body (vertices in posed and canonical space) and a learned global garment code as input, and produces a complete clothed human point cloud with per-point normals. The body surface is first segmented into three regions via a garment-specific clothing-cut map (computed using SAM on rendered normal maps), which routes each point to the appropriate modeling branch. The final output merges unclothed points (replicated directly), LBS-deformed points, and free-form generated points into a single point cloud.

## Method

FreeCloth is a hybrid point-cloud framework that segments the body surface into unclothed, deformed, and generated regions using a SAM-based clothing-cut map, then applies a dedicated strategy to each. Near-body (deformed) regions use an LBS-based local deformation network: PointNet++ extracts multi-scale local pose features from canonical and posed vertices, which are diffused via barycentric interpolation and fed with spatial-aligned garment codes into a pose decoder that predicts canonical-space displacements, subsequently transformed by LBS. Loose (generated) regions are modeled by a modified SpareNet generator conditioned on a structure-aware pose code—part-wise features extracted by PointNet++ from semantically segmented body parts and fused via max-pooling—along with the shared global garment code, entirely bypassing LBS. A collision loss based on the signed distance to the body prevents garment-body intersection, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with Chamfer distance, normal, regularization, and collision losses.

## Results

On the ReSynth dataset (5 subjects with loose skirts/dresses), FreeCloth achieves the best overall FID of 37.75 and MSE of 2.61 (×10⁻²) on rendered multi-view normal maps, outperforming FITE (39.02 / 2.70), SkiRT (53.32 / 2.72), and POP (57.87 / 2.88). The advantage is most pronounced on the most challenging loose-clothing subjects: on janett-025, FreeCloth reaches FID 27.95 vs. FITE's 35.81, and on christine-027, FID 37.43 vs. FITE's 40.83. In a perceptual study with 50 volunteers, 63.4% prefer FreeCloth over all baselines combined, and GPT-4o selects FreeCloth in 56% of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeCloth outperforms all three baselines (POP, SkiRT, FITE) on the aggregate FID and MSE across all five subjects, with the largest margins on the most challenging loose-clothing cases (e.g., FID 27.95 vs. 35.81 for FITE on janett-025). However, on the single most challenging subject felice-004, FITE achieves a slightly lower FID (38.61 vs. 42.41), suggesting that the free-form generator's advantage is most consistent when the garment is very loose but not extreme. POP and SkiRT are clearly outperformed across all subjects and metrics, with POP exhibiting severe 'split' artifacts on skirts. In the perceptual study, FreeCloth receives 63.4% of human votes versus 29.9% for FITE, 5.9% for SkiRT, and 0.8% for POP; for the two most difficult skirts, over 85% of evaluators prefer FreeCloth. FITE is the closest competitor and performs comparably on tighter skirts, but suffers from an 'open-surface' sealing artifact that FreeCloth avoids by not relying on implicit template deformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LBS-based deformation paradigm (POP, SkiRT, FITE, CloSET) that predicts local canonical-space displacements driven by SMPL skinning weights. Its key conceptual departure is a hybrid framework that segments the body into unclothed, deformed, and generated regions, applying LBS only to near-body clothing while using a structure-aware free-form generator (modified SpareNet with part-based PointNet++ pose encoding) for loose regions. The clothing-cut map, derived via SAM segmentation of rendered normal maps, is a novel mechanism to automatically delineate where each strategy applies, and the shared global garment code ensures consistency between the two modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid LBS + free-form paradigm offers a practical and interpretable solution to the long-standing failure of LBS-based methods on loose garments (skirts, long dresses), eliminating split and pant-like artifacts without requiring subject-specific templates or 2D positional maps. However, the approach is evaluated only on the synthetic ReSynth dataset with five subjects, relies on SAM for segmentation (adding a dependency and potential failure mode), and the free-form generator is conditioned on a discrete garment type code, limiting generalization to unseen clothing styles. The authors note that integrating with 3D Gaussian Splatting for texture rendering is future work, and open questions remain about multi-garment scenarios, real-world data, and whether the part-segmentation boundary can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on a foundation model.
