## Problem

Latent-optimization-based video editing methods (e.g., those using Delta Denoising Score) suffer from two fundamental issues: self-supervised gradients inevitably carry noise that accumulates over iterations, causing blurriness and oversaturation, and the optimization process is uncontrollable, making it impossible to pre-determine the optimal stopping point. Existing approaches update each gradient independently and simply use the final-step latent, without any mechanism to filter noisy directions or identify a stable target representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (8–32 frames) and a target text prompt, compresses the video into a low-dimensional latent via a VAE encoder, and outputs an edited video that preserves the original motion while altering appearance and shape. The editing is performed entirely in latent space using a pretrained ZeroScope T2V diffusion model as the backbone, with no architectural modifications to the model. Evaluation videos are drawn from DAVIS and WebVid, covering object transformation, color modification, shape alteration, background changes, and style transfer.

## Method

The proposed Gradient-Latent Purification (GLP) framework operates in two stages. In gradient purification, recent J=10 gradients are collected and their covariance matrix is decomposed via SVD; the eigenvectors corresponding to the smallest K_grad=10 eigenvalues (minor components) define a local coordinate system along which projection variance is minimized, yielding a concordant update direction. A Gaussian constraint (skewness→0, kurtosis→3) is imposed on the linear combination coefficients to preserve the diffusion process's statistical properties, and temporal smoothing is achieved by extending each frame's axes with adjacent frames' eigenvectors. In latent purification, the final M=5 latent states are similarly decomposed, and a stable target latent is reconstructed by projecting onto minor-component axes with inverse-eigenvalue weighting, giving greater emphasis to low-variance (consistent) directions. Gradient purification is invoked periodically (10 times over 100 optimization steps), while latent purification is applied once at the end to select the final representation.

## Results

On the DAVIS and WebVid video editing benchmark (40 videos, 8–32 frames), GLP achieves the best semantic consistency across all three metrics: Text-Align 28.67, Frame-Pick 21.46, and Frame-Acc 0.84, outperforming the strongest baseline RAVE (28.35 / 21.28 / 0.74). On temporal coherence, GLP attains Motion-TC of −5.45 (best) and Object-TC of 0.92 (tied with STEM-Inv), while Semantic-TC of 0.93 is marginally below STEM-Inv's 0.94. In the user study (20 participants, 1–5 scale), GLP leads on all three criteria: motion/scene preservation 3.96, temporal coherence 3.88, and text alignment 3.61, substantially ahead of the next-best method (STEM-Inv at 3.07 / 3.31 / 2.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLP outperforms all five external baselines on every semantic-consistency metric. Against the closest forward-based competitor RAVE, GLP gains +0.32 in Text-Align and a substantial +0.10 in Frame-Acc (0.84 vs 0.74), while also improving Motion-TC from −41.53 to −5.45, indicating far better motion preservation. Compared to the latent-optimization baseline STDF (same ZeroScope backbone), GLP improves Text-Align by +2.49 and Frame-Acc by +0.10, and wins the user study on all three axes (e.g., text alignment 3.61 vs 2.63). STEM-Inv is the only method that edges out GLP on one temporal metric (Semantic-TC 0.94 vs 0.93) and is competitive on Motion-TC (−5.88 vs −5.45), yet GLP still leads on Text-Align (+2.45), Frame-Acc (+0.27), and all user-study scores. In the ablation against the raw DDS baseline, adding gradient purification alone lifts Text-Align from 27.09 to 28.05 and user-study TC from 2.89 to 3.35; adding latent purification on top further raises Text-Align to 28.67 and user-study text alignment to 3.22, confirming both modules contribute meaningfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent-optimization-based video editing paradigm (exemplified by DreamMotion and STDF) by introducing a unified purification framework that operates on intermediate optimization states rather than modifying the editing objective itself. Conceptually, it repurposes minor component analysis and SVD-based gradient direction finding—techniques previously applied in domain generalization and meta-learning (PGrad, Fisher)—to the specific problem of denoising self-supervised gradients in diffusion-based video editing. The key departure from prior latent-optimization methods is the dual purification strategy: periodic gradient purification during optimization (addressing noise accumulation) and a one-time latent purification at the end (addressing over/under-optimization), combined with a temporal smoothing axis extension that has no direct analogue in prior video editing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GLP framework is loss-agnostic and could be plugged into any latent-optimization-based editing pipeline, making it a potentially reusable component rather than a monolithic system. However, the evaluation is limited to 8–32 frame videos on a single backbone (ZeroScope), and the paper acknowledges failure cases with fast motion and orientation preservation, suggesting the approach has not yet been stress-tested at scale. Open questions include whether the SVD-based purification generalizes to longer videos or higher-resolution latents where the dimensionality D becomes prohibitive, and whether the Gaussian constraint (skewness/kurtosis regularization) is truly necessary or merely a heuristic that happens to work in the tested regime.
