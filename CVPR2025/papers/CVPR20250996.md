## Problem

Estimating the absolute 6-DOF pose of a single rolling shutter camera from only a single radially distorted scanline is fundamentally challenging because 1D observations of a planar marker are collinear in the undistorted image, leaving one degree of freedom undetermined. Existing scanline-based calibration methods either assume no distortion, require multiple scanlines, or rely on iterative optimization. No closed-form monocular solution existed that exploits radial distortion as a geometric signal to recover the full pose from a single row of 1D edge detections.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1D edge positions of eight coplanar marker lines detected along a single pixel row of a radially distorted image (tested at 3840×2160), together with known marker geometry (two co-planar pencils of lines with five central lines intersecting at a fixed point), Brown-model distortion coefficients (k₁, k₂), and camera intrinsics. The output is the full 6-DOF camera pose (rotation R and translation t) in closed form. A key constraint is that all eight pattern lines must be visible in the scanline, and the observations must be sufficiently separated (≥16–32 pixels) and away from the distortion center to avoid near-collinearity degeneracy.

## Method

The core insight is that radial distortion breaks the collinearity of 1D scanline observations, yielding 2D points on a curve in the undistorted image that encode the missing sixth degree of freedom. The framework first undistorts the 1D observations, then uses Chasles's Theorem to locate the image projection p_o of the marker's line-intersection point: four observations of the central lines plus their known cross-ratio define a conic, and p_o is found as the intersection of two such conics (reduced to a line intersection via the three degenerate conics in the pencil). Three solvers are provided: a 6-point minimal algebraic solver that parameterizes R with a homogeneous quaternion and solves three quadratic equations via E3Q3 (yielding up to 8 solutions), a 7-point unique closed-form geometric solver that uses the law of cosines on triangles formed by the recovered 3D points to determine the translation scale directly, and an 8+ point homography-based solver that linearly estimates the image-to-pattern homography via SVD and then applies the 7-point procedure. The 8+ solver is notably more robust to near-collinear configurations and noise since it bypasses cross-ratio and conic-intersection steps.

## Results

On real data (LI-IMX274MIPI-FMC, 3840×2160), the 8⁺-point solver using a block of 400 rows achieves 2.25° orientation error and 1.30% translation error, while the 7-point solver with interleaved points from two scanlines (400-row separation) yields 5.60° orientation error and 3.46% translation error. In execution time, the proposed 6-point solver runs in 411 μs (median, MATLAB), compared to 177,126 μs for the prior 6-point solver from [5]—a ~430× speedup. The 7-point and 8⁺-point solvers run in 31 μs and 54 μs respectively, versus 17 μs for the 10-point solver of [5].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the binocular rolling-shutter localization framework of Dibene et al. [5] (IEEE TVCG 2022), which uses a pair of undistorted scanlines from two cameras with a 400-row vertical separation. On execution time, the proposed 6-point minimal solver is approximately 430× faster than the prior 6-point solver (411 μs vs. 177,126 μs), while the 7-point and 8⁺-point solvers (31 μs, 54 μs) are in the same order of magnitude as the 10-point solver of [5] (17 μs). On numerical stability, however, the solvers of [5] demonstrate greater accuracy, which the authors attribute to the broader spatial context afforded by the binocular 400-row separation. The paper's own 8⁺-point solver partially closes this gap when applied to a 400-row block (2.25° / 1.30% vs. the 5.60° / 3.46% of the 7-point solver on two interleaved scanlines), suggesting that sufficient distortion-induced non-collinearity is needed to compensate for the monocular single-scanline setup's inherent sensitivity to measurement noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-ratio-based line scan camera calibration literature (Horaud et al., Li et al., Su et al.) and the authors' own prior binocular rolling shutter localization work [5], which required two scanlines from a stereo pair. The key conceptual departure is treating radial distortion not as an error to be corrected but as a geometric feature that breaks the collinearity of undistorted observations, thereby providing the sixth degree of freedom that a single scanline otherwise lacks. The 6-point solver introduces a Chasles's-theorem-based conic intersection strategy (reduced to line intersections via degenerate conics in the pencil) combined with E3Q3 for the rotation, while the 8+ point solver sidesteps cross-ratios entirely via a homography formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first closed-form geometric framework for absolute monocular pose from a single radially distorted scanline, eliminating the need for intra-frame motion models and whole-image undistortion that plague existing rolling shutter pose methods. However, the practical impact is currently limited: all three solvers are highly sensitive to measurement noise, and real-data experiments require ~400 rows of scanlines (not a single row) to achieve acceptable accuracy (~2–5° orientation error). Open questions include whether the noise sensitivity can be mitigated without sacrificing the single-scanline premise, how to handle partial occlusions of the pattern, and whether the framework generalizes to non-planar markers or unknown distortion parameters.
