## Problem

While VLMs have been explored as end-to-end motion planners (with limited success) and as critics in scenarios requiring only high-level visual perception, no systematic benchmark exists for evaluating VLMs as path evaluators in complex continuous path-planning settings that demand fine-grained low-level visual perception (e.g., clearance to obstacles) combined with commonsense scenario reasoning. This paper asks whether SOTA VLMs can perceive precise path properties from rendered images and integrate them with natural-language scenario constraints to select the better of two candidate paths.

## Input / Output

Each task instance presents a VLM with two rendered images (2D top-down or 3D perspective) showing two RRT-generated paths in the same obstacle environment, a natural-language scenario description (one of 15 scenarios specifying which path descriptors to optimize), and optionally verbalized numeric descriptor values. The model must output a binary selection of the better path along with a free-text explanation. The benchmark comprises 14,550 path-pair instances across 1,150 environments with four obstacle arrangement types (rings, waves, mazes, random), with 1,050 pairs held out for testing and 13,500 for fine-tuning experiments.

## Method

PATHEVAL is a controllable benchmark that decomposes path evaluation into three testable levels: attribute abstraction (identifying which descriptors a scenario requires), low-level perception (reading those descriptors from images), and information integration (synthesizing both to decide). Paths are synthesized via RRT-Connect in OMPL, and pairs are selected to maximize normalized Euclidean distance across a 7-dimensional descriptor vector (min/max/avg clearance, path length, smoothness, sharp turns, max angle), ensuring visually distinguishable differences. The paper evaluates 9 SOTA VLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, LLaVA-NeXT-7b/13b, Qwen2-VL-7b, LLaVA-OneVision-7b, LLaMA-3.2-11b, Intern-VL2-8b/40b) zero-shot, then probes the vision bottleneck by (a) providing verbalized descriptors to isolate perception from reasoning, (b) fine-tuning LLaVA-NeXT-7b end-to-end on 13,500 training pairs, and (c) applying linear probes to six vision encoders (CLIP, OpenCLIP, SigLip, DINO, DINOv2) to measure representational separability. The key finding is that vision encoders produce near-identical representations for distinct paths (cosine similarity >0.88), and while end-to-end fine-tuning fails to fix this, task-specific discriminative fine-tuning of the encoder (training it to distinguish identical vs. different path images) substantially improves separability and downstream accuracy.

## Results

On PATHEVAL (1,050 test pairs), GPT-4o is the only model to substantially exceed the 50% random baseline, achieving 66.5% accuracy on 2D images and 62.4% on 3D images in the zero-shot setting. All seven open-source VLMs (LLaVA-NeXT-7b/13b, Qwen2-VL-7b, LLaVA-OneVision-7b, LLaMA-3.2-11b, Intern-VL2-8b/40b) perform at or near chance level (48.0%–51.1%). When verbalized path descriptor values are provided in the prompt, most models improve by 11.1%–27.1% (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7b jumps from 50.2% to 73.1%), revealing that the vision encoder—not the language reasoning component—is the critical bottleneck for path evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates all other VLMs on PATHEVAL, achieving 66.5% (2D) and 62.4% (3D) zero-shot accuracy versus 48.0%–51.1% for every open-source model, a gap of roughly 15–18 percentage points. Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL-7b (50.2% 2D) and LLaVA-NeXT-13b (50.9% 2D) are marginally above chance, while LLaMA-3.2-11b (48.0%) and Intern-VL2-8b (48.9%) actually fall below it. The descriptor ablation is the most revealing comparison: GPT-4o reaches 89.4% with text-only descriptors, and Qwen2-VL-7b reaches 73.7%, yet both drop to ~50% when they must extract those same values from images—confirming the vision encoder as the shared bottleneck across architectures. LLaVA-NeXT-7b is a notable outlier, remaining near chance (51.4%) even with descriptors provided, attributed to severe hallucination in its explanations. Scaling from 7B to 40B (Intern-VL2-8b → 40b) yields only a modest 1.7-point gain in zero-shot 2D accuracy (48.9% → 50.6%), suggesting that model size alone does not resolve the perception deficit. Fine-tuning LLaVA-NeXT-7b end-to-end on 13,500 training pairs fails to close the gap (52% image-only vs. 96% with descriptors), whereas task-specific discriminative fine-tuning of the vision encoder (e.g., DINO-vit-16) improves probing accuracy from 49.5% to 76.3% and reduces inter-path cosine similarity from 0.911 to 0.754.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'evaluation is easier than generation' paradigm (Leike, 2022) and prior work on VLMs as plan critics (Baumli et al., Guan et al., Zhong et al.) by targeting a domain that demands fine-grained spatial perception rather than high-level commonsense. While prior VLM-as-evaluator efforts focused on scenarios with limited perceptual demands, and prior VLM perception studies (Tong et al., Kamath et al.) examined spatial reasoning in isolation, this work is the first to specifically evaluate VLMs as path critics in continuous path planning with complex obstacle environments. The benchmark construction leverages standard RRT-based path synthesis (via OMPL) and draws on the environment taxonomy from Plaku et al., but the evaluation framework—decomposing success into attribute abstraction, low-level perception, and information integration—is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of vision encoders as the critical bottleneck—and the demonstration that end-to-end fine-tuning fails to resolve it—provides a clear diagnostic for why VLMs underperform in planning-adjacent tasks and points toward task-specific discriminative adaptation of vision encoders as a necessary (not optional) step. The controllable, extensible PATHEVAL benchmark (14,550 instances, 15 scenarios, 2D/3D) offers a reusable tool for tracking progress in VLM spatial perception. Open questions include whether the discriminative adaptation strategy generalizes beyond path evaluation to other fine-grained spatial tasks, how performance scales with environment complexity, and whether the counterfactual scenarios (e.g., maximizing path length) reveal deeper reasoning limitations that the current benchmark only partially probes.
