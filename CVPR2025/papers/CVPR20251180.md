## Problem

Single-step diffusion models offer dramatic speed advantages over multi-step counterparts but suffer from significant quality degradation, including blurry outputs, loss of fine details, and global incoherence. Existing adversarial distillation approaches face training instability and diversity collapse when pushed to one-step generation, while traditional score- or flow-matching distillation struggles to compress an entire denoising trajectory into a single transformation without quality loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251180/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and produces a photorealistic or vibrant RGB image in a single forward pass through the student generator in SDXL's latent space. A distinctive feature is bottom-up multi-step refinement: the same trained weights support 1, 2, 3, or 4 denoising steps on demand, letting users trade speed for quality without switching models. Training requires no paired prompt-image data—only synthetic images generated by multi-step teacher models (DMD2 or Hyper-SDXL) with prompts from Pick-a-Pic and LAION.

## Method

NitroFusion distills a multi-step teacher into a one-step student using adversarial loss only, with a Dynamic Adversarial Framework built on three mechanisms. First, a pool of 480 lightweight discriminator heads is attached to a frozen UNet encoder backbone and compartmentalized by noise level (t* ∈ {10, 250, 500, 750}), so each head acts as a specialist for a specific noise regime; a random subset is sampled and trained each iteration. Second, a pool refresh mechanism re-initializes ~1% of heads per iteration to prevent discriminator overfitting while preserving collective knowledge. Third, multi-scale dual-objective training splits heads into global (full-image) and local (patch) discriminators in a 1:2 ratio, each further divided into conditional and unconditional variants to balance prompt alignment with image coherence and suppress Janus artifacts. The student is initialized from pre-trained one-step weights and trained with AdamW on a single A100 GPU, producing two variants (NitroSD-Realism and NitroSD-Vibrant) from different teacher models.

## Results

On COCO-5K, at 1-step inference, NitroSD-Realism achieves CLIP 0.320, FID 25.61, Aesthetic Score 5.56, and ImageReward 0.856, while NitroSD-Vibrant achieves Aesthetic Score 5.92 and ImageReward 0.991. The paper highlights that NitroSD particularly excels on Aesthetic Score and ImageReward: NitroSD-Realism outperforms its teacher DMD2 on both metrics (5.56 vs 5.47 Aesthetic; 0.856 vs 0.825 ImageReward at 1-step), and NitroSD-Vibrant achieves one of the highest ImageReward scores among all 1-step methods (0.991). At 4-step, NitroSD-Vibrant reaches ImageReward 1.034 and Aesthetic 5.85, surpassing DMD2 (0.880, 5.54) and SDXL-Lightning (0.749, 5.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DMD2 (its teacher for NitroSD-Realism), NitroSD-Realism wins on Aesthetic Score (5.56 vs 5.47) and ImageReward (0.856 vs 0.825) at 1-step, and on both metrics at 4-step (5.60 vs 5.54; 0.945 vs 0.880), but trails on FID (25.61 vs 23.91 at 1-step). Against SDXL-Lightning, NitroSD-Realism wins on ImageReward (0.856 vs 0.557 at 1-step) and Aesthetic is comparable (5.56 vs 5.65), while NitroSD-Vibrant dominates on ImageReward (0.991 vs 0.557). Against SDXL-Turbo, NitroSD-Realism matches CLIP (0.320 vs 0.318) and wins on Aesthetic (5.56 vs 5.38) and ImageReward (0.856 vs 0.782) at 1-step. Against Hyper-SDXL, NitroSD-Vibrant is competitive on Aesthetic (5.92 vs 6.00) and ImageReward (0.991 vs 1.169) at 1-step, with Hyper-SDXL retaining a slight edge on both; however, the user study reports NitroSD-Vibrant is preferred over Hyper-SDXL. Overall, NitroSD trades a modest FID increase for substantially better perceptual quality metrics (Aesthetic, ImageReward), which the authors argue better reflect user satisfaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of adversarial diffusion distillation (ADD, SDXL-Lightning, DMD2) and multi-discriminator GAN training (MCL-GAN, StyleGAN-XL/T, GMA). It departs from prior adversarial distillation methods that rely on a single or small set of discriminators by introducing a large dynamic pool of specialized discriminator heads with periodic re-initialization, drawing on the multi-discriminator stability literature that had remained under-explored in the diffusion distillation setting. The bottom-up multi-step refinement (same weights for 1–4 steps) contrasts with the top-down progressive distillation used by Hyper-SDXL and SDXL-Lightning, which require separate models per step count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Single-step text-to-image generation is a practically critical capability for real-time and interactive applications, and the dynamic pool + refresh mechanism offers a principled answer to the well-known discriminator overfitting problem that has limited prior adversarial distillation approaches. The ability to use identical model weights for 1–4 denoising steps provides a deployment flexibility that competing methods lack. However, the FID results (particularly NitroSD-Vibrant at 38.49 vs. DMD2's 23.91 at 1-step) suggest the quality gains are concentrated in perceptual/aesthetic metrics rather than distributional fidelity, and the approach has only been validated on SDXL-based teachers. Open questions include whether the pool size and refresh rate generalize to other backbones or modalities, and whether the aesthetic-metric advantage holds under stricter evaluation protocols.
