## Problem

Standard video codecs optimize PSNR, which is a poor proxy for perceived visual quality, yet existing perceptual optimization approaches in encoders require multiple passes, target only a single quality metric, rely on hand-crafted shallow models, and yield only marginal BD-rate gains. This paper asks whether neural pre- and postprocessing components can be trained jointly to provide consistent, codec-aware perceptual improvements across all major quality scores without breaking standard codec compatibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 1080p YUV 4:2:0 8-bit video frame sequences. At inference, the preprocessor modifies each source frame before it enters the standard codec (SVT-AV1 or VVenC), and the postprocessor enhances the decoded frame; the neural codec model used during training is removed entirely. A single trained wrapper model is applied across the full rate-quality curve (VMAF 40–96, 100 kbps–15 Mbps) without any resolution scaling, and the same model works for both AV1 and VVC.

## Method

The core idea is a 'neural wrapper': a CNN preprocessor P and postprocessor O that sandwich a non-differentiable standard codec C, with a differentiable neural codec model M (scale-space flow + hyperprior architecture) serving as a training-time proxy for C. A novel two-phase pretraining aligns M to C: Phase 1 minimizes MSE between M's output and C's output to match distortion, and Phase 2 maximizes the hyperprior likelihood conditioned on C's output to match rate, followed by an affine calibration of the rate estimate. End-to-end training then jointly optimizes P and O with a weighted perceptual loss (MAE + SSIM + MS-SSIM + DLM), while iteratively re-pretraining M to counter domain shift caused by the evolving preprocessor. A stop-gradient trick (Eq. 8) feeds the actual target codec output into the postprocessor but backpropagates through M, ensuring gradients are aligned with the true codec behavior rather than the proxy.

## Results

The proposed neural wrapper achieves an average BD-rate saving of -18.5% across all objective quality scores (SSIM, MS-SSIM, AVQT, VMAF) when combined with AV1, with per-dataset ALL BD-rates ranging from -19.2% (XIPH) to -21.2% (HEVC-B). Against VVC, the average saving is -17.0% with per-dataset ALL BD-rates from -11.2% (UVG) to -29.4% (HEVC-B). The largest individual metric gain is -41.8% BD-rate on VMAF for HEVC-B with AV1, and -33.3% on VMAF for HEVC-B with VVC. Subjective ITU-T P.910 MOS testing confirms perceptual improvement over both AV1 and VVC baselines across the full rate-quality range (VMAF 40–96, 100 kbps–15 Mbps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPP [11] (deep perceptual preprocessing), the proposed method wins on the ALL BD-rate across all four datasets (e.g., -19.7% vs. -10.0% on Gaming), with particularly large gaps on AVQT (-19.6% vs. -17.6%) and VMAF (-30.0% vs. -11.3%). Against Hu et al. [24] (switchable neural wrappers), the proposed method is decisively superior: [24] actually degrades quality (positive BD-rates) on SSIM, MS-SSIM, and AVQT for most datasets, while the proposed method achieves consistent negative BD-rates across all metrics. Against DCVC-DC [35], the comparison is mixed: DCVC-DC achieves slightly better SSIM/MS-SSIM on some datasets (e.g., -36.1% MS-SSIM on Gaming) but suffers severe VMAF regressions (+21.8% on Gaming, +32.2% on XIPH), resulting in the proposed method winning on the ALL metric for every dataset. Against DCVC-FM [36], the proposed method dominates across all metrics and datasets, with DCVC-FM showing positive (worse-than-baseline) BD-rates on SSIM and MS-SSIM for most datasets while the proposed method maintains consistent gains. The codec-native perceptual modes (AV1+SSIM, VVC+QPA) provide only marginal improvements (ALL BD-rates of -1.4% to -3.2% for AV1), far below the proposed wrapper's -19% to -21% savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'sandwiched compression' line of work (Guleryuz et al., Hu et al., Son et al.) that wraps neural pre- and postprocessors around a standard codec, but departs from prior approaches in three key ways: (1) it uses a learned neural video codec (scale-space flow with hyperprior) as a differentiable proxy for the target codec rather than relying on differentiable JPEG/DCT blocks or hand-crafted rate-distortion models; (2) it introduces a two-phase pretraining strategy that separately aligns distortion (via MSE to target codec output) and rate (via hyperprior maximum likelihood) to the target codec, avoiding the non-trivial Lagrangian parameter selection; and (3) it employs a stop-gradient mechanism combined with iterative codec model refinement to handle the domain shift between natural training data and preprocessor-modified inputs. Unlike prior sandwich methods that operate only in intra mode, limited bitrate ranges, or low-delay presets, this work targets the full rate-quality curve of modern AV1 and VVC encoders with slow presets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a single neural wrapper model delivers ~18.5% average BD-rate savings across all perceptual quality metrics (SSIM, MS-SSIM, VMAF, AVQT) and subjective MOS over both AV1 and VVC, without breaking standards compatibility—streaming systems can simply disable the neural components if unsupported. The ablation revealing that the stop-gradient mechanism is critical (SSIM BD-rate swings from −25.6% to +16.4% without it) provides a useful training insight for any differentiable-proxy-based codec optimization. Open questions include real-time deployment feasibility of the pre/post CNNs at 4K/8K, generalization to content types beyond gaming and natural video, and whether the approach extends to interactive/low-latency encoding scenarios where the iterative codec model updates during training have no inference-time counterpart.
