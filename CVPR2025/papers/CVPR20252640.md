## Problem

Test-time prompt tuning (TPT) for vision-language models improves classification accuracy but produces severely miscalibrated, overconfident predictions. The prior calibration method C-TPT addresses this via L2-based text feature dispersion, but the paper shows this approach underutilizes the feature space and fails to enforce adequate angular separation between class embeddings, particularly on challenging fine-grained datasets where text features remain clustered.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252640/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and a set of learnable text prompt embeddings (one per class, initialized from hand-crafted templates such as 'a photo of a {class}'). A single-step AdamW update (lr=0.005) refines the prompts using the TPT entropy-minimization loss plus the orthogonality regularizer, after which the image is encoded and matched against the updated text features via cosine similarity to yield a class label and a calibrated confidence score. No labeled data or validation set is required at test time.

## Method

The key insight is that calibration quality correlates strongly with the angular separation (cosine similarity) between text feature vectors, not merely their L2 distance from a centroid. O-TPT enforces this by adding an orthogonality regularization term λ‖EEᵀ − I_C‖₂² to the standard TPT loss, where E is the C×D text feature matrix and I_C is the identity, driving pairwise cosine similarities toward zero so that class embeddings point in distinct directions on the unit hypersphere. A Householder transformation is applied to EEᵀ to further enhance the single-step optimization. The hyperparameter λ is fixed at 18 for in-distribution datasets and 2 for distribution-shift benchmarks. This angular constraint consistently yields lower ECE than C-TPT's dispersion objective across 11 datasets, two CLIP backbones, and multiple prompt-tuning baselines (TPT, CoOp, MAPLE), while also surpassing zero-shot CLIP calibration on fine-grained tasks.

## Results

On the CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbone across 11 fine-grained and general datasets, O-TPT achieves an average ECE of 4.23, outperforming C-TPT (5.13), the best Robust-adapt variant (4.66), and even zero-shot CLIP (4.43), while maintaining competitive accuracy (64.41% vs. 64.57% for C-TPT). On the CLIP-RN50 backbone, O-TPT reduces average ECE to 5.45 versus 6.19 for C-TPT and 11.7 for TPT. Under natural distribution shifts (ImageNet-A/V2/R/S), O-TPT achieves average ECE of 4.88 (ViT-B/16) and 9.69 (RN50), substantially below TPT (12.0 and 16.7) and C-TPT (5.82 and 12.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

O-TPT consistently outperforms all calibration baselines on ECE. Against TPT (the uncalibrated baseline), O-TPT reduces average ECE from 11.6 to 4.23 (ViT-B/16) at only a 0.6% accuracy cost. Against C-TPT, which uses L2-based text feature dispersion, O-TPT improves average ECE from 5.13 to 4.23, with the gap widening on challenging fine-grained datasets (e.g., DTD: 7.88 vs. 11.9; Food: 1.46 vs. 3.43). O-TPT also beats all three Robust-adapt variants (best: 4.66 for Penalty-CTPT) and notably surpasses zero-shot CLIP's calibration (4.43), a feat no other test-time method achieves. When combined with supervised prompt baselines, O-TPT reduces ECE from 10.04 to 7.91 (CoOp) and from 6.94 to 4.11 (MAPLE), confirming its plug-and-play nature. On distribution-shift datasets, O-TPT achieves 4.88 average ECE (ViT-B/16) versus 5.82 for C-TPT and 12.0 for TPT, though it trails zero-shot CLIP (5.04) on this specific setting, suggesting the orthogonality constraint is most beneficial when the prompt has been perturbed by TPT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT) [34], which refines CLIP prompts at inference via entropy minimization, and on C-TPT [40], which introduced an Average Text Feature Dispersion (ATFD) loss to counteract the overconfidence that TPT introduces. The key departure is a geometric re-framing: rather than maximizing L2 distance from the centroid (as C-TPT does), O-TPT enforces orthogonality on the text-feature matrix via an EE^T ≈ I regularizer, arguing that angular separation on the unit hypersphere is the operative quantity for calibration. The method also subsumes logit-normalization approaches (Robust-adapt [26]) and is shown to compose with supervised prompt baselines (CoOp, MAPLE), positioning it as a drop-in calibration layer rather than a standalone pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibration of test-time prompt tuning in VLMs was essentially unaddressed before C-TPT, and this paper provides the first evidence that the choice of geometric objective (angular vs. L2) materially changes calibration quality, especially on fine-grained and challenging classes. The practical stakes are real: overconfident VLM predictions are a known risk in healthcare and autonomous-driving deployments. Open questions include whether the orthogonality constraint generalizes to multi-step or iterative prompt updates, to larger VLMs (e.g., Flamingo, LLaVA), and to non-CLIP architectures where text features are not naturally normalized to the unit sphere; the current evaluation is confined to single-step AdamW updates on CLIP-RN50 and ViT-B/16.
