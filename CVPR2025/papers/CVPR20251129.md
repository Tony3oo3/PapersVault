## Problem

HOI detection suffers from high ambiguity and indeterminacy: the same interaction can appear vastly different across human-object pairs, and different interactions can look visually similar, compounded by occlusions and cluttered backgrounds. This paper exploits the observation that per-pair HOI detection outputs can be recast as images, enabling the use of diffusion models' step-wise denoising to progressively resolve this indeterminacy into accurate predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, processes it with a pre-trained DDETR detector to obtain human/object bounding boxes and prior object category distributions, then forms human-object pairs. For each pair, the diffusion model generates an HOI image of shape H×W×2 (e.g., 80×117×2 on HICO-DET), where each vertical slice is a joint probability distribution over object category and interaction presence/absence. Final HOI triplets are extracted by argmax over object categories and thresholding interaction presence probabilities.

## Method

HOI-IDiff reformulates HOI detection as generating a 2-channel HOI image (the outer product of an object probability vector v^obj of size H and an interaction probability matrix m^int of size W×2) via a customized diffusion process. The forward process injects Multinomial-distributed noise (rather than Gaussian) with linear coefficients (1−β_k, β_k) so that every intermediate step remains a valid probability distribution, and diffuses toward a detector-initialized noisy HOI image rather than pure random noise. The reverse process is conditioned on appearance features from the detector and step embeddings. The diffusion model uses a slice patchification architecture built on DiT: instead of local spatial patches, the HOI image is split into H horizontal and W vertical slice patches, converted to tokens via MLPs, and processed by Multi-Head Self-Attention to capture within-slice correlations that dominate in HOI images.

## Results

On HICO-DET (Default setting), HOI-IDiff achieves 47.71 mAP (Full), 48.36 mAP (Rare), and 47.52 mAP (Non-rare), outperforming the previous best (Wu et al. [55] at 46.01/46.74/45.80) by 1.7/1.6/1.7 points. Under the Known objects setting, it reaches 50.56/51.95/50.14 mAP. On V-COCO, HOI-IDiff attains 73.4 AP_S1_role and 76.1 AP_S2_role, surpassing the prior best (Luo et al. [39] at 71.1/75.6) by 2.3 and 0.5 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOI-IDiff achieves state-of-the-art results across all evaluation settings on both benchmarks, outperforming all 13 compared baselines. On HICO-DET (Default), it leads the closest competitor Wu et al. [55] by 1.7 mAP (Full) and 1.6 mAP (Rare), and surpasses Luo et al. [39] by 2.7 mAP (Full). On HICO-DET (Known objects), the margin over Wu et al. [55] narrows to 1.1 mAP (Full) but remains consistent across Rare (+1.4) and Non-rare (+1.0). On V-COCO, HOI-IDiff beats Luo et al. [39] by 2.3 AP_S1_role and 0.5 AP_S2_role, and outperforms Yuan et al. [61] (the previous V-COCO leader) by 1.3 and 2.0 points respectively. Notably, the gap is largest on the Rare subset of HICO-DET (+1.6 over Wu et al.), suggesting the diffusion-based generation approach is particularly effective for underrepresented interaction categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work applying diffusion models to structured prediction tasks (building on Foo et al.'s image-diffusion approach for action detection [11]) to the HOI detection domain. Architecturally, it builds on DiT [43] but replaces local patchification with a slice patchification operation tailored to the row/column correlation structure of HOI images. The diffusion process itself departs from standard DDPM [19] by substituting Gaussian noise with multinomial sampling and modifying the noise schedule coefficients to preserve the probability-distribution property of each vertical slice throughout the forward process. Conceptually, the key departure from prior HOI methods is reframing the two interrelated sub-tasks (object classification and interaction prediction) as a joint image generation problem rather than separate classification heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of HOI detection as HOI image generation via a customized multinomial diffusion process offers a principled way to handle the inherent ambiguity and indeterminacy in interaction prediction, with the step-wise denoising naturally decomposing the large gap between noisy priors and confident predictions. The ablation showing a ~5-point drop when using a standard natural-image diffusion process (42.50 vs. 47.71) underscores that the probability-distribution-preserving design is not merely cosmetic but functionally critical. Open questions include whether this 'recast structured outputs as images + domain-specific diffusion' paradigm generalizes to other structured prediction tasks (e.g., scene graph generation, multi-label classification), and whether the reliance on a strong pre-trained detector (DDETR) limits the approach's end-to-end applicability.
