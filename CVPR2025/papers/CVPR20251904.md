## Problem

Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) produce image captions that lack fine-grained details (actions, object attributes, spatial relationships) and suffer from hallucinations, describing non-existent objects or events. These deficiencies persist across both open-source and closed-source models, and existing training-free enhancement methods either rely on object-level information without capturing intra-region interactions or degrade performance on certain metrics. The paper seeks a training-free pipeline that simultaneously improves detail richness and reduces hallucination without retraining the underlying MLLM.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a detailed, long-form natural-language caption as output. The image is internally divided into five patches (four spatial quadrants refined by object-detection proposals and one semantic patch formed from overlapping object bounding boxes), and multiple candidate descriptions are generated per patch at temperature 0.7. The pipeline is model-agnostic: it wraps around any MLLM (open-source like LLaVA-1.5/1.6, Mini-Gemini, or closed-source like GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, GLM-4V-Plus) as a post-hoc enhancement step, with LLaMA 3.1 (8B) handling the semantic filtering and aggregation logic.

## Method

The core contribution is a divide-then-aggregate pipeline inspired by Feature-Integration theory. In the division stage, spatial patches are created by assigning object-detection proposals (from OVDet, confidence > 0.3) to four quadrants via IoU to preserve object integrity, while a semantic patch is formed by combining bounding boxes of objects identified as overlapping between a BLIP-2 high-level description and detected objects. In the aggregation stage, a two-level hierarchical process first merges multiple candidate captions within each patch (intra-patch) and then fuses patch-level descriptions into a global caption (inter-patch), using IoU between adjacent patches to decide when to merge. A semantic filtering strategy is applied at both levels: an LLM classifies candidate sentences into Same (high-certainty consensus), Contradictory (resolved via Blip2Score image-text matching), and Unique (validated by Blip2Score threshold), producing a high-certainty sentence set that the LLM uses to generate the final refined caption, effectively suppressing hallucinations while retaining fine-grained details.

## Results

On DID-Bench with LLaVA-1.5-7B (GT-{LLaVA}), the proposed method achieves CIDEr 5.17, METEOR 17.73, and SPICE 20.38, representing a +4.7 CIDEr improvement over the global baseline (0.47) and +7.11 over the best training-free baseline (Synthesized, 0.96). For closed-source GPT-4o, CIDEr improves from 1.03 to 2.12 (+2.07), and the hallucination metric CHAIRs drops from 11.18 to 8.42. On D2I-Bench, LLaVA-1.5+Ours achieves the highest DINO-score (66.32) and CLIP-score (73.67) among all methods, confirming better visual detail capture for image reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three training-free baselines, the proposed method dominates across all metrics: CIDEr 5.17 vs. IT (0.73), PoCa (0.00), and Synthesized (0.96) on LLaVA-1.5 GT-{LLaVA}. Notably, PoCa actually degrades performance below the global baseline (CIDEr 0.00 vs. 0.47), while IT and Synthesized provide only marginal gains. Compared to fine-tuned models, Ours (LLaVA-1.5+Ours) outperforms ShareCaptioner [17] on GT-{LLaVA} (CIDEr 5.17 vs. 2.30) and ALLaVA [18] on both ground truths (CIDEr 5.17 vs. 2.59 on GT-{LLaVA}; 2.04 vs. 2.16 on GT-{GPT4-V}), though ShareCaptioner edges ahead on GT-{GPT4-V} (2.69 vs. 2.04) due to its fine-tuning on GPT-4V data. For closed-source models, the method consistently improves CIDEr (e.g., GPT-4o: 1.03→2.12; Claude-3.5-Sonnet: 4.04→4.27) and reduces hallucinations (CHAIRs: 11.18→8.42 for GPT-4o), confirming generalizability beyond open-source MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining cognitive psychology's Feature-Integration theory (Treisman & Gelade, 1980) with practical MLLM captioning, proposing a divide-then-aggregate pipeline that departs from prior training-free methods (IT, PoCa, Synthesized) which rely on object-level or phrase-level corrections without capturing intra-region object interactions. It also distinguishes itself from LLaVA-OneVision's AnyRes strategy, which uses uniform spatial patches, by introducing semantic patch division guided by a small VLM and an LLM-based overlap detection. The hierarchical aggregation with a three-category semantic filtering (Same/Contradictory/Unique) is a novel mechanism not present in prior caption-enhancement pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature makes this pipeline immediately applicable to both open-source (LLaVA, Mini-Gemini) and closed-source (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, GLM-4V-Plus) models without retraining, which is practically valuable for practitioners who cannot fine-tune proprietary APIs. The semantic filtering component yields a dramatic hallucination reduction (CHAIRs from 30.2% to 8.42% for GPT-4o), addressing a persistent and costly failure mode in MLLM captioning. However, the 7.04s per-image inference cost (vs. 0.55s for the base model) limits scalability for large-scale caption generation, and the authors explicitly flag reducing this computational overhead as the key open problem for future work.
