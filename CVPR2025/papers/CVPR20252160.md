## Problem

Existing Dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods for urban driving scenes (e.g., StreetGaussian, DrivingGaussian) rely on accurate 3D bounding box annotations and object trackers to decompose static and dynamic elements, which is expensive and limits scalability to in-the-wild data. The paper addresses this by learning 4D space-time representations entirely through self-supervision, eliminating the need for tracked 3D bounding boxes while maintaining high-fidelity dynamic scene reconstruction and rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-camera RGB images (1920×1280 on Waymo, 1242×375 on KITTI), temporally consecutive LiDAR point clouds, and calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics with timestamps. It produces novel-view RGB images, depth maps, and optical flow at arbitrary timestamps and camera poses. Notably, SEA-RAFT optical flow and SegFormer sky masks are used as pseudo-ground-truth only during training and are not required at inference, and no 3D bounding boxes are needed at any stage.

## Method

SplatFlow introduces a Neural Motion Flow Field (NMFF), a set of 8 ReLU-MLP stacks that model 3D motion as a continuous flow field between consecutive timestamps. NMFF is pre-trained on LiDAR point clouds via bidirectional Chamfer Distance to learn ego-motion-compensated 3D motion, then used to (1) generate a dynamic mask that separates static and dynamic LiDAR points for Gaussian initialization, and (2) warp 4D Gaussians (time-varying center and rotation, time-invariant opacity and color) across time via step-by-step propagation to a target timestamp. Static background is represented by 3D Gaussians from merged static points, while dynamic objects use aggregated 4D Gaussians in a mid-timestamp reference frame. An optical flow distillation loss renders 2D flow from the 3D motion field and supervises it against SEA-RAFT pseudo-ground-truth, uplifting 2D foundation-model knowledge into the 4D space-time representation. All components are optimized end-to-end with a combined loss of image (L1+SSIM), depth, optical flow, sky opacity, and regularization terms.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, SplatFlow achieves PSNR 33.64, SSIM 0.951, and LPIPS 0.198 for image reconstruction, and PSNR 28.71, SSIM 0.874, and LPIPS 0.239 for novel view synthesis, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On KITTI, SplatFlow reaches PSNR 33.37 / SSIM 0.943 / LPIPS 0.057 (reconstruction) and PSNR 28.32 / SSIM 0.932 / LPIPS 0.089 (NVS). The closest competitor, PVG, trails by 1.18 dB in PSNR and 0.041 in SSIM on Waymo reconstruction, and by 0.89 dB in PSNR on Waymo NVS. SplatFlow also maintains its lead under reduced training data on KITTI (75%/50%/25% splits), e.g., 26.10 dB PSNR at 25% training data vs. 22.55 dB for PVG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatFlow outperforms all 10 baselines on both Waymo and KITTI across every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) for both image reconstruction and novel view synthesis. The closest competitor is PVG [1], which SplatFlow beats by 1.18 dB PSNR and 0.041 SSIM on Waymo reconstruction, and by 0.89 dB PSNR / 0.025 SSIM on Waymo NVS; on KITTI the gap narrows slightly (0.54 dB PSNR reconstruction, 0.89 dB PSNR NVS) but SplatFlow still leads. Among NeRF-based methods, SUDS [19] and EmerNeRF [25] are the strongest, yet SplatFlow surpasses them by 4.81 dB and 5.53 dB PSNR respectively on Waymo NVS, highlighting the advantage of explicit Gaussian representations over implicit fields. Static-scene methods (3DGS [7], StreetSurf [5]) and scene-graph approaches (NSG [12], MARS [21]) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 5 dB PSNR on NVS. On the KITTI 25% training split, SplatFlow achieves 26.10 dB PSNR vs. 22.55 dB for PVG and 24.53 dB for StreetGS [24], demonstrating superior data efficiency. Notably, SplatFlow achieves these results without requiring tracked 3D bounding boxes, unlike StreetGS [24] and DrivingGaussian, which rely on explicit object-level annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatFlow extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to a 4D temporal representation for dynamic driving scenes, building directly on PVG's self-supervised decomposition strategy for static/sky Gaussians while replacing PVG's periodic vibration mechanism with Neural Motion Flow Fields (NMFF) — a set of MLPs that model continuous 3D motion between consecutive frames. It departs from the dominant paradigm of StreetGaussian, DrivingGaussian, and HUGS, which all require tracked 3D bounding boxes to separate dynamic from static elements, by using LiDAR-based motion flow pre-training and 2D optical flow distillation to achieve decomposition without any object-level annotations. The NMFF concept draws from scene flow literature (EmerNeRF) but is instantiated as an explicit per-frame flow field operating on Gaussian centers rather than an implicit radiance field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on 3D bounding box annotations, SplatFlow opens the door to training on large-scale in-the-wild driving data that lacks object-level labels, which is a significant practical barrier for deploying dynamic scene reconstruction in autonomous driving simulation pipelines. However, the method still requires synchronized LiDAR and camera data, limiting applicability to camera-only settings, and the per-frame NMFF (eight ReLU-MLP stacks) may struggle with very long temporal sequences or highly non-rigid motions (pedestrians, cyclists). Open questions include whether the NMFF can be replaced by a single learned flow field for arbitrary time intervals, whether the 2D foundation model distillation (SEA-RAFT) can be internalized, and how the approach scales to multi-kilometer urban sequences beyond the short clips evaluated here.
