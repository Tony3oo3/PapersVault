## Problem

Existing monocular 3D estimation methods rely on restrictive camera assumptions—pinhole models, known intrinsics, or rectified images—that break down for fisheye, panoramic, or other non-standard lenses. No prior method can recover metric 3D geometry from a single image without any camera information at test time, and traditional depth/disparity representations become mathematically ill-posed beyond 180° FoV.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251137/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image with no camera intrinsics, distortion parameters, or rectification as input. It produces a dense 3D point cloud in Cartesian coordinates, a camera representation encoded as 15 spherical-harmonic coefficients plus 3 domain parameters (pole and horizontal FoV, totaling 19 parameters), a radial distance map, and a confidence map. The internal output is spherical (polar angle, azimuthal angle, Euclidean radius) and converted to Cartesian via a closed-form bijective transform, with supervision applied directly on angular and radial coordinates rather than Cartesian depth.

## Method

UniK3D uses a DINO-pretrained ViT encoder whose class tokens feed an Angular Module (2 Transformer Encoder layers) that predicts 15 spherical-harmonic coefficients and 3 domain parameters; an inverse SH transform reconstructs the dense pencil of rays (θ, φ) on the unit sphere, providing a model-independent camera representation. Dense encoder features are then conditioned on the sine-encoded angular field via a Radial Module (4 parallel Transformer Decoder layers at different resolutions) to produce a log-radius map, which is upsampled to input resolution. Two key design choices prevent FoV contraction: an asymmetric angular loss based on quantile regression (α=0.7 for polar, α=0.5 for azimuthal) that penalizes underestimation of wide angles more heavily, and a curriculum that gradually transitions from ground-truth to predicted camera rays while applying stop-gradient to the camera-to-radial information flow. The fully spherical output space (radial distance instead of perpendicular depth) ensures projected object dimensions vary univocally with radius, resolving the ill-posedness of depth at extreme FoVs and improving numerical stability near the xy-plane.

## Results

On the large-FoV (L.FoV) domain, UniK3D-Large achieves a δ₁^SSI of 91.2% and F_A of 71.6%, surpassing the second-best method (UniDepth) by 22.6 and 41.9 percentage points respectively. In the panoramic (Pano) setting, UniK3D-Large reaches δ₁^SSI of 81.4% and F_A of 80.2%, compared to 33.0% and 2.0% for the next-best method (UniDepth). In the conventional small-FoV (S.FoV) domain, UniK3D-Large maintains top performance with δ₁^SSI of 96.1% and F_A of 68.1%, outperforming UniDepth (94.9%, 59.0%) and all other baselines. Against equirectangular-specialized methods on Stanford-2D3D, UniK3D achieves δ₁ of 96.8% and A.Rel of 8.01, beating the best specialist (BiFuse++: 91.4%, 10.7) without any panoramic-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniK3D-Large outperforms all 11 baselines across every camera domain and metric. The most dramatic gains appear in L.FoV and Pano: on L.FoV, UniK3D's F_A of 71.6% exceeds MASt3R (29.7%) by over 40 points and UniDepth (16.9%) by over 50 points; on Pano, its F_A of 80.2% is roughly 40× higher than UniDepth (2.0%) and MASt3R (3.7%). In S.FoV, UniK3D leads UniDepth by 1.2 points in δ₁^SSI (96.1 vs. 94.9) and by 9.1 points in F_A (68.1 vs. 59.0), while also being the only method that does not require GT camera parameters at test time. Metric3D, Metric3Dv2, and ZoeDepth rely on GT camera intrinsics for their F_A scores, making their 3D reconstruction advantage partly artificial; UniK3D achieves superior F_A without this privilege. Against equirectangular-specialized methods (BiFuse, BiFuse++, UniFuse) on Stanford-2D3D, UniK3D wins by 5.4 points in δ₁ and 2.7 points in A.Rel despite being trained on only 2% Matterport3D sampling rather than being purpose-built for panoramic inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniK3D builds directly on UniDepth's [53] decomposition of camera prediction from depth estimation, but fundamentally departs from the pinhole-camera assumption that constrains all prior metric 3D methods (Metric3D, Metric3Dv2, ZoeDepth, DepthPro). Instead of predicting explicit intrinsics and then encoding rays, it replaces the camera module's output space with a learned superposition of spherical harmonics (15 coefficients + 4 domain parameters), yielding a model-independent pencil-of-rays representation. The fully spherical output space (radial distance rather than perpendicular depth) and the asymmetric quantile-regression angular loss are novel design choices that address the FoV-contraction failure mode observed when training on diverse camera types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first monocular 3D estimation framework that requires no camera model assumption, no rectification, and no test-time calibration, making it directly deployable in settings where fisheye, omnidirectional, or panoramic cameras are standard (autonomous driving, robotics, AR/VR). The SH-based camera representation is compact (19 parameters) yet spans virtually all practical projection geometries, offering a principled alternative to the zoo of parametric distortion models. Limitations include still-modest absolute accuracy on panoramic scenes (δ1 ≈ 81%) compared to pinhole (≈96%), and the method's reliance on 26 diverse training sources for generalization; whether the SH basis can be extended to handle even more exotic or multi-pinhole geometries remains open.
