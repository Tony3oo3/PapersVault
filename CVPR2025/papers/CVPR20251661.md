## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) suffers from severe overfitting when trained on only a few input views, as Gaussians far from the camera receive insufficient gradient feedback due to occlusion and limited visibility. Existing remedies rely on strong external priors—monocular depth, 2D generative models, or optical flow—that introduce error propagation, high computational cost, or training instability. The paper asks whether a prior-free, zero-cost structural modification to the training loop can achieve comparable regularization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251661/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of RGB images (3/6/9 views on LLFF, 12/24 on Mip-NeRF360, 8 on Blender, 2 on Replica) with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, downsampled by a factor of 8 (LLFF, Mip-NeRF360) or 2 (Blender). It outputs a per-scene 3D Gaussian representation (position, anisotropic scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color coefficients per Gaussian) that can be rasterized in real time to synthesize novel-view RGB images at arbitrary camera poses.

## Method

DropGaussian randomly removes a fraction r of Gaussians at each training iteration, analogous to dropout, so that the surviving Gaussians—particularly those far from the camera that would otherwise be occluded—gain higher visibility and receive larger gradients. A compensation factor of 1/(1−r) is applied to the opacity of remaining Gaussians to preserve the total per-pixel color contribution, preventing brightness collapse. Because overfitting intensifies in later training stages, the dropping rate follows a progressive schedule r_t = γ·t/t_total (default γ = 0.2), starting at zero and ramping linearly to the target rate. No additional loss terms, external depth or generative priors, or architectural changes are introduced; training uses the standard L1 + D-SSIM color reconstruction loss with the original 3DGS densification pipeline.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset (3-view), DropGaussian achieves 20.76 PSNR, 0.713 SSIM, and 0.200 LPIPS, outperforming all NeRF-based and 3DGS-based baselines. On Mip-NeRF360 (12-view), it reaches 19.74 PSNR, 0.577 SSIM, and 0.364 LPIPS, surpassing the strongest 3DGS baseline (CoR-GS) by 0.22 dB in PSNR. On the Blender dataset (8-view), it attains 25.42 PSNR, the highest among all compared methods, while on Replica (2-view) it achieves 27.76 PSNR, outperforming feed-forward methods like FreeSplat (26.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DropGaussian outperforms the vanilla 3DGS baseline by 1.54 dB PSNR on LLFF 3-view (20.76 vs. 19.22) and by 1.22 dB on Mip-NeRF360 12-view (19.74 vs. 18.52), confirming that the overfitting problem is severe without regularization. Against the strongest 3DGS-based competitor CoR-GS, DropGaussian wins on PSNR across all three main datasets (e.g., 20.76 vs. 20.45 on LLFF 3-view, 25.42 vs. 24.43 on Blender) but trails slightly on LPIPS in the 3-view LLFF setting (0.200 vs. 0.196) and Blender (0.089 vs. 0.084), indicating marginally lower perceptual quality in those cases. FSGS and DNGaussian are consistently outperformed on all metrics across all datasets. Among NeRF-based methods, DropGaussian surpasses the best (FreeNeRF, 19.63 PSNR) by 1.13 dB on LLFF 3-view while requiring no external priors. On the Replica dataset, DropGaussian (27.76 PSNR) beats all three feed-forward baselines (pixelSplat 26.24, MVSplat 26.16, FreeSplat 26.98) despite being an optimization-based method, though it is slightly behind FreeSplat on LPIPS (0.179 vs. 0.171).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework of Kerbl et al. (2023), applying a minimal structural modification—randomly dropping Gaussians during training—rather than introducing new architectural components. It deliberately departs from the dominant line of sparse-view 3DGS work (FSGS, CoR-GS, DNGaussian, CoherentGS) that relies on external priors such as monocular depth estimation, 2D generative score distillation, or optical flow, positioning itself as a prior-free alternative. The conceptual lineage draws an analogy to dropout in neural network training, repurposing the idea of stochastic inactivation from weight-space to the space of 3D Gaussian primitives, while the progressive schedule is motivated by the empirical observation that overfitting intensifies in later training stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in offering a zero-cost, plug-and-play regularization that matches or slightly exceeds prior-based methods on standard benchmarks without requiring depth estimators, diffusion models, or flow networks—reducing both implementation complexity and error-propagation risk. However, the method remains per-scene optimization with a dataset-sensitive hyperparameter (γ), and the paper itself acknowledges the need for adaptive mechanisms. Open questions include whether the random-dropping principle transfers to dynamic or unbounded scenes, whether it composes with other regularization strategies, and whether the modest PSNR gains (≈0.3 dB over CoR-GS on LLFF 3-view) persist at larger scene scales or with more aggressive view sparsity.
