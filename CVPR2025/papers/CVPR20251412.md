## Problem

Existing physics-based simulation methods built on NeRF or Gaussian Splatting either require synthetic objects with full-view observations or simulate only elastic jittering where objects remain constrained to their contacted surface. No prior system can truly decouple a real-world object from its supporting surface—restoring the hidden, occluded geometry of both the object and the scene—so that the object can detach and move independently under user-specified impulses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes in-the-wild video frames (720p–1K) from a consumer camera, processed through COLMAP for intrinsics/extrinsics, plus a user click to select the target object and a user-specified impulse vector for simulation. It outputs decoupled object Gaussians with restored closed-surface geometry and texture, a patched scene surface filling the object's footprint, and a real-time MLS-MPM simulation running at ~10 FPS on an A40 GPU. Notably, 2D inpainting (LaMa) is used only for texture refinement in masked regions, while all geometric restoration is driven by 3D priors (Poisson fields, TSDF fusion, planar-based depth).

## Method

DecoupledGaussian first compresses Gaussians into planar kernels (PGSR) to obtain unbiased depth maps and reduce floaters, then segments the object via SAM2-guided affinity features. The core restoration uses joint Poisson fields: indicator functions for both object and scene are solved independently via screened Poisson reconstruction on 128³ grids, then mapped to a common canonical space where intersection conflicts are resolved by prioritizing the scene. For the object, proxy points extracted from TSDF fusion of masked unbiased depth maps (rather than raw Gaussian centers) provide accurate surface input to the Poisson solver, and dense interior points are extracted for continuum simulation. A novel Unilateral Negative Cross Entropy (UNCE) loss performs multi-view carving to remove geometry expansion beyond observable viewpoints, while isometric Gaussians initialized from proxy and interior points are fine-tuned with masked rendering losses. The restored Gaussians are then simulated with MLS-MPM, applying Dirichlet boundary conditions on the scene and RANSAC-estimated gravity alignment.

## Results

On the decoupling benchmark, DecoupledGaussian achieves the best scores across all three tasks. For scene restoration, it reaches PSNR 27.32, LPIPS 0.30, FID 32.07, and Chamfer Distance 4.40×10⁻³, outperforming GScream (PSNR 17.82) and VR-GS (PSNR 25.13). For object restoration, it attains PSNR 30.32, LPIPS 0.04, FID 138.75, and CD 0.17×10⁻³, surpassing PhysGaussian (PSNR 24.46) and GIC (PSNR 26.62). In interactive simulation, it achieves the lowest Motion-FID of 31.69 versus 54.79 (PhysGaussian) and 47.47 (GIC), with a user study rating of 4.35/5 for simulation fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecoupledGaussian outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against GScream [83] (scene restoration), it improves PSNR by 9.5 dB (27.32 vs. 17.82) and reduces Chamfer Distance by nearly 10× (4.40 vs. 44.00×10⁻³), attributed to GScream's reliance on a single reference image and 2D inpainting. Against VR-GS [38], it gains 2.2 dB PSNR and 31% lower CD, while VR-GS's inpainting-based geometry leads to pass-through artifacts in simulation. Against PhysGaussian [89] (object restoration and simulation), it achieves 5.9 dB higher PSNR (30.32 vs. 24.46), 39% lower FID (138.75 vs. 227.60), and 42% lower Motion-FID (31.69 vs. 54.79), with PhysGaussian suffering from incomplete opacity assumptions and particle imbalance. Against GIC [6], it leads by 3.7 dB PSNR, 31% lower FID, and 33% lower Motion-FID, while GIC exhibits white-dot artifacts from non-zero internal opacities. In the user study, the method scores 3.48/5 for scene restoration (vs. 1.94 GScream, 2.12 VR-GS), 4.03/5 for object restoration (vs. 1.40 PhysGaussian, 1.60 GIC), and 4.35/5 for interactive simulation fidelity, with the largest gap appearing in simulation where the full pipeline (Ours S + Ours O) nearly triples the score of the best mixed baseline (2.73).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) representation and MLS-MPM continuum simulation frameworks (PhysGaussian, VR-GS, GIC), but departs from them by introducing a decoupling step that prior methods lack: prior GS simulation work either operates on synthetic objects with full-view access or simulates elastic jittering where objects remain constrained to the contact surface. The key methodological departure is the use of joint Poisson fields with conflict resolution to simultaneously restore object and scene geometry, combined with TSDF-fused proxy points (rather than raw Gaussian centers) for object reconstruction and a novel UNCE carving loss for multi-view geometry refinement. The paper also positions itself against 2D inpainting-based scene restoration (GScream, VR-GS), arguing that geometry should be restored via 3D priors while inpainting is reserved for texture only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Decoupling objects from contact surfaces is a prerequisite for realistic Newtonian simulation of in-the-wild scenes, with direct applications in VR, robotics, and autonomous driving where 4D interactive assets are needed. The paper's limitation to single-object, single-contact configurations and its reliance on training-view interpolation for texture (leading to high FID on object restoration) leave open questions about scaling to multi-object scenes with complex contact topologies and high-frequency texture completion. The new decoupling benchmark, while small, fills a gap in evaluation for this specific problem and could be extended as the field matures.
