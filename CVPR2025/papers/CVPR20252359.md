## Problem

Existing 3D affordance segmentation methods are confined to a single-object, single-affordance paradigm where each instruction maps to exactly one fixed affordance region. They cannot actively reason about complex, long-horizon user intentions (e.g., 'reheat food in a bowl using the microwave') that require decomposing a task into a sequence of affordances across multiple objects, a capability essential for next-generation embodied agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252359/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D point cloud of one or more target objects and a free-form natural language instruction as input. It produces a text reasoning response containing one or more special <SEG> tokens, where each token corresponds to a per-point affordance segmentation mask for one step in the inferred action sequence. The benchmark spans 180K instruction–point-cloud pairs over 18,371 point clouds in 23 categories, with instructions generated by GPT-4o via four distinct prompting strategies (text-only, mesh rendering, mesh+HOI images, mesh+scenario description) to ensure diversity and reduce hallucination.

## Method

SeqAfford builds on ShapeLLM (ReCon++ 3D encoder + LLaMA LLM) and introduces <SEG> tokens into the vocabulary so the MLLM can emit a variable-length sequence of segmentation cues within its autoregressive text output. A Multi-Granular Language-Point Integration (MGLP) module then bridges reasoning and dense prediction: it hierarchically up-samples Uni3D point features to multiple granularities via FPS-based propagation, performs cross-attention with each projected <SEG> embedding as query and dense point features as key/value, and fuses the result with sparse point features before a decoder produces the final mask. Training is end-to-end with a combined loss (autoregressive cross-entropy for text, Dice + BCE for masks), using LoRA (rank 8) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of the LLM while freezing the 3D encoder. The key insight is that a pre-aligned 3D MLLM already internalizes world knowledge for sequential reasoning, and the MGLP module injects those reasoning results into dense geometric features at multiple scales, unifying cognition and perception in one framework.

## Results

On the Seen single-affordance setting, SeqAfford achieves 19.5 mIoU and 86.9 AUC, outperforming the strongest baseline LASO (16.3 mIoU, 84.3 AUC) by 3.2 mIoU and 2.6 AUC. On the Unseen generalization setting, SeqAfford reaches 13.8 mIoU and 82.4 AUC, a 1.4 mIoU and 6.3 AUC improvement over LASO. On the Sequential affordance reasoning task, SeqAfford attains 14.6 mIoU and 84.2 AUC, surpassing LASO (14.3 mIoU, 80.7 AUC) and IAGNet (13.5 mIoU, 78.2 AUC). The model also achieves the best SIM (0.594 Seen, 0.518 Unseen, 0.573 Sequential) and lowest MAE (0.098 Seen, 0.128 Unseen, 0.118 Sequential) across all three settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeqAfford outperforms all five baselines across every metric in all three evaluation settings. Against the closest competitor LASO (PointRefer) [16], SeqAfford gains +3.2 mIoU on Seen (19.5 vs. 16.3), +1.4 mIoU on Unseen (13.8 vs. 12.4), and +0.3 mIoU on Sequential (14.6 vs. 14.3), with the largest AUC margin on Unseen (+6.3). IAGNet [50], the strongest non-language-guided baseline, trails SeqAfford by 5.3 mIoU on Seen and 1.1 mIoU on Sequential. The visual-grounding baselines ReferTrans [15], ReLA [17], and 3D-SPS [25] are consistently the weakest, with mIoU values 5–10 points below SeqAfford in every setting, highlighting the advantage of integrating 3D MLLM reasoning over purely geometric or 2D-referencing approaches. Notably, on the Unseen setting the gap between SeqAfford and LASO widens in AUC (82.4 vs. 76.1), suggesting the 3D MLLM's internalized world knowledge aids generalization to unseen affordance-object pairings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D affordance segmentation line (3D AffordanceNet, IAGNet, LASO) by replacing separate point-language encoders with a unified 3D MLLM backbone (ShapeLLM) and introducing a <SEG> token mechanism borrowed from 2D reasoning segmentation (LiSA). The key conceptual departure is from LASO, which pairs a pure LLM with a point encoder for single-affordance grounding; SeqAfford instead leverages the internalized world knowledge of a pre-aligned 3D MLLM to decompose complex instructions into sequential affordance chains, and couples this with a multi-granular language-point integration module (drawing on PointNet++/DGCNN up-sampling) to bridge reasoning tokens and dense point features. The dataset construction repurposes 3D AffordanceNet point clouds and PartNet mesh renderings, but generates instructions via GPT-4o prompted with four modality combinations, a pipeline distinct from LASO's category-level text generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 180K-pair benchmark is the first to include sequential and multi-object affordance instructions, filling a gap between single-affordance datasets and the long-horizon tasks that embodied agents actually face. However, the sequential results reveal a modest gap over GPT-decomposed baselines (14.6 vs 14.3 mIoU), suggesting that explicit instruction decomposition may partially substitute for end-to-end sequential reasoning, which raises open questions about whether the 3D MLLM's advantage in this setting is primarily architectural or knowledge-driven. The paper acknowledges that scene-level fine-grained reasoning remains future work, and the reliance on GPT-4o for instruction generation introduces a potential ceiling on instruction diversity and realism that could limit real-world transfer.
