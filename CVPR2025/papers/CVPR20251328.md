## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods for continual learning rely on threshold-based scoring, depend heavily on model output quality, and only provide binary ID/OOD classification without identifying which task an in-distribution sample belongs to. This paper introduces the open-world Task Incremental Learning (TIL) scenario, where incoming samples may be OOD and their task identities are unknown, requiring simultaneous OOD detection and task-id prediction without manual threshold tuning.

## Input / Output

At each detection step, the method consumes a new test image and a matched set of source images randomly drawn from the memory buffer of the current task layer. The TIL model (ResNet-18 or a two-layer FC network) extracts feature maps from both, which serve as inputs to the source-target classifiers rather than raw pixels or softmax outputs. The output is a threshold-free ID/OOD verdict; if ID, the specific task-id is also returned, enabling downstream label prediction or use of OOD samples as new training data.

## Method

H2ST cascades T task-specific two-sample test layers, each containing a binary source-target classifier operating on feature maps. Per layer, the algorithm performs three steps: (1) predict source/target labels on feature maps of the new sample and a memory-buffer sample, (2) online-update the classifier treating source as 0 and target as 1, and (3) apply a calibrated Clopper-Pearson interval test on a sliding window of classifier accuracies to decide ID vs. OOD without any threshold. The hierarchical design enables early-exit: a sample identified as ID at layer j terminates immediately, reducing average detection operations from T to (T+1)/2 under uniform distribution. As layers deepen, each classifier faces fewer OOD tasks (T−j remaining), progressively simplifying the discrimination problem and improving detection accuracy over a flat C2ST baseline.

## Results

H2ST achieves an average F1 of 86.49% and average task-id accuracy (TA) of 88.05% with ER, and 89.12% F1 / 89.10% TA with GEM, across seven datasets. On CIFAR-10 with ER, H2ST reaches 88.89% F1 and 89.59% TA, surpassing the second-best method (MORE) by 25.92% in F1 and 30.79% in TA. On the more complex CoRe50 (10 tasks), H2ST attains 94.11% F1 and 94.06% TA with ER. In the ablation against non-hierarchical C2ST, H2ST improves F1 by 11.37% and TA by 4.20% on average while reducing per-sample processing time from 22.0 ms to 16.7 ms at T=9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H2ST decisively outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and both TIL frameworks (ER and GEM). Among score-based OOD methods (MSP, Energy, ODIN, Maxlogit, Gentropy, FeatureNorm), the best performer is Maxlogit with an average F1 of 56.43% (ER) and 56.10% (GEM), yet H2ST exceeds it by roughly 30 percentage points in F1 and 55+ points in TA. The strongest baseline overall is MORE, an OOD-based continual learning method, which reaches 64.54% F1 / 67.01% TA (ER) and 65.89% / 68.70% (GEM); H2ST surpasses MORE by 21.95% F1 and 21.04% TA with ER, and by 23.23% F1 and 20.40% TA with GEM. Notably, all score-based methods require manually tuned thresholds (assumed known in evaluation), whereas H2ST is threshold-free via the Clopper-Pearson hypothesis test. In the direct ablation against non-hierarchical C2ST (same number of classifiers, no early-exit), H2ST gains 11.37% F1 and 4.20% TA on average while reducing per-sample latency from 22.0 ms to 16.7 ms at T=9, confirming the hierarchical architecture's dual benefit of accuracy and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

H2ST is built directly on the classifier two-sample test (C2ST) framework of Lopez-Paz & Oquab (2016) and the sequential covariate shift detection algorithm of Jang et al. (ICML 2022), which provides the three-step loop of source-target prediction, Clopper-Pearson calibrated detection, and online classifier update. The key departures are: (1) replacing raw-sample inputs with TIL model feature maps to reduce dimensionality and avoid over-reliance on model outputs, and (2) organizing one binary classifier per learned task in a cascaded hierarchy with early-exit, rather than using a single unified classifier or a flat per-task set. This positions H2ST as a statistical-hypothesis-testing alternative to the dominant score-and-threshold paradigm (MSP, Energy, ODIN, etc.) and to CIL-oriented OOD methods like MORE that assume disjoint label spaces and known task boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing open-world TIL—where task-id is unknown, OOD samples can appear at any stage, and tasks may share labels—this paper opens a more realistic deployment scenario than closed-world TIL or open-world CIL. The threshold-free, hypothesis-testing detection mechanism is practically attractive for safety-critical systems where manual threshold tuning is infeasible. However, the method is currently validated only with replay-based TIL (GEM, ER) and on relatively small-scale benchmarks; compatibility with regularization- or architecture-based CL methods, scalability to hundreds of tasks, and the acknowledged forgetting trade-off (better OOD detection → more new-task training → more forgetting) remain open. The large performance gap over traditional OOD baselines is partly attributable to those methods being fundamentally mismatched to the dynamic, multi-task setting, so the practical ceiling relative to future CL-aware OOD methods is uncertain.
