## Problem

Multi-modal medical diagnosis faces a fundamental tension: single-modal large models (e.g., DINOv2, Me-Llama) have strong feature extraction but cannot model cross-modal interactions, while multi-modal small models capture cross-modal relationships but lack representational depth. Naive concatenation of single-modal large model embeddings leads to 'modality domination,' where encoder capability rather than clinical significance determines modality contribution, risking deviation from established medical practice. No existing framework effectively synergizes the complementary strengths of both model scales without requiring extensive paired multi-modal training data.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts up to six heterogeneous medical modalities per patient: chest X-ray images, radiology report text, time-series EHR data (46 clinical variables), CT scans, whole slide pathology images, and structured clinical tabular data (20 variables). Clinical tabular data is converted to text format via prompting before being encoded by the text large model. The output is a classification prediction (mortality, length-of-stay, readmission, or 12-month survival) produced by adaptively weighted logits from both the large-model MoME pathway and the small multi-modal pathway, with the weighting dynamically adjusted during training based on inter-pathway representation similarity.

## Method

AdaCoMed operates two parallel pathways: (1) modality-specific large models (DINOv2, Me-Llama, MOIRAI, Merlin, UNI/CLAM) produce embeddings that are fused through a Mixture-of-Modality-Experts (MoME) module containing C = Σ binomial(M,k) experts, each specializing in a unique subset of modality combinations, and (2) a pretrained task-driven small multi-modal model produces a joint representation. The two pathways are aligned via an InfoNCE-style contrastive loss that pulls large-model fused representations toward the small model's naturally balanced cross-modal features, reinforced by a shared co-training task head initialized from the small model. At inference, an adaptive weighting strategy (four variants: Learnable, Inverse Contrastive, Gaussian Contrastive, Threshold Contrastive) computes per-sample weights from the cosine similarity between projected large and small embeddings, dynamically shifting reliance from the small model's reliable early-training guidance to the large model's increasingly refined representations. The total training objective combines cross-entropy task loss on the weighted logit sum with the contrastive alignment loss, requiring only ~21M trainable parameters versus 300–380M for unified multi-modal encoders.

## Results

On MIMIC-IV-MM, AdaCoMed achieves 0.805 Auroc and 0.663 mF1 on mortality prediction, 0.732 Auroc and 0.668 mF1 on long-stay prediction, and 0.619 Auroc and 0.528 mF1 on readmission prediction, outperforming the strongest baseline (Large-Ensemble) by 3.1% Auroc on mortality and 6.1% Auroc on long-stay. On MMIST ccRCC (12-month survival), AdaCoMed reaches 0.898 Auroc and 0.628 mF1, surpassing Large-Ensemble by 15.4% Auroc and 32.8% mF1. The method also requires only 21.3M trainable parameters and 1.0 GB GPU memory per batch, an order of magnitude less than Meta-Transformer (302.8M params, 19.9 GB) or OneLLM (383.0M params, 55.6 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaCoMed consistently outperforms all three main baseline categories. Against Meta-Transformer (fine-tuned unified multimodal encoder), AdaCoMed gains 8.8% Auroc on mortality (0.805 vs. 0.717) and 18.0% mF1 on long-stay (0.668 vs. 0.605), while Meta-Transformer's higher mAR on mortality (0.641 vs. 0.654) is offset by substantially lower stability metrics. Against OneLLM, the advantage is even more pronounced: AdaCoMed achieves 57.5% higher Auroc on mortality (0.805 vs. 0.511) and 23.4% higher Auroc on long-stay (0.732 vs. 0.505), indicating that general-purpose multimodal LLMs struggle with medical-specific multi-modal fusion. Against MultimodalPred-Small (the strongest small-model baseline), AdaCoMed leads by 16.4% Auroc on mortality (0.805 vs. 0.641) and 4.2% Auroc on long-stay (0.732 vs. 0.690), while MultimodalPred-Small retains a slight edge on readmission Auroc (0.548 vs. 0.619 is actually AdaCoMed winning; the closest margin is readmission mF1 at 0.528 vs. 0.514). On MMIST ccRCC, AdaCoMed's 0.898 Auroc surpasses Large-Ensemble (0.778) by 15.4% and MultimodalPred-Small (0.769) by 16.8%, confirming the advantage holds even in data-scarce settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing existing single-modal medical large models (DINOv2, Me-Llama, Merlin, MOIRAI, UNI/CLAM) alongside a small multi-modal model, rather than training a new unified multi-modal architecture from scratch. Conceptually, it draws from Mixture-of-Experts (applied here to modality combinations via MoME), contrastive representation alignment (reminiscent of knowledge distillation but bidirectional), and adaptive ensemble weighting, but combines these into a novel large-small collaboration paradigm specifically for medical diagnosis. It departs from prior multi-modal medical work (Meta-Transformer, OneLLM, HetMed) that either requires extensive paired multi-modal training data or operates at small model scale, and from prior model-collaboration work (ensemble, co-training, distillation) by introducing a structured contrastive alignment mechanism between heterogeneous model scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: AdaCoMed achieves competitive or superior multi-modal medical diagnosis with only 21.3M trainable parameters and 0.02 min/epoch training cost, compared to 300M+ parameters and 40+ min/epoch for unified multi-modal encoders, making it accessible for resource-constrained clinical settings. The identification of 'modality domination' in naive large-model fusion is a useful diagnostic that warns against simply concatenating pretrained encoder outputs. However, the evidence base is limited to two datasets and four classification tasks, the adaptive weighting strategies appear somewhat ad hoc (four variants with task-specific winners), and the approach has not been validated on generative or retrieval tasks, leaving open whether the collaboration paradigm generalizes beyond discriminative classification.
