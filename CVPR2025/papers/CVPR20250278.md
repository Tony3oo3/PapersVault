## Problem

Temporal action segmentation (TAS) datasets consist of long procedural videos with frame-level annotations, creating substantial storage and processing burdens. No prior dataset condensation method handles the unique challenges of TAS: varying segment lengths requiring full temporal resolution restoration, pre-computed feature inputs rather than raw RGB, and redundant action sequences across videos. This paper is the first to propose a condensation framework specifically for TAS datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed I3D frame features (2048-dim per frame), per-frame action labels, and segment boundary annotations from standard TAS splits. It produces a compact representation per segment consisting of K optimized latent codes (256-dim each), the action class label, and the segment length; at the video level, a pruned set of diverse action sequences is retained. During downstream TAS training, these latent codes are decoded back to full-resolution frame features (matching the original temporal length) via the frozen TCA decoder, so the segmentation backbone receives inputs at the same resolution as the original dataset.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. First, a Temporally Coherent Action (TCA) conditional VAE is trained on the full dataset, where the encoder takes frame features, action labels, and a coherence variable (relative position within a segment) to produce a 256-dim latent code, and the decoder reconstructs frame features conditioned on the same inputs. Second, network inversion optimizes K latent codes per segment (evenly split into K instances) by minimizing L2 reconstruction error against the original frame features while keeping the decoder frozen, yielding segment-specific codes that faithfully approximate the source data. Orthogonally, a diversity-based sequence sampling strategy uses normalized edit distance and furthest-point sampling to retain only half the videos, eliminating redundant action orderings. The condensation factor per segment is ℓ·D/(K·d), and the overall storage reduction on Breakfast exceeds 500× while retaining 83% of full-dataset performance.

## Results

On the Breakfast dataset (28 GB original), the proposed method reduces storage to 44 MB (≈500× compression) while retaining 83% of the original segmentation performance (MSTCN: Acc 55.5 vs. 67.2; Edit 45.6 vs. 60.6). On GTEA (245 MB → 3.6 MB), the method achieves 75.2% Acc and 71.9 Edit versus 79.0/76.3 for the original, and on 50Salads (4.5 GB → 3.9 MB) it reaches 74.4% Acc and 59.5 Edit versus 80.6/63.1. Under the 10-task incremental setup on Breakfast, the method yields a 10.7% average improvement over the state-of-the-art TCA baseline with ASFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all baselines across every dataset and both backbones. Against the 'Mean' and 'Coreset' baselines (which condense only temporally by storing one representative frame per segment), the method achieves substantially higher segmental metrics at roughly half the storage (e.g., on Breakfast with MSTCN: Edit 45.6 vs. 31.8/36.8 at 44 MB vs. 96 MB). Against the 'TCA' baseline (random latent code generation without inversion), the gain is dramatic—on Breakfast the Edit score jumps from 20.7 to 45.6—confirming that network inversion is critical for aligning decoded features with real data. Against the closest competitor 'Encoded' (which stores mean encoder outputs without inversion), the method adds a consistent 5–14% improvement in F1 scores (e.g., 50Salads average F1 rises from 44.7% to 58.8%), demonstrating that optimizing latent codes via inversion captures segment-specific dynamics that a single mean code cannot. In the incremental learning setting (Table 5), the method surpasses both the 'Mean' replay baseline and the TCA baseline by 6.8–10.7% in average score, validating its practical utility for continual learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset condensation paradigm—well established for images and recently applied to action recognition videos [37]—to the distinct setting of temporal action segmentation (TAS), where frame-level predictions, variable-length segments, and action-sequence redundancy introduce challenges absent in prior formulations. The generative backbone is the TCA conditional VAE [11], repurposed here not for synthesis but as an invertible compression operator: network inversion on its decoder yields per-segment latent codes that replace raw frame features. The diversity-based sequence pruning borrows furthest-point sampling from point-cloud literature, adapted via normalized edit distance over action label sequences—a criterion specific to procedural video structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 500× storage reduction on Breakfast (28 GB → 44 MB) while retaining 83% of full-dataset performance offers a practical path for deploying TAS in storage-constrained or federated settings, and the 10.7% gain in incremental learning demonstrates that condensed replay data can outperform raw replay. Limitations include reliance on pre-computed I3D features (no end-to-end RGB pipeline), a generative model whose expressiveness caps performance at high K, and evaluation on only three mid-scale benchmarks. Open questions include whether the 50% sequence-sampling ratio generalizes to more diverse or larger-scale procedural domains, and whether the framework extends to other feature extractors or to online/streaming condensation.
