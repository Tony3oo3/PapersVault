## Problem

Existing AIGC image defect detectors (e.g., RAHF) are small specialized models that cannot explain subtle defects or generalize beyond their training domain, while general-purpose MLLMs lack the ability to produce pixel-level implausibility heatmaps and struggle to localize fine-grained defects like distorted fingers or misaligned eyes. No prior method simultaneously provides explainable, pixel-accurate, and generalizable implausibility evaluation for AIGC images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB AIGC image as input and produces three jointly generated outputs: (1) a pixel-level implausibility heatmap constructed by adaptively fusing local patch-level maps (from N resolution-dependent sub-images) with a global thumbnail map via Laplace-uncertainty weighting, (2) a scalar verisimilitude score calibrated from both a dedicated [SCORE] token and the predicted heatmap, and (3) a structured textual explanation following a five-step CoT template (image description, problem region identification, defect analysis, and score rationale). The number of local [MAP] tokens adapts to image resolution and aspect ratio at inference time.

## Method

Built on InternVL-8B, HEIE introduces special [MAP] and [SCORE] tokens into the LLM vocabulary. The Adaptive Hierarchical Implausibility Mapper extracts the final hidden state of each [MAP] token and performs two stacked two-way cross-attention layers with ViT image features (alternating query/value roles) to regress a heatmap; the image is processed both as a global thumbnail and as N adaptive local patches, each yielding its own local heatmap, which are then fused with a global heatmap using an uncertainty-based strategy that models each map as a Laplace distribution and weights them by exp(−σ). The CoT-Driven Explainable Trinity Evaluator structures the LLM's generation into five progressive steps (description → region ID → MAP token → analysis → SCORE token) so that textual reasoning, heatmap prediction, and score regression are mutually reinforcing. The Verisimilitude Scorer regresses the final score by calibrating an FFN output from the [SCORE] token with a conv+FFN score derived from the predicted heatmap. Training uses focal loss for the heatmap (to handle the severe foreground-background imbalance) and standard supervised fine-tuning via DeepSpeed on the newly constructed Expl-AIGI-Eval dataset.

## Results

On the RichHF-18K benchmark, HEIE achieves the best heatmap prediction with MSE(All Data) of 0.00825, KLD of 1.634, CC of 0.574, and AUC-Judd of 0.915, outperforming the strongest baseline RAHF (augmented prompt) on all heatmap metrics. For plausibility scoring, HEIE reaches PLCC 0.697 and SRCC 0.683, marginally surpassing RAHF (0.693/0.681). On the Expl-AIGI-Eval dataset, HEIE's CoT-driven explanations achieve a GPT-4o evaluation score of 4.58 and human evaluation of 4.35, exceeding even GPT-4o (3.83/4.00) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (3.94/4.08). On AbHuman, HEIE attains MSE(All Data) of 0.00510 and AUC-Judd of 0.938, substantially outperforming CLIP (0.00916/0.636) and EVA-CLIP (0.00924/0.607).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEIE outperforms all baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor RAHF (augmented prompt) [26], HEIE improves MSE(All Data) from 0.00920 to 0.00825 (−10.3%), KLD from 1.652 to 1.634, CC from 0.556 to 0.574, and AUC-Judd from 0.913 to 0.915, with PLCC/SRCC gains of 0.004/0.002. Conventional vision encoders (CLIP [31], EVA-CLIP [34]) are substantially weaker, with CLIP achieving only CC 0.251 and AUC-Judd 0.747 versus HEIE's 0.574 and 0.915. PickScore [22] (off-the-shelf) is nearly useless for this task, reporting PLCC of only 0.010. For explanation quality on Expl-AIGI-Eval, HEIE (GPT-4o eval 4.58, human eval 4.35) surpasses all open-source MLLMs (Qwen2-VL [4]: 1.91/1.98; DeepSeek-VL [27]: 1.95/1.88; InternVL2 [9]: 2.70/2.60; GLM-4V [14]: 2.49/2.65) and even the proprietary GPT-4o (3.83/4.00) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet [3] (3.94/4.08), with perplexity of 1.03 versus 1.32–1.92 for open-source models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AIGC image defect-localization task introduced by RichHF/RAHF [26] and HumanRefiner [13] by replacing specialized small models with an MLLM backbone (InternVL-8B) augmented with a dedicated pixel-level mapper. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) introducing a CoT-driven pipeline that jointly produces heatmaps, scores, and textual explanations as mutually reinforcing outputs, and (2) designing an adaptive hierarchical mapper that fuses global and local predictions via Laplace-distribution uncertainty, addressing the fine-grained localization gap that vanilla MLLMs cannot fill. The approach also builds on the broader trend of using MLLMs for visual quality assessment (Q-Bench, Q-Align, A-Bench) but is the first to target pixel-level implausibility heatmaps within this framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified heatmap + score + explanation output format is practically valuable for AIGC pipeline developers who need actionable, interpretable feedback rather than a single scalar. The zero-shot cross-domain generalization results (Tab. 4) suggest MLLM-based evaluators can transfer across defect types without retraining, a significant advantage over specialized models. However, open questions remain: the explanation quality is evaluated partly by GPT-4o as judge (circularity concern), the dataset is an annotation layer on top of RichHF-18K and AbHuman rather than independently collected, and the 5-step CoT pipeline's inference cost and latency are not discussed. Whether the CoT-driven mutual reinforcement between text and heatmap generalizes beyond AIGC defects to broader visual quality tasks is unexplored.
