## Problem

Generative replay has proven effective for class-incremental image classification, but extending it to Class Incremental Object Detection (CIOD) has been limited because prior approaches require costly modifications to diffusion models for precise multi-object spatial control. This paper identifies that catastrophic forgetting in CIOD manifests predominantly in the classification sub-task rather than bounding box localization, suggesting that complex scene generation is unnecessary and that simpler image-level replay can suffice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252361/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector (Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50 backbone) takes RGB images and outputs bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores. The Stable Diffusion 1.5 model generates synthetic replay images from text prompts following the template 'A realistic clear photo of [cls]', producing single-object images. Pseudo-labels are assigned via confidence-thresholded detection (τ for IGR quality filtering, η for SCS cross-sampling IoU matching), and the final training set merges synthetic images from all prior and current tasks with real current-task images.

## Method

RGR employs two key modules. First, Image-Level Generative Replay (IGR) uses a standard Stable Diffusion model (fine-tuned on the target dataset) to generate single-object images for both old and new tasks, retaining only images where the old detector M_{t-1} (for old tasks) or a stage-wise detector M_{s_t} (for new tasks) assigns a confidence score above threshold τ. Second, Similarity-based Cross Sampling (SCS) cross-filters generated old-task images using M_{s_t} and new-task images using M_{t-1}, selecting samples where the two detectors produce high-IoU overlapping boxes (IoU_min > η), thereby isolating confusing instances that are correctly localized but prone to misclassification. During training, the old detector also generates pseudo-labels on current-task real data to address missing annotations, with misclassified boxes filtered out via IoU matching against ground truth. The combined synthetic and real data are trained with a weighted loss where synthetic contributions are scaled by λ to account for their lower regression reliability.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2007, RGR achieves mAP of 75.4 (19-1), 74.0 (15-5), 75.8 (10-10), and 75.3 (5-15) across four single-step incremental settings, outperforming the strongest baseline BPF by 1.3–2.9% in all settings. On MS COCO 2017, RGR reaches 35.6 AP (40+40) and 36.6 AP (70+10), surpassing BPF by 1.2% and 0.4% respectively. In multi-step settings (Table 2), RGR gains 3.1–6.1% mAP over BPF, with the largest improvement (6.1%) in the 5-5 (4 tasks) configuration where forgetting is most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGR consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated setting. The closest competitor is BPF [26], which RGR surpasses by 1.3% (19-1), 1.3% (15-5), 2.9% (10-10), and 2.3% (5-15) mAP on PASCAL VOC 2007, and by 1.2% AP on MS COCO 40+40. Among replay-based methods, ABR [21] is the strongest, but RGR leads it by 4.5% (19-1), 3.0% (15-5), 3.8% (10-10), and 5.9% (5-15) mAP. Knowledge-distillation methods MMA [5] and PseudoRM [42] trail RGR by 4.7–9.6% and 2.8–6.9% respectively across settings. On MS COCO, RGR also beats BPF by 0.4% AP and 0.7% AP75 in the 70+10 setting, and outperforms ABR by 4.5% AP in 40+40. Notably, RGR maintains high performance on both old and new classes simultaneously (e.g., 75.4/76.3 in 10-10), whereas baselines like PROB [49] sacrifice new-class performance (48.5) to preserve old classes (73.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the recent line of generative replay for CIOD (SDDGR, InstanceDiffusion, ODGen) that modifies or constrains diffusion models to produce complex multi-object scenes with precise spatial control. Instead, it revisits a simpler formulation: using an unmodified Stable Diffusion model to generate single-object images, motivated by the empirical observation that catastrophic forgetting in CIOD manifests primarily in classification rather than bounding-box regression. The method builds on the broader generative replay paradigm for continual classification (DDGR, DiffClass) and the pseudo-labeling strategies in CIOD (BPF, MMA), but introduces a Similarity-based Cross Sampling mechanism that has no direct analogue in prior CIOD or generative replay literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is significant: practitioners can leverage an off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion model without domain-specific fine-tuning, geometric conditioning, or iterative refinement, substantially reducing the engineering and compute overhead of generative replay for CIOD. However, the approach is limited to two-stage R-CNN detectors and single-object generation, raising open questions about whether it transfers to one-stage detectors or whether the domain gap between synthetic single-object images and real multi-object scenes becomes a bottleneck at scale. The SCS mechanism, while effective, relies on a stage-wise detector trained on current-task data alone, which may not generalize when new and old classes share very little visual overlap.
