## Problem

Existing vision-based MoCap methods optimize 2D image alignment while ignoring the physical interaction between the human body and the ground, causing temporal drift, jitter, sliding, and penetration when driving virtual humans or humanoid robots. The paper asks whether ground pressure signals—which directly encode body-ground contact and force distribution—can supply the missing physical prior to make estimated motion globally plausible in 3D scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252585/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two synchronized streams: a 120×160 whole-body pressure mat signal (downsampled to 30 Hz) and monocular RGB video (30 Hz, four fixed cameras). Because the pressure image is extremely sparse when only feet touch the ground, the network ingests consecutive multi-frame pressure sequences rather than a single frame. Outputs are full SMPL parameters (pose θ∈ℝ⁷², shape β∈ℝ¹⁰, global translation T∈ℝ³), 3D joint positions, and per-joint binary ground-contact labels, all expressed in a world coordinate system aligned with the pressure mat.

## Method

The core contribution is FRAPPE, which fuses a pressure branch (small-kernel ResNet encoder → Long-Short-term Attention Module combining GRU for short-term dynamics and multi-head self-attention for long-term dependencies → VIBE-style pose regressor) with a frozen HRNet RGB branch via a Fusion Cross-Attention Module (FCAM) that designates pressure features as Query and image features as Key/Value, reflecting the belief that pressure carries the dominant physical-world signal. A key architectural choice is replacing the standard weak-perspective camera model with orthographic projection, which decouples pose from global trajectory and prevents the 2D-alignment paradox where improving pixel fit degrades 3D accuracy. Training uses a composite loss combining pose-parameter MSE, pelvis-aligned 3D joint MSE, orthographic 2D projection MSE, global translation MSE, and a contact loss that penalizes height error only on joints annotated as ground-contact, directly enforcing physically plausible body-ground interaction.

## Results

On MotionPRO, FRAPPE achieves 41.8 mm MPJPE and 30.2 mm PMPJPE for global pose estimation, outperforming the best RGB-only baselines (SMPLer-X: 51.6/32.8, CLIFF: 54.7/39.7) by 10+ mm on MPJPE. For global trajectory estimation, FRAPPE reduces Root Translation Error to 41.6 mm, a 24× improvement over WHAM (1023 mm) and 29× over TRACE (1193 mm), while also achieving the lowest Whole Body Contact Error (110.2 mm vs. 1217.6 mm for WHAM). The method also matches the best acceleration smoothness (3.0 m/s²) on par with WHAM and PhysPT, while maintaining superior geometric accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRAPPE wins on MPJPE (41.8 mm) against all six baselines, with the closest competitor being SMPLer-X at 51.6 mm (a 10.8 mm gap). On PMPJPE, WHAM achieves the best score (28.3 mm) narrowly beating FRAPPE (30.2 mm), but WHAM's MPJPE is catastrophically high (160.4 mm) due to unstable global orientation over time, making it unsuitable for driving virtual humans or robots. For global trajectory (Table 4), FRAPPE dominates on RTE (41.6 mm vs. 1023 mm for WHAM and 1193 mm for TRACE) and WBCE (110.2 mm vs. 1217.6 mm and 10272.4 mm), indicating that pressure-derived contact constraints eliminate the vertical drift that plagues vision-only methods. TRACE is the weakest baseline overall, with the highest WMPJPE (141.2 mm), Jitter (68.6), and WBCE (10272.4 mm), as it ignores all body-ground contact relationships. PhysPT achieves comparable acceleration smoothness (3.0 m/s²) but lags on all geometric accuracy metrics. In the humanoid robot evaluation (Table 6), FRAPPE outperforms CLIFF and CLIFF-P on both human pose accuracy (MPJPE-H: 65.11 vs. 83.60/84.65) and robot tracking accuracy (MPJPE-R: 15.98 vs. 19.10/16.77), as well as stability metrics E_com and E_cop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of pressure-based pose estimation, which was previously confined to lying-in-bed scenarios (Clever et al., Liu et al.) or single-sport settings (Yoga in MoYo, Taiji in PSU-TMM100), to a broad range of 400 motion types with whole-body pressure. Methodologically, it builds on VIBE's pose regressor and HRNet's image encoder, but departs from the dominant RGB-only paradigm (CLIFF, SMPLer-X, WHAM, TRACE) by introducing a cross-attention fusion of pressure and visual features and by replacing weak-perspective with orthographic projection to decouple pose from global trajectory. The pressure-only baseline draws on Intelligent Carpet and TIP for multi-frame pressure encoding but adds a long-short-term attention module (GRU + self-attention) to resolve the ill-posedness of sparse ground contact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MotionPRO dataset (12.4M frames, 70 subjects, 400 motion types, whole-body pressure at 120×160 resolution) fills a critical gap: no prior dataset combined diverse full-body motions with whole-body pressure and multi-view RGB, making it a valuable resource for physically-grounded motion research. The demonstration that pressure signals alone yield competitive global trajectory (85.9 mm GTraj) and that fusing pressure with RGB dramatically reduces vertical drift and foot sliding (WBCE: 110 mm vs. 1218 mm for WHAM) provides concrete evidence that physical interaction cues are necessary for downstream applications like humanoid robot actuation. Open questions include generalization beyond the fixed pressure mat area, real-time deployment on embedded hardware, and whether the orthographic-projection choice generalizes to uncalibrated or moving-camera settings.
