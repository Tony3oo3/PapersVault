## Problem

Existing pixel-level surface defect detection methods rely on static convolution for mask prediction, equivalent to a single image-independent query that lacks semantic representation, leading to missed weak defects and false detections on cluttered backgrounds. While query-based transformer decoders address this, they operate solely in the spatial domain and depend on potentially incomplete mask priors to filter redundant background information, which propagates errors into subsequent decoding layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250574/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 384×384 as input and produces a binary pixel-level defect segmentation mask at the same spatial resolution. Internally, 16 learnable queries are iteratively refined through a 3-layer decoder, and the final mask is a sigmoid-weighted fusion of per-query mask predictions decoded from 1/4-resolution FPN features via a 3-layer MLP head.

## Method

WPFormer introduces a Dual-Domain Transformer (D2T) decoder that updates queries in both frequency and spatial domains. In the frequency domain, Wavelet-enhanced Cross-Attention (WCA) applies Haar wavelet decomposition to split features into low- and high-frequency subbands, then uses a multi-scale context module to generate channel weights that modulate high-frequency components for noise suppression before cross-attention updates queries. In the spatial domain, Prototype-guided Cross-Attention (PCA) employs a Prototype Learning Unit (3×3 conv + 1×1 conv + softmax) to adaptively cluster image features into N prototypes, then captures both global and local query-prototype relationships via MSCM to refine queries without relying on mask priors. The full pipeline uses PVTv2 (ImageNet-pretrained) as backbone, FPN for multi-scale features, a 2-layer transformer for initial query refinement on high-resolution features, followed by 3 D2T decoder layers operating on progressively higher-resolution features, with auxiliary supervision at each layer.

## Results

On ESDIs-SOD, WPFormer achieves M=0.0171, Fβw=0.8901, Sα=0.9136, mFβ=0.8865, and mEξ=0.9656. On CrackSeg9k, it reaches M=0.0135, Fβw=0.7672, Sα=0.8493, mFβ=0.7679, and mEξ=0.9481. On ZJU-Leaper, the method obtains M=0.0175, Fβw=0.7972, Sα=0.8404, mFβ=0.8146, and mEξ=0.9356. All five metrics achieve the best values among 17 compared methods across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WPFormer outperforms all 17 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest SDD-specific method A3Net, it improves M by 13.85%, Fβw by 5.09%, and Sα by 2.56% on average. Among methods sharing the PVTv2 backbone, it surpasses IdeNet (M=0.0184→0.0171, Fβw=0.8822→0.8901 on ESDIs-SOD) and ZoomNeXt (M=0.0195→0.0171) by clear margins. Compared to the closest query-based competitors Mask2Former and PEM (both PVTv2-based), WPFormer gains 12.14% on M and 2.88% on Fβw over Mask2Former, and 13.64% on M and 2.54% on Fβw over PEM, averaged across datasets. The largest absolute gap appears on CrackSeg9k, where WPFormer's Fβw of 0.7672 exceeds the next-best IdeNet (0.7510) by 1.62 percentage points, and its M of 0.0135 is the lowest among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mask2Former query-based transformer decoder architecture, adopting its segmentation head, training paradigm, and cross-attention/self-attention ordering, but departs from the spatial-domain-only interaction by introducing a dual-domain transformer decoder. The Wavelet-enhanced Cross-Attention (WCA) draws on frequency-domain learning literature (Haar wavelet decomposition, multi-scale channel attention) to modulate high-frequency components before query interaction, while the Prototype-guided Cross-Attention (PCA) extends the prototype mechanism from PEM by replacing masked cross-attention with adaptive clustering and adding global relationship capture alongside local. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both FCN-based methods (which use a single static convolutional query) and prior query-based methods that rely on mask priors for attention masking, arguing that neither adequately handles weak defects and cluttered backgrounds simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-domain (frequency + spatial) query refinement strategy offers a principled way to address two distinct failure modes in industrial defect detection—missing fine-grained defect edges and false positives from background clutter—without requiring strong mask priors that can propagate errors. However, the approach is tied to the Haar wavelet (fixed filters) and the PVTv2 backbone, leaving open whether other wavelet bases or backbone architectures yield similar gains, and whether the dual-domain decoder generalizes beyond the three tested datasets to other industrial inspection scenarios. The modest per-component ablation gains (WCA: ~0.69% Fβw over additive fusion; PCA: ~0.3% over single-scale) suggest the architecture's value lies in the combination rather than any single module, which may limit its transferability to other segmentation tasks.
