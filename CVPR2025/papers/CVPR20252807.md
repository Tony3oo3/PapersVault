## Problem

Current text-to-image models produce visually appealing but scientifically inaccurate images because they lack understanding of underlying physical, chemical, and biological principles. Existing evaluation metrics (FID, CLIPScore) and even large multimodal models like GPT-4o fail to detect these scientific illusions, leaving no reliable way to measure or correct the gap between generated content and real-world scientific reality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252807/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For the reward model (SciScore), input is a text prompt (implicit, explicit, or superficial variant) paired with an RGB image, producing a scalar reward score via cosine similarity in a shared embedding space. For the fine-tuned generator, input is an implicit text prompt requiring scientific inference (e.g., 'an unripe apple' implying greenness), and output is a synthesized image. The dataset structures each example as a 5-tuple (implicit prompt, explicit prompt, superficial prompt, explicit image, superficial image), enabling preference-based training on adversarial image pairs.

## Method

SciScore extends CLIP-H with two complementary training objectives: Implicit Prompt Alignment (IPA), which minimizes KL divergence to force the implicit prompt embedding toward the explicit image over the superficial image, and Image Encoder Enhancement (IEE), which applies separate preference losses on explicit and superficial images to sharpen fine-grained visual discrimination. For T2I fine-tuning, the paper uses a two-stage pipeline on FLUX.1[dev]: first, supervised fine-tuning with LoRA on Science-T2I to establish scientific understanding within the model's distribution; second, online fine-tuning via DPO where SciScore provides the reward signal and a subject-based masking strategy (using GroundingDINO bounding boxes) restricts gradient backpropagation to only the scientifically relevant spatial regions, preventing the model from indiscriminately treating all image features as preferred.

## Results

On the two-choice scientific reasoning task, SciScore achieves 93.14% average accuracy on Science-T2I S and 91.19% on Science-T2I C, surpassing human expert evaluation (87.01% and 86.02% respectively) by approximately 5–6 points. For the T2I fine-tuning experiment, applying SFT+OFT to FLUX.1[dev] raises the SciScore from 23.56 to 31.18 on Science-T2I S (82.02% relative improvement toward the explicit-prompt upper bound) and from 27.26 to 32.15 on Science-T2I C (65.73% RI). The ablation confirms that SFT is necessary before OFT, and the subject-based masking strategy stabilizes training and prevents image collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SciScore decisively outperforms all reward-model baselines: CLIP-H (54.69% avg on S) and BLIPScore (55.00% avg on S) perform near random, while GPT-4o mini with chain-of-thought reaches 70.83% (S) and 74.78% (C), still trailing SciScore by 22.3 and 16.4 points respectively. SciScore also exceeds the 10-expert human evaluation by 6.1 points on S and 5.2 points on C, indicating that the fine-tuned CLIP-based model captures fine-grained scientific visual details that even domain experts miss. Among T2I generation baselines in Table 3, FLUX.1[dev] (IP score 23.56 on S) outperforms Stable Diffusion v1.5 (22.37) and Stable Diffusion 3 (22.31) on implicit-prompt alignment, but SDXL (25.47) and FLUX.1[schnell] (24.43) score higher, suggesting that no current model reliably infers implicit scientific meaning. After SFT+OFT fine-tuning, the modified FLUX.1[dev] achieves 31.18 (S) and 32.15 (C), approaching the explicit-prompt upper bound of 32.85 and 34.70, confirming that the two-stage pipeline effectively transfers scientific reasoning into the generator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP contrastive architecture into a scientific-knowledge-aware reward model (SciScore), departing from prior work like PhyBench that relies on VLMs with discrete scoring and complex prompt engineering. For the generative side, it builds on the DPO/RL-for-diffusion line (DDPO, Diffusion-DPO) but introduces a subject-based masking strategy via GroundingDINO to constrain gradient flow to scientifically relevant regions—a departure from prior approaches that treat the full image uniformly. The two-stage SFT-then-OFT pipeline addresses a gap identified in prior post-training methods (PPO, DPO) that assume the optimization objective stays within the pre-trained distribution, which fails when the target (scientific reasoning) is absent from the base model's training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first end-to-end, continuous reward model specifically for scientific accuracy in T2I, and demonstrates that a fine-tuned CLIP-H can surpass both GPT-4o-mini with CoT and human experts on this narrow task, suggesting specialized small models can outperform general-purpose LMMs on domain-specific evaluation. The 50%+ SciScore improvement on FLUX after two-stage fine-tuning is practically significant for anyone deploying T2I models in science-communication or education contexts. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 16 curated phenomena, whether it transfers to video generation (where physical consistency is even harder), and whether the subject-based masking strategy scales to multi-object or scene-level scientific reasoning.
