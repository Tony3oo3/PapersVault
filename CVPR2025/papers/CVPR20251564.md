## Problem

Existing burst super-resolution methods rely on frame-by-frame fusion or simple concatenation, which cannot effectively distinguish informative subpixels (consistently distributed across frames) from random high-frequency noise (appearing at different locations per frame). This leads to residual artifacts and over-smoothed details in the reconstructed high-resolution output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251564/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a burst sequence of N low-resolution frames (N=14 in experiments), where the first frame serves as the base frame and the remaining frames supply sub-pixel information. Input can be RAW or RGB format. The output is a single high-resolution RGB image at ×4 super-resolution factor. Training uses patches ranging from 48×48 (synthetic) to 80×80 (real-world) with a batch size of 8 on 8 V100 GPUs.

## Method

The core contribution is the Query State Space Model (QSSM), which modifies the standard SSM by letting the base frame generate the control matrix B and discretization step Δ while current frames drive the state transitions, enabling the base frame to simultaneously query all current frames in both inter-frame and intra-frame manners. This exploits the observation that subpixels maintain consistent intensity across frames while noise is randomly distributed, allowing joint sub-pixel extraction and noise suppression in a single pass. A Multi-scale Fusion Module (MSFM) combines a 3×3 CNN for local features, horizontal/vertical SSM for medium-range features, and a channel Transformer for global context. An Adaptive Upsampling (AdaUp) module uses adaptive pooling to perceive the spatial distribution of sub-pixels and element-wise modulates the transposed convolution kernel, replacing static upsampling with content-aware reconstruction.

## Results

On the Synthetic BurstSR dataset (×4), QMambaBSR achieves 43.12 dB PSNR and 0.97 SSIM, surpassing the previous SOTA Burstormer by 0.29 dB in PSNR. On RealBSR-RAW, it reaches 27.558 dB PSNR and 32.791 dB L-PSNR, outperforming Burstormer by 0.268 dB and 0.258 dB respectively. On RealBSR-RGB, it achieves 31.401 dB PSNR and 0.908 SSIM, exceeding Burstormer by 0.204 dB in PSNR. A user study with 20 volunteers on 10 real burst images yields the highest average score of 8.56/10 for QMambaBSR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QMambaBSR outperforms all 11 compared baselines across all four benchmarks. Against the closest competitor Burstormer [12], it gains 0.29 dB PSNR on Synthetic BurstSR (43.12 vs 42.83), 0.268 dB on RealBSR-RAW (27.558 vs 27.290), and 0.204 dB on RealBSR-RGB (31.401 vs 31.197). Over the cross-attention-based GMTNet [31], the improvement is 0.76 dB on Synthetic BurstSR (43.12 vs 42.36). Against the recurrent RBSR [46], the gap is 0.68 dB (43.12 vs 42.44). The channel-shuffling BIPNet [11] trails by 1.19 dB (43.12 vs 41.93), while the optical-flow-based DBSR [1] and MFIR [2] lag further behind at 40.76 and 41.56 dB respectively. On RealBSR-RAW, the older BSRT [29] (22.579 dB) and FBANet [45] (23.423 dB) are substantially outperformed, with QMambaBSR achieving 27.558 dB. The earliest baseline HighRes-Net [8] (37.45 dB) is surpassed by a margin of 5.67 dB, reflecting the generational gap between recursive fusion and the proposed query-based SSM approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MambaIR line of work [16], which first applied state space models to image restoration, by repurposing the SSM's state-transition mechanism for multi-frame burst super-resolution. It departs from the dominant cross-attention paradigm (GMTNet, Burstormer) that queries the base frame against each current frame independently, and from RNN-based sequential fusion (RBSR), by instead letting the base frame gate the SSM's control matrix B and discretization step Δ to simultaneously query all current frames in a single pass. The AdaUp module also breaks from the static upsampling (pixel shuffle, transposed convolution) used by BIPNet and Burstormer, introducing content-adaptive kernel modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that sub-pixels maintain consistent spatial distribution across frames while noise appears randomly provides a principled justification for multi-frame joint querying over frame-by-frame fusion, and the QSSM operationalizes this insight with linear complexity. For smartphone photography, where burst capture is standard, the practical impact of better noise-subpixel separation is significant. Open questions include whether the SSM-based multi-frame querying generalizes to other multi-frame restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring), how performance scales with burst size beyond 14 frames, and whether the modest AdaUp gains (0.16–0.26 dB) justify its added complexity over well-tuned transposed convolutions.
