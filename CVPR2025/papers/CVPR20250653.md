## Problem

Pretrained text-to-image diffusion models, when used with guided samplers like classifier-free guidance, inherently focus on high-density regions of the data manifold, making it difficult to generate minority samples that reside in low-density regions. This limitation perpetuates demographic biases in downstream applications and limits creative AI use cases that require novel, uncommon visual outputs. Existing minority samplers either rely on external classifiers or are tailored to canonical image benchmarks, leaving a gap for effective minority generation in the T2I setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-provided text prompt (e.g., from MS-COCO captions) and produces a single generated image exhibiting minority/low-density visual features while preserving the prompt's semantic content. It operates entirely at inference time on pretrained latent diffusion models (SDv1.5, SDv2.0, SDXL-Lightning) without any retraining. The prompt is internally augmented with a placeholder string marking the position of a learnable token, whose embedding is iteratively refined across sampling timesteps; the final image is decoded from the latent space via the model's VAE decoder.

## Method

MinorityPrompt introduces a token-based online prompt optimization framework that appends a learnable token to the user prompt and updates only that token's embedding during inference, preserving the semantics of the original prompt tokens. The key objective is a reconstruction-based likelihood metric: it measures how much a clean estimate of the current noisy latent (conditioned on the optimized token) changes after a noise-perturb-denoise cycle, which the paper proves (Proposition 1) is equivalent up to a constant to the negative ELBO of the sample's log-likelihood under the base text conditioning. Three theoretical flaws in the naive adaptation of prior likelihood metrics are identified and corrected: removing CFG from the clean estimates, eliminating stop-gradient blocking of gradient flow, and using the base text embedding (rather than the optimized one) in the reconstruction term. Practical stabilizations include a symmetric stop-gradient trick (λ=1), an annealed perturbation timestep s = T − t, and intermittent optimization every N steps, yielding state-of-the-art minority generation across multiple SD backbones with minimal quality loss.

## Results

On MS-COCO (10K captions), MinorityPrompt achieves the lowest log-likelihood (most minority) across all three Stable Diffusion variants: 0.8971 bpd on SDv1.5, 0.9143 bpd on SDv2.0, and 0.5457 bpd on SDXL-Lightning, compared to 1.0367, 1.1100, and 0.6082 for standard DDIM respectively. Text alignment (CLIPScore) is maintained or improved (e.g., 31.96 vs 31.85 on SDv2.0), while user-preference metrics (PickScore, ImageReward) show only minor trade-offs (e.g., PickScore 21.31 vs 21.48 on SDv1.5). The method also achieves the highest Coverage (0.7328 on SDv1.5) among all minority-focused baselines, indicating broader exploration of low-density regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard DDIM, MinorityPrompt achieves substantially lower likelihood (more minority) on all three models (e.g., 0.897 vs 1.037 bpd on SDv1.5, a 13.5% reduction) while matching or exceeding DDIM on CLIPScore and ImageReward; the trade-off is a small drop in PickScore (~0.1–0.2) and Density. Against SGMS [52], the prior state-of-the-art minority sampler, MinorityPrompt wins on likelihood across all models (0.897 vs 0.954 on SDv1.5; 0.914 vs 0.990 on SDv2.0) and dominates on every quality and alignment metric (CLIPScore, PickScore, ImageReward, Precision, Recall, Density, Coverage), with the gap being largest on SDv2.0. Against CADS [42], the state-of-the-art diversity sampler, MinorityPrompt achieves lower likelihood (0.897 vs 1.013 on SDv1.5) and higher ImageReward (0.235 vs 0.101), though CADS retains a slight edge on Recall and Coverage. On the 4-step SDXL-Lightning model, the likelihood gap over SGMS narrows to a near-tie (0.5457 vs 0.5462), but MinorityPrompt still leads on CLIPScore, PickScore, and ImageReward, confirming robustness under aggressive distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: minority sample generation in diffusion models (SGMS [52], Sehwag et al. [44]) and online prompt optimization for T2I diffusion (Chung et al. [6], Park et al. [36]). It departs from prior minority samplers by moving from latent-space guidance or external classifiers to a prompt-space formulation, and from prior prompt optimizers by restricting updates to a small set of learnable tokens rather than the full text embedding. The key theoretical contribution is showing that a naively adapted likelihood objective (Eq. 6) has three specific flaws that break its connection to the target log-likelihood, and that the corrected formulation (Eq. 7) is equivalent to a weighted negative ELBO (Proposition 1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As T2I-generated data increasingly feeds downstream applications (representation learning, data augmentation), the demographic and attribute biases baked into high-density sampling get perpetuated; this work offers a practical, training-free mechanism to counteract those biases while preserving prompt semantics. The framework's versatility—demonstrated by its application to diversity sampling (Tab. 3) and its compatibility with distilled 4-step models like SDXL-Lightning—suggests it could become a general-purpose inference-time tool. Limitations include a measurable quality trade-off (lower PickScore and Density vs. DDIM) and the additional per-step optimization cost, and it remains open whether the approach scales gracefully to very long or compositional prompts where a single learnable token may be insufficient.
