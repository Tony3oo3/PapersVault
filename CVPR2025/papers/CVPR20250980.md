## Problem

Omnidirectional (360°) stereo depth estimation remains underexplored due to two obstacles: the absence of real-world datasets with pixel-wise depth labels from omnidirectional cameras, and the severe spherical distortions in equirectangular projections that break assumptions of conventional stereo matching models designed for rectilinear left-right camera pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250980/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired top-bottom equirectangular images at 1920×512 resolution from two Ricoh Theta V cameras in a vertical arrangement (19.1 cm baseline), augmented with a polar angle map of equal spatial size. It outputs spherical disparity maps (in degrees, defined as the angular difference between camera rays) and depth maps in meters. Ground-truth labels are derived from an Ouster OS1-64 LiDAR sensor via BFGS-optimized point-cloud-to-image projection, with a depth completion pipeline increasing labeled pixel coverage from 12% to 61%.

## Method

The paper introduces 360-IGEV-Stereo, which adapts the IGEV-Stereo architecture for omnidirectional imaging through two key modifications: (1) a shared polar angle map encoder (strided convolutions) whose outputs are concatenated with feature maps at 1/32 resolution and context maps at 1/4 resolution, compensating for distortion that varies with polar angle in the vertical cost volume; and (2) circular padding of 64 pixel columns along the horizontal axis to exploit the left-right continuity of 360° images, reducing boundary inconsistency (LRCE from 1.18m to 0.36m). The dataset contribution includes a fully automated depth completion pipeline that temporally aggregates LiDAR point clouds over 8 frames, performs k-nearest-neighbor interpolation in spherical coordinates with inverse-distance weighting, and filters points by relative weighted variance and neighbor-distance thresholds. The paper also benchmarks PSMNet, 360SD-Net, and unmodified IGEV-Stereo on the 39,553-frame HELVIPAD dataset, showing that the adapted model achieves the best disparity MAE (0.188°) and depth MAE (1.720m) across all metrics.

## Results

On the HELVIPAD test set (10,146 frames), the proposed 360-IGEV-Stereo achieves the best performance across all disparity and depth metrics: disparity MAE of 0.188°, depth MAE of 1.720 m, depth MARE of 0.130, and LRCE of 0.388 m. Compared to the unmodified IGEV-Stereo, the omnidirectional adaptations reduce depth MAE from 1.860 m to 1.720 m and disparity MAE from 0.225° to 0.188°, while cutting LRCE from 1.203 m to 0.388 m. Runtime remains nearly identical (0.25 s vs. 0.24 s), confirming minimal computational overhead from the polar angle input and circular padding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

360-IGEV-Stereo outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor, unmodified IGEV-Stereo, it reduces depth MAE by 0.14 m (1.720 vs. 1.860), disparity MAE by 0.037° (0.188 vs. 0.225), and LRCE by 0.815 m (0.388 vs. 1.203), with the LRCE gap driven primarily by circular padding. Against 360SD-Net, the only other omnidirectional top-bottom method, 360-IGEV-Stereo improves depth MAE by 0.40 m (1.720 vs. 2.122) and depth MARE by 0.022 (0.130 vs. 0.152), while also achieving a lower LRCE (0.388 vs. 0.904 m). Against PSMNet, the conventional stereo baseline, the proposed method reduces depth MAE by 0.79 m and disparity MAE by 0.098°, while running 2.6× faster (0.25 s vs. 0.66 s). Notably, 360SD-Net beats PSMNet on all metrics, confirming that omnidirectional-specific design helps, but its older spatial-pyramid-pooling architecture limits it relative to the iterative IGEV-Stereo backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IGEV-Stereo architecture (Xu et al., CVPR 2023) to the omnidirectional setting by incorporating a polar angle map as input and circular padding at inference, drawing the polar angle idea from 360SD-Net (Wang et al., ICRA 2020), the only prior method designed for top-bottom 360° stereo. On the data side, it carves out a gap not filled by existing stereo datasets (KITTI, ApolloScape, nuScenes, Waymo), which are limited to rectilinear or multi-view driving configurations, nor by prior omnidirectional datasets (OmniHouse, OmniThings, Stereo-MP3D, Stereo-SF3D), which are synthetic and indoor-only, nor by JRDB, which lacks pixel-wise depth. The depth completion pipeline is positioned as a practical augmentation technique that bridges the sparsity of LiDAR point clouds to high-resolution equirectangular images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HELVIPAD is the first real-world omnidirectional stereo dataset with pixel-wise depth labels spanning indoor, outdoor, and night conditions, filling a critical data gap that has hindered progress in 360° depth estimation. The finding that omnidirectional models generalize better to unseen conditions (especially night outdoor) than their rectilinear counterparts is a useful signal for practitioners deploying robots in diverse environments. Open questions remain around real-time inference for omnidirectional stereo, the performance ceiling of current architectures under severe equirectangular distortion, and whether the modest gains from polar angle and circular padding scale to more complex multi-camera or dynamic-scene settings.
