## Problem

Existing contrastive audio-visual masked autoencoder methods align a single global audio representation to individual video frames, creating a granularity mismatch that prevents fine-grained temporal correspondence. Additionally, enforcing both contrastive alignment and masked reconstruction on the same joint-layer representation creates conflicting optimization objectives that degrade performance on both tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250537/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes uniformly sampled video frames (10–16 per video) and fixed-length Mel spectrogram segments (3–4 seconds) temporally aligned to each frame via index mapping. For retrieval, it outputs per-frame global tokens whose diagonal similarity across the temporal axis yields a video-level score; for classification, it concatenates audio-visual global tokens across time and prepends a CLS token; for segmentation, it computes cosine similarity between the audio global token and each visual patch token to produce a 14×14 localization map upsampled to 224×224.

## Method

CAV-MAE Sync extends CAV-MAE with three architectural changes: (1) audio is encoded as a temporal sequence of frame-aligned spectrogram segments rather than one global spectrogram, enabling per-frame contrastive matching; (2) dedicated learnable global tokens are introduced so the contrastive loss operates exclusively on these tokens while patch tokens focus on reconstruction, disentangling the two objectives; (3) learnable register tokens are appended to both modalities to absorb high-norm computation artifacts from the ViT, freeing patch tokens for spatial reconstruction. The architecture retains separate ViT encoders for audio and vision, a joint transformer layer with modality-specific layer normalizations, and a joint decoder for masked patch reconstruction. For retrieval, a diagonal-similarity strategy averages only temporally corresponding token pairs across the T-frame sequence, promoting both semantic and temporal alignment.

## Results

On zero-shot audio-visual retrieval, CAV-MAE Sync achieves R@1 of 35.2 (Visual→Audio) and 27.9 (Audio→Visual) on AudioSet, and 27.9 / 23.2 on VGGSound, outperforming all self-supervised baselines. For linear-probing classification, the model reaches 30.5 mAP on AudioSet-20K and 52.7% accuracy on VGGSound. On sound-prompted semantic segmentation (ADE20K Sound), it achieves 22.7 mIoU, surpassing CAV-MAE (20.9) and ImageBind (19.1). The only method that outperforms CAV-MAE Sync on retrieval is VAB-Encodec, which is fine-tuned specifically for retrieval and serves as an upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAV-MAE Sync substantially outperforms the original CAV-MAE on all retrieval metrics (e.g., V→A AudioSet R@1: 35.2 vs. 16.1) and on classification (30.5 vs. 27.3 mAP on AudioSet-20K). Against ImageBind, the improvement is consistent across both retrieval directions and both datasets (e.g., V→A AudioSet R@1: 35.2 vs. 22.1), and on segmentation mIoU (22.7 vs. 19.1). AVSiam and LanguageBind are clearly outperformed on all reported metrics, with LanguageBind being the weakest baseline (R@1 as low as 4.4–6.5). On classification, CAV-MAE Sync edges out MaViL (30.5 vs. 30 mAP on AudioSet-20K) while using only AudioSet-2M for pretraining. The sole method that surpasses CAV-MAE Sync is VAB-Encodec on retrieval (39.5 vs. 35.2 R@1 on AudioSet V→A) and classification (33.3 vs. 30.5 mAP), but VAB-Encodec is fine-tuned with a separate retrieval objective and uses both AudioSet-2M and VGGSound for pretraining, making it an upper bound rather than a direct self-supervised comparison. On sound-prompted segmentation, CAV-MAE Sync (22.7 mIoU) beats DAVENet (17.0) and CAV-MAE (20.9), though DenseAV with DinoV2+LoRA achieves 24.2 mIoU using a different backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CAV-MAE (Gong et al., ICLR 2023), the foundational contrastive audio-visual masked autoencoder, by addressing three architectural limitations: the use of a single global audio representation, the entanglement of contrastive and reconstruction objectives in a shared joint layer, and the absence of register tokens. It also draws on the register token concept from Darcet et al. (ICLR 2024) to reduce semantic load on patch tokens. The key conceptual departure from the broader CAV-MAE lineage (CrossMAE, MaViL, AVSiam) is treating audio as a temporally aligned sequence of segments matched to individual video frames rather than a single spectrogram, enabling fine-grained cross-modal alignment without adding architectural complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that three relatively simple architectural modifications—temporal audio segmentation, dedicated global tokens for contrastive loss, and register tokens—collectively yield state-of-the-art zero-shot retrieval and competitive classification/localization with a simpler model than more complex alternatives like DenseAV or VAB. This suggests that the field may have been over-engineering audio-visual alignment when the core issue was granularity mismatch and objective conflict. Open questions include whether the temporal alignment strategy generalizes to longer videos or streaming settings, whether the 3–4 second optimal segment length holds across domains, and whether the global-token disentanglement principle transfers to other multi-objective self-supervised frameworks beyond audio-visual learning.
