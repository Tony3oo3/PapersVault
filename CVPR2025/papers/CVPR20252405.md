## Problem

LangSplat achieves precise 3D language fields by grounding CLIP features into 3D Gaussians, but CLIP is designed for static image-text matching and cannot capture temporal dynamics such as state changes, actions, and object conditions in video. Existing vision models extract only global, video-level features rather than the pixel-aligned, object-level features needed for a precise 4D language field, leaving dynamic open-vocabulary querying in 4D scenes largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252405/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video frames of a dynamic scene and a free-form text query (e.g., 'dog' for time-agnostic or 'running dog' for time-sensitive) and produces per-frame segmentation masks identifying the queried object, plus for time-sensitive queries, the specific time segments where the queried state is active. Object tracking is maintained via SAM + DEVA across frames, and the MLLM (Qwen2-VL-7B) generates per-object, per-frame captions that are encoded by e5-mistral-7b into 6-dimensional sentence embeddings, while CLIP features are compressed to 3 dimensions via an autoencoder.

## Method

4D LangSplat constructs two complementary semantic fields on top of a 4D-GS deformable Gaussian representation: a time-agnostic field using CLIP features on SAM hierarchical masks (as in LangSplat), and a time-varying field learned from text rather than vision features. For the time-varying field, a multimodal object-wise video prompting method combines visual prompts (red contour, grayscale background, Gaussian blur) with text prompts (a video-level motion description followed by frame-specific instructions) to guide an MLLM in generating temporally consistent, object-specific captions, which are then encoded into pixel-aligned sentence embeddings as supervision. A status deformable network models each Gaussian's semantic feature as a convex combination of K learned state prototypes, with an MLP decoder predicting per-state weights from HexPlane spatiotemporal features, enforcing smooth transitions between a limited set of semantic states rather than arbitrary feature deformation.

## Results

On time-agnostic open-vocabulary querying, 4D LangSplat achieves 82.48% mIoU and 98.01% mAcc on HyperNeRF, and 85.11% mIoU and 98.32% mAcc on Neu3D, outperforming all baselines. On time-sensitive querying (HyperNeRF), the method reaches 90.83% accuracy and 72.26% vIoU, surpassing the strongest baseline (Non-Status Field) by 3.25% in accuracy and 4.19% in vIoU, and the CLIP-based Deformable CLIP by 29.03% in accuracy and 28.04% in vIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4D LangSplat outperforms all three baselines on both datasets for time-agnostic querying. Against LangSplat [34], the closest 3D language-field baseline, the method gains 7.56% mIoU on HyperNeRF (82.48 vs. 74.92) and a substantial 23.62% mIoU on Neu3D (85.11 vs. 61.49), while also improving mAcc by 0.29% and 6.43% respectively. Against Gaussian Grouping [58], a segmentation-based approach adapted for 4D, the gains are even larger: 31.99% mIoU on HyperNeRF and 35.18% on Neu3D. Feature-3DGS [62], which distills CLIP features into 3D Gaussians without SAM masks, is the weakest baseline, trailing 4D LangSplat by over 45% mIoU on both datasets. The large Neu3D gap over LangSplat (23.62% mIoU) highlights that static 3D language fields degrade significantly on more complex dynamic scenes, whereas 4D LangSplat's deformable representation preserves segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LangSplat's 3D language Gaussian splatting to the 4D dynamic setting by combining it with 4D-GS deformable Gaussians, but makes a key conceptual departure: rather than lifting CLIP vision features into 4D (which fails to capture temporal semantics), it learns the time-varying semantic field directly from sentence embeddings of MLLM-generated object-level video captions. The multimodal object-wise video prompting strategy (visual prompts + video-level motion descriptions + frame-specific captions) is a novel pipeline for producing pixel-aligned, object-specific textual supervision. The status deformable network, which constrains semantic features to evolve as convex combinations of a small set of state prototypes, is a new architectural component not present in prior 4D-GS or language field work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to construct 4D language fields that support both time-agnostic and time-sensitive open-vocabulary queries in dynamic scenes, filling a clear gap left by 3D-only methods like LangSplat and LERF. The approach of bypassing vision features entirely in favor of MLLM text embeddings as supervision is a promising paradigm that could generalize to other dynamic scene understanding tasks. However, evaluation is limited to two small datasets (HyperNeRF, Neu3D) requiring manual annotations, and the method's dependence on MLLM caption quality and SAM+DEVA tracking accuracy introduces practical fragility. Open questions include scalability to longer videos, more complex multi-object interactions, and whether the status deformable network generalizes beyond the small number of discrete states assumed.
