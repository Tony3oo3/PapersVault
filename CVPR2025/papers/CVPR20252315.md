## Problem

Camera-only multi-view 3D object detection has seen steady gains in average performance, but partially occluded objects remain a critical yet underexplored failure mode for safe autonomous driving. Occlusion diminishes the quality of learned visual representations, creating a chicken-and-egg problem where detecting occluded targets requires contextual cues that are themselves limited by the occlusion. Existing multi-view 3D detection methods lack a mechanism to compensate for this diminished representation using external prior knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB camera images (6 surround cameras at 1600×900 from nuScenes) along with two sets of pre-computed prototypes: visual prototypes (DeViT feature embeddings of cropped 2D bounding boxes, clustered by visibility level and category into a tensor of shape (N×M)×D) and language prototypes (BERT embeddings of category names, shape N×D). These prototypes are injected as convolution kernels into a depth-wise correlation operation over backbone features, producing a correlation feature map that is then sampled alongside the original backbone features via dual-path 3D query projection. The output is standard 3D bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated additionally by per-visibility-level recall (Vis1–Vis4) to measure occlusion robustness.

## Method

CorrBEV is a plug-and-play framework with three components. First, a Multi-modal Prototype Generator produces occlusion-aware visual prototypes (DeViT embeddings averaged per visibility level and category) and language prototypes (fine-tuned BERT embeddings), concatenated into a unified prototype tensor. Second, a Correlation-guided Query Learner applies a depth-wise correlation operator (equivalent to 1×1 convolution with the prototype tensor as kernel) to fuse prototypes with backbone features, yielding a correlation feature map; this map is used both to initialize 3D object queries via predicted confidence maps (Object Query Initializer) and to provide a second sampling path alongside backbone features (Dual-path Mixed Sampling), so that 3D queries aggregate information from both sources. Third, an Occlusion-aware Trainer introduces a pseudo-occlusion processor that randomly drops pixels in non-occluded training objects to balance visibility-level distribution, and a multi-modal contrastive loss that aligns visual query embeddings to their corresponding language prototype, pulling same-category objects of different occlusion levels into a unified representation space while separating different categories.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation split, CorrBEV_sp (built on SparseBEV with ResNet-50) achieves 47.5% mAP and 57.4% NDS, improving over the SparseBEV baseline by +2.7% mAP and +1.6% NDS. With ResNet-101, CorrBEV_sp reaches 51.7% mAP and 60.6% NDS on validation, and 56.2% mAP / 64.0% NDS on the test split. CorrBEV_fm (built on BEVFormer small with ResNet-101) gains +2.6% mAP and +2.6% NDS over its baseline on validation (39.6% vs 37.0% mAP). The largest occlusion-specific gains appear at Vis1 (severely occluded): 69.1% recall vs 60.4% for SparseBEV-Res50, a +8.7-point improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CorrBEV_sp (Res50) outperforms all Res50-based baselines in Table 1, surpassing the strongest competitor RayDN [27] by +0.6% mAP (47.5 vs 46.9) and +1.1% NDS, while also leading on every visibility-level recall (e.g., Vis1: 69.1 vs 60.5). Against its direct baseline SparseBEV [28], CorrBEV_sp gains +2.7% mAP and +1.6% NDS (Res50) or +1.6% mAP and +1.4% NDS (Res101). CorrBEV_fm improves over BEVFormer small [25] by +2.6% mAP and +2.6% NDS on validation, and over the single-frame variant BEVFormer†small by +2.0% mAP. On the test split (Table 2), CorrBEV_sp (Res101) achieves 56.2% mAP / 64.0% NDS, beating FrustumFormer [45] (47.8/56.1) and BEVFormer base [25] (44.5/53.5) by large margins. Notably, SOLOFusion [33] achieves higher overall mAP than BEVFormer small but substantially lower Vis1/Vis2 recalls (48.8/59.1 vs 50.7/60.6), illustrating that average performance does not guarantee occlusion robustness — a gap CorrBEV explicitly addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

CorrBEV repurposes the depth-wise correlation operator from Siamese object tracking (Bertinetto et al., 2016) as a mechanism to inject multi-modal prior knowledge into multi-view 3D detection, a use well outside the tracking domain. It builds directly on two representative BEV detection architectures—BEVFormer (dense-query) and SparseBEV (sparse-query)—as plug-in baselines, while drawing conceptual motivation from human amodal perception to justify the use of auxiliary visual (DeViT) and language (BERT) prototypes. Unlike prior camera-based occlusion handling that relies on temporal fusion or LiDAR-derived masks, CorrBEV operates on single-frame information and requires no additional sensor data, positioning it as a lightweight, architecture-agnostic complement rather than a replacement for existing pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical disconnect: methods that improve average mAP (e.g., SOLOFusion) can simultaneously underperform on severely occluded objects, which are critical for safe driving. By demonstrating that a prototype-guided correlation module improves recall across all visibility levels with only ~7% additional training cost and minimal FPS loss, it offers a broadly adoptable recipe for hardening existing detectors. The unexpected finding that occlusion-targeted training also boosts robustness under weather corruptions and color quantization (RoboBEV) suggests the learned multi-modal alignment may be addressing a more general feature-degradation problem. Open questions include whether the offline visual prototypes scale to open-vocabulary or long-tail categories, and whether the correlation mechanism retains its benefit when the backbone is significantly larger or the scene complexity increases beyond nuScenes.
