## Problem

Motion-aware image-to-video generation requires a reliable motion intensity signal, but traditional metrics like SSIM and optical flow fail to align with human motion perception and cannot generalize to arbitrary videos. Moreover, no prior work has decoupled object motion from camera movement, which are fundamentally different types of motion that require separate control for precise video generation.

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes 49-frame videos at 480×720 resolution, using the first frame as the conditioning image alongside a text prompt and two motion intensity scores (object and camera, each ranging 1–10) predicted by the frozen motion estimator. At inference, users can override the predicted intensities with custom values to control the generated video's motion characteristics. The output is a temporally coherent video where object and camera motions are independently modulated.

## Method

MotionStone introduces a dedicated motion estimator built on a TAdaConv backbone with two separate MLP heads that independently predict object and camera motion scores. The estimator is trained via contrastive learning on 5,000 human-annotated video pairs using a pairwise ranking loss, augmented with a regression loss against pseudo-labels derived from tracking trajectories to ensure score differentiation. For injection into the diffusion model (CogVideoX-based Diffusion Transformer), the two motion scores are each mapped through separate MLPs to high-dimensional embeddings, concatenated along the channel dimension, and added to the time-step embedding—this decoupled injection prevents ambiguity between the two motion types. The full pipeline is trained via supervised fine-tuning on 100K videos with the motion estimator frozen, so the diffusion network learns to condition on disentangled motion signals at every denoising step.

## Results

On the WebVID validation set evaluated with VBench metrics, MotionStone achieves 95.76% Background Consistency, 46.78% Aesthetic Quality, and 62.29% Imaging Quality, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The motion estimator also demonstrates strong predictive accuracy, correctly ranking relative motion intensity in video pairs at 72.80%, surpassing the SSIM-based baseline (44.56%) by 28 percentage points. MotionStone is trained on 100,000 high-quality videos at 480×720 resolution using 8 A100 GPUs with supervised fine-tuning on the CogVideoX framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionStone outperforms all four I2V baselines on all three VBench metrics. Against the closest competitor CogVideoX-5B (its base model), MotionStone gains +0.85% Background Consistency, +0.90% Aesthetic Quality, and +0.30% Imaging Quality, confirming that the decoupled motion intensity injection provides meaningful improvement over the unmodified diffusion transformer. The gap widens substantially against U-Net-based methods: SVD trails by 2.59% in Background Consistency and 4.40% in Aesthetic Quality, while I2VGen-XL lags by 4.83% and 6.64% respectively. AnimateAnything, which also supports coarse motion intensity control, is outperformed by 1.87% in Background Consistency and 0.74% in Aesthetic Quality. In the motion estimation sub-task (Table 3), the proposed contrastive-trained estimator achieves 72.80% pairwise ranking accuracy versus only 44.56% for SSIM, a 28-point margin that validates the decoupled object/camera motion modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionStone builds directly on CogVideoX's diffusion transformer backbone and 3D VAE, extending it with a dedicated motion intensity estimator based on TAdaConv. It departs from prior text-guided motion control methods (LivePhoto, Cinemo, AnimateAnything) that relied on SSIM or feature-difference proxies for motion intensity, arguing these metrics misalign with human perception and cannot distinguish object motion from camera movement. The key conceptual departure is the decoupled formulation: rather than a single scalar motion signal, the model predicts and injects separate object and camera motion intensities, trained via pairwise contrastive ranking rather than absolute scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled motion estimator is positioned as a general plug-in for both data curation and video generation training, which could benefit a range of I2V pipelines beyond MotionStone itself. The contrastive annotation strategy (comparing video pairs rather than assigning absolute scores) offers a practical path to scaling motion labels, addressing a real bottleneck in training motion-aware video models. Open questions include whether the 1–10 intensity scale captures sufficiently fine-grained control, whether the frozen estimator could benefit from joint fine-tuning with the diffusion model, and how the approach generalizes to longer or higher-resolution videos beyond the 49-frame, 480×720 setting evaluated here.
