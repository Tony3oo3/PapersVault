## Problem

Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting requires SfM preprocessing to obtain camera poses and a sparse point cloud for initialization, but SfM is slow, fails on textureless or repetitive regions, and is non-differentiable. Two specific challenges arise when removing SfM: estimating camera poses from video without ground-truth poses, and initializing/growing 3D Gaussians in regions not visible in the first frame where adaptive density control struggles due to sparse coverage and small gradients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250684/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence with small inter-frame camera motion and known camera intrinsics (though it degrades gracefully with a fixed 70° FoV heuristic). It produces a single unified 3DGS representation covering the entire scene along with estimated camera poses for all frames. Initialization uses monocular depth maps (Zoedepth/Metric3D) from the first frame of each segment rather than an SfM point cloud, and the training pipeline additionally consumes VFI-interpolated frames and pseudo-views rendered from base models as auxiliary supervision.

## Method

The method has three core components. First, camera poses are estimated by stacking relative poses between adjacent frames, where each relative pose is obtained by optimizing an affine transformation on a single-image 3DGS; a VFI model (IFRNet) doubles the frame count to halve per-step camera motion, reducing artifacts in pose estimation. Second, a hierarchical training strategy partitions the video into 2^L overlapping segments, trains a base 3DGS per segment, then iteratively merges adjacent pairs by pruning Gaussians below a gradient-based importance threshold and concatenating the survivors, repeating until one unified model remains (L=2 in practice). Third, multi-source supervision retrains each merged model using original frames, pseudo-views sampled via SE(3) interpolation between base-model poses, and VFI-interpolated frames, reducing overfitting and covering viewpoints absent from the original training set.

## Results

On Tanks and Temples, HT-3DGS achieves a mean PSNR of 33.53 dB, SSIM of 0.96, and LPIPS of 0.07, improving over the closest 3DGS baseline CF-3DGS (31.28 dB) by 2.25 dB on average, with a maximum per-scene gain of 3.72 dB (Barn). On the more challenging CO3D-V2 dataset (larger camera motions), the method achieves a mean PSNR of 28.34 dB, outperforming CF-3DGS (26.61 dB) by 1.74 dB and Nope-NeRF (25.14 dB) by 3.20 dB, with a peak gain of 3.90 dB on the teddybear scene. Camera pose estimation on CO3D-V2 shows RPE_t reduced to 0.041–0.093 (vs. 0.094–0.505 for CF-3DGS) and RPE_r to 0.170–0.438 (vs. 0.211–0.472).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four SfM-free novel view synthesis methods: three NeRF-based (BARF, SC-NeRF, Nope-NeRF) and one 3DGS-based (CF-3DGS). HT-3DGS dominates all baselines on every scene and metric. Against the strongest 3DGS competitor CF-3DGS, the gains are 2.25 dB mean PSNR on Tanks and Temples and 1.74 dB on CO3D-V2, with the largest single-scene improvement being 3.72 dB (Barn) and 3.90 dB (teddybear on CO3D-V2). Against the best NeRF baseline Nope-NeRF, the PSNR advantage is 7.19 dB on Tanks and Temples and 3.20 dB on CO3D-V2. On camera pose estimation (CO3D-V2), HT-3DGS reduces RPE_t by up to 0.464 and RPE_r by up to 0.078 relative to CF-3DGS, though ATE is slightly worse in some scenes due to sensitivity to VFI interpolation errors. Even without known camera intrinsics, the method still outperforms CF-3DGS by 0.89 dB PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SfM-free 3DGS line initiated by CF-3DGS [11], which estimates relative poses via affine transformations on 3D Gaussians and grows the representation sequentially from the first frame. The key departure is a hierarchical training strategy that partitions the video into overlapping segments, trains segment-specific base 3DGS models, and merges them using importance-score-based pruning and concatenation—effectively repurposing the merge as a densification step to overcome the sparse-initialization problem that plagues sequential growth. Video frame interpolation (IFRNet) is borrowed as an off-the-shelf tool to halve inter-frame camera displacement, improving relative pose estimation, while multi-source supervision reuses base-model pseudo-views and interpolated frames to reduce overfitting during retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating SfM preprocessing, the method removes a brittle, non-differentiable bottleneck that fails on textureless or repetitive scenes, making 3DGS applicable to a broader range of video inputs. The hierarchical merging-as-densification idea is a practical and interpretable solution to the sparse-Gaussian initialization problem, and the counterintuitive finding that it also reduces memory (by ~0.22–0.26 GB) suggests that segment-wise optimization yields more representative Gaussians than global densification. However, the approach is constrained to video sequences with small camera motion, requires all frames in advance for the hierarchical variant, and training time increases substantially; open questions include whether the strategy scales to longer or more dynamic sequences and whether the VFI dependency can be replaced by learned pose priors.
