## Problem

Existing concept bottleneck generative models (CBGM) require training the entire generative model from scratch with concept-labeled real images, making them computationally expensive (e.g., 240 V100-hours for DDPM-256×256) and difficult to scale. This paper asks whether interpretability can be added to already-pretrained generative models via lightweight post-hoc training that avoids both full retraining and the need for real concept-labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251285/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained generative model g = g₂∘g₁ (GAN or diffusion model) and a random noise vector z ∈ Z. At inference, it produces a generated image x, a concept prediction vector c (with 2 logits per binary concept and N logits per categorical concept, plus a 40-dim unsupervised embedding), and optionally an intervened image x_intervened after swapping concept logits. For optimization-based interventions, the input additionally specifies target concept values c*, and the output is a minimally perturbed image x* = g₂(w+δ) that matches c* while remaining close to the original x. Pseudo-labels for training come from CLIP zero-shot, TIP few-shot (128 images), or supervised classifiers—no real concept-labeled images are required for the core method.

## Method

The paper proposes two post-hoc methods. First, a concept-bottleneck autoencoder (CB-AE) f = D∘E is inserted at an intermediate layer of the frozen generator, so the full pipeline becomes g₂∘f∘g₁. The encoder E maps the intermediate latent w = g₁(z) to a concept vector c, and the decoder D reconstructs w′ = D(c). Training uses three simultaneous objectives: (1) reconstruction losses (MSE on latents and images) to preserve generation quality, (2) a cross-entropy concept alignment loss against pseudo-labels from a source M, and (3) intervention losses that swap concept logits and enforce both that the intervened image matches the modified pseudo-label and that the encoder cyclically recovers the intervened concepts. Second, a concept controller (CC) Ω is a simplified post-hoc module that only predicts concepts (no decoder), trained solely with the concept alignment loss, and combined with optimization-based interventions via I-RFGSM (50 steps, ε=0.1) to find minimal latent perturbations δ that steer the encoder output toward desired concepts. This CC approach trades inherent interpretability for 4–15× faster training and higher steerability (average +25% over CBGM).

## Results

On CelebA (8 concepts) with GAN, CB-AE achieves 47.1% steerability and CB-AE+opt-int reaches 59.9%, compared to CBGM's 25.6%. For DDPM on CelebA (8 concepts), CC+opt-int achieves 41.8% steerability versus CB-DDPM's 13.8%. Concept accuracy reaches 86–90% across datasets and model families. Training is 4–15× faster than CBGM (e.g., 14 V100-hrs vs. 50 for StyleGAN2, 8.3 V100-hrs vs. 240 for DDPM-256).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary prior-work baseline CBGM [16], both CB-AE and CC substantially outperform on steerability: on CelebA GAN, CB-AE+opt-int reaches 59.9% vs. CBGM's 25.6% (+34.3 pp), and on CelebA DDPM, CC+opt-int reaches 41.8% vs. CB-DDPM's 13.8% (+28 pp). The paper also reports 4–15× faster training (e.g., 14 vs. 50 V100-hrs for StyleGAN2; 8.3 vs. 240 for DDPM-256). Against GAN intervention baselines CGAN [27] and ACGAN [30], the proposed methods achieve roughly 5–7× higher steerability (e.g., 59.9% vs. 8.7% and 9.2% on CelebA 8-concept). For diffusion models, CF-DDPM [14] and CG-DDPM [6] achieve only 6.8–7.2% steerability on CelebA, while CC-DDPM+opt-int reaches 41.8%, a ~6× improvement. The one area where CBGM retains an edge is FID (9.1 vs. 9.52 for CB-AE on CelebA-HQ StyleGAN2), reflecting a trade-off between image quality and interpretability, though the gap narrows to 7.67 vs. 9.1 when using optimization-based interventions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept bottleneck model (CBM) framework from classification [20, 45] to image generation, departing from the sole prior generative CBM work (CBGM [16]) by replacing expensive from-scratch training with a post-hoc insertion of a lightweight autoencoder (CB-AE) or classifier (CC) into a frozen pretrained generative model. The optimization-based intervention mechanism borrows directly from adversarial attack methods (I-RFGSM [42]), repurposing gradient-based perturbation for concept steering rather than robustness. The pseudo-labeling pipeline leverages CLIP zero-shot or few-shot classifiers [35, 48] to eliminate the need for concept-labeled real images, a strategy previously applied to classification CBMs [32, 39, 43, 44] but not to generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing training cost by 4–15× and removing the dependency on concept-labeled real images, this work makes interpretable generative models practically deployable at scale across both GAN and diffusion model families, which is critical for auditing and content moderation in high-stakes applications. A key open question is the quality–interpretability tradeoff: CB-AE incurs a measurable FID penalty relative to CBGM, and the paper acknowledges this needs future work. Additionally, whether the post-hoc bottleneck approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or compositional concepts beyond the fixed 8–40 concept regimes tested remains unexplored.
