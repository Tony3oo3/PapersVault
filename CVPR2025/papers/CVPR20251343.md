## Problem

Existing generalizable human digitalization methods either require per-subject optimization (minutes to hours), depend on human parametric models that fail for loose garments, or produce incomplete geometry with floating artifacts. No prior approach simultaneously reconstructs watertight full-body geometry and photorealistic appearance from very sparse views in a single feed-forward pass without parametric model constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251343/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4 rectified binocular stereo pairs (8 cameras arranged in a ~2 m circle) as input, along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics. It outputs a watertight 3D mesh and a 2D Gaussian Splatting representation whose primitives are anchored to mesh vertices, enabling 6-DoF novel view rendering in under 0.3 s. Unlike pixel-aligned Gaussian methods that place primitives per input view, here each Gaussian's position, orientation, and anisotropy are determined by the shared reconstructed surface, eliminating redundancy in overlapping view regions.

## Method

GBC-Splat employs a coarse-to-fine geometry pipeline: a neural implicit occupancy field regressed via cross-view attention (3D sparse features as queries, multi-view 2D pixel-aligned features as keys/values, processed by a submanifold sparse 3D U-Net) yields a coarse mesh via Dual Contouring, which is then refined by RAFT-Stereo-style disparity and normal estimation followed by Poisson Surface Reconstruction. For appearance, 2D Gaussian surfels are spawned at mesh vertices with orientation aligned to surface normals and texture-gradient directions; scale, opacity, and color are regressed by MLPs consuming fused 3D/2D features. A non-learned texel-based subdivision step applies spectral clustering on 8×8 image patches to split high-frequency Gaussians into sub-primitives, boosting rendering sharpness without additional training. The full model is trained in three stages (geometry-only, joint geometry+Gaussian, and joint finetuning) with a combination of occupancy, disparity, normal, and differentiable-rendering losses.

## Results

On novel-view rendering (8 input views), GBC-Splat achieves PSNR 24.94, SSIM 0.8881, and LPIPS 0.1269, with the subdivision module further improving to PSNR 25.86, SSIM 0.8970, and LPIPS 0.1188. For geometry reconstruction, the method attains P2S 1.089 and Chamfer distance 1.218 in 300 ms, substantially outperforming all baselines. The LPIPS advantage is particularly pronounced: 0.1269 vs. 0.1719 for the best optimization-based baseline (2DGS) and 0.2008 for GPS-Gaussian, indicating superior texture-level fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GBC-Splat outperforms all five baselines on LPIPS (texture fidelity) across the board. Against the optimization-based 2DGS (~3 min per subject), GBC-Splat matches PSNR (24.94 vs. 25.81) and exceeds SSIM (0.8881 vs. 0.8675) while achieving a 4.5× LPIPS improvement (0.1269 vs. 0.1719) in 300 ms. Against the feed-forward GPS-Gaussian (200 ms), GBC-Splat wins on LPIPS (0.1269 vs. 0.2008) and SSIM (0.8881 vs. 0.8847) with comparable PSNR, and also produces watertight geometry (P2S 1.089 vs. 2.173) whereas GPS-Gaussian yields only partial surfaces. The fully implicit DoubleField (40 s) is clearly outperformed on both geometry (P2S 4.878 vs. 1.089) and rendering. The parametric-model-based GHG is the weakest baseline, scoring PSNR 17.88 and LPIPS 0.3381, confirming that template-dependent methods struggle with loose garments. The texel-based subdivision module adds ~0.9 dB PSNR and ~0.008 SSIM over the base model without any additional learning, closing the remaining gap to 2DGS on PSNR while maintaining the LPIPS advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: PIFu-style pixel-aligned implicit occupancy fields for human geometry, RAFT-Stereo-style explicit disparity estimation, and 2D Gaussian Splatting (Huang et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) for appearance rendering. The key departure from prior work is the coarse-to-fine pipeline that fuses implicit and explicit geometry in a single feed-forward model, then anchors 2D Gaussian surfels directly on the extracted mesh rather than on per-pixel depth estimates (as in GPS-Gaussian, EVA-Gaussian) or parametric templates (as in GHG). The texel-based subdivision algorithm is a novel non-learning upsampling step that has no direct precedent in the Gaussian Splatting literature, leveraging input-view texture to densify primitives in high-frequency regions without additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving sub-0.3s feed-forward inference for full-body 6-DoF human digitalization from only 4 sparse stereo pairs, this work makes real-time generalizable human capture practically deployable in telepresence, AR/VR, and volumetric video pipelines that previously required per-subject optimization or dense camera rigs. The ability to handle loose garments without parametric body models broadens applicability beyond the tightly-fitted clothing assumption of methods like GHG. However, the frame-by-frame processing introduces temporal flickering and jitter during motion, and the lack of topological consistency limits use in compressed volumetric video — both acknowledged as open problems for future work.
