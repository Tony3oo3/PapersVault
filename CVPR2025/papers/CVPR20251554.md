## Problem

Existing multi-label image classification methods overemphasize co-occurrence relationships among labels, causing the model to incorrectly infer the presence of one label from a co-occurring one (e.g., predicting 'dog' whenever 'frisbee' appears). This overfitting to co-occurrence, combined with the need for full fine-tuning of large pre-trained models, leads to suboptimal performance and high computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251554/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at 224×224 or 448×448 resolution and produces multi-label class predictions over K classes. The output is structured as two parallel prediction streams—one from the correlative (CO) branch and one from the discriminative (DC) branch—each passing through its own linear classifier with sigmoid activation, then combined via a simple 0.5·(ŷ⁺ + ŷ⁻) weighted average. Only a small number of parameters are learned (e.g., 1.02M for the full ML-VPT on ViT-B vs. 86.64M for full fine-tuning), making it a parameter-efficient fine-tuning approach.

## Method

ML-VPT first groups all K classes into N_c co-occurrence (CO) subsets and N_c discriminative (DC) subsets via spectral clustering on the co-occurrence and dis-occurrence graphs, respectively. Two sets of prompt tokens (CO and DC) are injected into every ViT transformer block, with N_m tokens per group to capture intra-group label relationships; a predefined mask prevents cross-group information leakage between prompt tokens. After the final transformer block, each group produces N_m group-aware representations, which are then mapped to per-class label-aware representations through a Group-Aware Mixture-of-Experts module: per-class gating networks (softmax over N_m experts) select and weight the appropriate group-level representations, and lightweight FFN experts (hidden dim 5) perform the transformation. Two non-shared linear classifiers with ASL loss produce the final predictions from the CO and DC branches, jointly optimized end-to-end.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2014, ML-VPT achieves a best mAP of 90.6% using DINOv2/B at 448×448 resolution, outperforming all VPT-based baselines. At the standard 224×224 resolution, the method improves mAP by 1.2%–8.7%, CF1 by 0.7%–7.0%, and OF1 by 0.7%–5.8% over the strongest baseline across six pre-trained backbones (ViT-B, MAE, MoCo v3, ViT-B-21k, DINOv2/B, DINOv2/S). On NUS-WIDE with DINOv2/B, ML-VPT reaches 68.7% mAP and 65.5% CF1, and on Visual Genome it achieves 50.9% mAP and 51.7% CF1, both leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ML-VPT is compared against three VPT variants: VPT (Jia et al., ECCV 2022), GateVPT (Yoo et al., ICML 2023), and E2VPT (Han et al., ICCV 2023), all adapted to multi-label classification via a linear head with ASL loss. ML-VPT consistently outperforms all three baselines across every backbone and dataset. On NUS-WIDE with DINOv2/B, ML-VPT achieves 68.7% mAP versus 68.1% (VPT), 67.2% (GateVPT), and 67.9% (E2VPT); on VG256 the gap widens to 50.9% vs. 49.3%/48.6%/49.2%. Notably, GateVPT and E2VPT do not consistently surpass vanilla VPT (e.g., on COCO with ViT-B, MAE, and MoCo v3 at 224×224, VPT outperforms both modifications), which the authors attribute to these methods being designed for single-label tasks and ignoring label correlations. The largest gains over baselines appear with self-supervised backbones (MAE, DINOv2), where the group-aware prompt tokens and MoE provide the most benefit from structured label relationship modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) from single-label to multi-label image classification by introducing group-aware prompt tokens that explicitly model both correlative (co-occurrence) and discriminative (dis-occurrence) label relationships within a frozen ViT. The grouping strategy—spectral clustering on co-occurrence and dis-occurrence graphs—is adopted from BoostMLC, but here it serves a different purpose: rather than driving a complex multi-teacher training pipeline, it structures a lightweight set of prompt tokens inside the transformer. The group-aware MoE module, which adaptively maps multiple group-level representations to per-class label-aware representations, departs from prior MoE-in-MLC work (e.g., HQS) by operating on group-level rather than label-level representations, enabling a balanced treatment of CO and DC signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, parameter-efficient (~1M learnable parameters vs. 86M for full fine-tuning) recipe for MLC that consistently outperforms vanilla VPT variants across four datasets and six backbones, while explicitly mitigating the well-documented co-occurrence overfitting problem. A key open question is whether the static, pre-computed grouping can be made end-to-end learnable or adaptive to the input image, which could further improve performance on datasets with highly variable label co-occurrence patterns. The modest MoE gains on smaller datasets (VOC07) suggest the grouping granularity and expert count may need dataset-specific tuning, and the approach has not been tested on very large label spaces (e.g., 1000+ classes) where the combinatorial grouping challenge becomes more acute.
