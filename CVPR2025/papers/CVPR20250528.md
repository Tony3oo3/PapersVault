## Problem

Seamlessly adding objects to images from textual instructions without user-provided masks remains challenging: existing mask-free editing models like InstructPix2Pix rely on synthetic training pairs that suffer from source-target inconsistency, and their local edits often alter regions outside the intended edit area. The paper identifies that object removal (inpainting) is fundamentally easier than its inverse (object addition) because inpainting models benefit from explicit mask guidance, and exploits this asymmetry to construct a high-quality training dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250528/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source RGB image and a natural-language editing instruction (e.g., 'add a red sports car on the left side of the road') and produces a single edited image with the described object composited into the scene. Conditioning is achieved through cross-attention on the text encoding plus an additional image conditioning path, with classifier-free guidance applied jointly over both modalities at inference. The output is expected to preserve all non-object regions of the source image while inserting a photorealistic object matching the instruction's style, scale, and position.

## Method

The core insight is to invert the inpainting process: objects are removed from real images using a Stable Diffusion inpainting model guided by class-specific positive/negative prompts, and the resulting source-target pairs (inpaint → original) are used to train a diffusion model to add objects back. A multi-stage filtering pipeline (CLIP consensus across three inpainting candidates, multimodal CLIP similarity to the removed class, α-blending for boundary consistency, and global importance filtering) ensures training data quality. Instructions are generated via a two-stage VLM-LLM pipeline where CogVLM captions the masked object region and Mistral-7B reformulates captions into diverse natural-language editing commands, supplemented by class-name templates and RefCOCO reference descriptions. The editing model is initialized from SD 1.5 and trained with multi-condition classifier-free guidance, dropping text, image, or both conditions each with probability 0.05 to support joint CFG at inference.

## Results

On the MagicBrush object-addition benchmark, the proposed model achieves the best scores on L1 (0.072), L2 (0.025), CLIP-I (0.900), DINO (0.852), and CMMD (0.301), outperforming all seven baselines. On the PIPE held-out test set, it reaches CLIP-I of 0.945 and DINO of 0.903, substantially exceeding IP2P (0.861/0.753) and Hive (0.849/0.754). On OPA, it attains the highest scores across all metrics (L1=0.084, CLIP-I=0.848, DINO=0.735, CMMD=0.360). In human evaluation, the model is preferred over IP2P 73.6% of the time for edit faithfulness and 71.5% for output quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of metrics (L1, L2, CLIP-I, DINO, CMMD) across MagicBrush, PIPE, and OPA benchmarks. The only metric where it trails is CLIP-T (text-image alignment), where VQGAN-CLIP achieves 0.484 vs. 0.302 for the proposed method — an expected gap since VQGAN-CLIP directly optimizes that objective during editing. Against the closest instruction-following competitors IP2P and Hive, the gains are consistent: on the PIPE test set, the proposed model improves CLIP-I from 0.861 (IP2P) to 0.945 and DINO from 0.753 to 0.903, while reducing L1 from 0.098 to 0.057. On OPA, it beats IP2P by 0.025 in L1 and 0.042 in CLIP-I. In the human evaluation (1,833 responses from 57 annotators), the model is preferred over IP2P 73.6% of the time for instruction faithfulness and 71.5% for quality. When fine-tuned on MagicBrush, the proposed model also surpasses fine-tuned IP2P on every metric (e.g., CLIP-I 0.910 vs. 0.902, CMMD 0.298 vs. 0.352), confirming the advantage generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) and the instruction-based editing paradigm established by InstructPix2Pix (Brooks et al., 2023), adopting the same U-Net + VAE + cross-attention conditioning with classifier-free guidance. The key conceptual departure is the 'paint by inpaint' reframing: rather than synthesizing source-target pairs via prompt-to-prompt or other generative tricks (as IP2P does), the authors exploit the asymmetry that mask-based inpainting (object removal) is far more reliable than its inverse (object addition), using a frozen SD inpainting model to create training pairs from real segmentation datasets. The VLM-LLM instruction generation pipeline (CogVLM + Mistral-7B) extends the simpler GPT-3-based captioning in IP2P with a two-stage hallucination-mitigation design, while the overall model training procedure remains essentially IP2P's formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PIPE dataset (~1M pairs, 1.88M instructions, 1400+ classes) is the first large-scale instruction-based editing dataset with real natural target images and guaranteed source-target consistency by construction, addressing a fundamental weakness of IP2P's synthetic pairs. The 'inverse inpainting' principle is a clean, generalizable recipe that could be adapted to other editing sub-tasks (e.g., style transfer via inverse style removal). Limitations include dependence on inpainting model quality (residual artifacts can leak into training data), the complexity of the multi-stage filtering pipeline, and the fact that the model architecture itself is unmodified SD 1.5, so gains are entirely data-driven. An open question is whether this data-construction strategy transfers to non-object-addition edits where no clean 'inverse' operation exists.
