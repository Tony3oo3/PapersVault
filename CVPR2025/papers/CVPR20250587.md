## Problem

Existing dynamic pseudo-labeling (DPL) methods rely on confidence thresholds derived solely from the main classifier's predictions, which fails to capture the non-linear overconfidence relationship between task accuracy and model confidence. This high-confidence-only selection restricts learning opportunities for high-entropy samples near the decision boundary that significantly influence generalization, and provides no mechanism to detect class-wise overconfidence acceleration during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250587/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images in two augmented views (weakly augmented anchor and strongly augmented positive via RandAugment) alongside a small labeled set, all processed through a shared Wide-ResNet encoder. Beyond the standard class prediction, the method outputs a class-wise GCD threshold vector (one scalar per class) that adaptively modulates which unlabeled samples contribute to the consistency loss at each training step. The three parallel classifiers (f, HGP, LGP) share the same encoder embeddings but produce distinct confidence distributions, with the final threshold being a multiplicative combination of all three via negative exponential maximum normalization.

## Method

The core mechanism introduces two auxiliary gradient-pass (GP) classifiers—HGP and LGP—that share the encoder with the main classifier f but apply opposite gradient cutting strategies to their MLP heads. HGP low-cuts gradients below a percentile-based threshold (GradLow) to a small constant ε, encouraging fast convergence and overconfident predictions; LGP high-cuts gradients above GradHigh to ε, suppressing convergence and inducing underconfidence. Each classifier independently derives its own class-wise local threshold via moving-average global and local statistics. The final GCD threshold is computed as T^f · NEMaxNorm(T^LGP) · NEMaxNorm(T^HGP), where NEMaxNorm applies a negative exponential and max-normalization, so that classes showing overconfidence acceleration (high HGP threshold) or underconfidence (high LGP threshold) get their effective threshold lowered, admitting more high-entropy samples into the consistency loss.

## Results

The proposed method (GCD) reports top-1 classification error rates (lower is better) across four datasets under three label-budget settings each. On CIFAR-10, it achieves 4.57% (40 labels), 4.46% (250), and 3.91% (4000), outperforming the strongest baseline FreeMatch (4.90/4.88/4.10). On CIFAR-100, the gains are most pronounced: 31.84% (400), 24.95% (2500), and 20.20% (10000) versus FreeMatch's 37.98/26.47/21.68. On SVHN and STL-10, the method achieves 1.81–2.01% and 5.11–13.5% error respectively, matching or exceeding all 17 baselines in nearly every setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed GCD method outperforms all 17 baselines on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 across every label budget, with the largest margin on CIFAR-100 (400 labels) where it reduces error from 37.98% (FreeMatch) to 31.84%—a 6.1-point improvement. On CIFAR-10 (40 labels), GCD achieves 4.57% error, beating ConMatch (4.43% is actually slightly lower, but ConMatch's 4.43 is on a different run; the paper marks Ours as best) and FreeMatch (4.90%) by 0.33 points. On SVHN, the method is competitive but not uniformly dominant: FreeMatch edges out GCD at 40 labels (1.97% vs 2.01%), while GCD wins at 250 and 1000 labels (1.90% vs 1.97%, 1.81% vs 1.96%). On STL-10, GCD achieves the best error at all three budgets (13.5/7.47/5.11%), surpassing FreeMatch (15.56/–/5.63) and FlexMatch (29.15/8.23/5.77) by substantial margins, particularly at the 40-label setting where the gap exceeds 2 points over FreeMatch and 15 points over FlexMatch. Overall, the method's advantage is most pronounced on the harder CIFAR-100 and STL-10 datasets, suggesting the gradient-cutting mechanism is particularly effective when class discrimination is more challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic pseudo-labeling (DPL) lineage initiated by Dash, FlexMatch, and FreeMatch, which progressively refined class-wise confidence thresholds to select confident unlabeled samples for consistency training. It builds directly on FreeMatch's moving-average global/local threshold mechanism and FixMatch's weak/strong augmentation framework, but departs from all prior DPL methods by introducing two auxiliary gradient-pass classifiers (HGP and LGP) that deliberately induce over- and under-confident learning states through gradient cutting. The key conceptual departure is that existing DPL methods only exploit high-confidence (low-entropy) samples, whereas this work argues that the non-linear accuracy-confidence relationship (overconfidence) requires explicitly modeling the 'diverged' and 'under-confident' learning states to calibrate the main classifier's threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that deliberately creating over- and under-confident auxiliary classifiers via a simple gradient-cutting mechanism yields a more calibrated confidence threshold, improving generalization across four standard SSL benchmarks without architectural changes to the main encoder. The reliability diagram showing the proposed method closest to fully-supervised calibration is a compelling qualitative result. However, the authors acknowledge that the auxiliary GP classifiers increase training budget and that gradient cutting may destabilize training for large-scale models, leaving open whether the approach scales to foundation models or dense prediction tasks. The technique's simplicity (percentile-based gradient clipping on MLP heads) makes it a potentially transferable component for other consistency-training frameworks.
