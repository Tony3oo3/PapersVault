## Problem

Transformer-based diffusion models (DiT, U-ViT, PixArt) achieve strong generative quality but suffer from significant computational overhead and latency due to self-attention, making them impractical for real-time or resource-constrained applications. This paper asks whether a purely 3x3-convolutional diffusion model—exploiting the hardware efficiency of Winograd-accelerated convolutions—can match or surpass transformer-based diffusion models in generation quality while maintaining a substantial speed advantage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252442/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in the latent space of a pretrained VAE (sd-vae-ft-ema), taking a 4x-downsampled noisy latent tensor and an ImageNet class label as input, and producing a denoised latent that is decoded back to a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image. Conditioning is applied via class-embedding tables injected into the network, with classifier-free guidance (cfg) used at inference for conditional generation. All experiments use the standard DiT training protocol with a global batch size of 256 and AdamW optimizer.

## Method

DiC replaces all self-attention in the denoiser with a U-Net hourglass architecture built entirely from 3×3 stride-1 convolution blocks (two convs + GroupNorm + GELU + residual), where the encoder-decoder downsampling/upsampling hierarchy expands the effective receptive field to compensate for the narrow local scope of 3×3 kernels. Dense block-wise skip connections are replaced with sparse (strided) skips that connect only every few blocks, reducing channel-concatenation overhead at scale. Conditioning is redesigned with three improvements: stage-specific embedding tables so each encoder/decoder stage receives an independent condition vector matched to its channel dimension, mid-block injection (condition added to the second convolution rather than the first), and DiT-style AdaLN conditional gating for dynamic channel-wise scaling. The model is trained with the standard SiT flow-matching objective on ImageNet latents, and at the largest scale (DiC-H, 1.03B params) it achieves FID 2.25 at 2M iterations with ~2.4× the throughput of DiT-XL/2, while the Winograd-optimized FLOPs are roughly half the nominal count.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 (400K iterations, no CFG), DiC-XL achieves FID 13.11 and IS 100.15, substantially outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 20.05, IS 66.74) at comparable FLOPs (116.1G vs 118.6G). The larger DiC-H model further reduces FID to 11.36 (IS 106.52). In terms of throughput, DiC-XL reaches 313.7 samples/s on a single A100, approximately 4.7× faster than DiT-XL/2 (66.8). With classifier-free guidance and 2M training iterations, DiC-H achieves FID 2.25, matching DiT-XL/2's 2.27 (which required 7M iterations) while maintaining 2.4× higher throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiC-XL outperforms all five transformer-based baselines on both FID and IS at comparable FLOPs: it beats DiT-XL/2 by 6.94 FID points (13.11 vs 20.05) and U-ViT-XL by 5.24 points (13.11 vs 18.35), with the largest margin over DiffiT-XL/2 (13.11 vs 36.86). The throughput advantage is even more striking—DiC-XL runs at 313.7 samples/s versus 64–73 for all baselines, a 4.3–4.9× speedup attributable to Winograd-optimized 3×3 convolutions. At the scaled-up setting with CFG (Table 9), DiC-H achieves FID 2.25 in 2M iterations, slightly beating DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27 at 7M iterations) and U-ViT-H (FID 2.29 at 500K with batch 1024), while maintaining 2.4× the throughput of DiT-XL/2. Across all model scales (S, B, XL), DiC consistently surpasses the corresponding DiT variant by 8–7 FID points, confirming the advantage is not scale-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant transformer-based diffusion paradigm established by DiT, U-ViT, and PixArt, which replaced convolutional U-Nets with full self-attention for scalability. Instead, it repositions the simplest convolutional primitive—stride-1 3×3 Conv—as the sole building block, drawing architectural inspiration from classical U-Net hourglass designs and efficiency insights from RepVGG. It borrows the AdaLN conditional gating from DiT and the flow-based training framework from SiT, but restructures conditioning with stage-specific embeddings and mid-block injection tailored to the encoder-decoder hierarchy. The key conceptual departure is showing that the receptive-field limitation of 3×3 convolutions can be overcome by hierarchical downsampling rather than by global attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiC challenges the widely held assumption that self-attention is necessary for high-quality, scalable diffusion generation, offering a 4–5× throughput advantage (313.7 vs. ~66 for DiT-XL/2) while achieving substantially lower FID across all model scales. This makes real-time or resource-constrained diffusion inference practically feasible, opening the door to on-device and large-scale deployment scenarios where transformers are prohibitive. Open questions include whether the advantage extends to text-to-image, video generation, and much larger resolutions where the quadratic cost of attention becomes most acute, and whether the hourglass + sparse-skip design generalizes beyond class-conditional ImageNet. The paper is limited to ImageNet 256×256 and 512×512 class-conditional generation, so broader applicability remains to be demonstrated.
