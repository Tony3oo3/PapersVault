## Problem

Adapting pre-trained Vision Transformers for downstream tasks in privacy-sensitive domains (healthcare, finance) is blocked by a dual constraint: clients cannot share raw data, and model owners cannot release the full pre-trained ViT due to IP and computational cost. Existing split learning leaks data through intermediate representations vulnerable to reconstruction attacks, while offsite tuning fully exposes the model to the client. The paper asks how to achieve effective few-shot adaptation while simultaneously protecting both the client's data and the server's model parameters.

## Input / Output

The client holds a small labeled set (3/5/10 images per class from CIFAR-100, Places365, or DomainNet-Clipart) and receives an 8-bit quantized frontend (2/3 of the ViT backbone) from the server. The client extracts patch-level representations (dimension (N+1)×d) through the noisy quantized frontend, augments them via patch retrieval, adds Laplace noise, and uploads only these noisy representations. The server produces an adapted task module (single or double linear layer) and sends its predictions back to the client for loss computation, so labels never leave the client. The final output is a class label per input image.

## Method

SA splits the pre-trained ViT into a frontend and backend, then quantizes the frontend to 8-bit using Hilbert Transform-based OOD data augmentation (frequency-domain phase shift with amplitude replacement) to ensure cross-distribution generalization when the client data differs from the server's public dataset. On the client side, a bi-level noise mechanism injects Gaussian noise into the quantized frontend parameters (model-level) and Laplace noise into the extracted representations (data-level), making the representations unrecoverable by the server. To combat few-shot overfitting, SA performs patch retrieval augmentation: for each representation, 50 randomly selected patches are replaced with the most cosine-similar patches from other samples, generating diverse augmented representations. On the server, the backend is pre-tuned via OOD quantization-aware tuning with representation-mixup (interpolating dequantized and original frontend outputs), and the final adaptation trains only the task module on high-level features from the noisy augmented representations, with loss computed client-side to protect labels.

## Results

On the 5-shot adaptation setting, SA achieves 81.98% accuracy on CIFAR-100, 35.31% on Places365, and 65.03% on DomainNet-Clipart, outperforming the closest baseline (Split Learning) by 2.35, 4.82, and 1.08 points respectively. In the 10-shot setting, SA reaches 85.45% (CIFAR-100), 39.26% (Places365), and 71.13% (DomainNet-Clipart). SA also requires the lowest client-side cost: only 2233 MB GPU memory and 2.5 minutes of computation, compared to 4896–10302 MB and 15–66 minutes for other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA outperforms Split Learning (the closest competitor) on 8 out of 9 dataset-shot combinations, with the largest margins on Places365 (e.g., +4.82 pts at 5-shot) and CIFAR-100 (e.g., +2.35 pts at 5-shot); the sole exception is DomainNet-Clipart 3-shot where Split Learning edges ahead by 1.88 pts. SA substantially surpasses Linear Probing by 2.19–3.17 points across all settings, and dramatically outperforms Fine Tuning, LN-TUNE, and Offsite Tuning, which suffer severe overfitting in few-shot regimes (e.g., LN-TUNE drops to 13.18% on CIFAR-100 3-shot vs. SA's 76.24%). In terms of privacy, SA additionally defends against FORA data reconstruction attacks (SSIM 0.23 vs. 0.80 for Split Learning on CIFAR-100) while incurring the lowest client-side memory (2233 MB) and computation (2.5 min) among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends split learning (SL) by replacing the full-precision frontend sent to the client with a low-bit quantized version, thereby protecting model IP in addition to data privacy. It departs from offsite tuning, which sends a simplified emulator to the client, by keeping the backend on the server and minimizing client computation. The Hilbert Transform-based OOD augmentation and patch retrieval augmentation are novel components that address the performance degradation introduced by quantization and noise, respectively, in a way not present in prior SL or offsite tuning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SA addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying pre-trained ViTs to sensitive domains (healthcare, finance) where both data and model IP must remain protected, and it does so with the lowest client-side computation cost among all compared methods (2.5 min vs. 15–66 min). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-ViT architectures, whether lower-bit quantization (4-bit or below) remains viable, and how the method performs under fully adversarial (rather than semi-honest) server or client assumptions. The reliance on a server-side dataset with relevant semantics also limits applicability in domains where no public proxy data exists.
