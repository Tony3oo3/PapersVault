## Problem

Token reduction (TR) techniques reduce inference cost in Vision Transformers but cause attention heads to collapse into homogeneous distributions and discard high-frequency components, severely degrading pixel-level tasks like segmentation that depend on diverse attention patterns and fine-grained spatial details. Prior TR-PEFT methods (e.g., Dynamic-Tuning, CoDA) couple the adapter with the token reduction module, failing to independently preserve spatial structure and restore the lost representational diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251982/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 518×518 pixels, processed by a frozen DINOv2-B backbone into patch tokens (plus class and register tokens). It produces a binary segmentation mask at the original spatial resolution. During inference, the learnable mask dynamically selects a subset of patch tokens (default activation rate 0.5) to pass through the FFN, reducing FLOPs from ~117G to ~91G while the full token sequence is retained for the pixel decoder.

## Method

The paper proposes a decoupled architecture where a PEFT module (adapter) processes the full token sequence for task adaptation, while a separate lightweight token compensator (depth-wise 3×3 conv + low-rank 1×1 convs) processes only patch tokens to preserve spatial structure and restore high-frequency components. Token reduction is implemented via a learnable binary mask generated by a 3×3 convolution over reshaped patch tokens, with Gumbel noise for differentiability during training and hard thresholding at inference. A key technical insight is applying the mask after the FFN rather than before, preventing perturbation of pre-trained bias weights that otherwise causes convergence failure in the PEFT setting. Training uses a composite loss combining BCE+DICE segmentation loss, an Ada rate regularizer controlling token activation, and cosine distillation toward the pre-trained model to further recover high-frequency representations.

## Results

On the DUTS salient object segmentation benchmark, the proposed method with distillation (Ours†) achieves a weighted F-measure (Fβw) of 0.901 with only 91.54 GFLOPs, nearly matching AdaptFormer (0.906) which uses no token reduction and 118.70 GFLOPs. On the medical Kvasir polyp segmentation dataset, Ours† reaches Fβw = 0.940, surpassing all non-TR PEFT baselines (best: LoRA at 0.936). Across all six datasets, the method with TR consistently outperforms the Dynamic-Tuning (DyT) baseline by 3–6 points in Fβw while also reducing FLOPs from 95.51G to 90.83G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary TR baseline DyT [65], the proposed method wins on all six datasets in Fβw, with the largest gains on CUHK (0.793 vs 0.735, +5.8 pts) and Kvasir (0.938 vs 0.897, +4.1 pts), while also reducing FLOPs by ~5 GFLOPs. Among non-TR PEFT baselines, Ours† (0.901 Fβw on DUTS) nearly matches AdaptFormer (0.906) and LoRA (0.897) at ~23% fewer FLOPs, and clearly outperforms VPT (0.896) and EVP (0.887). On the medical Kvasir dataset, Ours† (0.940) surpasses even the best non-TR method LoRA (0.936). In the zero-shot camouflaged object segmentation evaluation (Table 3), the method trained only on DUTS outperforms both zero-shot baselines CaMF [29] and GenSAM [24] on S-measure and E-measure for both CAMO and COD10K, though it trails the supervised ZoomNeXt [45] (0.857 vs 0.730 Fβw on CAMO) as expected given the supervision gap. On CUHK defocus blur detection, Ours† (0.755) lags behind VPT (0.800) and AdaptFormer (0.767), indicating that the token reduction strategy is less beneficial for this particular task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token reduction (TR) line of work from DynamicViT, CoDA, and Dynamic-Tuning by identifying a previously underexplored failure mode: TR collapses attention diversity (measured via normalized mutual information) and erodes high-frequency spectral components in ViT representations. While prior methods like CoDA and Dynamic-Tuning couple the adapter and TR into a single parallel module, this paper decouples them into an independent PEFT module and a dedicated token compensator (depth-wise convolution), and shifts the masking operation from before to after the FFN to prevent bias perturbation during PEFT. The learnable Gumbel-Softmax mask for dynamic token selection follows DynamicViT's sparsification paradigm but is adapted for the frozen-backbone PEFT setting where convergence is more fragile.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pixel-level tasks (segmentation, depth estimation) are precisely where TR is most needed due to long token sequences yet most damaging, making this a practically important gap. The Fourier and NMI diagnostics provide a reusable analytical framework for understanding how TR degrades representations, which could inform future efficient-ViT designs beyond segmentation. However, the evaluation is limited to DINOv2-B and binary segmentation; whether the approach generalizes to multi-class semantic segmentation, larger backbones, or non-adapter PEFT methods (e.g., LoRA) remains open. The zero-shot generalization test, while encouraging, covers only one task (camouflaged object segmentation) and would benefit from broader validation.
