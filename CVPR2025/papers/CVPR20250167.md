## Problem

Previous text-to-motion generation methods oversimplify representations by treating each pose as a single vector (ignoring spatial interactions between skeletal joints) and compressing entire sentences into one conditioning vector (losing word-level nuances), limiting text-motion alignment. Additionally, pretrained motion diffusion models lack interpretable intermediate representations, so zero-shot editing requires manual masks, optimization, or fine-tuning rather than simple text-based manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250167/cropped_page7_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language text prompt as input and produces a 3D human motion sequence represented as joint positions over N frames. Internally, motion is encoded into a skeleto-temporal latent space with 7 atomic joints (root, spine, head, both arms, both legs) and compressed temporal frames (N′ < N), reducing the diffusion sampling dimensionality. For editing, the same text prompt plus a source motion's cross-attention maps serve as input, with no additional user-provided masks or optimization required.

## Method

SALAD first trains a VAE using skeleto-temporal convolution (STConv) and topology-preserving pooling (STPool) layers to build a compact latent space that decouples joint and frame dimensions while facilitating information exchange between adjacent components. A transformer-based denoiser then operates in this latent space, with each layer containing separate temporal attention (frame-wise), skeletal attention (joint-wise), and cross-attention (word-to-skeleto-temporal-unit) blocks, each modulated by FiLM conditioning on the diffusion timestep. The diffusion model uses v-prediction parametrization for improved stability at high noise levels, trained with classifier-free guidance. For zero-shot editing, the paper modulates the learned cross-attention maps via four strategies—word swap, prompt refinement, attention re-weighting, and attention mirroring—enabling text-driven motion editing without any additional training, optimization, or manual masking.

## Results

On HumanML3D, SALAD achieves the best R-Precision (Top-1: 0.581, Top-2: 0.769, Top-3: 0.857) and best MM-Dist (2.649) among all compared methods, with a competitive FID of 0.076 (second-best after MoMask's 0.045). On KIT-ML, SALAD again leads in R-Precision (Top-1: 0.477, Top-3: 0.828) and MM-Dist (2.585), while its FID of 0.296 trails ReMoDiffuse (0.155). In the user study for motion editing, SALAD scores 4.60/5 on preservation, 4.65/5 on semantic alignment, and 4.60/5 on overall quality, substantially outperforming MDM (3.20–3.73) and MotionFix (3.36–3.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALAD achieves the best R-Precision (all three levels) and MM-Dist on both HumanML3D and KIT-ML, outperforming all nine baselines on text-motion alignment. On generation quality (FID), SALAD is second-best on HumanML3D (0.076 vs. MoMask's 0.045) and second-best on KIT-ML (0.296 vs. ReMoDiffuse's 0.155), indicating a slight quality trade-off for its superior alignment. Against autoregressive baselines (T2M, AttT2M, ParCo, MoMask), SALAD leads by 6–11 points in R-Precision Top-1 on HumanML3D while maintaining competitive FID. Against diffusion-based baselines (MDM, MLD, MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse), the margins are even larger: MDM lags by 26 points in R-Precision Top-1 and has an FID of 0.544 vs. SALAD's 0.076. In the motion editing user study, SALAD outperforms MDM (which uses joint masking) and MotionFix (a dedicated editing method) by roughly 1 point on all three Likert-scale criteria, despite requiring no explicit masks or fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALAD builds directly on skeleton-aware networks (Aberman et al., Yan et al.) for its VAE, adopting skeleto-temporal convolutions and topology-preserving pooling to construct a structured motion latent space, and on Prompt-to-Prompt (Hertz et al.) for its attention-modulation editing strategies. It departs from the dominant text-to-motion paradigm (MDM, MotionDiffuse, MoMask) that treats each pose as a single vector, instead explicitly disentangling skeletal and temporal dimensions throughout both the VAE and the denoiser. For editing, it moves beyond prior motion-editing methods (FLAME, MDM, MotionFix) that rely on manual joint masks, optimization, or fine-tuning, by repurposing the cross-attention maps already present in the diffusion denoiser as a training-free editing interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling joint-level and frame-level interactions in a diffusion denoiser yields substantially better text-motion alignment (R-Precision Top-1: 0.581 vs. 0.521 for the prior best) while maintaining competitive FID, and that the resulting cross-attention maps are interpretable enough to support zero-shot motion editing without any additional training or user-specified masks. The VAE's extreme parameter efficiency (0.16M vs. 19.44M for MoMask) while achieving superior reconstruction suggests that structured inductive biases can replace brute-force capacity in motion representation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-character interactions, longer sequences, and editing of real (rather than generated) motions via diffusion inversion, and how to reconcile the strong text-fidelity with the somewhat limited diversity and multimodality scores.
