## Problem

Existing video VAEs that inflate a pre-trained image VAE into a 3D causal structure suffer from two fundamental issues: (1) initializing from a same-dimension image VAE provides insufficient spatial compression headroom for 4× temporal compression, causing slow convergence and degraded quality, and (2) unidirectional causal convolution creates unequal information flow within a frame group, making the first frame reconstruct poorly and producing visible flickering. Additionally, no open-source high-resolution benchmark with diverse motion speeds exists for comprehensive video VAE evaluation.

## Input / Output

The model accepts video clips of variable length (evaluated on 17 frames) at resolutions up to 1080P (1920×1280) and produces a compressed latent tensor with 4× temporal and 8×8 spatial compression (FCR 4×8×8) at configurable latent channel counts Z ∈ {4, 8, 16}. The first frame is treated as an independent keyframe, and subsequent frames are processed in non-overlapping groups of 4. For long-video inference, a cache mechanism stores the last P_t frames of intermediate feature maps at each layer, enabling exact one-step-equivalent reconstruction without the semantic bias introduced by overlap-based clipping.

## Method

IV-VAE introduces a dual-branch Keyframe-based Temporal Compression (KTC) architecture where a 2D branch initialized from a lower-dimension (Z/2) image VAE handles keyframe spatial compression, while a 3D branch initialized via center inflation handles temporal motion compression; the two branches each output C_out/2 channels, are RMSNorm-normalized, and concatenated, initially achieving 2× temporal compression with strong image priors. Within the 3D branch, Group Causal Convolution (GCConv) replaces standard causal convolution by applying bidirectional standard convolution within each 4-frame group and causal zero-padding between groups, ensuring equal inter-frame information interaction while preserving causal logic across groups. Temporal Motion Perception Enhancement (TMPE) adds parallel atrous convolutions (borrowed from ASPP) in the encoder's final layer and expands attention modules from 2 to 7 (applied after full compression) to enlarge the receptive field for high-resolution motion capture. The overall UNet-based network uses RMSNorm instead of GroupNorm to preserve temporal causality, and a 3D GAN discriminator is added in the final training stage for perceptual quality.

## Results

On Kinetics-600 with Z=4 latent channels, IV-VAE achieves FVD 8.01, PSNR 34.29, SSIM 0.9281, and LPIPS 0.0521, outperforming OD-VAE (FVD 10.69) by 2.68 FVD with less than half the parameters (107M vs. 239M). At Z=16, IV-VAE reaches FVD 2.97 and PSNR 39.02 on Kinetics-600, surpassing CogX-VAE (FVD 3.17, PSNR 38.38) across all metrics. On the MotionHD benchmark at 1080P, IV-VAE (Z=16) achieves PSNR 40.33 and LPIPS 0.027, extending its lead over CogX-VAE from 0.28 PSNR at 480P to 1.65 PSNR at 1080P. For video generation with Latte-XL, IV-VAE (Z=4) achieves the best FVD of 541.6 on Kinetics-400 and 118.6 on SkyTimelapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IV-VAE consistently outperforms all five baselines (SVD-VAE, CV-VAE, OS-VAE, OD-VAE, CogX-VAE) across both Kinetics-600 and ActivityNet at every latent channel setting (Z=4, 8, 16). At Z=4, IV-VAE (107M params) beats OD-VAE (239M) by 2.68 FVD on Kinetics-600 and 2.02 FVD on ActivityNet, while using less than half the parameters; against OS-VAE (393M), the FVD gap widens to 11.04 on Kinetics-600. At Z=16, IV-VAE surpasses CogX-VAE (215M) on all four metrics, achieving 0.20 lower FVD, 0.64 higher PSNR, and 0.0059 lower LPIPS on Kinetics-600, with the gap widening at higher resolutions (1.65 PSNR advantage at 1080P on MotionHD). The only case where a baseline leads is SVD-VAE at Z=4, which has no temporal compression (1×8×8 FCR) and thus retains more spatial information, but IV-VAE at Z=8 with 4× temporal compression still surpasses SVD-VAE on all metrics. For video generation via Latte-XL, IV-VAE (Z=4) achieves the best FVD on both Kinetics-400 (541.6 vs. 548.7 for OD-VAE) and SkyTimelapse (118.6 vs. 128.0 for CV-VAE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of 3D causal video VAEs for latent video diffusion (SVD-VAE, OD-VAE, OpenSora VAE, CogVideoX VAE, CV-VAE), which all share the recipe of inflating a pre-trained 2D image VAE into 3D causal convolutions with same-dimension latent channels. It departs from this recipe in two ways: (1) a dual-branch keyframe-based temporal compression (KTC) architecture that initializes one branch from a lower-dimension (Z/2) image VAE and dedicates the other half to temporal compression, and (2) a group causal convolution (GCConv) that replaces standard 3D causal convolution with bidirectional interaction within frame groups while preserving causal logic between groups. The temporal motion perception enhancement (parallel atrous convolutions + expanded attention) draws on ASPP-style receptive-field expansion from segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video VAEs are the compression backbone of every major latent video diffusion model (Sora, CogVideoX, OpenSora, Latte), so improvements in reconstruction quality and temporal consistency directly translate to better generated video. The finding that same-dimension image-VAE initialization is suboptimal challenges a widely adopted practice and offers a simpler, faster-converging alternative. Open questions include whether the dual-branch KTC design generalizes to higher compression ratios (e.g., 8× or 16× temporal), whether GCConv scales to very long sequences without accumulating group-boundary artifacts, and whether the MotionHD benchmark (2000 clips) is sufficiently diverse for robust evaluation. The cache mechanism for long-video inference is a practical contribution that could be adopted broadly.
