## Problem

Existing AVQA methods incorporate question information only at the final reasoning stage and rely on uniform or discrete Top-K frame sampling, which treats the temporal dimension as a set of isolated frames. This dual limitation prevents the model from progressively focusing on question-relevant temporal segments and from capturing fine-grained continuous dynamics that span both consecutive and non-consecutive frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252335/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip with synchronized audio (sampled at 1 fps and split into T non-overlapping 1-second segments) and a free-form text question. Visual features are extracted per segment via CLIP-ViT-L/14 (frame-level [CLS] tokens plus patch-level tokens reduced by ToMe), audio features via VGGish, and question features via the CLIP text encoder (sentence-level [EOT] and word-level tokens). All features are projected to 512 dimensions. The output is a probability distribution over C multiple-choice answer options, trained with cross-entropy loss.

## Method

QA-TIGER introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, a Question-Aware Fusion module injects question context early via a sequence of self-attention and two cross-attention layers per modality (cross-modal attention followed by word-level question attention), producing question-aligned visual and audio features that are further refined at the patch level. Second, a Temporal Integration of Gaussian Experts module implements a Mixture of Experts with E=7 Gaussian distributions per modality, whose centers are spread along the timeline with learned offsets to minimize overlap; a softmax router derived from sentence-level question cross-attention assigns per-expert weights, and each expert applies a Gaussian-weighted transformation to the features, yielding temporally integrated representations that capture both consecutive and non-consecutive question-relevant spans. A final Question-Guided Reasoning stage fuses the temporal audio and visual features with additional question cross-attention before a linear classifier produces the answer.

## Results

On the MUSIC-AVQA test set, QA-TIGER achieves 77.62% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous best method TSPM (76.79%) by 0.83%. The model shows particularly strong performance on complex reasoning sub-tasks: 78.58% on AV-Counting and 72.50% on AV-Local. On MUSIC-AVQA-R (rare/OOD samples), QA-TIGER reaches 67.99% overall without explicit bias handling, and on MUSIC-AVQA-v2.0 balanced test it achieves 76.43%, outperforming LAST-Att (75.44%). Inference time is comparable to TSPM (1.737s vs 1.767s) despite processing full temporal information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QA-TIGER outperforms all 16 compared baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest prior method TSPM [19] (Top-K frame selection), QA-TIGER gains +0.83% on MUSIC-AVQA (77.62 vs 76.79) and +1.69% on MUSIC-AVQA-R (67.99 vs 66.30), with the largest margin on the AV-Counting sub-task (+2.37%). Against APL [23] and LAVISH [24], the gains are +3.09% and +3.16% respectively on MUSIC-AVQA. On MUSIC-AVQA-v2.0, QA-TIGER trained on the balanced set beats LAST-Att [26] by +0.99% on the balanced test (76.43 vs 75.44) and by +1.48% on the biased test (76.57 vs 75.45). Notably, QA-TIGER matches TSPM's inference speed (1.737s vs 1.767s) while processing the full temporal sequence rather than a discrete Top-K subset, and it surpasses all earlier methods (ST-AVQA, COCA, PSTP-Net) by 3–6% on the primary benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines in AVQA: (1) frame sampling, where prior methods use either uniform sampling (ST-AVQA, LAVISH) or discrete Top-K selection (PSTP, TSPM), and (2) question integration, where most models inject question context only at the final reasoning stage. QA-TIGER repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts framework (Shazeer et al., 2017) not for routing token-level computations but for generating multiple question-conditioned Gaussian distributions over the temporal axis, providing continuous rather than discrete temporal grounding. The question-aware fusion module extends the standard cross-attention pattern used in multimodal transformers by applying it sequentially across modalities and question tokens at the encoding stage, rather than deferring question interaction to the prediction head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous Gaussian MoE formulation offers a principled alternative to discrete frame selection that preserves temporal coherence while remaining computationally comparable to Top-K methods (1.737s vs. 1.767s inference). However, all experiments are confined to the MUSIC-AVQA family (music instrument videos), leaving open whether the Gaussian temporal experts generalize to other AVQA domains with different temporal structures (e.g., action recognition, scene understanding). The paper also notes that adaptive expert selection tailored to question type remains future work, and the modest ~0.8% improvement over TSPM on MUSIC-AVQA suggests the gains from continuous temporal modeling may be bounded in this setting.
