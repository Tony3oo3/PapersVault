## Problem

Diffusion-based video generators suffer from appearance drift, where objects gradually mutate or degrade across frames, breaking visual coherence. The authors hypothesize this stems from the absence of explicit spatial tracking supervision at the feature level: training relies solely on denoising score matching in pixel/latent space without guidance on temporal correspondences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251104/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a single reference image (encoded via CLIP) and produces a 24-frame video at 320×576 resolution in the VAE latent space. For tracking, it accepts a video sequence with arbitrary query points on the first frame and outputs dense point trajectories across all frames. Training supervision for tracking uses dense point trajectories derived from optical flow on real videos, balanced between foreground and background via segmentation masks from public datasets.

## Method

Track4Gen augments Stable Video Diffusion with a trainable refiner module (eight stacked 2D convolutions) attached to the upsampler layer of the third decoder block, which projects raw diffusion features into a correspondence-rich space. A differentiable soft-argmax over a cosine-similarity cost volume predicts target point locations, supervised by a Huber-based correspondence loss. Refined features are fed back into the backbone through a trainable zero-convolution layer with stop-gradient, so the refiner acquires correspondence priors without disrupting the generation pathway. Training jointly optimizes the standard v-prediction diffusion loss and the tracking loss (λ=8), finetuning only the temporal transformer blocks, the refiner, and the zero convolution for 20K steps. The key architectural insight is that the third decoder block's upsampler consistently yields the strongest temporal correspondences, and that this joint supervision produces temporally stable features that reduce appearance drift without degrading standard video quality metrics.

## Results

On VBench-I2V (355 images, 24-frame videos at 320×576), Track4Gen achieves the best scores across all five VBench metrics: Subject Consistency 0.9746, Temporal Flickering 0.9806, Motion Smoothness 0.9921, Imaging Quality 0.6835, and Video-Image Alignment 0.9814, along with the lowest FID (26.6) and second-lowest FVD (724). For zero-shot video tracking, Track4Gen features substantially outperform raw SVD features (δ_avg^x: 69.7 vs. 42.4 on DAVIS-480p 24-frame) and approach RAFT optical-flow chaining (73.3). In user studies with 64 participants, Track4Gen is preferred over baselines for both identity preservation and motion naturalness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original SVD*, Track4Gen improves Subject Consistency by +0.021 (0.9746 vs. 0.9535) and Temporal Flickering by +0.034 (0.9806 vs. 0.9464), while also reducing FID from 29.0 to 26.6 and FVD from 776 to 724. Compared to finetuned SVD (same training data, no correspondence loss), Track4Gen gains +0.008 in Subject Consistency and +0.004 in Video-Image Alignment, confirming that the tracking supervision—not merely additional training—drives the improvement. The ablation without the refiner module (Track4Gen w/o refiner) drops Subject Consistency to 0.9506, below even the original SVD*, showing the refiner is critical for preserving generation quality while adding tracking. On zero-shot tracking (Table 2), Track4Gen features (δ_avg^x = 69.7) substantially outperform raw SVD* (42.4) and ZeroScope (46.2) features but remain slightly below RAFT optical-flow chaining (73.3) on DAVIS-480p 24-frame. When combined with DINO-Tracker's test-time adaptation, Track4Gen features (δ_avg^x = 72.5) underperform DINO-Tracker with DINOv2 features (80.4), suggesting the diffusion features are less suited to that particular optimization protocol, though they still remain competitive with supervised trackers like TAP-Net (66.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its backbone, adding a lightweight refiner module and a correspondence tracking loss to the standard v-prediction diffusion objective. It departs from prior work on foundational model features (e.g., DINOv2, diffusion hyperfeatures) that typically post-process or fine-tune features for downstream tasks; instead, Track4Gen trains the generator itself to produce temporally consistent features by jointly optimizing a tracking objective. The paper is the first to exploit the internal features of a video diffusion model for dense point tracking, bridging two communities—video generation and point tracking—that have previously been treated as entirely separate problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Appearance drift remains a persistent and visually jarring failure mode in current video generators, and this paper shows that a relatively simple architectural addition (a refiner module plus a zero-convolution re-routing path) combined with a tracking loss can meaningfully reduce it without degrading standard generation metrics. The dual benefit—improved generation coherence and substantially better zero-shot tracking accuracy (nearly doubling δ_avg^x over raw SVD features)—suggests that feature-space temporal consistency is a useful inductive bias for both tasks. However, the authors note that generated videos exhibit less dynamic motion, and the approach relies on optical-flow-derived pseudo-labels since no dense ground-truth trajectory annotations exist at scale; leveraging recent high-capacity trackers to auto-annotate real videos is identified as a key future direction.
