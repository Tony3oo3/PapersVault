## Problem

Existing PET-CT lung tumor segmentation datasets are small-scale (at most ~7,000 pairs) and almost entirely private, severely limiting model development and reproducibility. Additionally, current Mamba-based segmentation models rely on 2D selective scanning that cannot effectively exploit the complementary regional and structural information between PET (high-contrast metabolic) and CT (high-resolution anatomical) modalities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired PET-CT image slice (both resized to 512×512; CT HU values clipped to [-1200, -200] and normalized to [0,255]; PET converted to SUV and scaled to [0,255]) and produces a pixel-level binary tumor segmentation mask. The two modalities are processed through parallel encoder branches with shared weights, and the output is a single segmentation map per slice pair.

## Method

CIPA is a U-shaped architecture entirely composed of state space models, with two key modules. The Channel-wise Rectification Module (CRM) concatenates PET and CT features along the channel axis, applies a 1D selective SSM across the flattened channel dimension to learn cross-modal correlated representations, and then uses sigmoid-gated channel-wise multiplication to suppress modality-specific noise. The Dynamic Cross-Modality Interaction Module (DCIM) splits the CT feature into regional patches each containing 4×4 local tokens, applies a region Mamba block to low-resolution PET regional tokens to capture global lesion position, and a local Mamba block to CT local tokens to model fine-grained structural dependencies; PET regional tokens are then added to each corresponding CT local token to transfer position information. The encoder uses VSS blocks (from VMamba) with shared weights across both branches, and the decoder uses CVSS blocks (from Sigma), trained end-to-end with AdamW and cosine annealing over 50 epochs.

## Results

On the PCLT20K test set, CIPA achieves 63.81% IoU, 77.91% F1, 89.01% accuracy, and 17.74 HD95, outperforming all 12 compared methods across every metric. The closest competitor, Sigma, reaches 63.26% IoU and 22.56 HD95, yielding a 0.55-point IoU gap and a 4.82-point HD95 advantage for CIPA. On the publicly available STS dataset, CIPA also leads with 60.33% IoU, 75.26% F1, and 86.03% accuracy, surpassing the next-best GeminiFusion by 1.31 IoU points. CIPA maintains a competitive computational budget of 76.18 GFLOPs and 54.57M parameters, comparable to Sigma (76.18G / 48.28M) and well below CEN (524.75G / 118.11M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIPA outperforms all 12 baselines on every reported metric for PCLT20K. The nearest competitor is Sigma (63.26% IoU, 22.56 HD95), which CIPA surpasses by 0.55 IoU points and 4.82 HD95 points; GeminiFusion (62.49% IoU, 20.70 HD95) trails by 1.32 IoU but has a slightly lower HD95. Among CNN/Transformer-based methods, CMNeXt (63.12% IoU) and nnSAM (62.93% IoU) are the strongest, yet both fall 0.7–0.9 IoU points short of CIPA. On the STS dataset, CIPA leads GeminiFusion (59.02% IoU) by 1.31 points and Sigma (58.14% IoU) by 2.19 points. Notably, CIPA achieves its best results with the Mamba backbone (54.57M params, 76.18G FLOPs), outperforming its own ResNet-50 variant (53.54% IoU) by 10.27 IoU points and its Swin-T variant (62.82% IoU) by 0.99 points, while using fewer parameters than both. Swin-Unet and TransUNet perform poorly (36.41% and 51.72% IoU respectively), with HD95 values exceeding 100, indicating severe boundary errors on this challenging dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba/SSM-based segmentation line (U-Mamba, SegMamba, Swin-UMamba, VMamba) to the multi-modal PET-CT setting, where prior SSM work has been limited to single-modality inputs. It departs from VMamba's 2D selective scan by introducing a channel-wise rectification module (CRM) that explicitly models cross-modal channel correlations, and a dynamic cross-modality interaction module (DCIM) that assigns region-level Mamba to PET (for lesion localization) and local-level Mamba to CT (for structural detail), addressing the complementary strengths of each modality. The broader multi-modal PET-CT fusion literature (MFCNet, MoSNet, DFCN) informs the fusion strategy, but those methods rely on CNN or Transformer backbones rather than state space models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PCLT20K dataset (21,930 pairs, 605 patients, publicly available) is the most significant practical contribution, being roughly 3× larger than the previous largest lung PET-CT dataset and the first to be openly accessible, directly addressing the field's long-standing data scarcity bottleneck. The method achieves modest but consistent SOTA improvements (e.g., 63.81 vs. 63.26 IoU over Sigma; 17.74 vs. 22.56 HD95), with the HD95 gap suggesting better boundary fidelity. Open questions include whether the Mamba-based architecture's advantage persists at larger scale or with 3D volumetric inputs, and whether the cross-modal Mamba assignment strategy (region→PET, local→CT) generalizes to other organ pairs or imaging modalities.
