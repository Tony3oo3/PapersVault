## Problem

Existing explainable video anomaly detection (VAD) methods that leverage vision-language models either decouple description and reasoning into separate VLM + external LLM pipelines (adding inference overhead) or require instruction tuning on fine-grained, frame-level annotated datasets (adding substantial annotation and training cost). The paper asks whether a frozen VLM can be adapted for explainable VAD without any parameter modification or external reasoning modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252626/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, VERA consumes uniformly sampled video frames (8 per video) paired only with coarse video-level binary labels (normal/abnormal), requiring no frame-level annotations. During inference, the video is divided into 10-second segments with 8 uniformly sampled frames each, and the frozen VLM is queried per segment with the learned guiding questions embedded in a prompt template. The output is a continuous frame-level anomaly score in [0,1] for every frame, plus a one-sentence natural-language explanation generated by the VLM during reasoning.

## Method

VERA treats a set of m natural-language guiding questions as learnable parameters and optimizes them via verbalized learning: a learner VLM performs binary video classification on sampled frames using the current questions, while an optimizer VLM reads the batch predictions against ground-truth labels (alongside the actual video frames) and rewrites the questions to better characterize anomaly patterns. No VLM weights are modified at any point. At inference, VERA computes frame-level scores in a coarse-to-fine pipeline: (1) segment-level binary scores from the VLM prompted with the learned questions, (2) scene-context ensembling by retrieving top-K visually similar segments via ImageBind cosine similarity and softmax-weighted aggregation, and (3) frame-level refinement via Gaussian temporal smoothing and a position-weighting function that suppresses scores near video boundaries to model anomaly progression.

## Results

VERA achieves 86.55% AUC on UCF-Crime and 88.26% AUC on XD-Violence, setting new state-of-the-art among explainable VAD methods. On UCF-Crime, VERA outperforms the strongest explainable baseline VADor (85.90%) by 0.65 points and LAVAD (80.28%) by 6.27 points, while remaining competitive with top non-explainable methods like CLIP-TSA (87.58%) and SSRL (87.43%). On XD-Violence, VERA surpasses LAVAD (85.36%) by 2.9 points and LLaVA-1.5 (79.62%) by 8.64 points, and dominates all non-explainable baselines by a wide margin (next best: RareAnom at 68.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VERA outperforms all explainable VAD baselines on both datasets: on UCF-Crime it beats VADor (85.90%) by 0.65 points, Holmes-VAD (84.61%) by 1.94 points, LAVAD (80.28%) by 6.27 points, and LLaVA-1.5 (72.84%) by 13.71 points; on XD-Violence it beats LAVAD (85.36%) by 2.9 points and LLaVA-1.5 (79.62%) by 8.64 points. Against non-explainable methods, VERA is competitive on UCF-Crime (86.55% vs. CLIP-TSA 87.58%, SSRL 87.43%, MGFN 86.98%) while uniquely providing text explanations, and dominates on XD-Violence where the best non-explainable method (RareAnom) reaches only 68.33%. The zero-shot baselines from LAVAD (ZS CLIP, ZS IMAGEBIND) perform poorly (38–59% AUC), confirming that simple prompting of frozen VLMs is insufficient without VERA's learned guiding questions. VERA's key advantage over instruction-tuned methods (Holmes-VAD, VADor) is that it requires no model parameter modification or fine-grained frame-level annotations, relying solely on coarsely labeled video-level data.

## Relation to Prior Work

VERA extends the Verbalized Machine Learning (VML) framework of Xiao et al. [45]—which optimizes verbal rules through learner-optimizer LLM interactions for static image tasks—to the video domain, introducing temporality and scene dynamics as new challenges. It departs from two dominant VAD-with-VLMs paradigms: (1) pipeline approaches that decouple a frozen VLM's captioning from an external LLM's reasoning (e.g., LAVAD), and (2) instruction-tuning methods that fine-tune VLMs on frame-annotated datasets (e.g., Holmes-VAD, VADor). VERA eliminates both by treating anomaly-characterization questions as learnable parameters optimized purely through verbal feedback, requiring only coarsely labeled video-level annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: VERA adapts a frozen VLM for explainable VAD without parameter modification, external reasoning modules, or fine-grained annotation, making it a lightweight and scalable alternative to IT-based pipelines. The learned questions are natural-language artifacts that can be inspected, edited, and (with caveats) transferred across models and datasets, offering a form of portable VAD knowledge. However, transferability across VLM architectures remains limited (Table 8 shows notable AUC drops), and the approach still depends on a capable VLM backbone. Open questions include whether a universal set of guiding questions can elicit reasoning across heterogeneous VLM structures, and whether the verbalized-learning paradigm generalizes beyond VAD to other weakly-supervised video tasks.
