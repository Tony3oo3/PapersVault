## Problem

Diffusion-based conditional generation methods require task-specific architectural designs and learning objectives, making it difficult to build a single unified model for diverse tasks like image editing, depth-to-image, and segmentation-to-image. While autoregressive models offer a naturally unified token-based framework, it remains unproven whether the standard next-token prediction paradigm can handle conditional image generation tasks beyond text-to-image synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252092/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a conditioning image (natural RGB, depth map, canny edge, or segmentation mask at 512×512) and a free-form text instruction. The conditioning image is encoded via a shared VQ-VAE encoder into 1024 discrete tokens (16× downsampling), while the text is mapped to latent embeddings by a frozen text encoder. The text phrasing is modality-dependent: for sparse conditions (depth, edge, mask) the prompt is prefixed with 'Given the [modality], generate the image following the instruction:', whereas for natural image editing only the instruction is used. Output is a sequence of 1024 VQ-VAE token indices decoded into a 512×512 RGB image.

## Method

EditAR extends LlamaGen (a Llama2-based autoregressive transformer) by prefilling the model with VQ-VAE token indices from the conditioning image, using separate positional embeddings to distinguish the control sequence from the target sequence. A DINOv2 distillation loss is introduced, where an alignment convolutional layer projects the autoregressive model's last hidden-layer features to match DINOv2's feature space, injecting general visual knowledge that improves text-to-image alignment. Training jointly optimizes cross-entropy on next-token prediction plus the distillation loss (λ=0.5), with 5% dropout on text, image, or both conditions to preserve unconditional generation. At inference, classifier-free guidance (η=3.0) is applied to both the image and text conditions to balance reconstruction fidelity against instruction following.

## Results

On image editing (PIE-Bench), EditAR achieves a background preservation distance of 39.43 and whole-image CLIP similarity of 24.87, the best overall among feed-forward methods, while narrowing the gap with inversion-based approaches like PnPInversion (distance 11.65). On image translation, EditAR achieves the best FID across all three tasks: 15.97 (depth-to-image), 13.91 (canny-to-image), and 16.13 (segmentation-to-image), outperforming unified baselines UniControl (18.66/19.94/—) and UniControlNet (22.27/17.14/—). However, single-modality methods like ControlNet++ still lead on reconstruction metrics (e.g., depth RMSE 28.32 vs. 34.93, segmentation mIOU 34.56 vs. 22.62).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among feed-forward editing baselines, EditAR outperforms InstructPix2Pix (distance 107.43, whole CLIP 23.49) and MGIE (distance 67.41, whole CLIP 24.28) on overall background preservation and achieves a competitive edited CLIP score of 21.87 (vs. InstructPix2Pix's 22.20). Against inversion-based methods (Prompt-to-Prompt, Null-text Inversion, PnPInversion), EditAR trails significantly on reconstruction fidelity (distance 39.43 vs. 11.65 for PnPInversion) but avoids the need for a source prompt and per-image optimization. On image translation, EditAR beats both unified diffusion baselines—UniControl and UniControlNet—on FID across all three modalities (e.g., depth FID 15.97 vs. 18.66 and 22.27; canny FID 13.91 vs. 19.94 and 17.14). However, single-modality specialists like ControlNet++ still lead on reconstruction metrics (depth RMSE 28.32 vs. 34.93, canny SSIM 57.06 vs. 48.11, segmentation mIOU 34.56 vs. 22.62), reflecting the trade-off of a unified model handling multiple conditioning types simultaneously. ControlNet (single-modality) also beats EditAR on canny FID (14.73 vs. 13.91 is actually EditAR winning) and depth FID (17.76 vs. 15.97, EditAR winning), confirming EditAR's FID advantage even against task-specific models.

## Relation to Prior Work

EditAR extends LlamaGen, a text-to-image autoregressive model built on the Llama2 architecture, by prefilling the transformer with image tokens from a conditioning input and introducing a DINOv2-based distillation loss to inject visual knowledge into the token-prediction process. This departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm (ControlNet, InstructPix2Pix, Prompt-to-Prompt) where each conditioning modality typically requires a separate adapter or inversion procedure. Conceptually, it follows the broader trend of treating image generation as sequence modeling (VQ-VAE + autoregressive transformer) but is the first to apply the vanilla next-token prediction paradigm to both image editing and sparse-signal translation tasks within a single unified model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that a single autoregressive model with standard next-token prediction can handle diverse conditional generation tasks—editing, depth-to-image, edge-to-image, and segmentation-to-image—without task-specific architectural modifications, potentially simplifying deployment from many specialized diffusion models to one. However, EditAR still trails the best inversion-based methods on background preservation metrics and underperforms ControlNet++ on segmentation mIOU, suggesting that the AR paradigm has not yet fully closed the quality gap for content-preserving edits. Open questions include whether this approach scales to higher resolutions, more complex multi-step editing, and whether the distillation loss generalizes to other foundation-model backbones beyond DINOv2.
