## Problem

Existing identity-preserving text-to-video (IPT2V) methods either require per-identity finetuning (DreamBooth, LoRA-style) or are limited to U-Net architectures that cannot transfer to the emerging DiT-based video models. The paper identifies two specific DiT deficiencies from frequency-domain analysis: (1) DiT lacks the skip-connection mechanism that U-Net uses to aggregate low-frequency features for pixel-level prediction, and (2) DiT has limited perception of high-frequency information critical for facial identity markers. No prior work has addressed how to inject identity control signals into DiT in a frequency-aware manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference face image (cropped and aligned via Crop & Align) and a free-form text prompt describing expressions, actions, and backgrounds. It produces a 49-frame video at 480×720 resolution (extracted at stride 3 from training clips). The reference image is consumed in two parallel pathways: one through facial key-point extraction converted to an RGB image, and another through a dual-tower feature extractor (ArcFace backbone + CLIP encoder fused by Q-Former). No per-identity finetuning is required at inference; the model is fully tuning-free.

## Method

ConsisID decomposes identity into low-frequency signals (facial key points + reference image, concatenated with the noise latent to ease DiT convergence) and high-frequency signals (intrinsic identity features from ArcFace penultimate layer + CLIP features, fused via Q-Former and injected through cross-attention inside each DiT attention block as Z'_i = Z_i + Attention(Q_i^v, K_i^f, V_i^f)). A coarse-to-fine training strategy first trains with only the global (low-frequency) extractor, then introduces the local (high-frequency) extractor. Two dynamic losses improve generalization: a mask loss that randomly restricts supervision to face regions (probability α=0.5) to avoid background noise, and a cross-face loss that randomly substitutes the reference image with a face from outside the training frames plus Gaussian noise (probability β=0.5) to prevent copy-paste overfitting. The entire pipeline is built on CogVideoX-5B with 1.8k training steps at batch size 80.

## Results

On the identity-preserving text-to-video (IPT2V) benchmark, ConsisID achieves FaceSim-Arc of 0.58 and FaceSim-Cur of 0.60, substantially outperforming ID-Animator (0.32 and 0.33 respectively). For text relevance, ConsisID scores 27.93 CLIPScore versus 24.97 for ID-Animator. On visual quality (FID), ConsisID reports 151.82 compared to ID-Animator's 117.46, though the authors note FID has limited alignment with human perception and direct readers to qualitative and user-study evidence. In a user study with 103 valid questionnaires, ConsisID is preferred over ID-Animator across all four dimensions: identity preservation, visual quality, text alignment, and motion amplitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single external baseline, ID-Animator [15], the only available open-source tuning-free IPT2V model. ConsisID decisively wins on both identity-preservation metrics: FaceSim-Arc improves from 0.32 to 0.58 (+81%) and FaceSim-Cur from 0.33 to 0.60 (+82%), reflecting the benefit of frequency-decomposed control signals tailored to DiT. Text relevance (CLIPScore) also improves from 24.97 to 27.93, as ConsisID can generate full-body videos with specified actions and backgrounds, whereas ID-Animator is limited to face-region talking-head outputs. The one metric where ConsisID trails is FID (151.82 vs. 117.46), but the authors explicitly caveat that FID has poor alignment with human perceptual quality and point to their user study and qualitative comparisons as more reliable evidence. In the user study, ConsisID is preferred in all four evaluated dimensions, confirming that the FID gap does not reflect a true quality deficit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts tuning-free identity-preserving generation (previously explored in image models like IP-Adapter, InstantID, and PhotoMaker) to the emerging DiT-based video generation paradigm, using CogVideoX-5B as the base architecture. Its key conceptual departure is a frequency-domain analysis of why U-Net-based identity control methods fail to transfer to DiT: the absence of skip connections deprives DiT of low-frequency aggregation, and the transformer's limited high-frequency perception undermines facial detail preservation. The injection strategy (low-frequency keypoint signals concatenated to the noise latent, high-frequency ArcFace/CLIP features via cross-attention inside attention blocks) is informed by prior frequency analyses of vision and diffusion transformers, while the feature extraction pipeline borrows the penultimate-layer and multiscale concatenation ideas from UniPortrait.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first tuning-free, DiT-native recipe for identity-preserving video generation, offering a frequency-aware control scheme that could generalize to other DiT-based controllable generation tasks beyond identity. However, the evidence base is narrow: only one external baseline (ID-Animator) is compared, the training dataset is in-house and undisclosed, and the FID score is actually worse than the baseline, weakening the visual-quality claim. Open questions include whether the frequency decomposition strategy extends to non-human subjects or multi-person scenes, whether it transfers to other DiT backbones, and whether the coarse-to-fine training and dynamic losses are necessary at scale or are artifacts of the small training budget (1.8k steps).
