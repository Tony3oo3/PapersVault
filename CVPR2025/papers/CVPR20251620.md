## Problem

Existing tracking-any-point (TAP) methods rely on frame-based sensors that degrade under high-speed motion and challenging lighting due to motion blur, limited framerate, and saturation. Event cameras offer high temporal resolution and high dynamic range to overcome these limitations, but their inherently motion-dependent signal makes feature correlation non-trivial, and no event-only TAP method existed prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251620/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams binned into fixed-size windows (N_e = 4×10⁵ events per timestep) represented as H×W×B event stacks (B=10 time bins), along with user-specified query points (2D pixel coordinates at initialization timesteps). It outputs per-point 2D positions at discrete tracking timesteps, binary visibility flags, and d-dimensional feature descriptors, operating over a sliding window of w=8 timesteps with stride T_s=4. Training uses 512×512 resolution; evaluation spans 240×180 to 640×480 across different camera hardware.

## Method

ETAP combines a CNN encoder that extracts multi-scale spatial features (4 scales, 4× downsampled) from event stacks with a CoTracker-style transformer that iteratively refines point positions and descriptors via alternating intra-point spatial attention and temporal attention over M iterations. The key architectural novelty is a feature-alignment (FA) loss: for each training sample, a time-inverted and randomly rotated (0°/90°/180°/270°) variant is generated, and the loss enforces high cosine similarity between unitized descriptors sampled at corresponding track positions across the two motion directions, explicitly promoting motion-invariant correlation features. The method is trained on EventKubric, a new 10,173-sample synthetic dataset rendered with Kubric at 48 FPS (with BULLET physics and Blender ray-tracing), adaptively upsampled via FILM, and converted to events using ESIM with random contrast sensitivities C~U(0.16,0.34), providing 2048 GT point tracks per sample. The total loss combines weighted track prediction error, visibility cross-entropy, and the FA-loss (L = 0.1·L_tp + L_vis + 0.1·L_fa), with the FA-loss introduced after an initial 10⁵-step warmup.

## Results

On the established feature tracking benchmark (EDS), ETAP achieves a Feature Age of 0.704 and Expected FA of 0.598, outperforming the best event-only method (DDFT E2VID, FA 0.589) by 20% and the best events-and-frames method (FE-TAP, FA 0.676) by 4.1%. On the TAP task, ETAP surpasses the E2Vid+CoTracker baseline by 136% in average Jaccard on EventKubric (0.546 vs. 0.236) and by 24% on EVIMO2 (0.661 vs. 0.531). On the extreme E2D2 fidget-spinner sequence, ETAP achieves 68% higher AJ than the event-based baseline (0.308 vs. 0.183) while the frame-based CoTracker fails almost entirely (AJ 0.007).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETAP (event-only) decisively outperforms all event-only baselines: DDFT E2VID (the strongest event-only method) is beaten by 20% on EDS Feature Age (0.704 vs. 0.589) and by 12% on EC (0.888 vs. 0.794); HASTE, EM-ICP, and ICP are far behind (FA ≤ 0.161 on EDS). Remarkably, ETAP also surpasses the best events-and-frames method FE-TAP by 4.1% on EDS (0.704 vs. 0.676) and by 5.2% on EC (0.888 vs. 0.844), despite using no frame input. On the TAP task, ETAP outperforms E2Vid+CoTracker (which reconstructs frames from events before applying a frame-based tracker) by 136% AJ on EventKubric and 24% on EVIMO2. On the extreme E2D2 fidget-spinner sequence, frame-based CoTracker fails catastrophically (AJ 0.007) due to motion blur and aliasing, while ETAP achieves 0.308 AJ, demonstrating the advantage of event cameras under high-speed motion and low light. The FA-loss ablation (ETAP w/o FA-loss) shows a small but consistent gain from the contrastive loss on real datasets (e.g., 0.704 vs. 0.698 on EDS), confirming its role in learning motion-robust features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the CoTracker [29] transformer architecture (iterative refinement with spatial and temporal attention over point tokens) to the event-camera modality, replacing frame-based feature encoders with event-stack encoders. The TAP problem formulation, evaluation metrics (AJ, δ_avg, OA), and point-track generation protocol follow TAP-Vid [13]. The key conceptual departure is addressing the motion-dependence of event signals: unlike frames, events encode different patterns for the same scene under different camera motions, so the paper introduces a contrastive feature-alignment loss (FA-loss) that enforces motion-invariant descriptors via time-inverted and rotated event variants. The training data pipeline (Kubric → FILM upsampling → ESIM event generation) is a new combination, but each component is established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first purely event-based TAP method, demonstrating that high-speed and HDR tracking scenarios where frame-based sensors fail (motion blur, aliasing, saturation) can be handled without auxiliary frame input. The EventKubric dataset (10K+ samples with full 3D PBR annotations) fills a critical gap in synthetic event data for point tracking, and the FA-loss offers a simple, generalizable mechanism for handling motion-dependent sensor signals. Limitations include reliance on monochrome events (no color cues), the need for scene motion to initialize track features, and the modest (though consistent) gain from the FA-loss over its ablated variant, suggesting the loss helps but is not the sole driver of performance. Open questions include whether the approach extends to color event sensors and whether the motion-invariance objective generalizes to more complex, non-rigid scene dynamics.
