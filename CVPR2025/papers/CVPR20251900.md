## Problem

Existing keypoint estimation models are trained on a fixed set of pre-defined keypoints and cannot detect newly-added keypoints at test time, yet downstream tasks (e.g., medical analysis) continually demand new keypoints. Prior solutions—training separate estimators per keypoint set, unsupervised keypoint learning, or category-agnostic pose estimation—suffer from limited generalizability, restricted transferability, or linear growth in model count. This paper introduces Incremental Object Keypoint Learning (IKL), a paradigm where only new keypoints are labeled in new data and the model is incrementally updated without retaining any old data, addressing the label non-co-occurrence challenge that makes it hard to capture inter-keypoint anatomical constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D RGB image as input and produces a stack of 2D keypoint heatmaps (one per keypoint, each a Gaussian peak around the ground-truth location) via a stack of convolutional estimation heads on top of an HRNet backbone. In the incremental setting, the new training data contains ground-truth labels only for the newly introduced keypoints; old keypoint labels are absent, so the model must rely on pseudo-labels from the frozen old model and the auxiliary KA-Net to preserve old keypoint knowledge. The KA-Net itself consumes the ground-truth heatmaps of two related new keypoints concatenated with rescaled holistic visual features from the frozen backbone, and outputs a predicted heatmap for one selected old keypoint.

## Method

KAMP is a two-stage, exemplar-free scheme tailored to IKL. In Stage-I (Knowledge Association), an auxiliary KA-Net is trained to predict a selected old keypoint's heatmap conditioned on the ground-truth heatmaps of two spatially/anatomically adjacent new keypoints plus holistic visual features, effectively learning the triangulation constraint between related keypoints; it is supervised by pseudo-labels from the frozen old model. In Stage-II (Mutual Promotion), the new model is jointly optimized on all old and new keypoints with a combined loss: L2 regression on new keypoints, a keypoint-oriented spatial distillation loss (KSD) that applies softmax independently over the height and width dimensions of each old keypoint's heatmap (rather than across the keypoint channel as in LWF) to preserve keypoint-specific spatial knowledge, and the KA-Net loss on the selected old keypoint to distill the learned anatomical association. The old model and KA-Net are both frozen during Stage-II, serving as teachers. This explicit modeling of old–new keypoint relations enables not just anti-forgetting but positive transfer, where old keypoint accuracy can improve after incremental steps.

## Results

On Split Head-2023 (5-step IKL), KAMP achieves A-MRE₄ = 2.32, outperforming the best baseline CPR (3.71) by 1.39 points and approaching the Joint Training upper bound (2.12). On Split MPII (5-step IKL), KAMP reaches AAA₄ = 79.93% PCK, surpassing CPR (75.52%) and LWF (75.75%) by over 4 points. KAMP is the only method to achieve consistently positive Average Transfer (AT) across Chest (+0.29), Head-2023 (+0.41), and MPII (+1.80), indicating positive transfer beyond anti-forgetting. On Split ATRW, KAMP attains AAA₃ = 93.16% with MT₃ = 5.13%, the largest single-keypoint improvement among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAMP outperforms all 11 exemplar-free IL baselines (EWC, RW, MAS, LWF, AFEC, CPR, SFD, WF, GBD) on every dataset and every metric in Table 1. The closest competitor is CPR [8], which KAMP beats by 1.39 A-MRE on Head-2023 (2.32 vs. 3.71) and by 4.41 AAA on MPII (79.93 vs. 75.52). Methods adapted from continual semantic segmentation and class-incremental learning (SFD, WF, GBD) achieve near-zero AT but sacrifice overall accuracy, confirming that anti-forgetting-only strategies are insufficient for IKL. KAMP is the only method achieving positive AT on three of four datasets, and even on ATRW where all methods show negative AT, KAMP's −0.84 is the least negative. In the low-shot comparison (Tables 3–4), KAMP also surpasses one-shot medical keypoint methods CC2D and EGT on Head-2023 (e.g., 4.35 vs. 5.01 MRE at 1-shot) and outperforms CAPE-based MetaPoint+ on MPII (70.09 vs. 65.71 PCK at 1-shot), with the combined KAMP+MetaPoint+ reaching 73.49 PCK.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem formulation at the intersection of incremental learning and keypoint estimation: Incremental Object Keypoint Learning (IKL), where a model incrementally acquires newly defined keypoints using only labels for those new points, without retaining any old training data. It departs from unsupervised keypoint learning (UKL), which requires rigid motion or video consistency, and from category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE), which freezes a pretrained model and thus cannot expand its knowledge. The method KAMP draws on knowledge distillation (LWF-style output matching) but adapts it with a keypoint-oriented spatial softmax, and introduces an auxiliary Knowledge Association Network (KA-Net) that explicitly models triangulation constraints between spatially adjacent old and new keypoints — a design inspired by structured feature learning in pose estimation but repurposed for incremental knowledge transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IKL addresses a practical bottleneck in real-world keypoint estimation (e.g., medical cephalometric analysis) where new clinical demands sequentially require new landmarks but re-labeling all old keypoints is prohibitively expensive. The demonstration of positive transfer — old keypoints improving rather than merely being preserved — suggests that anatomical structure can be exploited to make incremental learning strictly beneficial, a finding that could inform other structured regression tasks. Open questions include whether IKL scales to many incremental steps, whether it extends to novel object categories (beyond the same-category setting studied here), and whether the KA-Net's reliance on pseudo-labels from the old model degrades under large distribution shifts.
