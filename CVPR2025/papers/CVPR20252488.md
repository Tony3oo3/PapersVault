## Problem

Existing scene graph datasets are sparse (Visual Genome averages only 1.5 relations per subject), limited in relationship diversity (predominantly spatial, lacking interactional, emotional, functional, and social types), and cover only ~100K images. This scarcity prevents effective instruction-tuning of multimodal language models for dense relationship reasoning, while frontier models like GPT-4V hallucinate when generating scene graphs from scratch due to weak 3D grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252488/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ROBIN-3B consumes an RGB image alongside pixel-level segmentation masks (encoded as mask tokens via a dedicated extractor) and bounding box coordinates referenced in text, following the Osprey architecture. It outputs dense scene graphs as structured text containing subject-predicate-object triplets across five relationship categories (spatial, interactional, functional, social, emotional), object descriptions with attributes, and region class labels. The dataset SVG contains 146K images with 5.6M relationships for 2.6M objects, averaging 4× more relations per object than Visual Genome.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. Stage 1 (SVG-RELATIONS): GPT-4V is prompted to complete missing relationships for K≥5 semantically significant objects (selected via SAM/Semantic-SAM IoU > 0.5) in 33K COCO seed images that already carry human annotations (COCO/LVIS detections, RefCOCO descriptions, VG/GQA scene graphs, Depth-Anything depth maps), with rule-based and model-based VQA filtering to remove errors. Stage 2 (SG-EDIT): The trained ROBIN-3B (ConvNext-Large vision encoder + Qwen2.5-3B LM with a mask-aware token extractor) generates dense scene graphs for 113K new images from ADE20K, PSG, and VG, and GPT-4o then edits these outputs by removing noisy relations, adding relevant ones, and refining object descriptions. The model is retrained on the GPT-4o-refined graphs, yielding consistent 2.2% average gains over self-generated graphs without editing. This self-distillation loop—model generates, frontier model edits, model retrains—scales dense scene graph annotation without human labeling.

## Results

ROBIN-3B, trained on fewer than 3M instances, achieves state-of-the-art performance among ≤4B models on relationship understanding: 80.1% on What's Up (vs. 78.7% Phi-3-Vision, 78.2% BLIP-3), 89.4% on SugarCrepe, 77.0% on MMBench, and 68.0% on CRPE. On referring expression comprehension, ROBIN-3B reaches 88.2% average Recall@1 (IoU>0.5) on the test split, surpassing the previous best of 87.4% (ASM-V2-13B) and the prior 3B SOTA of 85.6% (MM1.5-3B). On open-ended panoptic scene graph generation (PSG), ROBIN-3B achieves R@20 of 20.6 and mR@20 of 13.2, outperforming all prior open-ended generation models and matching early closed-set detection baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among ≤4B models, ROBIN-3B outperforms Phi-3-Vision and BLIP-3 on What's Up by 1.4–1.9 points (80.1% vs. 78.7%/78.2%), despite being trained on <3M instances versus their ~300M pre-training instances. On SugarCrepe, ROBIN-3B leads all ≤4B models (89.4% vs. 89.0% BLIP-3), and on MMBench it achieves the top score (77.0–77.1%). Against 7B–13B models, ROBIN-3B surpasses LLaVA-1.5-13B, LLaVA-1.6-Next-13B, and ASM-V2-13B on VSR (70.5% vs. 65.3–69.5%), SugarCrepe (89.4% vs. 87.1–87.8%), and What's Up (80.1% vs. 49.5–56.8%), though it trails on GQA where data overlap exists. On referring expression comprehension, ROBIN-3B (88.2% avg test) beats ASM-V2-13B (87.3%) and MM1.5-3B (85.6%), the previous 3B SOTA. On PSG scene graph generation, ROBIN-3B (R@20=20.6, mR@20=13.2) outperforms ASM-V2-13B (14.2/10.3) and TextPSG (4.8/–) among open-ended models, and matches MOTIFS and VC Tree (~20.0 R@20) which are single-task fine-tuned closed-set detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Osprey-7B architecture (vision encoder + mask-aware extractor + LM) and extends it from single-region understanding to full-image dense scene graph generation. It departs from prior scene graph generation research—which has focused on classification-based SGG models or distilling relationships into task-specific architectures—by instead using densely annotated scene graphs as instruction-tuning data to improve a general-purpose MLM's relationship reasoning. The data curation pipeline also diverges from prior efforts that either rely solely on human annotations (Visual Genome, GQA) or prompt frontier models to generate scene graphs from scratch (which hallucinate); instead, it completes missing relationships in existing human annotations with GPT-4V and then applies a self-distillation editing loop (SG-EDIT) inspired by Segment Anything's iterative refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVG is the first automatically generated large-scale dense scene graph dataset (146K images, 5.6M relationships), and the SG-EDIT pipeline offers a reusable, scalable recipe for producing relationship-rich training data without prohibitive human annotation costs. The finding that targeted relationship data (<3M instances) can match or exceed models pre-trained on 300M+ general instances suggests that data quality and task alignment matter more than raw scale for relationship understanding. Key open questions include evaluating scene graph generation on truly in-the-wild images beyond COCO-derived annotations, extending to 3D spatial reasoning and video, and reducing dependence on proprietary frontier models (GPT-4V/GPT-4o) for the curation pipeline.
