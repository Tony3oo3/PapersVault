## Problem

Diffusion-model-based anomaly detection suffers from 'faithfulness hallucination' and 'catastrophic forgetting' during continual learning: as new classes are added, the model's reconstruction of previously learned classes degrades and becomes inconsistent with earlier training. Moreover, the gradient-projection strategy needed to prevent forgetting requires computing a significant representation over all intermediate features of the Markov diffusion process, incurring prohibitive memory costs (e.g., ~157 GB for just 10 images).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251872/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB anomaly image as input, encodes it into latent space via a pre-trained VAE, and runs a 10-step DDIM denoising process conditioned on the anomaly-masked network's output. It produces a pixel-level anomaly score map (sum of L2 distances between multi-layer ResNet-50 features of the reconstructed normal image and the original VAE round-trip, upsampled to full resolution), an image-level score (max of the map), and the reconstructed normal image itself. The text encoder input is a single space character, so no textual conditioning is used.

## Method

CDAD combines three components: (1) a Continual Diffusion Model that projects each new task's gradient onto the orthogonal complement of a k-rank SVD basis (significant representation) of all previous tasks' intermediate features, ensuring X_pre · ∇W_orth ≈ 0 and thus preserving prior outputs; (2) an Iterative SVD (iSVD) that splits the large feature matrix into n blocks and iteratively computes k-rank SVD on [previous_basis, next_block], reducing memory from 157 GB to ~17 GB while preserving correctness via a linear-transitivity lemma; and (3) an Anomaly-Masked Network (AMN) comprising a CNN local encoder and a transformer non-local encoder with neighbor-masked self-attention, trained with a patch-perturbation and anomaly-masked loss so that abnormal features are suppressed and the diffusion model focuses on reconstructing defect regions rather than overfitting to normal appearance.

## Results

On MVTec, CDAD achieves A-AUROC of 96.4/96.6 (image/pixel-level) with a negative forgetting measure (FM = -0.57/-0.04) on the 14-1 setting, and 94.9/95.7 (FM = 1.01/0.74) on the 10-1×5 setting with 5 incremental steps. On VisA, CDAD reaches 88.3/97.2 (FM = -1.28/-0.06) on the 11-1 setting and 83.4/95.8 (FM = 3.79/1.49) on the 8-1×4 setting. The paper reports first place in 17 out of 18 continual anomaly detection settings across both datasets, with the largest margin over the second-best method being +4.3/+1.1 A-AUROC on MVTec setting 3 (3×5, 5 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDAD outperforms all 9 baselines across nearly all settings. Against the strongest non-diffusion baseline IUF, CDAD gains +0.4/+0.3 A-AUROC on MVTec 14-1 and +0.7/+0.6 on 10-1×5, while reducing FM from 3.2/1.0 to 1.01/0.74 on the 5-step setting. The diffusion-based baselines DiAD and ControlNet perform competitively on single-step settings (e.g., ControlNet achieves 92.7/93.8 on 10-5) but suffer severe catastrophic forgetting on multi-step settings, dropping to 79.2/89.6 (ControlNet) and 80.5/89.0 (DiAD) on 3×5, where CDAD maintains 89.1/92.2. The UniAD variants with classical continual learning regularizers (EWC, SI, MAS, LVT) show only marginal improvement over vanilla UniAD and remain 5–20 points below CDAD on A-AUROC. UCAD is competitive on 1-step settings (93.8/95.7 on 14-1) but degrades to 84.8/90.1 on 3×5, still trailing CDAD by 4.3/2.1. On cross-dataset transfer (MVTec→VisA), CDAD leads the second-best method (DiAD) by 4.5/1.9 A-AUROC while halving the forgetting measure (4.71 vs. 10.24 image-level FM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-model-based anomaly detection (DiAD, ControlNet, GLAD) and continual learning via gradient projection (GPM, Prompt Gradient Projection). It departs from prior diffusion-based anomaly detection methods that assume all classes are available simultaneously (one-for-all) by introducing a task-by-task continual training protocol, and from prior continual anomaly detection methods (IUF, UCAD, DNE) that rely on memory banks or semantic compression by instead modifying the gradient direction to protect previously learned feature subspaces. The iterative SVD (iSVD) is a novel memory-efficient alternative to full SVD for computing the significant representation, motivated by the Markov chain structure of diffusion processes that inflates intermediate feature dimensions. The anomaly-masked network draws on UniAD's neighbor-masked self-attention but repurposes it to mask anomalous features in the conditioning signal rather than to prevent identical shortcuts in reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: industrial anomaly detection systems must adapt to new product classes as they appear, and diffusion-based methods—despite their strong reconstruction quality—were previously shown to suffer severe catastrophic forgetting and 'faithfulness hallucination' in this setting. The iSVD technique is a general-purpose tool that could benefit any continual learning scenario involving diffusion models with large intermediate feature spaces, not just anomaly detection. Limitations include evaluation only on two industrial datasets at 256×256 resolution, reliance on a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion backbone, and the fact that the k-rank approximation threshold (γ=0.999) requires careful tuning per setting. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video anomaly detection, whether the gradient projection strategy remains effective with more than 15–20 incremental steps, and whether the memory savings of iSVD hold for larger diffusion architectures (e.g., SDXL).
