## Problem

State-of-the-art deep learning pan-sharpening methods demand high-end GPUs and large memory, making them impractical for deployment on resource-constrained remote sensing satellites. While binarization offers extreme compression by restricting weights and activations to ±1, it causes severe information degradation that has not been systematically addressed for the pixel-level precision required in pan-sharpening. This paper is the first to investigate binary neural networks for pan-sharpening, identifying that binarization degrades different frequency components of an image to varying degrees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution multi-spectral image (upsampled to match PAN resolution) and a paired high-resolution panchromatic image as input, producing a high-resolution multi-spectral image as output. The upsampled MS and PAN images are channel-concatenated and encoded into a latent condition vector that guides the diffusion-based prior generation. All convolutional operations in the backbone use binary weights and activations (±1) computed via bitwise XNOR and bit-counting, replacing floating-point matrix multiplication.

## Method

BNNPan is built around the Prior-Integrated Binary Frequency (PIBF) module with three components. First, Binary Wavelet Transform Convolution (BWTC) applies DWT to decompose features into four frequency subbands, processes each with a normalized binary convolution unit that uses full-precision residual statistics (mean and variance) to redistribute the binary output and narrow the value gap, then reconstructs via IDWT. Second, Latent Diffusion Prior Compensation (LDPC) integrates a compact prior feature generated by a latent diffusion model (conditioned on the PAN+MS input) to compensate for information lost during binarization, injected via channel-wise scaling and shifting. Third, Channel-wise Distribution Calibration applies a lightweight channel attention to recalibrate the enriched features. Training follows a three-phase strategy: (1) train the latent encoder and backbone with reconstruction loss, (2) train the diffusion denoiser to generate the prior from pure noise, and (3) jointly fine-tune the backbone and diffusion module end-to-end.

## Results

On the WorldView-3 dataset, BNNPan achieves PSNR of 38.38 dB and SAM of 3.196 (reduced resolution), surpassing all binary baselines (best binary: BBCU at 37.08 dB PSNR) and approaching full-precision methods (best: HFEAN at 38.88 dB). On full-resolution evaluation, BNNPan attains HQNR of 0.9328, outperforming all binary methods (best: FABNet at 0.9087) and rivaling full-precision baselines. On Gaofen-2, BNNPan reaches 41.84 dB PSNR and 0.9767 Q2n, leading all compared methods by a substantial margin over the next-best binary method (BTM at 39.80 dB). The model uses only 78.2K parameters and 0.12 GFLOPs with 4 PIBF modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BNNPan outperforms all seven binary baselines across every metric on all three datasets. On WV3, it gains +1.30 dB PSNR over the strongest binary method (BBCU, 37.08 dB) and +2.35 dB over the weakest (IRNet, 35.17 dB), while also achieving the lowest SAM (3.196 vs. 3.680 for BBCU). Against full-precision methods, BNNPan surpasses PNN, MSDCNN, FusionNet, and LAGConv in PSNR, and is within 0.5 dB of the top full-precision methods (HFEAN 38.88, GPPNN 38.57). On Gaofen-2, the margin over binary baselines is even larger: BNNPan achieves 41.84 dB PSNR versus 39.85 dB for the best binary method (FABNet), a gap of nearly 2 dB. On QuickBird, BNNPan leads all binary methods in PSNR (37.94 vs. 37.43 for BBCU) and D_s (0.0661 vs. 0.0878 for FABNet), though FABNet retains a slight edge in D_λ (0.0738 vs. 0.0761). In full-resolution HQNR evaluation, BNNPan achieves the highest scores on all three datasets (0.9328 WV3, 0.9259 GF2, 0.8627 QB), confirming superior joint spectral-spatial quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of research: deep-learning-based pan-sharpening (PNN, PanNet, FusionNet, transformer and diffusion-based methods) and binary neural networks for low-level vision (XNOR-Net, ReActNet, FABNet, BBCU, E2FIF). It is the first work to transplant BNNs into the pan-sharpening setting, repurposing frequency-aware binarization ideas from super-resolution (FABNet) and the full-precision residual flow concept from E2FIF, while introducing a novel full-precision statistics normalization that has no direct predecessor in either community. The latent diffusion prior, borrowed from generative modeling, is repurposed here not as a generative engine but as a compact, degradation-free information source to patch the representational gap introduced by 1-bit quantization—a use case not explored in prior BNN or diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 78K-parameter, 0.12 GFLOPs binary network can match or exceed most full-precision pan-sharpening baselines, the paper opens a realistic path toward on-board image fusion on resource-constrained satellites, where GPU-class hardware is unavailable. The frequency-wise binarization insight (different subbands tolerate quantization differently) and the three-phase training strategy for the diffusion prior are transferable ideas that could benefit other quantized low-level vision tasks. However, practical deployment remains unvalidated: the paper does not report inference latency on embedded hardware, energy consumption, or robustness to real sensor noise, and the three-phase training pipeline adds non-trivial engineering complexity that may limit adoption. Open questions include whether the approach extends to hyperspectral or SAR–MS fusion, and whether the diffusion prior can be replaced by a lighter mechanism without sacrificing the observed gains.
