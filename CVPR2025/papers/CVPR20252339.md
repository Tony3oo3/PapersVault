## Problem

Standard convolution in CNN-based pansharpening uses a fixed square sampling window with a predetermined number of sampling points, making it unable to adapt to the wide range of object sizes present in remote sensing imagery (e.g., small vehicles versus large buildings). Existing deformable and multi-scale convolutions address only part of this rigidity—either adjusting positions but not point counts, or using fixed kernel sizes across scales—leaving a gap in scale-adaptive feature extraction.

## Input / Output

The network receives a low-resolution multispectral (LRMS) image and a high-resolution panchromatic (PAN) image; the LRMS is first upsampled to PAN resolution and the two are concatenated along the channel dimension before entering the encoder. The output is a high-resolution multispectral (HRMS) image produced by injecting learned detail back into the upsampled LRMS. Because true HRMS ground truth is unavailable, training pairs are synthesized via Wald's protocol on WV3 (8-band), QuickBird (4-band), and GaoFen-2 (4-band) datasets.

## Method

ARConv learns per-pixel kernel height and width through two lightweight subnets (a shared feature extractor plus distinct Sigmoid-output learners, then affine-scaled to a bounded range), requiring only two learnable parameters regardless of kernel size. The number of sampling points is derived from the spatial mean of the learned height and width via floor division by modulation coefficients, with the result forced to an odd integer in each dimension. Sampling offsets are computed by element-wise multiplying a standard grid matrix with a per-pixel scale matrix, and bilinear interpolation retrieves values at the resulting non-integer positions; an expansion step assembles these into a regular grid so a single convolution with stride (k_h, k_w) completes the operation. A learned affine transformation (matrices M and B) is applied to the output for additional spatial flexibility. ARNet embeds ARConv as a drop-in replacement for standard convolutions inside U-Net ResBlocks, trained with L1 loss and Adam, using a 100-epoch exploratory phase before fixing the kernel-size selection for the remainder of training.

## Results

On the WV3 dataset, ARNet achieves the best performance across all six metrics: SAM 2.885, ERGAS 2.139, Q8 0.921 (reduced-resolution), and Dλ 0.0146, Ds 0.0279, HQNR 0.958 (full-resolution). On QB, it attains SAM 4.430, ERGAS 3.633, Q4 0.939; on GF2, SAM 0.698, ERGAS 0.626, Q4 0.983. ARNet outperforms the strongest baseline (CANNet, CVPR 2024) by 0.045 in SAM and 0.019 in Dλ on WV3, and by 0.009 in SAM on QB, while matching CANNet's Q4 on GF2 (0.983).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARNet achieves the best score on every metric across all three datasets (WV3, QB, GF2), outperforming all 16 baselines. The closest competitor is CANNet (CVPR 2024), which ARNet beats by 0.045 in SAM (2.885 vs. 2.930) and 0.005 in Dλ (0.0146 vs. 0.0196) on WV3, and by 0.077 in SAM (4.430 vs. 4.507) on QB; on GF2, ARNet edges out CANNet by 0.009 in SAM (0.698 vs. 0.707) while matching its Q4 (0.983). Among traditional methods, ARNet improves SAM by over 2.9 points compared to EXP (2.885 vs. 5.800 on WV3). Among CNN-based pansharpening methods, ARNet surpasses DCFNet by 0.153 in SAM and 0.010 in HQNR on WV3, and outperforms LAGConv by 0.219 in SAM. A direct comparison with DCNv2 (Table 7) shows ARConv achieving SAM 2.881 vs. 3.151 and ERGAS 2.149 vs. 2.425, confirming that learning only two shape parameters (height and width) is more effective than DCNv2's per-point offset learning for pansharpening.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARConv carves new ground at the intersection of deformable convolutions (which learn per-point offsets, e.g., DCN/DCNv2) and multi-scale convolutions (which use fixed-size kernels at multiple scales, e.g., PyConv, Selective Kernel Networks). Its key departure is learning only two parameters per pixel—the kernel's height and width—rather than per-point offsets or a bank of fixed kernels, and dynamically adjusting the number of sampling points based on the learned scale. Spatially adaptive convolutions like LAGConv and CANConv adapt kernel parameters per location but still use fixed kernel shapes and point counts; ARConv generalizes this by making both the shape and the point count content-dependent. The overall ARNet architecture repurposes the U-Net encoder-decoder with skip connections, replacing standard convolutions in ResBlocks with ARConv.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play demonstration (Table 6) showing consistent gains when ARConv replaces convolutions in FusionNet, LAGNet, and CANNet suggests the module could be broadly useful beyond pansharpening, though this is only validated on one task. A notable limitation is the unusual training protocol—fixing the kernel-size selection after 100 exploratory epochs—which introduces a non-standard hyperparameter and may limit reproducibility. The improvements over the strongest baseline (CANNet) are modest (e.g., SAM 2.885 vs. 2.930 on WV3), and the method has not been tested on tasks with more extreme scale variation or on larger-scale benchmarks. Open questions include whether the two-parameter (height, width) formulation generalizes to 3D or video settings, and whether the fixed kernel selection strategy is truly optimal versus end-to-end differentiable alternatives.
