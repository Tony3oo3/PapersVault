## Problem

3D Class-Incremental Learning suffers from severe catastrophic forgetting because 3D classifiers over-rely on shortcut local features (e.g., legs for both tables and chairs), causing misclassification of old classes when new tasks share similar local geometry. Existing 3D CIL methods largely extend 2D replay or dynamic-expansion strategies without addressing this shortcut-feature bias, leaving a gap in how to represent shape parts in a way that generalizes across tasks while remaining discriminative within each task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud x ∈ R^{L×3} from the current task plus a small exemplar set E_t from all previous tasks, and produces a unified class-label probability over all classes seen so far. Internally, farthest-point sampling selects N_p points which are grouped into K-neighborhood parts; the output is a fused distribution combining task-specific softmax predictions weighted by learnable inter-task affinities, rather than a single monolithic classifier.

## Method

ILPC builds on DNIK's part codebook P = {P^m}_{m=1}^M but introduces a TopK sparse-activation mechanism: for each part-level feature, only the k most similar part concepts are activated (one-hot gating), producing part composite features Z_p that span a reduced, task-relevant subspace. A 3-layer Transformer head with a learnable class token then encodes mutual spatial relations among part composite features for task-specific classification. To prevent task bias during head fusion, learnable scalar affinities α_{k,i} are introduced to rescale the contribution of each older task's head relative to the current task, so that predictions for old classes are not suppressed by the larger training signal of the new task. Training combines cross-entropy, a supervised contrastive loss on part activations, and a novel pseudo-part label loss that enforces cosine similarity between composite features of parts sharing similar activation maps while pushing apart dissimilar ones.

## Results

On Co3D, ILPC achieves 81.18% last-task accuracy and 86.37% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline DS-AL (78.87%/81.74%) by +2.31% and +4.63% respectively. On nuScenes, ILPC reaches 82.44% last accuracy and 87.27% average accuracy, surpassing DS-AL by +4.16% and +1.16%. On ShapeNet, ILPC attains 82.27% last accuracy and 86.08% average accuracy, improving over DS-AL by +1.31% and +1.34%. The method consistently leads on both metrics across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ILPC outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and both metrics (last-task and average accuracy). The largest gains are on Co3D, where ILPC beats the runner-up DS-AL by 2.31% in last accuracy and 4.63% in average accuracy, and on nuScenes where the last-accuracy margin over DS-AL reaches 4.16%. Among replay-based methods, ER and iCaRL trail ILPC by 18–20% in last accuracy on Co3D, confirming that exemplar replay alone is insufficient for 3D CIL. Dynamic network methods (DER, FOSTER, MEMO) perform better than replay methods but still fall 3–11% short of ILPC on last accuracy. The most competitive baseline is DS-AL, which uses dual-stream analytic learning; ILPC still leads it by 1.3–4.2% depending on dataset. DGR (gradient reweighting) is the weakest among the newer methods, lagging ILPC by 9–10% on Co3D last accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DNIK [43], which learns a part concept codebook for 3D novel class discovery, and repurposes its part projection mechanism for the class-incremental setting. It departs from prior 3D CIL methods (e.g., INOR-Net, L3DOC, static-dynamic co-teaching) that primarily extend 2D CIL strategies via neighborhood-based geometric aggregations, instead arguing that shortcut local features are a root cause of catastrophic forgetting in 3D. The sparse TopK activation of part concepts and the learnable affinity for multi-task head fusion are the two key architectural departures from both the DNIK baseline and standard 2D CIL fusion schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3D CIL is critical for robotics and autonomous driving where models must continuously acquire new object categories without retraining, yet the field is still young with only a handful of prior methods. Identifying shortcut features as an amplifier of forgetting in 3D (beyond the well-known 2D issue) and showing that shared part concepts naturally mitigate it provides a useful inductive-bias perspective for future 3D continual learning. The authors note that learned part concepts are not always human-interpretable and suggest reconstruction losses or pre-trained model adaptation as open directions, leaving room for more semantically grounded part discovery in incremental settings.
