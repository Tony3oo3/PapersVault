## Problem

In expansion-based class-incremental learning, new task-specific features can collide with previously learned features (e.g., relying on the same spurious color cue across tasks), causing inter-task misclassification. Existing mitigations depend on a small rehearsal buffer and an auxiliary classifier to encourage diversity, but the limited and imbalanced rehearsal samples make this suboptimal. The paper asks whether a more efficient source of diverse, discriminative features can be obtained beyond what the current task's classification objective alone provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252665/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a class label over all seen classes. At inference time, only the task-specific ResNet-18 models and their shared classifier are used—no extra parameters are introduced. During training, a separate task-agnostic ResNet-18 model and a merge-attention module process the same input, and a rehearsal buffer of 2,000 (or 20,000 for ImageNet-1000) samples is maintained via herding for the auxiliary classifier.

## Method

TagFex comprises three coupled components. First, a separate task-agnostic model is trained with continual self-supervised learning (CaSSLe built on SimCLR's InfoNCE loss plus a predictive consistency term against the previous task's frozen model), so its feature space grows in expressiveness across tasks without being shaped by classification. Second, a merge attention module treats spatial feature-map locations as patch tokens, uses the task-specific feature as query and concatenates keys/values from both task-specific and (stop-gradient) task-agnostic features, producing an aggregated representation that adaptively pulls in task-agnostic information; a merge classifier trained on this representation provides supervision. Third, knowledge is transferred back to the task-specific model via a KL-divergence loss between the merge classifier's logits and the task-specific classifier's logits, so the task-specific backbone absorbs diverse features while the task-agnostic model remains untouched. At inference only the task-specific models are used, and an optional FPGM pruning step (TagFex-P) reduces the accumulated parameter count from ~61 M to ~12 M per incremental stage.

## Results

TagFex achieves the best average accuracy across all six CIL scenarios tested. On CIFAR-100 10-10, it reaches 78.45% Avg accuracy (68.23% Last), outperforming the strongest baseline DER (75.36% Avg) by ~3.1 points. On ImageNet-100 50-10, TagFex attains 80.64% Avg (75.54% Last), a ~2.9-point gain over DER (77.71% Avg). On the most challenging ImageNet-1000 100-100 setting, TagFex achieves 68.32% Avg, still leading DER (66.87% Avg) by 1.45 points. The pruned variant TagFex-P retains most of the gains while reducing average incremental parameters from 61.6M to ~12M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TagFex outperforms all five baselines on every scenario where they are compared. Against the closest competitor DER (the direct architectural base), TagFex gains 3.1–3.9 points in Avg accuracy on CIFAR-100 and 2.1–2.9 points on ImageNet-100, with a smaller but consistent 1.45-point margin on ImageNet-1000. Compared to BEEF, the gap is even larger: TagFex leads by 6.5 points on CIFAR-100 10-10 Avg and 3.4 points on ImageNet-100 50-10 Avg. DyTox, which only reports results on 10-10 splits, trails TagFex by 4.95 points on CIFAR-100 10-10 Avg and 2.76 points on ImageNet-100 10-10 Avg. The older rehearsal-based methods iCaRL and BiC are substantially behind, with iCaRL lagging TagFex by over 13 points on CIFAR-100 10-10 Avg. Notably, even under a memory-aligned protocol (Table 4), TagFex with 2000 rehearsal samples beats DER with 2300 samples by 1.71 points on ImageNet-100 10-10 Avg, and with 3000 samples beats DER with 3300 by 2.44 points, indicating more effective use of limited rehearsal memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DER's expansion-based CIL framework, where a new backbone is trained per task while old ones are frozen, and on CaSSLe's predictive continual self-supervised learning for maintaining representations across tasks. It departs from prior contrastive-learning-in-CIL work (OCM, C4IL) that applies contrastive objectives directly to the inference model, instead isolating task-agnostic feature learning into a separate model and bridging it to the task-specific model via a novel merge attention mechanism. The FPGM pruning strategy is borrowed from model compression literature to address the parameter growth inherent in expansion-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3–4% average accuracy gain over DER across CIFAR-100 and ImageNet scenarios, combined with the CKA evidence that expanded features become measurably less correlated, provides a concrete mechanism for mitigating feature collision that rehearsal samples alone cannot achieve. The memory-aligned comparison (Table 4) showing TagFex outperforming DER with 300+ extra rehearsal samples is practically important, suggesting the task-agnostic model is a more efficient use of memory than additional exemplars. The main open question is scalability: the parameter growth from one new backbone per task remains a concern even with FPGM pruning, and the paper does not explore whether the approach extends to very long task sequences or non-CNN backbones.
