## Problem

CNN-based point cloud geometry compression methods are limited by small receptive fields that cannot capture long-range dependencies among octree nodes, while existing Transformer-based approaches suffer from high parameter counts, computational overhead, and a tendency to neglect fine-grained local geometric details due to reliance on global self-attention. No prior architecture efficiently combines local spatial modeling with long-range context in a lightweight octree entropy model suitable for resource-constrained deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a point cloud (sparse LiDAR at up to 12 octree levels or dense human body at 9–10 levels) partitioned into an octree, where each node carries an occupancy code, level index, and octant index. A sliding context window of N sequential non-empty nodes (default N=1024, feature dimension C=256) is fed to the network, which outputs an 8-way occupancy probability distribution per node. These distributions drive an arithmetic encoder to produce the final compressed bitstream; at decode time, the arithmetic decoder reconstructs the full octree and hence the point cloud.

## Method

TopNet is a CNN–Transformer cooperation architecture with four novel modules that replace corresponding components in OctAttention. (1) Locally-enhanced Context Encoding (LeCE) substitutes absolute positional encoding with a depth-wise convolution plus Inception-Residual Network block, yielding translation-invariant node embeddings that capture relative local geometry. (2) Adaptive-Length Sliding Window Attention (AL-SWA) replaces standard scaled dot-product attention with a length-scaled cosine attention (learnable temperature × log N scaling) and a learned query embedding, while Q/K/V are generated via point-wise + depth-wise convolutions rather than linear projections, enabling robust attention across varying window lengths. (3) Spatial-Gated-enhanced Channel Mixer (SG-CM) replaces the feed-forward network with a split-channel design where one branch undergoes depth-wise convolution for spatial context and the other acts as a multiplicative gate, achieving joint spatial-channel aggregation with fewer parameters. (4) Latent-guided Node Occupancy Predictor (LNOP) replaces linear projections with a star operation (element-wise multiplication between projection layers) to introduce nonlinear feature interactions in a higher-dimensional latent space. The full model uses only 3.37 M parameters (20 % fewer than OctAttention) and is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss on the predicted vs. ground-truth occupancy distributions.

## Results

On the lossless 8iVFB dataset, TopNet achieves an average BPP of 0.56, a 43.4% reduction over G-PCC (0.99) and 8.1% lower than the best prior method ECM-OPCC (0.58). On MVUB, TopNet reaches 0.71 BPP (35.5% reduction vs G-PCC), outperforming ECM-OPCC (0.75) and OctAttention (0.76). For lossy LiDAR compression on SemanticKITTI, TopNet achieves a 33.4% BPP reduction relative to G-PCC, surpassing OctAttention's 24.7% reduction, while using only 3.37M parameters (20% fewer than OctAttention's 4.23M). The model also generalizes to nuScenes, LiDAR-CS, and ScanNet without retraining, maintaining superior R-D performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopNet consistently outperforms all compared baselines across both lossless and lossy settings. On 8iVFB, TopNet (0.56 BPP) beats the strongest prior method ECM-OPCC (0.58) by 3.4% and OctAttention (0.64) by 8.1%; on MVUB, it leads ECM-OPCC (0.75) by 5.3% and OctAttention (0.76) by 6.6%. Against the traditional G-PCC baseline, TopNet achieves 43.4% and 35.5% BPP reductions on 8iVFB and MVUB respectively, substantially exceeding OctAttention's 35.4% and 30.9%. On the lossy SemanticKITTI benchmark, TopNet's 33.4% reduction over G-PCC surpasses OctAttention (24.7%), OctFormer, EHEM, and SparsePCGC in R-D performance. In terms of efficiency, TopNet uses 20% fewer parameters than OctAttention (3.37M vs 4.23M) and 74% fewer than EHEM (13.01M), while also reducing memory consumption (0.33 GB vs 0.37 GB for LiDAR) and achieving faster decoding. Notably, TopNet generalizes to unseen datasets (nuScenes, LiDAR-CS, ScanNet) without retraining, a capability not demonstrated by most baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopNet sits directly in the lineage of octree-based Transformer entropy models for PCGC, with OctAttention (Fu et al., AAAI 2022) as its immediate predecessor and ablation baseline. Its core conceptual departure is a CNN-Transformer cooperation strategy (inspired by CTCNet) that replaces four OctAttention components—absolute positional encoding, standard self-attention, feed-forward network, and linear projection—with locally-enhanced convolutional encoding, length-scaled cosine attention, a spatial-gated channel mixer, and a star-operation-based predictor, respectively. The length-scaled cosine attention modifies Swin Transformer V2's scaled cosine attention by introducing a learnable temperature and log-N scaling to handle variable window lengths, while the star operation in LNOP is borrowed from the 'Rewrite the Stars' work. This positions TopNet as an efficiency-oriented refinement of the Transformer-for-PCGC paradigm rather than a fundamentally new compression framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20% parameter reduction (3.37M vs. 4.23M) combined with improved BPP across six diverse datasets makes TopNet a practical candidate for edge-deployed point cloud compression, where memory and compute budgets are tight. The length-scaled cosine attention addresses a known degradation mode of standard attention at long sequences, which is directly relevant as context windows grow with scene complexity. However, the approach remains confined to octree-based entropy modeling and does not address the broader PCGC pipeline (e.g., synthesis transform, coordinate refinement), and the generalization claim (trained on SemanticKITTI, tested on nuScenes/LiDAR-CS/ScanNet) is supported by only two comparison baselines due to limited code availability. Open questions include whether the CNN-Transformer cooperation strategy scales to higher-resolution or multi-modal point cloud compression tasks.
