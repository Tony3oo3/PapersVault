## Problem

Standard CLIP's one-to-one (image, text) contrastive paradigm overfits to noisy, brief web captions, producing a 'myopic' model biased toward monotonous short texts and shallow visual expressivity. A single image embedding cannot faithfully represent the multi-level, multi-granular semantics of a colorful image, leading to poor performance on long-context retrieval, fine-grained perception, and complex reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250910/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with M diverse text captions (M=2–5, generated by VLMs under different prompt spirits) plus the original web alt-text, and produces H multi-branch visual embeddings (H=M for part-to-part matching) alongside M text embeddings. At inference, the H visual embeddings can be averaged for general tasks or selectively combined (e.g., main-object branches vs. long-detail branches) to customize the representation per downstream task without retraining.

## Method

The pipeline has three upgrades over vanilla CLIP. (1) Data: a single VLM (InternVL2-8B) or a pool of VLMs generates M captions per image using diverse prompts spanning four 'spirits'—focus guide (foreground vs. background), physical vs. sensory, gaze (dense) vs. glance (compact), and complex reasoning (relationships/sequences)—yielding multi-view, multi-level textual descriptions. (2) Architecture: the CLIP ViT image encoder is modified into a multi-branch output via either Ψ_CLS (H learnable CLS tokens) or Ψ_MLP (the last three FFN layers are extended into H parallel sub-MLPs, adding only ~3%×H parameters), producing H distinct visual embeddings in one forward pass. (3) Optimization: a novel multi-to-multi (M2M) symmetric InfoNCE loss performs part-to-part matching between the H image embeddings and M text embeddings, replacing the one-to-multi-positive (O2M) contrastive objective that forces all texts onto a single image vector. This disentangled alignment avoids the 'chaotic semantics' and 'visual damage' of O2M, yielding interpretable, task-adaptable visual embeddings.

## Results

On MSCOCO short-text retrieval (CC12M), the best Holistic CLIP (Ψ_MLP, multi-VLM, 5 texts) achieves R@1 of 39.2 (I2T) and 40.2 (T2I), surpassing CLIP (25.2/25.3) by ~14 points and the strongest O2M baseline (36.6/38.5) by ~2.6 points. On Flickr30, the same configuration reaches R@1 of 77.7 (I2T) and 79.2 (T2I) vs. CLIP's 56.9/55.2. For long-text retrieval on Sharegpt4v-5K, Holistic CLIP attains R@1 of 63.1 (I2T) and 64.4 (T2I), outperforming OpenAI CLIP (49.4/45.8) by over 13 points and LongCLIP-finetuned O2M (62.4/63.7) without any Sharegpt4v fine-tuning data. On zero-shot ImageNet classification, the best model reaches 48.6% Top-1 (vs. CLIP 39.0%) and 62.1% on ImageNet-R (vs. 49.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CLIP [63] (trained on CC12M), Holistic CLIP wins decisively across all benchmarks: +14 R@1 on MSCOCO I2T, +21 R@1 on Flickr30 I2T, +9 R@1 on Sharegpt4v-5K I2T, and +9.6 points on ImageNet Top-1. Against the one-to-multi (O2M) baselines [19,50,88] (Fan et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2023, Zheng et al. 2025) reproduced under identical data, M2M consistently outperforms by 1.5–3 R@1 on short-text retrieval and 4–7 R@1 on long-text retrieval (Sharegpt4v-5K), with the gap widening as text diversity increases from 2 to 5 captions. Against LongCLIP [85] (Zhang et al., ECCV 2025), which is fine-tuned on 1M Sharegpt4v samples, Holistic CLIP trained only on CC12M still surpasses it on Fashion200-5K (16.7 vs. 13.2 I2T R@1) and Sharegpt4v-5K (63.1 vs. 62.4 I2T R@1), and leads on Urban1K (79.6 vs. 77.2 I2T R@1). The multi-VLM + uni-prompt data strategy yields the strongest overall results, while the multi-prompt + uni-VLM strategy is competitive and more practical, confirming that explicit prompt diversity is as effective as implicit VLM diversity. Both Ψ_CLS and Ψ_MLP multi-branch variants perform comparably, indicating that the M2M contrastive objective—not the specific architectural modification—is the primary driver of gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) by replacing its one-to-one contrastive alignment with a multi-to-multi (M2M) paradigm, where each image is matched against multiple diverse texts via a multi-branch visual encoder. It departs from recent one-to-multi (O2M) approaches (Fan et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2023, Zheng et al. 2025) that simply add multiple positive texts while retaining a single image embedding; here, the image encoder itself is modified (via additional CLS tokens or parallel MLP branches) to produce H differentiated visual embeddings for part-to-part matching. The data construction pipeline, which uses diverse prompts to steer a single VLM (InternVL2) into generating multi-view, multi-granularity captions, builds on the re-captioning idea from prior work but explicitly designs prompt 'spirits' (focus, physical/sensory, gaze/glance, complex reasoning) to maximize textual diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The M2M framework provides a practical path to richer, disentangled visual embeddings that can be selectively combined at inference time for different downstream tasks (e.g., main-object focus vs. long-text detail matching) without retraining, offering a degree of interpretability and robustness that single-embedding CLIP lacks. A notable practical finding is that carefully designed diverse prompts with one VLM match or exceed the performance of ensembling multiple VLMs, reducing deployment complexity. Open questions include whether the multi-branch approach scales to video or 3D modalities, how to automatically learn which branches to activate for a given task (beyond manual selection), and whether the gains persist when training on truly massive datasets (e.g., LAION-400M) rather than CC12M.
