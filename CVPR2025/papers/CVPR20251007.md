## Problem

Multi-class unified anomaly detection (MUAD) models, which train a single model across all object categories, still lag significantly behind class-separated UAD methods due to the 'identity mapping' phenomenon where the network reconstructs both normal and anomalous patterns. Existing solutions rely on complex, hand-crafted modules (neighbor-masked attention, vector quantization, diffusion models, synthetic anomalies) that limit universality and ease of use. This paper asks whether a minimalist, pure-Transformer reconstruction framework can close the performance gap without such elaborate designs.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 448² and center-cropped to 392², which are tokenized by a ViT-Base/14 encoder (patch size 14) into 28×28 spatial feature maps. A single unified model processes all classes simultaneously without class-specific parameters. At inference, it produces an image-level anomaly score from the overall reconstruction discrepancy and a pixel-level anomaly map computed as the mean per-point cosine distance across two semantic-level feature groups (low-level and high-level).

## Method

Dinomaly is a reconstruction-based autoencoder built exclusively from Self-Attention and MLP blocks, with a pretrained DINOv2-Register ViT-Base/14 as the frozen encoder and an 8-layer Transformer decoder. Four key components address identity mapping: (1) a Noisy Bottleneck MLP that applies Dropout (rate 0.2–0.4) to randomly discard encoder features, forcing the decoder to perform restoration rather than copying; (2) Linear Attention in the decoder (replacing Softmax) whose inability to focus on local regions prevents forwarding identical information; (3) Loose Constraint that groups multiple encoder layers into two semantic-level groups instead of enforcing layer-to-layer correspondence, giving the decoder more freedom to deviate on unseen patterns; and (4) Loose Loss (hard-mining global cosine loss) that shrinks gradients for well-reconstructed feature points, discarding easy regions during optimization. Training maximizes cosine similarity between grouped encoder and decoder features using StableAdamW with AMSGrad, and the anomaly score at test time is the per-point cosine distance that the decoder fails to minimize for anomalous regions.

## Results

On MVTec-AD (15 classes), Dinomaly achieves 99.6% image-level AUROC and 98.4% pixel-level AUROC (94.7% AUPRO), surpassing the previous best MUAD method (MambaAD, 98.6% I-AUROC) by 1.0 points. On VisA (12 classes), it reaches 98.7% I-AUROC and 98.7% P-AUROC (94.5% AUPRO), outperforming the prior SoTA by 3.2 points on image-level AUROC. On the more challenging Real-IAD (30 classes, 5 views), Dinomaly attains 89.3% I-AUROC and 98.8% P-AUROC (93.9% AUPRO), exceeding the previous best by 3.0 points on image-level AUROC. Scaling to ViT-Large further pushes MVTec-AD to 99.8% I-AUROC, and the class-separated variant reaches 99.7% I-AUROC on MVTec-AD, matching or exceeding dedicated per-class models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dinomaly outperforms all eight MUAD baselines across every dataset and every metric. The closest competitor, MambaAD [17], is beaten by 1.0 points on MVTec-AD I-AUROC (99.6 vs. 98.6) but by a much larger margin on pixel-level AP (69.3 vs. 56.3) and AUPRO (94.7 vs. 93.1); on VisA the gap widens to 4.4 points on I-AUROC (98.7 vs. 94.3), and on Real-IAD to 3.0 points (89.3 vs. 86.3). ReContrast [14], the second-strongest baseline, trails Dinomaly by 1.3 points on MVTec-AD I-AUROC and by 9.1 points on pixel-level AP (69.3 vs. 60.2). The diffusion-based DiAD [18] and vector-quantization-based UniAD [60] show substantially larger gaps, with DiAD losing 12.1 points on VisA I-AUROC (98.7 vs. 86.8) and UniAD losing 10.2 points on MVTec-AD I-AUROC (99.6 vs. 89.4). Against class-separated SoTAs (Table 2), Dinomaly under MUAD setting is nearly on par with its own per-class variant (99.6 vs. 99.7 on MVTec-AD I-AUROC) and matches or exceeds RD4AD [10], PatchCore [45], and SimpleNet [34] on all three datasets, confirming that the unified multi-class model no longer suffers the severe performance degradation that previously limited MUAD adoption.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dinomaly extends the feature-reconstruction UAD paradigm established by RD4AD and ViTAD, and the multi-class UAD (MUAD) setting introduced by UniAD, but deliberately departs from the trend of adding complex modules (neighbor-masked attention, vector quantization, diffusion models, Mamba) to address the identity-mapping problem. Instead, it reframes identity mapping as over-generalization and solves it with four minimal components: a DINOv2-pretrained ViT encoder, Dropout in an MLP bottleneck, Linear Attention in the decoder, and a loosened group-to-group reconstruction constraint. The hard-mining cosine loss is borrowed from ReContrast, and the overall architecture is a pure Transformer encoder-decoder without convolutions or specialized sub-modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the performance gap between multi-class unified models and class-separated SoTAs (e.g., 99.6% vs. 99.7% image-level AUROC on MVTec-AD), Dinomaly makes a single unified model practically viable for industrial deployment, eliminating the storage and maintenance overhead of per-class models. The finding that Dinomaly follows a clear scaling law in both model size and input resolution—contradicting prior reports that UAD networks do not scale—opens the door to simply adopting future foundation models for further gains. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video anomaly detection, 3D/medical volumes, or domains where the 'unfocusing' property of Linear Attention might be less beneficial, and whether the loose reconstruction strategy can be further generalized beyond cosine-similarity objectives.
