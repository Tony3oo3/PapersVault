## Problem

Existing multi-teacher distillation methods (AM-RADIO, UNIC, Theia) only unify encoders from homogeneous teachers trained on similar generic web-crawled images, and ImageNet-1K suffices as distillation data. This paper asks whether a single encoder can be distilled from teachers that differ significantly in both their design objectives (task-agnostic foundation models vs. specialized 3D/human-perception models) and their training data domains (natural images vs. synthetic 3D engine renders, CAD models, and simulator outputs).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252139/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at 336×336 (or 448×448 for higher-resolution variants) and produces a set of patch-level feature vectors plus an optional CLS token from a ViT-Base encoder. After distillation, the teacher-specific projectors are discarded; the frozen encoder's features are then fed to independently fine-tuned task decoders to yield segmentation masks, depth maps, SMPL body parameters, camera poses, or dense 3D matches. The encoder is resolution-agnostic at inference and requires no additional modules beyond the task head.

## Method

DUNE distills a ViT-Base student from three ViT-Large teachers (DINO-v2, MASt3R, Multi-HMR) using a combined cosine-similarity and smooth-L1 loss on both global and patch tokens. The key architectural contribution is the Transformer Projector (TP): a single transformer block (self-attention + MLP + linear projection) appended per teacher, which captures inter-patch interactions specific to each teacher's representation structure—unlike prior per-patch MLP projectors that force all cross-patch modeling into the shared encoder. The paper shows that full data sharing (all 19 teacher datasets fed to every projector) outperforms no-sharing or generic-only sharing, and that TP yields higher teacher-loss correlation and better performance than the Ladder of Projectors or simple MLP projectors. After distillation, projectors are removed and each task decoder is fine-tuned independently on the frozen encoder, enabling plug-and-play multi-task inference with no extra parameters at test time.

## Results

DUNE (ViT-Base, 336×336) achieves 44.9 mIoU on ADE20K, 0.377 RMSE on NYUv2, 91 F1 / 68.3 PA-PVE on BEDLAM, and 93.7 AUC on Map-free Visual Relocalization. At 448×448 resolution, DUNE improves to 45.6 mIoU, 0.358 RMSE, 94 F1 / 56.0 PA-PVE, and 94.7 AUC. Notably, DUNE surpasses its ViT-Large teacher MASt3R (91.2 AUC) on Map-free Visual Relocalization while using a significantly smaller encoder, and outperforms the ViT-Large Multi-HMR teacher on BEDLAM F1 (94 vs. 95 is close, but PA-PVE 56.0 vs. 36.9 shows the gap narrows substantially). DUNE also outperforms the prior best ViT-Base encoder AM-RADIO-v2.5 on NYUv2 depth (0.377 vs. 0.718 RMSE), BEDLAM F1 (91 vs. 89), and Map-free AUC (93.7 vs. 93.1), though it trails on ADE20K segmentation (44.9 vs. 50.0 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINO-v2 (ViT-Base), DUNE wins on NYUv2 depth (0.377 vs. 0.399 RMSE), BEDLAM F1 (91 vs. 86), BEDLAM PA-PVE (68.3 vs. 76.5), and Map-free AUC (93.7 vs. 89.6), but loses on ADE20K segmentation (44.9 vs. 47.3 mIoU). Against AM-RADIO-v2.5 (ViT-Base), DUNE wins on NYUv2 (0.377 vs. 0.718 RMSE), BEDLAM F1 (91 vs. 89), BEDLAM PA-PVE (68.3 vs. 83.2), and Map-free AUC (93.7 vs. 93.1), but loses on ADE20K (44.9 vs. 50.0 mIoU), which the authors attribute to AM-RADIO's semantically rich CLIP/OpenCLIP/SAM teachers. Against the ViT-Large teacher MASt3R, DUNE (ViT-Base) outperforms it on Map-free Visual Relocalization AUC (93.7 vs. 91.2 at 336 res; 94.7 vs. 91.2 at 448 res) and on the official leaderboard (Table 4: 94.3 vs. 93.3 AUC at VCRE<90px, 47.4 vs. 48.8 px median reprojection error). Against the ViT-Large teacher Multi-HMR, DUNE at 448 res narrows the BEDLAM gap substantially (F1 94 vs. 95; PA-PVE 56.0 vs. 36.9). On the Map-free leaderboard, DUNE also outperforms DUSt3R (69.8 AUC), MicKey (74.8 AUC), and LoFTR (61.8 AUC) at VCRE<90px by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-teacher distillation line of work (AM-RADIO, UNIC, Theia, PHI-S) by formulating and tackling the heterogeneous teacher setting, where teachers differ not only in architecture but also in their training objectives (task-agnostic vs. specialized) and training data domains (generic web images vs. synthetic 3D renders, CAD models, and human-centric datasets). The key methodological departure is the introduction of a Transformer Projector (a single attention-based block) to replace per-patch MLP projectors, motivated by the need to capture teacher-specific inter-patch interactions that differ dramatically across heterogeneous teachers. The data-sharing strategies studied here (no sharing, generic-only, full sharing) have no direct analogue in prior homogeneous-teacher distillation, where a single generic dataset sufficed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a single ViT-Base encoder that handles 2D segmentation, monocular depth, 3D scene reconstruction, and multi-person human mesh recovery without architecture changes, reducing deployment complexity for multi-task vision systems. The finding that full data sharing across heterogeneous domains outperforms domain-restricted sharing challenges the intuition that out-of-domain data is harmful, suggesting teacher representations remain informative even on unfamiliar inputs. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond three teachers, whether the Transformer Projector advantage persists with more architecturally diverse teachers, and whether the modest semantic-segmentation gap versus AM-RADIO-v2.5 (which benefits from CLIP/OpenCLIP semantic supervision) can be closed without sacrificing 3D performance.
