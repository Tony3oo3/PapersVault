## Problem

Centralized federated learning merges all client knowledge into a single aggregated model at a server, causing knowledge damage before results return to each client, and creates a single point of failure that destabilizes training if the server or connections malfunction. This paper eliminates both issues by removing the central server entirely and replacing global aggregation with per-client, adaptive knowledge fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250746/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client processes its own modality-specific input (histopathological images at varying resolutions, endoscopic images, sleep time-series signals, or low-resolution medical images) through a local body network to extract features. Only the lightweight head (final prediction layer) is transmitted between clients, reducing communication to ~0.02% of full-model sharing. The final output is produced by a local Mixture-of-Experts module that adaptively combines predictions from all received expert heads with the client's own head, yielding class labels, super-resolved images, or segmentation masks depending on the task.

## Method

dFLMoE splits each client's model into a body (feature extractor), a head (lightweight predictor), a Feature Space Transform (FST), and a cross-attention MoE module. Training proceeds in three phases per round: (1) local training of body and head on private data, (2) peer-to-peer exchange of only the head parameters among all N clients, and (3) MoE decision training where the FST and attention weights are fine-tuned while body and all heads are frozen. The FST maps local body features into each expert's feature space via a shared linear/conv layer followed by per-expert projections, so heterogeneous architectures can coexist without structural alignment. The MoE uses a cross-attention mechanism where the local body feature serves as the Query and the concatenated expert predictions serve as both Key and Value, enabling the client to adaptively weight experts and filter out local overfitting biases while retaining shared generalizable knowledge.

## Results

On the BreaKHis classification task (4 clients, different resolutions), dFLMoE achieves an average ACC of 0.8319 (homogeneous ResNet5) and 0.8880 (heterogeneous ResNet{17,11,8,5}), outperforming the strongest baseline MH-pFLID by 2.4 and 3.0 percentage points respectively. On the Polyp segmentation task, dFLMoE reaches an average Dice of 0.7813 (homogeneous Unet) and 0.7806 (heterogeneous Unet++/Unet/ResUnet/FCN), surpassing FedSM (0.7146) and MH-pFLID (0.7671). On the SleepEDF time-series task, dFLMoE attains an average ACC of 0.9041 (homogeneous TCN) and 0.9136 (heterogeneous TCN/Transformer/RNN), exceeding MH-pFLID by 3.4 and 3.4 points. Across all five medical tasks and both model settings, dFLMoE consistently achieves the best performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

dFLMoE outperforms all ten baselines across every task and both model settings. The closest competitor is MH-pFLID [52], a centralized model-heterogeneous personalized FL method; dFLMoE exceeds it by 2.4–3.4 ACC points on classification and time-series tasks, by ~0.03–0.07 Dice on segmentation, and by ~3.1–3.8 PSNR on super-resolution. Among the standard centralized FL baselines (FedAvg [36], SCAFFOLD [21], FedProx [24]), dFLMoE leads by 10–28 ACC points on the BreaKHis classification task, reflecting the severe knowledge damage from centralized weighted-sum aggregation under non-IID data. Personalized FL methods (Ditto [25], APFL [9], FedRep [8], LG-FedAvg [27]) perform better than vanilla FL but still trail dFLMoE by 3–10 ACC points, as they rely on a central server for knowledge fusion. Distillation-based methods (FedMD [23], FedDF [28]) and the heterogeneous-specific MH-pFLID [52] are the strongest baselines, yet dFLMoE's decentralized MoE with cross-attention consistently yields 1–4 point improvements, demonstrating that client-specific adaptive expert selection avoids the one-size-fits-all aggregation bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant centralized federated learning paradigm (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, FedProx, Ditto, APFL, FedRep) by eliminating the central server entirely and having clients exchange knowledge peer-to-peer. It repurposes the Mixture of Experts architecture—previously used for model capacity scaling—into a cross-client knowledge fusion mechanism, where each client's lightweight head model serves as an 'expert' and a cross-attention module adaptively weights their predictions. The feature space transform module is a novel component that aligns heterogeneous expert outputs into a common decision space, addressing the model heterogeneity problem that prior decentralized FL works (mostly blockchain-focused) have not tackled. The paper also builds on the personalized FL line (MH-pFLID, FedMD, pFedDF) but inverts the aggregation direction: instead of merging knowledge centrally, it fuses it locally per client.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the central server addresses a genuine deployment concern in medical federated learning, where institutional infrastructure may not support a reliable aggregation node, and the 0.02% communication overhead relative to full-model sharing makes the decentralized design practically viable. The consistent outperformance across five heterogeneous medical tasks (classification, super-resolution, segmentation, time-series) under both homogeneous and heterogeneous model settings strengthens the case that local MoE fusion preserves client-specific knowledge better than weighted-sum aggregation. However, the evaluation is limited to 3–8 clients, and scalability to dozens or hundreds of clients—where the O(N²) pairwise communication becomes prohibitive—remains an open question. The paper also does not address security threats (e.g., inference attacks on shared heads) or formal privacy guarantees beyond the absence of raw data exchange.
