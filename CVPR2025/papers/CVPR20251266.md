## Problem

Reconstructing 3D geometry, camera poses, and appearance from uncalibrated sparse-view images (2–8 views) remains challenging: traditional SfM-MVS pipelines are non-differentiable and fail under sparse views, while existing deep methods either require per-scene optimization, rely on pairwise geometry followed by slow global alignment (DUSt3R, MASt3R), or lack a unified feed-forward solution that jointly estimates all three quantities with strong generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251266/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 2–8 uncalibrated RGB images at 512×384 resolution with no camera intrinsics or extrinsics required, and generalizes at inference to as many as 25 views. It outputs 7D camera poses (absolute translation + normalized quaternion), dense 3D point maps in both camera-centric and global coordinates with per-pixel confidence scores, and a full set of 3D Gaussian parameters (position, opacity, rotation, scale, spherical harmonics) that enable photorealistic novel-view rendering in under 0.5 seconds.

## Method

FLARE introduces a cascaded learning paradigm that decomposes the joint estimation problem into sequential stages, using camera poses as geometric proxies to reduce complexity for downstream tasks. A decoder-only transformer (neural pose predictor) first regresses coarse poses from image patches and learnable camera latents; these poses then condition a second transformer that simultaneously refines poses and predicts camera-centric point tokens, which a DPT decoder upsamples into dense local point maps. A learnable global geometry projector (another transformer) unifies the local point tokens into a coherent world-coordinate structure, avoiding brittle direct geometric reprojection under pose noise. Finally, a Gaussian regression head—fusing DPT features with pretrained VGG image features through a shallow CNN—predicts all 3D Gaussian parameters for differentiable rendering. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss combining Huber pose loss, confidence-aware 3D regression loss, and a Gaussian splatting rendering loss (L2 + VGG perceptual + monocular depth), with Gaussian pose augmentation during training to build robustness to imperfect pose estimates.

## Results

On RealEstate10K pose estimation, FLARE achieves RRA@5° = 0.97, RTA@5° = 0.65, and AUC@30° = 79.9, substantially outperforming DUSt3R (0.83/0.37/54.9) and MASt3R (0.87/0.45/61.1). For sparse-view 3D reconstruction, FLARE attains the best overall Chamfer distance on all three benchmarks: DTU (3.1152 vs. DUSt3R 3.4891, MASt3R 3.7537), ETH3D (0.4978 vs. 0.5984/0.3522), and TUM RGBD (0.2048 vs. 0.3808/0.2810). On novel-view synthesis, FLARE reaches PSNR 23.77 dB on RealEstate10K (2 views) and 23.33 dB on DL3DV (8 views), surpassing pose-required methods MVSplat (22.57 dB) and pixelSplat (22.50 dB) despite requiring no camera extrinsics. All results are produced in a single feed-forward pass under 0.5 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLARE outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated task. In pose estimation (Table 1), FLARE's AUC@30° of 79.9 surpasses the previous best feed-forward method PoseDiffusion (51.6) by 28.3 points and the optimization-based VGGSfM (72.1) by 7.8 points; traditional SfM methods COLMAP (16.0) and PixSfM (29.9) lag far behind. In sparse-view geometry (Table 2), FLARE beats DUSt3R and MASt3R on overall Chamfer distance across all three datasets (e.g., TUM: 0.2048 vs. 0.3808 and 0.2810) and also improves pose AUC@30° on TUM from 37.4 (MASt3R) to 53.6. Spann3R, the only other feed-forward geometry baseline, is outperformed on both accuracy and completeness on all datasets. In novel-view synthesis (Tables 3–4), FLARE exceeds pose-required methods MVSplat (22.57 dB) and pixelSplat (22.50 dB) on RealEstate10K by over 1.2 dB PSNR, and outperforms pose-free methods CoPoNeRF (19.84 dB) and Splatt3R (15.11 dB) by 3.9 and 8.7 dB respectively, all without requiring any camera extrinsics as input.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLARE builds directly on DUSt3R's point-map geometry representation and confidence-aware regression loss, and on VGGSfM's differentiable pose loss formulation, but departs from both by replacing their two-view-plus-global-alignment pipeline with a fully multi-view, feed-forward transformer. The cascaded learning paradigm—using coarse camera poses as geometric proxies to condition downstream geometry and appearance learning—is the paper's key architectural departure from prior work, which either treats pose and geometry as jointly optimized (BARF, NeRF--) or regresses geometry without explicit pose conditioning (DUSt3R, MASt3R, Spann3R). For appearance, FLARE adopts 3D Gaussian Splatting with a DPT-based regression head, extending the Gaussian splatting line (MVSplat, PixelSplat, Splatt3R) to the uncalibrated, pose-free setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance of FLARE lies in its sub-0.5-second feed-forward inference for simultaneous pose, geometry, and photorealistic appearance from as few as 2 uncalibrated images, making it applicable to real-time or interactive 3D capture scenarios where optimization-based pipelines are infeasible. The cascaded 'poses-as-proxies' decomposition offers a reusable conceptual template for other multi-task 3D perception problems. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to highly dynamic or textureless scenes, whether the 8-view training limit constrains performance at the extremes (2 vs. 25 views), and whether the learned global geometry projector can be replaced by more principled geometric constraints as pose accuracy improves.
