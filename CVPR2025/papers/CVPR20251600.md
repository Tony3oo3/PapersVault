## Problem

Generating dense multiview images from text prompts is essential for high-fidelity 3D asset creation, but existing methods struggle with space-view correspondences, producing sparse or inconsistent outputs. Dense cross-view attention is computationally prohibitive at high view counts, sparse attention introduces inter-view inconsistencies, and temporal attention borrowed from video models fails to capture the substantial content jitter caused by azimuthal rotation around an object. The paper tackles two open questions: how to design efficient cross-view interactions that ensure dense multiview consistency, and which operator (attention vs. state space model) best serves different interaction scales.

## Input / Output

CoSER takes a text prompt (wrapped in the template 'A DSLR photo of [prompt], 3d asset') and a set of camera parameters with a fixed elevation angle and uniformly distributed azimuth angles spanning 0°–360° as input, and produces 12 dense multiview images at the same elevation. During training, the model is fine-tuned on 12 rendered views per object from G-Objaverse; during evaluation, 120 evenly spaced azimuth angles from −180° to 180° are rendered for CLIP scoring and user studies. The generated multiview images can be directly fed into reconstruction pipelines such as NeuS to obtain a 3D asset.

## Method

CoSER fine-tunes a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model with a hybrid attention + Mamba (selective SSM) architecture organized into four modules. For neighbor-view consistency, Appearance Awareness replaces self-attention with adjacent-attention over the previous, current, and next views to capture rough structure, while Detail Refinement applies trajectory-based attention that uses a physical rotation formula to identify co-located pixels across neighboring frames within a 3×3 window, enforcing point-to-point alignment. For global-view coherence, Rapid Glance processes all views through a spiral bidirectional scan strategy that organizes spatial tokens from center outward in a spiral and reverses scan order across viewpoints, leveraging Mamba's linear-time SSM to aggregate holistic information efficiently. Finally, Accumulated Inconsistency Rectification scores each patch's textual relevance via a 2-layer MLP, performs score-weighted 2D pooling on key and value maps (with higher resolution retained for queries), and applies sparse attention to correct accumulated inconsistencies across all views with lightweight computation.

## Results

On T3Bench (100 prompts, 120 rendered views per object), CoSER achieves a CLIP Score of 33.07 and a prompt-recall alignment of 37.7, surpassing all baselines. In user studies (100 responses per metric on 30 text-3D pairs), CoSER is selected as best in 31% of cases for semantic alignment, 29% for multiview consistency, and 32% for texture detail. The method uses 969.95M parameters and 1487G MACs for the full pipeline (AA+DR+RG+AIR), with the SSM-based modules (RG+AIR) adding only 3.31M parameters and 15G MACs over the attention-only variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSER outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against GaussianDreamer [69], CoSER gains +2.29 in CLIP Score and +4.0 in alignment, with the largest user-study margin in texture detail (+0.11). Against Hash3D [68], the advantage is most pronounced in alignment (+7.5) and consistency (+0.10), reflecting Hash3D's weaker cross-view coherence. Against the closest competitor VideoMV [74], CoSER leads by +0.88 in CLIP Score, +3.3 in alignment, and +0.10 in texture, while the consistency gap is smaller (+0.04) — consistent with VideoMV's use of video-diffusion priors that partially capture rotational continuity. Qualitatively, the paper notes that VideoMV can generate images from a broader range of perspectives but significantly compromises cross-view consistency, whereas CoSER's dense 12-view output yields more faithful 3D reconstructions via NeuS.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSER extends the multiview text-to-image generation line initiated by MVDream and Wonder3D, which rely on dense cross-view attention to capture 3D structure but suffer from quadratic complexity. It departs from sparse attention approaches (ERA3D) and video-diffusion-based methods (VideoMV, SV3D) that use temporal attention, arguing that azimuthal rotation around an object causes content jitter that temporal continuity priors cannot handle. The key architectural departure is the hybrid design: local neighbor interactions use attention (adjacent attention + trajectory-based detail refinement), while global cross-view coherence is handled by a selective state space model (Mamba) with a novel spiral bidirectional scan, replacing the default quadratic attention for long-range dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for scaling multiview diffusion to dense viewpoints (12+ views) without the prohibitive cost of full space-view attention, which is directly relevant to downstream 3D reconstruction pipelines. The spiral bidirectional scan and score-conditioned downsampling are reusable components that could transfer to other dense-view or long-sequence generation tasks. However, evaluation is limited to a single training dataset (G-Objaverse), a single reconstruction backend (NeuS), and a fixed elevation angle; generalization to open scenes, variable elevations, or more modern reconstruction methods (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting) remains untested. The unsupervised score map mechanism, while effective, lacks theoretical grounding and its behavior under distribution shift is unclear.
