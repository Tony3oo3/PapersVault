## Problem

Existing diffusion models for histopathology generate images directly without explicitly modeling the spatial arrangement and topological relationships of individual cells, making it difficult to validate outputs against pathologists' domain knowledge, control generation, or ensure biologically faithful cell distributions. No prior method simultaneously preserves intra-class spatial structure, inter-class topological interactions, and accurate per-type cell counts in synthetic multi-class cell layouts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250558/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a cell count vector c = [c₁, …, cₙ] specifying the desired number of cells per type as its sole conditioning signal. It produces a multi-channel binary layout where each channel corresponds to one cell type, with each cell rendered as a 3×3 pixel square (value 1) on a zero-valued background. These layouts can subsequently condition a separate guided diffusion model to synthesize full H&E-stained pathology images with spatially consistent cell placement.

## Method

TopoCellGen builds on a standard DDPM (UNet denoiser) but augments the noise-prediction objective with three topology-aware losses computed on a deterministically predicted noiseless layout x̂₀ᵗ. A cell counting loss uses the Straight-Through Estimator to make hard binarization differentiable, then penalizes the L1 difference between predicted and ground-truth per-channel cell counts normalized by the 3×3 cell area. An intra-class spatial consistency loss computes Euclidean distance-transform maps per channel, extracts 1D persistence diagrams, and minimizes the squared Wasserstein distance between predicted and target diagrams. An inter-class structural regularization aggregates all channels into a single binary mask and applies the same persistence-diagram Wasserstein loss to enforce collective spatial coherence across cell types. The total objective is a weighted sum of the standard diffusion loss and these three terms, with weights tuned to 5e-4 for optimal balance.

## Results

On BRCA-M2C, TopoCellGen achieves a FID of 0.005, Total Count Error (TCE) of 5.192, TopoFD of 69.354, and MMD of 0.421, substantially outperforming all baselines. On Lizard, it reaches FID 0.027, TCE 11.590, TopoFD 31.607, and MMD 0.536. For downstream cell detection and classification on BRCA-M2C, augmenting with TopoCellGen-generated data yields the best mean F1 (0.678 with UNet, 0.684 with MCSpatNet) and detection F1 (0.860 and 0.862 respectively), surpassing both real-data-only training and augmentation with other synthetic methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoCellGen outperforms all three baselines (ADM, TMCCG, and Spatial Diffusion) on every reported metric across both datasets. On BRCA-M2C, it reduces FID from 0.263 (Spatial Diffusion, the closest competitor) to 0.005, cuts TCE from 19.687 (TMCCG) to 5.192, and lowers TopoFD from 89.252 (TMCCG) to 69.354. On Lizard, the gains are even more pronounced: TopoFD drops from 63.120 (TMCCG) to 31.607, and TCE falls from 20.606 (Spatial Diffusion) to 11.590. For downstream tasks, TopoCellGen-augmented training achieves the highest mean F1 (0.678/0.684) and detection F1 (0.860/0.862) with UNet/MCSpatNet, edging out Spatial Diffusion (0.666/0.669) and TMCCG (0.643/0.667) by 1–2 percentage points. Notably, ADM performs worst on BRCA-M2C (FID 1.150, TCE 22.465) but is competitive on Lizard FID (0.059), suggesting its limitations are more pronounced in multi-class settings with more cell types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDPM as its generative backbone but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion models for histopathology (e.g., DiffInfinite, Graikos et al.) that generate pixel-level images without explicit cell-level structure. It extends the topology-guided generation line initiated by TMCCG (Abousamra et al., CVPR 2023, which used GANs with spatial statistics) and TopoDiffusionNet (Gupta et al., 2024, which applied persistent homology to natural image synthesis) by formulating the first diffusion model specifically for multi-class cell topology in pathology, with both intra-class and inter-class persistent homology losses. The cell counting loss via Straight-Through Estimator addresses a known failure mode in prior diffusion-based layout generators (e.g., Spatial Diffusion) that produce unrealistic cell densities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By generating cell layouts as an intermediate representation rather than raw images, TopoCellGen makes synthetic pathology data directly verifiable against pathologists' domain knowledge and enables controllable generation of the tumor microenvironment. The TopoFD metric fills a genuine gap: FID cannot distinguish spatially coherent layouts from those with correct counts but wrong topology, a limitation the paper demonstrates concretely. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with binary-mask layouts (no cell morphology or size variation), a single-pathologist plausibility check, and modest downstream F1 gains (~1–2 points), leaving open whether the topological fidelity translates to meaningful improvements on more complex, multi-organ, or 3D tissue tasks.
