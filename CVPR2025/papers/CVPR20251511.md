## Problem

3D object discovery remains under-explored relative to 2D, with existing 3D approaches relying exclusively on sparse 3D motion cues that demand extensive domain-specific fine-tuning. The substantial advances in motion-guided 2D object discovery have not been transferred to the 3D setting, and no framework exists for cross-modal object discovery that exploits the complementary strengths of RGB and LiDAR sensors to handle modality-specific failure cases (e.g., night scenes for cameras, low-reflectivity objects for LiDAR).

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video sequences and LiDAR point cloud sequences, where point clouds are front-projected into 4-channel (X, Y, Z, distance) 2D images at 480×968 resolution; 2D motion masks from optical flow serve as guidance, filtered to exclude regions lacking corresponding 3D data to prevent hallucinations in the upper FOV. At inference, the system supports RGB-only, point-cloud-only, or multi-modal inputs, producing per-slot object segmentation masks and (for the 3D branch) completed point clouds, with an optional late-fusion step that retains only predictions overlapping by at least threshold τ across both modalities.

## Method

The core contribution is xMOD, a cross-modal distillation framework that jointly trains two teacher-student systems (one per modality) where each teacher's binarized attention maps serve as pseudo-labels for the other modality's student, leveraging the 2D/3D domain gap to reduce confirmation bias while always using 2D motion cues as the shared guidance signal. For the 3D branch (DIOD-3D), scene completion replaces scene reconstruction as the pretext task: the model predicts missing point positions from neighbors via MSE loss, directly addressing LiDAR sparsity that would otherwise propagate into incomplete object segments. The slot-attention architecture with Hungarian matching and confidence-weighted BCE loss handles object assignment, and a late-fusion strategy at inference retains only predictions consistent across both modalities to boost reliability. Training proceeds in two phases: a burn-in period supervised solely by motion pseudo-labels, followed by a distillation phase where cross-modal pseudo-label exchange (2D→3D and 3D→2D) replaces intra-modal distillation, which ablations show to be a redundant constraint.

## Results

On the multi-modal object discovery task (Table 1), xMOD(2D+3D) achieves F1@50 scores of 42.5 (TRI-PD), 27.4 (KITTI), and 42.6 (WOD), outperforming the 2D SOTA DIOD by +11.9, +8.7, and +15.1 points respectively. The 3D baseline DIOD-3D reaches 39.6 F1@50 on dense synthetic TRI-PD but drops to 15.5 on sparse real-world KITTI, highlighting the sparsity challenge. Cross-modal training alone (without late fusion) already improves the 2D branch by +4.9 F1 on TRI-PD and +7.6 on WOD over DIOD, while the 3D branch gains +3.4 and +4.6 on KITTI and WOD. In the 2D-only evaluation (Table 2, fg-ARI), xMOD(2D) scores 75.5 on KITTI, surpassing DIOD (73.5) and DINOSAUR (70.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

xMOD(2D+3D) outperforms all seven compared baselines on every dataset and metric reported. Against the closest 2D SOTA DIOD [15], the full multi-modal pipeline gains +11.9 F1@50 on TRI-PD (42.5 vs 30.6), +8.7 on KITTI (27.4 vs 18.7), and +15.1 on WOD (42.6 vs 27.5). In the 2D-only fg-ARI evaluation (Table 2), xMOD(2D) beats DIOD by +2.0 on KITTI (75.5 vs 73.5) and +1.9 on TRI-PD (68.0 vs 66.1), and surpasses DINOSAUR [30] by +5.2 on KITTI (75.5 vs 70.3). The gap over earlier motion-guided methods is substantial: MoTok [2] (64.4), BMOD [14] (54.7), Bao et al. [1] (47.1), PPMP [16] (51.9), and SAVI++ [10] (23.9) all trail xMOD(2D) by at least 11 points in fg-ARI on KITTI. Notably, even the 3D-only xMOD(3D) branch (18.9 F1@50 on KITTI) exceeds the 2D DIOD baseline (18.7), and the DIOD-3D baseline alone (39.6 F1@50) already surpasses DIOD on dense synthetic TRI-PD, confirming that 3D geometry is a strong complementary signal when point density is sufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DIOD self-distillation framework for 2D motion-guided object discovery [15] to the 3D domain, introducing DIOD-3D as the first baseline for multi-object discovery from LiDAR point clouds using 2D motion cues rather than 3D scene flow. It departs from prior 3DOD work (which relies on 3D motion or clustering) by projecting point clouds into 2D front-view images and reusing optical-flow-derived pseudo-labels, and from intra-modal distillation by proposing cross-modal teacher-student interactions between 2D and 3D branches (xMOD). The background-aware slot supervision from [14] and the motion pseudo-label generation from [1] are carried over as foundational components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-modal distillation framework addresses a practical gap in multi-sensor autonomous driving: when one modality fails (camera at night, LiDAR on low-reflectivity surfaces), the other can still provide supervision, and the domain gap between modalities reduces confirmation bias. The late-fusion strategy using inter-domain consistency as a confidence criterion is a simple but effective inference-time technique. However, performance degrades sharply for distant objects (F1 drops to 7.2 beyond 30 m) and very close objects (F1 = 21.7 under 10 m), limiting real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond driving scenes, whether it scales to additional modalities (e.g., radar, thermal), and whether the reliance on pre-computed optical flow can be replaced by learned motion representations.
