## Problem

Existing single-view 3D reconstruction methods trained from image collections with 2D annotations focus exclusively on whole-object shape and ignore the decomposition into semantic parts (partonomy), which is essential for agents interacting with physical environments. Adding part decomposition expands the solution space dramatically and is further complicated by frequent part occlusions in single-view images, making direct 2D-to-3D partonomic mapping extremely difficult with limited supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250733/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 64×64 RGB image as input and produces a 642-vertex, 1280-face 3D mesh with soft part assignments (K part classes per category), an object pose (rotation and translation), and a 32×32 texture map upsampled to 64×64. During training, the only supervision signals are 2D object masks and part masks rendered at 64×64 resolution; no 3D shape, pose, depth, or multi-view annotations are used. The mesh is generated from a 162-vertex sphere template upsampled via Loop subdivision to 642 vertices.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical compositional representation that disentangles large shape variations from fine part deformations: a Conditional Shape Template (CST) maps a coarse sphere mesh to a coarse object shape with soft part assignments via an MLP conditioned on an object latent vector, while Part Deformation Fields (PDFs) apply per-part MLP-based deformations after subdivision upsampling, conditioned on part-specific latent vectors. A Part Transformer recovers these latent vectors from the image by using K learnable part tokens that cross-attend to U-Net-encoded image features (grounding each part to relevant pixels) and then self-attend among themselves (modeling part-to-part relations to handle occlusions). Training follows an analysis-by-synthesis paradigm with a differentiable renderer, optimizing jointly over RGB image synthesis loss, object mask IoU loss, part mask cross-entropy loss, and a smoothness/consistency regularizer, all with only 2D mask supervision.

## Results

On ShapeNetPart, the proposed method achieves an average Chamfer-L1 of 0.197, Part Chamfer-L1 of 0.379, Part Classification Accuracy of 0.675, and Part mIoU of 0.514, outperforming both Unicorn* and AST* across all four metrics. On PartNet, it achieves the lowest average Chamfer-L1 (0.209) and Part Chamfer-L1 (0.403), compared to Unicorn* (0.223 / 0.480) and AST* (0.224 / 0.414). The method shows the largest gains on complex shapes such as lamp (Chamfer-L1: 0.340 vs. 0.499 for Unicorn*) and table (Chamfer-L1: 0.189 vs. 0.247 for Unicorn*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines constructed by extending state-of-the-art whole-object reconstruction methods (Unicorn and AST) with per-vertex part classification and a part rendering loss. The proposed method consistently outperforms both baselines on all four metrics across both ShapeNetPart and PartNet. On ShapeNetPart, it reduces average Chamfer-L1 by 0.052 over Unicorn* (0.197 vs. 0.249) and by 0.020 over AST* (0.197 vs. 0.217), while improving Part mIoU by 5.6 and 6.8 percentage points respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on complex categories: for lamp, Chamfer-L1 drops from 0.499 (Unicorn*) to 0.340, and for table from 0.247 to 0.189. On PartNet, the Part Chamfer-L1 improvement over Unicorn* is particularly large (0.403 vs. 0.480, a 16% relative reduction), indicating that the compositional representation and part transformer are especially effective at recovering part-level geometry that the baselines struggle with.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analysis-by-synthesis paradigm for single-view 3D reconstruction (CMR, Unicorn, AST) from whole-object shape recovery to partonomic reconstruction, introducing a hierarchical compositional representation (conditional shape template + part deformation fields) and a part transformer with learnable part tokens. It departs from shape abstraction methods (cuboids, superquadrics, CSG trees) and part discovery methods (LPD, LASSIE, Hi-LASSIE) by requiring no 3D supervision whatsoever, relying solely on 2D object and part masks. The key conceptual departure from the naive extension of whole-object methods with per-vertex part labels is the explicit modeling of part-whole structural compositionality through disentangled multi-granularity neural fields and cross-part attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Partonomic understanding is essential for intelligent agents (robotics, AR/VR) that must reason about object affordances, yet no prior method learns partonomic 3D reconstruction from image collections with only 2D annotations. The paper demonstrates that the compositional representation and part transformer effectively handle the expanded solution space and frequent part occlusions, with the visible/invisible ablation showing minimal performance degradation on occluded regions. Limitations include per-category training, small 64×64 resolution, and evaluation on only 5 categories per dataset; open questions include scalability to open-vocabulary partonomies, variable part counts, and real-world deployment without category-specific training.
