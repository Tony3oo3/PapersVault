## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image restoration methods either require cumbersome prompt engineering that causes mutual interference between content and degradation in visual embeddings, or rely on text descriptions that cannot accurately capture complex illumination-related degradations (e.g., haze). Moreover, most approaches handle only a single degradation type at inference time and struggle with real-world images containing multiple co-occurring corruptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded RGB image (512×512, matching SD V2.1-512) and a precomputed Feature Difference Instruction (FDI) vector derived from reference clean/degraded image pairs via BLIP-2's Q-former subtraction. The FDI is a fixed-length feature vector per degradation type, not an image, and can be sourced from any dataset of the same degradation class. Output is a restored clean image at the same resolution; optionally, an MLP classifier produces multi-label degradation type scores that weight composable adapter branches for mixed-corruption scenarios.

## Method

The key insight is that BLIP-2's Q-former features for clean and degraded images are linearly separable, so their simple subtraction yields a degradation-specific instruction (FDI) that is interpretable by LLMs and directly usable as restoration guidance. The FDI adapter injects this guidance into a frozen Stable Diffusion V2.1-512 through three mechanisms: (1) cross-attention-based noise decoupling that refines FDI against multi-scale image features extracted from the U-Net, (2) SEBlock channel-attention weights applied to encoding layers via direct feature addition (similar to T2I-Adapter), and (3) an AdaIN-based visual modulator in decoding layers that regularizes features toward the clean-image distribution. Content is preserved via DDIM inversion of the degraded image into the latent space, and only the 45M-parameter adapter is trained (AdamW, 50 epochs, ~8 h on 4 A100s) while the diffusion backbone remains frozen. An optional multi-label degradation classifier enables both composite (weighted sum of multiple adapter branches) and progressive (sequential single-task adapters) mixed-degradation restoration without retraining.

## Results

On the degradation-specific setting (Table 1), DiffRes achieves the best PSNR/SSIM on 7 out of 9 tasks, e.g., 34.55/0.935 on Rain100H deraining (vs. DA-CLIP's 33.91/0.926), 30.87/0.970 on RESIDE dehazing (vs. 30.16/0.936), and 31.32/0.925 on Snow100K desnowing (vs. 30.83/0.907). In the all-in-one setting (Table 2), DiffRes attains the best average PSNR of 29.78 and SSIM of 0.911 across 5 tasks, surpassing InstructIR-5D (29.55/0.907) and DA-CLIP (29.23/0.898). For mixed-degradation removal (Table 3), the composite adapter achieves 25.11 PSNR / 0.702 SSIM / 0.230 LPIPS on RainDS, outperforming InstructIR-5D (24.26/0.647/0.330) by a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffRes consistently outperforms DA-CLIP [45], the primary CLIP-based restoration baseline, across nearly all tasks: e.g., +0.64 dB PSNR on deraining (34.55 vs. 33.91), +0.71 dB on dehazing (30.87 vs. 30.16), and +0.49 dB on desnowing (31.32 vs. 30.83). Against the strongest all-in-one competitor InstructIR-5D [14], DiffRes gains +0.23 dB average PSNR (29.78 vs. 29.55) and +0.004 SSIM, with the largest margin on deraining (+0.41 dB) and denoising (+0.67 dB), though it trails slightly on deblurring (29.33 vs. 29.40 PSNR). PromptIR [55] is surpassed on 7 of 9 single-task benchmarks, with notable gains on raindrop removal (+1.65 dB) and low-light enhancement (+0.80 dB). On mixed-degradation removal, DiffRes's composite adapter beats InstructIR-5D by +0.85 dB PSNR and −0.10 LPIPS on RainDS, and by +0.85 dB PSNR on OutDoor-Rain. Task-specific baselines (MPRNet, MAXIM, DehazeFormer, DesnowNet, etc.) are generally outperformed, confirming that the FDI-based guidance from BLIP-2 provides more targeted degradation cues than hand-crafted or prompt-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the intersection of vision-language understanding (BLIP-2) and latent diffusion generation (Stable Diffusion), following the adapter-injection paradigm established by T2I-Adapter. It departs from prior CLIP-based restoration methods (DA-CLIP, MPerciver) that derive degradation prompts from the input degraded image—causing content-degradation interference—by instead computing a Feature Difference Instruction through simple subtraction of Q-former outputs from reference clean/degraded image pairs. The DDIM inversion mechanism provides content anchoring in latent space, while the multi-scale FDI adapter (SEBlock channel attention + AdaIN visual modulator) injects degradation guidance without modifying the frozen diffusion backbone, positioning the work as a lightweight tuning alternative to full retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play adapter design (45M parameters, ~8 hours training) makes DiffRes practically deployable across 10+ restoration tasks with composable branches for mixed degradations, addressing a real-world gap where multiple corruptions co-occur. The finding that BLIP-2 features are linearly separable between clean and degraded images—and that this separation is interpretable by LLMs—opens a path for using vision-language models as degradation oracles beyond prompt engineering. Open questions include whether the reference-pair requirement limits real-world applicability (no clean reference at test time), how FDI quality degrades with domain shift, and whether the linear-separability assumption generalizes to more complex or novel degradation types not seen during adapter training.
