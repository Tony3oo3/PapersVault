## Problem

AIGC text-to-image and text-to-video models frequently generate human photos with structurally abnormal bodies (absent or redundant limbs, heads, etc.), yet existing Vision-Language Models perform near-randomly on detecting these abnormalities despite excelling at other visual perception tasks. This paper formalizes the Fine-grained Human-body Abnormality Detection (FHAD) task, constructs two evaluation datasets, and demonstrates that the gap between human and VLM abnormality perception is substantial and unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single human photo (image or video frame) and a predefined set of six body part classes (head, ear, hand, arm, leg, foot) as input. It outputs, for each detected abnormality, a bounding box and a semantic label specifying both the body part and whether it is absent or redundant (12 predefined categories total). In repair mode, it additionally produces a corrected image where only the abnormal region is regenerated while all other visual content (background, expression, pose) is preserved.

## Method

HumanCalibrator decomposes detection into two specialized sub-problems. For absent body parts, it fine-tunes LLaVA-7B using a body-part-correlation training strategy: each body part is sequentially masked in normal COCO images, and the model is trained autoregressively to predict the missing part's identity and location from the remaining visible parts. For redundant body parts, it combines a grounding model (which localizes all body parts) with a diffusion-based inpainting model: each grounded region is inpainted, and if the regenerated content semantically diverges from the original (e.g., a redundant arm is replaced by background), the part is flagged as redundant. Repair then applies the inpainting model iteratively to each detected abnormality using predefined prompt templates, preserving all non-abnormal content.

## Results

On the AIGC Human-Aware 1K dataset, HumanCalibrator achieves 80.69% average accuracy for absent body part detection and 58.57% for redundant body part detection, substantially outperforming the best baseline (CLIP-Large-14 at 25.82% and 22.75% respectively). On the COCO Human-Aware Val (real-world images with only absent abnormalities), the trained Absent Human-body Detector (AHD) also significantly surpasses all VLMs, whose accuracies are close to random guessing. For repair quality, HumanCalibrator improves Human Concept Score from 22.59 to 22.77 and achieves a FID of 16.55 versus 98.86 for the pose-conditioned baseline (HumanRefiner), while maintaining a Latent Consistency of 0.964 vs. 0.668.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HumanCalibrator decisively outperforms all four baselines on both absent and redundant detection. Against LLaVA-34B (9.80% absent / 16.56% redundant avg accuracy), the gap is roughly 71 and 42 percentage points respectively. InternVL2-26B (19.37% / 15.99%) and GPT-4o (8.62% / 8.18%) are even further behind, confirming that even the strongest closed-source VLMs lack fine-grained abnormality perception. CLIP-Large-14 is the strongest baseline (25.82% / 22.75%) but still trails HumanCalibrator by over 55 and 36 points. For repair quality, HumanCalibrator reduces FID from 98.86 to 16.55 and raises Latent Consistency from 0.668 to 0.964 compared to the pose-conditioned HumanRefiner [14], demonstrating that fine-grained inpainting preserves far more of the original visual content than whole-image regeneration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as distinct from both AIGC authenticity detection (is this image AI-generated?) and AIGC quality assessment (how good is the generation?), instead introducing fine-grained structural abnormality detection with specific body-part localization. It builds on LLaVA as the backbone for the Absent Human-body Detector, fine-tuned via a novel body-part-correlation masking strategy, and leverages diffusion-based inpainting (in the spirit of HumanRefiner [14]) for redundant-part detection and repair. The key departure from HumanRefiner is the shift from coarse-grained whole-image regeneration to fine-grained, part-level detection and localized repair that preserves surrounding content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal benchmark (two datasets) for fine-grained human body abnormality detection in AIGC content, a task with clear practical relevance for content pipelines in entertainment, VR/AR, and e-commerce. The consistent near-random performance of state-of-the-art VLMs (GPT-4o, InternVL2, LLaVA) on this task highlights a specific perceptual blind spot in current multimodal models that the community should address. Limitations include the predefined set of 12 body-part abnormality classes, the complex multi-stage pipeline that limits stability, and the modest repair quality gains; open questions include generalization to arbitrary body structures, video-level temporal consistency, and whether the VLM deficit reflects a fundamental training-data gap or a task-specific reasoning failure.
