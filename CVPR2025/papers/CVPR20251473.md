## Problem

Prompt tuning is widely regarded as an efficient paradigm for adapting pre-trained vision-language models, but this paper reveals that freezing VLM parameters during context-vector learning yields only marginal memory (6.3%) and time (15.8%) savings over full fine-tuning while sacrificing 3–4% accuracy. The paper asks whether both the length and width of feature-gradient propagation flows in the FT baseline can be reduced simultaneously to achieve genuine efficiency gains without performance loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251473/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ViT-B/16) and hand-crafted text prompts of the form 'a photo of a [CLS]' for each class. During training, the image-text matching loss is computed only over a filtered subset of class tokens (m ≪ M) per image, and only through the deep layers of both encoders. At inference, the full model is used normally: the image encoder produces a feature vector, the text encoder produces per-class features, and the predicted class is the one with highest cosine similarity.

## Method

Skip Tuning modifies the full fine-tuning procedure with two complementary strategies that reduce the length and width of feature-gradient propagation flows (FGPFs) without adding any extra parameters. Layer-wise Skipping (LSkip) pre-computes and caches intermediate features at the ω-th layer of both the vision and text encoders, then fine-tunes only the remaining N−ω deep layers, shortening every forward/backward pass. Class-wise Skipping (CSkip) applies an exponential image-conditioned sampling strategy: for each training image, the top r×M closest class tokens (by cosine similarity to the image feature) are always retained, while the remaining (1−r)×M classes are sampled with probability e^{−λ(o_j − r×M)}, where o_j is the sorted rank. This adaptively narrows the softmax denominator in the ITM loss per image, filtering redundant text features. The key empirical finding is that shallow layers (measured by Feature Sensitivity) and most class tokens (measured by Gradient Dependence) contribute negligibly to task-specific knowledge, so skipping them improves both efficiency and generalization.

## Results

On the base-to-new generalization benchmark (11 datasets), Skip Tuning achieves an average harmonic-mean accuracy of 81.11% (Base 85.04%, New 77.53%), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art PromptSRC (79.97%) by 1.14% while reducing training time from 1735 s to 239 s (×7.2) and memory from 2041 M to 404 M (×5.1). On cross-dataset generalization (ImageNet → 10 target datasets), Skip Tuning reaches 67.00% average target accuracy (vs. 65.81% for PromptSRC) with ×44 time and ×21.5 memory efficiency. Against adapter-based methods, Skip Tuning surpasses LoRA by 3.59% in H accuracy while using ×3.8 less time and ×3.9 less memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skip Tuning outperforms all nine prompt-tuning baselines on the average harmonic-mean accuracy across 11 datasets, with the largest margin over CoOp (+9.45% H) and CoCoOp (+5.28% H), and a more modest but consistent gain over the strongest competitors PromptSRC (+1.14% H) and TCP (+1.60% H). On efficiency, Skip Tuning is dramatically faster and lighter than every prompt-tuning method: it uses 239 s and 404 M versus 1735 s / 2041 M for PromptSRC and 1186 s / 3204 M for CoOp. Against adapter-based methods (Table 5), Skip Tuning beats LoRA by 3.59% H accuracy (81.11 vs. 77.52) while cutting training time from 910 s to 239 s and memory from 1580 M to 404 M; it also surpasses CLIP-adapter by 6.97% H accuracy (81.11 vs. 74.14). On cross-dataset and domain generalization, Skip Tuning improves average target accuracy by 1.19% and 0.55% respectively over PromptSRC while reducing training time by a factor of 44 and memory by a factor of 21.5. Notably, Skip Tuning is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best base-task and new-task accuracy, avoiding the base/new trade-off observed in methods like CoPrompt and DePT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant prompt tuning (PT) paradigm (CoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC, DePT) by arguing that freezing VLM parameters to learn context vectors is neither necessary nor particularly effective for memory/time efficiency. It also contrasts with adapter-based methods (LoRA, CLIP-adapter) that inject extra trainable modules into the frozen backbone. Instead, Skip Tuning operates directly on the full fine-tuning baseline, reducing computation by skipping shallow layers (LSkip) and filtering unimportant class tokens (CSkip) without introducing any new parameters or modules. The implementation builds on DePT's codebase, and the conceptual framing—reducing the 'length and width' of feature-gradient propagation flows—repurposes the skip-connection idea from a training-regularization context into an efficiency mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely-held assumption in the VLM adaptation community: that parameter efficiency (fewer learnable weights) is the right proxy for practical efficiency. By showing that PT saves only ~6% memory and ~16% time while sacrificing 3–4% accuracy, it redirects attention toward reducing actual computation. The practical impact is significant—×7–44× time and ×5–21× memory savings with equal or better accuracy—making VLM fine-tuning accessible on modest hardware. However, the evaluation is confined to CLIP ViT-B/16 with the ITM loss, leaving open whether the layer-skip and class-skip heuristics transfer to larger backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H), other VLM architectures (e.g., Flamingo, BLIP-2), or non-contrastive training objectives.
