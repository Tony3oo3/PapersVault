## Problem

Existing online continual learning (OCL) methods rely heavily on replay, regularization, and distillation to preserve old knowledge, but they largely overlook the model's adaptability for learning generalizable and discriminative features from data that appears only once. While selective state space models (S6/Mamba) offer dynamic operation weights that could improve adaptation, a single discretization mechanism struggles to capture precise scan patterns under the limited context of one-epoch online training, and may fail on nonlinear or multi-scale dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) arriving in mini-batches of 10 samples per step, supplemented by 64 buffer samples drawn from a memory of configurable size M. The S6MOD module is inserted after a ResNet-18 backbone and produces a feature vector that is classified by a fixed equiangular tight frame (ETF) classifier, whose output distribution is used to supervise the base method's own classification head via KL divergence. The module is plug-and-play: it does not alter the base method's architecture or training loop, only adds an auxiliary branch and loss terms.

## Method

S6MOD introduces a plug-and-play branch after the backbone that applies SS2D (four-directional selective scan) to backbone features, with a key modification: instead of a single discretization Δ, a sparse mixture-of-experts produces N candidate Δ values via separate projection layers, and a sparse gating mechanism selects the top-Nk candidates weighted by importance. The number Nk is dynamically set per class via class-conditional routing: feature prototypes are maintained by moving average, class uncertainty σk is computed from inter-class margins, and Nk = ceil(N·σk) allocates more discretization capacity to uncertain (hard-to-classify) classes while stabilizing well-learned ones. A contrastive discretization loss enforces within-class consistency and between-class diversity on the aggregated Δ, and a KL-divergence loss (L_Diff) aligns the ETF classifier's predictions with the base method's output to guide feature learning. The total loss combines the base method's loss with L_DR (ETF classification), α·L_Diff, β·L_Cont, and a load-balancing term L_z.

## Results

S6MOD is a plug-and-play module that consistently improves accuracy across all 7 baselines on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet. The most substantial gains appear on OnPro, where adding S6MOD yields +4.24% on CIFAR-100 (M=2k: 33.33→37.57) and +4.49% on Tiny-ImageNet (M=5k: 21.13→25.62). On OCM, improvements reach +3.28% on CIFAR-100 (M=2k: 35.69→38.97). Forgetting is also reduced in most settings, with OnPro on CIFAR-10 (M=500) dropping from 17.94% to 7.13%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S6MOD improves every baseline it is combined with, confirming its plug-and-play nature. The largest gains are on OnPro, where S6MOD adds +4.24% accuracy on CIFAR-100 (M=2k) and +4.49% on Tiny-ImageNet (M=5k), while also cutting forgetting from 12.42% to 9.81% on CIFAR-100. OCM benefits by +2.16% on CIFAR-10 (M=1k) and +3.28% on CIFAR-100 (M=2k). ER, the weakest baseline, sees consistent ~1–3% accuracy gains and reduced forgetting across all datasets. The CCLDC variants (OCM-CCLDC, OnPro-CCLDC) already incorporate distillation and show smaller but still positive improvements of ~1–2%. MOSE and MOE-MOSE, the strongest baselines, gain ~1–2% accuracy, with MOE-MOSE reaching 47.25% on CIFAR-100 (M=2k) and 33.53% on Tiny-ImageNet (M=5k) as the best overall results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the selective state space model (S6/Mamba) and its visual adaptation via SS2D (Vmamba), extending the recent Mamba-FSCIL line of work that first demonstrated SSMs' dynamic weights benefit class-incremental learning. It departs from prior MoE-Mamba approaches (BlackMamba, MoE-Mamba) by restricting each expert to a simple linear projection layer and introducing a class-conditional gating mechanism that dynamically controls how many discretization patterns are active per class. The plug-and-play design positions S6MOD as an orthogonal add-on to replay-based OCL methods (ER, OCM, OnPro, CCLDC, MOSE) rather than a replacement, targeting the underexplored dimension of model adaptability that these methods largely ignore in favor of memory retention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play formulation is practically valuable: any existing OCL pipeline can incorporate S6MOD without architectural redesign, and the class-conditional routing provides an interpretable mechanism for balancing stability and plasticity. However, the evaluation is confined to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet) with a single backbone (ResNet-18), leaving open whether the gains persist at ImageNet scale or with transformer backbones. The modest improvements in several settings (e.g., MOSE on Tiny-ImageNet shows slightly worse forgetting) and the hand-wavy explanation for those cases suggest the method's benefits may be dataset- and method-dependent. A key open question is whether the mixture-of-discretization mechanism generalizes beyond classification to other OCL formulations such as domain or task-incremental settings.
