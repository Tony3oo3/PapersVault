## Problem

Existing language-embedded 3DGS methods (LangSplat, LeGaussians) associate CLIP embeddings with 3D Gaussians through a 2D rendering distillation step, which introduces a measurable discrepancy between the optimized 3D features and the original 2D language-aligned embeddings. This rendering dependency also prevents direct 3D-space search for localization and segmentation tasks, forcing expensive multi-view rendering at inference and making holistic spatial coverage computationally intractable. Additionally, storing full 512-dimensional CLIP vectors per Gaussian is memory-prohibitive at scale, and prior compression schemes require per-scene codebook or network optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images of a scene along with a pre-trained RGB-only 3D Gaussian set (frozen during feature allocation) and a text query at inference. CLIP embeddings are extracted from SAM-segmented patches in the training images, and a pre-trained Product Quantization codebook (built once on LVIS's 1.2M instances) is applied to compress each Gaussian's feature into a sequence of 8-bit centroid indices. The output is a set of 3D Gaussians each carrying only a PQ index, which can be queried to yield selected Gaussian subsets (object selection), volumetric 3D localization regions, or per-Gaussian open-vocabulary segmentation labels.

## Method

Dr. Splat replaces gradient-based volume-rendering distillation with a training-free feature registration: for each pixel ray across all training views, it identifies the Top-k dominant Gaussians, computes per-Gaussian weights w_i = T_i · α̃_i directly from the 3DGS rendering equation, aggregates these weights across views and SAM masks, and assigns each Gaussian a weighted average of the corresponding CLIP patch embeddings (Eq. 6). To achieve memory efficiency without per-scene optimization, a Product Quantization codebook is pre-trained once on patch-wise CLIP embeddings from the LVIS dataset; each Gaussian then stores only the PQ centroid indices (achieving up to 6.25× compression) rather than a full 512-dim float vector. At inference, a text query is encoded by CLIP, the stored PQ indices are decoded back to approximate features, and a cosine similarity combined with LeRF-style relevance re-ranking identifies the relevant Gaussians for downstream 3D tasks. The entire pipeline requires no per-scene gradient optimization, reducing feature registration time from hours to approximately 10 minutes.

## Results

On the ScanNet open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation task (10 classes), Dr. Splat (Top-40) achieves 50.2 mIoU and 73.5 mAcc, outperforming OpenGaussian (49.7 mIoU, 71.4 mAcc) and substantially exceeding rasterization-based baselines LangSplat-m (8.0 mIoU) and LEGaussians-m (7.7 mIoU). For 3D object localization, Dr. Splat (Top-40) reaches 25.4 3DmIoU and 25.6% IoU>0.45, surpassing OpenGaussian (25.2 3DmIoU, 18.3% IoU>0.45). On the LeRF-OVS 3D object selection benchmark, Dr. Splat (Top-20) attains 43.26 mIoU and 64.32 mAcc@0.25, beating OpenGaussian (43.06 mIoU, 59.61 mAcc) by over 4 points in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the rasterization-based baselines LangSplat-m and LEGaussians-m, Dr. Splat achieves dramatic gains: on 10-class ScanNet segmentation, mIoU jumps from 8.0/7.7 to 50.2, and on 3D localization, 3DmIoU rises from 8.0/9.5 to 25.4, confirming that bypassing the rendering step preserves language-embedding fidelity. Against the closest competitor OpenGaussian (the only method also performing direct 3D search), Dr. Splat wins on 10-class segmentation (50.2 vs 49.7 mIoU; 73.5 vs 71.4 mAcc), 3D localization at the strictest threshold (25.6% vs 18.3% IoU>0.45), and LeRF object selection accuracy (64.32 vs 59.61 mAcc@0.25). The margin over OpenGaussian is modest on 19-class and 15-class segmentation mIoU (29.6 vs 30.1; 38.2 vs 38.1), where the two methods are essentially tied, but Dr. Splat pulls ahead as the number of classes decreases, suggesting better discriminability with fewer categories. A key practical advantage is that Dr. Splat requires no per-scene codebook or encoder training (~10 min feature registration vs ~1 h for OpenGaussian), while maintaining or exceeding accuracy across all three evaluated tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dr. Splat departs from the dominant rendering-based paradigm for language-embedded 3DGS (LangSplat, LEGaussians), which distills CLIP features into Gaussians through iterative volume rendering optimization. Instead, it performs a training-free, direct registration of patch-wise CLIP embeddings (extracted following LangSplat's SAM-based pipeline) onto Gaussians via weighted aggregation along pixel rays, framed as an inverse volume rendering without gradient descent. For compact storage, it replaces per-scene codebooks (as in OpenGaussian, the closest concurrent work) with a single Product Quantization codebook trained once on the LVIS dataset, and adopts LeRF's relevance scoring for inference-time re-ranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the rendering bottleneck, Dr. Splat enables direct 3D-space search over Gaussians, which is more efficient and avoids the NP-hard camera-coverage problem inherent to 2D-rendering-based retrieval. The general PQ codebook (6.25% compression) removes per-scene optimization, improving scalability to new scenes without retraining. However, evaluation is limited to 8 ScanNet scenes and 4 LeRF scenes, and gains over the concurrent OpenGaussian are modest (e.g., 25.4 vs. 25.2 3DmIoU). Open questions include whether the direct registration approach generalizes beyond CLIP to other foundation models, how it scales to very large outdoor scenes, and whether the volume-aware IoU metric becomes a standard for 3DGS evaluation.
