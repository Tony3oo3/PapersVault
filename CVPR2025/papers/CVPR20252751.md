## Problem

State-of-the-art BEV perception relies on 3D projection methods that are computationally expensive and lack explicit uncertainty modeling, while efficient unprojection-based methods like Lift-Splat-Shoot suffer from depth ambiguity due to the inherently ill-posed nature of monocular depth estimation. No prior unprojection-based approach explicitly models depth uncertainty to capture object extents, limiting robustness in real-world driving scenarios with occlusions and long-range objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252751/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes six surround-view RGB camera images (resized to 224×480 or 448×800) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a 200×200 BEV segmentation mask covering a 100m×100m area (0.5m per cell) around the ego-vehicle. Internally, it generates per-pixel depth distributions (B bins), 3D Gaussian parameters (mean and 3×3 covariance), and per-pixel opacity values, which are splatted at three resolutions (50×50, 100×100, 200×200) and fused to the target BEV resolution before the segmentation head.

## Method

GaussianLSS revisits the Lift-Splat-Shoot paradigm by replacing discrete depth-bin weighting with an explicit depth uncertainty model: it computes the mean μ and variance σ² of the predicted per-pixel depth distribution, then defines a soft depth range [μ−kσ, μ+kσ] (k=0.5) that implicitly encodes object extents. Each depth bin is unprojected to 3D via camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and the resulting 3D mean μ_3d and covariance Σ form a Gaussian whose ellipsoidal support captures spatial uncertainty. These 3D Gaussians, combined with CNN-extracted splat features and learned opacity, are rasterized onto the BEV plane using a modified Gaussian Splatting alpha-blending formulation. To mitigate depth-mean inconsistencies between adjacent pixels, a multi-scale rendering strategy projects Gaussians at 50×50, 100×100, and 200×200 resolutions, upsamples, and fuses them before the task head. The model is trained end-to-end with focal, L1, and L2 losses under an AdamW optimizer for 50 epochs, achieving 67.9 FPS and 0.36 GiB memory—roughly 2× faster and 3.5× less memory than PointBEV—while matching its IoU within 0.7%.

## Results

On nuScenes vehicle BEV segmentation (224×480, no visibility filtering), GaussianLSS achieves 38.0 IoU with EfficientNet-B4, outperforming all unprojection-based baselines (best: BAEFormer at 36.0) and reaching within 0.7% of the best projection-based method PointBEV (38.7). With visibility filtering at 448×800, GaussianLSS scores 46.1 IoU versus PointBEV's 47.6. In terms of efficiency, GaussianLSS runs at 67.9 FPS with 0.36 GiB memory, making it ~2× faster and using ~0.3× the memory of PointBEV (32.0 FPS, 1.26 GiB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianLSS outperforms all unprojection-based baselines across every setting in Table 1: it beats BAEFormer by +2.0 IoU (38.0 vs 36.0), FIERY static by +2.2, LaRa by +2.6, CVT by +6.6, and LSS by +13.0 (pedestrian, Table 2). Against the strongest projection-based method PointBEV, GaussianLSS trails by only 0.7 IoU (38.0 vs 38.7) while running 2.1× faster (67.9 vs 32.0 FPS) and using 0.3× the GPU memory (0.36 vs 1.26 GiB). Compared to BEVFormer and Simple-BEV (both RN-50), GaussianLSS gains +2.2 and +1.1 IoU respectively, though these use a different backbone. On pedestrian segmentation (Table 2), GaussianLSS scores 17.4 IoU, trailing PointBEV (18.5) by 1.1 but surpassing SimpleBEV (17.1), FIERY (17.2), and BEVFormer (16.4). The paper also notes that GaussianLSS captures farther objects (>30 m) more accurately than PointBEV due to its explicit depth uncertainty modeling, despite slightly worse shape fidelity at close range.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianLSS revisits the Lift-Splat-Shoot (LSS) unprojection paradigm and replaces its discrete softmax-based depth weighting with an explicit depth uncertainty model (mean and variance of the per-pixel depth distribution). The key conceptual departure is borrowing 3D Gaussian Splatting rasterization from the radiance-field rendering literature to project these uncertainty-aware 3D Gaussians onto the BEV plane, rather than using the grid-based lifting of LSS or the 3D-grid projection of methods like BEVFormer and PointBEV. This positions the work at the intersection of two previously separate communities: multi-view BEV perception and differentiable 3D rendering, with the multi-scale BEV rendering and learned opacity serving as practical bridges between the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in closing the efficiency gap between unprojection and projection methods: 2× faster inference and 0.3× memory at only 0.7% IoU cost makes real-time BEV perception on embedded hardware more feasible. The insight that depth variance naturally encodes object spatial extents—reducing reliance on precise depth estimation—offers a principled way to handle the inherently ill-posed depth problem in multi-view settings. However, evaluation is limited to nuScenes with a single backbone (EfficientNet-B4), and the paper explicitly defers temporal modeling and broader task generalization to future work, leaving open whether the uncertainty-aware splatting approach transfers to dynamic scenes or other sensor modalities.
