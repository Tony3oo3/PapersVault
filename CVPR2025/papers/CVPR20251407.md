## Problem

Open World Object Detection requires identifying both known and previously unseen objects without confusing them with backgrounds. Existing approaches rely on probability-based 'objectness' models that lack explainability and can misclassify backgrounds resembling unknown objects. The paper addresses the challenge of curating a compact, task-relevant subset of class-agnostic attributes from a large LLM-generated pool in an end-to-end, explainable manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes z-shot labeled images per known class (one bounding box and one class label per image), a large attribute text pool generated by GPT-3.5 from known class names, and test images at inference. Features are extracted via a frozen CLIP-pretrained OWL-ViT (B/16 or L/14) backbone. The output consists of bounding boxes with known-class labels, an unknown-object indicator computed as the product of task-relevance (max sigmoid similarity to selected attributes) and unknownness (1 − max class probability), and a compact set of N′ selected attributes with per-attribute in-distribution scores.

## Method

PASS formulates attribute selection as a Partial Optimal Transport problem between visual object embeddings and attribute embeddings, where the visual distribution serves as a baseline with an equality marginal constraint while the attribute distribution uses an inequality constraint, allowing only a targeted fraction of attribute mass to be transported. A curriculum schedule divides selection into η steps, progressively reducing the attribute pool by retaining only the top-ID-score attributes at each step, with the redundancy rate α = (N/N′)^(1/η) controlling the per-step reduction. Training is end-to-end, jointly minimizing the entropically regularized POT distance (solved via Sinkhorn iterations) and a cross-entropy classification loss through a learnable mapping matrix W that assigns N′ reweighted attributes to K known classes. At inference, the learned attributes serve a dual role: supporting known-class recognition via the W mapping and enabling unknown-object detection by measuring correlation between proposals and the selected attribute set.

## Results

On the five real-world OWOD datasets (Aquatic, Aerial, Game, Medical, Surgery) in the 100-shot regime, PASS with the L/14 backbone achieves an overall K-mAP of 38.5 and U-mAP of 19.1, surpassing FOMO (27.4 K-mAP, 15.2 U-mAP) by +11.1 and +3.9 points respectively. The gains are especially pronounced on previously-known classes (PK-mAP: 38.9 vs 26.1, +12.8) and currently-known classes (CK-mAP: 36.0 vs 23.5, +12.5). With the smaller B/16 backbone, PASS still leads FOMO by +4.4 U-mAP (9.6 vs 5.2) and +2.5 K-mAP (18.3 vs 15.7). On the Surgery dataset with L/14, the CK-mAP improvement reaches +34.8 points (43.3 vs 8.5), demonstrating strong continual-learning capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against FOMO [78], the most recent attribute-based OWOD method that also leverages foundation models. PASS outperforms FOMO across all five datasets and both backbones on every reported metric (U-mAP, K-mAP, PK-mAP, CK-mAP). With the L/14 backbone, the overall K-mAP gap is +11.1 points (38.5 vs 27.4) and the CK-mAP gap is +12.5 points (36.0 vs 23.5), indicating that PASS's curriculum attribute selection and POT-based optimization yield substantially more accurate and generalizable attributes. The advantage is most dramatic on the Surgery dataset (L/14), where CK-mAP jumps from 8.5 (FOMO) to 43.3 (PASS), a +34.8-point gain, suggesting FOMO's multi-stage attribute refinement fails to capture relevant features in that domain. On the B/16 backbone, PASS still leads FOMO by +4.4 U-mAP and +2.5 K-mAP overall, though the margin is smaller. PASS also consistently outperforms the few-shot baseline BASE-FS by a wide margin on all metrics, confirming that learned attribute curation is far more effective than simple image-exemplar averaging for open-world detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Partial Optimal Transport (POT) framework from optimal transport theory [6] and the foundation-model-based OWOD pipeline established by FOMO [78], which introduced attribute-based detection using LLM-generated attribute pools. The key departure is reframing attribute curation as a POT problem between visual object embeddings and the attribute pool, enabling end-to-end selection and optimization in a single training loop, in contrast to FOMO's multi-stage attribute selection and refinement pipeline that is computationally expensive and error-accumulating. The curriculum selection strategy further distinguishes the method by progressively narrowing the attribute subset during training, a design not present in prior attribute-based OWOD approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers an explainable, end-to-end alternative to probability-based objectness modeling for OWOD, which is practically important for deployment in dynamic real-world settings where interpretability matters. The large margins over FOMO (e.g., +12.8 PK-mAP on L/14 overall) suggest that the multi-stage attribute curation in prior work was a significant bottleneck. However, the evaluation is confined to five small real-world datasets (Roboflow 100) with a single main competitor, and the method depends on a frozen backbone and LLM-generated attribute pools, leaving open questions about scalability to larger benchmarks and robustness to attribute pool quality. The authors note future work on hierarchical attribute taxonomies as a natural extension.
