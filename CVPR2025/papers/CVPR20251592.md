## Problem

Existing low-light image enhancement methods operating in sRGB suffer from color bias and brightness artifacts due to the strong coupling between brightness and color channels. While HSV space decouples these, it introduces red discontinuity noise (from the modular hue axis) and black plane noise (from the origin singularity), producing severe artifacts in red-dominated or extremely dark regions. No prior color space simultaneously preserves the brightness-color decoupling of HSV while eliminating both types of noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light sRGB image and produces an enhanced sRGB image. Internally, the input is decomposed into a Max-RGB intensity map (computed analytically, not learned) and a 2-channel HV color map formed by polarizing the hue axis into cos/sin components scaled by saturation and a learnable intensity collapse factor. The network operates on these decoupled components at 400×400 or 256×256 patch resolutions depending on the dataset, and the final output is reconstructed via a perceptual-inverse HVI-to-sRGB mapping with adjustable saturation and brightness parameters.

## Method

The paper introduces the HVI color space, which polarizes the HSV hue axis into orthogonal h=cos(πh/3) and v=sin(πh/3) coordinates to eliminate red discontinuity, and applies a learnable intensity collapse function C_k(x)=sin(πI_max(x)/2+ε)^(1/k) to compress dark-region radii toward zero, removing black plane noise. CIDNet is a dual-branch UNet (1.88M params, 7.57 GFLOPs) where the HV-branch handles chromatic denoising and the I-branch handles brightness estimation, coupled via Lighten Cross-Attention modules that exploit the inverse relationship between illumination and noise. Training uses a combined loss in both HVI and sRGB spaces to enforce both color-space distributional fidelity and pixel-level structural detail. The HVI transformation is also demonstrated as a plug-and-play module that improves six other SOTA LLIE methods when applied to their sRGB inputs.

## Results

On the LOLv1 benchmark, CIDNet achieves 28.20 dB PSNR, 0.889 SSIM, and 0.079 LPIPS, outperforming the best prior method (GSAD, 27.61 dB) by 0.6 dB while using only 10.8% of its parameters (1.88M vs. 17.36M) and 1.7% of its FLOPs (7.57G vs. 442.02G). On LOLv2-Real and LOLv2-Synthetic, CIDNet leads all baselines by 2.63 dB and 0.04 dB in PSNR respectively, with the largest gap over SNR-Aware on LOLv2-Real (24.11 vs. 21.48 dB). On the extreme Sony-Total-Dark dataset, CIDNet surpasses the second-best method by 6.68 dB in PSNR (22.90 vs. 16.23 dB), and achieves the best NIQE score (3.523) among all methods on the five unpaired datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIDNet outperforms all 13 baselines in Table 1 across every metric on all three LOL splits. Against the strongest RGB/diffusion baseline GSAD, CIDNet gains +0.60 dB PSNR on LOLv1, +3.96 dB on LOLv2-Real, and +1.23 dB on LOLv2-Synthetic, while using only 10.8% of GSAD's parameters and 1.7% of its FLOPs. Compared to the Retinex-based SOTA RetinexFormer, CIDNet improves PSNR by +1.06 dB (LOLv1), +1.32 dB (LOLv2-Real), and +0.04 dB (LOLv2-Synthetic) with roughly half the FLOPs. On the extreme Sony-Total-Dark dataset, CIDNet leads the second-best LLFlow by a substantial 6.68 dB in PSNR (22.90 vs. 16.23), attributed to the learnable intensity collapse function C_k preserving signal-to-noise ratio in near-black regions. On the five unpaired datasets, CIDNet achieves the best NIQE (3.523) but trails RetinexNet slightly on BRISQUE (23.521 vs. 23.286), suggesting the HVI space excels at perceptual naturalness but not always at no-reference sharpness. The plug-in experiment (Table 3) further confirms HVI's generality: applying HVI transformation to six independent SOTA methods (FourLLIE, LEDNet, SNR-Aware, LLFormer, GSAD, DiffLight) consistently improves PSNR by 0.38–3.56 dB, with GSAD benefiting the most (+3.56 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the color-space design for LLIE by identifying two specific failure modes of the HSV representation (red discontinuity noise and black plane noise) and proposing targeted geometric fixes: polarization of the HS plane to close the hue-axis gap at red, and a learnable intensity collapse function C_k to compress dark regions. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the Kubelka-Munk-inspired HSV decoupling line (Zhou et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2021) and the Retinex-based brightness estimation tradition (RetinexNet, KinD, RetinexFormer), but departs from both by treating the color space itself as a trainable, task-specific construct rather than a fixed coordinate system. The CIDNet architecture is a dual-branch UNet with cross-attention, drawing on the encoder-decoder and attention patterns common in recent restoration work (Restormer, UNet-Transformer), but the cross-attention is specifically motivated by the inverse relationship between illumination and noise intensity in the HVI decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that HVI transformation acts as a plug-and-play module that improves PSNR by 0.4–3.6 dB across six architecturally diverse LLIE methods (CNN, Transformer, Diffusion), suggesting the color-space fix is orthogonal to network design and could be adopted broadly. The efficiency profile (1.88M params, 7.57 GFLOPs, 0.053 s inference) makes it competitive with diffusion-based SOTA at a fraction of the cost, which matters for real-time or edge deployment. Open questions include whether the learnable parameter k generalizes across vastly different lighting distributions without retraining, whether HVI extends naturally to video or multi-frame enhancement, and whether the polarization trick has analogous benefits in other color-space-sensitive tasks beyond LLIE.
