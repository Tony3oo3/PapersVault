## Problem

Combining multiple independently trained LoRA models into a single unified model for multi-concept image generation remains difficult: existing methods suffer from feature interference, require simultaneous training on all concepts, need access to original training data, or become unstable as the number of merged LoRAs grows. No prior approach can merge arbitrary pre-existing community LoRA models without retraining or specialized LoRA variants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251555/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of pre-trained single-concept LoRA (or ED-LoRA) models—each fine-tuned on a distinct subject or style—and a text prompt specifying which concepts to compose and in what scene. It produces a single merged delta weight matrix (ΔW) that, when added to the base Stable Diffusion (ChilloutMix) model, generates composite RGB images containing all requested concepts simultaneously. The merging of 12 LoRA models completes in approximately 5 minutes on a single A100 GPU, and the resulting unified model can be queried with any prompt combining any subset of the merged concepts without further training.

## Method

LoRACLR introduces a contrastive merging objective that aligns the weight spaces of independently trained LoRA models without retraining them. For each LoRA V_i, input-output feature pairs (X_i, Y_i) are extracted, and the merged model's predictions Ŷ_i = (W + ΔW)X_i are computed; a margin-based contrastive loss attracts positive pairs (same-concept outputs) while repelling negative pairs (cross-concept outputs) to preserve identity distinctiveness. The merging is performed via a learned additive delta ΔW initialized to zero, regularized with an L2 term (λ_delta = 0.001) to enforce sparsity and minimal perturbation of the base weights. The total objective L_total = L_contrastive + L_delta is optimized with gradient descent (lr = 1e-4, margin m = 0.5), converging to a weight configuration where each concept retains high-fidelity representation while remaining compatible in joint compositions. This post-training approach requires no access to original training data and is compatible with any standard LoRA format available in the community.

## Results

On the 12-concept identity merging benchmark, LoRACLR achieves the highest merged Image Alignment (0.776) and Identity Alignment (0.828), and is the only method that improves both metrics after merging (Δ +0.010 and +0.029 respectively). Text Alignment remains competitive at 0.665 merged (Δ −0.003). In a 50-participant user study, LoRACLR scores 3.42/5 on identity alignment versus 2.41 for Orthogonal Adaptation, 2.21 for Mix-of-Show, and 2.01 for P+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRACLR outperforms all five baselines on both Image Alignment and Identity Alignment after merging. The closest competitor, Orthogonal Adaptation [25], reaches 0.741 Image and 0.745 Identity, trailing LoRACLR by 0.035 and 0.083 respectively; moreover, Orthogonal Adaptation's Image Alignment actually degrades (−0.007) while LoRACLR's improves. Mix-of-Show [10] (both FedAvg and Grad Fusion variants) shows modest drops in both Image (−0.010 to −0.016) and Identity (−0.011 to −0.022) after merging. DB-LoRA with FedAvg [33] suffers a catastrophic Identity Alignment collapse from 0.683 to 0.098 (−0.585), rendering it unusable for multi-concept generation. Custom Diffusion [21] and P+ [40] start from much lower single-concept baselines (Identity 0.504 and 0.515) and either degrade or remain flat after merging, confirming that LoRACLR's contrastive merging is the only approach that simultaneously preserves and enhances concept fidelity at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoRACLR sits at the intersection of LoRA-based personalization (DreamBooth, DB-LoRA) and multi-concept composition (Mix-of-Show, Orthogonal Adaptation, Custom Diffusion, P+). It departs from prior merging strategies—weighted summation, FedAvg, gradient fusion, orthogonal constraints, and segmentation-based isolation—by introducing a contrastive objective that aligns positive pairs (same-concept outputs) and repels negative pairs (cross-concept outputs) in feature space, learned via a single additive delta ΔW. Crucially, unlike Mix-of-Show (which requires ED-LoRAs and original training data) or Orthogonal Adaptation (which mandates retraining each LoRA with orthogonal constraints), LoRACLR operates purely post-training on pre-existing standard LoRAs without accessing their training sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain is notable: merging 12 LoRAs in ~5 minutes versus 120+ minutes for Orthogonal Adaptation makes multi-concept composition accessible for community LoRA ecosystems (e.g., civitai). However, the method's performance is inherently bounded by the quality of the underlying single-concept LoRAs, and the paper acknowledges deepfake misuse risk without proposing mitigations. Open questions include whether the contrastive merging generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion architectures, how it scales beyond 12 concepts, and whether the modest identity-alignment gains (+0.029 over Orthogonal Adaptation) persist with more diverse or harder-to-distinguish concepts.
