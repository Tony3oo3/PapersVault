## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models are designed for offline video understanding, where entire videos are processed post-capture, and lack the architectural and training foundations needed for real-time streaming video scenarios. The unique challenges of online video—dynamic temporal perspectives (past/current/future), time-dependent answers that evolve as context changes, and the need to process infinitely long visual streams with bounded memory—remain unaddressed by both current benchmarks and model architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251843/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames incrementally as they arrive in a stream (sampled at 1 fps during training, 2 fps during evaluation), with text queries posed at arbitrary timestamps that reference past, current, or future temporal contexts. Outputs are free-form text answers that may include bounding box coordinates, temporal intervals, and action sequences. The Pyramid Memory Bank constrains the total visual token budget to 832 tokens across all memory layers, enabling real-time inference on a 4B-parameter model suitable for mobile deployment.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is the Pyramid Memory Bank (PMB), a multi-layer memory structure where each layer progressively trades spatial resolution for temporal coverage: layer 1 stores frames at full resolution with high sampling rate for spatial detail, while deeper layers use lower resolution and higher sampling rates for temporal abstraction. Frame eviction identifies the most similar adjacent frame pair via cosine similarity after average pooling, removes the older frame, and downscales it to the next layer's resolution, maintaining KVCache synchronization by erasing tokens after the evicted frame's timestamp. For training, the paper introduces an offline-to-online curriculum: the model is first trained on offline video data to establish robust understanding, then fine-tuned on 96K online samples formatted as interleaved multi-turn dialogues where queries are inserted at specific temporal positions along the video timeline. Built on InternVL2-4B (InternViT-300M encoder + Phi-3 LLM), the resulting VideoChat-Online achieves state-of-the-art performance on both the new OVBench and established offline benchmarks with only 4B parameters.

## Results

On OVBench (streaming setting), VideoChat-Online (4B) achieves 54.9% average accuracy, outperforming the best prior online model Flash-VStream (7B) by 23.7% (54.9 vs. 31.2) and the best offline MLLM Qwen2-VL (7B, sliding window) by 4.2% (54.9 vs. 49.7). On established offline video benchmarks, the 4B model achieves 54.7% on EgoSchema, 60.8% on MLVU, 54.4% on VideoMME (Overall), 65.2% on MVBench, and 54.1% on LongVideoBench, matching or exceeding larger 7B–9B models. The model uses only 832 visual tokens per inference step via the Pyramid Memory Bank, enabling real-time streaming at 2 fps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the streaming setting, VideoChat-Online (4B, 54.9%) decisively outperforms all prior online models: Flash-VStream (7B, 31.2%), MovieChat (7B, 30.9%), and VideoLLM-Online (7B, 9.6%), with margins of 23.7, 24.0, and 45.3 percentage points respectively. Against offline MLLMs evaluated via a 32-second sliding window, VideoChat-Online (53.9%) surpasses Qwen2-VL (7B, 49.7%) by 4.2% and LLaVA-OneVision (7B, 49.5%) by 4.4%, while trailing Gemini-1.5-Flash (50.7%) by a narrow 3.2% in the streaming setting but exceeding it in the sliding window. On offline benchmarks (Table 3), the 4B model matches or beats larger competitors: it leads on MLVU (60.8% vs. Video-CCAM 9B at 58.5%), MVBench (65.2% vs. Video-CCAM 9B at 64.6%), and LongVideoBench (54.1% vs. LLaVA-Next-Video 7B at 43.5%), while being competitive on EgoSchema (54.7% vs. VideoChat2 7B at 54.4%) and VideoMME Overall (54.4% vs. LongVA 7B at 52.6%). The consistent advantage over 7B and 9B baselines with only 4B parameters and 832 visual tokens underscores the efficiency of the Pyramid Memory Bank and offline-to-online training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on InternVL2-4B (InternViT + Phi-3) as its base architecture, adding a Pyramid Memory Bank for streaming context compression and an offline-to-online training paradigm. It departs from prior online video MLLMs (VideoLLM-Online, Flash-VStream, MovieChat) by arguing that both architecture and training strategy must be co-designed: existing streaming models either lack effective context compression or are trained exclusively on offline data, introducing biases that hurt real-time adaptability. The offline-to-online curriculum, with interleaved dialogue formatting, is positioned as a key differentiator from prior work that treats online and offline training as separate problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OVBench is the first comprehensive benchmark explicitly evaluating past/current/future temporal contexts in streaming video, filling a gap where prior evaluations (VStream-QA, MovieChat-1K) largely mirrored offline paradigms. The finding that a 4B model with only 96K online samples (6% of training data) outperforms 7B offline MLLMs on online tasks suggests that targeted online data is highly efficient, which is practically important for mobile deployment. Open questions include whether the pyramid memory scales to truly unbounded streams, how performance degrades under adversarial or rapidly changing scenes, and whether the benchmark's multiple-choice format adequately captures open-ended streaming interaction.
