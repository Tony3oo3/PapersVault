## Problem

Existing Mamba-based point cloud methods (Point-Mamba, PCM) naively extend 2D grid traversal to 3D grids, which is view-dependent, fails to respect surface adjacency, and breaks MAE pretraining because Mamba's direction-sensitive sequential processing cannot recover masked token positions without self-attention. This paper addresses all three limitations by grounding token ordering in the spectral structure of the point cloud's surface manifold.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250145/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered 3D point cloud, converts it to patches via Farthest Point Sampling and KNN, and builds a weighted patch-connectivity graph. For classification it outputs a single class label; for segmentation it outputs per-point part labels; and during self-supervised pretraining it reconstructs masked patches measured by Chamfer distance. The traversal order is determined entirely by the Laplacian spectrum of the patch graph, making it invariant to rigid transformations of the input.

## Method

The core contribution is a Spectral Spatial Traversing (SST) framework with three components. First, Surface-Aware Spectral Traversing (SAST) computes the s smallest non-constant eigenvectors of the Random Walk Laplacian of the patch-connectivity graph and uses each eigenvector to define a forward and reverse token ordering, concatenated per Mamba block, yielding an isometry-invariant traversal that follows the surface manifold. Second, Hierarchical Local Traversing (HLT) recursively binary-partitions tokens using all s eigenvectors simultaneously (building a binary tree), producing a lexicographic ordering that captures fine-grained local geometry for segmentation. Third, Traverse-Aware Repositioning (TAR) restores masked learnable tokens to their original spectral positions in the sequence during MAE pretraining, preserving the spatial adjacency that Mamba's sequential scan depends on. The full pipeline is pretrained on ShapeNet via MAE and fine-tuned on downstream classification, segmentation, and few-shot tasks.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN (training from scratch), the proposed method achieves 92.25% on OBJ-BG, 91.39% on OBJ-ONLY, and 87.30% on PB-T50-RS, outperforming PointMamba (90.87/90.18/85.60) and Point-MAE (86.75/86.92/80.78). When fine-tuned from pretrained weights, it reaches 94.32% on OBJ-BG and 93.4% on ModelNet40, surpassing PointMamba (93.29/92.8) and Point-MAE (92.77/93.2). On ShapeNetPart segmentation, HLT achieves 85.9% mIoU from scratch and 86.1% from pretrained, matching or exceeding PointMamba and Point-MAE. In few-shot classification on ModelNet40, the method attains 98.515% (5-way 20-shot) and 95.136% (10-way 20-shot), competitive with Point-M2AE (98.314/95.030).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms PointMamba [26] across all ScanObjectNN benchmarks in both scratch and pretrained settings (e.g., 94.32% vs 93.29% on OBJ-BG pretrained; 87.30% vs 85.60% on PB-T50-RS scratch), as well as on ModelNet40 (93.4% vs 92.8% pretrained) and ShapeNetPart segmentation (86.1% vs 86.0% mIoU pretrained). Against the Transformer-based Point-MAE [32], the method wins on all ScanObjectNN splits and ModelNet40, but ties on ShapeNetPart pretrained (86.1% mIoU) and slightly trails on 10-way 10-shot few-shot (92.05% vs 92.64%). Compared to the stronger Point-M2AE [55], the method achieves substantially higher ScanObjectNN OBJ-BG accuracy (94.32% vs 91.22%) but is outperformed on ModelNet40 pretrained (93.4% vs 94.0%) and ShapeNetPart pretrained (86.1% vs 86.5% mIoU). Against I2P-MAE [56], the method trails on ShapeNetPart pretrained (86.1% vs 86.8% mIoU). In few-shot settings, the method matches or exceeds Point-MAE and PointMamba but remains slightly below Point-M2AE on 5-way 10-shot (96.43% vs 96.82%). Overall, the spectral traversing strategies provide the strongest gains for Mamba-based point cloud models, particularly on real-world ScanObjectNN data where grid-based traversal is most detrimental.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba-based point cloud processing line initiated by Point-Mamba and PCM, which both rely on a straightforward 3D grid traversal inherited from 2D image processing. The key departure is replacing this grid-based ordering with a spectral graph traversal (SAST/HLT) derived from the Random Walk Laplacian of a patch-connectivity graph, yielding an isometry-invariant token order that better captures the surface manifold. The TAR strategy further adapts the MAE self-supervised pretraining paradigm (from Point-MAE, Point-M2AE) to Mamba's order-sensitive architecture by restoring masked tokens to their original spectral positions rather than appending them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The isometry-invariance property of spectral traversal is a genuine theoretical advantage over grid-based methods, making the approach robust to viewpoint changes without additional data augmentation. However, the empirical gains over Point-Mamba are modest (typically 1–2% accuracy), and the neighborhood size K remains a dataset-specific hyperparameter that limits generalizability. An open question is whether the spectral traversal overhead (Laplacian eigendecomposition via Arnoldi) scales favorably to very large point clouds or dynamic scenes where the graph must be recomputed per frame. The HLT vs. SAST distinction for segmentation is conceptually clean but yields only marginal mIoU differences, leaving room for more principled hierarchical strategies.
