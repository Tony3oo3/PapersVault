## Problem

Existing video stylization methods fail to properly decouple content and style: they either capture only global style while losing local textures (e.g., Van Gogh brushstrokes), or over-copy reference features causing severe content leakage. Additionally, most methods focus solely on stylized generation and lack a practical mechanism for video-to-video style transfer, while also suffering from temporal flickering and limited motion dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250220/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a reference style image (processed through CLIP into 256×1024 patch features and a 1×1024 image embedding), a text prompt, and optionally a source video for style transfer. For style transfer, the source video is converted to a grayscale tile image that feeds a dedicated ControlNet branch for content guidance. The output is a temporally coherent stylized video generated by a DiT-based denoising network with a 3D causal VAE, supporting both text-to-video stylized generation and video-to-video style transfer in a unified framework.

## Method

StyleMaster extracts style through a dual local-global mechanism: locally, it selects only k=15 CLIP patch features with the *lowest* similarity to the text prompt (filtering out content-bearing patches) and aggregates them via a Q-Former; globally, it applies a lightweight MLP projection to the CLIP image embedding, trained with triplet contrastive loss on paired images generated via model illusion (pixel rearrangement of a single image), ensuring absolute style consistency within pairs. Style is injected into the DiT backbone via dual cross-attention (text cross-attention + style cross-attention) in an IP-Adapter-style formulation. A LoRA-based motion adapter trained on still videos is applied to temporal attention blocks with a negative scale (α=−0.3) at inference, which simultaneously increases dynamic range and pushes outputs away from the real-world domain to enhance stylization. For video style transfer, a gray tile ControlNet (N/2 vanilla DiT blocks) provides content guidance without the color interference that RGB tiles introduce.

## Results

On stylized video generation (192 style-prompt pairs), StyleMaster achieves 0.305 CLIP-Text similarity, 2.329 UMT-Score, 0.463 CSD-Score, 2.370 Visual Quality, 2.496 Dynamic Quality, and 0.994 Motion Smoothness, outperforming both VideoComposer and StyleCrafter on five of six metrics. On image style transfer (96 content-style pairs), StyleMaster attains the best CSD-Score (0.45), ArtFID (36.89), and FID (22.11) among all compared methods, though it trades off slightly on content-preservation metrics (LPIPS 0.61, CFSD 2.37). The motion adapter ablation shows that setting the scale to −0.3 yields the best balance, improving CSD-Score from 0.443 to 0.463 while maintaining 0.994 motion smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VideoComposer [46], StyleMaster wins decisively on text-video alignment (CLIP-Text 0.305 vs 0.057; UMT-Score 2.329 vs −2.268), visual quality (2.370 vs 2.159), dynamic quality (2.496 vs 2.284), and motion smoothness (0.994 vs 0.975), but trails on CSD-Score (0.463 vs 0.680) because VideoComposer directly copies reference-image content, inflating its style score at the cost of text alignment. Against StyleCrafter [28], StyleMaster wins on all six metrics, with the largest gaps in UMT-Score (2.329 vs 1.994) and Dynamic Quality (2.496 vs 2.306), confirming that the combined global+local style extraction captures richer style cues than StyleCrafter's Q-Former-only approach. On image style transfer, StyleMaster outperforms StyleID [10] (CSD 0.45 vs 0.40; ArtFID 36.89 vs 38.57), InstantStyle [44] (CSD 0.45 vs 0.32; ArtFID 36.89 vs 42.48), and CSGO [51] (CSD 0.45 vs 0.35; ArtFID 36.89 vs 41.42) on the primary style-fidelity metrics, while conceding on LPIPS and CFSD content-preservation scores. The paper also qualitatively outperforms the InstantStyle+AnyV2V [22] pipeline in video style transfer (Fig. 1), where the combined baseline suffers from temporal inconsistency and content leakage.

## Relation to Prior Work

StyleMaster composes three distinct prior techniques into a unified video stylization pipeline: the IP-Adapter's dual cross-attention mechanism for style injection, StillMoving's LoRA-on-temporal-attention motion adapter for temporal quality, and Visual Anagrams' pixel-rearrangement (model illusion) for generating perfectly style-consistent training pairs. It departs from StyleTokenizer by replacing full CLIP fine-tuning with a lightweight MLP projection, from VideoComposer by filtering content-correlated patches via text-prompt similarity to prevent leakage, and from StyleCrafter by adding a gray tile ControlNet to support video style transfer rather than only stylized generation. The model illusion dataset construction is the most original conceptual contribution, repurposing an optical-illusion generation trick as a near-infinite, zero-annotation source of contrastive style pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model illusion dataset generation is a broadly useful tool for any style-related research that requires perfectly consistent style groups, removing the labor-intensive curation bottleneck that plagues datasets like Style30K. The negative-alpha motion adapter trick—where pushing the model away from the real-world training domain simultaneously increases dynamics and style extent—is a practical insight likely to be adopted in other stylization pipelines. However, the paper explicitly limits itself to graphic/visual style and acknowledges that dynamic style elements (particle effects, motion characteristics) remain unaddressed, leaving an open question about whether the same decoupling strategy can extend to temporal style descriptors. The approach is also computationally modest (8 A800 GPUs, ~2 days), making it accessible for reproduction.
