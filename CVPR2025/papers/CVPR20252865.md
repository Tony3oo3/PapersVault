## Problem

Standard rate-distortion (R-D) optimization in learned image compression sums rate and distortion gradients with a fixed Lagrangian weight, but the two gradients often have very different magnitudes and directions, causing one objective to dominate the update and leading to suboptimal compression performance. This paper identifies this gradient imbalance as a fundamental limitation and asks how to adaptively reweight the two objectives so that both rate and distortion improve equitably at every training step.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (256×256 patches cropped from COCO 2017 for training) and produces the same outputs as any LIC model: a compressed bitstream via entropy coding and a reconstructed image. The key distinction is that the method modifies only the training-time gradient computation, not the model architecture or inference pipeline, so inference cost remains unchanged. It is applied as a drop-in optimization strategy to existing LIC architectures (M&S Hyperprior, ELIC, TCM-S) rather than introducing a new codec.

## Method

The paper reformulates R-D optimization as a two-objective MOO problem, defining a per-objective loss improvement speed and seeking the update direction that maximizes the minimum speed across rate and distortion. Via a first-order Taylor approximation and Lagrangian duality, this saddle-point problem reduces to finding gradient weights w_R, w_D on the 2-simplex that minimize the norm of the weighted gradient combination. Solution 1 iteratively refines these weights along the training trajectory using a coarse-to-fine gradient descent with softmax reparameterization and a decay term, making it suitable for training from scratch. Solution 2 solves the resulting equality-constrained quadratic program analytically via KKT conditions, yielding a closed-form weight expression that is ideal for fine-tuning pre-trained models. Both solutions apply a normalization constant c_t to prevent numerical instability when individual losses become small during late training.

## Results

The proposed balanced R-D optimization achieves consistent BD-Rate reductions of approximately 1.5–2.4% across three LIC models (M&S Hyperprior, ELIC, TCM-S) and three test datasets (Kodak, Tecnick, CLIC 2022). Solution 1 (coarse-to-fine gradient descent) yields the strongest gains, with ELIC Sol.1 reaching −2.39% BD-Rate on CLIC 2022 and TCM-S Sol.1 reaching −2.12%. Solution 2 (analytical QP fine-tuning) provides smaller but still meaningful improvements over standard training. The additional training cost is moderate: +20% epoch time for Solution 1 and +47–50% for Solution 2, with no impact on inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its balanced R-D optimization against the standard Lagrangian R-D training of three state-of-the-art LIC models: M&S Hyperprior [50], ELIC [27], and TCM-S [43]. Solution 1 consistently outperforms the standard training across all three models and all three test datasets, with BD-Rate improvements ranging from −1.55% (M&S Hyperprior on Tecnick) to −2.39% (ELIC on CLIC 2022). ELIC benefits the most from the balanced approach, achieving −1.81% to −2.39% BD-Rate reduction, while M&S Hyperprior shows the smallest gains at −1.55% to −1.81%. Solution 2 (QP-based fine-tuning) also improves over standard training but with smaller margins, as it is applied for only 50 fine-tuning epochs. The paper notes that Solution 1 slightly outperforms Solution 2 overall, and that cascading both solutions (fine-tuning a Sol.1 model with Sol.2) yields only marginal additional gains, making the two solutions complementary rather than sequential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies multi-objective optimization (MOO) gradient-balancing techniques—specifically the MGDA quadratic programming formulation (Sener & Koltun 2018) and the coarse-to-fine trajectory refinement of FAMO (Liu et al. 2023)—to the specific problem of rate-distortion optimization in learned image compression. Standard LIC training uses a fixed Lagrangian multiplier λ to sum rate and distortion gradients, which the authors identify as causing imbalanced updates where one objective dominates. The key departure is treating rate and distortion as two concurrent objectives whose gradient contributions are adaptively reweighted via a softmax-reparameterized simplex, rather than being statically combined. The QP solution (Solution 2) is a direct application of the KKT conditions from the MOO literature, while Solution 1 adapts the iterative weight-update scheme from multi-task learning to the LIC training trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~2% BD-Rate improvement is meaningful in LIC, where state-of-the-art gains are typically in the 1–3% range, and the method is model-agnostic (validated on M&S Hyperprior, ELIC, and TCM-S). However, the 20–50% increase in training time is a non-trivial cost, and the approach has only been tested on three architectures and a single training dataset (COCO 2017). Open questions include whether Tchebycheff scalarization (noted by the authors) can reduce the computational overhead, whether the method extends to video compression or other R-D settings, and whether the imbalance problem persists at different scales or with alternative loss formulations (e.g., perceptual losses). The practical takeaway is that the standard Lagrangian R-D loss, used universally in LIC, may be leaving performance on the table due to gradient imbalance.
