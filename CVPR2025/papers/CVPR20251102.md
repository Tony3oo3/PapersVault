## Problem

Directly applying 3D Gaussian Splatting to images captured under adverse illumination (low light or overexposure) fails because SfM yields sparse, unreliable point clouds, the images contain severe noise and color distortion without ground-truth references for supervision, and naively combining per-view exposure correction with 3DGS introduces multi-view illumination inconsistency. No prior 3DGS-based method addresses this setting in an unsupervised, end-to-end manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251102/cropped_page0_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 25–48 sRGB images per scene captured under adverse lighting (LOM dataset, DJI Osmo Action 3), a COLMAP-estimated SfM point cloud for Gaussian initialization, and a Marigold monocular depth map as geometric guidance. It produces novel-view renderings at normal exposure, a 3D Gaussian scene representation with four extra per-primitive attributes (structure, illumination, depth, noise), and intermediate illumination-invariant structure and depth maps. Training is entirely reference-free: no normal-light ground-truth images are required, only the original adverse-illumination inputs.

## Method

LITA-GS first extracts an illumination-invariant structure prior P from each input image using Kubelka-Munk reflectance theory and invariant edge detectors (spatial, spectral-slope, and spectral-curvature derivatives combined via a weighted gradient magnitude). Each 3D Gaussian is then augmented with four learnable scalar/vector attributes—structure p_i, depth d_i, illumination l_i, and noise—and all are rendered per-view via standard front-to-back volume splatting alongside the color image. A three-stage Progressive Denoising Module (three 3×3 conv + ReLU) iteratively refines a noise estimate using a bootstrapping high-frequency residual, subtracting it from the rendered image to suppress illumination-correlated noise. The entire system is optimized unsupervised with a composite loss: exposure control (L1 against an intensity-modulated input), structural consistency (L1 on structure + global/local PCC on depth), denoising (L2 + total-variation), and reconstruction (L1 + SSIM between the product of the denoised image and rendered illumination map and the original input), converging in ~15 minutes on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

On the LOM low-light benchmark (5 scenes), LITA-GS achieves a mean PSNR of 21.14 dB, SSIM of 0.829, and LPIPS of 0.233, surpassing the strongest end-to-end baseline Aleth-NeRF (19.87 dB, 0.754, 0.417) by 1.27 dB in PSNR and 0.075 in SSIM. On the over-exposure setting, LITA-GS reaches 20.17 dB PSNR and 0.841 SSIM, outperforming Aleth-NeRF (18.09 dB, 0.745) by 2.08 dB. All pre-/post-processing combinations of image enhancement with NeRF or 3DGS (e.g., SCI+3DGS at 14.41 dB) fall well short of the end-to-end methods. Training completes in ~15 minutes on a single RTX 3090, enabling real-time rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LITA-GS decisively outperforms the closest end-to-end competitor Aleth-NeRF [4] on both low-light (+1.27 dB PSNR, +0.075 SSIM, −0.184 LPIPS) and over-exposure (+2.08 dB PSNR, +0.096 SSIM, −0.216 LPIPS) settings. Against all pre-/post-processing pipelines that combine image enhancement (Zero-DCE, SCI, IAT, MSEC) with vanilla NeRF or 3DGS, LITA-GS leads by 6–14 dB in PSNR, confirming that per-view enhancement introduces multi-view illumination inconsistency that degrades 3D reconstruction. Vanilla NeRF and 3DGS trained directly on adverse-illumination images produce near-useless results (PSNR ≈ 7 dB), highlighting the necessity of the illumination-invariant prior and reference-free optimization. Among the enhancement+3DGS combinations, SCI+3DGS (14.41 dB) and 3DGS+Zero-DCE (14.03 dB) are the strongest, yet still trail LITA-GS by over 6 dB. The paper also notes that LITA-GS trains in ~15 minutes and renders in real time, whereas Aleth-NeRF requires significantly longer training due to its NeRF ray-tracing paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

LITA-GS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) to the adverse-illumination novel view synthesis setting previously addressed only by NeRF-based methods (LLNeRF, Aleth-NeRF, RawNeRF). Its key conceptual departure is from the common pipeline of applying image enhancement as pre- or post-processing to 3DGS: rather than enhancing each view independently (which causes multi-view illumination inconsistency), the paper embeds an illumination-invariant physical prior—derived from Kubelka-Munk theory and Geusebroek's invariant edge detectors—directly as a learnable attribute on each Gaussian primitive. This is complemented by a reference-free (unsupervised) training strategy, a notable departure from the supervised paradigm dominant in both NeRF and 3DGS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3DGS-based method to handle adverse illumination in a reference-free manner, offering real-time rendering speed that NeRF-based alternatives (Aleth-NeRF, LLNeRF) cannot match, which is critical for AR/VR and robotic applications in dark or over-exposed environments. However, evaluation is limited to the small LOM dataset (5 scenes, 25–48 images each), and the method still depends on COLMAP for SfM initialization—a known weak point in dark scenes—along with an external depth estimator (Marigold). Open questions include scalability to larger, more complex scenes, robustness when SfM fails entirely, and whether the physical-prior approach generalizes to other degradation types (haze, motion blur) beyond exposure extremes.
