## Problem

Existing adversarially robust concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models rely on adversarial training as their sole defense, which fails to identify 'blind spots' in the text embedding space and leaves models vulnerable to inversion-based attacks (e.g., CCE) that regenerate erased concepts. These methods also sacrifice generation quality for benign concepts, creating an unresolved trade-off between robustness and utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251854/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 1.4 model, a target concept to erase (nudity, art style, or object), a GPT-4-generated set of ~200 diverse anchor prompts containing the target word, and a pre-generated gallery of images depicting the target concept (used only during the STE stage for textual inversion). It outputs a modified UNet (cross-attention layers for style erasure, non-cross-attention layers for object/nudity erasure) that suppresses the target concept under both natural and adversarial prompts while preserving fidelity on benign concepts. The gallery sets used for training are disjoint from evaluation sets to prevent overlap.

## Method

STEREO decouples vulnerability identification from concept erasure into two stages. In Stage 1 (Search Thoroughly Enough), it performs K iterations of min-max optimization: the minimization step erases the target concept using APG negative guidance with a frozen copy of the current UNet as ground truth, while the maximization step discovers a new adversarial prompt via textual inversion (optimizing a new token embedding against a gallery of target-concept images). This yields a diverse set of strong adversarial prompts. In Stage 2 (Robustly Erase Once), the original (unmodified) model is fine-tuned in a single pass using a compositional objective that applies positive guidance from randomly sampled anchor prompts and negative guidance averaged over all adversarial prompts from Stage 1, steering the model toward benign backgrounds while away from every identified adversarial direction simultaneously. This compositional structure is the key mechanism that preserves utility (by anchoring to benign concepts) while achieving robustness (by covering the full adversarial prompt set), avoiding the utility degradation of iterative erasure and the blind-spot vulnerability of adversarial-training-only approaches.

## Results

On nudity erasure (Table 2), STEREO achieves an attack success rate (ASR) of 4.21% under the strongest CCE inversion attack, compared to 46.31% (RECE), 66.31% (AdvUnlearn), and 83.15% (RACE), while maintaining erasure effectiveness of 1.05% (matching AC and AdvUnlearn). On Van Gogh art-style erasure, STEREO reduces CCE ASR to 17.00% versus 51.80% for the best baseline (AdvUnlearn), and on tench object erasure, STEREO achieves 9.78% CCE ASR versus 91.00% for AdvUnlearn. Utility is largely preserved: STEREO's average FID of 16.1 and CLIP score of 30.5 deviate from the base Stable Diffusion model by only 1.99 (FID) and 0.81 (CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STEREO dominates all seven baselines on the CCE inversion attack, the most challenging setting: it achieves 4.21% ASR versus 46.31% (RECE), 66.31% (AdvUnlearn, AC, MACE, UCE), 83.15% (RACE), and 86.31% (ESD). On the text-prompt attacks UD and RAB, STEREO is competitive with the only other method that resists them (AdvUnlearn: UD 3.40%, RAB 0.00%), posting 4.21% and 2.10% respectively, while all other baselines exceed 5% on at least one of these attacks. The main trade-off is a modest FID increase (15.70 vs. 14.13 for base SD), though this is comparable to AdvUnlearn (15.84) and far better than RACE (20.28) or the STE-only ablation (50.32). On art-style and object erasure, the gap widens dramatically: STEREO's CCE ASR of 17.00% (Van Gogh) and 9.78% (tench) versus 51.80%/91.00% for the next-best method (AdvUnlearn) represent relative improvements of ~67% and ~89%. The paper reports an average 88.89% relative robustness improvement across all tasks and baselines, attributing this to the compositional anchor-concept objective in the REO stage that precisely separates erased from benign concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

STEREO builds on the line of adversarially robust concept erasure methods (RECE, RACE, AdvUnlearn) that use iterative adversarial training as the sole defense mechanism, but fundamentally reframes adversarial training's role: rather than being the erasure process itself, it becomes a vulnerability-identification stage that produces a set of strong adversarial prompts. The second stage then departs from prior work by introducing an anchor-concept-based compositional objective (building on composable diffusion guidance from Liu et al. and APG) that erases the concept from the original model in a single fine-tuning pass, rather than accumulating erasures iteratively. This contrasts with RECE's closed-form embedding alignment, RACE's single-step textual inversion, and AdvUnlearn's bilevel optimization with external data, all of which integrate adversarial prompts directly into the erasure loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the robustness-utility trade-off in concept erasure is not an inherent constraint but an artifact of conflating vulnerability discovery with erasure; separating these into two stages allows near-complete robustness (4.21% ASR under CCE) while keeping FID within ~2 of the base model. However, the evaluation is limited to a single architecture (SD 1.4) and a small number of concepts, and the iterative min-max stage introduces non-trivial computational overhead. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-concept erasure, newer diffusion architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), and whether the anchor-concept mechanism can be automated without GPT-4 prompt generation.
