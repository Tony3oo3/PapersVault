## Problem

Existing anomaly detection datasets (MVTec AD, VisA, Real-IAD) target large industrial objects captured from a single viewpoint, leaving tiny objects (4–20 mm) — where full-surface inspection inherently requires multiple angles and anomalies are harder to discern — largely unaddressed. No prior dataset combines multi-view imaging of tiny objects with structured text annotations that encode domain expert knowledge and support visual-language reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250656/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Visual inputs are five-view composite images (vertically merged from cameras at 1170/1250 DPI, resolutions 162×705 to 1117×3365 px) or individual single-view crops; text inputs comprise 875 structured anomaly descriptions (DeclK) with three-level what/why/how annotations and 2K multiple-choice questions (ConsL) each paired with a normal-normal or normal-anomaly image pair and five options. Outputs span image-level and pixel-level anomaly scores (I-/P-AUROC), binary anomaly masks, and MCQ answers scored by both per-option and whole-question accuracy.

## Method

The primary contribution is the MANTA dataset: 137,338 multi-view images across 38 categories in five domains with 8,617 pixel-annotated anomalies, plus two text subsets (DeclK and ConsL). For the visual-language baseline, the authors fine-tune BLIP-2 using LoRA adapters on both the frozen ViT image encoder and the frozen OPT language model, with a Querying Transformer bridging the modality gap; two vertically concatenated images are encoded and combined with tokenized MCQ options, trained with cross-entropy classification loss plus autoregressive generation loss. The paper further benchmarks 15+ existing anomaly detection and text-prompt methods (RD, PatchCore, CDO, DMAD, SimpleNet, UniAD, CRAD, HGAD, WinCLIP, PromptAD, VCP-CLIP, LLaVA, InternVL2, Molmo) under five evaluation settings (single-view, multi-view, multi-class, text-prompt, visual-language), conducting over 400 evaluations in total.

## Results

On the single-view setting (Table 2), PatchCore achieves the best average I-AUROC of 93.7% (view-eval) and 95.2% (object-eval), while CDO follows at 91.2%/91.3%. All methods remain below 97% I-AUROC, with agriculture and groceries domains averaging below 90%, highlighting the difficulty of tiny-object anomaly detection. In the multi-view setting (Table 3), PatchCore again leads with 95.0%/95.7% I-/P-AUROC, and in the multi-class setting (Table 4), CRAD achieves the best average of 88.6%/91.6%. For the visual-language MCQ task (Table 5), the authors' BLIP-2+LoRA baseline reaches 52.7% option accuracy and 3.0% question accuracy in one-shot, marginally outperforming Molmo (51.5%/0.8%) and InternVL2 (51.6%/0.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among single-view one-for-one methods, PatchCore [50] dominates with 93.7% I-AUROC, outperforming CDO [7] (91.2%), SimpleNet [41] (87.1%), DMAD [39] (82.6%), and RD [12] (82.0%); reconstruction-based methods (RD, DMAD) consistently trail memory-based (PatchCore) and discrepancy-based (CDO) approaches by 5–12 points. In the multi-view setting, PatchCore again leads (95.0% I-AUROC) over CDO (91.5%), RD (90.6%), and DMAD (90.2%), confirming that multi-view inputs boost all methods by ~3–5 points over single-view. For multi-class training, CRAD [29] achieves the best average (88.6% I-AUROC), beating HGAD [61] (85.5%) and UniAD [62] (82.4%), with agriculture and groceries remaining the weakest domains across all multi-class models. In the text-prompt one-shot setting, WinCLIP [27] surpasses 75% I-AUROC and 90% P-AUROC, outperforming PromptAD [33] and VCP-CLIP [46], demonstrating that DeclK instructions provide effective supervision. For the visual-language MCQ task, the authors' BLIP-2+LoRA baseline [32] achieves 52.7% option accuracy, narrowly beating Molmo [11] (51.5%), InternVL2 [9] (51.6%), Llava1.5 [35] (47.8%), and LlavaNext [31] (47.4%), though question accuracy remains very low (≤4%) across all models, indicating the task's difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

MANTA extends the anomaly detection dataset lineage (MVTec AD, VisA, LOCO-AD, Real-IAD) by shifting focus from large industrial objects to tiny objects (4³–20³ mm³) captured from five synchronized viewpoints, addressing the coverage limitations of single-view setups. It further departs from purely visual AD benchmarks by introducing two structured text subsets—Declarative Knowledge (expert anomaly descriptions at what/why/how levels) and Constructivist Learning (image-pair multiple-choice questions)—grounded in cognitive load and dual coding theories. The visual-language baseline builds on BLIP-2 with LoRA fine-tuning, connecting the AD literature to the broader multimodal LLM evaluation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MANTA is the first large-scale multi-view dataset purpose-built for tiny objects across five domains, filling a gap where prior AD benchmarks either ignore size constraints or lack full-surface coverage. The visual-language component opens a new evaluation axis for how well foundation models can reason about anomalies through comparative visual-text tasks, though current results (~50% option accuracy, ~3% question accuracy) reveal a substantial domain gap. Key limitations include incomplete surface coverage for non-rectangular geometries (e.g., hexagonal sides) and a relatively small text subset, leaving open whether scaling text annotations or adding more viewpoints would meaningfully close the performance gap.
