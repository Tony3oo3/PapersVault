## Problem

Pixel-level annotation for satellite image time series (SITS) crop mapping is prohibitively expensive due to low resolution, unclear parcel boundaries, and varying phenological cycles. Existing weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS) methods designed for natural images fail to transfer to SITS because of two inherent challenges: spatial noise perturbation from the uniform appearance of neighboring crop parcels, and erroneous semantic bias from anomalous temporal periods where different crops exhibit similar spectral signatures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250909/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-spectral SITS tensors (T×C×H×W, e.g., 10 bands × 128×128 spatial × 33–61 temporal observations from Sentinel-2) together with only image-level crop category labels. It first produces clue-based CAMs (CB-CAMs) as high-quality pseudo labels by measuring per-pixel similarity against class-wise positive and negative prototypes in the temporal dense embedding space, then feeds these pseudo labels into a TSViT segmentation decoder to yield pixel-level crop type masks at the original spatial resolution.

## Method

Exact builds on the TSViT temporal-spatio ViT backbone and introduces two complementary mechanisms in the temporal embedding space. For spatial noise, it maintains class-wise positive and negative prototype sets updated via Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport over filtered CAM regions, then applies a contrastive loss (L_cbl) that pulls pixel embeddings toward their nearest positive prototype and away from all other prototypes, sharpening the decision boundary. For temporal bias, it extracts temporal-to-class attention from the transformer's self-attention matrix to reweight temporal sequence embeddings, computes pairwise affinities from these modulated representations, and propagates them to denoise the raw CAM (L_tap). After training, the final pseudo labels are generated by taking the ReLU of (max similarity to positive prototypes − max similarity to negative prototypes) per pixel, which suppresses both spatial over-activation and temporal false positives more effectively than the raw classifier-weight CAM.

## Results

On the PASTIS test set, Exact achieves 80.3% OA and 61.8% mIoU using only image-level labels, reaching 95% of the fully supervised TSViT oracle (83.4% OA, 65.1% mIoU). On the Germany dataset, Exact-generated pseudo labels attain 88.3% OA and 80.6% mIoU, surpassing the best adapted natural-image WSSS module (PAMR: 83.9% OA, 76.3% mIoU) by 4.4% OA and 4.3% mIoU. The best RGB-domain WSSS method (ViT-PCME) reaches only 53.2% mIoU on PASTIS, highlighting the gap between natural-image and SITS weak supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Exact outperforms all four adapted SITS WSSS modules (PAMR, TS-CAM, SIE, FPR) by 1.1–3.5% mIoU on PASTIS segmentation, with the largest gap over TS-CAM (56.5% → 61.8%, +5.3% mIoU). All six RGB-domain WSSS methods (MCTFormer, ViT-PCME, LP-CAM, TSCDA, DuPL, SeCo) achieve at most 53.2% mIoU on PASTIS pseudo-label quality, far below Exact's 75.6%, confirming that natural-image WSSS techniques do not transfer to SITS. Among fully supervised SITS segmentation networks, Exact's weakly supervised result (61.8% mIoU) exceeds ConvLSTM (50.1%), BiCGRU (56.2%), FPN-ConvLSTM (59.5%), Unet-3D (60.4%), and Unet-3Df (60.2%), approaching U-TAE (62.4%) and TSViT (65.1%). The 95% ratio to fully supervised performance is notably higher than the ~90% ceiling typically reported for image-level WSSS in natural images, suggesting SITS data characteristics are more amenable to weak supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS) paradigm from natural images to Satellite Image Time Series (SITS) crop mapping, a domain where pixel-level annotation is prohibitively expensive. It builds on the TSViT temporal-spatio transformer architecture as its backbone and repurposes the CAM mechanism as the pseudo-label generator, but departs from natural-image WSSS by identifying two SITS-specific failure modes—spatial over-activation from uniform intra-class appearance and temporal semantic bias from phenologically confusable periods. The spatial perceptive clues module adapts prototype-contrastive learning (Caron et al.) with optimal-transport-based clustering, while the temporal-aware affinity propagation borrows the denoising mechanism from Li et al. (NeurIPS 2023) but replaces low-level cues with high-level temporal-to-class attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 95% of fully supervised mIoU with only image-level labels is notably higher than the ~90% ceiling typically reported for natural-image WSSS, suggesting that the structured, repetitive nature of crop phenology actually makes weak supervision more effective here than in general vision. This opens a practical path toward scalable crop mapping without the costly pixel-level annotation pipeline. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (PASTIS, Germany) and a single backbone (TSViT), and the authors acknowledge persistent edge-segmentation weaknesses. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other SITS architectures, finer-grained crop taxonomies, or regions with more heterogeneous parcel sizes.
