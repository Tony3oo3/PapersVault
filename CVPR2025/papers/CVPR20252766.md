## Problem

Compressed video exhibits complex spatiotemporal degradation patterns from the interplay of motion estimation and quantization, requiring both global context modeling and precise motion compensation. CNNs are limited by small receptive fields, Transformers incur prohibitive attention-map costs, and while MLPs theoretically possess global perception, their structural characteristics hinder effective long-range dependency modeling—making efficient compressed video restoration an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252766/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes a 7-frame window (target frame plus T=3 surrounding frames) of HEVC-compressed video at QP 27–42, cropped to 128×128 patches during training. It produces a single enhanced target frame via residual addition (F′ = F_t + R), evaluated on the MFQEv2.0 benchmark across five resolution classes from 480×240 to 2560×1600 under the HEVC LowDelay-P configuration.

## Method

RivuletMLP combines three modules: (1) a Dynamically Guided Deformable Alignment (DDA) module that progressively refines spatiotemporal offsets in a U-Net-like fashion to align multi-frame features without error accumulation; (2) a Spatiotemporal Feature Flow (SFF) module that partitions features into p×p patches, then applies a circular permutation mapping m(i)=(p+n)/2+(i−1) to relocate features from beyond the fixed receptive field into local regions, enabling patch-based fully-connected layers to capture long-range dependencies at low cost; and (3) a Benign Selection Compensation (BSC) module that cascades depth-wise and point-wise convolutions with a Local Region MLP (LR-MLP) over overlapping p×p windows to restore inter-frame motion continuity. A Spacetime-Gate MLP (SG-MLP) fuses channel-MLP with depth-wise convolution gating for intra-module aggregation. The full model uses 128 channels, patch size 4, and 12 blocks, trained with Charbonnier loss and cosine-annealed Adam, achieving 1.13 dB ΔPSNR at QP37 with only ~36.6% of STCF's FLOPs.

## Results

On the MFQEv2.0 benchmark (LDP configuration, QP=37), RivuletMLP achieves the best average ΔPSNR of 1.13 dB and ΔSSIM of 1.93×10⁻² across 18 JCT-VC test sequences, outperforming the strongest baseline STCF (1.02 dB / 1.81×10⁻²) by 0.11 dB. At QP=42, it reaches 1.01 dB ΔPSNR, again leading all methods. In terms of efficiency, RivuletMLP requires only 615.7 GFLOPs (≈36.6% of STCF's 1677.1 GFLOPs) and 6.9M parameters, while running at 10.0 FPS on an RTX 4090 versus STCF's 2.8 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RivuletMLP outperforms all six baselines at QP=37 and QP=42, the two most heavily compressed settings. Against the strongest Transformer-based competitor STCF, it gains 0.11 dB ΔPSNR at QP=37 and 0.13 dB at QP=42, while using only 36.6% of STCF's FLOPs (615.7 G vs. 1677.1 G) and running 3.6× faster (10.0 vs. 2.8 FPS). Compared to the CNN-based RFDA, the improvement is 0.22 dB at QP=37 with comparable FLOPs (615.7 G vs. 524.9 G). Against CPGA (CVPR'24), RivuletMLP leads by 0.21 dB at QP=37 while requiring only 39% of CPGA's FLOPs. Notably, at lighter compression levels (QP=27 and QP=32), STCF edges out RivuletMLP by 0.10 dB and 0.02 dB respectively, suggesting the MLP-based global modeling is most advantageous under severe compression where artifacts exhibit stronger long-range spatiotemporal correlations.

## Relation to Prior Work

RivuletMLP positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: MLP-based vision architectures (MLP-Mixer, Vision Permutator) and compressed video quality enhancement (MFQEv2.0, STDF, STCF). It repurposes the ordered feature reorganization mechanism from Vision Permutator as the core of its Spatiotemporal Feature Flow module, adapting a 2D image-level permutation strategy to establish long-range spatiotemporal dependencies without self-attention. The DDA module extends the SKFF adaptive fusion framework with a U-Net-like progressive offset refinement, departing from STDF's frame-by-frame alignment to avoid error accumulation over temporal propagation. Conceptually, the paper argues that patch-based FC layers with strategic feature relocation can match or exceed the global modeling of attention mechanisms at a fraction of the cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a carefully designed MLP architecture with feature permutation can achieve competitive quality enhancement (1.13 dB ΔPSNR) at roughly 37% of the FLOPs of the prior best Transformer-based method (STCF), offering a practical path toward real-time video enhancement deployment. However, the evaluation is confined to the MFQEv2.0 benchmark under HEVC LDP configuration, leaving open questions about generalization to other coding standards (AV1, VVC), higher resolutions, and real-time streaming scenarios. The modest 0.11 dB gain over STCF, while accompanied by a large efficiency win, suggests the quality ceiling of MLP-based approaches for this task may still lag behind attention-based methods at higher compute budgets.
