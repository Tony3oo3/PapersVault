## Problem

Existing unsupervised camouflaged object detection (UCOD) methods rely on fixed-strategy pseudo-labels that contain substantial noise, causing models to overfit incorrect knowledge, and use a simple 1×1 convolutional decoder that cannot capture the semantic features of camouflaged objects—especially small ones—due to low-resolution labels and severe foreground-background confusion. This results in segmentation performance far below fully-supervised methods, limiting real-world applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251378/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (no pixel-level annotations during training) and produces a binary foreground segmentation mask for camouflaged objects. During training, the Look-Twice mechanism additionally generates cropped patches of small connected components (area ratio < 0.15 of the image), resizes them to the original input resolution, and treats them as augmented samples. At test time, these patches are re-inferred and pasted back into the coarse mask to produce the final refined segmentation.

## Method

UCOD-DPL employs a teacher-student framework (teacher updated via EMA with momentum 0.99) built on frozen DINOv1/DINOv2 backbones, with three key components. The Adaptive Pseudo-label Mixing (APM) module uses a discriminator trained in a GAN-like alternating fashion to score the agreement between fixed-strategy pseudo-labels and the student's predictions, then dynamically weights the mix of fixed-strategy and teacher pseudo-labels via a temporal scoring function that shifts reliance from the noisy fixed strategy early in training toward the more stable teacher later. The Dual-Branch Adversarial (DBA) decoder splits backbone features into parallel foreground and background branches, each using learnable embeddings for multiplicative attention, and is regularized by an orthogonal loss that forces the two branches to attend to distinct feature subspaces. The Look-Twice mechanism identifies small connected components in the coarse prediction, expands their bounding boxes to retain background context, crops and re-infers them at full resolution, and pastes the refined masks back—mimicking the human strategy of zooming in on small camouflaged targets.

## Results

On COD10K-Test (2,026 images), UCOD-DPL with DINOv2 achieves S_m=0.834, F_β^ω=0.763, F_β^m=0.779, E_φ^m=0.916, and M=0.031, outperforming all prior unsupervised methods by a wide margin (e.g., +6.7 S_m over FOUND-DINOv2's 0.767 and +17.9 S_m over UCOS-DA-DINOv2's 0.655). On CHAMELEON, it reaches S_m=0.864, surpassing the best unsupervised baseline (FOUND-DINOv2, 0.829) and all semi-supervised methods. On NC4K, it achieves S_m=0.850 and F_β^ω=0.818, again leading all unsupervised and semi-supervised approaches. While it does not match the strongest fully-supervised methods (BiRefNet: S_m=0.913 on COD10K; HitNet: S_m=0.871), it is competitive with several fully-supervised baselines such as ZoomNet (S_m=0.838 on COD10K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCOD-DPL (DINOv2) decisively outperforms all unsupervised baselines: on COD10K-Test it gains +6.7 S_m over FOUND-DINOv2 (0.834 vs 0.767) and +17.9 S_m over UCOS-DA-DINOv2 (0.834 vs 0.655), with similar margins on CHAMELEON, CAMO, and NC4K. It also surpasses all semi-supervised methods, beating CamoTeacher at 10% labels by +7.5 S_m on COD10K (0.834 vs 0.759) and SCOD-ND at 10% by +1.5 S_m (0.834 vs 0.819). Against fully-supervised methods, UCOD-DPL trails the strongest models—BiRefNet (S_m 0.913) and HitNet (S_m 0.871) on COD10K—but is competitive with mid-tier supervised methods: it slightly underperforms ZoomNet (0.834 vs 0.838) and BGNet (0.834 vs 0.831) on COD10K S_m while matching or exceeding them on the M metric (0.031 vs 0.029/0.033). On CHAMELEON, the gap to top supervised methods is larger (0.864 vs 0.929 for BiRefNet), but it still exceeds several supervised baselines like SINet (0.872) and C²FNet (0.888) on F_β^ω. The DINOv1 variant also outperforms all prior unsupervised methods using DINOv1, confirming the framework's backbone-agnostic effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-label-based unsupervised COD line initiated by FOUND [44] and UCOS-DA [63], which both rely on fixed-strategy pseudo-labels (background seed, MaskCut) to train a simple 1×1 convolutional decoder. UCOD-DPL departs from this paradigm by introducing a teacher-student framework with an adaptive mixing mechanism (APM) that dynamically weights fixed-strategy and teacher-generated pseudo-labels via a GAN-like discriminator, a Dual-Branch Adversarial decoder that explicitly separates foreground and background feature learning with an orthogonal constraint, and a Look-Twice refinement inspired by ZoomNet/ZoomNext [37, 38]. The self-supervised backbone (DINOv1/DINOv2) follows the standard practice in recent unsupervised segmentation, while the adversarial training loop and orthogonal loss draw from GAN and disentanglement literature respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that UCOD-DPL closes much of the gap between unsupervised and fully-supervised COD, surpassing several supervised methods (HitNet, FSPNet, ZoomNet) on all four benchmarks with DINOv2, which reduces the annotation burden for a task where pixel-level labels are notoriously difficult to obtain. The APM module's adaptive mixing strategy is a generalizable principle for any pseudo-label-based self-training pipeline where label quality varies over training. However, the Look-Twice mechanism explicitly fails on occluded or fragmented objects (acknowledged by the authors), and the method's strong dependence on DINOv2's feature quality raises questions about transferability to domains where such pretraining is unavailable. Open questions include whether the adversarial decoder and APM can be decoupled and applied to other unsupervised segmentation tasks beyond COD.
