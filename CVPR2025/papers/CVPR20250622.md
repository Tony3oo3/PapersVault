## Problem

Decompositional 3D scene reconstruction from sparse views remains challenging because existing methods lack supplementary information for underconstrained regions such as occluded or uncaptured areas, leading to incomplete geometry and degraded appearance. While semantic or geometric regularizations help, they cannot hallucinate plausible content where no observations exist, and directly applying diffusion priors to the full scene breaks per-object 3D consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250622/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of posed RGB images (typically 10 views, with experiments at 5–15) along with per-pixel instance masks that segment each object and the background. It produces per-object SDF-based meshes with detailed UV texture maps suitable for photorealistic rendering in standard 3D software (e.g., Blender), as well as novel-view renderings and text-conditioned edits of geometry and appearance. The background is treated as an additional object and reconstructed via depth-guided inpainting of a panorama to avoid shape-appearance ambiguity.

## Method

DP-RECON is a three-stage pipeline: (1) object-compositional SDF reconstruction using standard rendering losses, (2) geometry optimization where each object's SDF is refined via Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from a frozen Stable Diffusion model applied to rendered normal+mask maps, and (3) appearance optimization where exported meshes are textured via NVDiffRast differentiable rendering with SDS on color maps. The key technical contribution is a visibility-guided mechanism: a learnable 3D visibility grid G(p) is trained from accumulated transmittance in volume rendering, then used to compute per-pixel visibility maps under novel views. A piecewise-linear weighting function w^v(z) reduces SDS loss in high-visibility (well-observed) regions to preserve fidelity to input images, while amplifying it in low-visibility regions to let the generative prior fill in missing content. Fast OccGrid sampling (0.01 s per 128×128 render) makes iterative SDS optimization practical, and the visibility grid also filters floating artifacts to define object-centric camera poses for SDS.

## Results

On Replica, DP-RECON achieves the best reconstruction quality with CD 7.91, F-Score 50.99, and NC 89.36, outperforming the closest baseline ObjectSDF++ (CD 8.57, F-Score 50.11, NC 85.44). On ScanNet++, it reaches CD 10.18, F-Score 45.13, NC 81.87, again leading all baselines. For rendering, the full model achieves MUSIQ 49.22 (Replica) and 45.71 (ScanNet++), substantially surpassing ObjectSDF++ (41.42 / 33.64) and MonoSDF (36.02 / 28.63), indicating markedly better visual quality in unobserved regions. Notably, with only 10 input views, DP-RECON achieves object reconstruction quality (CD 5.54, mIoU 88.21 on Replica) that exceeds baselines using 100 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-RECON outperforms all five baselines on every reconstruction metric (CD, F-Score, NC) across both datasets. Against the closest competitor ObjectSDF++, it reduces CD from 8.57 to 7.91 (Replica) and 10.67 to 10.18 (ScanNet++), while improving NC by ~4 points (89.36 vs 85.44; 81.87 vs 78.18). The gap widens dramatically against MonoSDF (CD 12.57→7.91 on Replica) and RICO (CD 17.36→7.91), confirming that generative priors fill in information that geometric regularization alone cannot. FreeNeRF and ZeroNVS* are far behind on geometry (CD 67.75 and 21.53 on Replica), as they lack explicit surface reconstruction. On rendering, DP-RECON's full model trades a small amount of full-reference fidelity (PSNR 24.52 vs ObjectSDF++'s 24.66 on Replica) for a large gain in no-reference quality (MUSIQ 49.22 vs 41.42), reflecting better visual quality in previously unobserved regions. The decompositional object reconstruction (Table 2) shows an even larger margin: mIoU 88.21 vs 71.72 (ObjectSDF++) on Replica, indicating far more complete and multi-view-consistent object shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DP-RECON sits at the intersection of two research threads: decompositional neural implicit surface reconstruction (ObjectSDF++, RICO) and diffusion-prior-guided 3D optimization (DreamFusion, ReconFusion, ExtraNeRF). It departs from prior diffusion-prior reconstruction work by applying SDS per-object rather than to the whole scene, and from prior decompositional methods by introducing a learnable visibility grid—derived from accumulated transmittance in volume rendering—to dynamically modulate the SDS loss. This visibility-guided mechanism is the paper's core methodological departure, addressing a conflict between reconstruction fidelity and generative guidance that prior work largely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method produces decomposed object meshes with detailed UV maps directly usable in standard 3D software (Blender), bridging the gap between neural reconstruction and practical VFX/robotics pipelines. The visibility-guided SDS weighting offers a general recipe for harmonizing generative priors with data-driven reconstruction that could transfer to other SDS-based 3D methods. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor or unbounded scenes, how sensitive it is to mask quality, and whether the reliance on a pre-trained 2D diffusion model limits geometric fidelity in structurally novel regions.
