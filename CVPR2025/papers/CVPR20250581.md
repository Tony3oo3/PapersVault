## Problem

One-shot pruning methods like SparseGPT and Wanda perform well at low sparsity (10%–50%) but degrade sharply at mid-to-high sparsity (50%–70%) because they prune blocks independently, allowing errors to accumulate and propagate to deeper layers. Full fine-tuning-based pruning could mitigate this but is infeasible on consumer-grade GPUs for 7B-parameter models, and Wanda's language-specific priors make it particularly weak on vision models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained Transformer backbone weights (OPT, Llama 2, SAM), 128 calibration samples (2048-token C4 segments for language; 128 SA-1B images for vision), and a target sparsity level, producing pruned model weights. A key operational constraint is that at most one Transformer block resides in GPU memory at any time: the sliding window holds two blocks, and intermediate activations are offloaded to host memory via chunked storage, keeping peak memory at 7.8 GB for OPT-6.7B on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

ICP introduces Block-wise Compensate Pruning, a sliding-window procedure that processes the backbone sequentially: after pruning block B^i with SparseGPT, the next block B^{i+1} is fine-tuned (1–12 epochs, Adam, MSE loss) on a labeled feature dataset where the input is the biased output of the pruned B^i and the target is the unbiased output of the unpruned B^i, thereby absorbing inter-block error before it propagates further. A second component, Sparsity Rearrangement, redistributes the global sparsity budget using two priors: inter-block, the last block receives reduced sparsity (controlled by α) since it has no successor to compensate its errors; intra-block, QKV matrices receive lower sparsity while fc2 receives higher sparsity (controlled by β) because fc2 errors are closer to the block output and thus easier for the next block to correct. The entire pipeline requires no full-model fine-tuning, no gradient storage across blocks, and no additional peak memory beyond SparseGPT.

## Results

On OPT-1.3B pruned to 70% unstructured sparsity, ICP achieves 36.78 perplexity on Wikitext-v2, compared to 51.75 (SparseGPT) and 99.53 (Wanda). On Llama 2-7B at 70% sparsity, ICP attains 46.94% mean zero-shot accuracy across seven tasks, outperforming SparseGPT (41.79%) and Wanda (34.34%). For vision, SAM-H at 70% sparsity on SA-1B reaches 72.85% IoU with ICP versus 65.69% (SparseGPT) and 53.00% (Wanda). Peak memory usage is 7.8 GB for ICP (1-epoch), slightly below SparseGPT's 8.3 GB, with a runtime of 1551 s versus 1004 s for SparseGPT on OPT-6.7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICP consistently outperforms all three baselines (Magnitude, SparseGPT, Wanda) across every model, sparsity level, and evaluation metric tested. Against SparseGPT, the closest competitor, ICP's advantage grows with sparsity: at 50% sparsity on Llama 2-7B the mean accuracy gap is modest (56.96% vs 56.11%), but at 70% it widens to 5.15 points (46.94% vs 41.79%), and on OPT-1.3B Wikitext-v2 perplexity drops from 51.75 to 36.78. Against Wanda, ICP's lead is even larger at high sparsity (e.g., 46.94% vs 34.34% on Llama 2-7B at 70%), and Wanda is particularly weak on vision models where its language-specific priors are inapplicable (SAM-H at 70%: 53.00% IoU vs ICP's 72.85%). Magnitude pruning collapses at sparsity ≥ 50% across all models and is not competitive. ICP also uses less peak GPU memory than SparseGPT (7.8 GB vs 8.3 GB on OPT-6.7B) while incurring roughly 1.5× the runtime, which the authors attribute to the single compensation epoch per block.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICP builds directly on SparseGPT's Optimal Brain Surgeon framework, inheriting its per-layer pruning-and-compensation mechanism, but departs from it by introducing a sliding-window inter-block compensation step that SparseGPT and Wanda both lack. It positions itself as a middle ground between resource-intensive iterative fine-tuning methods (LLM-Pruner, LLM Surgeon) and zero-fine-tuning approaches (Wanda), requiring only a single additional block in memory. The sparsity rearrangement component (inter-block α and intra-block β) is a novel addition that leverages structural priors of Transformer blocks—treating QKV as inner and fc2 as outer functions—to redistribute pruning budget without global information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that limits real-world deployment: 7B-scale models can be inferred but not fine-tuned on a single consumer GPU, and existing one-shot pruning methods degrade sharply at the 50–70% sparsity range where most compression targets fall. ICP's simplicity (one extra block, one epoch of MSE fine-tuning, no additional peak memory) makes it immediately adoptable for practitioners working under hardware constraints. Open questions remain around automatic tuning of α and β, extension to multimodal architectures, and whether the per-block compensation can be further exploited (e.g., block-level importance scoring). The method's model-agnosticism (validated on both OPT/Llama and SAM) suggests broader applicability, but the lack of multimodal testing and the reliance on hand-tuned hyperparameters limit its turnkey utility.
