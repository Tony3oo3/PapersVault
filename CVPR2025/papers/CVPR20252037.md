## Problem

Existing blind face restoration methods are designed for single images and, when naively applied to video, produce temporally inconsistent results due to frame-to-frame flickering. The core challenge is to handle unknown, diverse degradations in each frame while ensuring the restored sequence maintains temporal coherence — a problem that optical-flow-based alignment methods struggle with under severe degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252037/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 8-frame degraded face video clips as input, where degradation is synthesized via Gaussian blur, downsampling, Gaussian noise, and JPEG compression (following the CodeFormer protocol). It outputs a high-quality 8-frame face video at the original resolution. The model is trained on VFHQ (15,000+ HQ clips) and evaluated on HDTF, VFHQ-Test, and in-the-wild YouTube Faces subsets (YTF-Medium/Hard) with no ground truth available for the latter.

## Method

DCP-MP learns a motion-aware semantic dictionary via VQGAN-style vector quantization on HQ videos, where each spatial-temporal location is encoded as a discrete element and a neighbor set N(v) records which elements co-occur across adjacent frames, thereby capturing motion as element-to-element relationships. A temporal-aware element predictor (stacked self-attention + frame attention layers) predicts the dictionary index map from degraded encoder features, and the predicted elements are refined via cross-attention against the neighbor-set elements to enforce motion-consistent temporal transitions. A conditional structure feature correction module, conditioned on the refined latent code, adaptively filters and corrects the degraded skip-connection features from the encoder before they reach the generator, reducing structural noise. The VQGAN-based generator then synthesizes the HQ video from the corrected multi-scale features and the temporally coherent latent code, trained jointly with pixel/VGG consistency loss, adversarial loss, L2 structure-consistency loss, feature-consistency regularization, and cross-entropy element prediction loss.

## Results

On VFHQ-Test-LQ, DCP-MP achieves PSNR 25.10, LPIPS 0.2418, FID 53.12, and IFD 3.71, outperforming all image and video restoration baselines across every metric. On HDTF-LQ, it reaches PSNR 24.70, LPIPS 0.2411, FID 26.24, and IFD 3.62. On in-the-wild data (YTF-Medium/Hard), DCP-MP attains the lowest FID (52.81/54.10) and IFD (5.49/7.36), confirming superior temporal coherence and realism under real degradations. A user study on 40 in-the-wild clips further confirms DCP-MP receives the highest average scores in both single-frame quality and temporal coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCP-MP outperforms all five image restoration baselines (CodeFormer, RestoreFormer, DR2, DiffBIR, DiffFace) on every metric across all four test sets, with particularly large margins on IFD (e.g., 3.71 vs. 9.86 for CodeFormer on VFHQ-Test-LQ), confirming that image-only methods suffer from temporal inconsistency. Against the five video restoration baselines (BasicVSR++, MIA-VSR, IA-RT, FMA-Net, PGTFormer), DCP-MP achieves substantially lower FID (53.12 vs. 102.73–210.36 on VFHQ-Test-LQ) and LPIPS (0.2418 vs. 0.4086–0.5223), demonstrating that video methods without generative priors fail to recover high-frequency facial textures under severe degradation. The closest competitor, PGTFormer, still trails DCP-MP by 114.31 in FID and 0.1668 in LPIPS on VFHQ-Test-LQ. On in-the-wild YTF-Hard data, DCP-MP achieves FID 54.10 versus the next-best PGTFormer at 109.87, more than halving the gap. The ablation study further shows that removing the element predictor (replacing it with nearest-neighbor matching) degrades LPIPS by 0.2094 and FID by 52.78, while removing motion regularization increases IFD from 3.71 to 4.01, validating each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dictionary-based blind face restoration paradigm of CodeFormer from static images to the video domain by introducing a motion-aware semantic dictionary that encodes temporal dynamics as element-relationship structures. It builds on the VQGAN architecture for discrete representation learning and departs from standard video super-resolution methods (BasicVSR++, PGTFormer) by incorporating generative priors rather than relying solely on optical-flow-based alignment, which degrades under severe corruption. The conditional structure feature correction module represents a novel architectural intervention on U-Net skip connections, conditioning them on predicted dictionary elements to denoise structural features before they reach the generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: image-based blind face restoration methods lose temporal coherence when naively applied frame-by-frame, while video super-resolution methods lack the generative capacity to recover severe degradation. The motion-aware dictionary offers an alternative to optical-flow-based temporal modeling that is robust to the very degradations that break flow estimation. Limitations include evaluation on only 8-frame clips, synthetic degradation for training, and absence of ground truth for in-the-wild testing, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences and robustness to truly unknown real-world degradation patterns.
