## Problem

While soft-Dice loss is commonly preferred over cross-entropy for medical image segmentation to improve Dice performance, the theoretical reason for this discrepancy was not well understood. This paper shows that the difference arises from how label noise affects the optimal solutions of the two losses and from the threshold used to convert soft segmentations to binary labels, rather than from an inherent superiority of one loss over the other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252580/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D CT volumes sub-sampled to half resolution and extracts 64³ voxel patches centered on target structures (kidney, aorta, esophagus from TotalSegmentor). It produces a soft segmentation probability map that can be thresholded either at the standard 0.5 or at the paper's alternative threshold t_m to yield a binary segmentation mask. The noise model deforms ground-truth labels via Gaussian field deformations with controllable intensity (parameter a) and smoothness (parameter b), producing one noisy sample per data point.

## Method

The paper introduces a Gaussian field deformation noise model that is approximately volume-preserving, with closed-form marginal probability expressions and efficient sampling via independently scattered Gaussian measures. The core theoretical result shows that the optimal soft-Dice solution can be exactly recovered by thresholding the optimal cross-entropy solution (which equals the marginal probability) with an a priori unknown but computable threshold t_m = sup_{s'} D_m(s')/2, rather than the standard 0.5. This explains why soft-Dice appears to outperform cross-entropy under noise: the 0.5 threshold is suboptimal for cross-entropy as noise increases, while soft-Dice implicitly uses the correct threshold. Empirically, a standard 3D U-Net trained with cross-entropy and thresholded at t_m matches or exceeds soft-Dice performance on three organ segmentation tasks while retaining cross-entropy's convexity and calibration properties.

## Results

Experiments are conducted on three organ segmentation tasks (kidney, aorta, esophagus) from the TotalSegmentor dataset (400 cases each, 5-fold CV) with Gaussian field deformation noise at levels a = 0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3. The primary metric is average Dice score. At zero noise, cross-entropy with 0.5-threshold (CE⁽⁰⁾) and soft-Dice with 0.5-threshold (SD⁽⁰⁾) are indistinguishable, but their performance gap widens monotonically as noise increases. Cross-entropy with the alternative threshold t_m (CE⁽∗⁾) consistently outperforms CE⁽⁰⁾, with the improvement growing with noise level, and closes the gap to SD⁽⁰⁾ as predicted by the theory (Eq. 23). Specific numerical values are reported in the Supplementary Material; the main paper presents results as Dice-vs-noise-level plots (Figure 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three loss/threshold combinations: CE⁽⁰⁾ (cross-entropy, 0.5 threshold), SD⁽⁰⁾ (soft-Dice, 0.5 threshold), and CE⁽∗⁾ (cross-entropy, alternative threshold t_m from Eq. 22). SD⁽⁰⁾ outperforms CE⁽⁰⁾ on Dice as noise increases, confirming prior experimental observations [2, 4, 12] that the standard 0.5 threshold is suboptimal for cross-entropy under label noise. CE⁽∗⁾ outperforms CE⁽⁰⁾ across all noise levels and organs, with the advantage growing as noise increases, and its performance approaches that of SD⁽⁰⁾, validating the theoretical equivalence (Eq. 23). The key practical implication is that CE⁽∗⁾ achieves soft-Dice-level Dice performance while retaining the convexity and calibration properties of cross-entropy, avoiding the known stability issues of soft-Dice [24]. The comparison is limited to these three configurations on a single dataset (TotalSegmentor) with a specific noise model, so generalizability to other noise types or datasets is not directly tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two prior theoretical threads: the characterization of optimal segmentations for Accuracy and Dice under label noise (Nordström et al., NeurIPS 2022) and the analogous characterization for soft-Dice (Nordström et al., 2023). By combining these with the optimal-thresholding framework from statistical learning (Lipton et al., 2014), it shows that the optimal soft-Dice solution is exactly recoverable by thresholding the optimal cross-entropy solution with a data-dependent threshold t_m. The paper also formalizes a Gaussian field deformation noise model that prior experimental work (Heller et al. 2018; Vorontsov & Kadoury 2021) used informally, providing closed-form marginal probabilities and a stochastic-integral representation for efficient sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result reframes the widely observed empirical superiority of soft-Dice over cross-entropy under label noise as a thresholding artifact rather than an intrinsic property of the loss, suggesting practitioners can retain cross-entropy's convexity, calibration, and informative probability maps while matching soft-Dice's Dice performance. However, the equivalence between soft-label and expectation-based formulations of Dice holds only approximately (requiring near-constant label volume), and the experimental validation is limited to three organs on TotalSegmentor with a single parametric noise family. Open questions include how well the alternative threshold generalizes to multi-class segmentation, other noise types (e.g., independent label flipping), and whether the per-prediction threshold computation remains tractable at clinical scale.
