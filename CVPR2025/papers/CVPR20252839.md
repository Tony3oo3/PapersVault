## Problem

Pose-free NeRF methods can jointly optimize camera poses and scene geometry, but they suffer from poor reconstruction quality due to the lack of long-range cross-frame consistency constraints. Prior work either uses optical flow merely as a regularizer for pose optimization or relies on off-the-shelf flow estimators, leaving the potential for jointly learning geometry, poses, and dense flow within a single neural representation largely unexplored. Additionally, inferring dense correspondences (flow) between arbitrary novel views—a capability absent from vanilla NeRF—remains an open problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of unposed RGB images and known camera intrinsics as input, requiring no SfM preprocessing. It simultaneously outputs rendered novel-view images, 6-DoF camera poses, metric depth maps, and dense optical flow. Unusually, the flow output is pose-conditioned rather than time-conditioned, enabling inference of long-range flow (both forward and backward) between arbitrary novel views at test time, even though training supervision uses only consecutive forward pseudo-flow computed by RAFT.

## Method

Flow-NeRF employs a two-branch architecture (geometry and flow) coupled through three key mechanisms. First, a shared point sampling strategy back-projects the same 2D pixels to world space for the geometry branch and to camera space for the flow branch, with a fixed depth ratio (z_i = α·d_i) ensuring both branches represent the same physical scene points. Second, the flow branch uses a RealNVP invertible network to learn a bijective 3D mapping between camera-space points of two frames, conditioned on 6-DoF pose vectors (rather than time indices) to enable novel-view flow inference. Third, a feature message-passing module extracts canonical-space features via a GaborNet from the flow branch and concatenates them into an intermediate layer of the geometry branch MLP, injecting complementary motion cues into the appearance/geometry representation. The two branches are jointly optimized with a photometric rendering loss and a flow loss against RAFT-computed pseudo-flow, with additional 3D point cloud and 2D warping losses for regularization.

## Results

On Tanks and Temples, Flow-NeRF achieves a mean PSNR of 28.73, SSIM of 0.82, and LPIPS of 0.29, surpassing the closest competitor Nope-NeRF (26.34 PSNR) by over 2 points. On ScanNet, the method reaches 32.55 mean PSNR and 0.85 SSIM, outperforming all baselines. For depth estimation on ScanNet, Flow-NeRF achieves Abs Rel of 0.047 and δ₁ of 0.982, dramatically outperforming Nope-NeRF (0.141 Abs Rel, 0.828 δ₁). For long-range novel-view flow on Sintel at frame interval 16, Flow-NeRF achieves an EPE of 1.683 versus RAFT's 2.089.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flow-NeRF outperforms all four pose-free NeRF baselines (Nope-NeRF, BARF, NeRFmm, SC-NeRF) on novel view synthesis across both datasets, with the largest gap over BARF and NeRFmm on Tanks and Temples (28.73 vs. 23.42 and 22.50 PSNR respectively). Against the closest competitor Nope-NeRF, Flow-NeRF gains ~2.4 PSNR on Tanks and Temples and ~0.7 PSNR on ScanNet, while also achieving substantially better depth accuracy (Abs Rel 0.047 vs. 0.141) and pose accuracy (ATE 0.014 vs. 0.030). On camera pose estimation, Flow-NeRF matches Nope-NeRF on RPE_r but clearly wins on ATE and RPE_t, and dominates BARF, NeRFmm, and SC-NeRF by large margins. For long-range novel-view flow on Sintel, Flow-NeRF surpasses RAFT (which is pre-trained on a large dataset and fine-tuned) by 0.406 EPE at frame interval 16, demonstrating that learning flow within a unified NeRF representation can exceed a dedicated flow estimator on long-range correspondences.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flow-NeRF extends the pose-free NeRF line (BARF, Nope-NeRF) by elevating dense optical flow from a mere regularizer to a first-class jointly-optimized objective within the same neural representation. The flow branch's bijective mapping formulation is directly motivated by Omnimotion's correspondence-field approach, but the key departure is replacing Omnimotion's time-conditioned input with a pose-conditioned design, which enables generalization to novel views rather than being restricted to training views. The shared point sampling and feature message-passing mechanism are novel architectural contributions that couple the geometry and flow branches, whereas prior work (e.g., FlowCam, CorresNeRF) used flow only as an external supervision signal without integrating it into the scene representation itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces novel-view flow as a new inference capability—rendering dense correspondences between arbitrary unseen views—which is genuinely absent from prior NeRF literature and could benefit downstream tasks like localization and 3D tracking. The finding that flow features learned under motion supervision substantially improve geometry (4-point PSNR gain, large depth metric improvements) suggests that cross-view correspondence signals carry structural information that photometric loss alone cannot capture. Limitations include the static-scene assumption, reliance on RAFT pseudo-flow for supervision, and evaluation on relatively small-scale datasets (4 ScanNet scenes, 8 Tanks and Temples scenes), leaving open questions about scalability to dynamic scenes and larger benchmarks.
