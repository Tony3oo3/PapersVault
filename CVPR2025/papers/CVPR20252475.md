## Problem

LLMs excel as high-level planners for embodied tasks but cannot directly generate precise low-level actions because they lack spatial awareness of real environments. The core barrier is the ineffective communication between the LLM ('brain') and the robot ('body'): the robot cannot convey updated spatial information in a format the LLM can reason over, forcing reliance on external navigation APIs and causing frequent re-planning. This paper asks whether a compact, interpretable environmental representation can unlock LLMs as unified high-level and low-level action planners.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural language instruction and a stream of egocentric RGB-D observations, which are processed into a 64×64 chessboard (grid size 0.25 m, matching the robot's step length in a 16 m room). The chessboard state is serialized as textual object occupancy coordinate sets (e.g., 'target at {(3,7),(3,8)}') rather than raw images. Output is a two-level plan: a sequence of (Action, Object) subgoals at the high level, and at each timestep a single next-position coordinate on the chessboard, which is directly converted into a short sequence of primitive actions (MoveForward 0.25 m, TurnLeft 90°, TurnRight 90°). The action space is restricted to four adjacent grids to match current LLM spatial-reasoning limits.

## Method

R2C introduces an Environment Filter that converts each RGB-D observation into a 2D semantic map via instance segmentation and depth voxelization, then applies a dilation kernel (for collision avoidance) and a max-pooling kernel (for downsampling) to produce a compact chessboard, with a goal-aware aggregation function resolving overlapping objects by subgoal relevance and size. The LLM then plays a 'chess game': given the textual chessboard state, current subgoal, game rules, and recent action history, it predicts the next grid position. To make this feasible, the paper proposes a Chain-of-Thought Decision (CoT-D) fine-tuning paradigm with four sequential subtasks—key information extraction, direction judgment, target prediction, and selection analysis—linked into a coherent reasoning chain that is jointly trained with high-level task decomposition. The full pipeline is fine-tuned end-to-end on Mistral-7B and Llama-7B using 264,915 samples collected from the ALFRED training set, enabling 7B models to match or exceed GPT-4 zero-shot performance on the ALFRED benchmark.

## Results

On the ALFRED benchmark, R2C-Mistral-7B achieves 22.31% success rate (SR) and 32.40% goal-conditioned success rate (GC) on Val Seen, and 22.35% SR / 31.97% GC on Val Unseen, setting the state-of-the-art among LLM-based methods. R2C-GPT-4 in zero-shot achieves 20.00% SR on Val Seen and 24.00% SR on Val Unseen, showing a +4.00 ΔSR (improvement on unseen over seen), whereas all specialist baselines suffer large generalization drops (e.g., E.T. drops by −39.27). On the AI2THOR ObjectNav task, R2C-Mistral-7B (trained only on ALFRED) achieves 52.25% SR and 29.56% SPL, outperforming S2P (46.16% SR) and SAVN (40.86% SR) without additional fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LLM-based generalists, R2C-Mistral-7B (22.31% SR seen) outperforms SayCan (12.30%) by 10.01 points and LLM-P/GPT (16.45%) by 5.86 points on Val Seen, and surpasses both on Val Unseen as well (22.35% vs. 9.88% and 15.36%). R2C-GPT-4 in zero-shot also beats SayCan and LLM-P on unseen environments (24.00% vs. 9.88% and 15.36%), and is the only method that shows a positive ΔSR, indicating better performance on unseen than seen scenes. Against specialist models trained from scratch, R2C-Mistral-7B is slightly behind FILM (24.63%) and LEBP (27.63%) on Val Seen but matches or exceeds them on Val Unseen (22.35% vs. 20.10% and 22.36%), and dramatically outperforms E.T. (7.32%) and HiTUT (12.44%) on unseen, where specialists collapse. On the AI2THOR ObjectNav task, R2C (52.25% SR, 29.56% SPL) beats S2P (46.16% SR, 28.01% SPL) and SAVN (40.86% SR, 16.15% SPL) without any additional fine-tuning, demonstrating cross-task generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM-based embodied agents (SayCan, LLM-P, Inner Monologue) where LLMs serve exclusively as high-level planners and low-level execution is delegated to separate policy networks or deterministic algorithms. It draws on semantic mapping techniques from FILM and chain-of-thought reasoning, but introduces the Room-to-Chessboard representation as a novel 'common language' that enables a single LLM to perform both high-level task decomposition and low-level coordinate-level action planning simultaneously. The CoT-D fine-tuning paradigm, which structures spatial reasoning into four sequential subtasks (extraction, direction, prediction, selection), is a new training formulation not present in prior LLM-planning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R2C provides the first practical demonstration that LLMs can generate precise, environment-aware low-level navigation actions without external navigation APIs, closing the 'brain-body' communication gap that has limited LLM-based embodied agents. The chessboard representation offers a scalable, interpretable interface that could be adopted by other embodied systems. Key limitations include the segmentation bottleneck (ground-truth segmentation nearly doubles success rate), the restricted 4-direction action space, and difficulty with very large scenes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to continuous action spaces, multi-agent settings, and significantly larger environments beyond the 16m room size tested.
