## Problem

Current medical vision-language pre-training (VLP) frameworks focus almost exclusively on feature extraction and cross-modal comprehension (contrastive alignment, matching, text generation), while largely neglecting the generation of visual content. This gap limits the model's capacity to synthesize coherent, fine-grained visual representations from textual prompts, which is particularly important in radiology where subtle details (small nodules, slight opacities) carry diagnostic significance. Existing attempts to incorporate masked image modelling into VLP do not fully enable generation of comprehensive visual content nor capture detailed pixel-level information.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 chest X-ray images and clinical reports (up to 95 tokens) as paired inputs. Images are split into patches and encoded by a frozen ViT-g to produce preliminary visual embeddings, while text is tokenized into word embeddings. The unified framework simultaneously produces four types of outputs: aligned visual-textual embeddings (32 learnable query vectors projected to a shared space), a binary image-text match logit, an autoregressively generated medical report, and a reconstructed or text-conditioned synthetic chest X-ray decoded from hierarchical discrete latent codes (top and bottom codebooks of 512 entries, 768-dim).

## Method

MedUnifier builds a 12-layer BERT-styled transformer encoder that fuses 32 learnable query embeddings with tokenized clinical reports, interacting with frozen ViT-g visual features via cross-attention layers within each block. The key novelty is a text-grounded image generation (TIG) module that uses a hierarchical VQ-VAE: a top-level latent adapter transforms the text-informed query embeddings into a spatial feature map quantized by a codebook, while a bottom-level adapter quantizes local visual features concatenated with the text CLS embedding, conditioned on the top-level code. A residual decoder reconstructs the image from both discrete levels, and the TIG loss combines pixel-level MSE with standard VQ commitment terms. All four objectives (ITC, ITM, ITG, TIG) are co-trained end-to-end with equal weights on MIMIC-CXR v2.0.0, with a text generator that shares weights with the encoder's text branch for report generation. The discrete visual representations align naturally with the encoder's learnable-embedding dictionary, avoiding the need for a separately pre-trained image tokenizer or iterative denoising as required by diffusion-based alternatives.

## Results

MedUnifier (with TIG) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all evaluated task categories. On fine-tuned image classification with 100% training data, it reaches 91.7% AUC on RSNA, 94.8% AUC on SIIM-ACR, and 93.5% accuracy on COVIDx, surpassing the best prior methods (LoVT, PRIOR, MGCA) by 2.4, 2.5, and 1.2 points respectively. For cross-modal retrieval on MIMIC-CXR 5×200, it achieves 60.7 mAP@1 for image-to-text retrieval (vs. 52.4 for REFERS, the next best) and 63.1 mAP@1 for text-to-image retrieval. On medical report generation, it scores 41.5 BLEU-1, 22.7 METEOR, and 34.0 ROUGE-L, outperforming all baselines including MOTOR (15.6 BLEU-4, 19.3 METEOR, 31.4 ROUGE-L).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedUnifier (with TIG) outperforms all 17 baselines on fine-tuned image classification, cross-modal retrieval, and medical report generation. It surpasses the strongest dual-encoder baselines (REFERS, CXR-CLIP, MedCLIP) by 8.3 mAP@1 on image-to-text retrieval and the strongest prior unified framework (PTUnifier [10]) by 2.2 BLEU-4 and 3.3 ROUGE-L on report generation. The only losses are: (1) GLoRIA [26] achieves higher zero-shot accuracy on CheXpert 5×200 (45.0 vs. 43.5), which the authors attribute to GLoRIA's pre-training on the full CheXpert dataset; (2) LLM-CXR [33] and RoentGen [6] achieve lower FID scores for synthetic image generation (22.8 and 42.4 vs. MedUnifier's 46.2), though both require additional pre-training stages (VQ-GAN tokenizer or iterative diffusion denoising) that MedUnifier avoids through its one-stage VQ-VAE framework. MedUnifier also outperforms UniXGer [32] by 32.0 FID points, demonstrating the advantage of discrete VQ-VAE representations over autoregressive generation for medical imaging. The ablation (Table 6) confirms that the TIG module contributes the largest single gain over ITC-only pre-training (+5.8 mAP@1 on ITR, +9.0 ACC on MIMIC zero-shot), and that combining all four objectives yields the best overall performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedUnifier extends the BLIP-2-style architecture (frozen ViT + learnable query embeddings + cross-attention) into the medical domain and augments the standard Med-VLP triad of ITC, ITM, and ITG objectives with a fourth, text-grounded image generation (TIG) loss built on hierarchical VQ-VAE quantization inspired by VQ-VAE-2. This departs from prior unified Med-VLP frameworks such as PTUnifier, which handled multi-modal understanding but lacked any visual generative capacity during pre-training, and from diffusion-based medical image generators (RoentGen, MedM2G) that require separate tokenization or iterative denoising stages. The paper positions itself as the first to jointly optimize discrete visual generation within a single Med-VLP pre-training pass, eliminating the need for an expert image tokenizer or multi-stage training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a pixel-level reconstruction objective (TIG) improves downstream understanding tasks (retrieval, classification, report generation) beyond what text generation alone provides, the paper offers a practical recipe for medical VLP practitioners seeking stronger representations without additional pre-training stages. However, the evaluation is confined to 2D chest X-rays on a single pre-training corpus (MIMIC-CXR), and the synthetic-image FID (46.2) remains notably higher than dedicated generators like LLM-CXR (22.8), leaving open whether the VQ-VAE-based generation quality is sufficient for clinical data augmentation at scale. Broader applicability to 3D volumes, other imaging modalities, and larger-scale medical corpora remains untested.
