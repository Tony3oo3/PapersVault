## Problem

Existing aerial-ground place recognition models handle only same-modal inputs (ground images vs. aerial images), while multi-modal PR models address only ground-ground same-view retrieval. The combination of fusing ground camera and LiDAR data for cross-view matching against an aerial database remains unexplored, leaving a gap for practical ground-robot localization that leverages both rich multi-sensor ground data and efficiently collected aerial maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250111/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The query side takes a ground RGB image and a LiDAR point cloud (converted to 3D voxel format) simultaneously, while the database side uses 2D aerial images (satellite or road map tiles from Google Maps Static API at zoom 20, 640×640 px, covering ~75×75 m²). The output is a single ground scene descriptor formed as a weighted sum of a decoded 2D embedding, a decoded 3D embedding, and the manifold fusion embedding, which is then matched against aerial descriptors via L2 distance for retrieval at a 25 m positive threshold.

## Method

AGPlace employs a two-stage manifold-based fusion strategy. In Stage 1, learnable manifold chart functions map pooled 2D and 3D backbone features onto a shared C-dimensional manifold, and the resulting fusion state is evolved block-by-block (from L to 1) via parameterized neural ODEs whose non-intersection property theoretically guarantees distinct descriptors for different places. In Stage 2, the final fusion embedding is projected back into 2D and 3D spaces through inverse chart functions, concatenated with the last-block feature maps, and decoded to produce modality-specific embeddings that, together with the fusion embedding, form the final descriptor. Training uses a multi-view multi-modal (MVMM) loss that simultaneously enforces pairwise alignment across aerial-aerial, ground-ground, ground-aerial, and per-modality-to-aerial domains, combined with a triplet loss using hard negative mining to provide relative distance guidance.

## Results

On the KITTI360-AG dataset, AGPlace achieves R@1/5/10 of 32.0/47.6/54.9 with satellite imagery and 28.2/43.3/52.0 with road maps, outperforming all 21 baselines across both aerial database types. On nuScenes-AG, AGPlace reaches R@1/5 of 75.6/87.2 with full sensors, and maintains 22.8/33.1 (camera failure) and 12.9/23.0 (LiDAR failure), substantially exceeding the best baseline (MinkLoc++: 16.9/27.3 and 2.6/7.3 respectively). On the Oxford RobotCar benchmark, AGPlace attains AR@1% of 99.7/98.3 (Oxford-Mink+) and 99.9/99.3 (Oxford-Ada) without extra training data, re-ranking, or multi-camera inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGPlace outperforms all 21 baselines on KITTI360-AG in both satellite and road map settings. Among cross-view 2D baselines, ArcGeo (28.2 R@1) and Sample4Geo (27.0 R@1) are the strongest, yet AGPlace exceeds them by 3.8 and 5.0 R@1 points respectively. Among multi-modal ground-ground baselines adapted to cross-view, LCPR (27.7 R@1) and MinkLoc++ (28.9 R@1) lead, with AGPlace surpassing them by 4.3 and 3.1 R@1 points. On nuScenes-AG, AGPlace's advantage is most pronounced under sensor failure: when LiDAR is dropped, AGPlace achieves 12.9 R@1 versus 2.6 for MinkLoc++ and 0.8 for UMF, a 5–16× improvement. On Oxford RobotCar, AGPlace matches or exceeds the best prior results (HMPR+re-rank: 99.7/99.0; UMF+re-rank: 99.3/98.3) while using no extra training data, no re-ranking, and a single camera, achieving 99.9/99.3 on Oxford-Ada.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGPlace sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: multi-modal ground-ground PR (MinkLoc++, AdaFusion, LCPR) and single-modal aerial-ground cross-view PR (TransGeo, Sample4Geo, ArcGeo). The backbone architecture is directly inherited from MinkLoc++, while the cross-view alignment problem follows the formulation established by TransGeo and Sample4Geo. The key departure is the introduction of a manifold-based neural ODE fusion mechanism and a multi-view multi-modal (MVMM) loss that simultaneously optimizes aerial-aerial, ground-ground, and cross-view/cross-modal pair distances — a combination not present in either prior thread.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first multi-modal aerial-ground cross-view PR benchmark (KITTI360-AG, nuScenes-AG) and demonstrates that easily accessible road maps can rival satellite imagery for this task, lowering the practical barrier to deployment. The sensor-failure robustness results (e.g., 12.9 R@1 under LiDAR dropout vs. 2.6–8.6 for baselines) are directly relevant to real-world autonomous driving where sensors degrade. Limitations include evaluation restricted to urban environments and the absence of aerial-side multi-modal fusion, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to rural, forested, or indoor-adjacent settings.
