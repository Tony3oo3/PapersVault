## Problem

Existing video generation models produce realistic videos but lack explicit 3D structure, causing temporal inconsistencies (objects popping in and out) and imprecise camera control, since camera parameters are merely network inputs that the model must implicitly map to visual content. This limits their use in production workflows requiring long, spatially coherent videos with user-specified camera trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252125/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or more RGB images (or time-synchronized dynamic videos) with known or estimated camera poses (via DROID-SLAM), plus a user-specified camera trajectory. It produces a photorealistic video (14 frames in ~30 s on one A100) that precisely follows the requested trajectory, along with a maintained 3D point-cloud cache that can be updated autoregressively for long-video generation. The internal representation is a spatiotemporal L×V array of colored point clouds, rendered to RGB+mask video pairs at 8× spatial compression in VAE latent space.

## Method

GEN3C constructs a 3D cache by unprojecting per-pixel depth estimates (DAV2) of input images or generated frames into colored point clouds, then renders this cache along the user's camera trajectory to produce a 2D conditioning video and a disocclusion mask. The rendered video is encoded by a frozen VAE, masked, and channel-concatenated with the noisy target latent before being fed into a fine-tuned video diffusion model (Stable Video Diffusion or Cosmos); for multi-view inputs, each view's latent is passed through the first layer independently and combined via max-pooling, a permutation-invariant fusion that avoids bounding the number of views. The mask is applied by element-wise multiplication rather than channel concatenation, keeping the architecture unchanged and avoiding untrained parameters. Training uses standard denoising score matching with paired data created by treating past/future frames as the 3D cache and current frames as ground truth. For long videos, an autoregressive scheme generates overlapping L-frame chunks and incrementally updates the 3D cache by depth-estimating and unprojecting newly generated frames, ensuring consistency across the full sequence.

## Results

On single-view-to-video generation, GEN3C achieves PSNR 19.88 / SSIM 0.78 / LPIPS 0.20 on in-domain RE10K and PSNR 18.66 / SSIM 0.67 / LPIPS 0.20 on out-of-domain Tanks-and-Temples, outperforming all four baselines by 1.7–5.2 dB in PSNR. For two-view NVS, GEN3C reaches PSNR 24.08 (interpolation) and 21.56 (extrapolation) on RE10K, surpassing MVSplat by 2.68 and 6.05 dB respectively. On driving-scene NVS, GEN3C achieves FID 7.93 at 0 m deviation, compared to 48.34 (Nerfacto) and 34.81 (3D-GS), and maintains FID 35.33 at 4 m deviation where baselines degrade to 112.40 and 81.26. On monocular dynamic NVS, GEN3C matches GCD on PSNR (19.41 vs 19.37) but substantially improves LPIPS (0.29 vs 0.48) and FID (98.58 vs 150.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEN3C outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. In single-view-to-video (Table 1), it beats the strongest baseline CameraCtrl [16] by 2.78 dB PSNR on T&T and 1.48 dB on RE10K, while Plücker-embedding-based methods (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl) show the largest out-of-domain degradation, confirming that explicit 3D geometry generalizes better than learned camera-parameter mappings. In two-view NVS (Table 2), GEN3C surpasses MVSplat [8] by 2.68 dB (interpolation) and 6.05 dB (extrapolation) on RE10K, and PixelSplat [6] by 2.34 dB and 3.06 dB respectively, with the gap widening sharply for extrapolation. For driving-scene NVS (Table 3), GEN3C's FID of 7.93 at 0 m is 6.3× lower than Nerfacto [50] (48.34) and 4.4× lower than 3D-GS [25] (34.81), and the advantage grows to 3.2× and 2.3× at 4 m deviation, where reconstruction methods produce severe artifacts. On monocular dynamic NVS (Table 4), GEN3C ties GCD [53] on PSNR (19.41 vs 19.37) but halves LPIPS (0.29 vs 0.48) and reduces FID by 35% (98.58 vs 150.64), indicating substantially better perceptual quality and distributional fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEN3C builds on Stable Video Diffusion (and later Cosmos) as a base video diffusion model, but departs fundamentally from prior camera-controllable video generation (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl) that conditions on Plücker embeddings or numerical camera parameters. Instead, it introduces an explicit 3D point-cloud cache—constructed by unprojecting per-pixel depth estimates—as the conditioning signal, rendered to 2D along the user's camera trajectory before being injected into the diffusion model. This positions the work at the intersection of classical 3D graphics pipelines (depth unprojection, point-cloud rendering) and modern generative video models, and contrasts with per-scene NVS reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS) by leveraging the rich prior of a pre-trained diffusion model rather than optimizing scene-specific parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D cache provides a practical, modular bridge between explicit geometric control and the generative flexibility of large video models, enabling precise camera control and long-video temporal consistency without per-scene optimization. A key limitation is that dynamic content still requires a pre-generated input video to supply object motion, and the autoregressive cache-update strategy for long videos introduces cumulative depth-estimation error. The approach's modularity—swapping base models from SVD to Cosmos with minimal retraining—suggests it will benefit from future advances in video generation, making it a potentially widely adopted technique for production pipelines in driving simulation, film, and VR/AR.
