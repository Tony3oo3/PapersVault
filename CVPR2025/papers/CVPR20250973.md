## Problem

Existing 3D diffusion models are largely confined to object-level generation in canonical bounded spaces, and scene-level methods are restricted to single rooms or low-resolution outputs. Generating large-scale, arbitrary-sized 3D scenes with coherent global structure and high-frequency surface detail remains an open challenge due to the highly unstructured geometry, diverse object arrangements, and varying spatial extents of real scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250973/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the method starts from random Gaussian noise; for probabilistic scene completion, it takes a partial TUDF voxel grid as conditioning. Scenes are represented as truncated unsigned distance field (TUDF) grids at 2.2 cm voxel resolution with a 10 cm truncation threshold, decomposed into a 3-level latent tree with 4-channel latent feature volumes at each level. The output is a full-resolution TUDF voxel grid (convertible to mesh) of arbitrary spatial extent, synthesized patch-by-patch with overlapping regions to ensure seamlessness.

## Method

LT3SD introduces a latent tree representation that hierarchically factorizes a 3D scene into complementary lower-frequency geometry (TUDF) and higher-frequency latent feature volumes at each of N resolution levels, learned via 3D CNN encoder-decoder pairs trained with L2 reconstruction loss. A 3D UNet diffusion model is then trained at each level to generate the latent feature patch conditioned on the corresponding geometry patch (or both unconditionally at the coarsest level), using standard denoising score matching on randomly cropped fixed-size patches. At inference, generation proceeds coarse-to-fine: the coarsest level uses autoregressive Stable Inpainting to lay out patches breadth-first on the ground plane, while higher levels employ a MultiDiffusion-style parallel denoising scheme where all patches are denoised simultaneously at each timestep and blended via overlap averaging, enabling efficient synthesis of arbitrarily large scenes.

## Results

On the 3D-FRONT dataset, LT3SD with the full 3-level latent tree (17.6-8.8-2.2 cm) achieves a FID of 13.39, a 70% improvement over the strongest baseline BlockFusion (45.55). It also attains the best COV (53.10% CD / 54.66% EMD), lowest MMD (3.51 CD / 27.30 EMD), and 1-NNA closest to the ideal 50% (53.22% CD / 52.56% EMD), indicating superior coverage, fidelity, and distributional similarity. The single-level variant (17.6-2.2 cm) scores FID 59.23, confirming the importance of the hierarchical coarse-to-fine structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LT3SD outperforms all three baselines on every metric in its full 3-level configuration. Against BlockFusion (the strongest baseline), LT3SD reduces FID from 45.55 to 13.39 (≈70% improvement) and more than doubles COV (53.10% vs 24.32% CD), while also achieving a lower MMD (3.51 vs 5.10 CD) and a 1-NNA far closer to the ideal 50% (53.22% vs 89.01% CD), indicating much better global structural coherence. Against NFD, LT3SD improves FID from 266.27 to 13.39 and COV from 44.65% to 53.10% (CD), with a lower MMD (3.51 vs 3.65 CD). Against PVD, the gains are even more dramatic: FID drops from 237.85 to 13.39, and 1-NNA improves from 70.83% to 53.22% (CD). Qualitatively, BlockFusion produces smooth local surfaces but fragmented room structures (misaligned walls), NFD generates plausible layouts but lacks fine surface detail, and PVD yields only sparse point clouds; LT3SD combines coherent room-level geometry with high-frequency surface detail. In terms of efficiency, LT3SD generates a 45.1 m × 90.3 m × 2.8 m scene (~170 rooms) in 2 hours on a single GPU, whereas BlockFusion requires 3 hours for only 7 rooms.

## Relation to Prior Work

LT3SD extends the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) from 2D images and 3D objects to large-scale 3D scenes by introducing a latent tree representation that factorizes each resolution level into a TUDF geometry volume and a latent feature volume. It departs from prior 3D scene diffusion methods (BlockFusion, NeuralFieldLDM, XCube) that operate on bounded, fixed-size scenes by adopting patch-based training and inference, enabling arbitrary-sized and effectively infinite scene synthesis. The coarse-to-fine generation strategy borrows the Stable Inpainting scheme from LDMs for the coarsest level and adapts MultiDiffusion's parallel denoising fusion for higher-resolution refinement, combining these 2D techniques into a novel 3D hierarchical pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LT3SD demonstrates that high-quality, large-scale 3D scene generation is feasible with a single-GPU pipeline (170 rooms in ~2 hours), which is practically significant for content creation pipelines that previously required hours of per-scene optimization or produced only single-room outputs. The latent tree factorization offers a reusable architectural principle for hierarchical 3D generation beyond scenes. Key limitations include unconditional generation only (no text or layout conditioning), training exclusively on indoor 3D-FRONT data, and reliance on voxel-based TUDF representations that may struggle with very large outdoor extents. Open questions include whether the approach scales to city-scale outdoor scenes, how it integrates with semantic or text conditioning, and whether the patch-based strategy generalizes to non-grid-aligned or open-world topologies.
