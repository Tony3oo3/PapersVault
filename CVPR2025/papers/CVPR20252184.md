## Problem

Existing autoregressive mesh generation methods (MeshAnything, MeshAnythingV2, EdgeRunner) are limited to 800–4,000 triangular faces due to long token sequences and the quadratic complexity of Transformers, and they commonly produce flipped normals and topological artifacts. The paper asks whether a more efficient sequencing strategy can push face-count capacity higher while simultaneously enforcing consistent normal orientation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a point cloud of 8,192 surface points as conditioning, which is encoded via a cross-attention layer with 2,048 query embeddings into a latent code prepended to the decoder. It outputs a 3D triangular mesh with up to 5,500 faces at 7-bit coordinate discretization (128 classes per axis), where each face is represented by only two sequence tokens (a directed edge input and a predicted opposite vertex), achieving ~22% compression over the 9-token-per-face naive scheme. The mesh is assembled from all (v₁, v₂, v₃) triples collected during the autoregressive traversal, with [STOP] and [EOS] tokens delimiting boundaries and component/sequence ends.

## Method

TreeMeshGPT replaces conventional next-token prediction with an Autoregressive Tree Sequencing scheme: a half-edge data structure is traversed via depth-first search using a dynamic stack, so the next input to the 24-layer Transformer decoder (16 heads, 1024 hidden dim) is always the top edge on the stack rather than the previous output. At each step the decoder predicts either a new opposite vertex (forming a triangle) or a [STOP] label, and newly created counter-clockwise edges are pushed onto the stack, ensuring the mesh grows locally from the last generated face. Vertex coordinates are predicted with hierarchical MLP heads that maintain sequential x→y→z dependency, and the loss is a sum of cross-entropies over the three discretized axes plus [STOP]/[EOS] classes. Point cloud conditioning is injected by prepending the 2,048-token latent code to the [SOS] token with full self-attention, while the autoregressive portion uses a causal mask implemented via PyTorch FlexAttention. The DFS traversal (ablated against BFS) provides stronger local dependencies that reduce training perplexity, and the adjacency-based sequencing inherently enforces consistent face winding, minimizing flipped normals.

## Results

On the Objaverse validation set (200 samples), TreeMeshGPT achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0070 and Normal Consistency of 0.798, substantially outperforming MeshAnything (CD=0.0115, NC=0.223) and MeshAnythingV2 (CD=0.0102, NC=0.167). On the GSO real-world scan dataset, TreeMeshGPT again leads with CD=0.0077 and NC=0.842 versus MeshAnything (CD=0.0105, NC=0.453) and MeshAnythingV2 (CD=0.0116, NC=0.327). The most striking improvement is in Normal Consistency, where TreeMeshGPT's half-edge-based tree sequencing enforces consistent face orientation, raising NC by over 3× compared to baselines that suffer from flipped normals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TreeMeshGPT outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across both evaluation datasets. Against MeshAnything [3], it reduces Chamfer Distance by ~39% (0.0070 vs 0.0115 on Objaverse) and improves Normal Consistency by 3.6× (0.798 vs 0.223), indicating far fewer flipped normals. Against MeshAnythingV2 [4], the CD improvement is ~31% (0.0070 vs 0.0102) and NC improves by 4.8× (0.798 vs 0.167). In the controlled tokenization experiment (Table 2), TreeMeshGPT's 2-token-per-face representation achieves CD=0.0100 and NC=0.734, beating the naive 9-token baseline (CD=0.0376, NC=0.639), the VQ-VAE 6-token baseline (CD=0.0352, NC=0.673), and the AMT adjacency-based tokenizer (CD=0.0327, NC=−0.069) while using the shortest sequence length (2N_f + 2N_c). On the GSO real-world scan dataset, TreeMeshGPT maintains its lead (CD=0.0077, NC=0.842) over both MeshAnything (CD=0.0105, NC=0.453) and MeshAnythingV2 (CD=0.0116, NC=0.327), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

TreeMeshGPT extends the autoregressive Transformer pipeline for artistic mesh generation established by MeshGPT and MeshAnything, and directly improves upon the adjacency-based tokenization of MeshAnything V2 and EdgeRunner. The key conceptual departure is replacing conventional next-token prediction with an Autoregressive Tree Sequencing scheme that uses a half-edge data structure and DFS traversal over a dynamically growing tree, so the model only makes localized predictions from the most recently generated face. This yields a 2-token-per-face representation (vs. 9 for naive tokenization or ~4 for adjacency-based methods), roughly doubling the compression of prior adjacency approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a meaningful capacity jump from 4,000 to 5,500 triangular faces under strong point-cloud conditioning, plus a large improvement in normal-orientation consistency (NC 0.798 vs. 0.17–0.22 for baselines), which reduces a common failure mode in downstream editing and rendering pipelines. The controlled tokenization experiment isolates the sequencing inductive bias and shows a ~3× Chamfer Distance improvement over the best prior tokenizer, suggesting the tree structure itself—rather than just shorter sequences—drives quality gains. Open questions remain: success rate still degrades with sequence length, optimal mesh topology is not explicitly enforced, and whether the DFS-based local dependency assumption holds for highly non-manifold or multi-component scenes at even higher face counts is untested.
