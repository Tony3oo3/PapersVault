## Problem

Prompt-based continual learning methods store knowledge in a prompt pool but treat all learned features uniformly, simultaneously encoding discriminative and non-discriminative information. In few-shot class-incremental settings, this conflation destabilizes training: the model overfits to noise in limited samples while failing to generalize to novel classes, worsening the plasticity-stability dilemma.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250456/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images and outputs a class label via a cosine classifier (replaced by class prototypes at inference). The frozen ViT-B/16 backbone processes the image, while learnable prompt tokens (length 3–5) are prepended to selected transformer layer inputs and discarded from outputs. During incremental sessions, ND-Prompt generates n perturbed copies of each training sample via spherical Gaussian noise on similarity scores, effectively multiplying the small N-way K-shot training set.

## Method

SEC-Prompt decomposes prompt knowledge into two complementary streams: a Discriminative Prompt (D-Prompt) that applies softmax-normalized attention weights to amplify class-specific features, and a Non-Discriminative Prompt (ND-Prompt) that uses reverse attention with ReLU truncation to capture task-agnostic, style-like features. An Adaptive Hierarchical Query replaces the fixed final-layer [class] token with the [class] token from each layer's input, allowing prompts at different depths to adapt to evolving feature abstractions. A Prompt Clustering Loss minimizes intra-class distance among D-Prompt outputs to prevent noise contamination, while ND-Prompt-based data augmentation injects spherical Gaussian perturbations into similarity scores to generate diverse training samples without altering discriminative content. During incremental sessions, D-Prompt parameters are frozen and expanded with new components, while ND-Prompt remains fixed after the base session, balancing stability and plasticity.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (10-way 5-shot, 10 incremental sessions), SEC-Prompt achieves a final-session accuracy of 74.5%, a performance dropping rate (PD) of 8.3%, and a Harmonic Accuracy (HAcc) of 74.2%, surpassing the strongest baseline ASP by 4.8% in final accuracy, 5.2% in PD, and 7.2% in HAcc. On CUB200, SEC-Prompt reaches 84.8% final accuracy with PD=2.6% and HAcc=84.7%; on CIFAR100, it achieves 87.5% final accuracy with PD=4.5% and HAcc=87.1%. The frozen variant (Ours-F), which introduces no new trainable parameters during incremental sessions, still outperforms all baselines except SEC-Prompt itself, confirming strong generalization from the base session.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEC-Prompt outperforms all six baselines on every incremental session across all three datasets, with the largest margins on ImageNet-R. Against the strongest FSCIL baseline ASP [25], SEC-Prompt gains +4.8% final accuracy, −5.2 PD, and +7.2 HAcc on ImageNet-R; +1.3% final accuracy, −1.0 PD, and +1.3 HAcc on CUB200; and +0.8% final accuracy, −1.0 PD, and +1.8 HAcc on CIFAR100. Among prompt-based CIL methods, Coda-Prompt [34] (the direct architectural predecessor) is surpassed by +8.1% final accuracy and +12.5 HAcc on ImageNet-R, while L2P [44] and DualP [43] trail by even larger margins (17.7% and 12.0% final accuracy, respectively). The non-prompt FSCIL methods TEEN [42] and PriViLege [30] suffer severe forgetting (PD 29.7% and 13.5% on ImageNet-R) due to backbone fine-tuning, whereas SEC-Prompt's frozen-backbone design keeps PD at 8.3%. Notably, even SEC-Prompt-F (no new parameters in incremental sessions) beats all baselines, indicating that the complementary D-Prompt/ND-Prompt decomposition learned in the base session transfers effectively to novel classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEC-Prompt extends the prompt-pool architecture established by L2P and refined by Coda-Prompt, which uses a key-query mechanism to select task-specific prompts from a frozen ViT backbone. The key departure is the explicit decomposition of prompt knowledge into discriminative (D-Prompt, learned via softmax attention) and non-discriminative (ND-Prompt, learned via ReLU-gated reverse attention) components, motivated by the observation that Coda-Prompt's unified attention simultaneously amplifies and suppresses features in a way that destabilizes few-shot incremental training. The Adaptive Hierarchical Query further departs from prior work by using per-layer [class] tokens rather than the fixed final-layer output, and the Prompt Clustering Loss plus ND-Prompt-based data augmentation are new training strategies absent from existing prompt-based FSCIL methods like ASP and PriViLege.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean conceptual resolution to the plasticity-stability dilemma in FSCIL: by explicitly modeling non-discriminative features (which prior prompt-based methods implicitly treat as noise) and using them for data augmentation, the framework reduces overfitting on few-shot new classes while the frozen ND-Prompt preserves base-class knowledge. The 5.2% PD and 7.2% HAcc gains over the prior SOTA (ASP) on ImageNet-R, achieved with fewer trainable parameters, make this a practical option for rehearsal-free incremental deployment. However, the authors note that the quality of features learned from few-shot samples remains suboptimal (larger prompt pools actually increase forgetting), and the approach is validated only on ViT-B/16 backbones with specific FSCIL protocols, leaving open whether the discriminative/non-discriminative decomposition generalizes to other architectures or more extreme few-shot regimes.
