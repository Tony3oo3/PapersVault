## Problem

Existing motion representations for human action analysis are inadequate: optical flow captures all pixel motion indiscriminately (including irrelevant background dynamics) and is typically trained on synthetic data lacking real biological entities, while human pose representations lack body shape information critical for tasks like gait recognition. This paper addresses the need for a precise human-centric motion representation that preserves both motion and shape while filtering background movement, learned directly from real-world video without ground-truth flow supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250912/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive video frames (resized to 512×384, 16 frames per sequence for most datasets, 8 for Diving48) along with auxiliary signals: 17-joint 2D pose keypoints (from ED-Pose) and semantic segmentation masks (from U2-Net) used to derive human boundaries via Canny edge detection. It produces two flow maps: a world flow M_w (per-pixel displacement relative to the environment) and a local flow M_l (per-pixel displacement relative to the subject), where M_l is computed as M_w minus a global subject motion vector v_s rather than estimated by a separate network, keeping inference at 34 fps.

## Method

H-MoRe uses a RAFT-small backbone augmented with two self-attention blocks to estimate world flow, trained self-supervisedly via a joint constraint learning framework with two fidelity terms. The skeleton constraint F matches each body point to its nearest visible skeleton keypoint and enforces angular and intensity consistency between the estimated flow vector and the skeleton offset, bounding feasible body motion. The boundary constraint G aligns flow-map edges with human silhouette boundaries using a multi-scale patch-centroid approximation of Chamfer distance, preserving shape details at hands, feet, and head. Local flow is derived via Galilean-style vector decomposition: a lightweight cross-attention network Ψ (depth 4) estimates the subject's overall motion v_s from M_w and input frames, then M_l = M_w − v_s; the skeleton constraint is adapted for M_l by first aligning the two skeleton maps (via homography or head-based alignment) to convert environment-relative offsets into subject-relative ones. No ground-truth flow is required at any point, and the entire pipeline is trained on real-world sequences from CASIA-B, Diving48, and UTD-MHAD.

## Results

On gait recognition (CASIA-B, GaitBase), H-MoRe achieves 87.66% CL@R1, outperforming the best optical flow baseline (VideoFlow, 86.12%) and the no-flow baseline (78.02%) by 9.64 points. On action recognition (Diving48), H-MoRe reaches 72.99% Acc@1 and 97.62% Acc@5, surpassing the best flow baseline (GMFlow, 71.74% Acc@1) by 1.25 points. On video generation (UTD-MHAD), H-MoRe achieves the lowest FVD of 108.38 (vs. RAFT's 118.13) and highest SSIM of 0.9574, while maintaining high inference efficiency at 34 fps with only 5.57M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-MoRe outperforms all seven optical flow baselines across every task and metric. In gait recognition (GaitBase, CL@R1), H-MoRe (87.66%) beats the closest competitor VideoFlow (86.12%) by 1.54 points and RAFT (80.52%) by 7.14 points; with the lighter GaitSet model, the margin over GMFlow (72.75%) widens to 12.50 points. In action recognition (Diving48, Acc@1), H-MoRe (72.99%) edges out GMFlow (71.74%) by 1.25 points and RAFT (65.90%) by 7.09 points. In video generation (UTD-MHAD), H-MoRe achieves the best FVD (108.38) and SSIM (0.9574), improving over RAFT by 5.74% in FVD. Against pose-based representations (Table 5), H-MoRe surpasses 2D Pose/AlphaPose (82.17% CL@R1) and 3D Pose/MotionBERT (84.05% CL@R1) by 5.49 and 3.61 points respectively, while using far fewer parameters (~5.7M vs. ~25M and ~41M). In the subject-overlap robustness experiment (Fig. 9), H-MoRe degrades more slowly than both RAFT and MHOF [40] as overlap increases beyond 20%, confirming its advantage in multi-person scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

H-MoRe departs from the dominant paradigm of fully-supervised optical flow estimation (RAFT, FlowFormer, GMFlow) that relies on EPE loss over synthetic datasets, instead learning from real-world video via a self-supervised joint constraint framework combining skeleton and boundary constraints. It also moves beyond human pose representations (2D/3D skeletal keypoints) that discard body shape information, by encoding motion in a dense matrix format that preserves both. The conceptual bridge to kinematics—using Galilean transformations to derive subject-relative local flow from environment-relative world flow via a lightweight network rather than a second full flow estimator—is the paper's most distinctive departure from prior motion representation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single motion representation that simultaneously captures precise human motion, body shape, and dual reference-frame information (world and local), H-MoRe offers a drop-in upgrade for a range of downstream tasks (gait recognition, action recognition, video generation) without requiring task-specific redesign. The self-supervised training from real data eliminates the synthetic-to-real gap that plagues supervised flow methods, and the 34 fps inference rate makes it viable for real-time deployment. Key limitations remain: the method is validated only in 2D with a limited number of subjects, and the reliance on external pose and segmentation models introduces a dependency chain that could propagate errors. Extending to 3D and dense multi-subject scenarios is the natural next step the authors identify.
