## Problem

Open-vocabulary attribute detection requires identifying arbitrary natural-language attributes in images, but existing approaches either depend on ambiguous, labor-intensive annotations or are training-based and biased toward their specific training sets. No training-free, cache-based strategy existed for this task, and naive caching is infeasible because the output space comprises all attribute-object combinations (e.g., 117! for OVAD).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251150/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a cropped RGB image (bounding box provided in the primary box-given setting), a list of n target attributes in natural language, and a list of m target objects, COMCA outputs a score in [-1, 1] per attribute indicating presence or absence. No labeled training data is required; the only configuration inputs are the attribute and object lists, which automatically trigger cache regeneration. In the box-free variant, a YOLOv11M detector supplies the bounding boxes.

## Method

COMCA builds an auxiliary image cache using two compositional principles to keep storage at K images per attribute while remaining semantically rich. First, attribute-object compatibility is estimated by multiplying co-occurrence counts from CC12M captions with LLM (GPT-3.5 Turbo) compatibility scores, yielding a distribution over objects for each attribute from which cache images are retrieved via CLIP-based text-to-image search. Second, each cache image receives soft multi-attribute labels computed as normalized VLM similarity scores between the image embedding and every target attribute text embedding, correcting for the fact that a single image exhibits multiple attributes simultaneously. At inference, the cache-based prediction weights each cache element's contribution by its soft label for the queried attribute times its visual similarity to the input, and this score is blended with the standard CLIP cosine-similarity score via a softmax-normalized combination, requiring no gradient updates or task-specific training.

## Results

On OVAD (box-given), COMCA achieves 27.4 mAP, outperforming the best zero-shot baseline (CLIP ViT-L/14, 18.3 mAP) by +9.1 and the best cache-based baseline (SuS-X, 20.2 mAP) by +7.2. On VAW, COMCA scores 58.1 mAP, surpassing the best zero-shot (CLIP ViT-L/14, 51.0 mAP) by +7.1 and TIP-Adapter (57.5 mAP) by +0.6. COMCA also outperforms the training-based LOWA (18.7 mAP on OVAD, 42.6 mAP on VAW) despite requiring no training data, and remains competitive with OvarNet (28.6 mAP on OVAD) and ArtVLM (71.9 mAP on VAW).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMCA outperforms all training-free baselines on both OVAD and VAW. Against cache-based methods, COMCA beats TIP-Adapter by +10.7 mAP on OVAD and +0.6 on VAW, and SuS-X by +7.2 on OVAD and +27.9 on VAW. The TIP-Adapter+IAP combination (which naively conditions on object co-occurrences) is the weakest cache-based method, trailing COMCA by +12.3 mAP on OVAD and +30.4 on VAW, confirming that simple attribute-object co-occurrence is insufficient. Among training-based competitors, COMCA surpasses LOWA (trained on 1.33M samples) on both benchmarks (27.4 vs 18.7 on OVAD; 58.1 vs 42.6 on VAW) without any training. COMCA is competitive with OvarNet on OVAD (27.4 vs 28.6 mAP, a gap of only 1.2) but trails ArtVLM on VAW (58.1 vs 71.9 mAP), indicating that generative VLM-based approaches still hold an advantage on the larger, sparser VAW benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMCA extends the caching paradigm introduced by TIP-Adapter and SuS-X for image classification to the substantially harder setting of open-vocabulary attribute detection, where the output space is combinatorial (all attribute-object combinations). The key conceptual departure is making the cache compositional: rather than storing one-hot-labeled examples per class, COMCA samples cache entries according to attribute-object compatibility priors (from web-scale databases and LLMs) and assigns soft multi-attribute labels via VLM similarity scores. This positions COMCA as the first training-free, cache-based method for OVAD, in contrast to prior OVAD approaches (OVAD, OvarNet, LOWA, ArtVLM) that all require large-scale annotated training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COMCA demonstrates that a carefully designed training-free pipeline can close most of the gap to training-based OVAD methods (27.4 vs 28.6 mAP on OVAD; 58.1 vs 71.9 on VAW) while offering a practical advantage: no sensitivity to training-set bias, as evidenced by its stable cross-dataset performance where training-based methods degrade significantly. The model-agnostic nature (consistent +7–9 mAP gains across CLIP, SigLIP, CoCa, BLIP, X-VLM) makes it a broadly applicable adapter. Open questions include performance in the box-free setting (where gains shrink), reliance on external web-scale databases and LLMs for cache construction, and whether the approach extends to scenarios where neither objects nor attributes are pre-specified.
