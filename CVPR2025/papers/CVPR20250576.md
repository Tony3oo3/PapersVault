## Problem

Existing Medical VQA pipelines lack a unified approach to modality alignment that simultaneously handles global and local levels, inter- and intra-modality pairs, and multi-view imaging. Hard negatives—abundant in medical data due to similar disease presentations and standardized reporting—are either ignored (one-hot labels) or only partially addressed (soft labels that still assign high similarity to hard negatives without explicit discrimination). Additionally, knowledge fusion methods that inject comprehensive medical terminology (e.g., UMLS) risk introducing irrelevant information for a specific imaging modality or organ.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model consumes multi-view medical images (two views per study, sampled or augmented) paired with radiology reports from MIMIC-CXR, ROCO, and MedICaT. During fine-tuning, it takes a single medical image and a text question, and produces an open-form answer generated auto-regressively (rather than selecting from a fixed answer list). The answer vocabulary extracted from the target VQA dataset serves as structured prior knowledge injected into the decoder.

## Method

AMiF trains in two pre-training stages followed by fine-tuning. Stage 1 performs global-level alignment using contrastive learning with soft labels (generated by BiomedCLIP) across inter-modality (image-text) and intra-modality (multi-view image-image, text-text) pairs, minimizing KL-divergence between the model's similarity distributions and the soft-label targets. Stage 2 adds local-level alignment via Optimal Transport (IPOT) over token embeddings after a Modality Co-Attention Module fuses cross-modal information, plus a hard negative mining loss that samples high-similarity negative pairs and enforces their discrimination from positives through a binary classification head on concatenated representations. For fine-tuning, a Gated Cross-Attention Module cross-attends the image-question joint representation against the answer vocabulary, then a sigmoid gate selectively blends the knowledge-fused representation with the original, and an auto-regressive decoder generates the final answer.

## Results

AMiF achieves the best Overall accuracy across all four Med-VQA benchmarks: 80.49% on VQA-RAD, 86.71% on SLAKE, 65.33% on PathVQA, and 83.20% on VQA-2019. On the more challenging Open-set questions, AMiF also leads on every dataset (72.00% VQA-RAD, 84.84% SLAKE, 40.33% PathVQA). Compared to BiomedCLIP, the pre-trained model used to generate soft labels, AMiF improves Overall accuracy by 5.29% on VQA-RAD and 1.31% on SLAKE. The total trainable model size is 326M parameters, with the Gated Cross-Attention Module contributing 14.8M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMiF achieves the best Overall accuracy on all four benchmarks, outperforming the strongest prior methods by 1.11–3.48% depending on the dataset. On VQA-RAD, AMiF (80.49%) surpasses MUMC (79.20%) by 1.29%, LaPA (79.38%) by 1.11%, and RAMM (78.27%) by 2.22%. On SLAKE, AMiF (86.71%) leads RAMM (86.05%) by 0.66%, BiomedCLIP (85.40%) by 1.31%, and LaPA (84.73%) by 1.98%. On PathVQA, AMiF (65.33%) edges out MUMC (65.10%) by 0.23% and PeFoMed (63.60%) by 1.73%. On VQA-2019, AMiF (83.20%) beats RAMM (82.13%) by 1.07% and LaPA (81.60%) by 1.60%. Notably, AMiF does not win on every Closed-set sub-metric: M2I2 achieves 91.10% on SLAKE Closed and PeFoMed achieves 91.30% on PathVQA Closed, both slightly above AMiF's 90.23% and 90.18% respectively, though AMiF still leads on the Overall scores for those datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMiF extends the CLIP-style contrastive learning paradigm used in medical VLP (BiomedCLIP, PubMedCLIP, MUMC) by unifying global-level alignment across both inter-modality (image-text) and intra-modality (image-image, text-text) axes, and by introducing multi-view image alignment that prior works like LIMITR only compared but did not explicitly align. For local-level alignment, the paper is the first to apply Optimal Transport theory (IPOT) from the natural-domain UNITER/ViLT line to medical VLP, replacing the contrastive patch-token matching used by GLoRIA, LoVT, and MGCA. The hard negative mining departs from soft-label-only approaches (MedCLIP, KoBo, MUMC) by adding an explicit classification head that enforces discrimination between hard negatives and positives, and the Gated Cross-Attention Module for knowledge fusion replaces the full knowledge-graph integration used by LaPA with a more selective, task-specific answer-vocabulary mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses three concrete and previously fragmented gaps in Med-VQA—heterogeneous modality alignment, hard negative handling, and indiscriminate knowledge fusion—within a single coherent two-stage framework, achieving consistent SOTA across four benchmarks (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA, VQA-2019). A notable limitation is the reliance on BiomedCLIP as the soft-label teacher, creating a dependency on an external pre-trained model whose quality bounds the alignment signal; the multi-view augmentation via random data augmentation for non-multi-view datasets (ROCO, MedICaT) is also a practical workaround rather than a principled solution. Open questions include whether the OT-based local alignment scales efficiently to larger pre-training corpora, whether the hard negative mining generalizes to other medical imaging modalities beyond radiology and pathology, and whether the gated knowledge fusion can be extended to richer structured knowledge sources beyond flat answer vocabularies.
