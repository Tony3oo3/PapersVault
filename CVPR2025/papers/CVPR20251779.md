## Problem

Existing self-supervised line segment detection methods (HAWPv3, SOLD2, DeepLSD) are constrained to small training scales of a few thousand images because their pseudo-labeling pipelines—relying on homographic adaptation or classical LSD's local alignment—suffer from low recall or inherited locality issues, preventing effective large-scale training. This limits generalization to natural images in the wild, where the classical non-deep LSD often still achieves higher recall than deep self-supervised approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251779/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and produces a dense 4-component HAT field (perpendicular distance d, direction θ, and endpoint angles α, β) via a DPT head, along with a 2D heatmap of junction locations. The dense field is sparsely decoded into a set of line segments by binding each pixel's predicted endpoints to the nearest learned junction (within a 10-pixel threshold), yielding a parsimonious vectorized boundary representation. The ViT-Base backbone processes the image at standard resolution, and the entire pipeline is designed for zero-shot inference on arbitrary natural images.

## Method

ScaleLSD streamlines the HAWPv3 architecture with two key design changes: (1) a HAT-induced proposal verification that replaces LOI-based confidence scoring with a geometrically meaningful support-degree count—measuring how many pixels in the dense HAT field agree on the same junction-pair index, which is more robust under noisy pseudo labels; and (2) an LSD-Rectifier for pseudo-label generation that uses the classical LSD's level-line field to replace the θ component of the network's HAT prediction, eliminating the need for computationally expensive homographic adaptation (which costs ~70× more time and memory per batch). The model uses a ViT-Base backbone with a DPT head, trained via a synthetic-to-real curriculum: 10 epochs on 16k synthetic images, then 6 epochs on over 10M unlabeled SA1B images with pseudo labels generated by the LSD-Rectifier, achieving the first deep LSD model to outperform the classical LSD across all evaluated downstream tasks.

## Results

ScaleLSD trained on 10M SA1B images achieves the best zero-shot repeatability across all four out-of-domain datasets: Rep-5(S) of 0.725 on YorkUrban, 0.367 on HPatches, 0.337 on RDNIM, and 0.666 on COCO val-2017, while also detecting the most line segments (708/image on YorkUrban vs. 591 for LSD and 225 for HAWPv3). On vanishing point estimation, ScaleLSD@SA1B attains the lowest VP Error (1.55 on YUD+, 3.18 on NYU-VP) and highest AUC (87.1 and 70.4 respectively). For 3D line reconstruction on DTU, ScaleLSD achieves the best accuracy (ACC-L = 0.709) while reconstructing 2,445 lines per scene, outperforming DeepLSD (ACC-L = 1.090) and LSD (ACC-L = 0.848).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaleLSD@SA1B outperforms all five baselines on nearly every metric across all four repeatability datasets. Against the classical LSD [40], ScaleLSD improves Rep-5(S) by 73% on YorkUrban (0.725 vs. 0.419) and 33% on HPatches (0.367 vs. 0.275), while detecting 20% more line segments. Compared to HAWPv3 [51], the closest deep competitor, ScaleLSD achieves higher repeatability on all datasets (e.g., 0.725 vs. 0.711 on YorkUrban, 0.666 vs. 0.644 on COCO) and detects 3× more lines (708 vs. 225 on YorkUrban), though HAWPv3 retains a slight edge on RDNIM localization error (2.200 vs. 2.407). Against DeepLSD [31], which inherits the locality constraint of classical LSD, ScaleLSD wins on all four datasets in repeatability and detects substantially more segments. On vanishing point estimation (Table 2), ScaleLSD@SA1B achieves the best VP Error on both YUD+ (1.55°) and NYU-VP (3.18°), surpassing DeepLSD (1.63° and 3.24°) and HAWPv3 (1.76° and 3.35°). In 3D line reconstruction on DTU (Table 3), ScaleLSD attains the lowest ACC-L (0.709) while reconstructing the most 3D lines (2,445), outperforming LSD (0.848 ACC, 1,824 lines) and DeepLSD (1.090 ACC, 1,888 lines).

## Relation to Prior Work

ScaleLSD streamlines the HAWPv3 [51] architecture (HAT field + junction learning) by replacing the LOI-based proposal verification with a geometrically-grounded HAT-induced verification, and by substituting the computationally expensive homographic adaptation pseudo-labeling (used in SOLD2 [30] and HAWPv3 [51]) with a lightweight LSD-Rectifier that borrows only the direction field from the classical LSD [40]. Unlike DeepLSD [31], which integrates the classical LSD's local alignment as the core detection mechanism, ScaleLSD repurposes LSD solely as an auxiliary rectifier for the HAT field's θ component, preserving the holistic field representation while fixing its bootstrapping errors. The method further departs from prior SSL LSD work by scaling training from ~20k images to 10M (SA1B) with a ViT-Base + DPT backbone, arguing that Transformer expressivity is critical for ingesting large-scale data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first deep LSD to outperform the classical LSD [40] across all evaluated aspects (repeatability, VP estimation, line matching, 3D reconstruction), resolving a long-standing gap where deep methods could not match the recall of the 2010 handcrafted detector. The practical implication is significant: a single scalable, self-supervised model can now serve as a drop-in replacement for classical LSD in 3D vision pipelines without requiring per-domain annotation. Open questions include whether the method can explicitly model curves (it currently approximates them as polylines), whether further scaling beyond 10M images yields diminishing returns, and whether the HAT-induced verification generalizes to other structured detection tasks beyond line segments.
