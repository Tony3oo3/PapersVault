## Problem

Existing text-guided motion editing methods depend on fixed, pre-collected triplets of original motion, edited motion, and instruction, which severely limits generalization to novel motion-instruction combinations and diverse editing scenarios. Additionally, current approaches require explicit body-part specifications as auxiliary input and struggle to produce smooth spatial-temporal transitions in edited motions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251826/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original 3D human motion sequence (represented as 28 SMPL-X keypoints per frame, covering 22 primary joints plus finger and head joints) and a free-form natural language editing instruction as the sole inputs. It outputs a full edited motion sequence in the same keypoint format, convertible to SMPL-X parameters via forward kinematics. Notably, no body-part masks, temporal boundaries, or LLM-generated specifications are required—the model autonomously interprets which parts and time segments to modify from the text alone.

## Method

MotionReFit is an auto-regressive conditional diffusion model that generates edited motion in overlapping 16-frame sliding windows, with each segment conditioned on the original motion, the text instruction (via CLIP), two preceding frames for continuity, and a sinusoidal progress indicator. Training is augmented by MotionCutMix, which dynamically synthesizes new triplets by spatially blending body parts from a large unannotated motion database using soft-mask SLERP interpolation, expanding N annotated pairs into N×N_L composited examples. To counteract the body-part incoordination introduced by composited training data, a motion discriminator (body part coordinator) is trained to distinguish natural from composited segments and applied as classifier guidance during the final 20 denoising steps, steering the generated motion toward coherent coordination patterns. Classifier-free guidance on the text instruction further strengthens instruction adherence during sampling.

## Results

On the STANCE body part replacement task, MotionReFit achieves the best FID (0.20 vs. 0.44 for MDM-BP and 0.52 for TMED) and the best Edited-to-Target retrieval (R@1=61.4%, AvgR=4.65), indicating the most faithful execution of the requested edit while preserving the original context. On style transfer, the advantage is even more pronounced: MotionReFit reaches FID 0.14 and E2T R@1 of 63.8%, compared to TMED's FID 1.54 and E2T R@1 of 24.8%. On the MotionFix benchmark (without MotionCutMix), the auto-regressive architecture alone yields E2S R@1 of 83.5% and E2T R@1 of 66.3%, surpassing TMED (71.8% / 62.9%) and MDM-BP (61.3% / 39.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionReFit outperforms both baselines on every primary metric across all three evaluation settings. Against TMED [7], the full model reduces FID from 0.52 to 0.20 (body part replacement) and from 1.54 to 0.14 (style transfer), while improving E2T R@1 by ~19 and ~39 percentage points respectively, showing substantially better instruction adherence. Against MDM-BP [56], MotionReFit achieves lower FID (0.20 vs. 0.44; 0.14 vs. 0.39) and far superior E2T retrieval (R@1 61.4% vs. 39.1% for BP; 63.8% vs. 38.9% for ST), indicating that MDM-BP's part-level inpainting fails to produce coherent whole-body edits. On MotionFix, even without MotionCutMix, the auto-regressive architecture alone beats TMED by 11.7 points on E2S R@1 (83.5% vs. 71.8%) and 3.4 points on E2T R@1 (66.3% vs. 62.9%), and dominates MDM-BP by 22.2 and 27.2 points on the same metrics. Notably, adding MotionCutMix to TMED (TMED w/ MCM) narrows the gap on E2T (R@1 50.6% vs. 61.4% for BP) but does not close the FID gap, confirming that the architectural and coordinator contributions are complementary to the data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM diffusion framework for motion generation but departs significantly from prior text-guided motion editing methods (TMED, MDM-BP, FineMoGen) in three ways: it replaces fixed pre-collected editing triplets with MotionCutMix, a CutMix-inspired online augmentation that composites body parts from unannotated motion databases; it adopts an auto-regressive segment-by-segment diffusion architecture rather than single-shot generation, enabling temporal editing with smooth transitions; and it introduces a discriminator-based body part coordinator to counteract incoordination artifacts from composited training data. The approach also removes the dependency on LLMs for instruction interpretation and explicit body-part specification that characterizes SINC and FineMoGen, operating purely from natural language and the original motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data bottleneck in motion editing—reliance on expensive annotated triplets—is a practical barrier that limits deployment, and MotionCutMix offers a concrete, generalizable solution that the authors validate transfers to other architectures (TMED). The STANCE dataset and the finding that minimal annotated data suffices when augmented with MotionCutMix lower the entry cost for new editing scenarios. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in long-term temporal dependency modeling and spatial awareness for position-dependent instructions, and the auto-regressive windowing (16 frames) constrains global coherence. Open questions include whether the body part coordinator generalizes to multi-character or object-interaction editing, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences without accumulating drift.
