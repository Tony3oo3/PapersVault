## Problem

Existing methods for learning 3D physics from dynamic videos either rely on PINN losses that degrade accuracy in boundary regions or require object priors such as masks and type labels. This paper addresses the gap by learning physically meaningful 3D velocity fields from multi-view videos alone, without any PINN constraints or object-specific assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251489/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB video sequences with known camera poses and intrinsics (e.g., 20 GoPro views at 960×540 in the new FreeGave-GoPro dataset) and produces a canonical 3D Gaussian scene representation augmented with per-Gaussian latent physics codes and divergence-free velocity fields. From these, it renders future frames beyond the training time window and yields unsupervised motion segmentation masks by clustering the learned physics codes via K-means, all without any human-provided labels or object masks.

## Method

FreeGave extends 3D Gaussian Splatting by treating each Gaussian kernel as a rigid particle and learning a shared latent physics code z via an MLP on its canonical position. The key architectural insight is a divergence-free velocity parametrization: the per-Gaussian velocity v(p_t, t) is decomposed into six basic components V_t (three linear, three angular) multiplied by a position-dependent basis matrix B(p_t), guaranteeing the divergence-free property by construction rather than through PINN losses. The velocity components are computed via a bottleneck decomposition V_t = f_neck(z) · f_weight(t), where f_neck decodes the physics code into K motion patterns and f_weight generates timestamp-specific selection weights. An auxiliary deformation field f_deform(p_0, t, z) provides position, rotation, and scale displacements to aid optimization convergence, and an interleaved mid-point integration scheme transports Gaussians from a sampled time t' to target time t for rendering supervision via standard 3DGS photometric losses.

## Results

On the future frame extrapolation task (the primary evaluation), FreeGave achieves PSNR 31.99 / SSIM 0.990 / LPIPS 0.007 on the Dynamic Object Dataset, 35.02 / 0.966 / 0.051 on the Dynamic Indoor Scene Dataset, and 26.66 / 0.956 / 0.030 on the ParticleNeRF Dataset, outperforming the strongest baseline DefGS_nvfi by 3.2, 3.9, and 3.9 dB PSNR respectively. On the newly collected FreeGave-GoPro real-world dataset, FreeGave reaches 28.09 PSNR for extrapolation, surpassing DefGS_nvfi (26.43) and NVFi (22.75). For unsupervised motion segmentation, FreeGave achieves 99.75 AP and 80.52 mIoU, far exceeding the fully-supervised Mask2Former (65.37 AP, 64.42 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeGave outperforms all six baselines on future frame extrapolation across every dataset. Against the closest NeRF-based physics method NVFi [30], FreeGave gains 4.4 dB PSNR on Dynamic Object (31.99 vs 27.59) and 5.3 dB on Dynamic Indoor (35.02 vs 29.75), with a particularly large LPIPS gap (0.007 vs 0.036) indicating much sharper predictions. Against the strongest 3DGS-based baseline DefGS_nvfi [75+30], FreeGave still leads by 3.2–3.9 dB PSNR, confirming that the divergence-free parametrization and physics code design are more effective than simply grafting NVFi's PINN-based velocity onto DefGS. The NeRF-only baselines (T-NeRF, D-NeRF, NSFF, TiNeuVox) all fall 5–12 dB behind FreeGave on extrapolation, reflecting their lack of explicit physics modeling. On the real-world FreeGave-GoPro dataset, DefGS (without physics) drops to 21.34 PSNR while FreeGave maintains 28.09, a 6.75 dB gap that highlights the importance of learned physics for challenging real-world dynamics. For motion segmentation, FreeGave's unsupervised physics-code grouping (99.75 AP, 80.52 mIoU) surpasses even the fully-supervised Mask2Former [11] (65.37 AP, 64.42 mIoU), demonstrating that the learned physics codes implicitly encode object identity without any segmentation labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation to dynamic scenes by treating each Gaussian kernel as a rigid particle with a learned latent physics code, building directly on the velocity-field formulation of NVFi (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023) and the deformation architecture of DefGS (Yang et al., CVPR 2024). The key departure from NVFi is replacing inefficient PINN losses with a divergence-free velocity parametrization that decomposes motion into linear and angular components via a basis matrix, and from DefGS is the introduction of per-Gaussian physics codes that drive both velocity and deformation. The bottleneck-vector decomposition of velocity components (a small MLP on the physics code multiplied by a time-conditioned weight matrix) is a novel architectural choice that constrains the velocity field to a small set of reusable motion patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the FreeGave-GoPro dataset is practically significant as the first real-world multi-view benchmark specifically designed for 3D physics learning, filling a gap where prior work relied on synthetic scenes or repurposed general dynamic datasets. The finding that latent physics codes naturally cluster into object-level motion patterns (achieving near-perfect unsupervised segmentation) suggests a promising route to physics-aware scene decomposition without any segmentation supervision. Limitations include the rigid-particle assumption (no intra-Gaussian deformation), the requirement for known camera poses, and evaluation primarily on relatively simple scenes; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly deformable objects, uncalibrated video, and integration with 4D generative pipelines.
