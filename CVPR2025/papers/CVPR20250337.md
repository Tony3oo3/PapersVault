## Problem

Existing visual localization datasets and benchmarks primarily evaluate single-device scenarios, overlooking the critical challenge of cross-device localization where agents with different sensors, viewpoints, and motion patterns must be registered to a shared reference. This gap is particularly acute for human-robot collaboration and shared augmented reality, where a legged robot, a hand-held phone, and a head-mounted headset must all be localized consistently. CroCoDL addresses this by providing the first real-world dataset with sensor recordings from robots, phones, and MR headsets across 10 diverse environments, enabling systematic evaluation of cross-device visual registration.

## Input / Output

The localization pipeline takes query RGB images from one device (HoloLens 2 with 4 cameras at 83° FOV, iOS with 1 camera at 64° FOV, or Spot with 4 grayscale cameras at 103° FOV plus Azure Kinect and ZED2i RGB-D) and a mapping set from a different device, from which a sparse SfM model is built. The output is a 6DOF camera pose per query, evaluated with recall at 5° rotation and 0.5 m translation thresholds (relaxed from LaMAR's tighter thresholds to account for Spot's lower GT accuracy). The dataset provides 1.5K+ sequences totaling over 100 hours of raw recordings, with ground truth derived from NavVis VLX LiDAR scans (~10 cm accuracy via PGO+PGO-BA), and includes synchronized RGB, depth, IMU, LiDAR, and WiFi/BT streams per device.

## Method

CroCoDL's core contribution is a data collection and ground-truthing pipeline that integrates Boston Dynamics Spot robot recordings into the LaMAR pseudo-GT framework. The robot pre-processing converts rosbags to the LaMAR Capture format, applies on-site Kalibr calibration corrections, and creates virtual rigs that temporally synchronize heterogeneous sensor readings (Spot's 5 grayscale cameras, Azure Kinect, ZED2i) to a common timestamp by interpolating the robot's kinematic odometry. Ground truth is generated by aligning all device trajectories to high-quality NavVis VLX LiDAR meshes using the LaMAR PGO+PGO-BA pipeline. The benchmark evaluates cross-device localization by building SfM maps from one device's mapping set and localizing another device's query set, testing all device-pair combinations with seven retrieval methods (NetVLAD, APGeM, CosPlace, SALAD, OpenIBL, Fusion, and GT-overlap upper bound), SuperPoint feature extraction, LightGlue matching, and LO-RANSAC pose estimation (P3P for single images, GP3P for multi-camera rigs). Results reveal that cross-device retrieval is the primary bottleneck, with iOS-on-Spot recall dropping to 0.30–0.42 even with perfect retrieval, indicating fundamental limitations in current feature matching under large viewpoint and baseline differences.

## Results

On the hardest cross-device scenario (iOS queries in Spot-built maps, Hydrology Lab), the best retrieval method (CosPlace) combined with SuperPoint + LightGlue achieves only 49% recall at 5°/0.5m thresholds, while the upper bound with perfect retrieval (Overlap Top-10) reaches 67%. Aggregated across five locations, same-device localization (e.g., HoloLens-to-HoloLens) achieves 90–96% recall with NetVLAD, whereas the worst cross-device pair (iOS-to-Spot) drops to 36–56% depending on retrieval method. Even with ground-truth overlap retrieval isolating the matching step, iOS-on-Spot recall remains below 60%, indicating that both retrieval and feature matching are significant bottlenecks for cross-device localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among retrieval methods, CosPlace (0.49) outperforms NetVLAD (0.42), APGeM (0.36), and SALAD (0.32) in the hardest iOS-on-Spot scenario, while FusionOpenIBL and OpenIBL tie at 0.43. For feature matching, SuperPoint + LightGlue (0.42) beats SIFT + LightGlue (0.25) by a wide margin, and SuperPoint + SuperGlue (0.43) slightly edges out SuperPoint + LightGlue; the dense matcher LoFTR (0.40) underperforms both. The two-view method MASt3R (0.34) trails the multi-view SuperPoint + LightGlue pipeline (0.42) by 8 points, suggesting that multi-view triangulation remains advantageous even in cross-device settings. The gap between the best learned pipeline (CosPlace + SuperPoint + LightGlue at 0.49) and the perfect-retrieval upper bound (0.67) quantifies the retrieval bottleneck, while the 0.67-to-0.57 drop from Top-10 to Top-1 overlap highlights the sensitivity to retrieval quality. Overall, no SOTA combination exceeds 50% recall in the hardest cross-device pair, revealing substantial headroom for improvement in both image retrieval and feature matching for heterogeneous sensor setups.

## Relation to Prior Work

CroCoDL directly extends LaMAR [35] by integrating Boston Dynamics Spot robot recordings into its LiDAR-based pseudo-ground-truth pipeline and adding seven new locations, while retaining the same data modalities (RGB, LiDAR, depth, IMU, WiFi/BT). It departs from prior multi-agent datasets (FusionPortable, TUM RGBD, Graco, UMA VI) that combine at most two device types per location, by providing four distinct sensor platforms (legged robot, drone, MR headset, handheld phone) with overlapping trajectories in the same scenes. The choice to use NavVis LiDAR scans for ground truth rather than motion capture or total stations follows LaMAR's scalability argument, trading sub-centimeter accuracy for the ability to cover large, diverse, and structurally changing environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art visual localization methods degrade substantially under cross-device conditions — iOS queries in Spot-built maps achieve only 36–42% recall versus 82–97% same-device — and that image retrieval, not feature matching, is the primary bottleneck, since even ground-truth overlap retrieval caps recall at 67%. This quantifies a gap that the AR and human-robot collaboration communities have informally recognized but lacked a standardized evaluation for. Open questions include whether the retrieval bottleneck can be addressed with cross-device-aware embeddings, whether the findings generalize beyond the five evaluated locations, and how the benchmark extends to full SLAM with heterogeneous agents, which the authors flag as future work.
