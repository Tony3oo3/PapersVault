## Problem

Learning fine-grained view-invariant video representations from unpaired ego-exo videos is challenging due to fundamental differences in perspective, motion patterns, field-of-view, and context between first-person and third-person views. Prior approaches are limited to clip-level coarse representations or rely on additional hand-object interaction detectors and manual annotations, restricting scalability to diverse action classes. The compositional, temporally structured nature of human actions has not been effectively leveraged for cross-view understanding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired, asynchronous ego and exo video sequences (same action class, no temporal alignment) and processes them through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder. A selective token merging step retains only the top-K most-changed patch tokens per frame (based on inter-frame embedding differences), reducing the sequence to a single d-dimensional frame token per frame. After training, the decoder is discarded and the 12-layer transformer encoder produces view-invariant frame-level embeddings that support downstream tasks: SVM-based action phase classification, nearest-neighbor frame retrieval, linear-regression phase progression, and Kendall's tau temporal alignment scoring.

## Method

BYOV employs an asymmetric encoder-decoder architecture (12-layer encoder, 4-layer decoder, 256-dim embeddings) trained with two complementary masked modeling objectives. Masked Self-View Modeling (MSM) randomly masks 40% of frame tokens and reconstructs them using a causal mask in the decoder so that each frame is predicted only from prior frames, capturing fine-grained temporal causality within each view. Masked Cross-View Modeling (MCM) masks a larger fraction (80%) of tokens in one view and forces the decoder to reconstruct them using the full latent sequence from the other view, thereby learning view-invariant alignment. Both objectives use MSE on frame token embeddings (not pixel reconstruction), preserving spatial information from the frozen image encoder while the encoder focuses on temporal context. The selective token merging strategy replaces expensive hand-object interaction detectors with a simple inter-frame difference criterion, and the two losses are jointly optimized with shared parameters.

## Results

On the AE2 benchmark (four ego-exo datasets: Break Eggs, Pour Milk, Pour Liquid, Tennis Forehand), BYOV achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four downstream tasks. Compared to the previous SoTA (AE2), BYOV gains over 10% F1 in classification (e.g., 74.30 vs. 66.23 on Break Eggs Regular), 3.78 mAP@10 in frame retrieval (e.g., 90.61 vs. 86.83 on Tennis Forehand), 0.3424 in phase progression (e.g., 0.8533 vs. 0.5109 on Break Eggs), and 0.3135 in Kendall's τ (e.g., 0.9451 vs. 0.6316 on Break Eggs). On Charades-Ego video-level action recognition, BYOV improves mAP by 18.1% (Regular), 18.2% (Ego2Exo), and 16.6% (Exo2Ego) over CLIP features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BYOV outperforms all eight baselines across every metric and dataset. Against the previous SoTA AE2 [50], BYOV achieves the largest margins in phase progression (e.g., 0.8533 vs. 0.5109 on Break Eggs, a 0.34 gap) and Kendall's τ (0.9451 vs. 0.6316 on Break Eggs, a 0.31 gap), while also leading in classification F1 (74.30 vs. 66.23) and frame retrieval mAP@10 (67.17 vs. 65.85) on Break Eggs. The gap is most dramatic on the in-the-wild Pour Liquid dataset, where BYOV scores 79.48 F1 vs. AE2's 66.56 and 0.4483 phase progression vs. AE2's 0.1380. Among earlier baselines, GTA [18] and TCC [12] are the strongest competitors on classification and retrieval, but both fall well short of BYOV on temporal metrics (phase progression and Kendall's τ). Pretrained image features (CLIP ViT-B/16, ResNet-50 ImageNet) struggle severely with cross-view transfer and temporal ordering, often scoring negative phase progression values, confirming that static image representations cannot capture fine-grained action dynamics. On Charades-Ego, BYOV (31.8 mAP Regular) more than doubles CLIP features (13.7 mAP), validating the approach at the video level.

## Relation to Prior Work

BYOV extends the masked autoencoding paradigm (MAE, VideoMAE) from single-view video to the cross-view ego-exo setting, introducing a novel cross-view masking objective (MCM) that forces the decoder to reconstruct one view's tokens using the other view's latents. It departs from prior ego-exo representation learning (Ego-Exo, AE2) which relied on paired videos, hand-object interaction detectors, or DTW-based temporal alignment, by operating entirely on unpaired, asynchronous videos with a self-supervised reconstruction objective. The selective token merging strategy (top-K tokens by inter-frame embedding difference) is positioned as a lightweight alternative to the hand-object interaction detectors used in AE2, drawing on the observation that fine-grained actions manifest as small local spatial changes between consecutive frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to learn fine-grained, view-invariant representations without paired ego-exo recordings or auxiliary detectors addresses a key scalability bottleneck for deploying video understanding in robotics and AR, where synchronized multi-camera capture is impractical. The large and consistent margins over AE2 across four distinct action datasets and four evaluation tasks (classification, retrieval, phase progression, temporal alignment) suggest the masked ego-exo formulation captures temporal structure more faithfully than alignment-based objectives. However, the evaluation is confined to four relatively simple, short-duration actions (egg breaking, pouring, tennis forehand), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to longer, more complex procedural activities or to the full diversity of egocentric benchmarks like Ego4D. The reliance on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 as the image encoder also raises questions about whether the temporal modeling benefits transfer to other backbone architectures or larger models.
