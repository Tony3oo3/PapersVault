## Problem

Vision Graph Neural Networks (ViGs) and Vision Hypergraph Neural Networks (ViHGNNs) rely on iterative clustering algorithms (KNN, Fuzzy C-Means) for edge generation, creating significant computational bottlenecks, and their edge features are not integrated across successive layers for cumulative learning. Existing efficiency-oriented variants like MobileViG and GreedyViG sacrifice adaptability for speed without achieving a well-balanced solution between dynamic structure and computational cost.

## Input / Output

HgVT takes RGB images at 224×224 (or 160×160 for the lite variant) and splits them into patches that become image vertices in a bipartite hypergraph, augmented with learned virtual vertices and virtual hyperedges that have no direct spatial correspondence. Beyond the standard class label, the model produces a learned hypergraph adjacency matrix from the final layer, which is directly exploited for graph-based image retrieval (volumetric and adaptive similarity search), and a pooled embedding combining expert-selected virtual hyperedge features with an image-level embedding.

## Method

HgVT replaces graph convolutions with structured multi-head attention over a hierarchical bipartite hypergraph, where each block performs three sequential attention operations—vertex self-attention (V→V within shared hyperedges), edge aggregate attention (V→E), and edge distribution attention (E→V)—to approximate all-to-all message passing at reduced quadratic cost. The hypergraph structure is built dynamically per layer via cosine similarity between vertex and hyperedge adjacency features, passed through a sharpened sigmoid (α=4) to yield soft memberships, then thresholded for sparse attention masking, achieving O(|V|·E) construction without any clustering. Population regularization bounds each hyperedge's soft vertex density within [γ, β] to enforce learned sparsity, while diversity regularization penalizes non-orthogonality among virtual vertex and hyperedge embeddings to prevent representation collapse. For classification, expert edge pooling treats virtual hyperedges as confidence-scored experts (softmax-gated weighted averaging with a density loss to prevent underutilization), and virtual hyperedges are restricted to connect only with virtual vertices, creating a hierarchical separation between visual and abstract semantic levels.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, HgVT-Ti achieves 76.2% Top-1 accuracy (7.7M params, 1.8B FLOPs), surpassing ViHGNN-Ti (74.3%) by 1.9% and DeiT-Ti (72.2%) by 4.0%. At the S-scale, HgVT-S reaches 81.2% Top-1, matching DeiT-B's ImageNet ReaL accuracy (86.7%) at nearly a quarter of its parameter count (22.9M vs. 86.4M). For image retrieval on ImageNet-1k, HgVT-Ti with combined image+expert pooling achieves 73.23% mAP@10, exceeding the MRL-256 baseline (65.04%) by over 8%. On Oxford/Paris retrieval, HgVT-S (VS) scores 28.0/56.7 mAP on ROxford-M/RParis-M, remaining competitive with similarly sized extractors but trailing larger CNN-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HgVT-Ti clearly outperforms both ViHGNN-Ti (74.3%) and ViG-Ti (73.9%) on ImageNet-1k by 1.9% and 2.3% respectively, while also exceeding DeiT-Ti (72.2%) by 4.0%. The smaller HgVT-Mi (5.8M params) matches ViHGNN-Ti (74.3%) with only 70% of the parameters. At the S-scale, HgVT-S (81.2%) slightly trails ViHGNN-S (81.5%) by 0.3% but beats ViG-S (80.4%) by 0.8% and approaches DeiT-B (81.8%) at a fraction of its size. On robustness benchmarks, HgVT-S ties DeiT-B on ImageNet ReaL (86.7%) and leads DeiT-Ti by 3.1% (83.2% vs. 80.1%). For retrieval, HgVT-Ti surpasses MRL-256 by 8.2% mAP@10 on ImageNet-1k, but on Oxford/Paris it lags behind RN101+R-MAC (49.8/74.0 mAP) and DINOv1-S trained on GLDv2 (51.5/75.3 mAP), suggesting the classifier-trained hypergraph captures coarser semantics than multi-scale or self-supervised features for fine-grained landmark retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

HgVT sits at the intersection of Vision Transformers (ViT/DeiT) and Vision Graph/Hypergraph Neural Networks (ViG/ViHGNN), inheriting the patch-based representation from ViTs and the structured relational modeling from graph-based vision models. The key departure from ViG and ViHGNN is the replacement of iterative clustering algorithms (KNN, Fuzzy C-Means) for edge generation with a dynamic, non-iterative cosine-similarity-based adjacency construction, and the reconceptualization of graph convolutions as segmented attention operations (vertex self-attention, edge aggregate, edge distribution) within a bipartite hypergraph. The introduction of virtual vertices and virtual hyperedges, inspired by register tokens, creates a hierarchical semantic structure that prior vision GNNs did not explicitly model, while population and diversity regularization replace fixed cluster-size constraints with learned sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the clustering bottleneck that has limited the practical adoption of vision GNNs, HgVT offers a more scalable path toward structured relational modeling in vision transformers, with a consistent ≥1.4× speedup over ViG and competitive accuracy at smaller parameter counts. The emergent macro-class prediction behavior in expert pooling and the demonstrated utility of the learned hypergraph for image retrieval suggest the architecture could serve as a general-purpose semantic backbone beyond classification. However, the isotropic-only design (no pyramidal variant), the fact that HgVT-S merely matches rather than surpasses ViHGNN-S, and the underwhelming Oxford/Paris retrieval results relative to self-supervised baselines like DINOv2 indicate that the approach has not yet fully realized its potential, and the authors themselves flag scalability and self-supervised integration as key open directions.
