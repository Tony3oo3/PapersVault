## Problem

Existing text-to-3D avatar methods either entangle hair, clothing, and body into a single geometry or rely on implicit representations (NeRF, SDF) that cannot be converted to the clean meshes and strand structures required by physics or neural simulators. This makes it impossible to produce avatars with realistic, pose-dependent dynamic motion of loose garments and flowing hair while still leveraging the rich appearance priors of foundational image diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250891/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing the avatar's hairstyle, garment type/pattern, and body characteristics, along with a target pose sequence for animation. It produces a fully layered, simulation-ready 3D avatar consisting of an SMPL body mesh, a non-watertight garment mesh suitable for cloth simulators, a strand-based hair point cloud (N_s strands × N_l line segments each), and per-layer 3D Gaussians that encode high-fidelity appearance. The garment mesh is generated by a dedicated text-conditioned latent diffusion model trained on ~20K meshes, ensuring the output topology is clean and compact enough for direct simulator input.

## Method

SimAvatar decomposes avatar generation into three text-conditioned 3D generators (a novel VAE + latent diffusion model for garment meshes, HAAR for hair strands, and BodyShapeGPT for SMPL shape parameters), then attaches 3D Gaussians to each layer with part-specific parameterization: face-local coordinate systems for body/garment meshes and segment-aligned elongated Gaussians (γ=0.001) for hair strands. Gaussian color and opacity are modeled by separate implicit fields per layer to prevent texture entanglement, and optimized via Score Distillation Sampling against a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model. A novel hair opacity regularization (L_hair) enforces monotonically decreasing opacity along each strand from root to tip, preventing broken-hair artifacts from transparent mid-strand Gaussians. At animation time, the body deforms via linear blend skinning, the garment mesh is simulated by the HOOD neural simulator, and hair strands are simulated by a dedicated hair simulator; the resulting vertex/strand motions are transferred to the attached Gaussians through the local-to-global transforms, yielding realistic wrinkle and flow effects without re-optimization.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative evaluation is a user study with 540 votes from 18 users across 22 prompts. SimAvatar achieves 100% preference over Fantasia3D on both appearance and motion, 89.55% and 91.87% over TADA, and 87.03% and 94.47% over GAvatar, respectively. Qualitatively, SimAvatar is the only method that produces physically plausible motion for loose garments (e.g., dresses) during animation, while baselines using single-layer geometry with linear blend skinning fail to model pose-dependent deformations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimAvatar is compared against three state-of-the-art text-to-3D avatar methods: Fantasia3D, TADA, and GAvatar. Against Fantasia3D, SimAvatar wins unanimously (100%) on both appearance and motion, largely because Fantasia3D uses implicit representations that cannot be readily animated. Against TADA, SimAvatar is preferred 89.55% of the time for appearance and 91.87% for motion; TADA uses a single mesh layer with linear blend skinning, causing loose garments like dresses to tear into two pieces during leg-raising motions. Against GAvatar, SimAvatar leads on motion preference (94.47%) but trails slightly on appearance (87.03%), as GAvatar's 3D Gaussian representation with implicit mesh learning produces competitive static textures but still relies on single-layer geometry for animation. The key differentiator across all comparisons is SimAvatar's layered simulation-ready structure (separate SMPL body, garment mesh, and hair strands), which enables physically plausible dynamic effects such as flowing hair and garment wrinkles that none of the baselines can reproduce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of text-to-3D avatar generation (DreamAvatar, GAvatar, TADA) and layered avatar representations (SO-SMPL, TELA, GALA, TECA), but departs from both in a key way: prior layered methods rely on implicit representations (NeRF, DMTet) that are incompatible with existing physics or neural simulators, while prior simulation-ready methods (SMPL+D) cannot capture loose clothing or complex hairstyles. SimAvatar resolves this by using simulation-native geometry (SMPL body, garment meshes, hair strands) as the structural backbone and attaching 3D Gaussians for high-fidelity appearance, optimized via SDS with a diffusion prior. The garment generation component draws on the vectorized latent space and UDF-based meshing paradigm from 3DShape2VecSet and MeshUDF, while the hair and body generation reuse existing text-conditioned models (HAAR, BodyShapeGPT) rather than training new ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to produce fully simulation-ready 3D avatars with independently simulable body, garment, and hair layers, enabling realistic pose-dependent effects (flowing hair, garment wrinkles) that single-layer methods fundamentally cannot replicate. The layered 3D Gaussian-on-simulation-geometry paradigm is a clean, reusable architectural pattern likely to be adopted by subsequent avatar and virtual try-on work. Key limitations remain: sequential (rather than joint) hair-and-garment simulation fails on complex cases like hoods, training data diversity bottlenecks generalization, and accessories/footwear are still entangled with body or garment layers. Open questions include whether joint simulation, larger-scale garment training data, and fully disentangled multi-layer avatars can close these gaps.
