## Problem

Existing B-rep generative models (SolidGen, BrepGen) predominantly emphasize geometric attributes while neglecting or post-processing topological structure, resulting in low validity rates and limited geometric complexity. The core challenge is that topology and geometry are tightly interdependent—topological constraints (closed face loops, shared edges) must be satisfied simultaneously with accurate geometric placement—yet prior methods couple these processes, making it difficult to guarantee structural validity.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise as the primary input for unconditional generation, with optional class labels (via learnable embeddings + classifier-free guidance) or 2,000-point point clouds (encoded by PointNet++ into a 512-d embedding) for conditional generation. Outputs are complete watertight B-rep solids: a valid topology defined by edge-face and edge-vertex adjacency matrices, vertex 3D coordinates, cubic B-spline edges (4 control points each), and bi-cubic B-spline faces (4×4 control point grids). A post-processing step attempts to fit basic primitives (planes, quadrics) to generated surfaces before sewing the final solid via OpenCascade.

## Method

DTGBrepGen decouples B-rep generation into topology and geometry stages. Topology is produced in two phases: first, a Transformer VAE generates edge-face adjacencies by modeling a face-to-face shared-edge-count matrix (FeF) as a discrete sequence; second, a Transformer encoder with GCN-aggregated face features plus a pointer-network decoder autoregressively generates edge-vertex connections as a sequential edge-ordering problem, with constraint checking at inference. Geometry is then generated sequentially by four Transformer-based diffusion models (face bounding boxes → vertex coordinates → edge B-splines → face B-splines), where each stage conditions on previously generated geometry and incorporates topological information (shared-edge counts, vertex connectivity) into attention scores. A key design choice is representing curves and surfaces as B-splines with directly learned control-point distributions rather than discrete point clouds, avoiding the geometric inaccuracies of point sampling or VAE-based latent encoding.

## Results

On the DeepCAD dataset, DTGBrepGen achieves 79.80% Valid (watertight B-rep) rate, substantially outperforming BrepGen (68.23%) and DeepCAD (58.10%), while also leading in COV (74.52 vs. 72.38 vs. 70.81), MMD (1.07 vs. 1.13 vs. 1.31), and JSD (1.02 vs. 1.29 vs. 1.79). On the larger ABC dataset, DTGBrepGen reaches 62.08% Valid compared to BrepGen's 47.11%, with improvements across all distribution metrics (COV 71.13 vs. 64.29, MMD 1.33 vs. 1.53, JSD 1.59 vs. 1.86). Topology generation alone achieves 88–92% validity across all three datasets, and the ablation variant (Ours*, replacing B-splines with point-cloud VAE encoding) drops Valid by ~8 percentage points, confirming the benefit of B-spline control-point learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTGBrepGen is compared against two baselines: DeepCAD (autoregressive sketch-extrude command generation) and BrepGen (hierarchical diffusion with structured latent geometry). Against DeepCAD on the DeepCAD dataset, DTGBrepGen wins on every metric, with the most dramatic gain in Valid (79.80% vs. 58.10%, a +21.7 pp improvement) and JSD (1.02 vs. 1.79), reflecting both higher structural validity and closer distributional match. Against BrepGen, DTGBrepGen also dominates on all metrics for both DeepCAD and ABC: on DeepCAD, Valid improves by +11.6 pp (79.80 vs. 68.23) and JSD drops from 1.29 to 1.02; on ABC, Valid jumps by +15.0 pp (62.08 vs. 47.11) and COV rises by 6.8 pp (71.13 vs. 64.29). The consistent advantage in Valid across datasets underscores the benefit of explicitly decoupling topology generation (two-phase Transformer encoder-decoder) from geometry generation (diffusion over B-spline control points), whereas BrepGen's post-hoc topology recovery remains its primary weakness. In conditional generation on the Furniture dataset, DTGBrepGen also outperforms BrepGen by roughly 10 pp in Valid across all 10 categories (e.g., Bathtub 49.82 vs. 28.50, Table 68.27 vs. 55.67).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the B-rep generation line of work initiated by SolidGen (autoregressive, prismatic-only) and BrepGen (hierarchical diffusion with post-hoc topology recovery), and on the broader CAD command-sequence generation paradigm (DeepCAD, SKExGen). Its key conceptual departure is the explicit decoupling of topology from geometry: rather than generating geometric attributes and attempting to recover connectivity in post-processing (BrepGen) or restricting to simple extruded shapes (SolidGen), DTGBrepGen first produces a valid topological scaffold via a two-phase Transformer encoder-decoder (edge-face adjacencies, then edge-vertex adjacencies with a pointer network), and only then conditions sequential diffusion models on that scaffold to generate B-spline control points for vertices, edges, and faces. The B-spline parameterization also distinguishes it from prior B-rep methods that rely on discrete point sampling (UV-Net, BrepGen) or VAE-encoded point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

B-rep is the dominant industrial CAD format, so a generative model that produces topologically valid and geometrically precise B-reps has direct practical value for design exploration, synthesis, and downstream manufacturing workflows. The decoupling strategy offers a clean, modular recipe that other B-rep or structured-geometry generators could adopt, and the B-spline representation avoids the geometric inaccuracies inherent in point-cloud discretization. However, the paper itself acknowledges that overall B-rep validity (62–80%) still lags behind topology validity (88–92%), revealing a non-trivial gap between 'valid topology' and 'valid geometry on that topology,' and that complex models with intricate details remain challenging. Open questions include whether hierarchical or multi-scale strategies can close this gap and whether the framework scales beyond the 50-face / 30-edge-per-face limits imposed here.
