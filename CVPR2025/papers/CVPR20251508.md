## Problem

Existing VideoQA datasets either depend on costly manual annotations that lack fine-grained reasoning granularity, or use automatic generation pipelines that process every frame redundantly, making them neither scalable nor effective for complex video reasoning. Additionally, current LVLMs struggle with video reasoning because they either sample frames uniformly (introducing massive redundancy) or lack explicit intermediate reasoning annotations that guide step-by-step inference over spatiotemporal content.

## Input / Output

The system takes a video clip and a text question as input. The Frame Selector samples frames at a content-adaptive FPS (1–4 depending on scene dynamics), generates per-frame captions via a 1B-parameter LVLM, and selects approximately 2–3 question-relevant core frames. The reasoning LVLM then produces a two-part output: first, a chain-of-thought evidence passage identifying key objects with spatial bounding boxes (via GroundingDINO) and temporal grounding timestamps, and second, the final text answer. The dataset itself contains QA pairs with multimodal CoT annotations including bounding boxes, temporal indices, and natural-language evidence linking key items across space and time.

## Method

The dataset is built through a fully automated pipeline: video frames are mapped to the linguistic space via InternVL2-8B captions, redundant frames are removed using BGE-M3 cosine similarity with a LIFO filter, and 15 consecutive captions are grouped and fed to GPT-4o to generate complex reasoning QA pairs (validated by Claude 3 for hallucination and factual accuracy). For CoT annotation, GPT-4o extracts core frames, key items, and textual evidence, while GroundingDINO provides spatial bounding boxes (verified by CLIP) and BGE-M3 retrieves temporal grounding from the original caption set. At inference, a Hybrid LVLMs Collaboration framework pairs a lightweight Frame Selector (InternVL2-1B + QwenLM-0.5B, ~1.5B total parameters) that performs question-driven core frame selection, with a two-stage instruction-tuned reasoning LVLM (LLaVA-Next-interleave backbone) that first generates multimodal evidence (stage 1) and then produces the answer conditioned on that evidence (stage 2), reducing input frames to ~2.4 on average versus 16–128 for baselines while achieving 34.1% average accuracy across 14 tasks.

## Results

On the VideoEspresso objective benchmark (14 video reasoning tasks), the proposed Hybrid LVLMs Collaboration framework achieves an average accuracy of 34.1%, setting state-of-the-art on 12 out of 14 tasks. This surpasses the strongest open-source baseline InternVL2 (28.7%) by 5.4% and the closed-source GPT-4o (26.4%) by 7.7%. The method also demonstrates superior efficiency, requiring only 2.36 input frames on average and 9.26 TFLOPs, compared to 16–128 frames and 60–465 TFLOPs for competing models. On the subjective benchmark, the method scores 65.84 overall, narrowly trailing GPT-4o (66.13) while outperforming all open-source LVLMs by 5–14 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 8 baselines (2 closed-source, 7 open-source) on both objective and subjective evaluations. On the objective benchmark, the proposed method wins on 12 of 14 tasks, outperforming GPT-4o by 7.7% (34.1% vs 26.4%) and the best open-source model InternVL2 by 5.4% (34.1% vs 28.7%). The two tasks where the method does not lead are Theme Analysis (InternVL2: 42.6% vs Ours: 39.3%) and Cooking Process (LLaVA-1.5: 35.8% tied with Ours: 35.8%), which the authors attribute to those models' exposure to large volumes of similar training data. On the subjective benchmark, GPT-4o edges out the proposed method by 0.29 points overall (66.13 vs 65.84), but the method surpasses GPT-4o on Conciseness by 5% and leads all open-source models across all four dimensions (Logic, Factuality, Accuracy, Conciseness). In terms of efficiency, the method uses only 2.36 frames and 9.26 TFLOPs, reducing frame input to 1.8% of LongVA-DPO and computational cost to 14.7% of LLaVA-Next-interleave, while maintaining the highest accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—automatic VideoQA dataset construction (departing from manual-annotation datasets like TVQA and ActivityNet-QA), semantic-aware frame selection (replacing uniform sampling or image-similarity-based keyframe extraction with BGE-M3 caption-level deduplication), and multimodal Chain-of-Thought annotation (extending text-only CoT approaches like VideoCoT and VoT with spatial bounding boxes via GroundingDINO and temporal grounding). The Hybrid LVLMs Collaboration framework positions a tiny 1B+0.5B selector before a fine-tuned reasoning LVLM, contrasting with prior work that either processes all frames uniformly or relies on additional architectural modules for token efficiency. Unlike MVBench and similar benchmarks that emphasize basic object/action queries, VideoEspresso targets complex multi-step reasoning with explicit intermediate evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset and 14-task benchmark provide a much-needed resource for fine-grained video reasoning evaluation, and the 98% frame reduction (2.36 vs. 128 frames) while matching or exceeding accuracy demonstrates a practical path to efficient video understanding. A key open question is the circularity risk: GPT-4o is used for both data generation and evaluation scoring, which may inflate apparent performance. The mixed generalization results of the selector (helping GPT-4o/InternVL2 but slightly hurting LongVA/LLaVA-N-i) suggest the frame-selection strategy is not yet model-agnostic, and whether the CoT annotations transfer to longer or more diverse video domains remains untested.
