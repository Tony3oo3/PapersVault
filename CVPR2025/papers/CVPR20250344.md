## Problem

When the original teacher training data is unavailable (e.g., proprietary datasets, continual learning), practitioners must find surrogate data for knowledge distillation, but it was assumed that only real in-domain imagery would be viable. This paper asks whether even unnatural synthetic out-of-domain imagery can successfully transfer knowledge, and systematically identifies what criteria make a surrogate dataset effective for distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250344/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained teacher network (ResNet50, ConvNeXt-T, or ViT-S), a surrogate image dataset capped at 50K examples (real ID/OOD images, OpenGL shaders, random-shape 'Leaves', or Gaussian noise), and a randomly initialized student network (ResNet18, WRN-40x2, or MobileNet-v2). For synthetic imagery, the pipeline applies stronger augmentation (RandAugment n=4, elastic transforms, random inversion) plus mixup, since no semantic label constraint applies. The output is a trained student network evaluated on the teacher's original test set.

## Method

The paper uses standard KD (KL-divergence on temperature-scaled softmax outputs) but contributes an empirical framework for evaluating surrogate datasets. The key insight is that KD is a sufficient-sampling problem: the teacher's argmax class prediction histogram over the surrogate data should have high relative entropy (close to 1), meaning all classes are predicted roughly equally. They demonstrate that unoptimized OpenGL shader images (rendered from 1089 TwiGL programs) satisfy this criterion and achieve within 2% of original-data distillation on CIFAR-10. Additionally, they propose an adversarial perturbation strategy that generates pairs of examples (post-success and pre-success) near decision boundaries using a Bold Driver adaptive step size, which substantially improves knowledge transfer for poorly-performing surrogate datasets by forcing the teacher to be sampled more uniformly across its decision space.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, distilling a ResNet50 teacher to a ResNet18 student using OpenGL shader images achieves 94.02% test accuracy, within 2% of the 95.98% obtained with the original CIFAR-10 data. On CIFAR-100, OpenGL shaders reach 73.27% vs. 78.35% for the original data. In the direct comparison against data-free KD baselines (Table 7, ResNet34→ResNet18), the OpenGL shader approach achieves 93.79% on CIFAR-10 and 73.86% on CIFAR-100, outperforming CMI (82.40/55.20) and PRE-DFKD (87.40/70.20) while being competitive with FAST (94.05/74.34) and Spaceship (95.19/73.40). The proposed adversarial perturbation strategy further boosts the worst-performing surrogate datasets by up to 76.8% (e.g., FGVCA data for a CIFAR-10 teacher goes from 11.39% to 88.14%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The OpenGL shader approach (93.79/73.86) decisively outperforms the data-free methods CMI [16] (82.40/55.20) and PRE-DFKD [6] (87.40/70.20) by 11.4%/18.7% and 6.4%/3.7% on CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 respectively, while also avoiding the 48-hour training overhead of generator-based methods like Spaceship [57]. Against the strongest data-free baselines, the method is slightly behind FAST [17] (94.05/74.34) by ~0.3–0.5% and Spaceship [57] (95.19/73.40) by 1.4% on CIFAR-10, though it edges out Spaceship on CIFAR-100 (73.86 vs. 73.40). The single-image approach [2] is outperformed on both datasets, with the "Animals" variant (93.69/71.01) trailing by 0.1%/2.9% and the "City" variant (93.47/69.34) by 0.3%/4.5%. Notably, the paper argues that the OpenGL shader method achieves these results with only 2 hours of compute (vs. ~48 hours for Spaceship) and without a generator network, eliminating risks of mode collapse and non-convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the standard KD framework of Hinton et al. [25] (KL-divergence with temperature scaling) and adopts the 'function matching' perspective and mixup strategy from Beyer et al. [5], which had previously shown that OOD data causes significant performance loss in distillation. It departs from that line by demonstrating that even unoptimized, unnatural synthetic OOD imagery (OpenGL shaders) can serve as a viable distillation dataset, a scenario not explored in prior surrogate-data KD work [5, 15, 2, 45]. The adversarial perturbation strategy extends the decision-boundary-aware KD of Heo et al. [24] and Tian et al. [49] by using pairs of adversarial examples and a Bold Driver heuristic [41] to more thoroughly outline and sample decision regions, and it positions itself against data-free KD methods [6, 16, 17, 57] by showing that a generator network is unnecessary when diverse synthetic imagery is available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with concrete, measurable criteria (relative entropy of the teacher's argmax class prediction histogram, data diversity, image complexity, and decision boundary information) for selecting or constructing surrogate distillation datasets when the original training data is inaccessible—a common scenario in continual learning and proprietary-model distillation. The finding that unoptimized OpenGL shader images can approach original-data distillation performance on general-purpose datasets is practically useful but is tempered by substantially weaker results on fine-grained tasks (FGVCA, Pets) and the relatively small scale of experiments (≤50K samples, CIFAR/Tiny ImageNet). Open questions include whether these criteria and the synthetic-data approach generalize to larger-scale benchmarks (full ImageNet, 1000+ classes), non-vision modalities, and whether the Nyquist-sampling analogy can be made more formal to predict required dataset sizes.
