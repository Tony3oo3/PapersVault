## Problem

Recent large-kernel CNNs (SLaK, RepLKNet, UniRepLKNet) have shown diminishing returns from simply enlarging convolutional kernel sizes, yet the hidden factors that make large kernels effective remain unexplored. The paper asks whether long-distance dependency modeling can be decoupled from kernel size itself, separating fine-grained feature extraction from multi-path feature fusion, and whether standard 3×3 convolutions can replace large kernels entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251086/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images at task-specific resolutions (224×224 for ImageNet classification, 800×1280 for COCO detection, 512×2048 for ADE20K segmentation) and produces class labels, pixel-wise segmentation maps, 2D bounding boxes with instance masks, or monocular 3D bounding boxes. A notable design constraint is that the SW module operates on group convolutions where each input channel produces ⌈M/3⌉ output channels (M being the equivalent large-kernel size per stage), and coarse-grained filter pruning during training permanently removes output channels while preserving the module's structural integrity.

## Method

The Shiftwise (SW) Convolution replaces SLaK's M×N strip convolutions with a pipeline of: (1) a 3×3 group convolution (groups = input channels C, output channels = ⌈M/3⌉ per group) that extracts features at fine granularity, (2) a spatial shift operation that repositions the multi-channel outputs to emulate the sliding-window coverage of the original large kernel, and (3) multiple parallel reparameterization (Rep) branches with shared sparse masks that add training-time expressiveness while collapsing to a single 3×3 conv at inference. Feature utilization is enhanced by shuffling channel order across multiple shift edges (multi-path fusion), breaking the predictable coverage pattern of fixed offsets. The architecture follows UniRepLKNet's depth-first design (block counts [3,3,27,3], channels [96,192,384,768] for SW-small) with SE modules, and uses coarse-grained filter pruning (summing absolute values per filter, removing lowest-ranked) to eliminate redundant connections without altering the network topology.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, SW-tiny (31M params, 5.0G FLOPs) achieves 83.4% top-1 accuracy, surpassing UniRepLKNet-T (83.2%) and SLaK-T (82.5%) at comparable model size. SW-small (56M params, 9.4G FLOPs) reaches 83.9% top-1 accuracy, matching UniRepLKNet-S and outperforming SLaK-S (83.8%) and ConvNeXt-S (83.1%). On COCO detection, SW-tiny achieves 52.21 AP_box / 45.19 AP_mask, and on ADE20K segmentation, SW-tiny achieves 49.22 mIoU (SS) / 50.06 mIoU (MS), both leading among same-size pure CNN backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest large-kernel CNN baselines, SW-tiny outperforms UniRepLKNet-T by 0.2% (83.4 vs 83.2) and SLaK-T by 0.9% (83.4 vs 82.5) on ImageNet; SW-small ties UniRepLKNet-S at 83.9% and edges out SLaK-S (83.8%) and ConvNeXt-S (83.1%). On COCO detection, SW-tiny leads UniRepLKNet-T by 0.41 AP_box (52.21 vs 51.8) and SLaK-T by 0.91 AP_box (52.21 vs 51.3), while SW-small trails UniRepLKNet-S slightly (52.7 vs 53.0 AP_box). On ADE20K segmentation, SW-tiny surpasses UniRepLKNet-T by 0.62 mIoU (49.22 vs 48.6) and SLaK-T by 1.62 mIoU (49.22 vs 47.6), but SW-small falls short of UniRepLKNet-S (49.79 vs 50.5 mIoU SS) and InternImage-S (49.79 vs 50.1). Against transformer baselines, SW-tiny exceeds SwinV2-T/8 by 1.6% (83.4 vs 81.8), FastViT-SA24 by 0.8%, and CoAtNet-0 by 1.8% on ImageNet, and on ADE20K SW-tiny leads Swin-T by 4.72 mIoU (49.22 vs 44.5). On nuScenes 3D detection, FCOS3D-SW-small achieves 31.42 mAP, narrowly beating FCOS3D-UniRepLKNet-S (31.38) and substantially outperforming FCOS3D-ResNet101 w/DCN (29.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on SLaK's large M×N strip convolution architecture and ConvNeXt's four-stage backbone, but departs fundamentally from the 'bigger kernel is better' paradigm that has driven recent large-kernel CNN research (RepLKNet, SLaK, UniRepLKNet, ParCNetV2, PeLK). The key conceptual departure is a decoupling argument: the paper argues that large kernels' effectiveness stems from two separable factors—feature extraction at a fixed granularity and multi-path feature fusion—rather than from kernel size per se. By replacing SLaK's 51×5 strip convolutions with 3×3 group convolutions followed by a shift-based multi-path fusion operator, the paper repositions the shift operation (originally a zero-cost alternative to convolution) as a connectivity mechanism, drawing on reparameterization (RepVGG, RepLKNet), ghost-style channel slimming (GhostNet), and coarse-grained sparse pruning (SLaK).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that standard 3×3 convolutions can replace 51×51 strip convolutions while matching or exceeding their performance—challenges a core assumption in the recent large-kernel CNN literature and echoes VGG's original small-kernel philosophy in a modern context. However, the practical gains are modest (e.g., 83.4% vs. 82.5% for tiny models on ImageNet; parity at the small scale), and the method's reliance on a specific shift-plus-group-convolution construction limits immediate generalizability. Open questions include whether the decoupling principle (granularity vs. connectivity) transfers to other architectures or modalities, whether the data-driven sparsity patterns observed in Section 5 can be exploited for further architectural design, and whether the shift operation's computational advantages persist under modern hardware where large-kernel convolutions are increasingly well-optimized.
