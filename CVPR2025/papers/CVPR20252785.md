## Problem

Hyperspectral imaging systems face an inherent three-way trade-off among temporal resolution, spectral resolution, and data bandwidth, with no existing approach achieving all three simultaneously. While event cameras offer microsecond temporal resolution and sparse data representation, their use for hyperspectral imaging remains unexplored due to the challenge of encoding spectral information into asynchronous temporal events and the ill-posedness of reconstructing spectra from sparse, noisy measurements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252785/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sparse, asynchronous event stream (pixel coordinates, polarity, microsecond timestamps) from an IMX636 event camera, synchronized with a calibrated 'sweeping rainbow' illumination pattern produced by a rotating mirror array and blazed grating. It outputs a 72-channel hyperspectral image spanning 400–760 nm at 5 nm intervals per pixel, rendered as sRGB for visualization. The system operates at up to 30 FPS for static scenes and 10 FPS for dynamic scenes, achieving 59.53% bandwidth reduction relative to full-bandwidth frame-based capture.

## Method

The key insight is that a synchronized rotating mirror array and blazed grating create a deterministic 'sweeping rainbow' illumination that maps wavelength to time, so each pair of consecutive events at a pixel yields a 'null spectrum vector' orthogonal to the unknown reflectance spectrum. A constant light source added via a beam splitter augments these vectors to resolve absolute intensity ambiguity, converting the problem into finding the null-space vector of a constraint matrix. The reconstruction is solved as a constrained quadratic program combining the least-squares null-space objective with three regularizers: non-negativity constraints (eliminating unphysical negative spectra), an anti-drift L2 penalty suppressing intensity hallucinations in long event gaps, and a cosine-basis spectral smoothness term for underdetermined pixels. SVD provides the initial spectral estimate, which is then refined by the QP solver to enforce all physical and smoothness constraints.

## Results

On the high-frequency rainbow pattern, the proposed method achieves SAM 23.27, PSNR 24.61, and SSIM 0.40, outperforming the bandwidth-matched frame-based baseline (PSNR 18.73, SSIM 0.14) and the Parkkinen basis method (SAM 25.97, PSNR 23.79) in spectral accuracy. On the ColorChecker chart, the system achieves an average SAM of 21.05 and PSNR of 28.46 across all 24 patches. The system achieves a 59.53% reduction in data bandwidth compared to full-bandwidth frame-based capture while maintaining comparable spectral reconstruction quality, and enables real-time dynamic scene capture at up to 30 FPS (10 FPS for hyperspectral video at 600 rpm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Parkkinen basis method [38], the proposed system achieves lower SAM (23.27 vs. 25.97) and higher PSNR (24.61 vs. 23.79), indicating better spectral accuracy on sharp spectral transitions, though the basis method yields higher SSIM (0.68 vs. 0.40) likely due to its inherent smoothing. Against the bandwidth-matched frame-based method, the proposed approach clearly dominates on all metrics (PSNR 24.61 vs. 18.73; SSIM 0.40 vs. 0.14), demonstrating that event-based encoding captures spectral information far more efficiently than simply reducing frame count under the same bandwidth budget. The paper also compares against CASSI-based methods [25, 58], reporting that the event-based approach achieves superior event efficiency, though detailed quantitative comparisons are deferred to the supplementary material. Overall, the proposed method wins on spectral accuracy (SAM, PSNR) against both baselines while using only 40.47% of the data bandwidth of the full frame-based system.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining active dispersive illumination (rotating mirror array + blazed grating) with event-camera sensing for hyperspectral imaging, a combination the authors explicitly identify as unexplored. Conceptually, it draws on two separate lineages: active illumination strategies that encode spectral information in structured light patterns (Park et al., Shin et al.) and event-based vision that exploits microsecond temporal resolution for high-speed imaging (deblurring, HDR, 3D). The key departure is the mathematical reframing: rather than treating events as noisy samples of a frame, the authors derive 'null spectrum vectors' from consecutive event pairs, converting spectral reconstruction into a geometric constraint optimization problem solved via SVD and quadratic programming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The system demonstrates that the long-standing spectral-resolution vs. temporal-resolution vs. bandwidth trade-off in hyperspectral imaging can be partially broken by co-designing optical hardware with event-camera sensing, achieving 59.53% bandwidth reduction at 30 FPS (static) / 10 FPS (dynamic) with unconstrained spectral resolution. However, absolute reconstruction quality remains moderate (SAM ~21, PSNR ~28 on ColorChecker), and the system does not model inter-reflection, sub-surface scattering, or fluorescence, which limits applicability to complex materials. Open questions include whether the null-spectrum-vector framework can be extended to handle these physical effects, whether deep learning can accelerate the per-pixel QP solver, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the visible spectrum.
