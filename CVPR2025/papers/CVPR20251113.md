## Problem

State-of-the-art one-stage multi-person 3D mesh estimation methods (e.g., Multi-HMR, AiOS) rely on uniformly high-resolution inputs to achieve accurate results, but this incurs prohibitive computational cost (~5 FPS), precluding real-time use. The paper identifies that high resolution primarily benefits small-scale individuals (children, distant subjects) while offering marginal gains for large-scale ones, making uniform high-resolution processing wasteful.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251113/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to a base resolution of 644 (max dimension) with adaptive high-resolution regions up to 1288, denoted as '644*'. It outputs SMPL parameters (24-joint pose, 10-dim shape, 3D translation) for up to 50 detected individuals, along with bounding boxes and confidence scores. The mixed-resolution input is the key distinction: rather than a single fixed resolution, the network internally allocates higher-resolution tokens only where needed.

## Method

SAT-HMR extends a DETR-style pipeline (DINOv2-pretrained ViT-B encoder, 6-layer decoder with 50 human queries, MLP prediction heads) by introducing scale-adaptive tokens in the encoder. A shallow 3-layer transformer processes low-resolution patches and a scale head predicts a patch-level scale map that classifies each patch as background, small-scale, or large-scale. Small-scale patches are replaced by their high-resolution counterparts (4× more tokens from the 1288 image), background tokens are spatially pooled 4:1 to reduce redundancy, and large-scale tokens remain at low resolution. The resulting heterogeneous token set is processed by a 9-layer transformer encoder before decoding. Training uses Hungarian matching with a composite loss including focal loss for the scale map, L1 losses for pose/shape/joints, GIoU for boxes, and focal loss for detection confidence.

## Results

On the AGORA test set, SAT-HMR achieves 63.3 mm MVE and 67.9 mm MPJPE with a base resolution of 644 (adaptively up to 1288), matching the F1-Score (0.95) of Multi-HMR at 1288 resolution while using only 133.1G MACs vs. 6104.6G (46× fewer) and running at 42 ms (≈24 FPS) vs. 231.7 ms. On 3DPW, it achieves 73.7 mm MVE, outperforming Multi-HMR (75.9 mm) at lower resolution. On MuPoTS, it reaches 89.0% PCK (All) and 90.1% (Matched), surpassing Multi-HMR (85.0%/89.3%). On CMU Panoptic, it achieves 84.2 mm average MPJPE, a 21.5 mm improvement over the next-best method (PSVT, 105.7 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest SOTA baselines, SAT-HMR trades a small accuracy gap for a large efficiency gain: on AGORA it is 2.2 mm worse in MVE than Multi-HMR at 1288 (63.3 vs. 61.1 mm) and 5.8 mm worse than AiOS (63.3 vs. 57.5 mm), but uses 46× fewer MACs than Multi-HMR and 9.6× fewer than AiOS, running at 24 FPS versus ~4–5 FPS. Against the lower-resolution Multi-HMR (896), SAT-HMR is clearly superior in both accuracy (63.3 vs. 77.6 mm MVE) and speed (42 ms vs. 97.4 ms). Compared to earlier one-stage methods (ROMP, BEV, PSVT at 512), SAT-HMR reduces MVE by 31–40 mm while maintaining comparable or faster inference. On 3DPW, SAT-HMR edges out Multi-HMR (73.7 vs. 75.9 mm MVE) and substantially outperforms PSVT (84.9 mm) and BEV (92.3 mm). On MuPoTS, it achieves 89.0% PCK (All), beating Multi-HMR by 4 points. On CMU Panoptic, it leads all baselines by a wide margin (84.2 mm avg. MPJPE vs. 105.7 mm for PSVT, the next best), demonstrating strong generalization to in-the-wild social interaction scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR-style one-stage pipeline for multi-person 3D mesh estimation established by Multi-HMR and AiOS, which rely on uniformly high-resolution inputs to achieve SOTA accuracy. It departs from this uniform-resolution paradigm by introducing scale-adaptive tokens that dynamically allocate higher resolution to small-scale individuals and distill background regions, drawing conceptually from efficient ViT techniques (token dropping, merging, mixed-resolution tokenization) but applying them in a human-scale-aware manner. The encoder is initialized with DINOv2 pretrained features, and the overall architecture (encoder-decoder with Hungarian matching) follows the DETR lineage, with the novelty concentrated in the token representation stage rather than the decoder or prediction heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 24 FPS inference while matching the accuracy of methods running at ~5 FPS, this work makes high-quality multi-person 3D mesh estimation practical for real-time applications such as interactive systems, AR/VR, and video analysis. The empirical finding that resolution gains are concentrated in small-scale individuals (up to 35mm MVE reduction for 0–10% scale) provides a clear, actionable principle for future efficiency-oriented designs. Limitations include the absence of height-aware scale estimation (which can cause depth errors) and the restriction to body-only SMPL rather than whole-body SMPL-X, both of which are noted as future directions. An open question is whether the scale-adaptive token strategy generalizes to other parametric models or to video-based estimation where temporal consistency adds constraints.
