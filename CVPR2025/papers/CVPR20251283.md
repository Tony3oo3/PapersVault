## Problem

Standard flow matching and diffusion models learn a mapping from Gaussian noise to the target distribution, requiring a conditioning mechanism (e.g., cross-attention) for cross-modal tasks like text-to-image. However, flow matching is not theoretically constrained to use noise as the source, raising the question of whether one can directly evolve from one modality's distribution to another, eliminating both the noise distribution and the conditioning mechanism entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251283/cropped_page0_idx10.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image, a text prompt is encoded by a language model (CLIP, T5, or Llama3) into token embeddings (e.g., 77×768 for CLIP), which a Text Variational Encoder compresses into a spatial latent (4×32×32) matching the image latent shape; the model then evolves this latent into an image latent decoded at 256×256 or 512×512. For other tasks, the same framework maps RGB images to text latents (captioning), RGB images to depth maps (pixel space), or upsampled 64×64 images to 256×256 high-resolution images. Critically, no external conditioning signal or noise is injected at inference—the source modality itself drives the evolution.

## Method

CrossFlow replaces the Gaussian noise source with a Variational Encoder (VE) that maps the source modality into a regularized latent distribution of the same shape as the target, trained with a contrastive loss (image-text CLIP loss) rather than reconstruction to preserve semantic structure. A vanilla transformer (DiMR backbone with self-attention only, no cross-attention) is trained via flow matching to predict the velocity field evolving the source latent to the target latent. To enable classifier-free guidance without an explicit conditioning input, the authors introduce a binary indicator 1_c ∈ {0,1} concatenated to the transformer tokens: when 1_c=1 the model maps z_0 to a specific semantic region of the target manifold, and when 1_c=0 it maps to the entire manifold, allowing standard CFG extrapolation at inference. The VE and flow matching model are jointly trained with a combined loss (FM MSE + encoding loss + KL divergence), and the framework requires no task-specific architectural changes across text-to-image, captioning, depth estimation, and super-resolution.

## Results

On COCO zero-shot FID-30K, CrossFlow (0.95B) achieves 9.63, and with sin-cos matching improves to 8.95, while scoring 0.57 on GenEval. Compared to the standard flow matching baseline with cross-attention (same training budget), CrossFlow reduces FID from 10.79 to 10.13 at 300K steps. On secondary tasks, CrossFlow achieves FID 3.0 / PSNR 25.6 on ImageNet super-resolution (beating Flow Matching's 3.4 / 24.7), AbsRel 0.053 on KITTI depth estimation, and B@4 36.4 on COCO captioning, all without task-specific architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossFlow (Sin-Cos) at 0.95B parameters achieves FID-30K of 8.95, outperforming DALL-E 2 (10.39), LDM v1.5 (9.62), GLIDE (12.24), LDM (12.63), and DALL-E (27.50), but trailing Imagen (7.27), PixArt-α (7.32), and RAPHAEL (6.61). On GenEval, CrossFlow (Sin-Cos) scores 0.57, beating DALL-E 2 (0.52), PixArt-α (0.48), and LDM v1.5 (0.43), though LDM v3 at 8B parameters leads with 0.68. Against the directly comparable standard flow matching baseline (same data, budget, and architecture family), CrossFlow reduces FID from 10.79 to 10.13 at 300K steps and shows stronger scaling with both model size (70M→1B) and training iterations. On super-resolution, CrossFlow beats both standard Flow Matching (FID 3.4→3.0) and SR3 (FID 5.2→3.0) while matching PSNR. For depth estimation, CrossFlow (AbsRel 0.053 KITTI / 0.094 NYUv2) is competitive with DiffusionDepth (0.050/0.085) and BinsFormer (0.052/0.094) without task-specific modules. For captioning, CrossFlow (B@4 36.4) is close to SCD-Net (37.3) and outperforms MNIC, MIR, NAIC-CMAL, and SATIC, while the inverted T2I model (CrossFlow-T2I) reaches B@4 33.1 with no additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends flow matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2022) by replacing the standard Gaussian noise source distribution with a variational encoding of the conditioning modality, enabling direct cross-modal evolution without cross-attention. Prior work on bridging arbitrary distributions (InterFlow, Schrödinger Bridge, α-blending) was restricted to same-domain pairs (faces-to-faces, sketches-to-images); CrossFlow is the first to apply this principle to genuinely cross-modal settings like text-to-image. The classifier-free guidance mechanism is adapted from Ho & Salimans (2022) via a binary indicator that replaces the explicit conditioning input, and the backbone is a vanilla DiMR transformer without the cross-attention layers standard in T2I models like LDM and Movie Gen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a cross-attention-free architecture can match or slightly exceed standard flow matching with cross-attention at scale challenges a core architectural assumption in T2I generation and suggests a simpler, more unified framework for cross-modal tasks. The latent arithmetic capability (addition/subtraction in text latent space yielding semantic image edits) and the bi-directional property (inverting T2I for captioning) are practical bonuses that standard noise-based models cannot offer. However, the paper is limited to 256×256 and 512×512 resolutions, the FID gains over the direct baseline are modest (~0.7), and it remains to be seen whether the paradigm scales to video, 3D, or higher resolutions where cross-attention may still provide inductive bias advantages at smaller model sizes.
