## Problem

M-P model-based neural networks lock their structure during training, causing over-fitting and over-parameterization. Existing remedies—empirical pruning, NAS, and dynamic neural networks—suffer from unstable performance, high computational cost, or accuracy loss from threshold-based connection removal. The A-N model (AstroNet) improves biological plausibility but still relies on a fixed pruning threshold, limiting its ability to discover optimal neuron subsets and connection combinations.

## Input / Output

GliaNet takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 or 384×384 for ImageNet, variable resolution for COCO) and wraps around a conventional neural module (ResNet, WRN, DenseNet, ViT, or Swin) as the backbone. Depending on the downstream task, it outputs class labels for classification, pixel-level instance masks for segmentation, or bounding boxes with class scores for object detection. The Glia module itself introduces no new input channels; it operates on the internal neuron states and connection weights of the wrapped module.

## Method

The core contribution is the Glia-Neuron (G-N) model, where each Glia unit pairs an Oligodendrocyte (Oli) that adaptively selects 'winning neurons' from the pre-neuron pool with an Astrocyte (Ast) that modulates the connections of those selected neurons—replacing empirical threshold pruning with learned, data-driven selection. The model is formulated at three levels: instance (single Oli-Ast pair), group (M=4 Glia units each handling a subset of pre-neurons), and interaction (temporal memory via Q=3-layer LSTMs capturing optimization history, plus spatial collaboration via a chain optimization strategy across Glia units). An integration function H() compresses large neuron-state and weight matrices using average pooling followed by a lightweight ViT to handle high-dimensional inputs. Training alternates between optimizing the neural module on the training set and the Glia module on a small validation set, with a contrastive regularization term encouraging consistent representations across Glia units.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, GliaNet achieves 96.90% top-1 accuracy with RNT18 (6.95M params), 97.26% with RNT50 (7.34M params), and 97.42% with WRN50 (38.26M params), outperforming all baselines while reducing parameters by 37–71%. On ImageNet-1k, GliaNet (RNT50) reaches 79.15% top-1 accuracy with only 8.15M parameters, surpassing the full RNT50 (75.30%, 26.11M) by 3.85%. On COCO, the pre-trained GliaNet backbone yields AP improvements of 0.8–1.0% for segmentation and 0.7–1.1% for object detection over the second-best method (MA-Net).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GliaNet consistently outperforms all A-N model-based baselines: on CIFAR-10 it beats CDS by 0.40%, AstroNet by 0.39%, and MA-Net by 0.14% (average across RNT18/50/WRN50), while on ImageNet-1k the margins widen to +0.62%, +0.60%, and +0.16% respectively. Against the SOTA dynamic network method Boost-DyNN [52], GliaNet (RNT50) achieves 79.15% vs. 76.05% on ImageNet-1k with fewer parameters (8.15M vs. 11.38M) and faster inference (7 ms vs. 11 ms). Compared to NAS methods, GliaNet (DNT-BC) reaches 97.82% on CIFAR-10, surpassing NAS-LID (97.52%) and HOTNAS (97.54%) while requiring only 0.70 GPU days of search time versus 2.30 and 3.40 respectively. For vision transformers, GliaNet improves ViT-B from 77.90% to 79.52% and Swin-B from 84.50% to 85.70% on ImageNet-1k, while reducing parameters by 18.31% and 10.93% respectively. In transfer learning on CIFAR-100, pre-trained GliaNet (ViT-B) achieves 88.05% vs. 87.13% for standard ViT-B, confirming superior transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own line of work on biologically-inspired neural network units, specifically the Astrocyte-Neuron (A-N) model introduced in AstroNet (CVPR 2023) and MA-Net (AAAI 2024). The key conceptual departure is the addition of Oligodendrocytes (Oli) alongside Astrocytes (Ast) to form a complete Glia unit, replacing the empirical threshold-based pruning of the A-N model with an adaptive, learned selection of 'winning neurons.' The formulation is further expanded from a single Glia unit to a group of M units with temporal (LSTM-based) and inter-unit interaction mechanisms, positioning the work as a more complete emulation of mammalian neural communication than prior A-N models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that adaptive structure learning inspired by glial biology can simultaneously reduce parameters by 37–71% and improve accuracy across classification, segmentation, and detection, offering a biologically-motivated alternative to NAS and DyNN approaches. However, the gains over the closest prior method (MA-Net) are modest (0.14–0.16% on classification), and the method is applied as a wrapper around existing backbones rather than a standalone architecture, limiting its immediate practical adoption. Open questions include whether the biological fidelity of the Oli-Ast interaction translates to meaningful advantages at scale, whether the LSTM-based temporal mechanism is truly necessary versus simpler alternatives, and how the method behaves on very large or non-visual tasks.
