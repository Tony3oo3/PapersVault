## Problem

Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation models suffer significant performance degradation under domain shifts, yet existing domain adaptation methods are typically slow, require source data access, target a single domain, and lack explainability. No prior work has addressed training-free, test-time adaptation specifically for the open-vocabulary setting where the label space is unbounded and defined by text queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252242/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes a single RGB image and a set of text queries defining the target vocabulary. The image is encoded by CLIP to produce an embedding that serves as a retrieval query against a pre-built library of LoRA adapters indexed by domain centroid embeddings. The output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask over the queried vocabulary. Adaptation is performed per-image: each test image receives its own uniquely fused adapter, making the process effectively real-time and requiring no gradient updates.

## Method

SemLA constructs an offline library of LoRA adapters, each fine-tuned on a specific domain and indexed by the CLIP centroid of that domain's training images. At test time, the CLIP embedding of the input image is compared against all library centroids; the top-K nearest adapters are selected and weighted via a softmax over distance reciprocals (with temperature τ). The selected adapters are merged by scaling each A-matrix by its weight and concatenating the B-matrices, producing a single fused low-rank update applied to the frozen backbone (CAT-Seg or SED). This concatenation-based fusion preserves the individual adapter contributions while integrating multi-domain knowledge, and the entire retrieval-and-merge pipeline is training-free, backbone-agnostic, and scales linearly with library size.

## Results

On the 20-domain leave-one-out benchmark with CAT-Seg backbone, SemLA achieves a harmonic mean mIoU of 54.16, outperforming the zero-shot baseline (39.39) by +14.77 and the uniform LoRA merging baseline [35] (51.89) by +2.27. The method approaches the Oracle upper bound (61.05) and even surpasses it on individual domains such as ACDC-fog (71.92 vs. 69.98) and ACDC-night (51.73 vs. 51.55). With the SED backbone, SemLA similarly achieves 51.82 h-mean mIoU, beating zero-shot SED (35.56) and uniform merging (48.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemLA outperforms the zero-shot CAT-Seg baseline [10] by a large margin (+14.77 h-mean mIoU, 54.16 vs. 39.39), confirming that domain-specific adapter retrieval and fusion is far more effective than relying solely on the pre-trained model. Against the uniform LoRA merging baseline [35] (which averages all adapters without domain awareness), SemLA gains +2.27 h-mean (54.16 vs. 51.89), demonstrating that CLIP-guided proximity-based weighting of a small subset of adapters (K=7) is more effective than indiscriminately merging all 19 available adapters. The late-fusion variants (both Uniform and SemLA) underperform their parameter-level counterparts despite higher computational cost, indicating that adjusting internal representations via LoRA merging is more beneficial than averaging output logits. On the SED backbone (Table 3), SemLA again clearly wins: 51.82 h-mean vs. 35.56 (zero-shot SED) and 48.60 (uniform merging), confirming backbone-agnostic effectiveness. Notably, in the all-inclusive setting (Table 2), SemLA with low temperature (τ=0.005) nearly matches the Oracle (60.57 vs. 61.05), while with higher temperature (τ=0.05) it surpasses the Oracle on 14 out of 20 domains, showing that adapter synergies can exceed single-domain fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemLA sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) and model merging, repurposing the concatenation-based LoRA merging from PEFT [42] for domain adaptation rather than multi-task composition. It departs from classical unsupervised domain adaptation (which requires source data and targets a single domain) and from online adaptation (which is slow and lacks explainability) by introducing a training-free, retrieval-based test-time approach. The closest prior work is LoraRetriever [84], which also retrieves and composes LoRA adapters, but uses instruction fine-tuning to train a dedicated retriever; SemLA instead leverages CLIP's pre-trained semantic space as a zero-shot domain navigator, avoiding any additional training. The uniform merging baseline [35] demonstrates that linear mode connectivity in LoRA weight space is effective, but SemLA shows that proximity-weighted selection of a small subset of adapters substantially outperforms uniform averaging of all available adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free test-time adaptation method for open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, and the 20-domain benchmark (spanning driving, weather, and general-purpose scenes with vastly different label sets) provides a rigorous stress test that prior adaptation literature has lacked. The framework's explainability (tracking which adapters contribute) and data-privacy guarantee (no source data access at test time) make it attractive for sensitive deployment scenarios. Key open questions include: how to handle vocabulary misalignment between source and target domains, how to automatically discover and fill gaps in the adapter library, and whether the approach extends to multi-task settings like panoptic segmentation or depth estimation. The finding that merged adapters can occasionally outperform the single-domain Oracle (14/20 domains in the all-inclusive setting) suggests that LoRA synergies capture complementary domain knowledge, but this result is in a non-standard setting where the target adapter is available.
