## Problem

Dataset distillation methods degrade significantly at high IPC (images-per-class) settings, sometimes underperforming random selection. The current combination-based paradigm (SelMatch) mitigates this by merging distilled and real images, but its one-shot, independent selection of real data creates an incompatibility issue: the selected real images are not adapted to the distilled set, reducing their complementary effect and capping performance well below full-dataset training.

## Input / Output

The method takes the original training dataset (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet), pre-calculated Forgetting difficulty scores, and a distillation algorithm (CDA by default) as inputs. It produces a synthetic dataset of target size IPC×C that combines α·IPC distilled images per class with (1−α)·IPC real images per class, where the real images are selected progressively across J curriculum phases. The filter model (ResNet-18) is trained from scratch on the current synthetic dataset in each phase, and previously selected samples are excluded from subsequent selections to ensure non-redundant coverage.

## Method

CCFS structures real-data selection as a curriculum learning problem: starting from the already-distilled dataset, it iteratively incorporates real samples in J phases (default 3), each time selecting based on the current synthetic dataset rather than a fixed initialization. Within each phase, a two-step coarse-to-fine strategy operates: the coarse stage trains a filter model on the current synthetic set and evaluates it on the original dataset to identify misclassified samples (reflecting unlearned patterns), while the fine stage selects the simplest misclassified samples per class using pre-calculated Forgetting difficulty scores. This design ensures that each curriculum phase introduces features the current synthetic set cannot yet capture, while the difficulty-based ranking avoids introducing overly complex samples that would hinder training. The progressive nature means the filter's classification boundary rises across phases, so the lower bound of selected sample difficulty increases, guaranteeing monotonic enrichment of the synthetic dataset.

## Results

CCFS achieves top-1 test accuracies of 92.5% on CIFAR-10 (10% compression), 71.5% on CIFAR-100 (10% compression), and 60.2% on Tiny-ImageNet (20% compression), surpassing the previous SOTA (SelMatch) by +6.6%, +5.8%, and +3.4% respectively. On Tiny-ImageNet at 20% compression, CCFS reaches 60.2% accuracy, only 0.3% below full-dataset training (60.5%). Across all compression ratios from 5% to 30%, CCFS consistently outperforms all baselines, with the gap widening as IPC increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCFS outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and compression ratio where comparisons are available. Against the closest SOTA competitor SelMatch [19], CCFS gains +5.1% on CIFAR-10 (10%), +17.0% on CIFAR-100 (10%), and +9.8% on Tiny-ImageNet (20%). The improvement over CDA [36] (the distillation backbone used by CCFS) is +8.1% on CIFAR-10 (10%) and +7.7% on CIFAR-100 (10%), confirming that the curriculum selection framework adds substantial value on top of the distillation step. Among coreset selection baselines, CCFS's selection-only variant (self-evolved selection) beats Oracle window [19] by +1.2% on CIFAR-10 (10%) and +3.4% on CIFAR-100 (20%), while Forgetting [30] and Glister [13] are far behind (e.g., 41.5% vs. 92.5% on CIFAR-10 at 10%). CUDD [24] is the strongest recent competitor on CIFAR-100 (65.7% at 10%), but CCFS still leads by +5.8%. On Tiny-ImageNet at 20% compression, CCFS (60.2%) surpasses CUDD (56.8%) by +3.4% and CDA (52.4%) by +7.8%, approaching the full-dataset accuracy of 60.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the combination-based dataset distillation paradigm of SelMatch, which merges a distilled image set with a real image set to address the known degradation of pure distillation at high IPC. It departs from SelMatch's one-shot, independent sliding-window selection by arguing that the fixed ordering (select real data first, then distill) creates an incompatibility between the two subsets. The curriculum learning framework draws on prior applications in dataset distillation (SeqMatch, CDA, CUDD), but CCFS is the first to structure the *real-data selection* itself as a multi-phase curriculum that is explicitly conditioned on the evolving synthetic dataset, combined with a coarse-to-fine filtering strategy based on a trained filter model and pre-computed difficulty scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-closure of the gap to full-dataset training (0.3% on Tiny-ImageNet at 20% compression) demonstrates that combination-based distillation can be made highly effective at practical compression ratios, which is relevant for privacy-sensitive or compute-constrained settings. The identification of the incompatibility issue provides a useful diagnostic for why prior combination methods underperform, and the curriculum framework is a modular plug-in that can be paired with different distillation backbones (demonstrated with CDA and MTT). Open questions include scalability to ImageNet-scale datasets, the computational overhead of training a filter model per curriculum phase, and whether the approach extends to non-image modalities or generative distillation settings.
