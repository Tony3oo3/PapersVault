## Problem

Existing self-supervised sound source localization methods represent an image with a single local feature and an audio with a global feature for contrastive learning. The global audio embedding is contaminated by noise and irrelevant sounds, while the single local image feature causes the model to localize only a small discriminative part of the sound source rather than its full extent. This paper addresses both limitations by jointly decomposing image and audio features into target and off-target representations before contrastive learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a 5-second audio clip converted to a log-mel spectrogram (257 frequency bins, 16 kHz) as input. The image encoder (ResNet-18) produces a 7×7×512 feature map, and the audio encoder (ResNet-18 with frequency-axis max-pooling) produces a 16×512 feature sequence. At inference, the cross-modal attention map between the audio target slot and image features is interpolated to the original image resolution and thresholded at a hyperparameter θ to yield a binary localization mask; the same target slot representations are also used for cross-modal retrieval by ranking cosine similarities across a category-labeled test set.

## Method

The core mechanism is Joint Slot Attention (JSA), which uses two learnable slots (a target slot and an off-target slot) shared across both modalities to iteratively decompose image and audio features via competitive attention with GRU-based slot updates over 5 iterations. Contrastive learning (InfoNCE) is applied only to the target slots, so the audio target slot captures the relevant sound while the image target slot covers the full source region rather than a single patch. A cross-modal attention matching loss provides pseudo-supervision by forcing the cross-modal attention map (audio target query → image keys) to approximate the intra-modal attention map (image target query → image keys), applied symmetrically to both modalities. Additional losses include slot divergence (pushing target and off-target slots apart) and slot reconstruction (auxiliary MLP decoders). At inference, an Image-Query based Refinement (IQR) step blends the cross-modal and intra-modal attention maps with a weighting α to refine the localization mask without external priors.

## Results

On the largest training setting (VGGSound-144k), the method achieves 40.62 cIoU / 41.66 AUC on VGG-SS and 83.60 cIoU / 64.68 AUC on Flickr-SoundNet-Test without object-guided localization (OGL), and 42.56 cIoU / 42.46 AUC (VGG-SS) and 84.80 cIoU / 64.82 AUC (Flickr) with OGL. On AVSBench (zero-shot), it reaches 31.33 mIoU and 44.30 F-score. In cross-modal retrieval on VGG-SS, it achieves R@1 of 30.61 (audio→image) and 31.70 (image→audio), substantially exceeding the prior best of 16.47 and 20.09 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines on VGG-SS AUC in every training setting, with the largest gap over FNAC [33] (41.66 vs 39.66 AUC without OGL; 42.46 vs 40.80 with OGL) and Alignment [31] (41.66 vs 40.02; 42.46 vs 41.48). On Flickr-SoundNet-Test cIoU, FNAC [33] narrowly edges out the proposed method in two settings (84.73 vs 83.60 without OGL; 85.14 vs 84.80 with OGL), but the proposed method wins on Flickr AUC in all cases. The advantage is most dramatic in cross-modal retrieval on VGG-SS, where R@1 improves by 16.3 percentage points over Alignment [31] (30.61 vs 16.47 audio→image) and 13.7 points (31.70 vs 20.09 image→audio), indicating the model learns genuine audio-visual correspondence rather than mere saliency. On AVSBench, the method surpasses Alignment [31] by 2.03%p mIoU and 8.7%p F-score in a zero-shot setting. On the generalization benchmarks (Unheard 110), the method achieves the best cIoU (43.11) and AUC (42.04) with OGL, outperforming FNAC [33] (42.91 / 41.17) and Alignment [31] (42.94 / 41.54).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes slot attention (Locatello et al., NeurIPS 2020), originally designed for unsupervised object-centric decomposition, as a joint cross-modal feature decomposition mechanism for sound source localization. It departs from the dominant SSL paradigm of contrasting a single most-relevant image patch against a global audio embedding, instead decomposing both modalities into target and off-target slots and performing contrastive learning exclusively on target representations. The cross-modal attention matching loss is a novel training signal that has no direct analogue in prior SSL work, providing pseudo-supervision for the localization attention map via intra-modal attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantially improved cross-modal retrieval (16.3%p R@1 over the prior best) suggests that prior SSL methods, despite competitive localization scores, were not truly learning audio-visual semantic alignment but rather detecting salient objects likely to produce sound. This distinction matters for downstream applications requiring genuine cross-modal understanding. The binary target/off-target decomposition limits the method to single-source scenarios, and the authors explicitly identify multi-source localization as future work. The reliance on a single architecture (ResNet-18) and two training dataset sizes leaves open questions about scalability to larger backbones and more complex scenes.
