## Problem

BEV feature maps produced by existing BEV encoders are inherently noisy due to sensor imperfections and the indirect optimization of BEV representations for downstream tasks, yet this noise has remained largely unaddressed. Prior diffusion-based approaches (DiffBEV, DiffUSER) condition on noisy features themselves and require multiple inference passes, making them less effective and computationally expensive for latency-critical autonomous driving deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251478/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

BEVDiffuser takes a noisy BEV feature map (e.g., 50×50, 200×200, or 180×180 spatial resolution depending on the source BEV model) and a ground-truth object layout consisting of category IDs and normalized 3D bounding boxes (position, size, orientation, velocity) for up to n objects, padded with empty objects when fewer are present. It outputs a denoised BEV feature map of the same spatial dimensions, which is then used as a proxy ground-truth to supervise retraining of the BEV encoder via MSE loss. At inference time, BEVDiffuser is entirely removed, so the deployed model produces standard BEV feature maps with no additional I/O.

## Method

BEVDiffuser is a U-Net (built on the LayoutDiffusion architecture) that predicts the clean BEV directly from a noisy BEV, conditioned on the ground-truth object layout via a transformer-based layout fusion module for global conditioning and an object-aware cross-attention mechanism for local conditioning. Because no ground-truth BEV exists, training adds diffusion noise to the BEV encoder's own prediction (x_t0) and optimizes f_θ to recover x_t0 rather than a true x_0, using a joint loss combining a diffusion loss (MSE between x_t0 and f_θ output) and a task-specific loss from attached detection heads. Sampling employs classifier-free guidance, interpolating between the layout-conditioned and empty-layout (unconditional) predictions to sharpen the denoised output. The plug-and-play mechanism works by running K=5 denoising steps on the existing BEV encoder's output and using the result as supervision to retrain the BEV model from scratch with an additional MSE loss on the BEV feature map, after which BEVDiffuser is discarded, leaving an architecturally identical but improved model with zero inference overhead.

## Results

On the nuScenes val dataset, BEVDiffuser-enhanced BEVFormer-tiny achieves 39.1 NDS and 28.3 mAP, representing improvements of 10.1% and 12.3% over the baseline (35.5 NDS, 25.2 mAP). BEVFormerV2-base sees the largest relative gains at 8.8% NDS and 13.5% mAP (41.1→44.7 NDS, 32.7→37.1 mAP). Even for the strongest baseline, BEVFusion (70.9 NDS, 67.6 mAP), BEVDiffuser still yields consistent gains (71.9 NDS, 69.2 mAP) without any additional inference-time cost, as the diffusion model is removed at deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEVDiffuser is evaluated as a plug-and-play enhancement on four BEV baselines: BEVFormer-tiny, BEVFormer-base, BEVFormerV2-base, and BEVFusion. The largest absolute gains appear on BEVFormer-tiny (+3.6 NDS, +3.1 mAP) and BEVFormerV2-base (+3.6 NDS, +4.4 mAP), where the baseline BEV feature maps are noisier and the ground-truth layout guidance provides stronger corrective signal. For the already-strong BEVFormer-base and BEVFusion, gains are smaller but still consistent across all metrics (+1.9/+1.3 NDS/mAP and +1.0/+1.6 NDS/mAP respectively). BEVDiffuser wins on every metric for every baseline, with particularly notable improvements in orientation error (mAOE) and velocity error (mAVE), suggesting the denoised BEV features better encode object geometry and motion. The paper also reports that BEVDiffuser substantially improves long-tail object detection (e.g., +24.1% mAP for construction vehicles on BEVFormer-tiny) and night-scenario performance (+20.0% mAP for BEVFormer-tiny), further confirming its denoising benefit under challenging conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEVDiffuser builds directly on LayoutDiffusion [39] for its U-Net architecture, adopting its transformer-based layout fusion module and object-aware cross-attention mechanism to condition denoising on object categories and 3D bounding boxes. It departs from prior diffusion-based BEV enhancement work (DiffBEV [41], DiffUSER [12]) in two key ways: it conditions on ground-truth object layout rather than noisy learned features, and it operates exclusively at training time as a plug-and-play supervision signal rather than requiring multiple inference-time passes through an integrated diffusion model. The classifier-free guidance framework [5] is repurposed to interpolate between layout-conditioned and empty-layout predictions during sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—where BEVDiffuser is entirely removed at deployment, leaving an architecturally unchanged BEV model with no additional inference latency—makes this approach particularly attractive for latency-critical autonomous driving systems where prior diffusion-based methods (DiffBEV, DiffUSER) are impractical. The consistent gains across four heterogeneous BEV architectures (camera-only and multi-modal) and the substantial long-tail improvements (up to 88% mAP on rare classes) suggest the denoising addresses a fundamental limitation of task-only BEV optimization. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond nuScenes and 3D detection to other BEV tasks (map segmentation, planning), whether the ground-truth layout requirement limits applicability in unsupervised or online settings, and whether the generation capability from pure noise can be leveraged for scalable data augmentation of corner cases.
