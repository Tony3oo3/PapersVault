## Problem

Existing bi-temporal change detection and captioning methods rely on a shared-weight image encoder followed by task-specific change extractors, creating two fundamental issues: the image encoder is task-agnostic and cannot effectively attend to changed regions, and the need for separate change extractors per task prevents a unified framework. Moreover, the parameter distribution is heavily skewed toward independent image encoding rather than change extraction, leaving insufficient capacity for the actual change-detection objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250871/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of bi-temporal RGB images (H×W×C), cropped to 256×256 patches, and inserts learnable perception frames (K=1 for BCD/CC, K=3 for SCD, K=2 for BDA) between them along a temporal axis to form a 3D volume of T=K+2 frames. Outputs are task-dependent: pixel-wise change maps at original resolution (binary, semantic with N classes, or damage with N levels) for detection tasks, and a variable-length word sequence for captioning, all produced from the perception frame features extracted by the video encoder.

## Method

Change3D reinterprets bi-temporal change detection as a video modeling problem by stacking the two images with learnable perception frames along the temporal dimension and processing the resulting 3D volume with a pre-trained video encoder (I3D, SlowFast, X3D-L, or UniFormer-XS). The perception frames function analogously to CLS tokens in ViTs, interacting with both bi-temporal images through the video encoder's inter-frame modeling to directly capture differences without any dedicated change extractor. A 1×1 convolution with ReLU augments perception features by integrating differential information from the first and last frame features. For detection tasks, a simple cascade decoder (1×1 conv + 4×4 deconv with skip connections) progressively upsamples multi-layer perception features to full resolution; for captioning, a standard transformer decoder with cross-attention uses the deepest perception feature as keys/values. The video encoder is initialized from Kinetics-400 pre-training while perception frames and decoders are randomly initialized, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with task-appropriate losses (cross-entropy + dice for detection, cross-entropy for captioning).

## Results

Change3D achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four tasks and eight benchmarks. On binary change detection, it reaches 91.82% F1 on LEVIR-CD, 94.56% F1 on WHU-CD, and 78.03% F1 on CLCD, outperforming the previous best (AMTNet) by 1.06, 2.29, and 2.93 points respectively. On building damage assessment (xBD), it attains 79.42% F1_overall, surpassing PCDASNet (77.33%) by 2.09 points. Critically, Change3D uses only 1.54M parameters (9% of AMTNet) and 8.29G FLOPs (34% of AMTNet) for BCD, and 1.60M parameters (6% of PCDASNet) with 11.74G FLOPs (12%) for BDA, while also achieving the fastest inference speed (0.015–0.016 s/sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Change3D outperforms all 27 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. On binary change detection, it surpasses the strongest 2D-based competitor AMTNet [50] by 1.06 F1 on LEVIR-CD, 2.29 F1 on WHU-CD, and 2.93 F1 on CLCD, while using only 9% of its parameters and 34% of its FLOPs. On semantic change detection, it beats Bi-SRNet [15] (the SOTA) by 1.57 F1 on HRSCD and 0.98 F1 on SECOND with just 7% of the parameters and 8% of the FLOPs. On building damage assessment, it exceeds PCDASNet [72] by 2.09 F1_overall and 2.88 F1_cls on xBD with 6% of the parameters. On change captioning, it outperforms SEN [89] (SOTA) by 0.71 BLEU-1 and 0.44 METEOR on LEVIR-CC, and by 8.13 BLEU-1 and 3.39 METEOR on DUBAI-CC, with only 13% of the parameters. Notably, the efficiency gap is dramatic: methods like ChangeFormer [1] (250% params, 822% FLOPs) and PromptCC [48] (1024% params) are far heavier yet underperform Change3D, confirming that the video-modeling paradigm with perception frames is both more accurate and more efficient than the shared-weight image encoder + change extractor paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in bi-temporal change detection, where a shared-weight image encoder processes each image independently and a dedicated change extractor (cross-attention, spatial-channel attention, etc.) captures inter-temporal differences. Instead, it repurposes video understanding architectures (I3D, SlowFast, X3D, UniFormer) by treating the bi-temporal pair as a two-frame 'tiny video' and inserting learnable perception frames that interact with both images through the video encoder's temporal modeling. The perception frame concept is analogous to the CLS token in Vision Transformers but operates along the temporal axis, and the approach eliminates the need for task-specific change extractors entirely, providing a unified framework across binary/semantic change detection, damage assessment, and captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a unified, ultra-light framework (~6–13% parameters, ~8–34% FLOPs) that achieves SOTA across eight benchmarks and four tasks without architectural redesign, which could simplify the change detection pipeline considerably. However, the 'video' consists of only 2–4 frames, a regime far removed from the hundreds-of-frames setting in which the video encoders were pre-trained, raising questions about whether the temporal modeling capacity is truly being leveraged or whether the video encoder is effectively acting as a more expressive 2D encoder with a learnable query. The paper suggests extensions to depth completion and image matting, but it remains open whether this paradigm scales to longer multi-temporal sequences or benefits from video-specific pre-training beyond action recognition.
