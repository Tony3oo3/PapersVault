## Problem

Reconstructing fine-grained deformable surface details (e.g., cloth wrinkles) from monocular RGB video remains unsolved. Existing shape-from-template methods rely on discrete mesh representations, frame-by-frame optimisation, and mesh-based differentiable renderers, which limit resolution adaptivity, propagate errors across frames, and fail to capture high-frequency geometric details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252329/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video sequence from a static calibrated camera, a template mesh corresponding to the first frame, and optional per-frame segmentation masks. It produces a continuous neural deformation field that can be queried at arbitrary spatio-temporal resolutions, a set of surface-induced 3D Gaussians for differentiable rendering, and temporally coherent meshes generated via ball-pivot meshing in the parametric domain. Unlike mesh-based methods, the output surface has no fixed vertex count and adapts its effective resolution to the deformation details present.

## Method

Thin-Shell-SfT replaces discrete mesh representations with two SIREN-based neural fields: a Neural Reference Field (NRF) fitting the template and a Neural Deformation Field (NDF) predicting per-point, per-frame displacement offsets with a momentum-conserving recurrence. A continuous Kirchhoff-Love thin shell physics prior is imposed by computing membrane and bending strains via automatic differentiation on the NDF at randomly sampled parametric points each iteration, minimising the hyperelastic energy without requiring known external forces or contacts. For differentiable rendering, 3D Gaussians are initialised from Poisson-disk-sampled template points with rotations locked to the template's local covariant basis and normal scale fixed to a small ε, so that only tangential scales, opacity, and colour are optimised from the first frame while all subsequent frames backpropagate solely to the NDF. Joint space-time optimisation over all frames (rather than random frame sampling) respects deformation causality and avoids local minima in fold reconstruction.

## Results

On the φ-SfT benchmark (9 cloth deformation sequences), Thin-Shell-SfT achieves an average Chamfer distance of 3.3×10⁻⁴ (with temporal coherency variant) / 3.5×10⁻⁴ (default), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art φ-SfT (3.93×10⁻⁴) and all other baselines. In terms of normal consistency, the method achieves 0.009 ℓ₂ and 0.034 cosine normal error, compared to 0.013 and 0.041 for φ-SfT. The method wins on 6 out of 9 sequences against φ-SfT, with particularly large margins on S2 (0.55 vs 2.75) and S4 (5.5 vs 7.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor φ-SfT [33] (Chamfer avg 3.93), Thin-Shell-SfT (3.3) wins on 6 of 9 sequences, with the largest gains on S2 (0.55 vs 2.75, a 5× improvement) and S4 (5.5 vs 7.60), while losing on S1 (1.17 vs 0.79), S5 (8.69 vs 6.15), and S9 (3.00 vs 2.36). The physics-based Stotko et al. [67] (avg 9.93) and mesh-based DDD [82] (avg 10.87) are substantially outperformed across nearly all sequences. NRSfM methods (N-NRSfM avg 54.69, D-NRSfM avg 15.11) and learning-based IsMo-GAN [61] (avg 49.22) perform poorly on fine-grained cloth wrinkles. Ngo et al. [50] (avg 5.92) fails entirely on sequences S8 and S9 due to occlusion handling limitations. For dynamic view synthesis, K-Planes [15] and Deformable Gaussians [81] fail to recover coherent surface geometry from monocular static-camera input, confirming that multi-view radiance field methods are unsuitable for this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-based Shape-from-Template (SfT) line of work (φ-SfT, Stotko et al.) by replacing the discrete mesh representation with a continuous neural deformation field (NDF) and a neural reference field (NRF), both using SIREN activations. It adapts the Kirchhoff-Love thin shell physics from NeuralClothSim (a forward quasistatic cloth simulator) into a continuous spatial regulariser for the inverse tracking problem, which is a novel repurposing since the forward model cannot be naively applied when external forces and material properties are unknown. For rendering, it repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting from its original multi-view radiance field context into a monocular surface-tracking renderer with surface-induced Gaussian parameterisation (fixed normal scale, template-derived rotations), departing from both the original 3DGS and dynamic Gaussian methods that rely on multi-view cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous formulation enables reconstruction of fine-grained wrinkles and folds that mesh-based methods fundamentally cannot capture at practical resolutions, and the continuous physics prior provides a principled occlusion-handling mechanism without requiring high-resolution FEM meshes. However, the method is limited to textured surfaces with a known template, a static camera, and still requires 30 minutes to an hour per sequence on an A100 GPU. Open questions include extending to textureless surfaces, handling extreme self-collisions, and whether the continuous formulation generalises beyond the single φ-SfT benchmark used for evaluation.
