## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction (HOI) generation methods rely on 2D conditioning signals (depth maps, normal maps, masks) that cannot capture complex 3D occlusion relationships between fingers and objects, producing artifacts in occluded regions during dynamic manipulation. Additionally, the scarcity of diverse HOI video data limits generalization to unseen objects, as most datasets contain only ~10 object categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251044/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes MANO hand motion sequences (θ, β) and 3D object meshes with simulated motion trajectories (quaternion rotations and translations), which are preprocessed into a multi-layer occlusion representation (per-finger occlusion-free normal maps + depth-based occlusion confidence maps) and an object representation (6-viewpoint appearance images + 2048-point cloud). It outputs temporally coherent bimanual hand-object manipulation videos at 512×512 resolution, with optional human skeleton driving for human-centric generation.

## Method

The core contribution is a Multi-Layer Occlusion (MLO) representation that decomposes hands into seven independently rendered layers (palm, thumb, index, middle, ring, little finger, object) as occlusion-free normal maps, paired with depth-based occlusion confidence maps that encode the degree of occlusion per region. MLO is embedded into the denoising UNet in two complementary ways: spatially aligned to the initial noise via a lightweight pose guider (coarse structure), and injected into added transformer blocks via cross-attention (fine 3D occlusion reasoning). Object appearance and geometry are separately embedded through an AppearanceNet (multi-view reference images) and cross-attention (normal map + point cloud), enabling dynamic consistency from Objaverse. Training follows a two-stage protocol (image stage → temporal stage) that simultaneously samples from HOI video data, Objaverse object-only data (with hand layers zeroed), and human data, allowing the model to learn interaction patterns from HOI videos while acquiring object diversity and human identity generalization from the other sources.

## Results

On the DexYCB benchmark, ManiVideo achieves the best FID of 49.96, LPIPS of 0.079, PSNR of 30.10, SSIM of 0.913, and MPJPE of 57.30, outperforming all three baselines across every metric. On the authors' own bimanual manipulation dataset (722 videos, 376k frames, 15 objects), ManiVideo achieves FID 37.70, LPIPS 0.113, PSNR 29.59, SSIM 0.905, and MPJPE 32.89, again leading all comparisons. The largest margin is in FID on DexYCB, where ManiVideo improves over the next-best method (ADiff) by ~4 points and over CDiff by ~35 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ManiVideo is compared against three baselines: HOGAN (a GAN-based single-hand HOI image generator), ADiff (Affordance Diffusion, a diffusion-based HOI image synthesizer), and CDiff (ControlNet + Stable Diffusion v1.5 as a generic conditional diffusion baseline). ManiVideo wins on all five metrics (FID, LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM, MPJPE) on both datasets. On DexYCB, the gap is most pronounced against CDiff (FID 49.96 vs. 84.74; MPJPE 57.30 vs. 68.01), while the margin over ADiff is smaller but consistent (FID 49.96 vs. 53.95; MPJPE 57.30 vs. 59.12). On the authors' bimanual dataset, ManiVideo improves FID by 2.2 over ADiff and 7.8 over CDiff, and reduces MPJPE by 4.56 and 10.00 respectively. HOGAN is excluded from the bimanual dataset comparison because it is designed for single-hand interactions only. Qualitatively, the paper highlights that ManiVideo handles finger self-occlusion and hand-object mutual occlusion more accurately, where 2D-conditioned baselines produce artifacts or penetration.

## Relation to Prior Work

ManiVideo extends the HOI image generation line (HOGAN, Affordance Diffusion, HoiDiffusion) from 2D conditioning signals (depth maps, normal maps, masks) to a multi-layer occlusion (MLO) representation inspired by multi-plane images, explicitly modeling 3D occlusion between fingers and objects. The video generation architecture follows Animate Anyone's AppearanceNet + denoising UNet design, with MLO and object geometry injected via both additive noise conditioning and cross-attention in transformer blocks. A key departure from prior HOI work is the training strategy that jointly learns from HOI video data, object-only data from Objaverse, and human-centered data, bridging the gap between object appearance learning and interaction pattern learning in a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to generate temporally coherent bimanual HOI manipulation videos with generalizable objects, addressing a gap the authors explicitly identify as 'unexplored' in the literature. The MLO representation offers a principled solution to the occlusion problem that plagues 2D-conditioned HOI generation, and the multi-dataset training strategy provides a reusable recipe for integrating large-scale 3D object priors into interaction generation. Key limitations include the reliance on laboratory-captured 3D motion sequences as input (not yet replaceable by motion generation), the 512×512 resolution constraint, and the open-set generalization that still fails under unreasonable driving signals. Whether the MLO concept transfers to other articulated-body interaction tasks (e.g., face, full body) remains an open question.
