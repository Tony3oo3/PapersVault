## Problem

Deep neural networks frequently produce over-confident or under-confident predictions, and while focal loss and its variants improve calibration, they suffer from two fundamental issues: the differentiable loss weighting factor disrupts the positive correlation between cross-entropy value and gradient magnitude during backpropagation (causing misalignment), and the single-logit-based uncertainty estimate is insufficiently precise for multi-class settings. This paper identifies and addresses both gaps to achieve optimal calibration performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces calibrated class probabilities that require no post-hoc temperature scaling (optimal T≈1 in most cases). The gradient weighting is applied specifically to the last linear layer's gradients during training, and the output is a K-dimensional probability vector whose confidence scores directly reflect true class-posterior probabilities at convergence.

## Method

The paper reformulates calibration optimization from a gradient perspective: instead of multiplying a differentiable uncertainty weight u onto the loss (which disrupts the CE-to-gradient positive correlation), it directly scales the cross-entropy gradient by u, i.e., ∂L/∂θ = u(p̂(x))·∂L_CE/∂θ, implemented in practice by detaching u's gradient and multiplying it with CE. For the uncertainty metric u, the paper introduces the generalized Brier Score (gBS) computed over all K classes, which provides a more accurate sample-wise uncertainty estimate than focal loss's single-logit term or dual focal loss's two-logit term. The resulting BSCE-GRA loss is proven to converge to the true class-posterior probability η(x), preventing over/under-confidence. Experiments across ResNet-50/110, WideResNet, and DenseNet on three datasets confirm SOTA ECE with inherently calibrated models.

## Results

BSCE-GRA achieves state-of-the-art ECE across most dataset-model combinations. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50, it reaches 0.74% pre-temperature-scaling ECE, outperforming FLSD (1.26%), DFL (1.00%), and BSCE (0.88%). On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-50, BSCE-GRA achieves 1.59% ECE versus 5.53% for FLSD and 2.54% for DFL. Notably, the optimal temperature for BSCE-GRA is frequently T=1, indicating the method trains inherently calibrated models that require minimal post-hoc correction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSCE-GRA consistently outperforms all four baselines on pre-temperature-scaling ECE across nearly all dataset-model combinations. Against FLSD (Focal Loss with adaptive exponent), BSCE-GRA reduces ECE by 0.52 points on CIFAR-10/ResNet-50 (0.74 vs 1.26) and by 3.94 points on CIFAR-100/ResNet-50 (1.59 vs 5.53). Against DFL, BSCE-GRA achieves 0.74 vs 1.00 on CIFAR-10/ResNet-50 and 1.59 vs 2.54 on CIFAR-100/ResNet-50. Against Brier Loss and MMCE, the gap is even larger (e.g., 0.74 vs 4.29 and 4.48 on CIFAR-10/ResNet-50), confirming that gradient-weighted Brier Score substantially surpasses both direct Brier minimization and kernel-mean-embedding calibration. The one notable exception is CIFAR-100/DenseNet post-temperature-scaling, where FLSD (1.30) slightly edges out BSCE-GRA (1.61), though BSCE-GRA still leads pre-scaling (1.62 vs 3.38).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies Focal Loss and Dual Focal Loss under a common sample-weighting framework (u·CE), revealing that FL's complexity term is mathematically equivalent to the Brier Score in the binary case and DFL's term extends this to three-class settings. It then departs from prior loss-weighting approaches by identifying a fundamental misalignment: differentiable loss weights disrupt the positive correlation between CE value and gradient magnitude, causing the model to over-focus on moderately uncertain rather than maximally uncertain samples. The proposed solution—applying the uncertainty weight directly to gradients rather than the loss—combined with the generalized Brier Score (gBS) as a K-class uncertainty metric, yields the BSCE-GRA loss function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-weighting framework is general and simple, accommodating both differentiable and non-differentiable uncertainty metrics, which broadens the design space for calibration-aware training. The finding that BSCE-GRA produces inherently calibrated models (optimal temperature ≈ 1) is practically valuable, reducing reliance on post-hoc temperature scaling. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet with moderate-sized CNNs, leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale datasets, transformer architectures, or tasks beyond classification. The theoretical guarantee (convergence to true class-posterior) is deferred to the appendix and its practical tightness remains to be verified.
