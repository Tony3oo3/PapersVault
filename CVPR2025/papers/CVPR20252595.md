## Problem

Current text-to-video evaluation metrics such as VBench emphasize visual quality and smoothness while neglecting temporal fidelity and text-to-video alignment, which are critical for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and robotics. Naive evaluation of one neural network's output using another is neither rigorous nor interpretable, and existing approaches that claim temporal evaluation focus on playback speed or movement rather than true temporal reasoning over event sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252595/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt T and a synthetic video V (a sequence of frames) as input, processing the video in sliding windows of 3 frames to stay within the VLM's context window. The prompt is first decomposed into atomic propositions and a Temporal Logic specification via PULS (powered by GPT-4o/o1-preview), while the video is converted into a Discrete-Time Markov Chain automaton. The output is a single NeuS-V score S_NeuS-V, computed as the average of four per-mode scores (object existence, spatial relationship, object-action alignment, overall consistency), each derived from a satisfaction probability calibrated through an empirical CDF mapping.

## Method

NeuS-V is a neuro-symbolic pipeline with five stages: (1) PULS, a two-step LLM module (Text-to-Proposition then Text-to-TL) that translates the prompt into atomic propositions and a TL specification for each of four evaluation modes, with few-shot examples optimized via MIPROv2/DSPy; (2) a VLM (InternVL2-8B) scores each atomic proposition per 3-frame window by computing token-level probabilities over True/False responses, calibrated with a false-positive threshold; (3) the calibrated confidence scores are assembled into a video automaton (DTMC) where each state encodes a truth-value configuration of the propositions and transition probabilities are products of the per-proposition confidences; (4) the STORM probabilistic model checker computes the satisfaction probability P[A_V ⊨ Φ] using a PCTL variant of temporal logic; and (5) the satisfaction probability is mapped to a final score via an ECDF-based calibration. The key differentiator is grounding VLM outputs in formal temporal logic and model checking, which the ablation in Section 6.2 shows yields significantly higher Pearson correlation with human annotations than VLM-only baselines.

## Results

NeuS-V achieves over 5× higher Pearson correlation with human text-to-video alignment annotations compared to VBench across all four evaluated T2V models (Gen-3, Pika, T2V-Turbo-v2, CogVideoX-5B). On the MSR-VTT validation set, NeuS-V separates aligned from misaligned video-caption pairs by a margin of 0.52 (0.82 vs 0.30), substantially outperforming VBench's 0.38 gap (0.78 vs 0.40). On the 360-prompt NeuS-V suite, Gen-3 attains the highest overall score (0.723), followed by CogVideoX-5B (0.589), T2V-Turbo-v2 (0.552), and Pika (0.508), with all models degrading as temporal complexity increases from basic (1 TL operator) to advanced (3 TL operators).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is VBench, the de-facto T2V evaluation benchmark. NeuS-V consistently outperforms VBench in correlation with human text-to-video alignment annotations across all four models: for example, on Gen-3 the Pearson coefficient rises from VBench's lower value to 0.48 for NeuS-V, and on Pika from a weaker correlation to 0.62. On the MSR-VTT robustness test, NeuS-V achieves a 0.52 gap between aligned and misaligned captions versus VBench's 0.38, confirming superior discriminative power for true text-video alignment. An ablation replacing the formal temporal-logic pipeline with a VLM-based VQA approach (GPT-4o + LLaMA-3.2-11B or LLaVA-Video-7B) yields lower Pearson coefficients than NeuS-V, demonstrating that the formal verification grounding is essential rather than merely the VLM perception. The video-capable LLaVA-Video-7B variant is notably overconfident and correlates worse than the image-only LLaMA-3.2, further underscoring the value of the structured automaton-based evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neurosymbolic video understanding line of work (Choi et al., ECCV 2025), which first combined VLMs with formal verification for scene detection, by repurposing that framework as an evaluation metric for text-to-video generation. It departs from the dominant T2V evaluation paradigm (VBench, EvalCrafter, FETV) that scores visual quality or uses VLMs as black-box judges, instead grounding evaluation in temporal logic specifications and probabilistic model checking (STORM/PCTL). The PULS module for prompt-to-TL translation builds on NL2TL work but extends it to capture spatial and multi-object temporal relationships across four evaluation modes, while the automaton construction leverages VLM token probabilities as transition weights in a DTMC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest SOTA model (Gen-3) achieves only ~0.72 on temporally complex prompts, with performance degrading as temporal operator count increases, suggesting current T2V models rely on simplistic camera movements rather than genuine temporal dynamics. The 5× correlation improvement over VBench with human annotations is promising but measured on a 160-prompt subset, and the method currently cannot penalize unintended artifacts in generated videos. Open questions include whether the formal verification approach scales to longer videos or more complex multi-agent scenarios, and whether NeuS-V scores can be used as training signals or for iterative video refinement without retraining the generator.
