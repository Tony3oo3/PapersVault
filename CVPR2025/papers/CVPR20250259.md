## Problem

Concept-based interpretable methods (e.g., CBM) link predictions to global concepts but cannot localize which image patches activate those concepts, while prototype-based methods (e.g., ProtoPNet) provide patch-level explanations but require post-hoc expert analysis to associate learned prototypes with semantically meaningful concepts—a challenge that is especially acute in medical imaging where subtle findings make concept assignment difficult. Additionally, prior interactive methods only allow intervention at the concept-logit level, lacking a mechanism for clinicians to guide model attention to specific image regions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single medical image (chest X-ray or skin lesion) and produces a disease class prediction along with per-concept 2D similarity maps that localize where each concept prototype is activated on the input, scalar similarity scores per concept, and a reference prototype image (retrieved from the training set) showing the corresponding activated region. The similarity score for each concept is the maximum cosine similarity between its prototype and any projected feature patch, so the explanation is inherently patch-localized rather than image-level. The task head H is constrained to be a white-box model (e.g., a single linear layer or shallow tree) to preserve interpretability of the s→y reasoning step.

## Method

CSR comprises a Concept model (feature extractor F plus a 1×1-conv concept head producing class activation maps per concept), a projector P trained via a multi-prototype contrastive loss, and a white-box task head H. Local concept vectors are soft-selected by weighting feature patches with spatially-softmax-normalized activation maps; a contrastive objective (Eqn. 9) then pulls each projected concept vector toward its M nearest concept prototypes while pushing it away from prototypes of other concepts, yielding a compact, generalizable prototype atlas of M·K vectors. At inference, the model computes cosine similarity between each prototype and every projected feature patch, takes the spatial maximum as the concept similarity score, and feeds the aggregated score vector to H for classification. The key distinction from ProtoPNet is that prototypes are grounded on interpretable concepts by design (no post-hoc labeling), and the explanation size equals the number of concepts rather than the number of prototypes. The paper further introduces spatial-level interaction (positive/negative bounding boxes that reweight similarity maps via an importance map A) and concept-level interaction (zeroing rejected concept scores), enabling doctor-in-the-loop correction at both the patch and concept granularity.

## Results

CSR achieves a Macro F1-score of 94.4 on TBX11K, 54.6 on VinDr-CXR, and 71.5 on ISIC, outperforming all interpretable baselines by up to 4.5% (on VinDr-CXR vs. PIP-Net). On the Pointing Game (TBX11K, Tuberculosis class), CSR attains a 60.9% hit rate, substantially exceeding part-prototype methods (ProtoPNet: 8.8%, ProtoTree: 7.8%, PIP-Net: 19.5%) and CBM (55.1%); refining the concept atlas with a doctor further raises this to 79.5%. CSR uses only 14 explanation entries per prediction (matching CBM's concept count) while maintaining accuracy competitive with methods using 600–3000 prototypes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSR outperforms all five baselines on every dataset. Against CBM (joint training), CSR gains +5.8 F1 on TBX11K (94.4 vs. 88.6), +4.5 on VinDr-CXR (54.6 vs. 50.1), and +26.0 on ISIC (71.5 vs. 45.5), demonstrating that patch-level prototype grounding substantially exceeds image-level concept prediction. Against ProtoPNet, CSR wins on all three datasets (94.4 vs. 94.1, 54.6 vs. 41.4, 71.5 vs. 38.5) while using far fewer explanation entries (14 vs. 600–3000). Against PIP-Net, CSR leads by +0.4 on TBX11K, +9.5 on VinDr-CXR, and +1.6 on ISIC. Against ProtoTree, CSR edges ahead on TBX11K (94.4 vs. 94.0) and ISIC (71.5 vs. 66.1). Against ProtoPool, CSR gains +3.7 on TBX11K and +40.5 on ISIC. On the Pointing Game, CSR's 60.9% hit rate far surpasses part-prototype methods (8.8–19.5%) and slightly exceeds CBM (55.1%), confirming that concept-grounded prototypes align better with human-relevant anatomy than class-level prototypes.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSR sits at the intersection of Concept Bottleneck Models (which predict interpretable concepts before classification but only at the image level) and prototype-based methods like ProtoPNet and PIP-Net (which provide patch-level explanations but require post-hoc expert analysis to link prototypes to semantic concepts). The paper departs from both by learning concept-grounded prototypes by design—each prototype is explicitly tied to an interpretable concept through a contrastive learning objective—eliminating the need for post-hoc annotation. It further extends the interactivity paradigm beyond concept-level (CBM) and prototype-level (ProtoPDebug) adjustments by introducing spatial-level interaction via bounding boxes on the input image, a mechanism absent from prior interpretable methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a critical gap between interpretability and trustworthiness: prototype-based methods that appear to offer fine-grained explanations (ProtoPNet, ProtoTree) actually show very poor alignment with semantic ground truth in the pointing game (8.8% and 7.8% hit rate vs. 60.9% for CSR), challenging the field's assumption that more visual prototypes equal better explanations. The finding that doctor-refined prototypes reduce F1 slightly but dramatically improve PG hit rate (79.5%) suggests that accuracy gains in the unrefined model may stem from Clever-Hans shortcuts. Open questions include whether the spatial interaction mechanism generalizes beyond 2D bounding boxes to more complex interventions, whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric medical imaging, and whether the modest interaction gains (0.1–0.7% F1) are sufficient to justify the additional human effort in clinical workflows.
