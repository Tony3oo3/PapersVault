## Problem

Existing audio-driven talking head generation methods struggle with emotional expressiveness and controllability because audio contains tightly coupled information that hinders learning the speech-to-expression mapping. Additionally, diffusion-based approaches face instability in long-time generation due to autoregressive inference and difficulty scaling to high resolutions. The paper addresses these gaps by decoupling lip and expression information from audio and designing a stable, expressive video diffusion backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252654/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static portrait image (x_ref), a driven speech audio sequence, and an optional emotion source that can be a temporal video, a single expression image, or a text prompt. It produces a synchronized expressive talking head video at 512×512 resolution (up to 120 frames per training window, supporting up to 1024×1024). Inference is non-autoregressive using overlapping clips with DDIM sampling, enabling arbitrary-length generation without error accumulation.

## Method

EmotiveTalk comprises two stages: (1) a Vision-guided Audio Information Decoupling (V-AID) module that uses a Wav2Vec encoder followed by a Perceiver-based audio-to-lip projector (aligned via infoNCE contrastive loss + MSE) and a Diffusion-based Co-speech Temporal Expansion (Di-CTE) module for frame-level expression generation, with a CLUB mutual-information penalty enforcing separation between lip and expression latents; and (2) an Emotional Talking Head Diffusion (ETHD) backbone built on a 3D U-Net with spatial-temporal separable attention (from SVD), featuring an Expression Decoupling Injector (EDI) that subtracts cross-attention with the reference portrait's expression from cross-attention with the driving expression to transfer emotion while preserving identity, plus spatial attention masks (from OpenFace landmarks) to confine expression effects to the face region excluding lips. Training is two-stage: V-AID is pretrained then frozen while ETHD is trained with randomly chosen expression sources (audio-generated or video-derived) for multi-source emotion control.

## Results

On HDTF, EmotiveTalk achieves the best FVD (140.96 audio-only, 120.70 audio+video), Sync-C (8.24, 8.41), Sync-D (7.09, 7.11), and E-FID (0.54, 0.34) among all compared methods. On MEAD, it similarly leads in FVD (207.67, 153.71), Sync-C (6.82, 6.79), and E-FID (0.57, 0.40). The FID scores are competitive (16.64/53.21 audio-only; 16.09/50.84 audio+video), with the audio+video setting achieving the lowest FID overall. In the user study, EmotiveTalk scores 4.15/3.96/3.98/4.03 on Lip-Sync/Exp-Q/Realness/V-Q, outperforming all baselines and approaching ground-truth scores of 4.51/4.49/4.44/4.40.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmotiveTalk outperforms all seven baselines across nearly every metric in both audio-only and audio+video settings. In the audio-only category, it surpasses the strongest diffusion-based competitor Hallo by a large margin on FVD (140.96 vs. 276.31 on HDTF) and E-FID (0.54 vs. 0.65), while also achieving the best Sync-C (8.24 vs. 7.99). SadTalker and AniTalker lag significantly on FVD (589.63 and 583.70 respectively) and E-FID, and AniPortrait shows poor lip sync (Sync-C 3.75). In the audio+video category, EmotiveTalk dominates with FVD of 120.70 (vs. 184.60 for StyleTalk, 263.78 for DreamTalk, 464.90 for PD-FGC) and E-FID of 0.34 (vs. 0.42, 0.55, 0.74). The only metric where a baseline is competitive is FID in the audio-only setting, where Hallo (17.15) is marginally lower than EmotiveTalk (16.64) on HDTF, though EmotiveTalk still wins on MEAD (53.21 vs. 52.07 is actually Hallo slightly better on MEAD FID). The user study confirms these quantitative gains, with EmotiveTalk scoring 0.4–0.5 points higher than the next-best method (DreamTalk) on all four subjective criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

EmotiveTalk builds on the Stable Video Diffusion 3D U-Net backbone and adapts Hallo's spatial attention masking for facial region control, but its core departure is the Vision-guided Audio Information Decoupling (V-AID) module, which uses vision-space lip and expression encoders as contrastive supervision to separate coupled audio information into distinct lip-related and expression-related latents. This contrasts with prior audio-driven methods (EMO, Hallo, DreamTalk) that directly synthesize expressions from raw audio under weak supervision, and with controllable methods (PD-FGC, AniTalker) that rely on 3DMM coefficients or contrastive learning with identity leakage. The Expression Decoupling Injector (EDI) further departs from prior work by using a subtraction of two cross-attention outputs to explicitly remove reference-portrait expression before injecting the driving expression, and the Di-CTE module repurposes diffusion for temporal expansion of utterance-level emotion into frame-level expression sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of lip and expression representations from audio, guided by vision-space supervision, offers a cleaner architectural solution to the long-standing coupling problem in audio-driven talking head generation, and the multi-source emotion control pipeline (video, image, text) broadens practical applicability for content creation. The non-autoregressive inference with overlapping clips achieving stable generation up to 1500 frames without metric degradation addresses a key practical limitation of prior diffusion-based talking head methods. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-person scenes, whether the expression encoder's capacity limits the range of controllable emotions, and how the method performs with significantly longer sequences or higher resolutions beyond the 512×512/120-frame training configuration.
