## Problem

Existing infrared-visible image fusion methods face a fundamental trade-off: visual-quality-focused approaches neglect downstream task performance, while task-specific methods introduce conflicting optimization objectives that degrade both visual fidelity and task accuracy. Additionally, integrating the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for rich semantic priors is computationally prohibitive at inference, making such approaches impractical for real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250612/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of aligned infrared and visible images (randomly cropped to 192×256 during training) and produces a single fused image. During training, SAM generates semantic patches from both modalities that guide the main network, but at inference only the lightweight sub-network (0.136M parameters, 10.47 ms) is deployed without SAM. The fused image can subsequently be fed into segmentation backbones (SegFormer-B3 or X-Decoder) for downstream perception tasks.

## Method

The SAGE framework consists of two key components. First, a Semantic Persistent Attention (SPA) module uses a Persistent Repository (PR) that stores latent representations and key-value pairs of source features as a static memory; SAM-derived semantic patches serve as queries in a cross-attention mechanism that retrieves full-scene context from the PR, enriching incomplete patch-level semantics with modality-specific details. Second, a DARTS-style bi-level optimization distillation scheme transfers knowledge from the SAM-enhanced main network to a compact sub-network of stacked dense blocks, eliminating SAM at inference. The distillation is driven by a triplet loss combining feature alignment (cosine similarity between dense and SPA block features), context preservation (Sobel gradient + MSE), and a contrastive semantic loss that uses SAM's encoder to construct positive/negative pairs via binary masks, ensuring the student's feature space aligns with the teacher while remaining distinct from individual source modalities.

## Results

On the FMB dataset with SegFormer-B3, SAGE achieves 61.2 mIoU, surpassing the second-best DDFM (58.2) by 3.0 points and the next cluster (SegMiF 57.3, SHIP 57.2, U2Fusion 57.0) by 4+ points. On MFNet, SAGE reaches 54.0 mIoU versus FILM's 52.7 and SegMiF's 52.4. In the open-vocabulary setting (X-Decoder, no retraining), SAGE also leads with 51.1 mIoU on FMB. In terms of efficiency, SAGE runs at 10.47 ms with 52.06 G FLOPs and only 0.136M parameters, making it the fastest and lightest method among all 10 compared approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE outperforms all 9 baselines on both FMB and MFNet in the supervised SegFormer-B3 setting. On FMB, the margin is most pronounced: SAGE (61.2 mIoU) leads DDFM (58.2) by 3.0 points, SegMiF (57.3) by 3.9, and the weakest baseline TIMFusion (55.5) by 5.7. On MFNet, SAGE (54.0) edges out FILM (52.7) by 1.3 and SegMiF (52.4) by 1.6, with a larger gap over MRFS (50.4) and TIMFusion (49.8). In the open-vocabulary X-Decoder setting (no retraining, prompt-based), SAGE still achieves the best mIoU (51.1 on FMB), confirming that SAM's semantic priors transfer to zero-shot segmentation. Notably, SAGE also achieves the lowest inference time (10.47 ms) and FLOPs (52.06 G) among all methods, while DDFM requires 280K ms and 1.34M G FLOPs, highlighting a >26000× speed advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) deep-learning-based infrared-visible image fusion, which has evolved from purely visual-quality objectives (U2Fusion, DDFM) to task-coupled designs (TarDAL, SegMiF, MRFS) that introduce conflicting optimization goals; (2) the Segment Anything Model and its growing use as a semantic prior in low-level vision (deblurring, dehazing, super-resolution); and (3) knowledge distillation, here instantiated through a DARTS-style bi-level optimization. The key departure from prior task-specific fusion methods is that SAGE avoids embedding task losses directly into the fusion network; instead, it extracts SAM's semantic priors during training and distills them into a lightweight sub-network, thereby decoupling the fusion process from both SAM and explicit task supervision at inference time. The Semantic Persistent Attention module with its Persistent Repository is a new architectural primitive not seen in prior IVIF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: SAM-based fusion methods are computationally infeasible at inference, and task-specific fusion methods suffer from optimization conflicts between visual quality and downstream accuracy. SAGE's distillation scheme resolves both, yielding a 0.136M-parameter model running at ~10 ms that outperforms much larger competitors on both fusion quality and segmentation mIoU. However, the evaluation is limited to semantic segmentation as the sole downstream task, and the bi-level distillation's sensitivity to the capacity gap between teacher and student is acknowledged but not deeply analyzed. Open questions include whether the SPA + distillation recipe generalizes to detection, tracking, or other perception tasks, and whether the approach extends beyond the infrared-visible pair to other multi-modality combinations.
