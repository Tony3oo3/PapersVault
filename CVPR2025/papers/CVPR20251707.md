## Problem

Multimodal language models (MLMs) struggle with perception-demanding tasks like relative depth estimation and object counting because they cannot produce intermediate visual representations (depth maps, bounding boxes) to reason over, unlike specialized vision models. Fine-tuning MLMs on task-specific data yields poor generalization, and outsourcing computation to external depth estimators or object detectors is computationally expensive and memory-intensive. The paper asks whether MLMs can be augmented with intrinsic image representations as intermediate reasoning tokens, analogous to chain-of-thought in language models.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text question as input. For 3D depth tasks, it autoregressively generates a 100-token depth map sequence (a 10×10 grid over a 320×320 depth map, each token drawn from a 128-entry VQVAE codebook) before producing a text answer. For 2D counting tasks, it generates bounding boxes encoded as 4-tuples of pixel-coordinate tokens (PIXEL_0 through PIXEL_335, corresponding to a 336×336 resized image) before outputting the final count. At inference, constrained decoding restricts sampling to auxiliary tokens at designated reasoning steps to ensure coherent intermediate representations.

## Method

AURORA augments an MLM (LLaVA 1.5 13B) with an expanded vocabulary of perception tokens and trains it via a specialist-to-generalist distillation objective: a pre-trained VQVAE (codebook size 128) tokenizes depth maps, and a one-to-one mapping aligns the MLM's token predictions with the specialist's output distribution via cross-entropy loss, supplemented by an optional reconstruction loss through a lightweight decoder. Bounding boxes use structured pixel-coordinate tokens rather than learned codes. Training follows a curriculum learning schedule that progressively shifts from atomic token-prediction tasks to multi-step chain-of-thought reasoning to direct labeling, using a temperature-scaled softmax over task difficulty to control the data mixture and avoid catastrophic forgetting. A multi-task sequential sampler interleaves CoT and direct-labeling prompts per image, and constrained decoding at inference guides the model to emit auxiliary tokens at the appropriate reasoning steps.

## Results

On relative depth estimation, LLaVA-AURORA achieves 61.6% average accuracy across BLINK 2-point and HardBLINK 3/4/5-point configurations, outperforming the fine-tuned LLaVA baseline (55.2%) by 6.4% and the base LLaVA 1.5 13B (39.1%) by 22.5%. On object counting, LLaVA-AURORA reaches 56.0% on CV-Bench, 54.6% on SEED-Bench, and 45.8% on BLINK, improving over the fine-tuned baseline by +11.3%, +8.3%, and +45.6% respectively. In depth map generation (Table 3), LLaVA-AURORA achieves 79.2% average relative depth accuracy, surpassing Unified-IO (74.4%) and Unified-IO 2 (35.1%), though falling short of the VQVAE upper bound (95.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-AURORA decisively outperforms the fine-tuned LLaVA baseline (direct labeling only) on all relative depth configurations, with the gap widening as task complexity increases: +8.0% on HardBLINK 3-point, +8.1% on 4-point, and +13.7% on 5-point, demonstrating that perception tokens scale better with difficulty than language-only fine-tuning. Against the much larger LLaVA OneVision 72B, LLaVA-AURORA (13B) wins by 8.2% average on relative depth, showing the token-based approach compensates for model size. On counting, LLaVA-AURORA beats LLaVA OneVision (56.0 vs. 34.4 on CV-Bench) and the fine-tuned baseline (56.0 vs. 44.7), but remains below GPT-4o (70.18) and GPT-4 Turbo (61.3) on CV-Bench. Notably, GPT-4 Turbo + Tool (which uses Grounding DINO externally) scores only 48.6% on CV-Bench counting and 49.6% average on relative depth, both below LLaVA-AURORA, suggesting that internal perception tokens avoid the error propagation of tool-augmented pipelines. On depth map generation (Table 3), LLaVA-AURORA (79.2% avg) surpasses Unified-IO (74.4%) and Unified-IO 2 (35.1%) but trails the VQVAE upper bound (95.7%), indicating room for improvement in token fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the chain-of-thought reasoning paradigm from language models to visual perception by introducing discrete visual tokens (depth map codes, bounding box coordinates) as intermediate reasoning steps within MLMs. It departs from any-to-any generation models (Unified-IO, LISA, GSVA) that produce visual outputs as final answers, instead using those outputs as *reasoning intermediates* the model conditions on for subsequent steps. The specialist-to-generalist distillation via VQVAE tokenization and the curriculum learning schedule draw from prior work on step-by-step distillation (Hsieh et al.) and multi-task training, but the key conceptual departure is from both vanilla fine-tuning (which implicitly hopes the model learns perception) and tool-using MLMs (which outsource to external detectors/estimators), positioning perception tokens as an intrinsic, lightweight middle ground.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Perception tokens open a new modality for MLM reasoning beyond language, and the framework is designed to be extensible to arbitrary visual representations (segmentation masks, poses, keypoints) without architectural changes. However, the experimental scope is limited to a single 13B model (LLaVA 1.5) and two task families (depth, counting), with training data of only 20k depth images and 5k counting images; generalization to larger models, more perception types, and open-world settings remains untested. The gap between AURORA's depth generation (79.2%) and the VQVAE upper bound (95.7%) suggests the autoregressive generation of visual tokens is still lossy, and whether this bottleneck persists at scale is an open question. The approach also does not yet close the gap to GPT-4 Turbo on counting, indicating that structured visual reasoning remains challenging even with explicit intermediate tokens.
