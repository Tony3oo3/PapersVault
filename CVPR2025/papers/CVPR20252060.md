## Problem

Diffusion models often generate images that fail to faithfully align with input prompts, and existing latent-optimization approaches like InitNo are constrained to a local ℓ∞ neighborhood of the initial point and rely solely on attention maps, which capture limited semantic information. This paper addresses both limitations by introducing an LVLM-based supervision signal and a distribution-preserving latent update mechanism that can explore a broader region of the noise space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252060/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a standard Gaussian initial noisy latent z_T (as produced by a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model) and outputs a refined RGB image whose content more closely matches the prompt. The LVLM is queried with a binary VQA question ('Does this figure show "{prompt}"? Please answer yes or no?'), and the probability of the 'Yes' token serves as the scalar optimization objective. The method is plug-and-play across SD V-1.4 through V-2.1 and multiple LVLMs (CLIP-FlanT5-11B, InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5, ShareGPT4V), requiring no retraining of the diffusion model.

## Method

The core mechanism is a Noise Diffusion update that applies a forward diffusion step to the initial latent: z'_T = √(1−γ)·z_T + √γ·σ, which provably preserves the standard Gaussian distribution for any step size γ. The step size is score-aware (γ = 1 − √s(z_T)), making perturbations larger when the current VQA score is low. Rather than using a single random noise, the method samples N candidate noises and selects the one that maximizes the ratio ∇_{z_T}s(z_T)·v / ‖v‖², where v is the step difference; a theoretical analysis shows this ratio exceeding a threshold (c/2 + δ) guarantees a score increase. The gradient ∇_{z_T}s(z_T) is computed via a simplified chain-rule approximation that treats the noise prediction ε_θ(z_t) as constant across timesteps, reducing backpropagation cost to a single pass through the VAE decoder and LVLM.

## Results

On simple-case prompts, Noise Diffusion achieves a VQA score of 0.9790 at epoch 50, surpassing InitNo (0.8717) by 0.1073, and already leads at epoch 10 (0.9352 vs. 0.8717). On complex-case prompts involving spatial relationships, the advantage widens: Noise Diffusion reaches 0.9578 VQA at epoch 50 versus InitNo's 0.7648, a gap of 0.1930. CLIP scores follow the same trend, with both metrics converging by epoch 50. Across four SD versions (V-1.4 through V-2.1) and four LVLMs, the method consistently improves CLIP scores over vanilla generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InitNo (the primary baseline), Noise Diffusion wins decisively on both simple and complex cases: +0.1073 VQA on simple prompts and +0.1930 on complex prompts at convergence, with the gap widening as optimization progresses. Against vanilla Stable Diffusion (the epoch-0 starting point), the method improves VQA from the initial score to 0.9790 (simple) and 0.9578 (complex), confirming that latent optimization alone yields substantial gains without any model retraining. In the optimization-technique ablation (Fig. 6), PGD and Mean-Variance (InitNo's update rule) both converge to local optima with minimal visual change, while Random Sampling and Random Diffusion show global movement but fail to match the prompt; Noise Diffusion, which adds gradient-guided noise selection, produces a prompt-faithful image by epoch 5 and remains stable. The method also generalizes across SD V-1.4/1.5/2.0/2.1 and four different LVLMs (CLIP-FlanT5-11B, InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5, ShareGPT4V), with CLIP scores improving in every combination tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on optimizing initial noisy latents for text-to-image diffusion, building directly on InitNo (Guo et al., 2024), which uses attention-map gradients to refine the starting latent. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing attention-based supervision with LVLM-based VQA scoring to capture richer semantic understanding of prompt-image alignment, and (2) replacing InitNo's gradient-based mean/variance perturbation with a forward-diffusion-style update (Noise Diffusion) that provably preserves the standard Gaussian distribution of the latent. The theoretical analysis draws on standard Taylor expansion and bounded-curvature assumptions to characterize when the update increases the VQA score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism to improve semantic faithfulness across multiple diffusion backbones (SD V-1.4 through V-2.1) and LVLMs, making it broadly applicable without retraining. The distribution-preserving property addresses a practical limitation of InitNo (local-optimum trapping due to distribution shift), and the ~0.19 VQA gain on complex spatial prompts is meaningful for compositional generation. Limitations include the additional per-epoch computational cost (~6.7 s on an A6000), dependence on LVLM quality for the supervision signal, and evaluation restricted to relatively short prompts; whether the approach scales to long, multi-object prompts or video generation remains open.
