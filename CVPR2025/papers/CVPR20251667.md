## Problem

Single-image novel view synthesis has traditionally relied on depth-estimation-and-warping pipelines or latent-space diffusion models, both of which suffer from depth errors or irrecoverable high-frequency detail loss between source and target views. Additionally, the relative contribution of complex 3D geometry encodings versus simply using a stronger generative backbone remains unclear, and multi-view training data is scarce and semantically biased toward indoor scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a source RGB image, a 3×4 camera extrinsics transformation matrix T_{s→t}, and 3×3 intrinsic matrices for both source and target cameras. It outputs a novel-view RGB image via a cascaded pipeline: a base model generates a low-resolution (64×64) target view, which is then upscaled by a super-resolution model to 256×256 pixels. The SR model additionally receives the high-resolution source image to preserve fine texture details.

## Method

VIVID uses two parallel EDM2 U-Net architectures—an encoder processing the source view and a decoder denoising the target view—connected through joint self-and-cross-attention layers where target tokens attend to both their own and the source's features at multiple resolutions. The diffusion process operates directly in pixel space (not a pretrained autoencoder's latent space) to avoid the texture degradation inherent to encode-decode round trips. Geometry is encoded via a simple scalar embedding of the camera pose matrices into a shallow perceptron that shifts network activations, which ablations show outperforms more complex epipolar attention, depth, or coordinate-warping schemes. A cascaded base+SR design replaces latent-space compression for resolution scaling. For training on single-view data, the authors propose a homography-based augmentation that simulates camera rotations by warping a single image with two randomly sampled rotation matrices and cropping the overlapping region, enabling NVS training on large single-view datasets like OpenImages.

## Results

On the RealEstate10K benchmark, VIVID achieves FID of 2.89 and PSNR of 17.36 for mid-range (30–60 frames) novel view synthesis, and FID of 3.89 and PSNR of 15.21 for long-range (60–120 frames), substantially outperforming all prior methods. The improvement over the previous SOTA (GenWarp) exceeds 24% on every metric and range, with FID improvements reaching ~50% in the mid-range setting. The model also outperforms the naive source-view baseline on PSNR by 4.2 dB (mid-range) and 3.3 dB (long-range), confirming it genuinely synthesizes new content rather than copying the input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIVID dominates all three baselines across both mid-range and long-range settings on both FID and PSNR. Against GenWarp [37] (the closest prior method), VIVID reduces mid-range FID from 5.91 to 2.89 (−51%) and improves PSNR from 13.43 to 17.36 (+29%); for long-range, FID drops from 7.38 to 3.89 (−47%) and PSNR rises from 12.10 to 15.21 (+26%). Against GeoGPT [29], the gains are even larger: mid-range FID improves from 6.43 to 2.89 and PSNR from 14.06 to 17.36. PhotoNVS [54] is the weakest baseline, with VIVID cutting its mid-range FID from 7.12 to 2.89 and long-range FID from 9.22 to 3.89. The authors attribute the advantage primarily to operating in pixel space (avoiding autoencoder detail loss) and using a dedicated encoder trained end-to-end for NVS, rather than to more complex geometry encodings.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIVID builds on the EDM2 UNet architecture and cascaded diffusion design, adapting them for NVS by introducing a dedicated encoder-decoder pair with joint self-and-cross-view attention. It departs from prior diffusion-based NVS methods (GenWarp, Photometric-NVS) in two key ways: operating in pixel space rather than a pretrained autoencoder's latent space, and training from scratch rather than fine-tuning text-to-image models. The paper's central empirical claim—that a simple scalar pose embedding nearly matches complex geometry encodings (epipolar attention, depth warping, coordinate embeddings)—directly challenges the design choices in recent NVS diffusion works that invest heavily in 3D-aware conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that pixel-space diffusion and a powerful generative backbone matter far more than intricate geometry encoding simplifies the NVS design space and redirects effort toward model capacity and data diversity rather than architectural complexity. The homography-based single-view augmentation offers a practical path to training NVS models on the abundant single-view image datasets, partially addressing the data scarcity and domain bias of multi-view benchmarks like RealEstate10K. Open questions remain around camera translation (absent from the augmentation), scale ambiguity in SfM-derived extrinsics, and extending the approach to consistent multi-view or video NVS.
