## Problem

Existing Reversible Adversarial Example (RAE) methods for dataset IP protection rely on labeled datasets and supervised classifiers to generate adversarial perturbations, making them inapplicable to the growing ecosystem of unlabeled datasets and SSL pre-trained encoders. Moreover, these methods exhibit poor adversarial transferability when the protected data is processed by SSL pre-trained encoders, allowing attackers to easily neutralize the protection. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing the first label-free RAE framework that directly targets SSL pre-trained encoders.

## Input / Output

The noise encoder takes a fixed signature noise S (a small tensor of the same spatial dimensions as the image, unique per authorized user) as its sole input and outputs a single universal adversarial perturbation δ bounded by ε=10/255 in l_p norm. This perturbation is added pixel-wise to any original image to produce a protected sample; authorized users recover the original by subtracting the regenerated δ. The noise encoder architecture consists of consecutive deconvolution layers followed by a convolutional layer, and the entire pipeline operates on RGB images at standard resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10/STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet).

## Method

RAEncoder trains a noise encoder to generate a universal adversarial perturbation by optimizing an InfoNCE-based loss that maximizes the feature-space distance between the protected sample and the original under a target SSL pre-trained encoder, rather than targeting a supervised classifier. An interference pattern is introduced via three additional losses (adversarial, high-frequency, and MSE) that force the encoder to output a near-zero perturbation when fed random noise instead of the correct signature, preventing attackers from recovering the original image. A high-frequency filter constrains the perturbation to high-frequency components for imperceptibility, while an MSE loss further limits pixel-level distortion. The total training objective combines six weighted losses (three for the normal pattern and three for the interference pattern), with the interference weights set equal to the normal weights (φ=1) to balance adversarial strength against security against adaptive attacks.

## Results

On ImageNet, RAEncoder achieves ASR of 98.22% (VGG16), 91.46% (ResNet18), 88.30% (ResNet50), and 99.00% (Dense121) against downstream SL models, and 98.97% (BYOL) and 89.04% (Barlow Twins) against SSL pre-trained encoders. On CIFAR-10, ASR ranges from 58.79% (VGG16) to 89.79% (DeepCluster v2), remaining competitive with SRAE. In terms of imperceptibility, RAEncoder achieves SSIM of 0.85 and PSNR of 28.26 on CIFAR-10 (best among all compared methods), and 100% sample recovery (PSNR = ∞) without any information embedding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Xue et al. [32] (RDH-based), RAEncoder substantially outperforms on nearly all settings: on ImageNet, ASR jumps from 73.04% to 98.22% (VGG16) and from 0.60% to 86.00% (DINO), while also achieving better visual quality (SSIM 0.82 vs. 0.58). Against PGN [31], RAEncoder wins on most ImageNet SL-model targets (e.g., 98.22% vs. 81.32% on VGG16, 99.00% vs. 51.76% on DenseNet121), though PGN retains a visual-quality edge (SSIM 0.94 vs. 0.82) at the cost of weaker adversarial strength. Against SRAE [38], RAEncoder is slightly lower on a few CIFAR-10 SL models (e.g., 58.79% vs. 61.84% on VGG16, 84.79% vs. 88.40% on ResNet18) but dramatically surpasses it on ImageNet (98.22% vs. 72.30% on VGG16, 98.97% vs. 92.00% on BYOL) and achieves far superior imperceptibility (SSIM 0.85 vs. 0.42 on CIFAR-10) with 100% lossless recovery, whereas SRAE only recovers to SSIM 0.76. GEAA [41] is compared only on visual quality, where RAEncoder dominates (SSIM 0.85 vs. 0.32 on CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Reversible Adversarial Example (RAE) line of work, which has been dominated by RDH-based methods (Liu et al., Xue et al., Xiong et al.) and generative methods (SRAE, GEAA), all of which generate perturbations by attacking supervised classifiers. The key conceptual departure is targeting SSL pre-trained encoders directly using an InfoNCE-based formulation borrowed from Zhou et al.'s downstream-agnostic adversarial example work [44], thereby eliminating the need for labels entirely. The noise encoder architecture with a fixed signature noise input and the interference pattern for authorization differentiation represent a new mechanism that replaces the information-embedding step used in all prior RDH-based RAE schemes, achieving 100% fidelity without the adversarial-strength penalty that embedding imposes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SSL pre-trained encoders become the default feature extraction pipeline in industry, the inability of existing RAE methods to protect unlabeled datasets represents a practical gap that this paper addresses. The demonstration that a single universal perturbation generated by a small noise encoder can simultaneously achieve high ASR across six SSL methods and four downstream architectures, while maintaining 100% recovery fidelity, is a meaningful step toward deployable dataset IP protection. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger datasets (beyond ImageNet), how it performs against diffusion-model-based purification attacks, and whether the fixed noise encoder can be made more robust to adaptive threats beyond random-noise probing. The authors also note future work on extending the attack to variational auto-encoders.
