## Problem

The standard Mean Teacher framework for domain adaptive object detection couples pseudo-label generation to the student model itself (via an EMA copy), creating a brittle bootstrapping loop: if the source-trained student cannot produce accurate target-domain labels, the positive feedback never starts. The paper argues this coupling is unnecessarily constrained, since large self-supervised vision foundation models like DINOv2, trained on far more data, can generate more accurate target labels and provide more generalizable feature representations than a small student-derived teacher.

## Input / Output

The method takes labelled source-domain images (Cityscapes, 8 object classes) and unlabelled target-domain images (BDD100k, Foggy Cityscapes, or ACDC splits) as input, and produces bounding boxes with class labels as output. The DINOv2 ViT-G labeller runs offline as a single forward pass over all target training images to generate pseudo-labels before student training begins. During online training, images are scaled to 588 px height (divisible by the ViT patch size of 14) so that the frozen DINOv2 ViT-B alignment encoder can process them alongside the student backbone; at inference, the alignment encoder and projection MLP are discarded.

## Method

DINO Teacher decouples two components that Mean Teacher entangles. First, a DINO labeller is built by attaching a Faster R-CNN head to a frozen DINOv2 ViT-G encoder and training it exclusively on source data; this labeller then generates all target pseudo-labels in a single offline pass, replacing the EMA teacher. Second, a patch-level alignment loss drives the student backbone's features toward those of a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B encoder, applied independently to each source and target image via a 2-layer projection MLP and cosine similarity, requiring no cross-domain instance matching or pseudo-labels. The full training loss combines the standard Faster R-CNN detection loss on source ground truth and target pseudo-labels with this similarity loss, and the student (VGG16 or ResNet-50) is trained with weak-strong augmentations and an EMA evaluation model. The key design choice is that alignment starts on source images before any pseudo-labels are introduced, reducing unaligned gradients from supervised training across domains.

## Results

On Cityscapes→BDD100k, DINO Teacher (DT) achieves 47.8 mAP@50, exceeding the previous best (Harmonious Teacher, 40.2) by +7.6%. On Cityscapes→Foggy Cityscapes, DT reaches 55.4 mAP@50, improving over REACT (53.1) by +2.3% and over the Adaptive Teacher baseline (50.9) by +4.5%. On the four ACDC adverse-weather splits, DT-G improves over Adaptive Teacher by +6.4% (Fog), +6.9% (Night), +1.3% (Rain), and +1.6% (Snow).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DT outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every evaluated dataset. On BDD100k, the +7.6% gain over Harmonious Teacher [11] (40.2→47.8) is the largest, with particularly strong improvements on rare classes (Truck: 27.4→44.3, Bus: 30.6→45.9, Motor: 28.2→38.3). On Foggy Cityscapes, DT (55.4) surpasses REACT [35] (53.1) by +2.3% and the Adaptive Teacher baseline [38] (50.9) by +4.5%, with the largest per-class gains on Truck (37.7→47.2) and Bus (58.4→66.5). On the small ACDC splits, DT-G consistently beats the re-implemented Adaptive Teacher [38] baseline, with the most dramatic improvement on Night (+6.9%, 29.5→36.4), where the domain gap is most severe. The paper also shows that the DINO ViT-G labeller alone (51.1 mAP on BDD100k) substantially outperforms the source-only student (29.1) and even the Mean Teacher EMA model (35.3), validating the core hypothesis that a frozen VFM backbone produces more transferable pseudo-labels than a student-derived teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Adaptive Teacher (AT) framework for domain adaptive object detection, which itself extends the Mean Teacher self-labelling paradigm with adversarial domain alignment. The key conceptual departure is decoupling both labelling and alignment from the student model: instead of using an EMA of the student as the teacher, a frozen DINOv2 ViT-G backbone with a trained detection head generates pseudo-labels, and instead of label-conditional or adversarial alignment, the student's patch features are aligned to a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B encoder independently on source and target images. This repurposes vision foundation models—previously used in DAOD for augmentation or generative domain transfer—as direct labellers and alignment proxies, arguing that the symmetric student-teacher architecture of Mean Teacher is an unnecessary constraint when large pretrained encoders are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple detector on a frozen VFM backbone, trained only on source data, produces substantially more accurate target-domain pseudo-labels than the student-derived Mean Teacher, with the largest gains (+7.6% mAP) on the most challenging domain gap (Cityscapes→BDD100k). However, on small target datasets (ACDC splits), pseudo-label quality still limits improvements and the method can lag behind target-only training, suggesting that better labellers or complementary strategies (e.g., rare-class oversampling) remain needed. An open question is whether the alignment component's benefit is truly additive or partially subsumed by the improved labels, and whether the approach generalizes beyond driving scenarios where DINOv2's pretraining distribution overlaps well with the target domain.
