## Problem

Current grounding MLLMs are designed for common objects and perform poorly on fine-grained face understanding, failing to segment small facial parts, recognize detailed attributes, or answer face-specific questions. Existing face datasets further compound this gap by offering only 19 coarse mask categories, template-generated captions without attribute relationships, and no pixel-level grounded QA data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252699/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a high-resolution close-up single-face RGB image and a text prompt (captioning instruction, attribute question, or referring expression). It produces a natural-language response interleaved with one or multiple pixel-level segmentation masks over 47 fine-grained face part categories (e.g., individual wrinkles, eyeliner, cheekbones), enabling both grounded captioning and referring segmentation in a single forward pass.

## Method

GroundingFace extends GLaMM with three components: (1) an HQ-SAM Adapter that fuses shallow and deep ViT features via a Shallow-Deep Fusion module to recover pixel-level detail lost in deep layers, enabling precise small-part mask decoding; (2) a face-prior sampler that crops SAM's 64×64 shallow feature grid to the facial region using a 1024-resolution affine transform anchored on five-point landmarks, then compresses tokens via pixel unshuffle and a learnable linear projector, injecting high-resolution facial information without increasing encoder cost; (3) a two-stage LoRA training recipe where Stage 1 integrates face concepts with general scene knowledge, and Stage 2 fine-tunes on high-quality manual annotations for small parts, with a Top-1 MoE router that dynamically assigns each token to either the HQ or LQ LoRA expert, preserving general object segmentation while enhancing fine-grained face understanding.

## Results

On zero-shot face attribute recognition, GroundingFace (7B) achieves 91.7% emotion accuracy on RAF-DB, 73.1% attribute accuracy on LFW, 98.5% gender accuracy, and 6.92 mean age error on AgeDB, outperforming all compared MLLMs including the much larger InternVL-v1.5 (26B). On pixel-grounded face captioning, GroundingFace reaches METEOR 21.9, AP50 32.9, and mIoU 52.0, compared to GLaMM's 0.9, 0.0, and 27.0. On grounded VQA, the method achieves METEOR 20.6, AP50 53.7, and mIoU 76.9 versus GLaMM's 1.6, 5.0, and 5.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroundingFace (7B) decisively outperforms all four external MLLM baselines on zero-shot face attribute recognition. On RAF-DB emotion recognition, it achieves 91.7% versus InternVL-v1.5's 67.2% (26B, the strongest competitor), LLaVA-v1.5's 55.4%, Qwen-VL's 42.9%, and InstructBLIP's 27.9%. On LFW attribute recognition, GroundingFace scores 73.1% compared to LLaVA-v1.5's 58.3% and InternVL-v1.5's 61.1%, while Qwen-VL fails entirely (0.0%). On AgeDB gender, GroundingFace ties LLaVA-v1.5 at 98.5% and beats InternVL-v1.5 (94.4%) and Qwen-VL (93.9%). On AgeDB age estimation, GroundingFace achieves the lowest mean error of 6.92 years, substantially better than InstructBLIP (9.97), LLaVA-v1.5 (10.22), Qwen-VL (11.44), and InternVL-v1.5 (19.26). Notably, GroundingFace matches or exceeds the 26B InternVL-v1.5 on every metric while using only 7B parameters, and on the core pixel-grounded captioning/VQA tasks (Table 3), it improves METEOR from 0.9 to 21.9 and mIoU from 27.0 to 52.0 over the GLaMM baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GLaMM pixel-grounded MLLM framework [44] from general object understanding to fine-grained face understanding, incorporating HQ-SAM [20] for high-precision mask decoding and reusing SAM's [22] shallow ViT features via a face-prior sampler to inject high-resolution facial detail without increasing encoder cost. It departs from prior grounding MLLMs (LISA, Shikra, GPT4RoI) that treat faces as monolithic objects, and from prior face datasets (CelebAMask-HQ, FFHQ-Text, CelebV-Text) that lack pixel-level masks, relational captions, and grounded QA. The two-stage LoRA + MoE training recipe is a practical adaptation of parameter-efficient fine-tuning to handle the quality gap between auto-generated and manually annotated face part masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FacePlayGround-240K fills a clear gap as the first large-scale pixel-grounded face dataset (240K images, 47 mask categories, 5.4M masks), enabling training of MLLMs for fine-grained face tasks that were previously infeasible. The framework's ability to achieve strong zero-shot face attribute recognition (e.g., 91.7% emotion accuracy with a 7B model vs. 67.2% for a 26B InternVL) suggests that domain-specific pixel grounding can substantially outperform scale alone on narrow perceptual tasks. Limitations include reliance on single-face close-up images (no multi-person scenes), dependence on commercial APIs for skin analysis and attribute extraction, and evaluation limited to the authors' own dataset for grounding tasks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to in-the-wild multi-face scenarios.
