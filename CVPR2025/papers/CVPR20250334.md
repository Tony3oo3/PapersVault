## Problem

Accurate material retrieval for 3D asset creation requires aligning visual representations with material-specific properties (texture, reflectance, roughness), but existing approaches directly apply general-purpose image search techniques that fail to capture the unique structure of material spaces. The absence of comprehensive material datasets with effective descriptors, combined with the domain gap between synthetic and real-world materials, makes this cross-domain alignment particularly challenging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250334/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a binary segmentation mask, applying element-wise multiplication to isolate the material region before encoding. The output is a ranked list of materials from a gallery, where each material is represented as a neutral sphere with the material appearance transferred onto it (via PBR rendering for synthetic materials or ZeST zero-shot transfer for real-world materials). Retrieval is performed via scaled dot-product cosine similarity between the masked image embedding and material sphere embeddings in a shared feature space.

## Method

MaRI employs a dual-encoder architecture inspired by CLIP, with separate DINOv2-based encoders for masked images and material spheres, fine-tuning only the last Transformer block of each to capture domain-specific variations while preserving general pre-trained features. Training uses InfoNCE contrastive loss (τ=0.07) to pull matched image-material pairs closer and push mismatched pairs apart in the shared embedding space. The training dataset combines 394,560 synthetic samples (Objaverse objects + 1,605 AmbientCG PBR materials + 712 HDRI Haven lighting environments rendered in Blender with 8 viewpoints each) with 30,000 real-world samples (segmented via Grounded-SAM and converted to standardized material spheres via ZeST), bridging the domain gap between controlled synthetic rendering and diverse real-world appearances.

## Results

On the Trained Materials gallery (1605 AmbientCG materials), MaRI achieves 26.0% Top-1 instance accuracy, 90.0% Top-5 instance accuracy, and 81.5% Top-1 class accuracy, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the Unseen Materials gallery (200 Textures.com materials), MaRI reaches 54.0% Top-1 and 89.0% Top-5 instance accuracy, demonstrating strong generalization to previously unseen materials. The only metric where MaRI trails a baseline is Top-3 IoU on Trained (0.77 vs. MaPa's 0.80), attributable to MaPa's category-constrained retrieval strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaRI dominates all five baselines on instance-level retrieval metrics across both datasets. On the Trained gallery, MaRI's 26.0% Top-1 instance accuracy exceeds the next-best Make-it-Real (8.5%) by 17.5 points, and its 90.0% Top-5 instance accuracy surpasses DINOv2 (28.0%) by a factor of over 3×. On the Unseen gallery, MaRI (54.0% T1I, 89.0% T5I) outperforms Make-it-Real (42.5%, 75.0%) by 11.5 and 14.0 points respectively, confirming that the dual-encoder contrastive approach generalizes better than GPT-4V-based hierarchical search. MaPa achieves the highest Top-3 IoU on Trained (0.80 vs. MaRI's 0.77) because its GPT-4V category-first pipeline constrains retrieval within a single class, inflating IoU at the cost of instance-level accuracy (2.5% T1I). General-purpose image models (ViT, CLIP, DINOv2) perform poorly on material retrieval, with CLIP scoring only 2.0% T1I on Trained, confirming that standard image search features are insufficient for capturing material-specific properties like reflectance and roughness.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaRI adapts CLIP's dual-encoder contrastive learning paradigm from cross-modal (text–image) alignment to intra-modal, cross-domain (image–material) alignment, using DINOv2 as a frozen backbone with only the final Transformer block fine-tuned. It departs from prior material retrieval systems (MaPa, Make-it-Real) that rely on GPT-4V for coarse categorization followed by CLIP-based fine search, instead learning a unified embedding space where retrieval is a single nearest-neighbor lookup. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes ZeST's zero-shot material transfer and Grounded-SAM segmentation to generate real-world material spheres, complementing a large-scale synthetic rendering pipeline built on Objaverse, AmbientCG, and HDRI Haven.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that general-purpose image search models (CLIP, DINOv2, ViT) fail substantially on material retrieval, the paper establishes that material-specific feature spaces are necessary for realistic 3D asset pipelines in AR/VR and digital content creation. The practical impact is a plug-and-play retrieval module that can replace ad-hoc GPT-4V-based material assignment in existing 3D generation workflows. Limitations include the real-world dataset covering only 8 material categories, reliance on ZeST for material sphere generation (which may introduce its own biases), and evaluation on relatively small galleries (~200 materials), leaving open whether the approach scales to industrial-scale material libraries with thousands of categories.
