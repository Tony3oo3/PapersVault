## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks are either visual-only fine-grained (e.g., ActivityNet, Charades-STA), coarse-grained multi-modal (global captions for short clips), or multi-modal but lacking detailed captions. No prior dataset provides precise temporal boundaries together with cross-modal correlation-aware captions for omni-modal (vision, audio, speech) events in long videos, leaving a critical gap for training models that perform both fine-grained temporal localization and cross-modal reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 100 uniformly sampled visual frames (encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14), audio and speech waveforms processed in 5.12-second clips (encoded by BEATs and Whisper-Large-v2 respectively), and a text instruction or query. Outputs include precise start/end timestamps for omni-modal events, dense relation-aware captions describing audio-visual correlations (synchronicity, causality, complementarity, etc.), per-segment captions, and free-form answers to audio-visual questions. The benchmark itself contains 8,411 videos (avg. 235 s) with 105,730 non-overlapping events (avg. 12.6 per video, avg. 16.7 s duration) covering 89% of total video duration.

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable automatic annotation pipeline that (1) filters 100K raw videos from ACAV-100M down to 8.4K high-quality clips using resolution, speech-dominance, static-scene, and C-MCR audio-visual similarity criteria; (2) detects omni-modal event boundaries by first identifying visual scene boundaries (two-stage split-and-merge) and generic audio boundaries (MFCC delta thresholding + CLAP semantic stitching), then merging them so that audio events are never truncated within a visual event; and (3) generates relation-aware captions via Gemini-1.5-Pro that explicitly reason about audio-visual correlations and fine-grained temporal dynamics rather than concatenating modality-specific descriptions. For the model, LongVALE-LLM uses CLIP ViT-L/14, BEATs, and Whisper-Large-v2 as modality encoders feeding into randomly initialized adapters that project into a Vicuna-7B LLM (fine-tuned with LoRA). Training proceeds in two stages: boundary perception tuning on template-based single/multi-turn dialogues, followed by instruction tuning on 25.4K free-form QA dialogues generated by Gemini-1.5-Pro, with the visual adapter kept frozen (pre-trained on LCS-558k) while audio/speech adapters and the LLM are updated.

## Results

On the LongVALE test set, LongVALE-LLM (7B) achieves mIoU 11.0 on Omni-TVG, CIDEr 7.9 on Omni-DVC, and CIDEr 20.3 on Omni-SC, outperforming the strongest visual-only baseline VTimeLLM (mIoU 6.4, CIDEr 0.2, CIDEr 1.6) by large margins across all three omni-modal fine-grained tasks. In zero-shot general AVQA, LongVALE-LLM scores 54.8 on AVSD and 49.4 on Music-AVQA using only 0.7M instruction pairs, surpassing AVicuna (53.1 / 49.6) on AVSD while using less than 10% of the training data employed by competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongVALE-LLM decisively outperforms all seven video LLM baselines on all three omni-modal tasks. Among visual-only temporal models, VTimeLLM (mIoU 6.4) and TimeChat (mIoU 5.2) are the strongest but still trail LongVALE-LLM (mIoU 11.0) by 4.6 and 5.8 points respectively, and are far behind on captioning (CIDEr 0.2 vs. 7.9 for DVC). Audio-capable models VideoLLaMA, PandaGPT, and NExT-GPT perform poorly (mIoU 1.9–4.0) because they process only a handful of frames, limiting fine-grained temporal reasoning. On zero-shot AVQA, LongVALE-LLM (54.8 AVSD, 49.4 Music-AVQA) edges out AVicuna (53.1, 49.6) on AVSD and is within 0.2 on Music-AVQA, while using only 0.7M pairs versus AVicuna's 1.1M and OneLLM's 1007M. It also surpasses AV-LLM (52.6, 45.2) and VideoLLaMA (36.7, 36.6) by wide margins on both AVQA benchmarks, confirming that the omni-modal correlation-aware captions in LongVALE confer strong general cross-modal reasoning even with minimal training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the boundary-aware training paradigm of VTimeLLM [17] from visual-only events to omni-modal (vision + audio + speech) events, and adapts the two-stage visual scene detection method from Panda-70M [7] as one component of a broader multi-modal boundary detection pipeline. It departs from prior omni-modal benchmarks (VALOR, VAST) that only provide coarse-grained captions for short clips by introducing fine-grained temporal event boundaries and correlation-aware captions for long videos. The pipeline repurposes existing tools (CLIP, BEATs, Whisper, Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4o, Qwen-Audio, C-MCR, CLAP) in a novel orchestration for automatic omni-modal annotation, avoiding the high cost of manual labeling that limits prior fine-grained benchmarks like ActivityNet Caps and Charades-STA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LongVALE fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously provides omni-modal (vision, audio, speech) fine-grained temporal annotations with detailed captions for long videos, which is essential for building truly comprehensive video understanding systems. The zero-shot AVQA results—achieving SOTA with only 32.7K samples versus millions used by competitors—suggest that structured, temporally-grounded, cross-modal-correlation-rich data may be more effective than raw data volume for general cross-modal reasoning, an open question worth further investigation. Limitations include reliance on a single 7B model architecture, a test set of only 1,171 videos, and the pipeline's dependence on multiple proprietary LLMs (Gemini, GPT-4o) for caption generation, raising reproducibility concerns. Future work on scaling the dataset, improving cross-modal interaction in the model architecture, and validating the data-efficiency claim across architectures would strengthen the contribution.
