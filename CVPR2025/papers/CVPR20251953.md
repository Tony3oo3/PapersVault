## Problem

Multi-view generative models increasingly produce 3D scenes from 2D diffusion, but existing metrics for evaluating their 3D consistency are inadequate: TSED requires camera poses and misses obvious inconsistencies, while distribution-based metrics like FID and FVD measure image quality rather than geometric coherence. There is no metric that is scene-agnostic, pose-free, robust to view-dependent effects like lighting, and provides a gradual (rather than binary) measure of consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251953/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MEt3R takes a pair of RGB images at 256×256 resolution (with bilinear resizing for methods that natively operate at other resolutions) and produces a single scalar score in [0, 2] where lower indicates higher consistency. No camera poses are required as input. The score is symmetric, though in practice one direction of the internal similarity computation suffices under runtime constraints, and the metric is differentiable end-to-end.

## Method

MEt3R first applies DUSt3R to the image pair to obtain dense, pixel-aligned 3D point maps in a shared coordinate frame without requiring camera poses. DINO self-supervised features are then extracted from both images and upscaled to high resolution via FeatUp's image-adaptive Joint Bilateral Upsamplers. The point maps are used to unproject the upscaled features into 3D and reproject them onto the first camera's image plane via PyTorch3D point rasterization, yielding two aligned feature maps. Cosine similarity is computed pixel-wise over the overlapping region (masked by a boolean overlap mask) in both directions, and MEt3R is defined as 1 minus the average of the two directional similarities. Operating in DINO feature space rather than RGB makes the metric invariant to view-dependent effects such as lighting and reflections, which ablations confirm is critical for correct ranking of methods.

## Results

Among multi-view generation methods, DFM achieves the best MEt3R score (0.026) but the worst FID (73.02) due to blurry renderings, while GenWarp achieves the best FID (29.80) but the worst MEt3R (0.120). The proposed MV-LDM achieves the best quality-vs-consistency trade-off with MEt3R 0.036, FID 37.29, and FWS(PSNR) 28.46. Among video generation models, SVD achieves the best MEt3R (0.032) and FVD (674.6), followed by Ruyi-Mini-7B (MEt3R 0.047) and I2VGen-XL (MEt3R 0.050). Notably, TSED rates PhotoNVS, MV-LDM, and DFM all near 0.99, failing to differentiate them, whereas MEt3R clearly separates them (0.069, 0.036, 0.026 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEt3R clearly differentiates methods that TSED cannot: TSED rates PhotoNVS (0.996), MV-LDM (0.998), and DFM (0.990) nearly identically, while MEt3R separates them by a factor of ~2.7 (0.069 vs. 0.026). GenWarp, which generates one view at a time, shows the worst consistency (MEt3R 0.120) despite the best FID (29.80), confirming that image quality and 3D consistency are orthogonal. DFM, which uses an internal 3D representation, achieves the best MEt3R (0.026) and FWS-PSNR (39.56) but the worst FID (73.02) due to blurry pixelNeRF-based renderings. Among video models, SVD outperforms Ruyi-Mini-7B and I2VGen-XL on MEt3R (0.032 vs. 0.047/0.050) and FVD (674.6 vs. 850.5/1722.6), though it produces smoother, shorter camera trajectories. On object-level generation (GSO), SyncDreamer achieves the best MEt3R, followed by VideoMV and EpiDiff, with EpiDiff showing a slight increase in inconsistency as views move further from the conditioning frame. FVD, unlike MEt3R, cannot be applied to individual image pairs and is sensitive to blur, as evidenced by DFM scoring worse FVD (1174.6) than MV-LDM (945.8) despite being more 3D-consistent by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEt3R builds directly on DUSt3R for pose-free dense stereo reconstruction, repurposing its pixel-aligned point maps as a warping mechanism rather than a reconstruction output. It departs from existing consistency metrics (TSED, SED) that rely on epipolar geometry and camera poses, and from distribution-based metrics (FID, FVD) that conflate image quality with 3D consistency, by operating in a DINO feature space that is invariant to view-dependent effects like lighting. The accompanying MV-LDM is inspired by the proprietary CAT3D architecture but re-implemented on top of Stable Diffusion 2.1 with cross-view attention, filling a gap in open-source multi-view latent diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the field rapidly shifts toward repurposing video and image diffusion models for 3D scene generation, a reliable, pose-free consistency metric is essential for tracking progress without ground-truth 3D data. MEt3R's ability to operate on generated videos without camera poses makes it broadly applicable to the growing ecosystem of video diffusion models. Open questions include how the metric behaves on dynamic scenes with moving objects, whether the non-zero lower bound (from DUSt3R alignment errors and DINO feature inconsistencies) can be further reduced, and whether the metric transfers to 4D or temporally varying content. The open-source MV-LDM also provides a much-needed public baseline for multi-view latent diffusion.
