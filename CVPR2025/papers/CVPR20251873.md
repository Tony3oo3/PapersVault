## Problem

Data augmentation for IMU signals remains largely unexplored, with existing works naively applying general time-series techniques (jittering, moving average, random masking, flipping, slicing) that violate the fundamental properties of IMU data. Specifically, rotation matrices reside in SO(3) and are not closed under addition, and IMU signals must remain physically consistent with the motion they represent—constraints that generic augmentations ignore, leading to degraded or inconsistent performance.

## Input / Output

MODA takes raw IMU rotation matrices (3×3, in SO(3)) and acceleration vectors (3D, in R³) along with the underlying bone motion sequence (SMPL joint rotations and accelerations) as input, and produces physically valid augmented IMU signals that simulate realistic sensor drift. The augmented signals are then consumed by downstream HPE models (outputting 3D joint rotations) or HAR models (outputting activity class distributions), making MODA a plug-and-play preprocessing step rather than a standalone model.

## Method

MODA's key insight is that body-worn IMUs exhibit relative displacement (self-rotation and sliding) with respect to the bones they are attached to during motion, and this drift is a natural, physically grounded source of signal variation. The method models drift by making the rotational offset R_offset^G(t) and acceleration offset a_offset^M(t) time-varying: self-rotation is accumulated as a product of random SO(3) rotation matrices drawn from Gaussian angle perturbations (Eq. 9), while sliding is accumulated as a sum of acceleration perturbations computed as second derivatives of random displacement perturbations (Eq. 10). This formulation guarantees that augmented rotations remain valid elements of SO(3) and that augmented accelerations are kinematically consistent with the underlying bone motion, satisfying all three identified IMU signal properties. The initial offsets are obtained from a standard calibration procedure, and the entire augmentation is applied per-sample during training without modifying the downstream model architecture.

## Results

On the CIP dataset (Fully-Supervised HPE), MODA reduces Angular Error from 10.01° (baseline) to 8.02° (20% improvement) and SIP Error from 14.96 to 13.29 (11% improvement), while also minimizing Jitter to 0.07. On the BABEL-150 HAR subset, MODA achieves 48.71% top-1 accuracy (vs. 44.87% baseline, +3.84%) and 76.74% top-5 accuracy (vs. 68.29%, +8.45%). In the semi-supervised setting, MODA with only 1/8 of labeled data reaches 11.58° Angular Error on CIP, approaching the 10.01° achieved by the fully-supervised baseline. In Domain Generalization, MODA reduces Angular Error by 20–30% across CIP, ANDY, DIP, and TotalCapture compared to the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the HPE baseline (Wu et al. [53], no augmentation), MODA wins on every metric across both CIP and ANDY: Angular Error drops from 10.01° to 8.02° on CIP and from 5.20° to 4.81° on ANDY, while all five general DA methods either degrade performance (jittering, moving average) or yield only marginal gains (flipping, slicing). Against the HAR baseline (Miao et al. [28], no augmentation), MODA consistently outperforms on all three BABEL subsets; on the most challenging BABEL-150, top-1 accuracy improves from 44.87% to 48.71% and top-5 from 68.29% to 76.74%, whereas moving average causes severe degradation (37.21% top-1). Against PoseAugment (Li et al. [19]), MODA is orthogonal and complementary: PA alone reduces Angular Error on CIP from 18.39° to 15.93° but increases Jitter to 0.27, while PA+MODA further reduces Angular Error to 13.58° and Jitter to 0.14, demonstrating that MODA addresses the smoothness deficiency of generative augmentation. In the semi-supervised FixMatch setting, MODA also outperforms jittering, moving average, and random mask at every labeled-data ratio, achieving 9.43° Angular Error at 1/32 ratio versus 12.33–13.07° for the other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the IMU reading generation framework from Transpose [59], which models the relationship between bone motion and sensor readings using constant rotational and translational offsets. The key departure is making these offsets time-varying to simulate the natural drift of body-worn IMUs, rather than treating them as fixed calibration parameters. Conceptually, the paper also departs from prior work that naively transplants general time-series augmentation (jittering, moving average, random masking, flipping, slicing) to IMU signals, by providing a mathematical argument that several of these methods produce invalid rotation matrices outside SO(3) and violate motion-consistency constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard time-series DA methods are mathematically invalid for IMU rotation signals (violating orthogonality and non-closure under addition) is a clean theoretical contribution that explains why prior augmentation efforts yielded only marginal or negative results. Practically, MODA's plug-and-play nature across five learning settings and two tasks (HPE and HAR) makes it broadly applicable, and the semi-supervised results (1/8 data approaching full-data performance) suggest significant data-efficiency gains for expensive IMU annotation. Open questions include whether the drift parameters (μ, σ) can be learned per-subject or per-activity, and whether the method extends to non-body-worn IMU configurations where the bone-frame assumption breaks down.
