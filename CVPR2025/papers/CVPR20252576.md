## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves fast photorealistic rendering but relies on EWA splatting, which introduces three approximations to the volume rendering equation: linearization of the exponential transmittance, assumption of no self-occlusion, and linearization of the 3D-to-2D covariance. These approximations make it difficult for 3D Gaussians to represent opaque surfaces accurately, degrading reconstruction quality on piecewise-constant textures. The paper shows these approximations are unnecessary even within a rasterizer, since the volume rendering equation can be evaluated analytically by integrating 3D Gaussians directly in 3D space.

## Input / Output

For view synthesis, the method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and produces novel-view renderings at real-time speeds (~136–185 FPS on an RTX 3090-Ti). For tomography, it takes sparse 2D X-ray projection images (as few as 25 views) and recovers a 3D density field. The method is implemented as a drop-in replacement for the alpha computation within 3DGS's SLANG.D rasterizer, requiring no changes to the overall pipeline, densification heuristics, or training loop beyond a density reparameterization.

## Method

The key insight is that the volume rendering equation over a mixture of non-overlapping, sorted 3D Gaussians can be expressed in the same alpha-blending form as 3DGS, but with physically accurate alpha values computed by analytically integrating each Gaussian's density along the camera ray. Specifically, each 3D Gaussian is projected onto the ray as a 1D Gaussian with mean γ_j and variance β_j derived from the 3D covariance and ray direction, and the transmittance is computed in closed form via the error function: T̄_j = exp(−κ_j G_j(γ_j d) √(2π) β_j). This replaces 3DGS's 2D Gaussian alpha (which only reaches full opacity at its center) with a volumetrically consistent alpha that can saturate to 1 over a finite region, enabling accurate representation of opaque surfaces. The method also reparameterizes density as κ = −log(1−0.99θ)·(1/s_x+1/s_y+1/s_z)/3 to stabilize optimization of near-opaque primitives, and adjusts densification heuristics (halving density after splits, splitting high-density points, pruning low-density points). Because the forward model is physically accurate, it works out-of-the-box for tomography without the normalization fix required by R²-Gaussian.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF-360, the method achieves LPIPS 0.209 and SSIM 0.813, outperforming 3DGS-Slang (LPIPS 0.222, SSIM 0.813) while matching its PSNR (27.30 vs 27.52). On Tanks&Temples, it leads 3DGS-Slang on all three quality metrics (PSNR 23.74, SSIM 0.854, LPIPS 0.167 vs 23.64, 0.850, 0.176) at 185 FPS. For tomography (25 sparse views), the method matches R2-Gaussian on 3D PSNR/SSIM (35.08/0.922 synthetic; 34.98/0.835 real) while using fewer points (58.2K vs 66.4K synthetic; 67.4K vs 71K real).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS-Slang (the direct rasterization baseline), the method wins on LPIPS across all three datasets (0.209 vs 0.222 on Mip-NeRF360; 0.167 vs 0.176 on Tanks&Temples; 0.247 vs 0.248 on DeepBlending) and on SSIM for Tanks&Temples (0.854 vs 0.850) and DeepBlending (0.908 vs 0.906), while trailing slightly on PSNR (27.30 vs 27.52 on Mip-NeRF360; 29.72 vs 29.78 on DeepBlending). Against GES, the method dominates on all quality metrics (e.g., LPIPS 0.209 vs 0.250 on Mip-NeRF360) but runs at lower FPS (136 vs 186). Among ray-tracing baselines, EVER achieves marginally better SSIM/LPIPS (0.825/0.194 on Mip-NeRF360) but at 3–5× lower FPS (36 vs 136), while Don't Splat is substantially worse on all metrics (SSIM 0.536, PSNR 22.07). Against NeRF-based methods (Zip-NeRF, M-NeRF360, INGP-Big), the method is 10–1000× faster in rendering (136–185 FPS vs 0.03–9.43 FPS) with competitive or better perceptual quality. For tomography, the method matches R2-Gaussian on 3D PSNR/SSIM (35.08/0.922 vs 34.97/0.921 synthetic) while using 12% fewer points (58.2K vs 66.4K), and is competitive on real data (34.98 vs 35.01 PSNR) with 5% fewer points (67.4K vs 71K).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) by replacing its EWA splatting-based alpha computation with analytic 3D Gaussian integration along camera rays, all within the same rasterization framework. It departs from the broader 3DGS follow-up line (2DGS, GES, Gaussian Opacity Fields) that addresses individual splatting approximations, arguing instead that the entire splatting step—projecting 3D Gaussians to 2D screen-space Gaussians—is unnecessary. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between ray-tracing methods (Condor et al., Mai et al.) that compute physically accurate transmittance but are slow, and rasterization methods that are fast but physically approximate, showing the two are not mutually exclusive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The drop-in nature of the alpha replacement makes the method immediately compatible with the large ecosystem of 3DGS extensions (densification heuristics, order-independent transparency, etc.), and it works out-of-the-box for tomography, matching R2-Gaussian with fewer points and no architectural changes. However, it still inherits rasterization's per-tile sorting and no-overlap assumptions, and is more sensitive to poor camera calibration than 3DGS (producing opaque artifacts rather than graceful blurring). Open questions include whether compact-support primitives (e.g., Epanechnikov kernels) can further reduce overlap errors, and whether tile-size heuristics should be re-tuned for the new rendering model.
