## Problem

Existing radiance field and 3D Gaussian Splatting-based head avatar methods face a fundamental trade-off: those using parametric face models (FLAME meshes, blendshapes) limit the representation of intricate facial details, while those using neural implicit fields to predict Gaussian attributes incur high GPU memory during both training and inference. No prior method simultaneously achieves photorealistic rendering quality, real-time rendering speed, and minimal memory consumption for dynamic head avatars from monocular video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular portrait video (512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution, ~3–7 minutes per identity) and per-frame FLAME expression coefficients extracted via the Metrical Photometric Tracker as input. It produces a 4D Gaussian head avatar represented as a set of (n+3)-dimensional Gaussians that can be rendered at arbitrary pose and expression, supporting both novel-view synthesis and cross-identity facial reenactment. Backgrounds are pre-removed with MODNet, and the avatar is initialized by uniformly sampling 2,500 points on a sphere via the Golden Spiral algorithm.

## Method

GPAvatar extends each 3D Gaussian into an (n+3)-dimensional space by jointly modeling spatial position x ∈ ℝ³ and expression coefficient z ∈ ℝⁿ as a multivariate normal distribution. Conditioning on a target expression Z projects this high-dimensional Gaussian back to 3D via the conditional distribution of a multivariate normal, yielding a 3D Gaussian whose mean shifts by a linear transformation L(Z) (3n parameters per Gaussian) while its covariance remains constant. This linear projection is sufficient to capture expression variations without requiring MLPs or other neural networks, as validated by ablation studies showing comparable quality at 3–4× lower memory and 3× faster runtime. An adaptive densification strategy further allocates additional Gaussians to regions with high expression variance (e.g., teeth, wrinkles, hair) by duplicating Gaussians whose L-gradient exceeds a threshold. Training uses a composite loss of L1, SSIM, and sparsely-applied LPIPS with L2 regularization on L and sigmoid activation for stable gradients, converging in ~1 hour on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On the combined INSTA (512×512) and GBS (1024×1024) datasets, GPAvatar achieves an average PSNR of 32.54, SSIM of 0.9582, and LPIPS of 0.0551, outperforming all baselines across all three metrics. In terms of efficiency, the full model renders at 450 fps with 100k Gaussians on an RTX 4090, using only 2.5 GB training memory and 1.5 GB runtime memory, while the light configuration reaches 500 fps in just 10 minutes of training with 0.8 GB runtime memory. This represents a 4.5× speedup over FlashAvatar (100 fps) and a 1.2× speedup over GaussianBlendShapes (370 fps), with substantially lower memory than INSTA (16 GB) and GaussianBlendShapes (14 GB) during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPAvatar outperforms all five baselines on every quality metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across both the INSTA and GBS datasets. Against the closest Gaussian-based competitor, GaussianBlendShapes [18], GPAvatar gains +1.21 dB PSNR (32.54 vs 31.33) and reduces LPIPS by nearly half (0.0551 vs 0.1033), while also rendering 1.2× faster (450 vs 370 fps) and using 5.6× less training memory (2.5 GB vs 14 GB). Compared to FlashAvatar [31], GPAvatar improves PSNR by +2.24 dB and LPIPS by 0.032, at 4.5× the rendering speed (450 vs 100 fps) with the same 2.5 GB training memory. Against the NeRF-based methods INSTA [44] and PointAvatar [42], GPAvatar achieves substantially higher quality (+4.6 dB and +2.3 dB PSNR respectively) while being 6.4× and 90× faster at runtime, with dramatically lower memory (2.5 GB vs 16 GB and 40 GB training). NeRFBlendShape [7] is only compared on efficiency (Table 3), where GPAvatar is 17× faster (450 vs 26 fps) with less training memory (2.5 GB vs 7 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

GPAvatar extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to head avatar reconstruction by embedding each Gaussian in an (n+3)-dimensional space that jointly encodes spatial position and FLAME expression coefficients, then deriving a conditional 3D Gaussian via multivariate normal conditioning. This departs from prior Gaussian-based avatar methods (FlashAvatar, GaussianBlendShapes, MonoGaussianAvatar) that rely on MLPs or offset networks to predict per-Gaussian deformations, and from mesh-bound approaches (GaussianAvatars, SplattingAvatar) that require parametric face model geometry. The key conceptual move is showing that the conditional mean shift is a linear function of expression, making a per-Gaussian 3n-parameter linear layer sufficient—no neural network needed for expression-driven deformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves 450 fps rendering with 100k Gaussians at only 1.5 GB runtime memory, making high-fidelity head avatars practical on consumer hardware for VR/AR, film, and social media applications. The ablation confirming that a simple linear projection matches or exceeds MLP-based deformation in quality while being 3–5× faster at inference offers a clean, low-overhead design principle for future Gaussian-based avatar work. However, the paper acknowledges that the absence of geometric priors from parametric models leads to poor robustness under large pose/expression extrapolation, an open problem that limits generalization beyond the training distribution. Extending the approach to audio-driven animation and broader body avatars are noted as future directions.
