## Problem

Creating photorealistic, animatable 3D human avatars from monocular in-the-wild videos suffers from limited pose and viewpoint coverage, causing poor generalization to novel poses and rendering artifacts from unseen views. Existing approaches rely on priors not designed for clothed humans—parametric models like SMPL target minimally-clothed bodies without appearance, and heuristic regularizations like Laplacian penalties ignore material-dependent deformation—leaving a clear quality gap compared to studio-captured avatars.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video of a person performing casual motions as input. It first tracks SMPL-X pose/shape parameters and reconstructs a canonical textured template via SDF-based inverse rendering, then inpaints occluded texture regions with a DiT-based latent diffusion model before fine-tuning the universal prior. The output is a set of 3D Gaussians in canonical space that can be deformed via linear blend skinning to arbitrary poses and rendered from any viewpoint in real time via splatting rasterization. The universal prior is trained on 1,000 subjects captured by 200 synchronized cameras at 4096×2668 resolution with ~5,000 frames per person.

## Method

The core contribution is a universal prior model (UPM) for clothed humans built on 3D Gaussians, using front and back orthographic projection maps as a universal identity parameterization that accommodates diverse clothing topologies without requiring UV unwrapping. Canonical templates from all training subjects are normalized to an average skeleton scale to ensure spatial alignment across identities. A U-Net backbone takes the normalized front/back texture maps as identity conditioning and posed position maps (obtained by applying forward LBS to canonical positions) as pose conditioning, predicting per-pixel Gaussian attribute offsets (position and color) relative to the canonical maps. The model is trained via inverse rendering with L1, LPIPS, and offset-regularization losses across the full multi-view corpus. For personalization, the pipeline tracks pose, reconstructs the canonical template, inpaints missing textures with a diffusion model trained on synthetic visibility masks, and fine-tunes all UPM weights jointly with pose/shape parameters via a small number of inverse-rendering iterations.

## Results

On the NeuMan dataset (interpolation synthesis), Vid2Avatar-Pro achieves PSNR 32.71, SSIM 0.983, and LPIPS 1.19, outperforming the strongest baseline ExAvatar (PSNR 31.39, SSIM 0.981, LPIPS 1.64) by 1.32 dB in PSNR and 0.45 in LPIPS. On the more challenging MonoPerfCap dataset (extrapolation synthesis), the method reaches PSNR 31.97, SSIM 0.981, and LPIPS 1.37, again surpassing ExAvatar (PSNR 30.29, SSIM 0.979, LPIPS 2.19) by a substantial margin of 1.68 dB in PSNR and 0.82 in LPIPS. The method consistently leads on all three metrics across both evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vid2Avatar-Pro outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) in both the interpolation (NeuMan) and extrapolation (MonoPerfCap) settings. The closest competitor is ExAvatar [52], which achieves PSNR 31.39 / SSIM 0.981 / LPIPS 1.64 on NeuMan; Vid2Avatar-Pro improves by +1.32 dB PSNR and −0.45 LPIPS, with the gap widening to +1.68 dB PSNR and −0.82 LPIPS on the harder MonoPerfCap extrapolation task. Among 3DGS-based methods, GaussianAvatar [21] (PSNR 29.94, LPIPS 1.24) and 3DGS-Avatar [61] (PSNR 29.75, LPIPS 1.75) trail Vid2Avatar-Pro by roughly 2.8–3.0 dB in PSNR. The SDF/NeRF-based baselines—Vid2Avatar [15] (PSNR 26.87), HumanNeRF [78] (PSNR 27.06), InstantAvatar [28] (PSNR 28.47), and NeuMan [29] (PSNR 25.48)—are all surpassed by 4–7 dB in PSNR, reflecting the advantage of the learned universal prior over heuristic or shape-only regularization. Qualitatively, baselines exhibit artifacts on faces and feet and lack fine details (wrinkles, zippers), whereas Vid2Avatar-Pro preserves identity-specific appearance and produces plausible pose-dependent deformations for out-of-distribution poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal prior paradigm established for faces (Cao et al., URAvatar) and hands (URHand) to full-body clothed humans, replacing the per-identity optimization with heuristic priors (SMPL shape, Laplacian regularization) used by prior monocular avatar methods like Vid2Avatar, GaussianAvatar, and ExAvatar. The key architectural departure is the introduction of front-and-back maps as a universal spatial parameterization for clothed humans, inspired by Li et al.'s single-identity Animatable Gaussians but generalized to cross-identity training. The 3D Gaussian representation and SDF-based canonical template reconstruction are inherited from 3DGS and Vid2Avatar respectively, while the diffusion-based texture inpainting module (DiT-based) addresses the visibility gap inherent to monocular capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial SOTA margins (e.g., +1.3 PSNR over ExAvatar on NeuMan, +1.7 on MonoPerfCap) demonstrate that a learned universal prior from large-scale multi-view data is a fundamentally stronger inductive bias than heuristic regularization for the ill-posed monocular avatar problem, particularly for out-of-distribution pose generalization. However, the method currently cannot handle facial expressions, loose garments, or extreme lighting, limiting its applicability to a subset of real-world scenarios. An open question is whether the front-and-back map parameterization scales to more complex clothing topologies (e.g., flowing dresses) or whether a more flexible universal representation is needed. The pipeline's reliance on a 1000-subject multi-view capture corpus for pre-training also raises questions about data efficiency and transferability to underrepresented demographics or garment styles.
