## Problem

Existing video token compression strategies in MLLMs (e.g., average pooling, Q-Former, clustering) are unconditional, meaning they compress visual tokens without explicit guidance from the user's instruction. This inevitably discards instruction-relevant visual content while retaining irrelevant information, making it difficult to achieve high compression ratios with minimal task-specific information loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250441/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sampled video frames (32, 64, or 128 frames) encoded by SigLIP into features of shape T×H×W×D, along with a text instruction encoded into both a pooled global embedding and fine-grained token-level embeddings. The compressed output is a set of 680–2624 visual tokens (depending on frame count and downsampling ratio) projected into the LLM's embedding space via MLP layers, which the LLM then uses to generate a free-form or multiple-choice text answer. The default downsampling ratio is (4,3,3) across temporal, height, and width dimensions, reducing tokens by ~79% compared to uncompressed baselines.

## Method

HICom performs conditional token compression at two levels guided by the instruction. At the local level, frame features are partitioned into groups of size α_T×α_H×α_W; within each group, the instruction condition is injected into a pooled token (via direct injection) that serves as the attention query against the group's tokens as keys and values, compressing each group to a single token while preserving spatial-temporal structure. At the global level, a small set of learnable tokens with the instruction injected via adaptive layer norm (coarse injection) attend to all flattened frame features augmented with 3D position embeddings, capturing instruction-relevant information across the entire video as an auxiliary representation. A novel conditional pre-training stage is inserted between standard alignment and instruction tuning, using the constructed HICom-248K dataset (248K video clips with 739K instruction-followed descriptions generated by Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct) to pre-train the condition injection modules before full instruction tuning, improving average benchmark performance by ~1.17%.

## Results

HICom-7B with 32 frames and 680 tokens achieves 58.1 on VideoMME (w/o sub., all), 64.1 on MV-Bench, and 60.5 on Ego-Schema, outperforming LLaVA-Video-7B (6272 tokens) by 2.43% average across the three multiple-choice QA benchmarks while saving 78.8% visual tokens. At 128 frames (2624 tokens), HICom reaches 60.3 on VideoMME, 64.0 on MV-Bench, and 62.3 on Ego-Schema, gaining 3% average over LLaVA-Video while still saving 58.2% tokens. On open-ended benchmarks, HICom-7B (1328 tokens) attains 59.4/3.7 on ActivityNet (SOTA) and 3.20 average on VideoChatGPT Bench, matching LLaVA-OneVision (6272 tokens) with 79% fewer tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HICom-7B with only 680 tokens substantially outperforms VideoLLaMA2 (1152 tokens) on MV-Bench (64.1 vs. 54.6) and Ego-Schema (60.5 vs. 51.7), while also surpassing it on VideoMME (58.1 vs. 47.9). Against Tarsier (2304 tokens), HICom wins on MV-Bench (64.1 vs. 62.6) and Ego-Schema (60.5 vs. 56.0) with 70% fewer tokens. Compared to LLaVA-Video (6272 tokens), HICom at 1328 tokens achieves 2.43% higher average on the three MC benchmarks (58.9/64.7/62.2 vs. 60.6/58.6/57.3) while saving 78.8% tokens; notably, HICom beats LLaVA-Video on medium and long VideoMME segments but trails slightly on short videos. Against LLaVA-OneVision (6272 tokens), HICom at 1328 tokens matches or exceeds it on MV-Bench (64.7 vs. 56.7) and Ego-Schema (62.2 vs. 60.1) while being slightly lower on VideoMME (58.9 vs. 58.5). On open-ended ActivityNet, HICom (59.4/3.7) surpasses LLaVA-OneVision (56.6/3.6) and matches Tarsier (59.5/3.6) with far fewer tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

HICom departs from the dominant paradigm of unconditional video token compression (spatial pooling in LLaVA-Next-Video/OneVision, Q-Former in BLIP-2/Otter, convolution in VideoLLaMA2, clustering in Chat-Univi) by introducing the user instruction as an explicit condition that guides what to retain during compression. It also extends the instruction-guided idea from LLaMA-VID, which used instruction only as a per-frame auxiliary token, to a full hybrid-level (local grouped + global learnable) compression mechanism where the instruction shapes the attention at both granularities. Architecturally, it follows the LLaVA-style SigLIP + MLP projector + LLM pipeline but inserts a novel conditional pre-training stage between alignment and instruction tuning, repurposing adaptive layer norm (from DiT) and cross-attention as lightweight injection modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 78.8% token reduction with a 2.43% average performance gain over LLaVA-Video is practically significant for deploying video MLLMs under tight compute budgets, and the conditional pre-training stage with HICom-248K provides a reusable training recipe for any instruction-conditioned compression design. The finding that conditional compression benefits long videos more than short ones (where dense sampling already suffices) offers a clear deployment guideline. Open questions include whether the fixed 32-frame training sampling limits scalability to very long videos, whether the approach transfers to high-resolution image compression, and whether the conditional pre-training stage generalizes beyond the specific injection modules tested.
