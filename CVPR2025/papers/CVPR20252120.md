## Problem

Existing document image retrieval methods are restricted to single-modality matching: query-by-image limits retrieval to the same coarse semantic category (e.g., all receipts), while query-by-text relies on OCR and misses visual layout, style, and non-text elements. No benchmark exists for retrieving document images using natural language queries with fine-grained semantic descriptions, leaving a critical gap between real-world user needs and available evaluation resources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252120/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language query (typically 10–20 words with at least 3 words overlapping the document's OCR text) and a gallery of 41,795 real-world document images spanning 247 categories (letters, reports, forms, advertisements, charts, etc.) as input. The recall stage produces a top-100 candidate list via dot-product similarity between single-vector embeddings (4 KB per image for SigLIP), and the re-ranking stage refines this into a final ranked list scored by Recall@1, Recall@10, and MRR@10. Document images vary in resolution and quality, including handwritten text, out-of-vocabulary words, and visually rich elements like cartoon illustrations.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage retrieval pipeline. In the recall stage, SigLIP is fine-tuned with LoRA on its visual encoder (SigLIP-Image-LoRA) using contrastive learning on the NL-DIR training set, achieving 89.72 Recall@10; alternatively, CLIP/BLIP text encoders are aligned via LoRA with frozen visual encoders from generative VDU models (Pix2Struct, DocOwl1.5, UReader, TextMonkey). In the re-ranking stage, a cross-attention module (from BLIP-ITM) is combined with the Pix2Struct visual encoder to perform fine-grained image-text interaction over the top-100 candidates, trained with hard negative mining (top-10 hardest negatives from the recall stage) and a combined pointwise + pairwise loss, yielding 81.03 Recall@1. The NL-DIR dataset itself is constructed by generating layout-aware queries via ChatGPT prompted with OCR text formatted to simulate document layout, then filtering through weighted scoring (3:3:2:2) from ChatGPT, Qwen-VL-Plus, CLIP, and BLIP, followed by manual verification to remove hallucinated or overly generic queries.

## Results

On the NL-DIR benchmark, the proposed two-stage approach (SigLIP-Image-LoRA recall + BLIP-ITM/Pix2Struct re-ranking) achieves Recall@1 of 81.03, Recall@10 of 94.17, and MRR@10 of 85.68. In the zero-shot setting, SigLIP-So400m leads all contrastive VLMs with Recall@10 of 61.18, while generative VDU models (e.g., Qwen2-VL) achieve at most Recall@10 of 1.66 without fine-tuning. After fine-tuning, SigLIP-Image-LoRA reaches Recall@10 of 89.72 in the recall stage alone, and the full two-stage pipeline surpasses the strongest LVLM-based baseline ColPali (Recall@1: 79.65, Recall@10: 91.64) on all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed two-stage method (BLIP-ITM + Pix2Struct + extra cross-attention) outperforms all compared baselines on every metric. Against the strongest LVLM-based baseline ColPali (zero-shot, Recall@1: 79.65, Recall@10: 91.64, MRR@10: 83.79), the paper's method gains +1.38 on Recall@1, +2.53 on Recall@10, and +1.89 on MRR@10. DSE (zero-shot) trails further behind at Recall@1 of 69.43 and MRR@10 of 75.61. The OCR-based pipeline (Tesseract + BGE) is the weakest at Recall@1 of 52.83 and MRR@10 of 58.85, confirming the advantage of OCR-free visual encoders. Among the paper's own ablations, replacing Pix2Struct's visual encoder with SigLIP's or using Pix2Struct's original cross-attention instead of an extra module degrades performance substantially (e.g., Recall@1 drops from 81.03 to 72.68 or 25.99), highlighting the importance of fine-grained visual representation and additional cross-attention for re-ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of document image retrieval and cross-modal vision-language modeling. Traditional DIR methods are limited to single-modality matching (image-to-image within the same category, or OCR-based text retrieval), while existing cross-modal retrieval models (CLIP, SigLIP) are trained on natural images and lack document-specific understanding. The paper repurposes both contrastive VLMs and generative VDU models (Pix2Struct, Donut, Nougat, etc.) for a new task formulation—retrieving document images via fine-grained natural language queries—without proposing a fundamentally new architecture, instead combining a recall stage (contrastive matching) with a re-ranking stage (cross-attention interaction). Concurrent works like ColPali and DSE address similar goals but rely on web screenshots or PDF renderings rather than authentic real-world document images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NL-DIR provides the first dedicated, large-scale benchmark (41K real-world documents, 205K fine-grained queries) for evaluating cross-modal document retrieval, filling a critical gap that prevented existing models from receiving adequate supervised signals in the document domain. The finding that OCR-free models substantially outperform OCR-dependent pipelines on visually rich documents (charts, advertisements, handwritten text) suggests a practical path toward more robust document retrieval systems. Open questions include whether the benchmark generalizes to other document domains (scientific, legal, medical), how performance scales with corpus size beyond 41K images, and whether the two-stage approach can be further compressed into a single efficient model without sacrificing the fine-grained interaction gains.
