## Problem

Recent TAD models rely on large Transformer feature extractors whose computational cost is prohibitive for deployment in resource-constrained settings like autonomous driving and robotics. Existing channel pruning methods reduce layer width, producing slim-and-tall networks that suffer from poor GPU parallelization due to small matrix multiplications. This paper asks whether reducing model depth—dropping entire Transformer blocks while retaining layer width—can compress TAD models into a shallow-and-wide architecture that is both more hardware-efficient and easier to recover from.

## Input / Output

The model consumes untrimmed video as 768-frame segments at 160² resolution, processed by a sliding-window feature extractor (VideoMAE-S with 12 blocks or VideoMAE-L with 24 blocks) whose segment-level features are concatenated temporally and averaged spatially before being fed to an ActionFormer detection head. The output is a set of action instances, each with a start time, end time, and category label. Because the feature extractor processes many overlapping windows while the detection head sees only a single video-level feature, 95% of total forward-pass FLOPs are spent in the backbone, making it the primary compression target.

## Method

The proposed Progressive Block Drop iteratively removes one Transformer block per step, each step comprising (1) a Block Selection Evaluator that scores every candidate block by the mAP drop on the training set when that block is removed, and (2) a Cross-Depth Alignment fine-tuning stage that uses LoRA adapters on the attention layers of remaining blocks to recover performance. The alignment loss combines three terms: a feature-level MSE between intermediate block outputs of the pruned and uncompressed models (compressing multi-block information into a single block), a KL-divergence loss on action-class logits, and a GIoU loss on predicted temporal bounding boxes. After training, LoRA weights are merged back into the backbone so inference incurs no extra overhead. The iteration continues until the pruned model can no longer match the uncompressed model's performance, yielding a 25% MACs reduction with equal or better mAP on THUMOS14 and ActivityNet-1.3, and the method is shown to be orthogonal to channel pruning for further compression.

## Results

On THUMOS14 with VideoMAE-S, dropping 3 blocks reduces MACs by 23.9% (286.3G → 218.0G) while mAP@tIoU=0.5 improves from 70.43 to 70.47. On ActivityNet-1.3, the same 3-block drop reduces MACs by 24.9% (274.1G → 205.8G) with mAP(Avg) rising from 37.75 to 37.77. With the deeper VideoMAE-L backbone (24 blocks), dropping 6 blocks cuts MACs by 24.9% (3886.9G → 2918.6G) while mAP improves by 1.24 points (76.01 → 77.25), and inference time drops from 795.9 ms to 602.6 ms (1.32× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against channel pruning (DepGraph [13]) at matched computational cost. At nearly identical MACs (~218G) on THUMOS14 with VideoMAE-S, the proposed block drop method achieves a 1.29× inference speedup (81.2 ms) versus only 1.09× for channel pruning (96.6 ms), demonstrating that reducing depth is more hardware-efficient than reducing width for GPU inference. In terms of compression ratio at matched accuracy (~70.2% mAP), combining block drop with pruning reduces MACs by 30%, whereas pruning alone achieves only 10% reduction. The method also outperforms the uncompressed baseline in mAP after 1–3 block drops on both THUMOS14 and ActivityNet-1.3, a result the authors attribute to the cross-depth alignment acting as a form of regularization. On the deeper VideoMAE-L backbone, the performance gain over the uncompressed model is more pronounced (+1.24 mAP at 25% MACs reduction), suggesting greater redundancy in larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant channel/width pruning paradigm for model compression by targeting network depth instead, motivated by the observation that shallow-and-wide architectures exploit GPU parallelism more effectively than slim-and-tall ones. It repurposes ideas from curriculum learning (progressive multi-step dropping), parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), and knowledge distillation (cross-depth feature alignment from the uncompressed teacher) into a unified compression pipeline for TAD backbones. Unlike prior depth-pruning work such as UPDP (which compresses layers within a block) or dynamic block skipping (which only accelerates training), this method operates at the block level, permanently removes blocks, and recovers performance through structured alignment, yielding a deployable model with no inference overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that progressive depth reduction can simultaneously cut ~25% of MACs and improve mAP (up to +2.28% on VideoMAE-L) challenges the common assumption that compression must sacrifice accuracy, and the demonstrated orthogonality with channel pruning opens a path to stacked compression strategies for deployment in resource-constrained settings like autonomous driving and robotics. The hardware-efficiency argument (1.29× vs 1.09× speedup at matched MACs) is broadly applicable to any Transformer-based video model. Limitations include reliance on the uncompressed model during the compression phase, multiple sequential training rounds, and evaluation primarily on VideoMAE backbones with a small set of detection heads; whether the approach transfers to non-Transformer backbones or to pre-training stages remains open.
