## Problem

Existing methods that jointly optimize NeRF and camera poses either require good pose initialization, monocular depth priors, or are limited to forward-facing scenes, and they struggle with large camera motions because they directly optimize discrete camera-to-world mappings. This paper addresses the gap by eliminating all prior dependencies while still handling large rotations and translations in a monocular video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250560/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only a monocular RGB video and known camera intrinsics as input, requiring no depth maps, pose initialization, or COLMAP output. It produces per-frame camera poses (rotation and translation relative to a chosen world frame), a full-scene SDF geometry, novel-view rendered images, and depth maps. The world coordinate system is anchored at the middle frame of the training set, and all other poses are recovered by integrating the learned continuous motion up to that reference.

## Method

The core idea is to replace discrete per-frame pose optimization with a continuous camera motion model: an MLP φ_v(t) predicts angular velocity ω(t) and linear velocity v(t), which are integrated via Euler's method to yield relative transformations between any two time steps, thereby decomposing large motions into small increments. A time-dependent SDF-based NeRF represents local scene geometry at each time step (trained on neighboring frames), avoiding the instability of mapping 3D points through noisy early-stage poses into a global frame. Training is guided by five losses: standard RGB rendering, Eikonal regularization on the SDF, an SDF-flow consistency loss enforcing rigid scene change under the predicted motion (derived from [18]), a photometric consistency loss projecting surface points to neighboring frames, and an SDF consistency loss aligning local geometry with the world frame (introduced after 200 epochs). In a second stage, the motion network is frozen and the NeRF is fine-tuned in the world coordinate system using all frames with fixed poses, recovering full-scene geometry and appearance.

## Results

On the Co3D dataset (large camera rotations), the method achieves a minimum of 83% reduction in ATE (trajectory error), 63% in RPE_t (relative translation), and 83% in RPE_r (relative rotation) compared to the best baseline. For depth estimation, it reduces the square relative error by at least 82% on Co3D and 78% on ScanNet. On novel-view synthesis, it achieves comparable PSNR to CF3DGS (e.g., 26.36 vs. 26.21 on Bench) while significantly outperforming NeRFmm and NoPe-NeRF across all image quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRFmm [31], the proposed method wins decisively on all three evaluation axes (depth, pose, and image quality), with particularly large gaps on Co3D where NeRFmm's RPE_r reaches 2.5–3.5° versus the proposed method's 0.03–0.13°. Against NoPe-NeRF [2], which uses a pre-trained depth network as a prior, the method still achieves substantially lower pose errors (e.g., ATE 0.001 vs. 0.053 on Bench) and depth errors (SqRel 0.085 vs. 0.945 on Bench), demonstrating that the continuous motion modeling outperforms depth-prior-based approaches even without any external geometric information. Against CF3DGS [6] (a 3DGS-based method also using depth priors), the proposed method achieves comparable or better PSNR on novel-view synthesis (e.g., 26.36 vs. 26.21 on Bench) while producing significantly more accurate camera poses (ATE 0.001 vs. 0.017 on Bench) and depth maps. The key differentiator is that the proposed method is prior-free, requiring only RGB images and camera intrinsics, whereas both NoPe-NeRF and CF3DGS depend on a pre-trained depth estimator for initialization and guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDF-based NeRF formulation from NeuS [29] as its scene representation and borrows the SDF-flow/scene-flow linear relationship from Neural SDF Flow [18] to derive a motion-geometry consistency loss. It departs from prior joint NeRF+pose optimization methods (NeRFmm, BARF, NoPe-NeRF) that directly optimize discrete camera-to-world transformations, and from 3DGS-based SLAM-style pipelines (CF3DGS) that learn relative frame-to-frame mappings. The key conceptual shift is representing camera motion as continuous angular velocity and velocity integrated over time, combined with a time-dependent NeRF that captures local geometry per time step rather than a single global scene, thereby avoiding the optimization difficulty of large camera-to-world mappings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for COLMAP or depth priors while handling large camera rotations that break existing methods, this work offers a practical path toward fully self-supervised 3D reconstruction from casual monocular video. The finding that a prior-free method outperforms depth-prior-based methods (NoPe-NeRF, CF3DGS) in depth estimation is notable and suggests that temporal continuity modeling can substitute for external geometric supervision. However, the method's reliance on visual cues limits it in low-texture or reflective scenes, and the comparable (not superior) novel-view synthesis quality relative to CF3DGS indicates that rendering fidelity still lags behind depth-prior approaches. Open questions include scalability to longer videos, dynamic scenes, and whether the continuous motion formulation extends naturally to multi-view or sparse-view settings.
