## Problem

BEV-based radar-camera fusion for 3D object detection is bottlenecked by inaccurate depth estimation during image-to-BEV transformation, which causes unaligned visual content to be naively combined with sparse radar BEV features. This paper asks whether a query-based paradigm that samples instance-relevant features from both the original image view and the BEV can bypass this depth-estimation dependency while still exploiting the complementary strengths of both modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250940/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-camera RGB images (6 cameras on nuScenes at 256×704 to 640×1600; a single monocular camera on VoD) and multi-frame 4D radar point clouds carrying range, azimuth, velocity, and RCS attributes. Radar points undergo two distinct pre-processing paths: z-coordinates are forced to 1 before projection onto the image plane (to maximize in-FOV coverage for the depth head) and set to 0 before pillar-based BEV encoding. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes with class labels and velocity estimates, produced by 900 (nuScenes) or 600 (VoD) object queries refined over 6 decoder layers.

## Method

RaCFormer replaces BEV-only fusion with a query-based dual-view sampling framework: object queries are initialized in a linearly increasing circular distribution (k concentric circles with growth factor α≈1.25) that aligns with camera projection geometry and ensures adequate far-range density. A radar-aware depth head projects radar points (z=1) onto the image, extends each to full image height, embeds discretized depth and RCS values, and concatenates them with C4 image features to produce a refined depth distribution that feeds the lift-splat-shoot module for camera BEV generation. On the radar side, an Implicit Dynamic Catcher built on ConvGRU accumulates hidden states across T frames of radar BEV features to capture Doppler-derived temporal motion cues. The 6-layer transformer decoder then performs ray sampling in both the image view (projecting sampling points onto multi-camera images across timestamps) and the BEV (deformable attention over historical BEV features), aggregating spatio-temporal features via an adaptive mixer before classification and regression heads decode the refined queries.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, RaCFormer achieves 64.9% mAP and 70.2% NDS with VoVNet-99 and 6 past + 6 future frames, surpassing the strongest LiDAR-based method VoxelNeXt (64.5% mAP, 70.0% NDS). On the nuScenes validation set with ResNet-50 at 256×704, RaCFormer reaches 54.1% mAP and 61.3% NDS, outperforming the best radar-camera fusion baseline HyDRa by 4.7% mAP and 2.8% NDS. On the View-of-Delft dataset, RaCFormer achieves 54.4% mAP across the entire annotated area and 78.6% mAP in the region of interest, securing first-place ranking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the nuScenes validation set, RaCFormer (ResNet-50) outperforms all radar-camera fusion baselines: it beats HyDRa by 4.7% mAP (54.1 vs. 49.4) and 2.8% NDS, CRN by 5.1% mAP, and RCBEVDet by 8.8% mAP. On the nuScenes test set with VoVNet-99, RaCFormer surpasses HyDRa by 1.8% mAP and 1.7% NDS, and with 6 past + 6 future frames, it exceeds HVDetFusion(+8) by 4.0% mAP and 2.8% NDS while also matching or exceeding LiDAR-based VoxelNeXt (64.9 vs. 64.5 mAP). On the View-of-Delft dataset, RaCFormer leads RCBEVDet by 4.45% mAP in the entire annotated area and by 8.77% mAP in the region of interest (78.57% vs. 69.80%), and outperforms RCFusion by 9.34% mAP in the ROI. The method also demonstrates superior robustness: under full camera dropout, RaCFormer retains 27.2% car AP versus CRN's 12.8%, and under full radar dropout it achieves 52.8% versus CRN's 43.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaCFormer departs from the dominant BEV-based fusion paradigm (CRN, RCBEVDet, HyDRa, HVDetFusion) that transforms both camera and radar features into a shared BEV space before fusing, arguing that this approach is bottlenecked by inaccurate depth estimation in the image-to-BEV transformation. Instead, it adopts a query-based cross-perspective framework built on RayFormer's ray sampling mechanism, extending it with a circular (rather than radial) query initialization to address distance-dependent query sparsity. The BEV generation pipeline reuses Lift-Splat-Shoot for camera features and a pillar encoder for radar, while the temporal radar encoding introduces a ConvGRU-based implicit dynamic catcher—a component not previously applied to radar BEV in this fusion context. The transformer decoder integrates scale-adaptive self-attention from SparseBEV and the adaptive mixer from Adamixer, positioning the work as a careful recombination of established query-based detection components with radar-specific enhancements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that sampling distortion-free image-view features alongside BEV features via object queries can meaningfully close the accuracy gap between radar-camera and LiDAR-based detection (64.9% mAP vs. VoxelNeXt's 64.5% on nuScenes test), which is practically significant for cost-sensitive autonomous driving deployments. The radar-aware depth prediction—projecting radar points with a fixed height to guide LSS depth estimation—is a simple but effective trick that addresses a known weakness of monocular view transformation. However, the approach still depends on multi-frame temporal context (6 past + 6 future frames for best results) and the radar's limited vertical resolution constrains the depth correction; whether the cross-perspective sampling advantage persists in more cluttered or long-tail scenarios remains an open question. The VoD results (78.57% mAP in ROI) suggest the method generalizes beyond nuScenes, but the single-dataset ablation protocol limits confidence in the individual component contributions.
