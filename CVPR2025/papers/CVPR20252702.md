## Problem

Existing secure face recognition systems apply homomorphic encryption only to extracted features, leaving the raw face image and the feature extraction step exposed to an adversarial server. Performing full face recognition under fully homomorphic encryption is infeasible with standard CNNs due to their high multiplicative depth, inability to handle high-resolution images, and the fact that cosine similarity cannot be directly computed in the encrypted domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252702/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The client encrypts a probe face image (RGB, resized to 64×64, 96×96, or 128×128) using a CKKS public key and sends it to the server along with a claimed identity. The server performs feature extraction, L2-normalized cosine similarity scoring, and threshold comparison entirely in the ciphertext domain, returning a single encrypted scalar (positive or negative) that only the client can decrypt to determine match or no-match. An offline enrollment stage similarly encrypts and stores reference face features on the server.

## Method

CryptoFaceNet divides a face image into a grid of non-overlapping patches (e.g., 32×32) and processes each with an independent shallow patch CNN (PCNN), jointly trained with an ArcFace loss plus a jigsaw-puzzle auxiliary task that encodes positional information. Under FHE, all PCNNs are evaluated in parallel and their features are fused via a decomposed vector-matrix product reduced to simple ciphertext addition, yielding near-resolution-independent latency. A depth-optimal shifted AESPA block fuses the quadratic Hermite activation coefficient into the preceding convolution, saving two multiplicative levels per block so that the entire network requires only one bootstrapping operation. For the cosine similarity score, the paper introduces a distribution-aware quadratic polynomial p(t) = β₂t² + β₁t + β₀ to approximate 1/√t for L2 normalization, with control points set at mean±std of the feature norm distribution, achieving log₂|p(t)−q(t)| ≤ 2⁻¹⁰ at multiplicative depth 2.

## Results

On the five standard FR benchmarks (LFW, AgeDB, CALFW, CPLFW, CFP-FP) at 64×64 resolution, CryptoFaceNet4 achieves 89.42% average verification accuracy with 1,364 s online latency, compared to MPCNN-ResNet44 (89.64%, 9,845 s) and AutoFHE-ResNet32 (82.69%, 4,001 s). Scaling to 128×128 resolution, CryptoFaceNet16 reaches 91.46% average accuracy while maintaining near-constant latency (1,446 s) and modest RAM (277 GB). The system requires only 1 bootstrapping operation versus 31–43 for MPCNN, reducing bootstrapping's share of total latency from ~70% to ~10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MPCNN-ResNet44 (the highest-accuracy baseline at 89.64%), CryptoFaceNet4 achieves a near-matching 89.42% (−0.22%) while being 7.2× faster (1,364 s vs. 9,845 s) and using 17 GB less RAM. Against MPCNN-ResNet32, CryptoFaceNet4 wins on both accuracy (+3.82%, 89.42% vs. 85.60%) and speed (5.4× faster). Against AutoFHE-ResNet32, CryptoFaceNet4 improves accuracy by +6.73% (89.42% vs. 82.69%) and is 2.9× faster (1,364 s vs. 4,001 s). On the more challenging mixed-quality IJB-B and IJB-C benchmarks, CryptoFace consistently outperforms both AutoFHE and MPCNN in AUC. For the 1:128 closed-set identification task, CryptoFace achieves 92.19% Rank-1 accuracy, surpassing MPCNN's 88.28% by +3.91%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CryptoFace builds directly on two recent advances in FHE-compatible neural networks: MPCNN's multiplexed parallel convolutions for efficient encrypted convolution and AESPA's low-degree Hermite polynomial activations for reducing multiplicative depth. It departs from prior secure face recognition systems (Boddeti 2018, HERS 2022), which encrypted only extracted features and required the client to perform feature extraction locally, by moving the entire pipeline—feature extraction, matching, and thresholding—into the encrypted domain. The key architectural departure is the mixture of shallow patch CNNs, inspired by Vision Transformer patching, which replaces a single deep full-image CNN with many shallow networks evaluated in parallel, fundamentally changing the FHE computation graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that end-to-end encrypted face recognition is practically feasible, removing the need for clients to trust their own devices for feature extraction and protecting both user biometrics and server reference databases simultaneously. However, the system remains far from real-time deployment: a single verification takes over 20 minutes and requires ~270 GB of RAM, limiting near-term applicability to offline or batch scenarios. Open questions include whether the semi-honest threat model can be extended to malicious parties, whether the patch-based approach generalizes to other biometric modalities, and whether further FHE hardware acceleration can close the gap to practical latency.
