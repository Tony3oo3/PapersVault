## Problem

Absolute pose regression (APR) networks rely on fixed regression head weights that cannot adapt to the diverse appearance conditions (lighting, season, perspective) present in natural scenes, causing a significant domain gap between training and test distributions. This limitation degrades localization accuracy and forces scene-specific training, reducing the practical utility of APRs for real-world deployment across heterogeneous environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250328/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB query image as input and produces a 6-DoF camera pose in one forward pass: a 3D translation vector (x ∈ R³) and a unit quaternion (q ∈ R⁴) encoding orientation. No reference image database, feature matching, or multi-step pipeline is required at inference. For the multi-scene variant, the network additionally identifies which of N scenes the query belongs to before regressing the pose, all within a single model rather than per-scene networks.

## Method

HyperPose inserts a hypernetwork into existing APR architectures that generates input-adaptive weights for the position and orientation regression heads, replacing fixed pretrained weights with dynamically computed ones conditioned on the query image's feature representation. For single-scene models, the hypernetwork is an MLP that maps the backbone's feature vector (or AtLoc's attention-reweighted representation) to the weights of the regression head layers. For the multi-scene MS-HyperPose, the hypernetwork receives both the 1×1-convolution-encoded activation maps from the shared EfficientNet-B0 backbone and the scene-specific latent vectors from dual-branch Transformers (Deformable DETR-style encoders/decoders with positional encoding), producing weights for three-layer MLP regression heads. Training follows a three-step protocol: joint optimization with an uncertainty-weighted combined loss (Eq. 5), followed by separate fine-tuning of the position and orientation heads. The approach is architecture-agnostic and was validated by augmenting both a simple CNN+MLP baseline and the SOTA AtLoc model, as well as extending the dual-branch Transformer multi-scene APR.

## Results

On Cambridge Landmarks (outdoor), the single-scene Baseline APR with hypernetwork achieves the best APR average of 1.03 m / 2.67° (median position/orientation error), while the multi-scene MS-HyperPose achieves 1.27 m / 2.41°, outperforming the prior multi-scene SOTA MS-Trans (1.28 m / 2.73°). On 7 Scenes (indoor), Baseline APR w/ hypernet reaches 0.19 m / 7.48° and MS-HyperPose reaches 0.18 m / 7.00°, again the best among all APR variants. On Oxford RobotCar, the hypernetwork-enhanced AtLoc reduces median position error by 29–30% over vanilla AtLoc (e.g., 7.86 m vs. 11.10 m on FULL1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among all single-scene APRs on Cambridge Landmarks, Baseline APR w/ hypernet (1.03 m / 2.67°) outperforms the next-best APR, PoseNetLearn (1.42 m / 2.85°), by 0.39 m in position and 0.18° in orientation, and beats AtLoc (1.69 m / 4.39°) by 0.66 m / 1.72°. For multi-scene APR, MS-HyperPose (1.27 m / 2.41°) edges out MS-Trans (1.28 m / 2.73°) by 0.01 m / 0.32° and substantially outperforms MSPN (2.47 m / 5.34°) by 1.20 m / 2.93°. On 7 Scenes, MS-HyperPose (0.18 m / 7.00°) is the best multi-scene APR, beating MS-Trans (0.18 m / 7.28°) on orientation and MSPN (0.20 m / 7.56°) on both metrics. HyperPose remains well behind the structure-based SOTA DSAC* (0.15 m / 0.4° on Cambridge; 0.03 m / 1.41° on 7 Scenes), but is competitive with top RPR methods such as RelocGNN (0.91 m / 2.3°) and NC-EssNet (0.85 m / 2.8°) on Cambridge. On Oxford RobotCar, the hypernetwork-enhanced AtLoc consistently reduces median position error versus vanilla AtLoc across all four sequences (e.g., 7.86 m vs. 11.10 m on FULL1, 8.38 m vs. 12.2 m on FULL2), while still trailing the sequence-based MapNet on mean error but matching or exceeding it on median orientation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper injects hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2016) into the regression heads of existing absolute pose regression (APR) architectures, making the final pose-estimation layers input-adaptive rather than fixed. For single-scene APR, it extends both the original PoseNet formulation (Kendall et al., 2015) and the attention-based AtLoc model (Wang et al., 2020); for multi-scene APR, it augments the dual-branch Transformer architecture of MS-Trans (Shavit et al., 2021) with hypernetwork-generated weights. The conceptual departure from prior APR work is that the regression head weights are no longer a single learned constant but are dynamically computed per query image, addressing the domain gap between training and test appearance conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hypernetwork-augmented APR approach offers a general, drop-in mechanism to improve any existing APR model without altering the backbone, which is practically attractive for edge deployment where APR's low latency and memory footprint are key advantages. The Extended Cambridge Landmarks (ECL) dataset, generated via InstructPix2Pix, provides a useful stress-test for seasonal and lighting robustness that standard benchmarks lack. However, the performance gains are modest (e.g., ~4% position error reduction on Cambridge for the baseline APR), and the multi-scene model's size balloons from 71 MB to 571 MB, raising practical deployment concerns. Open questions include whether the hypernetwork overhead justifies the gains at scale, whether the synthetic ECL variations adequately capture real-world appearance drift, and whether the approach transfers to even larger multi-scene settings.
