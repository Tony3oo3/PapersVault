## Problem

Diffusion Transformer (DiT) video generation scales quadratically in the number of pixels, making high-resolution minute-length video generation prohibitively expensive and limiting most models to 10–20 second clips. While linear-complexity State Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba offer a path to linear scaling, they suffer from an adjacency preservation issue: rearranging 3D video tensors into 1D sequences breaks spatial and temporal locality, degrading generation quality. No prior framework simultaneously achieves linear complexity, high resolution, and minute-length generation without compromising video consistency.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (rewritten by LLaMa-3.1) as input, which is encoded by three text encoders (UL2, ByT5, MetaCLIP) to condition cross-attention layers. It produces photorealistic videos up to 68 seconds at 512p resolution and 16 fps (up to 1088 raw frames) on a single H100 GPU, operating in a Temporal AutoEncoder latent space. The model is trained on 300M ShutterStock text-image pairs and 24M ShutterStock text-video pairs, with fine-tuning on 3K curated high-quality videos from ShutterStock and RawFilm.

## Method

LinGen replaces the quadratic-complexity self-attention layers in a DiT with a MATE block composed of two linear-complexity branches. The MA-branch uses a bidirectional Mamba2 block equipped with Rotary-Major Scan (RMS), which alternates four scan schedules (spatial-row, spatial-column, temporal-row, temporal-column) across layers to preserve adjacency at negligible latency, plus review tokens (an average-pooled overview of the token tensor prepended to the sequence) to calibrate long-range correlations. The TE-branch is a TEmporal Swin Attention (TESA) block that computes windowed self-attention over small spatial windows (4×4) and medium-range temporal windows, with windows shifted alternately across layers to cross boundaries and enlarge the receptive field. Training follows a progressive recipe (256p image → 256p/512p video at increasing lengths from 17s to 68s) with hybrid text-image/video joint training and a final quality-tuning stage on 3K high-quality videos, all using Flow Matching with a linear-quadratic t-schedule in the latent space.

## Results

On VBench-Long, LinGen-4B achieves a Quality Score of 83.77% and Total Score of 81.76%, comparable to Runway Gen-3 (84.11% / 82.32%) and Kling (83.39% / 81.85%), while significantly outperforming OpenSora V1.2 (81.35% / 79.76%). LinGen generates up to 1088 raw frames on a single GPU, far exceeding Gen-3 (256), Kling (313), and OpenSora (408). In human evaluation, LinGen achieves a 75.6% win rate over its DiT-4B baseline and comparable win rates against commercial models (50.5% vs Gen-3, 52.1% vs LumaLabs, 49.1% vs Kling). In terms of efficiency, LinGen achieves up to 15× FLOPs reduction and 11.5× latency reduction over DiT-4B for 68-second 512p video generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Runway Gen-3, LinGen is slightly behind on Quality Score (83.77% vs 84.11%) and Total Score (81.76% vs 82.32%), but generates 4.2× more raw frames (1088 vs 256) on a single GPU; human evaluation shows a near-tie with a 50.5% win rate. Against Kling, LinGen edges ahead on Quality Score (83.77% vs 83.39%) but trails slightly on Total Score (81.76% vs 81.85%) and Semantic Score (73.73% vs 75.68%), with a 49.1% human win rate indicating parity. Against LumaLabs, LinGen achieves a 52.1% human win rate, suggesting comparable quality. Against OpenSora V1.2, LinGen clearly wins on all VBench-Long metrics (Quality Score +2.42%, Total Score +2.00%) and generates 2.7× more frames. Against its own DiT-4B baseline, LinGen achieves a 75.6% human win rate while delivering up to 15× FLOPs and 11.5× latency reduction, confirming that the MATE block improves quality over self-attention at a fraction of the cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

LinGen builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and the Flow Matching training objective (Lipman et al., 2022), adopting the Temporal AutoEncoder design and linear-quadratic t-schedule from Movie Gen (Polyak et al., 2024). Its core departure is replacing the quadratic-complexity self-attention layers with a linear-complexity MATE block whose MA-branch is built around bidirectional Mamba2 (Dao & Gu, 2024) augmented with Rotary-Major Scan and review tokens, while its TE-branch introduces a novel TESA block inspired by Swin-style windowed attention. This positions LinGen against prior efficient video generation work (factorized attention, Mamba-based models like Matten and DiM) that either retains quadratic complexity or is limited to low-resolution short clips, by comprehensively addressing the adjacency preservation issue that has been the primary obstacle to SSM-based video generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LinGen is the first framework to enable photorealistic high-resolution minute-length video generation on a single GPU with linear computational complexity, achieving up to 15× FLOPs and 11.5× latency reduction over DiT while matching the quality of commercial SOTA models (Gen-3, LumaLabs, Kling). This opens a path toward hour-length movie generation and real-time interactive video that was previously infeasible due to quadratic scaling. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to even longer sequences (beyond 68s), whether combining MATE with sampling distillation (e.g., consistency models) yields further gains, and whether the MATE block generalizes to other generative modalities such as 3D or audio-visual synthesis.
