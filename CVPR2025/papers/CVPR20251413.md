## Problem

Existing human pose methods treat comprehension (pose-to-text, pose-diff), generation (text-to-pose, pose estimation), and editing (pose correction) as isolated, single-modality tasks, with no unified framework handling all seven pose-relevant tasks simultaneously. Additionally, standard MLLMs relying solely on CLIP's visual encoder lack the fine-grained pose perception needed for accurate keypoint-level understanding and generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251413/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are 3D SMPL poses (root orientation γ∈R⁶ plus 6K joint rotations), RGB human images, and natural language instructions, which can be combined in various pairings (pose+text, image+text, pose pair, image pair, initial pose+modification instruction). 3D poses are first quantized into 80 discrete tokens from a 2048-entry codebook before entering the LLM's unified text-pose vocabulary. For pose generation and editing, the model uses L_p predefined learnable pose queries to predict all pose tokens in a single parallel forward step rather than autoregressive decoding, then a de-tokenizer reconstructs the SMPL parameters.

## Method

UniPose builds a unified representation space by tokenizing 3D SMPL poses via a VQ-VAE (1-D conv encoder/decoder with a 2048-entry codebook) into 80 discrete pose tokens that share a vocabulary with text tokens in an LLaVA-1.6 backbone. The visual branch employs a mixture-of-encoders: CLIP's ViT for global image-text alignment plus a pose-specific ViT pretrained on pose estimation for fine-grained keypoint features, concatenated along the channel dimension and projected to the LLM's embedding space. A mixed-attention mechanism applies causal attention to text tokens but bidirectional attention within pose token sequences, since joint positions lack causal dependencies; during generation, L_p learnable pose queries attend bidirectionally to each other while only accessing prior text tokens, enabling single-step parallel pose token prediction. Training follows four stages: (1) VQ-VAE pose tokenizer training, (2) pose-text alignment pretraining with LoRA on four pose-text tasks, (3) visual projector pretraining on three image-text tasks, and (4) multi-task instruction finetuning with 200 templates per task.

## Results

On pose comprehension tasks, UniPose achieves 85.6% Top-1 R-Precision on Pose-to-Text (vs. PoseScript's 91.6%), 67.9% on Pose-Diff (vs. PoseFix's 64.6%), 24.5% on Image-to-Text (vs. GPT-4V's 17.7%), and 13.5% on Image-Diff (vs. GPT-4V's 7.3%). On Text-to-Pose generation, UniPose reaches 73.7% Top-5 R^T2P and 308.6 MPJPE, outperforming PoseScript (73.3%, 318.0) and ChatPose (17.6%). On pose editing, UniPose achieves 270.3 MPJPE and 0.015 FID, surpassing PoseFix (300.2 MPJPE, 0.019 FID). On pose estimation, UniPose (94.7 MPJPE on 3DPW) trails dedicated methods like HMR2.0 (70.0) but substantially outperforms MLLM-based ChatPose (163.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPose outperforms PoseFix on Pose-Diff by 3.3 points in Top-1 R-Precision (67.9 vs. 64.6) and 5.6 points in Top-3 (88.6 vs. 83.0), and also leads on BLEU-4 (13.8 vs. 12.0). On Image-to-Text, UniPose substantially surpasses GPT-4V (24.5 vs. 17.7 Top-1), LLaVA (24.5 vs. 5.7), and Qwen-VL (24.5 vs. 8.9), with the gap attributed to the pose-specific visual encoder. On Text-to-Pose, UniPose edges out PoseScript (73.7 vs. 73.3 Top-5 R^T2P) and dramatically outperforms ChatPose (73.7 vs. 17.6) and ChatHuman (73.7 vs. 41.8). On pose editing, UniPose beats PoseFix by 29.9 MPJPE (270.3 vs. 300.2) and 0.004 FID (0.015 vs. 0.019). The one clear loss is on Pose-to-Text, where the task-specific PoseScript model retains a 6.0-point Top-1 advantage (91.6 vs. 85.6), and on pose estimation where dedicated methods like HMR2.0 (70.0 MPJPE) still outperform UniPose (94.7 MPJPE) on 3DPW.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPose builds on the VQ-VAE framework [58] to discretize 3D SMPL poses into a token sequence, and on LLaVA-1.6 as its visual-language backbone, but departs from prior pose-specific LLMs like ChatPose [18] (single-pose generation only) and task-isolated methods like PoseScript [13] and PoseFix [12] by unifying seven pose-related tasks under a shared text-pose vocabulary. The mixture-of-visual-encoders design (CLIP + pose-specific ViT) extends the standard single-CLIP-encoder paradigm in MLLMs, while the mixed-attention mechanism (causal for text, bidirectional for pose tokens) draws conceptual inspiration from Show-o [67] and Transfusion [76] but applies it specifically to the non-sequential nature of spatial joint positions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first framework to jointly handle pose comprehension, generation, and editing, UniPose demonstrates that multi-task training transfers knowledge across pose-relevant tasks and enables zero-shot capabilities (e.g., text-enhanced pose estimation) without additional training. However, it still lags behind specialized pose estimation methods (HMR2.0, SMPLer) on pure reconstruction accuracy, suggesting that unified frameworks may not yet replace task-specific models for precision-critical applications. Open questions include whether the unified vocabulary approach scales to multi-person scenes, video sequences, or more complex body models, and whether the mixed-attention trade-off (improved generation vs. slightly degraded comprehension) can be resolved.
