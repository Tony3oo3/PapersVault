## Problem

Existing single-view 3D reconstruction methods (depth estimation, latent embedding, novel-view synthesis) do not jointly model the object-ground relationship and camera parameters, causing reconstructed objects to appear floating or tilted on flat surfaces. This limitation severely degrades downstream 3D-aware editing tasks such as shadow/reflection synthesis and object pose manipulation, where accurate grounding and camera geometry are essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251932/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 object-centric RGB image and produces two dense per-pixel representations: (1) front and back surface pixel height maps (vertical distance in pixels between the object surface and its ground projection, normalized by image height), and (2) a perspective field consisting of a latitude map (elevation angle, normalized to [0,1]) and an up-vector map (roll angle encoded as (sin θ, cos θ) pairs). A post-hoc reprojection module then converts these into metric depth maps, 3D point clouds, and explicit camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters (focal length, rotation matrix).

## Method

ORG uses a PVTv2-b3 backbone (COCO-pretrained) with a SegFormer Mix Transformer-B3 decoder and residual CNN connections to regress both dense fields in a single forward pass, trained with L2 loss on 3.36M rendered images from Objaverse with randomized camera parameters. The key design choice is replacing depth with pixel height (object-to-ground distance in image space) for both front and back surfaces, which is disentangled from the camera model and naturally encodes the grounding constraint. A perspective-field-guided reprojection module then recovers camera parameters via grid search over FoV and rotation, and solves for per-pixel depth using two geometric constraints: the ground plane has constant Z, and each 3D point shares XY coordinates with its vertical ground projection. Ablations confirm that the joint pixel-height + perspective-field formulation outperforms depth + off-the-shelf camera estimation, validating that the representation design—not just the dataset—drives the improvement.

## Results

On the held-out Objaverse test set, ORG with its own estimated camera parameters achieves the best point-cloud reconstruction quality: LSIV 0.93 and Chamfer Distance 1.26, outperforming DUSt3R (LSIV 0.95, CD 1.29) and LeReS (LSIV 1.05, CD 1.31). For depth estimation, ORG attains AbsRel 19.1 and δ₁ 81.2%, slightly trailing DUSt3R (AbsRel 17.9, δ₁ 79.5%) but surpassing all other baselines. ORG also achieves the best pixel-height L1 error (15.4 px) and perspective-field errors (Lati-Vector 4.94°, Up-Vector 1.45°), demonstrating superior object-ground modeling. Even when paired with the off-the-shelf Ctrl-C camera estimator, ORG (LSIV 0.99, CD 1.27) still outperforms LeReS and MiDaS on point-cloud metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORG outperforms all depth-estimation baselines (MegaDepth, NDDepth, MiDaS, LeReS) on point-cloud metrics (LSIV and CD) when using its own camera estimation, and also beats them on pixel-height L1 error by a wide margin (15.4 vs. 20.4–31.1 px). Against DUSt3R, the strongest baseline, ORG achieves better LSIV (0.93 vs. 0.95) and CD (1.26 vs. 1.29) but slightly worse AbsRel (19.1 vs. 17.9) and δ₁ (81.2% vs. 79.5%), indicating a trade-off between depth-map accuracy and 3D geometric fidelity. On the unique perspective-field metrics (Lati-Vector and Up-Vector L1), ORG is the only method that directly predicts these, achieving 4.94° and 1.45° respectively, compared to MiDaS+Ctrl-C's 5.45° and 1.79°. Even with the off-the-shelf Ctrl-C camera estimator, ORG (LSIV 0.99, CD 1.27) surpasses LeReS (LSIV 1.05, CD 1.31) and MiDaS (LSIV 1.22, CD 1.39), confirming that the pixel-height representation itself contributes to better reconstruction. Qualitatively, ORG produces significantly more realistic shadows and reflections at object-ground contact points compared to LeReS and Zero-123, which suffer from floating or tilted objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two existing dense representations — pixel height (originally proposed for shadow generation under strict camera constraints by Sheng et al., ECCV 2022) and perspective fields (proposed for camera calibration by Jin et al., CVPR 2023) — into a joint framework that simultaneously estimates object geometry, ground relationship, and camera parameters from a single image. It departs from prior single-view 3D reconstruction methods (monocular depth estimation, latent embedding reconstruction, diffusion-based novel-view synthesis) that either assume known camera parameters, rely on simplified orthographic models, or ignore the object-ground relationship entirely, leading to 'floating' or tilted reconstructions. The architectural backbone (PVTv2 + SegFormer) is standard; the contribution lies in the formulation of jointly predicting both representations and the perspective-guided reprojection module that converts them into depth maps and point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in 3D-aware image editing: reconstructed objects that float or tilt on flat surfaces, which degrades shadow rendering, reflection synthesis, and pose manipulation. The finding that pixel height is a more natural and effective representation than depth for object-centric reconstruction (Table 3 ablation) suggests a useful design principle for future single-view geometry methods. Limitations include reliance on rendered Objaverse training data, scale-invariant (non-metric) output, and object-centric scope that does not extend to full multi-object scenes with complex ground geometry. Open questions include whether the joint formulation generalizes to scenes with multiple objects, occluded or non-planar ground, and whether metric scale can be recovered without additional sensors.
