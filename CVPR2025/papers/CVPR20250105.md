## Problem

Reference Audio-Visual Segmentation (Ref-AVS) requires locating a target object that can only be identified through the joint use of text, audio, and visual cues. Existing dual-modality SAM pipelines (text-visual or audio-visual) fail because the third modality is essential for disambiguation, while the prior triple-modality method EEMC suffers from poor spatio-temporal consistency, causing the segmented region to drift across frames. SAM2 offers strong video tracking but lacks any language or audio understanding, leaving a gap for pixel-level segmentation in Language-aided Audio-Visual Scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N video frames (encoded by a frozen ViT), a synchronized audio clip (encoded by VGGish into N temporal embeddings), and a natural-language expression (encoded by DistilRoBERTa). During training, SAM2 is supervised only on the first frame's mask, while the fusion transformer processes the full video sequence; at inference, SAM2's zero-shot video object segmentation propagates the first-frame prompt to all frames. The output is a per-frame binary segmentation mask for the referred target, evaluated with Jaccard, F-score, and their average.

## Method

SAM2-LOVE introduces a 6-layer bidirectional fusion transformer that compresses projected audio, visual, and text embeddings into a single learnable [seg] token, which then prompts SAM2's mask decoder. Two complementary token strategies ensure spatio-temporal consistency: (1) token propagation, where the [seg] token is prepended to each frame's multimodal sequence and updated through the transformer, capturing current-frame spatial features while retaining compressed history; and (2) token accumulation, where ViT [cls] tokens from all prior frames are concatenated into a [his] token appended to the sequence, preventing dilution of early-frame information. Training freezes SAM2's image encoder and memory attention while adapting its prompt encoder and mask decoder to the multimodal token, using a combined BCE + Dice loss on the first frame only. The unified perspective frames propagation as forward knowledge transfer and accumulation as backward knowledge transfer, making the two operations functionally opposed yet conceptually complementary.

## Results

On the Ref-AVS benchmark, SAM2-LOVE achieves a J&F of 47.7% on the Seen split, 69.4% on the Unseen split, and 58.5% on the Mix (S+U) split, outperforming the previous SOTA (EEMC) by +4.9, +12.2, and +8.5 percentage points respectively. The method also reports a NULLs score of 0.23, which is higher (worse) than EEMC's 0.007, indicating a weaker ability to reject null references. The largest gains appear on the Unseen split, where SAM2-LOVE reaches 66.5% J and 72.3% F, demonstrating strong generalization to out-of-domain categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM2-LOVE outperforms all six baselines across the Seen, Unseen, and Mix splits. Against the closest competitor EEMC (ECCV'24), it gains +4.9 J&F on Seen (47.7 vs. 42.8), +12.2 on Unseen (69.4 vs. 57.2), and +8.5 on Mix (58.5 vs. 50.0), with the Unseen improvement being particularly large. Compared to the dual-modality baselines augmented with the missing modality (GAVS+text at 39.6 Mix J&F, AVSegFormer+text at 41.7, ReferFormer+audio at 40.2, R2VOS+audio at 35.9, AVSBench+text at 40.3), SAM2-LOVE leads by 8–23 J&F points, confirming that fusing all three modalities simultaneously is critical. The sole area where SAM2-LOVE underperforms is the NULL split: its S-score of 0.23 is substantially worse than EEMC's 0.007, reflecting SAM2's inherent tendency to segment something even when no valid target exists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM2 video segmentation framework into the multimodal domain by introducing a fusion transformer that compresses text (DistilRoBERTa), audio (VGGish), and visual (ViT) representations into a single learnable [seg] token used to prompt SAM2. It departs from prior dual-modality SAM pipelines (EVF-SAM for text-visual, GAVS for audio-visual) that each lack one critical modality, and from EEMC which models all three modalities but suffers from spatio-temporal inconsistency. The token propagation and accumulation strategies are novel mechanisms for maintaining temporal coherence, framed as forward and backward knowledge transfer, which have no direct precedent in the SAM or referring segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a simple 6-layer fusion transformer combined with SAM2's zero-shot VOS capability can substantially outperform purpose-built multimodal segmentation architectures, suggesting that strong video tracking backbones are underutilized in multimodal settings. However, the null-reference limitation (S score 0.23 vs. EEMC's 0.007) reveals a fundamental weakness of the prompting paradigm: SAM2 is biased toward segmenting something, making rejection of invalid references an open problem. The approach's generality also raises questions about how it would handle scenarios where the three modalities provide conflicting or misaligned cues, which the authors acknowledge but do not address.
