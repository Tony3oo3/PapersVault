## Problem

Existing video-to-music generation methods optimize audio quality or rhythm alignment but fail to meet the professional standards of film music production: they lack the required 48kHz/24-bit fidelity, produce musically shallow results without thematic development, and ignore the full production workflow (spotting, composition, arrangement, mix). No prior work specifically targets the end-to-end professional pipeline for silent film clips with simultaneous control over rhythm, semantics, and visuals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252178/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a silent film clip (≥8 seconds) as input and produces a 48kHz cinematic-grade waveform as final output, along with intermediate MIDI and ABC notation representations. The visual processing stage extracts rhythm spots, text-based visual/motion descriptions, and shot-cut information from the clip; the multi-agent stage then operates a DAW (Reaper) to execute arrangement and mixing. A key distinguishing feature is that the output sampling rate reaches the 48kHz cinematic standard, which competing methods (32kHz or symbolic-only) cannot achieve, and every intermediate step is user-editable for interactive control.

## Method

FilmComposer is a three-module pipeline that simulates a professional musician's workflow. First, a visual processing module extracts rhythm spots via a modified Controllable Rhythm Transformer (CRT), visual attributes via GPT-4V, and motion/plot descriptions via optical flow and scene-cut detection. Second, a Rhythm-Controllable MusicGen (fine-tuned from MusicGen-Melody on the new MusicPro-7k dataset) jointly conditions on chromagram-based rhythm features and T5-encoded visual descriptions to generate a main melody aligned with the film's pacing. Third, an AutoGen-based multi-agent system assesses the melody's musicality (rejecting and regenerating if insufficient), then collaboratively arranges and mixes by producing a structured scheme (instrumentation, panning, reverb, volume envelopes) that an execution agent translates into Reaper DAW commands. The key architectural insight is bridging waveform generation (MusicGen) with symbolic music processing (MT3 transcription to MIDI/ABC notation) and LLM-driven multi-agent orchestration, enabling both high-fidelity audio output and fine-grained musical control that neither paradigm alone can provide.

## Results

On the MusicPro-7k test set, FilmComposer achieves the best performance across nearly all metrics: lowest FAD (0.207), highest ImageBind ranking score (0.131), highest Musicality score (10.78), best rhythm control (3834 cross-correlation), best dynamic variation (0.767 cosine similarity), and best instrumentation similarity (0.434). It is the only method besides ground truth to reach the 48 kHz cinematic sampling rate. The closest competitor, Rhythm-Controllable MusicGen (the ablated version without multi-agent arrangement), trails on every metric, with FAD 0.219, ImageBind 0.120, and Musicality 9.42.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FilmComposer outperforms all five external baselines on every reported metric. Against CMT [9] (symbolic, rhythm-focused), FilmComposer improves FAD from 0.644 to 0.207, Musicality from 10.02 to 10.78, and rhythm control from 3153 to 3834, while also reaching 48 kHz output that CMT cannot produce. Against Video2Music [19], the gap is even larger: FAD drops from 0.987 to 0.207 and ImageBind rises from 0.025 to 0.131. M2UGen [26] and VidMuse [36] are weaker on diversity (0.373 and 0.153 vs. 0.444) and musicality (9.40 and 8.02 vs. 10.78). The strongest single competitor is the internal ablation RC-MusicGen (without multi-agent arrangement), which is close on FAD (0.219 vs. 0.207) and rhythm (3646 vs. 3834) but lags significantly on musicality (9.42 vs. 10.78), dynamic variation (0.820 vs. 0.767), and instrumentation (0.506 vs. 0.434), confirming the multi-agent system's contribution. The only metric where FilmComposer does not lead is Diversity, where ground truth (0.451) slightly exceeds FilmComposer (0.444).

## Relation to Prior Work

FilmComposer builds directly on MusicGen (Copet et al., NeurIPS 2024) as its waveform generation backbone, adding a chromagram-based rhythm conditioner, and on CMT (Di et al., ACM MM 2021) for video rhythm extraction. It departs from prior video-to-music work (CMT, Video2Music, M2UGen, VidMuse) by combining waveform and symbolic music generation in a single pipeline and by introducing a multi-agent LLM system (via AutoGen) to handle arrangement, orchestration, and mixing—stages that prior methods entirely omit. The paper positions itself as the first to target professional film music production specifically, emphasizing the underexplored dimension of musical development (thematic evolution over time) alongside quality and musicality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MusicPro-7k, a professionally annotated film music dataset with rhythm spots and main melodies, addresses a genuine gap in the field and provides a resource for future rhythm-controlled music generation research. The multi-agent arrangement-and-mix pipeline, which drives a DAW (Reaper) via LLM-generated schemes, is a practical and extensible approach to post-generation music production that could transfer to other audio domains. However, the evaluation relies partly on self-proposed metrics (Chroma-based Diversity, multi-agent musicality scoring), the dataset is modest in scale (~7.4K samples), and the heavy dependence on prompt-engineered LLM agents raises questions about robustness and reproducibility. Open questions include whether the multi-agent arrangement generalizes beyond the tested genres, whether the pipeline scales to longer film scores, and whether the LLM-based mixing can truly match human sound-engineering judgment at scale.
