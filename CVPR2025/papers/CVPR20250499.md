## Problem

Fine-grained motion control in video diffusion models remains under-explored due to the intricate spatiotemporal correlations among frames and the complexity of modern architectures. Existing approaches require complex architectural modifications (e.g., guidance attention), are constrained to specific parameterized motion signals like camera poses, and sacrifice per-frame visual quality for motion rigidity. The paper asks whether motion control can be injected purely through structured latent noise sampling, without any changes to model architecture or training pipeline.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a reference video (from which RAFT optical flow is extracted), a text prompt, and optionally an initial frame image (for I2V) or user-defined polygon trajectories (for local object control). The optical flow is used to generate a warped noise tensor in image space, which is then downsampled into latent space (8×8 spatial, 4× temporal for CogVideoX) via nearest-neighbor temporal interpolation and mean-pooling spatially. A degradation parameter γ ∈ [0,1] blends the warped noise with fresh Gaussian noise, letting users dial motion adherence from loose (γ→1) to strict (γ→0). The output is a generated video at the base model's native resolution with the specified motion pattern.

## Method

The core contribution is a real-time noise warping algorithm that replaces random temporal Gaussianity with flow-correlated warped noise while preserving spatial i.i.d. Gaussianity. Rather than warping each frame through a chain from the initial frame (as in HIWYN), the algorithm iteratively warps noise between consecutive frames by building a bipartite graph that unifies expansion (one pixel maps to many) and contraction (gaps filled via backward flow) cases, tracking per-pixel flow density to weight contributions correctly. This yields O(D) time complexity per frame and a formal proof that output pixels remain i.i.d. standard Gaussians (Proposition 1). For training, the only change to the CogVideoX fine-tuning pipeline is substituting warped noise for random Gaussian noise; no architectural or loss modifications are made. At inference, warped noise from a reference video's optical flow initializes a standard DDIM sampling process, and the same mechanism supports local object control (via synthetic polygon flows), global camera control (via reference video flows), and arbitrary motion transfer (via flows from 3D engines or depth warping).

## Results

On local object motion control (VIPSeg), Go-with-the-Flow achieves FID 41.1, CoTracker mIoU 0.75, and FVD 0.47×10², substantially outperforming MotionClone (FID 85.2, FVD 1.26) and SG-I2V (FID 61.4, FVD 1.06). For camera movement transfer I2V on DL3DV, the method reaches FID 48.4 and FVD 0.34×10² versus MotionClone (FID 82.7, FVD 1.11) and ImageConductor (FID 89.2, FVD 0.91). In user studies, the method achieves 82% win rate for cut-and-drag local object motion and 90% for turnable camera movement control. The noise warping algorithm runs at 2.14 ms per 1024×1024 frame on an A100, 26× faster than HIWYN (55.2 ms) while preserving spatial Gaussianity (Moran's I = 0.00014, K-S p-value = 0.44).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Go-with-the-Flow outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated task. Against noise warping baselines (HIWYN, InfRes, PYoCo, CaV), the proposed algorithm achieves the lowest warping error (152.04 for super-resolution, 85.82 for relighting) while matching their Gaussianity guarantees, and runs 26× faster than HIWYN (2.14 ms vs. 55.2 ms per frame). For video motion control, the method dominates MotionClone on FID (41.1 vs. 85.2 on VIPSeg; 48.4 vs. 82.7 on DL3DV), FVD (0.47 vs. 1.26; 0.34 vs. 1.11), and optical flow error (0.36 vs. 0.48; 0.20 vs. 0.44). SG-I2V and DragAnything are outperformed on FID, FVD, and flow error for local object motion, with SG-I2V particularly struggling to distinguish local object motion from global camera shifts. DMT and MotionCtrl are surpassed on CoTracker mIoU (0.70 vs. 0.85 and 0.47) and flow error (0.41 vs. 0.28 and 0.85) for T2V motion transfer on DAVIS. ImageConductor is beaten on FID, FVD, mIoU, and flow error across both DAVIS and DL3DV/WonderJourney camera movement tasks. AnyV2V, compared qualitatively for first-frame editing, suffers from progressive foreground object disappearance, whereas Go-with-the-Flow preserves both the added object identity and the original camera motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noise warping paradigm introduced by HIWYN (Chang et al., ICLR 2024) from a training-free temporal consistency tool for image diffusion to a motion conditioning mechanism for video diffusion model fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is repurposing warped noise as a structured latent-space signal that encodes motion, rather than merely correlating noise across frames for visual coherence. The method also departs from prior motion control approaches (MotionClone, SG-I2V, MotionCtrl, DMT) that require architecture modifications, guidance attention, or parameterized camera signals, by treating the base diffusion model as a black box and injecting motion solely through the initial noise distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the approach—requiring only a data preprocessing change and standard fine-tuning—makes motion control accessible to any video diffusion model without architectural redesign, which is significant given the rapid evolution of base model designs. The real-time O(D) noise warping algorithm removes the primary practical barrier (quadratic cost in HIWYN) that previously limited noise warping to inference-time image editing. Open questions include how well the approach scales to very long videos, whether the noise-degradation parameter γ can be learned per-scene rather than manually tuned, and whether the method generalizes to 3D-consistent multi-object interactions beyond the single-polygon-per-object paradigm demonstrated here.
