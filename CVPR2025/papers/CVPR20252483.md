## Problem

Existing joint image-text probabilistic models suffer from image information loss: discretization-based methods (VQ-VAE) bottleneck per-token capacity due to codebook size, while diffusion-based methods (Transfusion-like) encode different image semantics at different noise levels, so clean-image input only activates representations suited to enhancement rather than full understanding. The paper asks how to efficiently ingest and fully exploit continuous image information within a single auto-regressive framework that handles both understanding and generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252483/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For image understanding, the model takes a 256×256 RGB image encoded into 256 continuous latent tokens (via LDM-KL 16 tokenizer) plus a text query, and produces a text response via standard auto-regressive decoding. For image generation, it takes a text prompt (or no prompt for unconditional generation) and sequentially samples 256 continuous image tokens through a lightweight diffusion head, which are decoded back to a 256×256 RGB image. The 256-token representation is 16× more compact than EMU-3's 4096 discrete tokens per image, and image tokens are randomly masked during training with a controlled mask ratio to support both conditional and unconditional generation.

## Method

MMAR unifies text and image modeling in a single auto-regressive transformer: text tokens use standard categorical AR (softmax over LM-head logits), while continuous image tokens are sampled by a lightweight MLP diffusion head conditioned on the backbone's hidden state, effectively disentangling the diffusion process from the AR backbone. The architecture embeds a PLoRA-based image expert into a pre-trained Qwen-2 LLM with bi-directional attention over image regions, plus an EmbeddingViT encoder, so the LLM simultaneously optimizes P(T|I), P(I|T), and P(I). A key theoretical contribution derives v-prediction as the optimal diffusion parameterization for minimizing numerical error under bfloat16 training, showing that ε-prediction's error term diverges at low SNR while v-prediction remains bounded. Training uses a two-stage strategy: first, large-scale mid-quality data (Capfusion-120M) with mask ratio [0.7, 1.0] to build diverse understanding capability, then smaller high-quality data with mask ratio (0, 1] to fix generation artifacts (holes in the last 30% of tokens) and boost image quality.

## Results

On 18 image understanding benchmarks, MMAR-7B achieves an average score (AVE@18Und.) of 48.25, surpassing all joint image-text probabilistic models including EMU-3 (no AVE reported but MMB 58.5 vs 70.45), Show-o (33.06), VILA-U (no AVE), and Transfusion* (28.26). MMAR-7B also matches or exceeds the understanding-only LLaVA-1.5 (47.08) despite using only 256 continuous image tokens at 256×256 resolution without any pre-trained CLIP encoder. On image generation, MMAR-7B achieves FID-30K of 22.9 on MSCOCO and FID of 15.6 / GenEval 0.51 on MJHQ-30K after a third-stage fine-tune, remaining competitive with joint probabilistic models like Show-o (FID 15.18, GenEval 0.53) and VILA-U (FID 12.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMAR-7B decisively outperforms all joint probabilistic baselines on understanding: it leads Chameleon-7B by ~30 points on AVE@18Und. (48.25 vs 18.34), Show-o by ~15 points (48.25 vs 33.06), and the re-implemented Transfusion* by ~20 points (48.25 vs 28.26). Against EMU-3, MMAR-7B wins on MMB (70.45 vs 58.5) and SEED (68.63 vs 68.2) while using only 256 tokens per image versus EMU-3's 4096, but trails on MM-Vet (30.64 vs 37.2). Compared to the understanding-only LLaVA-1.5 (which relies on a pre-trained CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336×336), MMAR-7B achieves a slightly higher AVE@18Und. (48.25 vs 47.08) and substantially higher SEED (68.63 vs 58.6), while being marginally behind on MME^P (1486.9 vs 1510.7) and POPE (84.02 vs 85.9). On generation quality, MMAR-7B (FID-30K 22.9) lags behind Show-o (9.24) and Transfusion (16.8) on MSCOCO, and on MJHQ-30K it is close to Show-o (FID 15.6 vs 15.18; GenEval 0.51 vs 0.53) but behind VILA-U (FID 12.81) and EMU-3 (GenEval 0.66). The ablation confirms that the v-prediction parameterization is critical: switching to ε-prediction drops AVE@18Und. from 34.56 to 32.21 and worsens FID-30K from 36.6 to 61.53 for the 0.5B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMAR builds directly on MAR's masked autoregressive framework with diffusion heads for continuous token sampling, and on PLoRA for integrating image experts into a pre-trained LLM. It departs from the dominant discrete-token line (Chameleon, MARS, EMU-3) by using continuous image tokens to avoid VQ codebook bottlenecks, and from Transfusion/MonoFormer by disentangling the diffusion process into a lightweight per-token MLP head rather than embedding a full diffusion transformer within the AR backbone. The v-prediction parameterization, originally proposed for diffusion distillation, is re-derived here from the first principle of minimizing floating-point numerical error under low-precision training, which the authors claim is a novel theoretical contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates a structural limitation of transfusion-like models: clean image inputs invoke only low-noise-level representations suited to enhancement rather than semantic understanding, creating an inherent tension between generation and comprehension. The two-stage training strategy and v-prediction parameterization are practical contributions that make continuous AR image modeling viable at LLM scale. However, the work is limited to 256×256 resolution, relatively small model sizes (0.5B–7B), and generation quality (FID 22.9 on MSCOCO) remains well behind dedicated generators, leaving open whether the framework scales to higher resolutions, longer sequences, and video without the efficiency advantages eroding.
