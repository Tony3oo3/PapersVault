## Problem

Existing distillation methods for diffusion models rely on external teachers or more-step samplers, inherently capping the student's performance at the teacher's level. This paper asks whether a diffusion model can serve as its own teacher in an N-to-N step self-distillation setting to surpass its original generation quality, a question that had not been explored for diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251162/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained diffusion models (DiT variants at 256×256 and 512×512, or Stable Diffusion v1.5) conditioned on class labels or text prompts, producing generated images. During training, the model consumes noisy inputs at two timesteps (t and s) simultaneously to compute fused targets, but at inference the model reverts entirely to its original single-timestep computational pipeline with no architectural changes. Training requires fewer than 10k optimization steps (~40 min for DiT-XL/2, ~3 hours for Stable Diffusion) and can even use synthetic data generated by the model itself.

## Method

Self Step-Distillation (SSD) constructs a teacher-student pair from a single pre-trained model by fusing predictions or intermediate activations across two adjacent timesteps. In explicit SSD, the teacher target is a weighted blend of noise predictions at timesteps t and s, while the student predicts at the earlier step; in implicit SSD (iSSD), the fusion occurs at intermediate layer activations, redefining the student's computational flow so that each layer receives a weighted combination of its own output and the frozen teacher's output at the preceding timestep. The key theoretical insight is that this fusion approximates the intermediate-step prediction of a second-order ODE solver (DPM-Solver-2) using only first-order evaluations, effectively distilling high-order solver accuracy into the same network without extra parameters. The teacher remains frozen while the student is trained to match it via an L2 noise-prediction loss, and after training the model is used in its original unfused form, yielding improved generation quality at the same number of sampling steps.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-XL/2, iSSD with 50 NFEs achieves FID 2.02, surpassing the 250-step base model (FID 2.14) by a large margin. The headline result is a 32-step DiT-XL/2 with iSSD applied on a progressively distilled model, reaching FID 1.99 — outperforming the original 250-step DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.26). On ImageNet 512×512, 50-step iSSD improves FID from 3.04 to 2.86. On COCO2014 text-to-image with Stable Diffusion v1.5, 10-NFE iSSD achieves FID 10.94, beating DDIM (12.78), PLMS (17.71), and DPM-Solver-2 (15.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against sampling-based baselines on COCO2014/2017 (Table 4), iSSD with 10 NFEs outperforms DDIM (FID 10.94 vs 12.78), PLMS (10.94 vs 17.71), and DPM-Solver-2 (10.94 vs 15.23), with the gap widening at 20 NFEs (10.56 vs 12.68/12.65/14.63). Against Progressive Distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), iSSD is applied as a complementary refinement: a 32-step PD model improves from FID 2.24 to 1.99, and a 10-step PD model improves from 4.19 to 3.56, though at 10 steps the directly distilled PD model (4.92) still slightly edges out the iSSD-enhanced version. Against the Shortcut Model (Frans et al., 2024) for 4-step generation on ImageNet 256×256, PD+iSSD achieves FID 6.10 versus the Shortcut Model's 7.8, a clear win. Overall, iSSD consistently wins or ties across all compared baselines, with the largest gains at low NFEs (10–20 steps) where the base model degrades most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant distillation paradigm for diffusion models (progressive distillation, consistency distillation) that relies on an external teacher or a higher-step teacher to compress N steps into M steps. Instead, it introduces N-to-N self-distillation where the model serves as its own teacher, drawing a conceptual bridge to self-distillation in conventional architectures (Born Again Networks) but exploiting the unique temporal structure of diffusion timesteps. The key theoretical grounding comes from DPM-Solver-2's high-order ODE formulation: the paper shows that fusing predictions or intermediate activations across adjacent timesteps is mathematically equivalent to distilling second-order solver behavior into a first-order solver without extra parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 32-step self-distilled DiT-XL/2 (FID 1.99) surpasses the original 250-step model (FID 2.26) with less than one epoch of training (~40 minutes), and the method is orthogonal to existing few-step distillation pipelines. It also works without real training data (synthetic data suffices), making it a nearly free post-training refinement. Limitations include sensitivity to the fusion weight λ and reliance on a suboptimal linear interpolation to approximate intermediate-step predictions; whether the approach can be iterated (self-distill the self-distilled model) or extended to video and 3D generation remains open.
