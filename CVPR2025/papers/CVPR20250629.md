## Problem

Despite image tokens comprising ~77% of the input sequence in MLLMs, their contribution to prediction outcomes is minimal (equivalent to only 0.03% of instruction token importance), yet they incur substantial computational cost. How MLLMs internally process and route visual information across layers remains unexplored, leaving no principled basis for reducing this overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250629/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard MLLM inputs: an RGB image encoded into 576 image tokens via a pre-trained vision encoder (e.g., CLIP-ViT) plus a text instruction, producing an autoregressive text response. HiMAP is plug-and-play and requires no retraining; it dynamically prunes image tokens at two specified layers during inference, reducing the effective sequence length from 576 image tokens to as few as ~72 in later layers while preserving the full instruction and system token sequences.

## Method

HiMAP (Hierarchical Modality-Aware Pruning) exploits a discovered shift in dominant visual information flow: in shallow layers, image tokens inject most visual information into instruction tokens (visual-textual flow), while in deeper layers, image tokens aggregate residual visual information among themselves (intra-visual flow). The shallow-layer pruning module at layer K₁ ranks image tokens by their summed attention scores to instruction tokens (φ_sh) and removes the bottom R₁%; the deeper-layer pruning module at layer K₂ ranks the surviving tokens by their summed attention scores from other image tokens (φ_dp) and removes the bottom R₂%. This two-stage, modality-aware ranking ensures that tokens critical for cross-modal semantic injection are preserved early, while tokens redundant for intra-visual aggregation are pruned later, achieving ~65–75% FLOPs reduction and ~50–60% latency reduction without performance degradation across QA, captioning, and open-ended generation tasks.

## Results

HiMAP reduces FLOPs to 20–25% of baseline across four MLLM architectures (LLaVA-7B/13B, QwenVL-7B, InternVL-7B) while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy on most benchmarks. For example, on LLaVA-7B, HiMAP achieves 78.6 on VQAv2, 58.4 on TextVQA, and 1785.1 on MME at only 24% FLOPs, surpassing the full-model baseline (78.3, 58.2, 1749.9). On the open-ended Natural QA task (LLaVA-Bench, MM-Vet), HiMAP scores 66.5 and 33.7 at 34% FLOPs, exceeding the baseline (65.7, 33.4). Real-world inference latency is reduced by approximately 60% on an A800 GPU, compared to 35% for FastV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline compared is FastV [Chen et al., 2024], which prunes 50% of image tokens after layer 2 using average attention as the importance criterion. HiMAP consistently outperforms FastV on both computational efficiency and accuracy: it achieves 20–25% FLOPs (vs. FastV's 52–54%) while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy on 5 out of 6 QA benchmarks for every model tested. FastV causes notable degradation on POPE for LLaVA-13B (84.8 vs. 87.2 baseline) and InternVL-7B (85.2 vs. 86.9), whereas HiMAP's drop is smaller (86.5 and 86.5 respectively). On the open-ended Natural QA task, HiMAP surpasses the baseline on LLaVA-Bench (66.5 vs. 65.7 for LLaVA-7B; 74.5 vs. 73.5 for LLaVA-13B), while FastV falls short (62.4 and 71.7). In real-world inference speed on an A800 GPU, HiMAP reduces latency by ~60% compared to FastV's ~35%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-token pruning line initiated by FastV, which removes a fixed fraction of image tokens at a single layer using average attention as the importance criterion. HiMAP departs from FastV by introducing a *hierarchical* and *modality-aware* strategy: it applies two distinct pruning stages with different importance criteria (attention to instruction tokens in shallow layers, attention among image tokens in deeper layers), motivated by a new empirical analysis of how visual information flows through MLLM depths. The saliency-based interpretability framework builds on Taylor-expansion attention analysis [Michel et al., 2019] and prior observations of sparse attention to image tokens, but the phased-processing hypothesis (injection vs. aggregation) is a novel conceptual framing not present in prior pruning or interpretability work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~65% FLOPs reduction with maintained or slightly improved accuracy across four MLLM architectures and six benchmarks makes HiMAP a practically relevant tool for deploying MLLMs under compute constraints, and the plug-and-play design lowers adoption barriers. The phased-processing hypothesis, if it generalizes beyond LLaVA-v1.5, could inform future architecture design (e.g., allocating more capacity to shallow cross-modal fusion). Open questions include whether the specific layer/ratio settings transfer to newer MLLM families (e.g., those with dynamic token counts or different vision encoders), whether the hypothesis holds for video or multi-image inputs, and whether the pruning can be made adaptive per-sample rather than relying on fixed hyperparameters.
