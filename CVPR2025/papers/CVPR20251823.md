## Problem

Vecset-based 3D VAEs rely on uniform point sampling to encode mesh surfaces, which fails to prioritize geometrically salient regions under limited point budgets, causing loss of fine details like sharp edges. Additionally, existing evaluation protocols use randomly selected test shapes and general metrics (F-score, Chamfer distance) that do not specifically measure reconstruction quality at these critical geometric features, making it difficult to assess where VAEs truly struggle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251823/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a watertight 3D mesh as input and produces a compact latent code (256 or 1,280 tokens) along with a reconstructed shape decoded from an occupancy field. The input mesh is consumed via a two-stage sampling process: Poisson disk sampling for uniform coverage combined with sharp-edge sampling for salient regions, yielding a dense point cloud of ~16,384 points. The latent code is designed to be small enough for efficient downstream diffusion model training, in contrast to volume-based methods that require >10,000 tokens.

## Method

Dora-VAE introduces Sharp Edge Sampling (SES), which identifies mesh edges with dihedral angles exceeding a threshold (τ=30°), collects their vertices, and generates salient points via FPS or edge interpolation to supplement uniformly sampled points. A dual cross-attention architecture then processes the uniform and salient point subsets separately—each attending to its own dense counterpart—before summing the features for self-attention encoding, allowing the network to focus on both global structure and fine details simultaneously. The model is trained with MSE loss on the predicted occupancy field, following the 3DShape2VecSet framework. For evaluation, Dora-Bench categorizes test shapes into four complexity levels based on salient edge count and introduces the Sharp Normal Error (SNE) metric, which measures normal-map MSE specifically within Canny-detected sharp regions, enabling targeted assessment of geometric detail preservation.

## Results

On Dora-Bench, Dora-VAE with 1,280 latent codes achieves F-score(0.01) of 99.170 and CD of 5.265 on the hardest complexity level (L4), surpassing the fine-tuned XCube-VAE† (>10,000 codes) which scores 99.079 and 7.627 respectively — a 47.77% CD improvement with 8× fewer latent codes. On the proposed Sharp Normal Error (SNE) metric, Dora-VAE achieves 1.579 on L4 versus 1.639 for XCube-VAE† (3.7% improvement) and 3.933 for Craftsman-VAE. Even with only 256 codes, Dora-VAE (CD 24.527, SNE 3.035 on L4) substantially outperforms Craftsman-VAE at the same code length (CD 33.441, SNE 3.933).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against XCube-VAE† (fine-tuned on the same data, >10,000 latent codes), Dora-VAE with 1,280 codes wins on CD (5.265 vs. 7.627, a 47.77% reduction) and SNE (1.579 vs. 1.639) at L4, while matching or slightly exceeding F-score(0.01) (99.170 vs. 99.079). The 8× reduction in latent code length makes Dora-VAE far more practical for downstream diffusion training, where XCube's >10,000 tokens are infeasible. Against Craftsman-VAE (256 codes), Dora-VAE at the same 256-code budget improves F-score(0.01) from 81.739 to 89.577 and CD from 33.441 to 24.527 on L4, with SNE dropping from 3.933 to 3.035. At 1,280 codes, the gap widens dramatically: Dora-VAE reaches near-XCube-level reconstruction (F-score 99.170) while Craftsman-VAE was not evaluated at that code length. In the single-image-to-3D diffusion application (Figure 7), Dora-VAE produces visibly sharper geometric details than Craftsman-VAE† under identical DiT architecture and training budget, confirming the VAE advantage translates to generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dora-VAE builds directly on 3DShape2VecSet [54], adopting its transformer-based VAE pipeline, occupancy-field decoding, and overall training protocol, while replacing the uniform Poisson-disk sampling with a dihedral-angle-driven Sharp Edge Sampling strategy and splitting the single cross-attention into a dual (uniform + salient) variant. The importance-sampling idea is borrowed from point-cloud processing (APES, Flex-Convolution) but repurposed for mesh-surface sampling in a VAE context, a setting where prior work had not applied it. The paper also departs from the standard evaluation practice of randomly selected test shapes by introducing Dora-Bench, which stratifies shapes by geometric complexity and adds the Sharp Normal Error metric to specifically probe fine-detail reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone training 3D latent diffusion models: matching XCube-VAE reconstruction quality with a latent space at least 8× smaller (1,280 vs. >10,000 tokens) directly reduces the sequence length and compute cost of the downstream diffusion stage. The SNE metric and complexity-stratified benchmark address a real gap—prior evaluations masked detail loss by averaging over shapes of varying complexity. Limitations include the diffusion-model comparison being limited to a 0.39B DiT trained for only 3 days, the VAE being trained on a single ~400K-mesh subset of Objaverse, and the XCube baseline using a different mesh-extraction method (NKSR) that may confound the comparison. Open questions include whether the dihedral-angle threshold generalizes to non-manifold or highly curved surfaces, and whether the dual-attention gain is truly architectural or simply a capacity effect.
