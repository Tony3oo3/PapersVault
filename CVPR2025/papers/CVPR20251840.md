## Problem

Tracking dynamic surfaces with 3D Gaussians remains challenging when topology changes occur—surfaces appearing, disappearing, or splitting. Existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: fixed-topology or template-based approaches enable tracking but degrade appearance and geometry under new poses, while overfitting methods achieve high static quality but lack temporal consistency and miss newly emerging geometry. No prior method simultaneously delivers photorealistic rendering, accurate surface reconstruction, and consistent 3D tracking through topology changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB-D video captured by 52 RGB and 52 IR cameras at 3004×4092 resolution and 30 fps, with depth generated from unstructured IR laser point clouds refined and projected onto camera views. It processes frames sequentially, warping the previous frame's Gaussian Surface via scene flow before per-frame optimization. Outputs include photorealistic novel-view renderings, an explicit 3D mesh with time-consistent face correspondences (Gaussian Surfaces with N=6 Gaussians per face), and per-vertex 3D tracking trajectories validated against AprilTag ground truth.

## Method

GauSTAR represents dynamic objects as Gaussian Surfaces—meshes with N Gaussians uniformly distributed per triangular face via barycentric coordinates, with z-axis aligned to face normals and z-scale fixed to a small δ. Each frame is initialized by backprojecting 2D optical flow into 3D scene flow using depth images, then refined through fixed-topology optimization with RGB, depth, and mask losses plus normal-smoothing, area-preservation, and spherical-harmonics temporal-consistency regularizers. The key novelty is adaptive Gaussian unbinding: each Gaussian receives additional rotation ΔR and translation Δt parameters, constrained by a loss weighted by (1−W(f)), where W(f) combines positional gradients and per-face reconstruction errors to detect topology changes. In high-W regions, Gaussians detach from their faces and new Gaussians are duplicated; these unbound Gaussians are then used to render multi-view depth maps, from which new mesh patches are extracted via TSDF fusion and stitched to the original mesh through boundary vertex matching with edge flipping and hole filling.

## Results

GauSTAR achieves the best performance across all evaluated metrics: 31.87 PSNR, 0.952 SSIM, and 0.102 LPIPS for appearance; 0.237 cm Chamfer Distance and 0.980 F-Score for geometry; and 0.452 cm 3D ATE and 2.03 px 2D ATE for tracking. Even without IR depth input, GauSTAR (30.05 PSNR, 0.335 cm CD, 0.671 cm 3D ATE) outperforms all baselines on geometry and tracking. The method is evaluated on 4 sequences totaling 850 frames with 5 test views for appearance, and 2 sequences with AprilTags for tracking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GauSTAR outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. In appearance, GauSTAR (31.87 PSNR) leads HumanRF (30.59) and 2DGS (30.17) by 1.3 and 1.7 dB respectively, with the largest gap over Dynamic 3D Gaussians (27.61) and PhysAvatar-general (22.69). For geometry, GauSTAR achieves 0.237 cm CD, beating the closest competitor HumanRF (0.284 cm) and substantially outperforming 2DGS (0.699 cm) and Dynamic 3D Gaussians (1.113 cm). On tracking, GauSTAR's 0.452 cm 3D ATE is 7× better than Dynamic 3D Gaussians (3.15 cm) and 20× better than PhysAvatar-SMPLX (8.98 cm); notably, HumanRF and 2DGS do not provide tracking at all. The ablation further shows that removing scene flow warping degrades 3D ATE from 0.45 to 6.56 cm, highlighting its critical role for large-motion tracking.

## Relation to Prior Work

GauSTAR extends the mesh-Gaussian binding paradigm introduced by SUGAR [15] and PhysAvatar [54] into a dynamic setting, where Gaussians are attached to mesh faces and move with them. It departs from prior dynamic tracking methods (Dynamic 3D Gaussians [28], PhysAvatar [54]) that assume fixed topology, and from NeRF-based dynamic reconstruction (HumanRF [18]) that lacks per-frame correspondence, by introducing an adaptive Gaussian unbinding mechanism that lets Gaussians detach from their faces when topology changes are detected. The scene flow warping for frame initialization draws on classical optical-flow-to-3D backprojection, and new surface generation reuses TSDF fusion [31], making the overall pipeline a principled composition of established components rather than a fundamentally new representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GauSTAR is the first method to simultaneously deliver photorealistic rendering, explicit surface reconstruction, and per-face 3D tracking under topology changes, filling a gap that prior methods addressed only partially (tracking without topology adaptation, or reconstruction without tracking). The practical impact is significant for VFX, markerless motion capture, and VR/XR pipelines where persistent surface identity across frames is essential. However, the method's dependence on a 52-camera multi-view capture studio limits immediate applicability to single- or few-camera settings, and it explicitly acknowledges struggles with sudden topology changes (e.g., a new person entering the scene) and transparent/specular surfaces—open problems that will constrain broader adoption.
