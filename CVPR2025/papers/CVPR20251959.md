## Problem

Existing video super-resolution methods face a consistency-quality dilemma: regression-based approaches (e.g., BasicVSR++) produce temporally stable but blurry outputs lacking high-frequency texture, while generative methods (diffusion models, image GANs) hallucinate rich details but introduce severe temporal flickering. The paper asks whether the large-scale GigaGAN image upsampler, trained on billions of images, can be adapted for VSR while resolving this trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251959/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution video of T frames (T×h×w×3) and produces a high-resolution video (T×H×W×3) at a scale factor α (4× or 8×). During training it consumes 10-frame clips with 64×64 patches from REDS (1280×720) and Vimeo-90K (448×256); at inference it handles arbitrary-length videos by first extracting flow-guided features globally, then processing non-overlapping chunks independently through the inflated GigaGAN. Unlike diffusion-based VSR, VideoGigaGAN generates output in a single feedforward pass.

## Method

VideoGigaGAN inflates the pretrained GigaGAN asymmetric U-Net (3 encoder + 3+k decoder blocks with adaptive kernels and spatial self-attention) into a video model by inserting zero-initialized 1D temporal convolutions (kernel 3) and temporal self-attention layers after each spatial attention block. A recurrent flow-guided feature propagation module (bi-directional RNN + backward warping, using SpyNet optical flow) is prepended to aggregate temporal context across the full video before chunked inference, addressing the limited receptive field of local temporal attention. To suppress aliasing-induced flickering, strided convolutions in the encoder are replaced with BlurPool anti-aliasing blocks (conv stride-1 + low-pass filter + subsampling). A high-frequency shuttle then decomposes each encoder feature map into low-frequency (filtered) and high-frequency (residual) components, injecting the HF component directly via skip connections into the corresponding decoder block to recover fine-grained detail lost during anti-aliasing. Training uses non-saturating GAN loss with R1 regularization, LPIPS, and Charbonnier loss on 32 A100 GPUs for 100K iterations.

## Results

On REDS4 (BI degradation), VideoGigaGAN achieves the best LPIPS of 0.1582, outperforming the next-best regression-based method IA-RT (0.1629) and the diffusion-based StableVSR (0.1934). On Vimeo-90K-T, it reaches 0.1120 LPIPS, substantially better than BasicVSR++ (0.1506) and all other baselines. However, PSNR and SSIM are lower than regression-based methods (e.g., 30.46 dB vs. 32.89 dB for IA-RT on REDS4), which the authors attribute to PSNR/SSIM favoring blurry outputs. For temporal consistency (RWE), VideoGigaGAN scores 2.177×10⁻³, slightly worse than BasicVSR++ (2.121×10⁻³) but far better than diffusion-based methods (e.g., UAV at 7.524×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoGigaGAN achieves the best LPIPS (perceptual quality) across all evaluated datasets, beating the strongest regression-based baseline IA-RT by 0.0047 on REDS4 (0.1582 vs. 0.1629) and by a large margin on Vimeo-90K-T (0.1120 vs. 0.1498). It trails regression-based methods on PSNR/SSIM (e.g., 30.46 vs. 32.89 dB on REDS4), which the authors argue reflects PSNR/SSIM's known bias toward blurry outputs. On temporal consistency (RWE), VideoGigaGAN (2.177×10⁻³) is slightly worse than BasicVSR++ (2.121×10⁻³) and IA-RT (2.117×10⁻³) but dramatically outperforms diffusion-based methods: UAV (7.524×10⁻³), VEnhancer (2.738×10⁻³), and DiffIR2VR-Zero (3.094×10⁻³). In runtime, VideoGigaGAN (295 ms/frame) is competitive with regression-based methods (169–219 ms for RVRT/VRT) and 17–41× faster than diffusion-based approaches (5,168–12,212 ms). Scaling up BasicVSR++ to 368M parameters does not improve LPIPS (0.1941 vs. 0.1786 at 7.3M) and destabilizes training, whereas VideoGigaGAN at 369M parameters achieves 0.1582 LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoGigaGAN adapts the large-scale GigaGAN image upsampler (Kang et al., CVPR 2023), which itself scales StyleGAN2 with adaptive kernel selection and self-attention, into a video setting by inflating 2D spatial modules with 1D temporal convolutions and temporal self-attention. It further borrows the recurrent flow-guided feature propagation architecture from BasicVSR++ (Chan et al., CVPR 2022) to handle large inter-frame motion. The key conceptual departure from prior VSR work is identifying and addressing the 'consistency-quality dilemma': regression-based VSR methods sacrifice high-frequency detail for temporal smoothness, while naively applying a generative image model to each frame produces severe flickering. The paper's novel components—anti-aliasing BlurPool blocks in the encoder and the high-frequency shuttle via skip connections—target specific failure modes (aliasing-induced flickering and detail loss from anti-aliasing) that prior VSR literature had not explicitly addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale GAN-based VSR model, demonstrating that the generative capacity of billion-image-trained upsamplers can be harnessed for video while maintaining temporal consistency, and it extends the practical upsampling range to 8×. The proposed referenced warping error (RWE) metric addresses a known flaw in the standard warping error that rewards over-smoothed outputs, though the authors acknowledge RWE still biases toward blurrier results and leave a truly perceptually-aligned temporal metric as open work. A notable limitation is that VideoGigaGAN's temporal consistency (RWE) remains slightly worse than the best regression-based methods, and the model's 369M parameters and 295 ms/frame runtime, while competitive, are heavier than lightweight regression baselines. The finding that simply scaling up BasicVSR++ does not improve performance (Table 3) reinforces that architectural generative capacity, not just model size, is the key differentiator for detail-rich VSR.
