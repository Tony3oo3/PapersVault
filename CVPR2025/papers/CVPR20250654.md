## Problem

In incomplete multi-view clustering, missing data induces prototype shifts within views and semantic misalignment across views. Existing methods address this through instance-level or cluster-level consistency learning followed by imputation and alignment, but instance-level paradigms treat unpaired observations sharing the same semantics as false negatives, while cluster-level paradigms neglect fine-grained intra-cluster relationships within views. Moreover, both rely heavily on cross-view paired data, making imputation and alignment unreliable when missing rates are high.

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view data where each of V views contains only a subset of N total instances (N_v observations per view), along with pair-observed subsets indicating which instances appear in multiple views. A key design choice is the instance-level fine-grained fusion weight w_i^v, computed as the indicator that instance i is observed in view v divided by the total number of views observing i, which adaptively down-weights views with more missing data. The output is K cluster assignments obtained by applying k-means to the learned consensus semantic representations H, which are linearly weighted fusions of per-view semantic representations.

## Method

FreeCSL replaces imputation and alignment with a semantic-level paradigm built on three jointly optimized modules. The cross-view consensus semantic learning (CSL) module fuses per-view latent representations into consensus representations via completeness-weighted averaging, then performs prototype-based contrastive clustering: semantic representations are projected onto k-means-derived consensus prototypes to yield soft assignments, and 'swapped knowledge distillation' enforces distributional consistency between paired observations across views using pseudo-labels solved by trace maximization with entropy regularization. The within-view cluster semantic enhancement (CSE) module constructs KNN graphs per view, applies a GCN for graph pooling, and optimizes node assignments by maximizing spectral modularity (Tr(P^T B P)) while regularizing toward high-confidence pseudo-labels generated via a Student's t-distribution kernel. Training proceeds in two stages—autoencoder warm-up followed by joint fine-tuning of all three losses—and final clustering is performed by k-means on the fused consensus semantic representations, requiring no explicit imputation or alignment step.

## Results

FreeCSL achieves the best ACC across all four datasets at every missing rate (r=0.1–0.7). On the most challenging ALOI-100 (100 clusters), it reaches 91.13% ACC at r=0.1 and maintains 76.44% at r=0.7, outperforming the next-best method (DIVIDE) by 15.12 and 26.21 percentage points respectively. On YouTubeFace10 (30,000 samples), FreeCSL scores 82.93% ACC at r=0.1 and 76.62% at r=0.7. On NoisyMNIST, it achieves 99.13% ACC at r=0.1 and 92.19% at r=0.7, with NMI of 97.23% and 82.50% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeCSL outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset at every missing rate. The most dramatic margins appear on ALOI-100 (100 clusters): at r=0.7, FreeCSL achieves 76.44% ACC versus SURE (50.23%), DIVIDE (42.66%), and ICMVC (26.84%), a gap of 26+ points over the next-best. On YouTubeFace10 (30K samples), FreeCSL leads DIMVC (the strongest competitor) by 8.81% ACC at r=0.3 and 1.68% at r=0.7. On NoisyMNIST, FreeCSL edges out SURE and ICMVC at low missing rates (99.13% vs. 98.61%/98.78% at r=0.1) but pulls decisively ahead at r=0.7 (92.19% vs. 84.00%/53.91%). Instance-level methods (CPM-Nets, COMPLETER, DIMVC, ProImp, ICMVC) degrade sharply as missing rates increase due to false-negative noise and unreliable imputation, while cluster-level methods (SURE, DIVIDE) handle cross-view consistency better but still neglect within-view cluster structure, limiting their performance on multi-cluster tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant consistency-learning paradigms in incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC)—instance-level (pulling paired cross-view observations closer, e.g., CPM-Nets, COMPLETER) and cluster-level (matching cluster counterparts across views, e.g., SURE, DIVIDE)—by proposing a third, semantic-level paradigm built on prototypical contrastive clustering with shared consensus prototypes. Rather than imputing missing data or aligning cross-view assignments/prototypes/distributions as prior methods do, FreeCSL directly learns a shared semantic space where all observations (paired or unpaired) converge toward common prototypes, and supplements this with modularity-based GCN graph clustering for within-view structure. The individual components (autoencoders, swapped knowledge distillation, Student's t-distribution soft assignments, spectral modularity) are drawn from existing literature, but their combination into an imputation- and alignment-free IMVC framework is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that imputation and alignment are unnecessary if one can directly learn consensus semantics across all observations—challenges a core assumption in the IMVC literature where most SOTA methods invest heavily in cross-view imputation or alignment. The robustness at high missing rates (r=0.7) is practically significant: on ALOI-100, FreeCSL retains 76% ACC while instance-level methods drop below 50%, suggesting the approach is less sensitive to the scarcity of paired observations that plagues consistency-dependent methods. Limitations include evaluation on only four datasets (all under 30K samples), continued reliance on paired observations for the swapped KD signal, and theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) deferred to an appendix without detailed proof. Open questions include scalability to truly large-scale or highly heterogeneous multi-view settings, performance when no paired observations exist at all, and whether the consensus-prototype formulation generalizes beyond the k-means-based prototype initialization used here.
