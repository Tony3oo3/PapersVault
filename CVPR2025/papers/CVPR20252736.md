## Problem

While the Platonic Representation Hypothesis has been validated for vision-text encoders, the relationship between 3D uni-modal encoders and text encoders remains unexplored. Existing 3D foundation models are trained with explicit alignment objectives against frozen 2D/text encoders, precluding clean post-training comparison. This paper asks whether independently trained 3D and text encoders can be aligned after the fact, and if so, how.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen 512-dimensional feature vectors from uni-modal 3D encoders (PointBERT, SparseConv/MinkowskiNet, PointNet) and text encoders (CLIP ViT-BigG, BERT, RoBERTa), paired via 30,000 anchor samples (or 1,000 for local CKA) from Objaverse with Cap3D-generated captions. A query set of 500 shape-caption pairs is evaluated on two tasks: permutation matching via the Hungarian algorithm and top-k retrieval by cosine similarity in the aligned space. The subspace dimension is fixed at 50, and all encoders are frozen throughout.

## Method

The core insight is that 3D and text latent spaces are poorly aligned globally (max CKA ≈ 0.12 for uni-modal encoders) but become significantly more aligned when projected into a shared lower-dimensional subspace. The pipeline first applies Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) to the anchor pairs to identify a k-dimensional subspace (k=50) that maximizes correlation between the two modalities, yielding projection matrices W_X and W_Y. All features are then projected into this reduced space, after which standard alignment methods—affine transformation (learned via least squares on projected anchors) or local CKA matching—are applied in the lower-dimensional setting. This two-stage design (subspace selection + alignment) consistently outperforms applying either alignment method directly in the full 512-dimensional space, and the analysis reveals that the projected subspaces selectively amplify semantic structure in 3D features and geometric sensitivity in text features.

## Results

On Objaverse-LVIS with uni-modal PointBERT and CLIP text encoder, the proposed Affine+Subspace Projection achieves 30.8% matching accuracy and 42.2% top-5 retrieval, roughly doubling the Affine-only baseline (15.8% / 23.6%). Local CKA+Subspace Projection reaches 60.19% top-5 retrieval, a ~4× improvement over Local CKA alone (15.2%). Multi-modal OpenShape with CLIP establishes the upper bound at 67.6% matching and 85.6% top-5 retrieval. The subspace dimension is fixed at 50 with 30,000 anchors for affine methods and 1,000 anchors for local CKA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two existing alignment baselines: affine transformation [Maiorca et al., NeurIPS 2024] and local CKA matching [Maniparambil et al., CVPR 2024]. For uni-modal PointBERT with CLIP, the proposed Affine+Subspace Projection more than doubles matching accuracy (30.8% vs. 15.8%) and nearly doubles top-5 retrieval (42.2% vs. 23.6%) over the Affine baseline. The Local CKA+Subspace Projection variant shows an even more dramatic gain, improving top-5 retrieval from 15.2% to 60.2% (~4×) and matching from 5.8% to 29.4% (~5×) over the Local CKA baseline. These improvements are consistent across all three uni-modal 3D encoders (PointBERT, SparseConv, PointNet) and all three text encoders (CLIP, RoBERTa, BERT). Multi-modal encoders (OpenShape, ULIP-2, Uni3D) trained with explicit CLIP alignment serve as an upper bound, with OpenShape reaching 67.6% matching and 85.6% top-5 retrieval; the proposed subspace approach narrows the gap between uni-modal and multi-modal performance substantially but does not close it entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Platonic Representation Hypothesis and the vision-text latent space alignment literature (Maniparambil et al., CVPR 2024; Huh et al., 2024) to the 3D-text setting, asking whether uni-modal 3D encoders converge to structures similar to text encoders the way vision and text encoders do. Methodologically, it repurposes two existing alignment tools — affine transformation (Maiorca et al., NeurIPS 2024) and local CKA matching (Maniparambil et al., CVPR 2024) — but wraps them in a CCA-based subspace projection step that is the paper's core contribution. The key departure from prior alignment work is the hypothesis that meaningful 3D-text similarity is confined to lower-dimensional correlated subspaces rather than the full latent space, a distinction not explored in the vision-text literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study establishing that 3D and text latent spaces are fundamentally less aligned than vision and text spaces (max CKA 0.12 vs. 0.30–0.48), challenging the assumption that all modalities converge to a shared Platonic structure at scale. The subspace projection approach provides a practical, training-free baseline for 3D-text cross-modal tasks, but absolute performance remains modest (best uni-modal matching ~31%), leaving a large gap to multi-modal upper bounds (~94% top-5 retrieval). Open questions include whether scaling to Objaverse-XL (10M+ shapes) closes this gap, how scene-level vs. object-level annotations affect the geometric/semantic subspace decomposition, and whether the CCA subspace framework generalizes to other modality pairs that also show imperfect alignment.
