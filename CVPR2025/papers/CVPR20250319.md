## Problem

Existing diffusion-based human animation methods assume a fixed character-camera distance, restricting generated videos to the same shot type as the reference image. This prevents cinematic applications such as zoom-in/zoom-out transitions or multi-shot camera planning, where dramatic viewpoint and distance changes expose missing appearance details (e.g., a full-body reference lacks facial detail for a close-up, and self-occlusions reveal hidden body regions).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250319/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N reference images from diverse viewpoints (each paired with its pose image) and a target pose sequence of T frames as input, producing a novel-view human video. At test time, more than M reference images can be supplied, and videos longer than 12 frames are generated via temporal aggregation of multiple clips. The reference images need not share the same shot type or camera distance as the target, enabling cross-shot-type synthesis (e.g., full-body reference → close-up output).

## Method

Built on a double-UNet diffusion backbone (denoising UNet, reference UNet, pose guider), the key contribution is a Pose Correlation Module that encodes each reference and target pose with separate encoders and computes a cross-attention correlation map via a transformer block, highlighting spatially which regions of each reference are informative for the current target frame. These correlation maps are multiplied element-wise with reference features at every UNet layer to emphasize relevant regions. To avoid linearly growing computation with more references, an adaptive reference selection strategy flattens all reference features, sorts them by correlation score, and retains only the top-K_l tokens per layer, which are then concatenated along the spatial dimension into the denoising UNet's self-attention layers. During training, a compensated uniform-sampling term is added alongside the top-K selection to provide scattered gradients and prevent the non-differentiable argsort from trapping the network in local minima; at inference, only the correlated top-K features are used. The model is trained on the newly introduced MSTed dataset (1,084 identities, 15,260 clips with varied camera distances and viewpoints) and DyMVHumans, using randomly sampled reference counts between 1 and M per step.

## Results

On the MSTed dataset, the proposed method with 2 reference images (R=2) achieves FVD of 7.044, PSNR of 32.49, LPIPS of 0.1869, and MOVIE of 55.60, substantially outperforming all baselines. On DyMVHumans, even with a single reference image (R=1), the method attains the best FVD (9.047) and PSNR (34.22), and scaling to 10 references further reduces FVD to 5.459 and LPIPS to 0.1383. The L1 loss drops from 74.20 (R=2) to 35.27 (R=10) on DyMVHumans, demonstrating strong scaling with additional reference views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three SOTA diffusion-based human animation methods: MagicAnimate, AnimateAnyone, and Champ. On MSTed, the proposed method (R=2) outperforms Champ by a large margin on FVD (7.044 vs. 25.68, a 3.6× improvement) and LPIPS (0.1869 vs. 0.4618), while also leading on PSNR (32.49 vs. 30.87) and MOVIE (55.60 vs. 67.84). On DyMVHumans, the method with a single reference (R=1) already surpasses AnimateAnyone on FVD (9.047 vs. 12.300) and LPIPS (0.1660 vs. 0.2179), and beats Champ on every metric (FVD 9.047 vs. 45.762, PSNR 34.22 vs. 30.87). MagicAnimate is consistently the weakest baseline across both datasets, with FVD values of 35.08 (MSTed) and 51.433 (DyMVHumans). The key finding is that training with multiple references enables the model to generalize well even at single-reference inference time, and that the pose-correlation-guided selection strategy yields the largest FVD gains (from 26.32 baseline to 7.044 with full method on MSTed).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the double-UNet diffusion architecture established by AnimateAnyone and Champ, which animate a character from a single reference image under a fixed camera viewpoint. The key departure is the introduction of multiple reference images with a pose-correlation module that computes attention maps between non-aligned target and reference poses, plus an adaptive top-K reference selection strategy to control computational cost. The authors also introduce the MSTed dataset (1,084 identities, ~30 hours of multi-shot TED videos) to fill a gap: existing multi-view human datasets (DyMVHumans, MVHuman) are captured in controlled studios with uniform camera-to-subject distance, failing to represent real-world zoom-in/zoom-out scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MSTed dataset is a practically valuable resource for free-viewpoint human animation research, as no prior public dataset captures substantial camera-distance variation. The pose-correlated reference selection mechanism offers a reusable design pattern for any multi-reference generation task where computational budget is constrained. However, the method still depends heavily on the diffusion model's generative capacity to hallucinate occluded or unseen body regions, and the evaluation is limited to two datasets with relatively short clips; scalability to very long multi-shot sequences, real-time inference, and generalization beyond human subjects remain open.
