## Problem

Photorealistic 3D head avatar reconstruction from short monocular videos captured on commodity devices is ill-posed because limited head coverage leaves unobserved regions (e.g., face sides from front-facing recordings) under-constrained, producing artifacts in novel views. Existing monocular methods only reconstruct visible regions without effective priors to hallucinate missing geometry and appearance, while multi-view methods require controlled studio setups with dense camera arrays.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250050/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short monocular RGB video (70–300 frames at 802×550, 512×512, or 1280×720) and per-frame FLAME mesh parameters from the VHAP tracker as input. It produces an animatable 3D Gaussian head avatar (splats bound to FLAME triangles) that can be rendered in real time at 62 FPS at 802×550, driven with arbitrary novel poses and expressions. During optimization, the diffusion model generates pseudo-ground-truth images at 512×512 by iteratively denoising 4-view latents conditioned on the input frame and normal maps rendered from the FLAME mesh at randomly sampled target viewpoints.

## Method

The key contribution is a head-specific multi-view latent diffusion model, initialized from ImageDream/Stable Diffusion 2.1 and fine-tuned on NeRSemble, that jointly denoises multiple novel-view latents conditioned on (i) VAE latents of the input image for identity preservation, (ii) CLIP embeddings for global semantics, and (iii) normal maps rendered from FLAME tracking for pixel-aligned viewpoint control. For Gaussian avatar optimization, instead of single-step Score Distillation Sampling (which causes over-saturation), the method uses iteratively denoised images via DDIM (with 20× step reduction) as deterministic pseudo-ground-truths to supervise novel-view renderings. A pretrained latent upsampler (32×32 → 64×64) sharpens facial details in the denoised latents before decoding. The overall loss combines input-view reconstruction (L1 + SSIM + LPIPS) with the diffusion-based novel-view loss against pseudo-GTs, plus splat position and scale regularization, optimizing FLAME-rigged Gaussians for 6,000 iterations with adaptive densification.

## Results

On the NeRSemble dataset (12 sequences, front-view monocular input), GAF achieves LPIPS 0.125, PSNR 20.88, and SSIM 88.91 for novel view synthesis, and LPIPS 0.087, PSNR 24.12, and SSIM 90.66 for novel expression synthesis. On the Monocular Video dataset (smartphone and INSTA captures), GAF achieves L1 0.0229, LPIPS 0.090, PSNR 23.16, and SSIM 89.76, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The method renders at 62 FPS (0.016 s per frame at 802×550) after a ~12-hour optimization on a single A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAF outperforms all seven baselines on LPIPS and SSIM for novel view synthesis (0.125/88.91 vs. best baseline P4D-v2 at 0.161/85.44 and PanoHead at 86.11), though GAGAvatar edges it on PSNR (21.20 vs. 20.88). For novel expression synthesis, GAF dominates across all three metrics, improving LPIPS by 37% over the best baseline GA (0.087 vs. 0.138), PSNR by 2.5 dB (24.12 vs. 21.63), and SSIM by 3.7 points (90.66 vs. 87.00). On the Monocular Video dataset (smartphone/INSTA captures), GAF wins every metric, with the closest competitor GA trailing by 0.0034 in L1, 0.019 in LPIPS, 0.61 dB in PSNR, and 1.15 in SSIM. The static methods PanoHead and SphereHead cannot be evaluated on novel expressions since they do not support animation. Feed-forward methods P4D-v2 and GAGAvatar show identity drift and blurriness in extreme novel views, while optimization-based methods (INSTA, FlashAvatar, GA) leave unobserved side regions unconstrained, producing visible artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GaussianAvatars representation (rigging 3D Gaussians to FLAME mesh triangles) and the multi-view diffusion architecture from ImageDream, but departs from the standard score distillation sampling (SDS) paradigm by training a head-specific multi-view diffusion model and using iteratively denoised images as pseudo-ground truths (following ReconFusion). A key conceptual departure from prior multi-view diffusion work (Zero-123, MVDream, CAT3D) is the use of FLAME-rendered normal maps as pixel-aligned conditioning for viewpoint control, rather than camera pose embeddings or Plücker ray maps, and the conditioning on VAE latents of the input image rather than only CLIP embeddings. The paper also introduces a latent upsampler diffusion model to refine denoised latents before decoding, a component absent from prior 3D-reconstruction-with-diffusion pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By distilling multi-view diffusion priors into Gaussian splatting optimization, GAF addresses the fundamental under-constrained nature of monocular head reconstruction, enabling photorealistic novel views from short smartphone videos without studio multi-view capture. This has direct practical implications for democratizing high-fidelity avatar creation in VR/AR, videoconferencing, and content production. However, the 12-hour per-subject optimization time on a single GPU limits real-time applicability, and the method inherits FLAME's limitations (no detailed hair geometry, no material/appearance separation for re-lighting). Open questions include whether feed-forward large reconstruction models can replace the per-subject optimization, and whether the normal-map-conditioned diffusion approach generalizes beyond heads to full-body avatars.
