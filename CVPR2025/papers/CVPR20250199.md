## Problem

Existing video prompting methods adapt frozen CLIP models to video tasks by learning a single static prompt shared across all videos, overlooking the diverse temporal dynamics and spatial variations that differ across frames and regions. This static approach prevents the model from focusing on discriminative regions with significant motion or on keyframes that carry the most temporal information, ultimately limiting video understanding performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250199/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (uniformly sampled frames resized to 224×224, split into 49 patches of 16×16 each) and a text prompt (natural language sentence for retrieval, or a hand-crafted template like 'a video of a person doing [CLASS]' for recognition). The video passes through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder, and the output is a video representation whose cosine similarity with the text embedding yields either a class label (action recognition) or a retrieval score. Frame counts vary by dataset: 12 frames for MSR-VTT and VATEX, 64 for others.

## Method

STOP introduces two complementary dynamic prompting modules on top of a frozen CLIP backbone. The intra-frame spatial prompting module combines a 3D convolutional layer (capturing per-patch temporal dynamics) with intra-frame self-attention to score each patch, selects the top N_s patches as discriminative regions, and overlays generated spatial prompts (from a lightweight prompter conditioned on neighboring frames) onto those tokens. The inter-frame temporal prompting module computes frame-to-frame variation in the discriminative regions (weighted by a hyperparameter β to emphasize foreground over background), then dynamically determines the number of prompt tokens to insert between each pair of adjacent frames (N_i^t = ⌈η·W_i^t⌉) and generates them via a second prompter. Only 7.53M parameters are trainable (two 3D conv layers and two prompters), and the model is trained with cross-entropy loss for action recognition or contrastive loss for retrieval, keeping all CLIP weights frozen.

## Results

On video action recognition, STOP achieves 72.0% ACC@1 on HMDB51, 95.3% on UCF101, and 21.4% on SS-V2, outperforming the best prompt-based baseline DGL-Transformer (69.8%, 93.6%, 18.1%) by 2.2%, 1.7%, and 3.3% respectively. On MSR-VTT text-to-video retrieval, STOP reaches R@1 of 47.7 and MnR of 15.2, surpassing MPT-Transformer (R@1 46.3, MnR 15.6) while using fewer trainable parameters (7.53M vs. 9.61M). On ActivityNet, DiDeMo, and VATEX, STOP consistently leads on R@1 (43.1, 47.5, 57.5) and achieves the lowest MnR (6.9, 12.5, 4.0) among all prompt-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STOP outperforms all prompt-based baselines across all three action recognition benchmarks. Against the strongest prompt competitor DGL-Transformer [61], STOP gains +2.2% on HMDB51 (72.0 vs. 69.8), +1.7% on UCF101 (95.3 vs. 93.6), and +3.3% on SS-V2 (21.4 vs. 18.1). On MSR-VTT retrieval, STOP beats MPT-Transformer [64] (the best prompt baseline) on R@1 by +1.4 (T2V) and +1.1 (V2T) while reducing trainable parameters from 9.61M to 7.53M. On ActivityNet, STOP improves R@1 over MPT-Transformer by 1.7 points (43.1 vs. 41.4) and reduces MnR from 7.8 to 6.9. On VATEX, STOP achieves the best R@1 (57.5) and lowest MnR (4.0) among all prompt methods, edging out DGL-Linear [61] (56.2, 4.1). Notably, STOP still trails the full-tuning CLIP4Clip baseline [42] on SS-V2 (21.4 vs. 69.4) and HMDB51 (72.0 vs. 75.2), indicating that parameter-efficient prompting has not yet fully closed the gap to full fine-tuning on the most challenging temporal reasoning benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video prompting line of work (VoP, DGL, MPT, UniPT) that adapts frozen CLIP/CLIP4Clip to video tasks via learnable prompts. The key departure is replacing the single static prompt shared across all videos with two complementary dynamic mechanisms: intra-frame spatial prompts whose positions are determined per-frame by combining self-attention with 3D-convolutional temporal dynamics, and inter-frame temporal prompts whose count scales with measured frame-to-frame variation. Conceptually, it repurposes the prompt-learning paradigm from NLP and image VLMs (CoOp, VPT) but introduces a spatially and temporally adaptive instantiation that prior video prompting methods had not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STOP addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation of existing video prompting—static prompts cannot adapt to the diverse temporal dynamics and spatial focus required across different videos—yet the improvements over strong baselines (DGL, MPT) are modest (1–3% on action recognition, ~1–2% on retrieval), suggesting the static-prompt bottleneck may not be the dominant factor limiting performance. The method adds two 3D convolutional layers and two prompters (~7.5M trainable params), which is competitive but introduces architectural complexity that may not scale trivially to larger backbones or longer video sequences. Open questions include whether the dynamic prompt mechanism generalizes beyond ViT-B/32, to longer videos or other video understanding tasks (e.g., video QA, temporal grounding), and whether the 3D-convolutional temporal-dynamics estimation can be replaced by lighter alternatives.
