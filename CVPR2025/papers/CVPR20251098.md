## Problem

Existing image protection methods against diffusion-based personalization (e.g., PhotoGuard, Glaze, AdvDM) all rely on iterative PGD optimization at inference, requiring 7–200 s on a high-end GPU and up to 120 min on CPU for a single 512² image. This latency makes them impractical for real-world deployment, and no prior work has systematically addressed the joint trade-off among protection efficacy, perturbation invisibility, and inference speed.

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary-resolution RGB image (tested from 512² up to 2048²) and outputs a protected image of the same resolution. Perturbations are pre-trained at 512×512 and resized via bilinear interpolation to match the input, so no re-optimization is needed at inference. Protection is evaluated by attempting to personalize a diffusion model (LoRA, DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, DreamStyler) on the protected image and measuring FID of the generated outputs.

## Method

FastProtect replaces per-image PGD optimization with a pre-trained Mixture-of-Perturbations (MoP): K=4 perturbations are clustered via K-means++ over VAE latent codes, and at inference the VAE encoder's latent code selects which perturbation to apply, yielding semi-image-specific protection at near-zero cost. A Multi-Layer Protection (MLP) loss extends the standard texture loss to intermediate VAE encoder feature layers, strengthening the push toward the target latent during pre-training without any inference overhead. At inference, an Adaptive Targeted Protection module selects among three pre-trained MoPs (low/mid/high pattern-repetition targets) by matching the L1 norm of entropy of the input's latent code to each target's, and an Adaptive Protection Strength module uses a single LPIPS forward pass to generate a spatial perceptual map that modulates perturbation magnitude, suppressing visible artifacts in flat-texture regions.

## Results

FastProtect achieves a 125× speedup on CPU (2.9s vs. 370s for PhotoGuard) and 175× speedup on GPU (0.04s vs. 7s) while maintaining comparable protection efficacy (FID) across all four domains. On invisibility (DISTS, lower is better), FastProtect achieves the best scores in every domain: 0.155 (Object), 0.149 (Face), 0.110 (Painting), and 0.186 (Cartoon), outperforming the next-best method by 0.02–0.09 DISTS. Protection efficacy (FID, higher is better) is on par with or slightly above baselines, e.g., 308.9 vs. 308.7 (PhotoGuard) on Face and 356.1 vs. 357.6 (AdvDM) on Painting. Inference requires only 1.7 GB VRAM, compared to >8 GB for iterative-optimization methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastProtect outperforms all six baselines on invisibility (DISTS) across every domain, with the largest margins against AdvDM (0.271→0.186 on Cartoon) and SDST (0.242→0.155 on Object). On protection efficacy (FID), FastProtect matches or slightly exceeds PhotoGuard (308.9 vs. 308.7 on Face; 223.0 vs. 223.0 on Object) and Mist (308.9 vs. 307.5 on Face), while trailing AdvDM by only 1.5 FID on Painting (356.1 vs. 357.6). Against Anti-DB, FastProtect achieves higher FID on Object (223.0 vs. 214.4) and Face (308.9 vs. 301.4) with substantially better DISTS. Compared to Impasto, FastProtect wins on both DISTS and FID in every domain (e.g., 0.149 vs. 0.198 DISTS and 308.9 vs. 298.4 FID on Face). The most striking advantage is latency: FastProtect runs in 2.9 s on CPU and 0.04 s on GPU, versus 370 s / 7 s for the fastest baseline (PhotoGuard) and up to 7278 s / 225 s for Anti-DB, making it the only method practical for real-time or consumer-device use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant iterative-optimization paradigm (PGD-based) used by all prior image-protection methods (AdvDM, PhotoGuard, Anti-DB, Mist, Impasto, Diff-Protect) by adopting a pre-trained perturbation approach inspired by Universal Adversarial Perturbation (UAP). However, it identifies that UAP's image-agnostic nature is insufficient for protection, and introduces Mixture-of-Perturbations (MoP) with a K-means++-based assignment function to achieve semi-image-specific protection at near-zero inference cost. The targeted protection objective builds directly on PhotoGuard's texture loss, extended here with a Multi-Layer Protection Loss that leverages intermediate VAE encoder features, and an adaptive target-selection mechanism that matches target pattern repetition to input texture complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing protection latency from minutes to fractions of a second (200–3500× speedup), FastProtect removes the primary practical barrier to deploying image protection in real-world applications, making it accessible to ordinary users on consumer hardware. The paper also demonstrates that this speed is achieved without sacrificing protection efficacy and with improved invisibility, particularly in flat-texture regions common in cartoons and illustrations. The acknowledged limitation—that adversarial perturbations inevitably leave visible traces—points to an open question about whether fundamentally new paradigms (beyond perturbation-based approaches) can achieve truly imperceptible protection. The finding that FastProtect serves as a superior initialization for subsequent PGD refinement (converging in 25 vs. 100 steps) suggests a useful hybrid workflow for users with more compute.
