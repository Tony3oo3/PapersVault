## Problem

Existing spatial gene expression prediction methods from whole slide images fail to effectively model long-range interactions between morphologically similar but spatially distant tissue patches. The only prior GNN approach (Hist2ST) relies solely on 1-hop neighbor edges, limiting message passing, while ViT-based methods require self-attention to be learned from data and are sensitive to the scarcity and noise inherent in spatial transcriptomics. Additionally, standard spatial smoothing of ST data further degrades already sparse gene expression values without being transcriptome-aware.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 pixel patches extracted at ×20 magnification from WSI histopathology images, centered at Visium spot coordinates (100 μm spot diameter, 200 μm center-to-center spacing). Each patch is encoded into a 256-dimensional feature vector by a gene-aware fine-tuned ResNet18. The output is a jointly predicted 250-dimensional gene expression vector at every spot/node in the graph, corresponding to the 250 most expressed genes selected per dataset.

## Method

MERGE constructs a multi-faceted hierarchical graph by performing two independent clusterings of all patches: spatial clustering on tissue coordinates and feature-space clustering on patch embeddings. Internal edges connect each spot to its cluster centroid (the spot nearest the feature-space centroid), while shortcut edges form a complete graph among all centroids from both clusterings, ensuring any node reaches any other in at most three hops. A 4-layer Graph Attention Network (8 heads, layer normalization, edge dropout p=0.2) performs joint gene expression prediction across all nodes with MSE loss. The patch encoder is a ResNet18 pre-trained with SimCLR on TCGA-BRCA and fine-tuned with a gene-expression regression head to make features gene-aware. As a complementary contribution, the paper replaces conventional 8-neighbor spatial smoothing with SPCS, a two-factor method combining spatial distance decay with PCA-based gene expression pattern similarity, which better preserves biologically meaningful expression structure.

## Results

On the ST-Net dataset, MERGE with the multi-faceted hierarchical graph achieves MSE 0.1347, MAE 0.2834, and PCC 0.6795, substantially outperforming the prior state-of-the-art TRIPLEX (MSE 0.1472, MAE 0.2943, PCC 0.2320). On Her2ST, MERGE reaches PCC 0.5037 versus TRIPLEX's 0.3927, and on SCC it achieves PCC 0.5512 versus TRIPLEX's 0.5416. The PCC gains over TRIPLEX are most dramatic on ST-Net (+0.448) and Her2ST (+0.111), while on SCC TRIPLEX retains a slight edge on MSE (0.4891 vs. 0.5353) and MAE (0.5356 vs. 0.5838).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERGE with the hierarchical graph outperforms all five baselines on PCC across all three datasets, with the largest margin over TRIPLEX on ST-Net (0.6795 vs. 0.2320, a +0.448 gain) and Her2ST (0.5037 vs. 0.3927, +0.111). On SCC, MERGE edges out TRIPLEX on PCC (0.5512 vs. 0.5416) but TRIPLEX achieves lower MSE (0.4891 vs. 0.5353) and MAE (0.5356 vs. 0.5838), making it the only dataset where MERGE does not dominate on all three metrics. Against the GNN-based Hist2ST (1-hop 4-neighbor graph), MERGE improves PCC by 0.527 on ST-Net, 0.256 on Her2ST, and 0.251 on SCC, demonstrating that the multi-faceted hierarchical graph is far more effective than simple spatial proximity edges. Compared to the non-GNN baselines (ResNet+FCN, BLEEP, HisToGene, THItoGene), MERGE consistently achieves the best or near-best PCC, with the exception of ResNet+FCN on ST-Net where the simple encoder reaches PCC 0.5221 (still well below MERGE's 0.6795). The ablation in Table 2 further confirms that the combined spatial + feature-space clustering (PCC 0.6795) outperforms either clustering alone (0.6356 spatial-only, 0.6719 feature-only) and that selecting the feature-centroid spot as cluster representative is critical (0.6795 vs. 0.6583 for spatial centroid).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial gene expression prediction line initiated by ST-Net and advanced by TRIPLEX, BLEEP, and HisToGene, but departs from the dominant attention-based and multi-resolution encoder paradigms by adopting a GNN with a purpose-built multi-faceted hierarchical graph. The sole prior GNN approach, Hist2ST, used only 1-hop spatial edges, making long-range message passing impractical; MERGE addresses this gap by combining spatial and feature-space clustering to create shortcut edges that connect morphologically similar but spatially distant spots in at most three hops. Additionally, the paper repurposes the SPCS two-factor smoothing technique from the scRNA-seq literature and demonstrates its superiority over the spatial-only 8n smoothing commonly used in prior ST prediction work, positioning this as a separate methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-faceted hierarchical graph offers a principled, sparse alternative to full self-attention for modeling long-range tissue interactions, which is particularly relevant given the limited size of ST datasets and the high cost of acquiring new ones. The smoothing analysis provides practical guidance for the broader ST community, showing that gene-aware smoothing substantially improves morphology-expression alignment and can shift model performance by large margins (e.g., PCC from 0.27 to 0.52 for ResNet+FCN). Open questions include whether the graph construction strategy generalizes to higher-resolution ST platforms (MERFISH, seqFISH+) where spot definitions differ, whether the clustering-based graph remains effective as tissue heterogeneity increases, and whether the approach scales to whole-slide inference with thousands of spots without prohibitive memory costs.
