## Problem

Existing 3D HOI datasets are confined to indoor scenes with only 10–40 object categories derived from fixed CAD models, making it impossible to evaluate generalization to real-world, open-vocabulary interactions. Additionally, prior training-free 3D HOI reconstruction methods rely on silhouette-based optimization and manually annotated contact pairs, limiting their accuracy and scalability to arbitrary objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251836/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The optimizer takes a single RGB image, a pre-reconstructed object mesh (from InstantMesh), SMPL-X body parameters (from OSX), monocular depth (from ZoeDepth), and SAM masks as input, and produces the object's 6D pose (rotation, scale, translation) plus refined human LBS parameters and part-level contact regions on the 34-region SMPL-X body. The accompanying Open3DHOI dataset provides 2.5k+ images with both 2D annotations (bounding boxes, HOI triplets, object labels, masks) and 3D annotations (object meshes, SMPL-X parameters, 6D poses for human and object, and contact regions), spanning 133 object categories and 120 interaction categories.

## Method

The HOI-Gaussian optimizer initializes 3D Gaussians from SMPL-X vertices (human) and object mesh vertices (object), then jointly optimizes the object's 6D pose and the human's LBS parameters via a staged procedure: 100 iterations of rendering loss only, followed by 60 iterations adding HOI losses. The key insight is that Gaussian opacity naturally encodes occlusion: where the human and object overlap in the image, the occluded Gaussian points receive lower opacity, revealing potential contact regions without requiring predefined contact pairs. A contact score c combines normalized human opacity with Chamfer distance to the object, and the HOI loss integrates this contact loss with a collision loss and an ordinal depth loss. The rendering loss (L1, L2, SSIM, LPIPS) is computed separately for the human, object, and combined Gaussians to ensure individual and joint 2D alignment, while the 3D Gaussian representation leverages depth and color information more effectively than silhouette-only methods for pose refinement.

## Results

On the Open3DHOI test set, the proposed Gaussian-HOI optimizer substantially outperforms the PHOSA baseline across all object pose metrics: scale error drops from 0.39 to 0.16, translation error from 77.79 cm to 38.44 cm, rotation error from 0.95 to 0.41, and Chamfer distance from 49.1 cm to 19.3 cm. On the paper's proposed Co² interaction metric, the full method (Gs&depth&colli&cont) achieves 0.181 versus PHOSA's 0.431, with collision score improving from 0.105 to 0.053 and contact score from 0.326 to 0.128. The ablation shows that adding depth, collision, and contact losses progressively improves Co² from 0.287 (Gaussians only) to 0.181 (full HOI loss).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single external baseline, PHOSA [51], which is the only fair comparison since Open3DHOI is open-vocabulary and training-based methods tuned for specific object categories would be unfair. The proposed Gaussian-HOI optimizer wins decisively on every metric: it halves the scale error (0.16 vs 0.39), halves the translation error (38.44 vs 77.79 cm), more than halves the rotation error (0.41 vs 0.95), and reduces Chamfer distance by 61% (19.3 vs 49.1 cm). On the interaction-quality Co² metric, the full method scores 0.181 versus PHOSA's 0.431, a 58% relative improvement. The contact score improves from 0.326 to 0.128, indicating that the Gaussian-based contact region identification effectively captures 3D human-object interaction geometry that silhouette-only optimization (PHOSA) cannot. The paper notes that PHOSA relies on manually annotated human-object part pairs as priors, whereas the Gaussian approach infers contacts from opacity and depth, reducing reliance on such priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D HOI reconstruction line (PHOSA, WildHOI, 3DIR) from limited indoor/CAD-object settings to open-vocabulary, in-the-wild scenes by leveraging recent image-to-3D tools (InstantMesh) and SMPL-X-based human reconstruction. Methodologically, it builds directly on GauHuman's articulated Gaussian splatting framework, repurposing the opacity and depth signals of 3D Gaussians to jointly optimize human-object spatial interactions and identify contact regions, departing from PHOSA's silhouette-only optimization. The dataset construction pipeline itself is novel, combining occlusion inpainting (Stable Diffusion), monocular depth (ZoeDepth), and coarse-to-fine manual annotation to produce fine-grained 3D HOI assets from existing 2D HOI images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Open3DHOI is the first open-vocabulary, in-the-wild 3D HOI benchmark (133 object categories, 120 interaction categories), addressing a critical gap where prior 3D HOI datasets were confined to a handful of CAD objects in controlled indoor environments. The training-free Gaussian-HOI optimizer demonstrates that 3D Gaussian Splatting can serve as a general-purpose interaction optimizer without per-category training, and the proposed Co² metric offers a more holistic evaluation of spatial interaction quality. However, the dataset's 2.5k scale and reliance on InstantMesh reconstruction quality limit its breadth, and the LLM evaluation (PointLLM, ChatPose) reveals that current 3D-aware models remain far from competent at 3D HOI understanding, leaving the question of whether richer data can close this gap open.
