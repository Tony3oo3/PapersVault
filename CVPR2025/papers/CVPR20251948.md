## Problem

Existing text-driven style editing methods either apply style changes to the entire image rather than a specific object, distort the object's structural content during stylization, or inadvertently alter background regions. Prior object-centric approaches rely on segmentation masks or reference images, and their directional losses in CLIP space can cause semantic collapse by focusing solely on vector direction without preserving the distribution of patch-level features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251948/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 source image, a source text describing the target object (e.g., 'red apple'), and a style text (e.g., 'green' or 'Starry Night by Van Gogh'), and produces a single stylized image where only the text-specified object is restyled while the background remains unchanged. No segmentation masks, reference style images, or additional training data are required; each image is processed independently via per-image optimization (~45 s on an A6000 GPU). The target text for the directional loss is constructed by merging the style text with the central word of the source text (e.g., 'green apple').

## Method

Style-Editor trains a lightweight U-Net (3 down/up blocks, 16–64 channels) per image using a composite loss. The Patch-wise Co-Directional (PCD) loss combines a CLIP-embedding directional loss on TMPS-selected foreground patches with a Jensen-Shannon divergence term that aligns the distribution of patch-to-image similarity scores between source and output, preventing the semantic collapse that pure directional losses cause. The Text-Matched Patch Selection (TMPS) and Pre-fixed Region Selection (PRS) modules use frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 zero-shot classification to identify object patches and delineate a coarse foreground mask without any segmentation network. The Adaptive Background Preservation (ABP) loss applies MS-SSIM and L1 penalties to a dynamically updated background mask (complement of the union of all selected foreground patches across iterations), ensuring the background retains its original appearance. A VGG-19 content loss and total-variation regularization complete the objective, optimized with Adam over 200 iterations.

## Results

On the MSCOCO 2017 evaluation set (16 images × 10 style prompts = 160 stylized images), Style-Editor achieves the best scores across all 8 foreground and background quality metrics. For foreground quality, it attains Sim_F = 0.33 and Con_F = 3.75, outperforming the closest competitor Text2LIVE (0.32 / 4.13). For background preservation, it achieves L1_B = 0.10, SSIM_B = 0.90, DISTS_B = 0.07, and PSNR_B = 27.65, substantially surpassing all baselines (next best: Text2LIVE at 0.14 / 0.87 / 0.09 / 24.69). The method also achieves the lowest background style change (Sty_B = 0.10) and content distortion (Con_B = 1.15), confirming effective object-centric editing with minimal background alteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Style-Editor outperforms all 11 quantitative baselines on every single metric. The closest competitor is Text2LIVE [2], which also uses a text-driven object-centric approach; Style-Editor improves Sim_F by 0.01 (0.33 vs 0.32), reduces Con_F by 0.38 (3.75 vs 4.13), and achieves substantially better background preservation (PSNR_B 27.65 vs 24.69, SSIM_B 0.90 vs 0.87). Among diffusion-based methods, Null-text inversion [40] and LEDITS++ [3] show the best background fidelity (PSNR_B 23.48 and 21.66) but lag significantly on foreground style alignment (Sim_F 0.20 and 0.22). Non-diffusion methods like CLIPstyler [34] achieve moderate foreground similarity (Sim_F 0.28) but suffer severe background distortion (PSNR_B 13.20, L1_B 0.66). The six diffusion-based editing models (Instruct Pix2Pix, Plug and Play, pix2pix-zero, FlexIT, ZeCon, StylerDALLE) consistently show the worst foreground content preservation (Con_F 6.51–9.65) and background quality, indicating that diffusion-based approaches tend to alter object structure during style editing. Qualitatively, the paper notes that Text2LIVE occasionally applies style to unintended regions and fails to reflect the target style text, while diffusion models frequently reconstruct object geometry rather than preserving it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-guided style transfer lineage from StyleGAN-NADA's directional loss in CLIP space and CLIPstyler's single-text-condition style editing to the object-centric setting. It departs from prior object-centric methods (Text2LIVE, diffusion-based editors) by replacing reference-image or mask-based localization with CLIP zero-shot patch classification (TMPS/PRS), and by introducing a patch-level distribution consistency term (JSD) alongside the directional loss to prevent semantic collapse. The U-Net-based per-image optimization framework is a deliberate contrast to the dominant GAN and diffusion paradigms, positioning the work as a lightweight, mask-free alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of segmentation masks and reference style images lowers the practical barrier for object-centric style editing in creative industries, and the ABP loss provides a clean, adaptive mechanism for background preservation that prior methods handled poorly. However, the evaluation is limited to 16 COCO images with 10 style prompts each, and the per-image optimization (~45 s on A6000) limits real-time applicability. Open questions include scalability to multiple simultaneous objects, more complex compositional style descriptions, and whether the CLIP-based patch selection generalizes to out-of-domain or abstract objects.
