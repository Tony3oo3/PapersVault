## Problem

Learning unsigned distance fields (UDFs) for 3D surface reconstruction is challenging because UDFs are non-differentiable near surfaces, making loss design and training unstable. Existing supervised methods like GeoUDF require training on large shape-specific datasets (e.g., ShapeNet, 120 GB), limiting generalizability and incurring high computational cost, while unsupervised methods suffer from slow convergence. The paper exploits the fact that UDF values are inherently local quantities—dependent only on nearby geometry—to eliminate the need for shape-specific training entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (dense or sparse) normalized to a [-0.5, 0.5] bounding box. For each uniformly sampled grid query point, a local patch of points within radius r=0.018 is extracted and fed to the network, which outputs a single scalar UDF value. Meshes are then extracted from the predicted UDF field using the DCUDF double-covering algorithm. The trained model is extremely lightweight (653 KB parameters, 2 MB total) and is trained once on a 0.5 GB synthetic dataset of shape-independent local patches, after which it generalizes to arbitrary point clouds without retraining.

## Method

The core insight is that local 3D geometry can be fully described by a small set of mathematical functions: quadratic surfaces (parameterized by principal curvatures κ₁, κ₂) for smooth regions, and four simple analytic forms (creases, cusps, corners, v-saddles) for sharp features. A synthetic training dataset is generated by sampling point cloud patches from these functions, with ground-truth UDF values computed analytically. The network architecture uses two PointNets—one encoding the local patch points and one encoding relative vectors from patch points to the query—whose features are fused via a cross-attention module (patch features as KV, vector features as Q) to produce a geometric feature code. A denoising module predicts per-point displacement corrections to mitigate noise and outliers in the distance estimates. Training uses L1 loss on UDF values plus L1 sparsity regularization on the denoising displacements, and the resulting model can also serve as a lightweight initialization for unsupervised UDF methods (CAP-UDF, LevelSetUDF, SIREN-based DEUDF) via a time-decaying weighted loss.

## Results

On ShapeNetCars, LoSF-UDF achieves the best CD (0.611×100), F1^0.01 (0.927), and NC (0.967) on clean data, and dramatically outperforms all baselines under noise (CD 0.614 vs. 1.176–1.939) and outliers (CD 0.705 vs. 2.598–2.949). On DeepFashion3D, it achieves the best NC (0.988 clean, 0.906 noisy) and the lowest CD under outliers (0.559 vs. 2.558–3.883 for baselines). The method is also the most lightweight, with only 653 KB of parameters and 0.5 GB training data, versus 1.8 MB and 120 GB for GeoUDF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoSF-UDF consistently outperforms all four baselines (CAP-UDF, LevelSetUDF, GeoUDF, DUDF) on both ShapeNetCars and DeepFashion3D, with the most dramatic gains under noise and outliers. On ShapeNetCars with 10% outliers, LoSF-UDF achieves CD 0.705 versus 2.598–2.949 for all baselines (a 3.6–4.2× improvement), and F1^0.01 of 0.751 versus 0.304–0.379. Under 0.25% Gaussian noise, the CD gap is similarly large: 0.614 vs. 1.176–1.939. On DeepFashion3D, the advantage is even more pronounced for outliers (CD 0.559 vs. 2.558–5.150). On clean data, LoSF-UDF matches or slightly trails DUDF on CD for DeepFashion3D (0.548 vs. 0.506) but leads on NC (0.988 vs. 0.965) and F1^0.01 (0.966 vs. 0.972 is close). The method also integrates with unsupervised approaches (CAP-UDF, LevelSetUDF, SIREN/DEUDF) to provide better initialization, reducing training time by ~55–60% while maintaining or improving reconstruction quality on the Threescan dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant UDF learning paradigm (CAP-UDF, LevelSetUDF, DUDF, GeoUDF) by replacing shape-specific training data with a synthetic dataset of point cloud patches generated from simple mathematical functions (quadratic surfaces for smooth regions, piecewise functions for sharp features). Conceptually, it extends the local-reconstruction lineage (Ohtake et al.'s partition of unity, DeepLS, PatchNets) from SDFs to UDFs, but the key departure is the training strategy: rather than learning geometric priors from large 3D shape repositories like GeoUDF does on ShapeNet, it exploits the observation that UDF values are purely local quantities that can be captured by a small set of parametric surface families. The network architecture (PointNet + cross-attention) is standard, and the DCUDF mesh extraction is borrowed, so the novelty is concentrated in the data generation and the denoising module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 653 KB parameter count and 0.5 GB training dataset (versus GeoUDF's 120 GB ShapeNet and 36-hour training) make UDF-based reconstruction dramatically more accessible and deployable, and the demonstrated robustness to up to 50% outliers addresses a practical pain point in real scanned data. The integration framework that uses LoSF-UDF as initialization for unsupervised methods (halving convergence time for CAP-UDF and LevelSetUDF) is a useful recipe for practitioners who want both speed and detail. A clear limitation is the inability to handle missing geometry, since the local-only training strategy has no global shape awareness; extending this to shape completion or integrating with global priors remains an open direction.
