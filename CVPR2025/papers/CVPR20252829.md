## Problem

Existing SfM pipelines built on DUSt3R/MASt3R rely on expensive iterative optimization (bundle adjustment, joint rigid-transform optimization) for global alignment, which limits scalability to large image collections and incurs high memory and runtime costs. This paper asks whether a fully feed-forward, optimization-free approach can achieve competitive pose accuracy on large-scale unconstrained image sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252829/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of N RGB images (no temporal or spatial ordering required) at 224×224 or 512×512 resolution and produces, per image, camera extrinsics (4×4), intrinsics (3×3), a dense 3D pointmap at full image resolution (H×W×3) in a shared global coordinate frame, and a per-point confidence map (H×W). Camera poses are extracted from the globally aligned pointmaps via PnP, and the confidence maps enable flexible thresholding to trade off completeness against pose accuracy at inference time.

## Method

Light3R-SfM replaces optimization-based global alignment with a learnable latent attention module: each image is encoded to spatial tokens, a single global token per image is computed by spatial averaging, self-attention is applied across all N global tokens, and the updated global information is propagated back to dense tokens via cross-attention, yielding globally aligned features in O(N² + N·T) time. A shortest-path tree (SPT) built from encoder-embedding cosine similarities selects N−1 edges for pairwise stereo decoding, keeping the graph flat to minimize drift during accumulation. Pairwise pointmaps are decoded only along SPT edges and merged into a global reconstruction by breadth-first traversal with per-node Procrustes alignment (closed-form, linear in N). Training uses a combined pairwise pointmap loss and a global accumulation loss (λ=0.1), initialized from MASt3R pretrained weights, jointly supervising both local and globally aligned predictions.

## Results

On Tanks&Temples (200 views), Light3R-SfM achieves RRA@5=52.4, RTA@5=53.1, ATE=0.016, and 100% registration rate in 33.4 s, compared to MASt3R-SfM's RRA@5=68.2, RTA@5=68.4, ATE=0.013 in 1609 s (~49× slower). On CO3Dv2 (10-view), Light3R-SfM attains RRA@15=94.7, RTA@15=85.8, mAA@30=72.8, the best among all feed-forward methods. On Waymo driving scenes, Light3R-SfM reaches RRA@5=78.3 and RTA@5=57.7 in 8.5 s, matching MASt3R-SfM's accuracy (RRA@5=75.7) at ~195× lower runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Light3R-SfM is the only feed-forward method that maintains 100% registration rate across all Tanks&Temples view settings while achieving RRA@5 scores of 50.9–52.4, substantially outperforming Spann3R (20.3–22.8) by 145% in RRA and 84% in RTA on average, at roughly half the runtime. Against optimization-based methods, Light3R-SfM trails MASt3R-SfM (RRA@5=68.2 at 200 views) and GLOMAP (73.5) in pose accuracy but runs 49× and 16× faster respectively; it also outperforms VGGSfM (84.5 RRA@5) in registration rate (100% vs 47.6%) and ACE0/FlowMap in both accuracy and speed. On CO3Dv2 (10-view), Light3R-SfM (RRA@15=94.7, RTA@15=85.8, mAA@30=72.8) surpasses all other feed-forward baselines including Spann3R (89.5/83.2/70.3), PoseDiff (80.5/79.8/66.5), and RelPose++ (82.3/77.2/65.1), and nearly matches the optimization-based MASt3R-SfM (96.0/93.1/88.0). On Waymo driving scenes, Light3R-SfM achieves RRA@5=78.3, exceeding MASt3R-SfM (75.7) while running in 8.5 s versus 1662 s, and outperforms Spann3R (55.1/14.5) by ~4× in RTA@5 at >6× lower runtime. The key trade-off is that optimization-based methods (MASt3R-SfM, GLOMAP, COLMAP) still lead in tight-threshold accuracy, particularly on dense full-sequence settings, but at 30–43× the runtime cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

Light3R-SfM builds directly on the DUSt3R/MASt3R pointmap regression paradigm, initializing its encoder-decoder from MASt3R pretrained weights and reusing MASt3R's retrieval embeddings for scene graph construction. Its key architectural departure is replacing the iterative global optimization (bundle adjustment or pairwise rigid transformation optimization) used by DUSt3R, MASt3R-SfM, and classical SfM with a learnable latent attention module that factorizes cross-image information sharing through per-image global tokens—a technique adapted from CoTracker's point-track attention. The SPT-based scene graph construction departs from the MST used in classical SfM to produce a flatter tree that reduces drift during pointmap accumulation, and the approach contrasts with concurrent Spann3R by using latent attention over unordered image sets rather than an explicit memory bank over sequential video frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 49× speedup over MASt3R-SfM (33 s vs. ~27 min for 200 images) with competitive accuracy demonstrates that feed-forward SfM is now a practical alternative to optimization-based pipelines for real-time and large-scale 3D reconstruction, potentially unblocking applications like autonomous driving and AR where iterative solvers are prohibitive. However, the accuracy gap to SOTA optimization methods (e.g., 52–54 RRA@5 vs. 68–75 for MASt3R-SfM/GLOMAP on dense view settings) and the acknowledged limitations—drift in long trajectories, failure at tens of thousands of images, and sensitivity to retrieval errors under extreme visual similarity—mean that feed-forward SfM is not yet a drop-in replacement. Open questions include whether higher-resolution processing, better retrieval, or hybrid feed-forward + light optimization can close the remaining accuracy gap without sacrificing the efficiency advantage.
