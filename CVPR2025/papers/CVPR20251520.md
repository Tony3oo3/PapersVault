## Problem

Pre-trained static 3D point cloud models capture only spatial structure and fail to model temporal dynamics, so directly transferring them to point cloud video tasks yields significantly inferior performance compared to 4D-specific self-supervised methods. The paper investigates how to adapt these frozen static pre-trained models for point cloud video understanding without the high cost of training 4D-specific models from scratch, given the limited availability of 4D pre-training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251520/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4D point cloud videos as coordinate sequences P ∈ R^{L×N×3} (no color or intensity), with L ∈ {8, 12, 16, 24} frames and N = 2048 points per frame. Points are grouped into point tubes of temporal length l=3 and spatial radius r_s=0.3 (32 points per frame), and the frozen pre-trained backbone processes the resulting tube embeddings. The model outputs a single action class (20 classes, MSR-Action3D) or gesture class (28 classes, SHREC'17) per video, obtained by averaging clip-level prediction probabilities.

## Method

The Cross-frame Spatio-temporal Adaptation (CSA) strategy introduces two trainable modules around a frozen static point cloud Transformer backbone. The Point Tube Adapter replaces the original embedding layer, encoding local spatio-temporal displacements (δx, δy, δz, δt) via a lightweight 4D PointNet with max-pooling to capture short-term structural changes. The Geometric Constraint Temporal Adapter (GCTA) constructs a directed cross-frame KNN graph over all point tubes based purely on spatial distance (not temporal adjacency), then applies EdgeConv-style edge features e_ij = φ(concat(f_j − f_i, f_i)) with max-pooling to aggregate long-term dynamics from spatially similar tubes across the entire video. At each Transformer layer, the interaction is F_{i+1} = FFN(Attention(F_i)) + GCTA(F_i) + PE(F_i), where Attention and FFN weights remain frozen while GCTA and per-layer MLP position encoders are trained. Only the PTA, GCTA, and task head parameters are updated during fine-tuning, keeping the pre-trained spatial encoder intact.

## Results

On MSR-Action3D action recognition, Point-MAE+CSA achieves 95.12% accuracy at 24 frames, outperforming all supervised and self-supervised baselines (best prior: C2P at 94.76%). At 8 frames, Point-MAE+CSA reaches 91.41%, surpassing the strongest prior method PointCMP (89.56%) by nearly 2 points. On SHREC'17 gesture recognition, PointGPT-S+CSA attains 96.5% accuracy, exceeding the best supervised baseline Kinet (95.2%) and all self-supervised methods (PointCMP 93.3%, MaST-Pre 92.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MSR-Action3D, the CSA-adapted models consistently outperform all 14 supervised and self-supervised baselines across every frame count (8/12/16/24). At 24 frames, Point-MAE+CSA (95.12%) edges out C2P (94.76%) by 0.36 points and MaST-Pre (94.08%) by 1.04 points; at 8 frames, the margin over the best prior method PointCMP (89.56%) widens to 1.85 points. On SHREC'17, PointGPT-S+CSA (96.5%) surpasses the strongest supervised baseline Kinet (95.2%) by 1.3 points and the best self-supervised method PointCMP (93.3%) by 3.2 points. The advantage is most pronounced at low temporal resolutions (8–12 frames), where the frozen pre-trained spatial features provide a stronger prior than methods trained end-to-end on limited 4D data. Among the three base models, Point-MAE+CSA and PointGPT-S+CSA are the strongest on action recognition, while PointGPT-S+CSA leads on gesture recognition, suggesting the adaptation strategy is robust to the choice of pre-trained backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: static 3D point cloud pre-training (PointBERT, Point-MAE, PointGPT) and 4D point cloud video understanding (P4Transformer, PSTNet, MaST-Pre). It repurposes the point tube concept from P4Transformer/PSTNet as an embedding adapter and borrows EdgeConv's edge-feature computation from Dynamic Graph CNN to build the GCTA module, but the key departure is treating the static pre-trained Transformer backbone as a frozen spatial encoder and injecting temporal awareness exclusively through lightweight, trainable adapters. Unlike prior 4D self-supervised methods that train end-to-end on limited 4D data, this work is the first to demonstrate that spatial semantics learned on ShapeNet can be transferred to dynamic point cloud video tasks with only adapter-level parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: it eliminates the need for expensive 4D-specific pre-training on scarce 4D datasets, instead leveraging the abundant ShapeNet pre-training already available for static point clouds. This parameter-efficient adaptation paradigm (frozen backbone + ~small trainable adapters) could be broadly applicable to other 3D→4D transfer scenarios. However, the evaluation is limited to two relatively small datasets (MSR-Action3D, SHREC'17) and two task types (action and gesture recognition), using only point coordinates without color or other modalities. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale 4D benchmarks, denser point clouds, other downstream tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the frozen-backbone assumption holds when the 4D domain shift is more severe.
