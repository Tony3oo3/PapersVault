## Problem

Existing video tokenizers are trained to reconstruct all frames simultaneously, causing training cost to scale linearly with video length and limiting them to short 16-frame clips. This prevents them from exploiting the temporal coherence present in longer videos, resulting in inefficient tokenization that requires 6144–8192 tokens to encode a 128-frame clip. The paper asks whether a tokenizer can be trained directly on long videos without prohibitive memory and compute costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251965/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128-frame video at 128×128 resolution, divides it into non-overlapping space-time patches, and encodes it into three 2D triplane latent planes (z^xy of shape H'×W', z^yt of shape W'×T', z^xt of shape H'×T') totaling only 1280 tokens. During decoding, the model does not reconstruct the full video; instead it accepts N randomly sampled (i,j,k) coordinates in [0,1]³ and outputs the RGB pixels of the corresponding patches, with a fine-tuning phase that switches to sampling entire frames to compute LPIPS loss.

## Method

CoordTok's encoder processes space-time patches through transformer layers, then uses cross-self attention layers to project learnable embeddings into factorized triplane representations, separating global content (z^xy) from motion along two spatial axes (z^yt, z^xt). The decoder, inspired by coordinate-based 3D generative models (e.g., LRM), queries each sampled (i,j,k) coordinate from the three triplanes via bilinear interpolation to form a coordinate-based representation, concatenates the three lookups, and passes them through self-attention layers before a linear projection to patch pixels. Training minimizes an ℓ₂ loss over only ~3% of randomly sampled patches per iteration, avoiding the need to reconstruct the entire 128-frame video and enabling large batch sizes on a single GPU. A subsequent fine-tuning phase samples a few full frames to compute LPIPS loss, improving perceptual quality without sacrificing the memory efficiency of coordinate sampling.

## Results

On UCF-101 (128-frame, 128×128 resolution), CoordTok encodes a 128-frame video into only 1280 tokens while achieving PSNR 28.6, LPIPS 0.066, SSIM 0.892, and rFVD 102.9. In contrast, baselines require 6144–8192 tokens to reach comparable quality (e.g., PVDM-AE at 6144 tokens: PSNR 26.5, rFVD 66.5; OmniTokenizer-CV at 8192 tokens: PSNR 28.3, rFVD 49.5). For unconditional video generation, CoordTok-SiT-L/2 achieves the best FVD of 369.3 (vs. 505.0 for PVDM-L and 549.7 for HVDM) while generating 128-frame videos in 9.8 s on a single 4090 GPU, roughly 5× faster than HVDM (52.1 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoordTok (1280 tokens, 128 frames) outperforms all baselines at comparable token budgets: at ~1024–1152 tokens, it achieves PSNR 28.6 vs. 23.2 (OmniTokenizer-CV), 24.0 (CosmosTokenizer-CV), and 19.1 (PVDM-AE), with rFVD 102.9 vs. 396.7, 519.6, and 1270.1 respectively. Against the strongest 8192-token baselines, CoordTok matches or exceeds PSNR (28.6 vs. 28.3 for OmniTokenizer-CV, 28.5 for CosmosTokenizer-CV) and LPIPS (0.066 vs. 0.081/0.119) while using only ~16% of the tokens, though it trails on rFVD (102.9 vs. 49.5 for OmniTokenizer-CV). Compared to PVDM-AE, which shares the same triplane latent structure, CoordTok's coordinate-based decoder yields substantially better reconstruction at far fewer tokens (1280 vs. 6144), confirming the benefit of the patch-sampling training strategy. For video generation, CoordTok-SiT-L/2 achieves FVD 369.3, beating PVDM-L (505.0), HVDM (549.7), and Latte-L/2 (1901.8), while also being the fastest at 9.8 s per 128-frame video (vs. 52.1 s for HVDM and 21.4 s for Latte-L/2) on a single 4090 GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoordTok builds on two distinct lines of work: (1) factorized triplane representations for video encoding (PVDM, HVDM, Kim et al.), which decompose a video into spatial and spatiotemporal latent planes, and (2) coordinate-based reconstruction from 3D generative models (LRM, Shape-E, One-2-3-45), which learn mappings from randomly sampled coordinates to output values instead of processing all coordinates simultaneously. The key departure from prior video tokenizers (TATS, MAGVIT, LARP, OmniTokenizer) is that CoordTok avoids reconstructing all frames at once during training, replacing the full-frame decoder with a coordinate-sampled patch decoder. This design, inspired by 3D generation, is what enables training directly on 128-frame videos rather than the 16-frame clips used by all baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~5-6× reduction in token count (1280 vs. 6144–8192) for comparable reconstruction quality, and the ability to train a diffusion transformer to generate 128-frame videos at once with lower memory and faster inference than prior methods. The main limitation is that the triplane decomposition struggles with highly dynamic videos (correlation r = −0.87 between dynamics and reconstruction quality), suggesting the content/motion factorization is a bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer videos, whether adaptive content planes (analogous to keyframes in video codecs) can address the dynamics limitation, and whether the coordinate-sampling strategy transfers to other modalities or larger datasets beyond UCF-101.
