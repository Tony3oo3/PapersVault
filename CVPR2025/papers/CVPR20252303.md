## Problem

Standard ViT fine-tuning for image aesthetic assessment requires resizing or cropping images to a small fixed size, which destroys aspect ratio, composition, high-resolution details, and multi-scale information—all of which directly influence aesthetic scores. Existing approaches preserve at most one or two of these factors (e.g., aspect ratio via padding, or multi-scale via MUSIQ), but none simultaneously preserves all four while remaining computationally practical.

## Input / Output

Charm takes RGB images at their original resolution with no resizing, cropping, or normalization (normalization is explicitly avoided as it introduces distortions affecting aesthetic scores). The tokenization produces a fixed-length token sequence (512–1024 tokens depending on dataset) regardless of input image size, enabling batch processing. The model outputs either a scalar aesthetic/quality score (L1 loss) or a full aesthetic score distribution (EMD loss), depending on the dataset.

## Method

Charm is a two-scale tokenization strategy that first partitions the image into large patches (size p′ = p×n), then selects a subset of patches to re-tokenize at the original small patch size p (high-resolution) while the remainder stay at p′ (low-resolution), yielding a fixed-length sequence. Position embeddings are adapted from the pre-trained ViT via bilinear interpolation at both patch scales, and learnable scale embeddings are added element-wise to help the transformer distinguish tokens across scales. The approach is plug-and-play: it replaces only the patch encoding module of a pre-trained ViT (e.g., Dinov2-small) without modifying the transformer architecture, positional embedding scheme, or requiring any additional pre-training. Patch selection for high-resolution retention can be random (best performing, acting as implicit data augmentation), frequency-based, gradient-based, entropy-based, or saliency-based. Fine-tuning uses AdamW with cosine annealing, limited augmentation (horizontal flip, 90° rotations), and no image normalization.

## Results

On the AVA dataset, Dinov2-small + Charm achieves PLCC 0.779, SRCC 0.777, and ACC 0.826, outperforming all compared baselines. Across seven IAA/IQA datasets, replacing standard ViT tokenization with Charm yields improvements of up to 7.5% in PLCC (AADB), 8.1% in SRCC (KonIQ10k), and 14.8% in classification accuracy (TAD66k). The method also reduces computational cost by over 84% in GMACs and 83% in GPU memory when processing 640×640 images compared to full-resolution processing, while maintaining a fixed 512-token sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Charm (Dinov2-small) outperforms all five baselines on the AVA dataset in both PLCC and SRCC. It beats Ghosal et al.'s graph attention network [11] by 1.5% PLCC (0.779 vs. 0.764) and 1.5% SRCC, and substantially outperforms Chen et al.'s adaptive fractional dilated convolution [6] by 10.8% PLCC and 12.8% SRCC. Against multi-scale ViT approaches, Charm surpasses MUSIQ [21] by 4.1% PLCC and 5.1% SRCC, FlexiViT [2] by 4.2% PLCC and 4.2% SRCC, and Swin [32] by 3.1% PLCC and 2.6% SRCC. Notably, Charm is the only method that simultaneously preserves aspect ratio, high-resolution details, and multi-scale information, whereas each baseline addresses at most one or two of these factors. The lightweight Dinov2-small + Charm configuration also matches or exceeds the performance of the much larger Dinov2-large + Charm, making it suitable for resource-constrained deployments.

## Relation to Prior Work

Charm is positioned as a tokenization strategy for pre-trained ViTs that departs from the closest prior work—mixed-resolution tokenization [36] and MSViT [13]—by avoiding the initial uniform downscaling to a fixed low resolution that those methods require for batch processing. Instead, Charm selectively preserves patches at their original resolution while downscaling others, maintaining high-frequency detail critical for aesthetic scoring. It also differs from multi-scale approaches like MUSIQ [21] and FlexiViT [2] by not modifying the ViT architecture or introducing new position embeddings, making it a drop-in replacement compatible with any pre-trained ViT backbone. The method draws conceptual inspiration from the human visual system's hierarchical processing of multi-scale information and from NaViT's [8] handling of variable aspect ratios, but achieves this with a fixed-length token sequence and frozen position embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Charm addresses a practical gap in IAA: no prior method simultaneously preserves composition, high-resolution detail, aspect ratio, and multi-scale information while remaining computationally tractable. The plug-in design (no retraining, no architecture changes) makes it immediately applicable to existing ViT backbones, and the 84% GMAC reduction versus full-resolution processing makes it viable on modest hardware. Open questions include whether more sophisticated patch selection strategies can close the gap with random selection, how much information is irreversibly lost through selective downscaling, and whether the approach transfers to other resolution-sensitive tasks beyond aesthetics. The finding that random patch selection outperforms content-aware strategies (saliency, frequency, gradient) suggests the diversity it introduces as implicit data augmentation may matter more than preserving specific 'important' regions.
