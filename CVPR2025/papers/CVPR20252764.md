## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks for spatial understanding focus on single images, spatially discrete multi-image sets, or dynamic video frames, but largely overlook the compositional structure of images captured from a fixed viewpoint while rotating orientation. The paper identifies this gap as 'Continuous Space Perception'—the ability to reconstruct a complete surrounding scene from overlapping, directionally-ordered views—and argues it is essential for real-world tasks like navigation and embodied planning yet remains unmeasured.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252764/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance provides 4–5 RGB images captured from the same static viewpoint facing different directions (e.g., front, right, back, left), with natural overlaps between adjacent views, accompanied by a text prompt specifying the direction of the first image. Outputs vary by task: four-option multiple choice for direction recognition and planning, a single integer for counting, an image index for rotation-difference, and an ordered action sequence (turn/go-ahead) for planning-decision. For Direction and Counting tasks, each question is posed twice with the image sequence rotated (preserving clockwise order but changing the starting view) to measure response consistency via pair-wise accuracy (ACC_p) alongside query-wise accuracy (ACC_q).

## Method

CoSpace is constructed via a collect–filter–annotate pipeline: images are automatically gathered from Baidu Map Panorama API (2,302 real-world images) and HM3D 3D reconstructions rendered through Habitat-Sim (616 synthetic images), then manually filtered for sufficient visual information, with GPT-4o generating candidate questions via few-shot prompting and human annotators (≥2 per question) verifying answers. The benchmark comprises 2,918 images and 1,626 QA pairs organized into four categories and seven tasks: Direction Recognition, Directional Object Perception, Rotation-Angle, Rotation-Difference, Counting, Planning-QA, and Planning-Decision. Evaluation spans 19 VLMs (3 proprietary APIs, 16 open-source checkpoints from 2B to 78B parameters), with additional analyses including disrupted image-order robustness, a single-image caption pipeline for comparison, and conditional accuracy measuring whether correct object localization transfers to correct navigation decisions.

## Results

On the CoSpace benchmark (1,626 QA pairs, 2,918 images), the best proprietary model Claude-3.7-sonnet achieves an average accuracy of 51.97%, followed by Gemini-1.5-pro (44.39%) and GPT-4o (42.10%). Among open-source models, InternVL2_5-78B leads with 45.50% average, while the best <13B model MiniCPM-V 2.6 reaches 37.10%. Notably, all open-source models score near 50% on the ROT-Ang task (equivalent to random guessing between two options), whereas proprietary models achieve 58–64%. Human annotators achieve 86.25% average ACCq, substantially outperforming the best model (Claude-3.7-sonnet at 59.56%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Claude-3.7-sonnet leads overall (51.97% avg) but is outperformed by InternVL2_5-78B on DIR-Obj (54.40 vs. 45.60 ACCq) and by InternVL2-76B on CNT (50.00 vs. 45.00 ACCq), showing proprietary models do not dominate every task. On ROT-Ang, all open-source models collapse to ~50% (random level) due to fixed predictions, while proprietary models achieve 58–64%, revealing a critical gap in angle-reasoning capability. The key differentiator between proprietary and open-source models is response consistency: proprietary models show smaller ACCq-to-ACCp gaps (e.g., Claude: 44.40→29.20 on DIR-Rec) compared to open-source models with similar ACCq but much lower ACCp. Among >70B open-source models, InternVL2_5-78B (45.50%) and Qwen2-VL-72B (44.82%) are competitive with Gemini-1.5-pro (44.39%) on average, while LLaVA-OneVision-72B (29.17%) underperforms even many <13B models. Single-image models (LLaVA-v1.6 at 25.60%, Llama-3.2-11B at 24.25%) are substantially outperformed by multi-image models, confirming the necessity of multi-image input for continuous space perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing 'continuous space perception' as a distinct evaluation axis, positioned between existing spatial-reasoning benchmarks (MMRel, GSR-Bench, SpatialEval) that operate on single images and multi-image understanding benchmarks (MIBench, MileBench, Mementos-Val) that use spatially unrelated or temporally sequential images. The key conceptual departure is the static-viewpoint, overlapping-image formulation: images are captured from one fixed position at different orientations so that adjacent views share visual content, requiring models to recognize object identity across image boundaries. This is explicitly contrasted with video-based benchmarks (Video-MME, SEED-Bench) where temporal dynamics confound spatial reasoning, and with object-centric multi-view benchmarks (BLINK) where views are discrete rather than continuous.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even top proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-pro, Claude-3.7-sonnet) achieve only ~42–52% average accuracy on continuous space perception, with a notable consistency gap: proprietary models are less stable across input perturbations than some open-source counterparts. The near-random performance of all open-source models on rotation-angle reasoning (fixed predictions across all queries) highlights a fundamental gap in fine-grained spatial inference that current architectures do not address. Open questions include whether larger context windows or explicit spatial representations (e.g., 3D scene graphs) could close the consistency gap, and whether the disrupted-order robustness observed in small models reflects genuine spatial understanding or prediction instability. For embodied AI and navigation applications, this benchmark provides a concrete diagnostic for the spatial integration ability that planning and decision-making tasks depend on.
