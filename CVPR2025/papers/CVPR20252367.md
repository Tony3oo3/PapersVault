## Problem

DNN architectures of comparable depth and width (e.g., AlexNet vs. ViT) exhibit drastically different performance, yet existing expressivity theory ties capacity to depth or width and cannot compare across architecture families. The authors argue that quantifying the non-linearity of activation functions is the missing fine-grained measure needed to understand the decade-long evolution of vision architectures and explain why transformers broke the prior trend toward increasing linearity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252367/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained DNNs (AlexNet through ConvNeXt) and passes batches of 512 images from the full ImageNet-1K validation set (50,000 images) through them, recording pre-activation and post-activation tensors at every activation function. The output is a non-linearity signature: an ordered vector of affinity scores (one per activation function, in layer order) that encodes the entire network's non-linearity profile. Unlike scalar metrics, this signature preserves the layer-wise structure, enabling pairwise comparisons via dynamic time warping and revealing architectural patterns such as ResNet's characteristic bimodal distribution.

## Method

The core contribution is the affinity score, a theoretically grounded measure of activation non-linearity built on optimal transport. Given pre-activation X and post-activation Y = σ(X), the method computes the closed-form affine OT map T_aff between their Gaussian approximations (parameterized by mean and covariance via Theorem 3.3), then normalizes the Wasserstein-2 distance W2(T_aff X, Y) by the output variance to yield a score in [0,1]. The Gelbrich bound and a novel upper bound (Proposition 3.5) guarantee the normalization is valid, and Corollary 3.2 proves that when σ is truly linear, the OT map exactly recovers it, making the score identifiably zero for linear activations. Crucially, T_aff is a globally optimal non-linear mapping (unlike the linear regression alternative), and the resulting score does not correlate with other standard metrics (CKA, sparsity, entropy, R²) across architectures, establishing its uniqueness as a non-linearity measure.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative finding is that statistics extracted from non-linearity signatures (median, minimum, spread of affinity scores) correlate more strongly with ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy than traditional architecture descriptors (depth, parameter count, GFLOPS) for ResNets, DenseNets, ViTs, and post-ViT models (Figure 6). Table 1 demonstrates the uniqueness of the affinity score: no other metric (CKA, NORM, SPARSITY, ENTROPY, R²) correlates consistently with the non-linearity signature across all eight architecture families, with the best single-metric correlation being SPARSITY at −0.89 for DenseNets only. The headline empirical finding is a trend reversal: from AlexNet (2012) through EfficientNet (2019), landmark vision models progressively *decreased* the average non-linearity of their activation functions, while ViTs (2020) and Swin Transformers (2021) sharply reversed this, becoming the most non-linear architectures studied.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main empirical comparison is with Teney et al. (Neural Redshift, CVPR 2024) [62], who claimed that ReLU activations carry a strong, weight-magnitude-independent bias toward low-complexity functions in randomly initialized MLPs. The authors reproduce this claim (Figure 4A) but then show it is not universal: by reducing the Glorot initialization scale by just 0.05 (s′ = s − 0.05), the ReLU simplicity bias vanishes entirely, and the average affinity scores of ReLU and Tanh activations become nearly indistinguishable. This demonstrates that the apparent complexity bias is an artifact of the input domain rather than an intrinsic property of the activation function. In terms of predictive power for ImageNet accuracy, the non-linearity signature statistics outperform traditional descriptors: for ResNets, DenseNets, ViTs, and post-ViT models, the best non-linearity statistic achieves a higher R² than depth, parameter count, or GFLOPS (Figure 6), whereas for NAS-based models (EfficientNet, MNASNet) parameter count remains the strongest predictor as expected from their design objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on classical optimal transport theory—specifically the characterization of OT maps as gradients of convex functions (Smith & Knott, 1987), the Gelbrich bound on Wasserstein distances between measures and their Gaussian approximations (1990), and the closed-form W2 between Gaussians (Peyré & Cuturi, 2019)—to derive a new, theoretically grounded measure of activation-function non-linearity. It departs from prior expressiveness analyses that reduce to depth or width arguments, and from the ad-hoc non-linearity coefficient of Philipp (2021) which requires differentiability and has no practical implementation. The paper also explicitly revisits and challenges the 'neural redshift' findings of Teney et al. (2024), showing that ReLU's apparent simplicity bias is an artifact of the input domain rather than an intrinsic property of the activation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-linearity signature offers a new, interpretable lens for understanding why transformers outperform convnets of comparable size, and the finding that pre-transformer architectures were trending toward *greater* linearity (not less) reframes a decade of architectural evolution. The affinity score is fully differentiable, opening the door to using it as a regularization term to control inductive bias during training, and to monitoring training dynamics via signature deviations. Open questions include whether the absence of a 'perfect non-linearity' in 1D extends to higher dimensions, whether the framework generalizes beyond vision to NLP and multimodal foundation models, and whether the observed accuracy correlations are causal or merely correlational.
