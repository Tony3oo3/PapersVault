## Problem

Existing self-supervised video hashing methods rely on random frame sampling that treats all frames equally, ignoring the varying information density and reconstruction difficulty across frames. This uniform treatment leads to suboptimal hash codes because the model never prioritizes the most informative or challenging frames for learning, degrading retrieval performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251021/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted frame features from frozen 2D CNNs (VGG16 at 1024-dim with 30 frames for ActivityNet; ResNet-50 at 2048-dim with 25 frames for other datasets) and produces K-bit binary hash codes in {-1,+1}^K for Hamming-space retrieval. A Gumbel-Softmax TopK mechanism adaptively masks M frames per video, leaving M₀−M frames for the Transformer encoder, and two distinct views are generated per video (with different Gumbel noise) to form positive pairs for contrastive learning.

## Method

AutoSSVH introduces an adversarial automated frame sampler where a lightweight Grade-Net MLP scores each frame, a Gumbel-Softmax TopK operation performs differentiable frame selection, and a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) enables a single-stage min-max game: the sampler maximizes reconstruction difficulty while the Transformer hashing network minimizes it, so the model autonomously identifies and processes the hardest frames. The hashing network is a Transformer encoder-decoder with a tanh hash layer and mean-pooling-plus-sign quantization to produce video-level binary codes. To accelerate adversarial convergence and capture global semantics, the method adds a Point-to-Set (P2Set) hash-based contrastive objective: k-means clustering on current-epoch embeddings yields pseudo-labels, component voting computes a binary anchor code per cluster, and a contrastive loss pulls each video's hash code toward its cluster anchor. The total loss combines frame reconstruction (L_FR), view contrastive learning (L_VC), and P2Set alignment (L_P2Set), with the GRL ensuring the sampler's gradients are flipped so it learns to select increasingly challenging frames throughout training.

## Results

AutoSSVH achieves state-of-the-art mAP@N across all four benchmark datasets (ActivityNet, FCVID, UCF101, HMDB51) at 16, 32, and 64-bit hash lengths, consistently outperforming all six baselines. In cross-dataset validation (trained on UCF101, tested on HMDB51), AutoSSVH achieves GMAP scores of 0.0410, 0.0550, and 0.0780 at 16/32/64 bits respectively, representing a 20.7% geometric-mean improvement over the strongest competitor ConMH. On UCF101, improvements over MCMSH reach 34.4%, 66.7%, and 50.9% at different N values, with particularly strong gains at higher retrieval thresholds (larger N).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoSSVH outperforms all six baselines (SSVH, BTH, DKPH, MCMSH, ConMH, BerVAE) across all four datasets and all three bit lengths (16, 32, 64). The strongest competitor is ConMH [Wang et al., AAAI 2023], which AutoSSVH surpasses by 20.7% in geometric-mean GMAP on the cross-dataset UCF101→HMDB51 evaluation. BerVAE [Wang & Zhou, AISTATS 2023] is the second-strongest baseline, trailing AutoSSVH by roughly 16–21% in GMAP at 32 and 64 bits. The gap over older methods is larger: MCMSH [Hao et al., ACM MM 2022] is outperformed by 34.4–66.7% depending on N, while BTH [Li et al., CVPR 2021] and DKPH [Li et al., ECCV 2022] lag by 11.5–18.1%. The paper also reports that AutoSSVH's advantage grows at higher N values (e.g., mAP@100), suggesting the component-voting strategy particularly benefits retrieval of less-obvious matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoSSVH builds directly on ConMH (Wang et al., AAAI 2023), which established the MAE-based frame reconstruction and contrastive learning paradigm for self-supervised video hashing, but replaces its random frame sampling with an adversarial, differentiable Top-K sampler. Conceptually, it draws from the broader MIM masking-strategy literature (AttMask, AdaMAE, ADIOS) yet distinguishes itself by using a lightweight Grade-Net with Gumbel-Softmax and a single-stage gradient-reversal adversarial loop rather than multi-stage or teacher-student frameworks. The P2Set hash-based contrastive objective is adapted from DHTA's point-to-set distance formulation, repurposed here to anchor hash codes to k-means cluster centroids via component voting, bridging the hashing and clustering literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that adversarially selecting hard-to-reconstruct frames is a meaningful improvement over random sampling in video hashing, and that a simple component-voting anchor mechanism can both accelerate adversarial convergence (70% speedup) and capture higher-level semantics. However, the approach still depends on pre-trained 2D CNNs for frame features and k-means pseudo-labels, which introduce noise and limit scalability to very long or high-resolution videos. Open questions include whether the adversarial sampling generalizes beyond hashing to other video understanding tasks, whether the Gumbel-Softmax Top-K mechanism remains stable at larger frame counts, and whether the two-phase training (warm-up then component voting) can be unified into a single schedule.
