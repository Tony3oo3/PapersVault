## Problem

Existing noisy correspondence (NC) methods for cross-modal retrieval rely primarily on cross-modal similarity matching to identify true correspondences, neglecting intra-modal relation consistency. This omission causes hard NCs—mismatched pairs with high object-level similarity—to be misidentified as positives, introducing false supervisions that degrade retrieval performance. The paper asks whether jointly enforcing both cross-modal and intra-modal relation consistency can more reliably discriminate true correspondences from mismatches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image region sequences (N_V regions) and text word sequences (N_L words) extracted by the SGRAF backbone, operating at the fine-grained region/word level rather than holistic embeddings. It produces standard bidirectional retrieval rankings (image-to-text and text-to-image) evaluated by R@K metrics. A key detail is that the intra-modal relation consistency requires constructing proxy relation matrices by selecting the most relevant item in the opposite modality for each anchor item, making the method sensitive to the quality of cross-modal alignment during training.

## Method

ReCon introduces a dual relation consistency learning framework with two complementary constraints: (1) cross-modal relation consistency via bidirectional InfoNCE loss that maximizes the similarity gap between matched and mismatched pairs, and (2) intra-modal relation consistency via symmetric KL divergence between each modality's internal relation matrix and a proxy relation matrix reconstructed from the opposite modality's relations. For true correspondence discrimination, the framework performs a two-stage data partition: first, a GMM fits per-sample cross-modal losses to roughly separate clean from noisy pairs, then intra-modal relation discrepancies (measured by L_IM) further refine the clean partition into true positives (D_c) and local-associated pairs (D_l). Each partition is trained with a tailored strategy—D_c uses full cross-modal and intra-modal losses, D_l applies a penalization factor λ=exp(y_i^IM/α) to downweight intra-modal contributions, and D_n uses momentum-averaged pseudo labels with intra-modal loss excluded to prevent incorrect supervisions.

## Results

On CC152K with real-world noisy correspondences (3%–20% unknown NCs), ReCon achieves rSum of 380.5, outperforming the second-best ESC (375.9) by 4.4%. Under 40% simulated NCs on Flickr30K, ReCon attains rSum of 505.2 (I2T R@1=79.4, T2I R@1=59.9), surpassing the best baseline GSC (497.3) by 7.9 points. On MS-COCO 1K at 40% noise, ReCon reaches rSum 524.5, edging out ESC (523.9). Notably, under 50% noise on MS-COCO 5K, ReCon (rSum 428.3) substantially exceeds the zero-shot CLIP-32 (rSum 131.1), demonstrating robustness far beyond pre-trained vision-language models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCon consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every noise level and dataset. On CC152K (real-world NCs), ReCon leads ESC (375.9) and GSC (375.1) by 4.4 and 5.4 rSum points respectively, with the largest gap over SCAN (295.7) at 84.8 points. Under 40% simulated NCs on Flickr30K, ReCon (505.2) beats GSC (497.3) and SREM (495.4) by 7.9 and 9.8 rSum points; on MS-COCO 1K at the same noise level, the margin narrows to 0.6 over ESC (523.9) and 4.6 over GSC (519.9). At the extreme 60% noise setting, ReCon maintains a clear lead of 14.9 rSum over ESC on Flickr30K (489.9 vs. 475.0) and 2.8 over L2RM on MS-COCO 1K (517.8 vs. 515.0). Against the CLIP pre-trained model on MS-COCO 5K, ReCon under 50% noise (rSum 428.3) surpasses zero-shot CLIP-32 (131.1) by nearly 300 points, highlighting the necessity of explicit NC handling over scale alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCon extends the noisy correspondence learning framework established by NCR [15], which introduced small-loss criterion partitioning, GMM-based data division, and momentum pseudo-labeling for cross-modal retrieval. Its key conceptual departure is the incorporation of intra-modal relation consistency, adapted from the IAIS [29] relation alignment formulation, as a complementary discriminative signal alongside the standard cross-modal similarity. While prior NC methods (MSCN, BiCro, RCL, CRCL, SREM, PC², L2RM, ESC, GSC) all operate solely on cross-modal matching scores to partition data, ReCon jointly enforces both cross-modal and intra-modal relation alignment, arguing that mismatches with high cross-modal similarity (hard NCs) can still be detected through inconsistent intra-modal relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important problem—real-world multimodal datasets inevitably contain mismatched pairs that silently degrade retrieval models—and provides a principled mechanism (dual relation consistency) to filter them more reliably than cross-modal similarity alone. The consistent gains across three datasets and multiple noise levels, including outperforming zero-shot CLIP under 50% noise, suggest the intra-modal relation signal is a robust and underutilized source of discriminative information. Open questions include whether the fixed-threshold GMM partitioning generalizes to unknown noise distributions, whether the proxy relation matrix construction (requiring an anchor modality) extends naturally to more than two modalities, and whether the approach remains effective when the backbone is changed from SGRAF to other architectures.
