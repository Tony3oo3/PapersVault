## Problem

Gradient-based adversarial attacks on SNNs face two critical obstacles: (1) the surrogate gradient (SG) used in spatial-temporal backpropagation is invisible to an attacker with only inference access, and existing universal SGs lack correlation with the victim model, yielding sub-optimal attacks; (2) binary dynamic images from dynamic vision sensors are incompatible with standard floating-point gradient attacks, and existing sparse binary attacks suffer from gradient vanishing and insufficient perturbation sparsity.

## Input / Output

The method accepts three input formats: RGB images (encoded via direct or Poisson coding to extend the temporal dimension), integer dynamic frames (event counts per pixel per time window), and binary dynamic frames (binarized event aggregations for hardware compatibility). Input resolutions range from 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) to 224×224 (ImageNet) for static data and 34×34 to 128×128 for dynamic data. For static and integer inputs, the output is an adversarial example with ℓ∞-bounded perturbations; for binary dynamic inputs, the output is a sparsely perturbed binary frame with ℓ0-bounded modifications (e.g., only 0.24% of pixels changed on CIFAR10-DVS).

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary techniques. First, the Potential-Dependent Surrogate Gradient (PDSG) derives the SG shape from the two-point zeroth-order estimator, yielding a Gaussian whose width equals the standard deviation σ of the runtime membrane potential distribution, making the SG adapt to each model without knowing its training-phase SG. A calibration step right-shifts the Gaussian by b=0.5σ to balance gradients on both sides of the firing threshold, preventing the attack from disproportionately increasing firing rates. Second, the Sparse Dynamic Attack (SDA) employs a generation-reduction paradigm for binary inputs: the generation phase selects contributing gradients (sign-matched to the binary input change), applies top-k filtering, then refines selection via finite differences that capture the actual loss change under ±1 pixel flips; the reduction phase sorts all perturbed pixels by FD magnitude and uses binary search to identify the minimal subset of perturbations that still maintains adversariality, fully optimizing sparsity.

## Results

On static and integer dynamic inputs, PDSG achieves 100% attack success rate (ASR) on ImageNet (HST-10-768, PGD, ε=8/255) and 100% on CIFAR-10 (ResNet18, PGD, ε=8/255), outperforming STBP (99.89%, 99.81%), RGA (2.84%, 92.47%), and HART (99.98%, 98.64%) respectively. On binary dynamic images, SDA achieves 82% ASR under ℓ₀<800 on CIFAR10-DVS (only 0.24% of pixels modified) and 100% ASR in unbounded dynamic evaluation across all three binary datasets (N-MNIST, DVS-Gesture, CIFAR10-DVS), while perturbing a median of only 303 pixels on CIFAR10-DVS versus 491 for SpikeFool.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDSG consistently outperforms RGA [5] and HART [26] across nearly all static and integer-dynamic settings. On ImageNet with PGD (ε=8/255), PDSG reaches 100% ASR versus 99.98% (HART) and a catastrophic 2.84% (RGA), demonstrating that RGA's universal SG is particularly ill-suited for large-scale models. On CIFAR-10 with adversarially trained ResNet18, PDSG (62.16%) still leads HART (59.56%) and STBP (28.23%), showing robustness to defense. For binary dynamic attacks, SDA dominates all three baselines: on CIFAR10-DVS with ℓ₀<800, SDA achieves 82% ASR versus 8% (SpikeFool [6]), 0% (SCG [42]), and 0% (GSAttack [76]). In unbounded dynamic evaluation, SDA perturbs a median of 303 pixels on CIFAR10-DVS, compared to 491 for SpikeFool, 2191 for SCG, and 8741 for GSAttack, confirming that the generation-reduction paradigm yields substantially sparser yet equally effective perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the STBP framework (Wu et al., 2018) for gradient computation in SNNs and the two-point zeroth-order estimation method (Mukhoty et al., 2023) as the mathematical foundation for deriving the PDSG. It departs from prior SNN attack methods (RGA, HART) that employ universal, model-agnostic surrogate gradients by instead adapting the SG shape to the runtime membrane potential distribution of the specific victim model. For binary dynamic images, it extends the sparse attack paradigm (SpikeFool, SCG) with a generation-reduction loop that combines gradient-based coarse selection with finite-difference-based fine selection and a binary-search reduction step, addressing the gradient vanishing and sparsity limitations of prior binary-input attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PDSG provides a practical solution to a real deployment concern: SNNs on neuromorphic hardware expose only inference parameters, making the training-phase SG invisible to an attacker, yet the paper shows that a model-adaptive SG derived from runtime statistics can outperform even the true training SG. The SDA's ability to achieve 82% ASR by modifying only 0.24% of pixels on CIFAR10DVS raises the bar for what constitutes a detectable perturbation on sparse binary inputs, directly informing defense design. Open questions include whether the PDSG's Gaussian assumption on membrane potential holds for non-LIF neuron models, whether adversarial training can be adapted to counter model-adaptive SGs, and whether the generation-reduction paradigm extends to multi-bit or continuous-valued dynamic inputs.
