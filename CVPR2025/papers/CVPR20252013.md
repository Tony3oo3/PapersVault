## Problem

Gaussian Splatting offers fast, editable 3D rendering, but its complex data structure—sparse, multi-channel point clouds with non-Euclidean attributes like quaternions—makes direct 3D generation extremely difficult. Existing text-to-3D methods either rely on slow 2D-lifting (SDS) with Janus artifacts or operate on other representations (NeRF, SDF, meshes), leaving a gap for efficient, direct GS generation in 3D space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252013/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting point cloud (position, scaling, rotation quaternion, spherical harmonics coefficients, and opacity per splat) that can be rendered to multi-view images in real time. Internally, the 3D object is encoded as a triplane tensor of size 3×128×128×16 with a 64×64×64 deformable SDF grid, compressed by a VAE (down-sample factor 4) into a latent code that is rolled out to an image-like tensor for diffusion. Full inference (diffusion sampling + VAE decode + TriRenderer) takes approximately 16 seconds on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

DirectTriGS encodes discrete GS point clouds into a continuous triplane field, separating geometry channels (decoded via a deformable SDF volume + FlexiCubes mesh extraction + surface sampling) from GS attribute channels (decoded by querying the triplane at sampled surface points). The fully differentiable TriRenderer bridges this triplane field to standard GS rendering, enabling end-to-end training with only 2D multi-view rendering losses (L1, SSIM, PIP, alpha) plus SDF geometric regularization. A VAE with decoupled geometry/attribute encoders compresses triplanes to latent space, and a two-stage latent diffusion model first generates the geometry latent, then the GS attribute latent conditioned on it, following DDPM with text conditioning. An optional SDS-based texture booster refines the generated GS by optimizing splat attributes against a pretrained 2D diffusion model while keeping splats mesh-bound to avoid Janus artifacts.

## Results

In the user study (49 raters, 1–5 scale) on direct text-to-3D generation, DirectTriGS achieves an overall score of 3.423, outperforming Direct3D (3.183) and Shap-E (3.042) across all sub-metrics (geometry, texture, realism, consistency). On CLIP text-image alignment, DirectTriGS scores 0.2456 (ViT-L-14) and 0.2462 (ViT-L-14-336), surpassing Shap-E (0.2398 / 0.2426) and Direct3D (0.2152 / 0.2220). With the optional SDS refinement, DirectTriGS wins the user preference study over GaussianDreamer (46.3% vs. 42.5% overall) and GSGEN (46.3% vs. 11.3%). Inference takes 16.2 s on a single RTX 3090 with 2.94 GB GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Shap-E [19], DirectTriGS wins on every user-study sub-metric (e.g., geometry 3.456 vs. 3.080, overall 3.423 vs. 3.042) and on both CLIP variants (0.2456 vs. 0.2398 on ViT-L-14). Against Direct3D [26], the margin is similar: overall user score 3.423 vs. 3.183 and CLIP 0.2456 vs. 0.2152, with the largest gap in consistency (3.520 vs. 3.242). In the SDS-refinement comparison (Table 3), DirectTriGS outperforms GaussianDreamer [50] on overall preference (46.3% vs. 42.5%) and on the 'more realistic' criterion (47.5% vs. 35.0%), while GaussianDreamer edges ahead on 'more detailed' (50.0% vs. 45.0%). Against GSGEN [4], DirectTriGS dominates decisively, winning overall preference 46.3% to 11.3% and 'more realistic' 47.5% to 17.5%. All comparisons are conducted on the Objaverse dataset with 200K training objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: 3D Gaussian Splatting as a rendering representation (Kerbl et al.), triplane-based continuous field encoding (Shue et al.), and latent diffusion for generative modeling (Rombach et al.). It departs from the dominant SDS/2D-lifting paradigm (DreamFusion, GSGEN, GaussianDreamer) by performing generation entirely in 3D latent space, and from other direct 3D generation methods (Shap-E, Direct3D) by targeting the GS representation specifically rather than NeRF or SDF volumes. The key architectural departure is encoding the discrete, multi-channel GS point cloud as a continuous triplane field with decoupled geometry and appearance channels, then applying a VAE and two-stage latent diffusion — a pipeline not previously applied to GS generation. The geometry branch's use of deformable SDF + FlexiCubes for differentiable mesh extraction, combined with surface-constrained splat sampling, is a novel coupling of implicit geometry and explicit GS attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing text-to-3D GS generation to ~16 seconds (vs. 10+ minutes for SDS-based methods) while avoiding the Janus problem inherent in 2D-lifting approaches, making interactive 3D content creation more feasible. However, the paper acknowledges that the SDS refiner is still needed for competitive texture quality, and the ablation reveals that 3D diffusion on voxel-based GS attributes fails entirely due to non-Euclidean channels (quaternions), suggesting the triplane formulation is not merely a convenience but a necessity for this representation. Open questions include whether the two-stage geometry-then-appearance diffusion is truly required or an artifact of the decoupled VAE design, how the method scales to larger or more complex objects, and whether the triplane resolution (128×128) is sufficient for high-frequency detail without the SDS boost.
