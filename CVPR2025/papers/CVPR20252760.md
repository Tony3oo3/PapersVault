## Problem

Existing sparse supervised 3D object detection methods rely on GT Sampling to guarantee all object categories appear in each scene, a strategy that works for outdoor driving (car, pedestrian, bicycle) but fails for indoor scenes where categories are scene-specific (e.g., a toilet belongs in a bathroom, not a living room). This limits prior work to outdoor settings and leaves indoor sparse detection unsolved. The paper addresses this by proposing a unified sparse supervised 3D detection framework applicable to both indoor and outdoor environments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw 3D point clouds and, under a one-object-per-scene sparse supervised setting (a single precisely annotated 3D bounding box per scene), outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels. During training, the detector's intermediate proposal features are projected through MLPs and matched against learned class prototypes to generate additional 'prototype labels' for unlabeled objects, while iterative pseudo labeling produces high-confidence box predictions that supplement the sparse ground truth. At inference, the model operates as a standard single-stage or two-stage 3D detector (TR3D for indoor, Voxel-RCNN with CenterHead for outdoor) requiring no prototype computation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a prototype-based object mining module that formulates unlabeled object identification as an optimal transport matching problem: class-aware prototypes (O per class, O=10) are learned by clustering labeled features across all scenes via Sinkhorn-Knopp iteration with momentum updates, then unlabeled features in any scene are assigned 'prototype labels' by combining detector classification scores with feature-prototype affinity (Hadamard product). A multi-label cooperative refinement module then performs iterative self-training where high-quality pseudo labels (filtered by score, IoU, and collision thresholds) are combined with prototype labels to recover missed detections that high-threshold pseudo labeling alone would discard. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: stage 1 trains the detector with sparse labels plus prototype contrastive (Info-NCE) and classification (Focal) losses; stage 2 adds iterative pseudo-label detection loss on top. The key insight is that cross-scene prototype matching bypasses the single-scene category coverage requirement of GT Sampling, enabling the same pipeline to handle both scene-specific indoor categories and the more uniform outdoor category set.

## Results

Under the one-object-per-scene sparse supervised setting, CPDet3D achieves mAP@0.25 of 56.1 and mAP@0.5 of 40.8 on ScanNet V2, and mAP@0.25 of 60.2 and mAP@0.5 of 43.3 on SUN RGB-D, corresponding to approximately 78% and 90% of the fully supervised TR3D performance, respectively. On the outdoor KITTI benchmark, the method reaches Car-3D AP (Moderate) of 82.2 and Car-BEV AP (Moderate) of 91.8, achieving about 96% of the fully supervised Voxel-RCNN baseline. Against the strongest semi-supervised method DQS3D on ScanNet V2, CPDet3D gains +5.4 mAP@0.25 and +1.6 mAP@0.5 with a comparable number of labeled objects (~1200).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanNet V2, CPDet3D outperforms the strongest sparse-supervised baseline SparseDet [26] by +11.3 mAP@0.25 (56.1 vs 46.0) and +12.6 mAP@0.5 (40.8 vs 28.2), and surpasses CoIn [42] by +17.8 mAP@0.25. On SUN RGB-D, the gains over SparseDet are +4.0 mAP@0.25 and +4.5 mAP@0.5. On KITTI, CPDet3D beats CoIn++ [42] (the strongest outdoor sparse baseline) by +2.7 Car-3D AP Moderate (82.2 vs 79.5) and +3.0 Car-BEV AP Moderate (91.8 vs 88.8), while also exceeding SS3D [20] by +3.3 Car-3D AP Moderate. In the cross-paradigm comparison against semi-supervised methods on ScanNet V2 (Table 3), CPDet3D achieves 54.6 mAP@0.25, surpassing DQS3D [7] (49.2) by +5.4 and Diff3DETR [6] (45.1) by +9.5, despite using a sparser annotation protocol (one object per scene vs. 5% of scenes fully labeled). The method consistently wins across all three datasets and both indoor/outdoor settings, with the largest margins observed on ScanNet V2 where scene-specific categories make GT sampling infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends sparse supervised 3D object detection from outdoor-only settings (SS3D, CoIn) to a unified indoor-and-outdoor framework by replacing the GT Sampling strategy—which requires all categories to co-occur in each scene—with a prototype-based object mining module. The prototype clustering mechanism borrows the Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport solver from prototype-based segmentation (ContrastiveSeg, ProtoSeg) and applies it to 3D detection features, while the multi-label cooperative refinement (sparse + pseudo + prototype labels) generalizes the iterative self-training paradigm from semi-supervised 3D detection (DQS3D, SESS). The base detectors (TR3D for indoor, Voxel-RCNN for outdoor) are used as-is, positioning the contribution squarely in the training pipeline rather than the architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving 78–96% of fully supervised performance with only one annotated object per scene dramatically reduces the annotation burden for both indoor robotics and autonomous driving pipelines. A key open question is whether the prototype approach generalizes to truly open-vocabulary or long-tail category distributions, since the current formulation assumes a fixed set of K categories with at least one labeled example per class across the training set. The relatively low precision of prototype labels (71.1% vs. 95.5% for pseudo labels) suggests that the method's upper bound is still constrained by the quality of cross-scene feature matching, and whether this gap narrows with larger datasets or more prototypes remains to be explored.
