## Problem

Existing 3D open-set recognition methods rely on global object features to separate known from unknown classes, treating the entire point cloud uniformly and ignoring that different parts carry vastly different semantic importance for classification. This whole-object perspective limits the model's ability to exploit the locally discriminative geometric structures (e.g., wheels of a car, wings of an airplane) that humans use to recognize objects even from partial views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251611/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw 3D point clouds (uniformly sampled from mesh surfaces or real scans, scaled to a unit sphere) and produces a class label over C known classes plus an unknown class, along with a confidence score computed as the maximum logit from a prototype-based cosine-similarity classifier rather than a standard linear head. The per-point saliency map generated internally by Grad-CAM drives the decomposition, and the model is evaluated with AUROC and FPR95 for unknown identification and ACC/mACC for known-class accuracy.

## Method

SASep decomposes each input point cloud into high-saliency and low-saliency parts via a Tunable Semantic Decomposition (TSD) module that computes per-point Grad-CAM scores and applies tunable thresholds (τ_h, τ_l) to blend semantic and geometric criteria. High-saliency parts are re-fed as augmented instances of the same known class to strengthen intra-class feature learning, while low-saliency parts from multiple objects are rigidly mixed to synthesize pseudo-unknown samples supervised with a label-smoothed target that retains partial credit for the source classes (GSS). A Synth-aided Margin Separation (SMS) module then enforces a weighted triplet loss in feature space, pulling the object feature toward its high-saliency part (or a Gaussian-noise-augmented pseudo-feature) and pushing it away from other-class features, with the negative weight η set much larger than the positive weight ρ to prioritize inter-class separation. The classification head uses learnable class prototypes with cosine similarity instead of a linear layer, and the total loss combines cross-entropy on raw inputs, high-saliency parts, synthetic unknowns, and the margin term.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (ShapeNetCore, DGCNN backbone), SASep achieves 92.5% AUROC and 28.7% FPR95, surpassing the best baseline (SubArcFace at 89.3% AUROC / 48.0% FPR95) by 3.2% and 19.3% respectively. On the more challenging synthetic-to-real benchmark (ModelNet40→ScanObjectNN), SASep reaches 77.9% AUROC and 75.4% FPR95 (DGCNN), improving over the best competitor (SubArcFace, 71.6% / 86.7%) by 6.3% and 11.3%. With the PointNet++ backbone, SASep achieves 92.8% AUROC / 29.1% FPR95 (synthetic) and 84.8% AUROC / 55.0% FPR95 (real-to-real), again leading all baselines across every benchmark and metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SASep outperforms all 13 baselines on every benchmark and metric where a comparison is available. Among discriminative-score methods (MSP, MLS, ODIN, Energy, GradNorm, ReAct), SASep leads by 3–20% in AUROC and 10–48% in FPR95, with the largest gaps on the synthetic benchmark where ReAct (88.4/43.9) is the closest competitor. Reconstruction-based VAE [27] and synthesis-based methods (NF, OE+mixup [22], PointCaM [15]) are consistently weaker, with NF being the strongest synthesis baseline (71.3 AUROC on S2R) yet still trailing SASep by 6.6% AUROC. Feature-embedding approaches (ARPL+CS [4], Cosine proto [10], SupCon [18]) show moderate performance; Cosine proto is the closest competitor on real-to-real (78.0 AUROC DGCNN, 83.6 PointNet++) but is surpassed by 4.0% and 1.2% respectively. Margin-based SubArcFace [7] is the strongest overall baseline (89.3 AUROC synthetic, 71.6 S2R with DGCNN), yet SASep still improves by 3.2% and 6.3% AUROC. Notably, SASep's advantage is most pronounced on the synthetic benchmark (up to 19.3% FPR95 reduction) and the cross-domain synthetic-to-real setting, while the gap narrows on real-to-real where data noise limits saliency-based decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

SASep builds on the 3D open-set recognition task established by the 3DOS benchmark [1] and prior 3D OSR methods that operate at the whole-object level (reconstruction-based [27], pseudo-unknown mixing [2, 22], part-codebook learning [43]). Its key conceptual departure is introducing part-level semantic decomposition via Grad-CAM [34] to split objects into class-relevant and class-irrelevant regions, then routing them to different training objectives. The prototype-based classification head [10] and visibility-based partial-view cropping [29] are repurposed from anomaly detection and point cloud processing respectively, but their combination with saliency-guided decomposition for open-set learning is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3D open-set recognition remains underdeveloped relative to its 2D counterpart, and SASep demonstrates that exploiting intra-object part semantics can substantially improve unknown-class rejection, particularly under cross-domain shifts (synthetic-to-real). The ablation reveals that the TSD module alone accounts for most of the performance gain, suggesting that the core insight—treating object parts asymmetrically based on semantic relevance—is more impactful than the auxiliary synthesis and margin-separation mechanisms. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that Grad-CAM-based saliency estimation degrades under severe corruption (occlusion, noise), leaving open the question of how to robustly decompose objects when geometric structure is heavily damaged.
