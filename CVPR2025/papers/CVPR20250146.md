## Problem

Conventional imaging systems perform brute-force pixel-grid sampling, requiring full sensor arrays and high bandwidth even when only semantic information (identity, attributes, landmarks) is needed. While compressive sensing and single-pixel cameras reduce measurement counts, they still target pixel-level reconstruction and do not exploit the structured, semantically rich latent spaces of generative models. This paper asks whether image information can be encoded directly into a generative model's latent space at extreme compression ratios, enabling both reconstruction and downstream tasks from a handful of scalar measurements.

## Input / Output

The input is a face scene optically modulated by binary DMD masks and integrated by a single-pixel photodiode, yielding d scalar measurements (as few as 4 at 1:16384 compression, versus 65,536 pixels for a 256×256 image). The primary output is a latent tensor L ∈ R^{512×18} aligned to StyleGAN-XL's 18 resolution levels; this can be fed into the frozen generator for 256×256 RGB face reconstruction or linearly projected (via P_A, P_S, P_L) to produce 40 binary attribute labels, 18-class segmentation masks, and 68 2D landmark coordinates without any image reconstruction step.

## Method

LSI co-designs a linear optical encoder O (binary DMD masks, constrained to [0,1] and quantized via a straight-through estimator) with a nonlinear digital encoder D_θ (a multi-level network with linear layers and attention, whose depth increases from coarse to fine to match StyleGAN-XL's 18 resolution levels). Training minimizes an L1 latent-space loss between D_θ(O·I) and a reference encoder E(I) (trained per Richardson et al.), supplemented by ArcFace identity, L2, and DINO/LPIPS perceptual terms, plus an energy-efficiency loss enforcing ≥1% intensity difference between masks. The key insight is that targeting the generative model's latent representation—rather than pixel values—captures domain-specific semantics in a compact, linearly structured space, so that once O and D_θ are frozen, downstream tasks (attribute classification, segmentation, landmark detection) require only lightweight linear projections from L, leveraging the known linear boundaries within GAN latent spaces. A mixer block aggregates the 18-level latent features before feeding them into the frozen StyleGAN-XL generator for reconstruction.

## Results

At a 1:256 compression ratio (256 scalar measurements for a 256×256 image), LSI achieves 90.98% VGGFace and 92.68% DLib face-recognition accuracy with an FID of 26.62, while maintaining 89.15% attribute-classification accuracy, 70.94% segmentation F1, and 1.43 NME for landmarks. Remarkably, only 64 measurements are sufficient for accurate face recognition, and even at an extreme 1:16384 ratio (just 4 measurements), attribute classification retains 81.75% accuracy. Real-hardware experiments on 100 CelebAHQ test images confirm these results, with consistent performance across compression ratios from 1:512 to 1:8192 for downstream tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSI decisively outperforms both baselines at the 1:256 compression ratio. Against Fourier single-pixel imaging with deep learning (FSI-DL [71]), LSI improves VGGFace accuracy from 3.30% to 90.98% and DLib from 13.15% to 92.68%, while reducing FID from 123.5 to 26.62. Against the spatial-attention unfolding network SAUNet [60] with 1-bit quantized sensing matrix (matching LSI's binary mask constraint), LSI achieves 90.98% vs. 17.84% VGGFace and 92.68% vs. 50.12% DLib. Even when SAUNet is retrained with 8-bit quantization (SAUNet†), it reaches only 39.47% VGGFace and 65.97% DLib with FID 65.79, still far below LSI's 90.98% / 92.68% / 26.62. The paper attributes this gap to LSI's latent-space targeting strategy, which preserves identity-critical features (eye shape, facial hair, landmarks) that pixel-level reconstruction methods oversmooth under heavy compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of computational imaging (optics-algorithm co-design), compressive sensing, and GAN latent space manipulation. It builds directly on StyleGAN-XL as the frozen generative decoder and on Richardson et al.'s StyleGAN encoder for ground-truth latent representations, but departs from prior compressive sensing and single-pixel imaging work by targeting the generative model's latent space as the optimization objective rather than pixel-level reconstruction. Unlike generative-prior methods (Jalal et al., Bora et al.) that use iterative optimization, LSI reconstructs in a single forward pass, and unlike minimalist freeform-pixel cameras, it leverages the latent space's linear semantic boundaries to support fine-grained downstream tasks from the same compressed measurements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that 4 scalar measurements (1:16384 compression) still yield 80% facial attribute classification accuracy, and that 64 measurements suffice for face recognition, suggests a fundamentally different trade-off curve between measurement count and task performance compared to pixel-fidelity metrics. The working hardware prototype validates physical feasibility, but the framework is currently locked to a single domain (faces) and a single generative model, inheriting its limitations (failure on hats, glasses, makeup, multi-domain generalization). Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to other domains and generative architectures, what alternative hardware realizations (beyond single-pixel/DMD) are practical, and whether the approach can serve as a privacy-preserving imaging primitive where raw pixels are never captured.
