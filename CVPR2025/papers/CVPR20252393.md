## Problem

All-in-one image restoration requires a single model to handle multiple degradation types simultaneously, but shared parameters cause task interference where gradient update directions for different degradations diverge or oppose each other. Existing prompt-based approaches either add substantial parameter overhead or fail to sufficiently reduce mutual influence between degradation-specific features while preserving the inherent structural information of the image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252393/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a degraded RGB image and a short degradation-type text prompt (e.g., 'haze', 'rain', 'noise') that is encoded into a fixed-length vector via a pre-trained CLIP text encoder. The output is a restored clean RGB image at the same spatial resolution. Training uses 128×128 patches with L1 loss, and the degradation type is provided as a known label at both training and inference time, distinguishing this from blind restoration settings.

## Method

DFPIR builds on a Restormer encoder-decoder backbone and inserts a Degradation-Guided Perturbation Block (DGPB) at the skip-connection stage between encoder and decoder. The DGPB contains two submodules: (1) DGCPM expands the feature channels 2×, uses the CLIP-encoded degradation prompt to compute a top-K channel permutation via an MLP, shuffles channels in this higher-dimensional space, then halves channels back—preserving all channel information while reordering them differently per degradation type; (2) CAAPM performs cross-attention where the shuffled features serve as queries and the original features as keys/values, with a top-K masking matrix (γ=0.9) that zeros out a fraction of attention entries to further suppress degradation-specific interference. This two-stage perturbation strategy balances preserving inherent image structure (channel shuffle loses no information) with actively reducing cross-task gradient conflicts (attention masking discards interfering attention paths).

## Results

On the five-task all-in-one setting (dehazing, deraining, denoising, deblurring, low-light enhancement), DFPIR achieves an average PSNR of 30.64 dB and SSIM of 0.913, surpassing the previous best all-in-one method InstructIR by 1.09 dB. On the three-task setting, DFPIR reaches 32.88 dB average PSNR, a 0.45 dB gain over InstructIR and 0.82 dB over PromptIR. The largest per-task gains are on dehazing (SOTS: 31.64 dB vs. 27.10 dB for InstructIR) and deraining (Rain100L: 37.62 dB vs. 36.84 dB). Compared to general-purpose restorers trained in the same all-in-one setting, DFPIR outperforms Restormer by 3.04 dB and NAFNet by 2.88 dB in average PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFPIR outperforms all 13 baselines across every task in both the three-task and five-task settings. Against the closest all-in-one competitor InstructIR [8], DFPIR gains 0.45 dB (three-task) and 1.09 dB (five-task) in average PSNR, with the largest per-task margin on dehazing (+4.54 dB on SOTS in the five-task setting). PromptIR [39] is surpassed by 0.82 dB on average in the three-task setting, while IDR [59] trails by 2.30 dB in the five-task average. Among general-purpose restorers trained in the same all-in-one protocol, Restormer [57] and NAFNet [3] are outperformed by 3.04 dB and 2.88 dB respectively, confirming that the degradation-guided perturbation strategy is critical for multi-task performance. Older all-in-one methods (DL [11], AirNet [21], TAPE [28], Transweather [46]) and general restorers (SwinIR [25], DGUNet [33]) are all surpassed by 2–10 dB depending on the task, with the largest gaps on dehazing and low-light enhancement where task interference is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFPIR positions itself within the all-in-one image restoration literature, specifically in the feature-space modulation paradigm shared by PromptIR, InstructIR, and MedIR, but departs from their reliance on channel attention or expert routing. Instead of learning soft attention weights or hard-routing to task-specific experts, DFPIR introduces a 'perturbation' framing: degradation-guided channel shuffling in an expanded high-dimensional space (DGCPM) combined with top-K attention masking (CAAPM), both guided by CLIP-encoded text prompts. The backbone is Restormer, and the overall encoder-decoder with skip connections follows standard practice, with the DGPB inserted at the skip-connection stage as the sole architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized pain point in multi-task restoration—gradient conflict and feature interference between degradation types—without inflating model size, making it a practical alternative to parameter-space approaches like AirNet or IDR. The finding that simple channel reordering outperforms learned channel attention for cross-degradation disentanglement is a useful design insight for future multi-task architectures. However, the method still requires the degradation type to be known at inference (via text prompt), limiting its applicability to truly unknown-corruption scenarios, and the evaluation is confined to five standard benchmarks, leaving open whether the perturbation strategy generalizes to more diverse or compound degradations.
