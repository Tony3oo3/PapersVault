## Problem

Existing multimodal generative models fail as practical design copilots because they (1) cannot maintain instance identity consistency with user-provided references while allowing natural variations, and (2) produce nearly identical outputs from the same prompt due to deterministic regression of continuous visual features without an effective sampling mechanism. Additionally, current visual generation tools require users to manually orchestrate multiple task-specific models, creating a steep learning curve that limits accessibility for general users.

## Input / Output

WeGen accepts inputs through a natural conversation interface in an interleaved text-visual token format: text prompts, reference images (encoded as 64 CLIP visual features), condition images (canny edges, depth maps, pose skeletons), and images to edit or restore. The LLM processes these alternating tokens and generates multi-modal output embeddings that the SDXL decoder converts into final images. During training, bounding boxes and segmentation images in the structured instruction format are randomly dropped with 0.3 probability to enhance robustness, and the model learns to generate the target frame conditioned on the structured context.

## Method

WeGen unifies EVA-CLIP (visual encoder), LLaMA-2-7B-chat (LLM), and SDXL (diffusion decoder) in a two-stage training pipeline: first fine-tuning SDXL to reconstruct images from CLIP features, then fine-tuning the LLM on interleaved visual-text instruction data with all tasks reformulated into a single format. The key innovation for identity consistency is the DIIC data pipeline, which extracts training pairs from video sequences using InternVL2 for captioning, Grounding DINO for bounding box detection, and SAM2 for instance tracking across frames, with a temporal interval t_ref controlling the degree of natural variation while preserving core identity. For generation diversity, the PSR mechanism leverages the MLLM to autoregressively rewrite brief prompts into detailed descriptions using Top-P (p=0.9) and temperature (t=0.8) sampling, introducing controlled randomness through discrete text token sampling before the relatively deterministic visual feature generation step.

## Results

On COCO2014 text-to-image generation, WeGen achieves FID 9.39 and CLIP-T 0.308, setting a new SOTA among MLLM-based approaches while using only 7B parameters and 20K training iterations. On DreamBench subject-driven generation, WeGen attains DINO 0.823, CLIP-I 0.882, and CLIP-T 0.302, outperforming all prior methods on both identity preservation and text alignment. For generation diversity, WeGen achieves PSNR_d of 9.66 and LPIPS_d of 0.6286, substantially exceeding SEED-X (PSNR_d 15.78) and Emu2-Gen (PSNR_d 18.34), confirming that PSR enables meaningfully diverse outputs from a single prompt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On text-to-image generation (COCO2014), WeGen (FID 9.39, CLIP-T 0.308) outperforms all MLLM-based baselines: Emu2-Gen (CLIP-T 0.297, 33B params), Emu (FID 11.66, CLIP-T 0.286), Kosmos-G (FID 10.99), GILL (FID 12.20), and SEED-X (FID 12.68), while using fewer parameters (7B) and far less compute (20K iterations vs. 300K–2.5M). WeGen is competitive with pure diffusion models like SD v1.5 (FID 9.93, CLIP-T 0.302) and SDXL (CLIP-T 0.310), though it trails the strongest diffusion-only methods (EDiff-I FID 6.95, Imagen FID 7.27) on FID. On subject-driven generation (DreamBench), WeGen leads all baselines on identity metrics—DINO 0.823 vs. OmniGen 0.801, Emu2-Gen 0.766, SEED-X 0.702—and CLIP-I 0.882 vs. Emu2-Gen 0.850, Kosmos-G 0.847, while matching or exceeding their CLIP-T scores. On generation diversity, WeGen's PSR mechanism yields PSNR_d of 9.66 and LPIPS_d of 0.6286, dramatically more diverse than SEED-X (PSNR_d 15.78, LPIPS_d 0.2292) and Emu2-Gen (PSNR_d 18.34, LPIPS_d 0.2104), confirming that prior methods produce near-identical outputs across seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeGen extends the MLLM-plus-diffusion architecture established by GILL, Kosmos-G, and Emu2, which all compose a CLIP encoder, an LLM, and a diffusion decoder. It departs from these in two key ways: (1) replacing single-image training pairs (which encourage copy-paste behavior) with a video-based DIIC pipeline that captures natural instance variation while preserving identity, and (2) introducing Prompt Self-Rewriting to inject diversity through autoregressive text token sampling before visual feature regression, rather than relying on deterministic continuous-feature mapping. The paper also positions itself against SEED-X's approach of concatenating reference images directly as latent maps, arguing that this limits generative flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DIIC data pipeline and PSR mechanism are both practical, reusable components: DIIC provides a general recipe for constructing identity-consistent training data from any video corpus, and PSR is explicitly plug-and-play for other MLLM+diffusion systems. The unified conversational interface lowers the barrier for non-expert users, though the paper's 'design copilot' framing is somewhat aspirational given the limited multi-turn interaction demonstrated. Open questions include whether this approach extends to video generation, how well the DIIC pipeline generalizes to rare or abstract subjects, and whether the 7B LLM backbone is sufficient for truly complex multi-step design workflows.
