## Problem

Current hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures for biomedical image segmentation suffer from a fundamental design contradiction: they force transformer layers to extract global features from local inputs and convolutional layers to extract local details from global inputs, causing alternating and confusing transmission of feature types that leads to training instability and performance degradation. Additionally, the field lacks a unified evaluation benchmark, with significant discrepancies in preprocessing, data splits, and hyperparameters across studies, making fair architectural comparison nearly impossible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251318/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 2D biomedical images (fundus, dermoscopic, colonoscopic, electron microscopy) and 3D volumetric scans (CTA, CT, MRI) of varying sizes and modalities, processing them through Overlapped Patch Embedding (3×3 convolutions with stride 1 and down-sampling) rather than standard non-overlapping patch tokenization. It produces pixel-wise (or voxel-wise) segmentation masks evaluated via Jaccard, Dice, 95HD, and ASD metrics under a standardized nnUNet protocol with five-fold cross-validation, without any post-processing of model outputs.

## Method

WNet consists of two cascaded encoder-decoder architectures where Local Scope Blocks (LSBs, implemented as 3×3 residual blocks) handle multi-scale encoding and decoding to capture local details, while Global Scope Bridges (GSBs, implemented as transformers with pooling-based global attention) are inserted between the encoder and decoder at each scale to capture long-range dependencies. The key architectural insight is that global and local features flow continuously through the network and are fused via channel concatenation at every scale, avoiding the alternating generation pattern that plagues prior hybrid designs. No explicit positional encoding is added to GSBs because the convolutional LSBs already encode implicit spatial relationships and exchange information with the transformer bridges. The architecture is integrated into the nnUNet framework as nnWNet, which automatically configures preprocessing, augmentation, and training hyperparameters, enabling a unified and fair benchmark across eight 2D and 3D datasets.

## Results

nnWNet achieves state-of-the-art performance across all eight datasets. On 2D benchmarks, it attains Jaccard scores of 69.86 (DRIVE), 82.62 (ISIC-2017), 84.58 (Kvasir-SEG), and 79.86 (CREMI), with Dice coefficients of 82.18, 89.44, 89.98, and 88.43 respectively. On 3D benchmarks, it reaches Jaccard of 73.71 (Parse2022), 78.93 (AMOS22), 74.76 (BTCV), and 71.58 (ImageCAS), with Dice of 84.70, 86.39, 83.70, and 83.25. Notably, nnWNet uses only 7M parameters on 2D tasks (vs. 92M for nnUNet and 102M for BCUNet) and 56M on 3D tasks (vs. 88M for nnUNet), while also achieving the best boundary metrics (95HD and ASD) on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

nnWNet outperforms all pure convolutional baselines (BCUNet, CMUNet, GFANet, U2Net, nnUNet) on Jaccard and Dice across all four 2D datasets, with the largest margin on CREMI (+2.29 Jaccard over nnUNet) and Kvasir-SEG (+0.66 over nnUNet). Among hybrid models, nnWNet surpasses TransAttUNet on ISIC-2017 (+0.84 Jaccard), Kvasir-SEG (+1.45), and CREMI (+0.15), though TransAttUNet narrowly edges it on DRIVE Jaccard (69.93 vs. 69.86). Pure transformer SwinUNet underperforms all convolutional baselines on DRIVE (64.25 vs. 68.70 Jaccard for nnUNet), confirming the paper's argument that ignoring local details is detrimental. On 3D datasets, nnWNet (56M params) beats nnUNet (88M) on all four benchmarks, with the largest gains on AMOS22 (+0.81 Jaccard) and BTCV (+1.66 Jaccard). Several hybrid 3D models show severe degradation under the unified benchmark: UNETR drops to 56.34 Jaccard on AMOS22 and SegFormer3D to 64.49, both far below nnUNet, supporting the paper's claim that contradictory feature transmission causes training instability. Statistically, nnWNet achieves significant improvement (p<0.05) in 28 out of 32 t-tests against compared models on the Jaccard metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on U-Net's encoder-decoder architecture and the nnU-Net auto-configuring framework, proposing WNet as a minimal modification that inserts transformer-based Global Scope Bridges between convolutional Local Scope Blocks at each scale. It positions itself against a taxonomy of existing hybrid CNN-Transformer designs (alternating stacks, Conv-encoder/Trans-decoder, bridge/bottleneck insertions) and argues that all of them suffer from a fundamental contradiction: forcing transformers to extract global features from local inputs and convolutions to extract local details from global inputs, disrupting continuous feature flow. The paper departs from prior work not by introducing new components but by reorganizing how existing ones (residual blocks, pooling-based attention) are arranged to maintain parallel, uninterrupted streams of local and global features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The call for a unified evaluation benchmark is arguably as important as the architecture itself: under nnU-Net's standardized protocol, several models claiming SOTA (ConvFormer, nnFormer, D-Former) fail to converge or degrade significantly, and some hybrid models underperform plain UNet, challenging the field's assumption that adding transformers is always beneficial. The architectural critique—that alternating local/global feature generation causes training instability—offers a useful design principle, though it is supported primarily by empirical ablations rather than formal analysis. Open questions include whether the continuous-transmission principle generalizes to other modalities (e.g., histopathology, ultrasound) and whether the modest 1–2% Jaccard gains over nnU-Net persist at larger data scales or with stronger pre-training.
