## Problem

Adversarial training under long-tailed distributions suffers from robustness overfitting: while clean accuracy improves, adversarial robustness does not scale proportionally and can even decline, leaving tail and weak classes particularly vulnerable. Existing methods like AT-BSL rely on fixed sample-count-based reweighting that fails to identify genuinely weak classes, and the field lacks a balanced evaluation metric that captures per-class defense capability under attack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250436/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10-LT/CIFAR-100-LT, variable-resolution dermatoscopic images for DermaMNIST) and outputs a single class label per image. Adversarial perturbations are generated within an ℓ∞ ball of radius 8/255, and evaluation is reported under FGSM, PGD-20, PGD-100, CW-100, and AutoAttack. The key output distinction is the use of Balanced Accuracy and the newly proposed Balanced Robustness metric (per-class recall averaged over all classes) rather than standard top-1 accuracy.

## Method

TAET is a two-stage training framework. Stage 1 (Initial Stabilization) trains with standard cross-entropy loss for ~40 epochs to establish clean accuracy and stable gradients before adversarial pressure is introduced, mitigating robust overfitting. Stage 2 (HARL) performs multi-step PGD adversarial training where the loss is a Hierarchical Equalization Loss (HEL) combining three components: Balanced Cross-Class Loss (BCL) that averages per-class losses, Hierarchical Deviation Loss (HDL) that penalizes variance of class losses around the mean, and Rare Class Emphasis Loss (RCEL) that squares normalized per-class loss contributions to amplify rare-class gradients. Unlike AT-BSL's fixed sample-count reweighting, HARL dynamically identifies weak classes by their mean training loss, enabling targeted compensation for both tail classes and non-tail weak classes (e.g., Class 3 in CIFAR-10-LT). The framework is evaluated with ResNet-18 and ViT-B/16 across imbalance ratios of 10–100.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (IR=10) with ResNet-18, TAET achieves the highest balanced accuracy (74.67%) and balanced robustness across all attack types, including 35.15% under PGD-20, 34.29% under PGD-100, 57.59% under CW, and 30.57% under AutoAttack. Compared to the closest long-tail-specific baseline AT-BSL, TAET improves PGD-20 robustness by 8.29 points and CW robustness by 41.92 points. On the real-world DermaMNIST dataset (IR=58.66), TAET achieves 48.42% clean balanced accuracy and 29.26% PGD-20 robustness, outperforming AT-BSL (48.55% clean, 27.85% PGD-20) and all other baselines on attacked metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAET outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric (clean balanced accuracy, FGSM, PGD-20, PGD-100, CW, and AutoAttack) on CIFAR-10-LT. The largest margin is against AT-BSL [45], the most directly comparable long-tail adversarial method: TAET gains +8.29 points on PGD-20 (35.15 vs. 26.86) and +41.92 points on CW (57.59 vs. 15.67), while also improving clean accuracy by +1.93 points. Against REAT [21], which achieves the second-best clean accuracy (74.56%), TAET leads by +11.13 on PGD-20 and +46.52 on CW, indicating that REAT's high clean accuracy does not translate to robustness. General adversarial training methods (AT [26], TRADES [46], MART [38], ADT [12], HE [30], GAIRAT [47]) all trail TAET by 5–16 points in clean accuracy and 5–10 points in PGD-20 robustness, confirming that standard AT does not address long-tail imbalance. On DermaMNIST (IR=58.66), TAET edges out AT-BSL on all attacked metrics (e.g., 29.26 vs. 27.85 on PGD-20) while matching its clean accuracy (48.42 vs. 48.55), and substantially outperforms ADT, MART, and TRADES by 10–18 points under attack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the AT-BSL line (Yue et al., CVPR 2024) and its predecessor RoBal (Wu et al., CVPR 2021), which established adversarial training under long-tailed distributions using sample-count-based logit reweighting. The key departure is replacing Balanced Softmax Loss with a Hierarchical Equalization Loss (repurposed from dense detection and instance segmentation literature) that identifies weak classes by mean training loss rather than sample count, combined with a two-stage schedule that uses cross-entropy before adversarial training to combat robust overfitting. The adversarial sample generation follows the standard PGD paradigm from Madry et al. (ICLR 2018), and the overall framework is positioned as a targeted fix for two diagnosed failures of AT-BSL: poor tail-class robustness and mid-training robustness collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Balanced Robustness as a per-class recall-averaged metric under attack fills a genuine gap in long-tail robustness evaluation, where prior work relied on aggregate accuracy that masks tail-class vulnerability. The two-stage CE-then-adversarial schedule is a practical, low-cost strategy that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes. However, the experimental scope is narrow (primarily CIFAR-10-LT with ResNet-18 and one medical dataset), the gains on extremely rare classes remain modest, and the three-component loss introduces hyperparameter sensitivity that limits straightforward adoption. Open questions include whether the balanced robustness metric gains community traction, whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale long-tail data, and whether the HEL repurposing generalizes beyond the specific loss decomposition used here.
