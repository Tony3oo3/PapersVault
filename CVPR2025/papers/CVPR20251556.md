## Problem

Generating 3D articulated objects requires simultaneously producing high-quality geometry for each sub-part and accurate kinematic relations between them, a dual challenge that existing methods handle poorly. Prior approaches like NAP, CAGE, and SINGAPO are limited to predefined graph structures, and CAGE/SINGAPO retrieve shapes from static datasets rather than generating novel geometry, restricting diversity. The paper addresses this flexibility-quality tradeoff by enabling truly generative, diverse articulated object synthesis from text or image conditions.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a free-form text description (encoded by a pre-trained T5 text encoder) or a single RGB image (encoded by BLIP-2) as conditioning, and outputs a complete 3D articulated object mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from an SDF, along with per-part kinematic parameters (joint axis, motion limits, bounding box). Each articulated object is structured as a tree where every node carries a 785-dimensional token (bounding box, 768-dim geometry latent, joint axis, limits, parent index), and the number of parts is not fixed a priori. Training uses 6 categories from PartNet-Mobility with text descriptions auto-generated by ChatGPT-4o from Blender renders.

## Method

ArtFormer parameterizes each articulated object as a tree of tokens and uses a transformer with a novel tree position embedding (GRU-based path encoding from root) to autoregressively generate the tree via iterative decoding, where each round predicts children for all existing nodes. Rather than generating geometry directly, the transformer outputs a compact latent code that is decoded by a learned SDF shape prior (a VAE with tri-plane features plus a conditional diffusion model), decoupling geometry quality from kinematic prediction. Diversity is introduced through Gumbel-Softmax sampling over four codebooks that discretize the geometry condition space, expanding the effective latent capacity from 4N to N⁴. Conditioning is injected via cross-attention layers, and the shape prior is trained first on PartNet/PartNet-Mobility before the transformer is trained with combined BCE (terminal tokens), MSE (kinematic attributes), and KL (codebook logits) losses.

## Results

On PartNet-Mobility (6 categories), ArtFormer (direct generation) achieves POR 0.709×10⁻², MMD 0.0292, COV 0.5213, and 1-NNA 0.5266, outperforming NAP-768 on all four metrics. In the human study, ArtFormer attains an alignment score of 0.459 and diversity score of 0.67, substantially exceeding CAGE's 0.343 and 0.07. With part retrieval (Ours-PR), the method further improves to COV 0.6400 and 1-NNA 0.3950, surpassing CAGE on both distribution-level metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NAP (both 128-d and 768-d variants), ArtFormer wins on every metric: it reduces POR from 1.620 to 0.709 (×10⁻²), lowers MMD from 0.0632 to 0.0292, raises COV from 0.3723 to 0.5213, and cuts 1-NNA from 0.6543 to 0.5266, indicating substantially better geometry quality and kinematic accuracy. Against CAGE, the comparison is mixed: CAGE achieves a lower MMD (0.0193 vs. 0.0292) and POR (0.251 vs. 0.709), reflecting its advantage in object-level reconstruction via dataset retrieval, but ArtFormer surpasses CAGE on COV (0.5213 vs. 0.6064) and decisively on the human study (AL 0.459 vs. 0.343; DS 0.67 vs. 0.07), demonstrating superior diversity and text alignment. When part retrieval is added (Ours-PR), the gap narrows further: Ours-PR beats CAGE on COV (0.6400 vs. 0.6064) and 1-NNA (0.3950 vs. 0.5319) while remaining competitive on MMD (0.0214 vs. 0.0193), confirming that the articulation transformer's structural predictions are the primary differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Diffusion-SDF shape prior for high-quality SDF geometry decoding and on tree-structured positional encodings for transformers, combining them into a unified framework for articulated object generation. It departs from prior articulated-object generators (NAP, CAGE, SINGAPO) that either rely on predefined graph structures or retrieve sub-part shapes from static datasets, by parameterizing objects as variable-depth trees of tokens and generating geometry through a learned diffusion-based SDF prior rather than retrieval. The iterative decoding scheme and Gumbel-Softmax codebook sampling for diverse geometry conditions represent the main architectural departures from the DDPM-based approaches in NAP and CAGE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in robot learning and simulation: generating diverse, physically plausible articulated objects from natural language without being constrained to a fixed set of dataset shapes. However, evaluation is limited to only 6 object categories in PartNet-Mobility, and the authors acknowledge that geometry quality degrades with more categories due to SDF generalization limits. Open questions include scaling to objects with many parts (>10), incorporating quantitative joint parameters (e.g., specific rotation angles) from text, and extending to additional conditioning modalities such as point clouds or partial joint structures.
