## Problem

Existing MLLM pre-training tasks for document understanding (text transcription, visual text grounding) predominantly emphasize semantic alignment while only implicitly capturing the spatial location of text within images. Without explicit spatially-aware supervision, models may over-rely on the LLM's semantic context rather than optimizing visual encoder features, leading to hallucinations when parsing dense or small visual texts in high-resolution documents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250631/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-resolution document image (dynamically cropped into up to 6 non-overlapping sub-images at 448×448 resolution) and a text question as input, producing a free-form text answer. During training, a binary mask at the original image resolution is additionally generated by the Mask Generator Module and supervised against ground-truth masks, but this mask output is entirely discarded at inference, adding zero inference cost. Visual tokens are compressed to 256 via local 4×4 window pixel shuffle (inspired by Swin Transformer) to preserve spatial structure.

## Method

Marten introduces VQA-Mask, a joint pre-training objective that simultaneously optimizes VQA-based text parsing (cross-entropy on answer tokens) and mask generation. The mask generation is handled by a Mask Generator Module (MGM) inserted at an intermediate LLM layer, which computes cross-attention between visual tokens (query) and concatenated question+answer tokens (key), then applies transposed convolutions to upsample the attention maps back to full image resolution, supervised by Dice + Cross-Entropy loss. Ground-truth masks are generated automatically via a three-stage pipeline: PaddleOCR detects text regions, K-means clustering on each cropped text instance separates foreground from background (with center-distance heuristic and edge-calibration), and masks are reassembled to full-image resolution. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 (VQA-Mask alignment) freezes the LLM and trains the visual encoder, MLP connector, and MGM on 6M image-mask pairs (MTMask6M); Stage 2 discards the MGM and performs full-parameter supervised fine-tuning on 3.3M document VQA samples. The local window pixel shuffle (4×4) preserves spatial structure of visual tokens, enabling the MGM to produce spatially coherent attention maps.

## Results

Marten (8.1B) achieves 92.0 on DocVQA, 75.2 on InfoVQA, 75.1 on DeepForm, 39.5 on KLC, 52.4 on WTQ, 84.4 on TabFact, 44.4 on FUNSD, and 80.4 on SROIE, leading among 8B-class MLLMs on 8 of 11 text-rich benchmarks. On OCRBench, Marten scores 820, surpassing MiniMonkey (802) and InternVL2 (794) by 18 and 26 points respectively. The paper reports average improvements of 1.97% on document benchmarks, 5.09% on table benchmarks, and 3.73% on key information extraction benchmarks over the best prior methods per category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Marten outperforms InternVL2 (8.1B) on DocVQA (92.0 vs 91.6) and InfoVQA (75.2 vs 74.8) but trails on ChartQA (81.7 vs 83.3) and TextVQA (74.4 vs 77.4), which the authors attribute to InternVL2's training on hundreds of millions of samples. Against TextHawk2 (7.4B), Marten leads on DocVQA (+2.4), InfoVQA (+7.4), WTQ (+6.2), and TabFact (+6.3), while TextHawk2 retains a slight edge on TextVQA (75.1 vs 74.4). Compared to MM1.5 (7.3B), Marten gains +3.9 on DocVQA, +15.7 on InfoVQA, and +8.5 on TabFact, though MM1.5 edges ahead on TextVQA (76.8 vs 74.4). Against the much smaller Mini-Monkey (2B), Marten achieves substantial gains on DocVQA (+4.6), InfoVQA (+15.1), WTQ (+52.4 vs –), TabFact (+84.4 vs –), FUNSD (+1.5), and SROIE (+10.1), while Mini-Monkey slightly leads on POIE (69.9 vs 69.5). On OCRBench, Marten (820) surpasses all compared models, with the closest competitor MiniMonkey at 802 and InternVL2 at 794.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQA-based pre-training paradigm for document MLLMs (UReader's Read Full Text, Park et al.'s RPT/PTP, KOSMOS-2.5's Visual Text Grounding, mPLUG-DocOWL's struct-aware parsing) by adding an explicit spatially-aware mask generation auxiliary task. Prior methods rely on semantic alignment alone, implicitly hoping the LLM's context window captures spatial structure; here, a Mask Generator Module (MGM) inserted at an intermediate LLM layer uses cross-attention between visual and language tokens to produce pixel-level masks supervised by a clustering-based binarization pipeline (inspired by CCD). The local-window pixel shuffle (borrowed from Swin Transformer) replaces the global shuffle used in InternVL, preserving spatial structure needed for the mask task. The approach is orthogonal to and compatible with existing visual encoders (InternViT, Swin) and LLMs (InternLM2, Vicuna), as validated by ablations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated failure mode—hallucination in document MLLMs caused by insufficient spatial grounding—and shows that a training-only auxiliary task (discarded at inference, zero extra cost) can consistently improve performance across 8+ benchmarks. The released MTMask6M dataset (6M image-mask pairs) and the open-source code lower the barrier for others to adopt spatially-aware pre-training. Limitations remain: Marten still trails InternVL2 on ChartQA and TextVQA, suggesting the mask-based spatial supervision is most effective for text-dense document layouts rather than charts or natural scenes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video documents, multi-page reasoning, or finer-grained layout understanding beyond binary text masks.
