## Problem

Generating 3D scenes from a single image remains ill-posed due to missing geometric cues and occluded regions. Regression-based methods (e.g., Flash3D) produce geometric distortions and fail to recover unseen areas, while generative video diffusion models (e.g., CogVideoX, ViewCrafter) introduce scene inconsistency by altering existing components during enhancement. The key unresolved challenge is balancing the generative prior needed for unseen regions with the scene consistency required to preserve known content across iterative reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a predefined camera trajectory (a sequence of N=25 camera poses per step, with n=10 overlapping frames between consecutive steps) as input. The image is first processed by a frozen UniDepth network for metric depth estimation and then by a Flash3D encoder-decoder to initialize per-pixel Gaussian parameters. The output is a global 3D Gaussian scene representation (positions, covariance matrices, opacities, and spherical harmonic coefficients) that can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints, iteratively refined over h steps along the trajectory.

## Method

Scene Splatter introduces a cascaded momentum paradigm for video diffusion-based scene generation. At the latent level, noisy samples constructed from original latent features are injected at each denoising step with a spatially-varying coefficient λ computed via maximum cosine similarity between each latent feature and a reference pool (the input image latent plus the first n well-generated frame latents), preserving existing components while enhancing details. At the pixel level, the consistent video produced by latent-level momentum (Φ_λ) is blended with a vanilla diffusion output (Φ_0) using a pixel-wise coefficient μ derived from rendered Gaussian scale maps—regions with small Gaussian volumes (well-reconstructed) receive higher weight from Φ_λ, while unseen regions rely more on Φ_0's full generative capacity. The enhanced frames then supervise 3DGS optimization (L1 + SSIM loss, 5000 steps per iteration), and the process repeats along the camera trajectory to iteratively recover the full scene, circumventing the inherent video-length limitation of diffusion models.

## Results

On the RealEstate10K evaluation, Scene Splatter achieves 20.95 PSNR / 0.800 SSIM / 0.145 LPIPS on the Easy set and 17.62 PSNR / 0.707 SSIM / 0.233 LPIPS on the Hard set, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The advantage is most pronounced on the Hard set (larger view ranges), where the method gains +1.99 dB PSNR and −0.025 LPIPS over the strongest baseline (ViewCrafter). Notably, directly applying CogVideoX degrades PSNR below the regression-based Flash3D (17.25 vs. 17.94 on Easy) and inflates LPIPS to 0.352, confirming that uncontrolled generative changes harm reconstruction fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scene Splatter outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both Easy and Hard evaluation sets. Against the regression-based Flash3D, the gains are substantial: +3.01 dB PSNR and +0.111 SSIM on Easy, and +3.21 dB PSNR and +0.108 SSIM on Hard, reflecting Flash3D's inability to recover unseen regions without generative priors. Against CogVideoX (5B-parameter video diffusion used without scene-consistency constraints), Scene Splatter improves PSNR by +3.70 dB (Easy) and +2.20 dB (Hard) while reducing LPIPS from 0.352 to 0.145, demonstrating that unguided video generation introduces severe scene inconsistency that actively degrades 3D reconstruction. The closest competitor is ViewCrafter, which also uses a video diffusion model with camera conditioning; Scene Splatter edges ahead by +0.25 dB PSNR on Easy but pulls significantly further on Hard (+1.99 dB PSNR, −0.025 LPIPS), indicating that the cascaded momentum strategy becomes increasingly critical as viewpoint displacement grows and accumulated inconsistency would otherwise dominate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting for scene representation, video diffusion models for novel view synthesis, and feed-forward Gaussian prediction (Flash3D) for initialization. It departs from prior generative approaches (CogVideoX, ViewCrafter) that directly enhance rendered videos but alter existing scene components, and from regression-based methods (Flash3D) that cannot recover unseen regions. The key conceptual contribution is reframing 3D scene consistency as temporal consistency within video generation, then introducing a two-level momentum mechanism—latent-level to anchor known regions and pixel-level (scale-map-weighted) to preserve generative freedom in unknown areas—that prior video-diffusion-based 3D methods did not employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cascaded momentum paradigm offers a principled way to balance the generative prior of large video diffusion models against the hard constraint of scene consistency, a tension that has limited prior single-image 3D reconstruction. The iterative reconstruction loop elegantly circumvents the fixed video-length limitation of diffusion models, enabling arbitrarily long camera trajectories. However, the method is computationally expensive (video diffusion per iteration plus 5000-step 3DGS optimization), restricted to static scenes, and evaluated only on a RealEstate10K subset, leaving open questions about scalability to outdoor/4D scenes and whether the momentum formulation generalizes beyond indoor environments.
