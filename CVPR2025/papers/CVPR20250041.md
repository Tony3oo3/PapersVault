## Problem

Existing 2D-to-3D human pose estimation methods address occlusion by enriching the lifting stage with temporal or visual cues, but overlook the fundamental limitation of the sparse 2D skeleton input (e.g., only 17 joints), which inherently restricts local context exploitation and worsens occluded joint prediction. This paper argues that a hierarchical dense 2D skeleton representation is essential for robust occluded 2D-to-3D lifting, yet acquiring such dense poses in real-world scenarios remains unsolved since 3D ground truth meshes are unavailable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250041/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse 2D pose of 17 joints (detected by off-the-shelf detectors like CPN or Stacked Hourglass) as input and generates hierarchical dense 2D poses at two levels—48 joints and 96 joints—via autoregressive token generation. These generated dense poses are then concatenated with the original sparse pose and fed into a vanilla 12-block spatial transformer for 2D-to-3D lifting, producing the final 3D joint positions. The dense poses are derived by coarsening the 3D ground truth mesh (6890 vertices → 96 → 48) and projecting to 2D pixel space, serving as training targets.

## Method

HiPART is a two-stage generative densification pipeline. Stage 1 (Multi-Scale Skeletal Tokenization) uses a VQ-VAE-2-style architecture with MLP-Mixer encoders/decoders to progressively quantize a highly dense 2D pose into hierarchical discrete tokens (16 sparse + 48 dense), augmented by a Skeleton-aware Alignment strategy that enforces part-wise local consistency via Info-NCE loss and action-wise global semantic alignment via cross-entropy. Stage 2 (Hierarchical AutoRegressive Modeling) generates these tokens conditioned on the sparse 2D pose using a pose-aware transformer with Local Self-Attention Blocks (within-part), Global Causal Self-Attention Blocks (cross-part), and a hierarchical Prediction Head. Unlike standard next-token prediction, the autoregression follows two novel strategies: center-to-periphery (generating joints from root outward to reduce depth uncertainty) and sparse-to-dense (generating coarse sparse tokens first, then r dense tokens in parallel per part), reducing generation from 1+r steps to 2 steps per joint and improving both accuracy and inference speed.

## Results

On 3DPW, HiPART achieves 77.2 mm MPJPE and 48.8 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming the previous best Di²Pose (79.3 / 50.1) by 2.1 mm and 1.3 mm respectively. On the occlusion-focused 3DPW-AdvOcc@80 benchmark, HiPART records 143.2 mm MPJPE and 72.1 mm PA-MPJPE, the best in both metrics, with a relative performance drop of only 77% (MPJPE) and 44% (PA-MPJPE) versus the average 120% and 67% drop of other methods. On Human3.6M with CPN-detected 2D poses, HiPART achieves 42.0 mm average MPJPE in a single-frame setting, surpassing temporal methods like MixSTE (42.4 mm, f=81) and PoseFormer (44.3 mm, f=81) while using only 2.4 M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiPART outperforms all single-frame baselines on Human3.6M with CPN-detected 2D poses, achieving 42.0 mm average MPJPE versus 49.2 mm for Di²Pose, 49.7 mm for DiffPose, and 51.0 mm for Lifting by Image. It also surpasses multi-frame temporal methods (PoseFormer 44.3 mm, MixSTE 42.4 mm, MHFormer 43.0 mm) despite using only a single frame and 2.4 M parameters, compared to 9.7–33.7 M for those methods. On 3DPW and 3DPW-Occ, HiPART sets new SOTA in both MPJPE and PA-MPJPE, beating Di²Pose by 2.1 mm and 1.3 mm respectively. The one notable exception is 3DPW-AdvOcc@40, where Di²Pose achieves a lower MPJPE (108.4 vs. 124.6 mm), though HiPART still leads in PA-MPJPE (64.3 vs. 59.8 mm is actually Di²Pose winning here too). On the more severe AdvOcc@80, HiPART clearly dominates with 143.2 mm MPJPE and 72.1 mm PA-MPJPE versus Di²Pose's 153.6 and 78.7. The paper further shows that HiPART is orthogonal to temporal lifting: combining it with MixSTE reduces MPJPE from 42.4 to 39.3 mm, and with PoseFormer from 44.3 to 40.8 mm.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiPART builds on the hierarchical multi-scale quantization architecture of VQ-VAE-2, adapting it from image generation to skeletal data via MLP-Mixer encoders/decoders, and extends the two-stage codebook tokenization paradigm of PCT by replacing its classification-based Stage 2 with autoregressive modeling. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant line of work that enriches the 2D-to-3D lifting stage with temporal context (VideoPose, PoseFormer, MHFormer) or visual cues (SemGCN, Lifting by Image), arguing instead that the sparse 17-joint 2D skeleton input is the fundamental bottleneck. It also contrasts with diffusion-based pose generators (DiffPose, Di²Pose) by using a faster posed-based autoregressive scheme with skeleton-specific generation orders (center-to-periphery, sparse-to-dense) rather than iterative denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that addressing the input representation (sparse 2D pose) rather than the lifting model's capacity is a more effective and lightweight strategy for occlusion robustness, yielding single-frame SOTA that outperforms multi-frame temporal methods with far fewer parameters. Its orthogonality to temporal approaches means it can be stacked on top of PoseFormer or MixSTE for further gains, making it a practical plug-in for existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the hierarchical joint hierarchy (16→48→96) generalizes to other body models or joint counts, how the method behaves under severe real-world occlusions beyond synthetic DTD patches, and whether the autoregressive generation latency (396 FPS) remains acceptable for real-time deployment on edge devices.
