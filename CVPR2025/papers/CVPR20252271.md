## Problem

Existing semi-supervised semantic segmentation methods enforce consistency regularization only at the decoder output, ignoring the rich information in backbone encoder feature representations. Moreover, the uniformity objective—ensuring features are evenly distributed across the hypersphere to prevent representational collapse and maximize information—has been entirely absent from semi-supervised segmentation, despite its proven importance in self-supervised contrastive learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252271/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in two augmented streams: weakly augmented (horizontal flip, random crop, random scale) and strongly augmented (color jitter, grayscale, blur, CutMix) versions of unlabeled data, alongside normally labeled data. Training resolutions are 513×513 (PASCAL VOC), 769×769 (Cityscapes), and 512×512 (ADE20K). The output is a per-pixel class prediction from a segmentation head (DeepLabV3+ or SegFormer decoder), while an intermediate feature embedding from the backbone encoder is additionally projected through a two-layer Conv-BatchNorm-ReLU MLP to compute the SWD loss during training only.

## Method

SWSEG introduces a Sliced-Wasserstein Distance (SWD) loss computed between backbone encoder feature embeddings of weakly and strongly augmented unlabeled images, simultaneously optimizing alignment (minimizing distance between augmented views) and uniformity (spreading features across the hypersphere). To avoid the non-deterministic and memory-heavy Monte Carlo estimation of standard SWD, the authors project features onto a Gaussian distribution, yielding a closed-form 2-Wasserstein distance between Gaussians (Eq. 5) that is both deterministic and computationally efficient. A cross-covariance regularization term (Eq. 6) decorrelates feature dimensions to satisfy the Central Limit Theorem conditions underlying the Gaussian projection. The total training objective (Eq. 7) combines supervised cross-entropy, FixMatch-style unsupervised consistency loss, the Gaussian-SWD loss, and the decorrelation penalty, with the MLP projection head discarded at inference so the segmentation head operates on the richer pre-projection features.

## Results

On ADE20K (SegFormer MiT-B1), SWSEG achieves 24.4, 26.7, 30.9, 34.6, and 38.0 mIoU at 1/32, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, and 1/2 labeled splits, outperforming the supervised-only baseline by up to 8.2% mIoU and the best prior method (DT) by 1.8% at the 1/32 split. On PASCAL VOC 2012 (ResNet-101 + DeepLabV3+), SWSEG reaches 79.0, 79.3, and 79.9 mIoU at 1/16, 1/8, and 1/4 splits, surpassing the supervised baseline by up to 11.8% mIoU. On Cityscapes, SWSEG achieves 76.8, 78.6, and 79.5 mIoU, improving over supervised by up to 8.9% mIoU at the 1/16 split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ADE20K, SWSEG outperforms all 12 compared methods across every data split, with the largest margin over DT (NeurIPS'23) at the 1/32 split (24.4 vs. 22.6 mIoU, +1.8%) and over GTA-Seg at 1/4 (34.6 vs. 27.8 mIoU, +6.8%). On PASCAL VOC 2012, SWSEG beats the strongest baselines RankMatch (78.9, 79.2, 80.0) and DAW (78.5, 78.9, 79.6) at the 1/16 and 1/8 splits (79.0 and 79.3 mIoU respectively) but trails RankMatch by 0.1% at the 1/4 split (79.9 vs. 80.0). On Cityscapes, SWSEG is competitive but not uniformly dominant: it matches RankMatch at 1/8 (78.6) and slightly trails DAW (79.8) and RankMatch (80.0) at 1/4, while matching DT at 1/16 (76.8). Overall, SWSEG's advantage is most pronounced in low-label regimes (1/32 and 1/16 splits) and on the more complex ADE20K dataset, where the feature uniformity and alignment objectives provide the greatest benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWSEG extends the FixMatch-style weak/strong consistency framework for semi-supervised segmentation by moving the consistency objective from the decoder output to the backbone encoder's feature space, drawing directly on the alignment-and-uniformity objectives from self-supervised contrastive learning (Wang & Isola, SimCLR, VICReg). The Gaussian-projected Sliced-Wasserstein Distance is adapted from Nadjahi et al.'s fast SWD approximation, repurposed here as a joint alignment-and-uniformity loss rather than a general distributional distance. Unlike recent feature-level SSL segmentation methods (AllSpark, DDFP, CorrMatch) that use cross-attention, normalizing flows, or correlation maps, SWSEG frames the problem purely through distributional geometry on the feature space, unifying alignment and uniformity under a single metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly connect the uniformity metric from self-supervised representation learning to semi-supervised semantic segmentation, suggesting that preventing feature collapse is as important in the semi-supervised setting as in the fully self-supervised one. The Gaussian-SWD approximation offers a practical, closed-form alternative to Monte Carlo SWD that could be useful beyond segmentation for any task requiring efficient distributional alignment. Open questions include whether the uniformity benefit persists with larger backbones or in fully self-supervised segmentation, whether the decorrelation regularization (L_reg) is strictly necessary or merely a practical crutch for the Gaussian approximation, and how the method behaves under extreme label scarcity (e.g., 1/64 splits) where pseudo-label quality degrades.
