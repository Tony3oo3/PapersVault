## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models consistently fail to generate the correct number of objects specified in a prompt, with accuracy dropping sharply beyond three instances. This failure stems from two intertwined challenges: the model must maintain separate identity representations for each object instance (objectness) and simultaneously satisfy a global spatial constraint linking all instances, neither of which is well understood in current diffusion architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250362/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language prompt specifying a single object class and a target count (2–10) and produces a standard RGB image containing exactly that many instances. Internally, the pipeline intervenes at an intermediate denoising timestep (t=500) in SDXL to extract and correct the object layout before resuming generation, so the user-facing interface is identical to standard text-to-image generation with no additional conditioning signals required.

## Method

CountGen operates in three stages. First, it identifies object instances during early denoising by combining cross-attention foreground masks (via Otsu thresholding) with self-attention features from layer l52^up, then clustering foreground pixels with DBSCAN to obtain per-instance masks. Second, a ReLayout U-Net—trained on ~10K layout pairs (k vs. k+1 objects) generated by SDXL itself with matched seeds—corrects under-generation by iteratively predicting an additional instance in a natural location, while over-generation is handled by removing the smallest clusters. Third, layout-guided generation applies inference-time optimization using a weighted binary cross-entropy loss on cross-attention scores to pull objects into their target masks, combined with self-attention masking that zeros attention from background to foreground pixels to prevent spurious object formation outside the layout.

## Results

On CoCoCount, CountGen achieves 50% YOLOv9 count accuracy and 52% human-evaluated count accuracy, nearly doubling SDXL's 28%/26% baseline. On T2I-CompBench-Count, CountGen reaches 48% human accuracy versus 29% for SDXL. CountGen outperforms all seven baselines on every metric, with the largest margin over Token Optimization (the strongest competitor at 34%/36% on CoCoCount) being 16 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CountGen wins against all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest competitor, Token Optimization [40] (34% YOLOv9 / 36% human on CoCoCount), CountGen leads by 16 points on both metrics. DALL-E 3 [3] is the second-closest on human evaluation (38% CoCoCount, 36% CompBench) but trails CountGen by 14 and 12 points respectively; the authors attribute DALL-E 3's relative strength to its larger model size and training data. Random + BoundedAttn [8] (29%/30% on CoCoCount) and SDXL itself (28%/26%) are mid-pack, while LLM-based layout methods—Reason Layout [7] (21%/26%) and RPG [38] (21%/28%)—and the counting-guidance approach of Kang et al. [17] (21%/22%) all fall well below CountGen by 24–30 points. The naive Repeated Object prompt strategy (17%/18%) performs worst, confirming that simply repeating the object name in the prompt does not help.

## Relation to Prior Work

CountGen builds on SDXL as its base diffusion model and on Hertz et al.'s cross-attention localization to identify object regions, but departs fundamentally from prior layout-based approaches (LayoutGPT, Reason Out Your Layout, RPG) that rely on external LLMs to propose spatial arrangements. Instead, it discovers object-instance identity directly from SDXL's internal self-attention features (specifically layer l52^up), eliminating the need for external layout sources. The ReLayout U-Net, trained on self-generated image pairs, and the dual self-attention masking + cross-attention loss for layout-guided generation are novel components not present in prior counting-guidance methods like Kang et al. or Bounded Attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Counting accuracy is a persistent and visible failure mode of text-to-image models, and this paper nearly doubles SDXL's accuracy (26%→52%) while revealing that even DALL-E 3 plateaus around 36–38%, suggesting scale alone does not solve the problem. The discovery that a specific self-attention layer encodes per-instance identity is a conceptual contribution that may inform future interpretability and controllability work. However, the method is limited to single-object-type prompts with up to 10 instances, and open questions remain about extending to multi-object scenes, different architectures, and whether the self-attention instance representation generalizes beyond SDXL.
