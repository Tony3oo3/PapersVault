## Problem

Transformer-based super-resolution methods compute dense attention weights across all tokens in a window, incurring quadratic cost on many irrelevant tokens that degrade reconstruction quality. Existing sparse attention variants (e.g., top-k) still calculate similarities for every token before filtering, and no prior method leverages attention information from earlier layers to pre-identify which tokens are worth computing against.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250380/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single low-resolution RGB image and outputs a high-resolution reconstruction at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale. Training uses 64×64 patches from the DF2K dataset (DIV2K + Flickr2K) for 500K iterations with AdamW. The architecture operates on 32×32 non-overlapping windows with shifted-window attention and LePE positional encoding; the classical PFT variant uses 240 channels and 6 attention heads across 6 blocks, while PFT-light uses 52 channels and 4 heads across 5 blocks.

## Method

The core mechanism is Progressive Focused Attention (PFA), which links attention maps across adjacent layers via a Hadamard product (A^l = Norm(A^{l-1} ⊙ A_cal^l)), so that tokens consistently similar across layers receive amplified weights while irrelevant tokens are progressively suppressed. Crucially, the previous layer's attention map also serves as a pre-filter: positions with near-zero inherited weights are excluded from the current layer's QK^T computation via a Sparse Matrix Multiplication (SMM) operation, skipping similarity calculations entirely. The number of retained attention values per query decays geometrically with depth (K^l = αK^{l-1}, α≈0.5), enabling a large 32×32 window at a fraction of the cost of dense attention. The full PFT network follows a SwinIR-style architecture with shifted windows, and custom CUDA kernels implement the SMM to maintain inference speed comparable to standard Transformer SR models.

## Results

PFT achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all five benchmark datasets at ×2, ×3, and ×4 scales. At ×4, PFT reaches 33.15 dB on Set5, 29.29 dB on Set14, 28.02 dB on BSD100, 28.20 dB on Urban100, and 32.63 dB on Manga109, outperforming the previous best (ATD) by 0.05–0.03 dB while reducing FLOPs from 1.52T to 1.26T (17.1% reduction). At ×2, PFT achieves 38.68 dB on Set5 and 34.90 dB on Urban100 with only 5.03T FLOPs versus ATD's 6.07T. The lightweight variant PFT-light similarly outperforms ATD-light on nearly all datasets while cutting computational complexity by 20.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFT outperforms all 17 compared baselines on PSNR across every dataset and scale. Against the closest competitor ATD [41], PFT gains +0.07 dB on Set5, +0.05 dB on Set14, +0.01 dB on BSD100, +0.03 dB on Urban100, and +0.01 dB on Manga109 at ×4, while using 17.1% fewer FLOPs (1.26T vs. 1.52T). PFT also surpasses HAT [6] by +0.11 dB on Set5 and +0.23 dB on Urban100 at ×4, and beats IPG [31] by +0.07 dB on Urban100 at ×4. For the lightweight setting, PFT-light outperforms ATD-light by +0.40 dB on Urban100 at ×2 and +0.23 dB at ×4, while reducing FLOPs from 87.1G to 69.6G. PFT also edges out SwinIR [22] by +0.23 dB on Set5 and +0.75 dB on Urban100 at ×4, demonstrating that the progressive focusing mechanism yields consistent gains over standard shifted-window attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

PFT builds directly on the SwinIR architecture (shifted window attention, residual blocks) and incorporates LePE positional encoding from CSWin Transformer, while replacing standard self-attention with the proposed Progressive Focused Attention (PFA) block. Conceptually, it departs from prior sparse-attention methods (top-k, DRSformer, NLSA) that operate within a single layer by linking attention maps across adjacent layers via a Hadamard product, so that relevance is assessed cumulatively rather than from one similarity computation. It also differs from window-enlarging approaches (HAT, CAT, ATD) that increase the token pool at the cost of higher FLOPs; PFA instead uses the previous layer's attention map as a pre-filter to skip similarity calculations for irrelevant positions, enabling a 32×32 window at lower computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive attention inheritance mechanism offers a practical recipe for scaling window size in Transformer-based SR without proportional FLOPs growth, which is a persistent bottleneck in the field. The efficiency gain (17.1% fewer FLOPs than ATD while matching or exceeding PSNR) makes larger receptive fields accessible to resource-constrained deployments. However, the method's effectiveness depends on custom CUDA kernels for sparse matrix multiplication, and the focus ratio α requires careful per-block tuning; whether the progressive focusing principle transfers cleanly to other restoration tasks (deraining, denoising) or to non-windowed architectures remains an open question the authors note for future work.
