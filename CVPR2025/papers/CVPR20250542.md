## Problem

Existing monocular hand reconstruction methods rely on a weak-perspective camera model that assumes a static camera, making them unable to disentangle hand motion from camera motion in dynamic (e.g., egocentric) settings. This leads to depth ambiguity, noisy trajectories, and failure to recover accurate 4D global hand motion. Furthermore, no prior approach jointly addresses the challenges of dynamic cameras, complex two-hand interactions with frequent occlusions, and global trajectory recovery in a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of T frames containing two interacting hands captured by an arbitrarily moving camera as input. It outputs the per-frame MANO parameters (15 joint rotations, 10 shape coefficients, axis-angle root orientation, and 3D translation) for both hands in the world coordinate system, along with the camera extrinsics (rotation and translation) at each timestep. Optimization is performed on 128-frame segments, and the hand shape is assumed constant across the sequence.

## Method

Dyn-HaMR is a three-stage, multi-objective optimization pipeline (solved with L-BFGS) that progressively refines hand motion from camera-frame to world-frame to interaction-aware reconstruction. Stage I performs hierarchical initialization by fusing ViTPose bounding boxes with MediaPipe, HaMeR, and ACR per-frame estimates, then uses the HMP neural motion field prior to generatively infill missed detections and enforce temporal coherence. Stage II recovers the world-coordinate trajectory by composing the camera-frame hand motion with DPVO SLAM camera poses, jointly optimizing a learnable world scale factor ω that explicitly disambiguates the relative contributions of camera displacement and hand displacement to the observed 2D motion. Stage III refines the interaction by optimizing the HMP latent code alongside biomechanical constraints (joint-angle convex hulls, bone-length intervals, palm curvature bounds) and a vertex-level penetration loss between the two hand meshes, yielding physically plausible and temporally smooth 4D trajectories.

## Results

On the H2O dynamic-camera dataset, Dyn-HaMR achieves a G-MPJPE of 45.6 mm and GA-MPJPE of 34.2 mm, roughly halving the error of the best baseline HaMeR (96.9 / 75.7 mm) and reducing ACR's error (113.6 / 88.5 mm) by more than 2×. On the more challenging HOI4D dataset, the advantage widens dramatically: Dyn-HaMR reaches G-MPJPE 58.5 mm versus 201.6 mm (HaMeR), 251.1 mm (ACR), and 291.3 mm (IntagHand) — a 3–5× improvement. Even on the static-camera InterHand2.6M benchmark, Dyn-HaMR attains the best MPJPE (7.94 mm) and lowest acceleration error (2.76 mm/s²), outperforming ACR (8.75 / 3.99) and HaMeR (9.84 / 5.13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dyn-HaMR outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across all seven evaluated datasets. Against the strongest single-frame baseline HaMeR [37], Dyn-HaMR reduces G-MPJPE by ~53% on H2O (45.6 vs 96.9 mm) and ~71% on HOI4D (58.5 vs 201.6 mm), while also cutting acceleration error from 9.21 to 4.2 mm/s² on H2O. ACR [51] and IntagHand [25] suffer even larger global-motion errors (G-MPJPE 105–291 mm) due to their inability to disentangle camera and hand motion. On the static-camera InterHand2.6M benchmark, Dyn-HaMR still leads ACR [51] by 0.81 mm MPJPE and IntagHand [25] by 1.32 mm, confirming that the optimization pipeline improves local pose quality as well. The canonical HaMeR+DPVO pipeline (used as a strong dynamic-camera baseline on Ego-Exo4D and HOT3D) is outperformed by ~4× in G-MPJPE (53.9 vs 213.8 on Ego-Exo4D; 42.4 vs 129.5 on HOT3D) and by ~40× in jerk (5.26 vs 200.45), demonstrating that simply appending SLAM to a per-frame regressor is insufficient without the joint multi-objective optimization and interaction priors proposed here.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dyn-HaMR extends the optimization-based reconstruction paradigm established for dynamic human body motion (WHAM, Decoupling) to the interacting-hand setting, combining a data-driven SLAM system (DPVO) with a learned hand motion prior (HMP/NeMF) in a multi-stage pipeline. It departs from the dominant line of monocular hand reconstruction work (HaMeR, ACR, IntagHand) that relies on weak-perspective cameras and operates in camera or root-relative coordinates, by explicitly disentangling camera and hand motion through a learnable world scale factor. The hierarchical initialization fuses multiple off-the-shelf trackers (ViTPose, MediaPipe, HaMeR, ACR), while the Stage III interaction prior with biomechanical and penetration constraints is novel for the hand domain, drawing on body-level biomechanical constraint formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to recover 4D global hand trajectories from egocentric/dynamic-camera video, a capability essential for AR/VR interaction understanding and hand-object manipulation research. The large global-motion error reductions (e.g., ~2× on H2O, ~3.4× on HOI4D) establish a new benchmark for the field. Key limitations include restricted time horizons (128-frame segments), absence of explicit object interaction modeling, and the computational cost of per-sequence L-BFGS optimization, which limits real-time deployment. Open questions include extending to long sequences via regression or generative extrapolation, incorporating object priors, and whether the SLAM-based camera estimation can be replaced by learned camera pose networks.
