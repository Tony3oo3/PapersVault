## Problem

Dataset distillation methods achieve near-lossless performance on simple benchmarks like CIFAR-10 and TinyImageNet but degrade significantly on complex scenarios such as ImageNet-1K subsets, where discriminative features occupy a much smaller fraction of each image. Existing matching-based methods treat all pixels equally during optimization and apply supervision signals uniformly, causing non-discriminative common patterns (backgrounds, general colors) to dominate the learning process and erode class-specific features in synthetic images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250365/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image dataset (e.g., 10-class ImageNet-1K subsets with ~13,000 training images at 224×224, or CIFAR-10 at 32×32) and optimizes a small set of synthetic images (IPC images per class, typically 1–500) to the same resolution. The output is a compact synthetic dataset that, when used to train a 5-layer ConvNet (ConvNetD5), recovers accuracy close to training on the full real dataset. Cross-architecture evaluation is also supported using AlexNet, VGG11, and ResNet18.

## Method

EDF builds on trajectory matching (DATM) and introduces two modules. First, Common Pattern Dropout (CPD) decomposes the trajectory matching loss into per-parameter losses, sorts them in ascending order, and discards the lowest-loss elements (controlled by a dropout ratio α) before summing the remainder, thereby filtering out supervision that encodes common, non-discriminative patterns. Second, Discriminative Area Enhancement (DAE) dynamically extracts Grad-CAM activation maps from a pre-trained model on the evolving synthetic images and uses them to rescale pixel gradients: pixels whose activation exceeds the per-image mean receive an amplified gradient weight of (β + activation value), while others retain weight 1, focusing updates on class-relevant regions. Activation maps are recomputed every K iterations to track shifting discriminative areas, and both modules are designed to be plug-and-play with other matching-based distillation backbones.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K subsets, EDF consistently achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all IPC settings (1, 10, 50, 200, 300), outperforming all trajectory-matching baselines (MTT, FTD, DATM). Notably, EDF achieves lossless performance on ImageMeow (65.9% vs. 65.2% full-data) and ImageYellow (84.2% vs. 83.2% full-data) at IPC300, the first reported lossless results on ImageNet-1K subsets. On simpler datasets, EDF reaches 84.8% on CIFAR-10 (IPC500) and 41.1% on Tiny-ImageNet (IPC50), surpassing DATM by 1.3 and 1.4 points respectively. On the Comp-DD benchmark, EDF achieves recovery ratios of 77.7–87.4% on easy subsets and 67.8–84.2% on hard subsets, confirming that harder (more complex) subsets are more challenging for DD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDF outperforms all three trajectory-matching baselines (MTT, FTD, DATM) across every ImageNet-1K subset and IPC setting tested, with DATM being the closest competitor (typically 0.5–2.0 points behind). Against the knowledge-distillation evaluation baselines SRe2L and RDED, EDF achieves SOTA on 16 out of 21 settings; the exceptions occur at very low IPCs (IPC1, IPC10) where KD-based evaluation actually hurts EDF's pure distillation performance due to insufficient synthetic data capacity to absorb teacher knowledge. On CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet, EDF still leads DATM by 0.9–1.4 points in the DD setting, confirming generalization beyond complex scenarios. On the Comp-DD benchmark, EDF improves over DATM by 0.4–2.3 points across Bird, Dog, and Car categories, with the largest gains on harder subsets (e.g., Car-Hard IPC50: 61.0% vs. 58.7%), validating that the discriminative-feature emphasis is most beneficial when class-irrelevant content dominates the image.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDF is built directly on the trajectory-matching backbone (MTT [1], DATM [13]), adding two modules that address a specific failure mode: in complex scenarios, discriminative features occupy a much smaller fraction of the image, so uniform pixel updates let non-discriminative regions dominate learning. The Discriminative Area Enhancement module repurposes Grad-CAM activation maps—previously used only for interpretability—as a gradient-weighting signal for pixel-level updates, while Common Pattern Dropout filters low-loss trajectory-matching signals that the authors show embed common patterns (backgrounds, colors) into synthetic images. The paper also introduces the Comp-DD benchmark, extending the DD evaluation landscape beyond the well-trodden CIFAR/TinyImageNet setting to ImageNet-1K subsets with controlled complexity levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving lossless performance on ImageNet-1K subsets (ImageMeow, ImageYellow at IPC300) is a first for the DD community and suggests that trajectory matching can scale to realistic image complexity with the right inductive biases. The Comp-DD benchmark, with its complexity-graded easy/hard subsets across eight categories, gives the field a structured way to measure progress on complex scenarios, though only three of eight categories are reported here. Open questions include whether the Grad-CAM-based weighting generalizes to non-ConvNet architectures (the paper uses ConvNetD5 primarily), whether the approach scales to full ImageNet-1K (1000 classes), and whether the dropout ratio and update frequency heuristics transfer across domains without re-tuning.
