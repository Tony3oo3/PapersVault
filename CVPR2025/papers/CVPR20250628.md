## Problem

MLLM-based image quality assessment methods struggle to regress accurate numerical scores because quality scores are inherently continuous (modeled as Gaussian distributions) while MLLMs produce discrete token outputs. Prior approaches like Q-Align discretize the mean score into a one-hot label, causing significant information loss and failing to preserve inter-image quality relationships. Additionally, different IQA datasets exhibit distinct score distributions, making straightforward multi-dataset co-training infeasible.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a text response following the template 'The quality of this image is <level>', where <level> is one of five discrete tokens (bad, poor, fair, good, excellent). From the predicted probabilities over these five tokens, the model recovers a continuous quality score (mean) and a full score distribution (mean and variance) via closed-form computation. The vision encoder outputs 64 visual tokens via a Q-Former abstractor, and the LLM (LLaMA-2-7B) generates the response autoregressively.

## Method

The key insight is to discretize the entire Gaussian score distribution (not just its mean) into a soft label over five level tokens, preserving inter-image relationships and reducing discretization error by 10–35× compared to one-hot labels. A linear post-adjustment corrects truncation errors so the discrete distribution sums to 1 and matches the original mean. During training, a KL divergence loss is applied to the level token (matching predicted probabilities to the soft label) while standard cross-entropy handles other response tokens. For multi-dataset co-training, a fidelity loss based on Thurstone's model is added, which compares predicted pairwise quality probabilities within each dataset to ground-truth probabilities, enabling the model to learn intra-dataset ranking relationships despite cross-distribution score shifts. The architecture follows mPLUG-Owl2: CLIP ViT-L encoder → Q-Former abstractor (64 tokens) → LLaMA-2-7B, trained with AdamW at lr 2e-5 for 3 epochs.

## Results

Trained on KonIQ alone, DeQA-Score achieves 0.953 PLCC / 0.941 SRCC on the in-distribution KonIQ test set, a 1.3% PLCC improvement over Q-Align (0.941/0.940). On out-of-distribution benchmarks, DeQA-Score leads across nearly all datasets, with a 4.6% PLCC gain on LIVE-Wild (0.892 vs. 0.853) and a 17.1% PLCC gain on PIPAL (0.472 vs. 0.403). In multi-dataset co-training (KonIQ+SPAQ+KADID+PIPAL), DeQA-Score reaches 0.958 PLCC on KonIQ and 0.724 on PIPAL, consistently outperforming Q-Align by 2–7% PLCC across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeQA-Score outperforms the primary MLLM baseline Q-Align on all eight benchmarks, with the largest margins on PIPAL (+6.9% PLCC) and LIVE-Wild (+3.9% PLCC), confirming that distribution-based soft labels and fidelity loss jointly improve score regression. Against the strongest non-MLLM deep-learning baselines, DeQA-Score surpasses MUSIQ (0.924) and CLIP-IQA+ (0.909) on in-distribution KonIQ, and beats ManIQA (0.457) and Compare2Score (0.354) on the challenging PIPAL dataset. On AI-generated images (AGIQA-3K), DeQA-Score achieves 0.809 PLCC, exceeding Compare2Score (0.777) and Q-Align (0.772) despite never being trained on AIGC data. Handcrafted methods (NIQE, BRISQUE) are substantially outperformed across all benchmarks, with gaps exceeding 30% PLCC on most datasets. In multi-dataset co-training (Table 4), DeQA-Score maintains its lead over Q-Align by 2–7% PLCC across all training combinations, with the advantage growing as more diverse distortion types are included.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Q-Align (Wu et al., ICML 2024), which was the first to teach MLLMs to regress quality scores by discretizing mean MOS into one-hot level tokens. The key departure is replacing the one-hot label with a distribution-based soft label that discretizes the full Gaussian score distribution, preserving inter-image ordering and uncertainty. Additionally, the paper adapts the fidelity loss under Thurstone's model from UNIQUE (Zhang et al., IEEE TIP 2021)—previously used for multi-dataset co-training of traditional IQA networks—into the MLLM setting, a combination made possible only because the soft label enables the model to predict a full score distribution. Within the broader MLLM-based IQA landscape (Q-Bench, Q-Instruct, CoInstruct, DepictQA, Compare2Score), this work specifically targets the underexplored scoring task rather than the more common quality description task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-based soft label offers a principled solution to the continuous-to-discrete mismatch that plagues any MLLM-based regression task, and the technique could generalize beyond IQA to other continuous prediction problems (e.g., age estimation, aesthetic scoring). The paper demonstrates that MLLMs can predict score distributions closely aligned with human annotations, opening the door to uncertainty-aware quality assessment. However, performance on model-processed distortions (PIPAL) remains weak (~0.47 PLCC), and the Gaussian assumption for score distributions may not hold universally. Open questions include whether the approach scales to finer-grained score ranges, non-Gaussian annotation distributions, or video quality assessment.
