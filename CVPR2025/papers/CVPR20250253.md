## Problem

Existing guidance techniques for video diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off: CFG reduces sample diversity and motion dynamics, while Autoguidance requires training a separate weak model that is impractical for large-scale video models. Training-free self-perturbation methods (PAG, SEG, SAG) exist but are designed exclusively for 2D spatial attention in image diffusion, leaving the spatiotemporal attention layers in video models unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250253/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

STG is a training-free inference-time modification applied to pre-trained transformer-based video diffusion models. For text-to-video models (Mochi, Open-Sora), the input is a text prompt; for the image-to-video model (SVD), the input is an initial RGB frame. The output is a generated video sequence. The method requires no architectural changes or retraining—only selective layer skipping during the forward pass, and it supports both full 3D spatiotemporal attention (Mochi) and factorized spatial+temporal attention (Open-Sora, SVD) with separate guidance weights for each.

## Method

STG constructs an implicit aligned weak model by selectively skipping spatiotemporal layers in the main model's forward pass, eliminating the need for external models or additional training. Two skip variants are used: (1) residual skip, which bypasses an entire residual block by passing the input directly to the next layer, and (2) attention skip, which replaces the attention matrix A with an identity matrix so the value tensor passes through unmodified (a 3D extension of PAG). For factorized-attention models, spatial and temporal skips are applied independently and combined with separate guidance weights w₁ and w₂ in the formula ε̃ = ε + w₁(ε − εˢ) + w₂(ε − εᵗ). An optional orthogonalization step projects the temporal guidance vector onto the subspace orthogonal to the spatial one to decouple their effects. Optional manifold-constrained techniques (latent rescaling and stochastic restart sampling) prevent off-manifold drift at high guidance scales.

## Results

On VBench T2V benchmarks, STG improves Imaging Quality from 0.524 to 0.628 on Mochi (+0.104) and from 0.561 to 0.606 on Open-Sora, while Aesthetic Quality rises from 0.507 to 0.554 (Mochi) and 0.493 to 0.509 (Open-Sora). On the I2V benchmark (SVD), STG reduces FVD from 151.3 to 128.7 and increases Dynamic Degree from 0.562 to 0.694, indicating better video diversity and motion. Motion Smoothness and Temporal Flickering show only marginal gains (e.g., 0.985→0.988), as these metrics are near saturation (~0.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), the standard sampling guidance for diffusion models. STG consistently outperforms CFG on frame-level quality metrics: Imaging Quality improves by 0.045–0.104 across all three models, and Aesthetic Quality gains 0.016–0.047. On the I2V setting (SVD), STG reduces FVD by 14.9% (151.3→128.7) and substantially increases Dynamic Degree (0.562→0.694), showing that CFG suppresses motion while STG preserves it. For T2V models, Dynamic Degree is roughly maintained (0.87→0.86 for Mochi; 0.902→0.895 for Open-Sora), confirming STG does not sacrifice diversity. Motion Smoothness and Temporal Flickering show only negligible differences (≤0.003), as both methods already operate near the saturation ceiling of ~0.98 on these metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

STG operationalizes the autoguidance principle (Karras et al., 2024) — that an aligned weak model sharing the same task and conditioning as the main model yields quality-improving guidance — but eliminates the need to train that weak model by constructing it through spatiotemporal layer skipping. It extends the image-domain self-perturbation guidance line (PAG, SAG, SEG), which operates on 2D spatial attention, to the video setting by applying the same identity-matrix attention replacement and residual skipping to both spatial and temporal (or full 3D) attention layers. The guidance formulation itself is a direct generalization of the CFG linear-combination structure (Ho & Salimans, 2022), with the unconditional branch replaced by a perturbed self-prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because video diffusion models are prohibitively expensive to retrain, a training-free guidance technique that improves fidelity without sacrificing diversity or motion dynamics is practically valuable for anyone deploying or fine-tuning such models. The method's simplicity (skip a few layers) and architecture-agnosticism (works on 3D-attention Mochi, factorized-attention Open-Sora, and UNet-based SVD) make it easy to adopt. Open questions remain: optimal layer selection and guidance scale still require per-model heuristic tuning, the independence assumption for factorized spatial/temporal perturbations is only loosely justified, and it is unclear whether the approach transfers to non-transformer video architectures or to longer-horizon generation where error accumulation may compound.
