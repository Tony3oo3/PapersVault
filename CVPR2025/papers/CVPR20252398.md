## Problem

Existing material editing methods either rely on crude geometry and illumination approximations (traditional graphics), fine-tune diffusion models on synthetic data risking overfitting and loss of object priors (Alchemist), or perform zero-shot transfer without fine-grained attribute control (ZeST). No prior approach unifies exemplar-based material transfer, blending between multiple materials, and parametric control of individual attributes (roughness, metallic, transparency, glow) within a single framework that preserves the base diffusion model's learned priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image, one or two material exemplar images, a foreground mask, a depth map, and a grayscale initial image (serving as an illumination cue), plus an optional editing strength δ for parametric control. It produces a material-edited image via the SDXL inpainting pipeline. A key practical property is that multiple attribute edits (e.g., simultaneously increasing roughness and metallic) can be composed in a single forward pass by summing the respective CLIP-space offsets, since each attribute network is trained independently.

## Method

MARBLE's core architectural insight is that a specific U-Net block near the bottleneck of Stable Diffusion is responsible for material attribution; injecting CLIP image features only into this block (rather than all blocks as in ZeST) preserves object geometry and lighting while still transferring material appearance. For material blending, the method linearly interpolates CLIP features from two exemplar images (α·z_m1 + (1−α)·z_m2) before injection. For parametric control, a 2-layer MLP is trained per attribute on a small synthetic Blender dataset (300 objects with controlled shader parameters) to predict a low-rank SVD-projected editing direction in CLIP-space, using a combined cosine-similarity and MSE loss. At inference, the predicted offset is added to the CLIP embedding of the material exemplar, and the modified feature is injected into the identified U-Net block. The base diffusion model remains entirely unmodified, enabling the learned directions to generalize across image styles (photorealistic, anime, painting) and allowing multiple attribute networks to be composed additively.

## Results

On a 50-object synthetic validation set, MARBLE outperforms image-trained Concept Sliders across all four material attributes (roughness, metallic, transparency, glow) and all four metrics. For roughness, MARBLE achieves PSNR 26.56 vs. 18.87, LPIPS 0.056 vs. 0.356, CLIP score 0.931 vs. 0.597, and DreamSim 0.129 vs. 0.567. The largest relative gains appear in LPIPS (roughly 5–6× lower) and DreamSim (roughly 3–4× lower), indicating substantially better perceptual fidelity. In a user study with 16 participants on 20 real-world images, 87.5% preferred MARBLE's outputs over Concept Slider's.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARBLE is compared against two baselines: image-trained Concept Sliders [20] (quantitative) and InstructPix2Pix [7] (qualitative only, since it does not support continuous parametric control). Against Concept Sliders, MARBLE wins decisively on every metric for every attribute: PSNR improves by 2.8–7.7 dB, LPIPS drops by roughly 5–6×, CLIP score rises by 0.23–0.33, and DreamSim falls by 0.30–0.44. Qualitatively, Concept Sliders fail to capture transparency and glow edits entirely and introduce geometry distortions, while InstructPix2Pix causes unintended changes to albedo, geometry, or background. The user study (87.5% preference for MARBLE over Concept Slider on real-world images) further confirms the practical advantage. Notably, MARBLE achieves these gains without fine-tuning the base diffusion model, whereas Concept Sliders require LoRA training per attribute.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARBLE extends ZeST's zero-shot exemplar-based material transfer by identifying a specific U-Net block near the bottleneck responsible for material attribution (inspired by InstantStyle's style-block discovery) and injecting CLIP features only there, rather than across all blocks. It deliberately departs from Alchemist's approach of fine-tuning the diffusion model on synthetic renders, instead keeping the base model intact and operating purely in CLIP-space. The parametric control component draws on the broader line of work on interpretable directions in pre-trained embeddings (GANSpace, Concept Sliders, Baumann et al.), but applies it specifically to fine-grained material attributes (roughness, metallic, transparency, glow) via lightweight MLPs trained on small synthetic datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARBLE is the first framework to unify exemplar-based material transfer, multi-exemplar blending, and parametric fine-grained attribute control within a single CLIP-space pipeline, enabling multiple edits in one forward pass without any fine-tuning of the diffusion model. The generalization to non-photorealistic styles (Van Gogh, cyberpunk) without style-specific training suggests CLIP-space material directions are somewhat style-agnostic, which could benefit graphic design workflows. Open questions include whether the single-block injection finding generalizes across diffusion architectures, whether more material attributes can be controlled, and how to address the observed texture-pattern drift and artifacts at extreme parameter values.
