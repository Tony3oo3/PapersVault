## Problem

3D morphable models (3DMMs) are powerful for single-image 3D reconstruction but are only available for a handful of categories (faces, bodies) due to the need for expensive multi-view 3D scanning and manual registration. Learning 3D priors from 2D images or videos has emerged as an alternative, but existing approaches either require category-specific design choices, are restricted to animal categories, or represent appearance in RGB space, making inverse rendering inherently difficult. Common3D addresses this by learning category-specific 3DMMs for common everyday objects in a fully self-supervised manner from casually captured object-centric videos.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single 448×448 RGB image of an object and produces a deformed 3D mesh (the instance shape), a 3-DOF rotation pose, dense 2D-3D correspondences, and an instance segmentation mask. The 3D shape is represented as a mesh extracted from a signed distance field via DMTet, with per-vertex feature vectors encoding semantic identity. At training time, the model consumes object-centric videos (up to 50 per category from CO3D) supplemented with SFM point clouds and PointRend masks, with canonical camera poses estimated unsupervised via the method of Sommer et al. [52].

## Method

Common3D represents each object category with a learned prototypical SDF (converted to a mesh via DMTet) and an instance-level affine deformation field parameterized by an MLP conditioned on a latent code extracted from a frozen DINOv2 ViT-S backbone. The key architectural novelty is representing appearance as a neural feature field (per-vertex D-dimensional vectors) rather than RGB colors, with a trainable adapter (three ResNet blocks upsampling 32×32 to 64×64) on top of DINOv2 that enhances features for correspondence. Training proceeds in two phases: first optimizing the template shape with geometric losses (chamfer distance, mask MSE, distance-transform overlap, Eikonal regularization), then jointly optimizing the feature field, adapter, and deformation model using a contrastive appearance loss that matches rendered surface probabilities (from Euclidean vertex distances) against feature-similarity probabilities, preventing collapse to trivial constant features. At inference, the shape latent is predicted feedforward while the pose is refined via inverse rendering that maximizes pixel-wise surface probability alignment between rendered and extracted features.

## Results

On PASCAL3D+ 3D pose estimation (30° accuracy), Common3D achieves 75.3% average, outperforming the prior unsupervised state-of-the-art UOP3D (69.2%) by +6.3 points and ZSP (46.0%) by +29.3 points, while remaining competitive with supervised methods StarMap (82.5%) and VoGE (90.9%). On ObjectNet3D (20 categories), Common3D reaches 56.8% 30° accuracy vs. UOP3D's 52.4% (+4.4 points). For instance segmentation, Common3D improves IoU from 60.3% to 64.1% on PASCAL3D+ and from 64.0% to 67.0% on ObjectNet3D over UOP3D. On semantic correspondence (SPair-71k PCK@0.1), the adapter-enhanced features achieve 54.0%, surpassing DINOv2 (47.9%) by +6.1 points and approaching the supervised SpherMaps (59.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UOP3D [52], the closest unsupervised baseline (also trained on CO3D videos), Common3D wins on nearly all categories for 3D pose estimation, gaining +6.3 points average on PASCAL3D+ and +4.4 on ObjectNet3D, with the largest per-category gains on Truck (+15.9) and Motorcycle (+11.1). Common3D also outperforms UOP3D on instance segmentation by +3.8 IoU on PASCAL3D+ and +3.0 on ObjectNet3D, and on semantic correspondence by a large margin. Against ZSP [14], Common3D achieves substantially higher 30° accuracy (75.3% vs. 46.0% on PASCAL3D+), though ZSP requires depth input and runs an order of magnitude slower. Compared to supervised methods StarMap [83] and VoGE [63], Common3D trails by 7.2 and 15.6 points respectively on PASCAL3D+, but these methods are trained directly on PASCAL3D+ annotations while Common3D is evaluated out-of-domain. For semantic correspondence, Common3D (54.0% PCK@0.1) surpasses DINOv2 [41] (47.9%) by +6.1 points but remains below SpherMaps [35] (59.4%), which uses coarse viewpoint annotations that Common3D does not require.

## Relation to Prior Work

Common3D extends the line of self-supervised 3D morphable model learning from faces and human bodies to common everyday objects, building directly on the hybrid volumetric-mesh representation of DMTet [50] and the affine deformation field of Deep Implicit Templates [82]. It departs from prior feature-based 3DMM works (e.g., MagicPony, Fauna) that are restricted to animal categories and use frozen DINO features, by jointly training a DINOv2 adapter with a contrastive objective that exploits 3D correspondences from the deformable template. The unsupervised camera pose alignment is inherited from the authors' prior UOP3D work [52], but Common3D adds instance-level deformations and a semantic feature field, moving from a rigid template to a fully deformable 3DMM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully self-supervised method that learns deformable 3DMMs for common objects and uses them for zero-shot pose estimation, segmentation, and correspondence from a single image, removing the need for expensive multi-view studio captures or category-specific 3D scans. The key insight that representing appearance as learned neural features (rather than RGB) makes inverse rendering tractable and simultaneously improves correspondence quality is a practical recipe likely to be adopted in follow-up work. Limitations include the domain gap between CO3D training videos and in-the-wild test images, the requirement for object-centric video collections, and the fact that performance on categories with low shape variance (cellphone, microwave) does not improve over the rigid-template baseline. Open questions include whether the approach scales to highly deformable objects (clothing, soft bodies) and whether the adapter can be made category-agnostic.
