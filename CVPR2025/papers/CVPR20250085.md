## Problem

Existing monocular video human reconstruction methods either produce an inseparable clothed body or reconstruct only clothing while leaving the underlying body without detail. The core difficulty is that clothing occludes the body in the input video, so both the visible region (requiring high geometric fidelity) and the invisible region (requiring plausible completion) must be handled simultaneously to enable downstream tasks like clothing transfer and animation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video of N frames along with per-frame 2D human parsing masks (from SAM2) and normal maps (from Sapiens) as supervision. It outputs two independent, spatio-temporally continuous meshes — a clothing mesh and a body mesh — defined in a canonical pose space and deformed to each observed frame via LBS plus per-part non-rigid deformation fields. The decoupled representation allows the two meshes to be swapped, re-posed, or fed into physics-based simulation independently.

## Method

D³-Human operates in two stages. In template generation, a DMTet hybrid representation (tetrahedral grid + neural SDF) is optimized into a watertight clothed human, then segmented into clothing and body using a novel human manifold signed distance field (hmSDF) that assigns negative values to body interior, zero to the boundary curve, and positive values to clothing interior — unlike G-Shell's mSDF which only encodes one side. A region-aggregation step corrects segmentation errors by reassigning small misclassified connected components, and invisible body regions are completed from SMPL. In the detailed deformation stage, two separate MLPs model non-rigid deformations for clothing and body independently, while a shared LBS handles skeletal motion. Training uses occlusion-aware differentiable rasterization (rendering both meshes simultaneously to resolve body-clothing occlusion), supervised by RGB, mask, and perceptual normal losses, regularized by Eikonal, hole-opening, collision, and geometry terms.

## Results

On four synthetic sequences from SelfRecon, D³-Human achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (CD, in 10⁻³ cm) across all metrics (clothing, body, and full clothed human). For example, on Male1 the method reports CD of 0.478 (clothing), 0.321 (body), and 0.270 (all), outperforming the next-best method (DELTA) by 64% on clothing CD and 78% on full-body CD. On Female1, D³-Human achieves 0.959 CD for the full clothed human versus 1.148 for REC-MV and 3.420 for SelfRecon. The method also reconstructs a decoupled template in approximately 20 minutes, compared to over a day for prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D³-Human outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across all four sequences. Against REC-MV [43], which reconstructs clothing well but relies on SMPL for the body (causing mesh penetration), D³-Human reduces full-body CD by 17–78% (e.g., 0.270 vs. 0.619 on Male1) while also achieving lower clothing CD. BCNet [20] is the weakest baseline, with body CD values 10–30× higher than D³-Human (e.g., 10.252 vs. 0.966 on Female1), reflecting its limited body detail. DELTA [10] is the closest competitor on body CD (0.973 vs. 0.966 on Female1) but lags significantly on clothing CD (2.177 vs. 1.065) and full-body CD (1.388 vs. 0.959). SelfRecon [21] and GoMAvatar [52] do not support decoupling and report only full-body CD; D³-Human improves upon them by 72% (0.959 vs. 3.420) and 87% (0.959 vs. 7.319) on Female1, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hybrid explicit-implicit representation of DMTet (tetrahedral grid + neural SDF) and the manifold SDF concept from G-Shell, modifying the latter into a human manifold SDF (hmSDF) that segments a watertight clothed surface into clothing and body using only 2D parsing masks. It departs from prior implicit methods (SelfRecon, GoMAvatar, SCARF) that produce inseparable whole-body reconstructions, and from clothing-only methods (REC-MV, BCNet) that rely on SMPL as a fixed underlying body. The key conceptual departure is treating the clothed surface as a single watertight entity that can be split by a learned signed field, then completing invisible body regions with SMPL, rather than modeling clothing and body as independent representations from the start.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled output directly enables downstream applications—clothing transfer and physics-based animation via HOOD—that were impractical with prior inseparable reconstructions, and the ~20-minute template generation is a significant practical speedup over methods requiring over a day. However, quantitative evaluation relies solely on synthetic data from SelfRecon, and the invisible body regions are constrained by SMPL's parametric expressiveness, limiting detail in occluded areas. Open questions include whether the hmSDF approach generalizes to multi-person scenes, extreme clothing (e.g., very loose garments where the body-clothing boundary is ambiguous), and whether the SMPL completion can be replaced by learned priors for higher-fidelity occluded regions.
