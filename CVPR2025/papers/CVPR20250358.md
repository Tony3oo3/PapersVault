## Problem

Rolling shutter cameras dominate consumer and smartphone markets, but their multi-view geometry differs fundamentally from global shutter cameras because a generic space point is seen multiple times during the sequential line-by-line capture. While absolute pose methods for RS cameras have appeared over the past 20 years, the relative pose problem remains unsolved, and no unified geometric theory exists for the important subclass of RS cameras that see each point exactly once. This paper provides a complete algebraic-geometric characterization of order-one RS (RS₁) cameras and classifies all their minimal relative pose problems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes point and line correspondences from 2 to 5 linear RS₁ images, with prescribed point-line incidences, and optionally the special point (a:b:0) at infinity for each camera. A key structural detail is that a 3D line is imaged as a conic (not a line) passing through two fixed points at infinity, so the image data consists of points and conics rather than points and lines. The output is the set of all complex solutions (with computed degrees) for the relative camera poses and 3D point/line reconstruction, with 31 distinct minimal problems identified across 2–5 cameras.

## Method

The paper introduces a back-projection RS camera model that parameterizes camera rays via a map Λ and rolling planes via a map Σ, connecting ray geometry in the Grassmannian Gr(1,ℙ³) to the image projection. The central characterization (Theorem 4) shows that an RS camera is order-one if and only if all rolling planes intersect in a common line K, the rolling-planes map is birational, and the camera center moves on a curve C that either equals K or meets K in deg(C)−1 points. From this, explicit parameter spaces are constructed for three types of RS₁ cameras (center on a rational curve off K, center on K, or static center on K), with dimensions computed for each. The image of a space line under an RS₁ camera is shown to be a rational curve of degree deg(Φ) with multiplicity deg(Φ)−1 at the rolling-line intersection point, leaving exactly one additional point at infinity that depends only on camera parameters. Using these geometric constraints, the paper classifies all 31 minimal SfM problems for linear RS₁ cameras (2–5 views) and computes their solution degrees, discovering practical cases such as a 9-point two-camera problem of degree 364 and a 7-point problem of degree 140.

## Results

The paper classifies exactly 31 minimal problems for structure-from-motion with linear RS₁ cameras across 2, 3, 4, and 5 cameras, all of which are new. The degree of the picture-taking map Φ (Theorem 12) ranges from 2 (for constant-rotation linear RS₁ cameras) to 2d+δ+2 (for general type-II RS₁ cameras), determining the degree of image curves of space lines. Practical two-camera minimal problems include 7-point (degree 140), 9-point (degree 364), and 3-point+2-line (degree 60) configurations, with smaller-degree variants (28, 48) suitable for symbolic-numeric solvers. A three-camera problem with degree 160 is also identified as practical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no direct empirical baselines; instead, it positions its contributions relative to prior RS camera models. Compared to Dai et al. [12], who formulated relative pose for linear RS cameras, the paper shows that general linear RS cameras have order two (not one) and identifies the precise condition (motion line parallel to projection plane) under which they become RS₁, enabling a solvable 9-point minimal problem where [12] only identified an 11-point problem without a solver. Compared to the Straight-Cayley model of Albl et al. [7], the paper proves (Theorem 19) that the SC model produces RS₁ cameras only under two specific algebraic constraints on its rotation-axis and translation parameters, and that generically the SC camera has order four with a twisted-cubic center locus. The paper extends the line-congruence framework of Ponce et al. [47,53] from abstract rational cameras to concrete RS camera imaging, providing the first explicit parameterizations and minimal problem classification for RS₁ cameras.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line-congruence framework of Ponce, Sturmfels, and Trager [47, 53]—which characterized order-one congruences and rational 'photographic camera' maps—to the concrete setting of rolling shutter cameras, providing the first unified algebraic-geometric theory for the RS₁ subclass. It departs from Dai et al. [12], who formulated RS relative pose problems but worked with order-two linear RS cameras and non-algebraic Rodrigues parameterization, by showing that order-one RS cameras require the Cayley parameterization and a motion line parallel to the projection plane. The Straight-Cayley absolute pose model of Albl et al. [7] is reinterpreted through the RS₁ lens, revealing explicit parameter constraints under which that practical model yields order-one cameras.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complete classification of exactly 31 minimal problems for linear RS₁ relative pose (with no problems for 1 or >5 cameras) provides a definitive theoretical foundation for building efficient solvers, and the discovery of practical low-degree problems (e.g., 7-point with degree 140, 9-point with degree 364) opens a path to competitive RS SfM pipelines. A key open question is whether the higher-degree minimal problems for 4–5 cameras decompose via monodromy groups, and whether the Straight-Cayley model admits tractable relative pose solvers analogous to its absolute pose results. The theory also clarifies that the widely-used linear RS model is generically order-two, meaning prior RS relative pose formulations were solving a harder problem than necessary for many practical scenarios.
