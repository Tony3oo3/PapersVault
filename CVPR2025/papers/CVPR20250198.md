## Problem

Open-vocabulary segmentation methods require a human to specify target categories via text prompts or pre-labeled datasets, limiting scalability and preventing detection of rare or unexpected objects. No prior work addresses auto-vocabulary segmentation for 3D point clouds, where the vocabulary is entirely unknown and must be generated per scene at runtime without any human in the loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250198/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a LiDAR point cloud (N×3 coordinates, aggregated over 0.5 s for outdoor scenes) and optionally K synchronized RGB images as input. It produces a scene-specific vocabulary of M semantic noun tags (e.g., 671 unique categories on nuScenes validation) and assigns each point a label from that vocabulary. At inference, the point captioner operates without camera intrinsics, partitioning the point cloud into T cylindrical sectors (outdoor) or square pillars (indoor) to generate a controllable number of captions purely from geometry.

## Method

3D-AVS decouples vocabulary generation from segmentation. Two captioning branches run in parallel: an off-the-shelf image captioner (xGen-MM/BLIP-3) and a novel Point Captioner trained via 2D-to-3D distillation, where a CLIP-aligned point encoder is supervised to match CLIP image features through a Sparse Masked Attention Pooling (SMAP) module. SMAP applies relative positional encoding, masks points into subsets, uses global average pooling as query and multi-head attention within each subset to produce one pooled feature per mask, which is then decoded into a caption. Captions from both branches are parsed into noun tags via spaCy lemmatization and WordNet validation (Caption2Tag), then fed to a CLIP text encoder. Segmentation assigns each point the tag with maximum dot-product similarity in CLIP space, combining point features and optionally projected image features via column-wise max.

## Results

On nuScenes, 3D-AVS achieves 36.2 mIoU, outperforming the best fixed-vocabulary baseline (CNS at 33.5) by 2.7 points, despite having no access to predefined categories at test time. On ScanNet200, 3D-AVS reaches 14.6 mIoU, setting a new state-of-the-art over Diff2Scene (14.2) and OpenScene (11.7). On ScanNet (20 coarse classes), 3D-AVS scores 40.5 mIoU, trailing OpenScene (47.0) and Diff2Scene (48.6), which the authors attribute to the difficulty of mapping fine-grained auto-generated labels onto a small fixed vocabulary. In terms of vocabulary quality, 3D-AVS achieves a TPSS of 9.65 on nuScenes and 3.78 on ScanNet, surpassing both the official label sets (7.39 / 3.44) and OpenScene's manually extended 43-category set (8.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes, 3D-AVS (36.2 mIoU) outperforms all seven baselines that report results on this dataset, with the largest margin over CLIP2Scene (+15.4) and a 2.7-point lead over the strongest competitor CNS (33.5). On ScanNet (20 classes), 3D-AVS (40.5) trails OpenScene (47.0) by 6.5 points and Diff2Scene (48.6) by 8.1 points, which the authors explain as an artifact of mapping fine-grained auto-generated labels onto a coarse 20-class vocabulary. However, on ScanNet200 (200 fine-grained classes), 3D-AVS (14.6) surpasses Diff2Scene (14.2) and OpenScene (11.7), confirming that the auto-vocabulary approach is more faithful when the evaluation vocabulary is sufficiently granular. Compared to the closest fixed-vocabulary methods (OpenScene, Diff2Scene), 3D-AVS operates under a strictly harder setting—no predefined categories are available at inference—yet still matches or exceeds them on the two most challenging benchmarks (nuScenes and ScanNet200). The TPSS evaluation further shows that 3D-AVS's auto-generated vocabulary is semantically more aligned with the point clouds than both the official 16/20-class sets and OpenScene's manually curated 43-category extension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D auto-vocabulary segmentation (AVS) line of work (ZeroSeg, AutoSeg, CaSED) to 3D LiDAR point clouds, a setting the authors explicitly note remains unexplored. It builds on CLIP's vision-language alignment and the CLIP-aligned 3D encoders from OpenScene and CLIP2Scene, but departs from the open-vocabulary segmentation paradigm by eliminating the human-specified query entirely. The SMAP module repurposes relative positional encoding from Point Transformer V3 and multi-head attention to produce region-wise features, while the point captioner adapts the LidarCLIP encode-then-decode pipeline from a single global caption to multiple controllable captions via geometric partitioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the human from vocabulary generation, 3D-AVS addresses a scalability bottleneck in autonomous driving perception where pre-defined categories inevitably miss rare or atypical objects. The TPSS metric fills a genuine evaluation gap for open-ended label sets, though its low absolute scores (7–10) and dependence on a frozen CLIP-aligned encoder limit its discriminative power. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to real-time inference, how it handles truly novel categories never seen in any training distribution, and whether the LLM-based mapping to fixed categories for mIoU evaluation introduces systematic bias that masks the true quality of generated labels.
