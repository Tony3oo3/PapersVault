## Problem

Applying auto-regressive transformers to 3D mesh generation faces three fundamental obstacles: the unordered nature of 3D latent tokens conflicts with sequential next-token prediction, vector quantization introduces substantial geometric compression loss, and the quadratic computational cost of transformers makes directly scaling to higher-resolution latents impractical (requiring hundreds of GPUs). No existing strategy efficiently and progressively increases token resolution while preserving fine geometric detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252198/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, extracting complementary semantic and pixel-level features via CLIP and DINOv2 as conditioning tokens. It produces a 3D mesh extracted by Marching Cubes from a continuous occupancy field decoded from 1024 high-resolution latent tokens. The VAE encoder consumes multi-resolution point clouds (16384, 4096, 1024 points) with surface normals, and all latent tokens remain in continuous space rather than being discretized through vector quantization.

## Method

MAR-3D combines a Pyramid VAE with a cascaded masked auto-regressive transformer (Cascaded MAR). The Pyramid VAE encodes multi-resolution point clouds through separate cross-attention layers at each scale, producing continuous latent tokens that capture both global structure and fine geometry. Generation proceeds in two stages: MAR-LR produces 256 low-resolution tokens conditioned on image features, then MAR-HR upscales to 1024 high-resolution tokens conditioned on both the LR tokens and image features. To handle the unordered nature of 3D tokens, training uses random masking (MAE-style) with a diffusion-based denoising loss instead of cross-entropy, enabling parallel token generation in random order at inference. A condition augmentation strategy applies Gaussian noise to the LR tokens before feeding them to MAR-HR, mitigating compounding error between the VAE-generated training conditions and MAR-LR-generated inference conditions. Token generation follows a cosine schedule that progressively increases the number of tokens decoded per step, with classifier-free guidance using a linearly increasing CFG coefficient.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, MAR-3D achieves an F-Score of 0.944, Chamfer Distance of 0.351, and Normal Consistency of 0.835, outperforming the strongest baseline InstantMesh (0.923 / 0.415 / 0.780) by 15.4% in CD and 5.5 points in NC. On OmniObject3D, MAR-3D records 0.931 F-Score, 0.364 CD, and 0.826 NC, again leading all competitors. The method demonstrates consistent superiority across both unseen real-scanned object benchmarks, with the largest margins over diffusion-based baselines like CraftsMan (CD 0.785 on GSO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAR-3D outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both GSO and OmniObject3D. The closest competitor is InstantMesh (sparse-view LRM + multi-view diffusion), which MAR-3D beats by 0.021 F-Score, 0.064 CD, and 0.055 NC on GSO, and by 0.013 F-Score, 0.063 CD, and 0.047 NC on OmniObject3D. TripoSR (large reconstruction model without diffusion) trails further behind, with CD roughly 1.8× higher than MAR-3D on GSO. The VAE-diffusion baselines CraftsMan and LGM are substantially weaker, with CD values of 0.785 and 0.813 on GSO respectively—more than double MAR-3D's 0.351—indicating that the cascaded MAR with Pyramid VAE provides a clear advantage over both naive diffusion and feed-forward reconstruction approaches for preserving fine geometric detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAR-3D extends the continuous auto-regressive framework of MAR (Li et al., 2024) from 2D image generation to 3D mesh synthesis, replacing discrete vector-quantized tokens with continuous latent representations supervised by a lightweight diffusion denoiser. It inherits the parallel random-order decoding strategy from MaskGIT/MUSE and the MAE masking paradigm, but adapts them to the unordered nature of 3D latent tokens via learnable positional embeddings. The cascaded LR→HR design with condition augmentation draws directly from cascaded diffusion (Ho et al., 2021), while the Pyramid VAE departs from single-resolution VAEs like 3DShape2VecSet by processing multi-scale point clouds through separate cross-attention layers simultaneously. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the dominant DiT-based joint distribution modeling paradigm (CLAY, CraftsMan, Michelangelo), arguing that decomposing generation into sequential auto-regressive steps with progressive upscaling is more scalable than modeling the full joint distribution at high token counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D generation: the quadratic scaling of transformer attention with token count makes high-resolution latent prediction prohibitively expensive (hundreds of GPUs for direct 1024-token generation). The cascaded MAR approach offers a concrete, efficient alternative that achieves SOTA among open-sourced methods while requiring far fewer resources, making high-fidelity 3D generation more accessible. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (GSO, OmniObject3D) with only 100 samples each, and comparisons are restricted to four baselines; broader validation on diverse object categories, multi-object scenes, and text-to-3D settings would strengthen the claims. Open questions include whether the pyramid VAE design generalizes to other 3D representations (e.g., Gaussians, SDFs), whether the cascaded strategy extends beyond two stages, and how the approach performs under the stricter evaluation protocols used by closed-source systems.
