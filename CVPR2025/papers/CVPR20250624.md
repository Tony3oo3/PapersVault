## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image (T2I) generative models depend heavily on high-quality text-image pairs, making large-scale training expensive and limiting scalability to unannotated data. The paper argues that cross-modality text-image alignment is not a prerequisite for a strong visual generative prior, whose core role should be texture modeling, and asks whether a purely vision-based, self-supervised image-to-image (I2I) framework can serve as a more foundational prior for diverse visual synthesis tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250624/cropped_page0_idx16.webp)

## Input / Output

In the I2I pre-training stage, the model takes a single RGB image, encodes it into a latent code via a frozen autoencoder, and conditions a DiT-XL-2 diffusion transformer on visual semantic features (local patch tokens) extracted by a frozen DINO-B encoder to generate a new image in the same latent space. For downstream T2I, the conditioning switches to text embeddings from Flan-T5-XXL; for novel view synthesis, a single-view image plus camera extrinsics condition generation of new viewpoints; for image-to-video, a still input image conditions generation of subsequent video frames. The I2I model is trained on 190M filtered images, while T2I fine-tuning uses only 30M text-image pairs and 65k steps.

## Method

Lumos is a two-stage framework: (1) an I2I pre-training phase where a DiT-XL-2 latent diffusion model is trained to denoise images conditioned on frozen DINO-B patch features injected via cross-attention, learning texture distributions from 190M unannotated images in a self-supervised manner; (2) a transfer-learning phase where the I2I weights are fine-tuned to downstream tasks by swapping the conditioning mechanism (e.g., replacing image features with T5 text embeddings for T2I, or adding camera/video conditioning for NVS and I2V). A key finding is that pure-vision self-supervised encoders (DINO, MoCo) outperform language-image aligned encoders (CLIP) for I2I generation and, as 'late bloomers,' achieve better final FID on T2I transfer, suggesting that texture modeling and text-image alignment are separable concerns. Local patch features accelerate I2I convergence but increase condition dependence, while class-free guidance provides robustness during I2I inference. The I2I prior consistently outperforms T2I priors on text-irrelevant tasks (NVS, I2V) because it naturally requires no text prompt and transfers directly.

## Results

On the I2I generation task, Lumos-I2I achieves FID 4.82 on MS-COCO-30K and 2.60 on ImageNet-50K, substantially outperforming RCG (12.70 and 4.89 respectively). For T2I generation, Lumos-T2I reaches FID 12.20 on COCO-30K using only 30M text-image pairs and 65k training steps, and with long captions achieves FID 6.44, surpassing all listed T2I models including Imagen (7.27) and RAPHAEL (6.61). On text-image alignment, Lumos-T2I (0.8B params) scores 0.57 overall on GenEval and 79.9 average on DPG-Bench, matching or exceeding larger models like SDXL (0.55, 74.7) and PixArt-Σ (0.52, 79.5). On novel view synthesis, Lumos-NVS achieves PSNR 19.63/20.28 and LPIPS 0.1526/0.1317 at 0°/30° elevation, outperforming Zero123 and SyncDreamer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On I2I generation, Lumos-I2I decisively beats RCG [28] (FID 4.82 vs 12.70 on COCO-30K; 2.60 vs 4.89 on ImageNet-50K), demonstrating superior scalability on 190M unannotated images. For T2I FID, Lumos-T2I (12.20) with 30M pairs and 65k steps outperforms PixArt-α (7.32 with 24M pairs/240k steps) in data efficiency, and with long captions (6.44) surpasses Imagen (7.27, 860M pairs/5000k steps) and RAPHAEL (6.61, 5000M+ pairs). On GenEval/DPG-Bench alignment, Lumos-T2I (0.8B) beats PixArt-α (0.48/71.6), PixArt-Σ (0.52/79.5), SDv1.5 (0.43/63.2), and SDv2.1 (0.50/68.1), and is competitive with SDXL (0.55/74.7) and Playground v2.5 (0.56/75.5), though it trails the 12B FLUX models (0.67–0.71/84.0–84.8). On NVS, Lumos-NVS outperforms Zero123 [30] (PSNR 19.63 vs 17.73 at 0°), Zero123-XL [12] (19.63 vs 17.68), and SyncDreamer [31] (20.28 vs 18.98 at 30°) across all three metrics. On I2V, the I2I prior yields FVD 399.59 on UCF-101, beating the T2I prior (532.77) and no-prior baseline (2892.09), confirming I2I priors are more suitable for text-irrelevant generative tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion framework of LDM and the DiT-based training recipe of PixArt-α, but departs from the standard T2I paradigm by repositioning image-to-image generation (conditioned on self-supervised vision encoder features like DINO) as the foundational generative prior rather than a mere intermediate bridge (as in DALLE-2). It extends the self-supervised generation line of RCG by systematically studying which vision encoders, feature granularities, and data scales produce the most transferable I2I prior, and by demonstrating that this prior outperforms T2I priors on text-irrelevant downstream tasks. Conceptually, it challenges the assumption that cross-modal text-image alignment is a prerequisite for a strong visual generative model, arguing instead that texture modeling is the core competency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a strong I2I prior trained on 190M unannotated images allows T2I fine-tuning with only 30M text-image pairs (roughly 1/10 of competitors) and 65k steps to reach competitive FID, dramatically reducing the cost of building text-conditioned generators. The consistent superiority of I2I over T2I priors on novel view synthesis and image-to-video generation suggests a broader principle—pure visual priors are more natural and effective for tasks where text prompts are ill-defined or unavailable. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at much larger scales (beyond 200M images), whether the DINO-based conditioning can be replaced by learned representations to further reduce dependency on a fixed encoder, and whether the I2I prior's texture focus introduces any systematic biases that become apparent in long-horizon or compositional generation.
