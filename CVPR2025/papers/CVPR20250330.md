## Problem

Existing Visual Language Tracking (VLT) methods are confined to 2D spatial extents and cannot perceive 3D object geometry, while established 3D tracking approaches depend on expensive multi-sensor inputs (LiDAR, radar, depth cameras) that diverge from natural human perception. No prior work combines monocular video with natural language cues to perform 3D single-object tracking, leaving a gap between human-like passive visual+linguistic tracking and machine-based 3D tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250330/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video sequence and a natural language description (averaging 176 words) that encodes static attributes (color, type, dimensions, state) and dynamic attributes (truncation, occlusion, rotation, distance, direction, spatial relations) of a single target object. It outputs per-frame 3D bounding boxes (center x,y; dimensions h,w,l; orientation θ; depth d_reg), 2D bounding boxes (l,r,t,b), and an object category prediction. The language descriptions are generated by ChatGPT from extracted object attributes and then manually verified, distinguishing this from purely hand-annotated VLT datasets.

## Method

Mono3DVLT-MT is a four-stage pipeline: (1) a multi-modal feature extractor using RoBERTa for language tokens, Swin Transformer for four-level multi-scale visual features, and a MonoDETR-style depth predictor for geometric features; (2) a visual-language encoder where multiscale deformable attention (MSDA) replaces standard MHSA on visual tokens with language-guided cross-attention, while a parallel depth encoder applies language-guided self-attention to geometry tokens, and pixel-wise attention scores are computed via element-wise products of L2-normalized original and language-associated feature maps shaped by a learnable Gaussian; (3) a memory-augmented tracking decoder built on a Memory-improved Token Turing Machine (TTM) that structures memory into past/present/future blocks, reads multi-temporal context to refine a learnable query, processes it through a transformer unit, and writes the updated query back to memory for iterative cross-frame refinement; (4) a tracking head with separate MLPs for category, 2D box, 3D center, and 3D size/orientation/depth. Training uses a composite loss combining focal classification, L1 box regression, GIoU, 3D IoU, Multi-Bin orientation, and Laplacian depth uncertainty losses, with Hungarian matching for query-to-GT assignment.

## Results

On the Mono3DVLT-V2X dataset, Mono3DVLT-MT achieves an Average Overlap Rate (AOR) of 85.12%, a Success Rate at IoU 0.9 (SR@0.9) of 58.86%, a Precision Rate at 1.0 (PR@1.0) of 81.56%, and an Average Center Error (ACE) of 0.521. These represent improvements of +5.99 AOR, +9.93 SR@0.9, +5.67 PR@1.0, and −0.073 ACE over the strongest baseline (Mono3DVG-TR). The method also leads on all SR thresholds from 0.5 to 0.9, with the largest margin at SR@0.5 (+9.88) and SR@0.9 (+9.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mono3DVLT-MT outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. The strongest baseline, Mono3DVG-TR (which directly predicts 3D boxes from monocular images with language guidance), is surpassed by +9.88 SR@0.5, +5.99 AOR, +5.67 PR@1.0, and −0.073 ACE. The five 2D-grounding-plus-back-projection baselines (ZSGNet, FAOA, ReSC, TransVG, JointNLT) trail significantly, with the best among them (JointNLT+backproj) achieving only 61.40 SR@0.5 and 68.31 AOR—roughly 20 points below Mono3DVLT-MT on SR@0.5. The gap widens at stricter IoU thresholds: at SR@0.9, Mono3DVLT-MT reaches 58.86 versus 49.13 for Mono3DVG-TR and 45.27 for JointNLT+backproj. The paper attributes the advantage to explicit fusion of visual, linguistic, and geometric (depth) features within a unified end-to-end framework, whereas the back-projection baselines inherit 2D localization errors into 3D predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 2D Visual Language Tracking (VLT) and 3D object tracking, two well-studied but previously disjoint areas. It builds directly on the Token Turing Machine architecture [31] by augmenting it with temporal memory blocks for iterative query refinement, and adopts the depth prediction strategy from MonoDETR [50] for geometric feature extraction. Conceptually, it departs from prior 3D tracking work (which relies on LiDAR/radar/depth sensors) and from 2D VLT (which ignores spatial depth) by unifying language-guided tracking with monocular 3D estimation in a single end-to-end framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the first monocular-video 3D VLT benchmark with 79,158 annotated sequences, this paper opens a practical avenue for language-guided 3D tracking without expensive multi-sensor setups, which is relevant to autonomous driving and embodied AI. However, the empirical validation is limited: all baselines are 2D grounding methods with back-projection rather than native 3D trackers, the evaluation is confined to a single driving-scenario dataset (V2X-Seq), and the LLM-generated descriptions (averaging 176 words) may not reflect the brevity of real human language queries. Open questions include whether the memory-augmented TTM generalizes beyond short video clips, how performance degrades with more natural/ambiguous language, and whether the approach scales to multi-object 3D VLT.
