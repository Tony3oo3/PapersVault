## Problem

Monocular depth foundation models like Depth Anything V2 produce flickering and temporally inconsistent depth when applied to video, limiting their use in robotics, AR, and video editing. Existing remedies either depend on error-prone optical flow or camera pose priors, or repurpose video diffusion models that are computationally expensive and restricted to short clips (<10 s). No prior method achieves high-quality, temporally consistent depth for arbitrarily long videos while preserving the generalization and efficiency of the underlying image foundation model.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames of arbitrary length, processed in 32-frame windows where each new window includes 8 overlapping frames and 2 key frames from the preceding segment to maintain continuity. It outputs an affine-invariant depth map per frame with a single shared scale and shift across the entire video, so all frames in a clip are on the same depth scale. At 518×518 resolution the large model runs at ~67 ms/frame on an A100, and the small model achieves real-time 30 FPS at 9.1 ms/frame.

## Method

Video Depth Anything freezes the Depth Anything V2 (DINOv2-based ViT) encoder and replaces its DPT head with a Spatiotemporal Head that inserts four temporal self-attention + FFN layers along the temporal dimension at lower-resolution feature levels, enabling inter-frame interaction without corrupting the well-learned image representations. For training, the authors propose a Temporal Gradient Matching Loss (TGM) that penalizes the mismatch between predicted and ground-truth temporal depth gradients at the same pixel coordinate, eliminating the need for optical flow or pose priors; this is combined with the standard scale-shift-invariant spatial loss. The model is trained jointly on 730 K depth-annotated video frames (supervised) and 0.62 M unlabeled images (self-distillation from a teacher), preserving the original generalization. At inference, a key-frame referencing strategy concatenates two subsampled key frames from the previous window into each new 32-frame clip, and overlapping frames are linearly interpolated between consecutive segments to prevent scale drift and flickering over videos lasting several minutes.

## Results

On zero-shot video depth estimation, VDA-L achieves δ₁ of 0.944 (KITTI), 0.926 (ScanNet), 0.959 (Bonn), and 0.971 (NYUv2), surpassing all baselines by ~10% on KITTI, ScanNet, and Bonn. In terms of temporal consistency, VDA-L achieves a TAE of 1.7 on ScanNet (170 frames), compared to 5.0 for DepthCrafter and 5.5 for DAv2-L. The small model VDA-S runs at 9.1 ms/frame on A100 (≈30 FPS), while VDA-L runs at 67 ms/frame—only ~10% slower than the image-only DAv2-L (60 ms) and 2–14× faster than diffusion-based competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDA-L outperforms DAv2-L (the frozen image-only backbone) on every video dataset in both spatial accuracy and temporal consistency: e.g., δ₁ improves from 0.815→0.944 on KITTI and TAE drops from 5.5→1.7 on ScanNet, confirming the spatiotemporal head adds substantial value. Against the plug-and-play NVDS module (and its DAv2 variant), VDA-L wins by a wide margin (~10–30% in δ₁), showing that optical-flow-based warping constraints are inferior to the proposed temporal gradient matching loss. Compared to the diffusion-based DepthCrafter, VDA-L achieves higher δ₁ on KITTI (0.944 vs 0.753), ScanNet (0.926 vs 0.730), Bonn (0.959 vs 0.803), and NYUv2 (0.971 vs 0.822), and a 3× lower TAE (1.7 vs 5.0); the sole exception is Sintel, where DepthCrafter's δ₁ (0.695) edges out VDA-L (0.644), attributed to missing movie-style focal lengths in VDA's training data. Versus DepthAnyVideo, VDA-L wins on Sintel spatial accuracy (δ₁ 0.644 vs 0.659, AbsRel 0.295 vs 0.405) and supports arbitrarily long videos, whereas DepthAnyVideo is capped at 192 frames. In inference latency, VDA-L (67 ms) is only 7 ms slower than DAv2-L (60 ms) yet 2.4× faster than DepthAnyVideo (159 ms), 3× faster than NVDS (204 ms), 7.5× faster than ChronoDepth (506 ms), and 13.6× faster than DepthCrafter (910 ms); VDA-S reaches 9.1 ms, enabling real-time 30 FPS operation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Depth Anything V2, a state-of-the-art monocular depth foundation model trained exclusively on static images, into a video depth estimator by inserting a lightweight spatiotemporal head (four temporal self-attention layers) into the DPT head while freezing the DINOv2 encoder. It deliberately departs from two dominant prior paradigms: plug-and-play temporal stabilizers (e.g., NVDS) that depend on optical flow or camera pose priors, and video-diffusion-based methods (DepthCrafter, DepthAnyVideo, ChronoDepth) that repurpose generative models but are computationally expensive and limited to short clips. The key conceptual shift is replacing geometric or generative priors with a simple temporal gradient matching loss that constrains depth changes at fixed pixel positions, and a key-frame referencing inference strategy that enables arbitrarily long video processing without accumulated scale drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce temporally consistent depth for videos spanning several minutes (demonstrated on a 4690-frame clip) while simultaneously matching or exceeding the spatial accuracy of the underlying image model and running at near-real-time speed (9.1 ms/frame for the small model). The practical impact is significant for robotics, AR, and video editing pipelines that require long, stable depth sequences without the latency of diffusion-based approaches. Open questions include whether the affine-invariant formulation can be extended to metric depth, whether the key-frame strategy generalizes to highly dynamic scenes with large camera motion, and whether the modest Sintel gap (lacking movie-like focal lengths in training) can be closed with targeted data augmentation.
