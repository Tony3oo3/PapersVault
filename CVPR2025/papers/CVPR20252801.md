## Problem

Human recognition has been handled by separate specialized models for face and body, each requiring rigid preprocessing (5-landmark face alignment or consistent camera setups) and trained on a single dataset, making them brittle to pose, scale, and visibility variations in real-world settings. No single model has effectively unified recognition across diverse poses, visible body parts, and camera conditions, and existing body ReID models fail to generalize across different evaluation datasets.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image of a person at 384×384 resolution (white-padded if aspect ratio ≠ 1) and produces a fixed-dimension recognition feature vector. Unlike prior face or body models, the input requires no alignment, cropping, or camera-specific preprocessing — it can be a close-up portrait, a full-body shot, a back-facing image, or any intermediate pose/scale. Internally, the image is tokenized via three ROIs (whole image, upper torso, face) each with a 12×12 grid, yielding up to 432 patches, of which up to 66% are masked during training.

## Method

SapiensID combines three architectural innovations over a ViT-Base backbone with KP-RPE. (1) Retina Patch dynamically allocates more patches to semantically critical regions (face, upper torso) while guaranteeing non-overlapping full-image coverage, using Region-Sampled Position Embeddings (bilinear interpolation from a fixed 2D sin-cos PE plus a learnable ROI-level offset) to preserve spatial context. (2) Masked Recognition Model handles the resulting variable token counts by replacing masked tokens with a single learnable mask token whose attention scores are scaled by log(n_m) (the Attention Scaling Trick), enabling batched training with ~8× speedup; a variable masking rate further acts as augmentation. (3) Semantic Attention Head constructs queries from 2D position embeddings sampled at body keypoints (duplicated n times with a learnable bias), then performs cross-attention with the backbone feature map as value to pool features around each keypoint, yielding pose-invariant part features that are flattened and passed through an MLP. Training uses AdaFace margin-based softmax loss on the newly introduced WebBody4M dataset (4.9M images, 263,920 subjects) with per-dataset learnable part weights to balance short-term and long-term cues.

## Results

SapiensID achieves 73.05% average mAP on short-term ReID benchmarks (LTCC, PRCC, CCVID, Market1501, MSMT17) and 66.30% average mAP on long-term (clothing-changing) ReID, both state-of-the-art among single-model approaches. On the newly introduced Cross Pose-Scale ReID task (WebBody-test), SapiensID reaches 64.41% top-1 and 52.00% mAP, substantially outperforming prior ReID models (best prior: 5.96% top-1). On aligned face recognition benchmarks, SapiensID achieves 97.31% average accuracy, within 0.32% of the dedicated AdaFace model (97.63%), while simultaneously achieving 80.10% average on body ReID where AdaFace drops to 14.76%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SapiensID outperforms all body ReID baselines on average mAP in both short-term (73.05% vs. best prior 70.19% by SOLDIER) and long-term (66.30% vs. best prior 44.90% by HAP on WebBody4M) settings. Against CAL and CLIP3DReID, SapiensID leads by 20+ mAP points on average, reflecting the advantage of a unified architecture over single-dataset-trained models. Compared to SOLDIER (LU4M+MSMT17), SapiensID gains ~3 mAP on average while also achieving far stronger CCVID (77.82 vs. 27.76 mAP) and LTCC (34.56 vs. 36.74 mAP) results, though SOLDIER retains an edge on MSMT17 (78.01 vs. 31.02 mAP) due to its domain-specific training. Against HAP trained on the same WebBody4M dataset, SapiensID improves average mAP from 61.49% to 73.05% (short-term) and 44.90% to 66.30% (long-term), confirming that the architectural innovations (Retina Patch + SAH) are essential beyond data alone. On OccludedReID, SapiensID achieves higher top-1 (87.30% vs. 84.80%) but lower mAP (75.57% vs. 82.60%) than KPR, which was explicitly trained on occluded data. On face recognition, AdaFace retains a marginal 0.32% average accuracy edge (97.63% vs. 97.31%), but SapiensID's combined face+body average (89.80%) far exceeds AdaFace's (56.19%), demonstrating the value of a unified model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the long-standing separation of face recognition and body ReID into modality-specific pipelines, proposing a single ViT-based model that handles both. Architecturally, it builds on MAE's masking paradigm but replaces token discarding with a learnable mask token plus an attention scaling trick to handle variable token counts, and introduces Retina Patch (dynamic multi-scale patching inspired by retinal foveation) and Semantic Attention Head (keypoint-guided attention pooling) as departures from fixed grid tokenization and alignment-dependent pooling. The WebBody4M dataset follows the WebFace260M collection-and-clustering recipe but extends it to full-body images with pose and scale diversity, filling a gap left by existing ReID datasets that assume standing/walking poses and consistent camera setups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SapiensID's practical significance lies in eliminating preprocessing dependencies (face alignment, fixed camera geometry) that cause cascading failures in real-world deployment, and in providing the first large-scale dataset (4.9M images, 264K subjects) with genuine pose-scale diversity for training and evaluation. The Cross Pose-Scale ReID benchmark and WebBody-test set open a concrete research direction for robustness under viewpoint and scale variation that prior ReID benchmarks do not capture. Limitations include a notable mAP gap on OccludedReID compared to specialized occlusion methods, and the Cross Pose-Scale task still shows substantial headroom (best mAP ~52%), suggesting the unified paradigm is not yet fully mature for extreme conditions.
