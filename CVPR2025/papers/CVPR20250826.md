## Problem

In mixed-domain semi-supervised medical image segmentation, foundation models like MedSAM produce overconfident but sometimes incorrect predictions whose errors accumulate and block further improvement, while conventional models like U-Net overfit to limited labeled data under domain shift. Existing approaches treat the foundation model as a dominant, frozen supervisor, capping the conventional model's performance and failing to exploit the complementary strengths of both architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250826/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D medical image slices (384×384 for Prostate, 256×256 for Fundus/BUSI, 288×288 for M&Ms) with a small set of labeled samples from one domain and unlabeled samples from multiple mixed domains. MedSAM's input is upsampled to 512×512×L and its 128×128 output logits are interpolated to match U-Net's native W×H resolution before joint loss computation. At test time, only the student MedSAM model is retained and its output is resized to W×H for inference.

## Method

SynFoC jointly trains MedSAM (ViT-B with LoRA rank-4 on a frozen image encoder) and U-Net under a Mean Teacher paradigm, dynamically shifting dominance between the two models via an instance-wise ensemble ratio α = Φ^self × Φ^mut (Self-Mutual Confidence), where self-confidence measures student-teacher agreement and mutual confidence measures cross-model prediction overlap. Pseudo-labels from both teachers are blended at ratio α to supervise intermediate Copy-Paste samples, with a confidence threshold (τ=0.95) filtering noisy regions. A Consensus-Divergence Consistency Regularization (CDCR) applies Shannon entropy minimization on regions where both models agree (encouraging high-confidence predictions) and MSE minimization on divergent regions (aligning representational capabilities), replacing a uniform consistency loss. The overall loss combines supervised cross-entropy/Dice on labeled data with the unsupervised pseudo-label loss and the two region-specific consistency terms, weighted by a time-dependent Gaussian warm-up schedule.

## Results

On the Prostate dataset (20 labeled samples), SynFoC_MedSAM achieves 87.16% average DSC, 79.30% Jaccard, 10.26 95HD, and 4.41 ASD, outperforming the best baseline SymGD (76.85% DSC) by 10.31% in DSC. On Fundus (20 labels), SynFoC_MedSAM reaches 88.60% DSC vs. 87.66% for SymGD. On M&Ms (only 5 labels), SynFoC_MedSAM achieves 80.84% DSC vs. 78.16% for SymGD. On BUSI (129 labels), SynFoC_MedSAM obtains 74.75% DSC vs. 72.57% for CPC-SAM, the strongest foundation-model baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynFoC outperforms all 11 baselines across all four main datasets on every metric (DSC, Jaccard, 95HD, ASD). The closest competitors are the foundation-model-based methods: on Prostate, CPC-SAM (73.77% DSC) and SemiSAM (71.12%) trail SynFoC_MedSAM (87.16%) by 13.4% and 16.0% respectively; on M&Ms, CPC-SAM (76.81%) is the nearest rival at 4.0% behind. Among conventional-model baselines, SymGD (CVPR'24) is the strongest, achieving 76.85% DSC on Prostate but still 10.31% below SynFoC; FixMatch and BCP are intermediate (60.41% and 58.96%), while UA-MT, SS-Net, and CauSSL collapse under domain shift (17–25% DSC). On BUSI with 129 labels, SynFoC_MedSAM (74.75% DSC) beats CPC-SAM (72.57%) by 2.18% and SymGD (66.89%) by 7.86%. The consistent pattern is that foundation-model baselines suffer from overconfident mispredictions under domain shift, while conventional baselines overfit to the single labeled domain—SynFoC's synergistic training addresses both failure modes simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MiDSS training paradigm established by SymGD [32] (intermediate sample generation via Copy-Paste) and the Mean Teacher architecture from UA-MT [56], while adapting MedSAM via LoRA following SAMed [58]. It departs from the dominant paradigm in recent SAM-based semi-supervised methods (SemiSAM, CPC-SAM, H-SAM) that treat the foundation model as a frozen or guiding oracle, instead proposing a synergistic co-training where a lightweight U-Net dynamically corrects the foundation model's high-confidence errors. The key conceptual shift is from 'foundation model dominates' to 'mutual aid with time-varying dominance,' formalized through the Self-Mutual Confidence (SMC) ensemble ratio and the Consensus-Divergence Consistency Regularization (CDCR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.31% DSC gain on Prostate (and consistent improvements across four multi-domain datasets) demonstrates that the error-accumulation problem in foundation models under domain shift is addressable without full fine-tuning, offering a practical recipe for clinical deployment where labeled data is scarce and heterogeneous. The insight that a from-scratch conventional model can rectify high-confidence mispredictions of a large pretrained model challenges the assumption that scale guarantees reliability in distribution-shifted settings. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other foundation models (beyond MedSAM), 3D volumetric segmentation, and scenarios with more than two semantic classes, as well as the sensitivity of the SMC weighting to the choice of confidence threshold τ.
