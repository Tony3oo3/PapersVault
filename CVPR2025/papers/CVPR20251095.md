## Problem

Modern multi-agent trajectory prediction models are known to be fragile under distribution shifts, but it remains unclear whether their latent representations actually capture the causal structure of agent interactions. The CausalAgents benchmark highlighted robustness failures under non-causal agent removal, yet the paper shows this is a misleading diagnostic: existing models are already partially resilient to non-causal perturbations, while they systematically underestimate the influence of indirect causal agents (mediators). The core gap is how to explicitly regularize representations with causal annotations of varying granularity and how to transfer such causal knowledge from simulation to real-world settings where annotations are unavailable.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 time steps of joint agent states (2D positions) for the ego agent and up to 17 neighbors, and outputs a single deterministic 12-step future trajectory for the ego agent. Causal supervision is provided as either binary labels (causal vs. non-causal) or continuous per-agent causal effect values computed from ORCA counterfactual simulations (removing one agent at a time and measuring the ego trajectory displacement). In the sim-to-real setting, causal annotations come exclusively from the simulation domain; the real-world branch receives only standard trajectory reconstruction supervision, making the framework applicable without any real-world causal labels.

## Method

The approach builds on AutoBots (a sequential set transformer) and adds a metric-learning regularizer on a projection head over the encoder's latent representation. The key insight is that a causally-aware embedding should preserve the ordering of causal effects: if agent i has a smaller causal effect than agent j, the cosine distance between the factual embedding and the counterfactual embedding (with i removed) should be smaller than that with j removed. Two loss variants enforce this: a contrastive loss that pulls non-causal counterfactuals toward the factual embedding and pushes causal ones away, and a margin ranking loss that enforces pairwise ordering of embedding distances according to continuous causal effect values. For sim-to-real transfer, the framework jointly optimizes a real-world prediction loss and a simulation causal loss (L = L_pred^real + α·L_causal^syn), exploiting the domain-invariance of causal mechanisms like collision avoidance to inject causal structure into the shared encoder without any real-world causal annotations.

## Results

On the NBA rebound dataset, the proposed ranking-based causal transfer achieves ADE 0.544 m and FDE 1.235 m, outperforming the AutoBots baseline (ADE 0.562, FDE 1.271) by ~3.2% and ~2.9% respectively. On ETH-UCY, the ranking-based method with only 25% of real-world data already surpasses the AutoBots baseline trained on 100% of data, and with 50% of real data it outperforms all other methods in prediction accuracy. On the ORCA diagnostic dataset, all evaluated models show minimal ACE-NC (0.024–0.049), confirming partial robustness to non-causal perturbations, but suffer substantially higher ACE-DC and ACE-IC (e.g., AutoBots: 0.595 and 0.616), revealing a systematic underestimation of causal effects, especially indirect ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed ranking-based causal transfer outperforms all three baselines on NBA rebound: AutoBots (ADE 0.562→0.544, FDE 1.271→1.235), vanilla SimAug transfer (0.567→0.544), and CausalAgents non-causal augmentation (0.569→0.544). The contrastive variant also beats all baselines but by a smaller margin (ADE 0.550, FDE 1.248), confirming that fine-grained causal-effect annotations are more informative than binary causal/non-causal labels. On the ORCA diagnostic dataset, STGCNN achieves the lowest ACE-DC (0.330) and ACE-IC (0.354) among baselines, while Multi-TransMotion attains the best prediction accuracy (ADE 0.237, FDE 0.466) but the worst causal awareness (ACE-DC 0.723, ACE-IC 0.724), illustrating a clear accuracy–causal-awareness tradeoff. On ETH-UCY, the causal transfer framework with 50% real data surpasses both the full-data AutoBots baseline and the vanilla SimAug transfer, whereas SimAug alone actually degrades performance in low-data regimes (hypothesized to stem from non-causal style mismatches between simulation and reality). The CausalAgents augmentation strategy, which removes non-causal agents during training, provides only marginal gains and is consistently outperformed by both proposed regularizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on AutoBots (Girgis et al., ICLR 2021) as the base trajectory prediction architecture and extends the sim-to-real transfer paradigm of SimAug (Liang et al., ECCV 2020) by injecting a causal regularization task in the simulation domain. It directly critiques the CausalAgents benchmark (Roelofs et al., 2022), identifying two specific caveats—indirect causal effects overlooked in human labeling and group-removal evaluation that inflates perceived robustness—and replaces it with a counterfactual diagnostic dataset generated via ORCA simulations. The core methodological contribution is a metric-learning regularizer (contrastive or ranking) that enforces monotonicity between embedding distances and ground-truth causal effects, a formulation that departs from prior causal-invariance approaches which relied on domain labels or structural priors rather than per-agent effect annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the robustness discussion in multi-agent forecasting: the real weakness is not over-sensitivity to non-causal agents (which models already handle) but systematic underestimation of indirect causal effects through mediator agents, a gap that prior benchmarks failed to surface. The sim-to-real causal transfer result—outperforming full-data baselines with only 25% of real-world data—suggests a practical pathway for data-efficient deployment in safety-critical settings where causal annotations are unavailable. However, the authors acknowledge that supervision alone is unlikely to fully solve high-order causal reasoning, pointing toward structural inductive biases as a necessary next step, and the evaluation is limited to a single base architecture (AutoBots) and synthetic diagnostics, leaving open whether the findings generalize across model families and real-world causal structures.
