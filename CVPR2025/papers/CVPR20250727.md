## Problem

Existing multi-view and video diffusion models learn 3D consistency only implicitly through cross-frame attention, offering no explicit geometric guarantees and frequently producing 3D-inconsistent outputs. Incorporating explicit 3D inductive biases (e.g., volume rendering) imposes heavy constraints on data and architecture, making it difficult to scale to diverse, large-scale datasets. The paper asks whether a diffusion model can receive explicit 3D supervision while remaining compatible with standard 2D Transformer architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250727/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256 RGB frames (single image, unposed multi-view set, or image plus a target camera trajectory) and produces a 6D video: paired RGB and XYZ frames where each XYZ pixel encodes the global 3D coordinate of the corresponding scene point. At inference, a flexible inpainting strategy lets the user condition on any subset of the 6D channels (e.g., fix RGB and denoise XYZ for depth, or fix projected XYZ and denoise RGB for novel views). A post-optimization PnP step refines the raw XYZ predictions into calibrated camera parameters and dense depth maps.

## Method

WVD represents 3D geometry as XYZ images—texture-free images where each pixel stores the normalized global 3D coordinate—making point-cloud supervision directly compatible with 2D DiT architectures. A 2B-parameter Diffusion Transformer (rotary positional embedding, RMSNorm) jointly denoises concatenated RGB and XYZ VAE latents (channel-dimension concatenation, 2D latent channels per frame) over a sequence of N frames, trained for 1M steps at batch size 128 on a mixture of RealEstate10K, ScanNet, MVImgNet, CO3D, and Habitat. Because the model learns the joint distribution P(RGB, XYZ), a single inpainting-based inference procedure unifies monocular depth estimation, multi-view stereo, single-image-to-3D, and camera-controlled video generation without task-specific heads. The key insight is that shared XYZ values across views provide explicit, texture-free pixel correspondence, giving the diffusion process direct 3D supervision that implicit attention-based consistency cannot offer.

## Results

On monocular depth estimation (zero-shot, never trained on depth benchmarks), WVD achieves Rel=9.7 and δ₁.₂₅=90.8 on NYU-v2, and Rel=7.0 and δ₁.₂₅=96.4 on BONN, outperforming DUSt3R-224 by a wide margin on both datasets. On video depth estimation (ScanNet++), WVD achieves AbsRel=5.0 and δ₁.₀₃=57.2, competitive with DUSt3R-512 (AbsRel=4.9, δ₁.₀₃=60.2) and surpassing all other baselines. For single-image-to-3D video generation, WVD achieves the best multi-view consistency (KPM=95.8, FC=95.4) among all compared methods, though with a slightly higher FID (15.8) than CameraCtrl (12.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On single-image-to-3D video generation (Table 1), WVD outperforms both CameraCtrl and MotionCtrl on multi-view consistency (KPM: 95.8 vs. 88.6 and 68.6; FC: 95.4 vs. 94.0 and 94.6) but trails on per-frame appearance quality (FID: 15.8 vs. 12.1 and 12.9). For monocular depth estimation (Table 2), WVD beats DUSt3R-224 on both NYU-v2 (Rel 9.7 vs. 10.3) and BONN (Rel 7.0 vs. 11.1), and surpasses DUSt3R-512 on BONN (Rel 7.0 vs. 8.1, δ₁.₂₅ 96.4 vs. 93.6) despite being trained at lower resolution (256×256 vs. 512×512); however, DUSt3R-512 retains an edge on NYU-v2 (Rel 6.5 vs. 9.7). On video depth estimation (Table 3), WVD (AbsRel 5.0, δ₁.₀₃ 57.2) clearly outperforms classical and learning-based MVS methods (COLMAP, Vis-MVSSNet, MVSNet, Robust MVD) and DUSt3R-224, but is slightly behind DUSt3R-512 (AbsRel 4.9, δ₁.₀₃ 60.2) and DeepV2D on AbsRel (4.4). The ablation (WVD w/o XYZ) confirms that jointly learning XYZ frames is critical: removing them degrades KPM from 95.8 to 72.3 and FID from 15.8 to 18.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

WVD builds on the DiT-based video diffusion paradigm (following CAT3D's latent-space multi-view formulation) but departs from two dominant strategies for enforcing 3D consistency: implicit cross-view attention and explicit camera-ray conditioning. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of XYZ images—per-pixel global 3D coordinates rasterized into image format—as a texture-free geometric signal that is natively compatible with 2D Transformer architectures, eliminating the need for camera normalization or ray-map inputs. This representation choice allows WVD to jointly diffuse RGB and XYZ channels within a single DiT, inheriting the scalability of video diffusion while providing direct pixel-level 3D supervision that prior multi-view diffusion models (MVDream, SyncDreamer, ViewDiff) lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying single-image-to-3D, multi-view stereo, monocular depth estimation, and camera-controlled video generation under one pretrained model via a flexible inpainting strategy, WVD offers a practical path toward a general-purpose 3D foundation model without task-specific architectures. The ablation showing a 23-point KPM drop when XYZ channels are removed (95.8→72.3) provides strong evidence that explicit geometric supervision is substantially more effective than implicit attention for multi-view consistency. Key limitations remain: the model is restricted to static scenes at 256×256 resolution, lacks confidence modeling for unbounded/outdoor scenes, and its FID (15.8) trails CameraCtrl (12.1) in per-frame appearance quality. Extending to dynamic 4D content, higher resolutions, and additional modalities (optical flow, splatter images) are natural next steps that could broaden the framework's applicability.
