## Problem

Segment Anything Models (SAM, SAM2) suffer significant performance drops on severely degraded images (noise, blur, compression artifacts) because their latent features become excessively noisy, and the large distribution gap between low-quality and high-quality features hinders consistency-based robustness learning. Existing approaches like RobustSAM handle simple degradations but fail on complex, multi-type, or high-severity degradations commonly encountered in real-world scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image and visual prompts (default 3 point clicks) and outputs a binary segmentation mask. Internally, the image is encoded by SAM's frozen image encoder into a 256-channel latent feature map, which is then enhanced in-place via a single-step generative denoising operation before being passed to the mask decoder. The approach adds only 47 MB of learnable parameters and incurs a 0.06 s inference overhead over vanilla SAM.

## Method

GleSAM treats SAM's low-quality latent features as a noisy observation of the corresponding high-quality features and reconstructs them via a single-step denoising at a fixed diffusion timestep T, using a pre-trained U-Net from Stable Diffusion 2.1 as the denoising backbone. Two compatibility techniques bridge the gap between the LDM and segmentation spaces: Feature Distribution Alignment (FDA) scales features by a learnable weight γ (default 5) to match the VAE latent variance, and Channel Replicate and Expansion (CRE) replicates and concatenates the pre-trained 4-channel head/tail weights to match SAM's 256-channel latent dimension while keeping them frozen. The U-Net is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 8) on its attention layers using an MSE reconstruction loss against ground-truth HQ features, followed by a second stage that fine-tunes SAM's mask decoder with Dice + Focal loss. Training requires only 4×A100 GPUs for ~30 hours (100K U-Net iterations + 20K decoder iterations), and the framework is applicable to both SAM and SAM2 without modifying their original segmentation architecture.

## Results

On the most challenging LQ-3 degradation level, GleSAM2 achieves 79.47% IoU on ThinObject-5K and 57.38% IoU on LVIS, outperforming SAM2 (71.74% and 51.18%) by 7.7 and 6.2 points respectively. On the seen LVIS dataset at LQ-3, GleSAM reaches 55.35% IoU versus 40.41% for SAM and 45.17% for RobustSAM. On unseen datasets (ECSSD, COCO), GleSAM2 similarly leads with 73.35% and 57.24% IoU at LQ-3, surpassing DiffBIR-SAM2 (71.58% and 56.80%). The method also generalizes to RobustSeg-style degradations it was never trained on, achieving 86.11% IoU on ECSSD versus 84.27% for RobustSAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GleSAM and GleSAM2 consistently outperform all five baselines across every degradation level and dataset. Against the direct SAM-based competitor RobustSAM, GleSAM gains +5.8 IoU on ThinObject-5K LQ-3 (0.7594 vs 0.7015) and +10.2 IoU on LVIS LQ-3 (0.5535 vs 0.4517), with the gap widening as degradation severity increases. The two-stage restoration baselines (PromptIR-SAM/SAM2) actually underperform the unmodified SAM/SAM2 on several settings, suggesting that pixel-space restoration introduces artifacts that harm segmentation; DiffBIR-SAM/SAM2 is the strongest baseline but still trails GleSAM2 by 5.99 IoU on ThinObject-5K LQ-3 and 0.87 IoU on LVIS LQ-3. Notably, on RobustSeg-style degradations the model was never trained on, GleSAM (85.68% IoU on ECSSD) surpasses RobustSAM (84.27%), which was explicitly trained on those degradations, demonstrating superior generalization to unseen corruption types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM and SAM2 as the segmentation backbone and repurposes the pre-trained U-Net from Stable Diffusion (a Latent Diffusion Model) not for image generation or as a perception backbone, but as a single-step denoiser operating within SAM's own latent feature space. It departs from RobustSAM's distillation-based consistency learning and from two-stage pipelines (restore-then-segment with IR networks like DiffBIR) by performing generative enhancement in the feature domain rather than the pixel domain. The use of LoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation and the Channel Replication and Expansion trick for bridging the 4-channel U-Net to 256-channel SAM latents are practical engineering contributions that distinguish this work from prior diffusion-for-perception efforts that typically retrain or heavily modify the diffusion backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAM is a foundational model underpinning a large ecosystem of downstream applications (medical imaging, remote sensing, autonomous driving), so its documented fragility on degraded inputs is a significant practical bottleneck that this work addresses with a lightweight, plug-in solution (47 MB learnable parameters, 30 h on 4 GPUs). The LQSeg dataset with multi-type, multi-level degradations provides a more realistic and comprehensive benchmark than prior single-degradation evaluations. Open questions include whether the single-step denoising generalizes to other foundation-model architectures beyond SAM, whether the approach extends naturally to video segmentation (SAM2's domain), and whether the paired HQ-LQ training requirement can be relaxed for truly blind scenarios.
