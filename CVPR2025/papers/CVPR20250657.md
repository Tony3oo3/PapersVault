## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods face a dual challenge: they require substantial storage due to multi-dimensional attributes on millions of Gaussians, and they struggle to represent complex real-world motions that combine global and local movements within a single unified model. While compact anchor-based representations (e.g., Scaffold-GS) reduce storage for static scenes, extending them to dynamic videos with long-duration, large-motion content remains unsolved without sacrificing rendering quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250657/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular video sequences (single-camera footage) along with a COLMAP-initialized point cloud, and produces a compact dynamic 3D Gaussian model that renders novel views in real-time via alpha-compositing. Notably, the temporal segmentation of the video into local canonical segments is determined automatically during training via gradient-based adjustment, requiring no precomputed optical flow, tracking, or external motion annotations. The resulting model achieves roughly 70% average storage reduction over SOTA dynamic 3DGS methods while matching or exceeding their rendering quality.

## Method

MoDec-GS introduces Global-to-Local Motion Decomposition (GLMD), a two-stage deformation pipeline built on an anchor-based canonical representation (Scaffold-GS). In the first stage, Global Anchor Deformation (GAD) queries a tiny global hexplane to deform anchor positions and local context features, transforming a Global Canonical Scaffold into a Local Canonical Scaffold dedicated to a specific temporal segment; learnable anchor dynamics (a binary mask d_G for global motion and a scalar d_L for local activation) control which anchors move. In the second stage, Local Gaussian Deformation (LGD) explicitly deforms the reconstructed 3D Gaussians using a shared local multi-resolution hexplane, capturing fine-grained residual motion within each segment. Temporal Interval Adjustment (TIA) then adaptively shrinks temporal segments with high accumulated positional gradients during training, automatically allocating more representational capacity to high-motion regions without any external motion signal.

## Results

On the Dycheck-iPhone dataset (primary evaluation), MoDec-GS achieves an average PSNR of 14.60 dB, SSIM of 0.480, and LPIPS of 0.443, while using only 18.37 MB of storage — a 92% reduction compared to SC-GS (232.4 MB) and a 77% reduction compared to 4DGS (78.54 MB). On HyperNeRF, it attains the best PSNR (27.78 dB) and SSIM (0.827) with 40.82 MB storage (82% less than SC-GS). On the Nvidia monocular dataset, it outperforms 4DGS on all three quality metrics (PSNR 26.65 vs 25.82, SSIM 0.876 vs 0.844, LPIPS 0.171 vs 0.219) while using 41% less storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoDec-GS outperforms all three baselines on PSNR and SSIM across all three datasets. On Dycheck-iPhone, it gains +0.70 dB PSNR over SC-GS (14.60 vs 13.90) while reducing storage by 92% (18.37 vs 232.4 MB); it also beats Deformable 3DGS by +0.88 dB and 4DGS by +0.88 dB in PSNR. On HyperNeRF, MoDec-GS achieves the best PSNR (27.78) and SSIM (0.827), surpassing 4DGS (27.44 / 0.797) and SC-GS (26.95 / 0.815), with LPIPS (0.219) nearly matching SC-GS (0.213) at 18% of its storage. On the Nvidia monocular dataset, MoDec-GS dominates 4DGS on every metric (PSNR +0.83, SSIM +0.032, LPIPS −0.048) while using 41% less storage. The only metric where MoDec-GS does not achieve the absolute best is LPIPS on iPhone (0.443 vs Deformable 3DGS's 0.430), a marginal 0.013 gap. Overall, the paper demonstrates that the Global-to-Local Motion Decomposition and Temporal Interval Adjustment enable superior rendering quality at a fraction of the storage cost of prior dynamic 3DGS methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoDec-GS combines two established lines of work: the deformation-based dynamic rendering pipeline of 4DGS (Wu et al., CVPR 2024), which uses a hexplane to deform canonical Gaussians, and the anchor-based compact representation of Scaffold-GS (Lu et al., CVPR 2024), which predicts Gaussian attributes from sparse anchor features. The key conceptual departure is the Global-to-Local Motion Decomposition (GLMD), which splits deformation into a global anchor-level stage (GAD) and a local Gaussian-level stage (LGD), motivated by the observation that natural motion has hierarchical structure. Additionally, the Temporal Interval Adjustment (TIA) mechanism differs from prior temporal segmentation approaches like SWINGS (Shaw et al., ECCV 2024) by integrating interval optimization directly into training via gradient accumulation, eliminating the need for precomputed optical flow or empirical interval selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~70% average storage reduction while matching or exceeding SOTA rendering quality makes compact dynamic 3DGS practically viable for mobile and streaming applications where memory is constrained. The TIA mechanism is notable for removing the dependency on external motion estimation (optical flow, tracking) that prior segmentation-based methods require, simplifying the pipeline. However, the method still requires offline training per video, the TIA heuristic (gradient-threshold-based shrinking) lacks theoretical grounding, and evaluation is limited to monocular sequences of moderate length. Open questions include whether the two-stage decomposition generalizes to very long or highly non-stationary videos, and whether the learnable anchor dynamics (d_G, d_L) can be replaced by more interpretable motion priors.
