## Problem

Existing radiance field deblurring methods rely on linear blur models that fail under real-world non-linear degradations (saturated pixels, sensor noise, in-camera processing), lack sharp-image priors (causing failure when all views share similar blur directions), and require multiple ray samples per pixel leading to slow training. The paper asks whether DNN-based deblurring networks—which handle non-linearities and operate in a single feedforward pass—can be effectively integrated with radiance field construction to overcome these limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252423/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of M multi-view blurred RGB images (with camera motion blur or defocus blur, potentially containing noise and saturated pixels) and produces a sharp radiance field from which novel views are rendered. Camera poses are estimated via COLMAP at each iteration rather than provided as ground truth. The deblurring networks are pre-trained on BlurRF-Synth (4,350 blurred-sharp pairs across 150 scenes), and the full pipeline runs N=5 iterations, with the final output being a radiance field parameterized either as Plenoxels voxel grids or 3D Gaussians.

## Method

DeepDeblurRF alternates between radiance field construction and RF-guided deblurring over N iterations. The key mechanism is RF-guided deblurring: at each iteration, the current radiance field is rendered at the input camera poses to produce guidance images that aggregate multi-view information, and a modified NAFNet (with its first layer accepting a concatenation of the rendered image and the original blurred image) uses this guidance to produce higher-quality deblurred images than single-image deblurring alone. The first iteration uses standard single-image NAFNet deblurring, after which COLMAP re-estimates poses and the radiance field is retrained on the improved deblurred images. This iterative loop progressively refines both the deblurred images and the radiance field, with gains diminishing after N=5. The framework is representation-agnostic, demonstrated with both Plenoxels (DeepDeblurRF-P) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (DeepDeblurRF-G).

## Results

On the BlurRF-Synth test set, DeepDeblurRF-G achieves the best novel-view synthesis quality across both blur types: PSNR 29.94 / SSIM 0.8681 / LPIPS 0.1059 for camera motion blur and PSNR 32.58 / SSIM 0.9060 / LPIPS 0.0774 for defocus blur. The voxel-grid variant DeepDeblurRF-P also leads its category with PSNR 29.81 (motion) and 32.51 (defocus). Computation time is dramatically reduced: DeepDeblurRF-G completes in 0.28 hours versus 30.00 hours for DP-NeRF and 31.33 hours for Deblur-NeRF, while DeepDeblurRF-P takes 1.14 hours versus 4.26 hours for PDRF-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepDeblurRF outperforms all seven baselines on every metric for both camera motion blur and defocus blur. For camera motion blur, DeepDeblurRF-G (PSNR 29.94) surpasses the strongest MLP-based baseline DP-NeRF (28.03) by ~1.9 dB and the strongest voxel-grid baseline PDRF-10 (28.33) by ~1.6 dB, while also being 107× faster than DP-NeRF. For defocus blur, the gap widens: DeepDeblurRF-G (PSNR 32.58) beats DP-NeRF (30.15) by 2.4 dB and PDRF-10 (30.03) by 2.5 dB. Among 3D Gaussian methods, DeepDeblurRF-G improves over BAGS (27.41) by 2.5 dB on motion blur and over Deblurring-3DGS (26.30) by 3.6 dB, while also being faster than both. On the challenging BlurRF-SB test set where all views share the same blur direction, DeepDeblurRF-G achieves PSNR 29.59 versus 27.07 for DP-NeRF, confirming that DNN-based priors are essential when complementary multi-view information is unavailable. On the low-light BlurRF-Real dataset, DeepDeblurRF-G attains the best no-reference quality scores (NIQE 5.423, ARNIQA 0.329), outperforming BAGS (NIQE 5.837) and PDRF-10 (NIQE 6.243).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of radiance field deblurring methods (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF, ExBluRF, BAGS) that model blur through linear convolution kernels jointly optimized with the scene representation. Instead, it repurposes pre-trained DNN-based single-image deblurring networks (specifically NAFNet) as the deblurring backbone, which inherently handles non-linear outliers like saturated pixels and sensor noise that violate the linear blur assumption. The key conceptual bridge is the RF-guided deblurring scheme: since single-image deblurring alone is insufficient (as noted by Ma et al.), the paper uses multi-view information aggregated in a radiance field to guide a second, higher-quality deblurring pass, iterated in an alternating loop with RF construction. This positions the work at the intersection of learned image restoration and 3D scene reconstruction, rather than extending either line independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~27× reduction in training time (1.14 hr vs. 31.33 hr for Deblur-NeRF) and the ability to handle real-world non-linear degradations that fundamentally break prior linear-model approaches. The BlurRF-Synth dataset (4,350 blurred-sharp pairs across 150 scenes) is the first large-scale resource for training and evaluating RF deblurring, filling a gap that limited prior work to small evaluation-only sets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to blur severities beyond the training distribution, whether incorporating cross-view priors or depth maps into the RF-guided deblurring step would further improve quality, and whether the iterative framework's gains saturate or could be accelerated with learned schedules.
