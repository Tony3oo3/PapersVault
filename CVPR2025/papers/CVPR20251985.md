## Problem

Existing responsible text-to-image generation methods handle fairness and safety concepts in a discretized, individual manner, lack interpretability, and often require modifying the original model, which degrades its performance. There is no scalable, plug-and-play approach that simultaneously controls multiple responsible concepts (race, gender, age, safety) in a continuous, interpretable space while preserving the base model's generative quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and a user-defined set of responsible concepts A_X (e.g., {race, gender, age, safe}) as conditioning, and produces a single generated RGB image from the underlying Stable Diffusion v1.4 pipeline. The two plug-in modules (RICE and RIIDL) are additively injected at the text-encoder embedding and U-Net latent stages respectively, so the base pipeline architecture and weights remain frozen. At inference, per-concept influence weights gamma_k and dual-space blending factors lambda_E, lambda_D allow the user to dial in the strength and combination of responsible attributes without retraining.

## Method

The approach distills two lightweight student modules from the frozen T2I pipeline: RICE (Responsible Interpretable CLIP Embedding) learns a continuous responsible embedding space by minimizing an L2 knowledge-distillation loss against the CLIP text encoder, and RIIDL (Responsible Interpretable Intermediate Diffusion Latents) does the same against the U-Net's intermediate latents across uniformly sampled denoising time-steps with SNR-weighted loss. Both distilled representations are then decorrelated via concept whitening (a covariance-based linear transform) to yield interpretable, disentangled concept directions. At inference, the original prompt embedding or latent is modulated by additively injecting weighted responsible concept vectors, and the two modules can be combined in a dual-space integration where lambda_E * D(E_resp(t)) + lambda_D * D_resp(E(t)) blends embedding-space and latent-space control. This design is fully plug-and-play: no base-model parameters are updated, and the concept set A_X is extendable to arbitrary semantically meaningful attributes beyond the core fairness/safety set.

## Results

On the WinoBias fairness benchmark, the proposed method achieves a deviation ratio of 0.04–0.05 for gender bias and 0.07–0.14 for race bias across five professions, substantially outperforming Stable Diffusion (0.30–1.00 gender, 0.26–0.92 race), Unified Concept Editing (0.06–0.39 gender), and Vector-SD (0.08–0.96 gender). Averaged over all 35 WinoBias professions, the method reaches a deviation ratio of 0.05±0.02, compared to 0.31 for the strongest prior method (TIMEP) and 0.67 for the SD baseline. On the I2P safety benchmark, the method achieves an average inappropriateness proportion of 0.20, matching SLD (0.20) and outperforming V-SD (0.27), ESD (0.32), and SD (0.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on fair generation. Against Stable Diffusion (SD), the deviation ratio drops from 0.30–1.00 (gender) to 0.04–0.05, a reduction of 85–96%. Compared to Unified Concept Editing (UCE/Gandikota et al. 2024), the method achieves 0.04–0.05 vs. UCE's 0.06–0.39 on gender, and 0.07–0.14 vs. UCE's 0.07–0.25 on race. Vector-SD (Li et al. 2024) is the closest competitor on some individual professions (e.g., 0.08 for Doctor gender) but degrades severely on others (0.96 for Nurse gender), while the proposed method maintains 0.04 uniformly. On the 35-profession average (Table 2), the method achieves 0.05 vs. TIMEP (0.31), TIME (0.44), Concept Algebra (0.43), and Debias-VL (0.55). On safe generation (I2P), the method ties SLD at 0.20 average inappropriateness and outperforms V-SD (0.27), ESD (0.32), and SD (0.41), with the largest gains in Sexual (0.15 vs. 0.39) and Illegal (0.14 vs. 0.35) categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the discretized single-concept vector approach of Vector-SD [23] and the model-modification strategies of unlearning/editing methods (UCE, ESD, TIME) by learning a continuous composite responsible space via knowledge distillation conditioned on a user-defined concept set. It repurposes concept whitening [9]—originally developed for interpretable image classification—to disentangle multi-facet responsibility concepts within both the CLIP text-embedding space and the diffusion U-Net latent space. The dual-space plug-and-play framing (RICE for embeddings, RIIDL for latents) is the key architectural departure: prior work either targets one space or modifies the base model, whereas here both add-on modules are trained independently and combined at inference without altering the frozen T2I pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play dual-space design offers a practical path to responsible T2I generation that avoids the performance degradation and retraining cost of model-editing or unlearning approaches, and the continuous composite space enables simultaneous multi-concept control (e.g., race + gender + age) that prior discretized methods handle only individually. However, the evaluation is confined to Stable Diffusion v1.4, and the multiple weighting hyperparameters (λ_E, λ_D, γ_k, α) introduce a tuning burden that limits out-of-the-box usability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), how it scales to dozens of concepts without degrading interpretability, and the acknowledged misuse risk where A_resp could be swapped for an irresponsible concept set.
