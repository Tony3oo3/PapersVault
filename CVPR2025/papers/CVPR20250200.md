## Problem

Event-based video frame interpolation struggles with fast non-linear motion because events are spatially sparse yet temporally continuous, while images are spatially dense yet temporally discrete. Existing methods either estimate only sparse flow from events or fuse both modalities for dense flow, but the temporal misalignment between continuous event motion cues and discrete image spatial information introduces motion errors that degrade interpolation quality, particularly in high-speed, non-linear scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250200/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of boundary RGB frames and the asynchronous event stream recorded between them; events are voxelized into tensors with a configurable temporal bin size (0.001–0.01 s) before network consumption. It produces intermediate frames at any arbitrary timestamp between the boundaries along with a dense any-time bidirectional optical flow field, supporting multi-frame interpolation (e.g., 7-skip, 15-skip) via selective timestamp sampling rather than recursive frame-by-frame generation. Training crops are 256×256 pixels.

## Method

TimeTracker reframes dense any-time optical flow estimation as a local feature tracking problem by exploiting the spatial continuity of object motion. A Scene-Aware Region Segmentation module uses SLIC superpixels to partition the image into locally appearance-consistent patches, filtered by a motion mask derived from morphologically closed event trigger positions. A Continuous Trajectory guided Motion Estimation module initializes SIFT-based query points per superpixel, extracts multi-scale features (scales 4–32) from events and images separately, computes 4D local correlation volumes, and tracks trajectories with a CoTracker-style transformer using sliding windows over continuous time. A global RAFT-based optimization module then refines the coarse tracking flow via a self-supervised consistency loss that enforces agreement between tracking-derived flow and flow estimated from the interpolated frames. Finally, bidirectional warping with confidence-weighted fusion generates the intermediate frame, and a U-shaped refinement network repairs occluded regions using event and boundary-frame cues. Training is two-stage: the tracker is pre-trained on Tap-Vid (events synthesized via ESIM) and fine-tuned on Multiflow, then frozen while the full VFI pipeline is trained on GoPro.

## Results

On the GoPro 7-skips benchmark, TimeTracker achieves 37.13 PSNR / 0.962 SSIM, outperforming the closest competitor TimeLens-XL (37.02 / 0.959) and CBMNet (36.86 / 0.955). The advantage grows substantially on harder settings: on SNU-FILM Extreme, TimeTracker reaches 29.27 PSNR / 0.915 SSIM versus 27.93 / 0.894 for TimeLens-XL and 27.56 / 0.884 for CBMNet. On the new CHMD dataset with fast nonlinear motion (15 skips), TimeTracker scores 27.69 PSNR / 0.805 SSIM, a 2.92 dB PSNR gain over TimeLens-XL (24.77 / 0.732), the largest margin observed across all evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeTracker outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and skip setting. Against the strongest event-based competitor TimeLens-XL [13], the margin is modest on GoPro 7-skips (+0.11 dB PSNR) but widens to +1.91 dB on SNU-FILM Extreme and +2.92 dB on CHMD 15-skips, indicating the tracking-based approach scales better with motion complexity. Frame-based methods (SuperSlomo, PerVFI, VFIT-B) trail by 4–9 dB PSNR on GoPro 7-skips, confirming that image-only flow estimation is fundamentally limited under large displacements. Among event-based methods, TimeLens [12] (sparse event-only flow) and SuperFast [17] (synthesis-based) are consistently 2–4 dB below TimeTracker, while CBMNet [23] (fused image+event flow) is the closest runner-up on GoPro but still loses by 0.27 dB on 7-skips and 1.91 dB on SNU-FILM Extreme. The largest relative gains appear on the authors' own CHMD dataset, where the fast nonlinear motion (200 RPM turntable) amplifies the weaknesses of linear-motion assumptions and synthesis-based flow estimation in all prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeTracker repurposes the continuous point-tracking paradigm from CoTracker and local all-pair correspondence methods, originally designed for long-term video tracking, and adapts it to the VFI setting by combining SLIC superpixel segmentation (for spatial initialization) with event-based trajectory tracking (for temporal continuity). This departs from the dominant event-based VFI lineage (TimeLens, TimeLens-XL, CBMNet) that estimates optical flow either directly from events or by fusing event and image features, and from frame-based methods (SuperSlomo, PerVFI) that assume linear inter-frame motion. The key conceptual shift is reframing any-time dense optical flow estimation as a local feature tracking problem, leveraging the spatial richness of images to define tracking templates and the temporal richness of events to trace their trajectories, thereby avoiding the correlation-matching errors inherent in global cost-volume computation over sparse event data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness in event-based VFI: the spatial sparsity of events makes dense flow estimation ill-posed, and prior work (notably TimeLens-XL) introduces a chicken-and-egg dependency where flow accuracy depends on frame synthesis quality. The tracking-based formulation sidesteps this by never requiring intermediate frame synthesis for flow estimation. The introduced CHMD dataset fills a gap in evaluation benchmarks for fast, non-linear motion (e.g., 200 RPM turntable), which existing datasets like BS-ERGB and GoPro do not adequately stress-test. A clear limitation is dynamic texture scenes (fluids, splashing water) where tracking templates lose identity over time, forcing a fallback to synthesis-based refinement; the authors acknowledge this and point toward event-based fluid measurement (EBOS, EBIV) as a future direction.
