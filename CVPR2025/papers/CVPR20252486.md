## Problem

Existing DETR-like models employ a cascaded decoder architecture that constrains object query updates to a single sequential direction, limiting the diversity of information queries can extract from image features. This limitation is particularly detrimental for challenging detection scenarios involving extremely-small, heavily-occluded, or background-confused objects that demand comprehensive feature utilization. The paper asks whether a parallel decoder architecture can enable queries to learn multi-pattern information simultaneously rather than sequentially.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252486/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image as input, which is processed by a backbone (ResNet-50 or Swin-L) and an L-layer transformer encoder to produce multi-scale feature tokens. The output consists of bounding boxes and category predictions for up to 300 object queries, produced end-to-end without NMS post-processing. The MI decoder operates on the same query count as standard DETR variants, with the parallelism occurring within each decoder layer rather than across additional layers.

## Method

The core contribution is the parallel Multi-time Inquiries (MI) decoder, where each of the L decoder layers contains M parameters-independent inquiry heads (each comprising self-attention, cross-attention, and FFN) that process the same input queries in parallel, followed by a concatenation-and-linear-projection fusion to combine the M resulting query groups. A complementary U-like Feature Interaction (UFI) module routes encoder features from layer j = L−i+1 as Key&Value to the i-th decoder layer, enabling multi-level feature access analogous to U-Net skip connections. A Lite-MI variant shares the self-attention layer across inquiry heads to reduce parameters while retaining the parallel cross-attention diversity. The architecture is designed as a drop-in replacement for the standard transformer decoder in any DETR-like model, validated by plugging into both DINO and Relation-DETR with consistent AP gains of +0.6 to +1.2.

## Results

On COCO val2017 with ResNet-50 backbone, MI-DETR achieves 52.4 AP at 12 epochs and 52.7 AP at 24 epochs, outperforming all existing DETR-like models. With the stronger Swin-L backbone, it reaches 58.2 AP at 12 epochs. The method improves over the SOTA Relation-DETR by +0.7 AP (12 epochs) and +0.6 AP (24 epochs) under ResNet-50, and by +0.4 AP under Swin-L, while surpassing the widely-used DINO baseline by +3.4 AP (12 epochs, ResNet-50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MI-DETR outperforms all 11 compared DETR-like baselines across every backbone and training schedule tested. Against the SOTA Relation-DETR [12], it gains +0.7 AP (12 epochs) and +0.6 AP (24 epochs) on ResNet-50, and +0.4 AP on Swin-L, with the largest margin on small objects (AP_S: +0.6 to +1.3). Versus the widely-used DINO [44] baseline, improvements are +3.4 AP (12 epochs, ResNet-50) and +1.4 AP (12 epochs, Swin-L). Among other recent methods, MI-DETR leads DDQ-DETR [45] by +1.1 AP (12 epochs) and +0.7 AP (24 epochs) on ResNet-50, Stable-DINO [21] by +2.0 AP (12 epochs), Rank-DETR [29] by +2.0 AP (12 epochs), and H-DETR [15] by +3.7 AP (12 epochs). Notably, plugging MI into Relation-DETR at 12 epochs (52.4 AP) surpasses Relation-DETR trained for 24 epochs (52.1 AP), indicating faster convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DETR family, specifically DINO and Relation-DETR, by replacing their cascaded transformer decoder layers with a parallel Multi-time Inquiries (MI) decoder. Conceptually, it reframes the standard SA+CA+FFN decoder layer as an 'inquiry head' and argues that the cascaded update direction constrains object queries to learn only limited information patterns, drawing an analogy to how CNNs use parallel branches (Inception, Wide ResNet) to extract multi-pattern features. The paper explicitly distinguishes its parameter-independent parallel design from the parameter-sharing parallel architectures in H-DETR and Group-DETR, which it terms 'pseudo parallel' because shared parameters force all query groups to learn the same information pattern. A complementary U-like Feature Interaction module, inspired by U-Net skip connections, feeds encoder features at each layer to the corresponding decoder layer rather than using only the final encoder output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MI mechanism is simple, plug-and-play, and achieves the best reported AP among DETR-like models on COCO (52.7 AP with ResNet-50 at 24 epochs), demonstrating that the cascaded decoder architecture has left feature utilization on the table. The finding that simply adding more cascaded decoder layers degrades performance (47.3 AP at 24 layers vs. 49.0 at 6 layers) provides empirical support for the paper's core argument about cascaded-direction constraints. However, the improvements over the strongest baseline (Relation-DETR) are modest (+0.6 AP), and the paper does not explore whether the MI mechanism generalizes beyond COCO or to other detection tasks (e.g., instance segmentation, video detection). An open question is whether the optimal number of inquiry heads and the fusion strategy scale gracefully with model size or dataset complexity.
