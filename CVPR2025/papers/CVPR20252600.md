## Problem

Traditional point-and-shoot cameras handle focus and exposure but offer no guidance on composition, and existing automatic image composition methods are post-processing cropping operations applied to already-captured images. No prior system can guide a user to adjust camera pose in real time to achieve a better composition of a live scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252600/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single perspective-projected view image (generated from a 360° equirectangular panorama with a known camera pose) and produces two outputs: a binary suggestion indicating whether the current composition can be improved, and, conditionally, a 2D vector of camera pose adjustment angles (Δθ for yaw rotation around the vertical axis, Δφ for pitch rotation around the horizontal axis). The roll angle is assumed fixed, and the adjustment angles are bounded to [−180°, 180°] for both axes. The underlying dataset (PCARD) provides 320K such views from 4000 scenes, each with 81 candidate views at 5° sampling intervals on the sphere.

## Method

The pipeline has three components. First, a CLIP-based Composition Quality Assessment (CCQA) model assigns pseudo composition scores to views using learnable continuous text embeddings over five quality levels {bad, poor, fair, good, perfect}, with RoIAlign-extracted sub-view features, residual feature adapters, and a multi-task loss combining regression, pairwise ranking, and cosine-similarity consistency terms. Second, these scores drive a score-guided labeling procedure that generates binary suggestion labels and adjustment angle labels via an adaptive top-25% threshold per scene. Third, the Camera Pose Adjustment Model (CPAM) uses a multi-gate mixture-of-experts architecture with a ResNet-50 backbone, where task-specific softmax gates route shared features through M=2 experts to handle the sequential dependency between the binary suggestion task (cross-entropy loss) and the conditional adjustment regression task (cosine-similarity + norm loss), with a gated indicator function ensuring adjustment gradients propagate only for positive samples.

## Results

On the CPC dataset (unseen at training time), CCQA achieves the best generalization across all metrics: top-5 accuracy of 56.1% and top-10 accuracy of 72.6%, outperforming the strongest baseline by ~5 and ~6 points respectively. For the CPAM model on PCARD, the optimal 2-expert configuration yields 79.3% AUC for the suggestion predictor and 0.415 cosine similarity / 0.507 MAE for the adjustment predictor. In subjective user studies, the system correctly identifies when no adjustment is needed 82% of the time and produces a composition improvement 64% of the time when an adjustment is suggested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCQA is compared against three composition scoring baselines—TransView, GAICv2, and SFRC—all of which were originally designed for image cropping tasks (hence the asterisk indicating RoIAlign was removed to adapt them). CCQA wins decisively on every metric: it leads TransView by 5.0 points on Acc̄₅ (56.1 vs 51.1) and 6.2 points on Acc̄₁₀ (72.6 vs 66.4), and leads on the weighted variants by 3.0–3.1 points. The three baselines are tightly clustered (within ~0.6 points of each other on Acc̄₅ and ~0.6 on Acc̄₁₀), suggesting that standard cropping-based scoring models struggle to generalize to the subtle quality differences present in near-identical panoramic views. CCQA's learnable text embedding approach provides a clear and consistent advantage, validating its use as the pseudo-labeler for the PCARD dataset. No direct CPAM comparison against external camera-pose baselines is reported, as this is the first system to address live camera pose adjustment for composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established image composition literature, which treats composition as a post-capture cropping problem (GAICv2, CPC, FCDB, etc.), by reframing it as a pre-capture camera pose adjustment task. It builds on the CLIP-based image quality assessment paradigm (Q-Align, BLIP-based IQA) but introduces learnable continuous text embeddings to handle the fine-grained quality distinctions needed for near-identical views. The mixture-of-experts architecture with a gated indicator loss for sequential multi-task learning is a targeted adaptation of MoE to handle the asymmetric sample sets inherent in the suggest-then-adjust pipeline, rather than a general MoE contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PCARD dataset (320K images with explicit camera pose from 360° panoramas) is the first of its kind and opens a new research direction on pose-aware composition that prior cropping-based work cannot address. Key limitations include reliance on pseudo-labels from a CCQA model trained on cropping datasets (introducing potential bias), restriction to rotational adjustments only (no translation or roll), and evaluation limited to a single held-out split of PCARD. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to scenes requiring positional changes, how it performs with real user interaction latency, and whether the pseudo-labeling pipeline can be replaced with direct human annotation at scale.
