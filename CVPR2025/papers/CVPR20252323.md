## Problem

Existing Gaussian human avatar methods face a fundamental trade-off: single-MLP approaches achieve real-time rendering (30–60 fps) but lack the learning capacity to capture high-frequency pose-dependent details, while large-network methods like AnimatableGaussians (StyleUNet) reconstruct the finest details but degrade to ~10 fps due to heavy per-frame network inference. This paper asks whether high-fidelity pose-dependent appearance and real-time rendering can be achieved simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252323/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (16–39 views at 1K–2K resolution), pre-computed SMPL-X pose registrations, and a canonical template mesh (extracted via AnimatableGaussians). The pose vector fed to the MLPs excludes finger joints, and novel poses are projected into the training pose manifold via PCA before inference. The output is a set of 200K Gaussians parameterized by neutral properties plus 15 offset basis vectors, rendered at 166 fps on a single NVIDIA 3090 without CUDA or TensorRT acceleration.

## Method

The core architecture places F=300 spatially distributed MLPs at anchor points on the template mesh, each taking only the pose vector as input (not per-Gaussian positions) and outputting anchor coefficients. Gaussian coefficients for the offset basis are obtained by inverse-distance interpolation from the three nearest anchor points, so each MLP is evaluated once per pose rather than once per Gaussian. The key insight is that while interpolated coefficients vary smoothly across the body, the B=15 Gaussian offset basis vectors are learned freely without smoothness constraints, so their linear combination can still produce sharply varying property offsets that capture high-frequency spatial signals. A separate set of C=10K control points on the mesh constrains Gaussian position offsets via interpolation, preventing Gaussians from drifting inside the body and ensuring surface-layer distribution for better novel-pose generalization. Training jointly optimizes MLPs, neutral properties, offset basis, and control-point offsets using L1 + LPIPS + control-point smoothness + scale-regularization losses over 800K iterations.

## Results

Under training poses, the proposed method achieves PSNR 32.75, SSIM 0.9868, LPIPS 0.0226, and FID 10.12, outperforming AnimatableGaussians (PSNR 31.30, FID 11.34) by ~1.4 dB in PSNR. Under novel poses, it reaches PSNR 28.33 and FID 19.02, again surpassing AnimatableGaussians (PSNR 28.11, FID 19.23). In terms of speed, the method renders at 166 FPS with 200K Gaussians (vs. 10 FPS for AnimatableGaussians) and trains in 17.5 hours (vs. 100 hours), all on a single NVIDIA 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against 3DGS-Avatar [42] and MeshAvatar [4], the gains are substantial (e.g., +3.8 dB PSNR over 3DGS-Avatar under training poses), with both baselines struggling to capture fine wrinkles and text textures. Against the strongest baseline AnimatableGaussians [28], the method wins on all four quality metrics (PSNR +1.45, SSIM +0.0037, LPIPS −0.0025, FID −1.22 under training poses) while rendering 16.6× faster (166 vs. 10 FPS) and training 5.7× faster (17.5 h vs. 100 h). Under novel poses, the advantage over AnimatableGaussians narrows (PSNR +0.22, FID −0.21) but remains consistent across all metrics. Against PoseVocab [27] (novel-pose setting only), the method leads by +1.95 dB PSNR and −30.4 FID. Against DEGAS [43], the method is 5.5× faster in rendering (166 vs. 30 FPS) and 3.1× faster in training (17.5 h vs. 55 h), though no direct quality comparison is provided since DEGAS code is unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting-based human avatar line of work by replacing the single-MLP or heavy-CNN property prediction (as in 3DGS-Avatar, AnimatableGaussians, DEGAS) with spatially distributed MLPs that each handle only a local body region, combined with a Gaussian offset basis that decouples smooth spatial interpolation from high-frequency detail learning. It inherits the AnimatableGaussians pipeline (SMPL-X registration, template mesh, PCA pose projection) but departs fundamentally from the StyleUNet-based property map prediction, which is the primary source of AnimatableGaussians' 10 fps rendering bottleneck. The control-point mechanism for constraining Gaussian positions to a surface layer is a novel regularization strategy not present in prior 3DGS avatar methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16× rendering speedup (166 fps vs. 10 fps) while matching or slightly exceeding AnimatableGaussians in quality makes high-fidelity animatable avatars practical for real-time applications like VR, interactive content creation, and telepresence. The spatially-distributed-MLP-plus-offset-basis architecture is a reusable pattern that could transfer to other articulated or deformable 3DGS representations. Key limitations remain: the method still requires multi-view capture, SMPL-X pose estimation, and template mesh extraction (a heavy pipeline), cannot model complex cloth dynamics, and has not been validated on monocular video. The authors identify monocular reconstruction and depth-assisted capture as natural next steps.
