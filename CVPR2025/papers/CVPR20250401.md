## Problem

Existing adversarial training methods generate adversarial examples that only partially perturb non-robust features, leaving residual correlations between unperturbed non-robust features and labels that degrade robustness. Achieving complete perturbation is difficult because robust and non-robust features lack explicit supervisory signals and labeled data is sparse in semi-supervised settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250401/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet32-100) in a semi-supervised setting where only a subset of training samples carry ground-truth labels (e.g., 4,000 labeled + 46,000 unlabeled for CIFAR-10). Pseudo-labels for unlabeled data are generated via a Mean Teacher model, and adversarial perturbations are constrained to an l∞ ball of radius 8/255. The output is a 10- or 100-way class prediction from a WRN-28-10 classifier.

## Method

WSCAT addresses incomplete perturbation by formulating adversarial example generation as an information-theoretic optimization that minimizes mutual information between natural and adversarial embeddings, thereby disrupting all non-robust feature–label correlations. Since mutual information is intractable, it is replaced by a weakly supervised dynamic loss (l_con) that modifies InfoNCE by selecting positive samples based on the current classifier's predictions rather than ground-truth labels, enabling dynamic, label-free correlation blocking over both labeled and unlabeled data. The complete AE generation objective jointly maximizes KL divergence (TRADES-style) and β·l_con, with β tuned via harmonic mean of natural and robust validation accuracy. The full training is a min-max game where the outer minimization enforces the classifier to retain only robust features (those shared between natural samples and completely perturbed AEs), supported by theoretical guarantees that the learned features satisfy a ρ-γ robustness definition.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, WSCAT achieves 80.93% natural accuracy and 58.52% PGD robust accuracy (ε=8/255), yielding a harmonic mean of 59.40%—surpassing the best semi-supervised baseline PUAT (52.12% mean) by 7.3 points. On CIFAR-100, WSCAT reaches 27.43% harmonic mean vs. PUAT's 22.40%, and on ImageNet32-100 it achieves 10.59% vs. PUAT's 7.42%. WSCAT also outperforms all supervised AT methods (TRADES, RLFAT, DMAT) by a wide margin on robust accuracy while maintaining competitive natural accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WSCAT outperforms all seven baselines on every robust accuracy metric (FGSM, PGD, CW, AA) across all three datasets. Against the strongest semi-supervised defensive baseline PUAT, WSCAT gains +12.1 points on PGD accuracy (58.52 vs. 46.63) and +7.3 points on harmonic mean (59.40 vs. 52.12) on CIFAR-10, with similar margins on CIFAR-100 (+5.8 PGD, +5.0 mean) and ImageNet32-100 (+3.6 PGD, +3.2 mean). Compared to supervised AT methods, WSCAT's advantage is even larger: over TRADES it gains +30.4 points on PGD and +28.8 on mean (CIFAR-10). WSCAT slightly trails PUAT on natural accuracy on CIFAR-10 (80.93 vs. 81.88) but surpasses it on CIFAR-100 (55.14 vs. 50.21) and ImageNet32-100 (34.64 vs. 25.63), indicating that the complete perturbation strategy preserves more useful features on harder datasets. The supervised variant WSCAT-sup already outperforms all supervised baselines (TRADES, RLFAT, DMAT) on harmonic mean, confirming that the complete AE generation mechanism itself is the key driver of improvement, with unlabeled data providing additional gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

WSCAT builds directly on the TRADES adversarial loss (KL divergence between natural and adversarial predictions) and the InfoNCE mutual information lower bound, but departs from both by introducing a weakly supervised dynamic loss that replaces fixed positive/negative sample sets with predictions from the current classifier, making the contrastive objective evolve during training. The semi-supervised framework inherits pseudo-label generation from Mean Teacher and RST, while the information-theoretic formulation of complete AE generation via mutual information minimization under a Wasserstein constraint extends distributional adversarial training (Staib & Jegelka) with an explicit perturbation-completeness objective. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the robust-feature-learning literature (feature disentanglement, causal inference, knowledge distillation) by arguing that those methods implicitly assume complete perturbation already occurs, whereas WSCAT makes completeness an explicit optimization target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of incomplete perturbation as a concrete, measurable failure mode in existing AT methods provides a unifying explanation for the persistent natural-vs-robust accuracy gap, and the weakly supervised dynamic loss offers a practical mechanism to address it without requiring explicit feature identification or additional supervision. The theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1 and 2) connecting the training objective to the formal robust-feature definition strengthen the method's credibility. However, all experiments are limited to WRN-28-10 on CIFAR-scale and ImageNet32 datasets, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger architectures (ResNet-50, ViT) and full ImageNet, and whether the β tuning procedure generalizes without a held-out validation set. The reliance on a 10-step PGD for AE generation and the computational overhead of computing the dynamic positive set per batch also raise practical questions for large-scale deployment.
