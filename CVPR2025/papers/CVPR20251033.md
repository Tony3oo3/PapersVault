## Problem

Single-image depth estimators treat frames independently, producing flickering and temporally inconsistent depth over video sequences. Existing video depth methods either fail to propagate context across clips in a streamed inference setting or rely on precise camera poses and test-time optimization. The paper tackles how to achieve state-of-the-art temporal consistency for arbitrarily long videos processed autoregressively, without requiring camera poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251033/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary-length RGB video at 576×768 resolution and produces per-frame depth maps with high temporal consistency. At inference, the video is split into 10-frame clips with 5 overlapping frames processed via a sliding window; depth is encoded as a 3-channel signal (replicated) to reuse SVD's VAE, then decoded and channel-averaged to recover the final depth map. The first clip is denoised from pure Gaussian noise, while subsequent clips condition on previously predicted depth in the overlap region.

## Method

ChronoDepth fine-tunes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) by adding an RGB-video conditioning branch (concatenated on the feature dimension) and training the UNet denoiser via denoising score matching. The key inference innovation is a consistent context-aware strategy: overlapping frames are initialized with previously predicted depth at a very small noise level σ_ε rather than re-noised at each sampling step (as in the 'replacement trick'), ensuring the conditioning remains stable across all diffusion steps. To enable this, training samples independent noise levels per frame within a clip, so the model learns to denoise frames at heterogeneous noise levels simultaneously. A sequential spatial-temporal fine-tuning protocol is used: spatial layers are first trained on single-frame depth data and then frozen while temporal layers are trained on randomly-lengthed video clips, which the ablations show yields the best temporal consistency without sacrificing spatial accuracy.

## Results

ChronoDepth achieves state-of-the-art temporal consistency (MFC) across all three benchmarks: 0.407 on KITTI-360 (a 98% relative improvement over the next best video method), 0.092 on ScanNet++, and 0.728 on Sintel, while maintaining spatial accuracy (AbsRel) comparable to SOTA single-image estimators (0.215 on KITTI-360, 0.176 on ScanNet++). The method attains the best average rank of 1.00 on MFC across all datasets, confirming its superiority in temporal consistency. On Sintel, ChronoDepth achieves the best δ1 (0.555) and MFC* (0.516) among all methods, though its AbsRel (0.493) is slightly higher than single-image methods, reflecting the trade-off between spatial detail and temporal smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-image baselines (Marigold, DepthAnything, DepthAnything V2), ChronoDepth matches or slightly trails on AbsRel (e.g., 0.215 vs. 0.207 for DepthAnything V2 on KITTI-360) but dramatically outperforms them on MFC: 0.407 vs. 0.807 (DepthAnything V2) and 0.407 vs. 0.776 (Marigold) on KITTI-360, a 98% relative improvement over the best single-image method. Against the concurrent video method DepthCrafter [33], which uses the replacement trick for cross-clip context, ChronoDepth wins on MFC across all three datasets (0.407 vs. 0.655 on KITTI-360, 0.092 vs. 0.094 on ScanNet++, 0.728 vs. 1.270 on Sintel) while also achieving better AbsRel on KITTI-360 (0.215 vs. 0.293). Against NVDS [70], a discriminative video depth stabilizer, ChronoDepth dominates on every metric across all datasets. The key finding is that ChronoDepth's consistent context-aware inference (noising-free overlap initialization) eliminates the per-clip flickering that plagues both the replacement trick and naive sliding window approaches, yielding the best MFC rank (1.00) while preserving spatial accuracy within ~1% of the best single-image methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChronoDepth extends the Marigold paradigm of repurposing diffusion models for depth estimation from single images to video, using Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as the backbone rather than Stable Diffusion. It departs from the concurrent DepthCrafter by replacing the 'replacement trick' (which re-noises previously predicted frames at each sampling step) with a consistent context-aware strategy that conditions on clean prior predictions with a minimal noise level, drawing conceptual inspiration from Diffusion Forcing's per-frame independent noise sampling. The sequential spatial-then-temporal fine-tuning protocol is a novel training recipe that contrasts with SVD's joint full-network fine-tuning, and the paper systematically ablates this choice against alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video diffusion priors can be effectively tamed for deterministic depth estimation while achieving state-of-the-art temporal consistency in a streaming, autoregressive setting—critical for AR/VR, robotics, and video editing where flickering is unacceptable. The consistent context-aware inference strategy is a practical, generalizable technique for any video diffusion model performing autoregressive generation over arbitrarily long sequences. Limitations include training exclusively on synthetic data at 576×768 resolution, reliance on a heuristic small noise level (σ_ε) for context frames, and the multi-step denoising cost (T=10 steps per clip). Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, handles fast camera motion or large dynamic scenes robustly, and whether the sequential training protocol generalizes to other video prediction tasks.
