## Problem

Most video-text retrieval methods ignore audio, which carries 'invisible but audible' cues (speaker identity, background sounds, emotional nuances) essential for comprehensive video understanding. The few methods that do incorporate audio (ECLIPSE, TEFAL) blindly fuse it regardless of relevance, degrading video representation when audio is irrelevant (e.g., background noise), and TEFAL additionally requires joint video-text processing at query time, making retrieval computationally expensive.

## Input / Output

The method takes N uniformly sampled video frames, the corresponding audio signal (converted to a mel-spectrogram and processed by a frozen AST into N_a patches, then resampled to M fixed-length embeddings via a query-based transformer), and a text description. Video and text are encoded independently, so video representations are pre-computable and stored; at query time only the text embedding is extracted and compared against stored video representations, yielding O(A+V+T) retrieval complexity. The output is a combined similarity score averaging a global term (average-pooled video embedding vs. text embedding) and a local term (per-frame text similarities aggregated via log-sum-exp).

## Method

AVIGATE fuses audio and visual modalities through a Gated Fusion Transformer consisting of L layers, each containing a gated fusion block (cross-attention MHA + FFN, both modulated by learned gating scores) followed by a refining block (MHSA + FFN). The gating function computes per-layer scores g_mha and g_ffn by concatenating average-pooled audio and frame embeddings, passing them through two MLPs with tanh activation, thereby dynamically scaling audio's contribution—high scores emphasize informative audio, low scores suppress irrelevant signals. For training, the paper proposes an adaptive margin-based contrastive loss where each negative pair's margin m_ij is derived from the average of intra-modal cosine similarities (visual and textual), scaled by λ and capped at δ, making the loss sensitive to semantic relatedness among negatives. The similarity score used in the loss combines global alignment (pooled video vs. text) and local alignment (frame-level similarities aggregated via LSE), and the entire framework is trained end-to-end with frozen AST and CLIP encoders, enabling efficient independent encoding of video and text.

## Results

On MSR-VTT 9k with CLIP ViT-B/16, AVIGATE achieves 52.1% R@1 for text-to-video and 51.2% R@1 for video-to-text retrieval, yielding an RSum of 429.0, outperforming all prior methods. With the smaller ViT-B/32 backbone, it reaches 50.2% R@1 (T2V) and 49.7% R@1 (V2T) with RSum 416.4. On VATEX (ViT-B/16), AVIGATE attains 67.5% R@1 for text-to-video retrieval, and on Charades it reaches 24.1% R@1 (ViT-B/16). The method also demonstrates superior retrieval efficiency with O(A+V+T) time complexity, achieving total latency of 9.90 ms per query versus 140.57 ms for TEFAL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVIGATE outperforms all eight baselines on R@1 across all three datasets and both backbone sizes. Against the strongest audio-enhanced baseline TEFAL [14], AVIGATE gains +0.8%p T2V R@1 and +2.6%p V2T R@1 on MSR-VTT (ViT-B/32), widening to +2.2%p T2V and +3.1%p V2T with ViT-B/16; on VATEX the T2V R@1 gap grows to +3.0%p (67.5 vs. 64.5 for UATVR, the best no-audio method). Against the strongest visual-only baselines, AVIGATE surpasses UATVR [8] by +1.8%p T2V R@1 on VATEX (ViT-B/32) and by +1.3%p on MSR-VTT (ViT-B/16), and beats ProST [19] by +2.6%p T2V R@1 on MSR-VTT (ViT-B/16). The RSum advantage over TEFAL is +6.6%p on MSR-VTT (ViT-B/16: 429.0 vs. 416.3 for TEFAL's partial results). In terms of efficiency, AVIGATE's O(A+V+T) complexity yields 9.90 ms total latency per query, over 14× faster than TEFAL (140.57 ms) and 6.7× faster than X-Pool (66.31 ms), while CLIP4Clip [23] is slightly faster at 9.76 ms but achieves substantially lower retrieval accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVIGATE builds directly on the CLIP4Clip paradigm of using pre-trained CLIP encoders for frame-level video representation and multi-grained alignment, extending it with an audio modality. It departs from prior audio-enhanced retrieval methods (ECLIPSE, TEFAL) that blindly fuse audio with visual features by introducing a gated fusion transformer that dynamically suppresses irrelevant audio signals. The adaptive margin-based contrastive loss extends standard InfoNCE-style contrastive objectives by incorporating intra-modal semantic similarity as a per-pair margin, a concept borrowed from face recognition (ArcFace) but applied here to cross-modal video-text alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment concern: audio-enhanced retrieval methods like TEFAL require joint processing of video and text at query time, making them 14× slower than AVIGATE's independent-encoding approach. The gated mechanism provides a simple, interpretable solution to the problem of irrelevant audio degrading video representations, which prior methods overlooked. Open questions include whether the gating strategy generalizes to other auxiliary modalities (e.g., subtitles, metadata), whether the adaptive margin formulation transfers to other contrastive learning settings beyond video-text retrieval, and whether performance gains hold on larger-scale or more diverse benchmarks beyond the three relatively small datasets evaluated.
