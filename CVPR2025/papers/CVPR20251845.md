## Problem

While numerous self-supervised pre-training methods have been proposed for 3D point cloud processing, the common mechanism underlying their effectiveness remains unclear, limiting insights into effective training strategies. This paper asks: what do different pre-training methods share in terms of how they alter the internal representations of DNNs?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251845/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud x ∈ R^{P×3} as input and divides it into n spatial regions via farthest point sampling (FPS) for region centers and KDTree for point assignment. For classification, the DNN outputs a class label; for semantic segmentation, it outputs per-point categorical labels. The interaction framework decomposes the DNN's log-odds output score into the sum of 2^n interaction effects, each corresponding to a distinct subset of regions, enabling per-structure analysis of the network's reasoning.

## Method

The paper introduces game-theoretic (Harsanyi) interactions as a unified lens to interpret pre-training: the DNN output score is decomposed into the sum of numerical effects I(S) over all 2^n region subsets, where each interaction encodes an AND relationship among point cloud regions. Empirical analysis across five pre-training methods (IAE, OcCo, Jigsaw, STRL, CrossPoint) and three architectures reveals a common mechanism—pre-training consistently strengthens high-order interactions (global 3D structures) while weakening low-order interactions (local structures). Building on this, the authors propose a new interaction loss L_interaction that directly penalizes low-order interaction strength and rewards high-order interaction strength, approximated by sampling three disjoint low-order subsets and treating their union as a high-order interaction. This loss is added to the standard classification loss with a small weight α (0.0005), eliminating the need for large-scale pre-training while achieving performance comparable to traditional pre-training methods on ModelNet40, ScanObjectNN, and S3DIS.

## Results

On ModelNet40, the proposed interaction loss (L_interaction) boosts DGCNN from 92.5% to 93.3% and PointNet from 89.2% to 90.1%, matching or approaching pre-training methods like OcCo (93.0%) and IAE (94.2%) without requiring large-scale pre-training. On ScanObjectNN (PB_T50_RS), DGCNN+L_interaction reaches 79.4% accuracy, a modest gain over the 78.1% baseline but below pre-trained variants (83.5–85.6%). On S3DIS semantic segmentation, DGCNN+L_interaction achieves the best overall accuracy (86.8% OA) among all compared methods, with 59.0% mIoU, slightly trailing IAE's 60.7% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its interaction loss against five self-supervised pre-training methods (IAE, OcCo, JigSaw, STRL, CrossPoint) across three network architectures (PointNet, DGCNN, CurveNet/GDANet). On ModelNet40, DGCNN+L_interaction (93.3%) is competitive with OcCo (93.0%) and STRL (93.1%) but trails IAE (94.2%) by 0.9 points; on ScanObjectNN, the gap to pre-trained methods is larger (79.4% vs. 83.5–85.6%), suggesting the loss is less effective on real-world data. On S3DIS, DGCNN+L_interaction achieves the highest OA (86.8%) among all methods, surpassing IAE (85.9%), OcCo (85.1%), and JigSaw (84.4%), though IAE retains a 1.7-point mIoU advantage (60.7% vs. 59.0%). The key differentiator is that all gains are achieved without any pre-training on large-scale unlabeled datasets, whereas every competing method requires a separate pre-training phase. The loss also consistently improves CurveNet (+0.3% ModelNet40, +2.8% ScanObjectNN) and GDANet (+0.5%, +1.3%), demonstrating architecture-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the game-theoretic interaction framework previously applied to 3D point cloud DNNs (Shen et al., NeurIPS 2021) and the theoretical grounding for interactions as faithful concept encodings (Li et al., ICML 2023) into a new comparative role: using interactions as a unified lens to explain why five architecturally distinct self-supervised pre-training methods (IAE, OcCo, Jigsaw, STRL, CrossPoint) all improve downstream performance. Rather than proposing a new pre-training objective, the paper identifies a shared mechanism—enhanced high-order and suppressed low-order interaction strengths—and then converts that insight into a direct, pre-training-free interaction loss. This positions the work at the intersection of interpretability (explaining pre-training) and practical training design (bypassing pre-training via a heuristic loss term).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified mechanism finding gives practitioners a principled diagnostic: if a pre-trained model overfits on an unseen dataset, the cause may be excessively strong high-order interactions rather than a generic capacity issue. The transferability-risk observation (Conclusion 3) is a useful caution that pre-training is not monotonically beneficial when fine-tuning data is abundant, which could reshape how teams allocate compute between pre-training and fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the interaction loss generalizes beyond classification (the paper requires a two-stage workaround for segmentation), whether the high/low-order split at n/3 and 2n/3 is optimal, and whether the mechanism holds for transformer-based architectures trained end-to-end rather than compared post-hoc.
