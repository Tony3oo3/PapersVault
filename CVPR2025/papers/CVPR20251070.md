## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP are highly vulnerable to small adversarial perturbations in the visual modality, yet existing defenses either require expensive adversarial training or task-specific annotated data for prompt tuning. This leaves a critical gap: achieving zero-shot adversarial robustness on unseen downstream tasks without any task-specific training set or annotations during inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251070/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TAPT takes a single adversarial test image (no task-specific training data) and pre-computed layer-wise embedding statistics (mean and variance) from ImageNet, computed separately with adversarial and clean prompts. It generates 64 augmented views of the test image, selects the top 10% lowest-entropy views, and performs a single step of prompt optimization per inference. The output is a set of optimized bimodal prompts (visual and/or textual) used to produce a class label, with the prompt reset before each new sample to prevent poisoning.

## Method

TAPT is a test-time defense that dynamically tunes defensive bimodal prompts for each test sample by minimizing a combined loss of multi-view entropy and adversarial-clean embedding alignment. The multi-view entropy loss encourages consistent predictions across selected augmented views, while the alignment loss matches the layer-wise mean and variance of the test sample's embeddings to pre-computed reference statistics from ImageNet, weighted by a hyperparameter α (default 0.5) between adversarial and clean distributions. Prompts are initialized via APT on ImageNet (100 epochs) and then fine-tuned with a single AdamW step (lr=5e-4) during inference, keeping the per-sample overhead to ~0.1–0.17 seconds. This design makes TAPT task-agnostic, unsupervised, and lightweight, requiring no downstream training data while simultaneously improving adversarial robustness and preserving clean accuracy.

## Results

Under AutoAttack (AA) with ε=1/255, TAPT achieves an average zero-shot adversarial robustness of 53.8% (ViT-B/16) and 49.0% (ViT-B/32) with the V-L independent prompt design, compared to 17.2% and 11.0% for the corresponding APT baselines — improvements of 36.6% and 38.0% respectively. Against PGD-100, TAPT-VLI reaches 48.1% average (ViT-B/16) versus 22.7% for APT-VLI. On clean accuracy (Table 2, ViT-B/16), TAPT-VLI achieves 61.4% average across 11 datasets, outperforming APT-VLI (51.0%) by 10.4% while simultaneously boosting adversarial robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP Vanilla (Radford et al., ICML 2021), which achieves near-0% robustness under AutoAttack (0.1% average), TAPT-VLI with ViT-B/16 recovers 53.8% average robustness — a 48.9% absolute gain. Against the APT baselines from Li et al. (CVPR 2024) and Zhang et al. (ECCV 2024), TAPT consistently wins across all three prompt designs and both ViT architectures: the average AA improvement ranges from 25.9% (V-L joint, ViT-B/16) to 38.0% (V-L independent, ViT-B/32). TAPT also outperforms APT on clean accuracy (Table 2), with the V-L independent design gaining 10.4% average clean accuracy (61.4% vs. 51.0%), indicating that test-time adaptation does not sacrifice generalization. The V-L independent prompt design yields the strongest adversarial robustness, while the V-L joint design performs best on the source domain (ImageNet) but generalizes less well to downstream datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAPT sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: adversarial prompt tuning (APT) methods that defend VLMs at training time by aligning learnable text prompts with adversarial image embeddings, and test-time adaptation (TTA) methods like TPT and PromptAlign that tune prompts per-sample using multi-view entropy minimization. The key departure is moving prompt optimization from the training phase to the inference phase, eliminating the need for task-specific annotated data while introducing a novel adversarial-clean embedding alignment loss that anchors test-sample statistics to pre-computed distributions from a public dataset. This repurposes the distribution-alignment idea from PromptAlign for adversarial defense rather than generalization, and the multi-view entropy mechanism from TPT for robustness rather than clean accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAPT is positioned as the first inference-time adversarial defense for pre-trained VLMs, addressing a practical gap: real-world deployments face an unbounded set of downstream tasks where task-specific adversarial training is infeasible. The method's low per-image overhead (~0.1–0.17s) and task-agnostic nature make it deployable without retraining, which is significant for safety-critical applications. However, the current scope is limited to image-modality attacks on image classification; extending to multimodal attacks, visual reasoning, and VQA in models like GPT-4V or Gemini remains open. The catastrophic failure of continuous (non-reset) prompt adaptation (0.48% on ImageNet) also raises unresolved questions about the stability of test-time optimization under adversarial conditions.
