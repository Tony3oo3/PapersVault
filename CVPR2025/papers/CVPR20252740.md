## Problem

Vision Transformers are too large to fit in the on-chip memory of edge devices (e.g., DeiT-S at 85 MB vs. 8 MB on-chip RAM on ESP32-S3), forcing costly off-chip memory accesses that inflate latency and energy. Existing compression methods such as pruning and quantization suffer severe accuracy degradation at the >10× compression ratios required to fit on-chip memory, because their lossy weight encoding introduces large perturbation error with no mechanism to recover it at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet classification, 384×384 for COCO detection/segmentation) and produces class labels, bounding boxes, or instance segmentation masks as usual. The unusual aspect is that at inference the model stores only 4-bit quantized encoded weight embeddings and a lightweight 4-bit decoder in on-chip memory; the full-precision weights are never materialized, and the decoder is applied on-the-fly per layer during the forward pass.

## Method

DeepCompress-ViT has two phases. In Unified Compression Training (UCT), an encoder compresses each layer's weights to a lower-dimensional embedding while a single-linear-layer decoder reconstructs them; both are trained jointly with the ViT using a combined loss of weight-reconstruction MSE, cross-entropy on ground-truth labels, and knowledge distillation from a pretrained teacher, so the ViT learns to perform well on the decoded (perturbed) weights. After training, only the encoded embeddings and decoder are retained. At inference, Optimized Test-Time Decoding exploits the associative property of matrix multiplication to reorder the computation from input × (encoded_weight × decoder_weight) to (input × encoded_weight) × decoder_weight, which first projects the input from dimension C down to the compressed dimension r and then expands it back, eliminating the extra FLOPs that naive weight decoding would introduce. The compressed dimension r is chosen to satisfy r ≤ 2N·C·d / (2N·(C+d)), guaranteeing zero additional computation relative to the uncompressed baseline.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, DeepCompress-ViT compresses DeiT-Small from 84.1 MB to 5.64 MB (14.9× compression) while retaining 78.74% top-1 accuracy, a drop of only 0.98% from the original 79.72%. For DeiT-Base, it achieves 17.4× compression (330.2 MB → 18.9 MB) with just 0.12% accuracy loss (81.62% vs. 81.74%). Hardware evaluation on an Eyeriss-based accelerator shows energy consumption drops by 673× (DeiT-S) and 1475× (DeiT-B), and latency drops by 44.5× and 68.4× respectively, enabling the full model to reside in 8 MB on-chip memory. On COCO object detection (384×384), the compressed DeiT-S backbone (5.27 MB, 15.7×) achieves 23.0 AP^b and 20.9 AP^m versus 26.2 and 23.7 for the uncompressed model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepCompress-ViT dominates all three SOTA compression baselines on both compression ratio and accuracy. Against WDPruning [31], it achieves 18.23% higher top-1 accuracy (78.74% vs. 60.51%) while compressing 6× more aggressively (14.9× vs. 2.5×). Against LSQ [8], it gains 10.74% accuracy (78.74% vs. 68.00%) at a slightly higher compression ratio (14.9× vs. 13.6×) and a smaller model size (5.64 MB vs. 6.18 MB). Against Q-ViT [11], it improves accuracy by 6.84% (78.74% vs. 71.90%) while also achieving a higher compression ratio. In every head-to-head comparison, DeepCompress-ViT is the only method that simultaneously achieves >14× compression and <1% accuracy loss, making it the sole practical option for fitting ViTs into 8 MB on-chip edge memory without severe performance degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes ViT compression (pruning, quantization) as a lossy weight-encoding problem and introduces a complementary decoding step at inference, positioning itself as orthogonal to existing structured pruning (WDPruning, X-Pruner) and quantization (Q-ViT, LSQ, PTQ4ViT) methods. The encoder-decoder weight compression draws on autoencoder-style parameter compression, but the key departure is the Unified Compression Training that jointly optimizes encoder, decoder, and the ViT itself with cross-entropy and knowledge-distillation losses, rather than training the compression module in isolation. The Optimized Test-Time Decoding, which exploits the associativity of matrix multiplication to fold the decoder into the forward pass at zero extra FLOPs, is a novel systems-level insight not present in prior ViT compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling a ~85 MB DeiT-S to fit within 8 MB of on-chip SRAM, this work removes the primary barrier to deploying ViTs on resource-constrained edge devices (e.g., ESP32-S3), with reported >1470× energy and >68× latency reductions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-linear or attention-specific layers beyond the four linear projections compressed here, whether the simulator-based (gem5-Aladdin) hardware numbers translate to real edge silicon, and how the method scales to larger ViT variants (ViT-L, ViT-H) or non-ViT architectures. The reliance on a 4-bit quantized decoder and the fixed optimal-r derivation also raise questions about robustness across input batch sizes and sequence lengths.
