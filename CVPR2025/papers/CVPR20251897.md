## Problem

Diffusion-based single image-to-3D models require 40–50 denoising steps for acceptable quality, making them impractical for real-time or interactive use. The fundamental obstacle to few-step inference is that the score function is poorly estimated in the low-SNR (pure noise) endpoint, so accumulated integration errors severely degrade the final output. This paper addresses how to regularize and correct the endpoint score function so that 2–4 step generation matches or exceeds multi-step quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251897/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image as input and produces six multi-view images (front, back, left, right, front-right, front-left) together with corresponding normal maps, all at 512×512 resolution, in as few as 2 NFE. These multi-view outputs are then fed into NeuS to reconstruct a textured 3D mesh. The model is built on top of Era3D's row-wise attention architecture, inheriting its camera prediction module and cross-domain self-attention for joint image–normal generation.

## Method

Acc3D introduces two synergistic training modules. First, edge consistency-guided distillation applies consistency constraints only in the high-SNR edge region (e.g., t ∈ [0, 0.4T]) rather than the full noise range, producing a refined target X̃₀ by re-noising the coarse one-step output X_{0|T} to an intermediate timestep and evaluating the consistency model there; a distillation loss then pulls X_{0|T} toward X̃₀, progressively correcting the endpoint score function without the instability of full-range consistency. Second, disentangled adversarial regularization uses the frozen pre-trained diffusion model as a discriminator but splits it into two separate discriminators—one for normal maps and one for texture images—to bridge the large distribution gap between geometry and appearance modalities. The distillation module stabilizes adversarial training against mode collapse, while the adversarial loss sharpens perceptual detail, and together they enable 20× speedup (2–4 NFE vs. 40–50) with improved reconstruction quality (CD 0.0191, IoU 0.6681 on GSO).

## Results

On the GSO dataset for 3D reconstruction, Acc3D achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0191 and Volume IoU of 0.6681 using only 2 NFE, outperforming Wonder3D (CD 0.0199, IoU 0.6244 at 40 NFE) and Era3D (CD 0.0217, IoU 0.5973 at 40 NFE) while being 20× faster. For novel view synthesis on GSO, Acc3D (4 NFE) attains MUSIQ 69.26, CLIPIQA 0.65, and MANIQA 0.64, surpassing Era3D (MUSIQ 63.64, CLIPIQA 0.53) and SyncDreamer (MUSIQ 64.46, CLIPIQA 0.52). On the DTC dataset, Acc3D leads with PSNR 24.01, SSIM 0.88, and LPIPS 0.12, compared to Era3D's PSNR 23.36 and SSIM 0.87.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Acc3D outperforms all 12 baselines on both CD and IoU for 3D reconstruction on GSO, with the closest competitor being Wonder3D (CD 0.0199 vs. 0.0191; IoU 0.6244 vs. 0.6681), yet Acc3D requires only 2 NFE versus Wonder3D's 40—a 20× speedup. Against its direct base model Era3D, Acc3D improves IoU by 7.1 percentage points (0.6681 vs. 0.5973) while reducing inference steps from 40 to 2. On novel view synthesis (Table 1), Acc3D dominates on all perceptual metrics (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA, MANIQA) on both GSO and DTC, with the largest margin over Era3D on GSO CLIPIQA (0.65 vs. 0.53). Direct-mesh methods like TripoSR (CD 0.0326) and Stable-Fast-3D (CD 0.0421) are significantly outperformed despite avoiding iterative sampling. The only baseline that approaches Acc3D's CD is Wonder3D, but it still lags by 0.0008 in CD and 4.4 points in IoU while being 20× slower.

## Relation to Prior Work

Acc3D builds directly on Era3D as its base architecture for concurrent multiview image and normal map generation, and repurposes two established acceleration techniques—consistency models (Song et al., 2023) and adversarial diffusion distillation (Sauer et al., 2023)—in a novel combined pipeline. The key conceptual departure is restricting consistency regularization to the high-SNR 'edge' region rather than the full noise range, using it as a progressive score corrector for the pure-noise endpoint rather than as a standalone few-step generator. Additionally, the disentangled dual-discriminator design for geometry (normal maps) and texture (RGB) is a practical extension of the single-discriminator adversarial distillation paradigm, motivated by the large distribution gap between these two modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving a 20× inference speedup (from 40–50 NFE to 2–4 NFE) while simultaneously improving reconstruction quality (CD 0.0191, IoU 0.6681) makes high-fidelity image-to-3D generation practically deployable in interactive or near-real-time settings, which was previously infeasible with iterative diffusion sampling. The edge consistency insight—that regularizing score estimation in high-SNR regions is more effective than full-range consistency for correcting endpoint errors—offers a transferable principle for other few-step diffusion applications. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 512×512 resolution and six-view setup, whether the 24-hour training cost on 4 A6000 GPUs is prohibitive for broader adoption, and whether the NeuS-based reconstruction pipeline introduces bottlenecks that the accelerated generation does not fully address.
