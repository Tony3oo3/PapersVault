## Problem

Constructing anatomical atlases for a specific population or imaging modality currently requires days to weeks of iterative optimization or model retraining, forcing biomedical researchers to rely on precomputed atlases from mismatched populations. This computational barrier discourages rapid experimentation and degrades downstream analyses such as segmentation transfer and shape quantification when the atlas does not match the study population.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252485/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a variable-sized group (m images, tested from 2 to 319) of 3D brain MRI volumes at 1 mm isotropic resolution, skull-stripped and affinely aligned, with no constraint on group size at inference. It outputs m diffeomorphic deformation fields (as stationary velocity fields integrated via scaling and squaring), the group-specific atlas image (mean of warped inputs), and optionally an atlas segmentation map. A key property is that the atlas is unbiased by construction: the output velocity fields are centered to zero mean, so no subject or structure is systematically favored.

## Method

MultiMorph is a modified UNet in which standard convolution layers are replaced by a proposed GroupBlock: at each layer, the elementwise mean of feature maps across the group dimension is computed and concatenated with each individual image's features before a shared convolution, enabling inter-image communication at linear (not quadratic) memory cost. The network outputs stationary velocity fields for each input image, which are then passed through a centrality layer that subtracts the group mean velocity to guarantee an unbiased atlas. Training is self-supervised, combining normalized cross-correlation similarity, gradient-based deformation regularization, and an optional soft-Dice segmentation loss, with 50% of training groups drawn from domain-randomized synthetic volumes to achieve modality-agnostic generalization. The entire atlas is produced in a single forward pass on CPU in seconds to minutes, with no per-population fine-tuning required.

## Results

On the held-out IXI dataset (319 subjects, 3 modalities), MultiMorph achieves the highest Dice score across all modalities (0.913 T1-w, 0.906 T2-w, 0.900 PD-w) while producing atlases in 7.8–10.5 minutes on CPU, a ~100× speedup over ANTs and AtlasMorph and ~30× over Aladdin. MultiMorph also yields near-zero folding (1.1–2.0 voxels) and the lowest centrality bias (0.9–1.5 ×10⁻²), confirming unbiased atlas construction. Notably, the PD-w modality was never seen during training, yet MultiMorph still outperforms all baselines on Dice by 1.6–5.1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiMorph outperforms all three baselines on Dice across every modality, with the largest margin over ANTs (e.g., 0.913 vs. 0.863 on T1-w, +5.0 points) and a consistent 1.9–5.1 point advantage over AtlasMorph and Aladdin. On deformation regularity, Aladdin achieves zero folds (perfect topology) while MultiMorph has only 1.1–2.0 folded voxels, a negligible difference; ANTs and AtlasMorph produce hundreds of folds. On centrality (mean displacement), MultiMorph is dramatically better: 1.4×10⁻² vs. 106.8 (Aladdin, T1-w), 771.5 (Aladdin, T2-w), and 1175.7 (Aladdin, PD-w), indicating Aladdin's atlases are heavily biased. In terms of speed, MultiMorph requires 7.8–10.5 minutes on CPU versus 4320–4380 minutes for ANTs (~400×), 831–1141 minutes for AtlasMorph (~100×), and 164–325 minutes for Aladdin (~30×). The key differentiator is that MultiMorph was never trained on IXI data or PD-w images, whereas all baselines were trained or optimized on the evaluation set, making the comparison particularly favorable to the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiMorph builds directly on the AtlasMorph (Dalca et al., NeurIPS 2019) registration network, reusing its UNet-based diffeomorphic registration backbone, but reframes atlas construction as a single-pass group registration problem rather than an iterative optimization or per-population training task. The key architectural departure is the GroupBlock layer, a linear-complexity group-interaction mechanism that replaces standard convolutions to share features across a variable number of input images, complemented by a centrality layer that enforces zero-mean displacement fields by construction. The use of synthetic neuroimaging data via domain randomization follows the SynthMorph/SynthSeg lineage for modality-agnostic generalization, while the overall formulation departs from both iterative methods (ANTs/SyGN) and prior learning-based atlas constructors (AtlasMorph, Aladdin) that require retraining or fine-tuning for each new population.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing atlas construction from days of optimization to minutes of CPU inference, MultiMorph removes the primary barrier preventing biomedical researchers from generating population-specific atlases, potentially eliminating the reliance on mismatched precomputed templates that biases downstream analyses. The demonstrated generalization to unseen modalities (PD-w) and entirely held-out datasets (IXI) without retraining is practically significant for multi-site, multi-modal studies. Open questions include extending the diffeomorphic assumption to handle topology-changing pathologies, scaling to very large 3D groups under memory constraints, and adapting the framework beyond neuroimaging to arbitrary anatomical structures.
