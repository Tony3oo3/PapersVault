## Problem

Privacy-preserving visual localization methods like SegLoc rely on sparse SfM point clouds with 2D-learned segmentations, offering no guarantee of multi-view label consistency and limited geometric grounding. Dense renderable representations (NeRFs, 3DGS) enable accurate feature-based pose refinement but inherently store privacy-critical photometric and high-dimensional feature information. The paper addresses how to learn a 3D-grounded feature representation on a dense, explicitly finite scene model that can be seamlessly converted to discrete segmentations for privacy-preserving localization without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB query image, a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Opacity Fields scene model, and an initial pose estimate from global-descriptor image retrieval (DenseVLAD). In the privacy-preserving variant, the stored scene model contains only geometric Gaussian parameters (center, scale, rotation, opacity) and a single integer cluster label per Gaussian—no color, no spherical harmonics, no feature vectors, and no triplane field—reducing storage by orders of magnitude. The output is a refined 6DoF camera pose obtained through iterative hierarchical refinement (coarse R=256 then fine R=1024 triplane levels), with the pose updated on the SE(3) Lie algebra at each step.

## Method

GSFFs associates each 3D Gaussian with a scale-aware volumetric feature extracted from a triplane grid via RBF kernels parametrized by the Gaussian's 2D projections, so large Gaussians aggregate features over wider spatial regions. The triplane feature field and a 2D DINOv2 ViT encoder are jointly trained self-supervisedly: rendered 3D features are aligned to extracted 2D features through a contrastive loss (L_NCE), while a prototypical contrastive loss (L_PRO) enforces that pixel-aligned 2D/3D feature pairs are close to the same spatial prototype derived from spectral clustering on a Delaunay graph of Gaussian centers. Multi-view consistency is enforced by randomly substituting cross-view feature pairs in the contrastive objectives. For privacy-preserving localization, features are soft-assigned to cluster prototypes to produce segmentation maps, a shallow classification head is trained with cross-entropy (L_CE), and at inference only hard integer labels per Gaussian are retained; pose refinement minimizes segmentation cross-entropy by explicitly backpropagating through the differentiable 3DGS rasterizer to the camera pose.

## Results

On 7Scenes (SfM pseudo GT), GSFFs-PR Feature achieves the best median position error on 4 out of 7 scenes (e.g., 0.4 cm on Chess, 0.6 cm on Fire, 1.0 cm on Office, 0.6 cm on Redkitchen) with recall at 5 cm/5° up to 98% (Fire), while using a less accurate global descriptor (DenseVLAD) than competing methods. On Cambridge Landmarks, the tuned feature pipeline reaches 17 cm / 0.26° on King's College and 4 cm / 0.25° on Shop, outperforming all rendering-based baselines. The privacy-preserving variant (GSFFs-PR Privacy) achieves 26 cm / 0.49° on Old Hospital and 5 cm / 0.27° on Shop, substantially outperforming SegLoc (36 cm / 0.52° and 11 cm / 0.34° respectively) while discarding all photometric and feature information from the 3D model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 7Scenes, GSFFs-PR Feature achieves the best position error on 4 of 7 scenes (Chess 0.4 cm, Fire 0.6 cm, Office 1.0 cm, Redkitchen 0.6 cm) and is second-best on two more (Heads, Pumpkin), despite using the less accurate DenseVLAD global descriptor compared to DFNet or ACE used by competitors; it fails on Stairs (25.1 cm) due to the textureless, flat layout degrading the underlying Gaussian Opacity Field geometry. On Cambridge Landmarks, the tuned feature pipeline (17 cm / 0.26° on King's, 4 cm / 0.25° on Shop) outperforms all rendering-based baselines including GSLoc (26 cm / 0.34° on King's) and GSplatLoc (27 cm / 0.46°), and is competitive with structure-based methods like KaptureR2D2 (5 cm / 0.10° on King's). For privacy-preserving localization, GSFFs-PR Privacy beats SegLoc on 3 of 4 Cambridge Landmarks scenes (Old Hospital 26 vs 36 cm, Shop 5 vs 11 cm, St. Mary's 13 vs 17 cm) and on all 6 Indoor6 scenes in average recall (84-class variant), while also outperforming descriptor-less methods GoMatch and DGC-GNN by large margins. On Indoor6, the 84-class feature pipeline achieves the best recall across all scenes (e.g., 77% on scene2a, 73% on scene3), surpassing NBE+SLD and DSAC*.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised feature-field learning framework of SSL-Nif [53] (NeRF-based) to the 3D Gaussian Splatting domain, replacing the implicit radiance field with the explicit, finite Gaussian primitives of Gaussian Opacity Fields [83]. It draws on SegLoc [52] for the privacy-preserving segmentation-label formulation and on TensoRF [10] for the triplane parameterization of volumetric features, but departs from both by grounding features in the 3DGS geometry via scale-aware RBF-kernel encoding and by using spectral clustering on a Delaunay graph to derive spatially-informed prototypes. Unlike concurrent 3DGS localization works (GSLoc [39], GSplatLoc [64], SplatLoc [84]) that rely on matching + PnP, GSFFs performs dense feature-metric or segmentation-based pose refinement by explicitly backpropagating through the differentiable rasterizer to the SE(3) Lie algebra, as in Gaussian Splatting SLAM [43].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that the explicit, finite nature of 3DGS makes it inherently more amenable to privacy-preserving localization than NeRFs, since features can be quantized to per-Gaussian labels without losing the dense rendering signal that sparse methods like SegLoc lack. The seamless feature-to-segmentation transition via structure-informed clustering is a clean architectural insight that could transfer to other 3DGS-based perception tasks. Limitations include per-scene training (no cross-scene generalization), degradation on textureless geometry (Stairs), and the reliance on a global-descriptor retrieval step for initialization. Open questions include whether the approach scales to city-level scenes, whether the triplane resolution and cluster count can be adapted automatically, and whether the privacy guarantees hold under adversarial probing of the rendered segmentation maps.
