## Problem

Existing neural scene representations (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) are optimized for photorealistic rendering but produce inherently blurry geometry, and extracting sharp surfaces requires dense view sampling followed by sequential volumetric reconstruction. No prior method can simultaneously recover sharp, high-frequency surface meshes of unbounded scenes and achieve photorealistic novel view synthesis from just a few sparse, unposed RGB images within minutes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252651/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3–10 unposed RGB images, from which MASt3R-SfM estimates camera poses and a sparse point cloud, and DepthAnythingV2 provides per-view monocular depth maps used to initialize the chart manifolds. It outputs a single unified 3D surface mesh covering both foreground and background objects (extracted via adaptive tetrahedralization) plus photorealistic novel view renderings. The full pipeline runs in under 15 minutes on a single GPU (≈3 min for chart alignment, 5–10 min for Gaussian refinement), and the 2DGS-based rasterization requires centered principal points, necessitating image cropping in some cases.

## Method

MAtCha models the scene surface as an atlas of n ≤ N charts (one per input view), each a 2D manifold initialized by backprojecting a monocular depth map. A lightweight neural deformation model—comprising a low-resolution 2D feature grid (chart encodings) and a 1D depth-dependent encoding fed into a tiny MLP—deforms each chart to align with SfM points while preserving high-frequency surface details and handling scale discontinuities at object boundaries. Alignment is driven by a fitting loss (with learnable confidence maps for outlier robustness), a structure loss on normals and mean curvature to retain sharp edges, and a mutual alignment loss for cross-chart consistency. The aligned charts are then refined by attaching 2D Gaussian surfels via barycentric coordinates within chart triangles, followed by a free-Gaussian densification stage constrained by depth, normal, and curvature regularizations weighted by the confidence maps, enabling photorealistic rendering while keeping Gaussians anchored to the explicit surface.

## Results

On the DTU dataset (3 input views), MAtCha Gaussians achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.04 mm, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art Spurfies (1.36 mm) by 24% and the next-best 2DGS+MASt3R-SfM (1.79 mm) by 42%. On unbounded Tanks&Temples scenes, MAtCha achieves F-Scores of 0.072 (5 views) and 0.156 (10 views), surpassing GOF+MASt3R-SfM (0.054/0.144) and 2DGS+MASt3R-SfM (0.052/0.121). For novel view synthesis on Mip-NeRF 360 with 10 training views, MAtCha reaches 21.55 10%Q PSNR and 25.10 average PSNR, exceeding GOF+MASt3R-SfM (20.99/24.50) and 2DGS+MASt3R-SfM (19.94/24.31). The full pipeline completes in under 15 minutes on a single RTX A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAtCha Gaussians outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. Against the closest sparse-view surface reconstruction competitor Spurfies [39], MAtCha reduces mean Chamfer Distance on DTU from 1.36 to 1.04 mm (24% improvement), while also generalizing to unbounded Tanks&Temples scenes where Spurfies was not designed to operate. The strongest volumetric-rendering baselines—2DGS+MASt3R-SfM and GOF+MASt3R-SfM—achieve 1.79 and 1.76 mm CD on DTU respectively, still 70% worse than MAtCha, and trail by 2–3 dB in 10%Q PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 novel view synthesis. Older neural implicit methods (NeuS, VolSDF, SparseNeuS, VolRecon, S-VolSDF) and classical approaches (Points2Surf, CAP-UDF) are all surpassed by a wide margin (2.08–3.41 mm CD vs. 1.04 mm). On unbounded T&T scenes, MAtCha's F-Score of 0.156 (10 views) exceeds GOF+MASt3R-SfM (0.144) and 2DGS+MASt3R-SfM (0.121), confirming that the chart-based explicit surface representation provides a consistent advantage over purely volumetric Gaussian methods in sparse-view settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAtCha Gaussians departs from the dominant volumetric rendering paradigm (NeRF, 3DGS) where surfaces are extracted post-hoc from learned radiance fields, instead making the explicit surface the primary representation modeled as an atlas of 2D charts. It builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) for photorealistic rendering with surfels and adapts the adaptive tetrahedralization from Gaussian Opacity Fields (GOF) for mesh extraction, but fundamentally repositions these as refinement tools on top of a chart-based manifold initialized by monocular depth estimation (DepthAnythingV2). The neural deformation model with chart encodings and depth encodings is novel, addressing the scale ambiguity of monodepth predictions in a way that prior affine rescaling approaches (DN-Splatter, 2DGS+MASt3R-SfM) cannot. Conceptually, the atlas-of-charts formulation echoes AtlasNet's use of local parametric patches but differs in using full depth maps as chart domains and treating the deformation as a scale-alignment problem rather than a generation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAtCha demonstrates that explicit surface geometry and photorealistic rendering—previously treated as sequential or competing objectives—can be achieved simultaneously from just 3–10 unposed images in minutes, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as unsolved for unbounded scenes. The practical speed advantage (minutes vs. hours) and the ability to handle both foreground and background in a single unified mesh make this directly applicable to robotics, AR/VR, and scene editing pipelines. Limitations include sensitivity to strong specularities and transparency (where both monodepth and SfM fail), the 2DGS requirement for centered principal points (necessitating image cropping), and the reliance on MASt3R-SfM for pose estimation. Open questions include extension to dynamic/deformable scenes and whether the chart-based paradigm can replace volumetric methods more broadly.
