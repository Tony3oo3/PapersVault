## Problem

Text prompts are the dominant control signal for video generation but struggle to convey the fine-grained nuances of motion—exact trajectories, timing, acceleration, and synchronized multi-object movement. Prior motion-conditioned video generation methods require complex engineering such as two-stage training, specialized losses, or separate adapters per motion type, limiting flexibility and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252625/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 128×128 RGB frame, a text prompt, and a motion prompt consisting of N point trajectories (each a sequence of 2D coordinates with a binary visibility flag over T timesteps) and produces a 5-second video at 16 fps. The motion prompt is encoded as a T×H×W×C space-time volume where each track carries a unique random embedding at every visited location, with all other positions zeroed. The model generalizes from dense training (16,384 tracks) to arbitrarily sparse inference (as few as 1 track) and to temporally partial tracks that do not start at frame zero.

## Method

The core contribution is a single-stage ControlNet adapter trained on top of the Lumiere video diffusion model, conditioned on point trajectories encoded as a space-time volume with per-track random embeddings. Training uses uniformly sampled dense tracks (16,384 per video) extracted by BootsTAP from 2.2M unfiltered 128×128 videos, with no specialized losses, two-stage schedules, or motion-type-specific modules. Despite this simplicity, the model generalizes to sparse, localized, and temporally partial tracks at inference. The paper further introduces motion prompt expansion—translating high-level user inputs (mouse drags, camera pose trajectories via monocular depth, geometric primitives) into detailed semi-dense track sets—enabling applications like object control, camera control, motion transfer, and drag-based image editing from a single unified model.

## Results

On the DAVIS validation set, the method achieves the best appearance and motion metrics across nearly all track densities. At 2048 tracks, it reaches PSNR 19.33, SSIM 0.608, LPIPS 0.227, FVD 655.9, and EPE 3.89, substantially outperforming both baselines. In a 2AFC human study (N≈103–115 per comparison), the method is preferred over Image Conductor and DragAnything with 74–81% win rates on motion adherence, motion quality, and visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against Image Conductor (finetuned AnimateDiff) and DragAnything (finetuned Stable Video Diffusion) on the DAVIS validation set. The proposed method outperforms both baselines on all appearance metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD) at every track density tested (1, 16, 512, 2048 tracks). On motion adherence (EPE), the method wins at 16, 512, and 2048 tracks but loses to DragAnything at the single-track setting (14.62 vs. 9.14 EPE), which the authors attribute to DragAnything's latent-warping module that enforces motion at the cost of visual quality. The gap widens substantially with more tracks: at 2048 tracks, the method achieves 655.9 FVD versus 1468.4 (DragAnything) and 1890.7 (Image Conductor), and 3.89 EPE versus 12.49 and 33.56 respectively. In the human study, the method is preferred over both baselines in all three categories (motion adherence, motion quality, visual quality) with win rates of 73.7–80.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ControlNet paradigm for conditioning pre-trained diffusion models, applied here to the Lumiere video diffusion model, and on recent advances in point trajectory estimation (BootsTAP, CoTracker, TAPIR) for generating training data. It departs from prior motion-conditioned video generation work (Tora, MotionCtrl, Image Conductor, DragAnything, DragNUWA) that typically requires multi-stage training, specialized losses, separate adapters per motion type, or elaborate data filtering pipelines; here a single-stage ControlNet trained on uniformly sampled dense trajectories generalizes to sparse, dense, object-specific, and global scene motion without task-specific engineering. The 'motion prompt expansion' concept draws an analogy to prompt expansion/rewriting in text-to-image generation, translating high-level user intent (mouse drags, camera paths, geometric primitives) into detailed spatio-temporal track conditioning at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified motion prompt representation offers a practical, flexible interface for controlling video generation across diverse tasks (object animation, camera control, motion transfer, drag-based editing) with a single trained model, reducing the need for task-specific architectures. The emergent physics behaviors (hair dynamics, sand displacement) observed when probing the video prior with simple track inputs suggest motion prompts could serve as a diagnostic tool for evaluating world-model understanding in future generative systems. Key limitations include the 12-minute generation time (not real-time or temporally causal), the 2D approximation in composing object and camera motions, and reliance on a 128×128 base model; open questions include whether this approach scales to higher resolutions, longer durations, and whether the emergent physics generalize beyond the training distribution.
