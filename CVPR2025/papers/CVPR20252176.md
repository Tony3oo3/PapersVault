## Problem

Existing feed-forward image-to-3D reconstruction methods produce blurred textures due to triplane resolution limits and represent appearance only as per-vertex colors with baked-in lighting, making outputs unsuitable for downstream applications requiring dynamic relighting. Additionally, the material-illumination ambiguity in sparse-view inputs remains an ill-posed problem that prior inverse-rendering approaches handle poorly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252176/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, which is expanded to six sparse-view images at known camera poses via the Zero123++ diffusion model. The output is a watertight 3D mesh (extracted from a 256³ density grid via differentiable marching cubes) paired with UV-space PBR texture maps—diffuse albedo, roughness, and metalness—enabling realistic relighting under arbitrary novel environmental lighting.

## Method

ARM decouples geometry and appearance into three specialized models: GeoRM (triplane synthesizer + decoding MLP) predicts density for shape extraction, GlossyRM (same architecture, initialized from GeoRM) predicts per-vertex roughness and metalness, and InstantAlbedo operates entirely in UV texture space. InstantAlbedo back-projects multi-view images and auxiliary data (masks, positions, normals, view directions) onto the unwrapped texture atlas, then fuses per-view features via max-pooling and refines/inpaints with an FFC-Net that provides a global receptive field. A DINO-based ViT material prior encodes semantic appearance from input images and is back-projected alongside other measurements to resolve the material-illumination ambiguity without relying on a rendering loss. All three models are trained supervised on a 150K Objaverse subset (8 H100 GPUs, ~5 days), with GeoRM and GlossyRM using differentiable rendering losses and InstantAlbedo trained on pre-unwrapped 55K shapes with L1+LPIPS+SSIM losses on both lighting-baked color and decomposed albedo.

## Results

On the GSO dataset (1,030 shapes), ARM achieves the best geometry (F-Score 0.968, CD 0.049) and appearance (PSNR 21.69, SSIM 0.880, LPIPS 0.137) among all compared methods. On OmniObject3D (1,038 shapes), ARM similarly leads with F-Score 0.936, CD 0.067, PSNR 20.87, SSIM 0.850, and LPIPS 0.165. For relightable appearance evaluation, ARM achieves PSNR-A of 21.75 and LPIPS-A of 0.171, outperforming SF3D (18.59 / 0.200) by over 3 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARM outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both GSO and OmniObject3D. The closest competitor is MeshFormer [35], which achieves a near-identical F-Score on GSO (0.966 vs. 0.968) but trails ARM by 1.19 dB in PSNR (20.50 vs. 21.69) and 0.013 in LPIPS, indicating ARM's UV-space texture processing yields noticeably sharper and more detailed textures. InstantMesh [81] is competitive on geometry (F-Score 0.938 on GSO) but lags significantly on appearance (PSNR 19.74 vs. 21.69). SF3D [4], the only other method producing PBR maps, is the weakest on appearance (PSNR 18.54) and also underperforms on relighting (PSNR-A 18.59 vs. ARM's 21.75), with its reconstructed albedo suffering from baked-in lighting artifacts. LGM [65] and CRM [75] are clearly outperformed across all metrics, with LGM showing the largest gap (F-Score 0.784, PSNR 18.17 on GSO). Overall, ARM's advantage is most pronounced in texture quality (PSNR, LPIPS) and relighting fidelity, consistent with its design of processing appearance in UV space with a material prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARM builds directly on the LRM (Large Reconstruction Model) architecture [21], which uses transformer-based triplane synthesizers for feed-forward single-image-to-3D generation, and shares the broader feed-forward paradigm with CRM, InstantMesh, MeshFormer, and SF3D. Its key departure is decoupling geometry (GeoRM) from appearance (GlossyRM + InstantAlbedo) and shifting texture reconstruction from triplane space into UV texture space via explicit back-projection of multi-view measurements, combined with a DINO-based material prior for illumination-material disentanglement. This contrasts with prior LRM-based methods that decode all outputs (color, material) from triplanes through small MLPs, and with inverse-rendering approaches that rely on rendering losses for appearance decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARM addresses a practical bottleneck in feed-forward 3D generation: the blurred, low-detail textures and baked-in lighting that make generated assets unsuitable for downstream applications like gaming and the metaverse where dynamic relighting is essential. The UV-space processing strategy and material prior offer a reusable architectural pattern for any pipeline that needs high-fidelity, spatially-varying PBR appearance from sparse views. A key open limitation is that inconsistencies in multi-view images produced by upstream diffusion models (e.g., Zero123++) can introduce artifacts, and the authors identify view-weighting or conflict-resolution strategies as a valuable direction. The modest training cost (8 H100 GPUs, ~5 days) makes the approach accessible and likely to influence subsequent feed-forward 3D generation work.
