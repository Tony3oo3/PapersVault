## Problem

Existing vision foundation models either require massive training data and compute to cover multiple tasks, or use shared decoders that limit compatibility with task-specific architectures. There is a lack of a compact, training-efficient model that supports a broad range of vision tasks while remaining extensible to new tasks without full retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251189/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Argus takes a single RGB image as input and routes backbone features to one of 12 task-specific decoders, producing outputs ranging from class labels and bounding boxes to pixel-level maps (segmentation, depth, surface normals, boundaries, saliency, parsing) and keypoint locations. For tasks requiring multiscale features (e.g., object detection), the backbone's features at 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32 resolutions are forwarded directly to the decoder; for single-scale tasks (e.g., classification), features are flattened and concatenated before the decoder.

## Method

Argus uses a two-stage training strategy built on a frozen DINOv2 ViT-Base backbone augmented with a lightweight adapter (13.6% of backbone parameters). The adapter contains a convolutional spatial prior module producing multiscale features, N cross-attention injector/extractor blocks that bidirectionally interact with ViT tokens, and a projection module for final aggregation. Three key MTL recipes improve the adapter: freezing the ViT to prevent catastrophic forgetting, replacing batch normalization with group normalization to avoid conflicting statistics across tasks, and scaling ViT tokens by a per-image factor before interaction to stabilize norm magnitudes. In stage 1, the adapter and five core-task decoders (Mask DINO, UperNet, DPT, attentional pooler) are trained jointly on ~1.6M images; in stage 2, the entire backbone is frozen and new task-specific decoders (Mask2Former, UperNet, DPT variants) are trained independently, enabling plug-and-play extension to additional tasks.

## Results

Argus (100M params, 13.6M trainable) achieves top performance on 10 of 12 vision tasks. Headline numbers include 56.5% mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation, 58.6 APb on COCO object detection, 51.8 APm on COCO instance segmentation, 77.0 APk on COCO pose estimation, 0.290 RMSE on NYUv2 depth estimation, and 86.3% Top-1 on ImageNet classification. The model is trained on only 1.6M images with 300K iterations (~4 days on 8×H100 GPUs), and new task decoders can be added within 12 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Argus outperforms DINOv2 (86M, same decoders) on all 12 tasks, with the largest gains in pose estimation (77.0 vs 57.2 APk) and depth estimation (0.290 vs 0.307 RMSE). Against Florence-2-B (232M), Argus wins on semantic segmentation (56.5 vs 54.9 mIoU) and object detection (58.6 vs 53.6 APb fine-tuned) despite being less than half the size. Argus matches Uni-Perceiver v2 (308M) on object detection (58.6 APb) and classification (86.3% Top-1) while surpassing it on instance segmentation (51.8 vs 50.6 APm). The only losses are to Florence (893M) on object detection (58.6 vs 62.4 APb) and classification (86.3 vs 90.0%), but Florence uses 8× more parameters and was trained on a 9M-image detection dataset. Against the much larger Unified-IO XL (2.9B) and Unified-IO 2 (1.1B), Argus substantially outperforms on depth estimation (0.290 vs 0.385/0.423 RMSE) and classification (86.3 vs 79.1% Top-1). On unseen datasets (Pascal VOC Person, Person Camera Security, Detecting Cars), Argus achieves 56.6/68.5/79.1 AP50:95, far exceeding 4M-B (18.1/28.7/51.8) and Florence-2-B (43.8/39.1/39.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

Argus sits at the intersection of two VFM paradigms: representation learning (via DINOv2's self-supervised ViT backbone) and task unification (via multiple task-specific decoders). It departs from the dominant task-unification approach (Florence-2, Unified-IO, Uni-Perceiver) that relies on a single shared decoder and requires massive retraining for new tasks, instead adopting an encoder-focused multitask learning strategy with a frozen ViT and a lightweight adapter. The adapter design extends ViT-Adapter with three MTL-specific modifications (freezing the ViT, replacing BN with GN, and scaling ViT features before cross-attention interaction), positioning Argus as a practical middle ground between specialist fine-tuning and fully unified sequence-to-sequence models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Argus demonstrates that a 100M-parameter model trained on only 1.6M images can match or exceed foundation models with 2–30× more parameters across 12 diverse vision tasks, offering a compelling recipe for resource-constrained deployment. The frozen-backbone, decoder-only adaptation strategy makes the model highly extensible—new tasks require only a new decoder without touching the backbone—aligning with the rapid pace of task-specific decoder development. Limitations include coverage restricted to RGB perception tasks (no language, 3D, or low-level vision), reliance on DINOv2's pretrained quality, and the finding that sophisticated MTL algorithms (FAMO, GradNorm) underperform simple empirical weighting, leaving the optimal task-balancing strategy for heterogeneous task sets an open question.
