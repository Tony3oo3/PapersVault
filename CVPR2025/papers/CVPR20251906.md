## Problem

Generating 6DoF object manipulation trajectories from textual action descriptions in egocentric vision requires large-scale, diverse manipulation demonstrations, yet existing datasets (HOT3D, H2O, EgoPAT3D) are small-scale, lab-recorded with expensive multi-camera or depth setups, and cover only a handful of objects and verbs. No prior work has extracted 6DoF manipulation trajectories at scale from ordinary single-camera egocentric videos without pre-recorded camera coordinates, nor established baseline models for text-guided trajectory generation in this setting.

## Input / Output

The model takes a textual action description, a visual input (RGB image, RGB+depth pair, or colored 3D point cloud), and the object's initial pose, and produces a sequence of 6DoF poses (x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw) over time. Each continuous pose dimension is discretized into 256 uniformly spaced bins, yielding an N×6 array of integer tokens in [0,255]⁶; trajectories are sampled at 20 fps and cropped to a maximum of 20 frames to fit the token sequence length. The VLM backbone consumes the visual tokens and text tokens as a prompt and autoregressively generates the trajectory token sequence.

## Method

The paper contributes two components: (1) an automated dataset construction pipeline that extracts 6DoF trajectories from Ego-Exo4D egocentric videos using GPT-4o for temporal action localization, Grounded SAM for open-vocabulary segmentation, SpaTracker for dense 3D point tracking, FPFH+ICP point cloud registration to recover inter-frame camera extrinsics, and SVD on point cloud correspondences to compute object rotations, yielding 28,497 trajectories; and (2) VLM-based trajectory generation models (BLIP-2, VILA, PointLLM, MiniGPT-3D) that extend the vocabulary with 256 special trajectory tokens and frame generation as next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss. The key architectural insight is that discretizing continuous 6DoF poses into uniform bins allows standard autoregressive language modeling to produce trajectories, enabling nucleus sampling to generate multiple plausible trajectory candidates for a single action description. Depth information is incorporated into 2D VLMs via an additional pre-trained image encoder whose features are concatenated with RGB features before the LLM decoder.

## Results

On HOT3D, the best model (PointLLM 7B with point cloud input) achieves ADE 0.271 m and FDE 0.458 m for 3D position, ADE 0.208 m and FDE 0.327 m for 2D position, and GD 0.541 rad for 3D rotation in the 6DoF setting. VLM-based models consistently outperform the Seq2Seq transformer baseline, with PointLLM reducing 3D ADE by 0.103 m over Seq2Seq (0.271 vs. 0.374). Point cloud inputs yield the best performance across all metrics, and incorporating depth maps into 2D VLMs (BLIP-2, VILA) also improves results. Probabilistic sampling with 10 candidates further reduces 3D ADE to 0.212 m and FDE to 0.363 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Seq2Seq transformer baseline [86], all VLM-based models achieve substantially lower errors: PointLLM (7B) reduces 3D ADE from 0.374 m to 0.271 m (−27%) and 3D FDE from 0.625 m to 0.458 m (−27%), while also improving GD from 0.559 to 0.541 rad. The Seq2Seq model with Image+Depth input (ADE 0.341 m) still trails the best VLM by 0.07 m in 3D ADE, confirming that the language-modeling formulation with trajectory tokens is more effective than a direct regression head. Against USST [5], a dedicated 3D hand-trajectory forecasting model, the paper shows that USST trained from scratch on the automatically extracted EgoTraj (ADE 0.319 m) performs on par with USST pre-trained on densely recorded H2O/EgoPAT3D datasets (ADE 0.303 m), and fine-tuning the pre-trained USST with EgoTraj further improves 3D ADE to 0.254 m. This validates that the automatically extracted trajectories are a high-quality training signal comparable to expensive lab recordings. Notably, model scale does not monotonically help: BLIP-2 (2.7B) outperforms VILA (8B) by a wide margin (ADE 0.280 vs. 0.293 m), suggesting task-specific architecture matters more than raw parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of egocentric vision datasets and VLM-based trajectory generation. Prior datasets for 6DoF object manipulation (HOT3D, H2O, EgoPAT3D) rely on lab recordings with optical markers, multiple cameras, or depth sensors, limiting scale and diversity. The paper repurposes off-the-shelf components (Grounded SAM, SpaTracker, Depth Anything, FPFH/ICP registration, SVD) into an automated pipeline that extracts 6DoF trajectories from ordinary single-camera egocentric video without pre-recorded camera poses. On the modeling side, it extends the VLM-as-next-token-predictor paradigm (established for detection, grounding, and robotic control) to the specific problem of generating discretized 6DoF pose sequences from text, a formulation not previously applied to object manipulation trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 28,497-trajectory EgoTraj dataset is an order of magnitude larger in action-verb and object diversity than existing manipulation datasets, directly addressing the data-scarcity bottleneck for learning general-purpose robot manipulation from human demonstrations. The automated extraction pipeline is applicable to any egocentric video dataset, making it a reusable tool for the community. Key limitations include restriction to rigid objects, failure modes in segmentation (similar objects) and registration (abrupt camera motion), and evaluation on only 986 HOT3D test samples. Open questions include whether the extracted trajectories transfer to actual robotic manipulation, how the framework extends to deformable objects, and whether the modest absolute performance (ADE ~0.27 m) is sufficient for downstream control tasks.
