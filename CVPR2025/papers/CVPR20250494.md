## Problem

Diffusion models consistently produce local visual artifacts (blurred patches, broken textures) even when trained on massive datasets, yet existing detection methods treat this as a post-hoc spatial classification problem without explaining why or when artifacts form during generation. Uncertainty-based approaches capture only spatial variance in the final output, missing the temporal dynamics that actually drive artifact emergence. This paper asks: what is the temporal mechanism by which artifacts arise in the diffusion process, and can we intervene before they become irreversible?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250494/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely during the inference loop of a pre-trained DDPM/DDIM model, consuming the score network's output s_θ(x_t, t) at each diffusion step rather than pixel values directly. It produces two outputs: a binary artifact region mask Ω^a (spatial locations flagged by abnormal score dynamics) and the corrected image after applying targeted perturbations at a chosen correction timestep T_c. No additional training, annotation, or domain-specific data is required; the only hyperparameters are the detection window [T_d, T_c] and perturbation intensity γ.

## Method

ASCED decomposes the diffusion generation process into three temporal phases—Profiling (global layout), Mutation (local stochastic exploration), and Refinement (contextual rationalization)—and identifies artifacts as 'score traps' where certain regions exhibit abrupt acceleration-then-deceleration in their score trajectories during Mutation, decoupling from neighborhood evolution. Detection tracks weighted score dynamics Δ(w(t)·s_θ) between adjacent timesteps, with temporal weighting w(t)=(1−ᾱ_t)/√ᾱ_t derived from the clean-image prediction coefficient to equalize score scales across timesteps; regions exceeding an adaptive threshold τ (set via MAD of the score bank) are flagged. Correction uses Trajectory-aware Targeted Correction (TTC): at timestep T_c, only artifact-flagged pixels are replaced with a re-noised prediction (√ᾱ_Tc·x̂_0 + √(1−ᾱ_Tc)·ε) scaled by γ, disrupting the trapped score pattern while leaving non-artifact regions untouched to preserve generation diversity. The approach is fully training-free and operates in ~0.09 s per image, matching or surpassing supervised detectors (PAL, SARGD) across five datasets without domain-specific training.

## Results

ASCED achieves the best FID among all unsupervised methods across all five datasets, with headline values of 36.28 (FFHQ), 14.41 (ImageNet), 21.91 (LSUN-Cat), 27.66 (LSUN-Horse), and 12.53 (LSUN-Bedroom). It also attains the highest Precision (0.750 on ImageNet) and Recall (0.652 on LSUN-Horse) among unsupervised approaches, indicating simultaneous gains in fidelity and diversity. Compared to the supervised SARGD, ASCED improves FID on every dataset (e.g., 36.28 vs. 38.37 on FFHQ, 12.53 vs. 13.82 on LSUN-Bedroom) while requiring no training data. The method runs in ~0.09 s per image, 8.8× faster than the supervised PAL detector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unsupervised BayesDiff [20], ASCED improves FID on all five datasets (e.g., 36.28 vs. 36.99 on FFHQ, 27.66 vs. 28.70 on LSUN-Horse) while also achieving higher Recall, confirming that temporal score dynamics capture artifact signals that spatial-only uncertainty misses. Against the supervised SARGD [49], ASCED wins on FID across every dataset (e.g., 36.28 vs. 38.37 on FFHQ, 12.53 vs. 13.82 on LSUN-Bedroom) and substantially outperforms it on Recall (0.590 vs. 0.554 on LSUN-Bedroom), showing that SARGD's domain-specific detector degrades diversity. Against the supervised PAL+TTC hybrid [43], ASCED achieves lower FID on FFHQ (36.28 vs. 36.35), LSUN-Horse (27.66 vs. 28.68), and LSUN-Bedroom (12.53 vs. 12.71), but trails slightly on ImageNet (14.41 vs. 14.01) and LSUN-Cat (21.91 vs. 21.83), which the authors attribute to PAL being specifically trained on those domains. Compared to the unmodified DDIM baseline, ASCED consistently reduces FID (e.g., 29.36→27.66 on LSUN-Horse, a 5.8% relative improvement) while increasing Recall on all datasets, demonstrating that the trajectory-aware correction removes artifacts without collapsing generation diversity. The two internal baselines (State Replace, Score Clipping) confirm that naive correction strategies either degrade FID (State Replace: 37.09 on FFHQ) or fail to improve Recall, validating the need for the targeted perturbation design in TTC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based SDE framework (Song et al.) and DDPM's clean-image prediction to introduce a temporal analysis of score dynamics during diffusion inference, departing fundamentally from prior artifact detection approaches that treat the problem as a spatial classification task on final outputs (PAL, SARGD) or as pixel-level spatial uncertainty estimation (BayesDiff). The key conceptual departure is the identification of three temporal phases (Profiling, Mutation, Refinement) and the 'score trap' mechanism, which reframes artifact formation as a temporal anomaly rather than a purely spatial irregularity. The correction strategy (Trajectory-aware Targeted Correction) also differs from prior post-hoc noising-denoising schemes by operating within the ongoing diffusion process, drawing on SDEdit's insight about the model's refinement capability while avoiding its per-sample manipulation overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, domain-agnostic nature of ASCED makes it immediately applicable to any pre-trained diffusion model without dataset-specific annotation, addressing a practical gap that supervised detectors (PAL, SARGD) cannot fill in new or protected domains. The mechanistic explanation of artifacts as score traps during the Mutation phase provides a principled reason why spatial-only uncertainty methods fail, guiding future work toward temporal monitoring. However, detection accuracy remains below both supervised (PAL) and zero-shot LMM (LLaVA) baselines on most datasets, and the method struggles with low-contrast images and cases where the model successfully rationalizes initially abnormal patterns. Open questions include whether the three-phase decomposition generalizes to latent diffusion models, text-to-image systems, and video generation, and whether more robust discrimination between transient and persistent score anomalies can close the detection gap.
