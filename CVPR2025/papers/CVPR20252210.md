## Problem

Existing hair appearance methods face a fundamental trade-off: analytical BSDF models generalize to novel lighting and geometry but cannot reproduce fine appearance details like specular highlights and color variations, while neural rendering approaches capture photorealistic detail but overfit to training views and lighting, yielding poor relighting. No prior method achieves both high-fidelity reconstruction and fully functional relighting for real-world human hair.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252210/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera OLAT (one-light-at-a-time) captures from a light stage (50 cameras, 331 lights), strand-level hair geometry reconstructed by GroomCap (~150K scalp-rooted B-spline strands), a parametric head mesh, and SAM-derived hair segmentation masks. It produces a two-component relightable appearance model: (1) spatially varying BSDF parameters stored in a scalp-aligned 3D volume (absorption coefficients, per-segment rotation correctives, global roughness and IOR), and (2) dual-level spherical harmonic coefficients on ~52K chained cylindrical Gaussians. The model supports rendering under arbitrary HDR environment maps, direct material editing in scalp space, and animation of the hair geometry.

## Method

GroomLight employs a two-stage hybrid inverse rendering pipeline. Stage 1 optimizes an extended Chiang et al. hair BSDF via differentiable path tracing (Mitsuba 3 with path replay backpropagation), where the extension introduces a scalp-aligned albedo parameterization (spatially varying absorption coefficients on a 2D head-surface unwrap) and per-segment rotation correctives (Rodrigues-parameterized rotations) to compensate for geometry reconstruction errors. Stage 2 fits a light-aware residual on top of the BSDF rendering using 3D Gaussian Splatting with fixed-geometry chained cylindrical Gaussians along downsampled strands, whose appearance is encoded by nested dual-level spherical harmonics (light-conditioned inner SH feeding a view-conditioned outer SH) attached as 8 anchor SHs per strand with linear interpolation between segments. The decomposition ensures the BSDF handles primary light transport and generalization while the residual captures diffraction, interference, and other sub-BSDF details, enabling both photorealistic rendering and physically consistent relighting.

## Results

On 10 real-world hair subjects evaluated under 160 novel lighting conditions and 2 novel views, GroomLight (Full) achieves PSNR 29.71, SSIM 0.7727, and LPIPS 0.2461, substantially outperforming both baselines. The Stage 1 BSDF-only variant already reaches PSNR 28.67, while the full pipeline with the residual model adds approximately 1.0 dB PSNR and 0.03 SSIM. The method is evaluated on diverse hair characteristics including short/medium/long length, curly/wavy/straight style, and black/blond/gray color.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroomLight outperforms both baselines by a large margin across all three metrics. Against HairInverse [43] (a physics-based inverse rendering method using a homogeneous BSDF), GroomLight gains +3.21 dB PSNR, +0.096 SSIM, and −0.071 LPIPS; HairInverse's uniform material assumption cannot capture real-world color variation. Against GaussianHair [27] (a 3DGS-based neural rendering method), GroomLight gains +2.49 dB PSNR, +0.039 SSIM, and −0.046 LPIPS; GaussianHair overfits to training views and lightings, leading to poor generalization under novel conditions. Notably, even GroomLight's Stage 1 alone (BSDF only, no residual) already surpasses GaussianHair on PSNR (28.67 vs. 27.22), indicating that the extended BSDF with scalp-aligned albedo and rotation correctives is a strong baseline in its own right. The residual model in Stage 2 then adds the final ~1 dB improvement to reach photorealistic fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GroomLight sits at the intersection of two well-studied but previously separate lines of work: analytical hair BSDF models (Chiang et al. [11], Marschner et al. [30]) used in inverse rendering pipelines (Sun et al. [43]), and neural rendering representations based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al. [24]) applied to hair (GaussianHair [27]). The key conceptual departure is a hybrid inverse rendering pipeline that uses the BSDF for primary light transport and a light-aware dual-level spherical harmonics residual on chained cylindrical Gaussians for fine details, rather than committing to one paradigm. The geometry input comes from GroomCap [61], and differentiable path tracing via Mitsuba 3 [19] with path replay backpropagation [46] handles gradient computation in Stage 1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve photorealistic, fully functional relighting of real human hair from multi-view OLAT captures, bridging a long-standing gap between physically grounded models (which generalize but lack detail) and neural renderers (which capture detail but overfit to training lighting). The practical impact extends to virtual production, digital fashion, and VR where relightable hair assets are needed. Key limitations remain: the pipeline requires a calibrated light stage (50 cameras, 331 lights), runs at ~1 FPS, and depends on accurate geometry and segmentation, leaving in-the-wild generalization and real-time rendering as open problems the authors explicitly flag for future work.
