## Problem

Existing text-guided image editing methods rely on costly multi-step diffusion processes for both inversion and sampling, making them too slow for real-time or on-device applications. While few-step distillation approaches have reduced inference time, they still fall short of instant editing. No prior work had demonstrated one-step text-guided image editing that maintains competitive quality while achieving orders-of-magnitude speedup.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a source text prompt describing the original image, an edit text prompt specifying the desired change, and optionally a user-provided editing mask. If no mask is supplied, one is auto-generated by feeding the source image latent through the inversion network under both the source and edit prompts and taking the normalized difference of the resulting noise maps. The output is a single edited RGB image produced in 0.23 seconds on one A100 GPU, with the entire pipeline (inversion + editing) completed in one forward pass through the one-step generator.

## Method

SwiftEdit builds on SwiftBrushv2, a one-step text-to-image diffusion model, and introduces a one-step inversion network F_θ trained via a two-stage strategy: stage 1 pretrains on synthetic (noise, latent) pairs with regression and reconstruction losses, while stage 2 fine-tunes on real images using a DISTS perceptual loss plus an SDS-inspired regularization that keeps the inverted noise close to N(0,1) for editability. An IP-Adapter-style image-conditioned branch is added to the generator so that visual details are carried by the image embedding rather than being overfit into the inverted noise, preserving editing flexibility. At inference, a novel Attention Rescaling for Mask-aware Editing (ARaM) mechanism replaces the global image-condition scale with region-specific scales—amplifying source-image influence in non-edited regions for background preservation while reducing it in edited regions for prompt alignment—and introduces a separate text-alignment scale to control edit strength.

## Results

On PieBench (700 samples, 10 editing types), SwiftEdit achieves a runtime of 0.23 s on a single A100 GPU, making it at least 50× faster than multi-step methods (12.6–134 s) and 5–6× faster than few-step methods (1.3–5.1 s). In background preservation, SwiftEdit scores PSNR 23.33 and MSE 6.60×10⁴, surpassing all multi-step baselines except NT-Inv+P2P (PSNR 27.03) and ICD (PSNR 26.93). For editing semantics, SwiftEdit attains CLIP-Whole 25.16 (second-best overall) and CLIP-Edited 21.25, remaining competitive with the strongest multi-step and few-step methods. With ground-truth masks, CLIP-Whole improves to 25.56 and CLIP-Edited to 21.91, confirming the self-guided mask is nearly as effective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against multi-step methods, SwiftEdit (0.23 s) is roughly 55× faster than DDIM+PnP (12.62 s) and over 500× faster than NT-Inv+P2P (134.06 s), while achieving a higher PSNR (23.33 vs. 17.87–22.64) and lower MSE (6.60 vs. 81–220) than all multi-step baselines except NT-Inv+P2P and ICD. In CLIP-Whole, SwiftEdit (25.16) trails only TurboEdit (25.49) and DDIM+PnP (25.41) but leads all other multi-step methods; in CLIP-Edited it scores 21.25, slightly below DDIM+PnP (22.55) and DDIM+P2P (22.44). Among few-step methods, SwiftEdit is 5.7× faster than TurboEdit (1.32 s) and 7× faster than ICD (1.62 s), with a better MSE (6.60 vs. 9.48 for TurboEdit) and substantially higher CLIP-Whole (25.16 vs. 22.42 for ICD), though ICD retains a PSNR advantage (26.93 vs. 23.33). In the user study (140 participants, 20 prompts), SwiftEdit was preferred by 47.8% for editing semantics and 40% for background preservation over Null-text Inversion and TurboEdit. The gap between self-guided and ground-truth masks is minimal (CLIP-Whole 25.16 vs. 25.56; CLIP-Edited 21.25 vs. 21.91), validating the automatic mask extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

SwiftEdit departs from the dominant multi-step inversion-and-edit paradigm (DDIM/Null-text inversion followed by attention manipulation) by building entirely on a one-step text-to-image model (SwiftBrushv2) for both inversion and generation. Conceptually, it adapts encoder-based GAN inversion to the diffusion setting, using a network initialized from the one-step generator to predict editable noise in a single forward pass, and borrows the IP-Adapter decoupled cross-attention mechanism to condition reconstruction on the source image without overfitting the noise. The stage-2 regularization loss is a sign-flipped adaptation of Score Distillation Sampling that constrains the inverted noise to remain near N(0,1), preserving editability. Few-step editing methods (ReNoise, TurboEdit, ICD) represent the closest prior work, but SwiftEdit eliminates all iterative denoising, reducing both inversion and editing to a single step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving text-guided editing in 0.23 seconds on a single A100 GPU opens the door to on-device and interactive editing applications that were previously infeasible with multi-step or even few-step diffusion pipelines. However, the editing quality is competitive rather than superior to multi-step baselines (second-best in most CLIP and PSNR metrics), and the method inherits biases and quality ceilings from the underlying SwiftBrushv2 generator. The paper itself acknowledges the gap between 'instant-level' (0.23s) and true real-time editing, and the reliance on a single one-step backbone limits generalizability. Open questions include whether the one-step inversion framework transfers to higher-resolution or video editing settings, and whether the attention-rescaling mechanism can handle more complex, multi-object edits without explicit masks.
