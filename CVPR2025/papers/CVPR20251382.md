## Problem

Video-LLMs perform well on basic video understanding but fail at compositional reasoning requiring multi-step spatio-temporal inference across object relations, interactions, and events. Existing approaches to close this gap suffer from three limitations: compositional training data demands extensive manual annotation and lacks generalization, video semantics are captured only at coarse clip-level granularity, and standard black-box training provides no explicit supervision over intermediate reasoning steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251382/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw, untrimmed videos with no manual annotation or pre-labeled metadata, segmenting them via PySceneDetect and clustering-based keyframe extraction. It first produces a Spatio-Temporal Scene Graph (STSG) as a structured intermediate representation encoding objects, attributes, relations, motion edges, and cross-clip reference edges. From the STSG, it generates multi-step compositional QA pairs where each question is grounded in a specific temporal context, and the corresponding output includes both a final answer and a step-by-step Chain-of-Thought rationale whose length matches the number of reasoning steps in the sampled graph path.

## Method

STEP is a model-agnostic, graph-guided self-training framework with two core stages. First, Symbolic Structure Induction builds a unified STSG from raw videos through four sequential operations: visual splitting (scene-cut detection and keyframe clustering), semantics parsing (inducing Frame Scene Graphs with object, attribute, and relation nodes via dual-verification to suppress hallucination), dynamic merging (collapsing redundant static nodes and introducing motion edges to form Temporal Scene Graphs), and cross-clip bridging (adding reference edges between corresponding objects across clips plus event edges). Second, Stepwise Graph-driven Rationale Learning samples multi-step reasoning paths of controllable length N over the STSG, where each node expansion adds a sub-question and answer, progressively constructing a compositional question with an explicit CoT rationale. Training uses a joint loss L = L_answer + λL_rationale (λ=1) that supervises both the final answer and the intermediate rationale, and the entire pipeline is iterated so the model progressively improves its own data generation in a self-enhancing closed loop.

## Results

On compositional reasoning benchmarks, VideoChat2*STEP achieves 39.2% accuracy (score 3.2) on AGQA and 39.8% (score 2.8) on STAR, while VILA*STEP reaches 38.9% (3.2) and 40.3% (2.8) respectively. The headline result is a 21.3% relative improvement on ≥3-step reasoning tasks in STAR (27.7% → 33.6% for VideoChat2*STEP), demonstrating the method's particular strength on complex multi-step inference. On standard QA, STEP also yields consistent gains (e.g., MSVD-QA 79.8% vs. 78.5% baseline; ActivityNet-QA 56.0% vs. 54.8%), and on comprehensive/long-video benchmarks (MVBench 49.2%, TempCompass 55.4%, MovieChat-1K 67.6%, MLVU 46.4% for VideoChat2*STEP), STEP outperforms both the Instruct and Distillation controls.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STEP outperforms all external Video-LLM baselines on compositional reasoning: VideoChat2*STEP (39.2% AGQA, 39.8% STAR) surpasses Video-LLaVA (34.8%, 24.9%), VideoChatGPT (no compositional results), and VideoChat (no compositional results) by large margins. Against the two internal controls, STEP beats Instruct (trained on 2× more manually annotated data) by 1.8% on AGQA and 3.6% on STAR for VideoChat2, and by 1.5% and 2.3% for VILA, showing that rationale-rich self-generated data is more effective than larger human-annotated sets. STEP also outperforms the GPT-4V Distillation control (38.2% AGQA, 37.6% STAR) by 1.0% and 2.2% respectively, attributed to better alignment with the base model's knowledge and iterative self-improvement. On standard QA, STEP maintains or slightly improves over the VideoChat2 backbone (79.8% vs. 79.2% MSVD-QA), confirming no degradation on general tasks. On long-video and comprehensive benchmarks (Table 3), STEP leads all controls: e.g., MVBench 49.2% vs. 48.5% (Distillation) and 47.9% (Instruct), and MLVU 46.4% vs. 46.0% and 44.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from Video-of-Thought (VoT), which feeds STSG representations directly into the model as input and relies on task-specific CoT prompts, by instead using STSGs as a *generative scaffold* to produce training data (QA pairs with step-by-step rationales) that the model then learns from. It also extends the video self-training line initiated by Video-STAR, which required labeled metadata and simplistic prompts, by operating on raw unlabeled videos and leveraging structured graph reasoning paths to produce multi-step compositional tasks. Conceptually, the work bridges graph-based reasoning literature (reasoning over structured hierarchies) with the Video-LLM self-improvement paradigm, repurposing scene graphs not as model inputs but as data-generation engines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STEP offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe for improving compositional reasoning in Video-LLMs without expensive manual annotation, which is a significant bottleneck in the field. The finding that self-generated rationale-rich data outperforms both manually annotated instruction data (at half the size) and GPT-4V distillation suggests that structured, graph-guided data generation may be a more effective path than simply scaling annotation or relying on stronger teacher models. Open questions include whether the iterative self-training loop converges or degrades over many cycles, how STSG quality scales to very long or highly dynamic videos, and whether the approach transfers to other modalities (e.g., 3D scenes or embodied agents) where spatio-temporal compositionality is equally critical.
