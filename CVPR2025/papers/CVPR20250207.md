## Problem

NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting methods model view-dependent appearance as a black box without decomposing it into materials and illumination, leading to physically inconsistent reconstructions with artifacts like 'baked-in' specular highlights in the diffuse albedo. Existing neural inverse rendering methods such as NeILF++ lack informative physics-based priors to disambiguate the specular and diffuse BRDF lobes, leaving the material-lighting-geometry ambiguity largely unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images (HDR 1600×1200 on NeILF++, LDR 1600×1200 on DTU) along with camera poses and, for the NeILF++ benchmark, ground-truth SDF geometry. It outputs a signed distance field for geometry, spatially-varying Disney BRDF parameters (albedo, metallicness, roughness), a neural incident light field representing illumination, and synthesized novel-view RGB images. The rendering integral is approximated with a fixed set of 256 Fibonacci-sampled incident directions, and training proceeds in three stages (geometry 5K, material 1K, joint 30K iterations) on a single A6000 GPU.

## Method

PBR-NeRF extends the NeILF++ implicit differential renderer with two novel physics-based losses applied to the Disney BRDF. The Conservation of Energy Loss (L_cons) is a ReLU-style penalty that activates only when the hemisphere-integrated BRDF weight exceeds unity, preventing the model from reflecting more light than it receives. The NDF-weighted Specular Loss (L_spec) uses a softmax-weighted, detached normal distribution function to selectively penalize the diffuse lobe in angular regions where specular reflection dominates, thereby forcing the specular lobe to account for highlights rather than baking them into albedo. These losses are added to the standard RGB rendering, reflection, and smoothness losses in a three-phase optimization (geometry → material → joint), with the NDF term detached from the computation graph so that gradients flow only through the diffuse contribution, ensuring the specular lobe compensates rather than the diffuse lobe being arbitrarily suppressed.

## Results

On the NeILF++ dataset, PBR-NeRF achieves state-of-the-art material estimation with mean albedo PSNR of 20.08 (vs. 17.29 for NeILF++), roughness PSNR of 22.47 (vs. 21.83), and metallicness PSNR of 21.62 (vs. 18.84), while maintaining competitive novel view synthesis (RGB PSNR 31.27 vs. 30.51). On DTU, PBR-NeRF improves mean RGB PSNR to 28.63±0.29 (vs. 27.85±1.04 for NeILF++) and reduces Chamfer Distance to 0.828±0.09 mm (vs. 1.167±0.68 mm), with notably lower variance across 3 runs. The ablation confirms both physics-based losses contribute: adding only L_cons or L_spec each yields ~2.3 PSNR albedo gain, while combining both achieves 3.28 PSNR albedo improvement over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeILF++ (the direct baseline), PBR-NeRF wins on albedo PSNR (+2.79), albedo SSIM (+10.95), roughness PSNR (+0.64), metallicness PSNR (+2.78), and RGB PSNR (+0.76), but loses on metallicness SSIM (74.08 vs. 83.46), suggesting the specular loss trades some structural consistency in metallicness maps for more accurate PSNR. Against NeILF, PBR-NeRF dominates on roughness (+6.28 PSNR), metallicness PSNR (+2.20), and albedo (+1.93 PSNR, +12.29 SSIM), though NeILF retains a marginal RGB PSNR edge (31.50 vs. 31.27). Against SG-ENV, PBR-NeRF wins on all material metrics except albedo PSNR on the three Env scenes (where SG-ENV's explicit Spherical Gaussian lighting is better suited), but PBR-NeRF surpasses SG-ENV by over 3 albedo PSNR on the more challenging Mix scenes. On DTU, PBR-NeRF outperforms NeRFactor by 4.35 mean RGB PSNR and beats both VolSDF (0.857 mm) and NeuS (0.837 mm) on Chamfer Distance (0.828 mm), while also improving over NeILF++ by 0.78 PSNR and 0.339 mm with substantially lower variance (±0.29 vs. ±1.04 PSNR). PhySG is the weakest baseline across all metrics, trailing PBR-NeRF by 5–15 PSNR depending on the quantity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PBR-NeRF is a direct extension of NeILF++ [51], reusing its three-network architecture (NeRF SDF, NeILF, BRDF MLP) and Disney BRDF formulation without modifying the forward rendering pipeline. The conceptual departure is the introduction of two physics-based losses—Conservation of Energy and NDF-weighted Specular—that replace the Lambertian prior used in NeILF/NeILF++, which the authors argue suppresses the specular lobe and causes 'baked-in' highlights in the diffuse albedo. More broadly, the paper positions itself against the NeRF/3DGS family that models view-dependent appearance as a black box, arguing that explicit PBR constraints are necessary for physically consistent material estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two proposed losses are simple, architecture-agnostic, and directly transferable to other inverse rendering or 3D reconstruction frameworks that estimate BRDF parameters, making them a practical tool for the community. However, the method remains tied to the NeILF++ pipeline (SDF-based geometry, implicit incident light field), and evaluation is limited to two datasets with only qualitative material assessment on DTU due to LDR constraints. Open questions include whether these physics-based priors generalize to 3D Gaussian Splatting representations, unbounded outdoor scenes, or real-world captures with unknown tone mapping, and whether the fixed Fibonacci sampling of incident directions limits scalability to more complex lighting.
