## Problem

The standard Adaptive Density Control (ADC) in 3D Gaussian Splatting relies on view-averaged gradient thresholding for densification and global opacity resets for pruning, which systematically overlooks under-optimized points (especially large Gaussians with low average gradients) and cannot identify all 3D zones requiring point addition. Additionally, ADC lacks a mechanism to handle ill-conditioned points whose falsely high opacity occludes valid geometry, leading to incorrect depth estimates and rendering artifacts in challenging regions such as transparent surfaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images, an SfM-initialized 3D Gaussian point cloud, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and offline-extracted feature correspondences (via LightGlue) between adjacent views. It produces a novel-view rendered image, an optimized 3D Gaussian point cloud with calibrated geometry, and a depth map. The rendering error map is computed per-view against ground truth, and cross-view region matching is performed at the feature level rather than pixel level, making the approach resolution-agnostic and compatible with half-resolution training protocols used in dynamic scene benchmarks.

## Method

LPM identifies error-contributing 3D zones by first generating a per-view rendering error map, then using LightGlue feature matching to locate corresponding error regions in a neighboring view, and finally casting cone-shaped ray bundles from both camera centers through the paired 2D regions to compute their 3D intersection as the error source zone. Within each identified zone, LPM performs two operations: (1) localized point densification at a lower threshold than the global ADC (cloning small Gaussians along positional gradients, splitting large ones) or adding new Gaussians at the zone center when sparse, and (2) opacity reset of high-opacity points located in front of the zone, providing a correction opportunity for ill-conditioned points that would otherwise occlude valid geometry. A density-aware pruning step then removes low-opacity points to bound model expansion. The entire pipeline is model-agnostic and integrates as a plugin into existing 3DGS, 2DGS, MipGS, PixelGS, and SpaceTimeGS with minimal additional training cost.

## Results

On static 3D scenes, LPM as a plugin consistently improves all tested 3DGS variants. PixelGS*+LPM achieves the best PSNR of 27.80 on Mip-NeRF 360, 24.02 on Tanks & Temples, and 29.65 on Deep Blending, setting new state-of-the-art on the first two. On the dynamic Neural 3D Video dataset, STGS*+LPM reaches 32.40 PSNR at 140 FPS, surpassing STGS (32.05) and 4DGS (32.01) while maintaining real-time rendering speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPM acts as a drop-in plugin that improves every 3DGS variant tested: 3DGS* gains +0.12/+0.16/+0.21 PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360/T&T/Deep Blending, 2DGS* gains +0.27/+0.07/+0.17, MipGS* gains +0.19/+0.13/+0.03, and PixelGS* gains +0.26/+0.27/+0.07. PixelGS*+LPM achieves the best PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 (27.80) and Tanks & Temples (24.02), surpassing MipGS*+LPM (27.70/23.82) and 3DGS*+LPM (27.59/23.83). On the dynamic Neural 3D Video dataset, STGS*+LPM (32.40 PSNR, 140 FPS) outperforms STGS (32.05), 4DGS (32.01, 114 FPS), and Dynamic-4DGS (31.15, 30 FPS), while matching the rendering speed of STGS. Compared to alternative point-management methods, LPM achieves comparable or better quality with far lower training cost: on the PlayRoom scene, 3DGS+LPM reaches 30.22 PSNR in 23 min with 186k Gaussians, whereas GaussianPro runs out of memory and PixelGS requires 35 min; on Truck, 3DGS+LPM (25.61 PSNR, 21 min) beats GaussianPro (25.40, 36 min) and PixelGS (25.51, 37 min). The gains are most pronounced on challenging regions involving transparency and fine geometry, where LPM's opacity reset mechanism corrects ill-conditioned points that standard ADC cannot address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its dynamic extension SpaceTimeGS, targeting the Adaptive Density Control (ADC) mechanism that governs point densification and pruning during optimization. While prior work such as GaussianPro (progressive gradient propagation) and PixelGS (pixel-aware gradient scaling) attempt to improve densification by modifying gradient signals, LPM departs from this gradient-centric paradigm by leveraging multiview geometry constraints and rendering error maps to identify specific 3D zones requiring intervention. The use of LightGlue for cross-view feature correspondence to triangulate error source zones is a repurposing of standard SfM-style matching in a novel context, and the opacity reset of front-facing ill-conditioned points is a mechanism not present in any prior GS variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LPM's value as a model-agnostic plugin that consistently improves 3DGS, 2DGS, MipGS, PixelGS, and SpaceTimeGS with minimal training overhead makes it practically attractive for the GS community. The opacity reset mechanism addresses a previously underappreciated failure mode—high-opacity points occluding valid geometry behind error zones—offering a geometry calibration path beyond mere densification. However, the authors acknowledge that the densification rule within identified zones still follows 3DGS conventions, leaving open whether zone-specific densification strategies could yield further gains. The modest PSNR improvements (typically 0.1–0.5 dB) and the reliance on offline feature matching limit the method's applicability to fully online or streaming settings.
