## Problem

Frame-level autoregressive video generation suffers from a fundamental training-inference gap: existing methods like Genie condition next-frame prediction on masked (partially observable) frames during training but must rely on complete generated frames at inference, degrading motion coherence. Meanwhile, patch-level autoregressive approaches avoid this gap but inherit the suboptimal raster-scan generation order from image generation, which is ill-suited to the strong temporal causality in video. The paper asks whether a proper form of teacher forcing can be achieved at the frame level to bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

MAGI takes complete observation frames (encoded by a 3D-VAE from OmniTokenizer/Cosmos or a 2D-VAE from SD 1.4) as conditioning context, along with masked frames to be generated, at 256×256 resolution. For first-frame conditional generation, a single input frame seeds the sequence; for video prediction, multiple context frames are provided. The model outputs a video sequence of latent tokens decoded back to pixels, with each frame generated via 64 iterative denoising steps. Trained on 16–17 frame clips, it can autoregressively extend to 100+ frames using KV Cache for near-linear inference scaling.

## Method

MAGI introduces Complete Teacher Forcing (CTF), which prepends complete (unmasked) observation frames to the masked input sequence and uses a custom temporal attention mask so that each masked frame attends to all preceding observation frames plus itself, making training and inference attention patterns identical. The architecture is a Transformer decoder with interleaved 2D spatial attention and 1D temporal attention layers, topped by a diffusion head (from MAR) that predicts masked tokens via denoising. To combat exposure bias and error accumulation, MAGI employs dynamic interval training (randomly sampling frames at varying temporal intervals with learnable interval embeddings) and dynamic noise injection (adding Gaussian noise to observation latents with learnable noise-level embeddings). This hybrid design—masked modeling within each frame, causal modeling across frames—achieves a +23% FVD improvement over Masked Teacher Forcing and outperforms patch-level AR baselines like Omni by 21.4 FVD points on Kinetics-600.

## Results

On Kinetics-600 video prediction, MAGI achieves an FVD of 11.5, outperforming the best patch-level AR baseline Omni (FVD 32.9) by 21.4 points and establishing a new state-of-the-art among autoregressive methods. On UCF-101 unconditional video generation, MAGI with the Cosmos VAE achieves FVD 297.8, outperforming the DiT-based NAR model Latte (FVD 477.9) by ~180 points and approaching the best NAR method Matten (FVD 210.6). The paper also demonstrates that MAGI can generate coherent sequences exceeding 100 frames when trained on only 16-frame clips.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Kinetics-600, MAGI (FVD 11.5) decisively outperforms all patch-level AR baselines: Omni (32.9), Phenaki (36.4), and ViVQVAE (64.3), with the largest gap of 21.4 FVD points over Omni. However, MAGI still trails the strongest NAR methods—MAGVIT-v2 (4.3) and RIN (10.8)—indicating that frame-level AR generation has not yet closed the gap with bidirectional NAR approaches on this benchmark. On UCF-101 unconditional generation, MAGI with the Cosmos VAE (FVD 297.8) beats VideoGPT (2880.6), Latte (477.9), and DiM (358.8), but remains behind the best NAR method Matten (210.6) by ~87 FVD points. With the weaker SD1.4 VAE, MAGI (420.6) still surpasses Latte (477.9) and all GAN-based baselines (MoCoGAN 2886.9, StyleGAN-V 1431.0, etc.). The paper also shows that CTF outperforms MTF by ~23% in FVD on first-frame conditional prediction, and that combining dynamic interval training with noise injection yields the best overall performance in ablation studies.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGI carves new ground at the intersection of masked image generation (MAR, MaskGIT) and causal video modeling by introducing Complete Teacher Forcing (CTF), a training paradigm that conditions next-frame prediction on fully observed history frames rather than masked ones. It departs from Genie and Diffusion Forcing, which both condition on partially observed (masked or noisy) teacher frames, and from patch-level AR methods (VideoGPT, Omni) that rely on raster-scan spatial order. The architecture borrows MAR's diffusion head and GameNGen's noise injection, but the core contribution is the attention-mask design that makes frame-level causality consistent between training and inference while retaining KV Cache efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CTF yields a 23% FVD improvement over MTF—despite MTF achieving slightly better per-frame FID—challenges the assumption that individual frame quality tracks temporal coherence, and suggests that the teacher forcing paradigm is a first-order design choice in AR video generation. The ability to extrapolate 100+ frames from 16-frame training with KV Cache linear scaling is practically significant for long-form generation. Limitations include degradation on non-periodic motions and evaluation primarily on UCF-101 (a relatively simple, low-diversity dataset); open questions include whether CTF's advantages persist at higher resolutions, longer training sequences, and more complex action categories, and whether the frame-level granularity generalizes to text-conditioned or multi-scene generation.
