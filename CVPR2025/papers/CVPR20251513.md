## Problem

High-dynamic scene optical flow suffers from spatial blur and temporal discontinuity caused by large displacement in frame imaging, breaking the basic spatiotemporal assumptions of optical flow. Existing multimodal methods that introduce event cameras to assist frame cameras perform direct spatiotemporal feature fusion, but this is ineffective due to the large modality gap arising from heterogeneous data representations between frame and event modalities. The paper addresses how to bridge this gap to achieve truly complementary fusion for dense and continuous optical flow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251513/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pixel-aligned frame images and event streams collected via a coaxial optical device with a beam splitter, where hardware calibration ensures shared optics and software calibration (stereo rectification plus pixel offset) refines alignment. The event stream is segmented into T=20 temporal slices, and the frame is processed through a shared feature pyramid network. The output is a temporally continuous optical flow field that must be accumulated over matched points along the time axis to produce a final displacement estimate comparable to frame-based methods.

## Method

The core idea is to construct a common spatiotemporal gradient space as an intermediate bridge: both frame (via ∇I·U) and event (via U·Σp_i·C) modalities are theoretically transformed into the same data representation, and their similarity distribution is shown to be consistent with extracted boundary distributions. In the visual boundary localization stage, a shared feature pyramid extracts boundary features from both modalities, and a K-L divergence loss constrains the boundary similarity distribution to match the gradient similarity distribution, yielding reference boundary points as a template. In the motion correlation fusion stage, the reference template guides K-Means clustering of frame-based correlation features for spatial density and extended Kalman filter tracking of event-based correlation features for temporal continuity, with a cross-modal transformer fusing the two. Training is self-supervised in two steps: first training encoders and boundary localization with photometric and KL/entropy losses, then training the fusion module with spatial/temporal correlation errors and cross-modal flow consistency losses.

## Results

On the synthetic Event-KITTI dataset, ComST-Flow achieves 0.72 EPE and 3.83% F1-all, outperforming the best frame-only method GMA (0.79 EPE, 4.85%) and the closest multimodal baseline ABDA-Flow (0.75 EPE, 4.27%). On real DSEC with fast motion (Fast-DSEC), ComST-Flow reaches 0.58 EPE and 1.96% F1-all, a 18% relative EPE reduction over ABDA-Flow (0.71) and a 53% reduction over the best frame-only method GMA (1.24). On Slow-DSEC, the advantage is even larger: 0.47 EPE vs. 0.63 for ABDA-Flow and 0.93 for GMA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComST-Flow outperforms all seven baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest frame-only supervised method GMA (Jiang, ICCV 2021), ComST-Flow reduces EPE from 1.24 to 0.58 on Fast-DSEC (53% improvement) and from 0.93 to 0.47 on Slow-DSEC (49% improvement), with the gap widening under fast motion where spatial blur and temporal discontinuity are most severe. The event-only method E-RAFT (Gehrig, 3DV 2021) performs competitively in slow scenes (0.95 EPE on Slow-DSEC) but degrades to 2.48 EPE on Event-KITTI due to spatial sparsity of events. Among multimodal baselines, BFlow (Gehrig, TPAMI 2024) uses direct frame-event fusion and achieves 0.87 EPE on Fast-DSEC, while ABDA-Flow (Zhou, ICLR 2024) improves to 0.71 via domain-adaptation bridging; ComST-Flow further reduces this to 0.58 by explicitly fusing spatially dense frame correlation with temporally continuous event correlation under a common spatiotemporal gradient constraint. The unsupervised frame-only methods ARFlow (Liu, CVPR 2020) and Selfflow (Liu, CVPR 2019) are far less competitive (EPE > 14 on DSEC), confirming that high-dynamic degradation fundamentally breaks the photometric constancy assumption they rely on.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frame-event multimodal optical flow line initiated by BFlow and the authors' own ABDA-Flow (ICLR 2024), which performed uni-directional motion knowledge transfer from event to frame modality. The key departure is the introduction of a common spatiotemporal gradient space as an interpretable intermediate bridge, arguing that direct feature-level fusion (as in BFlow) is ineffective due to the heterogeneous data representation between frame and event modalities. The fusion is made bi-directional and guided by boundary templates located in this common space, combining K-Means spatial clustering (for frame density) with Extended Kalman Filter temporal tracking (for event continuity) before a cross-modal transformer fuses the results. The method repurposes standard optical flow backbones (RAFT, FlowFormer) and feature pyramid networks rather than proposing a new backbone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: frame cameras fail under fast motion due to motion blur and low frame rate, and the common spatiotemporal gradient space provides an interpretable mechanism for cross-modal alignment that could inform other frame-event fusion tasks. However, the method is complex with many hand-crafted components (Canny boundaries, K-Means, EKF, multiple loss terms), and the authors acknowledge a fundamental limitation for radial (z-axis) motion that neither frame nor event cameras can capture. The proposed dataset is small and manually annotated, limiting generalization claims, and the performance gains over ABDA-Flow, while consistent, are moderate rather than transformative. Open questions include whether the common gradient space generalizes to other sensor pairs and whether the multi-stage training protocol can be simplified.
