## Problem

Scaling input image resolution is critical for VLM performance on text-rich tasks, but ViT-based encoders become prohibitively slow and token-heavy at high resolutions, inflating both vision encoding latency and LLM prefilling time. Prior approaches rely on tiling strategies or post-hoc token pruning to manage this cost, adding architectural complexity. The paper asks whether a single, well-designed vision backbone can achieve the optimal resolution-latency-accuracy tradeoff simply by scaling input resolution, without any token pruning or resampling.

## Input / Output

FastVLM accepts a high-resolution RGB image (256×256 up to 2048×2048, or a 2×2 tiled grid at 1024 tile size for dynamic resolution) paired with a text question or prompt. The FastViTHD encoder compresses the image into a compact set of visual tokens (16 at 256×256, 64 at 512×512, 144 at 768×768, 256 at 1024×1024, 1280 at 2048×2048) that are projected and concatenated with text tokens for the LLM decoder. The output is a free-form text response generated autoregressively by the LLM (Qwen2-0.5B/1.5B/7B or Vicuna-7B).

## Method

FastViTHD is a 5-stage hybrid encoder (125.1M params) with three RepMixer convolutional stages followed by two multi-headed self-attention stages, using embedding dimensions [96, 192, 384, 768, 1536] and depths [2, 12, 24, 4, 2]. The key architectural intervention over prior FastViT is an additional downsampling stage that reduces the spatial resolution by a factor of 32 (vs. 16 in ViTamin), yielding 4× fewer tokens than FastViT and 16× fewer than ViT-L/14 at 336×336, while multi-scale features from earlier stages are fused via 2D depthwise convolutions to preserve fine-grained detail. The encoder is CLIP-pretrained on DataComp-DR-1B, then fine-tuned end-to-end with the LLM using a 2-stage (LLaVA-1.5) or 3-stage (adding a 15M-sample resolution-scaling stage) visual instruction tuning procedure. The paper's central empirical contribution is a systematic Pareto analysis across (resolution, LLM size) pairs on real hardware (M1 MacBook Pro), demonstrating that FastViTHD shifts the accuracy-vs-TTFT frontier by >2.5 points over FastViT and achieves 3.2× faster TTFT than SigLIP-SO400M-based VLMs at comparable accuracy.

## Results

FastVLM with the FastViTHD vision encoder (125M params) achieves 78.3% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy and 67.7 average retrieval performance on CLIP benchmarks, matching ViT-L/14 (304M params) while being 2.4× smaller and 6.9× faster. In the VLM setting, FastVLM with Qwen2-0.5B at 1024×1024 resolution (256 visual tokens) achieves 65.6 SeedBench and 61.0 DocVQA, outperforming LLaVA-OneVision (65.5 SeedBench, 70.0 DocVQA at 1152×1152 with 7290 tokens) while being 85× faster in TTFT (166ms vs 14124ms). At the 7B scale with Qwen2, FastVLM at 1024×1024 (R41) reaches 73.4 TextVQA, 82.7 DocVQA, 47.3 MMMU, and 74.1 SeedBench, surpassing MM1 and LLaVA-NeXT across text-rich benchmarks. The vision encoder alone encodes a 768×768 image in 122.6ms vs 127.4ms for ViT-L/14 at 336×336, while producing only 144 tokens vs 576.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViT-L/14 (304M), FastViTHD (125M) is 2.4× smaller and 6.9× faster while achieving comparable CLIP zero-shot performance (78.3% vs 79.2% ImageNet, 66.3 vs 66.3 on 38 tasks) and superior VLM accuracy at matched token counts (62.8 vs 60.1 Avg-5 at 576 tokens). Compared to ConvNeXT-XXL (632M), FastViTHD is 6.8× smaller and 3.3× faster while matching VLM performance (63.9 vs 63.9 Avg-5 at 256 tokens). Against LLaVA-OneVision at 1152×1152, FastVLM with the same Qwen2-0.5B LLM achieves comparable SeedBench (69.2 vs 65.5) and DocVQA (70.4 vs 70.0) with 85× lower TTFT (166ms vs 14124ms) and a 3.4× smaller encoder. Versus MM1 (ViT-H, 720 tokens), FastVLM at 1024×1024 (256 tokens) matches or surpasses across benchmarks while generating 5× fewer visual tokens; at 2048×2048 it leads by 3+ points on DocVQA (87.6 vs 68.4) and MMMU (40.1 vs 33.2). Against ConvLLaVA (ConvNeXT-L), FastVLM gains +8.4% on TextVQA and +12.5% on DocVQA at 768 resolution while being 22% faster, and the gap widens to 2× faster at 1024 resolution. Among token-pruning methods (M3, MQT, PruMerge, VisionZip, DynamicLLaVA), FastViTHD trained at 256×256 (16 tokens) outperforms all of them at 336×336 with the same or fewer tokens, demonstrating that hierarchical downsampling is more effective than post-hoc pruning on isotropic ViTs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FastViT hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture (ICCV 2023) and its MobileCLIP pretraining recipe (CVPR 2024) into the VLM setting, introducing FastViTHD with an additional downsampling stage to further reduce visual tokens. It departs from the dominant ViT-based VLM paradigm (LLaVA, SigLIP, InternViT) by arguing that hierarchical architectures naturally produce fewer tokens through progressive downsampling, eliminating the need for post-hoc token pruning methods (PruMerge, Matryoshka, VisionZip) or tiling strategies (AnyRes, Sphinx). The training methodology follows the LLaVA-1.5 two-stage setup, with an optional intermediate resolution-scaling stage inspired by LLaVA-NeXT and MM1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 85× TTFT improvement over LLaVA-OneVision at comparable accuracy with the same 0.5B LLM is a significant practical result for on-device and latency-sensitive VLM deployment. The systematic Pareto-optimal analysis across (resolution, LLM size) pairs provides a useful framework for practitioners to select operating points under specific latency budgets. Open questions include whether the efficiency gains generalize to non-Apple hardware, whether the 125M encoder ceiling limits performance at very high resolutions beyond 2048, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-image inputs where token budgets are even tighter.
