## Problem

Existing learned video compression (LVC) frameworks rely heavily on motion estimation and CNN-based context models, which limit generalizability and restrict global perception to local receptive fields. While Transformer-based LVC (e.g., VCT) partially addresses these issues, it introduces high computational overhead and a spatial feature bottleneck from its single-scale latent representation, leaving a gap between global context modeling and efficient, high-fidelity compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251063/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of RGB video frames of shape [3, H, W] as input, with training on 256×256 crops from Vimeo90K and evaluation on 1080p (UVG, MCL-JCV, HEVC Class B) and 480p (HEVC Class C) sequences. Each frame is independently encoded into a 192-channel latent feature map at 1/16 spatial resolution, which is then quantized and entropy-coded using two previously transmitted latent features as temporal context. The output is a compressed bitstream and reconstructed frames, evaluated via PSNR and MS-SSIM rate-distortion curves with BD-rate against HM-16.26 (HEVC) as baseline.

## Method

FLAVC introduces a Feature-Level Attention (FLA) module comprising Feature-Level Self-Attention (FLSA) for spatial context and Feature-Level Cross-Attention (FLCA) for temporal context, which replaces standard attention weight matrices with a global context matrix computed via k^T·v over batch-wise 1D vectors of high-dimensional patch embeddings. This design, inspired by Efficient Attention but extended to feature-level operations, resolves sequence-length mismatches between current and context latents through channel-independent matrix multiplication without added parameters. A Dense Overlapping Patcher (DP) segments each latent into 64 overlapping 4×4 patches to preserve local spatial detail while reducing per-patch computational cost. A Transformer-CNN Mixed (TCM) encoder/decoder with four 2× down-sampling stages replaces VCT's ELIC encoder, enriching the single-scale 192-channel latent with multi-scale global features to alleviate the spatial bottleneck. The full model is trained end-to-end with a rate-distortion loss (Lagrangian formulation) on Vimeo90K, with a lighter FLAVC-Small variant using 4× instead of 8× linear projection expansion.

## Results

FLAVC achieves state-of-the-art BD-rate reductions across all four evaluation datasets, with headline results of −61.46% (PSNR) and −72.85% (MS-SSIM) on UVG, −63.76% and −80.15% on MCL-JCV, −58.76% and −76.36% on HEVC Class B, and −58.71% and −68.64% on HEVC Class C, all relative to the HM-16.26 (HEVC) baseline. The lighter FLAVC-Small variant retains strong performance (e.g., −41.94% PSNR on UVG) while reducing encoding cost from 1695 to 1093 kMACs/pixel. FLAVC also outperforms the VVC reference codec VTM-13.2 (−27.32% PSNR on UVG) by a wide margin, and surpasses the closest learned competitor SDD (−42.08% PSNR on UVG) by approximately 19 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLAVC outperforms all 10 compared baselines on every dataset and metric where results are available. Against the strongest learned competitor SDD [26], FLAVC improves PSNR BD-rate by 19.4 points on UVG (−61.46% vs −42.08%) and by 34.0 points on MCL-JCV (−63.76% vs −29.74%), while also surpassing SDD on MS-SSIM across all four datasets. Compared to the Transformer-based VCT [20], which actually performs worse than the HEVC baseline on PSNR (+12.57% on UVG), FLAVC achieves a 74-point improvement, demonstrating that the feature-level attention mechanism and TCM encoder resolve VCT's feature bottleneck. Against the DCVC family (DCVC-TCM [25], DCVC-DC [14]), FLAVC leads by 51–69 points in PSNR BD-rate on UVG, confirming that explicit feature-level global perception outperforms motion-based context modeling. FLAVC also surpasses the VVC reference codec VTM-13.2 by 34 points on UVG PSNR, and the lighter FLAVC-Small variant still exceeds VTM-13.2 by 15 points while using fewer parameters (89.9M vs. VCT's 154.2M) and lower encoding cost (1093 vs. 1571 kMACs/pixel).

## Relation to Prior Work

FLAVC extends the transformer-based LVC paradigm introduced by VCT, replacing its standard multi-head self-attention with a Feature-Level Attention (FLA) module that operates on high-dimensional feature vectors rather than single-channel embeddings, drawing directly on the global context matrix formulation from Efficient Attention. It departs from the dominant conditional coding lineage (DCVC series) by eliminating motion estimation and compensation entirely, instead relying on explicit feature maps and attention for temporal context. The Transformer-CNN Mixed (TCM) encoder is adopted from prior learned image compression work to address the single-scale feature bottleneck inherited from VCT's ELIC-based encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLAVC demonstrates that motion estimation—a near-universal component in learned video compression—can be entirely replaced by feature-level attention for temporal context, achieving substantially lower BD-rates than both motion-based and prior transformer-based methods across four datasets. The FLAVC-Small variant offers a practical efficiency-performance trade-off that may be more deployable than full-size models. Open limitations include performance saturation at high bit rates due to the 1/16 down-sampled feature map bottleneck and residual background noise, which the authors acknowledge as directions for future work. The dense overlapping patcher and FLA module are modular components that could inform other transformer-based compression or coding architectures.
