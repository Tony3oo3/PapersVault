## Problem

Tokenized multi-agent traffic policies trained via open-loop behavior cloning suffer from covariate shift when unrolled in closed-loop, as small per-step errors compound into unseen states where the policy degrades. Existing remedies—DAgger (requires expert queries), RL (hard-to-design realism rewards), and GAIL (training instability)—have not achieved competitive realism on the WOSAC leaderboard. The paper asks whether closed-loop fine-tuning can be done using only existing trajectory data, without RL or adversarial training.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes H steps of per-agent state history (position, heading, velocity), HD map polylines, and traffic light states, and outputs discrete action tokens drawn from a vocabulary of 2048 agent-trajectory tokens (each encoding a 0.5 s, 10 Hz trajectory) and 1024 map-polyline tokens (10 segments × 0.5 m). The NTP model re-plans at 2 Hz, yielding 10 Hz output for the 8 s WOSAC rollout. For the ego-planning variant, the output is a 16-mode GMM over 3-D (Δx, Δy, Δyaw) changes, with fixed per-mode standard deviations.

## Method

CAT-K rollouts unroll the policy during fine-tuning by, at each timestep and for each agent, retrieving the K most probable action tokens from the policy's categorical distribution and selecting the one whose forward-dynamics next state minimizes the bounding-box-corner distance to the GT next state. The resulting rollout states serve as policy inputs, while DAD-style recovery targets (the token that brings each agent closest to GT from the rollout state) provide supervised labels via cross-entropy. Training is two-stage: BC pre-training to convergence, followed by closed-loop supervised fine-tuning with CAT-K rollouts, which keeps visited states near GT (preserving supervision validity) while remaining on-policy (addressing covariate shift). The hyperparameter K interpolates between pure policy rollout (K=1) and noise-free BC (K=|V|), and the approach extends naturally to continuous-action GMM policies by selecting the K most-likely mixture modes and using their means.

## Results

On the WOSAC 2024 leaderboard (test split), SMART-tiny (7M params) fine-tuned with CAT-K achieves an RMM of 0.7702, outperforming the 14× larger SMART-large (102M) by +0.0088 RMM and the unmodified SMART-tiny by +0.0111 RMM. The method also improves minADE from 1.4062 (SMART-tiny) to 1.3068, and leads on all sub-metrics: kinematic (0.4931), interactive (0.8119), and map-based (0.8749). For the ego-motion planning task, CAT-3 fine-tuning of a GMM policy reduces collision rate by 25.7% (0.0568→0.0422) and off-road rate by 33.9% (0.0053→0.0035) relative to BC pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAT-K fine-tuning of the 7M SMART-tiny model outperforms all five baselines on the WOSAC leaderboard. Against the same-family SMART-large (102M), it gains +0.0088 RMM, demonstrating that closed-loop fine-tuning is more effective than a 14× model scale-up. Compared to the unmodified SMART-tiny (7M), the improvement is +0.0111 RMM, and against the much larger GUMP (523M) the margin is +0.0271 RMM. On the local validation ablation (Table 2), CAT-K is the only fine-tuning strategy that significantly beats the BC pre-training baseline (0.7616 vs. 0.7581 RMM); Trajeglish's noisy tokenization and SMART's trajectory perturbation either match or degrade performance, while naive top-K rollouts and deterministic rollouts drop RMM to 0.64–0.71. The paper also notes that even after an extensive hyperparameter grid search pushing the BC baseline to 0.7671 RMM, CAT-K still leads by +0.0031, confirming the gain is not attributable to tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NTP traffic simulation paradigm (SMART, Trajeglish, GUMP) and the DAD recovery-action framework, positioning CAT-K as a closed-loop variant of the 'winner-takes-all' training strategy used in open-loop motion prediction. It departs from prior data augmentation approaches (noisy tokenization in Trajeglish, trajectory perturbation in SMART) by incorporating the learned policy's own top-K predictions into the rollout process, rather than blindly perturbing GT tokens. It also contrasts with RL-based and GAIL-based closed-loop training, which the authors show are not competitive on WOSAC realism metrics, by using only existing trajectory data with a simple greedy selection rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAT-K fine-tuning is the first closed-loop supervised fine-tuning method to reach the top of the WOSAC leaderboard, and the finding that a 7M-parameter model outperforms a 14x larger model from the same family suggests that training methodology can outweigh model scale for traffic simulation realism. The technique's generality (validated on both NTP and GMM policies) and simplicity (no RL, no GAN, no expert queries) make it a practical drop-in for existing NTP pipelines. Open questions include whether it extends to stochastic dynamics, diffusion-based policies, and end-to-end driving, and whether the two-stage BC-then-CAT-K procedure is necessary or whether a single-phase approach could suffice.
