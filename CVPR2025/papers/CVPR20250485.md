## Problem

Existing generative image compression methods at extreme-low bitrates (≤0.05 bpp) treat the entire image uniformly, failing to balance global perceptual quality with local texture consistency in Regions of Interest. The paper identifies that distortion can be decoupled from perception by refining the granularity of the distortion measure to the region level, allowing adaptive allocation of limited coding resources to key areas while generating realistic content elsewhere.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250485/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a user-specified RoI map (edge map from RCF, bounding box, or random mask) as input, and produces an arithmetic-coded bitstream plus a reconstructed 512×512 image. The RoI map is applied in a training-free manner to guide latent masking, and the bitrate is controlled by the spatial size of the local latent grid (32×32 for ~0.04 bpp, 16×16 for ~0.01 bpp) combined with a masking ratio α (20–40%) that determines how many non-RoI tokens are replaced by a special [mask] token.

## Method

MRIDC combines a dual-encoder VQ front-end with a diffusion-based decoder. On the encoding side, a pre-trained VQGAN encoder (f_l) captures local spatial details while a lightweight VQ encoder (f_h) condenses global semantics into an 8×8×320 latent; the Map-guided Latent Masking (MLM) module then replaces non-RoI tokens in z_l with a [mask] token in a training-free manner, concentrating bit budget on key regions. On the decoding side, the Bidirectional Prediction Controllable Generation (BPCG) module first uses a Mask Prediction Transformer (MPT) to iteratively fill masked tokens via bidirectional attention with confidence-based retention, then injects the completed latent into the middle layers of a ControlNet-guided Stable Diffusion v2.1 denoising process that starts from the global semantic latent z_h. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a composite loss (VQ reconstruction + MPT cross-entropy + diffusion noise-prediction + LPIPS), keeping the VQGAN encoder and diffusion backbone frozen while training f_h, MPT, and the control module.

## Results

MRIDC operates at extreme-low bitrates of 0.01–0.05 bpp (using 32×32 and 16×16 latent grids with 20–40% RoI masking), achieving perceptual quality (FID/KID) comparable to PerCo while delivering superior distortion (PSNR, LPIPS, DISTS) even without RoI guidance. With RoI enabled (Edge map, 30% mask), MRIDC shows a marked PSNR improvement in the RoI region relative to PICS and PerCo at comparable KID, demonstrating better rate-distortion-perception balance. In terms of speed, MRIDC encodes in 0.078–0.083 s and decodes in 1.28–1.35 s per image, vastly outperforming PICS (221.10 s encode, 42.80 s decode) while remaining slightly slower than PerCo (0.04 s encode, 0.98 s decode).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional codec VTM (0.040 bpp) and VAE-based ELIC (0.054 bpp), MRIDC (0.034 bpp) achieves substantially better perceptual quality with far fewer bits, as both baselines suffer severe blocking and distortion artifacts at these rates. Among GAN-based methods (HiFiC, MS-ILLM, MRIC), MRIDC's diffusion-based decoder produces more realistic reconstructions at comparable or lower bitrates, though the paper does not report exact numerical deltas for these comparisons (results are shown in Figure 4). Against the two diffusion-based baselines, MRIDC matches PerCo's global perceptual quality (FID/KID) while achieving better distortion metrics, and with RoI masking it significantly outperforms both PerCo and PICS in RoI-region PSNR at equivalent KID. In encoding speed, MRIDC is ~2700× faster than PICS (which requires text-prompt search) and only ~2× slower than PerCo, making it practical for real-time applications. The ablation on MPT (Table 3) further confirms that removing the mask-prediction step degrades FID from 49.4 to 73.2 and KID from 4×10⁻⁶ to 3×10⁻³, validating that the BPCG module is essential for maintaining perceptual quality that diffusion alone cannot recover.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRIDC extends the diffusion-based generative image compression line (PerCo, PICS) by introducing a region-adaptive mechanism that decouples local distortion from global perceptual quality. It builds directly on VQGAN for local latent encoding and Stable Diffusion v2.1 with ControlNet for the generative decoder, but departs from prior generative codecs by treating the image as a non-uniform resource allocation problem rather than a whole-image optimization. The Map-guided Latent Masking module is explicitly training-free, distinguishing it from RoI compression methods [45–49] that require retraining or architectural changes, and the Bidirectional Prediction Controllable Generation (MPT + ControlNet) decoding pipeline is a novel combination not seen in prior diffusion-based compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in extreme-low bitrate compression: the inability of current generative codecs to preserve detail in user-specified important regions while maintaining global realism. The training-free RoI masking makes the approach immediately deployable with different priors (edge maps, bounding boxes) without retraining, which is valuable for applications like satellite imaging or medical imaging where regions of interest vary per image. Key limitations include the fixed latent grid size constraining the achievable bitrate range, evaluation limited to 512×512 resolution, and reliance on relatively simple RoI map types. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video compression, whether more complex or learned RoI priors would further improve the DP frontier, and how the method behaves at even lower bitrates where the masked latent becomes extremely sparse.
