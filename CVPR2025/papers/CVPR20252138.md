## Problem

Existing multimodal semi-supervised learning methods suffer from a Modality Information Gap: cross-modal consistency approaches capture only shared representations, while co-pseudo-labeling methods rely on incomplete unimodal classifiers, both overlooking task-relevant information that resides in modality-specific features. Furthermore, semi-supervised learning has not been explored for the image-tabular domain, where labeled data is particularly scarce (e.g., rare disease classification).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252138/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (resized to 128×128, encoded by a ResNet-50 CNN into patch-level sequences of dimension L^i × 512) and structured tabular features (17 columns for DVM, 75 columns for UKBB, encoded by a transformer into sequences of dimension L^t × 512). It produces a class prediction from a multimodal classifier as the primary output, with two auxiliary unimodal classifiers (image-only and tabular-only) used internally for pseudo-label generation. Training jointly uses a small labeled set (1% or 10%) and a large unlabeled set (7× the labeled size), with evaluation via accuracy (DVM, 283 classes) or AUC (UKBB cardiac tasks).

## Method

STiL combines three novel components within a teacher-student (EMA) framework. First, the Disentangled Contrastive Consistency (DCC) module enforces cross-modal InfoNCE contrastive loss on shared representations while applying CLUB-based mutual information minimization to disentangle modality-specific features, augmented by a transformer layer with self-attention (intra-modality) and cross-attention (inter-modality) to model synergistic interactions. Second, Consensus-Guided Pseudo-Labeling (CGPL) generates pseudo-labels via a rule-based strategy that averages predictions only from classifiers that agree with the multimodal classifier, and selectively updates only the disagreeing classifier to preserve diversity and reduce confirmation bias. Third, Prototype-Guided Label Smoothing (PGLS) computes class prototypes from labeled and confident unlabeled embeddings, then blends classifier predictions with prototype similarity scores to refine pseudo-labels. The overall loss combines labeled cross-entropy, DCC losses, a prototypical contrastive clustering objective, and thresholded unlabeled classification loss.

## Results

On the DVM car-model classification task (283 classes), STiL achieves 91.92% accuracy with 1% labeled data and 99.27% with 10%, outperforming the best SemiSL baseline (Self-KD at 90.45% and FreeMatchM at 98.77% respectively). On the UK Biobank cardiac MRI tasks, STiL reaches 83.54% AUC for CAD and 82.64% AUC for Infarction at 1% labeled data, surpassing the strongest baselines (Co-training at 81.00% and 79.35% AUC) by 2.54 and 3.29 percentage points. STiL also outperforms the best SSL pre-training method (TIP) by up to 7.01% AUC on 1% labeled Infarction, demonstrating its advantage in extreme low-data regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STiL outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset and label ratio. Against supervised methods (ResNet-50, DAFT, IF, TIP w/o SSL), STiL gains 56–89 percentage points on DVM 1% and 12–24 points on CAD 1%, confirming the benefit of semi-supervised learning over purely supervised training. Among SSL pre-training methods (SimCLR, BYOL, SCARF, SAINT, MMCL, TIP), STiL leads by 2.99% accuracy on DVM 1% and up to 7.01% AUC on Infarction 1%, showing that joint labeled+unlabeled task learning surpasses task-agnostic pre-training. In the most direct SemiSL comparison, STiL beats the strongest image-only method (FreeMatch at 71.91% DVM 1%) by 20 points, the best multimodal co-pseudo-labeling method (Co-training at 85.88%) by 6 points, and the best contrastive-consistency method (Self-KD at 90.45%) by 1.47 points on DVM 1%. The largest margins appear on the medical Infarction task at 1% labels, where STiL leads Co-training by 3.29% AUC and Self-KD by 7.97% AUC, highlighting its advantage in extreme low-data medical settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads: (1) multimodal SemiSL methods that rely on cross-modal consistency or co-pseudo-labeling (e.g., TCGM, Self-KD, MMatch), (2) disentangled contrastive representation learning from the SSL literature (e.g., Factorized Contrastive Learning, Decoupling Common/Unique Representations), and (3) prototype-based semi-supervised techniques (e.g., iCaRL, CoMatch). It departs from prior multimodal SemiSL work by explicitly identifying the 'Modality Information Gap'—the assumption that task-relevant information resides only in shared or unimodal features—and by applying SemiSL to the dissimilar-modality setting of image-tabular data, where prior methods were designed for similar views. The DCC module repurposes InfoNCE contrastive learning and CLUB mutual-information bounds in a supervised-consistency context, while CGPL adapts multi-agent consensus principles to classifier collaboration for pseudo-label generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STiL provides the first practical SemiSL framework for image-tabular classification, a setting of growing importance in healthcare where labeled data for rare conditions is scarce. The Modality Information Gap framing offers a useful diagnostic for why existing multimodal SemiSL methods underperform on dissimilar modality pairs, and the prototype-guided smoothing strategy (storing only class prototypes vs. instance embeddings) is a lightweight, efficient technique transferable to other SemiSL settings. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets (one natural, one medical), reliance on pre-trained encoders for best results, and the approach being specific to image-tabular rather than general multimodal pairs. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to text-video or other heterogeneous modality combinations, and how it scales to very large tabular feature spaces.
