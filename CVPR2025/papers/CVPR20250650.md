## Problem

Camera-based occupancy prediction suffers from three intertwined issues: occluded regions receive misleading supervision along camera rays, voxelized representations lack explicit motion modeling, and severe class/speed imbalances degrade performance on rare dynamic objects. Existing 2D rendering supervision methods (e.g., NeRF-based volume rendering) sample densely along rays, wasting capacity on empty space, and do not naturally support explicit per-object motion modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive multi-view RGB frames (6 surround-view cameras on nuScenes) and produces a 3D semantic occupancy grid (H×W×D) plus x-y scene flow vectors (H×W×D×2). During training, it additionally decodes ~100,000 3D Gaussians per scene from occupied voxel centers and renders them to 2D semantic and depth maps across multiple camera views (including adjacent-frame views); at inference, only the original voxel pipeline runs with zero additional overhead.

## Method

VoxelSplat is a plug-and-play training-time loss that decodes dynamic 3D Gaussians from occupied voxel embeddings via a 2-layer MLP (predicting opacity, rotation, and scaling), while directly using voxel centers, semantic logits, and predicted scene flow as Gaussian positions, semantics, and displacement vectors. A weighted sampling strategy (Eq. 3) balances sampling across P semantic classes × Q speed bins to counter class and velocity imbalances. Gaussians are decomposed into static and dynamic sets; dynamic Gaussians are displaced by the predicted flow to form future-frame Gaussians, which are then splatted alongside current-frame dynamic Gaussians. Differentiable Gaussian rasterization renders semantic and depth maps to multiple camera views (current and adjacent frames), supervised by 2D cross-entropy and L1 depth losses against online-generated 2D labels. This provides camera-visible-space supervision that reinforces 3D semantics learning and enables self-supervised flow learning from adjacent-frame ground truths, all without modifying the inference pipeline.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, VoxelSplat (built on FB-Occ) achieves a RayIoU of 36.9 and mIoU of 42.3, improving over the FB-Occ baseline by +3.4 and +3.1 respectively. For scene flow prediction, VoxelSplat reduces the mean absolute velocity error (mAVE) to 0.303, a 0.202 improvement over the FB-Occ-flow baseline (0.505). The framework also generalizes as a plug-and-play loss: when added to BEVDet-Occ and SparseOcc, it yields +3.6 and +2.9 RayIoU gains respectively, with mAVE values of 0.314 and 0.301.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoxelSplat outperforms all compared baselines on both occupancy and flow metrics. Against the strongest occupancy-only baseline SparseOcc (RayIoU 35.1, mIoU 30.6), VoxelSplat gains +1.8 RayIoU and a substantial +11.7 mIoU, while also providing flow predictions (mAVE 0.303) that SparseOcc does not offer. Compared to the direct backbone FB-Occ (RayIoU 33.5, mIoU 39.4), VoxelSplat improves RayIoU by +3.4 and mIoU by +3.1. Against the flow-augmented FB-Occ-flow baseline (RayIoU 33.5, mAVE 0.505, mIoU 39.2), VoxelSplat achieves a dramatic 0.202 reduction in mAVE (to 0.303) alongside +3.4 RayIoU and +3.1 mIoU. The plug-and-play nature is confirmed by consistent gains across three architectures: BEVDet-Occ (+3.6 RayIoU), FB-Occ (+3.4), and SparseOcc (+2.9). The per-category results (Table 2) show particularly large improvements on rare dynamic classes (e.g., bicycle +3.2, motorcycle +5.8, pedestrian +1.9 IoU), attributed to the weighted sampling strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoxelSplat repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from a scene-representation and rendering paradigm into a training-time regularization loss for voxel-based occupancy networks. It departs from prior rendering-supervision approaches like RenderOcc, which use implicit NeRF-style volume rendering, by instead decoding explicit dynamic Gaussians from occupied voxels and splatting them into camera views for 2D supervision. The method is architecturally agnostic, plugging into existing backbones (BEVDet-Occ, FB-Occ, SparseOcc) as an additional header and loss without altering the inference pipeline, and introduces a speed-aware weighted sampling strategy to counter class and velocity imbalances in the nuScenes annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt VoxelSplat on top of any existing occupancy architecture with zero inference overhead, making it a practical tool for the autonomous-driving perception community. The 20.2% relative mAVE reduction and ~3.4 RayIoU gain over FB-Occ are meaningful for safe driving, particularly for small dynamic objects (pedestrians, bicycles) where the weighted sampling strategy shows the largest per-category improvements. However, the method still requires 3D scene-flow ground truth during training, and the authors note it could be extended to occupancy forecasting and other 4D tasks, leaving open whether the 2D rendering signal can eventually replace explicit 3D flow supervision entirely.
