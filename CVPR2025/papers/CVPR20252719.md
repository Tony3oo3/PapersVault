## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian relighting methods suffer from the albedo-lighting ambiguity: with only single-environment captures, the learned environment maps absorb unmodeled residual terms (object color tints, tone shifts, intensity scaling) rather than representing physically meaningful lighting. This makes the learned maps incompatible with standard HDR maps, so relighting with novel environments produces unrealistic results. The paper addresses this gap by introducing cross-environment photometric supervision to force a physically sound decoupling of material and lighting.

## Input / Output

The method takes k sets of 200 multi-view RGB images of the same object captured under k different unknown lighting environments (dual-env by default, up to 5 tested), with camera poses known per environment. At relighting time, a standard HDR environment map (e.g., 256×256 equirectangular) is applied directly without normalization or adjustment. The output is a 3D Gaussian model augmented with per-Gaussian material attributes (basecolor, roughness, 3D specular tint) plus k learned 6×256×256 cube-map environment representations in linear HDR space, from which relit novel views are rendered at interactive rates.

## Method

ReCap jointly optimizes k independent learnable environment maps (6×256×256 cube maps pre-filtered into diffuse and specular mipmaps via NVDIFFRAST) that share a common set of Gaussian material attributes, framing cross-environment captures as multi-task targets that constrain the material-lighting factorization. The shading function is a modified split-sum approximation of the Disney Principled BRDF that eliminates the ambiguous metallic parameter by expanding specular into a 3D tint vector, with a saturation penalty and energy-conservation regularizer to keep values physical. Post-shading processing constrains environment maps to positive values and applies simple clipping followed by gamma correction, placing learned lighting in a linear HDR space so that standard HDR maps can be swapped in without normalization. Surface normals are estimated from the shortest axis of each Gaussian and regularized against depth-derived normals, which the cross-environment consistency naturally improves by preventing specular highlights from being baked into geometry.

## Results

On the dual-environment RelightObj benchmark, ReCap achieves 25.82 average PSNR on unseen relighting, outperforming the next-best method TensoIR with GT albedo scaling (24.49) by 1.33 dB and TensoIR without scaling (23.11) by 2.71 dB. ReCap also leads on SSIM (0.930 vs. 0.906 for GShader+ours) and LPIPS (0.068 vs. 0.090 for GShader+ours), while simultaneously achieving the best NVS PSNR of 30.76. In the single-environment setup, ReCap still leads among Gaussian methods with 23.27 average unseen-relight PSNR, surpassing GS-IR (22.57) and R3DG (22.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCap outperforms all five baselines on unseen relighting PSNR in the dual-env setup: it beats TensoIR with GT albedo scaling by 1.33 dB (25.82 vs. 24.49) and TensoIR without scaling by 2.71 dB (25.82 vs. 23.11), while also surpassing the Gaussian methods GS-IR (22.45), R3DG (22.21), 3DGS-DR (21.78), and GShader (21.17) by 3.4–4.7 dB. On perceptual quality (LPIPS), ReCap achieves 0.068, substantially better than TensoIR (0.137–0.140) and all Gaussian baselines (0.090–0.112), indicating more realistic relit appearances. TensoIR with GT albedo scaling remains competitive on PSNR but suffers from poor LPIPS and tone-shift artifacts without the GT scaling, and struggles with metallic objects. Among single-env Gaussian methods, ReCap leads by 0.7–2.8 dB in unseen relight PSNR, confirming that cross-environment supervision is the key differentiator. ReCap also achieves the best NVS PSNR (30.76) in the dual-env setting, showing that the multi-task formulation does not sacrifice reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting relighting methods (GShader, GS-IR, R3DG, 3DGS-DR) that rely on single-environment captures by introducing cross-environment photometric supervision as a multi-task learning signal to resolve the albedo-lighting ambiguity. Conceptually, it draws from photometric appearance modeling (NeRV, NeRD, TensoIR) but departs by requiring only unknown lighting conditions rather than controlled or known environments. The shading function is a modified split-sum approximation of the Disney Principled BRDF that removes the metallic parameter to eliminate a source of optimization ambiguity, and the post-processing pipeline (linear HDR space with clipping + gamma) is designed for direct compatibility with standard HDR maps without normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The albedo-lighting ambiguity is a fundamental bottleneck in inverse rendering, and this paper shows that even a modest number of cross-environment captures (as few as 2) substantially improves relighting fidelity without requiring controlled lighting hardware. The expanded RelightObj benchmark covering both diffuse and specular objects addresses a gap where prior evaluations focused primarily on diffuse surfaces. Open questions include whether the approach scales to indirect illumination and subsurface scattering (explicitly excluded), how sensitive results are to pose calibration accuracy in real captures, and whether the consistent improvement with more environments (no plateau up to 5) suggests a path toward fully general relighting from arbitrary multi-scene captures.
