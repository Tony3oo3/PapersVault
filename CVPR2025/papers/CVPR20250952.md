## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods designed for LLMs treat all tokens equally during calibration, overlooking the fact that vision and language tokens in VLMs have significantly different sensitivities to quantization. This equal treatment over-emphasizes the less-sensitive vision tokens, causing substantial accuracy degradation (up to 17% drop) when applying SOTA LLM quantization methods to VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250952/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The quantization calibration process consumes 128 image-caption pairs from the COCO Caption dataset (with GPT4V-generated captions) formatted into each VLM's conversation template. The method produces quantized model weights in W3A16, W4A16, W4A8, or W8A8 formats, where the VLM backbone uses weight-only quantization for the decode stage and weight-activation quantization for the prefill stage, while the ViT encoder uses W4A8 weight-activation quantization. The quantized model then takes an RGB image and text prompt and generates text output, evaluated across six vision-language benchmarks plus MMLU.

## Method

MBQ derives modality-balanced reconstruction losses by computing the average absolute gradient of the SFT loss w.r.t. vision and language token features at each linear layer, then weighting the per-modality quantization error by these gradient magnitudes. This is incorporated into the channel-wise equalization (CWE) framework: for weight-only quantization, the objective minimizes |g̅_v|·‖WX_v − Q(W*E)(E⁻¹*X_v)‖ + |g̅_l|·‖WX_l − Q(W*E)(E⁻¹*X_l)‖, and for weight-activation quantization both weight and activation are quantized within the same balanced objective. The derivation uses a first-order Taylor approximation of the SFT loss, naturally yielding an MAE-based (rather than MSE-based) reconstruction loss, which ablations confirm is superior. The approach is model-agnostic and composable with rotation-based quantization (MBQ-Rot), and a custom fused W3A16 GEMV CUDA kernel packs eight 3-bit weights into three bytes to achieve up to 5× kernel-level speedup on RTX 4090.

## Results

On small VLMs (7B/8B), MBQ achieves average accuracy of 65.3% (W3A16) and 63.1% (W4A8) on LLaVA-onevision-7B, closing the gap to FP16 (67.5%) to within 2.2% and 4.4% respectively. On large VLMs (72B/26B), MBQ reaches 73.6% (W3A16) and 70.8% (W4A8) on LLaVA-onevision-72B, losing only 0.7% and 3.5% to FP16 (74.3%). The headline improvements over SOTA baselines are up to 4.4% (W3A16) and 11.6% (W4A8) in average accuracy across six vision-language benchmarks (MMMU, SEED-Bench, OCRBench, VizWiz, ScienceQA, TextVQA). A custom fused W3A16 GEMV kernel achieves 1.2×–5.0× single-kernel speedup and 1.4× end-to-end decode speedup on RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AWQ (W3A16), MBQ wins on every model: +4.7% avg on LLaVA-7B (65.3 vs 60.6), +0.5% on InternVL2-8B (71.1 vs 70.6), +0.5% on Qwen2-VL-7B (71.6 vs 71.1), and a dramatic +18.5% on LLaVA-72B (73.6 vs 55.1), where AWQ suffers a 17% collapse relative to RTN. Against GPTQ (W3A16), MBQ leads by +1.2% on LLaVA-7B, +3.1% on InternVL2-8B, +2.9% on Qwen2-VL-7B, and +1.3% on LLaVA-72B. Against SmoothQuant (W4A8), MBQ achieves the largest gains: +12.0% on LLaVA-7B (63.1 vs 51.1), +1.5% on InternVL2-8B, +5.2% on Qwen2-VL-7B, and +7.2% on InternVL2-26B (72.7 vs 65.5). Against rotation-based methods QuaRot and SpinQuant (W4A8, large models), MBQ(Rot) outperforms both by up to 9.2% (e.g., 55.8 vs 53.0 MMMU on LLaVA-72B vs QuaRot; 60.0 vs 50.8 on Qwen2-VL-72B vs SpinQuant). Notably, SmoothQuant frequently underperforms even naive RTN quantization on VLMs, confirming that ignoring modality sensitivity differences is particularly harmful when activations are also quantized.

## Relation to Prior Work

MBQ extends the channel-wise equalization (CWE) framework established by AWQ (weight-only) and SmoothQuant (weight-activation) by incorporating modality-specific gradient weights into the reconstruction loss during calibration. While prior PTQ methods for LLMs treat all tokens uniformly when searching for optimal scaling factors, MBQ departs from this by using SFT-loss gradients w.r.t. vision and language tokens as sensitivity indicators, yielding an MAE-based balanced objective. The approach is composable with other PTQ techniques, as demonstrated by its integration with rotation-based quantization (FlatQuant) for large models, positioning it as a drop-in calibration modification rather than a new quantization architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that language tokens are roughly 10× more sensitive to perturbation than vision tokens provides a concrete, actionable principle for anyone quantizing multi-modal models, and the resulting accuracy gains (up to 4.4% W3A16, 11.6% W4A8) are substantial for deployment-critical settings. The method's simplicity—requiring only gradient computation during calibration and no architectural changes—makes it easy to adopt alongside existing PTQ pipelines. Open questions include whether the sensitivity ratio generalizes to other multi-modal settings (audio, video, 3D), whether it remains stable across training regimes, and whether the gradient-based calibration overhead becomes prohibitive for very large models or long calibration sequences.
