## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting assumes multi-view geometric consistency, which transient objects (pedestrians, vehicles) in casually captured images violate, causing artifacts and degraded novel view synthesis. Prior methods either rely on semantic features that conflate transients with statics or merely down-weight inconsistent observations without explicitly modeling the transient content, leaving the scene incompletely represented.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252850/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of casually captured RGB images with COLMAP-estimated camera poses, where images may contain 5% to 30%+ transient distractors. It outputs a hybrid scene representation consisting of a shared set of 3D Gaussians (statics) plus per-image sets of 2D Gaussians (transients), from which it renders novel views, per-pixel transient masks, and decomposed static/transient renderings. The 2D Gaussians are view-independent and stored separately per image, so the total representation grows linearly with the number of input views.

## Method

HybridGS decomposes each input image into a static component rendered by multi-view-consistent 3D Gaussians and a transient component rendered by single-view 2D Gaussians that treat transients as planar objects, combined via alpha-blending with a learned transient mask. A multi-view regulated supervision scheme trains the 3D Gaussians on K=4 randomly sampled views per iteration, restricting gradient updates to Gaussians within the intersection of their viewing frustums to enforce cross-view consistency. Training proceeds in three stages: a 3DGS-only warm-up, an iterative phase where 2DGS and 3DGS are alternately optimized (2DGS refines transients and masks; 3DGS refines statics using the mask to suppress transient regions), and a joint fine-tuning phase where both branches update simultaneously. The 2D Gaussian opacity naturally yields the transient mask without any segmentation network or pre-trained semantic features, and the entire pipeline runs in approximately 0.18 GPU-hours on a single RTX 4090 for a 100-image scene.

## Results

On the NeRF On-the-go dataset (6 scenes spanning low to high occlusion), HybridGS achieves an average PSNR improvement of 1.10 dB and 5.27% SSIM gain over the previous SOTA (SLS-mlp), with peak PSNR of 25.03 on Corner and 24.33 on Spot. On RobustNeRF, HybridGS reaches 35.32 PSNR on Yoda and 36.31 on Crab(1), outperforming SLS-mlp by 1.66 and 0.46 dB respectively. The method also produces transient masks without any segmentation network or pre-trained semantic features, and the full training pipeline (warm-up + iterative + joint) completes in ~0.18 GPU hours on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HybridGS outperforms all four baselines across every scene and metric on both NeRF On-the-go and RobustNeRF. Against the strongest baseline SLS-mlp (SpotLessSplats), which uses DINOv2 semantic features plus robust optimization, HybridGS gains ~1.1 dB average PSNR on NeRF On-the-go and up to 1.66 dB on RobustNeRF's Yoda scene, while also achieving substantially lower LPIPS (e.g., 0.077 vs. 0.166 on Corner). Compared to vanilla 3DGS, the improvement is most dramatic in high-occlusion settings (e.g., 24.33 vs. 20.77 PSNR on Spot; 21.77 vs. 17.29 on Patio-High), confirming that naive 3DGS overfits transients into Gaussian kernels. Against the NeRF-based methods (RobustNeRF and NeRF On-the-go), HybridGS consistently leads by 1.5–4 dB in PSNR, demonstrating that the hybrid 2D+3D Gaussian representation captures scene structure more faithfully than implicit radiance fields. The paper notes that SLS-mlp's semantic masks do not perfectly align with actual transients (e.g., motion blur, shadows), whereas HybridGS's 2D-Gaussian-derived masks capture these low-level artifacts at the pixel level without any external feature extractor.

## Relation to Prior Work

HybridGS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework by introducing a hybrid 2D+3D Gaussian representation, drawing the 2D Gaussian component from the image-representation literature (GaussianImage) and the 3D component from standard 3DGS. It departs from the dominant line of transient-handling work (RobustNeRF, NeRF On-the-go, SpotLessSplats) that relies on external semantic features (e.g., DINOv2) or robust loss functions to suppress distractors, instead exploiting the geometric principle that transients lack multi-view consistency and can be modeled as per-view planar objects. The multi-view regulated supervision scheme and multi-stage training strategy are novel training procedures layered on top of the gsplat/Taming-3DGS implementation base.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling transients as 2D Gaussians rather than merely suppressing them, HybridGS provides a principled decomposition of scenes into statics and transients without requiring any pre-trained semantic features, which simplifies the pipeline and avoids the semantic-transient ambiguity identified in prior work. The large LPIPS gaps (e.g., 0.077 vs. 0.166 on Corner) suggest perceptually cleaner static reconstructions that matter for downstream applications like AR/VR. However, the method does not yet handle illumination variation in unconstrained photo collections (as shown on Photo Tourism), and incorporating appearance embeddings remains an open direction. The approach also assumes relatively stable lighting across views, limiting its applicability to truly in-the-wild collections.
