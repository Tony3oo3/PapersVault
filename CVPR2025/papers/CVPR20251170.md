## Problem

Existing open-world semi-supervised learning (OWSSL) methods that incorporate textual information fail to effectively align images with text, as they rely on global visual features and pre-collected textual descriptions that contain task-irrelevant background noise. Additionally, teacher models used to match unlabeled samples with textual descriptions introduce noise due to their suboptimal performance, degrading alignment quality and limiting the model's ability to extract discriminative features across visually similar classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251170/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images encoded by a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP backbone (512-dim output) and a class name knowledge base containing only class names. For labeled samples, class names are retrieved directly from the knowledge base via ground-truth labels; for unlabeled samples, a teacher model assigns the most similar class name. The model outputs a class label by averaging global and local similarity scores across all classes in the knowledge base, enabling recognition of both known and unknown classes.

## Method

The proposed TP-OWSSL method introduces two key strategies. First, a global-and-local textual prompt learning strategy: learnable global prompts (4 context tokens, shared across classes) align with global visual features, while multiple local prompts (4 prompts with Top-K scales of 5, 10, 15, 20) align with class-relevant local visual features extracted from the second-to-last transformer block, filtering out background noise. Second, a forward-and-backward strategy for unlabeled samples: the forward strategy uses a teacher model to pseudo-label unlabeled samples by matching them to the most similar class name, while the backward strategy uses CLIPN's learnable 'no' prompt to validate assignment reliability, gating the unlabeled sample losses with an indicator function that zeros out unreliable pseudo-labels. The overall loss combines global and multiscale local prompt contrastive losses on both labeled and unlabeled samples, with only the projection layer and textual prompts trained while the CLIP encoders remain frozen.

## Results

On the four fine-grained OWSSL benchmarks, TP-OWSSL (CLIP backbone) achieves CACC of 80.6% on CUB, 90.7% on Stanford Cars, 87.3% on Flowers, and 93.9% on Oxford Pets. The largest gains over the prior state-of-the-art (TextGCD) appear on CUB (+4.0% CACC, +5.6% ACC on known, +5.6% CACC on unknown) and Stanford Cars (+3.8% CACC, +8.1% ACC on known). Notably, TP-OWSSL does not employ self-distillation loss, a technique used by nearly all competing OWSSL methods, yet still surpasses them on three of four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TP-OWSSL outperforms the strongest text-based baseline, TextGCD, on CUB by +4.0% CACC (80.6 vs 76.6), +6.0% ACC on known (86.6 vs 80.6), and +5.6% CACC on unknown (80.3 vs 74.7), and on Stanford Cars by +3.8% CACC (90.7 vs 86.9) and +8.1% ACC on known (95.5 vs 87.4). On Flowers the margin is smaller but consistent: +0.1% CACC, +0.2% ACC on known, and +1.5% CACC on unknown. On Oxford Pets, however, TextGCD retains the edge on overall CACC (95.5 vs 93.9) and unknown CACC (96.4 vs 93.5), though TP-OWSSL wins on known-class ACC (94.7 vs 93.9). Against all DINO-pretrained baselines (ORCA, GCD, SimGCD, GPC, DCCL, PromptCAL), TP-OWSSL leads by a wide margin on every reported metric, with the gap exceeding 15% CACC on CUB and 37% on Stanford Cars. The paper also notes that TP-OWSSL achieves these results without self-distillation loss, a component present in nearly all competing methods, suggesting the gains come from the global-and-local prompt learning and forward-and-backward strategies themselves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of open-world semi-supervised learning (OWSSL) and vision-language prompt learning. It builds directly on CoOp's learnable global prompts and GalLoP's multi-scale local prompt strategy, repurposing both for the OWSSL setting where prior text-augmented methods (CLIP-GCD, TextGCD) align global visual features with static class-name text without learnable refinement. The key conceptual departure is the forward-and-backward strategy, which borrows CLIPN's 'no' prompt mechanism to validate pseudo-label assignments from a teacher model—a use of CLIPN that goes beyond its original OOD-detection purpose. The method thus synthesizes prompt-learning techniques from the VLM adaptation literature with the pseudo-labeling paradigm of semi-supervised learning in a way that prior OWSSL work had not combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and acknowledged weakness in text-augmented OWSSL: that static class-name text introduces background noise that degrades image-text alignment, particularly for visually similar fine-grained classes. The practical takeaway is that learnable prompts (both global and local) plus a CLIPN-based reliability check on pseudo-labels can yield meaningful gains (~4–5 points over TextGCD) without requiring self-distillation losses that most OWSSL baselines depend on. Limitations include the assumption that the number of unknown classes is known, evaluation restricted to four fine-grained datasets, and reliance on a frozen CLIP backbone. The authors note that handling the continuous emergence of new unknown classes remains an open challenge for this line of work.
