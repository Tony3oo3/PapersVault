## Problem

Existing T2I safety benchmarks cover only narrow dimensions (e.g., toxicity alone or a single fairness axis), lack a unified taxonomy spanning multiple safety domains, and rely on unreliable evaluators such as pretrained CLIP or small-scale fine-tuned classifiers. This leaves critical risks—particularly the intersection of fairness, toxicity, and privacy—unexplored and forces practitioners to depend on costly human judgment or proprietary LLMs for safety assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds 2,669 curated text prompts (236 for fairness, 1,787 for toxicity, 646 for privacy) into 15 T2I models to generate images, which are then scored by ImageGuard. ImageGuard accepts a single RGB image plus a structured instruction (task description, category definitions, and a fixed output format) and produces either a safe/unsafe label with a specific toxicity or privacy category, or fairness attributes (one of 2 genders, 4 age groups, 5 races). At the benchmark level, results are aggregated into per-domain safety rates (higher is safer) and normalized KL divergence scores for fairness (lower is fairer).

## Method

T2ISafety defines a three-level taxonomy (3 domains → 12 tasks → 44 categories) grounded in regulatory documents and T2I service-provider policies, then constructs 70K prompts via LSH deduplication and LLM consensus voting, and 68K images (real + model-generated) annotated through a two-round majority-vote protocol. The evaluator, ImageGuard, is fine-tuned from InternLM-XComposer2 with two key architectural additions: (1) Cross-Modality Attention (CMA) modules inserted into 24 transformer layers, where vision tokens serve as keys/values and text tokens as queries, with a learnable zero-initialized gating factor controlling the merge ratio; and (2) an InfoNCE contrastive loss between globally pooled vision and text embeddings, combined with the standard autoregressive loss as L_f = 0.01·L_con + L_reg. For fairness measurement, the paper introduces normalized KL divergence (NKL-Div), which bounds the score to [0, 1] by dividing D_KL(P‖Q) by log n when the reference distribution is uniform, enabling cross-dimension comparison that L1/L2 distance and accuracy-based metrics cannot provide.

## Results

ImageGuard achieves F1 scores of 0.869 (fairness), 0.779 (toxicity), and 0.875 (privacy) on the T2ISafety test set, substantially outperforming all open-source MLLMs and proprietary models. On out-of-distribution datasets, ImageGuard scores 0.689 on UnsafeDiff, 0.704 on SMID, and 0.683 on UnsafeBench, surpassing GPT-4o (0.625/0.521/0.555) and Claude 3.5-Sonnet (0.489/0.644/0.534) across all three. The ablation shows that adding 24 CMA blocks plus the contrastive loss (L_f) yields the best overall F1 of 0.860, up from 0.551 for the unfinetuned InternLM-XComposer2 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImageGuard dominates on the T2ISafety in-domain metrics: it leads all open-source MLLMs (Idefics2, LlavaNext, InternVL2) by 7–88 points on toxicity and privacy F1, and surpasses proprietary models GPT-4o (0.470) and Claude 3.5-Sonnet (0.429) by over 30 points on toxicity. On OOD datasets, ImageGuard outperforms GPT-4o on all three (UnsafeDiff 0.689 vs 0.625, SMID 0.704 vs 0.521, UnsafeBench 0.683 vs 0.555) and beats Claude 3.5-Sonnet on UnsafeDiff and UnsafeBench. The main exceptions are Multiheaded (0.942 on its own UnsafeDiff data) and PerspectiveVision (0.810 on UnsafeBench), both of which are purpose-built safety classifiers that excel on their training distribution but degrade sharply OOD (Multiheaded drops to 0.175 on SMID; PerspectiveVision is not evaluated on the T2ISafety in-domain tasks). Among open-source MLLMs, Idefics2 is the closest competitor on SMID (0.700 vs 0.704) but trails ImageGuard by 58 points on toxicity and 66 points on privacy. Gemini 1.5-Pro performs worst overall, scoring below 0.14 on both toxicity and privacy in-domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of T2I safety evaluation benchmarks (HEIM, I2P, HRS-Bench, FAIntbench, DALL-EVAL), which each cover only one or two safety dimensions with a single category, by unifying fairness, toxicity, and privacy into a single hierarchical taxonomy of 12 tasks and 44 categories. For the evaluator, it departs from CLIP-based classifiers (Q16, MHSC) that struggle with context and subtle bias, instead building on InternLM-XComposer2 as a base MLLM and introducing a Cross-Modality Attention module plus contrastive loss to improve safety-specific discrimination. The fairness metric (normalized KL divergence) is a departure from the L1/L2 distance, accuracy, and variance metrics used in prior fairness benchmarks, motivated by the need for scale-invariant, interpretable comparison across dimensions with different numbers of categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

T2ISafety provides the first comprehensive, multi-domain safety benchmark for T2I models with 70K prompts and 68K annotated images, filling a critical gap where prior benchmarks covered at most two dimensions and lacked privacy entirely. The finding that even GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 fail at toxicity/privacy detection (F1 ≈ 0.35–0.55) while a purpose-built MLLM evaluator achieves F1 ≈ 0.78–0.88 suggests that general-purpose MLLMs are not yet reliable safety auditors, motivating continued investment in specialized evaluators. Open questions include whether the taxonomy captures emerging risk categories as models evolve, whether the evaluator generalizes to future architectures (e.g., autoregressive image models), and whether concept erasing can be made to close the remaining gaps in humiliation and violence detection without degrading image quality.
