## Problem

Generating dynamic anime-style animations from static illustrations is either labor-intensive (hand-drawn frame-by-frame) or produces physically implausible results (purely data-driven video diffusion models). Existing traditional animation tools assume simple inputs like lineart or separated layers, while data-driven methods lack geometric understanding and physical constraints, leading to distorted motions and artifacts. This paper addresses the gap by producing physically consistent, anime-stylized animations from complex, in-the-wild anime illustrations with intricate textures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static anime illustration as the primary input, along with optional user-specified energy strokes (carrying flow particles that propagate external forces like wind to nearby mesh vertices) and rigging points (anchoring regions to predefined trajectories). The output is a temporally consistent sequence of high-quality animated video frames. Internally, the pipeline produces a sequence of optical flow fields from the simulation, which are used to warp an extracted sketch into a texture-agnostic intermediate representation before final rendering.

## Method

PhysAnimator segments target objects via SAM, constructs 2D triangulated meshes using conforming Delaunay triangulation, and performs image-space deformable body simulation with the Fixed Corotated constitutive model to compute physically consistent optical flow fields. Users interact through customizable energy strokes (flow particles propagating forces) and rigging points, with the motion equation solved via semi-implicit Euler integration. Rather than warping the full image (which suffers from occlusion artifacts), the method extracts and warps a sparse sketch representation, then feeds it as ControlNet guidance into a sketch-guided video diffusion model (trained following LVCD on Stable Video Diffusion) for high-quality frame synthesis. An optional ToonCrafter-based cartoon interpolation module is applied between keyframes to introduce expressive, non-physical anime dynamics such as fluid contour deformations and dynamic sketch line generation.

## Results

On a 20-image anime test set (200 videos per method), PhysAnimator achieves the best VideoScore for visual quality (2.89), temporal consistency (2.86), and factual consistency (2.64), outperforming all four baselines. Its FID of 90.4 is competitive with DynamiCrafter (94.9) and far better than DragAnything (148.9) and Motion-I2V (121.8), though Cinemo's near-static outputs yield a lower FID (49.5). In a two-alternative forced-choice user study, PhysAnimator is preferred over every baseline across all four criteria (visual quality, temporal consistency, motion plausibility, overall), with the largest margins against DynamiCrafter (e.g., 84% vs 76% on motion plausibility).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysAnimator wins on the three most important quality metrics (visual quality, temporal consistency, factual consistency) against all four baselines. Against Cinemo, it leads on every VideoScore metric (e.g., VSTC 2.86 vs 2.80, VSFC 2.64 vs 2.58) and in the user study on all four criteria (86–83–82–81% preference), though Cinemo's near-static outputs give it a much lower FID (49.5 vs 90.4). Against DragAnything, PhysAnimator substantially outperforms on FID (90.4 vs 148.9) and temporal consistency (2.86 vs 2.45), while DragAnything's inflated dynamic-degree score (2.97 vs 2.48) is attributed to misinterpreting object motion as camera panning. Against DynamiCrafter, PhysAnimator wins on FID (90.4 vs 94.9), visual quality (2.89 vs 2.78), temporal consistency (2.86 vs 2.68), and factual consistency (2.64 vs 2.51), with the user study showing the largest gap (84% vs 76% on motion plausibility). Against Motion-I2V, PhysAnimator leads on FID (90.4 vs 121.8), visual quality (2.89 vs 2.70), temporal consistency (2.86 vs 2.50), and factual consistency (2.64 vs 2.39), and is preferred by 95–97% of users across all criteria. The only metric where PhysAnimator trails is dynamic degree (VSDD), which the authors attribute to baselines producing distorted or camera-shift motions that inflate the score without genuine object dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysAnimator sits at the intersection of two established threads: physics-based 2D animation (energy brushes, deformable body simulation, squash/stretch from Xing et al., Kazi et al., Willett et al.) and data-driven video diffusion models (Stable Video Diffusion, ControlNet, Motion-I2V, DragAnything). It departs from prior physics-based animation tools that assume simple inputs like lineart or pre-separated layers by handling complex in-the-wild anime illustrations via SAM segmentation and Delaunay mesh extraction. It also moves beyond PhysGen's rigid-body formulation by employing a Fixed Corotated deformable-body model, which is more appropriate for the fluid, elastic dynamics characteristic of anime (hair, cloth). The sketch-guided rendering pipeline—warping extracted lineart rather than full images to avoid occlusion artifacts, then colorizing via a ControlNet-conditioned video diffusion model—adapts the LVCD and ToonCrafter frameworks into a unified animation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical, user-controllable pipeline for animating complex anime illustrations that neither pure simulation tools (which require layered inputs) nor pure generative models (which lack physical grounding) can handle well. The sketch-warping strategy is a clean solution to the occlusion problem that plagues direct image warping, and the complementary dynamics module (ToonCrafter interpolation) acknowledges that anime aesthetics often violate strict physics. Limitations include the 2D-only formulation, the need for manual energy-stroke/rigging-point specification, and the somewhat ad-hoc two-stage rendering (physics simulation followed by generative interpolation). Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object interactions, 3D-consistent animation, or fully autonomous (non-interactive) motion generation.
