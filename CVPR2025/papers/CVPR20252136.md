## Problem

Reconstructing a 3D volumetric density field (e.g., a cloud) from a single image is severely ill-posed because multiple light scattering inside the volume obscures internal structure, and no publicly available 3D dataset existed on which to train a generative prior for such fields. Existing differentiable volume rendering methods require dozens to hundreds of views to constrain the density distribution, and standard NeRF-style approaches cannot handle the recursive radiance dependencies of participating media.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a cloud (or alternatively a 2D transmittance image) as observation, along with unknown physical scene parameters (background radiance, environment lighting, scattering albedo, phase function) that must be jointly recovered. It outputs a 3D volumetric density field on a 256×128×256 grid, the recovered physical parameters φ, and novel view renderings. The volume is internally represented by a compact monoplanar latent code of 128×128×32 features (~2 MB, a 50× compression over the raw ~100 MB volume), and the same pipeline supports super-resolution from a 32×16×32 coarse grid to full resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is Parametric Diffusion Posterior Sampling (PDPS), which couples a latent diffusion model (trained on 1,000 JangaFX-simulated cumulus clouds from the novel Cloudy dataset) with a physically-based differentiable volume path-tracer. A novel monoplanar representation compresses each volume into a single 2D feature grid (128×128×32) decoded by a windowed 1D sampling and MLP, achieving better reconstruction fidelity than triplanar or dense-grid alternatives at equal parameter count. During the reverse diffusion process, the gradient of the image-space loss—computed through differentiable volumetric path-tracing with Monte Carlo importance sampling for multiple scattering—is injected at each denoising step to steer the latent toward the observation, while physical parameters φ are updated via SGD on the same loss. An optional data-consistency refinement step (direct latent optimization without prior) is applied at intermediate stages to avoid local minima, and the DPS guidance strength ζ is gradually scaled from 0.1 to 1 to let global structure form before fine details are constrained.

## Results

On the Cloudy dataset (32 test cases), the proposed DPS method with a single view (DPS1) achieves a novel-view LPIPS of 0.1126, matching or exceeding baselines that use three views (DRT3: 0.1188, SPS3: 0.1081). The multi-pass variant DPS3x3 achieves the best novel-view LPIPS of 0.0572 and a target-view LPIPS of 0.0124, competitive with SPS3 (0.0118). Reconstruction time is dramatically reduced: DPS1 completes in 3 min 44 s versus 30–31 min for DRT1 and SPS1, an approximately 8× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two differentiable volume rendering baselines: DRT (Nimier-David et al., ACM TOG 2022) and SPS (Leonard & Westermann, Computers & Graphics 2024). On novel-view quality (N-LPIPS), DPS1 (0.1126) substantially outperforms both DRT1 (0.2937) and SPS1 (0.2869) with a single view, and even matches or beats their three-view variants (DRT3: 0.1188, SPS3: 0.1081). The multi-pass DPS3x3 setting achieves the best novel-view LPIPS (0.0572), roughly halving the error of SPS3 (0.1081) and DRT3 (0.1188). On target-view fidelity (T-LPIPS), SPS3 (0.0118) and DPS3x3 (0.0124) are nearly tied, while DPS1 (0.0205) is slightly worse than SPS1 (0.0123), indicating the diffusion prior trades a small amount of in-view accuracy for dramatically better generalization to unseen angles. Across all settings, DPS is 8–10× faster than both baselines (e.g., 3 min 44 s vs. 30 min 40 s for single-view reconstruction).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate threads: diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) for inverse problems [9] and differentiable volumetric path-tracing for multi-scattering media [27]. While DPS has been applied to image and signal reconstruction, and differentiable rendering has been used for multi-view volume recovery, no prior work integrates an unconditional diffusion prior trained on volumetric density fields with a physically-based differentiable volume renderer in a single-view setting. The monoplanar latent representation departs from the more common triplanar or tensor-decomposition encodings used in 3D generative models [14,54], and the Cloudy dataset fills a gap where no public 3D volumetric density benchmark existed for training such priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a shape-centric diffusion prior can substitute for the dozens of views traditionally required to constrain volumetric reconstruction, achieving competitive novel-view quality from a single image at roughly 8× lower cost than multi-view differentiable ratio-tracking. However, the approach is limited to a single phenomenon (cumulus clouds), relies on synthetic data, and the joint optimization of physical parameters φ and latent θ introduces an acknowledged ambiguity that can cause local minima. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other participating media (smoke, fire), whether direct 2D-to-3D volume diffusion models could eliminate the expensive latent-encoding pipeline, and whether the method can operate in near-real-time for time-varying scenes.
