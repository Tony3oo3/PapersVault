## Problem

Existing unlearnable example (UE) methods assume unauthorized trainers have large-scale data access or train models from scratch, assumptions that break down in practical few-shot scenarios leveraging pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP. This paper investigates whether UEs remain effective against prompt learning (PL) algorithms that fine-tune only prompt embeddings on minimal data, and finds that standard UEs are significantly less effective in this regime, motivating both adaptive UE generation and a new countermeasure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (potentially perturbed with UEs) paired with class labels in a few-shot setting (default 16-shot per class), and outputs a predicted class label via cosine similarity between image features and augmented text prompt embeddings. Unlike standard PL that only learns prompt vectors, A³ additionally augments both the image and text modalities at each training step, producing K_im × K_tx cross-modal pairs per sample whose least-similar pair drives the gradient signal.

## Method

A³ performs cross-modal adversarial feature alignment: for each image-class pair, it samples K_im diverse image augmentations (fractal transforms, TrivialAugment, random crops/rotations) and K_tx text augmentations (token masking, reordering, embedding rotations), then optimizes CoCoOp-style prompt embeddings and a meta-net by maximizing the cross-entropy loss of the least-similar image-text augmented pair (a min-max objective). The meta-net generates additional prompt embedding diversity by predicting prompts from augmented image features, expanding the effective augmentation space beyond what either modality alone provides. This adversarial selection of the worst-case cross-modal pair forces the model to rely on underlying semantic features rather than the spurious shortcuts embedded in UEs. The paper also introduces adaptive variants of EM, REM, and HYPO that use the PL process itself as the surrogate model, producing stronger UEs specifically targeting prompt learners.

## Results

Under ℓ∞-bounded EM perturbations (ε=8/255), A³ achieves an average harmonic mean accuracy (α_h) of 82.27% across 7 datasets, compared to CoCoOp's 65.31% — a 16.96 percentage-point gain. Against the strongest UE methods (REM and HYPO), A³ recovers α_h to 81.35% and 80.66% respectively, versus CoCoOp's 51.54% and 49.21%, yielding gains of ~30 percentage points. Even under the most resistant ℓ0-bounded OPS perturbations, A³ improves α_h from 72.37% to 79.10%. A³ consistently surpasses zero-shot CLIP (α_h avg 72.20%) across all UE settings, confirming that learning from UEs with A³ is preferable to not learning at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A³ decisively outperforms CoCoOp [42], the primary baseline, by 15–33 percentage points in α_h across all six UE methods (e.g., 82.27% vs. 65.31% under EM; 80.66% vs. 49.21% under HYPO). Against zero-shot CLIP [27] (α_h avg 72.20%), A³ achieves 79–82% under all UE settings, confirming that learning from UEs with A³ is strictly better than not learning at all. In the ablation (Table 3), A³ surpasses UEraser [25] by 2–6 points (e.g., 94.28% vs. 90.37% under EM 8/255), adversarial training [19] by 11–21 points, and simple Grayscale/JPEG [16] by 15–30 points. Among prompt learning variants in Table 1, CoOp [43] and ProDA [17] show moderate UE resistance (α_h ~71–72% under adaptive EM at 16-shot), while KgCoOp [39] is the most fragile (69.34%), confirming that A³'s cross-modal alignment provides substantially stronger protection than any existing PL variant. A³ also generalizes to novel classes: under REM, α_n improves from 48.05% (CoCoOp) to 78.26% (A³), a 30-point gain on unseen classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of unlearnable example (UE) research (EM, REM, HYPO, LSP, AR, OPS) and prompt learning (PL) with pretrained VLMs (CoOp, CoCoOp, ProDA, KgCoOp). It departs from prior UE work, which assumes training from scratch with full datasets, by adapting surrogate-based UEs to target the PL process specifically, and from prior UE countermeasures (ISS, UEraser, adversarial training) by introducing text-side augmentations alongside image augmentations. The A³ method builds directly on CoCoOp's meta-net architecture and UEraser's stochastic augmentation pipeline, extending both to a cross-modal adversarial alignment objective that has no direct precedent in either the UE or PL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As pretrained VLMs become the default for few-shot and zero-shot classification, the finding that UEs designed for from-scratch training are substantially less effective against PL has direct implications for content creators relying on data protection. The paper opens the question of whether UEs can be made robust to PL in the first place, and whether the arms race between data protection and pretrained-model generalization will continue. Limitations include evaluation only on CLIP-based PL methods and a single VLM family; whether findings transfer to other VLMs (e.g., ALIGN, Flamingo) or to generative tasks remains open. The partial-poisoning analysis also suggests that in practice, where only a fraction of data is poisoned, the protection gap may be even smaller than reported.
