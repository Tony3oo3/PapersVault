## Problem

Pre-trained video diffusion models are architecturally constrained to fixed output resolutions (e.g., 512×512), and existing generative VSR methods inherit this limitation by operating on full-frame videos with expensive full-range attention. No prior work has explored patch-wise VSR with video diffusion priors, which is non-trivial because these models are trained on complete frames and suffer significant degradation when generating patch-level details with incomplete semantics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video, a text prompt, and a target upscale factor. The LR video is bicubically upsampled and partitioned into non-overlapping patches whose spatial dimensions match the pre-trained base model's generation size (512×512); a resized full-video frame and a binary location mask are additionally fed as global context. The output is a high-resolution video at arbitrary resolution (demonstrated up to 4K) assembled from individually enhanced patches, with the patch size decoupled from the final output resolution.

## Method

PatchVSR adapts a pre-trained Rectified Flow DiT video diffusion model for patch-level VSR via a dual-stream adapter: a patch condition branch (several Transformer blocks) extracts features from each input patch and injects them additively into the base model's blocks, while a global context branch (a token-reducing Transformer encoder) encodes the resized full video concatenated with a binary patch-location mask and fuses it into each block via cross-attention that shares queries with the text-prompt cross-attention. The base model is further fine-tuned with LoRA to bridge the distribution gap between full-frame training data and local patch inputs. For cross-patch consistency, a training-free multi-patch joint modulation scheme (adapted from multi-diffusion) creates auxiliary bridging patches with 50% overlap and applies spatially weighted averaging during denoising to eliminate seam artifacts without additional training.

## Results

On the three evaluation benchmarks, PatchVSR achieves the best perceptual and quality metrics across all datasets: LPIPS of 0.326 (REDS30) and 0.183 (SynVideo30), MUSIQ of 44.956, 50.695, and 50.559, and Aesthetics of 0.493, 0.520, and 0.602 on REDS30, SynVideo30, and VideoGen30 respectively. However, it trails on pixel-wise fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM) where RealBasicVSR and Upscale-A-Video lead, which the authors attribute to their method generating richer, more vivid details that deviate from the ground-truth pixels. In terms of efficiency, PatchVSR generates a 2K video in 680 s using 40 GB memory, compared to 1562–2743 s and 47–68 GB for competing diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatchVSR consistently outperforms all four baselines on perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, MUSIQ, Aesthetics) across all three datasets. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor Upscale-A-Video, PatchVSR wins on LPIPS (0.326 vs 0.328 on REDS30; 0.183 vs 0.205 on SynVideo30), MUSIQ (44.956 vs 42.575; 50.695 vs 49.839), and Aesthetics (0.493 vs 0.471; 0.520 vs 0.494), but loses on PSNR (30.111 vs 31.257) and SSIM (0.647 vs 0.661) on REDS30. Against the CNN-based RealBasicVSR, PatchVSR dominates on LPIPS, DOVER, MUSIQ, and Aesthetics on both synthetic datasets, while RealBasicVSR retains a clear PSNR/SSIM advantage (e.g., 33.507 vs 30.857 PSNR on SynVideo30). VEnhancer is the weakest diffusion baseline, trailing PatchVSR on every metric except DOVER on REDS30 (0.344 vs 0.323). LaVie-SR is outperformed across all metrics on all datasets. The authors note that the PSNR/SSIM gap is expected because their generative approach produces richer, more vivid details that deviate from the pixel-level ground truth, and argue that LPIPS is a more informative fidelity measure for generative VSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in diffusion-based VSR (VEnhancer, Upscale-A-Video, LaVie-SR) where the pre-trained video diffusion model is fine-tuned to operate on full frames at an expanded resolution. Instead, PatchVSR maintains the base model's fixed 512×512 resolution and processes the video as independent patches, motivated by the observation that VSR is more locally focused than open-domain video generation. The dual-branch adapter (patch condition + global context with location mask) is a novel conditioning mechanism specifically designed to bridge the distribution gap between full-frame training and patch-level inference, while the multi-patch joint modulation adapts the multi-diffusion framework [1] from image generation to video VSR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 512×512 base model can now produce 4K super-resolved video with roughly 2–4× lower inference time and memory than full-frame diffusion VSR methods, making generative VSR more accessible. However, two limitations remain open: performance degrades on real-world degraded inputs (the training pipeline uses only synthetic bicubic degradation), and the iterative 50-step PNDM sampling still makes inference slow for practical deployment. The paper also leaves open whether the number of denoising steps can be substantially reduced by exploiting the strong low-resolution input as a prior, which would be critical for real-time applications.
