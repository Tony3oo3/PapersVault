## Problem

VLMs remain vulnerable to harmful outputs through the visual modality even when their language backbones have undergone safety alignment, yet no large-scale, high-quality open-source preference dataset exists for safety alignment of VLMs. Existing work predominantly focuses on evaluation benchmarks or jailbreak detection rather than providing the training data needed to actually align VLMs with safety and helpfulness criteria.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251862/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is a quadruple (image, text question, chosen response, rejected response) totaling 100,788 training pairs. Images are sourced from LAION-5B and matched to harm categories via CLIP-based search; questions come in three types—easy questions (direct image-related), hard questions (more challenging queries), and hard statements (declarative prompts)—generated by Gemini 1.0 Pro Vision. Responses are collected from 12 different VLMs (both open-source like QwenVL and closed-source like Gemini), and preference pairs are annotated by GPT-4V using both harmlessness and helpfulness criteria, with order-swapped queries to mitigate position bias.

## Method

SPA-VL is constructed through a fully automated three-stage pipeline: (1) image collection from LAION-5B using CLIP-based search across 6 domains, 13 categories, and 53 subcategories; (2) question generation where Gemini 1.0 Pro Vision produces three question types per image, with a jailbreak strategy to elicit more challenging queries; (3) preference annotation where responses from 12 VLMs are first scored by MD-Judge, then paired by GPT-4V based on harmlessness and helpfulness. For alignment training, DPO and PPO are applied to LLaVA-1.5 (7B) with a frozen visual encoder, updating only the projection layer and LLM. Ablation studies reveal that mixing safe and unsafe responses (rather than using only one safety tier) and combining all three question types are both critical for robust safety alignment, and that training the projection layer alongside the LLM improves image-based safety detection.

## Results

After training on SPA-VL with DPO, LLaVA-7B achieves near-zero attack success rates across all safety benchmarks: MM-SafetyBench Avg ASR drops from 20.54% to 0.6%, AdvBench vanilla ASR from 98.08% to 0%, AdvBench suffix ASR from 99.81% to 0%, and HarmEval USR from 44.15% to 0%. PPO training yields similar results (MM-SafetyBench Avg 0.45%, AdvBench vanilla 0.19%, suffix 2.12%, HarmEval USR 0%). These safety-aligned models also maintain helpfulness, with Anthropic-Helpful scores reaching 70.00 (DPO) and 70.00 (PPO) at full 90K scale, and HelpEval scores of 50.63 (DPO) and 71.04 (PPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA+SPA-VL-DPO achieves the best safety scores across all four benchmarks, surpassing every open-source baseline by large margins: it reduces MM-SafetyBench Avg ASR from 20.54% (LLaVA-7B base) to 0.6%, and AdvBench suffix ASR from 99.81% to 0%, outperforming even the strongest open-source baselines like QwenVL-Chat-7B (14.29% / 72.73%) and InternVL-Chat-7B (10.12% / 89.42%). Against closed-source models, LLaVA+SPA-VL-DPO matches or exceeds Gemini-1.5-pro (0% / 0.38% / 0.38% / 1.89%) on MM-SafetyBench and AdvBench, and ties GPT-4-0125-preview on MM-SafetyBench Avg (0.6% vs. 0.45%) while achieving a lower HarmEval USR (0% vs. 2.26%). LLaVA+SPA-VL-PPO is comparable to DPO, with slightly lower MM-SafetyBench Avg (0.45%) but marginally higher AdvBench suffix ASR (2.12% vs. 0%). The paper also demonstrates that the same SPA-VL dataset transfers effectively to other backbones: InternLMXComposer-7B improves from 16.22% to 1.04% MM-SafetyBench Avg, and QwenVL-Chat-7B from 14.29% to 1.64%, confirming the dataset's generalizability across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF paradigm well-established for LLM safety alignment (PPO, DPO) to the VLM setting, where prior safety work has predominantly relied on SFT (VLGuard, DRESS), in-context learning, or inference-time guardrails (MLLM-Protector, InferAligner). It departs from these approaches by providing a large-scale preference dataset specifically structured for RLHF training, rather than supervised safety examples. The dataset construction draws on the LAION-5B image corpus, Gemini for question generation and captioning, and GPT-4V for preference annotation, while the harm taxonomy references industry usage policies (OpenAI, LLaMA, Gemini, Claude) and prior safety benchmarks (SALAD-Bench, Weidinger et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPA-VL fills a critical gap: prior VLM safety research focused on evaluation benchmarks and attack methods, with no large-scale open-source training dataset for preference-based alignment. The fully automated pipeline (image collection → question generation → multi-model response collection → GPT-4V preference annotation) makes the dataset reproducible and extensible, and the ablations demonstrate that response diversity and question-type mixing are essential for robust safety. Open questions remain around the honesty dimension of the 3H framework, transferability of alignment across modalities, and whether these gains hold under more sophisticated or novel attack distributions beyond the tested benchmarks.
