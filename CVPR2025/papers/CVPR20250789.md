## Problem

Complete ViT architectures are too cumbersome for real-time UAV tracking, which demands both high precision and strict efficiency due to limited onboard computing resources. While prior work (e.g., AVTrack) dynamically activates or disables ViT layers, it requires attaching classifiers to most layers and estimating input complexity, which is redundant and time-consuming. Crucially, the layer redundancy within lightweight ViT-based trackers—where many deep layers learn repetitive target representations—had remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a 128×128 template image and a 256×256 search image, which are patch-embedded and concatenated into a single token sequence processed by a one-stream ViT backbone. The selection module consumes only the first-dimension features (a single token vector) at the saturated layer (l*=6) to decide which subsequent layer to retain, keeping its overhead minimal. The prediction head outputs a classification score map, a normalized bounding box size, and a sub-pixel offset, from which the final target bounding box is derived.

## Method

SGLATrack exploits the observation that search features in tiny ViTs saturate around layer 6, after which subsequent layers produce highly similar (redundant) representations. A lightweight 3-layer MLP selection module, fed by partial features at the saturated layer, dynamically selects one optimal subsequent layer to retain while disabling all others. The selection is optimized via a layer-wise similarity loss that encourages the retained layer's output to maximize cosine similarity with the saturated layer's output, thereby preserving focused target attention despite massive layer reduction. The overall loss combines focal loss for classification, L1 and GIoU losses for box regression, and the similarity loss (weighted by γ=0.2). The approach is plug-and-play, demonstrated with ViT-tiny, DeiT-tiny, and EVA-tiny backbones, achieving ~225 FPS on GPU with only 0.2–0.4% AUC drop over the full model.

## Results

SGLATrack-DeiT* achieves 66.9% AUC on UAV123 and 67.5% AUC on UAVTrack112, setting new state-of-the-art scores on four of five main benchmarks (DTB70, UAV123, UAVTrack112, UAVTrack112_L) while maintaining a competitive 64.7% average AUC across all five datasets. In terms of speed, SGLATrack-DeiT* runs at 224.7 FPS on GPU and 74.8 FPS on CPU, surpassing the next-fastest competitor AVTrack-DeiT (197.3 FPS GPU). On UAV123@10fps, SGLATrack-DeiT* achieves 65.5% AUC at 224.7 FPS, slightly trailing AVTrack-DeiT's 65.8% AUC but running ~14% faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGLATrack-DeiT* outperforms the closest competitor AVTrack-DeiT (ICML'24) on all five main benchmarks in AUC, with the largest margin on UAVTrack112 (+2.1%) and UAVTrack112_L (+1.3%), while also running ~14% faster (224.7 vs. 197.3 FPS). On UAV123@10fps, AVTrack-DeiT narrowly edges out SGLATrack-DeiT* in AUC (65.8% vs. 65.5%) and precision (83.2% vs. 82.6%), but SGLATrack still leads in speed. Against older ViT-based trackers like HiT-Small (ICCV'23) and SMAT (WACV'24), SGLATrack-DeiT* gains 3.1% and 2.3% AUC respectively on UAV123, while also being significantly faster. Compared to DCF-based trackers (AutoTrack, ARCF, STRCF, ECO-HC, fDSST), SGLATrack-DeiT* achieves roughly 19–22% higher AUC on UAV123 while matching or exceeding their CPU speeds. Among the three SGLATrack variants, SGLATrack-EVA achieves the highest GPU speed (236.9 FPS) with a slightly lower AUC (65.1% on UAV123), while SGLATrack-ViT offers the best UAVTrack112 AUC (67.3%) at 223.2 FPS, confirming the generalizability of the layer-adaptation approach across different ViT backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGLATrack builds directly on the one-stream ViT tracking framework of OSTrack and the dynamic layer-activation idea of AVTrack, but departs from AVTrack's design of attaching a classifier before every ViT layer. Instead, it introduces a single lightweight MLP selection module placed at a fixed saturation point, guided by a novel layer-wise cosine-similarity loss that encourages retaining the subsequent layer whose output most closely matches the saturated representation. The paper also contributes the first systematic empirical study of layer redundancy in tiny ViTs for tracking, showing via per-layer cosine similarity and early-exit AUC curves that deep layers in lightweight trackers learn highly repetitive features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For UAV tracking, where onboard compute is severely limited, the ability to cut ~30% of FLOPs and ~27% of parameters while losing less than 0.4% AUC is practically significant, enabling 225 FPS on GPU and 33 FPS on a Jetson TX2 without TensorRT. The similarity-guided selection principle is architecture-agnostic and could transfer to other ViT-based tracking or detection pipelines, though the paper only validates it on tiny backbones in a one-stream setting. Open questions include whether the fixed saturation depth (l*=6) generalizes to deeper ViTs or different tracking paradigms (e.g., two-stream Siamese), and whether the approach remains effective under domain shift or long-term tracking where feature saturation behavior may differ.
