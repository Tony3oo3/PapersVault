## Problem

Existing multimodal math benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse) restrict evaluation to single-image contexts, diverging from real-world K-12 math problems that routinely interleave multiple figures with text. The few multi-visual math datasets that do exist (MathVerse-mv with 788 samples, CMM-Math with 765) are small, lack fine-grained difficulty and subject categorization, and suffer from quality or diversity issues, leaving a gap in rigorously assessing MLLMs' cross-image mathematical reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252864/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem presents 2–8 images interleaved with text in a natural reading order (not simply appended), mimicking how multi-figure problems appear in authentic K-12 textbooks. Outputs span three formats: 1,109 multiple-choice items, 800 one-step free-form answers, and 100 multi-step free-form solutions where intermediate steps are individually scored via Step Accuracy Rate (SAR) and Question Completeness Rate (QCR). Every sample is annotated with image relevance (mutually dependent vs. independent), 11 fine-grained subjects, and 3 difficulty levels, enabling granular error decomposition.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage data construction pipeline: (1) extracting 49,538 candidate multi-image problems from 300K+ K-12 PDFs via the Mathpix API; (2) filtering through text-image alignment verification, semantic accuracy inspection, and manual removal of low-quality images, yielding 2,009 final samples cross-validated by at least two annotators; (3) labeling difficulty (weighted by question and analysis lengths at 0.4/0.6), subject, and image relevance via majority voting across GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, and Qwen-VL-Max with manual correction. Evaluation covers 25 MLLMs under three prompting conditions (original, CoT, CoT+2-shot) with answer extraction via the DeepSeek API, and includes controlled ablations on image input order (sequential vs. merged) and image relevance (MD vs. ID sets). A 100-error qualitative analysis of Claude-3.5-Sonnet categorizes failures into visual perception (42%), reasoning, knowledge, calculation, and rejection errors, while a staged-training comparison of LLaVA-OneVision variants (SI → SFT → Chat) isolates the contribution of multi-image training and DPO to multi-visual math performance.

## Results

On MV-MATH (2,009 multi-visual math problems), the best-performing model Claude-3.5-Sonnet achieves only 33.9% overall accuracy, far below the human baseline of 76.5% (measured on the 200-sample test-mini set). Among open-source models, LLaVA-OneVision-Chat-72B leads at 26.2%, while the specialized math model Math-LLaVA-13B performs poorly at just 3.0%. Performance drops sharply on image-dependent subjects: even Claude scores only 27.0% on Combinatorial Geometry versus 54.2% on Arithmetic, and CoT/few-shot prompting does not consistently improve results across models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Claude-3.5-Sonnet leads all 25 evaluated models at 33.9% overall, but trails the human baseline (76.5%) by over 42 points, highlighting a substantial gap in multi-visual math reasoning. GPT-4o (32.1%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (29.1%) follow closely among closed-source models, while LLaVA-OneVision-Chat-72B (26.2%) is the strongest open-source model, surpassing GPT-4V (24.5%) by 1.7 points. The specialized math model Math-LLaVA-13B (3.0%) dramatically underperforms general-purpose MLLMs, suggesting poor out-of-distribution generalization from its Math360K training data. Across all open-source models, CoT and 2-shot prompting consistently degrade performance (e.g., LLaVA-OV-Chat-72B drops from 27.3% to 26.2%), whereas for closed-source models the effect is mixed (Claude gains +3.4% with CoT, GPT-4o drops −0.9%). Sequential image input outperforms merged input across all models, and mutually dependent (MD) image sets are harder than independent (ID) sets, with Gemini-1.5-Pro showing the largest MD-vs-ID gap at 7.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of multimodal math reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, GeoEval) from single-visual to multi-visual contexts, where problems require reasoning across multiple interleaved images. It departs from the two existing multi-visual math datasets—MathVerse-mv (788 artificially rephrased multiple-choice items) and CMM-Math (765 Chinese-language samples with quality issues)—by sourcing authentic K-12 problems with diverse question types, fine-grained subject and difficulty annotations, and image-relevance labels. Conceptually, it also draws on the broader multi-image understanding literature (Mantis, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave, BLINK) to frame the evaluation of cross-image reasoning as a distinct capability beyond single-image perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 42.6-point gap between the best model (Claude-3.5-Sonnet at 33.9%) and human performance (76.5%) quantifies how far MLLMs are from handling real-world multi-visual math, and the finding that CoT prompting consistently degrades all open-source models challenges a widely adopted assumption in the field. The observation that a specialized math MLLM (Math-LLaVA) scores only 3.0% raises open questions about whether domain-specific training on single-visual data actually transfers to multi-visual settings. Future work should investigate whether architectural changes (e.g., explicit cross-image attention) or training on interleaved multi-image data can close the gap, and whether the sequential-over-merged input finding generalizes to video and longer multi-image sequences.
