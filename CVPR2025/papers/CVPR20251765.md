## Problem

Existing robust message-in-image steganography methods cannot simultaneously achieve high stego image quality, large embedding capacity, and resilience to extreme real-world distortions such as printing and photography. CNN-based normalizing flow architectures produce perceptible artifacts due to the lack of inner-channel feature fusion, while GAN-based approaches like StegaStamp degrade visual quality under stronger distortion levels. This paper tackles the open problem of embedding QR Code messages into natural images with imperceptible modifications that survive physical print-and-photograph cycles.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB host image and a secret message encoded as a QR Code (version 5, ECC level H, 368 bits by default; up to version 8 with 688 bits) as input, and produces a stego image that is perceptually indistinguishable from the host. During decoding, the system accepts a potentially distorted stego image (after printing, photography, JPEG compression, or Gaussian noise) and recovers the QR Code, which is then scanned by standard devices to retrieve the original message. The ViT patch size is 16×16, yielding 196 tokens per image.

## Method

RMSteg integrates three components: (1) an Invertible QR Code Transition (IQRT) that uses a two-block INN to adaptively transform the QR Code based on host image features before embedding, reducing artifacts while preserving scannability via a Gaussian-kernel constraint; (2) an Invertible Token Fusion (ITF) module that applies a learnable N×N matrix (initialized via Cholesky decomposition) to QR Code tokens, enabling patch-wise inner-channel feature interaction absent in CNN-based designs; and (3) AttnFlow, a normalizing flow backbone where each Affine Coupling Block replaces convolutional sub-networks with self-attention and cross-attention layers (the cross-attention queries the initial host tokens against evolving QR tokens to progressively inject message information). All three networks are trained jointly with a modified StegaStamp distortion simulation module (Gaussian noise σ up to 0.07, JPEG quality down to 25) and a composite loss combining L1, SSIM, LPIPS, QR reconstruction, and QR transition terms. The key architectural insight is that tokenized ViT representations naturally support the inner-channel feature fusion needed for high-quality robust steganography, which CNN-based coupling blocks cannot provide.

## Results

On the COCO test set, RMSteg achieves the best stego image quality across all metrics: PSNR 32.88 dB, SSIM 0.9109, and LPIPS 0.0707, substantially outperforming the next-best method (ISN† at PSNR 32.18, LPIPS 0.3266). Under the most demanding real-world distortion (printing and photography), RMSteg achieves a perfect TRA of 1.000 with an EMR of only 0.606%, compared to 0.960/1.125% for ISN† and 0.750/3.214% for StegaStamp. Under mixed digital distortions, RMSteg also leads with TRA 0.859 and EMR 0.861%, well ahead of ISN† (0.713/3.131%) and HiNet† (0.677/3.426%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RMSteg outperforms all three baselines on stego image quality, with the most dramatic gap in LPIPS (0.0707 vs. 0.30–0.34 for all competitors), indicating far fewer perceptible artifacts. Against ISN† and HiNet† (both CNN-based normalizing flow methods retrained with distortion simulation), RMSteg wins on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and decoding accuracy under mixed and printing distortions, though HiNet† marginally edges out RMSteg on TRA under mild Gaussian noise (σ=0.1: 0.827 vs. 0.794). Against StegaStamp and its retrained variant StegaStamp†, RMSteg dominates by a wide margin on every metric—achieving over 11 dB higher PSNR and roughly 2× higher TRA under printing—while also embedding more than 600 bits in a 224×224 image versus StegaStamp's 200 bits in a 400×400 image. The ablation further confirms that the attention-based AACB and tokenized representation are critical: replacing them with CNN-based ISN or HiNet backbones degrades LPIPS from 0.0707 to 0.32–0.34 and drops printing TRA from 1.000 to below 0.72.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the normalizing-flow-based steganography lineage (NICE → RealNVP → GLOW → ISN/HiNet) by replacing the CNN-based affine coupling blocks with attention-based ones (AACBs), making it the first work to integrate transformer attention into normalizing flow for data hiding. It borrows the distortion simulation pipeline from StegaStamp and the QR Code scanning constraint from ArtCoder, and uses ISN's two-block invertible architecture for the QR Code transition module. The key conceptual departure from prior robust steganography (StegaStamp, ChartStamp, VisCode) is the shift from GAN-based or rule-based quality guidance to a tokenized, attention-driven invertible framework that simultaneously achieves high capacity and perceptual fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that tokenized image representation with attention-based coupling is substantially more effective than CNN-based coupling for robust steganography (LPIPS 0.07 vs. 0.32+ in ablations), suggesting the technique may transfer to other invertible steganography tasks beyond QR Code embedding. However, the method is constrained to 224×224 images and QR Code as the message carrier, and the IQRT module can occasionally introduce information loss. Open questions include whether the attention-flow architecture scales to higher resolutions, whether it generalizes to arbitrary (non-QR) message formats, and whether the distortion simulation adequately covers all real-world degradation modes beyond printing/photography.
