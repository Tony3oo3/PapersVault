## Problem

Existing face VQA datasets for MLLMs suffer from severe view limitations—FABA-Instruct covers only emotion and action units, while FairFace addresses just three identity attributes—and none capture the hierarchical, multi-level granularity with which humans perceive facial details. This leaves a critical gap in comprehensively benchmarking MLLMs' face perception across the full spectrum of biological, social, environmental, and affective dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252372/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each VQA pair consists of a single face image (sourced from CelebA-HQ, FFHQ, FairFace, RAF-DB, and RAF-AU; 15,842 images total) and a text question drawn from 194 templates spanning four formats: true/false, single-choice, multiple-choice, and open-ended. The model produces a text answer, scored by accuracy (TFQ/SCQ), macro F1 (MCQ), or ROUGE-L (OEQ), with ChatGPT used to parse verbose model responses into the closest option. The test set contains 49,919 pairs and the training set 23,841 pairs, distributed across five views and three attribute levels.

## Method

The paper first designs a hierarchical facial attribute taxonomy with five views (Appearance, Accessories, Surrounding, Psychology, Identity) organized into three levels—coarse features (e.g., eyes, ears), sub-components (e.g., pupils, earlobes), and fine-grained dimensions (size, color, shape, type)—yielding 211 attributes and 701 values. Questions are generated from 194 templates, and 300 images from CelebA-HQ and FFHQ are manually annotated by 200 volunteers using a web-based system with dynamic question display, majority voting for TFQ/SCQ, a 40% threshold for MCQ, and ChatGPT-synthesized answers for OEQ. For the model baseline, LLaVA1.5-13B is instruction-tuned on the 23,841 training VQA pairs to produce Face-LLaVA. The benchmark is then used to evaluate 12 MLLMs (9 open-source, 2 commercial) plus a human evaluation cohort of 11 annotators, revealing that even GPT-4o trails human performance by ~8% and that fine-grained intrinsic attributes (lips, neck, background) remain particularly challenging.

## Results

On FaceBench, Face-LLaVA-13B achieves 61.16% overall accuracy, outperforming all nine open-source MLLMs (best prior: MiniCPM-V-8B at 60.67%) and approaching commercial models GPT-4o (63.21%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (62.72%). Human evaluators score 67.38% overall, indicating a ~6% gap even for the best commercial model. Face-LLaVA leads all models in Identity (71.64%) and is within 0.4% of GPT-4o in Appearance (60.29% vs. 60.69%), while GPT-4o dominates Psychology (74.94%) and Accessories (68.23%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Face-LLaVA-13B (61.16% overall) surpasses all nine open-source baselines, with the largest margins over LLaMA-3.2-11B (+15.29%) and Qwen-VL-Chat-7B (+12.89%), and a narrower but consistent lead over MiniCPM-V-8B (+0.49%). Against the unmodified LLaVA1.5-13B baseline, fine-tuning on FaceBench yields a +7.50% overall gain, with the most dramatic improvement in Identity (+3.54%) and Appearance (+9.07%). Compared to GPT-4o, Face-LLaVA trails by 2.05% overall, losing most in Psychology (61.85% vs. 74.94%) and Accessories (61.62% vs. 68.23%), but actually exceeds GPT-4o in Identity (71.64% vs. 63.53%). Against Gemini-1.5-Pro, Face-LLaVA is 1.56% behind overall, with Gemini leading in Psychology (68.98% vs. 61.85%) and Accessories (66.55% vs. 61.62%), while Face-LLaVA edges ahead in Identity (71.64% vs. 71.11%). All models remain 4–21% below human performance (67.38%), confirming that fine-grained face perception remains an open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the face VQA paradigm established by FABA-Instruct (emotion/AU only) and CelebA-Dialog (5 attributes) into a comprehensive multi-view, multi-level hierarchical framework covering 211 attributes across 701 values. Conceptually, it departs from prior face datasets (CelebA-HQ, FFHQ-Text, FairFace) that treat attributes as flat, single-level labels by introducing a three-level hierarchy (coarse features → sub-components → dimensional properties) and five distinct perceptual views. The model component builds directly on LLaVA's instruction-tuning architecture, fine-tuned with the newly constructed face-specific VQA data to produce Face-LLaVA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FaceBench is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate MLLMs on fine-grained, hierarchical face perception, revealing that even GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro lag human performance by roughly 8% on the evaluated views. The finding that a modest 23,841-pair fine-tuning set can bring an open-source model (Face-LLaVA) to parity or above commercial models on face-specific views suggests domain-structured data is highly effective for narrow perceptual tasks. Open questions include whether the hierarchical attribute structure generalizes to face generation or editing, whether the large performance gap on Surrounding (background) and fine-grained Appearance attributes reflects a fundamental limitation of current vision encoders, and whether scaling training data beyond 15K images would close the remaining gap to human-level perception.
