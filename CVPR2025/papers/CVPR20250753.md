## Problem

Existing co-salient object detection methods rely on a three-stage encode-consensus-predict architecture where the extracted consensus cannot feed back to guide feature encoding in a timely manner, and they employ full fine-tuning that is parameter-inefficient and risks overwriting the foundation model's pretrained knowledge. No prior work has applied a prompt-tuning paradigm that embeds group-level consensus directly into the prompts used to condition a frozen backbone for CoSOD.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a group of N related RGB images (resized to 288x288) and outputs a per-image co-salient binary prediction map at the same spatial resolution. The Segformer backbone is entirely frozen; only ~4.94 M parameters are tunable, and inference runs at ~65 FPS on a single NVIDIA 3090. A lightweight classifier head additionally predicts a group-level class label, and the CPG produces intermediate multi-scale saliency maps that are supervised during training but discarded at inference.

## Method

VCP freezes a Segformer hierarchical Transformer and introduces two tunable modules. The Consensus Prompt Generator (CPG) learns j saliency-seed prototypes via soft-assignment clustering over the frozen embedding features, produces a saliency estimation map, and then selects top-k pixel embeddings most correlated with the consensus to form consensus prompts P_Co. The Consensus Prompt Disperser (CPD) concatenates P_Co with a dimension-reduced embedding prompt and a handcrafted FFT-based prompt to yield Visual Consensus Prompts, which are projected through stage-specific unshared MLPs and a shared up-projection MLP to match each frozen Transformer stage's channel dimension. A concise FPN-like prediction head (ASPP + linear classifier, 1.49 M params) replaces Segformer's original 3.15 M decoder. Training minimises a weighted combination of BCE+IoU on the final map, multi-stage BCE+IoU on the CPG saliency maps, and cross-entropy on the class label, with the backbone held at zero gradient throughout.

## Results

On the most challenging CoCA dataset, VCP achieves Sm=0.819, Fmax=0.752, MAE=0.054, and Fm=0.708, outperforming the best full fine-tuning baseline (SCED) by +5.6% in Sm and +6.8% in Fm. On CoSOD3k and CoSal2015, VCP similarly leads with Sm=0.895/0.927 and Fm=0.860/0.915 respectively. Crucially, VCP achieves these results with only 4.94M tunable parameters and a 19 MB model size, compared to 156.7M/1750 MB for SCED and 392.85M/1498.7 MB for CADC, demonstrating a >30× parameter reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCP outperforms all 12 full fine-tuning CoSOD baselines across all three benchmark datasets and all six metrics. On the most challenging CoCA dataset, VCP (S+D) achieves Sm=0.819 and Fm=0.708, surpassing the strongest full fine-tuning competitor CONDA (Sm=0.763, Fm=0.640) by +5.6% and +6.8% respectively, and GCoNet+ (Fm=0.612) by +6.8% in Fm. Against the only other prompt-based method EVP, VCP leads by a wide margin (e.g., Sm 0.819 vs. 0.686, Fm 0.708 vs. 0.510 on CoCA), confirming that simple visual prompts without consensus modeling are insufficient for group-based CoSOD. In terms of efficiency, VCP uses only 4.94M tunable parameters and a 19 MB model, compared to 156.7M/1750 MB for SCED, 392.85M/1498.7 MB for CADC, and 52.3M/199.7 MB for GEM — a 30–80× reduction in both parameters and storage. Even when trained on the smaller C+D dataset (vs. S+D for most competitors), VCP still achieves competitive or superior results (e.g., CoCA Sm=0.774 vs. CONDA's 0.763), highlighting the data efficiency of the prompt tuning paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the prompt tuning paradigm (originating in NLP and extended to vision via VPT, AdaptFormer, and EVP) for Co-Salient Object Detection, a task previously addressed exclusively through full fine-tuning of large foundation models. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the consensus extraction and dispersion steps—central to the standard three-stage CoSOD architecture—can be embedded directly into visual prompts, thereby closing the interaction gap between encoding and consensus that plagues prior methods. Unlike EVP and VSCode, which apply simple tunable embeddings to single-image saliency, VCP introduces a Consensus Prompt Generator that mines intra-group co-salient representations via prototype learning, making the prompts explicitly group-aware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.94M tunable parameters (vs. 150–400M for competing full fine-tuning methods) while achieving 6.8% Fm improvement on CoCA demonstrates that task-specific consensus can be encoded in a compact prompt without sacrificing performance, offering a practical path for deploying CoSOD in resource-constrained settings. The conceptual insight—that the consensus mining step naturally maps to a prompt generation step—suggests a broader template for other group-based or consensus-driven vision tasks. Open questions include whether this prompt-embedding strategy generalizes to larger foundation models (e.g., ViT-L/16 or MAE-based backbones), whether it extends to other group-based tasks like co-segmentation or co-localization, and how it scales with group size beyond the N≤16 constraint.
