## Problem

Existing Video LLMs primarily perform holistic scene-level understanding and struggle to capture fine-grained spatial and temporal details of specific objects throughout a video. Prior video referring approaches either rely on impractical textual bounding-box prompts or coarse box-level representations that cannot handle multi-object relationships and complex reasoning. Additionally, the field lacks both high-quality object-level video instruction data and a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating spatial-temporal object comprehension.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a video (N frames) with user-specified object regions expressed as unified 2D binary masks (value 1 inside, 0 outside) at one or multiple timestamps, combined with a text prompt or question. It supports two input modes: single-frame (one randomly selected frame with its mask) and multi-frame (k uniformly sampled frames each with corresponding masks). Outputs include free-form object-level descriptions, multiple-choice answers, and fine-grained semantic reasoning about object actions, relationships, and future states.

## Method

VideoRefer builds on VideoLLaMA2.1 and introduces a versatile spatial-temporal object encoder (REnc) with two key components. The Spatial Token Extractor applies mask pooling over the shared visual encoder's feature map to produce a single object-level token per region, supporting arbitrary free-form mask inputs. The Temporal Token Merge Module aggregates multi-frame object tokens by computing pairwise cosine similarity between adjacent frames, selecting the top (k−u) most similar pairs, and merging them via average pooling to yield u compact representative tokens that preserve both spatial integrity and temporal coherence. These object-level tokens are interleaved with scene-level visual tokens and linguistic tokens as input to the LLM. Training follows a progressive four-stage scheme (image-text alignment, region-text alignment, high-quality knowledge learning, and visual instruction tuning) on the curated VideoRefer-700K dataset, which is generated by a five-agent pipeline (Analyzer, Annotator, Segmentor, Reviewer, Refiner) combining Qwen2, InternVL2, GroundingDINO, HQSAM, SAM 2, Osprey, and GPT-4o.

## Results

On VideoRefer-Bench^D, VideoRefer-7B achieves the best average score of 3.42 (single-frame) and 3.46 (multi-frame), surpassing GPT-4o (2.95/3.25) and all specialist models. On VideoRefer-Bench^Q, VideoRefer-7B attains 71.9 average accuracy, outperforming GPT-4o (71.3) and significantly exceeding image-level specialists Osprey (39.9) and Ferret (48.8). On the HC-STVG test set, VideoRefer improves over Artemis by +15.4% on CIDEr and +1.0% on BLEU@4. On general video understanding, VideoRefer gains +1.4% on Perception-Test, +2.3% on MVBench, and +1.0%/+1.2% on VideoMME over its VideoLLaMA2.1 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoRefer-7B outperforms GPT-4o on both VideoRefer-Bench sub-benchmarks: 3.42 vs. 2.95 average on Bench^D (single-frame) and 71.9 vs. 71.3 on Bench^Q, with the largest gap in Subject Correspondence (4.41 vs. 3.34) and Basic Questions (75.4 vs. 62.3). Against image-level specialists, VideoRefer dominates: Osprey-7B scores only 39.9 and Ferret-7B 48.8 on Bench^Q, reflecting their inability to handle temporal and multi-object reasoning. Among video-level specialists, Artemis-7B (2.26 avg on Bench^D multi-frame) and Elysium-7B (1.57 single-frame) are far surpassed, with VideoRefer gaining +1.18 and +1.89 respectively. On HC-STVG, VideoRefer improves over Artemis by +15.4% on CIDEr (68.6 vs. 53.2) and +2.9% on SPICE. On general video understanding benchmarks, VideoRefer consistently improves over its VideoLLaMA2.1 backbone (+1.4% Perception-Test, +2.3% MVBench), while Artemis degrades substantially (e.g., 34.1 vs. 57.3 on MVBench), confirming that the spatial-temporal object encoder does not sacrifice holistic understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VideoLLaMA2.1 as its base Video LLM, adding a spatial-temporal object encoder on top of the existing visual encoder and STC connector. It departs from image-based region understanding methods (Ferret, Osprey, GPT4RoI) by extending mask-level object encoding to the video domain, and from prior video referring approaches (Artemis, Elysium, Merlin) by replacing coarse box-level or text-coordinate representations with unified pixel-level mask inputs and multi-frame temporal aggregation. The multi-agent data engine for curating object-level video instruction data represents a novel pipeline that composes existing expert models (GroundingDINO, HQSAM, SAM 2, InternVL2, GPT-4o) into a quality-controlled annotation system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fine-grained, object-level video understanding is a prerequisite for interactive applications in navigation, surveillance, and robotics where users need to query specific objects across time, yet prior Video LLMs have focused almost exclusively on holistic scene comprehension. The VideoRefer-Bench and VideoRefer-700K dataset fill a critical gap in evaluation and training resources for this subfield. Open questions remain around scalability to very long videos, real-time mask-based interaction latency, handling heavy occlusion, and whether the GPT-4o-based evaluation pipeline in Bench^D introduces systematic biases. The reliance on SAM 2 for mask propagation also ties the system's practicality to the quality of upstream segmentation.
