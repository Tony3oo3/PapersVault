## Problem

Existing deep learning MRI reconstruction methods (CNNs, variational networks) operate on fixed discretizations, requiring separate trained models for each combination of undersampling pattern and image resolution. This is highly impractical in clinical settings where undersampling patterns and resolutions change frequently to accommodate different diagnostic needs. The paper asks whether a single unified model can generalize across all such settings without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes undersampled multi-coil k-space measurements (complex-valued, represented as 2 real channels) along with coil sensitivity maps, at arbitrary undersampling patterns (equispaced, random, magic, Gaussian, radial, Poisson) and rates (2×–16×). It produces a reconstructed MRI image at any spatial resolution without retraining. A key property is zero-shot super-resolution: increasing k-space discretization extends the field of view, while increasing image-space discretization enhances detail, both without any fine-tuning. Training uses 320×320 knee and 160×160 brain slices, but inference generalizes to 640×640 and 320×320 respectively.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing standard CNN convolutions with DISCO (Discrete-Continuous) convolutions in a U-Shaped DISCO Neural Operator (UDNO) architecture, making the entire network discretization-agnostic. DISCO kernels are parameterized as linear combinations of predefined basis functions defined in continuous function space, so the kernel maintains a fixed physical size regardless of grid resolution—unlike CNN kernels that shrink to points as resolution increases. The framework uses two NOs exploiting Fourier duality: NO_k operates in k-space to capture global image features, and NO_i operates in image space to capture local features, with NO_k applied once at the start and NO_i applied within 12 unrolled cascades, each paired with a data-consistency update. The UDNO follows a U-Net encoder/decoder structure with DISCO layers replacing all convolutions, closely mirroring the E2E-VN architecture but with resolution-agnostic operators. Training minimizes SSIM loss in a supervised setting, and the model is fine-tuned on a small multi-pattern dataset to achieve robustness across undersampling configurations.

## Results

On 4× equispaced undersampling, the proposed NO achieves 39.14 ± 2.93 dB PSNR and 0.9219 ± 0.0724 SSIM on fastMRI knee, and 38.82 ± 2.77 dB PSNR and 0.9621 ± 0.0086 SSIM on fastMRI brain, outperforming the strongest baseline E2E-VN by 0.81 dB PSNR and 1.7% SSIM (knee) and 0.76 dB PSNR (brain). The key advantage is cross-pattern robustness: on out-of-domain irregular patterns (radial, Poisson, Gaussian), NO maintains 31–36 dB PSNR while E2E-VN drops to 23–27 dB, a gap of up to 9 dB. Inference takes 0.158 s per image, approximately 600× faster than diffusion baselines (84–96 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical ℓ₁-Wavelet baseline [27], NO wins by approximately 13.5 dB PSNR on knee (39.14 vs 25.67), a decisive margin. All three diffusion baselines—CSGM [17], ScoreMRI [5], and PnP-DM [43]—are outperformed by 12–13 dB PSNR on knee, and additionally require ~6 hours of pattern-specific hyperparameter tuning per undersampling pattern versus none for NO. Against the U-Net [37] end-to-end baseline, NO gains ~2 dB PSNR and ~4% SSIM on both datasets. The closest competitor is E2E-VN [40], which NO surpasses by 0.81 dB PSNR and 1.7% SSIM on in-domain 4× equispaced knee data; however, the critical advantage emerges on out-of-domain irregular patterns (radial, Poisson, Gaussian), where NO maintains 31–36 dB PSNR while E2E-VN collapses to 23–27 dB—a gap of up to 9 dB—demonstrating that the resolution-agnostic DISCO operators generalize across undersampling geometries without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper replaces the standard CNN convolutions in the E2E-VN unrolled network architecture [40] with DISCO discrete-continuous convolution operators [26,31], a neural operator design that learns in function space and is agnostic to input discretization. The unrolled gradient-descent-with-data-prior framework from VarNet [15] and E2E-VN [40] is preserved, but the learned prior is now a U-shaped DISCO neural operator (UDNO) applied in both k-space and image space, exploiting Fourier duality to capture global and local features simultaneously. The key conceptual departure from prior MRI deep learning is that the model is no longer tied to a fixed resolution or undersampling pattern, positioning it as the first resolution-agnostic framework for compressed sensing MRI reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for clinical MRI, where undersampling patterns and image resolutions change frequently; a single model that generalizes across both eliminates the need to train and maintain separate networks per setting. The 600× inference speedup over diffusion-based methods makes the approach far more deployable in real-time imaging. However, the authors acknowledge two important limitations: only one neural operator design (DISCO) is explored, and reconstruction quality metrics (PSNR/SSIM) are not validated against diagnostic accuracy, which is the ultimate clinical endpoint. Open questions include whether other operator architectures (e.g., FNO, graph-based NOs) could further improve performance, and whether the resolution-agnostic property extends to 3D volumetric reconstruction.
