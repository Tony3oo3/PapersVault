## Problem

Real-time free-view human rendering from sparse (≤4) RGB cameras is hindered by methods that either jointly learn geometry and appearance from a single imperfect texture unprojection, degrading rendering quality and pose robustness, or rely solely on motion signals for geometry, ignoring readily available image cues and failing on out-of-distribution motions. No prior approach simultaneously achieves photorealistic 4K rendering, real-time speed, and robust generalization to unseen poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250682/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the method consumes 1–4 segmented RGB video frames plus per-frame skeletal motion parameters and produces a photorealistic 4K novel-view rendering in real time on a single GPU. Training additionally requires dense-view video sequences with ground-truth foreground segmentations and skeletal motions, and the geometry network is supervised against point clouds reconstructed by NeuS2. All intermediate computation (deformation prediction, Gaussian parameter regression) is performed in 2D texture space at 256×256 (or 512×512 for the large variant), keeping the pipeline to lightweight 2D CNNs.

## Method

DUT performs a double texture unprojection to decouple coarse geometry recovery from fine-grained appearance synthesis. In stage one, a first unprojection warps sparse-view pixels onto a posed-but-undeformed body template to produce a coarse texture map; a UNet (GeoNet) then predicts per-vertex displacements in canonical pose from this map plus a normal map, yielding a deformed template that better matches the true surface. In stage two, a second unprojection onto the deformed template produces a less-distorted texture map, which a second UNet (GauNet) converts into per-texel 3D Gaussian parameters (displacement, spherical harmonics, scale, rotation, opacity). A Gaussian scale refinement step multiplies predicted scales by the maximum edge-stretching ratio from LBS to prevent scale-related artifacts at extreme poses. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with Chamfer/Laplacian/isometry losses for geometry and L1/SSIM/IDMRF/regularization losses for appearance, achieving 3× faster inference than the prior state of the art while outperforming it on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across three benchmarks.

## Results

On the 4K/full-resolution evaluation, DUT achieves PSNR of 29.84 (S3), 30.64 (S22), and 29.38 (S2618), consistently outperforming all four baselines on LPIPS (0.1389, 0.1387, 0.1355) and SSIM across all subjects. The larger variant (Ours-Large, 512 texture resolution) further improves to PSNR 30.03, 30.81, and 29.69 with LPIPS as low as 0.1235. On 1K resolution, DUT reaches PSNR 32.89 (S3) and 34.11 (S22), surpassing the best baseline (GHG) by ~1.5 dB. The method also generalizes to out-of-distribution motions, achieving PSNR 32.64 vs. 30.22 for HoloChar on a standing long jump sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUT outperforms all four baselines on LPIPS (perceptual quality) across every subject and resolution, with margins of 0.016–0.032 over the next-best method. On PSNR, DUT leads on S3-4K (29.84 vs. 29.37 for DVA) and S2618-full (29.38 vs. 29.28 for DVA), but DVA narrowly edges DUT on S22-4K PSNR (31.20 vs. 30.64), though DUT still wins on SSIM and LPIPS for that setting. GHG is the closest competitor on 1K PSNR (31.41 vs. 32.89 on S3; 33.32 vs. 34.11 on S22), trailing DUT by ~1.4–1.5 dB. HoloChar, the prior real-time state of the art, is beaten by 0.5–2.9 dB in PSNR depending on subject and resolution, and is notably weaker on out-of-distribution motions (PSNR 30.22 vs. 32.64 for DUT on a standing long jump). ENeRF, a general-scene NeRF method, performs worst overall due to its reliance on dense camera baselines for feature matching, which breaks down under the 4-camera sparse-view setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUT extends the texel-space rendering pipeline established by HoloChar, DVA, and GHG, which all unproject sparse-view images onto a posed template and learn rendering primitives with 2D CNNs. The key departure is a two-stage decoupling: an image-conditioned UNet (GeoNet) first estimates template deformation from a coarse unprojected texture, and a second unprojection onto the deformed geometry feeds a second UNet (GauNet) that predicts 3D Gaussian parameters. This contrasts with HoloChar's motion-only deformation (no image conditioning) and DVA/GHG's joint geometry-appearance learning, and it repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting as the rendering primitive in place of volumetric primitives or neural textures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time 4K free-view human rendering from as few as one camera is directly relevant to immersive telepresence and remote communication, and DUT's 3× speedup over the prior SOTA (HoloChar) while improving PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS makes it a practical candidate for deployment. Open limitations include inability to preserve finger rigidity, sensitivity to topological changes (e.g., unzipping a jacket), and reliance on per-frame NeuS2 point-cloud supervision for the geometry stage, which is computationally expensive. Future directions noted by the authors include silhouette- or depth-prior-based supervision to remove the per-frame reconstruction bottleneck, and reducing training to sparse-camera-only configurations.
