## Problem

Existing multi-view 3D reconstruction methods like DUSt3R are fundamentally pairwise, requiring O(N²) pairwise pointmap predictions followed by costly global alignment optimization. This pairwise bottleneck limits the model's context during both training and inference, causes error accumulation, and quickly exhausts GPU memory (OOM at 48 views on an A100), making scalable reconstruction from hundreds or thousands of views impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251353/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Fast3R takes N unordered, unposed RGB images (512px on the longest side) with no assumption on image ordering or camera intrinsics, and simultaneously outputs N local pointmaps (3D XYZ per pixel in each camera's own frame), N global pointmaps (in the first camera's frame), and corresponding per-pixel confidence maps. The architecture is trained on N=20 views but generalizes to 1000+ views at inference via position interpolation on image index embeddings, with a pool size of N'=1000 controlling the effective masking ratio.

## Method

Fast3R replaces DUSt3R's pairwise architecture with a three-component Transformer: a CroCo ViT-L image encoder, a 24-layer ViT-L fusion transformer that performs all-to-all self-attention over concatenated patch tokens from all views simultaneously, and two DPT decoding heads producing local and global pointmaps. The key architectural insight is that all-to-all attention gives each frame full context from every other frame in a single forward pass, eliminating sequential dependencies and pairwise bottlenecks. A randomized position interpolation scheme (drawing N indices uniformly from a pool of N'=1000) enables the model to handle far more views at inference than seen during training. Training uses a confidence-weighted normalized 3D pointwise regression loss on both local and global pointmaps, with the local head learning view-invariant 2D-to-3D geometry while the global head additionally learns the rigid transformation to a shared frame. Memory efficiency is achieved through FlashAttention, DeepSpeed ZeRO stage 2 during training, and tensor parallelism across GPUs for the DPT heads at inference, enabling 1500 views in a single pass on one A100.

## Results

On CO3Dv2, Fast3R achieves 96.2% RRA@15 and 75.0% mAA(30) for camera pose estimation, while the no-outdoor variant reaches 99.7% RRA@15 — a 14× error reduction over DUSt3R with global alignment. Inference speed is 251.1 FPS, making it 320× faster than DUSt3R (0.78 FPS) and 1000× faster than MASt3R (0.23 FPS). For 3D reconstruction on 7-Scenes, Fast3R achieves 1.58 median accuracy and 0.93 completion (×100 scale), competitive with DUSt3R (1.23/0.91) while processing 1500+ views in a single forward pass versus DUSt3R's OOM at 48 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DUSt3R, Fast3R matches RRA@15 (96.2%) but trails on RTA@15 (81.6% vs 86.8%) and mAA(30) on CO3Dv2 (75.0% vs 76.7%), while being 320× faster (251.1 vs 0.78 FPS) and handling 1500+ views in one pass versus DUSt3R's OOM at 48 views. Against MASt3R, Fast3R wins on RRA@15 (96.2% vs 94.6%) and mAA on RealEstate10K (72.7% vs 76.4% — actually MASt3R wins here at 76.4%), but is 1000× faster (251.1 vs 0.23 FPS). Against PoseDiff, Fast3R dominates on every reported metric (RRA@15: 96.2 vs 80.5; mAA: 75.0 vs 66.5) with a 16,700× speed advantage. Against traditional SfM (COLMAP+SG, PixSfM), Fast3R improves RRA@15 by over 60 points. On 3D reconstruction (7-Scenes, NRGBD, DTU), Fast3R is slightly behind DUSt3R on accuracy (e.g., 1.58 vs 1.23 on 7-Scenes) but beats Spann3R on DTU (1.706 vs 2.268 accuracy) and is 4× faster than Spann3R (251.1 vs 65.4 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

Fast3R is a direct multi-view generalization of DUSt3R, replacing its pairwise pointmap regression and subsequent O(N²) global alignment with a single Transformer that performs all-to-all self-attention over all input views simultaneously. It departs from the broader MVG pipeline tradition (COLMAP, OrbSLAM2) and from DUSt3R's follow-ups (MASt3R, Spann3R) that retain either the pairwise bottleneck or sequential processing with external memory. The key architectural novelty is the fusion Transformer with randomized position interpolation (borrowed from LLM context extension) that enables 'train short, test long' scaling to 1000+ views, combined with a dual local/global DPT decoding head design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fast3R eliminates the fundamental pairwise bottleneck that has constrained both classical SfM/MVS pipelines and DUSt3R-style methods, offering a 320× speedup and the ability to process 1500 images in one forward pass where DUSt3R runs out of memory at 48. The demonstrated scaling behavior—performance improving with more views at both training and inference time—suggests the task can benefit from the same compute/data scaling laws as large language models. Open questions include the observed pointmap drifting at extreme view counts (>300), the reliance on real-world scan data with systematic label noise, and whether synthetic data or better long-context positional embeddings can address these limitations.
