## Problem

Sparse-input 3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from two critical yet overlooked issues in real-world scenarios: extrapolation (regions outside the field of view of the sparse inputs) and occlusion (regions hidden in training views but visible in novel views). These produce severe artifacts such as black holes in rendered images, and existing methods that rely on face-forwarding or outside-in viewing settings fail to address them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251018/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 6 sparse RGB images with known camera poses per scene, plus a DUSt3R point cloud for 3DGS initialization. It produces an optimized 3DGS model capable of rendering novel views, augmented by 25-frame video sequences generated by a camera-controlled image-to-video diffusion model (ViewCrafter) that serve as auxiliary supervision. The generated sequences are produced along interpolated camera trajectories that specifically target uncovered or occluded regions identified via transmittance maps from the baseline 3DGS.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free scene-grounding guidance that modifies the video diffusion model's denoising sampler: at each step, the noisy latent is decoded to a clean-space prediction, and a gradient computed from a masked L1 + perceptual loss against a rendered sequence from the optimized 3DGS is subtracted from the denoising update, steering generation toward consistency and preventing hallucinated elements. A trajectory initialization strategy samples candidate poses around each input view, renders them with the baseline 3DGS, selects those revealing significant holes (via thresholded transmittance maps), and interpolates camera paths between input and selected poses to form a trajectory pool. The 3DGS optimization scheme uses perceptual loss (rather than pure reconstruction loss) on generated views to drive hole-filling, combined with local sampling within intervals and a global list of previously generated views to prevent forgetting of other regions.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, the proposed method achieves 26.35 PSNR, 0.872 SSIM, and 0.122 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest 3DGS-based baseline (FSGS† at 22.99 PSNR) by over 3.3 dB. On the more challenging ScanNet++ dataset, it reaches 23.89 PSNR, 0.850 SSIM, and 0.182 LPIPS, surpassing the best baseline (3DGS† at 21.41 PSNR) by over 2.5 dB. The method also outperforms inpainting-based alternatives (LaMa and SDS+SDInpaint) by more than 1.0 dB PSNR on Replica, confirming that scene-grounding guided video generation provides superior hole-filling compared to 2D inpainting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all 3DGS-based baselines on both datasets. On Replica, it beats FSGS† (22.99 PSNR) by 3.36 dB and DNGaussian† (22.71 PSNR) by 3.64 dB; on ScanNet++, it surpasses 3DGS† (21.41 PSNR) by 2.48 dB and FSGS† (21.23 PSNR) by 2.66 dB. Among NeRF-based methods, FreeNeRF [52] is the strongest (20.99 PSNR on Replica, 20.17 on ScanNet++), yet still trails the proposed method by over 5 dB on Replica and 3.7 dB on ScanNet++. The depth-regularized methods DNGaussian and FSGS (without DUSt3R initialization) perform notably worse, with DNGaussian dropping to 17.63 PSNR on Replica, suggesting that depth regularization alone cannot compensate for missing views. In the inpainting comparison (Table 3), the method exceeds LaMa (24.56 PSNR) and SDS+SDInpaint (24.33 PSNR) by 1.79 and 2.02 dB respectively, and even the trajectory-augmented SDS variant (25.15 PSNR) by 1.20 dB, indicating that multi-view video generation with scene-grounding guidance provides more geometrically consistent hole-filling than single-image inpainting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-view 3DGS line of work (DNGaussian, FSGS) by replacing regularization-based approaches with a 'reconstruction by generation' pipeline that leverages a camera-controlled video diffusion model (ViewCrafter) to synthesize additional views. The key departure is the scene-grounding guidance, which adapts training-free guidance techniques from controllable diffusion literature (Bansal et al., Song et al.) to the multi-view consistency problem, using rendered sequences from an optimized 3DGS as the guidance signal rather than an external pre-trained model. The baseline itself builds on DUSt3R for point cloud initialization and FSGS for gaussian unpooling, positioning the contribution as a generation-augmented optimization layer on top of existing 3DGS infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and explicitly addresses two failure modes (extrapolation and occlusion) that prior sparse-view benchmarks and methods largely ignore, providing a more realistic evaluation setting. The training-free nature of the scene-grounding guidance makes the approach practical without requiring fine-tuning of large diffusion models, and the 3.5 dB / 2.5 dB gains over strong baselines are substantial. However, the evaluation is limited to 10 indoor scenes across two datasets, and the method's dependence on a specific video diffusion model (ViewCrafter) and its 25-frame sequence length constrains scalability to larger or outdoor scenes. Open questions include whether the guidance strategy generalizes to other diffusion backbones, whether it can handle dynamic scenes, and whether the trajectory initialization scales beyond the candidate-pose sampling used here.
