## Problem

Visual information extraction from relation-rich documents (forms, invoices, receipts) remains challenging due to layout diversity and severe training data scarcity—open-set VIE datasets like FUNSD contain only 199 images. Existing synthetic document generators rely on manually designed templates or rule-based layouts that limit diversity, and current layout generation methods produce bounding boxes without considering the textual content that must populate them, making them impractical for generating documents with meaningful content-layout associations.

## Input / Output

For content generation, the system takes a document title as input and produces text in Hierarchical Structure Text (HST) format, where colon-separated pairs encode key-value relations and varying numbers of hyphens encode hierarchical nesting, enabling automatic parsing into entity categories and links. For layout generation, the model receives a JSON-serialized document (width, height, and a list of text entities with masked bounding boxes) and outputs a JSON dictionary mapping each mask token to its predicted [x_left, y_top, x_right, y_bottom] coordinates. The final output is a rendered RGB document image with full annotations (entity categories, linking pairs, and bounding boxes), with non-normalized canvas sizes to support diverse document dimensions.

## Method

RIDGE employs a two-stage pipeline: (1) Content Generation uses GPT-4o-mini to produce document text in HST format, which directly encodes entity categories (header, key, value, other) and binary linking relationships through its hierarchical structure; (2) Content-driven Layout Generation (CLGM) fine-tunes LLaMA-3.1-8B with LoRA via self-supervised learning, where bounding box coordinates are replaced with mask tokens and the model predicts them solely from text content—requiring no human category or linking annotations. A key design choice is random permutation of entity order during training, forcing the model to infer spatial relationships purely from text semantics rather than positional cues. The layout is serialized as compact JSON (rather than HTML/CSS) to reduce sequence length for documents with hundreds of entities. Additionally, the paper introduces Hierarchical Structure Learning, a training paradigm where MLLMs are prompted to parse documents into HST format (optionally with localization or combined with VIE queries), strengthening their comprehension of hierarchical document structure beyond standard text-layout reconstruction.

## Results

After fine-tuning with RIDGE-generated documents, Qwen2-VL-7B achieves 66.48% F1 on FUNSD (+6.59%), 69.84% F1 on XFUND-ZH (+7.76%), 84.47% F1 on CORD (+1.76%), and 97.74% ANLS on SROIE⁻ (+0.24%), establishing new state-of-the-art results across all benchmarks. LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B similarly improves from 31.18% to 33.41% F1 on FUNSD and from 46.58% to 52.32% F1 on CORD. Gains are most pronounced on open-set VIE benchmarks (exceeding 6% for Qwen2-VL), consistent with RIDGE's design focus on relation-rich, open-set document generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIDGE-fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B substantially outperforms all prior baselines: it surpasses DocOwl-1.5-Chat by +15.60% F1 on FUNSD and +20.46% F1 on CORD, and exceeds Monkey by +32.21% on FUNSD and +29.93% on CORD. On the open-set XFUND-ZH benchmark, Qwen2-VL-7B+RIDGE (69.84%) dramatically outperforms Monkey (28.02%) and DocOwl-1.5-Chat (10.14%), reflecting RIDGE's strength in generating diverse, relation-rich form documents. LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B+RIDGE also surpasses DocOwl-1.5-Chat on FUNSD (33.41% vs. 50.88%—actually DocOwl still leads here) but exceeds it on CORD (52.32% vs. 64.01%—DocOwl still leads). The only marginal loss is LLaVA-NeXT on SROIE⁻ (−0.01% ANLS), while all other metrics show improvement. Domain-specific generation further boosts EPHOIE accuracy by +4.00% and ANLS by +3.30% over the general RIDGE baseline, confirming that targeted synthetic data complements the general open-set generator.

## Relation to Prior Work

RIDGE builds directly on LayoutNUWA's insight that LLMs possess latent layout expertise, but departs from it in two key ways: it makes layout generation content-driven (predicting bounding boxes conditioned on text entities rather than category labels) and eliminates the need for supervised layout annotations by using a self-supervised masking strategy over OCR results. It also extends the synthetic document generation line (DocSynth, SynthDoG, DocILE) by replacing template- or rule-based layout construction with an LLM-based pipeline that simultaneously produces meaningful text content, entity categories, and entity-linking annotations via the Hierarchical Structure Text format. The content generation stage repurposes LLMs as structured text generators, while the layout stage repurposes them as spatial planners, unifying two previously separate sub-problems into a single end-to-end pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RIDGE addresses a critical data bottleneck for open-set VIE, where datasets like FUNSD contain only 199 images and sensitive documents are hard to collect publicly. The self-supervised layout learning paradigm—requiring only off-the-shelf OCR output and no human annotation of categories or links—makes large-scale synthetic data generation practically feasible. The Hierarchical Structure Learning training paradigm and the interpretability it induces (models outputting document structure before answering) offer a reusable recipe for improving MLLM document comprehension. However, the paper acknowledges that RIDGE is primarily validated on form-like documents, and generalization to significantly different document types (e.g., multi-column academic papers, dense tables) remains an open question that may require additional domain-specific training.
