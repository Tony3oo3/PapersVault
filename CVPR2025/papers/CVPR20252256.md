## Problem

DETR-based detectors suffer from slow convergence due to two intertwined issues: one-to-one (O2O) Hungarian matching provides far fewer positive samples per target than one-to-many (O2M) strategies, and the small number of randomly initialized queries produces many low-quality matches that existing losses like Varifocal Loss under-penalize. This makes DETR training both slower and less effective than anchor-based O2M detectors, particularly for small objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252256/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (evaluated at 640×640 on COCO) and outputs bounding boxes with class labels in an end-to-end manner without NMS. During training, Dense O2O composites multiple images into a single canvas via mosaic or mixup augmentations, effectively multiplying the number of ground-truth targets per training sample while keeping the model's query count and architecture unchanged. At inference, the pipeline is identical to standard DETR: a fixed set of queries (100 or 300) directly produce final detections.

## Method

DEIM combines two complementary components to accelerate DETR convergence. First, Dense O2O matching increases the number of targets per training image using classical augmentations (mosaic, mixup), which naturally generates more positive samples under the existing one-to-one Hungarian matching without adding extra decoders or queries. Second, the Matchability-Aware Loss (MAL) replaces Varifocal Loss by raising the IoU-based matchability score q to a power γ (default 1.5) as the soft target label, making the loss sharply increase for low-quality matches while remaining comparable to VFL for high-quality ones; this also eliminates the α balancing hyperparameter. Together, these changes halve training epochs (e.g., 36 vs. 72 for RT-DETRv2-R50) while improving AP, and when paired with efficient backbones in D-FINE, DEIM achieves 56.5% AP at 78 FPS on T4, surpassing YOLOv11-X.

## Results

On COCO val2017, DEIM-D-FINE-X achieves 56.5 AP at 12.89 ms latency (78 FPS on T4), and DEIM-D-FINE-L achieves 54.7 AP at 8.07 ms (124 FPS), both trained in only 50 epochs. With a ResNet-50 backbone, DEIM-RT-DETRv2 reaches 53.9 AP in 36 epochs (half the 72 epochs of RT-DETRv2) and 54.3 AP in 60 epochs, outperforming DINO-Deformable-DETR by 2.7 AP. On CrowdHuman, DEIM boosts D-FINE-L from 56.0 to 57.5 AP (+1.5), with a >3% gain on small objects (APs: 29.0→33.2). Training a ResNet-50 DETR on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU reaches 53.2 AP within one day (~24 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEIM-D-FINE-L (54.7 AP, 8.07 ms) outperforms YOLO11-X (54.1 AP, 10.52 ms) by 0.6 AP while reducing inference latency by ~23%, and beats YOLOv10-X (54.4 AP, 10.74 ms) by 0.3 AP with lower latency. DEIM-D-FINE-X (56.5 AP) surpasses YOLOv9-E (55.6 AP) by 0.9 AP overall, though YOLOv9-E retains a 1.4 AP edge on small objects (40.2 vs 38.8 APs). Against DETR-based baselines, DEIM-D-FINE-X improves over D-FINE-X by 0.7 AP (56.5 vs 55.8) using only 50 epochs instead of 72, and DEIM-RT-DETRv2-R50 (53.9 AP, 36 epochs) exceeds DINO-Deformable-DETR (50.9 AP, 36 epochs) by 3.0 AP and RT-DETRv2 (53.4 AP, 72 epochs) by 0.5 AP with half the training time. On CrowdHuman, DEIM adds 1.5 AP over D-FINE-L (57.5 vs 56.0), with the largest gains on small objects (+4.2 APs) and high-IoU detections (+3.5 AP75). The method consistently wins on overall AP and training efficiency across all compared baselines, with the sole exception being small-object AP where YOLOv9-E's dense-anchor O2M strategy still holds a narrow advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEIM positions itself within the DETR training-efficiency literature, building directly on the one-to-one Hungarian matching framework of DETR and its real-time descendants (RT-DETR, D-FINE). It departs from prior approaches to increase positive samples—Group DETR, Co-DETR, H-DETR—which add parallel decoders or auxiliary heads, by instead using standard data augmentation (mosaic, mixup) to increase the number of targets per image while preserving the O2O matching structure. The Matchability-Aware Loss (MAL) is a targeted modification of Varifocal Loss, replacing the target label q with q^γ to better penalize low-quality matches, a regime where VFL's loss landscape is nearly flat. Conceptually, DEIM reframes the sparse-supervision problem as a target-count problem solvable with off-the-shelf augmentation rather than architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By halving training epochs while improving AP, DEIM offers a practical, plug-and-play recipe that is likely to become a standard training procedure for DETR-based real-time detectors, reducing the compute barrier for iterative research. The acknowledged gap in small-object AP relative to YOLO models (e.g., YOLOv9-E outperforms DEIM-D-FINE-L by ~1.4 AP on APs) highlights that the DETR paradigm still struggles with dense small-object detection, an open challenge for the field. The simplicity of the approach (augmentation + loss modification, no architectural changes) suggests broad applicability, but whether the gains persist under extreme scale or novel backbones remains to be tested.
