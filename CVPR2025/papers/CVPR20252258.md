## Problem

Existing 4D occupancy world models cannot decompose dynamic scenes into individual instances, struggle to capture fine-grained object geometry, and produce inaccurate forecasts. Instance-based methods suffer from information bottlenecks (finite trajectory sets), while BEV occupancy fields lack instance-level interpretability needed for right-of-way reasoning. Both paradigms are typically supervised only with coarse bounding boxes, poorly approximating true object shapes.

## Input / Output

DIO consumes 16 past LiDAR sweeps (3.0 s at 0.2 s intervals) plus ego poses, and is prompted with a 3D source point s and a 4D query point q=(x,y,z,t). The output is a continuous occupancy probability and 3D flow vector at q, queryable at arbitrary spatial-temporal locations rather than a fixed voxel grid. An empty source point (s=∅) yields scene-level occupancy, while a non-empty source point yields instance-specific occupancy, enabling decomposition without retraining.

## Method

The encoder pairs a sparse 3D backbone (SECOND-style sub-manifold convolutions) producing multi-resolution sparse volumes at 2×–16× downsampling with a dense BEV neck that densifies the 16× volume and applies multi-head spatial attention for large receptive fields. The decoder samples these multi-scale features at the query point, source point, and learned offsets via Multi-Scale Interpolation, then applies Multi-Head Deformable Attention to predict occupancy and flow. Training combines LiDAR ray-based self-supervision (positive segments past returns, negative segments before returns) with bounding-box-labeled instance prompts, including hard negatives between scaled boxes and free-space negatives to enforce proper instance separation. A novel flow-consistency loss enforces occupancy invariance by propagating query points along predicted flow vectors and penalizing occupancy changes, ensuring temporally coherent instance permanence.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 vehicle class, DIO-D (using HEDNet detector centroids as source points) achieves 94.36 mAP_I and 75.97 S-IoU_I for instance-level completion, and 86.01 mAP_I / 62.72 S-IoU_I for forecasting, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline UNO (89.43 / 70.00 completion; 82.99 / 60.74 forecasting) and the supervised ImplicitO-4D (65.04 / 52.96 completion; 61.51 / 45.51 forecasting). On the Argoverse 2 LiDAR forecasting leaderboard, DIO-∅ achieves the best L1 depth error of 2.11 m and 11.00% AbsRel, beating UNO (2.24 m / 12.00%). In the object-based LiDAR completion setting, DIO-NL reduces L1 error to 0.79 m, a 4.3× improvement over the strongest baseline (UNO at 3.42 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIO-D outperforms all baselines on instance-level occupancy metrics for both completion and forecasting. Against the strongest unsupervised baseline UNO, DIO-D gains +4.9 mAP_I and +6.0 S-IoU_I on completion, and +3.0 mAP_I and +2.0 S-IoU_I on forecasting. Against the supervised ImplicitO-4D, the margin is much larger: +29.3 mAP_I on completion and +24.5 mAP_I on forecasting, reflecting the advantage of LiDAR self-supervision over box-only labels. DIO-D also surpasses the Box Oracle (perfect 3D boxes) on instance-level metrics (94.36 vs. 50.49 mAP_I), demonstrating that predicting true object geometry far exceeds what axis-aligned boxes can represent, though the Oracle retains a slight edge on scene-level mAP_S (99.40 vs. 98.47). On the LiDAR forecasting leaderboard (Table 4), DIO-∅ achieves the best L1 (2.11 m) and AbsRel (11.00%) but a higher CD (13.96 m²) than UNO (8.10 m²), suggesting DIO's scene-level point cloud is slightly less compact overall while being more accurate per-ray.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIO extends the unsupervised 4D occupancy line initiated by UnO [18] and 4D-Occ [19], which established LiDAR-ray-based self-supervision for occupancy prediction, and the implicit occupancy architecture of ImplicitO-4D [16]. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of source-point prompting (inspired by Segment Anything) to decompose a single scene-level occupancy field into per-instance occupancy, a capability absent from all prior occupancy world models. Architecturally, DIO combines a sparse 3D backbone (SECOND) with a dense BEV deformable-attention neck and a multi-scale interpolation decoder, and introduces a flow-consistency loss that enforces occupancy invariance along predicted flow vectors across timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIO is the first model to jointly predict 4D scene occupancy, per-instance occupancy, and flow in a single framework, enabling downstream tasks (LiDAR point cloud forecasting, semantic occupancy) to be performed at the instance level with fine-grained geometry rather than coarse boxes. Its open-set generalization—trained on a subset of classes and evaluated on held-out ones—suggests the learned occupancy representation is not tied to specific object categories. A key limitation is the continued reliance on 3D bounding box labels during training, which the authors acknowledge as a barrier to fully unsupervised deployment; combining DIO with unsupervised detection is identified as a natural next step. The single-dataset evaluation (Argoverse 2) leaves open whether the decomposition and open-set properties transfer to other sensor suites or driving environments.
