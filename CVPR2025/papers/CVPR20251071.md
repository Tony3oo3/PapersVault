## Problem

Compressing large contrastive vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) into smaller, inference-efficient variants typically relies on complex knowledge distillation (KD) pipelines involving multi-teacher ensembles, synthetic captions, weight inheritance, and bespoke architectures. Prior active data curation methods assume reference models must be smaller than or equal to the student due to capacity-gap concerns, yet no work has explored whether a large reference model can effectively curate data for a smaller learner in the distillation setting. This paper asks whether simple online batch selection using a large reference model can serve as an effective and complementary alternative to explicit KD.

## Input / Output

The student model consumes 256×256 RGB images and text captions truncated to 64 tokens (tokenized with a 32K-vocabulary SentencePiece model), producing L2-normalized image and text embeddings whose pairwise similarities yield contrastive probabilities. At each training step, a super-batch of B=163,840 image-text pairs is drawn from DataComp-1B, from which a mini-batch of b=32,678 is actively selected via the reference model's loss scores. The trained two-tower VLM outputs image-text similarity scores used for zero-shot classification (27 benchmarks) and bidirectional image-text retrieval.

## Method

ACID (Active Curation as Implicit Distillation) selects training batches by scoring them with a large pretrained reference model: easy-reference scoring prefers batches the reference finds easy, while learnability scoring (H-ACID) additionally weights batches the student finds hard. The key theoretical result shows that under easy-reference scoring with softmax loss, the expected training objective equals a novel implicit distillation loss (Eq. 4) that combines reference-model predictions with real labels as targets, explaining why larger reference models improve student performance—contrary to standard active learning. Joint batch selection is implemented via blocked Gibbs sampling over the super-batch. The final recipe, ACED, combines H-ACID batch selection with an explicit softmax contrastive distillation loss on the same batch, demonstrating that the implicit and explicit distillation objectives are complementary and together outperform either alone across 27 zero-shot and retrieval benchmarks with up to 11% fewer inference FLOPs.

## Results

ACED achieves state-of-the-art performance across 27 zero-shot classification and image-text retrieval benchmarks while using up to 11% fewer inference FLOPs than prior SoTA. At the smallest scale (3.30 GFLOPs), ACED-F0 averages 64.0% across 27 evals, outperforming MobileCLIP-S0 (63.6%) and TinyCLIP-63M/32 (62.1%) while using 10.8% and 41.5% fewer FLOPs respectively. At the mid scale (7.14 GFLOPs), ACED-F1 reaches 69.7% average, beating MobileCLIP-S1 (67.9%) by 1.8% and TinyCLIP-39M/16 (59.5%) by 10.2%. At the largest scale (10.29 GFLOPs), ACED-F2 achieves 70.9% average, surpassing MobileCLIP-S2 (69.8%) by 1.1% with 4.8% fewer FLOPs. On ImageNet specifically, ACED-F1 (74.9%) even outperforms MobileCLIP-S2 (74.4%) while using 34% fewer GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACED consistently outperforms all prior SoTA FLOP-efficient methods at every compute scale. Against MobileCLIP (the strongest prior baseline), ACED-F0 wins by 0.4% average with 10.8% fewer FLOPs, ACED-F1 wins by 1.8% with 6.5% fewer FLOPs, and ACED-F2 wins by 1.1% with 4.8% fewer FLOPs; on ImageNet specifically, ACED-F1 (74.9%) surpasses MobileCLIP-S2 (74.4%) despite using 34% fewer GFLOPs. Against TinyCLIP, the margins are larger: ACED-F0 beats TinyCLIP-63M/32 by 1.9% average with 41.5% fewer FLOPs, and ACED-F1 beats TinyCLIP-39M/16 by 10.2% with 24.6% fewer FLOPs. Against CLIP-KD-RN50 (52.2% avg) and CLIP-CID-B/32 (62.7% IN-val only), ACED models dominate by wide margins. Against proprietary DatologyAI models, ACED-F0 outperforms DatologyAI-cls-S/32 (50.5%) by 13.5% average. In the LiT-Decoder setting (Table 3), ACED(B/16) outperforms SigLIP-B/16 (trained on 40B samples) on Flickr30k captioning (55.5 vs 53.4 CIDEr) and VQAv2 (66.6 vs 64.5), and outperforms SiLC-B/16 (trained on 20B samples) on all three generative tasks, demonstrating that ACED produces stronger frozen vision encoders for downstream generative tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) online batch selection for data curation (RHO-Loss [107], joint example selection [35]), and (2) knowledge distillation of contrastive vision-language models (MobileCLIP [155], TinyCLIP [173], CLIP-KD [180]). It departs from the data curation line by inverting a key assumption—prior work held that reference models should be smaller than or equal to the learner to avoid unlearnable prioritization, whereas here a larger reference is shown to be strictly beneficial. It departs from the KD line by replacing complex multi-objective, multi-teacher, and weight-inheritance strategies with a single online batch-selection criterion, and provides a theoretical derivation (Eq. 4) showing that easy-reference scoring under softmax loss is mathematically equivalent to a novel implicit distillation objective combining model predictions and real labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple, architecture-agnostic batch-selection procedure outperforms the current SoTA distillation pipelines (which rely on bespoke augmentations, synthetic captions, multi-teacher ensembles, and weight inheritance) while using fewer inference FLOPs, suggesting that much of the complexity in prior CLIP distillation work may be unnecessary. The complementarity finding—that ACID and explicit KD capture different information and combine additively—opens a clear path for further gains. Open questions include whether the optimal reference-to-student capacity ratio generalizes beyond ViT/BERT encoders, whether the approach transfers to generative or autoregressive VLMs, and whether the StableEval reliability framework can be adopted as a community standard to reduce the noise in cross-paper comparisons.
