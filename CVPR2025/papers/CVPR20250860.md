## Problem

Prior pathology large multimodal models were trained separately for patch-level and whole-slide image (WSI) tasks, preventing knowledge transfer across scales and producing redundant, task-specific models. No single foundation model existed that could unify both granularities while supporting diverse downstream tasks such as VQA, classification, captioning, and visual referring prompting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250860/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts two input modalities: pathology image patches (processed via a 3×3 AnyRes grid) and gigapixel WSIs up to 100,000×100,000 pixels (split into 2048×2048 regions, each further tiled at three scales: 512×512, 1024×1024, and 2048×2048), combined with free-form text instructions. Outputs include class labels for subtyping tasks, free-text VQA answers, structured pathology reports for WSIs, and visually grounded referring descriptions with highlighted regions. WSI inputs are compressed to a fixed 1152-token representation regardless of original slide size, ensuring stable LLM processing.

## Method

CPath-Omni builds on the LLaVA-Next framework with two key vision components. CPath-CLIP is the first pathology CLIP model to replace GPT-2 with an LLM (Qwen2-1.5B) as the text encoder and to concatenate features from both OpenAI CLIP-L and the DINOv2-based Virchow2 as dual visual encoders, trained on 700K curated image-caption pairs. SlideParser handles WSIs via multi-scale tile encoding (three resolutions per 2048×2048 region) followed by CoCa-style cross-attention compression to a fixed 1152 tokens. Training proceeds in four stages: (1) pre-aligning CPath-CLIP with Qwen2.5-14B through a two-layer MLP, (2) full fine-tuning on 351K patch instruction samples spanning four tasks, (3) WSI pretraining with only SlideParser unfrozen, and (4) joint patch-WSI instruction tuning mixing 15% patch data with WSI data to enable cross-scale knowledge transfer.

## Results

CPath-CLIP achieves 83.2% average zero-shot classification accuracy across 9 pathology datasets, surpassing the previous SOTA PathGen-CLIP-L (79.7%) by 3.5 points, with particularly large gains on Osteo (+6.1%), Pcam (+7.7%), and LC-Lung (+7.3%). On the PathMMU VQA benchmark, CPath-Omni scores 72.2% overall accuracy, exceeding the best pathology-specific model PathGen-LLaVA (58.4%) by 13.8 points and GPT-4V (49.8%) by 22.4 points, slightly surpassing human pathologist performance (71.8%). At the WSI level, CPath-Omni achieves 67.3% and 70.8% accuracy on open- and closed-ended VQA respectively, nearly doubling GPT-4o's 20.5% and 35.5%, and attains BLEU-1 of 33.7 for report generation, marginally outperforming HistGen (31.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPath-CLIP outperforms all seven compared CLIP-based models (OpenAI-CLIP-L, PLIP, QuiltNet, PathCLIP, KEP, BiomedCLIP, PathGen-CLIP-L) on 8 of 9 zero-shot classification datasets, with the largest margin over PathGen-CLIP-L on Pcam (95.9% vs. 88.2%) and Osteo (80.7% vs. 74.6%). On PathMMU VQA, CPath-Omni decisively beats all general-purpose LMMs (GPT-4V: 49.8%, Gemini Pro Vision: 42.7%, Qwen-VL-MAX: 45.9%) and all pathology-specific LMMs (PathGen-LLaVA: 58.4%, Quilt-LLaVA: 41.5%, LLaVA-Med: 26.2%), with the PathCLS subset showing the largest gap (79.0% vs. 48.9% for PathGen-LLaVA). In WSI classification (Fig. 4c), CPath-Omni surpasses task-specific ABMIL and DSMIL on 3 of 8 TCGA subtyping tasks and slightly leads overall, while significantly outperforming PRISM and GPT-4o. For WSI report generation, CPath-Omni edges out HistGen (BLEU-1: 33.7 vs. 31.8) and substantially exceeds GPT-4o (15.8) and PRISM (0.0). In WSI VQA, CPath-Omni nearly triples GPT-4o's performance (67.3% vs. 20.5% open-ended; 70.8% vs. 35.5% closed-ended). In VPR and captioning (Table 3), CPath-Omni achieves 84–100% win rates against Quilt-LLaVA, PathGen-LLaVA, and LLaVA-Med under both GPT-4o and human evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPath-Omni extends the LLaVA-Next multimodal framework to computational pathology by replacing the standard CLIP vision encoder with a hybrid CPath-CLIP that concatenates features from OpenAI CLIP-L and the DINOv2-based Virchow2, while substituting CLIP's GPT-2 text encoder with Qwen2-1.5B for stronger semantic alignment. For WSI processing, it adapts CoCa's query-token mechanism to compress variable-length patch sequences into a fixed 1152-token representation, addressing the scale variability inherent in gigapixel slides. The paper positions itself against a fragmented landscape where prior pathology LMMs (PathGen-LLaVA, Quilt-LLaVA, PathChat) handle only patches and WSI-specific models (WsiCaption, HistGen, PRISM) handle only slides, with no prior work unifying both scales in a single generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single 15B-parameter model can match or exceed task-specific models across 39 of 42 pathology datasets, CPath-Omni provides a credible blueprint for the 'one-for-all' paradigm in computational pathology, potentially reducing the need to maintain separate models for each task and scale. The finding that CPath-CLIP reaches SOTA zero-shot performance with only 700K training pairs (versus 400M+ for general-domain CLIP) suggests pathology-specific LLM-based text encoders may dramatically reduce data requirements. Open questions include whether the unified training genuinely transfers knowledge across scales or merely benefits from data overlap, how the model performs in prospective clinical validation, and whether the 15B parameter count is necessary or whether smaller unified models could achieve comparable results.
