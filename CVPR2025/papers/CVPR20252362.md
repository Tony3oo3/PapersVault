## Problem

Large vision models (e.g., DINOv2) capture local semantics well but fail to encode global geometric relationships between semantic regions, causing semantic correspondence to degrade under extreme view variation and symmetric objects. Existing remedies such as feature fine-tuning or spatial bias still leave substantial errors on rigid categories with large viewpoint changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252362/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time the method consumes a small set of sparsely annotated RGB images (L labeled keypoints per image, e.g., nose, wing tips) plus monocular depth maps from DepthAnythingV2 to build a per-category 3D representation. At inference it takes a single RGB image, the pre-built 3D object-class representation, and the GeoAware feature backbone, and produces (i) an aligned dense point cloud and (ii) a dense correspondence map that assigns every query pixel in one image to its semantically matching pixel in another image via the shared canonical 3D space.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, 3D object-class representations are constructed by back-projecting sparse keypoints with monocular depth, optimizing per-image focal lengths to minimize variance of scale-invariant geometric features (inter-keypoint angles and edge-length ratios), fitting Beta distributions over those features, and iteratively placing canonical keypoints; a dense point cloud is then obtained by back-projecting full depth maps, aligning them to the sparse cloud via barycentric parametrization, merging, and k-means clustering, with per-point semantic features averaged from GeoAware patch embeddings. Second, at inference the sparse keypoint positions and focal length are optimized by gradient descent on a composite alignment loss comprising a dense+sparse reconstruction term (negative expected image likelihood combining spatial and semantic Gaussian kernels), a geometric-consistency term (negative log-likelihood under the fitted Beta distributions), a background-mask penalty, and a depth-regularization term; the spatial kernel's variance is annealed from large to small to escape local minima. Correspondence between two images is then obtained by projecting each query point into the canonical 3D space of its image and reading off the matching pixel in the other image's aligned projection.

## Results

On SPAIR-71K, SEMALIGN3D achieves 88.9% PCK@0.1 overall, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art GeoAware (85.6%) by 3.3 points. The method outperforms GeoAware in 13 of 18 categories, with the largest gains on rigid objects: Bottle (+11.7 pts), Chair (+14.9 pts), and TV (+10.8 pts). At the stricter PCK@0.05 level, the method still leads (77.5% vs 75.3%), but at PCK@0.01 it underperforms GeoAware (15.8% vs 22.0%), suggesting the spatial bias in the 3D alignment trades some precision for robustness at extreme view changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary supervised baseline GeoAware (AP-10k P.T.), SEMALIGN3D wins on 13/18 SPAIR-71K categories, with the largest margins on rigid objects (Bottle +11.7, Chair +14.9, TV +10.8) but loses on Bird (94.0 vs 97.2), Boat (66.5 vs 70.4), and Cat (91.4 vs 92.7), where non-rigid or highly variable anatomy limits the 3D representation's benefit. Against the best unsupervised method SphericalMaps (67.3% overall), the gap is 21.6 points, and against SD+DINOv2(S) (74.6%), it is 14.3 points. On cross-dataset evaluation (Table 3), the method underperforms GeoAware on AP-10K (89.1 vs 92.8 PCK@0.1) and PF-PASCAL (86.6 vs 89.6 PCK@0.15), which the authors attribute to AP-10K's lack of multi-instance cases and PF-PASCAL's pose similarity between source and target images favoring feature-based matching. The method also trails GeoAware at PCK@0.01 (15.8 vs 22.0), indicating reduced precision at tight tolerances, while maintaining the lead at PCK@0.05 (77.5 vs 75.3) and PCK@0.1 (88.9 vs 85.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of 2D feature-based semantic correspondence (fine-tuning DINOv2/Stable Diffusion features, optimizing correlation matrices, or learning canonical coordinate mappings) by introducing an explicit 3D object-class representation that is aligned to each input image at inference time. It builds directly on GeoAware's fine-tuned backbone features for semantic similarity but adds a geometric layer: monocular depth estimates from DepthAnythingV2 are back-projected and combined with Beta-distribution-fitted geometric invariants (angles, edge ratios) to construct sparse and dense 3D point clouds per object category. The alignment step—minimizing a multi-term loss (reconstruction, geometric consistency, background penalization, depth regularization) via gradient descent with barycentric parametrization—is a new formulation not present in prior semantic correspondence work, which typically operates entirely in 2D feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even noisy monocular depth estimates, when combined with LVM features and simple geometric priors, can yield 3D class representations robust enough to handle extreme view variation and symmetry—failure modes that purely 2D feature methods struggle with. This opens a path toward data-efficient, geometry-aware correspondence that could benefit robotics (category-level manipulation) and image editing. Key open questions include: improving representation quality to capture joint limits and bilateral symmetry (the authors show the right wing doesn't mirror the left under distortion), enabling end-to-end differentiable learning, reducing the 10–30 s per-image runtime, and extending the approach to non-rigid objects where current performance degrades significantly (AP-10K, PF-PASCAL results).
