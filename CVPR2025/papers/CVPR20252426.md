## Problem

Existing Concept Bottleneck Models learn only coarse-grained, global image-to-concept mappings, producing spurious visual-concept correlations at three granularity levels (part-background, part-object, part-part) and requiring post-hoc Grad-CAM to localize concepts in image space. Additionally, statistical biases in training data (e.g., class-background co-occurrence, concept-concept co-occurrence) cause shortcut learning that further degrades both interpretability and out-of-distribution generalization.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images resized to 252×252, which are split into non-overlapping patches by a pre-trained DINOv2 ViT-L/14 encoder, alongside a set of M pre-defined text concepts encoded by a CLIP text encoder (both 768-dim). Beyond the standard class label and M-dimensional concept activation vector, DOT-CBM uniquely produces a per-concept inversion mask of size N (number of patches) that explicitly indicates which image regions support each concept prediction—something prior CBMs can only approximate via Grad-CAM heatmaps.

## Method

DOT-CBM replaces the black-box global-to-concept mapping with a Disentangled Optimal Transport framework operating on local ViT patch features. Two orthogonal projection regularizers (VOP for patches, TOP for concepts) enforce intra-modality disentanglement by pushing features onto a unit hypersphere while penalizing cosine similarity within foreground regions. An entropic OT problem (solved via Sinkhorn iterations) then computes an explicit N×M transport assignment matrix Q* between patches and concepts, from which concept activations are derived via OT distance and inversion masks are read directly from Q* columns. Two discrete priors address data bias: a saliency-map-derived patch prior θ penalizes background-to-concept transport, and a conditioned co-occurrence matrix prior γ penalizes transport to concepts that are statistically confounded with others. The final class prediction uses a standard linear layer on the concept activation vector, trained with cross-entropy.

## Results

DOT-CBM achieves state-of-the-art classification accuracy on 3 of 4 benchmarks: 83.84% on ImageNet (+0.91), 85.39% on CUB (+3.29), and 85.83% on CIFAR-100 (+1.08), while trailing CoopCBM by only 0.25 points on AwA2 (96.83% vs. 97.08%). On part detection (mAP@0.5), DOT-CBM substantially outperforms all baselines by 7–9 points across all four benchmarks, reaching 50.12 on Part-ImageNet, 53.47 on CUB, 50.93 on RIVAL-10, and 44.18 on PASCAL-Parts. On OOD generalization with background shifts, DOT-CBM with priors achieves 49.7% on CUB-OOD and 52.4% on Dogs-OOD, surpassing the best baseline by over 10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOT-CBM outperforms all five baselines on three of four classification benchmarks (ImageNet, CUB, CIFAR-100) and on all four part detection benchmarks. The largest classification gains are on CUB (+3.29 over CoopCBM's 82.10) and CIFAR-100 (+1.08 over SparseCBM's 84.75), while DOT-CBM narrowly loses to CoopCBM on AwA2 by 0.25 points (96.83 vs. 97.08). The most dramatic improvements appear in part detection: DOT-CBM surpasses CoopCBM (the strongest baseline) by 8.95 mAP on Part-ImageNet, 8.31 on CUB, 7.11 on RIVAL-10, and 8.43 on PASCAL-Parts, reflecting the benefit of explicit patch-concept transport assignment over Grad-CAM-based localization. Vanilla-CBM and CEM, which rely on global image features without fine-grained alignment, lag far behind on both classification and part detection. The OOD experiments further show that the prior distribution modeling (saliency-based patch prior + co-occurrence-based concept prior) adds roughly 10 points of OOD accuracy over the no-prior variant, confirming that the priors specifically target the part-background and part-part spurious correlations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) lineage initiated by Koh et al. (2020) and its recent variants (CEM, LaBo, SparseCBM, CoopCBM), all of which model the image-to-concept mapping as a black-box neural network requiring post-hoc Grad-CAM for localization. DOT-CBM departs from this by replacing the global image-to-concept mapping with an explicit optimal transport (Sinkhorn) assignment between ViT patch features and CLIP text embeddings, yielding a closed-form correlation matrix that supports bidirectional interpretability. The orthogonal projection losses for intra-modality disentanglement and the two prior distributions (saliency-based patch prior, co-occurrence-based concept prior) are novel regularizers not present in prior CBM work, though the OT machinery itself is borrowed from the broader optimal transport literature in domain adaptation and cross-modal alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that DOT-CBM provides native, training-free concept-to-image localization (inversion masks) without relying on post-hoc attribution methods, which is a meaningful step toward truly transparent concept-based classifiers. The ~10-point OOD generalization gain on background-shifted CUB and Dogs datasets suggests that much of the generalization failure in CBMs stems from the very spurious correlations the OT priors target, offering a concrete remedy for deployment in distributionally shifted settings. Open questions include scalability to very large concept banks (the Sinkhorn cost is O(N×M)), whether the approach transfers to video or multi-modal concept prediction, and whether the reliance on DINOv2/CLIP as frozen backbones limits adaptability to domain-specific concept vocabularies.
