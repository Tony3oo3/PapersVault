## Problem

Existing SNN knowledge distillation methods naively average the student's outputs across the temporal dimension before computing distillation losses, discarding the rich per-time-step information that is central to SNN computation. This averaging also masks local outliers and allows the teacher's overconfident errors to propagate unchecked into the student, limiting the achievable accuracy gap between SNNs and ANNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251275/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The student SNN receives a standard RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and processes it over T discrete time steps using LIF neurons, producing a logit vector at each step. The final prediction is the argmax of the time-averaged logits, while the teacher ANN produces a single logit vector from the same image. The output is a single class label from C categories.

## Method

The proposed TSER loss replaces the conventional temporal-averaging step in SNN distillation with a temporal separation strategy: KL divergence and cross-entropy are computed independently at each of the T time steps and then averaged, so the student learns to match the teacher's distribution and the true label at every individual time step rather than only in aggregate. On top of this, an entropy regularization term (−λ·mean_t H(Q^stu(t))) is added to the loss, which penalizes the student for becoming overconfident and thereby counteracts the teacher's erroneous high-confidence predictions. The total training objective is L_TSER = (1−α)·(1/T)Σ CE(Q^stu(t), y) + α·(1/T)Σ KL(Q^tea‖Q^stu(t)) − λ·(1/T)Σ H(Q^stu(t)). Training uses surrogate-gradient BPTT through the LIF neurons, and at inference the mean of per-step logits is used for classification.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-19 (T=2), TSER achieves 81.29% accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA KDSNN (80.88%) by 0.41% and LaSNN (80.30%) by 0.99%. On CIFAR-10 with the same architecture, it reaches 96.72%, outperforming KDSNN (96.15%) by 0.57%. On ImageNet with SEW ResNet-34 (T=4), TSER achieves 73.16% top-1 accuracy, exceeding KDSNN (67.28%) by a substantial 5.88% margin. The method also maintains competitive energy consumption (0.42 mJ for ResNet-18) and the second-fastest training time (2.70 s/batch) among compared KD methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSER outperforms all three prior SNN distillation methods (LaSNN, KDSNN, BKDSNN) across every architecture and dataset tested. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-19 (T=2), TSER (81.29%) beats KDSNN (80.88%) by 0.41%, LaSNN (80.30%) by 0.99%, and BKDSNN (75.74%) by 5.55%. The advantage is most dramatic on ImageNet, where TSER with SEW ResNet-34 (73.16%) surpasses KDSNN (67.28%) by 5.88% and LaSNN (66.98%) by 6.18%, indicating that temporal separation becomes increasingly beneficial at scale. Against non-KD baselines, TSER with ResNet-19 (81.29% CIFAR-100) exceeds the best direct-training method MPBN (80.10%) by 1.19% and the best transformer-based SNN SpikingFormer (79.09%) by 2.20%. On CIFAR-10, TSER (96.72%) also surpasses the strongest non-KD baseline PFA (95.71%) by 1.01%. The method achieves these gains while maintaining comparable energy consumption (0.42 mJ) and the second-fastest per-batch training time (2.70 s) among KD methods, confirming that the improvements come from the loss design rather than additional computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard approach in SNN knowledge distillation (KDSNN, LaSNN, BKDSNN) where outputs across the temporal dimension are averaged before computing KL and CE losses. Rather than building on a single prior method, it identifies a structural mismatch in how ANN distillation was naively ported to SNNs: the temporal averaging step conflates per-time-step information that is inherently informative in spike-based processing. The proposed temporal separation strategy repurposes standard logit distillation (KL divergence + cross-entropy) by computing losses independently at each time step, and adds an entropy regularization term to counteract overconfident teacher errors—both ideas drawn from general KD and regularization literature but applied in a novel SNN-specific configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a simple, drop-in loss modification that narrows the SNN–ANN accuracy gap without increasing model complexity, energy consumption, or training time, which is practically valuable for neuromorphic deployment. However, the gains are modest (≈0.5–1% on CIFAR, ≈2% on ImageNet) and the method is validated only on image classification with LIF neurons and CNN/SEW-ResNet backbones. Open questions include whether temporal separation generalizes to transformer-based SNNs, non-LIF neuron models, or non-classification tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the entropy regularization interacts differently with feature-based distillation objectives.
