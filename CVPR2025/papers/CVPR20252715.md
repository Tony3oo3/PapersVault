## Problem

LiDAR place recognition in natural forests remains largely under-explored compared to urban settings, due to high self-similarity, reduced salient geometric features, and substantial seasonal variation in vegetation. Existing learning-based methods trained on urban data exhibit a domain gap when transferred to forests, and single-height 2D projections lose discriminative information along the elevation axis where tree structures vary most.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252715/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 60 m-diameter LiDAR submap, preprocesses it by removing ground points (via CSF segmentation) and all points above 6 m, then slices the remaining cloud into 5 horizontal layers (1–6 m at 1 m intervals) and projects each into a 480×480 BEV density image at 0.5 m resolution. The output is a single 1024-dimensional L2-normalized global descriptor that is yaw-invariant, suitable for cosine-similarity retrieval against a database of previously seen submaps.

## Method

ForestLPR processes each of the S=5 BEV density images independently through a shared DeiT-S-384 backbone, extracting patch tokens from three transformer depths (layers 2, 7, 12) and concatenating them along the channel axis to form multi-scale local features. The key architectural contribution is a patch-level multi-BEV interaction module that computes adaptive per-patch weights across height slices: for each spatial location, it subtracts the cross-slice mean to obtain relative features, projects them through a learned linear layer, and applies softmax to produce a weight vector over slices, then forms a weighted sum of the slice features. This allows each patch to attend to whichever height carries the most discriminative signal, mitigating occlusion and seasonal variation. A GeM pooling layer aggregates the weighted patch tokens, which are concatenated with the weighted [class] and [distillation] tokens and projected to 1024 dimensions. Training uses a two-stage strategy—first fine-tuning the backbone on single-BEV images, then end-to-end fine-tuning the full multi-BEV pipeline—with triplet loss and overlap-based positive mining (octree IoU > 0.9) to handle partial visibility in dense forests.

## Results

On intra-sequence loop closure detection across all datasets (Wild-Places V-03/V-04/K-03/K-04, ANYmal, Botanic), ForestLPR achieves a mean F1 of 75.46% and Recall@1 of 78.45%, outperforming the best baseline LoGG3D-Net (F1 72.29%, R@1 71.07%) by 7.38% on R@1. On inter-sequence re-localization in Wild-Places, ForestLPR reaches a mean R@1 of 78.73%, surpassing LoGG3D-Net (69.62%) by 9.11%. The method also generalizes without fine-tuning to the ANYmal and Botanic datasets, where it outperforms the best baseline by 4.06% and 2.47% on R@1, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForestLPR outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and both evaluation protocols. The closest competitor is LoGG3D-Net (sparse convolutional 3D point cloud method), which ForestLPR beats by 7.38% mean R@1 on intra-sequence and 9.11% on inter-sequence evaluation; the gap widens on the more challenging V-03 and K-03 sequences with heavy reverse revisits and occlusion. Among 3D point cloud baselines, MinkLoc3Dv2 and TransLoc3D trail LoGG3D-Net by 14–16% R@1, while PointPillar is weakest overall (54.88% mean R@1). Among BEV-based baselines, Scan-Context achieves the highest mean R@1 (62.54%) but still lags ForestLPR by 15.91%; BEVPlace (60.56%) and MapClosures (43.64%) are further behind, with MapClosures particularly poor on Wild-Places sequences. Notably, ForestLPR generalizes to ANYmal and Botanic without fine-tuning, outperforming Scan-Context (the best baseline there) by 4.06% R@1 on ANYmal and LoGG3D-Net by 2.47% on Botanic, confirming robustness across diverse forest conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ForestLPR extends the BEV-based LiDAR place recognition line (ScanContext, BEVPlace, MapClosures) by replacing the single BEV density image with multiple height-sliced BEV images processed through a shared DeiT-S-384 backbone, drawing on multi-level feature aggregation ideas from TransVPR and channel-wise attention mechanisms. The key conceptual departure is the multi-BEV interaction module, which performs patch-level adaptive weighting across height slices rather than treating the elevation axis as a single collapsed projection or simply concatenating multi-height inputs. The paper also builds on the Wild-Places benchmark and its forest-specific challenges (self-similarity, seasonal vegetation change), positioning itself as a domain-specific solution where prior urban-trained methods show a clear domain gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Forest and natural-environment place recognition remains a practical bottleneck for robotic navigation in unstructured terrain, and this paper provides the first method to consistently outperform SOTA across multiple forest datasets with strong cross-dataset generalization (no fine-tuning needed for ANYmal or Botanic). The low computational footprint (16.9 ms extraction, 538 MB memory) makes onboard deployment on resource-constrained platforms like quadrupedal robots feasible. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dense jungles or other highly vegetated environments, whether the fixed 1–6 m height window is optimal across all forest types, and how the method performs under significant sensor degradation or partial occlusion beyond what the current benchmarks capture.
