## Problem

Existing LLM-based video understanding models are limited to short videos (5–15 seconds) because memory consumption grows rapidly with frame count. Prior long-video approaches compress visual tokens using only single-modality (frame-to-frame) similarity, ignoring the correlation between visual features and the text query, and apply fixed compression intervals that fail to capture the dynamic redundancy structure across video layers. This leads to loss of critical visual tokens relevant to complex text prompts and inflexible memory management.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250748/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (arbitrarily long, up to hours) sampled at 10 fps and a text prompt (question, captioning instruction, or classification label) as input. Video frames are encoded individually by a frozen ViT-G/14 encoder into per-frame token sequences of shape P×C. The output is a free-form text generation (answer, caption, or class label) produced by a frozen Vicuna-7B LLM conditioned on a fixed-size learnable query vector (N×C) that aggregates the entire video's information, making the output independent of video length.

## Method

AdaCM² introduces an adaptive cross-modality memory reduction mechanism inside the Q-Former that processes video frames regressively (one frame at a time) rather than all at once. A video KV cache is maintained and partitioned into a 'recent' portion (fully preserved) and a 'previous' portion, from which only the top-β visual tokens ranked by their accumulated cross-modality attention score with the text prompt are retained. The split ratio α and conserve ratio β are set per layer, exploiting the observation that cross-attention redundancy increases in deeper layers. This yields a cache size that converges to the constant Pr/(1−r) as video length grows unbounded, where r = α + (1−α)β. The resulting fixed-size query embedding is then passed to a frozen Vicuna-7B LLM for autoregressive text generation, with only the Q-Former parameters fine-tuned during training.

## Results

On the LVU long-term video understanding benchmark, AdaCM² achieves 67.5% average Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline MA-LMM (63.0%) by 4.5 percentage points. On Breakfast and COIN, AdaCM² reaches 94.4% and 93.3% Top-1 accuracy respectively, surpassing MA-LMM by 1.4% and 0.1%. For video captioning on MSVD, AdaCM² attains 51.4% METEOR and 189.4 CIDEr, improving over MA-LMM by 0.4% and 10.3 points. Critically, AdaCM² reduces peak GPU memory consumption by up to 65% while maintaining near-constant memory usage as video length grows, whereas baselines like InstructBLIP hit OOM errors after ~100 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaCM² outperforms all 8 LVU baselines in average Top-1 accuracy, with the largest margin over S5 (59.2%) at +8.3 points and over MA-LMM (63.0%) at +4.5 points; it leads on 6 of 7 individual LVU sub-tasks (losing only on 'Speak' to MA-LMM's 44.8 vs. 40.2). On Breakfast, AdaCM² (94.4%) beats MA-LMM (93.0%) by 1.4% and S5 (90.7%) by 3.7%; on COIN it edges out MA-LMM (93.2%) by 0.1% while matching S5 (90.8%) with a 2.5% advantage. For video captioning, AdaCM² surpasses MA-LMM on MSVD CIDEr (189.4 vs. 179.1) and METEOR (51.4 vs. 51.0), and matches MA-LMM on MSRVTT METEOR (33.0 vs. 33.4) and YouCook2 METEOR (17.6 vs. 17.6), though mPLUG-2 retains a slight MSRVTT METEOR edge (34.9 vs. 33.0) at the cost of extensive pre-training data. The older non-LLM baselines (Obj_T4mer, Performer, Orthoformer, VideoBERT, TSN, VideoGraph, Timeception, GHRM, D-Sprv.) are all substantially outperformed, with gaps of 10–25 points on LVU and 5–25 points on Breakfast/COIN, confirming that the LLM-based architecture with adaptive memory reduction is the primary source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BLIP-2/InstructBLIP Q-Former architecture for visual-textual alignment, extending it from single-image to long-video processing. It departs from prior long-video compression methods (MA-LMM, Koala, Vis4mer) that reduce visual tokens based solely on intra-modality similarity (e.g., cosine similarity between adjacent frames), instead introducing cross-modality attention scores as the criterion for token retention. The approach also draws conceptual inspiration from KV cache eviction techniques in LLM inference (H2O, KeyFormer, MiniCache), repurposing the 'keep only high-attention tokens' philosophy for the video domain with an added layer-wise adaptive schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-constant memory footprint (65% reduction) makes extremely long video understanding (hours-long) feasible on standard hardware, which is a practical bottleneck for real-world applications like movie analysis and video retrieval. The plug-and-play compatibility with existing BLIP-based models lowers the adoption barrier. However, the paper sets α and β uniformly to 0.1 across all layers despite motivating layer-wise adaptivity, and the LLM backbone is limited to Vicuna-7B, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other architectures or benefits from truly per-layer tuned ratios. The modest performance gains on some captioning metrics (e.g., MSRVTT CIDEr slightly below MA-LMM) suggest the memory savings come at a small accuracy cost in certain settings.
