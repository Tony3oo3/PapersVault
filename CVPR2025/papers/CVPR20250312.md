## Problem

Generative diffusion models produce high-quality individual samples but systematically fail to cover the full training distribution, with no state-of-the-art model surpassing 77% of training data diversity. Existing metrics (FID, Precision, Recall, Coverage) are inadequate for detecting or quantifying this diversity gap due to hyperparameter sensitivity, large sample requirements, and reliance on feature extractors that collapse real data into smaller representation spaces (the 'measurement gap').

## Input / Output

For the IRS metric, input is a small set of synthetic images (as few as n << N_train) and a reference real dataset; output is a diversity score in [0,1] with statistical confidence intervals, computed via nearest-neighbor retrieval in SwAV feature space. For DiADM, the model consumes precomputed Inception-v3 features of training images as pseudo-unconditional conditioning (replacing placeholder labels) plus random noise, and outputs 512×512 RGB images. The adjusted IRS (IRS_∞,a) normalizes synthetic diversity by real-data diversity to eliminate the measurement gap, and can exceed 1 at low sampling ratios.

## Method

The paper introduces two contributions. First, the Image Retrieval Score (IRS) frames diversity as an image retrieval problem: each synthetic image is matched to its nearest real image in feature space, and the fraction of unique real images retrieved estimates coverage. Using the coupon collector problem, IRS_∞ is estimated via maximum likelihood over the number of 'learned' images, with lower and upper confidence bounds derived from combinatorial probability (Stirling numbers of the second kind). A measurement-gap adjustment normalizes by the IRS computed on real data, making the score interpretable as relative diversity. Second, DiADM (Diversity-Aware Diffusion Models) replaces the placeholder label in unconditional EDM-2 with precomputed Inception-v3 features of each training image, creating a 'pseudo-unconditional' conditioning where each instance acts as its own class. This disentangles fidelity (handled by the diffusion backbone) from diversity (handled by the feature conditioning), enabling the model to be queried for diverse outputs at sampling time without sacrificing image quality.

## Results

On ImageNet-512, the best-performing model (EDM-2-XL-512) achieves an adjusted IRS of 0.77 (conditional) and 0.03 (unconditional), with FID of 2.92, confirming that no current SOTA diffusion model surpasses 77% of training data diversity. The proposed DiADM substantially improves over the EDM-2 baseline across all five datasets: on ImageNet-512, FID drops from 51.59 to 22.28 and IRS rises from 0.09 to 0.15; on FFHQ, FID drops from 40.92 to 6.24 and IRS rises from 0.23 to 1.51 (exceeding the real-data reference). The measurement gap analysis shows that commonly used feature extractors (Inception, DINOv2, CLIP) collapse real-data diversity to 65–98%, with SwAV performing best (89–100% consensus agreement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDM-2 [24] is the strongest baseline on both FID and IRS among all compared models, with the XL variant reaching 0.77 adjusted IRS (conditional) and FID 2.92; however, its unconditional IRS collapses to 0.03, revealing severe diversity loss without class conditioning. MAR [27] achieves competitive FID (3.11–3.79) and moderate IRS (0.45–0.64 conditional), but its unconditional IRS plateaus at 0.38 regardless of model size, indicating a diversity ceiling. DiT [36] performs poorly on diversity (IRS 0.21–0.23 conditional) despite high precision (0.94–0.96), and LDM-256 [43] is the weakest overall with IRS 0.16 and FID 26.09. ADM-256 [12] sits in the middle with IRS 0.44 and FID 6.01. The proposed DiADM, trained with only 574 A40 GPU hours (one-tenth of EDM-2's budget), outperforms the EDM-2 baseline on both FID and IRS across all five datasets, with the most dramatic gains on FFHQ (IRS 0.23→1.51) and ChestX-ray14 (IRS 0.19→1.08), where it even exceeds the real-data diversity reference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the growing body of work addressing diversity in generative models, which has largely focused on GANs and on bias-mitigation or noise-perturbation strategies for diffusion models (e.g., Liu et al., Meng et al., Miao et al.) that trade diversity for fidelity. It departs from prior metric work (FID, Precision/Recall, Coverage/Density, Vendi Score) by reframing diversity measurement as an image retrieval problem grounded in the coupon collector framework, and by drawing on Stein et al.'s [49] observation that feature extractor choice critically affects generative metrics. The DiADM method extends EDM (Karras et al.) by replacing placeholder labels with precomputed Inception-v3 features, creating a 'pseudo-unconditional' conditioning that disentangles diversity from image quality rather than trading one against the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no state-of-the-art diffusion model exceeds 77% of training-data diversity, combined with the demonstration that all commonly used feature extractors exhibit a 'measurement gap' that invalidates current diversity metrics, fundamentally challenges how the field evaluates generative models. DiADM shows that diversity can be improved without sacrificing FID, breaking the assumed diversity-quality tradeoff, though experiments are limited to unconditional models and a small compute budget (574 GPU hours, ~1/10 of EDM training). Open questions include extending the approach to conditional generation, identifying or training feature extractors specifically optimized for diversity measurement, and understanding whether the 77% ceiling reflects a fundamental limitation of diffusion training or is addressable with more compute and better conditioning.
