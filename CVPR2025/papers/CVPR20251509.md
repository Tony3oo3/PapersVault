## Problem

Existing audio-driven head generation methods either handle only single-sided communication (talking or listening in isolation) or require manual role assignment and explicit role switching between separate Speaker and Listener generators for dyadic conversations. This paradigm produces unnatural transitions and cannot cover all conversational states such as simultaneous speaking. The paper asks whether a single unified model can let an agent dynamically and naturally alternate between speaking and listening states guided solely by the dual-track dialogue audio.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251509/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two audio tracks (the agent's speech A_self and the conversation partner's speech A_other, each encoded by a frozen HuBERT model), a single static portrait image of the agent for appearance, and optionally a style video from which a global motion style vector is extracted. The output is a photorealistic head video of the agent with synchronized lip movements, facial expressions, and rhythmic head pose. The diffusion transformer uses only 4 blocks and 20 DDIM steps at inference, targeting real-time latency suitable for video conferencing.

## Method

INFP is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (Motion-Based Head Imitation) trains a motion encoder to compress verbal and non-verbal communicative behaviors from real conversation videos into a low-dimensional 1-D latent code, using a hybrid facial representation (masked eye/lip pixels plus projected facial contour vertices) to enforce appearance disentanglement; a motion-flow pipeline then warps a 3D appearance feature volume to animate a static portrait. Stage 2 (Audio-Guided Motion Generation) introduces an interactive motion guider containing two learnable memory banks (verbal M_v and non-verbal M_nv, each 64×512) that are queried via cross-attention by the respective audio track, so that whichever track carries richer information naturally dominates the fused motion feature without any explicit role label. A 4-block conditional diffusion transformer then denoises the motion latent codes conditioned on this interactive feature, a StyleGAN2-style modulation vector for global emotion/attitude control, and the previous window's last 10 frames for temporal continuity. Training uses a warm-up on single-sided clips before full multi-turn sampling, with random dropping of style and motion features for robustness.

## Results

On the primary interactive head generation task (DyConv test set), INFP achieves SSIM 0.834, PSNR 31.562, FID 15.727, and SyncScore 7.188, substantially outperforming the DIM baseline (SSIM 0.651, PSNR 20.417, FID 34.361, SyncScore 4.778). Motion diversity is dramatically improved: SID rises from 0.766 (DIM) to 2.613 (INFP), approaching the ground-truth value of 2.891. On the talking head task (HDTF), INFP achieves the best FID (18.582) and SyncScore (6.670) among all compared methods, while on listening head generation (ViCo) it attains the lowest FD (18.63 for expression) and highest SID (4.78 for expression).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DIM [39], the primary interactive head generation baseline, INFP wins on every metric: SSIM improves from 0.651 to 0.834, FID drops from 34.361 to 15.727, and motion diversity (SID) jumps from 0.766 to 2.613, indicating far richer and more varied head movements. For listening head generation on ViCo, INFP outperforms L2L [23] (FD 33.93→18.63, SID 2.77→4.78), RLHG [58] (FD 39.02→18.63, SID 3.62→4.78), and DIM [39] (FD 23.88→18.63, SID 3.71→4.78) on both expression and pose metrics. On talking head generation (HDTF), INFP surpasses SadTalker [52], AniTalker [17], and EchoMimic [2] in FID (18.582 vs. 28.516, 28.778, 20.873), SyncScore (6.670 vs. 6.125, 5.738, 6.251), and identity preservation CSIM (0.886 vs. 0.814, 0.858, 0.863), though it trails EchoMimic slightly on SSIM (0.804 vs. 0.807) and PSNR (28.657 vs. 28.983) due to EchoMimic's Stable Diffusion backbone. The user study (MOS) further confirms INFP's advantage over DIM across naturalness (4.38 vs. 2.71), motion diversity (4.49 vs. 2.14), and audio-visual alignment (4.33 vs. 2.65).

## Relation to Prior Work

INFP departs from the dominant paradigm in dyadic head generation (DIM, Zhou et al.) that splits the model into separate Speaker and Listener Generators requiring explicit role assignment and switching. Instead, it unifies both communicative states into a single conditional diffusion transformer conditioned on dual-track audio, using learnable verbal and non-verbal motion memory banks with cross-attention to dynamically construct mixed speaking-listening motions. The motion latent space design draws on disentangled representation learning principles, while the style modulation layer borrows from StyleGAN2's adaptive instance normalization. The framework also builds on the motion-flow-based rendering pipeline (face encoder → flow warping → face decoder) common in recent talking head work, and leverages HuBERT for audio encoding and AnimateDiff-style temporal attention for cross-window smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified, role-agnostic paradigm addresses a practical gap: real conversational agents must fluidly transition between speaking and listening without external orchestration, which is critical for deployment in video conferencing and interactive avatar systems. The introduction of DyConv (200+ hours of multi-turn dyadic conversations with separated dual-track audio) fills a significant data gap, as prior datasets (ViCo: 1.6h, ViCo-X: 0.4h) are too small or lack multi-turn structure. Key limitations include reliance on audio-only conditioning (no visual or textual context), head-only generation (no upper body), and the fact that the primary interactive baseline (DIM) is not open-sourced, making direct comparison difficult. Future work on multi-modal conditioning and full-body extension is explicitly noted as open.
