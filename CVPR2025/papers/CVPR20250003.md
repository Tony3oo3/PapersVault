## Problem

Despite rapid progress in individual visual foundation models (depth, segmentation, tracking), holistic 4D scene understanding from casual video remains unsolved. The core difficulty is twofold: high-quality 4D ground-truth data is scarce and expensive to collect, and 4D understanding is a coupled problem spanning camera pose estimation, static/dynamic reconstruction, and motion tracking that no single model handles well. The paper asks whether pretrained foundation models can be unified—without retraining—into a coherent 4D modeling pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250003/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video as input and produces camera poses (SE(3) transforms), shared camera intrinsics (focal lengths f_x, f_y), a static 3D point cloud, a temporally varying dynamic 3D point cloud, per-frame dynamic segmentation masks, and dense per-pixel depth maps. Co-Tracker3 is run bi-directionally on a dense 50×50 pixel grid (75×75 for Sintel) with tracklets sampled every 10 frames, and all optimization hyperparameters are fixed across datasets. The entire pipeline runs in roughly 5 minutes for a 50-frame video on an RTX A6000.

## Method

Uni4D formulates 4D reconstruction as a joint energy minimization over camera parameters, static geometry, and dynamic point trajectories, decomposed into a three-stage divide-and-conquer pipeline. Pretrained foundation models—UniDepthV2 (depth), CoTracker3 (dense tracking), and a RAM + GPT-4o + Grounding-SAM + DEVA chain (dynamic segmentation)—provide data-driven cues that are treated as 2D projections of the underlying 4D scene. Stage 1 initializes camera poses by unprojecting depth and minimizing tracklet reprojection error over a 5-frame sliding window; Stage 2 jointly refines poses and static geometry via bundle adjustment with a camera smoothness prior; Stage 3 optimizes dynamic geometry with frozen cameras using non-rigid bundle adjustment plus ARAP and temporal smoothness priors. A final depth-based fusion step densifies the semi-dense point cloud into per-pixel 4D geometry. The entire framework is training-free, requiring no fine-tuning of any integrated model.

## Results

On camera pose estimation (Table 1), Uni4D achieves the best ATE on TUM-dynamics (0.012) and Bonn (0.017), and competitive ATE on Sintel (0.110, matching MonST3R's 0.108). With known intrinsics (Uni4D*), Sintel ATE drops to 0.092 and RPE rot to 0.141, the best across all methods. On video depth (Table 2), Uni4D achieves the best Abs Rel among joint depth-and-pose methods on Sintel (0.216) and Bonn (0.038), closely matching single-frame metric depth models like UniDepth (0.198 on Sintel). Uni4D also significantly improves depth temporal consistency over raw UniDepth predictions (SC 0.043 vs 0.109 on Sintel).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni4D outperforms all joint depth-and-pose baselines (Robust-CVD, CasualSAM, MonST3R) on TUM-dynamics across all three pose metrics, with ATE of 0.012 vs. 0.096/0.036/0.108 respectively. On Sintel, Uni4D matches MonST3R on ATE (0.110 vs. 0.108) but substantially outperforms it on RPE rotation (0.338° vs. 0.729°) and RPE translation (0.032 vs. 0.043); with known intrinsics, Uni4D* achieves the best ATE (0.092) and RPE rot (0.141°). On Bonn, Uni4D achieves the best ATE (0.017) and RPE trans (0.010), though its RPE rot (0.818°) is marginally worse than MonST3R (0.807°) and Robust-CVD (0.803°). For video depth, Uni4D beats CasualSAM (0.292) and MonST3R (0.358) on Sintel Abs Rel (0.216) and Robust-CVD (0.358), while on KITTI MonST3R retains a slight edge (0.085 vs. 0.098 Abs Rel). Training-based pose-only methods (LEAP-VO, DPVO) perform well on synthetic Sintel but degrade significantly on real-world TUM-dynamics and Bonn, whereas Uni4D generalizes consistently across all three datasets without any task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni4D sits at the intersection of classical non-rigid structure-from-motion (which has relied on category-specific rigidity or articulation priors) and the recent wave of visual foundation models for depth, segmentation, and tracking. Unlike prior optimization-based 4D methods (CasualSAM, MonST3R, Robust-CVD) that either fine-tune network weights or optimize specific layers of a pretrained model, Uni4D takes a fully modular, training-free stance: it treats each foundation model's output as a 2D projection of the 4D scene and unifies them through a multi-stage energy-minimization pipeline rooted in bundle adjustment. This repurposes the classical SfM/SLAM optimization machinery for dynamic scenes while sidestepping the need for 4D ground-truth data or any task-specific retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that as new foundation models emerge (better depth estimators, trackers, segmenters), they can be dropped into the Uni4D pipeline without retraining, making the system future-proof in a way that fine-tuned end-to-end methods are not. Limitations include the ~5-minute runtime for 50 frames, reliance on the quality of individual foundation model cues (errors in depth or tracking propagate), and the divide-and-conquer pipeline's sensitivity to initialization quality. Open questions include scalability to longer videos, handling of highly deformable objects beyond what ARAP and smoothness priors can regularize, and whether the three-stage decomposition is truly optimal versus a more tightly coupled joint optimization.
