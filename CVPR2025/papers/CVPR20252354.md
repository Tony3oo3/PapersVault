## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods for CLIP either require access to the original pre-training dataset or only support coarse-grained forgetting of entire classes, leaving a critical gap for fine-grained, subgroup-level unlearning. This paper tackles the challenge of selectively forgetting specific image subgroups within a class (e.g., one breed of monkey) without access to pre-trained data, while preserving the model's zero-shot generalization across unrelated datasets and semantically similar subgroups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252354/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small forgetting set of 224×224 images paired with coarse text labels (superclass names), a manually constructed retaining set with similar distribution to the forgetting set, and a pre-trained CLIP model (RN50 or RN101). No access to the original LAION-5B pre-training data is required. The output is a single unlearned CLIP model whose image-text cosine similarity drops below a threshold on the target subgroup while maintaining near-original zero-shot accuracy on retain subgroups and diverse external benchmarks (CIFAR-10, Food-101, STL-10, ObjectNet).

## Method

The proposed three-stage pipeline first computes a relative Fisher information score (ratio of second-order parameter sensitivity on the forgetting set to that on the retaining set) to identify layers critical for the target subgroup but less important for retained knowledge, then applies LoRA fine-tuning only on those layers to minimize image-text cosine similarity on the forgetting set. In the reminding stage, perturbations are optimized on the retaining images to align their per-layer feature statistics (mean and variance) with the BatchNorm parameters of the original CLIP, effectively matching the pre-training distribution without accessing it; the model is then fine-tuned on this aligned retaining set using EMA to limit parameter drift. Finally, a model souping step linearly interpolates the unlearned weights with the original weights, selecting the optimal mixing coefficient α via a small calibration set to fully restore zero-shot classification accuracy while preserving the targeted forgetting.

## Results

On the in-domain ImageNet-1K subgroup forgetting task (forgetting 'marmoset' within 'monkey'), the proposed method achieves a Score of 91.0 (RN50) and 92.9 (RN101), meaning it retains ~91–93% of the original zero-shot accuracy ratio across all evaluated datasets while reducing target-subgroup accuracy to 0.0%. The best baseline (EMMN) achieves only a Score of 55.6 (RN50), and Fisher/LIP methods collapse to near-random performance (Scores ~19). On the cross-domain CIFAR-10 'airplane' forgetting task, the method retains 88.9% Score (RN50) versus 51.1% for the best baseline (CLIP-LIP), and on style forgetting (sketch-style cars) it achieves 87.2% Score versus 83.2% for GA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all six baselines on the comprehensive Score metric across every experimental setting. On the primary in-domain ImageNet task (RN50, marmoset), the method achieves a Score of 91.0 versus 55.6 for EMMN (the strongest baseline), 51.6 for FT, 45.3 for GA, 33.2 for CLIP-LIP, and only ~19 for Fisher and LIP, which essentially destroy the model's zero-shot capability. Fisher and LIP fail catastrophically because their regularization-based unlearning is incompatible with CLIP's contrastive architecture, reducing all-dataset accuracy to near-random levels. EMMN and FT partially retain the target class's sibling subgroups but suffer severe cross-domain forgetting (CIFAR drops from 70.4% to 13.1–13.5%). CLIP-LIP, the only baseline designed specifically for CLIP, still loses over 70% of zero-shot performance on Food and ObjectNet. The proposed method's advantage stems from its three-stage pipeline: relative Fisher information for selective layer fine-tuning, distribution-aligned reminding via BN statistics, and model souping for zero-shot recovery—collectively preserving 88–100% of original accuracy on non-target datasets while driving target-subgroup accuracy to 0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the machine unlearning literature (FT, GA, Fisher, LIP, EMMN) and CLIP-specific unlearning (CLIP-LIP, Safe-CLIP) to a finer granularity: forgetting a *subgroup* within a class rather than an entire class or concept, and without access to the pre-training dataset. Methodologically, it repurposes several known techniques in a novel pipeline—relative Fisher information (a ratio of forgetting-to-retaining sensitivity) for layer selection, LoRA for targeted fine-tuning, BN-statistic-based distribution alignment borrowed from dataset condensation, EMA for parameter stabilization, and model souping for zero-shot restoration. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit treatment of distributional disparity between the small forgetting/retaining sets and the unseen pre-training distribution, which the authors argue is the root cause of catastrophic over-forgetting in CLIP unlearning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: CLIP models trained on LAION-5B inherit harmful, copyrighted, or biased content, and selective removal of specific subgroups (e.g., a particular artist's style, a specific brand) is a realistic deployment requirement that whole-class unlearning cannot address. The finding that standard Fisher and LIP unlearning methods collapse to near-zero accuracy when naively adapted to CLIP highlights a genuine gap in the field. Limitations include reliance on manually constructed retaining sets, testing only on ResNet-50/101 backbones (no ViT), and the need for a small calibration set for model merging; open questions include whether the approach scales to larger ViT-based CLIP variants, whether subgroup boundaries can be defined automatically, and whether the pipeline generalizes to other foundation models such as LLMs or video-language models.
