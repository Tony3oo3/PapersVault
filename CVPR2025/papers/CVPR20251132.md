## Problem

Vision transformer-based WSSS methods suffer from CAM over-expansion, where high activation values incorrectly propagate from target objects into background regions. This occurs because ViT lacks the pixel-level local inductive bias of CNNs, and image-level weak supervision is insufficient to learn fine-grained spatial boundaries, causing unreliable pseudo-labels that degrade segmentation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251132/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a one-hot image-level class label as input, patchified into h×w tokens by a ViT-B/16 backbone. It produces a pixel-level segmentation mask (h×w per class) and a class activation map. Notably, the mask generation branch assigns each patch a local mask of size h×w constrained to a circular region of radius r around that patch's center, and the final pseudo-label is computed as the average of the initial CAM and the progressively refined mask.

## Method

PCRE comprises two modules: Confidence Region Mask Expansion (CRME) and Class-Prototype Enhancement (CPE). CRME computes a joint patch-class confidence score via Hadamard product of class-dimension and patch-dimension softmax scores, then progressively expands a mask set starting from the highest-confidence patch, iteratively adding candidate masks whose Reference Overlap Ratio (ROR) with the current set falls between τ_low and τ_high while discarding redundant high-overlap masks. CPE introduces C learnable class tokens whose similarity to patch features is supervised by intermediate-layer CAMs, and maintains dataset-level class prototypes via EMA that preserve early-stage discriminative local features; the prototype-based similarity map (CAM_pro) serves as supervision for the CRME refined mask. The final pseudo-label combines the initial CAM and refined mask to supervise a segmentation head, with the total loss weighting confidence, refinement, CPE alignment, and classification terms.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, PCRE achieves 75.5% mIoU on the validation set and 75.9% mIoU on the test set, setting new state-of-the-art among both single-stage and multi-stage WSSS methods. On MS COCO 2014 validation, the method reaches 47.2% mIoU, surpassing the previous best single-stage method DIAL by 2.8% and the best multi-stage method PCSS by 1.5%. In terms of CAM pseudo-label quality (Table 1), PCRE achieves 77.6% mIoU on the VOC train set, outperforming DuPL by 2.2% on the validation set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCRE outperforms all 12 compared baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest single-stage competitor DIAL (ECCV 24, which uses image+language supervision), PCRE gains 1.0% mIoU on VOC Val/Test and 2.8% on COCO Val, while using only image-level labels. Against the strongest multi-stage method PCSS (ECCV 24), PCRE improves by 2.3% on VOC Val and 1.5% on COCO Val. Among ViT-based one-stage methods, PCRE surpasses DuPL (CVPR 24) by 2.2% on VOC Val and 2.6% on COCO Val, and ToCo (CVPR 23) by 4.4% on VOC Val and 4.9% on COCO Val. The closest competitor overall is CTI (CVPR 24, multi-stage, ResNet38), which PCRE exceeds by 1.4% on VOC Val and 1.7% on COCO Val. Notably, PCRE achieves the highest performance ratio (91.7%) relative to its fully-supervised ViT-B/16 counterpart (82.3% mIoU), tying with CTI but surpassing it in absolute mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViT-based WSSS line of work initiated by ToCo, DuPL, and DIAL, which use class tokens and contrastive or cross-modal alignment to improve CAM quality. It departs from these methods by identifying and directly targeting the 'over-expansion' failure mode—where high CAM activations bleed from target objects into visually similar backgrounds—through a progressive, confidence-gated mask expansion strategy rather than relying solely on global attention or contrastive objectives. The progressive expansion concept is borrowed from unrelated domains (domain generalization, open-world learning, NeRF), and the mask generation leverages a lightweight MaskFormer decoder, while the class-prototype enhancement draws on EMA-based prototype learning common in clustering and contrastive learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-motivated solution to a recognized weakness of ViT-based WSSS (over-expansion), achieving new SOTA on both VOC and COCO with a single-stage pipeline. The progressive expansion mechanism is modular and could potentially be adapted to other weak supervision settings (scribbles, points) or to instance segmentation. However, the method adds considerable architectural complexity (dual-branch confidence scoring, iterative mask selection with ROR thresholds, EMA prototypes, CRF post-processing), and the gains over the strongest single-stage baseline (DIAL) are modest (~1% on VOC, ~2.8% on COCO), leaving open whether the added complexity is justified in practice. A key open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond the two standard benchmarks and whether the progressive expansion can be made more computationally efficient at inference time.
