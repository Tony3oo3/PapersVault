## Problem

Existing motion transfer methods for video generation are predominantly UNet-based and rely on separate temporal and spatial attention modules, failing to exploit the joint spatio-temporal attention inherent to Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). This limits the quality of extracted motion signals and prevents zero-shot transfer to new prompts. The paper addresses this by proposing the first motion transfer method specifically designed for DiT architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (encoded to latent space via a pre-trained autoencoder) and a free-form text prompt as input, producing a 21-frame synthesized video that reproduces the reference video's motion while rendering entirely new content described by the prompt. The reference video is processed at denoising step t=0 with an empty prompt to extract motion features, avoiding expensive DDIM inversion. Generation uses CogVideoX (2B or 5B parameters) with 50 denoising steps, and optimization is applied only during the first 20% of timesteps for 5 steps per denoising step.

## Method

DiTFlow extracts an Attention Motion Flow (AMF) by computing cross-frame attention maps between all frame pairs from a specific DiT block's self-attention (averaged over heads), then applying argmax to assign each spatial patch in frame i to its most-attended patch in frame j, yielding patch-wise 2D displacement vectors. During generation, the method optimizes either the latent z_t or the positional embeddings ρ_t by backpropagating an L2 loss between the reference AMF and a soft (gradient-preserving) AMF computed from the current denoising step. The key insight is that DiT's global spatio-temporal attention naturally encodes patch-level motion relationships, unlike UNet's separable temporal attention. Optimizing positional embeddings rather than latents enables zero-shot transfer: the learned ρ encodes motion independent of content, so it can be reused with arbitrary new prompts without re-optimization, a capability unavailable to UNet-based approaches.

## Results

On the DAVIS benchmark (50 videos × 3 prompt types), DiTFlow achieves a Motion Fidelity (MF) of 0.785 and Image Quality (IQ) of 0.319 on CogVideoX-5B, and MF 0.726 / IQ 0.317 on CogVideoX-2B, outperforming all baselines. The best baseline, SMM, reaches MF 0.766 (5B) and 0.688 (2B), while MOFT scores MF 0.726 (5B) and 0.504 (2B). DiTFlow's advantage is most pronounced on Subject prompts (changing the subject while keeping the background), where SMM drops to MF 0.741 on 5B but DiTFlow maintains 0.775. Human evaluation (66 preferences, 30 raters) confirms DiTFlow significantly outperforms both SMM and MOFT on both motion adherence and prompt adherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTFlow outperforms all three optimization-based baselines on Motion Fidelity across both model sizes. Against SMM (Yatim et al., CVPR 2024), the closest competitor, DiTFlow gains +0.019 MF on CogVideoX-5B (0.785 vs 0.766) and +0.038 on CogVideoX-2B (0.726 vs 0.688); the gap widens on Subject prompts where SMM's spatial-averaging approach entangles with reference content (0.741 vs DiTFlow's 0.775 on 5B). Against MOFT (Xiao et al., 2024), DiTFlow leads by +0.059 MF on 5B (0.785 vs 0.726) and a substantial +0.222 on 2B (0.726 vs 0.504), with MOFT particularly struggling to assign motion to the correct scene elements. Against MotionClone (Ling et al., 2024), adapted to operate on the temporal axis of DiT attention, DiTFlow achieves +0.151 MF on 5B (0.785 vs 0.634), attributed to DiTFlow guiding the evolution of cross-frame attention relationships rather than static attention values. IQ scores remain within 0.001–0.015 across all methods, confirming that the DiT backbone dominates image quality while the guidance strategy primarily affects motion fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiTFlow extends the line of optimization-based, training-free motion transfer methods (SMM, MOFT, MotionClone) by reformulating the motion signal extraction specifically for Diffusion Transformers, which process spatio-temporal information jointly through full attention rather than separate temporal and spatial modules. The key departure is the Attention Motion Flow (AMF), which derives patch-wise displacement vectors from cross-frame self-attention in DiTs, contrasting with SMM's spatially-averaged features and MOFT's per-location temporal deviations. The zero-shot capability via positional embedding optimization is a novel extension enabled by the DiT architecture's explicit positional encodings, which have no direct analogue in UNet-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiTFlow provides the first motion transfer method designed for the increasingly dominant DiT video generation architecture, and the positional-embedding optimization trick offers a practical path to amortizing motion transfer across multiple prompts without per-video optimization. However, the quantitative gains over the best baseline (SMM) are modest (0.785 vs 0.766 MF on CogVideoX-5B), and evaluation is limited to 21-frame videos on a single dataset (DAVIS) with one backbone family (CogVideoX). Open questions include whether AMF generalizes to longer videos, other DiT architectures, and more complex multi-object motion, and whether the zero-shot positional-embedding approach degrades with increasingly dissimilar prompts.
