## Problem

Slicing-based high-resolution LVLMs divide images into fixed-size patches for a pre-trained vision encoder, but this fragments objects at slice boundaries, disrupting spatial context and causing systematic misclassification (e.g., a tomato labeled as a watermelon). Existing token-compression samplers like Q-Former further degrade performance by discarding positional information and requiring substantial training data. The paper identifies this 'context fragmentation' as an under-addressed failure mode and quantifies it with a new benchmark.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an arbitrary-resolution RGB image (up to 1344×1344) and a text prompt. The image is automatically sliced into a grid of m×n patches (up to 16 slices for CLIP-ViT-224px, 9 for InternViT-448px) whose count adapts to the image's aspect ratio, plus a single low-resolution (224×224) global view. The visual token sequence fed to the LLM interleaves the compressed low-resolution tokens with compressed high-resolution slice tokens, separated by three types of learnable separator tokens (between low-res and high-res, between slices, and at row boundaries). Output is a free-form text response.

## Method

HiRes-LLaVA introduces two lightweight modules inserted into a standard CLIP-ViT or InternViT encoder. The SliceRestore Adapter (SRA), placed in the last four ViT blocks, merges all slice features back into the full spatial grid, applies a parallel local fusion (single 3×3 depth-wise convolution to exchange edge details across slice boundaries) and a global fusion (self-attention on a downsampled coarse view, then bilinear upsampling), then re-slices the enhanced feature—restoring cross-patch context at near-linear cost. The Self-Mining Sampler (SMS) replaces Q-Former by average-pooling the 2D token grid with an S×S kernel to produce compressed queries that attend only to their local S×S neighborhood via cross-attention, preserving positional structure and reducing training data needs by ~40%. Training follows a three-stage protocol (alignment → full fine-tuning → instruction tuning with SRA loaded at LR 2e-4) using LoRA on the LLM, with a maximum of 16 slices and batch size 256.

## Results

On 9 public benchmarks, HiRes-LLaVA (Llama3.1-8B, 1344×1344 max resolution) achieves 74.2 on VQA-Text, 77.4 on ChartQA, 84.9 on DocVQA, 55.7 on InfoVQA, 90.3 on ScienceQA, 74.9 on AI2D, 2213 on MME, 75.7 on MMBench, and 53.5 on MM-Vet, surpassing all compared baselines on document and science tasks. On the newly introduced EntityGrid-QA benchmark, HiRes-LLaVA achieves 70.20 mean accuracy with a Precision Discrepancy (PD₁) of 94.34%, closely matching the non-slicing LLaVA-1.5 (PD₁ = 94.50%) while outperforming slicing-based LLaVA-NeXT (PD₁ = 88.92%) and IXC-4KHD (PD₁ = 81.69%) by ~5–13 percentage points on edge-position accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiRes-LLaVA outperforms the strongest document-specialized model, mPLUG-DocOwl2, by 7.5 points on VQA-Text (74.2 vs 66.7), 7.4 on ChartQA (77.4 vs 70.0), 4.2 on DocVQA (84.9 vs 80.7), and 9.3 on InfoVQA (55.7 vs 46.4). Against the strongest general high-resolution model, Cambrian-1-13B (which uses 4 vision encoders and 7M SFT data), HiRes-LLaVA (single encoder, 8B LLM, ~50% less training data) leads on VQA-Text (74.2 vs 72.8), ChartQA (77.4 vs 73.8), DocVQA (84.9 vs 76.8), ScienceQA (90.3 vs 79.3), AI2D (74.9 vs 73.6), and MME (2213 vs 1610), while tying on MMBench (75.7 vs 75.7) and slightly trailing on MMBench vs Cambrian-1-8B (75.7 vs 75.9). On EntityGrid-QA, HiRes-LLaVA achieves 70.20 mean accuracy, surpassing LLaVA-NeXT (65.22) by 5.0 points and IXC-4KHD (63.78) by 6.4 points; critically, its edge-position accuracy (68.4%) is 6.6 points above LLaVA-NeXT (61.8%) and 10.4 points above IXC-4KHD (58.0%), with a PD₁ of 94.34% nearly matching the non-slicing LLaVA-1.5 (94.50%) and far exceeding IXC-4KHD (81.69%). On MM-Vet, HiRes-LLaVA (53.5) leads LLaVA-NeXT-13B (48.4) and mPLUG-Owl3 (40.1). The only marginal loss is MMBench vs Cambrian-1-8B (75.7 vs 75.9), a 0.2-point difference within noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (vision encoder + projector + LLM) to handle high-resolution inputs via slicing, but explicitly departs from the dominant slicing-based paradigm (LLaVA-NeXT, Monkey, TextMonkey) by treating the resulting context fragmentation as a first-class problem to solve rather than an accepted trade-off. The SliceRestore Adapter introduces a lightweight merge-capture-slice mechanism (depth-wise convolution plus downsampled self-attention) that is orthogonal to and composable with existing vision encoders, while the Self-Mining Sampler replaces Q-Former-style fixed learnable queries with self-referential cross-attention that preserves positional information. The work also carves out a new evaluation axis—fragmentation sensitivity—through the EntityGrid-QA benchmark, which no prior high-resolution LVLM paper had systematically measured.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fragmentation problem is a practical bottleneck for the most widely adopted strategy (sliding-window slicing) to bring high-resolution inputs into LVLMs, and the ~12% accuracy gap on edge-positioned entities quantifies a failure mode that document and OCR-heavy applications will encounter in production. The EntityGrid-QA benchmark, while synthetic, provides a controlled and reproducible way to isolate this specific capability, filling a gap that existing document VQA benchmarks cannot address. Open questions include whether the SRA's benefits transfer to real-world scenes with irregular object boundaries (as opposed to grid-aligned synthetic entities), whether the approach scales to video or extremely high resolutions beyond 1344×1344, and whether the self-mining sampler's data-efficiency advantage persists at larger model scales.
