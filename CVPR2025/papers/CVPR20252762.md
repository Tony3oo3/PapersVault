## Problem

Traditional amodal appearance completion methods are constrained to closed sets of predefined object categories and require extensive training data, limiting their applicability to diverse, open-world scenes. This paper addresses the gap by introducing reasoning amodal completion, where the target object is specified via flexible natural language queries—ranging from concrete terms to abstract, context-dependent descriptions—without any category restrictions or additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252762/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image and a free-form natural language text query (e.g., "polar bear" or "the mammal in this image") as input. It produces a transparent RGBA amodal completion where the alpha channel encodes the full amodal segmentation mask, making the output directly layerable for downstream tasks like 3D reconstruction, AR compositing, and image editing. The inpainting is limited to a maximum of three iterations per object for computational efficiency.

## Method

The approach is a training-free pipeline that chains four pre-trained models: LISA interprets the text query to produce a visible object mask; Grounded SAM (open-set detector + Segment Anything) segments all nameable objects, while morphological erosion/dilation partitions residual unsegmented regions into background segments; InstaOrderNet performs pairwise occlusion ordering to build the occluder mask, with boundary-aware dilation for edge-touching objects. A CLIP-based similarity step selects the optimal inpainting prompt by matching the visible target against auto-generated image tags and the query. Finally, Stable Diffusion v2 performs iterative masked inpainting with adaptive termination (occluder mask L1-stability or iteration cap), followed by alpha blending to produce the seamless RGBA output.

## Results

On the overall evaluation dataset (2,379 images, 553 classes), the proposed method achieves 41.86% human preference, substantially outperforming Pix2gestalt (27.95%), PD-MC (14.41%), and PD w/o MC (15.78%). In quantitative metrics, it attains the best CLIP score (28.181), lowest LPIPS (0.320), highest VGG16 feature similarity (0.646), SSIM (0.731), and PSNR (12.880) across all four data sources. The method leads on every metric in every individual dataset (VG, COCO-A, Free Image, LAION), with the largest margins over Pix2gestalt in LPIPS (0.320 vs. 0.442) and PSNR (12.880 vs. 11.178).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Pix2gestalt (synthetic-data-trained amodal segmentation), PD-MC (progressive mixed-context diffusion with predefined categories), and PD w/o MC (PD-MC without the mixed-context component). The proposed method wins on every metric against all three baselines across all four data sources. Against the strongest baseline Pix2gestalt, the gains are most pronounced in perceptual quality: LPIPS improves from 0.442 to 0.320 (27.6% reduction), PSNR from 11.178 to 12.880, and human preference from 27.95% to 41.86% (a 13.9-point margin). Against PD-MC, the advantage is even larger in structural metrics (SSIM 0.731 vs. 0.413, PSNR 12.880 vs. 7.081), reflecting that category-constrained diffusion struggles with open-world objects. In the human preference study, when all three evaluators agreed (3/3 consensus), the proposed method was selected 57% of the time versus 28% for Pix2gestalt, indicating a clear perceptual quality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper assembles a training-free pipeline for amodal completion by chaining existing components—LISA for language-grounded segmentation, Grounding DINO + SAM for open-set object detection, InstaOrderNet for occlusion ordering, and Stable Diffusion v2 for iterative inpainting—into a unified system that accepts free-form text queries. It departs from prior amodal completion methods (Pix2gestalt, PD-MC, CSDNet) that require category-specific training or synthetic occlusion data, and from vision-language models (CogVLM, VisionLLM) that handle visible objects but not occluded regions. The key conceptual contribution is framing 'reasoning amodal completion' as a task where the system must infer the full appearance of an object from a potentially abstract query (e.g., 'the mammal in this image') without any predefined category set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for category-specific training, this framework makes amodal completion applicable to arbitrary real-world objects, which is a practical prerequisite for AR, 3D reconstruction, and image editing pipelines that must handle open-world scenes. The RGBA output format and the newly collected 2,379-image, 553-class evaluation dataset provide useful infrastructure for the community. However, the approach inherits artifacts from its diffusion-based inpainting backbone (e.g., pose mismatches in animal completions), and the absence of ground-truth amodal appearance limits quantitative evaluation to proxy metrics. Open questions include developing evaluation protocols for scenarios where true occluded appearance is unknowable and reducing reliance on the iterative inpainting loop for efficiency.
