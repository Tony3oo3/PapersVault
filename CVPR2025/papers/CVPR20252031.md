## Problem

Existing physics-aware dynamic 3D reconstruction methods rely on heavy per-scene optimization with differentiable MPM simulators, requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning and roughly 1.5 hours of optimization per scene, while their grid-based representations and symplectic integrators limit expressiveness for complex deformations. No prior approach achieves accurate, generalizable, and efficient joint recovery of appearance, geometry, and physical properties from video in a feed-forward manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252031/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view video sequences (12 views at 448×448 for synthetic GSO objects, 4 posed cameras for real-world captures), where the first 16 frames serve as observation and the remaining 8 as future-state prediction targets. A single front-view video clip feeds the VideoMAE-based physical property predictor, while the first multi-view frames are processed by LGM for geometry and appearance. The output is a simulation-ready model comprising 3D Gaussians (canonical geometry with view-dependent appearance), scalar physical parameters (Young's modulus E ∈ [10⁴, 10⁶], Poisson's ratio ν ∈ [0.2, 0.5]), and per-point LBS deformation weights that together drive a mesh-free reduced-order simulation.

## Method

Vid2Sim employs a two-stage pipeline. Stage I is a generalizable feed-forward model: a VideoMAE backbone extracts video features decoded by small MLP regression heads that predict E, ν, and—via a HyperNetwork that outputs only the final linear layer weights of an LBS MLP—the per-point skinning weights; simultaneously, LGM reconstructs geometry and appearance as 3D Gaussians from multi-view frames. Stage II performs a lightweight (~15 min) joint optimization of 3D Gaussians, LBS weights, physical parameters, and a Neural Jacobian module under a rendering loss over randomly sampled 4-frame windows. The Neural Jacobian replaces explicit auto-differentiation of the deformation gradient with a neural network trained via a data-free L1 loss, dramatically reducing per-iteration cost. Simulation uses a mesh-free reduced-order formulation with m handles (full affine transformations) and n points deformed via LBS, advanced by an implicit Euler solver with Newton's method and line search, operating on a small set of cubature points sampled by FPS.

## Results

On the GSO synthetic dataset (12 objects, 12 views, 24 frames), Vid2Sim achieves a mean PSNR of 30.17, SSIM of 0.961, and FoVVDP of 8.363 for dynamic reconstruction, substantially outperforming all baselines. For future state prediction (8 frames), Vid2Sim reaches PSNR 25.07, SSIM 0.945, and FoVVDP 7.770. In terms of efficiency, the full pipeline completes in 15 minutes (1.44 s/iteration) versus 54–120 minutes for baselines, a 3.6×–8× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vid2Sim decisively outperforms all three baselines on every metric. On PSNR, Vid2Sim (30.17) exceeds PAC-NeRF (22.06) by ~8 dB, Spring-Gaus (20.91) by ~9 dB, and GIC (21.01) by ~9 dB. On SSIM, Vid2Sim (0.961) leads GIC (0.934), PAC-NeRF (0.924), and Spring-Gaus (0.920) by 0.027–0.041. On the perceptual FoVVDP metric, Vid2Sim (8.363) surpasses PAC-NeRF (6.674), GIC (6.544), and Spring-Gaus (6.410) by 1.8–2.0 points. The efficiency gap is equally striking: Vid2Sim's 15-minute total training time is 3.6× faster than Spring-Gaus (54 min), 5.6× faster than PAC-NeRF (84 min), and 8× faster than GIC (120 min), with per-iteration cost of 1.44 s versus 8.08–37.33 s for baselines. On the real-world dataset (orange, bird, cup), Vid2Sim also beats Spring-Gaus on PSNR (27.12 vs 26.05), SSIM (0.983 vs 0.982), and FoVVDP (7.875 vs 7.773).

## Relation to Prior Work

Vid2Sim builds directly on Simplicits [38] for its mesh-free, reduced-order elastic simulation via Linear Blend Skinning, and on HyperNetworks [16] to predict LBS MLP weights in a feed-forward manner. It departs from the dominant line of physics-aware dynamic reconstruction (PAC-NeRF, GIC, Spring-Gaus) by replacing heavy per-scene MPM-based differentiable simulation with a two-stage pipeline: a VideoMAE-based feed-forward predictor for generalizable initial estimates of physical parameters, coupled with LGM for geometry/appearance, followed by a lightweight refinement using an implicit Euler solver and a novel Neural Jacobian module. This positions the work at the intersection of large-scale 3D reconstruction models and differentiable simulation, shifting the paradigm from per-scene optimization to amortized prediction plus short fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a generalizable, feed-forward approach can match or exceed per-scene optimization methods in both accuracy and speed (15 min vs. 84–120 min), which is practically significant for applications requiring rapid system identification across many scenes. The Neural Jacobian and reduced-order LBS simulation offer reusable components for efficient differentiable simulation beyond this specific task. However, the method is currently limited to elastic (Neo-Hookean) materials and cannot handle fluids or inelastic dynamics, and the two-branch Stage I architecture (separate VideoMAE and LGM pipelines) remains a design limitation that the authors acknowledge as a direction for future unified models.
