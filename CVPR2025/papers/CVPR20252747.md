## Problem

Existing blurry video frame interpolation methods rely on linear or quadratic motion models that capture only horizontal and vertical velocity variations, overlooking depth changes (object scale changes) caused by 3D motion. The quadratic theory lacks a linearly independent vector to represent depth, and optical flow inherently cannot distinguish a nearby slow object from a faraway fast one, causing interpolation failures in scenes with significant depth variation such as vehicle motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252747/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of temporally adjacent blurry frames B_i and B_{i+η} (e.g., 640×352 on Adobe) along with an interpolation time index t, and produces a sharp intermediate frame I_t. Internally, each frame is converted into a 3D camera-space point map [α,β,γ] ∈ R^{H×W×3}, and the motion between point maps is represented as a 3D scene flow SF_{i→t} ∈ R^{H×W×3} rather than a 2D optical flow. Training uses 256×256 crops with a batch size of 4 over 900 epochs on 4× NVIDIA 3090ti GPUs.

## Method

DC-BVFI reformulates frame interpolation under differential curves theory, where each pixel is a slight 3D curve segment with a unit basis [α,β,γ] and motion parameters (curvature κ, torsion τ) that jointly encode spatial position and 3D displacement. UBNet converts blurry 2D frames into explicit 3D camera-space point maps using a ViT-Base backbone: a Discriminative Deblur (DD) strategy replaces naive frame stacking to avoid misalignment, and a general 3D reconstruction paradigm (MoGe) estimates camera intrinsics, depth maps, and point maps. MPNet upgrades 2D optical flow from IFNet into camera-space scene flow via SF = (F|D) × K⁻¹, where D is the depth difference between frames, then refines the coarse scene flow with Local Self-Attention and Residual Blocks. The warped point map is projected back to 2D via the camera intrinsics and grid sampling to synthesize the intermediate frame, trained end-to-end with an MSE loss against ground-truth sharp frames.

## Results

On the Adobe dataset, DC-BVFI achieves an average PSNR of 34.14 dB and SSIM of 0.942, outperforming the best prior method IQ-VFI (33.79 dB) by 0.35 dB. On the RBI dataset, which contains challenging depth-varying scenes such as vehicle motion, DC-BVFI reaches 31.03 dB average PSNR, a 1.12 dB improvement over IQ-VFI (29.91 dB). Cross-dataset generalization is confirmed on GoPro (32.08 dB vs. 31.28 dB for IQ-VFI) and YouTube (33.35 dB vs. 32.94 dB for IQ-VFI), where DC-BVFI consistently leads all 12 baselines in both PSNR and SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-BVFI outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is IQ-VFI [11] (CVPR 2024), which uses implicit quadratic motion estimation; DC-BVFI surpasses it by 0.35 dB on Adobe, 1.12 dB on RBI, 0.41 dB on YouTube, and 0.80 dB on GoPro in average PSNR. DeMFI [36] (ECCV 2022) and BiT [58] (CVPR 2023) are the next strongest baselines, trailing DC-BVFI by 0.35–1.68 dB depending on the dataset. Quadratic-flow methods (QVI [51], EQVI [28], UTI-VFI [56]) consistently underperform, with QVI lagging by over 5 dB on RBI, confirming the paper's claim that 2D quadratic models fail to capture depth-induced size changes. The gap is largest on RBI (1.12 dB over IQ-VFI), which contains many depth-varying vehicle-motion scenes, and on GoPro (0.80 dB), which features high-magnitude dynamic scenes—both settings where depth variation is most pronounced. On the deblurring sub-task specifically, DC-BVFI leads by 0.34 dB on Adobe and 1.07 dB on RBI over IQ-VFI, indicating that the 3D point-map representation also improves blur recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the blurry video frame interpolation (BVFI) line of work by departing from the 2D optical-flow paradigm (linear and quadratic methods) and reformulating interpolation in 3D camera space. It builds on IFNet for intermediate optical flow estimation and MoGe for monocular geometry estimation (camera intrinsics, depth, and point maps), upgrading 2D flow to 3D scene flow via a simple depth-augmented back-projection. The conceptual departure is the differential curves framing, which motivates why quadratic velocity models fail when objects change apparent size due to depth changes, though the actual computation reduces to point-map warping by scene flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of existing BVFI methods (depth-induced scale changes during fast motion) and shows that explicit 3D modeling yields a meaningful ~2 dB gain over 2D baselines in the ablation. However, the approach inherits dependencies on IFNet and MoGe, raising questions about error propagation and real-time feasibility. Open questions include whether the differential curves formalism provides practical advantages beyond standard scene-flow warping, how the method behaves under large camera motion or occlusion, and whether the MiD evaluation (which uses estimated depth as ground truth) truly validates the depth-awareness claim.
