## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary detectors that co-train with language tasks (e.g., CapDet, DetCLIPv3) rely on short, coarse region-level captions such as class names or hierarchical labels, which are individual, lack inter-object relations, and provide limited information about the full scene. The paper asks whether long, detailed image-level captions generated by a large language model can provide richer supervision to build stronger vision-language representations and improve open-vocabulary detection, particularly on rare classes.

## Input / Output

At inference, LLMDet takes an RGB image and free-form text class labels as input and outputs bounding boxes with class labels, identical in cost to the baseline MM-Grounding-DINO since the LLM is discarded. During training, the detector's p4 (27×27) and p5 (20×20) feature maps are concatenated as visual tokens fed to the LLM, alongside conversation-formatted prompts; the LLM generates both a long image-level caption (~115 words) and short region-level phrases (≤40 tokens each, up to 16 regions per image). The training data is the newly constructed GroundingCap-1M dataset of 1.12M quadruples (image, grounding text, bounding boxes, detailed caption).

## Method

LLMDet appends a large language model (LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B) to a pretrained DETR-based open-vocabulary detector (MM-Grounding-DINO) and trains them jointly under a combined objective of grounding loss (word-region alignment + box regression) and two language-modeling losses for image-level and region-level caption generation. A two-step pipeline is used: first, a lightweight projector is trained to map the detector's feature maps into the LLM's input space while freezing both the detector and LLM; then all components are finetuned end-to-end. For region-level generation, cross-attention layers are inserted into the LLM so that single object queries can gather contextual information from the full feature maps, enabling precise entity-to-region mapping that image-level generation alone cannot achieve. The LLM is forwarded twice per image (once for image-level, once for region-level) to save memory, and is entirely discarded at inference time, incurring no additional cost.

## Results

On LVIS minival, LLMDet achieves 44.7/48.3/51.1 AP with Swin-T/B/L backbones, outperforming the direct baseline MM-GDINO by 3.3%/3.8%/14.3% AP and 3.1%/3.3%/17.0% AP_r. On LVIS val, LLMDet (Swin-L) reaches 42.0 AP, surpassing DetCLIPv3 (Swin-L) at 41.4 AP while using substantially less training data (~1M vs. 50M+ samples). On ODinW35, LLMDet (Swin-T) achieves 23.8 AP, the highest among all compared methods, and on COCO-O it reaches 36.1 AP versus 34.0 for MM-GDINO, demonstrating stronger domain-shift robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct baseline MM-GDINO [60], LLMDet consistently wins across all backbone sizes: +3.3% AP (Swin-T), +3.8% AP (Swin-B), and +14.3% AP (Swin-L) on LVIS minival, with the Swin-L gap driven by MM-GDINO's weaker pretraining data lacking V3Det. Against the strongest prior method DetCLIPv3 [54], LLMDet (Swin-T, 44.7 AP) trails by 2.3% AP but surpasses it on AP_f (50.7 vs. 46.7); at the Swin-L scale, LLMDet (51.1 AP) clearly outperforms DetCLIPv3 (48.8 AP) while using ~50× less training data. LLMDet also outperforms Grounding-DINO [33] by 17.3% AP, GLIP [25] by 18.7% AP, T-Rex2 [17] by 1.9% AP, and OV-DINO [45] by 4.6% AP on LVIS minival (Swin-T). On ODinW35, LLMDet (23.8 AP) edges out MM-GDINO (23.1) and Grounding-DINO (22.7), and on COCO-O it achieves 36.1 AP versus 34.0 for MM-GDINO and 22.5 for DINO [58], confirming improved distribution-shift robustness. On referring expression comprehension (RefCOCO val), LLMDet reaches 69.5% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing MM-GDINO (66.0%) and Grounding-DINO (50.8%), though it is slightly below MM-GDINO on RefCOCOg val (72.1 vs. 72.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

LLMDet extends the multi-task training paradigm for open-vocabulary detection established by GLIPv2 (masked language modeling), CapDet, and DetCLIPv3 (dense region-level captioning). The key departure is replacing short, per-object region captions with long, detailed image-level captions generated by a large language model (Qwen2-VL-72b), arguing that whole-image descriptions capture inter-object relations, background context, and fine-grained attributes that region-level phrases miss. Architecturally, it builds directly on MM_Grounding_DINO as the detector backbone and appends a small LLM (LLaVA-OneVision-0.5b) via a projector alignment step, introducing cross-attention layers specifically for region-level generation to compensate for the limited visual information in a single object query.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLM supervision—both as a data generator and as a co-training partner providing gradient signals—can substantially improve open-vocabulary detectors, with the largest gains (14.3% AP) on rare classes where fine-grained visual representations matter most. The mutual-benefit finding (an improved detector can in turn build a stronger large multimodal model) suggests a virtuous cycle between vision and language foundation models. Limitations include reliance on a 72B-parameter model for caption generation, a relatively small 1M training set compared to competitors using 50M+ data, and the approach being validated primarily on one detector architecture (DETR-based). Open questions include whether the technique transfers to non-DETR detectors, whether smaller caption-generation models suffice, and whether the gains persist at much larger training scales.
