## Problem

Existing multimodal trajectory generation methods in end-to-end autonomous driving suffer from excessive trajectory divergence (especially diffusion-based approaches), inconsistencies between guiding information and scene context, and a lack of explicit constraints ensuring the vehicle remains within drivable areas. When guiding signals (discrete commands or pre-defined goal points) deviate from the ground truth, these methods produce low-quality trajectories, and the resulting selection problem becomes complex without clear modality boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three forward/left/right camera views concatenated into a single RGB image, a LiDAR point cloud tensor (K×3), and ego vehicle kinematic status. It outputs 128 or 256 multimodal trajectory candidates (each a 2 Hz, 4-second sequence) from which a single optimal trajectory is selected via a scoring mechanism; the final trajectory is interpolated through an LQR controller to 10 Hz for closed-loop evaluation. The goal point vocabulary contains 4096–8192 discrete (x, y, θ) endpoints clustered from training data, serving as the intermediate guidance signal between perception and trajectory generation.

## Method

GoalFlow decomposes trajectory planning into two sub-tasks: (1) selecting a precise goal point from a dense vocabulary using a Transformer-based scorer that predicts both a continuous distance score (softmax over Euclidean distances to the GT endpoint) and a binary drivable-area-compliance score (checking whether a shadow vehicle's bounding box lies inside the drivable polygon), and (2) generating constrained multimodal trajectories via Rectified Flow conditioned on the BEV feature, ego status, and the selected goal point. The flow matching network predicts the velocity field v_t = τ_norm − x_0 along a straight-line interpolation path, requiring as few as one denoising step at inference (10.4 ms vs. 177.8 ms for 20 steps). A trajectory scorer then selects the optimal candidate by balancing L2 distance to the goal point against ego progress, and a shadow-trajectory mechanism (generated with the goal point masked) provides a fallback when the predicted goal point is unreliable.

## Results

On the NavSim test set, GoalFlow achieves a PDM score of 90.3, surpassing the previous best (PARA-Drive at 84.0) by 6.3 points. The method leads on every individual metric: S_NC=98.4, S_DAC=98.3, S_TTC=94.6, S_CF=100, and S_EP=85.0. Notably, the DAC score of 98.3 exceeds the second-best by 5.5 points, and S_EP exceeds it by 5.7 points, indicating substantially better drivable-area compliance and ego progress. With only a single denoising step (10.4 ms inference), GoalFlow retains a PDM of 88.9, a mere 1.6% drop from the 5-step optimum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoalFlow outperforms all three main baselines across every PDM sub-metric. Against TransFuser (84.0 PDM), GoalFlow gains +6.3 PDM, with the largest margins in S_DAC (+5.5) and S_EP (+5.8), reflecting the benefit of goal-point-constrained generation over unconstrained regression. Against UniAD (83.4 PDM), the gap widens to +6.9 PDM, with S_DAC improving by +6.4 points, suggesting that UniAD's unified-query design is less effective at maintaining drivable-area compliance. Against PARA-Drive (84.0 PDM), GoalFlow leads by +6.3 PDM and +5.7 S_EP, indicating that parallel task execution does not compensate for the lack of explicit goal-point guidance. When the predicted goal point is replaced with the ground-truth endpoint (GoalFlow†), the PDM rises to 92.1, approaching the human trajectory score of 94.8, which isolates the goal-point prediction error as the primary remaining bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoalFlow sits at the intersection of two research threads: end-to-end autonomous driving perception (building directly on Transfuser's image-LiDAR BEV fusion) and generative trajectory modeling (adopting Rectified Flow, a specific form of Flow Matching, as the generative backbone). It departs from prior diffusion-based trajectory generators (MotionDiffuser, Diffusion-ES) that suffer from unconstrained divergence, and from command-based multimodal planners (VAD, SparseDrive) that rely on discrete or fixed guidance signals. The key conceptual departure is introducing a learned, scene-conditioned goal point vocabulary as a precise spatial constraint on the flow-matching process, effectively decomposing planning into goal prediction followed by constrained trajectory generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the single-step inference capability: flow matching with goal-point guidance achieves near-optimal performance (only 1.6% PDMS drop) with one denoising step versus 20, reducing inference time to ~10 ms per sample — a critical property for real-time autonomous driving deployment. The 5.5-point DAC improvement over prior SOTA highlights that existing end-to-end methods under-address drivable-area compliance, a safety-relevant gap. Open questions include whether the goal-point vocabulary approach generalizes to longer horizons or more complex multi-agent interactions, and whether the training-time dependence on ground-truth trajectory endpoints (used to guide flow matching) limits true closed-loop robustness.
