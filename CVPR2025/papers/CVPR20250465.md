## Problem

The recently released CheXpert Plus dataset lacks any comparative evaluation algorithms or baseline results, leaving researchers without a reference point for training and benchmarking X-ray report generation models. Additionally, mainstream X-ray MRG architectures rely on Transformer vision backbones with O(N²) computational cost and single-stage pre-training that fails to exploit the large pool of unpaired X-ray images available in clinical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250465/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a chest X-ray image at 192×192×3 resolution, partitions it into 144 non-overlapping 16×16 patches projected to 1024-dim visual tokens, and generates a free-text radiology report conditioned on the instruction prompt 'Generate a comprehensive and detailed diagnosis report for this chest X-ray image.' For benchmarking, all 19 mainstream MRG models and 16 LLMs/VLMs are evaluated against complete (non-truncated) ground truth reports using NLG metrics (BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, METEOR, CIDEr) and Clinical Efficacy metrics (Precision, Recall, F1 via the CheXpert toolkit with macro-averaging).

## Method

MambaXray-VL employs a three-stage pre-training strategy built on the Vim (Mamba-based) vision backbone with O(N) complexity. Stage 1 performs self-supervised autoregressive generation (ARG) on ~1.27M unpaired X-ray images, predicting the next visual token given all preceding tokens, which the authors show outperforms MAE pre-training (e.g., +45% CIDEr on MIMIC-CXR). Stage 2 conducts CLIP-style contrastive learning on 480k paired X-ray–report samples, using Bio_ClinicalBERT as the text encoder to project both modalities into a shared feature space. Stage 3 fine-tunes on downstream datasets by concatenating visual tokens with a generation prompt and feeding them into a frozen LLM decoder (Llama2-7B or Qwen-1.5-1.8B), training only the visual encoder and a visual-to-text mapper layer. The key design insight is that multi-stage training decouples the abundant unpaired visual pre-training from the scarce paired alignment, avoiding task conflicts and enabling the Mamba backbone to learn strong X-ray representations before being adapted for report generation.

## Results

On the CheXpert Plus dataset, MambaXray-VL-L achieves state-of-the-art performance across all NLG metrics: BLEU-4 = 0.112, ROUGE-L = 0.276, METEOR = 0.157, and CIDEr = 0.139, surpassing the previous best (R2GenGPT at B4=0.101, CIDEr=0.123). On clinical efficacy metrics, it reaches P=0.377, R=0.319, F1=0.335, a substantial margin over the next-best Token-Mixer (F1=0.288). The smaller MambaXray-VL-B variant (57.31M params) also outperforms all prior methods on BLEU-4 (0.105) and METEOR (0.149) while running in 50.66 minutes, demonstrating strong efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaXray-VL-L outperforms all 19 prior mainstream MRG models on every NLG and clinical efficacy metric on CheXpert Plus. The closest NLG competitors are R2GenGPT (B4=0.101, CIDEr=0.123), XProNet (B4=0.100, CIDEr=0.121), and R2GenCSR (B4=0.100, CIDEr=0.121), all of which trail MambaXray-VL-L by 11% on BLEU-4 and 13% on CIDEr. On the clinical F1 metric, MambaXray-VL-L (0.335) leads Token-Mixer (0.288) by 4.7 points and PromptMRG (0.281) by 5.4 points. Among the 16 LLMs evaluated in Table 2 (using the R2GenGPT SwinTransformer encoder), Vicuna-V1.5 achieves the best BLEU-4 (0.104) and InternLM the best F1 (0.284), yet both remain below MambaXray-VL-L's scores, confirming that the Mamba-based multi-stage pre-training provides a meaningful advantage over simply swapping in a stronger LLM decoder. The two VLMs tested (InternVL-2, MiniCPM-V2.5) perform substantially worse than LLM-based approaches (F1=0.132 and 0.122 vs. 0.260+), indicating that general-purpose vision-language pre-training transfers poorly to X-ray report generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the R2GenGPT paradigm of freezing a large language model decoder while training a visual encoder, but replaces the conventional SwinTransformer vision backbone with the linear-complexity Mamba (Vim) architecture. It departs from the dominant MAE-based self-supervised pre-training by substituting autoregressive generation (ARG), inspired by ARM and Apple's AIM, and adds a CLIP-style contrastive alignment stage between X-ray images and reports before supervised fine-tuning. The multi-stage strategy (ARG → contrastive → SFT) is positioned as a response to the scarcity of paired image-report data relative to the abundance of unlabeled X-ray images, and the CXPMRG-Bench benchmark fills a gap left by the CheXpert Plus dataset release, which provided no comparative baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CXPMRG-Bench is the first comprehensive evaluation of 19 MRG algorithms, 14 LLMs, and 2 VLMs on CheXpert Plus, giving the community a much-needed reference point for a dataset that previously lacked any published results. The finding that general-purpose VLMs (InternVL-2, MiniCPM-V2.5) substantially underperform LLM-based MRG pipelines on X-ray images highlights a significant domain gap that medical-specific pre-training must address. Open questions include whether the multi-stage strategy generalizes to other modalities (CT, MRI), whether medical-domain LLMs would further close the gap identified in the Bio_ClinicalBERT vs. Llama2 contrastive-learning comparison, and whether the Mamba advantage over Transformers persists at larger scales or with more complex imaging tasks.
