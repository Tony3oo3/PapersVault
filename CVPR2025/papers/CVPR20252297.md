## Problem

Multi-view multi-label classification in practice suffers from two simultaneous data deficiencies—missing views due to acquisition limitations and multi-label noise (both over-labeled and missing labels) from imperfect annotation—yet existing methods address each issue in isolation. Moreover, prior noise-handling strategies rely on unstable heuristics (early stopping, cyclical learning rates) or decouple transition matrix estimation from classifier training, causing error accumulation. No framework jointly handles incomplete views and multi-label noise with a theoretically grounded, end-to-end training procedure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes V views per sample (each of dimension d_v) where a binary mask matrix W indicates which views are available per instance (at least one view is guaranteed), along with a noisy binary label matrix Y̅ ∈ {0,1}^{N×C} in which a uniform rate ρ of positive and negative labels are flipped per class. The noise model is class-dependent but instance-independent, parameterized by a 2×2 transition matrix T^j per label. The output is a set of C class-posterior probabilities h_θ(x̂) ∈ [0,1]^C, from which multi-label predictions are derived by thresholding.

## Method

DMMIvNL comprises three coupled modules. First, an information-bottleneck feature extractor maximizes mutual information between shared representations z^v across views while minimizing it between specific representations s^v, with an orthogonality constraint I(z^v; s^v)=0; the resulting variational upper bound is computed via contrastive joint/marginal distribution matrices and a balance factor α. Second, a volume-minimization network jointly learns per-label 2×2 transition matrices T^j and the classifier h_θ by minimizing the simplex volume vol(T^j) subject to T^j matching the noisy class-posterior, with a theorem proving statistical consistency under the sufficiently scattered assumption. Third, a cycle-consistency framework (forward noisy-posterior matching, backward clean-posterior recovery via approximate inverse T̃^j, and forward-backward invertibility) stabilizes transition matrix estimation, while regularization losses exploit raw label fidelity and label co-occurrence correlations to prevent overfitting to noise.

## Results

Under the standard setting (PER=50%, ρ=30%), DMMIvNL achieves the best average ranking (Ave.R) on all six datasets, with Ave.R values of 1.17 (Yeast), 1.00 (Corel 5k), 1.67 (VOC 2007), 1.50 (Espgame), 1.00 (IAPR TC-12), and 1.00 (MIR FLICKR). The most representative AP metric shows the largest gains: 72.08 on Yeast, 19.18 on Corel 5k, 43.86 on VOC 2007, 19.13 on Espgame, 19.66 on IAPR TC-12, and 54.89 on MIR FLICKR. AUC values range from 69.86 (Espgame) to 81.14 (MIR FLICKR), consistently outperforming all baselines. The method maintains top performance as noise rate ρ varies from 10% to 40% and PER varies from 10% to 70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMMIvNL achieves the best Ave.R on all six datasets, with the closest competitor being MTD (Ave.R=1.83 on Yeast) and AIMNet (Ave.R=2.17 on Espgame, 2.67 on Corel 5k, 3.17 on IAPR TC-12). On the AP metric, DMMIvNL shows the most dramatic improvements: 72.08 vs. 68.87 (MTD) on Yeast, 19.18 vs. 18.49 (AIMNet) on Corel 5k, 43.86 vs. 41.41 (ENTMLC) on VOC 2007, and 54.89 vs. 45.69 (ENTMLC) on MIR FLICKR. Against the noise-specialized baseline ENTMLC, DMMIvNL wins on AP, AUC, 1-RL, 1-Cov, and 1-HL across all datasets, but loses on 1-OE for Espgame (31.77 vs. 1.48) and IAPR TC-12 (34.33 vs. 1.49), where ENTMLC's stepwise transition-matrix estimation excels at reducing the top-error rate. Compared to the incomplete-view specialists (DICNet, DIMC, MTD, SIP, AIMNet, iMVWL, TM3L), DMMIvNL consistently outperforms on AP and AUC, with the gap widening as noise increases (e.g., at ρ=40%, methods like TM3L and DIMC degrade substantially while DMMIvNL remains stable). The paper also notes that DMMIvNL improves 1-OE by nearly 50% over SIP on MIR FLICKR at 50% PER, attributing this to its information-bottleneck-based separation of shared and specific features.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMMIvNL sits at the intersection of three established research threads: multi-view multi-label classification (MvMLC), incomplete-view recovery (iMvWL, AIMNet, DICNet, MTD, SIP), and noisy-label learning via transition matrices (ENTMLC, anchor-point methods, volume minimization of Li et al. 2021). The paper departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it formulates the first joint treatment of missing views and multi-label noise (both over-labeled and missing labels) within a single framework, whereas existing methods address each deficiency in isolation; and (2) it replaces geometric orthogonality constraints (as in MDDRL, MTD) with an information-bottleneck-based mutual information objective whose variational upper bound is derived through a VAE-style decomposition, enabling non-linear inter-view relation modeling. The cycle-consistency estimation for the transition matrix extends the volume-minimization principle of Li et al. (ICML 2021) by adding a backward projection and forward-backward consistency term, drawing on the adaptive loss framework of Cheng et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously handle incomplete views and multi-label noise in MvMLC, a combination that is prevalent in real-world settings such as medical diagnostics where both sensor failures and subjective annotation errors co-occur. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that minimizing transition-matrix volume yields a statistically consistent classifier under the sufficiently scattered assumption provides a principled foundation that prior heuristic approaches (early stopping, cyclical learning rates) lack. However, the method assumes class-dependent (instance-independent) noise and a uniform flip rate per label, which limits applicability to more complex noise structures; the sufficiently scattered assumption is also difficult to verify in practice. The authors note future directions including LLM-based label correction and class-imbalance handling, suggesting the current framework is a foundation rather than a final solution.
