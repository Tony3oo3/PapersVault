## Problem

Existing automated tiling methods using generative models (e.g., Seamless Tile Inpainting, Asymmetric Tiling) are restricted to self-tiling or simple one-to-one connections, primarily within texture synthesis. There is no approach that supports complex many-to-many tiling constraints across multiple images in diverse visual domains (landscapes, paintings, textures), where any side of one image must seamlessly connect to any side of another.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250539/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts (or input images in img2img mode) plus a set of tiling constraints, where each constraint C_j = {A_j, B_j} specifies that any side in set A_j must seamlessly connect to any side in set B_j. It produces N simultaneously generated images in latent space (H_latent × W_latent × D_latent) that, after VAE decoding and cropping, satisfy all predefined tiling constraints. The method is model-agnostic, demonstrated on Stable Diffusion 1.5/2.0, SDXL, SD3, and ControlNet, with no retraining required.

## Method

Tiled Diffusion operates entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained diffusion model, applying two constraints at every denoising step without any training. The tiling constraint copies a context window of size w from one latent to pad the adjacent latent (and vice versa) for each constraint pair, ensuring global structural coherence in regions that are later cropped away. The similarity constraint copies a fixed-width-5 latent strip inward from the connecting side, directly enforcing pixel-level similarity in the retained output region, which is critical for many-to-many scenarios. When a constraint involves multiple sides, a round-robin mechanism cycles through available sides across diffusion steps so each side is exposed to all its potential matches. After the full diffusion process, latents are decoded via the VAE and cropped to original dimensions, yielding inherently tileable images that share tiling information during generation rather than being stitched as a post-processing step.

## Results

On 1,000 LAION 400M prompts, Tiled Diffusion achieves a Tiling Score (TS) of 0.03–0.04 across all three tiling scenarios (self-tiling, one-to-one, many-to-many), matching the pseudo ground-truth Swapped VT2I baseline (TS 0.03) and far outperforming STI (TS 0.14 on one-to-one). FID scores are 47.9 (self-tiling), 48.1 (one-to-one), and 50.5 (many-to-many), all substantially lower than STI's 59.2 and 77.1. CLIP-Score remains stable at 0.30 across scenarios, indicating prompt fidelity is preserved. The method uniquely supports many-to-many tiling, where no baseline is applicable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Asymmetric Tiling (AT) [31], Tiled Diffusion wins on FID (47.9 vs. 49.2) and LPIPS (0.76 vs. 0.79) in self-tiling, while matching on TS (0.03) and slightly exceeding on CLIP-Score (0.30 vs. 0.29). AT is restricted to self-tiling only and cannot handle one-to-one or many-to-many scenarios. Against Seamless Tile Inpainting (STI) [4], Tiled Diffusion achieves a dramatically lower FID in one-to-one tiling (48.1 vs. 77.1) and a much better TS (0.04 vs. 0.14), indicating far more seamless connections; STI's per-image independent generation causes visible discontinuities at tile borders. In self-tiling, Tiled Diffusion also outperforms STI on FID (47.9 vs. 59.2) and LPIPS (0.76 vs. 0.77), though STI edges ahead marginally on CLIP-Score (0.31 vs. 0.30). Tiled Diffusion is the only method supporting many-to-many tiling, where it achieves TS 0.03 and FID 50.5 with no comparable baseline available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automated tiling problem from prior generative-model approaches (Seamless Tile Inpainting, Asymmetric Tiling) that were limited to self-tiling or simple one-to-one connections. Rather than modifying the diffusion architecture (as AT does with circular padding) or applying inpainting post-hoc (as STI does), Tiled Diffusion operates directly on latent representations during generation, simultaneously creating all tiles and sharing tiling information between them. Conceptually, it draws on the classical Wang Tiles idea of defining connectivity constraints on image edges, but replaces the discrete color-edge matching with continuous latent-space copying, and generalizes the constraint formulation to arbitrary many-to-many side connections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The many-to-many tiling capability is genuinely new and opens up applications (mix-and-match texture sets, complex panoramic arrangements) that prior methods cannot address. The method's model-agnosticism (demonstrated across SD 1.5, 2.0, SDXL, SD3, and ControlNet) makes it broadly applicable. A noted limitation is minor artifacts at cross-axis (X-to-Y) connections due to latent-space rotation not perfectly aligning with pixel-space rotation after decoding, which remains an open problem. The near-constant performance as the number of connected sides grows (Figure 5) suggests the approach scales well, though the evaluation is limited to 512×512 tiles and a single base model family.
