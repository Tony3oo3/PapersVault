## Problem

Existing diffusion-image detection datasets and methods fail to address open-world settings where user prompts are diverse, diffusion models rapidly evolve, and manipulations range from full-image synthesis to precise local edits. No prior benchmark simultaneously covers all three dimensions (user diversity, model innovation, and full manipulation scope), and most detection/localization approaches overfit to specific generator artifacts or traditional splicing operations, limiting generalization to unseen T2I diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251976/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, resized to 224×224 for the CLIP vision encoder and 512×512 for the MAE encoder. It produces two outputs simultaneously: a binary real/fake classification derived from cosine similarity between the aggregated image representation and learned continuous text prompt embeddings, and a pixel-level forgery localization mask (H×W) generated by the FPN decoder. The dataset covers both globally synthesized images and locally edited regions (via SAM/Florence-2 masks), with ground-truth masks for the latter.

## Method

MaskCLIP implements the Synergizing Pretrained Models (SPM) scheme by aligning a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 (image-level semantics) with a tunable MAE ViT-B/32 (spatial reconstruction) through two mechanisms: prompting and attending. Prompting introduces learnable continuous prompt vectors for 'real' and 'fake' concepts processed by the CLIP text encoder, preserving pretrained visual-linguistic knowledge. Attending uses three cross-attention blocks: VCA fuses CLIP visual tokens into MAE features at multiple layers via multi-head attention, TVCA injects CLIP text embeddings into the FPN decoder output for mask generation, and VSA aggregates [CLS] tokens from multiple CLIP encoder layers via self-attention for robust global representation. An FPN-based decoder processes multi-scale MAE features (0.5×, 2×, 4×) for pixel-level predictions, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with a combined cross-entropy, binary cross-entropy, and edge-weighted loss.

## Results

On the OpenSDID benchmark, MaskCLIP achieves an average IoU of 0.4271 and F1 of 0.4941 for pixel-level localization, surpassing the second-best method (IML-ViT) by 14.23% in IoU and 14.11% in F1. For image-level detection, MaskCLIP attains an average F1 of 0.7779 and accuracy of 0.8198, outperforming the best image-level detector (RINE) by 2.38% in F1 and 2.05% in accuracy. The largest cross-domain gains appear on Flux.1 (44.69% IoU improvement) and SD3 (23.07% IoU improvement), demonstrating strong generalization to unseen diffusion models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskCLIP outperforms all 13 baselines on average localization metrics, with the closest competitor being IML-ViT (avg IoU 0.3251 vs. 0.4271). The gap widens dramatically on unseen generators: on Flux.1, MaskCLIP achieves IoU 0.1622 versus 0.0611 for IML-ViT (a 44.69% relative gain), and on SD3 it reaches 0.4375 versus 0.2363 (23.07% gain). Among image-level detection baselines, RINE leads with avg F1 0.7598, but MaskCLIP surpasses it by 2.38% (0.7779) while simultaneously providing pixel-level localization. On the Flux.1 detection subset, NPR (F1 0.6762) edges out MaskCLIP (0.5649), making it the one model where a dedicated image-level detector still wins. Traditional manipulation-localization methods (CAT-Net, TruFor, PSCC-Net) degrade severely on newer diffusion models (SD3, Flux.1), with IoU dropping below 0.36, whereas MaskCLIP maintains IoU above 0.43 on SD3 and 0.16 on Flux.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021) for image-level semantic understanding and MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) for fine-grained spatial reconstruction, following the FPN-based decoder design from IML-ViT (Ma et al., AAAI 2024). Its key departure is the Synergizing Pretrained Models (SPM) framework, which combines multiple frozen foundation models through prompt tuning and cross-attention rather than full fine-tuning, positioning itself against both traditional manipulation detectors (TruFor, CAT-Net) and recent CLIP-based binary classifiers (UniFD, RINE) that lack localization capability. The paper also carves new ground by defining the OpenSDI challenge as a unified problem spanning user diversity, model innovation, and full manipulation scope—dimensions that no prior dataset or method jointly addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenSDID (300K images across five diffusion models with both global and local manipulations) fills a critical gap in benchmarking for diffusion-specific forgery detection, and the SPM framework offers a generalizable recipe for combining complementary pretrained models. However, the cross-domain performance remains weak—Flux.1 IoU drops to 0.16 and SD3 to 0.44—indicating that robust open-world detection of diffusion images is far from solved. Open questions include whether the SPM scheme scales to future generative architectures (e.g., autoregressive or flow-based models), whether the reliance on specific CLIP/MAE backbones limits long-term applicability, and how the approach performs under adversarial or compressed real-world deployment conditions beyond the mild degradations tested here.
