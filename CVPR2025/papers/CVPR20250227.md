## Problem

Existing radar-camera depth estimation methods rely on multi-stage frameworks that predict an intermediate quasi-dense depth to bridge the extreme sparsity of radar returns (1000× sparser than LiDAR) before producing final dense depth. These intermediate predictions remain sparse and noisy—sometimes yielding no valid pixels in adverse lighting—causing disrupted structures and artifacts in final outputs, while the multi-stage pipelines are too slow for real-time deployment on vehicles and robots.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250227/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (900×1600 on nuScenes, 300×1280 on ZJU-4DRadarCam) and a sparse radar point cloud of ~30 points (K×3 coordinates) as required inputs, producing a dense H×W metric depth map in millimeters. In plug-in mode, an optional initial relative depth map from external predictors (DPT-Hybrid, MiDaS, DepthAnything-v2) is fed through an auxiliary branch to convert relative depth to metric scale. The independent mode achieves 37.5 fps real-time inference on a single GPU without any external depth predictor.

## Method

TacoDepth replaces multi-stage fusion with a one-stage pipeline built on two key components. A graph-based radar structure extractor (3-layer GNN using PCA-GM) treats radar points as graph nodes and their adjacency as edges, capturing geometric structures and topologies that are more robust to individual point noise than raw coordinate features. A pyramid-based radar fusion module then hierarchically integrates these radar features with multi-scale ResNet-18 image features: node features N_l fuse with image feature F_{2l-1} and edge features E_l with F_{2l}, using a radar-centered flash attention mechanism that restricts cross-modal attention to horizontal-coordinate neighborhoods (widths 48/32/16 pixels per layer) for computational efficiency. Training uses L1 loss against both LiDAR ground-truth depth and reprojection-accumulated depth, with half the batch receiving an initial relative depth and half receiving zeros to jointly optimize both independent and plug-in inference modes.

## Results

On nuScenes (0–70 m), TacoDepth achieves MAE 1712.6 mm and RMSE 3960.5 mm as an independent model, outperforming the best prior independent method Li et al. [20] (MAE 1822.9, RMSE 4303.6) by 4.9% and 8.1% respectively, while running 60.5% faster (26.7 ms vs. 67.6 ms). In plug-in mode, TacoDepth reaches MAE 1347.1 mm and RMSE 3152.8 mm, surpassing RadarCam-Depth [21] (MAE 1587.9, RMSE 3662.5) by 11.7% and 13.9% while being 91.8% faster (29.3 ms vs. 358.3 ms). On ZJU-4DRadarCam (0–70 m), TacoDepth reduces MAE by 12.8% and RMSE by 10.8% over RadarCam-Depth in plug-in mode. The model uses only 13.47 M parameters and 139.30 G FLOPs, a 72.2% FLOPs reduction over the two-stage Singh et al. [37] baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TacoDepth outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric across both datasets. Among independent models, it beats the strongest prior method Li et al. [20] (ECCV'24) by 4.9% MAE and 8.1% RMSE at 0–70 m on nuScenes while running 2.5× faster (26.7 ms vs. 67.6 ms). Against the two-stage Singh et al. [37] (CVPR'23), TacoDepth reduces MAE by 17.5% and RMSE by 13.7% at 0–70 m, with a 72.2% FLOPs reduction. The plug-in comparison is most striking: TacoDepth surpasses RadarCam-Depth [21] (ICRA'24) by 11.7% MAE and 13.9% RMSE on nuScenes while being 12.2× faster (29.3 ms vs. 358.3 ms), and by 12.8% MAE / 10.8% RMSE on ZJU-4DRadarCam. Older multi-stage baselines (Lin et al. [26], DORN [28], RC-PDA [31]) are dominated by large margins—e.g., TacoDepth cuts RMSE by over 25% versus DORN [28] at 0–70 m. The only baseline not directly comparable on all ranges is R4Dyn [12], which reports only 0–80 m RMSE (6434.0 mm) versus TacoDepth's 4150.2 mm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-stage paradigm in radar-camera depth estimation, where prior methods (Lin et al. 2020, DORN 2021, RC-PDA 2021, Singh et al. 2023, CaFNet 2024) predict an intermediate quasi-dense depth before refining to final dense depth, and from the four-stage plug-in approach of RadarCam-Depth (Li et al. 2024). Rather than building on a single predecessor, TacoDepth repurposes graph neural network concepts (PCA-GM, graph matching) for radar point cloud structure extraction and introduces a radar-centered flash attention mechanism for efficient cross-modal fusion, collapsing the entire pipeline into one stage. The image backbone (ResNet-18) and depth decoder follow prior conventions, so the novelty is concentrated in the radar feature extraction and fusion architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 91.8% speed improvement over the best plug-in method while simultaneously improving accuracy by 12.8% is practically significant for real-time autonomous driving and robotics, where radar-camera fusion must run at 37+ fps. The finding that intermediate quasi-dense depth is not merely unnecessary but actively harmful (sparse, noisy, and failing entirely in adverse lighting) challenges a core assumption in the field and suggests that multi-stage designs may be suboptimal more broadly. Limitations include a single efficient implementation, evaluation on only two datasets, and the reliance on horizontal-coordinate precision that may not generalize to all radar configurations. Open questions include whether the one-stage approach extends to other sparse-sensor fusion tasks (e.g., radar-LiDAR) and whether deeper or more diverse radar structure extractors could further close the gap to LiDAR-based methods.
