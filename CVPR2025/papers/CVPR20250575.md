## Problem

Oriented object detection (OOD) relies on OBB parameterizations (e.g., long-edge, OpenCV) that exhibit angular boundary discontinuities, causing large regression losses for nearly identical boxes near the ±90° boundary. While Gaussian-based loss functions mitigate this during training, they still suffer from discontinuity at inference because the regression head outputs OBB parameters that are then mapped to Gaussians. Additionally, OBB-based representations suffer from encoding ambiguity for circular objects, where any rotated square is an equally valid annotation.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB aerial/remote sensing images as input and produces oriented object detections with class labels. Rather than outputting OBB parameters (x, y, w, h, θ), the regression head outputs the mean vector and the lower-triangular Cholesky decomposition (α, β, γ) of a 2D Gaussian covariance matrix. These Gaussian parameters can be decoded into either OBBs (via eigendecomposition) or Oriented Ellipses (OEs), with OEs advocated as the preferred output representation since they naturally handle circular objects without artificial orientation.

## Method

GauCho replaces the standard OBB regression head with one that directly regresses the Cholesky decomposition of a 2D Gaussian covariance matrix, providing a bijective and continuous mapping from network outputs to Gaussian distributions that theoretically eliminates angular boundary discontinuity. The paper derives theoretical bounds (Propositions 3.1–3.2) linking Cholesky parameters to OBB dimensions, which are exploited to design both anchor-free heads (FCOS-style multiplicative offsets scaled by feature-map stride) and anchor-based heads (RetinaNet, R3Det, RoI-Transformer) with appropriate offset parameterizations. The head is fully compatible with any Gaussian-based regression loss (GWD, KLD, ProbIoU) and requires no architectural changes to the backbone or classification branch. For anchor-based detectors, a δ parameter is introduced in the γ regression to allow square anchors to stretch to non-square aspect ratios, and oriented anchors in refinement stages are handled via direct Cholesky parameter offsets.

## Results

On DOTA v1.0 with multiscale training/testing, GauCho-ROITransformer achieves the best AP50 of 80.61, outperforming RoI-Transformer-ACM (79.45) and CenterNet-ACM (78.53). FCOS-GauCho reaches 78.85 AP50, surpassing the anchor-free CenterNet-ACM (78.53) and KLD-based detection (78.32). In single-scale experiments (Table 1), GauCho consistently improves over the OBB head for FCOS on DOTA v1.0 (e.g., 40.65 vs. 39.80 AP with ProbIoU), while other detectors show comparable results. On DOTA v1.5, FCOS-GauCho yields ~1.1% average AP50 improvement over FCOS-OBB across all loss functions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GauCho-ROITransformer (80.61 AP50) outperforms all 13 baselines on DOTA v1.0 multiscale, beating the strongest prior method RoI-Transformer-ACM [27] by 1.16 points and CenterNet-ACM [27] by 2.08 points. FCOS-GauCho (78.85) edges out the anchor-free CenterNet-ACM (78.53) by 0.32 points and surpasses KLD [33] (78.32) and PSCD [37] (78.07). Against Gaussian-loss-based methods, FCOS-GauCho beats GWD [32] (76.30) by 2.55 points and KLD [33] by 0.53 points. Against boundary-discontinuity-focused methods, it outperforms DCL [30] (77.37), KFIoU [36] (77.35), and SCRDet++ [34] (76.81) by 1.5–2.0 points. The paper also notes that GauCho requires no additional hyperparameter (unlike ACM loss [27]), and in single-scale experiments (Table 1), GauCho and OBB heads produce nearly identical AP for RetinaNet, R3Det, and RoI-Transformer, with the clearest gains appearing for FCOS on DOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian-based OOD paradigm (GWD, KLD, ProbIoU losses) by shifting the Gaussian from a loss-function intermediary to the primary regression target. Rather than regressing OBB parameters (w, h, θ) and converting to a covariance matrix for loss computation, GauCho directly regresses the Cholesky decomposition of the covariance matrix, leveraging its bijective mapping to positive-definite matrices to avoid constrained optimization. The anchor-free formulation builds on FCOS's stride-based offset regression, while the anchor-based formulation adapts the multiplicative offset strategy from prior Gaussian-OOD work. The paper also introduces Oriented Ellipses as a natural decoding target, departing from the standard OBB output used by all prior Gaussian-based detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GauCho provides a theoretically clean solution to the angular boundary discontinuity problem by ensuring all regressed parameters are continuous functions of orientation, and it inherently resolves the encoding ambiguity for circular objects that plagues all OBB-based representations. However, the empirical gains are modest and inconsistent: FCOS on DOTA shows clear improvements, while other detectors (RetinaNet, R3Det, RoI-Transformer) show mostly comparable results to OBB heads, suggesting the theoretical advantage may be more impactful in specific architectural contexts. The decoding ambiguity for square-like objects remains unresolved, and the paper acknowledges that hyperparameter tuning specific to GauCho could yield further gains. Open questions include whether the continuous representation advantage becomes more pronounced with larger models or more challenging datasets, and whether OE-based evaluation should become standard practice for aerial imagery detection.
