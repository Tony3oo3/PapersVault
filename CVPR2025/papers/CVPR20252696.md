## Problem

The scaling properties of audio-visual Masked Autoencoders for Affective Video Facial Analysis remain largely unexplored, with prior methods capping at the ten-million parameter scale and limited data scaling. Additionally, existing AVFA SSL methods inadequately capture intra- and inter-modal correlations through scalable representations, and direct fine-tuning on small downstream datasets causes severe overfitting that limits the potential of pre-trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252696/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired facial video clips (T_v × H × W × 3, divided into 2×16×16 3D patches) and audio spectrograms (T_a × F, divided into 16×16 2D patches). During pre-training, high masking ratios (90% video, 80% audio) are applied and the model reconstructs only the decoder-masked subset (50% of remaining tokens). During downstream evaluation, the tuned model outputs discrete emotion category labels (CEA), continuous arousal/valence/dominance scores (DEA), or micro-expression labels (MER), with three model sizes ranging from 169M to 521M parameters.

## Method

AVF-MAE++ introduces an audio-visual dual masking strategy (separate encoder and decoder masks per modality) that reduces decoder computation by ~1.5× while maintaining reconstruction quality. The modality encoder replaces vanilla ViT with a four-stage local-global interaction attention (LGI-Former extended with a global-local interaction stage) that handles facial redundancy at sub-quadratic cost. The IAV-CL Module performs iterative cross-modal correlation learning via DiER Units (parallel MHSA+MHCA with sigmoid channel gating) and a HAFE Layer that hierarchically aggregates multi-scale features with feedback cross-attention. A Progressive Semantics Injection strategy structures training in three stages—large-scale SSL pre-training on 1.36M unlabeled clips, supervised post-pre-training on merged labeled hybrid datasets, and targeted fine-tuning—bridging the distribution gap between pre-training and small downstream benchmarks.

## Results

On the flagship MAFW (11-class) dataset, AVF-MAE++(H) achieves 46.05% UAR and 60.24% WAR, becoming the first method to surpass 60% WAR. On DFEW, the Huge variant reaches 66.88% UAR / 77.45% WAR, and on MER24-T&V it attains 72.28% WAR / 71.75% WA-F1. For dimensional emotion analysis, AVF-MAE++(H) posts 47.25% Arousal and 49.66% Valence WA-F1 on AVCAffe, and 44.99/72.19/52.35 PCC on Werewolf-XL (Arousal/Valence/Dominance). Across the five MER datasets, the best variant achieves up to 94.11% UF1 on CASME II and 84.33% on MMEW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVF-MAE++(H) outperforms the closest audio-visual SSL baseline HiCMAE [60] by 3.40% UAR and 4.07% WAR on MAFW (11-class), and by 3.12% UAR / 2.44% WAR on DFEW. It also surpasses the text+video method FineCLIPER [8] by 1.04% WAR on MAFW (11-class) and 1.24% WAR on DFEW, and exceeds the video-only UniLearn [11] by 2.33% UAR / 1.80% WAR on MAFW. On dimensional emotion analysis, AVF-MAE++(H) improves over HiCMAE by 4.07% WA-F1 (Arousal) and 5.46% WA-F1 (Valence) on AVCAffe, and by up to 11.69% PCC across dimensions on Werewolf-XL. On MER, the largest gain is 10.21% UF1 over HTNet [73] on CAS(ME)³. The one notable exception is RAVDESS, where AVF-MAE++(H) (87.44% UAR) slightly trails HiCMAE (87.96%), which the authors attribute to overfitting of the larger model on this small dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends HiCMAE [60], the prior state-of-the-art audio-visual MAE for emotion recognition, by scaling it to hundred-million parameter models and introducing three key architectural departures: an audio-visual dual masking strategy adapted from VideoMAE V2 [67], an improved local-global interaction encoder (building on LGI-Former [58]) with a holistic global-local interaction stage, and the IAV-CL Module for explicit iterative audio-visual correlation learning. The progressive semantics injection training pipeline is a novel adaptation strategy not present in prior AVFA MAE work, drawing inspiration from BEiT [2] and VideoMAE V2 [67] but tailored to the domain-shift and data-scarcity challenges specific to affective computing. The overall asymmetric encoder-decoder MAE framework traces back to He et al. [28], and the pre-training data construction follows the multi-source mixing approach of MAE-DFER [58] and HiCMAE [60] but at substantially larger scale (1.36M clips).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that audio-visual MAE pre-training is scalable in both model capacity and data size for affective computing, a property previously unverified in the AVFA domain, and provides the first hundred-million parameter model in this area. The progressive semantics injection strategy offers a practical recipe for adapting large pre-trained models to small downstream datasets, addressing a persistent overfitting bottleneck. However, the authors acknowledge that overfitting on small datasets (e.g., RAVDESS, MER datasets) still limits the benefits of the largest model, and data scaling remains far below what is achievable in general vision domains, leaving the full scaling potential of AVFA unexplored. The findings have broader implications for MLLMs, talking face generation, and deepfake detection, as noted by the authors.
