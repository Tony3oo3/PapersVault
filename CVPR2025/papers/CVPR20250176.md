## Problem

Existing facial reflectance estimation methods either rely on BRDF models that cannot capture the subsurface scattering (SSS) properties of skin, or use data-driven approaches constrained by small Light Stage datasets (fewer than 300 subjects), limiting generalization to diverse real-world identities. No prior work has estimated hemoglobin and melanin maps from in-the-wild images, which are essential for SSS-compliant rendering. The paper asks whether the rich visual priors in internet-scale diffusion models can enable broadly generalizable reflectance estimation with minimal captured data.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single in-the-wild RGB facial image (first completed to a full UV texture via OSTEC), multi-view images (fused via YUV color-space matching after per-view estimation), or pre-existing UV textures, all processed at 768×768 resolution. It produces five UV-space reflectance maps: diffuse albedo, specular albedo (single-channel, replicated to 3 channels for VAE encoding), detailed normal, hemoglobin, and melanin. The hemoglobin and melanin maps are the first to be estimated directly from raw input images, and the pipeline supports arbitrary input face topology without requiring a specific UV parameterization.

## Method

S³-Face fine-tunes Stable Diffusion V2 in two stages. In stage 1, DSN-Net modifies the U-Net input layer (doubled channels with halved weights) to concatenate color and reflectance latents, and introduces a joint reflectance attention module that merges keys and values across diffuse, specular, normal, and color domains within each transformer block to enforce geometric consistency; training alternates between 'single image–multiple reflectances' and 'multiple images–single diffuse albedo' paradigms to promote illumination-invariant albedo. In stage 2, HM-Net conditions on the predicted diffuse albedo (rather than the raw color image) to generate hemoglobin and melanin maps, decoupling illumination-sensitive estimation from the first stage. Both stages use multi-resolution noise and v-prediction as the training objective, and the entire system is fine-tuned on only 100 Light Stage subjects supplemented with 3D Scan Store albedo maps for skin-tone diversity.

## Results

On the Light Stage test set (10 held-out participants), S³-Face achieves 26.07 PSNR / 0.859 SSIM for diffuse albedo, 25.21 PSNR / 0.817 SSIM for specular albedo, and 24.48 PSNR / 0.784 SSIM for normal maps, outperforming the strongest prior method ID2Reflectance by 1.94, 2.54, and 2.65 PSNR respectively. For the first-time hemoglobin and melanin estimation, two-stage training yields 24.63 PSNR / 0.7156 SSIM (hemoglobin) and 23.28 PSNR / 0.6123 SSIM (melanin) against VISIA ground truth, substantially exceeding single-stage baselines (18.51 and 14.56 PSNR). Qualitative evaluation on Digital Emily and in-the-wild images confirms superior detail preservation, particularly in mid-frequency features like freckles and eye-corner textures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S³-Face outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor ID2Reflectance [38], gains are 1.94 PSNR (diffuse), 2.54 PSNR (specular), and 2.65 PSNR (normal), with corresponding SSIM improvements of 0.077, 0.046, and 0.061. The gap widens substantially against earlier methods: ReflectanceMM [17] trails by 7.28 PSNR on diffuse and 6.09 PSNR on specular, while ID2Albedo [37] and TRUST [11] (diffuse-only methods) lag by 5.73 and 8.26 PSNR respectively. On Digital Emily, S³-Face visually preserves identity-specific details such as freckles that are lost or blurred in Dib et al. [9], AvatarMe++ [27], and Relightify [35]. The specular maps also show a notable reduction in baked-lighting artifacts compared to ID2Reflectance. For hemoglobin and melanin (a first for this task), the two-stage HM-Net achieves 6.12 and 8.72 PSNR over the single-stage ablation, validated against VISIA skin-analyzer ground truth on 128 real patients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training facial reflectance models from scratch on limited Light Stage captures (AvatarMe++, FitMe, Relightify) by repurposing a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model as a reflectance prior, fine-tuned on only 100 subjects. It also moves beyond BRDF-based differentiable rendering approaches (MoSAR, Dib et al.) that cannot model subsurface scattering, instead directly generating SSS-compliant maps including hemoglobin and melanin. The joint reflectance attention module and two-stage DSN-Net/HM-Net architecture are novel architectural contributions that build on the latent diffusion framework and the structural-consistency insights from ID2Reflectance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first demonstration of hemoglobin and melanin map prediction from a single in-the-wild image opens a path toward SSS-compliant rendering that standard PBR cannot achieve, and raises the prospect of large-scale, non-invasive skin screening (melasma, rosacea) using consumer photos. However, the medical application remains speculative—ground truth comes from only 128 VISIA scans, and no clinical validation is provided. The method still requires 100 subjects of Light Stage data for fine-tuning, and the generalization to very diverse skin tones is supported by only 142 supplementary albedo maps, leaving open whether the internet-scale prior truly covers the full range of human pigmentation.
