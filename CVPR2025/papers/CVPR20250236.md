## Problem

Existing 4D avatarization methods are restricted to realistic human portraits and fail to generalize to open-domain inputs such as 3D cartoons, sculptures, or skulls, primarily due to the absence of multi-domain 4D training data. While 2D diffusion-based methods can handle diverse styles, they lack 3D geometric consistency under large pose changes and incur high per-frame computational costs. The paper tackles the core challenge of constructing a sufficiently diverse and consistent 4D dataset to train a general-purpose open-domain avatar generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250236/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source portrait image from any visual domain (realistic, anime, LEGO, sculpture, skull, etc.) and a driving image or video sequence providing target pose and expression. It produces a parametric triplane (256×256×4×32) as the intermediate 4D representation, which is then rendered into high-resolution frames from arbitrary camera viewpoints via volumetric rendering and super-resolution. The driving signal can come from real human video (VFHQ) or any expression sequence, enabling cross-domain reenactment where the source and driving subjects differ in both identity and visual domain.

## Method

The key insight is a synergistic data pipeline: 2D diffusion models (SDEdit + landmark-guided ControlNet) transfer FFHQ portraits into 28 target domains while preserving pose and expression, enabling direct reuse of 3DMM mesh labels; domain-specific Next3D GANs are then fine-tuned on these images to generate 560K image-parametric triplane pairs without multi-view supervision. For generation, a triplane VAE compresses the 4D representation into a 64×64×4×8 latent, and a 28-layer DiT-XL/2 (following Direct3D/PixArt-α) denoises this latent conditioned on CLIP semantic embeddings (cross-attention) and DINO image tokens (self-attention concatenation). A motion-aware cross-domain renderer replaces Next3D's CNN with a ViT that injects the generated triplane into self-attention to canonicalize pose/expression and an implicit motion embedding via cross-attention to drive animation, while an encoder extracts source identity features to prevent cross-domain identity leakage.

## Results

On the VFHQ dataset, AvatarArtist achieves the best LPIPS (0.26) and identity score (ID 0.69) in self-reenactment, while also delivering the best keypoint distance (AKD 2.58) and pose distance (APD 52.3) in cross-reenactment. In cross-reenactment, the method reduces FID to 89.3 (vs. 194.7 for InvertAvatar and 140.5 for Portrait4Dv2) and achieves a CLIP score of 0.84. In self-reenactment, 2D methods (LivePortrait, XPortrait) still lead on expression distance (AED 0.025 vs. 0.032) and pose distance (APD 4.28 vs. 11.72), but AvatarArtist surpasses all 4D baselines on identity preservation and perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 4D baselines (InvertAvatar, Portrait4Dv2), AvatarArtist clearly dominates: it reduces cross-reenactment FID from 194.7/140.5 to 89.3, cuts AKD from 20.78/7.13 to 2.58, and improves APD from 134.9/63.3 to 52.3, while also achieving the highest identity score (0.69 vs. 0.32/0.58) in self-reenactment. Against the 2D baselines (LivePortrait, XPortrait), the picture is mixed: LivePortrait retains an edge on self-reenactment expression (AED 0.025 vs. 0.032) and pose (APD 4.28 vs. 11.72), and on cross-reenactment identity (CLIP 0.91 vs. 0.84) and FID (100.3 vs. 89.3 for XPortrait's 78.6). However, AvatarArtist substantially outperforms both 2D methods on cross-reenactment motion accuracy, achieving AKD 2.58 vs. 4.92 (LivePortrait) and 10.67 (XPortrait), and APD 52.3 vs. 139.35 and 237.4 respectively. The paper attributes the 2D methods' advantage in identity to their direct pixel-level warping, while the 4D formulation's strength lies in geometrically consistent pose and expression transfer, especially for non-realistic domains where 2D warping breaks down.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Next3D's parametric triplane 4D representation and Portrait4D's synthetic-data training paradigm, but departs from both by addressing the open-domain setting that neither handles. The key conceptual departure is a three-stage data curation pipeline: 2D diffusion models (SDEdit + ControlNet) generate multi-domain images to overcome GAN mode collapse, domain-specific 4D GANs (fine-tuned Next3D) then produce image-triplane pairs unsupervised, and a latent DiT models the resulting multi-domain 4D distribution. This inverts the typical role of diffusion models (which usually serve as the final generator) by using them as a data-augmentation tool for GANs, while the GANs in turn provide the 4D supervision that diffusion models alone cannot supply.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to deliver 4D avatarization across genuinely diverse visual domains (cartoons, sculptures, skulls, game characters) while maintaining geometric consistency, filling a gap that 2D diffusion-based methods cannot address (geometric distortion under large pose changes) and that prior 4D methods cannot (domain restriction to realistic humans). The data curation pipeline—using diffusion models to diversify GAN training data, then GANs to create 4D representations for a diffusion model—is a potentially generalizable recipe for other 3D/4D generation tasks lacking diverse training data. Limitations include dependence on 3DMM extraction (which degrades for highly stylized inputs), restriction to head/face avatars, and the computational cost of training 28 separate domain GANs plus the DiT and renderer.
