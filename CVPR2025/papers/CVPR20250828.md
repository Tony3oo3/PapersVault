## Problem

Diffusion models struggle with fine-grained pixel-level color control: existing conditioning approaches (text prompts, reference images, structural maps) either loosely constrain the sampled color manifold, allowing out-of-bounds colors, or enforce spatial alignment of colors that prevents disentanglement into new structures. The paper targets the simultaneous satisfaction of three criteria—color accuracy, color completeness (proportion coverage), and color disentanglement into diverse structures—without requiring explicit spatial layout.

## Input / Output

The method takes a color pattern (an in-the-wild image or a rough manual drawing) that specifies which color values appear and their relative proportions, combined with a free-form text prompt for semantic guidance. The color pattern is spatially permuted before use to strip positional information. Outputs are synthesized images at 64×64 (image-space DDPM) or 512×512 (latent-space Stable Diffusion) whose pixel colors are confined to the condition's palette while forming coherent structures matching the prompt.

## Method

The core mechanism is a color alignment function f(x_t, c) that, at each diffusion step, maps every pixel of the noisy sample to its nearest color in the (spatially permuted) condition before the denoising network processes it, with an adapted noise target ε' that accounts for this projection. This confines the sampling manifold to the target color space while the non-spatial mapping preserves structural diversity. The approach is instantiated in three settings: full re-training, fine-tuning a pre-trained latent diffusion model (with blurring of x₀ before encoding and suspension of alignment at late steps t < 0.2T), and a zero-shot variant that applies a one-to-one color mapping g(x̂₀, c) to predicted clean samples during sampling without any parameter updates. The zero-shot version leverages the pre-trained model's early-step semantic clustering to clean up the mechanically mapped colors.

## Results

On the latent diffusion benchmark (Text-to-image-2M, 512×512), the fine-tuned color-aligned model achieves the best CLIP-Score (29.0 manual / 27.4 in-the-wild) among all methods, confirming superior color disentanglement. It also dramatically reduces Chamfer Distance for color accuracy (CD-A: 73.9/4.98) and completeness (CD-C: 15.8/4.87) compared to all baselines, while maintaining FID on par with the original Stable Diffusion (69.4 vs. 72.6 on in-the-wild conditions). The zero-shot variant achieves even lower CD scores (35.2/2.68 CD-A; 3.19/3.93 CD-C) at the cost of higher FID (104/77.9) and 3.52× inference overhead. Inference cost for the fine-tuned version is only 1.03× that of the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The fine-tuned color-aligned model outperforms all four reference-based baselines (IP-Adapter, T2I-Adapter, Style-Aligned, ControlNet-Color) on every color-fidelity metric: CD-A is reduced by 51–71% (e.g., 73.9 vs. 145 for IP-Adapter on manual conditions) and CD-C by 65–85% (e.g., 15.8 vs. 106 for IP-Adapter). It also achieves the highest CLIP-Score (29.0/27.4), surpassing the next-best baseline (T2I-Adapter at 24.3/24.9) by roughly 4–5 points, confirming that colors are successfully disentangled from their original spatial layout. Against the unconditioned Stable Diffusion baseline, the fine-tuned model trades a modest FID increase on manual conditions (86.7 vs. 72.6) for a 2.2× reduction in CD-A (73.9 vs. 166) and 4.1× reduction in CD-C (15.8 vs. 65.3), while actually improving FID on in-the-wild conditions (69.4 vs. 72.6). The zero-shot variant achieves the lowest CD scores overall but at 3.52× inference cost and noticeably higher FID, making it a quality-vs-convenience trade-off. Inference cost for the fine-tuned version (1.03×) is nearly identical to the base model, whereas Style-Aligned requires 5.69× and the other baselines 1.44–1.78×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM and Stable Diffusion (latent diffusion) frameworks, extending them with a color alignment operation applied to intermediate diffusion samples. It departs from prior reference-based conditional diffusion methods (IP-Adapter, T2I-Adapter, Style-Aligned, ControlNet) by explicitly removing spatial information from color references via pixel permutation, thereby achieving color disentanglement that those methods cannot. The zero-shot variant, which applies a one-to-one nearest-neighbor color mapping to the predicted x̂₀ without any retraining, is a novel training-free extension that has no direct counterpart in the sample-altering diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, low-overhead mechanism for precise color control in diffusion-based image generation, directly applicable to creative design and graphic production workflows. The zero-shot variant's 3.52× inference cost and reduced fine-grained detail (blurry lighting, missing shadows) are notable practical limitations, and the paper acknowledges that the influence of spatial information in color conditions remains unexplored. Extending color alignment to video and 3D generation, potentially with multi-view consistency, is identified as a promising but open direction.
