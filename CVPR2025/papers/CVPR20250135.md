## Problem

Existing LiDAR simulators for adverse weather (snow, fog, rain) rely on single non-learnable physical models per condition, producing data with poor fidelity due to incomplete sensor knowledge and complex optical propagation. No unified generative framework exists that can learn from real-world data to produce high-fidelity, diverse-weather LiDAR point clouds, and the scarcity of such data (only 200–3,000 frames per weather type) makes direct training infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes clear-weather LiDAR scans converted to range maps (where rows correspond to beam circles and columns to central rays), a weather text prompt encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, and a stylized LiDAR range map as the control signal. It outputs a denoised range map that can be back-projected into a 3D LiDAR point cloud with intensity or a BEV occupancy grid. The range-map representation is central: it preserves the annular LiDAR geometry as a regular 2D grid, enabling the spider mamba scan to operate along physically meaningful axes rather than arbitrary image patches.

## Method

WeatherGen is a DDPM-based diffusion framework whose denoising backbone is a U-shaped Spider Mamba Generator (SMG) that scans features at the point level along LiDAR beam circles (rows) and central rays (columns) of the range map, preserving the physical structure that convolution (local) and self-attention (global) disrupt. A Map-based Data Producer (MDP) generates diverse-weather training data via a learnable map-to-map transformation combining Bernoulli-distribution point dropping, distance-dependent attenuation masks, and a learned residual mask, enabling pre-training on abundant synthetic data before fine-tuning on scarce real data. A Latent Feature Aligner (LFA) projects one-step generated and real range maps into a shared Gaussian latent space and minimizes KL divergence to transfer real-world distributional knowledge. A Contrastive Learning-based Controller (CLC) uses an information-bottleneck objective with CLIP text embeddings to compress the weather control signal into compact, discriminative semantic knowledge that is channel-concatenated into the SMG at each timestep.

## Results

On KITTI-360 unconditional generation, WeatherGen achieves FPD 6.15, FRD 138.62, MMD 0.39×10⁻⁴, and JSD 1.99×10⁻², outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline R2DM (FRD 149.66, MMD 1.91, JSD 3.05) and Text2LiDAR (FRD 164.16, MMD 0.49, JSD 2.01) on three of four metrics. On the Seeing Through Fog weather-conditioned task, WeatherGen substantially outperforms simulation-based methods: for snow, FPD drops from 106.37 (LSS) to 59.28; for rain, FPD drops from 301.11 (LISA) to 86.40; and for fog, JSD improves from 3.69 (FSRL) to 2.66. In the downstream 3D detection task, using only 7.4% mini-weather data yields up to +7.77 AP for cyclists in heavy snow and +5.73 AP for pedestrians in dense fog over the no-augmentation baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GAN/VAE baselines (LiDARGAN, LiDARVAE, ProjectedGAN), WeatherGen dominates by a wide margin on all shared metrics (e.g., FRD 138.62 vs. 2117–3004). Among diffusion-based methods, WeatherGen beats R2DM on FRD (138.62 vs. 149.66), MMD (0.39 vs. 1.91), and JSD (1.99 vs. 3.05), while R2DM edges ahead on FPD (6.24 vs. 6.15 — actually WeatherGen is slightly better at 6.15). Text2LiDAR achieves the best FPD (4.81) but WeatherGen surpasses it on FRD (138.62 vs. 164.16), MMD (0.39 vs. 0.49), and JSD (1.99 vs. 2.01), while also using fewer parameters and generating faster. For weather-conditioned generation, WeatherGen outperforms all three simulation baselines (LSS, FSRL, LISA) on every metric for snow and rain, and on FRD/MMD/JSD for fog (FPD is close: 314.14 vs. 319.32 for FSRL). In the 3D detection downstream task, WeatherGen's mini-weather data augmentation beats DROR, LISA, FSRL, and LSS on 18 of 21 detection metrics across dense fog, heavy snow, and rain splits, with the largest gains in cyclist AP (+7.77 in snow) and pedestrian AP (+5.73 in fog).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LiDAR diffusion generation (R2DM, LiDM, Text2LiDAR) and state-space sequence modeling (Mamba, Vision Mamba), introducing a 'spider mamba' scan that traverses range-map rows and columns to mirror LiDAR beam circles and central rays—a structural departure from the patch-level scanning of Vision Mamba and the local/global operations of convolution and self-attention used in prior LiDAR generators. It also departs from the non-learnable, single-weather simulators (DROR, FSRL, LSS, LISA) by learning weather degradation parameters end-to-end via a map-based data producer and a contrastive learning controller anchored to CLIP text embeddings. The DDPM training paradigm and the U-Net denoising backbone are inherited directly from R2DM and LiDM, while the point-level (non-patch) scanning follows UltraLight VM-UNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WeatherGen addresses a genuine bottleneck: adverse-weather LiDAR data is extremely expensive to collect (years for ~1,000 frames), and prior simulators produce low-fidelity, single-weather outputs. The unified framework and the constructed mini-weather dataset (256 frames per weather) demonstrate that generated data can meaningfully boost 3D detection (up to +7.77 AP for cyclists in heavy snow) with only 7.4% of training data replaced. However, the fog-condition results are notably weaker (FPD 314 vs. FSRL's 319), suggesting the approach struggles with the most severe attenuation regime. Open questions include whether the spider mamba scan generalizes to multi-sensor or higher-resolution LiDAR, whether the CLIP-anchored controller scales beyond four discrete weather categories to continuous severity levels, and whether the pre-training-plus-fine-tuning recipe remains effective as real-world adverse-weather data grows.
