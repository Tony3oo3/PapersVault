## Problem

Existing OCT-to-OCTA 3D translation methods learn the mapping in continuous, infinite space guided by only a single view (the 2D OCTA projection map), leading to suboptimal translation quality and dependence on expensive pre-trained vascular segmentation models. A vanilla VQVAE applied to this task suffers from low codebook utilization due to the large domain gap between OCT and OCTA, making it difficult to learn an informative discrete representation for the translation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250928/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D OCT volume as input and produces a 3D OCTA volume as output. During training, it additionally consumes 2D OCTA projection maps (obtained by averaging pixel values along the depth dimension of the 3D OCTA volume) as a structural guidance signal. Evaluated volumes range from 256×256×256 (OCTA2024) to 400×400×640 (OCTA-6M), with corresponding projection maps of 256×256 to 400×400.

## Method

MuTri is a two-stage VQVAE framework. In Stage 1, two separate VQVAEs are pre-trained to reconstruct 3D OCT and 3D OCTA volumes, providing domain-specific semantic priors. In Stage 2, a translation VQVAE (encoder → codebook → decoder) maps OCT to OCTA in discrete, finite space, guided by a multi-view tri-alignment loss: (i) contrastive-inspired semantic alignment maximizes mutual information between the translation model's unquantized features and the pre-trained OCT model, and between its quantized features and the pre-trained OCTA model, using patch-level InfoNCE-style losses to encourage the codebook to explore more codewords; (ii) vessel structure alignment enforces patch-level cosine-similarity consistency between the translation model's predicted 2D OCTA projection map and the pre-trained OCTA model's reconstruction, capturing vessel continuity. A theoretical guarantee shows that minimizing the contrastive loss upper-bounds the mutual information between the two feature spaces.

## Results

On OCTA-3M, MuTri achieves MAE 0.0707, PSNR 34.10 dB, and SSIM 89.86%, outperforming the strongest baseline TransPro (MAE 0.0782, PSNR 32.56 dB, SSIM 88.22%) by 0.75% in MAE, 1.54 dB in PSNR, and 1.64% in SSIM. On OCTA-6M, MuTri reaches MAE 0.0741, PSNR 33.08 dB, and SSIM 90.04%, improving over TransPro by 2.55 dB in PSNR. On the newly introduced OCTA2024 dataset, MuTri achieves MAE 0.0828, PSNR 43.38 dB, and SSIM 79.66% on OCTA volumes, and MAE 0.00870, PSNR 39.65 dB, and SSIM 90.31% on projection maps, surpassing all baselines by a large margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuTri outperforms all six baselines across all three metrics on both OCTA-3M and OCTA-6M. The closest competitor is TransPro [25], which uses single-view guidance from a pre-trained 2D projection map translation model plus a vascular segmentation model; MuTri improves over it by 1.54 dB (PSNR) on OCTA-3M and 2.55 dB on OCTA-6M, attributed to multi-view tri-alignment in discrete space versus TransPro's continuous-space GAN approach. Among GAN-based methods, Pix2Pix3D [16] and AdjacentGAN [24] trail MuTri by roughly 2.5–4.5 dB in PSNR, while the diffusion-based Palette [34] is competitive but still 1.68 dB below MuTri on OCTA-3M PSNR. The VQ-based VQ-I2I [6] is the nearest method in architecture family but lacks the multi-view alignment mechanism, resulting in a 2.38 dB PSNR gap on OCTA-3M. On the new OCTA2024 dataset (Table 2), the gap widens further: MuTri achieves 43.38 dB PSNR on volumes versus TransPro's 42.69 dB, and 39.65 dB on projection maps versus TransPro's 37.85 dB, suggesting the multi-view pre-training strategy scales better with dataset size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in OCT-to-OCTA translation, where prior methods (Pix2Pix, AdjacentGAN, TransPro) learn the mapping in continuous, infinite feature space, by instead formulating the translation as a vector-quantization problem in discrete, finite codebook space. It builds on the VQVAE architecture (VQ-I2I, Taming Transformers) and contrastive learning (CUT, MoCo) but repurposes both for a specific medical imaging task: contrastive-inspired semantic alignment is applied not for feature representation learning but to drive codebook exploration across the OCT–OCTA domain gap. The multi-view tri-alignment strategy (3D OCT, 3D OCTA, 2D OCTA projection map) is a novel combination that no prior OCTA translation method employs, and the vessel structure alignment via patch-level cosine similarity on projection maps is a new loss design specific to this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of OCTA2024 (846 paired OCT/OCTA volumes) as the first large-scale 3D OCT-to-OCTA translation benchmark is a practical contribution that will enable fairer comparisons in this under-resourced area. The discrete-space formulation with multi-view guidance offers a template that the authors explicitly suggest could transfer to other 3D medical translation tasks (e.g., CT-to-MRI), though this remains untested. Limitations include the continued reliance on paired training data, the observation that fine vessel details retain 'some ambiguity' in translated outputs, and the fact that the largest performance margins appear on the authors' own dataset, making cross-dataset generalization less certain.
