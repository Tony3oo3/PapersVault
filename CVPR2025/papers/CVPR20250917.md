## Problem

State-of-the-art table tennis robot systems struggle against fast-paced rallies because they cannot anticipate an opponent's future intent before the ball is struck. Prior anticipation approaches were constrained by small in-house datasets (fewer than 1000 exchanges), making it difficult to learn the rich dynamics of expert-level gameplay. This paper addresses the data bottleneck by building a scalable pipeline to reconstruct 3D gameplay from publicly available monocular video, yielding a dataset of 73,222 exchanges from professional matches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250917/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The reconstruction pipeline ingests 640×360 monocular video under the assumption of a fixed camera with translation along y/z and small rotation about x, and outputs per-frame 3D SMPL meshes for both players and a 3D ball trajectory in a world frame anchored to the table. The prediction model consumes a sequence of reconstructed frames (opponent SMPL joint positions, ego root position, ball position) tokenized as concatenated vectors, and produces an autoregressive forecast of future ball positions wrapped in axis-wise conformal confidence intervals that form a 3D confidence region with a formal coverage guarantee of at least 1−3α.

## Method

The reconstruction system chains YOLOv8 segmentation (table, rackets), a custom UNet for six table key points, HMR 2.0 for 3D player pose, and a fine-tuned TrackNetV3 for 2D ball tracking, then performs camera calibration from 10 image points derived from table geometry and reconstructs the ball trajectory by fitting a Stokes-drag projectile model between hit and bounce points via reprojection-error minimization. For anticipation, a decoder-only transformer (4 layers, 256-dim embeddings, 16 heads, ~3.2M parameters) is trained autoregressively with a Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss on tokenized sequences of reconstructed frames. An ensemble of five transformers trained on disjoint data subsets provides epistemic uncertainty, which is combined with conformal prediction on a held-out calibration set to yield prediction intervals with finite-sample coverage guarantees. The resulting confidence regions drive a pre-positioning controller that selects a target point (with shrinkage toward table center) the robot reaches before the opponent strikes, improving simulated ball return rate from 49.9% to 59.0%.

## Results

The anticipatory pre-positioning strategy improves the robot's ball return rate from 49.9% (baseline, no pre-positioning) to 59.0%, closing 78% of the gap to the oracle pre-positioning upper bound of 64.5%. Return accuracy also improves from 0.497 m to 0.463 m, and pose accuracy improves from 0.25 m / 13.3° to 0.19 m / 9.86°. Conformal prediction coverage closely matches theoretical guarantees (e.g., 0.885–0.906 empirical vs. 0.90 theoretical at α=0.10 per axis), and the 3D confidence region achieves 0.763 coverage versus the 0.70 union-bound lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external published methods but instead evaluates against two internal baselines: a non-anticipatory policy (robot stays at center until the ball is struck) and an oracle policy (robot receives the ground-truth future ball trajectory). The anticipatory method outperforms the non-anticipatory baseline by 9.1 percentage points in ball return rate (59.0% vs. 49.9%) and reduces return accuracy error by 0.034 m. Against the oracle upper bound (64.5%), the anticipatory approach recovers approximately 78% of the achievable improvement, indicating that the transformer-based prediction with conformal uncertainty captures most of the useful anticipatory signal without requiring perfect foresight. The oracle's remaining 5.5-point advantage over the anticipatory method likely reflects residual prediction error and the conservative nature of the conformal confidence regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several pre-trained vision models (YOLOv8, HMR 2.0, TrackNetV3, UNet) into a novel monocular 3D reconstruction pipeline for table tennis, a task previously addressed only with multi-camera or RGB-D setups. For the anticipation component, it departs from prior table tennis robotics work (Wang et al. using IDDM, Wu et al. using LSTM) by replacing small in-house datasets (<1000 exchanges) with a 73K-exchange dataset of professional gameplay and by adopting a decoder-only transformer trained via next-token prediction (following Radosavovic et al.) combined with conformal prediction for calibrated uncertainty. The reconstruction itself draws on established techniques—projectile models with Stokes drag for ball trajectories and SMPL for player poses—but is the first to combine them at scale from open-world monocular video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is the largest publicly referenced table tennis dataset (73,222 exchanges of professional human-human play), which removes the data bottleneck that constrained prior anticipation research. However, the validation is simulation-only (MuJoCo with a KUKA arm), reconstruction errors are substantial (8.9 cm for the ball, 28 cm for player joints), and the dataset has acknowledged biases (missing final segments, right-side hitting preference). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to physical hardware, how to handle the missing winning-move segments, and whether the conformal uncertainty regions provide actionable information beyond simple ensemble variance in more complex controller settings.
