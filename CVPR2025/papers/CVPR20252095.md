## Problem

Existing model diagnosis methods rely on human-specified linguistic attributes or pre-defined classifiers to generate counterfactual examples, leaving implicit causal attributes—such as image style, lighting patterns, or background characteristics that resist natural-language description—undiscovered and untestable. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an automated framework that discovers both linguistic and implicit causal attributes from model errors without user intervention, and provides an editing mechanism capable of manipulating implicit attributes for counterfactual hypothesis testing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252095/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a pre-trained classification model F, a validation set X (from which error cases are extracted), and a training set. The Attribute Discovery Model (Claude-3-Sonnet) groups error images into P implicit attribute clusters and predicts Q linguistic attributes, forming a guidance list of P+Q prompts. The editing model then generates P+Q counterfactual validation sets for hypothesis testing, and upon identifying verified causal attributes, synthesizes counterfactual training samples that replace up to 50% of the original training data (split equally among selected attributes) before retraining the classifier.

## Method

The core novelty is a soft token learning strategy that converts implicit image-group patterns into conditioning-friendly text embeddings: learnable tokens are injected after a base prompt and optimized via a CLIP-based direction loss (aligning pairwise prompt differences with pairwise image-embedding differences across groups) plus a regularization loss to prevent language drift. The editing model builds on DiffAE, using P+Q ResNet1D mapping networks to transform the semantic embedding z_sem into attribute-specific edited embeddings z_edit, which condition a frozen U-Net during denoising. Training combines four losses—direction (edit success), distance (image fidelity), classification (label consistency), and diffusion (image realism)—to simultaneously optimize all mapping networks. Causality is verified by measuring accuracy drops on counterfactual validation sets relative to the original, and verified attributes drive targeted synthesis of training samples for model correction.

## Results

On the face anti-spoofing task (MSU-MFSD test set), MDC-Top2 reduces HTER from 6.19% (original) to 2.46% with CLIP ViT-B/16, a 62.01% relative improvement over the original model, while boosting TPR@FPR=1% from 50.48% to 93.33%. On Tiny ImageNet general classification, MDC-Top1 achieves 93.75% top-1 accuracy with ViT-L/14 versus 91.22% original (3.46% relative improvement). For editing quality, MDC achieves the lowest FID scores across all three domains (78.24 on Stanford Dogs, 55.13 on CUB200, 40.81 on Idiap) and the highest Unchanged Ratio (88.16%, 85.75%, 90.12%), outperforming all language-guided editing baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LANCE [55], the primary model-diagnosis baseline, MDC achieves substantially larger gains: on MSU-MFSD, MDC-Top2 reduces HTER to 2.46% versus LANCE-Top2's 5.74% (a 57% relative reduction over LANCE), and on Tiny ImageNet, MDC-Top1 reaches 93.75% top-1 accuracy versus LANCE-Top1's 91.74%. The gap is most pronounced in the face security domain, where MDC's ability to discover implicit causal attributes (e.g., 'orange hue style', 'slight shadow') that LANCE's language-guided approach cannot capture leads to dramatically better correction. For editing quality (Table 4), MDC outperforms InstructPix2Pix [8], LEDITS [67], CycleDiffusion [76], and FreePromptEditing [46] on FID by 14–40 points across domains, confirming superior implicit attribute simulation. On CLIP Score for linguistic attribute editing, MDC is competitive but not always best (InstructPix2Pix edges ahead on Stanford Dogs at 20.03 vs. 19.94), while MDC dominates on FID and Unchanged Ratio, indicating better preservation of unrelated attributes during editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model diagnosis paradigm established by LANCE and ZOOM, which rely on user-specified or pre-defined linguistic attributes, by introducing autonomous discovery of both linguistic and implicit (non-linguistic) causal attributes via MLLM-based grouping and unsupervised clustering. The editing model builds directly on DiffAE's semantic encoder and forward process, and adopts the direction loss from StyleGAN-NADA to align learned soft tokens with image group embeddings. The key departure is the soft token learning strategy that converts implicit image-group patterns into text-embedding-compatible conditioning signals, enabling a single diffusion-based editing model to handle both attribute types without requiring natural language descriptions for every causal factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to diagnose and correct model failures caused by attributes that resist natural language description (e.g., overall color cast, lighting style) broadens the practical scope of model auditing in high-stakes domains like face security, where the 62% HTER reduction demonstrates tangible safety impact. However, the approach depends on MLLM quality for attribute grouping, requires expensive pre-training of the editing model on ImageNet-22K, and the autonomous discovery pipeline has not been stress-tested on highly heterogeneous or open-vocabulary error distributions. Open questions include whether the implicit attribute clustering generalizes to tasks with very large class spaces, whether the 50% data-replacement correction strategy is optimal, and whether the framework can be extended to multi-modal or video models.
