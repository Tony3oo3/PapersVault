## Problem

Traditional SfM pipelines rely on sparse keypoint matching, which limits both accuracy and point density, particularly in textureless regions. Dense matching methods overcome this but produce fragmentary pairwise tracks that are difficult to integrate into SfM; the prior workaround (quantization in DFSfM) sacrifices matching accuracy and point cloud completeness. The paper asks how to harness dense matching for SfM while preserving subpixel accuracy and generating long, consistent multi-view tracks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250926/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a set of multi-view RGB images of a scene and produces refined camera poses (extrinsics and intrinsics) together with a dense 3D point cloud. When camera poses are not provided, the system first recovers coarse poses via quantized incremental mapping before switching to raw dense matches for triangulation. The refinement module operates on local image patches (15×15 from CNN feature maps) centered at each track keypoint, with a 7×7 search boundary for subpixel coordinate regression.

## Method

Dense-SfM is a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 builds an initial SfM model from dense two-view matches (DKM or RoMa) filtered by bidirectional mutual verification. Stage 2 extends track length via Gaussian Splatting: SfM points are initialized as small 3D Gaussians, the GS model is optimized and densified while keeping initial Gaussian positions fixed, and visibility of each 3D point from every camera is determined by ray-based alpha compositing, projecting visible points onto new images to lengthen tracks. Stage 3 performs iterative refinement with a multi-view kernelized matching module that fuses two paths—a multi-view transformer (self/cross-attention on local feature patches) and a Gaussian Process (exponential cosine-similarity kernel over Fourier positional encodings)—into a CNN decoder that outputs per-pixel coordinate probability distributions and learned confidence scores; refined tracks are then optimized jointly with poses via geometric bundle adjustment over two iterations. The training loss jointly regresses subpixel coordinates and confidence, penalizing underconfidence, distinguishing it from prior variance-based uncertainty approaches.

## Results

On ETH3D, RoMa+Ours achieves the best multi-view pose accuracy with AUC@1°=60.92, AUC@3°=78.41, and AUC@5°=82.63, outperforming the strongest baseline LoFTR+DFSfM (59.12/75.59/79.53) by 1.8/2.8/3.1 points respectively. On the Texture-Poor SfM dataset, RoMa+Ours leads with AUC@3°=49.94 and AUC@10°=81.41, a substantial margin over LoFTR+DFSfM (26.07/45.43). For 3D triangulation on ETH3D, RoMa+Ours reaches 84.79% accuracy at 1 cm and 36.35% completeness at 5 cm, surpassing RoMa+DFSfM (79.32%/29.10%) and LoFTR+DFSfM (80.38%/29.54%) on both metrics simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMa+Ours outperforms the strongest semi-dense baseline LoFTR+DFSfM on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest gains on Texture-Poor SfM (AUC@3°: 49.94 vs 26.07, a 23.9-point improvement) where dense matching excels in low-texture regions. Against the same dense matcher with DFSfM's quantization-based refinement (RoMa+DFSfM), RoMa+Ours gains 2.95 points on ETH3D AUC@1° and 12.4 points on Texture-Poor AUC@3°, confirming that GS-based track extension avoids the accuracy loss inherent to quantization. On IMC 2021, RoMa+Ours is competitive with VGGSfM (73.90 vs 73.92 at AUC@10°) and DKM+Ours (74.37), where the paper notes that GS track extension is challenging due to occlusion and appearance changes, so a quantization fallback is used. Against point-based MASt3R-SfM, RoMa+Ours dominates on ETH3D (60.92 vs 35.85 at AUC@1°) and Texture-Poor (49.94 vs 43.46 at AUC@3°), but MASt3R-SfM is more competitive on IMC (67.97 vs 73.90 at AUC@10°). For 3D triangulation (Table 1), RoMa+Ours achieves the best accuracy at all thresholds (84.79% at 1 cm) while also maintaining the highest completeness (36.35% at 5 cm), breaking the typical accuracy–completeness trade-off seen in baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the detector-free SfM line initiated by DFSfM (CVPR 2024), which addressed fragmented tracks from pairwise dense matching via quantization. The key departure is replacing quantization with a Gaussian Splatting-based visibility filter for track extension, preserving subpixel matching accuracy while still producing longer multi-view tracks. The refinement module builds on DFSfM's multi-view transformer but augments it with a Gaussian Process path (inheriting the exponential cosine kernel from DKM) and replaces statistical uncertainty with a learned per-track confidence score, trained with a novel confidence-weighted regression loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that dense matching can be made practical for full SfM pipelines without the accuracy penalty of quantization, opening the door to reliable reconstruction in texture-poor scenes that have been a persistent failure mode for detector-based methods. The repurposing of Gaussian Splatting as a geometric visibility oracle (rather than a rendering tool) is a creative cross-pollination that could inspire similar uses in other geometry pipelines. Limitations include the additional GS training cost, the need to fall back to quantization on the IMC dataset due to occlusion/appearance changes, and evaluation limited to relatively small scene sets (13–22 scenes per dataset), leaving open questions about scalability to large-scale or outdoor SfM.
