## Problem

Existing federated learning frameworks lack mechanisms for auditable data valuation and verifiable client contributions that operate independently of a trusted central server. Reliance on server-side validation or post-training anomaly detection creates single points of failure, limits transparency in how client data is assessed, and fails to distinguish high-impact contributions from those generated by poisoned or low-quality data before training begins.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252299/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client consumes its local image shard (medical or natural images, l2-normalized to unit norm), the current global model parameters, and the server-maintained global density function parameters (mean vector and covariance matrix). The method produces FAVD-valuated clean data used for local training, verified gradient updates (θ_k^t − θ_g^t), and noise-perturbed local density parameters (μ̃_k, Σ̃_k) sent back to the server. Notably, valuation occurs pre-training on a low-dimensional representation extracted from the model's output layer rather than on raw pixel data, and the shared density parameters are obfuscated with additive Gaussian noise to prevent data leakage.

## Method

FAVD performs proactive, pre-training data valuation by having each client compute a low-dimensional representation ζ(X_k) from the local model's output layer, apply K-means clustering (with early stopping) to obtain cluster means and covariances, and then score each sample via Mahalanobis distance to the server-aggregated global density function ρ_g. Samples whose distance exceeds an auditing threshold γ are discarded as anomalous (potentially poisoned), while the remainder form the FAVD-valuated dataset D_k^F used for local training. To preserve privacy, Gaussian noise (μ_N, Σ_N) is added to each client's local density parameters before transmission, so the server only ever sees masked statistics. The server aggregates these masked parameters into an updated global density function each round, enabling the next round's valuation. The framework is theoretically grounded with convergence bounds under cross-Lipschitz continuity, a verifiability condition ensuring FAVD-audited updates reduce aggregate loss, and an ι-separability guarantee that the valued data space remains disjoint from ω-bounded adversarial perturbations.

## Results

Under 50% maliciousness (M-SimBA attack), FAVD achieves the lowest utility degradation (U) across all five datasets in both uniform and non-IID settings. On CIFAR-10 (uniform), FAVD limits U to 4.08% versus 64.89% for vanilla FL and 6.38% for the next-best method FedBary; on CIFAR-100 (uniform), FAVD achieves 5.93% vs. 60.24% (No Val) and 6.04% (FedBary). In benign (no-threat) settings, FAVD improves global test accuracy by ~2–3% over vanilla FL on uniform data and ~3–4% on non-IID data (e.g., 93.75% vs. 91.37% on ChestX-ray, 88.42% vs. 85.59% on CIFAR-10). FAVD also achieves the best Malicious Sample Detection Rate (MSDR = 0.90 at 30% maliciousness) and lowest Benign Misclassification Rate (BMR = 0.17) on CIFAR-10, outperforming FedBary (MSDR = 0.88, BMR = 0.19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAVD outperforms all eight baselines on the primary metric (utility degradation U) across all five datasets in both uniform and non-IID settings at 50% maliciousness. The closest competitor is FedBary [31], which FAVD beats by 1.3–2.3 percentage points on uniform settings (e.g., 4.08% vs. 6.38% on CIFAR-10, 5.93% vs. 6.04% on CIFAR-100) and by 0.6–2.3 points on non-IID. Server-side aggregation methods (Krum, TM, Median) and FLTrust perform substantially worse, with U values 10–75 percentage points higher than FAVD, confirming that post-hoc update filtering is less effective than pre-training data valuation. zPROBE and FedCE are competitive on smaller datasets (Camelyon17, HAM10000) but trail FAVD by 1–2 points on larger-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100). On the auditability metrics (Table 5), FAVD achieves MSDR of 0.90/0.86 and BMR of 0.17/0.24 at 30%/50% maliciousness on CIFAR-10, surpassing FedBary (0.88/0.82 MSDR, 0.19/0.27 BMR), while other baselines do not report these metrics as they focus on update-level rather than sample-level detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying two previously separate concerns in FL security—data valuation (as in FedBary, Shapley-based methods) and verifiable client contributions (as in blockchain-based or Byzantine-robust aggregation methods like Krum, TM, FLTrust)—into a single pre-training, client-side audit mechanism. Rather than relying on post-training anomaly detection at the server or on Shapley-value-based valuation that assumes clean data, FAVD repurposes classical anomaly-detection tools (K-means clustering and Mahalanobis distance) in a low-dimensional feature space to perform auditable data valuation before local training begins. The addition of Gaussian noise to shared density parameters is a lightweight privacy mechanism distinct from differential privacy or secure multi-party computation approaches used in prior FL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAVD addresses a practical gap in FL deployments where organizations need to verify data quality and client integrity without exposing raw data or relying on a trusted central authority for valuation. The proactive (pre-training) design is a meaningful architectural shift from the dominant reactive (post-training) paradigm, potentially reducing the window of vulnerability. However, the paper acknowledges that benign-data exploitation and adaptive attackers who mimic legitimate distributions remain open threats, and the theoretical analysis is framed as 'possible' rather than rigorous, leaving the convergence and separability guarantees somewhat informal. The modest performance margins over FedBary (e.g., MSDR 0.90 vs. 0.88) suggest the practical advantage lies more in the unified auditability/verifiability framework than in raw detection accuracy.
