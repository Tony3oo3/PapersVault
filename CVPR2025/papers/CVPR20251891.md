## Problem

LiDAR point clouds are extremely sparse, with most of the 3D volume empty, which breaks standard masked autoencoder approaches: passing masked voxel positions to the decoder leaks occupancy information, while reconstructing the full scene is computationally prohibitive and forces existing methods to operate only on 2D bird's-eye-view grids or coarse 3D reconstructions. This limits self-supervised pre-training to architectures that cannot exploit modern high-resolution 3D sparse transformers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251891/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw LiDAR point clouds, voxelizes them at 0.05 m resolution, and feeds only the visible (non-masked) occupied voxels into a sparse PTv3 encoder, producing hierarchical features at four scales (0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40 m). The decoders output binary occupancy predictions restricted to a local neighborhood (default 9×9×9 voxels) around each visible voxel rather than the full scene volume. Downstream, the frozen or fine-tuned encoder representations are attached to task heads for semantic segmentation (per-voxel class labels) and 3D object detection (bounding boxes with orientation).

## Method

NOMAE's core mechanism is neighborhood-limited occupancy reconstruction: instead of reconstructing the entire 3D volume or passing masked positions to the decoder, it predicts binary occupancy only within a fixed neighborhood of visible voxels, eliminating both information leakage and the cost of dense reconstruction. A token upsampling module (one lightweight PTv3 block per scale) fuses coarse-to-fine features so each scale's decoder receives multi-scale context. A Hierarchical Mask Generator masks the coarsest scale first and then progressively masks visible voxels at finer scales, guaranteeing cross-scale consistency while maintaining adequate masking ratios at every level. Separate sparse-convolution decoders with scale-dependent neighborhood sizes are trained simultaneously (Multi-Scale Pretext) with a per-scale BCE loss averaged over all scales, encouraging coarse features to encode large-area context and fine features to capture local detail. The entire pipeline operates on sparse voxel representations, keeping computation tractable at high resolutions and enabling direct use with modern 3D transformer backbones.

## Results

On nuScenes semantic segmentation, NOMAE+PTv3 achieves 81.8% val mIoU and 87.7% val mAcc, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses the fully supervised PTv3 baseline (80.4% mIoU) by 1.4 points and the best SSL method (UniPAD, 79.4%) by 2.4 points. On the Waymo Open Dataset, NOMAE reaches 72.3% val mIoU and 70.3% test mIoU, outperforming the PTv3 baseline by 1.0 mIoU and establishing a new SOTA on the single-frame segmentation challenge. For 3D object detection on nuScenes with only 20% labeled data, NOMAE achieves 60.9 NDS and 54.4 mAP, improving by 14.2 NDS and 15.4 mAP over the UVTR-L baseline and by 5.1 NDS over the closest SSL competitor (UniPAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOMAE+PTv3 outperforms all supervised baselines on nuScenes val mIoU: it beats PTv3 (80.4) by 1.4, PTv2 (80.2) by 1.6, SphereFormer (78.4) by 3.4, and Cylinder3D (76.1) by 5.7. Among SSL methods, NOMAE surpasses Occupancy-MAE+PTv3 (80.0) by 1.8 mIoU, GEO-MAE+PTv3 (78.9) by 2.9, and UniPAD (79.4) by 2.4, establishing that neighborhood-limited multi-scale occupancy reconstruction yields richer representations than whole-scene or single-scale approaches. On Waymo, NOMAE improves over PTv3 by 1.0 mIoU (72.3 vs 71.3) and 2.0 mAcc (82.5 vs 80.5), and sets a new test-set SOTA at 70.3 mIoU versus MinkUNet's 69.8. For object detection with 20% labeled data, NOMAE (60.9 NDS / 54.4 mAP) dominates all SSL baselines: +5.1 NDS over UniPAD (55.8), +11.7 over Learning-from-2D (49.2), +12.1 over GD-MAE (48.8), and +12.7 over ALSO (48.2). The NonLP ablation (Table 3) further confirms the representation quality gap: NOMAE reaches 74.8 mIoU with a 39.8 ms iteration time, versus 59.0 for Occupancy-MAE (74.0 ms) and 53.8 for GEO-MAE (40.1 ms), showing both superior accuracy and lower per-step cost despite multi-scale supervision at 0.05 m resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

NOMAE builds directly on Occupancy-MAE's formulation of reconstructing voxel occupancy as a self-supervised pretext task, but departs fundamentally by restricting reconstruction to the local neighborhood of visible (unmasked) voxels rather than the entire 3D volume. This localized approach is motivated by the empirical observation that LiDAR points cluster on object surfaces, and it eliminates the information leakage problem that plagued earlier methods (GeoMAE, GD-MAE, UniPAD) which either leaked occupancy signals to the decoder or required expensive full-scene reconstruction. The multi-scale pretext with separate decoder instances at each scale and the hierarchical mask generation scheme extend the single-scale masking strategies of Point-M2AE and GeoMAE into a consistent multi-scale framework that preserves masking ratios across resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NOMAE is the first SSL pretraining method to match or exceed strong supervised baselines (PTv3) on both nuScenes and Waymo Open semantic segmentation, and it achieves a substantial 14.2 NDS gain over the UVTR-L baseline in low-data object detection, demonstrating that efficient sparse SSL can close the gap between pretraining and supervised training for 3D perception. The neighborhood-reconstruction principle is broadly applicable and unblocks SSL pretraining for modern high-resolution 3D transformer architectures that were previously infeasible due to computational cost. Open questions remain: the method's sensitivity to point cloud density limits its applicability to sparser sensors like radar, temporal information is unused, and 2D BEV representations still dominate in detection, suggesting further gains are possible with fully 3D sparse encoders.
