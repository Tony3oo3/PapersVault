## Problem

Existing semi-supervised learning methods in the X-Match family rely solely on model confidence to select unlabeled data for training, which becomes unreliable when labeled data is extremely scarce (e.g., 4–25 per class). This leads to noisy training and poor generalization because confidence alone cannot accurately assess the model's learning state or distinguish genuinely useful low-confidence samples from truly uninformative ones during early training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251785/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled RGB images (4, 10, or 25 per class on CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, STL-10) and a large pool of unlabeled images, producing a single class label per input. A key operational detail is that each unlabeled sample maintains a rolling queue of per-class prediction counts over the last 1000 iterations, which is used to compute the Count-Gap metric at every training step. The model is a Wide ResNet (WRN-28-2 or WRN-VAR-37-2) trained with SGD and cosine LR decay over 2^20 iterations.

## Method

CGMatch introduces a Count-Gap (CG) metric that quantifies the difference between the most and second-most frequent pseudo-label predictions for each unlabeled sample over recent iterations, providing a complementary signal to confidence for assessing prediction stability. A Fine-grained Dynamic Selection (FDS) strategy combines CG and confidence with dynamically adjusted thresholds (via EMA) to partition unlabeled data into three subsets: easy-to-learn (high confidence), ambiguous (low confidence but high CG), and hard-to-learn (low confidence and low CG). Easy-to-learn samples are trained with standard cross-entropy loss on strongly-augmented views, while ambiguous samples use a noise-robust generalized cross-entropy (GCE) loss, and hard-to-learn samples are excluded. The overall framework follows the FixMatch paradigm of weak/strong augmentation with consistency regularization, but the three-way partitioning and subset-specific losses are the core novelty, enabling higher unlabeled-data utilization without the negative impact of incorrect pseudo-labels.

## Results

CGMatch reports top-1 error rates across four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, STL-10) with 4, 10, or 25 labels per class. On CIFAR-10-4, CGMatch achieves 4.87% error, outperforming all baselines including SoftMatch (5.06%) and FreeMatch (4.97%). On CIFAR-100-4, it reaches 47.55% error, a 2.60% improvement over FlexMatch (50.15%). On SVHN-4, CGMatch attains 2.39% error, the best among all methods. The method is best in 10 out of 12 settings, losing only on STL-10-10 (16.23% vs. SoftMatch's 11.59%) and STL-10-25 (9.48% vs. SoftMatch's 8.01%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGMatch is compared against nine baselines spanning pseudo-labeling (PseudoLabel, MeanTeacher), consistency regularization (MixMatch, ReMixMatch), and the X-Match family (FixMatch, FlexMatch, SoftMatch, FreeMatch, FullFlex). CGMatch achieves the best top-1 error in 10 out of 12 settings, losing only to SoftMatch on STL-10-10 (16.23% vs. 11.59%) and STL-10-25 (9.48% vs. 8.01%), which the authors attribute to STL-10's open-set nature. Against the most recent competitors SoftMatch and FullFlex, CGMatch wins on all CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN settings, with a notable 2.60% error reduction over FlexMatch on CIFAR-100-4. On SVHN-4, CGMatch (2.39%) substantially outperforms FixMatch (3.56%) and FlexMatch (3.36%). The advantage is most pronounced in the extreme low-label regime (4 labels/class), where confidence-only methods struggle to calibrate thresholds, while Count-Gap provides a complementary signal for identifying useful unlabeled samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGMatch extends the FixMatch paradigm (weak/strong augmentation with consistency regularization and confidence thresholding) by introducing Count-Gap, a metric adapted from the data maps framework of Swayamdipta et al. that quantifies the gap between the most and second-most frequent pseudo-label predictions over training iterations. Unlike prior X-Match variants (FlexMatch, FreeMatch, SoftMatch, FullFlex) that rely exclusively on confidence—whether fixed, class-wise, or dynamically adjusted—to select unlabeled examples, CGMatch adds a second, complementary selection axis that does not require ground-truth labels. The Fine-grained Dynamic Selection strategy then routes the three resulting subsets (easy, ambiguous, hard) through tailored losses, borrowing generalized cross-entropy from the noisy-label literature to handle ambiguous samples more robustly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper targets the practically important regime of very few labels per class (4–25), where confidence-only selection is least reliable, and demonstrates that a simple count-based metric can recover useful structure in unlabeled data that confidence alone misses. However, the gains over the strongest baselines (SoftMatch, FullFlex) are modest (typically <1% error reduction), and the method underperforms on open-set benchmarks like STL10 where unseen classes inflate calibration error. Open questions include whether Count-Gap generalizes to open-set SSL, whether hard-to-learn samples can be productively incorporated rather than discarded, and whether the approach transfers to non-vision modalities or larger-scale datasets.
