## Problem

Existing repetitive action counting methods rely on handcrafted regression networks with limited representational capacity, making them unable to capture variable and non-homogeneous periodic patterns. Moreover, supervised training on narrow datasets dominated by human fitness activities causes severe overfitting, preventing generalization to out-of-domain actions such as animal movements or periodic motions of non-living entities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252418/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 32-frame video clip and a structured periodic text prompt (describing periodicity properties like temporal intervals and visual similarities) as input. The output is a standardized format [abcd, e, f], where abcd is a fixed-length four-digit decimal string (zero-padded, 0000–9999) representing the count within the clip, and e and f are binary flags indicating whether an incomplete action cycle exists at the start or end of the clip. This flag mechanism enables correct aggregation across sequentially processed clips of long videos by incrementing the video-level count only when both the trailing flag of one clip and the leading flag of the next are 1.

## Method

CountLLM formulates RAC as a video question-answering task, replacing the regression head with a pre-trained LLM (Vicuna-7B) guided by explicit periodic text prompts. The architecture comprises a pre-trained video encoder (VideoMAE or Video Swin Transformer), a 12-layer periodicity transformer with 64 learnable queries that compresses video features into periodic representations via cross-attention, a linear projector mapping these to LLM-compatible tokens, and the LLM itself. Training follows a three-stage progressive paradigm: (1) semantic-aware alignment on WebVid-10M using video-text contrastive learning to bridge the modality gap, (2) periodicity-aware alignment using binary contrastive learning against textual periodicity descriptions on RAC datasets, and (3) LoRA-based instruction tuning on the full pipeline. The key design insight is that class-agnostic periodicity descriptions in the prompt, combined with the LLM's reasoning capacity, prevent overfitting to specific action categories and enable counting of entirely unseen periodic motions.

## Results

On RepCount, CountLLM with VideoMAE achieves MAE 0.162 and OBO 0.639, surpassing the previous best (ESCounts, MAE 0.213 / OBO 0.563) by 0.077 in MAE and 0.076 in OBO. On UCFRep, CountLLM (VideoMAE) reaches MAE 0.116 and OBO 0.839, outperforming the prior best (Si&So, MAE 0.143 / OBO 0.800). On Countix, CountLLM (VideoMAE) attains MAE 0.215 and OBO 0.801, improving over ESCounts (MAE 0.276 / OBO 0.673) by 0.061 in MAE and 0.128 in OBO. In cross-dataset generalization (trained on RepCount, tested on UCFRep/Countix), CountLLM achieves OBO 0.816 and 0.730 respectively, far exceeding ESCounts (0.571 and 0.521).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CountLLM outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all four evaluation settings. On RepCount, CountLLM (VideoMAE) beats the closest competitor ESCounts by 0.051 in MAE (0.162 vs. 0.213) and 0.076 in OBO (0.639 vs. 0.563); over TransRAC the OBO gap widens to 0.348. On UCFRep, CountLLM surpasses Si&So (the prior best OBO) by 0.039 in OBO and 0.027 in MAE, and exceeds ESCounts by 0.135 in OBO. On Countix, CountLLM improves OBO by 0.128 over ESCounts (0.801 vs. 0.673). In the cross-dataset generalization setting (RepCount→UCFRep), CountLLM achieves OBO 0.816 versus ESCounts' 0.571, a margin of 0.245; on RepCount→Countix the gap is 0.209 in OBO. On the OVR-Kinetics open-vocabulary benchmark, CountLLM (MAE 0.26, OBO 0.65) outperforms OVRCounter (MAE 0.39, OBO 0.59) and RepNet (MAE 0.86, OBO 0.35), confirming strong generalization to unseen action categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established RAC paradigm of encoding video features and regressing density maps (RepNet, TransRAC, ESCounts) by replacing the regression head with a pre-trained LLM guided by explicit periodic text prompts. It builds on the video MLLM architecture pattern (video encoder → projector → LLM) seen in VideoChatGPT, VideoChat2, and ST-LLM, but introduces a dedicated periodicity transformer with cross-attention queries and a three-stage progressive training scheme (semantic-aware alignment via CLIP, periodicity-aware contrastive alignment, and LoRA instruction tuning) that no prior video MLLM employs. The structured instruction template with a fixed-length decimal answer format and incomplete-cycle flags is a novel task-specific design not present in general video QA systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatically improved cross-dataset generalization (e.g., 0.816 vs. 0.571 OBO on UCFRep when trained only on RepCount) and the ability to count repetitions in entirely out-of-domain scenarios (animal movements, clock hands, planetary orbits) where prior RAC methods fail suggest that LLM-based reasoning with explicit periodicity semantics can overcome the overfitting bottleneck of narrow supervised training. Limitations include the substantial GPU cost of a 7B LLM and the reliance on GPT-4-generated periodicity descriptions; open questions include whether lightweight LLMs or in-context learning with targeted examples can preserve these gains at lower compute, and whether the approach extends to other temporal counting or periodicity-sensitive tasks beyond RAC.
