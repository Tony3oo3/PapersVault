## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering speed but suffers from view-inconsistent popping artifacts due to incorrect depth ordering and ill-defined volume density when Gaussians overlap, making surface reconstruction non-trivial. Grid-based voxel representations inherently solve both issues but are limited to slow ray-casting rendering. The paper asks whether the rasterization efficiency of 3DGS can be combined with the well-defined volumetric properties of voxel grids.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251244/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics as input, requiring no SfM sparse point cloud prior (unlike 3DGS). Beyond novel-view rendering, the fully explicit sparse voxel grid directly supports mesh extraction via TSDF Fusion and Marching Cubes, depth/normal map rendering, and 2D-to-3D feature fusion (e.g., semantic segmentation or foundation model features) through Volume Fusion and Voxel Pooling, all without additional neural network inference.

## Method

SVRaster represents scenes as multi-level sparse voxels on an Octree layout (up to 2^16 grid resolution), where each voxel stores a trilinear density field at its 8 shared corner grid points and view-dependent color via Spherical Harmonic coefficients assumed constant within the voxel. The key rendering innovation is a direction-dependent Morton ordering: because voxels follow an Octree partition, the correct depth sort under any Octree node depends only on the sign pattern of the ray direction (8 permutations in 3D), guaranteeing correct front-to-back order and eliminating popping artifacts without per-primitive distance sorting. The rasterizer, built on 3DGS's CUDA pipeline, projects voxels to image tiles, pre-computes per-voxel colors and normals once, then performs alpha compositing with K-point sampling along ray-voxel intersections. Training uses progressive adaptive subdivision (driven by accumulated loss gradients weighted by alpha) and pruning (by maximum blending weight) every 1000 iterations, starting from a constant-initialized dense grid and growing to fit scene detail levels without any neural network or sparse-point prior.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, SVRaster achieves PSNR 27.33, LPIPS 0.185, and SSIM 0.822 at 121 FPS with 15 min training time. It attains the best LPIPS among all compared methods, surpassing 3DGS (0.216) and even Zip-NeRF (0.187), while matching 3DGS in rendering speed (121 vs. 131 FPS). On Deep Blending, SVRaster further outperforms 3DGS on all metrics (366 vs. 140 FPS, LPIPS 0.228 vs. 0.244, PSNR 29.84 vs. 29.60). For mesh reconstruction, SVRaster achieves F-score 0.40 on Tanks&Temples and Chamfer distance 0.76 on DTU, substantially outperforming 3DGS (0.19 and 1.96 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS, SVRaster wins on LPIPS (0.185 vs. 0.216) and SSIM (0.822 vs. 0.815) while trailing slightly on PSNR (27.33 vs. 27.45) and FPS (121 vs. 131); training is also faster (15 min vs. 24 min). Against Zip-NeRF, SVRaster matches LPIPS (0.185 vs. 0.187) and SSIM (0.822 vs. 0.828) but lags on PSNR (27.33 vs. 28.55), while being orders of magnitude faster (121 FPS vs. <1). Against Plenoxels and INGP, SVRaster dominates across all metrics (e.g., PSNR 27.33 vs. 23.08/25.68, FPS 121 vs. <10/~10). On Deep Blending, SVRaster clearly beats 3DGS on every metric (366 vs. 140 FPS, 0.228 vs. 0.244 LPIPS, 29.84 vs. 29.60 PSNR), but on Tanks&Temples it underperforms 3DGS in PSNR (23.04 vs. 23.75) and FPS (125 vs. 180) due to sensitivity to large exposure variation in training views. For mesh reconstruction, SVRaster substantially outperforms 3DGS on both F-score (0.40 vs. 0.19 on Tanks&Temples) and Chamfer distance (0.76 vs. 1.96 on DTU), and is competitive with SDF-based methods like NeuS and Neuralangelo while training in minutes rather than hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVRaster bridges two previously separate lines of work: explicit voxel-based radiance fields (Plenoxels, INGP) that suffer from slow ray-casting rendering, and 3D Gaussian Splatting that achieves real-time rasterization but suffers from popping artifacts and ill-defined volume due to overlapping primitives. The key departure is from both: rather than using Gaussians or neural networks, the paper stores adaptive sparse voxels in a flat 1D array (no Octree pointers, no dense pointer grid) and introduces a direction-dependent Morton ordering that guarantees correct depth ordering during rasterization. This eliminates the need for SfM sparse points (required by 3DGS) and the complex data structures used by prior sparse voxel methods (VDB, Sparse Voxel Octrees).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a fully explicit, neural-free voxel grid can match 3DGS in both rendering speed and perceptual quality (LPIPS) while offering well-defined volume for downstream tasks (mesh extraction via Marching Cubes/TSDF, 2D feature fusion via Volume Fusion) is practically significant for pipelines that need geometrically consistent outputs. Limitations include higher GPU memory usage (~2x 3DGS), sensitivity to photometric variation in training views (Tanks&Temples failure), and tendency to produce geometric bumps on textured surfaces. Open questions include whether direct SDF modeling on the sparse voxel grid can improve surface quality, whether training-free distillation from large implicit fields is feasible, and how the method scales to 3D neural network integration for understanding tasks.
