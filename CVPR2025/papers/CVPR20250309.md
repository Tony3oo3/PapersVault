## Problem

Diffusion-based facial privacy protection methods suffer from the diffusion purification effect, where the reverse denoising process interprets adversarial identity modifications as high-frequency noise and progressively removes them, resulting in low protection success rates. Additionally, prior diffusion-based approaches like DiffProtect modify semantic latent codes, causing visible structural distortions, while makeup-based methods require reference images or full retraining for each new target identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250309/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original face image and a target face image (real or synthesized) as input and produces a single protected face image that impersonates the target identity while preserving the original person's structural appearance. The original image is encoded into the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model via DDIM inversion (20 steps), and adversarial optimization begins at timestamp t=3. The output is a decoded RGB image that should be recognizable as the same individual by human observers but unidentifiable by unauthorized face recognition systems.

## Method

The framework operates entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion) in a text-free manner. In a first stage, per-timestamp unconditional embeddings are learned via DDIM inversion to serve as null-text guidance during the reverse diffusion process, which weakens the purification effect and retains fine-grained adversarial details. In a second stage, the adversarial latent code z_adv is optimized (starting from the inverted noise at t=3) to minimize cosine distance to the target identity across K surrogate face recognition models, while a self-attention alignment loss constrains the generated image's structural geometry to match the original. The key insight is that learned unconditional embeddings act as a regularizer that prevents the denoising U-Net from projecting adversarial perturbations back onto the clean data manifold, allowing identity-specific modifications to persist in the final image without requiring norm-bounded perturbations.

## Results

On the CelebA-HQ and LADN datasets, the proposed method achieves an average Protection Success Rate (PSR) of 79.17% under black-box evaluation against four FR models (IRSE50, IR152, FaceNet, MobileFaceNet), outperforming the strongest makeup-based baseline DiffAM (77.88%) and the diffusion-based DiffProtect (51.05%). In terms of visual quality, the method attains the best FID (15.32) and PSNR (27.72 dB) among all methods with competitive PSR, though it trails DiffAM (SSIM 0.886) and DiffProtect (SSIM 0.879) on SSIM. The method also demonstrates strong per-model PSR, reaching 98.17% on MobileFaceNet (LADN) and 95.78% on IRSE50 (LADN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on average PSR. Against the strongest makeup-based SOTA, DiffAM (77.88%), the gain is modest (+1.29%) but consistent across both datasets, while the method also achieves a substantially lower FID (15.32 vs. 26.10), indicating more natural-looking protected images. Compared to the prior diffusion-based method DiffProtect (51.05%), the improvement is dramatic (+28.12% PSR), attributed to weakening the diffusion purification effect via learned unconditional embeddings. Against noise-based methods (PGD 25.60%, MI-FGSM 30.63%, TI-DIM 41.64%, TIP-IM 50.06%), the method achieves 29–54 percentage-point PSR gains while also producing far more natural images (FID 15.32 vs. 28–39). The method loses on SSIM to DiffAM (0.839 vs. 0.886) and DiffProtect (0.839 vs. 0.879), suggesting slightly less pixel-level structural similarity, but compensates with superior FID and PSNR. Adv-Makeup achieves the best FID (4.23) and SSIM (0.985) but is effectively non-functional for privacy (PSR 14.72%), confirming the trade-off between minimal perturbation and protection strength.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based facial privacy protection line initiated by DiffProtect, which first showed that modifying semantic latent codes in a Diffusion Autoencoder can generate adversarial face images. The key departure is shifting from Diff-AE's semantic code manipulation to direct adversarial optimization of latent codes in a standard Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion), combined with learned unconditional embeddings adapted from null-text inversion (Mokady et al., CVPR 2023) to counteract the diffusion purification effect. For structure preservation, the paper replaces DiffProtect's L∞ norm constraint on code modification with self-attention map alignment, drawing on findings that self-attention encodes geometric structure while cross-attention encodes semantic content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification and mitigation of diffusion purification as a fundamental bottleneck for diffusion-based adversarial generation is a useful insight that could transfer to other tasks where adversarial perturbations must survive a denoising process. However, the practical gains over the strongest makeup-based baseline (DiffAM) are modest (~1.5% PSR), and the per-image optimization (20 inversion steps + 35 adversarial iterations) limits real-time applicability. Open questions include whether the null-text guidance strategy generalizes to other diffusion architectures or adversarial tasks, and whether the approach scales to multi-identity or video settings without per-frame optimization.
