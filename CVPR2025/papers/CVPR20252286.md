## Problem

Existing attention mechanisms for vision—transformers, linear attention, and state-space models like Mamba—process 2D image data as 1D sequences, sacrificing spatial coherence, while transformers suffer from quadratic complexity. Extending linear propagation to 2D introduces a fundamental stability challenge: matrix-valued propagation weights can cause exponential signal growth or rapid decay, making it difficult to balance stability with long-range receptive fields.

## Input / Output

For classification, GSPN takes 224×224 RGB images and outputs class labels. For class-conditional generation, it consumes a class label plus Gaussian noise to produce 256×256 images. For text-to-image, it takes a text prompt (embedded and inserted as visual tokens within the propagation sequence) plus noise, generating images up to 16K×8K resolution on a single A100 GPU. Notably, GSPN requires no positional embeddings and adapts to arbitrary resolutions without extra normalization, since the row-stochastic propagation weights inherently encode spatial position through the scanning order.

## Method

GSPN replaces 1D raster-scan propagation with a 2D line-scan approach that performs parallel row-by-row and column-by-column linear propagation, reducing the effective sequence length from N to √N for square inputs. The core theoretical contribution is the Stability-Context Condition: propagation weights are constrained to be row-stochastic tridiagonal matrices (via sigmoid activation followed by row-wise normalization), which guarantees both numerical stability (Theorem 2) and dense long-range context propagation (Theorem 1), since products of tridiagonal matrices become dense. Four scanning directions (left-to-right, top-to-bottom, and their reverses) with a learnable linear merger ensure full pairwise pixel connectivity. Weights u, w, and λ are input-dependent, generated by 1×1 convolutions, and the architecture combines local GSPN blocks (grouped propagation for efficiency) in early layers with global GSPN blocks in deeper layers. For text-to-image, GSPN is integrated into Stable Diffusion via knowledge distillation, initializing propagation parameters from the pre-trained SD's Q, K, V matrices to leverage the mathematical equivalence between GSPN and linear attention.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification at 224×224, GSPN achieves 83.0% (Tiny), 83.8% (Small), and 84.3% (Base) top-1 accuracy, matching or exceeding SoTA ConvNet and Transformer baselines at each scale. For class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 generation, GSPN-XL/2 sets a new SoTA FID of 15.26, while GSPN-L/2 achieves FID 17.25 using only 65.6% of the parameters of prior models (443M vs. 675M). On COCO text-to-image at 1024×1024, SD-v1.5-GSPN reaches FID 30.86 and CLIP-T 0.307, surpassing the SD-v1.5 baseline (32.71 / 0.290) and all Mamba/Linfusion variants. Notably, GSPN accelerates SD-XL inference by over 84× when generating 16K-resolution images on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In ImageNet classification, GSPN-B (84.3%) edges out MambaOut-Base (84.2%), CSwin-B (84.2%), and MambaVision-B (84.2%) by a narrow margin, while clearly surpassing raster-scan methods like VMamba-B (83.9%), ConvNeXT-B (83.8%), and Swin-B (83.5%). At the Tiny scale, GSPN-T (83.0%) leads all sequential/raster baselines by 0.3–2.5 points over LocalVMamba-T (82.7%), VMamba-T (82.2%), and Vim-S (80.5%). In class-conditional generation, GSPN-XL/2 (FID 15.26) substantially outperforms SiT-XL/2 (18.04), DiT-XL/2 (20.05), U-ViT-H/2 (21.71), and PixArt-α-XL/2 (24.81), and GSPN-L/2 (FID 17.25) beats all of them while using only 443M parameters versus 641–675M. For text-to-image on COCO, SD-v1.5-GSPN (FID 30.86, CLIP-T 0.307) improves over the SD-v1.5 baseline (32.71, 0.290) and decisively outperforms Mamba (50.30), Mamba2 (37.02), and Linfusion (36.33), matching Linfusion's quality without requiring pretrained weight initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSPN directly extends the Spatial Propagation Network (SPN, Liu et al., NeurIPS 2017), which first introduced 2D linear propagation but only as a single-layer module atop CNNs for sparse-to-dense prediction. The key departure from SPN is parallel row/column-wise propagation with the Stability-Context Condition (row-stochastic tridiagonal weight matrices), enabling GSPN to function as a foundational block in deep hierarchical architectures rather than a shallow refinement layer. GSPN also contrasts with the dominant 1D raster-scan paradigm adopted by Mamba, VMamba, Vim, and other SSM-based vision models, which flatten 2D images into 1D sequences and thereby discard spatial coherence. By operating natively on 2D structure via line-scan, GSPN reduces effective sequence length to √N and eliminates the need for positional embeddings, positioning it as a drop-in replacement for self-attention in both ViT-style discriminative and DiT-style generative architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 84× inference speedup at 16K resolution with SD-XL is a practically significant efficiency win that could make ultra-high-resolution generation feasible on consumer hardware, and the method's drop-in compatibility with existing architectures lowers the adoption barrier. The theoretical grounding via the Stability-Context Condition (row-stochastic matrices guaranteeing both stability and dense long-range context) provides a principled answer to the longstanding tension in 2D linear propagation between stability and receptive field. Limitations include an acknowledged gap between theoretical and measured CUDA kernel performance at larger batch/channel sizes, and the classification gains over strong baselines (e.g., 83.0% vs 82.7% for LocalVMamba-T) are marginal, suggesting the method's primary value lies in generative and high-resolution settings rather than discriminative accuracy. Open questions include whether the tridiagonal 3-way connection is optimal, whether the approach extends naturally to 3D video data, and whether the lack of positional embeddings generalizes to tasks requiring explicit spatial reasoning beyond what scanning encodes.
