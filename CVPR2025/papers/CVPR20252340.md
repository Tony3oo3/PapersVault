## Problem

Applying a single-image depth estimator frame-by-frame to a video produces flickering and drift because it ignores temporal continuity, while video diffusion models (e.g., SVD-based) are computationally expensive, restricted to short clips (~100 frames), and require stitching that introduces low-frequency artifacts. The paper asks whether a single-image latent diffusion model can be augmented to deliver state-of-the-art video depth without the overhead of a dedicated video model.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconstrained RGB video of arbitrary length (demonstrated up to 1000+ frames) and produces a temporally consistent depth video with one inverse-depth map per frame, all sharing a common scale and shift. At inference, the video is processed as overlapping 3-frame snippets sampled at three dilation rates {1, 10, 25} (i.e., frame spacings of 1, 10, and 25 frames), with input frames resized to a maximum side of 768 pixels. The output depth is affine-invariant (inverse depth), not metric, and is up-sampled to the original resolution for evaluation.

## Method

RollingDepth extends the Marigold single-image LDM by modifying its self-attention layers to flatten tokens from all frames in a snippet into a single sequence, enabling cross-frame information exchange without factorized spatial-temporal attention. A dilated rolling kernel samples 3-frame snippets at multiple dilation rates to capture temporal context at different scales, and each snippet is processed with 1-step diffusion inference. A robust L1-optimization-based global co-alignment then jointly estimates per-snippet scale and shift parameters so that all overlapping depth predictions are registered into a single consistent video. An optional refinement step re-encodes the aligned depth video into latent space, adds moderate noise (timestep T/2), and denoises it with the same LDM in a coarse-to-fine dilation schedule to recover fine spatial detail. The model is fine-tuned from Marigold on TartanAir (synthetic video) and Hypersim (photorealistic single images), with per-snippet inverse-depth normalization to handle sudden depth-range changes.

## Results

RollingDepth achieves the best AbsRel and δ1 scores on 4 out of 5 evaluation datasets. On PointOdyssey (250 frames), it reaches 9.6% AbsRel and 90.5% δ1, substantially outperforming the best single-frame method (DepthAnythingv2: 14.4% AbsRel) and the best video method (DepthCrafter: 36.3% AbsRel). On DyDTOF (200 frames), RollingDepth achieves 17.3% AbsRel vs. 22.1% for DepthCrafter and 24.7% for NVDS. The only dataset where RollingDepth is not the best is Bonn RGBD, where DepthCrafter achieves a lower AbsRel of 6.6% vs. RollingDepth's 7.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RollingDepth outperforms all three single-frame baselines (Marigold, DepthAnything, DepthAnythingv2) on every dataset, with the largest margins on PointOdyssey (9.6 vs. 14.4 AbsRel for DepthAnythingv2) and DyDTOF-200 (17.3 vs. 24.8 for DepthAnythingv2). Against video-based methods, RollingDepth dominates on PointOdyssey where DepthCrafter (36.3) and ChronoDepth (51.7) struggle badly with sudden depth-range changes, and on DyDTOF where dynamic objects challenge temporal consistency. The sole exception is Bonn RGBD, where DepthCrafter achieves 6.6% AbsRel and 96.7% δ1 versus RollingDepth's 7.9% and 93.9%, likely because Bonn scenes are dominated by foreground humans where DepthCrafter's video prior excels. NVDS and ChronoDepth are consistently the weakest video baselines across all datasets, with ChronoDepth particularly poor on PointOdyssey (51.7% AbsRel). The paper attributes RollingDepth's advantage to combining per-frame accuracy of image diffusion models with temporal coherence from multi-frame self-attention and global co-alignment, without the rigidity of fixed-length video diffusion priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

RollingDepth extends the Marigold single-image latent diffusion model for depth estimation by adding multi-frame self-attention over short snippets (typically 3 frames) and a dilated rolling kernel that samples snippets at varying temporal resolutions. It deliberately departs from the concurrent line of work that repurposes video diffusion models (SVD-based ChronoDepth, DepthCrafter, DepthAnyVideo) for depth, arguing that the high training/inference cost, fixed short-clip limitation, and stitching artifacts of video LDMs are unnecessary. The global co-alignment optimization is a novel contribution that has no direct analogue in prior video depth work, which typically relies on learned temporal consistency or test-time optimization with camera poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single-image diffusion model, augmented with lightweight temporal mechanisms, can outperform dedicated video diffusion models on video depth by large margins while handling arbitrarily long sequences (1000+ frames vs. ~100 for video LDMs). This challenges the prevailing assumption that temporal consistency in depth requires a video-native generative model, and offers a practical, lower-cost alternative for applications like robotics and AR. Open questions include whether the co-alignment optimization remains robust under extreme depth-range shifts, whether the snippet-based refinement can be replaced by flow-based or generative video methods for better motion fidelity, and how the approach scales to very high frame rates or 4K resolution.
