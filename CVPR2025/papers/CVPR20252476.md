## Problem

Existing concept extraction methods from a single image either require human-annotated masks, multiple similar images, or only capture object-level concepts without decomposing them into interpretable intrinsic attributes like colour and material. There is no systematic, fully unsupervised framework that automatically localizes multiple objects in an unlabelled image and further decomposes each into its underlying intrinsic visual properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252476/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unlabelled RGB image as input and produces a hierarchical set of outputs: text-based concept labels with corresponding segmentation masks (from Stage One), learned concept-specific and instance-specific token embeddings for each object, and intrinsic attribute tokens (e.g., colour, material) for each object (from Stage Two). The segmentation masks are generated training-free via the T2I model's self-attention layers, and the final learned tokens can be used to generate novel images of each extracted concept or intrinsic attribute. All processing is performed within a single Stable Diffusion v2.1 model without external pretrained segmenters or VQA models.

## Method

ICE is a two-stage framework operating entirely within a single T2I diffusion model. Stage One (training-free) iteratively retrieves the top text-based concept via SpLiCE and generates its segmentation mask via DiffSeg, removing each masked region before the next iteration until no objects remain. Stage Two performs structured concept learning in two phases: Phase 1 introduces concept-specific and instance-specific tokens per object, trained with a triplet loss that pulls the concept-specific token toward the retrieved text anchor while pushing the instance-specific token away; Phase 2 introduces intrinsic tokens (e.g., colour, material) anchored to combined object-level representations, trained with a second triplet loss that separates tokens by attribute type. A Wasserstein attention-mask loss aligns cross-attention regions with the Stage One masks, and a final concept refinement step fine-tunes both the U-Net and text encoder for 300 steps to sharpen intrinsic attribute alignment. The total objective combines reconstruction, attention, and triplet losses with a prior preservation term from DreamBooth.

## Results

On the UCE D1 benchmark with a CLIP encoder, ICE achieves 73.8% Identity Similarity (SIM^I), 82.2% Compositional Similarity (SIM^C), 32.5% top-1 classification accuracy (ACC^1), and 51.8% top-3 accuracy (ACC^3), outperforming ConceptExpress (68.9%, 78.4%, 26.3%, 38.5%) and Break-A-Scene (62.7%, 77.3%, 17.4%, 28.2%). With a DINO encoder, the gains are even more pronounced: ICE reaches 67.7% SIM^I and 47.6% ACC^1, compared to ConceptExpress's 31.9% and 32.4% respectively. ICE also produces higher-quality segmentation masks (mIoU 0.635 vs. 0.483 for ConceptExpress) in its Stage One localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICE is compared against two baselines: Break-A-Scene (which requires human-annotated masks) and ConceptExpress (the closest concurrent method that also works from a single unlabelled image without extra annotations). ICE outperforms both on all four UCE metrics under both CLIP and DINO evaluation. Against ConceptExpress, ICE gains +4.9 points in SIM^I and +3.8 points in SIM^C (CLIP), and a striking +35.8 points in SIM^I and +15.7 points in SIM^C (DINO), indicating that the structured two-stage decomposition is especially beneficial when the evaluation encoder is less aligned with the T2I model's text space. Against Break-A-Scene, ICE leads by +11.1 points in SIM^I and +14.4 points in ACC^3 (CLIP), despite Break-A-Scene having access to ground-truth masks. The mask quality comparison (Table 5) further confirms ICE's advantage: its Stage One segmentation achieves mIoU 0.635 and recall 0.893 versus ConceptExpress's 0.483 and 0.676, suggesting that the automatic concept localization module is a key contributor to the downstream gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICE extends the single-image concept extraction line initiated by Break-A-Scene, MCPL, and ConceptExpress, which all operate at the object level, by introducing a structured two-stage decomposition that further breaks object-level concepts into intrinsic attributes (color, material, shape). Its Stage One repurposes existing training-free modules within Stable Diffusion—SpLiCE for CLIP-based concept retrieval and DiffSeg for zero-shot segmentation—rather than introducing new segmentation machinery, while Stage Two builds on the triplet-loss and prior-preservation-loss formulations from Textual Inversion and DreamBooth. The key departure from ConceptExpress (the closest prior work) is the explicit hierarchical token structure (concept-specific vs. instance-specific vs. intrinsic tokens) and the use of Wasserstein attention alignment to bind learned tokens to spatially grounded masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICE provides the first fully automated, single-model pipeline that extracts both object-level and intrinsic visual concepts from an unlabelled multi-object image without external VQA models or human annotations, making structured concept learning more accessible and interpretable. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (D1/Unsplash) and a single GPU, and the intrinsic axes (color, material) appear to be predefined rather than discovered, raising open questions about scalability to open-vocabulary attribute spaces and more complex scenes. A key limitation is that the structured decomposition relies on a fixed hierarchy, and whether this generalizes to arbitrary compositional attributes without manual axis specification remains unaddressed.
