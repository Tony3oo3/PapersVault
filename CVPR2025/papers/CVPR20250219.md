## Problem

Since 2023, VQ-based discrete token methods have dominated text-to-motion generation over diffusion-based approaches, yet the reasons for this performance gap remain unclear. The paper identifies two key issues: the standard motion representation contains redundant dimensions that inadvertently regularize VQ-VAE training while causing distribution mismatch and error amplification in diffusion models, and existing evaluation metrics disproportionately penalize diffusion methods by scoring redundant features that do not affect the final rendered motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt as input and produces a 3D human motion sequence as output. Crucially, the motion is represented using only essential dimensions (root angular velocity, root linear velocities in XZ-plane, root height, and local joint positions) rather than the full 7-component canonical pose, and is further compressed into a compact latent space via a 1D ResNet AutoEncoder before the diffusion process operates on it. The autoregressive generation produces latents in a pseudo-sequential order, with each step generating one or more latent vectors conditioned on previously generated ones.

## Method

The method (MARDM) combines three key components: (1) a reformed motion representation that removes redundant 6D rotation and foot-contact features, retaining only 3D continuous essential features, then projects them through a 1D ResNet AutoEncoder into a compact latent space with per-frame smoothness; (2) a masked autoregressive diffusion architecture where a single-layer AdaLN Transformer with bidirectional attention processes unmasked latents to produce a context vector z, which conditions per-latent Diffusion MLPs that denoise individual masked latents using either DDPM noise prediction or SiT-style velocity prediction; (3) a cosine masking schedule for training and sequential mask-vector appending for inference. The autoregressive formulation simplifies the training objective to per-latent conditional denoising, while the reformed uniform distribution enables stable noise/velocity prediction that was previously infeasible with the mixed-distribution full representation.

## Results

On HumanML3D, Ours-SiT achieves the best FID of 0.114, R-Precision Top-1 of 0.500, and CLIP-score of 0.642, outperforming the strongest VQ baseline MoMask (FID 0.116, Top-1 0.490) and the strongest diffusion baseline ReMoDiffuse (FID 0.883, Top-1 0.468). On KIT-ML, Ours-SiT similarly leads with FID 0.242, R-Precision Top-1 0.387, and CLIP-score 0.692, surpassing MoMask (FID 0.411, Top-1 0.369) and ReMoDiffuse (FID 1.725, Top-1 0.356). The method also achieves the best Matching score on both datasets (3.270 on HumanML3D, 3.374 on KIT-ML) and higher MultiModality than all VQ baselines, confirming superior diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VQ-based methods, Ours-SiT achieves a lower FID than MoMask (0.114 vs. 0.116 on HumanML3D; 0.242 vs. 0.411 on KIT-ML) and higher R-Precision Top-1 (0.500 vs. 0.490; 0.387 vs. 0.369), while also delivering substantially higher MultiModality (2.231 vs. 1.263 on HumanML3D), confirming the paper's claim that VQ methods underperform in diversity. Against diffusion-based baselines, the advantage is more pronounced: Ours-SiT reduces FID by 7.7× over ReMoDiffuse on HumanML3D (0.114 vs. 0.883) and by 7.1× on KIT-ML (0.242 vs. 1.725), without requiring the retrieval-augmented database that ReMoDiffuse depends on. Compared to MDM-50Step, the proposed method improves FID from 0.518 to 0.114 on HumanML3D and from 0.585 to 0.242 on KIT-ML, while also surpassing it on all R-Precision and CLIP-score metrics. The only metric where the proposed method does not lead is MultiModality on HumanML3D, where MDM (3.604) and MLD (3.506) score higher, though these methods have substantially worse FID and R-Precision. Overall, Ours-SiT achieves the best or near-best result on every reported metric across both datasets, establishing a new state of the art for text-to-motion generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a corrective to the 2023+ shift toward VQ-based discrete motion generation (T2M-GPT, MoMask, MMM), arguing that the perceived superiority of VQ methods is partly an artifact of redundant data representations and biased evaluation metrics. Methodologically, it synthesizes three threads: the masked autoregressive-diffusion hybrid from MAR [51] (originally for images), the velocity/flow-matching formulation from SiT [62], and the cosine masking schedule and bidirectional attention from MoMask [26], repurposing them for continuous motion latents. It departs from prior diffusion-based motion work (MDM, MLD, ReMoDiffuse) by removing redundant pose dimensions, replacing the VAE with a deterministic 1D-ResNet autoencoder, and introducing per-latent diffusion MLPs conditioned on a single-layer autoregressive transformer rather than a full-sequence denoising network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic analysis (Sec. 2) is the paper's most broadly useful contribution: it shows that the FID gap between VQ and diffusion methods largely vanishes when evaluation is restricted to essential dimensions, and that redundant features act as implicit regularization for VQ codebooks. This reframes an active research debate and provides a concrete recipe (remove redundancy, use essential-dimension evaluators) that the community can adopt immediately. Open questions include whether the single-layer autoregressive transformer and simple MLP diffusion branch scale to longer sequences or more complex tasks (multi-person, human-object interaction), and whether the evaluation reform will be widely accepted given that all prior results in the literature were computed with the original all-dimension evaluator.
