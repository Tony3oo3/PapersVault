## Problem

Existing HD mapping and topology reasoning methods model spatial relations between lanes but overlook the control and guide semantics that road signals (e.g., 'turn left', 'no right turn') impose on lane connectivity. TopoNet introduced a heterogeneous graph for lanes and road signals but still neglected the directional semantics each signal carries. The paper asks: can a unified scene graph simultaneously encode which road signal guides each lane and how lanes connect, enabling more accurate and explainable topology reasoning?

## Input / Output

The method consumes N_I multi-view RGB images (resized to 1550×2048) and produces a Traffic Topology Scene Graph T²SG whose nodes are directed lane centerlines—each an ordered list of 11 3D points sampled at 2 Hz along the driving direction—paired with one of 10 category labels that include road-signal types. Edges are binary (connected/not) directed pairs indicating that the end point of one centerline links to the start point of another. For the downstream topology-reasoning benchmark, the system additionally outputs 2D bounding boxes for traffic elements (including traffic lights) and a lane-to-element assignment matrix.

## Method

TopoFormer is a one-stage Transformer built on a Deformable DETR lane detector fed by BEVFormer-style bird's-eye-view features. Two novel encoder layers drive the architecture: (1) a Lane Aggregation Layer (LAL) whose Geometry-guided Self-Attention adds a Spatial Proximity Matrix—normalized inverse ℓ₁ distances between lane endpoints—to the standard attention logits, explicitly injecting spatial proximity into global feature aggregation; and (2) a Counterfactual Intervention Layer (CIL) whose Counterfactual Self-Attention replaces the learned attention matrix with a zero matrix (do(A = Ā)) while keeping lane features and the SPM fixed, then computes the Total Indirect Effect (TIE) as the difference between factual and counterfactual edge scores. During training, a focal loss is applied to the TIE to encourage the model to learn road structures whose causal contribution to connectivity predictions is maximized; at inference, only the factual (non-intervened) predictions are used. Edge scores are produced by two independent 3-layer MLPs (start-lane and end-lane projections) whose outputs are concatenated and passed through a final sigmoid MLP.

## Results

On the OpenLane-V2 subset_A traffic topology reasoning benchmark, TopoFormer achieves a state-of-the-art OLS of 46.3, surpassing the previous best (TopoMLP and TopoLogic at 44.1) by 2.2 points. The gains are driven by strong lane detection (DET_l = 34.7, +4.8 over TopoLogic) and lane-to-traffic-element topology (TOP_lt = 29.5, +3.6 over TopoLogic). On subset_B, TopoFormer further extends the lead to 47.5 OLS (+5.2 over TopoLogic). For the T²SG generation task, TopoFormer achieves the best node AP_1.0 of 11.8 and edge A@1_3.0 of 35.6, outperforming all scene graph generation baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoFormer outperforms all 10 baselines on the primary OLS metric. Against the closest competitors TopoMLP [37] and TopoLogic [7] (both at 44.1 OLS on subset_A), TopoFormer gains +2.2 OLS, primarily through superior DET_l (+4.8 over TopoLogic, +6.4 over TopoMLP) and TOP_lt (+3.6 over TopoLogic, +2.6 over TopoMLP), though it trails TopoMLP slightly on DET_t (48.2 vs 49.5). Against the graph-based TopoNet [13], TopoFormer nearly doubles TOP_ll (24.1 vs 10.9) and improves OLS by 6.5 points, demonstrating the benefit of unified scene graph modeling over heterogeneous graph approaches. On the T²SG generation task (Table 1), GCN-based methods 3DSSG [31] and EdgeGCN [42] collapse on edge prediction (A@1_1.0 = 0.4 vs TopoFormer's 8.8), while Transformer-based EGTR [10] and SGFormer [21] are closer but still trail TopoFormer by 0.7 and 0.3 in AP_1.0 respectively. On subset_B, where centerlines are annotated in 2D space, TopoFormer's advantage widens to +5.2 OLS over TopoLogic, indicating stronger generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the traffic topology reasoning line initiated by TopoNet [13], which first modeled lanes and road signals as nodes in a heterogeneous graph, and TopoLogic [7], which leveraged geometric distances among centerlines for global dependency modeling. The key departure is the unification of lane-lane connectivity and lane-to-traffic-signal control relationships into a single scene graph (T²SG), whereas prior methods treat these as separate sub-tasks. The Counterfactual Intervention Layer adapts the do-calculus-inspired attention intervention from Rao et al. [28] (originally for ReID) to the novel context of road-structure reasoning, while the Lane Aggregation Layer's geometry-guided self-attention builds on the spatial proximity matrix concept from 3DVGTransformer [44] but applies it to lane centerline endpoints rather than point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified T²SG formulation addresses a genuine gap in HD mapping: prior methods either model spatial lane connectivity or semantic signal-lane associations but not both jointly, limiting downstream planning and prediction. The counterfactual intervention mechanism offers an interpretable way to quantify how learned road structure (attention patterns) causally influences topology predictions, which could inform debugging and trust in autonomous driving systems. However, the evaluation is confined to OpenLane-V2 (two subsets of 1,000 scenes each), and the modest 2.2-point OLS improvement over TopoLogic leaves open whether the CIL's benefits persist at larger scale or in more complex urban environments with denser signal-lane interactions.
