## Problem

Existing spatial video grounding methods require explicit training with localization losses or fine-tuned backbones to localize actions in videos, while training-free localization approaches like GEM only handle object grounding in images. Actions are fundamentally harder to localize than objects because they lack clear physical boundaries, involve interactions between multiple entities, and are described by higher-level semantic concepts that emerge in deeper network layers. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first training-free method for spatial action grounding in videos.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of 8 frames (4 before and 3 after a labeled frame, sampled for ViCLIP) and a natural language action description as input. The text prompt is decomposed into three sub-prompts (verb, object, full action) using fixed templates, each encoded separately by the text encoder. The output is a single 2D center-point coordinate for the action, computed as a weighted average of three individual center-point predictions (one per sub-prompt), along with per-prompt attention heatmaps. A prediction is scored as correct if the center point falls within the ground-truth bounding box.

## Method

VideoGEM extends GEM's self-self attention pathway to video by concatenating patch tokens from all 8 frames into a single sequence processed jointly through the transformer, so that self-self attention automatically aggregates across both space and time. To capture higher-level action concepts that emerge in deeper layers, it introduces a combined static-and-dynamic layer weighting: static weights monotonically increase across the last K=7 self-self attention layers, while dynamic weights (applied to the last D=3 layers) are computed per-prompt via softmax over cosine similarities between [CLS] tokens with each layer removed and the text [EOS] embedding. To counteract the object bias in vision-language models, a prompt decomposition splits the action query into verb, object, and full-action prompts processed independently, with their center-point predictions combined via weighted averaging (weights 0.2, 0.2, 0.6). The entire pipeline is training-free, using pretrained CLIP, OpenCLIP, or ViCLIP weights without any fine-tuning or additional projection heads.

## Results

VideoGEM achieves an average accuracy of 66.58% (ViCLIP), 65.53% (OpenCLIP), and 65.37% (CLIP) across four video grounding datasets, outperforming all previously trained state-of-the-art methods by more than 3% on average. The best trained baseline, WWW-CLIP with a fine-tuned CLIP backbone, reaches 62.26% average accuracy. On V-HICO, VideoGEM with CLIP reaches 76.90% accuracy, and on Daly it reaches 84.53%, while on the more challenging GroundingYouTube (untrimmed cooking videos), ViCLIP-based VideoGEM achieves 57.21%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoGEM outperforms all eight compared baselines on average. Against the strongest trained baseline WWW-CLIP (62.26% avg with fine-tuned CLIP), VideoGEM with ViCLIP leads by 4.32 points (66.58%), and with CLIP by 3.11 points (65.37%). Compared to the training-free GEM baseline (60.93% avg with ViCLIP), VideoGEM improves by 5.65 points, with the largest gains on V-HICO (+10.67) and Daly (+4.50). On YouCook-Interactions specifically, VideoGEM (55.10% with ViCLIP) slightly trails VideoGrounding-DINO (57.73%) and STCAT (55.90%), but surpasses them on all other datasets where those methods are not evaluated. On GroundingYouTube, VideoGEM with ViCLIP (57.21%) outperforms GLIP (53.62%) and WWW-CLIP (56.98%), while CLIP-based VideoGEM (47.46%) underperforms on this dataset, suggesting that video-specific backbones are critical for untrimmed, action-centric grounding. Overall, no baseline matches VideoGEM's consistent performance across all four datasets simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoGEM directly extends GEM (Bousselham et al., CVPR 2024), which introduced self-self attention as a training-free localization pathway for object grounding in images, by adapting the mechanism to multi-frame video inputs and adding two key modifications: a static-plus-dynamic layer weighting scheme that prioritizes higher transformer layers where abstract concepts like actions emerge, and a prompt decomposition that separately processes verb, object, and full-action prompts to counteract the well-documented object bias in vision-language models. This positions the work at the intersection of two prior lines: training-free image localization (MaskCLIP, CLIPSurgery, GEM) and spatial video grounding (CoMMA, TubeDETR, STCAT, VideoGrounding-DINO, What-When-Where), from which it departs by eliminating the need for any task-specific training or localization loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a purely training-free method outperforms all previously reported trained spatial video grounding methods by over 3% on average challenges the prevailing assumption that localization in video requires dedicated supervision, potentially reducing the data and compute barrier for deploying action grounding in new domains. Limitations include the output being a single center point rather than a bounding box or tube, reliance on NLP tools for verb/object extraction in natural-language annotations, and evaluation limited to four datasets with relatively small test sets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-person interactions, longer video segments, or more complex compositional actions, and whether the center-point formulation is sufficient for downstream tasks requiring spatial extent.
