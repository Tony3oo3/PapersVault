## Problem

Training a single universal policy for dexterous robotic grasping across thousands of diverse objects is hindered by complex multi-step training pipelines (e.g., geometry-aware curriculum learning plus iterative generalist-specialist distillation in UniDexGrasp++) that limit scalability and efficiency. Moreover, prior universal networks suffer significant performance degradation when tasked with a large, diverse object set, and tend to produce repetitive, low-diversity grasp poses.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the network receives six input groups—167-d proprioception, 24-d previous action, 16-d object state, 36-d hand-object distance, 29-d time embedding, and a 128-d object feature from a PointNet-style encoder—each projected to a 256-d token via a dedicated MLP, yielding six tokens fed to the Transformer. The output is a 24-d action vector (18 finger joint angles + 6 wrist force/torque) predicting the next control command. In the vision-based variant, the object point cloud is a partial reconstruction from five depth cameras and object pose is approximated via PCA on the partial cloud rather than provided as oracle state.

## Method

UniGraspTransformer follows a three-stage offline distillation pipeline: (1) 3,200 lightweight PPO policies (4-layer MLP, hidden dims 1024-1024-512-512) are each trained on a single object with a composite reward (grasp, open, lift, goal, success) to achieve 94.1% average success; (2) each policy generates 1,000 successful 200-step trajectories, producing 3.2M annotated sequences; (3) a universal Transformer with up to 12 self-attention blocks followed by a 4-layer MLP head is trained via supervised L2 regression on all trajectories. The key design insight is that offline distillation decouples teacher training from student capacity, allowing the student to scale to 12 self-attention blocks (vs. small MLPs required by online DAgger), which yields a 4.3-point success-rate gain over the online baseline and substantially broader grasp-pose diversity. A PointNet-like S-Encoder (or V-Encoder with distillation loss from S-Encoder for partial clouds) converts object point clouds into 128-d features, enabling the same architecture to operate in both idealized state-based and realistic vision-based settings.

## Results

On the UniDexGrasp++ benchmark (3,200 objects, 133 categories), UniGraspTransformer achieves 91.2% success rate on seen objects and 88.3% on fully unseen objects in the state-based setting, and 88.9% / 86.8% in the vision-based setting. In the vision-based setting, it outperforms the previous state-of-the-art UniDexGrasp++ by 3.5% on seen objects, 7.7% on unseen objects within seen categories, and 10.1% on completely unseen objects from unseen categories. The dedicated per-object RL policies achieve an average 94.1% success rate before distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniGraspTransformer outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor UniDexGrasp++ [49], it gains 3.3% (state-based) and 3.5% (vision-based) on seen objects, with much larger margins on generalization: +4.9%/+7.7% on unseen objects within seen categories and +5.2%/+10.1% on fully unseen objects (state/vision). The gap widens substantially in the vision-based setting, where noisy partial point clouds and estimated poses amplify the advantage of the larger Transformer architecture. UniDexGrasp [56] trails by 11.8–21.7 points, while earlier generalist-specialist methods (GSL [13]) and single-policy RL baselines (PPO [42], DAPG [39], ILAD [55]) are far behind, with PPO and DAPG scoring below 25% in most settings. The consistent win across all six conditions (2 settings × 3 object categories) indicates the improvement is not confined to a particular evaluation regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper simplifies the generalist-specialist learning framework established by UniDexGrasp++ (Wan et al., 2023) and GSL (Jia et al., 2022), which rely on complex multi-step pipelines involving geometry-aware curriculum learning and iterative online distillation. The key departure is replacing this with a straightforward two-stage process: per-object PPO training followed by offline supervised distillation into a single Transformer with up to 12 self-attention blocks. Unlike prior universal grasping policies that use small MLPs to ensure convergence during online distillation, UniGraspTransformer leverages the decoupling of teacher training from student training to scale the student network substantially, while also enabling flexible transfer from idealized state-based observations to noisy vision-based inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simpler training pipeline can outperform a more complex one, suggesting that the added machinery in prior methods (curriculum learning, iterative generalist-specialist loops) may not be necessary when the student network has sufficient capacity. The consistent scaling with self-attention blocks and trajectory count provides a clear recipe for practitioners seeking to improve dexterous grasping performance. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real hardware (all evaluation is in Isaac Gym simulation), whether it generalizes to different hand morphologies beyond the Shadow Hand, and whether the 3,200-object training set is sufficient for truly open-world deployment. The sim-to-real gap for dexterous manipulation remains a significant barrier not addressed here.
