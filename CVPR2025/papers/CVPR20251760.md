## Problem

Existing point cloud models, including pre-training-based and cross-modal approaches, have not fundamentally resolved the challenge of local structure representation. The limited representational capacity of pure point cloud architectures constrains both cross-modal fusion and overall performance across 3D tasks, making efficient capture of fine-grained local geometric features a critical unsolved problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251760/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes raw 3D point clouds (coordinate and feature vectors per point) and processes them through local neighborhood sampling with a configurable neighborhood size K. For few-shot semantic segmentation, it additionally takes support and query point clouds, using a Query module from SegPN to bridge domain gaps. Outputs are class labels for shape classification, per-point part labels for ShapeNetPart segmentation, and per-point semantic labels for S3DIS/ScanNet few-shot segmentation, with the encoder-decoder variant using feature propagation to restore full point resolution.

## Method

DAFNet models local feature aggregation as acoustic field propagation, where the center point acts as a sound source and neighbors as observation points. The core AF-Conv divides the local neighborhood into near-field (exponential decay e^{-βd}) and far-field (reciprocal 1/(d+ε)) zones to capture different geometric scales. DAF-Conv extends this by generating H parallel frequency components with dynamically learned weights (α_h, β_h) from local geometric priors [p_i, p_j, p_j−p_i, ‖p_j−p_i‖] via MLP, then fusing them through learned attention. A Global Shape-Aware (GSA) layer combining EdgeConv and multi-head attention provides global context before each DAF-Conv, and stacked DAF Blocks form the hierarchical network. The full model uses only 2.47M parameters and 1.1G FLOPs while achieving 96.7% OA on ScanObjectNN.

## Results

On the challenging ScanObjectNN (PB_T50_RS) benchmark, DAFNet achieves 96.7% overall accuracy (OA) and 96.3% mean class accuracy (mAcc) with test-time voting, setting a new state-of-the-art with only 2.47M parameters and 1.1G FLOPs. On ModelNet40, DAFNet reaches 94.1% OA and 92.2% mAcc, competitive with but slightly below the best pre-training methods. For few-shot semantic segmentation, DAFNet attains 69.20% mIoU (2-way 1-shot) on S3DIS and 69.37% mIoU (2-way 1-shot) on ScanNet, surpassing the previous best (Seg-PN) by 2.79% and 5.63% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanObjectNN, DAFNet (96.7% OA) clearly outperforms the strongest supervised baseline GPSFormer (95.4%) by 1.3% and the best pre-training method ReCon++ (95.25%) by 1.45%, while also surpassing PointGPT (93.6%), Mamba3D (92.6%), and PointMamba (89.3%) by substantial margins. On ModelNet40, DAFNet (94.1% OA) trails the top pre-training methods PointGPT (94.9%), ReCon++ (94.8%), and RepSurf-U (94.7%) by 0.2–0.8%, though it matches Mamba3D (94.1%) and exceeds GPSFormer (94.2%) and PointMLP (94.5%). For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, DAFNet (85.2% class mIoU / 86.8% instance mIoU) is competitive with SPoTr (85.4/87.2) and PointNeXt (85.2/87.1) but slightly behind on instance mIoU. In few-shot segmentation, DAFNet consistently beats Seg-PN across all settings: on S3DIS it gains +2.79% (2-way 1-shot) and +3.67% (3-way 1-shot), while on ScanNet the improvements are even larger at +5.63% (2-way 1-shot) and +2.69% (3-way 1-shot). Notably, DAFNet achieves its SOTA ScanObjectNN result with only 2.47M parameters and 1.1G FLOPs, far more efficient than MVTN (11.2M params, 43.7G FLOPs) and PointMLP (12.6M params, 31.4G FLOPs).

## Relation to Prior Work

DAFNet sits within the CNN-based point cloud processing lineage (PointNet → PointNet++ → DGCNN → PAConv) but introduces a physics-inspired reframing of local feature aggregation as acoustic energy propagation, decomposing neighborhoods into near-field and far-field zones with distinct attenuation models. The dynamic multi-frequency weight generation in DAF-Conv directly extends the conditional parameterization idea from CondConv [62] and PAConv, while the few-shot segmentation pipeline borrows the query module from SegPN [77]. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior work by grounding the near/far field split and multi-frequency decomposition in acoustic wave physics rather than purely geometric or learned criteria, though the underlying operations (exponential decay, reciprocal distance) remain standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAFNet demonstrates that a pre-training-free, lightweight architecture (2.47M params, 1.1G FLOPs) can match or exceed much larger pre-trained models on challenging real-world benchmarks like ScanObjectNN, suggesting that better inductive biases for local structure may be more effective than scaling pre-training data. The near-field/far-field decomposition provides an interpretable mechanism for balancing fine-grained local detail against broader contextual information, which could inform future local aggregation designs. However, the acoustic field framing is largely metaphorical—the actual weight functions are simple parametric forms—and it remains open whether this physics-inspired perspective generalizes beyond point clouds or whether the gains are primarily attributable to the multi-frequency dynamic convolution rather than the specific acoustic formulation.
