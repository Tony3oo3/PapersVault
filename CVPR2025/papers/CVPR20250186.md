## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently produce subject mixing when synthesizing multiple similar-looking subjects (e.g., a leopard and a tiger), where visual features bleed across subjects. Prior training-free guidance methods rely on self-attention or cross-attention maps in isolation and typically consider only the single most discriminant patch, leaving subject mixing unresolved. No prior work had explored the interaction between self-attention and cross-attention maps as a mechanism for eliminating subject mixing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt containing two or more subject tokens (e.g., 'a bear and an elephant') along with their token indices, and produces a single synthesized RGB image. It is entirely training-free and operates as an inference-time modification on pre-trained latent diffusion models, supporting both UNet-based architectures (SD1.4, SD2.1) and Transformer-based DiT architectures (SD3-medium). The guidance is applied only during the first half of the reverse denoising process and only at selected intermediate layers, leaving the remaining denoising steps unmodified.

## Method

Self-Cross Diffusion Guidance penalizes the overlap between one subject's aggregated self-attention map and another subject's cross-attention map (and vice versa), operating on the insight that a subject should not attend to other subjects via its own self-attention. For each subject, patches are selected via Otsu thresholding on its cross-attention map, and their self-attention maps are aggregated into a single weighted map representing the full region the subject attends to—going beyond the single most discriminant patch used by prior methods. The resulting Self-Cross loss (Eq. 6-7) is combined with a cross-attention response score and optimized via gradient descent on latents during early timesteps, with initial noise optimization (INITNO) to find a favorable starting point. The method is applied only at early timesteps and intermediate layers where attention maps are semantically meaningful, and is complementary to existing guidance such as CONFORM, as demonstrated by the CONFORM+Ours combination.

## Results

On the primary TIFA-GPT4o evaluation with SD1.4, Self-Cross achieves 92.94% absence-of-mixing (w/o M) on Animal-Animal and 86.45% on the proposed SSD-2 dataset, outperforming the second-best methods (CONFORM at 84.22% and INITNO at 79.70% respectively) by 8.7 and 6.7 percentage points. On the three-subject SSD-3 benchmark with SD3-medium, Self-Cross improves existence from 33.54% to 57.92% and recognizability from 30.31% to 53.08%. Text-text similarity (BLIP score) also reaches SOTA across all benchmarks, e.g., 84.3% on Animal-Animal and 73.6% on SSD-2 with SD1.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-Cross decisively outperforms vanilla SD1.4 across all metrics, with the most dramatic gains on the challenging SSD-2 dataset (w/o M: 86.45% vs 77.47%) and Animal-Animal existence (94.55% vs 39.51%). Against INITNO, Self-Cross wins on every metric for both Animal-Animal and SSD-2, with the largest margin on SSD-2 w/o M (86.45% vs 79.70%, +6.75 pp). Against CONFORM, Self-Cross leads on the two most challenging benchmarks (Animal-Animal w/o M: 92.94% vs 84.22%; SSD-2 w/o M: 86.45% vs 79.50%) but CONFORM retains a slight edge on the easier Animal-Object and Object-Object sets for w/o M (97.16% and 97.48% vs 98.30% and 98.65% respectively, where Self-Cross actually wins). On SD3-medium (Table 1), Self-Cross improves existence by 24.4 pp and recognizability by 22.8 pp over the base model, confirming the method generalizes to DiT-based architectures. The paper also shows that combining CONFORM (for early steps) with Self-Cross (for later steps) yields the best SD2.1 results (94.66% w/o M on Animal-Animal), indicating the two methods are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free, attention-based guidance for text-to-image diffusion (Attend-and-Excite, INITNO, CONFORM) by introducing the first regularization that couples self-attention and cross-attention maps rather than treating them independently. It builds directly on INITNO's initial noise optimization and Attend-and-Excite's cross-attention response score, but departs from both by aggregating self-attention across multiple Otsu-masked patches instead of relying on the single most discriminant patch. The evaluation framework also departs from prior work by replacing CLIP-based scoring with GPT-4o VQA (TIFA-GPT4o) and introducing the Similar Subjects Dataset (SSD) as a dedicated benchmark for the similar-subject mixing failure mode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Subject mixing for visually similar subjects (e.g., leopard vs. tiger) remains a persistent and practically important failure mode that existing training-free methods do not adequately resolve; this paper provides a clean, architecture-agnostic fix that works across UNet-based (SD1/SD2) and DiT-based (SD3) backbones without retraining. The SSD benchmark and GPT-4o-based evaluation address a real gap in the field where prior datasets were too easy and CLIP scores poorly correlated with human judgment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video generation of similar subjects, whether newer backbone architectures will naturally reduce mixing, and how the method interacts with attribute binding, which the authors acknowledge as a separate unresolved issue.
