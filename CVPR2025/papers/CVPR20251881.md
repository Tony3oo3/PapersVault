## Problem

Video face restoration remains significantly under-explored compared to its image counterpart, suffering from temporal jitter, low generation quality, and limited high-quality training data. Moreover, existing methods treat blind face restoration in isolation, overlooking that complementary subtasks such as face colorization and inpainting share common priors and can mutually enhance restoration performance through shared representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251881/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a degraded video clip (low-quality for BFR, brush-stroke-masked for inpainting, or grayscale for colorization) along with an optional reference identity image. The degraded video is encoded into latent space via a VAE encoder and concatenated with noise in the diffusion U-Net, while the reference image provides identity features through a pre-trained ArcFace encoder and a mapping network injected into cross-attention layers. During inference, the first clip is generated without a reference frame, after which a selected output frame becomes the reference for all subsequent clips, enabling long-sequence consistency.

## Method

SVFR builds on Stable Video Diffusion's pretrained U-Net and VAE, adapting them for multi-task face restoration through three key mechanisms. First, a learnable Task Embedding maps a binary task indicator vector into the U-Net's time embedding, while a Unified Latent Regularization applies a contrastive loss on intermediate U-Net features (processed by a small Latent Transformer) to align representations of different degradations of the same video. Second, a Facial Prior Learning objective trains a landmark predictor on the U-Net's middle-block features using a wing loss on 68-point landmarks, injecting structural face priors into the denoising process. Third, a Self-referred Refinement strategy at inference feeds previously generated frames back as reference conditioning, with 50% reference dropout during training to preserve unconditional generation capability. The composite training objective combines the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss with the ULR contrastive term and the facial prior regression loss, all optimized jointly.

## Results

On the VFHQ-test dataset, SVFR achieves the best performance across all three GVFR subtasks (BFR, colorization, inpainting) and all six metrics. For BFR, SVFR reaches 29.563 PSNR, 0.902 IDS, and 89.316 FVD, outperforming the strongest video-based baseline PGTFormer (28.996 PSNR, 154.875 FVD) by a large margin on temporal quality. For inpainting, SVFR attains 29.119 PSNR and 88.354 FVD, nearly halving the FVD of CodeFormer (170.631). For colorization, SVFR achieves 23.079 PSNR and 204.260 FVD, substantially surpassing GPEN (22.012 PSNR, 366.248 FVD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVFR outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across all three subtasks. Against the strongest BFR baseline PGTFormer, SVFR improves PSNR from 28.996 to 29.563 and reduces FVD from 154.875 to 89.316 (a 42% reduction), indicating substantially better temporal coherence. Against KEEP, the gap is even wider on FVD (399.239 → 89.316) and VIDD (0.787 → 0.479), showing that SVFR's self-referred refinement and multi-task learning yield far superior frame-to-frame stability. For inpainting, SVFR nearly halves CodeFormer's FVD (170.631 → 88.354) while also improving PSNR (28.672 → 29.119) and LPIPS (0.156 → 0.153). For colorization, SVFR leads GPEN by 1.07 dB in PSNR and reduces FVD by 44% (366.248 → 204.260), while CodeFormer and PGDiff lag significantly on both reconstruction and identity metrics. Notably, SVFR achieves all results with a single unified model, whereas each baseline is trained separately per task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its generative backbone, repurposing SVD's 3D convolution and temporal attention layers for face-specific restoration rather than general video generation. It departs from prior video face restoration methods (KEEP, PGTFormer, StableBFVR) that address only blind face restoration in isolation, by formulating a unified 'Generalized Video Face Restoration' task that jointly trains on BFR, inpainting, and colorization. The multi-task learning paradigm is borrowed from NLP and image restoration (e.g., GPEN, CodeFormer, PGDiff), but the specific mechanisms—task embedding via binary vectors, Unified Latent Regularization with contrastive alignment across degradation types, and a wing-loss-based facial landmark predictor—are novel contributions to the face restoration literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video face restoration has lagged behind its image counterpart due to temporal consistency challenges and scarce high-quality training data; this paper demonstrates that multi-task supervision from complementary subtasks can partially alleviate the data scarcity problem while improving temporal coherence. However, evaluation is limited to 50 VFHQ-test videos, and the colorization/inpainting baselines are image-only methods (GPEN, CodeFormer, PGDiff) rather than video-specific approaches, making the magnitude of gains on those subtasks harder to interpret. Open questions include whether the unified framework scales to longer videos, additional degradation types (e.g., motion blur, extreme compression), and whether the SVD backbone can be replaced by lighter architectures for real-time deployment.
