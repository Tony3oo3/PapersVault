## Problem

Existing cross-view pose optimization methods rely exclusively on cross-view features at a single given pose, analogous to a proportional-only controller. This neglects global context needed to avoid local optima in repetitive urban patterns and fine-grained context needed for sub-meter precision, causing significant degradation under large initial pose errors (e.g., ±30 m).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251389/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a ground-view RGB image, a 512×512 satellite-view RGB image (0.2–0.22 m/pixel from Google Maps), a LiDAR point cloud (5,000 points sampled within the camera FoV), and an initial coarse 3-DoF pose (±20 m position, ±10° orientation). It outputs a refined 3-DoF vehicle pose (x, y, θ) via iterative refinement over 5 iterations at 3 feature-map levels, with a total inference time of 370 ms. LiDAR points are projected onto both views to define sparse sampling coordinates for cross-view feature correspondence rather than being used as a separate modality for matching.

## Method

PIDLoc models cross-view feature relationships through three parallel branches inspired by a PID controller: the P branch computes the feature difference at the given pose (local context), the I branch concatenates feature differences across a grid of pose candidates to encode global spatial context, and the D branch computes ℓ₂-normed gradients of the feature difference with respect to (x, y, θ) to capture fine-grained sensitivity. These PID-branch features are fed into a Spatially Aware Pose Estimator (SPE) that applies channel-shared MLPs with positional embeddings to model spatial dependencies among feature points, producing a single consistent pose offset rather than averaging independent per-feature estimates. Feature extraction uses a shared-weight U-Net with a VGG-16 encoder pretrained on ImageNet, and the network is trained end-to-end with an L1 loss on the 3-DoF pose across three feature-map levels.

## Results

On the KITTI dataset (±20 m position, ±10° orientation noise), PIDLoc achieves a mean position error of 1.15 m (same-area) and 4.96 m (cross-area), with mean orientation errors of 0.11° and 0.17° respectively. On FMAVS, it reaches 1.42 m (same-area) and 4.81 m (cross-area) mean position error. The cross-area KITTI result represents a 37.8% reduction in mean position error over the previous state-of-the-art (Song et al., 7.97 m), and PIDLoc attains 99.97% orientation recall within 1° in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIDLoc outperforms all nine baselines on both KITTI and FMAVS in both same-area and cross-area settings. On KITTI cross-area, it reduces mean position error from 7.97 m (Song et al. [25], previous SOTA) to 4.96 m (−37.8%) and from 9.16 m (CCVPE [34]) to 4.96 m (−45.9%), while achieving 99.96% orientation recall within 1° versus 43.44% for Song et al. and 57.72% for CCVPE. On FMAVS cross-area, PIDLoc (4.81 m) substantially outperforms SIBCL [30] (15.20 m) and PureACL 4C [29] (6.43 m), matching or exceeding the 4-camera variant with a single camera. The LM-optimizer-based methods (HighlyAccurate [19], PureACL [29], VFA [31], SIBCL [30]) all suffer large longitudinal recall drops (4–11% R@1m) due to convergence to local optima in repetitive patterns, whereas PIDLoc's I branch lifts longitudinal R@1m to 94.22% (same-area) and 50.02% (cross-area). Boosting [22] achieves near-perfect orientation (99.10% R@1°) but degrades position accuracy (10.01 m same-area), while PIDLoc balances both (0.11° / 1.15 m). CCVPE [34] and Song et al. [25] are competitive in same-area KITTI but degrade sharply in cross-area (9.16 m and 7.97 m respectively), whereas PIDLoc maintains 4.96 m, demonstrating superior generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the cross-view pose optimization line of work (HighlyAccurate, SIBCL, PureACL, VFA, Boosting, CCVPE, Song et al.), which iteratively refines a coarse pose by comparing ground-view and satellite-view features. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the PID controller framework from control theory: existing methods operate analogously to a proportional-only controller (relying solely on features at the given pose), while PIDLoc adds an integral branch (aggregating feature differences across a grid of pose candidates for global context) and a derivative branch (gradients of feature differences for fine-grained refinement). The spatially aware pose estimator (SPE) further departs from the per-feature independent estimation and confidence-weighted averaging used in prior work, instead modeling spatial dependencies via channel-shared MLPs with positional embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37.8% reduction in position error over the previous SOTA, particularly the large cross-area improvement (4.96m vs. 9.73m for SIBCL), demonstrates that explicitly modeling global and fine-grained contexts is critical for robust localization under large initial pose errors in repetitive urban environments. The PID analogy provides a principled, interpretable framework for combining complementary context types that could transfer to other iterative refinement tasks. Limitations include the requirement for LiDAR (restricting applicability to LiDAR-equipped vehicles), the 3-DoF assumption (no roll/pitch), and evaluation on only two datasets. The authors note future integration with SLAM pipelines to reduce loop-closure dependency, and the approach's generalization to 6-DoF or LiDAR-free settings remains open.
