## Problem

Existing joint structured pruning and quantization methods are rarely used in practice due to three limitations: they require multi-stage pipelines (search then retrain), they operate as black boxes without explicit control over final sparsity and bit width, and they are restricted to CNN architectures. No prior framework provides a one-shot, white-box, architecture-agnostic solution that simultaneously controls both compression knobs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes any quantization-aware DNN (CNN or transformer) with parameterized quantization layers inserted, along with standard training data, and produces a compressed model with a user-specified sparsity ratio K and per-layer bit widths constrained to [b_l, b_u]. Unlike post-hoc compression, the output is a jointly optimized set of pruned weights and quantization parameters (d, q_m, t) per layer, where the bit width is an emergent property of the learned quantization step size rather than a fixed hyperparameter.

## Method

GETA introduces three key components. First, a Quantization-Aware Dependency Graph (QADG) that merges attached branches (from weight quantization) and inserted branches (from activation quantization) into single vertices, enabling automatic pruning search space construction for any architecture. Second, a Partial Projected Stochastic Gradient (PPSG) method that projects only the quantization step size d onto a feasible interval derived from the bit width constraint, avoiding the instability of projecting q_m or t. Third, a joint learning strategy in the QASSO optimizer that uses a quantization-aware forget rate γ (Eq. 13) and step size d rule (Eq. 14) to progressively zero out redundant groups while adapting bit width to the amount of information removed, with a descent-direction guarantee (Proposition 5.1). The full training follows four stages: warm-up SGD, progressive bit-width projection, joint pruning-quantization, and cool-down fine-tuning.

## Results

On VGG7/CIFAR-10, GETA achieves 92.57% test accuracy at 0.41% relative BOPs, outperforming DJPQ (91.54%, 0.48%) and approaching BB (91.96%, 0.29%). On ResNet50/ImageNet, GETA at 40% sparsity reaches 75.10% top-1 accuracy with 6.97% relative BOPs, surpassing OBC (71.47%) and Clip-Q (73.70%). On BERT/SQuAD at 50% sparsity, GETA achieves 76.74% EM and 85.87% F1 versus 72.71% EM and 83.30% F1 for the OTO+PTQ sequential baseline, while also achieving better compression (14.4% vs 16.7% relative BOPs). On Phi2-2.7B, GETA consistently outperforms all four prune-then-quantize baselines (SliceGPT, LoraShear, LoraPrune, LLM-Pruner) across seven common-sense benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GETA outperforms all directly comparable joint structured pruning + quantization baselines. On VGG7/CIFAR-10, GETA beats DJPQ by 1.03% accuracy (92.57 vs 91.54) and 0.07% BOPs, and beats BB by 0.61% accuracy (92.57 vs 91.96) though BB achieves slightly lower BOPs (0.29 vs 0.41). On ResNet20/CIFAR-10, GETA surpasses ANNC by 0.52% accuracy and 1.6% BOPs, and edges out QST-B on BOPs (4.5% vs 5.1%) at a negligible 0.08% accuracy cost. On ResNet50/ImageNet, GETA (40% sparsity) leads OBC by 3.63% accuracy (75.10 vs 71.47) and Clip-Q by 1.40% (75.10 vs 73.70), though both baselines achieve marginally lower BOPs. On BERT/SQuAD, GETA dominates the OTO+PTQ sequential baseline by 4.03% EM at 50% sparsity (76.74 vs 72.71) while compressing more aggressively (14.4% vs 16.7% relative BOPs). On Phi2-2.7B, GETA outperforms all four prune-then-quantize baselines (SliceGPT, LoraShear, LoraPrune, LLM-Pruner) in average common-sense benchmark performance, confirming that joint optimization during training is superior to sequential compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

GETA extends the dependency-graph-based structured pruning lineage (OTOv2, HESSO) by introducing a quantization-aware dependency graph (QADG) that handles the attached and inserted branches created by parameterized quantization layers — a structural complication absent from prior pruning-only frameworks. It departs from existing joint pruning-and-quantization methods (DJPQ, BB, Clip-Q, ANNC) in two key ways: it is a one-shot, white-box optimizer with explicit per-layer bit-width and sparsity constraints (via the PPSG projection and the QASSO joint stage), rather than a two-stage or regularization-coefficient-based pipeline. The saliency-based group partitioning is inherited directly from HESSO, and the final dependency graph analysis step reuses the OTOv2 algorithm, positioning GETA as a natural unification of these two prior systems with quantization-aware training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GETA addresses a practical bottleneck in model compression: the engineering complexity and hyperparameter sensitivity that have kept joint pruning-and-quantization methods out of widespread use. By providing a single, architecture-agnostic, one-shot pipeline with explicit control over sparsity and bit width, it lowers the barrier for practitioners deploying compressed models on edge devices. Open questions include whether the framework scales to LLMs beyond ~3B parameters (the Phi-2 experiments are limited by single-GPU feasibility), whether the QADG construction generalizes to novel quantization schemes beyond the parameterized quantizer used here, and how the method performs when co-designed with specific hardware constraints (e.g., INT4/INT8 tensor-core layouts). The paper itself flags hardware-specific adaptation as future work.
