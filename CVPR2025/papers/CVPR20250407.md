## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks (e.g., VBench, EvalCrafter) evaluate fine-grained quality metrics like aesthetic score, temporal flickering, and spatial consistency, but none assess whether a model can coherently present multiple sequential events in a story. This story-completion capability is essential for future long video generation and industrial applications (commercials, educational content), yet even top T2V models fail to complete all events in simple 2–4 step stories, revealing a critical and unmeasured gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250407/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 423 text prompts, each describing a short story of 2–4 consecutive events (e.g., 'A man opens the refrigerator door, puts the elephant in, and then closes the door'), and feeds them to 11 T2V models to generate 4–10 second videos. For evaluation, key frames are sampled from each generated video and combined with the original prompt and an explicit event list as input to a VLM verifier. The output is a binary completion list (one 0/1 per event) and the average story completion rate, with contextual referent consistency enforced so that if the subject or object changes between events, later events are marked incomplete.

## Method

StoryEval constructs its prompt suite via three complementary approaches—retrieving and captioning real-world videos, human brainstorming of creative/impossible scenarios, and GPT-4o-guided generation—followed by post-processing filtration that removes prompts involving facial expressions, causing VLM verifier failures, or showing weak human-alignment. Evaluation employs a two-step VLM querying protocol: the verifier first describes the key frames in detail, then scores each event as completed (1) or not (0) using the description, prompt, and event list. To counteract VLM overestimation on blurry synthetic videos, the paper applies unanimous voting (N-over-N) across multiple independent evaluations (N=3 for GPT-4o, N=2 for LLaVA-OV-Chat-72B), which ablation shows yields the best human alignment (Kendall's τ = 0.93 vs. 0.75 for simple averaging). The benchmark is validated against human annotations on ~900 anonymized video-prompt pairs, confirming high rank correlation between automated and human scoring across all 7 categories.

## Results

On StoryEval, none of the 11 evaluated T2V models exceeds a 50% average story-completion rate, meaning even the best model successfully presents fewer than half of the prompted events. Kling-1.5 achieves the highest average completion rate at 40.1%, followed by Hailuo (35.1%) and Vchitect-2.0 (21.7%), while the weakest model (Open-Sora-Plan 1.3.0) scores only 9.4%. On the hardest subset ('Hard' class), no model surpasses 20% completion, and on 'Creative' stories, closed-source models (Kling-1.5: 36.0%, Hailuo: 18.0%) substantially outperform all open-source alternatives (best: Vchitect-2.0 at 15.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kling-1.5 leads all 11 models on average completion rate (40.1%), outperforming Hailuo (35.1%) by ~5 points and Pika-1.5 (19.4%) by over 20 points, with the largest gap on 'Creative' stories (36.0% vs. 18.0% and 20.6% respectively). Among open-source models, Vchitect-2.0 (21.7%) surpasses the closed-source Pika-1.5 (19.4%), indicating that open-source models can be competitive on story completion. The paper further contrasts StoryEval with VBench's temporal consistency metric (Table 2), revealing a tradeoff: Vchitect-2.0 ranks 2nd on StoryEval but 7th on temporal consistency, while Open-Sora-Plan 1.3.0 ranks 4th on VBench but last on StoryEval (9.4%). Closed-source models consistently dominate the 'Creative' class (Kling-1.5: 36.0%, Hailuo: 18.0%) over all open-source models (best: Vchitect-2.0: 15.2%), suggesting imaginative story presentation remains a significant gap for open-source T2V systems. On the 'Retrieval' class (where real-world videos serve as a 100% upper bound), Hailuo achieves the highest score (42.6%), yet no model approaches the trivial upper bound, confirming that even in-distribution stories are not reliably completed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in T2V evaluation by shifting the focus from fine-grained quality metrics (aesthetic score, temporal flickering, subject consistency) used by VBench, EvalCrafter, and T2V-Bench to a coarser but arguably more fundamental question: does the generated video actually present the sequential events described in the prompt? Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes the VLM-as-judge paradigm (GPT-4o, LLaVA-OV-Chat-72B) with a two-step querying protocol and unanimous voting to make event-level completion scoring reliable. The prompt construction pipeline—combining real-world video retrieval, human brainstorming, and GPT-4o-guided generation—draws on standard data curation practices but applies them to the novel goal of creating multi-event story prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that no evaluated model exceeds 50% story completion rate while simultaneously scoring 95%+ on temporal consistency—exposes a fundamental disconnect between local visual quality and global narrative coherence in current T2V systems. This gap is particularly acute for creative/out-of-distribution stories, suggesting that models trained on real-world video distributions struggle with imaginative sequences. Open questions include whether architectural changes (e.g., explicit event planning, autoregressive token-level story structure) can close this gap, whether the 2–4 event / 10-second constraint is the binding limitation, and how story completion scales as video length increases. The benchmark's reliance on VLM verifiers, despite strong human alignment (Kendall's τ = 0.93), remains a potential vulnerability if future models generate videos that systematically fool VLM judges.
