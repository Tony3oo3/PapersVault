## Problem

Existing active reconstruction methods based on NeRFs are computationally expensive, limiting real-time exploration and often neglecting photometric reconstruction quality in favor of geometric accuracy. Additionally, many prior approaches restrict camera motion to constrained spaces (teleporting, hemispheres, or 2D planes), reducing flexibility in complex 3D environments. This paper addresses both limitations by leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting's real-time rendering to enable unrestricted 6DoF active exploration that simultaneously optimizes geometric completeness and photometric fidelity.

## Input / Output

The system consumes posed RGB-D images at 680×1200 resolution (60° vertical, 90° horizontal FOV) from the Habitat simulator, with a 5 cm voxel grid for the exploration map. It incrementally maintains a 3D Gaussian Map (each Gaussian parameterized by color, center, radius, and opacity) and outputs rendered RGB/depth images, a next-best-view goal pose selected from a candidate pool, and a collision-free RRT path. The system operates in two phases: an exploration phase producing the Exploration Model, and a post-refinement phase producing a Refinement Model optimized for rendering quality.

## Method

ActiveGAMER builds on SplaTAM's simplified isotropic Gaussian mapping backbone and introduces a rendering-based information gain module that computes, for each candidate viewpoint, the number of missing pixels in the rendered silhouette mask weighted by travel distance via a softmax-normalized formulation. A coarse-to-fine exploration strategy first covers the scene with sparse candidates (1 m steps, 5 viewing directions) then refines with denser sampling (0.5 m steps, 15 directions), while a global-local keyframe selection strategy balances local overlapping frames with global supporting keyframes (selected by completeness >10% new pixels or below-threshold rendering quality) to prevent local overfitting. A post-refinement stage further optimizes the Gaussian Map for photometric quality using global keyframes, trading some geometric completeness for improved rendering fidelity. Path planning uses an efficient RRT implementation on the occupancy-based exploration map with a rotation planning module for smooth orientation transitions.

## Results

On MP3D, ActiveGAMER achieves 1.66 cm accuracy, 2.30 cm completion, and 95.32% completion ratio, substantially outperforming the closest competitor NARUTO (6.31 cm, 3.00 cm, 90.18%). On Replica, the method attains 32.02 dB PSNR, 0.97 SSIM, 0.11 LPIPS, and 1.12 cm depth L1 for novel view rendering, surpassing both the passive SplaTAM baseline (29.08 dB PSNR) and the active NARUTO baseline (26.01 dB PSNR) by wide margins. The ablation on Replica confirms that the full pipeline (coarse-to-fine exploration + global-local keyframes + post-refinement) yields the best combined geometric and photometric performance (95.45% completion ratio, 32.02 dB PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActiveGAMER outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the frontier-based FBE [68], UPEN [19], and OccAnt [50] (which report only completion metrics on MP3D), the method reduces completion error from 9.4–10.6 cm to 2.30 cm and raises completion ratio from ~70% to 95.32%. Against ANM [69], the nearest prior active neural mapping method, ActiveGAMER improves accuracy from 7.80 cm to 1.66 cm and completion ratio from 73.15% to 95.32%. Against NARUTO [18], the strongest active reconstruction baseline, the method cuts accuracy error by 74% (6.31→1.66 cm) and gains 5.14 points in completion ratio; on Replica rendering, it also leads NARUTO by 6.01 dB PSNR and reduces depth L1 from 9.54 cm to 1.12 cm. Against the passive SplaTAM [25] baseline (evaluated only on Replica rendering), ActiveGAMER improves PSNR by 2.94 dB and LPIPS from 0.14 to 0.11, demonstrating that active exploration with 3DGS yields meaningfully better photometric fidelity than manual scanning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ActiveGAMER builds directly on SplaTAM's simplified 3D Gaussian Splatting mapping backbone (isotropic Gaussians with view-independent color) and extends it into the active reconstruction setting, a task previously dominated by NeRF-based methods (ANM, NARUTO, ActiveNeRF) that are computationally limited in rendering speed. The key conceptual departure is exploiting 3DGS's real-time rendering to compute a rendering-based information gain (missing silhouette pixels) for next-best-view planning, something infeasible with implicit radiance fields. The coarse-to-fine exploration strategy and global-local keyframe selection are novel system-level contributions that address limitations of SplaTAM's purely local keyframe optimization, while the local path planner is adapted from NARUTO's RRT-based approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is among the first systems to demonstrate that 3DGS's rendering efficiency can be directly leveraged for active exploration planning, opening a path toward real-time autonomous 3D mapping in robotics. The large geometric and photometric gains over NARUTO (1.66 cm vs. 6.31 cm accuracy; 32.02 vs. 26.01 dB PSNR) validate 3DGS as a practical backbone for active vision. However, the system assumes known localization and perfect action execution, and the rendering-based information gain may miss occluded or double-sided surfaces, leaving open questions about robustness in real-world deployment with motion constraints and sensor noise.
