## Problem

Despite weight decay being ubiquitous in deep learning practice, there is no convergence theory for SGD with weight decay (SGDW) in the context of the original unregularized model under mild assumptions, and no theoretical proof explaining why weight decay improves generalization in nonconvex optimization. Prior analyses either required bounded gradient assumptions, analyzed the ℓ₂-regularized surrogate rather than the original objective, or could not characterize the generalization benefit of weight decay.

## Input / Output

The empirical component trains standard RGB image classifiers (VGG16 on CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ResNet20 on CIFAR-100 at 32×32, ResNet18 on ImageNet at 224×224) using supervised cross-entropy loss. The theoretical component takes a nonconvex loss function with Lipschitz gradients and bounded variance as input, and produces convergence rate bounds on the average gradient norm of the unregularized objective plus generalization error bounds expressed in terms of the dataset size n, iteration count T, and the alignment quantity δ_T between the gradient and the iterate.

## Method

The paper establishes convergence of SGDW for the unregularized objective under only gradient Lipschitz and bounded variance assumptions, showing that weight decay does not accelerate convergence and in fact slows it when the gradient-weight alignment δ_T < 1. For generalization, it builds on the algorithmic gradient stability framework of Lei (2023) to prove that weight decay tightens the stability bound B_A from O(√(T/n)) (no decay) to O(√(γT/n)) (large decay), yielding a generalization error of O(1/((1−δ_T)^{1/4} n^{1/4})) that strictly improves over the O(1/n^{1/6}) bound for vanilla SGD when δ_T is sufficiently small. The analysis is extended to SignSGD with momentum and weight decay (SignSGDW), where the ℓ₁-norm structure introduces a √d factor but eliminates the need for bounded gradients in the generalization proof. Two weight-decay regimes are characterized: a small-decay regime (1−α ≤ γ²) matching decoupled weight decay practice, and a large-decay regime (1−α = γ) that reveals the generalization advantage.

## Results

The paper establishes that weight decay does not accelerate nonconvex SGD convergence: under a small decay rate (1−α ≤ min{γ², 1/(2T)}), SGDW achieves the standard O(1/√T) rate, while under a large decay rate (1−α = γ), the rate degrades to O(1/((1−δ_T)√T)) where δ_T < 1 measures gradient-weight alignment. For generalization, Theorem 2 shows that with 1−α = 1/√T and T = n/(1−δ_T), SGDW achieves O(1/((1−δ_T)^{1/4} n^{1/4})), which strictly improves over the O(1/n^{1/6}) bound for decay-free SGD when δ_T ≪ 1 − 1/n^{1/3}. Empirically on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet (VGG16, ResNet20, ResNet18), moderate weight decay (e.g., wd=1e-3) consistently improves test accuracy over no weight decay, while SGDW outperforms SignSGDW in generalization across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three configurations: SGD without weight decay, SGDW, and SignSGDW. On convergence, SGD without weight decay (wd=0) achieves the fastest training loss decrease across all three benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet), confirming that weight decay slows convergence. On generalization, SGDW with moderate weight decay (wd=1e-3 for CIFAR tasks, wd=5e-6 for ImageNet) consistently outperforms SGD without weight decay in both test accuracy and test loss, validating Theorem 2's prediction that δ_T < 1 yields a tighter generalization bound. SGDW outperforms SignSGDW in generalization on all three benchmarks (Figure 7), consistent with the √d factor penalty in SignSGDW's bounds. Notably, SignSGDW is more robust to large weight decay values (e.g., wd=5e-2) than SGDW, which fails to converge on CIFAR-10 at that setting, reflecting the different hyperparameter regimes in Theorems 1 and 3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the algorithmic gradient stability framework of Lei (COLT 2023) for nonconvex generalization analysis and integrates the momentum-augmented SignSGD convergence theory of Sun et al. (ICML 2023) to extend the analysis to sign-based methods. It departs from prior weight decay analyses (e.g., Xie et al. 2023, Bjorck et al. 2021) that either treated weight decay as equivalent to L2 regularization or required bounded gradient assumptions, by establishing convergence and generalization theory for SGDW in the context of the original unregularized model under only Lipschitz gradient and bounded variance conditions. The paper also extends the scope from standard SGD to sign-based stochastic gradient methods, providing the first unified treatment of weight decay's role across both families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical proof that weight decay improves generalization in nonconvex optimization, identifying the key mechanism as the non-linear-dependence between gradient and weight vectors (δ_T < 1). The finding that weight decay does not accelerate convergence—rather, it slows it—clarifies a long-standing practical observation and explains why decoupled weight decay (with diminishing effective decay) outperforms fixed L2 regularization. Open questions include whether the δ_T condition can be verified or controlled a priori, how these results extend to adaptive optimizers like Adam where the equivalence between weight decay and L2 regularization breaks, and whether the specific hyperparameter relationships (1−α = O(γ²) or 1−α = γ) are tight or merely sufficient.
