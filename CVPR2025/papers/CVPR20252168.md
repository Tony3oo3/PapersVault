## Problem

Large-scale diffusion model training requires synchronized gradient communication across thousands of GPUs within a single high-bandwidth cluster, making infrastructure expensive, fragile to hardware failures, and inaccessible to academic or smaller research groups. No existing method allows diffusion models to be trained across independent, isolated compute nodes without a centralized networking fabric while still achieving equivalent or better generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252168/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a text prompt (encoded via T5 XL or CLIP) or class label plus random noise as input and produces a generated image. The router conditions on the noisy latent x_t and the text/class condition to predict per-expert relevance scores, and the top-1 selected expert's flow prediction is used for the denoising step. The full ensemble can optionally be distilled into a single dense DiT that accepts the same inputs and produces images without any routing overhead.

## Method

Decentralized Flow Matching (DFM) decomposes the marginal flow-matching objective into a linear combination of per-cluster expert flows, showing that the global objective is exactly recovered by an ensemble of independently trained experts. Training data is partitioned into K clusters (via DINOv2 features consolidated to K coarse centroids), and each expert is a standard DiT trained with the usual flow-matching loss on its assigned subset with zero cross-model communication. A lightweight router (small DiT with a learned CLS token and linear classification head) is trained separately with cross-entropy to predict which data partition a noisy sample belongs to. At inference, top-1 expert selection yields FLOP cost comparable to a monolithic model while providing K× more parameters, and the paper shows this outperforms a same-FLOP monolith. An optional distillation step compresses the K-expert ensemble into a single dense model matching monolith performance at one-third the training FLOPs.

## Results

On ImageNet (DiT XL/2, 895M params), the 8-expert DDM achieves FID 6.081 at 800k training steps, a 28% improvement over the monolithic baseline (FID 8.494) at equal total FLOPs. On LAION Aesthetics, the DDM reaches FID 6.48 at just 200k steps versus the monolith's 6.52 at 800k steps, representing a 4× training speedup with a lower convergence FID. Top-1 expert selection is the most efficient inference strategy (FID 9.84, 334 GFLOPs), outperforming the full ensemble (FID 10.52, 2490 GFLOPs) and all other combination methods. At scale, eight 3B-parameter FLUX MMDiT experts (24B total) trained on eight independent GPU nodes in under a week produce high-quality 1024×1024 image generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a monolithic DiT XL/2 diffusion model (895M parameters) trained with the same total FLOPs, with batch size divided evenly among experts to ensure fair comparison (e.g., 256 monolith batch → 8×32 expert batches). The 8-expert DDM consistently outperforms the monolith FLOP-for-FLOP on both ImageNet (FID 6.081 vs 8.494 at 800k steps) and LAION Aesthetics (FID 6.48 at 200k steps vs 6.52 at 800k steps). The performance gain is attributed to the additional parametrization from multiple specialized experts, which the paper shows is a key factor: 4-expert and 16-expert configurations underperform the 8-expert sweet spot. The router adds only 4% training FLOP overhead, and the DDM's correctness is verified by showing that deterministic samples from the DDM and monolith given the same noise input are highly correlated (Figure 5). For deployment, distilling the DDM ensemble into a single dense model matches the monolith's FID (7.76 vs 7.82) at one-third the training FLOPs, making the approach practical for production serving.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper decomposes the flow matching objective (Lipman et al., 2022) into a linear combination of per-cluster expert flows, showing that the marginal flow naturally factors into a router-weighted sum of data-expert flows. At inference, it borrows the sparse-activation principle from Mixture of Experts (Fedus et al., 2022), but routes entire data distributions to separate expert denoisers rather than routing individual tokens to different FFN layers. The approach is conceptually adjacent to low-communication training methods like DiLoCo and Branch-Train-Merge, but departs from them by eliminating all cross-cluster communication during training and by providing a principled objective (DFM) that guarantees the ensemble optimizes the same global loss as a monolithic model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 24B-parameter diffusion model trained on just eight independent 8-GPU nodes in under a week removes the dependency on monolithic, high-bandwidth clusters that currently gatekeep large-scale diffusion training. The FLOP-for-FLOP outperformance (28% lower FID on ImageNet, 4× training speedup on LAION) suggests that the additional parametrization from specialized experts is strictly beneficial, not merely a cost of decentralization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video generation, whether combining DDM with step-reduction distillation (consistency models, DMD) yields further gains, and how performance scales beyond the empirically optimal 8-expert configuration. The memory overhead of loading K experts remains a deployment concern, partially addressed by the dense distillation procedure.
