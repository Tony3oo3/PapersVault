## Problem

While uniformity in feature space has been extensively studied for imbalanced classification (where representations form discrete clusters), the representation geometry of imbalanced regression remains unexplored. Regression requires continuous, ordered representations rather than separated clusters, making classification-based uniformity metrics inapplicable. Specifically, how data representations distribute across the entire feature space — beyond just maintaining label-order consistency — has not been examined for deep imbalanced regression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250143/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous inputs (facial images, sentence pairs, tabular feature vectors, or input functions paired with domain points) and produces a continuous scalar regression value or function value. Internally, all representations are L2-normalized to lie on the unit hypersphere, and labels are discretized into K bins (e.g., 1-year bins for age, 0.1 bins for similarity scores). The geometric losses operate on a surrogate of K bin centroids spanning the full label range, not on raw mini-batch representations, since a single batch typically covers only a subset of the label spectrum.

## Method

The core contribution is two geometric losses applied to the 'latent trace' — the ordered path of bin centroids from minimum to maximum label. The enveloping loss maximizes the volume of a tubular neighborhood around the trace relative to the full hypersphere (approximated via Monte Carlo sampling of N uniform points), encouraging the trace to fill the feature space surface. The homogeneity loss penalizes the squared arc length of the trace, ensuring smoothness and uniform spacing of representations along it (formally minimized iff representations are evenly distributed, per Theorem 1). Because a single mini-batch rarely spans the full label range, a Surrogate-driven Representation Learning (SRL) framework constructs a complete surrogate by averaging in-batch representations per bin and 're-filling' missing bins with stored centroids from the previous epoch (with momentum). A contrastive loss then pulls individual representations toward their bin centroid while repelling them from other centroids, and the total training objective combines MSE regression loss, the geometric losses, and the contrastive term.

## Results

On AgeDB-DIR, SRL achieves the best overall MAE of 7.22 (vs. 7.41 for RankSim/ConR) and the best few-shot MAE of 9.81 (vs. 9.93 for BalancedMSE), while also leading on geometric mean (GM) across all regions (4.50 All, 6.29 Few). On UCI-DIR, SRL attains the lowest MAE on all four tabular tasks (e.g., 5.10 on Airfoil, 4.16 on Abalone, 0.28 on Real Estate, 5.94 on Concrete) with particularly large gains in the few-shot region (e.g., 6.60 vs. 9.74 on Concrete). On the new Imbalanced Operator Learning benchmark (OL-DIR), SRL reduces few-shot MAE from 27.00 (VANILLA) to 9.33 for the linear operator and from 19.77 to 17.00 for the nonlinear operator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRL outperforms all six baselines on the overall MAE for AgeDB-DIR (7.22 vs. 7.41 for RankSim and ConR, 7.55 for LDS+FDS) and achieves the best few-shot MAE (9.81) and GM (6.29) across all regions. On UCI-DIR, SRL dominates every sub-task's 'All' and 'Few' MAE, with the largest gap on Concrete few-shot (6.60 vs. 9.74 for VANILLA, a 32% reduction). On IMDB-WIKI-DIR, SRL leads on All MAE (7.69 vs. 7.72 RankSim) and Few-shot GM (15.25 vs. 15.51 BalancedMSE), though the margins are smaller than on AgeDB. On STS-B-DIR, SRL achieves the best MSE (0.877 All, 0.745 Few) and highest Pearson correlation in the medium and few-shot regions (0.749, 0.844). On the new OL-DIR operator learning benchmark, SRL substantially outperforms both VANILLA DeepONet and Ordinal Entropy, reducing linear few-shot MAE by 66% (9.33 vs. 27.00) and nonlinear few-shot MAE by 14% (17.00 vs. 19.77). The paper also shows SRL is orthogonal to existing DIR methods: combining SRL with LDS+FDS, RankSim, BalancedMSE, or ConR yields additional 1–17% MAE improvements over those methods alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the concept of uniformity from imbalanced classification (where it means even distribution of discrete class centroids on a hypersphere) to the continuous, ordered setting of regression, where representations form a trace rather than clusters. It departs from prior DIR methods (LDS+FDS, RankSim, BalancedMSE, ConR) that focus on training unbiased regressors or smoothing label/feature densities, by instead imposing explicit geometric constraints (enveloping and homogeneity) on how the latent trace occupies the feature space. The SRL framework is a practical mechanism borrowed from the idea of maintaining a running estimate across epochs, but its specific application to enable global geometric losses over a full label range from local mini-batches is novel. The paper also extends the DIR setting to operator learning (IOL), a task not previously explored in the imbalanced regression literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly study representation learning for deep imbalanced regression, filling a conceptual gap where prior DIR methods focused on loss reweighting or density smoothing without addressing how features distribute in the latent space. The geometric framing (enveloping + homogeneity) provides a principled, interpretable lens for understanding under-representation in regression, and the method's orthogonality to existing DIR techniques makes it a composable regularizer. Limitations include inability to handle higher-dimensional labels, added computational overhead from the surrogate mechanism and Monte Carlo approximation, and the reliance on pre-binned labels. Open questions include whether the geometric constraints generalize to multi-output or structured regression, and whether the surrogate mechanism can be replaced by more efficient global estimators.
