## Problem

Existing Deep Unfolding Networks for image compressive sensing suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) insufficient multi-scale feature representation within each iterative reconstruction phase, and (2) significant loss of high-dimensional feature information during phase-to-phase transmission. These issues collectively cap the achievable reconstruction quality, as intermediate features are either underexploited or discarded before contributing to the final output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252050/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1D measurement vector y obtained by applying a convolution-based sampling operator S(·) to image patches of size H×W (default 64×64) at compressive sensing ratios τ ∈ {0.01, 0.04, 0.10, 0.25, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50}. The output is a reconstructed image patch that is reassembled via the inverse block function F_B^{-1}(·) into the full image. The network uses 7 iterative phases (n=7) with 3 internal scale levels (Θ=3) per phase and 48 feature channels by default.

## Method

HUNet maps each iterative step of the traditional homotopy algorithm to a Multi-scale Homotopy Iterative Module (MHIM) that performs gradient descent, expands the signal to C channels via a Deep Feature Extraction Block, then processes it through a U-shaped encoder-decoder of Window-based Transformer Blocks (WTBs) using Permuted Window Attention and Depthwise MLPs for efficient multi-scale feature extraction. At the bottleneck, a Deep Homotopy Continuation Strategy (DHCS) replaces the scalar soft-thresholding operator with a learnable, channel-wise tensor η_k = η_0·γ_k, preserving the homotopy path's mathematical structure in high-dimensional space while enabling dynamic, data-driven regularization. A Dual-path Feature Fusion Module (DFFM) then collects inter-phase feature maps {x_k} (concatenated along channels) and intra-phase high-dimensional maps {ω_k} (summed) from all 7 phases, integrating them through a 3×3 convolution, DMLP, Squeeze-and-Excitation re-weighting, and a learnable scaling λ on the intra-phase path before final channel compression, thereby mitigating the feature loss that plagues sequential-only DUN architectures.

## Results

HUNet achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all six main benchmark datasets (LIVE29, BSD68, OST300, Set14, McM18, General100) at all four CS ratios (0.01, 0.04, 0.10, 0.25). Average PSNR improvements over the strongest competitor range from 0.28 dB (LIVE29, vs. NesTD-Net) to 0.81 dB (General100, vs. OCTUF), with average SSIM gains of 0.005–0.011. On Set11 at high CS ratios (0.30–0.50), HUNet leads the best baseline (CPP-Net) by 0.31 dB average PSNR and 0.0003 SSIM. The model uses 21.1M parameters and 207.2 GFLOPs, comparable to NesTD-Net (21.44M / 347.92 GFLOPs) and CPP-Net (16.9M / 205.24 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUNet outperforms all 19 compared baselines on every dataset and CS ratio tested, with no losses or ties. The closest competitors are NesTD-Net (TIP 2024), OCTUF (CVPR 2023), and DGU-Net+ (CVPR 2022), which trail HUNet by 0.28–0.81 dB average PSNR depending on dataset. At the most challenging CS ratio of 0.01, the margin narrows to ~0.1–0.2 dB (e.g., 22.55 vs. 22.39 on LIVE29), while at 0.25 the gap widens to 0.5–0.7 dB (e.g., 33.22 vs. 32.70 on LIVE29). On Set11 at high CS ratios (0.30–0.50), HUNet leads CPP-Net (CVPR 2024) by 0.26–0.35 dB and NesTD-Net by 0.24–0.35 dB. Transformer-based methods (UFC-Net, CSformer, TCS-Net) and pure CNN baselines (DPA-Net, CSNet+) show larger gaps, with UFC-Net trailing by 0.5–1.1 dB on average. The consistent superiority across all settings suggests the multi-scale homotopy iterative design and dual-path fusion provide robust gains regardless of sampling rate or image content complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUNet extends the Deep Unfolding Network (DUN) lineage initiated by ISTA-Net, but departs from the dominant ISTA/NESTA-based unfolding by instead unrolling the homotopy algorithm (Zhang et al., AISTATS 2015), which traces solutions along a path of decreasing regularization parameters. The key architectural departure from prior DUNs (OCTUF, UFC-Net, CPP-Net, NesTD-Net) is twofold: (1) each iterative phase is replaced by a Multi-scale Homotopy Iterative Module (MHIM) containing a U-shaped stack of window-based Transformer blocks for intra-phase multi-scale feature extraction, and (2) a Dual-path Feature Fusion Module (DFFM) collects both inter-phase and intra-phase feature maps to mitigate the information loss that occurs when only the final output of each phase is passed forward. The window-based attention mechanism (PWA/PSWA) is borrowed from SRFormer, and the soft-thresholding operator is generalized into the Deep Homotopy Continuation Strategy (DHCS) to operate in high-dimensional channel space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-identified weakness in DUNs—feature degradation during sequential phase-to-phase transmission—by introducing a dual-path fusion that bypasses the bottleneck, yielding consistent 0.2–0.7 dB PSNR gains across seven datasets and four sampling ratios. The homotopy-path interpretation provides a principled, interpretable alternative to the more common ISTA/NESTA unfolding, and the ablation confirms that both the multi-scale intra-phase processing and the dual-path fusion contribute meaningfully. Open questions include whether the homotopy formulation offers advantages over other iterative strategies at scale, whether the approach generalizes to other inverse problems (MRI, phase retrieval), and whether the modest gains justify the added architectural complexity compared to simpler DUN variants.
