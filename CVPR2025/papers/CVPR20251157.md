## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks focus on general-purpose tasks (action recognition, captioning, temporal reasoning) over natural scenes, while multi-discipline benchmarks for expert-level reasoning are limited to text and static images. There is no benchmark that evaluates whether foundation models can apply domain-specific knowledge to perform expert-level reasoning over specialized-domain videos in fields like healthcare, engineering, and science, where temporal dynamics and procedural knowledge are essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251157/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each example pairs a CC-licensed video clip (average 51.4 s, max 228 s; audio removed, minimal on-screen text to enforce visual comprehension) with a text question in either 5-option multiple-choice or open-ended format. The model produces a final answer, and evaluation uses GPT-4o to extract and judge correctness. Uniquely, each example also ships with expert-annotated step-by-step reasoning rationale and a list of relevant domain-knowledge concepts linked to Wikipedia pages, enabling fine-grained error attribution beyond simple accuracy.

## Method

MMVU is constructed via a textbook-guided annotation pipeline: 67 domain experts first identify key concepts from authoritative textbooks across 27 subjects in four disciplines, then locate CC-licensed YouTube videos that visually illustrate those concepts, and finally author 2–3 questions per video requiring both video comprehension and domain knowledge to answer. Each question is annotated with a detailed solution rationale and linked domain knowledge, and all examples undergo human expert validation to confirm that video content is necessary for answering (523 examples revised, 72 excluded). The benchmark is split into a 1,000-example validation set and a hidden 2,000-example test set. Evaluation covers 36 frontier multimodal models using both direct-answer and chain-of-thought prompts, with a human expert study (closed-book, open-book, oracle) establishing performance baselines, followed by a six-category error analysis (visual perception, domain-knowledge misuse in perception, domain-knowledge misuse in reasoning, textual reliance, logical inconsistency, other) grounded in the expert-annotated rationales.

## Results

On the MMVU test set (2,000 examples), the top-performing model o1 achieves 76.1% average accuracy with CoT prompting, followed by Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking (69.5%) and GPT-4o (66.7%). These scores remain substantially below human expert performance: 86.8% in the open-book setting and 95.3% in the oracle setting. Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL-72B leads at 53.2%, still trailing the best proprietary models by over 20 points. CoT reasoning generally improves performance over direct answering, with gains ranging from marginal (GPT-4o) to substantial (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, +11.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMVU evaluates 36 frontier multimodal models, revealing a clear hierarchy. o1 (76.1%) leads all proprietary models, outperforming GPT-4o (66.7%) by 9.4 points and Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking (69.5%) by 6.6 points, confirming the advantage of System-2 long-CoT reasoning. Among proprietary models, GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash are closely matched (66.7% vs. 66.5%), while Claude 3.5 Sonnet (64.1%) and Grok-2-Vision (63.4%) trail by roughly 3 points. The gap between proprietary and open-source models is stark: the best open-source model, Qwen2-VL-72B (53.2%), lags o1 by 22.9 points, though it exceeds the human closed-book baseline (49.7%). DeepSeek-VL2 (51.5%) and InternVL2.5-38B (50.7%) follow, while smaller models like H2OVL Mississippi-2B (28.8%) and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B (28.7%) perform near chance level. Notably, no model approaches the 86.8% human open-book accuracy, underscoring that expert-level video reasoning remains an open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMVU extends the multi-discipline evaluation line (MMMU, MMMU-Pro, CMMMU) from static images to the video modality, and simultaneously extends general-purpose video benchmarks (VideoMME, MMBench-Video) from natural-scene comprehension to expert-level, knowledge-intensive reasoning. The closest prior effort, MMWorld, also targets multi-discipline video understanding but relies heavily on LLM-generated examples (76.4%) and only 39.5% of its data requires domain expertise. MMVU departs from both lines by mandating that every example be annotated from scratch by human experts using a textbook-guided pipeline, and by requiring that video comprehension (not just text) is necessary for correct answering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark quantifies a substantial and previously unmeasured gap: even the strongest System-2 model (o1, 76.1%) trails human open-book performance (86.8%) by over 10 points, and most models fall 20+ points below. The error taxonomy (27% domain-knowledge-in-reasoning failures, 20% heavy reliance on text) gives concrete targets for improving how models integrate specialized knowledge with temporal visual evidence. Open questions include whether scaling test-time compute (System-2 thinking) can close the remaining gap, how to better ground domain knowledge in video-specific visual cues, and whether the CC-licensed video constraint limits coverage of certain subjects (e.g., sports).
