## Problem

Generating articulated 3D objects as compact triangle meshes with functional part motions remains underexplored. Existing approaches either produce over-smoothed implicit geometry (e.g., via Marching Cubes), rely on retrieval-based part assembly that introduces geometric inconsistencies, or lack the ability to model articulation structures jointly with part geometry. The core challenge is producing clean, artist-like mesh geometry that respects joint constraints while maintaining diversity in articulation configurations.

## Input / Output

The method operates primarily unconditionally, sampling structures and meshes from learned distributions without any input. For conditional generation, it accepts 3D point clouds or sketch images injected as condition tokens into the structure transformer. The output is a complete articulated triangle mesh decomposed into functional parts, each with <700 faces (post-decimation), along with articulation properties (joint type from {fixed, revolute, prismatic}, 3D joint location, and unit-vector orientation). The mesh is assembled by merging close vertices across parts, and can be animated across articulation states via the predicted joint parameters.

## Method

MeshArt decomposes articulated mesh generation into two hierarchical autoregressive stages, both formulated as triangle sequence prediction over learned codebooks. First, a VQ-VAE encodes triangulated part bounding boxes (augmented with CLIP semantic features, latent geometry, and articulation properties) into quantized structure tokens via residual vector quantization (RQ, depth 2, 6 codes per face); a GPT-2-small decoder-only transformer then autoregressively predicts this structure sequence. Second, a separate VQ-VAE encodes part mesh triangles along with junction-face probabilities into geometry tokens; a GPT-2-medium transformer generates each part's triangle sequence conditioned on the global structure tokens (via cross-attention) and junction face tokens from spatially neighboring parts, ensuring smooth inter-part connectivity. A flexible positional embedding scheme fixes positions for the mesh tokens while applying a constant embedding to the variable-length condition prefix, preventing positional inconsistency. The key unifying insight is representing bounding boxes as triangle meshes, enabling both structure and geometry to share the same sequence-prediction formulation.

## Results

On the Articulated PartNet dataset (chairs, tables, storage furniture), MeshArt achieves an average 57.1% improvement in structure coverage (COV) over baselines and a 209-point average improvement in mesh generation FID. Specifically, on mesh generation (Table 3), MeshArt reduces FID from 267.7/252.6/170.6 (NAP) to 40.8/14.3/8.1 across chair/table/storage categories, and KID from 0.263/0.238/0.167 to 0.008/0.002/0.002. On structure generation (Table 2), MeshArt achieves COV of 43.3/40.2/39.1 vs. 28.3/21.1/30.6 (NAP) and 32.9/25.9/33.4 (CAGE), while also attaining the lowest MMD in all categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshArt is compared against two baselines: NAP (NeurIPS 2023) and CAGE (CVPR 2024). Against NAP, MeshArt wins decisively on all shape-quality and perceptual-quality metrics across all three categories: COV improves by 7–11 points, MMD drops by 0.8–2.1 (×10⁻³), FID drops by 162–238 points, and KID drops by 1–2 orders of magnitude. The 1-NNA metric is slightly lower for MeshArt (73–83 vs. 89–94), but since 50% is the optimal value, MeshArt's scores are actually closer to the ideal, indicating better distributional balance. Against CAGE (compared only on structure generation in Table 2), MeshArt achieves substantially higher COV (e.g., 40.2 vs. 25.9 on tables) and lower MMD (2.3 vs. 3.9 on tables), while CAGE's generated structures exhibit repetitive patterns that limit coverage. The paper attributes NAP's weaker visual quality to its reliance on Marching Cubes for decoding, which produces over-smoothed and over-tessellated meshes, whereas MeshArt's direct triangle generation preserves sharp geometric details.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshArt extends the autoregressive mesh generation line (MeshGPT, PolyGen) from static objects to articulated ones by introducing a two-stage hierarchical decomposition: a structure transformer predicts part bounding boxes with articulation properties, and a geometry transformer then generates each part's triangles conditioned on that structure. It departs from prior articulated object generators (NAP, CAGE) that rely on implicit neural fields or part retrieval, instead producing compact triangle meshes directly via quantized triangle embeddings. The junction-face conditioning mechanism, which injects local connectivity cues from already-generated neighboring parts into the geometry codebook, is a novel design not present in either the static mesh or articulated object literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× expansion of articulated object training data (Articulated PartNet) is a practically valuable resource for the field, and the hierarchical triangle-sequence formulation offers a clean, unified framework that could extend to more categories and object types. However, the non-differentiable codebook selection limits enforcement of physical plausibility (e.g., joint collisions), and the dataset, while larger, remains modest for large-scale training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-joint objects, whether the triangle-based structure parameterization generalizes beyond the three tested categories, and how to incorporate differentiable physics constraints into the autoregressive generation loop.
