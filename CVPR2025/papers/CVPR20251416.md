## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting optimization still takes tens of minutes per scene, and existing acceleration methods either prune primitives (sacrificing quality) or refine the rendering pipeline (engineering-only gains). The paper identifies that computational resources are misallocated across the optimization timeline: early iterations waste time rendering high-resolution images with sparse primitives, while late iterations spend disproportionate time on a large primitive count that yields diminishing quality returns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251416/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the standard 3DGS inputs—posed RGB training images and an SfM-derived point cloud—but dynamically downsamples the training views to progressively higher resolutions during optimization according to a scene-adaptive schedule. The rendering resolution is a continuous scalar that is floored to integer values in practice. The output is a set of optimized 3D Gaussian primitives (center, covariance, opacity, color) that render novel views; the method is designed as a plug-and-play wrapper that can be layered onto any existing 3DGS backbone without modifying its internal parameters.

## Method

DashGaussian formulates 3DGS optimization as progressively fitting higher-frequency components of the training views, and schedules two coupled variables—rendering resolution and primitive count—across the optimization timeline. The frequency-guided resolution scheduler computes the DFT energy of training views at multiple resolutions, defines a 'significance' ratio between high- and low-resolution frequency content, and uses it to determine at which iteration to switch between resolution levels, producing a scene-adaptive resolution curve. The resolution-guided primitive scheduler then couples primitive growth to the scheduled resolution via a concave-up curve (Eq. 4) that suppresses early densification and encourages mid-stage growth, while a momentum-based budgeting rule (Eq. 5) adaptively estimates the final primitive count from the natural densification rate rather than relying on dataset-specific heuristics. Together, these schedulers reduce the area under the optimization-complexity curve by ~45.7% on average across backbones without degrading rendering quality.

## Results

DashGaussian (plugged into Taming-3DGS) completes 3DGS optimization in 3.23 min on Mip-NeRF 360, 2.20 min on Deep Blending, and 2.62 min on Tanks and Temples, while achieving the best rendering quality among all compared methods (PSNR 27.92, 30.02, 23.97 respectively). Across all three datasets, DashGaussian outperforms the vanilla 3DGS baseline by 75–87% in optimization time while simultaneously improving PSNR by 0.2–0.5 dB. When plugged into various 3DGS backbones (3DGS, Mip-Splatting, Revising-3DGS, Taming-3DGS), DashGaussian delivers a 45.7% average speedup with negligible or positive rendering quality change.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS [22], DashGaussian reduces optimization time from 18.31 to 3.23 min on Mip-NeRF 360 (82% faster) while improving PSNR from 27.72 to 27.92 dB; similar gains appear on Deep Blending (17.27→2.20 min, PSNR +0.52) and Tanks and Temples (10.59→2.62 min, PSNR +0.35). Compared to Taming-3DGS [32] (the engineering-optimized backbone), DashGaussian further cuts time by 41–51% across datasets while matching or exceeding its rendering quality (e.g., PSNR 27.92 vs. 27.61 on Mip-NeRF 360). Against the pruning-based methods Reduced-3DGS [35] and Mini-Splatting [13], DashGaussian is 2–5× faster and achieves higher PSNR (27.92 vs. 27.28/27.22 on Mip-NeRF 360), because it avoids hard parameter removal. When plugged into Mip-Splatting [47] and Revising-3DGS [5], DashGaussian still delivers 45–55% speedup with quality preserved, confirming its plug-and-play generalization across different optimizers, primitive representations, and densification strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

DashGaussian builds directly on the 3DGS optimization framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) and positions itself as a scheduling layer over existing 3DGS backbones rather than a new rendering or representation method. It departs from prior acceleration strategies that either prune primitives (Mini-Splatting, Reduced-3DGS) or refine engineering pipelines (Taming-3DGS, FlashGS) by instead redistributing computational resources across optimization stages via a frequency-domain formulation. The frequency-guided resolution scheduler draws on classical image processing theory (DFT, Nyquist sampling) to justify progressive multi-resolution training, while the momentum-based primitive budgeting repurposes the momentum optimizer concept to adaptively determine the final primitive count without dataset-specific priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing 3DGS optimization to ~200 seconds on a consumer GPU with a 45.7% average speedup across backbones while preserving or improving quality makes 3DGS feasible on resource-constrained devices and for large-scale reconstruction pipelines. The plug-and-play design (validated across 3DGS, Mip-Splatting, Taming-3DGS, and Revising-3DGS) suggests broad adoptability without backbone-specific tuning. Open questions include whether the frequency-based scheduling generalizes to very large or unbounded scenes beyond the tested benchmarks, and the authors themselves note that a deeper theoretical connection between primitive count and rendering resolution remains to be explored.
