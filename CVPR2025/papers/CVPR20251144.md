## Problem

Existing infrared small target detection (IRSTD) methods rely exclusively on image modalities, which struggle to distinguish weak, featureless targets from complex, high-intensity backgrounds. While CLIP-SAM collaboration shows promise for cross-modal tasks, directly applying these models trained on natural images to infrared imagery faces a significant domain gap: infrared targets lack the color, shape, and texture cues that CLIP's semantic understanding depends on, and noisy backgrounds further degrade SAM's segmentation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251144/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an infrared image (resized to 1024×1024, with the target mask removed) and a 25-word textual scene description in the fixed template 'a photo of [scene] with [object], [object], and [object]' as joint input. Notably, the text describes the background scene rather than the target itself, exploiting the observation that background radiative features are more stable and easier to articulate than weak target signals. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask localizing the small infrared target, evaluated via IoU, probability of detection (Pd), and false alarm rate (Fa).

## Method

SAIST comprises two coupled modules. SR-CLIP constructs object-object similarity and scene-object correlation matrices from CLIP's image encoder features, then feeds a concatenated visual-textual description set through the CLIP text encoder; a text decoder (Text-to-Text + Visual-to-Text) and a visual decoder (Visual-to-Visual + Text-to-Visual) perform bidirectional cross-modal refinement, with an MMD loss aligning the resulting textual and visual prompt distributions. CG-SAM fine-tunes the frozen SAM image encoder via LoRA and feeds the SR-CLIP prompts into the SAM mask decoder, where they extract background features A(z); a ResNet-based depth estimator then plugs into a Beer-Lambert-law-derived infrared imaging equation J(z) = I(z)·e^{β·F(I(z))} − A(z)·(e^{β·F(I(z))} − 1) to invert the degraded observation and recover the true target radiation, effectively suppressing background interference at the mask-decoder stage.

## Results

SAIST achieves the best performance across all three benchmark datasets (NUDT-SIRST, IRSTD-1k, NUAA-SIRST) on all three metrics (IoU, Pd, Fa). On NUDT-SIRST, SAIST reaches IoU 95.23%, Pd 99.28%, and Fa 1.31×10⁻⁶, surpassing the next-best hybrid method SCTransNet (IoU 94.09%, Fa 4.29×10⁻⁶). On NUAA-SIRST, SAIST attains IoU 80.82%, Pd 99.56%, and Fa 0.87×10⁻⁶, dramatically reducing false alarms compared to ISNet (Fa 4.61×10⁻⁶) and GCI-Net (Fa 4.11×10⁻⁶). On IRSTD-1k, SAIST achieves IoU 72.14% and Fa 4.76×10⁻⁶, outperforming UIU-Net (IoU 69.96%, Fa 22.07×10⁻⁶) by a wide margin in false alarm suppression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAIST outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest CNN baselines, SAIST gains +2.8 IoU over GCI-Net (92.43→95.23) and +5.2 IoU over ISNet (80.02→80.82) on NUAA-SIRST, while reducing Fa from 4.61 to 0.87 (×5.3 reduction). Against hybrid methods, SAIST surpasses SCTransNet by +1.14 IoU and reduces Fa from 4.29 to 1.31 on NUDT-SIRST. Against SAM-based methods, SAIST improves IoU by +11.03 over SAM (84.20→95.23) and +7.21 over SAM-HQ (88.02→95.23) on NUDT-SIRST, with Fa reduced from 13.28 to 1.31. On IRSTD-1k, the most challenging dataset, SAIST achieves the largest relative gains: +2.18 IoU over UIU-Net and a 4.6× reduction in Fa (22.07→4.76). Traditional methods (IPI, Top-Hat, MSLSTIPT) are far behind, with Fa values orders of magnitude higher (up to 30,467×10⁻⁶), confirming that deep learning and multimodal approaches are essential for complex backgrounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-SAM collaboration line (ClipSAM, CRIS, OpenVocabulary SAM) from natural-image tasks to infrared small target detection, where the domain gap between pre-trained vision-language models and thermal imagery is substantially larger. It departs from prior IRSTD methods—which are exclusively image-modality (CNN, GAN, or hybrid ViT-CNN architectures)—by introducing textual scene descriptions as an additional input, and from prior CLIP prompt-learning work (CoOp) by adding a cross-modal interaction mechanism with MMD alignment to bridge the infrared-specific vision-language gap. The CG-SAM component further distinguishes itself by embedding the Beer-Lambert infrared imaging equation directly into the mask decoder, a physically grounded departure from purely data-driven segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAIST is the first to demonstrate that textual scene context can meaningfully reduce false alarms in IRSTD (Fa drops from ~4–15 to <1.3 across three benchmarks), and the MIRSTD dataset provides the first multimodal resource for this domain. However, the approach's practical deployment is constrained by the need for accurate scene descriptions (here GPT-4V-generated), raising open questions about real-time text acquisition and whether the gains persist when descriptions are noisy or unavailable. The Beer-Lambert integration, while interpretable, relies on a ResNet-based depth estimator whose accuracy in unstructured infrared scenes remains unvalidated, and the evaluation is limited to three relatively small datasets with a single-seed protocol.
