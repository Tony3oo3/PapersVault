## Problem

CLIP models are highly sensitive to errors in object categories and attributes but fail to distinguish incorrect spatial relationship descriptions, often assigning higher similarity scores to captions with wrong spatial phrases (e.g., 'behind' vs. 'in front of'). This spatial blindness limits the spatial intelligence of downstream CLIP-based systems, including MLLMs that rely on CLIP as their visual encoder.

## Input / Output

The visual encoder consumes RGB images (224×224 or 336×336) augmented with monocular depth maps predicted by a lightweight Depth Anything v2 ViT-Small model (24.8M params), rather than requiring explicit depth sensors. The language encoder processes text captions, and the model outputs cosine similarity scores between image and text embeddings for contrastive training. In the SpatialLLaVA variant, the spatially-enhanced visual features are projected into an LLM that generates free-form text responses to spatial questions.

## Method

SpatialCLIP enhances CLIP along two axes. For the visual model, a 3D-Inspired ViT lifts each transformer layer's 2D token map into a sparse 3D voxel grid using per-patch depth values, then applies 3D depth-wise convolution (kernel 3, depth size 6) and 3D relative position encoding (learnable bias over relative 3D offsets) before compressing back to 2D via a residual shortcut. For language supervision, images are recaptioned with MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5-13B, CoCa, ShareGPT4V) to obtain spatially rich descriptions, and spatial phrases are then perturbed to their antonyms (e.g., 'in front of' → 'behind') to create hard negatives. The contrastive loss simultaneously pulls the image embedding toward its correct caption while pushing it away from both in-batch negatives and its own perturbed caption, forcing the model to encode spatial cues to discriminate between them.

## Results

On SpatialBench, SpatialCLIP-B@224px achieves 41.51 (outdoor) and 34.28 (indoor) accuracy, outperforming OpenAI CLIP-B@224px (35.22 / 30.71) by +6.3 and +3.6 points respectively. On the BLINK spatial subset, SpatialCLIP-B reaches 57.34 vs. 51.05 for the original CLIP-B, while also improving zero-shot retrieval on COCO (R@1: 48.33 vs. 42.19) and Flickr30K (R@1: 38.86 vs. 34.40). For the MLLM variant, SpatialLLaVA-7B scores 71.32 on BLINK Spatial and 40.25/40.17 on SpatialBench (outdoor/indoor), substantially exceeding LLaVA-1.5-7B (61.54 / 29.55 / 32.47) while maintaining comparable general-purpose performance (e.g., VQAV2: 79.9 vs. 78.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenAI CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021), SpatialCLIP consistently wins on all spatial-focused metrics: SpatialCLIP-B improves SpatialBench outdoor by +6.3 points and BLINK Spatial by +6.3 points, while also gaining +6.1 on COCO R@1 and +4.5 on Flickr30K R@1, showing that spatial enhancement does not come at the cost of general recognition. Compared to LLaVA-1.5 (Liu et al., CVPR 2024), SpatialLLaVA-7B (same training data and protocol) achieves a dramatic +10.7 gain on SpatialBench outdoor and +9.8 on BLINK Spatial, yet remains within 1.4 points on general benchmarks like VQAV2 (79.9 vs. 78.5) and POPE (86.5 vs. 85.9). Against Spatial-RGPT (Cheng et al., arXiv 2024), which is trained on spatial-focused data, SpatialLLaVA still leads on SpatialBench (40.25/40.17 vs. 37.73/38.57) and BLINK Spatial (71.32 vs. 65.73), but trails on CV-Bench_3D depth (57.33 vs. 62.33) and distance (52.16 vs. 59.00), suggesting Spatial-RGPT's explicit 3D training data gives it an edge on those specific sub-tasks. The ablation against AlphaCLIP's early-fusion depth strategy (Sun et al., CVPR 2024) shows the 3D-Inspired ViT outperforms it by +1.9 on SpatialBench outdoor and +5.6 on BLINK Spatial, confirming that simply adding a depth channel is insufficient compared to the proposed 3D-aware architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP's contrastive pretraining framework by modifying both its visual encoder and language supervision to target spatial understanding. For the visual model, it departs from prior depth-integration approaches (AlphaCLIP's early fusion, SpatialRGPT's late fusion) by proposing a 3D-inspired ViT that lifts 2D tokens into 3D voxel space and incorporates 3D convolution and 3D relative position encoding borrowed from point cloud networks. For language supervision, it introduces a recaption-and-perturbation strategy that contrasts correct spatial descriptions against antonymic perturbations, addressing the scarcity of spatially accurate captions in standard web-scale datasets. In the MLLM context, it positions itself against data-centric approaches (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, CubeLLM) by demonstrating that improving the visual representation alone—without new spatial training data—can substantially enhance spatial reasoning in LLaVA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spatial understanding is a fundamental gap in current vision-language systems that limits their utility in robotics, navigation, AR/VR, and embodied AI. The paper's key insight—that the visual representation, not just the training data, is a primary bottleneck for spatial intelligence—offers a complementary and potentially more scalable path than data-centric approaches. However, the method depends on monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything v2), which introduces its own errors, and the perturbation strategy (antonymic substitution) is relatively simple, raising questions about whether it captures the full complexity of 3D spatial reasoning. The small size of SpatialBench (299 images) and the single training configuration limit the generality of the conclusions, and it remains open whether the 3D-inspired architecture provides benefits beyond spatial tasks or scales to larger models and datasets.
