## Problem

Existing multi-view driving video generation methods face three coupled limitations: (1) simultaneous denoising of all camera views incurs prohibitive memory and compute costs, capping spatial resolution; (2) reliance on per-frame 3D annotations restricts temporal resolution to the low annotation rate (2 Hz on nuScenes); and (3) different annotation types (e.g., lane lines vs. bounding boxes) are labeled at different frequencies, yet prior models cannot handle such sparse, heterogeneous conditions without post-processing refinement.

## Input / Output

The model consumes BEV road maps, 3D bounding boxes, ego-state embeddings, a text prompt, the first frame of each camera (key-frame), and video clips from adjacent key-view cameras as conditions, all projected into each camera's first-person view. It outputs a 576×1024 multi-view driving video at 2–10 fps, where conditions may be provided sparsely at 2 Hz while the generated video runs at a higher frame rate. Unlike prior pipelines (e.g., DrivingDiffusion), no post-refinement or multi-stage correction is applied; the single end-to-end pass produces the final video.

## Method

DriveScape builds on Stable Video Diffusion as a latent diffusion backbone but restructures the denoising loop so that each step processes a single viewpoint, with camera identity encoded via learnable embeddings passed through an MLP–SiLU block into cross-attention. The core architectural contribution is the Bi-Directional Modulated Transformer (BiMoT) module, which contains a latent-to-condition cross-attention layer (query = condition features, key/value = video latents), a temporal self-attention layer, and a condition-to-latent cross-attention layer, enabling bidirectional information flow and temporal coherence across heterogeneous condition modalities. Views are partitioned into non-adjacent key views (generated in parallel) and neighbor views (conditioned on adjacent key-view videos), while key-frame conditions from all cameras establish inter-view correlations at the initial frame. Sparse conditions are handled by zero-padding missing frames and appending a binary mask channel, with learned embeddings for unconditioned frames, allowing the model to be trained at 2–10 Hz video rates while conditions remain at 2 Hz. Conditions are injected in a coarse-to-fine order—maps and 3D layouts first, then BEV key-frames, then neighbor camera videos—so structural guidance precedes pixel-level detail.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, DriveScape achieves a FID of 8.34 and FVD of 76.39, substantially outperforming all prior multi-view driving video generation methods (next best: SparseCtrl at FID 11.15 / FVD 116.00; Delphi at FVD 113.5). For perception-based controllability, DriveScape attains Road mIoU of 68.21, Vehicle mIoU of 30.27, and Object NDS of 36.5, approaching the oracle upper bounds (73.68, 34.84, 41.21 respectively). Under the video-based StreamPETR evaluation, DriveScape achieves NDS 44.5 (vs. oracle 53.7), a 9.2-point gap, compared to Panacea's 14.8-point gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveScape decisively outperforms all nine baselines on both FID (8.34 vs. 11.15–52.60) and FVD (76.39 vs. 113.5–452.0), with the largest margins over DriveDreamer (FVD 452.0) and DrivingDiffusion (FVD 332.0). On Road mIoU, DriveScape (68.21) surpasses DrivingDiffusion (63.20) and Drive-WM (65.07), though it trails the oracle by 5.47 points. On Vehicle mIoU, DrivingDiffusion (31.60) edges out DriveScape (30.27) by 1.33 points, the only metric where a baseline wins. On Object NDS, Delphi (36.58) marginally exceeds DriveScape (36.5) by 0.08, while DriveScape substantially outperforms MagicDrive (23.32) and DrivingDiffusion (33.1). Under the video-based StreamPETR protocol (Table 3), DriveScape closes the oracle gap to 9.2 NDS points versus Panacea's 14.8, while also achieving a much lower FID (8.34 vs. 16.96) and FVD (76.39 vs. 139). Notably, DriveScape is the only method that simultaneously reports competitive scores across all five metrics without any post-refinement pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveScape builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its denoising backbone and the latent diffusion model (LDM) pipeline, extending them from single-view text-to-video to multi-view 3D-condition-guided driving video generation. It departs from prior multi-view driving generators (MagicDrive, DrivingDiffusion, DriveDreamer) in two key ways: (1) it avoids simultaneous multi-view denoising by separating cameras into key and neighbor views with a sequential generation scheme, and (2) it introduces the BiMoT module to fuse heterogeneous conditions (BEV maps, 3D bounding boxes, key-frames, neighbor videos) in a unified latent space. Unlike DrivingDiffusion, it requires no post-refinement stage, and unlike image-only methods (BEVGen, BEVControl), it handles temporal consistency natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DriveScape addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving simulation: generating high-resolution (576×1024), high-frame-rate (up to 10 Hz) multi-view videos from sparse 2 Hz annotations without post-processing, which is critical for training perception models on synthetic data. The sparse condition control capability is particularly relevant given the real-world cost asymmetry between annotation types (e.g., lane lines vs. bounding boxes). However, evaluation is limited to the nuScenes dataset, and perception metrics (Road mIoU 68.21 vs. oracle 73.68; Object NDS 36.5 vs. oracle 41.21) still show meaningful gaps, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other driving datasets, longer video horizons, or more diverse scene compositions.
