## Problem

Existing 3D human pose estimation methods fail to reconstruct plausible close human interactions from in-the-wild videos due to severe visual ambiguity and inter-person occlusions. Even state-of-the-art foundation models (SAM, ViTPose++) cannot reliably distinguish individual human semantics in such scenarios, causing both regression-based and optimization-based approaches to produce incorrect depth ordering and body poses. The paper identifies that human appearance, previously underutilized as a reconstruction cue, can directly resolve these ambiguities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular in-the-wild video of two interacting people, along with 2D keypoints (ViTPose++) and whole-group bounding boxes/masks (AutoTrackAnything) as auxiliary observations. Notably, only a combined segmentation of all individuals is required to mask the background, avoiding the need for per-person semantic parsing that fails in close interactions. The output consists of per-frame SMPL parameters (pose, shape, translation) for both individuals plus 14-channel Gaussian UV maps (offset, color, opacity, rotation, scale, identity) encoding each person's appearance, with the full optimization taking approximately 3–5 minutes for a 128-frame clip.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-branch optimization framework that simultaneously reconstructs motion and appearance. The motion branch uses a diffusion model trained as a proxemics prior (with mask strategies for temporal inpainting and reactive generation), whose parameters are finetuned during optimization rather than performing traditional reverse diffusion, making it more robust to depth ambiguity and local minima. The appearance branch decodes two optimizable latent tensors through a U-Net into Gaussian UV maps, which are projected onto the SMPL body surface and splatted to the image plane, enabling direct RGB comparison to resolve depth ordering and occlusion. The joint objective combines appearance losses (L1, SSIM, LPIPS), 2D keypoint reprojection, differentiable distance-field mesh penetration penalties, temporal smoothness, and regularization toward the prior's initial predictions, all optimized with Adam at different learning rates per branch.

## Results

On Hi4D, the proposed method achieves the best performance across all metrics: MPJPE 59.1 mm, PA-MPJPE 44.3 mm, MPVPE 72.0 mm, and interaction error 80.2, outperforming the closest competitor CloseInt (63.1/47.5/76.4/81.4). On the in-the-wild 3DPW dataset, the method similarly leads with MPJPE 64.5 mm, PA-MPJPE 45.6 mm, MPVPE 75.2 mm, and interaction error 96.4. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that the full dual-branch framework (appearance + proxemics + physics) yields the best A-PD of 0.81 mm, confirming that all three constraint types contribute to reducing mesh penetration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric for both Hi4D and 3DPW. Against the closest regression-based competitor CloseInt [16], the gains are 4.0 mm in MPJPE and 3.2 mm in PA-MPJPE on Hi4D, and 6.1 mm / 5.8 mm on 3DPW, with interaction error reduced from 81.4 to 80.2 (Hi4D) and 100.3 to 96.4 (3DPW). Single-person methods Human4D and CLIFF suffer large interaction errors (141.5 on Hi4D for CLIFF) due to depth ambiguity and weak-perspective cameras. Multi-person methods BEV and GroupRec produce more reasonable spatial distributions but still ignore close body contacts, yielding interaction errors of 131.0 and 98.8 on Hi4D respectively. The optimization-based BUDDI, despite being designed for close interactions, degrades severely (MPJPE 96.8 on Hi4D, 83.6 on 3DPW) because its reliance on 2D keypoints from ViTPose++ fails under visual ambiguity. Notably, adding the proposed WildCHI dataset to CloseInt's training improves its 3DPW MPJPE from 70.6 to 66.4, validating the dataset's utility for in-the-wild generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the close human interaction reconstruction line (BUDDI, CloseInt, CHI3D) by replacing reliance on 2D keypoints and masks—which fail under severe visual ambiguity—with a dual-branch optimization that simultaneously reconstructs SMPL motions and 3D Gaussian appearances. The motion branch builds on diffusion-based proxemics priors (as in CloseInt and BUDDI) but reformulates them as a finetunable network rather than a fixed prior, while the appearance branch repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (previously used for single-person avatar rendering) as a dense image-level constraint for two-person depth ordering. The WildCHI dataset extends the pseudo-ground-truth generation paradigm (NeuralAnnot, BUDDI) to in-the-wild close interactions, filling a gap left by indoor-only datasets like Inter-X and InterHuman that lack RGB images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even foundation models (SAM, ViTPose++) cannot resolve human semantics in close interactions, and that coarse RGB appearance via Gaussian splatting can recover depth ordering where keypoints and masks fail—a practical insight for any multi-person reconstruction pipeline. The WildCHI dataset (100 in-the-wild videos with temporal annotations) is the first outdoor close-interaction dataset with RGB, and it measurably improves regression-based methods like CloseInt, suggesting broader utility. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to two-person interactions, requires some non-contact frames for appearance anchoring, and produces only coarse textures, leaving crowd-scale interaction reconstruction and high-fidelity appearance as open problems.
