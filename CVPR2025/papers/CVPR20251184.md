## Problem

Training-free guided diffusion methods rely on a manually tuned weight hyperparameter to balance the unconditional diffusion prior against a differentiable guidance loss, making it difficult to generalize across tasks and conditions. This paper asks whether the two components can be fundamentally decoupled so that their interaction is governed by an adaptive optimization mechanism rather than a fixed scalar.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained unconditional diffusion model (e.g., DDPM on CelebA-HQ, FFHQ, or ImageNet at 256×256) and a plug-and-play differentiable guidance function—ranging from non-linear semantic parsers (BiSeNet segmentation, AODA sketch, CLIP text embeddings) to linear measurement operators (super-resolution, inpainting, deblurring) or trajectory losses for motion. It outputs a single generated sample (image or motion sequence) that simultaneously satisfies the diffusion prior and the guidance condition, with no additional training or fine-tuning required. The guidance function is evaluated on a Tweedie-estimated clean sample at each reverse step, allowing off-the-shelf classifiers trained on clean data to be used directly.

## Method

The paper reformulates conditional generation as a constrained optimization over two variables—x (governed by the diffusion prior) and z (governed by the guidance function)—linked by the equality constraint x = z, then solves it with ADMM. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 1) is that a single reverse diffusion step is a first-order approximation to the proximal operator of −log q_φ(x) with ρ = β/(1−β), so the x-update is simply one denoising step of the pretrained model. The z-update performs K_t steps of gradient descent on the guidance loss, using Tweedie's formula to estimate the clean sample for evaluating the guidance function. A dual variable ν is updated to adaptively enforce the coupling constraint, replacing the fixed weight hyperparameter. The paper further proves convergence to a stationary point at rate o(1/T) under L-smoothness assumptions and a sufficiently small ρ, via a Lyapunov-function argument that bounds the inexact proximal approximation error.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ non-linear guided image synthesis, ADMMDiff achieves the best FID across all three guidance types: 30.18 (segmentation), 42.43 (sketch), and 43.84 (text), while also attaining the lowest guidance distance (1586.2, 32.28, 10.08 respectively). On linear inverse problems (FFHQ and ImageNet), ADMMDiff achieves the best FID in 9 out of 10 tasks and best LPIPS in 9 out of 10 tasks, e.g., FID of 26.8 on 4× super-resolution (FFHQ) vs. 39.35 for DPS. For controllable motion generation, ADMMDiff achieves the lowest trajectory-following objective (e.g., 0.042 for 'walking') while maintaining text-motion embedding distance closest to the unconditional prior (3.38 vs. 4.62 for GMD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADMMDiff outperforms all four non-linear guidance baselines (DPS, LGD-MC, FreeDoM, MPGD) on every metric in Table 1, with the most dramatic gains in FID for segmentation guidance (30.18 vs. 43.97–57.38) and sketch guidance (42.43 vs. 60.56–70.97). On linear inverse problems (Table 3), ADMMDiff beats DPS by a wide margin on FFHQ (e.g., FID 26.8 vs. 39.35 for 4× SR) and also surpasses DDRM, MCG, PnP-ADMM, and Score-SDE on 9/10 tasks for both FID and LPIPS. For motion generation (Table 4), ADMMDiff achieves the lowest trajectory objective across all four prompts (e.g., 0.042 vs. 0.095 for GMD on 'walking') while keeping the text-motion embedding distance closest to the unconditional model (3.38 vs. 4.62 for GMD), indicating better preservation of semantic content. The unconditional baseline [51] serves only as a reference for embedding distance and is not a direct competitor since it lacks guidance. Overall, ADMMDiff wins or ties on essentially all reported metrics across all three task categories, with the largest relative improvements seen in FID for non-linear guidance and in trajectory-following objective for motion synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions training-free guided diffusion through the lens of ADMM, a classic primal-dual optimization algorithm (Gabay & Mercier 1976; Glowinski & Marroco 1975), rather than the single-trajectory gradient perturbation paradigm used by DPS, LGD-MC, FreeDoM, and MPGD. The key conceptual departure is decoupling the diffusion prior and guidance loss into two separate variables (x and z) linked by an equality constraint, so that two trajectories gradually couple rather than one trajectory being directly perturbed by guidance gradients. The theoretical bridge between the diffusion reverse step and the proximal operator builds on Zhu et al. (CVPR 2023), while the use of a generative model as a proximal operator extends the PnP-ADMM framework (Chan et al. 2016) from restoration to conditional generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the manually tuned guidance weight hyperparameter that plagues all prior training-free guided diffusion methods, ADMMDiff offers a more principled and task-agnostic balancing mechanism, which is practically significant given how sensitive existing methods are to this parameter. The convergence guarantee (Theorem 2) is a rare theoretical contribution in this area, lending credibility to the inexact ADMM updates. However, the method still requires K_t gradient descent steps per reverse step for the z-update, raising computational cost, and the authors acknowledge the need for acceleration techniques for real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the ADMM framework extends naturally to latent diffusion models at scale and whether the sublinear convergence rate is tight in practice.
