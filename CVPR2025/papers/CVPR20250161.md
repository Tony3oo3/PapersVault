## Problem

State-of-the-art one-step diffusion-based Real-ISR methods like OSEDiff and S3Diff still rely on complete Stable Diffusion models (1775M parameters, 0.11s inference), making them too computationally expensive for real deployment on resource-limited edge devices. No existing compression technique is specifically designed for diffusion-based Real-ISR, where the LR input provides rich structural information that makes many SD components (VAE encoder, text conditioning, time embeddings) redundant.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250161/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128×128 low-resolution RGB image as input and produces a 512×512 super-resolved image (×4 scaling) in a single forward pass. Unlike typical SD-based pipelines, the LR image is consumed directly via a PixelUnshuffle operation (rearranging spatial pixels into channels) rather than being encoded into a 4-channel latent code, and no text prompts or timestep conditioning are required. Inference runs at 34.79 FPS on an NVIDIA A100 GPU with 456M parameters and 496G MACs.

## Method

AdcSR applies the proposed Adversarial Diffusion Compression (ADC) framework to OSEDiff in two structural steps: (1) removing unnecessary modules (VAE encoder replaced by PixelUnshuffle, prompt extractor, text encoder, cross-attention layers, and time embeddings) and (2) channel-pruning the remaining UNet (75% retained) and VAE decoder (50% retained), while also optimizing the UNet-to-decoder connection to pass high-dimensional features directly. To compensate for capacity loss, a two-stage training scheme is used: Stage 1 pretrains the pruned VAE decoder from scratch on OpenImage/LAION data, and Stage 2 performs adversarial distillation where the student's features are aligned to the OSEDiff teacher's features via L1 loss in the smallest-spatial-size decoder level, while a LoRA-finetuned SD UNet acts as a discriminator enforcing that student features match the distribution of GT-image features. This yields a 74% parameter reduction and 3.7× speedup over OSEDiff while maintaining competitive perceptual quality.

## Results

On DRealSR, AdcSR achieves PSNR 28.10 dB, SSIM 0.7726, LPIPS 0.3046, DISTS 0.2200, MUSIQ 66.26, and CLIPIQA 0.7049, ranking in the top 3 across full-reference perceptual metrics (SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS) among all compared methods. In terms of efficiency, AdcSR requires only 0.03 s inference time, 496 G MACs, and 456 M parameters for a 512×512 output, yielding a 3.7× speedup over its teacher OSEDiff (0.11 s, 2265 G, 1775 M) and a 9.3× speedup over S3Diff (0.28 s, 2621 G, 1327 M), while reducing computation by 78% and parameters by 74% relative to OSEDiff. This enables real-time inference at 34.79 FPS on an NVIDIA A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct teacher OSEDiff, AdcSR improves PSNR (28.10 vs 27.92), MUSIQ (66.26 vs 64.65), and CLIPIQA (0.7049 vs 0.6963) while slightly trailing on SSIM (0.7726 vs 0.7835), LPIPS (0.3046 vs 0.2968), and DISTS (0.2200 vs 0.2165), all while being 3.7× faster and using 74% fewer parameters. Compared to S3Diff, AdcSR wins on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS, NIQE, and MUSIQ but is marginally behind on CLIPIQA (0.7049 vs 0.7156), while achieving a 9.3× inference speedup. Versus the other one-step method SinSR, AdcSR substantially outperforms on LPIPS (0.3046 vs 0.3665), DISTS (0.2200 vs 0.2485), MUSIQ (66.26 vs 55.33), and CLIPIQA (0.7049 vs 0.6383), though SinSR retains a small PSNR edge (28.36 vs 28.10). Against multi-step methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, PASD, ResShift), AdcSR achieves competitive or superior perceptual quality (top-3 in SSIM/LPIPS/DISTS) while being 23.7×–383× faster in inference time. Overall, AdcSR is the only method that simultaneously achieves one-step inference, sub-500 G MACs, and top-tier perceptual quality, making it the most efficient option for real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on OSEDiff (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2024), a one-step diffusion-based Real-ISR method that relies on a full Stable Diffusion pipeline (VAE encoder, prompt extractor, text encoder, cross-attention, UNet, VAE decoder). The ADC framework departs from prior SD compression work (BK-SDM, SnapFusion) by arguing that Real-ISR's LR input carries sufficient information to make several SD modules redundant, and from prior one-step diffusion distillation (SinSR, S3Diff) by combining structural compression with adversarial distillation in a unified two-stage scheme. The adversarial distillation component draws on the diffusion-GAN literature (Diffusion2GAN, LADD, DMD2, UFOGen), while the channel-pruning strategy is informed by structural pruning work on diffusion models. No prior work had specifically designed a compression pipeline for diffusion-based Real-ISR, which the authors identify as a gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 74% parameter reduction and 9.3× speedup over S3Diff make diffusion-based Real-ISR viable on resource-constrained edge devices, achieving real-time 34.79 FPS inference — a practical milestone for deploying generative SR in production. The finding that removing the VAE encoder *improves* fidelity (not merely reduces cost) challenges the assumption that SD's full pipeline is necessary for any downstream task. However, the method still struggles with fine textures and heavily degraded details, and the ADC framework has only been validated on OSEDiff; whether it generalizes to other SD-based Real-ISR architectures (e.g., S3Diff's degradation-guided LoRA) remains an open question.
