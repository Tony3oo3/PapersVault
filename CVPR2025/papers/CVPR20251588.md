## Problem

Existing test-time prompt tuning methods for CLIP are designed for single-target-domain adaptation and lose validity when extended to multi-task scenarios where a model must simultaneously adapt to multiple target domains. The paper addresses the unexplored challenge of jointly adapting a pre-trained vision-language model to M target domains using only unlabeled test data streams, requiring both exploitation of task-specific contextual knowledge and inter-task complementarity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251588/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled test images in a streaming fashion (batch size 4, one image per target task per iteration) along with handcrafted text prompts for the frozen teacher CLIP (ViT-L/14) and a learnable 4-token task-shared prompt for the student CLIP (ViT-B/16). During testing, the dual knowledge graph is updated in real time as new samples arrive, with the teacher's pseudo-labels and entropy scores guiding feature allocation into class-specific memory buckets. The output is a per-task probability distribution and predicted class label, computed from enhanced textual prompt features (refined by hierarchical knowledge) and original image features via cosine similarity.

## Method

HKPT constructs a CLIP prompt distillation framework where a larger teacher CLIP (ViT-L/14) with handcrafted prompts guides a lightweight student CLIP (ViT-B/16) through three modules. The dual knowledge graph module builds per-task visual subgraphs (N class-specific memory buckets that absorb image features guided by teacher pseudo-labels with an entropy threshold for noise mitigation) and textual subgraphs (cosine-similarity edges between class prompt features), linked by virtual nodes processed through a shared GCN for cross-modal interaction. The cross-task knowledge sharing module computes an inter-task affinity matrix from task-specific contextual knowledge and learnable task embeddings, exhaustively searches all groupings into R groups, and selects the one maximizing mean intra-group affinity to extract complementary knowledge. The multi-view prompt distillation module aligns student and teacher at both the logits level (KL divergence) and feature level (MSE on linearly projected image and text features), with the total loss being their sum. The hierarchical knowledge representation combines task-specific contextual knowledge with group-level complementary knowledge via weighted aggregation, which refines the textual prompt features before final prediction.

## Results

In the multi-task test-time adaptation setting, HKPT achieves 87.3% average top-1 accuracy on Office-Home and 87.7% on MiniDomainNet, surpassing the strongest supervised multi-task baseline MmAP by 0.8% and 1.6% respectively. In the single-task setting, HKPT attains 69.7% average accuracy on the 10-dataset cross-domain benchmark and 67.3% on the 5-dataset OOD benchmark, outperforming the strongest test-time adaptation baseline TDA by 2.2% and 2.3% respectively. HKPT achieves the best per-dataset accuracy in 14 out of 15 single-task datasets and in all but one multi-task subtask (Clipart on Office-Home, where Full FT leads by 1.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the multi-task setting, HKPT outperforms the strongest supervised multi-task baseline MmAP (86.5% / 86.1%) by 0.8% and 1.6% on Office-Home and MiniDomainNet respectively, and beats all prompt-learning multi-task variants (CoOp-MT 85.2%/84.9%, MaPLe-MT 85.4%/85.1%). The only subtask where HKPT trails a baseline is Clipart on Office-Home (74.5% vs. Full FT 76.3% and MmAP 76.3%), while it leads on all other subtasks. In the single-task cross-domain benchmark, HKPT (69.7% avg) surpasses TDA (67.5%), C-TPT (65.5%), TPT (65.1%), and DiffTPT (64.9%), with the largest margin on Aircraft (+5.3% over TDA) and StanfordCars (+6.2% over TDA). On the OOD benchmark, HKPT (67.3% avg) leads TDA (65.0%) by 2.3%, with particularly strong gains on ImageNet-S (+4.3%) and ImageNet-A (+0.9%). Compared to zero-shot CLIP, HKPT improves average accuracy by 4.7% (Office-Home), 3.7% (MiniDomainNet), 5.1% (cross-domain), and 6.1% (OOD), confirming that the hierarchical knowledge modeling and distillation framework effectively transfers broader source-domain discriminative knowledge to the lightweight student model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-task test-time prompt tuning paradigm (TPT, DiffTPT, SwapPrompt, TDA) to the multi-task setting, where a pre-trained VLM must simultaneously adapt to multiple target domains using unlabeled test streams. It builds on CoOp's learnable textual prompts for the student CLIP and borrows the virtual-node mechanism from graph neural networks to enable cross-modal knowledge interaction within a dual knowledge graph. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a teacher-student CLIP distillation framework (ViT-L/14 guiding ViT-B/16) combined with adaptive task grouping, which has no direct analogue in prior test-time adaptation or multi-task prompt learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to formalize and address multi-task test-time adaptation for VLMs, this paper opens a practical direction for deploying a single pre-trained model across multiple shifting domains without labeled data. The hierarchical decomposition into task-specific contextual knowledge (via dual knowledge graphs) and inter-task complementary knowledge (via adaptive grouping) provides a structured recipe that could inform future multi-domain adaptation systems. Limitations include the computational overhead of maintaining a larger frozen teacher model alongside the student, the exhaustive search over task groupings that may not scale beyond a handful of tasks, and evaluation limited to four-domain benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to streaming or non-stationary multi-task settings and whether the teacher-student gap can be reduced without sacrificing performance.
