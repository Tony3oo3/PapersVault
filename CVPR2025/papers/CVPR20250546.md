## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) rely on predefined concept banks that are inherently incomplete, causing a trade-off between interpretability and classification accuracy. Prior remedies such as residual linear layers (e.g., ResCBM) recover some accuracy but sacrifice interpretability and remain constrained by the scope of their candidate concept bank. The paper asks whether a model can dynamically discover missing, visually-grounded concepts during training while retaining human-readable explanations for every concept it uses.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image, encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder (ViT-L/14 or RN50) into a d-dimensional feature vector. It produces three outputs: (1) a class label via a sparse linear classifier over concept scores, (2) a vector of N_C concept scores (cosine similarities between the image embedding and the hybrid concept bank), and (3) human-readable textual descriptions for the dynamic concepts, generated by a pre-trained GPT-2 translator that inverts the CLIP text encoder. The static concepts are fixed CLIP text embeddings of GPT-3.5-generated phrases, while the dynamic concepts are learned vectors that are only translated to text after training.

## Method

HybridCBM constructs a hybrid concept bank by concatenating a Static Concept Bank (N_S CLIP text embeddings of LLM-generated concepts per class, selected via submodular or random sampling) with a Dynamic Concept Bank (N_D randomly initialized learnable vectors). The dynamic vectors are optimized end-to-end with a Concept-Interpretability Training Scheme comprising three regularizers: a discriminability loss (intra-class attraction to the class embedding plus inter-class repulsion, using reparameterized sampling), an orthogonality loss (intra-class diversity among dynamic vectors plus inter-bank diversity between static and dynamic vectors), and a distribution alignment loss (Sinkhorn divergence between the dynamic and static concept distributions). A pre-trained GPT-2 concept translator, trained with prefix language modeling on 1.7M concept–caption pairs, maps each learned dynamic vector to a natural-language description, providing post-hoc interpretability. The final classifier is a sparse linear layer over the concatenated concept scores, and the entire pipeline is trained with cross-entropy plus the three regularization terms under the Adam optimizer.

## Results

On the mean accuracy across 11 datasets, HybridCBM (submodular) achieves 75.12% average accuracy, outperforming the Linear Probe baseline (72.59%) by 2.53 points. In the few-shot regime, the advantage is more pronounced: at 1-shot, HybridCBM reaches 58.64% versus 52.30% for Linear Probe (+6.34 points), while in the fully-supervised setting the two are essentially tied (86.83% vs. 86.84%). On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, HybridCBM achieves 88.03% and 68.38% respectively, setting a new state-of-the-art among interpretable CBMs and matching the non-interpretable Linear Probe (88.8% / 70.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Linear Probe (the strongest non-interpretable baseline), HybridCBM matches performance in the fully-supervised setting (86.83% vs. 86.84% mean accuracy) but substantially outperforms it in few-shot regimes, gaining 6.34 points at 1-shot and 4.92 points at 2-shot. Compared to Labo (the strongest prior CBM using LLM-generated concepts with submodular selection), HybridCBM improves full-supervised mean accuracy from 85.72% to 86.83%. On CIFAR-10, HybridCBM (88.03%) edges out ResCBM (88.0%) and PCBM-h (87.6%), while on CIFAR-100 it achieves 68.38%, surpassing ResCBM (67.9%) and Label-Free CBM (65.2%) but falling slightly short of PCBM-h (69.9%). Overall, HybridCBM is the only interpretable CBM that simultaneously matches the black-box Linear Probe in full supervision and dominates it in data-scarce settings, while also outperforming all prior interpretable methods on the majority of evaluated datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based Concept Bottleneck Model lineage initiated by Label-Free CBM [26] and Labo [52], adopting their use of CLIP's shared visual-textual space and GPT-3.5-generated concept banks. It departs from residual-based approaches like PCBM-h [54] and ResCBM [39], which recover accuracy by adding uninterpretable shortcut paths or constraining learned vectors to a fixed candidate bank. The key architectural departure is the Dynamic Concept Bank: learnable vectors regularized by discriminability, orthogonality, and Sinkhorn-divergence alignment losses, whose outputs are then mapped to human-readable text via a pre-trained GPT-2 translator that inverts the CLIP text encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HybridCBM addresses a core practical limitation of CBMs—the incompleteness of any hand-curated or LLM-generated concept set—by allowing the model to discover complementary concepts directly from visual data while retaining interpretability through post-hoc translation. However, the absolute interpretability metrics remain modest (32.8% semantic alignment for dynamic vs. 38.3% for static concepts; 46.2% vs. 47.3% precision@t), and the evaluation itself depends on GPT-4o's judgment, introducing circularity. Open questions include whether the GPT-2 translator can be improved to produce more faithful concept labels, whether the dynamic concept ratio should be dataset-adaptive, and whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or fine-grained settings where the static bank is inherently less complete.
