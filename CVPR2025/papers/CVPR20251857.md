## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) suffers severe performance degradation under sparse training views, producing hollow artifacts and overfitting to training data. The paper identifies this as an over-parameterization problem: too many Gaussian primitives relative to the limited training signal cause the model to memorize views rather than capture 3D structure. Prior sparse-view methods rely on external depth priors that introduce additional error sources, leaving the fundamental overfitting issue unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of sparse RGB views (3 views for LLFF and DTU, 8 views for Blender) and produces novel view renderings at arbitrary camera poses along with a 3D Gaussian scene representation. It operates as a plug-in modification to the training loop of existing 3DGS pipelines, requiring no additional input modalities (e.g., depth maps) beyond the standard RGB images. During inference, all Gaussian primitives are activated for full-quality rendering.

## Method

DropoutGS introduces two complementary mechanisms into the 3DGS training loop. First, Random Dropout Regularization (RDR) randomly deactivates a fraction p of Gaussian primitives each iteration and optimizes the remaining sub-model using the full model's rendered output as supervision rather than ground-truth pixels; this localizes gradients to neighbors of the dropped Gaussian, preventing counteracting gradient effects across the scene and approximating ensemble learning over low-complexity sub-models. Second, Edge-guided Splitting Strategy (ESS) computes per-Gaussian edge scores by projecting primitives onto Canny-style edge maps and accumulating across viewpoints, then splits large Gaussians with high edge scores into smaller ones to recover high-frequency details lost due to RDR-induced enlargement. The overall loss combines the standard 3DGS photometric loss, an optional depth regularization term (e.g., from DNGaussian), and the RDR loss, making the approach compatible with diverse 3DGS variants.

## Results

On the DTU dataset (3 views), DropoutGS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all metrics: PSNR 20.22, LPIPS 0.150, SSIM 0.830, and AVGE 0.086, outperforming DNGaussian (PSNR 18.91, LPIPS 0.176) by a clear margin. On the Blender dataset (8 views), DropoutGS also leads all baselines with PSNR 24.476, SSIM 0.889, and LPIPS 0.085. On LLFF (3 views), DropoutGS achieves the best LPIPS (0.282) and competitive SSIM (0.622) and AVGE (0.128), though FreeNeRF and SparseNeRF report slightly higher PSNR (19.63 and 19.86 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DropoutGS clearly outperforms DNGaussian [23], the strongest 3DGS-based baseline, on all metrics across all three datasets: on DTU it gains +1.31 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.176 to 0.150; on Blender it improves PSNR from 24.305 to 24.476 and LPIPS from 0.088 to 0.085. Against NeRF-based sparse-view methods, DropoutGS surpasses FreeNeRF [45] and SparseNeRF [37] on DTU by +0.30 and +0.67 dB PSNR respectively, and on Blender by +0.22 and +2.07 dB PSNR. On LLFF, however, FreeNeRF (19.63) and SparseNeRF (19.86) report slightly higher PSNR than DropoutGS (19.35), though DropoutGS achieves the best LPIPS (0.282 vs. 0.308 and 0.328) and competitive SSIM/AVGE. Compared to vanilla 3DGS [21], DropoutGS yields substantial gains of +3.83 dB PSNR on LLFF and +9.23 dB on DTU, confirming that the overfitting issue is the primary bottleneck. The compatibility experiments (Table 3) further show that DropoutGS can be plugged into other 3DGS variants (3DGS†, CoR-GS, FSGS) to produce consistent improvements of 0.1–1.6 dB PSNR, demonstrating its generality as a plug-in regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DropoutGS builds directly on 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its scene representation and incorporates DNGaussian's (Li et al., 2024) depth regularization into its loss, but departs conceptually from the dominant sparse-view paradigm of adding external geometric constraints (monocular depth, normals, frequency priors). Instead, it reframes the performance degradation under sparse views as a classical overfitting problem and applies a random dropout regularization on Gaussian primitives, drawing on the well-established dropout literature in deep learning. The Edge-guided Splitting Strategy is a novel auxiliary mechanism that addresses the detail loss inherent in the coarser representations produced by dropout, positioning the method as a coarse-to-fine refinement rather than a constraint-based approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DropoutGS offers a simple, plug-in regularization that is compatible with multiple 3DGS variants (3DGS, FSGS, CoR-GS, DNGaussian) without requiring additional depth estimation modules, reducing both computational overhead and sensitivity to depth errors that plague prior methods. The ensemble-learning interpretation of the dropout process provides a useful theoretical lens for understanding why the full model outperforms any single sub-model. However, the approach still depends on an edge detector for ESS, introduces two additional hyperparameters (dropout rate, edge threshold), and the performance gains diminish as the number of training views increases, suggesting the method's value is most concentrated in the extreme few-shot regime. Open questions include whether the dropout-ensemble framing extends to other explicit scene representations and whether the edge-guided splitting can be replaced by a learned, differentiable alternative.
