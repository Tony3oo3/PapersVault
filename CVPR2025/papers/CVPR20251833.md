## Problem

ViT-based single-stage WSSS methods suffer from over-smoothed segmentation along object boundaries, but the underlying cause has not been identified. This paper shows that multi-head self-attention acts as a low-pass filter that irreversibly attenuates high-frequency signals, and the resulting attenuated high-frequency features in the decoder mislead segmentation by producing false activations at boundary regions. No prior work had diagnosed this frequency-domain mechanism or proposed a targeted rectification strategy for it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251833/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (randomly resized-cropped to 448×448) with only image-level class labels during training, and produces pixel-level segmentation masks via a dual-branch DeepLab-LargeFOV decoder. The ViT-B encoder outputs patch tokens that are simultaneously fed to a classifier (for CAM and prototype generation) and the decoder (for segmentation). At test time, dense CRF post-processing is applied to the final segmentation output, and the framework also yields class activation maps as an intermediate byproduct used for pseudo-label generation.

## Method

FFR decomposes the decoder's ViT feature map via DFT into low- and high-frequency components using a binary spatial mask (ratio μ=0.4), then applies IDFT to recover a reliable low-frequency feature map. A False Segmentation Tokens Selection module computes cosine-similarity scores between image-specific prototypes and both the original and low-frequency tokens; tokens where the score difference exceeds a threshold (α=0.3) are flagged as falsely segmented attenuated high-frequency noise. A feature rectification loss L_r then transfers knowledge from the corresponding low-frequency tokens to correct these flagged tokens during training. In parallel, a dual-branch segmentation head processes the original features with a high dilation rate and the high-frequency features with a low dilation rate, enabling the decoder to better capture boundary details. The overall objective combines classification loss, intermediate classification loss, the rectification loss, and a weighted high-frequency-enhanced segmentation loss.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, FFR achieves 76.0% mIoU on the validation set and 75.5% mIoU on the test set (with dense CRF), setting a new state-of-the-art for single-stage WSSS. On MS COCO 2014, FFR reaches 46.8% mIoU on the validation set. For pseudo-label quality, FFR generates 76.4% mIoU on VOC val, outperforming the best single-stage competitor DuPL by 2.3% and even surpassing multi-stage methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FFR outperforms the previous single-stage SOTA PFSR [18] by 0.3% (val) and 0.5% (test) on VOC 2012, and by 1.0% on COCO 2014 (46.8% vs 45.8%). Among other single-stage ViT-based methods, FFR leads SeCo [49] by 2.0%/1.7% and DuPL [43] by 2.7%/2.7% on VOC val/test. Against multi-stage methods, FFR exceeds CTI [51] by 1.9%/2.3% and KTSE [12] by 3.0%/2.6% on VOC, while slightly trailing PSDPM [57] on the test split (75.5% vs 74.9%) but leading on validation (76.0% vs 74.1%). On COCO 2014, FFR surpasses the best multi-stage method KTSE [12] by 0.9% and edges out single-stage SeCo [49] by 0.1%. Notably, FFR uses only image-level supervision (no language or saliency), yet outperforms methods with additional supervision such as CLIP-ES [30] (73.8%/73.9%) and DIAL [20] (74.5%/74.9%) on VOC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-stage ViT-based WSSS pipeline (exemplified by ToCo) by introducing a frequency-domain rectification step between the encoder and decoder. It builds on the theoretical observation from Park & Kim that multi-head self-attention acts as a low-pass filter, and on SIPE's image-specific prototype mechanism for semantic correlation scoring. Unlike prior single-stage methods (AFA, DuPL, PFSR) that focus on refining pseudo labels or attention affinities, FFR directly operates on the feature map: it decomposes patch tokens via DFT, identifies falsely segmented high-frequency tokens using prototype-guided correlation differences, and applies a targeted rectification loss. The dual-branch dilated convolution head draws on frequency-adaptive convolution ideas from Chen et al. to complement the rectification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for the over-smoothing failure mode in ViT-based WSSS and a targeted fix, which is more interpretable than the pseudo-label refinement strategies that dominate the field. However, the gains are modest (0.3–0.5% over PFSR on VOC), the best results still depend on CRF post-processing, and the frequency masking parameters (μ, α) are fixed heuristically rather than learned. Open questions include whether the frequency rectification principle generalizes to other ViT-based dense prediction tasks (e.g., instance segmentation, depth estimation) and whether the dual-branch head is truly necessary or whether the rectification loss alone suffices at scale.
