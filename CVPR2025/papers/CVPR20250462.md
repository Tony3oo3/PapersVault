## Problem

In-bed human shape reconstruction from pressure sensing is hindered by three interrelated gaps: existing methods naively adopt vision pipelines without accounting for pressure data's unique characteristics (zero-valued backgrounds, information ambiguity from non-contact joints), in-bed pressure datasets suffer from limited participant diversity and sensor heterogeneity, and monocular SMPL optimization in top-view in-bed scenes produces unreliable pseudo-ground-truths due to depth ambiguity and self-penetration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250462/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal sequence of single-channel pressure images (matrix-formatted contact pressure distributions) from a fixed pressure-sensing bedsheet, where each pixel encodes applied force and the sensor grid has known physical spacing (0.0311 m × 0.0195 m in TIP). It outputs SMPL parameters (pose θ, shape β, translation t) for the mid-frame of the sequence, which can be rendered as a full 3D human mesh, along with auxiliary 2D joint positions in the pressure image plane. The spatial position embedding leverages the fixed sensor geometry to inject world-coordinate priors unavailable in standard visual inputs.

## Method

PI-HMR is a three-stage temporal network: a ResNet50 static encoder extracts per-frame features, a Multi-Scale Feature Fusion (MFF) module fuses global (average-pooled), local (spatial-position-embedded and learnable-mask-weighted with AttentionPooling), and sampling (TopK high-pressure regions plus 12 auxiliary 2D joint positions) features into a unified representation, and a two-layer Transformer encoder captures long-term temporal dependencies before an IEF SMPL regressor produces the final prediction. The MFF explicitly encodes pressure-specific priors—spatial position embedding from fixed sensor geometry, TopK high-pressure masks as contour cues, and a learnable attention mask—to direct the model toward informative contact regions while suppressing the redundant zero-background. Cross-modal knowledge distillation pre-trains the encoder by matching both hidden feature maps and outputs of a CLIFF RGB teacher, transferring body and motion priors across modalities. A VQ-VAE-based Test-Time Optimization then refines predictions by matching them to learned discrete motion prototypes, acting as an unsupervised prior that resolves residual ambiguity. For label generation, SMPLify-IB introduces a gravity constraint that penalizes implausible static limb lifts (detected via 2D keypoint velocity thresholds) and a lightweight segment-based self-penetration detector (53.9× faster than SMPLify-XMC at 98.32% accuracy) with potential-based push/pull losses to produce higher-quality SMPL pseudo-ground-truths.

## Results

On the TIP dataset, PI-HMR achieves 59.46 mm MPJPE and 44.53 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming the closest pressure-based baseline PI-Mesh (76.47 mm) by 17.01 mm and the SOTA vision-based temporal architecture TCMR (64.37 mm) by 4.91 mm. The full pipeline with cross-modal KD and Test-Time Optimization (PI-HMR+KD+TTO) further reduces MPJPE to 55.50 mm and PA-MPJPE to 41.81 mm. Notably, PI-HMR also achieves a dramatically lower acceleration error (9.12 mm/s²) compared to all baselines (20.12–31.52 mm/s²), indicating substantially smoother temporal predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PI-HMR outperforms all seven baselines on MPJPE. Against the most directly comparable pressure-based method PI-Mesh [52], PI-HMR reduces MPJPE by 17.01 mm (76.47→59.46) and ACC-ERR by 12.74 mm/s² (21.86→9.12). Against the SOTA vision-based temporal architecture TCMR [7] (re-trained on pressure images), PI-HMR improves MPJPE by 4.91 mm (64.37→59.46) while halving the acceleration error (20.12→9.12). The single-frame baselines HMR [23] and BodyMap-WS [46] are beaten by 15.6 mm and 12.02 mm in MPJPE respectively. MPS-NET [51] performs poorly (160.59 mm MPJPE), likely due to its architecture being ill-suited to the sparse, zero-heavy pressure modality. The additional KD and TTO modules contribute incremental gains of 2.33 mm and 1.7 mm MPJPE respectively, with the combined PI-HMR+KD+TTO achieving the best overall result of 55.50 mm MPJPE and 65.15 mm MPVE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMPLify optimization framework (Bogo et al., ECCV 2016) with gravity constraints and a lightweight self-penetration detector tailored to in-bed scenes, and builds upon the HMR (Kanazawa et al., CVPR 2018) IEF regressor and TCMR (Choi et al., CVPR 2021) temporal Transformer backbone for its regression network. It departs from prior pressure-based in-bed HPS work (Clever et al. 2018/2020, PI-Mesh 2024, BodyMap-WS 2024) by explicitly designing the Multi-Scale Feature Fusion module around pressure-specific characteristics—high-pressure contour priors, spatial position embeddings, and joint-conditioned sampling—rather than repurposing vision pipelines. The cross-modal KD from CLIFF and the VQ-VAE-based test-time optimization represent novel prior-expansion strategies not previously explored in the pressure-sensing HPS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical end-to-end toolchain (label generation via SMPLify-IB, network design via PI-HMR, and prior expansion via KD/TTO) that could accelerate development of privacy-preserving in-bed monitoring systems for healthcare. However, evaluation is confined to the TIP dataset with only 9 subjects, leaving generalization to other sensor hardware, body types, and motion distributions untested. Open questions include whether the gravity constraint generalizes beyond supine/semi-reclined postures, whether the VQ-VAE motion prior scales with larger and more diverse datasets, and whether the spatial position embedding transfers across different sensor geometries.
