## Problem

Existing LLM-based visual generation methods (e.g., LlamaGen) directly transfer the next-token prediction paradigm from language to vision without exploiting the fundamental difference that image token indices map to continuous embeddings rather than discrete words. The paper identifies that similar embeddings in the VQGAN codebook decode to nearly identical images, yet standard token-oriented cross-entropy loss penalizes predictions of nearby-but-wrong tokens, wasting the inherent redundancy in the embedding space and leading to suboptimal training efficiency and generation robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252822/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single ImageNet class label as conditioning input and autoregressively predicts a grid of discrete image tokens (16×16 for 256×256 images or 24×24 for 384×384 images) from a VQGAN codebook of size 16,384. The predicted token indices are looked up in the rearranged codebook to retrieve embeddings, which are then decoded by a VQGAN decoder into a full RGB image. Evaluation uses 50K generated images scored by FID, IS, precision, and recall against the ImageNet validation set.

## Method

IAR introduces two coupled modifications to the LlamaGen training pipeline. First, a Codebook Rearrangement step applies balanced K-means clustering (Algorithm 1) to partition the 16,384 VQGAN embeddings into n equal-sized clusters of m = N/n codes, then reorders the codebook so that all embeddings in cluster j occupy contiguous indices [jm, (j+1)m), ensuring adjacent indices correspond to visually similar patches. Second, a Cluster-oriented Cross-entropy Loss (L_CCE) is added alongside the standard token-oriented loss: the model's token-level probability distribution is aggregated into a cluster-level distribution via a LogSumExp operation over each cluster's token probabilities, and the model is trained to maximize the probability of the correct cluster. The final loss is L = L_TCE + λ·L_CCE (optimal λ=1). This design means that even when the model predicts an incorrect token index, the rearranged codebook and cluster-level supervision make it highly probable that the predicted token falls within the correct cluster, yielding a decoded image that closely resembles the target and improving both training speed (~42% faster to reach the same FID) and final generation quality across model scales from 111M to 1.4B parameters.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, IAR-XXL (1.4B) with CFG=1.7 achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 2.19 and IS of 278.9, surpassing LlamaGen-XXL (FID 2.34, IS 253.9) and DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, IS 278.2). With CFG=2.5, IAR-XXL further improves IS to 362.0 while maintaining competitive FID (3.74). Across all model scales (111M to 1.4B), IAR consistently outperforms LlamaGen by 0.3–0.5 FID points, and reduces training time by ~42% to reach equivalent FID (175 epochs vs. 300 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAR-XXL (CFG=1.7) achieves FID 2.19, beating the strongest diffusion baseline DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) and the strongest GAN baseline StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30), as well as the direct AR baseline LlamaGen-XXL (FID 2.34). At the IS metric with CFG=2.5, IAR-XXL reaches 362.0, surpassing MaskGIT-re (355.6) and DiT-XL/2 (278.2). Against LlamaGen at every model scale, IAR improves FID by 0.32 (B: 5.14 vs 5.46), 0.11 (L: 3.18 vs 3.29), 0.11 (XL: 2.52 vs 2.63), and 0.15 (XXL: 2.19 vs 2.34), while also improving IS at each scale. IAR also outperforms earlier AR methods including VQGAN (FID 18.65/15.78), ViT-VQGAN (FID 4.17/3.04), and RQTran. (FID 7.55/3.80) by large margins. The method does not directly compare against GigaGAN (FID 3.45) or ADM (FID 10.94) at the same parameter scale, but IAR-L (343M, FID 3.18) already surpasses GigaGAN (569M, FID 3.45) with fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LlamaGen, which adapts the LLaMA decoder-only architecture to class-conditional image generation via next-token prediction over VQGAN-quantized tokens. It departs from prior autoregressive and masked-image-modeling visual generation methods (LlamaGen, VAR, MaskGIT) by identifying a structural asymmetry they overlook: text generation only requires predicting a discrete index, whereas image generation requires the embedding behind that index, and similar embeddings in the codebook decode to nearly identical patches. The proposed codebook rearrangement (balanced k-means) and cluster-oriented cross-entropy loss are lightweight modifications that exploit this embedding-space geometry without altering the underlying transformer architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of IAR (a one-time codebook reordering plus an auxiliary loss term) makes it immediately applicable to any LLM-based visual generation pipeline, and the ~42% reduction in training epochs to reach a given FID is a practically significant efficiency gain for large-scale training. The method adheres to the scaling law across 111M–1.4B parameters, suggesting the benefit persists at larger scales. However, evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256/384×384; open questions include whether the cluster-oriented loss transfers to text-to-image generation, video token prediction, or multi-scale autoregressive schemes like VAR, where the embedding-space structure may differ.
