## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for multimodal semantic segmentation use asymmetric architectures that relegate the X modality (depth, thermal, event, etc.) to a supplementary prompt role, failing to fully exploit its representational potential. Additionally, prevalent global attention-based cross-modality fusion introduces noise by capturing irrelevant correlations across modalities. This paper addresses both limitations by proposing a symmetric PEFT framework that treats RGB and X as equal contributors and a sparse fusion mechanism that avoids noise from global correlations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250178/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of spatially aligned images — an RGB image and an X-modality image (depth, thermal, event, polarization, or near-infrared) — and produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation map. Both modalities are independently embedded into token sequences via patch embedding and processed through a shared symmetric architecture. The framework is modality-agnostic: the same Swin-Transformer backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet) and identical module structure handle any X modality without architectural changes, evaluated across datasets with resolutions ranging from 480×480 to 1024×1224.

## Method

The framework freezes a Swin-Transformer backbone and introduces three key components. First, the Modality-Aware Prompting and Adaptation (MAPA) module unifies dual-modality tokens in a low-rank space, projects the unified representation into modality-specific prompts, and inserts modality-specific hourglass adapters into each frozen transformer layer to transfer pre-trained knowledge to both RGB and X equally. Second, the Dynamic Sparse Cross-modality Fusion (DSCF) module uses a deformable-attention-inspired mechanism to predict sparse offsets that identify dominant regions in each modality, then applies a learnable modality-dominance weighting to sparsely fuse only the most informative regions, avoiding noise from global attention. Third, the Masked Modality Self-Teaching (MMST) training strategy randomly masks one modality during training and uses correctly predicted regions from the fused dual-modality output to supervise the single-modality heads, implicitly strengthening cross-modality feature adaptation. The entire trainable parameter count is only 7.5M (Swin-B) or 16.1M (Swin-L), roughly 4% of full fine-tuning methods.

## Results

On NYUDv2, the Swin-L variant achieves 59.9% mIoU with only 16.1M trainable parameters (4.4% of GeminiFusion's 369.2M), surpassing the best PEFT method (GoPT, 54.3%) by 5.6% and approaching the best FFT method (GeminiFusion, 60.2%). On SUN RGB-D, Swin-L reaches 55.0% mIoU, outperforming all FFT and PEFT baselines. On PST900, the method achieves 88.7% mIoU (Swin-L), a 7.2% absolute gain over the best PEFT method (GoPT, 81.5%) and 2.1% over the best FFT method (CACFNet, 86.6%). On DeLiVER (RGB-E), Swin-B achieves 58.4% mIoU, a +0.6% gain over the previous SOTA (Any2Seg, 57.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PEFT baselines, the method dominates across all six datasets: it outperforms GoPT by 5.6% on NYUDv2, 2.7% on SUN RGB-D, 2.2% on MFNet, and 7.2% on PST900, while DPLNet trails by 4.1% on NYUDv2 and 6.5% on PST900. Against FFT baselines, the Swin-L model achieves 59.9% on NYUDv2 (vs. GeminiFusion's 60.2%, a 0.3% gap) while training only 4.4% of the parameters, and 55.0% on SUN RGB-D (vs. GeminiFusion's 54.6%, a 0.4% gain). On MFNet, Swin-B matches CMNeXt at 59.9% mIoU with 7.5M vs. 119.6M trainable parameters. On MCubeS, the method achieves SOTA across all three modality pairs (RGB-N: 53.8%, RGB-D: 54.5%, RGB-A: 53.7%), surpassing StitchFusion+FFM by 0.6–1.8%. On DeLiVER (RGB-E), Swin-B reaches 58.4% mIoU, edging out Any2Seg (57.8%) and CMNeXt (57.5%) by 0.6% and 0.9% respectively. The method's key advantage is achieving near-FFT performance with 4–20× fewer trainable parameters, making it substantially more deployable in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves a middle path between two existing lines of work in multimodal semantic segmentation: full fine-tuning (FFT) methods like CMNeXt and GeminiFusion that use symmetric dual-backbone architectures but require training hundreds of millions of parameters, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like DPLNet and GoPT that freeze a pre-trained backbone but treat the X modality as a supplementary prompt to RGB in an asymmetric design. The key departure is applying PEFT (adapters + prompts) symmetrically to both modalities via the MAPA module, so the X modality receives the same representational enhancement as RGB rather than serving merely as a prompt. For cross-modality fusion, the paper replaces the global attention mechanisms common in prior PEFT methods with a sparse deformable-attention-based module (DSCF), and introduces a masked self-teaching training strategy (MMST) that has no direct precedent in the multimodal segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency story: matching or exceeding FFT-based SOTA with only 4–7% of the trainable parameters (7.5M vs. 369M) makes multimodal segmentation far more deployable in resource-constrained settings like autonomous driving. The symmetric PEFT paradigm—treating all modalities as first-class citizens through shared pre-trained backbones with modality-specific adapters—offers a general recipe that could transfer to other dense prediction tasks beyond segmentation. The authors acknowledge a clear limitation: the framework has not been explored for visual-language tasks, leaving open whether the symmetric PEFT design extends to incorporating text or other non-pixel modalities. An open question is whether the MMST self-teaching strategy generalizes to more than two modalities or to scenarios where one modality is systematically degraded rather than randomly masked.
