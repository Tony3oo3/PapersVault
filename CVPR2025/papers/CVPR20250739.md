## Problem

Existing unified audio-visual models naively joint-train on heterogeneous tasks (temporal localization, spatial localization, spatio-temporal reasoning, pixel-level understanding), which causes interference due to the complex inter-task relationships and data heterogeneity. The paper argues that effective unification requires explicit cooperation among tasks, which is absent in prior implicit joint-training paradigms where simple word labels cannot establish meaningful cross-task connections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250739/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10 uniformly sampled video frames at 224×224, corresponding audio segments converted to 128-dim Mel-filter bank features (16 kHz, 25 ms Povey window, 10 ms shift), and a text instruction. Outputs are task-dependent: temporal event boundaries for AVE/AVVP, bounding boxes [x_Left, y_Top, x_Right, y_Bottom] for ARIG, free-text answers augmented with an explicit multi-dimensional reasoning chain for AVQA, and pixel-level segmentation masks for AVS/Ref-AVS. The segmentation output is produced by special <MASK> tokens appended to the LLM vocabulary, whose last-layer embeddings are fed to a SAM-style mask decoder alongside multi-scale visual features.

## Method

Crab builds a unified audio-visual interface with three branches—visual (CLIP-ViT-L/14 + Q-Former + MLP), audio (BEATs + Q-Former + MLP), and segmentation (SAM-style mask decoder with <MASK> tokens)—all aligned to a LLaMA-2-7B-Chat backbone. The key architectural contribution is an interaction-aware LoRA structure: a shared low-rank matrix A learns common multimodal representations, while multiple independent B matrices (LoRA heads) each specialize in a distinct data-interaction aspect (temporal, spatial, pixel-level), with a learned router (dense layer + softmax) dynamically weighting the heads per token. On the data side, the authors construct AV-UIE (~200K samples) by prompting MLLMs to transform simple labels into explicit reasoning chains that span multiple spatio-temporal dimensions, thereby making cross-task cooperation concrete in the supervision signal. Training proceeds in two stages: feature-alignment pre-training of the three branches, followed by instruction-tuning on AV-UIE with a combined auto-regressive cross-entropy and segmentation (BCE + Dice + CE) loss.

## Results

Crab achieves 80.15% accuracy on AVE, 59.00% segment-level F1 on AVVP, 78.94% accuracy on MUSIC-AVQA, 41.78 cIoU on ARIG, 58.21 mIOU on AVS-Bench MS3, and 40.54 mIOU on Ref-AVS Seen. Among general-purpose audio-visual models, Crab is the only one evaluated across all five task categories (temporal localization, spatial localization, spatio-temporal reasoning, and pixel-level understanding), and it outperforms the strongest general baselines on AVVP (59.00 vs. 54.96 over MEERKAT) and matches VALOR on AVQA (78.94 vs. 78.90) with a smaller training dataset. Against task-specialized models, Crab surpasses the best temporal localizer (MM-Pyramid, 80.15 vs. 77.80 AVE Acc), the best AVQA model (TSPM, 78.94 vs. 76.79 Avg), and the best spatial localizer (FNAC, 41.78 vs. 27.15 cIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against general-purpose audio-visual models, Crab outperforms MEERKAT on AVVP segment-level F1 (59.00 vs. 54.96) and TimeChat (59.00 vs. 51.28), and matches VALOR on AVQA accuracy (78.94 vs. 78.90) while using a smaller dataset. Crab loses to GroundingGPT on ARIG spatial grounding (41.78 vs. 44.02 cIoU; 0.42 vs. 0.45 AUC), which is attributed to GroundingGPT's focus on fine-grained grounding. On pixel-level tasks, Crab surpasses AnyRef on MS3 mIOU (58.21 vs. 55.6) and is the only general model to report AVSS and Ref-AVS results. Against specialized models, Crab wins on temporal localization (80.15 vs. 77.80 AVE Acc over MM-Pyramid), spatio-temporal reasoning (78.94 vs. 76.79 Avg over TSPM), and spatial localization (41.78 vs. 27.15 cIoU over FNAC, a 14.6-point margin). On pixel-level understanding, Crab ties AVSegFormer on MS3 mIOU (58.21 vs. 58.40) and wins on Ref-AVS Seen (40.54 vs. 33.47), but trails EEMC on Ref-AVS Unseen (45.55 vs. 49.54).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified audio-visual MLLM line (VideoLLaMA 2, MEERKAT, Video-LLaMA) by replacing their implicit joint-training paradigm with an explicit cooperation framework operating at both the data level (AV-UIE dataset with reasoning processes) and the model level (interaction-aware LoRA). The interaction-aware LoRA is directly inspired by HydraLoRA's shared-A/multiple-B architecture, but repurposes the heads from handling distinct downstream tasks to learning distinct aspects of audio-visual data interaction (temporal, spatial, pixel-level). The segmentation branch adapts SAM's mask decoder, and the overall architecture follows the standard Q-Former + LLM multimodal recipe (BLIP-2 style) with CLIP-ViT and BEATs as frozen encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified model and dataset covering all four categories of audio-visual scene understanding (temporal, spatial, spatio-temporal, and pixel-level), and the explicit cooperation framework offers a principled solution to the interference problem in multi-task learning with heterogeneous modalities. The emergent specialization of LoRA heads into temporal, spatial, and pixel-level capabilities—without explicit task assignment—suggests a general mechanism for decomposing multi-modal reasoning. Key limitations include weaker pixel-level performance compared to dedicated segmentation models, and the reliance on MLLM-generated reasoning processes whose accuracy is not guaranteed, which the authors identify as a major open direction for improving reliability.
