## Problem

Standard contrastive learning in multimodal pretraining relies on one-to-one distance metrics (cosine, Euclidean) that assume data lies on a spherical or Euclidean space. In practice, multimodal features inhabit complex nonlinear manifolds where samples can be close in cosine distance yet semantically very different, making it difficult to distinguish true positives from false negatives. This paper introduces geodesic distance as a global, topology-aware metric for the first time in multimodal contrastive learning to better capture the underlying data structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-text pairs encoded by a CLIP-ViT image encoder and a BERT text encoder (first 6 layers for text, last 6 for fusion), producing 256-dimensional feature vectors. Unlike standard contrastive methods that compute pairwise distances directly, the geodesic distance computation requires a momentum feature queue of 65,536 samples as a reference pool to construct the graph topology. The output is a contrastive InfoNCE loss (with geodesic distance replacing cosine), combined with MLM and ITM losses, used to update the encoder parameters.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces cosine distance in the InfoNCE loss with geodesic distance computed over a hierarchical graph. Samples in the feature queue are clustered via K-Means (256 clusters), and a two-layer graph is built: the bottom layer uses trivial cosine distance within small clusters (satisfying the simple manifold assumption), while the top layer connects cluster centers via 8-neighbor edges and computes all-pairs shortest paths with the Floyd algorithm. For new batch features outside the graph, distance is decomposed into the trivial distance to the nearest bottom-level cluster center plus the precomputed geodesic path from that center to the target. The accumulated angular path is truncated at 4π, normalized to [0, π], and passed through cosine to yield the final similarity score. Incremental updates attach new samples to their nearest cluster center each step, with a full graph reconstruction every 100 iterations to prevent staleness.

## Results

On fine-tuned image-text retrieval, Geo-MAFA achieves the best overall performance: MSCOCO text retrieval R@1 of 79.3% and image retrieval R@1 of 62.5%, while on Flickr30K it reaches 96.9% (text) and 85.6% (image) R@1. On downstream tasks, Geo-MAFA attains 76.19% on VQA test-standard, 82.95% on NLVR2 test-P, and 81.63% on SNLI-VE test, consistently outperforming its base methods (MAFA, TCL, ALBEF) by 0.4–1.1 points across all benchmarks. The method also generalizes to other frameworks: Geo-CLIPFT improves over CLIPFT by +1.1 R@1 on text retrieval, and Geo-MOCOv2 gains +0.7 Top-1 on ImageNet over MoCo v2 at 200 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo-MAFA outperforms MAFA by +1.3 R@1 on MSCOCO text retrieval (79.3 vs. 78.0) and +0.7 on image retrieval (62.5 vs. 61.2), with similar gains on Flickr30K (+0.8 TR, +0.7 IR). Geo-TCL beats TCL by +2.8 R@1 on MSCOCO text retrieval (78.4 vs. 75.6) and +1.9 on image retrieval (60.9 vs. 59.0). Geo-ALBEF improves over ALBEF by +3.1 R@1 on MSCOCO text retrieval (76.2 vs. 73.1) and +2.4 on image retrieval (59.2 vs. 56.8). On downstream tasks, Geo-MAFA leads MAFA by +0.44 on VQA test-standard, +0.87 on NLVR2 test-P, and +0.67 on SNLI-VE test. The method also surpasses the much larger ALIGN model (1.2B images) on MSCOCO text retrieval (79.3 vs. 77.0) while using only 4M images. In generalization experiments, geodesic distance consistently improves over the base metric in CLIPFT (+1.1 TR), FLIPFT (+1.6 TR), MoCo v2 (+0.7 Top-1), and SwAV (+0.6 Top-1), confirming broad applicability beyond the primary ALBEF/TCL/MAFA frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: (1) multimodal contrastive pre-training with momentum feature queues (ALBEF, TCL, MAFA), which rely on one-to-one trivial distance metrics like cosine similarity to mine positive/negative pairs, and (2) geodesic distance methods from dimensionality reduction (ISOMAP) and 3D vision (GCNN, GeoNet, Geodesic-Former), which capture manifold topology via shortest paths on graphs. The key departure is repurposing geodesic distance as a contrastive metric in multimodal learning for the first time, replacing the point-to-point cosine distance with a many-to-many shortest-path distance computed over a hierarchical K-Means graph. The paper also extends the momentum-queue paradigm of ALBEF/TCL/MAFA with incremental graph-update strategies to keep the topological structure current during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that samples close in cosine distance can be far in geodesic distance due to manifold topology, and that this distinction improves negative-sample mining—is conceptually clean and generalizes beyond multimodal learning (validated on MoCo, SwAV, CLIP, FLIP). However, the practical gains are modest (1–2 R@1 points over MAFA), and the method introduces substantial engineering complexity (hierarchical graph construction, Floyd-Warshall at each layer, circular index queues, periodic full-graph rebuilds). Open questions include whether the hierarchical clustering structure can be learned end-to-end, how the approach behaves at much larger queue sizes (beyond 65K), and whether the simple-manifold assumption (Def. 2) holds in practice for high-dimensional feature spaces. The theoretical connectivity guarantees (Theorems 1–2) are reassuring but somewhat loose in practice.
