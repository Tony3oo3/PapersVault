## Problem

Traditional photogrammetry pipelines require dense image collections and chain together independent algorithm blocks (SfM, MVS, etc.) that are not jointly optimized, causing accumulated errors and making sparse-view reconstruction unreliable. No existing single model can simultaneously handle pose estimation, depth prediction, and novel view synthesis with flexible input/output configurations, forcing practitioners to rely on multiple task-specific models or ground-truth poses that are unavailable in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251746/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any subset of up to 8 views, each carrying any combination of RGB images (encoded via SDXL VAE into latent space), camera poses (represented as Plücker ray maps), and depth maps (converted to disparities with a fixed shift/scale for Gaussian normalization). At inference, the user specifies which modalities are known (fed as clean conditions) and which are unknown (fed as noisy targets), and the model denoises the missing ones in a single forward pass. For 3D reconstruction, the generated multi-view images, poses, and depth maps are back-projected into point clouds to initialize a 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization.

## Method

Matrix3D is a multi-modal diffusion transformer built on the Hunyuan-DiT architecture, extended with a multi-view encoder that embeds conditioning data from all views and modalities into a shared latent space, and a multi-view decoder that processes noisy targets across views with cross-attention to the encoder. The key training strategy is masked learning at the image level: during training, specific views or modalities are randomly masked (or assigned to task-specific roles in a 3:3:3:1 ratio for NVS, pose, depth, and fully random), so the model learns the joint distribution over all modalities and can handle arbitrary I/O combinations at inference. Positional encoding uses RoPE for intra-image patch tokens and absolute sinusoidal codes with distinct base frequencies for view and modality IDs. Training proceeds in three stages (4-view/256px → 8-view/256px → 8-view/512px) using v-prediction loss with 10% condition dropout for classifier-free guidance, initialized from pre-trained Hunyuan-DiT checkpoints and trained on a mixture of six datasets covering objects and indoor/outdoor scenes.

## Results

On CO3D pose estimation, Matrix3D (RGB only) achieves 95.6–96.5% relative rotation accuracy@15° and 87.8–93.5% camera center accuracy@0.1 across 2–8 input views, outperforming the strongest baseline RayDiffusion (90.4–92.4% rotation, 81.9–93.1% center) by 3–6 points. On GSO novel view synthesis, Matrix3D reaches 20.45 PSNR (vs. SyncDreamer 19.22) and 18.97 PSNR (vs. Wonder3D 13.28) under matched view configurations. For monocular metric depth on DTU, Matrix3D without CFG achieves 0.038 AbsRel and 40.2 RMS, substantially beating Metric3D v2 (0.064 AbsRel, 75.5 RMS) and Depth Anything v2 (0.077 AbsRel, 85.2 RMS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Matrix3D decisively outperforms all pose estimation baselines on CO3D: it beats RayDiffusion (the strongest prior method) by ~4–5 points in rotation accuracy and ~6–7 points in camera center accuracy at 8 views, and surpasses DUSt3R by ~4–17 points depending on the metric. For novel view synthesis on GSO, Matrix3D wins on PSNR against SyncDreamer (+1.23), Wonder3D (+5.69), InstantMesh (+4.88), and Zero123 (+1.31), and edges out Zero123-XL by 0.12 PSNR while matching or exceeding SSIM/LPIPS. On monocular depth (DTU), Matrix3D without CFG achieves 0.038 AbsRel, halving the error of Metric3D v2 (0.064) and Depth Anything v2 (0.077). In multi-view depth, Matrix3D (1.83 rel error) outperforms DUSt3R-224 (2.76) and the Robust MVD baseline (2.7). However, for end-to-end 3D reconstruction, Matrix3D trails CAT3D slightly on posed few-shot PSNR (20.02 vs. 20.57) and LPIPS (0.396 vs. 0.351), and falls short of IM3D on monocular CLIP score (88.76 vs. 91.40), though the authors note they use fewer than half the novel views and a single GPU versus CAT3D's 16 A100s.

## Relation to Prior Work

Matrix3D extends the end-to-end photogrammetry line initiated by DUSt3R and PF-LRM (which jointly predict poses and point maps) by adding novel view synthesis and depth as first-class generative outputs within a single diffusion transformer. Architecturally, it builds on HunyuanDiT as the base DiT backbone, adopts Plücker ray-map camera encoding from RayDiffusion, and applies a multi-view, multi-modal masked learning strategy inspired by 4M and MAE. The key conceptual departure from prior feed-forward approaches (DUSt3R, PF-LRM) is the use of a generative diffusion model with flexible I/O configurations, enabling arbitrary combinations of input modalities and output targets rather than a fixed task pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying pose estimation, depth prediction, and novel view synthesis into one model, Matrix3D eliminates the multi-stage photogrammetry pipeline and its accumulated errors, offering a practical single-model solution for single/few-shot 3D reconstruction. The masked learning strategy is particularly impactful because it allows training on partially complete data (e.g., image-pose or image-depth pairs), substantially expanding the usable training pool. Limitations include the still-required 3DGS optimization step for final reconstruction, inferior point-cloud quality compared to DUSt3R (attributed to lack of direct point-position supervision), and the high training cost (~20 days on 64–128 A100s). Open questions include scalability beyond 8 views, whether the unified generative approach can fully replace optimization-based refinement, and how well the model generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes.
