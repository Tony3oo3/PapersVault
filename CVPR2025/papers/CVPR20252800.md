## Problem

State-of-the-art top-down human pose estimation methods ignore out-of-image keypoints in both training and evaluation, and rely on uncalibrated heatmaps that restrict keypoint representation to fixed-Gaussian point estimates. This means models are never penalized for guessing locations of absent keypoints, and the output cannot express uncertainty, calibrated probabilities, or the critical in/out-of-window decision needed for real-world applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252800/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a cropped person image (bounding box region, optionally enlarged to include context beyond the image border) and produces, per keypoint: a calibrated probability map over the activation window (values in [0,1] summing to 1 via Sparsemax), a scalar presence probability indicating whether the keypoint lies within the activation window, a visibility estimate, and an OKS quality score. Point estimates are decoded by maximizing expected OKS across pixels rather than taking the argmax, and the activation window can extend beyond the image boundary to capture out-of-image keypoints.

## Method

ProbPose replaces traditional Gaussian heatmaps with probability maps trained via a pixel-wise OKSLoss formulated as expected risk minimization, removing the fixed-Gaussian shape assumption and aligning training directly with the evaluation metric. A separate presence probability head (trained with BCE on crop-augmented samples) explicitly predicts whether each keypoint is inside the activation window, complementing the probability map which only models location conditional on presence. Decoding uses expected OKS maximization—computing the OKS-weighted expectation over the probability map for each candidate pixel—making it robust to sharp local peaks and bimodal distributions. Crop-based data augmentation generates out-of-image training samples by randomly cropping annotated keypoints, simultaneously improving in-image localization (analogous to Hide&Seek) and enabling presence probability learning. An optional double-probmap variant pairs a high-resolution expert map with a wider-field-of-view map to localize keypoints up to 25% outside the bounding box with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On COCO, ProbPose-s achieves 76.6 mAP and 76.4 Ex-mAP, outperforming the ViTPose-s baseline (75.9 / 75.3) by ~0.7 points. The most dramatic gain appears on CropCOCO, where ProbPose-s reaches 81.7 mAP and 73.9 Ex-mAP versus 72.7 / 66.5 for ViTPose-s — a +9.0 mAP and +7.4 Ex-mAP improvement in out-of-image keypoint localization. On OCHuman (unseen during training), ProbPose-s matches or slightly exceeds all baselines (60.4 mAP, 60.2 Ex-mAP), while the double-probmap variant ProbPose-s-DP leads with 61.4 mAP and 61.2 Ex-mAP, benefiting from its wider field of view in multi-body scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProbPose-s outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and metric. On standard COCO, the gains over ViTPose-s are modest (+0.7 mAP, +1.1 Ex-mAP), consistent with the paper's claim that probability maps match heatmap localization accuracy while adding versatility. The decisive advantage emerges on CropCOCO: ProbPose-s surpasses ViTPose-s by +9.0 mAP and +7.4 Ex-mAP, and leads the next-best model (SWIN-t) by +10.4 mAP, demonstrating that crop data augmentation and explicit presence probability prediction are critical for out-of-image keypoints. On OCHuman, ProbPose-s edges out ViTPose-s and HRFormer-s by 0.1 mAP, while the double-probmap variant ProbPose-s-DP achieves the best OCHuman scores (61.4 mAP, 61.2 Ex-mAP), suggesting its wider activation window aids multi-body disambiguation. The Ex-mAP gap over ViTPose-s is consistently larger than the mAP gap across all three datasets, confirming that the presence probability head — not just localization — drives the improvement. ProbPose-s-DP trades ~0.4 mAP on COCO and ~0.8 mAP on CropCOCO relative to ProbPose-s for the broader field of view, a cost the authors attribute to the larger activation window making presence classification harder.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProbPose builds directly on ViTPose as its backbone and on the OKSLoss formulation from Yolo-pose [19], but departs from the heatmap paradigm by replacing Gaussian-targeted heatmaps with Sparsemax-normalized probability maps trained via a pixel-wise expected-risk OKS loss. The crop-based data augmentation draws on the information-dropping lineage of Hide&Seek [10], but repurposes it to generate out-of-image training samples rather than merely occluding visible keypoints. The double-probmap design for expanding the activation window is a novel architectural choice with no direct prior, and the Ex-OKS metric extends the standard OKS evaluation to a setting (out-of-image keypoints) that prior top-down methods have entirely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap: real-world deployments (human-robot interaction, cropped video) routinely encounter keypoints outside the visible frame, yet no prior evaluation protocol or training procedure accounted for them. The CropCOCO dataset and Ex-OKS metric give the community a concrete tool to measure this, and the presence-probability head offers a calibrated, interpretable alternative to the ad-hoc confidence thresholding that current pipelines rely on. Open questions remain around whether probability maps and expected-OKS decoding help in multi-body scenes (where bimodal distributions are common), and the unresolved COCO annotation bias near bounding-box borders (~0.2% AP) limits the ceiling of in-image gains. The double-probmap's modest COCO regression also suggests that expanding the field of view is not free, and the trade-off needs careful per-application tuning.
