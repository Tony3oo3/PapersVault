## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based head avatar methods suffer from three coupled limitations: rigid parametric-model rigging cannot capture person-specific deformations, pre-fitted pseudo-2D keypoint tracking introduces expression errors that degrade reconstruction, and direct color fitting prevents realistic relighting under novel lighting. No prior 3DGS method simultaneously addresses all three while maintaining real-time rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252083/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular head video (cropped and matted to 512×512) along with pre-tracked FLAME shape parameters (shared across frames) and pose parameters from IMavatar. It produces an animatable 3D Gaussian avatar whose appearance is decomposed into per-point albedo, roughness, and Fresnel base reflectance, enabling real-time rendering (~155 FPS) under arbitrary environment maps. Expression and jaw parameters are not fixed inputs but are refined by an end-to-end encoder during training, so the final avatar generalizes to unseen expressions without per-frame post-optimization.

## Method

HRAvatar replaces rigid FLAME mesh rigging with independently learnable per-point blendshapes (shape, expression, pose bases) and per-point linear blend skinning weights, all initialized from FLAME priors but freely optimized to capture individual deformations. An expression encoder, initialized from SMIRK and trained end-to-end with photometric supervision, extracts accurate expression and jaw parameters, reducing the tracking-error propagation that plagues fitting-based pipelines. For appearance, the method decomposes each Gaussian's color into albedo, roughness, and Fresnel base reflectance, then applies a SplitSum-approximated BRDF with optimizable irradiance and prefiltered environment cube maps for physically-based shading. Normal maps are supervised against depth-derived normals to ensure smooth relighting, and an albedo pseudo-prior from IntrinsicAnything prevents local lighting from being erroneously baked into the albedo channel.

## Results

HRAvatar achieves the best scores across all four metrics (PSNR, MAE*, SSIM, LPIPS) on all three evaluation datasets. On the INSTA dataset, it reaches 30.36 PSNR, 0.845 MAE*, 0.9482 SSIM, and 0.0569 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline GBS (29.64 PSNR, 0.0823 LPIPS) by 0.72 dB in PSNR and 31% in LPIPS. On the more challenging HDTF dataset, HRAvatar scores 28.55 PSNR and 0.0825 LPIPS, again leading all baselines. The method also renders at approximately 155 FPS for animation and relighting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRAvatar outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest margins in LPIPS (perceptual quality). Against the closest competitor GBS [43], HRAvatar gains 0.72 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.0823 to 0.0569 on INSTA (a 31% improvement), and from 0.1297 to 0.0825 on HDTF (36% improvement). Against Flash-avatar [59], the gains are 1.23 dB PSNR and 21% lower LPIPS on INSTA. The older NeRF-based INSTA [80] and Point-avatar [78] lag substantially, with Point-avatar scoring the lowest PSNR (26.84) on INSTA. For relighting, HRAvatar is compared qualitatively against FLARE [2], which exhibits blocky artifacts from corrupted normals, while HRAvatar produces smoother, more consistent lighting. Notably, HRAvatar achieves these gains while also enabling real-time relighting (~155 FPS), a capability none of the other baselines provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

HRAvatar sits at the intersection of 3DGS-based head avatar reconstruction and physically-based relighting. It builds directly on 3DGS for real-time rendering, FLAME for geometric initialization, and GaussianShader's normal estimation strategy, while departing from prior 3DGS avatar methods (Point-avatar, Flash-avatar, GBS) by replacing shared-MLP or mesh-rigged deformation with independently learned per-point blendshapes and linear blend skinning. The end-to-end expression encoder (initialized from SMIRK) and the BRDF/SplitSum shading pipeline with IntrinsicAnything albedo priors represent the key conceptual departures from methods that either pre-track expressions or fit appearance via spherical harmonics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HRAvatar closes a practical gap by delivering high-fidelity, animatable head avatars with realistic relighting at ~155 FPS from a single phone video, making it directly applicable to AR/VR, virtual meetings, and content creation. However, the authors acknowledge that FLAME priors still limit control over hair and accessories, albedo estimation remains imperfect (shadows and specular highlights can leak into material maps), and full-head reconstruction fails at extreme yaw angles due to monocular pose estimation limitations. Open questions include whether the learnable deformation framework can generalize beyond per-subject training and whether better intrinsic decomposition models will resolve the remaining relighting artifacts.
