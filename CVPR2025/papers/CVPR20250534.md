## Problem

Learning signed distance functions from sparse, noisy point clouds remains challenging because standard empirical risk minimization overfits on limited queries, producing missing geometry and hallucinations. While recent work (NAP) introduced pointwise adversarial regularization via first-order Taylor approximations, it never optimizes over true worst-case distributions. This paper is the first to formulate SDF learning as a distributionally robust optimization problem with Wasserstein uncertainty sets, yielding tractable dual objectives that produce globally coherent worst-case query distributions rather than independent local perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250534/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unoriented, sparse point cloud (typically 1024 points) with arbitrary noise as input and produces a neural SDF parameterized by an MLP, from which a watertight triangle mesh is extracted via Marching Cubes. No ground-truth geometry, normals, or labels are required during training. Inputs span synthetic meshes with added Gaussian noise (σ up to 0.025), real 3D body scans, LiDAR road scenes, and SfM-reconstructed point clouds from sparse multi-view images, all at densities as low as 100 points/m³.

## Method

Building on Neural-Pull's query-pulling loss, the paper replaces the fixed empirical query distribution with a worst-case distribution within a Wasserstein ball around it, solved via a dual reformulation. Two variants are derived: SDF-WDRO uses the classical Wasserstein dual with an iteratively updated dual variable λ and gradient-ascent inner loop, while SDF-SDRO substitutes the Sinkhorn distance (Wasserstein + entropic regularization), yielding a closed-form sampling distribution Q_{q,ρ} = N(q, ρI₃) and a log-sum-exp loss that is significantly faster to optimize. The final training objective combines the original NP loss and the DRO loss with learnable weights (following Liebel & Körner), and the entropic regularization in SDRO produces diffused spatial adversaries that distribute SDF approximation errors more evenly across the shape, improving both accuracy and convergence speed over WDRO and the NP baseline.

## Results

On ShapeNet (1024 sparse noisy points), SDF SDRO achieves the best reconstruction quality across all metrics: CD1=0.63, CD2=0.012, NC=0.90, FS=0.86, outperforming the closest competitors NAP and SparseOcc (CD1=0.76, CD2=0.020) by ~17% in CD1. On the 3D Scene benchmark, SDRO achieves the best mean CD1 (0.020) and CD2 (0.0013), roughly halving the error of NAP (CD1=0.041). On Faust, the method is competitive but slightly behind NAP (CD1 0.251 vs 0.220), with the paper noting NAP performs better under lower noise conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDF SDRO outperforms all 14 baselines on ShapeNet CD1 and CD2, with the largest gains over NP (CD1 0.63 vs 1.16, a 46% reduction) and SPSR (CD1 0.63 vs 2.34). Against the closest competitors NAP and SparseOcc, SDRO improves CD1 by 17% (0.63 vs 0.76) and CD2 by 40% (0.012 vs 0.020), while also achieving the best NC (0.90) and FS (0.86). On the 3D Scene benchmark, SDRO achieves the best mean CD1 (0.020) and CD2 (0.0013), roughly halving NAP's error (0.041/0.004) and outperforming SparseOcc (0.027/0.003). On Faust, NAP slightly edges out SDRO (CD1 0.220 vs 0.251), which the authors attribute to NAP's local adaptive radii being more effective under lower noise; supervised methods (POCO, NKSR) still lead on Faust but require large training datasets. The noise ablation (Table 4) confirms SDRO's advantage grows with noise level: at σ=0.025, SDRO achieves CD1=1.54 vs NAP's 2.21 and SparseOcc's 2.16, a 30–35% improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Pull (NP) framework for SDF learning by replacing its fixed empirical query distribution with worst-case distributions from a Wasserstein uncertainty set, building directly on the adversarial DRO formulation introduced by NAP. The key conceptual departure from NAP is the shift from pointwise, first-order Taylor-approximated adversaries to full worst-case distributions found via tractable dual reformulations of Wasserstein DRO. The authors further draw on recent Sinkhorn DRO results to substitute the Wasserstein distance with an entropically regularized Sinkhorn distance, yielding a computationally efficient training procedure that produces smoother, more diffused spatial adversaries. The multi-task training strategy with learnable weights from Liebel & Körner is used to balance the original NP loss with the DRO regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first application of distributionally robust optimization with Wasserstein/Sinkhorn uncertainty sets to neural SDF learning, opening a path toward principled regularization of implicit representations beyond ad-hoc smoothness priors. The practical impact is demonstrated across object, articulated, and scene-level reconstruction, with consistent gains under high noise where prior methods degrade. A notable limitation is that NAP remains competitive on clean, dense inputs, suggesting the DRO overhead is most beneficial in the sparse-noisy regime; the authors identify combining NAP's local adaptive radii with SDRO's global distribution control as a promising direction. More broadly, the framework suggests DRO could serve as a general regularization paradigm for learning neural implicit functions from incomplete or noisy observations.
