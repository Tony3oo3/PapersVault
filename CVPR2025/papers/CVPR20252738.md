## Problem

Existing 3D inpainting methods are largely limited to front-facing scenes with small view changes, producing artifacts such as floaters and blurry textures when applied to unconstrained scenes (e.g., 360° environments) with complex geometry and large view variation. The core challenge is maintaining both geometric coherence and texture consistency across many views without relying on large multi-view training datasets that lack sufficient diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252738/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D Gaussian Splatting scene (175 images at 1920×1080, with 125 training and 50 test views), associated camera poses, and object masks identifying targets for removal. It produces an inpainted GS scene with objects removed and occluded regions filled, along with a set of multi-view consistent inpainted images. A key detail is that the method distinguishes between object masks and the actual inpainting masks (never-before-seen regions), which on average cover only ~51% of the object mask area, avoiding unnecessary inpainting of already-visible regions.

## Method

The pipeline first removes object Gaussians by optimizing per-primitive binary parameters against rendered masks, then prunes the scene. For inpainting, a reference view is inpainted with Stable Diffusion Inpainting, and geometry is reconstructed via TSDF using monocular depth estimation to fill depth holes in the masked region. The inpainted reference content is warped to other views using the restored geometry, providing a coarse but consistent initialization. A multi-view refinement network—built by replacing self-attention in the pre-trained inpainting model with sparse space-time attention (attending only to neighboring views plus the reference)—is then fine-tuned per scene via test-time adaptation on synthesized paired data (warped images with augmented artifacts vs. ground-truth captures). Finally, the GS scene is fine-tuned for 7000 steps with L1 and SSIM losses against the refined multi-view images. A novel inpainting mask detection step dilates object masks, maps them to 3D via mask optimization, renders neighbor masks, and uses SAM to extract precise NBS-region masks.

## Results

On the authors' diverse unconstrained benchmark (20 scenes with 90°–360° view changes), IMFine achieves PSNR 19.67, LPIPS 0.2685, and FID 149.52, outperforming all baselines by substantial margins (e.g., +0.92 PSNR and −56.91 FID over the next-best SPInNeRF). On the front-facing SPINeRF dataset, IMFine achieves PSNR 17.58, LPIPS 0.4513, and FID 154.34, matching or exceeding prior methods on LPIPS and FID while being marginally behind MVIPNeRF on PSNR (17.58 vs. 17.67). The inpainting mask detection module achieves 81.12% mIoU against manual annotations, compared to 42.55% for the GaussianEditor approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMFine decisively outperforms all four baselines on the unconstrained benchmark: it leads GaussianEditor by +3.96 PSNR and −225.51 FID, GScream by +2.49 PSNR and −141.11 FID, SPInNeRF by +0.92 PSNR and −56.91 FID, and MVIPNeRF by +1.04 PSNR and −129.47 FID. On the front-facing SPINeRF dataset, the advantage narrows: IMFine ties GScream on FID (154.34 vs. 154.71) and beats it on LPIPS (0.4513 vs. 0.3931), but trails MVIPNeRF by 0.09 PSNR (17.58 vs. 17.67) while still leading on LPIPS and FID. The paper notes that SPINeRF's limited front-facing scope makes it harder to differentiate methods, whereas the unconstrained setting exposes the weaknesses of prior approaches (floaters, blurry textures, view inconsistency). Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 5) confirm that baselines produce severe artifacts in complex 360° scenes, while IMFine maintains coherent geometry and texture detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 2D diffusion-based inpainting and 3D scene representation, building directly on Stable Diffusion Inpainting as the base model and adopting sparse space-time attention from video diffusion models to enforce multi-view consistency. It departs from prior 3D inpainting methods (SPINeRF, MVIPNeRF, InNeRF360) that either rely on score distillation (suffering over-smoothing) or enforce consistency via perceptual loss (yielding blurry textures) by instead using geometry-guided warping as a coarse-to-fine strategy followed by a test-time-adapted refinement network. The key conceptual departure is treating the multi-view refinement problem as a per-scene fine-tuning task on a pre-trained inpainting model rather than training a general multi-view inpainting network on limited datasets, and introducing the distinction between object masks and true inpainting (never-before-seen) masks for 360° scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing 3D inpainting methods degrade significantly on unconstrained scenes with large view variation, which are common in real-world applications like AR/VR and the Metaverse. The test-time adaptation strategy is attractive because it avoids the need for large multi-view training datasets, though the ~1-hour per-scene fine-tuning cost limits scalability. The finding that inpainting masks cover only ~51% of object mask area in 360° scenes is a useful practical insight that prior methods overlooked. Open questions include whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning or personalization techniques can eliminate the per-scene adaptation bottleneck, and whether the reliance on monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything) and SAM for mask detection introduces failure modes in challenging scenes.
