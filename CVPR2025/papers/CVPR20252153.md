## Problem

Existing polarized inverse rendering methods only model dielectric materials, producing significant errors when conductors (steel, gold, aluminum) are present. Two compounding issues arise: conductors lack subsurface scattering (so diffuse polarization does not exist) and have complex refractive indices, both of which break the standard Baek pBRDF; additionally, conductors are typically glossy, and strong specular reflections degrade geometry initialization from intensity-based volume rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252153/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view polarized images (four linearly polarized views per camera pose, composited to HDR for real data), a user-specified binary conductor-dielectric mask, and an object mask. It outputs a signed distance field for geometry, a BRDF field parameterized by roughness, diffuse albedo, and a complex refractive index (η − ki, with η and k each being 3-channel RGB values), and an incident Stokes field for multi-bounce lighting. The dielectric refractive index is fixed at 1.5, while the conductor refractive index is fully estimated per-surface-point; the binary mask is a discrete (not continuous) parameter that gates the diffuse polarization term.

## Method

NeISF++ extends the NeISF pipeline with two key modifications. First, it replaces the dielectric-only Baek pBRDF with a general pBRDF that (a) multiplies the diffuse polarization term by a binary indicator m (0 for conductors, 1 for dielectrics) to suppress non-existent subsurface scattering, and (b) computes the Fresnel reflection matrix F^R using the full Fresnel wave theory so that reflection coefficients and phase delay correctly handle complex refractive indices. Second, it introduces a DoLP-based geometry initialization stage: a VolSDF network is first trained to match ground-truth degree-of-linear-polarization images (which are invariant to light intensity and thus robust to strong specular highlights) with only a tiny-weight intensity loss, before entering the joint optimization stage. In the joint stage, the SDF, BRDF field (now outputting {r, a, η, k}), and incident Stokes field are optimized end-to-end via self-supervised L1 losses on rendered Stokes vectors and DoLP, with complex-number autodifferentiation handled natively in PyTorch.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, NeISF++ achieves mean angular errors of 6.487° (Stanford scan) and 1.789° (Helmet) for surface normal reconstruction, outperforming all baselines. For material reconstruction, NeISF++ reduces roughness error to 0.0706/0.0161 and albedo error to 0.0468/0.0615 on the two scenes, compared to NeISF's 0.2425/0.2075 and 0.2954/0.4467 respectively. The DoLP-based geometry initialization (VolSDF-DoLP) alone achieves 7.641°/4.715°, already surpassing standard VolSDF (11.754°/8.829°) and NeISF (14.022°/10.303°), confirming the robustness of DoLP to strong specular reflections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeISF++ outperforms all four baselines on geometry reconstruction. Against NeISF [33] (the closest prior work), NeISF++ reduces mean angular error from 14.022° to 6.487° on Stanford scan and from 10.303° to 1.789° on Helmet, a 2.2× and 5.8× improvement respectively, attributed to the correct conductor material model and DoLP-based initialization. Against NeRO [36], which is designed for specular objects but does not use polarization, NeISF++ wins on both scenes (6.487° vs 13.352°; 1.789° vs 5.001°). Against PANDORA [14], the gap is largest (21.740° → 6.487° on Stanford scan), reflecting the advantage of full pBRDF decomposition over geometry-only reconstruction. Against standard VolSDF [55], the improvement (11.754° → 6.487°) validates the DoLP initialization strategy. On material reconstruction (Table 2), NeISF++ reduces roughness and albedo errors by roughly 3–7× compared to NeISF, and additionally reconstructs the complex refractive index (ior real/imag) which NeISF cannot estimate at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends NeISF [33], the first neural polarized inverse rendering method with pBRDF decomposition, by generalizing its material model from dielectrics-only to also support conductors. The key modifications are: (1) extending the Baek pBRDF [4] with a binary diffuse indicator and a complex-number-capable Fresnel term, and (2) replacing the standard VolSDF [55] intensity-based geometry initialization with a DoLP-based one that exploits the intensity-invariance of polarization. The paper positions itself as the first polarized inverse rendering pipeline handling both material classes simultaneously, filling a gap left by PANDORA [14], NeISF [33], and related works that all assumed dielectric-only scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Conductors (steel, gold, aluminum) are ubiquitous in real scenes, yet all prior polarized inverse rendering methods silently assumed dielectric materials, producing qualitatively wrong geometry and material for any metallic object. The DoLP-based initialization is a physically motivated and elegant solution to the specular-reflection problem that could benefit other neural implicit surface methods beyond polarization. However, the requirement for a user-provided conductor-dielectric mask is a significant practical limitation that the authors acknowledge, and the binary (rather than continuous) metallic indicator, while physically justified, constrains the method's flexibility. Open questions include automatic mask generation, whether the approach extends to partially transparent conductors, and whether the complex refractive index estimation generalizes to spectrally varying materials beyond the three-band RGB assumption.
