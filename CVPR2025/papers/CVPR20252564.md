## Problem

Existing marine fog datasets are confined to a single region or a single satellite, preventing rigorous evaluation of model generalization across diverse geographic and observational conditions. This limitation has led to over-optimistic local performance assessments and constrained exploration of the underlying mechanisms governing marine fog formation and dissipation on a global scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252564/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset organizes multi-spectral geostationary satellite imagery in a 'cube-stream' structure with dimensions T×C×H×W (timestamp × spectral band × latitude × longitude), where spatial resolution is standardized to 1 km (1024×1024 pixels) and the minimum temporal interval is 30 minutes. The number of spectral bands C varies by satellite (14 for FY4A, 16 for H8/9, etc.), and the full dataset spans 15 coastal regions across 6 satellites with 68,000 samples, of which 11,600 carry pixel-level fog annotations. For detection, models map a single frame (C×H×W) to a binary mask (H×W); for forecasting, models map a temporal sequence (T×C×H×W) to a future sequence (T′×C×H×W).

## Method

The core contribution is the MFogHub dataset construction and a comprehensive 16-model benchmarking framework. Data collection begins with 9.5 million ICOADS ship observations (2015–2024) aggregated onto a 0.25°×0.25° global grid, from which 15 high-traffic, fog-prone coastal regions are identified via a 12.8° sliding window. Multi-spectral L1 data from six geostationary satellites are projected and standardized to 1 km resolution, then organized into 21 spatio-temporally aligned cube-streams that support flexible slicing across region, satellite, and spectral band dimensions. Eight detection baselines (Deeplabv3+, UNet, UNet++, ABCNet, BANet, Unetformer, ViT, DlinkViT) and eight forecasting baselines (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, MIM, PhyDNet, SimVP-v2, Uniformer, VAN, TAU) are evaluated to reveal generalization fluctuations across regions and satellites. Additional analyses examine spectral band sensitivity (true-color vs. natural-color combinations), the benefit of multi-region training over single-region training, and the impact of positive-to-negative sample ratios on detection performance.

## Results

On the MFogHub dataset, DlinkViT achieves the best marine fog detection performance across both satellites, reaching CSI of 60.25 (H8/9) and 56.93 (FY4A) in the Yellow/Bohai Sea region, outperforming ViT (57.03/52.77) and Deeplabv3+ (51.83/44.07). For forecasting, PredRNN leads on H8/9 data (MSE 636.37, SSIM 0.8090) while SimVP-v2 dominates on FY4A (MSE 832.23, SSIM 0.7416), demonstrating that model rankings shift across satellite platforms. Regional generalization experiments reveal substantial performance drops: Deeplabv3+ CSI falls from 51.17 (C.C.) to 20.73 (B.C.), and BANet CSI drops from 63.03 (C.C.) to 43.22 (G.A.), highlighting the dataset's ability to expose domain gaps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For detection, DlinkViT [39] consistently outperforms all seven other baselines on both H8/9 and FY4A satellites, achieving the highest CSI (60.25 vs. 57.36 for ABCNet on H8/9; 56.93 vs. 52.77 for ViT on FY4A) and mIoU (79.24 and 77.38 respectively). ViT [4] and ABCNet [17] are the strongest runners-up on H8/9 but show notable sensitivity to satellite switch, with ViT gaining ~5.7 CSI points on FY4A while ABCNet gains only ~0.9. Deeplabv3+ [3] is the weakest detector across all settings (CSI 44.07–51.83). For forecasting, PredRNN [34] dominates on H8/9 (MSE 636.37, SSIM 0.8090) but drops to third on FY4A, where SimVP-v2 [29] takes the lead (MSE 832.23, SSIM 0.7416), revealing satellite-dependent model rankings. ConvLSTM [27] and PhyDNet [6] are consistently the weakest forecasters. The key empirical finding is that no single model generalizes uniformly: model rankings reshuffle across both regional (Table 2) and satellite (Tables 4–5) dimensions, which is precisely the generalization insight MFogHub is designed to expose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cube-stream data structure introduced by Kondylatos et al. (Mesogeos, NeurIPS 2024) from wildfire modeling to marine fog, adapting it to organize multi-spectral, multi-temporal satellite observations. All prior marine fog datasets (Nilo et al. 2018, Huang et al. 2021, Mahdavi et al. 2020/2024, Zhou et al. 2022, etc.) are confined to a single region and a single satellite, limiting cross-domain evaluation. MFogHub departs from this by integrating 15 coastal regions and 6 geostationary satellites into a unified, open-source benchmark, enabling the first systematic assessment of regional and satellite generalization for fog detection and forecasting models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MFogHub fills a critical gap in the marine fog literature by providing the first publicly available multi-regional, multi-satellite resource, enabling researchers to move beyond region-specific validation and probe true model generalizability. The empirical finding that no single model dominates across all regions and satellites, and that multi-region training improves cross-region performance, provides actionable guidance for building robust operational fog monitoring systems. Open questions remain on how to best fuse heterogeneous multi-satellite data (different band counts and wavelengths), whether the 2:1 positive-to-negative ratio generalizes to all regions, and how to exploit the complementary spectral coverage of FY4A and H8/9 for joint training.
