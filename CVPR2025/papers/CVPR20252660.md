## Problem

Generating task-agnostic, slide-level representations from pathology WSIs without human annotations remains challenging: existing SSL approaches either require multimodal data (text, gene expression), rely on stochastic image augmentations that modern FMs are designed to be invariant to, or demand very large pretraining corpora (10K–1M+ WSIs). This paper asks whether a single-modality, feature-space contrastive method can produce competitive slide embeddings with only ~3K WSIs while remaining compatible with arbitrary foundation models at inference time.

## Input / Output

COBRA consumes frozen tile embeddings (224×224 px patches after Canny background removal) from four FMs of varying sizes (768–1536 dimensional) extracted at three magnifications (0.5, 1.14, 2 MPP), producing a single d-dimensional patient-level vector. At inference, the single-FM mode computes attention weights from the encoded (Mamba-processed) embeddings but aggregates the original unencoded patch embeddings via a weighted average, while the multi-FM mode averages across all pretrained FMs after the per-FM embedding MLPs before encoding.

## Method

The slide encoder consists of per-FM embedding MLPs (LayerNorm → Linear → SiLU → Linear) that project heterogeneous FM dimensions into a shared space, followed by two Mamba-2 state-space dual (SSD) layers with residual connections for efficient sequence encoding, and a multi-head gated attention module that aggregates tile embeddings into a single vector. Training uses a MoCo-v3-style InfoNCE contrastive objective where different FMs and magnifications of the same patient's WSI serve as positive pairs in feature space, eliminating the need for stochastic image augmentations entirely. The key architectural insight is that multiple FMs and multiple magnifications act as natural feature-space augmentations, making the method FM-agnostic: at inference, COBRA can accept embeddings from any previously unseen FM and improve upon its mean-patch baseline without retraining. Pretraining on only 3048 TCGA WSIs across five cancer types yields slide embeddings that outperform all prior slide encoders by at least +4.4% average AUC on four external CPTAC cohorts.

## Results

COBRA achieves a 75.3% average AUC across 15 downstream biomarker prediction and classification tasks on four CPTAC external validation cohorts, outperforming the next-best slide encoder PRISM (70.9%) by +4.4% average AUC. On the COAD tasks, COBRA shows particularly large gains: 94.1% AUC on MSI status (vs. 79.1% for PRISM) and 87.8% on BRAF mutation (vs. 59.9% for PRISM). COBRA is the only slide encoder that surpasses the mean patch embedding baseline of Virchow2 (73.8%), achieving +1.5% average AUC over it, despite being pretrained on only 3,048 WSIs versus over 587K for PRISM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COBRA outperforms all four existing slide encoders (GigaPath-SE, MADELEINE, CHIEF, PRISM) on average AUC, with the largest margin over PRISM (+4.4%) and GigaPath-SE (+12.5%). Against the mean patch embedding baselines of individual FMs, COBRA surpasses Virchow2 mean (73.8%) by +1.5% and H-Optimus mean (71.0%) by +4.3%, but falls slightly below the concatenated mean of all four FMs (74.1%). On COAD-specific tasks, COBRA achieves particularly large gains: MSI status (94.1% vs. 79.1% PRISM, +15.0%) and BRAF mutation (87.8% vs. 59.9% PRISM, +27.9%). On BRCA tasks, COBRA outperforms MADELEINE (which was trained only on BRCA) by +9.5% on ESR1, +5.5% on PGR, and +4.9% on ERBB2, though MADELEINE slightly edges COBRA on PIK3CA (−1.3%). COBRA also demonstrates generalization to unseen FMs: when applied to GigaPath patch embeddings (not used during pretraining), it improves over the GigaPath mean baseline by +2.5% average AUC and over PRISM by +3.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

COBRA positions itself within the slide-level representation learning line that began with GigaSSL (contrastive learning on augmented patches) and GigaPath (masked autoencoding on tile embeddings), and was recently extended by multimodal approaches like PRISM, MADELEINE, and CHIEF. It departs from these by replacing image-level or cross-modal augmentations with feature-space augmentations derived from multiple frozen foundation models and multiple magnifications, eliminating the need for stochastic image transforms or paired text/gene-expression data. The architectural choice of Mamba-2 state-space dual modules (inspired by MambaMIL) for sequence encoding, combined with a MoCo-v3-style contrastive objective and multi-head gated attention for aggregation, distinguishes it from ViT-based slide encoders like GigaPath-SE and PRISM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is the dramatic data efficiency: COBRA matches or exceeds slide encoders pretrained on 10K–587K WSIs using only 3,048 WSIs, and its FM-agnostic inference mode means it can immediately benefit from future foundation models without retraining. This is particularly valuable in computational pathology where annotated data is scarce and new FMs emerge frequently. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the five TCGA tissue types used for pretraining, whether non-contrastive SSL objectives (e.g., masked modeling) could further improve representations, and whether the performance ceiling is ultimately bounded by the quality of the frozen FMs it aggregates.
