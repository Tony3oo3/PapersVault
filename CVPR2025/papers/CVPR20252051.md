## Problem

Existing hallucination detection methods for VLMs rely on computationally expensive off-the-shelf models (e.g., GPT-4) for cross-verification, introducing high latency and resource demands unsuitable for real-time deployment. Moreover, these methods produce binary hallucinated/truthful labels that fail to capture the nuanced confidence spectrum needed for ambiguous cases, and no large-scale dataset provides token-level hallucination type annotations across diverse vision-language tasks.

## Input / Output

HalLocalizer takes an RGB image paired with a VLM-generated text response (ranging from ~1-word VQA answers to ~57-word captions) and optionally the last hidden states of the LVLM during generation. For every token in the response, it outputs four independent probability scores—one per hallucination type (object, attribute, relationship, scene)—enabling concurrent, token-level detection with graded confidence rather than a single binary verdict. The model is designed as a plug-and-play module that runs alongside the VLM's autoregressive decoding without modifying the base model.

## Method

HalLocalizer consists of a bidirectional VisualBERT encoder followed by four linear classification heads, one per hallucination type, operating at the token level. Two architecture variants are explored: one ingests LVLM hidden states (cached from MiniGPT4, InstructBLIP, LLaVA, or InternVL) as visual-linguistic input, and the other processes the raw text response with VisualBERT's own vision encoder. The model is trained via supervised learning on the HalLoc dataset (155K samples) using AdamW for 25 epochs on four A6000 GPUs (~10 hours), with only the detection module parameters updated. The HalLoc dataset itself is constructed through a two-stage HQA-Injection Pipeline: GPT-4 first crafts hallucinated QA pairs mimicking concept association bias and statistical bias patterns observed in VLMs, then injects these hallucinations into source texts spanning VQA, instruction-following, and captioning tasks, with up to three verification rounds per injection to ensure contextual coherence.

## Results

On the VQA subset, HalLocalizer with InternVL embeddings achieves the highest F1-scores across all four hallucination types: 0.71 (Object), 0.94 (Attribute), 0.71 (Relationship), and 0.93 (Scene). On the Instruct subset, HalLocalizer without LVLM embeddings leads with F1-scores of 0.82 (Object), 0.97 (Attribute), 0.83 (Relationship), and 0.94 (Scene). On the Caption subset, the text-only VisualBERT variant (w/o embedding) performs best, reaching F1-scores of 0.68 (Object), 0.64 (Attribute), 0.71 (Relationship), and 0.76 (Scene), substantially outperforming all LVLM-embedding variants on this longer-text task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is CHAIR [Rohrbach et al., 2019], a hand-crafted rule-based method that detects only object hallucinations by comparing mentioned objects against ground-truth data. HalLocalizer dramatically outperforms CHAIR on Object F1 across all three subsets: 0.71 vs. 0.19 on VQA, 0.82 vs. 0.13 on Instruct, and 0.68 vs. 0.04 on Caption. Moreover, CHAIR cannot detect attribute, relationship, or scene hallucinations at all, whereas HalLocalizer provides token-level probabilistic scores for all four types. Against the naive Always-1 baseline (which predicts every token as hallucinated), HalLocalizer achieves substantially higher precision while maintaining strong recall, e.g., 0.91 precision vs. 0.28 on VQA Object detection. The ablation across LVLM embeddings reveals that InternVL embeddings are most beneficial for short VQA responses, while the text-only VisualBERT variant (no LVLM embedding) is best for longer Instruct and Caption responses, suggesting LVLM hidden states may lose calibration as sequence length increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in VLM hallucination detection: (1) LLM-as-judge methods (GAVIE, FAITHScore, UNIHD) that are computationally expensive and produce binary verdicts, and (2) trained detectors (HaELM, MHalDetect) that operate at sentence or response level without type-specific annotations. HalLoc carves new ground by combining token-level granularity, four-way type classification (object, attribute, relationship, scene), and probabilistic output at a scale (155K samples) far exceeding prior datasets, while the HalLocalizer baseline repurposes VisualBERT as a lightweight concurrent detector rather than a post-hoc verifier. The HQA-Injection pipeline is a novel annotation strategy that leverages GQA scene graphs and GPT-4 to systematically craft and inject typed hallucinations, enabling controllable dataset construction at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HalLoc provides the first large-scale, token-level, multi-task benchmark for hallucination detection, filling a critical gap between coarse-grained evaluation suites (CHAIR, MHaluBench) and the need for fine-grained, type-aware detection in production VLMs. The plug-and-play HalLocalizer demonstrates that concurrent, low-overhead detection is feasible, opening a path toward real-time reliability monitoring in deployed systems. Open questions include whether the four-type taxonomy captures all meaningful hallucination modes, how detection performance generalizes to VLMs and tasks outside the training distribution, and the puzzling finding that LVLM embeddings can hurt detection on longer responses—suggesting calibration issues that remain unexplained. The probabilistic output also raises the question of how downstream systems should threshold or act on graded confidence scores.
