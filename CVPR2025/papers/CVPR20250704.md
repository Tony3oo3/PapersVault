## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks evaluate standalone clips and focus narrowly on visual elements like actions and object states, ignoring the narrative structure, character development, and multimodal composition (script, audio, effects) that define real-world series content. This gap limits the ability to assess whether MLLMs can perform the deep narrative reasoning—tracking plot causality, character motivations, and cross-episode continuity—that is essential for applications like series recommendation and interactive media.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes video frames sampled from interconnected drama series episodes (105 series, 1,072 videos, avg. 79.2s each), paired with a text question and optionally subtitles and thematic/character descriptions. Inputs can be ablated across five configurations (question-only, +frames, +subtitles, +both, +thematic-character info) to isolate modality contributions. Outputs span three formats—multiple-choice, true/false judgment, and open-ended text—across 28 fine-grained sub-tasks organized under five dimensions (Visuals, Script, Audio, Augmentation, Comprehension), with open-ended answers scored via BLEU-2, METEOR, and BERTScore F1.

## Method

PC-DCoT (Plot & Character Dual Chain of Thought) is an inference-time reasoning framework that decomposes narrative understanding into two parallel tracking chains. First, the MLLM extracts question-relevant events and characters from the raw video; a trained video clip model then isolates key scenes, and the MLLM generates sequential event descriptions (Plot Event Chain) while simultaneously tracking each character's appearances and behaviors across disjointed segments (Character Temporal Chains). Because both chains carry precise temporal annotations, events and character appearances are aligned on a shared time axis, allowing the MLLM to synthesize which characters participate in which events and how their interactions drive the plot. The benchmark construction itself employs a long-span narrative annotation method (annotators label events across extended temporal spans and summarize them in declarative statements) followed by a full-information transformation step where GPT-4o converts each annotation into multiple question types using subtitles, themes, and character bios as distractor material, yielding 29,196 tasks.

## Results

On SeriesBench, the best baseline GPT-4o achieves 62.8% overall accuracy, while the best open-source model Qwen2-VL reaches 60.3%. Applying the PC-DCoT framework yields substantial gains: GPT-4o† reaches 76.2% (↑13.4), Qwen2-VL† reaches 73.9% (↑13.6), InternVL2† reaches 73.3% (↑14.1), and MiniCPM-V 2.6† reaches 72.0% (↑12.9). Human performance is 95.8% overall, indicating a large remaining gap. Open-ended tasks are additionally measured with BLEU-2, METEOR, and BERTScore F1, where MiniCPM-V 2.6† achieves the highest BERTScore F1 (72.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 10 baselines, GPT-4o leads with 62.8% overall accuracy, outperforming the best open-source model Qwen2-VL (60.3%) by 2.5 points, primarily driven by stronger [Script] performance (62.8 vs. 57.5). Open-source models cluster tightly between 51.8% (VITA) and 60.3% (Qwen2-VL), with InternVL2 (59.2%) and MiniCPM-V 2.6 (59.1%) close behind. Notably, VideoLLaMA2.1-AV performs worse with audio input (53.1%) than without (51.7%), suggesting current audio-visual alignment is counterproductive. GPT-4o-mini (49.8%) trails GPT-4o by 13 points, highlighting the gap between smaller and larger commercial models. After applying PC-DCoT, all four tested models surpass the unassisted GPT-4o baseline, with GPT-4o† reaching 76.2%—still 19.6 points below human performance (95.8%), underscoring that narrative series understanding remains far from solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of video understanding benchmarks (Video-MME, VideoVista, MVBench, EgoSchema) that evaluate MLLMs on standalone clips focused on visual elements like actions and object states. It carves new ground by introducing narrative-driven series understanding as a distinct evaluation axis, requiring comprehension of plot development, character relationships, and cross-episode continuity that prior benchmarks do not capture. The PC-DCoT framework repurposes chain-of-thought reasoning and CLIP-based retrieval (for scene/character localization) into a structured dual-chain pipeline inspired by human binge-watching cognition, rather than relying on the sparse frame-sampling paradigm used by existing Video-MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeriesBench exposes a substantial and previously unmeasured gap: even GPT-4o achieves only ~63% overall versus 95.8% human performance, indicating that current MLLMs fundamentally struggle with narrative structure across connected episodes. The finding that subtitles outperform visual frames for narrative tasks, and that adding audio can *degrade* performance (VideoLLaMA2.1-AV), challenges assumptions about multimodal integration in video understanding. Open questions include whether the ~13% gain from PC-DCoT scales to longer series, how to close the remaining ~20-point gap to human-level narrative comprehension, and whether the benchmark's Kuaishou-sourced content generalizes to other narrative domains.
