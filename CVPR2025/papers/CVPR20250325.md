## Problem

Previous visual token pruning methods for Large Vision Language Models rely on a fixed, pre-defined pruning ratio applied uniformly across all LLM layers and all input instances. The paper demonstrates through preliminary experiments that the optimal pruning ratio is both layer-dependent (shallower layers are more sensitive) and instance-dependent (fine-grained tasks like counting require more tokens than coarse-grained scene understanding), making a single fixed ratio suboptimal for both efficiency and accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image encoded by CLIP-ViT-L into 576 visual tokens plus a text prompt, and produces a free-form text response. Visual tokens are adaptively pruned at selected intermediate LLM decoder layers (e.g., layers 4, 14, 24), with the number of retained tokens varying per instance and per layer rather than being fixed. Pruned tokens are irretrievable in subsequent layers, and the average retained token count across all 32 LLaMA layers is constrained to a target value (e.g., 144 or 88) via a training-time loss rather than a hard architectural constraint.

## Method

ATP-LLaVA inserts a lightweight Adaptive Token Pruning (ATP) module between selected LLM decoder layers. Each module computes two importance scores per visual token: a redundant pruning score combining self-attention (intra-modal) and cross-attention (text-to-vision) logits, and a spatial pruning score from uniform spatial sampling at a fixed rate. A small MLP with dual sigmoid heads takes the concatenated scores as input and predicts instance-specific, layer-specific thresholds for both pruning types; the final mask is the element-wise maximum of the two. During training, hard masks are replaced with differentiable soft masks (sigmoid with temperature) applied inside the attention softmax to enable end-to-end gradient flow, while at inference tokens below threshold are simply discarded. An ATP-Loss penalizes the sum of retained tokens weighted by layer depth, and a target-loss constrains the batch-average token count to a desired value, jointly balancing compression and performance. Retained tokens preserve their original 2D rotary positional embeddings to maintain spatial structure.

## Results

ATP-LLaVA achieves an average compression retention rate of 98.1% across seven benchmarks (GQA, MMBench, MME, POPE, SEED-Bench, ScienceQA, VQA v2) while reducing the average token count from 576 to 144 (75% reduction). At a more aggressive 88-token budget, it retains 94.6% of the upper-bound performance. Notably, ATP-LLaVA surpasses the unpruned LLaVA-1.5 upper bound on MMBench (66.0 vs. 64.3) and SEED-Bench (57.3 vs. 58.6 is close, but MMBench is a clear win), demonstrating that adaptive pruning can even enhance performance. In terms of efficiency, ATP-LLaVA reduces KV cache memory by 75%, CUDA inference time by 38.4%, and FLOPs by 78.1% compared to the unpruned model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PruMerge+ (the closest competitor at 144 tokens), ATP-LLaVA wins on GQA (59.5 vs. not reported), MMBench (66.0 vs. 64.9), MME (1473.9 vs. 1462.4), POPE (84.2 vs. 84.0), and ScienceQA (69.1 vs. 68.3), with only a marginal loss on VQA v2 (76.4 vs. 76.8); the average retention improves from 97.8% to 98.1%. Compared to FastV at 192 tokens, ATP-LLaVA at 144 tokens achieves substantially higher scores on every benchmark (e.g., MMB 66.0 vs. 61.2, POPE 84.2 vs. 64.8) while using 25% fewer tokens, yielding a 98.1% vs. 86.4% average retention. Against SparseVLM at 192 tokens, ATP-LLaVA at 144 tokens again dominates across all metrics (e.g., GQA 59.5 vs. 57.6, MMB 66.0 vs. 62.5, SEED 57.3 vs. 53.0), improving average retention from 94.2% to 98.1%. Even at the aggressive 88-token budget, ATP-LLaVA outperforms SparseVLM at 128 tokens on MMBench (64.7 vs. 60.0), MME (1401.5 vs. 1296.7), POPE (82.6 vs. 80.5), and SEED (55.7 vs. 50.2), with only a 0.5-point gap on VQA v2 (73.3 vs. 73.8). The key differentiator is that ATP-LLaVA's pruning ratio is instance- and layer-adaptive rather than fixed, which the ablation in Table 2 confirms yields 1.6–2.8 absolute point gains over the best fixed-ratio strategy at the same token budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of visual token pruning within LLM decoder layers initiated by FastV and LLaVA-PruMerge, which demonstrated that internal pruning is less costly than pre-LLM compression. The key departure is from fixed, pre-defined pruning ratios (used by FastV, PruMerge+, and SparseVLM) to a fully adaptive strategy where learnable thresholds determine per-instance, per-layer pruning decisions. The Spatial Augmented Pruning strategy builds on PruMerge's uniform spatial sampling but makes the sampling rate dynamic, and the differentiable mask mechanism is borrowed from dynamic spatial sparsification for Vision Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75% average token reduction with only 1.9% performance degradation across seven benchmarks makes LVLMs substantially more deployable on resource-constrained devices, addressing a critical bottleneck for real-world applications. The finding that optimal pruning ratios vary both across layers and across instances (fine-grained vs. coarse-grained tasks) provides a principled justification for adaptive over fixed strategies. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video LVLMs, larger LLM backbones, or architectures without 2D positional encodings, and whether the ATP module's benefits persist under more aggressive compression budgets.
