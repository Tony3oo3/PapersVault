## Problem

Large vision-language models achieve strong VQA performance but are suspected of exploiting spurious textual correlations between questions and likely answers rather than performing genuine video-grounded reasoning, a concern compounded by their black-box decision-making. Prior entailment-tree approaches for video (e.g., TV-trees) assume transcripts are provided, sidestepping the harder problem of grounding hypotheses into actual video content. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing the first video-grounded entailment tree reasoning method for commonsense VQA.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip (uniformly sampled into 24 frames by default) and a multiple-choice question with N answer options. It produces a selected answer along with a full entailment tree where each node carries a confidence score in [0,1], making the reasoning path explicitly inspectable. The video is not fed as a monolithic input to the prover; instead, it is decomposed into frame-wise captions, parsed into structured triplets, and grounded to a specific temporal interval (via anchor-frame retrieval and temporal/causal navigation) before any statement verification occurs.

## Method

The framework operates in four stages: (i) an LLM (LLaMA-3-8B) recursively decomposes each answer candidate into sub-statements forming an entailment tree, where a statement is true iff all its children are true; (ii) video-language entailment verification grounds each leaf statement to a specific video moment using question-aware captioning (conditioned on the anchor fact from the question), structured triplet-based anchor-frame retrieval, and temporal/causal navigation to select the evidence interval; (iii) a VLM prover scores each statement as a binary True/False QA task using normalized next-token logits; (iv) dynamic tree expansion prunes any decomposition whose proof score (product of child scores) does not exceed the parent's direct score, and backtracking computes final scores as max(direct, proof). The entire pipeline is training-free and zero-shot, plugging into any existing video- or image-based VLM as the prover while using an LLM purely for linguistic decomposition and retrieval.

## Results

On the original test sets, integrating entailment tree reasoning improves all five VLMs by 1–4% on average across NExT-QA, IntentQA, and Video-MME, with the largest gains for image-based models (LLaVA-1.5: 37.1→41.5 avg; BLIP-2: 34.0→37.9 avg) and temporal reasoning (improved in 14 of 15 cases). On de-biased answer sets, video-based VLMs suffer 8–10% accuracy drops, but the framework recovers most of the loss (e.g., VideoLLaVA: 51.1→55.4 on NExT-QA). Against state-of-the-art methods using GPT-4 (1.8T parameters) as reasoner, the framework with a 7B VideoLLaVA prover achieves four new SOTA results on de-biased benchmarks while using 257× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against image-based VLMs, the framework consistently wins: LLaVA-1.5 improves from 37.1 to 41.5 average accuracy (+4.4) and BLIP-2 from 34.0 to 37.9 (+3.9), with the largest single-partition gain being LLaVA-1.5 on NExT-QA temporal (37.8→45.6). Against video-based VLMs, gains are smaller but still positive: VideoLLaVA improves from 52.3 to 54.2 avg, VideoChat2 from 55.6 to 56.4, and VideoLLaMA from 51.7 to 53.1. In the head-to-head comparison with SOTA methods on de-biased sets (Table 3), the framework (using 7B VideoLLaVA as prover) beats LLoVi on all eight reported metrics and beats VideoAgent on 6 of 8, losing only on NExT-QA causal (65.9 vs 66.6). Against VideoTree, the framework wins on NExT-QA temporal (60.8 vs 60.2), IntentQA temporal (61.0 vs 56.7), IntentQA causal (62.6 vs 60.1), and VideoMME temporal (55.9 vs 55.7), but trails on NExT-QA causal (65.9 vs 66.4), VideoMME spatial (53.8 vs 54.3), and VideoMME object (50.8 vs 52.6). Notably, all SOTA competitors use GPT-4 (1.8T parameters) as their reasoner, whereas this method uses a 7B model, yielding a 257× parameter efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the entailment tree reasoning paradigm from NLP (Entailer, REFLEX, NELLIE) into the video domain, where prior work (TV-trees) assumed video transcripts were explicitly available and thus sidestepped the core challenge of grounding hypotheses into visual content. It departs from LLM-agent-based VQA methods (VideoAgent, LLoVi) that remain black-box in their reasoning, and from visual grounding approaches that localize evidence without explaining how it supports an answer. The key conceptual contribution is a four-step pipeline—tree construction, video-language entailment verification, tree reasoning, and dynamic expansion—that explicitly aligns each entailment node to a grounded video fragment, making the reasoning chain both interpretable and verifiable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The de-biasing procedure exposes a practical evaluation gap: video VLMs lose 8–10% accuracy when answer distractors are rewritten to remove textual shortcuts, confirming that much reported performance relies on spurious associations rather than video-grounded reasoning. The framework's ability to partially recover these losses (and even exceed original-set performance) suggests structured, evidence-grounded reasoning is a viable path toward more robust VQA. Open questions include scalability to longer videos (experiments use <2 min clips), sensitivity to grounding accuracy (ground-truth fragments still outperform the learned grounding), and whether the approach generalizes beyond multiple-choice to open-ended VQA.
