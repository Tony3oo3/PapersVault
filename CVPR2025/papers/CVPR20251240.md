## Problem

Personalizing diffusion models with only a few reference images causes overfitting that shifts the noise prediction distribution, disrupting the pretrained model's denoising trajectory and degrading text alignment, prior knowledge, and generalization. Existing regularization approaches (prior preservation loss, KL-divergence constraints, attention masks) target specific components but fail to holistically preserve the full diffusion trajectory and the model's ability to incorporate contextual prior knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–5 reference images of a target subject (from DreamBooth or Textual Inversion datasets) along with a text prompt, and produces a personalized image that captures the subject's identity while preserving scene context and prior knowledge. Captions for training are generated by GPT-4o with explicit instructions to describe backgrounds while omitting the target concept, preventing interference with the learned identifier token. The base model is Stable Diffusion XL, fine-tuned via rank-32 LoRA on both the U-Net and text encoder with batch size 1.

## Method

APT introduces three complementary regularization mechanisms on top of standard DreamBooth fine-tuning. First, an Adaptive Training Adjustment computes a per-bin overfitting indicator γ_t from the EMA difference in denoising loss between the pretrained and fine-tuned models, then uses it to drive both adaptive affine data augmentation (probability clamped to γ_t) and adaptive loss scaling (multiplying the denoising loss by 1−γ_t), targeting the time steps where overfitting is most severe. Second, Representation Stabilization constrains the mean and variance of intermediate U-Net feature maps by matching them between the fine-tuned model (conditioned on the identifier token) and the pretrained model (conditioned on the class token), preserving the statistical structure of internal representations. Third, Attention Alignment regularizes cross-attention maps by aligning the head-averaged attention of the fine-tuned model with that of the pretrained model across all text tokens, ensuring the model retains its learned semantic associations. The total loss combines the adaptively weighted denoising loss with weighted representation and attention regularization terms (λ_dist=30, λ_attn=3×10⁻⁴).

## Results

On the DreamBooth and Textual Inversion datasets, APT achieves the best FID (41.97) among all personalized methods, substantially outperforming DreamBooth (53.13), DCO (52.30), and Custom Diffusion (45.53). APT also attains the highest Recall (0.738) and the best HPSv2 text-alignment score (0.288) among personalized methods, approaching the SDXL prior (0.295). In the user study, 56.1% of participants preferred APT-generated images over DreamBooth (22.8%) and DCO (21.1%). APT's DINOv2 image similarity (0.660) is slightly lower than DCO (0.687) and DreamBooth (0.681), which the authors attribute to APT generating broader scene context rather than tightly cropped, object-centric images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APT clearly outperforms all three baselines on FID (41.97 vs. 45.53 for Custom Diffusion, 52.30 for DCO, and 53.13 for DreamBooth), indicating substantially better preservation of the pretrained model's generation quality. On the user study, APT is preferred by 56.1% of participants, roughly 2.5× the preference for DreamBooth (22.8%) and DCO (21.1%). APT also leads on Recall (0.738 vs. 0.649, 0.660, 0.608) and HPSv2 (0.288 vs. 0.273, 0.277, 0.272), reflecting better diversity and text alignment. However, APT trails DCO (0.687) and DreamBooth (0.681) on DINOv2 image similarity (0.660), which the authors explain as a consequence of generating wider scene context rather than tightly cropped objects. CLIP-T scores are nearly tied across all methods (0.661–0.666), with APT (0.664) slightly below the SDXL prior (0.666) but above all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

APT builds directly on the DreamBooth + Textual Inversion personalization paradigm (fine-tuning with an identifier token) and departs from prior regularization methods like DCO and Attndreambooth, which target specific components (KL divergence or cross-attention alone). Its key conceptual departure is a time-step-aware, adaptive overfitting indicator that dynamically modulates both data augmentation probability and loss weighting per diffusion step bin, drawing inspiration from StyleGAN ADA's adaptive augmentation but transplanting it into the diffusion fine-tuning setting. The multi-level regularization (intermediate feature statistics plus cross-attention alignment) extends the scope beyond what prior attention-only or trajectory-only methods address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APT offers a practical, modular toolkit for practitioners personalizing diffusion models with few reference images, addressing the persistent overfitting–prior-knowledge trade-off that plagues DreamBooth-style fine-tuning. The adaptive overfitting indicator is a reusable diagnostic that could inform training schedules in other few-shot fine-tuning contexts. However, the method introduces hyperparameter sensitivity (λ_attn, p_max) and the trade-off between prior preservation and concept fidelity remains unresolved; the paper acknowledges that strong class-word priors can override desired concept attributes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-concept personalization, different backbone architectures beyond SDXL, and whether the per-bin overfitting indicator can be replaced by a learned or parameter-free signal.
