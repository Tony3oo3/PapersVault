## Problem

Extending frontal virtual try-on to multi-view (any-view) try-on is non-trivial: explicit warping-based methods introduce artifacts under occlusion and non-flat garment inputs, while implicit encoding-based methods lose spatial clothing detail. The core challenge is adaptively fusing front- and back-view clothing features so that irrelevant view information does not interfere with the target view, a problem that prior work (MV-VTON) addresses only with a crude hard/soft pose-similarity selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251485/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two clothed-person images (front-view and back-view garment) plus a target person image in an arbitrary view (front, back, or side) at 512×384 resolution. Unlike flat in-shop garments, the input clothing is already distorted by the source person's pose. Auxiliary signals extracted at runtime include OpenPose skeletons, a pixel-wise spatial color label map (encoding front/back/side orientation via Neck-Shoulder geometry), and a human-parsing clothing mask; the output is a single try-on image of the target person wearing the garment in the target view.

## Method

The key insight is that clothing on a person is spatially aligned with the pose skeleton, so a learned pose-alignment transformation can be directly transferred to clothing features. Concretely, a Swin Transformer extracts 4-scale pose features from front and back views; two shared linear projectors map each into the target-view space, and their sum is supervised by a novel multi-scale L1 pose-alignment loss against the target pose features. The same projectors are then applied to 4-scale clothing features (from a second Swin Transformer on parsed clothing masks) to produce fused multi-scale clothing representations. A pixel-wise spatial color label (front vs. back vs. side) is encoded and aligned through the same projectors with an additional label loss to reinforce view disambiguation. Finally, a coarse-to-fine latent diffusion model (adapted from CFLD) consumes the fused clothing features via a perception-refined decoder and hybrid-granularity attention, conditioned on the target pose, spatial label, and clothing-agnostic image, and is trained end-to-end with a combined diffusion + pose + label loss (λ=μ=0.01).

## Results

On DeepFashion, Robust-MVTON achieves FID 11.40, LPIPS 0.158, SSIM 0.820, and a 75.2% user preference rate, outperforming all baselines by a large margin. On MPV, it reaches FID 12.51, LPIPS 0.174, SSIM 0.818, and 83.3% user preference. The closest baseline (CFLD*) trails by approximately 3 FID points on both datasets, while the only prior multi-view method (MV-VTON) is outperformed by over 8 FID points. In the user study, Robust-MVTON is preferred over all baselines by a factor of 5–13×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Robust-MVTON outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the frontal-only methods CAT-DM (FID 16.47/17.81) and StableVITON (FID 15.70/16.70), it gains roughly 4–5 FID points, confirming that single-view inputs are insufficient for multi-view try-on. The strongest baseline, CFLD* (which spatially concatenates front and back views into a diffusion model), achieves FID 14.31/14.36 but still trails Robust-MVTON by ~3 FID points and is heavily outperformed in the user study (12.6%/6.5% vs. 75.2%/83.3%). The only prior multi-view method, MV-VTON, performs worst overall (FID 21.13/19.20), largely due to its reliance on explicit warping of pose-distorted garments and a crude hard/soft view-selection strategy that fails to adaptively suppress irrelevant view features. The ablation rows in Table 1 further show that removing the spatial label or pose loss degrades FID to 11.42 and 12.45 respectively, validating both components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coarse-to-fine latent diffusion framework of CFLD [34] from pose-guided person synthesis to the multi-view virtual try-on setting, replacing its single-reference conditioning with a learned cross-pose feature alignment module. It departs from the only prior MVTON method, MV-VTON [53], by abandoning explicit warping and hard/soft view selection in favor of implicit alignment via shared linear projectors supervised by a pose-matching loss. The approach also builds on the broader diffusion-based VTON lineage (StableVITON, CAT-DM) and the Swin Transformer encoder, but introduces the key conceptual shift of using pose skeleton alignment as a proxy supervisory signal for clothing feature fusion across views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-view try-on is a practically important extension for e-commerce and virtual fashion, yet the paper highlights a critical data bottleneck: no large-scale 2D dataset covers more than front/back pairs, limiting generalization to arbitrary viewpoints. The finding that the sole prior MVTON method (MV-VTON) performs worse than single-view baselines underscores how poorly warping-based preprocessing transfers to non-flat, pose-distorted clothing inputs. Open questions include whether the cross-pose alignment strategy scales beyond two input views, whether the linear projector fusion can be replaced by richer attention mechanisms, and whether the approach generalizes to 3D-consistent multi-view generation.
