## Problem

Remote sensing image object detection (RSIOD) is severely limited by the scarcity of labeled training data, and existing data augmentation techniques (rotation, copy-paste, semi-supervised learning) cannot fundamentally address this insufficiency, especially for rare object classes. While layout-controllable diffusion models have advanced in the natural image domain, no dedicated generative solution existed for RSIOD that supports both horizontal and rotated bounding box conditions simultaneously—a critical requirement given that many remote sensing targets (ships, vehicles, aircraft) are naturally oriented at arbitrary angles.

## Input / Output

AeroGen takes as input a set of bounding box layouts—each uniformly represented as eight coordinates [x₁,y₁,…,x₄,y₄] to handle both axis-aligned and rotated boxes—along with category labels encoded as fixed CLIP text embeddings. The model outputs a synthetic remote sensing image whose object placements and categories match the specified layout. The full pipeline additionally produces layout labels via a DDPM-based conditional sampler (operating on H×W×N binary matrices) and applies dual-criteria filtering (CLIP semantic score + ResNet101 classification accuracy) to retain only high-quality, layout-consistent images for downstream training.

## Method

AeroGen is a layout-conditional latent diffusion model initialized from CRS-Diff (a remote-sensing fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 1.5) that introduces three key mechanisms: (1) a Layout Embedding module that Fourier-encodes bounding box coordinates and fuses them with CLIP category codes via a linear projection to produce layout control tokens; (2) a Layout Mask Attention (LMA) layer that, at each denoising step, applies per-object 0/1 spatial masks to steer local noise characteristics, enabling precise placement especially for small targets; and (3) a Dual Cross Attention (DCA) module that combines global text-condition cross-attention with local layout-token cross-attention, weighted by a learnable balance factor λ. Only the UNet attention layers and LMA are trained (AdamW, lr=1e-5, 100 epochs) while all other weights remain frozen. The end-to-end augmentation pipeline further employs a DDPM to learn and sample the conditional layout distribution, a normal-distribution-based label filter to enforce realistic box attributes, and a dual-criteria image filter to ensure semantic and layout consistency of the final synthetic dataset.

## Results

On the DIOR dataset (horizontal bounding boxes), AeroGen achieves a FID of 38.57, CAS of 76.84, and YOLO Score of 29.8/54.2/31.6, outperforming all baselines. On rotated bounding box datasets, the advantage is even more pronounced: on DIOR-R, AeroGen reaches FID 35.07 and CAS 74.13 (vs. 48.43 and 58.89 for modified GLIGEN), and on HRSC, FID 45.86 and CAS 51.19 (vs. 66.69 and 43.35). For downstream data augmentation, adding 50k synthetic images improves mAP by 3.7% on DIOR (54.22→57.92), 4.3% on DIOR-R (37.39→41.69), and 2.43% on HRSC (63.49→65.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AeroGen outperforms all five baselines (LostGAN, LayoutDiffusion, ReCo, GLIGEN, and modified GLIGEN†) across every metric and dataset. On DIOR (HBB), AeroGen improves FID by 2.74 over the closest competitor GLIGEN (38.57 vs. 41.31) and CAS by 13.34 points (76.84 vs. 63.50), with the largest gap over LostGAN (FID 57.10). The advantage is most dramatic on rotated bounding box tasks: on DIOR-R, AeroGen reduces FID by 13.36 points over modified GLIGEN (35.07 vs. 48.43), and on HRSC the FID gap widens to 20.83 points (45.86 vs. 66.69), confirming that the unified 8-coordinate layout encoding and Layout Mask Attention are particularly effective for oriented objects. In the data augmentation comparison (Table 4), AeroGen-augmented YOLOv8 achieves 74.79 mAP50 on DIOR and 71.38 on DOTA, surpassing GLIGEN (73.39/70.22), LayoutDiffusion (73.07/68.91), ReCo (72.89/68.56), and LostGAN (69.49/60.52). Against traditional augmentation (Copy-Paste + Flip), AeroGen + Flip yields 41.69 mAP vs. 38.75 on DIOR-R, a 2.94-point gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

AeroGen adapts the layout-to-image generation paradigm established in the natural image domain (GLIGEN's gated self-attention, LayoutDiffusion's adaptive layer normalization, ReCo's cross-attention modulation) to the remote sensing setting, building on the latent diffusion framework (LDM) and the remote-sensing-specific fine-tuning from CRS-Diff. Its key architectural departure is the unified eight-coordinate representation that handles both horizontal and rotated bounding boxes through a shared Fourier-encoding pathway, combined with a Layout Mask Attention module that injects spatial constraints at the noise level during denoising. The end-to-end data augmentation pipeline draws on SatSynth's DDPM-based condition sampling and X-Paste/DriverGen's quality-filtering philosophy, but eliminates the manual instance-pasting step by generating complete scenes directly from layout conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in remote sensing object detection—labeled data scarcity—by providing a drop-in synthetic data generator that supports the rotated bounding box annotations common in aerial/satellite imagery (ships, vehicles), which prior layout-to-image methods did not handle. The moderate overall mAP gains (2.4–4.3%) are more meaningful in the context of rare classes (12–18% improvements), suggesting the approach is most valuable for long-tail detection scenarios. Open questions include scalability to larger category counts and higher-resolution imagery, the computational cost of generating tens of thousands of training images, and whether the filtering thresholds generalize across different sensor modalities (SAR, multispectral) without re-tuning.
