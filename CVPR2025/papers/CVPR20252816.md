## Problem

Existing rendering-based pre-training methods for autonomous driving (e.g., UniPAD, ViDAR) rely on explicit LiDAR depth supervision to learn 3D geometry, limiting their applicability to camera-only systems. This paper asks whether purely image-based self-supervision can achieve comparable or superior pre-training quality for vision-centric perception tasks without any depth annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252816/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-frame, multi-view surround images (6 cameras at 1600×900) from historical frames, which are lifted into a 180×180×5 voxel volume (256 channels) via a shared ConvNeXt-S backbone and view transformation. The 3D-GS decoder renders multi-view images and depth maps from these voxel features, while an auxiliary velocity head outputs per-voxel velocity vectors in world coordinates. The final pre-trained voxel features are transferred to downstream detectors (UVTR, BEVDet) for 3D object detection, occupancy prediction, and map segmentation.

## Method

VisionPAD replaces the NeRF-style volume rendering used by UniPAD with an anchor-based 3D Gaussian Splatting decoder, where each voxel center serves as an anchor from which MLPs regress Gaussian offsets, SH coefficients, opacity, scale, and rotation; low-confidence Gaussians (negative tanh opacity) are filtered to reduce cost. For motion learning, a self-supervised voxel velocity estimation head predicts per-voxel velocities, which are scaled by inter-frame timestamps to warp current-frame voxels into adjacent frames; the 3D-GS decoder then renders those warped voxels and is supervised by the adjacent frame's images, with only velocity-head parameters updated during backpropagation. A photometric consistency loss further enforces 3D geometry by re-projecting adjacent frames into the current view using rendered depth maps and known relative poses, combining SSIM and L1 terms. The total pre-training objective weights image reconstruction (0.5), velocity-guided reconstruction (1.0), and photometric consistency (1.0), requiring no depth or LiDAR supervision at any stage.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set for 3D object detection, VisionPAD (camera-only pre-training) achieves 46.7 NDS and 41.0 mAP with UVTR as backbone, surpassing the UVTR baseline by +1.7 NDS and +3.8 mAP, and outperforming UniPAD (same camera-only supervision) by +1.9 NDS and +2.5 mAP. For semantic occupancy prediction on Occ3D, VisionPAD lifts mIoU from 30.1% to 35.4% (+5.4), while UniPAD yields only +0.9. On map segmentation, VisionPAD improves lane IoU from 15.0% to 20.4% (+5.4), compared to UniPAD's +1.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is against UniPAD [46], the prior rendering-based pre-training paradigm. Under identical camera-only supervision, VisionPAD outperforms UniPAD by +1.9 NDS and +2.5 mAP on 3D detection, +4.5 mIoU on occupancy, and +4.1 IoU on map segmentation; UniPAD actually degrades NDS slightly (45.0→44.8) without LiDAR depth. Against non-pre-trained SOTA detectors (BEVFormerV2 at 46.7 NDS, CMT-C at 46.0 NDS), VisionPAD with camera-only pre-training matches or exceeds them (46.7 NDS, 41.0 mAP) without any multi-frame input. With camera sweeps and LiDAR depth added, VisionPAD reaches 50.4 NDS / 43.1 mAP, surpassing UniPAD's 50.2 / 42.8. On occupancy, VisionPAD (35.4 mIoU) surpasses TPVFormer (34.2) and approaches OPUS (36.2) despite using a lighter backbone and no temporal frames. The ablation confirms that the 3D-GS decoder alone (Model A) is neutral, Gaussian filtering adds +0.6 NDS, velocity estimation adds +1.2 mAP, and photometric consistency contributes the largest single gain of +2.4 NDS / +4.4 mAP.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisionPAD departs from the rendering-based pre-training line initiated by UniPAD and ViDAR, which rely on differentiable volume rendering (NeRF-style) with explicit LiDAR-derived depth supervision to learn 3D geometry. The key architectural shift is replacing volume rendering with an anchor-based 3D Gaussian Splatting decoder (building on Kerbl et al. and the PixelSplat anchor formulation), which enables higher-resolution image reconstruction at lower computational cost. For geometric and motion supervision, the paper repurposes photometric consistency from self-supervised monocular depth estimation (Godard et al.) and introduces a novel self-supervised voxel velocity estimation mechanism, eliminating the need for any explicit depth or flow labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on LiDAR depth supervision, VisionPAD makes rendering-based pre-training applicable to camera-only autonomous driving systems, which is a significant practical constraint in deployment. The ablation showing that photometric consistency alone contributes +4.4 mAP suggests that cross-frame geometric consistency is a more effective geometric signal than explicit depth regression for this setting. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (nuScenes) and a single backbone (ConvNeXt-S), and the method still requires known relative poses for the photometric consistency term, leaving open questions about robustness to pose noise and generalization to larger-scale or more diverse driving datasets.
