## Problem

Learned image compression has achieved strong coding gains primarily through increasingly complex backbones (Swin-Transformers, Mamba), but each percent of improvement has come with substantial computational overhead. Linear attention mechanisms that capture long-range dependencies at linear complexity have not been explored for this task, leaving a gap between high compression efficiency and practical decoding speed, especially for high-resolution images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252320/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and produces a compressed bitstream via arithmetic coding of quantized latent and hyper-latent representations, along with a reconstructed image at the decoder. Training uses 512×512 crops from OpenImages (400K images), while evaluation spans Kodak (768×512), Tecnick (1200×1200), and CLIC Professional Validation (up to 2K resolution). The latent representation has 320 channels and the hyper-latent 192 channels, with the analysis/synthesis transforms using 96/144/256 channel configurations across four resolution stages.

## Method

LALIC replaces the standard CNN or Transformer transform backbone with stacked Bi-RWKV blocks, each containing a Spatial-Mix branch (BiWKV attention over a flattened 2D sequence with Omni-Shift for local context) and a Channel-Mix branch (squared-ReLU MLP with sigmoid-gated receptance). The BiWKV attention introduces channel-wise decay parameters w and u that balance local and global token interactions at linear complexity, yielding a global effective receptive field without window partitioning. For entropy modeling, the proposed RWKV-SCCTX extends the ELIC-style spatial-channel context by using Bi-RWKV blocks to model channel-wise dependencies across unevenly grouped chunks ({16,16,32,64,M−128}), combined with checkerboard-masked spatial context and hyperprior, predicting per-location Gaussian parameters for causal arithmetic coding. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a Lagrangian rate-distortion objective using MSE or MS-SSIM distortion.

## Results

LALIC achieves BD-rate (PSNR) improvements of -15.26%, -15.41%, and -17.63% over VTM-9.1 on Kodak, CLIC, and Tecnick datasets respectively, setting new state-of-the-art among learned image compression methods. The model uses 63.24M parameters and 286.16 GFLOPs, with a decoding time of 150 ms and encoding time of 274 ms on an RTX 4090 GPU. Notably, LALIC outperforms the closest competitor MLIC++ (83.27M params, 443.17 GFLOPs) on all three datasets while using fewer parameters and significantly lower FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LALIC outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset where results are available. Against the strongest learned compression baseline MLIC++ (83.27M params, 443.17 GFLOPs), LALIC gains 0.24%, 0.96%, and 0.42% BD-rate on Kodak, CLIC, and Tecnick respectively, while using 24% fewer parameters and 35% fewer FLOPs. Compared to FAT (69.78M params), LALIC improves by 0.70%, 4.62%, and 3.23% on the three datasets, and critically reduces decoding time from >10 s to 0.15 s. Against TCM-large (75.90M params, 700.66 GFLOPs), LALIC achieves 3.53%, 6.00%, and 6.70% BD-rate gains with less than half the FLOPs. MambaVC (47.88M params) is outperformed by 5.53% on Kodak, though it was not evaluated on CLIC or Tecnick. The older CNN-based baselines (Minnen20, Cheng20-Parallel, ELIC) are substantially outperformed, with ELIC showing the largest gap at 8.24% on Kodak.

## Relation to Prior Work

LALIC adapts the RWKV linear-attention family (Peng et al., 2023; Duan et al., 2024) to the learned image compression setting, where prior work has been dominated by Swin-Transformer-based transforms (TCM, FAT) and, more recently, Mamba/SSM-based transforms (MambaVC). The spatial-channel context entropy model is structurally inherited from ELIC, with the key departure being the replacement of convolutional channel-context networks with Bi-RWKV blocks. The Omni-Shift module, borrowed from the medical-imaging restoration literature, serves as the 2D local-context mechanism that makes the 1D sequence-based RWKV attention applicable to image latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a linear-complexity, RNN-style attention mechanism can match or slightly exceed transformer-based LIC methods in rate-distortion performance, validating a new architectural direction that avoids the quadratic cost of self-attention and the window-partitioning heuristics of Swin-Transformer. The global effective receptive field (visualized in Fig. 3) suggests particular promise for high-resolution and video compression, where long-range dependency capture is most critical. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video compression, whether other linear-attention variants (Mamba, GLA) yield similar gains, and whether the higher encoding latency (274 ms vs. 140–190 ms for FAT/MLIC++) can be reduced without sacrificing R-D performance.
