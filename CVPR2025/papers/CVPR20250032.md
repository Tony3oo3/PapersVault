## Problem

Estimating a head-mounted device wearer's full body motion in the world frame is difficult because most body parts are never directly visible in egocentric frames, and prior egocentric motion estimation works address only body pose while ignoring height variation and hand motion. A critical but underexplored aspect is how head pose conditioning is parameterized: existing approaches lack desirable spatial and temporal invariance properties that reduce representational redundancy and improve generalization.

## Input / Output

The system takes metric SLAM central pupil frame (CPF) poses and egocentric RGB video from devices like Project Aria, assuming a flat floor at z=0. The diffusion model outputs local SMPL-H parameters (joint rotations, shape, contact indicators) that are then globally aligned to the allocentric frame via the known CPF-to-root transform. At test time, HaMeR hand detections from egocentric frames are incorporated as guidance constraints, and optionally 3D wrist poses from Project Aria's grayscale cameras further refine hand estimates.

## Method

EgoAllo uses a transformer-based denoising diffusion model conditioned on an invariant head motion representation that achieves both spatial invariance (invariant to arbitrary SE(3) transformations along the floor plane) and temporal invariance (shift-equivariant). The conditioning encodes per-timestep relative CPF motion in the local frame plus a transform to a per-timestep canonical frame grounded on the floor, replacing prior sequence-level canonicalization that introduces temporal dependencies. At test time, DDIM sampling is combined with a Levenberg-Marquardt guidance optimizer that minimizes hand reprojection/3D pose losses, foot skating costs, and a prior cost against the denoiser output. For sequences longer than 128 timesteps, MultiDiffusion fuses overlapping window predictions after each denoising step.

## Results

On AMASS (seq 32), EgoAllo achieves 129.8 mm MPJPE and 109.8 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming EgoEgo (184.0 / 158.6 mm) by ~29% and VAE+Opt (199.5 / 191.4 mm) by ~35%. On the more challenging RICH dataset, EgoAllo reaches 193.7 mm MPJPE versus 215.4 mm (EgoEgo) and 352.0 mm (VAE+Opt). For hand estimation on EgoExo4D, EgoAllo-Mono reduces world-frame hand MPJPE from 237.90 mm (HaMeR) to 131.45 mm (−45%), and EgoAllo-Wrist3D further drops it to 60.08 mm (−75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoAllo consistently outperforms EgoEgo [48] across all three body-estimation datasets: 29% lower MPJPE on AMASS (129.8 vs 184.0 mm), 10% on RICH (193.7 vs 215.4 mm), and 18% on ADT (173.5 vs 212.5 mm), with the gap driven primarily by shape/height estimation and the invariant conditioning parameterization. Against VAE+Opt [74,102], EgoAllo's advantage is even more pronounced—VAE+Opt degrades severely on out-of-distribution data (352.0 mm on RICH vs EgoAllo's 193.7 mm) and produces overly smoothed, unexpressive motions. For hand estimation, EgoAllo-Mono substantially beats HaMeR [66] in world-frame accuracy (131.45 vs 237.90 mm MPJPE, a 45% reduction), though HaMeR retains a slight edge in Procrustes-aligned local pose (13.04 vs 14.71 mm PA-MPJPE), confirming that the body prior resolves monocular scale and drift ambiguities. The NoShape ablation (138.1 mm on AMASS) shows that body shape/height estimation contributes a 6–7% MPJPE improvement and is critical for grounding (GND 0.98 vs 0.94) and head alignment (T_head 6.4 vs 44.7 mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoAllo builds most directly on EgoEgo [48], which first demonstrated offline body pose estimation from egocentric head poses using a diffusion model, and on BoDiffusion [7] / AvatarPoser [28], which parameterize head and controller poses for VR-based motion estimation. The key departure is a principled invariant conditioning parameterization (spatial and temporal invariance) that replaces the sequence canonicalization used by EgoEgo and the absolute-plus-delta schemes used by BoDiffusion/AvatarPoser. The system also extends the egocentric estimation task beyond body pose to include body shape/height and hand parameters, leveraging HaMeR [66] for visual hand observations and a Levenberg-Marquardt guidance framework to couple hand and body estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The invariance-based conditioning provides a clear, reusable design principle for egocentric motion priors, with the systematic ablation showing that representation choice alone accounts for up to 18% error reduction—information that is easy to transfer to related egocentric estimation problems. The demonstration that body-level kinematic and temporal constraints can reduce world-frame hand errors by over 40% suggests a general recipe for improving sparse-sensor hand tracking in AR/robotics pipelines. Open questions include extending beyond flat floors, eliminating the test-time optimization overhead of guidance, and handling monocular hand detection failures (left/right flips, spurious detections) that currently limit robustness.
