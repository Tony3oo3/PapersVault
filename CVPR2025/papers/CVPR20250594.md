## Problem

Existing self-supervised 6D pose estimation methods translate synthetic images into the real domain to reduce the domain gap, but a noticeable residual gap remains between translated and real images, and the translated images cannot capture the occlusions prevalent in real scenes. This paper reverses the translation direction, proposing to translate unannotated real images into the synthetic domain instead, thereby simultaneously narrowing the domain gap and enabling occlusion-aware training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250594/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes a single monocular RGB image, crops the object region using a YOLOv4 detector, and outputs a 6D pose as a 3×4 transformation matrix (rotation + translation). At training time, it additionally requires a set of multi-view unannotated real images (with overlapping local regions for SfM-based camera pose recovery via COLMAP) and a corresponding CAD model. The training images need not be annotated with object poses, which is the key self-supervised requirement.

## Method

The core contribution is a CAD-like Radiance Field (CARF), a two-MLP NeRF-style architecture (geometry block + radiance block) trained to render synthetic images from real-image camera poses while preserving CAD-model textures. The geometry block is pre-trained on real images with predicted visible/amodal masks to capture accurate object geometry, then the radiance block is fine-tuned with a transient radiance branch and patch-based adversarial loss to match CAD textures. A global object pose refiner then jointly optimizes initial poses (from a GDR-Net backbone) across all training views using a multi-view geometric consistency constraint derived from SfM camera poses, producing pseudo pose labels. Finally, the backbone is self-supervised by minimizing a combined loss of point matching (pose alignment), RGB similarity (CAD-rendered vs. CARF-rendered images via a differentiable renderer), and reweighted cross-entropy mask similarity, all computed in the synthetic domain to avoid occlusion interference.

## Results

On LineMOD, ONDA-Pose achieves a mean ADD/ADD-S score of 93.2%, approaching the best fully-supervised method DPODv2 (93.5%) while outperforming all self-supervised baselines, including TexPose (91.7%) and SMOC-Net (91.3%). On Occluded-LineMOD, where severe occlusions are present, ONDA-Pose achieves 69.3% mean, surpassing the next-best self-supervised method TexPose (66.7%) by 2.6 points and the synthetic-only GDR-Net (52.9%) by 16.4 points. On HomebrewedDB, ONDA-Pose demonstrates strong generalization with 95.8% mean, outperforming TexPose (89.1%) and Self6D++ (80.6%) by 6.7 and 15.2 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ONDA-Pose (93.2% mean on LineMOD) outperforms all self-supervised baselines: TexPose (91.7%), SMOC-Net (91.3%), Self6D++ with depth (88.5%), Self6D++ without depth (85.6%), and Self6D (58.9%), while remaining within 0.3 points of the best fully-supervised method DPODv2 (93.5%). It also surpasses the other fully-supervised methods PVNet (86.3%), CDPN (89.9%), and GDR-Net (91.0%), demonstrating that self-supervision with the proposed CAD-like radiance field can match or exceed fully-supervised approaches. On Occluded-LineMOD, ONDA-Pose (69.3%) leads TexPose (66.7%) by 2.6 points and SMOC-Net (63.3%) by 6.0 points, with a particularly large margin over synthetic-only methods like GDR-Net (52.9%) and CosyPose (46.7%), confirming its occlusion-handling advantage. On HomebrewedDB, ONDA-Pose (95.8%) generalizes substantially better than TexPose (89.1%) and Self6D++ (80.6%), suggesting the real-to-synthetic translation strategy transfers more robustly to unseen objects. The ablation further shows that removing the point matching loss collapses performance to 21.3% mean, highlighting its critical role in the self-supervision pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the domain-translation direction used by prior self-supervised pose estimation methods: whereas Bousmalis et al. and TexPose translate synthetic images into the real domain, ONDA-Pose translates un-annotated real images into the synthetic domain via a CAD-like radiance field (CARF) built on NeRF's geometry/radiance decomposition and GRAF's patch-based adversarial training. The global object pose refiner departs from per-image refinement strategies (e.g., in Self6D++ and SMOC-Net) by jointly optimizing all initial poses under a shared world-coordinate constraint derived from SfM camera poses. The backbone estimator (GDR-Net) and the SfM pipeline (COLMAP) are adopted directly from prior work, positioning ONDA-Pose as a re-orchestration of existing components around the real-to-synthetic translation insight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing most of the gap between self-supervised and fully-supervised 6D pose estimation (93.2% vs. 93.5% mean ADD on LineMOD) without any pose annotations, the method offers a practical path to pose estimation in settings where labeling is infeasible. The occlusion-handling improvement on Occluded-LineMOD (69.3% vs. 66.7% for TexPose) is meaningful but modest, and the requirement for multi-view training images with overlapping regions (for SfM) limits applicability to single-view or sparse-capture scenarios. Open questions include whether the CARF approach generalizes to multi-object or deformable-object settings, and whether the multi-view constraint can be relaxed with learned camera pose estimation.
