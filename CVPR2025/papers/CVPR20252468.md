## Problem

Polar-based instance segmentation methods formulate the task as starting point classification and dense distance regression, offering significant efficiency gains over pixel-wise or contour-deformation approaches. However, they suffer a substantial performance gap relative to mainstream methods because the Representation Error—the deviation between the assembled polygon and the true contour due to limited polygon capacity—has been entirely neglected in training. Existing sample decisions rely solely on a fixed center prior that cannot capture the positional variation of this error, and the only available polygonal metric (Polar IoU) is blind to it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (shortest edge 800 for COCO, up to 1024×2048 for Cityscapes) and, as a one-stage detector, requires no pre-predicted bounding boxes or RoIs. For each detected instance it outputs a class label, a classified starting point, and a dense set of m regressed radial distances that together define a bounding polygon (vertex set) approximating the instance contour. The polygon is assembled by emitting m uniformly spaced rays from the starting point, making the output a compact geometric representation rather than a pixel-wise mask.

## Method

PolarNeXt builds on the PolarMask one-stage architecture (ResNet-50 + FPN) and introduces two tailored components. The Union-aligned Rasterization Module (URM) aligns the predicted polygon and ground-truth contour within their minimal Union Box, converts both to 64×64 rasterized masks via a differentiable rasterizer, and computes RMask IoU—a metric that directly measures polygon-contour overlap and thus incorporates representation error. The Adaptive Polygonal Sample Decision (APSD) strategy operates in three stages: Multi-layer Center Sampling gathers candidates across all FPN layers; a matching stage assigns positive labels to the k candidates with the lowest combined cost (Focal Loss + Polar IoU Loss + RMask IoU Loss); and a weighting stage replaces the heuristic Centerness score with RMask IoU to modulate each positive sample's contribution to the loss. The overall loss additionally retains Polar IoU Loss as an internal constraint on dense distance regression, complementing RMask IoU Loss as an external polygonal constraint. This dynamic, error-aware sample decision enables the network to select the optimal starting point per instance at inference, yielding a 4.8% AP gain over PolarMask while using fewer parameters (32.4M) and FLOPs (186G) than competing mask- and contour-based methods.

## Results

On COCO test-dev, PolarNeXt achieves 36.1% mask AP with 32.36M parameters and 186G FLOPs, surpassing PolarMask (31.3% AP) by 4.8% AP while using fewer parameters (32.36M vs. 34.74M) and FLOPs (186G vs. 264G). It runs at 49 FPS, faster than Mask R-CNN (33 FPS), SOLOv2 (35 FPS), and DANCE (16 FPS), while achieving comparable accuracy to these heavier methods. On Cityscapes val, PolarNeXt reaches 30.7% AP at 31 FPS with only 566 MB peak memory, compared to Mask R-CNN's 31.5% AP at 16 FPS with 1968 MB memory, demonstrating a 3.5× memory advantage at high resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolarNeXt outperforms its direct predecessor PolarMask by 4.8% AP on COCO (36.1 vs. 31.3) and 3.3% AP on Cityscapes (30.7 vs. 27.4), while also being faster (49 vs. 42 FPS) and lighter (32.36M vs. 34.74M params). Against mask-based methods, PolarNeXt trails Mask R-CNN by 1.4% AP and SOLOv2 by 2.3% AP on COCO, but runs 49% faster than Mask R-CNN (49 vs. 33 FPS) and 40% faster than SOLOv2 (49 vs. 35 FPS) with substantially fewer FLOPs (186G vs. 240G and 227G). It beats SparseInst by 2.9% AP (36.1 vs. 33.2) using only 36 epochs versus 146, and outperforms DANCE in speed (49 vs. 16 FPS) with only a 0.7% AP gap. On Cityscapes, PolarNeXt matches Mask R-CNN in AP50 (58.8 vs. 58.6) while using 3.5× less peak memory (566 MB vs. 1968 MB) and running nearly 2× faster (31 vs. 16 FPS). Among contour-based methods, PolarNeXt exceeds E2EC by 2.3% AP and PolySnake by 1.7% AP on COCO, while being significantly faster than DANCE (49 vs. 16 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

PolarNeXt is a direct extension of PolarMask (Xie et al., CVPR 2020), reusing its network architecture, polar representation formulation, and Polar IoU metric, but fundamentally rethinking the training pipeline. The key departure is the identification of 'Representation Error'—the inherent deviation between a polygon and its target contour—as a previously ignored source of performance degradation, and the demonstration that the center prior used in PolarMask for label assignment and sample weighting is poorly correlated with representation quality. The Union-aligned Rasterization Module borrows the differentiable rasterizer from Lazarow et al. (CVPR 2022) to enable mask-level polygon assessment, while the Adaptive Polygonal Sample Decision strategy replaces the fixed center-based heuristics with a cost-driven matching and weighting scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PolarNeXt is the first polar-based instance segmentation method to close the gap with mainstream mask-based and contour-based approaches (36.1 AP vs. 38.7 AP for HTC) while using roughly half the parameters, FLOPs, and inference memory, making it a practical option for high-resolution imagery and resource-constrained deployment. The conceptual contribution—showing that Polar IoU is essentially blind to representation errors and that the center prior misallocates training attention—reframes how one should think about polygon-based segmentation training. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to multi-component or highly concave instances beyond the Cityscapes multi-component strategy, and whether the 64×64 rasterization resolution is sufficient for very fine-grained boundaries at extreme resolutions.
