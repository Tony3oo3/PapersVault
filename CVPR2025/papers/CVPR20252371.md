## Problem

Contrastive representation learning directly optimizes for semantic object representations, bypassing the multi-stage visual processing hierarchy (boundaries → surfaces → semantics) that human vision follows. This leads to texture bias, slower convergence, and a well-documented trade-off between image-level tasks (classification) and pixel-level tasks (segmentation, depth estimation). The paper asks whether perceptual constructs from early vision—shape prototypes and intrinsic image decomposition—can inject useful inductive bias into contrastive learning to resolve these issues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252371/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder (ResNet-18) consumes RGB images alongside two types of offline-preprocessed auxiliary inputs: figure-ground shape silhouettes generated by TRACER and intrinsic images (reflectance and shading) decomposed via Retinex. Unlike the original images, which receive standard data augmentations (random crop, color jitter, etc.), the silhouettes and intrinsic images undergo only resizing and normalization. The network outputs a 256-D representation vector, which is then evaluated via linear probing or fine-tuning on downstream classification, segmentation, and depth estimation tasks.

## Method

The paper introduces three perceptual inductive biases integrated into contrastive learning. First, Shape Prototypical Contrastive Learning (S-PCL) performs K-Means clustering on shape-silhouette embeddings to form shape prototypes, then maximizes mutual information between each image's representation and its assigned prototype via a ShapeProtoNCE objective (averaged over multiple K values). Second, intrinsic images (reflectance or shading from Retinex) are treated as additional augmented views in the standard InfoNCE loss. Third, the combined MidVCL approach sums all three losses. Crucially, the paper proposes a sequential coarse-to-fine training schedule: 100 epochs of perceptual-biased pretraining (S-PCL or MidVCL) followed by 300 epochs of standard contrastive learning (e.g., MoCo v2), which achieves 2× faster convergence, resolves the image-level vs. pixel-level task trade-off, and increases the model's shape bias as measured by the model-vs-human benchmark.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 linear classification at 100 epochs, S-PCL achieves 70.2% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (BYOL at 65.8%) by 4.4 points, and MidVCL reaches 70.5%. At 400 epochs, the hybrid S-PCL+MoCoV2 (100 epochs S-PCL warm-up + 300 epochs MoCoV2) achieves 78.0% Top-1, surpassing SimCLR (77.2%) and MoCoV2 (77.0%) trained for the full 400 epochs. On segmentation (ADE20K mIoU), MidVCL achieves 32.1% vs. 30.9% for the best baseline (BYOL), and on depth estimation (NYU Depth V2 RME), MidVCL achieves 0.1354 vs. 0.1420 for SimCLR. The hybrid approach achieves comparable or better final accuracy with roughly half the training epochs, demonstrating a 2× convergence speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six contrastive learning baselines: SimCLR, MoCoV2, PCL, SwAV, BYOL, and DINO. At 100 epochs, S-PCL and MidVCL decisively outperform all baselines on ImageNet-100 Top-1 (70.2–70.5% vs. 61.7–65.8%), with a 4.4–8.5 point margin, and on ImageNet-1k transfer (37.0–37.2% vs. 30.5–33.4%). However, when trained for the full 400 epochs without the hybrid schedule, S-PCL and MidVCL underperform the baselines on classification (71.8–72.4% vs. 75.8–77.2%), revealing rapid saturation of shape-only inductive bias. The hybrid S-PCL+MoCoV2 resolves this, achieving 78.0% Top-1 at 400 epochs—beating SimCLR (77.2%) and MoCoV2 (77.0%)—while reaching PCL-level accuracy in under half the epochs. On pixel-level tasks, MidVCL consistently wins: ADE20K mIoU of 32.1% (vs. 30.2–30.9% for baselines) and NYU Depth V2 RME of 0.1354 (vs. 0.1420–0.1459), with S-PCL also strong on depth (0.1381). ShadCL specifically improves depth estimation (RME 0.1399) but hurts classification, while ReflCL aids segmentation and classification but not depth, confirming that each perceptual construct targets different downstream capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Prototypical Contrastive Learning (PCL), repurposing its k-means clustering mechanism to operate on shape silhouettes rather than full image embeddings, thereby injecting figure-ground perceptual structure into the representation space. It departs from the standard contrastive learning paradigm (SimCLR, MoCo, SwAV, BYOL, DINO) that treats the objective as direct semantic representation learning, instead grounding the pipeline in Marr's multi-stage theory of vision by first constructing boundary and surface-level representations before refining toward object semantics. The use of Retinex-based intrinsic image decomposition and TRACER-based silhouette extraction as additional contrastive views extends the data augmentation philosophy of contrastive learning with perceptually motivated, task-specific views rather than purely geometric or photometric transforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a sequential perceptual-to-semantic training schedule (S-PCL or MidVCL warm-up followed by standard contrastive learning) resolves the documented trade-off between image-level classification and pixel-level tasks (segmentation, depth), a problem previously identified but not addressed by prior contrastive methods. However, the evidence is constrained to ResNet-18 on ImageNet-100 and STL-10, leaving open whether the 2× convergence speedup and the differential benefits of shape vs. reflectance vs. shading inductive biases persist at scale (larger backbones, full ImageNet-1K pretraining). The finding that shape prototypes must be explicitly disabled in later training to avoid semantic saturation suggests a broader principle about the temporal scheduling of inductive biases in self-supervised learning that warrants further investigation.
