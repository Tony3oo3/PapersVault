## Problem

Existing large-scale video datasets (WebVid-10M, Panda-70M, OpenVid) contain insufficient human diversity, lack fine-grained text-video alignment for human appearance and motion, and omit motion conditions such as skeleton sequences and speech audio. Meanwhile, human-specific datasets are too small or task-narrow to support pretraining general video generation models. This gap limits the ability of diffusion-based video generators to produce identity-consistent, motion-aligned human videos.

## Input / Output

The generation model consumes text prompts in three formats (short ~20-30 words, long, and structured with explicit appearance/motion/emotion/background fields), optional DWPose skeleton sequences, and SyncNet-verified speech audio, producing 720×480 videos of 49 frames at 24 FPS. The dataset itself spans 52.3M clips (13.2M after quality filtering) at 720P or 1080P, each 2-20 seconds long, sourced from films, TV series, and documentaries across 58 languages and 15 genres.

## Method

The paper introduces a four-stage data pipeline: (1) video preprocessing (H.264 re-encoding, CRAFT subtitle cropping, SceneDetect splitting, 2-20s duration filtering), (2) multi-metric quality filtering (luminance, blur, aesthetics, motion, technical quality), (3) human motion extraction (DWPose skeletons, SyncNet-verified speech audio, and dual-model captioning via MiniCPM + CogVLM with BLIP2 voting and LLaMA 3.1 reformatting into short/long/structured captions), and (4) human quality filtering that enforces text-video alignment on appearance, body motion, and facial motion using BLIP2 similarity scores. For validation, the authors extend CogVideoX by injecting LoRA adapters (rank 1024) into the full 3D attention modules, freezing the 6.6B base weights and training only 1.02B parameters for 20K steps at 5e-4 learning rate. The key empirical finding is that raising the training sampling rate from 8 to 24 FPS and applying the three alignment filters (appearance, motion, face) each yield measurable gains in face/body consistency and text-motion semantics without degrading general VBench metrics, demonstrating that targeted text-to-human-attribute alignment is the critical lever for human video quality.

## Results

After further pretraining on 6.05K hours of OpenHumanVid with LoRA (rank 1024), the extended CogVideoX model improves Face Consistency from 0.7108 to 0.7418 and Body Consistency from 0.9405 to 0.9678. Human semantic alignment also improves, with Body Semantics rising from 0.4166 to 0.4333 and Face Semantics from 0.3018 to 0.3136. General VBench metrics show consistent gains: I2V Consistency increases from 0.9759 to 0.9885, Imaging Quality from 0.6530 to 0.6662, and Motion Smoothness from 0.9905 to 0.9948, while Aesthetic Quality remains nearly unchanged (0.5562 → 0.5594).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed LoRA-extended CogVideoX model against the original CogVideoX baseline [59] after further pretraining on 6.05K hours of OpenHumanVid. The proposed method wins on all eight evaluated metrics. The most notable gains are in human-specific metrics: Face Consistency improves by +0.031 (0.7108 → 0.7418) and Body Consistency by +0.027 (0.9405 → 0.9678), indicating substantially better identity preservation across frames. Body Semantics improves by +0.017 and Face Semantics by +0.012, showing better text-video alignment for motion and expressions. General video quality metrics also improve, with I2V Consistency gaining +0.013 and Motion Smoothness +0.004, confirming that the human-centric pretraining does not degrade general generation capability. The Aesthetic Quality gain is minimal (+0.003), suggesting the dataset primarily targets structural and semantic fidelity rather than visual appeal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two dataset lineages: large-scale general video datasets (WebVid-10M, Panda-70M, OpenVid-1M, Koala-36M) that lack human diversity and motion conditions, and narrow human-specific datasets (UCF-101, NTU RGB+D, TikTok-v4) that are too task-specific for general video generation pretraining. Methodologically, it extends the CogVideoX diffusion transformer architecture with LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning, leveraging the same causal 3D VAE, T5 text encoding, and 3D RoPE components as the baseline. The data pipeline repurposes existing tools—MiniCPM and CogVLM for captioning, BLIP2 for alignment scoring, DWPose for skeleton extraction, and SyncNet for audio-visual consistency—into a unified human-centric annotation pipeline, rather than introducing new model components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is a 52.3M-clip (13.2M filtered) high-resolution human video dataset with multi-format captions, skeleton sequences, and speech audio, filling a clear gap for researchers working on human-centric video generation. However, the methodological validation is limited to a single architecture (CogVideoX) with modest metric improvements (e.g., Face Consistency 0.71→0.74, Body Consistency 0.94→0.97), and it remains open whether the training strategies (higher FPS, text-video alignment filters) transfer to other diffusion transformer or autoregressive architectures. The paper does not explore the full potential of the provided motion conditions (skeleton, audio) in the generation pipeline, leaving their utility for controllable generation largely untested.
