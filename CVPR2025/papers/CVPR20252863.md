## Problem

Iterative-based stereo matching methods (RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo, Selective-IGEV) have become mainstream but depend heavily on labeled data, while existing self-training approaches for stereo matching were designed for cost-volume-based methods and cannot effectively assess pseudo-label reliability for recurrent, iterative architectures. The paper identifies that error-prone regions exhibit pronounced oscillation in predictions across resolutions and iterative steps, and that prior binary (all-or-nothing) filtering discards potentially useful training signal from challenging areas.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes rectified stereo image pairs (left and right) from unlabeled datasets. The teacher model processes each pair at three resolutions (upscaled, original, downscaled) and records intermediate disparity predictions from the second half of its iterative refinement loop. The student receives strongly augmented versions of the same pair and produces a pixel-wise disparity map supervised by the teacher's original-resolution prediction, weighted per-pixel by the computed reliability scores.

## Method

CST-Stereo introduces a consistency-aware soft filtering module with two complementary filters: a multi-resolution prediction consistency filter (MRPCF) that computes per-pixel variance across high/original/low-resolution teacher predictions, and an iterative prediction consistency filter (IPCF) that measures the mean absolute difference between consecutive disparity predictions in the latter half of the recurrent iterations. Both variance and oscillation maps are converted to [0,1] reliability weights via sigmoid soft-thresholding functions, then multiplied element-wise to yield a combined soft weight. This weight modulates an L1 self-training loss so that unreliable pseudo-labels contribute minimally while still allowing the student to learn from difficult regions. The teacher is updated via exponential moving average (EMA, λ=0.99) every 100 student iterations, creating a cyclic teacher-student loop that progressively refines pseudo-label quality without requiring any labeled target-domain data.

## Results

On the in-domain leaderboard (Table 1), CST-Stereo achieves D1-all of 1.50 on KITTI 2015, bad-1.0 of 6.23 and Avgerr of 0.87 on Middlebury, and bad-1.0 of 1.02 and Avgerr of 0.12 on ETH3D, all setting new SOTA. In domain adaptation (Table 2), CST-Selective-IGEV reduces Middlebury F EPE from 5.28 to 1.32 (−75%) and D1 from 12.07 to 7.75 (−36%) over the Selective-IGEV baseline, while also improving KITTI D1 from 6.05 to 4.91 and Instereo2k bad-1.0 from 17.35 to 10.16. In domain generalization (no target data used), CST-Selective-IGEV still improves Middlebury F EPE from 5.28 to 4.11 and D1 from 12.07 to 9.86 using only Holopix50k unlabeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CST-Stereo outperforms all eight baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor Selective-IGEV [22] (the prior SOTA), CST-Stereo improves KITTI D1-all from 1.55 to 1.50, Middlebury bad-1.0 from 6.53 to 6.23, and ETH3D bad-1.0 from 1.23 to 1.02. The gap widens substantially against earlier iterative methods: RAFT-Stereo [14] is beaten by 0.46 in KITTI D1-all (1.96→1.50) and 3.14 in Middlebury bad-1.0 (9.37→6.23), while CREStereo [12] is outperformed by 0.19 in KITTI D1-all (1.69→1.50). Cost-volume-based PSMNet [5] is far behind, with KITTI D1-all of 2.32 and Middlebury bad-1.0 of 63.9. In the domain adaptation setting (Table 2), CST-Selective-IGEV reduces Middlebury F EPE from 5.28 (Selective-IGEV) to 1.32, a 75% reduction, and cuts Instereo2k bad-1.0 from 17.35 to 10.16, while also surpassing CST-RAFT-Stereo and CST-CREStereo on most metrics, confirming the benefit of building on the strongest base architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-training paradigm for stereo matching from cost-volume-based methods (PSMNet, SemiStereo) to the now-mainstream iterative-based (recurrent) architectures such as RAFT-Stereo, CREStereo, and Selective-IGEV. The key conceptual departure is the filtering mechanism: prior work relied on cost-volume probability distributions to assess pseudo-label reliability, which is unavailable in iterative methods that update disparity via GRU-based recurrent blocks. Instead, this paper introduces two consistency-based filters—multi-resolution prediction variance and iterative prediction oscillation—as proxies for reliability, and replaces binary thresholding with a soft-weighted loss. The base model is Selective-IGEV, the current SOTA iterative stereo matcher, and the teacher-student EMA framework follows DINO-style self-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since iterative-based methods have become the dominant paradigm in stereo matching, a self-training framework tailored to them addresses a practical gap: unlabeled real-world stereo data is abundant but was previously unusable for these architectures. The method's demonstrated effectiveness across in-domain, domain adaptation, and domain generalization settings makes it broadly applicable. However, the authors acknowledge that post-self-training performance remains bounded by the base model's architecture, and the orthogonality claim with domain adaptation methods (Tab. 6) suggests room for combined frameworks. An open question is whether the consistency-based reliability signals generalize to other dense prediction tasks beyond stereo matching.
