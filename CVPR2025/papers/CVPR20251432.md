## Problem

Existing learning-based rPPG methods rely on spatial skin-region features and temporal rhythmic priors that work under controlled illumination but fail in real-world outdoor scenarios where extreme, time-varying light changes can mask subtle biosignal amplitudes or introduce periodic perturbations that mimic cardiac signals. Prior robust approaches either require expensive near-infrared hardware or accumulate heavy disentanglement modules (GANs, adversarial discriminators) that are impractical for edge deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251432/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only RGB facial video (no auxiliary NIR or thermal channels), which is converted to a YUV spatiotemporal map (STMap) partitioned into 64 non-overlapping regions defined by 68 facial landmarks, with a temporal window of 320 frames at 25 fps. It outputs a continuous BVP waveform and a scalar heart-rate estimate. The full pipeline is lightweight at 6.03 M parameters and 53.04 G FLOPs, running in ~25 ms on a single RTX 4090, making it suitable for on-device deployment.

## Method

The architecture is a U-shaped Swin video transformer that jointly processes three STMaps: a foreground face map, a background map (outside the facial bounding box), and a global map (uniform L-block averaging of the full frame). A biological-prior sliding-window enhancement (BioSE) normalizes each landmark region within a 100-frame window to amplify subtle BVP color variations without global normalization that would magnify interference. The core disentanglement mechanism uses the background STMap as a self-supervised reference: a softmax-weighted subtraction removes foreground sub-blocks whose temporal PSD correlates with background, and a contrastive loss (with PSD-based distance metric restricted to 0.66–4 Hz) pulls irrelevant facial regions away from background patterns. The U-shaped decoder reconstructs the global map from stacked GT BVP signals to provide coarse-to-fine guidance, while a separate temporal-deconvolution head regresses the fine-grained BVP. Swin's standard temporal window shifting is replaced with random spatial (landmark-dimension) reorganization to preserve temporal continuity and increase spatial-topology diversity.

## Results

The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art results across all four datasets. On MR-NIRP-DRV (the most challenging outdoor driving scenario), it attains MAE 2.69 bpm, RMSE 6.03 bpm, and ρ 0.87, outperforming the previous best (ND-DeeprPPG: MAE 3.47, RMSE 6.54, ρ 0.85). On MR-NIRP-IND, it reaches MAE 0.45, RMSE 0.91, ρ 0.92; on VIPL-HR, MAE 1.43, RMSE 3.05, ρ 0.88; and on BUAA-MIHR, MAE 0.53, RMSE 1.20, ρ 0.95. The model also maintains a lightweight footprint of 6.03M parameters and 53.04G FLOPs, with 24.64 ms inference on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best MAE, RMSE, and ρ on all four datasets, outperforming every baseline. The closest competitor is RhythmFormer [25] (arXiv 2024), which is second-best on most metrics: on MR-NIRP-DRV it trails by 0.75 bpm MAE (3.44 vs 2.69) and 0.69 bpm RMSE (6.72 vs 6.03); on VIPL-HR by 0.40 bpm MAE (4.83 vs 1.43) and 5.00 bpm RMSE (8.05 vs 3.05). ND-DeeprPPG [16] (IEEE TIP 2024), the strongest noise-disentanglement baseline, is consistently outperformed: on MR-NIRP-DRV it shows MAE 3.47 vs 2.69 and RMSE 6.54 vs 6.03; on BUAA-MIHR it achieves ρ 0.95 (tied) but with higher MAE (0.58 vs 0.53) and RMSE (1.81 vs 1.20). Among transformer-based methods, PhysFormer++ [24] and EfficientPhys [21] lag significantly on the outdoor driving dataset (RMSE 7.59 and 12.28 vs 6.03), highlighting the advantage of the proposed interference disentanglement. Classic chrominance methods (CHROM, POS, LGI) and early CNNs (DeepPhys, TS-CAN) are far behind, with RMSE values 2–3× higher on MR-NIRP-DRV. In the RGB-only outdoor comparison (Table 2), the method also outperforms NIR-assisted approaches (AutoSparsePPG, Wu et al., Chiu et al.) on day and garage driving, confirming that visible-light disentanglement suffices without expensive infrared hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Swin Video Transformer backbone but departs fundamentally from prior rPPG disentanglement approaches (ND-DeeprPPG, Dual-GAN, PFDNet) that rely on adversarial training or generative networks to separate noise from signal. Instead, it exploits the physical observation that facial skin and subject background share the same time-varying illumination interference, using background perturbation as a self-supervised reference for correlation-based disentanglement via PSD contrastive learning. The landmark-based STMap with BioSE augmentation also represents a departure from the rectangular sub-patch pooling used in prior spatiotemporal rPPG methods, aligning spatial units with subcutaneous tissue distribution rather than arbitrary grid windows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RGB-only rPPG can match or exceed NIR-assisted methods in outdoor driving scenarios, challenging the field's recent trend toward multimodal (RGB+NIR) pipelines and their associated hardware costs. The lightweight architecture (6.03M parameters, 53 GFLOPs) makes real-time deployment on edge devices feasible, which is critical for practical applications like driver fatigue monitoring. Open questions remain regarding generalization to other vital signs (respiration, SpO2), performance under occlusion or extreme motion, and whether the background-reference disentanglement strategy holds when the background is highly dynamic or absent (e.g., studio settings with uniform backgrounds).
