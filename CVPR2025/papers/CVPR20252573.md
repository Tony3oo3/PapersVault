## Problem

State-of-the-art SfM systems inherently require three-view overlap with sufficient baseline and parallax to perform multi-view consistent 3D reconstruction, causing catastrophic failures in low-overlap, low-parallax, and high-symmetry scenarios that are common in data captured by non-expert users. This paper addresses that gap by integrating monocular depth and surface normal priors from deep networks into the classical incremental SfM paradigm, lifting the three-view track requirement and enabling robust reconstruction from two-view tracks alone.

## Input / Output

The system consumes unordered RGB images with known pinhole intrinsics, per-image monocular depth maps with pixel-wise confidence (e.g., from Metric3D-v2), surface normal maps with confidence (e.g., from DSINE), sparse feature correspondences (SuperPoint+LightGlue or SIFT), and optionally dense pixel-wise correspondences (RoMa or MASt3R). It produces camera poses in SE(3) for the subset of confidently registered images, a set of 3D scene points, and per-pixel refined depth maps that are globally consistent across views. The refined depth maps are a novel output beyond standard SfM, enabling downstream dense geometry tasks and the depth consistency check.

## Method

The core mechanism augments COLMAP's incremental SfM at four stages. First, two-view initialization lifts feature points using monocular depth to form 2D-3D matches for PnP-based absolute pose estimation when no stable relative pose exists, and scales depth maps to be consistent with initial 3D points. Second, next-view registration uses single-view lifted 3D points alongside multi-view triangulated points in 2D-3D correspondences, enabling registration without three-view overlap. Third, local and global refinement alternates between a GPU-based depth optimization (regularization against 3D points plus bilateral normal integration with uncertainty-weighted prior terms) and a CPU-based bundle adjustment (reprojection plus depth regularization), solved via block coordinate descent to maintain tractability. Fourth, a dense depth consistency check forward-backward reprojection of refined depth maps identifies and rejects incorrectly registered images, particularly those caused by scene symmetries. Principled uncertainty propagation from the monocular priors throughout the pipeline makes the system robust to noisy depth estimates and compatible with different off-the-shelf depth and normal models without retraining.

## Results

On the ETH3D triplet evaluation (minimal three-view overlap), MP-SfM with SuperPoint+LightGlue achieves AUC@1°/5°/20° of 27.3/55.9/71.8, compared to 1.1/2.3/3.0 for COLMAP and 8.4/15.8/22.5 for GLOMAP. On the SMERF dataset (minimal overlap), MP-SfM with MASt3R matching reaches 17.2/54.6/77.1, outperforming MASt3R-SfM (3.9/10.4/18.0) by a large margin. On the low-parallax RealEstate10k benchmark, MP-SfM with MASt3R achieves 35.5/81.9/91.5 (AUC@1°/10°/30°), surpassing both MASt3R-SfM (33.4/80.8/91.2) and GLOMAP (34.2/81.0/90.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MP-SfM dominates all classical baselines in low-overlap settings: on ETH3D triplets with minimal overlap, it achieves AUC@20° of 71.8 vs. 3.0 for COLMAP, 15.2 for SLR, and 22.5 for GLOMAP. Against the learned MASt3R-SfM pipeline, MP-SfM wins decisively in low-overlap scenarios (e.g., 17.2 vs. 3.9 AUC@1° on SMERF minimal overlap) but is slightly less accurate at AUC@1° on T&T high-overlap scenes (25.9 vs. 28.5), attributed to a lack of foreground matches in object-centric scenes. On the low-parallax RealEstate10k benchmark, MP-SfM with MASt3R (35.5/81.9/91.5) outperforms MASt3R-SfM (33.4/80.8/91.2), StudioSfM (19.9/72.3/86.5), and even the global GLOMAP (34.2/81.0/90.7), largely closing the gap between incremental and global SfM. The depth consistency check outperforms the learned Doppelgangers approach for symmetry rejection, achieving 9.2/41.0/69.8 vs. 0.2/1.0/1.4 (filter) on SMERF minimal overlap, as Doppelgangers suffers from domain shift. VGG-SfM and DF-SfM are substantially outperformed across all overlap levels, with VGG-SfM running out of memory on medium/high-overlap SMERF scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends COLMAP's incremental SfM framework by tightly integrating monocular depth and surface normal priors (from off-the-shelf networks like Metric3D-v2 and DSINE) into every stage of the pipeline—initialization, view registration, joint optimization, and registration rejection. It departs from prior depth-guided SfM work (StudioSfM, which uses depth only for initialization and regularization in low-parallax video) by performing full multi-view depth refinement with principled uncertainty propagation and bilateral normal integration [11]. Unlike MASt3R-SfM, which also operates on two-view correspondences, this approach explicitly models and propagates prior uncertainty, making it robust to noisy monocular estimates and compatible with diverse depth models without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first approach capable of reliably reconstructing challenging indoor environments from few images with low overlap, low parallax, or high symmetry—scenarios that are common for non-expert users and have been fundamental failure modes of SfM for years. The method's design means it will automatically benefit from future progress in monocular depth and normal estimation with little tuning, making it a durable contribution. Key limitations include dependence on reliable uncertainty estimates (which current depth models often over-confidently provide), degraded performance on vegetation-heavy outdoor scenes, and increased runtime. Open questions include scalability to very large scenes and whether the depth consistency check generalizes to more extreme symmetry cases.
