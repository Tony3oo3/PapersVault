## Problem

SAM 2 achieves strong promptable video segmentation but requires 256 A100 GPUs for 108 hours (2.2M G·Hour) of training from scratch, creating a prohibitive barrier for exploring alternative architectures and for resource-constrained research groups. The paper asks whether a pretrained image-based SAM can be upgraded to support PVS at a fraction of this cost while retaining most of SAM 2's accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252733/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8-frame video sequences (during training) or arbitrary-length videos (at inference) along with interactive user prompts—positive/negative point clicks, bounding boxes, or binary masks—applied at a designated prompt frame and optionally at corrective frames. It outputs a per-frame binary segmentation mask for the target object. Evaluation follows both an online protocol (single pass, pausing when IoU < 0.75 to insert corrective clicks) and an offline protocol (multiple passes, selecting the worst-error frame each pass), with prompts accumulated across passes in the offline setting.

## Method

SAM-I2V upgrades the TinySAM image encoder into a video perceptor via three new modules trained from scratch (10 epochs, 8× RTX A5000, 4.6k G·Hour). (1) A Temporal Feature Integrator (TFI) adds a parallel temporal branch of 3D convolutions and an integration branch that iteratively fuses spatial and temporal features at each encoder stage via spatial-to-temporal and temporal-to-spatial fusion operations. (2) A Memory Selective Associator (MSA) replaces SAM 2's fixed consecutive-frame memory with a similarity-based selector that partitions past frames into short-term (local) and long-term (global) pools, samples the most relevant frames via softmax-weighted sampling, and applies cross-attention to enrich current-frame features. (3) A Memory Prompt Generator (MPG) introduces learnable memory prompt tokens that attend to flattened memory features through masked cross-attention (masking out background pixels), followed by self-attention and an MLP, producing a memory-as-prompt embedding fed into the mask decoder alongside user prompts. The entire pipeline is trained jointly on 10K SA-1B images and 50,583 SA-V videos with a combined BCE + IoU + MAE + cross-entropy loss, achieving 91% of SAM 2.1's online J&F at 0.2% of its training cost.

## Results

On the online promptable video segmentation (PVS) task averaged across four benchmarks (ESD, PUMaVOS, LV-VIS, SAV-Test), SAM-I2V achieves an overall J&F score of 65.3, reaching 91% of SAM 2.1's 71.9 while using only 0.2% of its training cost (4.6k vs. 2.2m G·Hour) and 49% of its parameters (18.9M vs. 38.9M). On the offline PVS setting, SAM-I2V scores 66.0 J&F (90% of SAM 2.1's 73.3). The method consistently outperforms all other upgradation baselines (e.g., TinySAM+XMem++ at 65.8, TinySAM+Cutie at 68.1) across 1-click, 3-click, and 5-click interaction settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM 2.1 [36], SAM-I2V achieves 91% of its online J&F (65.3 vs. 71.9) and 90% offline (66.0 vs. 73.3) at 0.2% of the training cost, a trade-off the authors attribute partly to SAM 2.1's access to 62.9k proprietary videos. Compared to SAM+XMem++ [1] (157.0M params, OA 60.4) and SAM+Cutie [11] (129.8M params, OA 61.7), SAM-I2V (18.9M params) improves OA by 4.9 and 3.6 points respectively while being 8–9× smaller. Against the lightweight temporal-adapter baseline TinySAM+TA [36] (OA 58.6) and TinySAM+SA+TA [4] (OA 59.3), SAM-I2V gains 6.7 and 6.0 OA points, demonstrating that its dedicated TFI, MSA, and MPG components substantially outperform generic spatial/temporal adapters. In the ablation (Table 3), SAM-I2V also upgrades EdgeSAM [57] from 58.5 (EdgeSAM+TA) to 65.1 OA, confirming its generality across different SAM backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as an image-to-video upgradation of the pretrained SAM family, drawing architectural inspiration from SAM 2's memory encoder and temporal adapter while departing fundamentally from its train-from-scratch paradigm. It repurposes ideas from image-to-video transfer learning (ST-Adapter, AdaptFormer) and interactive video object segmentation (XMem++'s memory mechanism) but recombines them into a segmentation-specific pipeline with three novel components: a dual-branch temporal feature integrator, a relevance-based memory selector, and a masked cross-attention memory prompt generator. The key conceptual departure from SAM 2 is treating video segmentation as an incremental upgrade of a strong image model rather than a joint spatiotemporal learning problem requiring massive data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.2% training cost (4.6k vs. 2.2m G·Hour) is a substantial practical win that lowers the barrier for PVS research in academic and resource-constrained settings, enabling rapid iteration on architecture design. However, the ~10-point gap to SAM 2.1 is partly attributable to 62.9k proprietary videos used in SAM 2.1's training that are unavailable here, so the true methodological gap may be smaller than the headline numbers suggest. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos, more complex occlusion patterns, or other foundation model families, and whether the selective memory mechanism scales to very long sequences without degrading.
