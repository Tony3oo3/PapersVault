## Problem

Transformer-based diffusion models (DiT/MDT) offer better scalability and efficiency than U-Net-based virtual try-on models, but they lack the multi-scale encoder-decoder structure that naturally preserves fine-grained garment details. Directly applying MDT to IVTON is insufficient because the task requires high fidelity across multiple reference images, and naively feeding all ViT encoder layers as conditioning either loses information or incurs prohibitive computational cost.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a garment image (encoded at 224×224×3 through DINOv2 for feature extraction and at 512×512×3 through SDXL VAE for latent conditioning), a person image with its DensePose map, and a garment agnostic mask. The garment agnostic map, DensePose, and mask are concatenated with the noised latent as the query for the masked diffusion process, while garment features and salient-region features serve as cross-attention conditioning. The output is a 512×512 virtual try-on image blended with the agnostic person image in the non-garment regions.

## Method

ITA-MDT builds on MDTv2 with three key additions. First, MDT-IVTON adapts the masked diffusion transformer for IVTON by concatenating garment-agnostic reference latents with the noised latent and training with three objectives: standard denoising MSE, a mask reconstruction loss using a Side-Interpolator module, and a garment-mask-weighted inpainting loss that focuses reconstruction on the garment region. Second, the Image-Timestep Adaptive Feature Aggregator (ITAFA) dynamically combines features from all DINOv2 hidden layers into a single representation by computing softmax-normalized weights from a learned combination of the diffusion timestep embedding and a garment complexity score (sparsity, variance, gradient magnitude), enabling the model to shift from global semantic features in early timesteps to fine local details in later timesteps. Third, the Salient Region Extractor (SRE) identifies high-entropy regions in the garment via Shannon entropy thresholding and circular expansion from the entropy centroid, providing an additional high-resolution local condition that avoids the cost of processing the entire garment image at high resolution.

## Results

On VITON-HD, ITA-MDT (Base+ITAFA+SRE) achieves the best paired FID of 5.462 and unpaired FID of 8.676, with LPIPS 0.083 and SSIM 0.885, while using only 671M parameters and 4.6 s inference time. On DressCode Upper, it reaches the best unpaired FID of 10.069 and ties for the best LPIPS (0.034) and SSIM (0.951), again with the smallest model size (671M) among all compared methods. The ablation shows that adding ITAFA to the MDT-IVTON base reduces VITON-HD paired FID from 7.773 to 5.717, and SRE further improves DressCode unpaired FID from 13.111 to 10.069 without increasing inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ITA-MDT outperforms all six U-Net-based baselines on paired and unpaired FID for VITON-HD (5.462/8.676 vs. best baseline DCI-VTON at 5.559/8.750) and on unpaired FID for DressCode Upper (10.069 vs. IDM-VTON's 11.915), while using roughly 22–78% fewer parameters (671M vs. 859M–2,991M). On LPIPS, ITA-MDT is competitive: it ties for best on DressCode Upper (0.034) and is second-best on VITON-HD (0.083 vs. IDM-VTON's 0.082). On SSIM, DCI-VTON leads on VITON-HD (0.896 vs. 0.885) and TPD leads on DressCode Upper (0.956 vs. 0.951), but ITA-MDT remains within 0.005–0.011 of the best. Inference speed is a clear advantage: ITA-MDT runs in 4.6 s per image, matching LaDI-VTON (4.2 s) and far faster than DCI-VTON (15.4 s), StableVITON (13.4 s), IDM-VTON (12.3 s), and TPD (28.8 s). Overall, ITA-MDT achieves the best FID on both datasets with the smallest model and among the fastest inference, at the cost of slightly lower SSIM than the top U-Net methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Masked Diffusion Transformer (MDT), originally designed for class-conditional ImageNet generation, to the image-based virtual try-on (IVTON) task, departing from the dominant U-Net-based diffusion paradigm (Stable Diffusion, LaDI-VTON, DCI-VTON, StableVITON) that has characterized prior IVTON work. It specifically addresses the fine-grained detail preservation gap that transformer-based denoisers exhibit relative to U-Net's multi-scale encoder-decoder structure, introducing ITAFA (timestep- and complexity-adaptive feature aggregation from a DINOv2 ViT encoder) and SRE (entropy-based salient region extraction) as targeted conditioning mechanisms. The approach also contrasts with PICTURE's manual cluster-based feature selection and with [25]'s single-vector condition compression, arguing that IVTON's multi-reference-image setting demands enhanced rather than compressed condition information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 671M-parameter model matching or exceeding 859–2991M U-Net-based competitors on both LPIPS and FID while running faster offers a practical efficiency path for deploying IVTON in commercial settings where inference cost matters. The ITAFA mechanism—dynamically shifting feature emphasis from early (global) to late (local) ViT layers as denoising progresses—provides a generalizable recipe for conditioning transformer diffusion models on multi-scale image features, potentially transferable to other image-to-image tasks. Open questions include whether the heuristic entropy-based SRE generalizes to garments with distributed fine detail (e.g., all-over prints), whether the framework scales to higher resolutions beyond 512×512, and whether the three-objective training (denoising + mask reconstruction + inpainting) is necessary or if simpler formulations suffice.
