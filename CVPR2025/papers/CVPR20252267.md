## Problem

Existing egocentric motion capture methods rely on a single input modality (IMU, camera, or text), yet consumer wearable devices naturally provide diverse, intermittently available sensor combinations. No prior framework flexibly fuses 1–3 sparse IMUs, egocentric images, and motion descriptions in a unified system that simultaneously performs motion capture and generates motion understanding, while gracefully degrading when any modality is missing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252267/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts 1–3 IMU sensors (placed at head, wrists, or hips) providing 3D acceleration and 3×3 rotation matrices (converted to 6D representations), an optional single egocentric RGB image, and an optional natural-language motion description. It outputs a full-body 3D motion sequence in HumanML3D format (263-dim per timestep, downsampled by 4× in the codebook) and, when no description is provided, a generated natural-language motion description. The critical design constraint is that any subset of these inputs may be absent at inference time, and the IMU count is not fixed.

## Method

Ego4o builds on a part-aware VQ-VAE (adapted from TLControl) that maintains six independent codebooks—one per body segment (head, left/right arm, left/right leg, root)—so that available IMU signals project directly into their corresponding codebook while unmonitored segments are inferred analogously to text infilling. A transformer-based multi-modal encoder, trained with random masking of images, text, and IMU subsets, maps CLIP-encoded image/text features and reshaped IMU sequences to Gumbel-Softmax-sampled codebook indices, which are decoded by the VQ-VAE into motion; an optional test-time optimization refines the latent code to match IMU acceleration and orientation. For motion understanding, the same motion codes are embedded and concatenated with CLIP image features before being fed into a LoRA-fine-tuned LLaVA (Vicuna-7B) LLM that generates descriptions via autoregressive decoding. A key insight is that LLM-generated descriptions, even without ground-truth text, can be fed back into the motion capture encoder as a conditioning signal, closing a capture–understanding feedback loop that further reduces MPJPE.

## Results

On the Nymeria dataset, the full Ego4o system (1–3 IMUs + egocentric image + ground-truth motion description) achieves 84.82 mm MPJPE and 62.33 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming IMUPoser (105.7 mm MPJPE) by ~20 mm. Even in the IMU-only setting (Ego4o-IMU), the method reaches 95.86 mm MPJPE vs. IMUPoser's 105.7 mm. For motion description generation, Ego4o attains a BERTScore of 30.13 and ROUGE-L of 38.95, substantially exceeding MotionGPT (14.09 / 32.33) and TM2T (11.08 / 30.70), though it trails both on BLEU@4 (7.46 vs. 10.31 and 8.99).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Nymeria dataset, Ego4o-IMU (1–3 IMUs only) outperforms IMUPoser by ~10 mm in MPJPE (95.86 vs. 105.7) and ~4 mm in PA-MPJPE (69.03 vs. 72.94), while also achieving lower jitter (0.049 vs. 0.054). Adding the egocentric image and ground-truth motion description further reduces MPJPE to 84.82 mm, a ~21 mm improvement over IMUPoser. On DIP-IMU, Ego4o-IMU (84.06 mm MPJPE with 1–3 IMUs) is competitive with IMUPoser (97 mm) despite using fewer sensors, though it trails TransPose (59 mm) and DIP (73 mm), which both use 6 IMUs. For motion description generation, Ego4o substantially outperforms both TM2T and MotionGPT on BERTScore (30.13 vs. 14.09 and 11.08) and ROUGE-L (38.95 vs. 32.33 and 30.70), but underperforms on BLEU@4 (7.46 vs. 10.31 and 8.99), which the authors attribute to BLEU's reliance on n-gram overlap rather than semantic quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—IMU-based human motion capture (IMUPoser, TransPose, Diffusion-Poser) and motion-to-text generation (TM2T, MotionGPT)—into a single framework with flexible multi-modal egocentric inputs. The motion representation backbone is a part-aware VQ-VAE adapted from TLControl, which establishes per-body-segment codebooks to handle variable IMU placements, while the motion understanding component extends LLaVA (Vicuna-7B) with a new motion token modality. The key departure from prior multi-modal egocentric work (EgoLocate, EMHI, HMD²) is the random masking training strategy that allows the model to gracefully degrade across arbitrary combinations of 1–3 IMUs, an optional egocentric image, and optional text, rather than requiring a fixed sensor configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that AI-generated motion descriptions can be fed back as conditioning signals to improve motion capture accuracy, the paper introduces a practical feedback loop that reduces reliance on human-provided annotations in real-world deployment. The framework's flexibility with consumer-grade sensors (VR headsets, smartwatches, phones) makes it directly applicable to sports, healthcare, and personal-assistant scenarios. However, the method requires full motion sequences as input (introducing latency for online use), multi-round conversational ability remains limited, and the motion capture improvement from generated text, while consistent, is modest relative to the full multi-modal setup—suggesting the feedback loop's ceiling may be constrained by the quality gap between generated and ground-truth descriptions.
