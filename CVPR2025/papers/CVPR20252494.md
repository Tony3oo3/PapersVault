## Problem

Adapting Transformer self-attention to spiking neural networks is non-trivial because the vanilla mechanism relies on matrix multiplication, softmax, and scaling operations that conflict with SNNs' addition-only, energy-efficient computation. Prior spiking self-attention methods (e.g., Spikformer, Spikingformer) convert all of Q, K, and V into binary spikes to replace multiplication with addition, but this causes significant information loss that degrades accuracy. The paper asks: can one preserve the addition-only advantage while reducing the representational bottleneck of binary-only spikes?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252494/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2D image sequence I ∈ R^{T×C×H×W} where images are repeated T times (T=4 timesteps) to provide a temporal dimension for the LIF spiking neurons. Images are resized to 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and 224×224 for ImageNet-1K. The sequence is split into N flattened spike patches of D-dimensional channels, processed through L transformer encoder blocks, and a Global Average Pooling followed by a linear head produces the final class label.

## Method

The core contribution is the Accurate Addition-Only Spiking Self-Attention (A²OS²A) mechanism, which assigns different activation types to Q, K, and V: a binary spiking neuron for Q, a ReLU activation for K, and a ternary spiking neuron (outputting {-1,0,1}) for V. Because Q ∈ {0,1} and K ∈ R_{≥0}, the product Q·K^T is naturally non-negative, eliminating the need for softmax; because K is unbounded real-valued, no scaling factor is required. All matrix operations reduce to addition since Q and V are sparse (binary/ternary). The paper further provides an entropy-based theoretical analysis showing that binary spikes have 1/32 the representational capacity of 32-bit real values, motivating the hybrid precision design. The full Spiking Transformer stacks L encoder blocks (each with A²OS²A and an MLP, both with residual connections on membrane potentials) after a Spiking Patch Splitting module, and is trained end-to-end via supervised learning with 4 timesteps.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the proposed Spiking Transformer-10-512 achieves a top-1 accuracy of 78.66% with only 4 timesteps, setting a new SNN benchmark and surpassing the previous best (Spike-driven Transformer-8-768 at 77.07%) by 1.59%. On CIFAR-10, Spiking Transformer-2-512 reaches 96.42% accuracy, and on CIFAR-100 it reaches 79.90%, both with 4 timesteps. The method consistently outperforms all prior SNN-based Transformer approaches (Spikformer, Spikingformer, Spike-driven Transformer) across all three datasets and model scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three main SNN-based Transformer baselines: Spikformer, Spikingformer, and Spike-driven Transformer, as well as several SNN-based CNN methods (SEW ResNet, Spiking ResNet, TET, STBP-tdBN). On ImageNet-1K with the 8-512 architecture, Spiking Transformer achieves 76.28%, outperforming Spikformer-8-512 (73.38%) by 2.90%, Spikingformer-8-512 (74.79%) by 1.49%, and Spike-driven Transformer-8-512 (74.57%) by 1.71%. The headline result of 78.66% (Spiking Transformer-10-512, 36.01M params) surpasses even the larger Spike-driven Transformer-8-768 (77.07%, 66.34M params) by 1.59% while using nearly half the parameters. On CIFAR-10, Spiking Transformer-4-384 (96.32%) beats Spikformer-4-384 (95.19%) by 1.13% and Spikingformer-4-384 (95.61%) by 0.71%; on CIFAR-100 the gap widens to 1.83% over Spikformer-4-384. All comparisons use 4 timesteps, and the proposed method wins every head-to-head comparison against prior SNN-Transformer methods across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNN-based Transformer lineage initiated by Spikformer, Spikingformer, and Spike-driven Transformer, all of which convert Q, K, and V into binary spike form to replace matrix multiplication with addition. The key departure is a hybrid design: binary spiking neurons for Q, a standard ReLU for K, and a ternary (−1, 0, 1) spiking neuron for V, motivated by an entropy-based analysis showing that full binarization of all three components causes substantial representational loss. The overall architecture (patch splitting, residual connections, MLP blocks) follows Spike-driven Transformer [58], and the paper positions itself as the first to provide a theoretical account of information loss specific to spiking self-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 78.66% ImageNet-1K result sets a new benchmark for SNN-based Transformers and demonstrates that selectively preserving precision in K and V is more effective than uniform binarization, a finding that could guide future hybrid SNN-ANN designs. The entropy-based framework for quantifying representational capacity of spike layers is a useful analytical tool for the broader SNN community. However, the 'addition-only' framing is somewhat generous since ReLU on K is a full-precision real-valued operation, and the paper does not report energy measurements on neuromorphic hardware to validate the efficiency claims. Open questions include whether the ternary V is strictly necessary versus a simpler ReLU, and whether the approach generalizes to other modalities (video, point clouds) or tasks beyond classification.
