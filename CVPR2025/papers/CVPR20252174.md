## Problem

Existing DGM4 methods either use separate shallow and deep decoders for different sub-tasks (limiting cross-task correlation) or employ a unified multi-modal decoder that ignores the consistency relationship between genuine and forged content. This leads to inadequate fine-grained perception of localized forgery and unreliable grounding of manipulated regions, particularly for text manipulation where previous methods rely on token-level prediction heads rather than explicit cross-modal evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252174/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an image-text news pair as input, with images resized to 256×256 (split into 256 patches by ViT-B/16) and text padded to 50 tokens (processed by RoBERTa). Outputs include a binary real/fake label, multi-label manipulation type (face swap, face attribute, text swap, text attribute), a bounding box for the manipulated face, and per-token manipulation labels for text. Notably, text grounding is produced by a threshold filter (0.5) applied to semantic consistency scores between each token and the image global embedding, eliminating the need for a separate token-level prediction head.

## Method

CSCL introduces two cascaded decoders operating on fine-grained embeddings from a ViT-B/16 + RoBERTa backbone with 6 co-attention layers. The Contextual Consistency Decoder (CCD) constructs an n×n intra-modal consistency matrix by computing cosine similarity (scaled to [0,1]) between every pair of patch/token embeddings after a 3-layer self-attention consistency processor, supervised with binary cross-entropy against ground-truth same-source labels. The Semantic Consistency Decoder (SCD) builds an n×1 cross-modal consistency matrix by comparing each fine-grained embedding against the global embedding of the other modality. In both decoders, a forgery-aware reasoning/aggregating module selects k most similar (reliable) and k most dissimilar (suspicious) embeddings for selective attention, reducing interference from confused content. A consistency loss (BCE on both matrices) is added to the standard sub-task losses, and the final aggregated features feed MLP classifiers for all prediction heads.

## Results

On the DGM4 dataset (entire dataset setting), CSCL achieves 96.34% AUC, 90.32% ACC, 92.48% mAP, 84.07% IoU_m, and 76.62% F1 for text grounding, setting new state-of-the-art across all sub-tasks. The most significant gains are in grounding: CSCL improves IoU_m by +3.24% and IoU75 by +6.78% over the previous best (Wang et al., ICASSP 2024), and F1 for text grounding by +3.18%. On the image sub-dataset, CSCL reaches 82.78% IoU_m, surpassing UFAFormer by +5.50%; on the text sub-dataset, it achieves 80.32% F1, exceeding UFAFormer by +4.74%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSCL outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every metric on the DGM4 dataset. Against the closest prior SOTA (Wang et al., ICASSP 2024), CSCL gains +1.23% AUC, +1.57% ACC, +1.06% mAP, +3.24% IoU_m, +6.78% IoU75, and +3.18% F1 (text grounding). Against UFAFormer (IJCV 2024), the improvements are even larger: +2.53% AUC, +4.63% mAP, +5.74% IoU_m, and +4.60% F1. On the image sub-dataset, CSCL leads UFAFormer by +5.50% IoU_m (82.78 vs. 77.28) and +4.74% F1 on the text sub-dataset (80.32 vs. 75.58). The original HAMMER (CVPR 2023) and its extension HAMMER++ (TPAMI 2024) are surpassed by +3.15% AUC and +5.27% IoU_m respectively. Notably, CSCL's advantage is most pronounced in grounding tasks (IoU and F1), while binary classification gains are more modest, confirming that consistency learning primarily benefits fine-grained localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSCL builds on the DGM4 task introduced by HAMMER [43] and extended by HAMMER++ [44], but departs from prior multi-modal manipulation detection methods (UFAFormer, Wang et al.) that rely on unified cross-modal decoders or frequency-domain features without explicitly modeling consistency. The key conceptual shift is repurposing consistency learning—previously applied at the global embedding level in deepfake detection (PCL, self-consistency)—to the fine-grained patch/token level, constructing explicit consistency matrices supervised by binary same-source/different-source labels. The forgery-aware reasoning module, which selectively attends to the most similar and most dissimilar embeddings, is a novel architectural choice not present in prior DGM4 methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fine-grained consistency learning is a more effective signal for localizing manipulated content than cross-modal attention alone, with particularly large gains on grounding metrics (IoU75 +6.78% over prior SOTA). The threshold filter design, which uses semantic consistency scores directly for text grounding rather than a separate prediction head, is a clean simplification that could inform other token-level detection tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset of 230 news pairs, and it remains open whether the consistency-matrix formulation generalizes to other manipulation types (e.g., video, audio) or scales to larger, more diverse benchmarks.
