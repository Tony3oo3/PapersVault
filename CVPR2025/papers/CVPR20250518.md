## Problem

Existing hallucination benchmarks for video MLLMs are small (fewer than 1,000 videos), focus primarily on static elements like objects and spatial relationships rather than dynamic temporal content, and use limited question formats. This leaves a critical gap in evaluating how MLLMs handle action, temporal sequence, and scene transition hallucinations—hallucination types inherent to video understanding that arise when models over-rely on semantic cues from CLIP-series encoders while failing to distinguish visually different yet semantically similar content.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes video clips (average ~25 seconds, sourced from ActivityNet, YouCook2, and VALOR32K) presented as semantically similar but visually distinct pairs, alongside text questions in four formats: binary yes/no, 4-option multiple choice, action-sequence sorting, and open-ended scene description. A key design choice is that adversarial questions reference the action or scene from the *paired* video rather than the input video, forcing models to resist semantic confusion. For scene transition evaluation, concatenated video segments (with and without actual scene changes) are used, and outputs are scored via MCC for classification and SimCSE cosine similarity for description quality.

## Method

VIDHALLUC is constructed via a semi-automated pipeline that flags video pairs scoring above 0.9 semantic similarity (CLIP/SigLIP average-pooled features) but below 0.6 visual similarity (DINOv2 features), then applies GPT-4 annotation, human validation, and automatic question generation for three hallucination types. DINO-HEAL is a training-free inference-time method that extracts saliency maps from DINOv2's last-layer attention by averaging across heads and taking the [CLS] token's attention weights over spatial tokens. These saliency scores are reshaped into a 2D grid, bilinearly upsampled to match the visual encoder's spatial dimensions, flattened, and passed through a sigmoid to produce normalized weights in [0,1]. The final step element-wise multiplies these weights with the frozen visual encoder's frame features, amplifying salient regions and suppressing background noise without any architectural modification or additional training.

## Results

On VIDHALLUC, GPT-4o achieves the highest scores among all tested MLLMs (81.15% ACH Binary QA, 90.95% MCQ, 82.00% TSH, 71.58% STH), yet still trails human performance (95.14%, 93.29%, 90.17%, 87.43%) by 13.99% on ACH Binary QA and 15.85% on STH. Most open-source models score below 50% on TSH and below 40% on STH, revealing severe vulnerabilities in temporal and scene-transition reasoning. DINO-HEAL, the proposed training-free method, yields an average improvement of 3.02% across five models and three hallucination categories, with the largest gains on TSH (VideoLLaMA2: +18.33%, Video-ChatGPT: +11.66%) and ACH Binary QA (Video-LLaVA: +6.96%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source models, VILA1.5 (13B, SigLIP) leads on ACH Binary QA (58.46%) and TSH (63.33%), while VideoLLaMA2 (7B, CLIP-336) achieves the best STH score (65.12%) and competitive ACH MCQ (83.84%). GPT-4o outperforms all open-source models on every metric, with the largest gaps on ACH Binary QA (81.15% vs. 58.46% for VILA1.5) and STH (71.58% vs. 65.12% for VideoLLaMA2). Gemini-1.5-Pro trails GPT-4o on ACH (75.27% vs. 81.15% Binary QA) and STH (63.96% vs. 71.58%), and is slightly outperformed by VideoLLaMA2 on STH (63.96% vs. 65.12%). DINO-HEAL consistently improves all five tested backbones: Video-ChatGPT gains +4.46% on ACH Binary QA and +11.66% on TSH; Video-LLaVA gains +6.96% on ACH Binary QA; VideoLLaMA2 gains +18.33% on TSH; VILA1.5 gains +2.17% on ACH Binary QA; and ShareGPT4Video gains +5.83% on TSH. The method is most effective on TSH and ACH (foreground-action tasks) but provides only marginal STH improvements (~1%), attributed to DINOv2's foreground bias limiting its utility for background scene-change detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of hallucination evaluation benchmarks for MLLMs (HallusionBench, VideoHallucer, Vript-HAL, EventHallusion) by shifting focus from static elements (objects, attributes, spatial relations) to dynamic temporal dimensions—action, temporal sequence, and scene transition—using paired adversarial video design. The data collection pipeline builds directly on Tong et al.'s 'Eyes Wide Shut?' observation that CLIP-based encoders conflate semantically similar but visually distinct content, formalizing this gap into a systematic benchmark construction procedure. DINO-HEAL departs from prior hallucination mitigation strategies (instruction tuning, RLHF, self-correction frameworks) by offering a training-free inference-time reweighting that leverages DINOv2's self-supervised saliency maps, requiring no architectural changes or additional data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIDHALLUC fills a critical gap by providing the largest-scale, multi-format evaluation of temporal hallucinations in video MLLMs, giving the community a rigorous tool to track progress on a previously underexplored failure mode. The finding that model scale and frame count do not reliably reduce hallucinations (e.g., a 34B/32-frame model underperforms a 13B/8-frame model) challenges the assumption that simply scaling video MLLMs will resolve these issues. DINO-HEAL's modest but consistent gains (3.02% average) and its foreground bias limiting scene-transition improvements highlight that the semantic-visual encoder gap is not easily closed by spatial reweighting alone, pointing toward the need for temporal saliency or dual-stream designs as future directions.
