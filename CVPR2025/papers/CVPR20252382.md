## Problem

Existing 3D avatar creation methods produce exact digital twins from video inputs but lack the generative ability to edit facial attributes (hairstyle, beard, hat, etc.) while preserving identity. Prior generative avatar work like PEGASUS requires videos from multiple individuals to synthesize attribute variations, making it impractical for single-image input and limiting scalability. PERSE addresses this by building a personalized, animatable 3D avatar from a single portrait with continuous, disentangled control over facial attributes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252382/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image as the sole identity input, along with FLAME parameters (shape β, pose θ, expression ψ) for animation and text prompts specifying target attributes. It produces an animatable 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar whose appearance is governed by a disentangled latent code z ∈ R^{Nc×d}, where each subpart latent independently controls one attribute category (hair, beard, hat, etc.) while a shared identity latent preserves the person's core appearance. The latent space is continuous, enabling smooth interpolation between attribute states (e.g., short-to-long hair) rather than only discrete swaps.

## Method

PERSE builds a 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar (replacing PEGASUS's point cloud) with a CLIP-guided latent space: subpart latents are produced by a shallow MLP conditioned on CLIP image and text features, preventing per-subject overfitting and enabling zero-shot retrieval of new attributes. To train from a single portrait, the paper synthesizes ~957 attribute-edited videos via a two-stage pipeline: (1) text-conditioned inpainting with automatically generated masks (derived from a text-to-image model aligned via ControlNet, then segmented with Sapiens) to create diverse attribute variants, and (2) animation using LivePortrait for most categories and a custom face-specialized video diffusion model (portrait-Champ) for hair, beard, hat, earrings, and headphones. A key training contribution is an interpolation regularization loss that uses DiffMorpher-generated 2D face morphs as pseudo-supervision for interpolated latent codes, enforcing smooth continuous transitions in the latent space and reducing artifacts on unseen attribute combinations. Finally, LoRA fine-tuning allows efficient integration of arbitrary new attributes from in-the-wild images without disrupting the learned latent distribution.

## Results

On unseen pose reconstruction (Table 1), PERSE achieves the best scores across all metrics: PSNR 23.84, SSIM 0.8852, LPIPS 0.1458, and ArcFace identity similarity 0.7059. On interpolated attribute renderings (Table 2), PERSE attains FID_FFHQ 214.46, KID_syn 0.0420, PPL 0.3308, and a user-study preference score of 63.7, outperforming all baselines. The method also achieves the best FID_syn (57.78) and KID_FFHQ (0.2201), confirming both identity preservation and alignment with real-face distributions for generated interpolations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PERSE is compared against three baselines: PEGASUS (colorized point cloud), Cond.TA (iNGP-based INSTA with latent conditioning), and Cond.SA (mesh-embedded 3D Gaussian Splatting with latent conditioning). On reconstruction (Table 1), PERSE outperforms PEGASUS by +0.28 PSNR and +0.059 identity similarity, and substantially outperforms Cond.TA (+4.83 PSNR) and Cond.SA (+1.67 PSNR). On interpolation quality (Table 2), PERSE beats PEGASUS on FID_FFHQ (214.46 vs. 223.86), FID_syn (57.78 vs. 84.94), PPL (0.3308 vs. 0.4047), and user preference (63.7 vs. 36.3), though PEGASUS edges out slightly on PDV* (0.2373 vs. 0.2481). Against Cond.TA and Cond.SA, PERSE dominates on every interpolation metric, with FID_syn improvements of 69.67 and 123.01 points respectively. The user study confirms that PERSE's interpolated avatars are perceived as significantly more natural than PEGASUS's, which exhibits unnatural hair color and texture transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PERSE is a direct extension of PEGASUS (same group, CVPR 2024), replacing its colorized point cloud representation with 3D Gaussian Splatting and, critically, eliminating the need for multi-person video inputs by synthesizing attribute-varied videos from a single portrait image. The synthetic data pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf diffusion tools (SDXL, FLUX, ControlNet) for attribute-edited image generation and combines LivePortrait with a newly trained face-specialized Champ variant (portrait-Champ) for animation. A key methodological departure from PEGASUS is the CLIP-conditioned latent space, which constrains subpart latent codes through a shallow MLP on CLIP image-text features rather than directly optimizing free latent vectors, yielding a more compact and generalizable representation. The interpolation regularization via DiffMorpher and the LoRA-based attribute transfer are additional contributions not present in prior generative avatar work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PERSE demonstrates that a single portrait image is sufficient to bootstrap a full synthetic training corpus (957 attribute-varied videos) for a personalized 3D generative avatar, removing the practical barrier of collecting multi-person video data that limited PEGASUS's scalability. The disentangled, continuous latent space with CLIP grounding offers a practical interface for users to edit facial attributes (hair, beard, hat, etc.) while preserving identity, which is directly relevant to VR/AR avatar applications. However, the 1.5-day training cost on 8× RTX A6000 GPUs and the acknowledged gap in photorealism (especially fine hair strands) limit immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the synthetic-data pipeline generalizes to more diverse identities and attribute categories, whether the CLIP-conditioned latent space can be made fully text-driven for zero-shot attribute specification, and whether the approach can be made real-time or near-real-time for interactive use.
