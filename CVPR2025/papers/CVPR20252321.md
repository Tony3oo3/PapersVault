## Problem

Existing visual geolocation methods produce deterministic point predictions, ignoring the fact that image localizability varies enormously—from meter-level precision for landmarks to near-uniform uncertainty for featureless scenes. This paper introduces the first generative framework for global visual geolocation that explicitly models this spatial ambiguity, and further defines the new task of probabilistic visual geolocation where the output is a full distribution over the Earth's surface rather than a single coordinate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252321/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, embedded by a frozen DINOv2-L (or StreetCLIP ViT-L for OSV-5M) encoder into a fixed-dimensional vector, and produces a point estimate on the unit sphere S², a full probability density p(y|c) over all locations, and the log-density at any queried coordinate. The denoising trajectory itself is a continuous path on the sphere (or in R³ projected onto S²), and the model can be queried at any point to obtain a likelihood without re-running the full generation. A skewed sigmoid scheduler concentrates computation on early timesteps where coordinates remain near the true location, encouraging fine-grained cue learning.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-variant generative denoising framework—diffusion in R³, flow matching in R³, and Riemannian flow matching on S²—where a 6-block DiT-style network (MLP with GELU, AdaLN conditioning on image embedding and noise level) predicts either the noise or the velocity field to iteratively denoise a random coordinate into a location estimate. The Riemannian variant is the key architectural insight: noisy coordinates are defined via geodesic interpolation using the exponential and logarithmic maps on S², so the entire denoising trajectory and velocity field live on the sphere, respecting Earth's spherical geometry without projection artifacts. Training is supervised with a simple L2 loss on the predicted noise/velocity, and classifier-free guidance (randomly dropping the image condition during training) enables post-hoc sharpening of predictions via a guidance scale ω. For density estimation, the paper extends the logarithmic mass conservation theorem to compute log p(y|c) by solving an augmented ODE that accumulates the negative divergence of the velocity field along the trajectory, using a 5th-order Runge-Kutta solver.

## Results

On OpenStreetView-5M, the best model (RFM on S²) achieves a GeoScore of 3767/5000 and average Haversine distance of 1069 km, outperforming the strongest deterministic baseline (SC Retrieval, 3597 GeoScore, 1386 km) and the hybrid regression approach (3361 GeoScore, 1814 km). On iNat21, RFM S² reduces the average geolocation distance to 2500 km, compared to 4701 km for the vMF mixture baseline and 6270 km for single vMF regression. On the probabilistic geolocation task (Table 2), RFM S² achieves the best NLL (−1.51 on OSV-5M, −3.71 on YFCC) and the highest generative metrics (precision 0.88, recall 0.95, density 0.78, coverage 0.59 on iNat21), substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OSV-5M, RFM S² beats the strongest retrieval-based baseline (SC Retrieval [23]) by 170 GeoScore points (3767 vs. 3597) and reduces distance by 317 km (1069 vs. 1386), though SC Retrieval retains an advantage at city-level accuracy (19.9% vs. 5.4%) due to its million-image database. Against the hybrid regression of Astruc et al. [2], the paper reports a +406 GeoScore gain, −745 km distance reduction, and +8.2% country accuracy. On iNat21, RFM S² (2500 km) outperforms the vMF mixture of Izbicki et al. [33] (4701 km) by nearly half, and the single vMF regression (6270 km) by 2.5×. Among the iNat21 classification baselines (PlaNet [73], CPlaNet [63], Translocator [60], GeoDecoder [11], PIGEON [24]), the generative models achieve substantially lower average distances. On the probabilistic task (Table 2), RFM S² dominates all baselines: it achieves NLL of −1.51 on OSV-5M versus 0.06 for vMF Mix and 1.22 for Uniform, and leads on all four generative metrics (precision, recall, density, coverage) on iNat21. Flow matching in R³ consistently outperforms diffusion in R³ across both geolocation and probabilistic metrics, and the Riemannian variant on S² further improves over the Euclidean flow matching, confirming the benefit of respecting spherical geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply generative models (diffusion and flow matching) to global visual geolocation, departing from the dominant paradigm of deterministic regression, classification, or retrieval-based methods. It extends Riemannian flow matching (Chen & Lipman, ICLR 2024) to the sphere S², so that the denoising trajectory respects Earth's spherical geometry via geodesic interpolation, and adapts classifier-free guidance and DDIM sampling to the geolocation setting. The density estimation machinery from flow matching (Lipman et al., 2024) is repurposed to compute location likelihoods, enabling a new probabilistic geolocation task that prior work (e.g., Izbicki et al. 2020) only touched upon without dedicated metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing geolocation as a generative process, the paper provides calibrated uncertainty (localizability scores) alongside point predictions, which is critical for downstream applications like forensic verification or archive retrieval where knowing *how confident* the model is matters as much as the prediction itself. The introduction of probabilistic visual geolocation with NLL, localizability, and generative metrics (precision, recall, density, coverage) gives the community a principled way to evaluate distributional predictions. Open questions include whether the approach can close the gap with retrieval-based methods at very fine granularity (25 km), whether the Riemannian formulation generalizes to other curved manifolds in geospatial tasks, and how computational cost scales when querying the full spatial density field.
