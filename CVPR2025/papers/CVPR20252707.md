## Problem

Existing video stylization methods face three critical limitations: they are designed for short clips and fail on videos with complex scene transitions, they treat open-ended user queries as direct prompts despite their ambiguity, and they apply fixed ControlNet weights regardless of style-specific structural requirements. No prior system can automatically and robustly stylize an arbitrary video into an arbitrary style specified by a non-expert user.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252707/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a video clip (TVSBench videos average 30 seconds at 30 FPS) and an open-ended natural-language style query that may be vague or contain contradictory descriptors (e.g., 'This Japanese anime movie would be more appealing if it was performed by real people'). The video is first decomposed into individual shots via boundary detection, and each shot is stylized independently using Stable Diffusion v1.5 with AnimateDiff for temporal consistency. The output is a temporally coherent stylized video where each shot is rendered with its own content prompt and adaptively tuned structural controls.

## Method

V-Stylist is a zero-shot multi-agent pipeline of three MLLM-driven roles that mimics a human video editor's workflow. The Video Parser uses AutoShot for shot detection, Qwen2-VL for per-shot captioning, and Mistral for prompt translation, enabling shot-level stylization of long videos. The Style Parser extracts the style intent from the open query via LLM, then performs tree-of-thought searching over a hierarchical style tree (17 styles, 25 leaf models, depth 3) where five LLM experts debate and a chairman selects the best-matching pre-trained diffusion model from HuggingFace/Civitai. The Style Artist renders each shot using the selected model with four ControlNets (tile, depth, softedge, lineart), then iteratively evaluates the result with Qwen2-VL as a style scorer and adjusts ControlNet weights via a control refiner for up to three rounds of self-reflection, adaptively matching structural detail to the target style.

## Results

On TVSBench, V-Stylist achieves the best overall average score of 0.6011, surpassing the strongest baselines FRESCO (0.5405) and ControlVideo (0.5560) by 6.05% and 4.51% respectively. V-Stylist leads on 7 out of 8 individual metrics, including Structure (0.9020), Semantics (0.9772), Aesthetic-V (0.5826), and Distortion-V (0.7445), with the only loss being CLIP-W (0.1528 vs. ControlVideo's 0.1570). The ablation on TVSBench-highlight confirms that each component contributes: adding Style Parser improves style alignment by 25.16%, and Style Artist further boosts Distortion-V from 0.6630 to 0.7028.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-Stylist outperforms all five baselines on the overall average metric, with the largest margins over FRESCO (+6.05%) and ControlVideo (+4.51%). Against ControlVideo, V-Stylist wins on 7 of 8 metrics (CLIP-T, Structure, Semantics, Aesthetic-I, Aesthetic-V, Distortion-I, Distortion-V) but trails slightly on CLIP-W (0.1528 vs. 0.1570). Against FRESCO, V-Stylist leads on every metric except Semantics, where FRESCO edges ahead (0.9762 vs. 0.9772 is actually V-Stylist winning; FRESCO's 0.9762 is lower). The most dramatic gap is on Distortion-V, where V-Stylist scores 0.7445 versus 0.5413 (ControlVideo) and 0.5710 (FRESCO), indicating substantially fewer temporal distortions. Rerender and FLATTEN are clearly weaker, with overall averages of 0.5022 and 0.4870 respectively, and both suffer from poor structure preservation (0.8499 and 0.6083) and low aesthetic scores. Control-A-Video, despite its name, performs poorly on condition alignment (CLIP-T 0.2044) and video quality, ranking second-to-last overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training a single diffusion-based model for video stylization (ControlVideo, FRESCO, Rerender-A-Video) by instead decomposing the task into a multi-agent pipeline of MLLMs that orchestrate existing components. It repurposes tree-of-thought reasoning from NLP for style model selection over a hierarchical style tree, and borrows the self-reflection pattern from agent literature (GenArtist, DiffusionGPT, MUSES) but applies it to iteratively adjust ControlNet weights for structure detail. The individual building blocks—AutoShot, Qwen2-VL, AnimateDiff, ControlNets—are all pre-existing; the contribution lies in the collaborative orchestration and the video-to-shot prompting paradigm that handles multi-scene transitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses three practical pain points (long videos with transitions, ambiguous user queries, rigid structure control) that limit real-world deployment of video stylization, and introduces TVSBench as the first benchmark specifically targeting complex multi-shot video stylization with open-ended style queries. However, the evaluation is limited to 50 videos and 17 styles, the style tree requires manual curation of open-source models, and the multi-round reflection loop adds significant computational cost (8× A6000 GPUs). Open questions include whether the agent-based decomposition generalizes beyond the curated style tree, how efficiency can be improved for practical use, and whether the approach scales to truly arbitrary styles without pre-trained models.
