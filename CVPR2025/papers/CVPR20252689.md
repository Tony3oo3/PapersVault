## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting extensions handle either dynamic scene reconstruction or specular surface rendering, but none successfully combine both. Dynamic scene methods fail to represent specular surfaces accurately because they do not account for how surface normals change during deformation, while specular rendering methods are restricted to static scenes. This gap prevents photorealistic novel view synthesis of real-world scenes containing moving reflective objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence with corresponding camera poses as input and produces a deformable 3D Gaussian representation with per-Gaussian PBR attributes (roughness, specular tint) plus a deformable environment map. Rendering is real-time (>30 FPS on an RTX 4090) when the Gaussian count stays below 178k. The system also outputs per-frame normal maps and depth maps, which are used both as intermediate supervision signals during training and as diagnostic visualizations.

## Method

SpectroMotion employs a three-stage coarse-to-fine training strategy: a static stage stabilizes geometry with vanilla 3DGS, a dynamic stage adds a deformation MLP (following Deformable 3DGS) with a normal loss that supervises rendered normals against depth-derived normals, and a specular stage introduces PBR shading with a deformable environment map. The key technical contribution is a physical normal estimation scheme that computes a rotation matrix from both the shortest and longest axes of canonical and deformed Gaussians, then scales the learned normal residual by the oblateness ratio between canonical and deformed shapes, ensuring accurate normals even when deformation changes Gaussian aspect ratios. For time-varying lighting, a deformable reflection MLP predicts a residual added to the reflection direction before querying a canonical cubemap, enabling the specular color to adapt across frames. The final pixel color decomposes into a view-independent diffuse term (zero-order spherical harmonics) and a view-dependent specular term computed via the Cook-Torrance model with GGX distribution, geometric attenuation, and Fresnel terms, with a shape-dependent regularization penalty γ^k that modulates how strongly the normal residual is penalized based on Gaussian flatness.

## Results

On the NeRF-DS dataset (7 real-world dynamic specular scenes), SpectroMotion achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all scenes, with headline values of 26.80 PSNR / 0.8843 SSIM / 0.1761 LPIPS on 'As' and 26.49 PSNR / 0.8657 SSIM / 0.1889 LPIPS on 'Press'. On the HyperNeRF dataset (dynamic but non-specular scenes), the method reports 22.22 PSNR, 0.6088 SSIM, and 0.3295 LPIPS, trailing 4DGS (24.89 PSNR) and NeRF-DS (23.65 PSNR) on PSNR/SSIM but achieving the best LPIPS. The method achieves real-time rendering (>30 FPS on RTX 4090) when the Gaussian count is below 178k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the NeRF-DS dataset (dynamic specular scenes), SpectroMotion decisively outperforms all six baselines on every scene for all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). The largest margins are against Gaussian Shader (e.g., +4.91 PSNR on 'As', +5.91 on 'Bell') and GS-IR (e.g., +5.22 PSNR on 'As'), which are static-scene methods that struggle with motion. Against the closest dynamic baselines, SpectroMotion leads Deformable 3DGS by +0.76 PSNR on 'As' and +1.50 on 'Bell', and 4DGS by +1.95 PSNR on 'As' and +1.63 on 'Bell'. On the HyperNeRF dataset (dynamic but non-specular), SpectroMotion trails 4DGS (24.89 vs. 22.22 PSNR) and NeRF-DS (23.65 vs. 22.22 PSNR) on PSNR and SSIM, but achieves the best LPIPS (0.3295 vs. 0.3422 and 0.3972), suggesting better perceptual quality despite lower pixel-level fidelity. The paper acknowledges this gap, noting the method's strength is specifically in specular rendering rather than general dynamic scene reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpectroMotion sits at the intersection of two previously separate 3DGS research threads: dynamic scene reconstruction (Deformable 3DGS, 4DGS) and specular/reflective rendering (GaussianShader, GS-IR). The paper explicitly notes that no prior 3DGS method has combined both capabilities, and its core contribution is bridging this gap by grafting a PBR specular pipeline (Cook-Torrance with GGX) onto a deformation-field-based 3DGS framework. The technical novelty lies in the residual normal correction for deformed Gaussians—addressing the fact that shape changes during deformation invalidate the shortest-axis-as-normal heuristic—and in the deformable environment map, which extends static IBL cubemaps to handle time-varying lighting without re-optimizing the full environment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3DGS method to produce photorealistic novel views of real-world dynamic specular scenes, filling a practical gap for applications like AR/VR, robotics, and digital twins where moving reflective objects are common. The coarse-to-fine training strategy and the oblateness-scaled normal residual are reusable components that could benefit other 3DGS extensions dealing with deformation. However, the method remains limited to monocular video input, requires three sequential training stages, and still exhibits failure cases; generalization to multi-view dynamic specular capture, topologically changing scenes, and larger-scale environments remain open.
