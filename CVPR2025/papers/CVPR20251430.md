## Problem

Existing visual anomaly detection methods are tightly coupled to specific domains (industrial, logical, medical), each requiring custom architectures and per-category training on large normal-sample sets. This domain- and category-specific paradigm prevents unified evaluation, limits cross-domain transfer, and imposes heavy development and training costs. The paper asks whether a single training-free model can detect anomalies across all these domains using only a handful of normal reference images at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251430/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, UniVAD receives one query image and K reference normal images (all resized to 448×448) of the target category, plus fixed textual prompts describing 'normal' and 'anomalous' states encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. It produces a pixel-level anomaly score map that fuses a structural anomaly map (from patch matching) and a logical anomaly map (from component-level graph modeling). The image-level score is derived by taking either the max (for localized defects) or the mean (for globally different samples like histopathology slides) of the pixel-level map, a choice made per dataset. No gradient updates or domain-specific training occur; all image encoders (CLIP-L/14@336px, DINOv2-G/14) remain frozen.

## Method

UniVAD is a three-stage, training-free pipeline. First, the Contextual Component Clustering (C³) module segments the image into semantic components by combining RAM for object tagging, Grounded SAM for initial masks, and K-means clustering on frozen DINOv2/CLIP features to control segmentation granularity, resolving SAM's tendency toward inconsistent mask sizes. Second, the Component-Aware Patch Matching (CAPM) module detects structural anomalies by restricting cosine-distance patch matching to within-component subsets and adding an image-text similarity term against normal/anomalous CLIP text embeddings. Third, the Graph-Enhanced Component Modeling (GECM) module detects logical anomalies (missing, added, misplaced components) by treating each component as a graph node, building an adjacency matrix from pairwise cosine similarities, applying a graph attention operation to aggregate relational context, and combining the resulting deep feature distances with geometric feature distances (area, color, position). The final anomaly map is a weighted sum of the structural and logical score maps, with all weights fixed a priori (α=β=γ=1/3, φ=ψ=δ=η=0.5).

## Results

Under the 1-normal-shot setting, UniVAD achieves state-of-the-art performance across all nine datasets spanning industrial, logical, and medical domains. On MVTec-AD, UniVAD reaches 97.8% image-level AUC and 96.5% pixel-level AUC, outperforming the best domain-specific baselines (AnomalyGPT: 94.1/95.3). On MVTec LOCO (logical), UniVAD scores 71.0% image-level AUC vs. 62.2% for ComAD, and on BrainMRI (medical), it achieves 80.2% vs. 69.7% for MedCLIP. The paper reports an average improvement of 6.2% in image-level AUC and 1.7% in pixel-level AUC over the best domain-specific method per domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniVAD outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset in the 1-normal-shot setting. Against the strongest industrial baselines, UniVAD leads AnomalyGPT by 3.7% image-level AUC on MVTec-AD (97.8 vs. 94.1) and WinCLIP by 4.7% (97.8 vs. 93.1). For logical anomaly detection on MVTec LOCO, UniVAD surpasses ComAD (the domain-specific SOTA) by 8.8% image-level AUC (71.0 vs. 62.2) and PatchCore by 9.0%. In the medical domain, UniVAD beats MedCLIP by 10.5% on BrainMRI (80.2 vs. 69.7) and by 29.5% on LiverCT (70.0 vs. 40.5). Under the 4-abnormal-shot medical setting (Table 2), UniVAD also wins across all six datasets, e.g., 94.1% vs. 92.4% (MVFA) on BrainMRI and 87.5% vs. 81.2% on LiverCT, confirming its advantage even when competitors are allowed to train on a few abnormal samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniVAD extends the patch feature matching paradigm (PatchCore, WinCLIP) by constraining matching within semantically segmented components and adding image-text similarity scoring, while its Contextual Component Clustering module builds on ComAD's clustering-based segmentation by fusing it with vision foundation models (RAM, Grounding DINO, SAM) to handle few-shot settings where pure clustering fails. The Graph-Enhanced Component Modeling module introduces graph attention over component-level features to capture inter-component relationships, a departure from prior logical anomaly methods that evaluate components in isolation. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant 'one-category-one-model' and domain-specific architecture paradigm by proposing a single training-free pipeline that operates uniformly across industrial, logical, and medical anomaly detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single training-free model can match or exceed domain-specific trained methods across nine datasets in three distinct fields, UniVAD offers a practical path toward standardizing anomaly detection research and reducing the engineering burden of maintaining separate pipelines per domain. The main limitation is computational latency from chaining multiple heavy foundation models (RAM, Grounding DINO, SAM, CLIP/DINOv2), which constrains real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to domains beyond the three tested (e.g., video, 3D medical imaging), whether the reliance on open-vocabulary segmentation degrades on highly textured or low-contrast industrial surfaces, and whether the fixed hyperparameter weighting (all set to 1/3 or 0.5) is robust across unseen categories.
