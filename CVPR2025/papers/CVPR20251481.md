## Problem

Reconstructing sharp 3D scene representations from motion-blurred multi-view images remains challenging because blurry images alone cannot recover fine-grained details. While event cameras provide blur-free microsecond-level brightness measurements, existing event-assisted 3D reconstruction methods (e.g., Ev-DeblurNeRF) treat each RGB channel as brightness in the EDI formulation, leading to over-smoothed details or color artifacts. The paper addresses how to simultaneously achieve accurate color and well-defined edge details in a 3DGS framework by properly modeling the brightness-to-color relationship and leveraging generative priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251481/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of blurry multi-view RGB images paired with corresponding event streams captured during the same exposure window (40 ms synthetic, 1000 ms real-world at 346×260 resolution), along with camera poses estimated via SfM on EDI-recovered sharp images. It produces a trained 3D Gaussian Splatting representation that renders sharp deblurred novel views; in Stage 2 (DiET-GS++), it additionally renders a 2D latent residual per Gaussian that is added to the VAE-encoded rendered image and decoded to produce the final output. The diffusion model uses the ground-truth blurry image as conditional input to the UNet since no clean reference is available.

## Method

DiET-GS employs a two-stage training strategy. Stage 1 optimizes 3DGS with four loss terms: a blur reconstruction loss simulating motion blur via temporal averaging, an event reconstruction loss matching simulated log-brightness changes to observed events, an EDI loss with three sub-components (brightness-domain supervision via a learnable camera response function for fine details, RGB-domain supervision for accurate color, and a cycle-consistency regularization term), and an RSD loss from a pretrained diffusion model. The key insight is that modeling EDI in both the brightness domain (with a learnable CRF bridging RGB-to-intensity) and the RGB domain enables mutual compensation between detail sharpness and color fidelity. Stage 2 (DiET-GS++) freezes all Stage 1 parameters and adds zero-initialized per-Gaussian feature vectors that render a 2D latent residual, optimizing solely with RSD to fully exploit the diffusion prior without interference from scene-specific event supervision.

## Results

On the synthetic EvDeblur-Blender dataset, DiET-GS achieves 26.69 PSNR, 0.8607 SSIM, and 0.1064 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline Ev-DeblurNeRF (24.76 PSNR, 0.8038 SSIM, 0.1788 LPIPS) by +1.93 dB in PSNR and a 40% reduction in LPIPS. On the real-world EvDeblur-CDAVIS dataset, DiET-GS reaches 34.22 PSNR and 0.0496 LPIPS, again surpassing Ev-DeblurNeRF (32.30 PSNR, 0.0571 LPIPS). DiET-GS++ further improves no-reference quality metrics, achieving 59.91 MUSIQ and 0.2960 CLIP-IQA on synthetic data and 50.44 MUSIQ and 0.2415 CLIP-IQA on real-world data, at a slight cost to PSNR/SSIM due to generative-model-guided refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiET-GS dominates all seven baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across both datasets. On synthetic data, it beats the closest competitor Ev-DeblurNeRF by +1.93 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.1788 to 0.1064 (a 40% improvement in perceptual quality); on real-world data the gap is +1.92 dB PSNR and LPIPS drops from 0.0571 to 0.0496. The naive deblurring-then-3DGS pipelines (MPRNet+GS, EDI+GS, EFNet+GS) perform worst on perceptual metrics, with EDI+GS being the strongest among them (23.69 PSNR synthetic) but still trailing DiET-GS by over 3 dB. Frame-only methods BAD-NeRF and BAD-GS are consistently outperformed, confirming that event streams provide critical high-frequency information. DiET-GS++ trades a small PSNR/SSIM drop (−0.46 dB synthetic, −1.06 dB real-world) for substantial gains in no-reference quality: +2.24 MUSIQ and +0.0191 CLIP-IQA on synthetic, +4.64 MUSIQ and +0.0204 CLIP-IQA on real-world, indicating that the diffusion prior in Stage 2 produces more natural and visually pleasing results even when pixel-level fidelity slightly decreases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based deblurring 3D representation line (E-NeRF, E2NeRF, Ev-DeblurNeRF) to the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework, while introducing a novel multi-faceted EDI constraint that operates in both the brightness domain (via a learnable camera response function) and the RGB domain, plus a cycle-consistency regularization term. It departs from Ev-DeblurNeRF [3] by arguing that treating each RGB channel as brightness distorts color, and instead models the RGB-to-brightness mapping explicitly. For the diffusion prior component, it builds on DiSR-NeRF's [20] RSD optimization but proposes a simpler framework where 3D Gaussians directly render latent residuals, eliminating the manual feature-map creation and post-hoc synchronization step. The two-stage training strategy (scene-specific event supervision first, then diffusion-only refinement) is a novel design choice motivated by the observed weakening of diffusion guidance under joint optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for combining real-captured event data with generative priors in 3D reconstruction, addressing a genuine tension between data-driven fidelity and prior-driven naturalness. The learnable camera response function in the EDI formulation is a reusable component that could benefit other event-based reconstruction tasks. However, evaluation is limited to two small datasets (4 synthetic + 5 real scenes), and the method requires synchronized event streams, which constrains real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the two-stage strategy generalizes to other restoration tasks (e.g., de-noising, super-resolution) and whether the approach scales to larger, more complex scenes.
