## Problem

Infrared UAV target detection under temperature-dependent nonuniformity bias fields has not been addressed in a unified manner. Existing approaches either detect directly on degraded images (causing missed detections) or apply NUC as a separate preprocessing step, where the correction module receives no supervision from the detection task and thus cannot enhance detection-conducive information. No prior work jointly optimizes both NUC and UAV detection in a single end-to-end framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded infrared UAV image of size 640×512 as input and simultaneously produces a corrected infrared image and UAV target bounding boxes. The NUC module does not predict a full corrected image directly; instead it estimates 10 polynomial coefficients (degree-3 bivariate polynomial) that parameterize the spatially smooth bias field, which is then subtracted from the input. The full pipeline runs at 32 FPS on an RTX 4090, and the NUC module can be decoupled and plugged into any existing detector as a lightweight preprocessing stage.

## Method

UniCD unifies NUC and detection through three mechanisms. First, a lightweight bias-field prediction network combines a Swin Transformer-based global encoder (GBFE) and a spatial-attention-based local encoder (LBFE) on a downsampled input, followed by five 3×3 convolutions and an FC layer to predict the 10 coefficients of a degree-3 bivariate polynomial that models the bias field. Second, a target-enhancement and background-suppression (TEBS) auxiliary loss applies binary cross-entropy against ground-truth target masks at four backbone stages of the DANet detector, strengthening target discriminability without adding parameters. Third, a bias-robust (BR) self-supervised loss computes the average cosine similarity between feature maps extracted from the clear reference image and the corrected image at each backbone stage, pulling the corrected image's features toward the detection-relevant representation and resolving the optimization conflict between pixel-level correction and feature-level detection. The full system is trained end-to-end with L_uni = L_det + L_BR, after pre-training the NUC module with an MAE loss on coefficient prediction.

## Results

On the synthetic IRBFD-syn dataset, UniCD achieves PSNR=31.961, SSIM=0.9827, Precision=0.999, Recall=0.822, and 32 FPS, outperforming all Direct and Separate baselines on every metric simultaneously. On the real IRBFD-real dataset, UniCD attains SCRG=1.286, P=0.994, and R=0.901, surpassing the best Separate method (AHBC+YOLO11L: SCRG=1.146, P=0.940, R=0.633) by a large margin in recall. The method maintains real-time throughput (32 FPS) while the Separate strategies with traditional NUC (Liu, Shi, AHBC) drop below 1 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among Direct detection baselines, DAGNet achieves the highest precision (0.994) but suffers from low recall (0.635) due to bias-field-induced contrast loss; DINO and YOLO11L show moderate P/R (0.904/0.640 and 0.963/0.602 respectively). Among Separate correction-then-detection pipelines, DMRN+YOLO11L yields the best correction quality (PSNR=24.467) but still lags UniCD by 7.5 dB in PSNR and 0.27 in recall; traditional methods (Liu, Shi, AHBC) produce poor corrections (PSNR 13–17) and drop below 1 FPS. On the real dataset (Table 2), UniCD (P=0.994, R=0.901) outperforms the best Separate method AHBC+YOLO11L (P=0.940, R=0.633) by 0.268 in recall, and also beats Direct DAGNet (P=0.992, R=0.871) in both precision and recall. The scalability ablation (Table 9) further shows that plugging the UniCD NUC module into existing detectors (YOLO11L, DAGNet, MSHNet) boosts recall by 0.34–0.69 over their uncorrected counterparts, confirming the module's plug-and-play utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DANet as its detection backbone and extends the joint low-level/high-level vision paradigm established by IA-YOLO and BAD-Net (designed for visible-light adverse weather) to the infrared NUC + UAV detection setting. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant correction-then-detection pipeline by formulating NUC as a low-dimensional polynomial coefficient prediction problem (10 coefficients for a 3rd-order bivariate polynomial) rather than full image-to-image translation, and by coupling it end-to-end with detection via a detection-guided self-supervised loss. The NUC module itself combines a Swin-Transformer-inspired global encoder with spatial-attention CNN local encoders, drawing from both the model-driven NUC literature (polynomial bias field modeling) and the data-driven DL correction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of IRBFD (50,000 annotated infrared UAV images with controlled nonuniformity levels) fills a notable gap in the infrared small-target detection literature, where prior benchmarks lacked systematic nonuniformity variation. The NUC module's extreme lightness (0.4M parameters, 1.7 GFLOPs) and demonstrated plug-and-play scalability into existing detectors (Table 9) make it practically deployable on edge devices for real-time UAV surveillance. Open questions include whether the 3rd-order polynomial model generalizes to more complex or spatially varying bias fields beyond the synthetic training distribution, and whether the framework extends to other infrared sensing tasks (e.g., maritime or ground-vehicle detection) where nonuniformity patterns differ.
