## Problem

Achieving high-fidelity 3D Virtual Try-On that supports any-view rendering remains an open challenge. Existing approaches either rely on costly 3D scanning (not fully virtual), use text-to-image diffusion models whose learned concepts are semantic rather than pixel-level (sacrificing fidelity), or apply 2D VTON methods that cannot generate multi-view images with 3D consistency. No prior method simultaneously preserves intricate garment details (text, logos, patterns) and produces coherent results across all 360° viewing directions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D human model in Gaussian Splatting representation, paired front and back garment images (segmented from clothed human images via parse maps), and multi-view clothing-agnostic body images rendered at random azimuth angles from the 3D model. Multi-view images are cropped to the person bounding box and resized to 768×576. At inference, 32 views are uniformly sampled around the subject and edited in two batches of 16, then reconstructed into a new Gaussian Splatting 3D model that supports arbitrary 360° rendering.

## Method

VTON 360 reformulates 3D VTON as a consistent multi-view 2D inpainting problem by exploiting the equivalence between a 3D model and its rendered views, extending a Stable Diffusion V1.5-based latent diffusion VTON network with three key mechanisms. First, a pseudo-3D pose representation replaces 2D DensePose with SMPL-X normal maps encoded by a lightweight PoseEncoder, providing geometrically consistent surface orientation across views. Second, a Multi-view Spatial Attention (MVAttention) layer models inter-view correlations via a correlation matrix C computed from camera rotation matrices (C_ij = cosine of angular disparity), modulating attention scores between features from different viewing angles. Third, a multi-view CLIP embedding augments the garment CLIP token with sinusoidal positional encoding of the camera rotation matrix, injected into cross-attention to make garment features view-aware. Training proceeds in two stages: single-view training to establish feature extraction and generation, followed by multi-view training (M=8 random views) to learn cross-view consistency through MVAttention.

## Results

On Thuman2.0, VTON 360 achieves a DINO similarity of 0.633, substantially outperforming GaussCtrl (0.577), TIP-Editor (0.569), and DreamWaltz (0.556) in garment texture preservation. On MVHumanNet, the gap widens further: VTON 360 reaches 0.623 DINO similarity versus 0.521 for GaussCtrl, 0.512 for TIP-Editor, and 0.495 for DreamWaltz. In the user study (50 participants), VTON 360 receives 97.54% quality votes and 96.46% alignment votes on Thuman2.0, and 95.69% / 96.92% on MVHumanNet, while the best baseline (GaussCtrl) receives only 1.08% / 1.38% and 1.69% / 1.23% respectively. On CLIP directional consistency, VTON 360 scores 0.923 (Thuman2.0) and 0.933 (MVHumanNet), slightly below TIP-Editor (0.939 / 0.948), which the authors attribute to SDS-based smoothing in those methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTON 360 decisively outperforms all three baselines on DINO similarity (garment-to-person texture alignment), gaining +0.056 over GaussCtrl (0.633 vs 0.577) on Thuman2.0 and +0.102 over GaussCtrl (0.623 vs 0.521) on MVHumanNet, indicating far superior preservation of fine garment details such as text, stripes, and logos. In the user study, VTON 360 dominates overwhelmingly: it receives 97.54% quality and 96.46% alignment votes on Thuman2.0, while the strongest baseline (GaussCtrl) receives only 1.08% and 1.38%. On CLIP directional consistency, VTON 360 is slightly behind TIP-Editor (0.923 vs 0.939 on Thuman2.0; 0.933 vs 0.948 on MVHumanNet), but the authors argue this metric is inflated for SDS-based methods that smooth 3D representations and produce flatter textures. DreamWaltz, which regenerates 3D humans from text prompts, performs worst on DINO similarity (0.556 / 0.495) and user votes, confirming that text-driven generation cannot retain pixel-level garment fidelity. Overall, VTON 360 wins on the most perceptually relevant metrics (texture preservation and human preference) while trading a small margin on the consistency score that favors smoother, less detailed outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D VTON framework of OOTDiffusion [53] and its GarmentNet architecture [9] into a multi-view 3D-consistent setting, using 3D Gaussian Splatting [25] as the underlying 3D representation and a PoseEncoder from Animate Anyone [22] for pseudo-3D pose encoding. It departs from SDS-based 3D VTON methods (DreamVTON, TeCH, DreamWaltz) that rely on semantic T2I concepts and sacrifice pixel-level fidelity, and from GaussianVTON [6] which formulates 3D VTON as scene editing but cannot ensure multi-view garment consistency. The key conceptual move is reframing 3D VTON as a unified multi-view 2D inpainting problem with explicit cross-view attention and camera-conditioned embeddings, rather than optimizing a 3D representation directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses what the authors identify as an open challenge—high-fidelity 3D VTON with any-view rendering—by showing that a carefully augmented 2D VTON network can produce 3D-consistent results without SDS or iterative 3D optimization, which is both more faithful to input garment textures and more computationally tractable. Limitations include the requirement for paired front/back garment images and a pre-existing 3D human model, and the two-stage training pipeline adds complexity. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to garments without paired views, to dynamic/animated try-on, and whether the multi-view spatial attention mechanism scales to very large view counts beyond the 32 views tested.
