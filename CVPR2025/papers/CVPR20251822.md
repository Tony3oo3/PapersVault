## Problem

Detecting and segmenting small objects (often <0.1% of image area) in medical images via text prompts remains difficult because standard transformer attention wastes computation on irrelevant pixels and introduces distracting noise, while existing sparse attention mechanisms rely on rigid hierarchical structures ill-suited to the variability and location uncertainty of small objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251822/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a medical image (CT, MRI, etc.) and a free-form natural language text prompt (e.g., 'neoplasm in lung CT') as input. The image is encoded by a Hiera or Focal-L backbone into multiscale visual features and a full-resolution semantic map, while the text is encoded via UniCL into a sequence of embeddings. The output is a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask (probability after sigmoid) for the object described in the prompt, produced without any spatial prompts such as bounding boxes or points.

## Method

BoltzFormer replaces full cross-attention with a Boltzmann attention sampling mechanism: an ensemble of m latent queries (initialized via text-conditioned self-attention) iteratively compute a Boltzmann distribution over the image using their current state, sample N patches from that distribution, and attend exclusively to the sampled visual features. A temperature annealing schedule τ_ℓ = τ_0/(1+ℓ) starts with high temperature (broad exploration) in early layers and cools toward focused exploitation in later layers, mimicking a reinforcement learning exploration-exploitation tradeoff. After L layers, the ensemble predictions are aggregated by a PiGMA module that combines query-ensemble averaging with a lightweight two-layer convolutional pixel-grounded correction on the original image. The modular Boltzmann sampling block can be dropped into any existing transformer decoder, and the approach reduces attention computation by an order of magnitude while improving mean Dice by 3–12 points over prior methods on small objects.

## Results

BoltzFormer achieves a mean Dice score of 75.2% (FocalL backbone), 74.5% (Hiera-BP), and 73.8% (Hiera-S) across seven medical image segmentation benchmarks spanning object sizes from 0.002% to over 20% of image area. On small objects (<1% image area), BoltzFormer reaches 71.4% Dice, outperforming the next-best method SEEM (68.9%) by 2.5 points and SAM (64.5%) by 6.9 points, while the gap on large objects narrows to just 0.4 points over SEEM. The method reduces attention computation by an order of magnitude compared to full attention, with only 10% of visual features sampled per layer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM (67.0% avg) and SAM 2 (65.6% avg) with the same Hiera-S backbone, BoltzFormer (73.8%) gains 12.4 and 12.6 absolute Dice points respectively, with the largest per-dataset margins on MSD-Colon (+13.2 over SAM) and MSD-Lung (+8.8 over SAM 2). Against SEEM (71.5% with Hiera-S, 73.8% with Hiera-BP), BoltzFormer improves by 2.1–2.3 points on average, with the advantage concentrated on small objects (71.4% vs. 68.9% Dice on <1% area targets). BoltzFormer also surpasses the pre-trained BiomedParse foundation model (73.0% avg) by 0.8–2.2 points depending on backbone, winning on every individual dataset. Finally, a single BoltzFormer model outperforms 35 separately trained nnU-Net expert models (67.3% avg) by 6.5–7.9 points, demonstrating that the text-prompted end-to-end approach eliminates the need for per-class supervision while still exceeding task-specific performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

BoltzFormer extends the Mask2Former/SEEM lineage of transformer mask decoders for text-prompted segmentation, replacing the hard-thresholded masked attention of Mask2Former and the ground-truth-mask-guided sampling of MP-Former with a learned probabilistic Boltzmann distribution over the image. The annealing temperature schedule borrows the exploration-to-exploitation principle from simulated annealing and reinforcement learning, but applies it to spatial attention sampling rather than policy optimization. Conceptually, the paper distinguishes itself from long-sequence sparse attention methods (Longformer, Sparse Transformer) by sampling to *focus* on relevant regions rather than to *approximate* full attention, and from Deformable DETR by learning a stochastic spatial distribution rather than relying on fixed deformable patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude reduction in attention computation while simultaneously improving small-object segmentation accuracy (3–12 Dice points over prior SOTA) addresses a core efficiency-accuracy tradeoff in transformer-based medical image analysis, making text-prompted segmentation of tiny structures (lung nodules, tumor lesions) more practical at scale. The modular Boltzmann sampling block can be dropped into existing transformer decoders, lowering the adoption barrier. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumetric data, whether the stochastic sampling introduces reproducibility concerns in clinical settings, and how performance holds when object locations are highly ambiguous or when multiple small objects of the same class coexist in a single image.
