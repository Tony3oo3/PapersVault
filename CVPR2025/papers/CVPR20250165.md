## Problem

Existing MLLM fine-tuning datasets for visual document understanding contain only brief question-answer pairs that lack detailed contextual and spatial information, leading to hallucinations and poor comprehension of complex layouts. The paper asks how to build contextually grounded training data and devise effective training pipelines that generalize across the wide variety of document types (PDFs, charts, webpages, receipts, formulas, diagrams) with their diverse semantic structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250165/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a document image (dynamically split into up to 12 sub-images of 448×448 resolution) and a text question. It produces a two-round output: first, an adaptive markup language representation (Markdown, LaTeX, HTML, JSON, TikZ, or plain text) wrapped in special type tokens that serves as intermediate context, and second, a final text answer derived from both the image and the extracted context. The markup language type is not specified by the user but is autonomously selected by the model based on the document type.

## Method

The approach uses a two-stage training pipeline on an InternViT + InternLM backbone with dynamic resolution. Stage 1 pretrains on DocMark-Pile (3.8M pairs) across six markup language conversion tasks (TXT, Markdown, LaTeX, HTML, JSON, TikZ), each tailored to specific document types. Stage 2 fine-tunes on DocMark-Instruct (624k chain-of-thought annotations) constructed by first converting images to markup languages with the pretrained model, then using ChatGPT-3.5 to extract relevant context for each question. The key mechanism is a two-round conversational template where the model autonomously determines the appropriate markup format and extracts contextually relevant information as an intermediate rationale before formulating the final answer, mimicking human scan-then-retrieve reasoning. Cross-entropy loss is applied to both the context and answer rounds during training.

## Results

On downstream document understanding (Table 2), DocMark-2B achieves 87.8 on DocVQA, 79.8 on ChartQA, 82.5 on AI2D, 813 on OCRBench, and 18.8 on MathVision, outperforming InternVL2-2B on all eight benchmarks. DocMark-8B reaches 84.2 on ChartQA, 86.2 on AI2D, 823 on OCRBench, 78.9 on WebQA, and 21.1 on MathVision, surpassing InternVL2-8B on 6 of 8 benchmarks. On specialized markup generation tasks (Table 1), DocMark-8B achieves 73.4 on HME100K (vs. 64.3 for CAN-ABM), 68.5 on FUNSD (vs. 55.4 for Nougat), and 0.64 on DaTikZ-test (vs. 0.68 for DT-CL_7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocMark-2B outperforms InternVL2-2B on all eight downstream benchmarks, with the largest gains on AI2D (+8.3), OCRBench (+30), and MathVision (+3.1). DocMark-8B beats InternVL2-8B on six of eight benchmarks (ChartQA +2.0, AI2D +2.5, OCRBench +29, WebQA +1.4, MathVision +0.9, TextVQA +0.6) but trails on DocVQA (89.8 vs. 90.8) and InfoVQA (68.3 vs. 72.8). Against larger specialist models, DocMark-2B surpasses CogAgent-17B on DocVQA (+6.2), InfoVQA (+16.7), and ChartQA (+11.4) despite being 8.5× smaller, though it lags on TextVQA (74.8 vs. 76.1). DocMark-2B also dominates Monkey-9B, TextMonkey-9B, UReader-7B, and DocPedia-7B across all shared benchmarks by wide margins (e.g., +21.3 on DocVQA vs. Monkey). On specialized markup tasks (Table 1), DocMark-8B achieves 73.4 on HME100K (vs. 64.3 for CAN-ABM) and 68.5 on FUNSD (vs. 55.4 for Nougat), but slightly trails DT-CL_7B on DaTikZ-test (0.64 vs. 0.68). Overall, the adaptive markup generation pipeline yields consistent improvements over the InternVL2 backbone with substantially less training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the InternVL/InternVL2 architecture (InternViT encoder + InternLM backbone with dynamic resolution) but departs from the dominant line of document understanding work (UReader, Monkey, DocOwl, LLaVA-Next, CogAgent) that primarily improves visual perception through higher resolution, multi-encoder compositions, or OCR pretraining. Instead of enhancing the perception pipeline, it reframes document understanding as an adaptive code-generation problem: the model first produces a structured markup language (Markdown, LaTeX, HTML, JSON, or TikZ) as an intermediate chain-of-thought rationale before answering. This positions the paper at the intersection of MLLM instruction tuning and program synthesis, leveraging the LLM's existing code-reading capabilities rather than pushing the vision encoder further.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: existing document QA fine-tuning data consists of bare question-answer pairs that encourage hallucination and fail to teach spatial/structural reasoning. The two introduced datasets (3.8M pretraining pairs, 624k CoT annotations) are substantial and publicly released, making them immediately useful to the document-understanding community. However, the CoT annotations are generated by ChatGPT-3.5 rather than verified by humans or stronger models, raising quality concerns; the two-round inference adds latency; and the approach is inherently tied to document-type images with recoverable source code, limiting generalization to arbitrary visual content. An open question is whether the adaptive markup step remains beneficial when the LLM backbone is scaled significantly larger, or whether it becomes redundant as the model's internal representations improve.
