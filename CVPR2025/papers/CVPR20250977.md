## Problem

Existing 3D generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: SDS-based approaches are slow and unstable, multi-view reconstruction methods produce artifacts on complex or self-occluded geometries, and 3D-native diffusion models lack surface detail due to limited training data and high-dimensional representations. Crucially, none of these methods support interactive user editing to refine generated geometry, limiting their practical utility in 3D modeling workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250977/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a single reference image or a text prompt as input. Internally, a pre-trained multi-view diffusion model (e.g., MVDream or Wonder3D) expands this into a set of multi-view images that condition the 3D generation. The output is a watertight 3D mesh with detailed surfaces, produced in approximately 25 seconds total. In interactive mode, users additionally provide a binary paint mask over specific mesh regions to direct localized geometry editing via the refiner.

## Method

CraftsMan3D is a two-stage pipeline inspired by a craftsman's workflow. Stage 1 generates a coarse mesh using a 3D-native DiT diffusion model operating on a Perceiver-based latent set representation (Shape VAE with TSDF fields); the DiT is conditioned on multi-view images whose features are modulated by camera parameters via adaLN, enabling view-aware generation without requiring precise back-projection. Stage 2 refines surface details by rendering normal maps from the coarse mesh, enhancing them with a ControlNet-Tile normal diffusion model (with training-free cross-view attention for consistency), then optimizing mesh vertices via differentiable normal rendering. Two key regularizations stabilize this optimization: Poisson Normal Blending removes low-frequency global distortions from the diffusion-enhanced normals, and Relative Laplacian Smoothing penalizes deviation from the initial coarse shape's Laplacian coordinates rather than forcing them to zero, preventing shape shrinkage. A winding-number-enhanced visibility check in the data pipeline improves watertight mesh conversion success from 60% to 80% on Objaverse.

## Results

On the GSO dataset (30 shapes), CraftsMan3D achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0291 and IoU of 0.5347 in ~5 seconds, outperforming all 3D-native generative baselines (best prior: Michelangelo at CD 0.0404, IoU 0.4002). On a self-occlusion subset from Objaverse, the method surpasses the reconstruction-based InstantMesh (CD 0.03943 vs. 0.04909; IoU 0.53215 vs. 0.50151). For mesh refinement, CraftsMan3D attains the highest CLIP similarity (0.2821) while running in ~20 seconds, compared to Fantasia3D (~15 min) and Latent-NeRF (~1 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 3D-native generative models, CraftsMan3D outperforms all five baselines on both CD and IoU: Michelangelo (CD 0.0404→0.0291, IoU 0.4002→0.5347), Shap-E (0.0436→0.0291, 0.3584→0.5347), Point-E (0.0426→0.0291, 0.2875→0.5347), One2345++ (0.0437→0.0291, 0.3386→0.5347), and One-2-3-45 (0.0629→0.0291, 0.4086→0.5347). Against SDS-based methods (Realfusion, Magic123) and the reconstruction-based zero123, the gains are even larger in both accuracy and speed (minutes-to-hours reduced to seconds). The one notable loss is against InstantMesh on the standard GSO set (CD 0.0187, IoU 0.6353), but on a harder self-occlusion subset from Objaverse, CraftsMan3D reverses the result (CD 0.03943 vs. 0.04909; IoU 0.53215 vs. 0.50151), suggesting the GSO distribution is too simple to stress-test the method. For the refinement stage, CraftsMan3D beats both Fantasia3D (CLIP 0.2821 vs. 0.2567) and Latent-NeRF (0.2821 vs. 0.2725) while being 45× and 180× faster respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the latent-set diffusion paradigm established by Michelangelo and 3DShape2VecSet, adopting their Perceiver-based VAE and DiT backbone, while introducing multi-view image conditioning (via pre-trained MV diffusion models) to improve zero-shot generalization over text-only or single-image conditioning. The geometry refinement stage repurposes ControlNet-Tile for normal map enhancement and continuous remeshing for vertex optimization, combining these with a novel Poisson Blending step and relative Laplacian smoothing to stabilize detail enhancement. The key conceptual departure from prior 3D native generators (CLAY, Direct3D, Michelangelo) is the craftsman-inspired two-stage workflow—coarse generation followed by an interactive, user-controllable surface refiner—which no prior 3D generation system supports.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interactive geometry refiner addresses a practical gap in 3D generation pipelines: users can now locally edit surface details without re-running the full generation, making the output directly usable in 3D modeling software. The winding-number-enhanced preprocessing pipeline (60%→80% watertight conversion) is a practical contribution that could benefit other 3D-native training efforts struggling with Objaverse data quality. Open questions remain around the controllability of the latent set diffusion model (the authors acknowledge this explicitly) and the absence of texture generation, which limits the system's readiness for production 3D asset pipelines. The 25-second end-to-end latency is competitive with reconstruction-based methods while offering better handling of self-occlusion and complex topology.
