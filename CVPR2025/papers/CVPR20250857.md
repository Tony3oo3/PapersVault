## Problem

Existing multiview human mesh recovery methods either use feed-forward regression that cannot iteratively reference complementary views, or volumetric aggregation that is severely degraded by the frequent, severe occlusions present in real-world environments. There is no method that fully leverages multiview observations through iterative refinement while remaining agnostic to the number of cameras, their configuration, and calibration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250857/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N static RGB views of a person (typically 4, but any number) at 384×384 resolution for heatmap extraction and 256×256 for the Transformer backbone, along with optional camera extrinsics. It produces view-dependent SMPL shape (β ∈ ℝ¹⁰) and pose (θ ∈ ℝ²⁴ˣ³) parameters that can be consolidated into a single 3D mesh or selected per-view for downstream use. The architecture is fundamentally agnostic to view count and configuration, and can operate with either calibrated cameras (using extrinsics for translation recovery) or uncalibrated cameras (using weak-perspective parameters regressed from image features).

## Method

HeatFormer reformulates multiview HMR as neural optimization by unrolling a Transformer encoder-decoder into 3–4 iterative forward passes that progressively refine SMPL parameters. The key insight is representing body joints as heatmaps and recasting 2D–3D keypoint alignment as 2D–2D heatmap alignment, which provides stable spatial gradients for end-to-end backpropagation through the unrolled loop. A novel HeatEncoder consolidates per-joint heatmaps from each view into a single spatially-coordinated heatmap via patch-level self-attention with joint-order and position embeddings, while the decoder cross-attends between these consolidated observations (key/value) and heatmaps generated from the current SMPL estimate (queries), outputting parameter deltas. The HeatEncoder is pre-trained on single-view data to regress SMPL parameters, then frozen while the full model is trained with a composite loss including 3D/2D joint MSE, SMPL parameter MSE, a step-weighted heatmap alignment loss that increases in weight across iterations, a vertex loss to prevent body twisting, and an adversarial pose prior.

## Results

On Human3.6M, HeatFormer (3 iterations) achieves 30.7 mm MPJPE and 23.3 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming the previous best multiview method U-HMR (31.0 / 22.8) and the monocular HMR2.0 (44.8 / 33.6). On MPI-INF-3DHP, HeatFormer reaches 39.8 mm MPJPE, 99.5% PCK, and 72.8 AUC, surpassing PaFF (48.4 / 98.6 / 67.3) and LVS (50.2 / 97.4 / 65.0). On the BEHAVE occlusion benchmark, HeatFormer achieves 51.1 mm MPJPE (Protocol 1) and 34.2 mm MPJPE (Protocol 2), substantially outperforming HMR2.0 (72.2 / 48.1) and ProHMR (111.2 / 74.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HeatFormer outperforms all six compared baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest multiview competitor U-HMR [28], HeatFormer (iter 3) improves MPJPE from 31.0 to 30.7 mm and PA-MPJPE from 22.8 to 23.3 mm on Human3.6M; the gap widens in cross-dataset transfer (Table 5: 56.0 vs. 73.2 mm MPJPE on MPI-INF-3DHP), suggesting U-HMR overfits to Human3.6M. Against PaFF [19], HeatFormer reduces MPJPE by 2.3 mm (30.7 vs. 33.0) and PA-MPJPE by 3.6 mm (23.3 vs. 26.9) on Human3.6M, and by 8.6 mm MPJPE (39.8 vs. 48.4) on MPI-INF-3DHP. Monocular baselines HMR2.0 [13] and ProHMR [26] are substantially outperformed (30.7 vs. 44.8 and 62.2 mm MPJPE), and the advantage is even more pronounced under occlusion on BEHAVE where HeatFormer achieves 51.1 mm MPJPE versus 72.2 (HMR2.0a) and 111.2 (ProHMR). Shape-Aware [30] and Yu et al. [52] are the weakest baselines, with HeatFormer improving PA-MPJPE by over 20 mm in both cases. Notably, HeatFormer's 4-iteration variant (29.5 / 22.4) further narrows the gap to the reference upper bound that uses ground-truth translation (28.6 / 23.1), indicating the remaining error is largely translational.

## Relation to Prior Work

HeatFormer departs from the dominant feed-forward paradigm in human mesh recovery (HMR2.0, U-HMR, PaFF) by reformulating SMPL parameter estimation as an unrolled iterative optimization within a Transformer encoder-decoder. It repurposes the heatmap representation—originally introduced for 2D joint localization (Pavlakos et al., Tompson et al.)—as the alignment signal between view-dependent SMPL projections and observed multiview evidence, replacing the volumetric aggregation or direct keypoint regression used by prior multiview methods. The architecture builds on ViT for image features and AdaFuse for multiview joint detection, but the key conceptual departure is treating the decoder's repeated forward passes as optimization iterations that refine SMPL parameters through heatmap matching, making the method fundamentally agnostic to the number and configuration of views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HeatFormer addresses a practical gap in fixed-multiview monitoring (elderly care, security, robotics) where camera configurations vary and occlusions are severe, offering a single model that adapts to arbitrary view counts without retraining. The cross-dataset generalization results (56.0 vs. 73.2 MPJPE on MPI-INF-3DHP when trained only on Human3.6M) suggest that the neural optimization formulation avoids the overfitting that plagues feed-forward multiview methods. Open questions include extending to multi-person scenes, incorporating temporal information for video, and reducing inference cost from the three unrolled decoder passes. The reliance on AdaFuse for initial joint detection and the single-person assumption limit immediate applicability to crowded or dynamic environments.
