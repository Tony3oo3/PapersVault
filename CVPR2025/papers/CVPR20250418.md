## Problem

Precise blood flow speed measurement in cerebral cortex regions is extremely challenging due to complex vascular architecture and flow dynamics. Existing techniques like Optical Doppler Tomography (ODT) are costly, require complex hardware control, and suffer from inherent angle artifacts that cause drastic flow errors and vessel disruptions. No prior work directly estimates blood flow speed from OCTA images using generic OCT systems, despite OCTA being widely available for vascular structure analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250418/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 8-bit OCTA image of the mouse cerebral cortex (H×W) as input and produces a pixel-wise blood flow speed map of the same spatial resolution as output. During training, paired 16-bit ODT flow measurements serve as pseudo labels, but at inference only the OCTA image is needed. The 8-bit-to-16-bit bit-depth mismatch between input and pseudo label adds an additional difficulty to the regression task.

## Method

OCTA-Flow uses a Swin Transformer encoder-decoder architecture with two key novel modules. The Adaptive Window Fusion (AWF) module extracts multiscale context via three window attention blocks (window sizes 3, 5, 7) with shifted-window attention, then adaptively fuses them using a convolutional gate that generates per-pixel spatial attention weights conditioned on the input vasculature. The Conditional Random Field Decoder (CRFD) cascades three Hierarchical CRF blocks, each employing a Neural CRF unit (window-attention-based fully-connected CRF) to model interdependencies between adjacent feature levels, enforcing local smoothness and consistency in the flow prediction. Training uses ODT data as noisy pseudo labels with a scale-invariant logarithmic loss that compresses the wide dynamic range of flow speeds and mitigates the influence of ODT measurement artifacts.

## Results

On the Anesthetized Dataset, OCTA-Flow achieves an Abs Rel of 0.353±0.042 and RMSE of 6.278±0.480, outperforming the second-best method (Ord Ent) by 0.009 on Abs Rel and 0.150 on RMSE. On the more challenging Awake Dataset (with motion artifacts), OCTA-Flow achieves Abs Rel of 0.318±0.018 and RMSE of 6.037±0.674, with a larger margin over the second-best method (0.041 on Abs Rel, 0.278 on RMSE). The low standard deviation on the Awake Dataset (±0.018 vs. ±0.036 for Ord Ent) indicates notably more consistent predictions under motion artifact conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCTA-Flow outperforms all six baselines on both datasets across both metrics. On the Anesthetized Dataset, the closest competitor is Ord Ent [47] (Abs Rel 0.362, RMSE 6.442), followed by NeuWin [46] (0.366, 6.428); OCTA-Flow improves over Ord Ent by 0.009 Abs Rel and 0.150 RMSE. On the Awake Dataset, the gap widens substantially: OCTA-Flow (0.318, 6.037) beats Ord Ent (0.359, 6.315) by 0.041 Abs Rel and 0.278 RMSE, and NeuWin (0.367, 6.324) by 0.049 and 0.287 respectively. The two diffusion-based methods (Diff Depth [6] and ECoDepth [27]) perform worst overall, with ECoDepth degrading severely on the Awake Dataset (Abs Rel 0.766), suggesting diffusion models struggle with the noisy, artifact-laden inputs. The Swin-based baselines (IEBins, NeuWin, Ord Ent) cluster closely together, indicating that the backbone alone is insufficient and that the proposed AWF and CRF Decoder modules provide the decisive advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of medical imaging and deep regression, departing from established blood flow measurement techniques (ODT, Doppler Ultrasound, Phase-Contrast MRI) by repurposing OCTA images—previously used only for vascular structure analysis—as the input for flow speed estimation. Methodologically, it builds on the Neural CRF implementation from Yuan et al. [46] for its decoder and on Swin Transformer's window attention mechanism, but reconfigures both for a multiscale vascular context problem rather than depth estimation. The pseudo-labeling strategy draws from weakly-supervised learning, using noisy ODT measurements as supervision in the absence of ideal ground truth, a departure from the typical paired-data assumption in dense regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By leveraging the clinically approved and widely available OCTA acquisition pipeline, this work could make quantitative blood flow measurement substantially more accessible than ODT, which requires expensive hardware and complex signal processing. However, the evaluation is limited to mouse cerebral cortex with only 106 samples, and the pseudo-label approach means the model's ceiling is bounded by ODT quality; generalization to human imaging, other brain regions, or different species remains an open question. The robustness to motion artifacts in awake animals is promising but untested at scale, and whether the hemodynamic priors encoded in OCTA transfer across species and vascular beds is unclear.
