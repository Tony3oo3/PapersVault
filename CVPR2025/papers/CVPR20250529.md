## Problem

Existing motion understanding tasks (action recognition, temporal action localization, spatiotemporal action detection, video object segmentation) each address only one or two aspects of motion comprehension, lacking fine-grained spatiotemporal perception, implicit reasoning, or pixel-level visual grounding simultaneously. No prior benchmark requires a model to both reason about motion-related questions with implicit spatiotemporal context AND produce a visually grounded, temporally localized segmentation mask as the answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250529/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a 720p video clip (typically 5–15 seconds) and a motion-related question of one of four types (Causal, Sequential, Counterfactual, Descriptive) that implicitly references the target object without naming it. The output is a binary spatiotemporal segmentation mask M ∈ R^{t'×h×w} where t' ≤ t, meaning the mask is confined to the temporal span in which the queried motion actually occurs rather than spanning the entire clip. This pixel-level, temporally bounded output distinguishes the task from both text-answer video QA and spatial-only referring VOS.

## Method

MORA builds on LISA (LLaVA + SAM decoder) and extends it to video via spatiotemporal pooling from Video-ChatGPT for efficient frame encoding. The key architectural addition is a dedicated [LOC] token that encodes temporal boundary information in the language space; an MLP head decodes this token into a binary temporal mask that gates the SAM decoder's frame-wise outputs, suppressing false activations outside the motion's temporal span. A [SEG] token (inherited from LISA) drives spatial segmentation end-to-end. Training proceeds in two stages: first, 20 epochs of pre-training on Ref-YouTubeVOS and MeViS (converted to QA format) without the temporal module to establish vision-language alignment; then 20 epochs of fine-tuning on the GROUNDMORE training split with the temporal localization branch enabled, supervised by annotated motion timestamps.

## Results

On the GROUNDMORE benchmark, MORA achieves an overall J&F of 23.13 (J=22.79, F=23.46), outperforming the best existing video reasoning grounding model (PG-Video-LLaVA, J&F=11.17) by 11.28 points and the strongest two-stage baseline (SeViLA+SgMg, J&F=22.34) by 0.79 points. MORA's advantage is most pronounced on Counterfactual questions (J&F=26.37 vs. 20.46 for SeViLA+SgMg), while two-stage baselines built on ViLA/SeViLA slightly outperform MORA on Causal (21.62 vs. 21.60) and Descriptive (22.63 vs. 24.53) questions. The paper notes that even the SOTA referring VOS model HTR, which reaches 67.1 J&F on RefYouTubeVOS, drops to only 14.48 on GROUNDMORE, underscoring the task's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MORA outperforms all 12 baseline groups on overall J&F. Among pure RVOS models, SgMg (17.49) is the strongest, yet MORA still leads by 5.64 points; HTR (14.48) and LMPM (13.34) trail further behind. Image reasoning segmentation models (LISA-13B at 6.47, PixelLM-13B at 11.54) perform poorly due to the absence of temporal modeling, scoring below even the random title-based baseline in some cases. Video reasoning segmentation models are the weakest category: VISA (5.31) and PG-Video-LLaVA (11.17) both fall well short, with the paper attributing this to frame-sampling failures and ambiguous LLM responses that cause false-positive grounding. The closest competitor is the two-stage SeViLA+SgMg pipeline (22.34), which MORA edges out by 0.79 J&F overall; however, SeViLA+SgMg surpasses MORA on Descriptive questions (24.53 vs. 22.63), while MORA dominates on Counterfactual (26.37 vs. 20.46) and Sequential (22.43 vs. 21.68) questions, where its dedicated [LOC] temporal localization head provides a clear advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task at the intersection of video reasoning segmentation (VISA, VideoReasonSeg) and referring video object segmentation (MeViS, Ref-YouTube-VOS), combining implicit question-based reasoning with full spatiotemporal pixel-level grounding. The method MORA builds directly on LISA's image reasoning segmentation framework (LLaVA + SAM with [SEG] tokens) and extends it to video via spatiotemporal pooling from Video-ChatGPT, adding a novel [LOC] token and temporal localization head to produce binary temporal masks. Conceptually, it departs from prior video reasoning segmentation by requiring temporal localization (not just spatial grounding) and from referring VOS by replacing explicit referring expressions with implicit motion-related questions that demand causal, sequential, counterfactual, or descriptive reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GROUNDMORE fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously evaluates spatial context, temporal context, motion abstraction, pixel-level output, and implicit reasoning (as shown in Table 1). The dramatic performance drop (HTR: 67.1 J&F on RefYouTubeVOS vs. 10.41 on GROUNDMORE) underscores that current models lack genuine spatiotemporal motion reasoning, and the best MORA baseline still achieves only 27.15 J&F, leaving substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether motion-sensitive LLMs, better temporal modeling beyond a simple binary head, and more sophisticated spatiotemporal pooling can close this gap. The task has practical relevance for security (identifying suspicious behavior), autonomous driving, and human-computer interaction where ambiguous queries must be resolved with grounded visual evidence.
