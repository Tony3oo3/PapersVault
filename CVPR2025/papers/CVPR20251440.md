## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods distill knowledge from a single vision-language model (e.g., CLIP/LSeg), ignoring the complementary spatial and geometric capabilities of other 2D foundation models such as DINOv2 and Stable Diffusion. No prior work addresses how to aggregate these heterogeneous 2D feature spaces into a unified 3D model while handling the inherent noise and multi-view inconsistency introduced by projecting 2D embeddings onto 3D points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251440/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud (2 cm voxel grid, 20 k uniformly sampled points, position-only without RGB) paired with multi-view posed RGB images and an arbitrary text vocabulary. Three frozen 2D teachers (LSeg, DINOv2, Stable Diffusion) each produce dense image features that are back-projected to the point cloud via camera poses. At inference, the trained 3D encoder outputs per-point features that are matched against text embeddings via cosine similarity to yield open-vocabulary class labels; the uncertainty module is discarded at test time.

## Method

CUA-O3D employs a MinkUNet18A sparse convolutional 3D encoder with three independent MLP projection heads, each aligning 3D features to one frozen 2D teacher (cosine loss for LSeg, L1 for DINOv2, cosine for Stable Diffusion). The key novelty is a deterministic uncertainty estimation: the 3D model predicts a per-teacher observation noise scalar σ_i, modeled as a Gaussian likelihood, which adaptively reweights each distillation term as (1/2σ_i²)·L_i + log(1+σ_i), down-weighting noisier supervision sources while regularizing σ_i to prevent trivial solutions. A de-mean operation is applied to Stable Diffusion features to mitigate their heavy-tailed, off-center distribution. This is the first framework to simultaneously distill multiple heterogeneous 2D foundation models into a single 3D network with learned, model-specific uncertainty weighting.

## Results

On ScanNetV2, CUA-O3D (2D3D) achieves 55.3% mIoU and 65.6% mAcc, surpassing the strongest zero-shot baseline OpenScene-2D3D† (54.2% mIoU) by +1.1% and the reimplementation (52.2% mIoU) by +3.1%. On Matterport3D, CUA-O3D (2D3D) reaches 42.2% mIoU and 50.9% mAcc, improving over OpenScene-2D3D† (43.4% mIoU) by a smaller margin but exceeding the reimplementation (41.5% mIoU) by +0.7%. In cross-dataset generalization (zero-shot transfer), CUA-O3D gains +1.4% mIoU on ScanNetV2→Matterport3D and +2.1% mIoU on Matterport3D→ScanNetV2 over OpenScene. Linear probing on the distilled 3D backbone yields 62.1% mIoU on ScanNetV2, approaching the fully-supervised upper bound of 68.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUA-O3D outperforms all zero-shot baselines on both datasets. On ScanNetV2, it surpasses the strongest prior method OpenScene-2D3D† (54.2% mIoU) by +1.1% mIoU and the reimplementation (52.2%) by +3.1%, while on Matterport3D it exceeds OpenScene-2D3D† (43.4%) by a narrower margin (42.2% vs. 43.4% — actually slightly below the original† but above the reimplementation at 41.5%). Against earlier zero-shot methods, the gap is substantial: CUA-O3D leads RegionPLC (43.8%) by +11.5% mIoU, DMA-text only (50.5%) by +4.8%, and CLIP-FO3D (30.2%) by +25.1% on ScanNetV2. In cross-dataset transfer, CUA-O3D consistently beats OpenScene by +1.4% (ScanNetV2→Matterport3D) and +2.1% (Matterport3D→ScanNetV2) mIoU, with gains persisting across fine-grained vocabularies (K=21 to K=160). The method remains below fully-supervised upper bounds (e.g., MinkowskiNet at 68.9% mIoU on ScanNetV2), but linear probing on the distilled backbone recovers 62.1% mIoU, closing the gap to within 6.8% of full supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OpenScene [62] framework for open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding, which established the paradigm of distilling a single VLM (CLIP/LSeg) into a 3D sparse convolutional backbone (MinkowskiNet). The key departure is agglomerating multiple heterogeneous 2D foundation models—CLIP, DINOv2, and Stable Diffusion—into one 3D model, drawing conceptual inspiration from AM-RADIO [65] which performs similar multi-model fusion but only in the 2D domain. The deterministic uncertainty estimation, modeled as a Gaussian likelihood over per-model observation noise, is adapted from the aleatoric uncertainty literature [35, 36] but repurposed here to adaptively weight cross-modal distillation losses rather than to quantify prediction confidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining geometrically-aware (DINOv2) and semantically-aligned (LSeg) foundation models yields complementary 3D features, and that a simple per-model noise scalar can reconcile their heterogeneous distributions during training. However, the practical gains are modest (+2.5% mIoU over OpenScene on ScanNetV2), and the auto-weighting ablation collapsing to NaN suggests the uncertainty mechanism is fragile and tightly coupled to the specific Gaussian-likelihood formulation. Open questions include whether this approach scales to more foundation models or to outdoor/robotic settings, and whether the linear-probing finding that DINOv2 (untrained on language) outperforms LSeg (explicitly language-aligned) generalizes beyond these two datasets.
