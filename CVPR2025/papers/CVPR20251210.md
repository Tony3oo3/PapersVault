## Problem

Existing CLIP-based zero-shot counting methods exhibit poor text sensitivity, consistently failing to count the text-specified category when it differs from the majority object class in an image. This limitation is further masked by evaluation bias in standard benchmarks like FSC-147, where annotations overwhelmingly correspond to the numerically dominant class, creating an illusion of strong text-guided performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251210/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text prompt specifying the target object category. At inference, the image is processed via a 384×384 sliding window with full overlap (stride 384), and overlapping density map regions are averaged. The image is encoded into a compact latent space via a frozen VAE encoder, while the text is encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder; only the U-Net and the proposed correction module are trainable. The output is a pixel-level density map whose integral yields the object count.

## Method

T2ICount extracts features from a single denoising step (t=1) of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net for computational efficiency, but this sacrifices the multi-step text-image alignment that diffusion models normally build. To compensate, a Hierarchical Semantic Correction Module (HSCM) operates on four multi-scale feature maps in a cascaded fashion: a Semantic Enhancement Module performs bidirectional text-image cross-attention to produce similarity maps, and a Semantic Correction Module uses those maps to gate and redirect features toward text-relevant regions at each scale. A novel Representational Regional Coherence Loss (L_RRC) supervises the intermediate similarity maps by constructing Positive-Negative-Ambiguous (PNA) supervision from fused cross-attention maps of the U-Net, which reliably delineate foreground regions even when single-step attention is category-insensitive, thereby solving the challenge of identifying negative pixels without instance-level annotations. The U-Net and HSCM are fine-tuned with a reduced learning rate (1/10 of base) to preserve pre-trained knowledge, while the VAE encoder and CLIP text encoder remain frozen throughout training.

## Results

On FSC-147 (test set), T2ICount achieves MAE 11.76 and RMSE 97.86, outperforming the best prior zero-shot method GeCo (MAE 13.30, RMSE 108.72) and the best CLIP-based method CounTX (MAE 15.88, RMSE 106.29) by 25.9% in MAE and 7.9% in RMSE. On the newly introduced FSC-147-S subset (where the text-specified class is the minority), T2ICount achieves MAE 4.69 and RMSE 8.06, reducing MAE by 85.1% and RMSE by 86.9% over the best competitor (PseCo, MAE 39.01, RMSE 61.34). On the cross-dataset CARPK benchmark, T2ICount achieves MAE 8.61 and RMSE 13.47, placing second only to CounTX (MAE 8.13, RMSE 10.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2ICount outperforms all eight zero-shot baselines on FSC-147 test, with the largest margins over CLIP-based methods: it reduces MAE by 25.9% (11.76 vs. 15.88) and RMSE by 7.9% (97.86 vs. 106.29) over CounTX, and by 33.3% in MAE over CLIP-Count (11.76 vs. 17.78). The closest competitor is GeCo (NeurIPS'24) at MAE 13.30 / RMSE 108.72, still trailing T2ICount by 1.54 MAE and 10.86 RMSE. On the critical FSC-147-S subset, T2ICount (MAE 4.69, RMSE 8.06) dominates every baseline by an order of magnitude: the next-best PseCo scores MAE 39.01 / RMSE 61.34, while CLIP-Count and DAVE both exceed MAE 48. On CARPK, T2ICount (MAE 8.61, RMSE 13.47) is competitive but slightly behind CounTX (MAE 8.13, RMSE 10.87) in both metrics, and marginally behind VA-Count (MAE 10.63, RMSE 13.20) in RMSE, indicating that cross-dataset generalization remains a relative weakness compared to the in-domain FSC-147 results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CLIP-based paradigm in zero-shot object counting (CLIP-Count, VLCounter, CounTX) by repurposing a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) as the feature backbone, extracting single-step denoising features from the U-Net decoder rather than fine-tuning a CLIP image encoder. The key conceptual shift is that diffusion models, trained with CLIP-like text guidance at scale, provide richer pixel-level prior knowledge, but the efficiency of single-step extraction weakens text-image alignment—motivating the Hierarchical Semantic Correction Module and the Representational Regional Coherence Loss as corrective mechanisms. The paper also positions itself against the broader class-agnostic counting literature (few-shot, reference-less) by arguing that zero-shot text-guided counting is the most general formulation, while simultaneously critiquing the FSC-147 benchmark for masking text insensitivity through its majority-class annotation bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a systematic evaluation bias in FSC-147—where single-label annotations overwhelmingly correspond to the majority object class, creating an illusion of text sensitivity in CLIP-based models—is a useful diagnostic that the field should take seriously. The FSC-147-S subset (196 images) provides a sharper probe of conditional counting, though its small size limits statistical confidence in the dramatic 85% MAE reduction. Open questions include whether the single-step diffusion feature approach generalizes to more complex, cluttered scenes beyond FSC-147's relatively simple compositions, whether the cross-attention-based PNA supervision transfers to datasets without clean foreground-background separation, and whether the diffusion backbone's advantage persists as CLIP-based counting methods continue to improve. The CARPK cross-dataset result (second-best MAE) suggests reasonable but not dominant generalizability.
