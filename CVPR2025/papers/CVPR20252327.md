## Problem

Existing transfer-based adversarial attacks on vision-language models rely on uni-modality targets (e.g., only the target image embedding) to guide perturbation learning, neglecting the semantic correspondences between vision and text modalities and producing sub-optimal adversarial examples. Additionally, there is no unified and comprehensive attack success rate (ASR) computation strategy for evaluating response generation tasks, with prior work relying on labor-intensive human evaluation or coarse metrics like CLIP scores that lack human-interpretable judgment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252327/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (224×224 from ImageNet-1K validation) and a target reference text (sampled from MS-COCO captions, refined by GPT-4 to extract key visual entities) as inputs, and produces a single adversarial image with an L∞-bounded perturbation (default ε=8/255) that is visually imperceptible. The adversarial image is then fed to a frozen victim VLM (e.g., LLaVA, ViECap, Unidiffuser) to elicit a targeted text response. Evaluation uses both CLIP text-encoder scores across five encoder variants and a proposed LLM-based ASR where GPT-4 assigns scores of 1 (targeted success), 0.5 (fooled-only), or 0 (failure) with step-by-step reasoning.

## Method

Chain of Attack (CoA) is a transfer-based black-box attack that iteratively refines adversarial examples through explicit step-by-step multi-modal semantic updates. At each iteration, a pretrained image-to-text model (ClipCap) generates a fresh caption for the current adversarial image, and a Modality-Aware Embedding (MAE) is computed by fusing the CLIP image and text encoder outputs with a balancing hyperparameter α. The perturbation is optimized via Projected Gradient Descent using a Targeted Contrastive Matching (TCM) loss that maximizes the cosine similarity between the adversarial MAE and the target MAE while simultaneously minimizing similarity to the clean MAE, with a margin term γ controlling the desired separation. This chain-of-attacks process—where each step conditions on the evolving multi-modal semantics of the previous adversarial example—yields richer semantic alignment than single-step uni-modality attacks, achieving 98.4% targeted ASR on ViECap and 94.2% on Unidiffuser with only a CLIP surrogate and no victim-model access.

## Results

On the five victim VLMs (ViECap, SmallCap, Unidiffuser, LLaVA-7B, LLaVA-13B), CoA achieves Ensemble CLIP scores of 82.3, 67.2, 75.2, 48.7, and 45.8 respectively, with targeted LLM-based ASR of 98.4%, 56.8%, 94.2%, 14.5%, and 12.3%. CoA gains 6.9%, 2.1%, 7.4%, 7.5%, and 1.1% relative CLIP score improvements over the second-best method on each victim model. The fooled-only ASR (measuring the ability to generate unrelated responses) reaches 99.5%, 66.5%, 98.9%, 28.4%, and 24.3% on the same models, substantially exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoA consistently outperforms all three baseline families across every victim VLM on both CLIP score and LLM-based ASR. Against AttackBard [17] and Mix.Attack [47], which rely on uni-modality visual targets, CoA achieves dramatically higher targeted ASR (e.g., 98.4% vs. 15.5% on ViECap; 14.5% vs. 2.0% on LLaVA-7B), confirming that multi-modal semantic guidance is critical for effective transfer. Against the strongest baseline MF-ii [54] (which uses multi-modal fusion but without the chain-of-attack update), CoA gains 6.9 percentage points in targeted ASR on ViECap (98.4% vs. 76.6%), 9.7 points on SmallCap (56.8% vs. 47.1%), 4.2 points on Unidiffuser (94.2% vs. 90.0%), and 11.9 points on LLaVA-7B (14.5% vs. 2.6%). The gap is most pronounced on LLaVA-7B and LLaVA-13B, where baselines struggle to produce targeted responses (ASR < 4%) while CoA achieves 12–15%, suggesting that the step-by-step semantic evolution is especially beneficial for larger, more robust models. On the fooled-only metric, CoA also leads across all models, with the largest margin on LLaVA-7B (28.4% vs. 4.7% for MF-ii).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of transfer-based adversarial attacks on vision-language models initiated by Zhao et al. (NeurIPS 2024) and Dong et al. (AttackBard), which craft perturbations using surrogate models to fool black-box VLMs. The key departure is the introduction of multi-modal semantic evolution: prior methods guide perturbation learning with a single uni-modality target (e.g., target image embedding only), whereas CoA explicitly updates both image and text embeddings at each step via Modality-Aware Embeddings and a Targeted Contrastive Matching objective, creating a step-by-step 'chain' of semantic transitions. The LLM-based ASR evaluation also departs from prior CLIP-score or NLP-metric approaches by using GPT-4 as a judge with human-interpretable reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results demonstrate that even large VLMs (LLaVA-13B) remain vulnerable to black-box targeted attacks, with CoA achieving 12.3% targeted ASR where prior methods achieve under 4%, and the LLM-based ASR provides a more interpretable and comprehensive evaluation framework likely to be adopted by future robustness studies. A notable open question is whether the chain-of-attack semantic evolution generalizes beyond image captioning to other VLM tasks (e.g., visual question answering, multi-turn dialogue) and whether the observed vulnerability gap between targeted and untargeted attacks on large models can be closed by targeted defenses. The paper does not explore any defensive mechanisms, leaving the practical mitigation of these attacks as an open problem.
