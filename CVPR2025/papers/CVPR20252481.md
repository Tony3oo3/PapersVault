## Problem

Training-free class-agnostic 3D instance segmentation methods (e.g., Open3DIS) lift dense 2D masks from many views into 3D and merge them via heuristic criteria, producing large numbers of redundant and inconsistent 3D proposals due to over-segmentation. The lack of cross-view object consistency and the absence of a principled merging mechanism make these methods both inaccurate and computationally expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252481/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a posed RGB-D video sequence (T frames of H×W color and depth images with per-frame extrinsics and a shared intrinsic matrix) together with the corresponding 3D point cloud (N points with spatial coordinates and RGB), which is pre-partitioned into L superpoints via a normal-based graph cut. RGB-D frames are subsampled at an interval of 10. The output is a set of Q binary 3D instance masks (each an N-dimensional vector over the point cloud) with associated confidence scores, ready for downstream class-agnostic, open-vocabulary, or open-ended segmentation tasks.

## Method

Any3DIS replaces unsupervised 3D mask merging with 2D mask tracking. First, Farthest Point Sampling selects initial superpoints, and for each one a 'pivot view' is identified as the frame where the superpoint and its KNN neighbors are jointly most visible; SAM-2 then segments the object in the pivot view and tracks it forward and backward across all frames, with re-prompting at reappearance frames to handle occlusions beyond SAM-2's 7-frame memory window. The resulting 2D mask tracks are lifted to 3D by selecting superpoints whose projections overlap any track mask above an IoU threshold τ=0.5. A dynamic programming algorithm (Algorithm 1) then refines the 3D candidate by traversing views sequentially and at each step choosing whether to retain the current superpoint set or add all newly visible superpoints, maximizing a multi-view alignment objective that rewards points inside 2D masks and penalizes points outside them. The entire process is repeated iteratively until no unassigned superpoints remain, yielding the final set of 3D proposals.

## Results

On ScanNet++ (class-agnostic 3DIS, 1,554 classes), Any3DIS with SAM2-L achieves AP 22.2 / AP50 35.8 / AP25 47.0, surpassing Open3DIS (AP 17.9 with the same SAM2-L backbone) and Open3DIS with the higher-quality SAM-HQ segmenter (AP 20.7). On ScanNet200 (198 classes), Any3DIS reaches AP 32.5 with 2D-only proposals and AP 42.5 when combined with 3D proposals from ISB-Net, outperforming Open3DIS (AP 31.5 / 41.5 respectively). The method also reduces runtime by ~10× (488 s vs. 4,226 s) and cuts the number of 3D proposals nearly in half (484 vs. 931) compared to Open3DIS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Any3DIS consistently outperforms Open3DIS [19], the strongest prior method, across all three evaluation settings. On ScanNet++ class-agnostic 3DIS, Any3DIS (SAM2-L) gains +4.3 AP over Open3DIS (SAM2-L: 17.9) and +1.5 AP over Open3DIS (SAM-HQ: 20.7); on ScanNet200 it gains +1.0 AP (32.5 vs. 31.5) in the 2D-only setting and +1.0 AP (42.5 vs. 41.5) in the 3D+2D setting. In the open-vocabulary setting, Any3DIS beats Open3DIS by +3.1 AP on ScanNet++ (12.9 vs. 9.8, both SAM2-L) and by +2.1 AP on ScanNet200 (25.8 vs. 23.7, 3D+2D). In the open-ended setting, Any3DIS surpasses the Point-Wise baseline [18] by +1.7 AP on ScanNet++ (20.1 vs. 18.4) and +3.1 AP on ScanNet200 (19.1 vs. 16.0). Training-based methods such as Segment3D [12] (AP 19.0) and SAM-Graph [10] (AP 15.3) are also outperformed on ScanNet++ despite relying on ground-truth supervision. Notably, Any3DIS achieves these gains while running ~10× faster and producing roughly half the number of 3D proposals compared to Open3DIS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct evolution of Open3DIS (same group), shifting the core paradigm from 'segment all 2D masks independently across views, then heuristically merge in 3D' to 'track 2D masks temporally via SAM-2, then lift consistent tracks to 3D.' It builds on SAM-2's video tracking capability and repurposes it with 3D geometric cues (superpoint-based pivot view selection) to maintain cross-frame consistency. The dynamic programming formulation for 3D mask optimization replaces the unsupervised hierarchical merging that plagues prior training-free methods like Open3DIS, SAI3D, and SAMPro3D, which all suffer from over-segmentation due to inconsistent per-frame masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× runtime reduction (488s vs. 4,226s) while simultaneously halving redundant proposals and improving AP is practically significant for downstream robotics and AR applications where inference speed matters. The method's plug-and-play nature—swapping SAM-2 for SAM-2.1 or adding 3D proposals from ISB-Net—suggests it will benefit from future foundation model improvements without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the approximate dynamic programming solution (which commits to all-or-nothing superpoint inclusion per view) can be tightened, and whether the approach scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes where superpoint partitioning and multi-view consistency become more challenging.
