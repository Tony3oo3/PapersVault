## Problem

Existing visual hallucination evaluation (VHE) benchmarks for MLLMs suffer from non-image-specific hallucinatory item (hitem) selection, lack explicit linkage to the three major causes of hallucination (visual ambiguity, multi-modal input inconsistency, and counter-common-sense content), and are too small to prevent rapid performance saturation. This paper introduces PhD, a much larger and more structured benchmark that explicitly targets all three causes through four distinct evaluation modes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250028/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample consists of an RGB image (either a daily MS-COCO image or an AI-generated counter-common-sense image) paired with a binary Yes/No visual question, optionally preceded by a specious or incorrect text context. The MLLM produces a single-word Yes or No answer, and performance is aggregated into the PhD Index, defined as the harmonic mean of recall on Yes and No questions (a model always answering one way scores 0, random guessing scores 0.5). The full dataset contains 102,564 VQA triplets spanning 20 mode-task combinations (4 modes × 5 tasks), with perfectly balanced Yes/No splits per mode.

## Method

PhD is constructed via a ChatGPT-assisted semi-automated pipeline that adapts TDIUC annotations over MS-COCO images. The pipeline has four modules: (1) task-specific hitem selection, where candidate hallucinatory items are generated per task vocabulary, ranked by CLIP cosine similarity between the hitem+subject phrase and the image, and manually verified; (2) hitem-embedded question generation, where ChatGPT embeds the selected hitem into a No-question and the ground-truth answer into a Yes-question; (3) specious/incorrect context generation, where ChatGPT produces mildly inconsistent or directly contradicting text that is merged with original captions; and (4) CCS image generation, where ChatGPT produces counter-common-sense descriptions (e.g., 'a car with square wheels') that are rendered into images via Doubao and DALL-E3. The resulting dataset supports four evaluation modes—PhD-base (no context), PhD-sec (specious context), PhD-icc (incorrect context), and PhD-ccs (CCS images)—each explicitly probing one of the three hallucination causes.

## Results

On the proposed PhD benchmark (PhD Index, harmonic mean of Yes/No recall), GPT-4o achieves the highest score of 0.812 (random-2k), followed by Claude 3.5 Sonnet (0.746) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (0.691). Among open-source MLLMs, LLaVA-OneVision leads with 0.698, followed by Molmo (0.690) and InternVL-1.5 (0.561), while the weakest model LLaVA-1.1 scores only 0.135. Crucially, PhD reveals a substantial gap between proprietary and top open-source models (0.812 vs. 0.698) that is largely invisible on POPE (0.88 vs. 0.84) and AMBER (0.87 vs. 0.90), demonstrating PhD's superior discrimination ability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a dataset paper, PhD's primary comparison is against existing VHE benchmarks (POPE, AMBER, NOPE, CIEM, ROME) in terms of scale and discrimination. PhD is the largest (102,564 VQA triplets vs. 3,000 for POPE and 14,216 for AMBER) and covers five tasks across four evaluation modes. On discrimination, PhD separates GPT-4o (0.812) from the best open-source model LLaVA-OneVision (0.698) by 0.114, whereas POPE shows only a 0.04 gap (0.88 vs. 0.84) and AMBER actually ranks LLaVA-OneVision (0.90) above GPT-4o (0.87). Among open-source models, LLaVA-OneVision and Molmo (both using Qwen2-72B) lead, while older architectures like LLaVA-1.1 (0.135) and InstructBLIP (0.305) lag significantly. Hallucination mitigation methods (VCD, Woodpecker) provide modest gains: VCD lifts Qwen-VL from 0.488 to 0.560 and LLaVA-1.6-L from 0.423 to 0.511, but neither closes the gap to top models. The paper also reveals that larger LLMs (13B vs. 7B) help on context-heavy modes (PhD-sec, PhD-icc) but can hurt on visual-ambiguity modes (PhD-base, PhD-ccs), a nuance invisible in prior single-mode benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of objective visual hallucination evaluation (VHE) benchmarks—POPE, ROME, NOPE, CIEM, and AMBER—by introducing a ChatGPT-assisted semi-automated pipeline that selects hallucinatory items (hitems) in an image-specific and task-specific manner, departing from co-occurrence-based selection (POPE, ROME), no explicit selection (NOPE, CIEM), or fully manual annotation (AMBER). It builds on TDIUC as its base VQA source and adds three new evaluation dimensions absent from all predecessors: specious context, incorrect context, and counter-common-sense (CCS) images, each explicitly linked to a distinct causal mechanism of hallucination. The resulting four-mode, five-task structure (20 combinations) is substantially larger in scale (102k VQA triplets) and more structured than any prior VHE benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhD is likely to become a standard large-scale VHE benchmark given its size, structured mode-task design, and explicit causal framing, enabling practitioners to diagnose *why* a model hallucinates rather than just *how much*. A key open question it surfaces is how to resolve conflicts between visual input and an MLLM's internal world knowledge (CCS mode), which no current architecture addresses adequately. The finding that larger LLMs improve text-inconsistency robustness but degrade pure visual recognition suggests that vision-language alignment training, not raw LLM scale, is the critical bottleneck—a direction for future model development. Limitations include restriction to binary Yes/No answers and low/middle-level tasks, leaving high-level reasoning hallucination unaddressed.
