## Problem

Existing egocentric video grounding methods for the Natural Language Query (NLQ) task suffer from two key limitations: (1) video features extracted via action-centric video-text pretraining lack fine-grained background object information that NLQ queries frequently target (e.g., a 'measuring tape' casually placed on a table), and (2) the frequent head-turning movements inherent to egocentric videos—which implicitly encode the wearer's attention shifts—are not leveraged for modality alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251091/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed egocentric video (segmented into non-overlapping 16-frame clips) and a natural language question-type query, and outputs start and end timestamps [t_s, t_e] of the answering moment. Object detection (Co-DETR, confidence threshold 0.6) and shot boundary detection (head-movement captions from LAVILA) are performed as auxiliary preprocessing steps. The main branch produces predictions via a multi-scale feature pyramid with anchor points at progressively coarser temporal resolutions, while the shot branch produces per-shot embeddings for contrastive alignment.

## Method

OSGNet comprises a main branch and a shot branch. The main branch integrates three feature streams—video (BiMamba for long-range dependencies), text (transformer encoder), and query-relevant objects (Co-DETR detections encoded via CLIP text embeddings, refined by a cross-attention object encoder)—through a multi-modal fusion module that applies parallel cross-attention for query and object features, combined via a learned gating mechanism, followed by a multi-scale network (1D depthwise convolution + self-attention) and classification/regression task heads. The shot branch segments the video at head-movement boundaries identified from LAVILA captions, extracts query-independent video features via shared BiMamba layers, and aligns shot representations with query sentences using a Q-Former-style encoder trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss. Training proceeds in two phases: pretraining on the NaQ narration dataset (main branch only, localization loss), then fine-tuning with a combined loss of focal + Distance-IoU localization loss and the contrastive loss.

## Results

On Ego4D-NLQ v2 (test set), OSGNet† achieves R@1@0.5 = 19.78 and R@5@0.5 = 32.77, outperforming the strongest baseline GroundVQA (17.63 / 27.70) by 2.15% and 5.07% respectively. On Ego4D-Goal-Step (test set), OSGNet* with order prior reaches R@1@0.3 = 38.83, surpassing BayesianVSLNet* (35.18) by 3.65%. On TACoS (test set), OSGNet with C3D features achieves R@1@0.5 = 48.18, improving over SnAG (44.86) by 3.32%, and with E+I features plus pretraining reaches R@1@0.5 = 55.77.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSGNet† outperforms all six baselines on Ego4D-NLQ v2 across every metric, with the largest gains over GroundVQA (the strongest prior method): +2.15% on R@1@0.5 and +5.07% on R@5@0.5 on the test set. On Ego4D-Goal-Step, OSGNet* with order prior beats BayesianVSLNet* by 3.65% on R@1@0.3 (38.83 vs. 35.18) and by a substantial 9.68% on R@1@0.5 (30.16 vs. 20.48); without prior, OSGNet still edges out EgoVideo by ~1.6% on R@1@0.3. On TACoS, OSGNet (C3D) surpasses SnAG by 3.32% on R@1@0.5 (48.18 vs. 44.86) and by 1.13% on R@1@0.3; with E+I features and pretraining, the gap widens to 55.77% on R@1@0.5. The paper does not report any setting where OSGNet loses to a baseline; the only caveat is that on Ego4D-NLQ v1 (test set), OSGNet† (R@1@0.5 = 15.46) slightly trails RGNet*† (11.69) on R@1@0.3 but leads on R@1@0.5, and the v1 results are less directly comparable due to different validation protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSGNet builds directly on the multi-scale feature pyramid and task-head architecture from GroundNLQ [12], adopts the NaQ narration-based pretraining strategy [37] as its training foundation, and borrows the Q-Former-style learnable-query aggregation from BLIP-2 [19] for the shot branch. Its key departure from prior NLQ methods (GroundVQA, EgoVideo, RGNet) is the explicit injection of fine-grained object features via Co-DETR + CLIP and the introduction of a shot-level contrastive learning branch driven by head-movement captions from LAVILA—two signals that existing egocentric grounding pipelines treat as implicit or ignore entirely. The use of BiMamba (a state-space model) in place of conventional self-attention for long-range video dependency modeling follows the broader 2024 trend toward SSM-based sequence encoders but is applied here in a novel multi-modal fusion context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in egocentric video grounding: prior methods trained on action-centric narrations systematically under-represent background objects that NLQ queries frequently target, and the egocentric camera's head-motion signal is an underexploited structural cue. The ablations confirm both components contribute (object features ~2%, shot contrastive loss ~0.4%), though the shot branch's contribution is modest, raising the question of whether caption-based shot segmentation is the most reliable proxy for attention shifts. Open questions include whether the fixed object-detection threshold (0.6) and the reliance on LAVILA-generated captions generalize to domains with atypical camera motion, and whether the BiMamba-based fusion offers meaningful efficiency gains over transformer self-attention at the sequence lengths typical of untrimmed egocentric videos.
