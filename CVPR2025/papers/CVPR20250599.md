## Problem

RL-based trajectory planners struggle with training inefficiency (model-free rollouts on CPU simulators) and poor performance on large-scale, diverse real-world driving scenarios; no RL method had previously achieved competitive results on the nuPlan benchmark. Additionally, vanilla auto-regressive planners suffer from long-term inconsistency because each time step independently samples from the action distribution, unnecessarily expanding the exploration space and degrading multi-step coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250599/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner consumes vectorized map elements (lane-center/boundary polylines with 9-D attributes, intersection/crosswalk polygons) and agent pose histories (ego + N traffic agents over H past steps, each 10-D), plus routes extracted from the map via graph search and scalar speed values forming a longitudinal-lateral decomposed mode. An Invariant-View Module preprocesses all inputs by transforming to the ego's current coordinate frame, applying KNN filtering, and subtracting historical time indices to yield time-agnostic features. The output is a set of N_mode multi-modal ego trajectories (each a sequence of decoded poses) with associated mode scores, from which a rule-augmented selector picks the final trajectory at inference.

## Method

CarPlanner introduces a consistent auto-regressive formulation where a longitudinal-lateral decomposed mode c (scalar speed × map-derived route) is sampled once from the initial state and held fixed across all time steps, conditioning the policy to produce temporally coherent trajectories without re-sampling. The architecture decomposes into four modules: a frozen non-reactive transition model (trajectory predictor generating all future agent poses in one shot), a query-based Transformer mode selector scoring each mode, an auto-regressive trajectory generator (same Transformer backbone, Gaussian policy head + value head) that decodes the next ego pose per step, and a rule-augmented selector combining mode scores with safety/comfort/progress metrics at inference. Training uses PPO with an expert-guided universal reward (negative displacement error to ground-truth trajectory plus collision/drivable-area penalties), a winner-takes-all mode assignment strategy, and mode dropout to prevent over-reliance on route queries. The IVM and coordinate transformation provide time-agnostic inputs that improve value estimation and closed-loop generalization, while the model-based formulation (neural transition model on GPU) makes RL training time comparable to IL baselines.

## Results

On the Test14-Random benchmark, CarPlanner achieves a closed-loop score (CLS-NR) of 94.07 in the non-reactive setting, surpassing the best IL method PLUTO (91.92) by 2.15 points and the best rule-based method PDM-Closed (90.05) by 4.02 points. In the reactive setting, CarPlanner scores 91.1 CLS-R, slightly below PDM-Closed (91.64) due to training exclusively in non-reactive environments. On Reduced-Val14 (non-reactive), CarPlanner achieves 91.45 CLS-NR with a notably higher progress metric (S-PR = 95.37 vs. 92.68 for PDM-Closed), demonstrating improved driving efficiency while maintaining competitive safety (S-CR = 96.38 vs. 97.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CarPlanner outperforms all IL baselines on CLS-NR, beating the strongest IL method PLUTO (91.92) by 2.15 points and PEP (91.45) by 2.62 points on Test14-Random. Against rule-based methods, CarPlanner surpasses PDM-Closed (90.05) by 4.02 CLS-NR but trails it by 0.54 on CLS-R (91.10 vs. 91.64), which the authors attribute to training only in non-reactive settings. On Reduced-Val14, CarPlanner achieves the highest CLS-NR (91.45) and a substantial progress advantage (S-PR 95.37 vs. 92.68 for PDM-Closed), while PDM-Closed retains a slight edge on collision rate (S-CR 97.01 vs. 96.38). Compared to the concurrent RL work Gen-Drive, CarPlanner leads by 3.92 CLS-NR (91.45 vs. 87.53) and 5.43 S-PR (95.37 vs. 89.94) on Reduced-Val14. The paper also shows that CarPlanner achieves these results without common IL techniques like data augmentation or ego-history masking, highlighting the intrinsic advantage of the RL formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant IL-based planning paradigm (PDM, PlanTF, PLUTO) by formulating trajectory planning as a model-based RL problem, and from prior RL driving work (ASAP-RL, highway/lane-change RL) by adopting a model-based, trajectory-level action space rather than control-level or model-free approaches. Architecturally, it extends vanilla auto-regressive trajectory decoding by introducing a consistent longitudinal-lateral mode representation that remains fixed across time steps, addressing the temporal inconsistency inherent in step-by-step random sampling. The generation-selection framework and vectorized state encoding follow established patterns from PDM-Closed and VectorNet, while the PPO-based training with an expert-guided reward function and Invariant-View Module are the paper's distinctive contributions to making large-scale RL training tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that an RL-based planner can surpass both IL and rule-based SOTAs on the large-scale nuPlan benchmark, validating RL as a viable training paradigm for closed-loop driving planning. However, the method's reliance on expert-guided reward design (displacement error to ground-truth trajectories) inherently constrains the policy to human-level behavior, and the reactive-setting underperformance relative to PDM-Closed reveals a gap in robustness to interactive agents. Open questions include whether reward-free or preference-based RL can unlock behaviors beyond human expertise, and whether the consistent-mode formulation generalizes to reactive or multi-agent adversarial settings without retraining.
