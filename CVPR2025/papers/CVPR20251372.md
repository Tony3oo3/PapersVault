## Problem

Existing multi-concept customization methods either require joint training on all reference images or merging of single-concept models with compatible architectures, making it difficult to compose separately trained heterogeneous models (e.g., Textual Inversion, LoRA, DreamBooth) that users obtain from the community. Methods that avoid retraining, such as Cones 2, introduce inconvenient additional conditioning signals like bounding boxes or segmentation masks, limiting practical usability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251372/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n separately trained single-concept customized models of arbitrary architectures (Textual Inversion, LoRA, DreamBooth, etc.) and a text prompt describing the desired composition as input. No reference images, bounding boxes, or layout annotations are required at inference time. The output is a single RGB image (512×512 via Stable Diffusion v1-5) containing all n specified concepts with correct attribute binding, generated in 30 DPM++ sampling steps with per-step MCG optimization.

## Method

MC² runs n parallel diffusion models (each with a different customized module) on the same latent noise map at every denoising step. At each step, Multi-concept Guidance (MCG) extracts 16×16 cross-attention maps for trigger tokens, then optimizes the latent via gradient descent on two losses: an intra-prompt aggregation loss (maximizing IoU between attention regions of tokens belonging to the same concept) and an inter-prompt disentanglement loss (minimizing overlap between different concepts' activated regions). The updated latent is fed back into the parallel models, whose outputs are combined via semantic merging—a weighted sum analogous to classifier-free guidance. The authors additionally propose attention grounding training, which uses SAM-generated masks to add two auxiliary losses during single-concept model fine-tuning, sharpening the correspondence between trigger tokens and subject regions so that MCG operates on cleaner attention signals.

## Results

On the MC++ benchmark for customized multi-concept generation, MC² achieves the best overall subject fidelity with CLIP-I of 0.658 and DINO of 0.336, while also attaining the highest prompt fidelity (CLIP-T 0.803). In the two-concept setting, MC² shows the largest margin, reaching CLIP-I 0.692 and DINO 0.416 versus the next-best Mix-of-Show (0.670 and 0.394). For compositional text-to-image generation, MC² outperforms all baselines on Full Prompt Similarity (0.356/0.374 for two/three objects), Minimum Object Similarity (0.266/0.235), and Text-Text Similarity (0.833/0.802).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC² outperforms all five baselines on subject fidelity (CLIP-I and DINO) in the overall MC++ evaluation, with the largest gains over Custom Diffusion (+0.040 CLIP-I, +0.055 DINO) and Cones 2 (+0.042 CLIP-I, +0.078 DINO). On prompt fidelity (CLIP-T), MC² edges out Cones 2 and MoLE by a narrow margin (0.803 vs. 0.802), while Cones 2 slightly leads in the three-concept sub-setting (0.806 vs. 0.805). Mix-of-Show is the closest competitor on subject fidelity (CLIP-I 0.647, DINO 0.334) but lags MC² by 0.011 and 0.002 respectively, and trails significantly on CLIP-T (0.731). For compositional generation, MC² surpasses Attend-and-Excite, Divide-and-Bind, CONFORM, and Composable Diffusion on all three metrics (Full Prompt, Min. Object, Text-Text), with the most notable gap over Composable Diffusion in Text-Text similarity (0.833 vs. 0.738 for two objects). The user study further confirms these quantitative findings, with MC² selected as most prompt-consistent 78.75% of the time versus 8.93% for the next-best method (Cones 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

MC² builds directly on the parallel diffusion model composition framework of Composable Diffusion [29], which treats multiple diffusion models as composable energy-based models, and on the cross-attention map extraction strategy from Attend-and-Excite [8]. However, it departs from prior multi-concept customization methods (Custom Diffusion, Mix-of-Show, Cones 2) that require joint training, model merging, or additional spatial conditioning (bounding boxes, masks) by performing all composition at inference time through a novel Multi-concept Guidance (MCG) loss that spatially disentangles concepts via cross-attention refinement. The attention grounding training is adapted from TokenCompose [52], and the semantic merging step generalizes classifier-free guidance to the multi-model setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that users can combine separately trained heterogeneous models (Textual Inversion, LoRA, DreamBooth) from the community without access to training data or additional conditioning, lowering the barrier to multi-concept customization. The MC++ benchmark (2,064 prompts for 2–4 concept compositions) fills a gap left by CustomConcept101, which only covers two-concept compositions. A key limitation is failure on composing two human identities, where cross-attention maps are insufficient to distinguish fine-grained identity features; the authors note a refinement stage could help but omit it for simplicity. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond four concepts, whether it transfers to video generation, and whether the inference-time optimization cost becomes prohibitive at higher resolutions or more sampling steps.
