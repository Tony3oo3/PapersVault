## Problem

Camera-based semantic scene completion methods suffer from a distance-dependent completion imbalance: perspective and occlusion cause projected geometry to become sparse and unreliable in distant regions, leading to significant drops in recall, IoU, and mIoU as distance from the viewpoint increases. This underestimation of distant geometry poses a critical safety risk for autonomous driving systems that must perceive objects far ahead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250089/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ScanSSC takes stereo RGB images as input and follows CGFormer's viewing transformation pipeline, which lifts 2D features into 3D via an LSS feature volume (outer product of image features and discretized depth probability) projected onto a voxel grid using deformable cross-attention. The output is a 3D semantic voxel grid of spatial dimensions (X, Y, Z) with P per-voxel class logits, upsampled from a coarser intermediate grid via trilinear interpolation to match the target resolution.

## Method

ScanSSC introduces two synergistic components designed to refine distant voxels using near-viewpoint context. The Scan Module applies three parallel axis-wise masked self-attention blocks (depth, width, height), each using a near-to-far cascade mask that allows distant voxels to attend to preceding voxels while blocking the reverse; mask shapes are upper-triangular (depth), hourglass (width), and lower-triangular (height), with margin regions in near areas permitting unrestricted interaction. Each axis branch is followed by a spatial mixing network (lightweight ResNet + 3D FPN), and the three outputs are fused via learned voxel-wise softmax weights. The Scan Loss computes cross-entropy between cumulatively averaged logits (accumulated from distant voxels toward near ones) and the corresponding accumulated ground-truth class distributions along each axis, so that distant voxels are repeatedly supervised under diverse regional contexts during training. Together, these components achieve SOTA IoU of 44.54 and mIoU of 17.40 on SemanticKITTI and 48.29/20.14 on SSCBench-KITTI-360.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI hidden test set, ScanSSC achieves SOTA IoU of 44.54 and mIoU of 17.40 with stereo input, surpassing the previous best stereo method CGFormer (44.41 IoU, 16.63 mIoU) by 0.13 IoU and 0.77 mIoU. On SSCBench-KITTI-360, ScanSSC reaches 48.29 IoU and 20.14 mIoU, outperforming CGFormer (48.07, 20.05). Notably, ScanSSC also surpasses the temporal stereo method HTCL-S (44.23 IoU, 17.09 mIoU) despite using only a single stereo frame rather than temporal context. Axis-wise analysis confirms the largest gains appear in distant regions, where ScanSSC achieves 13.19 mIoU in the farthest depth segment versus 7.93 for VoxFormer and 9.31 for CGFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScanSSC outperforms all 13 compared baselines on both benchmarks. On SemanticKITTI, it edges out CGFormer (the strongest stereo baseline) by 0.13 IoU and 0.77 mIoU, and surpasses the temporal stereo method HTCL-S by 0.31 IoU and 0.31 mIoU despite using only a single stereo frame. On SSCBench-KITTI-360, ScanSSC leads CGFormer by 0.22 IoU and 0.09 mIoU, and Symphonies by 4.17 IoU and 1.56 mIoU. The most dramatic gains appear in distant regions: in the farthest depth segment, ScanSSC achieves 13.19 mIoU versus 7.93 (VoxFormer) and 9.31 (CGFormer), a 4–5 point improvement. For side regions (width axis segments 1 and 4), ScanSSC also leads by 0.59 and 1.38 mIoU over CGFormer respectively. Monocular baselines (MonoScene, TPVFormer, OccFormer) trail ScanSSC by 10+ IoU points, underscoring the benefit of stereo input combined with the tri-axis scanning strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScanSSC builds directly on the CGFormer [38] viewing transformation pipeline (LSS feature volume combined with depth-based query proposal) and the autoregressive masked self-attention paradigm from the original Transformer [26]. Its conceptual departure from prior camera-based SSC methods (VoxFormer, CGFormer, Symphonies) is the explicit treatment of distance-dependent completion imbalance: rather than treating all voxels symmetrically, it introduces axis-wise near-to-far cascading masks and a cumulative cross-entropy loss that structurally propagate context from well-established near-viewpoint voxels to uncertain distant ones. The spatial mixing network (ResNet + 3D FPN) and tri-feature fusion with learned weights are standard components repurposed to integrate the three parallel axis-specific scan branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic axis-wise analysis of distance-dependent degradation (Sec. 3.1) provides a clear diagnostic framework that the camera-based SSC community can adopt to evaluate and target this underexplored failure mode. The synergy between the Scan Module and Scan Loss—where each alone yields modest gains but together produce a larger mIoU jump—suggests that architectural and training-level context propagation are complementary rather than redundant. Open questions include whether the hand-tuned margin region ratios (50%/25%/0%) generalize across scenes and sensor configurations, whether the approach extends naturally to multi-frame or surround-camera settings, and whether the inherent depth-estimation error at long range ultimately caps the achievable improvement.
