## Problem

Existing DPO methods for diffusion models propagate preference labels from clean image pairs to all intermediate denoising steps, but generic preference annotations (e.g., from Pick-a-Pic) blend layout and aesthetic judgments that often conflict with pure aesthetic preference. Moreover, the two-trajectory design produces large layout discrepancies between paired images at each step, making it difficult for the model to learn subtle aesthetic differences. Collecting a dedicated aesthetic-only preference dataset is prohibitively expensive, so a cost-efficient solution using existing generic data is needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (SD1.5 or SDXL) using only text prompts (4k prompts from Pick-a-Pic V1, no images or preference labels) as training input, with win-lose pairs generated online during training. The step-aware preference model (SPM) takes a noisy intermediate latent, its timestep index, and the text prompt as input and outputs a quality score. The final output is a LoRA-adapted diffusion model that generates aesthetically improved images from text prompts, evaluated on 500 validation prompts from Pick-a-Pic and 553 GenEval prompts.

## Method

SPO replaces trajectory-level preference propagation with an online, step-by-step mechanism: at each denoising step t, it samples a pool of k candidates from the same noisy latent x_t, uses a step-aware preference model (SPM) to select the highest- and lowest-quality samples as a win-lose pair, and then randomly picks one candidate to initialize the next step. The SPM is built on CLIP with time-conditional adaptive layernorm, initialized from PickScore and fine-tuned on noisy image pairs to assess quality at intermediate timesteps. Because win-lose pairs originate from the same latent and differ by only one (or a few) denoising steps, their differences are confined to fine-grained visual details rather than layout, allowing the model to focus on aesthetics. For SDXL where single-step differences are too small, the method extends to MSPO by performing j inner denoising steps (j=4) to increase candidate diversity. Training uses a revised DPO loss applied per-timestep with LoRA fine-tuning, and SPO is only applied for timesteps t ≤ κ (κ=750) to avoid very noisy early stages.

## Results

On SDXL, SPO achieves the best scores across all four metrics: PickScore 23.06, HPSV2 31.80, ImageReward 1.0803, and Aesthetic 6.364, outperforming Diffusion-DPO (22.64, 29.31, 0.9436, 6.015) and MAPO (22.11, 28.22, 0.7165, 6.096). On SD-1.5, SPO similarly leads with PickScore 21.43, HPSV2 26.45, ImageReward 0.1712, and Aesthetic 5.887, surpassing DDPO, D3PO, and Diffusion-DPO. SPO also converges dramatically faster, requiring only ~20.8% (SD-1.5) and ~4.9% (SDXL) of the GPU hours needed by Diffusion-DPO. On GenEval image-text alignment, SPO scores 55.20 overall, slightly below Diffusion-DPO (59.58) but above vanilla SDXL (53.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPO outperforms all four baselines on every aesthetic and preference metric for both SDXL and SD-1.5. On SDXL, SPO beats Diffusion-DPO by +0.42 PickScore, +2.49 HPSV2, +0.137 ImageReward, and +0.349 Aesthetic; it also surpasses MAPO by +0.95 PickScore and +0.268 Aesthetic. On SD-1.5, SPO leads Diffusion-DPO by +0.45 PickScore, +1.40 HPSV2, +0.060 ImageReward, and +0.382 Aesthetic, and outperforms DDPO and D3PO on all metrics. The one area where SPO trails is image-text alignment: on GenEval, SPO scores 55.20 overall versus Diffusion-DPO's 59.58, though SPO still improves over vanilla SDXL (53.43). The authors attribute this gap to SPO's design focusing on fine-grained detail rather than layout changes. In user studies, SPO wins 58.27% of visual-appeal comparisons against Diffusion-DPO and 52.8% on general preference, confirming the automatic metric gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DPO-for-diffusion lineage (Diffusion-DPO, D3PO), which propagates clean-image preference labels to all intermediate denoising steps along two full trajectories. SPO departs fundamentally by discarding trajectory-level propagation entirely: at each step it samples a small pool of candidates from a shared noisy latent, uses a step-aware preference model (a CLIP variant with time-conditional adaptive layer norm) to identify a win-lose pair with minimal layout difference, and then randomly selects one candidate to seed the next step. The broader RLHF-for-diffusion context (DDPO, DPOK, ReFL, DRaFT, AlignProp) is acknowledged, but SPO's specific contribution is the step-by-step online pair construction that isolates fine-grained aesthetic differences from layout-level discrepancies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: collecting aesthetic-only preference data is prohibitively expensive, and generic preference datasets (e.g., Pick-a-Pic) often encode layout judgments that conflict with pure aesthetic quality. SPO shows that aesthetics can be distilled from generic data by controlling comparison granularity, yielding ~5–20× compute savings over Diffusion-DPO while improving aesthetic scores. Key limitations include inapplicability to flow-matching models (SD3, Flux), limited benefit for image-text alignment, and the exclusion of subjective/cultural dimensions of aesthetics. An open question is whether the step-by-step mechanism can be adapted to deterministic samplers or extended to jointly optimize alignment and aesthetics.
