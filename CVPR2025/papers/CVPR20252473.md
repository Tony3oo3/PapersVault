## Problem

Late fusion interactive segmentation models like SAM extract image embeddings once and fuse them with prompts in later interactions, achieving high efficiency but sacrificing the ability to capture fine details of the prompted target because the original image-prompt interaction is discarded. Early fusion methods capture these details by re-encoding the image each interaction but suffer from high latency. The paper addresses this accuracy-efficiency gap by reintroducing image-prompt synergy into the late fusion pipeline without incurring the cost of full re-encoding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 RGB image and sequential interactive prompts (positive/negative clicks, boxes, or scribbles) as input. The image is encoded once by SAM's ViT-H or ViT-B backbone before interaction begins; during each click, only the lightweight prompt encoder, SAM decoder, and the proposed refiner run. The output is a binary segmentation mask at 256×256 resolution, produced by progressively refining SAM's coarse prediction through global and local refinement stages, with a dynamic selector determining whether to update the full mask or only the locally refined region.

## Method

SAM-REF adds a decoupled lightweight refiner after SAM's mask decoder, operating in a coarse-to-fine manner. The Global Fusion Refiner (GFR) replaces SAM's sparse 1-D prompt vectors with dense disk-based prompt maps, concatenates them with the coarse mask, and fuses the result with the original image and upsampled SAM encoder features through N=3 scale-invariant ResBasicBlocks, each iteration re-adding SAM embeddings to preserve semantic information; an error map then selectively replaces only error-prone regions in SAM's prediction. The Local Fusion Refiner (LFR) uses RoiAlign to crop the largest error region from GFR's output, refines it with the same fusion mechanism, and pastes it back. A Dynamic Selector compares error-map magnitudes across iterations to adaptively choose whether to paste the local refinement onto the global refined mask (early interactions) or onto the previous mask (later interactions when the full mask is already converged). Training follows a two-stage protocol: 320k iterations fine-tuning SAM's decoder, then 80k iterations training the refiner with all SAM components frozen, using a composite loss of normalized focal loss, Dice loss, BCE loss, and weighted focal loss on error regions.

## Results

On the four mainstream benchmarks (GrabCut, Berkeley, DAVIS, SBD), SAM-REF with ViT-H achieves state-of-the-art NoC90 across all datasets (1.36, 1.43, 4.54, 4.44 respectively) and best NoC95 on Berkeley (3.18) and SBD (10.41). On the high-quality HQSeg-44K validation set, SAM-REF (ViT-H) attains the best 5-mIoU (89.6%), NoC90 (5.44), NoC95 (9.16), and NoF95 (566) among all compared methods. The method adds only 0.1s SPC over SAM (0.511s vs 0.413s) and 0.7MB parameters, preserving the low-latency advantage of late fusion while closing the accuracy gap with early fusion methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM (the base model), SAM-REF substantially reduces NoC90 on all four datasets (e.g., 1.88→1.36 on GrabCut, 7.62→4.44 on SBD) while adding only 0.1s SPC. Compared to FocSAM, SAM-REF improves NoC90 on GrabCut (1.44→1.36), Berkeley (1.50→1.43), DAVIS (4.76→4.54), and SBD (5.07→4.44), and NoC95 on DAVIS (10.77→9.10) and SBD (11.67→10.41). Against HQ-SAM, SAM-REF wins on NoC90 for all datasets and NoC95 on DAVIS (9.65→9.10) and SBD (15.08→10.41). Versus the early-fusion SimpleClick, SAM-REF is dramatically faster (0.511s vs 8.24s SPC, ~6.2% of the cost) and matches or beats it on NoC90 for all datasets, though SimpleClick retains a slight NoC95 edge on DAVIS (8.88 vs 9.10) and GrabCut (1.66 vs 2.20). The ViT-B variant of SAM-REF (0.278s SPC, 4.75 NoC90 on DAVIS) matches the speed and accuracy of the fastest early-fusion method FocalClick (0.232s SPC, 4.90 NoC90 on DAVIS) while operating under the late-fusion paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM-REF builds directly on the Segment Anything Model (SAM), preserving its ViT-based image encoder, prompt encoder, and mask decoder while appending a lightweight two-stage refiner (GFR + LFR) that reintroduces the original image into the interaction loop. Conceptually, it departs from prior SAM-based improvements (FocSAM, HQ-SAM) that attempt to extract more detail from the fixed pre-computed embeddings, instead treating the embeddings as a semantic prior and recovering low-level detail by fusing the raw image with dense prompt maps via a small convolutional network. The design borrows the dense prompt representation from RITM and the local crop-and-paste refinement from FocalClick, but embeds both within a late-fusion pipeline to retain SAM's efficiency advantage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because SAM has become a de facto foundation model for segmentation, any method that improves its interactive quality at negligible cost (0.7 MB extra parameters, ~0.1 s SPC) is practically significant and likely to be adopted in downstream applications. The paper demonstrates that the core limitation of late fusion—loss of target-specific detail—can be mitigated without reverting to expensive per-interaction image encoding, opening a path for other foundation models to adopt similar lightweight refinement stages. Open questions include whether the refiner generalizes to multi-object or video settings, whether the fixed threshold in the Dynamic Selector is optimal across domains, and whether the approach extends to other prompt types (text, language) beyond clicks and boxes.
