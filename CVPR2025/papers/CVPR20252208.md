## Problem

Cluttered garment manipulation—retrieving individual garments from piles of entangled, deformable items—remains largely unexplored, as prior work focuses on single-garment tasks or cluttered rigid objects. Two compounding challenges make this difficult: the high-dimensional state space and inter-garment relations obscure which garment to retrieve and where to grasp, and in highly tangled configurations no directly manipulable point may exist, requiring the scene to be reorganized first. Additionally, no simulation environment existed for benchmarking cluttered garment scenarios.

## Input / Output

The system takes a 3D point cloud observation O ∈ R^{N×3} of the garment clutter (captured by a depth sensor placed in front of or above the scene) and produces three per-point affordance maps, each a vector in R^N with scores normalized to [0,1]: a retrieval map for direct grasping, and a pick and place map for the adaptation action. At inference, the point with the highest score in each map is selected; the retrieval affordance is checked for sufficiency (fraction of points scoring >0.9 must exceed 0.1) before committing to retrieval, otherwise the pick-place adaptation loop is triggered and the cycle repeats with a new observation.

## Method

The framework comprises three PointNet++-based modules trained with a hierarchical supervision scheme that decomposes the joint pick-place action space. The Retrieval Affordance Module extracts 128-dim per-point features encoding local geometry, garment structure, and inter-object relations, then passes them through an MLP with sigmoid activation to output a binary retrieval score, trained with BCE loss against actual manipulation success/failure. The Place Affordance Module uses two separate PointNet++ networks (one for the pick point, one for the candidate place point), concatenates their features, and predicts a place score conditioned on the pick point; its supervision signal is whether the post-adaptation retrieval affordance improves by a margin. The Pick Affordance Module uses a single PointNet++ backbone and is supervised by the best place action selected by the trained Place Module, closing the hierarchical loop. Training uses 20,000 offline samples for retrieval (120 epochs) and 8,000 for pick/place (80 epochs), followed by an online adaptation phase where failure cases are collected and mixed with offline data to improve robustness on unseen clutter configurations.

## Results

On the GarmentLab simulation benchmark, GarmentPile achieves retrieval success rates of 0.805 (WashingMachine), 0.819 (Sofa), and 0.792 (Basket), outperforming all baselines by a substantial margin. In real-world experiments with a Franka Panda robot, the method achieves 12/15, 13/15, and 12/15 successes across the three scenarios. The ablation study confirms that the adaptation module contributes the largest gain: removing it drops success rates to 0.712/0.702/0.693, while removing pick or place affordance individually yields intermediate drops (0.724/0.704/0.716 and 0.778/0.743/0.731 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GarmentPile outperforms all three baselines across every scenario in both simulation and real-world settings. Where2Act [31], which predicts primitive-level (grasping/pulling) per-point actionability, achieves the weakest performance among the two learned methods (0.585/0.643/0.624 in simulation), because its affordance is not tailored to the retrieval task and cannot capture garment structure or inter-object relations. Support-M [18], which segments garments via a foundation model and retrieves the top-supported object, performs competitively on the open-space Sofa scene (0.784) but degrades sharply in constrained environments like WashingMachine (0.562) and Basket (0.684) due to severe occlusions and deformations that break segmentation. GPT-Fabric-M [36], which uses GPT-4o to infer spatial relations in garment clutters, performs worst overall (0.463/0.408/0.384) because the high complexity of tangled garments exceeds the model's relational reasoning capability. In the real world, GarmentPile leads by 3–6 successes out of 15 over the strongest baseline (Support-M) in constrained scenarios, while the gap narrows to 1 on Sofa where segmentation is more reliable. Additionally, Support-M and GPT-Fabric-M require 3 s and 8 s inference times respectively, making them less suitable for real-time operation compared to the affordance-based approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: point-level visual affordance learning for manipulation (Where2Act, AdaAfford, foresightful affordance for deformable objects), single-garment manipulation (folding, unfolding, hanging, dressing), and cluttered rigid-object retrieval/rearrangement. It departs from prior work by combining point-level affordance—previously applied to single deformable objects or articulated parts—with a novel affordance-guided adaptation module that iteratively reorganizes the scene before retrieval, a mechanism absent from both the deformable-manipulation and cluttered-rigid-object literatures. The evaluation environment builds on GarmentLab and ClothesNet, extending them from single-garment to multi-garment pile scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of cluttered garment retrieval, a task with clear practical relevance for household-assistive robots (e.g., loading a washing machine, organizing a laundry basket). The adaptation module introduces a useful paradigm—using learned affordance as a signal to decide *when* and *how* to reorganize a scene before attempting the target action—which could generalize beyond garments to other highly entangled deformable settings. Limitations include a maximum of 5 garments, 9 categories, and 3 fixed scenarios; scaling to larger piles, more diverse garment types, and open-world settings remains open. The reliance on a fixed threshold (P_high > 0.1) for triggering adaptation and the offline-then-online training pipeline also raise questions about robustness in truly novel, unbounded clutter.
