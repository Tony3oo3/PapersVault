## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods for LiDAR semantic segmentation under adverse weather exhibit a significant performance gap between 'things' classes (pedestrians, vehicles, cyclists) and 'stuff' classes (road, terrain, buildings), with things mIoU averaging 11.8 points lower. This is a safety-critical bottleneck because things are dynamic and directly associated with collision risk. The paper identifies two root causes: weather corruption degrades semantic-level features causing things to be misclassified as visually dissimilar stuff, and local point drops disproportionately harm small things objects that rely on dense local features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (64-beam for SemanticKITTI, 40-beam for SemanticPOSS) and produces per-point semantic labels across 19 classes (KITTI/STF) or 5 mapped classes (POSS→STF). During training, each scan is processed in two branches simultaneously: a clean branch (after Selective Jittering) and an augmented branch (after Learnable Point Drop simulates weather corruption). The physical beam structure of the LiDAR sensor is explicitly exploited as the unit of local region definition for the distillation loss, rather than arbitrary spatial neighborhoods.

## Method

NTN builds on the SJ+LPD training pipeline (MinkowskiNet-18 backbone) and introduces two complementary loss terms targeting different levels of weather-induced corruption. The Feature Binding (FB) loss constructs superclass prototypes by grouping visually similar classes (e.g., car/bicycle/truck → Vehicle; person/bicyclist/motorcyclist → Person), maintains them in a memory bank via exponential moving average from clean data, and constrains augmented-data features to remain close to their superclass prototype, preventing things→stuff misclassification. The Beam-wise Feature Distillation (BFD) loss slices each scan into individual LiDAR beams and applies an MSE between the mean features of clean and augmented data within each beam, restricted to things classes only; this leverages contextual information from all surviving points in a beam to compensate for dropped points, unlike point-wise distillation which cannot compute a loss for missing points. The total loss combines cross-entropy on both branches with weighted FB and BFD terms (λ_FB=0.5, λ_BFD=0.1).

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI-to-SemanticSTF benchmark, NTN achieves a +2.6 mIoU improvement over the prior state-of-the-art SJ+LPD, with a +4.8 mIoU gain specifically on things classes. The most dramatic per-class gains are +37.1 IoU for motorcyclist, +14.1 for motorcycle, and +5.8 for person. On the SemanticPOSS-to-SemanticSTF benchmark, NTN surpasses SJ+LPD by +7.9 mIoU overall and +15.5 IoU on the person class, establishing a new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTN outperforms all nine baselines on the SemanticKITTI-to-SemanticSTF benchmark, with the largest margin over SJ+LPD (+2.6 mIoU overall, +4.8 mIoU on things). The most striking per-class wins are +37.1 IoU on motorcyclist and +14.1 on motorcycle over SJ+LPD, classes where all prior methods (including DGLSS, DGUIL, and UniMix) score near zero. On the SemanticPOSS-to-SemanticSTF benchmark, NTN beats SJ+LPD by +7.9 mIoU and PointDR by +11.5 mIoU, with a +15.5 IoU gain on person. NTN slightly trails SJ+LPD on a few stuff classes (e.g., building −5.3, fence −9.0) but compensates with large gains on safety-critical things. Compared to the source-only MinkowskiNet baseline, NTN achieves +14.5 mIoU on the KITTI→STF benchmark, demonstrating the substantial benefit of the proposed domain-generalization training scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SJ+LPD (Park et al., ECCV 2025), which established a weather-simulation pipeline using Selective Jittering and Learnable Point Drop for domain-generalized LiDAR semantic segmentation. While prior DGLS-AW methods (PointDR, DGUIL, UniMix) focus on overall robustness through teacher-student frameworks, prototyping, or physical weather simulation, this work departs by identifying and targeting the systematic misclassification of 'things' as 'stuff' under corruption. The two proposed losses—Feature Binding via superclass prototypes and Beam-wise Feature Distillation—repurpose prototype-learning and feature-matching ideas but ground them in the physical beam structure of LiDAR sensors, a structural prior not exploited by prior domain-generalization methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The things-vs-stuff performance gap is a safety-critical blind spot: failing to detect a pedestrian or motorcyclist has far higher accident risk than missing road or terrain, yet prior SOTA methods showed an 11.8 mIoU deficit on things. The beam-wise distillation approach is a practical, architecture-independent technique that any LiDAR segmentation pipeline could adopt, and the +37.1 mIoU gain on motorcyclist demonstrates that previously near-unlearnable tail classes become tractable. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (MinkowskiNet-18), dependence on the SJ+LPD augmentation pipeline, and superclass groupings that are manually defined per dataset. Open questions include whether the beam-wise formulation transfers to non-beam-structured sensors or to other corruption types (e.g., occlusion, sensor degradation).
