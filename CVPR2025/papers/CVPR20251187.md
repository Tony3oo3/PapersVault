## Problem

Multi-view person association fails in challenging scenes where persons share similar appearances (e.g., uniformed soldiers, clinicians in scrubs), because appearance-based Re-ID features cannot distinguish them. Existing robust approaches either require labor-intensive identity annotations or calibrated camera parameters that are impractical to obtain, while prior self-supervised methods only train visual descriptors without incorporating cross-view geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251187/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes synchronized multi-view RGB images from C cameras along with 2D bounding boxes from an off-the-shelf detector (CrowdHuman) as pseudo-labels; no camera intrinsics or extrinsics are required. The number of detected persons P can vary per view due to occlusion. The model outputs per-person embeddings in a shared feature space, from which cross-view associations are obtained via Hungarian matching with a confidence-score threshold to filter false positives.

## Method

Self-MVA uses an encoder-decoder architecture where the encoder maps 2D box corners (via learnable Fourier positional encodings) concatenated with per-view learnable camera embeddings into a unified geometric feature space through fully-connected layers, while appearance features are extracted by a frozen OSNet Re-ID model. Training is driven by a self-supervised pretext task—cross-view image synchronization—that distinguishes whether two images from different views were captured at the same time, using Hungarian matching to convert instance-wise distances into image-wise distances and a triplet loss for optimization. Two additional self-supervised linear constraints reduce the solution space: a multi-view re-projection loss (L1 between decoder-predicted and original box positions) and a pairwise edge association loss that creates pseudo 'edge' persons at geometric midpoints of person pairs to impose linear structure on the learned representations. The final loss is the sum of the synchronization, re-projection, and edge triplet losses, requiring no identity labels or camera calibration.

## Results

On the WILDTRACK dataset, Self-MVA (self-supervised, no annotations) achieves 92.31% precision, 94.34% recall, 91.73% accuracy, and 54.64% IPAA-100, surpassing the fully-supervised ViT-P3DE on all association-accuracy metrics (IPAA-100: 54.64 vs 35.48; ACC: 91.73 vs 89.49). On MVOR, Self-MVA reaches 86.50% AP, outperforming both fully-supervised ViT-P3DE (81.32%) and GNN-CCA (84.70%). On SOLDIERS, it achieves 79.13% AP versus 56.90% for OSNet+ESC, demonstrating strong generalization to indoor scenes with uniform clothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-MVA decisively outperforms all unsupervised baselines: OSNet (AP 16.80) and MvMHAT (AP 4.44) fail to distinguish similarly-dressed persons, while GNN-CCA (AP 4.12, R 0.0) essentially collapses on WILDTRACK. Against the fully-supervised ViT-P3DE, Self-MVA wins on precision (92.31 vs 86.92), recall (94.34 vs 87.01), accuracy (91.73 vs 89.49), and all three IPAA thresholds (54.64/65.60/86.55 vs 35.48/53.10/84.17), but trails on AP (56.68 vs 70.46) and FPR-95 (11.19 vs 5.83), which the authors attribute to AP/FPR-95 not enforcing the one-to-one matching constraint. Compared to OSNet+ESC (which uses calibrated camera parameters), Self-MVA achieves higher precision, recall, accuracy, and IPAA scores while remaining uncalibrated. On MVOR, Self-MVA (AP 86.50) surpasses both ViT-P3DE (81.32) and GNN-CCA (84.70), and on SOLDIERS it reaches 79.13% AP versus 56.90% for OSNet+ESC, confirming robustness in uniform-clothing indoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Jenni & Favaro's (ACCV 2020) self-supervised multi-view synchronization learning from the person-level (distinguishing whether two views of the same person are synchronized) to the scene-level (distinguishing whether two views of the same scene are synchronized), thereby learning instance-level geometric representations without identity labels. It departs from prior self-supervised association work (e.g., MvMHAT) that trains only visual descriptors via cycle consistency, by explicitly encoding fine-grained geometric features through positional encodings and learnable camera embeddings in an encoder-decoder architecture. The method also diverges from geometric association approaches (epipolar constraints, homography-based) that require calibrated camera parameters, instead learning geometric correspondences implicitly through the synchronization pretext task and two linear constraints (re-projection and pairwise edge association).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: the approach requires neither identity annotations nor camera calibration, making it applicable to real-world multi-view deployments where both are typically unavailable. The ability to auto-generate identity labels (reducing manual annotation from 6+ hours to under 1 hour on MVOR) has direct implications for dataset construction in medical and military settings. A key limitation is that the method assumes static cameras, ruling out moving-camera scenarios that are common in practical deployments. Open questions include whether the synchronization-based geometric encoding generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether the approach extends to video-level association with temporal reasoning, and how it performs when the number of persons varies significantly across views due to occlusion.
