## Problem

3D Embodied Reference Understanding (3D-ERU), which combines pointing gestures with language instructions to ground target objects in 3D scenes, remains underexplored. The sole existing dataset (ScanERU) suffers from manually placed avatars positioned too close to targets (rendering language redundant), unrealistic pointing-unaware descriptions, and a model that assumes the human's 3D location is known rather than learning to localize it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250375/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a raw 3D point cloud scene (N points with 3D coordinates, surface normals, and RGB color) that already contains a human avatar performing a pointing gesture, along with a natural-language referring expression. It must simultaneously localize the human within the scene (predicting joint coordinates and left/right pointing hand) and output the 3D bounding box of the referred target object. Unlike prior 3D-REC settings where the human is absent, here the human is an integral part of the point cloud that the model must segment, pose, and reason about.

## Method

Ges3ViG is a unified feed-forward architecture built on a PointGroup detector for 3D object proposals and semantic features, with CLIP providing both multi-view 2D visual features (rendered from three views per proposal) and sentence/token-level language embeddings. A dedicated Gesture Feature Encoder extracts joint coordinates from the human segment via a PointNet-inspired MLP, then refines them through a joint-wise MLP. The key architectural insight is a multi-stage fusion strategy: early fusion concatenates gesture and language token features before a transformer-based reference-scene cross-attention module produces per-proposal confidence scores, followed by a late fusion stage that adds an angular biasing score (cosine similarity between the pointing vector and object-center direction) weighted by a learned left/right hand classifier. Training uses a combined loss spanning object detection (semantic, offset, score), reference understanding (cross-entropy + symmetric contrastive), and human localization (L2 joint regression + left/right classification), applied in two stages: first training the detector and gesture encoder, then freezing them to train the fusion module. The accompanying Imputer framework automates dataset creation via voxelized scene analysis, line-of-sight visibility search, and Gemini-generated pointing-aware language instructions, placing avatars at ~2× the distance of ScanERU to increase gestural reasoning difficulty.

## Results

On the ImputeRefer benchmark, Ges3ViG achieves 70.85% IoU@0.25 and 58.71% IoU@0.5 overall, surpassing the only prior gesture-enhanced model ScanERU (39.54% / 28.84%) by 29.87% in overall IoU@0.5. Against the strongest language-only baseline M3DRefCLIP (65.53% / 49.78%), Ges3ViG gains 8.93% in overall IoU@0.5. In the more challenging 'multiple' distractor setting, the advantage over ScanERU widens to 32.71% in IoU@0.5 (55.77% vs. 23.06%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ges3ViG outperforms all six baselines across every metric and category. Against the closest language-only competitor M3DRefCLIP [37], Ges3ViG leads by 5.32 points in overall IoU@0.25 (70.85 vs. 65.53) and 8.93 points in overall IoU@0.5 (58.71 vs. 49.78), with the gap widening in the 'multiple' distractor setting (55.77 vs. 47.27 IoU@0.5). Against ScanERU [25], the only prior gesture-enhanced 3D-ERU model, Ges3ViG achieves a dramatic 31.31-point gain in overall IoU@0.25 and 29.87 points in overall IoU@0.5, reflecting both the more challenging ImputeRefer dataset (≈2× larger human-to-target distance) and the unified human-localization + grounding architecture. The remaining language-only baselines (3DVG-Transformer [38], HAM [7], 3DJCG [3], RefMask-3D [18]) trail Ges3ViG by 22–36 points in overall IoU@0.5, confirming that neither pure language grounding nor the prior gesture approach matches the combined gesture-plus-language pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D referring expression comprehension (3D-REC) line of work by incorporating pointing gestures, building directly on PointGroup for point-cloud feature extraction and M3DRefCLIP's transformer-based reference-scene fusion and multi-view CLIP rendering strategy. It departs from the sole prior 3D-ERU effort, ScanERU, by replacing manual avatar placement with an automated voxel-based augmentation pipeline (Imputer), regenerating language instructions via Gemini to reflect realistic pointing-augmented speech, and integrating human localization into the model rather than assuming it as ground truth. The 2D gesture-enhanced REC literature (M2Gestic, YouRefIt, COSM2IC) informs the motivation but is not directly transferable due to the 3D point-cloud representation and larger search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ImputeRefer provides the first automated, scalable benchmark for 3D-ERU with a roughly 2× larger human-to-target distance than ScanERU, making gestural reasoning genuinely non-trivial and reducing the risk of models shortcutting to pointing alone. The finding that gesture-only grounding achieves only ~10% IoU@0.5 (vs. 58.71% with language) underscores that pointing and language are complementary rather than substitutable at realistic distances, a result with direct implications for embodied robot instruction design. Open questions include whether the Gemini-generated instructions faithfully capture the full distribution of natural pointing-augmented speech, whether the 9° pointing jitter adequately models real-world pointing error, and whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or multi-room scenes beyond ScanNetV2's indoor settings.
