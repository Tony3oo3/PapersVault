## Problem

Few-step distillation of diffusion models suffers from exposure bias: the student model conditions only on its own most recent prediction, so small estimation errors accumulate across iterative sampling steps and compound into degraded outputs. Existing step distillation methods lack access to the broader ODE trajectory context that could anchor predictions to the teacher's deterministic solution.

## Input / Output

The student model receives the initial noise x_τS plus the full sequence of its own prior predictions [x_τS, x̂_τS−1, …, x̂_τs] as multi-token inputs, along with either a class label (ImageNet-256) or a text prompt (1024p T2I). At each of S denoising steps it outputs the next sample x̂_τs−1; prior steps are stored in a kv-cache so that only the current step's query tokens are computed, keeping inference cost close to standard step distillation (only ~1.1% extra FLOPs for N=6).

## Method

ARD reformulates the distillation objective as an autoregressive factorization p(x_τs−1 | x_τS:τs) over the entire historical trajectory rather than the Markovian p(x_τs−1 | x_τs), so every prediction step retains access to the ground-truth initial noise and earlier (less error-accumulated) predictions. The DiT backbone is modified to accept multiple trajectory inputs simultaneously: a shared patch embedder tokenizes each x_τs, token-wise time-step embeddings distinguish trajectory positions, and a block-wise causal attention mask (M4) enforces the autoregressive ordering during training. An additional inductive bias restricts historical-trajectory attention to only the lower N transformer layers (N=6 optimal), aligning with the observation that lower DiT layers encode coarse-grained structure while upper layers refine details. Training uses a regression loss over all S steps on pre-computed teacher ODE trajectories (optionally augmented with a discriminator loss on the final output), and inference simply appends each new prediction to the kv-cache without any attention mask.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, ARD (N=6) with regression + discriminator loss achieves FID 1.84 in just 4 steps, surpassing the DiT-XL/2 teacher (FID 2.27 at 250 steps) and outperforming all public few-step generative models. The FID degradation from the teacher is 1.43 for ARD versus 7.36 for step distillation — a 5× reduction — while requiring only 1.1% extra FLOPs. On 1024p text-to-image generation (Emu teacher), ARD achieves the smallest teacher-to-student FID drop (2.06) and the highest CompBench average score (59.9%) among all public 1024p distilled models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against step distillation [62] (the closest baseline), ARD achieves FID 4.32 vs 10.25 at 4 steps and 6.29 vs 10.92 at 2 steps, a consistent ~2.4× FID improvement; step distillation also suffers a Recall drop (0.518→0.474) as steps increase, while ARD maintains or improves Recall. With the additional discriminator loss, ARD (FID 1.84) surpasses even the 250-step teacher (FID 2.27), whereas step distillation with R+D only reaches 3.84. In the broader ImageNet-256 comparison (Table 2), ARD at 4 steps beats VAR (FID 1.97, 10 steps), MAR (1.93, 32 steps), and StyleGAN-XL (2.30, 1 step) in both FID and step count. On 1024p T2I, ARD (CompBench AVG 59.9%) outperforms DMD2 (59.5%, 0.9B more params), Lightning (53.7%), and LCM-LoRA 2.8B (52.6%), and shows the smallest teacher-student FID gap (2.06) versus LCM-LoRA (2.66) and step distillation (2.54) from the same Emu teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes step distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022) and progressive distillation (Meng et al., 2023) by reformulating the few-step student as an autoregressive model that conditions on the entire historical ODE trajectory rather than only the most recent denoised sample. The autoregressive decomposition draws from the classical NAR framework (Larochelle & Murray, 2011), and the transformer architecture modifications—token-wise time embeddings, block-wise causal attention masks, and restricting historical inputs to lower layers—adapt the DiT backbone (Peebles & Xie, 2023) to handle variable-length trajectory inputs. The key conceptual departure from prior distillation work is treating the ODE trajectory history as a source of coarse-grained information, an idea supported by attention-weight analysis showing history tokens are predominantly activated in lower transformer layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: ARD achieves FID 1.84 on ImageNet-256 in 4 steps (outperforming the 250-step teacher at 2.27) with only 1.1% extra FLOPs, and scales to 1024p text-to-image generation where it outperforms public distilled models in prompt adherence. The finding that the historical trajectory primarily serves as a coarse-grained information source—validated by attention analysis and reinforced by the N-layer ablation—provides a useful inductive bias principle for future few-step diffusion architectures. Open questions include how the method behaves at very high step counts (where kv-cache memory grows), whether the autoregressive formulation can be combined with distribution-matching or adversarial objectives more deeply, and whether the lower-layer-only design generalizes to non-DiT backbones.
