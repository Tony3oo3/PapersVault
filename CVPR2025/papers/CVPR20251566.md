## Problem

Text-to-image generative models exhibit significant representational biases across demographic groups, yet no established methodology exists to systematically measure and control these harms, particularly for intersectional groups where multiple identity attributes interact. Prior evaluation approaches focus on single axes of identity (e.g., gender alone) or lack a theoretically grounded framework for selecting appropriate representation targets and controlling the granularity of intersectionality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a pre-trained diffusion-based T2I model, and a reference dataset with known demographic statistics (e.g., FairFace for balanced gender/age/race, or context-specific sources like CDC disability data or historical image searches). Rather than operating on raw pixels, MPR is computed over binary attribute vectors indicating group membership, with the function class C (bounded linear functions or binary decision trees of configurable depth) determining the granularity of intersectional measurement. The output is a scalar MPR score quantifying worst-case representational deviation, and optionally a fine-tuned model with reduced disparity.

## Method

The paper extends the Multi-Group Proportional Representation (MPR) metric—originally defined for retrieval—into a general fairness measure for T2I generation, defined as the supremum over a function class C of the absolute difference in expected function values between the generated and reference distributions. Closed-form expressions are derived for two function classes: bounded linear functions (yielding a quadratic form in the sample mean difference) and binary decision trees of depth ℓ (reducing to 2× the maximum total variation distance over all ℓ-attribute marginals). For fine-tuning, MPR serves as a training objective with a FIFO buffer of previously generated images and maximizing functions to approximate the supremum across updates, combined with CLIP and DINO cosine-similarity regularization to preserve image quality, and LoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation. The framework supports prompt-conditioned reference distributions, enabling context-aware evaluation (e.g., historical accuracy for ENIAC programmers) rather than enforcing a single uniform target.

## Results

On the fine-tuning task across seven professions (chef, therapist, taxi driver, flight attendant, housekeeper, nurse), the proposed MPR-based fine-tuning achieves the lowest MPR(G) of 0.08 and MPR(GAR) of 0.25, outperforming all baselines while maintaining a competitive CLIP score of 0.30. The vanilla SD v1.4 model exhibits the highest representational harm (MPR(G) 1.18, MPR(GAR) 1.89), and even the strongest prior debiasing method (ITI-GEN) still shows MPR(GAR) of 0.44, nearly double the proposed method. The method reduces intersectional disparity (MPR(GAR)) by 87% relative to vanilla while preserving image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed MPR fine-tuning method outperforms all four baselines on both MPR metrics. Against FairDiffusion [23], which targets only gender, the proposed method reduces MPR(G) from 0.27 to 0.08 and MPR(GAR) from 1.32 to 0.25, showing that gender-only debiasing leaves substantial intersectional harm. Against UCE [24], also gender-focused, the gap is even larger (MPR(GAR): 0.25 vs. 1.56). Against Entigen [9], the improvement is most dramatic on MPR(GAR) (0.25 vs. 1.61). The closest competitor is ITI-GEN [66], which also considers intersectional groups (gender, age, race); the proposed method still wins on both MPR(G) (0.08 vs. 0.13) and MPR(GAR) (0.25 vs. 0.44) while recovering a 0.02 CLIP score gap (0.30 vs. 0.28), indicating better preservation of image quality. All baselines were adapted to use the same FairFace reference statistics for a fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Multi-Group Proportional Representation (MPR) metric, originally developed for retrieval systems [48], to the text-to-image generation setting, providing the first theoretically grounded framework for measuring intersectional representational fairness in T2I models. It subsumes prior single-axis fairness metrics (e.g., maximum group probability difference in FairDiffusion [23], UCE [24]) as special cases and connects to the broader literature on maximum mean discrepancy, integral probability metrics, and multicalibration. For the fine-tuning component, the paper builds on Shen et al. [59] by adopting their CLIP/DINO regularization and gradient-adjusting technique, but replaces their fairness objective with the MPR-based loss and introduces a buffer-based optimization strategy to handle the supremum over function classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, flexible tool for quantifying representational harm across arbitrary intersectional groups and reference distributions, addressing a gap where prior work relied on qualitative assessments or single-axis quantitative metrics. A key practical finding is that gender-focused debiasing methods (UCE, FairDiffusion, Entigen) can actively degrade intersectional representation when race and age are considered simultaneously, with UCE performing worse than the vanilla model in some settings. Open challenges remain around robust attribute estimation (relying on CLIP-probed classifiers and BLIP-2 VQA), selecting context-appropriate reference distributions, and scaling to very high-dimensional attribute spaces where the number of intersectional groups becomes combinatorially intractable.
