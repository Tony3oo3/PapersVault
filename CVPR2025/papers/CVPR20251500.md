## Problem

Story visualization requires generating a sequence of frames from narrative text that contains ambiguous pronouns (he, she, they), demanding both accurate character identity across frames and correct resolution of anaphoric references. Prior methods either ignore coreference entirely or, like Story-LDM, resolve it via attention over all previous frames in latent pixel space, which is memory-intensive and loses fine-grained language understanding by operating in CLIP's compressed semantic space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of co-referential frame descriptions (e.g., 'She walks to the kitchen') along with previously generated frames, processed autoregressively as interleaved image-text tokens in the LLM. Each frame's visual context is compressed into just 4 token embeddings (via CLIP + linear mapper) rather than full latent pixels, enabling generation of 50+ frame stories within a single 80GB GPU. Output is a sequence of 512×512 RGB frames generated by the frozen Char-LDM conditioned on the LLM's projected [IMG] token representations.

## Method

The two-stage architecture first fine-tunes a Latent Diffusion Model (Char-LDM) with character-augmented embeddings—concatenating each character-name token's CLIP text embedding with the corresponding character's CLIP image features through an MLP—and a cross-attention regularization loss (L_reg) that uses SAM segmentation masks to force character tokens to attend to the correct spatial regions. In the second stage, an OPT-6.7B LLM is adapted to accept interleaved image-text context and emit R=8 [IMG] tokens, which a BLIP-2-style Q-Former (Mapper_LDM) projects into the Char-LDM's fused-embedding input space. Training jointly minimizes next-token prediction loss on [IMG] tokens, an L2 alignment loss between the projected output and the ground-truth fused embedding (computed from the de-referenced caption), and a pixel-level diffusion loss through the frozen Char-LDM U-Net, enabling the LLM's causal language modeling to implicitly resolve pronouns while producing visually consistent characters.

## Results

On FlintstonesSV with co-referential descriptions, StoryGPT-V achieves 88.45% Char-Acc and 94.94% Char-F1, substantially outperforming the strongest baselines (ContextualStory: 83.08/91.70; AR-LDM: 80.73/90.25). It also attains the best FID of 21.71 among methods reporting full metrics, and the highest text-image alignment scores (BLEU4 0.5037, CIDEr 1.6718). On PororoSV with coreferenced text, StoryGPT-V leads all baselines with 36.06% Char-Acc, 62.70% Char-F1, and 19.56 FID, compared to Story-LDM's 29.14/57.56/26.64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StoryGPT-V outperforms all six baselines on character accuracy metrics: it leads ContextualStory (83.08%) by 5.4 points on Char-Acc and AR-LDM (80.73%) by 7.7 points on FlintstonesSV with co-referential text. On PororoSV, the margin is even larger—StoryGPT-V achieves 36.06% Char-Acc versus Story-LDM's 29.14% and LDM's 27.81%, a 7-point gap over the strongest baseline. In terms of image quality (FID), StoryGPT-V (21.71) is competitive with ContextualStory (20.15) and StoryImager (22.27), while clearly surpassing Story-LDM (36.34) and LDM (32.36). For text-image alignment, StoryGPT-V achieves the best BLEU4 (0.5037) and CIDEr (1.6718), exceeding LDM (0.4911/1.5103) and Story-LDM (0.4585/1.4004). Notably, StoryGPT-V also generates stories exceeding 50 frames with stable character accuracy (80.92% at 50 frames), whereas Story-LDM hits the 80 GB GPU memory limit at 42 frames and degrades to 63.40% Char-Acc at 40 frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the story visualization lineage from StoryGAN through StoryDALL-E and Story-LDM, with the most direct departure from Story-LDM's pixel-space attention memory module. Rather than retaining all prior frames in latent pixel space for cross-attention, StoryGPT-V maps visual context into LLM token embeddings, leveraging the LLM's causal language modeling for anaphora resolution. The first-stage Char-LDM introduces a novel cross-attention control loss guided by SAM segmentation masks, a technique borrowed from prompt-to-prompt editing but repurposed for character-faithful generation. The second-stage alignment with character-augmented fused embeddings (rather than plain CLIP text embeddings) is a key architectural choice that ties the LLM's reasoning output to the diffusion model's conditional input space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLM-based reference resolution can substantially outperform pixel-space memory mechanisms for coreferential story generation, while also offering a practical memory advantage (50+ frames vs. 42-frame limit for Story-LDM on 80GB GPUs). However, evaluation is confined to two cartoon datasets with a fixed set of 7–9 characters, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to photorealistic or open-domain stories with unbounded character sets. The reliance on pre-computed SAM segmentation masks for the first-stage training also limits applicability to settings where character boundaries are well-defined. A key open question is whether the two-stage design can be unified or whether the LLM backbone (OPT-6.7B) can be scaled further without degrading the alignment to the frozen Char-LDM input space.
