## Problem

Existing video anomaly understanding methods operate at a single temporal granularity—either frame-level scoring or clip/video-level text generation—failing to capture the hierarchical nature of real-world anomalies that range from transient events (explosions) to sustained ones (theft, arson). No prior dataset provides multi-granularity annotations spanning clip, event, and video levels, and VLM-based approaches rely on uniform frame sampling that either misses critical anomaly frames or incurs prohibitive computational cost on long videos.

## Input / Output

The model consumes dense video frames sampled at 16-frame intervals (processed by a frozen InternVL2 ViT encoder) together with a free-form text query. It produces two types of output: per-frame anomaly scores from the trained anomaly scorer (used for temporal localization), and structured text responses containing anomaly judgment (category), description, and causal analysis. The text output granularity adapts to the query—clip-level captions for short segments, or event/video-level summaries with reasoning for longer spans.

## Method

Holmes-VAU integrates an Anomaly-focused Temporal Sampler (ATS) into a 2B-parameter multimodal LLM (InternVL2-2B). The ATS comprises a feature-based anomaly scorer (UR-DMU architecture) that assigns a score to each frame's class token, and a density-aware sampler that treats these scores as a probability mass function, computes the cumulative distribution S_cumsum(t) = Σ(s_i + τ), and uniformly samples N points along the cumulative axis to select frame indices—concentrating frames in high-anomaly regions while preserving temporal coverage. Training is two-stage: first, the anomaly scorer is trained with binary cross-entropy on frame-level labels from HIVAU-70k; second, the VLM is fine-tuned with LoRA (r=64, α=128) on 70k+ hierarchical instruction pairs spanning clip, event, and video granularities. The key insight is that anomalous frames exhibit greater visual variation, so anomaly-score-guided non-uniform sampling lets the LLM focus computational budget on informative regions, outperforming both uniform and top-K sampling at matched frame counts.

## Results

On anomaly detection, Holmes-VAU achieves 87.68% AP on XD-Violence and 88.96% AUC on UCF-Crime, surpassing the best non-explainable VAD baseline (VADCLIP: 84.51% AP, 88.02% AUC) and the best explainable multi-modal method (LAVAD: 62.01% AP, 80.28% AUC). On anomaly reasoning, Holmes-VAU (2B params) achieves BLEU-4 of 0.913/0.804/0.566 and CIDEr of 0.467/1.519/1.437 at clip/event/video levels, dramatically outperforming the strongest general MLLM (InternVL2, 8B: BLEU 0.331/0.101/0.145, CIDEr 0.052/0.022/0.035) despite using fewer parameters. The Anomaly-focused Temporal Sampler with N=16 frames achieves 0.566 BLEU and 1.437 CIDEr at video level with 566 ms latency, outperforming both Uniform (0.511/1.345) and Top-K (0.476/1.302) sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Holmes-VAU outperforms all 15 non-explainable VAD baselines on both XD-Violence AP and UCF-Crime AUC, with the largest margin over VADCLIP [58] (+3.17 AP, +0.94 AUC) and UR-DMU [74] (+6.02 AP, +2.0 AUC). Among explainable multi-modal VAD methods, Holmes-VAU surpasses LAVAD [67] by +25.67 AP on XD-Violence and +8.68 AUC on UCF-Crime, and LLaVA-1.5 [27] by +37.42 AP and +16.12 AUC, demonstrating that supervised instruction tuning on hierarchical anomaly data is critical for explainable detection. On the reasoning front (Table 2), Holmes-VAU (2B) dominates all six general MLLMs (7–8B) across every metric and granularity: it achieves 2.8× higher CIDEr at event level (1.519 vs. 0.022 for InternVL2) and 6.7× higher CIDEr at video level (1.437 vs. 0.035), confirming that domain-specific anomaly instruction data yields far superior reasoning than general video instruction tuning. The only setting where Holmes-VAU does not lead is the ablation with N=32 frames, where ATS (0.576 BLEU) edges out Uniform (0.558) by a smaller margin, suggesting diminishing returns beyond 16 sampled frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: traditional frame-level video anomaly detection (building on the UR-DMU anomaly scorer architecture), recent multimodal VAD approaches that leverage VLMs for explainable detection (LAVAD, HAWK), and hierarchical video understanding. It departs from prior multimodal VAU work by introducing multi-granularity (clip, event, video) instruction data rather than the single-level annotations used in LAVAD (clip-level) or HAWK (video-level), and by replacing uniform or dense frame sampling with the proposed Anomaly-focused Temporal Sampler. The base architecture is InternVL2-2B, fine-tuned with LoRA, making it a parameter-efficient adaptation of a general-purpose VLM to the anomaly domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HIVAU-70k benchmark fills a concrete gap in the VAU literature by providing hierarchical annotations that enable models to handle both transient anomalies (explosions, fights) and sustained contextual events (theft, arson) within a unified framework. The ATS offers a practical, low-overhead mechanism for directing VLM attention to anomaly-rich segments in long videos, addressing a key efficiency bottleneck. Limitations include reliance on a small 2B backbone, a separately trained anomaly scorer that may not generalize to truly open-world anomaly types beyond the 19 classes in UCF-Crime and XD-Violence, and evaluation confined to surveillance-style scenarios. Open questions include whether the hierarchical annotation paradigm scales to unstructured, truly open-world video and whether the ATS can be made end-to-end differentiable rather than relying on a frozen scorer.
