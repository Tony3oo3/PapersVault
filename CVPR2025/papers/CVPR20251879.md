## Problem

Diffusion-based manipulation policies suffer from two fundamental limitations: they lack specialized perception of task-relevant 3D regions, which cripples performance on high-precision tasks like USB insertion or screwing, and their iterative denoising process makes inference too slow for real-time robot control. No prior method exploits the generative process itself to dynamically adjust the spatial resolution of 3D perception during action generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251879/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (128×128 from four cameras: front, left shoulder, right shoulder, wrist), a 3D point cloud whose sampling region is dynamically determined by the current flow-matching time step, a natural-language task instruction, and robot proprioception. It outputs a discrete keyframe action consisting of a 6-DoF end-effector pose (3D position, 6D rotation representation to avoid quaternion discontinuities, and a binary gripper open/close flag), which is then executed via a BiRRT motion planner. The keyframe-based paradigm means the policy predicts bottleneck poses rather than continuous trajectories.

## Method

FlowRAM replaces diffusion with Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) to generate actions by regressing a deterministic velocity field along straight-line probability paths, requiring as few as 2–4 ODE integration steps at inference. The key architectural innovation is the Dynamic Radius Schedule (DRS): during the flow-matching generation process, the 3D perception radius shrinks monotonically (linearly by default) from a global workspace view to a tight local region around the noise-perturbed pose, so early steps capture scene semantics while later steps resolve fine geometric details of the manipulation target. A Mamba-based state-space model serves as the multimodal fusion backbone, concatenating PointMamba-encoded geometric tokens, CLIP+FPN RGB tokens, CLIP text tokens, and the noise-perturbed pose into a single sequence processed with linear-time complexity. Training minimizes a CFM velocity-matching loss (with Logit-Normal time sampling) plus a binary cross-entropy loss on the gripper state, using AdamW at lr 1e-4 for 300K steps with EMA.

## Results

On the 10-task RLBench multi-task benchmark, FlowRAM achieves a state-of-the-art average success rate of 82.3%, surpassing the previous best (3D Diffuser Actor at 78.4% and RVT-2 at 76.2%). On 7 high-precision RLBench tasks, FlowRAM reaches 52.0% average success rate, outperforming the next-best method (3D Diffuser Actor at 40.0%) by 12.0 percentage points. Inference with only 4 flow-matching steps yields competitive performance (77.8% avg success), whereas DDIM requires 16+ steps to reach comparable levels (79.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowRAM outperforms all six baselines on the 10-task RLBench multi-task benchmark, achieving 82.3% average success versus 78.4% for 3D Diffuser Actor and 76.2% for RVT-2. The largest multi-task gains are on Put Buttons (+13.0% over RVT-2) and Open Drawer (+11.3% over RVT-2). On the 7 high-precision tasks, the advantage widens substantially: FlowRAM reaches 52.0% average success compared to 40.0% (3D Diffuser Actor), 39.4% (RVT-2), and 20.2% (Act3D), with particularly large margins on Insert onto Square Peg (69.3% vs. 53.1% for RVT-2) and Plug Charger (52.0% vs. 34.7% for RVT-2). The paper attributes these high-precision gains to the Dynamic Radius Schedule, which overcomes occlusion issues that limit RVT-2's virtual-view rendering approach. Among older baselines, PerAct (20.4%) and GNFactor (31.7%) are far behind, reflecting the gap between voxel/field-based perception and the region-aware 3D sampling strategy. No baseline matches FlowRAM on any individual task in either the multi-task or high-precision settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowRAM sits at the intersection of three research threads: conditional flow matching for generative policy learning (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023), selective state space models for sequence processing (Gu & Dao, 2023), and 3D region-aware perception for manipulation (Act3D, RVT-2). It departs from diffusion-based policies (3D Diffuser Actor, Diffusion Policy) by replacing iterative denoising with a single ODE integration, and from prior 3D perception methods by coupling the perception radius to the flow matching generation timestep via a Dynamic Radius Schedule. The use of Mamba as a unified backbone for both point cloud encoding (via PointMamba) and multimodal token fusion is a notable architectural choice that consolidates what prior works handle with separate Transformer or CNN modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in diffusion-based manipulation policies—slow inference and insufficient local geometric detail—within a single framework, and validates the approach on both simulation (RLBench) and a real UR5 arm with limited demonstrations. The DRS idea of progressively narrowing perception as the flow converges is a clean conceptual contribution that could transfer to other flow-based or iterative generation settings in robotics. Open questions include whether the linear radius schedule is optimal for all task geometries, how the approach scales to multi-arm or dexterous manipulation, and whether the Mamba-based fusion generalizes beyond the relatively short token sequences encountered in single-keyframe prediction.
