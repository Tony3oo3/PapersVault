## Problem

Existing diffusion/flow models for human trajectory forecasting sample K future trajectories independently, causing spatial overlap and poor coverage of the multimodal distribution. Additionally, iterative ODE-based sampling requires 100+ neural function evaluations, making these models impractical for time-critical applications like autonomous driving. This paper addresses both issues by jointly modeling K correlated trajectories and distilling the flow model into a one-step generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251455/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes scene-level input: past 2D trajectories of A agents over T_p timestamps plus per-timestamp contextual features (absolute/relative coordinates, velocities, and agent identity encodings such as player vs. ball in NBA). It outputs K correlated (not i.i.d.) future trajectory sets, each containing A×2T_f waypoints for all agents simultaneously, ensuring inter-agent coherence and spatial diversity across the K predictions. The IMLE student produces the same K-shot output in a single forward pass rather than 100 ODE steps.

## Method

MoFlow is a conditional flow matching model that jointly denoises K correlated trajectory predictions using a shared noise vector, with a Transformer backbone applying multi-head self-attention across agents and sinusoidal encodings at both agent and prediction levels. The key training innovation is a closest-prediction loss: among K scene-level waypoint predictions, only the one nearest to ground truth receives regression loss, while a cross-entropy classification loss over K logits encourages the model to assign distinct predictions to different modes, promoting diversity without auxiliary models. For distillation, the paper adapts conditional IMLE: the student generator produces m samples per context, and the one minimizing Chamfer distance to the teacher's K-shot ODE output is selected for gradient computation, avoiding GAN instability and MCMC overhead. A logit-normal time schedule (μ=−0.5, σ=1.5) is used during training to prevent overfitting near t=1, and the student omits flow-time embeddings to enable single-step inference.

## Results

On the NBA SportVU dataset, MoFlow achieves 0.71 minADE / 0.87 minFDE at the full 4.0s horizon, improving over the previous state-of-the-art LED* (0.81/1.16) by approximately 12% in ADE and 25% in FDE. On ETH-UCY, MoFlow attains an average of 0.20/0.32 minADE/minFDE, matching or slightly exceeding the best baselines (SingularTraj: 0.21/0.32). On SDD, MoFlow achieves the best minADE of 7.50 pixels (vs. 7.81 for ET+HighGraph) with a competitive minFDE of 11.96. The IMLE-distilled one-step student model preserves these results nearly exactly (e.g., 0.71/0.86 on NBA) while sampling 100× faster than the 100-step teacher ODE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the NBA dataset, MoFlow decisively outperforms all 14 baselines at every prediction horizon; the largest gap is against the previous SOTA LED* (0.81/1.16 → 0.71/0.87 at 4.0s, a 12% ADE and 25% FDE reduction) and against non-diffusion methods like MemoNet (1.25/1.47) and NPSN (1.31/1.79). On ETH-UCY, MoFlow (0.20/0.32 avg) ties or marginally beats the strongest baselines SingularTraj (0.21/0.32) and LED* (0.21/0.33), while clearly surpassing earlier methods like GroupNet (0.25/0.44) and MID (0.21/0.38). On SDD, MoFlow achieves the best minADE (7.50 vs. 7.81 for ET+HighGraph and 8.48 for LED) but trails ET+HighGraph on minFDE (11.96 vs. 11.09), indicating a slight trade-off in endpoint accuracy. The IMLE one-step student matches the teacher's performance to within 0.01 on all three datasets, confirming that the 100-step ODE can be replaced by a single forward pass without quality loss. In the distillation ablation (Table 4), IMLE substantially outperforms GAN-based alternatives (WGAN: 1.78/2.90, DCGAN: 1.71/2.98 vs. IMLE: 0.71/0.87 on NBA 4.0s), demonstrating that adversarial objectives are unstable for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoFlow builds directly on the conditional flow matching framework (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) and the IMLE generative modeling approach (Li & Malik, Li et al.), repurposing both for multi-agent trajectory prediction. It departs from prior diffusion-based trajectory predictors (MID, LED) in two key ways: (1) it jointly models K correlated future trajectories with a closest-prediction loss that explicitly promotes diversity, rather than sampling K i.i.d. trajectories independently; and (2) it introduces IMLE as a distribution-matching distillation objective for flow models, contrasting with the more common consistency-distillation or progressive-distillation paradigms. The transformer-based context encoder follows standard multi-agent prediction architectures, so the novelty is concentrated in the flow-matching loss design and the distillation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× inference speedup (one-step vs. 100-step ODE) with negligible performance loss is practically significant for time-critical deployments like autonomous driving, where iterative denoising is a bottleneck. The finding that IMLE—a simple, stable, sample-based objective—matches or exceeds GAN-based distillation (WGAN, DCGAN) for flow models offers a cleaner alternative to adversarial training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger agent counts or longer horizons, whether the closest-prediction loss generalizes beyond trajectory prediction, and how the method performs when integrated with semantic scene representations (lanes, obstacles) as the authors themselves note as future work.
