## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods for text-to-image diffusion models effectively erase target concepts at inference time, but no prior work has examined whether this erasure survives subsequent fine-tuning. This paper identifies that unlearned concepts systematically revive when the model is fine-tuned on even unrelated concepts, undermining the privacy and legal guarantees (e.g., GDPR Right to be Forgotten) that unlearning is supposed to provide.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an unlearned Stable Diffusion v1.4 model and a curriculum of fine-tuning concepts (50 prompts each, with images generated by SD3-Medium) arranged in increasing order of semantic similarity to the target concept, as ranked by OpenAI embeddings. At each fine-tuning step (8 epochs per concept), the model is evaluated on 100 validation images (20 prompts × 5 samples) using CLIP score and a task-specific binary classifier (ViT for objects/celebrities, UnlearnCanvas classifier for styles, NudeNet for NSFW). The key output is the 'Revival Point' — the number of sequentially fine-tuned concepts before both metrics cross predefined thresholds (CLIP score set 0.02 below the base model's score; classifier accuracy > 0.3).

## Method

The paper introduces a Concept Revival Framework that sequentially fine-tunes an unlearned model on a curriculum of concepts ordered from semantically distant to semantically close to the target, measuring CLIP score and classifier accuracy after each step to pinpoint when the unlearned concept resurfaces. They apply this framework to nine state-of-the-art unlearning methods (SalUn, ESD, EDiff, CA, MACE, SPM, SA, Receler, UCE) across four unlearning settings (object, style, celebrity, NSFW) with ten target concepts. Two ablation axes are studied: mapping the target to a semantically distant versus close surrogate concept (distant mappings prove more robust), and modifying cross-attention-only versus broader parameter sets (cross-attention-only unlearning is most fragile). They hypothesize that fine-tuning disrupts the learned target-to-surrogate mapping, evidenced by increasing unlearning loss values post-fine-tuning, and propose a gradient-projection mitigation that orthogonalizes diffusion gradients against the unlearning objective to preserve the erasure during fine-tuning.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that unlearned concepts revive across all 9 tested machine unlearning methods when the unlearned model is fine-tuned on even unrelated concepts. MACE and UCE are the most robust: UCE with a semantically distant surrogate (Outdoor Recreational Activities) achieves a Revival Point of R (no revival observed), while MACE-O also reaches R, compared to SPM-O at epoch 4 and UCE-T (semantically close surrogate) at epoch 1. In the module ablation (Table 2), ESD-full (all parameters) is most robust with a Revival Point of 7, versus ESD-x (cross-attention only) at 4. Unlearning loss increases consistently post fine-tuning, e.g., UCE (Dog/Cat) rises from 55.4 to 674, and ESD (Golf Ball) from 5×10⁻⁴ to 1.1×10⁻³, confirming that fine-tuning destabilizes the unlearning objective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an evaluation paper: rather than proposing a new method that beats baselines, it systematically shows that all 9 state-of-the-art MU methods are vulnerable to concept revival upon fine-tuning. UCE [12] and MACE [20] are the most robust, with UCE achieving no revival (R) for object unlearning when mapped to a semantically distant surrogate, and MACE showing robustness for NSFW content. ESD [11] is notably vulnerable, especially its cross-attention-only variant (ESD-x, Revival Point 4) and for NSFW unlearning. SPM [21] and SA [15] show rapid revival for NSFW content. Even Receler [17], designed to be adversarially robust, exhibits concept revival (low for object/style but high for NSFW). SalUn [10], CA [19], and EDiff [39] all show clear revival in the sequential fine-tuning protocol. The key differentiator is not which method is 'best' but that none are truly robust: the choice of surrogate concept (semantically distant vs. close) and the scope of modified parameters (cross-attention only vs. full model) significantly affect vulnerability, with cross-attention-only methods (UCE, CA, ESD-x) being the most fragile.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new unlearning algorithm but instead performs a systematic meta-evaluation of nine existing concept-erasure methods (SalUn, ESD, EDiff, CA, MACE, SPM, SA, Receler, UCE) for text-to-image diffusion models. Prior work in this area has focused on whether a target concept can be successfully removed (measured by generation quality or UnlearnCanvas accuracy), but none had examined whether the unlearning survives subsequent fine-tuning. The paper repurposes standard fine-tuning as a stress test, introducing a 'Revival Point' metric and a curriculum-based fine-tuning protocol to quantify unlearning stability, thereby carving out a new evaluation axis orthogonal to the existing unlearning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all nine state-of-the-art unlearning methods exhibit concept revival after benign fine-tuning fundamentally undermines the privacy-compliance premise of machine unlearning: a model published as having 'forgotten' sensitive content can be made to regenerate it through routine downstream adaptation. The paper identifies two actionable levers—mapping to semantically distant surrogate concepts and modifying broader parameter sets beyond cross-attention—that improve robustness, but the core question of why fine-tuning disrupts the learned mapping remains open. A practical mitigation via gradient projection (keeping the unlearning loss constant during fine-tuning) is sketched but not fully validated, and the entire study is confined to Stable Diffusion v1.4, leaving open whether the vulnerability persists in newer architectures (SDXL, SD3, DiT-based models).
