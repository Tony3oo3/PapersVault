## Problem

Online automatic geometric calibration of 3D radar and camera systems remains largely unsolved due to the extreme sparsity and elevation ambiguity inherent in radar measurements, which make frontal-view depth maps noisy and structurally unreliable. Prior radar-camera auto-calibration work addressed only rotational calibration and relied on stationary highway radars, leaving translational calibration and vehicle-mounted 3D radar scenarios unexplored. No public benchmark existed for evaluating radar-camera auto-calibration methods.

## Input / Output

The network consumes an RGB image (400×192 px), a 3D radar point cloud, and an initial extrinsic estimate T_init (rotation matrix + translation vector). The image is first passed through DepthAnything and ZoeDepth to produce a metric depth map, and both the radar points and the pseudo point cloud derived from the depth map are projected into two unified representations: a frontal-view depth map and a bird's-eye-view map. The output is a 6-DoF correction (rotation vector in Euler angles and translation vector in cm) that is iteratively composed back into T_init over N refinement steps.

## Method

RC-AutoCalib introduces a Dual-Perspective representation that projects radar and camera data onto both a frontal view (rich but height-noisy) and a bird's-eye view (robust to elevation ambiguity), then fuses them via a Selective Fusion Mechanism inspired by SKNet that uses channel-wise attention to adaptively weight each perspective's features. Within each perspective branch, a Multi-Modal Cross-Attention Mechanism lets the dense camera features attend to the sparse non-zero radar regions and vice versa, explicitly locating cross-modal correspondences. During training, an Explicit Feature Matching Supervision branch (motivated by LightGlue) computes an assignment matrix and matchable scores that are directly supervised by ground-truth correspondences derived from the true calibration, rather than relying solely on the implicit calibration loss. A Noise-Resistant Matcher further filters unreliable radar points from the supervision signal by checking LiDAR occupancy in an adaptively sized 3D bounding box around each radar point. The final regression head is an LSTM-based sequence decoder (from CalibDepth) that autoregressively predicts the correction over N iterations, trained with a combined calibration loss and a weighted matching loss (β = 0.1).

## Results

On the nuScenes dataset with a small mis-calibration range R1 (±10°, ±0.25 m), RC-AutoCalib achieves a mean rotation error of 0.427° and a mean translation error of 9.498 cm, substantially outperforming the best LiDAR-camera baseline CalibDepth (0.807° / 12.608 cm). Under the larger mis-calibration range R2 (±20°, ±1.5 m), the method records 0.852° mean rotation error and 47.537 cm mean translation error, again leading all compared methods. The Yaw rotation error is particularly low at 0.953° (R1) and 1.754° (R2), and the Y-translation error drops to 3.295 cm (R1) and 5.415 cm (R2), demonstrating strong robustness to the sparse and noisy radar point clouds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RC-AutoCalib outperforms all four baselines on both rotation and translation across both mis-calibration ranges. Against the strongest LiDAR-camera baseline CalibDepth, the method reduces mean rotation error by 47% (0.427° vs. 0.807°) and mean translation error by 25% (9.498 cm vs. 12.608 cm) on R1, and by 49% (0.852° vs. 1.686°) and 14% (47.537 cm vs. 55.380 cm) on R2. Compared to LCCNet (1/3/5 iterations), the improvement is even more pronounced: on R2, LCCNet-5 still yields 1.898° rotation and 88.302 cm translation, whereas RC-AutoCalib achieves 0.852° and 47.537 cm. NetCalib2 is outperformed on every metric in both ranges. The only prior radar-camera method, Schöller et al. [26], reports rotation errors of 1.692° (Fine) and 3.334° (Fine) on R1 and R2 respectively, with no translation results; RC-AutoCalib cuts those rotation errors by roughly 75% and additionally provides full 6-DoF (rotation + translation) calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the iterative deep-learning auto-calibration paradigm established for LiDAR-camera pairs (LCCNet, CalibDepth, NetCalib2) to the substantially harder radar-camera setting, where point clouds are sparse and suffer from elevation ambiguity. It departs from prior radar-camera work (Schöller et al., 2019) by extending from rotational-only to full 6-DoF (rotation + translation) online calibration, and from LiDAR-camera methods by introducing a Dual-Perspective (frontal + BEV) representation with a Selective Fusion Mechanism to compensate for radar's missing height information. The feature-matching component draws on cross-attention designs from LightGlue and the iterative LSTM regression head from CalibDepth, while the Noise-Resistant Matcher is a novel training-time module that leverages LiDAR ground truth to filter unreliable radar correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end benchmark for online 6-DoF radar-camera auto-calibration on a public dataset (nuScenes), filling a clear gap where prior online methods addressed only rotation or relied on specialized hardware. The dual-perspective + selective-fusion strategy offers a practical template for handling sensor modalities with partial observability (here, missing elevation), which could transfer to other sparse-sensor calibration problems. Limitations include dependence on LiDAR data during training for the Noise-Resistant Matcher, reliance on pre-trained monocular depth estimators (DepthAnything + ZoeDepth) for the camera pseudo-BEV, and evaluation on a single dataset with a single radar type, leaving open questions about generalization to different radar geometries, real-time deployment, and more extreme miscalibration ranges.
