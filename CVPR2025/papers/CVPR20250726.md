## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) suffers from insufficient expressivity and low parameter efficiency because it is restricted to Gaussian primitives and operates only in the positive density space (additive splatting). The paper argues that the underlying unnormalized mixture model need not be limited to Gaussians or positive-only components, and that these restrictions force the use of large numbers of components to achieve adequate quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images and an SfM point cloud (without normals) as initialization, and produces a 3D scene representation consisting of a mixture of Student's t-distribution components, each parameterized by a 3D mean, covariance (via scale and rotation), degree of freedom ν, opacity in [-1,1], and view-dependent color via spherical harmonics. Rendering is performed via differentiable 2D rasterization (splatting) of projected t-distributions rather than ray marching, enabling real-time novel view synthesis. The model is evaluated on 11 scenes across Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, and Deep Blending using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.

## Method

SSS replaces 3D Gaussians with 3D Student's t-distributions whose degree of freedom ν is learnable, allowing each component to range from Cauchy-like (fat-tailed, covering large areas) to Gaussian-like (spiky, capturing details), while retaining closed-form evaluation under affine and projective transformations. The model extends splatting into the negative density space by allowing opacity to take values in [-1,1] (via tanh), so negative components 'scoop' (subtract) color from the mixture, improving parameter efficiency for topological features like holes. Because the resulting model with mixed-sign weights is not a well-defined probability distribution, optimization is framed as sampling from an energy-based posterior using Stochastic Gradient Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (SGHMC), with adaptive friction and noise scheduling on the mean parameter to decouple the tightly coupled parameters ν, μ, and Σ. A burn-in phase with anisotropic noise (scaled by Σ) precedes the full exploitation phase, and near-transparent components are recycled via a closed-form relocation rule that preserves the overall rendered color distribution.

## Results

SSS achieves the best result on 6 of 9 metrics across three datasets (Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, Deep Blending), with the largest margin on the Train scene (23.23 PSNR / 0.844 SSIM / 0.170 LPIPS vs. 3DHGS's 22.95 / 0.827 / 0.197, a 13.7% LPIPS improvement). On Tanks & Temples, SSS leads all baselines by a clear margin (24.87 PSNR, 0.873 SSIM, 0.138 LPIPS). In the parameter-efficiency experiments, SSS matches or exceeds the quality of methods using 1M+ components while employing as few as 180k–468k components, a reduction of up to 82%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSS outperforms all seven baselines on Tanks & Temples across all three metrics, with the largest gap over 3DGS (+1.73 PSNR) and Mip-NeRF (+2.65 PSNR). On Mip-NeRF 360, SSS leads in PSNR (29.90 vs. 3DGS-MCMC's 29.89) and LPIPS (0.145 vs. 3DHGS's 0.178), but trails 3DGS-MCMC on SSIM (0.893 vs. 0.900). On Deep Blending, SSS achieves the best SSIM (0.907) but is slightly behind Scaffold-GS on PSNR (30.07 vs. 30.21) and 3DHGS on LPIPS (0.247 vs. 0.242, a 7×10⁻³ gap). Against the closest competitor 3DGS-MCMC, SSS wins on 8 of 9 metrics, with the only loss being Mip-NeRF 360 SSIM. The parameter-efficiency experiments further show SSS surpassing 3DGS, Mip-NeRF, and GES at 180k components and approaching 3DHGS/3DGS-MCMC at ~300k components, while those methods require 1M+ components in their default settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) along three axes: replacing Gaussian primitives with Student's t-distributions (which subsume Gaussians as ν→∞ and include fat-tailed Cauchy-like shapes), extending the mixture from purely positive (splatting) to include negative (scooping) components inspired by subtractive mixture models, and replacing naive SGD with a principled SGHMC sampler to handle the tighter parameter coupling introduced by the learnable degree-of-freedom. It builds directly on 3DGS-MCMC for the loss formulation and component recycling strategy, and on the SGHMC framework from stochastic gradient MCMC literature, while departing from the broader 3DGS-improvement line (GES, 3DHGS, FreGS, Scaffold-GS) by changing the fundamental distribution family rather than the optimization schedule or spatial structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 82% component reduction at comparable quality is practically significant for memory-constrained and real-time applications where 3DGS is increasingly deployed as a foundational primitive. However, the absolute quality gains over the strongest baselines (3DGS-MCMC, 3DHGS) are modest (sub-0.1 dB PSNR on most datasets), and the method introduces additional hyperparameters (negative-component ratio, SGHMC friction/noise schedules) that require tuning. Open questions include whether the symmetric t-distribution family is sufficient for highly anisotropic or non-smooth geometry, whether the SGHMC approach scales to dynamic or 4D scenes, and whether combining t-distributions with other families (e.g., Laplace) as the authors suggest would yield further gains.
