## Problem

Existing VFI models suffer from time-to-location (TTL) ambiguity when handling non-uniform motions (acceleration, deceleration, direction changes), causing the network to average over all possible trajectories and produce blurry interpolated frames. Prior motion descriptors—time indexing and distance indexing—cannot fully resolve this ambiguity because they lack directional information, leaving the model unable to distinguish between different non-uniform motion patterns at the same target time.

## Input / Output

The model takes two source video frames I_0 and I_1 along with an arbitrary target time t ∈ (0,1) and produces a single interpolated frame at that time. During training, the ground-truth target frame I_t is additionally available and consumed by a teacher process for knowledge distillation, but at inference only the two source frames and t are used, with a uniform-motion BiM (ratio = t, angle = π) substituted as the motion descriptor. The architecture operates on a recurrent pyramid with multiple spatial levels, downsampled by a factor of 2 per level.

## Method

The key insight is a novel motion descriptor, the Bidirectional Motion Field (BiM), which encodes both a pixel-wise magnitude ratio R = r_0/(r_0+r_1) and an angle Φ between the two bidirectional optical flows from the target to each source frame, enabling it to distinguish non-uniform motions that time or distance indexing cannot. A BiM-guided FlowNet (BiMFN) incorporates BiM through a BiM-MConv module that element-wise multiplies cascaded convolution features with distance-embedding and angle-embedding outputs to produce refined residual flows. A Content-Aware Upsampling Network (CAUN) uses convex upsample layers to adaptively upsample flows while preserving small objects and sharp boundaries. Training employs Knowledge Distillation for VFI-centric Flow supervision (KDVCF): a teacher process (sharing weights with the student) leverages the ground-truth target frame to produce accurate bidirectional flows, which both compute the BiM input for the student and supervise its flow estimation, ensuring the learned flows are aligned with VFI objectives rather than generic optical-flow end-point error.

## Results

On arbitrary-time interpolation, BiM-VFI achieves the best perceptual metrics across all test sets: LPIPS 0.070 / STLPIPS 0.043 on Vimeo90K-septuplet, and LPIPS 0.023 / STLPIPS 0.008 on SNU-FILM-arb Medium. The paper reports 26% and 45% relative improvements in LPIPS and STLPIPS over the previous SOTA. On fixed-time interpolation (XTest), BiM-VFI also leads with LPIPS 0.089 and STLPIPS 0.060, despite not being specifically trained for fixed-time targets. Notably, BiM-VFI underperforms on pixel-centric metrics (PSNR 26.83 vs. 29.41 for EMA-VFI on Vimeo90K-septuplet) due to its uniform-motion assumption at inference, yet produces substantially sharper frames perceptually.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiM-VFI outperforms all eight baseline groups on every perceptual metric (LPIPS, STLPIPS, NIQE) across all arbitrary-time test sets. Against the strongest prior methods with distance indexing ([D,R]-IFRNet, [D,R]-RIFE), BiM-VFI improves LPIPS by 10–15% and STLPIPS by 20–35% on SNU-FILM-arb subsets, confirming that the angular component Φ in BiM resolves directional ambiguity that distance indexing alone cannot. Against flow-based SOTA methods (RIFE, IFRNet, AMT-S, UPRNet, EMA-VFI), the gains are even larger—up to 45% in STLPIPS on SNU-FILM-arb Extreme—because these methods trained with time indexing produce heavily blurred frames under non-uniform motion. M2M-PWC [7] is the only baseline that beats BiM-VFI on a single metric (NIQE 4.657 vs. 4.751 on SNU-FILM-arb Extreme), but it still trails on LPIPS and STLPIPS. On fixed-time interpolation (Table 2), BiM-VFI also leads on XTest (LPIPS 0.089 vs. 0.095 for UPRNet, 0.109 for XVFI) and SNU-FILM Extreme (LPIPS 0.052 vs. 0.056 for IFRNet), despite not being trained specifically for t=0.5 targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distance indexing paradigm of Zhong et al. (ECCV 2024), which resolved velocity ambiguity in VFI by encoding relative motion magnitudes, by adding an angular component to form the Bidirectional Motion Field (BiM) that also captures directional changes. It departs from prior flow-supervision strategies (pre-trained optical flow models, privileged blocks in RIFE) by introducing KDVCF, a self-distillation scheme where a teacher process with access to the target frame generates VFI-centric flow supervision. The recurrent pyramid architecture and cost-volume-based flow estimation follow the lineage of IFRNet, AMT, and EMA-VFI, while the Content-Aware Upsampling Network adapts the convex upsample layer from optical flow estimation (Teed et al.) to the VFI setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that resolving time-to-location ambiguity at training time (via a richer motion descriptor) rather than at inference time yields substantially cleaner interpolated frames, with 26% and 45% improvements in LPIPS and STLPIPS over SOTA. However, the method still relies on a uniform motion assumption at inference, meaning non-uniform motions produce clean but potentially misaligned frames—a limitation shared by all VFI methods but made explicit here. The significant PSNR/SSIM gap relative to competitors highlights an open tension between pixel accuracy and perceptual quality in VFI evaluation. Future work could explore relaxing the uniform-motion inference assumption or extending BiM to multi-frame settings.
