## Problem

Existing text-to-3D scene generation methods are constrained by the scarcity of high-quality 3D training data, requiring either extensive fine-tuning or in-context learning, and often rely on asset retrieval from limited 3D model pools that restrict stylistic diversity. This paper asks whether the rich spatial and aesthetic knowledge encoded in web-scale 2D image models can be leveraged as an intermediary to guide 3D scene synthesis without any additional 3D training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'a Barbie-themed clinic room') and produces a fully assembled 3D scene composed of individually generated object meshes, each with its own position, scale, and orientation, plus procedurally generated floor and wall texture planes. Because every object is an independent mesh, the output supports modular, object-level editing without regenerating the entire scene. The pipeline is entirely training-free and zero-shot, requiring no 3D dataset or fine-tuning.

## Method

ArtiScene uses a 2D isometric image as an intermediary between text and 3D: DALLE-3 first synthesizes an isometric-view layout image from the prompt, which is then decomposed via GroundedDINO detection and segmentation into individual object crops. Each crop is described by ChatGPT and fed with its segmented image into Edify 3D to produce a customized single-object mesh, while Depth Anything V2 estimates per-object 3D bounding boxes from the isometric image. Pose is resolved by rendering each mesh at 8 candidate yaw angles and selecting the one whose Stable Diffusion + DINOv2 feature embedding best matches the original 2D segmentation. Floor and wall textures are generated at runtime by DALLE-3, and a post-processing stage resolves residual overlaps and snaps objects to scene boundaries, yielding a physically plausible, modular scene.

## Results

On the end-to-end scene generation benchmark (111 samples across 8 room types and 21 styles/themes), ArtiScene achieves a CLIP score of 29.45 vs. Holodeck's 26.73, and a 95.46% GPT-4o winning rate on overall quality. In user studies with 75 participants, ArtiScene is preferred 79.73% of the time overall (82.96% on style, 72.58% on layout). For layout evaluation against LayoutGPT, ArtiScene reduces the Object Overlapping Rate by 6× on bedrooms (6.48% vs. 37.26%) and 10× on living rooms (2.19% vs. 27.77%), while also generating denser scenes (8.66 vs. 6.23 average furniture items in living rooms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LayoutGPT (NeurIPS 2024), ArtiScene achieves a dramatically lower Object Overlapping Rate—6.48% vs. 37.26% on bedrooms and 2.19% vs. 27.77% on living rooms—while also producing richer scenes with more furniture items (6.97 vs. 4.30 in bedrooms; 8.66 vs. 6.23 in living rooms). In the paired user study (60 participants), ArtiScene is preferred 66–87% of the time across layout plausibility, furniture diversity, and overall quality. Against Holodeck (CVPR 2024), the closest baseline in problem framing, ArtiScene wins on every metric: CLIP score (29.45 vs. 26.73), GPT-4o overall winning rate (95.46% vs. 4.53%), and human preference (79.73% vs. 20.26% in a 75-participant study). The margin is especially large on style consistency (98.16% GPT-4o win rate) and room functionality (88.15%), reflecting the benefit of using a high-quality 2D image intermediary for both layout and aesthetic guidance. LayoutGPT's results tend to memorize training-distribution patterns (e.g., symmetric nightstand placement), while Holodeck uses a fixed texture library, both of which limit diversity compared to ArtiScene's per-scene generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArtiScene departs from both the LLM-programming line (LayoutGPT, SceneCraft) that relies on asset retrieval from limited 3D pools, and the diffusion-inpainting-then-lift line (Text2Room, ControlRoom3D) that produces a monolithic scene mesh. Its core conceptual move is to repurpose high-quality text-to-image generation (DALLE-3) as a layout intermediary: an isometric 2D image is generated first, then decomposed via detection, depth estimation, and single-view 3D generation into individual object meshes with poses. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, the paper orchestrates a pipeline of off-the-shelf models (GroundedDINO, Depth Anything V2, Edify 3D, DINOv2) in a training-free composition, positioning itself as a systems-level contribution that sidesteps the 3D data scarcity problem by leaning on the far richer 2D image distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, modular pipeline offers a practical path to diverse, stylistically coherent 3D scenes without requiring large 3D training sets or per-scene optimization, which is directly relevant to interior design, VR content creation, and embodied AI environment generation. Key limitations remain: the isometric perspective degrades for large or complex scenes (museums, hospitals), fine-grained control over object count and placement is weak, and the multi-model pipeline introduces compounding error and complexity. Open questions include whether stronger 2D generation models will further improve layout fidelity, whether the approach scales to outdoor or multi-room scenes, and how to incorporate user feedback loops for iterative refinement.
