## Problem

Vision Transformers capture long-range dependencies effectively for image deblurring but suffer from quadratic computational complexity at high resolutions. Existing state space model (SSM) approaches for visual tasks rely on multi-direction scanning to compensate for the 1D nature of SSMs, which multiplies computational cost (e.g., 4× for VMamba) without fully exploiting the multi-directional information. The paper addresses how to adapt SSMs to 2D visual data for efficient non-local information exploration without this prohibitive overhead.

## Input / Output

The network takes a blurry RGB image of arbitrary resolution (H×W×3) and outputs a deblurred RGB image via residual learning: the encoder-decoder predicts a residual that is added to the input. Training uses progressive patch sizes (128×128 then 256×256) with a combined L1 pixel loss and L1 frequency-domain loss (λ=0.1). The model is trained on GoPro (2103 images) and RealBlur (3758 images), and evaluated zero-shot on HIDE, as well as cross-task on deraining and dehazing benchmarks.

## Method

EVSSM is a 3-level encoder-decoder (48 base channels, [6,6,12] EVSS modules per level) whose core building block is the Efficient Visual Scan (EVS) module. Instead of scanning in multiple directions simultaneously, the EVS block performs a single-direction S6 selective scan preceded by an alternating geometric transformation (horizontal+vertical flip for odd modules, transpose for even modules), so that the spatial orientation cycles back to original every 4 modules at zero extra FLOPs. Additionally, a 1D depthwise convolution (kernel 7) is applied to the SSM parameters B, C, and Δ after their linear projection, enabling them to encode local spatial context rather than identical channel-wise information. To complement the SSM's global focus, an Efficient Discriminative Frequency-domain FFN (EDFFN) performs FFT-based frequency screening at the output stage of the FFN (where channel count equals input) rather than at the expanded mid-stage, halving runtime while preserving performance. The full model achieves 34.51 dB PSNR on GoPro with only 126G FLOPs and 89 ms runtime, outperforming FFTformer (131G, 132 ms) and all prior SSM-based methods.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, EVSSM achieves 34.51 dB PSNR and 0.9713 SSIM, surpassing the previous best (FFTformer) by 0.30 dB. On the HIDE dataset (zero-shot generalization), it reaches 31.99 dB PSNR and 0.9503 SSIM, outperforming all competitors by at least 0.35 dB. On the real-world RealBlur dataset, EVSSM improves PSNR by 0.58 dB (RealBlur-J: 34.34) and 0.58 dB (RealBlur-R: 41.27) over the strongest baseline (MLWNet). The method also generalizes to deraining (49.00 dB on Rain2000H) and dehazing (32.05 dB on RESIDE-6K), setting new state-of-the-art on both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVSSM outperforms all 25 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. On GoPro, it leads the strongest Transformer-based method FFTformer [19] by 0.30 dB PSNR (34.51 vs. 34.21) and the best CNN-based method GRL [22] by 0.58 dB, while also surpassing the only other SSM-based method CU-mamba [6] by 0.98 dB. On the zero-shot HIDE benchmark, the margin over FFTformer widens to 0.37 dB (31.99 vs. 31.62), confirming better generalization. On the real-world RealBlur dataset, EVSSM beats the closest competitor MLWNet [12] by 0.58 dB on RealBlur-R (41.27 vs. 40.69) and 0.50 dB on RealBlur-J (34.34 vs. 33.84). For the secondary tasks, EVSSM achieves 49.00 dB on deraining (vs. 48.54 for DRSformer [3]) and 32.05 dB on dehazing (vs. 31.45 for DehazeFormer [33]), demonstrating broad applicability beyond deblurring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba's S6 selective scanning mechanism and the hierarchical encoder-decoder framework from FFTformer (Kong et al., CVPR 2023), which it also improves upon by relocating the frequency-domain quantization to the end of the FFN. Its key departure from prior visual SSM methods (VMamba, MambaIR, VMambaIR) is replacing multi-direction scanning with a single-direction scan preceded by alternating geometric transformations (flip and transpose), reducing computational cost to that of a single scan while still capturing multi-directional context. The addition of a 1D depthwise convolution to enrich the SSM parameters B, C, and Δ draws on the transformer insight that local context should be characterized before global aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving state-of-the-art deblurring at roughly one-quarter the computational cost of multi-direction SSM baselines, making SSM-based restoration viable for high-resolution images where quadratic-attention Transformers become prohibitive. The geometric transformation trick is simple enough to be adopted by other SSM-based visual architectures beyond deblurring. However, the authors acknowledge that only flip and transpose are explored; more powerful transformations (e.g., polar coordinates) remain unexplored, and the method's reliance on a fixed encoder-decoder hierarchy limits its flexibility for tasks with very different spatial structures.
