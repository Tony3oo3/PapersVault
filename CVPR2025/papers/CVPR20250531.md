## Problem

QPD sensors provide four sub-views enabling both horizontal and vertical disparity, but their uneven illumination, extremely narrow baseline (disparity range of only ±4 pixels), and the lack of any real-world QPD disparity dataset make disparity estimation fundamentally harder than conventional stereo matching. No prior method effectively fuses the two-directional disparity information, and existing DP-based approaches either rely on PSF symmetry (which breaks under real-world noise and illumination) or treat the problem as standard stereo matching (which fails due to the tiny disparity range).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250531/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes QPD four sub-images (each 3000×2000, split from a 50MP sensor) or DP left-right sub-image pairs as input and produces a full-resolution sub-pixel disparity map. The disparity range is constrained to −4 to +4 pixels, far smaller than conventional stereo datasets like KITTI or Middlebury, which necessitates sub-pixel-level refinement. For QPD input, the network processes both the horizontal pair (left/right) and vertical pair (up/down) independently before fusing the two directional estimates.

## Method

DPNet builds on RAFT-stereo's GRU-based iterative refinement but introduces two key modules: an illumination-invariant feature extractor using four differential convolutions (central, angular, horizontal-Sobel, vertical-Sobel) to obtain edge-based features robust to the uneven brightness between QPD sub-views, and a coarse-to-fine sub-pixel refiner that warps features to the coarse disparity, constructs a 32-group correlation volume over a ±2-pixel search window with bilinear sub-pixel shifts, applies 3D cost aggregation, and recovers sub-pixel offset via soft argmin. QuadNet extends DPNet by running it on both horizontal and vertical sub-image pairs, then fusing the two disparity maps through an edge-aware fusion module that uses HSDC/VSDC-extracted edge features as spatially varying reliability weights (horizontal edges favor vertical disparity and vice versa), followed by a census-transform-based warp error refinement through an hourglass network to suppress residual fusion artifacts. Training is fully supervised with a weighted L1 loss over iterative predictions; DPNet is trained first, then QuadNet is trained with DPNet weights frozen and only the fusion module updated.

## Results

On the QPD2K dataset, QuadNet achieves the best performance across all metrics: bad 0.3 = 0.229, bad 0.5 = 0.114, bad 1 = 0.017, and EPE = 0.201, outperforming the strongest stereo-matching baseline DLNR (bad 0.3 = 0.329, EPE = 0.272) by ~30% in bad 0.3 and ~26% in EPE. DPNet alone also surpasses all stereo-matching baselines, achieving bad 0.3 = 0.273 and EPE = 0.238. For zero-shot generalization, DPNet achieves the best AI(1) of 0.034 on DP5K and 0.025 on DP-disp, outperforming the next-best method (CCA) by ~37.5% on DP-disp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper groups baselines into three categories: PSF-symmetry methods (DPdisp, SFBD, CCA), stereo-matching methods (Mc-stereo, S-RAFT, S-IEGV, IEGV-Stereo, DLNR), and QPD-specific methods (QPDNet). QuadNet decisively outperforms all baselines on QPD2K, with the largest margins over PSF-symmetry methods (e.g., 4.2× lower EPE vs. DPdisp) and the QPD-specific QPDNet (4.9× lower EPE). Among stereo-matching baselines, DLNR is the strongest competitor (EPE 0.272), but QuadNet still improves by 26% in EPE and 30% in bad 0.3. DPNet alone already surpasses all stereo-matching methods, confirming that the illumination-invariant and coarse-to-fine modules are the primary drivers of improvement. On zero-shot DP-disp, DPNet achieves AI(1)=0.025 vs. CCA's 0.041 (~37.5% improvement), and on DP5K it leads with AI(1)=0.034 vs. SFBD's 0.078. The only area where the gap narrows is bad 1 on QPD2K, where DPNet (0.033) and DLNR (0.034) are nearly tied, suggesting both handle large-error pixels comparably.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RAFT-Stereo's GRU-based iterative refinement and correlation volume architecture, adapting it to the QPD/DP sensor domain where the disparity range is extremely small (±4 pixels) and illumination is uneven across sub-views. It departs from prior DP disparity methods (DPdisp, SFBD, CCA) that rely on PSF symmetry or treat the problem as standard stereo matching, instead introducing an illumination-invariant feature extractor based on differential convolution and a coarse-to-fine sub-pixel refinement inspired by video super-resolution. The key conceptual extension is the QuadNet's edge-aware fusion of horizontal and vertical disparity, which exploits QPD's four-sub-view structure in a way that no prior DP method addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The QPD2K dataset is the first real-world QPD disparity benchmark, filling a critical gap that has limited learning-based research on QPD sensors now widely deployed in mobile phones. The practical relevance is high: accurate QPD disparity could enable better autofocus, depth-aware image restoration (deblurring, deraining), and AR applications on consumer devices. However, the dataset is relatively small (2,100 images), ground truth is obtained indirectly via structured light rather than direct measurement, and the disparity range is very narrow, limiting generalizability. Open questions include whether the two-directional fusion strategy transfers to other multi-sub-view sensors and how to acquire reliable disparity ground truth in uncontrolled natural scenes without structured light.
