## Problem

Existing text-conditioned Human Scene Interaction (HSI) methods rely on task-specific cVAE or diffusion models that support only a single control modality, lack cross-task generalization, and treat 3D scenes, human motion, and language as isolated modalities without explicit alignment. This results in inflexible adaptation across interaction scenarios and physically implausible motions in diverse 3D environments. The paper asks whether a unified, general-purpose LLM-based framework can simultaneously handle diverse control signals and multiple HSI-related tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D scene point clouds (N_s × 6 RGB points), free-text descriptions, optional key-frame poses (initial, last, or random), and per-frame scene affordance maps (l2 distance fields between scene points and skeleton joints) as control conditions, all organized into a single instruction-following prompt. Outputs are discrete motion tokens from a VQ-VAE codebook (decoded to SMPL-X 3D body mesh sequences), natural-language motion captions, or completed/in-betweened motion sequences. Notably, scene features remain continuous (projected via MLP into LLM embedding space) while motion is discretized, and the LLM's output can flexibly denote text, motion tokens, or both depending on the task prompt.

## Method

HSI-GPT applies the autoregressive next-token prediction paradigm of LLMs to the HSI domain by extending LLaMA 3-8B's vocabulary with VQ-VAE-quantized motion tokens and a Multi-modal Interaction Aggregator (MIA) that uses Q-Former-style learnable query tokens to compress 3D point-cloud and affordance-map features into a fixed-length set of scene tokens projected into the LLM's embedding space. A three-stage training pipeline first trains the motion-aware VQ-VAE (with EMA and codebook reset to prevent collapse), then performs progressive scene-motion-language alignment by jointly training the MIA and projection layers on interleaved scene-motion-text triplets while freezing the LLM and 3D encoders (using LoRA for the LLM), and finally conducts unified instruction tuning with task-specific prompts across HSI generation, captioning, and completion tasks. The key insight is treating human motion as a non-verbal 'body language' that can be seamlessly interleaved with text tokens in a shared vocabulary, enabling any-to-any translation between scene, motion, and language through a single LLM.

## Results

On the HUMANISE dataset (primary HSI task), HSI-GPT achieves a contact score of 92.31% and non-collision rate of 99.82%, outperforming the cVAE baseline (84.06% contact) and MotionGPT (87.92% contact), though it trails the 2-stage Afford-Motion model on goal distance (0.182 vs. 0.156). On HumanML3D, HSI-GPT attains R-Precision Top-1 of 0.495, Top-3 of 0.788, FID of 0.187, and Multi-Modal Distance of 3.058, surpassing all LLM-based motion generation methods (MotionGPT, MotionLLM) while remaining competitive with diffusion-based specialists. On the Novel Evaluation Set for zero-shot generalization, HSI-GPT achieves R-Precision Top-3 of 0.587 and FID of 6.452 with affordance maps, outperforming Afford-Motion variants (Top-3 0.478, FID 7.887).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary HSI task (HUMANISE), HSI-GPT clearly outperforms cVAE [52] on contact (92.31% vs. 84.06%) and non-collision (99.82% vs. 99.77%), and beats both MotionGPT variants [18, 70] on contact by 4–8 percentage points, though it trails the 2-stage Afford-Motion [53] on goal distance (0.182 vs. 0.156) and APD (3.492 vs. 2.459). On HumanML3D, HSI-GPT surpasses all LLM-based baselines—MotionGPT [70] (Top-1 0.364, FID 0.805), MotionGPT [18] (Top-1 0.492, FID 0.232), and MotionLLM [54] (Top-1 0.482, FID 0.491)—while remaining within 0.004 of the best diffusion-based methods (AttT2M [72] Top-1 0.499; T2M-GPT [63] FID 0.116). Against Afford-Motion [53] on the Novel Evaluation Set, HSI-GPT improves R-Precision Top-3 from 0.478 to 0.587 and FID from 7.887 to 6.452 with affordance maps, while also achieving higher contact (85.47% vs. 71.98%). For HSI captioning, HSI-GPT leads TM2T [10] and MotionGPT [18] on R-Precision Top-1 (0.551 vs. 0.516/0.543), ROUGE (38.4 vs. 38.1/37.4), and CIDEr (30.8 vs. 16.8/29.2). In HSI completion, HSI-GPT achieves the best FID for motion prediction (0.572 vs. MDM [44] 6.031, MotionGPT [18] 0.905) and the lowest ADE for in-between (3.745 vs. 3.787/3.762), though MotionGPT [18] retains a slight FID edge on in-between (0.214 vs. 0.468).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant cVAE and diffusion-model paradigm for Human Scene Interaction (HSI) generation (HUMANISE, Afford-Motion, TeSMo) by adopting the autoregressive next-token prediction paradigm of LLMs. It directly builds on Afford-Motion's pre-trained 3D scene and affordance-map encoders for scene representation, and adapts BLIP-2's Q-Former learnable-query mechanism into a Multi-modal Interaction Aggregator for aligning permutation-invariant point-cloud features with the LLM's sequential token space. Conceptually, it extends the motion-as-foreign-language idea from MotionGPT/MotionPT (which handle scene-agnostic text-to-motion) into a unified scene-motion-language vocabulary, and generalizes single-task HSI models into a multi-task instruction-following framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single LLM-based model, fine-tuned with only ~1% of parameters via LoRA, can match or approach specialist diffusion/cVAE models across multiple HSI tasks (generation, captioning, completion) while gaining zero-shot transfer to unseen 3D scenes—a capability task-specific architectures lack. However, the performance gains over strong baselines like Afford-Motion's 2-stage variant are modest (e.g., Goal Dist 0.182 vs. 0.156), and the framework still depends on externally pre-trained scene encoders rather than learning scene understanding end-to-end. Open questions include whether this paradigm scales to multi-agent interactions, longer-horizon tasks, or real-time generation, and whether the VQ-VAE motion tokenizer's discrete bottleneck limits expressiveness for fine-grained contact dynamics.
