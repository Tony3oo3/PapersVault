## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods depend heavily on high-quality, multi-view-consistent images and produce poor results on degraded scenes (underwater, nighttime, hazy) where appearance varies non-uniformly across views. Prior restoration approaches are tailored to a single degradation type, leaving no unified framework that can handle multiple degraded scene categories simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250781/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes degraded multi-view RGB images (20–130 views per scene) along with camera poses estimated by COLMAP, and requires no clean ground-truth views during training. It outputs a restored 3D Gaussian scene renderable in real-time, along with per-pixel physical components: a normalized depth map, an exponential attenuation map A(x)=e^(−βd(x)), and a spatially varying illumination map L. The hazy-scene experiments additionally synthesize degraded views by combining β∈[0.1,0.8] and L∈[0.45,0.6] with estimated depth.

## Method

RestorGS introduces two collaborative modules on top of standard 3DGS. First, Appearance Decoupling embeds each Gaussian's position and spherical-harmonic coefficients into a 2-layer MLP that predicts an affine transform (η, δ) mapping degraded SH to clear SH, while a separate set of learnable degradation factors serves as the SH for a degraded Gaussian sharing all other attributes (position, scale, rotation, opacity) with the clear one. Second, Depth-Guided Modeling rasterizes the clear Gaussian to obtain a depth map, computes the attenuation map via A(x)=e^(−βd(x)), and feeds the rasterized degraded features concatenated with A(x) into a 3-layer CNN to predict the illumination map L; the degraded view is then reconstructed through the unified scattering equation I(x)=J(x)·A(x)+L·(1−A(x)). Training is fully self-supervised, jointly optimizing Gaussian parameters, the color MLP, and the CNN with an L1+D-SSIM reconstruction loss, an exposure regularizer on L, and a total-variation penalty on the depth map.

## Results

On the SeaThru-NeRF underwater dataset, RestorGS achieves an average PSNR of 29.19 dB across four scenes, outperforming the strongest baseline WaterSplatting (28.75 dB) by ~0.45 dB on average, with gains up to 2.59 dB on Curacao. On the Gaussian-DK nighttime dataset (12 scenes), RestorGS reaches an average PSNR of 25.04 dB, surpassing Gaussian-DK (24.70 dB) by 0.34 dB. On the Mip-NeRF360 hazy scenes, RestorGS achieves 28.32 dB (Garden) and 24.86 dB (Bicycle), beating all baselines on both PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. The method also achieves the best LPIPS scores across all three datasets, e.g., 0.055 on Curacao vs. 0.147 for WaterSplatting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RestorGS outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of scenes across all three datasets. Against WaterSplatting (the strongest underwater-specific baseline), RestorGS wins on 3 of 4 SeaThru-NeRF scenes in PSNR (e.g., +2.59 dB on Curacao, +0.83 dB on J.G. Red Sea) but loses on Panama (30.79 vs. 32.51 dB). Against Gaussian-DK (the strongest nighttime-specific baseline), RestorGS achieves a 0.34 dB average PSNR gain across all 12 Gaussian-DK scenes, with the largest margin on Staircase (+0.92 dB). Against vanilla 3DGS, RestorGS gains 3.64 dB on average in underwater scenes and 6.49 dB in nighttime scenes, confirming that the degradation-aware design is essential. NeRF-based baselines (SeaThru-NeRF, ZipNeRF, NeRF-W, HDR-NeRF) are consistently outperformed by 5–10 dB in PSNR, reflecting both the efficiency advantage of Gaussian splatting and the benefit of the unified physical modeling. On the hazy Mip-NeRF360 scenes, RestorGS beats WaterSplatting by 0.50 dB (Garden) and 2.19 dB (Bicycle) in PSNR, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.056 vs. 0.128 on Garden).

## Relation to Prior Work

RestorGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) by introducing a unified physical degradation model that subsumes previously scene-specific approaches: SeaThru-NeRF (underwater), Gaussian-DK (nighttime), and WaterSplatting (underwater) each handle one degradation type with tailored architectures. The key conceptual departure is treating all degradation types through a single scattering/attenuation formulation (Eq. 8) and decoupling clear from degraded appearance via spherical-harmonic affine mapping, rather than designing separate volumetric or exposure models per scene type. The depth-guided attenuation map connects to classical atmospheric scattering and underwater image formation models, but is applied here to optimize 3D Gaussian attributes in a self-supervised manner rather than restoring 2D images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to handle underwater, nighttime, and hazy 3D scenes within a single architecture, which is practically valuable for applications like underwater robotics or autonomous driving where degradation type is unknown a priori. However, the physical model remains simplified (single scattering coefficient β, no wavelength-dependent absorption), and depth is estimated from a modified 3DGS rather than ground truth, which may limit accuracy in highly occluded or textureless regions. Open questions include generalization to additional degradation types (rain, motion blur, sensor noise), scalability to large-scale outdoor scenes, and whether the unified physical model can be extended to dynamic or multi-material scenes.
