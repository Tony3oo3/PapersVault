## Problem

Existing caption-driven KBVQA methods generate general image descriptions that lack the specific visual detail needed to answer diverse questions, while alternatives like PromptCap cause the captioner to directly answer the question rather than providing evidence for the LLM to reason over. This undermines the desired separation between image observation and knowledge-based reasoning, where the captioner should describe what is visible and the LLM should supply world knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252294/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an RGB image and a free-form text question as input. The QACap module (a BLIP-based captioner) produces a single question-aware caption that describes only the visually relevant content for that specific question. This caption, combined with a task instruction and the original question, is fed to an off-the-shelf LLM (e.g., Claude 3.5) with zero in-context shots, which outputs a one-word/phrase or open-ended VQA answer. No external knowledge base, bounding boxes, or additional visual features are required at inference time.

## Method

QACap fine-tunes BLIP with three key components: (1) a Question-Aware Feature Distilling (QAFD) module that computes token-wise and sentence-wise similarity between BERT-encoded question features and ViT-encoded image patch features via cross-attention, then modulates image features through element-wise multiplication and a weighted mean to amplify question-relevant regions; (2) a Question-Aware Regional Consistency Learning (QARCL) loss that applies Jensen-Shannon divergence between the cross-attention maps of the QAFD module and the caption decoder's final cross-attention layer, forcing the model to attend to the same image regions during question interpretation and caption generation; and (3) Prompt-Enhanced Tuning (PET) that prepends the question as a prompt to the caption target. Training is two-stage (PromptCap dataset, then a GPT-4-curated QACap dataset of 29,569 entries with answer-leakage filtering and CLIP-scored caption selection), optimizing caption negative log-likelihood plus the JS consistency loss end-to-end.

## Results

On the OKVQA validation set, QACap paired with Claude 3.5 achieves 68.2% accuracy, setting a new state-of-the-art and surpassing the previous best caption+LLM method (Prophet, 61.1%) by 7.1 points and the best multimodal LLM (PaLI-X-VPD, 66.8%) by 1.4 points. On A-OKVQA, QACap+Claude 3.5 reaches 66.3% on Direct Answer and 76.7% on Multiple Choice (71.5% average), outperforming the prior best caption+LLM method PromptCap+GPT-3 (56.3% DA / 73.2% MC) by 10.0 and 3.5 points respectively. Notably, QACap uses only captions as image representation with zero in-context learning shots, whereas several competing methods rely on additional visual features, tags, or external knowledge bases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QACap+Claude 3.5 (68.2%) outperforms all 14 compared methods on OKVQA, with the largest margins over caption+LLM baselines: +7.8 over Prophet (61.1%), +7.8 over TOA (60.6%), +7.8 over PromptCap (60.4%), and +10.2 over KAT (54.4%). Against multimodal LLMs, QACap surpasses PaLI-X-VPD (66.8%) by 1.4 points and InstructBLIP (62.1%) by 6.1 points, despite using only a caption (no raw visual features) and zero in-context shots. On A-OKVQA, QACap leads PromptCap+GPT-3 by 10.0 points on Direct Answer (66.3% vs. 56.3%) and by 3.5 points on Multiple Choice (76.7% vs. 73.2%), and exceeds the next-best non-LLM method GPV-2 (60.3% DA / 48.6% MC) by a wide margin. The paper also shows that swapping the LLM (e.g., Intern2.5-20B) can push A-OKVQA MC to 74.8%, highlighting the plug-and-play nature of the caption module. Overall, QACap's advantage is most pronounced in open-ended (Direct Answer) settings where question-aware captions provide critical visual context that general captions miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the caption-then-LLM paradigm for KBVQA established by PICa, PromptCap, and Prophet, departing from two key limitations: general captions that lack question-specific detail, and captioners that inadvertently answer the question directly (violating the separation of visual observation from knowledge retrieval). Architecturally, QACap is fine-tuned from BLIP and adopts PromptCap's prompt-enhanced tuning (PET), but introduces two novel components — Question-Aware Feature Distillation (QAFD) to modulate image features by question relevance, and Question-Aware Regional Consistency Learning (QARCL) to align attention maps between question understanding and caption generation. The GPT-4-constructed training dataset extends the PromptCap annotation pipeline with explicit answer-leakage filtering and CLIP-based truthfulness scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (lightweight captioner + any off-the-shelf LLM) makes the method easy to upgrade as LLMs improve, and the question-aware captioning principle is broadly applicable beyond KBVQA to any task where a text intermediary bridges vision and reasoning. However, the approach remains limited to single-image, single-question settings and cannot handle spatial or fine-grained visual reasoning that resists textual description. Open questions include whether the GPT-4-constructed dataset introduces subtle biases that transfer to downstream performance, and whether the QAFD/QARCL modules generalize to more complex multi-step or compositional questions where a single caption is insufficient.
