## Problem

Statistical analysis of 4D surfaces (3D shapes evolving over time) traditionally requires upfront discretization in both space and time before computing spatiotemporal registration, geodesics, and summary statistics, leading to solutions whose quality depends on mesh resolution and temporal sampling density. This paper demonstrates that such discretization is unnecessary and shows how to perform full functional shape analysis directly on continuous representations of genus-0 4D surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251800/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sequences of genus-0 triangular meshes (spherically parameterized via Praun-Hoppe) as input and overfits an MLP to produce a continuous mapping F: S² × [0,1] → [-1,1]³. For registration, it accepts a pair of such D-SNS representations and outputs a rotation, a spatial diffeomorphism on S², and a temporal diffeomorphism on [0,1]. Geodesics and Karcher means are computed in the SRVF space and mapped back analytically; discretization is only required for visualization, not for any analysis step.

## Method

The core representation is a 6-block residual MLP (1024 nodes/block, SoftPlus activation, positional encoding on S² and [0,1]) that maps spherical coordinates and time to 3D surface points, with differential properties (normals, tangents) computed via automatic differentiation. Spatial registration maps surfaces to Square Root Normal Fields (SRNFs), where the elastic bending/stretching metric reduces to an L² norm; the spatial diffeomorphism is parameterized as a weighted sum of spherical harmonics and optimized alternately with rotation (via SVD) using gradient descent. Temporal registration projects 4D surfaces into a low-dimensional PCA space, then maps the resulting curves to Square Root Velocity Fields (SRVFs) where the elastic metric again becomes L²; a small MLP with sigmoid output and a monotonicity penalty learns the time-warping diffeomorphism. Geodesics are straight lines in SRVF space inverted analytically, and the Karcher mean is computed as the L² minimizer over all warped SRVF trajectories.

## Results

The D-SNS representation achieves pointwise L² errors on the order of 10⁻⁶ across all four datasets (e.g., 1.60×10⁻⁶ mean on DFAUST, 0.89×10⁻⁶ on VOCA), confirming faithful continuous representation of genus-0 4D surfaces. For spatiotemporal registration, the proposed method reduces the post-registration geodesic distance compared to 4D Atlas on all four datasets, with the largest gains on CAPE (0.4597 vs. 0.5358 mean) and VOCA (0.1171 vs. 0.1768 mean). Under sparse temporal sampling (25 frames), the proposed method maintains stable alignment error while 4D Atlas degrades, demonstrating the advantage of the continuous representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is 4D Atlas [19] (Laga et al., IEEE TPAMI 2023), the only prior method specifically designed for statistical analysis of 4D surfaces. The proposed D-SNS framework outperforms 4D Atlas on all four datasets in terms of post-registration geodesic distance: CAPE (0.4597 vs. 0.5358, ~14% reduction), DFAUST (0.7944 vs. 0.8891, ~11% reduction), COMA (0.0456 vs. 0.0478, ~5% reduction), and VOCA (0.1171 vs. 0.1768, ~34% reduction). The advantage is most pronounced on VOCA (faces) and CAPE (bodies with clothing), where larger deformations and articulation make elastic registration more challenging. A key differentiator is robustness to temporal sampling density: at 50 temporal samples both methods perform comparably, but at 25 samples the 4D Atlas error increases noticeably while the D-SNS method remains stable, attributable to its continuous (non-discretized) formulation. No comparison is made against discrete mesh-based registration pipelines or volumetric 4D methods, as the paper positions itself as the first to perform functional (continuous) 4D shape analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Neural Surface Maps (Morreale et al., CVPR 2021), which represent a static 3D surface as an MLP mapping from a 2D domain to 3D, to the dynamic 4D setting by adding a temporal input, yielding Dynamic Spherical Neural Surfaces (D-SNS). The statistical analysis pipeline is built on the SRNF framework (Jermyn et al., 2017; Laga et al., 2017) for spatial registration and the SRVF framework (Srivastava et al., 2010) for temporal registration, both of which convert complex elastic Riemannian metrics into simple L² metrics. The key departure from prior 4D shape analysis work, particularly 4D Atlas (Laga et al., TPAMI 2023), is that all operations—registration, geodesics, and mean computation—are performed directly on the continuous neural representation rather than on discretized point clouds, eliminating the need for upfront meshing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a principled foundation for 'functional' 4D shape analysis, showing that the continuous neural representation is less sensitive to temporal sampling density than discrete methods, which could reduce data requirements for 4D shape statistics. However, the framework is restricted to genus-0 surfaces, requires spherical parameterization as a preprocessing step, and is validated against only a single baseline (4D Atlas) on four relatively small datasets. Open questions include whether the continuous approach scales to higher-genus topologies, whether it outperforms well-discretized baselines at larger scale, and how it handles surfaces with changing topology over time.
