## Problem

Existing sewing pattern generation models (e.g., DressCode, SewFormer) are uni-modal, require large paired datasets, and produce oversimplified patterns with stitching errors because neural networks provide only statistical approximations rather than the centimeter-level geometric precision that garment construction demands. The multimodal nature of real design concepts (images, text, sketches, or combinations) further exceeds the capacity of these direct vector-quantized generation approaches.

## Input / Output

The system accepts any combination of text prompts, fashion photographs, or designer sketches as design input. Rather than outputting a raw vector pattern, it produces a GarmentCode parametric program plus a fixed-length configuration of 122 design parameters (topological and geometrical), which is at least 10× more compact than DressCode's 1,500-token sequence and remains constant regardless of pattern complexity. Executing this program through the GarmentCode engine yields a 2D sewing pattern and a 3D draped garment on a standard body model.

## Method

Design2GarmentCode is a neurosymbolic pipeline with three agents: a LoRA-finetuned LLM (DSL-GA) that learns GarmentCode syntax via code-commenting pairs and generates structured multiple-choice queries; a pre-trained LMM (MMUA) that interprets the multi-modal design input by answering those queries; and the GarmentCode execution engine that renders the final pattern. A lightweight text-conditioned transformer decoder (projector Ψ) converts the MMUA's descriptive answers (e.g., 'three-quarter length') into precise geometric parameters using type-based quantization with λ=100 for centimeter-level precision. The system incorporates two validation loops: rule-based checks during synthesis to ensure sufficient information for complete programs, and a post-generation comparison where the MMUA identifies discrepancies and issues correction instructions for iterative refinement.

## Results

On text-guided generation, Design2GarmentCode achieves a 100% Simulation Success Rate (SSR) versus DressCode's 84%, with Agreement scores of 79.83% vs. 7.17% and Aesthetic scores of 68.17% vs. 9.50% from a 30-participant user study. On image-guided generation, it reaches 94% SSR (vs. SewFormer's 65.33%), 88.67% Agreement (vs. 3.33%), and 77% Aesthetic (vs. 5.33%), while also improving geometric accuracy (F-Score 0.829 vs. 0.708; Chamfer Distance 2.091 vs. 9.7). In terms of diversity, the method produces more complex patterns with higher average panel, edge, and stitch counts in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Design2GarmentCode is compared against two baselines: DressCode (text-guided) and SewFormer (image-guided). Against DressCode, the method wins decisively on every reported metric: SSR improves from 84% to 100%, Agreement jumps from 7.17% to 79.83%, and Aesthetic from 9.50% to 68.17%, indicating that DressCode frequently produces structurally invalid or design-inconsistent patterns. Against SewFormer, the gains are equally large: SSR rises from 65.33% to 94%, Agreement from 3.33% to 88.67%, and Aesthetic from 5.33% to 77%, while geometric fidelity improves substantially (F-Score 0.829 vs. 0.708; Chamfer Distance 2.091 vs. 9.7). On diversity metrics, the method generates patterns with more panels, edges, and stitches in both settings, suggesting it captures richer design detail rather than defaulting to simplified shapes. The paper does not report any metric where a baseline outperforms the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GarmentCode (Korosteleva & Sorkine-Hornung, ACM TOG 2023), a domain-specific language for parametric sewing patterns, repurposing it as the target output of a program-synthesis pipeline rather than a manual authoring tool. It departs from prior learning-based sewing pattern generators (DressCode, SewFormer, NeuralTailor) that train vector-quantized decoders from scratch on paired design-pattern data, instead following the neurosymbolic paradigm (Ritchie et al., 2023) where an LLM synthesizes executable programs and an LMM interprets multimodal design inputs. The method also draws on the LLM-for-code-generation literature (Codex, AlphaCode) and DSL fine-tuning work (Bassamzadeh & Methani, 2024), applying LoRA-based adaptation to align a pre-trained LLM's latent pattern-making knowledge with GarmentCode's specific syntax.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach significantly lowers the data and compute barrier for sewing pattern generation by leveraging pre-trained LMM knowledge and requiring only LoRA fine-tuning plus a lightweight projector, rather than training large vector-quantized models from scratch. The multimodal interface (text, image, sketch, or combinations) and instruction-based editing open practical pathways for fashion designers to iterate on patterns without CAD expertise. A key limitation is that the system cannot substantially alter GarmentCode's underlying structure and logistics, meaning generation quality is bounded by the DSL's expressiveness for novel or unconventional garment designs. Open questions include whether the program-synthesis paradigm generalizes beyond GarmentCode's component vocabulary and how to handle designs that require new geometric primitives not yet encoded in the DSL.
