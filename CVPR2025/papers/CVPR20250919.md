## Problem

Existing BIQA methods that model image degradation apply distortions uniformly across entire images, establishing only global distortion priors. This is inadequate for authentic images where distortions are spatially heterogeneous, and it ignores the human visual system's dual strategy of local detection for subtle distortions and global perception for severe ones. The paper addresses this gap by explicitly modeling and leveraging fine-grained, spatially varying local distortions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250919/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a scalar quality score. During training, the input image is additionally processed through a Block-wise Degradation module that partitions it into K=4 vertical blocks and applies independently sampled distortions (from 24 types at 4 severity levels) to each block, yielding a spatially heterogeneous degraded image. The ViT encoder processes both the original and degraded images, with the embedding sequence augmented by K learnable DIS tokens (one per block) alongside the standard CLS token and image patches, adding only 0.2M parameters to the base ViT-S architecture.

## Method

SHDIQA introduces three key components. First, Block-wise Degradation Modelling applies distinct random distortions to different spatial blocks of the image, generating composite local distortion priors with both image-level multi-class and patch-level single-class labels. Second, Block-wise Aggregation and Filtering uses attention masks to constrain each DIS token to attend only to its corresponding distorted region, then a cross-attention adaptation head (with the CLS token as query attending to DIS features as key/value) filters quality-irrelevant distortion information before predicting the score via an MLP. Third, a Distortion-Guided Knowledge Distillation strategy combines Contrastive KD (treating teacher-student feature pairs from corresponding blocks as positives and cross-block pairs as negatives in a pseudo-classification loss) with Affinity KD (aligning pairwise cosine-similarity matrices between student and teacher DIS features via KL divergence), jointly improving distortion discrimination and inter-block relational modeling.

## Results

SHDIQA (24.2M params) achieves the best SRCC and PLCC across all 7 benchmark datasets. The most striking gain is on the largest authentic dataset LIVEFB, where SHDIQA reaches SRCC 0.639 / PLCC 0.735, surpassing the previous best (LoDa, 0.578/0.679) by over 6 SRCC points. On synthetic datasets, it also leads: LIVE (0.982/0.984), CSIQ (0.962/0.972), KADID (0.937/0.940), and on authentic datasets LIVEC (0.909/0.922), KonIQ (0.937/0.948), SPAQ (0.930/0.934). The model uses only 24.2M parameters, comparable to DEIQT (24M) and QPT (24M), yet outperforms much larger models like TReS (152M) and LoDa (118M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHDIQA outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset where results are reported. The largest margin is on LIVEFB (the hardest authentic dataset with 39,810 images), where SHDIQA's SRCC of 0.639 exceeds LoDa (0.578) by 6.1 points and QPT (0.575) by 6.4 points, confirming the benefit of spatially-heterogeneous local distortion modelling. On synthetic datasets (LIVE, CSIQ, KADID), SHDIQA leads by 0.1–3.5 SRCC points over the nearest competitors (QFM-IQM, LoDa, LIQE). On authentic datasets LIVEC and KonIQ, it beats LoDa by 3.3 and 0.5 SRCC points respectively, and surpasses DEIQT (its architectural base) by 3.4 and 1.6 points. Notably, SHDIQA achieves these gains with only 24.2M parameters, matching DEIQT and QPT in size while outperforming much larger models like TReS (152M) and LoDa (118M) across all benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DEIQT encoder-decoder architecture (ViT-S from DeiT-III with a one-layer decoder) and departs from prior degradation-modeling methods (Re-IQA, QPT) that apply distortions uniformly across entire images. Instead of modifying the ViT architecture for local feature extraction (as in LoDa, TReS), SHDIQA introduces spatially heterogeneous block-wise distortions during training and uses dedicated DIS tokens with attention masking to attend to specific degraded regions. The knowledge distillation components (CKD and AKD) extend standard KD from model compression into a distortion-discrimination and inter-block relationship modeling framework, which is a novel application of KD in the IQA context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~6-point SRCC gap on LIVEFB (the largest and hardest authentic dataset) over prior SOTA suggests that spatially localized distortion awareness is a critical missing piece for authentic BIQA, and the block-wise degradation strategy offers a practical, low-parameter (24.2M) way to inject this awareness. The data-efficiency results (20% training data approaching full-data SOTA) and gMAD competition generalization evidence strengthen the practical case. Open questions include whether the fixed 4-block partitioning generalizes to arbitrary image aspect ratios, whether the teacher-student KD overhead is justified at scale, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or multi-frame quality assessment where temporal distortion heterogeneity also matters.
