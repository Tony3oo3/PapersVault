## Problem

Knowledge distillation in recognition models naturally transfers unseen concepts to the student, but this phenomenon does not hold for conditional diffusion models: a student distilled on a subset of text conditions fails to generate images for conditions never encountered during training. Covering the full text-condition space would require generating and caching images for every prompt, which is prohibitively expensive in computation, time, and storage. The paper addresses this gap by enabling image-free distillation that still allows the student to generalize to unseen conditions.

## Input / Output

At inference, the compressed model takes a free-form text prompt and produces a 512×512 RGB image via iterative denoising in latent space, using the same Stable Diffusion v1.4 pipeline (CLIP text encoder + U-Net denoiser + VAE decoder). During training, the method consumes a small cache of teacher-generated images (e.g., 10K–212K) paired with a much larger pool of text prompts (up to 20M from LAION or 2.2M GPT-generated), so the number of available conditions far exceeds the number of available images. The output is a smaller U-Net (0.40B–0.76B parameters vs. 1.04B teacher) that can be further accelerated by step-reduction methods.

## Method

The core technique, random conditioning, replaces the original text condition of a noised training image with a randomly sampled prompt from a larger text dataset with a timestep-dependent probability p(t), typically an exponential function that is high at large t (noisy) and low at small t (clean). This is motivated by the empirical observation that at high timesteps the input is nearly pure noise so the condition fully determines the output, while at low timesteps the image content dominates and the condition has minimal effect—making the pairing between image and text largely irrelevant at either extreme. The student is trained with a combined loss of noise-prediction MSE (matching teacher ε) and per-block feature-matching MSE, using temporary projection modules to align architectures of differing sizes. Because the condition is decoupled from the image, the student explores the full text-condition space without requiring a generated image for every prompt, enabling image-free (or even fully data-free with LLM-generated captions) distillation that transfers knowledge of unseen concepts.

## Results

On MS-COCO 30K, the proposed B-Base model (0.76B params, 0 real images) achieves FID 14.47, IS 36.50, and CLIP 0.2932, outperforming the same-architecture BK-SDM-Base (FID 15.76, IS 33.79, CLIP 0.2878) trained with 220K real images and teacher initialization. The most aggressively compressed C-Micro model (0.40B params) reaches FID 13.42, IS 32.64, and CLIP 0.2813, surpassing BK-SDM-Small (0.66B params) across all metrics despite using 50% fewer UNet parameters and no real images. The C-Tiny variant (0.49B) achieves FID 13.90, beating even the larger BK-SDM-Base. All proposed models are trained entirely without real images, relying solely on teacher-generated images and random conditioning with additional text prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline BK-SDM (same compressed architectures, trained with 220K real images and teacher weight initialization), the proposed method wins on all three metrics at every size: B-Base improves FID from 15.76→14.47, IS from 33.79→36.50, and CLIP from 0.2878→0.2932; C-Micro (0.40B) beats BK-SDM-Small (0.66B) on FID (13.42 vs 16.98), IS (32.64 vs 31.68), and CLIP (0.2813 vs 0.2677) despite 40% fewer total parameters. Compared to the teacher SD v1.4 (FID 13.05, IS 36.76, CLIP 0.2958), B-Base closes most of the gap (FID 14.47, IS 36.50, CLIP 0.2932) while using only 73% of the parameters and zero real images. Against larger models, C-Tiny (0.49B) and C-Micro (0.40B) achieve lower FID than GLIDE (12.24), LDM-KL-8-G (12.63), and SnapFusion (~13.6), but trail DALL·E-2 (10.39), SDXL (12.15), and SD 3.5 Medium (16.23 FID but higher IS/CLIP). The channel-compressed C-Small (0.61B) achieves FID 14.43, matching C-Base (0.73B) at 16% fewer parameters, demonstrating that channel reduction is more parameter-efficient than block removal for this distillation setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation framework for diffusion model compression established by BK-SDM [24] and KOALA [27], which compress Stable Diffusion via block pruning and feature-level distillation. It draws a sharp conceptual contrast with classical distillation in recognition tasks (Hinton et al., Ojha et al.), where students naturally acquire knowledge of unseen classes, by demonstrating that this property breaks down for conditional diffusion models due to the high-dimensional output space and timestep-dependent conditioning. The proposed random conditioning technique departs from the standard paired image-text training paradigm by decoupling the noised input from its original text condition, enabling exploration of a much larger condition space without generating images for every prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The image-free distillation setting addresses a critical practical bottleneck: collecting large-scale, properly licensed image-text pairs is expensive and legally constrained, making this approach directly relevant to deploying diffusion models in resource-limited or privacy-sensitive environments. The negative result—that conditional diffusion models fail to transfer unseen concept knowledge without explicit condition coverage—reframes how practitioners should think about distillation data requirements for generative models. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to video and audio diffusion, whether more advanced teachers (SDXL, SD 3.5) yield proportionally better students, and whether the empirically-tuned exponential p(t) schedule has a principled theoretical justification. The approach is currently validated only on text-to-image with U-Net architectures, leaving diffusion transformers and other modalities unexplored.
