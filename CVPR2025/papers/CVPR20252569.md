## Problem

Deploying large vision-language models on resource-limited devices requires reducing model complexity, but conventional automatic pruning methods rely on the original model's training data to evaluate and select pruning policies. For LVLMs trained on web-scale corpora, this search cost is prohibitively high and the training data is often unavailable, leaving a gap between the few proxy samples used for policy search and the true data distribution that the pruned model must generalize to.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252569/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LVLM (LLaVA-SQA-7B or LLaVA-v1.5-7B) and 64 generic English text segments of 256 tokens each, randomly sampled from the Penn Treebank corpus with no task-specific data, as the sole calibration signal. It produces a pruned model with per-layer unstructured sparsity ratios assigned to MLP and attention weight matrices in the LLaMA backbone, along with an optimized vision projector. The visual encoder and projector are excluded from pruning to avoid disproportionate performance degradation from minimal computational savings.

## Method

EfficientLLaVA formulates the generalization gap between proxy samples and the unknown training distribution via the structural risk minimization principle, approximating the Rademacher complexity as the product of Frobenius norms across all pruned weight matrices. An evolutionary algorithm then searches over per-layer pruning ratios (drawn from a range around a target average sparsity) to maximize a fitness function combining empirical loss on the proxy samples and the norm-based generalization penalty. In parallel, the vision projector weights are iteratively optimized to raise the upper bound of accuracy and generalization for all policies in the search space, with candidate importance weighted by inverse Euclidean distance to their nearest neighbors. Given the selected ratios, the OBS algorithm performs the actual weight removal, and the entire search-prune-evolve loop repeats for 10 rounds with 16 candidates per round, requiring only 64 proxy samples total.

## Results

On ScienceQA with LLaVA-SQA-7B at 50% average pruning ratio, EfficientLLaVA achieves 83.05% accuracy, outperforming SparseGPT (80.45%) by 2.6 points while delivering a ×1.8 inference speedup over the dense model. On LLaVA-v1.5-7B at the same 50% ratio, EfficientLLaVA reaches 57.51% on ScienceQA, 50.28% on VizWiz, 31.70% on MM-Vet, and 53.00% on LLaVA-Bench. The method uses only 64 proxy text samples (256 tokens each) from the Penn Treebank for pruning policy search, requiring no task-specific data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EfficientLLaVA is compared against two LLM pruning baselines: SparseGPT (unstructured one-shot pruning via OBS) and LLM-Pruner (structural pruning via gradient-based importance). Against SparseGPT, EfficientLLaVA consistently wins at every pruning ratio on all four benchmarks: at 50% sparsity it gains +2.6 pts on ScienceQA (83.05 vs 80.45), +11.7 pts on VizWiz (50.28 vs 38.61), +13.8 pts on MM-Vet (31.70 vs 17.90), and +4.2 pts on LLaVA-Bench (53.00 vs 48.80) for LLaVA-v1.5-7B. Against LLM-Pruner, the gap is extreme: LLM-Pruner's accuracy collapses to near zero (0.28% on ScienceQA, 0.00% on VizWiz) at 40%+ pruning ratio, rendering it non-functional, while EfficientLLaVA retains strong performance. The key differentiator is the structural risk minimization objective, which prevents overfitting to the 64 proxy samples and yields policies that generalize to unseen data distributions, whereas SparseGPT ignores this generalization gap entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends automatic pruning from the LLM domain (SparseGPT, LLM-Pruner) to large vision-language models, addressing the critical constraint that LVLM training corpora are web-scale and inaccessible for policy search. It builds on the OBS weight-removal mechanism from SparseGPT and the norm-based Rademacher complexity bound from Neyshabur et al. to formulate a generalization-aware fitness function, departing from conventional automatic pruning methods that evaluate candidates on the original training data. The search space evolution step—iteratively optimizing the vision projector to raise the upper bound of achievable accuracy across all pruning policies—is a novel component not present in prior LLM pruning or NAS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EfficientLLaVA provides a practical pathway to deploy LVLMs on resource-constrained devices by achieving 1.8× speedup at 50% sparsity with only 64 proxy text samples, sidestepping the prohibitive cost of searching over web-scale training data. However, the evaluation is limited to LLaVA-family architectures and unstructured sparsity on CPU inference, leaving open whether the SRM-based generalization objective transfers to structured pruning, activation sparsity, or GPU-accelerated sparse kernels. The authors themselves note the need for more efficient search algorithms and activation-level pruning to reach higher compression ratios, suggesting the current method is a first step rather than a complete solution for edge deployment.
