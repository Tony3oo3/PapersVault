## Problem

Existing multi-person holistic human motion capture systems cascade a detector, tracker, and single-frame pose/shape estimator, which causes error propagation from the tracker, prevents end-to-end training, and scales inference time linearly with the number of people. No prior method estimates the full motions of all individuals in a video within a single differentiable network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250850/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular multi-person video of N frames, each resized to 448×448 (zero-padded to preserve aspect ratio), and at inference splits arbitrary-length videos into 16-frame clips with 8-frame overlap connected via 2D keypoint matching. It outputs per-person SMPL-X parameters (pose θ∈R^{53×3}, shape β∈R^{10}, expression α∈R^{10}) for every frame, along with auxiliary anchor heatmaps, per-frame visibility scores, and a global motion confidence used for filtering the 100 learned queries down to the actual number of people present.

## Method

Inspired by DETR, each of 100 learned motion queries is responsible for one person's entire motion sequence. A ViT backbone extracts per-frame features aggregated by 3D convolutions; the queries then perform cross-attention with each frame's features, followed by temporal self-attention to integrate information across the full sequence. Dynamic convolution, controlled by the temporally-consistent queries, produces per-person anchor heatmaps that implicitly perform detection and tracking. A temporal motion decoder concatenates anchor pixel features with the updated queries, applies another temporal self-attention for smoothing, and cross-attends with spatiotemporal features to regress final SMPL-X parameters. Training uses Hungarian bipartite matching between queries and ground-truth persons with a composite loss (trace, SMPL-X L1, 2D/3D joint, visibility, confidence, and bounding box terms), and the authors introduce the M3C synthetic dataset (6K clips, 1M frames, rendered in Unreal Engine 5 with moving cameras) to provide the multi-person whole-body annotations needed for end-to-end training.

## Results

On the BEDLAM whole-body benchmark, MaQ achieves 128 ID switches, 95.15 MOTA, 97.01 IDF1, and 97.73 HOTA, substantially outperforming the Multi-HMR + ByteTrack cascaded baseline (515 IDs, 91.35 MOTA, 92.54 IDF1, 84.59 HOTA) and the Fast-RCNN + SMPLer-X + ByteTrack baseline (1660 IDs, 78.83 MOTA). In terms of pose accuracy, MaQ reports 79.88 MPJPE and 86.40 PVE, slightly better than Multi-HMR + ByteTrack (81.58 MPJPE, 88.88 PVE) but with a small PAMPJPE gap (45.56 vs. 42.93). Inference time drops to 0.027 s per frame, roughly 7× faster than Multi-HMR + ByteTrack (0.201 s) and 17× faster than the Fast-RCNN cascade (0.463 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two cascaded whole-body baselines on BEDLAM, MaQ wins decisively on all four tracking metrics: it reduces ID switches from 515 (Multi-HMR + ByteTrack) and 1660 (Fast-RCNN + SMPLer-X + ByteTrack) to just 128, and lifts HOTA from 84.59 and 70.41 to 97.73. On pose accuracy, MaQ slightly trails Multi-HMR + ByteTrack on PAMPJPE (45.56 vs. 42.93) but leads on MPJPE (79.88 vs. 81.58) and PVE (86.40 vs. 88.88), while being ~7× faster at inference. On the Dyna3DPW tracking subset, MaQ achieves 0 ID switches and 99.8 IDF1, both the best among all methods, and 99.6 MOTA (marginally above TRACE's 99.3), though it trails TRACE on HOTA (70.8 vs. 74.7). On 3DPW video evaluation without ground-truth bounding boxes, MaQ (PAMPJPE 44.7, MPJPE 72.6, PVE 84.9) outperforms all other video-based methods (ROMP, BEV, TRACE) but remains slightly behind the single-frame Multi-HMR (PAMPJPE 43.8, MPJPE 64.6, PVE 79.7), which benefits from GT bounding boxes and dedicated single-frame training data. Overall, MaQ's one-stage design trades a small single-frame HPS accuracy gap for large gains in tracking consistency and a dramatic reduction in inference latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the DETR-style query mechanism (Carion et al., ECCV 2020) to the temporal domain, where each query tracks one person's motion across an entire video clip rather than detecting one object in a single frame. It departs from the dominant cascaded paradigm (detector → tracker → HPS estimator, e.g., Multi-HMR + ByteTrack) by unifying all three functions into a single end-to-end network, and incorporates dynamic convolution (Chen et al., CVPR 2020) as the localization operator. The M3C dataset extends the synthetic-data line (BEDLAM, HumanVid, M3Act) by uniquely combining multi-person scenes, moving cameras, and ground-truth SMPL-X annotations in outdoor settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup over cascaded baselines (0.027 s vs. 0.201 s per clip) makes real-time multi-person whole-body motion capture practically viable for applications like VR, gaming, and robotics. The M3C dataset addresses a genuine gap—no prior dataset simultaneously offers multi-person, moving-camera, and full-body SMPL-X ground truth—enabling future one-stage models to be trained at scale. Open challenges remain for individuals severely occluded over long periods, and the current 16-frame clip length limits applicability to longer sequences; the authors note that more robust temporal attention and larger challenging datasets are needed.
