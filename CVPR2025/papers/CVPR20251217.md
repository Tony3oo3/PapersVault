## Problem

Multimodal survival prediction models require complete pathology and genomic data, but clinical settings often have missing modalities due to cost and privacy constraints. Existing incomplete-modality methods (generation-based imputation, retrieval, and imputation-free approaches) only compensate for modality-common information, failing to recover the modality-specific knowledge unique to the missing modality. This paper addresses the gap of simultaneously compensating both common and specific information for missing modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251217/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pathology whole-slide images (processed as bags of patch embeddings via a frozen encoder) and genomic pathway gene-expression vectors (330 pathways) as input, where either or both modalities may be absent. Output is a set of hazard probabilities across discretized survival time intervals, from which cumulative survival probabilities are derived; the model handles right-censored labels via a negative log-likelihood loss. Due to the extreme size of WSIs (~100,000×100,000 pixels), the method operates in a multiple-instance-learning paradigm where only a subset of top-K discriminative patches is forwarded to the LLM.

## Method

DisPro is a two-stage prompt learning framework built on BioBERT. In Stage 1 (UniPro), CoOp-style learnable class-specific prompts are extended to the MIL setting: for each modality, prompts are distilled from all available samples to encode the modality's knowledge distribution, with TopK max-pooling producing slide-level hazard predictions. In Stage 2 (MultiPro), available modality tokens are concatenated with learnable placeholders for the missing modality and fed to the LLM, whose self-attention captures modality-common information for the missing modality. UniPro Distillation then supervises the imputed representations by enforcing alignment with the Stage-1 unimodal prompts (injecting modality-specific knowledge), while UniPro Scoring reuses both UniPro modules plus a self-attention mechanism to select the most discriminative and relevant tokens before LLM input, addressing the high redundancy in WSIs and pathways.

## Results

Under the primary 60% training missing rate setting, DisPro achieves average C-Index of 0.6796 (pathology-only test), 0.6802 (genomics-only test), and 0.7021 (complete test) across five TCGA cancer datasets, outperforming the best incomplete-modality baseline (M3Care) by +2.33% on complete test and +1.76% on pathology-only test. Notably, DisPro trained with 60% missing data surpasses the complete-multimodal SOTA (MOTCat, C-Index 0.6691) by +3.30% on the complete test scenario. When trained with full data (0% missing), DisPro further improves to 0.7113 average C-Index on complete test, a +2.57% gain over the best incomplete-modality method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisPro outperforms all five incomplete-modality baselines across every test scenario and dataset. Against the strongest incomplete-modality competitor M3Care [40], DisPro gains +2.33% C-Index on complete test (0.7021 vs. 0.6788) and +1.76% on pathology-only test. HGCN [9] is the second-best incomplete method (0.6745 complete test), trailing DisPro by 2.76%. COM [27] and MUSE [34] lag further behind at 0.6719 and 0.6576 respectively. MAP [17], the most recent LLM-based prompting method, performs worst among incomplete baselines (0.6340), suggesting that its missing-aware prompts capture only modality-common information without compensating modality-specific knowledge. Remarkably, DisPro trained with 60% missing data surpasses the complete-multimodal SOTA MOTCat [36] (0.6691) by 3.30% on complete test, and also exceeds unimodal baselines TransMIL [28] (0.6563) and SNN [15] (0.6671) when inferring from a single modality, demonstrating efficient multimodal knowledge transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CoOp prompt-learning paradigm from small natural images to the multiple-instance-learning setting for gigapixel whole-slide images, and repurposes it as a knowledge-distillation mechanism for missing-modality compensation. It departs from prior incomplete-multimodal approaches (generation-based imputation, retrieval-based imputation, and imputation-free robustness methods) by arguing that all of them only recover modality-common information, and introduces a two-stage decomposition—Unimodal Prompting for modality-specific knowledge and Multimodal Prompting for modality-common knowledge—that explicitly targets both. The LLM-based prompting line initiated by MAP [17] is the closest prior work, but DisPro adds the distilled unimodal supervision (UniPro Distillation) and the token-selection mechanism (UniPro Scoring) that MAP lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: genomic sequencing costs and privacy constraints make complete multimodal data unrealistic in many clinical settings, and a method that degrades gracefully under missing modalities is directly deployable. The finding that training with 60% missing data can match or exceed a complete-data SOTA (MOTCat) on several datasets suggests that LLM-based prompting may be a more data-efficient route than simply collecting more complete samples. However, the 'LLM' used is BioBERT (110M parameters), so the broader claim about leveraging LLM robustness remains untested at truly large scale, and the framework is validated only on two modalities (pathology + genomics) across five TCGA cancer types, leaving open whether the two-stage decomposition generalizes to three or more modalities or to non-survival clinical tasks.
