## Problem

Existing controllable image generation methods require separate specialized models or adapters for each type of conditional input (e.g., ControlNet for edge/depth maps, IP-Adapter for subject images), increasing training costs and complicating multi-condition integration. While unified approaches like UniControl and OmniGen exist, they either train separate sub-adapters per control type or require multi-stage full-model retraining, making them less efficient and not fully unified. The paper asks whether a single lightweight adapter, built on a pre-trained DiT-based model, can handle diverse conditional images guided by task instructions without retraining the base model.

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a natural-language text prompt, a short task instruction (e.g., 'Generate an image from this edge map'), and a conditional image that can be one of 14 types including Canny/HED edge maps, depth maps, segmentation maps, subject content images, and style-reference images. The conditional image is encoded via the VAE into latent features, while the task instruction is encoded by CLIP-G/14, CLIP-L/15, and T5-XXL text encoders. The output is a single generated image that simultaneously respects the text prompt, the spatial/content/style information in the conditional image, and the semantics specified by the task instruction.

## Method

UNIC-Adapter is built on SD3's MM-DiT architecture and introduces two new inputs—task instruction and conditional image—processed through a series of N adapter blocks where instruction features (Z_ist) and conditional image features (Z_con) mutually attend to each other via full cross-attention, enabling them to dynamically refine one another. The resulting features are injected into the main image generation branch through a cross-attention mechanism where image features serve as the query and both instruction and conditional image features serve as concatenated keys and values, with a dedicated linear projection L_cross^q for the query. To support pixel-level spatial control, 1-D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) is applied separately along height and width dimensions to both query and key features, ensuring spatially aligned attention. Training freezes the base SD3 model and the feed-forward layers in adapter blocks, optimizing approximately 1.2B newly introduced parameters on 16 H100 GPUs for 100K steps, with the adapter initialized from the base model's parameters to reduce learning difficulty.

## Results

On pixel-level spatial control (Table 1), UNIC-Adapter (SD3) achieves the best Canny F1 score (38.94) and HED SSIM (0.8369) among all methods, surpassing the best single-task method ControlNet++ (37.04, 0.8097) and the best multi-task method OmniGen (35.54, 0.8237). On segmentation (mIoU 42.89) and depth (RMSE 31.10), it is competitive but slightly behind OmniGen (44.23, 28.54) and ControlNet++ (43.64, 28.32). For subject-driven generation (Table 2), UNIC-Adapter achieves the highest DINO score (0.816) and CLIP-T score (0.306), while OmniGen edges it out on CLIP-I (0.847 vs. 0.841).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIC-Adapter outperforms all single-task baselines (ControlNet, ControlNet++, T2I-Adapter, Gligen) on Canny and HED metrics, with the largest gap over ControlNet (38.94 vs. 34.65 F1 on Canny). Against the strongest multi-task competitor OmniGen, UNIC-Adapter wins on Canny (+3.4 F1) and HED (+0.013 SSIM) but trails on segmentation (42.89 vs. 44.23 mIoU) and depth (31.10 vs. 28.54 RMSE), suggesting OmniGen's tokenized-sequence approach retains a slight edge on dense spatial tasks. Compared to UniControl and Uni-ControlNet, UNIC-Adapter dominates across all four pixel-level metrics, with particularly large margins on segmentation (42.89 vs. 25.44 and 19.39 mIoU). On subject-driven generation, UNIC-Adapter achieves the best DINO (0.816) and CLIP-T (0.306) scores, beating OmniGen (0.801, 0.301) and Kosmos-G (0.694, 0.287), though OmniGen marginally leads on CLIP-I (0.847 vs. 0.841). The ablation-reproduced baselines ControlNet* and IP-Adapter-Instruct* confirm the trade-off: ControlNet* matches UNIC-Adapter on pixel-level tasks but degrades subject fidelity (DINO 0.749 vs. 0.784), while IP-Adapter-Instruct* excels on subject tasks (CLIP-I 0.852) but fails on pixel-level control (Canny F1 15.81 vs. 37.95).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SD3's MM-DiT architecture, repurposing its full cross-attention mechanism (where text and image features attend to each other bidirectionally) as the core of a lightweight adapter rather than the main generation backbone. It departs from the ControlNet/UniControl line of work, which uses parallel encoders or task-specific adapters per condition type, and from IP-Adapter/Instruct-Imagen, which injects compressed CLIP embeddings via cross-attention, by instead processing full-resolution VAE-encoded conditional images through MM-DiT blocks alongside task instructions. Unlike OmniGen and Instruct-Imagen, which require multi-stage training of the entire model, UNIC-Adapter freezes the base SD3 and trains only ~1.2B adapter parameters, positioning itself as an efficient plug-in for existing DiT-based T2I models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in replacing a zoo of specialized control models (one per condition type) with a single adapter that handles 14 diverse conditional inputs, reducing deployment complexity and training cost for practitioners. The ablation cleanly isolating the complementary roles of K_con (spatial fidelity) and K_ist (semantic/task interpretation) provides useful design guidance for future unified conditioning architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, whether multiple simultaneous conditional images (e.g., depth + subject + style) can be handled without additional architectural changes, and whether the 1.2B trainable parameter budget can be reduced further while maintaining cross-task performance.
