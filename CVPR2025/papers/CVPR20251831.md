## Problem

Directly applying vanilla diffusion policy to end-to-end autonomous driving suffers from two critical issues: the 20 DDIM denoising steps are too slow for real-time driving (reducing FPS from 60 to 7), and different Gaussian noise samples collapse to nearly identical trajectories, failing to capture the inherent multi-mode nature of driving behaviors. Existing multi-mode alternatives like VADv2 rely on a large fixed vocabulary of 8192 anchor trajectories, which is computationally heavy and fundamentally limited to in-vocabulary scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three cropped forward-facing camera images concatenated into a single 1024×256 RGB image and a rasterized BEV LiDAR point cloud, processed by a shared ResNet-34 perception backbone into BEV features and agent/map queries. It outputs a set of N_infer ego-vehicle trajectories (each an 8-waypoint sequence of (x,y) coordinates over 4 seconds in the ego coordinate frame) along with per-trajectory confidence scores; the top-1 scoring trajectory is selected for evaluation, while the full set enables diverse multi-mode planning. A key design feature is inference flexibility: the model is trained with 20 anchors but can sample any number of trajectories at test time.

## Method

DiffusionDrive introduces a truncated diffusion policy that replaces the standard Gaussian starting distribution with an anchored Gaussian distribution: 20 K-Means-clustered anchor trajectories are perturbed with only a small fraction of noise (schedule truncated at 50/1000), so the model learns to denoise from near-anchor states rather than pure noise. This allows the denoising process to be truncated from 20 steps to just 2, achieving a 10× speedup. The diffusion decoder is a lightweight transformer (60M params) that at each denoising step applies deformable spatial cross-attention to BEV features, cross-attention to agent/map queries, timestep modulation, and an MLP predicting confidence and coordinate offsets; two such decoder layers are stacked in a cascade to iteratively refine trajectories. Training combines L1 reconstruction loss on the positive sample (noisy trajectory nearest to ground truth) with BCE classification over all anchors, and inference selects the highest-confidence trajectory after 2 denoising steps.

## Results

On the NAVSIM navtest split with a ResNet-34 backbone, DiffusionDrive achieves a record 88.1 PDMS (NC 98.2, DAC 96.2, TTC 94.7, Comf. 100, EP 82.2) using only 20 anchors and 2 denoising steps, running at 45 FPS on an NVIDIA 4090. On nuScenes with a ResNet-50 backbone, DiffusionDrive achieves the lowest average L2 error of 0.57 m and a 0.08% average collision rate, running 1.8× faster than VAD. The method reduces denoising steps by 10× compared to vanilla diffusion policy while improving mode diversity score from 11% to 74%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionDrive outperforms all baselines on NAVSIM navtest. Against the direct predecessor Transfuser (84.0 PDMS), it gains +4.1 PDMS across all sub-scores. Compared to VADv2-V₈₁₉₂ (80.9 PDMS), it improves by +7.2 PDMS while using 400× fewer anchors (20 vs. 8192). Against the strongest anchor-based method Hydra-MDP-V₈₁₉₂-W-EP (86.5 PDMS, which uses additional rule-based supervision and post-processing), DiffusionDrive still leads by +1.6 PDMS using only human demonstrations without post-processing. On nuScenes, DiffusionDrive (0.57 m avg L2, 0.08% collision) beats VAD (0.72 m, 0.22%) by 20.8% lower L2 and 63.6% lower collision rate while running 1.8× faster, and marginally improves over SparseDrive (0.61 m, 0.08%) by 0.04 m in L2 error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the robotic diffusion policy (Chi et al., RSS 2023) to end-to-end autonomous driving by building on Transfuser (Chitta et al., TPAMI 2022) as the base planner, replacing its deterministic MLP regression head with a conditional diffusion model. The key conceptual departure is from vanilla diffusion, which starts denoising from pure Gaussian noise, to a 'truncated diffusion' that begins from an anchored Gaussian distribution centered on K-Means-clustered driving patterns, reducing denoising steps from 20 to 2. This positions the work between the single-mode regression paradigm (Transfuser, UniAD, VAD) and the large-vocabulary sampling paradigm (VADv2, Hydra-MDP with 8192 anchors), using only 20 anchors as diffusion starting points rather than a fixed action vocabulary. For the nuScenes experiments, the method is integrated on top of SparseDrive's BEV-free architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: DiffusionDrive demonstrates that generative, multi-mode trajectory planning can run at real-time speed (45 FPS) for the first time, removing the primary barrier to deploying diffusion-based planners in production autonomous driving systems. The finding that vanilla diffusion policy suffers from severe mode collapse in driving (trajectories from different Gaussian noises converge to near-identical paths) is a useful negative result that motivates the anchored approach. Open questions include whether the truncated diffusion approach generalizes to reactive/closed-loop simulation, whether the 20-anchor K-Means clustering is sufficient for long-tail scenarios, and whether the method scales to higher-resolution or longer-horizon planning without additional denoising steps.
