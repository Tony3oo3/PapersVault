## Problem

Existing video-to-audio (Foley) methods are constrained by the limited scale of audio-visual training data (~550 hours in VGGSound), while alternatives that add control modules to pretrained text-to-audio models complicate the architecture and limit design flexibility. No prior approach jointly trains a single network on both audio-visual and the much larger audio-text data in a unified framework, missing the opportunity to leverage abundant text-audio pairs for learning richer audio semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (default 8s, generalizable to other durations via the absence of absolute position encoding) and an optional text prompt (class labels or free-form captions). Video is processed at 8 fps via CLIP for semantic features and at 24 fps via Synchformer for synchronization features; text is encoded as 77 CLIP tokens. Output is a Foley audio waveform (ambient sounds and event-triggered effects, excluding music and speech) generated in a 20-dimensional, 31.25 fps latent space and decoded through a VAE and BigVGAN vocoder at either 16 kHz or 44.1 kHz.

## Method

MMAudio introduces a multimodal joint training paradigm where a single transformer network processes video, audio, and text tokens simultaneously, masking absent modalities with learnable empty tokens so the same architecture trains on both audio-visual (VGGSound) and audio-text (AudioCaps, Clotho, WavCaps) data. The architecture combines N₁=4 MM-DiT blocks (joint attention across three modality branches, adapted from SD3) with N₂=8 audio-only blocks (from FLUX), using aligned RoPE positional embeddings that scale visual frequencies by 31.25/8 to match the audio frame rate, and 1D ConvMLPs instead of MLPs to capture local temporal structure. A conditional synchronization module extracts 24 fps features from Synchformer's visual encoder and injects them per-token via adaLN scales and biases in the audio stream, providing frame-level alignment beyond what cross-attention alone achieves. Training uses a conditional flow matching objective with classifier-free guidance (10% modality masking), and inference integrates the learned velocity field with 25 Euler steps at guidance strength 4.5, producing an 8s clip in ~1.23s on an H100 GPU.

## Results

On the VGGSound test set, MMAudio-S-16kHz (157M params) achieves the best distribution matching (FD_PaSST=70.19, FD_PANNs=5.22, FD_VGG=0.79), semantic alignment (IB-score=29.13), and temporal alignment (DeSync=0.483) among all public models, while generating an 8s clip in just 1.23s. The larger 44.1kHz variants further improve to FD_PaSST=60.60, IS=17.40, IB-score=33.22, and DeSync=0.442. On AudioCaps text-to-audio, MMAudio-M-44.1kHz attains state-of-the-art CLAP (0.351) and IS (12.02), and on Greatest Hits, MMAudio-S-16kHz achieves the best onset accuracy (0.7637), AP (0.9010), and F1 (0.7928).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMAudio-S-16kHz (157M) outperforms all video-to-audio baselines on distribution matching (FD_PaSST 70.19 vs. 84.57 for V2A-Mapper, the next best), temporal alignment (DeSync 0.483 vs. 0.654 for V-AURA), and inference speed (1.23s vs. 1.67s for FoleyCrafter), while using fewer parameters than every competitor. The sole exceptions are IB-score, where Seeing&Hearing (33.99) edges out MMAudio-S-16kHz (29.13) because SAH directly optimizes ImageBind at test time, and IS, where FoleyCrafter (15.68) slightly exceeds the 16kHz model (14.44) though the 44.1kHz variants surpass it (18.02). On text-to-audio (AudioCaps), MMAudio-M-44.1kHz achieves the best CLAP (0.351) and IS (12.02), beating GenAU-Large (CLAP 0.285, IS 11.75) and Make-An-Audio2 (CLAP 0.251, IS 9.58), though it trails on FD_VGG (4.07 vs. 1.21 for GenAU). On Greatest Hits, MMAudio-S-16kHz dominates all baselines on onset accuracy (0.7637 vs. 0.6949 for Frieren), AP (0.9010 vs. 0.8567 for V-AURA), and F1 (0.7928 vs. 0.6550 for Frieren), confirming the synchronization advantage with model-free metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMAudio adopts the MM-DiT block design from SD3 [8] and the audio-only single-modality blocks from FLUX [32], but departs from prior video-to-audio methods in its training paradigm: rather than training solely on scarce audio-visual data or bolting control modules onto pretrained text-to-audio models, it jointly trains on both audio-visual (VGGSound) and large-scale audio-text (WavCaps, AudioCaps, Clotho) data within a single unified transformer, masking absent modalities. For temporal alignment, it replaces handcrafted proxy features (onsets, energy, RMS) used by prior work with deep features from the self-supervised Synchformer [19], injected via a novel conditional synchronization module operating in adaLN space. The flow matching objective and VAE latent space follow Frieren [67], while the aligned RoPE positional embeddings and ConvMLP layers are new architectural contributions specific to cross-frame-rate multimodal sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multimodal joint training paradigm offers a practical data-scaling strategy for video-to-audio: by leveraging abundant audio-text data (7,600+ hours) alongside limited audio-visual data (~500 hours), it achieves SOTA with only 157M parameters and 1.23s inference, making high-quality Foley synthesis accessible. The finding that joint training does not degrade text-to-audio performance (and in fact yields competitive CLAP and IS scores) suggests this formulation is a general-purpose recipe for cross-modal generation. Key limitations include inability to generate intelligible speech and potential overfitting when audio-text data is removed; open questions include whether this paradigm extends to joint video+audio generation and whether the synchronization module's reliance on Synchformer features generalizes beyond the 24fps regime.
