## Problem

Existing training-free LLM-based video understanding methods suffer from two critical limitations: they flood the LLM with redundant, query-irrelevant frame captions (informational overload), and they flatten video content into an unstructured list of captions, ignoring the inherent coarse-to-fine hierarchical structure of long videos. This leads to both reduced reasoning accuracy and poor efficiency on long-form video question answering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251809/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (sampled at 1 FPS for EgoSchema/NExT-QA, 0.125 FPS for Video-MME) and a multiple-choice text query as input. It produces a single-letter answer (A–E) along with an intermediate hierarchical tree-based video representation where nodes at different depths correspond to different granularity levels of keyframes. The tree is human-readable, with each node associated with a keyframe caption, enabling interpretability of which visual evidence the LLM relied on.

## Method

VIDEOTREE is a training-free, three-stage framework that builds a query-adaptive hierarchical video representation. First, adaptive breadth expansion iteratively applies K-Means visual clustering (on EVA-CLIP-8B frame features), captions each cluster's keyframe via a VLM, and scores cluster relevance to the query using GPT-4 (1–3 scale), doubling the number of clusters until a threshold of highly-relevant clusters is met. Second, relevance-guided depth expansion sub-clusters high-relevance clusters into a two-level tree (branch width w) and moderately-relevant clusters into one additional level, allocating finer-grained keyframes only where the query demands detail. Finally, the tree is traversed root-to-leaf, keyframes are captioned and sorted temporally, and the concatenated description plus query is fed to GPT-4 for the final answer. The key design insight is a top-down, query-driven allocation of representational capacity—spending more frames on relevant, information-dense regions and fewer on irrelevant ones—rather than uniform dense sampling.

## Results

On EgoSchema (500-question subset), VIDEOTREE achieves 66.2% accuracy with GPT-4, outperforming the strongest training-free baselines LifelongMemory (64.1%) and VideoAgent (62.8%). On NExT-QA, VIDEOTREE reaches 75.6% average accuracy (70.6% Temporal, 76.5% Causal, 83.9% Descriptive), surpassing VideoAgent (71.3%) and LLoVi (67.7%). On the Video-MME long split (avg. 44-min videos), VIDEOTREE scores 54.2%, beating GPT-4V (53.5%) and LLoVi (48.8%) despite being training-free, though it trails GPT-4o (65.3%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (67.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among same-category baselines (open-source captioner + proprietary LLM), VIDEOTREE wins across the board: it beats LLoVi by 5.0% on EgoSchema subset (66.2 vs. 61.2) and 7.9% on NExT-QA average (75.6 vs. 67.7), and outperforms VideoAgent [67] by 6.0% on EgoSchema subset and 4.3% on NExT-QA average. Against the stronger LVNet (which uses GPT-4o for both captioning and reasoning), VIDEOTREE trails on EgoSchema subset (66.2 vs. 68.2) but matches on EgoSchema full (61.1) and leads on NExT-QA average (75.6 vs. 72.9). On Video-MME long split, VIDEOTREE (54.2%) surpasses GPT-4V (53.5%) by 0.7% and LLoVi (48.8%) by 5.4%, and outperforms several extensively-trained open-source MLLMs including VILA-1.5-40B (53.8%) and InternVL2-34B (52.6%), but remains below GPT-4o (65.3%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (67.4%). With open-source LLMs (Mistral 7B/12B), VIDEOTREE achieves 63.0%/71.0% on EgoSchema subset versus LangRepo's 60.8%/66.2%, while using only 32 captions vs. 180 and 72–73% less inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIDEOTREE carves new ground at the intersection of three existing threads: (1) training-free LLM-based video QA pipelines (LLoVi, VideoAgent, LangRepo) that treat video as a flat list of captions, (2) hierarchical video-language representations (HierVL, VideoReCap) that build structure bottom-up from all frames, and (3) query-adaptive keyframe selection. Its key conceptual departure is a top-down, query-adaptive tree construction: rather than captioning all frames and summarizing upward, it iteratively clusters, scores relevance, and descends only into query-relevant branches, allocating computational budget where the query demands detail. This repurposes K-Means clustering and VLM captioning as components of an LLM-guided search process rather than a fixed preprocessing pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a carefully structured, training-free pipeline of off-the-shelf components (EVA-CLIP + VLM captioner + GPT-4) can match or exceed a proprietary multimodal model (GPT-4V) on very long videos while using far fewer frames, suggesting that representation structure matters as much as model scale for long-video reasoning. However, a gap remains versus the strongest long-context MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro), and the method's performance is tightly coupled to the quality of the underlying LLM reasoner and captioner. Open questions include whether the tree structure generalizes to open-ended (non-multiple-choice) video QA, whether the relevance-scoring step can be replaced by learned signals to reduce LLM calls further, and how the approach scales to videos beyond one hour.
