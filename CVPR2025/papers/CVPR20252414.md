## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting stores a massive number of Gaussians, causing prohibitive memory usage that limits practical deployment. Existing simplification methods (Mini-Splatting, LightGaussian, LP-3DGS, etc.) rely on hand-crafted importance criteria for one-shot pruning, which causes irreversible information loss and degrades rendering quality even after extended retraining. The paper asks whether sparsity can be imposed gradually through an optimization framework that preserves information in the retained Gaussians.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252414/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (for SfM point-cloud initialization), the full set of 3DGS parameters (positions, covariance matrices, opacities, SH coefficients), and a user-specified target Gaussian count κ. It produces a compact 3DGS model with at most κ Gaussians (those with non-zero opacity) plus a light-tuning pass, yielding novel view renderings. Operationally, the 'optimizing-sparsifying' loop begins at iteration 15K of standard 3DGS training, and all near-zero-opacity Gaussians are physically removed at iteration 25K before a brief fine-tuning stage.

## Method

GaussianSpa formulates 3DGS simplification as a constrained optimization: minimize the standard reconstruction loss subject to an ℓ₀ norm constraint on the opacity vector (at most κ non-zeros). Because the ℓ₀ constraint is non-differentiable, the authors introduce an auxiliary variable z and solve the resulting problem via an ADMM-style 'optimizing-sparsifying' algorithm integrated into the 3DGS training loop. In the 'optimizing' step, gradient descent updates all 3DGS parameters with an extra quadratic regularization term that pulls opacities toward the sparse z. In the 'sparsifying' step, z is updated by an explicit proximal projection onto the ℓ₀ ball—keeping only the top-κ magnitude elements of (a + λ) and zeroing the rest. The dual multiplier λ is then updated, and the two steps alternate until ‖a − z‖² ≤ ε. This gradual, optimization-driven sparsification avoids the permanent information loss of one-shot pruning, allowing the retained Gaussians to absorb the contribution of removed ones.

## Results

GaussianSpa achieves the best PSNR across all three benchmarks while using substantially fewer Gaussians than vanilla 3DGS: 27.85 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (0.547M Gaussians vs. 3.110M), 23.98 dB on Tanks&Temples (0.269M vs. 1.830M), and 30.37 dB on Deep Blending (0.335M vs. 2.780M). This corresponds to a 0.4 dB average PSNR improvement over vanilla 3DGS on Mip-NeRF 360 and Tanks&Temples, and a 0.9 dB improvement on Deep Blending, with up to 10× fewer Gaussians. GaussianSpa also leads on SSIM and LPIPS across all datasets, confirming superior perceptual quality alongside the PSNR gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianSpa outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor Mini-Splatting [17], GaussianSpa gains +0.45 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 (27.85 vs. 27.40), +0.53 dB on Tanks&Temples (23.98 vs. 23.45), and +0.32 dB on Deep Blending (30.37 vs. 30.05), while using fewer Gaussians in every case (e.g., 0.269M vs. 0.319M on Tanks&Temples). Against the learnable-mask method LP-3DGS-R [51], the PSNR advantage is +0.38 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 and +0.38 dB on Tanks&Temples, with roughly 3.6× fewer Gaussians (0.547M vs. 1.959M). Compared to the hybrid-compression methods CompactGaussian [26] and CompGS [35], GaussianSpa leads by +0.77 dB and +0.73 dB PSNR respectively on Mip-NeRF 360, and by +0.58 dB and +0.47 dB on Deep Blending. On Tanks&Temples, GaussianSpa also edges out Taming 3DGS [31] (23.98 vs. 23.95 PSNR) despite using fewer Gaussians (0.269M vs. 0.290M). The quality-reduction-rate curves (Figure 7) further show GaussianSpa maintaining a consistent ~0.5 dB PSNR advantage over Mini-Splatting across multiple sparsity levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianSpa builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting training pipeline of Kerbl et al. [22], but departs fundamentally from the prevailing Gaussian simplification paradigm (Mini-Splatting, LightGaussian, LP-3DGS, CompactGaussian, CompGS) that relies on hand-crafted importance criteria for one-shot pruning. Instead, it reframes sparsification as a constrained optimization problem with an ℓ₀ norm on Gaussian opacities, solved via an ADMM-style 'optimizing-sparsifying' algorithm integrated into training. This connects the 3DGS compression literature to classical sparse optimization tools (proximal operators, alternating direction methods of multipliers) that had not previously been applied to point-based radiance field representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that gradual, optimization-driven sparsification during training can yield *higher* rendering quality than the unpruned model (up to 0.9 dB PSNR with 10× fewer Gaussians) challenges the implicit assumption in prior work that removing Gaussians necessarily degrades fidelity. This opens a path toward compact 3DGS models that are both smaller and better, which is practically important for real-time and resource-constrained applications. Open questions include whether the ADMM formulation scales to very large unbounded scenes, how it interacts with other compression axes (SH distillation, quantization) beyond the post-hoc add-on tested here, and whether the same 'gradual sparsification during training' principle transfers to other point-based or mesh-based representations.
