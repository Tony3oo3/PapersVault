## Problem

Training high-quality multi-environment world models like Genie requires expensive, labor-intensive collection of large video datasets with human or environment-specific agent action demonstrations. Even when using a random agent to avoid this cost, the resulting data is limited in exploration diversity, capping the model's visual fidelity and controllability. The paper asks: can we train and improve world models entirely from automatically collected virtual environment interactions, without any human annotation or environment-specific reward engineering?

## Input / Output

The world model consumes 16-frame sequences at 64×64 resolution along with a discrete 5-action sequence (left, right, up, down, jump) encoded as one-hot vectors concatenated to each Dynamics layer, and outputs T predicted future frames (upscaled to 256×256 via a U-Net superresolution network). The AutoExplore Agent separately takes 4 stacked, max-pooled frames as input and outputs an action per timestep, while its reward signal is computed internally by running the world model 5 steps back from the current observation and measuring classification entropy over 1024 codebook tokens per spatial position.

## Method

GenieRedux-G adapts the Genie architecture by discarding the Latent Action Model (since ground-truth actions are available from the agent) and replacing the Dynamics module's prediction head with a MaskGIT-style iterative token decoder over a 1024-entry codebook. Two key architectural changes improve fidelity: a skip connection that adds the learned token embedding to the token index itself, and a Token Distance Cross-Entropy (TDCE) loss that penalizes mispredictions proportionally to their cosine distance in the codebook rather than uniformly. The AutoExplore Agent is an actor-critic (CNN encoder + LSTM) trained with Policy Gradient whose reward is the mean entropy over the top-25% highest-uncertainty tokens of the world model's prediction, making it entirely environment-independent. The training pipeline pretrains on 200 platformer environments with a random agent, fine-tunes on 50 action-consistent environments, then uses the AutoExplore Agent to collect diverse 10k-session datasets per target environment for final fine-tuning of both the tokenizer decoder and the Dynamics module.

## Results

On the three evaluation environments, fine-tuning GenieRedux-G-50 with AutoExplore Agent data yields substantial gains over random-agent fine-tuning: PSNR improves from 27.04 to 30.60 on Adventure Island II (+3.56), from 34.54 to 36.13 on Super Mario Bros (+1.59), and from 21.83 to 27.45 on Smurfs (+5.62). Controllability (ΔPSNR) also improves, reaching 1.47 on Adventure Island II and 1.55 on Smurfs versus 1.19 and 0.65 for random fine-tuning. FID drops dramatically with exploration data (e.g., 42.34→12.77 on Adventure Island II, 80.61→20.01 on Smurfs), confirming that uncertainty-driven data collection exposes the model to visually diverse scenes. The paper reports up to 7.4 PSNR and up to 1.4 ΔPSNR improvements over random-agent pretraining across environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AutoExplore Agent against two curiosity-driven exploration baselines from Burda et al. (ICLR 2019): Random Feature (RF) distillation and VAE-based prediction error. In this comparison, lower PSNR, SSIM, and ΔPSNR indicate the world model performs worse on the agent-collected data, which is the desired outcome (the agent should target regions of maximum model uncertainty). AutoExplore achieves the lowest values on both environments—PSNR 23.81 / SSIM 0.85 / ΔPSNR 0.065 on Super Mario Bros and PSNR 15.20 / SSIM 0.41 / ΔPSNR 0.070 on Adventure Island II—outperforming RF (28.58 / 0.94 / 0.181 and 24.82 / 0.78 / 0.44) and VAE (24.40 / 0.86 / 0.087 and 16.57 / 0.50 / 0.072). This confirms that token-classification-entropy-based exploration is more effective at driving the world model into its uncertainty regions than feature-space or reconstruction-error-based curiosity signals. The gap is most pronounced on Adventure Island II, where RF's ΔPSNR (0.44) is over 6× higher than AutoExplore's (0.070), indicating RF fails to target the most uncertain regions as effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Genie [5] by providing an open-source reimplementation (GenieRedux) and extending it into GenieRedux-G, which replaces the Latent Action Model with ground-truth agent actions and introduces a Token Distance Cross-Entropy loss motivated by a tokenizer representation study. The exploration agent (AutoExplore) draws conceptual inspiration from curiosity-driven learning [6, 7] and Plan2Explore [53], but departs from them by using the world model's own token classification entropy as the sole reward signal, targeting world model improvement rather than agent performance. The MaskGIT architecture [8] underpins the Dynamics module, and the RetroAct dataset extends Stable Retro [48] with behavior and control annotations to enable multi-environment training with consistent action semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework significantly lowers the barrier to training multi-environment world models by eliminating the need for expensive human demonstrations or environment-specific reward engineering, making the pipeline accessible to groups without large-scale data collection infrastructure. The open-source GenieRedux/GenieRedux-G code and the annotated RetroAct dataset (974 environments) are practical resources for the community. However, the evaluation is confined to 64×64 retro platformer games across only three environments for the main exploration comparison, leaving open whether the uncertainty-driven exploration strategy and the TDCE loss generalize to higher-resolution, more visually diverse, or non-game environments. A key open question is whether the approach scales to the kind of open-ended, high-dimensional environments (e.g., driving, robotics) where Genie and GAIA-1 target.
