## Problem

Existing residual structures use feature addition mode (FAM) that retains too many low-level input features, causing a phenomenon the authors term 'network inertia' where adjacent modules produce nearly identical outputs and fail to learn meaningful new features. Additionally, Blueprint Separable Convolutions (BSConv) force blueprint scale transformation on all channels, but intra-kernel correlation does not exist uniformly across channels, introducing useless or disruptive features that hinder effective feature propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252722/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (H×W×C_in) as input, which is concatenated n times before shallow feature extraction, and produces a high-resolution RGB image at scale factors ×2, ×3, or ×4 via pixel-shuffle upsampling plus a bicubic-upsampled residual. During training, 64×64 LR patches are randomly cropped from DIV2K or DF2K training images, and the network is optimized with L1 loss. The full model (SLVR) has 485K parameters and 26.2G Mult-Adds at 1280×720 resolution for ×4, while a smaller variant SLVR-S uses 158K parameters for comparison with sub-200K methods.

## Method

The core contribution is B2Conv, which augments BSConv with an Access Control Unit (ACU) that adaptively gates which channels receive blueprint scale transformation. Concretely, the ACU produces a per-channel, per-blueprint coefficient T_{n,m} (softened via sigmoid to (0,1)) learned through backpropagation; the implementation decomposes into two 1×1 pointwise convolutions (for T and w), an element-wise sigmoid-gated multiplication, and a K×K depthwise convolution with the shared blueprint B. The second contribution is FDEL (Feature Diversity Evolution Link), which replaces the standard residual addition x_l + F_l(x_l) with a subtraction-based link that outputs the 'gain' (output minus input), thereby minimizing retention of low-level features and promoting diverse high-level feature evolution across stacked blocks. The full SLVR framework stacks 8 Efficient Controllable Distillation Blocks (ECDBs) built from Blueprint Controllable Residual Blocks (BCRBs using FDEL + B2Conv), aggregates intermediate features via a Diversified Feature Aggregation Strategy (DFAS) with GELU activation, refines with a final B2Conv, and uses long skip connections with pixel-shuffle upsampling for reconstruction.

## Results

SLVR achieves 28.78 dB PSNR on Set14 ×4 with only 485K parameters and 26.2G Mult-Adds, and 28.85 dB when trained on the larger DF2K dataset (SLVR*). On Set5 ×4, SLVR reaches 32.40 dB, outperforming the transformer-based NGswin (32.33 dB) with less than half the parameters (485K vs. 1019K) and 28% fewer FLOPs. The ultra-light SLVR-S variant (158K params, 8.5G) achieves 28.65 dB on Set14 ×4, surpassing SAFMN (240K params) by 0.05 dB. Across all three scale factors (×2, ×3, ×4), SLVR consistently ranks first or second among lightweight methods on all five benchmark datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLVR (485K) outperforms the transformer-based NGswin (1019K) on Set5 (32.40 vs 32.33 dB) and Manga109 (30.93 vs 30.80 dB) while using less than half the parameters and 28% fewer FLOPs, though NGswin edges ahead by 0.01–0.04 dB on BSD100 and Urban100. Against the closest same-parameter competitor OSFFNet (537K), SLVR wins on Set5 (+0.01 dB), Set14 (+0.03 dB), Urban100 (+0.05 dB), and Manga109 (+0.09 dB), with BSD100 essentially tied (27.65 vs 27.66). SLVR dominates the CNN-based baselines RFDN (550K) and BSRN (352K) by 0.14–0.35 dB across all benchmarks at ×4. The ultra-light SLVR-S (158K) beats SAFMN (240K) on Set5 (+0.02 dB), Set14 (+0.05 dB), Urban100 (+0.11 dB), and Manga109 (+0.10 dB) despite having 34% fewer parameters. On ×2 and ×3 scales, SLVR similarly leads all compared methods, achieving 33.87 dB on Set14 ×2 and 30.53 dB on Set14 ×3, surpassing NGswin by 0.08 and 0.00 dB respectively. The ablation in Table 2 confirms both FDEL (+0.02–0.07 dB) and B2Conv (+0.05–0.11 dB) contribute independently, with B2Conv providing the larger gain especially on texture-rich Urban100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two specific threads in lightweight SR: (1) BSConv (Haase & Amthor, CVPR 2020), which demonstrated that intra-kernel correlations along the depth axis allow decomposing convolutions into a shared blueprint plus per-channel scale factors, and (2) the residual block design in BSRN (Li et al., CVPRW 2022), which stacks ESDB modules using standard addition-mode skip connections. The authors identify two limitations—BSConv forces scale transformation on all channels even where correlation is absent, and addition-mode residuals retain low-level features that stall feature evolution—and address them with B2Conv (an access control unit that gates per-channel blueprint application) and FDEL (a subtraction-based link that passes only the 'gain' of each block). The overall SLVR framework is assembled from these two components within a BSRN-derived architecture, positioning the work as a targeted refinement of the blueprint-separable-convolution + residual-distillation lineage rather than a new architectural paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'network inertia' observation—that adjacent ESDB outputs in BSRN are nearly interchangeable (swapping one costs <0.005 dB)—is a concrete, if informal, diagnosis of why shallow residual stacks in lightweight SR may be underutilizing their depth. B2Conv and FDEL are presented as drop-in plugins (validated on BSRN, RFDN, and EDSR), which gives them practical utility beyond the SLVR framework itself. However, the performance gains are modest (typically 0.05–0.1 dB over strong baselines), the network-inertia analysis is limited to a single architecture (BSRN) without a formal metric, and the work is evaluated only on image SR, leaving open whether the FDEL/B2Conv principles transfer to video SR, restoration, or other low-level tasks. The ACU also adds a small parameter overhead (~63K in the RFDN comparison), which partially offsets the efficiency motivation.
