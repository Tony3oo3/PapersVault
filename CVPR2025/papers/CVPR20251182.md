## Problem

Existing text-to-point-cloud localization methods (Text2Pos, RET, Text2Loc) treat all 3D objects in a submap as equally relevant to the text query, ignoring the fact that natural language descriptions only mention a subset of nearby, significant objects. This partial relevance introduces uncertainty into cross-modal matching that degrades global submap representation and retrieval accuracy, yet no prior work explicitly models this uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language description (a set of hints describing spatial relationships between the target and surrounding objects) and a large-scale urban point cloud map divided into 30 m cubic submaps with 10 m stride, where each submap contains semantically segmented object point clouds. The output is a 2D position (x, y) within the map, produced via a two-stage pipeline: top-k submap retrieval followed by fine-grained coordinate regression. Notably, the text query is inherently partial—it describes only a fraction of the objects present in the target submap—so the system must handle irrelevant objects gracefully rather than assuming full scene coverage.

## Method

CMMLoc uses a coarse-to-fine pipeline. In the coarse stage, a Cauchy-Mixture-Model-based Transformer (CMMT) replaces standard attention with Cauchy-weighted attention: multiple parallel Cauchy windows with different scale parameters assign element-wise Cauchy weights to attention scores, where weights are determined by semantic similarity (objects sharing the same segmentation label are grouped adjacently). The heavy-tailed Cauchy distribution naturally attenuates the influence of irrelevant objects without discarding them entirely. A spatial consolidation scheme then adaptively aggregates multi-scale object features via learnable cross-attention queries, followed by max pooling to produce the submap descriptor. In the fine stage, a pre-alignment step pre-trains the text and object encoders to minimize embedding distance between matched color/label features, and a Cardinal Direction Integration module computes pairwise object distances and encodes relative cardinal directions (e.g., 'east') as a relative position matrix added to attention logits before softmax. Training uses contrastive loss for retrieval and MSE for pre-alignment and coordinate regression.

## Results

On the KITTI360Pose benchmark, CMMLoc achieves a Top-1 localization recall of 0.44 (validation) and 0.39 (test) under the strictest error threshold (ε<5m), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art Text2Loc (0.37/0.33) by 19% and 18% respectively. At the relaxed ε<15m threshold with k=10 candidates, CMMLoc reaches 0.95 (val) and 0.89 (test). For the coarse submap retrieval stage, CMMLoc attains a Top-1 recall of 0.35 (val) and 0.32 (test), exceeding Text2Loc by 9% and 14% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMMLoc outperforms all three baselines (Text2Pos, RET, Text2Loc) across every reported metric on both validation and test sets. Against the strongest baseline Text2Loc, CMMLoc gains 19% relative improvement in Top-1 localization recall at ε<5m on the validation set (0.44 vs. 0.37) and 18% on the test set (0.39 vs. 0.33). The advantage is even more pronounced at the coarse retrieval stage, where CMMLoc's Top-1 submap recall exceeds Text2Loc by 9% (val) and 14% (test). RET and Text2Pos trail substantially behind, with Top-1 localization recall at ε<5m of only 0.19/0.16 and 0.14/0.13 respectively on val/test, indicating that the partially relevant modeling and CMM-based attention provide a clear and consistent benefit over prior cross-modal matching approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coarse-to-fine text-to-point-cloud localization pipeline established by Text2Pos [18] and refined by Text2Loc [48], but reframes the coarse submap retrieval stage as a partially relevant retrieval problem—a characteristic the authors argue prior work overlooked. The core architectural contribution, the Cauchy-Mixture-Model-based Transformer (CMMT), is a direct adaptation of GMMFormer [45] (designed for partially relevant video retrieval), replacing Gaussian mixture priors with Cauchy mixture priors to exploit the heavy-tailed distribution's natural attenuation of irrelevant objects. The fine localization stage builds on Text2Loc's matching-free regression backbone, augmented with a pre-alignment pretraining step and a novel Cardinal Direction Integration module that encodes pairwise spatial relations as textual direction tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The partial relevance formulation addresses a genuine practical gap: in real-world scenarios like vehicle pickup, users describe only nearby or salient objects rather than the full scene, and the Cauchy distribution's heavy tails provide a principled way to down-weight (rather than discard) irrelevant objects. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (KITTI360Pose) and a single city scale, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger urban environments, dynamic scenes, or other cross-modal retrieval tasks. The reliance on semantic segmentation quality (performance degrades with label noise, though robustly) also constrains deployment in settings where reliable 3D semantic labels are unavailable.
