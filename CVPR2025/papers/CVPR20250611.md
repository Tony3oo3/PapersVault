## Problem

Neural Architecture Search (NAS) requires training thousands of candidate architectures from scratch to evaluate their accuracy, making the feasible search space a tiny fraction of all possible networks. Existing training-free (zero-shot) proxies lack strong theoretical grounding, and the standard evaluation metrics (Kendall's τ, Spearman's ρ) fail to properly reward proxies that accurately identify the best networks while penalizing errors on irrelevant low-accuracy ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250611/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrained network architecture (weights at random initialization) and 64 randomly generated white-noise images as input, requiring no real dataset or true labels. It produces a scalar VKDNW score in [0,1] measuring the entropy of the FIM eigenvalue spectrum, which is size-independent; the practical ranking VKDNW_single adds the integer count of trainable layers ℵ(f) to group by size before ordering by VKDNW within each group. The aggregated variant VKDNW_agg further multiplies (in log-space) the VKDNW_single rank with Jacov, Expressivity, Trainability, and FLOPs ranks.

## Method

The core contribution is computing the empirical Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) of the network's softmax output at initialization and extracting the entropy of its eigenvalue spectrum (9 deciles) as a proxy for training difficulty. Three technical enablers make this tractable for deep networks: (1) a numerically stable FIM factorization F̂ = (1/n)Σ AₙᵀAₙ using analytical Cholesky decompositions of the multinomial covariance to avoid underflow/overflow from imbalanced softmax outputs; (2) sampling one parameter per trainable layer (excluding batch norm) to reduce the p×p eigenvalue problem to a small, stable computation; and (3) exploiting the symmetric positive-semidefinite structure to compute singular values instead of eigenvalues for better conditioning. The resulting VKDNW score is high when all weight directions have similar estimation uncertainty (per Cramér-Rao bound) and no perturbation direction disproportionately affects predictions, making it orthogonal to network size and complementary to capacity-based proxies.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-201, VKDNW_agg achieves the best scores across all three metrics (Kendall's τ, Spearman's ρ, nDCG) on all three datasets: e.g., on ImageNet16-120 it reaches KT=0.743, SPR=0.906, nDCG=0.664, outperforming the previous best zero-shot method AZ-NAS (KT=0.673, SPR=0.859, nDCG=0.534). The single-rank VKDNW_single also leads all single-proxy methods on ImageNet16-120 (nDCG=0.608 vs. 0.523 for ZiCo). On ImageNet-1K in the MobileNetV2 search space, VKDNW_agg finds a network achieving 78.8% Top-1 accuracy at ~480M FLOPs, surpassing training-based methods DONNA (78.0%) and OFA (77.7%) while requiring only 0.4 GPU days of search cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VKDNW_agg outperforms all zero-shot baselines (single and aggregated) on every metric across all three NAS-Bench-201 datasets; the largest margin is on ImageNet16-120 nDCG where it scores 0.664 vs. 0.534 for AZ-NAS and 0.523 for ZiCo. Among single-rank proxies, VKDNW_single leads on ImageNet16-120 in all three metrics (nDCG 0.608 vs. 0.523 for ZiCo, 0.511 for SynFlow) and on SPR for all three datasets. On the full ImageNet-1K MobileNetV2 search, VKDNW_agg achieves 78.8% Top-1 accuracy, beating the previous zero-shot best AZ-NAS (78.6%) and ZiCo (78.1%), and also surpassing training-based methods DONNA (78.0%) and OFA (77.7%) at a fraction of the search cost (0.4 vs. 405 and 50 GPU days). The model-driven variant (VKDNW+ZCS+GRAF)_m further improves over the standalone GRAF baseline [14] on all metrics (e.g., CIFAR-10: 0.859 vs. 0.820 KT), confirming that VKDNW features are complementary to graph-based features. A key differentiator is orthogonality to network size: VKDNW has near-zero correlation with the number of trainable layers (KT ≈ −0.107 on ImageNet16-120), whereas AZ-NAS components (expressivity, trainability, progressivity) show strong positive correlations (0.39–0.54), meaning VKDNW captures architecture-specific information that size-based proxies miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies Fisher Information theory (Ly et al. 2017) to the zero-shot NAS setting for the first time, using the empirical FIM decomposition from Kunstner et al. (2019) to make the computation tractable for large networks. It departs from the dominant gradient- and Jacobian-based proxy family (GradNorm, GraSP, SNIP, SynFlow, Jacov, NASWOT, ZiCo, AZ-NAS) by instead measuring the entropy of the FIM eigenvalue spectrum, which captures estimation uncertainty in a way orthogonal to network size. The paper also introduces nDCG as a complementary evaluation metric, arguing that the field's reliance on Kendall's τ and Spearman's ρ masks important differences in how well proxies identify top-performing architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The nDCG metric reveals that previously celebrated methods like AZ-NAS are actually worse than simpler baselines (e.g., ZiCo) on the property that matters most for NAS—discriminating top networks—which reframes the competitive landscape of zero-shot NAS. The orthogonality of VKDNW to network size is a structurally useful property for scaling to larger search spaces where size and architecture quality are confounded. Open questions include whether the initialization-based FIM evaluation (which departs from the Cramér-Rao assumption of evaluating at the true parameter) remains informative for much deeper or differently parameterized architectures, and whether the 9-decile eigenvalue summary is sufficient for very high-dimensional FIMs.
