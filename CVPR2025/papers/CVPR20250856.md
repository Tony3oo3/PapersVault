## Problem

Existing adversarial patch recovery methods for CNNs suffer from three fundamental limitations: they rely on fixed saliency thresholds to identify adversarial regions, they execute the full recovery pipeline on every input without first checking whether an attack is present, and they assume a single patch of known shape and size. No prior method explicitly detects patch attacks before recovery, handles arbitrary numbers of noncontiguous patches of varying shapes, and avoids dependence on a single fixed threshold.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250856/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Saliuitl takes a standard RGB image (potentially containing one or more adversarial patches of arbitrary shape, size, and number) and produces two outputs: a binary attack detection decision (via a continuous score in [0,1] compared to a threshold α*) and a recovered prediction—either a class label (image classification) or object detection results (object detection). The detection stage operates on a 1D ensemble attribute vector derived from the victim model's shallow feature map, while recovery modifies the input image via inpainting before re-querying the victim model. Stopping conditions are task-specific: for detection, inpainting halts when ≥50% of pixels are covered; for classification, it also halts when the predicted label changes.

## Method

Saliuitl is a two-stage defense. In the detection stage, a shallow feature map is extracted from the victim CNN and binarized across an ensemble of 20 ascending saliency thresholds; from each binary map, four attributes are extracted (active neuron count, DBSCAN cluster count, and mean/std of intra-cluster distances), concatenated into an ensemble attribute vector, and fed to a lightweight 1D CNN attack detector that outputs a detection score. In the recovery stage, the same feature map is binarized across a descending threshold set, DBSCAN identifies salient clusters, and the corresponding image regions are inpainted using biharmonic inpainting; the model is re-queried after each iteration and the recovered output is updated if the prediction changes. The key insight is that adversarial patches produce a characteristic evolution pattern in how salient-region attributes change across thresholds, which the 1D CNN learns to distinguish from clean inputs—eliminating the need for any single fixed saliency threshold and making the method agnostic to patch count, shape, and size.

## Results

On image classification (CIFAR-10), Saliuitl achieves recovery rates of 97.38–97.89% with lost prediction rates of 0–0.08% across single, double, quadruple, and triangular patch scenarios, outperforming all baselines by up to 97.81 percentage points in recovery rate at the same lost prediction rate. On object detection (INRIA/VOC), Saliuitl achieves 59.09% recovery rate at 1.52% lost predictions for single-patch attacks on INRIA, surpassing all non-certifiable defenses by up to 42.63 percentage points at the same lost prediction rate. In terms of adversarial nmAP, Saliuitl dominates all baselines across all object detection scenarios, e.g., 0.6897 adversarial / 0.9998 clean nmAP on INRIA single-patch vs. 0.6040/1.0 for Object Seeker. Saliuitl also maintains robustness against adaptive white-box attacks, with only a modest drop in recovery rate when the attacker explicitly optimizes to evade the detection module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Saliuitl dominates all empirical recovery baselines (NutNet, PAD, Jedi) across nearly all scenarios: on CIFAR-10 single-patch it achieves 97.38% RR at 0.08% LPR versus NutNet's 96.41%/0.25% and PAD's 96.15%/1.78%, and on INRIA single-patch it reaches 59.09% RR at 1.52% LPR versus NutNet's 31.82%/15.15% and Jedi's 30.30%/45.45%. Against the certifiable defense Object Seeker, Saliuitl trades a slightly lower raw recovery rate on single/double INRIA patches (59.09% vs. 65.15%) for a dramatically better nmAP (0.6897 vs. 0.6040 adversarial nmAP) and superior multi-object performance (47.49% vs. 41.90% RR on INRIA-MO); Object Seeker also produces duplicate/non-existing object predictions that degrade practical utility. Against Patch Cleanser (certifiable, image classification), Saliuitl is overwhelmingly superior: 97.38% RR at 0.08% LPR on CIFAR-10 single-patch versus Patch Cleanser's 46.30%/42.60%. Themis achieves zero lost predictions but at the cost of very low recovery rates (e.g., 22.73% on INRIA-1, 15.94% on VOC-1), and its performance is sensitive to the unrealistic assumption of knowing the patch size bound. FNS (feature norm suppressing) is competitive on ImageNet single-patch (84.16% RR) but falls well behind Saliuitl on multi-patch and triangular scenarios. Against adaptive white-box attacks that explicitly optimize to evade Saliuitl's detector, Saliuitl's recovery rate drops but so does attack effectiveness, while the same adaptive attack degrades most baselines (PAD, NutNet) even though they have no knowledge of Saliuitl's internals.

## Relation to Prior Work

Saliuitl departs from the dominant paradigm in adversarial patch recovery, where methods like Themis, Jedi, PAD, and NutNet rely on a single fixed saliency threshold to identify adversarial regions, and certifiable methods like Object Seeker and Patch Cleanser perform exhaustive spatial searches under single-patch assumptions. Instead, Saliuitl (building on the authors' prior SpaNN detection work) observes how attributes of binarized feature maps evolve across an ensemble of thresholds, using a 1D CNN on the resulting attribute vector for explicit attack detection before any recovery is attempted. The recovery stage then leverages the same ensemble of binary feature maps with DBSCAN-based spatial clustering and biharmonic inpainting, removing the need for assumptions about patch shape, size, or count that constrain all prior empirical and certifiable defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit detection stage is practically significant for deployment in autonomous systems where the vast majority of inputs are clean, avoiding unnecessary recovery computation and preventing performance degradation on clean images. The method's robustness to adaptive white-box attacks (where the attacker directly optimizes against the detection score) and its agnosticism to patch count and shape address realistic attack scenarios that prior defenses either ignore or handle poorly. Key limitations include a notable performance drop on triangular patches (attributed to square CNN kernels), evaluation restricted to CNN architectures (no ViT experiments), and per-scenario tuning of the detection threshold α*. Extending the approach to transformer-based vision models and non-rectangular patch recovery are identified as important open directions.
