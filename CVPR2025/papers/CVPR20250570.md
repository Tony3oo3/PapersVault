## Problem

Most computational pathology methods operate on single-stain H&E images, and the few that address multistain IHC focus on cell quantification or virtual staining rather than direct classification of unregistered, unannotated multistain WSI stacks. Even when multistain classification is attempted, existing approaches lack biological interpretability—they do not reveal which stains drive the decision, how cell populations interact across stains, or whether predictions align with known pathological mechanisms, limiting clinical trust and utility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a per-patient stack of multistain WSIs (one H&E slide plus ~3 IHC slides at 10× or 20× magnification), from which non-overlapping patches with >30–40% tissue coverage are extracted and encoded into feature vectors via the UNI foundation model. Beyond the patient-level class label, the model outputs four interpretability artifacts: per-stain attention weights (SAAP scores), per-stain entropy measures capturing attention concentration, a symmetric stain-stain interaction matrix derived from GAT edge weights, and a spatially mapped GNN attention heatmap.

## Method

BioX-CPath constructs a unified graph per patient by combining a k-NN feature-space graph with a region-adjacency graph (across both the x-y plane and the z-axis of the WSI stack), then augments node features with random-walk positional encodings to capture long-range topological structure. The backbone alternates Graph Attention Network layers with a novel Stain-Aware Attention Pooling (SAAP) module: SAAP computes node importance via SAGPool-style self-attention, retains the top-k nodes, scales their features by attention, then aggregates stain-specific weights (α_s) and pools weighted features per stain to produce a stain-aware readout via concatenated mean and max pooling. Multi-head self-attention over the stacked stain-aware encodings feeds a classification head. The key differentiator is that SAAP explicitly decomposes the graph readout by stain identity, yielding biologically grounded attention scores, entropy measures, and cross-stain interaction metrics that can be validated against known disease mechanisms.

## Results

On the Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) dataset, BioX-CPath achieves 0.90 (±0.019) accuracy, 0.96 (±0.007) AUC, and 0.98 (±0.004) average precision, representing a 4 percentage-point accuracy improvement over the next-best method (MUSTANG, 0.86). On the Sjögren's Disease dataset, BioX-CPath achieves 0.84 (±0.018) accuracy, 0.88 (±0.023) AUC, and 0.86 (±0.032) AP, outperforming all seven baselines on every metric. Ablation studies confirm that the SAAP module contributes the largest single gain (e.g., +4 pp accuracy on Sjögren's over the RW-only model), while random-walk positional encoding provides a secondary boost over the baseline GNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the RA dataset, BioX-CPath outperforms MUSTANG [17] by 4 pp in accuracy (0.90 vs. 0.86) and by 1 pp in AP (0.98 vs. 0.97), while matching it on AUC (both 0.96). It surpasses the next-best single-stain GNN, Patch-GCN [9], by 7 pp in accuracy (0.90 vs. 0.83) and 4 pp in AUC. On the Sjögren's dataset, BioX-CPath leads all seven baselines on every metric: it beats CLAM-SB [38] and MUSTANG [17] by 4 pp in accuracy (0.84 vs. 0.80), by 3 pp in AUC (0.88 vs. 0.85), and by 1–2 pp in AP. The weakest baselines are GTP [62] (0.62 acc on Sjögren's) and TransMIL [50] (0.73 AUC on Sjögren's), both of which were designed for single-stain H&E cancer tasks and struggle with the multistain autoimmune setting. Overall, BioX-CPath is the only method to simultaneously achieve top accuracy, AUC, and AP on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work MUSTANG [17] and the single-stain graph fusion of Dwivedi et al. [13] by introducing a Stain-Aware Attention Pooling (SAAP) module that explicitly computes per-stain importance weights during hierarchical graph pooling. The backbone follows the alternating GAT + pooling architecture from Cangea et al. [7] and DiffPool [59], while the graph construction (k-NN in feature space + region adjacency) is standard in the histopathology GNN literature (PatchGCN, GTP, HEAT). The key departure from prior multistain and single-stain GNN methods is the explicit biological interpretability layer: stain attention scores, entropy measures, and stain-stain interaction scores are designed to be validated against known pathological mechanisms rather than serving purely as classification features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in computational pathology: most SOTA methods target single-stain H&E cancer datasets, while multistain IHC for autoimmune diseases remains underexplored and lacks explainability. The biological interpretability metrics (stain entropy correlating with tissue organization, interaction scores reflecting known cell-cell relationships) provide a template for clinical validation that goes beyond attention heatmaps. However, the evaluation is limited to two small autoimmune datasets (153 and 93 patients), the MHSA layer slightly degrades performance, and generalization to other disease types or larger stain panels remains untested. Open questions include whether the SAAP module's stain-awareness transfers to immunofluorescence or multiplexed imaging, and whether the interpretability metrics can be formalized as clinical decision-support criteria.
