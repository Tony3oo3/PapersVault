## Problem

CLIP's 77-token input limit and short-text training data restrict its ability to handle long-text retrieval, and fine-tuning CLIP on long-text data (as in Long-CLIP) introduces knowledge forgetting where zero-shot classification accuracy degrades by ~2%. This paper addresses the simultaneous challenge of enhancing long-text representation while preserving the original model's foundational knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251493/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images and text prompts extended to 248 tokens (via linear interpolation of positional embeddings beyond the first 20), producing normalized image and text embeddings for cross-modal retrieval. The text encoder is designed to be plug-and-play compatible with Stable Diffusion XL for text-to-image generation, and the joint embeddings support zero-shot classification via prompt-based matching. During distillation, the student text encoder processes both short-text (≤77 tokens) and long-text (up to 248 tokens) inputs in a multi-task fashion.

## Method

The method is a two-stage dual-teacher distillation framework. In stage one, CLIP is fine-tuned on ShareGPT4V with contrastive learning using 248-token positional embeddings (first 20 retained, rest linearly interpolated), producing a long-text teacher that excels at long-text retrieval but suffers knowledge forgetting. In stage two, a student network is distilled from both this long-text teacher and the original CLIP: the text encoder uses multi-task cosine-similarity distillation (separate losses for short-text and long-text alignment) plus an L2 constraint on the first 20 word-token embeddings to minimize latent space drift; the image encoder uses a dimension-wise importance-weighted MSE loss where each dimension is re-weighted by its contribution to cross-modal cosine similarity (via an exponential temperature-scaled function), combined with cosine-similarity losses from both teachers, allowing the student to inherit fine-grained detail capture while retaining original knowledge.

## Results

On long-caption retrieval (ViT-L/14), LongD-CLIP achieves R@1 of 98.3% (I2T) and 98.6% (T2I) on ShareGPT4V, and 91.9% (I2T) and 90.8% (T2I) on Urban-1k, surpassing Long-CLIP by 2.5% and 9.2% respectively. On short-caption retrieval (ViT-L/14), it reaches R@1 of 64.4% (I2T) on COCO2017 and 60.2% (I2T) on Flickr30k. For zero-shot image classification (ViT-L/14), the average accuracy across five benchmarks is 70.56%, exceeding both CLIP (69.92%) and Long-CLIP (69.78%), confirming effective knowledge retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original CLIP, LongD-CLIP wins decisively on all three task categories: long-text retrieval improves by 16.5–23.2% in R@1 (e.g., 91.9 vs. 68.7 on Urban-1k I2T with L/14), short-text retrieval improves by 5.9–11.7% (e.g., 60.2 vs. 48.5 on Flickr30k I2T), and zero-shot classification average rises from 69.92% to 70.56%. Against Long-CLIP, the gains are more nuanced: long-text retrieval shows the largest margin (2.5% on ShareGPT4V I2T, 9.2% on Urban-1k I2T with L/14), short-text retrieval improves by 1.6–6.8% (e.g., 64.4 vs. 62.8 on COCO I2T), and zero-shot classification improves by 0.78% on average (70.56 vs. 69.78), confirming that the dual-teacher strategy recovers the knowledge forgetting that Long-CLIP suffers while still delivering superior long-text performance. The ablation (Table 4) further shows that the long-text teacher alone (L-teacher) achieves the best long-text retrieval (99.2 on ShareGPT4V I2T) but drops zero-shot classification to 67.86%, whereas the dual-teacher approach sacrifices only 0.9% on long-text retrieval to gain 2.7% on classification, validating the trade-off design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP → Long-CLIP lineage by addressing the knowledge-forgetting tradeoff that emerges when fine-tuning CLIP on long-text data. While Long-CLIP (Zhang et al., 2024) extends positional embeddings via interpolation and fine-tunes with a component-matching objective, and DreamLIP (Zheng et al., 2025) trains on MLLM-generated captions but produces a latent space incompatible with existing CLIP modules, this work introduces a dual-teacher distillation framework: a long-text-fine-tuned CLIP teacher provides long-text representation knowledge, while the original CLIP teacher anchors the student's foundational zero-shot capabilities. The dimension-wise importance-weighted distillation loss for the image encoder, which re-weights MSE by cross-modal similarity contribution, is a methodological departure from the uniform losses used in prior VLM distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in resolving the long-text vs. zero-shot classification tradeoff without sacrificing either, and in maintaining plug-and-play compatibility with existing generative pipelines like SDXL—something DreamLIP explicitly cannot do. The 248-token limit, while an improvement over 77, still constrains truly long-form understanding, and the approach has only been validated on ShareGPT4V-scale captions (~1000 words) rather than document-length inputs. Open questions include whether the dual-teacher strategy generalizes to other VLM architectures (e.g., SigLIP, EVA-CLIP), whether the importance-weighted loss transfers to other distillation settings, and whether the 20-token effective-length observation holds as models scale.
