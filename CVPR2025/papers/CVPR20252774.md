## Problem

4D Panoptic Scene Graph (4D-PSG) generation suffers from severe data scarcity (the benchmark contains only ~1.7% of the data available for 2D scene graphs), an out-of-vocabulary limitation where models are restricted to ~50 predefined object and ~40 relation classes, and error propagation inherent in pipeline-based generation architectures. These three issues collectively prevent the development of a practical, open-vocabulary 4D-PSG system capable of handling diverse real-world scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252774/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 4D scene as a sequence of T aligned RGB-D frames (each H×W×4) and produces a dynamic panoptic scene graph consisting of object labels, 3D mask tubes (binary volumetric segments tracking each object's spatial extent over time via SAM2), and relation quintuples of the form (object_i, relation_k, object_j, start_time, end_time). During training, the system additionally consumes large-scale 2D SG annotations (Visual Genome, PVSG) that are transcended into pseudo-4D features, and the LLM outputs a structured text sequence where a special [Obj] token triggers the mask decoder to produce segmentation for each identified object.

## Method

The framework couples a 4D-LLM (LLaMA2 with LoRA) and a SAM2-based 3D mask decoder, with ImageBind encoding RGB and depth modalities separately before spatial-temporal merging and MLP projection into language space. A chained SG inference mechanism decomposes generation into four iterative stages—object description and categorization, semantic relation identification, precise relation description, and temporal span determination—exploiting the LLM's open-vocabulary reasoning to go beyond fixed label sets. To combat data scarcity, a 2D-to-4D Visual Scene Transfer (D²→⁴-VST) framework learns dimension-invariant scene features through a four-step curriculum: (1) 4D initiation learning on PSG4D, (2) training three estimators (depth, RGB-temporal, depth-temporal) to transcend 2D features into 4D space using DIML/CVL and Action Genome, (3) pseudo-4D transfer initiation with consistency losses aligning transcended features, and (4) large-scale transfer on 150K 2D SG annotations where only RGB input is transcended into pseudo-4D scenes for end-to-end 4D-PSG training.

## Results

On PSG4D-GTA, the full 4D-LLM model with D²→⁴-VST achieves R/mR@20 of 18.48/9.43, R/mR@50 of 20.24/10.78, and R/mR@100 of 18.43/12.41. On PSG4D-HOI, it reaches R/mR@20 of 15.18/8.02, R/mR@50 of 19.68/10.23, and R/mR@100 of 21.79/11.54. The LLM-based baseline (trained only on PSG4D data, without 2D transfer) already yields average mR@20 improvements of 4.74 (PSG4D-GTA) and 5.50 (PSG4D-HOI) over the best specialist model (PSG4DFormer one-stage), and the full model with 2D-to-4D scene transfer further extends these gaps substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two families of baselines: 3DSGG [43] (a 3D scene graph method adapted to 4D) and PSG4DFormer [54] (the original 4D-PSG pipeline, including its ablated variants w/o temporal, w/o depth, and a one-stage end-to-end version). The proposed 4D-LLM with D²→⁴-VST decisively outperforms all baselines on every metric across both datasets. On PSG4D-GTA, it achieves 18.48/9.43 R/mR@20 versus 7.93/3.69 for the strongest specialist (PSG4DFormer one-stage) and 2.29/0.92 for 3DSGG — a roughly 2.3× improvement in mR@20 over the best specialist. On PSG4D-HOI, the gap is similarly large: 15.18/8.02 vs. 7.05/3.96 (PSG4DFormer one-stage) and 4.23/2.19 (3DSGG). Even the 4D-LLM base model (without 2D transfer) already surpasses all specialist baselines, confirming that the LLM backbone alone provides substantial gains, while the 2D-to-4D scene transfer learning further amplifies performance by an additional ~5–8 mR@20 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 4D-PSG benchmark and PSG4DFormer pipeline introduced by Yang et al. (2023), replacing the cascade architecture (4D panoptic segmentation → relation determination) with an end-to-end 4D-LLM framework that jointly generates scene graph triplets and 3D mask tubes. It draws on the autoregressive temporal feature generation approach from VALHALLA (Li et al., 2022) for its RGB and Depth Temporal Estimators, and leverages ImageBind for multi-modal scene encoding. The key conceptual departure is the 2D-to-4D visual scene transfer learning framework, which repurposes the vast 2D SG annotation corpus (Visual Genome, PVSG) as compensatory supervision for the data-starved 4D setting—a strategy not explored in prior 4D-PSG or 3D-SG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4D-PSG task is extremely data-scarce (only ~1.7% of 2D-SG data volume), and this paper demonstrates that cross-dimensional transfer from 2D to 4D can substantially close the gap, offering a template for addressing data scarcity in other emerging 3D/4D vision tasks. The open-vocabulary capability via chained LLM inference is practically important for real-world deployment where object and relation categories are unbounded. However, evaluation is limited to two small datasets (one synthetic, one egocentric), baselines are restricted to the original PSG4DFormer family, and the zero-shot transfer results, while promising, rely on a single configuration. Open questions include whether the 2D-to-4D transcending generalizes to more diverse 4D scenes (outdoor, multi-camera), whether the approach scales to longer temporal sequences, and whether the LLM backbone can be replaced by smaller models without losing the open-vocabulary advantage.
