## Problem

Existing synthetic image detection and source attribution methods rely on discriminative features learned from known generators, causing them to fail when confronted with novel or unseen generative models. Even zero-shot approaches based on non-forensic features (e.g., CLIP embeddings, reconstruction errors) exhibit inconsistent performance that varies across dataset pairs. The paper seeks a representation that captures the intrinsic forensic microstructures of any image, enabling reliable detection, attribution, and clustering without prior exposure to the source generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251015/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a corpus of grayscale real images to learn K=8 linear predictive filters (11×11 neighborhood, center weight fixed to 0, sum of weights = 1). At inference, a single RGB image is converted to grayscale, and the learned filters produce K residuals; these residuals are then modeled across L=3 downsampled scales to yield the forensic self-description—a set of 8 autoregressive filter parameter vectors (each 11×11, excluding center) per scale. This compact parameter set serves as the image's feature vector for downstream GMM-based detection/attribution or K-means clustering, with no per-image network forward pass required.

## Method

The method operates in two self-supervised stages. First, K diverse linear predictive filters are learned on real images by minimizing total residual energy plus a spectral diversity regularization term (negative log of singular values of the stacked filter-weight matrix), which enforces linear independence among filters so each captures a distinct aspect of forensic microstructures. Second, for any query image, the K residuals are jointly modeled across multiple scales using K autoregressive linear filters (11×11 neighborhood) optimized to minimize total model error power; the resulting filter parameters Φ constitute the forensic self-description. Downstream tasks use GMMs fitted to self-description distributions: a single GMM over real images enables zero-shot detection via likelihood thresholding, per-source GMMs enable open-set attribution with a rejection threshold for unknown sources, and K-means over self-descriptions enables unsupervised source clustering. The key insight is that jointly modeling residuals from diverse filters across scales captures the full forensic microstructure signature, which is source-specific and content-independent, unlike discriminative embeddings that only separate known classes.

## Results

On zero-shot detection, the method achieves an average AUC of 0.960 across 96 real-vs-synthetic dataset pairs, outperforming the best baseline NPR (0.926) by 3.4 points. In open-set source attribution, it attains the highest AU-CRR (0.933) and AU-OSCR (0.913) while achieving near-best known-source accuracy (0.964). For unsupervised clustering with N=8 sources, it reaches 0.78 accuracy, 0.77 purity, and 0.69 NMI at the true cluster count, and maintains the best performance (0.83 accuracy, 0.85 purity) even when over-clustering at 4N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On zero-shot detection, the proposed method outperforms all 8 baselines by a wide margin: it beats the strongest competitor NPR [67] by 3.4 AUC points on average (0.960 vs. 0.926) and by an even larger gap in worst-case scenarios (0.892+ vs. NPR's 0.239 on Firefly/IN-1k). Supervised methods (CNNDet, PatchFor) and reconstruction-based zero-shot methods (Aeroblade, ZED, DE-FAKE) all fall below 0.83 average AUC, confirming their poor generalization to unseen generators. On open-set attribution, the method achieves the best AU-CRR (0.933) and AU-OSCR (0.913), surpassing Fang et al. [28] (0.856/0.852) and POSE [71] (0.629/0.608), though it trails Fang et al. (0.988) and RepMix (0.982) slightly on known-source accuracy. For clustering, the method ties UFD [54] at N clusters (0.78 accuracy) but clearly leads at 2N and 4N (0.80/0.83 vs. UFD's 0.67/0.71), while all other baselines (CLIP, ResNet-50, FSM, EXIFNet, POSE, CNNDet, NPR, DE-FAKE) remain well below 0.70 accuracy across all cluster counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rich-model residual analysis tradition from steganalysis and camera forensics (Fridrich & Kódovský [30,38]) to AI-generated image detection, but departs fundamentally from prior forensic approaches by learning a diverse set of predictive filters in a self-supervised manner on real images only, rather than relying on handcrafted filters or discriminative CNN embeddings. Unlike supervised detectors (CNNDet, PatchFor) or zero-shot methods that exploit non-forensic features (CLIP embeddings, autoencoder reconstruction errors), the paper explicitly models forensic microstructures through a per-image parametric autoregressive model across multiple scales, with a novel spectral diversity regularization to ensure the learned filters capture complementary aspects of the microstructure. The resulting 'forensic self-description'—a compact set of filter parameters per image—serves as a universal representation for detection, attribution, and clustering, replacing the large fixed-dimensional embeddings used by competing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As new generative models (Stable Diffusion, DALLE 3, Midjourney, Firefly) emerge continuously, the inability of supervised detectors to generalize to unseen generators is a critical practical gap; this paper's self-supervised, generator-agnostic approach directly addresses that by requiring no synthetic training data. The consistently strong worst-case performance (AUC ≥ 0.89) across 24 generators and 4 real datasets is practically significant for deployment settings where adversarial or novel generators must be caught. Open questions remain around robustness to heavy post-processing (social media compression, cropping), the dependence on a diverse and high-quality real-image training corpus, and whether the approach extends to video or multimodal generation. The paper also notes that adaptive filter learning and domain-specific variants could further improve real-world robustness.
