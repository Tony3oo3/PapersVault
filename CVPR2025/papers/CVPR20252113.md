## Problem

Existing VLM defenses against jailbreak attacks—input preprocessing, adversarial training, and response evaluation pipelines—impose prohibitive computational costs for real-world deployment, requiring either full model fine-tuning or multiple inference passes per query. Additionally, directly transferring activation steering techniques from LLMs to VLMs fails empirically because textual and visual jailbreaks exploit different feature-space directions, leaving a gap for an efficient, single-pass defense that operates at the visual representation level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252113/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The defense operates on a standard VLM that accepts an RGB image (encoded as m visual tokens) paired with a text instruction (n textual tokens) and generates an autoregressive text response. ASTRA modifies only the internal activations of newly generated tokens at a single chosen layer (layer 20 for 13B models, layer 14 for 7B models), leaving input token activations unaltered. The steering vectors are precomputed offline from 16 PGD-generated adversarial images sampled from ImageNet, requiring only ~96 forward passes per image for the Lasso attribution step, and the inference-time steering adds negligible per-token overhead (~0.5 ms).

## Method

ASTRA constructs harmful-direction steering vectors via image attribution: it randomly ablates subsets of visual tokens from PGD adversarial images, fits a Lasso linear surrogate to score each token's contribution to the log-probability of a jailbreak response ('Sure, ...'), and defines the steering vector as the mean activation difference between the full image and the image with top-k attributed tokens masked, paired with an empty user query for generalization. At inference, ASTRA applies adaptive activation steering using a conditional projection: the steering magnitude is scaled by max((h^l − h_0^l)ᵀ v^l / (‖h^l − h_0^l‖ ‖v^l‖) · ‖h^l‖, 0), where h_0^l is a calibration activation (mean activation over a large set of benign inputs). This max-gated projection ensures zero modification when the calibrated activation has no component along the harmful direction (benign inputs), while strongly projecting away from it for adversarial inputs, avoiding the utility degradation caused by fixed-coefficient steering.

## Results

On MiniGPT-4, ASTRA achieves an average Toxicity Score of 7.28% and ASR of 11.71% across perturbation-based attacks (ε ∈ {16/255, 32/255, 64/255, unconstrained}), compared to 19.40% and 29.55% for the strongest baseline JailGuard. On Qwen2-VL, ASTRA reduces Toxicity Score to as low as 0.07% (unconstrained) and ASR to 5.00% (ε=32/255), versus 27.76% and 15.45% for JailGuard. ASTRA also matches the inference speed of the undefended model (173.77 ms/token vs. 173.19 ms/token on MiniGPT-4), while JailGuard requires 1557.98 ms/token (~9× slower) due to multiple inference passes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASTRA outperforms all five baselines on both Toxicity Score and ASR across every ε setting on MiniGPT-4 and Qwen2-VL. Against the strongest VLM defense, JailGuard [58], ASTRA reduces average Toxicity Score by 12.12 percentage points (7.28% vs. 19.40%) and ASR by 17.84 percentage points (11.71% vs. 29.55%) on MiniGPT-4, while running ~9× faster (173.77 vs. 1557.98 ms/token). The LLM steering baselines—Refusal Pairs [43] and Jailbreak Templates [4]—underperform ASTRA substantially, confirming that textual steering vectors do not transfer effectively to visual adversarial inputs; Jailbreak Templates in particular worsens ASR on Qwen2-VL (68–75% vs. 67–76% undefended). ECSO [18] and Self-reminder [54] offer only marginal or inconsistent improvements over no defense, with ECSO's Toxicity Score remaining above 32% in most settings. On Qwen2-VL, ASTRA achieves near-zero Toxicity (0.07%) under unconstrained perturbation, a setting where all baselines remain above 27%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the activation steering paradigm from LLM interpretability (contrastive activation addition by Rimsky et al., activation addition by Turner et al.) to the VLM safety setting, but explicitly departs from prior work by showing that text-derived steering vectors are insufficient for visual jailbreaks due to a modality mismatch. The key methodological departure is the introduction of image attribution via a Lasso-based linear surrogate to identify which visual tokens most strongly drive harmful responses, replacing the text-prompt-based vector construction used in LLM steering. The adaptive projection with calibration activation is a novel combination that addresses the utility degradation problem inherent in fixed-coefficient steering, positioning the work at the intersection of activation engineering, ML explainability, and adversarial robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency: ASTRA requires only a single inference pass at deployment time with negligible per-token overhead, making it deployable in real-world VLM systems where multi-pass defenses like JailGuard (9x slower) are prohibitive. The OOD transferability finding—that a single steering direction captures harmfulness regardless of whether it is triggered by perturbation, typography, or text-only attacks—suggests a shared 'harmful direction' in VLM feature space, which is a useful conceptual insight for future safety work. However, limitations remain: adaptive attacks still achieve meaningful success rates (especially on Qwen2-VL jailbreak, where ASR drops only from ~70% to ~60%), and the steering vectors must be reconstructed per model, limiting cross-model portability. Open questions include whether this approach extends to multi-turn or compositional attacks and whether the 'common harmful direction' hypothesis holds for more diverse model families.
