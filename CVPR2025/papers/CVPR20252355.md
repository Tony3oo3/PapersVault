## Problem

Few-shot image manipulation requires reasoning to disentangle transformation features from irrelevant content in exemplar images, a capability where diffusion models are weak. While autoregressive models (MLLMs) excel at in-context learning, they have not been applied to this task, and existing autoregressive approaches mix the learning and applying stages into a single self-attention pass, increasing problem complexity and creating a bottleneck for transferring manipulation rules to new images.

## Input / Output

The model receives a query image, a textual instruction, and one or more exemplar source-target image pairs. All images are encoded into 64 continuous visual tokens via Qwen ViT, and the full prompt is assembled with 30 learnable manipulation tokens placed between exemplars and the query image, plus 64 learnable generation tokens appended at the end. The output is a single manipulated image decoded from the generation tokens by a frozen SDXL decoder.

## Method

InstaManip is built on LLaMA2-13B and introduces a group self-attention (GSA) mechanism that splits the token sequence into two groups—(1) textual instruction + exemplar images + manipulation tokens, and (2) manipulation tokens + query image + generation tokens—and performs causal self-attention independently within each group, so manipulation tokens abstract transformation features without seeing the query image. A relation regularization loss aligns the pairwise similarity matrix of L2-normalized manipulation token embeddings with the CLIP text-encoder similarity matrix of instructions, encouraging the latent space to encode only manipulation-relevant features. Training uses LoRA (α=0.1) on the GSA and MLP layers while freezing the image encoder and decoder, with a combined MSE reconstruction loss and relation regularization loss (weighted by α=0.1).

## Results

On the InstructPix2Pix test set (325 unseen instructions, 1296 samples), InstaManip achieves 19.81 CLIP-Dir, 32.39 CLIP-Vis, 27.72 CLIP-T, and 80.11 CLIP-I in the in-distribution setting, and 18.27, 28.23, 26.81, and 79.71 respectively in the out-of-distribution setting. In the in-distribution setting, it surpasses the best prior method (PromptDiffusion) by 2.68% on CLIP-Dir, 4.70% on CLIP-Vis, and 1.27% on CLIP-T. In the out-of-distribution setting, the margins widen substantially, with InstaManip leading all competitors across every metric. In human evaluation, InstaManip is preferred over all baselines by at least 19% in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstaManip outperforms all four baselines on CLIP-Dir, CLIP-Vis, and CLIP-T in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings. Against the strongest few-shot baseline PromptDiffusion [74], it gains +2.68 on CLIP-Dir and +4.70 on CLIP-Vis in-distribution, and +2.86 on CLIP-Dir and +2.74 on CLIP-Vis out-of-distribution. Against ImageBrush [61], the in-distribution CLIP-Vis gap is +7.36 (32.39 vs 25.03), and out-of-distribution it is +4.34 (28.23 vs 23.89). Against VISII [42], the out-of-distribution CLIP-Dir margin is +3.58 (18.27 vs 14.69). The only metric where InstaManip does not lead is CLIP-I in the in-distribution setting, where InstructPix2Pix (text-only) scores 81.28 vs InstaManip's 80.11; however, the authors note CLIP-I has intrinsic limitations as a metric. In human evaluation, InstaManip is preferred over every baseline by ≥19% in both settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm for few-shot image manipulation (ImageBrush, VISII, PromptDiffusion) by instead leveraging the in-context learning capability of autoregressive MLLMs, arguing that their stronger reasoning ability is better suited to disentangling transformation features from irrelevant exemplar content. Architecturally, it builds on continuous-token autoregressive image generation (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024) and the LLaMA2-13B backbone, but introduces a novel group self-attention mechanism that explicitly separates the 'learning' and 'applying' stages of in-context learning, inspired by cognitive science models of skill acquisition. The relation regularization loss, which aligns manipulation-token geometry with CLIP text-embedding similarity, is a new training objective not present in prior few-shot editing or in-context learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that autoregressive models can outperform diffusion-based few-shot editors by a substantial margin (≥19% in human evaluation), suggesting that reasoning-oriented architectures may be the better substrate for tasks requiring compositional generalization from examples. The group self-attention principle—decomposing a complex in-context learning problem into separate learning and applying sub-problems—offers a general design pattern potentially transferable to other visual in-context tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-step or compositional edits, how it behaves with smaller backbones, and whether the non-monotonic scaling with exemplar count (diminishing returns from 1→2 but a jump from 2→3) reflects a fundamental representational threshold or a dataset-specific artifact.
