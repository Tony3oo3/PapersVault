## Problem

Modern ConvNet backbones rely on a bottom-up pyramid that progressively downsamples features, lacking the top-down attention mechanism by which the human visual system first forms a coarse overview of a scene and then directs fine-grained examination to salient regions. This absence of explicit top-down semantic guidance at intermediate layers limits localization accuracy, and existing dynamic or large-kernel convolutions cannot simultaneously model long-range dependencies and preserve strong local inductive biases as input resolution grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252630/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 for ImageNet-1K classification, 800×1280 for COCO detection/instance segmentation, and 512×2048 for ADE20K semantic segmentation. It produces a four-level feature pyramid (at H/4, H/8, H/16, H/32 resolutions) for dense prediction tasks, where the H/4 and H/8 features come from Base-Net and the H/16 and H/32 features come from Focus-Net. For classification, only Focus-Net's output is used; during pre-training, Overview-Net also receives its own auxiliary classification head.

## Method

OverLoCK introduces a Deep-stage Decomposition Strategy (DDS) that splits the backbone into three synergistic subnetworks: a Base-Net encoding low/mid-level features, a lightweight Overview-Net that immediately downsamples to H/32 to produce a semantically meaningful context prior, and a deeper Focus-Net that refines features guided by this prior at both the feature level (via a gated dynamic spatial aggregator) and the kernel-weight level. The key mixer, ContMix, computes per-token affinities against S×S region centers in the context prior, then applies a shared learnable linear projection to generate spatially varying dynamic convolution kernels whose weights carry global contextual information, enabling long-range dependency modeling while retaining fixed-size local inductive biases. The context prior is dynamically updated within each Focus-Net block via a residual connection to the initial prior (P_{i+1} = αP'_i + βP_o) to prevent information dilution, and half the kernel groups use large kernels (13–17) while the other half use 5×5 kernels for multi-scale perception.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), OverLoCK-T achieves 84.2% Top-1 accuracy with 5.5G FLOPs and 33M parameters, surpassing ConvNeXt-B (83.8%) while using only ~1/3 of its FLOPs. OverLoCK-S reaches 84.8% and OverLoCK-B reaches 85.1%, setting new state-of-the-art among pure ConvNets at their respective scales. On COCO detection (Mask R-CNN, 3× schedule), OverLoCK-S achieves 51.0 AP^b / 45.0 AP^m, and on ADE20K segmentation (UperNet, 160K iters), OverLoCK-T achieves 50.8 mIoU, outperforming all same-complexity baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OverLoCK-T (84.2%) outperforms all same-complexity baselines: UniRepLKNet-T (83.2%, +1.0%), VMamba-T (82.6%, +1.6%), MogaNet-S (83.4%, +0.8%), and InternImage-T (83.5%, +0.7%). At the Small scale, OverLoCK-S (84.8%) beats BiFormer-B (84.3%, +0.5%) and MogaNet-B (84.3%, +0.5%) with fewer FLOPs, and surpasses UniRepLKNet-S (83.9%, +0.9%). At the Base scale, OverLoCK-B (85.1%) edges out MaxViT-B (84.9%) by 0.2% while using 30% fewer FLOPs (16.7G vs 24.0G), and beats InternImage-B (84.9%) and MogaNet-L (84.7%). On COCO detection (Mask R-CNN 1×), OverLoCK-S achieves 49.4 AP^b, surpassing MogaNet-B (47.9%) by 1.5% and BiFormer-B (48.6%) by 0.8%; on ADE20K segmentation, OverLoCK-T (50.8 mIoU) exceeds UniRepLKNet-T (49.1%) by 1.7% and VMamba-T (48.8%) by 2.0%. The paper highlights that ConvNet baselines (e.g., MogaNet-B) match Transformer baselines (e.g., BiFormer-B) on classification but lag significantly on detection/segmentation due to limited receptive fields, a gap OverLoCK closes via its context-mixing dynamic kernels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the biomimetic top-down attention mechanism (overview-first, look-closely-next) into a pure ConvNet backbone for the first time, departing from both classic bottom-up pyramid architectures (ConvNeXt, Swin) and prior recurrent/feedback-based top-down approaches (AbsViT, feedback CNNs) that the authors show are computationally suboptimal. The Deep-stage Decomposition Strategy (DDS) repurposes the idea of a lightweight global-context branch feeding a deeper local-refinement branch, while the ContMix dynamic convolution synthesizes ideas from large-kernel convolutions (RepLKNet, SLaK) and spatially-varying dynamic kernels (DyConv, ODConv, Involution) into a single module that simultaneously achieves long-range modeling and local inductive bias—something no prior dynamic convolution has accomplished.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete limitation of fixed-kernel ConvNets: their receptive fields fail to scale with input resolution, causing measurable degradation on high-resolution tasks (detection, segmentation) even when classification accuracy matches Transformer/Mamba baselines. ContMix is positioned as a general plug-and-play module, and the DDS decomposition offers a reusable architectural pattern for injecting top-down context without recurrent overhead. Open questions include whether the three-subnetwork decomposition is the most parameter-efficient way to implement top-down guidance, how the approach behaves at very large input resolutions beyond 224×224, and whether the context-prior update mechanism (with learnable α, β scalars) is sensitive to initialization or training schedule.
