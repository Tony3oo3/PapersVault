## Problem

Existing text-to-3D human generation methods model the clothed human as a single holistic 3D asset, which prevents clothing replacement, causes unrealistic animation (garments adhering to the body), and limits user control over garment combinations. Additionally, textures produced directly by score-distillation optimization are typically low quality—overly smoothed and over-saturated—reducing visual appeal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250031/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts describing the body and each garment separately (e.g., 'a crew neck blue denim sleeveless A-skirt'), optionally augmented with a reference image encoded as a special token via TIP-Editor. It produces individual GS-enhanced mesh (GSM) models—one for the body and one per garment—each carrying UV color and opacity maps (U_c, U_α) that enable direct texture editing. Rendering operates at 1024×1024, and the decoupled mesh outputs are directly compatible with physics-based cloth simulators (e.g., Blender) for animation.

## Method

DAGSM introduces a GS-enhanced mesh (GSM) representation that binds 2D Gaussian disks to mesh triangles via barycentric coordinates and a local coordinate frame, so Gaussians deform with the mesh during animation while preserving high-fidelity rendering. The generation pipeline is sequential: first an unclothed body is optimized on SMPL-X with per-vertex displacement using RFDS loss, then each garment is generated as free 2DGS (unconstrained topology), filtered via SAM-based semantic segmentation to remove body leakage, extracted into a mesh via TSDF, and finally re-bound with 2DGS for texture optimization. A view-consistent refinement stage enhances texture quality by iteratively denoising rendered views with a cross-view attention mechanism (concatenating canonical and previous view features into key/value) and an incident-angle-weighted denoising (IAW-DE) strategy that allocates more noise and denoising steps to pixels observed near-perpendicular to the camera.

## Results

DAGSM achieves the highest CLIP text-alignment scores among all methods: 28.8 (ViT-L/14) and 45.8 (ViTbigG-14), outperforming the strongest baseline HumanGaussian (27.3 / 43.9) by 1.5 and 1.9 points respectively. In a 50-participant user study, DAGSM receives 74.0% votes for visual quality, 76.8% for text alignment, and 68.4% for animation realism, substantially exceeding the next-best baseline (SO-SMPL at 16.6%, 11.6%, and 20.4% respectively). The method also uniquely supports clothing replacement and physics-based cloth animation, capabilities absent in all three baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAGSM outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against DreamWaltz (NeRF + SDS), DAGSM leads by 4.6 points on ViT-L/14 CLIP and 3.9 on ViTbigG-14, while the user-study gap is dramatic (74.0% vs. 1.0% for visual quality); DreamWaltz also suffers from low-resolution rendering and broken body integrity during animation. Against HumanGaussian (3DGS + depth-guided SD), DAGSM wins by 1.5 / 1.9 CLIP points and 65.6 / 67.6 / 59.8 percentage points in the user study; HumanGaussian additionally produces unintended objects (e.g., basketballs) and garment tearing during animation. Against SO-SMPL (disentangled mesh + SDS), DAGSM leads by 3.2 / 3.8 CLIP points and 57.4 / 65.2 / 48.0 percentage points in the user study; SO-SMPL is fundamentally limited by SMPL-X's fixed topology and cannot generate garments with different topology (e.g., dresses). The qualitative gap is further widened by DAGSM's unique support for clothing replacement, UV-map-based texture editing, and physics-based cloth simulation, none of which the baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAGSM sits at the intersection of three active research threads: (1) SDS/RFDS-based text-to-3D generation (DreamFusion, Magic3D), (2) GS-on-mesh representations for animatable avatars (GaussianAvatars, SplattingAvatar, GoMAvatar), and (3) disentangled human generation (SO-SMPL, TELA). It departs from holistic text-to-3D human methods (DreamWaltz, HumanGaussian, DreamAvatar) by adopting a sequential body-then-garment pipeline, and from prior disentangled methods by replacing fixed-topology meshes (SO-SMPL) or implicit NeRFs (TELA) with a hybrid GS-enhanced mesh that supports arbitrary garment topologies and physics-based cloth simulation. The SAM-based filtering for body-garment separation and the view-consistent refinement (cross-view attention + IAW-DE) are novel contributions not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentangled GSM representation directly addresses a practical bottleneck in virtual try-on, gaming, and immersive telepresence: the inability to swap garments or simulate realistic cloth motion on text-generated avatars. The combination of 2DGS-on-mesh with TSDF extraction and UV-mapped texture storage makes the output directly editable in standard 3D pipelines (Blender), lowering the barrier between generative AI and production asset workflows. Key limitations include TSDF's inability to capture internal garment structures (pockets, linings), the multi-stage optimization cost (~2 hours total), and the reliance on SAM for clean separation, which may struggle with highly overlapping or transparent garments. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-person scenes, real-time generation, and more complex garment interactions (e.g., layered clothing with occlusion).
