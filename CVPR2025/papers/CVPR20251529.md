## Problem

Existing DGM⁴ methods for detecting and grounding multi-modal media manipulation rarely exploit the fine-grained cross-modal semantic misalignment that arises when an image or its text is tampered with. Prior approaches apply alignment learning only on unchanged media or rely solely on annotation-driven supervision, leaving the model without an explicit mechanism to perceive the image-text discordance that is the hallmark of manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251529/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a social media image (resized to 256×256) and its associated text description as input. It produces four outputs: a binary manipulation label, multi-label manipulation type (face swap, face attribute, text swap, text attribute), a bounding box localizing the manipulated image region, and per-token labels identifying manipulated words. During training, auxiliary texts (MLLM-generated captions and LLM-generated explanation texts) are constructed and consumed by the alignment and detection modules, but none of these are required at inference, so the framework imposes zero additional computational cost at test time.

## Method

ASAP introduces three training-only modules atop a ViT-B/16 + BERT-base backbone with dual multimodal encoders (vision-biased for image grounding, text-biased for text grounding). First, Large Model-assisted Alignment (LMA) uses VisCPM to caption the image and Mistral-7B to generate an explanation text from the original text, then performs contrastive learning between the image and both auxiliary texts to sharpen cross-modal alignment; the explanation text additionally serves as a detection signal where manipulated pairs are treated as negatives. Second, Manipulation-Guided Cross Attention (MGCA) constructs a binary guidance mask from manipulation annotations and applies a cross-entropy constraint on the attention matrix to force higher attention weights onto manipulated image patches and word tokens, counteracting the natural attention drop caused by semantic misalignment. Third, Patch Manipulation Modeling (PMM) introduces a patch-level binary classifier with Hard Negative Patch Selection—only patches adjacent to manipulated regions are treated as negatives—providing local spatial priors that improve bounding box regression. The full objective combines the standard DGM⁴ task losses with LMA, MGCA, and PMM terms weighted by hyperparameters α=0.1 and λ=0.01.

## Results

On the DGM⁴ dataset, ASAP achieves 94.38% AUC and 87.71% ACC for binary manipulation detection, 88.53% mAP for multi-label manipulation type identification, 77.35% IoUmean for image grounding, and 79.38% Precision / 73.86% Recall for text token grounding. The method delivers an average improvement of +2.54 over the HAMMER baseline across all four sub-tasks, with the largest gains in text grounding F1 (+5.17 over HAMMER) and binary classification AUC (+1.19). Notably, ASAP surpasses the prior state-of-the-art UFAFormer on AUC (+0.57), mAP (+0.68), and text grounding Precision (+6.03), though UFAFormer retains a slight edge on image grounding IoU metrics (78.33 vs. 77.35 IoUmean).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASAP outperforms all six baselines on binary classification (AUC, EER, ACC), multi-label classification (mAP, CF1, OF1), and text token grounding (Precision, Recall, F1). Against the closest competitor UFAFormer, ASAP gains +0.57 AUC, +0.68 mAP, and a substantial +6.03 Precision on text grounding, but concedes image grounding IoU metrics (IoUmean 77.35 vs. 78.33, IoU75 76.54 vs. 79.20). Compared to the original HAMMER baseline, ASAP improves AUC by +1.19, mAP by +2.31, and text grounding F1 by +5.17. VIKI leads on CF1 (81.07) among prior methods but is surpassed by ASAP on every other metric. The general-purpose vision-language models CLIP and VILT trail significantly, with AUC gaps of over 11 points, confirming that task-specific architectures remain necessary for fine-grained manipulation detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DGM4 task and HAMMER baseline (CVPR 2023), as well as subsequent improvements HAMMER++ (TPAMI 2024), VIKI (IF 2024), and UFAFormer (IJCV 2024). Its key conceptual departure is identifying fine-grained cross-modal semantic alignment as the underexplored bottleneck in DGM4, whereas prior methods focused on shallow/deep reasoning modules or metric-learning-based embedding alignment. Methodologically, it repurposes off-the-shelf MLLMs (VisCPM) and LLMs (Mistral 7B) as auxiliary text generators to construct training-only contrastive alignment signals, and introduces a Manipulation-Guided Cross Attention mechanism that uses ground-truth manipulation annotations to reshape the cross-attention matrix during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cross-modal alignment learning—largely ignored by prior DGM4 methods—yields the largest single ablation gain (AUC +1.12 from LMA alone) suggests a previously underappreciated lever for multimodal manipulation detection. The practical design of making all proposed modules training-only (zero inference overhead) makes the approach deployable without architectural changes. However, evaluation is confined to the DGM4 dataset with a single encoder pair (ViT-B/16 + BERT-base), leaving open whether the alignment strategy generalizes to other manipulation taxonomies, larger models, or out-of-distribution media. The reliance on specific large models for auxiliary text generation also raises questions about robustness to model quality and domain shift.
