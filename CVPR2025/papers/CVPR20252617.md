## Problem

Existing text-driven 3D scene editing methods that leverage 2D diffusion models suffer from multi-view inconsistency because they edit individual views independently, producing noisy gradients and suboptimal outputs such as minimal edits or blurriness. Additionally, when editing pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting models, the pre-trained Gaussians retain excessive source visual and geometric details, causing inefficient optimization and hindering convergence toward the edited target.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS model (with its camera poses and depth maps), a natural-language editing instruction (e.g., 'Turn the bear statue into a wild boar'), and the source views rendered from the 3DGS. Rather than iteratively editing rendered images, EditSplat directly edits the source images using InstructPix2Pix with extended multi-condition classifier-free guidance, then optimizes the trimmed 3DGS against these edited source images. The output is a fully edited 3DGS model that renders multi-view consistent results from any viewpoint, with the entire pipeline completing in approximately 6 minutes on a single RTX A6000 GPU.

## Method

EditSplat introduces two complementary mechanisms. Multi-view Fusion Guidance (MFG) projects initially edited multi-view images onto each target view using 3DGS depth maps with iterative depth-ordered alpha blending, then incorporates the resulting fused image as an additional guidance condition in the classifier-free guidance formula (Eq. 4) alongside the source image and text prompt, ensuring the diffusion model produces multi-view consistent edits without extra training or repeated diffusion passes. Attention-Guided Trimming (AGT) unprojects cross-attention maps from the diffusion U-Net onto 3D Gaussians via inverse rendering to compute per-Gaussian attention weights, then prunes the top-k% highest-attention Gaussians (which retain redundant source information) before optimization and selectively updates only high-attention Gaussians during training, enabling both faster convergence and semantically precise local editing without binary masks.

## Results

On 8 scenes with 2 prompts each, EditSplat achieves the highest scores across all three evaluation metrics: CLIP directional similarity (0.1431), CLIP text-image similarity (0.2531), and user study preference (0.4227). The user study, conducted with 100 participants via Amazon Mechanical Turk, shows the most dramatic margin, with EditSplat nearly doubling the score of the strongest baseline DGE (0.4227 vs. 0.2143). The full editing pipeline runs in approximately 6 minutes per scene on a single RTX A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EditSplat outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against GaussianEditor [9] (iterative IP2P-based editing), EditSplat improves CLIP_dir by 55% (0.1431 vs. 0.0923) and user study by 157% (0.4227 vs. 0.1643), attributed to MFG providing direct multi-view guidance rather than iterative rendered-image editing. Against GaussCtrl [48] (ControlNet-based depth conditioning), EditSplat gains 46% on CLIP_dir and 113% on user study, with qualitative results showing GaussCtrl produces blurry outputs while EditSplat yields sharper, more prompt-aligned edits. Against DGE [8] (IP2P with epipolar constraints), the gap is most striking in the user study (0.4227 vs. 0.2143, ~97% improvement), while CLIP metrics show more modest but consistent gains (0.1431 vs. 0.1222 on CLIP_dir; 0.2531 vs. 0.2394 on CLIP_sim). Qualitatively, EditSplat produces more pronounced and semantically precise edits, particularly in global transformations (e.g., 'Make it autumn') where AGT's attention-guided selective optimization preserves non-target regions that other methods inadvertently alter.

## Relation to Prior Work

EditSplat extends the text-driven 3D editing pipeline established by InstructNeRF2NeRF and its 3DGS-based successors (GaussianEditor, DGE, GaussCtrl) by addressing two specific failure modes: multi-view inconsistency from independent per-view 2D edits, and optimization inefficiency caused by pre-trained Gaussians retaining excessive source information. Its Multi-view Fusion Guidance departs from prior multi-view strategies (epipolar constraints in DGE, depth conditioning in GaussCtrl, iterative dataset updates in GaussianEditor) by projecting initially edited views and injecting them as an additional classifier-free guidance channel, requiring no extra training or repeated diffusion passes. Its Attention-Guided Trimming introduces a novel use of cross-attention maps to both prune redundant Gaussians and restrict optimization to semantically relevant regions, replacing the binary-mask localization used by most prior 3DGS editors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, training-free recipe for improving 3DGS editing quality that practitioners can adopt without retraining diffusion models, and the AGT insight—that high-attention Gaussians actively hinder convergence and should be pruned—offers a general principle for editing any pre-trained explicit 3D representation. The large user-study gap (0.42 vs ~0.21 for baselines) relative to modest CLIP improvements suggests current metrics understate the perceptual benefit of multi-view consistency, an open question for the field. Limitations include dependence on 2D diffusion model quality for complex prompts and on 3DGS depth-map accuracy for the projection step, both of which are still evolving.
