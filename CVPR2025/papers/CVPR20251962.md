## Problem

While prior work has shown that various ANNs can be deceived by visual illusions, no study had examined how diffusion models encode perceptual biases in their internal representations, nor whether this could be exploited to generate new illusions. The paper asks whether the iterative denoising process of diffusion models naturally replicates human brightness/color perceptual shifts, and if so, whether this can be leveraged to synthesize novel visual illusions in realistic, text-prompted images.

## Input / Output

For illusion replication, the method takes a single RGB or grayscale image containing a visual illusion and produces per-channel mean intensity measurements in user-annotated target regions at intermediate DDIM inversion steps (5 or 10 steps), which are compared against human perceptual ground truth. For illusion generation, the input is a text prompt plus user-defined target regions with specified desired intensities; the output is a high-resolution natural image (via DeepFloyd S1+S2) in which physically identical patches within those regions are perceived differently. Targets are small (e.g., 6×6 px in the S1 latent, expanding to 24×24 after super-resolution), and the perceptual effect is measured in the intermediate noise space z_t rather than the final decoded image.

## Method

The core observation is that DDIM inversion of an illusory stimulus produces progressive brightness and color shifts in intermediate latent representations z_t that align with human perception, because the model attempts to project out-of-distribution stimuli onto the learned natural-image manifold, introducing systematic 'corrections' that mirror perceptual biases. For replication, the method simply performs DDIM inversion and reads out mean per-channel intensities in target regions at a chosen intermediate step. For generation, a custom two-term loss is applied N times per diffusion step: a perceptual loss L_VI that drives the measured intensity of each target toward a user-specified desired value, and a similarity loss L_sim (MSE between the target region in z_t and the original target) that prevents the region from looking out of place. Targets are explicitly added to the intermediate representation before loss computation, and the image-space DeepFloyd model is used to avoid backpropagating through the VAE decoder. This differs from prior VI-generation work by explicitly localizing the perceptual effect to designated regions and operating within natural-image content guided by text prompts, rather than producing only high-frequency grating patterns.

## Results

On the BRI3L brightness illusion dataset, DDIM inversion with 10 steps achieves a Perceptual Accuracy Score (PAS) of 100.00% at threshold τ=1.0, 98.85% at τ=0.9, and 84.17% at τ=0.8, substantially outperforming all baselines at stricter thresholds. On color illusion datasets, DDIM-10 reaches 90.40% (IllusionVQA), 96.70% (GVIL), and 100.00% (HallusionBench). A VAE-only baseline (without the diffusion process) scores near zero at τ≤0.9, confirming that the iterative denoising trajectory—not the encoder alone—is responsible for the perceptual alignment. Psychophysical validation with 15 observers confirms generated illusions: targets were judged different in 64% (mean) of enforced-illusion trials versus only 13% (mean) for controls.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The DDIM inversion method dominates all four baselines across every dataset and threshold. At the strictest threshold (τ=0.8) on BRI3L, DDIM-10 scores 84.17% while ODOG reaches only 36.58%, CIWAM 5.69%, and both DNnet and RestoreNet collapse to 0.00%. On the color illusion benchmarks (Table 2), the gap widens further: DDIM-10 achieves 90.40–100.00% PAS versus CIWAM's 56.52–83.33% and DNnet/RestoreNet's 14.20–33.30%. ODOG, while competitive at τ=1.0 on BRI3L (72.44%), cannot be applied to color datasets at all. The VAE-only ablation (0.00–73.18% on BRI3L, 0.00–21.30% on color) is the most informative comparison, isolating the diffusion trajectory as the source of perceptual alignment rather than the autoencoder's reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes DDIM inversion [49]—originally designed for deterministic sampling and image editing—as a perceptual measurement tool, measuring visual illusion effects in intermediate latent representations rather than at model output. It departs from prior VI replication work (DN-NET, RestoreNet, ODOG, CIWAM) which either used image restoration backbones or shallow architectures, by showing that a deep autoencoder's diffusion trajectory captures human perception far better than its VAE component alone. For generation, it extends the line of diffusion-based VI synthesis [6, 10, 11, 46] from high-level cognitive illusions or uncontrolled keyword-based brightness patterns to explicit, region-targeted brightness/color illusions embedded in natural images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical observation that diffusion trajectories naturally encode human-like perceptual shifts provides a new computational lens on the wholly-empirical paradigm of vision, suggesting that statistical learning on natural images alone can produce illusory perception without explicit perceptual rules. The generation method is validated psychophysically (64% vs. 13% for controls), but limitations remain: spatial destruction of input during inversion limits replication of small-region effects, and the generation approach requires manually defined target regions that constrain the T2I model's expressiveness. An open question is whether this alignment between diffusion trajectories and human perception is a fundamental property of iterative denoising or an artifact of specific architectures and training data, and whether it can be leveraged to improve perception-awareness in vision-language models.
