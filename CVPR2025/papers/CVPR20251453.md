## Problem

Polynomial elimination solvers for geometric vision become intractable as problem degree and dimensionality grow, and existing homotopy continuation (HC) methods remain expensive because they must track all solutions in the complex domain or rely on classifiers trained over a limited set of real-world problem-solution pairs. The paper addresses how to efficiently initialize HC with a single, well-chosen starting problem-solution pair so that only one root needs to be tracked to the target problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251453/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generalized camera resectioning, the input is a set of 3D-2D generalized correspondences (3D world points, unit image rays, and ray origins) row-wise stacked into a matrix, and the output is the camera rotation (quaternion) and translation. For generalized relative pose and scale (GRPS), the input is 2D-2D generalized correspondences (image rays and ray origins from two non-central views), and the output is relative rotation (6D continuous representation), translation, and a scale factor. The GRPS minimal problem uses 7 correspondences, but the authors add one extra correspondence (8 total) to form an overdetermined system that disambiguates among multiple geometrically feasible solutions and raises success rate from ~70% to 95%.

## Method

The proposed simulate-and-solve paradigm combines three stages: (1) a 1D-convolutional regression network, trained on synthetic correspondences, predicts a rough initial pose; (2) an online simulator uses that predicted pose to reproject known 3D points (or solve for depths and reproject rays in GRPS), generating a new set of correspondences that is perfectly consistent with the predicted solution, thereby constructing a valid starting problem-solution pair; (3) homotopy continuation interpolates polynomial coefficients from the simulated starting problem to the original target problem, tracking only the single root corresponding to the predicted solution. The key insight is that the regressor need not be highly accurate—moderate prediction error is sufficient for HC to converge, since the simulated problem is by construction close to the target. This avoids the cost of parallel all-root tracking and the data limitations of classifier-based initialization, and the approach generalizes to any geometric problem where a simple re-projection rule can serve as the simulator.

## Results

On the generalized PnP (UPnP) problem with 16 noisy correspondences, the proposed Simulator HC achieves 100% success rate (2° rotation threshold) with 0.032° mean rotation error and 0.513% translation error in 0.316 ms, making it the fastest method among all 100%-success solvers. On the harder GRPS problem embedded in RANSAC (200 correspondences, 5 cameras), Simulator HC handles up to 40% outliers with 91% success rate in 1.35 s, versus 98.9% for Gröbner Bases in 4.59 s. On the real LaMAR dataset, Simulator HC achieves 68–86% success rate across three sequences with median times of 0.44–2.08 s, consistently outperforming Gröbner Bases (60–72% success, 0.65–2.83 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Gröbner Bases UPnP solver [31], Simulator HC matches the 100% success rate and 0.032° rotation error while being 4× faster (0.316 ms vs. 1.254 ms). Compared to parallel exhaustive-tracking HC on GPU [7] and CPU [8], which also achieve 100% success, Simulator HC is 3× faster than GPU-HC (0.316 vs. 1.002 ms) and 31× faster than CPU-HC (0.316 vs. 9.796 ms) by tracking only a single root. On the GRPS problem, the Eigen optimization solver [32] achieves only ~60% success on minimal correspondences and is the slowest method overall; Simulator HC with 8 points reaches 95% success and is ~40× faster than CPU-HC. Against the Gröbner Bases GRPS solver [40] (144×284 elimination template), Simulator HC is 17× faster in RANSAC (1.35 s vs. 4.59 s at 40% outliers) with a small success-rate gap (91% vs. 98.9%), and on LaMAR it achieves higher success (86% vs. 72% on LIN) in less time (0.44 s vs. 0.65 s). The raw regressor alone (32% success) and regressor+LM (54%) are far inferior, confirming that the online simulator + single-root HC is essential for compensating regression error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from Hruby et al.'s (CVPR 2022) classifier-based approach to selecting starting problem-solution pairs for homotopy continuation by replacing the fixed-set classifier with a regression network trained on arbitrarily large synthetic data, coupled with an online simulator that generates a geometrically consistent starting problem from the predicted solution. The polynomial formulations for generalized camera resectioning (UPnP) and generalized relative pose and scale (GRPS) are inherited from prior algebraic-geometric work, but the simulate-and-solve pipeline—regression → reproject → single-root HC tracking—is a new architectural combination. The method also extends the single-solution-tracking idea by adding one extra correspondence to resolve geometric ambiguity, a practical departure from minimal-problem formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical polynomial solver for the GRPS problem where previously only multi-start optimization existed, and demonstrates 17× speedup over Gröbner Bases while maintaining comparable success rates within RANSAC. The broader implication is that for polynomial systems where elimination templates become intractable (hundreds to thousands of solutions), the regression-simulator-HC pipeline offers a scalable alternative that runs efficiently on CPU. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to polynomial systems with highly non-convex solution landscapes, whether the regression network's synthetic-data training transfers to real-world noise distributions beyond what is modeled, and whether the one-extra-correspondence heuristic is sufficient for all ambiguous cases or whether a more principled disambiguation strategy is needed.
