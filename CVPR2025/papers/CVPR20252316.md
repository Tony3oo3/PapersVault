## Problem

Direct convolution over irregularly sampled sparse depth suffers from two fundamental issues: spatial mismatch between fixed kernel shapes and varying point distributions, and ambiguity in distinguishing valid from invalid (zero-valued) depth pixels. Existing data-driven methods (sparse convolutions, learned kernels, bilateral propagation) mitigate but never fully eliminate these problems because they still apply convolution to incomplete depth data. SigNet eliminates both issues entirely by reframing depth completion as depth enhancement, operating only on a fully dense intermediate representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse depth map (with irregularly distributed valid points) and a corresponding RGB image at the same resolution. The sparse depth is first densified via non-CNN tools (IP-Basic and Levin colorization) into a coarse dense depth map, which is then denoised before entering the CNN-based enhancement network. The output is a dense depth map at the same spatial resolution as the input, with the network containing only 3.3M parameters. Notably, the input and target share the same resolution, so the degradation model requires no downsampling component, unlike typical image restoration settings.

## Method

SigNet redefines depth completion as depth enhancement by establishing a self-supervised degradation bridge: the coarse dense depth Z is modeled as a convolution of the target depth Y with a learned degradation kernel K(D;θ) plus noise. The Degradation-Aware Decomposition and Fusion (DADF) module applies DCT to decompose the degradation into frequency-domain patches, which are used to generate an adaptive mask that selects the most informative high-frequency components from RGB features. These selected RGB features are then fused with depth features via a Conditional Mamba, where the SSM state parameters (B, C, Δ) are generated from both the RGB-D input and the degradation patches through gated recurrent units, enabling degradation-conditioned global information interaction. Training uses an L1 reconstruction loss plus a self-supervised degradation loss that enforces K⊗Y ≈ Z, providing an additional constraint linking input and output. The entire pipeline avoids any direct convolution on the original sparse depth, and the DADF module is iterated five times for progressive refinement.

## Results

SigNet achieves state-of-the-art depth completion across all four benchmarks with only 3.3M parameters. On NYUv2, it reaches 83 mm RMSE (5 mm below BPNet) and 0.012 REL; on DIML, 55 mm RMSE and 0.006 REL (19% and 45% lower than the runner-up); on SUN RGBD, 116 mm RMSE (8 mm below BPNet, 36 mm below AGG-Net); and on TOFDC, 87 mm RMSE and 0.012 REL (5 mm and 0.002 below TPVD). Notably, SigNet uses 96% fewer parameters than BPNet (3.3M vs. 89.9M) and 97% fewer than AGG-Net (3.3M vs. 129.1M) while outperforming them on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SigNet outperforms all 17 baselines on every metric across all four datasets. On NYUv2, it beats the previous best BPNet [38] by 5 mm RMSE (83 vs. 88) while using 96% fewer parameters (3.3M vs. 89.9M); on DIML, the margin widens to 13 mm RMSE (55 vs. 68) and 45% lower REL (0.006 vs. 0.011) over BPNet. On SUN RGBD, SigNet reduces RMSE by 8 mm over BPNet (116 vs. 124) and by 36 mm over AGG-Net [2] (116 vs. 152). On TOFDC, it surpasses TPVD [50] by 5 mm RMSE (87 vs. 92) and 0.002 REL while using 27.9M fewer parameters. The efficiency gap is striking: compared to CSPN [4], SigNet uses 81% fewer parameters and 82% less inference time while achieving 61% lower RMSE. The only dataset where SigNet does not achieve the absolute best on every single metric is NYUv2, where BPNet edges it by 0.001 in REL (0.011 vs. 0.012), though SigNet still leads on RMSE and δ₁.

## Relation to Prior Work

SigNet departs from the dominant paradigm in depth completion—directly convolving over irregularly sampled sparse depth (as in CSPN, BPNet, AGG-Net)—by first densifying sparse depth with classical non-CNN tools (IP-Basic, Levin colorization) and then reframing the remaining problem as an enhancement task analogous to blind image super-resolution. The degradation-aware fusion mechanism borrows the degradation-modeling philosophy from the blind super-resolution literature (Zhang et al. 2021, Liang et al. 2022) but applies it to RGB-D depth fusion for the first time. Architecturally, the Conditional Mamba is a targeted modification of Vision Mamba [25], where state parameters B, C, and Δ are generated from both the RGB-D input and degradation patches via gated recurrent convolutions, rather than from the input alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.3M-parameter model achieving SOTA across four benchmarks while using 96% fewer parameters than BPNet and 97% fewer than AGG-Net makes this practically attractive for deployment-constrained settings (embedded, real-time). The honest failure analysis on KITTI (outdoor, ~30% valid pixels, large depth ranges) reveals that the degradation-bridge assumption breaks down when ground-truth edges are too sparse to constrain the learned degradation, leaving outdoor generalization as a clear open problem. The paper suggests leveraging large depth models (DepthAnything v2) to provide edge supervision as a potential remedy, pointing toward a hybrid paradigm that remains unexplored.
