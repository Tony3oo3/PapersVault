## Problem

Concept ablation methods for diffusion models are typically evaluated only by checking whether text prompts for erased concepts fail to produce those concepts. This paper asks whether the erased concept information truly disappears from the model's latent space, or whether high-likelihood latent seeds capable of generating erased concept images still exist. No prior work had analyzed the likelihood of latent seeds corresponding to erased images, leaving open the question of whether erasure is merely a text-conditional failure rather than genuine forgetting.

## Input / Output

The analysis requires white-box access to an ablated Stable Diffusion v1.4 model (latent space 4×64×64), a set of query images with captions depicting the erased concept, and random support images from COCO. The pipeline produces latent seed vectors z_T, reconstructed images via diffusion inference, per-seed NLL scores under the standard normal, and a unitless Relative Distance metric (ratio of Earth Mover's Distances between erased-set and reference-set NLL distributions relative to N(0,I)). Inversion uses Renoise with 50 inversion and 5 renoising steps guided by image captions.

## Method

The core analysis inverts erased concept images through the ablated model's VAE encoder and Renoise diffusion inversion to recover latent seeds z_T, then measures their NLL under the model's training distribution N(0,I). A Relative Distance metric is defined as the ratio of EMD between the erased set's NLL distribution and N(0,I) to that of a reference set, providing a unitless measure of how much the erased concept's latents have been pushed away from the plausible region. To find multiple distinct seeds for the same query image, a Sequential Inversion Block (SIB) is introduced: it inverts the VAE decoder from a random support image, optimizes the latent toward the query image, then applies diffusion inversion to yield a new z_T. Across nine ablation methods and six concepts, the paper shows that all methods retain high-likelihood seeds that reconstruct erased images with PSNR ≥ 25 dB, and that multiple well-separated seeds (cosine distance measured) can generate the same image, lying approximately on a sphere in latent space.

## Results

Across all nine concept ablation methods and six concepts (Nudity, Van Gogh, Church, Garbage Truck, Parachute, Tench), every method achieves reconstruction PSNR ≥ 25 dB, confirming that high-quality images of erased concepts can still be generated from high-likelihood latent seeds. The best erasing performance (highest relative distance d_N) is achieved by ESD on the Parachute concept with d_N = 2.49, indicating non-negligible overlap between erased and reference latent distributions. Many ablation methods achieve relative distances comparable to the unmodified Vanilla SD 1.4 model, and in some concept–method pairs the distance drops below 1, suggesting the erased set is actually closer to the standard normal than the reference set. When searching for multiple distant seeds per image (Sequential Inversion Block), PSNR and d_N values decrease slightly but remain high, and the retrieved seeds lie on a sphere around the original seed with only 2% coefficient of variation in Euclidean distance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new erasure method but evaluates nine existing ones, and the central finding is that all nine fail to truly erase concepts: every method retains latent seeds with high likelihood that reconstruct erased-concept images at PSNR ≥ 25 dB. ESD (Gandikota et al., ICCV 2023) achieves the best relative distance of 2.49 on Parachute, but this still indicates substantial overlap with the reference distribution. FMN (Zhang et al., CVPR 2024), SPM (Lyu et al., CVPR 2024), and AdvUnlearn (Zhang et al., 2024) perform comparably to the unmodified Vanilla SD 1.4 model, meaning their erasure provides essentially no additional protection in latent space. EraseDiff (Wu et al.), Salun (Fan et al.), Scissorhands (Wu et al.), UCE (Gandikota et al., WACV 2024), and AC (Kumari et al., ICCV 2023) all exhibit relative distances at or below 1 on at least one concept, indicating the erased set is no less likely than the reference set. The Vanilla SD 1.4 baseline confirms that the analysis pipeline itself is sound: without any erasure, d_N ≈ 1 and PSNR is highest, while all ablation methods cluster near or below this baseline, demonstrating that current text-prompt-level erasure does not translate to latent-space forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a diagnostic analysis of existing concept ablation methods for latent diffusion models, departing from prior evaluations that test erasure via text-prompted generation or adversarial prompts (Zhang et al. [50], Pham et al. [27]). Rather than searching for prompts that circumvent erasure, it inverts erased concept images using diffusion inversion (Renoise) to recover latent seeds and measures their likelihood under the model's training distribution. The closest prior work, Pham et al., used textual inversion to find embeddings for erased images; this paper instead operates in the latent seed space, introducing a Relative Distance metric based on Earth Mover's Distance between NLL distributions of erased and reference sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all nine state-of-the-art ablation methods retain high-likelihood latent seeds capable of reconstructing erased concept images (PSNR ≥ 25 dB) suggests that current fine-tuning-based erasure may be fundamentally insufficient, raising the question of whether true concept erasure in diffusion models is tractable. The geometric observation that multiple distant seeds lie on a sphere around the target seed implies that re-mapping a single latent is insufficient for forgetting. Key limitations include the white-box assumption and the fact that methods do suppress text-prompted generation, so the practical threat depends on whether an adversary can access or search the latent space. Open questions include whether the intractability extends to black-box settings and whether the sphere of seeds can be systematically neutralized.
