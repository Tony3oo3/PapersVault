## Problem

Generating photorealistic, multi-view consistent images of a person from a single casually taken photo remains challenging because high-quality multi-view studio data is expensive and limited in identity diversity, while in-the-wild images lack ground-truth 3D structure. Existing methods either depend on explicit 3D priors (e.g., SMPL) that constrain generalization, produce low-resolution outputs, or lack rigorous 3D consistency evaluation—standard reconstruction metrics unfairly penalize plausible novel content in this ill-posed setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251327/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Pippo takes a single RGB image of a person (no camera parameters or fitted body model required), automatically extracts a face crop via FaceNet, and accepts target camera poses plus a Spatial Anchor—an oriented 3D point specifying approximate head position and gaze direction projected into each target view. The output is a dense set of 1K-resolution multi-view consistent images; at inference, an attention biasing technique allows generating more than 5× as many views as were jointly denoised during training, producing studio-quality turnaround sequences from a single iPhone photo.

## Method

Pippo is a multi-view diffusion transformer (DiT-like with parallel MLP/attention and VAE latent space) trained in three stages: pre-training on 3B in-the-wild human images with DINOv2 image-conditioned denoising, mid-training at 128×128 on studio captures to jointly denoise up to 48 views with coarse camera encoding via a shallow MLP, and post-training at 1024×1024 with pixel-aligned spatial controls. The key architectural contribution is ControlMLP, a lightweight ControlNet-inspired module that injects Plücker coordinates (processed through SIREN to amplify inter-pixel differences) and the Spatial Anchor as per-block scale-and-shift modulation, enabling 3D-consistent high-resolution generation. Reference conditioning uses self-attention (concatenating patchified reference and face-crop tokens with noisy tokens) rather than cross-attention, which ablations show is critical for identity preservation. The paper also introduces a Reprojection Error metric (RE@SG) that triangulates SuperPoint/SuperGlue correspondences across generated views and measures reprojection error, providing a ground-truth-free 3D consistency evaluation.

## Results

On the iPhone full-body dataset at 1K resolution, Pippo achieves a Reprojection Error (RE@SG) of 3.0, FaceNet similarity of 58.0, and CLIP body similarity of 68.1, outperforming all compared multi-view diffusion baselines on every metric. On internal studio data, the full-body model reaches RE@SG of 1.5 with PSNR 22.4 and SSIM 91.7 at 1K resolution, while the head-only model achieves RE@SG of 3.4 with FaceNet similarity of 73.5. On public benchmarks, Pippo scores RE@SG 3.8 on Ava-256 (head-only) and RE@SG 1.2 on Goliath (full-body), confirming generalizability beyond the proprietary training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pippo outperforms all three quantitative baselines (MV-Adapter, Era3D, Wonder3D) on every reported metric in Table 4: it achieves the lowest RE@SG (3.0 vs. 4.7, 4.1, 5.3), the highest FaceNet identity similarity (58.0 vs. 43.0, 38.1, 34.7), and the highest CLIP body similarity (68.1 vs. 64.1, 64.4, 58.8), while also operating at a higher resolution (1K vs. 768/512/256). Against DiffPortrait3D [20] and SiTH [25], the comparison is qualitative only (Figure 4), as those methods rely on explicit SMPL priors making direct quantitative comparison infeasible; the authors report that Pippo supports greater viewpoint variability and closer adherence to the input image than DiffPortrait3D, and produces higher-resolution, more accurate multi-view synthesis than SiTH. The largest margin of improvement is over Wonder3D, where Pippo reduces RE@SG by 43% (5.3→3.0) and increases face similarity by 23 points (34.7→58.0), reflecting Wonder3D's lower resolution (256) and cross-domain diffusion approach. Pippo's advantage over Era3D is most pronounced on identity preservation (Face: 58.0 vs. 38.1, a 20-point gap), suggesting that the multi-stage training with large-scale in-the-wild pretraining is critical for identity fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pippo extends the multi-view diffusion transformer line (Zero-1-to-3, MVDream, SPAD) to the human-specific domain, but deliberately avoids the explicit 3D representations (NeRF, 3D Gaussians, SMPL priors) that prior human-specific methods like SiTH and DiffPortrait3D rely on. Its architectural lineage traces to DiT with AdaLN modulation (Stable Diffusion 3, Flux), but introduces a lightweight ControlMLP module as a ControlNet alternative for pixel-aligned spatial conditioning, and a novel Spatial Anchor (a single oriented 3D point) that outperforms more complex camera-encoding strategies. The three-stage training recipe—large-scale in-the-wild pre-training, low-resolution multi-view mid-training, and high-resolution post-training with spatial controls—repurposes the DALL-E 2-style image-conditioned pre-training for a downstream multi-view objective, a combination not seen in prior multi-view human work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pippo is the first to produce 1K-resolution, multi-view-consistent human images from a single casually taken photo without requiring parametric body models or input camera parameters, making it directly applicable to consumer photography pipelines. The proposed Reprojection Error (RE@SG) metric fills a genuine evaluation gap by measuring 3D consistency without penalizing plausible novel content, and is likely to be adopted by the broader multi-view generation community. Key limitations include the restricted number of simultaneously generated views (memory-bound) and the reliance on proprietary studio data for mid/post-training, though the authors note public datasets like Codec Avatar should suffice. Extending to temporally consistent multi-view video generation is identified as the natural next step.
