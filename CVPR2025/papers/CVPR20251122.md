## Problem

Finding pixel-level correspondences between images from different visual modalities (e.g., RGB and depth, RGB and thermal) is difficult because existing self-supervised methods rely on photo-consistency or cross-modal translation assumptions that are frequently violated across modalities, and prior approaches require spatially aligned paired data from multiple sensors that is unavailable for dense correspondence. This paper asks whether cross-modal correspondences can be learned entirely from unlabeled, unpaired multimodal video through cycle consistency alone, without any hand-crafted similarity metric.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251122/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two images from potentially different modalities (RGB, depth, thermal, sketch, or stylistically varied images) captured at different times and positions, with no requirement for spatial or temporal alignment during training. For geometric tasks, a modality-specific CNN encoder extracts features at 4× downsampling (single-channel depth is replicated to 3 channels); for semantic tasks, a shared DINOv2 encoder initialized with pretrained weights is fine-tuned. The output is a dense all-pairs transition probability matrix between the two images, from which a predicted flow field (expected pixel displacement) is derived, supporting both forward and reverse matching directions simultaneously.

## Method

The paper extends the contrastive random walk framework to cross-modal matching by constructing a directed graph where nodes are image patches and edges connect patches across modalities, with a Global Matching Transformer (six blocks of self-attention, cross-attention, and feed-forward layers) producing transition probabilities via softmax over feature dot products. A key finding is that direct cross-modal cycle-consistency training plateaus at suboptimal solutions due to the substantial domain gap between modalities, so they introduce auxiliary intra-modal random walks on augmented crops of the same modality to stabilize optimization and prevent the model from collapsing to arbitrary intensity-based alignments. The training objective combines cross-modal cycle-consistency loss (label-warping cross-entropy on chained round-trip transition matrices), intra-modal cycle-consistency loss, and an edge-aware smoothness loss applied only to RGB-source flows. Training proceeds in three stages: intra-modal CRW, then adding cross-modal CRW, then adding smoothness regularization, all on unpaired frames sampled at random timesteps from multimodal video.

## Results

On RGB-Depth matching (NYU Depth V2), the method achieves 33.5% and 34.3% positional accuracy (<δ_avg^x>) for RGB→Depth and Depth→RGB, respectively, substantially outperforming the best unsupervised baselines (ARFlow: 7.5/7.4; CycleGAN+GMFlow: 16.2/16.6). On RGB-Thermal matching, it reaches 41.8/47.9% on Thermal-IM and 35.2/34.1% on KAIST, again leading all baselines by a wide margin. For semantic tasks, the method achieves 53.61/82.20 PCK-5/PCK-10 on photo-sketch (PSC6K) and 69.26/84.19 on the new cross-style benchmark, slightly surpassing the supervised GeoAwareSC (68.30/79.67) on cross-style matching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all baselines on cross-modal geometric matching. On NYU Depth V2, it achieves 33.5/34.3% (RGB→D / D→RGB), more than 2× the best prior method (CycleGAN D→RGB + GMFlow at 16.2/16.6%) and ~4.5× ARFlow (7.5/7.4%). On Thermal-IM, it reaches 41.8/47.9%, beating SD-DINO (29.3/34.9%) by 12–13 points and ARFlow (12.5/13.2%) by ~3×. On KAIST, it leads ARFlow (31.0/30.4%) by ~4 points and GMFlow (23.1/22.3%) by ~12 points. Supervised optical flow models (RAFT, GMFlow) collapse on cross-modal pairs (e.g., RAFT drops to 1.3% on D→RGB), confirming that photo-consistency assumptions fail across modalities. For semantic matching, the method is competitive but not dominant: on photo-sketch (PSC6K), it trails the supervised PSCNet (57.92 vs. 53.61 PCK-5) and trained WarpC-Net (56.78), while on the new cross-style benchmark it slightly edges out the supervised GeoAwareSC (69.26 vs. 68.30 PCK-5) and clearly beats SD-DINO (60.14) and DINOv2+NN (23.13).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive random walk framework (Jabri et al., 2020) and its recent transformer-based instantiation, GMRW (Shrivastava & Owens, 2024), from intra-modal space-time tracking to cross-modal pixel correspondence. The key conceptual departure is from prior cross-modal registration methods (e.g., Arar et al., 2020) that rely on explicit modality translation via CycleGAN or hand-crafted photo-consistency metrics; here, cycle consistency alone suffices to learn a shared embedding space across modalities. The introduction of auxiliary intra-modal random walks as a training stabilizer is a novel architectural choice not present in the original CRW or GMRW formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that dense cross-modal pixel correspondence can be learned entirely from unlabeled, unpaired multimodal video, removing the need for spatially aligned sensor pairs that have been a bottleneck in multimodal registration. The cross-style image matching benchmark (built with Flux) is a useful new evaluation setting for semantic correspondence under large appearance variation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to modalities lacking shared visual structure (e.g., LiDAR, audio), whether RGB is truly necessary as an anchor modality, and how the method scales to more than two modalities simultaneously.
