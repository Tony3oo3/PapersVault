## Problem

Movie dubbing (Visual Voice Cloning) requires generating speech that aligns temporally and emotionally with a movie clip while cloning a speaker's voice, but existing methods are hindered by the limited scale and background noise of dubbing datasets, which degrade acoustic quality, and by visual domain discrepancies across different movies that destabilize prosody alignment. Prior two-stage approaches like Speaker2Dubber only pre-train the phoneme encoder, which is insufficient to handle the exaggerated prosody variations and low audio quality inherent in dubbing data.

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs: a short reference audio clip for voice cloning, a dubbing script (text), and a silent movie clip sampled at 25 FPS. Audio is processed at 24 kHz into 80-bin mel-spectrograms (FFT 2048, hop 300, window 1200), and the video yields lip motion features, frame-level facial emotion features via S3FD+EmoFAN, and a video-level atmosphere embedding. The output is a dubbing speech waveform whose pronunciation duration and pitch/energy contours are aligned to the character's on-screen performance.

## Method

The method uses a two-stage training procedure. In Stage 1, the entire acoustic system (bidirectional LSTM text encoder, acoustic style encoder, and HiFi-GAN-based decoder with AdaIN) is pre-trained on LibriTTS with a prosody enhancement strategy that applies pitch shifting and duration stretching in the mel-spectrogram domain to bridge the prosodic gap between flat TTS speech and expressive dubbing. In Stage 2, the acoustic system is frozen and an acoustic-disentangled framework is introduced: a Prosodic Text BERT Encoder extracts phoneme-level prosodic text features, a Prosodic Style Diffusion module samples non-timbre style from noise conditioned on emotion-fused features, and an In-Domain Emotion Analysis module normalizes frame-level EmoFAN features with a video-level atmosphere prior to reduce cross-movie domain shifts. Multi-head cross-attention fuses phoneme-level prosody with emotion features, a separate duration predictor (using cross-attention between prosodic text and lip motion) aligns pronunciation timing, and the frozen decoder reconstructs the final waveform with the predicted pitch, energy, and speaker style.

## Results

On the V2C-Animation benchmark (Dub 1.0), the proposed method achieves the best WER of 8.04% (vs. 17.51% for the next-best Speaker2Dubber), the highest UT-MOS of 3.10 (vs. 2.41), the best emotion accuracy of 48.93%, and the lowest MCD-DTW of 9.29. On the GRID benchmark (Dub 1.0), it achieves WER 18.60%, UT-MOS 3.87, and MCD-DTW 5.60, outperforming all baselines on pronunciation and acoustic quality while maintaining competitive voice similarity (SECS 89.03%). In zero-shot evaluation with out-of-domain reference audio, the method reaches WER 9.85% and UT-MOS 3.63, substantially ahead of all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all seven baselines on pronunciation accuracy (WER) and acoustic quality (UT-MOS) across both benchmarks. On V2C-Animation Dub 1.0, it reduces WER from 17.51% (Speaker2Dubber, the closest two-stage competitor) to 8.04% and lifts UT-MOS from 2.41 to 3.10, a gap of 0.69 MOS points. It also achieves the best emotion accuracy (48.93% vs. 46.80% for Speaker2Dubber) and the lowest MCD-DTW (9.29 vs. 9.37 for StyleDubber), confirming superior prosody alignment. The one metric where it trails is speaker similarity (SECS): StyleDubber scores 82.26% and Speaker2Dubber 81.50%, versus 75.46% for the proposed method, though subjective MOS-S ratings (3.99 vs. 3.90 for StyleDubber) suggest the objective gap is narrower in perceptual terms. On the GRID benchmark, the method wins on WER (18.60% vs. 18.88% for StyleDubber), UT-MOS (3.87 vs. 3.73), and MCD-DTW (5.60 vs. 5.61) while remaining competitive on SECS (89.03% vs. 93.79% for StyleDubber). In the zero-shot generalization test, the advantage widens dramatically: WER drops to 9.85% (vs. 16.05% for Speaker2Dubber) and UT-MOS reaches 3.63 (vs. 3.08), demonstrating that the prosody-enhanced pre-training confers strong robustness to unseen speakers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual Voice Cloning (V2C) line of work by building on StyleTTS's acoustic architecture (text encoder, style encoder, prosody extractor, AdaIN-based decoder) and StyleTTS 2's style diffusion for prosodic modeling. Its most direct predecessor is Speaker2Dubber, which introduced a two-stage strategy of pre-training a phoneme encoder on a TTS corpus before fine-tuning on dubbing data; this paper generalizes that by pre-training the entire acoustic system on a prosody-enhanced corpus and then freezing it during prosody adaptation. The key conceptual departure is the explicit acoustic-prosody disentanglement: rather than letting a single text encoder serve both acoustic and prosodic roles (as in V2C-Net, HPMDubbing, and StyleDubber), the paper introduces a separate Prosodic Text BERT Encoder and a Prosodic Style Diffusion module, so that prosody adaptation never degrades the pre-trained acoustic quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practical bottleneck in movie dubbing: small, noisy dubbing datasets that limit acoustic quality. The prosody-enhancement strategy (frequency-domain pitch shifting and duration stretching on a large TTS corpus) is a simple but effective way to bridge the prosodic domain gap between clean audiobook speech and exaggerated film performance, and the frozen-acoustic-system design offers a general recipe for adapting prosody without sacrificing quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-English dubbing, multi-speaker scenes with overlapping dialogue, and real-time inference given the diffusion-based prosodic style sampling. The reliance on ground-truth duration and prosody during pre-training, and on a pre-trained monotonic aligner, also limits end-to-end flexibility.
