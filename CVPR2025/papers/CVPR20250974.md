## Problem

In Incremental Face Forgery Detection (IFFD), naively fine-tuning a binary Real/Fake classifier on new forgery data causes catastrophic forgetting because different forgery types override each other in the shared latent space. Existing replay-based methods preserve only a few representative points (e.g., center and hard samples) rather than the global feature distribution, making them unable to prevent distribution overriding or to accumulate diverse forgery knowledge across tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250974/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 256×256 RGB face crops (preprocessed per DeepFakeBench) and produces a single real/fake binary score. Because the forgery task of an incoming image is unknown at inference time, the final prediction is the average of all task-specific linear classifiers applied to the shared backbone feature, rather than a single classifier output. A replay buffer of 500 samples per domain (real and fake) per task is maintained for incremental training.

## Method

The proposed SUR-LID framework achieves 'aligned feature isolation' by stacking task-wise latent distributions brick by brick. Sparse Uniform Replay (SUR) selects replay samples that are simultaneously uniform in magnitude and angularity around the domain centroid and stable under grid shuffling, yielding a sparse proxy of the full global distribution. The Latent-space Incremental Detector (LID) then applies a supervised contrastive isolation loss with distribution re-filling (latent mixup between SUR points and centroids) to keep each task's real/fake distribution spatially separated, preventing override. Finally, Incremental Decision Alignment (IDA) enforces angular consistency between the new task's linear classifier and the previous one, recursively aligning all task boundaries so that the accumulated isolated forgery information can be aggregated into a single binary decision at inference.

## Results

On Protocol 1 (Dataset Incremental: SDv21 → FF++ → DFDCP → CDF), SUR-LID achieves a final-task average AUC of 0.9315, outperforming the best baseline HDP (0.8752) by 5.6 points. On Protocol 2 (Forgery Type Incremental: Hybrid → FR → FS → EFS), SUR-LID reaches 0.9433 average AUC, surpassing the best baseline CoReD (0.8141) by 12.9 points. The advantage is most pronounced on Protocol 2, where existing IFFD methods degrade severely (e.g., DFIL drops to 0.7929, DER to 0.7049) due to distribution overriding when real images are shared across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUR-LID outperforms all six baselines on both protocols at the final task (T4). On Protocol 1, it achieves 0.9315 average AUC versus HDP (0.8752, +5.6), DFIL (0.8628, +6.9), CoReD (0.8606, +7.1), iCaRL (0.8154, +11.6), LwF (0.7972, +13.4), and DER (0.7539, +17.8). On Protocol 2, the gap widens dramatically: SUR-LID reaches 0.9433 versus CoReD (0.8141, +12.9), DFIL (0.7929, +15.0), HDP (0.7826, +16.1), LwF (0.8049, +13.8), DER (0.7049, +23.8), and iCaRL (0.6828, +26.1). The largest relative gains appear on Protocol 2, where existing IFFD methods suffer severe forgetting because shared real images cause forgery-irrelevant features to dominate and override task-specific forgery patterns. Notably, even the strongest IFFD-specific baselines (CoReD, DFIL, HDP) drop below 0.82 on Protocol 2, while SUR-LID maintains above 0.87 on every individual dataset, confirming that aligned feature isolation effectively prevents the distribution overriding that plagues prior replay-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incremental face forgery detection (IFFD) line initiated by CoReD, DFIL, HDP, and DMP, which all rely on replaying a small subset of prior-task data to combat catastrophic forgetting. It departs from these methods by shifting the focus from preserving a few representative points (center/hard samples, adversarial perturbations, mixed prototypes) to preserving and organizing the *global* feature distribution of each task, using a Sparse Uniform Replay strategy that samples for high-dimensional uniformity and shuffle-consistency stability. The detection side draws on supervised contrastive learning for feature isolation and introduces a novel incremental decision alignment mechanism that recursively aligns per-task linear classifiers, a design not present in prior IFFD or general incremental learning work (LwF, iCaRL, DER).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: as new forgery techniques (diffusion-based, GAN-based, swap/reenactment) emerge continuously, retraining a detector from scratch is infeasible, making incremental adaptation essential for real-world deployment. The paper's Protocol 2 benchmark—where real images are shared across tasks and only forgery types change—exposes a critical weakness in existing IFFD methods that had been masked by the more forgiving Protocol 1, and the 2024 datasets (SDv21, MCNet, BlendFace, StyleGAN3) keep the evaluation current. Open questions include whether the multi-classifier averaging at inference scales gracefully to many tasks, whether the 500-sample replay budget is sufficient for very diverse forgery families, and whether the aligned-isolation principle transfers to other incremental detection domains beyond faces.
