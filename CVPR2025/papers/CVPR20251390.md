## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks rely on static, single-turn tasks with predetermined correct answers, failing to capture the open-ended, multi-turn, and stylistically diverse nature of real-world user interactions with vision-language models. This gap makes it difficult to understand how users actually deploy VLMs, how response style influences perceived quality, and where models genuinely fail on complex visual reasoning.

## Input / Output

The platform accepts user-submitted text prompts (often paired with an uploaded RGB image) in 138 languages, routing image-containing conversations to VLMs and text-only ones to LLMs. In battle mode, users see two anonymous model responses side-by-side and cast a preference vote (win/tie/loss); in direct chat mode, they interact with a named model freely. The dataset outputs include raw conversation logs, per-battle preference labels, Bradley-Terry arena scores per model and per category, and a 500-prompt offline benchmark (VisionArena-Bench) that produces pairwise preference scores via a GPT-4o judge on a 5-point Likert scale with position swapping.

## Method

VisionArena collects 230K real conversations from the Chatbot Arena platform (Feb–Sep 2024) and applies a Bradley-Terry logistic-regression model to pairwise battle votes to compute arena scores, extended with style-feature vectors (response length, markdown usage, named-entity count) to decouple stylistic bias from model capability. For instruction tuning, 100K conversations are sampled from the top-performing VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Qwen2-VL-72B, Llama-3.2-90B-Vision) and used to finetune Llama-3.2-11B-Vision with a frozen vision encoder for 3 epochs. VisionArena-Bench is constructed by filtering to single-turn English prompts, embedding them with CLIP-ViT-B-32 (averaging image and text embeddings), reducing dimensions via UMAP, clustering with HDBSCAN, and uniformly sampling 500 prompts across clusters; evaluation uses GPT-4o as a judge comparing each model against a fixed GPT-4-Turbo anchor, with the judge prompted twice with swapped positions to mitigate position bias.

## Results

Fine-tuning Llama-3.2-11B-Vision on 100K VisionArena-Chat conversations (Llama-3.2-VisionArena) outperforms the same base model fine-tuned on 100K LLaVA-Instruct-158K samples across all five benchmarks: MME Perception (1437.0 vs. 1067.6), HallusionBench (45.2 vs. 38.7), MMMU (43.0 vs. 27.9), MMMU-Pro (0.27 vs. 0.12), and WV-Bench (56.9 vs. 10.4). Notably, Llama-3.2-VisionArena also surpasses the original Llama3.2-11B-V-Instruct (trained on 3M+ samples) on MME (345.4 vs. 327.5 cognition) and WV-Bench (56.9 vs. 47.2) despite using 30× less data. For the offline benchmark, VisionArena-Bench achieves 97.3% Spearman and 89.7% Kendall Tau correlation with the live Chatbot Arena VLM leaderboard, compared to 80.2% and 69.2% for WildVision-Bench (WV-Bench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama3.2-11B-V-Instruct (the author-reported base model trained on 3M+ samples), Llama-3.2-VisionArena wins on MME Cognition (345.4 vs. 327.5) and WV-Bench (56.9 vs. 47.2) while using 30× less training data, but trails on MMMU (43.0 vs. 50.7) and HallusionBench (45.2 vs. 48.6). Against Llama-3.2-Llava-Instruct (same 100K budget, LLaVA-Instruct-158K data), VisionArena wins decisively on every benchmark, with the largest gaps on WV-Bench (+46.5 points) and MMMU (+15.1 points). For the offline benchmark comparison, VisionArena-Bench substantially outperforms WildVision-Bench (WV-Bench) in predicting the live Chatbot Arena VLM leaderboard: Spearman correlation of 97.3% vs. 80.2% (+17.1 pp) and Kendall Tau of 89.7% vs. 69.2% (+20.5 pp) across 16 shared models, indicating VisionArena-Bench is a far more faithful proxy for human preference rankings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chatbot Arena paradigm from LLMs to VLMs and scales the WildVision crowdsourced evaluation framework by roughly 4–5× in conversations, models, and languages. It departs from static, single-turn VLM benchmarks (MMMU, DocVQA, MME) by capturing open-ended, multi-turn, real-world user interactions, and adapts the Arena-Hard/BenchBuilder pipeline [23] to construct an offline VLM preference benchmark. The Bradley-Terry ranking framework is standard, but the paper follows recent work in extending it with style-feature controls (response length, markdown formatting, entity specificity) to decouple stylistic bias from model capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VisionArena is the largest and most linguistically diverse VLM conversation dataset to date, and its instruction-tuning subset (100K real user conversations) outperforms the widely-used LLaVA-Instruct-158K by 17 points on MMMU and 46 points on WV-Bench, suggesting that authentic user data can substitute for large curated corpora. The finding that response length and formatting substantially inflate rankings in open-ended categories (captioning, humor) has broad implications for how the community interprets VLM leaderboards. Key limitations include underrepresentation of medical, geospatial, and visual-assistance domains, insufficient data in many of the 138 languages for stable rankings, and the absence of multi-image conversations—areas the authors flag for future releases.
