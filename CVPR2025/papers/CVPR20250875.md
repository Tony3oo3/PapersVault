## Problem

Existing bimodal PAR methods (VTB, PromptPAR) rely on static prompt templates that embed only coarse attribute labels, failing to capture the extensive intra-class attribute variability across pedestrian images. Moreover, these methods fuse visual and textual features via simple concatenation, preventing active bidirectional collaboration between modalities during attribute learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250875/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB pedestrian image as input and produces a binary prediction vector over M attribute labels (e.g., 41 attributes on Celeb-PAR, 61 on PETA). Textual input consists of three groups of fixed prompt templates (e.g., 'This pedestrian contains [attribute].') plus L learnable prompt tokens prepended to each label. Classification is performed by computing dot-product similarity between the global-average-pooled linguistic-related visual feature and the visual-guided linguistic feature, followed by a sigmoid, rather than projecting onto a label space via a linear layer.

## Method

EVSITP builds on CLIP's image and text encoders and introduces three modules. The Image-Conditional Dual-Prompt Initialization Module (IDIM) processes three groups of fixed prompt templates and a learnable prompt through within-group self-attention (capturing inter-attribute co-occurrence) and image-conditional cross-attention (using the visual feature to modulate linguistic embeddings per image). The Prompt Enhancement and Regularization Module (PERM) applies across-group self-attention to fuse corresponding attribute features across the three fixed-prompt groups, concatenates the result with the learnable-prompt feature, and enforces a regularization loss that keeps learned prompt embeddings close to their fixed-template counterparts to prevent overfitting. The Bimodal Mutual Interaction Module (BMIM) performs bidirectional cross-attention—visual-to-linguistic (VLII) and linguistic-to-visual (LVII)—augmented by a shared V-L token injected into both pathways, enabling each modality to enrich the other before final attribute scoring.

## Results

EVSITP achieves the best mean Accuracy (mA) across all four standard PAR benchmarks: 89.65% on PETA, 88.66% on PA100K, 86.10% on RAPv1, and 83.83% on RAPv2. On the most challenging dataset PA100K, it surpasses the previous SOTA PromptPAR by 1.19% in mA and 0.83% in F1. On the newly proposed Celeb-PAR dataset (34,186 images, 41 attributes, multi-season/multi-scenario), EVSITP reaches 78.61% mA and 80.40% F1, outperforming PromptPAR by 0.25% and 0.74% respectively. On zero-shot generalization splits (PETA_zs, RAP_zs), EVSITP leads by 2.67% and 3.06% in mA over the best bimodal baseline VTB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVSITP outperforms all 16 baselines on every dataset and metric reported. Against the closest bimodal competitor PromptPAR [37], EVSITP gains 0.89–1.19% in mA across the four standard benchmarks, with the largest margin on PA100K (88.66 vs. 87.47). Compared to the best unimodal method DAFL [20], the improvement is 2.58–5.12% in mA, confirming the benefit of integrating the linguistic modality. Against the other bimodal method VTB [4], EVSITP leads by 3.31–4.34% in mA, attributable to adaptive image-conditional prompts and bidirectional interaction versus VTB's static templates and simple concatenation. On the zero-shot generalization splits (PETA_zs, RAP_zs), EVSITP surpasses VTB by 2.70% and 3.06% in mA, and outperforms the best unimodal SOFAFormer [42] by 3.13% and 4.92%. On the new Celeb-PAR dataset, EVSITP beats PromptPAR on four of five metrics (mA, Accu, Prec, F1) while PromptPAR retains a slight edge on Recall (83.72 vs. 82.56); the mFive composite metric favors EVSITP by 0.48%. All unimodal baselines (PGDM, GRL, VRKD, ALM, SSC, IAA-Caps, FEMDAR, EALC, AttExpIB-Net, Label2Label, RethinkingPAR, ADFL) are consistently outperformed by 1–6% in mA depending on the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bimodal PAR line initiated by VTB and PromptPAR, which first brought CLIP-based text prompts into pedestrian attribute recognition. It departs from their static, attribute-name-only prompt templates by introducing an image-conditional dual-prompt mechanism (fixed templates plus learnable tokens) and replacing the simple feature concatenation with a bidirectional cross-attention interaction module. The approach is conceptually aligned with conditional prompt learning in vision-language models (CoCoOp) but is adapted to the multi-label PAR setting with a more parameter-efficient design that reuses a single set of learnable prompts across all image-text pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The newly introduced Celeb-PAR dataset addresses a real gap: all prior PAR benchmarks were collected in limited scenes or seasons, making it difficult to assess robustness to clothing changes across seasons and environments. The dataset, built from a long-term ReID corpus with non-overlapping IDs and multi-season coverage, provides a more realistic evaluation protocol. However, the methodological contributions (dual prompts, cross-attention interaction) are incremental extensions of well-known techniques, and the performance gains over PromptPAR are modest (0.25–1.19% in mA). Open questions include whether the image-conditional prompting strategy transfers to other fine-grained multi-label recognition tasks and whether the ReID-derived annotation process introduces systematic biases in attribute distributions.
