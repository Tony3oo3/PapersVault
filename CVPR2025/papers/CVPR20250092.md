## Problem

Existing methods to improve ViT robustness—patch augmentation, contrastive learning, structural modifications—require extensive retraining or fine-tuning on large datasets, making them costly and impractical for large-scale architectures. Additionally, the self-attention maps in ViTs are highly sensitive to input corruption, degrading robustness. The paper asks whether spatial relationships among token features can be exploited in a training-free manner to simultaneously boost accuracy, robustness, and efficiency of pre-trained ViTs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250092/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 224×224 RGB images processed by pre-trained ViT backbones (DeiT-Tiny/16, DeiT-Small/16, DeiT-Base/16, ViT-Base/16) and produces standard class labels. The SATA module is inserted between the multi-head self-attention and FFN sub-layers within transformer blocks, acting on the N×d token embedding tensor. It is applied only from block ⌈γ×B⌉ onward (γ=0.7 by default), leaving early blocks untouched, and requires no retraining or architectural modification beyond the plug-in module.

## Method

SATA computes a local Moran's I spatial autocorrelation score for each token using the self-attention map as the spatial weight matrix, then splits tokens into two sets: set B (scores within α(μ_s ± |ŝ|) bounds) and set A (extreme scores beyond those bounds). Set A tokens are merged via a bipartite-matching-inspired algorithm that pairs each token in A₁ with its most similar counterpart in A₂, averaging their features to produce merged tokens while retaining residual tokens. Only set B and merged tokens are fed into the FFN, reducing computational load, and residual tokens are concatenated with the FFN output to restore the original token count N for subsequent blocks. This design prevents information loss (unlike token pruning) while filtering out non-informative tokens with extreme spatial autocorrelation that impede recognition and robustness in later layers.

## Results

SATA-B* (SATA integrated into pre-trained ViT-Base/16) achieves a new state-of-the-art 94.9% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K without any additional training or fine-tuning, surpassing the previous best ViT (TransNeXt at 84.8%) by over 10 points. On robustness benchmarks, SATA-B* sets new records: 13.6 mCE on ImageNet-C (vs. 43.5 for TransNeXt), 63.6% top-1 on ImageNet-A, 79.2% on ImageNet-R, and 57.9% on ImageNet-Sketch. The method also reduces computational cost, with SATA-B requiring only 15.9 GFLOPs versus 17.6 for the DeiT-Base baseline. Across all three model sizes (Tiny, Small, Base), SATA consistently improves both accuracy and robustness over the corresponding DeiT baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SATA-B* dominates all ViT-Base baselines across every reported metric. On ImageNet-1K top-1, it achieves 94.9%, exceeding TransNeXt (84.8%) by 10.1 points, TORA-ViT (84.1%) by 10.8 points, and the DeiT-B baseline (82.0%) by 12.9 points. On ImageNet-C (mCE), SATA-B* records 13.6, a 30-point improvement over TORA-ViT (31.7) and a 34.9-point improvement over DeiT-B (48.5). On natural adversarial robustness (ImageNet-A), SATA-B* reaches 63.6% versus 50.6% for TransNeXt and 27.4% for DeiT-B. On out-of-distribution ImageNet-R, SATA-B* achieves 79.2% compared to 57.6% for TORA-ViT and 44.9% for DeiT-B. Notably, SATA-B* also outperforms the much larger TORA-ViT (26.0 GFLOPs, 111.2M params) while using fewer FLOPs (15.9) and parameters (86.6M), and it does so without any retraining or fine-tuning, unlike all competing methods which require extensive additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial autocorrelation analysis introduced by Nikzad et al. [36] (CSA-Net) from CNN feature maps to ViT token embeddings, applying Moran's I — a metric from geographical statistics — for the first time to quantify spatial dependency among transformer tokens. It departs from the dominant paradigm of ViT robustness enhancement (RVT, FAN, RSPC, TORAViT), which relies on retraining or fine-tuning, by proposing a training-free, plug-and-play module inserted between attention and FFN. The token merging step is inspired by Bolya et al.'s [3] bipartite matching approach but differs in using spatial autocorrelation scores (rather than feature similarity) for grouping and in preserving residual tokens to avoid information loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the reported gains are reproducible, a training-free module that simultaneously boosts ImageNet-1K accuracy by ~12 points and cuts ImageNet-C mCE by over 30 points would be transformative for ViT deployment, eliminating the need for expensive retraining to improve robustness. The conceptual finding that spatial autocorrelation scores remain stable under corruption while attention maps degrade sharply offers a new lens for understanding ViT vulnerability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to window-based or hybrid ViTs, dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation), and whether the extraordinary magnitude of improvement holds under independent replication and across more diverse architectures.
