## Problem

Learning-based Structure-from-Motion methods assume a pinhole camera model and fail on distorted videos from wide-angle or fisheye lenses, leaving COLMAP as the default for calibration and rectification. Moreover, in dynamic scenes, naively learning dense motion alongside unknown distortion creates an ambiguity where motion estimates compensate for undistortion errors rather than capturing true scene dynamics, degrading both calibration and reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251270/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AnyMap consumes a video sequence of distorted frames (H×W×3) together with off-the-shelf pairwise optical flow (RAFT) and 2D point tracks (CoTracker V1) as correspondence supervision, plus monocular depth estimates (Depth Pro) for scale anchoring. It produces per-frame range maps (not depth, to avoid numerical instability near 180° FOV), SE(3) camera poses, and a learned bidirectional (un)projection function parameterized by the INN. Downstream, the learned model yields rectified images, 3D point clouds, and optionally fitted parametric camera calibration (focal length + distortion coefficients) via least-squares matching to a user-specified model.

## Method

The camera model is an invertible neural network (ResFlow, 3 nonlinear layers of dim 8) that learns a bidirectional mapping between image points in polar coordinates and incoming light-ray directions on the unit sphere, eliminating predefined parametric distortion models and their optimization ambiguities. Scene geometry is a MiDAS-based range network; camera poses are estimated per frame pair via differentiable Procrustes alignment of unprojected point clouds; and the induced optical flow from reprojection is supervised against known correspondences with a Huber loss. To handle dynamic scenes, a low-dimensional motion representation uses B=10 learnable SE(3) basis trajectories combined via per-pixel softmax weights predicted by an MLP from world coordinates and time, regularizing motion so it does not absorb distortion errors. The full system is optimized per-video via gradient descent (up to 8000 epochs) with a combined loss of reprojection error, a motion-weighted ordinal monocular depth loss, and a periodic unprojection regularization loss that fits the learned π⁻¹ to a parametric model to prevent divergence in texture-less regions.

## Results

On camera calibration and rectification (Table 1), AnyMap achieves PSNR 28.15 / Re 1.02 / Δf 0.65 on MipNeRF-360, surpassing COLMAP MVS (22.85 / 1.44 / 0.80) by 5.3 dB in PSNR and 29% in reprojection error. On pose estimation and novel view synthesis (Table 2), AnyMap attains ATE 0.0022 on MipNeRF-360 and 0.0092 on Tanks & Temples, outperforming COLMAP MVS (0.0023 and 0.0142) and DROID-SLAM (0.0428 and 0.0943), while producing 3DGS PSNR of 32.08 on MipNeRF-360. On dynamic scenes (Table 3), AnyMap with its low-dimensional motion representation achieves PSNR 22.54, Re_px 3.20, and ATE 0.106 on DAVIS, substantially outperforming AnyMap+CVD (17.05 / 8.21 / 0.130) and CasualSAM+CN (16.78 / 8.47 / 0.137).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyMap outperforms all monocular baselines (Auto-DC, PCN, GeoCalib, Porfiri et al.) by large margins on both calibration (Re, Δf) and rectification (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all four datasets; for example, on MipNeRF-360 AnyMap achieves PSNR 28.15 vs. 21.09 for Porfiri et al. and 16.20 for GeoCalib. Against the strongest multi-view baselines, AnyMap beats COLMAP MVS by 5.3 dB PSNR and 29% lower Re on MipNeRF-360, and by 2.7 dB PSNR and 37% lower Re on AEA (real fisheye), while also improving Δf from 0.80 to 0.65. In pose estimation (Table 2), AnyMap achieves the lowest ATE on MipNeRF-360 (0.0022 vs. 0.0023 for COLMAP MVS) and Tanks & Temples (0.0092 vs. 0.0142), and produces the best 3DGS PSNR (32.08) on MipNeRF-360; DROID-SLAM and MASt3R are substantially worse on ATE (0.0428 and 0.0612 respectively on MipNeRF-360). On dynamic scenes (Table 3), AnyMap with its low-dimensional motion representation dominates: it achieves PSNR 22.54 and Re_px 3.20, outperforming AnyMap+CVD (17.05 / 8.21), CasualSAM+CN (16.78 / 8.47), and even CasualSAM and MonST3R evaluated on undistorted images (ATE 0.130 and 0.121 vs. AnyMap's 0.106). The only metric where AnyMap does not lead is runtime: it is ~2.8× slower than COLMAP and ~56% slower than CasualSAM, though it is 3.1× faster than COLMAP MVS.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyMap extends the FlowMap/FlowCam line of per-video differentiable SfM by replacing the pinhole camera assumption with a model-free invertible neural network (INN) that learns bidirectional (un)projection functions, thereby supporting arbitrary radial and tangential lens distortions. The camera model conceptually builds on the viewing-angle mapping of Porfiri et al. [13] but generalizes it from radial-only, unprojection-only to a full bidirectional INN handling both distortion types. For dynamic scenes, the paper introduces a low-dimensional basis-trajectory motion representation inspired by 3D Gaussian Splatting for dynamic scenes [56], which regularizes motion estimation to prevent it from absorbing undistortion errors—a failure mode absent in static-scene methods like FlowMap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnyMap is, to the authors' knowledge, the first learning-based SfM framework that jointly handles unknown lens distortion and dynamic scene motion, unifying calibration, rectification, pose estimation, and novel view synthesis in a single differentiable pipeline. This removes the reliance on COLMAP for distorted-video preprocessing and opens the door to self-supervised monodepth and general NVS in the presence of distortion. Key limitations include per-video optimization cost (~2.8× COLMAP runtime), dependence on video sequences (no single-image support), and sensitivity to monodepth quality in motion-dominated scenes. Open questions include whether the INN camera model generalizes across radically different lens geometries without re-optimization, and whether the low-dimensional motion prior scales to scenes with many independent moving objects.
