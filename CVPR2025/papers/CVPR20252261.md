## Problem

Hierarchical data (e.g., nested image patches, spectral band groupings, multi-level features) is routinely flattened by standard decompositions like SVD or PCA, discarding the nested subspace structure that encodes meaningful relationships. While flag manifolds provide the right geometric language for nested subspaces, no general algorithm existed to factorize an arbitrary hierarchical data matrix into a hierarchy-preserving flag representation; SVD and QR can only recover flags for very restricted column hierarchies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix D ∈ R^{n×p} together with a column hierarchy (nested index sets A₁ ⊂ A₂ ⊂ … ⊂ Aₖ specifying which columns belong to each level) and a flag type (n₁,…,nₖ; n) with nₖ ≤ p, and produces a factorization D = QRP^T where Q is a block-structured orthonormal matrix whose blocks Q₁,…,Qₖ are Stiefel coordinates for the hierarchy-preserving flag, R is block upper-triangular, and P is a permutation matrix reordering columns by hierarchy level. In practice the algorithm receives a corrupted D̃ and a known hierarchy, and outputs a reduced-rank reconstruction D̂ = QRP^T, flag representations for downstream clustering, or flag prototypes for few-shot classification. The flag type must be specified a priori or estimated via singular-value truncation, and the decomposition is unique only up to block rotations within each Qᵢ.

## Method

The core contribution is the Flag Decomposition (FD), a matrix factorization D = QRP^T where Q = [Q₁|Q₂|…|Qₖ] has orthonormal blocks of sizes mᵢ = nᵢ − nᵢ₋₁, R is block upper-triangular, and P permutes columns by hierarchy level; the key constraint is the projection property Π_{Qᵢ⊥}…Π_{Q₁⊥}Bᵢ = 0, which guarantees that each flag level exactly spans the corresponding column subspace. The Flag-BMGS algorithm computes this decomposition iteratively: at step i it projects the i-th block Bᵢ onto the null space of all previously recovered subspaces, then solves a subspace recovery problem (Eq. 14) via truncated SVD (r=q=2) or an IRLS-SVD variant (r=2, q=1) for outlier robustness, and fills in the corresponding R blocks. Theoretical results establish existence (Prop. 1), explicit construction of R and P (Prop. 2), a necessary-and-sufficient condition for FD to exist (Prop. 3), and block-rotational uniqueness (Prop. 4). For few-shot learning, the method embeds s shots of a class into a flag prototype via FD and classifies queries by a sum of squared projection distances onto each flag level, making the entire pipeline differentiable.

## Results

In few-shot learning (5-way, 100 tasks, 10 queries), the Flag classifier achieves 77.7% / 81.8% / 83.9% accuracy on EuroSat and 90.2% / 93.2% / 94.5% on Flowers102 for 3/5/7 shots, consistently matching or exceeding the subspace baseline and outperforming the Euclidean (prototypical) baseline by 1–2 points. In hyperspectral denoising (KSC, Indian Pines), FD reduces LRSE relative to SVD across all SNR levels tested. In simulated reconstruction under additive noise and outlier contamination, RFD (robust FD) achieves the lowest chordal distance to the true flag and the best inlier reconstruction error compared to SVD and IRLS-SVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Flag classifier against two standard few-shot prototypes: Euclidean (prototypical networks [51]) and Subspace (adaptive subspace networks [50]). The Flag classifier consistently outperforms the Euclidean baseline across nearly all settings, with gains of 1.0–2.0 points (e.g., 90.2% vs. 88.2% on Flowers102 at 3-shot; 83.9% vs. 82.6% on EuroSat at 7-shot). Against the Subspace baseline, the Flag classifier ties or marginally wins in most cases (e.g., 77.7% vs. 77.6% on EuroSat at 3-shot; 83.9% vs. 83.8% on EuroSat at 7-shot), with the sole exception being CIFAR-10 at 7-shot where Euclidean (68.6%) slightly edges out both Flag (68.0%) and Subspace (68.1%). In the denoising and clustering experiments, FD outperforms both SVD and QR as flag-extraction baselines, with RFD providing the most robust flag recovery under outlier contamination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flag manifold literature (Ye et al. [60], Mankovich & Birdal [33], Mankovich et al. [34, 36]) by introducing a matrix decomposition specifically designed to recover hierarchy-preserving flags from data with arbitrary nested column hierarchies. The key departure from prior work is that SVD and QR, the standard tools for flag extraction, provably fail to recover the full hierarchy-preserving flag for general column hierarchies (Fig. 2), motivating the new Flag Decomposition. Algorithmically, Flag-BMGS adapts the Block Modified Gram-Schmidt procedure [3, 19] to operate on hierarchically structured blocks rather than a single matrix, and the few-shot learning component builds on prototypical networks [51] and adaptive subspace classifiers [50] by replacing point or subspace prototypes with flag prototypes and chordal flag distances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FD provides the first general-purpose decomposition that preserves arbitrary column hierarchies in data, addressing a structural limitation of SVD and QR that the paper demonstrates concretely. This opens a principled route for hierarchy-aware denoising, clustering, and few-shot classification without flattening the data. However, the method requires both the column hierarchy and the flag type as inputs, is susceptible to Gram-Schmidt numerical instabilities, and the empirical gains over SVD/QR baselines are modest (a few percentage points in classification, small LRSE improvements in denoising). Open questions include automated flag type detection, learning hierarchical structures directly from data, and whether the framework scales to very deep or high-dimensional hierarchies.
