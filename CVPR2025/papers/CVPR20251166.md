## Problem

Existing real object-centric 3D datasets fail to balance quality and scale: high-fidelity scanned datasets like OmniObject3D and GSO contain only a few thousand objects, while larger datasets like CO3Dv2 and MVImgNet (40k–220k objects) rely on less accurate COLMAP SfM annotations, partial object coverage, and contain a non-trivial fraction of low-quality videos. No publicly available dataset simultaneously offers full 360° coverage, accurate 3D annotations, broad category diversity, and sufficient scale for training large 3D generative models.

## Input / Output

The dataset ingests crowd-sourced 360° turn-table videos (60%+ at 1080p or higher) captured along sine-wave trajectories to ensure varying camera elevations, and outputs per-scene VGGSfM camera intrinsics/extrinsics for 200 uniformly sampled frames, sparse and semi-dense point clouds, a per-scene 3D Gaussian Splat reconstruction rigidly aligned to a canonical object-centric reference, temporally stable segmentation masks, and a single scene-level text caption. For downstream applications, LightplaneLRM consumes 4 source images and renders a triplane representation to novel views; CAT3D-like takes 3 known views plus a target camera pose and generates a novel RGB view; the text-to-3D pipeline takes a text caption and produces a 4-view grid that is reconstructed into a 3D asset.

## Method

The dataset construction pipeline uses VGGSfM (rather than COLMAP) for camera and point-cloud estimation, refines per-frame LangSAM segmentations with an XMem-based video segmenter for temporal stability, fits a 3D Gaussian Splat per scene via gsplat, and aligns all scenes to a shared canonical frame (horizontal ground plane, similar scale, centring, orientation). Captions are generated by first describing each view with a vision-language model and then summarising with LLaMA-3. Validation combines active-learning camera quality checks with novel-view synthesis accuracy from the 3DGS reconstruction, and each video is manually verified for full 360° coverage. For the text-to-3D application, the 3DGS reconstructions are re-rendered from four canonical orthogonal viewpoints to create a captioned 4-view grid dataset, enabling an Instant3D-like two-stage pipeline (text-to-4-view diffusion + LightplaneLRM feedforward reconstruction) trained entirely on real data rather than synthetic renders.

## Results

For few-view 3D reconstruction (LightplaneLRM), uCO3D achieves PSNR 24.61 and IoU 0.946 on OmniObject3D, outperforming CO3Dv2 (23.62, 0.926) and MVImgNet (23.39, 0.928). For novel-view synthesis (CAT3D-like), uCO3D-trained models achieve the best LPIPS and PSNR across all four evaluation datasets (RealEstate10K, LLFF, DTU, Mip-NeRF 360), with PSNR gains of 3–4 points over MVImgNet. For text-to-3D generation, the uCO3D-trained Instant3D-like model achieves Real-FID 63.9 versus 82.8 for the synthetic-data baseline, confirming more realistic outputs on real-object prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training datasets (uCO3D, CO3Dv2, MVImgNet) across three downstream tasks. On few-view 3D reconstruction (LightplaneLRM), uCO3D wins on PSNR and IoU for OmniObject3D (24.61/0.946 vs. 23.62/0.926 for CO3Dv2 and 23.39/0.928 for MVImgNet) and matches or slightly beats CO3Dv2 on StanfordORB (PSNR 25.715 vs. 25.617). On novel-view synthesis (CAT3D-like), uCO3D achieves the best LPIPS on all four test sets and the best PSNR on three of four (LLFF, DTU, Mip-NeRF 360), with a notable 4.18 PSNR gain over MVImgNet on DTU (16.97 vs. 12.79). On text-to-3D, the uCO3D-trained generator substantially outperforms the synthetic (Objaverse-like) baseline on real prompts (Real-FID 63.9 vs. 82.8) but underperforms on surreal prompts (Surreal-FID 68.9 vs. 42.3), indicating the synthetic data retains an advantage for out-of-distribution, fantastical objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

uCO3D extends the CO3D/CO3Dv2 line of crowdsourced 360° object videos by replacing COLMAP with the more accurate VGGSfM for camera and point-cloud estimation, adding per-scene 3D Gaussian Splat reconstructions and textual captions, and expanding category coverage from 50 (MS COCO) to 1,070 (LVIS taxonomy). It also departs from MVImgNet's partial-view captures by enforcing full turn-table coverage via a sine-wave camera trajectory and manual per-video verification. For downstream applications, the paper reuses existing architectures (LightplaneLRM, a CAT3D-like diffusion model, and an Instant3D-style two-stage pipeline) without architectural novelty, positioning the contribution squarely as a data-quality and diversity improvement rather than a methodological one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

uCO3D is likely to become a default training and evaluation resource for real-object 3D learning, given its 170k scenes, 1,070 categories, and the inclusion of 3DGS reconstructions that enable canonical-view 're-shooting'—a trick that, for the first time, allows Instant3D-style text-to-3D pipelines to be trained on purely real data rather than synthetic renders. The consistent (if modest) gains across LightplaneLRM, CAT3D-like NVS, and text-to-3D FID suggest that annotation quality and category diversity matter as much as raw scale. Open questions include whether the VGGSfM-based annotations are accurate enough for geometry-sensitive tasks (e.g., mesh extraction), how the dataset performs for models requiring dense per-pixel depth, and whether the 3DGS reconstructions generalize to objects with highly reflective or transparent surfaces.
