## Problem

Optimizing discrete text prompts for T2I diffusion models is difficult because the discrete token space prevents direct gradient descent and the combinatorial search space is intractable (e.g., 49152^10 candidates for a 10-token prompt). Existing approaches either introduce quantization errors by optimizing in continuous embedding space before projecting to discrete tokens, or fall into local optima via coordinate-wise greedy search, failing to explore the global prompt space effectively.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image or text (encoded to a fixed-dimensional embedding via a pre-trained CLIP encoder) and iteratively refines a discrete prompt of configurable length (5, 10, or 15 tokens) drawn from a vocabulary set. The output is an optimized discrete token sequence that maximizes cosine similarity with the target embedding, which can then be fed into any T2I diffusion model (SD v2, SDXL v1.0) for image generation. Notably, the optimization operates entirely in the discrete token space without any continuous relaxation, and the learned prompts transfer across different diffusion model architectures.

## Method

STEPS reformulates discrete prompt search as a sequential probability tensor estimation problem, representing the joint probability distribution over prompt configurations using a Tensor Train (TT) decomposition that exploits the empirically observed low-rank structure of the loss landscape tensor, reducing parameter complexity from exponential O(k^d) to linear O(dkR^2). At each iteration, a first-order Taylor expansion around the current best prompt identifies top-k candidate tokens per position, which are incrementally merged into per-position vocabulary sets; a memory-bounded resampling step (retaining only l entries from the previous distribution) keeps the search space at O((k+l)^d) independent of iteration count. The TT cores are trained via negative log-likelihood on the n minimal-loss prompts sampled from the current distribution, updated with Adam and a nonnegativity projection, while new candidate prompts are drawn by iterative conditional sampling within the TT framework. This design avoids both quantization errors (no continuous relaxation) and local-optima trapping (global probabilistic exploration over the full prompt space at each step).

## Results

On SD v2 image generation evaluated by cosine similarity, STEPS achieves 0.6720 on MS COCO and 0.6775 on Flick30k with 15-token prompts, outperforming PEZ (0.6356/0.6179) and MMA (0.6485/0.6288). The advantage grows with prompt length: at 10 tokens, STEPS reaches 0.6657 on MS COCO versus 0.6130 (PEZ) and 0.6154 (MMA). Cross-model transfer to SDXL v1.0 preserves the ranking, with STEPS scoring 0.6245 on MS COCO (15 tokens) versus 0.5641 (PEZ) and 0.5709 (MMA). In NSFW prompt optimization, STEPS achieves the highest average attack success rate of 78.50% on SD v2 (black-box), surpassing MMA (72.71%) and Genetic (71.15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STEPS consistently outperforms both PEZ [41] and MMA [42] across all six datasets and all three prompt lengths (5, 10, 15 tokens) on SD v2 cosine similarity. The margin over PEZ widens substantially with prompt length: at 15 tokens, STEPS gains +0.036 on MS COCO (0.6720 vs 0.6356) and +0.048 on Flick30k (0.6775 vs 0.6179), while at 5 tokens the gap is only +0.019 and +0.014 respectively. Against MMA, STEPS leads by +0.024 on MS COCO and +0.048 on Celeb-A at 15 tokens, though MMA occasionally matches or slightly exceeds STEPS on individual datasets at shorter lengths (e.g., MMA scores 0.6398 vs STEPS 0.6534 on Flick30k at 10 tokens, where MMA actually trails). In the NSFW attack setting (Table 3), STEPS achieves the highest average ASR of 56.21% on SD v1.5 (white-box) and 78.50% on SD v2 (black-box), beating MMA (49.73% / 72.71%) and Genetic [45] (51.38% / 71.15%), while the Original prompts [33] from SLD remain the weakest baseline at 23.44% / 54.61%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established line of discrete prompt optimization for T2I diffusion models, departing from two dominant paradigms: continuous embedding optimization with discrete projection (PEZ) and coordinate-wise greedy token search (MMA). The key conceptual move is importing Tensor Train (TT) decomposition—a tool well-developed in computational mathematics and probabilistic modeling—into the prompt optimization setting, reformulating the combinatorial search as a sequential probability tensor estimation problem. The first-order Taylor expansion on one-hot tokens (from AutoPrompt-style methods) is retained as the mechanism for incrementally expanding the vocabulary, but the global search dynamics are governed by a low-rank probability distribution over the joint prompt space rather than per-coordinate updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that the loss landscape tensor exhibits strong low-rank structure provides a principled justification for why tensor-based parameterization should work, and the memory-bounded strategy makes the approach tractable for practical prompt lengths. Cross-model transferability to SDXL suggests the learned prompts capture semantic structure rather than model-specific artifacts. However, the method still requires access to a CLIP text encoder during optimization, and the NSFW results—while framed as a security analysis—highlight that the same optimization machinery can be repurposed to circumvent content filters, raising open questions about robustness of current defense mechanisms against tensor-aware adversarial prompt search.
