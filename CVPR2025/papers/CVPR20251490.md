## Problem

Vision-based GUI agents offer cross-platform compatibility and low latency but suffer from poor element grounding accuracy, since current VLMs struggle to locate specific GUI elements (buttons, text boxes) from raw screenshots. Existing remedies either bolt on external OCR/icon-recognition tools (adding latency and complexity) or depend on expensive manual annotation. Additionally, the dynamic high-resolution block strategy used by modern VLMs introduces a location ambiguity problem where identical cropped blocks from differently-shaped images map to different global positions, degrading grounding during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251490/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent takes a single GUI screenshot at dynamic high resolution (resized to match a predefined aspect ratio, then divided into 448×448 pixel blocks) together with a natural-language task instruction. It outputs the next action (e.g., click, type, scroll) along with the target element's location. Under UBP, the model predicts block-relative coordinates [b_i, x′, y′] rather than global pixel coordinates; the global position is recovered in a lightweight post-processing step using the known block grid dimensions, making the learned mapping injective and unambiguous.

## Method

SpiritSight is built on InternVL2 (2B/8B/26B) and trained in two stages: continual pre-training on the newly constructed GUI-Lasagne dataset (5.73M samples across three levels—visual-text alignment, vision-function alignment via back-translation with InternVL, and CoT-style GUI navigation from cleaned Android-in-the-Wild data) followed by task-specific fine-tuning. The key architectural contribution is Universal Block Parsing (UBP), which replaces the global coordinate target with a block-indexed coordinate representation and adds 2D Block-wise Position Embeddings to each block, converting the inherently multi-valued global-to-block mapping into an injective one and eliminating the location ambiguity introduced by flattening differently-shaped block grids. This grounding-focused design, combined with the large-scale multi-level pre-training data, enables SpiritSight to outperform prior vision-based, language-based, and vision-language-based agents on six GUI navigation benchmarks without requiring candidate elements or auxiliary OCR/icon tools.

## Results

On Multimodal-Mind2Web (non-candidate setting), SpiritSight-26B achieves 54.7% Step SR on Cross-Task, 48.1% on Cross-Website, and 49.2% on Cross-Domain, outperforming the previous best non-candidate method OmniParser (39.4%/36.5%/42.0%) by 15.3/11.6/7.2 points respectively. On ScreenSpot, SpiritSight-26B reaches 74.4% (Web), 73.6% (Mobile), and 69.9% (Desktop), surpassing SeeClick (44.1%/65.0%/51.1%) by up to 30.3 points. Across four cross-platform benchmarks (GUI-Odyssey, AMEX, AndroidControl, GUIAct), SpiritSight-26B sets new SOTA on all six sub-tasks, e.g., 82.1% on AMEX-High vs. 70.7% previous SOTA and 88.2% on AndroidControl-Low vs. 80.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among non-candidate vision-based methods, SpiritSight-26B dominates: it beats OmniParser (39.4% → 54.7% Cross-Task Step SR, +15.3 pts), SeeAct (40.2% → 54.7%, +14.5 pts), and SeeClick (25.5% → 54.7%, +29.2 pts) on Multimodal-Mind2Web. On ScreenSpot, SpiritSight-26B (74.4/73.6/69.9%) outperforms SeeClick (44.1/65.0/51.1%) by 30.3/8.6/18.8 points on Web/Mobile/Desktop, and CogAgent (49.5/45.5/47.1%) by 24.9/28.1/22.8 points. On cross-platform benchmarks, SpiritSight-26B surpasses the previous SOTA on all six sub-tasks: GUI-Odyssey (75.9% vs. 74.3%), AMEX-High (82.1% vs. 70.7%), AndroidControl-High (69.6% vs. 64.8%), AndroidControl-Low (88.2% vs. 80.0%), GUIAct-Multi (52.6% vs. 45.4%), and GUIAct-Single (78.3% vs. 74.9%). The 2B variant already exceeds OmniParser and SeeClick on all three Multimodal-Mind2Web splits, demonstrating strong data efficiency. Language-based methods with top-50 candidates (AutoWebGLM 66.4%, HTML-T5-XL 71.5%) still lead in absolute Step SR, but SpiritSight-26B narrows the gap to within 6.8 points of AutoWebGLM without any candidate assistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpiritSight builds directly on InternVL2's dynamic high-resolution VLM architecture, but identifies and resolves a previously unacknowledged positional ambiguity introduced by its block-flattening strategy through the Universal Block Parsing (UBP) method. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant language-based and vision-language-based GUI agent paradigm (Mind2Web, WebAgent, SeeAct) by committing to a purely vision-based, end-to-end pipeline, and from prior vision-based agents (SeeClick, CogAgent, MobileAgent) by replacing auxiliary OCR/icon-recognition tools with a large-scale, multi-level grounding dataset (GUI-Lasagne) and a principled coordinate-representation fix. The dataset construction borrows the back-translation idea from NMT and leverages LLM-based data cleaning, while the 2D-BPE component is adapted from UReader.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UBP technique is a general, low-cost fix for any VLM that uses dynamic high-resolution block processing, making it broadly applicable beyond GUI tasks. The 5.73M-sample GUI-Lasagne dataset, with 90% collected for free, substantially lowers the barrier to training capable GUI agents and offers a reusable resource for the community. Key open questions include whether the grounding gains transfer to other high-resolution VLM tasks (document understanding, chart reading), whether the approach handles increasingly complex or animated GUIs, and how to mitigate the inherent privacy risk of continuous screenshot access that the authors acknowledge but do not address technically.
