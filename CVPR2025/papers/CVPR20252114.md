## Problem

Recovering surface normals and PBR materials from a single image is fundamentally ill-posed, as many geometry-material-lighting combinations produce identical appearances. Existing feed-forward regression methods struggle with this ambiguity, while photometric stereo techniques that resolve it require expensive multi-light capture rigs. The paper asks whether a diffusion model can synthesize the missing multi-lighting observations in silico, thereby converting an under-constrained single-image problem into a better-conditioned multi-image estimation task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces an 8-channel G-buffer: 3-channel surface normal, 3-channel albedo, 1-channel roughness, and 1-channel metallic maps, all pixel-aligned to the input. Internally, the multi-light diffusion model first generates 9 relit images arranged in a 3×3 grid, each illuminated by a point light at a known spherical coordinate (θ, φ) relative to the object. The G-buffer U-Net then consumes the original image concatenated with these 9 relit images plus their light-source poses to produce the final surface property maps.

## Method

Neural LightRig is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v2.1 into a multi-light diffusion model that generates 9 consistent relit images in a single 3×3 grid, using a hybrid conditioning strategy that combines channel-wise concatenation of the input with reference attention (where denoising-stream queries attend to input-image keys/values) to preserve texture and color fidelity. Training follows a two-phase schedule: first freezing all but the first convolution and attention layers, then unfreezing the full model at a lower learning rate. Stage 2 is a large U-Net regression model that takes the input image, the 9 generated multi-light images, and their spherical light poses as channel-concatenated input and predicts the 8-channel G-buffer, trained with cosine-similarity loss on normals and MSE on PBR channels. To close the domain gap between Blender-rendered training images and diffusion-generated inference images, the G-buffer model is trained with augmentations including random degradation, HSV intensity perturbation, light-pose jittering, and mixing of diffusion-generated samples into the training set. The entire system is trained on LightProp, a synthetic dataset of 80k Objaverse objects rendered in Blender with 5 views each under multiple lighting conditions.

## Results

On the LightProp held-out set (1,000 unseen objects), Neural LightRig achieves a mean angular error of 6.41° for surface normal estimation, outperforming the best baseline StableNormal (8.03°) by 20%. For PBR material estimation, it reaches 26.62 dB PSNR on albedo and 26.23 dB on metallic, surpassing IntrinsicAnything (23.88 / 22.00 dB) by ~2.7 dB. On single-image relighting, it attains 30.12 dB PSNR and 0.0371 LPIPS with a 5-second average latency, compared to IntrinsicAnything's 27.98 dB PSNR at 2 minutes per frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neural LightRig outperforms all nine baselines across every reported metric. On normal estimation, the closest competitor is StableNormal (8.03° mean error), which Neural LightRig beats by 1.62° in mean and 1.67° in median; the gap widens dramatically at the 3° threshold (38.66% vs. 21.39% accuracy), indicating superior fine-grained geometric detail. Among diffusion-based normal methods, GeoWizard and Marigold trail by ~2° in mean error. For PBR materials, IntrinsicAnything is the strongest baseline (23.88 dB albedo PSNR), yet Neural LightRig leads by 2.74 dB on albedo and 4.23 dB on roughness while running 24× faster (5 s vs. 2 min). On relighting, DiLightNet and IC-Light—pure diffusion-based relighting without explicit geometry—achieve only 22.68 and 20.29 dB PSNR respectively, well below Neural LightRig's 30.12 dB, confirming that explicit G-buffer estimation yields more physically faithful relighting. The data-driven baseline Yi et al. [54] is competitive on roughness (16.88 dB) but lags significantly on albedo (21.10 dB) and metallic (20.30 dB) compared to Neural LightRig.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multi-view diffusion paradigm (Zero123++, MVDream) for a different axis of variation: instead of generating consistent views from different camera angles, it generates consistent images under different point-light directions, effectively simulating photometric stereo captures via a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion model. The key conceptual departure from prior inverse-rendering work (optimization-based differentiable rendering, feed-forward regression) is treating the diffusion model not as a direct generator of surface properties but as an auxiliary condition producer whose outputs feed a separate regression U-Net. The reference-attention conditioning mechanism is borrowed from multi-view diffusion, and the two-phase fine-tuning scheme follows standard practice for adapting pre-trained diffusion models to new generation tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical middle ground between expensive photometric-stereo hardware and the inherent ambiguity of single-image estimation, with a 5-second inference time that matches or beats slower baselines. The LightProp dataset (80k objects with multi-light renders and G-buffers) is a useful resource for the community. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to complex multi-object scenes with inter-reflections, whether the synthetic-to-real gap (addressed here with augmentations) holds under truly in-the-wild conditions, and whether the fixed 9-light configuration is optimal or whether adaptive light placement could further reduce ambiguity for specific object geometries.
