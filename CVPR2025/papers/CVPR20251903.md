## Problem

Estimating 3D contact points on both human bodies and objects from single in-the-wild images is critical for joint human-object reconstruction, but existing methods depend on expensive 3D contact annotations from motion capture or manual labeling, limiting scalability. Additionally, prior work treats contact as a binary classification (contact with 'any' object), failing to capture the semantic relationships in multi-object interactions where different body parts contact different objects simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251903/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a text prompt specifying the object of interest. It produces binary per-vertex contact labels on a SMPL+H body mesh (10,475 vertices) and continuous per-point contact values in [0,1] on a 3D object mesh retrieved from Objaverse via OpenShape. For the downstream reconstruction task, these contacts serve as constraints to fit a SMPL-X body and object mesh to the image, yielding a full 3D human-object interaction reconstruction.

## Method

InteractVLM combines a VLM (LLaVA fine-tuned with LoRA rank-8) that reasons about the image and emits specialized contact tokens, with a novel Multi-view Localization model (MV-Loc) built on SAM. The key technical contribution is the Render-Localize-Lift (RLL) framework: 3D body and object geometry are rendered from multiple viewpoints using NOCS normal coloring, a feature-lifting network transforms the VLM's 2D reasoning tokens into 3D-aware features by incorporating camera parameters, and SAM-based decoders predict 2D contact masks on each view. These 2D masks are then back-projected to 3D by intersecting rays with the known geometry, ensuring multi-view consistency. This design leverages the VLM's broad visual commonsense to dramatically reduce reliance on 3D annotations—achieving F1 0.53 with just 1% of training data versus DECO's 0.55 with 100%.

## Results

On the DAMON dataset for binary human contact prediction, InteractVLM achieves 75.6% F1, 75.2% precision, 76.0% recall, and 2.89 cm geodesic distance, outperforming the prior best (DECO) by 20.6% in F1 and reducing geodesic error by ~7.4×. For object affordance prediction on PIAD, InteractVLM reaches 62.7% SIM, 86.47% AUC, 21.20% aIOU, and 0.81 MAE on seen objects, surpassing the previous best (IAGNet) on all four metrics. In the data-efficiency study, InteractVLM matches DECO's full-data F1 (0.55) using only 1% of training data and surpasses it (0.58 vs 0.55) at 5%. In a perceptual study for joint 3D HOI reconstruction, InteractVLM's reconstructions are preferred over PHOSA 62% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InteractVLM decisively outperforms all prior methods on binary human contact (Table 1): it beats DECO [58] by 20.6% F1 (75.6 vs 55.0) and reduces geodesic distance from 21.32 cm to 2.89 cm, and surpasses BSTRO [27] by 29.6% F1 and POSAPIXIE [23] by 44.6% F1. On object affordance prediction (Table 3), InteractVLM outperforms IAGNet [65] (the prior SotA) on all four PIAD metrics (SIM 62.7 vs 54.5, AUC 86.47 vs 84.85, aIOU 21.20 vs 20.51, MAE 0.81 vs 0.98) and also beats the point-cloud registration baselines PMF [70], ILN [10], PFusion [63], and XMF [1] by a wide margin. For joint 3D HOI reconstruction, InteractVLM is preferred over PHOSA [67] in 62% of perceptual study trials, despite PHOSA using handcrafted contact annotations while InteractVLM infers contacts from a single image. Notably, InteractVLM matches LEMON [66] on the 3DIR dataset despite training on unpaired human-only data rather than paired human-object geometry, demonstrating the benefit of leveraging VLM commonsense over paired supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

InteractVLM extends the LISA paradigm of language-guided segmentation by repurposing a VLM (LLaVA) and a segmentation foundation model (SAM) for 3D contact estimation, bridging the gap between 2D reasoning and 3D geometry through the novel Render-Localize-Lift framework. It departs from prior contact estimation methods (DECO, BSTRO, POSAPIXIE) that depend heavily on expensive 3D contact annotations by leveraging the broad visual commonsense of VLMs fine-tuned with LoRA on limited data. For joint reconstruction, it builds on the optimization-based fitting approach of PHOSA but is the first to use inferred contacts on both the human body and the object simultaneously as mutual anchoring constraints, rather than relying on handcrafted or body-only contacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is the dramatic reduction in 3D annotation dependency: 1% of DAMON training data yields performance nearly matching DECO trained on 100%, which is significant given that 3D contact labeling is prohibitively expensive at scale. The introduction of Semantic Human Contact (conditioning body contacts on a specific object) addresses a real limitation of binary contact estimation in multi-object scenes, though the evaluation is limited to the DAMON dataset. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-object interactions beyond the tested categories, how sensitive the pipeline is to OpenShape retrieval quality, and whether the optimization-based reconstruction can be made real-time. The lack of in-the-wild 3D ground truth for HOI means the reconstruction evaluation relies on a perceptual study rather than quantitative 3D metrics.
