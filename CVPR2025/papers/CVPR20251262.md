## Problem

Vision-Language Models require high-resolution inputs for document understanding, incurring substantial computational overhead, while simply inserting OCR text into prompts underperforms full-resolution baselines and produces long sequences that increase latency. The paper addresses how to efficiently integrate OCR-derived textual and layout information into existing VLMs without increasing resolution demands or sequence length, while preserving the VLM's original weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251262/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution document image (e.g., 448×448), OCR-extracted text tokens with their 2D bounding boxes, and a natural-language instruction as input. The OCR data is encoded and compressed into 64 learned query vectors that are projected to the VLM's hidden dimension and concatenated with the visual feature tokens before entering the LLM. The output is a free-form text answer, and for multipage documents the same 64-query compression is applied either globally (one set for the whole document) or per-page (64 queries per page concatenated).

## Method

DocVLM introduces a model-agnostic OCR module built on the DocFormerV2 encoder (344M parameters, T5-based) that processes OCR tokens, bounding boxes, and instruction embeddings alongside M=64 randomly initialized learnable queries. Only the 64 query-position outputs are retained from the encoder, projected to match the target VLM's hidden dimension, and appended to the visual token sequence before the LLM. Training uses a two-stage strategy with the VLM fully frozen: Stage I aligns the OCR encoder and queries to the LLM using text-only inputs (no image), and Stage II reintroduces visual features to teach the compressed OCR queries to complement rather than duplicate visual information. The approach is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated across LLaVA-OneVision (feature downsampling), InternVL2 (tile-based), and Qwen2-VL (resolution-based) token-control strategies, and extends to multipage documents via global or page-wise OCR encoding without any multipage-specific training.

## Results

Under the 1.5K token constraint, DocVLM with Qwen2-VL (576 visual tokens) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all benchmarks: 92.8% ANLS on DocVQA, 82.8% on TextVQA, 79.8% on ST-VQA, 66.8% on InfoVQA, and 150.4 CIDEr on TextCaps. On multipage MP-DocVQA, DocVLM reaches 86.3% ANLS (zero-shot, no multipage training), surpassing the previous SOTA of 80.3% (GRAM) by 6.0 points. In the most constrained regime (256 visual tokens), DocVLM with InternVL2 improves DocVQA from 56.0% to 86.6% (+30.6 points) using only 64 additional OCR tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocVLM consistently outperforms its host VLM baselines at matched token budgets: Qwen2-VL improves from 84.4% to 91.2% DocVQA (+6.8) at 320 tokens, InternVL2 from 56.0% to 86.6% (+30.6) at 256 tokens, and LLaVA-OneVision from 66.5% to 88.4% (+21.9) at 1.5K tokens. Against dedicated document models, DocVLM+Qwen2-VL (576 tokens) surpasses DocFormerV2 (87.8% DocVQA at 1K tokens) by 5.0 points while using fewer tokens, and beats Pali-3+OCR (88.6% at 6.3K tokens) by 4.2 points with 10× fewer tokens. On multipage MP-DocVQA, DocVLM achieves 86.3% ANLS zero-shot, outperforming GRAM (80.3%), DocOwl2 (69.4%), and DocFormerV2 (76.4%) by 6.0, 16.9, and 9.9 points respectively, despite no multipage training. DocVLM also surpasses ScreenAI+OCR (89.9% DocVQA at 4.3K tokens) by 1.3 points at 320 tokens, and KOSMOS-2.5-CHAT (81.1% at 4K tokens) by 10.1 points. The only baseline that exceeds DocVLM on any metric is GRAM on DUDE (51.2% vs 47.4%), though GRAM uses a different architecture and was explicitly trained for multipage reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

DocVLM sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: OCR-enhanced VLMs (Pali-3, ScreenAI, GRAM) that insert extracted text into prompts, and OCR-free high-resolution VLMs (LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL2, Qwen2-VL) that trade compute for visual fidelity. It departs from the former by replacing raw OCR token insertion with a learned-query compression mechanism (analogous to Q-Former/Resampler in the visual domain) applied to OCR encodings from DocFormerV2, and from the latter by showing that a small fixed OCR token budget can substitute for large visual token budgets. The method is explicitly model-agnostic, preserving frozen VLM weights and requiring only a lightweight OCR encoder plus projection layer to be trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: reducing document understanding to ~64 OCR tokens plus modest visual tokens enables multipage processing within standard context windows, and the zero-shot generalization to MP-DocVQA (86.3% vs. 80.3% prior SOTA) without multipage training suggests the approach scales to real-world document pipelines. Open questions include sensitivity to OCR quality (the paper notes robustness analysis in the supplement but the main text relies on a single OCR system), whether 64 tokens suffices for extremely dense or multi-column layouts, and whether the compression bottleneck degrades on tasks requiring verbatim text reproduction rather than comprehension. The model-agnostic design also raises the question of whether the same compression ratio transfers to VLMs with fundamentally different visual tokenization schemes.
