## Problem

Active learning for 3D object detection has been explored only in outdoor environments, while indoor 3D detection faces distinct challenges—fewer samples per class, more categories, severe class imbalance, diverse scene types, and high intra-class variance—that render existing outdoor AL strategies suboptimal. This paper presents the first study of active learning specifically for indoor 3D object detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250688/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes raw 3D point clouds (voxelized at 0.02 m resolution) and, through the CAGroup3D detector, outputs 7-parameter 3D bounding boxes (center, size, yaw) with class labels. In each active learning round, the method selects a subset of unlabeled point clouds from a large pool for oracle annotation, progressively expanding the labeled set until a fixed annotation budget is reached (e.g., 10% of the full training set).

## Method

The proposed framework combines two selection criteria. For uncertainty, it computes a localization-aware score (IoU-weighted Shannon entropy over detected proposals) and a lightweight MLP that predicts the number of undetected objects; both are normalized and multiplied into a unified score used for Top-K candidate selection. For diversity, it builds a Class-aware Adaptive Prototype (CAP) bank that dynamically allocates a variable number of prototypes per class (inspired by infinite mixture prototypes), then formulates diverse sample selection as an optimization maximizing the entropy and L1-norm of aggregate prototype histograms. This combinatorial problem is decomposed into tractable sub-problems by clustering the candidate pool with k-means++ and solving each cluster via greedy search, jointly capturing intra-class variance and scene-type diversity.

## Results

With only 10% of the annotation budget, the proposed method achieves 56.11% mAP@0.25 and 35.42% mAP@0.50 on SUN RGB-D, and 61.77% mAP@0.25 and 45.93% mAP@0.50 on ScanNetV2, reaching 87.24% and 85.02% of the fully-supervised (oracle) performance on the two datasets respectively. The method consistently outperforms all baselines across every annotation budget from 2% to 10%, with the largest gaps observed at lower budgets. On SUN RGB-D, it surpasses the strongest 2D object detection baseline (PPAL) by 3.56% in mAP@0.25, and the strongest 3D outdoor baseline (CRB) by 5.22% in mAP@0.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets across all annotation budgets. On SUN RGB-D (10% budget), it beats the best 2D object detection baseline PPAL [59] by 3.56% mAP@0.25 (56.11 vs. 52.55) and 1.91% mAP@0.50 (35.42 vs. 33.51), and the best 3D outdoor baseline CRB [25] by 5.22% mAP@0.25 (56.11 vs. 50.89). On ScanNetV2, the margin over PPAL narrows to 2.33% mAP@0.25 (61.77 vs. 59.44) but the mAP@0.50 gap widens to 2.78% (45.93 vs. 43.15). Notably, the 3D outdoor methods (CRB, KECOR) underperform the 2D object detection methods on both indoor datasets, which the authors attribute to their failure to model intra-class variance. Generic 2D methods like MC-Dropout [9] perform poorly on ScanNetV2 (45.71% mAP@0.25), even below random sampling (55.09%), highlighting the difficulty of transferring 2D uncertainty strategies to 3D indoor detection. The method achieves 87.24% of oracle performance on SUN RGB-D and 85.02% on ScanNetV2 with only 10% labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends active learning for 3D object detection from outdoor settings (CRB, KECOR) to indoor environments, where the authors argue that prior outdoor methods are suboptimal due to severe class imbalance, greater scene-type diversity, and high intra-class variance. Conceptually, it repurposes the Infinite Mixture Prototypes (IMP) idea from few-shot learning into a Class-aware Adaptive Prototype bank for diversity selection, and combines a localization-aware entropy score with a novel undetection-count network to form a unified epistemic uncertainty criterion. The diversity optimization borrows the k-means++ partitioning trick from BADGE but applies it to prototype-histogram space rather than gradient space, and the overall two-stage (uncertainty → diversity) pipeline follows the hybrid AL paradigm established by PPAL and DivProto in 2D detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first systematic study of AL for indoor 3D detection, this work identifies and addresses domain-specific failure modes (undetected objects, intra-class shape variance) that outdoor AL methods overlook, providing a template for future indoor AL research. The finding that a fixed number of prototypes per class (K-means) underperforms an adaptive count by ~2.4% mAP@0.50 on ScanNetV2 is a concrete, actionable insight for prototype-based methods in long-tail 3D settings. Open questions include whether the undetection-count network generalizes across detector architectures, whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or larger indoor benchmarks, and whether the decomposition-based optimization (Eq. 9) remains tight when the candidate pool is very large or the prototype bank is highly imbalanced.
