## Problem

Prior unified multimodal models (e.g., Chameleon, Show-o) rely on a single visual encoder for both understanding and generation, but these tasks demand fundamentally different information granularities—high-level semantic features for understanding versus fine-grained spatial and textural detail for generation. This shared-encoder design creates an inherent conflict that forces trade-offs, causing unified models to underperform task-specific understanding models by a significant margin.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250054/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For multimodal understanding, the model accepts an RGB image (resized to 384×384 with padding) plus a text query and produces a text response. For visual generation, it takes a text prompt and autoregressively predicts VQ codebook tokens to synthesize a 384×384 image, using classifier-free guidance (scale 5) at inference. Both pathways feed into a single 1.3B-parameter autoregressive transformer (DeepSeek-LLM) with a maximum sequence length of 4096, and the model uses a randomly initialized image prediction head alongside the LLM's built-in text head.

## Method

Janus decouples visual encoding into two independent pathways unified by one transformer: a SigLIP-Large-Patch16-384 encoder extracts high-dimensional semantic features for understanding (flattened and mapped via a 2-layer MLP adaptor), while a VQ tokenizer (codebook size 16,384, 16× downsampling) produces discrete IDs for generation (mapped via a separate 2-layer MLP adaptor). Training proceeds in three stages: (I) adaptors and image head are trained with frozen LLM and encoders to establish visual-linguistic alignment; (II) unified pretraining unfreezes the LLM and mixes text, understanding, and generation data (with ImageNet-1k used first for basic pixel dependencies); (III) instruction fine-tuning blends all data types to enhance dialogue and instruction-following. The entire framework uses standard cross-entropy loss without task-specific weighting or special attention masks, and the decoupled design allows each pathway to independently adopt the best encoder for its domain.

## Results

Janus (1.3B) achieves 87.0 on POPE, 69.4 on MMBench, 63.7 on SEED-Bench, and 34.3 on MM-Vet for multimodal understanding, outperforming the previous best unified model Show-o (1.3B) by 41% on MME (1338 vs. 948) and 30% on GQA (59.1 vs. 48.7). On visual generation, Janus attains 61% overall accuracy on GenEval (surpassing SDXL at 55% and DALL-E 2 at 52%) and an FID of 8.53 on MSCOCO-30K (vs. Show-o's 9.24). Notably, Janus also exceeds the 7B understanding-only model LLaVA-v1.5 on POPE (87.0 vs. 85.9), MMBench (69.4 vs. 64.3), SEED-Bench (63.7 vs. 58.6), and MM-Vet (34.3 vs. 31.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Janus (1.3B) decisively outperforms the closest unified model Show-o (1.3B) on every reported understanding metric: POPE 87.0 vs. 73.8, MME-P 1338 vs. 948 (+41%), and GQA 59.1 vs. 48.7 (+30%). Against the 7B unified models, Janus surpasses LWM on POPE (87.0 vs. 75.2) and MM-Vet (34.3 vs. 9.6), and beats VILA-U on POPE (87.0 vs. 85.8) and SEED (63.7 vs. 59.0), though VILA-U retains a slight edge on MME-P (1401.8 vs. 1338.0). Among understanding-only models, Janus exceeds LLaVA-v1.5 (7B) on POPE, MMBench, SEED-Bench, and MM-Vet, but trails it on MME-P (1338 vs. 1510.7) and VQAv2 (77.3 vs. 78.5). On visual generation, Janus achieves 61% GenEval overall accuracy, beating Show-o (53%), SDXL (55%), and DALL-E 2 (52%), while its COCO-30K FID of 8.53 improves over Show-o (9.24) and LWM (12.68) but remains slightly behind generation-only PixArt-α (7.32) and RAPHAEL (6.61). The ablation (Exp-D vs. Exp-A/B) confirms that the decoupled SigLIP+VQ encoding is the key driver: a shared VQ tokenizer drops MMBench from 69.4 to 35.0, and even a shared semantic tokenizer (Exp-B) only reaches 52.7, validating that task-specific encoders are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

Janus departs from prior unified multimodal models (Chameleon, Show-o, VILA-U) that share a single visual encoder for both understanding and generation, arguing that the differing granularity requirements of these tasks create an inherent conflict. It repurposes two well-established encoding strategies—SigLIP for high-level semantic features and a VQ tokenizer for fine-grained spatial structure—into separate pathways feeding a single autoregressive transformer (DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B). The training recipe draws on PixArt's staged ImageNet-then-open-domain generation curriculum and LLaVA-style instruction tuning, but the core architectural contribution is the explicit decoupling of visual encoding, which the authors claim is the first to be highlighted in this unified setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling principle offers a practical and extensible design pattern for next-generation unified multimodal models: each task can independently adopt its best encoder without trade-offs, and new modalities (audio, point clouds, EEG) can be added as additional encoder branches. Limitations include the 384×384 resolution ceiling, evaluation on a single 1.3B architecture, and the fact that the generation FID (8.53) still trails dedicated diffusion models like PixArt-α (7.32) and RAPHAEL (6.61). Open questions include whether the decoupling benefit persists at higher resolutions and larger scales, and whether the approach generalizes to video or other sequential visual tasks.
