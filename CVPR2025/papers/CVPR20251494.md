## Problem

Adapting pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP to downstream tasks with limited few-shot data causes overfitting, degrading the model's inherent zero-shot and generalization capabilities. Existing prompt learning and adapter-style methods solely optimize class token features through task-specific objectives, making them vulnerable to distribution shifts and unable to preserve the generalized knowledge encoded in the VLM's lower encoder layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251494/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of text class prompts (e.g., 'A photo of a [CLASS].') as input, producing a class label via cosine similarity in a shared V-L latent space. A key design choice is the decoupled inference strategy: for base (seen) classes, the prediction combines both representation-token features and class-token features weighted by α=0.7, while for novel (unseen) classes or new datasets, only the regularized class-token features are used. Most experiments operate in a 16-shot setting with 16 training examples per class.

## Method

MMRL introduces a shared, modality-agnostic learnable representation space R (K tokens of dimension d_r) that is projected into both the visual and textual modalities via separate learnable linear mapping functions, with the resulting representation tokens inserted into the higher layers (from layer J onward) of both CLIP encoders. This placement preserves generalizable knowledge in the lower layers while allowing dataset-specific adaptation at discriminative higher layers. During training, the projection layer for representation tokens is trainable while the original class token projection layer remains frozen, and a cosine-similarity regularization term explicitly aligns the class features and text features with the frozen CLIP's zero-shot features to safeguard generalization. The total loss combines cross-entropy on both class and representation features (weighted by α) plus the regularization penalty (weighted by λ). At inference, a decoupling strategy uses both feature streams for base classes but relies solely on the regularized class-token features for novel classes, enabling flexible deployment across seen and unseen tasks.

## Results

On the primary base-to-novel generalization benchmark (16-shot, 11 datasets), MMRL achieves an average harmonic mean (HM) of 81.20%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art MMA (79.87%) by 1.33% and the zero-shot CLIP baseline (71.70%) by 9.50%. In absolute terms, MMRL reaches 85.68% base accuracy and 77.16% novel accuracy on average, gaining +2.48% and +0.36% over MMA respectively. On cross-dataset transfer (ImageNet→10 targets), MMRL attains 67.25% average accuracy, outperforming MMA (66.61%) by 0.64%. In few-shot learning, MMRL achieves the best average across all shot settings (1/2/4/8/16), with margins widening as the number of shots increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMRL outperforms all 11 baselines on the average harmonic mean across 11 datasets (81.20% vs. MMA's 79.87%, PromptSRC's 79.97%, and CLIP's 71.70%). The most dramatic gains appear on fine-grained datasets: on FGVCAircraft, MMRL achieves 41.15% HM versus MMA's 38.33% and CLIP's 31.09%, a 10-point improvement over the zero-shot baseline. On cross-dataset transfer (ImageNet→10 targets), MMRL leads with 67.25% average accuracy, edging out MMA (66.61%) and MaPLe (66.30%). However, MMRL does not uniformly dominate on novel-class accuracy: on UCF101, EuroSAT, DTD, and FGVCAircraft, methods like PromptSRC or MMA retain slightly higher novel scores, while MMRL compensates with substantially higher base accuracy (e.g., +10.14% base over MMA on EuroSAT). On domain generalization (ImageNet→V2/Sketch/A/R), MMRL achieves top performance on 2 of 4 shifted datasets, with the largest margin on ImageNet-A (51.20% vs. MMA's 51.12%). In few-shot learning, MMRL consistently ranks first across all shot counts (1–16), with the performance gap over baselines widening as more shots are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMRL builds on the CLIP architecture and the observation from MMA that higher encoder layers encode dataset-specific features while lower layers retain generalizable knowledge. It departs from both prompt-learning methods (CoOp, MaPLe) that inject learnable vectors at shallow layers and adapter-style methods (CLIP-Adapter, MMA) that add lightweight MLPs, instead introducing a shared, modality-agnostic representation space projected into both encoders at higher layers. The decoupling strategy—using representation features for base classes but only class-token features for novel tasks—represents a conceptual departure from prior methods that rely on a single feature stream for all predictions, and the cosine-similarity regularization against frozen CLIP features is a new loss component not present in earlier prompt or adapter approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent tension in few-shot VLM adaptation: improving in-distribution accuracy without degrading zero-shot generalization. The decoupling inference strategy is practically useful, allowing a single trained model to serve both seen and unseen tasks without retraining. However, the gains over the prior SOTA (MMA) are modest (≈1.3% HM on average), and all experiments are confined to CLIP and image classification; whether the shared representation space and decoupling strategy transfer to other VLM backbones (e.g., SAM, LLaVA) or to generation/retrieval tasks remains open. The ablation showing that removing the shared space (w/o RS) slightly improves base accuracy while hurting novel accuracy suggests the multimodal coupling primarily serves generalization rather than task-specific discrimination—a nuance that could inform future design choices.
