## Problem

Old film restoration faces two intertwined challenges that existing methods handle incompletely: (1) old films exhibit complex, composite, and often unknown degradations (noise, blur, compression, plus structured defects like scratches and corrosion) that single-degradation video restoration methods cannot address jointly, and (2) structured defects propagate across frames during temporal feature aggregation, contaminating otherwise clean frames. Additionally, no unified public benchmark dataset existed to fairly evaluate old film restoration methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252566/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a T-length sequence of degraded old film frames (3×H×W RGB) and produces restored clean frames of the same resolution via a recurrent forward-backward pipeline. During training, input is a randomly selected 7-frame clip cropped to 256×256 pixels from the REDS dataset with online-synthesized compound degradation and aging masks. Evaluation uses the proposed SRWOV dataset comprising 30 synthetic clips (full-reference metrics) and 216 real-world clips at diverse resolutions and frame rates (no-reference metrics).

## Method

MambaOFR builds on a 2D Selective State Space Module (2D-SSM) that scans image patches in four directions to achieve a global receptive field at linear computational cost, replacing the expensive transformer attention used in prior old film restoration. Two key modules are added: (1) a Degradation-aware Prompt Block (DPB) that uses global average pooling and linear layers to generate soft weights over a bank of N=16 learned 3×3 convolution kernels, producing dynamic convolution parameters that adapt the network's feature processing to the current degradation type without requiring explicit degradation labels; (2) a Flow-guided Mask Deformable Alignment (FMDA) module that warps the previous frame's restored features using RAFT optical flow, refines alignment with deformable convolution whose offsets are conditioned on the previous frame's structural-defect mask, and generates a new soft mask that fuses aligned and current features while explicitly suppressing temporal propagation of defects. Training uses L1 + perceptual + spatial-temporal adversarial losses over 200K iterations on two L40 GPUs.

## Results

On the synthetic part of the SRWOV dataset, MambaOFR achieves 26.93 dB PSNR, 0.9271 SSIM, 0.0538 LPIPS, and 0.0583 DISTS, outperforming the best baseline ShiftNet (26.02 dB PSNR, 0.1001 LPIPS) by over 0.9 dB in PSNR and ~46% in LPIPS. On the real-world part, MambaOFR attains the best no-reference scores across all three metrics: NIQE 4.83, BRISQUE 19.18, and CLIPIQA+ 0.4142, with BRISQUE nearly halved compared to the next-best method RTN (28.93). The method consistently leads on every metric in both evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaOFR outperforms all seven baselines on every metric in both the synthetic and real-world evaluations. Against the strongest general video restoration method ShiftNet [30], MambaOFR gains +0.91 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.1001 to 0.0538 (−46%), showing that general-purpose methods struggle with structured defects like scratches and banding noise. Against the best old-film-specific method RTN [47], MambaOFR improves PSNR by +1.84 dB and LPIPS by nearly 2× (0.1025 → 0.0538), while also achieving a much lower BRISQUE (19.18 vs. 28.93) on real-world clips. The older photo/film methods (DeOldify, OldPhoto, DeepRemaster) lag substantially, with PSNR gaps of 3–5 dB, confirming that dedicated old-film restoration still trails general video restoration without the proposed degradation-aware and flow-guided alignment mechanisms. Notably, ShiftNet achieves the highest PSNR among baselines but the worst DISTS (0.1160), indicating it preserves pixel-level structure but fails on perceptual quality due to unremoved structured defects.

## Relation to Prior Work

MambaOFR builds on the 2D selective state space module from MambaIR for spatial feature aggregation, replacing the quadratic-complexity transformer blocks (VRT, RVRT, ShiftNet) with a linear-complexity global receptive field. For temporal alignment, it extends the deformable convolution paradigm from BasicVSR++ and TDAN by incorporating a learned soft mask and the previous frame's mask into the offset prediction, specifically to counteract the temporal propagation of structured defects (scratches, banding) that prior old-film methods (RTN, DeepRemaster) did not explicitly model. The degradation-aware prompt block draws from the dynamic convolution and prompt-based all-in-one restoration literature (PromptIR, contrastive degradation encoders) but adapts it to the Mamba architecture where direct prompt injection into the SSM scan is infeasible, instead modulating the post-SSM convolution layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the first public benchmark dataset (SRWOV: 30 synthetic + 216 real-world clips) addresses a long-standing gap that has hindered fair comparison in old film restoration, making this practically important for the subfield. The finding that general video restoration methods outperform specialized old-film methods on pixel-level metrics yet fail on perceptual quality highlights a fundamental tension between unstructured and structured degradation handling that the community has not resolved. Open questions remain around heavily occluded frames where optical flow breaks down, and the method's reliance on a pre-trained RAFT flow estimator limits robustness in the most severely degraded cases. The degradation-aware prompt mechanism, if it generalizes beyond old films, could inform broader all-in-one restoration pipelines.
