## Problem

Existing 3D semantic Gaussian representations (e.g., GaussianFormer) suffer from two critical inefficiencies: most Gaussians still describe empty space despite the spatial sparsity of driving scenes, and the additive aggregation of Gaussian contributions produces unbounded occupancy logits that encourage unnecessary overlapping among Gaussians. This results in a very low proportion of effective Gaussians actually modeling occupied regions independently, undermining the efficiency gains that sparse representations are supposed to provide over dense voxel grids.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-camera RGB images (6 views at 900×1600 for nuScenes) or a single monocular image (376×1408 for KITTI-360) as input and produces voxel-level 3D semantic occupancy predictions. The output is structured as a concatenation of an empty-class probability (1−α(x)) and class-conditional probabilities (α(x)·e(x)), where α is the overall occupancy probability and e is the GMM expectation over non-empty classes. The spatial resolution is 200×200×16 voxels (0.5 m) for nuScenes with 18 classes, and 256×256×32 for KITTI-360 with 19 classes.

## Method

GaussianFormer-2 reinterprets each 3D Gaussian as a probability distribution of its neighborhood being occupied, assigning 100% occupancy probability at the Gaussian center that decays exponentially with distance. Geometry is derived via the multiplication theorem of probability (α(x) = 1 − ∏(1−α(x;Gᵢ))), which guarantees α(x) ≥ α(x;Gᵢ) for any single Gaussian, thereby preventing unnecessary overlap. Semantics are computed as the expectation of a Gaussian Mixture Model where opacity serves as the prior, the Gaussian distribution as the conditional, and softmax-normalized class properties ensure bounded, normalized outputs. A distribution-based initialization module learns pixel-aligned binary occupancy distributions along camera rays (supervised by BCE loss on ground-truth occupancy) rather than depth values, providing sample-specific initialization that places Gaussians near occupied regions from the start. The refined Gaussians pass through B attention-based blocks (self-encoding, image cross-attention, refinement) before final aggregation, achieving SOTA performance with only 12,800 Gaussians versus 144,000 in GaussianFormer.

## Results

On nuScenes (surround-view), GaussianFormer-2 (Ch.=192) achieves 31.74 IoU and 20.82 mIoU, setting a new state of the art and surpassing the previous best dense-grid method SurroundOcc (31.49 IoU, 20.30 mIoU) and the prior Gaussian-based method GaussianFormer (29.83 IoU, 19.10 mIoU) while using only 12,800 Gaussians versus GaussianFormer's 144,000 (~8.9%). On SSCBench-KITTI-360 (monocular), the method achieves 13.90 mIoU, outperforming GaussianFormer by 7.6% in relative mIoU (12.92 → 13.90), though it trails OccFormer (40.27 IoU) and TPVFormer (40.22 IoU) on raw IoU while leading on mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes, GaussianFormer-2 (Ch.=192) outperforms all baselines on both IoU (31.74) and mIoU (20.82), beating the closest dense-grid competitor SurroundOcc by +0.25 IoU and +0.52 mIoU, and the prior Gaussian-based GaussianFormer by +1.91 IoU and +1.72 mIoU while using only 8.9% of the Gaussians (12,800 vs. 144,000). The advantage is most pronounced on small-object classes: bicycle IoU improves from 11.26 (GaussianFormer) to 13.44, and motorcycle from 13.83 to 15.84. On SSCBench-KITTI-360, the method achieves the best mIoU (13.90) but trails OccFormer (40.27 IoU) and TPVFormer (40.22 IoU) on raw IoU, suggesting the probabilistic formulation excels at balanced per-class accuracy rather than maximizing the dominant road/terrain class. Compared to GaussianFormer on KITTI-360, the improvement is substantial in mIoU (+0.98 absolute, ~7.6% relative) with notable gains on road (45.47→54.12), sidewalk (25.03→32.31), and building (28.44→32.01). The efficiency ablation (Table 3) confirms that even at 6,400 Gaussians the method already surpasses GaussianFormer's 25,600-Gaussian configuration in mIoU (19.87 vs. 16.00), demonstrating a fundamentally better performance-efficiency trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to GaussianFormer, which first introduced 3D semantic Gaussians as a sparse, object-centric alternative to dense voxel grids for occupancy prediction. The key conceptual departure is reinterpreting each Gaussian not as an additive contribution to occupancy but as a probability distribution over its neighborhood being occupied, with aggregation via the multiplication theorem of probability rather than summation. This probabilistic framing is paired with a Gaussian mixture model for semantics (replacing unbounded logit summation) and a distribution-based initialization module that learns pixel-aligned occupancy distributions from annotations, in contrast to the depth-based or LiDAR-based initialization used in prior sparse methods like VoxFormer and BEVDepth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic formulation provides a principled solution to the overlapping and empty-region waste that plagued the original Gaussian representation, yielding SOTA occupancy prediction with only ~9% of the Gaussians required by GaussianFormer. However, the efficiency gains are partially offset by higher inference latency (323 ms vs. 227 ms) due to the farthest-point-sampling step in the initialization module, leaving open the question of whether the probabilistic aggregation can be made faster at scale. Broader implications include whether the independence assumption in the multiplication rule holds in highly cluttered scenes, and whether the distribution-based initialization can be distilled or replaced by a lighter mechanism to close the latency gap.
