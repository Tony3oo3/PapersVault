## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate object hallucinations—textual responses inconsistent with actual image content—because they predominantly attend to prompt-irrelevant global image features while failing to capture prompt-relevant local features. This attention deficiency causes the model to be distracted by co-occurring objects through association (e.g., hallucinating 'cars' due to 'road'), and existing decoding-based mitigation methods (VCD, OPERA, DOLA) struggle to capture fine-grained local image features, resulting in poor visual grounding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a free-form text prompt (e.g., a Yes/No object query or an open-ended captioning instruction) as input. At each autoregressive decoding step, it produces a calibrated logit distribution over the vocabulary by fusing logits computed from the original image with logits computed from an augmented image in which prompt-irrelevant regions are masked. The final output is the generated text response sampled from this fused distribution, making the approach a drop-in replacement for any standard decoding strategy (greedy, top-p, top-k, temperature).

## Method

AGLA is a training-free, plug-and-play decoding approach with two components. First, an Image-Prompt Matching (IPM) module uses a BLIP-ITM matching model and applies GradCAM to its cross-attention layer to compute per-patch correlation scores with the prompt; an adaptive masking strategy (masking ratio = sim(v,t)/2) then suppresses low-correlation regions to produce an augmented image that highlights prompt-relevant content. Second, at each decoding step, AGLA fuses the logit from the original image (capturing generative global features) with a weighted logit from the augmented image (capturing discriminative local features) via additive combination, followed by adaptive plausibility constraints that truncate tokens whose original probability falls below a threshold β·max, preventing the augmented distribution from promoting implausible outputs. This differs from contrastive decoding (VCD), which subtracts a noise distribution, by instead adding a useful local-attention distribution as a supplement to the original.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark with LLaVA-1.5 (7B), AGLA achieves F1 scores of 87.71 (Random), 84.68 (Popular), and 81.36 (Adversarial), outperforming the best baseline VCD by 0.88, 1.31, and 2.08 points respectively. With InstructBLIP (7B), AGLA reaches F1 of 87.07, 82.58, and 79.16 across the three settings, surpassing VCD by up to 2.94 points (Random). On the CHAIR captioning benchmark, AGLA reduces hallucination scores to C_S=43.0 and C_I=14.1 for LLaVA-1.5, the lowest among all methods, while also achieving the highest Recall (78.9). The paper reports average improvements of 5.5% in accuracy and 5.1% in F1 over regular decoding across all LVLMs and POPE settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGLA consistently outperforms all three baselines (DOLA, OPERA, VCD) across every POPE setting and both LVLMs. The largest margins appear under the Adversarial setting, where AGLA beats VCD by 2.08 F1 points (LLaVA-1.5) and 1.08 F1 points (InstructBLIP), confirming its effectiveness against association-driven hallucinations. On the CHAIR captioning benchmark, AGLA achieves the lowest hallucination scores (C_S=43.0, C_I=14.1 for LLaVA-1.5) while also attaining the highest Recall (78.9), outperforming OPERA (C_S=47.0, C_I=14.6) and VCD (C_S=51.0, C_I=14.9). On LLaVA-Bench-Wild, AGLA scores 3.83 accuracy and 4.39 detailedness (GPT-4 evaluated, scale of 10), edging out OPERA (3.68/4.29) and DOLA (3.51/4.22) for LLaVA-1.5. DOLA, which contrasts logits across LLM layers, is the weakest baseline in most settings, suggesting that layer-level contrast alone is insufficient to address the visual attention deficiency identified in this work.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGLA extends the line of decoding-based hallucination mitigation (VCD, OPERA, DOLA) by reframing the problem as an attention deficiency: LVLMs over-attend to prompt-irrelevant global features while neglecting prompt-relevant local regions. Rather than VCD's contrastive subtraction of a noise distribution, AGLA adds a discriminative local distribution derived from a GradCAM-guided augmented image view, making the two approaches orthogonal and complementary. The image-prompt matching module repurposes interpretability techniques (GradCAM on cross-attention) from the network-explainability literature, applied here to a frozen BLIP-ITM matching model to produce adaptive, prompt-conditioned masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play decoding strategy, AGLA is immediately deployable across multiple LVLMs without retraining, which is practically valuable for practitioners who cannot afford fine-tuning. The attention-deficiency diagnosis offers a concrete mechanistic explanation that could guide future architectural or training-time interventions. Open questions include whether the reliance on a separate matching model (BLIP-ITM) can be eliminated by leveraging the LVLM's own cross-attention, whether the simple sim/2 masking ratio generalizes to video or dense multi-object scenes, and whether the approach transfers to hallucination types beyond object presence (e.g., attribute or spatial-relation errors) without task-specific tuning.
