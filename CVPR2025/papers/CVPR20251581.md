## Problem

Existing communication-efficient collaborative perception methods (e.g., Where2comm) still require far more bandwidth than real-world vehicular communication supports (~27 Mbps total), and under realistic bandwidth limits their detection accuracy degrades sharply—sometimes falling below simple late fusion. The paper addresses how to maintain high 3D detection accuracy under strict per-agent bandwidth budgets by refining which regions to communicate and by hybridizing intermediate and late collaboration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251581/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent processes its local LiDAR point cloud through a PointPillar encoder (0.4 m grid, max 32 points/pillar) to produce L=3 multi-scale BEV feature maps. The ego agent receives from each collaborating agent (up to 4–5) a compressed message containing only the non-zero elements of selected sparse features (converted to float16) plus their spatial coordinates, along with a small set of filtered detection boxes for late fusion. The final output is a set of 3D bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores, produced by merging intermediate-fusion detections with confidence-suppressed late-fusion detections before NMS.

## Method

CoSDH introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, a supply-demand-aware selection module decomposes the ego's low-confidence regions into areas that are genuinely unobservable (occlusion or point-cloud sparsity) versus areas that are simply background; only the former generate a demand mask (pillar point count below ε_a), which is intersected with the collaborator's supply mask (spatial confidence above ε_c) to yield a sparse selection mask. Second, separate per-scale autoencoders compress the selected features along the channel dimension (ratio 16 or 8), and the ego fuses decoded features from all agents via element-wise max at each scale. Third, a confidence-aware late fusion step filters other agents' detections by a confidence threshold ε_l and multiplies their scores by a suppression factor β∈(0,1) before merging with the ego's intermediate-fusion results, so that late fusion compensates for intermediate-fusion degradation under low bandwidth without introducing low-quality false positives.

## Results

Under the no-bandwidth-limit setting, CoSDH achieves 96.83% AP@0.5 and 92.99% AP@0.7 on OPV2V, 89.23% AP@0.5 and 86.31% AP@0.7 on V2XSim, and 76.75% AP@0.5 and 63.85% AP@0.7 on DAIR-V2X, while requiring only 13.4/1.1/7.1 Mbps per agent—roughly 1/100 to 1/300 of the bandwidth used by non-communication-efficient baselines (2,640–5,280 Mbps). Under the simulated real-world constraint of 6.75 Mbps per agent (27 Mbps total for 4 agents), CoSDH retains 92.92% AP@0.7 on OPV2V, 86.31% on V2XSim, and 63.76% on DAIR-V2X, with less than 0.3% AP degradation on OPV2V despite an 80–85% bandwidth reduction. The ablation confirms that the supply-demand selection reduces bandwidth by ~95% with negligible accuracy loss, and confidence-aware late fusion adds up to 4% AP@0.7 at very low bandwidths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSDH outperforms all eight baselines on AP@0.7 across all three datasets under both the no-limit and 6.75 Mbps-per-agent settings. Under the realistic bandwidth constraint, CoSDH leads Where2comm [11] by 3.06% AP@0.7 on OPV2V (92.92% vs. 89.86%), 4.19% on V2XSim (86.31% vs. 82.12%), and 4.25% on DAIR-V2X (63.76% vs. 59.51%), while using less bandwidth (2.0/1.1/1.4 Mbps vs. 5.4/4.7/5.3 Mbps). Against CoAlign [25], the closest competitor on DAIR-V2X AP@0.5, CoSDH achieves higher AP@0.7 (63.76% vs. 60.19%) at 1.4 Mbps versus 3.8 Mbps. Compared to V2XViT [35] and V2VNet [32], which require 500–1,000 Mbps under no-limit, CoSDH matches or exceeds their AP@0.7 (e.g., 92.99% vs. 89.88% on OPV2V) at roughly 1/200 the bandwidth. The largest gap appears under bandwidth constraints: When2com [21] drops to 50.88% AP@0.7 on OPV2V, while CoSDH retains 92.92%, a 42-point advantage attributable to the confidence-aware late fusion compensating for reduced intermediate-feature bandwidth.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSDH builds directly on the communication-efficient collaborative perception line initiated by Where2comm [11], which introduced sparse foreground-region selection via spatial confidence maps, and on the autoencoder-based channel compression first proposed in AttFuse/OPV2V [36]. The key conceptual departure is replacing the symmetric supply-demand assumption (high-confidence areas in the collaborator's view are supplied to low-confidence areas in the ego's view) with an asymmetric formulation: demand is derived from the ego's point-cloud density (identifying truly unobservable regions), while supply is derived from the collaborator's detection confidence. Additionally, the paper introduces an intermediate-late hybrid collaboration paradigm, departing from the field's near-exclusive focus on intermediate feature-level fusion by adding a confidence-aware late-fusion stage that compensates for bandwidth-induced degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical gap between simulated collaborative perception benchmarks (which often assume hundreds of Mbps) and real vehicular communication constraints (~27 Mbps total) has limited the deployment of intermediate-fusion methods; CoSDH demonstrates that a well-designed hybrid can maintain near-unconstrained accuracy at 1–2 Mbps per agent, making collaborative 3D detection viable under realistic C-V2X conditions. The finding that naive late fusion outperforms intermediate fusion below ~5 Mbps challenges the field's implicit assumption that feature-level collaboration is always superior, suggesting that hybrid paradigms may be the natural design space for bandwidth-constrained multi-agent perception. Open questions include whether the supply-demand heuristic (point-cloud density threshold) generalizes to camera-based or mixed-modality agents, whether the intermediate-late hybrid extends to other collaborative tasks (segmentation, occupancy, prediction), and how the method behaves under dynamic, time-varying bandwidth rather than fixed budgets.
