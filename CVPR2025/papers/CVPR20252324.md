## Problem

Existing driving scene reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS) are fundamentally limited by training data density and fail to render novel trajectories, particularly large maneuvers such as multi-lane shifts spanning up to 6 meters. While DriveDreamer4D demonstrated that pretrained world models can improve reconstruction quality, its training-free integration still struggles with complex maneuvers, leaving a gap in closed-loop simulation for end-to-end autonomous driving evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252324/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 40-frame front-camera video segments from the original trajectory, novel trajectory poses (lateral shifts of 3 m, 6 m, or full lane changes), 3D bounding boxes, and HDMaps as structural conditions. The 3D boxes and HDMaps are projected via transformation to align with the novel trajectory before conditioning the restoration. The output is a restored novel-trajectory video that is iteratively fed back into the reconstruction model's training, rather than being a one-shot generation.

## Method

ReconDreamer introduces two coupled components. First, DriveRestorer is a world model (DriveDreamer-2) fine-tuned for online video restoration: it takes degraded rendered frames (produced by an under-trained Gaussian Splatting model) as appearance priors, applies masks emphasizing sky and distant regions, and is supervised by ground-truth video using a standard diffusion denoising loss conditioned on 3D boxes and HDMaps. Second, the Progressive Data Update Strategy (PDUS) incrementally expands the novel trajectory by a fixed stride (1.5 m per update step), renders the new view, restores it with DriveRestorer, and blends the restored frames into the training set with a growing sampling weight w = k/sum(j). This progressive expansion reduces the per-step restoration difficulty, enabling the system to handle viewpoint shifts up to 6 m while the reconstruction model (e.g., Street Gaussians) is jointly optimized with RGB, depth, and SSIM losses on original data plus RGB and SSIM losses on restored novel data.

## Results

On the Waymo dataset, ReconDreamer with Street Gaussians achieves average NTA-IoU of 0.517, NTL-IoU of 54.60, and FID of 105.55 across three novel trajectory views (3 m lane shift, 6 m lane shift, lane change), outperforming Street Gaussians (0.456 / 52.83 / 143.42) by 13.38%, 3.35%, and 26.40% respectively. On the most challenging 6 m lane shift, the relative gains are 24.87% (NTA-IoU), 6.72% (NTL-IoU), and 29.97% (FID). Against DriveDreamer4D with PVG, ReconDreamer improves average NTA-IoU by 44.00% and achieves a 195.87% NTA-IoU gain on the 6 m lane shift. A user study confirms a 97.22% win rate over Street Gaussians and 96.88% over DriveDreamer4D with PVG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReconDreamer is evaluated against four 3DGS-based reconstruction baselines (PVG, S³Gaussian, Deformable-GS, Street Gaussians) and the world-model-augmented DriveDreamer4D, all on the Waymo, NuScenes, and PandaSet datasets. Against Street Gaussians (the strongest baseline), ReconDreamer wins on every metric across all three trajectory views, with the largest gains on the 6 m lane shift (NTA-IoU 0.467 vs 0.374, FID 149.19 vs 213.04). Against DriveDreamer4D with PVG, ReconDreamer improves average NTA-IoU from 0.300 to 0.432 (+44.00%) and achieves a 195.87% NTA-IoU improvement on the 6 m lane shift (0.358 vs 0.121); similar margins hold for S³Gaussian (+8.75% avg NTA-IoU) and Deformable-GS (+93.94% avg NTA-IoU). On NuScenes, ReconDreamer improves NTA-IoU by 86.36% over Street Gaussians on the 6 m lane shift (0.246 vs 0.132). In the user study, ReconDreamer achieves a 97.22% win rate over Street Gaussians and 96.88% over DriveDreamer4D with PVG, confirming perceptual quality gains beyond the quantitative metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two parallel lines of work: 3DGS-based dynamic driving scene reconstruction (Street Gaussians, PVG, S3Gaussian, Deformable-GS) and driving world models (DriveDreamer-2, DriveDreamer4D). The key departure from DriveDreamer4D is the shift from a training-free, one-shot use of a pretrained world model as a data machine to an online, iterative restoration process where the world model is fine-tuned as a DriveRestorer and progressively integrated via a Progressive Data Update Strategy. Conceptually, ReconDreamer reframes world model knowledge as a restorative signal applied incrementally during reconstruction training rather than as a static generative prior applied once.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReconDreamer is the first method to render large maneuvers (up to 6m multi-lane shifts) with acceptable quality, which is critical for closed-loop evaluation of end-to-end autonomous driving systems where agents must execute complex trajectory changes. The progressive data update strategy offers a generalizable training paradigm that could extend to other reconstruction tasks with distribution shift. However, the approach still depends on structure conditions (3D boxes, HDMaps) and is validated primarily on front-camera views in structured urban scenes; open questions include scalability to surround-view settings, unstructured environments, and whether the iterative restoration loop can be made more computationally efficient for real-time simulation.
