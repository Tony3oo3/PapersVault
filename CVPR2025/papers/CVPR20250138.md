## Problem

Existing linear approximation methods for MaxPool in neural network robustness verification (DeepPoly, CNN-Cert, 3DCertify, OSIP) produce coarse bounds that limit certified accuracy, while MaxLin achieves only block-wise tightest bounds that are suboptimal when ReLU is bounded by tighter BaB constraints. No prior work provides provably neuron-wise tightest linear bounds for the multi-variate MaxPool function, leaving significant over-approximation that propagates through the verification process.

## Input / Output

The verifier takes a MaxPool-based CNN or PointNet model, a single input data point (2D image or 3D point cloud), and a perturbation specification (radius ε and norm type l₁, l₂, or l∞). It outputs per-neuron upper and lower bounds on the network's output layer, from which certified accuracy (fraction of inputs verified safe) or a certified robustness bound (maximal safe radius via binary search) is derived. The linear bounds are computed per pooling region using only the local input interval [l, u] determined by the preceding verification step, requiring no global optimization.

## Method

Ti-Lin derives closed-form neuron-wise tightest linear bounds for MaxPool by exploiting its piece-wise linearity. The upper bound is a hyperplane passing through f(a) and f(b), where a and b are the two endpoints of the space diagonal of the input hyper-rectangle [l, u]; the lower bound is simply x_q where q = argmax(m_i) at the midpoint m. Four explicit cases (Theorem 1) handle different orderings of the top-3 upper bounds and the maximum lower bound, yielding coefficients in O(pool_size × log 3) time. Theorem 3 proves these bounds minimize the over-approximation volume ∫∫(u(x)−l(x))dx over the input region, making them neuron-wise tightest in the sense of Definition 4. Because the bounds are framework-agnostic, Ti-Lin plugs directly into backsubstitution (DeepPoly, CNN-Cert), multi-neuron relaxation (ERAN), and branch-and-bound (α,β-CROWN) verifiers without modification, achieving up to 78.6% improvement in certified accuracy over state-of-the-art baselines at comparable runtime.

## Results

Ti-Lin achieves up to 78.6 percentage-point improvement in certified accuracy over α,β-CROWN (e.g., 85.7% vs. 7.1% on CIFAR-10 CNN 6-layer) and 28.6% over MaxLin (57.1%) on the same network. Across all three verification frameworks (DeepPoly, ERAN, α,β-CROWN), Ti-Lin outperforms every baseline on every tested network, with improvements ranging from 1.5 to 78.6 percentage points depending on the baseline and network depth. In terms of efficiency, Ti-Lin incurs almost no additional time cost over the fastest baselines (DeepPoly, ERAN) and achieves up to 5.3×, 1.7×, and 420.9× speedup over 3DCertify, ERAN, and OSIP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ti-Lin dominates all eight baselines across every network and dataset tested. Against the backsubstitution baselines, Ti-Lin improves certified accuracy by up to 39.4% over DeepPoly and 7.1% over 3DCertify (e.g., 77.8% vs. 70.7% on MNIST CNN 8-layer), while also being faster than 3DCertify (up to 5.3×) due to its closed-form bounds versus 3DCertify's double-description search. Against the MN-based baselines, Ti-Lin (integrated into ERAN) beats MN-BaB by up to 39.4% and ERAN by 7.1% on MNIST CNN 8-layer (77.8% vs. 38.4% and 70.7%), with a 1.7× speedup over ERAN. Against the BaB-based baselines, the gains are most dramatic: Ti-Lin achieves 85.7% vs. 7.1% for α,β-CROWN and 57.1% for MaxLin on CIFAR-10 CNN 6-layer, confirming that neuron-wise tightest bounds substantially outperform both adaptive linear bounds and block-wise tightest bounds when ReLU is handled by BaB. Against OSIP (MaxPool-to-ReLU transformation), Ti-Lin improves certified accuracy by up to 65.0% while being up to 420.9× faster, demonstrating that the transformation approach is both less precise and far more expensive. The one case where Ti-Lin ties a baseline is MNIST CNN 4-layer on α,β-CROWN (92.6% for both MaxLin and Ti-Lin), where the network is shallow enough that the block-wise and neuron-wise tightest bounds coincide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuron-wise tightest linear bounds concept—previously established for univariate functions like ReLU and Sigmoid [72]—to the multivariate MaxPool function, which had only received coarse linear relaxations (DeepPoly, CNN-Cert) or block-wise tightest bounds (MaxLin, by the same first author). It departs from the MaxLin line by showing that neuron-wise tightness for MaxPool strictly dominates block-wise tightness when ReLU is bounded by BaB techniques, and from the OSIP line by avoiding the costly MaxPool-to-ReLU transformation. The resulting bounds plug directly into existing verification architectures (DeepPoly, ERAN, α,β-CROWN) without modifying their core logic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaxPool is ubiquitous in CNNs and PointNets yet was a persistent gap in tight verification; providing provably neuron-wise tightest bounds closes this gap and yields up to 78.6% certified-accuracy gains over prior methods with negligible runtime overhead. The practical impact is broad because the bounds are framework-agnostic and integrate into the VNN-COMP-winning α,β-CROWN pipeline. Open questions include whether the neuron-wise tightest principle extends to other multivariate pooling or aggregation functions (e.g., AvgPool, TopK), and whether the four-case structure of Theorem 1 generalizes cleanly to larger pool sizes or non-rectangular input regions.
