## Problem

T2V generative models are highly sensitive to prompt vocabulary and sentence structure, yet prior prompt-optimization work relies on LLMs to expand user prompts without tailored guidance aligned to the training prompt distribution. This leads to optimized prompts that are either too short (user prompts) or excessively long with misleading details (LLM-generated), degrading both static and dynamic video quality, especially for compositional scenes with multiple objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250080/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short, user-provided text prompt (e.g., 'a woman representing a funeral') and produces a single optimized prompt whose vocabulary, sentence structure, and length closely match the distribution of the T2V model's training captions (Vimeo25M). The optimized prompt is then fed to a frozen T2V model (LaVie or Latte) for video generation; no changes are made to the T2V model itself. The system is model-specific in that the relation graph and refactoring model are built from the target model's training data.

## Method

RAPO employs a dual-branch architecture with a final selection step. Branch 1 (word augmentation + sentence refactoring) first constructs a relation graph from ~2.1M training captions where scenes are core nodes and subject/action/atmosphere modifiers are sub-nodes; given a user prompt, it retrieves top-k relevant modifiers via all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence embeddings and cosine similarity, then iteratively merges them into the prompt using a frozen LLM. A LoRA-fine-tuned LLaMA 3.1 refactoring model then restructures the augmented prompt to match training prompt format and length. Branch 2 rewrites the user prompt directly via a frozen LLM following a fixed instruction set. A LoRA-fine-tuned discriminator LLM, trained on video-quality-evaluated prompt pairs, selects the superior prompt between the two branches, mitigating LLM hallucination and ambiguity.

## Results

On VBench, RAPO achieves a total score of 82.38% with LaVie (vs. 80.89% baseline) and 79.97% with Latte (vs. 77.03% baseline), surpassing both GPT-4 and Open-Sora prompt optimization. The most striking gain is on the multiple objects dimension, where RAPO lifts LaVie from 37.71% to 64.86% and Latte from 29.55% to 52.78%. On EvalCrafter, RAPO reaches a final sum score of 256 (LaVie) and 227 (Latte), outperforming all baselines. On T2V-CompBench, RAPO improves action binding from 0.483 to 0.635 (LaVie) and object interactions from 0.760 to 0.839 (LaVie).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPO consistently outperforms both GPT-4 and Open-Sora prompt optimization across all three benchmarks and both T2V backbones. On VBench, RAPO with LaVie scores 82.38% total versus 79.69% (GPT-4) and 79.75% (Open-Sora), with the gap widening dramatically on multiple objects (64.86% vs. 36.23% and 36.52%). On EvalCrafter, RAPO achieves 256 final sum score versus 246 (GPT-4) and 251 (Open-Sora) for LaVie. On T2V-CompBench, RAPO leads on all four compositional dimensions; for example, action binding reaches 0.635 with RAPO versus 0.428 (GPT-4) and 0.470 (Open-Sora) for LaVie. Notably, GPT-4 and Open-Sora actually degrade performance on several dimensions (e.g., human action drops from 95.80% to 83.80% with GPT-4 on LaVie), suggesting that overly verbose or misaligned prompts can mislead the T2V model, whereas RAPO's retrieval-grounded approach maintains fidelity to the training prompt distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based prompt optimization for text-to-image generation (Hao et al., Mo et al., Zhan et al.) and early T2V prompt rewriting (CogVideoX, Movie Gen) by introducing a retrieval-augmented component grounded in the actual training prompt distribution. Where prior T2V prompt optimizers rely solely on a frozen LLM to expand short user prompts into longer descriptions, RAPO constructs a relation graph from training captions to retrieve semantically relevant modifiers, then uses a fine-tuned refactoring LLM to align sentence structure with the training format, and a fine-tuned discriminator to select between two candidate prompts. The key conceptual departure is treating prompt optimization as a distribution-matching problem (aligning vocabulary and sentence length with training data) rather than a pure semantic enrichment problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic improvement on the multiple-objects dimension (nearly doubling on both LaVie and Latte) demonstrates that compositional T2V generation is bottlenecked by prompt format as much as by model capacity, offering a low-cost, training-free lever for practitioners. The finding that GPT-4 and Open-Sora prompt rewrites can *degrade* human-action and object-class scores challenges the assumption that more detailed prompts are always better, and the prompt-length distribution analysis provides a concrete diagnostic. Open questions include whether the relation graph approach generalizes to T2V models trained on different corpora, whether the discriminator's reliance on generated-video evaluation labels scales to new models, and whether the gains persist for longer, more complex multi-shot prompts beyond the single-scene setting tested here.
