## Problem

Autoregressive next-token/scale prediction has driven breakthroughs in language, 2D image, and multimodal generation, yet its application to 3D object generation and understanding remains largely unexplored. Existing 3D generation methods rely on slow diffusion sampling or inflexible feed-forward reconstruction, while 3D understanding approaches use point clouds that discard fine geometric and textural details. The paper asks whether a unified autoregressive framework can simultaneously achieve fast 3D generation and detailed 3D comprehension.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model accepts a single 256×256 RGB image (encoded by DINOv2) or a text prompt (encoded by CLIP ViT-L) and produces a 3D mesh via Flexicubes or a NeRF via volume rendering in ~0.82 s on an A6000. For understanding, a 3D model is rendered from 6 views into RGB-D images with Plücker camera embeddings, encoded into 10-scale triplane tokens, and only the first K−2 scales (37.5% of all tokens) are fed to the LLM to produce a detailed caption. The same VQVAE serves both tasks, with the full token sequence used for generation and a truncated sequence for understanding.

## Method

SAR3D introduces a multi-scale 3D VQVAE that encodes 6 multi-view RGB-D renderings (with Plücker ray embeddings) into a latent triplane, quantized across 10 resolution scales sharing a single codebook (V=16384, C=8). A GPT-style transformer with AdaLN then performs next-scale prediction—predicting an entire token map at each scale rather than individual tokens—conditioned on CLIP or DINOv2 features via cross-attention, reducing generation to 10 autoregressive steps and enabling sub-second inference. For 3D understanding, truncated scale tokens (r₁ through r_{K−2}) are projected through an MLP and concatenated with text instructions, then fed to a Vicuna-7B LLM fine-tuned in two stages (projector alignment, then joint tuning) following the PointLLM protocol. Classifier-free guidance (10% dropout) is applied during training to improve condition alignment at inference.

## Results

On the image-conditioned 3D generation benchmark (G-Objaverse), SAR3D-NeRF achieves the best KID (0.42%), Coverage (74.17%), and MMD (13.63‰) among all compared methods, with a competitive FID of 22.55 (second only to LGM's 19.93) and the highest MUSIQ score (65.77). Generation latency is 1.64 s on a V100 (0.82 s on an A6000), making it 4.5× faster than LN3Diff (7.51 s) and 36× faster than One-2-3-45 (59.23 s). The Flexicubes variant (SAR3D-Flexicubes) trades a small quality drop (FID 27.30, KID 0.63%) for direct mesh extraction at 2.92 s latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAR3D-NeRF dominates on 3D shape quality metrics: it achieves the best KID (0.42% vs. 0.55% for LGM and 0.89% for LN3Diff), the best Coverage (74.17% vs. 50.83% for LGM and 55.17% for LN3Diff), and the best MMD (13.63‰ vs. 22.06‰ for LGM and 19.94‰ for LN3Diff). The only metric where SAR3D trails is FID, where LGM (19.93) edges out SAR3D-NeRF (22.55), though LGM is substantially worse on MUSIQ (54.78 vs. 65.77) and 3D shape quality. Against single-image-to-3D baselines (Splatter-Image, OpenLRM), SAR3D improves FID by 26 and 16 points respectively while also being faster than OpenLRM (1.64 s vs. 7.21 s). Against multi-view-to-3D methods (One-2-3-45, Lara, CRM, LGM), SAR3D wins on every quality metric and is 4–36× faster. For text-to-3D generation (qualitative, Fig. 6), SAR3D produces sharper geometry and better text-3D alignment than Point-E, Shap-E, 3DTopia, and LN3Diff, e.g., correctly rendering red patterns on a sword handle where Point-E reverses colors and 3DTopia produces a uniformly red sword.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAR3D adapts the next-scale prediction paradigm from VAR (2D image generation) to the 3D domain by replacing the 2D feature map with a latent triplane representation, following the encoding strategy of LN3Diff. For 3D understanding, it repurposes the two-stage LLM alignment pipeline from PointLLM but replaces point-cloud tokens with multi-scale VQVAE tokens, enabling a single tokenizer to serve both generation and comprehension. The key conceptual departure from prior autoregressive 3D work (e.g., MeshGPT, MeshAnything) is operating in a continuous triplane latent space rather than discrete mesh topology, and from diffusion-based 3D generators (Shap-E, LN3Diff) is the shift to a single-pass autoregressive decoder that eliminates iterative denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-second generation time (0.82s on A6000) makes high-quality 3D asset creation practical for interactive applications, and the unified VQVAE tokenizer that supports both generation and LLM-based captioning reduces the architectural fragmentation common in prior 3D pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges that generation and understanding still rely on two separate autoregressive models, geometry/texture quality is bounded by volume rendering, and scalability beyond the current 176K-instance training set remains unverified. Open questions include whether the next-scale prediction approach exhibits favorable scaling laws in 3D (as VAR does in 2D) and whether a truly unified model can produce both 3D and text outputs from a single token stream.
