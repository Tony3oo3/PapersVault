## Problem

In multimodal learning, the early training stage (the 'prime learning window') is critical for information acquisition, yet information-sufficient modalities (e.g., audio) dominate this window and suppress information acquisition of weaker modalities (e.g., video). Existing imbalance methods apply adjustments uniformly across the entire training process, overlooking that the prime learning window is the decisive period where this imbalance must be corrected, and that missed information cannot be recovered by extending training later.

## Input / Output

Each modality (audio, video, and optionally text for CMU-MOSI) is encoded independently by a dedicated encoder (ResNet-18 for CREMA-D and Kinetics Sounds; a Transformer for CMU-MOSI), and the resulting features are concatenated via late fusion into a single-layer linear classifier that outputs a class label. The method's distinctive input is not the data itself but the per-modality training dynamics: it monitors the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix per modality at each epoch and the per-batch cross-entropy performance scores to decide when and how strongly to intervene.

## Method

InfoReg introduces an adaptive unimodal regulation term applied only during the prime learning window of information-sufficient modalities. It first identifies dominant modalities via their performance score gap Δ_m, then checks whether they are in the prime learning window by verifying that the relative rate of change of Tr(F_m) exceeds a threshold K. If both conditions hold, a regulation term P = (α/2)‖w_{m;b}^t − w_m^{t−1}‖² is added to the loss, where α = exp(β·tanh(Δ_m)) scales the regulation strength with the performance gap. The paper proves this term is proportional to the batch-level Fisher Information trace Tr(F_{m;b}^t) by showing that gradients across batches are approximately orthogonal, so the penalty directly constrains the information acquisition rate of the dominant modality without requiring full-dataset gradient computation.

## Results

On CREMA-D, InfoReg achieves 71.90% overall accuracy, surpassing the best baseline AGM (69.71%) by 2.19 points and Joint training (66.61%) by 5.29 points. The most striking gain is in the video modality, where InfoReg reaches 49.65% accuracy versus 35.14% for Joint training and 43.72% for AGM. On Kinetics Sounds, InfoReg attains 69.31% overall accuracy (vs. 66.63% for OGM) and 44.73% video accuracy (vs. 36.01% for Joint training). On the three-modality CMU-MOSI dataset, InfoReg achieves 62.31% accuracy and 62.03% Macro F1, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoReg outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric reported. Against OGM (CVPR 2022), InfoReg gains +3.20 points on CREMA-D (71.90 vs. 68.70) and +2.68 on Kinetics Sounds (69.31 vs. 66.63), with the largest margin in video accuracy (+10.13 on CREMA-D). Against Greedy (ICML 2022) and PMR (CVPR 2023), the improvements are even larger: +4.08 and +4.98 on CREMA-D respectively, as both methods struggle to lift video accuracy above 40%. Against AGM (ICCV 2023), the closest competitor, InfoReg still leads by +2.19 on CREMA-D and +2.77 on Kinetics Sounds. In the unimodal-loss setting (Table 3), InfoReg* (75.71% on CREMA-D) surpasses MMPareto (73.08%) by 2.63 points and G-Blending (69.11%) by 6.60 points, confirming that the prime-learning-window regulation is complementary to unimodal loss assistance. On the three-modality CMU-MOSI benchmark, InfoReg (62.31% acc, 62.03% F1) beats OGM (61.88/61.32), PMR (61.47/60.98), and AGM (61.39/60.43), demonstrating scalability beyond two modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoReg sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the critical-learning-periods literature (Achille et al.) showing that early training stages disproportionately shape information acquisition, and (2) the multimodal imbalance literature (OGM, AGM, PMR, MMPareto, G-Blending) that addresses modality dominance through gradient or loss adjustments. The key departure is temporal: prior imbalance methods apply their corrections uniformly across all training epochs, whereas InfoReg identifies a specific 'prime learning window' via the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix and applies a unimodal regulation term only during that window. This repurposes the FIM-based information-acquisition metric from a diagnostic tool into an adaptive gating signal for when to intervene.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why multimodal imbalance is hardest to fix late in training: information missed during the prime learning window cannot be recovered by extending training, which is a concrete and actionable insight for practitioners designing multimodal pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to three relatively small datasets (CREMA-D, Kinetics Sounds, CMU-MOSI) with late-fusion architectures, leaving open whether the prime-learning-window effect persists in large-scale early-fusion or transformer-based multimodal models (e.g., LLM-based vision-language systems). The method also introduces per-epoch FIM trace computation and per-batch performance-score tracking, adding non-trivial overhead that may be prohibitive at scale. A broader open question is whether the same temporal-window logic generalizes to other forms of training imbalance (e.g., data imbalance, task imbalance in multi-task learning).
