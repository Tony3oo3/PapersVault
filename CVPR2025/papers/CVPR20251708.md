## Problem

Despite state-of-the-art diffusion models achieving low FID scores and generating images that appear indistinguishable to humans, no systematic study has quantified how far the generated distribution actually is from the real distribution from a classifier's perspective. This paper asks whether neural network-based classifiers can detect the residual distributional gap that standard metrics and human judgment miss, and what that gap reveals about diffusion model limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251708/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images (resized from 32×32 CIFAR-10, 256×256 or 512×512 ImageNet, or variable-resolution COCO2014) and outputs a distribution label indicating which source the image came from. Classification ranges from binary (real vs. one generated model) to four-way (distinguishing among COCO real, Pixart-α, SDXL, and Playground-v2.5). During training, images undergo random 224×224 crops; during validation, they are resized to 256 on the shorter side and center-cropped to 224×224.

## Method

The paper introduces a distribution classification task where standard classifiers (ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-T, ViT-S) are trained to identify which distribution an image originates from, using both supervised and self-supervised (MAE, MoCo v3) feature extraction. The key finding is that classifiers achieve >98% accuracy distinguishing real from generated images across all settings, revealing a distributional gap invisible to FID and human judgment. The framework is then extended as a diagnostic tool: spatial analysis shows classifiers can identify generated images from any crop region, while frequency analysis reveals diffusion models learn low-frequency features well but fail at higher frequencies. The paper further demonstrates that this distributional mismatch explains Model Autophagy Disorder and shows that augmenting real data with generated data outperforms replacement in downstream tasks, and that classifier guidance during sampling reduces the detectability of generated images from 96.19% to 79.88%.

## Results

Across all label-to-image settings (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-256, ImageNet-512), classifiers achieve 86–100% binary distribution classification accuracy, with ResNet-50 reaching 99.95% on ImageNet-256 vs. U-ViT-H/2 and 99.73% on ImageNet-512 vs. EDM2-XXL. In the four-way text-to-image task (COCO, Pixart-α, SDXL, Playground-v2.5), classifiers achieve 76–93% accuracy with only 5k–10k training samples. Notably, classifiers struggle to distinguish EDM2-XS from EDM2-XXL (74.91% best, near random for others) despite a 1.1 FID gap, yet easily separate U-ViT-H/2 from DiT-XL/2 (99.66%) which have nearly identical FIDs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new generative model but instead uses standard classifiers (ResNet-50, ViT-S, ConvNeXt-T) as probes to evaluate diffusion models. ResNet-50 consistently outperforms ViT-S and ConvNeXt-T in distribution classification (e.g., 99.95% vs. 98.06% vs. 99.89% on ImageNet-256 vs. U-ViT-H/2), while self-supervised classifiers (MAE, MoCo v3) achieve lower but still strong accuracy (71–92%). A key finding is the contradiction with FID: classifiers easily separate U-ViT-H/2 from DiT-XL/2 (99.66% accuracy) despite near-identical FIDs, yet fail to separate EDM2-XS from EDM2-XXL (74.91% best) despite a 1.1 FID gap—humans show the opposite pattern. For downstream use of generated data, augmenting CIFAR-10 with EDM-generated samples (via DPT) improves semi-supervised accuracy to 4.68% error, outperforming real-data-only training (4.90% for FreeMatch), while replacing real data with generated data degrades performance (5.94% error). Classifier guidance reduces the fraction of generated images identified as fake from 96.19% to 79.88%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the dataset bias detection methodology (Torralba & Efros; Liu & He) for a new purpose: quantifying the distributional gap between real and diffusion-generated images via straightforward classification. It departs from the generated image detection literature by using a simpler, controlled setup where the classifier is trained on both real and generated data from the same domain, rather than requiring universal detection across unknown generators. The classifier guidance technique from ADM (Dhariwal & Nichol) is extended from label alignment to realism enhancement, and the Model Autophagy Disorder phenomenon (Alemohammad et al.; Shumailov et al.) is given a mechanistic explanation through the observed distribution mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that >98% classification accuracy is achievable despite FID scores below 3 challenges the widespread assumption that low FID implies near-indistinguishable distributions, suggesting FID may be an insufficient quality metric for diffusion models. The practical guidance that augmenting real data with generated data outperforms replacement is directly actionable for practitioners using synthetic data in training pipelines. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond the tested model families and datasets, whether the frequency-domain diagnostic (low-frequency well-learned, high-frequency not) holds for newer architectures, and whether classifier guidance can be scaled to close the remaining distributional gap.
