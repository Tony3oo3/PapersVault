## Problem

Diffusion models require dozens of sampling steps per image, and existing acceleration methods (distillation, rectified flow, consistency models) either degrade sample quality, reduce generation diversity, or lack theoretical justification for their sampling stability. Specifically, traditional diffusion suffers from varying backward-process expectations, overlapping probability paths, and sampling instability, while sample-noise matching approaches introduce path inconsistency and constrained diversity without proving optimality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252319/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a single RGB image, operating as a LoRA adapter on top of pre-trained diffusion backbones (SD15 0.98B UNet, SDXL 3.5B UNet, PixArt 610M DiT). Generation is compressed to 1–8 denoising steps (versus 25–50 for the base models), with a dedicated one-step variant (Algorithm 3) that reconstructs the image from a single noise prediction at timestep T. Training data is drawn from LAION and COYO subsets, and evaluation is performed on COCO-5k, ImageNet, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100.

## Method

RayFlow replaces the standard Gaussian target N(0,I) with an instance-specific target distribution N(ε_μ, σ²I), where ε_μ = E_t[E[ε_t]] is a unified noise expectation computed from a pre-trained model, so each sample follows a unique diffusion path toward its own target mean rather than a shared origin. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that this choice maximizes the joint forward-and-backward path probability, providing a theoretical guarantee of sampling stability. A complementary Time Sampler module uses Stochastic Stein Discrepancies parameterized by a neural network T_ϑ to learn an importance-sampling distribution over timesteps, concentrating training compute on high-loss regions and reducing estimator variance. Training is performed via LoRA fine-tuning with a two-phase procedure: first constructing distillation data pairs by running the base model's ODE solver, then jointly optimizing the denoiser ε_θ and the Time Sampler under the learned timestep weighting.

## Results

On COCO-5k, SDXL-Ray achieves FID 4.15 (1-step), 3.90 (4-step), and 3.67 (8-step), outperforming all few-step baselines including SDXL-Turbo (4.00 at 4-step) and SDXL-DMD2 (3.98 at 4-step). For aesthetic scores, SDXL-Ray reaches 6.24 at 8 steps, surpassing the base SDXL model (4.61) and all distillation methods. On the PixArt DiT architecture, PixArt-Ray achieves FID 4.12 at 4 steps versus 4.47 for PixArt-δ, while training in only 1 A100 day. The Time Sampler ablation shows consistent gains: removing it degrades FID from 4.05 to 4.85 on SDXL (COCO-5k, 2-step).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RayFlow consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across architectures. On SD15 (4-step FID), RayFlow achieves 4.78, beating PeRFlow (5.21), LCM (5.22), TCD (5.23), and Hyper-SD (5.21) by 0.4–0.5 FID points. On SDXL (4-step FID), RayFlow (3.90) edges out DMD2 (3.98), Turbo (4.00), PeRFlow (4.05), Lightning (4.08), LCM (4.17), TCD (4.20), and Hyper-SD (4.17), with the largest margin over TCD (0.30 FID). In the noise-matching comparison (Table 2, 2-step), SDXL-Ray achieves FID 4.15 and Image Reward 1.021, surpassing ReFlow (4.95/0.624), PeRFlow (4.99/0.630), ReDiff (4.51/0.785), and InstaFlow (4.69/0.442) on all five metrics. For the DiT architecture, PixArt-Ray (FID 4.12 at 4-step) improves over PixArt-δ (4.47) by 0.35 FID while matching its 1-day training cost. Notably, RayFlow's LoRA-based implementation (67.5M/197M params) matches or exceeds full-UNet distillation methods (3.5B params) like Turbo and PeRFlow, indicating superior parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

RayFlow rethinks the diffusion trajectory design by departing from the standard formulation where all samples converge to a shared N(0,I) target. Instead, it leverages a pretrained model to compute an instance-specific target mean ε_μ, guiding each sample along a unique path toward N(ε_μ, σ²I), which the authors prove maximizes path probability. This positions RayFlow as a theoretically grounded alternative to sample-noise matching methods (Rectified Flow, PeRFlow, InstaFlow) that achieve straighter paths but lack formal optimality guarantees. The Time Sampler component repurposes Stochastic Stein Discrepancies from Bayesian inference as a practical importance sampling mechanism for timestep selection during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RayFlow offers a principled answer to the persistent speed-quality tradeoff in diffusion acceleration, with the path probability maximization theorem providing a theoretical foundation that prior heuristic straightening methods lacked. The consistent gains across UNet and DiT architectures (SD15, SDXL, PixArt) suggest the trajectory redesign is architecture-agnostic. However, the approach requires a pretrained model to compute ε_μ, limiting its applicability to from-scratch training, and the one-step variant still injects residual noise (tilde_β_T·ε), leaving open whether truly noise-free single-step generation is achievable. Extension to video, 3D, and audio modalities remains future work.
