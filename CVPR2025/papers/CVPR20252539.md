## Problem

Existing VLM-based driving methods either achieve strong closed-loop driving performance or demonstrate vision-language understanding, but not both simultaneously. Moreover, the dominant approach to language understanding—visual question answering—operates in language space only, so a model's verbal claims can be completely inconsistent with its actual driving actions. The paper addresses this by proposing a unified model that handles closed-loop driving, language understanding, and a new task (Action Dreaming) that explicitly aligns language inputs with the action space.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single front-camera image (split into two 448×448 tiles for high-resolution encoding), navigational conditioning (either two GPS target points or a natural-language command like 'turn left'), ego vehicle speed, and a task-specific prompt. Actions are output as a disentangled pair: temporal speed waypoints (one coordinate every 0.25 s) and geometric path waypoints (one coordinate every meter), which are converted to steering and acceleration via two PID controllers. Language outputs (commentary, VQA answers, or action predictions) are generated auto-regressively, and at inference the model operates in a Chain-of-Thought mode where the action is conditioned on a self-generated commentary.

## Method

SimLingo builds on InternVL2-1B (InternViT-300M-448px vision encoder + Qwen2-0.5B LLM) with LoRA fine-tuning on the LLM and full fine-tuning of all other components. The key architectural insight is a disentangled action representation combining temporal speed waypoints and geometric path waypoints, which resolves steering failures during turns and obstacle avoidance that entangled waypoint representations suffer from. For the Action Dreaming task, the authors introduce a data-collection technique that uses the world-on-rails assumption and a kinematic bicycle model to simulate multiple alternative ego futures from the same visual state, generating diverse instruction-action pairs that force the model to attend to the language instruction rather than infer actions from visual cues alone. Training samples 50/50 between expert trajectories (with VQA, Commentary, or no language supervision) and dream trajectories (with the Dreamer flag toggled to train both instruction-following and unsafe-instruction rejection), using data buckets to oversample challenging scenarios from 3.1 M samples collected at 4 fps by the PDM-lite rule-based expert.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, SimLingo achieves a Driving Score of 85.07±0.95 and Success Rate of 67.27±2.11%, outperforming the previous best method (DriveAdapter, 64.22 DS / 33.08% SR) by over 20 DS points. On the official CARLA Leaderboard 2.0 Sensor track, SimLingo-BASE achieves 6.87 DS, surpassing the prior SOTA (CaRINA hybrid, 1.23 DS) by 4.6×. On the Action Dreaming benchmark, SimLingo reaches 81.13% average Success Rate across instruction categories, compared to 24.52% without the Dreaming training data. For language tasks, SimLingo-1B scores 58.48 GPT on DriveLM-hard VQA and 78.94 GPT on Commentary, substantially exceeding the zero-shot InternVL2-1B baselines (33.08 and 14.95 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimLingo decisively outperforms all prior Bench2Drive methods: it exceeds DriveAdapter (64.22 DS) by 20.85 DS and TCP-traj (59.90 DS) by 25.17 DS, while also matching or exceeding their Success Rates by 34+ percentage points. The paper isolates the contribution of its dataset by retraining TCP-traj w/o distillation on PDM-lite data (45.65 DS) and adding a tuned controller (63.45 DS), showing that the remaining ~22 DS gap to SimLingo stems from architectural and training advances rather than data alone. On the official CARLA Leaderboard 2.0, SimLingo-BASE (6.87 DS) beats TF++ (5.18 DS) by 33% and CaRINA hybrid (1.23 DS) by 4.6×, and is the only camera-only entry. For language tasks, SimLingo-1B (58.48 GPT on VQA, 78.94 GPT on Commentary) substantially outperforms zero-shot InternVL2-1B (33.08, 14.95) and even a DriveLM-only fine-tuned variant (63.85 GPT on VQA). On Action Dreaming, SimLingo (81.13% avg SR) dramatically surpasses the model trained only on native CARLA commands (24.52% avg SR), with the largest gains in Lane Change (83.02% vs. 3.23%) and Speed Up (92.45% vs. 56.45%). The only metric where prior methods partially perform better is Comfortness, which the authors attribute to SimLingo's higher driving speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimLingo builds on the InternVL2 VLM architecture (InternViT vision encoder + Qwen2 LLM) and extends the line of VLM-based driving work (DriveLM, LMDrive, DriveGPT4) by achieving state-of-the-art closed-loop driving with camera-only input, whereas prior VLM-driving systems were evaluated primarily in open-loop or simplified environments. It departs from the dominant multi-sensor (LiDAR + camera) paradigm of CARLA Leaderboard methods like TransFuser and TF++ by relying solely on a single camera, and introduces a disentangled temporal-geometric waypoint representation that replaces the standard entangled waypoint output. The Action Dreaming task is a novel contribution that goes beyond prior VQA-based driving evaluation by requiring the model to predict actions from diverse language instructions, creating a direct test of language-action alignment rather than language-only scene understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a compact 1B-parameter VLM can simultaneously achieve SOTA closed-loop driving and strong language understanding, suggesting that the perceived trade-off between driving performance and language capability may be resolvable. The Action Dreaming benchmark provides a practical way to evaluate instruction-following alignment without executing dangerous actions, filling a gap the authors identify in prior VQA-only evaluations. Key limitations include simulation-only evaluation, the full SimLingo model not being submitted to the official leaderboard (only SimLingo-BASE was), and the observation that Chain-of-Thought conditioning does not yet yield statistically significant driving improvements, leaving open whether CoT can be effectively harnessed for driving. Real-world deployment remains an open question given the added inference latency of VLM-based architectures.
