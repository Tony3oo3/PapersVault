## Problem

Tuning-free personalized human character generation faces a fundamental trade-off: models trained on paired data derived from the same source image either replicate the reference (high appearance consistency but poor text controllability) or extract only compressed features (good text controllability but degraded whole-body appearance). No existing method simultaneously achieves high text controllability and full-body appearance consistency (face, hair, clothing) without per-subject fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251742/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image of a human character (full-body or partial, e.g., head-only) and a free-form text prompt describing pose, background, expression, and viewpoint. At inference, the reference is first standardized to a canonical form (standard pose, white background, neutral expression, fixed face position at 512×768) before being encoded by the personalization model, which then generates the output image. A key property is that multiple serial images generated from different prompts on the same reference maintain consistent whole-body appearance, which is critical for applications like comic story generation.

## Method

SerialGen is a two-stage serial generation framework. Stage I trains a standardization model (built on Animate Anyone's dual-stream Denoising UNet + ReferenceNet architecture) on 10.5M synthetic pairs rendered from 2,924 animatable 3D characters, augmented with two novel modules: a Foreground-Background Distinction Module (FBDM) that injects learnable foreground/background class tokens guided by a segmentation mask, and a Reference Pose Injection Module (RPIM) that adds DW-Pose-derived features into the ReferenceNet to improve body-part localization. Stage II trains an IP-Adapter-based personalization model (SDXL + OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 + MLP adapter) on ~300K (standardized reference, real target) pairs, with the standardization model frozen. The core insight is that standardizing the reference's non-appearance elements (pose, background, expression, viewpoint) simplifies feature extraction, eliminates the input-output replication problem inherent in same-source paired training, and pre-generates the body in a canonical form so that serial outputs from diverse prompts share consistent appearance.

## Results

On a test set of 40 characters (20 anime, 20 real-life) with 20 diverse text prompts, SerialGen achieves the highest CLIP-I (85.49) and CLIP-T (21.76) scores among all compared tuning-free full-body personalization methods, indicating superior appearance consistency and text controllability. On Face Sim (ArcFace cosine similarity), SerialGen scores 0.53, narrowly trailing StoryMaker (0.54) which uses an additional face encoder. The method also achieves an AP-CLIP-I of 83.17, demonstrating strong cross-prompt appearance consistency for serial image generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SerialGen outperforms all three baselines on both CLIP-I (appearance consistency) and CLIP-T (text controllability). Against IP-Adapter, it gains +4.18 CLIP-I and +1.54 CLIP-T, with a substantial Face Sim improvement from 0.39 to 0.53. Against FastComposer, the margins are even larger: +11.51 CLIP-I and +0.33 CLIP-T. Against StoryMaker, SerialGen leads by +1.25 CLIP-I and +1.31 CLIP-T, while StoryMaker retains a marginal 0.01 advantage on Face Sim (0.54 vs. 0.53), which the authors attribute to StoryMaker's dedicated face encoder. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that the two-stage standardization design is critical: removing standardization (paired one-stage) drops CLIP-I by 5.49 points, and unpaired one-stage training suffers severe replication (CLIP-T drops to 16.08).

## Relation to Prior Work

SerialGen composes two well-established architectures into a novel two-stage pipeline: the Animate Anyone dual-stream Denoising UNet + ReferenceNet framework (minus temporal attention) serves as the standardization backbone, while IP-Adapter with SDXL and OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 handles the personalization stage. The key conceptual departure from prior tuning-free personalized generation methods (StoryMaker, IP-Adapter, FastComposer) is the insertion of an explicit standardization step that transforms arbitrary reference images into a canonical form (fixed pose, white background, neutral expression) before feeding them to the personalization model. This addresses a known failure mode—input-output replication in unpaired or same-source paired training—by ensuring the reference and target images share appearance but differ in non-appearance elements, a data construction strategy not previously exploited in this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple standardization step resolves the long-standing tension between text controllability and whole-body appearance consistency, with practical implications for applications like comic story generation where a character must remain visually consistent across many differently-prompted images. The cross-prompt consistency metric (AP-CLIP-I) and the finding that standardization pre-generates the body to prevent drift across serial outputs are practically valuable. Limitations include a residual 3D-rendering style bias from synthetic training data, the added inference latency of the two-stage pipeline, and evaluation limited to 40 characters with anime/real splits. Open questions include whether the standardization approach generalizes to non-human subjects, whether the synthetic-data domain gap can be further reduced, and whether the two-stage cost can be amortized in production settings.
