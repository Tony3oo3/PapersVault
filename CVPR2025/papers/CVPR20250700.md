## Problem

Standard VLN agents rely on learned cross-modal alignment to associate landmark noun phrases in instructions with their visual referents, yet visual grounding remains a persistent weakness, especially for out-of-distribution objects. This paper asks whether pre-generating visual images of instruction-referenced landmarks using off-the-shelf text-to-image models can serve as explicit navigational cues that reinforce visual understanding beyond what language instructions alone provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250700/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language instruction, a sequence of panoramic observations (36 single-view images per panorama from 12 headings × 3 pitches), and a set of visual imaginations—1024×1024 images generated by SDXL for filtered landmark noun phrases in sub-instructions (averaging 2.96 per instruction). The output is a distribution over navigable directions plus a stop action at each timestep. Imaginations are generated upfront with indoor/real-estate positive prompts and humans/collage negative prompts to match Matterport3D-style environments, and are filtered via FG-R2R segmentation and Spacy noun-phrase extraction with a manual blacklist to exclude non-visual or ambiguous terms.

## Method

The approach has two components: a generation pipeline and a model-agnostic integration. For generation, instructions are segmented into sub-instructions via FG-R2R, noun phrases are extracted and filtered, and SDXL synthesizes a 1024×1024 image per valid sub-instruction, yielding the R2R-Imagine dataset (41k+ images). For integration, each imagination is encoded by a pretrained ViT-B/16, passed through a 3-layer MLP with a modality type embedding to produce imagination embeddings, which are concatenated with the instruction text encoding before the VLN agent's cross-modal encoder. A cosine-similarity auxiliary loss explicitly aligns each imagination embedding with the mean embedding of its corresponding sub-instruction's noun-phrase tokens from the text encoder. The full system is finetuned in three stages—first the imagination encoder alone, then jointly with a reduced learning rate for the base model, then all parameters at a common rate—to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. Applied to HAMT and DUET, this yields ~1 SR and ~0.5 SPL gains on R2R val-unseen, with the improvement persisting on the test set for DUET.

## Results

On R2R val-unseen, HAMT-Imagine improves over HAMT by +1.02 SR (66.24→67.26) and +0.51 SPL (61.51→62.02), while DUET-Imagine improves over DUET by +0.60 SR (71.52→72.12) and +0.07 SPL (60.41→60.48). On the R2R test set, DUET-Imagine gains +2 SR (69→71) and +1 SPL (59→60) over DUET. On REVERIE val-unseen, DUET-Imagine improves over DUET by +1.3 SR (46.98→48.28) and +0.82 RGS (32.15→32.97), indicating benefits extend to coarse-grained instruction navigation and object grounding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparisons are against its own base models: HAMT-Imagine outperforms HAMT by +1.02 SR and +0.51 SPL on R2R val-unseen, and DUET-Imagine outperforms DUET by +0.60 SR on val-unseen and +2 SR on the test set. On REVERIE, DUET-Imagine beats DUET by +1.3 SR and +0.82 RGS, showing the approach generalizes to coarse-grained goal-oriented navigation. Among external baselines in Table 1, DUET-Imagine (72.12 SR val-unseen) surpasses ADAPT (64 SR) and RecBERT (63 SR) but remains below BEVBert (75 SR), which additionally uses panoramic depth images. The data-scaling methods MARVAL and ScaleVLN achieve higher absolute numbers (87 SR) but rely on additional training environments beyond Matterport3D, making them not directly comparable. Ablations confirm that aligned imaginations are necessary: wrong imaginations degrade performance below baseline, while null imaginations at test time still retain a small training-time regularization benefit (+0.68 SR over baseline HAMT).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLN agent architectures HAMT and DUET by introducing a model-agnostic visual imagination modality generated via SDXL text-to-image diffusion, departing from prior image-augmentation approaches like ADAPT (which retrieves images only from training environments) and LAD (which imagines only the goal in a fixed architecture). It also contrasts with generative VLN work (Pathdreamer, PanoGen, VLN-Sig) that predicts future observations rather than synthesizing instruction-referenced landmarks. The approach draws conceptual motivation from cognitive science dual-coding theory, positioning visual imaginations as a pivot between language tokens and visual observations rather than as a replacement for either.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The R2R-Imagine dataset (41k+ synthesized landmark images) and the model-agnostic integration recipe provide a reusable resource for studying how off-the-shelf generative models can reinforce visual grounding in embodied agents. However, the gains are modest (~1 SR, ~0.5 SPL) and come with added computational cost for generation and encoding, limiting immediate practical deployment. Key open questions include whether imaginations can bridge the Sim2Real gap, how they interact with VLN world models, and whether lifelong grounding of personalized object names is possible within this framework.
