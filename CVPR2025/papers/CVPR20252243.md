## Problem

Existing physics-aware image editing methods either control material or lighting in isolation, leading to incomplete disentanglement where editing one property inadvertently alters the other. Additionally, these approaches largely ignore high-level semantic editing, making it difficult to simultaneously adjust physical appearance and scene semantics within a single coherent framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252243/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 512×512 RGB input image, a desired lighting condition (HDRI environment map), four material condition maps (albedo, roughness, metallicity, transparency), and a free-form text prompt. The material and lighting conditions are represented as pixel-aligned spatial maps rather than global scalars, enabling localized and continuous property adjustments (e.g., making only part of an object transparent). For real images without ground-truth environment maps, lighting is estimated via an off-the-shelf lighting estimator.

## Method

PhyS-EdiT employs a dual-network diffusion architecture: a low-level U-Net (U_low) conditioned on physical property maps via ControlNet handles material and lighting edits, while a high-level U-Net (U_high) handles text-driven semantic edits; a fusion network (U_fusion) combines their outputs at each denoising step. Physical properties are extracted as pixel-aligned condition maps (material maps from a pretrained decomposition model, lighting maps via depth-based proxy rendering), then encoded through a deferred-neural-relighting-inspired encoder and injected via element-wise multiplication before ControlNet conditioning. Training follows a sequential curriculum—material-only (15K steps), material+lighting (15K steps), then semantic (15K steps)—with the base U-Net frozen for the first 30K iterations to preserve generative priors. The authors also introduce PR-TIPS, a 300K-pair synthetic dataset rendered in Blender Cycles from 13K Objaverse models with systematically varied Principled BSDF materials and 500+ HDRI environments, annotated with GPT-4o-generated editing instructions.

## Results

On material editing (1K test samples), PhyS-EdiT achieves PSNR 26.01, LPIPS 0.15, and FID 23.38, substantially outperforming IP2P (PSNR 20.85, LPIPS 0.31, FID 40.80) and Subias et al. (PSNR 23.00, LPIPS 0.32, FID 44.17). On lighting editing, PhyS-EdiT achieves PSNR 26.96 and FID 21.59 versus DiLightNet's PSNR 17.15 and FID 51.08, a near-9 dB PSNR gap. On semantic editing, PhyS-EdiT attains the best CLIP score (0.85) and LPIPS (0.39), with PSNR 15.37, slightly ahead of IP2P (15.11) and SD3 (14.64), though IP2P achieves a marginally lower FID (45.14 vs. 47.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IP2P [3], PhyS-EdiT wins decisively on material editing (PSNR +5.16 dB, LPIPS halved from 0.31 to 0.15, FID nearly halved from 40.80 to 23.38) and on semantic editing (CLIP 0.85 vs. 0.81, LPIPS 0.39 vs. 0.41), though IP2P retains a slight FID edge on semantic editing (45.14 vs. 47.91). Against Subias et al. [40], PhyS-EdiT improves PSNR by 3.01 dB and FID by 20.79 points on material editing, while Subias holds a marginal SSIM advantage (0.80 vs. 0.79); the user study confirms PhyS-EdiT's superiority with 68% preference vs. 14% for Subias. Against DiLightNet [47] on lighting editing, PhyS-EdiT dominates across all four metrics (PSNR 26.96 vs. 17.15, FID 21.59 vs. 51.08), reflecting DiLightNet's lack of material awareness which causes material drift during relighting. Against SD3 [10] on semantic editing, PhyS-EdiT leads on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, CLIP, and FID, confirming that the physics-aware conditioning does not degrade high-level semantic editing capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based image editing lineage (InstructPix2Pix, Stable Diffusion) and the physics-aware editing line (Alchemist for material, DiLightNet for lighting, RGB-X for joint decomposition) by unifying previously isolated capabilities into a single framework. The key architectural departure is a dual-network design (U_low for physical properties via ControlNet-conditioned injection, U_high for semantic edits) with a fusion module, combined with a sequential training strategy (material → lighting → semantic) to prevent interference between editing objectives. The PR-TIPS dataset (300K Blender-rendered pairs with Principled BSDF materials and HDRI lighting) addresses a data gap: prior datasets like Alchemist's lack pixel-aligned maps for local editing, and DiLightNet's lacks material variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in enabling a single model to handle the full spectrum of creative editing—adjusting roughness, metallicity, albedo, transparency, lighting, and high-level semantics—without requiring separate pipelines, which is directly relevant to digital media and advertising workflows. However, the model is limited to single-object editing at 512×512 resolution and requires two forward passes, making it slower than single-pass alternatives. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to full multi-object scenes, whether the sequential training strategy scales to more physical properties, and whether the two-pass overhead can be eliminated without sacrificing disentanglement quality.
