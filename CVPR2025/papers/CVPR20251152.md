## Problem

Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) requires expensive triplet annotations (reference image, modification text, target image) that are scarce in scale and diversity. Existing zero-shot workarounds either rely on image-caption pairs that prevent joint multimodal embedding learning, or generate synthetic triplets with limited diversity and unnatural modification text. Additionally, current fusion mechanisms (shallow transformers, linear interpolation) lack the capacity to capture nuanced relationships between visual and textual modalities, and popular benchmarks suffer from label ambiguity that undermines reliable evaluation.

## Input / Output

At inference, CoLLM takes a reference image (processed at 224×224 or 384×384 depending on the vision encoder) and a free-form modification text, producing a single composed query embedding in a shared retrieval space. During pre-training, the reference image is not a real image but a synthesized embedding obtained via Slerp interpolation between the target image's augmented embedding and its in-batch nearest neighbor, while the modification text is generated from pre-defined interpolation templates combining the target caption with the nearest neighbor's caption. The model simultaneously produces three embedding types—image-only, text-only, and composed—each contrasted against the target image embedding.

## Method

CoLLM synthesizes CIR triplets on-the-fly from image-caption pairs: a vision encoder extracts features, Slerp interpolation between an augmented image embedding and its in-batch nearest neighbor creates a synthetic reference embedding, and template-based text interpolation generates the modification text. The composed query is formed by passing the reference embedding through a lightweight image adapter into the semantic space of a Large Language Embedding Model (e.g., SFR-Embedding-2), which processes the concatenated [adapted_image; modification_text] sequence to produce the final composed embedding via a projection layer. Training uses a contrastive loss averaged over three query types (image-only, text-only, composed) against the target image embedding, followed by fine-tuning on the newly introduced MTCIR dataset (3.4M image pairs, 17.7M multi-attribute modification texts generated by Claude 3 Sonnet from LLaVA-Next-34B captions). The key architectural distinction is replacing shallow fusion layers with a full LLM backbone, leveraging its deep semantic understanding and instruction-following capabilities for multimodal composition.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting (no triplet training), CoLLM with CLIP-L/14 achieves CIRCO mAP@50 of 23.4, CIRR Recall@10 of 72.8, and Fashion-IQ Recall@50 of 49.5, outperforming all non-LLM baselines and the LLM-based CIReVL on CIRR and FIQ. With BLIP-L/16, CoLLM reaches CIRR Recall@1 of 35.0 and FIQ Recall@50 of 56.0, surpassing Slerp-TAT by 5.9 points on CIRR@10. After fine-tuning on MTCIR, CoLLM (BLIP-L) achieves CIRR Recall@1 of 45.8 and FIQ Recall@50 of 60.7, outperforming CoVR2 (43.7 CIRR@1) and MagicLens (33.3 CIRR@1) despite using a smaller vision encoder and a 10–20× smaller dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLLM consistently outperforms non-LLM zero-shot baselines (PALAVRA, SEARLE, Pic2World, LinCIR, ContextI2W) across all benchmarks and encoder sizes, with the largest margins on CIRR (e.g., +11.8 Rec@10 over Slerp-TAT with BLIP-L). Against LLM-based methods, CoLLM beats CIReVL on CIRR Rec@10 (72.8 vs 64.9) and FIQ Rec@50 (49.5 vs 48.6) with CLIP-L, while CIReVL retains a slight CIRCO mAP edge (21.8 vs 23.4 is actually CoLLM winning); however, CIReVL relies on closed-source GPT-4, adding cost and latency. Slerp-TAT narrowly edges CoLLM on CIRR Rec@1 with CLIP-L (30.9 vs 29.7) but loses on all other metrics. In the fine-tuning setting, CoLLM+MTCIR (BLIP-L) surpasses CoVR2 (EVA-CLIP ViT-G) by 2.1 points on CIRR@1 (45.8 vs 43.7) and MagicLens (CoCa-L) by 12.5 points (45.8 vs 33.3), despite using a smaller encoder and a dataset 10–20× smaller. On the refined benchmarks, the performance gap between CoLLM and BLIP-L widens from 3.4% to 6.9% on CIRR@1, indicating CoLLM better handles nuanced modification texts.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLLM sits at the intersection of three CIR research threads: zero-shot methods using VLMs (SEARLE, Slerp-TAT, CIReVL), synthetic triplet generation (CompoDiff, MagicLens, LaSCo), and LLM-based embedding generation (MCL, E5-Mistral). It departs from prior work by repurposing Slerp (originally used for query interpolation in Slerp-TAT) to synthesize reference image embeddings on-the-fly from image-caption pairs, and by leveraging LLEMs (SFR-Embedding-2) directly for composed query embedding rather than using them for intermediate text generation as in CIReVL. The on-the-fly triplet synthesis strategy is novel in that it transforms ubiquitous image-caption data into supervised CIR training signal without any offline triplet construction pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: MTCIR (3.4M pairs, 17.7M modification texts) becomes the largest open-source synthetic CIR dataset, and the refined CIRR/Fashion-IQ benchmarks address a long-standing evaluation reliability problem (label ambiguity) that has likely inflated scores in prior work. The finding that a smaller, higher-quality dataset outperforms MagicLens (36.5M pairs) and CoVR2 challenges the field's assumption that scale alone drives CIR performance. Open questions include whether the single-visual-token constraint limits fine-grained retrieval, whether MLLMs (rather than LLMs) could further improve composed query understanding, and whether the on-the-fly synthesis strategy generalizes to domains where image-caption pairs are less informative.
