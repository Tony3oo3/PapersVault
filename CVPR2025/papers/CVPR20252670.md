## Problem

Existing diffusion-based scene text synthesis methods suffer from character repetition, absence, and distortion in polystylistic scenes. This stems from two limitations: (1) text encoders pre-trained on a single font type have a restricted representation domain that fails to generalize to unseen font styles, and (2) rigid character segmentation masks used to supervise attention over-constrain the model, preventing it from autonomously estimating optimal character generation positions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB input image, a binary mask specifying the text region, and a target text string, and produces a photorealistic image with the text rendered in the designated region. It operates in the latent space of SD-v2.0 inpainting (512×512 resolution), with inference completing in approximately 8.5 seconds. The model is evaluated on both reconstruction (rendering ground-truth text into masked regions) and editing (replacing existing text with new words) tasks.

## Method

DREAMTEXT reconstructs the diffusion training process by extracting latent character masks from cross-attention maps (via Gaussian blur and mean+2σ thresholding) at each training step, then using these masks to drive four complementary losses: a masked diffusion loss that up-weights text-region pixels, a cross-attention loss aligning each token to its mask, a cross-modal alignment loss matching text and visual embeddings, and a character ID loss ensuring distinguishable per-character representations. Because the objective involves discrete mask variables, the authors employ a heuristic alternate optimization strategy that iteratively fixes masks to update the text encoder and U-Net, then recomputes masks from the updated attention maps. A balanced supervision scheme provides ground-truth segmentation masks as a warm-up signal for the first ~25k steps, after which guidance is removed to let the model autonomously refine character positions. The text encoder (initialized from UDiffText) and U-Net are jointly trained on diverse-font datasets (LAION-OCR, SynthText, TextSeg, ICDAR13), creating a synergistic loop between character representation learning and attention re-estimation.

## Results

DREAMTEXT achieves the best performance across all metrics in both scene text reconstruction and editing tasks. On reconstruction, it reaches 0.94 SeqAcc on ICDAR13, 0.96 on TextSeg, and 0.93 on LAION-OCR; on editing, it reaches 0.89, 0.91, and 0.88 respectively. In terms of image quality, DREAMTEXT attains a FID of 12.13 and LPIPS of 0.0328, substantially outperforming the previous state-of-the-art UDiffText (FID 15.79, LPIPS 0.0564). The method also reports an inference time of only 8.5 seconds per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DREAMTEXT outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor UDiffText [29], it improves SeqAcc by 3–10 points on editing (e.g., 0.88 vs. 0.78 on LAION-OCR) and by 1–3 points on reconstruction, while reducing FID from 15.79 to 12.13 and LPIPS from 0.0564 to 0.0328. AnyText [19] is the second-best method on image quality (FID 22.73) but lags DREAMTEXT by 10.6 FID points and by 5–10 SeqAcc points on editing. TextDiffuser [5] achieves competitive reconstruction accuracy (0.80 on LAION-OCR) but drops sharply on editing (0.64) and has a much higher FID (32.25). MOSTEL [14] is strong on reconstruction (0.71 on LAION-OCR) but performs poorly on editing (0.44), reflecting its GAN-based style-transfer design. DiffSTE [8] and SD-Inpainting [15] are substantially weaker across all metrics, with DiffSTE showing the worst FID (51.67) and SD-Inpainting the lowest SeqAcc values overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

DREAMTEXT builds directly on UDiffText (ECCV 2024), which introduced a character-level text encoder to replace CLIP in Stable Diffusion for scene text synthesis, and on TextDiffuser (NeurIPS 2023), which used segmentation masks and character-aware losses for position control. The key departure is from the end-to-end training paradigm: rather than rigidly constraining character attention with pre-computed ground-truth segmentation masks (as UDiffText and TextDiffuser do), DREAMTEXT reconstructs the diffusion training loop with a heuristic alternate optimization strategy that iteratively re-estimates character positions from cross-attention maps and jointly refines the text encoder with diverse fonts. This shifts the problem from a purely continuous optimization to a hybrid discrete-continuous one, addressed through alternating updates between latent character masks and model parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical failure mode in scene text synthesis—character repetition, absence, and distortion in polystylistic scenes—by showing that overly rigid positional supervision actually degrades performance, and that a balanced warm-then-release strategy yields better autonomous position estimation. The heuristic alternate optimization framework and the joint text-encoder/generator training with diverse fonts are practical techniques that could transfer to other conditional generation tasks requiring fine-grained spatial control. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer text strings or more complex multi-line layouts, whether the 25k-step warm-up threshold generalizes across datasets, and whether the reliance on cross-attention maps as a proxy for character positions remains robust under distribution shift.
