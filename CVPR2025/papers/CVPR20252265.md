## Problem

Two widely adopted audio-video deepfake datasets (FakeAVCeleb and AV-Deepfake1M) contain a previously unidentified spurious feature: fake samples begin with 25–30 ms of leading silence, enabling a trivial classifier to reach >98% AUC. Supervised deepfake detectors trained on these datasets exploit this shortcut, yielding overly optimistic results that collapse when the silence is removed. The paper asks how to build a detector robust to such dataset-specific biases without access to fake samples at training time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full audio-video clip and produces a single video-level fakeness score. Internally, AV-HuBERT extracts 1024-dimensional frame-level features at 25 fps for each modality independently (audio features obtained by masking the visual input and vice versa). Per-frame misalignment scores are pooled via log-sum-exp into the final score, and the system is trained exclusively on 50k real VoxCeleb2 samples with no fake data.

## Method

AVH-Align learns a frame-level audio-video alignment score on top of pretrained AV-HuBERT features using only real data. After L2-normalizing the audio and visual feature vectors, a 4-layer MLP (1024→512→256→128→1) with LayerNorm and ReLU computes an alignment score for every audio-visual frame pair. Training maximizes the contrastive probability (InfoNCE-style) that each audio frame matches its temporally corresponding video frame over a 30-frame neighborhood, requiring no labels. At inference, the negative per-frame alignment scores are pooled with log-sum-exp to yield a video-level fakeness score; tampered regions produce higher misalignment because the manipulation desynchronizes the two streams. A supervised variant (AVH-Align/sup) replaces the contrastive loss with binary cross-entropy and serves as a control to demonstrate that the unsupervised formulation is what confers robustness to the leading-silence shortcut.

## Results

On the official AV-Deepfake1M test set, AVH-Align (unsupervised, trained only on real VoxCeleb2 data) achieves 85.24% AUC, substantially outperforming the best visual-only baseline (Xception at 68.68% AUC). On the validation splits, AVH-Align scores 94.6% AUC on FakeAVCeleb and 85.9% AUC on AV-Deepfake1M, with minimal degradation when the leading silence is trimmed (94.6→94.6 and 85.9→83.5). The supervised variant AVH-Align/sup reaches near-perfect 99.90% AUC on the official test set but relies on the silence bias, dropping from 100.0% to 83.1% AUC when silence is removed from AV-Deepfake1M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVH-Align outperforms both unsupervised baselines on AV-Deepfake1M: it achieves 85.9% AUC versus 54.3% for AVAD [17] and 68.8% for SpeechForensics [30], a margin of 17–32 points. On FakeAVCeleb, SpeechForensics edges out AVH-Align (98.8% vs. 94.6% AUC), likely because full-video manipulations leave stronger cues that its architecture captures. Against the supervised audio-only RawNet2 [53] (99.9% AUC untrimmed), AVH-Align is lower in absolute terms but far more robust: RawNet2 drops to 88.1% on trimmed AV-Deepfake1M while AVH-Align only loses 2.4 points. MDS [10] suffers the largest silence-bias collapse (99.2→54.9% on AV-Deepfake1M), whereas AVH-Align remains stable. On the official AV-Deepfake1M test set, AVH-Align (85.24% AUC) surpasses all visual-only methods (Meso4 54.53%, MesoInception4 57.16%, MARLIN 58.03%, Xception 68.68%, EfficientViT 65.51%) by at least 16.6 points, demonstrating that audio-visual alignment on real data alone is a stronger signal than visual forensics for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the pretrained AV-HuBERT model (Shi et al., ICLR 2022) as a shared audio-visual feature extractor, but departs from prior work that uses it in a fully supervised classification pipeline (e.g., AV-Lip-Sync+, AVTENet). Conceptually, it aligns with the unsupervised deepfake detection line initiated by AVAD (Feng et al., CVPR 2023) and SpeechForensics (Liang et al., NeurIPS 2024), which also train exclusively on real data, but replaces their from-scratch representation learning with a frozen self-supervised backbone plus a lightweight learned alignment network. The paper also positions itself against the broader supervised deepfake detection literature by demonstrating that dataset-level spurious features (here, leading silence) undermine the validity of supervised benchmarks, echoing earlier warnings about silence and bitrate leakage in audio-only datasets (Müller et al., 2021; Borzi et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that a 25–30 ms leading silence yields >98% AUC on both FakeAVCeleb and AV-Deepfake1M calls into question the reported performance of numerous prior methods that were evaluated on these datasets, and suggests that the field's progress may have been partially an artifact of dataset construction. The practical recommendation to shift toward unsupervised, real-data-only training is actionable and already validated by the authors' 85.24% AUC on the official AV-Deepfake1M test set without ever seeing a fake sample. Open questions include whether additional, subtler biases exist in other audio-visual datasets, whether the alignment-based approach extends to manipulation types that do not produce audio-visual desynchronization (e.g., pure visual face swaps with intact audio), and how to standardize dataset preparation to prevent such shortcuts from entering future benchmarks.
