## Problem

Existing robust fine-tuning evaluations for VQA are limited to unimodal shifts or specific OOD types, offering no systematic framework for assessing how models handle simultaneous shifts across visual, question, and answer modalities. This paper introduces FRAMES-VQA, a benchmark that categorizes ten VQA datasets into near and far OOD settings spanning uni-modal, multi-modal, and adversarial distribution shifts, enabling controlled comparison of robust fine-tuning methods under multi-modal conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251920/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluated models (PaliGemma-3B with LoRA) take a 224×224 RGB image and a free-form question as input and generate a short answer text. Evaluation follows the VQAv2 protocol where each question is paired with 10 human-annotated answers and accuracy is computed as min(count/3, 1). The benchmark spans nine OOD test sets (IV-VQA, CV-VQA, VQA-Rephrasings, VQA-CP, VQA-CE, AdVQA, TextVQA, VizWiz, OK-VQAv2) with sample sizes ranging from 3,173 to 219,928, covering image, question, answer, multi-modal, adversarial, and far distribution shifts relative to the VQAv2 training set.

## Method

FRAMES-VQA organizes ten existing VQA datasets into ID (VQAv2), six near OOD, and three far OOD categories, each associated with a specific shift type (image, question, answer, multi-modal, adversarial, or far). Distribution shifts are quantified via Mahalanobis distance computed on uni-modal embeddings (ViT for images, BERT for questions) and joint embeddings (PaliGemma with masked tokens for f(v), f(q), f(v,q)), enabling per-modality shift measurement. Seven fine-tuning baselines (Zero-Shot, Vanilla FT, Linear Probing, LP-FT, WiSE-FT, FTP, SPD) are compared under identical PaliGemma-3B + LoRA settings. The paper further introduces a Modality Importance (MI) metric based on intra- vs. inter-modality attention ratios to analyze how fine-tuning methods redistribute attention between image and question tokens across ID and OOD samples, revealing that robust methods emphasize intra-modality attention while OOD samples show increased question-to-image attention.

## Results

On the FRAMES-VQA benchmark using PaliGemma-3B with LoRA, SPD achieves the best overall OOD average (63.55), ID accuracy (87.39), and near-OOD average (75.88), while FTP achieves the best far-OOD average (41.95) despite underfitting on ID (81.77). Vanilla fine-tuning with LoRA reaches 86.29 ID and 75.08 near-OOD, showing that VQA fine-tuning does not degrade OOD performance as in image classification. WiSE-FT performs worst among fine-tuning methods (53.48 OOD avg), confirming that weight interpolation is ineffective when the zero-shot-to-fine-tuned gap is large in VQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPD (LoRA) outperforms all other methods on ID (87.39 vs. 86.29 for Vanilla FT), near-OOD average (75.88 vs. 75.08), and overall OOD average (63.55 vs. 62.64), making it the strongest all-around robust fine-tuning method for VQA. FTP (LoRA) dominates on far-OOD datasets, achieving 41.95 average (49.12 on TextVQA, 51.07 on OK-VQA) versus 37.77 for Vanilla FT, but sacrifices 4.5 points on ID due to its stronger projection-based regularization. WiSE-FT (LoRA) underperforms even Vanilla FT across all settings (53.48 vs. 62.64 OOD avg), because the large zero-shot-to-fine-tuned gap in VQA (54.42 → 86.29 on ID) makes weight interpolation counterproductive. LP-FT (LoRA) and Linear Probing (LoRA) both trail Vanilla FT on OOD performance, confirming that constrained fine-tuning does not automatically improve robustness in the VQA setting where the pre-training and fine-tuning objectives are aligned. The zero-shot PaliGemma baseline (37.17 OOD avg) is substantially outperformed by all fine-tuning methods, indicating that task adaptation remains critical even for a strong VLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust fine-tuning literature (WiSE-FT, LP-FT, FTP, SPD, TPGM, L2-SP) from unimodal image classification to the multi-modal VQA setting, building directly on the authors' preliminary directional gradient projection work [25]. Prior VQA robustness evaluations [3, 30, 34, 53] tested model generalization across datasets but did not systematically categorize shifts into uni-modal (image, question, answer) versus multi-modal types, nor distinguish near from far OOD. FRAMES-VQA unifies ten existing VQA benchmarks under this taxonomy and introduces Mahalanobis-distance-based shift quantification across multiple embedding spaces, positioning the work as a controlled comparison of fine-tuning algorithms rather than model architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first systematic, quantified framework for evaluating how robust fine-tuning methods handle multi-modal distribution shifts in VQA, filling a gap where prior evaluations were either unimodal or ad hoc. Key open questions remain: no single method dominates across all shift types (SPD wins ID/near OOD, FTP wins far OOD), and the finding that question-to-image attention rises for OOD samples suggests models exploit cross-modal shortcuts that degrade generalization. The analysis is limited to one backbone (PaliGemma-3B) with LoRA, so whether the modality-importance and correlation findings generalize to other architectures or full fine-tuning settings is unresolved.
