## Problem

Transformer-based MIL methods for WSI classification suffer from quadratic computational complexity, while vanilla Mamba, despite its linear complexity, exhibits catastrophic information forgetting over long sequences due to its RNN-like fixed-size memory. Existing Mamba-based WSI methods further overlook WSI-specific spatial hierarchies, suboptimal local representation within groups, and lack of inter-group interaction, making direct application of Mamba to large-scale WSI classification suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250999/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted instance feature vectors (512-dim from ResNet18-ImageNet or 384-dim from ViT-S/16-SSL) corresponding to 256×256 non-overlapping patches cropped from gigapixel WSIs at 10× or 20× magnification. These instances are randomly partitioned into T groups of M instances each (e.g., M=120 or 512), and the model outputs both a bag-level class label (binary or multi-class) and per-group class probabilities that serve as auxiliary supervision signals during training.

## Method

M3amba decomposes WSI classification into intra-group and inter-group sub-tasks. Within each group, a BiMamba Block (bidirectional Mamba + linear attention) captures local instance correlations, and a Dynamic Memory Bank (DMB) stores the K most discriminative historical group features ranked by label confidence; cross-attention fuses these stored features into the current group representation to counteract information forgetting. For inter-group modeling, a second BiMamba Block aggregates global relations across all group representations while a ConvBlock preserves group-specific local features, and a Mixture of Experts (two parallel conv modules with softmax-weighted fusion) harmonizes global and local signals for the final bag prediction. Training uses a composite cross-entropy loss over bag-level and group-level predictions, with the group-level supervision weight λ annealed from 0 to 0.2 via a cosine schedule to guide the model from coarse to label-related feature exploration.

## Results

M3amba achieves 93.1% accuracy and 97.1% AUC on TCGA BRCA, 93.7% accuracy and 97.5% AUC on TCGA Lung, 92.3% accuracy on TCGA ESCA, and 80.0% F1 / 93.9% AUC on BRACS (ResNet18 features), outperforming the strongest baselines by 6.2% and 7.0% in accuracy on BRCA and Lung respectively. On the multi-class BRACS dataset, M3amba improves F1 by 20.4% over MambaMIL and 24.8% over ACMIL. The method maintains low computational cost (72.4M FLOPs, 1.24M parameters), comparable to MambaMIL (71.5M FLOPs) and far below MHIM-TransMIL (1228.6M FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3amba outperforms all 10 baselines across all four datasets. Against the strongest attention-based method ACMIL, M3amba gains +6.2% accuracy on TCGA BRCA (93.1% vs 86.9%) and +9.3% on TCGA Lung (93.7% vs 84.4%), with AUC improvements of +7.1% and +5.6% respectively. Against the best Transformer-based method MHIM-TransMIL, M3amba achieves +8.3% accuracy on Lung while reducing FLOPs from 1228.6M to 72.4M (17× reduction). Among Mamba-based baselines, M3amba surpasses MambaMIL by +6.3% accuracy on BRCA and +8.1% on Lung, and by +20.4% F1 on BRACS (80.0% vs 59.6%), indicating that the dynamic memory bank and cross-attention fusion effectively address the information-forgetting limitation of vanilla Mamba. On TCGA ESCA, M3amba leads ACMIL by +3.8% accuracy (92.3% vs 88.5%) and +4.1% AUC (98.9% vs 94.8%). The method also generalizes across feature extractors, maintaining 93.4% accuracy on TCGA Lung with ResNet50-ImageNet features where most baselines drop by 5–10%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba-based MIL line (MambaMIL, SSMMIL) by addressing two specific weaknesses of vanilla Mamba in WSI analysis: long-context information forgetting and suboptimal local representation. It departs from Transformer-based MIL methods (TransMIL, DTFD, MHIM-TransMIL) that suffer quadratic complexity, and from grouping Mamba approaches (GroupMamba) that lack inter-group interaction, by introducing a Dynamic Memory Bank with cross-attention fusion and a Mixture-of-Experts bag predictor. The architectural lineage draws on state-space models for linear-complexity sequence modeling, attention mechanisms for memory retrieval, and MoE routing for adaptive feature fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The memory bank mechanism offers a practical, low-overhead remedy for the well-known forgetting problem in RNN-like sequence models applied to very long instance sequences, and the 6–7% accuracy gains over strong baselines across four datasets with comparable FLOPs make it a compelling alternative to Transformer MIL heads. However, all experiments use frozen pre-trained features (ResNet18-ImageNet or DINO ViT), so it remains open whether the gains persist under end-to-end training or with stronger backbones. The approach is also limited to binary/multi-class bag-level classification; whether the DMB and grouping strategy transfer to WSI segmentation, detection, or survival analysis is unexplored.
