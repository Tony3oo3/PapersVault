## Problem

Existing methods for Double Incomplete Multi-View Multi-Label Classification (DIMVMLC) either cannot handle missing views and labels simultaneously or fail to properly separate view-consistent from view-specific features. Prior approaches like SIMM rely on forced orthogonality that misses nonlinear shared-specific relationships, while MTD optimizes both objectives jointly, creating a complex trade-off that hinders convergence. This paper tackles the gap of achieving effective disentanglement under arbitrary view and label missingness.

## Input / Output

The method takes n samples each with up to 6 views (DenseHue, DenseSift, GIST, HSV, RGB, LAB; dimensions 100–4096) where a random subset of views may be missing per sample, along with a binary label vector of up to 268 categories where a subset of labels may be unobserved. Missing views and labels are zero-filled, and the model operates on the variable set of available views W and available labels U per sample. It outputs a probability for each label via per-label sigmoid classifiers, with the BCE loss computed only over observed labels to avoid penalizing missing annotations.

## Method

DRLS operates in two stages under a VAE framework. In stage one, view-consistent features are learned by minimizing KL divergence between each view's approximate posterior and the multi-view joint posterior, with cross-view reconstruction forcing each view's latent code to encode shared information; the fused consistent feature is obtained via Product-of-Experts. In stage two, view-specific features are disentangled by minimizing a variational upper bound on the mutual information I(c^(v); s^(v)), where the pre-trained consistent encoder is frozen at a reduced learning rate and a joint decoder reconstructs only the self-view from concatenated consistent and specific codes. A VGAE-based label semantic embedding module uses a GIN over a conditional-probability adjacency matrix (derived from label co-occurrence) to aggregate related label embeddings and preserve label-relevance topology. The final representation modulates the fused consistent feature with a sigmoid-gated specific feature, and per-label feature importance vectors (derived from label embeddings) select discriminative dimensions before a shared classifier produces predictions.

## Results

Under the standard DIMVMLC setting (50% missing views, 50% missing labels, 70% training samples), DRLS achieves the best average ranking (Ave.R = 1.000) across all six evaluation metrics on all five datasets. On the headline AP metric, DRLS reaches 0.433 (Corel5k), 0.567 (Pascal07), 0.326 (Espgame), 0.356 (IAPRTC12), and 0.630 (Mirflickr), outperforming the second-best method (SIP or MTD) by 1.7–2.4% on AP. On AUC, DRLS achieves 0.918, 0.864, 0.862, 0.898, and 0.872 respectively, consistently leading all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRLS outperforms all eight baselines on the average ranking (Ave.R) across all five datasets, achieving a perfect 1.000 in every case. The closest competitors are SIP and MTD, which rank second on most datasets (Ave.R ≈ 1.8–2.8); on IAPRTC12, DRLS's AP of 0.356 exceeds MTD's 0.332 by 2.4 percentage points, and on Corel5k it surpasses SIP's 0.416 by 1.7 points. The shallow methods (DM2L, iMvWL, NAIML) and the complete-data-only method CDMM (imputed with view means) are consistently the weakest, with Ave.R values of 4.5–9.0. Deep learning baselines (DDINet, DICNet, MTD, SIP) clearly outperform shallow ones, confirming the advantage of deep architectures for DIMVMLC. Under the complete-data setting (Table 3), DRLS also leads on AP for Corel5k (0.551 vs. SIP's 0.520), Espgame (0.399 vs. MTD's 0.397), IAPRTC12 (0.447 vs. MTD's 0.441), and Mirflickr (0.698 vs. MTD's 0.682), while MTD edges out DRLS on Pascal07 AP (0.652 vs. 0.655) by a negligible margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRLS extends the variational autoencoder framework for multi-view learning (Antelmi et al., Wu & Goodman) by introducing a two-stage disentangling strategy: first pre-training view-consistent features via cross-view reconstruction, then minimizing the variational upper bound of mutual information (Hwang et al.) to extract view-specific features. This departs from SIMM, which uses rigid orthogonality constraints and cannot handle missing views, and from MTD, which optimizes consistent and specific features simultaneously in a single complex objective. The label semantic embedding module is a novel combination of Graph Isomorphism Networks with a Variational Graph Autoencoder (Kipf & Welling), guided by a conditional-probability adjacency matrix derived from label co-occurrence statistics, building on the label-semantic-embedding idea of Hang & Zhang.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage pre-then-disentangle strategy offers a practical recipe for separating shared and view-specific information under missing data, avoiding the optimization difficulty of joint learning. However, the method is validated only on five image datasets with six fixed views and a uniform 50% missing rate, leaving open questions about scalability to more views, non-uniform or structured missingness patterns, and non-image modalities. The relatively small contribution of the label semantic embedding module (≈0.3% AP gain in ablation) suggests that the primary gains come from the disentangled representation, and whether the GIN-based label embedding generalizes beyond the tested label counts (20–291) remains unexplored.
