## Problem

Tiny MLLMs (2–3B parameters) have been developed to reduce LLM size, but they still rely on hundreds to thousands of visual tokens, which negates their speed advantage and makes response times comparable to or slower than 7B models like LLaVA-1.5. The core gap is that existing compression approaches either lose critical visual detail (local pooling, pixel shuffle) or require heavy auxiliary components (Q-Former with a separate BERT and dedicated VL pretraining), leaving no lightweight mechanism to simultaneously preserve visual saliency and capture instruction-relevant image information.

## Input / Output

The default FlashSloth takes a 384×384 RGB image and a text instruction; the HD variant takes a 768×768 image split into four quadrants plus a low-resolution thumbnail (following LLaVA-NeXT). The SigLIP-so400m encoder produces 729 visual tokens (or 405 for HD), which are compressed to 81 (or 405) tokens by SAP before entering the Phi-2 2.7B LLM, alongside 9 learnable query tokens appended to the sequence. The model outputs a free-form text answer generated autoregressively by the LLM.

## Method

FlashSloth introduces two embedded visual compression modules. First, Spatial Attention Pooling (SAP) partitions the 729 visual tokens into 81 regions of 3×3 tokens each; a shared two-layer MLP predicts a softmax attention weight per token within each region, and a weighted sum yields one salient feature vector per region, reducing token count by 89% while preserving visually prominent content. Second, the Embedded Query (EmbQ) module pads 9 learnable tokens (initialized from the 'dot' token embedding) at the end of the input sequence; after 8 transformer layers, these queries first cross-attend to the text tokens to absorb instruction context, then cross-attend to the *uncompressed* visual tokens to retrieve instruction-relevant details, and the resulting visual features are added back to the queries via a residual connection. Unlike Q-Former, EmbQ is embedded directly inside the LLM's forward pass—no separate language model, no dedicated vision-language alignment pretraining, and only a single up-projection for the visual tokens. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: a pretraining stage that optimizes only the projector and SAP for modality alignment, followed by an SFT stage that fine-tunes the LLM and EmbQ jointly.

## Results

FlashSloth (3.2B) achieves 73.0 on MMB, 39.7 on MMMU, and 88.6 on SQA, while FlashSloth-HD reaches 75.7 on MMB, 49.0 on MM-Vet, and 91.1 on SQA — competitive with or exceeding SOTA tiny MLLMs like InternVL2 (73.2 MMB, 44.6 MM-Vet) and MiniCPM-V2 (69.6 MMB, 41.0 MM-Vet). In terms of efficiency, FlashSloth uses only 90 visual tokens (vs. 576 for LLaVA-1.5-7B, 729 for IMP, up to 1699 for InternVL2), achieving 0.72 TFLOPs (84% reduction vs. LLaVA), 0.05s response time (2–5× faster), and 11.8 throughput (82% higher than LLaVA). FlashSloth-HD narrows the gap to Qwen2-VL-2B on most benchmarks but still lags on OCR-heavy tasks (DocVQA 74.8 vs. 90.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InternVL2-2.2B, FlashSloth-HD wins on MMB (75.7 vs. 73.2), MM-Vet (49.0 vs. 44.6), MMMU (37.8 vs. 36.3), AI2D (75.3 vs. 74.1), and RealWorldQA (59.9 vs. 57.3), but loses on SQA (91.1 vs. 94.1), TextVQA (71.0 vs. 73.4), ChartQA (69.8 vs. 76.2), and DocVQA (74.8 vs. 86.9). Against Qwen2-VL-2B, FlashSloth-HD edges ahead on MMB (75.7 vs. 74.9) and AI2D (75.3 vs. 74.7) but trails on MM-Vet (49.0 vs. 49.5), MMMU (37.8 vs. 41.1), TextVQA (71.0 vs. 79.7), ChartQA (69.8 vs. 73.5), and DocVQA (74.8 vs. 90.1). Against MiniCPM-V-2, FlashSloth-HD dominates on MMB (75.7 vs. 69.6), MM-Vet (49.0 vs. 41.0), SQA (91.1 vs. 80.7), AI2D (75.3 vs. 62.9), ChartQA (69.8 vs. 59.8), DocVQA (74.8 vs. 71.9), and RealWorldQA (59.9 vs. 55.8), losing only slightly on MMMU (37.8 vs. 38.2) and TextVQA (71.0 vs. 74.1). Against older tiny MLLMs (MobileVLM-V2, Mini-Gemini, IMP, LLaVA-Phi), FlashSloth-HD achieves new SOTA on MMB, GQA, and AI2D. The base FlashSloth (non-HD) is competitive on MMB (73.0) and MMMU (39.7) but underperforms on OCR tasks (DocVQA 48.6), which the HD variant addresses by increasing input resolution to 768×768.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashSloth sits at the intersection of two active research threads: tiny MLLMs (MobileVLM, Imp, MiniCPM-V, Qwen2-VL, InternVL) and visual token compression (Q-Former, pixel shuffle, token pruning). It departs from Q-Former-style bridges by embedding the query module (EmbQ) directly within the LLM layers, eliminating the need for a separate language model and dedicated VL alignment pretraining. It also builds on pre-MLLM attention-based visual compression (e.g., TRAR, bottom-up attention) by repurposing spatial attention pooling (SAP) for token reduction, while combining it with instruction-aware querying to compensate for lost detail. Unlike inference-time pruning methods (FastV, FitPrune), FlashSloth addresses efficiency through architecture design rather than post-hoc token selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlashSloth demonstrates that a tiny MLLM can achieve competitive multimodal performance with 80–89% fewer visual tokens and 2–5× faster first-token response than both larger MLLMs and other tiny MLLMs, making real-time deployment on edge devices more feasible. The finding that only 9 learnable query tokens suffice for instruction-aware visual grounding challenges the assumption that substantial query capacity is needed for cross-modal retrieval. A clear limitation is the performance gap on high-resolution OCR tasks (DocVQA) in the base model, which the HD variant partially addresses at the cost of more tokens. Open questions include whether the embedded query mechanism generalizes to video understanding or multi-image settings, and whether further token reduction is possible without sacrificing reasoning on complex benchmarks like MMMU.
