## Problem

Self-supervised scene flow estimation relies on the assumption that nearby points on rigid objects share the same motion, but existing methods enforce this rigidity only through post-processing, extra loss terms, or hard-coded clustering rather than as an architectural inductive bias. This leads to suboptimal learning efficiency, sensitivity to segmentation errors, and inferior performance compared to what a design-level rigidity prior could achieve.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two consecutive ego-motion-compensated LiDAR scans (10 Hz, 0.1 s interval) cropped to a 102.4 × 102.4 m region with ground points removed. Internally, each scan is pillarized into a 0.2 m-resolution BEV pseudo-image; the voting space per pillar is a 20 × 20 discrete grid covering ±2 m of translation. The output is a per-point 3D translation vector (scene flow) for every point in the source scan, produced by a 4-layer MLP decoder that fuses pillar features, fused U-Net features, voting features, and per-point offsets.

## Method

VoteFlow's core contribution is a differentiable Voting Module that encodes local rigidity as an architectural inductive bias. For each non-empty source pillar, the module selects M=8 spatially nearest neighbors at time t; each neighbor then identifies N=128 candidate correspondences at t+Δt via ball query, and the cosine similarity between their pillar embeddings serves as a voting score for the implied 2D translation. All M×N votes are accumulated into a discretized 20×20 voting space, which is summarized into a continuous feature vector by two convolutional layers, allowing the downstream decoder to access the full vote distribution rather than a single argmax. The overall pipeline (Pillar Feature Net → U-Net backbone → Voting Module → MLP decoder) is trained end-to-end in a self-supervised manner using bidirectional Chamfer, dynamic, static, and HDBSCAN-cluster losses, with no ground-truth flow labels required.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 test split, VoteFlow achieves a mean dynamic normalized EPE of 0.289, outperforming the previous best self-supervised method SeFlow (0.309) by 2.0 percentage points. VoteFlow is best on Pedestrian (0.417) and Wheeled VRU (0.249) categories, and competitive on Car (0.202) and Other Vehicles (0.288). Inference latency is approximately 25.6 ms per sample on an A100 GPU. On the Waymo Open validation split, VoteFlow trained on Argoverse 2 alone (cross-dataset) achieves 0.142 m FD EPE, outperforming SeFlow's cross-dataset result (0.155 m) without any Waymo-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoteFlow outperforms SeFlow [51], the strongest self-supervised baseline, by 2.0 pp in mean dynamic normalized EPE (0.289 vs. 0.309), with the largest gains on Pedestrian (0.417 vs. 0.463, −4.6 pp) and Wheeled VRU (0.249 vs. 0.267, −1.8 pp). Against ICP-Flow [25], which enforces rigidity via clustering, VoteFlow wins on Pedestrian (0.417 vs. 0.435) and Wheeled VRU (0.249 vs. 0.363) but trails slightly on Car (0.202 vs. 0.195). VoteFlow clearly surpasses test-time optimization methods NSFP [23] (0.422) and FastNSF [24] (0.383), as well as ZeroFlow [41] (0.594), by large margins. Among supervised baselines, Flow4D [17] remains the strongest at 0.174 mean dynamic EPE, leaving an 11.5 pp gap to VoteFlow. On Waymo Open, VoteFlow trained only on Argoverse 2 achieves 0.142 m FD EPE, beating SeFlow's cross-dataset result (0.155 m) and even SeFlow trained on Waymo (0.151 m) on foreground dynamic points, demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoteFlow extends the pillar-based feed-forward scene flow framework established by ZeroFlow, FastFlow3D, DeFlow, and SeFlow, adopting their U-Net backbone and pillarization pipeline. Its key departure is the differentiable Voting Module, which encodes local motion rigidity as an architectural inductive bias rather than relying on auxiliary loss terms (as in SeFlow or Multi-body NSF) or post-hoc clustering/ICP alignment (as in ICP-Flow). The voting mechanism draws on the Hough voting tradition in point cloud processing but replaces hand-crafted features with learned pillar embeddings, making the rigidity prior end-to-end optimizable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By baking rigidity into the network architecture, VoteFlow offers a cleaner and more data-efficient path to consistent per-object flow than loss-based or clustering-based alternatives, and its 25.6 ms inference latency makes it practical for online autonomous driving. The cross-dataset result—outperforming a Waymo-trained SeFlow while trained only on Argoverse 2—suggests the rigidity prior generalizes well, though the 11.5%pt gap to the best supervised method (Flow4D) remains substantial. Open questions include extending the voting space to rotations, handling longer time intervals where the voting grid grows quadratically, and whether multi-scan temporal fusion (as in Flow4D) can close the remaining gap to supervised performance.
