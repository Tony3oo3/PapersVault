## Problem

DPO-based algorithms for hallucination mitigation in LVLMs rely on preference pairs whose preferred responses are often off-policy relative to the reference policy, making them effectively unlearnable due to the reverse KL-divergence constraint. The paper identifies this on-policy property as the critical, previously overlooked factor that explains why existing methods (Hallucination Injection, Hallucination Recognition, Self Evolution) exhibit large performance gaps despite superficially similar DPO formulations.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a text prompt and produces a free-form natural language response (image caption or QA answer). During training, GPT-4V provides sentence-level hallucination severity scores (S_hal) and error-type classifications (S_img) on model-generated responses, along with minimally revised text. For the Image Focus component, a distorted image variant is created by randomly masking 30% of pixels, and preference pairs are formed between responses conditioned on the original versus distorted image.

## Method

OPA-DPO introduces a four-step pipeline: (1) collect responses from the base policy, (2) use GPT-4V to identify hallucinations and produce minimally revised responses, (3) perform LoRA-SFT on both ground-truth and revised responses to create an on-policy reference policy π_OPA, and (4) train DPO from π_OPA with a three-component loss. The loss combines hallucination-weighted language corrections (focusing updates on erroneous sentences), an image-focus mechanism that penalizes responses conditioned on distorted images (weighted by image-recognition error scores), and anchored preference terms that prevent the preferred response likelihood from collapsing. The key theoretical insight (Fact 1) is that any response with near-zero probability under the reference policy induces infinite KL-divergence, making it unlearnable by DPO; the LoRA-SFT step resolves this by shifting the reference policy's support to include the expert-revised responses before DPO training begins.

## Results

With only 4.8k training samples, OPA-DPO achieves SOTA hallucination reduction on LLaVA-1.5-7B: AMBER CHAIR of 2.2 (vs. 3.0 for RLAIF-V with 16k data), AMBER HalRate of 11.6% (vs. 16.2%), and Object-Hal CHAIRs of 13.00 (vs. 16.00). For LLaVA-1.5-13B, OPA-DPO reaches AMBER CHAIR 2.4, HalRate 12.8%, and Object-Hal CHAIRs 16.33, outperforming HSA-DPO (8k data) on HalRate. On POPE Adversarial, OPA-DPO achieves 95.61% precision (7B) and 96.31% precision (13B), the highest among all compared methods. The paper reports an additional 13.26% reduction in AMBER hallucination rate and 5.39% on Object-Hal relative to the previous SOTA (RLAIF-V, 16k data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPA-DPO (4.8k data) decisively outperforms the previous SOTA RLAIF-V (16k data) on AMBER HalRate (11.6% vs. 16.2%), AMBER CHAIR (2.2 vs. 3.0), and Object-Hal CHAIRs (13.00 vs. 16.00) for the 7B model, while using only 30% of the training data. Against mDPO (10k), OPA-DPO nearly halves AMBER HalRate (11.6% vs. 24.5%) and Object-Hal CHAIRs (13.00 vs. 35.70). Compared to hallucination-injection methods HALVA (21.5k) and POVID (17k), OPA-DPO achieves substantially lower hallucination rates (e.g., AMBER HalRate 11.6% vs. 32.2% and 34.3% respectively) with far less data. The PPO-based LLaVA-RLHF (122k) is the weakest baseline, with AMBER HalRate of 46.6%—worse than the untrained base model—highlighting the difficulty of PPO in this setting. For the 13B model, OPA-DPO edges out HSA-DPO (8k) on AMBER HalRate (12.8% vs. 13.4%) and achieves the best POPE precision (96.31%), though HSA-DPO has a marginally lower AMBER CHAIR (2.1 vs. 2.4). Overall, OPA-DPO achieves the best or near-best hallucination metric in 50% of columns for 7B and 70% for 13B, while the OPA-SFT step alone (without DPO) already provides meaningful gains, confirming the on-policy alignment as the critical component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al.) and mDPO (Wang et al.) for multimodal hallucination mitigation by identifying a previously overlooked theoretical constraint: DPO's reverse KL-divergence term makes it fundamentally unable to learn strictly off-policy preferred responses. It positions itself against three existing paradigms for DPO-based hallucination reduction—Hallucination Injection (POVID, HALVA), Hallucination Recognition (RLHF-V, HA-DPO, HSA-DPO), and Self Evolution (RLAIF-V)—arguing that the first two suffer from off-policy preferred or rejected responses, while the third lacks expert correction. The novel OPA step (LoRA-SFT on GPT-4V-revised responses before DPO) bridges the gap between expert-guided correction and on-policy data, combining strengths of Methods 2 and 3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The on-policy requirement for DPO is a broadly applicable insight that could influence how preference optimization is applied beyond hallucination mitigation, potentially affecting any DPO-based alignment pipeline where preferred responses are externally generated. The data efficiency result (4.8k samples matching or exceeding 16k-sample baselines) is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. However, the approach depends on GPT-4V as a correction oracle, and the resulting conservative generation strategy (reduced coverage, fewer affirmative answers) represents a trade-off that may not suit all downstream applications. Open questions include whether the OPA step generalizes to other preference-optimization tasks and whether the GPT-4V dependency can be replaced with cheaper or self-generated feedback.
