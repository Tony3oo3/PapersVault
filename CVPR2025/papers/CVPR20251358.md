## Problem

Fine-tuning large Vision-Language Models in federated learning is constrained by heterogeneous client resources, modalities, and data distributions, yet existing selective PEFT-FL methods either assume homogeneous compute or select layers based solely on local client data without considering global optimization needs. No prior work jointly addresses client-specific layer importance and inter-client layer diversity, leading to suboptimal convergence and underutilization of available resources across the federation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251358/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client provides a medical image (one of 9 modalities: CT, Ultrasound, Dermatoscopy, Fundus, Histology, Microscopy, OCT, X-Ray, or others) paired with a text question or radiology report, and specifies a computational budget of 2, 4, or 6 trainable layers. The server produces per-client layer selection masks (which specific layers to update) and the aggregated global model; clients produce VQA answers or multi-label disease predictions. The layer selection is re-computed every round, making the output a dynamic, round-varying set of layer updates rather than a fixed configuration.

## Method

F³OCUS employs a two-step 'define and refine' procedure each round. At the client level, layer importance scores are computed from the principal eigenvalue of the layerwise Neural Tangent Kernel (LNTK), which theoretically quantifies each layer's alignment with the client's data distribution and its contribution to loss reduction. At the server level, a data-free multi-objective meta-heuristic (NSGA-II, ABC, ACO, SA, or MOPSO) jointly maximizes the cumulative client-specific importance scores while minimizing the variance of the layer-selection histogram across clients, subject to per-client layer budget constraints. This server-side optimization explores the Pareto front of the two conflicting objectives without requiring any training data, and the resulting refined layer ranks are sent back to clients for the next round of local training. The paper provides convergence proofs showing that minimizing both the LNTK-based selection noise and the variance of selection count across clients tightens the convergence bound to a neighborhood of the standard FL stationary point.

## Results

On the heterogeneous-resource layer selection setting (Table 5), F³OCUS achieves 84.25% (ViLT) and 80.46% (BLIP) on Task 2 (modality gap), 79.65% and 78.95% on Task 3 (Ultra-MedVQA), and 86.40% and 89.69% on Task 5 (MIMIC), consistently outperforming the best baseline (SPT) by 4–5 points. On adapter selection (Table 2), F³OCUS reaches 81.31% (ViLT) on Task 2 and 85.28% (ViLT) on Task 6, surpassing the closest SOTA (SPT) by 5.81% and 5.15% respectively. The paper reports that F³OCUS outperforms the best pruning baseline by 5.35% (homogeneous) and 5.33% (heterogeneous) across all architectures, while LNTK alone already exceeds the best pruning method by 2.11–2.30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F³OCUS outperforms all 29 baselines across every task and VLM architecture tested. Among the 7 pruning methods (FD, Magnitude, FishMask, GradFlow, GraSP, SNIP, Synflow), the best is Synflow at 77.95% (ViLT, Task 2), while F³OCUS reaches 84.25%—a 6.3-point gap. Among the 4 layer-selection methods (Last, RGN, Fedselect, SPT), SPT is the strongest at 77.03% (ViLT, Task 2), still 7.2 points below F³OCUS. For PEFT baselines (LN, LoRA, Bias, PromptFL, FedDAT), F³OCUS matches or exceeds all while using only 9.7 MBits of communication vs. 28.8 MBits for full adapters and 86.1 MBits for FedDAT. Against the 12 personalized FL methods (perFedavg, MetaFed, FedPAC, FedAS, FLUTE, FedALA, FedProto, FedRod, FedAP, FedFomo, FedRep, Fedper), F³OCUS leads by 4–9 points on every task (e.g., 85.3% vs. 78.5% for FedAS on Task 6). The only method that approaches F³OCUS is LNTK (client-level only, without server optimization), which trails by 3.0–3.2 points, confirming the value of the multi-objective meta-heuristic refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the layerwise Neural Tangent Kernel (LNTK) framework from Shi et al. [64], repurposing the principal eigenvalue magnitude as a client-specific layer importance score for selective fine-tuning, and operates within the FedAvg federated learning paradigm [55]. It departs from prior FL+PEFT works (FedTune, FedSelect, SPT) that naively combine centralized layer-selection or pruning methods with federated aggregation by introducing a two-step 'define and refine' procedure: client-level LNTK-based scoring followed by server-level multi-objective meta-heuristic optimization that jointly maximizes cumulative importance while minimizing inter-client layer selection variance. The use of data-free meta-heuristic algorithms (NSGA-II, ABC, ACO, SA, MOPSO) for the server-side combinatorial layer assignment problem is a notable methodological departure from learned or greedy approaches, motivated by the absence of training data on the server and the high-dimensional discrete search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: fine-tuning large VLMs in federated settings where clients have heterogeneous computational budgets, modalities, and data distributions—conditions that prior FL+PEFT works largely ignore. The released Ultra-MedVQA dataset (707K VQA triplets, 9 modalities, 12 anatomies) is a significant resource for medical VLM research. Open questions include whether the meta-heuristic server optimization can be replaced by learned surrogates as model scale grows, how the approach extends to sub-layer or parameter-level granularity, and whether the convergence guarantees (which maintain a non-zero error floor due to layer selection noise) can be tightened. The comparable performance across all five meta-heuristic algorithms suggests the problem structure may be relatively forgiving, but also raises the question of whether simpler heuristics could suffice.
