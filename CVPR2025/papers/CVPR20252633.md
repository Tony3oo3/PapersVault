## Problem

Existing diffusion-based virtual try-on methods assume hands are positioned at the sides of the body and do not occlude garments, a constraint that fails in real-world scenarios where hands frequently overlap clothing. This hand occlusion causes severe degradation in try-on quality, as prior approaches either rely on imperfect parsing to preserve hand regions (leaving artifacts) or apply post-processing hand refinement that cannot reconstruct missing or malformed hands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252633/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target person image and a garment image (both at 1024×768 resolution) and produces a realistic outfitted image. The person image is first masked via human parsing and pose estimation to create a garment-agnostic representation, reformulating the task as example-based inpainting. Beyond the raw images, the pipeline derives auxiliary conditioning signals including DWPose skeletons, DensePose semantic maps, HaMeR 3D hand mesh parameters, DINOv2 hand-region features, and CLIP garment tokens.

## Method

VTON-HandFit extends Stable Diffusion v1.5 with two key modules: a Hand-Pose Aggregation Net (ControlNet-based) that fuses DWPose, DensePose, and 3D hand depth maps through zero-multilayer convolutions with an adaptive hand-weight, and a Hand-feature Disentanglement Embedding that splits hand priors into structure-parametric features (MANO vertices via BPS, joint rotations as 6D vectors, hand type) and visual-appearance features (DINOv2 embeddings of cropped hand regions). A mask cross-attention mechanism constrains these hand embeddings to operate exclusively within the hand mask area, preventing interference with garment features. A hand-canny constraint loss applied at low timesteps sharpens structural edges in the hand region. Training proceeds in two phases: first the Hand-Pose Net and disentanglement modules are trained with other components frozen, then the TryonNet and GarmentNet are fine-tuned jointly.

## Results

On the Handfit-3K dataset (specifically designed for hand-occlusion evaluation), VTON-HandFit achieves FID 5.60, KID 0.49, MPJPE 25.14, FID-H 7.06, and KID-H 1.25 in the paired setting, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline IDM-VTON (FID 7.72, KID 2.13, MPJPE 48.64, FID-H 11.54, KID-H 3.97). On the public VITON-HD dataset, VTON-HandFit reaches SSIM 0.885, LPIPS 0.073, FID 5.96, KID 0.97 (paired), and on DressCode it achieves SSIM 0.940, LPIPS 0.044, FID 2.87, KID 0.80. The method also leads in unpaired settings across all three datasets, with the largest gains in hand-region metrics (FID-H, KID-H) and joint-position accuracy (MPJPE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTON-HandFit outperforms all five baselines (LaDI-VTON, StableVTON, OOTDiffusion, IDM-VTON, CatVTON) on every metric across all three datasets. The most dramatic gains appear in hand-specific metrics on Handfit-3K: MPJPE drops from 48.6 (IDM-VTON) to 25.1, FID-H from 11.5 to 7.1, and KID-H from 4.0 to 1.25, indicating far more accurate hand geometry and appearance. On the public VITON-HD dataset, VTON-HandFit improves FID from 6.34 (IDM-VTON) to 5.96 and KID from 1.32 to 0.97, while on DressCode it achieves the best SSIM (0.940 vs. 0.923 for IDM-VTON) and FID (2.87 vs. 3.80). CatVTON, which uses a parsing model to retain hand regions, suffers from residual artifacts and background leakage, reflected in its higher FID (10.70) and KID (4.26) on Handfit-3K. The post-processed variant IDM-VTON* (with HandRefiner) actually degrades performance (FID 4.92 vs. 3.80 on DressCode), confirming that post-hoc hand correction is inferior to the proposed integrated hand-prior embedding approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based virtual try-on line (Stable Diffusion, TryOnDiffusion, IDM-VTON, CatVTON) by integrating hand reconstruction directly into the generation pipeline rather than relying on parsing-based hand preservation or post-processing (HandRefiner). Architecturally, it builds on ControlNet's zero-convolution paradigm for the Hand-Pose Aggregation Net and introduces a hand-feature disentanglement strategy that separates 3D structural priors (MANO parameters via HaMeR) from visual appearance features (DINOv2), injecting them into different network branches via masked cross-attention. The key departure from prior VTON work is treating hand occlusion as a first-class generation problem rather than an inpainting artifact to be patched after the fact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hand occlusion is a pervasive real-world scenario in e-commerce and digital-human applications that prior VTON methods largely ignore, and this paper provides both a targeted solution and the first dedicated benchmark (Handfit-3K) for evaluating it. The finding that post-processing hand refinement (HandRefiner) actually degrades overall metrics compared to the unprocessed baseline is a useful caution against modular patching approaches. Open questions include whether the disentanglement strategy generalizes to other body-part occlusions (e.g., hair, accessories), whether the reliance on multiple external models (HaMeR, DINOv2, DWPose, DensePose) limits deployment efficiency, and whether the 3,620-image test set is sufficient for robust generalization claims.
