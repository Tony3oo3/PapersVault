## Problem

INR training is computationally intensive due to dense forward passes over all coordinates at every iteration, and spectral bias further impedes high-frequency detail fitting. Existing sampling-based acceleration methods either rely on static signal structures (edges, frequency priors) that ignore evolving network dynamics, or employ expensive greedy coordinate-selection algorithms, limiting their efficiency gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250738/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a signal S = (x, y) where x ∈ R^m are spatial coordinates (e.g., 2D pixel positions) and y ∈ R^n are target attributes (e.g., RGB values), and produces a trained MLP F_θ that maps coordinates to attributes. At each iteration, only a sparse subset z_t ⊂ x (controlled by a constant ratio β or a step-wise schedule from 0.2N to N) is fed through the network, reducing forward passes from |x| to |z_t| = (1+α)k. The Laplacian-based cross-frequency loss requires cached full-volume reconstructions from the last key iteration to compute spatial convolution filters over the sparsified coordinates.

## Method

EVOS redefines three evolutionary-algorithm components for INR coordinate selection: (1) Sparse Fitness Evaluation computes reconstruction error D(F_θ(x), y) only at key iterations whose frequency increases linearly over training (interval τ − θt/T), caching results for intermediate steps; (2) Frequency-Guided Crossover selects two parent sets—one by L2 error (low-frequency preference) and one by Laplacian error (high-frequency preference)—then generates offspring by preserving cross-frequency intersections and stochastically sampling the remainder in proportion to current reconstruction quality per band; (3) Augmented Unbiased Mutation uniformly samples αk non-selected coordinates into the offspring to prevent deterministic selection bias from cached fitness. A cross-frequency supervision loss (L2 + λ_h · Laplacian) is applied to the mutated subset, and the network is updated via standard backpropagation on this sparsified batch, achieving 48%–66% training-time reduction while improving PSNR over full-data training.

## Results

On DIV2K with a 3×256 SIREN MLP, EVOS achieves 38.43 dB PSNR and 0.968 SSIM at 5k iterations (step-wise scheduler), surpassing standard full-coordinate training (37.10 dB PSNR, 0.964 SSIM) while reducing training time from 180.45 s to 108.60 s (39.82% reduction). When evaluated by time-to-target-PSNR, EVOS requires only 4.73 s, 12.48 s, and 29.73 s to reach 25, 30, and 35 dB respectively, corresponding to 47.68%, 59.41%, and 66.46% reductions over standard training. The method also outperforms the closest sampling-based competitor INT (incre.)† by 1.26 dB PSNR at 5k iterations while being 12.7% faster (108.60 s vs. 124.35 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVOS outperforms all five sampling-based baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and training time) at 5k iterations. Against the closest competitor INT (incre.)†, EVOS gains 1.26 dB PSNR (38.43 vs. 37.17) and 0.006 SSIM while being 12.7% faster (108.60 s vs. 124.35 s); the paper attributes INT's instability to abrupt reductions in MLP evaluation frequency inherent to its greedy algorithm. EGRA and Expansive Supervision, which rely on static image priors (edge detection, frequency priors), achieve competitive early-stage speed but degrade in later training, yielding 1.35–1.48 dB lower PSNR than EVOS at 5k iterations. Soft Mining, originally designed for NeRF's vast 3D sampling space, shows the weakest performance among baselines (35.31 dB PSNR, 0.045 LPIPS), confirming its incompatibility with 2D image INR tasks. Uniform Sampling, while fastest in wall-clock time (94.13 s), sacrifices 2.32 dB PSNR relative to EVOS, demonstrating that random subsampling cannot match the quality of fitness-guided selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVOS positions itself within the sampling-based INR acceleration paradigm, departing from prior methods (EGRA, Expansive Supervision, Soft Mining, INT) that rely on static signal priors or greedy coordinate selection. Its conceptual novelty lies in repurposing evolutionary algorithm machinery—fitness evaluation, crossover, mutation—for a fundamentally different objective: adaptive sample selection for training acceleration rather than iterative solution optimization. The frequency-guided crossover specifically addresses spectral bias by balancing low- and high-frequency coordinate selection, while the sparse fitness evaluation with cached results avoids the per-iteration forward passes that would make naive evolutionary selection prohibitively expensive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 48–66% training time reduction is practically significant for INR adoption in compression, inverse imaging, and simulation pipelines where encoding latency is a bottleneck. More conceptually, the finding that strategic sparsification can *improve* reconstruction quality over full-data training (e.g., 37.49 vs. 37.10 PSNR at 5k iterations) challenges the assumption that exhaustive data utilization is always optimal in overfitting scenarios, suggesting sample-weighting benefits extend beyond generalization. Limitations include evaluation primarily on 2D DIV2K images, modest quality gains (sub-1 dB in most comparisons), and the evolutionary framing, while creative, may be more decorative than mechanistically essential—the core contribution is adaptive frequency-balanced sampling with cached fitness.
