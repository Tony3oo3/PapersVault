## Problem

Existing feed-forward single-view 3D generation methods that learn 3D Gaussians from multi-view diffusion images produce distorted and blurry objects due to two jointly-caused issues: the inherent geometric ambiguity in 2D RGB images and the spatially unstructured nature of 3D Gaussians, which prevents effective 3D correlation learning. Prior methods also rely solely on RGB features, missing the explicit geometric cues that surface normals provide for recovering fine structural details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251999/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and internally generates 8 multi-view RGB and normal image pairs via Wonder3D at diverse camera poses. These are lifted into a 32×32×32 voxel grid via DINO ViT features and Plücker ray embeddings, and the final output is a set of 2D Gaussian primitives (center, 2D scaling, rotation quaternion, opacity, SH coefficients) constrained within the voxel grid, from which novel-view RGB images, depth maps, normal maps, and optionally textured meshes can be rendered.

## Method

GS-RGBN introduces a hybrid Voxel-Gaussian representation where each 2D Gaussian is anchored to a voxel in a 3D grid containing projected 2D image features, enabling standard 3D convolutions to capture spatial correlations among neighboring Gaussians for generalizable learning. A Cross-Volume Fusion (CVF) module fuses RGB and normal volumetric features through four voxel residual blocks for downsampling, two cross-attention blocks (RGB-guided and normal-guided) for complementary semantic-geometric aggregation, and a self-attention block for balanced weighting, producing a fine-grained RGBN volume. Per-voxel 2D Gaussian attributes are decoded by MLPs from the fused volume, with a small offset allowing each Gaussian to shift slightly from its voxel center. Training is supervised by L1+LPIPS color loss, L1 depth loss, and a regularization loss combining self-supervised distortion and normal consistency terms, all optimized end-to-end with AdamW over ~6.5 days on 4×A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) dataset, GS-RGBN achieves a PSNR of 23.02 dB, SSIM of 0.873, and LPIPS of 0.135 for novel view synthesis, outperforming the second-best method (DreamGaussian) by 5.59 dB in PSNR, 0.063 in SSIM, and 0.064 in LPIPS. For geometry reconstruction, GS-RGBN attains a Chamfer Distance of 27.49×10⁻³, more than 2× better than the next-best TriplaneGaussian (58.74×10⁻³). Inference is fast: generating multi-view images takes 4.31 s (same as Wonder3D) and rendering takes only 0.20 s, comparable to other feed-forward Gaussian methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-RGBN decisively outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against DreamGaussian (optimization-based SDS), it gains +5.59 dB PSNR and reduces Chamfer Distance from 205.23 to 27.49, while also being orders of magnitude faster (0.20 s vs. 28.32 s rendering). Against the feed-forward Gaussian methods LGM and TriplaneGaussian, GS-RGBN improves PSNR by ~5.9 dB and ~6.3 dB respectively, and halves the Chamfer Distance of TriplaneGaussian (27.49 vs. 58.74), confirming that the hybrid Voxel-Gaussian structure and RGBN fusion are critical for geometry fidelity. Compared to One-2-3-45 and Wonder3D (which rely on NeRF/mesh representations), GS-RGBN achieves substantially higher PSNR (23.02 vs. 15.20/16.35) and avoids their slow per-shape optimization (21.36 s / 6.05 min rendering). The only trade-off is a slightly longer multi-view generation time (4.31 s) than LGM (2.45 s), attributed to using more input views (8 vs. 4) for the voxel grid construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

GS-RGBN extends the feed-forward single-view 3D reconstruction paradigm pioneered by LRM and followed by LGM and TriplaneGaussian, but departs from their unstructured Gaussian or triplane representations by introducing a hybrid Voxel-Gaussian model that constrains each 2D Gaussian within a 3D voxel grid, enabling 3D convolutions to capture spatial correlations. It builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting for geometrically accurate surface representation and on Wonder3D as the multi-view diffusion backbone, while introducing a cross-volume fusion module that jointly exploits RGB semantic features and normal geometric features—something prior feed-forward methods (which use RGB-only) do not. The Plücker ray embedding and DINO feature extraction follow established conventions from LGM and LARA, positioning the paper as an architectural refinement within the established feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured Voxel-Gaussian representation addresses a recognized weakness of unstructured Gaussian splatting in feed-forward settings—namely, the lack of spatial regularization that leads to distorted geometry and blurry textures—and the 5.6 dB PSNR gap over the next-best feed-forward method (LGM) suggests this structural constraint is a meaningful lever for the field. However, the method remains heavily dependent on the quality and consistency of the upstream MVD model (Wonder3D), and the fixed voxel grid limits scalability to large scenes, motivating the authors' stated future work on octree-based representations. An open question is whether the Voxel-Gaussian inductive bias generalizes beyond object-scale reconstruction to scene-level or dynamic content, and whether the normal-fusion strategy can be replaced by learned geometric priors that do not require explicit normal estimation from the MVD model.
