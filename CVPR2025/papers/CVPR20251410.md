## Problem

Existing Masked Video Modeling methods face two key limitations: random temporal sampling of a single auxiliary frame introduces high reconstruction uncertainty (multiple valid predictions from one reference), and pixel-space reconstruction forces the model to retain excessive low-level details, hindering the learning of compact, high-level semantic representations needed for downstream video tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251410/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three frames sampled from a video clip: a masked current frame (224×224 global or 96×96 local views, with 10%–50% of patches masked) fed to the student branch, and unmasked past and future frames fed to an auxiliary branch. The output is a set of patch-level latent representations (N×d, where N = ⌈H×W/p²⌉) from the ViT encoder, which can be directly used to initialize downstream tasks such as video object segmentation, semantic part propagation, and human pose propagation without fine-tuning.

## Method

T-CoRe introduces a sandwich sampling strategy that selects one past and one future frame around the current frame, reducing reconstruction uncertainty via a two-side-squeezing mechanism. A Patch Matching Module (PMM) then retrieves masked patch representations from the auxiliary frames in latent space using cross-attention (masked patches as queries, auxiliary embeddings as keys/values), followed by self-attention and an FFN for spatial refinement. The overall architecture extends DINO's two-branch self-distillation with this auxiliary branch: the student's past-guided and future-guided reconstructions are each aligned to the EMA teacher's unmasked representations via cross-entropy losses, and an additional temporal squeezing loss (L_pf) enforces consistency between the two reconstructions. Frame-level DINO and KoLeo losses are combined with the patch-level losses to jointly optimize global and local representations, all trained in latent space rather than pixel space to promote information compression.

## Results

On Kinetics-400 pre-training with ViT-S/16 (400 epochs), T-CoRe achieves 64.7 J&Fm on DAVIS-2017, 37.8 mIoU on VIP, and 75.2 PCK@0.2 on JHMDB, outperforming the strongest latent-space baseline DINO v2 (63.0 J&Fm, 37.0 mIoU, 74.8 PCK@0.2) by 1.7%, 0.8%, and 0.4% respectively. With a larger ViT-B/16 backbone (200 epochs), T-CoRe reaches 66.4 J&Fm on DAVIS-2017, surpassing DINO v2 (64.9) by 1.5% and RSP (60.5, 400 epochs) by 5.9%. Compared to pixel-space masked modeling methods, T-CoRe gains at least 4.6% J&Fm, 4.0% mIoU, and 1.2% PCK@0.2, demonstrating the benefit of latent-space reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-CoRe consistently outperforms all 16 compared baselines across all three downstream tasks and both pre-training settings. Against the strongest latent-space competitors, T-CoRe beats DINO v2 by 1.7% J&Fm (64.7 vs. 63.0) and iBOT by 1.2% J&Fm (64.7 vs. 63.5) on DAVIS-2017, while also leading on VIP mIoU (37.8 vs. 37.0/37.4) and JHMDB PCK@0.2 (75.2 vs. 74.8/74.2). Against pixel-space masked modeling methods, the gap is substantially larger: T-CoRe surpasses RSP (the best pixel-space method) by 4.6% J&Fm and 4.0% mIoU, and outperforms SiamMAE by 6.8% J&Fm. On the larger ViT-B/16 backbone (Table 2), T-CoRe achieves 66.4 J&Fm in only 200 epochs, exceeding DINO v2 (64.9, 200 epochs) by 1.5% and RSP (60.5, 400 epochs) by 5.9%, demonstrating both superior representation quality and training efficiency. Notably, T-CoRe also surpasses the supervised ResNet50 baseline (66.0 J&Fm) on DAVIS-2017 under ImageNet-1k pre-training, and matches or exceeds supervised performance on VIP mIoU (39.7 vs. 39.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

T-CoRe extends the DINO/DINO v2 self-distillation architecture for latent-space reconstruction to the video domain, departing from pixel-space masked video modeling methods (MAE, VideoMAE, SiamMAE, CropMAE, RSP) that the authors argue overfit to low-level details. The key conceptual departure is the sandwich sampling strategy, which conditions reconstruction on both a past and a future frame rather than a single random frame, reducing temporal uncertainty in a two-side-squeezing manner. The Patch Matching Module (PMM) with cross-attention for retrieving masked patches from auxiliary frames, combined with a temporal squeezing loss (L_pf) that aligns past- and future-guided reconstructions, represents the architectural novelty on top of the iBOT/DINO v2 self-distillation backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical pain points in video MVM—temporal sampling uncertainty and insufficient information compression—with a clean, modular solution that is easy to integrate into existing self-distillation pipelines. The consistent 1–2% gains over DINO v2 across three dense propagation tasks (DAVIS-2017, VIP, JHMDB) and across backbones (ViT-S through ViT-L) suggest the sandwich + PMM recipe is robust rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video-level tasks (action recognition, retrieval) where temporal context spans many more frames, whether the k-NN trick for ImageNet pre-training is a viable long-term substitute for true video data, and whether the sandwich offset range [α, β] needs to be adapted per video length or motion regime.
