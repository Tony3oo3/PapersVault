## Problem

Existing MLLMs lack user-specific knowledge (e.g., a user's pet's name or personal contacts), and prior personalization methods like MyVLM and Yo'LLaVA require multiple labeled images per concept, large numbers of negative images, and continuous model retraining whenever new concepts are added. This makes them impractical for dynamic, real-world personalization where users need to add or edit concepts on the fly with minimal data and limited local compute.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes an RGB image (or a text-only query) plus a textual instruction, and additionally queries an external key-value database of personal concepts (each stored as a cropped avatar image and a short text description). An open-vocabulary detector (YOLO-World) identifies regions of interest in the input image, which are then matched against the database via CLIP embeddings and FAISS similarity search; the top-K concepts (K=2 for LLaVA, K=3 for Phi3-V, constrained by context length) along with the original image and query are fed to the MLLM. Outputs include personalized image captions, free-form text answers to visual or text-only questions, and binary recognition responses indicating whether a named concept appears in the image.

## Method

RAP operates in three stages: (1) Remember — a key-value database stores each personal concept as a visual feature (CLIP embedding of its avatar image) paired with a name and description; (2) Retrieve — YOLO-World detects candidate regions in the input image, and their CLIP embeddings are matched against database keys via Euclidean distance to select the top-K relevant concepts (text-name retrieval also supports text-only queries); (3) Generate — retrieved concept images are encoded by a vision encoder and projected into language tokens, concatenated with their text descriptions and the original query tokens, then fed to the MLLM for generation. Crucially, the detector and retriever remain frozen; only the MLLM (LLaVA-1.5-13B or Phi3-V-3.8B, trained with LoRA) is fine-tuned via instruction tuning on a large-scale dataset curated with Gemini-1.5 annotations from RefCOCO, Object365, TAO, CustomConcept101, ILSVRC2015-VID, and CelebA, augmented with novel-view synthesis (Wonder3D, SiTH) and negative noise samples to teach the model to filter irrelevant retrieved concepts. This one-time pretraining enables the model to generalize to infinite new user concepts at inference time simply by updating the external database, without any further training.

## Results

On personalized image captioning, RAP-LLaVA achieves an F1-score of 94.97 (Recall 93.51, Precision 96.47), substantially outperforming MyVLM-LLaVA (F1 85.50) and LLaVA-LoRA (F1 87.82). On the Yo'LLaVA QA benchmark, RAP-LLaVA attains a weighted QA accuracy of 0.936 and a weighted visual recognition score of 0.980 using only 1 image per concept, surpassing GPT-4V+Prompt (0.924 and 0.901) which requires 1–5 reference images. The smaller RAP-Phi3-V model also achieves competitive results (weighted QA 0.896, weighted recognition 0.955) while being roughly 3.4× smaller.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAP-LLaVA outperforms all fine-tuning-based baselines (LLaVA-LoRA, MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA) on text-only QA (0.938 vs. 0.583–0.615, N/A, 0.883) and negative visual recognition (0.982 vs. 0.444–0.662, 0.845, 0.898), while requiring only 1 image per concept versus 5 for the competitors. Against GPT-4V+Prompt, RAP-LLaVA wins on visual QA (0.935 vs. 0.866) and weighted recognition (0.980 vs. 0.901) with a single reference image, though GPT-4V retains an edge on text-only QA (0.982 vs. 0.938). On image captioning (Table 3), RAP-LLaVA's F1 of 94.97 exceeds MyVLM-LLaVA (85.50) by ~9.5 points and LLaVA-LoRA (87.82) by ~7 points, with the gap driven primarily by higher recall (93.51 vs. 84.65/82.97). The vanilla LLaVA+Retriever baseline collapses on negative recognition (0.025), confirming that retrieval alone without specialized training is insufficient. RAP-Phi3-V, despite being a 3.8B model, matches or exceeds the 13B RAP-LLaVA on visual QA (0.941 vs. 0.935) and negative recognition (0.988 vs. 0.982), demonstrating strong data efficiency of the proposed dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for the personalization of multimodal LLMs, a task previously addressed by MyVLM (external classification heads + concept embeddings) and Yo'LLaVA (learned special tokens per concept), both of which require model retraining and large numbers of labeled images for each new concept. The key conceptual departure is treating personal knowledge as an external, editable database queried at inference time via a frozen detector-retriever pipeline, rather than baking concept knowledge into model parameters. The authors explicitly note that no prior work applies RAG to MLLM personalization, positioning RAP as a new methodological bridge between the RAG literature (DPR, MuRAG, Self-RAG) and the personalized VLM line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's practical advantage—real-time concept editing by simply updating a database without any retraining—addresses a genuine deployment bottleneck for personalized assistants on resource-constrained devices. The dramatic ablation showing that removing negative training samples collapses precision by 38 points highlights an underappreciated challenge: teaching MLLMs to ignore irrelevant retrieved context. Open questions include scalability to very large personal databases, handling of abstract or non-visual personal attributes, and whether the detector-retriever bottleneck (YOLO-World + CLIP + FAISS) limits performance on fine-grained or occluded concepts.
