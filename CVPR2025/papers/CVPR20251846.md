## Problem

SAM-based medical segmentation models still depend on human-supplied geometric prompts (points, bounding boxes) during both training and inference, and existing unsupervised prompting strategies (self-prompting, CLIP saliency) fail to jointly encode semantic, spatial, and shape information. Moreover, human-in-the-loop feedback pipelines for medical imaging require explicit reward modeling and domain-expert annotation, preventing end-to-end training and escalating costs in low-annotation regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251846/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 medical image (chest X-ray, breast ultrasound, or abdominal CT slice) paired with a short text label (e.g., 'chest x-ray', 'benign breast tumor', 'left kidney'). This label drives three parallel prompt-generation pathways—BiomedCLIP saliency, MedVInT VQA, and GPT-4 description—whose outputs are fused into the prompt encoder. The output is a per-pixel binary segmentation mask; during the preference-alignment stage, four candidate masks are produced by thresholding the decoder's probability map at different levels before a single final mask is selected.

## Method

The framework has two training stages. In stage 1, unsupervised prompts are generated: BiomedCLIP + gScoreCAM + dense CRF yields bounding-box and point prompts, while MedVInT (a PMC-CLIP/PMC-LLaMA VQA model) and GPT-4 supply textual prompts describing organ/tumor shape and location; all three prompt types (point, box, text) are projected to 256-d vectors and fed into a SAM-Med2D prompt encoder and mask decoder, fine-tuned on 10% annotated data with a 20:1 focal+Dice loss. In stage 2, a DPO-inspired preference-alignment loss (Eq. 5) is applied to the remaining unannotated data: four segmentation candidates are generated by thresholding the decoder's probability scores, rated 1–4 by a virtual annotator based on IoU with ground truth (used only for rating, not as direct supervision), and the decoder is updated to increase the log-likelihood ratio of higher-rated candidates relative to a frozen reference policy while decreasing it for lower-rated ones, eliminating the need for a separate reward model.

## Results

On Chest-Xray lung segmentation with only 20% annotated data, the proposed method achieves a Dice score of 78.87, substantially outperforming U-Net (58.66), nnU-Net (60.97), SAM (61.64), SAM-Med2D (67.81), and Self-prompt SAM (68.41). On 3D abdominal CT organ segmentation (AMOS-CT) at 20% data, the method reaches a mean Dice of 77.69 and mean SDC of 78.34, surpassing SAM-Med3D (72.54) and Self-prompt (71.83). At 50% data on Chest-Xray, the semi-supervised framework achieves Dice 89.68, approaching the fully supervised prompt-only variant (91.42) while using only 10% annotated data for direct supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all six baselines in low-data regimes (10–50% annotated data) across all three datasets. On Chest-Xray at 20% data, it leads SAM-Med2D by +11.06 Dice and Self-prompt SAM by +10.46 Dice, with the largest gap over U-Net (+20.21). On AMOS-CT at 20% data, it surpasses the strongest 3D baseline SAM-Med3D by +5.15 mDice and Self-prompt by +5.86 mDice. The method narrows the gap to the fully supervised prompt-only variant (which uses 100% annotated data) to within ~1.7 Dice at 50% data on Chest-Xray (89.68 vs. 91.42) and ~0.66 mDice on AMOS-CT (85.70 vs. 86.36), demonstrating that the DPO-based preference alignment on unannotated data recovers most of the performance lost by using only 10% labels. The only setting where the method does not lead is the full-dataset (100%) regime on AMOS-CT, where fully supervised baselines have sufficient data to close the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SAM-Med2D by replacing its reliance on expert-supplied geometric prompts with an unsupervised prompting pipeline that fuses BiomedCLIP saliency maps (for location/shape), MedVInT VQA answers (for semantic description), and GPT-4 generic organ/disease knowledge into a unified prompt encoder. It departs from prior human-in-the-loop medical segmentation work (which typically trains an explicit reward function via RLHF) by directly adapting the DPO loss from LLM alignment to segmentation, eliminating the separate reward model and enabling end-to-end training. The semi-supervised framing—10% annotated data for prompt fine-tuning followed by preference alignment on unannotated data—positions the work between fully supervised SAM variants and weakly supervised pseudo-label approaches like MedCLIP-SAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing annotation burden for medical segmentation, a persistent bottleneck in clinical deployment; matching 20% fully supervised performance with only 10% annotated data plus 10% unannotated data is a meaningful efficiency gain for multi-modality settings. However, the 'virtual annotator' still requires ground truth to compute overlap-based ratings, so the method is semi-supervised rather than truly unsupervised, and the performance gap over fully supervised baselines narrows or disappears at 100% data. Open questions include whether the DPO-style preference alignment generalizes to fully 3D volumetric segmentation without per-slice processing, whether real human preference signals (rather than IoU-based simulation) yield comparable or better alignment, and whether the approach extends to tasks beyond segmentation (e.g., detection or classification) where preference ranking is less natural.
