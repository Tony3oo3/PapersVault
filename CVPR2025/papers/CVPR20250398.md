## Problem

Video-language alignment models trained with LLM-generated negative captions develop linguistic biases because certain concepts (e.g., 'sombrero', 'marshmallow') appear only as negatives and are never associated with any video. Existing databases lack the fine-grained video variations needed to cover all possible negatives, and it remains unexplored whether synthetic videos from text-to-video generators can remedy this gap without introducing harmful noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250398/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real video with its positive caption and an LLM-generated negative caption as input, then feeds the negative caption into a text-to-video generator (CogVideoX, LaVie, or VideoCrafter2) to produce a synthetic video. The trained video LLM (mPLUG-Owl 7B or Video-LLaVA) outputs a binary alignment score in [0,1] computed as the normalized probability of 'Yes' vs. 'No' from the LLM decoder given the question 'Does this video entail the description [t]?' over uniformly sampled frames.

## Method

SYNVITA is a model-agnostic training framework that adds two mechanisms on top of the standard VLA loss. First, an alignment-based weighting scheme assigns each synthetic video a weight ω = max(0, f̄(V^s, t^s) − f̄(V^s, t^r)), where f̄ is an ensemble of VQAScores from three VQA models, dynamically downweighting videos whose content is semantically inconsistent with their target caption. Second, a semantic consistency regularization computes the longest common subsequence (LCS) between the positive and negative captions to form a shared-semantic text t′, then applies a triplet loss that enforces f(V, t^z) > f(V, t′) > f(V, t^z̄), steering the model to discriminate on fine-grained semantic differences rather than synthetic-to-real appearance artifacts. The full objective is L = L_real + L_syn^φ + λ_scr · L_scr^φ, with only two hyperparameters (margin γ and weight λ_scr).

## Results

SYNVITA (Video-LLaVA) achieves the best overall performance, reaching 80.86 AUC on VideoCon Human, 76.86 AUC on Human-Hard, 20.10 mAP on SSv2-Temporal, 11.21 mAP on SSv2-Events, and 39.88% accuracy on ATP-Hard. SYNVITA (mPLUG-Owl 7B) similarly leads on SSv2-Temporal (17.32 mAP) and SSv2-Events (12.54 mAP), improving over its VideoCon counterpart by +4.32 and +2.17 mAP respectively. The method outperforms all baselines on 5 out of 6 evaluation tasks, with the sole exception being VideoCon (LLM), where the distribution of negatives in the test set overlaps with training negatives, causing a slight drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest baseline VideoCon (mPLUG-Owl 7B), SYNVITA (mPLUG-Owl 7B) gains +4.32 mAP on SSv2-Temporal (17.32 vs 13.00), +2.17 mAP on SSv2-Events (12.54 vs 10.37), and +1.85% on ATP-Hard (37.31 vs 35.46), while slightly dropping on VideoCon LLM (86.45 vs 88.39) due to distribution overlap. SYNVITA (Video-LLaVA) improves over VideoCon (Video-LLaVA) by +0.77 AUC on Human (80.86 vs 80.09), +1.12 AUC on Human-Hard (76.86 vs 75.74), +1.20 mAP on SSv2-Events (11.21 vs 10.01), and +1.12% on ATP-Hard (39.88 vs 38.76). Against off-the-shelf models, SYNVITA (Video-LLaVA) substantially outperforms Video-LLaVA [27] on all tasks (e.g., +10.49 AUC on Human, +8.46 mAP on SSv2-Temporal) and End-to-End VNLI [59] on retrieval and VQA. The method also surpasses specialized temporal models TACT [2] and VFC [35] on ATP-Hard (39.88% vs 27.6% and 31.4% respectively). The only task where SYNVITA underperforms its direct VideoCon counterpart is VideoCon (LLM), where the test negatives share the same LLM-generated distribution as training negatives, making synthetic video positives counterproductive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VideoCon [3], which introduced LLM-generated hard negative captions for video-language alignment (VLA) training, by pairing those negative captions with synthetic videos from text-to-video generators (CogVideoX, LaVie, VideoCrafter2). The idea of augmenting training with synthetic visual data has been explored in the image domain (e.g., TripletCLIP, StableRep), but this is the first systematic study of synthetic *videos* for VLA. The key departure from prior work is the recognition that naive inclusion of generated videos introduces noisy supervision, motivating two new components: alignment-based sample weighting (using VQAScore ensembles) and a semantic consistency regularization via longest-common-subsequence captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As text-to-video generators rapidly improve, the question of how to safely incorporate their outputs into discriminative training pipelines will become increasingly relevant; SYNVITA provides a principled, model-agnostic recipe (weighting + regularization) that practitioners can adopt. The finding that synthetic videos *harm* performance on the in-distribution entailment task while helping on temporally challenging downstream tasks is a useful cautionary result that tempers the 'more data is always better' assumption. Open questions include how the method scales as generator quality improves (reducing the need for aggressive weighting), whether the VQAScore ensemble can be replaced by a single learned scorer, and whether the approach extends to multi-event video generation, where current generators still struggle.
