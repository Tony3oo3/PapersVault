## Problem

Existing panorama outpainting methods such as MVDiffusion and PanoDiffusion require known camera intrinsic and pose parameters for the input image, limiting their use to images with predefined camera settings. This paper addresses the underexplored challenge of generating coherent 360° panoramas from camera-free inputs where the field of view and rotation are entirely unknown.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image with arbitrary unknown camera parameters (FOV ranging 60°–110°, rotations up to ±15°) and a text description as input, and produces eight 512×512 perspective views (each 90° FOV with 45° horizontal overlap) that are stitched into a single 360° equirectangular panorama. The canonical target view is defined as FOV=90° with zero x- and z-axis rotation, while the y-axis rotation is arbitrary since the input can be aligned to any of the eight canonical views.

## Method

CamFreeDiff formulates camera estimation as predicting a 3-DoF homography (field of view, x-axis rotation, z-axis rotation) from the input to a predefined canonical view, using a three-layer MLP (5120→2560→1280) on top of the frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net encoder. The predicted homography establishes point-level correspondences between the input and all target views, which are enforced through correspondence-aware attention (CAA) blocks. The key architectural choice (Variant 3) adds a dedicated condition branch for the original input image alongside eight standard generation branches, with CAA applied between the condition branch and generation branches to preserve input texture while correcting homography estimation errors. The model is fine-tuned from the Stable Diffusion inpainting model with all U-Net blocks frozen; only the MLP and CAA blocks are trained (lr=2×10⁻⁴, 30 epochs) on Matterport3D with random warping to simulate camera-free inputs.

## Results

On Matterport3D, CamFreeDiff with the new-view variant (Variant 3) achieves the best FID of 27.0, IS of 5.6, CLIP score of 24.4, and PSNR of 19.3, substantially outperforming PanoDiffusion (FID 48.7) and MVDiffusion (FID 42.4). On the out-of-domain Structured3D dataset, CamFreeDiff—without any fine-tuning or domain transfer—achieves FID 31.1 and IS 6.2, surpassing PanoDiffusion which was trained directly on Structured3D (FID 35.3, IS 3.2). The homography estimator built on the frozen Stable Diffusion encoder outperforms a dedicated HomographyNet (FID 27.0 vs. 29.2), and a neighborhood size of K=5 in CAA blocks yields the best trade-off between quality and efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PanoDiffusion, CamFreeDiff (new view) reduces FID from 48.7 to 27.0 (a 44.6% relative improvement) and achieves a CLIP score of 24.4, though PanoDiffusion reports a lower IS (3.1 vs. 5.6), likely reflecting its different training objective without text conditioning. Against MVDiffusion, CamFreeDiff improves FID from 42.4 to 27.0, IS from 5.4 to 5.6, and CLIP score from 21.9 to 24.4, while also reporting PSNR (19.3) which MVDiffusion does not. On the out-of-domain Structured3D test, CamFreeDiff—never trained on this dataset—achieves FID 31.1 and IS 6.2, beating PanoDiffusion (FID 35.3, IS 3.2) on FID and CLIP score (25.5) despite PanoDiffusion being trained directly on Structured3D, though PanoDiffusion retains an edge on IS. The key differentiator is that CamFreeDiff handles camera-free inputs natively, whereas both baselines require pre-rectified inputs from the authors' homography estimator for a fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

CamFreeDiff extends MVDiffusion's correspondence-aware attention framework for multi-view panorama generation by removing its requirement for known camera parameters. It draws on DeTone et al.'s insight that homography can be parameterized with fewer than 8 degrees of freedom, further simplifying to a 3-DoF prediction (FOV, x-rotation, z-rotation) that is sufficient for panorama outpainting. Unlike PanoDiffusion, which fine-tunes a latent diffusion model and degrades generalization, CamFreeDiff keeps the Stable Diffusion inpainting backbone frozen and only trains a lightweight MLP for homography estimation plus CAA blocks, preserving pre-trained generative priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first panorama generation model to handle truly camera-free inputs, which is practically important for converting existing photos or video frames into 360° content without requiring metadata. The finding that a dedicated 'new view' conditional branch (Variant 3) outperforms the more intuitive image/latent unwarping strategies highlights that error propagation from imperfect homography estimation is a real bottleneck in two-stage pipelines. Limitations include indoor-only evaluation at 512×512 resolution, no y-axis rotation prediction (argued unnecessary for single-image input), and reliance on a single frozen backbone; open questions include scaling to video-based 360° generation, outdoor scenes, and higher resolutions.
