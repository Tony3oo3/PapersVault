## Problem

Existing normalization methods (BN, GN, LN, GNP, LCN) all rely on fixed-size normalization sets, which limits their adaptability when data statistics evolve across spatial dimensions or training epochs. This rigidity becomes particularly detrimental in out-of-distribution generalization, long-tailed class distributions, and corrupted data, where channels with diverse statistical properties are forced into homogeneous groups, diluting minority-class features and suppressing discriminative information.

## Input / Output

DGN is a drop-in replacement for the normalization layer within any CNN or Vision Transformer architecture, consuming standard RGB images at the resolution of the target benchmark (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet, variable for COCO/Cityscapes). It produces the same task-specific outputs as the base model (class labels, bounding boxes, segmentation masks) but internally generates normalized activations where channel group compositions and sizes vary both spatially across layers and temporally across training epochs. The only additional parameters are a small set of scheduling hyperparameters (E_init, E_interval, E_final, α, p) that remain fixed across all experiments.

## Method

DGN operates in two complementary phases: an offline regrouping phase and an online normalization phase. During offline regrouping (triggered every E_interval epochs between E_init and E_final), each channel's statistical signature (mean, variance) is extracted, and a variance-guided selection identifies only the most statistically heterogeneous groups (those exceeding a percentile threshold σ_thresh) for reassignment, while preserving stable groups. The selected channels are then re-clustered via constrained KMeans with flexible cardinality bounds [L_k, H_k] = C_G_def·(1∓α), allowing group sizes to adapt to natural statistical structure. During the online phase, standard group-wise normalization is applied using the current group assignments, followed by learned per-channel affine transforms (γ, β). Crucially, channels remain in their original spatial positions and are referenced by index rather than physically reordered, avoiding feature disruption. The scheduling mechanism ensures sufficient adaptation time between regroupings, and the final grouping is frozen for inference.

## Results

Across 34 architectures and 11 benchmarks, DGN achieves average improvements of 0.98% over GN, 1.45% over LN, and 2.55% over GNP on image classification. On ImageNet, DGN reaches 83.78% top-1 accuracy with CAFormer-S36 (vs. 83.03% GN), 81.34% with T2T-ViT-14 (vs. 79.96% GN), and 80.93% with EfficientNet-B3 (vs. 79.95% GN). On COCO detection, DGN gains up to 2.01% mAP@50:95 over GN (YOLOv9-C: 53.89 vs. 51.88), and on Cityscapes segmentation, it achieves up to 5.89% mIoU improvement over GN (DeepLabV3+HRNetV2-48: 63.06 vs. 57.17). DGN also shows superior robustness: +2.49% AUROC on OOD detection, +2.38–2.66% accuracy on ImageNet-LT, and lower corruption error rates on CIFAR-100-C and ImageNet-C.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGN consistently outperforms all four baselines across every task and dataset tested. Against GN (Wu et al., CVPR 2018), DGN gains 0.98% average on classification (e.g., 73.44 vs. 72.48 on CIFAR-100 ResNet50; 83.78 vs. 83.03 on ImageNet CAFormer-S36), 2.31% mIoU on Cityscapes segmentation, and up to 2.01% mAP@50:95 on COCO detection. Against LN (Ba et al., 2016), DGN leads by 1.45% on classification and 1.94% on segmentation, with the gap widening on smaller datasets (e.g., 70.97 vs. 67.56 on CIFAR-100 MobileNetV2). Against GNP (Gunawan et al., 2022), DGN achieves the largest margins at 2.55% on classification and 1.73% on segmentation, suggesting GNP's fixed-size improvement over GN is insufficient for diverse statistics. Against LCN (Ortiz et al., 2019), DGN wins by 1.66% mIoU on Cityscapes, with the advantage most pronounced in non-pretrained models (e.g., 63.06 vs. 61.02 on DeepLabV3+HRNetV2-48). DGN also demonstrates lower variance across random initializations (avg std 0.46 vs. 0.52–0.58 for baselines), indicating more stable training.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGN directly extends Group Normalization (GN) by replacing its fixed-size channel groupings with dynamically reformed groups, using constrained K-Means clustering to partition channels based on their statistical signatures (mean and variance). It departs from the broader normalization lineage—BN and its batch-dependent variants (BRN, EN, MABN), batch-independent methods (IN, LN), and spatial variants (PN, LCN)—by identifying the rigid, fixed normalization set size as the core limitation and addressing it through periodic, variance-guided regrouping. The spatio-temporal adaptation schedule (reassigning only statistically heterogeneous groups at fixed intervals) draws conceptually on noise-injection and feature-perturbation strategies for robustness, while the constrained clustering formulation is adapted from Bradley et al.'s cardinality-bounded K-Means.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DGN offers a practical, drop-in normalization replacement with only ~1% runtime overhead that consistently improves performance across 34 architectures and 11 benchmarks, with particularly notable gains in long-tailed classification, OOD detection, and corrupted-data robustness—scenarios where fixed groupings are most harmful. The hyperparameter stability (≤1.89% variation across the search space) makes it broadly deployable without per-task tuning. Open questions include whether the periodic K-Means reassignment could be replaced by a learned, differentiable grouping mechanism, whether the approach transfers to non-vision modalities (audio, text), and whether the benefits persist in extremely deep or very wide networks where channel statistics may be more homogeneous.
