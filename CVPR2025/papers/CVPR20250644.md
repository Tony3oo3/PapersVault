## Problem

Single-view visual object tracking suffers from fundamental limitations in handling occlusion and target loss, as a single viewpoint cannot recover once the target leaves the field of view. Existing multi-view tracking datasets are restricted to narrow object categories (e.g., pedestrians or birds) and lack the scale and diversity needed for class-agnostic tracking, while current multi-view methods rely on detection-and-re-identification pipelines rather than end-to-end feature integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250644/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view video streams (3–4 Azure Kinect cameras at 1920×1080, 30 FPS) where each view provides a reference frame (182×182) indicating the target and a search frame (364×364) for the current timestep, along with full camera calibration (intrinsic and extrinsic parameters). It produces refined 2D bounding boxes independently for each view, a BEV score map on a 200×200 horizontal plane, and an intermediate 3D feature volume of shape 32×200×200×3 that encodes fused spatial information across all visible viewpoints.

## Method

MITracker comprises two modules: a view-specific ViT encoder (initialized from DINOv2) that processes each camera independently using learnable temporal tokens for streaming continuity and a CenterNet-based BBox head, and a multi-view integration module that projects each view's 2D feature map into a shared 3D feature volume via camera calibration, aggregates along the vertical axis with 1D convolutions, and supervises fusion with a BEV classification head. The key refinement step embeds the aggregated 3D features into a single spatial-aware token, which is concatenated with each view's unrefined feature sequence and processed through transformer blocks (spatial-enhanced attention) to explicitly correct target loss and mislocalization. Training is two-stage: first the view-specific encoder is trained on single-view data from GOT-10K and MVTrack, then the full multi-view framework is fine-tuned on MVTrack with 2–4 randomly sampled viewpoints per iteration.

## Results

On the MVTrack dataset (multi-view, 3–4 cameras), MITracker achieves 71.13% AUC, 91.87% PNorm, and 83.95% Precision, surpassing the best single-view baseline OSTrack (49.10% AUC, 65.19% PNorm) by approximately 26% in PNorm. In the single-view setting on MVTrack, MITracker reaches 68.57% AUC, outperforming ODTrack (63.36%) by ~5%. On the unseen GMTD dataset, MITracker attains 65.96% AUC and 87.05% PNorm, exceeding the best baseline EVPTrack (63.89% AUC). Additionally, MITracker achieves a 79.2% recovery rate within 10 frames after target disappearance, compared to 56.7% for SAM2Long.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MITracker outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric across both datasets. The most dramatic gain is in the multi-view MVTrack setting, where MITracker's 91.87% PNorm exceeds the best single-view baseline OSTrack (65.19%) by ~26 percentage points, demonstrating the value of native multi-view fusion over post-hoc geometric projection. In single-view MVTrack, MITracker leads ODTrack (the strongest single-view tracker) by 5.21 AUC points (68.57 vs 63.36) and 6.52 PNorm points. On the held-out GMTD dataset, MITracker surpasses EVPTrack (the best GMTD baseline) by 2.07 AUC and 3.29 PNorm, confirming generalization to unseen scenes and camera configurations. For recovery after target disappearance, MITracker achieves 79.2% success within 10 frames versus 56.7% for SAM2Long, and sustains continuous tracking ~100 frames longer than ODTrack with fewer manual restarts. Notably, all single-view baselines degrade when forced into a multi-view post-fusion pipeline, underscoring that geometric projection alone cannot compensate for the lack of learned cross-view feature integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

MITracker extends the single-view transformer-based tracking lineage (ODTrack, MixFormer, SeqTrack) into a multi-view setting by projecting per-view 2D features into a shared 3D feature volume guided by BEV supervision, drawing architectural ideas from BEV perception work (Simple-BEV, VoxelTrack). It departs from prior multi-view tracking efforts (GMT, CAMPUS, MMPTrack, DIVOTrack) that were restricted to specific object categories (humans, birds) or relied on detection-and-re-identification pipelines, by formulating the problem as class-agnostic tracking of arbitrary objects. The spatial-enhanced attention mechanism, which injects fused 3D spatial tokens back into per-view transformer blocks, is a novel integration strategy not seen in prior multi-view tracking or single-view tracking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MVTrack dataset (234K frames, 27 object categories, 9 tracking attributes, with both training and test splits) fills a critical gap: no prior multi-view tracking dataset supported class-agnostic training at this scale, forcing researchers to rely on single-view data or narrow-category benchmarks. The 79.2% recovery rate (vs. 56.7% for SAM2Long) and strong generalization to the unseen GMTD dataset demonstrate that multi-view integration can substantially mitigate the target-loss problem that plagues single-view trackers. Key limitations remain: the dataset is indoor-only, the method requires precise camera calibration, and the experimental comparison lacks other true multi-view tracking baselines (all competitors are single-view methods with post-hoc geometric fusion), leaving open whether the large performance gap would narrow against purpose-built multi-view trackers.
