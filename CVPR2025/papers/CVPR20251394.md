## Problem

Existing dynamic contextual human motion generation methods either lack textual guidance (undermining semantic coherence and generalization) or fail to incorporate fine-grained geometric representations (resulting in poor contact accuracy and geometric penetration). Prior work on static scenes is limited to fixed furniture and coarse trajectories, while joint human-object generation creates an excessively large search space. The paper addresses how to simultaneously ensure semantic rationality and geometric correctness in responsive human motion generation for dynamic, ever-changing interactive targets.

## Input / Output

The sole geometric input is a 4D sequential point cloud of the interactive target, downsampled to 1024 points per frame and encoded via Basis Point Set (BPS) representation into 256-dim per-frame features. The method produces two outputs simultaneously: a full-body human motion sequence (pelvis velocity, local joint positions/rotations/velocities in pelvis space, and binary foot-ground contact labels following MDM's format) and multi-level textual descriptions (a coarse action summary plus a fine-grained, phase-specific sentence describing contact locations and body movements at three interaction steps). Textual descriptions are auto-generated by an LLM Annotator rather than provided as input.

## Method

SemGeoMo employs a two-stage framework that decouples contact geometry generation from full-body motion synthesis. In Stage 1 (SemGeo Hierarchical Guidance Generation), a conditional diffusion model with a dual-branch transformer (JointTransformer and AffordanceTransformer) jointly generates hand joint positions and an affordance map in a coarse-to-fine manner, conditioned on CLIP text features and BPS point cloud features, with mutual cross-attention between branches to refine joint positions using affordance context. In Stage 2 (SemGeo-guided Motion Generation), a Motion ControlNet built on a frozen MDM backbone generates full-body motion conditioned on the Stage-1 outputs and text encoded by both CLIP and LongCLIP (to handle longer fine-grained descriptions), with a SemGeo Condition Module fusing joint-affordance features via cross-attention. During sampling, joint guidance loss (masking only contact-relevant joints) and foot-stability loss are optimized via L-BFGS at each denoising step to enforce geometric consistency and eliminate foot sliding. An LLM Annotator (LLaMA with LoRA fine-tuning) automatically produces the coarse-to-fine textual guidance from point cloud bounding boxes, trajectories, and predicted contact distances, eliminating manual labeling.

## Results

On the FullBodyManipulation dataset, SemGeoMo (with generated text) achieves a HandJPE of 30.35 cm and MPJPE of 17.98 cm, outperforming all baselines. It attains the best contact F1 score (0.74), FID (1.05), and R-score (0.64) among all methods, while also achieving the highest diversity (9.78). With ground-truth text, performance improves further to HandJPE 27.84, MPJPE 16.62, F1 0.77, FID 1.17, and R-score 0.66. The method also leads on the unseen HoDome dataset (HandJPE 44.22 vs. 76.74 for CHOIS) and on the more challenging IMHD² dataset (HandJPE 35.43 vs. 39.40 for OMOMO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemGeoMo outperforms all five baselines on the primary FullBodyManipulation benchmark across most metrics. Compared to OMOMO (the closest aligned baseline without text), SemGeoMo reduces HandJPE from 33.18 to 27.84 cm and improves F1 from 0.75 to 0.77, while dramatically improving FID (1.98→1.17) and R-score (0.38→0.66), indicating far more semantically coherent motions. Against CHOIS, which uses 2D waypoints and object geometry loss, SemGeoMo achieves lower HandJPE (27.84 vs. 31.68) and substantially better contact metrics (F1 0.77 vs. 0.59) and FID (1.17 vs. 2.27). MDM-PC, adapted with point cloud conditioning, shows competitive FID (0.65) but poor contact accuracy (F1 0.49) and high HandJPE (51.35). AffordMotion and SceneDiff, designed for static scenes, perform worst overall (HandJPE >95, F1 <0.27), confirming their unsuitability for dynamic interaction targets. The one metric where SemGeoMo trails is foot sliding (FS): with GT text it scores 0.57 vs. 0.43 for AffordMotion, though the generated-text variant (0.47) is competitive, suggesting a minor trade-off between contact precision and foot stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemGeoMo extends the dynamic contextual human motion generation line (OMOMO, ReGenNet, LaserHuman) by introducing textual/semantic guidance that prior dynamic-target methods lack, and by decomposing geometric guidance into a coarse affordance map and fine joint positions generated in a two-stage diffusion framework. It builds on the MDM/ControlNet architecture for motion synthesis and on the affordance-map idea from AffordMotion, but departs from joint human+object generation (HOI-Diff, CHOIS, Thor) by conditioning on a given sequential point cloud rather than co-generating the target. The use of a fine-tuned LLaMA (via LoRA) as an automated interaction-text annotator is a novel application of LLMs to the motion generation pipeline, replacing manual labeling with a coarse-to-fine language annotation system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: dynamic interaction motion generation that is both semantically coherent (via text) and geometrically accurate (via hierarchical affordance+joint cues), which is essential for humanoid robotics, HRI, and immersive AR/VR. The automated LLM annotator reduces the annotation bottleneck that has limited text-driven interaction studies, and the generalization to unseen objects, human-human, and deformable-object scenarios suggests broader applicability. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline can be made real-time, how well it scales to multi-person or multi-object interactions, and whether the LLM-generated text quality degrades on out-of-distribution interaction types beyond the training distribution.
