## Problem

Conventional PAN-sharpening methods (CNNs, GANs) produce overly smooth outputs because local receptive fields fail to capture long-range high-frequency dependencies, while existing diffusion-based approaches rely on simplistic conditioning that underutilizes the complementary spectral and spatial information in PAN and LRMS inputs. Additionally, standard knowledge distillation for image restoration treats all spatial regions equally, ignoring that the teacher model struggles disproportionately in certain high-frequency areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252244/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a high-resolution PAN image and a low-resolution multispectral (LRMS) image (upsampled to match PAN resolution) as channel-concatenated inputs and predicts the residual X₀ = I_MS^HR − I_MS^LR, which is added back to the LRMS to recover the HRMS output. The teacher network additionally outputs a pixel-wise uncertainty map θ̂ (via SoftPlus activation) indicating spatial confidence. The student network operates without the teacher's auxiliary conditioning (no FFA vectors or SWT terms), consuming only the noisy input, PAN, and LRMS channels, making it substantially lighter (9.1M vs. 25.5M parameters).

## Method

U-Know-DiffPAN is a two-stage diffusion framework. The teacher (FSA-T) is a U-Net whose encoder uses Feed Forward Attention (FFA) blocks to extract a compact vector representation v of PAN/LRMS that dynamically modulates each encoder stage via group normalization, while the decoder stacks High Quality Frequency Enhancement (HQFE) blocks combining Fourier Transform Channel Attention (self-attention in the frequency domain via 2D FFT on Q/K/V) and Stationary Wavelet Transform Cross Attention (two-stage cross-attention injecting PAN high-frequency and LRMS low-frequency SWT components). The teacher is trained with an uncertainty-driven diffusion loss that jointly predicts the denoised residual and a per-pixel variance map θ̂. The student (FSA-S), a ResBlock-only U-Net of matching depth, is trained via the U-Know loss: in high-uncertainty regions (large θ̂) the hard L1 loss against ground truth is up-weighted by (τ+θ̂), while in low-uncertainty regions the soft L1 loss against the frozen teacher output is up-weighted by (τ−θ̂), supplemented by a feature-alignment term across intermediate layers. This lets the student inherit the teacher's frequency-rich knowledge while specifically correcting the teacher's spatial weaknesses.

## Results

On the GF2 dataset, FSA-T achieves the best performance across all metrics: 44.757 dB PSNR, 0.988 SSIM, 0.603 SAM, and 0.953 HQNR (full-resolution), outperforming the best non-diffusion baseline CANConv (43.166 dB PSNR) by 1.6 dB and the best diffusion baseline PanDiff (42.827 dB PSNR) by 1.9 dB. On the more challenging WV3 dataset, FSA-S achieves 37.930 dB PSNR and 0.976 SSIM, surpassing CANConv (37.441 dB) and TMDiff (37.477 dB). On QB, FSA-S reaches 38.361 dB PSNR, exceeding DCPNet (38.079 dB) and PanDiff (37.842 dB). The student model FSA-S also achieves the lowest inference time (12.3 s) and memory usage (2.14 GB) among all diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U-Know-DiffPAN (both FSA-T and FSA-S) outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest non-diffusion baseline CANConv (CVPR 2024), FSA-T gains +1.6 dB PSNR on GF2, +0.3 dB on WV3, and +0.6 dB on QB, while also achieving substantially lower SAM (0.603 vs. 0.722 on GF2) and ERGAS (0.537 vs. 0.653). Against the strongest diffusion baselines, FSA-S surpasses PanDiff by +1.8 dB PSNR on GF2 and +0.5 dB on QB, and outperforms TMDiff by +2.7 dB PSNR on GF2 and +0.5 dB on WV3. Notably, on the more complex WV3 and QB datasets, the lightweight student FSA-S slightly exceeds the heavier teacher FSA-T (e.g., 37.930 vs. 37.894 dB PSNR on WV3), demonstrating that the uncertainty-aware distillation helps the student generalize better on challenging scenes. In terms of efficiency, FSA-S uses only 9.1M parameters and 0.346 TFLOPs, compared to TMDiff's 153.9M parameters and 5.5 TFLOPs, while delivering superior quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based PAN-sharpening line (PanDiff, TMDiff) by replacing their simple conditioning with a dual frequency-aware strategy: compact vector representations via Feed Forward Attention in the encoder and Fourier/SWT-based cross-attention in the decoder. It also departs from standard knowledge distillation by introducing an uncertainty map from the teacher that spatially reweights the student's hard and soft losses, so the student focuses learning on regions where the teacher is least confident. The overall framework composes DDPM-style denoising, U-Net architecture, and frequency-domain attention in a way not previously combined for PAN-sharpening.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The uncertainty-aware KD mechanism is a generalizable idea that could transfer to other restoration tasks where a large model's confidence varies spatially, and the student outperforming the teacher on complex datasets (WV3, QB) suggests the uncertainty signal adds genuine value beyond mere capacity reduction. The frequency conditioning strategy (FFT channel attention + SWT cross-attention) offers a principled way to exploit the complementary spectral/spatial information in PAN and LRMS inputs, which prior diffusion methods underutilized. Key open questions include whether the multi-step diffusion inference can be further accelerated (the authors acknowledge this limitation) and whether the uncertainty-aware KD generalizes to other remote-sensing fusion tasks or broader image restoration settings.
