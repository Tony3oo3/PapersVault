## Problem

Existing evaluation metrics for text-guided image editing suffer from context-blindness: preservation-centric metrics ignore the target text, modification-centric metrics ignore the source image, and even directional CLIP similarity applies a fixed evaluation standard regardless of the specific source-target context. This leads to systematic bias toward either over-modification or over-preservation, misaligning with human judgment across diverse editing scenarios.

## Input / Output

Given a source image, a target text instruction, and an edited image, AugCLIP produces a single scalar score in [0,1] measuring how closely the edit matches an estimated ideal. The source image and target text are first processed by GPT-4V to extract a variable number of short visual attribute descriptions (typically under 5 words each), which are then encoded via CLIP-ViT-B/16. The MLLM attribute extraction adds approximately 12.3 seconds of overhead per evaluation, while the score computation itself takes only 0.15 seconds.

## Method

AugCLIP estimates the CLIP representation of an ideally edited image as the source image embedding plus a minimum-norm modification vector, then scores the edited image by cosine similarity to this ideal. Source and target visual attributes are extracted via GPT-4V, encoded into CLIP space, and weighted by a combination of intra-set importance (cosine similarity to same-class attributes) and inter-set collision (cosine distance to opposite-set attributes). A weighted SVM hyperplane is then fitted to separate source from target attributes, and the modification vector is derived as the shortest projection from the source embedding onto the target side of this hyperplane. This minimum-modification formulation fundamentally differs from directional CLIP similarity's naive subtraction of source from target text embeddings, ensuring the ideal representation preserves source identity while satisfying the target instruction.

## Results

AugCLIP achieves the highest human alignment score (s_2AFC) across all three user-study benchmarks: 0.883 on CelebA, 0.831 on EditVal, and 0.857 on DreamBooth, outperforming the best preservation-centric baseline CLIP-I (0.848, 0.730, 0.857) and the only dual-aspect baseline CLIP_dir (0.673, 0.697, 0.357). In the Ground Truth Selection Test, AugCLIP achieves 0.570 accuracy on TEdBench (vs. 0.420 for the best baseline SC) and 0.889 on MagicBrush (vs. 0.936 for SC and 0.601 for CLIP_dir), demonstrating balanced evaluation of both preservation and modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AugCLIP outperforms all six baselines on CelebA (0.883 vs. best 0.848 for CLIP-I) and EditVal (0.831 vs. best 0.764 for SC), with the largest margin over CLIP_dir (0.673 and 0.697 respectively). On DreamBooth, AugCLIP ties with CLIP-I at 0.857 but substantially surpasses CLIP_dir (0.357), which fails to handle personalized generation. In the Ground Truth Selection Test, AugCLIP leads on TEdBench (0.570 vs. 0.420 for SC) and is competitive on MagicBrush (0.889 vs. 0.936 for SC), while dramatically outperforming CLIP_dir (0.601) on MagicBrush. Preservation-centric metrics (L2, LPIPS, DINO) perform poorly on EditVal and DreamBooth because they ignore the target text entirely, while the modification-centric CLIP-T (CLIPScore) scores below 0.50 on all three user-study datasets. The paper further shows that naively augmenting CLIP_dir with MLLM-extracted attributes (Table 6) does not close the gap, confirming that the minimum-modification hyperplane formulation is essential rather than the attribute extraction alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the existing landscape of evaluation metrics for text-guided image editing, which has relied on repurposed metrics from image generation (FID, LPIPS, DINO) or captioning (CLIPScore), and on directional CLIP similarity (Gal et al., 2022) as the only prior metric considering both source and target. AugCLIP departs from these by introducing a context-aware mechanism: it repurposes a multi-modal LLM (GPT-4V) as an attribute extractor and a weighted SVM hyperplane as a geometric separator in CLIP space, concepts borrowed from classical ML but applied here for the first time in an editing-evaluation context. The key conceptual departure is the 'minimum modification' formulation of the ideal edit, which contrasts with the naive source-minus-target subtraction used by directional CLIP similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that context-blindness is a measurable and systematic failure mode of existing metrics (e.g., directional CLIP similarity misjudges 63% of TEdBench cases), the paper provides a principled justification for context-aware evaluation and a practical tool that reduces dependence on costly human studies. The negative result that simply augmenting CLIP_dir with MLLM-extracted attributes does not reliably improve alignment (Table 6) underscores that the geometric formulation matters, not just richer descriptions. Open questions include the computational overhead of GPT-4V calls (~12.3 s per sample), whether the metric generalizes to video or 3D editing, and whether the hyperplane approach scales to highly multi-attribute scenes beyond the tested benchmarks.
