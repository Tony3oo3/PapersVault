## Problem

Video VAEs in Latent Video Diffusion Models rely on dense 3D convolutional backbones that impose prohibitive computational costs as video resolution and duration grow, becoming the primary bottleneck in large-scale LVDM training. Additionally, existing block-wise inference strategies (tiling, naive caching) introduce latent-space discontinuities that manifest as color distortions and temporal flickering in reconstructed long videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250834/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB video clips (evaluated at 256×256, 512×512, and 768×768 spatial resolutions with 33 frames) and produces a compressed latent tensor with 4×8×8 temporal×height×width compression in either 4 or 16 channels, plus a pixel-space reconstruction. The encoder simultaneously applies multi-level Haar wavelet decomposition alongside the convolutional backbone, so the input is consumed through two parallel branches: a wavelet pyramid and a reduced 3D-convolutional network. Causal Cache enables the same model to process arbitrarily long videos in temporal chunks with zero quality loss relative to direct inference.

## Method

WF-VAE applies multi-level 3D Haar wavelet transforms to decompose video into a frequency pyramid, then establishes a dedicated 'energy flow pathway' that routes the dominant low-frequency sub-band (S_hhh) directly into the latent representation via Inflow/Outflow blocks concatenated at each downsampling layer, bypassing the backbone for that information. This architectural shortcut allows the 3D-convolutional backbone to be substantially simplified, cutting memory by ~4× and doubling throughput. To enable lossless block-wise inference, Causal Cache exploits the one-sided padding of causal 3D convolutions: each temporal chunk caches a computed number of tail frames (determined by kernel size, stride, and chunk index) so that the convolution sliding window remains numerically identical to direct inference. Training combines L1, perceptual, adversarial, and KL losses with a novel WL loss that penalizes deviations of the decoder's reconstructed wavelet sub-bands from the encoder's, enforcing encoder-decoder structural symmetry.

## Results

On Panda70M with 4 latent channels, WF-VAE-L achieves PSNR 32.10, LPIPS 0.0411, and rFVD 146.24, outperforming OD-VAE (PSNR 30.31, rFVD 191.23) and Allegro (PSNR 31.70, rFVD 172.72). With 16 latent channels, WF-VAE-L reaches PSNR 35.87, LPIPS 0.0175, and rFVD 39.40 on Panda70M, surpassing CogVideoX-VAE (PSNR 35.79, LPIPS 0.0198, rFVD 43.23). In terms of computational efficiency, WF-VAE-L requires only 7170 MB of memory for encoding at 512×512 resolution versus 31944 MB for OD-VAE and 55664 MB for Allegro, while achieving 2× higher throughput and 4× lower memory consumption overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OD-VAE [6] and Open-Sora VAE [49], WF-VAE-L (4ch) wins decisively on all four metrics (e.g., PSNR 32.10 vs 30.31 and 31.37; rFVD 146.24 vs 191.23 and 298.47 on Panda70M). Compared to Allegro [50], WF-VAE-L achieves higher PSNR (32.10 vs 31.70), lower LPIPS (0.0411 vs 0.0421), and substantially lower rFVD (146.24 vs 172.72) on Panda70M, though Allegro retains a marginal SSIM advantage (0.9158 vs 0.9142). Against CogVideoX-VAE [40] at 16 latent channels, WF-VAE-L surpasses it on PSNR (35.87 vs 35.79), LPIPS (0.0175 vs 0.0198), and rFVD (39.40 vs 43.23) on Panda70M, with only a negligible SSIM gap on WebVid-10M (0.9430 vs 0.9434). CV-VAE [48] and SVD-VAE [4] are clearly outperformed by WF-VAE-L on PSNR, SSIM, and rFVD across both datasets. In the video generation evaluation (Table 2), WF-VAE-L (4ch) achieves the best FVD₁₆ on UCF-101 (929.55) and IS (70.53), while WF-VAE-L (16ch) attains the best FVD₁₆ on UCF-101 (947.18) and IS (71.86), outperforming CogVideoX (FVD 1117.57, IS 57.47) and Allegro (FVD 1045.66, IS 67.16). Critically, WF-VAE achieves these results with 2× higher encoding throughput and up to 776% lower memory consumption than the strongest baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

WF-VAE extends the video VAE lineage established by Rombach et al. (SD-VAE) and Esser et al. (VQGAN) by replacing the dense 3D convolutional backbone with a wavelet-driven energy flow pathway, drawing on the Haar wavelet decomposition to create a 'highway' for low-frequency information that bypasses the backbone. It builds on causal convolutions from Yu et al. (ICLR 2024) to introduce Causal Cache, a lossless block-wise inference mechanism that departs from the tiling strategies in Open-Sora/Allegro and the group-normalization-dependent caching in CogVideoX. The WL loss is a novel regularization term specific to the wavelet architecture, and the overall design contrasts with LiteVAE's wavelet-based encoder by explicitly modeling energy flow symmetry between encoder and decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video VAEs are a critical bottleneck in training large-scale latent video diffusion models, and WF-VAE's 2× throughput and 4× memory reduction while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality directly lowers the cost of pre-training on large video corpora. The Causal Cache mechanism provides a general, architecture-agnostic solution to the block-wise inference discontinuity problem that has plagued long-video generation, and its lossless guarantee (zero metric degradation) is a clean result. The authors acknowledge the decoder remains overly complex relative to the encoder, and future work aims to streamline it; an open question is whether the wavelet energy-flow paradigm generalizes beyond the specific 4×8×8 compression ratio tested here.
