## Problem

Self-supervised image denoising methods depend heavily on priors embedded in the network architecture, yet rotation equivariant prior has never been incorporated at the architecture level for this task. Moreover, no theoretical analysis exists for how upsampling and downsampling operators in U-Net architectures affect rotation equivariance, and rigid equivariant design can degrade the high-frequency reconstruction accuracy needed for denoising.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251197/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (or a pair of noisy images of the same scene for N2N) at arbitrary resolution and produces a denoised image of the same size. Noise types include Gaussian (σ randomized in [0,50] per sample), Poisson, Poisson-Gaussian, pepper-salt, and speckle. The adaptive variant AdaReNet additionally outputs a per-pixel mask that blends the vanilla CNN and equivariant CNN outputs, with the mask tending toward the vanilla branch at high-frequency edges and toward the equivariant branch in low-frequency regions.

## Method

The paper replaces all standard convolutions in U-Net or DnCNN architectures with Fconv (Fourier series expansion-based equivariant convolutions) to create rotation equivariant versions of N2N, N2V, and R2R. A key theoretical contribution proves that upsampling and downsampling introduce O(h) equivariant error while equivariant convolutions introduce O(h²) error, yielding a full-network equivariance bound of R₁h + R₂h² for discrete rotation angles (and an additional R₃t⁻¹h term for arbitrary angles). To address the accuracy loss from equivariant parameter sharing, AdaReNet introduces a parallel Vanilla Module and EQ Module whose outputs are fused by a learned MaskNetwork that assigns per-pixel blending weights, followed by a Self-correcting ResNet module. The training loss combines the main denoising loss with L₂ regularization terms on both sub-network outputs (α₁=α₂=0.1).

## Results

On the N2V task with U-Net architecture, the proposed N2V-EQ+ achieves an average PSNR of 29.05 dB (SSIM 0.832) at σ=25 and 26.92 dB (SSIM 0.753) at σ=50 across BSD500, Kodak24, and Set14, outperforming the vanilla N2V baseline by ~1.0 dB and ~0.85 dB respectively. For N2N with U-Net, N2N-EQ+ reaches 31.72 dB (Kodak, σ=25) and 28.69 dB (Kodak, σ=50), a consistent ~0.2–0.3 dB gain over standard N2N. The equivariant error drops from 0.233 (N2V) to 0.068 (N2V-EQ), confirming that the performance gains are driven by reduced equivariant error. On R2R with DnCNN, R2R-EQ achieves 29.14 dB (BSD68, σ=25) vs. 29.03 dB for R2R, with only 0.17M parameters versus 0.67M for the original.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three self-supervised denoising baselines, the proposed method consistently wins: N2V-EQ+ outperforms N2V by ~1.0 dB PSNR at σ=25 and ~0.85 dB at σ=50 (average across three datasets); N2N-EQ+ beats N2N by 0.2–0.3 dB across all tested datasets and noise levels; R2R-EQ edges out R2R by 0.11 dB at σ=25 but the adaptive R2R-EQ+ variant slightly underperforms at σ=50 (26.00 vs. 26.01 dB) due to its larger parameter count (0.84M vs. 0.67M) on the smaller BSD68 training set. In the ablation comparing equivariant convolution choices (Table 4), Fconv [49] outperforms G-CNN [8] by ~0.1 dB, E2-CNN [45] by ~0.3 dB, and PDO-eConv [39] by ~0.1–0.2 dB PSNR, confirming that Fourier-series-based filter parametrization is best suited for low-level vision tasks requiring high representation accuracy. The equivariant error metric further validates the architectural improvement: N2V-EQ reduces equivariant error from 0.233 to 0.068, while the adaptive N2V-EQ+ maintains a comparable 0.076, showing the fusion module does not sacrifice equivariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised denoising line (N2N, N2V, R2R) by replacing standard convolutions with rotation-equivariant convolutions (Fconv) at the architecture level, and introduces AdaReNet to adaptively fuse equivariant and vanilla CNN outputs. It departs from prior equivariant CNN work (G-CNN, E2-CNN, PDO-eConv), which targeted supervised classification or ResNet-based restoration, by providing the first theoretical analysis of how upsampling and downsampling operators in U-Net architectures affect rotation equivariance. The Fourier-series-based Fconv parametrization from Xie et al. serves as the core equivariant building block, chosen over harmonic-based alternatives for its superior representation accuracy in low-level vision tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that simply swapping convolutions for equivariant convolutions in a U-Net yields a rotation-equivariant network (with O(h) error from upsampling/downsampling) provides a principled foundation for equivariant self-supervised denoising that was previously absent. However, the practical gains are modest (typically 0.1–0.5 dB over strong baselines), and the adaptive AdaReNet variant adds parameter overhead without consistently outperforming the simpler equivariant version, especially on smaller datasets. Open questions include whether the equivariance prior yields larger benefits in truly data-starved regimes (e.g., single-image denoising), whether it transfers to other self-supervised restoration tasks, and whether the O(h) equivariance error from upsampling can be reduced with learned equivariant interpolation.
