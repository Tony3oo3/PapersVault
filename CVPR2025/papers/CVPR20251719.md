## Problem

While LLMs have made significant progress in long-chain reasoning through chain-of-thought and product-level solutions, MLLMs still lack scalable, high-quality long-chain reasoning data and effective training pipelines for visual reasoning. Directly supervising MLLMs with long, complex reasoning chains does not yield ideal reasoning ability, and the absence of structured data generation and multi-agent decomposition limits the model's capacity to leverage visual cues for precise step-by-step problem-solving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251719/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and a text question as input. The reasoning agent produces a structured, multi-step reasoning chain in JSON format where each step contains a brief summary, a detailed reasoning response, and an action token (continue or summary) that controls whether additional reasoning steps are generated. The summary agent then evaluates the reasoning chain for correctness and selectively incorporates or disregards its elements to produce the final answer, making the output robust to errors in the reasoning process.

## Method

Insight-V introduces a two-step data generation pipeline that progressively samples long, structured reasoning chains (iterating with continue/summary actions) and applies multi-granularity assessment (answer filtering via a strong LLM followed by reasoning-path scoring via a multimodal model) to ensure quality without human annotation. The core architectural contribution is a multi-agent decomposition: a reasoning agent trained on the highest-scoring reasoning paths generates detailed step-by-step reasoning, while a summary agent trained on both optimal and deliberately flawed reasoning samples learns to critically evaluate and selectively use the reasoning chain rather than blindly copying it. Training follows a two-stage procedure—supervised fine-tuning of both agents from a shared base MLLM, followed by iterative DPO applied to the reasoning agent (3 rounds, ~15K preference pairs per round) to progressively refine reasoning quality and generation stability.

## Results

On seven visual reasoning benchmarks, Insight-V (7B, built on Qwen-2.5-7B) achieves an average score of 51.6, surpassing the strongest reported baselines Idefics3-LLaMA3 (48.1) and Ovis1.5-LLaMA3 (49.4). Applied to LLaVA-NeXT-LLaMA3 (8B), the system yields a +7.0% average improvement (40.2 → 47.2), with the largest single-benchmark gain on MME (+9.1%) and ChartQA (+5.8%). On the stronger 7B base model, the multi-agent system plus iterative DPO adds +2.9% over the base (48.7 → 51.6), with gains of +3.1% on MMMU, +2.3% on MMMU-Pro, and +3.0% on MathVista.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Insight-V (7B) outperforms Idefics3-LLaMA3 (48.1) and Ovis1.5-LLaMA3 (49.4) by 3.5 and 2.2 points on average across the seven reasoning benchmarks. Against Qwen2-VL (7B), which reports no average, Insight-V leads on MMBench (82.3 vs 81.0) and MMStar (61.5 vs 60.7) but trails on MMMU (50.2 vs 53.7), ChartQA (81.5 vs 83.0), and MathVista (59.9 vs 61.4). On MMMU-Pro, MiniCPM-V-2.6 (27.2) still exceeds Insight-V (24.9), and on MME, MiniCPM-V-2.6 (2268.7) and InternLM-XComposer2 (2220.4) substantially outperform Insight-V (1685.1), indicating that the multi-agent reasoning system trades some raw perception scores for stronger structured reasoning. The most dramatic gain is on LLaVA-NeXT-LLaMA3, where the full pipeline lifts the average from 40.2 to 47.2 (+7.0%), with MMBench jumping from 72.3 to 81.7 and MMStar from 43.1 to 57.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chain-of-Thought reasoning paradigm from text-only LLMs (e.g., OpenAI o1) to multimodal settings, where prior work (MAVIS, G-LLaVA) has shown limited gains from directly applying CoT to MLLMs. It builds on the DPO formulation of Rafailov et al. and the iterative DPO strategy of Chen et al. for preference alignment, but departs from single-model reasoning by decomposing the task into a dedicated reasoning agent and a summary agent. The automated data generation pipeline with progressive multi-step generation and multi-granularity scoring addresses a key gap: the absence of scalable, high-quality long-chain visual reasoning data that has constrained prior MLLM reasoning efforts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for injecting long-chain reasoning into existing MLLMs without requiring new pretraining, which is valuable given the compute cost of training from scratch. The finding that a simple two-agent decomposition outperforms multi-turn or single-model CoT approaches suggests that task separation, rather than longer context windows, may be the key to robust visual reasoning. However, the approach still depends on strong external LLMs (Qwen2, Qwen2-VL) for data generation and assessment, and the iterative DPO adds training complexity; whether the multi-agent inference overhead is justified in latency-sensitive deployments remains an open question. Scaling beyond 7–8B models and to video or 3D modalities are natural next steps.
