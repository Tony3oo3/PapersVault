## Problem

Existing diffusion-based super-resolution methods, when applied to infrared imaging, ignore the unique thermal spectrum distribution of infrared images and overlook machine perception requirements for downstream tasks like detection and segmentation. The paper addresses the gap between visually appealing reconstruction and perceptually salient output by asking whether a single SR model can simultaneously optimize both visual fidelity and task-specific perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251023/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution infrared image and produces a high-resolution infrared image, operating within the ResShift residual-shifting framework where the HR–LR residual serves as the diffusion noise. The output is evaluated not only on no-reference visual quality metrics (CLIPIQA, MUSIQ) and reference-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) but also on downstream YOLOv5 object detection (mAP) and SegFormer semantic segmentation (mIoU) tasks, making the SR output directly usable for perception pipelines.

## Method

DifIISR injects the gradient of a composite guidance loss into the noise prediction at each denoising step of the diffusion reverse process, following the Diffusion Posterior Sampling framework. The guidance has two components: (1) a visual loss that matches the normalized log-magnitude Fourier spectra of the predicted HR image against the ground-truth HR image to preserve infrared-specific thermal frequency distributions, and (2) a perceptual loss combining VGG-16 feature-map MSE and SAM segmentation-mask MSE to infuse generalizable perceptual structure. Training is two-phase: 200K iterations to learn basic infrared SR capability, followed by 50K iterations with the gradient guidance active. The key mechanism is that the guidance gradient ∇L_g is scaled by √(1−α_t) and added to the denoiser's noise estimate (Eq. 9), steering the reverse diffusion trajectory toward both spectral fidelity and task-relevant semantics without modifying the backbone architecture.

## Results

On the M³FD dataset, DifIISR achieves the best no-reference quality scores across all three test sets: CLIPIQA of 0.6144/0.5906/0.5484 and MUSIQ of 55.194/54.504/53.636 (Set5/Set15/Set20), outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline SinSR (CLIPIQA 0.5877/0.5762/0.5357; MUSIQ 54.355/54.106/53.187). On reference-based metrics, DifIISR leads diffusion methods with PSNR of 32.279/32.351/33.451 dB and SSIM of 0.8637/0.8578/0.8941. For downstream tasks, the method achieves 33.1% mAP on infrared object detection and 42.4% mIoU on semantic segmentation, both the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DifIISR outperforms all 11 baselines on both no-reference metrics (CLIPIQA and MUSIQ) across all three M³FD test sets. The closest competitor is SinSR [49], which DifIISR surpasses by 0.027–0.045 in CLIPIQA and 0.4–0.8 in MUSIQ; the gap widens substantially against ResShift [55] (CLIPIQA +0.12–0.14) and all CNN/Transformer-based methods (ESRGAN, SwinIR, HAT, DAT, CoRPLE), which score 0.15–0.39 lower in CLIPIQA. On reference-based metrics (Table 2), DifIISR leads all diffusion baselines in PSNR and SSIM, though SinSR achieves a slightly lower LPIPS on Set20 (0.2549 vs. 0.2735), indicating marginally better perceptual similarity to the HR ground truth in that setting. For downstream detection, DifIISR achieves 33.1% mAP, a 5.6% improvement over the best competing method in the truck category; for segmentation, it reaches 42.4% mIoU with a 7.4% gain on the truck class. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that combining both visual and perceptual guidance yields the best joint performance (PSNR 34.575, mAP 33.1%, mIoU 42.4%), while either guidance alone provides partial gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ResShift's residual-shifting diffusion framework for super-resolution and adapts the gradient-injection mechanism from Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) to impose task-specific constraints during the reverse diffusion process. It departs from prior infrared SR work (CNN-based, Transformer-based, and earlier diffusion methods like CoRPLE) by explicitly addressing two gaps: preserving infrared-specific thermal spectral distributions via a Fourier-domain visual loss, and aligning reconstruction with downstream machine perception via VGG and SAM feature/mask losses. The key conceptual move is treating both visual fidelity and perceptual utility as gradient signals injected into the noise prediction at each denoising step, rather than as post-hoc loss terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion-based SR can be steered to simultaneously optimize for human-perceived quality and machine-perception performance (detection, segmentation), which prior methods treated as separate objectives. A notable limitation is that the SR output outperforms the HR ground truth on no-reference metrics (CLIPIQA, MUSIQ), suggesting the model generates 'hallucinated' quality that diverges from the true scene — a trade-off that may not be acceptable in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. Open questions include whether the gradient-guidance framework generalizes to other modalities or inverse problems, the computational overhead of running SAM and VGG at every training step, and whether the gains hold under more diverse degradation conditions beyond the M³FD training distribution.
