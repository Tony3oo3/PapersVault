## Problem

Existing black-box model inversion attacks (MIAs) require over 100K queries per target ID, making them impractical for commercial face-recognition APIs, and they predominantly target low-resolution datasets with limited IDs. The paper further identifies that logits from GAN-synthesized samples exhibit an extreme long-tail distribution (65.9% of classes receive zero samples), which causes naive surrogate-model construction to fail and degrades the quality of initialization points for subsequent optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251743/cropped_page7_idx15.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker queries a black-box pre-trained face-recognition model (e.g., VGG16, ResNet50, InceptionV1, SphereFace at 112×196 to 224×224) with GAN-synthesized face images drawn from a distribution (CelebA or FFHQ) that differs from the private training data (VGGFace, VGGFace2, or CASIA with 2,622–10,575 IDs). A pre-trained face-recognition model from a different dataset initializes the surrogate's backbone. The output is a single reconstructed face image per target ID, evaluated via cross-model Acc@1/Acc@5, KNN distance, and feature distance against the true private sample.

## Method

SMILE is a two-step black-box MIA. Step 1 (long-tailed surrogate training) builds a local surrogate by freezing the backbone of a pre-trained model and training an ensemble of N=3 classifiers with a combined loss: KL-divergence distillation from the target model's logits, cross-entropy on pseudo-hard labels, a distribution-aware diversity loss (adapted from RIDE) that maximizes inter-model disagreement, and a novel Top-k reweighting scheme that up-weights classes appearing in the Top-k logits across all samples to counteract the extreme long-tail distribution. Step 2 (gradient-free black-box optimization) uses the surrogate-attacked result as initialization and applies NGOpt (which selects MetaModelOnePlusOne from Nevergrad) to refine the GAN latent vector against the true target model, with P-space clipping (k=1.7) to constrain the search. The entire pipeline requires only ~3.5K queries per ID (~5% of prior SOTA), with 2.5K for surrogate construction and 1K for optimization.

## Results

SMILE achieves state-of-the-art black-box MIA performance across VGGFace, VGGFace2, and CASIA with diverse target architectures, while requiring only ~3.5K queries per target ID (≈5% of Mirror-b's 120K or RLBMI's 80K). On VGGFace/VGG16 with FFHQ prior, SMILE (InceptionV1 surrogate) reaches 80.95% Acc@1, surpassing Mirror-b (59.18%) and RLBMI (67.35%). On the more challenging CASIA/SphereFace setting, SMILE attains 48.30% Acc@1 versus 22.45% (Mirror-b) and 16.33% (RLBMI). The method remains robust across six surrogate architectures (ResNet50, InceptionV1/V3, MobileNetV2, EfficientNetB0, Swin-T) with a fixed hyperparameter set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMILE consistently outperforms both black-box baselines (Mirror-b and RLBMI) across nearly all dataset–architecture combinations. On VGGFace/VGG16 (FFHQ prior), SMILE with InceptionV1 surrogate achieves 80.95% Acc@1, a 21.8-point gain over Mirror-b (59.18%) and a 13.6-point gain over RLBMI (67.35%). The advantage is most dramatic on CASIA/SphereFace, where SMILE reaches 48.30% Acc@1 versus only 22.45% (Mirror-b) and 16.33% (RLBMI). RLBMI occasionally matches or slightly exceeds SMILE on Acc@5 for VGGFace/VGG16 (91.84% vs 91.16%), but this comes at 23× the query cost and is accompanied by adversarial-sample artifacts that inflate its scores. Against the white-box upper bounds (Mirror-w, PPA), SMILE closes the gap substantially—e.g., 80.95% vs 93.88% on VGGFace/VGG16—while operating under a strict black-box constraint with only 3.5K queries per ID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the black-box model inversion attack (MIA) line initiated by Mirror [7] and RLBMI [21], which frame MIA as GAN latent-space optimization but require 80K–120K queries per target. Its key conceptual departure is reframing the surrogate model training step as a long-tailed recognition problem, borrowing the ensemble-with-diversity-loss paradigm from RIDE [59] and introducing a Top-k reweighting strategy tailored to the sparse class coverage observed in black-box logits. For the optimization step, it replaces the reinforcement learning (RLBMI) or genetic algorithm (Mirror-b) solvers with NGOpt's automatic gradient-free algorithm selection (MetaModelOnePlusOne), a shift from hand-crafted search to benchmarked black-box optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5% query reduction (3.5K vs. 80K–120K per ID) has direct practical implications: it lowers the monetary cost of attacking commercial face-recognition APIs and reduces the detectability of the attack by cloud providers, making black-box MIAs more realistic threats. The empirical finding that blindly increasing GAN sample size does not alleviate the extreme long-tail distribution in target-model logits challenges the common assumption that more queries always yield better surrogates. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to label-only settings under pre-trained models, how to handle the 'attack-sensitive ID' phenomenon where some IDs are inherently harder to reconstruct, and whether defenses (BiDO, MID, LS, TL) can be made ineffective against query-efficient attacks.
