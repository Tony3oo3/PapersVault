## Problem

Building digital twins of articulated objects requires simultaneously recovering 3D geometry, RGB appearance, and articulation motion parameters, yet prior self-supervised methods (e.g., PARIS) rely on NeRF-based representations with compromised visual fidelity and unstable optimization, while supervised approaches demand large annotated 3D datasets. No existing method reconstructs articulated objects in a high-fidelity, real-time-renderable representation (3D Gaussian Splatting) while jointly estimating part segmentation and motion from only two static states.

## Input / Output

The method takes two sets of multi-view RGB images (start state I₀ and end state I₁) with corresponding camera extrinsics and intrinsics, assuming the two states share a common world coordinate frame and similar lighting. It outputs a set of 3D Gaussians partitioned into movable (G_m) and unmovable (G_u) subsets via a per-Gaussian binary mask, plus articulation parameters (either a revolute joint defined by axis l, pivot p, and angle θ, or a prismatic joint defined by axis a and distance d). The resulting model supports real-time novel-view rendering in both articulation states and can be extended to multi-part objects by sequential sub-processes.

## Method

ArticulatedGS decouples the ill-posed joint optimization of Gaussian appearance, movable mask, and motion parameters into a four-stage progressive pipeline: (1) standard 3D-GS reconstruction of the start state; (2) a DeformNet (4-layer 1D CNN + 4-layer MLP) predicts per-Gaussian translation δx and rotation δr from Gaussian positions, supervised by end-state appearance loss plus an ARAP loss enforcing local rigidity among k-nearest Gaussians; (3) Gaussians with normalized displacement above a threshold (τ=0.3) are classified as movable, and motion parameters are optimized via a revolute-to-prismatic reclassification strategy (if pivot norm exceeds R=1, the motion is reinterpreted as prismatic), with Chamfer distance providing geometric supervision; (4) a final joint optimization refines Gaussians, the movable mask (reclassified at τ=0.1), and motion parameters simultaneously, with the mask co-optimizing through the standard 3D-GS densification and pruning schedule. This progressive scheme avoids the local-optimum trap that causes the 'Vanilla' simultaneous-optimization baseline to collapse all Gaussians into a single static category.

## Results

On the PARIS synthetic dataset, ArticulatedGS achieves motion angle errors of 0.02–0.30° and position errors of 0.000–0.007 across most objects, substantially outperforming PARIS (0.02–3.92° angle, 0.003–2.367 pos) and Ditto (0.96–89.86° angle, 0.010–5.700 pos). For visual quality, the method reaches PSNR of 35.37–43.36 dB and SSIM of 0.975–0.996, exceeding PARIS (33.84–38.28 dB, 0.953–0.988) by a clear margin. The Vanilla ablation (simultaneous optimization without progressive steps) degrades PSNR to 21.08–31.49 dB and collapses part segmentation, confirming the necessity of the multi-step scheme.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PARIS (the only other fully self-supervised method), ArticulatedGS achieves dramatically lower motion angle errors on most objects (e.g., 0.02° vs 7.90° on Foldchair, 0.03° vs 0.73° on Stapler) and lower geometry distances (e.g., 0.05 vs 131.82 on Foldchair, 0.06 vs 110.71 on Stapler). PARIS retains an edge on the Laptop and Oven categories where its NeRF-based optimization happens to converge well, and on real-world Fridge/Storage where ground-truth annotation noise affects evaluation. Against Ditto (a supervised method trained on four Shape2Motion categories), ArticulatedGS wins on nearly all metrics for unseen categories (Foldchair, Fridge, Scissor, Stapler, USB, Washer) where Ditto reports angle errors above 89°, while Ditto only performs well on its trained categories (Laptop, Oven, Storage). The Vanilla ablation (joint optimization without progressive steps) confirms that the multi-step decoupling is essential: it collapses part segmentation into a single class and yields PSNR drops of 10–20 dB relative to the full method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting [12] as its geometric and appearance representation, and on PARIS [21] for the problem formulation (two-state multi-view RGB input, prismatic/revolute joint parameterization, and the PARIS evaluation dataset). It departs from PARIS's NeRF-based pipeline by replacing the radiance field with 3D Gaussians, and from depth-assisted methods like DTA [39] and ArtGS [23] by requiring only RGB. The key methodological departure is a progressive, multi-step optimization that sequentially decouples deformation prediction (via a DeformNet with ARAP regularization), motion parameter fitting (with Chamfer distance geometric supervision), and joint refinement—addressing the ill-posedness that causes PARIS to exhibit instability and suboptimality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first self-supervised framework to produce photorealistic, part-segmented digital twins of articulated objects in 3D-GS form, enabling downstream applications in robotics simulation, animation, and sim-to-real transfer that require both geometry and appearance. The progressive optimization scheme is a practical recipe for tackling highly coupled parameter spaces that could generalize beyond articulated objects. Limitations include the need for world-coordinate alignment and consistent lighting between the two states, the single-movable-part assumption per sub-process (multi-part handled sequentially), and degraded performance on real-world scans where mask imperfections and annotation noise affect evaluation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous articulation (beyond two discrete states) and to objects with more than one degree of freedom per joint.
