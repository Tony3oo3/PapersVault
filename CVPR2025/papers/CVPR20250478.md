## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation methods that fine-tune text-to-audio diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off: simple control signals (onset masks, energy) are easy to predict from video but lose semantic detail, while richer signals (full mel-spectrograms) preserve information but are too complex to predict accurately. The paper asks how to represent the mel-spectrogram so that it is simultaneously easy to predict from visual features and precise enough to govern fine-grained audio synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent video (resampled to T×f_mel/4 frames) and a sound event text label as input. It produces a mel-spectrogram of shape K×(T×f_mel) that is decomposed into three predicted signals — a quantized semantic vector, a continuous energy scalar, and a continuous standard deviation scalar per time slot — which are recomposed into a Mel-QCD representation fed to ControlNet. The final mel-spectrogram is converted to an audio waveform via a HiFi-GAN vocoder.

## Method

Mel-QCD decomposes each mel time slot into a semantic shape vector S, an energy scalar E, and a standard deviation scalar D (M = E + S×D). The semantic vector is quantized by rounding and clamping to [-λ, λ], then treated as a classification over a codebook of (2λ+1)^K combinations (with K=8, λ=1, factorized into two 81-way classifications); energy and standard deviation are regressed as continuous scalars. A V2X predictor (SynchFormer visual encoder + transformer/MLP heads) predicts all three signals from the video. The recomposed Mel-QCD is injected as a ControlNet condition into an Auffusion-based T2A diffusion model, while a textual inversion module maps CLIP video embeddings to pseudo-word tokens that augment the text prompt for global semantic alignment. Training uses the standard diffusion denoising loss with both ControlNet and the inversion adapter trained jointly.

## Results

On the VGGSound test set, Mel-QCD achieves state-of-the-art performance on 6 out of 8 evaluation metrics: FID 11.73, MKL 2.96, Class ACC 45.91%, W-Dis 0.33, JS-Div 0.11, and IB-AA 0.52. It trails VTA-LDM by ~1.4 points on Onset ACC (25.42 vs. 26.83) and Seeing-and-Hearing on IB-AV (0.31 vs. 0.38), the latter attributed to Seeing-and-Hearing's use of ImageBind as its visual encoder. The method also outperforms the closest T2A-based competitor FoleyCrafter by a wide margin on every metric (e.g., FID 11.73 vs. 13.11, Class ACC 45.91% vs. 31.54%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mel-QCD outperforms all seven baselines on FID, MKL, Class ACC, W-Dis, and IB-AA. Against the closest from-scratch V2A method VTA-LDM, it wins on FID (11.73 vs. 11.77), MKL (2.96 vs. 4.72), Class ACC (45.91% vs. 27.72%), W-Dis (0.33 vs. 0.37), and IB-AA (0.52 vs. 0.44), but concedes Onset ACC by 1.4 points (25.42 vs. 26.83), which the authors attribute to VTA-LDM's larger training data. Against the most similar T2A-based pipeline FoleyCrafter, Mel-QCD leads on every metric, with the largest gaps in Class ACC (+14.37 pts) and MKL (−1.18). Seeing-and-Hearing is the only method that beats Mel-QCD on any metric (IB-AV: 0.38 vs. 0.31), an advantage the authors note stems from its shared ImageBind visual encoder. The from-scratch baselines (SpecVQGAN, Im2Wav, DiffFoley) and the T2A baseline Auffusion are all substantially outperformed across all eight metrics, with Auffusion showing the weakest overall performance (FID 22.26, Class ACC 18.36%). In the control-signal comparison (Table 2), Mel-QCD also surpasses onset, energy, low-resolution mel, CLIP embeddings, and EnCodec codecs on predicted-signal FID, MKL, and Class ACC, confirming its superior completeness–complexity trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet-based video-to-audio generation paradigm established by FoleyCrafter (onset signals) and ReWaS (energy signals) by proposing a richer yet still predictable control signal. It builds directly on Auffusion as the text-to-audio diffusion backbone and repurposes the ControlNet mechanism from text-to-image generation, but departs from prior V2A methods by decomposing the mel-spectrogram into a quantized semantic component (SVQ codebook) and continuous energy/standard-deviation components, rather than relying on a single coarse signal like onset or energy. The textual inversion module for global semantic refinement is borrowed from image personalization literature and adapted here to compensate for local prediction inaccuracies in the Mel-QCD signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete trade-off in V2A: control signals that are too simple (onset, energy) lose semantic detail, while those that are too complex (full mel, EnCodec codebooks) become hard to predict from video, and the optimal point lies in a hybrid quantization-continuum decomposition. This analytical framing could inform future work on other conditional generation tasks where a rich signal must be predicted from a different modality. Limitations include evaluation only on VGGSound (10-second clips, 100 sound classes), a slight deficit in Onset ACC versus VTA-LDM, and the IB-AV metric being confounded by Seeing-and-Hearing's use of ImageBind as its visual encoder. Open questions include whether the decomposition generalizes to longer-form audio, more diverse soundscapes, or streaming/real-time settings.
