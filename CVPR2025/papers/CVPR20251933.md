## Problem

Existing prototype-based WSSS methods rely on a single prototype per class to activate features, which is insufficient to capture the full range of intra-class variations. Foreground features in many images are sparse and dispersed across the feature space, so a single prototype can only fully activate nearby features while leaving distant clusters partially or unactivated, resulting in incomplete CAM predictions that degrade downstream segmentation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251933/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images with image-level multi-label class labels, using an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 encoder to extract multi-scale features (F1–F4) that are linearly projected into a shared space. It produces per-class prototype CAMs (more complete than standard classifier CAMs) which, after DenseCRF and IRN refinement, yield pseudo segmentation masks used to train a fully supervised segmentation network. The framework is plug-and-play and can be attached to existing WSSS baselines such as CLIP-ES or SFC without modifying their core architecture.

## Method

POT divides the feature allocation problem into three stages. First, k-means clustering (K=3) partitions confident CAM features (above threshold τ=0.7) into multiple clusters per class, generating multiple cluster prototypes that capture diverse intra-class attributes. Second, a similarity-aware optimal transport (solved via Sinkhorn) assigns all HW feature vectors to these clusters, where the target marginal constraint is weighted by the softmax of cosine similarities between each cluster prototype and the corresponding classifier CAM prototype—prioritizing more discriminative clusters over less informative ones. Third, cluster prototypes are regenerated from all allocated features, features are activated via cosine similarity with their assigned prototype, and the K per-cluster CAMs are reweighted by the transport plan and summed to form the final complete CAM. An OT-based consistency loss with pixel-level adaptive weights (derived from the binary entropy of transport plan values) further regularizes training by enforcing stronger constraints on high-certainty pixels and weaker constraints on uncertain ones.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, POT integrated with CLIP-ES achieves 76.1% mIoU (val) and 76.7% mIoU (test), surpassing the previous best (CLIP-ES+CPAL at 74.5%/74.7%) by over 1.5 points. On MS COCO 2014, the method reaches 47.9% mIoU (val), outperforming CLIP-ES+PSDPM (47.2%) and CLIP-ES+CPAL (46.8%). When plugged into the SFC baseline, POT also yields consistent gains: 74.5%/75.2% mIoU on VOC val/test and 47.6% on COCO val, compared to SFC alone at 71.2%/72.5%/46.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POT is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on two strong baselines: CLIP-ES and SFC. On CLIP-ES, POT improves VOC val mIoU from 71.1% to 76.1% (+5.0%) and COCO val from 45.4% to 47.9% (+2.5%), decisively outperforming other CLIP-ES extensions such as PSDPM (74.1%/47.2%) and CPAL (74.5%/46.8%). On SFC, POT lifts VOC val from 71.2% to 74.5% (+3.3%) and COCO val from 46.8% to 47.6% (+0.8%). Against all 17 baselines listed in Table 2 (spanning CVPR'21 through CVPR'24), CLIP-ES+Ours achieves the highest mIoU on every benchmark, with the largest margins over older methods like AdvCAM (68.1% VOC val) and SIPE (68.8% VOC val). The consistent gains across both baselines confirm that the single-prototype limitation is the primary bottleneck in prior prototype-based WSSS methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based WSSS line of work (SIPE, SFC, PSDPM, CPAL) by replacing the single per-class prototype with multiple cluster prototypes, motivated by the observation that intra-class variation causes foreground features to form sparse, dispersed clusters that a single prototype cannot fully activate. The key methodological departure is the use of optimal transport (specifically Sinkhorn OT) to learn an adaptive feature-to-cluster allocation, with a novel similarity-aware marginal constraint that biases the target distribution toward cluster prototypes most aligned with the classifier CAM prototype. The framework is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play module that can be grafted onto existing WSSS pipelines (demonstrated on CLIP-ES and SFC), rather than a standalone end-to-end system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and consistent gains across two strong baselines (CLIP-ES, SFC) make this a practically useful component for the WSSS community, and the similarity-aware OT constraint offers a principled way to handle the varying importance of cluster prototypes that uniform OT ignores. However, the method still depends on the quality of initial classifier CAMs for the confident-feature partitioning step, and the fixed K=3 clustering and threshold τ=0.7 are dataset-specific hyperparameters that may not transfer well to more complex scenes or larger class counts. Open questions include whether the multi-cluster formulation scales gracefully beyond 20–80 classes, whether the OT-based consistency loss generalizes to video or 3D segmentation, and whether the approach can be made fully training-free (as CLIP-ES is) rather than requiring a distillation step.
