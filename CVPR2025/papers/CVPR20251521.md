## Problem

Existing Video-LLMs suffer from action-scene hallucination, where models predict actions from scene context (e.g., 'skiing' for an empty snowy mountain) or infer scenes from observed actions (e.g., 'boxing ring' for someone boxing in a library). The paper identifies two root causes: standard causal attention re-entangles spatial and temporal features within the LLM, and standard RoPE assigns 1-D sequential position IDs that bias text tokens toward whichever token type is closer in sequence order.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (T frames at H×W resolution) and a text question as input. The video is encoded by CLIP into frame-level patch embeddings, then split into spatial tokens (via temporal pooling + 2×2 spatial pooling, yielding N tokens) and temporal tokens (CLS tokens + spatially pooled tokens + frame-difference tokens, yielding M=3T−1 tokens). Output is a free-form text answer; on UNSCENE this is either a binary yes/no response to hallucination-probing questions or an open-ended video description scored 0–5 by GPT-3.5.

## Method

MASH-VLM introduces two mechanisms within a 7B LLM backbone to disentangle spatial and temporal representations. First, DST-attention applies a disentangling mask that blocks all direct attention between spatial and temporal tokens, while using causal attention among temporal tokens (to preserve temporal order), bi-directional attention among spatial tokens (since spatial layout is inherently bidirectional), and allowing text tokens to attend to both types. Second, Harmonic-RoPE expands the positional ID dimensionality: even-indexed feature pairs receive 'balanced' IDs where spatial and temporal tokens share the same position relative to text tokens, while odd-indexed pairs retain standard distinct sequential IDs. This dual encoding gives the model both absolute ordering and balanced relative-distance information, preventing text tokens from over-relying on one token type. The model is instruction-tuned on ~430k video-text pairs using standard autoregressive loss.

## Results

On the novel UNSCENE benchmark, MASH-VLM achieves 57.85% average on the binary QA task, surpassing the previous best (VideoChat2 at 35.15%) by over 22 points, and 3.3/5 on the open-ended task (vs. 2.9 for VideoChat2). On MVBench, MASH-VLM attains a state-of-the-art average of 57.6%, outperforming ST-LLM (54.9%) and VideoChat2 (51.1%). On VCG-Bench, MASH-VLM scores 4.46/5 average, substantially exceeding ST-LLM (3.15) and Chat-UniVi (2.99). On zero-shot video QA, MASH-VLM achieves competitive scores (e.g., 74.4% accuracy on MSVD-QA, 72.5% on TGIF-QA) with the highest GPT-evaluated scores across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASH-VLM decisively outperforms all baselines on the UNSCENE benchmark: it leads the binary QA by 16.6 points over VideoChat2 (57.85% vs. 35.15%) and 16.6 points over Chat-UniVi (57.85% vs. 41.27%), with the largest margin on the unusual-context scene task (80.25% vs. 51.04% for Chat-UniVi). On MVBench, MASH-VLM surpasses ST-LLM (57.6% vs. 54.9%) and VideoChat2 (57.6% vs. 51.1%), ranking first or second on 14 of 20 sub-tasks. On VCG-Bench, MASH-VLM achieves a 1.31-point average advantage over ST-LLM (4.46 vs. 3.15), with particularly large gains in Correctness of Information (4.77 vs. 3.23) and Temporal Understanding (4.67 vs. 2.93). On zero-shot video QA, MASH-VLM matches or exceeds ST-LLM on GPT-evaluated scores across all four datasets (e.g., 4.0 vs. 3.9 on MSVD-QA, 4.1 vs. — on TGIF-QA), though ST-LLM retains a slight edge on raw accuracy for MSRVTT-QA (63.2% vs. 61.9%) and ActivityNet-QA (50.9% vs. 49.3%). GPT-4V, while strong on some MVBench sub-tasks (e.g., 83.5% on Action Order), lags significantly on overall MVBench (43.5%) and is not evaluated on UNSCENE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Video-ChatGPT line of work, which separates spatial and temporal features via input-level pooling before the LLM, by arguing that such disentanglement is insufficient because the LLM's standard causal attention re-entangles the features. The key departure is performing spatial-temporal disentanglement *within* the LLM via a masked attention mechanism (DST-attention) rather than only at the input boundary. Additionally, the paper builds on standard Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) by proposing Harmonic-RoPE, which expands the positional ID dimensionality to assign balanced relative positions to spatial and temporal tokens, addressing a multimodal-specific limitation of 1D sequential RoPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UNSCENE benchmark (1,320 videos, 4,078 QA pairs) fills a gap in quantitative evaluation of video hallucination, where prior work (Vista-LLaMA) offered only qualitative results. The practical insight that input-level feature separation is undone by LLM attention is broadly applicable to any multimodal architecture that pre-processes visual tokens. Open questions include whether the specific attention mask design generalizes to longer videos or more granular spatial-temporal decompositions, and whether the hallucination mitigation transfers to other failure modes beyond action-scene confusion. The large VCG-Bench gap (4.46 vs. 3.15) suggests the disentanglement may improve general video dialogue quality, not just hallucination.
