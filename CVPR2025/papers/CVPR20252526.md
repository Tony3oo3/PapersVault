## Problem

Human reconstruction and scene reconstruction/SfM have evolved independently: human meshes lack anchoring in their surrounding environment, while scene reconstructions exclude people and cannot recover metric scale. No prior method jointly reconstructs multiple human meshes, scene point clouds, and camera parameters in a unified metric world coordinate system from sparse uncalibrated multi-view images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252526/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of 2–6 uncalibrated RGB images of a scene with people at a single moment, assuming known person re-identification across views. It outputs SMPL-X human meshes (pose, shape, orientation, translation) in world coordinates, dense per-view scene pointmaps, camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and a global metric scale factor α that aligns the SfM reconstruction to real-world dimensions. Notably, the scale is recovered implicitly from human body statistics rather than from explicit calibration or known object sizes.

## Method

HSfM initializes all components from pretrained models (HMR2 for 3D human meshes, DUSt3R for scene pointmaps and cameras, ViTPose for 2D keypoints), then analytically estimates a consistent metric scale α and human world positions γ to place everything in a common world frame. Joint optimization proceeds in two phases: first refining {α, γ, β} without scene loss, then optimizing all parameters under a combined objective of (1) bundle adjustment on 2D human keypoint reprojection errors and (2) a global scene alignment loss (adapted from DUSt3R) that merges per-view pointmaps into world space. The key insight is that human mesh predictions inject metric scale into the reconstruction, 2D keypoints provide reliable correspondences for bundle adjustment, and scene structure stabilizes camera poses—creating a synergistic feedback loop where each component improves the others. Unlike DUSt3R, the scale is not regularized to avoid a trivial optimum because humans constrain it; unlike UnCaliPose, scene losses prevent camera overfitting to human keypoints alone.

## Results

On EgoHumans, HSfM reduces world-coordinate human localization error (W-MPJPE) from 3.51 m (UnCaliPose) to 1.04 m and improves relative rotation accuracy (RRA@15) from 0.39 to 0.89, while also surpassing MASt3R on metric camera translation error (TE: 2.09 m vs. 4.97 m) and camera center accuracy (CCA@15: 0.46 vs. 0.07). On EgoExo4D, W-MPJPE drops from 3.59 m (UnCaliPose) to 0.50 m, and CCA@15 improves by 33% over the initialization. The joint optimization also improves scene-relevant camera metrics over DUSt3R (RRA@15: 0.89 vs. 0.74 on EgoHumans) and MASt3R (s-CCA@15: 0.91 vs. 0.86).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UnCaliPose [55], HSfM achieves a 3.4× reduction in W-MPJPE on EgoHumans (1.04 m vs. 3.51 m) and a 7.2× reduction on EgoExo4D (0.50 m vs. 3.59 m), while also improving RRA@15 by over 100% on EgoHumans (0.89 vs. 0.39) and 187% on EgoExo4D (0.89 vs. 0.31). Against DUSt3R [53], HSfM wins on RRA@15 (0.89 vs. 0.74) and s-CCA@15 (0.91 vs. 0.74) on EgoHumans, confirming that incorporating human keypoints into SfM improves camera pose estimation. Against MASt3R [26], HSfM substantially outperforms on metric camera translation (TE: 2.09 m vs. 4.97 m) and CCA@15 (0.46 vs. 0.07) on EgoHumans, but on EgoExo4D MASt3R edges out HSfM on CCA@10/15 (0.09/0.17 vs. 0.05/0.14), likely because a single person provides weaker scale constraints. Overall, HSfM is the only method that simultaneously reports competitive human pose, camera pose, and scene reconstruction metrics, and it dominates on the multi-person EgoHumans benchmark where additional human correspondences strengthen the bundle adjustment.

## Relation to Prior Work

HSfM sits at the intersection of two independently developed lines of work: data-driven scene reconstruction (DUSt3R, MASt3R) that recovers pointmaps and camera poses without metric scale, and human mesh recovery (HMR2, SLAHMR) that provides statistically-scaled body poses but no scene context. It also extends the 'SfM with humans' thread (Virtual Correspondences, UnCaliPose) by adding the scene pointmap as a third jointly-optimized component rather than treating humans solely as correspondences for camera calibration. The key departure is the unified joint optimization that uses 2D human keypoints as bundle-adjustment correspondences and 3D human meshes as robust 3D structures within DUSt3R's global alignment loss, producing a single metric-scale world containing all three elements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a genuine bidirectional synergy: human body statistics provide the metric scale that pure SfM methods lack, while scene structure anchors camera poses that in turn correctly localize humans in the world. This opens the door to applications requiring coherent multi-person spatial understanding (social interaction analysis, AR/VR scene capture) without calibrated rigs. Limitations include sensitivity to optimization hyperparameters, the assumption of known cross-view re-identification, and the under-determined nature of the problem that necessitates strong data-driven initialization. Future directions noted by the authors include feed-forward formulations, integrated re-identification, and extension to video via MONSt3R.
