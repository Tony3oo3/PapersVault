## Problem

Video LLMs generate tens of thousands of visual tokens from video inputs, causing prohibitive inference costs due to quadratic attention complexity. Existing one-shot token pruning methods (e.g., FastV, PruMerge) are designed for image LLMs and fail for video because the model's attention shifts across different visual tokens at different decoding iterations, making static pruning prone to discarding critical tokens. No prior method dynamically accounts for both temporal redundancy across frames and spatial redundancy within frames during the decoding process.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play module on existing VLLMs (LLaVA-OneVision 0.5B/7B/72B), taking a default of 32 video frames (each yielding 196 visual tokens after the image encoder and projector) concatenated with a text prompt. The temporal merging stage uses a sliding window of 4 frames to group tokens, and the dynamic pruning stage evaluates attention at a configurable layer (default L=3) with a retention ratio P (default 0.7). The output is a standard generated text response; no architectural changes or parameter updates are required, and the method adds less than 10⁻³ seconds of overhead for 32 frames.

## Method

DyCoke employs a two-stage training-free compression. Stage 1 (Token Temporal Merging) operates at the prefilling stage: it slides a 4-frame window, splits tokens into Odd and Even groups, computes cosine similarity between corresponding positions in adjacent groups, and merges/prunes the most similar tokens, reducing visual tokens by 50–60%. Stage 2 (KV Cache Dynamic Pruning) operates at each decoding iteration: it computes cross-attention between the predicted token and visual tokens at layer L, retains only the top-P% attended tokens in the KV cache, and stores evicted tokens in a separate Dynamic Pruning (DP) cache. At subsequent iterations, if the attention distribution shifts (detected via cosine similarity between successive attention maps), tokens are dynamically swapped between the KV cache and DP cache, allowing the model to refocus on previously pruned tokens without recomputation. This dynamic mechanism is the key distinction from one-shot pruning, enabling the model to select a different subset of visual tokens at each decoding step while maintaining performance.

## Results

On LLaVA-OV-7B, DyCoke (K=0.5) retains only 18.75% of visual tokens (59% FLOPs ratio) while matching or exceeding the full-token baseline on most benchmarks: 52.08 vs 51.93 on ActivityNet-QA, 57.6 vs 57.1 on PerceptionTest, 59.5 vs 58.5 on VideoMME (wo), and 3.29 vs 3.30 on VideoDetailCaption. With 32 input frames, DyCoke achieves a 1.54× inference speedup and 1.4× memory reduction over the full-token baseline. On the 72B model, DyCoke (K=0.5) retains 18.75% of tokens at 60% FLOPs while matching full-token accuracy (52.81 vs 52.96 on ActNet-QA, 66.3 vs 66.2 on VideoMME).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyCoke is compared against two training-free token pruning baselines: FastV (one-shot pruning based on prefilling-stage attention scores) and LLaVA-PruMerge (one-shot pruning based on CLIP encoder attention). On LLaVA-OV-7B, DyCoke (K=0.5) outperforms FastV by +1.15 on ActivityNet-QA (52.08 vs 50.93), +0.9 on PerceptionTest (57.6 vs 56.7), +0.20 on VideoDC (3.29 vs 3.09), and +2.2 on VideoMME (59.5 vs 57.3), while using a higher FLOPs ratio (59% vs 43%). Against PruMerge, the gains are larger: +1.63 on ActivityNet-QA, +3.3 on PerceptionTest, +0.41 on VideoDC, and +6.6 on VideoMME. Notably, DyCoke at K=0.7 matches FastV's FLOPs ratio (43%) but still exceeds it on every benchmark. The key advantage is that DyCoke's dynamic pruning during decoding avoids the one-shot pruning failure mode where tokens important for later decoding steps are prematurely discarded, which causes FastV and PruMerge to degrade more sharply on long-sequence tasks like VideoDC and VideoMME.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyCoke extends the token compression line of work (ToMe, FastV, PruMerge, TESTA, TempMe) from static, one-shot pruning to a dynamic, two-stage scheme specifically designed for video LLMs. While FastV and PruMerge prune visual tokens once—based on prefilling-stage attention or CLIP encoder scores, respectively—DyCoke observes that VLLM attention over visual tokens is non-stationary across decoding iterations and introduces a dynamic KV-cache pruning mechanism that re-selects tokens at each step. The temporal merging stage (TTM) draws on the token-merging idea from ToMe and the temporal aggregation of TESTA/TempMe, but applies it as a lightweight sliding-window operation at the prefilling stage rather than as a learned module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-drop method delivering ~1.5× speedup and ~1.4× memory reduction while matching or slightly exceeding full-token accuracy, DyCoke is immediately deployable on existing VLLM checkpoints without retraining, which is practically significant for resource-constrained video understanding. The core insight—that video attention is temporally shifting and thus one-shot pruning is fundamentally inadequate—opens the door to more adaptive, inference-time token management strategies. Limitations include evaluation primarily on the LLaVA-OneVision family, the added bookkeeping overhead of the DP cache, and reliance on a single attention layer for pruning decisions; whether the dynamic pruning strategy generalizes to streaming video, autoregressive video generation, or non-LLaVA VLLM architectures remains open.
