## Problem

Echocardiography probe guidance requires models to simultaneously understand complex cardiac anatomy and the dynamic relationship between probe motion and resulting visual changes, yet prior methods have largely treated these as separate concerns. Existing probe guidance frameworks rely on imitation or reinforcement learning without principled representation learning strategies, and no prior work has addressed how to build a model that internalizes both anatomical knowledge and motion-induced visual dynamics in a unified way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of N historical visual-motion pairs, where each pair consists of a 2D ultrasound frame (30 fps) and a 6-DOF probe pose (3 translational + 3 rotational Euler angles) in the anatomical coordinate system. It outputs a single 6-DOF relative movement vector expressed as a rigid transformation (p* · p_t0^{-1}) that, when applied to the current probe pose, reaches the target standard plane. The dataset comprises ~1 million frames from 356 routine clinical scans (284 train / 72 test, no patient overlap), with ten clinically standard planes as guidance targets.

## Method

EchoWorld uses a two-stage pipeline. First, a JEPA-based cardiac world model is pre-trained with two complementary tasks: (a) spatial world modeling, which masks rectangular patch blocks and reconstructs their features via a predictor conditioned on mask tokens with sinusoidal positional encoding, and (b) motion world modeling, which predicts average-pooled target image features from context features plus a motion-encoded latent variable (the 6-DOF relative pose passed through a motion encoder), trained with an InfoNCE contrastive objective. The joint loss is L_spatial + 0.1·L_motion, with the target encoder maintained as an EMA of the context encoder (ViT-Small backbone, 6-block transformer predictor). Second, the pre-trained model is fine-tuned for probe guidance using a motion-aware attention mechanism that embeds pairwise 3D relative pose differences z_{i→j} into both key and value projections via MLP(h_j, z_{i→j}), so each query token attends over a unique set of key-value pairs encoding the spatial transformation to every other frame—replacing the standard permutation-invariant self-attention or interleaved image-action sequence formulations used by prior sequential guidance methods.

## Results

On the single-frame protocol, EchoWorld achieves a mean absolute guidance error of 8.15 (averaged over translation in mm and rotation in degrees across 10 standard planes), outperforming the best baseline EchoCLIP (8.37) by 0.22. On the sequential protocol, which simulates online deployment using historical visual-motion data, EchoWorld achieves 7.05 average error, surpassing the best prior method Seq-aware (7.42) by 0.37. EchoWorld attains the lowest error in 16 out of 20 per-plane translation/rotation subtasks under the single-frame setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the single-frame protocol, EchoWorld (8.15 avg) outperforms all 9 baselines, with the closest competitor being EchoCLIP (8.37), a vision-language foundation model for echocardiography, followed by USFM (8.42) and US-MAE (8.46). General-purpose models (DeiT 8.63, DINOv2 8.52) and medical foundation models (BioMedCLIP 8.74, LVM-Med 8.73) trail further behind, confirming the importance of in-domain ultrasound pre-training. In the sequential protocol, EchoWorld (7.05) beats Seq-aware (7.42), Decision Transformer (7.44), and US-GuideNet (7.72) by 0.37, 0.39, and 0.67 respectively, all using the same pre-trained visual backbone to isolate the contribution of the motion-aware attention module. The consistent advantage across both protocols indicates that the world-model pre-training improves representations while the motion-aware attention mechanism more effectively exploits historical visual-motion context than interleaved sequence encodings used by prior guidance frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoWorld builds on the JEPA framework (LeCun, 2022) for its world-modeling pre-training architecture and adapts MAE-style masked patch reconstruction (He et al., 2022) as the spatial modeling task, while introducing a contrastive motion-prediction objective as a novel second pre-training signal. It departs from prior probe guidance methods (US-GuideNet, Decision Transformer, Seq-aware) that organize historical data into interleaved image-action sequences processed by recurrent or transformer decoders; instead, EchoWorld embeds pairwise 3D relative pose differences directly into attention key-value features, aligning the attention mechanism with the rigid-body geometry of probe motion. The paper also positions itself against ultrasound-specific foundation models (USFM, EchoCLIP, US-MAE) by arguing that domain-specific pre-training alone is insufficient without explicitly modeling the visual-motion coupling inherent to scanning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled path toward AI-assisted or autonomous echocardiography, addressing the global shortage of trained sonographers by encoding both anatomical knowledge and motion-induced visual dynamics in a single representation. The world model's demonstrated ability to simulate visual changes from probe movements (via RCDM) opens the door to using it as a cardiac ultrasound simulator for data augmentation or policy evaluation. However, evaluation is limited to a single 356-scan dataset with offline protocols, and no real-time deployment or cross-patient generalization study is provided; whether the motion-aware attention mechanism transfers to other imaging modalities or longer-horizon planning tasks remains an open question.
