## Problem

Standard ℓ_p threat models used in adversarial robustness evaluation are isotropic and global: they assign the same threat to all perturbations of equal norm regardless of direction or input location. This makes them unable to distinguish safe corruptions (e.g., blur, noise, compression that preserve the true label) from unsafe ones that alter the label, rendering perfect robust accuracy under ℓ_p neither achievable nor desirable. The paper asks whether a threat model can be designed that is both anisotropic (direction-sensitive) and local (input-dependent) to properly disentangle safe from unsafe perturbations.

## Input / Output

Given an input image x, a perturbation δ, and a training set S (from which k representative points per class are selected via discrete k-center optimization), the method computes a scalar PD threat score d_PD(x, δ). The sublevel set S(x, d_PD, ε) is a convex polytope (intersection of at most k·C half-spaces), enabling both a fast approximate projection (scaling δ by ε/d_PD) and an exact ℓ_2 projection via an iterative greedy algorithm. Optional segmentation masks (from SAM) and WordNet class-hierarchy distances can be incorporated to produce PD-S and PD-W variants, respectively.

## Method

The Projected Displacement (PD) threat measures the maximum alignment of a perturbation δ with a set of locally estimated unsafe directions U(x), normalized by the approximate distance to the class boundary along each direction. Unsafe directions are computed from training data: for each input x with label y, directions toward k representative points of every other class c≠y (selected via discrete k-center) form U(x), and the normalization g(x,u) = β‖x̃−x‖₂ scales by the distance to the nearest observed point of a different class. This yields an anisotropic, local threat whose sublevel sets are convex, enabling plug-and-play adaptation of existing gradient-based attacks (e.g., AutoAttack) via projection. Two extensions refine the threat: PD-S restricts the inner product to foreground pixels identified by a segmentation mask, and PD-W rescales the normalization by a WordNet-based inter-class distance so that confusion between semantically nearby classes incurs higher threat than confusion between distant ones.

## Results

Under the combined (d_∞ ∩ d_PD) threat model with ε=16/255 on ImageNet-1k, robust accuracies uniformly improve over the standard d_∞ evaluation: ConvNeXt-L rises from 25% to 32%, Swin-B from 12% to 21%, and XCiT-L12 from 4% to 17%. The PD-S (segmentation-aware) and PD-W (WordNet-hierarchy-aware) variants yield nearly identical robust accuracies to the base PD threat (within 1 percentage point), suggesting that current adversarial attacks are already aligned with foreground regions and that models struggle to distinguish semantically distant classes. Qualitatively, PD threat matches DreamSim (d_DS) in separating safe corruptions (noise, blur, compression) from unsafe ones in the quadrant analysis of Figure 3, while d_∞ fails to do so.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DreamSim (d_DS) [15], the state-of-the-art neural perceptual threat model. In the quadrant analysis (Figure 3), PD threat and DreamSim are broadly aligned: both place low-severity corruptions in the low-threat quadrant and high-severity weather/digital corruptions in the high-threat quadrant, and both correctly assign low threat to blur and Gaussian noise that d_∞ misclassifies as high-threat. However, PD threat offers practical advantages over DreamSim: it requires no pre-trained large vision model or fine-tuning on human perceptual judgements, its sub-level sets are convex (enabling efficient exact projection via a greedy algorithm), and its growth along any direction is linear and interpretable. Against the standard d_∞ threat model, PD threat clearly wins on the core task of disentangling safe from unsafe perturbations: d_∞ assigns equal threat to a Gaussian noise corruption and a class-altering perturbation of the same magnitude, while PD threat naturally separates them. The segmentation-aware (PD-S) and hierarchy-aware (PD-W) variants show negligible differences from base PD in robust accuracy, indicating that the added task information does not substantially change the threat landscape for current attack methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in adversarial threat model specification by proposing a geometric construction—Projected Displacement (PD)—that sits conceptually between the simplicity of ℓp norms and the complexity of neural perceptual metrics like DreamSim [15] and Laidlaw et al. [32]. Rather than learning a perceptual embedding, it estimates unsafe directions directly from the class partition of observed training data via k-center optimization, yielding an anisotropic and local threat function with convex sub-level sets. The task-aware variants (PD-S with SAM segmentation masks, PD-W with WordNet class hierarchy) repurpose existing annotation tools in a way that neither ℓp nor DreamSim frameworks naturally support, positioning PD as a flexible, task-driven specification rather than a fixed metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PD threat model offers practitioners a drop-in replacement for ℓp norms that can admit large-norm safe corruptions (blur, noise, compression) while still excluding label-altering perturbations, without requiring pre-trained vision models or fine-tuning on human similarity judgements. The convexity of sub-level sets enables efficient exact projection, making it immediately compatible with existing gradient-based attack pipelines like AutoAttack. Open questions include whether adaptive attacks specifically designed for the PD geometry can further stress-test robust models, whether training strategies can be developed to encourage PD-robustness, and how the quality of the k-center approximation of unsafe directions degrades under class imbalance or limited data coverage.
