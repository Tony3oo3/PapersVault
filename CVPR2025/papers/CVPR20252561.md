## Problem

Existing reflection removal methods rely on additive degradation models designed for natural scene reflection, where the reflection layer can be separated from the background. However, eyeglass reflections degrade only a small localized region, making layer separation infeasible, and strong reflections cause complete loss of eye content that cannot be recovered by simple decomposition. Additionally, prior methods fail to restore fine local eye textures without artifacts and to maintain illumination consistency with the surrounding face.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image containing eyeglass reflections and produces a reflection-free image at the same resolution. The input image serves a dual role: it is both the degraded image to be restored and the reference for global illumination consistency in the final refinement stage. An eye-area mask obtained from a glass detector is used internally to partition features into eye and non-eye regions during the global consistency refinement step.

## Method

DL2G is a three-stage framework. First, a Degradation Estimation Module (DEM) trained as a simple U-Net estimates a multiplicative degradation map D under the model I = T ∘ D (inspired by Retinex theory), yielding a preliminary result T̃ = I./(D+η) that alleviates weak reflections. Second, a Local Structure-aware Diffusion Model (LSDM) is trained with a local structure sampling strategy: a sliding window (64×64, stride 16) segments both the target and condition images into overlapping patches, forcing the conditional denoiser to learn the true distribution of local eye textures rather than the global face, thereby recovering lost content in strong reflection regions without artifacts. Third, a Global Consistency Refinement (GCR) module uses two transformer blocks to fuse the eye-region features from the LSDM output with non-eye-region features from the original input image, ensuring the final result is harmonious in color and illumination with the surrounding face.

## Results

On the ReyeR+ dataset, DL2G achieves the best performance across all three metrics: PSNR 34.801, SSIM 0.947, and LPIPS 0.040, outperforming the strongest baseline ER²Net (34.301 / 0.935 / 0.050) by 0.5 dB in PSNR and 0.012 in SSIM. On the SHIQ specular highlight dataset, DL2G attains PSNR 28.145 and SSIM 0.946, surpassing TSHRNet (25.575 / 0.933) and SpecularityNet (23.420 / 0.920), though trailing JSHDR (34.131) on PSNR while leading on SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DL2G outperforms all six ReyeR+ baselines on every metric. The closest competitor is ER²Net [40], which is also purpose-built for eyeglass reflection removal; DL2G edges it by 0.5 dB PSNR, 0.012 SSIM, and 0.010 LPIPS, with the gap widening on complex-reflection samples where ER²Net struggles to restore invisible eye content. Among general reflection-removal methods, DSRNet [11] is the strongest (33.555 PSNR), still 1.25 dB behind DL2G, while IBCLN [16] and Robust SIRR [25] trail by roughly 2 dB. TSHRNet [8], a specular-highlight method repurposed for reflection removal, performs poorly (20.257 PSNR), confirming that highlight-removal architectures do not transfer well to the localized eyeglass setting. On the SHIQ specular highlight benchmark, DL2G beats TSHRNet and SpecularityNet on both PSNR and SSIM but falls short of JSHDR on PSNR (28.1 vs 34.1), though it leads JSHDR on SSIM (0.946 vs 0.860), suggesting better structural fidelity at the cost of pixel-level accuracy on that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant additive reflection degradation model (I = T + R + Φ) used by IBCLN, DSRNet, and most prior reflection removal work, arguing that for the localized eyeglass reflection case, a multiplicative model (I = T ∘ D) inspired by Retinex theory is more physically appropriate since the reflection only degrades a small region and separating a reflection layer is neither feasible nor useful. It builds on the diffusion-model-for-restoration line (ShadowDiffusion, Diff-Retinex) but introduces a local structure sampling strategy that processes overlapping patches rather than the full image, addressing the observation that whole-face conditioning prevents the diffusion model from focusing on eye-level detail. The framework also extends ER²Net's joint elimination-and-inpainting approach by explicitly separating degradation alleviation, local content restoration, and global illumination refinement into distinct stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ReyeR+ dataset (2,000 additional pairs with complex foregrounds and 33% eye-invisible cases) fills a practical gap in eyeglass reflection removal benchmarks, and the local structure sampling technique for conditional diffusion models is a reusable idea for other localized restoration tasks. However, the method has clear limitations: it fails when glasses are not on the eyes or when special lens materials project the front scene completely, and the multiplicative model cannot handle cases where the reflection and background are truly inseparable. Open questions include whether the local-to-global decomposition generalizes to other small-region degradation problems (e.g., lens smudges, partial occlusions) and whether the 100-step diffusion inference cost is acceptable for real-time applications.
