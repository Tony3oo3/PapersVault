## Problem

Existing post-hoc OOD detection methods primarily reshape features or adjust logits but neglect the rich structural information embedded within sample features themselves. In particular, no prior method exploits class-specific feature centroids or the fine-grained sign-alignment relationship between relative features and classifier weights to discriminate ID from OOD samples.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained classifier (no retraining required) and takes RGB images as input (224×224 for ImageNet-1k, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100). It requires a one-time precomputation of per-class average feature vectors from the ID training set. The output is a single scalar OOD score (the negative of a normalized relative error); samples with scores below a threshold γ are flagged as OOD and the model abstains from prediction.

## Method

CARef computes the normalized L1 distance between a test sample's penultimate-layer feature and the average feature of its predicted class, using this relative error as the OOD score. CADRef extends this by decoupling the relative feature into positive and negative components based on sign alignment between each relative feature dimension and the corresponding weight in the maximum-logit row of the classifier. The positive error (which contributes to the max logit) is divided by the sample's logit-based score (e.g., Energy), while the negative error is divided by the mean logit score over all ID training samples, yielding a fused score that amplifies the ID/OOD gap. This design specifically addresses the observed overlap in positive-error distributions at high logit scores, where logit-based scaling restores separability.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1k benchmark averaged across seven architectures (ResNet-50, RegNetX-8GF, DenseNet-201, ViT-B/16, Swin-B, ConvNeXt-B, MaxViT-T), CADRef achieves 88.19% AUROC and 50.30% FPR95, while CARef achieves 87.74% AUROC and 52.59% FPR95. These represent improvements of 3.27% AUROC and 6.32% FPR95 over the best baseline (OptFS: 84.92% AUROC, 56.62% FPR95). On CIFAR-10, CADRef achieves 97.77% AUROC and 11.56% FPR95 (best overall), while on CIFAR-100 it reaches 90.55% AUROC and 40.34% FPR95, slightly trailing ASH-S (90.29% AUROC) on AUROC but matching it on FPR95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADRef outperforms all ten baselines on average AUROC across the seven ImageNet-1k architectures, with the largest margins over ASH-S (+33.93% AUROC) and DICE (+21.71% AUROC), which suffer severe performance collapse on transformer and modern CNN architectures (ASH-S drops below 20% AUROC on ViT, Swin, and ConvNeXt). Against the strongest feature-based baseline OptFS (84.92% AUROC), CADRef gains +3.27% AUROC and −6.32% FPR95; against the strongest logit-based baseline GEN (83.15% AUROC), the gap is +5.04% AUROC. The closest competitor is ViM (84.80% AUROC, 59.97% FPR95), which CADRef surpasses by +3.39% AUROC and −9.67% FPR95 on average; notably, on DenseNet-201 ViM's performance nearly matches plain Energy, suggesting it underutilizes feature information. On CIFAR-10, CADRef (97.77% AUROC, 11.56% FPR95) beats ASH-S (97.25%, 13.62%) and ViM (96.70%, 17.75%), while on CIFAR-100 it trails ASH-S by 0.26% AUROC but leads by 4.41% FPR95. A key robustness finding is that feature-shaping methods (ASH-S, DICE, ReAct) exhibit architecture-specific behavior and collapse on non-ResNet models, whereas CARef/CADRef maintain top-2 rankings across all seven architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the post-hoc OOD detection landscape by departing from the dominant paradigm of feature reshaping (ReAct, ASH, DICE, OptFS) that modifies features and re-derives logits. Instead, CARef directly uses the normalized l1-deviation of a sample's feature from its class-aware average as the OOD score, treating the feature space itself as the discriminative space rather than a preprocessing step for logit computation. CADRef then bridges back to logits through a sign-alignment decoupling of relative features with classifier weights, a mechanism not present in prior feature-based or logit-based methods. The closest conceptual neighbor is ViM, which also fuses feature deviation with logit-based scores, but ViM projects onto a principal subspace rather than computing class-conditional relative errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in cross-architecture robustness: while feature-shaping methods like ASH-S collapse to near-random performance on ViT, Swin, and ConvNeXt, CARef/CADRef maintain top-two rankings across all seven tested architectures, making them a more reliable default for practitioners deploying diverse model families. A key limitation is that the logit-based scaling component (Energy) becomes actively harmful on extremely hard OOD benchmarks like ImageNet-O (where Energy drops to ~50% AUROC), suggesting the method's robustness is bounded by the quality of the chosen logit score. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned scaling could mitigate this vulnerability, and whether the class-aware averaging step generalizes to open-set or few-class settings where per-class statistics are unreliable.
