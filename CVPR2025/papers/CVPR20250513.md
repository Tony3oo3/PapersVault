## Problem

Existing event-based video super-resolution methods rely on CNNs and attention-based architectures, which suffer from limited receptive fields for capturing inter-frame correlations at high resolutions, poor adaptability to dynamic inputs, and high computational costs. No prior work has explored state space models (SSMs) for this task, despite their ability to provide global receptive field coverage with linear complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250513/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of low-resolution RGB frames (H×W×3) and forward/backward event streams as input. Event streams are converted to voxel grids (H×W×5) via temporal bilinear interpolation and normalized by clipping at the 98th percentile to suppress hot pixels. The output is a super-resolved video sequence at scale factor s (×2 or ×4), produced by a reconstructor that combines bidirectional cross-modality features with the LR frame and its encoded features through pixel-shuffling upsampling.

## Method

MamEVSR employs a bidirectional recurrent pipeline (For/Back cells) where each cell contains two novel blocks. The iMamba block interleaves tokens from two adjacent frames into a single sequence and applies multi-directional selective SSM scanning, enabling global inter-frame feature fusion with linear complexity while preserving local spatio-temporal context; it replaces the standard MLP with a channel-attention block for enhanced feature propagation. The cMamba block performs cross-modal fusion between RGB features (from iMamba) and event voxel features using a cross-selective SSM where the output projection matrices C_I and C_E are swapped between the two modality branches, allowing each branch to decode information from the other's hidden state. The reconstructor combines forward and backward cross-modality features with the LR frame via an iMamba block, residual blocks, and pixel-shuffling. Training uses Charbonnier loss over 300K iterations with Adam and cosine annealing on 64×64 patches from 15-frame clips.

## Results

On the CED dataset (×2 VSR), MamEVSR achieves an average PSNR of 41.14 dB and SSIM of 0.9831, outperforming the strongest event-based baseline EvTexture+ (40.57 dB / 0.9815) by 0.57 dB. On the REDS4 dataset (×4 VSR), MamEVSR reaches 32.94 dB / 0.9195 SSIM, surpassing the best RGB-based method VRT (32.19 dB / 0.9006) by 0.75 dB and the best event-based method EvTexture (32.79 dB / 0.9174) by 0.15 dB. MamEVSR also achieves the best per-scene PSNR on 10 out of 11 CED scenes and all 4 REDS4 clips, with a parameter count of 9.6M (×4) and 10.1M (×2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MamEVSR outperforms all 12 baselines on both datasets. Among event-based methods, it beats EvTexture+ (the closest competitor) by 0.57 dB PSNR on CED ×2 (41.14 vs. 40.57) and by 0.01 dB on REDS4 ×4 (32.94 vs. 32.93), while also achieving higher SSIM in both cases. Against the strongest RGB-based baselines, MamEVSR surpasses VRT by 0.75 dB on REDS4 ×4 and BasicVSR by 1.57 dB on CED ×2, demonstrating that event data combined with Mamba's global receptive field yields meaningful gains over frame-only approaches. The gap over older event-based methods is substantial: EGVSR trails by over 4 dB on REDS4 ×4, and E-VSR lags by 3.82 dB on CED ×2. Notably, MamEVSR achieves these gains with fewer parameters than VRT (9.6M vs. 35.6M) and comparable parameter counts to other event-based methods, positioning it in the upper-left (high performance, low cost) quadrant of the efficiency–quality trade-off shown in Figure 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

MamEVSR sits at the intersection of two active research threads: selective state space models (Mamba) applied to vision (Vim, Vmamba, VideoMamba, MambaIR) and event-based video super-resolution (E-VSR, EGVSR, EBVSR, EvTexture). Architecturally, it adopts the bidirectional recurrent propagation scheme from BasicVSR but replaces the conventional alignment and fusion modules with two novel SSM-based blocks: the iMamba block, which interleaves tokens from adjacent frames to enable global inter-frame modeling at linear complexity, and the cMamba block, which performs cross-modal selective scanning between RGB and event features. This is the first application of Mamba to event-based VSR, departing from the CNN- and Transformer-based designs that dominate prior event-VSR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that SSMs can serve as a viable, linear-complexity backbone for event-based VSR, offering a practical alternative to the quadratic-cost attention mechanisms in prior methods. However, the performance gains over the strongest prior method (EvTexture+) are modest (~0.5 dB PSNR), and the authors acknowledge that fine texture recovery remains limited by information loss in both the low-resolution RGB and sparse event inputs. Open questions include whether longer temporal sequences, perceptual losses (e.g., VGG), or lighter-weight SSM variants could close the remaining gap, and whether the iMamba/cMamba design generalizes to other video restoration tasks beyond super-resolution.
