## Problem

Existing stereo depth estimation methods struggle to resolve high-frequency details and high-resolution depth maps under realistic camera conditions with large depth variations, and they typically treat hardware configuration as fixed, neglecting the potential of optical encoding to enrich the information available to the network. The frequency-domain complementarity between left and right stereo views remains largely unexplored as a design lever for joint stereo and focus cue exploitation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252823/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a pair of blurred stereo images (left and right) captured through a physical camera prototype with rank-2 diffractive optical elements placed at the aperture planes, operating over a working range of 0.67–8 m (~1.4 diopter) at 1200×1920 resolution. It produces an all-in-focus RGB image and a detailed disparity/depth map. The two DOEs are designed to encode complementary high-frequency information in perpendicular spatial directions, so each channel captures different frequency components that the network must fuse.

## Method

The core contribution is an end-to-end hardware-software co-design that jointly optimizes a pair of rank-2 parameterized diffractive optical elements (DOEs) and a stereo imaging neural network. Each DOE height map is expressed as the sum of two rank-1 outer products (a_i b_i^T), rotated 45° relative to the pixel grid and replicated across four quadrants for symmetry, with the left and right DOEs initialized as compound cylindrical lenses enforcing perpendicular PSF spreading directions. The network comprises an IGEV-based stereo matching module (group-wise correlation volume processed by a lightweight 3D regularization network, iteratively refined by ConvGRU) followed by a U-Net RGBD refinement network with a cross-cue fusion mechanism that warps the right view using the estimated disparity and fuses complementary PSF-encoded features. Training uses a differentiable alpha-compositing image formation model with three wavelengths and seven depth layers, a composite loss combining PSF concentration, feature-similarity RGB, and multi-scale disparity terms, and data augmentation including DOE position jitter and mirrored sub-batches to improve robustness to fabrication tolerances.

## Results

On the SceneFlow test set at 0.8 diopter working distance, the proposed Rank-2 DOE stereo framework (D-S) achieves 32.96 dB PSNR and 0.926 SSIM for RGB reconstruction, and 0.066 m RMSE / 1.12 px EPE for depth estimation. At the wider 1.4 diopter range (0.67–8 m), it reaches 32.13 dB PSNR, 0.917 SSIM, 0.071 m RMSE, and 1.21 px EPE. The paper reports a 2.96 dB PSNR gain and a 0.28 px EPE reduction over its own thin-lens baseline, with real-world prototype captures confirming superior high-frequency detail recovery across 0.67–8 m distances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the monocular deep-optics baseline DfD [Ikoma et al., ICCP 2021], the proposed D-S Rank-2 method achieves a 1.94 dB PSNR improvement (32.96 vs. 31.02) and nearly halves the depth RMSE (0.066 vs. 0.132 m) at 0.8 diopter, demonstrating that the stereo + complementary-encoding strategy substantially outperforms single-view defocus-based depth estimation. Against the stereo-matching-only baseline IGEV-S [Xu et al., CVPR 2023], D-S Rank-2 reduces EPE from 1.52 to 1.12 px (−26%) and RMSE from 0.078 to 0.066 m, showing that learned optical encoding provides meaningful depth cues beyond pure stereo correspondence. Compared to the authors' own thin-lens baseline (no DOE), the Rank-2 encoding yields a 2.96 dB PSNR gain and 0.28 px EPE reduction at 0.8 diopter, and a 2.96 dB / 0.28 px improvement at 1.4 diopter, confirming that the hardware-software co-design is the primary source of the performance gain rather than the network architecture alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep optics lineage (Ikoma et al. 2021, Sun et al. 2020) from monocular to binocular settings, introducing a pair of complementary rank-2 diffractive optical elements jointly optimized with a stereo matching network. It builds directly on the rank-1 DOE parameterization of Sun et al. [32] and the IGEV iterative stereo matching framework [41], while departing from Tan et al. [33] (coded stereo) by using asymmetric, complementary encodings rather than identical masks with separate processing. The alpha-compositing image formation model from Ikoma et al. [10] is adapted for multi-layer depth simulation, and the overall system represents the first end-to-end hardware-software co-design specifically targeting joint stereo and focus cues in a snapshot binocular configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that hardware-level optical encoding can meaningfully augment software-based stereo matching, achieving a 2.96 dB PSNR gain and improved high-frequency depth detail over a 0.67–8 m range—gains that are difficult to obtain through algorithmic improvements alone. However, the system is not real-time, exhibits artifacts in high-dynamic-range scenes (circular halos, cross-shaped artifacts from limited diffraction efficiency), and real-world validation is limited to four qualitative scenes without quantitative metrics. Open questions include whether the complementary-encoding principle generalizes to more than two channels, whether commercial-grade fabrication can close the simulation-to-prototype gap, and whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or extreme environments (low light, hyperspectral) as the authors suggest.
