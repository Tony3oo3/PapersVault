## Problem

Recovering 3D human-object interaction from single natural images remains unsolved because no dataset provides dense 3D contact correspondences on both the body and the object, no statistical model exists for arbitrary object shapes, and existing methods either require known object categories, operate only in controlled lab settings, or infer contacts that lack image grounding. This paper addresses both the data gap and the method gap simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250409/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unconstrained RGB image as input and produces a 3D SMPL-X human mesh, a 3D object mesh (retrieved from the Objaverse database), their relative 3D pose (rotation, translation, and instance-specific scale), and dense bijective body-object contact correspondences. For unlabeled images, the pipeline additionally requires SAM segmentation masks and DECO body-contact predictions as auxiliary signals, and uses GPT-4V for object scale initialization and contact disambiguation.

## Method

PICO-fit is a three-stage render-and-compare optimization that exploits dense body-object contact correspondences as the key geometric constraint. Stage 1 registers the object to the body by minimizing vertex-to-point contact distance using correspondences retrieved from PICO-db (queried via nearest-neighbor IoU matching on DECO-inferred body contacts). Stage 2 refines object pose and scale against a SAM-derived object mask (IoU loss) while penalizing human-object penetration via a signed distance field. Stage 3 refines only the limb pose parameters of the contacting body part against a SAM-derived human mask, a contact loss, and a pose-regularization term that anchors to the OSX initialization. The dataset PICO-db is built by retrieving 3D object meshes via OpenShape's joint image-shape latent space and projecting DAMON's body contact patches onto objects using a PCA-derived axis representation that requires only two clicks per patch, establishing bijective correspondences at scale via crowdsourcing.

## Results

On InterCap (an out-of-domain in-lab dataset), PICO-fit without ground-truth contact achieves the best combined human+object Procrustes-Aligned Chamfer Distance (PA-CD_{h+o}) of 10.33 cm, outperforming PHOSA (13.38 cm), CONTHO (13.14 cm), and HDM (13.6 cm). With GT contact (PICO-fit*), the combined error drops to 8.36 cm, with object error (PA-CD_o) of 13.34 cm vs. 20.91–24.30 cm for baselines. In a perceptual study on 75 in-the-wild DAMON images across 42 object categories, PICO-fit* reconstructions are preferred over baselines 74.4% of the time on average (79.9% vs. HDM, 75.3% vs. CONTHO*, 68.0% vs. PHOSA*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PICO-fit (without GT contact) beats all three baselines on the combined human+object metric (PA-CD_{h+o}: 10.33 vs. 13.14–13.60 cm) and on human-only error (PA-CD_h: 7.43 vs. 8.36–17.34 cm), while its object-only error (21.85 cm) is competitive with PHOSA (23.36 cm) but worse than HDM (14.12 cm). With GT contact, PICO-fit* dominates all baselines on every metric, reducing object error from 20.91–24.30 cm to 13.34 cm and combined error from 12.81–13.60 cm to 8.36 cm. In the perceptual study on in-the-wild images, PICO-fit* is preferred over HDM 79.9% of the time, over CONTHO* 75.3%, and over PHOSA* 68.0%, with an average preference of 74.4%. Notably, PICO-fit without GT contact still outperforms PHOSA* and CONTHO* (which do use GT contact) on the combined metric, demonstrating the robustness of its contact-retrieval strategy. A key qualitative advantage is that PICO-fit handles object classes (couches, bananas, frisbees) that no baseline can reconstruct, due to its OpenShape-based retrieval from PICO-db.

## Relation to Prior Work

PICO departs from prior 3D HOI reconstruction methods (PHOSA, CONTHO, HDM) that either require known object shapes, infer contact only on the body, or rely on hand-crafted category-wise constraints trained on synthetic data. It builds on DECO/DAMON's body-only contact annotations and ContactEdit's axis-based patch representation, but repurposes them through a novel two-click projection that establishes dense bijective body-object correspondences on natural images. The object shape retrieval via OpenShape's joint image-mesh latent space and the render-and-compare fitting pipeline with SDF-based penetration avoidance are assembled from existing components, but the PICO-db dataset and the contact-correspondence-driven initialization strategy are genuinely new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PICO-db is the first dataset pairing natural images with dense 3D contact on both body and object, filling a gap that has kept 3D HOI methods confined to limited object categories. The 74.4% perceptual preference and successful handling of previously untackled classes (couches, bananas, frisbees) demonstrate practical scalability. Open questions include whether the nearest-neighbor retrieval from PICO-db can be replaced by a learned contact regressor (the authors plan to use PICO-fit for pseudo-label generation), and whether vision-language models can extend beyond the finite 4,123-image dataset to truly open-world objects.
