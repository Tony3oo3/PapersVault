## Problem

Whether modern generative image-to-image models can learn physically correct mappings from input-output image pairs—and what speedups they offer over differential equation solvers—remains an open question lacking a systematic benchmark. While generative models have been extensively studied for appearance-based tasks like inpainting and style transfer, their ability to capture complex physical relations, especially those governed by higher-order differential equations, has not been rigorously evaluated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250140/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the three tasks provides 100k input-output image pairs at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution, with ground truth generated from physics solvers. For sound propagation, the input is a binary urban layout (buildings in black, open space in white) plus source parameters (frequency, dB level), and the output is a decibel-level map. For lens distortion, the input is a cropped CelebA face plus tangential distortion coefficients (p₁, p₂), and the output is the geometrically distorted face. For ball dynamics, the input is a scene showing ball position on an inclined surface plus a variable time interval, and the output is the ball's position and rotation at the future time.

## Method

PhysicsGen is a benchmark that systematically evaluates seven generative architectures (convAE, VAE, U-Net, Pix2Pix, DDPM, Stable Diffusion with and without cross-attention, DDBM) under identical training protocols across three physical tasks of increasing complexity: iterative wave propagation (sound), closed-form geometric mapping (lens distortion), and time-series dynamics (bouncing ball). The tasks are designed to span 0th-, 1st-, and 2nd-order physical relations, with evaluation metrics tailored to each domain: MAE and wMAPE split by line-of-sight regions for sound, landmark Euclidean distance for lens distortion, and position/rotation/roundness/ground-angle errors for ball dynamics. The key empirical finding is that while generative models achieve up to 20× speedups over simulation, they systematically degrade on tasks requiring higher-order physical terms, with distinct failure modes per model family—GANs produce physically plausible but incorrect scenes, autoencoders blur uncertain regions, and diffusion models generate multiple candidate solutions.

## Results

Across all three PhysicsGen tasks, generative models achieve up to a 20,000× speedup over differential-equation-based simulations (e.g., Pix2Pix at 0.138 ms/sample vs. 204,700 ms for the sound propagation simulation). For the simplest task (baseline sound propagation), Pix2Pix attains the best MAE of 1.73 (LoS) and 1.19 (NLoS), while DDBM achieves the lowest LoS MAE of 1.61. However, performance degrades sharply for higher-order physics: in the reflection sub-task, NLoS MAE increases by 3–4× across all models (e.g., Pix2Pix rises from 1.19 to 4.79), and in the bouncing-ball task, Y-position error (involving second-order terms) consistently exceeds X-position error for every architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pix2Pix (GAN) consistently achieves the best or near-best physical accuracy across all three tasks: lowest NLoS MAE in sound propagation (1.19 Base, 3.36 Diffr., 4.79 Refl.), lowest combined landmark error in lens distortion (2.00 for p₁≠0, 1.77 for p₂≠0), and best position/rotation accuracy in the bouncing-ball task (6.28±8.0 px X, 11.7±13 px Y, 17.2° rotation) with only 11% non-evaluable predictions. UNet is a close second on sound propagation and lens distortion but produces 28% non-evaluable bouncing-ball outputs due to blurred or missing balls. Autoencoders (convAE, VAE) perform worst overall, with NLoS MAE 2–3× higher than Pix2Pix on sound tasks and >90% non-evaluable predictions on the bouncing-ball task due to severe blurring. Among diffusion models, plain Stable Diffusion outperforms DDPM and SD-with-cross-attention on most metrics (e.g., 1.08 NLoS MAE vs. 3.26 for DDPM on Base sound), but all diffusion models are 20,000–30,000× slower at inference than Pix2Pix/UNet (≈3,000–4,000 ms vs. 0.13 ms). DDBM achieves the lowest LoS MAE on the Base sound task (1.61) but degrades more than Pix2Pix on NLoS regions (2.17 vs. 1.19), confirming that all models struggle with higher-order physical dependencies. The key takeaway is that no generative model matches the simulation's zero error, and errors scale with the order of the governing differential equation, indicating a fundamental limitation in learning complex physical relations from image pairs alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Tenenbaum's earlier work on intuitive physics engines, which trained models to predict coarse physical properties from images, to the much more demanding setting of detailed, physically correct image-to-image mappings for complex simulations. It repurposes a diverse set of existing generative architectures (Pix2Pix, U-Net, VAE, DDPM, Stable Diffusion, DDBM) that were originally designed for appearance-based tasks like style transfer and inpainting, applying them to three distinct physical simulation domains. The paper also positions itself within the broader physics-informed ML literature (PUGAN, FEM-GAN, physics-guided GANs) but deliberately avoids incorporating physical constraints into the models, instead evaluating vanilla generative architectures to isolate their raw capacity for learning physical relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhysicsGen provides the first systematic benchmark with 300k image pairs for evaluating whether generative models can replace differential-equation-based simulations, a question with direct practical implications for computational efficiency in engineering and science. The consistent finding across all three tasks and all model families—that errors grow with the order of the governing differential equation—raises a critical open question: is this a fundamental representational limitation of current generative models, or simply a data-scarcity issue? If the former, it implies that generative models cannot serve as drop-in replacements for higher-order physics solvers without explicit physical inductive biases, redirecting the field toward physics-constrained architectures. The 20k speedup for first-order tasks, however, suggests near-term practical value in domains where lower-order approximations suffice.
