## Problem

Zero-shot image restoration with pretrained diffusion models requires many Neural Function Evaluations (NFEs) — typically tens to thousands — because the sampling scheme inherits the iterative denoising structure of the generative model. While Consistency Models (CMs) can generate images in 1–2 NFEs, existing guided-CM restoration methods still need ~40 NFEs or task-specific fine-tuning that degrades when test-time assumptions mismatch. The paper asks whether a truly zero-shot restoration scheme can operate with only 4 NFEs while outperforming methods that use 20–40 NFEs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded linear observation y = Ax* + e (e.g., a 4× downsampled image, a Gaussian-blurred image, or an image with 80% randomly missing pixels) together with the corresponding measurement operator A, and produces a full-resolution restored image. It operates on 256×256 RGB images (LSUN Bedroom, LSUN Cat) and 64×64 (ImageNet), requires no SVD of A (unlike DDRM), and uses only 4 forward passes of a pretrained CM denoiser. Initialization is data-driven via A†y (or median fill for inpainting) rather than pure Gaussian noise, and the noise level τ_N at initialization is set below the maximum T to avoid masking the informative initialization.

## Method

CM4IR combines three ingredients: (1) back-projection guidance using A†(Ax−y) for faster convergence, (2) data-driven initialization x_{τ_N} = A†y + τ_N z, and (3) a novel noise injection that decouples the denoiser's noise level (1+δ)τ_n from the injection level τ_n and splits the injected noise between stochastic z and an estimated component ẑ⁻ = (x_{0|τ_n} − x_{τ_n})/τ_n. The critical design choice is that ẑ⁻ is the *negative* of DDIM's estimated noise ẑ, which the paper identifies as a noisy variant of Polyak acceleration: it pushes the sample toward x_0 rather than pulling it back toward x_{τ_n}, and the residual noise in ẑ⁻ keeps the next denoiser input consistent with its training distribution. This sign flip, combined with the noise-level decoupling, allows high-quality restoration in just 4 NFEs and also mitigates the performance drop of existing DDIM-based DM methods (DDRM, DiffPIR) when their NFE count is reduced from 20 to 4.

## Results

On LSUN Bedroom, CM4IR with only 4 NFEs achieves 26.14 dB PSNR / 0.295 LPIPS on SR×4 (σ_y=0.025), 29.00 dB / 0.213 on Gaussian deblurring (σ_y=0.025), and 25.43 dB / 0.284 on 80% inpainting (σ_y=0), outperforming all baselines that use 20–40 NFEs. On LSUN Cat, CM4IR similarly leads: 27.18 dB / 0.328 (SR×4, σ_y=0.025), 29.76 dB / 0.247 (deblurring, σ_y=0.025), and 25.89 dB / 0.364 (inpainting, σ_y=0.025). Wall-clock runtime for SR×4 on a single LSUN Bedroom image is 0.159 s for CM4IR versus 0.446 s (DDRM), 0.659 s (DiffPIR), and 1.539 s (CM-40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CM4IR (4 NFEs) outperforms the guided CM baseline CM(40) (40 NFEs) in every setting on both LSUN Bedroom and LSUN Cat, with PSNR gains of 1.5–3.8 dB and LPIPS improvements of 0.07–0.24. Against DDRM (20 NFEs), CM4IR wins on SR and inpainting (e.g., 25.60 vs 25.08 dB on SR×4 σ_y=0.05; 25.43 vs 19.40 dB on inpainting σ_y=0) and is competitive on deblurring (29.00 vs 28.94 dB). CM4IR surpasses DiffPIR (20 NFEs) across all tasks and both datasets, with the largest gap on inpainting (up to 6 dB). CoSIGN (task-specific fine-tuning) is competitive only in its exact training setting (SR×4, σ_y=0.025: 26.10 vs 26.14 dB) but degrades catastrophically when the noise level or degradation model changes (e.g., 19.74 dB on deblurring vs CM4IR's 29.00 dB), confirming the fragility of task-specific approaches. The paper further shows that replacing DDIM's estimated noise sign in DDRM and DiffPIR with the proposed ẑ⁻ recovers most of the performance lost when reducing from 20 to 4 NFEs, though still below CM4IR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Consistency Models (CMs) as few-step generative priors [27], back-projection (BP) guidance for accelerating data-fidelity enforcement in inverse problems [28, 30, 31], and DDIM-style noise injection for reducing sampling steps [22]. The key conceptual departure is a sign flip in the estimated-noise component of the injection (using z⁻ = (x₀|τₙ − xₜₙ)/τₙ instead of DDIM's z = (xₜₙ − x₀|τₙ)/τₙ), combined with decoupling the denoiser's noise level from the injection noise level. The authors further connect their scheme to a noisy variant of Polyak acceleration [18], providing a theoretical lens absent from prior guided-DM restoration work. Unlike CoSIGN [39], which fine-tunes a ControlNet per task, CM4IR remains truly zero-shot, and unlike DDRM/DiffPIR [15, 40], it operates on CMs rather than full diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 4 NFEs (0.159 s per image) versus 20–40 NFEs for competing zero-shot methods, with competitive or superior PSNR/LPIPS across SR, deblurring, and inpainting. The finding that the same noise-injection modification also rescues DDRM and DiffPIR when their NFE count is slashed from 20 to 4 suggests a broadly applicable principle for few-step guided sampling, not just a CM-specific trick. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-linear or unknown observation models (the current framework assumes a known linear A), how it behaves at even fewer NFEs (1–2), and whether the noise-level decoupling (δ > 0) generalizes beyond the tested noise levels. The method still inherits the dependency on a pretrained CM (itself distilled from a DM), so the end-to-end pipeline is not fully self-contained.
