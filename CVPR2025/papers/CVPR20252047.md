## Problem

SNNs offer energy-efficient, sparse processing ideal for event camera data on neuromorphic hardware, but they fall significantly short of ANNs in object detection accuracy. Conversely, ANN-based detectors achieve strong accuracy but are too large and power-hungry for edge deployment, and they discard the sparsity and high temporal resolution that make event data attractive. The critical missing piece is an effective mechanism to convert sparse, spike-based SNN representations into dense ANN-compatible features without losing valuable spatiotemporal structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams from DVS cameras (304×240 for Gen1, 720×1280 for Gen4), discretized into 5 ms time bins forming T×2×H×W tensors with ~98% spatial sparsity. During training, detections are produced every 50 ms using the SNN's output from the last 10 time bins; at inference, temporal resolution is bounded only by the SNN timestep. The SNN front-end can run on neuromorphic hardware (Intel Loihi 2) while the ANN back-end runs on a GPU or edge processor, enabling a split deployment where only intermediate dense features cross the hardware boundary.

## Method

The architecture is a hybrid SNN-ANN backbone with three stages: (1) a low-level SNN feature extractor using PLIF neurons with trainable time constants that processes events at 5 ms timesteps, (2) a novel Attention-based SNN-ANN Bridge (ASAB) module that converts sparse spike tensors into dense feature maps via two sub-modules — Spatial-aware Temporal (SAT) attention, which applies Time-wise Separable Deformable Convolution to capture irregular spatial context per channel and then multi-head self-attention along the temporal axis to extract temporal relations, and Event-rate Spatial (ERS) attention, which weights spatial regions by summed spike activity, and (3) a standard CNN feature extractor (optionally augmented with DWConvLSTMs for multi-timescale dynamics) feeding a YOLOX detection head. The network is trained end-to-end with surrogate gradients for the SNN portion, and the SNN blocks are validated on Intel Loihi 2 with int8 quantization, achieving sub-real-time processing at ~1.7 W.

## Results

On the Gen1 Automotive Detection dataset, the proposed hybrid SNN-ANN model achieves mAP 0.35 with only 6.6M parameters, outperforming all SNN-based methods (best SNN: EMS-RES34 at 0.31 with 14.4M params) and matching or exceeding larger ANN methods like Events-RetinaNet (33M, 0.34) while using 5× fewer parameters. On Gen4, the model reaches mAP 0.27, surpassing Events-RetinaNet (0.18) and E2Vid-RetinaNet (0.25). The RNN variant (Proposed+RNN, 7.7M params) achieves mAP 0.43 on Gen1, competitive with RVT-T (4M, 0.44) and RVT-S (10M, 0.46) while using fewer parameters and lower MACs (1.63×10⁹ vs. RVT's 2.3×10⁹).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ANN-based methods, the proposed hybrid (6.6M params) achieves the best Gen1 mAP (0.35) while using 5–20× fewer parameters than Events-RetinaNet (33M, 0.34), RRC-Events (>100M, 0.31), and SparseConv (133M, 0.15); on Gen4 it reaches 0.27, beating Events-RetinaNet (0.18) and E2Vid-RetinaNet (0.25) but trailing Inception+SSD and RRC-Events (both 0.34). Against SNN-based methods, the hybrid decisively wins: it outperforms the best SNN (EMS-RES34, 14.4M, 0.31) by 4 mAP points while using fewer parameters (6.6M vs. 14.4M), and beats all other SNNs (VGG-11+SDD 0.17, MobileNet-64+SSD 0.15, DenseNet121-24+SSD 0.19, FP-DAGNet 0.22, SpikeFPN 0.22, EMS-RES10 0.27, EMS-RES18 0.29). Against RNN/SSM-based methods, the Proposed+RNN variant (7.7M, 0.43) is competitive with RVT-T (4M, 0.44) and RVT-S (10M, 0.46) but falls short of the larger S5-ViT-B (18.2M, 0.48), ASTMNet (100M, 0.48), and RVT-B (19M, 0.47); however, it uses substantially fewer MACs (1.63×10⁹) and avoids RVT's non-causal attention re-computation, making it more suitable for edge deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid SNN-ANN paradigm explored in prior work on simpler tasks (classification, optical flow) to the more demanding setting of large-scale event-based object detection, introducing a dedicated attention-based bridge module (ASAB) to convert sparse spike representations into dense ANN-compatible features. It builds directly on the DWConvLSTM architecture and event tensor formulation from Gehrig & Scaramuzza (CVPR 2023) for the RNN variant, and on deformable convolutions (Dai et al., ICCV 2017) for the spatial-aware temporal attention mechanism. The key departure from prior hybrid approaches is the explicit multi-timescale design: fast SNN processing at 5 ms timesteps for short-term dynamics, followed by slower DWConvLSTM processing on bridge-extracted features for long-term context, rather than a simple SNN-to-ANN handoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a concrete, hardware-validated path toward ANN-competitive object detection at a fraction of the parameter and power budget (6.6M params, ~1.7 W on Loihi 2), which is practically significant for edge and automotive deployment where full GPU inference is infeasible. However, the RNN/SSM-based methods (S5-ViT-B at 0.48 mAP) still outperform the hybrid+RNN variant (0.43 mAP), and the accuracy gap to the best ANN baselines on Gen4 remains notable. Open questions include whether the ASAB bridge generalizes to higher-resolution or more cluttered scenes, whether full end-to-end neuromorphic deployment (including the ANN head) is feasible, and whether the multi-timescale design scales to longer temporal horizons without the RNN variant's training cost (~6 days).
