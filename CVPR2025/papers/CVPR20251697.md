## Problem

3D human pose estimation struggles with physical contact (self-contact or inter-person) because occluded body parts are poorly recovered by 2D keypoint-based methods. Existing solutions require expensive contact annotations or motion capture data, with publicly available datasets limited to tens of thousands of images. This paper asks whether large multimodal models (LMMs), which encode rich knowledge of physical interaction in language, can serve as a zero-shot, scalable source of contact priors for pose refinement without any fine-tuning or contact labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of one or two people in contact, along with bounding boxes and initial 3D pose estimates from a regressor (BEV for two-person, HMR2 for one-person), plus pseudo-ground-truth 2D keypoints from OpenPose and ViTPose. The LMM is prompted with the cropped, segmented image and returns N=20 natural-language samples listing pairs of coarse body regions (e.g., hand–arm, shoulder–back) that should be in contact. The output is refined SMPL-X pose parameters (joint rotations θ, shape β, translation t) per person, yielding a 3D mesh whose vertices at predicted contact regions are brought into proximity.

## Method

ProsePose's key insight is that LMMs can articulate poses in structured language (e.g., 'arms around waist'), and these articulations can be operationalized as geometric distance constraints on a differentiable body model. The pipeline has three stages: (1) a pose regressor produces initial 3D poses; (2) the LMM is prompted (with chain-of-thought reasoning) to output a list of contact constraints as pairs of coarse body regions, sampled N times to mitigate hallucination; (3) each constraint set is converted into a loss L_LMM that minimizes the minimum vertex distance between the two constrained regions on the SMPL-X mesh, and the N losses are averaged (self-consistency). This LMM loss is jointly optimized with 2D keypoint, Gaussian Mixture pose prior, shape, initial-pose, and interpenetration losses in a two-stage procedure. Hallucination is further mitigated by frequency-filtering constraints across samples, automatically assigning left/right chirality by minimizing loss, and gracefully backoff to a heuristic when the LMM produces too many empty constraint sets. The entire approach is zero-shot: no fine-tuning of the LMM and no training on contact annotations.

## Results

On the two-person setting, ProsePose (without contact supervision) achieves joint PA-MPJPE of 93 mm on Hi4D, 58 mm on FlickrCI3D, and 100 mm on CHI3D, consistently outperforming the strongest unsupervised baseline (Heuristic: 116/67/105 mm). On FlickrCI3D, ProsePose also improves average PCC from 77.8% (Heuristic) to 79.9%, and on CHI3D from 74.1% to 75.8%. In the one-person yoga setting (MOYO), ProsePose achieves PCC of 87.8% versus 85.2% for HMR2+opt, while maintaining a comparable PA-MPJPE of 82 mm. The method closes 85% of the PA-MPJPE gap to the fully supervised BUDDI on Hi4D (93 vs. 89 mm) without requiring any contact annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BEV (the multi-person pose regressor used for initialization), ProsePose substantially reduces joint PA-MPJPE on Hi4D (93 vs. 144 mm) and FlickrCI3D (58 vs. 106 mm), though it slightly underperforms on CHI3D (100 vs. 96 mm) due to frequent fallback to the heuristic baseline (224/431 images). Against the Heuristic baseline (which adds a generic minimum-distance loss without semantic contact information), ProsePose wins on every dataset for both PA-MPJPE and PCC, with the largest gains on Hi4D (93 vs. 116 mm) and FlickrCI3D (58 vs. 67 mm). Against the supervised BUDDI method, ProsePose achieves a better PA-MPJPE on FlickrCI3D (58 vs. 66 mm) without any contact training data, but trails on Hi4D (93 vs. 89 mm) and CHI3D (100 vs. 68 mm), where BUDDI's learned diffusion prior from large annotated datasets provides a stronger signal. In the one-person MOYO setting, ProsePose improves PCC over HMR2+opt (87.8% vs. 85.2%) while matching its PA-MPJPE (82 vs. 81 mm), confirming that LMM-derived contact constraints benefit self-contact poses as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProsePose builds directly on the constrained optimization framework of BUDDI [34] (two-stage optimization, interpenetration loss via winding numbers, heuristic backoff) and the Gaussian Mixture pose prior from Keep it SMPL [4], but replaces BUDDI's learned diffusion prior—trained on expensive contact-annotated data—with a zero-shot LMM-derived loss. It departs from prior language-pose work (PoseScript, PoseFix, PoseGPT), which all require paired language-pose training data, by instead extracting structured contact constraints from a pretrained LMM's free-text output and converting them into a distance-based loss. The initialization pipeline borrows from BEV [42] for multi-person placement and HMR2 [12] for single-person pose, positioning the LMM as a post-hoc refinement signal rather than a primary regressor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pretrained LMMs encode enough implicit knowledge of physical contact to meaningfully improve 3D pose estimation without any contact-specific training, offering a scalable alternative to the costly manual annotation or motion capture pipelines that current supervised methods (BUDDI, REMIPS, CloseInt) depend on. Key limitations include LMMs' poor chirality identification, reliance on coarse body-part regions, and a high backoff rate (up to 52% on CHI3D) where the method falls back to the heuristic. Open questions include whether finer-grained LMM constraints, better chirality handling, or multi-view LMM reasoning could close the remaining gap to fully supervised methods, and whether the LMM-as-prior paradigm extends beyond contact to other physically constrained pose attributes.
