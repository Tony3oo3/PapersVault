## Problem

Existing domain generalization and open-set domain generalization methods assume fully supervised training, while few-shot DG methods ignore open-set detection. The combination of severe data scarcity (1–5 samples per class) with the need to reject unseen classes at inference—termed Low-Shot Open-Set Domain Generalization (LSOSDG)—remains unaddressed. CLIP-based prompt methods in particular falter in low-data regimes and produce coarse-grained pseudo-open samples that fail to separate fine-grained open-set instances from known classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251006/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images from multiple source domains (1 or 5 labeled examples per class) during training, and unlabeled target-domain images at inference. It outputs a class label from the union of all known source classes or a single 'Unknown' label, effectively performing |C|+1-way classification. The frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 image encoder and Transformer text encoder process the inputs, with learnable visual prompts (2 tokens) injected at the first ViT layer and domain-agnostic text prompts (4 context tokens) conditioning the text encoder.

## Method

OSLOPROMPT introduces two coupled innovations. First, it synthesizes fine-grained pseudo-open images by prompting GPT-4o to generate class names semantically close to but distinct from known classes, then using Stable Diffusion to render them in each source domain's visual style; these train a dedicated Unknown-class prompt. Second, it learns domain-agnostic text prompts whose context tokens are partially initialized from a shared learnable visual prompt (projected via a meta-net) and partially learned directly, while being regularized by domain-specific prompts enriched with GPT-4o-generated class attributes processed through a cross-attention module (image features as query, attribute text encodings as key/value). Training minimizes a combined objective: a contrastive loss on the augmented dataset (known + pseudo-open) for the domain-agnostic prompt, a contrastive loss on source data for the attribute-enriched domain-specific prompts, and a cosine-alignment loss pulling domain-agnostic context tokens toward domain-specific prompt encodings.

## Results

In the 1-shot LSOSDG setting, OSLOPROMPT achieves an average H-score of 75.57% and closed-set accuracy of 77.32% across five benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, Office-Home, Multi-Dataset, Mini-DomainNet), outperforming the strongest competitor ODG-CLIP by 11.93% in H-score. In the 5-shot setting, it reaches 76.21% H-score and 80.47% accuracy, a 9.01% H-score gain over ODG-CLIP. On the ImageNet closed-set DG suite (1-shot), OSLOPROMPT attains 49.70% average accuracy, marginally surpassing MaPLe (49.52%) and ODG-CLIP (46.05%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSLOPROMPT decisively outperforms all 14 baselines in the 1-shot LSOSDG setting. The strongest competitor, ODG-CLIP [46], achieves 63.64% average H-score but is surpassed by 11.93% (75.57%), with particularly large gaps on PACS (+19.3% H-score) and VLCS (+22.2%). Among CLIP-based DG+OSR methods, STYLIP [5] is the best at 44.53% H-score, still trailing OSLOPROMPT by 31%. Non-CLIP ODG methods (ODG-Net [4], MEDIC [53], SCI-PD [6]) and FSDG+OSR (2LM [36]) all remain below 30% H-score, struggling with both closed-set accuracy and open-set detection under extreme data scarcity. On the ImageNet closed-set DG benchmark (Table 2), OSLOPROMPT (49.70% avg) narrowly edges out MaPLe [19] (49.52%) and KgCoOp [56] (49.25%), while ODG-CLIP [46] drops to 46.05%, confirming that the open-set training objective in ODG-CLIP degrades closed-set generalization in low-shot regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSLOPROMPT extends ODG-CLIP [46], which first introduced an Unknown-class prompt and diffusion-generated pseudo-open samples for open-set domain generalization in CLIP, by replacing its generic pseudo-open synthesis with a controlled, fine-grained strategy guided by GPT-4o and Stable Diffusion. The prompt-learning architecture draws on CoOp [62] for learnable context tokens, CoCoOp [61] for the meta-net projector, and Visual Prompt Tuning [17] for learnable visual prompts injected at the first ViT layer, but departs from all three by using a shared (rather than per-image) visual prompt to initialize domain-agnostic text tokens and by introducing a cross-attention module that encodes GPT-4o-generated class attributes into image-conditioned domain-specific prompts. The paper also positions itself against FSDG methods [36] and attribute-based prompting [21, 29], arguing that existing approaches either over-rely on textual cues or isolate visual and textual attributes rather than cross-referencing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LSOSDG formulation is practically motivated by scenarios like medical diagnostics or autonomous driving where data is scarce, domain shifts are unpredictable, and new classes appear spontaneously—settings where neither standard ODG nor FSDG alone suffices. The ablation showing a 22.95% H-score drop when replacing fine-grained pseudo-open samples with ODG-CLIP's generic generation highlights that the quality of the closed-open decision boundary is a critical, previously underappreciated factor in open-set DG. Limitations include evaluation only with ViT-B/32, the absence of competing LSOSDG baselines (making the SOTA claim somewhat self-referential), and a slight 5-shot performance dip on Mini-DomainNet and Office-Home suggesting mild overfitting. Future work on broader open-world applications and structured prediction tasks is noted but not explored.
