## Problem

Adversarial attacks on visual object tracking have been extensively studied for RGB trackers, but the security of RGB-T multi-modal trackers—widely deployed in safety-critical domains like autonomous driving—remains largely unexplored. The modal balance mechanism and coupled multi-modal information in RGB-T trackers make it particularly difficult to disrupt tracking with single-modal perturbations, as the tracker can compensate for interference in one modality using the other.

## Input / Output

The method takes paired RGB and thermal video frames as input and produces two physically deployable adversarial patches: a visible patch with adversarial texture applied via a binary mask, and an infrared patch defined by an adversarial shape. Both patches share the same geometric shape to enable stacked physical deployment (on gel and paper) without mutual interference, and the visible patch additionally carries texture-based adversarial patterns. Patches are trained on RGBT234 and directly transferred to LasHeR for generalization testing, as well as to real-world video captured by a DJI Mavic 3T at 30 fps.

## Method

ACAttack employs a two-stage coarse-to-fine framework. Stage 1 uses PGD to generate K coarse adversarial samples that narrow the search space. Stage 2 introduces a modal-aware adaptive attack strategy that computes each modality's contribution via the reciprocal Euclidean distance between single-modal and dual-modal response maps, then alternately optimizes only the adversarial texture network (for visible) or adversarial shape network (for infrared) targeting the modality with higher contribution, achieving progressive modal decoupling. When modal contributions are balanced (|c_vi − c_ir| < ζ), a modal disturbance loss maximizes the L2 distance between the two single-modal response maps to break the tracker's balance judgment, while a spatio-temporal joint loss progressively enlarges the predicted bounding box over 5 consecutive frames using scaling factors [1.90, 1.95, 2.00, 2.05, 2.10]. The shared adversarial shape between modalities eliminates physical deployment interference and enables shape-based attacks in the visible channel.

## Results

On RGBT234, ACAttack reduces ViPT's precision rate (PR) from 0.835 to 0.621 and success rate (SR) from 0.617 to 0.417, and lowers SDSTrack's PR from 0.848 to 0.616 and SR from 0.625 to 0.426. On the LasHeR dataset (generalization test, no training on LasHeR), ACAttack similarly causes significant PR and SR drops across all three trackers (ViPT, BAT, SDSTrack), outperforming both random noise and the RGB-only MTD attack. Physical-domain experiments with a DJI Mavic 3T UAV confirm that the digitally trained patches sustain tracking degradation over extended video sequences in real-world scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ACAttack against two baselines: random noise patches and MTD [8], a gradient-based attack designed specifically for RGB trackers. On RGBT234, random noise causes only a modest decline in PR and SR, confirming that exploiting RGB-T tracker vulnerabilities requires a structured, optimized approach rather than unstructured perturbation. MTD, while effective on single-modal RGB trackers, produces a smaller performance drop than ACAttack, indicating that attacks designed without awareness of the RGB-T modal coupling mechanism cannot fully disrupt the feature enhancement from cross-modal interaction. ACAttack achieves the largest PR and SR reductions across all three victim trackers (ViPT, BAT, SDSTrack) on both RGBT234 and LasHeR, demonstrating that the modal decoupling strategy and modal balance interference loss are critical for multi-modal tracker attacks. The generalization to LasHeR (where no training was performed) further confirms that the learned multi-modal patches transfer effectively to unseen data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature on visual object trackers—previously confined to single-modal RGB methods such as APYVOT and MTD—into the RGB-T multi-modal tracking setting, where prior attacks fail to account for the modal coupling and balance mechanisms inherent to these trackers. Conceptually, it repurposes the idea of modality decoupling (normally a fusion/robustness technique) as an offensive strategy, and combines a coarse-to-fine optimization pipeline (PGD for initial adversarial discovery, followed by learned patch generation networks) with a novel adaptive iteration scheme that targets whichever modality currently dominates the tracker's response. The shared-shape stacking design for physical deployment is a practical departure from prior single-modal patch attacks, which do not address the interference between stacked visible and infrared patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RGB-T trackers are increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains (autonomous driving, urban security), this work provides the first systematic framework for probing their vulnerabilities, revealing that modal balance mechanisms—designed to improve robustness—can themselves be exploited. The physical-domain validation with a DJI Mavic 3T demonstrates real-world transferability, though the evaluation is limited to three trackers and two datasets, and the physical experiments cover only 30 video sequences. Open questions include whether the adaptive cross-attack strategy generalizes to other multi-modal combinations (e.g., RGB-D, RGB-NIR), whether defenders can develop robustness against modal-decoupling attacks without sacrificing tracking accuracy, and how the attack scales to longer sequences or more diverse target categories.
