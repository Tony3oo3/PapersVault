## Problem

Existing text-to-texture methods suffer from three interrelated failures: multi-view inconsistencies and the Janus problem when using independent 2D diffusion per view, over-smoothed textures lacking fine detail, and fragile dependence on UV unwrapping quality that causes visible seams and discontinuities. These issues stem from synchronizing in low-resolution latent space, performing inpainting directly in fragmented UV space, and lacking explicit 3D spatial awareness during refinement.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untextured 3D mesh (vertex set V and triangular face set F) plus a free-form text prompt describing the desired texture appearance, and produces a 2K-resolution multi-channel UV texture map T ∈ R^{H×W×C}. Internally, 8 views are sampled with evenly distributed azimuth angles and interleaved ±30° elevation; the UV map passes through a 1K intermediate stage before final 2K super-resolution. The method is explicitly designed to be robust to arbitrary UV unwrapping quality, including fragmented or randomly packed UV atlases.

## Method

MVPaint is a three-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. Stage 1 (SMG) uses MVDream as a text-to-multi-view diffusion base with a ControlNet for depth/normal conditioning; its key innovation is synchronizing views in the decoded image domain rather than latent space—projecting intermediate denoised latents to 256×256 images, fusing them into a UV map via cosine-weighted blending of view directions against surface normals, then re-encoding for continued denoising—followed by SDXL-based I2I refinement at 1K with dual ControlNets for texture detail and geometry enforcement. Stage 2 (S3I) is a learning-free 3D point-cloud inpainting that reformulates UV hole-filling as color propagation: it samples a dense colored point cloud from the incomplete UV map and iteratively assigns colors to unpainted points via k-nearest-neighbor aggregation weighted by inverse Euclidean distance and a piecewise surface-normal similarity function. Stage 3 (UVR) applies diffusion-based super-resolution in UV space, then a Spatial-aware Seam-smoothing Algorithm that detects seam boundaries via connectivity analysis on the valid-pixel mask and smooths seam-region colors in 3D point-cloud space using kd-tree neighbor lookups with normal-cosine and distance weighting.

## Results

On the Objaverse T2T benchmark (1000 meshes), MVPaint achieves the best FID (20.89) and KID (3.45), outperforming the previous best (SyncMVD) by 6.1 and 2.27 respectively, while TEXTure attains the highest CLIP score (20.30 vs. MVPaint's 19.87). In the user study, MVPaint leads on all three subjective criteria: Overall (4.19), Seamless (4.25), and Consistency (3.98). On the GSO T2T benchmark (1032 scanned objects), MVPaint again achieves the best FID (20.02) and KID (3.12), with the largest margin over Paint3D (FID 37.29), demonstrating strong generalizability beyond its Objaverse training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVPaint outperforms all three baselines on FID and KID across both benchmarks, with the largest FID gap over Paint3D on GSO (20.02 vs. 37.29), where Paint3D's Objaverse-trained inpainting submodule degrades severely on out-of-distribution scans. On CLIP, MVPaint trails TEXTure by a small margin (19.87 vs. 20.30 on Objaverse; 23.25 vs. 23.44 on GSO), which the authors attribute to TEXTure's tendency to produce Janus artifacts that inflate CLIP scores. In the user study, MVPaint leads all baselines on every subjective criterion—Overall, Seamless, and Consistency—by 0.1–1.4 points, with the largest advantage in seam visibility (4.25 vs. 3.36 for Paint3D on Objaverse). SyncMVD is the closest competitor on user-study Overall (3.96 vs. 4.19) but still lags on seam quality and consistency. Notably, MVPaint's performance gap between Objaverse and GSO is minimal (FID 20.89 → 20.02), whereas Paint3D's FID worsens by 47% (25.28 → 37.29), confirming MVPaint's reduced dependence on UV unwrapping quality and training-distribution overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVPaint builds directly on MVDream's multi-view diffusion architecture as its T2MV backbone and adopts SyncMVD's synchronized denoising mechanism for the refinement stage, but departs from both in a key conceptual shift: rather than synchronizing in latent space (as SyncMVD does) or relying on depth-conditioned single-view generation (as TEXTure and Text2Tex do), it performs synchronization in the decoded image domain and uses the T2MV model itself as a generalizable 3D prior. The most significant departure from prior work is the move from UV-space operations to 3D-space operations: both the inpainting (S3I) and seam-smoothing (SSA) modules operate on 3D point clouds rather than the 2D UV atlas, explicitly reducing dependence on UV unwrapping quality—a limitation that plagues Paint3D and Meta 3D TexGen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the GSO T2T benchmark is a practical contribution for the field, since most T2T methods are trained on Objaverse subsets and lack a held-out generalizability test; the finding that Paint3D degrades substantially (FID 25→37) while MVPaint holds steady (FID 21→20) on GSO suggests 3D-space operations offer genuine robustness beyond UV-space pipelines. However, the method remains a multi-stage pipeline chaining several pretrained diffusion models (MVDream, SDXL with two ControlNets, an upscale model), raising open questions about computational cost, end-to-end trainability, and whether the one-step image-domain synchronization can be extended to more aggressive multi-step synchronization without instability. The observation that 3D inpainting and seam smoothing barely move FID/KID yet visibly eliminate artifacts also highlights a gap between standard generative metrics and perceptual quality in 3D texturing.
