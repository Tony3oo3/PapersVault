## Problem

Automated waste sorting quality assessment in Shanghai requires semantic segmentation of top-view trash bin images, but pixel-level annotation is infeasible due to the extreme complexity of distorted, stained, and densely overlapping waste objects. Existing weakly-supervised segmentation methods designed for bounding boxes or image-level labels fail on heavily contaminated waste scenes, and no large-scale community waste dataset with reliable annotations exists. The paper tackles the gap of generating accurate pixel-level pseudo labels from only coarse, non-overlapping patch annotations on a severely class-imbalanced waste dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250715/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes top-view waste bin images (collected via smartphone by sanitation workers) annotated with non-overlapping rectangular patches indicating the category of waste within each box, rather than full bounding boxes or image-level labels. Unlike standard bounding boxes, patches guarantee that all enclosed pixels belong to a single object, eliminating overlap ambiguity. The output is pixel-level pseudo labels for all 21 waste categories plus a background (trash bin) class, which are then used to train a PANet few-shot segmentation model that produces final semantic segmentation masks for real-world waste images deployed at residential complexes.

## Method

WISNet comprises three stages: (1) Pre-trained Unsupervised Classification (PUC) applies K-Means (K=3) on ImageNet-pretrained VGG16 features to split each waste category into subcategories, providing finer-grained supervisory signals; (2) Cohesive Feature Extraction (CFE) trains the backbone via contrastive prototype learning with both positive and negative prototypes computed by masked average pooling over a support set, while simultaneously supervising a subcategory classification head to force the extractor to capture diverse intra-category attributes; (3) Iterative Patch Expansion (IPE) converts rectangular patches into pixel-level pseudo labels by alternating cosine-similarity-based label assignment against prototypes and prototype re-computation via MAP on the refined mask, overlaying original patch regions to prevent drift, and iterating until mask change falls below ε=0.1%. The resulting pseudo labels train a 1-way 5-shot PANet segmentation model, achieving 40.2% mIoU on the ShanghaiWaste-Seg benchmark.

## Results

On the ShanghaiWaste-Seg benchmark (928 pixel-level annotated images, 21 categories), WISNet with PANet achieves a state-of-the-art 40.2% mIoU, outperforming the second-best method (PPC at 36.1%) by 4.1 percentage points. With DeepLab as the segmentation backbone, WISNet reaches 31.8% mIoU, exceeding the best DeepLab-based baseline (ACR at 29.1%) by 2.7 points. The ablation confirms that the full pipeline (PUC + IPG + IPE) contributes +3.8% mIoU over the PANet baseline alone (36.4% → 40.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WISNet outperforms all seven compared weakly-supervised segmentation baselines under both segmentation backbones. With PANet, WISNet (40.2% mIoU) leads PPC (36.1%) by 4.1 points, ACR (35.9%) by 4.3 points, AEFT (35.6%) by 4.6 points, and DuPL (34.0%) by 6.2 points. The bounding-box-based methods (SDI, BANA, BBAM) perform substantially worse, with mIoU ranging from 21.3% to 30.1% under PANet, indicating that patch-level annotations are a fundamentally different and more informative supervision signal than bounding boxes for this task. Under DeepLab, WISNet (31.8%) still beats the best image-level baselines ACR (29.1%) and PPC (28.3%) by 2.7 and 3.5 points respectively. Notably, WISNet also shows stronger performance on rare categories (e.g., Leather 77.1% vs. 69.1% for DuPL; Paint Bucket 72.2% vs. 63.9% for PPC), confirming that the contrastive prototype learning effectively mitigates class imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

WISNet builds directly on PANet's prototype learning framework (support-query setting with masked average pooling) and extends it with a contrastive formulation using both positive and negative prototypes. It departs from the standard weakly-supervised segmentation literature, which relies on image-level labels or bounding boxes, by introducing patch-level annotations as a new intermediate supervision signal. The Pre-trained Unsupervised Classification module repurposes K-Means clustering over a frozen ImageNet backbone to generate subcategory labels, a technique borrowed from unsupervised domain adaptation but applied here as an auxiliary training signal for the feature extractor. The iterative patch expansion scheme is a novel refinement strategy that iteratively re-estimates prototypes from progressively refined masks, distinct from the one-shot CAM-based or graph-based refinement used in prior WSSS methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete, high-impact urban problem (automated waste sorting quality assessment in Shanghai) and introduces the first large-scale waste dataset with a practical annotation paradigm (patches) that balances cost and informativeness. The 40.2% mIoU, while SOTA on this benchmark, remains modest, highlighting that pixel-level segmentation of heavily occluded, stained, and imbalanced waste objects is far from solved. Open questions include whether the patch-level annotation paradigm generalizes to other domains with similar occlusion and imbalance, whether the pseudo-label quality can be further improved with end-to-end training, and how the system performs as the dataset grows beyond 12K images. The real-world deployment at two residential complexes provides rare evidence of practical viability for weakly-supervised segmentation in a non-laboratory setting.
