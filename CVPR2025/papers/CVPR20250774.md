## Problem

Video diffusion models require dozens of iterative denoising steps, making generation prohibitively slow (e.g., >30 s for a 2-second clip on an A100). Existing acceleration methods—consistency distillation (LCM) and adversarial training (GAN)—suffer from poor few-step quality, training instability, and the need to decode latents through a VAE before adversarial evaluation, and they are largely direct ports of image-diffusion techniques without addressing video-specific challenges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250774/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as the conditioning input and produces a short video clip (7 FPS) in the latent space of Stable Video Diffusion. Training uses videos at 1024×768 (Stage 1) and 576×320→1024×768 (Stage 2) on the OpenVid-1M dataset. At inference, the model supports 1-step generation (1 NFE) or multi-step refinement via the Time Travel Sampler (e.g., 2 NFE for 1-step TTS), with the image embedding serving as the condition throughout the ODE trajectory.

## Method

OSV employs a two-stage training framework: Stage 1 uses LoRA-adapted GAN training (Huber + adversarial loss against real data) for rapid convergence, while Stage 2 introduces consistency distillation with a multi-step ODE solver (m=5) and adversarial loss against teacher-generated data to raise the performance ceiling. A key architectural choice replaces the VAE decoder in the discriminator pipeline with a simple sub-pixel upsampling layer, feeding upsampled latents directly into a frozen DINOv2 backbone augmented with trainable 1D temporal and 2D spatial discriminator heads (StyleGAN-T style), reducing GPU memory from 73.5 GB to 35.8 GB. The paper removes classifier-free guidance entirely (showing it causes overexposure in distilled models) and introduces the Time Travel Sampler (TTS), a high-order inference scheme that 'travels' to an intermediate time step for a refined prediction before reverting, achieving FVD 171.15 at 1 step / 2 NFE—outperforming AnimateLCM's 8-step result (FVD 184.79).

## Results

On the OpenVid-1M validation set, OSV with the Time Travel Sampler (TTS) achieves an FVD of 171.15 in just 1 step (2 NFE), surpassing AnimateLCM's 8-step result (FVD 184.79) and approaching the 25-step Stable Video Diffusion teacher (FVD 156.94). Without TTS, OSV at 1 step achieves FVD 335.36, still substantially outperforming SF-V (FVD 425.68) and AnimateLCM (FVD 997.94) at the same 1-step setting. The 2-step OSV variant (FVD 181.95) further narrows the gap to the teacher's 8-step performance (FVD 229.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSV with TTS (1 step, FVD 171.15) outperforms AnimateLCM at 8 steps (FVD 184.79) by 13.64 FVD points while using only 2 NFE versus 8, representing a 4× reduction in network evaluations. Against SF-V (1 step, FVD 425.68), OSV at 1 step (FVD 335.36) improves FVD by 90.32 points, and OSV+TTS further reduces it to 171.15, a 254.53-point improvement. Compared to the 25-step SVD teacher (FVD 156.94), OSV+TTS at 1 step is within 14.21 FVD points, meaning a single forward pass nearly matches 50 NFE of iterative denoising. AnimateLCM degrades sharply at low steps (FVD 997.94 at 1 step vs. 184.79 at 8 steps), whereas OSV maintains strong quality even at 1 step, highlighting the advantage of the two-stage GAN + consistency distillation training over pure consistency distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSV positions itself at the intersection of two competing acceleration paradigms for video diffusion: consistency distillation (LCM, AnimateLCM) and adversarial distillation (ADD, SF-V). Rather than simply porting image-domain methods to video (as AnimateLCM and SF-V do), the paper argues these two approaches have complementary weaknesses—consistency models struggle at very low step counts and suffer from CFG-induced overexposure, while GAN-based methods face training instability and cannot support iterative refinement. The key architectural departure is a two-stage decomposition (GAN pretraining with LoRA, then adversarial consistency distillation) combined with operating the discriminator entirely in latent space via DINOv2 features and a simple upsampling layer, replacing the VAE decoder used by ADD and LADD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 1-step FVD of 171.15 approaches the 25-step teacher (156.94) while reducing inference from ~30 seconds to a single forward pass, making real-time or near-real-time video generation feasible. The finding that CFG is actively harmful for distilled video models (FVD degrades from 335 to 531 at scale 3.0) challenges a widely-used default and may influence how future distilled video models are configured. Limitations include degradation on human hand motion (requiring 4 steps to resolve), evaluation on a single base model (SVD) and single dataset (OpenVid-1M), and the approach's dependence on a frozen DINOv2 backbone whose capacity may bottleneck more complex scenes. Open questions include whether the two-stage recipe generalizes to text-to-video, longer sequences, or other base architectures.
