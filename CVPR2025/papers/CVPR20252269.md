## Problem

Training-free long video generation from short-video diffusion models faces a fundamental trade-off: global methods (feeding the full sequence) preserve appearance consistency but degrade quality with missing objects and slow motion, while local sliding-window methods retain generation quality but produce inconsistent appearances across windows. No existing training-free approach effectively integrates the complementary strengths of both global consistency and local quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252269/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates a 64-frame video using pre-trained 16-frame models (VideoCrafter2, LaVie) entirely at inference time with no additional training. It operates on the temporal transformer features within the U-net, slicing global features to match each sliding window position. The framework also extends to multi-prompt generation (different prompts per segment) and video continuation via DDIM inversion of a given short video.

## Method

FreePCA applies PCA along the temporal dimension to both global (full-sequence) and local (sliding-window) temporal attention features, projecting them into a shared principal component space. Cosine similarity between corresponding components identifies which principal components encode consistent appearance (high similarity) versus motion intensity (low similarity); the top-k most similar global components are extracted as consistency features while the remaining local components retain motion information. A progressive fusion strategy gradually increases k from 0 to K_max=3 as the window slides, so early windows preserve original quality and later windows enforce stronger consistency, with overlapping regions averaged. The fused features are mapped back via P^T, and the full FreePCA pipeline is applied only in the first 25 of 50 DDIM denoising steps (later steps use the local method alone). A reuse-mean-statistics trick replaces the mean of subsequent noise blocks with the mean of the first f frames to further anchor appearance consistency.

## Results

On VBench (326 prompts, 64-frame generation from 16-frame base models VideoCrafter2 and LaVie), FreePCA achieves the best scores across all six metrics: Subject Consistency 95.54, Background Consistency 95.24, Overall Consistency 25.69, Motion Smoothness 96.41, Dynamic Degree 59.72, and Imaging Quality 63.70. The largest gains are in Dynamic Degree (+6.95 over FreeNoise, +13.89 over FreeLong) and Subject Consistency (+1.77 over FreeLong, +3.56 over FreeNoise), indicating that FreePCA preserves motion richness while improving appearance consistency. Inference time is 4.7 minutes on an RTX 4090, slightly higher than baselines (3.6–4.3 min) but within a practical range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreePCA outperforms both baselines on all six VBench metrics. Against FreeNoise [31] (noise rescheduling), FreePCA gains +3.56 in Subject Consistency, +1.38 in Background Consistency, +0.07 in Overall Consistency, +1.58 in Motion Smoothness, +6.95 in Dynamic Degree, and +0.21 in Imaging Quality — the most significant advantage being in Dynamic Degree, where FreeNoise's strict noise constraints suppress motion richness. Against FreeLong [28] (spectral-blend temporal attention), FreePCA leads by +1.77 in Subject Consistency, +1.45 in Background Consistency, +0.93 in Overall Consistency, +1.92 in Motion Smoothness, +13.89 in Dynamic Degree, and +0.35 in Imaging Quality. FreeLong's simplistic frequency-domain fusion fails to recover motion diversity, resulting in near-static videos (Dynamic Degree 45.83 vs. 59.72). Direct sampling (no long-video strategy) performs worst on Overall Consistency (23.52) and Dynamic Degree (44.45), confirming the distribution-shift problem that motivates the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the training-free long video generation literature, specifically addressing the tension between global-aligned methods (which preserve consistency but degrade quality) and local-stitched methods (which preserve quality but lose consistency). It draws a conceptual bridge from PCA-based background subtraction in video segmentation (spatial dimension) to the temporal dimension, repurposing PCA's decoupling capacity to separate consistent appearance from motion intensity in video features. The method builds on FreeNoise's sliding-window framework and FreeLong's global-local fusion idea, but departs from both by performing the integration in a principal component space with cosine-similarity-based component selection rather than in the frequency domain or via noise rescheduling. Attention entropy scaling from [25] is borrowed to handle the distribution shift of global features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature and model-agnostic applicability (demonstrated on both U-Net-based VideoCrafter2 and LaVie) make this a practically accessible technique for practitioners who cannot afford retraining. The PCA-based decoupling provides an interpretable mechanism for the consistency-quality tradeoff, which could inform future work on feature-space interventions in diffusion models. However, the evaluation is limited to 16→64 frame generation, and the method adds ~30% inference time; scalability to much longer sequences (hundreds of frames) and to DiT-based architectures remains to be thoroughly validated. An open question is whether the fixed K_max=3 and the 25/25 step split generalize across different denoising schedules and model scales.
