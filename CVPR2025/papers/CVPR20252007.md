## Problem

Existing federated learning methods that use Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) on the client side (e.g., FedSAM) optimize local sharpness, but the paper provides the first empirical evidence that local flatness does not guarantee a globally flat minimum, especially under high data heterogeneity. Prior work addressing this gap (FedSmoo) doubles communication cost via ADMM-based alignment, making it impractical for real-world FL deployments where bandwidth is the primary bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB image classification: CIFAR-10/100 (32×32, 10/100 classes) split across 100 clients with Dirichlet-controlled heterogeneity, and Landmarks-User-160K (2,028 classes, 164K images across 1,262 clients). Each client trains locally on its private subset for E epochs and sends only model parameter updates to the server; the server produces a single global model that maps images to class labels. FEDGLOSS is architecture-agnostic (tested with CNN, ResNet18, MobileNetV2) and compatible with any local optimizer (SGD or SAM).

## Method

FEDGLOSS applies SAM as the server-side optimizer rather than the client-side optimizer, directly minimizing the global loss landscape's sharpness. The key communication-efficiency trick is approximating SAM's perturbation direction ε̂^t using the previous round's pseudo-gradient Δ̃_w^{t−1} (scaled to norm ρ), eliminating the need for a second client-server exchange that exact SAM would require. To reduce the approximation error between the true and estimated perturbation, FEDGLOSS incorporates ADMM to enforce directional consistency between local and global updates, which also prevents the parameter explosion that typically plagues ADMM-based FL methods. The server update becomes: perturb w^t with ρ·Δ̃_w^{t−1}/‖Δ̃_w^{t−1}‖, collect client updates on the perturbed model, and take a single SGD step along the resulting sharpness-aware pseudo-gradient. This achieves 1× communication cost (identical to FedAvg) while producing the flattest global minima across all tested benchmarks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (α=0) with a CNN, FEDGLOSS with local SAM achieves 83.9% accuracy, outperforming the best SOTA FedSmoo (81.3%) by ~2.5 points while using half the communication cost (1× vs. 2×). On the more challenging CIFAR-100 (α=0), FEDGLOSS reaches 50.6% vs. FedSmoo's 47.8% and FedSAM's 28.7%. With local SGD, FEDGLOSS still surpasses all SGD-based baselines by ~4 points (e.g., 69.5% vs. FedDyn's 65.5% on CIFAR-10 α=0). On the large-scale LANDMARKS-USER-160K dataset with MobileNetv2, FEDGLOSS achieves 59.7% accuracy, the best overall result, with 1× communication cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDGLOSS with local SAM consistently outperforms FedSmoo (the strongest SOTA) by ~2.5 percentage points across all CIFAR configurations while halving communication cost (1× vs. 2×). Against FedSAM, the improvement is dramatic on CIFAR-100 (50.6% vs. 28.7% at α=0), confirming that local sharpness optimization fails under high heterogeneity. FedDyn+SAM achieves competitive results on CIFAR-10 (79.3%) but fails to converge on CIFAR-100 due to ADMM parameter explosion, whereas FEDGLOSS avoids this issue through SAM's stabilizing effect. Scaffold and FedGamma fail to converge on CIFAR-100 entirely. With local SGD, FEDGLOSS still leads all SGD-based methods by ~4 points (69.5% vs. FedDyn's 65.5% on CIFAR-10 α=0). On the large-scale LANDMARKS-USER-160K benchmark, FEDGLOSS (59.7%) marginally edges out FedSmoo (59.5%) while using half the communication, and both SCAFFOLD and FedGamma fail to converge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work applying Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to federated learning, departing from the client-side approach of FedSAM/FedSpeed/FedGamma (which assume local flatness implies global flatness) and the communication-heavy ADMM-based FedSmoo. The key conceptual shift is moving SAM to the server side to directly optimize global sharpness, while borrowing the previous-gradient approximation strategy from centralized SAM efficiency work (DP-SAT, SAF) to avoid doubling communication. The ADMM component for local-global consistency builds on FedDyn, but the paper shows that server-side SAM stabilizes ADMM against the parameter explosion that plagues it in heterogeneous settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that local flatness does not guarantee global flatness—especially under high data heterogeneity and task complexity—challenges a core assumption in the FedSAM line of work and motivates server-side optimization for FL generalization. The practical impact is significant: FEDGLOSS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with 1× communication cost (vs. 2× for FedSmoo) and enables any local optimizer, reducing client-side compute. Limitations include the requirement for stateful clients (ADMM dual variables), evaluation limited to vision classification tasks, and dependence on directional consistency of pseudo-gradients across rounds. Open questions include whether the pseudo-gradient approximation degrades under extreme client churn or highly non-stationary data distributions, and whether the approach extends to generative or non-convex FL objectives.
