## Problem

Existing PEFT methods (Adapter, LoRA, Prompt Tuning) are all reducible to MLP structures with fixed activation functions, making them prone to overfitting on small downstream datasets with distribution shifts. Their reliance on high bottleneck ratios and fixed activations limits generalization, and the trade-off between parameter count and regularization demands extensive hyperparameter tuning that undermines the plug-and-play appeal of PEFT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250572/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (224×224 patches) or video clips (up to 32 frames) as input, processes them through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone initialized with either ImageNet-21K or CLIP pre-trained weights, and produces class labels for image classification or action class labels for video recognition. LoKi modules are inserted alongside the MLP in every ViT block, and for video tasks an additional Time LoKi and Time Attention module handle temporal modeling by reordering tensor dimensions from (B·T, N+1, D) to (B·(N+1), T, D). The bottleneck ratio r₁ is set as low as 0.0625, far below the ≥0.5 typically required by Adapter, yielding only ~4M tunable parameters for image tasks and ~6M for video tasks.

## Method

LoKi is an adapter-based PEFT module that disentangles nonlinear function fitting from linear dimension mapping: a single-layer linear encoder (FC Down) compresses features, a minimal 2-layer Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) serves as the learnable activation (each node computing a SiLU plus a sum of B-splines with learnable coefficients), and a single-layer linear decoder (FC Up) restores the original feature dimension. The KAN's internal width follows the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem (N₃ = 2N₂ + 1), and the ratio of parameters allocated to linear versus activation layers is tunable via hyperparameters r₁ and r₂. This design achieves roughly half the parameters of Adapter at a much lower bottleneck ratio while avoiding vanilla KAN's slow training speed and 33 GB memory footprint, because the linear encoder provides dense feature representations that reduce the computational load on the spline-based activation layer.

## Results

On image classification across 7 datasets, LoKi achieves the highest average top-1 accuracy of 85.4% with the lowest cross-weight variance of 2.3 (measured as |acc_IN21K − acc_CLIP|), using only 4M tunable parameters and 10.4 GB memory. On the most distribution-shifted datasets, LoKi's advantage is most pronounced: 74.2% on DDSM (vs. Adapter 67.5%, KAN 67.0%) and 71.6% on FGVC Aircraft (vs. Adapter 46.5%, KAN 43.7%). On video action recognition, LoKi(ViT) reaches 89.5% top-1 on Diving48 with 6M tunable params (vs. AIM's 88.9% with 11M) and 73.3% on HMDB51, outperforming all other PEFT baselines by 7+ points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoKi outperforms all 7 image-classification baselines on both average accuracy and cross-weight generalization variance. Against the closest competitor Bias Tuning (84.5%, variance 4.4), LoKi gains +0.9% accuracy while halving the variance to 2.3. The gap widens dramatically against Adapter (67.9%, variance 38.5) and vanilla KAN (64.8%, variance 40.6): LoKi achieves +17.5% and +20.6% average accuracy respectively, with variance reduced by 16× and 18×, while using fewer parameters (4M vs. 7M for Adapter) and far less memory (10.4 GB vs. 32.9 GB for KAN). On video, LoKi(ViT) beats AIM by +0.6% on Diving48 (89.5% vs. 88.9%) with only 56.9% of AIM's tunable parameters, and surpasses the next-best HMDB51 method (VideoPrompt, 66.4%) by 6.9 points. The one clear loss is on large-scale video datasets (K400: 80.0% vs. AIM's 83.9%; SSv2: 56.3% vs. 66.4%), where the authors note that Adapter's higher parameter count becomes advantageous when overfitting risk is low.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoKi extends the Adapter bottleneck architecture (Houlsby et al., 2019) by replacing the fixed-activation MLP with a minimal 2-layer Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (Liu et al., 2024) as a learnable activation layer, while keeping single-layer linear networks for dimension mapping. This departs from the uniform MLP representation shared by Adapter, LoRA, and prompt-based methods, where nonlinear fitting depends on fixed activations combined with linear layers. The key conceptual shift is disentangling activation from linear layers, allowing the parameter budget to be allocated to B-spline-based learnable functions rather than concentrated in weight matrices, and enabling a much lower bottleneck ratio (r₁=0.0625 vs. ≥0.5 for Adapter).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoKi addresses a practical pain point in PEFT: overfitting on small, distribution-shifted downstream datasets where Adapter and KAN show large accuracy variance across pre-trained weights. The finding that parameter distribution (activation vs. linear) matters more than total parameter count for generalization offers a useful design principle for future PEFT modules. However, the approach's advantage diminishes on large datasets (K400, SSv2) where Adapter outperforms it, and the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (ViT-B/16) with two pre-trained weight sets, leaving open whether the generalization benefits persist at scale or across architectures. The reduced per-task parameter footprint (4–6M) also has practical implications for multi-task deployment and federated learning settings.
