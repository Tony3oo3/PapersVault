## Problem

Skeleton data from different sensors naturally exhibit heterogeneity in coordinate dimensions (2D vs. 3D) and joint counts (17 vs. 25 joints), yet existing skeleton-based action recognition methods train separate models for each homogeneous skeleton type. This paper is the first to address the challenge of learning a single unified action representation that can process heterogeneous skeletons with varying joint dimensions and topological structures simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251265/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts two heterogeneous skeleton streams: a 3D 25-joint sequence (Kinect V2) and a 2D 17-joint sequence (extracted via HRNet from RGB video). The 2D skeleton is first augmented with three interpolated spine joints and converted to 3D by a 4-layer MLP, then both skeletons are padded with trainable skeleton-specific prompts (5×3 and 10×3) to form a unified 30-joint representation. A third modality—semantic motion encoding—encodes per-joint, per-axis motion direction (7 discrete words) through a frozen CLIP text encoder into low-dimensional features. The output is a D-dimensional unified action representation usable for linear-probe classification, cosine-similarity retrieval, and cross-dataset transfer.

## Method

The framework has two stages. In heterogeneous skeleton processing, a 3D pose estimation MLP lifts 2D skeletons to 3D, and trainable skeleton-specific prompts fill topology gaps so both skeletons share a unified 30-joint format; additionally, semantic motion encoding maps each joint's frame-to-frame displacement direction (left/right/up/down/front/back/unmove) through a pre-trained CLIP encoder and a 1-dim reduction to produce a coordinate-aligned semantic stream. In unified representation learning, embeddings from the 25-joint, 17-joint, and semantic-motion streams are early-fused by averaging and a learnable linear projection, then processed by two single-head Transformers (spatial and temporal, hidden dim 1024) to produce the final feature. Training is fully self-supervised: a feature consistency loss (MSE between fused and per-modality projected representations) enforces cross-modal alignment, VICREG variance-covariance regularization prevents representation collapse, and an L2 reconstruction loss on common joints supervises the 3D pose estimation module jointly.

## Results

On NTU-60, the proposed method (J+C+S) achieves 87.8% (x-sub) and 93.7% (x-view) top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baselines USDRL (87.1%/93.2%) and IGM (86.2%/91.2%). On NTU-120, it reaches 78.9% (x-sub) and 82.2% (x-set), surpassing 3s-UmURL (75.9%/77.2%) and 3s-CMD (74.7%/76.1%) but trailing IGM (80.0%/81.4%) and USDRL (79.3%/80.6%) on x-sub. On PKU-MMD II, it achieves 58.2% x-sub accuracy, below USDRL (59.7%) but well above 3s-UmURL (54.3%) and 3s-CMD (52.6%). The method uses only 2.54 GFLOPs, comparable to 3s-UmURL (5.22 GFLOPs) and far below 3s-CMD (17.28 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's full model (J+C+S) achieves the best NTU-60 results (87.8% x-sub, 93.7% x-view), beating USDRL by 0.7/0.5 points and IGM by 1.6/2.5 points. On NTU-120 x-set it leads all baselines (82.2% vs. IGM 81.4%, USDRL 80.6%), but on NTU-120 x-sub it trails IGM (78.9% vs. 80.0%) and USDRL (78.9% vs. 79.3%). On PKU-MMD II, USDRL retains a narrow edge (59.7% vs. 58.2%), while the proposed method substantially outperforms 3s-UmURL (54.3%) and 3s-CMD (52.6%). In the action retrieval task (Table 2), J+C+S achieves 72.7% (NTU-60 x-sub) and 61.9% (NTU-120 x-sub), surpassing UmURL (72.0%/59.5%) and CMD (70.6%/58.3%). In semi-supervised settings (Table 4), the method reaches 76.3% (5% x-sub) and 79.1% (5% x-view) on NTU-60, outperforming 3s-CMD (74.3%/77.2%) and UmURL (74.6%/78.6%), though it underperforms at the 1% label budget (55.0% vs. UmURL 59.6%). For transfer learning to PKU-MMD II (Table 5), the method achieves 64.3% (from NTU-60) and 63.1% (from NTU-120), exceeding UmURL (59.7%/58.5%) by roughly 4–5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised skeleton-based action recognition line (AimCLR, CPM, CMD, UmURL) by introducing a unified framework that processes heterogeneous skeletons—differing in both coordinate dimensionality (2D vs. 3D) and joint topology (17-joint vs. 25-joint)—within a single model. It builds directly on VICREG's variance-covariance regularization for the self-supervised training objective and borrows the early-fusion multi-modal strategy from UmURL, but departs from prior multi-modal work (which fuses joints/bones/motion of the *same* skeleton) by fusing fundamentally different skeleton structures. The use of CLIP's text encoder for semantic motion encoding and trainable skeleton-specific prompts to fill missing joints are novel repurposings of language-model and prompt-learning ideas for the skeleton domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a practical gap: real-world deployments (e.g., robots with different humanoid structures, mixed sensor pipelines) inevitably produce heterogeneous skeleton data, yet all prior self-supervised methods assume a single fixed topology. The unified framework's transferability to FineGYM (a 2D 17-joint dataset) without retraining the encoder suggests genuine cross-domain utility. However, the model is limited to two skeleton types and up to two people, the 1% semi-supervised setting underperforms (indicating data hunger), and the performance gains over strong baselines like USDRL are modest (e.g., 87.8% vs. 87.1% on NTU-60 x-sub), leaving open whether the heterogeneity framing scales to more than two skeleton families or to multi-person scenarios.
