## Problem

RGBD-based category-level 6D pose estimation methods rely on depth sensors that fail on transparent, reflective, or dark materials, while existing stereo methods suffer from parameter entanglement—without explicit depth, pose, scale, and shape become coupled, causing optimization to stall in local optima. Additionally, no benchmark exists that systematically evaluates stereo versus RGBD methods across diverse material types, as the TOD dataset covers only transparent objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252567/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a calibrated stereo image pair with known baseline and intrinsics, plus per-view object masks, and produces a 6D pose (rotation matrix and translation vector), a shared scale factor, and an intra-category occupancy field defined in a normalized coordinate system common to both views. The occupancy field is queried at arbitrary 3D points via cross-attention rather than stored as a voxel grid, and the pose is refined over 5 iterative GSF-Net steps. DINOv2 (ViT-B) and DPT are used as frozen feature extractors, so the trainable parameters are limited to the downstream heads.

## Method

The core idea is to use global stereo consistency as a hard constraint that decouples otherwise entangled parameters. A shared Intra-Category Occupancy Field (cross-attention over positional, shape, and category embeddings, following ZeroShape) provides a single unified shape across both views, eliminating shape ambiguity. GSF-Net then iteratively fits this shape to both views via an Adaptive Occupancy Query that renders normalized depth maps at coarse resolution and refines only around silhouette boundaries. A Shared Decoder jointly predicts rotation, y-translation, and λ = s/z offsets for both views (enforcing consistency), while independent per-view X-translation decoders handle the residual scale ambiguity. The Hybrid Feature Encoder fuses DINOv2 semantic features (with disparity-restricted attention) and DPT geometric features via bidirectional cross-attention, and training proceeds in three stages (shape → alignment → fitting) to avoid multitask instability.

## Results

On the S-RGBD dataset (10 categories, diverse materials), the method achieves 3D_avg scores of 82.9–89.7 on depth-friendly objects (Bowl, Box, Can, Cup, Bottle) and 66.4–82.6 on depth-challenging objects (Mug, Knife, Spoon, Plate, Wine Glass), outperforming all stereo baselines by 15–40 points. On the TOD benchmark (transparent objects), it reaches 3D_25 = 100% and 3D_75 = 64.1% (Bottle) / 95.9% (Mug), surpassing CODERS (31.5% / 92.8% at 3D_75) and all RGBD methods. On depth-challenging materials, the method outperforms the best RGBD baseline (Second) by 35–50 points (e.g., Wine Glass: 74.1 vs. 24.1; Knife: 67.9 vs. 49.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest RGBD baseline (Second [5]), the method trades a small margin on depth-friendly objects (e.g., Box: 89.7 vs. 91.2; Bottle: 86.2 vs. 87.8) for a dramatic advantage on depth-challenging materials (Wine Glass: 74.1 vs. 24.1; Knife: 67.9 vs. 49.1; Mug: 80.2 vs. 78.8; Plate: 81.2 vs. 84.1). Against the best prior stereo method (StereoPose [3]), the method wins on every category by 15–40 points (e.g., Wine Glass: 74.1 vs. 53.4; Mug: 80.2 vs. 64.8; Knife: 67.9 vs. 58.1). Against KeyPose [29], the margin is even larger (20–45 points across categories). On the TOD benchmark of transparent objects, the method achieves near-perfect 3D_25 (100%) and substantially improves 3D_75 over CODERS [56] (64.1% vs. 31.5% on Bottle; 95.9% vs. 92.8% on Mug) and over RGBD methods SPD/SGPA (which score below 50% at 3D_25). Overall, the method is the only approach that maintains high accuracy (>66%) across both depth-friendly and depth-challenging materials, whereas RGBD methods collapse to 22–54% on challenging surfaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the render-and-compare paradigm (Megapose) and occupancy-based shape representation (ZeroShape, Occupancy Networks) to the stereo setting, departing from prior stereo methods (StereoPose, CODERS) that rely on explicit or implicit stereo matching for depth. Rather than using NOCS maps or pixel-level correspondence to resolve scale ambiguity, the authors introduce a shared Intra-Category Occupancy Field and a Global Shape Fitting Network (GSF-Net) that leverages the fundamental stereo consistency constraint—shared rotation, y-translation, and λ=s/z across views—to decouple entangled parameters in a divide-and-conquer fashion. The hybrid feature encoder fusing DINOv2 semantic embeddings with DPT geometric embeddings via disparity-restricted cross-attention is a practical integration of foundation models into the category-level pose pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The S-RGBD dataset fills a notable gap by providing paired stereo and RGBD data across diverse materials (transparent, reflective, dark, textured), enabling the first systematic comparison of stereo vs. RGBD methods under controlled conditions. The finding that stereo methods can match RGBD on depth-friendly objects while substantially outperforming them on depth-challenging ones (e.g., 81.2 vs. 26.3 on mugs) provides practical guidance for system designers choosing sensor modalities. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or multi-object scenes, whether the 5-iteration GSF-Net can be accelerated for real-time robotic deployment, and how performance degrades under significant viewpoint changes or partial occlusion beyond the tested conditions.
