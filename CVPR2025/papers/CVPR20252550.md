## Problem

Personalizing diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) on edge devices is blocked by the memory cost of backpropagation: storing activations and gradients for gradient-based fine-tuning requires 6–19 GB of VRAM, far exceeding mobile or on-device budgets. Quantized models reduce inference memory, but training them still demands dequantization for accurate gradient computation. The paper asks whether a quantized diffusion model can be personalized using only forward passes, eliminating backpropagation entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252550/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of reference images (typically 2–5 per subject from the DreamBooth dataset) and a text prompt containing a single pseudo-token placeholder. It outputs a personalized token embedding of 3 KB (safetensors format) that is appended to the text encoder vocabulary of Stable Diffusion v1.5; at inference time, the token in the prompt triggers generation of the personalized subject. Training runs with batch size 1, INT8 quantization across all Linear and Conv2D layers, and requires only 2.37 GB peak VRAM on a single GPU.

## Method

ZOODiP personalizes a fully INT8-quantized Stable Diffusion model by optimizing a single Textual Inversion token via Random Gradient Estimation (RGE), which approximates the gradient using n forward passes with random perturbations (n=2, μ=10⁻³) and the ZOAdam optimizer—no backpropagation or activation storage is needed. To counter the high variance of ZO gradients, Subspace Gradient (SG) stores the token trajectory over τ=128 iterations, performs PCA, and projects out the low-variance eigendirections (controlled by ν=10⁻³) from each estimated gradient, effectively denoising the update. Partial Uniform Timestep Sampling (PUTS) restricts diffusion timestep sampling to U(500, 900), the range where text conditioning most strongly influences generation, skipping less informative early timesteps. Together these three components reduce peak training memory by up to 8.2× compared to DreamBooth while achieving comparable CLIP-I, CLIP-T, and DINOv2 alignment scores.

## Results

On the DreamBooth dataset with Stable Diffusion v1.5 (INT8 quantized, batch size 1), ZOODiP achieves CLIP-T 0.287, CLIP-I 0.772, and DINO 0.558 while consuming only 2.37 GB of training memory — an 8.2× reduction compared to DreamBooth (19.4 GB) and a 64.9% reduction compared to Textual Inversion (6.75 GB). ZOODiP also achieves the fastest training speed at 16.1–22.3 iter/sec (n=1/2, FP16), which is 2.2× faster than TI and 4.2× faster than TuneQDM. Storage for the optimized token is only 3 KB, making it suitable for edge deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamBooth (19.4 GB), ZOODiP uses 8.2× less memory while matching or slightly exceeding its CLIP-T score (0.287 vs 0.281), though it trails on CLIP-I (0.772 vs 0.782) and DINO (0.558 vs 0.592). Compared to its direct baseline Textual Inversion (6.75 GB), ZOODiP reduces memory by 64.9% with near-identical performance: +0.7% on CLIP-T, −0.8% on CLIP-I, and −0.2% on DINO. Against TuneQDM (8.96 GB), the closest quantized-diffusion competitor, ZOODiP uses 3.8× less memory with a negligible 0.7% drop on CLIP-T and a 0.5% gain on DINO. Against GF-TI, the only other gradient-free method at the same 2.37 GB memory budget, ZOODiP dramatically outperforms it: +43.0% on CLIP-I (0.772 vs 0.540) and +13.4% on CLIP-T (0.287 vs 0.253), while GF-TI's DINO score collapses to 0.011. QLoRA and PEQA achieve slightly higher CLIP-T and DINO scores but require 2.7–3.7× more memory and still rely on backpropagation, making them unsuitable for the on-device setting ZOODiP targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZOODiP sits at the intersection of three research threads: Textual Inversion (single-token personalization of diffusion models), zeroth-order optimization (forward-pass-only gradient estimation pioneered by MeZO for LLMs), and quantized model fine-tuning (QLoRA, PEQA, TuneQDM). It departs from prior efficient personalization methods in a key way: while QLoRA, PEQA, and TuneQDM still rely on backpropagation through quantized weights, and GF-TI avoids backprop but uses evolution strategies that are unstable at small batch sizes, ZOODiP combines Random Gradient Estimation with INT8-quantized networks, adding two novel components—Subspace Gradient (PCA-based projection of noisy ZO gradients onto the token's effective subspace) and Partial Uniform Timestep Sampling (restricting training to timesteps where text conditioning has maximal influence). The subspace observation, grounded in PCA analysis of Textual Inversion token trajectories, is the paper's most distinctive conceptual contribution, distinguishing it from both gradient-based and evolution-strategy baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for on-device and edge personalization: 2.37 GB peak VRAM (vs. 19.4 GB for DreamBooth) with comparable CLIP-I/DINO alignment scores makes private, user-specific fine-tuning feasible on consumer mobile hardware without cloud offloading. The finding that personalized tokens concentrate their updates in a low-dimensional subspace (over 80% of dimensions projectable out) suggests that the effective complexity of single-concept personalization is much lower than the nominal token dimensionality, which could inform future parameter-efficient methods beyond ZO. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger diffusion models (SDXL, DiT-based), multi-concept personalization, or other quantization schemes (INT4, mixed-precision), and whether the PUTS timestep window remains optimal across architectures and noise schedules.
