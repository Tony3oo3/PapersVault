## Problem

Existing federated unlearning methods either require auxiliary storage of historical model updates, direct access to the target data being removed, or prohibitively expensive retraining from scratch. No prior work explores unlearning through weight negation, and no method simultaneously avoids both storage overhead and the need to retain access to the data targeted for removal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251731/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a converged global FL model (trained via FedAvg), a binary unlearning request from the target client (no data is transmitted), and the retained data D_r from all clients for the fine-tuning phase. The output is an unlearned model approximating the gold-standard retrained model. Notably, the forget data D_u is never accessed, so the target client can delete it immediately after requesting unlearning, and no additional storage is required on either the client or server side.

## Method

NoT operates in two phases: (1) the server negates (multiplies by −1) the parameters of selected layers in the converged global model, and (2) the perturbed model is fine-tuned on retained data via standard gradient descent. The key insight is that negation is the strongest possible perturbation (it maximizes activation changes, Theorem 2) while simultaneously preserving layer-wise optimality and Jacobian control (Theorems 3–4), meaning the perturbed model remains well-positioned for rapid re-optimization. Typically only the first layer is negated, as this breaks inter-layer co-adaptation at the lowest level, cascading significant parameter updates through deeper layers during fine-tuning and effectively erasing user-specific high-level features. The approach is formalized within a theoretical unlearning framework requiring a perturbation to be both strong (maximizing the loss gap between retained and forget data) and resilient (preserving optimization geometry for fast recovery).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with CNN, NoT achieves an average gap of 0.29% from the Retrain gold standard (Retain 91.69%, Forget 83.86%, Test 82.65%, MIA 50.23%), with the lowest communication cost (7.09e9 bytes) and computation (3.06e16 FLOPs) among all methods. On Caltech-101 with ViT-B/16, NoT attains an average gap of 0.79% (Retain 99.70%, Forget 50.81%, Test 47.83%, MIA 50.07%), outperforming the next-best baselines MoDE (1.60%) and FCU (1.61%). In the centralized setting on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, NoT achieves an average gap of 0.34% with 2.19e15 FLOPs, compared to l1-sparse's 0.19% gap but at 2.98e15 FLOPs (36% more computation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoT achieves the lowest average gap in 3 of 6 federated settings: CIFAR-10 CNN (0.29% vs. FCU's 0.81%), CIFAR-100 CNN (0.75% vs. MoDE's 1.07%), and Caltech-101 ViT (0.79% vs. MoDE's 1.60%), while also recording the lowest communication and computation costs in the CNN settings. On ResNet-18, FCU outperforms NoT (0.84% vs. 1.78% on CIFAR-10; 2.51% vs. 4.73% on CIFAR-100), though NoT still maintains the lowest computation cost (2.49e18 vs. 2.64e18 FLOPs on CIFAR-10). FedEraser and FUKD perform substantially worse (avg gaps of 1.58–8.21%) and require growing auxiliary storage, making them impractical for larger architectures. In the centralized setting on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, l1-sparse achieves a slightly lower average gap (0.19% vs. 0.34%) but at 36% higher computation (2.98e15 vs. 2.19e15 FLOPs), while NoT outperforms all other baselines including SalUn (1.64%), GA (2.02%), and BadT (2.32%). Overall, NoT provides the best balance of unlearning efficacy, model fidelity, and efficiency, with its key advantage being zero additional storage and no access to the forget data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant federated unlearning paradigms—storing historical model updates (FedEraser, FUKD) and gradient modification requiring target data access (PGD, MoDE, FCU)—by proposing a perturbation-based approach that needs neither. While prior weight-perturbation unlearning methods (Golatkar et al., SSD, Tarun et al.) rely on Fisher information, NTK, or learned noise matrices, NoT repurposes the trivial Boolean NOT operation (multiplying layer weights by −1) as the perturbation, a direction the authors note has not been explored. The theoretical contribution is also novel: the 'strong + resilient' perturbation framework with layer-wise optimality (LWO) and Jacobian control provides a priori conditions for effective unlearning, rather than the a posteriori evaluation used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NoT removes two major practical barriers in federated unlearning—auxiliary storage on client/server and access to the data being forgotten—making compliance with regulations like GDPR's 'right to be forgotten' substantially more feasible in real deployments. The theoretical framework (LWO, Jacobian control) offers a general lens for designing future perturbation-based unlearning methods beyond negation. However, the evaluation is confined to vision classification on relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Caltech-101), and the method still requires access to retained data for fine-tuning recovery; scaling to ImageNet-scale or non-vision tasks remains open. The authors also note that negation does not cleanly cover ReLU-like activations (only conjectured 'approximately' LWOP), leaving a gap for the most common activation function.
