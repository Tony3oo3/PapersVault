## Problem

4D reconstruction from casual monocular videos with unknown camera parameters is highly ill-posed due to the absence of multi-view stereo cues, and existing methods either require synchronized multi-view inputs, known poses, or only fuse observations from a small temporal window. This paper addresses the full-stack challenge of going directly from an unposed, in-the-wild monocular video to a globally consistent, renderable 4D scene without relying on external pose estimation tools like COLMAP.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252444/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a sequence of monocular RGB frames (casual video) with no camera intrinsics or extrinsics as input, and internally leverages off-the-shelf 2D foundation models for depth estimation, long-term point tracking, and epipolar error computation. It produces a globally fused 4D dynamic Gaussian scene (approximately 106K foreground Gaussians driven by ~3K scaffold nodes) that can be rendered at any novel viewpoint and query time at near real-time speed (~38 FPS at 2× resolution), along with recovered camera poses/focal length and dense 3D correspondence across all frames.

## Method

The core contribution is the Motion Scaffold (MoSca), a sparse graph of 6-DoF SE(3) trajectory nodes connected by a curve-distance KNN topology, which encodes the scene's deformation as a compact, smoothly interpolated field via Dual Quaternion Blending with RBF-based skinning weights. The pipeline first initializes camera poses via tracklet-based bundle adjustment on confident static tracks, then lifts 2D foundation-model tracks and depth into 3D to seed MoSca nodes, and refines them with an as-rigid-as-possible (ARAP) loss over a multi-level topology pyramid plus velocity/acceleration smoothness. All foreground Gaussians from every timestep are deformed to a query time through the MoSca field and globally fused, then optimized end-to-end with Gaussian Splatting photometric losses, depth losses, and a tracking loss that supervises rendered XYZ flow maps against the 2D tracker output. A node control policy periodically prunes low-contribution nodes and densifies new ones from high-gradient tracking regions, keeping the scaffold compact (ratio ~46:1 Gaussians-to-nodes).

## Results

On the DyCheck benchmark (the most challenging monocular dynamic view synthesis dataset), MoSca achieves mPSNR 19.32, mSSIM 0.706, and mLPIPS 0.264 with known poses, outperforming the best prior method (Shape-of-Motion, mPSNR 17.32) by nearly 2 dB. Without camera poses, MoSca still achieves mPSNR 18.84 (19.02 with known focal length), surpassing the best pose-free baseline (RobustDynRF, mPSNR 17.10) by 1.7 dB. On the NVIDIA dataset, MoSca reaches PSNR 26.72 and LPIPS 0.070, marginally outperforming DynPoint (PSNR 26.53, LPIPS 0.068). For camera pose estimation, MoSca achieves the best ATE (0.031) and RPE rotation (0.426°) on TUM-dynamics without requiring known intrinsics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoSca decisively outperforms all 31 baselines on DyCheck, the most challenging monocular dynamic rendering benchmark. With known poses, it leads the nearest competitor Shape-of-Motion [100] by 2.0 dB mPSNR (19.32 vs. 17.32) and 0.108 mSSIM, while also achieving the best mLPIPS (0.264 vs. 0.296). In the pose-free setting, MoSca gains 1.74 dB over RobustDynRF [66] (18.84 vs. 17.10) and 3.05 dB over Gaussian Marbles [87] (18.84 vs. 15.79), with the gap widening for methods that rely on multi-view stereo cues (4D GS drops to 13.11, Dyn.Gaussians to 7.60). On NVIDIA, MoSca (PSNR 26.72) edges out DynPoint [120] (26.53) and CTNeRF [68] (26.13), though DynPoint achieves a slightly better LPIPS (0.068 vs. 0.070). For camera pose estimation, MoSca achieves the best ATE on TUM-dynamics (0.031 vs. MonST3R's 0.063) and the best RPE rotation on Sintel (0.312° vs. ParticleSfM's 0.535°), without requiring known intrinsics unlike DROID-SLAM or DPVO. For correspondence tracking, MoSca (PCK-T 0.824) surpasses CoTracker [40] (0.803), Gaussian Marbles [87] (0.806), and its own tracker BootsTAPIR [22] (0.779), demonstrating that the 4D reconstruction optimization refines the raw 2D tracks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoSca extends the Embedded Deformation Graph (EDG) framework from non-rigid SfM by connecting sparse SE(3) trajectory nodes to 3D Gaussian Splatting, using Dual Quaternion Blending for manifold-correct interpolation and ARAP regularization for physical plausibility. Its key departure from prior dynamic Gaussian methods (4D-GS, Gaussian Marbles, Shape-of-Motion) is the coordinated lifting of 2D foundation model priors (long-term tracking, monocular depth, epipolar error) into a shared 3D graph, rather than applying them in isolation in image space. The system also integrates tracklet-based bundle adjustment to solve camera poses end-to-end, eliminating the COLMAP dependency that most prior monocular dynamic NVS pipelines require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a fully automated, pose-free pipeline from casual monocular video to renderable 4D scenes, MoSca addresses the most practical input format for real-world deployment in spatial computing, embodied AI, and content creation. The compact representation (~3K nodes driving ~100K Gaussians) and near-real-time rendering (38 FPS) make it more deployable than dense per-frame alternatives. Key open questions remain: the method cannot hallucinate regions never visible in the video (requiring diffusion priors), does not model lighting effects like shadows and reflections, and its performance is tightly coupled to the quality of upstream 2D trackers and depth estimators.
