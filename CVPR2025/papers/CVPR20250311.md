## Problem

Existing head avatar methods rely on a single 3D representation (mesh, NeRF, or 3DGS) for all head components, but the face is predominantly surface-like while hair consists of volumetric thin structures. This forces a quality trade-off: mesh-based methods (e.g., PiCA) render faces well but produce texture-like hair with mesh-like boundaries, while 3DGS-based methods (e.g., GaussianAvatars) capture volumetric hair but suffer from blurry facial textures and interpenetration artifacts around eyes and mouth.

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view video (16 cameras, 802×550 resolution) along with per-frame FLAME parameters (shape β∈R³⁰⁰, expression ψ∈R¹⁰⁰, pose φ∈R¹⁵) and a view vector. It also requires pre-computed depth maps (from Metashape) and facial parsing masks for training supervision. The output is a photorealistic RGB rendering of the full head produced by mesh rasterization plus 3DGS splatting with occlusion-aware blending, as well as an animatable 3D avatar (enhanced FLAME mesh + 3DGS hair) that supports downstream editing such as hairstyle swapping and UV-space texture painting.

## Method

MeGA decouples head modeling into a neural mesh for the face and 3D Gaussian Splatting for the hair. For the face, an enhanced FLAME mesh (4-way subdivision + teeth, 16,428 vertices) is refined by a CNN-predicted UV displacement map conditioned on expression and pose, and colored via a disentangled neural texture (learnable diffuse map + view-dependent CNN + expression-dependent CNN) decoded by a compact 307-parameter per-pixel MLP. For the hair, a canonical 3DGS is optimized from a single frame, then animated per-frame via ICP rigid alignment to the scalp plus an MLP deformation field that predicts per-Gaussian offsets in position, rotation, scale, opacity, and SH coefficients. A key blending module uses a 'near-z' depth (first Gaussian exceeding an opacity threshold) rather than integrated 3DGS depth for stable occlusion testing, combined with early-stopping of alpha accumulation behind the mesh and Gaussian-smoothed soft boundaries to avoid hairline seams. Training proceeds in three sequential stages—facial mesh, canonical hair, then joint optimization—to avoid the underconstrained instability of optimizing the full hybrid from scratch.

## Results

On the NeRSemble dataset, MeGA achieves the best performance across all metrics for both novel-view and novel-expression synthesis. For novel-view synthesis, MeGA reaches PSNR 34.11, SSIM 0.954, and LPIPS 0.052, outperforming the second-best method (GaussianAvatars) by approximately 1 dB in PSNR. For novel-expression synthesis, MeGA scores PSNR 32.59, SSIM 0.949, and LPIPS 0.057, again leading all baselines. The ablation on subject 306 further confirms that each component (disentangled textures, UV displacement, occlusion-aware blending) contributes to the final quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeGA outperforms all four baselines on every metric for both novel-view and novel-expression synthesis. The closest competitor is GaussianAvatars [32], which uses a fully 3DGS-based representation; MeGA improves PSNR by 0.57 dB (novel-view) and 1.14 dB (novel-expression) over it, with the gap widening for novel expressions due to MeGA's expression-dependent dynamic texture. Gaussian Head Avatar [47] achieves competitive novel-view PSNR (29.48) but degrades severely on novel expressions (PSNR 22.02), revealing its heavy reliance on an implicit deformation and super-resolution module that does not generalize well. PointAvatar [52] and DELTA [10] trail significantly, with DELTA (a mesh+NeRF hybrid) scoring the lowest PSNR of 24.62/22.60, confirming that the 3DGS hair component in MeGA provides a substantial advantage over NeRF-based hair modeling. Qualitatively, MeGA avoids the interpenetration artifacts around eyes and mouth that plague 3DGS-only face rendering, and captures finer skin details (wrinkles, dimples) that mesh-only methods like PiCA miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeGA sits at the intersection of two established lines of head avatar research: mesh-based neural rendering (PiCA, Neural Head Avatars) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (GaussianAvatars, Gaussian Head Avatar). Its conceptual predecessor is DELTA, which also decouples face and hair into separate representations (mesh + NeRF), but MeGA advances this by replacing NeRF with 3DGS for hair and incorporating deferred neural rendering with disentangled textures for the face. The UV displacement map for geometry refinement draws on HeadCraft and Neural Point-based Volumetric Avatar, while the ICP-based rigid alignment plus MLP deformation field for hair animation follows the 3DGS-Avatar paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid mesh-Gaussian decomposition provides a practical recipe for combining the strengths of surface-based and volumetric representations, and the resulting disentangled components (diffuse/view/dynamic textures, separate hair Gaussians) naturally enable downstream editing tasks like hairstyle swapping and texture painting that monolithic representations cannot support. However, evaluation is limited to the NeRSemble dataset (9 subjects) and editing results are purely qualitative with no quantitative benchmark, leaving open questions about generalization to diverse appearances, real-time performance, and whether the three-stage optimization pipeline scales to longer or noisier sequences. The 'near-z' depth trick for stable occlusion testing is a useful practical insight that may transfer to other hybrid rendering pipelines.
