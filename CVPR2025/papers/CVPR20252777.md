## Problem

Existing video depth estimation methods either rely on per-frame image models that produce temporally inconsistent (flickering) results, or require camera poses and optical flow that are difficult to obtain for open-world videos with diverse content, motion, and length. No prior method can generate temporally consistent long depth sequences for arbitrary open-world videos without any supplementary geometric information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252777/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an open-world video of arbitrary length and content as input, requiring no camera poses, optical flow, or other geometric cues. It outputs an affine-invariant depth sequence (normalized to [0,1]) with a shared scale and shift across all frames, ensuring temporal consistency. The model is trained at 320×640 resolution but can infer at any resolution (e.g., 576×1024), and supports variable sequence lengths from a single frame up to arbitrarily long videos via segment-wise inference.

## Method

DepthCrafter repurposes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) from image-to-video generation into a video-to-depth conditional diffusion model by adapting the conditioning mechanism: video latents are concatenated frame-wise to the noisy depth latent, and CLIP embeddings of video frames are injected via cross-attention in a frame-to-frame manner. A three-stage training strategy progressively adapts the model: (1) full-model fine-tuning on a large realistic stereo-derived dataset with variable sequence lengths [1,25] to learn the video-to-depth task; (2) temporal-layer-only fine-tuning on the same realistic dataset with extended variable lengths [1,110] to support long temporal context while reducing memory; (3) spatial-layer-only fine-tuning on a small synthetic dataset (DynamicReplica + MatrixCity) with fixed 45-frame sequences to harvest precise depth details. For videos exceeding 110 frames, an inference strategy divides the video into overlapped segments, initializes each segment's latent from the previous segment's denoised output to anchor depth scale/shift, and stitches segments using a linearly-weighted latent interpolation in the overlap region.

## Results

DepthCrafter achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot video depth estimation across all four video benchmarks. On Sintel (~50 frames), it reaches AbsRel 0.270 and δ₁ 0.697; on KITTI (110 frames), AbsRel 0.104 and δ₁ 0.896; on ScanNet (90 frames), AbsRel 0.123 and δ₁ 0.856; and on Bonn (110 frames), AbsRel 0.071 and δ₁ 0.972. On the single-image NYU-v2 benchmark, it achieves AbsRel 0.072 and δ₁ 0.948, competitive with but slightly below Depth-Anything (V2). The model also supports variable-length sequences from 1 to 110 frames in a single inference pass, with an inference speed of 465.84 ms/frame at 1024×576 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DepthCrafter outperforms all five baselines on all four video datasets (Sintel, ScanNet, KITTI, Bonn) in both AbsRel and δ₁. Against the strongest video baseline ChronoDepth [54], it reduces AbsRel by 54% on Sintel (0.587→0.270) and 38% on KITTI (0.167→0.104). Against the best single-image method Depth-Anything-V2 [68], it achieves a 25.7% AbsRel improvement on KITTI (0.140→0.104) and a 5.4% improvement on ScanNet (0.135→0.123), with even larger δ₁ gains (e.g., 0.804→0.896 on KITTI). Against NVDS [63], the margins are largest on Sintel (AbsRel 0.408→0.270, a 34% reduction) and KITTI (0.253→0.104, a 59% reduction). On the single-image NYU-v2 benchmark, DepthCrafter (AbsRel 0.072) trails Depth-Anything [67] (0.042) and Depth-Anything-V2 [68] (0.043), reflecting that its design prioritizes temporal consistency over per-frame accuracy on static images.

## Relation to Prior Work

DepthCrafter repurposes the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) image-to-video generation model as a video-to-depth estimator, adapting its conditioning mechanism from single-image to frame-wise video conditioning and its VAE for depth sequences. This departs from prior video depth methods (NVDS, DeepV2D, MAMO) that rely on camera poses, optical flow, or test-time optimization, and from image depth methods (Depth-Anything, Marigold) that ignore temporal structure. The closest concurrent work, ChronoDepth, also uses video diffusion priors for depth but is limited to short fixed-length contexts, whereas DepthCrafter's three-stage training strategy enables variable-length sequences up to 110 frames and a segment-wise inference strategy for arbitrarily long videos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for camera poses or optical flow, DepthCrafter makes high-quality temporally consistent depth estimation practical for truly open-world videos, which is a significant step for downstream applications like AR/VR, video editing, and depth-conditioned generation. The three-stage training recipe (spatial layers on realistic data, temporal layers on long sequences, spatial layers on synthetic data) is a reusable strategy for adapting pretrained diffusion models to new modalities. Key limitations include high memory consumption (~24 GB for 110 frames at 1024×576) and inference speed (~466 ms/frame), leaving open whether distillation or quantization can bring this to real-time or mobile deployment. An open question is whether the same framework generalizes to other dense video prediction tasks (e.g., optical flow, surface normals) with similar three-stage adaptation.
