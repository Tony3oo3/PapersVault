## Problem

Training time-sensitive video LLMs for temporal grounding and dense captioning is bottlenecked by the scarcity of long videos with precise timestamp annotations—existing datasets like TimeIT (~125K) and VTG-IT (~120K) are an order of magnitude smaller than general video instruction data, and their captions lack the richness of high-quality image datasets. The paper asks whether sequential knowledge from abundant image and short-clip sequences can be transferred to enhance temporal understanding in long videos without requiring extensive manual timestamp annotation.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives 96 uniformly sampled frames from a long video plus a text query (an event description for grounding or a request for dense captioning) and outputs either a temporal interval [start, end] or a sequence of timestamped event captions. During training, inputs are either 96-image sequences (from LLaVA-ReCap) or 2–10 short Kinetics-700 clips with LongVA-generated captions, where temporal annotations are derived entirely from sequence positions rather than manual labeling. Positions are encoded as 4-digit normalized decimal tokens (e.g., the 7th image in a 96-image sequence becomes <0><7><2><9>), requiring only 10 new vocabulary tokens.

## Method

Seq2Time is a data-oriented training paradigm applied on top of TimeChat's dual Q-Former architecture, introducing no new model components. It constructs two complementary self-supervised data streams: (1) image sequence data with three pretext tasks—Image Index Grounding, Indexed Image Captioning, and Adjacent Location Reasoning—designed to force the LLM to link visual content to its sequential position, and (2) clip sequence data where multiple Kinetics-700 clips are concatenated with LongVA captions to simulate multi-event long videos. The key enabling mechanism is the Unified Relative Position Token (RPT), which normalizes any position i in a sequence of length L to a 4-digit decimal (round(i/L, 4)) and encodes each digit as a learnable embedding, creating a shared positional vocabulary across image indices, clip positions, and video frame timestamps. Training proceeds in two phases: one epoch over the full mixed dataset (300K image-sequence + 100K clip-sequence + 110K TimeIT + 133K general video captions) followed by three epochs of fine-tuning on TimeIT and Valley only, using LoRA (rank 32) with 96 frames per video.

## Results

On YouCook2 (dense video captioning), Seq2Time achieves CIDEr 4.2 (+44.8%), F1 16.2 (+27.6%), SODA_c 1.3 (+30.0%), and Meteor 1.3 (+18.2%) over the TimeChat baseline. On Charades-STA (temporal video grounding), it reaches R@1(IoU=0.5) of 31.2 (+14.7%) and R@1(IoU=0.7) of 13.7 (+17.1%). These gains are achieved using largely self-supervised sequence data (300K image sequences + 100K clip sequences) rather than additional manually annotated timestamps, and the unified relative position token (RPT) contributes the largest single-component improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seq2Time consistently outperforms the TimeChat baseline across all six metrics: +44.8% CIDEr, +27.6% F1, +30.0% SODA_c on YouCook2, and +14.7% / +17.1% R@1 on Charades-STA. Against VTimeLLM (CVPR 2024), Seq2Time leads by 3.7 points on R@1(IoU=0.5) and 2.3 points on R@1(IoU=0.7) on Charades-STA. Against Momentor (arXiv 2024), the margins are even larger: +4.6 on R@1(IoU=0.5) and +2.1 on R@1(IoU=0.7). Notably, Seq2Time achieves these gains without any architectural modification to TimeChat's dual Q-Former design—improvements come entirely from the data-oriented training paradigm and the unified relative position token. A cross-model validation on VideoLLaMA (a general video LLM without prior temporal training) confirms that image sequence data alone lifts F1 from 0.2 to 3.3, demonstrating that the sequential knowledge transfer is not specific to TimeChat's architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

Seq2Time builds on TimeChat's dual Q-Former architecture and training protocol but fundamentally departs from the architectural-innovation paradigm of prior time-sensitive MLLMs (VTimeLLM, Momentor, VTG-LLM, HawkEye) by taking a data-oriented approach instead. Rather than modifying how timestamps are encoded or how temporal attention is computed, it reframes the problem as sequential knowledge transfer: image indices and clip positions in self-constructed sequences serve as proxies for video timestamps. The unified relative position token (10 learnable digits) is a lightweight bridge that unifies these proxies with actual video timestamps in the LLM embedding space, a representation strategy distinct from both free-form language timestamps (TimeChat) and absolute-time special tokens (VTG-LLM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in temporal video understanding: the scarcity and cost of timestamp-annotated long-video data. The finding that static image sequences with index-caption correspondence can bootstrap temporal grounding (F1 jumping from 0.2 to 3.3 on VideoLLaMA with no video data) suggests a broader principle that sequential structure, not temporal continuity per se, is what MLLMs need to learn. However, limitations remain: the approach is validated only on TimeChat as a base architecture, object recognition errors and hallucinations persist in qualitative analysis, and the baseline reproduction uses partial training data, making the magnitude of gains somewhat uncertain. Open questions include whether the paradigm transfers to other video LLM backbones, whether it scales to longer/more complex videos, and whether the LongVA caption quality ceiling can be broken.
