## Problem

Existing diffusion-based point cloud generators produce unlabeled shapes, and the only prior method capable of generating segmentation-labeled point clouds (DiffFacto) does so by generating each part independently, resulting in limited inter-part coherence. Additionally, standard evaluation metrics (Chamfer distance, EMD) ignore semantic segmentation, making it impossible to properly assess the quality of generated labeled point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252749/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes random Gaussian noise as input and produces a point cloud of n points in R^{n×3} with point-wise part segmentation labels from c semantic classes. For part-aware editing, the input is an existing point cloud with a designated part to preserve; the model freezes the latent points of that part and applies a partial diffusion-denoise cycle (τ < T steps) to the remaining parts, outputting a modified shape where the preserved part remains geometrically unchanged while other parts exhibit novel deformations.

## Method

SeaLion extends Lion's hierarchical latent diffusion (global latent z₀ + point-wise latent features h₀) by incorporating segmentation encodings y as conditional inputs to both the point-level encoder and decoder, and by training the point-level diffusion U-Net to jointly predict denoising noise and segmentation labels at every diffusion step. The U-Net uses a single down-sampling path to extract shared representations, then splits into two parallel up-sampling branches—one for noise prediction and one for segmentation prediction—allowing the model to acquire semantic part awareness from intermediate features. Training proceeds in two stages: first a conditional VAE is trained via ELBO, then the global and point-level diffusion modules are trained with a combined loss of noise prediction MSE and segmentation cross-entropy. At inference, the global diffusion generates z₀, the point-level diffusion iteratively denoises h₀ while progressively refining the segmentation prediction via EMA smoothing, and the conditional decoder produces the final point cloud aligned with the predicted labels. The paper also introduces part-aware Chamfer distance (p-CD), which computes pairwise distance by summing per-part Chamfer terms, enabling 1-NNA and COV to capture both local structural quality and inter-part coherence.

## Results

On ShapeNet (six categories), SeaLion outperforms DiffFacto by an average of 13.33% on 1-NNA(p-CD), 11.61% on COV(p-CD), and 10.60% on MMD(p-CD) across the four shared categories (airplane, car, chair, lamp). On the real-world medical IntrA dataset, SeaLion achieves 65.22% 1-NNA(p-CD) versus DiffFacto's 71.74% (lower is better), a 6.52% improvement, while also surpassing DiffFacto by 21.74% on COV(p-CD) and 8.45% on MMD(p-CD). SeaLion also outperforms the two-step baseline (Lion + SPoTr segmentation) on all metrics, confirming that joint generation of geometry and labels yields better inter-part coherence than post-hoc label assignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeaLion is compared against two baselines: DiffFacto (part-wise diffusion with cross-attention assembly) and a two-step approach combining Lion (state-of-the-art unlabeled point cloud generation) with either PointNet++ or SPoTr (pretrained segmentation). On ShapeNet, SeaLion decisively outperforms DiffFacto on all three p-CD-based metrics across the four shared categories, with the largest gap on 1-NNA(p-CD) (e.g., 65.40% vs. 81.67% for airplane, a 16.27-point improvement). The two-step Lion+SPoTr baseline also loses to SeaLion on every metric, confirming that jointly predicting geometry and segmentation during diffusion produces more coherent part-to-part alignment than generating unlabeled shapes and assigning labels post-hoc. On the IntrA medical dataset, SeaLion again beats DiffFacto on all metrics (65.22% vs. 71.74% 1-NNA(p-CD); 60.87% vs. 39.13% COV(p-CD)), with the COV gap indicating substantially greater diversity in generated aneurysm shapes. The paper also notes that DiffFacto's scores drop noticeably when evaluated with the proposed p-CD metric versus its own intra-part/inter-part metrics, revealing that DiffFacto's part-wise generation produces implausible inter-part coherence that standard per-part metrics fail to capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeaLion extends the hierarchical latent diffusion paradigm of Lion by incorporating segmentation encodings as conditional information in both the point-level encoder/decoder and the diffusion module, enabling joint prediction of noise and part labels during denoising. It departs from DiffFacto's factorized approach (separate DDPMs per part plus pose assembly) by simultaneously diffusing latent points of all parts, which the authors argue yields stronger inter-part coherence. The design draws on the insight from Baranchuk et al. that intermediate activations of diffusion models encode semantic information useful for segmentation, repurposing this for a generative setting. The proposed part-aware Chamfer distance (p-CD) metric addresses a gap in evaluation: existing distances (CD, EMD) ignore segmentation, and DiffFacto's per-part averaging fails to capture whole-shape coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap in 3D generation by producing point clouds with accurate semantic labels in a single forward pass, which is directly useful for data augmentation of segmentation models (especially in label-scarce medical domains) and for part-aware shape editing. The p-CD metric is a broadly applicable contribution that could standardize evaluation of labeled point cloud generation beyond this specific model. However, the medical evaluation rests on a very small dataset (116 aneurysm segments), limiting confidence in real-world generalizability, and the ShapeNet experiments cover only six categories. Open questions include whether the joint-diffusion advantage over part-wise generation persists at larger scale or with more complex part hierarchies, and whether the semi-supervised gains hold when the labeled fraction drops below 10%.
