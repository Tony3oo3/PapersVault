## Problem

Existing text-driven style transfer methods suffer from three interrelated failures: style overfitting (generated images replicate dominant colors/patterns from the reference rather than following the text prompt), inability to selectively transfer individual style elements when a reference contains multiple (e.g., cartoon + nighttime), and layout instability artifacts such as checkerboard patterns. The root cause in adapter-based methods is the simple weighted-sum fusion of text and image cross-attention features, which forces both modalities into the same role and creates unresolvable conflicts without a tunable hyperparameter.

## Input / Output

The method takes a style reference image and a text prompt, operating on top of the CSGO adapter built on SDXL (1024×1024 latent diffusion). For the Style-Based CFG component, an additional negative style image is required, generated via ControlNet to preserve the reference's structure while omitting the target style element. The output is a single stylized image that adheres to the text prompt's semantic content while incorporating the desired style characteristics. The entire pipeline is training-free and can be plugged into existing adapter-based frameworks without fine-tuning.

## Method

Three complementary, training-free mechanisms are introduced on top of CSGO. First, Cross-Modal AdaIN replaces the weighted-sum fusion of text and style cross-attention features: the text-queried feature map is normalized by the style-queried feature map's mean and standard deviation (γ_style, β_style), then added back to the U-Net features in a residual connection, eliminating the need for a balancing hyperparameter. Second, Style-Based CFG (SCFG) generates a negative style image via ControlNet that retains layout but omits the target style, then modifies the noise prediction as (1+w)·ε_cond − w·ε_neg to selectively emphasize the desired style element while suppressing others. Third, a Teacher Model (the original SDXL without the style adapter) runs in parallel during early denoising timesteps, and its self-attention maps are selectively injected into the style model's U-Net across all layers to stabilize spatial layout and eliminate checkerboard artifacts; the teacher is discontinued after a chosen timestep to avoid diluting style details.

## Results

On a benchmark of 52 prompts and 20 style reference images, StyleStudio achieves the highest CLIP text-alignment score of 0.235, surpassing the closest baselines InstantStyle and DEADiff (both 0.229). In a 49-participant user study, the method receives 62.92% of votes for text alignment and 50.85% for style similarity, dramatically outperforming all baselines (next-best: CSGO at 7.99% text, InstantStyle/StyleCrafter at 8.67% style). The trade-off is inference time: StyleStudio takes 17 s per image versus 2–9 s for most adapter-based baselines, though it remains faster than StyleAlign (48 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyleStudio outperforms all seven baselines on CLIP text-alignment, with the largest margins over StyleAlign (+0.055) and StyleCrafter (+0.046), and a narrower but consistent lead over the strongest competitors InstantStyle and DEADiff (+0.006). In the user study, the gap is far more pronounced: StyleStudio captures 62.92% of text-alignment votes versus a maximum of 7.99% (CSGO) for any baseline, and 50.85% of style-similarity votes versus 8.67% (InstantStyle, StyleCrafter). The method is slower than every baseline except StyleAlign (17 s vs. 2–9 s for adapter-based methods), a cost attributed to the Teacher Model's additional denoising pass in early timesteps. Against its direct base model CSGO, StyleStudio improves text alignment by 8.7% (0.235 vs. 0.216) while roughly doubling inference time (17 s vs. 9 s). No baseline matches StyleStudio on both text alignment and style similarity simultaneously; the closest on style votes (InstantStyle, 8.67%) still trails by over 42 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the adapter-based style transfer framework CSGO (which itself extends IP-Adapter's dual cross-attention design) and repurposes the classic AdaIN normalization from GAN-based style transfer as a drop-in replacement for the weighted-sum fusion of text and style features in the adapter. It further extends the classifier-free guidance paradigm from text conditioning to style conditioning (SCFG), using ControlNet to synthesize a 'negative style' image, and borrows the teacher-student attention-sharing idea from image editing literature to stabilize spatial layout. The overall contribution is a training-free, plug-and-play combination of these three mechanisms on top of an existing diffusion-based style transfer pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses three concrete and frequently encountered failure modes in text-driven style transfer—style overfitting, text misalignment, and checkerboard artifacts—with a unified, fine-tuning-free solution that can be layered onto existing adapter-based frameworks. However, the practical cost is notable: inference time roughly triples (17 s vs. 6 s for IP-Adapter), and generating the negative style image for SCFG currently requires manual effort and domain expertise, limiting fully automated deployment. Open questions include whether the negative-style image can be generated automatically, whether the teacher-model timestep window can be learned rather than hand-tuned, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the SDXL/CSGO architecture to other diffusion backbones.
