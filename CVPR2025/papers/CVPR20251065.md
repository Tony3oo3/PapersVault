## Problem

UHD image restoration is hindered by the ultra-high resolution and pixel density that make full-resolution inference computationally prohibitive, while the common downsampling-enhancement-upsampling paradigm inevitably loses critical high-frequency information. No prior work has systematically decomposed the restoration process by frequency components, leaving the relative importance of global, coarse-grained, and fine-grained information unaddressed in a structured way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251065/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded UHD RGB image (4K resolution) and produces a restored UHD image of the same resolution. Internally, the pipeline operates at three resolution scales: 1/8 for the zero-frequency stage, 1/4 for the low-frequency stage, and full resolution for the high-frequency stage, with progressive residual connections passing information forward. Training uses 512×512 patches with a batch size of 6, and the full model contains only 1.131M parameters, enabling full-resolution inference in 0.532 seconds on a 4K image.

## Method

ERR deconstructs UHD restoration into three progressive stages guided by DCT-based frequency analysis: (1) a Zero-Frequency Enhancer (ZFE) at 1/8 resolution that uses an Adaptive Average Pooling unit to extract global priors and a Global Perception Transformer Block (GPTB) with bi-branch gated modulation to learn global mapping; (2) a Low-Frequency Restorer (LFR) at 1/4 resolution built on Residue State Space Blocks (Mamba) to reconstruct coarse-grained content; and (3) a High-Frequency Refiner (HFR) at full resolution that applies a Frequency-Windowed KAN (FW-KAN), which partitions the DCT spectrum into windows and applies stacked GR-KAN layers within each window to refine textures while reducing memory cost. Each stage is supervised by a frequency-specific L1 regularization loss (zero-frequency, low-frequency, and high-frequency components respectively) in addition to standard L1 and SSIM reconstruction losses, and progressive residual connections between stages ensure cumulative quality improvement.

## Results

On UHD-LL, ERR achieves 27.57 dB PSNR, 0.932 SSIM, and 0.214 LPIPS with only 1.131M parameters, outperforming the strongest baselines Wave-Mamba (27.35 dB) and UHDFormer (27.11 dB). On 4K-Rain13k, ERR reaches 34.48 dB PSNR and 0.952 SSIM, surpassing UDR-Mixer (34.28 dB). On UHD-Haze and UHD-Blur, ERR achieves 25.12 dB and 29.72 dB PSNR respectively, exceeding UHDFormer by 2.53 dB and 0.90 dB. Inference time on 4K images is 0.532 s, the fastest among compared UHD methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERR outperforms all 20 baselines across all four UHD restoration tasks. On UHD-LL, ERR leads Wave-Mamba [95] by 0.22 dB PSNR and UHDFormer [49] by 0.46 dB, while also achieving the best SSIM (0.932 vs. 0.927 for UHDFormer). On 4K-Rain13k, ERR surpasses UDR-Mixer [1] by 0.20 dB PSNR and 0.001 SSIM, and outperforms UDR-S2Former [2] by 1.12 dB. On UHD-Haze, ERR achieves a substantial 2.53 dB PSNR gain over UHDFormer [49] (25.12 vs. 22.59) with comparable parameter count. On UHD-Blur, ERR leads UHDFormer by 0.90 dB PSNR (29.72 vs. 28.82). Notably, ERR uses only 1.131M parameters—fewer than Wave-Mamba (1.258M) and far fewer than Restormer (26.11M) or Uformer (20.62M)—while achieving the best inference speed (0.532 s on 4K) among all compared UHD methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant downsampling-enhancement-upsampling paradigm in UHD restoration (UHDFour, UHDformer, UDR-Mixer), arguing that resolution reduction inevitably discards critical information. It builds on frequency-domain restoration methods (Fourmer, DWT-based approaches) but introduces a structured progressive decomposition via DCT, splitting restoration into zero-frequency, low-frequency, and high-frequency stages. The high-frequency refiner adapts GR-KAN [64] with window partitioning in DCT space to form FW-KAN, while the low-frequency stage leverages Mamba-style state-space blocks (RSSB/VSSM) for efficient long-range modeling. The global perception transformer block (GPTB) with bi-branch gated modulation is a novel integration mechanism for injecting global priors into low-resolution features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive spectral decomposition provides a clean, interpretable framework for UHD restoration that aligns with signal-processing intuition, and the frequency-specific regularization losses offer a principled way to guide each sub-network toward its designated frequency band. The very low parameter count (1.131M) and fastest inference time (0.532s on 4K) among compared methods make this practically relevant for edge deployment. However, the paper acknowledges failure in extreme low-light scenarios, and it remains open whether the three-stage frequency decomposition generalizes beyond UHD to other high-resolution or multi-modal restoration settings. The individual components (Transformer, Mamba, KAN) are all established; the contribution is primarily in their structured composition and the frequency-regularized training strategy.
