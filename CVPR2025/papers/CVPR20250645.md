## Problem

Existing 3D scene reconstructions represent scenes as static collections of objects and cannot model the physical consequences of interactions. While recent work has begun to capture action-conditioned visual dynamics (e.g., deformation, articulation), the complementary problem of predicting the sound a hand action would produce in a scene remains unexplored, despite sound carrying rich information about material properties and contact dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250645/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of 3D hand poses (21 keypoints × 2 hands at 30 Hz) as the action signal, combined with rendered video frames from the Gaussian Splatting scene at 4 Hz — both a global view with projected hands and a local close-up view of the interaction region. The output is an 8-second mel-spectrogram (80 bins, 16 kHz) decoded to a waveform via a pre-trained BigVGAN vocoder. The temporal mismatch between 30 Hz hand poses and 4 Hz video frames is resolved by nearest-neighbor upsampling both to 31.25 Hz before conditioning the generator.

## Method

The approach builds on Frieren's rectified flow matching framework but introduces two critical modifications: replacing CAVP video features with CLIP embeddings (for better spatial consistency and material understanding) and explicitly conditioning on the 3D hand pose trajectory (to capture precise contact timing that low-frame-rate video cannot convey). The 3D scene is reconstructed via Gaussian Splatting (Splatfacto), hand poses are extracted with HaMeR and registered to the scene coordinate frame via COLMAP, and the conditioning vector is formed by elementwise-summing CLIP frame embeddings with linearly-projected, unit-normalized hand pose embeddings. Training is supervised in a pre-trained autoencoder's latent space over 8-second chunks, using 40 epochs with Adam at batch size 128; inference uses 26 rectified flow sampling steps with a 4.5 guidance scale. The dataset comprises 24 scenes and 9.1 hours of hand-scene interactions, with 3D-consistent data augmentation via novel-view synthesis from the Gaussian Splatting representation.

## Results

On the Hearing Hands dataset, the proposed method achieves the best raw audio similarity (STFT 0.50, Envelope 0.66) and the highest CLAP accuracy across all sub-metrics (28.09% overall, 50.50% action, 45.62% material), outperforming Frieren (STFT 0.74, Envelope 0.81, CLAP-all 23.94%) and RegNet (STFT 0.62, CLAP-all 1.08%). In the real-or-fake user study with 59 participants, the full model achieves a 47.18% misclassification rate (near the 50% random-guess ceiling), compared to 43.79% for Frieren. The model also attains the highest Inception Score (17.82) and lowest CDPAM distance (3.32×10⁻⁴), though Frieren edges it slightly on FID (56.66 vs. 59.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Frieren (a rectified-flow video-to-audio model) and RegNet (a non-generative spectrogram regressor). The proposed method outperforms Frieren on every metric except FID (59.02 vs. 56.66), with particularly large gains in CLAP action accuracy (50.50% vs. 41.73%) and CLAP material accuracy (45.62% vs. 42.55%), as well as raw audio similarity (STFT 0.50 vs. 0.74). Against RegNet, the proposed method is dramatically better on CLAP overall accuracy (28.09% vs. 1.08%) and Inception Score (17.82 vs. 5.73), though RegNet is competitive on STFT (0.62) and CDPAM (3.38). In the real-or-fake study, the full model fools 47.18% of participants (near random), beating Frieren's 43.79% by ~3.4 percentage points. The ablations confirm that CLIP features are critical for material accuracy (dropping from 45.62% to 31.80% without them) and hand poses are critical for action accuracy (dropping from 50.50% to 38.21% without them).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Frieren's rectified flow matching framework for video-to-audio generation, but departs from it in two key ways: replacing CAVP video encoders with CLIP features (for better material understanding) and explicitly conditioning on 3D hand pose trajectories (for precise action synchronization). It also extends the 3D scene reconstruction literature (Gaussian Splatting, PhysGaussian, VR-GS) by adding a sound modality, and generalizes object-level impact sound work (ObjectFolder) to full scene-level interactions parameterized by diverse hand motions rather than simple strikes. The combination of a 3D scene representation, 3D hand trajectories as actions, and a flow-matching audio generator is a novel architectural pipeline not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dataset and method to spatially align human hand actions with their resulting sounds in 3D scene reconstructions, opening a path toward interactive 3D environments where users can query arbitrary hand motions and hear the predicted result. The 47% real-or-fake misclassification rate (near the 50% chance level) suggests the approach is close to perceptual indistinguishability, which is practically significant for AR/VR and robotics. Key limitations include the assumption that scene objects do not deform or move during interaction, reliance on imperfect 3D hand detection, and evaluation restricted to single scenes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across scenes without retraining, how to handle deformable or articulated objects, and whether the sound model can be made fully action-agnostic (independent of the specific hand trajectory used during training).
