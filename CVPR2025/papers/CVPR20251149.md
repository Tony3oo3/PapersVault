## Problem

Monocular depth estimators produce high-quality single-frame depth but cannot maintain a consistent scale factor across video frames, causing flickering artifacts. Video diffusion approaches address temporal consistency but are training-expensive, limited to fixed-length clips, and output only scale-invariant depth without camera poses. The paper tackles this by combining a monocular depth estimator with DUSt3R to produce both temporally consistent metric depth and camera poses for dynamic videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251149/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video and the estimated depth maps from a pre-trained monocular depth estimator (Depth Pro or Depth Anything V2) as auxiliary input. The depth maps are unprojected into 3D point maps using camera intrinsics (predicted focal length for Depth Pro, fixed focal length for Depth Anything V2) and normalized per-axis to [-1,1]. The output consists of globally consistent depth maps, SE(3) camera poses, and dynamic point clouds for all frames; for videos exceeding 30 frames, a hierarchical global-local optimization is applied to keep memory under ~6 GB on a single 4090 GPU.

## Method

Align3R's key insight is a 'pre-combination' strategy: rather than using DUSt3R's pairwise point maps as post-hoc constraints to align monocular depth (which yields sub-optimal results), the monocular depth is injected directly into the DUSt3R architecture during fine-tuning. Specifically, estimated depth maps are unprojected to 3D point maps, encoded by a new ViT-based encoder, and the resulting multi-level features are added to the DUSt3R decoder via zero-convolution layers (ControlNet-inspired), preserving the original encoder's feature distribution. Only the DUSt3R decoder and the point map ViT are fine-tuned (encoder frozen) on five synthetic datasets (SceneFlow, VKITTI, TartanAir, Spring, PointOdyssey) with a depth-filtering loss that discards points beyond 400 m to prevent far-away regions from dominating training. At inference, the fine-tuned model predicts pairwise point maps for selected frame pairs, and a global alignment optimization (Eq. 1) jointly solves for consistent depth maps and camera poses, with a hierarchical keyframe-clip strategy for long sequences.

## Results

On the challenging outdoor Sintel dataset, Align3R (Depth Anything V2) achieves Abs Rel 0.253 and δ<1.25 0.681, outperforming MonST3R (0.335/0.586) and DUSt3R (0.422/0.542) by a large margin. On the PointOdyssey validation set, Align3R reaches Abs Rel 0.078 (δ<1.25 0.929), nearly halving the error of DepthCrafter (0.229) and beating MonST3R (0.089). For camera pose estimation on TUM dynamics (30 frames), Align3R achieves ATE 0.011, RTE 0.010, and RRE 0.321°, surpassing MonST3R (0.020/0.014/0.478°) on all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Align3R (Depth Anything V2) outperforms all joint depth-and-pose baselines (DUSt3R, MonST3R) on every depth metric across all five evaluation datasets, with the largest gains on Sintel (Abs Rel 0.253 vs. 0.335 for MonST3R) and PointOdyssey (0.078 vs. 0.089). Against video-diffusion baselines, Align3R beats DepthCrafter on Sintel (0.253 vs. 0.292), PointOdyssey (0.078 vs. 0.229), and TUM dynamics (0.109 vs. 0.176), while matching it on Bonn (both 0.075). Compared to single-frame estimators, global alignment in Align3R improves temporal consistency on challenging outdoor scenes (e.g., Sintel Abs Rel drops from 0.348 to 0.253 with Depth Anything V2) but can slightly lose fine details on simpler indoor scenes where Depth Pro alone already achieves 0.067 Abs Rel on Bonn. For camera pose, Align3R achieves the best RTE and RRE on all datasets and the best ATE on real-world TUM dynamics, Bonn, and ScanNetV2, though it is marginally worse than MonST3R on ATE for synthetic Sintel (0.163 vs. 0.111). Overall, Align3R consistently wins or ties on depth metrics and wins on most pose metrics, with the only notable loss being Sintel ATE against MonST3R.

## Relation to Prior Work

Align3R sits at the intersection of two recent threads: DUSt3R's pairwise point-map prediction with global alignment (originally for static scenes) and high-quality monocular depth estimators like Depth Pro and Depth Anything V2. It departs from video-diffusion-based depth generation (DepthCrafter, ChronoDepth) by replacing the heavy generative model with a lighter pairwise prediction objective, and from the original DUSt3R by extending it to dynamic scenes and injecting monocular depth features via a ControlNet-style zero-convolution pathway. The key conceptual departure from the concurrent MonST3R is the 'pre-combination' strategy: rather than using monocular depth as a post-hoc alignment constraint, Align3R feeds unprojected depth point maps into the DUSt3R decoder during fine-tuning, making the model explicitly aware of the scale and detail structure of the depth estimates it must reconcile.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing both temporally consistent metric depth and camera poses for dynamic videos, Align3R fills a gap that scale-invariant video-diffusion methods leave open, directly enabling downstream tasks like 4D reconstruction, dynamic novel-view synthesis, and 3D tracking that require metric geometry. The hierarchical global-local optimization is a practical contribution for long-sequence processing on consumer GPUs. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to very long videos (>1000 frames) without accumulated drift, whether the synthetic-to-real fine-tuning gap can be closed with self-supervised or in-the-wild data, and whether the monocular-depth-injection strategy transfers to other DUSt3R-style architectures beyond the ViT decoder.
