## Problem

Most text-to-image diffusion models rely on T5 or CLIP as text encoders, yet the impact of different text encoder choices on generation quality remains largely unexplored. With the rise of decoder-only LLMs, it is unclear whether these models can serve as effective text encoders and, if so, how their rich multi-layer representations should be extracted and utilized for image generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252041/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language text prompt as input, which is encoded by a frozen text encoder (ranging from 354M to 9B parameters) into per-token embeddings. These embeddings are projected to 1024 dimensions via a linear layer and injected into a U-Net through cross-attention blocks, producing a 256×256 RGB image. All models are evaluated on 1,600 compositional prompts from GenAI-Bench using VQAScore computed with GPT-4o, with classifier-free guidance fixed at 7.0.

## Method

The paper establishes a standardized training pipeline based on the Stable Diffusion v2 U-Net architecture, where only the text encoder is swapped across 27 experiments while all other components (autoencoder, training recipe, 46M LAION-Aesthetics subset, 800K iterations at 256×256, batch size 2048 on 32 A100s) remain fixed. The key technical finding is that the standard practice of using last-layer embeddings is sub-optimal for decoder-only LLMs; instead, layer-normalized averaging across all transformer layers yields substantially better text-image alignment, particularly on advanced visio-linguistic reasoning skills like negation, comparison, and differentiation. They systematically ablate embedding extraction strategies (last layer, single middle layer, raw averaging, layer-normalized averaging) across 12 text encoders including T5-XXL, CLIP-ViT-H/14, four open-source LLMs, and three fine-tuned embedding models, revealing that bge-Gemma2 with layer-normalized averaging achieves the best overall VQAScore of 0.789.

## Results

Using layer-normalized average embeddings (norm avg) across all layers, bge-Gemma2 (9B) achieves the best overall VQAScore of 0.789 on GenAI-Bench, outperforming the T5-XXL baseline (0.741) by 0.048. Mistral-7B with norm avg reaches 0.769, a 0.094 improvement over its own last-layer embedding (0.675). The norm avg strategy consistently outperforms both last-layer and simple averaging across all tested models, with the largest gains in advanced visio-linguistic reasoning skills such as Negation (0.712 vs. 0.599 for T5) and Comparison (0.776 vs. 0.717 for T5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the T5-XXL baseline (0.741 VQAScore, last layer), bge-Gemma2 with norm avg wins by 0.048 overall and by 0.113 on Negation (0.712 vs. 0.599) and 0.059 on Comparison (0.776 vs. 0.717). Mistral-7B with norm avg (0.769) also surpasses T5-XXL by 0.028, while Mistral-7B with last-layer embeddings (0.675) underperforms T5 by 0.066, confirming that the standard last-layer approach is suboptimal for decoder-only LLMs. CLIP-ViT-H/14 (0.622) is the weakest baseline, trailing all LLM-based models by at least 0.053. Among fine-tuned embedding models, bge-Gemma2 (0.737 last layer) outperforms sfr-Mistral (0.710) and gte-Qwen2 (0.482), with the latter showing severe degradation likely due to its pooling-based training objective. Scaling within the same architecture (Qwen2 1.5B→7B, Gemma2 2B→9B) yields consistent but non-uniform gains, with Negation and Counting showing minimal improvement while Universality and Scene improve substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion v2 latent diffusion architecture and the established practice of using pre-trained text encoders (CLIP, T5) for text-to-image conditioning, as pioneered by DALLE2, Imagen, and eDiff-I. It departs from concurrent work (Playground-v3, Lumina-T2X, Sana) that began incorporating decoder-only LLMs as text encoders but used proprietary datasets and heterogeneous training setups, making it impossible to isolate the text encoder's contribution. The key conceptual departure is the finding that the de facto standard of extracting last-layer embeddings—universal in prior text-to-image models—does not transfer to LLMs, motivating the layer-normalized averaging strategy inspired by LLM interpretability literature on layer-wise semantic specialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that a simple layer-normalized averaging of all LLM layers substantially outperforms the standard last-layer approach and even surpasses T5-XXL—provides an immediately actionable recipe for practitioners integrating LLMs into diffusion pipelines. However, the study is confined to the SD2 U-Net architecture at 256×256 resolution, leaving open whether these findings generalize to newer DiT-based or flow-matching architectures that are now dominant. The paper also highlights that scaling LLM size does not uniformly improve all compositional skills (e.g., negation, counting), suggesting that hybrid or skill-specific approaches may be more efficient than brute-force scaling, a direction left for future work.
