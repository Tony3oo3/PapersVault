## Problem

Real image dehazing faces a fundamental tension: efficiency-oriented methods achieve real-time processing but lack scene adaptability, while adaptability-focused techniques dynamically adjust strategies at the cost of high computational complexity that hinders deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. No existing method successfully integrates both properties, leaving a gap between synthetic-domain performance and practical real-world utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252818/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image at arbitrary resolution (tested up to 2560×1440) and outputs a dehazed RGB image. Training operates on 256×256 random crops with standard augmentations (rotations, horizontal flips). The final compressed model is extremely lightweight at 1.69M parameters and 2.67G FLOPs, enabling inference in 24.33 ms at 1280×720 and 92.13 ms at 2560×1440 on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

CoA follows a divide-and-conquer strategy with two phases. In the MoC (Model Compression) phase, a teacher-student knowledge distillation is performed in the synthetic domain using a composite loss combining supervised losses (L1, SSIM, perceptual) with a layer-wise context alignment loss that matches teacher and student encoder feature maps at corresponding positions. In the BiA (Bilevel Adaptation) phase, a cross-domain bilevel optimization is formulated: the lower level updates the student model on unlabeled real data using a CLIP-based contrastive loss (trained text prompts distinguish hazy from clear images), while the upper level updates the real-domain parameters via exponential moving average (EMA) to balance synthetic and real domain knowledge. The architecture pairs a pretrained Res2Net encoder with an MSBDN decoder, and the approach is model-agnostic, reducing parameters by 73–94% across three different teacher models.

## Results

On three real-world benchmarks (RTTS, URHI, Fattal), CoA achieves the best FADE (0.8594, 0.9272, 0.3142), PM2.5 (88.87, 98.79, 91.64), Entropy (7.5798, 7.5928, 7.5858), and BIQME (0.5932, 0.5961, 0.6189) scores, outperforming all compared multi-weather and dehazing methods. In terms of efficiency, CoA uses only 1.69M parameters and 2.67G FLOPs, reducing model size by up to 94% compared to baselines like Dehamer (132.40M) and KANet (55.25M), while achieving the fastest inference at 2560×1440 resolution (92.13 ms vs. 10631 ms for KANet). The method also demonstrates stability across synthetic domains (RESIDE, Haze4K, THaze) and flexibility with different teacher models (MSBDN, DehazeFormer, DEA), improving average metrics by 14.2% across four evaluation criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoA decisively outperforms all 18 compared baselines across every evaluated metric and scene type. Against dedicated dehazing methods (SGID, Dehamer, C2P, KANet, DEA, D4, D4+, RIDCP), CoA achieves the lowest FADE and PM2.5 while attaining the highest Entropy and BIQME on all three real-world benchmarks, and simultaneously reduces parameter count by 54%–97% (e.g., 1.69M vs. Dehamer's 132.40M and KANet's 55.25M). Against multi-weather restorers (AirNet, WeatherDiff, DiffUIR), CoA improves FADE by 35–64% and PM2.5 by 42–54% on RTTS, with similar margins on URHI and Fattal. For nighttime haze (NHRW), CoA surpasses GS, MRP, OSFD, and GAPSF by effectively handling overexposure and low-light noise that cause distortion in those methods. For underwater scenes, CoA outperforms DM, SUIR, and SMDR in both UIQM and CCF, correcting color distortion that DM fails to address. The efficiency advantage is most dramatic at high resolution: at 2560×1440, CoA processes in 92.13 ms versus 10631 ms for KANet and 4409 ms for C2P, while RIDCP fails entirely due to memory constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying two previously separate research threads in image dehazing—model compression (knowledge distillation) and domain adaptation—into a single divide-and-conquer pipeline. Rather than building on a specific prior dehazing architecture, it repurposes bilevel optimization (originally from hyperparameter optimization) and CLIP-based contrastive losses to bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap, while using a standard teacher-student distillation framework for efficiency. The approach is explicitly model-agnostic, demonstrated across Res2Net+MSBDN, DehazeFormer, and DEA backbones, positioning CoA as a plug-in learning strategy rather than a new network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical tension between edge-deployment efficiency and scene adaptability in real-world dehazing is addressed here with a concrete, reproducible recipe (73–94% parameter reduction while improving no-reference quality metrics across three real benchmarks). The bilevel optimization framing for domain adaptation—where the lower level refines synthetic-domain parameters on real data and the upper level tracks them via EMA—offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc fine-tuning that could transfer to other low-level vision tasks. A noted limitation is failure on remote-sensing haze with non-uniform spectral occlusion, and the reliance on CLIP for the real-domain loss introduces a dependency on a large pretrained model that may limit the 'lightweight' narrative in deployment.
