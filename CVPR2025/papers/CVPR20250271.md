## Problem

Deploying multimodal large language models on mobile phones is hindered by limited memory and NPU computational capacity, while mainstream dynamic resolution schemes (LLaVA-NeXT, InternVL 1.5) exacerbate the problem by excessively enlarging images and generating long image token sequences. Prior mobile MLLM efforts also lacked detailed hardware-aware system optimization for NPU deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250271/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at dynamic resolutions (e.g., 768×1536 for phone screens, split into 9 patches for a 2:4 aspect ratio) alongside text or audio instructions (audio is transcribed to text before processing). After the token downsampler, each 384×384 patch yields 196 tokens instead of 729, capping the 2:4 case at 1764 image tokens. The model generates text autoregressively at 24.4 token/s on the Dimensity 9300 NPU, with optional on-the-fly conversion to audio output.

## Method

BlueLM-V-3B combines a SigLIP-400M ViT, a 2-layer MLP projector, and a 2.7B in-house BlueLM LLM in a LLaVA-style architecture, with two key algorithmic innovations: (1) a relaxed aspect ratio matching method that introduces a threshold α to prevent the dynamic resolution processor from always selecting larger-than-original resolutions, reducing image tokens without sacrificing accuracy; and (2) a VILA-style token downsampler that fuses 2×2 neighboring tokens to cut per-patch tokens from 729 to 196. On the system side, the paper designs batched NPU patch encoding (4 patches in parallel), pipeline parallelism between the CPU-side Conv2D embedding layer and NPU-side ViT blocks, chunked prefilling of 128 input tokens per iteration to match NPU throughput, and mixed-precision quantization (INT4 LLM weights, INT8 ViT/projector weights, INT16/FP16 activations, INT8 KV cache). Image encoding is decoupled from instruction processing so the ViT runs in parallel with user input, limiting peak memory to 2.2 GB.

## Results

On the OpenCompass benchmark (8 tasks), BlueLM-V-3B (3B params) achieves an average score of 66.1, ranking second overall and outperforming all 8B+ models except Qwen2-VL-8B (67.0). It leads on 4 of 8 individual tasks: MMBench (82.7), MMStar (62.3), AI2D (85.3), and MMVet (61.8). On OCR/text-centric benchmarks, BlueLM-V-3B scores 78.4 on TextVQA, 87.8 on DocVQA, and 32.7 on MTVQA (multilingual), where it substantially outperforms all baselines. In deployment on MediaTek Dimensity 9300 NPU, it achieves 24.4 token/s throughput with only 2.2 GB peak memory, compared to 4.9 token/s for the 8B MiniCPM-V on CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2-VL-8B, BlueLM-V-3B wins on MMBench (82.7 vs 81.0), MMStar (62.3 vs 60.7), and AI2D (85.3 vs 83.0), ties on MMVet (61.8), but trails on MMMU (45.1 vs 53.7), MathVista (60.8 vs 61.4), HallusionBench (48.0 vs 50.4), and OCRBench (829 vs 843). Against MiniCPM-V-2.6 (8B), BlueLM-V-3B wins on MMBench (82.7 vs 78.0), MMStar (62.3 vs 57.5), AI2D (85.3 vs 82.1), and MMVet (61.8 vs 60.0), while losing on MMMU (45.1 vs 49.8) and OCRBench (829 vs 852). Against InternVL2-8B, BlueLM-V-3B leads on MMBench (82.7 vs 79.4), MMStar (62.3 vs 61.5), AI2D (85.3 vs 83.6), HallusionBench (48.0 vs 45.0), and MMVet (61.8 vs 54.3), but loses on MMMU (45.1 vs 51.2) and OCRBench (829 vs 794 is actually a win; correction: 829 > 794, so it wins OCRBench too). On the multilingual MTVQA benchmark, BlueLM-V-3B (32.7) substantially outperforms Qwen2-VL-2B (20.7), InternVL2-4B (15.5), and MiniCPM-V-2 (9.3). In deployment, BlueLM-V-3B on NPU achieves 24.4 token/s versus 4.9 token/s for MiniCPM-V-2.5 (8B) on CPU, with 5x lower prefilling latency (2.7s vs 13.9s) and 1.6x faster image encoding (2.53s vs 4.0s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA architecture (ViT + MLP projector + LLM) and the dynamic resolution strategies introduced by LLaVA-NeXT and InternVL 1.5, but identifies a specific flaw in both: their aspect ratio selection tends to over-enlarge images, inflating token counts without adding useful information. The key algorithmic departure is the relaxed aspect ratio matching method, which introduces a threshold parameter to prefer smaller resolutions when the quality loss is bounded. On the systems side, unlike prior mobile MLLM efforts (MiniCPM-V, MobileVLM) that primarily focus on deploying a pre-trained model, BlueLM-V-3B performs joint algorithm-system co-design, incorporating NPU-specific optimizations (batched patch encoding, pipeline parallelism, chunked prefilling, mixed-precision quantization) that are tightly coupled to the model's token structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that careful algorithm-system co-design can make a 3B-parameter MLLM outperform 8B-parameter competitors on mobile hardware, with 5× higher token throughput and lower memory footprint (2.2 GB vs. ~4.5 GB for a 7B model). The finding that reducing image enlargement actually improves OCR and VQA accuracy challenges the prevailing assumption in dynamic-resolution MLLMs that higher resolution is always beneficial. Open questions include whether the relaxed aspect ratio matching generalizes to other ViT backbones and resolutions, whether the NPU-specific optimizations transfer to other mobile SoCs, and how the approach scales to video or multi-image inputs. The industry-driven nature (vivo AI Lab) means the deployment results are validated on a single device (Dimensity 9300), leaving broader hardware generalizability untested.
