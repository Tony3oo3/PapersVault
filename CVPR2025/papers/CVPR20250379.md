## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) and its image-editing variant DDS produce blurry, over-saturated results because text-conditioned scores do not precisely point to the source image, causing accumulated gradient errors that corrupt the source's identity (structure, pose, background). Existing de-biasing techniques (DDS, CDS) remove some noise but lack an explicit mechanism to align the score with the source, so structural drift persists especially for large semantic edits like cat-to-pig.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source RGB image, a source text prompt describing it, and a target text prompt, all processed in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion (LDM) model. For 2D editing it outputs a single edited image; for 3D editing it outputs an editable NeRF whose rendered views are optimized. The source and target prompts need not share vocabulary, and the method requires no paired training data or fine-tuning of the diffusion backbone.

## Method

IDS introduces Fixed-Point Regularization (FPR), which iteratively updates the source latent z_t^src to minimize the Euclidean distance between the Tweedie posterior mean E[z^src | z_t^src] and the actual source image z^src, thereby self-correcting the text-conditioned score so it genuinely points to the source. After N iterations (typically 3), a guided noise ε* is extracted from the refined source latent via the forward-diffusion inversion formula, replacing the random Gaussian noise used in DDS. The IDS gradient is then computed as the difference between the CFG-augmented target score and the corrected source score, both evaluated at latents sharing this guided noise. This ensures the update direction preserves the source's structural identity while still following the target prompt, and the paper shows that IDS perfectly reconstructs the source from the edited result (unlike DDS), confirming the gradient is correctly aligned.

## Results

On the Cat-to-Others task (LAION 5B), IDS achieves the best IoU of 0.74 (cat2pig) and 0.71 (cat2squirrel) with the lowest LPIPS of 0.22 and 0.24, respectively, outperforming CDS (0.72/0.71 IoU, 0.25/0.26 LPIPS) and DDS (0.69/0.65 IoU, 0.28/0.30 LPIPS). On InstructPix2Pix, IDS attains the highest background PSNR of 29.25 dB and lowest LPIPS of 0.19, compared to CDS (27.35 dB, 0.21) and DDS (26.02 dB, 0.24). In the user study, IDS receives the highest preference rates across all three criteria: 43.83% for text alignment, 60.49% for structural preservation, and 51.67% for quality. For NeRF editing on LLFF, IDS achieves the best CLIP score (0.1626) and highest user preference rates (40.42% text, 48.23% preserving, 46.10% quality).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDS consistently outperforms all four baselines on LPIPS across every evaluation task, with the largest margins over P2P (0.42→0.22 on cat2pig) and PnP (0.52→0.22). On IoU, IDS leads CDS by 0.02 on cat2pig (0.74 vs 0.72) and ties CDS on cat2squirrel (both 0.71), while surpassing DDS by 0.05 and 0.06 respectively. On InstructPix2Pix background PSNR, IDS achieves 29.25 dB, a 1.9 dB improvement over CDS (27.35) and 3.2 dB over DDS (26.02). In the user study, IDS dominates with 43.83% text-alignment preference (vs. 20.30% DDS, 17.02% CDS) and 60.49% structural-preservation preference (vs. 16.72% CDS). For NeRF editing, IDS achieves the highest CLIP score (0.1626 vs. 0.1597 CDS, 0.1596 DDS) and leads all user preference categories. However, IDS shows slightly lower CLIP scores than DDS on complex translations (e.g., 0.26 vs 0.27 for cat2dog), reflecting its trade-off of prioritizing identity preservation over prompt fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Delta Denoising Score (DDS) framework, which itself extends Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) for 2D image editing by subtracting the source score from the target score to isolate text-aligned gradients. The key departure is the identification of a specific failure mode in DDS: the text-conditioned score ε_φ^src is not precisely aligned to the source image, causing accumulated directional errors that degrade structural identity. The proposed Fixed-Point Regularization (FPR) introduces a self-correction loop using Tweedie's posterior mean to iteratively refine the source latent before computing the editing gradient, a mechanism absent from prior score-distillation editing methods (SDS, DDS, CDS, PDS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis of why score-distillation editing methods lose structural identity (score misalignment rather than merely noisy gradients) and offers a lightweight, training-free correction that can be plugged into existing SDS/DDS pipelines. However, the method's reliance solely on source-side information during FPR limits its CLIP alignment with target prompts, and the per-iteration FPR overhead roughly doubles runtime compared to DDS. An open direction the authors identify is extending the score correction to incorporate target-prompt conditioning, which could close the gap between identity preservation and prompt fidelity for complex translations.
