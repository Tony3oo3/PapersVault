## Problem

Super-resolution is an ill-posed inverse problem where the desired output is the highest-quality sample from a distribution of plausible solutions, not the pixel-wise average. While human-annotated multi-ground-truth frameworks (e.g., HGGT) improve perceptual quality, manual annotation is tedious, coarse, and non-differentiable. This paper asks whether practical no-reference image quality assessment (NR-IQA) metrics can fully replace human raters for both GT selection and direct optimization in SR training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded low-resolution RGB image (under either a two-round Real-ESRGAN degradation or a single-round setting) and produces a high-resolution SR image. During training, each LR input is supervised by one of multiple enhanced ground-truth patches selected via IQA-weighted sampling rather than uniform random choice. Evaluation is performed on the HGGT Test-100 set using both full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, LPIPS-ST, DISTS) and no-reference perceptual metrics (MUSIQ, NIMA, Q-Align, TOPIQ).

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary mechanisms for incorporating a differentiable NR-IQA model (MUSIQ) into SR training. First, IQA-weighted multimodal GT sampling replaces uniform sampling over human-ranked positives: the best variant (AMO) samples a patch from each candidate GT at the same location, scores each with MUSIQ, and selects the argmax, enabling fine-grained patch-level quality discrimination without any human labels. Second, a LoRA-regularized fine-tuning step adds a negative MUSIQ score as a differentiable loss term (L̃ = L − λ_Q·Q(Î)), where LoRA constrains the trainable parameters to prevent the gradient descent from acting as an adversarial attack on the IQA network (which would otherwise produce structured artifacts). The combined AMO+FT scheme outperforms the human-annotated HGGT baseline (UPos) on all NR-IQA metrics while maintaining competitive mid-level perceptual distortion scores.

## Results

On HGGT Test-100, the proposed AMO+FT method (IQA-weighted argmax sampling + LoRA-regularized NR-IQA fine-tuning) achieves the best NR-IQA scores across both architectures: MUSIQ 70.81 / NIMA 5.29 / Q-Align 3.75 / TOPIQ 0.70 for SwinIR, and MUSIQ 71.67 / NIMA 5.35 / Q-Align 3.68 / TOPIQ 0.71 for Real-ESRGAN. These surpass the human-annotated UPos baseline (MUSIQ 66.39 / 65.93) by 4.4 and 5.7 points on MUSIQ respectively, while also improving LPIPS-ST by ~6% (SwinIR) and ~12% (Real-ESRGAN). The trade-off is a small drop in PSNR (~0.5 dB) and SSIM (~0.02) relative to UPos, consistent with the perception-distortion tradeoff. AMO+FT also reaches or exceeds the 'Gold Standard' upper bound (best GT score per quintuplet) on several NR metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is UPos from HGGT [12], which uses uniform sampling over human-ranked positive GTs. AMO+FT outperforms UPos on all four NR-IQA metrics (MUSIQ, NIMA, Q-Align, TOPIQ) and on LPIPS-ST for both SwinIR and Real-ESRGAN, despite requiring no human annotations. Specifically, SwinIR-AMO+FT improves MUSIQ by +4.42 (70.81 vs 66.39) and LPIPS-ST by ~6% (0.121 vs 0.129), while Real-ESRGAN-AMO+FT improves MUSIQ by +5.74 (71.67 vs 65.93) and LPIPS-ST by ~12% (0.169 vs 0.192). The cost is a modest drop in PSNR (~0.5 dB) and SSIM (~0.02–0.03), reflecting the perception-distortion tradeoff. Notably, AMO+FT also surpasses UPos+FT (which does use human labels) on SwinIR NR metrics, though Real-ESRGAN-UPos+FT retains a slight edge on MUSIQ (72.69 vs 71.67). Among the paper's own sampling variants, AMO consistently outperforms SMA and SMP, indicating that fine-grained patch-level IQA scoring is more effective than image-level softmax weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HGGT (Chen et al., CVPR 2023), which introduced multi-ground-truth SR training with human-ranked quality labels, and on the broader perceptual-SR literature (SRGAN, ESRGAN, SwinIR) that uses perceptual losses and GANs to move beyond pixel-wise fidelity. The key conceptual departure is repurposing NR-IQA models—previously used only as evaluation metrics—as training signals: first as a differentiable sampling weight over multiple GTs (replacing HGGT's uniform or human-filtered sampling), and second as a direct optimization objective regularized by LoRA to prevent adversarial artifacts. This connects to the growing trend of using reward models or human feedback in generative training (e.g., DPOK, ImageReward for text-to-image), but applies it to the SR setting with the added constraint of a known degradation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: if NR-IQA metrics can replace human annotations for GT selection and quality optimization, SR training becomes far more scalable and reproducible, removing a major bottleneck in multi-GT training pipelines. The finding that LoRA regularization is necessary to prevent IQA-optimization from degenerating into adversarial artifacts is a useful caution for anyone applying differentiable reward signals to image restoration. Open questions include whether SR-specific IQA models (rather than general-purpose ones like MUSIQ) would provide stronger learning signals, whether the approach transfers to other ill-posed restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring), and whether the perception-distortion tradeoff can be further navigated by combining multiple complementary IQA metrics as joint objectives.
