## Problem

SDS-based 3D editing methods (e.g., DDS, PDS) require hundreds of optimization steps (up to 540 minutes) because their stochastic timestep sampling causes persistent internal conflicts between editing and preservation, especially in high-noise phases. The paper asks whether these conflicts can be reduced by separating editing and preservation temporally rather than spatially, and whether the editing process can be reframed as a direct diffusion bridge from source to target distribution to shorten the optimization path.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250658/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a differentiable 3D scene representation (NeRF or 3DGS), a source text prompt describing the original scene, and a target text instruction specifying the desired edit. It renders multi-view images from the 3D model, applies a decreasing diffusion time schedule (high-to-low noise) over ~2,500 steps on a single GPU (~30 min), and updates the 3D parameters in-place. The output is the edited 3D scene in the same representation, with the editing process structured as coarse-to-fine stages where early high-noise steps constrain structure and later low-noise steps add textural detail.

## Method

DaCapo reformulates 3D editing as a Stacked Bridge: a sampling-version DDS with a decreasing time schedule is shown to be an implicit approximation of the Dual Diffusion Implicit Bridge (DDIB), where DDIM inversion and DDIM sampling run simultaneously rather than sequentially, directly bridging source and target distributions. Three improvements address the approximation errors of this stacked formulation: (1) Source-Target Coupling adds an alignment loss between noisy source and target renderings to re-couple the decoupled inversion and editing trajectories, with strong weighting in early preservation stages; (2) improved score estimation conditions the target denoiser on source images via InstructPix2Pix and uses the implicit classifier score from classifier-free guidance to focus updates on edit-relevant regions; (3) a Manifold Correction Gradient (MCG) subtracts the unconditional source score from the unconditional target score to pull drifted data points back onto the data manifold, preventing blurring in low-noise stages. The final editing ODE combines the classifier-score edit gradient, the alignment gradient, and the MCG with time-varying weights, updating 3D parameters directly via backpropagation through the differentiable renderer.

## Results

On 8 real scenes (56 source-target pairs) from InstructNeRF2NeRF and PDS, DaCapo achieves a CLIP Direction Consistency of 0.9240 (best among all methods) and a CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity of 0.2356, while completing each edit in only 35 minutes on a single GPU. This represents a 15× speed-up over the state-of-the-art PDS (540 min) with comparable text-alignment quality (PDS: 0.2477 similarity). DaCapo also outperforms the reconstruction-based baselines IN2N (0.2280) and Inv2N (0.2232) on both metrics, and substantially outperforms DDS (0.2210) on text alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DaCapo is compared against four baselines: two reconstruction-based methods (IN2N and Inv2N) and two optimization-based methods (DDS and PDS). Against the state-of-the-art PDS, DaCapo achieves a 15× speed-up (35 min vs. 540 min) while matching or exceeding its CLIP Direction Consistency (0.9240 vs. 0.9218), though PDS retains a slight edge in CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity (0.2477 vs. 0.2356). Against IN2N, DaCapo wins on both text alignment (0.2356 vs. 0.2280) and consistency (0.9240 vs. 0.919) while being 3× faster (35 vs. 110 min). Against Inv2N, DaCapo improves text alignment by 0.0124 and consistency by 0.0417, and is 2.9× faster. Against DDS, DaCapo substantially outperforms on both metrics (similarity +0.0146, consistency +0.1677) and is 4.3× faster, with DDS also suffering from multi-view inconsistency that causes crashes on complex outdoor scenes. Qualitatively, DaCapo produces more realistic edits with better background preservation compared to all baselines, particularly in cases involving human figures and outdoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DaCapo builds directly on the Delta Denoising Score (DDS) framework for 3D editing, which itself extends Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DreamFusion by subtracting a source-branch gradient to preserve background content. The key conceptual departure is reframing the DDS optimization with a structured decreasing time schedule as a 'Stacked Bridge' that implicitly approximates the Dual Diffusion Implicit Bridge (DDIB) from the image-translation literature, thereby importing the theoretical guarantees of Schrödinger bridges (unique mapping, implicit optimal transport) into the 3D editing setting. On top of this reformulation, DaCapo adds source-target coupling (an alignment loss between noisy source and target renderings) and a Manifold Correction Gradient to address three identified approximation errors, while using InstructPix2Pix as a conditioned denoiser for more accurate target score estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× speedup (540 min → 35 min per edit) makes text-driven 3D scene editing substantially more practical for real-world pipelines in VR, film, and design, where prior SDS-based methods were prohibitively slow. The theoretical unification of DDS with DDIB provides a principled lens for understanding why random timestep sampling causes internal conflicts (editing vs. preservation) and how temporal separation resolves them, which could inform future 3D generation and editing methods beyond text-driven editing. A notable limitation is the hard dependency on InstructPix2Pix for the conditioned denoiser, meaning DaCapo inherits its failure modes on certain edit types; open questions include whether the stacked bridge framework generalizes to multi-object or structural edits and whether the InstructPix2Pix dependency can be replaced with a more flexible conditioning mechanism.
