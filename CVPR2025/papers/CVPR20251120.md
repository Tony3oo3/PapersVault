## Problem

Existing implicit video representations model a static canonical image plus a deformation field, which fails when objects exhibit temporally varying appearance (specular highlights, shadows, lighting changes) or when large motions and occlusions distort the canonical space. This makes semantics-aware, temporally consistent video editing unreliable in-the-wild, particularly for talking-head videos with hands, objects, or lighting changes occluding the face.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251120/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video clip (≥50 consecutive frames at 512×288 or 432×768) and a coarse foreground-background mask (extracted via SAM 2 and eroded up to 11 pixels to simulate imperfect segmentation). It produces, per object layer (foreground occluder and background face), a temporal canonical base color (albedo) image, a spatial deformation spline field (N×2 control points per coordinate), a color deformation spline field (N×3 control points per coordinate), and a refined alpha matte. The number of spline control points is set to the number of frames (or half for smoother fitting), and the model is trained by sampling 10k random (u,v,t) coordinates per batch for up to 100k iterations on a single A10G GPU (~4 GB VRAM).

## Method

VideoSPatS replaces the single static canonical image + deformation field paradigm with two independent cubic Hermite spline deformation fields: a spatial field D_s that maps each coordinate to N×2 control points defining the motion trajectory, and a color field D_c that maps deformed coordinates to a base albedo color c_0 plus N×3 control points defining time-varying appearance (highlights, shadows). Both fields are parameterized by 8-layer MLPs (256 channels, positional encoding, skip connection) that output spline control points rather than raw displacements or colors, so the Hermite interpolation at time t inherently produces smooth, temporally consistent trajectories without explicit flow regularization. A separate alpha MLP refines the coarse mask via a four-term layer separation loss (guidance, opacity regularization, boundary smoothing, and a maximum-exclusion error term that forces the background layer to explain occluded pixels). Training combines reconstruction, optical-flow-guided correspondence, spatial spline smoothness and velocity-direction regularization, L2 color-deformation penalty, and the layer separation loss; editing is then performed on the base albedo c_0 and the color spline deformation is re-applied to propagate temporally varying appearance into the edited video.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative metric is warping consistency (measured via RAFT optical flow between edited and warped-and-edited frames), where VideoSPatS outperforms CoDeF by 4.66 dB and Deformable Sprites by 0.4 dB. Qualitatively, VideoSPatS produces more consistent and semantically coherent canonical spaces than CoDeF across all three datasets (YT-in-the-wild, DAVIS, CoDeF), particularly for fast-moving objects and scenes with complex occlusions. On DAVIS, CoDeF achieves slightly better raw reconstruction quality, but VideoSPatS generates a more editable canonical space, which the authors argue is the more critical property for downstream video editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoSPatS is compared against two baselines: CoDeF (Ouyang et al., CVPR 2024) and Deformable Sprites (Ye et al., CVPR 2022). On the warping consistency metric, VideoSPatS beats CoDeF by a substantial 4.66 dB margin and Deformable Sprites by 0.4 dB. Qualitatively, CoDeF struggles to maintain a consistent canonical space for fast-moving objects (visible as repeated/duplicated instances in Fig. 4) and produces distorted canonical images under complex motion, while VideoSPatS generates clean, semantically coherent canonical spaces even with multiple moving objects. On DAVIS, CoDeF achieves marginally better pixel-level reconstruction but fails to produce a semantically reasonable canonical space (e.g., the noisy black swan example), whereas VideoSPatS separates foreground and background more reliably. On the CoDeF dataset, both methods produce reasonable character canonical spaces, but only VideoSPatS implicitly reconstructs a clean background and keeps the foreground well-aligned with the input video. Overall, VideoSPatS wins on editability and canonical space quality across all comparisons, while CoDeF retains a slight edge in raw reconstruction fidelity on DAVIS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the canonical-space video editing paradigm established by CoDeF [19] and Layered Neural Atlases [10] by replacing their static canonical image with a temporally varying canonical space parameterized by color spline deformation fields. The core deformation mechanism is adapted from Neural Spline Fields (NSF) [5], which originally modeled motion for burst image fusion, here generalized to continuous video with both spatial and color spline fields. The key conceptual departure from prior work is that appearance is no longer assumed to be static: the color spline field captures time-dependent effects (highlights, shadows, specularities) that previous methods either conflated with motion or failed to model, leading to distorted canonical spaces under complex dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of a temporal canonical space addresses a fundamental limitation in video editing: static canonical images cannot represent objects whose appearance changes over time, making semantics-aware editing unreliable for in-the-wild content with lighting variations and occlusions. The spline-based formulation also enables direct motion editing by modifying control points, a capability absent in hash-based or raw neural deformation approaches. However, the method remains constrained by the extent of deformation and self-occlusions in input videos, and the quantitative evaluation is limited to a single warping-consistency metric on small-scale datasets (50 frames, 512×288), leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, higher resolutions, and more diverse content beyond talking-head videos.
