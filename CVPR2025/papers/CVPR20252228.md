## Problem

Existing mask-based 3D face editing methods (e.g., NeRFFaceEditing, IDE-3D) rely on pre-trained segmentation networks with a fixed set of segment labels, making it impossible to edit regions outside that predefined layout. Adapting to a new layout (e.g., eyelid or nasal ala control) requires collecting and segmenting an extensive dataset, which is impractical for per-session customization needs in medical imaging or creative editing.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a real RGB face image (inverted into the W+ latent space via pivotal tuning) and a user-edited segmentation mask in an arbitrary custom layout (e.g., 17-class Base, 19-class Eyes, or 19-class Nose) as input, and produces a 3D-consistent edited face image along with a corresponding segmentation mask volume. During the few-shot adaptation phase, only 10 annotated masks from randomly generated images are required to train the geometry adapter for a new layout. The mask layout can have an arbitrary number of classes, and the editing vector is optimized per-edit (~31 seconds) to match the target mask while preserving unedited regions.

## Method

FFaceNeRF builds on EG3D's tri-plane architecture and NeRFFaceEditing's disentangled appearance/geometry decoders, adding a lightweight MLP geometry adapter (Φ_geo) at the output of the geometry decoder to remap segmentation labels to a user-specified layout. To prevent information loss, the adapter receives direct feature injection of the normalized tri-plane feature and view direction, preserving fine geometric details that the original geometry decoder discards. To avoid overfitting with only 10 training samples, the method employs Latent Mixing for Triplane Augmentation (LMTA), which blends latent codes at the top 5 layers (layers 10–14, selected via mIoU analysis) to increase data diversity while retaining semantic structure. Training uses a two-stage loss (cross-entropy followed by DICE overlap loss) and freezes all components except Φ_geo (~40 min on an A6000). At inference, an overlap-based optimization (DICE + LPIPS) replaces the conventional probability-based approach, enabling faithful editing of small regions like pupils and nasal ala that cross-entropy alone fails to capture.

## Results

In a perceptual study with 21 participants, FFaceNeRF is preferred over NeRFFaceEditing in 72.29% of faithfulness judgments, 67.83% of retention judgments, and 68.68% of quality judgments, and over IDE-3D in 79.65%, 80.17%, and 81.22% respectively. On the quantitative mask generation task (22 Base-layout test sets, averaged over 5 trials), FFaceNeRF achieves 85.33% mIoU versus 81.37% for NeRFFaceEditing. The geometry adapter is trained with only 10 annotated masks in approximately 40 minutes on a single RTX A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FFaceNeRF is compared against two state-of-the-art mask-based 3D face editing methods: NeRFFaceEditing (its backbone) and IDE-3D. In the perceptual A/B study, FFaceNeRF is preferred over NeRFFaceEditing on all three criteria (faithfulness 72.29%, retention 67.83%, quality 68.68%) and over IDE-3D by a wider margin (79.65%, 80.17%, 81.22%). On the quantitative mask generation task, FFaceNeRF achieves 85.33% mIoU versus 81.37% for NeRFFaceEditing, a roughly 4-point improvement attributed to the geometry adapter with feature injection and LMTA augmentation. Qualitatively, the paper reports that NeRFFaceEditing only partially reflects the target mask, while IDE-3D frequently fails to produce correctly edited results and often loses the original identity. Both baselines were adapted via transfer learning (freezing all components except the geometry decoder) to ensure a fair few-shot comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

FFaceNeRF builds directly on EG3D's tri-plane architecture and NeRFFaceEditing's disentangled appearance/geometry decoders, inheriting their pretraining pipeline. It departs from prior mask-based 3D face editing methods (NeRFFaceEditing, IDE-3D, FENeRF) by introducing a lightweight geometry adapter with tri-plane feature injection that can be retrained on ~10 samples to produce arbitrary mask layouts, rather than being locked to a fixed pretrained segmentation scheme. The overlap-based (DICE) optimization at inference also departs from the conventional cross-entropy/probability-based editing losses used in prior work, specifically to handle small-region edits like pupils and nasal ala.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The few-shot adaptation capability addresses a practical bottleneck: medical and creative applications require session-specific mask layouts (e.g., eyelid detail for makeup, nasal ala for surgical previsualization) that cannot be covered by a single pretrained segmentation network. The demonstrated generalization to non-NeRF architectures (FFaceGAN) suggests the adapter + LMTA recipe is broadly applicable. However, the ~31-second per-edit inference time and limited one-shot performance remain significant barriers to interactive use, and the paper itself identifies training a lightweight mask encoder or integrating SAM-like models as key open directions.
