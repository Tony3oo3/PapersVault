## Problem

Existing VQ image tokenizers optimized for reconstruction fidelity capture low-level pixel details but lack the high-level semantic representations needed for multimodal understanding, while semantic tokenizers distilled from CLIP cannot reconstruct fine-grained visual details. This creates a fundamental trade-off that prevents a single tokenizer from serving both perception and generation tasks effectively, leaving a gap in unified vision-language architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251573/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer accepts RGB images at 224×224, 256×256, or 384×384 resolution (depending on the B/L/XL variant) and produces multi-scale discrete visual tokens via a shared-index mapping into codebooks of up to 131,072 entries. For generation, text prompts are encoded via BPE and concatenated with the visual tokens for autoregressive next-scale prediction, yielding 256×256 images in ~25 sampling steps. For understanding, the final-scale quantized features are projected through an MLP and fed into a pretrained LLM (Vicuna-13B or Qwen-2.5-14B) following the LLaVA-1.5 paradigm.

## Method

TokenFlow employs a dual-encoder architecture with a semantic encoder (initialized from CLIP ViT-B/14, ViTaminXL, or SigLIP-SO400M) and a pixel encoder, each paired with a dedicated codebook of size K. The key mechanism is a shared-index mapping: the quantization index is determined by minimizing the weighted sum of l2 distances to both codebooks (d_sem + w_dis · d_pix), so a single index simultaneously encodes semantic and pixel-level information. A multi-scale VQ (MSVQ) structure provides hierarchical token representations, and two separate decoders independently reconstruct semantic features (via L2 loss against a CLIP teacher) and the original image (via L2 + LPIPS + adversarial loss). For generation, the paper introduces a multi-step sampling strategy that progressively narrows top-k/top-p parameters across refinement passes within each scale, addressing the pattern inconsistency inherent in single-pass autoregressive sampling.

## Results

On multimodal understanding, TokenFlow-XL with Qwen-2.5-14B achieves a 67.4% average across 12 benchmarks, surpassing LLaVA-1.5 13B (62.9%) by 7.2% and EMU3 (60.9%) by 10.7%—the first time a discrete visual tokenizer outperforms a continuous CLIP-based baseline. For image reconstruction at 384×384, TokenFlow achieves an rFID of 0.63, substantially outperforming VAR (2.09) and LlamaGen (0.94). In autoregressive text-to-image generation at 256×256, TokenFlow reaches a GenEval score of 0.55 in only 25 sampling steps, matching SDXL (0.55) while requiring far fewer steps than EMU3 (4096 steps) or Chameleon (1024 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenFlow-XL (Qwen-2.5-14B) outperforms LLaVA-1.5 13B by 7.2% on average (67.4 vs. 62.9), winning on 10 of 12 individual benchmarks including SEEDBench (72.6 vs. 68.1), MMVet (48.2 vs. 36.1), and MMBench (76.8 vs. 67.7), while slightly trailing on VQAv2 (77.6 vs. 80.0) and GQA (62.5 vs. 63.3). Against EMU3 (8B, trained from scratch), TokenFlow-XL achieves a 10.7% average improvement (67.4 vs. 60.9) with far less training cost (<24h on 8×A100 vs. from-scratch training). In autoregressive generation, TokenFlow matches SDXL's GenEval score (0.55) in 25 steps versus SDXL's 40 diffusion steps, and surpasses EMU3 (0.54) and VAR (0.53) while using 163× fewer steps than EMU3 (25 vs. 4096). For reconstruction at 384×384, TokenFlow's rFID of 0.63 beats VAR (2.09) by 3.3× and LlamaGen (0.94) by 33%, though VAR still leads on PSNR (22.73 vs. 22.77 is actually TokenFlow winning) and SSIM (0.774 vs. 0.731). The paper also shows TokenFlow outperforms VILA-U on both reconstruction (rFID 0.63 vs. 1.25 at 384) and understanding, while Janus and Show-o trail significantly on understanding benchmarks due to their single-codebook or separate-encoder designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenFlow builds on the multi-scale VQ (MSVQ) structure and next-scale prediction paradigm from VAR [51], while drawing on CLIP-style encoders for semantic initialization and VQGAN-style adversarial/perceptual losses for pixel reconstruction. It departs from prior unified approaches (Janus, VILA-U, EMU3) that either employ separate encoders for understanding vs. generation or constrain both feature levels within a single codebook, by introducing a dual-codebook architecture with shared index mapping that decouples semantic and pixel-level learning while preserving cross-level alignment. The paper also extends the LLaVA-1.5 training pipeline to discrete visual inputs and adapts the Llama-2 autoregressive framework for next-scale image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TokenFlow demonstrates for the first time that a single discrete tokenizer can simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art reconstruction (FID 0.63 at 384×384), competitive autoregressive generation (GenEval 0.55 at 256×256 in only 25 steps), and understanding performance surpassing the continuous-input LLaVA-1.5 13B baseline by 7.2%, suggesting that the discrete-vs-continuous representation trade-off may be less fundamental than previously assumed. Open questions include whether the dual-codebook approach scales to video tokenization or higher resolutions (beyond 384×384), whether the shared mapping generalizes to other modalities (audio, 3D), and whether the 95%+ codebook utilization at 131K entries holds under different training regimes. The practical efficiency (2.7s per image on a single A100) makes this a potentially widely adopted tokenizer for unified vision-language systems.
