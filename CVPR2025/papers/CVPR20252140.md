## Problem

Diffusion models can memorize and reproduce specific training identities, raising privacy concerns under regulations like GDPR and CCPA. While GAN latent spaces support linear identity manipulation, diffusion models' multistep Markov-chain structure makes identity removal difficult—random or orthogonal traversals in the high-dimensional latent space lead to semantically unvalidated regions and unrealistic outputs. This paper is the first to explore identity unlearning directly within the h-space (bottleneck activations) of diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single face image of the target identity (256×256) and a frozen pre-trained iDDPM model as input. The image is inverted via diffusion inversion to obtain its latent code in the U-Net bottleneck layer (8×8×512 = 32,768 dimensions). The output is a modified diffusion model (unlearned version) that no longer generates the target identity while preserving other attributes; inference uses 40–100 denoising steps (5–10 s on an RTX-4090). Approximately 1,000+ sampled images are needed to establish the black hole boundary, and unlearning requires 1,500 optimization iterations over 1,000 timesteps.

## Method

BIA uses a 'black hole' metaphor: the target identity's latent region acts as a gravitational attractor that absorbs identity features from surrounding latent points. First, the source image is inverted to obtain h_r, then n neighboring latent codes are sampled and classified via ArcFace cosine similarity and a linear SVM to define a boundary direction d_id, forming the black hole region B_h. Next, k nearest latent points outside B_h (representing distinct identities) are identified as wrapping points. The model is fine-tuned by minimizing a wrapper loss (identity loss via ArcFace, perceptual loss, and L2 regularization) that pulls these neighbors toward the black hole, effectively absorbing identity-specific features. A semantic loss (L2 distance between original and identity-edited bottleneck features) is added to preserve non-identity attributes like expression, lighting, and background, yielding the total loss L_total = L_wrapper + λ_sem · L_sem.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ (256×256), BIA achieves the lowest identity similarity (ID) score of 0.01 (CircularFace) and the best perceptual quality with LPIPS of 0.03 and SSIM of 0.73, while retaining the highest attribute preservation scores: Gender 87.8%, Age 82.4%, and Expression 90.2%. In user studies with 100 participants, BIA achieves 86% ID dissimilarity and 98% attribute quality satisfaction. The FID score is 8.63, slightly higher than GUIDE (7.80) but substantially better than Random (12.20) and Baseline (10.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA GAN-based method GUIDE [53], BIA substantially outperforms on perceptual and attribute metrics: LPIPS drops from 0.08 to 0.03, SSIM rises from 0.50 to 0.73, and attribute preservation scores jump from a flat 50.0% (Gender/Age/Expression) to 87.8/82.4/90.2%, reflecting that GUIDE produces near-identical copies of the source image rather than diverse, realistic outputs. GUIDE retains a slight edge on FID (7.80 vs. 8.63) and achieves 100% ID dissimilarity in user studies versus BIA's 86%, though this comes at the cost of severely degraded visual diversity. Compared to the internal Baseline (wrapper loss only), adding the semantic loss L_sem improves ID removal (0.08→0.01 CircularFace), LPIPS (0.12→0.03), SSIM (0.62→0.73), and all three attribute scores by 4–9 points. The Random baseline, which wraps the black hole with arbitrary latent points, degrades heavily on LPIPS (0.37) and SSIM (0.56) with attribute scores 10–15 points lower than BIA. The BIA_closest variant (k=1) fails to remove identity effectively (ID 0.57–0.68) and drops ID dissimilarity to 50%, confirming that multiple diverse neighbors (k=5) are necessary for robust absorption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identity unlearning paradigm from GAN latent spaces (GUIDE, CVPR 2024) to the h-space (bottleneck activations) of diffusion models, building directly on Kwon et al.'s finding that diffusion models possess a semantic latent space amenable to linear manipulation. It departs from prior diffusion unlearning methods (MAC-E, Forget-Me-Not) that operate via gradient-based parameter editing or classifier-free guidance, instead performing all modifications in the frozen model's latent space through a wrapper loss that pulls k-nearest non-identity latent points toward a SVM-defined boundary. The 'black hole' framing is a conceptual reorganization of GUIDE's adjacent-latent-code mechanism, repurposed for the multi-step, less-structured latent geometry of diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first reported attempt at identity unlearning specifically within diffusion model h-space, addressing a practical gap as diffusion models become the dominant generative architecture and privacy regulations (GDPR, CCPA) demand 'right to be forgotten' capabilities. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (CelebA-HQ) at 256×256 resolution, single-identity removal, and the acknowledged risk that latent-space continuity may inadvertently affect nearby identities. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multiple simultaneous identities, scales to higher resolutions or text-to-image settings, and how it compares to encoder-based latent spaces (e.g., DiffAutoencoder) that may offer cleaner disentanglement.
