## Problem

Existing long video temporal grounding methods process every clip through a large pre-trained expert encoder, leading to prohibitive computational costs as video duration grows. Since the query-relevant moment typically constitutes only ~2% of a long video, the vast majority of clips are irrelevant yet still incur full-scale encoding cost, creating a fundamental scalability bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252019/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (8–294 minutes) partitioned into fixed-duration clips at 224×224 resolution via sliding window, along with a free-form text query. The video is split into T clips; the sidekick encoder processes all T clips while the expert encoder processes only the top-c% (30% or 50%) most salient clips. The output is a temporal moment (t_s, t_e) representing start and end timestamps, predicted via regression and classification heads followed by Soft-NMS to merge overlapping proposals.

## Method

DeCafNet employs a delegate-and-conquer strategy with a lightweight sidekick encoder (ViViT-based with 4× convolutional spatio-temporal pooling and temporal interpolation at stride τ=2) that extracts dense features for all clips at ~31× lower TFLOPs than the expert encoder, while simultaneously generating a saliency map via inner product with the text CLS token to select the top-c% most relevant clips for full expert-encoder processing. The sidekick encoder is trained with a contrastive saliency loss (aligning video-text features) and a distillation loss (matching expert encoder features). DeCaf-Grounder then unifies the two feature sets at different temporal resolutions through Query-aware Temporal Aggregation (zero-padding, concatenation with saliency scores, and video-text cross-attention) and Multi-Scale Temporal Refinement (an 8-scale temporal transformer pyramid synchronized via dilated temporal convolution), enabling accurate grounding while reducing total computation by up to 47%.

## Results

On Ego4D-NLQ, DeCafNet-50% achieves an average recall of 24.44 (R1@0.3=18.10, R1@0.5=12.55, R5@0.3=38.85, R5@0.5=28.27), outperforming the prior best SnAG (23.14) by 1.3% and RGNet (21.81) by 2.6%. On Ego4D-Goalstep, DeCafNet-50% reaches 31.61 AVG recall, surpassing SnAG (29.49) by 2.1%. On MAD, DeCafNet achieves 16.47 AVG recall, exceeding SnAG (13.84) by 2.63%. Crucially, DeCafNet-50% reduces TFLOPs by 47% and inference time by 51% compared to processing all clips with the expert encoder, while DeCafNet-30% reduces them by 66% and 67% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCafNet-50% outperforms all 10 baselines on Ego4D-NLQ, with the closest competitor being SnAG [31] (23.14 AVG vs. 24.44, +1.3%) and RGNet [15] (21.81 AVG, +2.6%). On Ego4D-Goalstep, DeCafNet-50% (31.61) beats SnAG (29.49, +2.1%) and RGNet (30.49, +1.1%), while DeCafNet-30% (29.56) roughly matches SnAG. On MAD, DeCafNet (16.47) leads SnAG (13.84, +2.63%), RGNet (13.70), SOONet [33] (13.59), CONE [17] (11.01), and M-Guide [1] (9.26) by substantial margins. On short-video benchmarks, DeCaf-Grounder surpasses SnAG on Charades-STA (70.21 vs. 68.84, +1.37%) and TACoS (64.08 vs. 63.27, +0.81%), and also beats SMIN [45] (65.60) and MATN [59] (52.98). Notably, even DeCafNet-30%—processing only 30% of clips with the expert encoder—matches or exceeds SnAG on most Ego4D-NLQ metrics (R1@0.3: 18.07 vs. 15.87; R1@0.5: 12.41 vs. 11.26), demonstrating that the delegate-and-conquer strategy preserves accuracy while cutting computation by 66%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCafNet builds on the ViViT-style spatiotemporal transformer architecture [2] for its sidekick encoder, but departs fundamentally from prior LVTG methods (RGNet, SnAG, SOONet, CONE) that uniformly process every video clip through a full-scale expert encoder. The key conceptual departure is the 'delegate-and-conquer' strategy: a lightweight sidekick encoder handles all clips cheaply while generating a saliency map that routes only the top-c% most relevant clips to the expensive expert encoder. The DeCaf-Grounder module is a new grounding architecture designed specifically to fuse features at two different temporal resolutions (dense sidekick features + sparse expert features), which no prior LVTG method needed to address since they operated on a single uniform feature stream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 47% reduction in TFLOPs and 51% reduction in inference time while simultaneously improving grounding accuracy is practically significant for deploying LVTG on truly long videos (hours-long content) where full-encoder processing is prohibitive. The delegate-and-conquer paradigm—using a cheap model to triage which portions of a long sequence deserve expensive processing—could generalize beyond temporal grounding to other long-sequence multimodal tasks. Limitations include the need for source videos to train the sidekick encoder (precluding use on feature-only benchmarks like MAD without the dual-encoder design), and the saliency selection ratio (c%) remains a manual hyperparameter that trades off efficiency against recall of ground-truth clips. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even more extreme compression (e.g., top-10% expert processing) and whether the sidekick encoder can be replaced by a frozen, off-the-shelf lightweight model without task-specific training.
