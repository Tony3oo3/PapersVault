## Problem

Existing methods for Free-Viewpoint Video (FVV) compression handle dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and compression separately, neglecting inter-frame motion information and the rate-distortion trade-off during training. This separation leads to significant temporal redundancy, performance degradation in long sequences, and suboptimal compression efficiency, making high-quality FVV streaming at low bitrates impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250593/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video sequences (e.g., 300-frame sequences from N3DV, MeetRoom, Google Immersive) as input, using the first frame as a keyframe and remaining frames for sequential inter-frame modeling. It produces a compressed 4D Gaussian representation structured as initial keyframe Gaussians, per-frame motion grids, sparse compensated Gaussians, and two shared MLPs, which can be decoded and rendered at arbitrary viewpoints. The framework supports variable bitrates controlled by a single rate-distortion weight λ1, achieving sizes from 0.23 MB to 1.21 MB per inter-frame.

## Method

4DGC introduces a motion-aware dynamic Gaussian representation that uses a multi-resolution motion grid (L=3 levels) with two shared lightweight MLPs to estimate per-Gaussian rigid translation and rotation between adjacent frames, while sparse compensated Gaussians are added to capture newly emerging regions. Compression is achieved through a two-stage end-to-end scheme: Stage 1 jointly optimizes the motion grid and its implicit entropy model, and Stage 2 refines compensated Gaussian SH coefficients with a separate entropy model. Both stages employ differentiable quantization (simulated uniform noise) to enable gradient flow and a tiny trainable implicit entropy model for on-the-fly bitrate estimation. The entire framework is trained with a rate-distortion loss that balances photometric quality (L1 + D-SSIM) against estimated bitrate, ensuring the learned representation is inherently low-entropy and compression-friendly.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, 4DGC achieves 31.58 dB PSNR and 0.943 SSIM at only 0.5 MB per frame, outperforming 3DGStream (31.54 dB, 8.1 MB) by ~16× in compression while matching its quality. On MeetRoom and Google Immersive, 4DGC reaches 28.08 dB / 0.922 SSIM (0.42 MB) and 29.71 dB / 0.935 SSIM (0.61 MB) respectively, again leading all baselines in PSNR at the lowest bitrate. In BDBR terms, 4DGC reduces bitrate by 68.59%, 40.71%, and 59.99% relative to TeTriRF on N3DV, MeetRoom, and Google Immersive, while rendering at 145–213 FPS (vs. 2–3 FPS for ReRF/TeTriRF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

4DGC wins on PSNR, SSIM, and size simultaneously against all nine baselines on N3DV. Against the closest competitor 3DGStream [61], 4DGC matches PSNR (31.58 vs 31.54 dB) while using only 0.5 MB vs 8.1 MB (~16× smaller) and supports variable bitrate, which 3DGStream does not. Against the other streamable variable-bitrate methods ReRF [64] and TeTriRF [69], 4DGC gains +1.87 dB and +0.93 dB PSNR respectively at a comparable or lower bitrate, and renders 60–85× faster (168 FPS vs 2.0/2.7 FPS). On MeetRoom and Google Immersive (Table 2), 4DGC again leads in PSNR and size: 28.08 dB at 0.42 MB vs 3DGStream's 28.03 dB at 8.21 MB, and 29.71 dB at 0.61 MB vs 3DGStream's 29.66 dB at 10.33 MB. The BDBR analysis (Table 3) quantifies the RD advantage: −68.59% bitrate reduction vs TeTriRF on N3DV (BD-PSNR +1.12 dB), −40.71% on MeetRoom, and −59.99% on Google Immersive. Non-streamable methods (K-Planes, HyperReel, MixVoxels, D-3DG) are outperformed on quality or size but cannot support streaming or variable bitrate, making them less practical for the FVV use case.

## Relation to Prior Work

4DGC extends 3D Gaussian Splatting from static to dynamic scenes by introducing a multi-resolution motion grid with sparse compensated Gaussians, departing from prior 4DGS approaches (4DGS, SpacetimeGS) that model the entire sequence as a monolithic 4D representation unsuitable for streaming. It also diverges from prior FVV compression methods (ReRF, TeTriRF) that treat representation learning and entropy coding as separate stages, instead jointly optimizing both under a rate-distortion objective with differentiable quantization and an implicit entropy model. The motion-grid-plus-compensation design is conceptually analogous to inter-frame prediction in classical video coding but applied to explicit Gaussian primitives rather than pixel blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

4DGC demonstrates that 3DGS-based FVV can be compressed to sub-megabyte per-frame sizes while maintaining competitive quality and 168 FPS rendering, making real-time streaming of photorealistic free-viewpoint video practically viable for AR/VR applications. The variable-bitrate support via tunable λ₁ is a practical feature absent from most prior 3DGS streaming methods. Key limitations remain: dependence on first-frame reconstruction quality, requirement for multi-view input (no sparse-view support), and decoding speed still lagging behind rendering speed—addressing these would broaden applicability to longer sequences and bandwidth-constrained deployments.
