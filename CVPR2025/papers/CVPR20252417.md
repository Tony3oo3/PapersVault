## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based semantic field methods (e.g., Feature 3DGS) embed high-dimensional semantic features into the same large set of 3D Gaussians used for color rendering, resulting in prohibitive storage (~1.2 GB) and slow training/rendering. The paper identifies that semantic information is far sparser than color information, yet prior methods do not exploit this sparsity by keeping the two fields coupled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252417/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view images (640×480, ~900 per scene) with COLMAP-estimated camera poses, from which LSeg extracts 512-dim semantic feature maps at 360×480 resolution. It produces an independent 3D semantic field (using ~30k Gaussians vs. ~600k for the color field) that renders low-dimensional (9-dim) latent feature maps from novel viewpoints; these are decoded back to 512-dim and quantized to produce per-pixel semantic labels or to drive language-guided editing of the color field via spatial correspondence.

## Method

DF-3DGS decouples the semantic field from the color field by removing all color-related parameters (SH coefficients) from the semantic Gaussians, reducing the required Gaussian count by over 95% since semantic information lacks high-frequency components. A two-stage hierarchical compression then further reduces storage: first, an Adaptive Data Compression (ADC) module performs VQ-VAE-style quantization with a dynamically evolving codebook (initialized via Farthest Point Sampling, expanded when feature-to-codeword cosine similarity drops below α=0.9, and pruned when inter-codeword similarity exceeds β=0.99), which both extracts scene-specific core features and clusters similar ones for robustness; second, a scene-specific autoencoder compresses the codebook entries from 512-dim to a 9-dim latent space. The decoupled semantic field is trained with L1 loss against the compressed target feature maps, while the codebook is optimized with a class-balanced cosine distance loss to handle feature imbalance across semantic categories.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, DF-3DGS (decouple + Adaptive Data Compression) achieves the best mIoU of 77.9% for novel view semantic segmentation, outperforming Feature 3DGS (73.9%) by 4.0%. The full pipeline (decouple + ADC + Semantic Compression) achieves 76.6% mIoU while reducing training time to 8 minutes (vs. 351 min for Feature 3DGS), storage to 13.0 MB (vs. 1175.2 MB), and achieving 36.08 FPS (vs. 5.66 FPS). The number of 3D Gaussians in the semantic field drops from ~602k to ~29k (decouple+ADC) or ~110k (full pipeline), reflecting the sparsity of semantic information relative to color.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DF-3DGS decisively outperforms all three baselines on mIoU: 77.9% (ADC variant) or 76.6% (full pipeline) vs. 73.9% for Feature 3DGS, 58.5% for LangSplat, and 32.0% for LEGaussians. Against Feature 3DGS, the full pipeline reduces training time by ~44× (8 min vs. 351 min), storage by ~91× (13.0 MB vs. 1175.2 MB), and increases rendering speed by ~6.4× (36.08 vs. 5.66 FPS), while still improving mIoU by 2.7%. Against LangSplat, DF-3DGS gains 18.4 mIoU points and reduces training time from 25 min to 8 min, though LangSplat's per-level FPS (61.45/3 ≈ 20.5) remains lower than DF-3DGS's 36.08. Against LEGaussians, DF-3DGS achieves a 45.9 mIoU improvement and faster training (8 vs. 28 min), but LEGaussians retains a higher FPS (77.66 vs. 36.08) due to its extremely low 8-dimensional features. The key trade-off is that the full pipeline's 9-dimensional latent features cost 1.3 mIoU relative to the ADC-only variant (77.9 → 76.6), yet still surpass Feature 3DGS while enabling real-time rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based semantic field reconstruction line initiated by Feature 3DGS [47], LangSplat [31], and LEGaussians [36], all of which embed high-dimensional semantic features directly into the same Gaussians that encode color. The key conceptual departure is decoupling the semantic field from the color field entirely, motivated by the observation that semantic information is far sparser than appearance information and thus requires an order of magnitude fewer Gaussians. The hierarchical compression module adapts VQ-VAE-style quantization [39] with a dynamically evolving codebook (via expansion/pruning) and pairs it with a scene-specific autoencoder, a combination not previously applied to 3DGS semantic features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× training-time reduction and 20× storage reduction while simultaneously improving mIoU by 4% over Feature 3DGS make this a practically attractive option for 3D semantic understanding pipelines where storage and speed are bottlenecks. However, the evaluation is confined to five indoor Replica scenes with a single 2D feature extractor (LSeg), and the scene-specific autoencoder limits cross-scene generalizability. Open questions include whether the decoupling benefit persists for outdoor or highly textured scenes, whether the adaptive codebook can be shared or pre-trained across scenes, and how the method scales to open-vocabulary settings with thousands of categories.
