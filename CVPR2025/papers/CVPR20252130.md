## Problem

Existing prompt learning methods for vision-language models ensure task-awareness of learnable prompts through optimization, but the scarcity of task-specific data (typically 16 labeled images per class) causes overfitting, resulting in prompts with limited task-awareness and poor generalization. Rather than imposing stronger regularization constraints, this paper asks whether task-awareness can be directly injected by introducing task-aware pre-context derived from the task's class structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252130/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of class description strings as input. The class descriptions are encoded by CLIP's frozen textual encoder to produce class embeddings, which are then K-Means clustered into K centers (default K=5) to form compact task-aware pre-context vectors. The output is a per-class probability distribution computed via cosine similarity between the prompted class embeddings and the prompted image embedding, with the argmax yielding the predicted class label.

## Method

TAC generates task-aware pre-context by applying K-Means clustering to CLIP class embeddings and linearly transforming the K resulting centers into textual and visual pre-context vectors, preserving the backbone structure of the task while keeping the module lightweight. These pre-contexts are injected at every transformer layer of both the textual and visual encoders alongside learnable prompts, so that the pretrained encoders mediate their interaction and reinforce prompt task-awareness. A customized mask matrix is applied layer-wise in the visual encoder to block attention from patch embeddings to visual pre-context (mitigating modality-gap interference), while learnable prompts serve as an intermediary bridge. A self-regulation module imposes L1 consistency between prompted class/visual embeddings and their CLIP counterparts to suppress overfitting, and the overall loss combines cross-entropy with weighted textual and visual consistency terms.

## Results

In the base-to-novel setting across 11 datasets, TAC achieves the best average base accuracy (85.24% on ImageNet, 98.57% on StanfordCars) and the best harmonic mean (81.24% on ImageNet, 97.04% on OxfordPets), outperforming the strongest baselines ArGue and CoPrompt. In the domain generalization setting, TAC attains the best average accuracy of 61.63% across ImageNetV2, ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-A, and ImageNet-R, surpassing ArGue (60.67%) and PromptSRC (60.65%). In cross-dataset transfer, TAC achieves the best ImageNet accuracy (72.77%) and the second-best average across 10 target datasets (66.53%), slightly behind CoPrompt (67.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAC outperforms all 15 baselines on 9 out of 11 datasets in the base-to-novel setting, with the largest margins over CoOp (e.g., +9.58% HM on ImageNet) and Co-CoOp. The closest competitors are ArGue and CoPrompt: TAC beats ArGue on ImageNet (81.24 vs 80.78 HM), StanfordCars (96.89 vs 96.79), and Food101 (86.15 vs 85.36), but loses on DTD (72.58 vs 73.31) and EuroSAT (88.10 vs 91.23). In domain generalization, TAC achieves the best average (61.63%) and per-dataset accuracy on all four shifted targets, edging out ArGue (60.67%) and PromptSRC (60.65%). In cross-dataset transfer, TAC leads on the source ImageNet (72.77%) but trails CoPrompt by 0.47% in average target accuracy (66.53% vs 67.00%), while still winning on individual targets like DTD (47.57%) and FGVCAircraft (23.53%). Overall, TAC's advantage is most pronounced in the base-to-novel and domain generalization settings, where its task-aware pre-context provides the strongest gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt learning paradigm for CLIP-based VLMs, building directly on CoOp (learnable textual prompts optimized via cross-entropy) and PromptSRC (self-regulating constraints to prevent overfitting). It departs from the prevailing strategy of ensuring task-awareness through additional optimization constraints (e.g., self-regulating losses in PromptSRC, prediction consistency in CoPrompt) by instead injecting a compact, task-aware pre-context derived from K-Means clustering of class embeddings into both the textual and visual encoders. The masked attention mechanism that prevents visual pre-context from corrupting patch embeddings is a new technical element not present in prior prompt learning methods, and the overall framing—providing structural task knowledge rather than constraining optimization—represents a conceptual shift within the prompt learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in prompt learning: with only ~16 labeled images per class, learned prompts overfit and generalize poorly. TAC's clustering-based pre-context is lightweight (2.5% increase in visual encoder input) and consistently improves performance across base-to-novel, domain generalization, and cross-dataset transfer settings, making it a drop-in enhancement for existing CoOp/PromptSRC pipelines. However, the gains over strong baselines like CoPrompt and ArGue are modest (1–2% harmonic mean), and the method is validated only on CLIP ViT-B/16 for image classification, leaving open whether the clustering-based pre-context transfers to other VLM architectures, larger models, or non-classification tasks such as detection or segmentation.
