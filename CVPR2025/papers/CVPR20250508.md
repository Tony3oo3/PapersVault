## Problem

Generative inbetweening using image-to-video diffusion models suffers from ambiguous interpolation paths because only the start and end frames provide conditioning, leaving intermediate frames without explicit guidance. Existing time-reversal strategies (TRF, GI) fuse bidirectional denoising paths, but the stochastic nature of these paths causes misalignment and incoherent transitions, particularly under large motions. The paper addresses this by providing an explicit per-frame condition that constrains the interpolation trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250508/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB key frames (start and end) at 1024×576 resolution and produces N=25 intermediate video frames at the same resolution. From the input frames, it extracts line-match correspondences via GlueStick and human pose skeletons via DWPose to construct per-frame control conditions, which are then linearly (or user-specified non-linearly) interpolated across the temporal axis. Inference runs in 25 denoising steps without noise re-injection, roughly halving the step count of prior generative inbetweening methods.

## Method

FCVG constructs frame-wise conditions by extracting matched lines (GlueStick) and human poses (DWPose) from the two keyframes, visualizing them as color-coded images c₁ and c_N, and linearly interpolating between them to obtain a condition for every intermediate frame. These conditions are injected into Stable Video Diffusion via a ControlNeXt-style lightweight adapter (ResNet blocks + cross normalization), with only the value/output projection matrices in attention layers and the adapter blocks fine-tuned on 70k iterations. During inference, a time-reversal strategy runs forward denoising conditioned on the start frame and backward denoising conditioned on the end frame, each guided by its corresponding temporal sequence of frame-wise conditions; the two paths are fused with a linearly varying weight λ. Because the per-frame conditions align the forward and backward trajectories, simple average fusion suffices without noise re-injection, and a single tunable weight γ (default 1) controls the strength of condition influence, allowing users to trade off motion diversity against temporal stability.

## Results

On the collected test set (524 clips from DAVIS, RealEstate10K, and Pexels), FCVG achieves the best FID (24.05 at gap=23, 22.77 at gap=12), FVMD (5607.2 / 4537.4), and FVD (437.9 / 465.6) among all generative methods, while also matching or exceeding FILM on VBench. FCVG is marginally inferior to FILM on LPIPS (0.1832 vs. 0.1540 at gap=23) but substantially outperforms it on all temporal metrics (FVMD 5607 vs. 8209, FVD 438 vs. 543). Inference time is 523 s for 25 frames at 1024×576, roughly 47% faster than GI (975 s) and 58% faster than TRF (1230 s), thanks to halving the denoising steps from 50 to 25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCVG outperforms the strongest generative baseline GI [47] on every metric at both frame gaps: FID improves from 31.39→24.05 (gap=23) and 32.37→22.77 (gap=12), FVD drops from 524.0→437.9 and 565.8→465.6, and FVMD decreases from 5682.6→5607.2 and 4721.0→4537.4. Against TRF [8], FCVG achieves roughly 40% lower FVD (437.9 vs. 823.4 at gap=23) and 46% lower FVMD, reflecting substantially better temporal coherence. DynamiCrafter [52] is the weakest generative method, with FVD nearly 2.2× higher than FCVG (978.9 vs. 437.9). The only metric where FCVG trails is LPIPS versus the optical-flow method FILM [33] (0.1832 vs. 0.1540 at gap=23), but FILM degrades sharply on temporal metrics (FVMD 8208.7 vs. 5607.2) and produces visible artifacts under large motion, whereas FCVG maintains stable intermediate frames. In terms of efficiency, FCVG requires only 25 denoising steps (523 s) versus 50 steps for GI (975 s) and TRF (1230 s), a ~47–58% speedup at the same 1024×576 resolution and 25-frame length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the time reversal strategy introduced by TRF [8] and refined by Generative Inbetweening [47], which fuses bidirectional denoising paths conditioned on start and end frames. However, it departs from this lineage by identifying the core failure mode—ambiguity in the interpolation path when motion is large—and addressing it with explicit per-frame conditions (interpolated line matches from GlueStick and pose skeletons from DWPose) injected via a ControlNeXt-style lightweight adapter into SVD. Rather than relying on the model to implicitly discover a coherent path through stochastic denoising, FCVG prescribes a concrete geometric trajectory for every intermediate frame, reducing the problem to a simple weighted fusion of two well-aligned paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FCVG halves the inference steps (25 vs. 50) compared to GI while eliminating the need for noise re-injection, and it requires only a single tunable weight (γ) with default SVD hyperparameters, making it substantially easier to deploy. The approach also generalizes to animation and line-art without additional fine-tuning, broadening its applicability. Key limitations remain: line matching quality degrades when input frames are highly similar or extremely different, and the method inherits the inference cost of the underlying video diffusion model, precluding real-time use. Open questions include whether more robust I2V backbones or richer control signals (e.g., user-specified drag trajectories, text-driven conditions) can further close the gap in challenging large-motion scenarios.
