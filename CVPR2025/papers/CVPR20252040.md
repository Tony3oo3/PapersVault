## Problem

The deep fusion of frozen LLMs with trainable diffusion transformers (DiTs) via layer-wise shared self-attention has shown promising results for text-to-image synthesis, but prior work lacks controlled comparisons against established baselines, leaves critical design choices unexplored, and withholds training recipes. This makes it unclear whether the approach genuinely outperforms simpler conditioning strategies or whether its reported gains are artifacts of undisclosed implementation details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252040/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (padded or truncated to 256 tokens) and produces a 512×512 RGB image. Text is processed through the full frozen LLM stack (all layers), with key and value states from each layer cached and reused across all 25 Euler sampling steps at inference. Images are encoded/decoded via a 16-channel VAE from Stable Diffusion 3, and generation uses classifier-free guidance at scale 6 with 10% text dropout during training for unconditional generation.

## Method

The architecture is a two-stream transformer where a frozen decoder-only LLM (Gemma 2B or Gemma 2 2B) and a randomly initialized DiT of matching backbone size process text and noisy image latents respectively, with token sequences from both streams concatenated at each layer's self-attention. A causal mask is applied to text tokens and a bidirectional mask to image tokens, so image tokens attend to text but not vice versa, preserving the LLM's pretrained functionality. Training uses the rectified flow objective with a logit-normal timestep distribution. Key empirical findings include: removing adaLN-Zero timestep conditioning entirely yields the best FID with 20% fewer parameters, 1D+2D RoPE outperforms APE and M-RoPE for mixed-modal sequences, and upgrading the base LLM from Gemma 2B to Gemma 2 2B produces a large performance jump (GenEval 0.51→0.54, FID 27.33→23.94), indicating DiT performance is strongly coupled to LLM capability. The final recipe (no adaLN, 1D+2D RoPE, Gemma 2 2B) trained for 800K steps on 26M pairs achieves GenEval 0.60 and FID 7.54, competitive with much larger industrial systems.

## Results

The proposed 2B-parameter deep fusion model, trained on 26M image-caption pairs, achieves GenEval 0.60, DPG-Bench 81.6, and FID 7.54 on MJHQ-30K. This is competitive with substantially larger industrial systems: it surpasses SDXL (GenEval 0.55, FID 6.63) and PixArt-Σ (GenEval 0.54, FID 6.15) in text-image alignment while remaining within ~1 FID point of SDXL. Against the closest same-scale academic baselines, it outperforms Lumina Next (GenEval 0.46) and PixArt-α (GenEval 0.48) by a wide margin, though it trails Sana 1.0 1.6B (GenEval 0.66, FID 5.76) and SD 3 M (GenEval 0.62, FID 11.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model (GenEval 0.60, DPG 81.6, FID 7.54) clearly outperforms several industrial and academic baselines in text-image alignment: it beats SDXL (0.55), PG 2.5 (0.56), PixArt-Σ (0.54), DALL-E 2 (0.52), and Lumina Next (0.46) on GenEval. However, it trails the strongest systems—PG 3 (0.76), DALL-E 3 (0.67), FLUX.1 (0.67), Sana 1.0 1.6B (0.66), and SD 3 M (0.62)—by 0.02 to 0.16 GenEval points. On FID, the model achieves 7.54, which is notably better than SD 3 M (11.92) and FLUX.1 (10.15) but worse than SDXL (6.63), PG 2.5 (6.09), PixArt-Σ (6.15), and Sana 1.0 (5.76), reflecting the paper's observation that text-image alignment and visual quality often trade off. Against the closest same-scale academic competitor, Sana 1.0 1.6B, the proposed model lags by 0.06 GenEval and 1.78 FID despite using a similar parameter budget. The paper notes that its training used only 26M pairs versus billions for industrial systems, positioning the results as evidence of the deep fusion approach's data efficiency rather than a claim of state-of-the-art performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the deep fusion architecture introduced by Playground v3 [21], which fuses a frozen LLM with a trainable DiT via layer-wise shared self-attention. It positions itself as a controlled empirical study to fill gaps left by prior work that prioritized system-level benchmarks over apples-to-apples comparisons with shallow fusion baselines (single-layer text conditioning via self- or cross-attention) and left training recipes undisclosed. The broader lineage includes the unified autoregressive-plus-diffusion paradigm (Transfusion, Mixture-of-Transformers) and the long history of integrating LLMs into diffusion models as text encoders, planners, or caption generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a reproducible, open training recipe for deep fusion at scale and demonstrates that the approach achieves competitive GenEval/DPG scores with only 26M training pairs, surpassing several industry systems. Key open questions remain: how to effectively leverage instruction-tuned LLMs (which surprisingly hurt performance), how to design positional encodings for mixed-modal sequences (M-RoPE underperforms the simpler 1D+2D combination), and whether the finding that removing adaLN-Zero timestep conditioning improves FID generalizes beyond the 2B scale and CC12M dataset. The trade-off between text-image alignment (where deep fusion wins) and visual quality (where shallow fusion wins) remains unresolved and may constrain the approach's practical adoption.
