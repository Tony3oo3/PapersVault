## Problem

Existing image matching and visual localization benchmarks (HPatches, MegaDepth1500, ScanNet1500, Aachen Day-Night, InLoc) lack controlled geometric variations, making it difficult to systematically analyze how methods degrade under specific combinations of low overlap, large scale differences, and extreme viewpoint changes. RUBIK addresses this by organizing 16.5K image pairs from nuScenes into 33 well-defined difficulty levels along three complementary geometric criteria, enabling fine-grained diagnosis of method limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251400/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark pair consists of two RGB images from the same nuScenes scene (captured by 6 cameras with 360° coverage on a car), accompanied by 6 DoF metric camera poses (recovered via COLMAP SfM aligned to nuScenes 3 DoF ground truth), UniDepth metric depth maps, and Depth Anything V2 surface normal maps. The evaluation pipeline produces a relative pose (rotation + translation) via essential matrix estimation (MAGSAC++, 0.5 px threshold) with metric scale recovered from UniDepth at matched locations; success is defined as rotation error < 5° and translation error < 2 m. The benchmark additionally outputs per-pair co-visibility maps and assigns each pair to one of 33 difficulty bins defined by overlap (5–100%), scale ratio (1.0–6.0), and viewpoint angle (0–180°).

## Method

The benchmark construction proceeds in four stages: (1) lifting nuScenes 3 DoF ground-truth poses to full 6 DoF metric poses via COLMAP SfM followed by LO-RANSAC alignment (1 m threshold) to recover elevation and orientation while preserving metric scale; (2) generating per-image geometric cues using UniDepth for metric depth and Depth Anything V2 (aligned to COLMAP sparse reconstruction) for sharp surface normals; (3) computing dense co-visibility maps by warping depth between views and classifying pixels as co-visible, occluded, or outside field-of-view using a 5% relative depth check and a 5° normal-orientation check; (4) quantizing the three resulting criteria—overlap ratio, median scale ratio, and median line-of-sight angle—into 5×4×4 bins, yielding 33 physically valid difficulty levels each populated with 500 pairs. Fourteen methods (detector-based: SIFT, SuperPoint, DISK, ALIKED with LightGlue; XFeat variants; DeDoDe v2; detector-free: LoFTR, ELoFTR, ASpanFormer, RoMa, DUSt3R, MASt3R) are evaluated under an identical pipeline on an RTX 4090, revealing that detector-free 3D-aware methods (DUSt3R 54.8%, MASt3R 53.6%) substantially outperform detector-based approaches (best: ALIKED+LightGlue 36.8%) but at 2–15× higher runtime.

## Results

On the RUBIK benchmark (16.5K image pairs, 33 difficulty levels), the best-performing method DUSt3R achieves a 54.8% success rate (rotation error < 5° and translation error < 2m), followed by MASt3R (53.6%) and RoMa (47.3%). The best detector-based method, ALIKED+LightGlue, reaches only 36.8% success at 45 ms per pair, while the top detector-free methods require 173–614 ms. Even the best method fails on over 45% of pairs, with performance degrading sharply under combined low overlap, large scale ratio, and extreme viewpoint changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark reveals a clear hierarchy: the three 3D-aware detector-free methods (DUSt3R 54.8%, MASt3R 53.6%, RoMa 47.3%) substantially outperform all detector-based approaches, with DUSt3R exceeding the best detector-based method (ALIKED+LightGlue, 36.8%) by 18 percentage points. Among detector-based methods, ALIKED+LightGlue (36.8%), DISK+LightGlue (35.9%), and SP+LightGlue (35.7%) form a tight top cluster at 43–69 ms, while SIFT+LightGlue (33.1%) is slower (194 ms) and less accurate. The XFeat family shows high sensitivity to the matching strategy: plain XFeat (14.2%) and XFeat* (15.1%) perform poorly, but XFeat+LighterGlue jumps to 30.1%, a 16-point gain from the matcher alone. Among earlier detector-free methods, LoFTR (24.9%), ASpanFormer (24.8%), and ELoFTR (26.6%) all trail the newer 3D-grounded approaches by 20+ points, confirming that explicit 3D reasoning (as in DUSt3R/MASt3R) is the key differentiator. The speed-accuracy trade-off is stark: detector-based methods run in 40–70 ms versus 173–614 ms for the top detector-free methods, making ALIKED+LightGlue the most efficient option when sub-50 ms latency is required.

## Relation to Prior Work

RUBIK carves new ground in the image matching and camera pose estimation benchmarking landscape by introducing a structured, multi-axis difficulty taxonomy (overlap, scale ratio, viewpoint angle) that prior benchmarks like HPatches, MegaDepth1500, ScanNet1500, and KITTI lack. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes existing tools—COLMAP for 6-DoF pose lifting, UniDepth for metric depth, and Depth Anything V2 for normal maps—into a novel co-visibility map pipeline that enables fine-grained geometric difficulty quantification. The benchmark is positioned as complementary to existing evaluation suites (Image Matching Challenge 2024, Aachen Day-Night, InLoc) by isolating geometric challenges from confounding factors like weather or illumination changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even the best method (DUSt3R) succeeds on only 54.8% of pairs under 5°/2m thresholds reveals substantial headroom for improvement, particularly in scenarios combining low overlap, large scale differences, and extreme viewpoint changes. The structured 33-level difficulty grid enables targeted diagnosis of method weaknesses and could directly inform curriculum learning or occlusion-aware pose estimation strategies. A key limitation is that the co-visibility maps are not robust to instance changes (e.g., a different car occupying the same location), which may inflate overlap estimates. The demonstrated viability of monocular depth networks for cross-view 3D reasoning also opens a path toward pose estimation frameworks that go beyond classical matching-plus-minimal-solver pipelines.
