## Problem

Existing self-supervised scene text recognition methods fail to integrate visual and linguistic information at a global level: sequence contrastive learning (SeqCLR) aligns only local fragment-level features, while masked image modeling (MIM) reconstructs from local visual structures without leveraging inter-character linguistic context. This limits the model's ability to handle degraded visual quality where linguistic patterns are essential for correct recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250411/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes a pair of 32×128 RGB text images: a randomly masked version (80% of 4×4 patches) and a guidance view with identical text content but different visual appearance (medium augmentation including color, distortion, and perspective transforms). The pre-training output is a 384-dimensional MAE encoder feature vector per masked patch. During fine-tuning, a single 32×128 text image is fed to a ViT-Small encoder and transformer decoder to produce a recognized text string (max 25 characters for English, 40 for Chinese).

## Method

LMIM introduces a dual-branch self-supervised framework built on MAE with a ViT-Small encoder (12 blocks) and a 2-layer decoder. The masked reconstruction branch processes the masked image, while a linguistic guidance branch processes an unmasked image with the same text but different visual appearance; both share encoder parameters. A linguistics alignment module enforces consistency between the [cls] tokens of both branches via an L2 loss, extracting vision-independent linguistic features. The decoder integrates these linguistic features through a self-attention → cross-attention → FFN (SA-CA-FFN) architecture, where cross-attention uses the guidance branch features as keys and values. The reconstruction target is the MAE encoder's feature representation (not raw pixels), and the total loss combines reconstruction MSE with the alignment loss, compelling the model to exploit global linguistic context rather than relying solely on local visual shortcuts.

## Results

On the six common English benchmarks (IIIT5K, IC13, SVT, IC15, SVTP, CUTE80), LMIM with corrected labels achieves a state-of-the-art 97.0% average accuracy and 96.7% weighted average, surpassing the previous best (SSM-S at 96.1%). On the Union14M benchmark, LMIM pre-trained for 20 epochs reaches 86.3% average accuracy, a 2.0% improvement over SSM-S (84.3%). On the Chinese benchmark, LMIM achieves 82.2% average accuracy (85.5% weighted), outperforming SeqCLR (80.3%) and MIM (81.1%) with the same 36M parameter ViT-Small architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMIM (97.0% avg) outperforms all self-supervised baselines on the six common benchmarks: SSM-S (96.1%), SeqCLR (95.8%), DiG-S (95.5%), MAERec-S (95.1%), and MIM (95.0%), with the largest gains on irregular text datasets (IC15: +2.2% over SSM-S; SVTP: +1.4% over SSM-S). On the Union14M benchmark, LMIM (86.3% avg, 20-epoch pre-training) surpasses SSM-S (84.3%) by 2.0%, DiG-S (83.0%) by 3.3%, and MAERec-S (78.6%) by 7.7%, confirming that linguistic guidance provides substantial benefits over pure visual reconstruction. On the Chinese benchmark, LMIM (82.2% avg) beats SeqCLR (80.3%) and MIM (81.1%) by 1.9% and 1.1% respectively, and substantially outperforms supervised methods like DCTC-L (73.2%) and CCR-CLIP (74.8%). Among supervised baselines on the common benchmarks, LMIM also exceeds MATRN (95.5%), PARSeq (93.8%), and ABINet (94.0%), demonstrating that self-supervised pre-training with linguistic awareness can surpass fully supervised approaches. The consistent 36M parameter count across LMIM, SeqCLR, MIM, DiG-S, MAERec-S, and SSM-S isolates the architectural contribution from model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAE-based self-supervised pre-training paradigm (He et al., CVPR 2022) to scene text recognition by introducing a dual-branch architecture that channels linguistic information into the MIM decoding process. It departs from prior self-supervised STR methods—SeqCLR (local sequence contrastive alignment), DiG (combined CL+MIM), MAERec (pure MIM with feature targets), and SSM (symmetric superimposition)—by explicitly using a second image with identical text but different visual appearance to extract vision-independent linguistic features via a dedicated alignment module. The reconstruction target follows MAERec's encoder features rather than raw pixels, and the decoder architecture (self-attention followed by cross-attention) is a novel design choice that integrates the linguistic guidance branch into the masked reconstruction pathway.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in self-supervised STR: existing MIM and contrastive methods capture local visual structure but fail to model global linguistic context, which is critical for degraded or ambiguous text. The contribution of UCTI-11M (11 million unlabeled Chinese text images) is practically significant given the scarcity of public Chinese pre-training data, and the method's cross-lingual effectiveness (English and Chinese) broadens its applicability. Open questions remain: the approach is restricted to transformer architectures, the random masking strategy may be suboptimal for variable-length text, and it is unclear whether the dual-branch design generalizes to other self-supervised tasks beyond STR. The reliance on generating a guidance view via data augmentation also introduces a practical constraint on augmentation design.
