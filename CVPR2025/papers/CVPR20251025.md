## Problem

Extreme few-shot NeRF (2–4 input views) suffers from overfitting and prohibitively long training times. Existing remedies—frequency regularization (FreeNeRF), gradual voxel upsampling (VGOS), or pre-trained depth/feature priors (SparseNeRF, FSGS)—either require complex scheduling that limits generalization or introduce bias from external models. No prior method achieves fast, robust convergence while fully exploiting the available training data without learned priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251025/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2–4 calibrated RGB views (known intrinsics and extrinsics) plus a COLMAP sparse point cloud for absolute-scale depth constraints; optionally, DPT monocular depth maps from training views can be added. The scene is represented as a pair of density and appearance voxel grids at 640³ (LLFF, RealEstate-10K) or 300³ (DTU), hierarchically downsampled into three scales with a ratio of 4. Training runs 5,000 iterations with a batch of 4,096 rays, including 120 novel poses sampled along a spiral trajectory, and completes in roughly 10 minutes on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

FrugalNeRF builds on TensoRF's tensorial voxel representation but introduces weight-sharing multi-scale voxels: a single density–appearance voxel pair is hierarchically downsampled to L+1 scales, so all scales share parameters and no extra memory is incurred. The key mechanism is cross-scale geometric adaptation—rendered depth at every scale is warped to the nearest training view, and the scale whose reprojection error is smallest supplies a pseudo-ground-truth depth that supervises all other scales via an MSE loss, eliminating the need for a fixed coarse-to-fine schedule. The same adaptation is extended to 120 spiral-sampled novel views (using rendered color in place of GT RGB) to regularize regions with poor training-view overlap. The total loss combines multi-scale color fidelity, the geometric adaptation term, global regularization (total variation, patch-wise depth smoothness, L1 sparsity, distortion), sparse COLMAP depth, and an optional DPT depth term, all optimized with Adam over 5,000 iterations.

## Results

On LLFF with 2 input views, FrugalNeRF (no learned priors) achieves PSNR 18.07, SSIM 0.54, LPIPS 0.35 in just 10 minutes, outperforming the best no-prior baselines (SimpleNeRF: 17.57 PSNR, 9.5 hrs; FreeNeRF: 17.55 PSNR, 1.5 hrs) and matching or exceeding prior-based methods (SparseNeRF: 18.02 PSNR, 1 hr). With optional monocular depth regularization, FrugalNeRF reaches PSNR 18.26 in 11 minutes. On DTU (2-view), FrugalNeRF achieves PSNR 19.72 (no priors) and 20.77 (with mono depth) in 6–7 minutes, surpassing all baselines including SparseNeRF (19.83, 30 mins) and FSGS (16.82, 20 mins).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FrugalNeRF (no priors) outperforms all no-prior baselines on LLFF 2-view PSNR (18.07 vs. SimpleNeRF 17.57, FreeNeRF 17.55, DS-NeRF 16.93) and LPIPS (0.35 vs. 0.39, 0.38, 0.42), while training 6–57× faster. Against prior-based methods, FrugalNeRF (no priors) matches SparseNeRF on PSNR (18.07 vs. 18.02) but substantially outperforms it on LPIPS (0.35 vs. 0.45) and trains 6× faster (10 min vs. 1 hr). With monocular depth added, FrugalNeRF surpasses SparseNeRF on PSNR (18.26 vs. 18.02) and LPIPS (0.35 vs. 0.45) while remaining 5× faster. On DTU 2-view, FrugalNeRF (no priors) achieves PSNR 19.72, beating FreeNeRF (18.05) and ViP-NeRF (14.91) by large margins, though SparseNeRF edges it slightly (19.83); adding mono depth closes this gap decisively (20.77 vs. 19.83). FSGS, despite using monocular depth, underperforms FrugalNeRF on both datasets (LLFF 2-view: 15.26 vs. 18.07 PSNR; DTU: 16.82 vs. 19.72), confirming that FrugalNeRF's cross-scale geometric adaptation provides stronger geometric supervision than external depth priors alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

FrugalNeRF builds directly on TensoRF's tensorial voxel representation for fast radiance field querying, but departs from the broader few-shot NeRF literature in two key ways. Rather than following FreeNeRF's frequency regularization (gradually increasing positional encoding frequencies) or VGOS's coarse-to-fine voxel upsampling schedules, it maintains all multi-scale voxels simultaneously via weight-sharing, eliminating complex scheduling. It also diverges from prior methods that rely on pre-trained monocular depth estimators (SparseNeRF, FSGS, DDP-NeRF) or feature extractors (GeCoNeRF, DietNeRF) by introducing a self-supervised cross-scale geometric adaptation that selects pseudo-ground-truth depth purely from reprojection errors across voxel scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FrugalNeRF trains in ~10 minutes on an RTX 4090 versus 1–13.5 hours for competing methods while matching or exceeding their quality on LLFF and DTU, making few-shot NeRF feasible in near-real-time pipelines. The self-adaptive geometric supervision also offers a clean path to optionally incorporate learned priors (e.g., DPT depth) for further quality gains without sacrificing convergence speed. However, the method still requires accurate camera poses and struggles with large viewpoint changes (e.g., 360° scenes), and the quality improvements over the best no-prior baselines (FreeNeRF, SimpleNeRF) are modest (~0.5–1 dB PSNR), leaving open whether the geometric adaptation mechanism can close the gap with prior-based methods in more challenging settings.
