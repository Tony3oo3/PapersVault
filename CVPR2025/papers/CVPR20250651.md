## Problem

In semi-supervised LiDAR semantic segmentation, pseudo-labeled scenes contain points without pseudo-labels that participate in forward propagation but are excluded from backpropagation, creating intra-scene inconsistency where semantically ambiguous points interfere with learning of pseudo-labeled points. Additionally, existing data augmentation methods typically mix only two scenes at the scene level, limiting the semantic diversity of augmented training samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point cloud scenes (m points with 3D Cartesian coordinates plus r additional features such as intensity or reflectivity), restricted to a [-50, 50] m window in the X-Y plane for both SemanticKITTI (19 classes) and nuScenes (16 classes). Labeled scenes carry per-point semantic labels; unlabeled scenes are processed by the teacher network to generate pseudo-labels above a confidence threshold τ_s = 0.9. The student network outputs per-point semantic class predictions, evaluated by mIoU on validation sets.

## Method

AIScene introduces two complementary mechanisms within a teacher-student (EMA, α=0.99) framework. First, a point erasure strategy removes all points whose teacher confidence falls below τ_s from the forward pass entirely, so that both forward and backward propagation operate on the same set of points, eliminating the intra-scene inconsistency that lets ambiguous points corrupt feature computation for pseudo-labeled points. Second, a patch-based data augmentation (MixPatch + InsFill) builds scene-level and instance-level patch pools from all labeled scenes (BEV patchification into T non-overlapping n×n patches, n=18) and from the current batch of erased pseudo-labeled scenes; a binary mask M selects patches from the pool to replace corresponding regions in the target scene, and InsFill further inserts individual object instances from the instance pool at spatially consistent locations, with overlap and context checks via 3D bounding boxes. Training combines three segmentation losses (original labeled, augmented labeled, augmented unlabeled) plus a consistency loss, and the entire framework is segmentor-agnostic, validated with both MinkowskiNet and Cylinder3D backbones.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI, AIScene achieves an average mIoU of 65.7% across four labeled-data ratios (1%, 10%, 20%, 50%), outperforming the previous SOTA DDSemi (64.4%) by 1.3%. On nuScenes, AIScene reaches 70.9% average mIoU versus DDSemi's 69.7%, a 1.2% gain. The improvement is most pronounced in the most challenging 1% labeled setting, where AIScene scores 61.2% on SemanticKITTI (+1.9% over DDSemi) and 60.2% on nuScenes (+2.1% over DDSemi).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIScene outperforms all 14 compared baselines on both SemanticKITTI and nuScenes. The closest competitor is DDSemi [21] (CVPR 2024), which AIScene surpasses by 1.3% average mIoU on SemanticKITTI and 1.2% on nuScenes, with the largest gap appearing at the 1% split (+1.9% and +2.1% respectively). Among LiDAR-specific semi-supervised methods, LaserMix (Voxel) [15] is the strongest prior baseline at 58.7%/67.6% average, trailing AIScene by 7.0%/3.3%. Language-assisted LASS3D [22] and complexity-reduced LiM3D [23] report only SemanticKITTI results (62.5% and 61.8% avg), both clearly below AIScene's 65.7%. In the augmentation-strategy comparison (Fig. 6c), AIScene's patch-based mixing outperforms MixUp [51], CylinderMix [5], PolarMix [47], CutMix [50], and Mix3D [27], confirming that multi-scene patch and instance-level mixing yields greater semantic diversity than two-scene concatenation or swapping approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-teacher pseudo-labeling paradigm (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) and the LaserMix framework (Kong et al., 2023) for semi-supervised LiDAR segmentation by identifying and addressing a specific inconsistency: prior methods include all points in forward propagation but only pseudo-labeled points in backward propagation. The key conceptual departure is the point erasure strategy, which removes unlabeled points from forward propagation entirely, and a patch-based multi-scene augmentation that goes beyond the two-scene mixing (concatenation, swapping) used by LaserMix, PolarMix, and Mix3D. The method is segmentor-agnostic, validated on both MinkowskiNet and Cylinder3D backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The point erasure strategy is a simple, plug-and-play fix applicable to any pseudo-label-based semi-supervised 3D segmentation method, making it broadly adoptable. The multi-scene patch/instance mixing offers a more principled data augmentation than pairwise scene mixing, and the ablations confirm each component contributes independently. Limitations include evaluation on only two driving-scene datasets and two backbone architectures, and the reliance on a fixed confidence threshold (0.9) and patch size (n=18) that may not transfer to other domains. Open questions include whether the erasure strategy generalizes to other 3D tasks (detection, panoptic segmentation) and whether the patch mixing parameters can be learned adaptively.
