## Problem

Top-down 3D lane detection methods learn entire lane instances from training data, limiting their ability to generalize to previously unseen lane structures encountered worldwide. Bottom-up keypoint-based approaches improve generalization by detecting local parts, but struggle with the non-trivial problem of grouping scattered keypoints into coherent, continuous lane lines without introducing ambiguity or excessive computational cost.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single front-view RGB image (384×720 for lite/base, 512×960 for large) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics used for Inverse Perspective Mapping into a BEV feature map (56×32, 56×64, or 72×128 depending on model size). A LiDAR point cloud processed by SECOND can optionally be fused for the camera+LiDAR variant. The output is a set of up to 256 (or 384 for large) 3D keypoints with lateral offsets and heights, an S×S adjacency matrix encoding directed connections, and final 3D lane instances extracted via shortest-path traversal of the resulting directed graph.

## Method

GLane3D formulates 3D lane detection as keypoint localization followed by directed graph construction. A CNN backbone extracts front-view features that are projected to BEV via IPM using a customized non-uniform anchor grid (denser near the ego vehicle to counteract perspective sparsity). A transformer with deformable cross-attention predicts multiple keypoint proposals per target location within a lateral distance d_x, each refined by a regressed lateral offset; PointNMS then suppresses overlapping proposals to retain only the S strongest keypoints, reducing both ambiguity and connection-estimation cost. Directed connections are predicted by passing each keypoint's feature through two separate MLPs to produce origin and destination embeddings, whose element-wise product is mapped through a linear layer and sigmoid to yield the adjacency matrix. Lanes are extracted by identifying start/end keypoints (no incoming/outgoing edges) and running Dijkstra's shortest path with connection probability as edge cost. Training uses a two-pass Hungarian matcher (once for all proposals, once for post-NMS keypoints) with focal loss on connections, L1 on offsets/heights, and learned loss weights.

## Results

On the OpenLane dataset at the standard 1.5 m matching threshold, GLane3D-Large (Swin-B) achieves an F1 score of 66.0, surpassing the previous best PVALane (Swin-B) by 2.6 points and LATR (ResNet-50) by 4.1 points. Under the stricter 0.5 m threshold, GLane3D-Large reaches 61.1 F1, outperforming LATR (54.0) by 7.1 points. GLane3D also achieves the lowest localization errors across all x and z dimensions (e.g., 0.170 m near x-error, 0.087 m far z-error at 1.5 m). In cross-dataset evaluation (OpenLane-trained → Apollo), GLane3D attains 58.9 F1 at 1.5 m versus 53.2 for PersFormer and 34.3 for LATR, demonstrating substantially stronger generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLane3D outperforms all eight compared baselines on the OpenLane dataset. At the 1.5 m threshold, GLane3D-Large (Swin-B) leads PVALane (Swin-B, 63.4) by 2.6 F1 points and LATR (ResNet-50, 61.9) by 4.1 points; GLane3D-Base (ResNet-50, 63.9) also beats PVALane (ResNet-50, 62.7) by 1.2 points. Under the stricter 0.5 m threshold, the gap widens: GLane3D-Large (61.1) surpasses LATR (54.0) by 7.1 points and DV-3DLane (52.9) by 8.2 points. In cross-dataset generalization (OpenLane-trained evaluated on Apollo), GLane3D achieves 58.9 F1 at 1.5 m, exceeding PersFormer (53.2) by 5.7 points and LATR (34.3) by 24.6 points, with the gap even larger at 0.5 m (42.6 vs. 17.4 and 19.0). For camera+LiDAR fusion on OpenLane, GLane3D‡ (66.6 F1 at 1.5 m) edges out DV-3DLane (66.8) by a narrow margin while achieving substantially lower x-error (0.095 vs. 0.115 m near). The paper also reports that GLane3D-Lite runs at 62.2 FPS, far exceeding LATR (15.2 FPS) and PersFormer (20.3 FPS), making it the fastest method among those compared.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLane3D extends the bottom-up keypoint-based lane detection lineage (BEVLaneDet, Focus on Local, keypoint-based global association) by replacing clustering or iterative association for keypoint grouping with directed graph connection estimation and shortest-path lane extraction. It departs from top-down instance-based methods (PersFormer, LATR, LaneCPP) that predict entire lane instances, arguing that local keypoint detection generalizes better to unseen lane configurations. The IPM-based FV-to-BEV projection follows PersFormer and Gen-LaneNet, but introduces a customized BEV geometry that densifies sampling near the ego vehicle. The multiple-proposal-plus-PointNMS mechanism is a novel combination that borrows the NMS concept from object detection and adapts it to keypoint redundancy in a graph context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-dataset generalization results (OpenLane-trained model evaluated on Apollo) provide concrete evidence that bottom-up keypoint methods outperform top-down instance methods when facing unseen lane structures and camera geometries, with a particularly large gap versus LATR (58.9 vs 34.3 F1 at 1.5m). The GLane3D-Lite model's 62.2 FPS makes a transformer-based 3D lane detector practical for real-time in-vehicle deployment, addressing a key barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the directed-graph formulation scales to more complex road topologies (e.g., multi-lane intersections with merging/splitting), whether the custom BEV geometry transfers across different camera intrinsics without retraining, and how the method performs on datasets with significantly different lane conventions (e.g., left-hand traffic).
