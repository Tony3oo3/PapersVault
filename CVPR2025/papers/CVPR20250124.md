## Problem

Existing learning-based inertial odometry methods are trained on single-platform or single-motion-type datasets, making them prone to overfitting and unable to generalize across diverse robotic platforms. No prior work has applied the foundation-model paradigm—large-scale, diverse pre-training followed by efficient adaptation—to inertial positioning, despite IMU data's continuous, noisy, and domain-sensitive nature making cross-platform generalization particularly challenging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250124/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw IMU acceleration and angular velocity at a standardized 200 Hz sampling rate, with gravity removed and sensor biases assumed known, organized into 1-second windows (200 samples each) processed by a 10-window LSTM. It outputs a 3D relative body-frame velocity per window along with a diagonal covariance (parameterized as log-diagonal elements), supervised at 1 Hz. The loss is formulated in the body frame rather than global coordinates to encourage learning of generalized motion features rather than memorized trajectories.

## Method

Tartan IMU employs a three-stage pretrain–adapt–test framework. The pre-trained model uses a ResNet-style heterogeneous shared backbone that maps diverse IMU signals from four robot categories (wheeled, aerial, legged, human) into a unified latent space, followed by an LSTM for temporal dynamics and robot-specific multi-head decoders that regress velocity and covariance; it is trained on 100+ hours of multi-platform data with rotation-equivariance augmentation and a body-frame relative MSE loss. For unseen platforms, LoRA adapters (1.1M trainable parameters) are inserted into frozen pretrained weights via low-rank decomposition, enabling positive transfer with minimal data while preventing catastrophic forgetting. For real-time deployment, an online adaptation pipeline uses GMM-based clustering to maintain a diverse, balanced training buffer of motion samples (stationary, forward, left/right turns), with SLAM providing relative-pose supervision so the model continuously refines itself at 200 FPS during operation.

## Results

TartanIMU achieves an average 35.5% reduction in ATE and 41.0% reduction in T-RTE across four robotic platforms (wheeled, handheld, legged, aerial) compared to the best specialized baseline. The largest gains appear on legged robots (54.8% ATE improvement, 50.6% T-RTE improvement) and handheld/human motion (47.7% ATE, 60.1% T-RTE). On the aerial (drone) platform, TartanIMU still leads with 19.8% ATE and 29.0% T-RTE improvements. The model also adapts to a new domain (TartanDrive) 33% faster than the SOTA baseline during fine-tuning, and completes online adaptation to a new vehicle platform in 105 seconds at 200 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TartanIMU outperforms all four baselines on every platform and every metric. Against AI-IMU [Brossard, T-IV 2020], the largest ATE gap is on legged robots (1.46 vs 3.23, a 54.8% reduction), while on wheeled vehicles the gap is 6.17 vs 7.68 (19.6%). Against RNIN-VIO [Chen, ISMAR 2021], TartanIMU leads most decisively on handheld motion (4.32 vs 7.62 ATE) and legged motion (1.46 vs 3.10 ATE); RNIN-VIO is also used as the SOTA fine-tuning baseline in the domain-shift experiment, where TartanIMU converges 33% faster. Against TLIO [Liu, RA-L 2020], TartanIMU wins on all four platforms, with the most dramatic margin on handheld (4.32 vs 6.96 ATE, 47.7% improvement) and legged (1.46 vs 3.61 ATE, 54.8%). Against IMO [Cioffi, 2023], which is the strongest drone-specific baseline, TartanIMU still achieves 3.32 vs 3.72 ATE and 1.04 vs 1.34 T-RTE on aerial data. No baseline matches or exceeds TartanIMU on any platform-metric combination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the foundation-model paradigm—large diverse pre-training followed by lightweight adaptation—from NLP (GPT-4) and vision (DINOv2) into inertial odometry, a domain where prior methods (TLIO, AI-IMU, IMO, RNIN-VIO) are each tailored to a single platform or motion type. Architecturally it combines a ResNet-style heterogeneous shared backbone with an LSTM temporal encoder and robot-specific regression heads, building on the rotation-equivariance idea from RIO and the covariance-aware loss from TLIO. The LoRA-based fine-tuning stage and the GMM-guided online adaptation buffer are borrowed from LLM adaptation and continual-learning literature respectively, but their application to real-time IMU state estimation at 200 FPS is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single shared backbone trained on 100+ hours of multi-platform data can outperform every platform-specific model simultaneously (36% average ATE gain), the paper establishes a viable path toward a general-purpose IMU estimator that reduces the need to retrain from scratch for each new robot. The 1.1 M-parameter LoRA adapter and the 105-second online adaptation loop make the approach practical for field deployment, where collecting large labeled datasets is infeasible. Key open questions remain: whether the four motion categories (car, humanoid, quadruped, drone) truly cover the robotics landscape, whether the GMM-based buffer selection generalizes to highly dynamic or multi-modal motions, and whether integrating simulated IMU data can close the remaining domain gaps.
