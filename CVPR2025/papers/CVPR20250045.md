## Problem

Real-time rendering of large-scale, high-resolution 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes remains impractical due to three compounding inefficiencies in tile-based rasterization: (1) loose axis-aligned bounding box approximations produce substantial redundant Gaussian-tile pairs (at least 21.5% even in the ideal case), (2) varying Gaussian sizes create severe workload imbalance across threads, and (3) uneven spatial distribution of Gaussians causes tile-level latency bottlenecks. These issues collectively prevent 3DGS from achieving real-time frame rates on city-scale scenes (up to 2.7 km²) at 4K resolution.

## Input / Output

FlashGS is a drop-in CUDA rasterization library that consumes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian scene (millions of anisotropic Gaussians with spherical harmonic coefficients) and camera intrinsics/extrinsics, producing rendered RGB images at arbitrary resolutions up to 4K+. No retraining or modification of the scene representation is required; the method operates purely at the rendering stage. It is evaluated on scenes ranging from small indoor captures (Playroom, DrJohnson) to city-scale outdoor scenes (MatrixCity with 20M+ Gaussians, Rubble at 4608×3456), all on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

FlashGS integrates three complementary optimizations into the 3DGS rasterization pipeline. First, a precise ellipse-rectangle intersection algorithm replaces the AABB approximation by directly testing whether the projected Gaussian ellipse overlaps each tile, using geometric transformations (reducing the problem to checking at most two tile edges via symmetry) and algebraic simplifications that eliminate square roots and divisions from the quadratic equation check. Second, a size-aware adaptive scheduling strategy assigns a single thread to small Gaussians covering one tile but a full warp (32 threads) to large Gaussians spanning multiple tiles, balancing per-thread workload during the intersection and key-value generation phase. Third, a three-stage software pipeline overlaps Gaussian feature memory fetches with alpha blending computations across consecutive tiles, prefetching indices and features two tiles ahead to hide memory latency that cannot be resolved by additional thread-level parallelism due to inherent data dependencies.

## Results

FlashGS-Full achieves an average 7.2× speedup over vanilla 3DGS and 8.7× on the 7 large-scale/high-resolution tests (MatrixCity, Rubble, and 4K scenes), reaching a minimum of 125.9 FPS on the Rubble dataset where 3DGS manages only 4.7 FPS. Against the strongest software baseline AdRGS-Full, FlashGS-Full delivers a 1.8× average speedup (2.2× on large-scale scenes), e.g., 207.4 vs. 68.8 AvgFPS on MatrixCity-4K. Image quality is preserved: FlashGS-Full matches vanilla 3DGS within ~0.1 dB PSNR across all six scenes, while AdRGS-Full's retrained model shows noticeable degradation (e.g., Truck PSNR drops from 27.24 to 26.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS, FlashGS-Full wins decisively on every scene and resolution, with speedups ranging from ~3.7× (Playroom-1080p) to ~13.8× (Rubble), and the gap widens dramatically at 4K and on large-scale scenes (MatrixCity-4K: 207.4 vs. 15.0 FPS). Against gsplat, FlashGS-Full is faster in all settings; notably gsplat is actually slower than vanilla 3DGS on most scenes, attributed to its richer feature set and more complex interface. Against AdRGS-AABB (tighter AABB only), FlashGS-Pre already achieves a 1.5× average speedup, and FlashGS-Full extends this to ~2.5× on large-scale scenes. Against AdRGS-Full (which requires retraining to balance Gaussian distribution), FlashGS-Full wins on AvgFPS across all scenes (1.8× average, 2.2× on large-scale), but AdRGS-Full retains a MinFPS advantage on Truck-1080p (307.4 vs. 225.2) and Train-1080p (316.7 vs. 287.4), likely because retraining can redistribute Gaussians more evenly at lower resolutions. Against hardware rasterization methods (web-splat, fast_gauss), FlashGS achieves over 2.5× speedup, with web-splat failing entirely on MatrixCity due to its 20M+ Gaussians.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashGS is a systems-level optimization of the 3DGS tile-based rasterization pipeline introduced by Kerbl et al. (2023), building directly on the opacity-aware tighter AABB concept from AdR-Gaussian (Wang et al., 2024) but replacing the still-approximate bounding box with a precise ellipse-tile intersection test. It departs from prior rendering-focused work (gsplat, AdR-Gaussian) by addressing three distinct inefficiencies simultaneously—redundant Gaussian-tile pairs, size-induced workload imbalance, and memory-computation dependency stalls—rather than targeting a single bottleneck. The paper also positions itself against hardware rasterization approaches (web-splat, fast_gauss), arguing that a carefully optimized CUDA software pipeline outperforms GPU-fixed-function rasterization for Gaussian primitives due to the inherent 1−π/4 redundancy of quad-based representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving an order-of-magnitude speedup (7.2× average, 8.7× on large-scale scenes) and a minimum of 125.9 FPS on a 2.7 km² cityscape at 4K, FlashGS makes real-time 3DGS rendering of city-scale scenes practically feasible for the first time, opening the door to applications in autonomous driving simulation, VR city exploration, and digital twins. The open-source CUDA library with Python bindings lowers the adoption barrier significantly. However, the work is confined to single-GPU rendering and does not address the fundamental memory bottleneck of millions of Gaussians with SH coefficients; multi-GPU scaling, LOD strategies, and integration with scene compression remain open. The paper also notes that in low-resolution outdoor scenes with uneven object distribution, AdR-Gaussian's retraining-based load balancing can still outperform FlashGS on worst-case frame latency, suggesting that representation-level and rasterizer-level optimizations are complementary rather than substitutive.
