## Problem

Existing few-shot action recognition methods rely on pre-defined temporal units (frames or tuples) for alignment, making them inflexible for actions of varying durations and speeds, and incurring quadratic computational cost from pairwise unit comparisons. No prior approach simultaneously achieves flexible, length-agnostic action representation and efficient linear-time video matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251005/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes uniformly sampled T frames from support and query videos, extracts per-frame features via a frozen image backbone (ResNet-50 or ViT) into D-dimensional vectors, and aggregates them into a fixed set of M=60 pattern tokens per video. Classification is performed by token-wise cosine distance between the M support tokens and M query tokens (rather than T×T pairwise frame comparisons), followed by a softmax over N classes to yield the predicted action label.

## Method

TEAM replaces temporal alignment with a fixed pool of M learnable pattern tokens that aggregate video features via cross-attention, producing two complementary representations: instance tokens (P_m + CA) that capture shared discriminative patterns within a class, and exclusive tokens (P_m − CA) that encode 'otherness' by positioning near other classes' instance tokens. Matching is token-wise (M comparisons) rather than pairwise, yielding linear complexity in the number of frames. An episode-level adaptation process computes cosine similarity between support class tokens and subtracts shared cross-attention contributions (scaled by the entanglement score) to sharpen class boundaries for novel categories. Training uses episodic N-way K-shot meta-learning with a combined cross-entropy loss over both instance and exclusive token probabilities.

## Results

Under the standard 5-way 1-shot and 5-shot protocols, TEAM achieves 62.8% / 78.4% on HMDB51, 75.1% / 88.2% on Kinetics, and 87.2% / 96.2% on UCF101 with ResNet-50, setting new state-of-the-art on all settings except UCF101 5-shot. With a ViT backbone, TEAM further improves to 70.9% / 85.5% (HMDB51), 83.3% / 92.9% (Kinetics), and 94.5% / 98.8% (UCF101), surpassing all prior methods in every configuration. On the temporally challenging SSv2-Small benchmark, TEAM reaches 47.2% / 63.1%, outperforming TATs without its point tracker (44.6% / 62.5%) while remaining competitive with TATs using the tracker (47.9% / 64.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

With ResNet-50, TEAM outperforms the strongest prior methods—MoLo (60.8% HMDB51 1-shot, 86.0% UCF101 1-shot) and GgHM (61.2% HMDB51 1-shot, 74.9% Kinetics 1-shot)—by 1.6–2.0 points across most settings, with the sole exception of UCF101 5-shot where SloshNet leads by 0.9 points (97.1 vs. 96.2). With ViT, TEAM dominates every configuration, beating MoLo by 1.0–2.3 points and OTAM by 1.6–2.9 points across the three datasets. On SSv2-Small, TEAM (47.2% / 63.1%) trails TATs with its point tracker (47.9% / 64.4%) by a narrow margin but clearly surpasses TATs without the tracker (44.6% / 62.5%), indicating that the alignment-free matching is competitive even under high temporal complexity. In cross-domain evaluation (trained on Kinetics, tested on HMDB51 and UCF101), TEAM achieves 81.1% and 98.3%, exceeding the best prior method (STRM at 78.5% and 98.0%) by 2.6 and 0.3 points respectively. Overall, TEAM's consistent gains across backbones, shot numbers, and datasets validate that eliminating pre-defined temporal units and brute-force alignment yields both flexibility and efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEAM departs from the dominant FSAR paradigm of temporal alignment (frame-level methods like OTAM, HyRSM, MoLo; tuple-level methods like TRX, STRM, SloshNet) by eliminating pre-defined temporal units entirely. Conceptually, it repurposes the token-based feature aggregation idea from DETR, BLIP-2, and Flamingo—where learnable queries compress spatial or multimodal context—into a video action recognition setting, but with a distinctive dual instance/exclusive token design that encodes both shared and contrasting class patterns. The episode-level adaptation process, which subtracts inter-class common information from support tokens, has no direct analogue in prior FSAR alignment methods and draws closer to prototype refinement strategies in few-shot image classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing quadratic pairwise alignment with linear token-wise comparison, TEAM offers a practical efficiency advantage that becomes significant for untrimmed or long-duration videos, a regime underrepresented in current FSAR benchmarks. The dual instance/exclusive token formulation provides a principled way to handle the 'missing pattern' problem (where a video of a class lacks the most discriminative cue), which alignment methods address only implicitly through path search. Open questions include whether the fixed pattern token count (60) generalizes to much larger class spaces, whether the adaptation process degrades with very high N-way settings, and whether the approach extends naturally to streaming or online few-shot settings where support sets evolve over time.
