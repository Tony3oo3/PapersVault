## Problem

Gaussian Splatting-based methods rely on sufficient training view coverage and perform well only on interpolated views, but real-world capture often yields limited horizontal coverage while users need elevated or otherwise extrapolated viewpoints. Existing view-prior generation strategies for sparse-view reconstruction suffer from two unaddressed issues in the extrapolated setting: loss of view-dependent color information and occlusion artifacts in complex scenes, leading to corrupted pseudo-labels and degraded synthesis quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251514/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of sparse training RGB images with known camera poses covering only a limited angular range (e.g., horizontal or near-horizontal views) and produces rendered RGB images at extrapolated views (e.g., elevated pitch angles). During training, it additionally consumes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 2.1 model for appearance regularization and a mesh reconstructed from the pre-trained GS backbone for geometry supervision and occlusion-aware reprojection. The framework is backbone-agnostic and has been instantiated with 3DGS, Mip-Splatting, 2DGS, GOF, and RaDe-GS.

## Method

EVPGS is a two-stage coarse-to-fine view augmentation framework. At the coarse stage, Appearance and Geometry Regularization (AGR) fine-tunes the pre-trained GS model at augmented views by (a) using the DDM noise estimate as a gradient signal to push rendered images toward the data distribution, and (b) supervising GS-rendered depth maps with mesh-rasterized depth maps to smooth geometry. At the fine stage, Occlusion-Aware Reprojection and Refinement (OARR) generates Enhanced View Priors: a reverse-reprojection error criterion detects and replaces occlusion-corrupted pixels with coarse-stage renderings, and a frequency-domain blending step combines high-frequency detail from the reprojected training image with low-frequency view-dependent color from the coarse-stage rendering. These Enhanced View Priors then serve as pseudo-labels for photometric fine-tuning of the GS model at the augmented views.

## Results

EVPGS(RaDe-GS), the best-performing variant, achieves 26.49 dB PSNR / 0.899 SSIM / 0.067 LPIPS on DTU, 25.14 dB PSNR / 0.927 SSIM / 0.050 LPIPS on Merchandise3D, and 27.85 dB PSNR / 0.924 SSIM / 0.050 LPIPS on Synthetic-NeRF. These results represent consistent improvements of 0.6–1.6 dB PSNR over the corresponding GS backbones across all three datasets. The framework also outperforms VEGS*, the only other method specifically designed for extrapolated view synthesis, by 1.56 dB PSNR on DTU and 3.34 dB on Synthetic-NeRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVPGS(RaDe-GS) outperforms all six baselines across every metric on all three datasets. Compared to its own backbone RaDe-GS, EVPGS gains +0.71 dB PSNR on DTU (26.49 vs 25.78), +1.58 dB on Merchandise3D (25.14 vs 23.56), and +0.32 dB on Synthetic-NeRF (27.85 vs 27.53). The improvement is most pronounced on Merchandise3D, where the EVS-specific split has the largest pitch-angle disparity (39.6°). VEGS*, the only other EVS-specific method, underperforms all standard GS baselines on DTU (24.93 dB) and Synthetic-NeRF (24.51 dB), suggesting its urban-scene-specific design does not transfer well; EVPGS(RaDe-GS) surpasses it by 1.56 dB on DTU and 3.34 dB on Synthetic-NeRF. Among the GS backbones, integrating EVPGS consistently yields the largest absolute gains for weaker backbones (e.g., 3DGS gains +0.91 dB on DTU) while still improving the strongest baseline RaDe-GS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-view reconstruction line of work (Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS, MVPGS, LM-Gaussian) that generates view priors via reprojection, but deliberately shifts from the interpolated-view setting to the harder Extrapolated View Synthesis (EVS) scenario where training and testing views have large pitch-angle disparity. It builds on DDM-based regularization (VEGS, DiffusionNeRF) for artifact removal and on standard reprojection for view prior generation, but identifies two gaps in prior view-prior pipelines—missing view-dependent color and occlusion corruption—and addresses them through a coarse-to-fine framework combining mesh-supervised geometry regularization, reverse-reprojection-based occlusion filtering, and frequency-domain blending. The framework is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated across five GS variants (3DGS, Mip-Splatting, 2DGS, GOF, RaDe-GS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVS addresses a practical gap in real-world capture (horizontal-only training views vs. desired elevated test views) that standard NVS benchmarks do not stress-test, and the released Merchandise3D dataset provides a dedicated real-world benchmark for this scenario. The occlusion-aware reprojection and frequency-domain refinement strategies are modular and could be adopted by other GS-based pipelines facing limited view coverage. Key open questions remain: the method does not model complex lighting (causing residual color discrepancies), and its reliance on a reconstructed mesh limits applicability to scenes where mesh extraction is unreliable; the authors explicitly flag mesh-free approaches as future work.
