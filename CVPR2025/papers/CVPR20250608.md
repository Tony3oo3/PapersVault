## Problem

Diffusion models learn a score function (gradient of log-probability) over image latent space, but the geometric structure this induces has not been formally exploited. No prior work has shown how to compute geodesics in this space where the Riemannian metric is inversely proportional to probability density, which would reveal how 'short' realistic image transitions are versus unrealistic ones, and enable principled training-free interpolation and extrapolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250608/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For interpolation (BVP), the method takes a pair of RGB images and their corresponding text prompts (auto-generated by BLIP, then CLIP-encoded), and produces a 17-frame image sequence (15 intermediate frames) by solving for the geodesic in the 4×64×64 Stable Diffusion v2.1-base latent space. For extrapolation (IVP), it takes a single image with a source prompt and a target prompt, and generates a forward-evolving image sequence. Beyond the image sequence, the method also outputs the geodesic path in latent space, the relative log-probability at each point along the path, and the geodesic distance between endpoints. All operations are training-free: the pre-trained diffusion model is queried but never fine-tuned.

## Method

The core insight is that the Stein score function estimated by a diffusion model defines a spatially-varying inner product K(γ) = p(γ)^{-2}I, making the induced norm inversely proportional to probability density so that geodesics preferentially traverse high-density (realistic) regions. The Euler-Lagrange equations yield a nonlinear second-order ODE (Eq. 4) coupling acceleration to the log-probability gradient, which is solved via projected gradient descent on the functional derivative for the BVP or via RK4 for the IVP. To handle the 16,384-dimensional latent space efficiently, the path is parameterized as spherical piecewise-linear great-circle arcs with a coarse-to-fine bisection strategy (1→3→7→15 control points), and all updates are projected onto the sphere and its tangent space to stay within the high-density annulus predicted by the Gaussian Annulus Theorem. Score estimation in low-density regions uses noise-free score distillation at a non-zero diffusion timestep (τ=600) with classifier-free guidance, and conditional probability is handled by linearly interpolating CLIP text embeddings along the path parameter.

## Results

On the image interpolation task, the paper's training-free geodesic BVP solver achieves the best perceptual smoothness (PDV) across all three datasets: 0.016 on Animals & Humans, 0.022 on MorphBench, and 0.026 on Web, outperforming all baselines including fine-tuned methods. It also achieves competitive perceptual path length (PPL) of 0.960/0.921/1.565 and FID of 33.87/46.51/134.68, comparable to the best training-free baselines. The unoptimized great-circle baseline ('Ours w/o opt.') achieves the best FID among training-free methods (24.71 on AH), while the optimized geodesic trades a small FID increase for substantially lower PDV, confirming that geodesic optimization improves transition smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's optimized geodesic method ('Ours') achieves the best PDV (perceptual smoothness) on all three datasets, beating the closest competitor DiffMorpher (0.018/0.016/0.024) by a clear margin, and substantially outperforming training-free baselines NoiseDiffusion (0.117/0.111/0.099) and AID (0.181/0.188/0.208). On PPL (directness), the unoptimized great-circle baseline ('Ours w/o opt.') is best among all methods (0.874/0.841/1.473), while the optimized geodesic is slightly higher (0.960/0.921/1.565) but still competitive with SmoothDiffusion (0.903/0.879/1.371). On FID (fidelity to input distribution), fine-tuned methods IMPUS (25.75/36.33/89.73) and DiffMorpher (32.89/42.39/160.69) lead, with the unoptimized baseline matching or beating them on AH and MB; the optimized geodesic (33.87/46.51/134.68) is competitive but slightly worse. On TOPIQ (image quality), training-free baselines NoiseDiffusion and AID score highest (0.700/0.699 on AH), while the paper's method (0.607) is mid-pack, trailing DiffMorpher (0.686) and IMPUS (0.622). Overall, the method's key advantage is superior transition smoothness without any fine-tuning, at the cost of slightly lower per-frame image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper connects score-based diffusion models to Riemannian geometry by showing that the Stein score function (which diffusion models estimate) defines a natural Riemannian metric on latent space, where the norm is inversely proportional to probability density. It builds on the noise-free score distillation framework of Katzir et al. to handle out-of-distribution latents during path optimization, and on DDIM inversion for deterministic forward/backward diffusion. In the image interpolation literature, it departs from prior diffusion-based methods (DiffMorpher, IMPUS, SmoothDiffusion) by being entirely training-free and by grounding the interpolation in a variational principle (geodesic minimization) rather than learned or heuristic blending strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framing—treating diffusion latent space as a Riemannian manifold whose geometry is determined by the learned data distribution—provides a principled lens for understanding how generative models structure their output space, and the finding that simple video clips approximate geodesics offers a concrete, testable hypothesis about the geometry of learned representations. However, the method has notable limitations: it struggles with large camera motions and domain gaps (likely due to local optima from great-circle initialization), the score approximation via noise-free distillation is not guaranteed to be well-aligned with the true log-probability gradient, and the IVP-based extrapolation is unreliable due to the difficulty of choosing good initial velocities. Open questions include whether global search strategies or richer path representations could overcome the local-optimum issue, and whether the geodesic framework extends naturally to video diffusion models or multi-modal conditioning.
