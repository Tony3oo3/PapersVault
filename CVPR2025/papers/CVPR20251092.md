## Problem

Previous stochastic long-term dense anticipation methods (notably GTDA) rely on dilated temporal convolutions whose early layers have limited receptive fields—preventing observed frame information from reaching zero-padded future entries—and whose later layers have sparse receptive fields that restrict updates. These limitations are especially severe for long videos with extended anticipation horizons, where the future-padded portion of the input becomes difficult to process effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251092/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes pre-extracted per-frame visual features (I3D for Breakfast/50Salads, TSM for Assembly101) from P observed frames, zero-pads them to length P+F to form a conditioning vector, and concatenates this with noisy latent action variables along the channel dimension. It outputs per-frame one-hot action class predictions for all P+F frames (both past and future), generating S=25 stochastic samples per observation via DDIM sampling with D<T diffusion steps. The structured input pattern—observed features followed by zeros—requires the model to adaptively distinguish valid observations from masked future positions.

## Method

MANTA replaces GTDA's multi-stage dilated convolutional generator with a single-stage stack of 15 MANTA blocks, each containing a Bidirectional Selective State-Space Layer (BSSL) built on Mamba's S6 layer. The BSSL processes the sequence in both forward and backward directions through S6 sub-branches, each applying input-dependent SSM matrices (Δ, B̄, Ā, C) that act as data-driven gates controlling information flow between observed and masked regions. This selective mechanism provides a locally-aware global receptive field with linear complexity in sequence length, eliminating the need for multi-stage architectures. The full MANTA block applies LayerNorm → BSSL → feedforward with a residual connection, and the network is trained with standard diffusion L2 reconstruction loss on one-hot encoded action sequences.

## Results

On Breakfast, MANTA achieves 34.2 Mean MoC and 59.6 Top-1 MoC (α=0.3, β=0.1), outperforming GTDA (29.1 / 54.0) and DiffAnt (30.9 / 37.4). On 50Salads, it reaches 31.3 Mean MoC and 71.7 Top-1 MoC (α=0.3, β=0.1) vs. GTDA's 29.9 / 66.2. On Assembly101, MANTA attains 6.6 Mean MoC (α=0.3, β=0.1), slightly exceeding GTDA's 5.9, though Top-1 MoC is marginally lower (15.6 vs. 16.0). Beyond accuracy, MANTA achieves a 65.3× inference speedup and 6.6× training speedup over GTDA, with 2.8× fewer parameters (1.4M vs. 3.9M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MANTA outperforms GTDA [56] (the prior SOTA) on Breakfast across all settings, with the largest gains at longer horizons (e.g., +3.5 Mean MoC at α=0.3, β=0.5). On 50Salads, MANTA sets new SOTA for Mean MoC at all settings and for Top-1 MoC at α=0.3, though it trails GTDA by 1.3 points on Top-1 MoC at α=0.2, β=0.1 (68.3 vs. 69.6). On Assembly101, MANTA wins on Mean MoC for all settings but is slightly behind GTDA on Top-1 MoC at β=0.1 (15.6 vs. 16.0 for α=0.3; 16.9 vs. 18.0 for α=0.2). Compared to DiffAnt [64] on Breakfast, MANTA leads by a wide margin (e.g., +3.3 Mean MoC and +22.2 Top-1 MoC at α=0.3, β=0.1). Against the windowed Transformer baselines [5] (Transf.15 and Transf.18) in ablation, MANTA achieves 1.1–2.4% higher Mean/Top-1 MoC while being 16.5× faster in inference (1.1 s vs. 18.2 s per 25-sample generation). Overall, MANTA is the only method that simultaneously leads on both accuracy and efficiency across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends GTDA (Zatsarynna et al., ECCV), which introduced a gated temporal diffusion (GTAN) generator using multi-stage dilated temporal convolutions for stochastic long-term dense anticipation. The key architectural departure is replacing the multi-stage dilated convolutional backbone with a single-stage Mamba-based network (MANTA) built on the Bidirectional Selective State-Space Layer (BSSL), which inherits the data-dependent gating of S6 while maintaining a dense global receptive field at linear complexity. The diffusion formulation for joint past/future action modelling is retained from GTDA, and the bidirectional scanning design follows VideoMamba and Vision Mamba. The paper also positions itself against attention-based alternatives (windowed Transformers) to demonstrate that SSMs offer both superior efficiency and competitive accuracy for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 65× inference speedup and 6.6× training speedup over GTDA, combined with 2.8× fewer parameters, make stochastic long-term anticipation substantially more practical for real-world deployment in settings like autonomous driving and assembly-line automation. The results suggest that the multi-stage dilated-convolution design was an unnecessary architectural overhead, and that a single linear-complexity SSM backbone can handle the long-range temporal dependencies inherent in minutes-long anticipation horizons. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to end-to-end learning with raw video (the paper relies on pre-extracted I3D/TSM features), whether the bidirectional component remains critical at even longer sequence lengths, and whether the SSM-based generator can be extended to other stochastic prediction tasks beyond action anticipation.
