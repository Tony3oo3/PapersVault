## Problem

State-of-the-art video generation models struggle to produce coherent human body movements, and existing pose-guided animation methods (e.g., Animate Anyone, Animate-X) rely on poses extracted from existing videos, limiting flexibility. No prior work provides a unified pipeline that generates diverse, text-controlled human poses and then converts them into high-quality motion videos without requiring pre-existing pose references.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252672/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt describing a human action and an initial RGB frame image as input. The Text-to-Pose stage outputs a 64-frame 2D pose sequence of 128 keypoints per frame (body, face, hands) encoded as x, y coordinates plus confidence scores (f×128×3). The Pose-to-Video stage then consumes this pose sequence together with the initial frame to synthesize a human-motion video. All sequences are cropped to a fixed 64-frame length, and the pose VAE uses a downsampling factor of 8 to produce a compact latent representation.

## Method

HumanDreamer decouples generation into two stages: (1) MotionDiT, a Diffusion Transformer that generates structured 2D poses from text, and (2) a CogVideoX-based Pose-to-Video model. MotionDiT incorporates a local feature aggregation module (1D ResNet with kernel 3 + spatial self-attention) to capture adjacent-joint correlations, and a global attention block applied at the network's middle layer that reshapes the latent to (f×n)×c and performs self-attention across all frames and keypoints simultaneously. A novel LAMA loss aligns MotionDiT's intermediate latent features with CLoP (Contrastive Language-Motion Pretraining) pose embeddings, a 2D extension of TMR, thereby strengthening text-pose semantic alignment in latent space. The overall training objective combines standard diffusion noise-prediction MSE with the LAMA alignment term weighted by λ_f. The entire pipeline is trained on MotionVid, a 1.2M text-pose pair dataset built from 10M+ raw videos through a multi-stage cleaning pipeline (video quality, human quality, and caption quality filters).

## Results

On the MotionVid subset (50K samples), HumanDreamer achieves a FID of 149.007, a 62.4% improvement over the strongest baseline MLD (396.949). R-precision scores reach 0.451 (top-1), 0.638 (top-2), and 0.743 (top-3), representing 41.8%, 26.3%, and 18.3% gains over MLD respectively. The method also attains the highest diversity (68.220) and lowest MM Distance (32.761), indicating both semantic alignment with text prompts and output variety. Scaling to the full 1.25M dataset further improves top-1 R-precision to 0.513 and reduces MM Distance to 30.139.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HumanDreamer outperforms all three 3D motion generation baselines (T2M-GPT, PriorMDM, MLD) across every metric in Table 1, with the largest margin over MLD (the strongest baseline): FID drops from 396.9 to 149.0 and top-1 R-precision rises from 0.318 to 0.451. T2M-GPT and PriorMDM, which were originally designed for 3D motion and converted to 2D poses, lag substantially behind, with FID values above 490 and top-1 R-precision below 0.18. For the downstream Pose-to-Video task, qualitative comparisons (Fig. 5) show that HumanDreamer produces more coherent, dynamic human-motion videos with better facial detail and motion continuity compared to SOTA text-to-video models Mochi1 and CogVideoX, which exhibit body distortion and weak motion continuity. The paper notes that Tender [55], the only prior 2D text-to-pose method, could not be included in quantitative comparison due to unavailable code and dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture with a global attention block and local feature aggregation module specifically for 2D human-motion pose generation, and adapts the contrastive alignment paradigm from TMR (3D motion-text retrieval) into a 2D CLoP module. For the Pose-to-Video stage, it repurposes CogVideoX as a backbone with pose conditioning, following the pose-guided video generation line of work (Animate Anyone, Animate-X, Mimic Motion). The key conceptual departure from prior pose-guided methods is the decoupled pipeline: rather than requiring poses extracted from existing videos, the framework first synthesizes poses from text via MotionDiT, thereby removing the dependency on pre-defined motion references. The LAMA loss, which aligns intermediate MotionDiT features with CLoP embeddings, draws on the broader trend of using intermediate representations to improve diffusion model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled Text-to-Pose → Pose-to-Video paradigm offers a practical path to flexible human-motion video generation without requiring motion-capture data or pre-existing pose sequences, which is a meaningful limitation of current pose-guided methods. The 1.2M-sample MotionVid dataset with its multi-stage cleaning pipeline is a significant resource for the community, and the scaling-law results suggest further gains are achievable. However, the Pose-to-Video component is largely a wrapper around CogVideoX, and the Text-to-Pose evaluation relies on 3D motion generation baselines (T2M-GPT, MLD, PriorMDM) converted to 2D, which weakens the comparative evidence. Open questions include whether the decoupled approach generalizes to multi-person scenes, longer sequences, and whether the CLoP alignment can be replaced by stronger pre-trained motion encoders as they become available.
