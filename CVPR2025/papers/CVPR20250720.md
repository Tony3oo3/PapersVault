## Problem

Existing video analysis benchmarks (VideoMME, LongVideoBench) rely on pre-defined multiple-choice questions that diverge from how real users actually interact with video, while human-driven arena platforms like WildVision Video Arena are prohibitively slow for video tasks—yielding only 256 votes across 11 models in six months at roughly 7 annotations per hour. This leaves no scalable, user-centric evaluation framework that can rigorously assess LMMs on open-ended, adaptive video questions at arena-level scale.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 2,881 videos from LongVideoBench (average 479 s, spanning four duration buckets from 8 s to 3,600 s), uniformly sampled to 64 frames for model response generation and 128 frames for judging, with audio tracks converted to subtitles since most evaluated LMMs lack native audio support. Each battle pairs a video with a persona-conditioned open-ended question (generated by a simulated user with one of three background-relevance levels) and two anonymous model responses. The primary outputs are dynamic ELO ratings and win rates per model across video-length categories (from 12,479 head-to-head battles), supplemented by per-criterion scores (instruction following, accuracy, relevance, helpfulness) and, in VideoAutoBench, a single scalar score per model computed by comparing its answers against human-selected and human-rejected reference responses.

## Method

VideoAutoArena comprises four coupled components: (1) video content-constrained persona generation, where an LMM agent identifies three user archetypes (highly relevant, moderately relevant, unrelated) for each video and role-plays them to produce persona-specific open-ended questions; (2) peer battles in Chatbot Arena style, where two randomly drawn models answer the same video-question pair; (3) automatic judging by GPT-4o against four explicit criteria (instruction following, accuracy, relevance, helpfulness), validated at 87.29 % agreement with a 300-battle human gold standard; and (4) fault-driven question evolution, in which a response-analysis agent extracts specific faults from the losing response, a role-play agent synthesizes a targeted harder question, and a complexity-evaluation agent retains the new question only if its GPT-4o-scored difficulty exceeds the previous one. Rankings are computed via Online ELO updates refined by Bradley-Terry maximum-likelihood estimation over all pairwise comparisons. The auxiliary VideoAutoBench distills a subset of human-validated battles into a fixed reference set, allowing any model to be scored in a single pass against GPT-4o-judged comparisons with the selected and rejected answers, reproducing the arena ranking at a fraction of the compute cost.

## Results

Across 12,479 head-to-head battles on 2,881 videos (avg. 479s), GPT-4o achieves the highest ELO rating of 1505.69 with an 89.19% win rate, while the top open-source model Aria (8×3.5B) scores 1119.99, yielding a gap of −385.7 ELO points. The automatic judge (GPT-4o) achieves 87.29% agreement with human-annotated preferences on a 300-battle gold standard. In the auxiliary VideoAutoBench, GPT-4o scores 82.55 (avg.) versus 48.33 for Aria and 17.26 for LLaVA-OneVision-7B, confirming the same ranking order as the full arena.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates all proprietary and open-source baselines, leading Aria (top open-source) by 385.7 ELO points and Gemini-1.5-Pro by 318.7 points; the gap widens to over 900 ELO points against the weakest model (LLaVA-OneVision-7B). On short videos (15–60s), the gap narrows considerably—Aria (1273.77) even surpasses Gemini-1.5-Pro (1171.82)—but as video length increases beyond 180s, proprietary models pull ahead again (e.g., Gemini-1.5-Flash gains ~180 ELO from short to long videos while Qwen2-VL drops over 100). After fault-driven evolution introduces harder questions, all open-source models except Aria show further score declines, while proprietary models (except GPT-4o) improve, indicating that open-source models are more sensitive to increased question complexity. On the user-centric judging dimensions of helpfulness and relevance, the proprietary–open-source gap is most pronounced, suggesting open-source models are over-optimized for ability-centric multiple-choice benchmarks like VideoMME. In VideoAutoBench, GPT-4o scores 70.98 vs. human-selected answers (vs. 19.80 for Aria and 3.53 for LLaVA-OneVision-7B), confirming the arena ranking with a simpler single-model evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the LMSYS Chatbot Arena framework—pairwise battles, ELO ratings, and Bradley-Terry estimation—to the video domain, replacing human annotators with LMM-based user simulation (video content-constrained persona generation) to overcome the scalability bottleneck of video annotation. For question difficulty escalation, it modifies Evol-Instruct's heuristic evolution into a fault-driven strategy where new questions are generated from analysis of prior model battle failures, creating an adaptive rather than template-based progression. It departs from ability-centric multiple-choice video benchmarks (VideoMME, LongVideoBench, MVBench) by introducing open-ended, user-persona-driven questions, and from WildVision Video Arena by automating both question generation and judging to achieve 12,479 battles versus WildVision's 256.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantially larger performance gap between closed-source (GPT-4o) and open-source LMMs (−385.7 ELO) than multiple-choice benchmarks show (<10% in VideoMME), suggesting that user-centric evaluation exposes weaknesses in helpfulness and contextual relevance that ability-centric tests mask. This has direct implications for how the community should interpret video LMM progress and where to focus development. Open questions include whether GPT-4o-as-judge introduces circularity bias favoring OpenAI models, whether 64-frame sampling adequately captures temporal dynamics in 3600-second videos, and whether the fault-driven evolution converges or simply cycles through a finite set of failure modes.
