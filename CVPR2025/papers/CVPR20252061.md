## Problem

Monocular depth priors are widely used to supervise multi-view neural rendering, but they suffer from inherent inaccuracy and cross-view inconsistency that current methods ignore by treating the entire depth map as uniform ground truth. Existing uncertainty estimation approaches either rely on MVS-style projection (hindered by occlusions) or couple uncertainty prediction to the rendering branch (compromised by rendering quality), leaving a gap in robustly identifying where monocular priors are unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed RGB images and their corresponding monocular depth maps (from any pre-trained estimator such as Omnidata, Metric3D v2, or GeoWizard) and produces per-pixel, per-view uncertainty maps that highlight regions where depth priors are inconsistent across views. These uncertainty maps are then consumed by the host rendering framework to modulate depth/normal losses, guide ray sampling, and enforce instance-mask alignment. The method operates at the instance-object level via multi-view consistent segmentation, and is designed as a plug-in module compatible with both NeRF-based (MonoSDF, NeuSurf) and 3DGS-based (FSGS, COR-GS) pipelines without modifying their core architectures.

## Method

MonoInstance estimates monocular depth uncertainty by back-projecting segmented instance depths from all views into a shared 3D space and measuring local point density via ball queries: consistent (accurate) depths cluster tightly on the true surface yielding high density, while inconsistent (noisy) depths scatter anisotropically along viewing directions yielding low density, which is inverted to produce per-pixel uncertainty maps. Three strategies exploit these maps during training: (1) an adaptive depth/normal loss that down-weights supervision in high-uncertainty regions, (2) uncertainty-guided ray sampling that allocates more rays to uncertain areas, and (3) an instance mask constraint that projects sampled points from high-uncertainty rays into nearby views and enforces alignment with the corresponding instance mask, implicitly constraining geometry to object surfaces. Training is two-stage: a coarse pass with uniform monocular priors to establish scale alignment, followed by an uncertainty-aware refinement pass integrating all three strategies. The approach is framework-agnostic and requires no additional learnable parameters beyond the host model.

## Results

On dense-view 3D reconstruction, MonoInstance achieves the best F-score of 0.834 on ScanNet (vs. 0.799 for H2O-SDF) and the best Normal Consistency of 0.937 on Replica. On sparse-view DTU reconstruction, it reduces Chamfer Distance to 1.18, outperforming the best baseline NeuSurf† (1.30) by 9.2%. On sparse novel view synthesis (LLFF, 3 views), it attains PSNR 20.73, SSIM 0.731, and LPIPS 0.184, surpassing the strongest baseline COR-GS (20.45 / 0.712 / 0.196) across all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanNet, MonoInstance outperforms all six baselines on every metric, with the largest gap over MonoSDF (F-score 0.834 vs. 0.733) and a clear lead over the strongest competitor H2O-SDF (0.834 vs. 0.799). On Replica, it achieves the best accuracy (0.024) and Normal Consistency (0.937), though HybridNeRF marginally edges it on F-score (0.921 vs. 0.918). For sparse-view DTU reconstruction, MonoInstance (CD 1.18) beats NeuSurf† (1.30), NeuSurf (1.35), UFORecon (1.43), and all generalizable methods (COLMAP 2.61, ReTR 2.65, VolRecon 3.02, SparseNeuS 3.34). On LLFF novel view synthesis, it surpasses COR-GS (PSNR 20.73 vs. 20.45), FSGS (20.73 vs. 20.31), DNGaussian (20.73 vs. 19.12), and vanilla 3DGS (20.73 vs. 15.52), while also improving over NeRF-based baselines RegNeRF and FreeNeRF (both 19.08 PSNR). The ablation in Table 5 further shows consistent gains across three different monocular depth models (Omnidata, Metric3D v2, GeoWizard), confirming the method's model-agnostic nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoInstance builds directly on MonoSDF, which established the use of monocular depth priors as supervision for neural implicit surface reconstruction, and on NeuSurf for sparse-view settings. It departs from prior uncertainty-aware methods (DebSDF, NC-SDF) that either couple uncertainty prediction within the rendering pipeline or rely on multi-view projection that suffers from occlusions, by instead estimating uncertainty externally through instance-level multi-view depth alignment and point-cloud density measurement. The approach is positioned as a general, plug-and-play enhancement strategy applicable to both NeRF-based and 3DGS-based frameworks, rather than a new rendering architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in multi-view neural rendering: monocular priors are widely adopted but their cross-view inconsistency and per-view inaccuracy degrade reconstruction quality, especially in thin or complex structures. The framework-agnostic design (validated on MonoSDF, NeuSurf, and 3DGS pipelines) makes it broadly applicable, and the instance-level uncertainty estimation avoids the occlusion problems of projection-based methods. Open questions include scalability to outdoor or dynamic scenes where instance segmentation is less reliable, and whether the density-based uncertainty measure generalizes to scenes without clear object boundaries. The reliance on pre-trained segmentation models (GroundedSAM, MaskClustering) introduces an additional dependency that could limit robustness.
