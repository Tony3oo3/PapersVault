## Problem

Learning view-invariant representations that bridge the extreme visual gap between egocentric and exocentric video has been limited by reliance on costly, time-synchronized paired data or unpaired data with weak alignment signals (temporal cues, partial semantic overlap). This paper asks how to unlock large-scale, in-the-wild unpaired ego and exo video collections for view-invariant representation learning, enabling cross-view retrieval, recognition, and skill assessment at a scale previously inaccessible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252840/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes ego video clips (~1 s, 4 frames sampled), exo video clips, and associated text narrations (from ASR or video captioning) as inputs. Paired training data comes from Ego-Exo4D (~850k synchronized pairs), while unpaired ego data (562k clips from Ego4D) and unpaired exo data (~1.25M clips from HTM-AA/HowTo100M) are aligned via the RST. The output is a shared view-invariant feature space from a dual video-text encoder, which supports downstream cross-view retrieval (HR@k over 2,936 ego / 5,872 exo videos), 188-way keystep action recognition, and binary skill-level classification.

## Method

The core mechanism is a diffusion-based Rosetta Stone Translator (RST) built on a DiT architecture, trained on time-synchronized paired ego-exo video to learn a feature-space mapping from ego to exo representations using a DDPM denoising objective conditioned on the ego latent. At inference, the RST hallucinates a synthetic exo feature for any unpaired ego clip, which—concatenated with a BERT text embedding—serves as a query to mine pseudo-pairs from a large exo candidate pool via cosine similarity. A dual-encoder (TimeSformer video encoder with CLIP-initialized spatial attention + 12-layer Transformer text encoder) is then trained with a three-term contrastive objective: a video-video loss weighted by narration similarity (soft alignment to tolerate imperfect pseudo-pair matches), a video-text loss anchoring both views to shared semantics, and a video-synthetic loss that directly aligns each ego feature with its RST-generated exo counterpart, providing an explicit cross-view augmentation absent in prior approaches.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D cross-view benchmark, VIEWPOINTROSETTA achieves 52.68% average HR@5 for cross-view retrieval (58.14% ego→exo, 47.21% exo→ego), 34.47% top-1 and 64.85% top-5 accuracy for cross-view action recognition (188-way keystep), and 55.82% accuracy for cross-view skill assessment. These are the best scores across all three tasks, with the largest margins over the next-best baseline (SUM-L) on retrieval (+12.72 avg HR@5) and recognition (+9.64 top-1, +12.77 top-5). The skill assessment margin is smaller (+0.72 over SUM-L), which the authors attribute to the inherent difficulty of the task where most methods perform only slightly above random chance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIEWPOINTROSETTA outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three tasks. Against the strongest unpaired view-invariant baseline SUM-L [41], it gains +12.72 avg HR@5 on retrieval, +9.64 top-1 and +12.77 top-5 on recognition, and +0.72 on skill assessment. Against the best general video representation model LaViLa* [50] (which also has access to the same unpaired data), the margins are even larger: +19.90 avg HR@5, +8.96 top-1, and +10.32 top-5. Paired-only methods (VI Encoder [17], ActorObserverNet [36]) lag substantially, with the VI Encoder achieving only 26.97 avg HR@5 versus 52.68 for the proposed method, underscoring the value of unlocking unpaired data. The smallest margin is on skill assessment (+0.72 over SUM-L), which the authors note is near the ceiling since most methods hover around 54–56% on this inherently difficult binary task. The scaling analysis (Figure 5) further shows that VIEWPOINTROSETTA's advantage grows with unpaired data volume, while SUM-L and Random Align plateau earlier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ego-exo view-invariant representation learning from the paired-data-only regime (ActorObserverNet, VI Encoder) and the limited unpaired-data regime (SUM-L, EgoInstructor, which rely on language semantics for alignment) to a setting that unlocks large-scale unpaired video. The core architectural departure is repurposing a diffusion model (DDPM formulation with a DiT denoising backbone) not for pixel-space generation but as a feature-space translator trained on synchronized pairs, which then hallucinates cross-view features for unpaired samples. The contrastive training objective is also novel in its combination of soft alignment (text-similarity-weighted InfoNCE) and synthetic-feature augmentation, going beyond the standard video-text or video-video contrastive losses used in prior ego-exo work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by showing that a moderate amount of paired data can 'unlock' orders of magnitude more unpaired video, the paper opens a path to leveraging Internet-scale exocentric collections (HowTo100M, YouTube) for egocentric understanding, which is critical for AR guidance and robot learning from video. The new cross-view benchmark (retrieval, recognition, skill assessment) fills a gap left by Ego-Exo4D's ego-centric evaluation design. Open questions include whether the RST generalizes beyond the ego-exo pair to other viewpoint combinations, how much paired data is truly needed as the 'Rosetta Stone,' and whether the approach extends to longer-form or multi-action videos where single-clip alignment becomes insufficient.
