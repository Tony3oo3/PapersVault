## Problem

Diffusion-based adversarial purification methods like DiffPure require dozens to hundreds of denoising steps, incurring ~9–11 seconds per image and making real-time defense impractical. A deeper issue is that adversarial perturbations follow a distribution fundamentally different from the Gaussian noise that diffusion models are trained to remove, so naively applying consistency distillation to a pre-trained diffusion model violates the consistency property and degrades purification quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251169/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single adversarial RGB image (512×512 for ImageNet, 1024×1024 for CelebA-HQ), encodes it to latent space, adds forward noise at a user-chosen strength t* (empirically 200 for ImageNet), and produces one purified RGB image after a single U-Net forward pass (~0.1 s on an NVIDIA F40 GPU). The purified image is intended for downstream classification (ResNet, ViT, Swin) or face-recognition (ArcFace, FaceNet, MobileFaceNet) without any text prompt; the only conditioning signal is a non-learnable Canny edge map computed from the input.

## Method

OSCP combines two components. First, Gaussian Adversarial Noise Distillation (GAND) reformulates the LCM consistency objective by injecting latent-space adversarial noise δ_adv into the forward diffusion term (z_t* = √ᾱ_t·z + √(1−ᾱ_t)(ε+δ_adv)), so the model learns to denoise a shifted distribution that simultaneously contains Gaussian and adversarial noise; training uses LoRA on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with a Clear Image Guide (CIG) auxiliary loss and restricts the consistency constraint to the first half of timesteps. Second, Controlled Adversarial Purification (CAP) is the inference pipeline that feeds a Canny edge map through ControlNet as a non-learnable visual prompt and removes the c_skip(t)·z_t skip term from the LCM to prevent residual adversarial noise from persisting in the output. Together these yield a single-step purification that achieves 74.19% robust accuracy on ImageNet against AutoAttack while running ~100× faster than multi-step baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet, OSCP achieves 74.19% robust accuracy against AutoAttack (ResNet-50) and 73.89% against untargeted PGD-100, while maintaining 77.63% standard accuracy. Against random-targeted PGD-40 on ResNet-152, robust accuracy reaches 78.78%. Critically, OSCP completes purification in ~0.1s per image on an NVIDIA F40 GPU, a 100-fold speedup over GDMP (~9s) and DiffPure (~11s), with inference time independent of the purification strength t*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest diffusion-based purification baselines, OSCP consistently wins: it outperforms GDMP [44] by 0.92% on PGD-100 (73.89% vs. 72.97%) and by 0.92% on targeted PGD-40 (78.78% vs. 77.86%), and beats DiffPure [32] by 1.17% on AutoAttack (74.19% vs. 73.02%). Against adversarial training methods (MeanSparse [1] at 59.64% and Singh et al. [38] at 57.70% robust accuracy on AutoAttack), OSCP leads by over 14 percentage points, demonstrating that purification-based defense substantially outperforms training-based approaches on robust accuracy. On the face recognition task (CelebA-HQ), OSCP dramatically surpasses Shield [7], achieving 86.8% vs. 17.3% on ArcFace, 97.8% vs. 84.1% on FaceNet, and 84.9% vs. 27.6% on MobileFaceNet under targeted PGD-10. The most striking advantage is computational: OSCP purifies in ~0.1s regardless of t*, while GDMP and DiffPure require ~9s and ~11s respectively, enabling real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-based adversarial purification (DiffPure, GDMP) and fast inference via consistency distillation (LCM, LCM-LoRA). The key conceptual departure is identifying a theoretical disharmony: standard consistency distillation assumes Gaussian noise, but adversarial perturbations create a shifted latent distribution that violates the LCM consistency property. GAND resolves this by injecting adversarial noise into the forward diffusion process during distillation, while CAP repurposes ControlNet with non-learnable Canny edges as a robust alternative to text-based guidance. The framework is trained via LoRA (PEFT) on Stable Diffusion v1.5, making it a lightweight modification of the LCM-LoRA pipeline rather than a new architecture from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× speedup (0.1s vs. ~9–11s per image) makes diffusion-based adversarial purification viable for real-time deployment in safety-critical settings like autonomous driving and video streams, a regime where prior multi-step methods were impractical. Cross-domain transfer (ImageNet-trained GAND weights applied to CelebA-HQ face recognition) suggests the learned purification is not overfit to a single classifier. Open questions include whether adaptive purification strength (t*) could further improve the accuracy–fidelity tradeoff, whether the c_skip removal generalizes beyond the tested architectures, and why ViT-b-16 robustness (71.6%) lags behind CNNs, hinting at architecture-specific vulnerabilities in the purification pipeline.
