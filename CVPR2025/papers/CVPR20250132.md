## Problem

Existing diffusion-based neural steganography methods (BadDiffusion, TrojDiff, WDM) embed secret images by modifying the entire reverse diffusion chain, resulting in poor extraction accuracy (PSNR ≤ 25 dB), severe model fidelity degradation (FID increase > 100%), and high computational cost (≥ 10 GPU hours). The root cause is the entanglement of hiding and extraction with multiple denoising steps, which disrupts the original generative process and demands full model retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a complex natural image (32×32 or 256×256 RGB, sourced from COCO, DIV2K, LSUN Church, or Places), a privately shared secret key K_s = {k_s, t_s} where k_s is a deterministic seed producing Gaussian noise z_s and t_s is the chosen embedding timestep, and a pre-trained pixel-space DDPM. It produces a stego diffusion model that is publicly shareable and a single-step extraction function that recovers the secret image from z_s at timestep t_s. For multi-recipient scenarios, M independent keys yield M independently extractable images from the same stego model.

## Method

The core insight is to localize the intervention to a single timestep t_s in the reverse diffusion process rather than modifying the entire Markov chain. The secret image is embedded by editing the learned score function ε_θ at t_s so that a single-step denoising operation f_θ(z_s, t_s) = (1/√ᾱ_{t_s})(z_s − √(1−ᾱ_{t_s})·ε_θ(z_s, t_s)) reconstructs x_s. Training minimizes a combined loss: an extraction accuracy term (L2 reconstruction error at t_s) plus a model fidelity term (expected squared residual between the edited and original score functions over uniformly sampled timesteps, clean images, and noise), which eliminates the need for the original training dataset. To further improve fidelity and efficiency, a hybrid PEFT strategy first identifies the most sensitive layers via accumulated squared-gradient analysis, then applies LoRA (with rank stabilization and learning-rate decoupling) only to those layers, reducing trainable parameters by 86.3% versus full fine-tuning.

## Results

On 32×32 CIFAR-10 images, the method achieves 52.90 dB PSNR and 0.001 LPIPS for secret image extraction, outperforming the best prior method (Chen22 at 47.72 dB) by over 5 dB. On 256×256 LSUN Bedroom images, it reaches 39.33 dB PSNR and 0.018 DISTS, surpassing the best baseline (PRIS at 37.42 dB). Model fidelity is preserved with FID of 4.77 vs. 4.79 (original) on CIFAR-10 and 8.39 vs. 7.46 on LSUN Bedroom, while embedding time drops to 0.04 and 0.18 GPU hours respectively—over 50× faster than the next-best method (WDM at 2.35 and 19.22 GPU hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines on extraction accuracy at both resolutions. Among autoencoder-based methods, PRIS [55] is the strongest at 256×256 (37.42 dB PSNR) but still trails by ~2 dB; at 32×32, HiNet [25] leads (30.39 dB) yet is surpassed by over 22 dB. The GAN-based Chen22 [5] is the closest competitor at 32×32 (47.72 dB) but degrades to 36.44 dB at 256×256, where it is outperformed by 2.89 dB. Among diffusion-based baselines, TrojDiff [6] achieves 46.54 dB at 32×32 but collapses to 24.74 dB at 256×256; BadDiffusion [7] and WDM [36] perform worst overall (≤22 dB at 32×32, ≤18 dB at 256×256). On model fidelity, the method maintains FID within 1.2% of the original on CIFAR-10 (4.77 vs. 4.79) and within 12.5% on LSUN Bedroom (8.39 vs. 7.46), whereas BadDiffusion, TrojDiff, and WDM all degrade FID by >100% at 256×256. On hiding efficiency, the method requires only 0.04/0.18 GPU hours, making it 59× faster than WDM (2.35/19.22) and 465× faster than TrojDiff (12.72/83.68).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural steganography paradigm from GANs (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2022) to diffusion models, but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based hiding methods (BadDiffusion, TrojDiff, WDM) that modify the entire reverse diffusion chain. Instead, it localizes the intervention to a single timestep by editing the learned score function, combining a key-guided one-step extraction loss with a fidelity loss that directly compares against the original score function. The parameter-efficient fine-tuning component is a hybrid of gradient-based sensitivity-aware layer selection (He et al., ICCV 2023) and LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022), adapted here for convolutional layers in pixel-space U-Nets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50×+ reduction in embedding time (0.04–0.18 GPU hours vs. 2–84 hours for prior methods) while simultaneously improving extraction accuracy and preserving FID makes diffusion-based neural steganography practically deployable for the first time. The single-timestep design also naturally supports multi-recipient scenarios through independent extraction keys, addressing a long-standing limitation. Open questions include robustness against model perturbations (pruning, compression, quantization), theoretical upper bounds on detectability, and whether the approach generalizes to latent-space diffusion models like Stable Diffusion at scale.
