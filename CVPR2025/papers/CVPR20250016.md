## Problem

Existing MLLMs that incorporate multi-layer visual features rely on arbitrary layer selection (e.g., proportional splits or the latter half of encoder layers) and heterogeneous fusion architectures, with no systematic study of which layers to select or how to fuse them. This paper addresses two questions: how to select the most effective visual encoder layers, and which fusion strategy (position and pattern) best integrates multi-layer visual features with the LLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input. The image is encoded by a 24-layer ViT (CLIP-ViT-L/14 or SigLIP), from which features are extracted at multiple specific layers (e.g., 3rd, 18th, 23rd) rather than only the final layer. These multi-layer visual features are fused with the text tokens and fed into a compact LLM (1.4B MobileLLaMA or 2.7B MobileLLaMA), which produces a generated text response. The key design variable is which subset of encoder layers contributes features and at what point in the LLM (input stage vs. intermediate layers) they are injected.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-axis framework for multi-layer visual feature fusion: layer selection (similarity-based, dividing encoder layers into beginning/middle/ending stages by representational similarity, vs. proportion-based former/latter/all splits) and fusion strategy (position: external at input vs. internal at intermediate LLM layers; pattern: direct addition/concatenation vs. modular cross-attention). Using a lightweight Mini-LLaVA testbed (MobileLLaMA 1.4B + CLIP-ViT-L/14), they exhaustively evaluate all combinations across six layer sets and four fusion architectures. The key findings are: (1) selecting one representative layer from each similarity stage (beginning + middle + ending) yields the strongest generalization, while stacking multiple layers from the same stage degrades performance; (2) external direct fusion (simple element-wise addition or dimension concatenation at the input stage) consistently outperforms all other configurations, requiring only a single projector per layer set and no additional modules; (3) internal fusion shows greater sensitivity to layer choice but benefits more from larger training datasets, making it a viable alternative when data is abundant.

## Results

The best-performing configuration, External Direct Fusion with all layers (E+D, All), achieves an average score of 49.88 across seven benchmarks, improving over the Mini-LLaVA baseline (48.51) by 1.37 points. External Modular Fusion with Former layers (E+M, Former) reaches 49.78, while Internal Modular Fusion with Double layers (I+M, Double) and Internal Direct Fusion with Single layer (I+D, Single) achieve only 48.74 and 48.66 respectively. On individual benchmarks, E+D (All) scores 59.54 on GQA, 52.15 on MMBench, 38.01 on TextVQA, 255 on OCRBench, 44.78 on CV-Bench 2D, 53.08 on CV-Bench 3D, and 86.40 on POPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external MLLM baselines but instead ablates four fusion strategies (Internal Modular, Internal Direct, External Modular, External Direct) across six layer-selection sets on a shared Mini-LLaVA backbone. External Direct Fusion with all layers (49.88 avg) outperforms External Modular with Former layers (49.78) by 0.10 points, and both substantially exceed Internal Modular (48.74) and Internal Direct (48.66) by over 1 point. Within each strategy, layer selection matters: for Internal Modular, adding a third layer (Triple, 46.91) degrades performance by 1.83 points below Double (48.74), and using Latter layers alone collapses to 37.19. For External Direct, the All configuration (49.88) slightly edges out Single (49.87), showing that direct fusion is robust to the number of layers. Scaling experiments reveal that with 737k SFT data, Internal fusion strategies close the gap with External fusion, and replacing CLIP with SigLIP or upgrading to MobileLLaMA 2.7B lifts E+D from 49.18 to 52.63, confirming the scalability of the recommended recipe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture and the growing body of work incorporating multi-layer visual features into MLLMs (EVLM, Dense Connector, LION, DeepStack), but departs from these by replacing their ad hoc layer selection with two principled criteria—representational similarity and proportional depth—and by systematically comparing four fusion strategies (internal/external × modular/direct) under identical training conditions. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes existing fusion mechanisms (DeepStack's additive internal fusion, MMFuser's external modular fusion, Dense Connector's concatenation-based external direct fusion) as controlled experimental arms to isolate the effects of layer choice and fusion design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear, evidence-based recipe: select one representative layer from the beginning and middle stages of the visual encoder and fuse them via simple external direct operations (concatenation or averaging), avoiding the complexity and instability of cross-attention modules. A key open question is whether the advantage of external over internal fusion persists at much larger model scales (the experiments use a 1.4B LLM), and whether the modest gains observed here (≈1–2 points average) translate to larger encoders and datasets. The finding that internal fusion becomes more competitive with larger SFT data (737k vs. 332k) suggests the optimal strategy may be dataset-dependent, a nuance that remains underexplored.
