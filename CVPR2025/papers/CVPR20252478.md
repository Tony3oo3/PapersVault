## Problem

Existing generative models for image restoration (diffusion, score-based) treat degradation as a stochastic process, introducing unnecessary complexity and requiring many sampling steps to reverse. Since the degraded image is already known, the forward degradation could be modeled deterministically, but the ill-posed nature of restoration—where multiple HQ images can degrade to the same LQ image—makes direct inversion non-trivial. ResFlow addresses this gap by formulating degradation as a deterministic continuous normalizing flow augmented with an auxiliary variable that resolves the multi-correspondence ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252478/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (trained on 256×256 crops, evaluated at full resolution) and produces a restored HQ image in exactly 4 ODE integration steps. At inference, an auxiliary variable y₁ is sampled from N(0, I) to initialize the reverse flow, and the U-Net velocity network is queried at each of the 4 timesteps. The intermediate auxiliary states are discarded and replaced with ground-truth schedule values during inference, so the only user-facing input is the LQ image itself.

## Method

ResFlow models the degradation from HQ to LQ as a deterministic ODE (continuous normalizing flow) over an augmented state z_t = [x_t; y_t], where x_t is the image component and y_t is an auxiliary variable that encodes the information lost during degradation. The flow paths are defined as Euclidean geodesics with an entropy-preserving schedule: x_t follows a linear interpolation while y_t follows a nonlinear schedule σ_t^y = β(1−t+β)⁻¹ that keeps the total entropy of z_t constant. Training uses velocity field matching (flow matching) with a time-weighted L2 loss that emphasizes timesteps near t=1 where mutual information with the HQ image is lowest and velocity prediction is hardest. A U-Net with adaptive layer normalization for timestep conditioning predicts the velocity field, and the auxiliary variable is injected via a learned adapter module. At inference, the reverse ODE is integrated from t=1 to t=0 in just 4 steps, starting from the LQ image and a sampled Gaussian y₁, yielding the restored HQ image.

## Results

ResFlow achieves state-of-the-art results across all five restoration tasks with only 4 sampling steps. On synthetic benchmarks, it reaches 31.86 dB PSNR / 0.030 LPIPS on Snow100K desnowing, 32.82 dB PSNR / 0.0514 LPIPS on Outdoor-Rain deraining, and 17.12 dB PSNR on Dense-Haze dehazing. On real-world data, it attains 42.26 dB PSNR on SIDD denoising (a 2.24 dB gain over Restormer), 21.44 dB PSNR on NH-HAZE dehazing, and 26.96 dB PSNR / 0.131 LPIPS on DPDD defocus deblurring, outperforming all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResFlow outperforms all 48 baselines on PSNR across every task. The largest margins are on SIDD denoising (+2.24 dB over Restormer), Outdoor-Rain deraining (+1.83 dB over NAFNet), and NH-HAZE dehazing (+1.01 dB over FocalNet). On DPDD deblurring, ResFlow leads FocalNet by 0.78 dB PSNR and DTPM-4 by 0.98 dB, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.131 vs. 0.210 for FocalNet). Two minor losses exist: on Dense-Haze dehazing, FocalNet edges out ResFlow on SSIM (0.63 vs. 0.59) despite ResFlow's PSNR win, and on LHP real-world deraining, SCD-Former slightly surpasses ResFlow on SSIM (0.946 vs. 0.939) while ResFlow leads on PSNR (34.54 vs. 34.33). Overall, ResFlow sets new SOTA on the primary PSNR metric in all settings and on LPIPS where reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResFlow builds directly on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) for its velocity-field training objective and on rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022) for its geodesic (straight-line) path construction and transport-based coupling. It departs fundamentally from the dominant diffusion/score-based restoration paradigm (DDRM, IR-SDE, I2SB, ResShift, RDDM, Resfusion), which models degradation as a stochastic forward process, by instead treating it as a deterministic continuous normalizing flow. The key conceptual departure from prior flow-based restoration attempts (InDI, Helminger et al.) is the explicit augmentation with an auxiliary variable to handle the information loss mandated by the data processing inequality, making the otherwise irreversible degradation process fully invertible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: ResFlow achieves state-of-the-art quality across six restoration tasks (desnowing, deraining, dehazing, denoising, deblurring, JPEG artifact removal) in fewer than four sampling steps, a substantial efficiency gain over diffusion-based methods that typically require dozens to hundreds of steps. The clean DPI-based argument for why stochastic modeling is unnecessary for restoration provides a principled justification that may redirect the field toward deterministic formulations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video restoration, more complex or unknown degradation models, and whether the U-Net backbone can be replaced with more scalable architectures for high-resolution inference.
