## Problem

Existing scene-text VQA benchmarks assume ideal imaging conditions where users take focused photos and directly point to text regions, while existing egocentric VQA datasets neglect scene text entirely. This paper identifies that real assistive scenarios—particularly for visually impaired users—require answering questions that implicitly depend on scene text in dynamic, first-person video where the visual focus is not on the text, a setting no prior benchmark addresses.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 1,507 ego-view videos (outdoor driving clips of ~10s from RoadTextVQA and indoor house-keeping clips of ~3min from EgoSchema/Ego4D) with 7,064 timestamped questions. Models receive uniformly sampled video frames from content strictly before each question's timestamp (simulating real-time streaming QA), a natural-language question, and optionally auxiliary OCR scene text. Outputs are open-ended text answers evaluated by GPT-4o mini for semantic similarity, yielding both a binary accuracy (0–100%) and a graded score (0–5).

## Method

EgoTextVQA is constructed via a three-stage pipeline: (1) automatic filtering of source videos using GoMatching scene-text detection (thresholding at >5% text frames for Ego4D and >15% for RoadTextVQA), (2) GPT-4o-based QA generation from 3 frames sampled per 5-segment video split with prompts designed to elicit goal-oriented, scene-text-aware, first-person questions, and (3) a 5-stage manual annotation process by 9 university students that reduces generated QAs to ~30% and refines colloquial quality. The empirical study benchmarks 10 MLLMs (7 open-source, 3 closed-source) and investigates four heuristic directions: single-frame vs. multi-frame input, question-timestamp-aware backward sampling, high-resolution and super-resolved scene-text regions (via DiffTSR), and auxiliary OCR text as an additional modality. Key findings show all models struggle (best ~33–34% accuracy), that temporal grounding and multi-frame reasoning are critical for indoor long-video QA, and that combining video with auxiliary scene text yields the largest gains (e.g., GPT-4o reaches 52.9% on Outdoor).

## Results

On EgoTextVQA-Outdoor, Gemini 1.5 Pro achieves the best accuracy of 33.4% (score 2.0/5), followed by GPT-4o at 30.3% and Gemini 1.5 Flash at 30.1%. Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL leads at 28.2% accuracy, while ShareGPT4Video and MiniCPM-V 2.6 perform worst at 11.5% and 11.4% respectively. On EgoTextVQA-Indoor, Gemini 1.5 Pro again leads at 34.4%, with LLaVA-NeXT-Video as the best open-source model at 25.4%. Notably, human accuracy is 43.1% (Outdoor) and 27.7% (Indoor), confirming the task's difficulty even for humans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 10 evaluated MLLMs struggle on EgoTextVQA, with the best (Gemini 1.5 Pro) reaching only ~33-34% accuracy, far below the ~85% these models achieve on existing scene-text VQA benchmarks like ST-VQA. Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL (28.2% Outdoor, 23.3% Indoor) and LLaVA-NeXT-Video (19.5% Outdoor, 25.4% Indoor) are the strongest, while ShareGPT4Video (11.5%/7.0%) and MiniCPM-V 2.6 (11.4%/13.3%) perform worst despite being competitive on other video benchmarks. Closed-source models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Flash/Pro) consistently outperform open-source ones by 5-9%, with Gemini 1.5 Pro leading on both splits. Open-source models are particularly weak on 'Location' and 'Intention Reasoning' questions (Outdoor), while closed-source models frequently fail on 'Direction' questions by responding with 'unknown scene text.' Adding auxiliary OCR scene text as input boosts GPT-4o to 52.9% (Outdoor) and Gemini 1.5 Pro to 39.5% (Indoor), demonstrating that no single model can solve the task with video alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established but disjoint research threads: egocentric VideoQA (EgoTaskQA, QAEgo4D, AssistQ, EgoSchema) which assesses visual understanding but neglects scene text, and scene-text VQA (TextVQA, ST-VQA, RoadTextVQA) which assumes ideal imaging conditions and questions that directly point to text regions. EgoTextVQA deliberately departs from both by requiring models to comprehend scene text from an egocentric, dynamic, real-time perspective where questions reflect genuine user needs without visually focusing on the text. The dataset is constructed from existing ego-view sources (Ego4D/EgoSchema and RoadTextVQA) using GPT-4o for initial QA generation followed by multi-stage human annotation, rather than introducing a new model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark capability gap: models achieving ~85% on ST-VQA drop to ~33% here, and even humans score only 43%/27% on outdoor/indoor splits, underscoring that egocentric scene-text QA is fundamentally harder than what existing benchmarks measure. The heuristic investigations yield actionable insights—auxiliary OCR text provides the single largest boost (GPT-4o: 30.3%→52.9%), temporal grounding and multi-frame reasoning are critical for long indoor videos, and real-time streaming QA is substantially harder (best 20.2%)—all pointing toward concrete directions for future MLLM development. Open questions include how to efficiently combine video and text modalities, how to handle the long-tail distribution of OCR tokens in indoor scenes, and whether the current GPT-4o-mini-based evaluation metric adequately captures answer quality for open-ended assistance.
