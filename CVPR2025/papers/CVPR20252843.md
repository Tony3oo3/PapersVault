## Problem

RL-based fine-tuning of text-to-image diffusion models suffers from sparse reward: the alignment score is only available after the full denoising process completes, making it difficult to attribute credit to individual denoising steps. This sparsity causes two failures: models learn unnecessary shortcut policies (e.g., always generating cartoon style) that sacrifice diversity, and actions at different timesteps receive inappropriate rewards, limiting alignment gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252843/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (constructed from templates covering object-activity, color-attribute, and spatial-relation patterns) and produces a generated image via Stable Diffusion v1.4 with LoRA fine-tuning and DDIM sampling over T=20 timesteps. The reward signal is a scalar alignment score computed by CLIPScore (text-image embedding similarity) or BERTScore (semantic recall via LLaVA captioning), available only at the final denoised image.

## Method

B²-DiffuRL introduces two complementary strategies to mitigate sparse reward. Backward Progressive Training (BPT) starts RL training on only the final τ timesteps of denoising and gradually extends the training interval backward to cover all T timesteps, reducing learning difficulty by first mastering the less-noisy, more coherent late-stage denoising. Branch-based Sampling (BS) performs multiple stochastic rollouts from the same intermediate state at the start of each training interval, then selects the best- and worst-rewarded samples within each branch to form a contrastive pair, isolating the contribution of the current interval's policies from earlier steps. The contrastive gradient (Eq. 6) directly attributes reward differences to actions within the training interval, preventing the model from learning spurious style shortcuts. The framework is algorithm-agnostic and compatible with DDPO, DPOK, policy gradient, and DPO without increasing computational cost.

## Results

On held-out generalization prompts, B²-DiffuRL achieves CLIPScore of 0.3748, 0.3252, and 0.3183 on Templates 1–3, outperforming DDPO (0.3698, 0.3175, 0.3134) and vanilla Stable Diffusion (0.3515, 0.3168, 0.2977). In terms of diversity (Inception Score), B²-DiffuRL preserves IS much closer to the original SD (e.g., 1.3127 vs. 1.2886 on Template 1), whereas DDPO suffers a larger drop. When combined with other RL algorithms (DPOK, PG, DPO), B²-DiffuRL consistently improves both alignment and diversity over their vanilla counterparts (e.g., DPO IS improves from 1.2895 to 1.3051).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPO (Black et al., 2023), B²-DiffuRL wins on both alignment and diversity: it achieves higher CLIPScore on all three generalization templates (e.g., 0.3748 vs. 0.3698 on Template 1) while maintaining Inception Score much closer to the original SD (1.3127 vs. 1.2886 on Template 1), indicating less diversity collapse. Against vanilla Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022), B²-DiffuRL improves alignment on all templates (e.g., 0.3748 vs. 0.3515 on Template 1) with only a marginal diversity drop. In the compatibility experiments, B²-DiffuRL improves every tested RL algorithm: DPOK's IS rises from 1.2785 to 1.3005, PG's from 1.2462 to 1.2896, and DPO's from 1.2895 to 1.3051, while alignment curves (Figure 8) show consistent CLIPScore gains over each vanilla algorithm. Human evaluation (80 raters) further confirms that B²-DiffuRL-generated images receive higher preference rates than both SD and DDPO across all three prompt templates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based diffusion model fine-tuning framework established by DDPO (Black et al., 2023), which first cast the denoising process as a sequential decision-making problem. It departs from classic sparse-reward solutions in traditional RL (reward shaping, potential-based methods) by arguing they are inapplicable to diffusion models since intermediate noisy images cannot be meaningfully scored. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of backward progressive training (training only the final timesteps first, then extending backward) and branch-based sampling (comparing trajectories that share identical states up to the training interval boundary), which together isolate the contribution of each timestep's policy to the final reward without requiring dense intermediate rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck—sparse reward—that has limited the practical impact of RL for diffusion model alignment, and does so with a framework that is algorithm-agnostic (compatible with DDPO, DPOK, DPO, PG) and adds no extra computational cost. The finding that branch-based sampling prevents the model from collapsing to a single style (e.g., cartoon) as a reward shortcut is practically important for maintaining diversity in production text-to-image systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models with more timesteps (beyond the 20-step DDIM schedule used here), whether it generalizes to video or 3D generation, and whether the specific backward scheduling of training intervals is optimal or merely one of many possible curricula.
