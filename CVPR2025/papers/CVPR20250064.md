## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion-based image restoration methods rely on U-Net backbones that face quality and scalability limitations, while the emerging DiT architecture has not been effectively adapted for restoration due to the lack of skip connections and underexplored LQ conditioning mechanisms. Additionally, textual prompts alone cannot fully capture the rich visual details (structures, textures, global context) of a low-quality image needed for faithful restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250064/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (degraded to 256×256 via Real-ESRGAN-style degradation) and a free-form textual prompt as input, producing a 1024×1024 high-quality restored image. During training, the LQ image is encoded by a fine-tuned degradation-robust VAE into a 16-channel latent, and the dual prompting module additionally consumes CLIP image-encoder features from both a local patch and surrounding global context patches. The model is trained on 1024×1024 crops with a combination of 1k, 2k, and 4k resolutions to support multi-scale inference.

## Method

DPIR builds on SD3's MMDiT backbone with two parallel conditioning branches. The first is a lightweight LQ conditioning branch (inspired by ControlNeXt) that uses a few trainable convolutional layers plus an adaptive mean-variance alignment module to inject LQ latent features into the first DiT block. The second is a dual prompting control branch that replaces SD3's CLIP text embeddings with visual embeddings extracted from CLIP image encoders applied to both a local patch and a global context patch, then concatenates these global-local visual tokens with T5 text embeddings to form dual prompts fed via cross-attention to every DiT block. A degradation-robust VAE encoder is fine-tuned with L1, LPIPS, and GAN losses to produce a more faithful latent condition. All components are jointly trained under a rectified flow matching objective in a two-stage protocol (large-scale pre-training at batch 1024, then fine-tuning at batch 256) on over 20 million images.

## Results

On DRealSR, DPIR achieves the best DISTS (0.1998), CLIPIQA (0.6424), MUSIQ (62.95), NIQE (5.5463), and MANIQA (0.3880), while trailing StableSR (28.12) and Real-ESRGAN (28.05) on PSNR (27.31). On RealSR, DPIR leads in LPIPS (0.2641), DISTS (0.1642), CLIPIQA (0.6625), MUSIQ (69.28), and MANIQA (0.4857). On DIV2K-Val, DPIR achieves the best DISTS (0.1677), CLIPIQA (0.7416), MUSIQ (71.94), and MANIQA (0.5330). On real-world DRealSR (Table 4), DPIR attains the best no-reference scores: CLIPIQA 0.6980, MUSIQ 70.85, NIQE 3.7382, and MANIQA 0.5310.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPIR consistently outperforms all five main baselines on perceptual and no-reference metrics (DISTS, CLIPIQA, MUSIQ, MANIQA) across all four synthetic datasets, while trailing Real-ESRGAN and StableSR on PSNR/SSIM by 0.5–1.5 dB. Against BSRGAN [57], DPIR wins decisively on every metric across all datasets (e.g., DISTS 0.1998 vs. 0.3603 on DRealSR). Against Real-ESRGAN [43], DPIR matches PSNR on RealSR (26.06) but leads on LPIPS (0.2641 vs. 0.2827), DISTS (0.1642 vs. 0.1936), and all no-reference metrics. Against StableSR [42], DPIR sacrifices ~0.8 dB PSNR on DRealSR but gains substantially on DISTS (0.1998 vs. 0.2270), CLIPIQA (0.6424 vs. 0.4812), and MUSIQ (62.95 vs. 48.81). Against SinSR [45], DPIR wins on DISTS, LPIPS, and MUSIQ across all datasets, though SinSR edges ahead on CLIPIQA for CelebA (0.7940 vs. 0.7111). Against SUPIR [52], DPIR leads on DISTS, LPIPS, and MANIQA on all datasets, while SUPIR retains a slight MUSIQ advantage on CelebA (74.57 vs. 71.49). In the additional DACLIP [27] comparison on SR (PolyU) and denoising (DRealSR) tasks, DPIR achieves higher PSNR (35.46 vs. 32.84), lower LPIPS (0.1808 vs. 0.1983), and higher no-reference scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based image restoration from U-Net backbones (StableSR, SUPIR, SinSR) to the DiT architecture of Stable Diffusion 3, addressing the challenge that DiT lacks the skip connections that U-Net provides for preserving input image information. The lightweight LQ conditioning branch draws directly from ControlNeXt's efficient convolutional design and cross-normalization, while the dual prompting module repurposes SD3's existing CLIP image encoders to generate visual embeddings that replace the original CLIP text embeddings in the cross-attention mechanism. The global-local visual training strategy is a novel adaptation that bridges the semantic gap between local image patches and the global textual semantics the pre-trained DiT expects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPIR is among the first to demonstrate that DiT-based models can effectively perform image restoration with competitive or superior quality to U-Net-based diffusion methods, validating the scalability argument for transformer backbones in low-level vision. The dual prompting insight—that visual and textual conditioning are complementary rather than substitutable for restoration—provides a design principle likely applicable to other DiT-based conditional generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video restoration, whether the 1024×1024 patch constraint can be relaxed for true 4K+ outputs, and whether the heavy training requirement (20M+ images, two-stage training) can be reduced through distillation or more efficient fine-tuning.
