## Problem

Extending 2D vision-language models to full 3D understanding for autonomous driving remains an open challenge: existing driving LLM-agent datasets rely on sparse expert trajectories that provide limited supervision over the reasoning process, and open-loop evaluation is prone to overfitting ego status. Additionally, the fundamental design question of whether to migrate 2D VLMs into 3D space or integrate 3D perception stacks into a VLM framework has not been systematically explored.

## Input / Output

The model consumes six-camera multi-view RGB images (front, front-left, front-right, rear, rear-left, rear-right from nuScenes) along with text prompts, and optionally ego status (position, velocity, acceleration). Outputs are structured as text responses across five Q&A categories (scene description, attention object identification, counterfactual reasoning, decision-making/planning justification, and general conversation) plus a 3-second planning trajectory and 3D object detections. For data generation, GPT-4 receives stitched multi-view images, simulated counterfactual trajectories, expert log-replay trajectories, and rule-based checklist results as structured context rather than raw 3D annotations.

## Method

OmniDrive introduces a counterfactual-based synthetic Q&A generation pipeline: driving trajectories are clustered into behavioral categories (stopping, turning, accelerating, etc.), simulated per-scene to produce counterfactual outcomes, validated via a rule-based checklist (collision, red-light, road-boundary checks using nuScenes and OpenLane-v2 annotations), and fed to GPT-4 alongside multi-view captions to generate coherent Q&A pairs with human-in-the-loop quality assurance. Two agent architectures are proposed: Omni-L extends LLaVA's MLP projector to multi-view input with zero-initialized 3D positional encoding, while Omni-Q adapts StreamPETR's sparse query-based 3D perception into a Q-Former, using detection queries for 3D localization and carrier queries for vision-language alignment to the LLM. Both are trained in two stages—2D pre-training on LLaVA-style data followed by 3D fine-tuning on OmniDrive Q&A and planning tasks—using only text generation loss without contrastive or matching objectives.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop planning, Omni-L++ (with ego status) achieves an average L2 error of 0.40 m, collision rate of 0.35%, and intersection rate of 2.45%, while Omni-Q++ achieves the best L2 (0.33 m) and collision rate (0.30%) among all VLM-based methods. Without ego status, Omni-L reduces collision rate from 3.22% to 1.90% and intersection rate from 3.90% to 3.29% simply by training on OmniDrive Q&A data. On the DriveLM benchmark, pre-training with OmniDrive improves the overall score from 0.53 to 0.56, and combining with LLaVA665k further raises it to 0.58.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniAD (with ego status), Omni-L++ achieves a lower L2 error (0.40 m vs. 0.46 m) and lower collision rate (0.35% vs. 0.37%), but UniAD retains a significant advantage on intersection rate (1.59% vs. 2.45%). Compared to VAD-Base (with ego), Omni-L++ is broadly competitive: slightly worse on L2 (0.40 vs. 0.37 m) and collision (0.35% vs. 0.33%), but marginally better on intersection (2.45% vs. 2.47%). Against BEV-Planner++, Omni-L++ trades a small L2 penalty (0.40 vs. 0.35 m) for a substantially lower intersection rate (2.45% vs. 3.16%), while Omni-Q++ matches BEV-Planner++ on L2 (0.33 vs. 0.35 m) and collision (0.30% vs. 0.34%) but is worse on intersection (3.00% vs. 3.16%). Both OmniDrive agents dramatically outperform ST-P3 across all metrics, confirming that VLM-based planning with counterfactual-reasoning data is far more effective than the earlier trajectory-prediction baseline. The paper also notes that without ego status, Omni-L (L2 2.34 m, collision 1.90%) still substantially outperforms Omni-Q (L2 1.98 m, collision 3.79%), suggesting that 2D VLM-to-3D migration is more robust than integrating 3D perception stacks into a VLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of driving VLM agents (DriveLM, DriveGPT4, LingoQA) and 3D perception architectures (StreamPETR, UniAD, VAD), proposing a unified framework that bridges both. It extends LingoQA's counterfactual questions from 2D scene understanding to 3D trajectory-based reasoning, using simulated trajectories and rule-based checklists to generate structured Q&A data. Architecturally, it draws on LLaVA's MLP projection (Omni-L) and BLIP-2's Q-Former (Omni-Q), adapting both for multi-view 3D driving inputs, while the open-loop planning evaluation follows the protocol established by BEV-Planner and Ego-MLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OmniDrive dataset provides a scalable, high-quality resource for training driving VLMs with counterfactual reasoning, addressing the sparse-supervision problem of expert trajectories. The finding that migrating 2D VLMs to 3D (Omni-L) outperforms integrating 3D perception stacks into VLMs (Omni-Q) offers practical guidance for the field, though the gap is moderate and evaluated on a single dataset. A key limitation is the absence of multi-agent interaction in counterfactual simulation, leaving closed-loop evaluation as an important open question. The counterfactual data generation pipeline, combining GPT-4 with rule-based checklists, could be adapted to other safety-critical domains beyond driving.
