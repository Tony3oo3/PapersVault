## Problem

Existing online stereo rectification methods estimate relative camera poses and derive rectifying homographies from them, but they optimize for pose or epipolar constraints rather than the rectification constraint (zero vertical disparity) itself, yielding suboptimal rectification. Furthermore, the general-purpose feature matchers used to extract correspondences are not trained for rectification, and their training typically requires ground-truth correspondences that are unavailable in stereo rectification datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250346/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of left and right RGB stereo images (resized to 1024×512 for Carla-Flowguided and Semi-Truck Highway, 800×416 for Perturbed SceneFlow) together with known camera intrinsics K_l and K_r. It outputs a pair of 3×3 rectifying homographies H_l and H_r, parameterized by 5 degrees of freedom via Euler angles with a shared baseline-rotation constraint, which when applied to the input images produce a rectified stereo pair with nullified vertical disparity.

## Method

The framework consists of two stages: a semi-dense matcher (Efficient LoFTR) that extracts correspondences, and a rectification-constrained estimator that directly solves for H_l and H_r by minimizing vertical disparity via Levenberg-Marquardt nonlinear least squares with RANSAC-based outlier filtering. To train the matcher without ground-truth correspondences, the paper introduces three rectification-specific losses derived solely from camera pose annotations: a rectification-constrained classification loss at each matching layer that maximizes the likelihood of matches having rectification error below a threshold, a rectification error regression loss at the final sub-pixel refinement layer, and an auxiliary camera pose prediction loss (6D rotation + translation direction) at the feature extractor to encourage geometrically aware features. The key distinction from prior work is that the solver targets the rectification constraint directly rather than decomposing an estimated camera pose into homographies, and the matcher is supervised end-to-end for rectification quality rather than generic correspondence accuracy.

## Results

On the Semi-Truck Highway dataset, the proposed method achieves a vertical flow of 0.59 pixels and SP+SG keypoint offset of 0.58 pixels, outperforming the best prior method (FAR) by 21.3% in vertical flow. On Carla-Flowguided, it reaches 0.52 pixels vertical flow and 0.51 pixels keypoint offset, a 10.7% improvement over the best baseline. For downstream stereo depth estimation on Carla-Flowguided, the method reduces MAE to 3.20 and AbsRel to 7.31, compared to 4.27/12.12 for the next-best method (FlowGuidedNet). The method also achieves the lowest runtime at 79 ms, roughly 2× faster than EfficientLoFTR+MAGSAC (163 ms) and 21× faster than FAR (1672 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against eight baselines spanning matching-based pose estimation (SIFT+MAGSAC, SP+SG+MAGSAC, LoFTR+MAGSAC, EfficientLoFTR+MAGSAC), matching-free pose estimation (DirectionNet, ViTPose, FAR), and a flow-guided rectification network (FlowGuidedNet). The proposed method wins on all four rectification metrics (SP+SG offset and vertical flow on both datasets) against every baseline. Against the strongest matching-free method FAR, it improves vertical flow by 21.3% on Semi-Truck Highway (0.59 vs 0.75) and 8.8% on Carla-Flowguided (0.52 vs 0.57), while being 21× faster (79 ms vs 1672 ms). Against the prior rectification-specific method FlowGuidedNet [26], it reduces vertical flow from 1.05 to 0.59 on Semi-Truck Highway (43.8% reduction) and from 1.00 to 0.52 on Carla-Flowguided (48% reduction). Against the best matching-based pose solver pipeline (EfficientLoFTR+MAGSAC), it improves vertical flow from 0.82 to 0.59 on Semi-Truck Highway and from 0.62 to 0.52 on Carla-Flowguided, while also halving runtime. On the unbiased Perturbed SceneFlow evaluation with ground-truth optical flow, the method achieves 0.089 vertical flow versus 0.132 for EfficientLoFTR+MAGSAC, confirming the advantage is not an artifact of the evaluation metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the semi-dense matching paradigm of Efficient LoFTR [59] and the Euler-angle parameterization of rectifying homographies from Hansen et al. [20], but departs fundamentally from the dominant pipeline of estimating camera poses first and then decomposing them into rectification homographies. Instead, it directly optimizes the rectification constraint (zero vertical disparity) via a nonlinear least-squares solver, and introduces multi-level rectification-specific supervision signals derived from camera pose annotations to train the matcher without ground-truth correspondences. This contrasts with Kumar et al. [26], which uses a pretrained optical flow model for supervision (becoming noisy near ground truth), and with matching-free pose estimators that the authors show are consistently inferior for rectification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous vehicles and robots operating in dynamic environments where vibration and thermal stress degrade offline calibration, as even small extrinsic errors compound into large depth estimation errors. The key methodological insight—that directly minimizing vertical disparity rather than camera pose yields measurably better rectification—provides a clear design principle for future online calibration systems. Open questions include whether the known-intrinsics assumption holds under extreme conditions, whether the approach generalizes to multi-camera rigs, and whether the Perturbed SceneFlow dataset (synthetic, ±1.5° perturbations) adequately captures the distribution of real-world drift seen in the Semi-Truck Highway data.
