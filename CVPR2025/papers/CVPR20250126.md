## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs for text-rich document understanding either demand high-resolution image inputs and massive training data (OCR-free methods) or rely on auxiliary document encoders whose comprehension capacity lags behind the LLM itself, requiring extensive alignment training (OCR-dependent methods). The paper addresses how to efficiently extend an LLM to perceive document layout and visual structure without a heavy separate encoder or prohibitive computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250126/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a document image (resized to 224×224, yielding 196 visual patch tokens) and a free-form text question. An OCR engine first extracts N text segments with their bounding boxes; these are interleaved with the patch tokens and the question into a single natural-language prompt. The model outputs a free-form text answer, which may be a short extracted value (KIE), a multi-line span, or a reasoning response.

## Method

DocLayLLM augments a frozen LLM (Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Llama-2-7B-Chat) with four lightweight modules: a visual embedder/projector (VE/VP) for image patches and a layout embedder/projector (LE/LP) for 2D bounding-box coordinates, all initialized from LayoutLMv3 weights or randomly and trained end-to-end via LoRA (rank 64). Crucially, the OCR text content is encoded by the LLM's own native text embedder rather than a separate document encoder, preserving the LLM's full language comprehension while adding layout and visual perception. Training comprises two novel CoT-centric stages: (1) CoT Pre-training, where chain-of-thought reasoning traces (layout-type, table-structure via XY-Cut, and geometric-relations) are generated from non-QA annotations to produce ~3M instruction-tuning pairs that align the new visual/layout features with the LLM's textual space; and (2) CoT Annealing during SFT (~300K samples), which starts with CoT-augmented data and progressively shifts to direct-answer data, improving final answer precision without sacrificing the reasoning gains from CoT. The entire pipeline completes in ~36 A100-GPU-days.

## Results

DocLayLLM_Llama3 achieves 78.36 on DocVQA and 58.55 on VisualMRC (VQA avg 68.46), and 84.11 on FUNSD, 71.34 on CORD, 84.36 on SROIE (KIE avg 79.94), using only 3.1M pre-training data pairs. This outperforms LayoutLLM (5.7M data) by +4.11 on DocVQA and +13.39 on SROIE, and DocLLM (16.8M data) by +8.86 on DocVQA and +12.46 on FUNSD. When trained with downstream task data during SFT (Table 4), DocLayLLM_Llama3 reaches 86.52 on DocVQA, 58.36 on InfoVQA, and 70.99 average on table understanding, surpassing all OCR-free competitors including DocOwl 1.5 and TextSquare.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OCR-dependent baselines, DocLayLLM_Llama3 dominates: it beats LayoutLLM_llama2 (5.7M data) by +4.11 on DocVQA, +3.32 on VisualMRC, +5.46 on FUNSD, +9.13 on CORD, and +13.39 on SROIE, while using 46% less pre-training data. It also surpasses DocLLM (16.8M data, 542% more) by +8.86 on DocVQA and +12.46 on FUNSD in a zero-shot setting where DocLLM used benchmark training sets. Against OCR-free methods (Table 4, all trained on downstream data), DocLayLLM_Llama3 outperforms DocOwl 1.5 by +4.32 on DocVQA, +7.66 on InfoVQA, and +10.59 on WTQ, and beats TextSquare by +2.22 on DocVQA and +3.16 on TabFact. The text-only Llama3-8B-Instruct baseline scores only 12.30 on VisualMRC versus DocLayLLM's 58.55, confirming that the multi-modal extension is essential. Even the Llama2-based variant matches LayoutLLM on VQA while significantly outperforming it on KIE (77.58 vs 70.61 average), demonstrating the architecture's robustness across backbone choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

DocLayLLM departs from the dominant OCR-dependent TDU paradigm that relies on an auxiliary document encoder (e.g., LayoutLMv3 in LayoutLLM, BLIP-2 in Cream) to jointly encode text, layout, and image features before feeding them to an LLM. Instead, it integrates lightweight visual patch tokens and 2D positional tokens directly into the LLM's input sequence, preserving the LLM's native text comprehension while adding layout and visual perception through simple embedding and linear projection layers. The training strategy also diverges from prior instruction-tuning approaches by introducing CoT Pre-training (generating chain-of-thought data from non-QA annotations) and CoT Annealing (gradually shifting from CoT-augmented to direct-answer data during SFT), neither of which has been explored in prior document understanding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the dramatic reduction of training resources—3.1M pre-training samples and 36 A100 days versus 5.7M–16.8M samples for prior SOTA OCR-dependent methods—while matching or exceeding their performance, making high-quality TDU more accessible. The strong zero-shot generalization (outperforming methods that saw benchmark training data) suggests the unified-LLM encoding preserves the backbone's generalization better than DE-LLM alignment. Open questions include whether the fixed 224×224 resolution (196 patch tokens) suffices for very dense or multi-page documents, whether the approach extends to handwritten or heavily stylized layouts where OCR quality degrades, and whether CoT Pre-training and CoT Annealing transfer to other multi-modal LLM tasks beyond document understanding.
