## Problem

CLIP and other VLMs suffer from a severe shortcut problem where background regions become as influential as (or more than) foreground objects during classification, causing background-only OOD samples to be misidentified as in-distribution. Existing few-shot OOD detection methods for VLMs largely neglect this shortcut issue, and prior approaches like LoCoOp that attempt region-level regularization rely on CLIP's own feature similarity to delineate ID-irrelevant regions, which is itself corrupted by the shortcut.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (224×224 for ViT-B-16) as input along with learnable text prompts for K ID classes, and produces a binary ID/OOD decision via the GL-MCM score that combines the maximum global class probability with the maximum local patch-level probability. During training, the pipeline additionally consumes segmentation masks and inpainted background images to construct pseudo-OOD supervision and region-level supervision signals. Evaluation is conducted in a few-shot protocol (1–16 shots per class) on ImageNet-1K as ID with iNaturalist, SUN, Places, Texture, and the newly proposed ImageNet-Bg as OOD test sets.

## Method

OSPCoOp combines two mechanisms: (1) background decoupling, where a segmentation model extracts the ID-relevant mask, the foreground is removed and inpainted to produce pure-background pseudo-OOD images (filtered by CLIP similarity to ensure no residual ID content), and (2) mask-guided region regularization, which uses the segmentation mask to classify each image patch as ID-relevant or ID-irrelevant and applies a negative-entropy loss to force the model's local logits on ID-irrelevant patches to be uniform across all ID classes. The full training objective sums a standard cross-entropy loss on ID-global features, a region-level OOD irrelevance loss over all ID-irrelevant patches, and a global OOD irrelevance loss on the pseudo-OOD background images, all optimized via CoOp-style learnable context-word prompts. At inference, the GL-MCM score (max global probability + β·max local patch probability) provides the final OOD decision, and the mask-based region delineation replaces LoCoOp's CLIP-feature-similarity approach to avoid reinforcing the very shortcut being addressed.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K as ID with ViT-B-16 in the 16-shot setting, OSPCoOp achieves an average AUROC of 94.75% and average FPR95 of 25.13% across iNaturalist, SUN, Places, and Texture, outperforming the best few-shot baseline LoCoOp (93.52% AUROC, 28.66% FPR95) by 1.23% AUROC and 3.53% FPR95. On the proposed ImageNet-Bg background-interference benchmark, OSPCoOp achieves 91.30% AUROC and 33.84% FPR95, surpassing the next-best method LoCoOp (90.01% AUROC, 37.70% FPR95). In the 1-shot setting, the method still attains 93.81% AUROC and 27.73% FPR95, demonstrating strong few-shot robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among few-shot training methods, OSPCoOp outperforms LoCoOp (the strongest baseline) by 1.23% AUROC and 3.53% FPR95 on average, with the largest gains on SUN (+1.67% AUROC, −5.18% FPR95) and Places (+2.03% AUROC, −7.13% FPR95), datasets with heavy background interference. Against SCT, the improvement is 1.38% AUROC and 1.34% FPR95. Compared to the best outlier-label exposure method NegLabel (94.25% AUROC, 25.69% FPR95), OSPCoOp leads by 0.50% AUROC and 0.56% FPR95, while LAPT (94.68% AUROC, 23.40% FPR95) remains slightly ahead on FPR95 but trails on AUROC. On the proposed ImageNet-Bg benchmark, OSPCoOp achieves 91.30% AUROC / 33.84% FPR95, beating LoCoOp (90.01% / 37.70%) and all training-free methods (best: DPM-F at 89.53% / 40.78%), confirming that the shortcut-mitigation strategy specifically targets background interference. Notably, DPM-T degrades substantially on ImageNet-Bg (85.46% AUROC) compared to its training-free counterpart DPM-F (89.53%), suggesting that few-shot fine-tuning of the vision encoder can worsen shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot OOD detection framework established by LoCoOp (Miyai et al., NeurIPS 2023), which introduced local regularized prompt learning to suppress CLIP's response to ID-irrelevant regions. The key departure is identifying that LoCoOp's reliance on CLIP feature similarity for region delineation is itself corrupted by CLIP's shortcut problem—background regions get misclassified as ID-relevant—so the authors replace this with segmentation-mask-based delineation and add a background decoupling pipeline (segmentation + inpainting) to generate pseudo-OOD supervision. The prompt learning backbone follows CoOp (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022), and the OOD scoring uses GL-MCM, but the core novelty lies in the data augmentation strategy and mask-guided regularization rather than in the prompt architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that CLIP's pre-training creates a severe background shortcut—where removing the foreground actually increases the model's confidence in the ID class—is a useful warning for anyone deploying VLM-based OOD detectors in open-world settings. The ImageNet-Bg benchmark, while limited in scale, fills a gap by providing a controlled evaluation of background interference specifically, which prior OOD benchmarks (SUN, Places, Texture) only partially capture. Open questions include whether the shortcut problem persists in newer VLMs (e.g., those trained with more diverse or object-centric captions), whether the inpainting-based augmentation generalizes beyond ImageNet's object-centric classes, and whether a theoretical account of why fine-tuning the vision encoder (DPM-T) worsens background shortcuts can be developed.
