## Problem

Existing depth estimation models cannot simultaneously achieve meticulous spatial details, temporal consistency across video frames, and high inference efficiency. Discriminative models lose high-frequency details, diffusion-based methods like Marigold are slow due to many sampling steps, and video-specific methods sacrifice detail for consistency. The paper asks whether a single unified framework can deliver all three properties at once.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252402/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image or a sequence of RGB video frames as input. Each frame is encoded by a frozen 8×-downsampling VAE into a 4-channel latent code; ground-truth depth is log-normalized to [−1, 1] before encoding. The output is a decoded depth map (via the VAE decoder) for single images, or a temporally consistent depth sequence for video, where a sliding window of w=4 adjacent latent codes is fused by the LTS module before decoding.

## Method

CH₃ Depth operates entirely in VAE latent space using a flow-matching U-Net (8→4 channel conditional) trained with three key modifications. First, the optimization objective is reframed as InDI flow matching: instead of predicting a fixed global velocity field from Gaussian noise to the target, the network predicts the variable-scale transformation from the current noisy distribution to the target, equivalent to weighting the standard FM loss by (1−t) and thus adjusting ELBO coefficients per noise level. Second, a concave mapping function (e.g., f(x)=x^{1/2}) converts uniform timesteps into non-uniform steps, allocating larger integration steps early for rapid convergence and smaller steps late for detail refinement, enabling accurate 2-step inference. Third, a lightweight unconditional U-Net (the Latent Temporal Stabilizer) aggregates depth latent codes from a sliding window of w=4 adjacent frames to enforce temporal consistency, trained with a temporal consistent deviation loss that tolerates imperfect natural-scene annotations by only requiring consistent deviation from ground truth rather than exact matching.

## Results

On image depth estimation, CH3 Depth (96K synthetic) achieves AbsRel of 5.0 on NYUv2, 7.6 on KITTI, 5.1 on ETH3D, and 5.7 on ScanNet, with corresponding δ1 values of 97.2%, 94.9%, 97.6%, and 96.4%. On video depth estimation, it reaches AbsRel 19.4 and OPW 40.7 on Sintel, and AbsRel 5.1 and OPW 9.5 on NYUv2, outperforming all generative baselines in accuracy while matching the best temporal consistency. In efficiency, CH3 Depth completes inference in 0.36s per image with 2 sampling steps (vs. 0.69s/3 steps for DepthFM and 4.45s/50 steps for Marigold) on a single A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generative models, CH3 Depth (96K) outperforms Marigold by 2.5% δ1 on KITTI (94.9 vs. 91.6) and by 0.5 AbsRel on NYUv2 (5.0 vs. 5.5), while also surpassing DepthFM by 1.5 AbsRel on NYUv2 (5.0 vs. 6.5) and 0.7 on KITTI (7.6 vs. 8.3). Against Lotus, CH3 Depth leads on KITTI by 3.7 AbsRel (7.6 vs. 11.3) and 7.2% δ1 (94.9 vs. 87.7), though Lotus edges it slightly on ScanNet δ1 (96.0 vs. 96.4 is actually CH3 Depth winning). For video depth, CH3 Depth achieves the best accuracy on both Sintel (AbsRel 19.4 vs. 29.2 for DepthCrafter) and NYUv2 (AbsRel 5.1 vs. 7.6 for DepthCrafter), while its OPW of 9.5 on NYUv2 is comparable to DepthCrafter (8.8) and ChronoDepth (9.7) but far better than NVDS (36.4) and Marigold (45.5). On Sintel OPW, DepthCrafter (33.6) still leads over CH3 Depth (40.7), indicating a slight trade-off in temporal smoothness on synthetic video. Overall, CH3 Depth is competitive with the strongest discriminative model Depth Anything (4.3 AbsRel on NYUv2) while using only synthetic training data and offering a flexible image+video unified framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-matching-based depth estimation paradigm initiated by DepthFM, which itself repurposed the VAE latent-space architecture from Marigold. The key departure is replacing DepthFM's fixed-scale velocity field objective with InDI flow matching (borrowed from image restoration), which reweights the training loss by noise level to focus capacity on the current-to-target distribution rather than a global noise-to-target mapping. For video consistency, the paper introduces a latent-space temporal stabilizer (LTS), departing from prior video depth methods (NVDS, DepthCrafter, ChronoDepth) that operate on RGB features or decoded depth maps, and instead aggregates latent codes directly within the generative pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying image and video depth estimation in a single flow-matching framework with only 2–3 sampling steps, CH3 Depth offers a practical path toward deployment on resource-constrained platforms (robotics, autonomous driving) where 50-step diffusion is prohibitive. The LTS module's demonstrated transferability to Marigold and DepthFM suggests it could serve as a broadly applicable post-processing component for any latent-space generative depth model. Open questions include whether the InDI formulation generalizes beyond depth to other dense prediction tasks, and whether the reliance on synthetic training data limits robustness in truly novel real-world conditions (the authors note failures on mirror reflections and extreme lighting).
