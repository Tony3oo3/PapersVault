## Problem

Existing diffusion-based talking head methods operate in 2D image space and cannot produce geometrically consistent outputs for novel-view rendering, while 3D-representation-based approaches (NeRF, 3D Gaussians) require separate reconstruction stages or expensive multi-view data. This paper addresses how to directly generate 3D renderable talking heads from monocular videos in a single diffusion denoising process, eliminating post-processing steps for novel-view synthesis.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 512×512 reference identity image, a sequence of 3DMM parametric model parameters (facial expressions and head pose) rasterized into UV coordinate maps, and optionally a first-frame image for temporal continuity across clips. It outputs a 32-frame MPI video with 24 RGBA planes per frame (plus a frontal and residual RGB image following Tucker et al.), which can be re-rendered from arbitrary camera poses at 109 FPS on a single A100 GPU to produce novel-view videos or binocular stereo pairs.

## Method

The core contribution is a video diffusion framework where the denoising network predicts MPIs directly and a differentiable MPI rendering function converts them to 2D observations for loss computation, integrating 3D rendering into the reverse diffusion process. The key training innovation is Reference-Target Alternating Training: MPIs are randomly constructed in either the target camera (supervised by ground-truth frontal images for sharp details) or the reference camera (supervised by target images as side views for 3D consistency), enabling learning from monocular videos alone. A bootstrapping phase first trains the model with only frontal-view loss, then the model generates pseudo ground truth under the reference camera to enable the 3D-consistency loss. Late-stage noise sampling (restricting t to [T/2, T] for the side-view loss) mitigates distribution mismatch from imperfect pseudo ground truth. The architecture is built on Lumiere with separate color and geometry branches connected via zero convolution, trained end-to-end on 35k in-the-wild monocular videos at 512×512 resolution.

## Results

On HDTF, IM-Portrait achieves the best FVD of 69.04 (vs. 78.11 for Face-V2V, 117.54 for Follow-your-emoji, and 132.90 for X-Portrait), indicating superior overall video quality combining image fidelity and motion naturalness. On Talkinghead1kh, it again leads in FVD at 135.12 (vs. 141.02, 209.91, and 183.05 for the same baselines). Image-level metrics (L1, LPIPS, FID) are competitive but not uniformly best — e.g., X-Portrait achieves a slightly lower FID of 15.66 vs. 15.93 on HDTF — while IM-Portrait's novel-view rendering speed of 109 FPS at 512² is roughly 5× faster than the next-best method (Face-V2V at 22 FPS at 256²) and orders of magnitude faster than diffusion-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IM-Portrait achieves the best FVD on both HDTF (69.04) and Talkinghead1kh (135.12), outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baselines Follow-your-emoji (117.54 / 209.91) and X-Portrait (132.90 / 183.05) by 41% and 36% respectively on HDTF, and by 36% and 26% on Talkinghead1kh. On individual image metrics, it is competitive but not always best: X-Portrait edges it on HDTF FID (15.66 vs. 15.93) and LPIPS (0.119 vs. 0.118), while Face-V2V achieves the lowest L1 on HDTF (0.029 vs. 0.031). Against the NeRF-based Portrait4D-v2, IM-Portrait substantially outperforms on FVD (69.04 vs. 139.11 on HDTF) and renders novel views ~11× faster (109 FPS vs. 10 FPS at 512²). The most striking advantage is rendering speed: at 109 FPS in 512², IM-Portrait is ~5× faster than Face-V2V (22 FPS at 256²) and 200–1000× faster than the diffusion-based baselines that lack explicit camera control. Overall, the paper positions IM-Portrait as matching or exceeding 2D diffusion methods in image/video quality while uniquely providing efficient, consistent multi-view rendering in a single denoising pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-with-forward-models paradigm of Tewari et al. [35] from static scenes to dynamic talking heads, using the Lumiere [5] spacetime diffusion architecture as its backbone and Multiplane Images [36] as the 3D representation. It departs from prior diffusion-based talking head methods (Follow-your-emoji, X-Portrait) that operate purely in 2D image space, and from 3D talking head methods (NeRF-based, Gaussian Splatting) that forgo diffusion models, by unifying both in a single denoising process that directly outputs renderable MPIs. The reference-target alternating training with bootstrapping is a novel mechanism to extract 3D supervision from monocular video without multi-view data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables practical VR/AR use cases (binocular stereo, perspective distortion) at 109 FPS novel-view rendering—over an order of magnitude faster than NeRF-based alternatives—while maintaining competitive image and video quality. The key open question is whether the MPI representation's limited angular range can be overcome by other 3D representations or by incorporating multi-view training data, as the authors note excessive viewpoint changes remain unsupported. The bootstrapping strategy for learning 3D from monocular data may generalize to other dynamic 3D generation tasks beyond talking heads.
