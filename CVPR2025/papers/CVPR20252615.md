## Problem

Existing learned video compression (LVC) methods focus on capturing local temporal movements via optical flow but neglect non-local correlations (similar regions without explicit motion) across frames, and rely on a single reference frame that is insufficient for complex scenes. Additionally, the train-test mismatch in sequence length (training on 6–7 frames vs. testing on hundreds) causes significant error accumulation in long prediction chains, degrading coding efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252615/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method processes the t-th frame x_t together with propagated multi-scale features F̂_t^i and F̂_{t-1}^i (at three scales, i=0,1,2) and decoded motion vectors v̂_t, v̂_{t-1} from prior frames. It produces an arithmetic-encoded bitstream and reconstructed frames, operating on 256×256 patches during training and supporting up to 4K resolution at inference. The conditional coding paradigm means temporal contexts serve as priors for entropy modeling of mid-features y_t^i rather than being explicitly subtracted as residuals.

## Method

ECVC introduces Multiple Frame Non-Local Context Mining (MNLC), which extracts both local and non-local priors from two reference frames. Local correlations are captured via offset diversity on the first reference and via reusing the previous motion vector with a multi-scale refinement module on the second reference. Non-local correlations are captured by a Multi-Head Linear Cross Attention (MHLCA) that applies independent softmax along rows and columns of the query and key matrices, reducing complexity from O(H²W²d) to O(HWd²) while provably preserving normalized attention distributions (Theorem 1). To mitigate error accumulation, the Partial Cascaded Finetuning Strategy (PCFS) partitions long sequences into groups and sequentially updates the model with per-group cascaded losses, enabling finetuning on full-length sequences within a fixed GPU memory budget.

## Results

Under the standard IP 32 setting (96 frames, PSNR metric), ECVC achieves an average BD-Rate of −29.3% relative to VTM-13.2 LDB, outperforming DCVC-DC (−22.0%) and DCVC-FM (−18.8%). Under the long prediction chain setting (IP −1, all frames), ECVC reaches −32.1% average BD-Rate, reducing 11.5% more bit-rate than DCVC-FM (−20.6%). For MS-SSIM optimization under IP 32, ECVC achieves −56.6% average BD-Rate versus −52.9% for DCVC-DC. The model has 61.9M parameters and 1407 kMACs/pixel, with encoding/decoding times of 0.78s/0.62s per frame at 1080p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECVC outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and both IP settings. Against the most recent SOTA, DCVC-FM (CVPR'24), ECVC reduces 10.5% more bit-rate on average under IP 32 (−29.3% vs. −18.8%) and 11.5% more under IP −1 all-frames (−32.1% vs. −20.6%), with the largest gap on HEVC D (−36.7% vs. −26.5%) and UVG (−37.6% vs. −24.0%). Against DCVC-DC (CVPR'23), the improvement is 7.3% on average under IP 32, and under IP −1 all-frames DCVC-DC actually degrades to +8.4% average BD-Rate while ECVC maintains −32.1%, highlighting the effectiveness of the Partial Cascaded Finetuning Strategy in mitigating error accumulation. The older baselines DCVC-HEM and DCVC-TCM are substantially outperformed, with DCVC-TCM showing positive (worse-than-anchor) BD-Rates on most datasets under IP 32. The gains are most pronounced on high-motion sequences (HEVC D, UVG) where non-local correlations and multiple reference frames provide the greatest benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECVC extends the DCVC-DC (CVPR 2023) framework, which itself builds on the conditional coding paradigm introduced by DCVC (NeurIPS 2021), by introducing two key departures: (1) exploiting non-local spatial correlations across multiple reference frames via Multi-Head Linear Cross Attention, going beyond the local motion-based context mining (offset diversity) that all prior DCVC variants rely on; and (2) a Partial Cascaded Finetuning Strategy that addresses the train-test sequence-length mismatch more effectively than DCVC-FM's feature refreshment. The linear attention mechanism draws on the broader efficient-attention literature (Katharopoulos et al., Shen et al.) to make full-receptive-field cross-attention tractable at video resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.5–11.5% BD-rate gains over DCVC-FM are substantial in a field where typical year-over-year improvements are 1–3%, and the PCFS strategy offers a practical, low-cost training recipe that any LVC method could adopt to reduce error accumulation in long prediction chains. The paper acknowledges a key limitation: ECVC is trained on natural videos and may underperform on out-of-domain content (e.g., anime), pointing toward instance-adaptive optimization as a necessary next step. Open questions include whether the non-local correlation mining generalizes to B-frame coding structures and whether the multi-reference-frame approach can be extended beyond two references without prohibitive complexity.
