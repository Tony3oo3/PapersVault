## Problem

Human security inspectors rely on both vertical and side X-ray views to detect prohibited items, especially in challenging categories where single-view images suffer from heavy occlusion. However, existing X-ray datasets are either single-view or too small and diverse to support dual-view research, and no detection framework has been designed to exploit the complementary information across two views the way human experts do.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252824/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of X-ray images (main view I₁ and auxiliary view I₂) at approximately 1200×900 resolution (up to 2000×1040) as input. Crucially, only the main view carries ground-truth bounding boxes and class labels; the auxiliary view has no annotations, and the two views share a horizontal coordinate correspondence due to positional invariance of the imaging hardware. The output is a unified set of bounding boxes and class labels (12 categories) all referenced to the main-view image, formed by the union of detections from both pipelines.

## Method

AENet consists of two pipelines. The main-view pipeline performs standard object detection on I₁ while simultaneously training specialized 'expert models' on ground-truth-cropped patches to handle challenging categories. The auxiliary-view pipeline uses saliency detection on I₂ to obtain approximate object locations, transfers class labels from the main view via cross-view correspondence, and trains an auxiliary detector with a saliency-based regression loss. At inference, merged auxiliary-view boxes are mapped back to I₁ (exploiting the horizontal coordinate relationship), the corresponding patches are cropped from the main view, and passed through the expert models for refined predictions. The final detection set is the union of main-view and refined auxiliary-view results, yielding up to +24.7% AP improvement on the hardest category (umbrellas) while leaving simple categories unchanged.

## Results

On the LDXray dataset, AENet consistently improves mAP (AP50) across all seven base detection models, with gains ranging from +0.3% (CenterNet: 72.1→72.4) to +2.4% (RetinaNet: 62.7→65.1). The most dramatic improvements appear in challenging categories: the umbrella (UM) category sees gains of +20.1% to +25.1% depending on the base model (e.g., RetinaNet UM: 33.9→59.0), while columnar green liquid (CG) improves by +2.0% across all models. Simple categories (MP, OL, PC1, PC2, LA, GL, TA, BL) remain essentially unchanged, confirming that the dual-view mechanism selectively targets hard-to-detect objects without degrading easy ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AENet is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement on seven widely-used detectors, and it wins on every single comparison. The largest relative gains occur with weaker base models: RetinaNet benefits the most in mAP (+2.4%, 62.7→65.1) and in the umbrella category (+25.1%, 33.9→59.0), while Cascade R-CNN gains +2.0% mAP and +21.7% on UM. Stronger baselines like CenterNet (+0.3% mAP) and ATSS (+0.6% mAP) see smaller overall mAP improvements but still gain 3.2% and 7.6% respectively on the challenging UM category. Notably, all simple-category scores (MP, OL, PC1, PC2, LA, GL, TA, BL) remain identical or within 0.1% between baseline and AENet-augmented versions, confirming the dual-view mechanism does not introduce regressions. The non-metallic lighter (NL) category shows minimal improvement across all models, which the authors attribute to its near-invisibility under X-ray imaging. Overall, AENet acts as a consistent, architecture-agnostic booster that selectively rescues challenging categories without disturbing already-well-detected ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of dual-view X-ray security inspection work (MV-Xray, Dualray, DvXray) by introducing a dataset roughly 20× larger and a framework that explicitly emulates the human inspector's two-stage workflow: a general detector for common items plus specialized 'expert models' for challenging categories. Rather than proposing a new backbone or fusion architecture, AENet repurposes standard detection models (Faster R-CNN, ATSS, etc.) and adds a saliency-based localization step in the auxiliary view, leveraging the positional invariance of X-ray imaging geometry to transfer category labels across views. The expert-model cropping strategy is a novel repurposing of single-image classification/detection on tightly-cropped patches, applied here as a refinement stage rather than a primary detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LDXray dataset (353K instances, 12 categories, dual-view pairs) fills a critical gap: prior dual-view X-ray benchmarks were small (≤5K pairs) and limited in category diversity, making it difficult to train and evaluate models that handle the long-tail of challenging items. The consistent gains across seven architecturally diverse detectors suggest the dual-view mechanism is a plug-and-play enhancement rather than a model-specific trick. Open questions remain: the auxiliary view relies on saliency as a proxy for ground truth (no human annotations exist for I₂), the positional-invariance assumption may break under non-standard scanner configurations, and the near-zero performance on non-metallic lighters (NL) indicates a fundamental limitation of X-ray imaging for certain materials that no amount of dual-view reasoning can overcome.
