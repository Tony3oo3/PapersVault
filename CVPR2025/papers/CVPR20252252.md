## Problem

LMMs process entire images indiscriminately, so irrelevant visual content in complex scenes amplifies hallucinations and degrades fine-grained understanding. Existing visual-search solutions like SEAL require vocabulary expansion, dedicated localization/heatmap modules, and costly fine-tuning, limiting their applicability to general-purpose models such as Qwen2-VL. The paper asks whether a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism can let LMMs dynamically focus on task-relevant regions by collaborating with off-the-shelf visual experts.

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary-resolution RGB image and a natural-language text query (e.g., a VQA question). It produces a final text answer obtained via weighted multi-node voting across the focus tree, along with the focused image sub-region (a cropped bounding box) that the LMM should attend to. The visual expert (Lang-SAM) is queried with text cues to return detection boxes and segmentation masks, which define the image component of each focus state; no fixed input resolution or token budget is imposed on the LMM side.

## Method

DyFo frames visual search as a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) over a focus tree, where each node is a focus state f = (I, T) pairing an image region with a semantic text cue. A Focus Adjuster iteratively updates the state using two human-inspired actions—Semantic Focus (conjunction-search-style zoom onto query-relevant targets) and Semantic Scatter (divergence-style enlargement to avoid over-narrowing)—with the LMM refining T and the visual expert (Lang-SAM) updating I. Node rewards are computed as a consensus signal: an indicator of image-text semantic consistency multiplied by the effective area ratio, which encourages filtering irrelevant content while penalizing overly small or misaligned crops. The UCT selection policy balances exploration and exploitation over the tree, and the final answer is produced by a multi-grained weighted vote across all tree nodes, weighted by their accumulated rewards. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no vocabulary changes, additional localization heads, or architectural modifications to the LMM.

## Results

On POPE-COCO (hallucination benchmark), DyFo-Q (Qwen2-VL backbone) achieves 92.13% accuracy and 91.71 F1 under random sampling, 89.97% accuracy under popular sampling, and 86.77% accuracy under adversarial sampling, consistently outperforming the base Qwen2-VL (90.07/88.57/85.67) and all compared LMMs. On V* Bench (fine-grained high-resolution understanding), DyFo-Q reaches 81.15% overall accuracy (80.00% attribute, 82.89% spatial), surpassing SEAL (75.39%), GPT-4V (54.97%), and the base Qwen2-VL (72.25%). DyFo-L (LLaVA-1.5 backbone) also improves over its base model on both benchmarks, e.g., 91.40% vs. 88.73% accuracy on POPE-COCO random and 59.16% vs. 48.68% on V* Bench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyFo-Q outperforms all 10 compared LMMs on POPE-COCO across all three sampling settings, with the largest margins over MiniGPT4 (+12.46 Acc random) and mPLUG-Owl (+38.16 Acc random). On V* Bench, DyFo-Q (81.15% overall) surpasses the training-based visual search method SEAL (75.39%) by 5.76 points, as well as GPT-4V (54.97%), Gemini Pro (48.16%), and Bard (37.17%), despite requiring no additional training or architectural modifications. DyFo-L (LLaVA-1.5 backbone) also beats its base model LLaVA-1.5 (48.68% → 59.16% on V* Bench) and InstructBLIP (34.02%), though it trails SEAL on the spatial subtask (53.95% vs. 76.31%). Compared to tool-using pipelines (MM-React 41.36%, Visual ChatGPT 37.69%, VisProg 41.36% on V* Bench), DyFo-Q achieves substantially higher accuracy, demonstrating that the MCTS-based focus search is more effective than multi-tool orchestration for fine-grained tasks. The method also consistently improves over standalone visual experts (Lang-SAM alone: 88.87% Acc random on POPE-COCO vs. DyFo-Q's 92.13%), confirming that the LMM–expert collaboration yields mutual enhancement beyond either component alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyFo carves new ground by repurposing Monte Carlo Tree Search (originally from game AI) as a coordination mechanism between an off-the-shelf LMM and a visual expert (Lang-SAM), eliminating the need for the vocabulary expansion, localization modules, and LMM fine-tuning that prior visual-search-for-LMMs methods like SEAL (V*) require. Conceptually, it draws on cognitive science's model of human visual search (fixations and saccades) to design a two-action space (semantic focus and semantic scatter) that simulates endogenous orienting and divergence. The bidirectional LMM–expert interaction loop, where the LMM's text output guides the expert's cropping and the expert's image output refines the LMM's textual focus, is a novel architectural arrangement not present in prior tool-use pipelines (Visual ChatGPT, MM-React) that treat the LMM as a one-shot dispatcher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of DyFo is practically significant: it can be attached to any existing LMM (fixed- or dynamic-resolution) without retraining, lowering the barrier to improving fine-grained visual understanding and reducing hallucination. However, the approach introduces non-trivial inference-time computational overhead from MCTS iterations and repeated visual-expert calls, and the action space is limited to two hand-designed actions, raising questions about scalability to more complex or multi-object search scenarios. Open questions include whether the consensus-based reward (semantic consistency between image and text) generalizes to tasks where the correct region is not uniquely identifiable, and whether the method's gains persist as LMMs themselves improve in native fine-grained perception.
