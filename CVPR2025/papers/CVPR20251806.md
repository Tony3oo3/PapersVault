## Problem

Most grasp detection methods predict 6D poses directly from partial observations without explicitly modeling object geometry, leading to collisions and unstable contacts. Multi-view reconstruction approaches that do model geometry are impractical in confined spaces and introduce significant computational overhead. Additionally, no existing dataset simultaneously provides large-scale photorealistic RGB-D images, high-resolution 3D reconstructions, and physically validated dense grasp annotations for training zero-shot grasping.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB-D image as input; the RGB channel is encoded by a frozen ResNeXt backbone, the depth map is used to unproject image features into per-object 3D point clouds (segmented via fine-tuned SAM2 instance masks), and these are converted into octrees. The output is a per-object octree containing SDF values, surface normals, and dense 6D grasp poses (graspness score, force-closure quality, view direction, angle, width, depth) at the deepest octree level, followed by a post-hoc refinement that adjusts width and depth via contact points and discards colliding grasps using the reconstructed shape.

## Method

ZeroGrasp is built on an octree-based U-Net wrapped in a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) that models single-view reconstruction uncertainty via a latent Gaussian code. Two key architectural innovations improve accuracy: (1) a multi-object encoder that applies K transformer blocks with RoPE positional encoding in the latent space to model inter-object spatial relationships for collision-free reconstruction, and (2) 3D occlusion fields that cast rays from the camera to subdivided voxel blocks, producing self- and inter-object occlusion flags encoded by 3D CNNs to capture visibility in occluded regions. Training maximizes the ELBO with economic supervision for grasp poses (binary cross-entropy for occupancy, L1 for SDF/normals, cross-entropy for grasp parameters). A lightweight post-hoc refinement step then adjusts gripper width and depth to align fingertips with nearest contact points on the reconstruction and discards poses that collide with the predicted shape, replacing the slower multi-view TSDF-based optimization of prior work.

## Results

On the GraspNet-1Billion benchmark, ZeroGrasp achieves state-of-the-art grasp pose prediction with AP of 70.53 (Seen), 62.51 (Similar), and 26.46 (Novel), outperforming the previous best (EconomicGrasp) by 2.32, 1.32, and 0.98 AP points respectively. With fine-tuning on ZeroGrasp-11B (Ours+FT), performance further improves to 72.43, 65.45, and 28.49 AP. For 3D reconstruction, ZeroGrasp achieves the lowest Chamfer distance (6.05 mm) and highest F1-Score@10mm (84.08%) on GraspNet-1B, and 6.73 mm CD / 80.86% F1 on the hardest ReOcS split, surpassing Minkowski, OCNN, and OctMAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroGrasp outperforms all 12 baselines across every AP metric on the GraspNet-1Billion benchmark. Against the closest competitor EconomicGrasp [67], ZeroGrasp gains +2.32 AP on Seen, +1.32 on Similar, and +0.98 on Novel; with fine-tuning (Ours+FT), the margins widen to +4.22, +4.26, and +3.01. Compared to GSNet [57], the improvement is +3.41 AP (Seen) and +2.15 AP (Novel), while against Ma et al. [70] it is +6.70 AP (Seen) and +1.83 AP (Novel). For 3D reconstruction on ReOcS (Hard split), ZeroGrasp achieves 6.73 mm Chamfer distance, beating Minkowski [8] (9.11 mm), OCNN [43] (8.69 mm), and OctMAE [10] (6.76 mm), with the largest gap over Minkowski. Notably, ZeroGrasp is the only method that simultaneously predicts both reconstruction and grasp poses (G+R), whereas CenterGrasp [59] is the only other baseline doing so but performs far worse (1.60 Novel AP vs. 26.46). The Ours+FT variant demonstrates that pre-training on the 11.3B-grasp ZeroGrasp-11B synthetic dataset before fine-tuning on GraspNet-1B yields consistent 2–3% AP gains over training on GraspNet-1B alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroGrasp builds on the octree-based U-Net architecture from O-CNN [9] and the multi-object latent-space transformer from OctMAE [10], wrapping them in a CVAE framework (inspired by HUMOR [63]) to model reconstruction uncertainty. It departs from prior grasp detection methods (GSNet, EconomicGrasp) that predict grasps directly from partial observations without explicit geometry, by coupling 3D reconstruction with grasp prediction in a single forward pass and using the reconstruction for contact-based refinement and collision detection. The grasp refinement strategy is a simplified adaptation of Ma et al.'s [3] contact-based optimization, replacing their multi-view TSDF pipeline with a lightweight single-view reconstruction-based approach. The economic supervision training strategy from EconomicGrasp [67] is adopted for efficient grasp pose learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ZeroGrasp-11B dataset (11.3B physics-validated grasps across 12K diverse objects from Objaverse-LVIS) addresses a critical gap in zero-shot grasping data, and the ReOcS dataset provides a much-needed benchmark for evaluating reconstruction under occlusion. The practical finding that reconstruction-based collision detection substantially outperforms depth-map-based collision detection (70.53 vs. 59.93 AP) offers a clear recipe for improving grasp quality in cluttered scenes. Limitations include the lack of incremental or multi-view reconstruction (important for wrist-mounted cameras) and no modeling of placement poses, both of which the authors identify as important future directions. The 75% real-robot success rate with purely synthetic training demonstrates strong sim-to-real transfer but remains below what is needed for reliable deployment.
