## Problem

Despite the commercial maturity of fine-grained sketch-based image retrieval (FG-SBIR), no research addresses efficient inference specifically for sketch data. The paper demonstrates that state-of-the-art lightweight photo networks (e.g., MobileNetV2) fail on sketches, losing ~37% retrieval accuracy compared to VGG-16, because their limited parameters cannot accommodate the fine-grained, sparse, and abstract nature of human drawings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250108/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a freehand sketch in vector format (a sequence of 5D points: x, y coordinates plus three pen-state values, truncated to T_max=100 via Douglas-Peucker) and produces a retrieved photo match from a pre-computed gallery. Uniquely, the canvas-size selector first predicts a discrete rasterization resolution from {32×32, 64×64, 128×128, 256×256} before the sketch is rendered and fed to the retrieval network, exploiting the fact that vector sketches can be freely re-rendered at any resolution without quality loss.

## Method

The framework has two plug-and-play stages. SketchyNetV1 performs cross-modal relational knowledge distillation: instead of regressing features or logits, it distils inter-feature Euclidean distances (sketch-photo, sketch-negative, photo-negative) from a large teacher (VGG-16) to a small student (MobileNetV2) via Huber loss, preserving the structural orientation of the teacher's discriminative embedding space. SketchyNetV2 adds an RL-based canvas-size selector: a 1-layer GRU encodes the vector sketch, a linear head outputs a categorical distribution over K canvas-sizes, and the policy is trained via policy gradient where the frozen retrieval model acts as critic, rewarding both retrieval accuracy (inverse rank + negative triplet loss) and low FLOPs (a normalised FLOPs constraint regularisation). This yields a 99.37% FLOPs reduction (40.18G → 0.254G) while retaining accuracy (33.03% vs 32.77% Top-1 on ShoeV2).

## Results

On QMUL-ShoeV2, SketchyNetV2 (with VGG-16 Triplet-SN as teacher) achieves 32.77% Top-1 and 78.21% Top-10 accuracy at only 0.254G FLOPs and 2.27M parameters, compared to the full B-VGG16-SN baseline at 33.03% Top-1 with 40.18G FLOPs and 14.71M parameters — a 99.37% FLOPs reduction with only 0.26% accuracy loss. The same plug-and-play framework applied to StyleVAE retains 35.88% Top-1 (vs. 36.47% original) at 0.251G FLOPs, and to Triplet-RL retains 33.26% (vs. 34.10%) at 0.255G. On FS-COCO (scene-level), SketchyNetV2 achieves 4.69% Top-1 at 0.407G FLOPs versus 5.16% at 40.18G for the full model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four SoTA FG-SBIR methods (Triplet-SN, HOLEIF-SN, Triplet-RL, StyleVAE), SketchyNetV2 retains within 0.5–0.9% Top-1 accuracy of each respective teacher while reducing FLOPs by 20–24× (e.g., 32.77% vs. 33.03% for VGG16-SN; 35.88% vs. 36.47% for StyleVAE). Against the three KD baselines (B-Regress, B-AKD, B-PKT), SketchyNetV2 wins decisively on accuracy (32.77% vs. 22.82–24.31%) at comparable FLOPs (~0.25–0.27G), demonstrating that cross-modal relational distance distillation outperforms feature regression, attention transfer, and probabilistic transfer in the sketch-photo retrieval setting. Against the three resizing strategies (B-BFR, B-DRS, B-Crop), SketchyNetV2 achieves higher accuracy (32.77% vs. 20.32–25.12%) at dramatically lower FLOPs (0.254G vs. 2.6–6.6G), since the vector-space canvas selector avoids costly convolutional operations on full-resolution rasters. Against the two pruning baselines (B-ThiNet, B-Prune), SketchyNetV2 is overwhelmingly superior on both accuracy (32.77% vs. 3.92–7.51%) and FLOPs (0.254G vs. 17.3–22.3G), as pruning was not designed for the sparse nature of sketches. On categorical SBIR (Sketchy-ext), T-StyleVAE achieves 0.886 mAP@all vs. StyleVAE's 0.905, and T-D2S achieves 0.798 vs. B-D2S's 0.810, confirming the framework generalises beyond fine-grained retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the triplet Siamese FG-SBIR framework of Yu et al. (CVPR 2016) and the knowledge distillation paradigm of Romero et al. (ICLR 2015), but departs from both in important ways. Rather than designing a new efficient architecture from scratch, it repurposes existing photo-efficient backbones (MobileNetV2) via a cross-modal relational distance distillation loss that preserves pairwise geometric structure in the teacher's embedding space—addressing the incompatibility of standard logit or feature-regression KD with continuous cross-modal retrieval. The second component, an RL-trained canvas-size selector operating in vector space, is a novel application of policy-gradient methods to input-resolution selection, leveraging the unique property that sketches are natively vector-based and can be re-rasterised at arbitrary resolutions without quality loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic investigation into computational efficiency specifically for sketch data, demonstrating that off-the-shelf photo-efficient networks fail on sketches and providing a plug-n-play framework to fix this. The 99.37% FLOPs reduction (40.18G → 0.254G) while retaining near-identical accuracy (32.77% vs 33.03%) has direct implications for edge deployment of sketch retrieval, with measured iPhone 13 inference dropping from 51ms to 18ms. Open questions include whether combining KD with quantisation or pruning yields further gains (the paper shows promising but modest improvements), whether the canvas selector can inform photo-side resolution selection, and whether more powerful teachers (e.g., DINOv2) would improve the student's ceiling. A limitation is that the canvas selector, while shown to generalise across four teacher models (89.21% agreement), is still trained per-retrieval-model and may not transfer to fundamentally different architectures.
