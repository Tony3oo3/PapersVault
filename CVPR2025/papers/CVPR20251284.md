## Problem

Existing sketch animation methods either require users to specify precise motion paths and keyframes (reducing animation to a digital puppet show) or are constrained to stroke-by-stroke vector displacement that cannot add, remove, or freely reinterpret strokes across frames. This fundamentally limits the expressive freedom of traditional hand-drawn animation, where artists redraw and reinterpret subjects to convey dynamic 3D motion in 2D sketches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251284/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single raster sketch image (H×W×3, post-processed to black strokes on white canvas) and a free-form text prompt describing desired motion. It produces a sequence of M=10 raster animation frames at the same spatial resolution, where each frame is a full line-drawing that may contain entirely new strokes not present in the input. For longer animations, the final frame of one generated video is fed as the input sketch to the next, enabling chained multi-segment animations with different text prompts per segment.

## Method

FlipSketch fine-tunes a ModelScope T2V 3D UNet via LoRA (rank 4, 2500 steps) on synthetic text-to-vector-animation pairs to adapt the video diffusion prior to sketch-style generation. A reference frame mechanism DDIM-inverts the input sketch with null prompts to obtain canonical noise at t=T for the first frame, while remaining frames are sampled from N(0,I). During denoising, two mechanisms preserve sketch identity: (i) Iterative Frame Alignment backpropagates an L2 loss between the predicted and ground-truth denoising signal of the reference frame to refine sampled noise at early timesteps (t > τ₁), and (ii) Dual-Attention Composition replaces portions of spatial and temporal self-attention with cross-attention against reference query-key pairs at early timesteps (t > τ₂), injecting coarse and fine-grained sketch features into all frames. A scalar parameter λ scales the reference key in temporal attention to trade off motion dynamism against identity fidelity.

## Results

On CLIP-based metrics, FlipSketch achieves the best text-to-video alignment (0.172) among all compared methods, outperforming Live-Sketch (0.142), DynamiCrafter (0.127), and the T2V LoRA baseline (0.158). For sketch-to-video consistency, Live-Sketch leads slightly (0.965 vs. 0.956), but FlipSketch at λ=1 closes this gap (0.968). In the user study, FlipSketch wins on all three subjective metrics—consistency (0.54), faithfulness (0.54), and mean opinion score (0.70)—compared to Live-Sketch (0.51, 0.44, 0.63) and the T2V LoRA baseline (0.26, 0.27, 0.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlipSketch outperforms Live-Sketch [22] on text-to-video alignment by a wide margin (0.172 vs. 0.142), demonstrating superior distillation of motion priors, while Live-Sketch retains a slight edge in sketch-to-video consistency (0.965 vs. 0.956) due to its stroke-constrained approach that inherently preserves structure. Against I2V baselines, FlipSketch substantially outperforms DynamiCrafter [61] on both metrics (0.956 vs. 0.780 S2V; 0.172 vs. 0.127 T2V), as DynamiCrafter suffers from the sketch-photo domain gap. SVD [10] achieves reasonable sketch consistency (0.917) but cannot be evaluated on text alignment and also fails to preserve sketch identity qualitatively. In the user study, FlipSketch beats Live-Sketch on all three subjective scores (consistency 0.54 vs. 0.51, faithfulness 0.54 vs. 0.44, MOS 0.70 vs. 0.63), confirming that the motion-fidelity trade-off is resolved in favor of the proposed method. The T2V LoRA baseline (without sketch conditioning) is clearly inferior, scoring 0.26/0.27/0.53 in the user study, underscoring the importance of the reference frame and attention composition mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlipSketch departs from the dominant vector-based sketch animation paradigm (Live-Sketch, stroke displacement methods) by treating sketches as unconstrained raster frames, enabling dynamic transformations like adding or removing strokes that vector approaches fundamentally cannot support. The method is built on three prior pillars: ModelScope T2V as the motion-prior backbone, null-text DDIM inversion for extracting a canonical reference noise from the input sketch, and TF-ICON's attention injection strategy adapted here into a dual spatial-temporal composition for video. Unlike I2V approaches (SVD, DynamiCrafter) that suffer from a sketch-photo domain gap, FlipSketch fine-tunes the T2V model directly on synthetic sketch animations via LoRA, closing the style gap at the training level rather than the conditioning level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight LoRA (rank 4, 2500 steps) on a pre-trained T2V model, combined with inference-time attention manipulation, is sufficient to produce coherent sketch animations that outperform both vector-based and I2V baselines on both CLIP metrics and user studies. This lowers the barrier to sketch animation from requiring precise motion paths or keyframes to simply doodling and describing motion. Key limitations remain: the model inherits a stylistic bias toward CLIPasso-like sketches from its single-style training data, struggles with abstract or geometrically inaccurate inputs, and is bounded by the motion priors of the underlying T2V model (producing extra limbs or inconsistent geometry). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to diverse sketch styles, scales to longer sequences beyond the stitching heuristic, and can be extended to 3D or multi-view sketch animation.
