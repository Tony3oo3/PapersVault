## Problem

Existing multi-viewpoint lidar reconstruction methods either discard indirect light (using point clouds or direct-only time-resolved measurements) or rely on non-physically-based neural representations that cannot render under novel illumination. No prior technique performs physically-based inverse rendering from multi-viewpoint, time-resolved measurements of propagating light, leaving indirect light transport effects unmodeled in 3D reconstruction and view synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251409/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view photon count histograms from a picosecond-pulsed flash lidar system (SPAD-based), where both the light source and sensor move together across viewpoints, along with calibrated extrinsics. Beyond standard geometry and material recovery, the model produces time-resolved lidar frames at arbitrary viewpoints, decomposes captured measurements into direct and indirect components, and supports time-resolved relighting with arbitrary pulse patterns. Notably, the system can also be supervised with continuous-wave time-of-flight or steady-state intensity images (derived by convolving or integrating the time-resolved signal) while still recovering full time-resolved propagation videos.

## Method

The core contribution is a time-resolved radiance cache that replaces recursive path tracing with an amortized neural lookup of incident radiance at every point and direction, parameterized by a multi-resolution hash encoding for appearance and a separate network for geometry (density and normals). The outgoing radiance is decomposed into a direct component (a neural BRDF network evaluated against the known lidar source with inverse-square falloff and speed-of-light delay) and an indirect component (a split-sum approximation where two neural networks predict the integrated BRDF and integrated incident radiance, the latter conditioned on light source position). Time-resolved volume rendering along both primary and secondary rays incorporates per-sample time delays (t_k/c) to propagate the pulse through the scene. Training minimizes a composite loss combining photometric data fitting, cache self-supervision, and a radiometric prior that enforces consistency between the cache-predicted and physically-computed direct/indirect components, with a tonemapping-inspired weighting that emphasizes darker regions.

## Results

On simulated scenes, the method achieves 30.99 dB PSNR, 0.89 SSIM, and 0.76 T-IOU for novel-view lidar rendering, substantially outperforming T-NeRF (22.44 dB, 0.71 SSIM, 0.58 T-IOU) and FWP++ (29.00 dB, 0.87 SSIM, 0.73 T-IOU). The geometry recovery metrics show the largest gains: MAE of 8.45 and L1 depth error of 0.21, compared to 22.80/0.47 for FWP++ and 28.00/0.59 for T-NeRF. On captured scenes, the method achieves 27.39 dB PSNR and 0.54 T-IOU, slightly trailing FWP++ (28.45 dB, 0.55 T-IOU) due to the tighter physical constraints of the model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: T-NeRF [47], which models only direct light transport, and FWP++ [48], a modified version of Flying with Photons that predicts time-resolved radiance at every point without a physically-based rendering model. On simulated scenes, the proposed method clearly outperforms both: it achieves 1.99 dB higher PSNR and 0.03 higher T-IOU than FWP++, while the geometry metrics (MAE 8.45 vs. 22.80; L1 depth 0.21 vs. 0.47) show a 2–3× improvement over FWP++, reflecting the benefit of physically-based indirect light modeling. T-NeRF, lacking any indirect light model, produces floating artifacts and the worst geometry (MAE 28.00, L1 depth 0.59). On captured scenes, FWP++ edges out the proposed method by ~1 dB PSNR and 0.01 T-IOU, which the authors attribute to the physically constrained model being more sensitive to calibration and model mismatch in real hardware. The key qualitative advantage is that the proposed method recovers correct normals in regions with strong indirect light (wall corners, specular reflections) where FWP++ introduces geometric errors to explain those effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: time-resolved neural rendering (T-NeRF for direct light, Flying with Photons for direct+indirect) and radiance-cache-based physically-based inverse rendering (Flash Cache, NERO, and the original Ward radiance cache). The key departure is introducing a time-resolved radiance cache that stores infinite-bounce radiance arriving at any point from any direction, parameterized by neural networks with a Disney-GGX BRDF, thereby grounding time-resolved lidar rendering in a physically-based framework for the first time. It also adopts infrastructure from Zip-NeRF (hash encoding, distortion losses) and Hadadan et al.'s radiometric prior to constrain the cache against the full rendering equation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to recover both geometry and material properties from multi-view time-resolved lidar while enabling physically meaningful applications like time-resolved relighting and direct/indirect decomposition—capabilities that were previously impossible without a physically-based model. The state-of-the-art geometry reconstruction under strong indirect light (MAE 8.45 vs. 22.80 for FWP++ in simulation) demonstrates the practical value of physical constraints. However, the method requires over a day of GPU optimization per scene, shows slight underperformance on captured data relative to the less-constrained FWP++ baseline (suggesting model-mismatch sensitivity), and does not address non-line-of-sight imaging. Open questions include whether faster representations (3D Gaussian ray tracing, EVER) can reduce training cost, and whether the framework extends to unconstrained NLOS settings.
