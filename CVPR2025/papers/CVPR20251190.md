## Problem

Fine-grained open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL) requires separating in-distribution from out-of-distribution samples whose visual differences are extremely subtle, yet existing CLIP-based methods focus on general global attributes and fail to capture the fine-grained local details needed for this separation. Standard OSSL techniques relying on maximum softmax probability or ad-hoc thresholds break down when ID and OOD feature distributions are tightly intermingled, as is typical in fine-grained classification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed at ViT-B/16 resolution with both weak and strong augmentations for semi-supervised training) paired with text prompts consisting of 16 learnable prefix vectors concatenated with the class-name word embedding. It outputs per-class prediction probabilities for ID classification and implicitly identifies OOD samples via a confidence threshold (δ=0.95) on the maximum predicted probability, which gates whether pseudo-labels are applied during self-training.

## Method

CFSG-CLIP is a dual-branch architecture built on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 with learnable adapters. The coarse-guidance branch employs a semantic filtering module that first selects top-k patch tokens by cosine similarity to a CoOp-style textual prompt, then re-weights them by their similarity to the global [CLS] feature to produce an aggregated local representation. The fine-guidance branch applies a visual-semantic injection strategy: the coarse branch's aggregated local features replace the original [CLS] token and are injected into the first D transformer blocks of the visual encoder, propagating category-relevant visual priors through self-attention to refine patch-level features toward fine-grained details. Both branches are trained with a FixMatch-style objective (supervised cross-entropy on labeled data plus confidence-gated consistency loss on unlabeled data) using separate coarse and fine prompts, with the two branches optimized independently.

## Results

CFSG-CLIP achieves the best classification accuracy across four fine-grained OSSL benchmarks. On the most challenging FGVCAircraft dataset, it reaches 61.09% (5-shot) and 73.56% (20-shot), surpassing the second-best method MaPLe by 8.66% and 9.23% respectively. On Stanford Cars and CUB-200-2011, it leads by 2.22–2.74% and 1.43–2.98% over MaPLe. For open-set balanced accuracy (Table 2), CFSG-CLIP similarly dominates, e.g., 59.92% vs 51.79% on FGVCAircraft 5-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFSG-CLIP outperforms all seven baselines on three of four datasets (Stanford Cars, CUB-200-2011, FGVCAircraft) across both 5-shot and 20-shot settings. The largest margin is on FGVCAircraft, where it beats MaPLe (the strongest competitor) by 8.66% (5-shot) and 9.23% (20-shot), and beats CLIP zero-shot by over 30 points. On Stanford Dogs with 5 labeled samples, MaPLe narrowly edges CFSG-CLIP by 0.16% (85.64 vs 85.48), but CFSG-CLIP reverses the lead by 1.78% at 20 samples (89.42 vs 87.64). Against adapter-based methods (CLIP-Adapter, CLIP-LORA) and prompt-based methods (CoOp, LoCoOp, PLOT), CFSG-CLIP consistently leads by 2–15% depending on dataset difficulty. On the Semi-Aves dataset (Table 3), CFSG-CLIP achieves 65.63% (ID-only unlabeled) and 63.69% (mixed ID+OOD unlabeled), surpassing MaPLe by 5.24% and 4.01% respectively, confirming robustness under the open-set condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP-based prompt learning (CoOp, LoCoOp, PLOT, MaPLe) to the fine-grained open-set semi-supervised learning setting, adopting FixMatch's consistency-confidence training loop and IoMatch's OSSL evaluation protocol. The key architectural departure is a dual-branch coarse-to-fine design: the coarse branch filters patch-level features using both textual prompts and global visual features (whereas prior local-feature methods like LoCoOp and GalLoP rely solely on text), and the fine branch injects the filtered local features back into the visual encoder as visual-semantic priors, a mechanism not present in prior CLIP adaptation work. The method thus sits at the intersection of prompt learning, semi-supervised learning, and fine-grained recognition, combining elements of each into a unified pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fine-grained OSSL is a practically important but under-explored scenario where tight inter-class boundaries make standard pseudo-labeling and threshold-based OOD rejection unreliable; this paper demonstrates that progressive semantic filtering can substantially close the gap on the hardest benchmarks (e.g., ~9% over MaPLe on FGVCAircraft). The authors acknowledge the method is specifically designed for fine-grained datasets and leave open whether it transfers to coarse-grained settings or rarer fine-grained categories. A broader open question is whether the coarse-to-fine injection strategy generalizes beyond CLIP's ViT backbone or to other vision-language architectures with different tokenization schemes.
