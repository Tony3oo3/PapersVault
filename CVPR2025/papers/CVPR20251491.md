## Problem

Existing monocular scene flow estimation methods lack generalization beyond their training domain (typically autonomous driving), unlike large-scale models that have achieved zero-shot transfer for depth and segmentation. The paper identifies three root causes: geometry and motion are entangled in 2D observations and require joint reasoning, labeled scene flow data is scarce and scale-inconsistent across datasets, and the choice of scene flow parameterization significantly affects quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images (H×W×3) from a single potentially moving camera at two timestamps, along with camera intrinsics K, but no camera poses. It produces three H×W×3 maps: two pointmaps X̂₁ and X̂₂ (both expressed in camera C₁'s coordinate frame at times t₁ and t₂ respectively) and a 3D scene flow offset map Ŝ that encodes the mapping from (C₁, t₁) to (C₂, t₂). All outputs are in metric units, and the scene flow is parameterized as camera-space 3D offsets rather than end-points or depth-change plus optical flow.

## Method

The architecture is a ViT with dual weight-sharing encoder branches and cross-attended decoders per input frame, followed by three DPT-based heads predicting X̂₁, X̂₂, and Ŝ from shared intermediate features. It is initialized with MASt3R's pretrained weights (CroCoV2 backbone) to bootstrap geometry estimation, and the joint training of geometry and motion heads allows each to inform the other through the shared representation. Training uses a scale-adaptive loss that normalizes predictions and ground truth for non-metric datasets while preserving metric supervision for metric ones, combined with an auxiliary optical flow loss (weight 0.1) that enforces consistency between the predicted pointmap and offset via 2D projection. The data recipe aggregates over 1M samples from six diverse synthetic datasets (SHIFT, Dynamic Replica, VKITTI2, MOVi-F, PointOdyssey, Spring), with pseudo scene flow computed from depth, poses, and optical flow where annotations are missing.

## Results

On KITTI (in-domain), the method achieves EPE 0.452 and AccS 0.398, slightly outperforming Self-Mono-SF (EPE 0.454, AccS 0.345). In the zero-shot (out-of-domain) setting on KITTI, Ours-exclude achieves EPE 0.641, dramatically outperforming D+OF baselines (DUSt3R: 2.088, MASt3R: 3.320, MonST3R: 3.594) and OpticalExpansion (2.537). On VKITTI2 (in-domain), the method reaches EPE 0.190 vs. Self-Mono-SF's 0.294. On Spring (out-of-domain), Ours-exclude achieves EPE 0.014 and AccS 0.992, far surpassing Self-Mono-SF (EPE 1.005, AccS 0.251) and OpticalExpansion (EPE 0.029).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms Self-Mono-SF in-domain on both KITTI (EPE 0.452 vs. 0.454) and VKITTI2 (EPE 0.190 vs. 0.294), and dramatically in the zero-shot setting: on Spring, Ours-exclude achieves EPE 0.014 and AccS 0.992 while Self-Mono-SF (trained only on KITTI) collapses to EPE 1.005 and AccS 0.251. Against D+OF baselines, the joint approach is consistently superior: on KITTI out-of-domain, Ours-exclude (EPE 0.641) beats DUSt3R (2.088), MASt3R (3.320), MonST3R (3.594), and OpticalExpansion (2.537) by 3–5×. On VKITTI2 in-domain, the gap is even wider (EPE 0.190 vs. 5.914 for MASt3R and 8.822 for DUSt3R). OpticalExpansion, which relies on a separate depth estimator, is the strongest D+OF competitor on Spring (EPE 0.029) but still trails Ours-exclude (0.014) by 2×. The D+OF baselines also show poor metric depth (AbsR-m near 0.95–0.98 for DUSt3R/MonST3R), indicating they fail to recover correct scale, whereas the joint method maintains competitive metric depth (AbsR-m 0.236 on KITTI in-domain).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DUSt3R/MASt3R line of large-scale geometry prediction to the dynamic scene setting by adding a scene flow offset head to the shared CroCoV2 ViT backbone, rather than treating motion as a separate problem. It departs from prior monocular scene flow methods (Self-Mono-SF, RAFT-MSF, EMR-MSF) that were confined to autonomous driving and relied on decomposing the task into depth + optical flow subproblems, instead predicting geometry and motion jointly in a single feedforward pass. The scale-adaptive optimization mechanism, inspired by MiDaS's scale-invariant loss for depth, is adapted here to handle mixed metric and relative-scale datasets simultaneously, and the choice of camera-space 3D offsets (CSO) over end-point or ΔD+OF parameterizations is validated through systematic ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first monocular scene flow method to demonstrate zero-shot generalization to in-the-wild scenes (DAVIS, RoboTAP) beyond autonomous driving, making the task practically viable for AR, robotics, and other applications that were previously blocked by domain-specific training. The finding that joint motion training improves depth estimation in dynamic scenes suggests that geometry and motion are more deeply coupled than prior work assumed, potentially informing future unified 3D understanding models. Open questions include decomposing camera ego-motion from scene motion (which remain entangled in the current formulation), extending to multi-frame video sequences, and whether the scale-adaptive training strategy generalizes to even more heterogeneous data sources.
