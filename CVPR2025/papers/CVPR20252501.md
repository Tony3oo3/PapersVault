## Problem

Speech-driven 3D facial animation methods have largely focused on learning mesh/geometry motion while ignoring dynamic textures (wrinkles, pore compression), which significantly reduces rendering realism and can cause an uncanny valley effect. This gap is compounded by the absence of high-quality 4D datasets with temporal-consistent, pore-level dynamic textures, and by the lack of a principled approach to jointly model the intricate correlation between facial motion and texture variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252501/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a T-frame speech audio sequence (each frame containing k samples) encoded by a pretrained HuBERT model and produces a synchronized sequence of 3D facial mesh vertex offsets (8280 vertices, 16494 faces) and 8K-resolution dynamic wrinkle maps (per-pixel ratio relative to a neutral texture). During training, textures are downsampled to 512×512 for efficiency, and a separate super-resolution network upsamples the generated 512×512 wrinkle map back to 8K before compositing with the neutral texture. The system also accepts style pivots (mean latent codes from a reference identity) to independently control motion style and wrinkle style.

## Method

TexTalker first unifies the representation of geometry and texture by mapping vertex offsets to UV-space motion maps and expressing texture changes as wrinkle maps, then compresses each into an independent low-dimensional latent space via two VQGAN-style quantized autoencoders (codebook size 1024, latent grid 16×16×16). A transformer-based latent diffusion model is then trained to jointly denoise the concatenated motion-wrinkle latent sample under HuBERT audio guidance, using a windowed diffusion scheme (T_w=90, T_p=10) to capture long-term temporal dependencies. The key novelty is a pivot-based disentangled style injection: the style pivot is computed as the temporal mean of an identity's latent codes, and the LDM predicts only the style-free phoneme-relevant offset Δz from this pivot, so that motion and wrinkle styles can be swapped independently by adding back different pivots at inference time.

## Results

On TexTalk4D-Test-A, TexTalker achieves the best facial motion quality across all three metrics: LVE 1.49×10⁻² mm, MVE 2.34×10⁻² mm, and FDD 1.20×10⁻³ mm, outperforming FaceDiffuser (the closest competitor) by 2.6% in LVE and 26.8% in FDD. For dynamic texture generation, TexTalker attains PSNR 44.13 dB, SSIM 0.985, and LPIPS 0.0101, surpassing the image-translation baseline (Li et al.) by 1.79 dB in PSNR and 46% lower LPIPS. In the user study on TexTalk4D-Test-B, TexTalker scores 4.13/5 in realism and 3.97/5 in geometry-texture consistency, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TexTalker outperforms all three motion baselines on every metric. Against FaceFormer [18], TexTalker reduces LVE by 17.2% (1.49 vs 1.80) and FDD by 28.6% (1.20 vs 1.68), indicating substantially better lip-sync accuracy and upper-face dynamics. Against CodeTalker [65], the gains are similar in LVE (17.5% reduction) and MVE (16.4% reduction), with FDD improved by 12.9%. Against FaceDiffuser [52], the closest competitor, TexTalker leads by a narrow margin in LVE (1.49 vs 1.53) and MVE (2.34 vs 2.38) but achieves a decisive 26.8% improvement in FDD (1.20 vs 1.64), showing superior capture of style-relevant upper-face motions. For texture, TexTalker beats Li et al. [34] (Pix2PixHD-based image translation) by 1.79 dB in PSNR and 46% in LPIPS, and the user study confirms higher realism (4.13 vs 3.91) and geometry-texture consistency (3.97 vs 3.78). DiffPoseTalk [53] is only compared visually due to its FLAME-parameter-only interface, with no quantitative metrics reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established line of speech-driven 3D facial animation (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, FaceDiffuser, DiffPoseTalk) by adding dynamic texture generation as a co-output, a dimension that prior methods have largely ignored. Architecturally, it repurposes the VQGAN quantized autoencoder to compress both motion maps and wrinkle maps into discrete latent spaces, and employs a latent diffusion model with the windowed training strategy from DiffPoseTalk to capture long-term audio-motion-texture correlations. The key conceptual departure is the pivot-based style injection, which replaces one-hot or example-based style conditioning with a simple centroid of identity-specific latent codes, enabling disentangled motion and texture style control without additional networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TexTalk4D dataset (100 subjects, 100 minutes, 8K temporally-consistent textures with pore-level detail) fills a significant gap in academic 4D audio-visual resources, as prior datasets like Multiface and RenderMe-360 are limited in scale, resolution, or temporal consistency. The finding that joint motion-texture learning outperforms separate generation on most metrics, despite the risk of increased multi-modal ambiguity, suggests that geometry and texture carry complementary information that benefits mutual conditioning. However, the dataset's lack of linguistic, racial, and age diversity limits generalization claims, and the 512-resolution training (with a separate super-resolution step) means the full 8K fidelity is not end-to-end learned. Open questions include whether the approach scales to real-time inference and whether the pivot-based style representation generalizes to unseen identities without any per-identity data.
