## Problem

Prior work on 3D animal reconstruction has focused exclusively on the animal in isolation, neglecting the surrounding environment. No existing method concurrently reconstructs both 3D animals and their 3D natural habitat from a single image, yet environmental context (occlusions, physical boundaries, interactions) is essential for meaningful behavioral analysis in computational ethology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251828/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a natural outdoor scene and produces an executable Blender graphics program. The code is structured as an ordered sequence: scene-level attributes (sun intensity/elevation/size/strength/rotation, atmospheric density, ozone, dust, air density) appear first, followed by a ground-texture CLIP embedding, then objects sorted by descending pixel count, each specifying pixel count, 3D location, height, a 9-parameter rotation matrix, and a CLIP appearance embedding. The output is directly renderable in a standard 3D graphics engine to reproduce the observed scene.

## Method

Building on the IG-LLM framework, the method uses an instruction-tuned LLaMA-7b with a frozen CLIP visual tokenizer to autoregressively decode a CLIP image embedding into structured graphics code. The key architectural contribution is a CLIP projection head: a special [CLIP] token signals the LLM to bypass discrete tokenization and instead project its hidden state through a linear layer into continuous CLIP embedding space, enabling semantically meaningful appearance estimation for out-of-distribution objects rather than forcing exact discrete asset-name retrieval. A parallel [ROT] token routes the hidden state through an MLP to regress a continuous 9-parameter rotation matrix. Training uses cross-entropy on text tokens, MSE on rotation matrices (with symmetric orthogonalization), and cosine similarity on CLIP embeddings, with ±0.5% value fuzzing on scene attributes to prevent scene memorization and explicit pixel-count conditioning to aid saliency-based object ordering. The model is trained entirely on 1M synthetic images generated from a modified Infinigen pipeline (10,000 scenes × 100 views each, with 432,000 effective assets across 72 yaw orientations) yet generalizes to real-world animal-in-the-wild images.

## Results

On the in-distribution (ID) synthetic test set, the full RAW model achieves LPIPS 0.598, improving over the discrete-name IG-LLM baseline (0.720) by 17%, with CLIP cosine similarity rising from 0.748 to 0.815. On the real-world RAW-RW set, the full model reaches LPIPS 0.724 versus 0.772 for the baseline, and CLIP similarity jumps from 0.385 to 0.490, demonstrating meaningful syn-to-real generalization. The CLIP projection head alone (without fuzzing or pixel conditioning) already yields the largest single improvement, reducing ID LPIPS from 0.720 to 0.654.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external 3D scene reconstruction methods in its main tables; instead, it ablates its own design choices against the discrete-name IG-LLM baseline. The CLIP projection head is the dominant improvement: on ID it reduces LPIPS by 0.066 (0.720→0.654) and raises CLIP similarity by 0.058, while on RAW-RW it lifts CLIP similarity from 0.385 to 0.445, showing the model can semantically align real-world objects rather than defaulting to generic assets. Among embedding choices (Table 2), CLIP outperforms BioCLIP on both ID (LPIPS 0.598 vs 0.676) and RAW-RW (0.724 vs 0.730), suggesting BioCLIP's taxonomic fine-tuning hinders generalization. DINOv2 achieves the best ID LPIPS (0.597) and highest CLIP similarity (0.850) but underperforms CLIP on RAW-RW (LPIPS 0.743 vs 0.724), indicating its self-supervised feature space is harder for the LLM to map to real-world appearances. The authors note a YOLOX-6D-Pose baseline in supplementary material but do not include it in the main quantitative comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends IG-LLM [41], which used an LLM to decode CLIP embeddings into graphics code for simple primitive scenes, to the far more complex domain of natural outdoor environments containing animals and vegetation. It departs from prior animal-reconstruction work (Zuffi et al., Kanazawa et al., Wu et al.) that models animals in isolation by jointly reconstructing the surrounding 3D environment, and from indoor scene-reconstruction efforts (Avetisyan et al.) by targeting unstructured natural settings. The key architectural departure from IG-LLM is the CLIP projection head, which replaces discrete asset-name tokens with continuous semantic embeddings, addressing a fundamental limitation of token-based supervision for out-of-distribution generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to compositionally reconstruct both animals and their natural environment from a single image, opening a path toward 3D-grounded computational ethology where animal behavior can be analyzed in full habitat context rather than in an empty volume. The CLIP projection head is a clean, generalizable technique for any LLM-based generation task requiring continuous semantic output, and the 1M-image RAW dataset is a substantial resource for the community. Key limitations remain: assets are retrieved rather than generated (non-invertible procedural parameters), animal poses are static, and the model struggles with out-of-distribution camera configurations, leaving articulated pose estimation and broader asset diversity as important open questions.
