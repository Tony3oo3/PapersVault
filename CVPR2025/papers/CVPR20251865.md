## Problem

Existing text-guided image-to-image translation methods over-bind source-target correlation through attention map consistency or direct feature reuse, causing structure-semantic conflicts that produce contextually unnatural results when the goal is to dissolve a reference image into an entirely different text-described scene. No prior method simultaneously achieves faithful textual semantics and clearly discernible hidden structural cues in a single generated image, which is the defining requirement of optical illusion hidden pictures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251865/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB reference image (real photograph or synthetic, e.g., binary text) and a free-form text prompt describing the target scene as input, and produces a single 512×512 illusion picture where the reference image's structural cues are embedded within the text-described scene. The output is designed to be perceived differently at different viewing scales: zooming in reveals the text-described scene content, while zooming out exposes the hidden reference image structure. The method operates entirely on the off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion v1.5 backbone without any fine-tuning.

## Method

PTDiffusion constructs three parallel diffusion trajectories in the LDM latent space: a 1000-step DDIM inversion of the reference image, a 100-step reconstruction trajectory that denoises the inverted noise back to the reference, and a 100-step text-guided sampling trajectory from random noise. The core Phase Transfer Module (PTM) applies 2D FFT to both the reconstruction and sampling features at each step, linearly blends their phase spectra with a time-dependent coefficient, and recombines the fused phase with the sampling magnitude via IFFT, thereby transplanting structural information without altering the target's semantic content. A decayed blending schedule progressively reduces phase transfer intensity in later steps to avoid excessive structural penetration, and a dedicated refining stage (last 40% of steps) runs unconstrained to preserve image quality. Asynchronous phase transfer further enables controllable hidden-content discernibility by pre-estimating future reconstruction features and transferring their phase into earlier sampling steps (positive async distance) or vice versa (negative distance), all without any training or optimization.

## Results

On 80 reference images (each paired with ≥3 text prompts), PTDiffusion achieves the best scores across all three metrics: Aesthetic Score 6.37, CLIP Score 0.31, and LPIPS 0.64. The closest competitor, NTI, trails by 0.13 in Aesthetic Score (6.24) and 0.08 in CLIP Score (0.23). A 16-participant user study further confirms PTDiffusion's advantage, with the method outscoring all baselines by a large margin on both contextual naturalness and illusion balance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTDiffusion outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against NTI (the strongest baseline on Aesthetic Score at 6.24), PTDiffusion leads by 0.13 in Aesthetic Score and by a substantial 0.08 in CLIP Score (0.31 vs. 0.23), indicating markedly better text fidelity. Against FBSDiff (the closest on LPIPS at 0.56), PTDiffusion achieves 0.64, showing greater structural modification of the source image. SDEdit and ControlNet both struggle with the structure–semantic trade-off: SDEdit either overwhelms structural cues at high denoising strength or fails to reach target semantics at low strength, while ControlNet's fixed control weight similarly cannot balance both. PTI and FBSDiff produce some structure-semantic blending but suffer from visually unpleasant artifacts and less prominent hidden content. The user study (16 participants, 1–10 scale) confirms these quantitative findings, with PTDiffusion receiving the highest average ratings on both contextual naturalness and illusion balance, outscoring all competitors by a large margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

PTDiffusion builds on the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) and DDIM inversion as its backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior text-guided I2I methods (NTI, PTI, PAP, FBSDiff, SDEdit) that enforce source-target correlation through attention-map consistency, learnable embeddings, or direct feature reuse. Instead of these spatial-domain or attention-space binding mechanisms, the paper introduces a Fourier-domain phase transfer that disentangles structural information from semantic content, drawing on the signal-processing principle that phase encodes structure while magnitude encodes texture. It also contrasts with controllable T2I approaches like ControlNet, which over-constrain object contours and shapes, making them unsuitable for the harmonious blending that illusion pictures require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of the phase transfer module makes it immediately applicable to any off-the-shelf T2I diffusion model without fine-tuning, lowering the barrier for generating illusion pictures. The asynchronous phase transfer mechanism provides a simple, interpretable knob (async distance d) for controlling hidden-content discernibility without sacrificing text fidelity, which is a practical advantage over methods requiring careful hyperparameter tuning. However, the evaluation is limited to SD v1.5 with 80 reference images, and it remains open whether the phase-transfer mechanism generalizes to larger models (SDXL, Flux) or to other I2I tasks beyond illusion synthesis. The narrow task scope (optical illusion hidden pictures) limits immediate broad impact, though the Fourier-domain structure-semantic disentanglement idea could inspire future work in more general controllable generation.
