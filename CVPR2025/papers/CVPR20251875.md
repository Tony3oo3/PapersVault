## Problem

Existing region-level MLLMs handle images and videos with fundamentally different mechanisms—text-encoded box coordinates scale poorly with frame count, RoI features suffer temporal drift across video frames, and single-frame references lack robustness. No unified architecture provides consistent region-specific comprehension across both modalities without requiring full tracklet annotations or per-frame region prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251875/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (resized to 336×336) or a 4-frame video, along with region prompts (bounding boxes or masks) specified only on a single image or the initial video frame, and a text prompt containing <region> placeholders. It produces free-form region-level text responses including detailed captions (~60 words with temporal context), multiple-choice QA answers with rationales, and referring expression resolutions. Notably, for video inputs the region prompt is provided only on the first frame; the model must generalize the region identity to subsequent frames without additional spatial annotations.

## Method

The core mechanism is Token Mark: a fixed palette of 100 learnable vectors (dimension 256) from which N tokens are sampled without replacement to label N target regions. Each sampled token is projected into the LLM's word embedding space via a shared linear layer, then embedded spatially by computing a per-pixel weighted average over active masks, downsampling via adaptive average pooling (window 24), and adding the result as a residual to the visual tokens. The same token simultaneously replaces the <region> placeholder in the text prompt, creating a direct visual-to-text alignment that the LLM learns to exploit. For video robustness without tracklets, a Temporal Region Guide Head performs soft-label classification of each visual token's Token Mark identity across all frames using the LLM's hidden states, trained with cross-entropy at weight α=0.05 and discarded at inference. The full system builds on LLaVA (CLIP ViT-L-336 encoder, Llama-2 LLM, two-layer MLP projector) and is trained in two stages: image pre-training following RegionGPT's recipe, then joint image-video instruction fine-tuning on RegVID-300k and other region-level datasets.

## Results

On Causal-VidQA (video commonsense reasoning), Omni-RGPT 7B achieves 77.5% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous best (MotionEpic, 69.4%) by 8.1 points, with particularly large gains on Prediction (76.9% vs. 54.7% AR) and Counterfactual (64.3% vs. 58.6% AR). On VCR (image commonsense reasoning), Omni-RGPT reaches 79.9% Q→AR accuracy, outperforming ViP-LLaVA (78.9%) and GPT4RoI (78.6%). On RefCOCOg REC, Omni-RGPT achieves 95.99% (val) and 95.88% (test), substantially exceeding Groma (88.19/88.38) and GPT-4o (74.08/72.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Causal-VidQA, Omni-RGPT 7B (77.5% All) outperforms all 7 baselines, with the closest competitor MotionEpic (69.4%) trailing by 8.1 points; the gap widens dramatically on Prediction-AR (76.9 vs. 54.7) and Counterfactual-AR (64.3 vs. 58.6), indicating superior temporal reasoning. On VCR, Omni-RGPT (79.9% Q→AR) narrowly beats ViP-LLaVA (78.9%) and GPT4RoI (78.6%), while older models like ERNIE-ViL-L (65.8%) and VILBERT (54.0%) lag substantially. For region-level video captioning, Omni-RGPT leads on Vid-STG (14.2 METEOR vs. 10.5 Groma), Extended-Elysium (19.3 vs. 14.4 RegionGPT), and BenSMOT (14.6 vs. 14.0 RegionGPT), but underperforms the task-specific Elysium model (9.3 vs. 19.1) due to Elysium's extremely short noun-only captions. On RefCOCOg image captioning, Omni-RGPT (17.0 METEOR) is on par with RegionGPT (16.9) and Groma (16.8), confirming competitive image-domain performance. On RefCOCOg REC, Omni-RGPT (95.99% val) achieves a decisive 7.8-point margin over Groma (88.19%) and 9.6 points over RegionGPT (86.44%), while GPT-4o (74.08%) trails by over 21 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA architecture (vision encoder + projection + LLM) but inverts the standard region-representation paradigm: rather than extracting RoI features or encoding bounding-box coordinates as text tokens (as in RegionGPT, GPT4RoI, or Elysium), it predefines a palette of 'Token Mark' vectors and embeds them into spatial regions, creating a direct visual-to-text token link. For video, it departs from methods requiring per-frame tracklets or iterative scene-graph encoding (MotionEpic) by using a single first-frame region prompt plus an auxiliary Temporal Region Guide Head that classifies token marks across frames. The contribution is positioned as the first unified framework handling region-level understanding for both images and videos without off-the-shelf tracking, complemented by the first large-scale region-level video instruction dataset (RegVID-300k).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified image-and-video region-level MLLM addresses a genuine gap: prior methods either handle only images, rely on costly tracklets for video, or scale poorly with frame count. The Token Mark design is architecturally clean (no additional inference latency, no tracking dependency) and the Temporal Region Guide Head provides a practical solution to the temporal drift problem. However, the current 4-frame limit constrains applicability to long-form video, and the weaker Elysium captioning result (9.3 vs. 19.1 METEOR) suggests the approach trades off on very brief, noun-only captions. Open questions include whether the token-mark mechanism scales to longer sequences and whether the auxiliary head generalizes beyond the specific region-prompt format used in training.
