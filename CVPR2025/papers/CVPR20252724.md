## Problem

State-of-the-art image vectorization methods optimize toward a single target image, producing overly complex and scattered vector primitives that lack semantic alignment with underlying objects. This makes the resulting SVGs difficult to edit, recolor, or manage, as individual paths frequently cross object boundaries and do not correspond to meaningful structural units.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252724/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB raster image as input and produces a layered SVG where vector primitives are organized in back-to-front layers corresponding to semantic objects at multiple levels of detail (macro to fine). As an intermediate artifact, it generates a sequence of five progressively simplified images (at 20-step SDS intervals) that serve as stepping-stone optimization targets. The final output is a compact set of cubic Bezier curve primitives with assigned colors, structured so that each semantic region is captured by a coherent group of vectors within its own layer.

## Method

The core mechanism is an SDS-based image simplification that mutes the conditioned noise in Classifier-Free Guidance (by setting the text prompt to empty), harnessing the feature-averaging effect of SDS to progressively strip fine details while preserving macro object shapes. Guided by this simplified image sequence, vector reconstruction proceeds in two stages: (1) structural construction, where SAM segmentation of each simplified level yields masks that are layered back-to-front by overlap, and closed Bezier curves initialized from Douglas-Peucker-simplified boundaries are optimized via differential rendering under a layer-wise structure loss (MSE between mask and vector layers plus an overlap penalty); (2) visual refinement, where colors are fitted to the frozen structure vectors and additional visual-wise primitives are added to high-difference regions and optimized under an L2 RGB fidelity loss. This coarse-to-fine, sequence-guided pipeline is what enables vectors to align with implicit semantic objects (e.g., a whole robot body) that single-target segmentation fails to capture.

## Results

On a 100-image test set (realistic photos, clipart, emojis), the proposed method achieves a Vector Compactness (VeC) of 73.8% (std 11.9), substantially outperforming all four baselines: SGLIVE (65.9%), LIVE (43.4%), DiffVG (41.9%), and O&R (39.9%). The method also achieves lower pixel MSE and LPIPS (VGG-based) than all baselines across varying numbers of vector primitives, and higher CLIP similarity scores—particularly at low path counts—indicating better preservation of semantic content in the coarse vector layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. On Vector Compactness, it leads SGLIVE (the strongest baseline) by 7.9 percentage points (73.8% vs. 65.9%) and the remaining three methods (DiffVG, LIVE, O&R) by 29–34 points, while also showing the lowest standard deviation (11.9 vs. 15.1–20.4), indicating more consistent layer alignment. On visual fidelity, the method achieves lower MSE and LPIPS than all four baselines across the full range of primitive counts tested. On semantic alignment (CLIP score between target-image captions and rasterized SVGs), the method retains closer semantic match at every sampled path count, with the largest gap appearing at low path counts where only a few macro-level vectors exist. SGLIVE is the closest competitor, likely because it also uses segmentation-guided initialization, but it lacks the progressive simplification sequence that enables the proposed method to capture implicit whole-object shapes (e.g., 'entire body of the robot') that single-target methods miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Score Distillation Sampling (originally from DreamFusion for 3D generation) and the feature-averaging effect identified in Delta Denoising Score to perform 2D image simplification, a creative departure from traditional filtering or superpixel-based abstraction. For vector reconstruction, it builds on the differentiable rasterization framework of DiffVG and the progressive primitive-adding strategy of LIVE, but departs fundamentally from prior methods (LIVE, O&R, SGLIVE) that optimize toward a single target image by instead using a sequence of semantically simplified intermediate images as layered optimization targets. The two-stage structure (mask-aligned shape optimization followed by visual refinement) and the use of SAM for semantic segmentation to organize vectors into back-to-front layers represent a new pipeline architecture within the model-free, differential-rendering vectorization family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical gap in vectorization: existing methods produce visually faithful but semantically incoherent vector sets that are difficult to edit. By producing layered, semantically aligned vectors (73.8% VeC vs. ~40% for most baselines), the method enables downstream editing tasks like selective recoloring that were previously impractical. Limitations include dependence on pre-trained diffusion model quality for simplification, SAM segmentation accuracy for mask extraction, and the computational cost of SDS iterations; the 100-image evaluation set and qualitative ablations leave open questions about robustness on highly textured or adversarially complex images. Whether the progressive-simplification paradigm transfers to video vectorization or 3D surface vectorization remains an open direction.
