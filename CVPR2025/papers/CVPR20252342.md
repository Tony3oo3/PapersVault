## Problem

Automatic rigging of 3D humanoid characters is bottlenecked by the absence of a large-scale, standardized dataset: existing datasets like RigNetv1 (1,729 meshes) lack uniform skeleton topology, and SMPL is restricted to realistic hairless bodies. Additionally, GNN-based rigging methods that learn directly from mesh geometry struggle to generalize to AI-generated meshes with irregular face topologies, chaotic vertex distributions, and diverse body proportions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252342/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a T-pose 3D humanoid mesh (variable vertex count, 1K–5K+) and a front-view RGB image rendered from a fixed camera as input. It produces 22 3D skeleton joint positions (following the standard Mixamo topology) and a per-vertex skinning weight matrix of size [m, 22]. The front-view image is consumed by a fine-tuned RTMPose model to extract 2D joint priors, which are back-projected via camera intrinsics/extrinsics to initialize the 3D skeleton before refinement.

## Method

The framework operates in a coarse-to-fine pipeline: a Prior-Guided Skeleton Estimator (PGSE) back-projects 2D pose estimates onto the mesh surface to yield an initial 3D skeleton, which then augments vertex features with per-joint Euclidean distances. A U-shaped Point Transformer (rather than a GNN) encodes these skeleton-aware vertex features, deliberately ignoring edge connectivity to handle irregular AI-generated topologies, while a 3-layer MLP encodes the coarse skeleton. A Mesh-Skeleton Mutual Attention Network (MSMAN) performs bidirectional multi-head cross-attention between the two feature streams, enabling joint optimization of skeleton refinement and skinning prediction. Two MLP heads output the final joint positions (trained with MSE loss) and skinning weights (trained with KL divergence loss), and the entire system is trained in two stages—first the 2D pose estimator, then the full rigging network—on the 11,434-sample HumanRig dataset.

## Results

On the deformation error metric (lower is better), the proposed method achieves 0.00198 on RigNetv1-human and 0.00084 on HumanRig, outperforming all baselines. For skeleton construction on the HumanRig test set, the full model reaches CD-J2J of 0.0027, CD-J2B of 0.0025, and CD-B2B of 0.0012, with skinning precision of 0.9271 and L1-norm of 0.0610. The ablation confirms that removing PGSE degrades CD-J2J from 0.0027 to 0.0110, and removing MSMAN drops skinning precision from 0.9271 to 0.9214 while increasing L1 from 0.0610 to 0.0630.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on deformation quality. Against Heat Diffusion [1], it reduces deformation error by 51% on RigNetv1-human (0.00198 vs. 0.00405) and by 64% on HumanRig (0.00084 vs. 0.00236). Against GVB [5], the improvement is 35% on RigNetv1-human (0.00198 vs. 0.00306) and 63% on HumanRig (0.00084 vs. 0.00224). Against RigNet [25], the method achieves a 21% reduction on RigNetv1-human (0.00198 vs. 0.00252), while RigNet cannot be evaluated on HumanRig due to its 5K vertex limit. Against NBS [11], the comparison is qualitative (Fig. 6): NBS produces inaccurate joint positions especially in lower limbs and fails to generalize to diverse head-to-body ratios, whereas the proposed method yields plausible skeletons across all character types tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automatic rigging line initiated by RigNet [25] and NBS [11], which relied on GNN-based mesh encoders and either non-uniform skeleton topologies (RigNet) or the restrictive SMPL template (NBS). It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) it introduces a 2D-pose-to-3D-skeleton lifting strategy (PGSE via RTMPose) that provides a coarse skeleton prior, replacing direct joint regression from mesh geometry, and (2) it substitutes GNN mesh encoders with a U-shaped Point Transformer that ignores edge connectivity, motivated by the irregular topology of AI-generated meshes. The dataset itself is a novel contribution, leveraging a text-to-image (Stable Diffusion + ControlNet) and image-to-3D (InstantMesh, Unique3D) pipeline to produce 11,434 AI-generated T-pose meshes aligned to a uniform Mixamo skeleton, in contrast to the artist-created meshes in all prior rigging datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HumanRig dataset fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale, uniformly-rigged collection of AI-generated humanoid meshes, enabling data-driven rigging methods to generalize to the increasingly common AI-generated character models that differ substantially in topology from hand-crafted meshes. The practical impact is significant for animation pipelines that need to rig AI-generated characters at scale. However, the method is limited to a 22-joint Mixamo skeleton without fingers, and the authors note that extending to quadrupeds or arbitrary objects remains open. A key open question is whether the 2D-pose prior (PGSE) remains effective for non-frontal or occluded views, and whether the Point Transformer advantage over GNNs persists as AI-generated mesh quality and topology regularity improve over time.
