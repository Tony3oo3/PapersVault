## Problem

Standard full-reference image quality assessment (FR-IQA) models assume the reference image is of perfect quality, but this assumption is violated by sensor/optical limitations in real imaging systems and by modern generative enhancers that can produce images of higher quality than their originals. When the reference is imperfect or the test image surpasses it, existing FR-IQA models become biased and unreliable, yet no large-scale dataset or model has been designed to handle this generalized setting.

## Input / Output

A-FINE takes a pair of RGB images — a reference x and a test y (512×512 in the DiffIQA and SRIQA-Bench datasets) — and outputs a scalar quality score D(x,y) that is unbounded in sign: negative values indicate the test image is perceived as better than the reference, while positive values indicate worse. Both images are processed through a shared CLIP ViT-B/32@224 backbone with interpolated position embeddings to support arbitrary resolutions, and the model gracefully reduces to a standard FR-IQA score when the reference is substantially more natural than the test image.

## Method

A-FINE computes an adaptive linear combination D(x,y) = F(x,y) + λ(x,y)·N(y), where F is a DISTS-like multi-stage fidelity term (global texture and structure similarity over CLIP ViT feature maps with learnable per-channel weights) and N is a naturalness score derived from the same backbone via global pooling, linear projection, and a small MLP head. The adaptive weight λ(x,y) = exp(k(N(x)−N(y))) is a learnable function that shifts emphasis from fidelity to naturalness as the test image becomes more natural than the reference. Training uses a pairwise learning-to-rank objective under Thurstone's Case V model with a fidelity loss, optimized in three phases: (1) warm-up of the naturalness branch, (2) optimization of fidelity weights with the backbone frozen, and (3) joint refinement of the adaptive scale k and monotonic logistic mappings. The model is trained on a combined set of DiffIQA (177k diffusion-enhanced images with worse/similar/better quality labels) plus TID2013, KADID-10K, and PIPAL, using AdamW with cosine annealing on four V100 GPUs.

## Results

On the combined standard IQA test sets (TID2013, KADID-10K, PIPAL), A-FINE achieves 83.6% average pairwise accuracy, outperforming the best standard model TOPIQ-FT (80.6%) and the best generalized model CKDN-FT (72.0%). On the proposed DiffIQA dataset, A-FINE reaches 79.9% average accuracy (78.5% when the reference is worse than the test, 82.3% when the reference is better), surpassing TOPIQ-FT (77.0%) and CKDN-FT (76.4%). On SRIQA-Bench, A-FINE achieves 82.4% overall accuracy (83.3% regression-based, 78.9% generation-based), exceeding the next-best TOPIQ-FT (77.7%) by 4.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A-FINE outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset. Among standard FR-IQA models, the closest competitor is TOPIQ-FT (78.8% All Average), which A-FINE surpasses by 3.0 points; TOPIQ without fine-tuning (67.1%) drops sharply on DiffIQA (49.1%), revealing its inability to handle imperfect references. Among generalized models, CKDN-FT (74.2%) is the strongest prior method but still trails A-FINE by 7.6 points, while VIF (60.3%), PCQI (52.1%), and SFSN (58.0%) all fall well below 65%. On SRIQA-Bench, A-FINE's 82.4% overall accuracy exceeds TOPIQ-FT (77.7%) by 4.7 points and CKDN-FT (59.1%) by 23.3 points, with the largest gap appearing on generation-based SR images (78.9% vs. 73.0% for TOPIQ-FT). Notably, fine-tuned standard models (AHIQ-FT, TOPIQ-FT) show substantial gains on DiffIQA but suffer performance drops on standard datasets, whereas A-FINE maintains strong performance on both without such trade-offs.

## Relation to Prior Work

A-FINE directly extends the DISTS fidelity formulation (Ding et al., 2020) by replacing its fixed VGG backbone with a fine-tuned CLIP ViT-B/32 and adding an adaptive naturalness term that is weighted by the relative naturalness of reference and test images. For dataset construction, the paper adapts the PASD pixel-aware stable diffusion framework (Wu et al., 2024) into a generative enhancer to produce images of varying quality relative to their references. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior generalized FR-IQA efforts (VIF, PCQI, CKDN, SFSN), which handled only narrow scenarios like interpolation or denoising, by formulating a unified model that gracefully reverts to standard FR-IQA when the reference is clearly superior and shifts toward no-reference naturalness assessment when the test image outperforms the reference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in FR-IQA—that reference images are of perfect quality—which is increasingly untenable in the era of generative enhancement where outputs routinely surpass their inputs. The 180K-image DiffIQA dataset and the SRIQA-Bench benchmark provide much-needed resources for training and evaluating models under imperfect reference conditions, and the observed performance trade-off between standard and generalized scenarios (fine-tuned models drop significantly on conventional datasets) highlights an open tension the field must resolve. Key limitations include residual bias when reference quality is very poor, the empirically chosen exponential weighting function lacking theoretical justification, and the unresolved question of what the optimal asymmetric distance measure should be for generalized FR-IQA.
