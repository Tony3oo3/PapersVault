## Problem

Training-free diffusion model alignment methods suffer from inaccurate guidance, particularly in early denoising steps where one-step clean image approximations are too blurred for preference models to provide meaningful feedback. While semantic content (entities, layout) is shaped in these early noisy stages, existing training-free approaches lack a mechanism to effectively guide content formation, leading to poor text-image alignment and suboptimal layout composition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250872/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (e.g., SD V1.5, SDXL) as input, requiring no additional training or fine-tuning. An LLM (GPT-4 or Llama-3.3-70B) pre-processes the prompt to extract entities and attributes for semantic graph construction. The output is a single generated image produced over 50 denoising steps, with the method operating entirely at inference time as a plug-and-play module compatible with any latent diffusion backbone.

## Method

DyMO introduces a dual-objective training-free guidance framework that dynamically schedules a semantic alignment loss and a preference alignment loss across denoising steps. The semantic alignment loss minimizes discrepancies between cross-attention maps (reflecting image content) and a text semantic graph built from LLM-extracted entities and attributes, using cosine similarity with positive (entity-attribute) and negative (entity-entity) edge constraints to enforce correct binding and spatial separation. The preference alignment loss uses a pre-trained step-aware reward model (from SPO) to optimize human preference scores on one-step decoded images. An adaptive weight w = 1 − exp(−k·‖z′_{0|t} − z′_{0|t+1}‖/‖z′_{0|t+1}‖) transitions from semantic-dominant (early steps, t≥800) to preference-dominant (late steps, t<500) guidance based on latent stability. Additionally, a dynamic time-travel strategy sets the number of recurrent refinement iterations per step proportional to gradient magnitude (r_t = h_t·‖g_t‖), and a Polyak step size normalizes updates for stable convergence.

## Results

On the SDXL backbone (Table 2), DyMO achieves PickScore 24.90, HPSv2 0.2839, ImageReward 1.074, and Aesthetics 6.138, surpassing even the latest FLUX (22.04 / 0.2760 / 1.011 / 6.077) and SD V3.5 (21.93 / 0.2726 / 0.9697 / 5.775) models. On the SD V1.5 backbone (Table 1), DyMO reaches PickScore 23.07, HPSv2 0.2755, ImageReward 0.7170, and Aesthetics 5.831, outperforming all training-based and training-free baselines on every metric. The method is plug-and-play and requires no training, with a dynamic time-travel strategy achieving the best quality at ~190s average generation time (vs. 295s for 10 fixed iterations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyMO outperforms all training-free baselines (DNO, PromptOpt, FreeDom) by a wide margin on every metric; for example, on SDXL it gains +2.76 PickScore over DNO and +2.77 over FreeDom. Against training-based methods, DyMO beats AlignProp (+2.51 PickScore on SD V1.5), Diffusion-DPO (+2.60 PickScore on SDXL), and Diffusion-KTO (+1.92 PickScore on SD V1.5) on all four metrics. The closest competitor is SPO: on SDXL, SPO edges out DyMO on ImageReward (1.082 vs. 1.074) and Aesthetics (6.319 vs. 6.138), but DyMO leads on PickScore (+2.09) and HPSv2 (+0.006); on SD V1.5, DyMO wins all four metrics over SPO. Notably, DyMO applied on top of SPO (SPO+DyMO) further improves ImageReward to 1.166, confirming the method's plug-and-play compatibility. DyMO also surpasses the latest proprietary models FLUX and SD V3.5 on all metrics, with a +2.86 PickScore advantage over FLUX.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyMO extends the training-free diffusion guidance line (FreeDom, DNO, UGD, TFG) by addressing a specific weakness: one-step clean-image approximation yields underestimated and semantically uninformative guidance in early denoising steps. It departs from prior training-free methods that rely solely on a single preference or classifier loss by introducing a complementary semantic alignment objective built on cross-attention maps (inspired by Prompt-to-Prompt and PnP-Diffusion) and an LLM-constructed entity-attribute graph. The dynamic scheduling of these two objectives and the gradient-magnitude-driven recurrent iteration count represent a departure from the fixed-weight or fixed-iteration schemes used in FreeDom and related work, while the Polyak step size is adopted from Shen et al. for stable convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of DyMO makes it immediately applicable to any pre-trained T2I diffusion model without retraining or preference datasets, lowering the barrier to preference-aligned generation. A key open question is the dependency on an external LLM (GPT-4 or Llama) for semantic graph construction, which adds latency and cost and may limit deployment in latency-sensitive or offline settings. The method is validated on SD V1.5 and SDXL with 50-step sampling; whether the dynamic scheduling generalizes to fewer-step distilled models, video diffusion, or very long/complex multi-entity prompts remains unexplored. The gap to newer architectures (FLUX, SD V3.5) narrows but does not fully close on some metrics, suggesting that architectural improvements and alignment guidance are complementary rather than substitutable.
