## Problem

Existing robotic manipulation simulation platforms lack the scenario diversity, controllable layouts, and multi-dimensional evaluation needed to systematically compare how modular (foundation-model-based) and end-to-end (imitation-learning) policies generalize across varied instructions and everyday settings. While LLM-driven task generation has emerged, no standardized benchmark exists that tests instruction-following generalization across object appearances, spatial relations, commonsense reasoning, and long-horizon execution in realistic tabletop environments.

## Input / Output

The modular system consumes RGB-D observations with Set-of-Mark overlays (numbered SAM2 masks) and text instructions, producing 3D trajectory points (reprojected from VLM-selected grid centers with predicted heights) that are converted to robot actions via MPlib. The end-to-end policies (GR-1, ACT) take RGB images, language instructions, and proprioceptive robot state, outputting low-level action sequences. Evaluation transforms the final scene layout back into ToSG format and checks goal conditions (object-state pairs and spatial-relation triplets), yielding SR (fraction of goal conditions met) and SPL (path-length-weighted success).

## Method

The core contribution is the Task-Oriented Scene Graph (ToSG), a structured yet LLM-compatible representation where nodes encode intra-object states (e.g., open/closed) and edges encode inter-object spatial relations (left, right, near, on, in) relative to a camera viewpoint. GPT-4 synthesizes ToSGs from randomly sampled objects among 10K VL-annotated 3D assets (sourced from Objaverse, filtered and annotated via GPT-4V), enabling diverse task generation across four reasoning categories. Layout construction proceeds via topological sorting of on/in relations followed by sequential placement with collision resolution, ensuring relational consistency. For evaluation, the platform unifies modular methods (SAM2 + Coarse-to-Fine SoM + AnyGrasp + grid-based VLM motion planning + long-horizon replanning loop) and end-to-end policies (GR-1, ACT) trained via automated behavior-cloning data collection using MimicGen-style privileged scene information, all within Isaac Sim with photorealistic rendering.

## Results

On GENMANIP-BENCH (200 scenarios), the best modular system—CoPA with GPT-4.5—achieves an overall success rate (SR) of 23.0% and SPL of 5.26%, outperforming all other MLLM-backed configurations. Long-horizon tasks are the most challenging category, with all models averaging only ~9.07% SR, while appearance-based reasoning is the easiest (up to 38.24% SR for CoPA+Gemini-2.0-Flash). For end-to-end policies, GR-1 reaches 95.0% SR on a single task with 1K behavior-cloning episodes but drops to 45.5% under full-table randomization and 0.0% on unseen instructions or objects, revealing severe generalization limits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates two modular prompt-based systems (CoPA and MOKA) and two end-to-end policies (GR-1 and ACT) on GENMANIP-BENCH. CoPA consistently outperforms MOKA across all MLLM backbones: with GPT-4.5, CoPA achieves 23.0% overall SR versus MOKA's 14.0%, and with GPT-4o, 20.33% versus 10.0%. The gap is most pronounced on long-horizon tasks, where CoPA+GPT-4o reaches 17.59% SR while MOKA+GPT-4o manages only 5.56%. For end-to-end methods, GR-1 scales well on a single task (33.0%→95.0% SR from 100→1K episodes) but generalizes poorly: it drops to 45.5% under full-table randomization and fails entirely (0.0% SR) on unseen instructions and unseen objects. ACT shows similar but weaker scaling (40.0%→72.5% SR). Overall, modular systems with strong MLLMs generalize better across diverse scenarios, while end-to-end policies remain brittle to semantic and compositional changes despite benefiting from data scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the Task-oriented Scene Graph (ToSG) as a natural-language-compatible structured representation that unifies LLM-driven task generation with robotic evaluation, departing from both rigid PDDL-style planning languages and purely randomized layout approaches used in prior benchmarks (RoboCasa, CALVIN, LIBERO). It repurposes existing modular manipulation strategies (CoPA's constraint generation, MOKA's grid prompting, SAM2/AnyGrasp for perception) into a single unified pipeline for fair cross-method comparison, and leverages MimicGen-style privileged-information data generation for end-to-end policy training. Conceptually, it bridges the scene-graph-based scenario generation literature from autonomous driving and embodied AI with the robotic manipulation benchmarking tradition, adding the critical dimension of instruction-following generalization that prior tabletop benchmarks largely neglected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GENMANIP-BENCH provides the first standardized, human-curated 200-scenario benchmark that systematically stratifies manipulation generalization across spatial reasoning, appearance understanding, common-sense knowledge, and long-horizon execution—dimensions that prior platforms evaluated in isolation or not at all. The finding that end-to-end policies (GR-1, ACT) achieve 0% SR on unseen instructions and unseen objects, despite strong performance under spatial randomization, sharply delineates the current boundary of imitation learning generalization and motivates the dual-system (modular + learned) architecture. Key open questions include whether full-scale evaluation of end-to-end methods across all 200 scenarios would change the picture, whether the ToSG framework scales to non-tabletop or multi-robot settings, and how simulation findings transfer to real-world deployment.
