## Problem

Traditional State Space Models process data along fixed, one-dimensional scanning paths (e.g., raster order or a few predefined directions), which prevents them from capturing non-local dependencies that do not align with those trajectories. While Mamba and VMamba introduce selective or multi-directional scanning, they still rely on handcrafted, data-agnostic paths that cannot adapt to the diverse and irregular structures present in images, event streams, or multivariate time series.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251988/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of L feature embeddings (pixel patches for 224x224 RGB images, 80x60 event-based frames, image pairs for optical flow, or variate tokens for multivariate time series with lookback 96) and produces task-specific outputs: class labels, pupil center coordinates, flow fields, or forecast sequences of length 96-720. Notably, the same GG-SSM block is applied in both the spatial domain (over pixel/token embeddings within a frame) and the temporal domain (over a sequence of frame-level features), so the graph topology is re-derived from feature relationships at each application site rather than being fixed to a grid or sequence order.

## Method

GG-SSM replaces fixed scanning paths with a dynamically constructed graph: given input features {x_i}, pairwise cosine dissimilarities define edge weights on a fully connected graph, and Chazelle's near-linear-time MST algorithm selects the L-1 edges that best preserve the data's intrinsic structure. State propagation then follows the unique path between every pair of nodes in the MST, where the path weight S_ji is the ordered product of input-dependent state-transition matrices along that path, and each node's hidden state is the sum of all other nodes' contributions modulated by their respective path weights. Because the MST is a tree, every pair of nodes has exactly one path, guaranteeing O(L) forward and backward passes via a single leaf-to-root (and root-to-leaf) traversal with dynamic-programming storage of intermediate states. The MST construction is implemented in CUDA and runs in approximately the same wall-clock time as a Mamba forward pass, keeping overall complexity linear in the number of features.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, GG-SSM-B achieves 84.9% top-1 accuracy, surpassing VMamba-B (83.9%) and Swin-B (83.5%) while using fewer FLOPs (14.1G vs 15.4G). On KITTI-15 optical flow, GG-SSM sets a new state-of-the-art Fl-all of 2.77, outperforming the previous best VideoFlow-MOF (3.65) by 0.88. On the LPW event-based eye-tracking dataset, GG-SSM achieves 89.33%/98.89%/99.50% detection rates at p3/p5/p10 with only 0.22M parameters and 8.01 GFLOPs, beating VMamba+Mamba (89.00%/98.00%/99.30%) with fewer parameters. Across six time-series forecasting benchmarks, GG-SSM achieves the best or near-best MSE and MAE at all forecast horizons (96–720), with average MSE improvements of 0.003–0.02 over the strongest baselines (S-Mamba, iTransformer, RLinear).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GG-SSM consistently outperforms all SSM-based baselines: on ImageNet it beats VMamba by 1.0% (Base) and 1.4% (Tiny), S4ND-Conv-T by 1.4%, and Vim-T by 3.1%; on eye tracking it surpasses VMamba+Mamba by 0.33% at p3 with 37% fewer parameters; on time series it edges out S-Mamba by 0.003–0.02 MSE across all six datasets. Against transformer and CNN baselines, GG-SSM-B (84.9%) exceeds Swin-B (83.5%), ConvNeXt-B (83.8%), and DeiT-B (81.8%) on ImageNet. In optical flow, GG-SSM achieves the best Fl-all on KITTI-15 (2.77 vs. VideoFlow-MOF's 3.65, MemFlow's 4.10, and RAFT's 5.10) and the best Sintel EPE (0.97 clean / 1.58 final vs. VideoFlow-MOF's 0.99 / 1.65). On the INI-30 eye-tracking benchmark, GG-SSM achieves the lowest centroid error (3.11 px) versus Retina (3.24 px) and VMamba+Mamba (3.42 px), while matching Retina's parameter count (62k vs. 63k) and MACs (3.01M vs. 3.03M). The only near-ties are on the Exchange time-series dataset where S-Mamba and iTransformer match GG-SSM within 0.001–0.003 MSE at shorter horizons, but GG-SSM pulls ahead at the 720-step horizon.

## Relation to Prior Work

GG-SSM extends the selective state space model lineage initiated by Mamba and visualized for images by VMamba, but departs fundamentally from their fixed, handcrafted scanning trajectories (1D, bidirectional, four-way, zigzag) by replacing them with a dynamically constructed minimum spanning tree over feature embeddings. The graph construction leverages Chazelle's near-linear-time MST algorithm, which is borrowed from computational geometry rather than deep learning, and the edge weights are defined by cosine dissimilarity between feature vectors. This positions GG-SSM as a bridge between graph-based representation learning and SSMs, where the topology is data-driven rather than architecturally prescribed, in contrast to S4ND's stacked 1D modules or topology-based SSMs that still rely on predefined structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is a drop-in replacement for scanning strategies in visual SSMs that claims linear complexity and data-adaptive connectivity, which could simplify architecture design for multi-dimensional data. However, several open questions remain: the paper's claim of 'consistent best accuracy' on time series is not fully supported by Table 4 (e.g., S-Mamba and iTransformer outperform GG-SSM on Exchange at multiple horizons), and the ablation showing Kruskal's, Prim's, and Chazelle's MSTs yield nearly identical accuracy (±0.1%) raises the question of whether the specific graph structure matters or whether any sparse tree suffices. It is also unclear how the method scales when L (number of nodes) grows to millions, since the fully connected graph construction step before MST extraction still requires O(L²) pairwise dissimilarity computations.
