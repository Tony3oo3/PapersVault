## Problem

Existing image editing models are trained on synthetic or artificially constructed datasets that do not reflect the distribution of real-world user requests, leading to poor performance on practical tasks like removing photobombers, restoring damaged photos, or fixing lighting. No large-scale dataset existed that combined authentic user-submitted editing requests with human-completed edits, leaving a critical gap between academic benchmarks and actual user needs.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB input image paired with a refined text editing instruction (e.g., 'Remove the leash from my dog') and produces a single edited RGB image. Each dataset example contains one input image, one instruction, and 1–5 human-edited output images, totaling 151K images across 57K examples. Instructions are distilled from noisy Reddit posts using GPT-4o to isolate the core editing task, and the test set additionally includes VLM-generated captions for both input and output images to support caption-based evaluation metrics.

## Method

The paper introduces REALEDIT, a 57K-example dataset (48K train, 9.3K test) sourced from r/PhotoshopRequest and r/estoration, where real users both submit requests and complete edits. For the editing model, the authors finetune InstructPix2Pix on 39K examples after aligning the training data with InstructPix2Pix's pretraining distribution via CLIP-based filtering and SSIM-based structural similarity selection, excluding tasks incompatible with the architecture (e.g., resizing, file-type changes). A key architectural modification replaces the Stable Diffusion decoder with OpenAI's Consistency Decoder at inference time, improving face and fine-detail reconstruction without altering the diffusion process. Training follows MagicBrush's configuration with 51 epochs, cosine learning rate decay, and a warm-up phase. The dataset curation pipeline involves three stages: raw post/comment collection, GPT-4o instruction refinement, and multi-stage verification including NSFW filtering and dual-annotator manual review (Cohen's Kappa = 0.61).

## Results

On the REALEDIT test set, the REALEDIT model achieves a VIEScore (VIE_SC) of 4.61, a 92% relative improvement over the next-best model (Null-text Inversion at 2.14), and an overall VIEScore (VIE_O) of 3.68 versus 2.20 for AURORA. In human preference evaluation (N=4,196 pairwise comparisons), the model earns an Elo score of 1184, beating the next-best model (AURORA at 1019) by 165 points. On the GenAI Arena synthetic test set, the model ranks second (Elo 1043) behind MagicBrush (1107), confirming competitive performance on existing benchmarks while dominating on real-world requests.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The REALEDIT model outperforms all six baselines on the REALEDIT test set across nearly every metric. Against MagicBrush (Zhang et al., 2024), the strongest competitor on standard metrics (CLIP-I 0.830, DINO-I 0.782), RealEdit wins on all VQA-based and VIEScore metrics (VIE_SC 4.61 vs 1.89, TIFA 0.751 vs 0.741) while MagicBrush retains a marginal edge on pixel-level L1/L2 distance. Against InstructPix2Pix (Brooks et al., 2023), the backbone model, RealEdit improves VIE_SC by 2.6× (4.61 vs 1.76) and VQA-FLAN-T5 by 22% (0.795 vs 0.650). Null-text Inversion (Mokady et al., 2023) is the closest competitor on VIE_SC (2.14) but still trails by more than 2×. AURORA (Krojer et al., 2024) and HIVE (Zhang et al., 2024) are consistently outperformed across all semantic and perceptual metrics. SDEdit (Meng et al., 2021) performs worst overall (VIE_SC 0.75), indicating that stochastic differential equation-based editing is poorly suited to the diverse real-world tasks in REALEDIT. On the GenAI Arena synthetic benchmark, MagicBrush leads (Elo 1107 vs 1043), suggesting the advantage of training on synthetic data for synthetic evaluation, while RealEdit's dominance on REALEDIT (1184 vs 1019) confirms the value of ecologically valid training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on InstructPix2Pix as its model backbone, replacing the Stable Diffusion decoder with OpenAI's Consistency Decoder to better preserve human faces and fine details. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of synthetic or programmatically generated editing datasets (MagicBrush, Emu Edit, HIVE, UltraEdit) by sourcing all training and test examples from real user requests and human-completed edits on Reddit. The paper positions REALEDIT as the first large-scale dataset where both the request and the edit are human-authored, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where no prior dataset simultaneously offers scale, human edits, and real-world provenance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that existing SOTA models lose by 165 Elo points on real user requests while performing competitively on synthetic benchmarks—provides strong evidence that the field's evaluation and training distributions are misaligned with actual user needs, a gap that has persisted despite years of diffusion-model progress. The dataset's demonstrated cross-domain utility (14-point F1 gain in edited-image detection for TrueMedia.org) suggests broader value for content-authentication research. Limitations include reliance on a single backbone (InstructPix2Pix), exclusion of structurally divergent edits via SSIM filtering, and the fact that the Consistency Decoder swap is an inference-time change rather than an architectural innovation, leaving open whether other base models would benefit comparably from REALEDIT training.
