## Problem

Existing Personalized Face Diffusion (PFD) models, trained on face-centric datasets, overemphasize facial features at the expense of full-body coherence, producing portrait-focused outputs with missing objects or incoherent human-object interactions. The paper identifies that the core limitation is not identity fidelity but the diminished ability of PFD models to follow complex HOI text prompts after face-centric fine-tuning, despite the underlying pre-trained diffusion models (e.g., StableDiffusion) being capable of generating satisfactory HOI images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250601/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference face image and a free-form text prompt describing a human-object interaction (e.g., 'a person kicking football') and produces an identity-consistent HOI image at 512×512 (SD1.5-based) or 1024×1024 (SDXL-based) resolution. No additional training, fine-tuning, or test-time optimization is required; the framework is plug-and-play across existing PFD backbones. A head segmentation mask extracted via DensePose from the SD-generated image guides all spatial merging operations throughout the denoising process.

## Method

PersonaHOI introduces a dual-path architecture where a PFD model and a general StableDiffusion branch run in parallel from the same noisy latent, with three training-free merging strategies. Cross-Attention Constraint (CAC) uses the SD-derived head mask to zero out identity-token attention weights outside the facial region in the PFD cross-attention layers, preventing identity features from bleeding into body and object areas. Latent Merge (LM) performs element-wise masked blending of the two branches' latent representations at every denoising timestep, retaining PFD latents in the head region and SD latents elsewhere. Residual Merge (RM) operates within U-Net skip connections, applying a high-pass filter to PFD residuals (preserving fine-grained identity details) and a low-pass filter to SD residuals (retaining global scene layout), then fusing them via the head mask before concatenation with bottleneck features. This multi-level integration ensures facial identity fidelity from PFD coexists with coherent HOI context from SD without mutual interference.

## Results

On HOI-specific personalized face generation (V-COCO prompts), PersonaHOI improves HOI Alignment by +20.69% over FastComposer (35.96% → 56.65%), +19.24% over PhotoMaker (56.77% → 76.01%), and +18.47% over IP-Adapter (49.83% → 68.30%), while maintaining or slightly improving Identity Preservation and Prompt Consistency. The PhotoMaker + PersonaHOI combination achieves the highest HOI Alignment at 76.01%, surpassing even the text-only SDXL baseline (70.78%) while adding identity preservation. On general personalized face generation (Table 2), PersonaHOI-enhanced FastComposer matches or slightly exceeds the baseline across all four prompt categories (Mean Identity 52.84% vs. 50.95%, Mean Prompt 23.26% vs. 22.96%), confirming no degradation on non-HOI tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FastComposer (SD1.5 backbone), PersonaHOI wins on all three metrics: Identity +1.71%, Prompt +1.86%, and HOI Alignment +20.69%, with the HOI gain being the most dramatic (35.96% → 56.65%). Against PhotoMaker (SDXL backbone), PersonaHOI again wins across the board with a particularly large HOI improvement of +19.24% (56.77% → 76.01%), making it the best-performing configuration overall. Against IP-Adapter (SDXL backbone), PersonaHOI trades a 7.11% drop in Identity Preservation for a substantial +18.47% gain in HOI Alignment and +1.47% in Prompt Consistency; the authors attribute the identity drop to IP-Adapter's inherent face-centric bias inflating identity scores at the cost of interaction fidelity. On additional baselines (Table 3), PersonaHOI improves HOI Alignment by +16.09% over InstantID (45.73% → 61.82%) at a cost of −9.28% identity, and by a marginal +0.08% over PuLID (72.10% → 72.18%) while gaining +6.63% in identity. Overall, the consistent ~18–21% HOI Alignment improvement across all PFD backbones is the paper's central empirical claim, with the trade-off being a modest identity reduction for face-centric models (IP-Adapter, InstantID) but a small identity gain for body-aware models (FastComposer, PhotoMaker, PuLID).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of personalized face diffusion (PFD) models (FastComposer, IP-Adapter, PhotoMaker) and general text-to-image diffusion (StableDiffusion), identifying that face-centric fine-tuning in PFD models degrades their ability to follow complex HOI text prompts. Rather than training a new PFD model, PersonaHOI runs a frozen SD branch in parallel with an existing PFD model and introduces three training-free merging mechanisms (Cross-Attention Constraint, Latent Merge, Residual Merge) guided by a head segmentation mask from the SD output. This positions the work as a plug-and-play augmentation layer over any PFD architecture, distinct from prior HOI generation methods that rely on pose templates, bounding-box layouts, or in-context examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, backbone-agnostic design makes PersonaHOI immediately deployable across the growing ecosystem of PFD models without retraining, which is practically valuable for real-time content creation. The proposed HOI alignment metric (using UPT detector confidence) offers a more targeted evaluation than generic CLIP scores for interaction fidelity. However, the identity preservation drop observed with IP-Adapter (−7.11%) and InstantID (−9.28%) reveals that these models' high identity scores partly stem from face-centric output bias rather than true identity fidelity, raising questions about how to balance identity and interaction without sacrificing either. Open questions include scalability to video HOI, more than two subjects, and whether the head-mask-based spatial partitioning generalizes to occluded or heavily stylized faces.
