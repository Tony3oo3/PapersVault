## Problem

State-of-the-art multimodal language models perform only marginally better than text-only baselines on 3D spatial and long-video understanding benchmarks, indicating that spatial-temporal reasoning is a major bottleneck. Prior solutions require specialized architectures, 3D point-cloud inputs, or task-specific fine-tuning, making them costly and hard to generalize. This paper asks whether a simple, training-free visual prompting strategy can jointly boost both spatial and temporal reasoning in general-purpose MLLMs using only 2D image inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250792/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of 2D RGB images (video frames or multi-viewpoint scene captures) plus a free-form text question. Before feeding images to the MLLM, the pipeline overlays small colored circle markers (with instance-ID labels) at the centroid of selected tracked objects, producing modified images that the model interprets alongside the question. For QA tasks the output is a free-form text answer; for navigation (R2R/VLN-CE) the output is one of four discrete actions per step (FORWARD, TURN-LEFT, TURN-RIGHT, STOP) with associated distance or angle. A notable efficiency property is that the method uses far fewer input frames than competing approaches (e.g., 4–8 frames vs. 128–256) while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Method

COARSE CORRESPONDENCES is a four-step, training-free visual prompting pipeline: (1) an off-the-shelf video object tracker (e.g., Tracking Anything) extracts class-agnostic instance segmentation masks across all n input frames; (2) frames are temporally downsampled to m ≪ n uniformly sampled images to reduce MLLM token cost; (3) the top-K most prominent instances are selected by sorting on co-occurrence frequency and total pixel area; (4) fixed-size colored circles labeled with the instance ID are overlaid at each instance's centroid in every retained frame where it appears. The key insight is that even coarse, imprecise instance-level correspondences (rather than dense point-level matches) provide sufficient cross-view spatial structure for an MLLM to construct a 3D mental model of the scene. For proprietary models (GPT-4V/O) the method is applied purely at inference in a zero-shot manner; for open-source models (LLaVA) it can additionally be incorporated during instruction tuning as a data augmentation signal, yielding gains that generalize to unseen datasets.

## Results

On ScanQA, GPT-4O+CC achieves 35.4 BLEU-1, 25.5 BLEU-2, 18.0 METEOR, 42.6 ROUGE-L, and 87.0 CIDEr, surpassing all 3D-specific models (best: 3D-LLM at 39.3 BLEU-1, 69.4 CIDEr) while using only 4 input images. On OpenEQA's episodic memory split, GPT-4O+CC reaches 59.1% accuracy (vs. 49.4% for GPT-4O alone, +9.7%), and on EgoSchema, GPT-4O+CC achieves 73.2% accuracy with just 8 frames, outperforming fine-tuned architectures like MC-ViT-L (62.6%, 128+ frames) and LangRepo (66.2%). On R2R navigation, GPT-4O+CC doubles the success rate from 12.0% to 23.0% (+11%), and on out-of-domain SQA3D, LLaVA+CC gains +3.1% over the baseline fine-tuned without correspondences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanQA, GPT-4V+CC (39.7 BLEU-1, 79.2 CIDEr) and GPT-4O+CC (35.4 BLEU-1, 87.0 CIDEr) both surpass the strongest 3D-specific baseline 3D-LLM [13] (39.3 BLEU-1, 69.4 CIDEr) despite using only 4–8 2D images versus 3D point cloud inputs, and substantially outperform ScanQA [2] (26.9 BLEU-1) and ScanRefer+MCAN [49] (30.2 BLEU-1). LLaVA+CC (38.6 BLEU-1) beats the fine-tuned LLaVA baseline [22] (34.7 BLEU-1) by 3.9 BLEU-1 points. On OpenEQA, GPT-4O+CC (59.1%) and GPT-4V+CC (58.5%) exceed Gemini-Pro [38] (44.9%), Claude3 [1] (36.3%), GPT-4 [29] (33.5%), and LLaMA2 [40] (28.3%) by 13–31 points. On EgoSchema, GPT-4O+CC (73.2%, 8 frames) outperforms all fine-tuned video architectures—MC-ViT-L [4] (62.6%, 128+ frames), LongViviT [30] (56.8%, 256 frames), MVU [33] (60.3%, 16 frames), LLoVi [50] (58.3%, 180 frames), and both VideoAgent variants [10, 42] (62.8%, 60.2%)—as well as the Socratic-based LangRepo [15] (66.2%), all in a zero-shot setting. On R2R navigation, GPT-4O+CC doubles GPT-4O's success rate (23.0% vs. 12.0%) and improves SPL from 10.37 to 21.03, demonstrating gains in embodied spatial-temporal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of enhancing MLLM spatial-temporal reasoning through specialized architectures (3D-LLM, LongViviT), 3D data inputs (PointLLM), or task-specific fine-tuning (SpatialVLM), instead repurposing the long-established concept of visual correspondence from 3D reconstruction/structure-from-motion as a training-free visual prompting signal. It also distinguishes itself from prior visual prompting methods (Set-of-Mark, 3DAxiesPrompts) by encoding cross-image object identity rather than single-image grounding or axis annotations, and from Socratic-based video understanding (LangRepo, LLoVi) by preserving visual information rather than reducing frames to text captions. The key conceptual move is jointly addressing spatial and temporal reasoning through a single mechanism—instance-level tracking correspondences—whereas prior work typically tackles these dimensions separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a lightweight, architecture-agnostic prompting strategy that requires no retraining of the MLLM and uses far fewer input frames than competing methods (e.g., 8 frames vs. 128+ on EgoSchema) while matching or exceeding their accuracy, making it attractive for cost-sensitive deployments. The camera-motion invariance finding—that correspondences eliminate the left-to-right vs. right-to-left input-order sensitivity—suggests a previously unaddressed failure mode in MLLM spatial reasoning that this method resolves. Open questions include how the approach scales to highly dynamic scenes with many occluded or novel objects, whether the reliance on off-the-shelf tracker quality (Tracking Anything, SAMv2) becomes a bottleneck in cluttered environments, and whether the open-source model results (which require fine-tuning to interpret marks) can be achieved purely zero-shot with better-pretrained models.
