## Problem

Reconstructing accurate camera poses and floor plan layouts from sparsely captured wide-baseline 360° panoramas remains unsolved: limited co-visibility and featureless indoor regions break traditional SfM, while existing learning-based methods either lack view-consistency across views or require dense captures and RGB-D sensors. No prior approach jointly enforces both cross-view reprojection consistency and learned structural layout constraints (wall adjacency, collinearity, room topology) during a single refinement step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250088/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BADGR consumes a coarse scene state rather than raw images: per-panorama floor boundary predictions (1D pixel values per column), a pre-assigned one-hot column-to-wall mapping (N_p × w × N_l), fixed wall angles and connectivity, and initial 3-DoF SE(2) poses from CovisPose+. The model outputs refined 2-DoF camera xy positions and 1-DoF wall translations (along fixed normals) as 2D vertex coordinates normalized to [-1,1], representing closed-loop polygons for rooms and doors in a shared global frame. Rotations are corrected separately via vanishing-point snapping and are not optimized by BADGR.

## Method

BADGR is a conditional denoising diffusion model (non-Markovian DDPM) trained to simultaneously perform planar bundle adjustment and floor plan generation. At each denoising step, a differentiable single-step Levenberg-Marquardt layer computes dense per-column reprojection errors and adjustments (Δb, ΔT) for every image column against its assigned wall; these are compressed by a Column Geometry Encoder (1D convolutions over Fourier-feature-encoded mean/std statistics) into per-wall and per-camera embeddings that condition a 4-layer self-attention Transformer denoiser with component-wise, global, and relational cross-attention masks. The Transformer predicts 2D xy coordinates for cameras and room vertices, with an Angle-Constrained Layout representation (Hesse normal form walls with fixed directions) and a Layout Directional Regularizer enforcing that only the valid half of each vertex coordinate is free. Training uses a masked L2 reconstruction loss plus a reprojection loss (weight 100) computed at t=0, and the model is trained exclusively on 2D schematic floor plans (FloorPlan-60K, RPLAN) with simulated poses, enabling robust augmentation and generalization across input densities down to 0.6 images per room.

## Results

On the ZInD test set with 1 image per room, BADGR reduces mean pose translation error to 11.2 cm (median 8.8 cm) and mean visible-wall error to 7.0 cm (median 4.6 cm), compared to 19.7/15.6 cm and 12.3/7.0 cm for CovisPose+ and 17.6/12.6 cm and 12.6/6.7 cm for BA-Only. At 2 images per room, BADGR further improves to 10.7 cm mean pose translation and 6.4 cm mean wall error. On RPLAN with 1 image per room and 3.3% Gaussian noise, BADGR achieves 0.35% mean pose error and 0.33% mean layout error, roughly 2× better than BA-Only (0.58% and 0.62%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BADGR consistently outperforms all three baselines (CovisPose, CovisPose+, and BA-Only) on both pose translation and visible-wall error across every input density tested. Compared to CovisPose+ (the strongest non-BA baseline), BADGR reduces mean pose translation error by ~43% (11.2 vs. 19.7 cm at 1 img/room) and mean wall error by ~43% (7.0 vs. 12.3 cm). Against BA-Only, which applies iterative Levenberg-Marquardt optimization without learned structural priors, BADGR achieves ~36% lower mean pose error (11.2 vs. 17.6 cm) and ~44% lower mean wall error (7.0 vs. 12.6 cm) at 1 img/room, with the gap widening at lower densities (0.6 img/room: 12.2 vs. 19.1 cm pose, 7.1 vs. 12.8 cm walls). The advantage is most pronounced in the extreme-sparse regime (0.6 images/room), where BADGR's learned layout-structural constraints compensate for limited visual overlap. On RPLAN with controlled noise, BADGR also shows substantially better robustness to floor-boundary perturbations (e.g., 0.59% vs. 1.47% mean pose error under 5% noise at 1 img/room), confirming that the diffusion model's generative prior mitigates input noise more effectively than pure BA.

## Relation to Prior Work

BADGR sits at the intersection of three research threads: wide-baseline indoor pose estimation (CovisPose, SALVe, GraphCovis), diffusion-based floor plan generation (HouseDiffusion, HouseGAN), and learned/differentiable optimization (BA-Net, Theseus, PoseDiffusion). Its key architectural departure is from guided-diffusion models like PoseDiffusion and PhysDiff, which apply gradients from a differentiable objective to steer a pre-trained diffusion model at inference time; instead, BADGR trains the diffusion model directly with dense per-column Levenberg-Marquardt adjustments as conditioning inputs, making the BA signal part of the learned representation rather than an external steering force. The non-Markovian DDIM-style inference allows the model to integrate nonlinear optimization without specifying step sizes during training, and the FloorPlan-60K dataset (68K plans) extends ZInD to provide the scale needed for this training regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BADGR is the first learning-based method to jointly refine deformable room layouts and camera poses from extremely sparse RGB panoramas (down to 0.6 images per room), and it trains exclusively on 2D schematic floor plans, avoiding the need for 3D scans or RGB-D data. The demonstrated robustness to noisy floor boundary inputs (Table 3) and the substantial gap over BA-Only at low densities suggest that learned structural priors (wall adjacency, collinearity, room topology) are critical when geometric observations are insufficient. Limitations include the requirement for pre-assigned column-to-wall mappings, fixed wall angles under the Atlanta World assumption, and a human-in-the-loop step for wall connectivity; open questions include scalability to multi-floor or non-Manhattan buildings, removing the human annotation bottleneck, and whether the approach generalizes to curved or irregular wall geometries.
