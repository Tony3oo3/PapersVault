## Problem

Existing diffusion-based super-resolution methods apply uniform (isotropic) noise to all regions of the LR image, ignoring that flat areas are already close to the HR target while edge and texture regions are far away. This one-size-fits-all noise schedule either over-perturbs informative flat regions or under-perturbs degraded regions, limiting perceptual quality. Additionally, prior methods like ResShift rely on VQGAN for latent-space diffusion, incurring substantial model size and training overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251537/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 64×64 degraded LR image (produced via the RealESRGAN degradation pipeline) and outputs a 256×256 HR image after 5 diffusion sampling steps. The LR input is first processed by a frozen auxiliary SR network to produce both an SR prediction and a per-pixel uncertainty estimate, which together with the original LR image form the conditional input to the U-Net denoiser. The diffusion process operates in pixel space (compressed via PixelUnshuffle) rather than a learned latent space.

## Method

The core contribution is Uncertainty-guided Noise Weighting (UNW): an auxiliary SR network's residual |g(y)−y| serves as a per-pixel uncertainty estimate, which is mapped through a monotonically increasing function to produce a spatially varying weight w_u(y). This weight modulates the noise covariance in both the forward and backward transition distributions, reducing noise in low-uncertainty (flat) regions while maintaining full noise in high-uncertainty (edge/texture) regions, creating an anisotropic diffusion process. The denoiser receives both the SR prediction g(y₀) and the LR input y₀ as concatenated conditional channels, providing richer guidance than LR alone. Architecturally, VQGAN encoder/decoder are replaced with PixelUnshuffle and nearest-neighbor upsampling, reducing model size by ~30% and training memory by 38% while enabling direct pixel-space diffusion. Training uses a combined L2 + LPIPS loss over 200k iterations with the auxiliary SR network frozen.

## Results

On ImageNet-Test, UPSR-5 achieves the best perceptual quality among all methods: CLIPIQA 0.6328, MUSIQ 59.227, MANIQA 0.4591, and NIQE 5.24, outperforming ResShift-4 (0.6003, 52.019, 0.3885, 7.34) by substantial margins. On RealSR, UPSR-5 reaches MUSIQ 64.541 and NIQE 4.02, surpassing ResShift-4 (55.189, 6.93) and BSRGAN (63.587, 4.65). On RealSet65, UPSR-5 attains CLIPIQA 0.6392 and NIQE 4.23, the best among all compared methods. However, UPSR-5 trades some fidelity (PSNR 23.77 on ImageNet-Test vs. 25.02 for ResShift-4) for significantly better perceptual quality, while reducing overall model size by 30% and training time by 63% compared to ResShift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPSR-5 consistently outperforms ResShift-4 (the closest diffusion-based competitor) on all four perceptual quality metrics across all three datasets: on ImageNet-Test it gains +0.0325 CLIPIQA, +7.21 MUSIQ, +0.0706 MANIQA, and −2.10 NIQE; on RealSR it gains +0.0364 CLIPIQA, +9.35 MUSIQ, +0.0491 MANIQA, and −2.91 NIQE. Against the best GAN-based method BSRGAN, UPSR-5 surpasses it on every perceptual metric (e.g., MUSIQ 59.227 vs. 54.696 on ImageNet-Test; NIQE 4.02 vs. 4.65 on RealSR) while also beating RealESRGAN and SwinIR on perceptual scores. The main trade-off is fidelity: UPSR-5 scores lower PSNR (23.77 vs. 25.02) and SSIM (0.6296 vs. 0.6830) than ResShift-4 on ImageNet-Test, and lower LPIPS (0.2456 vs. 0.2075), indicating a fidelity-perceptual quality trade-off. In terms of efficiency, UPSR-5 uses 30% fewer parameters (119.42M + 2.50M auxiliary vs. 118.59M + 55.32M for ResShift) and trains 167% faster (0.45 s/iter vs. 1.20 s/iter) with 38% less GPU memory (14.9G vs. 24.1G), while matching or exceeding ResShift-4's inference speed (1.12s vs. 1.00s on 32×64×64 inputs). LDM-15 and DASR are clearly outperformed on both fidelity and perceptual metrics, confirming that the residual-shifting formulation of ResShift remains the strongest baseline architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ResShift, which embeds the LR image into the initial noise state to shift the diffusion process from generating the full HR image to estimating the LR-HR residual. The key departure is the introduction of Uncertainty-guided Noise Weighting (UNW), which replaces the isotropic Gaussian noise with an anisotropic, region-specific noise schedule driven by an auxiliary SR network's residual as a proxy for local uncertainty. Architecturally, the paper departs from the VQGAN-based latent-space formulation used by LDM and ResShift, replacing it with PixelUnshuffle and nearest-neighbor upsampling to operate in pixel space at comparable cost. The conceptual lineage runs from SR3 (pure-noise initialization) through ResShift (residual shifting) to UPSR (content-adaptive perturbation), each step progressively exploiting more LR information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: a 30% reduction in model size, 167% training speedup, and 38% memory savings while improving perceptual quality over ResShift-4, making diffusion-based SR more accessible for real-world deployment. The uncertainty-guided noise weighting principle—treating flat and textured regions as residing at different effective timesteps—is a simple, generalizable idea that could transfer to other restoration tasks (denoising, inpainting) where content-dependent noise scheduling is relevant. Open questions include whether the approach benefits from even fewer diffusion steps (1–2), whether the auxiliary SR network can be further simplified or distilled, and whether the anisotropic noise formulation interacts well with newer acceleration techniques like consistency distillation or adversarial diffusion distillation.
