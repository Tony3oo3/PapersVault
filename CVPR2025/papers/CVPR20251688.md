## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for domain generalization, such as LoRA, initialize adapters without considering how generalizable knowledge is structured within pre-trained vision foundation model weights, leading to interference with domain-invariant representations. The paper identifies that this gap stems from a lack of understanding of where generalizable vs. task-specific components reside in the spectral decomposition of pre-trained weights, and addresses it by selectively tuning only the minor singular components while preserving the principal ones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB driving-scene images (e.g., 2048×1024 for Cityscapes, 1280×720 for BDD100K, 1920×1080 for Mapillary) and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks, object detection bounding boxes with class labels, or generated images for subject personalization. Training is performed on synthetic datasets (GTAV, SYNTHIA, UrbanSyn) or a single real source domain, and the model must generalize to unseen target domains without any target-domain data. Because SoMA shares the same forward architecture as LoRA, the learned adapter matrices can be merged into the frozen backbone weights, introducing zero additional inference latency.

## Method

SoMA performs singular value decomposition on each pre-trained linear weight matrix W = UΣV^T, then initializes a LoRA-style adapter using only the r smallest singular components: B = U_{[:,−r:]}√Σ_{[−r:]} and A = √Σ_{[−r:]}(V^T)_{[−r:,:]}, while the residual W_res = W − BA is frozen to preserve generalizable principal components. At the block level, early VFM blocks (which extract domain-invariant low-level semantics) are frozen to prevent overfitting to the source domain. An annealing weight decay schedule starts with a large coefficient and cosine-decays to zero, balancing discriminability gains against interference with principal components. The resulting forward pass is y = (W_res + B'A')x, identical in structure to LoRA, so the adapter merges into the backbone at inference with no extra computation. The method trains only 0.58%–1.6% of total parameters while outperforming full fine-tuning and prior PEFT baselines across DGSS and DGOD benchmarks.

## Results

SoMA achieves 80.4% mIoU on Cityscapes (trained solely on GTAV+SYNTHIA+UrbanSyn with DINOv2-G and multi-scale flip augmentation) while training only 0.58% of parameters (6.6M out of 1.1B). In the single-source GTAV→Cityscapes setting with DINOv2-L, SoMA reaches 73.63% mIoU, outperforming LoRA (70.13%) and Rein (70.68%) by 3.5 and 3.0 points respectively. On the most challenging DGOD setting (Daytime-Sunny → Night-Rainy), SoMA achieves 47.6% mAP@0.5, a +23.5% absolute gain over the prior SOTA (S-DGOD at 16.6%). Across all DGSS and DGOD benchmarks, SoMA consistently surpasses full fine-tuning (FFT) while training 50–100× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoMA outperforms all PEFT baselines (LoRA, DoRA, AdaptFormer, SSF, VPT, SET) and the VFM-based DG method Rein across every DGSS setting. In the single-source GTAV→Cityscapes setting with DINOv2-L, SoMA (73.63% mIoU with flip augmentation) beats LoRA (70.13%) by +3.5 points and Rein (70.68%) by +3.0 points, while using fewer trainable parameters (4.9M vs. 7.3M and 3.0M respectively). In the multi-source setting, SoMA (73.12% avg) surpasses FFT (71.32%) and Rein (71.70%) by 1.8 and 1.4 points on average, despite training only 4.9M vs. 307.3M and 3.0M parameters. On DGOD with DINOv2-L/Co-DETR, SoMA achieves 54.3% avg mAP, exceeding LoRA (53.3%), DoRA (52.7%), AdaptFormer (52.8%), and FFT (51.0%), with the largest margin (+3.3 points) over FFT. SoMA also outperforms classic DGOD methods (S-DGOD, CLIP-Gap, OA-DG, PDOC, UFR, DivAlign) by 2.4–15.6 mAP points in the ResNet101/Faster R-CNN setting, confirming its generality across backbone architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoMA builds directly on LoRA's low-rank adaptation framework but rethinks its initialization: rather than random Kaiming initialization, it performs SVD on pre-trained VFM weights and initializes the adapter with the smallest-r singular components, freezing the principal (generalizable) components. This positions it alongside DoRA and PiSSA, which also decompose pre-trained weights but target principal or directional components to match full fine-tuning performance; SoMA instead deliberately targets minor components to *preserve* generalizability for domain generalization. At a higher level, the paper departs from the dominant DG paradigm of input/feature-space augmentation and alignment losses on classic CNN backbones, proposing a shift toward spectral-aware PEFT of vision foundation models (DINOv2, EVA02) as the primary mechanism for robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SVD-based decomposition provides a concrete, interpretable mechanism for the generalizability–discriminability trade-off in PEFT: principal components encode hierarchical, cross-class world knowledge while minor components encode context-specific features, and selectively tuning only the latter preserves the former. Practically, SoMA adds zero inference overhead (same forward pass as LoRA), requires no auxiliary regularization losses, and is backbone/head-agnostic, making it a drop-in replacement for LoRA in DG pipelines. Open questions include whether the spectral structure insight generalizes beyond ViT-based VFMs to other architectures, whether the block-freezing heuristic (freeze up to the highest-resolution feature map) is optimal across tasks, and whether the approach extends to other distribution-shift settings beyond weather/geography (e.g., sensor modality changes).
