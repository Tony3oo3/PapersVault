## Problem

Existing portrait animation methods either restrict facial control to lip synchronization, rely on sparse landmark guidance that limits expression fidelity, or use 3D representations like Triplane that produce multi-view artifacts such as the 'multi-face' issue. Text-guided portrait animation, the most user-friendly and flexible control modality, remains largely unexplored, and no prior framework simultaneously offers explicit motion/emotion control, multi-view consistency, and compatibility with diverse driving signals (text, audio, video).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250802/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image (resized to 512×512) and a free-form text prompt that is decomposed into motion descriptions (e.g., 'wagging his head') and emotion descriptions (e.g., 'surprised all the time'). It produces a 4-view portrait animation video at 512×512 resolution, with intermediate FLAME pose sequences (12-dim per frame, 6D head + 6D jaw) and expression sequences (50-dim per frame). Because FLAME serves as the intermediate representation, the same FLAME2Video stage can also accept FLAME sequences derived from audio (via Talkshow) or driver video (via DECA), making the framework signal-agnostic.

## Method

MVPortrait is a two-stage framework built around FLAME as an intermediate representation. In Text2FLAME, two separate transformer-based diffusion models (MotionDM and EmotionDM, both using the MDM backbone with a single-layer encoder and 64-dim latent) are trained in the FLAME parameter space to generate pose and expression sequences from text, regularized by a velocity geometric loss and a sliding-window smoothing step to suppress jitter. In FLAME2Video, the FLAME sequence is rendered from 4 viewpoints and fed into a Stable Diffusion 1.5-based generator augmented with AnimateDiff temporal layers, a FLAME encoder (architecturally identical to AniPortrait's pose guider) that injects pose/expression features into the denoising UNet, a Reference UNet with cross-attention for identity preservation, and a novel view attention module that performs self-attention along the view dimension to enforce cross-view consistency. The FLAME2Video stage is trained in three sequential phases (2D components → motion module → view module) with all other components frozen in each phase, and the entire pipeline requires only 2 GB VRAM for Text2FLAME and 14 GB for FLAME2Video.

## Results

On the CelebV-Text test set for text-guided portrait animation, MVPortrait achieves the best FID (28.6 vs. 34.9 for AnimateAnything and 217.4 for MMVID-interp), highest CLIPSIM (0.183), VideoCLIP (0.595), and Variability (0.110) scores, along with the strongest subjective ratings (MC 2.57, EC 2.29, VS 2.48 on a 3-point scale). For multi-view synthesis, MVPortrait attains the best SSIM (0.6224) and identity consistency (ID 0.8409) over Triplanenet (SSIM 0.5974, ID 0.7710) and Portrait4D-v2 (SSIM 0.5278, ID 0.8006). In the audio-driven setting, MVPortrait achieves the lowest FVD (333.2) and best SyncNet score (14.65), while in video-driven animation it leads on FID (48.5), ID (0.895), and FLAME-L1 (0.196).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnimateAnything, MVPortrait wins on FID (28.6 vs. 34.9), CLIPSIM (0.183 vs. 0.171), VideoCLIP (0.595 vs. 0.564), Variability (0.110 vs. 0.095), and all three subjective scores (MC 2.57 vs. 1.33, EC 2.29 vs. 1.23, VS 2.48 vs. 1.41), but trails on FVD (570 vs. 283), which the authors attribute to larger head-motion amplitudes in their outputs. Against MMVID-interp, MVPortrait dominates across nearly all metrics, with FID dropping from 217.4 to 28.6 and FVD from 1548.9 to 570.0. In multi-view synthesis (Table 4), MVPortrait outperforms Triplanenet on SSIM (0.6224 vs. 0.5974) and ID (0.8409 vs. 0.7710), and beats Portrait4D-v2 on both SSIM (0.6224 vs. 0.5278) and ID (0.8409 vs. 0.8006), though Triplanenet reports a lower LPIPS (0.0936 vs. 0.2101) which the authors argue is an artifact of black backgrounds. For video-driven animation (Table 2), MVPortrait surpasses FollowYourEmoji on FID (48.5 vs. 68.5), ID (0.895 vs. 0.888), and FLAME-L1 (0.196 vs. 0.213), but LivePortrait achieves lower L1 (62.0 vs. 74.9) and FVD (268.8 vs. 409.4). In audio-driven animation (Table 3), MVPortrait achieves the best FVD (333.2) and SyncNet (14.65), while AniPortrait edges it slightly on FID (51.5 vs. 56.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

MVPortrait builds on the transformer-based diffusion architecture of MDM for generating FLAME pose and expression sequences, and on AnimateDiff's temporal attention layers for video synthesis within a Stable Diffusion UNet. Its key conceptual departure is using FLAME as an explicit intermediate representation that decouples motion and emotion into two independent diffusion models (MotionDM and EmotionDM), rather than encoding text into latent features as prior portrait animation methods do. The multi-view generation extends the single-view portrait animation paradigm (AniPortrait, FollowYourEmoji) by adding a view-attention module, while the FLAME encoder for pose/expression guidance follows AniPortrait's pose guider design. This positions the work at the intersection of text-conditioned motion generation (MDM, MotionDiffuse) and diffusion-based portrait animation, filling the gap the authors identify for text-driven facial motion generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FLAME-as-bridge design makes MVPortrait the first controllable portrait animation framework compatible with text, speech, and video driving signals, which has practical value for content creators and virtual avatar applications. The multi-view capability addresses a real limitation of prior portrait animation methods that only produce single-view outputs. However, the authors acknowledge that FLAME's parametric space limits micro-expression fidelity, and the framework's quality depends on the accuracy of text annotations in training data. Open questions include whether finer-grained expression control is achievable within FLAME's 50-dim expression space, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively limited motion diversity in CelebV-Text and RenderMe-360.
