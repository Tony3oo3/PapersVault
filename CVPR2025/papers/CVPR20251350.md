## Problem

Existing infrared-visible image fusion methods treat fusion and downstream perception tasks (detection, segmentation) as independent processes, so fused images yield only marginal task gains and provide no feedback to improve fusion quality. Moreover, the common strategy of combining weighted losses for dual objectives relies on manual hyperparameter tuning that typically optimizes one task at the expense of the other, preventing simultaneous optimality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251350/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired infrared and visible images and produces a fused image alongside object detection bounding boxes (via YOLOv8s) and semantic segmentation masks (via SegFormer-b1). The fusion encoder is first pretrained on MSRS, then jointly trained with the detection network on M3FD using the evolutionary strategy; the detection network processes the visible image to extract task-level features that are patch-aligned and embedded back into the fusion pathway, creating a bidirectional supervision loop between the two networks.

## Method

DCEvo integrates three components: (1) a Discriminative Enhancer (DE) inspired by visual neuroscience that applies a sigmoid-based importance weight x_i·S(y_i²/μ_y) to each pixel, amplifying object-background contrast in feature maps within both the encoder and decoder; (2) a Cross-Dimensional Embedding (CDE) block that patch-aligns detection features to the fusion receptive field, then performs cross-attention with detection features as Query and infrared/visible features as Key/Value, followed by self-attention, enabling high-dimensional task semantics to supervise low-dimensional fusion features; (3) a Genetic Algorithm that treats the loss-function coefficients (balancing SSIM, gradient, intensity, decomposition, classification, CIOU, and DFL losses) as a multi-objective optimization problem, evolving a population of 5 coefficient vectors over 5 iterations per epoch with roulette-wheel selection, crossover, and mutation to find the Pareto-optimal trade-off between fusion quality and detection accuracy.

## Results

On the four IVIF benchmarks, DCEvo achieves the highest MI on all datasets (4.54 on M3FD, 3.69 on RoadScene, 4.22 on TNO, 4.38 on FMB) and the best SSIM on RoadScene (0.51), TNO (0.54), and FMB (0.49). For downstream tasks, DCEvo attains 62.13 mAP on M3FD object detection (using YOLOv8s) and 52.52 mIoU on FMB semantic segmentation (using SegFormer-b1), outperforming all 12 compared fusion methods. The paper reports an average 9.32% improvement in visual quality over state-of-the-art approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCEvo outperforms all 12 baselines on MI across every dataset, with the largest margin over SwinFusion (4.54 vs 4.16 MI on M3FD, +9.1%) and CDDFuse (4.54 vs 3.90, +16.4%). On SSIM, DCEvo ties CDDFuse on M3FD (0.51) but leads on RoadScene (0.51 vs 0.48), TNO (0.54 vs 0.52), and FMB (0.49 vs 0.35). For VIF, DCEvo surpasses the previous best (SegMiF at 0.87 on TNO) by 4.7% and Text-IF (0.83 on FMB) by 16.9%. In downstream object detection on M3FD, DCEvo achieves 62.13 mAP, edging out LRRNet (61.71) and SegMiF (61.69) by 0.4–0.5 points. For semantic segmentation on FMB, DCEvo reaches 52.52 mIoU, improving over EMMA (51.55) by nearly 1 full point and over CDDFuse (51.43) by 1.09 points. Notably, DCEvo also leads on the Bus class in detection (75.72) and the Bus class in segmentation (31.45 vs 27.22 for EMMA), suggesting the cross-dimensional embedding particularly benefits smaller or harder-to-separate objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCEvo extends the task-oriented IVIF line (TarDAL, MetaFusion, SegMiF) by introducing an evolutionary algorithm to dynamically optimize loss-function coefficients that balance fusion quality and downstream task accuracy, rather than relying on fixed or manually tuned weights. Architecturally, it builds on CDDFuse's decomposition loss and adopts a CNN-attention encoder-decoder backbone, but departs from prior work by adding a neuroscience-inspired Discriminative Enhancer for object-background contrast and a Cross-Dimensional Embedding block that injects detection features into the fusion pathway. The use of a genetic algorithm for multi-objective hyperparameter search in IVIF training is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such application in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in task-oriented fusion: the fragile, manual balancing of competing loss terms. The evolutionary approach offers a principled alternative, though the demonstrated gains are modest (≈0.4 mAP over experience-based tuning) and the EA is run at a very small scale (population 5, 5 iterations), raising questions about whether the benefit persists with larger, more complex multi-task settings. Open questions include whether the DE and CDE components transfer to other modality pairs or downstream tasks beyond detection and segmentation, and whether the evolutionary strategy scales to more than two simultaneous objectives. The framework could serve as a template for evolutionary hyperparameter search in other multi-task vision pipelines.
