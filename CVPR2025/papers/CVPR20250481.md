## Problem

Existing monocular 3D head avatar reconstruction methods suffer from two key limitations: they only model the frontal face and cannot recover the rear head (yielding incomplete avatars), and the standard 3DGS densification produces redundant, discrete Gaussian points that are inefficient and cannot be directly edited in UV texture space like traditional mesh textures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250481/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular portrait video (with consistent lighting) as input, preprocessed with a face tracker (MICA or DECA) to obtain per-frame FLAME pose and expression parameters. It outputs an animatable 3D Gaussian head avatar parameterized in UV space with ~40–60k Gaussians, supporting 360° full-head rendering after the completion stage, and continuous editable UV attribute maps (color, opacity, offset) after neural baking. The baked texture maps are fixed-resolution 2D images that can be edited with standard image tools without re-optimization.

## Method

FATE represents the head as Gaussians bound to FLAME mesh faces in UV space, with learnable expression/pose blendshapes to capture geometric deviation from the template. The key densification mechanism replaces 3DGS's threshold-based cloning/splitting with multinomial sampling: position gradients serve as importance scores, and new Gaussians are placed at random barycentric coordinates within the same face triangle, yielding a controlled, non-redundant point distribution. For texture editing, a two-stage neural baking process uses a U-Net (BakeNet) as a low-pass pre-filter combined with bilinear interpolation to convert discrete Gaussian attributes into smooth, continuous UV maps, enabling direct texture manipulation. A universal full-head completion framework extracts priors from SphereHead via coordinate alignment (TDDFA landmarks), image quality alignment (GFPGAN), and multi-view PTI inversion with cross-training to synthesize side and rear views, producing a 360°-renderable avatar.

## Results

FATE achieves the best overall performance across all four evaluation datasets, reaching 28.37 PSNR, 0.9439 SSIM, and 0.0586 LPIPS on average. On the PointAvatar dataset, FATE leads with 28.74 PSNR and 0.0719 LPIPS, outperforming the next-best MonoGaussianAvatar (28.16 PSNR, 0.0977 LPIPS). On NerFace, FATE achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.0257) while MonoGaussianAvatar edges ahead in PSNR (33.78 vs. 33.70) and SSIM (0.9765 vs. 0.9736). The baked variant (Ours-baked) retains strong performance at 28.23 PSNR and 0.0676 LPIPS overall, confirming that neural baking introduces only a minor quality trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FATE outperforms all four baselines in overall PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the closest competitor being MonoGaussianAvatar (MGA) at 28.07 PSNR vs. FATE's 28.37. On the PointAvatar dataset, FATE leads MGA by 0.58 dB in PSNR and 0.026 in LPIPS, a substantial gap. On NerFace, MGA narrowly beats FATE in PSNR (33.78 vs. 33.70) and SSIM (0.9765 vs. 0.9736), but FATE achieves the best LPIPS (0.0257 vs. 0.0315), indicating superior perceptual quality. FlashAvatar (FA) is competitive on INSTA LPIPS (0.0578 vs. 0.0603) but trails FATE in PSNR and SSIM across all datasets. SplattingAvatar (SA) and GaussianAvatars (GA) are consistently the weakest baselines, with SA showing particularly poor LPIPS (0.1135 overall) due to its high Gaussian count (558k±188k vs. FATE's 49k±6k), suggesting that FATE's sampling-based densification yields both fewer primitives and better reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FATE extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting-based monocular head avatar line (FlashAvatar, SplattingAvatar, MonoGaussianAvatar, GaussianAvatars) by addressing three specific limitations: threshold-based densification inefficiency, discrete Gaussian representation that resists UV-space editing, and frontal-only reconstruction. It builds on FLAME with learnable blendshapes for geometry, borrows the SphereHead generative prior via PTI inversion for rear-head completion, and introduces a U-Net-based 'neural baking' step that leverages CNN inductive biases as a spatial regularizer to convert discrete Gaussians into continuous texture maps. The sampling-based densification departs from the original 3DGS cloning/splitting mechanism by replacing a fixed gradient threshold with multinomial importance sampling over face-bound Gaussians, yielding more controlled point distribution in UV space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FATE is the first method to simultaneously deliver animatability, 360° full-head rendering, and monocular-video input, filling a gap where prior approaches either required multi-view capture for full-head coverage or produced only frontal avatars. The neural baking technique offers a practical, optimization-free path to texture editing that avoids the cost and uncontrollability of diffusion-model-based editing, and the universal completion framework can be grafted onto other reconstruction pipelines. Key limitations include the assumption of consistent lighting, dependence on SphereHead's training distribution (risking identity drift in the completed regions), and fixed-resolution texture maps that may fail for extreme geometry. Open questions include whether Mip-Map-style baking can handle multi-scale detail, and whether full-body priors (e.g., SMPL-X) can be integrated for broader immersive applications.
