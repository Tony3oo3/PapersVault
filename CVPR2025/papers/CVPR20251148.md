## Problem

C-ToF radiance field reconstruction is under-constrained because depth is only indirectly optimized through fitting sensor measurements rather than being directly supervised, making fast Gaussian splatting representations brittle and prone to producing highly inaccurate depth while still matching C-ToF data. Existing neural volumetric approaches (ToRF, F-ToRF) handle this with MLP-based representations that are inherently more robust but require 60–72 hours of optimization, making single-camera dynamic 4D reconstruction impractical.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronously captured raw C-ToF quartets (four phase-offset frames at 320×240, 30 fps, 5 m unambiguous range from a TI OPT8241 sensor) and an optional synchronized RGB video, along with known camera poses. It produces a canonical set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians with an MLP-based temporal deformation field, from which volumetric depth maps and novel-view renderings can be extracted at 100+ Hz. Unlike methods that take derived depth maps, the system operates directly on raw sensor samples, requiring it to resolve temporal correspondence within each asynchronously captured quartet.

## Method

The approach extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with a C-ToF phasor image formation model that accounts for inverse-square light falloff, view-dependent spherical-harmonic reflectivity, and squared transmittance for round-trip light travel, rendered via custom differentiable CUDA rasterization. A canonical Gaussian scene is deformed over time by an 8-layer MLP (256 neurons) that maps (position, time) to 3D offsets, with local linearity enforced between integer timesteps to handle asynchronous quartet capture. The key contribution is two optimization heuristics that stabilize the under-constrained depth estimation: an occupancy bias (10× lower learning rate for reflectivity so position/opacity changes dominate) and a low-reflectivity initialization (0.1 instead of 0.5) that prevents the inverse-square falloff from misplacing low-reflectivity Gaussians far from the camera. Training combines raw quad L2+SSIM loss, optional color loss, and weak optical-flow supervision (RAFT), with random background perturbation and a 2K-iteration static warm-up stage to seed useful Gaussian positions.

## Results

On the F-ToRF synthetic dataset (7 scenes), the method achieves the best volume-integrated depth MSE (d) on 5 out of 7 scenes, with notable wins on Sliding Cube (0.037 vs. 0.440 for F-ToRF), Axial Speed Test (0.525 vs. 0.938), and Arcing Cube (1.023 vs. 1.256). For depth derived from reconstructed C-ToF signals (d_ToF), it wins on 5 of 7 scenes, e.g., 0.005 vs. 0.023 on Sliding Cube and 14.933 vs. 27.488 on Orthogonal Speed Test. The method is 100× faster than NeRF-based baselines (40–60 min vs. 60–72 hours per scene) and renders at 100+ Hz in real time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against F-ToRF (the strongest NeRF-based baseline, 72 h training), the method wins on 5 of 7 synthetic scenes for volume-integrated depth d and 5 of 7 for d_ToF, while losing on 3 Chairs Speed Test (1.023 vs. 0.855 for d) and Orthogonal Speed Test (22.772 vs. 7.527 for d), where thin fast-moving structures and a local optimum degrade results. Against TöRF (60 h, phasor-based, assumes synchronous capture), the method is consistently better on d across all 7 scenes (e.g., 0.037 vs. 0.349 on Sliding Cube, 1.023 vs. 6.278 on Arcing Cube). Against DeformableGS with an added C-ToF depth loss, the method produces substantially better reconstructions of dynamic objects; DeformableGS often fails entirely on fast-moving scenes (Arcing Cube, 3 Chairs Speed Test). The 2D Flow baseline (softmax splatting + RAFT) is competitive on low-disparity scenes (Sliding Cube: 0.018 vs. 0.037) but degrades sharply on axial motion (Axial ST: 0.662 vs. 0.525; Ortho. ST: 19.805 vs. 22.772) and cannot handle the full 3D geometry that the volumetric method recovers. On real-world F-ToRF sequences, the method matches F-ToRF in overall quality while producing sharper static-region reconstructions (e.g., floor in JumpingJacks), and both outperform DeformableGS and 2D Flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the C-ToF radiance field formulation from TöRF (phasor-based NeRF) and F-ToRF (raw asynchronous NeRF) to the faster 3D Gaussian splatting framework, building on DeformableGS for monocular dynamic scenes. The key departure is identifying that GS, which serves as a drop-in replacement for NeRFs in well-constrained multi-view settings, becomes fundamentally brittle here because depth is only indirectly optimized through C-ToF signal fitting, and unlike MLPs, Gaussians lack an inherent low-entropy bias that would naturally concentrate density at surfaces. The two proposed heuristics (occupancy bias via reduced reflectivity learning rate, and low-reflectivity initialization) are the novel contribution that closes this gap without the multi-view or RGB constraints used by prior C-ToF NeRF methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× speedup (from ~60-72 hours to 40-60 minutes) makes single-camera 4D dynamic scene reconstruction from C-ToF practically viable, and the conceptual insight about why GS optimization fails in under-constrained indirect-property settings may inform other GS applications beyond C-ToF. However, the heuristics are explicitly acknowledged as lacking universal guarantees, depth wrapping remains unresolved for static cameras, and the method struggles with strongly nonlinear motion (spinning fans) and thin fast-moving objects. Open questions include whether more flexible motion models or spacetime rigidity constraints could address the nonlinear motion limitation, and whether the occupancy/reflectivity biases generalize to other under-constrained GS reconstruction problems.
