## Problem

Existing learning-based autofocus methods achieve strong single-step prediction accuracy but, when applied iteratively for multi-step focusing, produce back-and-forth lens movements (focus hunting) because they ignore sequential trajectory information and the long-term consequences of each prediction. This paper addresses the gap by formulating autofocus as a sequential decision-making problem that simultaneously improves multi-step in-focus accuracy and suppresses focus hunting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251446/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128×128 dual-pixel image patches (two channels: left and right sub-images) concatenated with RoI position encoding and lens position encoding, forming a 5-channel input tensor. The actor network outputs a discrete relative lens movement action over a range of [-k_max, k_max] (97 possible values), where the sign encodes direction and magnitude encodes distance; out-of-bound predictions are clipped. Unlike prior work that predicts an absolute in-focus index, the relative formulation directly maps dual-pixel disparity to lens displacement, avoiding an implicit identity mapping of the current lens position.

## Method

The core contribution is an actor-critic DRL framework (PPO-CLIP) where the actor (MobileNetV2 policy) is initialized from a pre-trained single-step prediction network whose output is reparameterized from absolute position to relative movement distribution, and the critic (same architecture) estimates the expected future accumulated reward to evaluate long-term action quality. A reward function combines negative distance-to-ground-truth with an explicit focus-hunting penalty term. To stabilize training and further suppress focus hunting, the authors generate an offline expert trajectory dataset (6M trajectories) via a deterministic rule that always moves the lens monotonically toward the ground-truth index, then regularize the actor with an imitation loss (negative log-likelihood of expert actions) weighted by λ=0.001 alongside the PPO clipped surrogate objective. This hybrid online-offline training overcomes the low sample efficiency of vanilla DRL in the 97-action discrete space while enforcing smooth, non-oscillatory lens trajectories.

## Results

On the Herrmann et al. AF dataset (49 focal depths, 128×128 dual-pixel patches), the proposed Expert Trajectory DRL method achieves 44.9% exact (=0) multi-step prediction accuracy at 4 steps, compared to 27.6% for the strongest baseline (Choi et al. [7]), while reducing the focus hunting (FH) rate from 38.3% to 17.8%. In single-step prediction, the method reaches 33.6% exact accuracy and 91.5% within ≤4 indices, outperforming [7] (24.3% and 88.2%) and Herrmann et al. [16] (16.8% and 85.1%). The MAE drops from 2.44 to 1.69 and RMSE from 6.28 to 5.48 in the 4-step setting, and the method requires fewer average steps to stop (1.82 vs. 2.21 for [7] with a stop model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest learning-based baseline, Choi et al. [7] (RoI-PE & Lens-PE, ICCV 2023), the proposed method improves 4-step exact accuracy from 27.6% to 44.9% (+17.3 pp) and cuts the FH rate from 38.3% to 17.8% (−53% relative). The relative-movement label modification alone (without DRL) already boosts [7]'s 4-step exact accuracy to 42.3% but barely reduces FH (35.8% vs. 38.3%), confirming that the DRL + expert trajectory component is essential for stabilizing the trajectory. Against Herrmann et al. [16] (CVPR 2020), the gap is even larger: 44.9% vs. 16.8% exact single-step accuracy. The two prior DRL baselines (Chan et al. [6] and Yu et al. [54]) achieve near-zero FH but at the cost of extremely low accuracy (≤5% exact), as they use scalar inputs or generalize poorly across views, making them non-competitive. The method also outperforms depth-estimation-based approaches (Garg et al. [11]: 11.2% exact) and classical phase-based methods (ZNCC/SSD disparity: 6.5–10.1% exact) by a wide margin on single-step prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-based autofocus line initiated by Herrmann et al. [16] and refined by Choi et al. [7], which use dual-pixel CNNs (MobileNetV2 with RoI-PE and Lens-PE) to predict the in-focus lens position in a single step. The key departure is reformulating autofocus as a multi-step sequential decision problem (MDP) solved via PPO actor-critic DRL, where the policy predicts relative lens movement rather than absolute position, and an offline expert-trajectory dataset regularizes training to suppress focus hunting. Prior DRL-based AF attempts (Chan et al. [6], Yu et al. [54]) focused on reward design with scalar inputs, while this work leverages full dual-pixel image features and explicitly targets the focus-hunting phenomenon that supervised single-step methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Focus hunting is a practical, user-visible failure mode in real cameras that prior learning-based AF methods have not addressed; this paper provides the first systematic DRL formulation that simultaneously improves multi-step accuracy and reduces FH rate (by ~18% at 4 steps). The critical dependence on expert trajectory regularization (vanilla DRL collapses to ~0.03 accuracy with ~0.99 FH rate) highlights that the 97-action discrete space makes pure online RL impractical without offline guidance, a finding relevant to other fine-grained sequential control tasks. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (Herrmann's Pixel 3 focal stacks), reliance on GT positions for expert trajectory generation, and the discrete action space that may not transfer directly to continuous lens actuators in production cameras.
