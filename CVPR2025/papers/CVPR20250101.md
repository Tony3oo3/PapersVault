## Problem

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) performing In-Context Learning (ICL) suffer from a 'Visual Context Overlook' phenomenon: they rely on textual pattern matching in demonstrations rather than integrating visual information, as evidenced by models performing equally well when demonstration images are replaced with blank placeholders. Existing DPO-based alignment methods are designed for general instruction-following and lack mechanisms to enforce multimodal ICD comprehension, making it difficult to collect reliable preference data when questions can be answered from text alone.

## Input / Output

The model receives a final image-question pair preceded by N in-context demonstrations, each consisting of an image, a question, and a semantically meaningless symbol string (e.g., 'rhondda', 'odwyer') in place of the original textual answer. One designated demonstration's symbol is aligned with the correct answer to the final question, while the rejected answer is either a different symbol or a semantically relevant but incorrect text answer. The model outputs a single text answer, and the DPO training signal compares the log-likelihood ratio of the chosen (symbolic) answer against the rejected answer relative to a frozen reference model.

## Method

SymDPO constructs preference data by replacing all textual answers in in-context demonstrations with contextually mismatched symbolic strings, forcing the model to build an implicit mapping between visual content and symbols rather than exploiting textual semantics. Five data configurations are generated by varying whether questions in ICDs are erased and whether the rejected answer is semantically relevant or purely symbolic, maintaining diversity in the final 10,000-sample training set drawn from 872,000 constructed items. The optimization uses the standard DPO loss (Eq. 4) with a KL-regularized reference model, applied as post-training fine-tuning on OpenFlamingo and IDEFICS at a learning rate of 5e-6 with linear annealing. A 70% symbolic-to-general DPO data ratio is found optimal for most tasks, while 100% symbolic data benefits knowledge-intensive QA (OK-VQA), indicating complementary roles for the two data types.

## Results

On OF-3B(I) with 4-shot ICL, SymDPO improves COCO Caption CIDEr from 82.7 to 87.4 (+4.7) and Flickr-30K CIDEr from 59.1 to 61.2 (+2.1), while also gaining on VQAv2 (45.7→46.2), OK-VQA (30.6→31.6), and TextVQA (28.1→28.3). At 8-shot, gains are larger: COCO rises to 91.2 (+3.4) and Flickr-30K to 65.3 (+4.6). The same pattern holds for OF-9B and IDEFICS-9B, where SymDPO consistently outperforms the base model across all five benchmarks at every shot count tested (4, 8, 16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymDPO clearly outperforms both Video DPO and MIA-DPO across all benchmarks and shot settings. Video DPO [Zhang et al., 2024] shows no meaningful improvement over the base model and occasionally degrades performance (e.g., COCO drops from 82.7 to 82.5 at 4-shot; OK-VQA drops from 30.6 to 30.3), likely because it is designed for video-specific semantic alignment rather than in-context multimodal instruction following. MIA-DPO [Liu et al., 2024] yields only marginal gains (e.g., +2.0 CIDEr on COCO at 4-shot) since it targets multi-image hallucination mitigation rather than the visual-context-overlook problem in ICL. General DPO (standard preference optimization without symbolic replacement) provides small but consistent improvements (+0.1 to +0.9), confirming that the symbolic answer replacement in SymDPO is the key differentiator. The largest SymDPO advantage appears on Flickr-30K (+2.1 to +4.6 CIDEr) and OK-VQA (+1.0 to +1.9 Acc), tasks where visual context in demonstrations is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

SymDPO builds directly on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023), repurposing its preference-based loss for a multimodal ICL-specific objective. It departs from prior DPO variants for LMMs (Video DPO, MIA-DPO) that target general instruction-following or multi-image hallucination, by introducing a data-construction mechanism—replacing demonstration answers with semantically neutral symbols—that forces the model to ground its reasoning in visual content. The symbolic substitution draws conceptual inspiration from Symbol Tuning (Wei et al., 2023), but SymDPO embeds this idea within a preference-optimization loop rather than autoregressive fine-tuning, and specifically targets the 'visual context overlook' phenomenon previously documented in multimodal ICL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-cost post-training recipe (≈1 hour on 8 A100s) that consistently improves multimodal ICL across captioning and QA benchmarks, addressing a well-recognized but under-addressed failure mode in LMMs. However, the evaluation is limited to two older architectures (OpenFlamingo, IDEFICS) and the gains, while consistent, are moderate (typically 1–5 points), leaving open whether the approach transfers to newer, more capable LMMs like LLaVA or GPT-4V. A key open question is whether symbolic demonstration strategies can be extended to more complex, multi-step reasoning tasks where the answer space is not a small closed set, and whether the 70% symbolic-data ratio finding generalizes across task types.
