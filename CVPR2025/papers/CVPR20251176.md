## Problem

Existing AD-VQA methods express spatial coordinates as raw numerical text (e.g., "<1018.5,510.8>"), creating a semantic gap between visual coordinate representations and linguistic descriptions. This misalignment forces LLMs to generate complex coordinate strings, undermining both perceptual accuracy and the precise articulation of spatial information needed for downstream prediction and planning tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view driving images (6 views at 448×448 for DriveLM, 1 front view for CODA-LM) and a text question. A detection expert (StreamPETR) pre-processes each image to produce object masks and centroid coordinates, which are rendered into a visual marker image with numerical labels overlaid at centroids and semi-transparent region overlays. The output is a text response in which spatial references appear as concise marker indices (e.g., "<ID 1>") that are post-mapped to pixel coordinates, rather than raw coordinate strings.

## Method

MPDrive introduces two core modules. Marker ControlNet (MCNet) freezes the original visual encoder and trains a LoRA-adapted copy (rank 16 on attention and FFN) on the marker image, connected via a zero-initialized linear layer so that marker-derived features are gradually injected without disrupting original image features; the two feature maps are fused by element-wise addition to yield scene-level features. Perception-Enhanced Spatial Prompt Learning (PSPL) then derives instance-level features by applying mask average pooling of the scene features over each detected object's binary mask, and passes both scene- and instance-level features through connected MLPs to produce dual-granularity visual prompts. The LLM (also LoRA-tuned at rank 16) receives these visual prompts alongside text tokens and predicts marker indices rather than raw coordinates, reducing linguistic complexity. Training uses AdamW with a cosine schedule (initial LR 5e-4, weight decay 0.01), keeping the visual encoder frozen throughout.

## Results

On the DriveLM dataset (multi-view, 6 cameras), MPDrive achieves a Match score of 13.43 and Accuracy of 85.18 for spatial perception, along with BLEU-4 of 52.71, CIDEr of 3.56, and METEOR of 38.31 for language quality, outperforming all single-turn baselines. On the CODA-LM dataset (single-view), MPDrive scores 41.80 on General perception and 58.20 on driving Suggestion generation, substantially exceeding the next-best methods (Qwen-VL-Max at 34.60 and 47.40 respectively). The method also demonstrates model-agnostic gains: when applied to LLaMA-Adapter, the Match score jumps from 1.48 to 10.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DriveLM, MPDrive outperforms InternVL-2 (the strongest single-turn baseline) on Match (13.43 vs. 7.59), Accuracy (85.18 vs. 82.54), BLEU-4 (52.71 vs. 51.42), CIDEr (3.56 vs. 3.53), and METEOR (38.31 vs. 37.12), though it trails by a negligible 0.10 on ROUGE_L (76.98 vs. 77.08). Against the graph-based multi-turn DriveLM-Agent, MPDrive achieves higher CIDEr (3.56 vs. 2.79), METEOR (38.31 vs. 36.19), and ROUGE_L (76.98 vs. 66.79) while being marginally lower on BLEU-4 (52.71 vs. 53.09), all with a simpler single-turn inference. On CODA-LM, MPDrive leads on General perception (41.80 vs. 34.60 for Qwen-VL-Max), Vehicle (79.48 vs. 69.83), VRU (70.00 vs. 60.00), and Suggestion (58.20 vs. 47.40), but concedes Cone (77.77 vs. 84.44 for MiniDrive) and Other (62.85 vs. 65.71 for Qwen-VL-Max). The ablation with LLaMA-Adapter confirms the framework's model-agnostic benefit, improving Match from 1.48 to 10.05 and METEOR from 33.66 to 35.58.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPDrive draws directly on the Set-of-Mark (SoM) prompting paradigm, which overlays numbered regions on images to aid visual grounding, but repurposes it for autonomous driving by converting spatial coordinate generation into discrete marker-index prediction. It departs from prior AD-VQA methods (ELM, LLM-Driver, Reason2Drive, DriveVLM) that encode object positions as textual coordinate strings, arguing this creates a semantic gap between visual and linguistic modalities. Architecturally, the Marker ControlNet follows the ControlNet recipe (frozen encoder + LoRA-trained copy with zero-initialized linear fusion), while the Perception-Enhanced Spatial Prompt Learning module extends standard visual prompting with instance-level features via mask average pooling over detection masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core reframing—predicting a small integer index rather than continuous coordinates—elegantly sidesteps the well-known difficulty of LLMs generating precise numerical values, and the model-agnostic design (validated on both InternVL-2 and LLaMA-Adapter) suggests the technique could transfer to other spatial-reasoning MLLM applications. However, the approach is tightly coupled to a strong detection expert (StreamPETR), so upstream detection errors propagate directly into the marker annotations and limit robustness. The authors themselves note that long-horizon temporal perception remains unaddressed, and the absolute match scores (13.43 on DriveLM) indicate that precise spatial grounding in driving scenes is still far from solved.
