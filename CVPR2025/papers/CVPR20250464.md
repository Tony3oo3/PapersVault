## Problem

Existing manipulation models rely solely on internal common sense and cannot comprehend multi-page appliance manuals to learn component-specific functions (e.g., which knob controls temperature vs. timer). Prior manual-related datasets from NLP research focus on question-answering and page retrieval without corresponding CAD models, leaving no benchmark or baseline for evaluating whether a robot can actually follow a manual to manipulate an appliance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250464/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a natural-language task instruction, a multi-page PDF appliance manual (processed into page-level images for OCR and MLLM-based visual element detection), RGB-D camera observations of the tabletop appliance, and optionally an articulated CAD model. Outputs are structured as: (1) a part-to-function-name alignment mapping between observed/CAD parts and manual annotations, (2) a step-by-step plan in dictionary format specifying part function names and target states (e.g., {'Brew Strength Lever': 'Rotate 60 degrees'}), and (3) low-level gripper actions (grasp poses, push directions, rotation angles) executed in the SAPIEN simulator or on a Franka Panda arm.

## Method

ManualPlan comprises three modules: Manual Resolution (splits PDF into page images, extracts text via Paddle OCR and detects figures/tables via MLLM), Manipulation Planning (LLM generates step-by-step plans constrained to manual-mentioned parts, with regeneration on unresolvable predictions), and Part Alignment (Set-of-Mark prompting using Grounding-DINO bounding boxes to prompt SAM for part masks, overlaid on observations for MLLM-based mapping to manual function names). For execution, Track 2 uses CAD-assisted primitive actions where FoundationPose estimates 6-DoF pose, AnyGrasp predicts part-level grasp poses constrained by 3D bounding boxes, and joint-axis information drives rotation/translation sequences; Track 3 delegates low-level control to VoxPoser, which receives grounded target components and language-described manipulation methods from ManualPlan. The CheckManual dataset itself is generated by an LLM-assisted pipeline (GPT-4o) that annotates PartNet-Mobility CAD models with part functions, creates manipulation tasks, designs manual figures, and compiles LaTeX into diverse PDF manuals, with over 2200 part annotations, 1400 tasks, and 1800 figures verified by humans.

## Results

On the CheckManual benchmark, ManualPlan achieves 27.32% Part Alignment Success Rate and 20.70% Task Planning Success Rate on Track 1 (CAD-aligned planning), dropping to 15.46% and 9.96% without the manual. For physical execution, ManualPlan+PrimitiveActions (Track 2) reaches 4.50% Task Completion Rate and 2.39% Task Success Rate, while ManualPlan+VoxPoser (Track 3, CAD-free) achieves 5.18% and 2.87% respectively. Ablating the manual input consistently degrades performance by roughly 40–50% relative across all three tracks, confirming the manual's critical role in guiding appliance manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CheckManual is a new benchmark, so no external competing methods are evaluated; the paper's own ManualPlan framework serves as the sole baseline across all three tracks. The primary comparison is an ablation: removing the appliance manual (w/o manual) degrades Part Alignment by ~12 points (27.32→15.46) and Task Planning by ~11 points (20.70→9.96) on Track 1, and reduces Task Success Rate from 2.39% to 1.42% on Track 2 and from 2.87% to 2.14% on Track 3. The authors attribute the low absolute success rates to error accumulation across part identification, task planning, and multi-step execution in long-horizon manipulation. Notably, Track 3 (CAD-free) slightly outperforms Track 2 (CAD-assisted) in Task Completion Rate (5.18% vs. 4.50%), suggesting that the open-vocabulary VoxPoser executor may be more robust than the rigid CAD-assisted primitive actions for this task distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new intersection between two previously disconnected research threads: (1) manipulation large models (RT-1/2, VoxPoser, CoPa, ReKep, OpenVLA) that plan and execute actions from internal common sense but cannot read multi-page documents, and (2) manual-based NLP benchmarks (TechQA, S10 QA, PM209) that evaluate QA and page retrieval over appliance manuals but lack CAD models and any physical manipulation component. The paper departs from both by introducing the first dataset and benchmark where a robot must comprehend a multi-page PDF manual and translate that understanding into physical appliance manipulation. For low-level execution it builds directly on VoxPoser (Track 3) and CAD-assisted primitive actions with AnyGrasp (Track 2), while its part-alignment module repurposes the Set-of-Mark prompting paradigm with Grounding-DINO and SAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CheckManual fills a clear gap: no prior dataset or benchmark allowed quantitative evaluation of whether a robot can actually follow a manufacturer's manual to operate an appliance, a capability that is essential for household robotics but has been entirely unmeasured. The ablation confirming that removing the manual substantially degrades performance validates the premise that appliance part functions are not inferable from appearance or common sense alone. However, the baselines are very preliminary (Track 2/3 completion rates of ~4–5%), the real-robot deployment section is brief with only a single task definition, and the LLM-assisted data generation pipeline, while human-verified, raises questions about how well synthetic manuals generalize to the messier, less structured real-world manuals the benchmark ultimately aims to emulate. Open questions include whether the three-track decomposition (planning → CAD-assisted execution → CAD-free execution) will become a standard evaluation protocol and how to close the large gap between partial planning success and end-to-end task completion.
