## Problem

Existing arbitrary-scale super-resolution methods suffer from two intertwined limitations: standard Fourier Neural Operators lose critical high-frequency details through mode truncation, and MLP-based approaches exhibit spectral bias that underrepresents fine textures. Meanwhile, diffusion-based refinement, while effective for recovering lost details, is computationally prohibitive due to the large number of reverse-diffusion sampling steps required.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250153/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single low-resolution RGB image and produces a high-resolution RGB image at an arbitrary user-specified scaling factor (integer or continuous, e.g., ×7.6). Training uses scales uniformly sampled from ×1 to ×4, so evaluation at ×6, ×8, and ×12 tests out-of-distribution generalization. The LR input is lifted into a feature space by a CNN encoder (EDSR-baseline or RDN), and the output is a full-resolution HR image in RGB space after the diffusion refinement loop.

## Method

DiffFNO replaces standard FNO mode truncation with a Mode Rebalancing mechanism that applies a learned frequency-dependent weight w_l(ξ) = 1 + γ_l·‖ξ‖^α to all Fourier modes, amplifying high-frequency components critical for texture reconstruction. A Gated Fusion Mechanism then adaptively blends WFNO's global spectral features with local spatial features from a lightweight Attention-based Neural Operator (AttnNO) via a learned per-pixel gating map, balancing long-range structure with fine-grained detail. The forward diffusion process is a modified variance-preserving SDE whose drift term explicitly models high-frequency loss from downsampling (x − Dx), and the reverse process is solved as a deterministic ODE using an Adaptive Time-Step solver that learns a polynomial mapping to non-uniformly allocate just 30 RK4 integration steps (vs. 1000 uniform steps), cutting inference time from 266 ms to 141 ms while maintaining or improving PSNR.

## Results

On the DIV2K validation set with EDSR encoder, DiffFNO achieves 35.72 dB PSNR / 0.932 SSIM at ×2, 30.88 dB / 0.870 at ×4, and 26.48 dB / 0.775 at ×12, consistently outperforming all baselines across every scaling factor. With the RDN encoder, gains are slightly larger (e.g., 31.03 dB at ×4, 26.63 dB at ×12). The advantage widens at out-of-distribution scales (×6, ×8, ×12), where DiffFNO leads the nearest competitor (SRNO or LMI) by 0.5–0.8 dB in PSNR. Inference time is 141 ms with only 30 ODE steps, faster than SRNO (147 ms) and substantially faster than the 1000-step variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffFNO outperforms all seven compared methods (MetaSR, LIIF, LTE, LIT, LMI, SRNO, HiNOTE) on every scaling factor and both encoders. Against the strongest implicit-function baselines (LMI, LIT), DiffFNO leads by 0.3–0.7 dB PSNR, with the gap widening at ×8 and ×12 (e.g., 26.78 vs 26.16 dB at ×8 EDSR). Against the closest neural-operator baselines SRNO and HiNOTE, DiffFNO gains 0.5–0.8 dB at higher scales (×6–×12), where SRNO's 25.91 dB at ×12 falls to 26.48 dB for DiffFNO. Against the weakest baseline MetaSR, the margin reaches 2.5–2.7 dB at ×4–×12. On the four standard benchmark datasets (Set5, Set14, BSD100, Urban100), DiffFNO similarly leads all methods at every scale, with the largest absolute gains on Urban100 (e.g., 27.74 vs 27.55 dB at ×4) where complex textures stress high-frequency reconstruction. Inference time (141 ms, 30 steps) is competitive with SRNO (147 ms) and far below the 1000-step ablation variants (204–266 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffFNO sits at the intersection of three research threads: Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) for resolution-invariant function-space mappings, score-based diffusion models for iterative image refinement, and arbitrary-scale super-resolution via neural operators (SRNO, HiNOTE). It extends FNO by replacing mode truncation with a learned Mode Rebalancing weighting (WFNO), and extends the SRNO architecture by adding a parallel Attention-based Neural Operator fused via a Gated Fusion Mechanism. The diffusion component repurposes the score-matching SDE framework from Song et al. but reformulates the forward process as a downscaling-aware VP-SDE, and accelerates inference with an Adaptive Time-Step RK4 ODE solver rather than the standard fixed-step DDIM or DPM-Solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in combining resolution-invariant neural operators with a diffusion refinement loop while keeping inference to 30 ODE steps, making diffusion-based SR more deployable. The out-of-distribution generalization to ×6, ×8, and ×12 (trained only on ×1–×4) is a meaningful step toward truly arbitrary-scale SR. However, the headline '2–4 dB' improvement is measured against weaker baselines (Meta-SR, LTE); against the strongest competitors (LMI, SRNO, HiNOTE), gains are closer to 0.3–1 dB. Open questions include whether the diffusion refinement is essential or acts primarily as a strong regularizer, whether the approach extends to video or multi-frame SR, and how the method scales to much larger images where the FNO's FFT cost becomes prohibitive.
