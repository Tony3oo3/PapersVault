## Problem

Large-scale 3D human-object interaction datasets captured in natural home environments are missing: existing datasets either omit objects entirely, lack dexterous hand manipulation, restrict to static postures or single-object grasping, or use constrained table setups with limited action diversity. No prior system captures full-body motion, finger-level manipulation, and multi-object (including articulated) dynamics simultaneously in a realistic room with sequential and concurrent interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251732/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The capture system ingests synchronized signals from 70 RGB cameras, an Xsens IMU body suit (23 joint angles per frame), and Manus hand gloves, producing a unified parameterized state S(t) in a common 3D spatio-temporal space: SMPL-X body shape/pose, per-hand joint orientations, 6-DoF rigid object transforms, and object-specific articulation scalars (radians or meters per movable part). For the generative modeling experiments, the model takes either a text description or a target object state trajectory plus initial person-to-object relative pose as input, and outputs a 743-dimensional per-frame motion vector (root height/velocity/rotation, 61 joint positions, 6D relative rotations, velocities, and foot contacts) over a temporal window.

## Method

ParaHome fuses a 70-camera multi-view system with wearable IMU mocap via a 3D ArUco marker cube (6 cm, markers on all six faces) attached to each object, enabling robust 6-DoF tracking even under severe hand occlusion; object poses are recovered by triangulating marker corners across cameras and applying the Kabsch algorithm to compute the marker-to-object transform. Spatial alignment between the camera and IMU systems is achieved by attaching ArUco markers to 11 near-rigid body parts and optimizing body skeleton offsets and marker positions via forward kinematics against multi-view triangulated positions, with a dedicated hand calibration protocol using a corner-touching structure to recover true hand skeleton scales. Post-processing leverages the complementary strengths of both systems: weighted interpolation between hand-marker and body-mocap wrist estimates reduces jitter, and body-joint temporal transforms recover object tracking failures during occlusion. For generative modeling, the paper trains an MDM diffusion model (directly predicting clean samples rather than noise) on a mixed ParaHome + HumanML3D dataset for text-conditioned motion synthesis, and extends the TEMOS transformer by replacing the text encoder with an Object State Encoder that encodes target object rigid and articulated trajectories for object-guided full-body motion synthesis.

## Results

For text-conditioned motion synthesis on the mixed ParaHome+HumanML3D dataset, the MDM-based model achieves the best R-Precision (0.73), lowest multimodal distance (3.75), lowest FID (0.85), and highest multimodality (2.37), outperforming both TEMOS (FID 7.16) and T2M (FID 1.03). For object-guided body motion synthesis, incorporating relative person-object spatial cues (S_{p→o}) consistently reduces mean positional error across all four tested objects (refrigerator, drawer, sink, washing machine), e.g., reducing global root error from 7.79 cm to 5.60 cm for the refrigerator and from 11.29 cm to 9.03 cm for the washing machine. Hand alignment achieves an average position error of 11 mm across 86 finger touches, and object tracking maintains a reprojection error of ~1.02 pixels with 8.94 average camera visibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three generative model architectures (MDM, T2M, TEMOS) trained on the same mixed ParaHome+HumanML3D dataset. MDM outperforms TEMOS on all five metrics, with the largest gap in FID (0.85 vs. 7.16, an 8.4× improvement) and R-Precision (0.73 vs. 0.38). MDM also beats T2M on R-Precision (0.73 vs. 0.68), multimodal distance (3.75 vs. 5.27), FID (0.85 vs. 1.03), and multimodality (2.37 vs. 2.17), though T2M achieves higher diversity (17.80 vs. 15.06). For the object-guided motion synthesis task, the paper does not compare against external baselines but ablates the use of relative person-object spatial cues: including S_{p→o} consistently reduces global joint MPE by 20–30% across all four objects (e.g., 14.43→11.46 cm for washing machine), confirming that spatial context is critical for accurate motion synthesis. The system validation also shows the 3D ArUco cube marker achieves 100% tracking success versus 76–93% for surface-attached IR markers (4–40 markers), justifying the design choice over prior approaches like ARCTIC [20].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against a fragmented landscape of HOI datasets, each capturing only a subset of the full interaction signal: body-only motion capture (AMASS, Human3.6M), hand-object grasping in static postures (ContactPose, DexYCB), or simple single-object manipulation in constrained table setups (ARCTIC, OAKINK2). ParaHome departs from these by combining a 70-camera multi-view system with wearable IMU mocap (Xsens suit + Manus gloves) to simultaneously capture full-body motion, dexterous hand manipulation, and multi-object dynamics (including articulated objects) in a natural home environment. For the generative modeling demonstrations, the paper builds directly on MDM's diffusion framework for text-conditioned synthesis and extends TEMOS's transformer architecture by replacing the text encoder with an Object State Encoder for object-guided motion synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap: no prior resource captures sequential, multi-object, articulated HOI with full-body and finger-level detail in a contextual home setting, making it a foundational resource for training and evaluating generative HOI models. The articulated object parameterization (revolute/sliding joints with per-part state) and the 3D ArUco cube tracking solution address practical challenges that prior surface-marker approaches handled poorly. Key limitations include the reliance on physical markers (the authors plan a markerless evolution), a single room layout, and the generative modeling experiments remaining preliminary feasibility studies rather than state-of-the-art results. Open questions include whether the captured correlations generalize across rooms and object categories, and whether the diffusion/transformer baselines can be scaled to exploit the full richness of the data.
