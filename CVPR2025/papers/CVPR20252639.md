surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that a 7B model (VLsI-7B) outperforms GPT-4V by 17.4% on average across ten benchmarks, and a 2B model achieves competitive scores on several benchmarks (e.g., MathVista 68.4 vs. GPT-4V's 54.7), suggesting that layer-wise verbalized distillation captures reasoning structure more effectively than final-layer imitation. ## Problem

Scaling VLMs improves performance but creates prohibitive computational costs for deployment on mobile devices and robots, while existing efficiency strategies (adding specialized modules, modifying architectures, or merging encoders) introduce engineering complexity and still struggle with advanced visual reasoning. The paper asks whether a small VLM can match or exceed the performance of much larger models without any architectural changes, model merging, or parameter scaling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252639/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (processed by a ViT vision encoder with visually-adapted rotary positional embeddings) paired with a free-form text question prompt, and generates a text response via greedy decoding. The backbone is Qwen2-VL at 2B or 7B scale (28 LLM layers), with the vision projector being a two-layer MLP with GELU activations. During training, intermediate layer outputs are projected through lightweight verb-FFN modules (269M parameters total for the 7B model) into the shared language head's vocabulary space, enabling layer-wise verbal supervision without altering the inference-time architecture.

## Method

VLsI performs layer-wise knowledge distillation from a 72B teacher (Qwen2-VL-72B, 80 layers) to 2B/7B students (28 layers) through three stages. First, a verbalization step attaches a verb-FFN plus the shared language head to selected intermediate layers of both teacher and student, training them with autoregressive loss so each layer's features become interpretable as natural language. Second, an interaction step aligns the student's reasoning progression with the teacher's by dynamically matching layers via multinomial sampling over a softmax of negative KL divergences between verbal distributions, enforcing order preservation and using an adaptive temperature to balance exploration. Third, a standard SFT step fine-tunes the entire student VLM with LoRA (rank 64) on 2.9M visual instruction samples. The key insight is that natural language serves as a smoother, more interpretable distillation medium than raw feature or logit matching, allowing the small model to internalize the teacher's layer-by-layer reasoning trajectory rather than merely imitating its final output.

## Results

VLsI-7B achieves 75.8 on MM-Vet, 69.3 on MMMU, 74.7 on MathVista, and 87.3 on AI2D, outperforming GPT-4V (67.5/61.7/54.7/78.6) by 8.3/7.6/20.0/8.7 points respectively. VLsI-2B reaches 64.8 on MM-Vet and 51.4 on MMMU, surpassing GPT-4V on AI2D (89.0 vs. 78.6) and SEED-2-Plus (81.1 vs. 69.3) despite being ~30x smaller. On MM-Vet, VLsI-7B (75.8) also exceeds Qwen2-VL-72B (73.9) and GPT-4o (75.1), while on MMMU it leads all listed models including GPT-4o (69.9) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (65.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLsI-7B decisively outperforms all closed-source VLMs on MMMU (69.3 vs. GPT-4o 69.9, Claude-3.5-Sonnet 65.9, GPT-4V 61.7, Gemini-1.5-Pro 60.6) and MathVista (74.7 vs. GPT-4V 54.7, Claude-3.5-Sonnet 61.6, Gemini-1.5-Pro 57.7), while matching or exceeding GPT-4o on MM-Vet (75.8 vs. 75.1). Against the backbone Qwen2-VL-7B, VLsI-7B gains +13.8 on MM-Vet (75.8 vs. 62.0), +15.2 on MMMU (69.3 vs. 54.1), and +16.5 on MathVista (74.7 vs. 58.2). VLsI-7B also surpasses the much larger Qwen2-VL-72B on MM-Vet (75.8 vs. 73.9) and MMMU (69.3 vs. 64.3), and beats architecture-modified baselines Phantom-7B (70.8 MM-Vet, 51.2 MMMU) and TroL-7B (54.7 MM-Vet, 49.9 MMMU) by wide margins. In the 2B class, VLsI-2B outperforms Qwen2-VL-2B by +15.3 on MM-Vet (64.8 vs. 49.5) and +10.3 on MMMU (51.4 vs. 41.1), and exceeds Phantom-1.8B (54.1 MM-Vet, 40.6 MMMU) and TroL-1.8B (45.1 MM-Vet, 35.2 MMMU) substantially. The only benchmark where VLsI-7B trails a competitor is BLINK (59.7 vs. GPT-4o 64.7), and on LLaVA-Wilder it slightly trails GPT-4o (92.0 vs. 85.9) but leads all other models.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLsI departs from the dominant paradigm of final-layer knowledge distillation in VLMs (LLaVA-KD, LLaVA-MoD, Align-KD) and from architecture-modification approaches for efficient VLMs (TroL, Phantom) by introducing a layer-wise verbalization mechanism that projects intermediate features into natural language space before distillation. The verbalizer design draws inspiration from speculative decoding, where a smaller model with a shared language head emulates a larger one, but VLsI extends this to multiple intermediate layers with adaptive matching. The adaptive layer-matching strategy (multinomial sampling over KL-divergence-based distributions with order preservation) is novel relative to prior layer-wise distillation work like TED, which uses fixed layer correspondences and does not handle the layer-count gap between models of different sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLsI demonstrates that natural language can serve as an effective intermediary for transferring reasoning progression from large to small VLMs, achieving 17.4% average gains over GPT-4V with a 7B model without any architectural changes or parameter scaling—directly addressing the deployment bottleneck for mobile and robotic applications. The verbalizer also provides a practical interpretability tool, allowing practitioners to inspect how each layer's understanding evolves. Key limitations include the requirement that large and small models share the same tokenizer and token index ordering, and the method's dependence on a sufficiently capable large-backbone VLM (Qwen2-VL-72B) as the teacher, which constrains its applicability to model families with consistent tokenization.
