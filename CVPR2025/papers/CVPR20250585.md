## Problem

Existing LVLM hallucination mitigation methods focus on response generation (object existence, image description) but overlook the question itself. When a question implicitly presupposes the existence of a non-existent object (a Counterfactual Presupposition Question, or CPQ), even models with anti-hallucination training accept the false presupposition and produce severe hallucinatory responses. No prior benchmark or training method addresses this vulnerability in the question's presuppositional structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250585/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a free-form text question (which may contain counterfactual presuppositions about absent objects) and produces a factual text response. During training, ground-truth factual priors (e.g., 'there is no bridge in this image') are injected into the prompt to elicit self-corrected responses, which then serve as the preferred samples in DPO. At inference time, no such priors are provided; the model must independently discern whether the question's presupposition is valid.

## Method

Antidote is a synthetic data-driven post-training framework that reformulates hallucination mitigation as a DPO preference optimization problem. Its data synthesis pipeline generates images where statistically co-occurring objects are deliberately decoupled (e.g., a speedboat without bridges) using Stable Diffusion 3 with hallucination candidates as negative prompts, verified by Grounding-DINO, and constructs targeted QA pairs (CPQs, TPQs, object existence, image descriptions) around the absent objects. Factual priors derived during synthesis are injected into the model's prompt to produce self-corrected 'preferred' responses, while the original hallucinatory outputs serve as 'rejected' samples. The model is then post-trained via DPO with LoRA (rank 64) to learn a preference constraint that discriminates counterfactual presuppositions and suppresses object-perception hallucinations simultaneously, without requiring external expert models or human feedback.

## Results

On the proposed CP-Bench, Antidote achieves dramatic improvements: LLaVA-1.5-7B F1-score jumps from 5.7 to 78.4 (+72.7), LLaVA-1.5-13B from 17.3 to 83.5 (+66.2), and LLaVA-Next-Mistral-7B from 26.7 to 76.8 (+50.1), approaching closed-source models like Claude-3.5-Sonnet (86.0) and GPT-4o (85.5). On object existence (POPE), Antidote improves LLaVA-1.5-7B F1 from 86.07 to 87.89 (+1.82%) and 13B from 85.67 to 88.99 (+3.32%). On image description hallucination, CHAIR_s drops from 19.4 to 9.4 (7B) and 30.0 to 12.6 (13B), while SHR decreases from 36.7 to 18.1 (7B) and 37.2 to 21.3 (13B), representing 30–50% reductions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CP-Bench, Antidote overwhelmingly outperforms all other mitigation methods on the same base models: on LLaVA-1.5-7B, it achieves F1 78.4 vs. SeVa (24.1), VCD (6.1), and HA-DPO (4.7); on LLaVA-1.5-13B, F1 83.5 vs. the unmitigated baseline (17.3). On POPE (object existence), Antidote (87.89 F1 for 7B, 88.99 for 13B) surpasses VDD (85.13), HACL (86.20), Volcano (86.67/87.17), HA-DPO (86.87), and SeVa (86.66). On CHAIR_s (image description), Antidote (9.4 for 7B, 12.6 for 13B) dramatically beats SID (15.1), OPERA (15.6), HA-DPO (18.0), VCD (17.9), and SeVa (18.4); similarly on SHR, Antidote (18.1/21.3) halves the hallucination rate compared to all baselines (33.1–34.9). On general capability benchmarks (Science-QA, MMBench, MMVet), Antidote maintains or slightly improves performance (e.g., +2.8% on Science-QA, +1.1% on MMBench for 7B), confirming no catastrophic forgetting, with only a minor drop on LLaVA-Wild (65.4→64.0) attributed to increased caution on uncertain questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) paradigm for post-training LVLMs, but departs from prior hallucination-mitigation work (HA-DPO, SeVa, VCD, HACL) in two key ways: it introduces a new hallucination dimension—counterfactual presupposition questions (CPQs) where the question itself contains a false premise—and it eliminates the need for external expert models (e.g., GPT-4V) by using a synthetic data pipeline (Stable Diffusion 3 + Grounding-DINO + DeepSeek-V2) to generate factual priors that enable self-correction. The preference pairs are constructed by prompting the baseline model with ground-truth facts from the synthesis process, reformulating hallucination mitigation as a self-supervised contrastive learning problem rather than relying on external supervision or purely dis-preferred data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CPQ framing exposes a practical vulnerability: models that correctly answer 'Is there a car?' still hallucinate when asked 'What brand is the car?', a gap that matters for real-world VQA where question validity cannot be guaranteed. The finding that even InternVL-2 and Qwen2-VL—models that surpass GPT-4o on general benchmarks—score below 65 F1 on CP-Bench suggests that large-scale instruction tuning may inadvertently reinforce presupposition-following biases. Limitations include evaluation only on the LLaVA family, a relatively small 1,000-sample test set, and the observation that post-trained models become overly 'cautious' on uncertain questions (hurting LLaVA-Wild scores). Open questions include whether the synthetic data pipeline generalizes to video or multi-image settings, and whether the self-correction signal degrades when factual priors are noisy.
