## Problem

While systematic generalization (SG) in deep neural networks has been studied through novel architectures, loss functions, and training procedures, the role of training data distributional properties as inductive biases for SG remains largely unexplored, particularly in the multimodal setting. The paper asks whether SG can be induced solely by changing properties of the training data, and if so, how and why this works at the level of learned representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250555/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a 224×224 RGB image containing 3–10 CLEVR-style objects alongside a text query that partially describes the scene with one or more attribute values masked (e.g., shape, color, material, size). The image is split into patches and projected to embeddings via a linear layer, while the text passes through a token embedding layer; both sequences are concatenated with learned positional and modality embeddings before entering a shared Transformer encoder, which outputs predictions for the masked attribute tokens.

## Method

The paper conducts an empirical study of three training-data distributional properties—diversity (increasing the number of color values from 8 to 216), burstiness (probabilistically restricting each image to at most 3 colors), and latent intervention (randomly altering hue via ColorJitter)—and measures their effect on OOD generalization in a multimodal masked language modeling task. A Transformer encoder is trained under each condition, and SG is evaluated by withholding shape–color combinations from training (Split B) while training on a restricted subset (Split A). The key finding is that diversity alone yields an 89% absolute accuracy gain on the OOD shape task, and that Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) between latent attributes in the training set strongly predicts OOD performance. To explain the mechanism, the authors show that lower NMI induces greater parallelism in neural representations (measured via a p-score based on cosine similarity of attribute-change vectors), linking data distribution to the geometry of learned features and the capacity for analogical reasoning.

## Results

On the CLEVR-style multimodal masked language modeling task, increasing color diversity from 8 to 216 in the training set yields an 89% absolute increase in OOD accuracy on the shape task, the paper's headline result. Burstiness (limiting colors per image to 3) adds up to 14.8% OOD shape accuracy at the cost of up to 14.3% OOD color accuracy, while latent intervention (random hue perturbation) adds up to 15% OOD shape accuracy. NMI between color and shape in the training set correlates with OOD shape accuracy at Pearson r = −0.79, and the p-score (representational parallelism) correlates at r = 0.73, identifying a geometric mechanism linking low-NMI data to better systematic generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external SG methods; instead it benchmarks a single Transformer encoder under different training-data distributions. The 8-color baseline model achieves near-random OOD shape accuracy (well below the 33% chance level for three shapes), confirming the known SG failure of DNNs. Increasing training data size (up to 100% of the 75k-sample set) does not improve OOD performance and slightly degrades it, ruling out a simple data-scaling explanation. Among the three data manipulations, diversity (8→216 colors) dominates with an 89% absolute OOD shape gain, while burstiness and latent intervention each contribute roughly 15% and are complementary to diversity. A capacity ablation (reducing hidden dim to 32/64 or expanding to 512/1024) shows that neither under- nor over-parameterization explains the SG gains, isolating the data-distribution mechanism. The p-score analysis further shows that for datasets with similar NMI, higher diversity still yields better OOD performance, indicating that diversity acts through a channel beyond NMI alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of the systematic generalization (SG) literature (CLEVR, gSCAN, COGS) and the emerging line of work on data-driven inductive biases. It adapts the burstiness concept from Chan et al. (NeurIPS 2022), who studied its role in in-context learning, and repurposes it for SG in a multimodal masked language modeling setting. While prior SG work has focused on architectural changes, loss modifications, or data complexity/scale, this paper isolates specific distributional properties (diversity, burstiness, latent intervention) as the operative inductive bias. It also connects to the linear representation hypothesis and the NMI-based analysis of CLIP models by Abbasi et al., extending the NMI–SG link with a representational geometry mechanism (parallelism/p-score) not previously reported.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that SG can be substantially improved through training data manipulation alone, without architectural or loss-function changes, which is a practically accessible lever for practitioners. The identification of parallelism in representations as a mechanism through which lower NMI promotes SG offers a testable, neuroscience-inspired hypothesis for future work. However, all experiments are conducted on a synthetic CLEVR-like dataset with a single Transformer architecture and four discrete attributes, leaving open whether these findings transfer to natural images, continuous attributes, or larger-scale multimodal models. The correlational (not causal) evidence for the p-score mechanism, and the limited attribute space, are notable limitations.
