## Problem

T5-XXL (11B parameters) has become the de facto text encoder for state-of-the-art diffusion models like FLUX and SD3, but it was trained on the C4 general NLP corpus containing substantial non-visual data that diffusion models never exploit. This raises the question of whether T5-XXL is overparameterized for text-to-image synthesis and whether a much smaller encoder can retain its visual embedding capabilities without degrading image quality, semantic understanding, or text-rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251929/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only text prompts (no paired image data) during training, using a step-following scheme that iteratively denoises from pure Gaussian noise through 20 timesteps. The output is a family of distilled T5 encoders (T5-Small 60M, T5-Base 220M, T5-Large 770M, T5-XL 3B) that are drop-in replacements for T5-XXL in existing diffusion pipelines. The smallest model, T5-Base, reduces the FLUX pipeline memory footprint from ~34 GB to ~23 GB, enabling consumer-grade GPU deployment without CPU offloading.

## Method

The core contribution is a vision-based knowledge distillation loss that replaces naive embedding-space matching (which causes mode collapse in the student) with a diffusion-model-guided objective: at each denoising timestep, the same noisy latent is passed through the frozen diffusion model with both teacher and student embeddings, and the student is trained to minimize the L2 distance between the two denoising predictions (Eq. 5). A step-following training algorithm (Algorithm 1) starts from pure noise and iteratively denoises, computing the distillation loss at every timestep so the student learns across the full noise schedule without requiring paired image data. An MLP projects the student's lower-dimensional embeddings into the teacher's embedding space before feeding them to the diffusion model. Training data is constructed from three complementary prompt sources—LAION-Aesthetics-6.5+ (100K prompts for visual quality), T2I-CompBench (4,200 prompts for semantic composition), and a newly created CommonText dataset (50K prompts for text-rendering)—and the student is trained in three sequential stages targeting each capability before a combined fine-tuning phase.

## Results

On MSCOCO-2014, the distilled T5-Base (220M params, 50× smaller than T5-XXL) achieves FID 24.32 and CLIP score 29.79, compared to T5-XXL's FID 22.36 and CLIP 31.30. T5-XL (3B, 4× smaller) narrows the gap further to FID 23.17 and CLIP 30.33, retaining ~97% of the teacher's performance. On text rendering (CommonText), T5-Base achieves 69.3% character-level, 42.4% word-level, and 35.6% sentence-level OCR accuracy versus T5-XXL's 76.7/59.3/51.2, while T5-Small (60M) drops sharply to 31.9/12.0/9.3, revealing a size threshold below which text-rendering collapses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD3 (FID 19.83, CLIP 32.21), the distilled Flux models trail on image quality — T5-Base's FID of 24.32 is ~4.5 points worse — but T5-Base substantially outperforms SD3 on text rendering (69.3% vs 38.7% character-level, 35.6% vs 14.0% sentence-level OCR accuracy). Against Pixart-Alpha, the distilled T5-Base wins on FID (24.32 vs 28.96) but is slightly behind on CLIP score (29.79 vs 30.61); on text rendering the gap is dramatic, with T5-Base scoring 69.3% character-level versus Pixart-Alpha's 1.7%. The most important internal comparison is T5-Base vs. its T5-XXL teacher: T5-Base retains ~93% of the teacher's FID and ~95% of its CLIP score while being 50× smaller, and T5-XL retains ~97% of both metrics at 4× smaller. On T2I-CompBench semantic understanding, all distilled T5 variants (Small through XL) cluster within 1–3 points of T5-XXL across spatial relationships and numeracy, with attribute binding showing the largest gap (e.g., color: 73.41 for T5-Base vs 79.11 for T5-XXL), which the authors attribute to limited training data for that category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge distillation (Hinton et al.) to the specific problem of compressing text encoders in text-to-image diffusion models, departing from the standard practice of matching final-layer embeddings by instead using the diffusion model's denoising predictions as the distillation signal. It builds on the T5 family (Raffel et al.) as the student architecture and on FLUX (Black Forest Labs) as the frozen diffusion backbone, introducing a step-following training scheme that eliminates the need for paired image data. Conceptually, it draws an analogy to NLP findings that LLMs are overparameterized for narrow downstream tasks, hypothesizing that T5-XXL's C4-trained representational capacity is largely redundant for visual prompt encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: replacing T5-XXL (11 GB) with T5-Base (285 MB) reduces the FLUX pipeline from ~34 GB to ~23 GB, making state-of-the-art T2I generation feasible on consumer-grade GPUs like the RTX 4090 without CPU offload. The finding that text-rendering is the most size-sensitive capability (collapsing below ~220M parameters) provides a useful design guideline for future encoder choices. Open questions include whether the vision-based distillation generalizes to non-T5 encoders or non-FLUX backbones, whether the step-following scheme can be shortened to reduce training cost, and whether the CommonText dataset adequately covers the full range of text-rendering scenarios (e.g., multilingual, stylized fonts).
