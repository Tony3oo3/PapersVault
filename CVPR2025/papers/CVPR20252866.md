## Problem

Weakly-supervised Temporal Action Localization (WTAL) methods rely on Multiple Instance Learning for localization-by-classification, creating a persistent framework and performance gap compared to fully-supervised regression-based TAL methods. While recent two-stage approaches generate pseudo labels to train regression models, they fail to fully leverage the diverse priors from the weak branch, produce pseudo labels of limited quality, and lack effective strategies for training with inherently noisy pseudo labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252866/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted RGB and optical flow snippet-level features (1024-dim, from Kinetics-pretrained I3D) at one snippet per 16 frames, along with only video-level class labels (no snippet-level annotations). The weak branch produces snippet-level predictions and proposal sets, which are refined into pseudo proposals and an uncertainty mask. The full branch outputs final action proposals with start, end, class, and confidence scores, plus video-level classification scores used to filter proposals at inference.

## Method

PseudoFormer is a two-branch framework where a MIL-based weak branch generates priors (snippet-level predictions and proposals) that train a regression-based full branch (ActionFormer/TriDet-style with FPN). The key novelty is RickerFusion, which maps each proposal to a Ricker wavelet distribution in a shared global space, weighted by OIC confidence scores, to fuse multi-scale predictions into higher-quality pseudo labels with more reliable boundaries. An uncertainty mask excludes boundary regions of proposals from training, with the excluded regions linearly shrinking over epochs to progressively incorporate noisier boundary information. The full branch is trained with a combined loss (focal classification, IoU-based regression, and snippet-level attention loss) in a teacher-student manner with EMA, and pseudo proposals plus the uncertainty mask are iteratively refined after warm-up using the regression model's own outputs re-fused through RickerFusion.

## Results

On THUMOS14, PseudoFormer achieves 51.9% average mAP (0.1:0.7), outperforming the previous SOTA weakly-supervised method PivoTAL (49.6%) by 2.3% and the base model DELU (46.4%) by 5.5%. On ActivityNet1.3, PseudoFormer reaches 29.0% average mAP (0.5:0.95), surpassing PivoTAL (28.1%) by 0.9%. Notably, PseudoFormer's THUMOS14 results (51.9%) exceed early fully-supervised methods like A2Net (47.1%) and approach AFormer (66.8%), narrowing the gap between weakly- and fully-supervised TAL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PseudoFormer outperforms all compared weakly-supervised baselines on both benchmarks. On THUMOS14, it beats the previous SOTA PivoTAL (also a two-stage regression-based method) by 2.3% in AVG(0.1:0.7) mAP (51.9% vs. 49.6%) and by 2.7% at IoU=0.5 (44.1% vs. 42.8%). Against the base model DELU, the improvement is 5.5% (51.9% vs. 46.4%), and against CO2-Net it is 7.3% (51.9% vs. 44.6%). On ActivityNet1.3, PseudoFormer leads PivoTAL by 0.9% in AVG(0.5:0.95) (29.0% vs. 28.1%) and by 1.8% at IoU=0.5 (46.9% vs. 45.1%). Compared to fully-supervised methods, PseudoFormer still trails TriDet by 22.3% on THUMOS14 and 7.8% on ActivityNet1.3, but it surpasses the earlier fully-supervised A2Net (47.1%) on THUMOS14, demonstrating a meaningful narrowing of the weak-to-full supervision gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

PseudoFormer sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: MIL-based weakly-supervised TAL (UntrimmedNets, DELU, DDG-Net) and regression-based fully-supervised TAL (ActionFormer, TriDet). It follows the two-stage pseudo-label paradigm introduced by PivoTAL and Zhou et al., but departs from them in three ways: (1) replacing Gaussian-based proposal grouping with Ricker wavelet fusion in a shared global space (inspired by BEV), (2) jointly training on both snippet-level and proposal-level priors rather than proposals alone, and (3) introducing an uncertainty mask with linear degradation to handle noisy boundaries. The OIC scoring from AutoLoc and the teacher-student EMA training from TriDet are adopted as building blocks rather than novel contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the quality of pseudo labels and the training strategy around them matter more than the specific MIL base model (CO2-Net + PseudoFormer reaches 51.2% vs DELU + PseudoFormer at 51.9%), suggesting a reusable pipeline that can upgrade any existing WTAL method. The uncertainty mask with progressive boundary inclusion is a practical, low-cost technique for noisy pseudo-label training that could transfer to other weakly-supervised settings. However, the approach remains a two-stage pipeline requiring a pre-trained base model, and evaluation is limited to two benchmarks; whether the RickerFusion strategy generalizes to higher-IoU thresholds on ActivityNet (where gains are smaller) or to other supervision levels (point, semi-supervised) remains open.
