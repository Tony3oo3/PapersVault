## Problem

Traditional point-feature detection and tracking requires transferring full images from sensor to external processor every frame, creating a severe bandwidth and latency bottleneck that limits performance to tens of FPS even on modern hardware. No prior work has performed both feature detection and tracking entirely on-sensor for Pixel Processor Array (PPA) architectures, where thousands of pixel-processors can compute in parallel at the focal plane. This paper addresses that gap by eliminating the image-transfer bottleneck entirely, reducing sensor output to sparse feature coordinates and descriptors only.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image frames captured directly into the SCAMP-7's 256×256 array of pixel-processors, where each PE stores one pixel as an analogue value in local memory. Output consists solely of tracked feature coordinates and 8-bit descriptors of newly initialized features, read out via an address-event interface at an average of 2 bytes per feature per frame — a >1000× reduction over raw image output. No raw image data ever leaves the sensor; any image shown in the paper is output purely for visualization and is not required for the algorithm to operate.

## Method

The core contribution is the Descriptor-In-Pixel paradigm: each of the 65,536 pixel-processors stores a complete 8-bit binary descriptor (computed via pixel-pair comparisons similar to BRIEF/BRISK) in its local digital registers. Every frame, a descriptor layout is generated in parallel — tracked features' descriptors are spread into 9×9 PE patches around their last-known locations, while all remaining PEs are loaded with a randomized search descriptor. The PPA then computes, in a single SIMD pass, the weighted descriptor response of every PE against the captured image (using analogue pixel-pair magnitudes rather than pure Hamming distance to reject low-texture regions), producing a 'patchwork response map.' Blob detection and patch-wise non-maximum suppression on this map simultaneously update tracked feature locations (tracking) and identify new feature candidates in search-descriptor regions (detection), all without any external processing. This achieves over 3000 FPS on the SCAMP-7 prototype with ~1 W power consumption.

## Results

The proposed Descriptor-In-Pixel approach achieves over 3000 FPS on the SCAMP-7 PPA prototype, with a total per-frame computation time of 321 μs (dominated by the 192 μs descriptor response map computation). Sensor output is reduced to just 2 bytes per feature per frame, yielding a greater than 1000× data reduction compared to raw image output. Under a 'Translate' sensor motion at 1000 FPS, 90% of features tracked by the FAST-based baseline are lost within 5 seconds and none survive beyond 15 seconds, whereas approximately 20% of features tracked by the proposed method persist for over 15 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its approach against a FAST keypoint detector with nearest-neighbour tracking (a modified implementation of Chen et al. [8]), both running at 1000 FPS on SCAMP-7 viewing the same scenes. The comparison is conducted via feature lifetime histograms across multiple motion sequences (translate, rotate, etc.). Under the 'Translate' motion, 90% of FAST-tracked features are lost within 5 seconds and 0% are tracked for over 15 seconds, compared to roughly 20% of the proposed method's features surviving beyond 15 seconds. The proposed method consistently produces significantly longer feature lifespans across all tested motion sequences, demonstrating far greater tracking reliability. The authors note that the FAST baseline also suffers from poor temporal stability because it uses no information from previous frames to inform detection, causing detected points to flicker between frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BRIEF-style binary pixel-pair descriptor but radically recontextualizes it: instead of computing descriptors externally for a sparse set of keypoints, each pixel-processor stores a full (8-bit) descriptor in local memory and the entire array computes descriptor responses in parallel via SIMD. It departs from prior PPA work (FAST corner detection at 2300 FPS, BITVO at 300 FPS) by eliminating all dense image output, performing both detection and tracking entirely on-sensor. Conceptually it also contrasts with event-camera feature tracking, which achieves high temporal resolution through asynchronous sparse events but at the cost of complex off-sensor processing, whereas the PPA retains frame-based computation while matching comparable temporal resolution through sheer in-pixel parallelism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Descriptor-In-Pixel paradigm demonstrates that a 1-watt, 256×256 research-prototype sensor can track features at 3000 FPS with >1000× data reduction, suggesting a path toward vision sensors that compute rather than merely capture. The key limitation is the 8-bit descriptor constraint imposed by SCAMP-7's 23 digital registers per PE, which precludes rotation and scale invariance; the authors argue this is a hardware constraint, not a fundamental one, and expect future PPAs with more per-PE memory to support richer descriptors. Open questions include whether the approach integrates cleanly into full SLAM pipelines, how it scales to larger sensor arrays, and whether the weighted pixel-pair response (vs. pure Hamming distance) generalizes to more complex descriptor families.
