## Problem

Existing micro-expression recognition (MER) methods rely heavily on apex frame annotations to compute inter-frame optical flow, making them impractical when apex labels are unavailable or lost during video resampling. Additionally, optical flow-based discrepancy estimation is sensitive to head-movement noise and struggles with extreme micro-expressions, while the scarcity of labeled ME samples limits supervised training. The paper tackles all three issues simultaneously by eliminating apex dependency, improving noise-robust local deformation perception, and leveraging self-supervised pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252389/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a variable-length sequence of facial frames from a micro-expression video (e.g., 190×230 at 100 fps for SMIC-HS) and requires only onset and offset frames as input—no apex annotation is needed. It outputs a three- or five-class emotion label, a detected apex frame A_det identified via contrastive triplet loss, and per-keypoint local deformation fields (spatial positions and 2×2 Jacobian matrices at K=10 keypoints). The SODE backbone is first pre-trained on the non-ME VoxCeleb dataset before fine-tuning on CASME II, SAMM, or SMIC-HS.

## Method

SODA4MER builds on a Self-supervised Oriented Deformation Estimator (SODE) that, following the first-order motion model, predicts per-keypoint displacements and local Jacobians relative to an abstract latent reference frame, enabling batch-based motion estimation without direct inter-frame supervision. A Gated Temporal Variance Gaussian (GTVG) prior adaptively weights spatial regions by combining temporal variance across frames with learnable Gaussian distributions centered on facial landmarks, filtering head-movement noise while amplifying ME-relevant muscle deformations. Apex detection is achieved through a contrastive triplet loss where the onset frame serves as anchor, the offset as positive, and all intermediate frames as negatives; the frame maximizing the combined position- and Jacobian-based triplet loss is selected as A_det. Guided by Dynamic Stereotype Theory, the Dual-Phase Temporal Modeling (DPTM) strategy splits the sequence at A_det into activation and decay phases, each encoded by a separate Bi-LSTM over spatial keypoint and local deformation features, with the concatenated representations fed to an MLP classifier trained with focal loss to handle class imbalance.

## Results

On the three-category classification task, SODA4MER achieves UF1=0.8870 and UAR=0.8809 on CASME II, and UF1=0.8855 and UAR=0.8881 on SMIC-HS, yielding average UF1=0.8863 and UAR=0.8845. On SMIC-HS, SODA4MER attains the best UF1 and UAR among all compared methods (both apex-free and apex-required), outperforming the second-best by 1.4% in UF1 and 2.4% in UAR. On the five-class task (Table 3), SODA4MER achieves UF1=0.8141/ACC=0.8418 on CASME II and UF1=0.7893/ACC=0.8030 on SAMM, ranking among the top methods without requiring apex annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among apex-free methods, SODA4MER substantially outperforms LBP-TOP (UF1 0.8870 vs. 0.7026 on CASME II) and GLEFFN (UF1 0.8855 vs. 0.7714 on SMIC-HS), confirming that learned local deformation estimation far surpasses handcrafted or global-flow features. Against the other apex-free method MERASTC, SODA4MER loses on CASME II (UF1 0.8870 vs. 0.9330) but wins decisively on SMIC-HS (UF1 0.8855 vs. 0.7900, UAR 0.8881 vs. 0.8620), which the authors attribute to MERASTC's sensitivity to lower-resolution inputs. Compared to apex-required SOTA methods, SODA4MER trails Shallow I3D (UF1 0.9370) and FRL-DGT (UF1 0.9190) on CASME II by 5.0% and 3.2% respectively, but surpasses all of them on SMIC-HS (UF1 0.8855 vs. 0.7739 for Shallow I3D, 0.7430 for FRL-DGT). On the five-class task, SODA4MER (UF1 0.8141 on CASME II, 0.7893 on SAMM) outperforms apex-free baselines CMNet (0.7399/0.7719) and AU GCN (0.7760/0.7568) but remains below apex-required MMNet (0.8676/0.7291) on CASME II, suggesting a residual gap when apex labels are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

SODA4MER builds directly on the First Order Motion Model (FOMM) [29] for keypoint-based local deformation estimation, repurposing its Jacobian-based motion representation from image animation to micro-expression analysis. It departs from the dominant optical-flow-between-onset-and-apex paradigm used by most prior MER methods (OFF-ApexNet, STSTNet, STRCN-G, etc.) by estimating oriented deformation relative to an abstract latent reference frame, thereby eliminating the need for apex annotations. The introduction of muscle-group priors via the Gated Temporal Variance Gaussian (GTVG) and the Dynamic Stereotype Theory (DST)-inspired dual-phase temporal modeling represent conceptual departures from both discrepancy-based and fixed-region prior-knowledge strategies that have dominated the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The apex-free formulation addresses a genuine practical barrier: in real-world deployment, apex annotations are often unavailable or lost during video resampling, making most SOTA MER methods inapplicable. The DST-based dual-phase modeling offers an interpretable, theory-grounded way to capture the asymmetric temporal dynamics of micro-expressions (rapid activation vs. gradual decay), which could inform future work on other subtle motion recognition tasks. However, the model's 126M parameter count, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and the observed keypoint distribution mismatch between pre-training and fine-tuning phases limit immediate practical adoption. Open questions include whether the oriented deformation framework generalizes beyond facial micro-expressions and whether the DST phasic decomposition can be formalized more rigorously beyond the heuristic activation/decay split.
