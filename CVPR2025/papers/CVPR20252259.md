## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) are vulnerable to dual exploits—gradient-based adversarial attacks that corrupt visual understanding and optimization-based jailbreak attacks that bypass safety alignment—yet existing robustness methods either sacrifice fine-grained semantic detail under perturbation or impose heavy computational overhead (large batch sizes, momentum encoders). No prior approach simultaneously defends against both attack types while preserving the nuanced, detail-rich captions that LVLMs are expected to produce.

## Input / Output

SLADE fine-tunes the CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder on unlabeled ImageNet images (224×224 RGB), generating adversarial counterparts via 10-step PGD at ℓ∞ radii of 2/255 or 4/255. At inference, the fine-tuned encoder is dropped into an existing LVLM (LLaVA-7B/13B or OpenFlamingo-9B) as a plug-and-play replacement for the default CLIP encoder, with the language model and projection layer kept frozen; the LVLM then consumes the robust visual embedding alongside a text prompt to produce a caption or VQA answer.

## Method

SLADE introduces a dual-level symmetric contrastive objective applied during unsupervised adversarial fine-tuning: a patch-level loss aligns embeddings of corresponding 32×32 patches between clean and PGD-perturbed images to preserve fine-grained spatial detail, while a global-level loss aligns full-image embeddings to maintain high-level semantic coherence. A stop-gradient mechanism (inspired by SimSiam) is applied asymmetrically within each contrastive term, eliminating the need for large negative-sample batches or a momentum encoder while preventing feature collapse. The two losses are combined with a weighting parameter λ=0.6, and the entire scheme is trained for two epochs with AdamW (LR=1e-5, batch size 64) on ImageNet without labels. Because only the vision encoder is updated, the resulting SLADE model can be swapped into any CLIP-based LVLM without retraining the language backbone.

## Results

On LLaVA-7B with untargeted ℓ∞ attacks, SLADE⁴ achieves a CIDEr score of 45.9 on COCO at ε=8/255, compared to 2.4 for original CLIP, 35.3 for TeCoA⁴, and 29.5 for FARE⁴. On OKVQA at ε=4/255, SLADE⁴ reaches 23.4% VQA accuracy versus 0.0% for CLIP, 12.3% for TeCoA⁴, and 15.3% for FARE⁴. Under targeted attacks at ε=4/255, SLADE⁴ achieves 0% attack success rate with a CIDEr score of 87.5, outperforming FARE⁴ (78.7) and TeCoA⁴ (67.3). Against VisualAdv jailbreak attacks at ε=128/255, SLADE⁴ reduces average toxicity from 30.4% (CLIP) to 13.7%, the lowest among all tested defenses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original CLIP encoder, SLADE⁴ preserves or exceeds clean performance (123.9 vs. 121.9 CIDEr on COCO for LLaVA-7B) while providing massive robustness gains under attack (45.9 vs. 2.4 CIDEr at ε=8/255). Compared to TeCoA⁴, SLADE⁴ consistently outperforms across all downstream tasks: 45.9 vs. 35.3 CIDEr on COCO at ε=8/255, 39.4% vs. 32.3% on OKVQA at ε=4/255, and 87.5 vs. 67.3 CIDEr under targeted attacks. Against FARE⁴, SLADE⁴ also leads on every metric: 45.9 vs. 29.5 CIDEr on COCO at ε=8/255, 23.4% vs. 15.3% on OKVQA at ε=4/255, and 13.7% vs. 15.2% average toxicity under VisualAdv jailbreak attacks. Against the external defense JailGuard, SLADE⁴ achieves a lower ImgJP attack success rate (15.0% vs. 21.0% on LLaVA-13B) without requiring LVLM-specific training or additional inference-time overhead. On non-adversarial tasks, SLADE⁴ maintains near-identical instruction-following (BERTScore F1: 0.889 vs. 0.892 for CLIP) and competitive reasoning accuracy (62.3% vs. 64.5% on SQA), confirming no significant degradation of core LVLM capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLADE extends the line of adversarial fine-tuning for CLIP-based vision encoders by introducing a dual-level (patch + global) symmetric contrastive objective, departing from prior methods that rely on supervised cross-modal contrastive loss (TeCoA) or whole-image ℓ₂ loss (FARE). The patch-level component draws on pretext-task-inspired spatial localization ideas, while the stop-gradient mechanism borrowed from SimSiam eliminates the need for large batch sizes or momentum encoders that SimCLR- and BYOL-based adversarial training (e.g., Gowal et al., 2020) typically require. Conceptually, SLADE positions itself as a plug-and-play robustness layer for the vision encoder, decoupled from the language model and downstream task, in contrast to LVLM-specific jailbreak defenses like JailGuard that require per-model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single fine-tuned CLIP encoder can be swapped into multiple LVLMs (LLaVA-7B, LLaVA-13B, OpenFlamingo-9B) to simultaneously resist gradient-based targeted attacks, untargeted perturbations, and universal jailbreak attacks without degrading instruction-following or reasoning, SLADE offers a practical, low-overhead defense strategy for deploying LVLMs in safety-critical settings. An open question is whether the patch-level contrastive objective generalizes to non-CLIP vision encoders (e.g., DINO, BEiT) or to video and audio modalities, and whether the fixed patch size of 32 is optimal across resolutions. The approach also raises the question of whether the stop-gradient trick can be extended to other self-supervised adversarial training regimes beyond the CLIP architecture.
