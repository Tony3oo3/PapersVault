## Problem

Existing 3D adversarial attacks target simple object classification models under white-box or soft-label black-box settings, but real-world 3D applications (autonomous driving, robotic navigation) operate on complex multi-object scenes and expose only hard-label predictions to users. This paper addresses the unexplored challenge of crafting stealthy, universal adversarial perturbations for 3D scene grounding models when the attacker has access solely to the model's predicted bounding boxes, with no gradient, confidence, or architectural information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251321/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set (~200) of 3D scene point clouds (each represented as N×(3+F) with coordinates and features like color/normal/height) paired with natural-language grounding queries, and iteratively queries a black-box 3D grounding model for predicted bounding boxes. It produces a single universal adversarial object trigger—a perturbed 3D point cloud initialized from a real-world ScanObjectNN object, scaled to λ=0.2 and resampled to match the scene's local point density—that can be injected into any unseen scene to cause the model to localize the wrong object (the trigger) instead of the text-referenced target.

## Method

UAO² (Universal Adversarial Object Optimization) operates in two stages. First, it initializes a natural object trigger by selecting a real-world object from ScanObjectNN, resampling it via MAD-based density matching to blend into the scene, and placing it on the nearest detected plane (via 3D Hough transform) around the ground-truth target to preserve geometric plausibility. Second, it optimizes the trigger through a weighted Monte Carlo gradient estimation: at each of 50 iterations, 100 Gaussian-perturbed copies of the trigger are queried, and an indicator function (IoU > 0.6 against the target bbox) assigns each noise direction an exponential weight if it causes mislocalization or a logarithmic weight if it does not, preventing opposing directions from canceling out. The trigger is then updated along the weighted mean gradient direction, yielding a single universal object that degrades grounding accuracy by 60–75% across seven models on three datasets while remaining imperceptible (Chamfer distance ~0.0003).

## Results

On ScanRefer, the UAO² attack reduces Acc@0.25 (Unique) from 67.64% to 15.27% for ScanRefer, from 81.63% to 19.29% for 3D-SPS, and from 85.76% to 20.61% for EDA, representing a 70–82% relative drop. On Nr3D and Sr3D, the attack similarly degrades performance, e.g., ViL3DRel drops from 64.4% to 21.3% (Nr3D Overall) and from 72.8% to 24.8% (Sr3D Overall). The attack requires only 100 Monte Carlo queries per step over 50 iterations, with perturbation norms as low as 0.0002–0.0009 (Chamfer distance), confirming imperceptibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares computational efficiency against three existing hard-label/decision-based attack methods: HopSkipJumpAttack [7], Graphite [24], and QEBa [40]. UAO² requires 9.4 h training and 0.4 h test time with 28.3 GB memory, which is slightly slower in training than HopSkipJumpAttack (6.8 h, 24.6 GB) and QEBa (7.3 h, 23.8 GB), but comparable to Graphite (8.9 h, 31.5 GB). Test-time cost (0.4 h) matches Graphite exactly and is only marginally higher than HopSkipJumpAttack (0.2 h) and QEBa (0.3 h). The key distinction is that UAO² operates in a fundamentally different and more challenging setting—hard-label 3D scene grounding with universal object triggers—whereas the three baselines target simpler 2D or single-object 3D classification tasks, so direct performance comparison is not applicable. The paper positions its method as competitive in efficiency while addressing a substantially harder problem (scene-level, multi-object, hard-label).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D point cloud adversarial attack literature, which has focused almost exclusively on perturbing single objects for classification tasks, to the more complex and practical setting of scene-level grounding. Methodologically, it builds on decision-based (hard-label) attack frameworks such as HopSkipJumpAttack [7], Graphite [24], and QEBa [40], which rely on Monte Carlo gradient estimation, but introduces a weighted variant that adaptively reweights sampled noise directions based on whether they cause the target mislocalization. The use of Hough transform for natural object placement and MAD-based density matching for trigger initialization are borrowed from 3D geometry processing and backdoor attack literature respectively, repurposed here for stealthy scene-level trigger injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first study to demonstrate that 3D scene grounding models—a task central to robotic navigation and autonomous driving—are vulnerable to universal, hard-label adversarial attacks via small, realistic object triggers, raising concrete safety concerns for deployed 3D perception systems. The finding that a single optimized object (with Chamfer distance perturbations as small as ~0.0003) can degrade seven architecturally diverse models by 60–75% on three datasets underscores a systematic fragility. Open questions include whether the attack generalizes to other scene-level tasks (detection, segmentation, captioning), whether the 200-sample optimization budget is truly minimal, and whether more principled defenses (beyond sampling, fine-tuning, and anomaly detection) can close the gap without sacrificing clean accuracy.
