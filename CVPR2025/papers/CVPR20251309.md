## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for diffusion models were designed for UNet architectures and rely on reconstruction-based calibration that does not scale to large models. When applied to Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), these methods suffer from biased quantization due to two unaddressed characteristics: significant spatial variance in weights and activations across input channels, and substantial distribution shifts in activations across denoising timesteps and individual samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251309/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Q-DiT takes a pretrained DiT model (DiT-XL/2 for images at 256×256 or 512×512, STDiT3 for video) and a small calibration set, producing a quantized model with reduced bit-widths (W6A8 or W4A8). At inference, the quantized model consumes the same inputs as the full-precision model—class label plus Gaussian noise for ImageNet generation, or text prompt plus noise for video generation—and produces images or 2-second videos via standard samplers (DDIM with 50/100 steps, or rectified flow with 30 steps). The quantization parameters for activations are computed on-the-fly per sample per timestep rather than being stored, keeping the model size close to the weight-only compressed size.

## Method

Q-DiT combines two techniques to address the identified DiT quantization challenges. First, it applies fine-grained group quantization (using GPTQ for weights) where weight and activation matrices are partitioned into groups sharing a common scale, but critically employs an evolutionary search algorithm to allocate different group sizes per layer, guided directly by FID/FVD of generated samples rather than layer-wise MSE sensitivity metrics, subject to a BitOps budget constraint. Second, it introduces sample-wise dynamic activation quantization that computes min-max-based scaling factors and zero points on-the-fly for each group of activations at every timestep and for each individual sample during inference, rather than pre-calibrating per-timestep parameters; this is fused into the preceding operator to make the overhead negligible. The evolutionary search operates over a group-size space of {32, 64, 128, 192, 288} with crossover and mutation, selecting configurations that minimize generation quality loss under a computational budget.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-XL/2 (100 steps, cfg=1.5), Q-DiT achieves near-lossless W6A8 quantization with FID 5.32 vs. full-precision 5.31, and maintains FID 6.40 under the more challenging W4A8 setting (only +1.09 over FP). On ImageNet 512×512 (50 steps, cfg=1.5), Q-DiT reaches FID 6.24 (W6A8) and 7.82 (W4A8) vs. FP 6.27. For video generation on VBench with STDiT3 at W4A8, Q-DiT outperforms the G4W+P4A baseline in 15 out of 16 evaluation dimensions, with minimal degradation from full precision (e.g., Subject Consistency 0.9498 vs. 0.9522 FP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-DiT outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated setting. Under W6A8 (cfg=1.5, 256×256), Q-DiT (FID 5.32) edges out the closest competitor PTQ4DiT (5.34) and substantially surpasses G4W+P4A (6.41), TFMQ-DM (8.87), PTQ4DM (8.41), and RepQ-ViT (10.77). Under the much harder W4A8 setting, the gap widens dramatically: PTQ4DM, RepQ-ViT, and TFMQ-DM all collapse to FIDs above 140, while Q-DiT (6.40) still beats PTQ4DiT (7.75) and G4W+P4A (7.66) by roughly 1.3 FID points. On video generation (VBench, W4A8), Q-DiT surpasses G4W+P4A in 15 of 16 dimensions, including Spatial Relationship (0.3810 vs. 0.3265) and Object Class (0.8475 vs. 0.8225), while matching or exceeding full-precision scores on most temporal metrics. The evolutionary group-size search (Alg. 1) further improves over ILP-based (FID 6.71) and Hessian-based (7.38) allocation strategies, confirming that direct FID-guided optimization is more effective than proxy sensitivity metrics for DiT quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-DiT builds on GPTQ for weight quantization and adapts group quantization (from LLM compression work like AWQ and Atom) to the diffusion transformer setting, where prior UNet-based PTQ methods (PTQ4DM, Q-diffusion, PTQD) rely on reconstruction-based calibration that does not scale to large transformer models. It departs from timestep-level activation quantization (TFMQ-DM, TDQ) by introducing sample-wise dynamic quantization inspired by LLM inference optimization (ZeroQuant-FP), and replaces conventional sensitivity-based search (ILP, Hessian) with an evolutionary algorithm directly optimizing FID/FVD. The work positions itself as the first PTQ framework that simultaneously addresses both the spatial channel variance and temporal activation shift specific to DiTs, complementing but outperforming the concurrent PTQ4DiT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DiTs become the dominant architecture for high-quality image and video generation (Stable Diffusion 3, Sora), efficient quantization is critical for real-world deployment at scale. Q-DiT demonstrates near-lossless W6A8 compression and dramatically outperforms all baselines at W4A8 (FID 15.76 vs. 213+ for PTQ4DM), establishing a practical path to 4-bit inference for DiTs. The main limitation is the computational cost of the evolutionary search for group size allocation, which the authors acknowledge as a bottleneck; reducing this search overhead and extending the approach to even larger video DiTs or autoregressive settings remain open questions.
