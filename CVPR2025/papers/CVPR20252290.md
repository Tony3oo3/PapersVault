## Problem

Existing human video generation methods rely on pre-extracted driving motion sequences from other videos, restricting them to specific motion domains (e.g., dancing) with minimal global movement and requiring scene matching. While 3D scene-aware motion generation exists, it demands expensive 3D reconstruction and is limited to simple indoor scenes. No prior work generates human motion conditioned directly on a 2D scene image and text prompt, which would enable diverse, scene-compatible motion without 3D reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D background scene image (168×280, human removed via inpainting) and a free-form text prompt as input. It outputs a sequence of 256 SMPL poses, each parameterized by 23-joint 6D rotations (θ_b ∈ R^{23×6}), a global body orientation (θ_g ∈ R^6), and a 3D camera translation (π ∈ R^3) that projects the pose onto the 2D image plane under a fixed-focal-length perspective camera. The scene image is encoded into 240 spatial tokens via DINO-B and the text into a single token via CLIP-B, yielding a 497-token sequence for the transformer.

## Method

The core architecture is a conditional diffusion transformer (8 blocks, 512 hidden dim) that denoises a motion sequence while receiving text and scene conditions through in-context conditioning—both are concatenated as additional tokens to the motion sequence in a shared token space, inspired by LLM in-context learning. The diffusion timestep is injected via AdaLN, and classifier-free guidance is applied jointly to both text and scene during sampling. Training uses a two-stage strategy: first on the full 300k HiC-Motion dataset to learn diverse motion, then fine-tuning on a mixed subset (60% large-motion, 40% fixed-background videos) to decouple camera-induced displacement from true human motion. The HiC-Motion dataset (300k internet videos) is annotated with pseudo-ground-truth SMPL poses via 4D-Humans, with humans removed from frames using Mask R-CNN and inpainting to create clean scene backgrounds.

## Results

On the HiC-Motion test set (957 samples), Move-in-2D achieves the best FID of 44.64, the highest text-alignment accuracy of 0.661, and the highest diversity score of 26.03 among all compared methods. In VLM-based automated evaluation (ChatGPT-4o, 0–5 scale), the full model scores 3.55 for scene alignment, 2.70 for text alignment, and 2.85 for pose quality (total 9.10), substantially outperforming the next-best method (Ours-scene, total 6.85). Training MDM on the 300k HiC-Motion dataset (MDM+) reduces FID by 72% and increases accuracy by 90% over the original MDM trained on HumanML3D (14k sequences), highlighting the dataset's contribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Move-in-2D (Ours) outperforms all four baselines on FID, Accuracy, and Diversity. Against the text-only baselines, MDM (FID 164.6, Acc 0.325) and MLD (FID 85.9, Acc 0.322) produce motions that are semantically plausible but lack scene compatibility; MLD is slightly better than MDM on FID but both are far behind Ours (FID 44.6, Acc 0.661). The 3D point-cloud-based baselines perform worst: SceneDiff (FID 543.8, Acc 0.203) generates implausible poses with very low diversity (4.2), and HUMANISE (FID 159.9, Acc 0.225) produces nearly static sequences, both struggling to generalize from limited synthetic indoor point clouds to real-world 2D scenes. The most informative comparison is against MDM+ (same backbone and HiC-Motion training data, text-only conditioning), where Ours improves FID from 46.0 to 44.6, accuracy from 0.620 to 0.661, and diversity from 23.0 to 26.0, demonstrating that the 2D scene image conditioning adds meaningful value beyond text alone. In VLM evaluation, Ours leads by a wide margin (total 9.10 vs. 6.70 for MLD, 6.24 for MDM+), with the largest gap in scene alignment (3.55 vs. 2.85 for MLD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-transformer architecture of Peebles & Xie (ICCV 2023) and the text-conditioned motion diffusion framework of MDM (Tevet et al., ICLR 2023) by introducing a 2D scene image as an additional conditioning modality. It departs from prior scene-aware motion generation (HUMANISE, SceneDiff, PROX), which require expensive 3D scene representations (point clouds, meshes, RGB-D), by showing that a single 2D image suffices to provide spatial and semantic affordance cues. The in-context conditioning strategy, borrowed from LLM design, is repurposed here to fuse text (via CLIP) and scene (via DINO) tokens into a shared sequence alongside the motion tokens, rather than using separate cross-attention pathways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a practical two-pass pipeline for human video generation: first synthesize a scene-compatible motion from a 2D image and text prompt, then feed that motion into existing video generators (Champ, commercial models), removing the need for pre-recorded driving sequences or 3D scene scans. The 300k-video HiC-Motion dataset is an order of magnitude larger than prior scene-aware motion datasets and covers both indoor and outdoor settings, which should accelerate follow-up research. Key limitations remain: the model does not control camera motion, the two-pass pipeline is not jointly optimized, and the 3D baselines are trained on very limited synthetic data (e.g., 643 ScanNet scenes), making direct comparisons somewhat uneven.
