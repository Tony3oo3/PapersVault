## Problem

Existing image re-ranking methods compare the query to each gallery image independently in a pair-wise fashion, missing interactions among gallery images such as transitive relationships or shared object parts that appear across multiple candidates. No prior method performs list-wise re-ranking at the local-descriptor level, where each image is represented by multiple descriptors rather than a single global vector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252028/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives L=50 local descriptors (d-dimensional vectors) per image from a visual backbone (DELG or DINOv2-ViT-B/14), concatenated into a single sequence of M=(L+1)(K+1) tokens with learnable [SEP] separation tokens between images. At inference, a sliding window strategy with stride S reuses the model over overlapping groups to re-rank up to N=400 gallery images despite a per-pass context of K=100. The output is one aggregated similarity score per gallery image, computed from L+1 token-level binary classification scores (default: [SEP] token score).

## Method

LOCORE fine-tunes a Longformer backbone whose sliding-window attention provides linear complexity in sequence length, enabling it to process up to 100 gallery images (5000+ tokens) in a single forward pass. All query-image tokens and [SEP] tokens are designated as global-attention tokens so that every descriptor token can attend to the query and image boundaries, while local descriptors attend only within a window of size W. Instead of a single global similarity score, the model performs token-level binary classification (analogous to NLP sequence tagging) on every (L+1)×K gallery tokens, trained with binary cross-entropy. A gallery-shuffled training strategy randomizes the order of gallery images each step to prevent the model from exploiting positional shortcuts from the global retrieval ranking. At test time, a sliding window with overlapping strides extends re-ranking beyond the training list size, and the per-image score is aggregated from its token predictions.

## Results

On the landmark benchmarks (ROxf, RPar and their 1M-distractor variants), LOCORE-base with DINOv2 local descriptors and sliding-window re-ranking over top-400 candidates achieves 92.0 mAP on ROxf Medium and 81.8 mAP on ROxf Hard, outperforming the prior best pair-wise re-ranker AMES by +2.6 and +6.2 mAP respectively. On metric-learning benchmarks, LOCORE-base attains 78.3 R@1 and 64.8 mAP on CUB-200, 83.8 R@1 and 71.0 mAP on SOP, and 87.9 R@1 and 77.0 mAP on In-Shop, setting new state-of-the-art re-ranking results on CUB-200 and SOP. The method uses only 50 local descriptors per image (vs. 500–3072 for prior re-rankers) while completing top-100 re-ranking in a single forward pass with latency comparable to pair-wise baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the pair-wise local-descriptor re-rankers, LOCORE-base consistently wins: with RN101-DELG descriptors it surpasses CVNet Reranker (which uses 3,072 descriptors) by +4.6 mAP on ROxf Medium and +5.6 mAP on RPar Hard, and outperforms RRT by +3.4 mAP on ROxf Medium. With DINOv2 descriptors, LOCORE-base beats AMES (retrained at matching 768-dim hidden size) by +3.3 mAP on ROxf Medium and +5.1 mAP on ROxf Hard, and exceeds the SuperGlobal Rerank global-descriptor baseline by +1.1 mAP on ROxf Medium with top-400 sliding-window re-ranking. On metric-learning benchmarks, LOCORE-base improves R@1 by +9.6 over RRT on CUB-200 (78.3 vs 68.7) and mAP by +3.8 on SOP (71.0 vs 67.2), while on In-Shop the base model's R@1 (87.9) is marginally below RRT (88.3), though the smaller LoCORE-small variant achieves 89.4. Query-expansion baselines (AQE, DQE, αQE) and SSR Rerank show inconsistent or negligible gains, with DQE actually degrading SOP performance from 65.1 to 47.8 mAP. Overall, LOCORE is the only method that improves re-ranking performance across all four metric-learning datasets simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOCORE extends the list-wise re-ranking paradigm introduced by Ouyang et al. (NeurIPS 2021), which processed global descriptors in a joint sequence, to the local-descriptor level where each image is represented by multiple tokens. Architecturally, it repurposes the Longformer's sliding-window attention mechanism—originally designed for long-document NLP—to handle the long sequences produced by concatenating local descriptors from a query and up to 100 gallery images. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant pair-wise re-ranking formulation (RRT, AMES, CVNet) by borrowing the span-extraction framing from NLP tasks like extractive QA, where token-level scores are predicted and then aggregated per image rather than a single global score per pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly processing the shortlist, LOCORE can exploit transitive relationships among gallery images (e.g., two positives sharing common local descriptors) that pair-wise methods fundamentally cannot access, yielding substantial gains especially on hard settings (+17.8 mAP on ROxf+1M Hard). The comparable latency to pair-wise re-rankers and the sliding-window strategy for extending beyond the training list size make the approach practically deployable. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger shortlists (beyond 400 images), whether other long-context architectures (e.g., Mamba, RWKV) could replace Longformer, and whether the NLP-inspired span-extraction framing generalizes to other retrieval modalities such as video or 3D.
