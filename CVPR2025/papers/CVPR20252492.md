## Problem

Conventional preference learning methods (DPO, RLHF) for mitigating object-level hallucinations in MLLMs operate at the full-response and full-image level, causing the model to learn from irrelevant signals—irrelevant text chunks and image regions unrelated to the hallucination. This global optimization dilutes the corrective signal and fails to specifically fix the hallucinated object, analogous to 'redrawing everything' instead of correcting only the erroneous part.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252492/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB image and a text question and produces a free-form text response. During training, the input is augmented with structured target annotations: bounding boxes identifying the specific hallucination-triggering object in the image, and token-level spans marking the hallucinated chunk (y_h^t) and its corrected counterpart (y_r^t) within the response. The preference dataset pairs a hallucinated response with a corrected one, each annotated with these target regions, enabling the model to learn corrections localized to specific objects and text spans rather than the entire image-response pair.

## Method

TL-DPO (Target-Learning Direct Preference Optimization) decomposes the DPO objective into two target-restricted losses. The Target Generation Loss (L_t) applies the standard DPO Bradley-Terry comparison only to the hallucinated and corrected response chunks (y_h^t, y_r^t), justified by a theorem showing that under the assumption that reward differences are confined to target chunks, target-restricted DPO is equivalent to full-response DPO. The Target Condition Loss (L_c) extends mDPO by masking the hallucination-triggering object (via its bounding box) in the image and comparing the model's preference for the correct response given the full image versus the masked image, forcing the model to ground its answer in the specific object region. The combined loss L_TL-DPO = L_t + L_c is optimized via LoRA fine-tuning on a preference dataset constructed from Visual Genome, where GPT-4 identifies and corrects hallucinated segments in baseline model outputs.

## Results

On the Hallucination Benchmark (LLaVA-1.5 base), TL-DPO achieves the lowest CHAIR_s (20.1) and CHAIR_i (5.2) scores, the highest POPE (86.95) and MMHal (2.72) scores, substantially outperforming all compared methods. On the Comprehensive Benchmark, TL-DPO also leads with SciQA-IMG 70.3, MM-Vet 32.2, MMBench 67.8, and LLaVA-Bench 71.2, demonstrating that hallucination reduction does not come at the cost of general multimodal performance. Across five additional MLLM backbones (Qwen VL Chat, LLaVA-Next, Llama3, InternVL-2.5), TL-DPO consistently reduces CHAIR_s by 15–50 points while maintaining or improving comprehensive scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TL-DPO dominates on the core hallucination metrics: it reduces CHAIR_s from 44.6 (RLHF-V) to 20.1 and CHAIR_i from 7.9 to 5.2, and improves MMHal from 2.59 to 2.72. Against HA-DPO, TL-DPO cuts CHAIR_s by 17.1 points (37.2→20.1) and CHAIR_i by 4.8 points (10.0→5.2), while matching its SciQA-IMG score (70.3) and surpassing it on MMBench (67.8 vs 64.0) and LLaVA-Bench (71.2 vs 66.2). HALVA achieves a slightly higher POPE (87.0 vs 86.95) and MMHal (2.25 vs 2.72), but TL-DPO far outperforms it on CHAIR_s (20.1 vs 46.6) and CHAIR_i (5.2 vs 23.1), and leads on all four comprehensive benchmarks. POVID and VLfeedback show only modest hallucination reductions (CHAIR_s 35.2 and 56.3 respectively) and trail TL-DPO by 15+ points on CHAIR_s. Overall, TL-DPO is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best or near-best score on all eight metrics, whereas competing methods trade off hallucination reduction against general multimodal capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al.) and mDPO (Wang et al.) by restricting preference optimization from the full response/sentence level to specific hallucinated chunks and their corresponding image regions (bounding boxes). It departs from the broader line of preference-based hallucination mitigation (RLHF-V, POVID, HA-DPO, HALVA) by arguing that these methods fail because they learn from irrelevant signals—chunks and image regions unrelated to the hallucination—rather than focusing exclusively on the target. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.3, Proposition 1) formalizes why target-restricted DPO is equivalent under a reward-separability assumption and more sample-efficient than holistic preference learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic insight that conventional preference learning for hallucination mitigation is diluted by irrelevant signals provides a clear rationale for why prior methods underperform, and the target-level formulation offers a principled fix. However, the strong Assumption 3.1 (reward differences exist only in target chunks) limits generality, and the generalization results are mixed: while LLaVA-1.5 and InternVL-2.5 show consistent gains, Qwen VL Chat and Llama3 exhibit notable drops in comprehensive metrics (e.g., Llama3 LLaVA-Bench 65.8→58.5). Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex or multi-object hallucinations, whether the GPT-4-dependent dataset construction introduces bias, and whether the target-level formulation scales to larger models without the observed comprehensive-performance trade-offs.
