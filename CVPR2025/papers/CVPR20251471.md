## Problem

Existing black-box MLLM jailbreak attacks either rely on generating semantically harmful images (which may not be truly out-of-distribution given MLLMs' extensive training) or require white-box gradient access. It remains unclear how to fully leverage the visual input space to construct effective OOD inputs for closed-source MLLMs, and the relationship between image complexity versus image content in driving jailbreak success was unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single harmful text query (from five categories: Violence, Financial, Privacy, Self-Harm, Animal) and a retrieval image dataset (10K subset of LLaVA-CC3M-Pretrain595K) as inputs. It produces a composite image arranged in a fixed 3-column grid containing 3 text-to-image transformed sub-queries plus 9 contrasting retrieved subimages, paired with a harmless instruction prompt structured into role-guiding, task-guiding, and visual-guiding sections. The composite image is fed to the target MLLM alongside the instruction, and the model's textual response is evaluated for harmfulness using Beaver-Dam-7b.

## Method

CS-DJ is a training-free, inference-time jailbreak framework built on the Distraction Hypothesis: encoding complex multi-subimage inputs increases token complexity and processing burden on MLLMs, weakening their safety alignment. The first component, structured distraction, uses Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct to decompose the harmful query into 3 sub-queries, each transformed into an image to obscure the original intent and induce a distributional shift. The second component, visual-enhanced distraction, retrieves 9 subimages from the dataset that minimize CLIP cosine similarity to both the query and all previously selected subimages, maximizing inter-subimage contrast. These 12 subimages are arranged in a 3×4 grid composite, and a carefully crafted harmless instruction directs the model to process all sub-queries simultaneously while treating the visual subimages as potentially useful, dispersing attention across multiple tasks. The approach requires no model gradients or fine-tuning, making it applicable to closed-source APIs.

## Results

CS-DJ achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 52.40% and Ensemble Attack Success Rate (EASR) of 74.10% across four closed-source MLLMs (GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o, GPT-4V, Gemini-1.5-Flash) and five harm categories. The highest per-model performance is on Gemini-1.5-Flash (64.11% ASR, 81.46% EASR), while the lowest is on GPT-4o (42.24% ASR, 65.86% EASR). The Financial and Privacy categories are most vulnerable, with ASR values exceeding 70% for CS-DJ on GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-1.5-Flash.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CS-DJ is compared against Hades [Li et al., ECCV 2024], the state-of-the-art black-box MLLM jailbreak method that exploits image harmfulness via prompt-embedded image infection. CS-DJ consistently outperforms Hades across all five harm categories and all four evaluated MLLMs on both ASR and EASR. The ASR improvement ranges from +25.11 pp (GPT-4V) to +54.91 pp (Gemini-1.5-Flash), while the EASR improvement ranges from +26.80 pp (GPT-4V) to +66.67 pp (Gemini-1.5-Flash). Notably, Hades achieves its best result on GPT-4V (20.33% ASR), suggesting that image harmfulness is more effective on that model, whereas CS-DJ's distraction strategy is most effective on Gemini-1.5-Flash (64.11% ASR), indicating that the two attack axes (harmfulness vs. distraction) exploit different model vulnerabilities. The paper attributes CS-DJ's advantage to the multi-level distraction mechanism—query decomposition plus contrasting subimages—which disperses the model's attention more effectively than simply increasing the harmfulness of a single image.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in MLLM jailbreak research, which focuses on making visual inputs semantically harmful (e.g., Hades generating harmful images via diffusion models, FigStep embedding text in images). Instead, CS-DJ introduces the Distraction Hypothesis: it is the structural complexity and contrast among multiple subimages—not their semantic content—that disrupts MLLM safety alignment. Conceptually, it repurposes the image-retrieval and contrastive-embedding machinery (CLIP) not to find harmful images but to find maximally *dissimilar* ones, shifting the attack axis from content harmfulness to attention dispersion. The query-decomposition component draws on the broader LLM jailbreak literature of prompt fragmentation but applies it to the multimodal setting in a way that feeds into the visual pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that subimage complexity, not content, drives jailbreak success reframes how the field should think about MLLM visual vulnerabilities: defenses tuned to detect harmful semantics may be blind to structurally distracting inputs. However, the experimental scope is narrow—only one comparison baseline (Hades), four closed-source models, and a single trial per query in ablations—limiting confidence in the generality of the results. The authors themselves note that CLIP-based distraction distance does not always track ASR trends, leaving open the question of how to properly measure and defend against distraction-based attacks. Broader implications include whether multi-subimage distraction generalizes to audio or video modalities and whether RLHF-based alignment can be made robust to attention-dispersing inputs.
