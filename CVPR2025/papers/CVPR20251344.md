## Problem

Existing camera-control methods for video diffusion transformers (e.g., VD3D, MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl) produce imprecise camera steering and noticeably degrade visual quality and scene dynamics. The root causes—how camera motion is spectrally structured in diffusion, where camera knowledge resides within a pre-trained VDiT, and the static-scene bias of the standard RE10K training set—had not been systematically analyzed or exploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251344/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded by T5) and a per-frame camera trajectory (4×4 extrinsics + 3×3 intrinsics, 25 values per frame) as joint conditions, and outputs a camera-controlled video. Camera conditioning is applied only during the first 40% of the rectified-flow denoising trajectory (t ∈ [0.6, 1]) and only into the first 8 of 32 DiT blocks; training is performed at 256² resolution but generalizes to higher resolutions out-of-the-box because the frozen VDiT backbone handles high-frequency detail.

## Method

AC3D builds on a frozen 11.5B-parameter VDiT backbone with a lightweight ControlNet-style camera branch (Plücker-coordinate camera tokens processed by 128-dim DiT-XS blocks, injected via summation and cross-attention feedback). Three key design choices drive its performance: (1) a truncated-normal noise schedule (location 0.8, scale 0.075, bounded to [0.6, 1]) for both training and inference, motivated by spectral analysis showing camera motion is low-frequency and determined in the first ~10% of denoising; (2) restricting camera conditioning to only the first 8 DiT blocks, based on linear probing that reveals VDiT implicitly encodes camera poses in its middle layers (blocks 13–21), yielding a ~4× reduction in trainable parameters; and (3) augmenting RE10K with 20K curated videos of dynamic scenes under stationary cameras to teach the model to disentangle camera motion from scene motion. Separate classifier-free guidance weights for text and camera, plus a bidirectional ControlNet feedback connection, further improve controllability and visual fidelity.

## Results

AC3D(VDiT) achieves the best performance across all metrics on both in-distribution (RealEstate10K) and out-of-distribution (MSR-VTT) evaluations. On MSR-VTT, it reaches FVD 110.71, FID 5.34, CLIP 28.58, translation error 0.428, and rotation error 0.038, outperforming the closest baseline VD3D(VDiT) by ~19% in FVD and ~12% in translation error. On RealEstate10K, AC3D(VDiT) attains FVD 36.55, FID 1.18, and rotation error 0.035. In a user study, AC3D is preferred over VD3D in 95% of cases overall (92.5% vs. the re-implemented VD3D+DiT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AC3D(VDiT) outperforms all three baselines on every metric on both datasets. Against the closest competitor VD3D(VDiT), AC3D reduces translation error by 15% (0.421→0.358) and rotation error by 37% (0.056→0.035) on RealEstate10K, and by 12% and 19% respectively on MSR-VTT. Visual quality improves substantially on out-of-distribution data: FVD drops from 137.62 to 110.71 (~19%) and FID from 6.88 to 5.34 (~22%) on MSR-VTT. The U-Net-based baselines (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl) are significantly weaker, with translation errors 30–40% higher and FVD 2–3× worse than AC3D. The paper reports 18% higher video fidelity and 25% more precise camera steering than the closest competitor, and a user study confirms 95% overall preference for AC3D over the original VD3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VD3D (Bahmani et al., 2024), the first method to bring 3D camera control into a video diffusion transformer, by replacing its pixel-space FIT backbone with a latent-space 11.5B VDiT and, more importantly, by grounding architectural and training decisions in three empirical analyses: spectral properties of camera motion, linear probing of implicit pose knowledge in VDiT layers, and dataset bias diagnostics. The conditioning architecture follows the ControlNet paradigm (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) with Plücker coordinate representations, consistent with the broader camera-control literature (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl), but the key departure is the principled restriction of camera injection to early denoising steps and early transformer blocks, motivated by the analyses rather than by architectural convention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that camera motion is a low-frequency signal determined within the first ~10% of the denoising trajectory provides a general principle for when and where to inject camera conditioning in any video diffusion model, and the 4× parameter reduction from limiting conditioning to 8 blocks makes camera control substantially cheaper to train and deploy. The discovery that a text-only VDiT implicitly performs camera pose estimation (via linear probing) opens questions about what 3D geometric knowledge is already encoded in large video models and whether this can be exploited for training-free or lightweight control. Remaining limitations include dependence on RealEstate10K for camera annotations (which constrains generalization to novel scene types), the acknowledged difficulty of handling camera trajectories far outside the training distribution, and the fact that the 20K stationary-camera dataset, while effective, is a heuristic fix rather than a solution to the fundamental problem of annotating dynamic scenes.
