## Problem

Model-based 6D pose estimation for unseen objects typically relies on exhaustive template matching with hundreds of pre-rendered views or on segmentation masks from external detectors, making it either computationally expensive or fragile to noisy detections. The paper addresses how to achieve fast, accurate, and generalizable pose estimation for novel objects using only a CAD model, without per-object fine-tuning, by reformulating pose estimation as a correspondence-finding problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252313/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and the 3D CAD model of the target object as input; object detection is handled externally by CNOS. The coarse model operates on 224×224 crops while the refinement and selection models use 256×256 crops. The CAD model is pre-rendered into 42 templates covering only out-of-plane rotations (in-plane rotation invariance is learned by the network). The output is a 6DoF pose (rotation matrix + translation vector), produced via a 5-hypothesis strategy where each hypothesis is independently refined and a learned selector picks the best.

## Method

Co-op is a two-stage pipeline built on CroCo-pretrained ViT encoders with transformer decoders. In coarse estimation, a hybrid representation combines patch-level classification (assigning each query patch to one of the template patches or a 'no-match' class) with sub-patch offset regression, enabling semi-dense correspondences from only 42 templates; the pose is recovered via EPnP+RANSAC. In pose refinement, a DPT head predicts a probabilistic dense flow (parameterized as a univariate Laplace distribution with learned mean and scale) between the query and rendered images, and a differentiable Levenberg-Marquardt PnP layer backpropagates pose loss through the flow confidence (certainty × sensitivity × flow probability) for end-to-end training. A separate pose selection network scores multiple refined hypotheses to pick the final pose.

## Results

On the seven core BOP Challenge datasets, Co-op achieves a mean Average Recall (AR) of 65.7 with 5 pose hypotheses (4.19 s/image), outperforming the previous best method FoundPose (59.6 AR, 20.52 s/image) by 6.1 points while being approximately 5× faster. In the single-hypothesis setting, Co-op reaches 64.0 mean AR (1.85 s), surpassing GenFlow (55.7) and MegaPose (50.9). Remarkably, Co-op's coarse estimation alone (58.4 mean AR, 0.98 s) already exceeds the full GigaPose + GenFlow pipeline (56.8 mean AR, 2.21 s), demonstrating the strength of the hybrid correspondence representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Co-op decisively outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation setting. In coarse-only estimation, Co-op (58.4 mean AR) surpasses FoundPose (37.3) by 21.1 points, GigaPose (27.6) by 30.8 points, and GenFlow (23.5) by 34.9 points. With 5-hypothesis refinement, Co-op (65.7) beats FoundPose (59.6) by 6.1 points, GenFlow (57.0) by 8.7 points, and MegaPose (54.7) by 11.0 points, while also being 5× faster than FoundPose (4.19 s vs. 20.52 s per image). A particularly striking result is that Co-op's coarse stage alone (58.4) already exceeds the full GigaPose + GenFlow pipeline (56.8), indicating that the hybrid patch-classification + offset-regression representation captures more discriminative correspondences than prior feature-matching approaches. ZS6D is only reported on three datasets (LM-O, T-LESS, YCB-V) and is outperformed by Co-op on all three (59.7 vs. 29.8, 59.2 vs. 21.0, 62.6 vs. 32.4). The ablation further confirms that pairing Co-op's refiner with GigaPose's coarse stage (58.3) outperforms GigaPose + GenFlow (56.8), isolating the refiner's contribution as a consistent +1.5 point gain over the prior best refiner.

## Relation to Prior Work

Co-op extends the render-and-compare paradigm established by MegaPose and GenFlow for pose refinement, but replaces deterministic flow with a probabilistic (Laplace) flow formulation and learns confidence end-to-end through a differentiable PnP layer. For coarse estimation, it departs from the detect-and-describe framework used by FoundPose and GigaPose (which rely on CNOS segmentation masks and DINOv2 patch descriptors) in favor of a detector-free correspondence estimation approach, while adopting GigaPose's strategy of using only out-of-plane rotation templates. The hybrid representation combining patch-level classification with sub-patch offset regression draws from the broader detector-free matching literature (ASPAN, RoMa) but is specifically tailored to the template-to-query correspondence problem in pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The large margin of improvement over prior SOTA (nearly 20 AR points in coarse estimation, ~6 points in the full pipeline) on the BOP Challenge's seven core datasets makes Co-op a strong new baseline for unseen object pose estimation, and the finding that its coarse stage alone outperforms the full GigaPose+GenFlow pipeline suggests that correspondence quality, not refinement, was the bottleneck in prior methods. The detector-free design removes dependence on segmentation quality, which is a practical advantage in cluttered or occluded scenes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes with many simultaneous objects, whether the 42-template requirement can be further reduced, and how well the method handles objects with extreme symmetry or very low texture beyond what the BOP datasets cover.
