## Problem

Vision-language models struggle with spatial reasoning tasks critical for robotics, such as determining where to place objects or interpreting spatial instructions from different perspectives. Existing spatial reasoning datasets are built from generic internet images with perception-model-generated annotations, lacking reference frame comprehension (ego-, world-, object-centric) and actionable spatial relationships like compatibility and context that are essential for real-world robotic manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251627/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB images, calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and oriented 3D bounding box annotations from source scene datasets, producing structured QA pairs where each entry pairs an image with a spatial question and a reference frame label. For spatial context tasks, the output is a list of 2D (x,y) image coordinates in free space; for configuration and compatibility tasks, the output is a binary True/False answer. Each spatial relation is generated in all three reference frames (ego, world, object), and the dataset pairs 2D egocentric images with 3D scans to support both 2D and 3D VLM consumption.

## Method

ROBOSPATIAL is constructed via a two-stage automated pipeline: Stage 1 extracts 3D spatial relationships between object pairs using oriented bounding boxes and heading vectors, computing directional relations (left, right, above, below, front, behind) independently in ego, world, and object frames via camera extrinsics; Stage 2 projects these into 2D image space, sampling free-space points with raycasting-based occlusion filtering for context tasks, and simulating virtual bounding box placement with a 10 cm margin for compatibility tasks. Questions are generated from deterministic templates in the format {TARGET} {RELATION} {ANCHOR} {REF.FRAME} to minimize reliance on linguistic priors. The resulting 3M QA pairs across 1M images and 5k scans are used to fine-tune a suite of 2D VLMs (VILA, LLaVA-NeXT, SpaceLLaVA, RoboPoint) and 3D VLMs (3D-LLM, LEO) via supervised fine-tuning, with an auxiliary object grounding dataset included to improve reference resolution.

## Results

On the held-out ROBOSPATIAL-Val benchmark, all models improve substantially after fine-tuning on ROBOSPATIAL. The best 3D VLM, LEO + ROBOSPATIAL, achieves 71.9% total average accuracy (73.1% indoor, 70.7% tabletop), while the best 2D VLM, RoboPoint + ROBOSPATIAL, reaches 70.6% total average. GPT-4o without fine-tuning scores 50.8% total, and Molmo scores 50.1%, confirming that task-specific spatial training yields large gains over general-purpose VLMs. On out-of-domain benchmarks, ROBOSPATIAL-trained models also lead: SpaceLLaVA + ROBOSPATIAL achieves 81.8% on BLINK and VILA + ROBOSPATIAL reaches 73.6% on SpatialBench, both surpassing GPT-4o (76.2% BLINK, 70.6% SpatialBench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROBOSPATIAL fine-tuning consistently outperforms all zero-shot baselines across every spatial task. LEO + ROBOSPATIAL (71.9% total) surpasses GPT-4o (50.8%) by 21.1 points and Molmo (50.1%) by 21.8 points on ROBOSPATIAL-Val, demonstrating that domain-specific spatial training far exceeds general-purpose VLMs. Among 2D VLMs, RoboPoint + ROBOSPATIAL (70.6%) edges out SpaceLLaVA + ROBOSPATIAL (65.7%) and VILA + ROBOSPATIAL (63.9%), while LLaVA-NeXT + ROBOSPATIAL (60.5%) trails by 5–10 points. On the out-of-domain BLINK benchmark, SpaceLLaVA + ROBOSPATIAL (81.8%) beats GPT-4o (76.2%) by 5.6 points, and on SpatialBench, VILA + ROBOSPATIAL (73.6%) exceeds GPT-4o (70.6%) by 3 points. In real-robot manipulation (Table 6), LLaVA-NeXT + ROBOSPATIAL achieves the highest success rate at 52.6%, outperforming GPT-4o (46.9%) and RoboPoint + ROBOSPATIAL (46.2%), confirming that spatial training translates to physical task completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of spatial reasoning VLMs (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT) and robotics-oriented pointing models (RoboPoint, Molmo), which have each addressed only part of the problem: the former lack embodied grounding and reference-frame awareness, while the latter ignore real-world placement constraints. Unlike prior spatial datasets (EmbSpatial-Bench, BLINK-Spatial, Visual Spatial) that are small, 2D-only, or lack reference-frame annotations, ROBOSPATIAL unifies 2D egocentric images with 3D scans and systematically encodes three reference frames (ego, world, object) across three robotics-specific spatial relations (context, compatibility, configuration). The automated generation pipeline repurposes existing 3D-annotated scene datasets (ScanNet, Matterport3D, 3RScan, HOPE, GraspNet-1B) rather than introducing new capture hardware, making the approach extensible to future annotated sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROBOSPATIAL addresses a clear data bottleneck for spatial understanding in robotics VLMs and is likely to become a standard pretraining/evaluation resource given its scale (1M images, 5k scans, 3M QA pairs) and 2D/3D-ready design. Key open questions remain: whether the apparent 3D-over-2D advantage is genuine or an artifact of overlapping pretraining data, how 2D spatial predictions degrade under viewpoint changes, and whether 3D VLMs can operate on partial point clouds for real-world deployment. The real-robot experiments, while encouraging, are limited in scale (~200 queries) and use simple objects, leaving open whether the gains transfer to cluttered, long-horizon manipulation.
