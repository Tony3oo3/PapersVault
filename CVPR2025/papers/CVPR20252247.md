## Problem

Existing point cloud upsampling methods rely on CD/EMD loss functions that are highly sensitive to outliers, making them ineffective at handling noisy real-world scans. Additionally, their extracted features lack sufficient distinguishability, producing scattered outputs without clear geometric structure. This paper addresses both gaps by incorporating adaptive noise suppression into a conditional diffusion framework that inherently avoids distance-based losses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252247/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse point cloud of 2,048 points and an integer upsampling factor r (tested at 4× and 16×) as input. The sparse cloud is first interpolated to a coarse dense cloud of rN points, which is then summed with the noisy diffusion state and fed into the DP-Net as a spatial condition, while the upsampling factor and timestep are encoded as global conditions. The output is a dense point cloud of 2,048×r points generated through the reverse diffusion process.

## Method

PDANS consists of two parallel networks—a Conditional Point Cloud (CP) network and a Diffusion Point Cloud (DP) network—connected via cross-attention at every encoder/decoder layer. The CP-Net extracts features from the sparse input using an Adaptive Noise Suppression (ANS) module that computes attention-weighted neighborhood sums to identify and down-weight outlier points before feature aggregation, replacing FPS-based downsampling. The DP-Net shares the same encoder-decoder skeleton but additionally receives the interpolated coarse cloud and encoded timestep/upsampling factor as conditions, and is trained with a standard DDPM noise-prediction objective (no CD or EMD loss). Decoding in both networks uses a TreeTrans (TT) module that propagates features via TreeGCN-style cyclic and ancestor operations, then fuses them with corresponding encoder features through sequential local and global attention. The CP-Net is additionally supervised with an MSE reconstruction loss on the sparse input to encourage informative feature extraction.

## Results

On PUGAN at 4× upsampling, PDANS achieves CD=0.119, HD=1.207, and P2F=1.897, outperforming the closest competitor PUDM (CD=0.131, HD=1.220, P2F=1.912). At 16× upsampling on PUGAN, PDANS reaches CD=0.078, HD=1.046, P2F=2.028, again leading all baselines. On the more complex PU1K dataset at 4×, PDANS achieves CD=0.155, HD=1.588, P2F=1.354, surpassing PUDM by 28.6% in CD and 26.6% in HD. Under high Gaussian noise (τ=0.1), PDANS maintains CD=0.543 and HD=4.327, while PUDM degrades to CD=0.853 and HD=6.239.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDANS outperforms all seven baselines across every metric (CD, HD, P2F) on both PUGAN and PU1K. The closest competitor is PUDM [29], the only other conditional diffusion-based upsampling method; PDANS improves CD by 9.2% (0.119 vs 0.131) and HD by 1.1% (1.207 vs 1.220) at 4× on PUGAN, and by 28.6% in CD (0.155 vs 0.217) and 26.6% in HD (1.588 vs 2.164) on PU1K. Among refinement-based methods, Grad-PU [8] and RepKpU [30] are the strongest non-diffusion baselines, but PDANS still leads them by 51% in CD at 4× on PUGAN (0.119 vs 0.245/0.254). Earlier generative methods (PU-Net, MPU, PU-GAN, PU-GCN) are substantially outperformed, with PDANS achieving roughly 4–7× lower CD than PU-Net. Under noisy conditions (Gaussian τ=0.1), the gap widens further: PDANS achieves CD=0.543 vs PUDM's 0.853 and Grad-PU's 1.118, demonstrating the ANS module's noise-robustness advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDANS sits at the intersection of two research threads: conditional diffusion models for 3D generation (building on DDPM and the closely related PUDM) and tree-structured graph convolutions (TreeGCN). It departs from the dominant point cloud upsampling paradigm—which relies on Chamfer Distance or Earth Mover's Distance losses that are inherently outlier-sensitive—by replacing those losses with a diffusion-based generative objective. The key architectural departure from PUDM is the ANS module, which explicitly reweights and removes noisy points during encoding, and the TreeTrans decoder, which replaces the standard UNet-style skip connections with cyclic and ancestor feature propagation combined with local and global attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-world point clouds from LiDAR or structured-light scanners are routinely noisy and non-uniform, yet most upsampling methods degrade sharply under even moderate noise; PDANS addresses this practical gap with a principled mechanism (attention-weighted neighborhood removal) rather than ad-hoc preprocessing. The elimination of CD/EMD losses in favor of a diffusion objective is a clean conceptual simplification that removes a known failure mode. However, the real-world validation on ScanObjectNN is limited to visual surface-reconstruction comparison without quantitative metrics, and the computational cost of iterative diffusion sampling at inference time is not discussed, leaving open questions about practical deployment in latency-sensitive applications like autonomous driving.
