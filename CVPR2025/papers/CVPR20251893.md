## Problem

Existing portrait animation methods cannot independently manipulate lighting during animation because they fail to disentangle intrinsic features (identity, appearance) from extrinsic ones (pose, lighting). This limits the ability to seamlessly blend generated portraits with backgrounds under user-specified or scene-matched lighting conditions, a critical need in video conferencing, VR, and film production.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251893/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static reference portrait image (512×512), a driving video providing per-frame pose, and target lighting specified as second-order spherical harmonics coefficients. A key preprocessing step renders a 'shading hint' image per frame by combining the reference portrait's 3D mesh (extracted via DECA/FLAME), the driving frame's pose, and the target lighting. The output is a temporally consistent video where the reference identity and appearance are preserved while pose follows the driving video and lighting matches the target, with a portrait mask applied to the reference latent to suppress the original lighting from leaking into the result.

## Method

LCVD builds on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) and introduces two lightweight adapters that carve out dedicated subspaces within SVD's feature space: a shading adapter maps rendered shading hints into an extrinsic subspace (encoding pose and lighting), and a reference adapter maps the reference image into an intrinsic subspace (encoding identity and appearance). During self-supervised training, the two adapter outputs are randomly included or excluded (each iteration uses either, both, or neither), forcing SVD to learn both subspaces jointly; a portrait-region mask in the loss further focuses learning on the face. At inference, multi-condition classifier-free guidance (adapted from Composer) combines the two subspaces by setting c₂ = F_s + F_r and c₁ = F_r, so the guidance weight ω selectively amplifies the extrinsic (lighting/pose) direction while leaving the intrinsic (identity) direction at unit strength, enabling continuous control over relighting intensity without sacrificing identity.

## Results

On the HDTF relighting benchmark, LCVD achieves the best FVD (273.3), FID (37.46), IQ (3.034), and LE (0.738), improving FVD by 32% and FID by 16% over the second-best method (DPR). On the cross-identity portrait animation task, LCVD leads all metrics including ID (0.876), POSE error (3.805), IQ (3.021), and FID (49.11), surpassing AnimateAnyone by 8.7% in ID and 29.7% in FID. The sole metric where LCVD trails is objective ID on HDTF relighting (0.585 vs. DPR's 0.730), though it achieves the highest user-rated ID score (4.000/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCVD outperforms all five relighting baselines (DPR, SMFR, NFL, StyleFlow, DiFaReli) on HDTF in every metric except objective ID, where DPR scores 0.730 vs. LCVD's 0.585; however, LCVD leads in user-rated ID (4.000 vs. 3.462). The largest margins are against StyleGAN-based methods: StyleFlow and NFL lose background details and degrade identity (ID drops to 0.474 and 0.199 respectively), while DiFaReli introduces facial artifacts despite preserving backgrounds. Among animation baselines on HDTF, LCVD beats AnimateAnyone (the strongest competitor) by 8.7% in ID (0.876 vs. 0.806), 25.3% in POSE error (3.805 vs. 5.086), and 29.7% in FID (49.11 vs. 69.85). DaGAN and StyleHEAT are substantially weaker, with StyleHEAT showing severe pose misalignment (POSE error 34.58) and poor identity preservation (ID 0.201). On FFHQ, LCVD leads in ID (0.765 vs. DiFaReli's 0.687) and IQ (4.465 vs. StyleFlow's 3.846) but trails DiFaReli in LE (0.938 vs. 0.749) and FID (26.71 vs. 25.98), which the authors attribute to different training data distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—portrait animation (LivePortrait, AnimateAnyone, DaGAN) and portrait relighting (DPR, SMFR, NFL, StyleFlow, DiFaReli)—into a single framework by building on Stable Video Diffusion as the generative backbone. The key architectural departure is the use of two lightweight adapters (shading and reference) to carve out dedicated extrinsic and intrinsic feature subspaces within SVD's latent space, conditioned on 3D-rendered shading hints from DECA/FLAME parameters. The multi-condition classifier-free guidance mechanism, borrowed from Composer, is repurposed to independently scale the lighting guidance strength relative to identity preservation, a control not available in prior animation or relighting methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously animate and relight a portrait in a single pass, which is practically important for video conferencing, virtual production, and film where foreground-background lighting consistency is critical. The main open question is the inherent trade-off between lighting fidelity and identity preservation governed by the guidance weight ω, which the paper acknowledges but does not resolve. Limitations include dependence on DECA's 3D mesh accuracy, single-person scope, and ~2-minute inference for 100 frames, suggesting real-time deployment remains out of reach. Whether the adapter-based subspace decomposition generalizes to multi-person scenes or more complex body animation is unexplored.
