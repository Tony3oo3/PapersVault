## Problem

Existing lidar place recognition methods are predominantly evaluated in urban ground-to-ground settings, leaving cross-view (ground-to-aerial) recognition in natural forest environments largely unexplored. Forest scenes present dense occlusions, scarce distinctive features, and highly variable point densities from spinning lidar, which cause standard sparse point cloud baselines (e.g., 4096 points) to fail. The paper also identifies that octree-based self-attention, while efficient, restricts receptive field to a single octree level and ignores the hierarchical multi-scale structure needed for robust scene-level descriptors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes variable-size lidar point cloud submaps (averaging ~28K points at 0.8 m voxel resolution for CS-Wild-Places, or 4096 points for urban benchmarks) within a 30 m horizontal radius, captured by spinning or pushbroom lidar from ground or aerial platforms. It produces a single 256-dimensional L2-normalized global descriptor vector used for nearest-neighbour retrieval against a database of submaps. The architecture is designed to be invariant to the differing point density distributions and noise patterns inherent to each sensor configuration, enabling cross-source matching without per-source fine-tuning.

## Method

HOTFormerLoc builds a hierarchical octree feature pyramid (L=3 levels) from an initial sparse octree convolution stem and OSA transformer blocks, then processes it through M=10 HOTFormer blocks. Each block alternates between Relay Token Self-Attention (RTSA), where compact relay tokens from all pyramid levels interact via full multi-head self-attention to model long-range multi-scale context, and Hierarchical Octree Self-Attention (H-OSA), where refined relay tokens attend jointly with local window tokens to propagate global cues back to fine-grained features. Attention windows are constructed in cylindrical coordinates (ρ, θ, z) rather than Cartesian, so window granularity adapts to the radial density falloff of spinning lidar. Relay tokens are initialised with an Absolute Distribution-aware Positional Encoding (ADaPE) that encodes the centroid and covariance of each window's point distribution, enabling the network to distinguish relay tokens representing different spatial extents. A Pyramid Attentional Pooling layer with per-level learnable queries adaptively aggregates multi-scale tokens, followed by a token fuser and MLP-Mixer to produce the final descriptor; training uses a TSAP ranking loss with large-batch multi-staged backpropagation.

## Results

On Oxford RobotCar, HOTFormerLoc achieves AR@1 of 92.1% and AR@1% of 96.5%, outperforming all prior methods. On CS-Campus3D (ground-to-aerial), it reaches AR@1 of 80.4% and AR@1% of 94.9%, a 9.7-point gain over CrossLoc3D. On the new CS-Wild-Places forest benchmark, HOTFormerLoc improves AR@1 by 5.5%–11.5% and AR@1% by 3.6%–4.5% over the best prior method. On Wild-Places, it achieves AR@1 of 74.8% and MRR of 83.7%, trailing LoGG3D-Net (77.3%/85.5%) by 2.5 points, though LoGG3D-Net uses a 1024-dim descriptor versus HOTFormerLoc's 256-dim.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOTFormerLoc outperforms all six baselines on Oxford RobotCar, achieving 92.1% mean AR@1 versus MinkLoc3Dv2 (90.0%), PPT-Net (88.1%), and CrossLoc3D (84.1%), with the largest gains on unseen environments R.A. (+2.7%) and B.D. (+4.1%). On CS-Campus3D (ground-to-aerial), it surpasses CrossLoc3D by 9.7 points in AR@1 (80.4% vs. 70.7%) and 9.2 points in AR@1%, demonstrating that hierarchical octree attention generalises to cross-source matching without diffusion-inspired refinement. On Wild-Places, LoGG3D-Net retains a 2.5-point AR@1 lead (77.3% vs. 74.8%), but HOTFormerLoc uses a 4× smaller descriptor (256-dim vs. 1024-dim) and gains 5.5 points over MinkLoc3Dv2. On CS-Wild-Places, CrossLoc3D's mean AR@1 drops to 10.1% (requiring downsampling to 4096 points), while HOTFormerLoc achieves 5.5%–11.5% higher AR@1 than the best competing method. LoGG3D-Net's local-consistency loss degrades on cross-source data due to fewer point correspondences, explaining its drop from Wild-Places to CS-Wild-Places. Overall, HOTFormerLoc is the only method to achieve SOTA on both single-source (Oxford, Wild-Places) and cross-source (CS-Campus3D, CS-Wild-Places) benchmarks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOTFormerLoc extends OctFormer's octree-based self-attention (which computes attention at a single octree level) into a hierarchical multi-scale framework by introducing relay tokens that enable global context propagation across pyramid levels, drawing conceptual inspiration from FasterViT's carrier tokens and hierarchical window attention in image transformers. The cylindrical octree coordinate system is a targeted departure from standard Cartesian octrees, motivated by the radial density gradient inherent in spinning lidar scans. For the pooling stage, the paper adapts the learnable-query attention pooling mechanism from SALSA into a multi-level Pyramid Attentional Pooling that handles variable token counts across resolutions. In the cross-source LPR setting, the work positions itself against CrossLoc3D (diffusion-inspired canonical space) and GAPR (overlap-aware triplet loss), arguing that hierarchical multi-scale feature learning is more general than task-specific refinement steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of CS-Wild-Places fills a notable gap: prior cross-source LPR benchmarks (CS-Campus3D) are confined to structured urban/campus environments, while unstructured forests present dense occlusions, perceptual aliasing, and long-term structural change that stress-test place recognition in ways urban data does not. The cylindrical octree insight—aligning attention windows with the sensor's native point distribution—is a simple, transferable design principle that could benefit other lidar-based 3D tasks. Limitations include the 35.4M parameter count and 270 ms inference time (roughly 2.6× slower than MinkLoc3Dv2), and the method's reliance on ~28K-point submaps that may not suit all deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether token pruning can close the efficiency gap and whether integrating RGB or semantic cues would further improve robustness in feature-sparse natural environments.
