## Problem

Existing RGB-guided depth super-resolution methods assume a fixed, known degradation (typically bicubic downsampling), but real-world depth captured by ToF sensors suffers from unknown, unconventional degradation (structural distortion, blur) that causes depth-RGB structural inconsistency. Because degradation labels are unavailable in practice, prior methods cannot explicitly estimate the degradation, leading to significant performance drops on real data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252514/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution depth map (h×w×1) with unknown degradation and a high-resolution RGB image (sh×sw×3) as input, producing a high-resolution depth map (sh×sw×1). The LR depth is bicubic-upsampled to match the RGB resolution before processing. A key design choice is that the degradation regularization branch operates only during training to supervise degradation representation learning, adding zero computational overhead at inference. A lightweight variant (DORNet-T) reduces all convolutional channels to 3/8 of the full model while preserving the architecture.

## Method

DORNet learns implicit degradation representations in a self-supervised manner: a degradation encoder maps the upsampled LR depth to representations D̃ and D, and a Mixture-of-Experts-style router dynamically selects k out of g multi-scale degradation kernel generators. The selected generators produce degradation kernels that convolve with the predicted HR depth to synthesize a new degraded depth D_d, which is supervised by an L1 loss against the upsampled LR depth and a VGG19-based contrastive loss that pulls D_d toward the LR depth while pushing it away from the HR depth. For RGB-D fusion, a Degradation-Oriented Feature Transformation (DOFT) module uses D̃ to generate spatial offsets and modulation scalars for deformable convolution on RGB features, and D to produce attention weights focusing on degraded regions; a learned affinity coefficient from D̃ then selectively gates the transfer of RGB content into depth features across five recursive DOFT iterations. The total training objective combines a reconstruction L1 loss with the degradation and contrastive losses (λ₁=λ₂=0.1).

## Results

On the real-world RGB-D-D dataset, DORNet achieves the lowest RMSE of 3.42 cm, outperforming the previous best SFG (3.88 cm) by 0.46 cm while using only 3.05M parameters (vs. 63.53M for SFG). On TOFDSR, DORNet reaches 4.21 cm RMSE, beating SGNet (4.33 cm) by 0.12 cm. The lightweight variant DORNet-T (0.46M params) achieves 3.84 cm on RGB-D-D, reducing RMSE by 29% and inference time by 35% compared to DCTNet (0.48M). On synthetic NYU-v2 (×4), DORNet achieves 1.19 cm RMSE, comparable to SGNet (1.10 cm) while using 91% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DORNet decisively outperforms all 15 baselines on both real-world datasets. On RGB-D-D, it beats the strongest competitor SFG [53] by 0.46 cm (3.42 vs. 3.88) while using 95% fewer parameters (3.05M vs. 63.53M), and surpasses SGNet [37] by 1.90 cm. On TOFDSR, DORNet edges out SGNet [37] by 0.12 cm (4.21 vs. 4.33) and SFG by 0.31 cm. The lightweight DORNet-T (0.46M) matches or exceeds DCTNet [56] (0.48M) on both real-world benchmarks, reducing RMSE by 29% on RGB-D-D. On synthetic NYU-v2 (×4), DORNet (1.19 cm) is slightly behind SGNet (1.10 cm) but ahead of all other methods, and DORNet-T (1.33 cm) outperforms DCTNet (1.59 cm) and FDSR (1.61 cm) by 16% and 17% respectively. In noisy settings (Table 2), DORNet maintains its lead, beating SFG by 0.40 cm on RGB-D-D with added Gaussian noise and blur.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in RGB-guided depth super-resolution, where prior methods (FDSR, DCTNet, SGNet, SFG) assume a known, fixed degradation (typically bicubic downsampling) and directly fuse RGB features with upsampled depth. DORNet instead repurposes the degradation-representation learning idea from blind image super-resolution (DASR, KDSR) to the depth domain, introducing a self-supervised strategy that learns implicit degradation kernels via a Mixture-of-Experts-style router and a re-blurring regularization loop. The degradation-oriented feature transformation module (DOFT) then uses these learned priors to conditionally gate deformable-convolution-based RGB-to-depth feature transfer, rather than performing unconditional fusion as in prior guided-filtering approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap: real ToF-camera depth exhibits structural distortion and blur that synthetic bicubic degradation cannot capture, and prior methods degrade significantly under this mismatch. The self-supervised degradation learning requires no degradation labels and the regularization branch adds zero inference overhead, making the approach deployable. Open questions include whether the learned degradation representations generalize across sensor types (e.g., structured-light vs. ToF), whether the approach extends to video depth SR with temporal consistency, and how well it performs when RGB and depth are misaligned beyond the small offsets typical of consumer devices.
