## Problem

Restoring high-quality color images from Bayer-pattern spike streams of color spike cameras (CSCs) is challenging due to three coupled issues: 75%/50% spatially missing pixels per color channel, motion blur from high-speed dynamics, and quantization noise introduced by discrete clock-based signal read-out. Existing supervised methods require paired ground-truth data that is impractical to collect from real CSC hardware, while learning-free methods fail to jointly address all three degradations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N=41 binary (0/1) spike frames in RGGB Bayer pattern at 128×128 spatial resolution, along with the corresponding 3×H×W binary color mask indicating which pixels belong to each channel. It produces a 3×H×W color image with pixel values in [0,1]. The binary nature of the input means each pixel at each time step is simply a fired/not-fired flag, and the color mask is fixed by the sensor's CFA layout.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline. First, a motion-guided preliminary reconstruction jointly estimates optical flow across all three color channels (adapting Spike2Flow for Bayer patterns via linear interpolation of missing pixels and averaging flows across channels), then fills missing pixel positions by warping temporally neighboring same-color pixels along the estimated motion trajectories. Second, a self-supervised CNN uses the preliminary reconstruction as pseudo-labels; its Masked Spike Encoder (MSE) employs masked 3D partial convolution (MPConv) to extract per-channel color and temporal features from the binary stream, and temporal masked convolution (TMConv) with a temporal blind spot to suppress the temporally correlated quantization noise identified through Pearson correlation analysis. The two convolution branches (standard 3DConv and TMConv) handle spatially independent and temporally correlated noise respectively, and their fused features are decoded via residual blocks into the final color image.

## Results

On the synthetic RBSS dataset (α=0.6), the proposed self-supervised method achieves 32.90 dB PSNR, 0.8583 SSIM, and 0.1949 LPIPS, the best among all methods without ground-truth supervision. On DBSS (α=0.6), it reaches 34.89 dB PSNR and 0.8969 SSIM. On the GBSS dataset with random brightness (α∈[0.6,1.0]), the method attains 35.73 dB PSNR and 0.9323 SSIM while using the fewest parameters (1.05 M) and FLOPs (3226.3 G) among all learning-based methods. On real-world BSS data, the method produces visually cleaner results with less noise than the SOTA supervised methods SJDD-Net and CSpkNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed self-supervised method (Ours) outperforms all learning-free baselines: it exceeds the best LF method Ours_mg by ~1.5 dB PSNR (32.90 vs. 31.42 on RBSS α=0.6) and 3DRI by ~3.3 dB. Against the prior self-supervised baseline SJRE [4], the improvement is substantial—6.14 dB PSNR on RBSS (32.90 vs. 26.76) and 8.50 dB on DBSS (34.89 vs. 26.39) at α=0.6—largely attributed to the motion-guided pseudo-labels and the masked spike encoder. However, on synthetic data the method does not surpass the supervised baselines: CSpkNet [13] leads by 2.72 dB on RBSS (35.62 vs. 32.90) and SJDD-Net [12] by 2.96 dB on DBSS (37.85 vs. 34.89) at α=0.6. The color-independent strategy (WGSE*, SJRE*) consistently yields higher PSNR than the end-to-end variant, confirming the benefit of per-channel processing. On real-world BSS data, the self-supervised method achieves better visual quality (less noise, sharper details) than both SOTA supervised methods SJDD-Net and CSpkNet, demonstrating a practical advantage despite lower synthetic-data metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the grayscale spike camera reconstruction literature to the color (Bayer-pattern) setting by adapting Spike2Flow [42] for joint motion estimation across color channels and repurposing partial convolution [27] as a 3D masked operation for color-channel-specific feature extraction. It departs from prior supervised CSC methods (SJDD-Net, CSpkNet) that rely on synthetic ground truth by introducing a self-supervised pipeline where motion-guided reconstruction produces pseudo-labels, and from blind-spot denoising [24] by shifting the masking from spatial to temporal dimensions based on a novel quantization-noise correlation analysis. The motion-guided pixel-filling strategy also contrasts with end-to-end CNN approaches that treat demosaicing and denoising as a single black-box problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised formulation addresses a critical practical limitation: real-world spike streams rarely come with high-quality ground truth, yet supervised methods trained on synthetic data suffer from a domain gap. The paper demonstrates that a self-supervised method can outperform supervised baselines on real captured data, validating the pseudo-label strategy. However, the method still trails supervised approaches on synthetic benchmarks, and the pipeline's reliance on accurate optical flow estimation (inherited from Spike2Flow) may limit robustness under extreme motion or low-light conditions. Open questions include whether the temporal blind-spot design generalizes to other neuromorphic sensors with similar quantization artifacts, and whether the motion-guided filling strategy scales to higher-resolution or multi-frame CSC streams.
