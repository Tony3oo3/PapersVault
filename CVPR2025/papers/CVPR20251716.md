## Problem

Existing subject-driven image editing methods preserve semantic consistency of the replaced object but fail to simulate photorealistic contextual interactions—reflections, illumination, and shadows—induced by merging the target into the source scene. Two-stage remove-then-insert pipelines suffer from inconsistencies between stages, while one-stage diffusion methods lack explicit physical control over these effects, producing implausible or missing shadows and lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251716/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image (with foreground object and background), a reference image containing the target subject, a source object mask, and a target mask that specifies the location and size of the replacement within the source. It produces a single edited image where the source object is replaced by the reference subject while contextual interactions (shadows, reflections, lighting) are consistently updated both within and outside the mask region. The pipeline operates on Stable Diffusion V2.1 latents and requires auxiliary per-pixel maps (normal, shading, albedo) extracted via off-the-shelf intrinsic image decomposition and normal estimation.

## Method

PS-Diffusion disentangles contextual interactions into foreground effects (within the mask, e.g., lighting) and background effects (outside the mask, e.g., shadows, reflections), each guided by a dedicated control branch. The Foreground Effect Condition Branch (FECB) estimates source lighting parameters via intrinsic image decomposition, reshades the reference object's normal map under that lighting, and fuses the result with the DINOv2 reference embedding to condition cross-attention. The Background Effect Control Branch (BECB) predicts a soft effect map via a U-Net (guided by a rough LAB-color-space mask) and feeds it through a second ControlNet branch to identify where background pixels need modification. An Effect Attention Module (EAM) inserted after cross-attention in the U-Net decoder uses the background effect features as query and the foreground effect features as key/value, decoupling the learning of scene interactions from subject appearance to prevent mutual interference. The model is trained end-to-end on Replace-5K, a 5,000-pair synthetic dataset rendered with Blender's Cycles engine to provide physically consistent ground-truth interactions.

## Results

On Replace-5K, PS-Diffusion achieves the best performance across all 7 metrics: PSNR 22.583, CLIP 0.945, DINO 0.921, LPIPS 0.174, FID 5.109, CLIP-I 0.827, and DINO-I 0.688. The strongest baseline (Remove-Insert) trails by 1.093 PSNR, 0.043 LPIPS, and 1.132 FID. On real-world DreamBooth and DreamEdit test sets, PS-Diffusion also leads on all three metrics (CLIP-I, DINO-I, FID), e.g., FID 22.919 on DreamBooth vs. 23.607 for Remove-Insert.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PS-Diffusion outperforms all six baselines on every metric in both Replace-5K and the real-world DreamBooth/DreamEdit evaluations. The closest competitor is the two-stage Remove-Insert pipeline (PowerPaint inpainting + PCT-Net harmonization), which achieves PSNR 21.490 and FID 6.241 on Replace-5K but is limited to the masked region and cannot generate shadows or reflections outside the mask. Among one-stage methods, AnyDoor* (fine-tuned on Replace-5K) is the strongest, reaching PSNR 19.473 and DINO-I 0.638, yet it still produces incorrect shadow directions and spurious shadows in qualitative comparisons. IP-Adapter, PBE*, and ObjectStitch* (all fine-tuned) lag substantially, with PBE* showing the worst FID (15.803) and lowest CLIP score (0.730), indicating poor perceptual quality. On DreamBooth, PS-Diffusion improves FID by 0.688 over Remove-Insert (22.919 vs. 23.607) and DINO-I by 0.004, while on DreamEdit the FID gap widens to 0.808 (23.383 vs. 24.191), confirming consistent gains on real-world data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends subject-driven image editing (SDIE) methods like Paint-by-Example, AnyDoor, and ObjectStitch, which focus on semantic consistency of the replaced subject but largely ignore physical contextual interactions (shadows, reflections, illumination) between the object and scene. It builds on Stable Diffusion with ControlNet for spatial control and on intrinsic image decomposition for illumination estimation, but departs from prior two-stage remove-then-insert pipelines by handling all contextual effects in a single end-to-end pass. The key conceptual departure is disentangling effects into foreground (within-mask, lighting) and background (outside-mask, shadow/reflection) channels, each guided by a dedicated ControlNet branch, and introducing an Effect Attention Module to prevent the denoising U-Net from conflating interaction learning with subject identity preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in SDIE: existing methods produce semantically correct but physically implausible composites, and the Replace-5K dataset (5,000 Blender-rendered pairs with ground-truth physical effects) provides a much-needed benchmark for evaluating photorealism in this setting. However, the method still struggles with strong specularity and extreme lighting (FID degrades from 5.109 to 8.781 on hard samples), and the reliance on off-the-shelf intrinsic decomposition and normal estimation limits robustness. Open questions include whether more expressive lighting models or explicit material/normal priors in the background branch could close the remaining gap, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the controlled 3D-rendered training distribution to fully unconstrained real-world scenes.
