## Problem

Multimodal table understanding (MTU) is constrained by the limited scale of existing datasets (~232K samples), and the two prevailing annotation strategies—human labeling and MLLM-based synthesis—fail to simultaneously balance efficiency, cost, and robustness. MLLM synthesis in particular suffers from hallucinations because the model must parse complex table images through visual tokens, leading to structural misinterpretations and inaccurate Q&A pairs that degrade downstream model training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250114/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a table image (resized to 1536×1536 for the high-resolution branch and 336×336 for the low-resolution branch) paired with a free-form text question, and produces a text answer that includes both a detailed step-by-step reasoning chain and a brief final answer. The hybrid encoder outputs exactly 576 visual tokens (concatenated from two 576-token streams), keeping the LLM's self-attention cost identical to standard LLaVA-1.5 despite the 16× higher input resolution.

## Method

The paper introduces a decoupled synthesis pipeline that separates MTU annotation into (1) table image rendering from structured code (HTML/LaTeX/Markdown) with diverse data augmentation, and (2) Q&A pair generation by an LLM (Doubao) operating directly on the code string rather than the rendered image, yielding 636K images and 1.8M samples for under $200. For the model, SynTab-LLaVA employs a hybrid multi-resolution vision encoder: a five-stage ConvNeXt at 1536×1536 captures local OCR content (576 tokens × 3072 dim), while a ViT-L/14 at 336×336 captures global cell-to-cell spatial relationships (576 tokens × 1024 dim); the two feature maps are concatenated along the channel dimension to form 576 tokens × 4096 dim, projected via a two-layer MLP into Vicuna-1.5 7B. Training follows a two-stage protocol—pre-training on 636K table-recognition samples plus 558K general image-text pairs, then instruction fine-tuning on 1.8M SynTab-SFT samples and 232K manually annotated MTU samples using LoRA (rank 128) on the LLM.

## Results

SynTab-LLaVA (7B, 1536×1536 input) achieves SOTA on 21 out of 24 in-domain and out-of-domain table understanding benchmarks. On TQA, it reaches 88.30% on TABMWP (vs. 60.50% for GPT-4V High-res and 57.78% for Table-LLaVA 7B), 51.94% on TAT-QA, and 70.55% on TabMCQ. On TFV, it scores 70.78% on TabFact and 69.42% on InfoTabs, surpassing GPT-4V High-res (45.50% and 65.60%). On TSU, it achieves 50.29% TCE accuracy, 60.80% TCL accuracy, and 74.58/79.17/76.42 TEDS on HTML/Markdown/LaTeX table recognition, all substantially ahead of prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynTab-LLaVA outperforms GPT-4V High-res on 14 of 20 reported benchmarks, with the largest margins on TABMWP (+27.8), TAT-QA (+19.4), TabFact (+25.3), and ToTTo (+34.6 BLEU). Against the strongest open-source tabular model Table-LLaVA 7B, SynTab-LLaVA leads on nearly all TQA and TFV benchmarks (e.g., TABMWP 88.30 vs. 57.78, TabFact 70.78 vs. 59.85) and on all T2T benchmarks (ToTTo 34.60 vs. 23.00). The one notable exception is AIT-QA, where Qwen2-VL 7B (67.12) and IX2.5 (51.08) surpass SynTab-LLaVA (28.57), which the authors attribute to excessive padding of wide, short table images. On TSU (Table 2), SynTab-LLaVA achieves SOTA on all in-domain benchmarks (e.g., TCE 50.29 vs. 19.45 for Table-LLaVA 7B; TR HTML TEDS 74.58 vs. 50.24) and also leads on out-of-domain splits, confirming strong generalization from the diverse rendered images in SynTab-Pre.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA two-stage training paradigm (pre-training + instruction fine-tuning) and the Table-LLaVA formulation of multimodal table understanding (MTU) with its 24-benchmark evaluation suite. The key conceptual departure is the decoupled synthesis strategy: rather than feeding table images to an MLLM for Q&A generation (as in TabPedia and other MLLM-based synthesis), the authors use structured table codes (HTML, LaTeX, Markdown) as an intermediary, separating image rendering from question generation. Architecturally, the hybrid multi-resolution encoder (ConvNeXt at 1536×1536 for local OCR + ViT-L/14 at 336×336 for global structure) departs from both the single low-resolution LLaVA approach and the patch-cropping strategies of Monkey, maintaining 576 visual tokens while capturing both fine-grained text and spatial relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of SynTab as the first million-level open-source MTU dataset (1.8M samples vs. 232K previously available) addresses a critical data bottleneck in table understanding research, and the 60× cost reduction ($200 vs. $12,000) makes large-scale MTU data synthesis accessible to academic labs. The decoupling insight—that structured code is a more reliable input than images for LLM-based Q&A generation—offers a generalizable principle for other structured-data synthesis tasks. However, the paper acknowledges that synthetic annotations contain errors and that performance degrades substantially on industrial table images with rotation, occlusion, or perspective distortion, leaving open the question of how to bridge the gap between clean rendered tables and real-world document scans.
