## Problem

Point-based models (PointNet, PointNet++, DGCNN) significantly underperform projection-based methods in 3D gait recognition from LiDAR point clouds, despite the latter's computational overhead and loss of size information during 3D-to-2D projection. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) point-based architectures rely on global max pooling that discards fine-grained local structure critical for gait discrimination, and (2) standard point cloud normalization erases body size, a strong prior for distinguishing pedestrians of similar shape but different height.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250790/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a temporal sequence of unordered LiDAR point clouds P ∈ R^{T×N×3} (T frames, N=1024 points for SUSTech1K or N=256 for FreeGait) and produces a single sequence-level identity descriptor via temporal pooling followed by a fully connected layer. Point cloud normalization is applied as data augmentation during both training and inference, while the size-aware mechanism selectively preserves raw altitude (z-axis) information to retain actual body scale. Training uses a combined triplet loss (weight 1) and cross-entropy loss (weight 0.1) with Euclidean distance for retrieval evaluation.

## Method

LidarGait++ stacks four Set Abstraction layers (with FPS sampling, kNN grouping, and shared MLP encoding) as a point feature encoder that captures local geometric structure hierarchically. The encoded features are aggregated by a novel Pyramid Point Pooling (P³) layer that partitions 128 points into 31 bins across 5 pyramid scales along the z-axis, pooling each bin with mean+max followed by an FC layer to produce multi-scale local descriptors. A size-aware learning mechanism is injected at two levels: relative altitudes are concatenated into the SA encoding layer (Eq. 2), and the P³ layer uses a Highest Altitude Partition strategy that aligns all subjects at ground level rather than normalizing head-to-foot, preserving actual body size. This design transfers the locality principles (horizontal bin partitioning and local convolution) that make projection-based LidarGait successful into a native point-based architecture, achieving 92.7% rank-1 on SUSTech1K and 82.0% on FreeGait.

## Results

On SUSTech1K (cross-view protocol), LidarGait++ achieves 92.7% rank-1 and 98.2% rank-5 overall accuracy, outperforming the best projection-based method HMRNet (90.2%) by +2.5% and LidarGait (86.8%) by +5.9%. On FreeGait (self-domain), LidarGait++ reaches 82.0% rank-1, 93.6% rank-5, and 87.2% mAP, surpassing HMRNet (80.8% rank-1) and LidarGait (74.2% rank-1). In the cross-domain setting (SUSTech1K→FreeGait), LidarGait++ obtains 15.1% rank-1 and 19.7% mAP, improving over the PointNet++ baseline by +4.4% rank-1 but trailing LidarGait (23.0%) due to domain gap in point density.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LidarGait++ outperforms all 13 compared baselines on both SUSTech1K and FreeGait self-domain evaluations. Against the strongest projection-based competitors, it beats HMRNet by +2.5% rank-1 on SUSTech1K and +1.2% on FreeGait, and surpasses LidarGait by +5.9% and +7.8% respectively. Over the point-based PointNet++ baseline, gains are substantial: +15.6% rank-1 on SUSTech1K and +22.7% on FreeGait, with the largest relative improvement in the clothing-changing subset (+21.0% on SUSTech1K, from 58.7% to 79.7%). Among other point-based methods (PointNet, PointTransformer, DGCNN, PointGait), LidarGait++ leads by 35–67% rank-1 on SUSTech1K. The one setting where LidarGait++ underperforms is cross-domain transfer (SUSTech1K→FreeGait), where it scores 15.1% rank-1 versus LidarGait's 23.0%, attributed to the large point-density gap (800 vs. 200 points/frame) that projection-based methods bridge more effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PointNet++'s Set Abstraction layers as its point feature encoder and adapts the Horizontal Pyramid Pooling concept from 2D gait recognition (GaitSet) and person re-identification (HPP) into a 3D point-cloud-native form (Pyramid Point Pooling). It departs from the dominant projection-based paradigm established by LidarGait and HMRNet, which convert point clouds into depth/voxel grids before applying CNNs, by processing unordered point sets end-to-end. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of size-aware learning—injecting relative altitudes into both the encoding and pooling stages—which prior point-based gait methods entirely neglected by normalizing all subjects to a unified scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for why point-based architectures (PointNet, PointNet++, DGCNN) dramatically underperform projection-based methods in 3D gait recognition: the absence of fine-grained local feature aggregation and size awareness. The plug-and-play nature of the P3 layer and size-aware encoding makes these components broadly applicable to other point-based recognition tasks. However, cross-domain generalization remains a significant open problem—LidarGait++ still trails projection-based methods when transferring between datasets with different point densities—and the lack of explicit temporal modeling and the computational cost of iterative FPS in SA layers limit practical deployment.
