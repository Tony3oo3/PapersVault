## Problem

Existing camouflaged image generation methods (e.g., LAKE-RED) produce images with missing background textures, overly salient objects, or distorted backgrounds, limiting their utility for training camouflaged object segmentation models. Additionally, standard image quality metrics like FID and KID fail to capture camouflage-specific quality, as they penalize highly camouflaged generations whose distributions differ from reference sets. This paper is the first to combine Controlled Out-painting with Representation Engineering to generate realistic camouflaged images from arbitrary segmented objects in generic or salient databases.

## Input / Output

The method takes a segmented object image (H×W×3 RGB) and its binary foreground mask as input, constructing a control image by preserving foreground pixels and replacing background pixels with the mean background color (during training) or mean foreground color (during inference). The output is a full-resolution camouflaged image where the object is embedded in a generated background that matches its appearance. At inference, since ground-truth text descriptions are unavailable, CLIP is used to retrieve the nearest text representation from a source image to drive the Representation Enhancement module.

## Method

The pipeline builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with a ControlNet-style trainable copy of the 12 encoder blocks and middle block, connected via zero-convolution layers, trained with the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss conditioned on a spatially localized control image. The Controlled Out-painting (CO) module out-paints a camouflaged background around the preserved foreground object, while the Representation Enhancement (RE) module computes a contrast vector between CLIP embeddings of a source and null text statement, applies a scaled attention mechanism to produce an updated representation, and blends it back into the original prompt embedding (α=0.1) for cross-attention conditioning in the U-Net. The paper also introduces CamOT, a camouflage-specific metric computing the second Wasserstein distance between foreground and background Gaussian Mixture Models (fitted via EM over a 30-pixel boundary region), normalized through a sigmoid to yield a score where higher values indicate better camouflage. Finally, LoRA adapters are inserted into the BiRefNet decoder and fine-tuned on 10,000 generated images to improve segmentation of general camouflaged objects beyond the COD10K training distribution.

## Results

On the LAKE-RED test set (6,473 image-mask pairs), the proposed CO+RE-BG configuration achieves an overall FID of 40.53 and KID of 0.0155, substantially outperforming the previous SOTA LAKE-RED (FID 64.27, KID 0.0355). Per-split, the method reaches FID 22.30 / KID 0.0039 on Camouflaged Objects, FID 61.78 / KID 0.0211 on Salient Objects, and FID 74.53 / KID 0.0387 on General Objects. Additionally, the proposed CamOT metric shows that the CO+RE+BG variant achieves the highest camouflage quality (e.g., 0.8975 mean on COD vs. 0.7922 for LAKE-RED), confirming that FID/KID alone under-reward true camouflage quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 baselines on both FID and KID across every dataset split. Against the closest competitor LAKE-RED (CVPR 2024), the gap is substantial: overall FID drops from 64.27 to 40.53 (−37%) and KID from 0.0355 to 0.0155 (−56%), with the largest relative gains on Camouflaged Objects (FID 39.55→22.30) and General Objects (FID 102.67→74.53). Image Blending baselines (AB, CI, AdaIN, DCI, LCGNet) are uniformly the weakest, with overall FIDs ranging from 120 to 131, roughly 3× worse than the proposed method. Among Image Inpainting baselines, TFill (FID 80.39) and LDM (FID 84.48) are intermediate, while RePaint-L (FID 96.14) performs worst in that group. On the CamOT metric (Table 3), the CO+RE+BG variant achieves the highest camouflage scores (e.g., 0.9244 on COD vs. 0.7922 for LAKE-RED), confirming that the method produces genuinely more camouflaged images rather than merely matching reference distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-based image generation and camouflage synthesis, building directly on ControlNet's zero-convolution conditioning architecture and Stable Diffusion's latent U-Net as its generative backbone. It departs from the prior camouflage generation lineage (hand-crafted blending → GAN-based composition → LAKE-RED's retrieval-augmented diffusion) by reframing the problem as a controlled out-painting task where the foreground is preserved and the background is synthesized to match, augmented by a CLIP-based representation engineering module that injects contrastive semantic conditioning via cross-attention. The secondary contribution of LoRA fine-tuning BiRefNet connects this generation pipeline to the downstream segmentation task, extending the utility of the generated data beyond mere dataset augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in camouflaged object detection research: the scarcity and high annotation cost of realistic camouflage datasets. The CamOT metric is a useful diagnostic that exposes a blind spot in FID/KID evaluation—highly camouflaged generations can score worse on traditional metrics despite being visually superior—giving the field a more targeted quality signal. However, the method inherits limitations from Stable Diffusion 1.5 (poor handling of human subjects, occasional implausible scene compositions), and the CamOT metric's reliance on a fixed 30-pixel boundary region and empirically tuned temperature parameter raises questions about its robustness across object scales and scene types. Whether the LoRA fine-tuning gains on SOD/SEG transfers to truly novel camouflage domains beyond the generated distribution remains an open question.
