## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models produce repetitive patterns and structural distortions when generating images at resolutions beyond their training resolution, and retraining at higher resolutions is computationally prohibitive. Existing solutions either rely on patch-based generation with large latency overheads or modify the UNet architecture at the cost of image quality. Additionally, inconsistent local texture patterns across semantically related regions—a problem largely overlooked in prior work—remain unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt and produces a high-resolution RGB image at 2×, 3×, or 4× the native training resolution (e.g., 2048×2048, 3072×3072, or 4096×4096 for SDXL trained at 1024×1024). Generation is performed in a single forward pass over the full target-resolution latent, avoiding the redundant overlapping patch-based inference used by prior methods. The approach is model-agnostic and integrates into any latent diffusion model without architectural modification or additional training.

## Method

FAM diffusion operates in two stages: first generating an image at native resolution, then performing a test-time diffuse-denoise process where the upsampled native latent is re-noised and denoised at the target resolution. The Frequency Modulation (FM) module replaces the skip residual by working in the Fourier domain: it selectively transfers low-frequency coefficients from the forward-diffused latent to guide global structure, while leaving high-frequency coefficients fully under the denoising process's control, implemented as a time-varying circular convolution in the spatial domain. The Attention Modulation (AM) module addresses local texture inconsistency by upscaling attention maps from the native-resolution denoising process and blending them into the high-resolution UNet's self-attention (specifically in up_block_0), regularizing which regions attend to each other based on semantically correct relationships learned at native resolution. Both modules are training-free and add only marginal latency overhead (0.2–0.7 min) compared to direct high-resolution inference.

## Results

Using SDXL as the backbone, FAM diffusion achieves the best FIDc, KIDc, and CLIP score across all tested scale factors (2×, 3×, 4×). At the most challenging 4× scale (4096×4096), SDXL+FAM diffusion reaches FIDc=43.65, KIDc=0.0130, and CLIP=32.33, substantially outperforming direct SDXL inference (FIDc=74.37, CLIP=26.70) and all prior methods. Latency overheads are minimal: only 0.2, 0.3, and 0.7 minutes above direct inference at 2×, 3×, and 4× respectively, compared to 14.2 minutes added by DemoFusion at 4×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAM diffusion outperforms all five baselines on FIDc, KIDc, and CLIP at every scale factor. Against the patch-based methods (DemoFusion, AccDiffusion, FouriScale), FAM achieves both better quality and substantially lower latency — e.g., at 4× scale, SDXL+FAM reaches FIDc=43.65 in 6.1 min versus DemoFusion's 48.44 in 19.6 min and FouriScale's 70.45 in 14.7 min. Against the single-pass HiDiffusion, FAM trades a small latency increase (6.1 vs 2.8 min at 4×) for a dramatic quality gain (FIDc 43.65 vs 156.98, CLIP 32.33 vs 24.32), and can also be plugged into HiDiffusion itself to improve it (FIDc 156.98→44.65). Compared to direct SDXL inference at high resolution, FAM consistently improves FIDc by 15–31 points and CLIP by 3–6 points, with only 0.2–0.7 min additional latency. The gap over baselines widens with scale factor, indicating that the frequency and attention modulation mechanisms become increasingly critical as the resolution gap from training grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the test-time diffuse-denoise strategy introduced by DemoFusion and AccDiffusion, which uses a native-resolution image to steer high-resolution generation, but replaces their indiscriminate skip-residual conditioning with a Fourier-domain frequency modulation that selectively transfers only low-frequency (global structure) information. It also draws on the frequency-domain perspective of FouriScale and the attention-swapping mechanism from PhotoSwap/SwapAnything, repurposing the latter to transfer semantic attention maps from native to high-resolution denoising. Unlike patch-based methods (DemoFusion, AccDiffusion) or architecture-modifying single-pass methods (ScaleCrafter, HiDiffusion), FAM diffusion requires no architectural changes, no retraining, and operates in a single forward pass, positioning itself as a lightweight plug-in for any latent diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a training-free, architecture-agnostic module with negligible latency overhead that achieves state-of-the-art high-resolution generation makes flexible-resolution diffusion accessible without prohibitive compute. The identification of local texture inconsistency as a distinct failure mode (separate from global structure) and its resolution via attention modulation is a genuinely new observation that prior frequency-domain or patch-based methods overlooked. Open questions include whether the AM module generalizes to non-UNet architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, and whether the frequency modulation can be made adaptive per semantic region rather than globally.
