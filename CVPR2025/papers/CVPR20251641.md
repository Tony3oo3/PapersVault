## Problem

Current pose-driven human image animation models built on video diffusion suffer from severe facial distortion and identity loss because temporal modeling layers alter the spatial distribution that image-domain ID-preserving methods depend on. Existing work patches this with third-party face-swapping tools (e.g., FaceFusion) as post-processing, which degrades overall video fidelity. No end-to-end video diffusion framework existed that could synthesize high-quality animated videos while preserving identity without any external post-processing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image and a sequence of skeleton poses (extracted via DWPose) as input, and produces a temporally consistent animated human video at 576×1024 resolution (16 frames per clip). The reference image is consumed through three parallel pathways: a frozen VAE encoder produces latent codes concatenated with noisy latents, a CLIP image encoder yields global appearance embeddings, and ArcFace extracts face embeddings that are further refined. No post-processing (face swapping or restoration) is applied to the output.

## Method

StableAnimator builds on Stable Video Diffusion and introduces three components. First, a Global Content-aware Face Encoder refines ArcFace embeddings via cross-attention with CLIP image embeddings, giving face features awareness of the reference's global layout. Second, a Distribution-aware ID Adapter performs separate cross-attention of U-Net latents with image and face embeddings, then aligns the two outputs by matching their mean and standard deviation before temporal layers, preventing distribution-shift interference from degrading ID signals. Third, at inference, a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation-based face optimization is integrated into the EDM denoising loop: at selected timesteps, the predicted latent is optimized for 10 Adam steps to minimize ArcFace cosine distance to the reference, and the optimized latent steers the subsequent denoising step, effectively constraining the denoising path toward ID consistency. Training uses a reconstruction loss weighted by ArcFace-extracted face masks, with the U-Net, Face Encoder, and PoseNet trained from SVD pre-trained weights on 3K internet-collected videos.

## Results

On the TikTok dataset, StableAnimator achieves a CSIM of 0.831 and FVD of 140.62, outperforming the strongest competitor Unianimate (CSIM 0.479, FVD 148.06) by 36.9% in face similarity and 5.0% in video fidelity. On the Unseen100 dataset, the gap widens: StableAnimator reaches CSIM 0.805 and FVD 349.94 versus Unianimate's 0.347 and 394.32, a 45.8% CSIM improvement. The paper highlights a 47.1% CSIM gain over ControlNeXt (0.360) on TikTok while simultaneously achieving the best FVD score among all methods. Single-frame quality metrics (PSNR 30.81, SSIM 0.801, LPIPS 0.232 on TikTok) are also competitive or best-in-class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableAnimator dominates all eight animation baselines on both CSIM (face identity) and FVD (video fidelity) simultaneously. Against the closest competitor Unianimate [52], it improves CSIM by 36.9% (TikTok) and 45.8% (Unseen100) while also achieving lower FVD (140.62 vs. 148.06; 349.94 vs. 394.32). Against ControlNeXt [35], the CSIM gap is 47.1% on TikTok (0.831 vs. 0.360), and FVD improves from 326.57 to 140.62. GAN-based MRAA [40] and early diffusion methods (DisCo [49], MimicMotion [68]) trail substantially on all metrics, with CSIM below 0.32 and FVD above 280. MagicAnimate [61] and AnimateAnyone [22] achieve moderate CSIM (~0.46) but still fall far short of StableAnimator's 0.831. In the face-enhancement ablation (Table 3), IP-Adapter [65] improves CSIM to 0.511 but degrades FVD to 484.77, while FaceFusion [15] post-processing reaches CSIM 0.798 at the cost of FVD 405.16—both inferior to StableAnimator's end-to-end approach (CSIM 0.805, FVD 349.94). A user study across 30 videos confirms these quantitative gains, with StableAnimator preferred over every competitor by 88–99% of participants on motion, appearance, background, and ID alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableAnimator builds on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its video diffusion backbone and adopts AnimateAnyone's PoseNet architecture for pose conditioning, following the established recipe of recent animation models. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) rather than relying on post-processing tools like FaceFusion (as ControlNeXt and MimicMotion do) or naively inserting image-domain ID adapters like IP-Adapter, it introduces a distribution-aware ID Adapter that aligns face and image embedding distributions before temporal modeling to avoid spatial feature distortion; (2) it integrates Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman optimal control theory into the diffusion denoising process for face optimization, a connection not previously explored in generative modeling. The theoretical grounding draws on EDM's denoising framework and Tweedie's formula to show the HJB solution is structurally equivalent to the SDE denoising step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end ID-preserving video diffusion framework that eliminates reliance on third-party face-swapping post-processing, addressing a practical quality bottleneck in current animation pipelines. The HJB-diffusion equivalence provides a principled framework for incorporating task-specific optimization objectives into the denoising trajectory, potentially generalizable beyond face identity to other attribute preservation tasks. Limitations include training on only 3K internet-collected videos, evaluation primarily on a single public benchmark (TikTok) plus a self-collected set, and the HJB optimization adding 10 inner-loop steps per denoising iteration, increasing inference cost. Open questions include whether the distribution alignment strategy transfers to other conditioning signals (e.g., expression, style) and whether the HJB formulation scales to longer sequences or higher resolutions.
