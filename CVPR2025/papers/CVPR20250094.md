## Problem

Current generative models struggle to capture multi-scale visual structures, from local textures to globally coherent scene layout, because conventional training objectives do not explicitly enforce semantic consistency across scales. Existing hierarchical approaches either rely on low-level feature pyramids (VAE bottlenecks, image resolutions) or hierarchical VAEs that suffer from training instability and representation collapse. This paper asks whether anchoring a diffusion-based generator to a frozen, multi-scale semantic feature hierarchy can guide progressive synthesis from abstract semantics to fine visual details.

## Input / Output

The system takes Gaussian noise at each of L hierarchical levels (2–5) as input, optionally augmented with a class label or text prompt for conditional generation. The bottom level operates in the 32×32×4 VAE latent space (corresponding to 256×256 RGB images), while the top level is a single 256-dimensional vector; intermediate levels have shapes like 4×128 and 16×64. The output is a 256×256 RGB image, generated by sequentially denoising from the most abstract (top) level down to the pixel-level (bottom) level, with each level's diffusion model conditioned on all higher-level latents.

## Method

The core architecture is a stack of L diffusion models (each a U-ViT with 12 transformer blocks, 768 channels, 12 heads) where level l generates latent z_l ∈ R^{d_l} conditioned on all higher-level latents z_{>l}, forming a non-Markovian generative chain. Hierarchical target latents are constructed by running a frozen pre-trained visual encoder (MoCo-v3 or CLIP ViT-B/16) on progressively smaller image patches, so that top levels encode global semantics and bottom levels capture local detail. Two capacity controls prevent the generator from degenerating into an autoencoder: SVD-based channel trimming (preserving only d/(L−l+1) leading channels per patch) and Gaussian noise injection (ẑ_l ~ N(z_l, σ_l²I)) during training. The training objective is a sum of per-level diffusion losses (Eq. 3), and a top-down greedy search strategy allows efficient hyperparameter tuning by reusing parameters from shallower models. At generation time, a gradual noise decay schedule (t/T)^γ · σ_l is applied to conditional features to maintain train-test consistency, particularly under classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256, the 5-level nested diffusion model (L=5, σ₂=1.0) achieves FID 9.87 (w/o CFG) and 3.97 (w/ CFG) for class-conditional generation, and FID 11.05 (w/o CFG) and 5.03 (w/ CFG) for unconditional generation, at only 34.0 GFlops. Notably, the unconditional model (FID 11.05) surpasses the single-level conditional baseline (FID 31.13). On COCO-2014 text-to-image generation, the L=2 model achieves FID 4.72 (w/ CFG) and the L=3 model achieves FID 6.97 (w/o CFG), outperforming U-ViT (5.42 w/ CFG, 14.98 w/o CFG) trained on the same 83K COCO images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against U-ViT (Mid) at 26.8 GFlops, the nested model (34.0 GFlops) reduces conditional FID from 31.13 to 9.87 (w/o CFG) and 13.75 to 3.97 (w/ CFG), and unconditional FID from 55.41 to 11.05. Against the parameter-matched U-ViT (Mid*) at 35.1 GFlops, the nested model (34.0 GFlops) improves conditional FID from 19.74 to 9.87 and unconditional FID from 45.19 to 11.05. Against DiT-XL/2+REPA at 118.6 GFlops, the nested model matches or beats the 850k-step variant (FID 9.60 vs 9.87 w/o CFG) while using only 29% of the compute, and substantially outperforms the 400k-step variant (12.30). On COCO-2014, the L=2 model (FID 4.72 w/ CFG) outperforms U-ViT (5.42), Frido (8.97), and VQ-Diffusion (13.86) trained on the same data, and even beats the 7B-parameter CM3Leon-7B (4.88) and approaches Re-Imagen (5.25), while the L=3 model (FID 6.97 w/o CFG) far exceeds U-ViT (14.98) without CFG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the denoising diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) but departs from both hierarchical VAEs, which learn latent variables end-to-end and suffer from training instability and representation collapse, and multi-resolution diffusion models (Matryoshka, cascaded diffusion), which organize generation by spatial scale rather than semantic abstraction. The key conceptual departure is using frozen pre-trained visual encoder features (CLIP, MoCo-v3) at multiple patch scales as hierarchical conditioning signals, combined with SVD-based channel reduction and Gaussian noise injection to control information capacity at each level. This contrasts with prior work that uses pre-trained encoders only as a single-level conditioning signal (REPA, DiffAE, SODA) or as a training-time alignment loss, rather than as a multi-level generative scaffold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a five-level unconditional system (FID 11.05) outperforms a class-conditional single-level baseline (FID 19.74) on ImageNet-1K suggests that explicit semantic hierarchy can substitute for explicit class labels, which has implications for how we think about what information is needed for coherent image synthesis. The approach is computationally efficient (only ~25% more GFlops than single-level diffusion), making it practical to adopt. Open questions include whether the hierarchy can scale beyond five levels, whether the specific pre-trained encoder choice (CLIP vs. MoCo-v3 vs. DINO) matters as much as the hierarchical structure itself, and whether this framework extends to video or higher-resolution generation. A limitation is the reliance on hand-tuned per-level noise schedules and the fact that the strongest comparisons are against single-level baselines rather than the most competitive multi-level systems.
