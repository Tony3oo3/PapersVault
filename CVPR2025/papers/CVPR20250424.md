## Problem

Existing CLIP evaluation benchmarks exclusively test on visual concepts encountered during pre-training (In-Pre-training, IP), leaving unexplored how these models generalize to concepts entirely absent from the pre-training vocabulary (Out-of-Pre-training, OOP). This paper identifies that while OOP image features already exhibit strong clustering margins, zero-shot transfer catastrophically fails due to the text encoder's inability to produce aligned classification weights for unseen concept names.

## Input / Output

FSNL consumes 1–16 image-text pairs per OOP class plus a class-name vocabulary, initializing OOP token embeddings from BERT and augmenting training by shuffling caption contexts across semantically similar concept pairs under the same categorical branch. ZSNL operates without any OOP text labels: it takes unlabeled OOP images alongside labeled IP images (for NCD supervision) and a vocabulary of OOP class names, producing pseudo-labels via bipartite graph matching before fine-tuning. In both cases the output is a set of fine-tuned OOP name embeddings (all other parameters frozen) used for open-vocabulary classification of arbitrary RGB images.

## Method

The core mechanism is name-tuning: only the token embeddings of OOP concept names are optimized via InfoNCE loss while both the image encoder and text encoder remain frozen, a strategy theoretically justified by a generalization bound (Proposition 1) showing it controls the ideal population risk, and a non-identification risk (Proposition 2) showing that arbitrary OOP embedding initialization breaks prompt-tuning and adapter approaches. FSNL augments the limited few-shot pairs by swapping caption contexts across similar OOP concepts (e.g., replacing 'A woman in a [-]' with 'People are dressed in [-]') to increase data efficiency. ZSNL first clusters OOP images using a novel-class-discovery head supervised by IP-class images (backbone frozen), then assigns pseudo-labels by solving a bipartite matching problem (Hungarian algorithm) between normalized cluster centers and OOP prompt embeddings, and finally fine-tunes name embeddings on the high-confidence matched samples. The approach is evaluated on the newly proposed LAION-Beyond benchmark (674 OOP + 324 IP classes across 9 domains, with 400M/2B/5B splits) against CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIP-Adapter, Linear Probe, TaskRes, and TransCLIP.

## Results

On LAION-Beyond (400M) with 4-shot OOP few-shot learning, FSNL achieves 77.74% average OOP accuracy and 62.55% H-mean across 9 domains, substantially outperforming CLIP-Adapter (68.69% OOP, 54.86% H-mean), CoOp (58.34% OOP, 45.12% H-mean), and OpenCLIP zero-shot (18.67% OOP, 26.92% H-mean). Critically, FSNL preserves IP-class accuracy at 53.38% (identical to OpenCLIP), while prompt-tuning and adapter baselines degrade IP performance by 10–20 points. In the open-world vocabulary transfer task (Table 3), FSNL reaches 60.72% average accuracy, exceeding the second-best CLIP-Adapter by +7.52 points. For zero-shot OOP learning (Table 4), ZSNL improves over OpenCLIP by up to +43.1 points (Pokemon domain, 400M split) and +42.4 points (Pokemon, 5B split).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSNL decisively outperforms all baselines on OOP accuracy: it beats CLIP-Adapter (68.69%) by +9.05 points, CoOp (58.34%) by +19.40 points, Learning-to-Name (39.27%) by +38.47 points, CoCoOp (27.84%) by +49.90 points, and OpenCLIP zero-shot (18.67%) by +59.07 points on average. A key differentiator is IP preservation: FSNL maintains OpenCLIP's IP accuracy (53.38%) unchanged, whereas CoOp drops IP to 38.24%, CLIP-Adapter to 46.73%, and CoCoOp to 40.93%, reflecting the theoretical risk of non-identification identified in Proposition 2. In the open-world transfer setting (Table 3), FSNL leads by +7.52 points over CLIP-Adapter (53.2% vs. 60.72%), and notably CoCoOp (23.88%) underperforms even OpenCLIP zero-shot (24.7%). For zero-shot OOP learning (Table 4), ZSNL surpasses TransCLIP by +3.9 to +43.1 points across domains in the 400M split, and by +14.5 to +42.4 points in the 5B split, confirming that name-embedding tuning via bipartite graph matching addresses the image-text misalignment that TransCLIP's positive transfer cannot resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the large body of CLIP generalization studies (robustness, long-tail bias, train-test similarity) that implicitly evaluate only on In-Pre-training (IP) concepts, arguing that this masks true out-of-distribution performance. It builds directly on OpenCLIP's reproducible LAION-trained encoders as the backbone and borrows the novel-class-discovery framework (Choi et al., CVPR 2024) for ZSNL's cluster initialization. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant prompt-tuning (CoOp, CoCoOp) and adapter (CLIP-Adapter) paradigms by isolating the text-encoder token embeddings of OOP concepts as the sole tunable parameter, supported by a formal generalization bound (Proposition 1) and a non-identification risk argument (Proposition 2) that explains why arbitrary OOP embedding initialization undermines prompt/adapter approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAION-Beyond provides the first systematic benchmark that cleanly separates OOP from IP concepts relative to a specific pre-training corpus, filling a gap that has allowed prior evaluations to conflate in-distribution and out-of-distribution performance. The diagnostic finding that the image encoder already produces well-separated OOP clusters while the text encoder fails to align them reframes the OOP problem as a text-side alignment issue rather than a representation deficiency, guiding practitioners toward targeted name-tuning over expensive full-model fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the OOP/IP boundary defined by LAION's noisy vocabulary is stable across model scales, how the approach extends to truly compositional or multi-word OOP concepts, and whether the theoretical guarantees (distribution gap γ, Rademacher terms) remain tight in practice as the OOP concept space grows unboundedly.
