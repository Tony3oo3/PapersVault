## Problem

Existing learning-based point cloud rendering methods (NeRF-based or 3D Gaussian-based) still rely on categorical priors, dense point clouds (≥80K points), or post-hoc refinement of rendered images, limiting their generalization across object categories and their ability to handle sparse inputs. This paper tackles the challenge of rendering photo-realistic images from arbitrarily sparse point clouds (as few as 2K points) with direct generalization to unseen categories, without per-scene fine-tuning or image refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251769/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud (3D coordinates, RGB colors, and estimated surface normals) of 2K–100K points along with camera intrinsic and extrinsic matrices, and outputs a set of 2D Gaussians (each parameterized by position, 2D scale, opacity, spherical-harmonic color, surface normal, and rotation angle around the normal) that are directly rasterized via differentiable splatting into rendered images at 256×256, 640×512, or 512×512 resolution. No refinement network is applied to the rendered output, preserving the real-time rendering speed of 2D Gaussian Splatting (~143 FPS on RTX 3090).

## Method

The core architecture is an entire-patch design with two identical 2D Gaussian prediction modules: N_e processes the full point cloud to provide a rendered 'background,' while N_p processes 2048-point patches to enable category-agnostic generalization. Each module first initializes one 2D Gaussian per point using the point's position, estimated normal (for orientation), color (for spherical harmonics), and nearest-neighbor distance (for scale), then encodes local features via PointMLP. Six weight-shared MLP splitting decoders each predict K=4 shifts per Gaussian, duplicating every initial Gaussian into K copies with refined positions, scales, colors, normals, rotation angles, and opacities—effectively upsampling sparse inputs into denser Gaussian sets. Training proceeds in two stages (N_e first, then N_p with N_e frozen) using a combined MSE + SSIM loss (β=0.8) over 8 rendered views, and at inference N_p tiles the point cloud into overlapping patches whose predicted Gaussians are merged for final rendering.

## Results

On the standard 100K-point evaluation, the proposed method achieves 20.24 PSNR / 0.759 SSIM / 0.490 LPIPS on ScanNet, 28.73 / 0.960 / 0.060 on Car, 29.10 / 0.965 / 0.075 on Chair, 32.08 / 0.978 / 0.042 on Shoe, and 35.43 / 0.987 / 0.009 on THuman2.0, outperforming all baselines across every dataset and metric. The paper reports average improvements of 2.60% in PSNR, 1.05% in SSIM, and 6.06% in LPIPS over the strongest prior method. Rendering speed reaches 142.86 FPS, roughly 4× faster than NPBG++ (37.45 FPS) and ~38× faster than TriVol (1.62 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines (GR, NPBG++, TriVol, PFGS) on every dataset and every metric at the standard 100K-point setting. Against the strongest prior method PFGS, the gains are most pronounced on Shoe (+2.55 PSNR, +0.021 SSIM, −0.016 LPIPS) and Car (+1.39 PSNR, +0.018 SSIM, −0.017 LPIPS), while on THuman2.0 the PSNR lead is smaller (+0.69) but LPIPS ties at 0.009. TriVol is the second-strongest baseline on objects (e.g., 28.85 PSNR on Chair) but is surpassed by Ours by 0.25 PSNR there and by a wider margin on ScanNet (20.24 vs 18.56). NPBG++ requires paired images as input and lags by 3–5 PSNR on most categories. In terms of rendering speed, Ours achieves 142.86 FPS, making it ~4× faster than NPBG++ (37.45 FPS), ~38× faster than TriVol (1.62 FPS), and ~38× faster than PFGS (3.80 FPS), since no post-hoc image refinement is needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) as its representation primitive, repurposing its explicit normals to enable straightforward initialization and prediction from point clouds, in contrast to prior methods (TriVol, Point2Pix, PFGS) that predict NeRFs or 3D Gaussians. It departs from the entire-point-cloud paradigm of those methods by introducing an entire-patch architecture borrowed from surface reconstruction and semantic segmentation, combined with a novel splitting decoder that duplicates Gaussians to handle sparsity. The encoder leverages PointMLP for local feature extraction, and the overall pipeline is a supervised network that predicts 2D Gaussian parameters without requiring per-scene training or input images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves real-time rendering (142.86 FPS) without post-hoc image refinement while maintaining SOTA quality, and its patch-based generalization—trained on one object category with 20K points, it outperforms PFGS trained on the target scene dataset with 1M points—suggests a path toward category-agnostic point cloud rendering. A key limitation is that the method cannot synthesize content for regions where no input points exist, restricting its applicability to incomplete scans. Future work on point cloud completion and robustness to noisy outdoor scans would address these gaps and broaden practical utility in AR/VR and autonomous driving.
