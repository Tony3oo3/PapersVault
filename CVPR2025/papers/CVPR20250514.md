## Problem

Existing trajectory-guided video generation models (e.g., DragNUWA, DragAnything) constrain objects to follow paths in 2D pixel space without accounting for 6D pose changes, causing them to fail when trajectories implicitly involve rotations. This limitation stems from the scarcity of rotation-rich training data and the inherent ill-posedness of inferring 3D rotations from 2D trajectories alone.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial RGB frame at 320×576, a user-drawn trajectory rendered as a sequence of line-segment images (with last-frame empty padding), and optionally per-frame camera extrinsic parameters. It produces 14 animated video frames at 320×576 where the target object follows the trajectory with pose-aware motion. Multiple trajectories can be specified simultaneously, and camera poses are dropped 50% of the time during training so the model can operate without them at inference.

## Method

PoseTraj builds on Stable Video Diffusion with a Traj-ControlNet (a trainable copy of SVD's encoder blocks) that injects trajectory features via a 3D ConvNet guider. The key innovation is a two-stage pose-aware pretraining pipeline on the synthetic PoseTraj-10K dataset: Stage 1 jointly generates 3D bounding boxes in pixel space alongside objects to instill 6D pose awareness, and Stage 2 removes bbox supervision to refine appearance details. A camera-disentangled finetuning stage on VIPSeg adds camera poses as an MLP-encoded input (concatenated with trajectory features) to separate active object motion from passive camera movement. A spatial enhancement loss (L_SPA) enforces per-frame reconstruction accuracy to prevent object collapse under large rotations, and the 3D information is injected via reconstruction targets rather than conditioning, enabling clean removal at inference.

## Results

On VIPSeg at 320×576, PoseTraj achieves ObjMC 77.48, FID 38.41, and FVD 267.33, outperforming DragAnything (91.12, 39.29, 275.93) and DragNUWA 1.5 (133.05, 41.88, 289.15) across all three metrics. On the out-of-distribution DAVIS benchmark, the gap widens substantially: PoseTraj reaches ObjMC 29.92 versus 47.01 for DragAnything and 74.07 for DragNUWA, a 36% improvement over the strongest baseline. In user evaluation, PoseTraj receives 43% more votes for trajectory-following accuracy and 39% more for video quality compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoseTraj consistently outperforms both baselines on trajectory accuracy (ObjMC) across all three evaluation settings, with the largest margin on the OOD DAVIS benchmark (29.92 vs. 47.01 for DragAnything, a 36% reduction). On VIPSeg 256×256, the FID improvement over DragAnything is particularly striking (46.60 vs. 61.69, a 15-point gap), reflecting the benefit of the two-stage pose-aware pretraining for single-frame quality. On VIPSeg 320×576, PoseTraj edges out DragAnything on FID (38.41 vs. 39.29) and FVD (267.33 vs. 275.93), while on DAVIS it slightly trails DragAnything on FID (51.48 vs. 50.83) but leads on ObjMC and FVD. DragNUWA 1.5 is dominated across every metric in every setting, confirming that the entity-aware and pose-aware designs in PoseTraj address fundamental limitations of the earlier trajectory-guided approach. The paper notes that Tora and MotionCtrl were excluded from comparison due to unavailability of public SVD-pretrained weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trajectory-guided video generation line (DragNUWA, DragAnything) by addressing their core limitation: both constrain objects to 2D translational motion without modeling 6D pose changes. It builds on SVD as the base latent diffusion model and adapts the ControlNet paradigm into a Traj-ControlNet for trajectory conditioning. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage pose-aware pretraining pipeline that uses 3D bounding boxes as an intermediate reconstruction target (injection-by-reconstruction) rather than as a conditioning input, followed by camera-disentangled fine-tuning on real-world video. The PoseTraj-10K synthetic dataset, rendered in Blender from Objaverse models, provides the precise trajectory-and-bbox pairs that are infeasible to obtain from open-domain video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D pose supervision (via 3D bounding boxes) during pretraining meaningfully improves rotational trajectory following, and that camera disentanglement is necessary for robustness on real-world video with moving cameras. The injection-by-reconstruction design is a practical pattern: it avoids inference-time dependency on external 3D estimators while still imparting 3D awareness. Limitations include reliance on SVD's 14-frame/320×576 constraint, the 3D bbox being a coarse proxy for full 6D pose (no per-vertex or articulated motion), and evaluation limited to single-object dragging. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to multi-object scenes with independent rotations, non-rigid deformations, and longer video sequences.
