## Problem

Conventional SfM and SLAM methods assume predominantly static scenes with large camera baselines, and fail on casual monocular videos featuring dynamic content, limited parallax, and unconstrained camera paths. Recent neural approaches that address these challenges are either computationally expensive (requiring per-video network fine-tuning or radiance field optimization) or brittle under uncontrolled conditions such as unknown focal length or rotational-dominant camera motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251477/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a casual monocular RGB video of arbitrary duration (20–600 frames) with unconstrained camera paths, dynamic scene content, and optionally unknown focal length. It outputs per-frame camera poses in SE(3), a shared focal length estimate, and dense video depth maps. Camera tracking operates at 1/8 resolution (H/8 × W/8) for efficiency, while the optional depth refinement stage produces full-resolution depth with per-frame aleatoric uncertainty maps.

## Method

MegaSaM extends DROID-SLAM's differentiable bundle adjustment layer with three key modifications for dynamic scenes. First, a motion probability network F_m is trained via a two-stage scheme (ego-motion pretraining on static scenes, then dynamic fine-tuning) to predict per-pixel movement maps that downweight dynamic content in the BA cost. Second, monocular depth priors from DepthAnything and UniDepth provide data-driven disparity initialization and focal length estimation, replacing DROID-SLAM's constant-1 initialization. Third, an uncertainty-aware global BA computes the diagonal of the disparity Hessian to measure parameter observability, adaptively enabling mono-depth regularization and disabling focal length optimization only when the video provides insufficient geometric constraint. The inference pipeline combines a sliding-window frontend with a global backend BA, followed by an optional first-order depth refinement using flow reprojection, temporal consistency, and surface-normal losses.

## Results

On the MPI Sintel benchmark, MegaSaM achieves ATE of 0.018 (calibrated) and 0.023 (uncalibrated), RTE of 0.008 in both settings, and RRE of 0.04/0.06, outperforming all baselines by large margins. On DyCheck, it reaches ATE 0.020, RTE 0.005, RRE 0.05 (calibrated) with a runtime of 0.8s per frame. For video depth estimation, MegaSaM achieves abs-rel of 0.21 and δ₁.₂₅ of 73.1% on Sintel, and abs-rel of 0.11 and δ₁.₂₅ of 94.1% on DyCheck, again leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MegaSaM outperforms every baseline on all camera metrics (ATE, RTE, RRE) in both calibrated and uncalibrated settings across Sintel, DyCheck, and in-the-wild footage. Against the closest concurrent work MonST3R [75], MegaSaM reduces ATE by ~3.4× (0.023 vs 0.078) and RRE by ~8× (0.06 vs 0.49) on Sintel while matching its 1.0s runtime. Compared to CasualSAM [77], the most directly related prior method, MegaSaM halves ATE (0.018 vs 0.036 calibrated), reduces RRE by 5× (0.04 vs 0.20), and runs ~96× faster (1.0s vs 1.6m per frame). Against optimization-heavy baselines like RoDynRF [34] and Particle-SfM [78], MegaSaM achieves 6× and 3.4× lower ATE respectively while being 900× and 21× faster. LEAP-VO [6] is competitive on RTE (0.023 vs 0.008) but only supports calibrated inputs and shows 2.9× higher RTE. For depth estimation, MegaSaM leads DepthCrafter [20] and MonST3R [75] by 0.06–0.10 in abs-rel and 5–12 points in δ₁.₂₅ on Sintel, and by 0.10–0.15 in abs-rel on DyCheck.

## Relation to Prior Work

MegaSaM extends the DROID-SLAM differentiable bundle adjustment framework—originally designed for static scenes with sufficient parallax—by integrating monocular depth priors (DepthAnything, UniDepth) and learned motion probability maps into both training and inference. It departs from the CasualSAM paradigm of test-time network fine-tuning by instead using a learned SLAM frontend for camera tracking followed by a lightweight depth optimization backend, and introduces an uncertainty-aware global BA that conditionally applies mono-depth regularization based on Hessian-derived observability. The two-stage training scheme (ego-motion pretraining on static scenes, then dynamic fine-tuning with frozen SLAM features) is a key methodological departure from prior work that trains end-to-end on mixed data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The system achieves near-real-time camera tracking (~1s) from casual dynamic videos while significantly outperforming optimization-based methods (CasualSAM: 1.6 min) and concurrent 3D-point-cloud approaches (MonST3R), making it a practical tool for downstream tasks like novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction. The uncertainty-aware BA provides a principled mechanism for handling the observability problem in limited-parallax videos, which is a common failure mode in prior methods. Limitations include failure when moving objects dominate the frame and inability to handle varying focal lengths or strong radial distortion; the authors suggest incorporating vision foundation model priors as a future direction.
