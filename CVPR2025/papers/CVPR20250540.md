## Problem

Existing amodal segmentation methods either operate on single frames and fail under heavy or complete occlusion, or operate on video but are restricted to rigid objects and require auxiliary inputs like camera poses or optical flow. No prior method can handle deformable objects undergoing severe occlusions in video while maintaining temporal consistency and generalizing to unseen categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250540/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 25-frame sequence of modal (visible) segmentation masks for a target object (obtained from SAM2) and pseudo-depth maps (from Depth Anything V2) as input to Stage 1, which outputs the corresponding amodal mask sequence. Stage 2 additionally consumes the modal RGB content of the object and the predicted amodal masks to produce inpainted amodal RGB content. Training uses 128×256 frames with batch size 8; inference uses 256×512 with 25 EDM denoising steps and guidance scale 1.5.

## Method

The core approach repurposes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as a conditional latent diffusion model with a 3D U-Net backbone, exploiting the shape and temporal-consistency priors baked in during SVD's large-scale pretraining. Stage 1 (amodal segmentation) conditions on modal masks via CLIP embeddings injected into transformer cross-attention layers and on pseudo-depth maps concatenated as extra input channels, with temporal convolutions and attention propagating visible shape information to occluded frames. Stage 2 (content completion) uses the same architecture but conditions on modal RGB content and predicted amodal masks to inpaint occluded regions. Training follows a two-stage fine-tuning strategy (mask-only first, then mask+depth) with zero-initialized convolution for the new depth channels (inspired by ControlNet), and the EDM framework for efficient denoising. The model is trained solely on synthetic SAIL-VOS yet generalizes zero-shot to real-world TAO-Amodal, and supports multi-modal generation by sampling with different random seeds.

## Results

On SAIL-VOS, the method achieves 77.07 mIoU and 55.12 mIoU_occ (Top-1), surpassing the best baseline PCNet-M (74.2 mIoU, 42.52 mIoU_occ) by nearly 13% in occluded-region IoU. On the real-world TAO-Amodal dataset (zero-shot, trained only on synthetic SAIL-VOS), it reaches 97.28 AP25, 89.25 AP50, and 71.99 AP75, outperforming all baselines. On MOVi-B/D, it achieves 83.51/53.75 and 77.03/44.23 mIoU/mIoU_occ respectively, beating the prior state-of-the-art EoRaS by over 4% in mIoU_occ on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. On SAIL-VOS, the largest gap is against PCNet-M (42.52 mIoU_occ) and SDAmodal (41.26 mIoU_occ), with the method reaching 55.12 mIoU_occ — a ~13-point improvement in occluded-region accuracy. The single-frame diffusion method pix2gestalt [34] performs worst among learned methods (26.59 mIoU_occ Top-1), confirming that temporal context is critical for heavy occlusions. Among video-based baselines, EoRaS [9] and Bi-LSTM [9] are the strongest on MOVi-B/D (49.39 and 46.21 mIoU_occ on MOVi-B), but the method still leads by 4.4 and 7.5 points respectively. Notably, the method generalizes zero-shot to the real-world TAO-Amodal dataset (trained only on synthetic SAIL-VOS), beating PCNet-M by 2.4 points in AP25 and 6 points in AP75, while pix2gestalt drops to 28.95 AP75 — a 43-point gap. The 3D-UNet regression baseline (SVD backbone without diffusion training) achieves 39.54 mIoU_occ on SAIL-VOS, showing that the diffusion training objective contributes ~15.6 points over a pure regression head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Stable Video Diffusion (a video generation model) as a conditional amodal segmentation model, extending the line of work that exploits diffusion priors for shape inference (pix2gestalt, SDAmodal) from the single-image to the multi-frame video setting. It departs from prior video amodal segmentation methods (SaVos, EoRaS, C2F-Seg) by removing dependencies on camera poses or optical flow and by handling deformable objects rather than only rigid ones. The two-stage pipeline (mask completion followed by RGB content inpainting) is novel in the video amodal context, drawing on self-supervised training-pair construction from image amodal completion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video diffusion models encode strong shape and temporal-consistency priors transferable to amodal segmentation, enabling handling of full occlusions that single-frame methods fundamentally cannot resolve. The zero-shot generalization from synthetic SAIL-VOS to real-world TAO-Amodal is practically significant, suggesting the approach could reduce the need for expensive real-world amodal annotations. Open questions include whether the method scales to longer sequences, whether the pseudo-depth conditioning generalizes to scenes with complex depth ambiguities, and whether the diffusion-based approach can be made fast enough for real-time applications like autonomous driving.
