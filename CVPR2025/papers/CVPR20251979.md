## Problem

Video portrait relighting requires results that are simultaneously photorealistic and temporally stable, but existing methods trade one for the other or depend on expensive dynamic OLAT video data captured with high-speed cameras. No prior approach achieves fine-grained HDR-controlled relighting of arbitrary in-the-wild portrait videos while maintaining temporal coherence across frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251979/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an in-the-wild portrait video (512×768 resolution) and a target HDR environment map, and produces a relit video at the same resolution. Internally it operates in two stages: a delighting pass converts the input video to a shading-free albedo video, then a relighting pass applies the HDR lighting to the albedo. The HDR map is consumed as a set of directional light tokens (local intensity sums) rather than as a raw image, and long sequences are handled autoregressively by overlapping 4-frame windows.

## Method

Lux Post Facto fine-tunes Stable Video Diffusion into a conditional generator with two key modifications: (1) a lighting injection mechanism that tokenizes the HDR map into directional light tokens, embeds them via an MLP with positional encodings, and feeds the resulting light embedding into the U-Net through cross-attention (replacing text conditioning), and (2) spatio-temporal conditioning via concatenation of input latents and binary masks distinguishing original frames from autoregressive predictions. Training uses a hybrid dataset combining static OLAT images with simulated camera motion (providing precise HDR supervision) and 15,000 in-the-wild VFHQ talking-head videos (providing diverse motion). To exploit the motion-rich data without HDR labels, the authors introduce an auxiliary reference-based appearance copy task where a randomly sampled lit frame serves as the lighting condition, jointly trained with HDR-conditioned relighting on the OLAT data. A two-stage training schedule first warms up on 2-frame clips for 200K steps, then fine-tunes temporal layers on 30-frame clips for 50K steps using v-prediction loss.

## Results

On the held-out OLAT test set for image relighting, Lux Post Facto achieves LPIPS 0.1158, NIQE 5.653, PSNR 24.62 dB, and SSIM 0.8278, outperforming Total Relighting (TR) by 35% in LPIPS and 2.18 dB in PSNR, and SwitchLight by 45% in LPIPS and 4.75 dB in PSNR. For video relighting on in-the-wild portrait videos, the comparison is qualitative (no ground-truth relit videos exist), but the authors report visually superior highlights, skin tones, shadows, and temporal stability relative to NVPR and SwitchLight. An ablation on the lighting control module shows the proposed light-embedding cross-attention mechanism improves PSNR from 20.73 to 24.62 dB over a CLIP image encoder baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Total Relighting (TR, Pandey et al. 2021), Lux Post Facto wins on every metric: LPIPS drops from 0.1794 to 0.1158 (−35%), PSNR rises from 22.44 to 24.62 dB (+2.18), and SSIM improves from 0.7793 to 0.8278. Against SwitchLight (Kim et al. 2024), the margin is even larger: LPIPS 0.1158 vs. 0.2129 (−45%), PSNR 24.62 vs. 19.87 dB (+4.75), and SSIM 0.8278 vs. 0.7481. For video relighting, NVPR (Zhang et al. 2021) is compared qualitatively in Figure 5; the authors report that Lux Post Facto produces more faithful highlights, finer skin details, and better temporal stability under expression changes and head motion, whereas NVPR loses facial detail and SwitchLight's video extension shows temporal smoothing artifacts. No quantitative video metrics are reported because no ground-truth relit video dataset is available for in-the-wild inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage delighting-then-relighting pipeline established by Total Relighting [40] and Holo-Relighting [35] from the image domain to video, building on Stable Video Diffusion [3] as the generative backbone. It departs from prior video relighting methods (NVPR, SwitchLight) that either require expensive dynamic OLAT video capture or sacrifice spatial fidelity for temporal consistency, by introducing a hybrid training strategy that combines static OLAT images with in-the-wild portrait videos. The lighting control mechanism—encoding HDR maps as directional light embeddings injected via cross-attention—replaces the CLIP-based or pixel-aligned conditioning used in prior diffusion-based relighting work, and the iterative autoregressive scheme follows Atomovideo [15] for long-sequence generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid dataset strategy is practically significant because it eliminates the need for paired dynamic OLAT video data, which has been a major bottleneck for video relighting research, making the approach accessible to studios without high-speed light stage infrastructure. The method positions video relighting as a viable post-production tool for everyday content creators, broadening the audience beyond researchers with specialized capture equipment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond frontal portrait videos to full-body or multi-subject scenes, whether the pseudo-albedo supervision from the image delighting model introduces systematic biases, and whether the method can operate in real-time or near-real-time for interactive editing.
