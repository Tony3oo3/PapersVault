## Problem

Existing automatic poster design methods fail to simultaneously achieve accurate text rendering, fine-grained user customization, and professional aesthetic quality required for artistic domains like movies and exhibitions. Prior approaches either produce garbled or incomplete text, offer only one-shot generation without element-level control, or prioritize functionality over visual appeal, and none provide a unified, modular workflow spanning background, layout, typography, and text stylization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252637/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts free-text descriptions, structured text content categorized into title/subtitle/information with optional attributes (position, alignment, font, layout style), and optionally a reference poster image or custom background. The Design MLLM outputs structured layout and typography parameters (coordinates, font type, size, color, alignment, rotation angle) that are rendered as editable vector text rather than rasterized images, preserving full post-generation editability. Backgrounds are generated at 1024×1024 resolution and artistic text stylization operates at 1216 resolution, with the final poster composited via mask-guided blending.

## Method

POSTA is a three-stage modular pipeline that decouples text accuracy from text aesthetics. Stage 1 (Background Diffusion) uses FLUX with style-specific LoRA adapters (minimalism, retro, modern art) and a GPT-4V-powered Magic Prompter to refine user prompts before generation. Stage 2 (Design MLLM) employs a LLaVA architecture (CLIP ViT-L/14 + Llama3 7B) fine-tuned via visual instruction tuning on PosterArt-Design to predict full layout and typography attributes; text is then rendered directly from these predictions in editable vector format, guaranteeing 100% character accuracy. Stage 3 (ArtText Diffusion) applies a BrushNet-based (ControlNet-like) inpainting model trained on PosterArt-Text to perform mask-guided artistic stylization on key text elements, compositing results with a Gaussian-kernel-blended mask to ensure seamless integration with the background. The key architectural insight is that text correctness is handled by deterministic rendering from MLLM predictions, while visual richness is added post-hoc by a separate diffusion model, avoiding the fundamental conflict between text fidelity and artistic generation.

## Results

POSTA is evaluated via human study (60 raters with AI/arts background) and GPT-4V scoring on visual appeal, text readability, and prompt-image relevance (1–10 scale), as well as OCR-based word-level precision and recall using GOT-OCR. POSTA achieves the highest scores across all three subjective criteria, outperforming general-purpose models (SD3, Playground-v3, FLUX-dev) and graphic-design-specific models (Recraft V3, Ideogram v2). On OCR-based text accuracy, POSTA attains the highest word-level precision and recall among all compared methods, as its text is rendered directly from predicted typography rather than generated by a diffusion model, guaranteeing 100% character accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against general-purpose diffusion models (SD3, Playground-v3, FLUX-dev), POSTA wins on all three subjective metrics; FLUX-dev leads among the general-purpose group but still trails POSTA in aesthetics and text readability. Against graphic-design-specific models, Recraft V3 outperforms open-source models on aesthetics but fails on longer text sequences (omitting specified text), while Ideogram v2 introduces irrelevant elements that compromise design quality; POSTA surpasses both in text accuracy and prompt relevance. Against systematic poster-design frameworks COLE and OpenCOLE, POSTA produces more accurate text and more aesthetically cohesive results, with COLE's multi-step pipeline being slower and OpenCOLE's non-artistic training data limiting its visual quality. Against text-rendering specialists AnyText and TextDiffuser-2, POSTA avoids their characteristic failures of garbled characters and misspellings on longer text, since POSTA renders text via vector typography prediction rather than pixel-level generation. Overall, POSTA wins or ties on every reported metric across all nine baselines, with the largest margins on text accuracy (OCR precision/recall) and prompt-image relevance.

## Relation to Prior Work

POSTA positions itself against the end-to-end poster generation paradigm (COLE, OpenCOLE) and text-aware diffusion models (AnyText, GlyphControl, TextDiffuser-2), departing from both by decomposing the pipeline into three independently controllable stages: FLUX-based background generation with style-specific LoRA adapters, an LLaVA-based MLLM for joint layout and typography prediction, and a BrushNet inpainting model for context-aware artistic text stylization. Unlike prior layout planners (PosterLayout, RADM, PosterLLaVa) that predict only bounding boxes, POSTA jointly predicts font type, size, color, alignment, and rotation, and unlike artistic text generators (Anything-to-Glyph, WordArt) that operate without background context, POSTA's stylization is conditioned on the surrounding image. The closest prior system, COLE, also proposes a multi-module workflow but is more complex, requires extensive intermediate checks, and is not specifically tailored to the artistic poster domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, fully editable pipeline addresses a practical gap between research prototypes and professional design workflows: designers need to adjust individual elements (background, font, layout) without regenerating the entire poster, and text must be pixel-perfect for commercial use. The PosterArt dataset (2,000+ annotated artistic posters with full typographic metadata and 3,500 pixel-level text segmentations) fills a resource gap, as prior layout datasets rely on commercial ads or social media posters with inferior aesthetic quality. Key limitations remain: the Design MLLM produces only relatively simple layouts with a restricted font vocabulary, and the ArtText Diffusion's quality is bounded by the curated dataset size. Open questions include whether the modular approach scales to more complex multi-element compositions and whether the MLLM-based planning can be replaced by more expressive generative models without sacrificing editability.
