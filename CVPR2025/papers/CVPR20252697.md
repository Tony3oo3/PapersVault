## Problem

Existing LVLM-based reasoning segmentation methods (e.g., LISA, PixelLM) suffer from two coupled failures: hallucinated text responses that reference non-existent objects, and imprecise segmentation with coarse boundaries or incorrect localization. Classical preference optimization methods like DPO address only text quality and cannot be directly applied to segmentation, since the likelihood of generating a continuous segmentation embedding is infeasible to compute. No preference-based training paradigm had been designed to jointly improve both text fidelity and pixel-level segmentation accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., a question about objects in the scene) and produces a natural-language response containing special <seg> tokens that mark segmentation targets, along with a binary mask for each target. The segmentation embedding is a codebook feature (C_seg) extracted at each <seg> token position and decoded by a SAM-based decoder. During inference, K=3 stochastic responses are generated and ensembled before final mask prediction, so the effective input is a single image-instruction pair but the internal computation operates over multiple candidate outputs.

## Method

POPEN introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, a task-tailored preference optimization stage fine-tunes the LVLM (built on PixelLM/LLaVA) using two preference losses: a standard DPO loss on text responses with weighted token likelihoods (λ=5 on ChatGPT-corrected tokens), and a novel segmentation DPO loss that replaces intractable embedding likelihoods with cosine-similarity comparisons among segmentation embeddings produced from noise-perturbed images. A curriculum collection strategy feeds localization-focused preference data (ranked by a composite IoU score s^i) in the first half of training and boundary-refinement data (ranked by boundary IoU) in the second half. Second, a preference-based ensemble at inference generates K=3 responses, computes per-token and per-sentence preference scores from token likelihoods, and modulates the LVLM's self-attention (via an additive γ term) so that higher-reliability tokens and segmentation embeddings receive greater weight during integration, yielding a refined text response and refined segmentation embedding before decoding.

## Results

On the MUSE benchmark (7B LLaVA backbone), POPEN achieves 45.4 gIoU and 55.2 cIoU for segmentation, while reducing hallucination metrics C_S and C_I to 9.3 and 4.3 respectively, compared to PixelLM's 41.9/48.9 and 22.0/9.8. With the 13B backbone, POPEN reaches 48.0 gIoU and 59.1 cIoU with C_S of 9.1. On referring expression segmentation, POPEN attains 78.5 IoU on refCOCO val, 70.3 on refCOCO+ val, and 73.8 on refCOCOg val, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POPEN outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. On MUSE (7B), POPEN surpasses the strongest LVLM baseline PixelLM by +3.5 gIoU (45.4 vs 41.9) and +6.3 cIoU (55.2 vs 48.9), while halving the hallucination rate (C_S: 9.3 vs 22.0). GSVA and GLaMM trail further behind at 38.9 and 41.5 gIoU respectively, and LISA is far behind at 17.2 gIoU due to its single-target limitation. On referring expression segmentation, POPEN (78.5 IoU on refCOCO val) beats the best traditional method ReLA (73.8) by +4.7, the best LVLM method GSVA (76.4) by +2.1, and PixelLM (73.0) by +5.5. On the harder refCOCO+ and refCOCOg splits, POPEN leads by +5.8 and +2.7 over GSVA respectively, and by +8.0 and +4.5 over PixelLM. POPEN also dominates the older RES baselines (MCN, VLT, CRIS, LAVT) by margins of 6–16 IoU points, and outperforms X-Decoder and SEEM on refCOCOg by +9.2 and +8.1 IoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

POPEN builds directly on PixelLM's architecture (LLaVA backbone + codebook feature extraction + SAM-based decoder) and adapts the DPO preference optimization framework from NLP to the multimodal segmentation setting. The key departure from standard DPO is the recognition that segmentation embeddings have no token-level likelihood, necessitating a novel cosine-similarity-based preference loss (Eq. 4) and a noise-filling data collection strategy. The text preference data collection follows RLHF-V's ChatGPT-correction pipeline, while the curriculum learning mechanism for segmentation preference data draws on spectral bias findings that models learn localization before boundary refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The more-than-halving of the hallucination rate (C_S from 22.0 to 9.3) alongside consistent segmentation gains addresses two of the most practically limiting failure modes in LVLM-based reasoning segmentation, making the approach relevant for any deployment where incorrect object generation is costly. The mutual-benefit finding—that text DPO loss improves segmentation and segmentation DPO loss improves text quality—suggests that joint optimization of language and pixel outputs is more effective than treating them independently. Open questions include whether the preference optimization approach generalizes beyond the PixelLM/LLaVA architecture, whether the noise-filling data collection strategy scales to more diverse or higher-resolution inputs, and whether the inference-time ensemble (K=3 forward passes) is acceptable for latency-sensitive applications.
