## Problem

Sparse-view novel view synthesis suffers from two compounding issues: sparse SfM point clouds lead to blurry backgrounds and over-fitting floaters, while dense point clouds from learned depth priors (DUSt3R/MASt3R) carry suboptimal camera poses that degrade reconstruction fidelity. No existing method successfully combines the pose accuracy of Structure-from-Motion with the geometric density of learned depth priors in a single coordinate frame.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250935/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3, 6, or 12 unposed RGB images as input, which are processed through two parallel pipelines: COLMAP (using MASt3R feature matches) for sparse SfM point cloud and camera calibration, and MASt3R for a dense per-pixel point cloud. The output is a dense, pose-aligned point cloud that initializes Splatfacto-based 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization, producing photorealistic novel view renderings. The evaluation uses a realistic setup where test camera poses are recovered via Procrustes alignment with RANSAC and rotation-point sampling rather than assumed perfect calibration.

## Method

SPARS3R performs a two-stage alignment between MASt3R's dense point cloud and COLMAP's sparse SfM point cloud. In Global Fusion Alignment, forward-backward projection establishes correspondences between the two clouds per view, and a RANSAC-wrapped Generalized Procrustes Analysis estimates a global similarity transform (scale, rotation, translation) while identifying inliers and outliers. In Semantic Outlier Alignment, outliers are used as prompts for SAM to generate semantically coherent 2D masks; each mask region receives its own local rigid transform estimated from the within-mask outlier correspondences, while inlier regions retain the global transform. The piece-wise transformed dense cloud is concatenated with the SfM cloud to form the final initialization for 3DGS optimization, which uses a combined L1 photometric and D-SSIM loss.

## Results

On the 12-view setting across 24 scenes, SPARS3R achieves 18.85 PSNR / 0.500 SSIM / 0.327 LPIPS / 0.127 DSIM on Mip-NeRF 360, 29.90 PSNR / 0.919 SSIM on Tanks & Temples, and 25.85 PSNR / 0.820 SSIM on MVImgNet, improving over the previous best (InstantSplat or FSGS) by an average of 2.7 dB in PSNR. Under the more extreme 3-view and 6-view settings on Tanks & Temples and MVImgNet, SPARS3R still leads by 0.9 dB and 1.3 dB respectively. In pose accuracy (Table 3), the COLMAP+MASt3R pipeline used by SPARS3R reduces relative translation error to 0.252 on Mip-NeRF 360, compared to 2.075 for DUSt3R and 1.186 for MASt3R alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARS3R outperforms all seven rendering baselines across all three datasets and all four metrics. Against the closest competitor InstantSplat [14], SPARS3R gains +2.62 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 (18.85 vs. 16.23), +2.93 dB on Tanks & Temples (29.90 vs. 26.97), and +2.63 dB on MVImgNet (25.85 vs. 23.22); the gap is attributed to InstantSplat inheriting suboptimal DUSt3R poses that limit densification. FSGS [65], the best depth-regularized method, trails SPARS3R by 1.25 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 and 4.18 dB on Tanks & Temples, with notably worse DSIM (0.243 vs. 0.127) indicating blurrier backgrounds. SparseGS [55] and DRGS [11] are further behind, with DRGS showing high PSNR but poor perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.649, DSIM 0.300 on Mip-NeRF 360). CF-3DGS [19] and Instant-NGP [36], which forgo or underutilize SfM point clouds, perform worst overall (13.27 and 14.82 PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360). In pose accuracy (Table 3), the COLMAP+MASt3R pipeline achieves RPEt of 0.252 on Mip-NeRF 360, substantially better than DUSt3R (2.075) and MASt3R alone (1.186), confirming that SfM-based triangulation is critical for reliable calibration in sparse-view settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPARS3R sits at the intersection of two parallel threads in sparse-view NVS: (1) dense prior-based initialization from DUSt3R/MASt3R, exemplified by InstantSplat, which provides rich geometry but suboptimal camera poses; and (2) SfM-based calibration via COLMAP, which yields accurate poses but sparse point clouds, as used by FSGS, SparseGS, and DRGS. The paper's core departure is a two-stage alignment pipeline—global Procrustes with RANSAC followed by SAM-guided semantic local alignment—that fuses the dense prior onto the SfM reference frame, rather than relying on either source alone. This positions SPARS3R as a bridge between learned depth priors and classical geometric calibration, using SAM as a semantic grouping mechanism to handle inter-object depth discrepancies that a single global transform cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-world sparse-view reconstruction where dense capture is infeasible (autonomous driving, site modeling, agriculture), and the 2.7 dB average improvement over prior best methods across three benchmarks is meaningful. The evaluation methodology contributions—incorporating rotation points and RANSAC into test-pose alignment, and adding DreamSim as a perceptual metric robust to moderate pose shifts—address a real gap in how sparse NVS is measured. Key open questions remain: whether piece-wise rigid transforms generalize to scenes with large non-rigid deformations, how the method scales when SAM produces overly fragmented or overly merged masks, and whether the alignment pipeline can be made differentiable to end-to-end optimize with the Gaussian rendering loss.
