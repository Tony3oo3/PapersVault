## Problem

Source-free open set domain adaptation requires distinguishing unknown target samples and assigning reliable pseudo labels to known ones, all without access to source data or prior knowledge of the unknown-class proportion. Existing open-set DA methods either assume source data availability, rely on hand-tuned thresholds or openness priors, or use unstable adversarial training, making them inapplicable under privacy-preserving, source-free constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251812/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method receives a pre-trained source model (CNN feature extractor + softmax classifier) and a batch of N=256 unlabeled target images; the source classifier is frozen while the target feature extractor is fine-tuned. Output is a (H+1)-dimensional probability vector per sample, where the first H dimensions correspond to known source classes and the final dimension indicates the unknown class, effectively partitioning target samples into known (with a soft class assignment from the sparse transport plan) and unknown groups.

## Method

DTE operates in two sequential stages. First, Weight Barcode Estimation solves a Partial Unbalanced Optimal Transport (PUOT) problem to compute per-sample marginal probabilities across seven temperature values, binarizes them into a binary barcode matrix, and applies K-Means to automatically separate known from unknown target samples without any prior on the unknown proportion. Second, Sparse Label Assignment formulates sample-prototype matching as an optimal transport problem regularized by an L2-norm proximal term (rather than entropy regularization), yielding a sparse transport plan that serves as soft pseudo-labels for known samples. The target feature extractor and a new unknown-class distinguisher are jointly trained via cross-entropy against these pseudo-labels, while class prototypes are updated incrementally with a momentum coefficient.

## Results

On Office-31, DTE achieves an average OS of 96.4% and OS* of 97.2%, outperforming the strongest baselines SCDA (95.8%/96.8%) and UOL (95.5%/96.2%). On Office-Home, DTE-S reaches 76.1% average OS, surpassing ANNA (74.8%) and BCL (74.4%). On VisDA-2017, DTE attains 72.4% OS and 68.7% OS*, a 2.4-point improvement over UOL (70.0%/66.3%). On the Digits benchmark, DTE achieves 92.9% OS and 92.7% OS*, leading UOL (92.2%/91.6%) and JPOT (87.5%/85.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTE consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across all four benchmarks. On Office-31, DTE leads SCDA (the strongest competitor) by 0.6% in OS and 0.4% in OS* on average, with the largest gap on the D→A task (95.6 vs. 92.1 OS). Against JPOT, which is the closest optimal-transport-based method, DTE gains 1.8% OS and 2.8% OS* on average, showing that the weight barcode estimation and sparse label assignment components are critical over standard partial OT. On Office-Home, DTE-S (76.1% OS) surpasses ANNA (74.8%) and BCL (74.4%) by 1.3 and 1.7 points respectively. On VisDA-2017, DTE achieves 72.4% OS and 68.7% OS*, exceeding UOL (70.0%/66.3%) by 2.4 points on both metrics. Notably, source-free methods like SFPGL (87.4% avg OS on Office-31) and SHOT (92.6%) lag significantly behind DTE, confirming the advantage of the proposed two-stage distinguish-then-exploit strategy under privacy constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of source-free domain adaptation (SHOT, SFPGL) and open set domain adaptation (OSBP, JPOT, STA), combining two constraints that prior work has largely addressed in isolation. It builds directly on the proximal point method for sparse optimal transport (Xie et al., UAI 2020) to formulate the sparse label assignment step, and extends partial unbalanced optimal transport (PUOT) with a novel 'weight barcode' mechanism that uses multiple temperature values to create a binary signature for clustering known/unknown samples without requiring prior knowledge of the unknown-class proportion. The key departure from JPOT and similar OT-based open set methods is the explicit two-stage design: first distinguishing unknown samples via PUOT-based reweighting, then exploiting the remaining known samples via sparse sample-prototype matching, rather than attempting a single joint optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source-free open set DA problem is practically important in privacy-sensitive deployments where source data cannot be shared and the target domain may contain novel categories. The weight barcode mechanism's ability to handle varying openness without prior knowledge is a useful property, and the sparse matching solution addresses a known weakness of entropy-regularized OT (dense, inaccurate transport plans). However, the method introduces several hyperparameters (tau range, epsilon, alpha, K-Means clustering) that may limit robustness, and the K-Means step on the barcode matrix is somewhat ad hoc. Open questions include scalability to larger-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet-level), whether the barcode approach generalizes to structured or hierarchical unknown classes, and how the method performs when the source and target domains share very few known classes.
