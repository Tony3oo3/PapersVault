## Problem

Existing VIT data selection methods assume access to fully annotated image-instruction pairs, requiring expensive instruction generation (via GPT APIs or human annotators) for every image before any selection can occur. This makes it impractical for resource-constrained users to build custom VIT datasets, as the instruction generation cost scales with the full dataset size rather than the selected subset. The paper asks: can we select the most beneficial unlabeled images first, and only then generate instructions for that small subset?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250504/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large pool of unlabeled RGB images organized into M vision tasks (e.g., VQA, OCR, captioning) as input, along with a small reference set (~5% of total images) for which instructions are generated solely to estimate task importance. It outputs a selected image subset (e.g., 15% of the pool) with per-task sampling budgets determined by the Instruction Relevance Score, ready for instruction generation and subsequent LVLM fine-tuning. Unlike prior methods that consume full image-instruction pairs, PreSel's selection pipeline operates exclusively on unlabeled images via DINOv2 feature extraction, with no access to instruction text during the clustering and selection stages.

## Method

PreSel operates in two stages. Stage 1 (Task-Importance Estimation) fine-tunes a reference LVLM for one epoch on a small 5% reference set, then computes the Instruction Relevance Score (IRS) for each sample as the ratio of next-token CE loss on the response with and without the question tokens as context; a lower IRS indicates the question is more informative, and per-task average IRS values are converted to sampling proportions via a softmax with temperature τ=1/√M. Stage 2 (Task-wise Cluster-based Selection) extracts DINOv2 [CLS] features for all remaining unlabeled images, applies k-means clustering within each task (C=|T_i|/100 clusters), and selects n_c images per cluster proportional to the task's importance weight and cluster size. Within each cluster, the most representative images are chosen by the Neighbor Centrality score, which averages cosine similarity to k nearest neighbors in feature space, favoring central points over outliers. This design ensures that instruction generation cost is limited to the selected fraction (e.g., 15%) while maintaining performance comparable to full-data fine-tuning.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 (15% sampling ratio, 93K samples), PreSel achieves 97.9% average relative performance, outperforming the second-best baseline COINCIDE (95.5%) and Random (95.7%), while requiring instruction generation for only 15% of images versus 100% for all other methods. On Vision-Flan (191 tasks, 28K samples at 15%), PreSel reaches 100.1% relative performance, slightly surpassing full-scale fine-tuning (100%) while all baselines fall short (best: COINCIDE at 97.1%). PreSel's selection cost is 9 GPU-hours versus 55.5 (COINCIDE) and 73.5 (Self-Filter), and it reduces instruction generation cost to 15%·C versus 100%·C for all competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreSel outperforms all seven baselines on both LLaVA-1.5 and Vision-Flan. The closest competitor is COINCIDE [15] (EMNLP 2024), which PreSel beats by 2.4% on LLaVA-1.5 (97.9% vs. 95.5%) and 3.0% on Vision-Flan (100.1% vs. 97.1%); critically, COINCIDE requires 100% instruction generation and 55.5 GPU-hours for selection versus PreSel's 15% and 9.0 GPU-hours. Self-Filter [5] (ACL 2024) is the weakest baseline at 88.8% on LLaVA-1.5, trailing PreSel by 9.1%, and its two-round training pipeline adds 73.5 GPU-hours of selection cost. Among NLP-derived methods, EL2N [31] (93.0%), Perplexity [28] (91.9%), and IFD [19] (91.8%) all underperform PreSel by 4.9–6.1% on LLaVA-1.5; IFD additionally fails to improve with larger sampling budgets because it over-allocates to LLaVA-v1 tasks. CLIP-Score [33] (90.3%) and TypiClust [11] (96.8%) are outperformed by 7.6% and 1.1% respectively on LLaVA-1.5, with the gap widening to 7.9% and 8.1% on the more task-diverse Vision-Flan dataset. The key structural advantage is that PreSel is the only method requiring instruction generation for only 15% of images, while all baselines need full 100% annotation to perform their selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established VIT data selection paradigm (SelfFilter, COINCIDE, IFD, EL2N, Perplexity) by introducing a pre-instruction formulation: selecting unlabeled images before any instruction generation occurs, rather than selecting from complete image-instruction pairs. Conceptually, it repurposes loss-based selection ideas from NLP (the IRS metric mirrors IFD's difficulty scoring) and active learning clustering (TypiClust) into a two-stage pipeline that first estimates task importance via a small reference set, then performs DINOv2-based k-means clustering with Neighbor Centrality scoring. The key departure is that prior methods all require 100% instruction generation as a prerequisite for selection, whereas PreSel inverts this dependency, making the expensive GPT-API or human annotation step conditional on the selection outcome.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing instruction generation costs by 85% (from 100% to 15% of images) while maintaining near-full-data performance (97.9% relative on LLaVA-1.5, 100.1% on Vision-Flan) makes custom VIT dataset creation feasible for resource-constrained users. The finding that random selection outperforms several sophisticated baselines (CLIP-Score, EL2N, IFD, Self-Filter) suggests that task distribution balance matters more than individual sample quality scoring in VIT, which challenges the assumption that per-sample difficulty metrics are the primary driver. Open questions include whether the 5% reference set requirement is sufficient for truly novel task compositions, whether the approach transfers to LVLMs beyond the LLaVA family, and whether the DINOv2 feature space remains discriminative for tasks with heavy textual or fine-grained visual content.
