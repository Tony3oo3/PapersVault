## Problem

Large-scale text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion and DeepFloyd cannot generate foreground objects over a specifiable chroma key background without fine-tuning. Existing solutions either rely on prompt engineering with manual post-processing (MAGICK) or require fine-tuning on large, non-public datasets (LayerDiffuse with 1M images), leaving no training-free approach for precise foreground-background separation with controlled background color.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251633/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the foreground content, a target background color (specified via per-channel shift percentages, e.g., +7% on channels 2 and 3 for green), and Gaussian mask parameters (σ, μi, μj) that control foreground position and size. It produces a 512×512 (SD1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL) chroma key image where the foreground aligns with the prompt and the background is a uniform monochromatic color. Multiple Gaussian masks can be applied simultaneously to generate multiple foreground objects at different positions and sizes within a single image, and the pipeline extends to produce chroma key video via AnimateDiff.

## Method

TKG-DM introduces channel mean shift, a training-free technique that adjusts the mean of each of the four latent channels in the initial Gaussian noise z_T while keeping standard deviation constant, iteratively finding the shift Δc that achieves a target positive ratio per channel to steer the generated image toward a specific background color. The color-shifted noise z_T* is then blended with the original noise z_T using a 2D Gaussian mask A(i,j), preserving original noise in the foreground region and applying color-shifted noise to the background. The separation mechanism exploits an inherent attention bias in training data: cross-attention strongly couples the foreground to the text prompt, while the background—less described in captions—is dominated by the color-shifted initial noise, effectively decoupling it from the prompt. This requires no fine-tuning, no additional datasets, and no model modification, operating entirely within vanilla Stable Diffusion or SDXL with standard DDIM sampling (50 steps, guidance scale 7.5).

## Results

On the MAGICK dataset (3,000 images), TKG-DM achieves FID 56.32 and m-FID 40.75 with SD1.5, improving FID and m-FID by 33.7% and 35.9% respectively over the SD1.5+GBP baseline. With SDXL, TKG-DM reaches FID 41.81 and m-FID 31.43, outperforming SDXL+GBP (FID 45.32, m-FID 39.17) while remaining competitive with the fine-tuned LayerDiffuse (FID 29.34, m-FID 29.82). In the ControlNet setting, TKG-DM achieves FID 17.09 and m-FID 17.22, surpassing SDXL+GBP (FID 22.04, m-FID 18.62) on all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD1.5+GBP, TKG-DM wins decisively on all metrics: FID drops from 85.00 to 56.32 (−33.7%), m-FID from 63.54 to 40.75 (−35.9%), while CLIP-I and CLIP-S both improve. Against the fine-tuned GB LoRA, TKG-DM also leads on every metric (FID 56.32 vs 60.29, m-FID 40.75 vs 49.03), showing that training-free noise manipulation surpasses a lightweight fine-tuning approach. In the SDXL setting, TKG-DM outperforms SDXL+GBP on FID (41.81 vs 45.32) and m-FID (31.43 vs 39.17), but trails the fine-tuned LayerDiffuse on FID (41.81 vs 29.34) and m-FID (31.43 vs 29.82); the authors attribute LayerDiffuse's FID advantage to its evaluation setup overlaying foregrounds on a lime-green background that matches the ground truth. DeepFloyd+GBP shows the best raw FID (31.57) and m-FID (20.31) in the SDXL group, but the paper argues this is an unfair comparison since the ground truth images were themselves generated with DeepFloyd, giving it an inherent similarity advantage. In the ControlNet extension, TKG-DM beats SDXL+GBP on all four metrics (FID 17.09 vs 22.04, m-FID 17.22 vs 18.62, CLIP-I 0.834 vs 0.819, CLIP-S 0.284 vs 0.279), confirming the method's generality across conditioning paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

TKG-DM builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework, extending the growing line of work on initial noise manipulation (noise optimization, seed selection, noise cropping for layout control) with a new operation: channel mean shift to control background chromaticity. It departs from prior chroma key generation approaches—MAGICK's prompt engineering with post-processing and LayerDiffuse's fine-tuning on a 1M-image dataset—by requiring neither prompt engineering nor any model modification. The method also connects to the observation that SDXL's latent space has interpretable color structure, repurposing this insight as a generation control mechanism rather than an analysis tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of TKG-DM removes the data curation and fine-tuning overhead that plagues existing chroma key generation pipelines, making foreground-background separation accessible without proprietary datasets or GPU-intensive training. Its extension to ControlNet, consistency models, and text-to-video (AnimateDiff) suggests a broadly applicable noise-level control primitive for any latent diffusion pipeline. However, the method is limited to foreground object generation (not landscapes or complex backgrounds), fails on very small objects, and in the SDXL setting still trails LayerDiffuse on FID and m-FID, leaving open whether noise-level control can match fine-tuned approaches for more demanding compositional tasks.
