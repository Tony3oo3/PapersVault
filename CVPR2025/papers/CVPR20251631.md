## Problem

Existing differentiable rendering representations (NeRF, 3DGS) rely on soft probabilistic primitives that inherently blur sharp edges and occlusion boundaries, especially in close-up or novel viewpoints. While meshes preserve explicit shape, they are not readily differentiable. The paper identifies a gap on the representation spectrum between deterministic geometry and probabilistic fields, seeking a primitive that maintains explicit boundary definitions while supporting end-to-end gradient-based optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251631/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images and a coarse point cloud (from SfM) as initialization, requiring no pre-existing mesh. It produces a compact set of Bezier Gaussian Triangle primitives—parametric surfaces with shared control-point attributes and multi-resolution attribute maps—that can be rendered at arbitrary resolution. A key output property is resolution independence: the same BG-Triangle set renders sharp boundaries even when the test camera is moved to 40% of the training distance, a regime where Gaussian-based methods exhibit visible blurring.

## Method

BG-Triangle is a hybrid primitive that uses degree-n Bezier triangle surfaces (defined by control points and barycentric coordinates) as shape descriptors, with pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian sub-primitives generated on-the-fly from the tessellated surface for differentiable rendering. Unlike 3DGS where Gaussians are fixed scene primitives, here each viewpoint produces a different set of Gaussians sampled from the same Bezier surface, enabling resolution-independent rendering. A discontinuity-aware alpha blending module identifies 2D boundary pixels between adjacent primitives, assigns per-pixel blending coefficients via a distance-based blurring function, and suppresses Gaussian uncertainty at boundaries to preserve sharp edges. Training uses a photometric loss (L2 + D-SSIM) with a splitting-and-pruning scheme driven by control-point gradient magnitude and back-projected edge priors, allowing reconstruction from a coarse point cloud without mesh initialization.

## Results

On NeRF Synthetic with ~343K parameters, BG-Triangle achieves SSIM 0.937, PSNR 29.16, and LPIPS 0.050, outperforming all Gaussian-based baselines at comparable parameter budgets. On Tanks & Temples (~867K params), it reaches SSIM 0.925, PSNR 27.23, and LPIPS 0.059, with the LPIPS advantage being particularly large (0.059 vs. 0.099 for 3DGS). In close-up views (40% test distance) on NeRF Synthetic, BG-Triangle leads all methods with SSIM 0.736, PSNR 21.19, and LPIPS 0.306, demonstrating superior boundary preservation under magnification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NeRF Synthetic at comparable parameter counts (~340–384K), BG-Triangle outperforms all four baselines on every metric: it beats Scaffold-GS (the strongest baseline) by +0.002 SSIM, +0.80 PSNR, and −0.031 LPIPS, and leads 3DGS by +0.015 SSIM, +1.98 PSNR, and −0.053 LPIPS. On Tanks & Temples (~721–888K params), the picture is more nuanced: 3DGS edges out BG-Triangle on SSIM (0.931 vs. 0.925) and PSNR (27.56 vs. 27.23), but BG-Triangle achieves a substantially lower LPIPS (0.059 vs. 0.099), indicating better perceptual similarity. In the close-up view evaluation (Table 2, 40% test distance), BG-Triangle wins decisively on all three metrics over every baseline, with the largest gap over 3DGS being +0.015 SSIM, +0.53 PSNR, and −0.027 LPIPS. The authors note that while PSNR/SSIM gaps are modest on Tanks & Temples, the LPIPS advantage and qualitative boundary sharpness (especially under zoom) are the method's primary differentiators over purely Gaussian-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

BG-Triangle sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: parametric Bezier triangle surfaces (Farin, 1986) for explicit shape description, and 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for differentiable, resolution-independent rendering. The key departure from both is the hybrid design: Bezier triangles provide deterministic, resolution-independent boundaries and connectivity constraints (like meshes), while pixel-aligned Gaussian sub-primitives generated on-the-fly from the Bezier surface supply the probabilistic, differentiable rendering proxy (like 3DGS). This positions BG-Triangle one step closer to the deterministic end of the representation spectrum compared to 3DGS or Scaffold-GS, which impose only soft or anchor-based constraints, and further from the fully implicit end occupied by NeRFs. The discontinuity-aware alpha blending mechanism is a new technique specifically designed to suppress Gaussian uncertainty at primitive boundaries without breaking differentiability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit vectorized shape descriptors can coexist with probabilistic rendering primitives to achieve sharp, resolution-independent novel view synthesis with significantly fewer parameters than purely Gaussian-based methods, suggesting a viable path toward bridging classic vector graphics and modern neural rendering. However, standard metrics (PSNR, SSIM) show only marginal or slightly worse performance compared to 3DGS, and the authors themselves acknowledge that these metrics fail to capture the boundary-sharpness advantage, leaving the field without an objective evaluation protocol for edge fidelity. Open questions include scalability to large, complex scenes beyond the tested Tanks & Temples and NeRF Synthetic benchmarks, the behavior under extreme viewpoint extrapolation, and whether the Bezier-degree and attribute-map resolution choices generalize across diverse geometries.
