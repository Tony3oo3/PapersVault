## Problem

Existing single-image 3D human reconstruction methods produce single-layer, non-animatable meshes where body and clothing are fused into one continuous surface, making virtual try-on, asset extraction, and re-animation impractical. No prior work achieves 4D layered human generation and animation from a single image, with prior layered methods requiring self-rotating video inputs and extensive processing times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250133/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB image of a clothed person and produces a disentangled 3D Gaussian avatar structured as a concatenation of S_body (SMPL-X-bound Gaussians) and S_cloth (free Gaussians with 15-dim identity encoding vectors per point). For 4D animation, an optional monocular video or pose sequence drives a deformation network applied only to clothing Gaussians. Editing accepts text or image prompts targeting specific clothing categories identified by the learned identity encodings, enabling per-asset colorization, removal, or swap without affecting other components.

## Method

Disco4D fixes SMPL-X Gaussians (bound to mesh triangles via SuGaR-style barycentric placement) as a rigid body anchor, then optimizes clothing Gaussians externally using an SDF loss and periodic pruning to enforce body-clothing separation. A novel identity encoding mechanism assigns each clothing Gaussian a learnable 15-dim vector rendered differentially into 2D segmentation masks (following Gaussian Grouping), trained with cross-entropy against SegFormer masks plus a 3D k-NN spatial consistency loss, enabling selective densification of only the relevant clothing category. Diffusion models (Stable Video Diffusion for multi-view initialization, Zero-1-to-3-XL for unseen-view SDS inpainting) enhance geometry and texture in occluded regions. For 4D animation, a DreamGaussian4D-style deformation network predicts per-Gaussian position, rotation, and scale offsets from a timestamp, applied exclusively to clothing Gaussians while the body follows SMPL-X kinematics, with skip connections and zero-initialized heads to preserve temporal consistency.

## Results

On the Synbody dataset, Disco4D achieves the highest CLIP similarity (0.851) and PSNR (15.691) among all compared methods, outperforming DreamGaussian (CLIP 0.751, PSNR 13.118) and LGM (CLIP 0.807, PSNR 12.884). On CloSe, Disco4D leads in CLIP (0.856) and novel-pose PSNR (17.96 vs. SHERF's 15.54), though LGM edges it slightly on novel-view PSNR (20.50 vs. 20.10). For 4D animation on 4D-Dress, Disco4D with learned deformations achieves CLIP 0.900, PSNR 25.46, SSIM 0.96, and LPIPS 0.035, substantially surpassing DreamGaussian4D (CLIP 0.784, PSNR 20.54, LPIPS 0.080). In a user study with 43 raters, Disco4D scores 3.142/5 on image consistency and 3.037/5 on overall quality, versus 2.017/1.852 for DreamGaussian and 2.338/2.017 for LGM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Disco4D consistently outperforms DreamGaussian [89] and LGM [91] on CLIP similarity across both Synbody and CloSe (e.g., 0.851 vs. 0.751/0.807 on Synbody), and wins the user study by a wide margin (3.142 vs. 2.017/2.338 on image consistency). Against SHERF [40], Disco4D achieves higher CLIP (0.851 vs. 0.766) and novel-pose PSNR (17.96 vs. 15.54), confirming the advantage of SMPL-X-based body representation over NeRF-based approaches. On the 4D-Dress benchmark, Disco4D with learned deformations dominates all video-to-4D baselines: it surpasses DreamGaussian4D [78] by +0.116 CLIP and +4.92 PSNR, MonoHuman [113] by +0.138 CLIP and +5.24 PSNR, GART [54] by +0.100 CLIP and +6.65 PSNR, and GaussianAvatar [38] by +0.078 CLIP and +5.45 PSNR. The only metric where Disco4D trails is novel-view PSNR on CloSe (20.10 vs. LGM's 20.50), which the authors attribute to optimizing Gaussians in canonical space for pose generalization. For 2D animation baselines (Animate Anyone [37], MagicAnimate [106], CHAMP [120]), comparisons are qualitative: Disco4D preserves fine-grained body parts (hands, face) and clothing details more faithfully, with greater temporal consistency than CHAMP.

## Relation to Prior Work

Disco4D builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its rendering backbone and SMPL-X as the body parametric model, combining them in a way that prior human-centric methods (SHERF, PIFu, ARCH) did not: the body is represented as fixed mesh-bound Gaussians while clothing is modeled as free Gaussians with a learned identity encoding for category separation. For 4D animation, it adapts the deformation network from DreamGaussian4D but applies it only to clothing Gaussians rather than all points, leveraging the SMPL-X pose for body deformation. The diffusion-model-based inpainting (SDS loss) and multi-view initialization via LGM/Stable Video Diffusion follow the generative 3D pipeline established by DreamGaussian and LGM, but are applied selectively to occluded clothing regions rather than the entire scene.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentangled body-clothing representation unlocks practical capabilities—virtual try-on, per-asset editing, and independent clothing animation—that monolithic 3D human reconstructions fundamentally cannot support, making it relevant to entertainment, fashion, and AR/VR pipelines. Key limitations remain: the method depends on robust pixel-aligned SMPL-X estimation (an unsolved problem), visual hull initialization quality is suboptimal for side/back views, and mesh extraction from clothing Gaussians loses fine geometric detail. Open questions include modeling multi-layered clothing, reconstructing occluded garments, and addressing the IP and deepfake risks that disentangled avatar generation introduces.
