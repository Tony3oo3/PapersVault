## Problem

Auxiliary-model-based heterogeneous federated learning suffers from two fundamental issues: local overfitting due to limited per-client data, and aggregation bias where uniform averaging ignores differences in how much each client's knowledge is already captured by the shared auxiliary model. Existing remedies rely on pre-trained models or fixed regularization, failing to dynamically leverage both early training trajectories and cross-client contribution signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252022/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, variable for Office10/Digit5) and produces a class label. Unusually, clients operate heterogeneous architectures (ResNet-18/34/50, ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32) assigned cyclically, while a single smallest model serves as the shared auxiliary model for knowledge transfer. The method outputs both a personalized local model prediction and an updated auxiliary model whose aggregation weights are computed per-client from feature-space and parameter-space update distances.

## Method

FedSCE introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, a hierarchical subspace constraint restricts the update direction of selected final layers (p layers) of the local model to a rank-R subspace via a projection loss, reducing update degrees of freedom from L to R; the subspace is dynamically refreshed every s rounds by concatenating the current basis with the latest parameter delta and applying truncated SVD, thereby tracking the evolving training trajectory while keeping storage at O(K−L+R). Second, an auxiliary model contribution estimation strategy computes each client's update distance in both parameter space (‖θ_{g,m}−θ_g‖₂) and feature space (‖z_{g,m}−z_g‖₂), normalizes across clients, and uses the combined score as an adaptive aggregation weight so that clients whose information is underrepresented in the auxiliary model receive higher weight. Local training additionally employs deep mutual learning with an adaptively scaled KL divergence between local and auxiliary model logits, where the scaling factor inversely depends on current task losses to balance distillation strength over training.

## Results

On Office10 (feature-skew), FedSCE achieves 76.34% D.Avg and 80.36% C.Avg, surpassing the best baseline (SOLO at 73.26% D.Avg / 78.66% C.Avg) by 3.08% and 1.70% respectively. On Digit5, FedSCE reaches 93.69% D.Avg, marginally exceeding FedKD (93.62%). Under label-skew settings, FedSCE leads on all three datasets: CIFAR-10 (62.93% at Dir0.5 vs. 61.39% for FedKD), CIFAR-100 (23.91% vs. 23.60%), and Tiny-ImageNet (15.94% vs. 15.26%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSCE outperforms all five baselines (FML, FedKD, FedDistill, FedProto, FedTGP) and the SOLO (local-only) baseline on Office10 D.Avg (76.34% vs. best 73.26% for SOLO), with the largest per-client gain on Amazon (+3.76% over SOLO). On Digit5, FedSCE edges out FedKD (93.69% vs. 93.62% D.Avg), the strongest baseline in that setting, while all other baselines trail by 2–3.5%. Under label-skew (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet), FedSCE consistently leads: at the most extreme Dir0.5 setting it beats FedKD by 1.54% on CIFAR-10, 0.31% on CIFAR-100, and 0.68% on Tiny-ImageNet. FedProto is the weakest baseline across nearly all settings, lagging FedSCE by 6–14% depending on dataset and skew level. Notably, FedSCE is the only method that improves over SOLO on both Office10 and Digit5 simultaneously, indicating that its subspace constraint and contribution estimation provide genuine federated gains rather than merely matching local training.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSCE builds directly on the auxiliary model-based HFL framework introduced by FML [39] and FedKD [48], adapting their mutual learning mechanism with an adaptive KL coefficient. It repurposes the subspace learning literature [27, 35, 34]—which showed neural networks can be trained in low-dimensional subspaces—by applying a *hierarchical* constraint to only the final layers of the local model rather than all parameters, and by dynamically updating the subspace via truncated SVD to track the training trajectory. The contribution estimation strategy departs from standard FedAvg-style uniform aggregation by weighting auxiliary models according to their update distance in both parameter and feature space, a design inspired by client contribution estimation in robust FL [20].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent pain points in HFL—local overfitting under limited private data and aggregation bias under non-IID distributions—within a single framework, and provides theoretical grounding (VC-dimension generalization bound and O(1/T) convergence rate) for the subspace constraint. However, the approach is validated only on image classification with up to 200 communication rounds and modest architectures (ResNet-50, ViT-B/32), leaving open whether the SVD-based subspace update scales to very deep or very wide networks, and whether the contribution estimation remains stable when client data distributions are extremely skewed. The practical overhead of periodic SVD and the need to tune the number of constrained layers (p) and subspace rank (R) per architecture are non-trivial deployment considerations.
