## Problem

Existing moving object segmentation methods rely on optical flow (limited to short-range, noise-sensitive) or affinity-matrix spectral clustering (poor global consistency, struggles with complex motions), and they process motion and appearance cues in separate stages, limiting effective integration. Additionally, promptable segmentation models like SAM2 lack any mechanism to determine which objects are moving, making them unusable for MOS without an external motion detector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251658/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes long-range 2D point tracks from BootsTAP (normalized pixel coordinates, per-point visibility and confidence scores), monocular depth maps from Depth Anything, and DINOv2 semantic features extracted from video frames. Tracks are highly irregular in length and visibility due to occlusion and camera motion, so the model applies NeRF-style frequency positional encodings and masks low-confidence points. The output is first per-trajectory binary motion labels (dynamic vs. static), which are then converted into pixel-level, per-object segmentation masks via SAM2, supporting both grouped-foreground and fine-grained per-object evaluation.

## Method

The core architecture is an encoder-decoder transformer operating on long-range tracks. The encoder applies Spatio-Temporal Trajectory Attention, interleaving attention layers that alternate across the track (spatial) and temporal dimensions, followed by max-pooling over time to produce one feature vector per trajectory. The decoder uses a Motion-Semantic Decoupled Embedding: self-attention operates exclusively on motion features, while multi-head cross-attention attends to a memory that includes DINO semantic features, preventing the model from over-relying on appearance and enabling it to distinguish moving from static objects of the same category. After predicting dynamic trajectories, a two-stage SAM2 iterative prompting strategy first groups trajectories by object (using the densest visible point as an initial prompt and dilation-based exclusion) and then prompts SAM2 at regular intervals along each trajectory to generate complete, fine-grained per-object masks. The model is trained with weighted binary cross-entropy on per-track labels derived from ground-truth dynamic masks across Kubric, Dynamic Replica, and HOI4D.

## Results

On the MOS task (grouping all foreground objects), the method achieves J&F of 89.5 on DAVIS2016-Moving, 90.9 on DAVIS2016, and F of 82.8 on FBMS-59, setting new state-of-the-art F-scores across all evaluated benchmarks. On the fine-grained per-object MOS task (DAVIS17-Moving), it reaches J&F of 80.5, substantially outperforming the best baseline (ABR at 51.1). The method also reports J=76.3 on SegTrackv2, where only the J metric is available for comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five baselines on DAVIS2016-Moving (J&F 89.5 vs. best baseline RCF-All at 79.6, a ~10-point gap), DAVIS2016 (J&F 90.9 vs. RCF-All at 80.7), and FBMS-59 (F 82.8 vs. ABR at 79.6). On SegTrackv2, where only the J metric is reported, RCF-All (79.6) and RCF-Stage1 (76.7) slightly exceed the proposed method (76.3), though the gap is modest. The advantage is most pronounced on DAVIS2016-Moving, where the trajectory-based approach nearly doubles the J&F of the weakest baseline (CIS at 66.2). On the fine-grained per-object task (DAVIS17-Moving), the method achieves J&F of 80.5 versus ABR's 51.1 and OCLR-TTA's 49.1, indicating a substantially larger margin in the harder per-object setting. All baselines rely on optical flow as their motion cue, whereas the proposed method uses long-range point trajectories, which the authors attribute as the key factor for robustness to occlusion, deformation, and long temporal spans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant optical-flow-based MOS paradigm (which suffers from short-range limitations and noise sensitivity) and from trajectory-based methods that rely on affinity matrices and spectral clustering (which struggle with global consistency and dynamic motion changes). Instead, it repurposes long-range point tracks (BootsTAP) as both motion cues and sparse prompts for SAM2, inspired by ParticleSFM's trajectory processing. The key architectural departure is the Motion-Semantic Decoupled Embedding, which processes DINOv2 features through a separate attention pathway rather than concatenating them with motion features, addressing the over-reliance on semantics that naive fusion causes. The iterative two-stage prompting strategy for SAM2 is a novel application of promptable segmentation to the MOS setting, where SAM2 previously had no mechanism to identify which objects are moving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10-point J&F gap over the best flow-based baseline on standard MOS benchmarks and the ~30-point gap in fine-grained per-object segmentation represent a substantial practical improvement, particularly for applications like autonomous driving and 4D reconstruction that require clean, per-object masks. The ablation revealing that naively injecting semantic features into the motion encoder degrades performance (63.0 vs 80.5 J&F) is a useful architectural lesson for multi-modal fusion in motion tasks. A key limitation is the dependence on off-the-shelf tracking quality (BootsTAP), which the authors acknowledge as a bottleneck; the approach's ceiling is tied to the tracker's robustness under occlusion and rapid motion. An open question is whether the learned trajectory encoder could be co-trained with the tracker to close this gap, and whether the SAM2 prompting strategy generalizes to longer sequences or higher frame rates.
