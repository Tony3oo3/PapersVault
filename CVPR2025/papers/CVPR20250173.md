## Problem

Existing generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods are designed for perspective images and struggle with wide-baseline panoramic inputs due to the large field of view, inherent equirectangular distortions, and the difficulty of learning precise geometry from sparse 360° views. NeRF-based panoramic methods (e.g., PanoGRF) handle wide baselines but rely on implicit representations requiring expensive volume sampling, making them unsuitable for real-time rendering on lightweight devices such as VR headsets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of wide-baseline 360° equirectangular panoramic images with known camera poses as input and operates entirely in the spherical domain without any explicit panorama-to-cubemap conversion. It outputs pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian primitives (centers, quaternions, spherical harmonics coefficients, scaling, and opacity) on the equirectangular grid, enabling real-time novel panoramic view synthesis via rasterization-based splatting, along with a per-pixel spherical depth map.

## Method

Splatter-360 introduces a 3D-aware bi-projection encoder that extracts features from both equirectangular and cube-map branches using a multi-view transformer with cross-view attention, and injects monocular depth features from a pre-trained DepthAnythingV2 network into the cube-map branch to compensate for ERP distortion near the poles. The key architectural contribution is a spherical cost volume constructed via a spherical sweep algorithm: depth candidates are sampled in spherical (r, θ, φ) space, 3D points are back-projected into source views using relative camera poses, and feature similarities are computed across views to form a volumetric cost map that avoids the behind-camera projection failure inherent to planar sweeps. A U-Net refines the initial cost volume to handle occlusions and textureless regions, and a softmax over depth candidates yields the final spherical depth estimate. A pixel-aligned Gaussian decoder then regresses all Gaussian parameters (centers from spherical depth, opacity from matching confidence, covariance and SH from convolutional layers) directly on the equirectangular grid, producing a feed-forward, end-to-end trainable pipeline that renders novel panoramic views in real time.

## Results

On HM3D, Splatter-360 achieves 28.293 PSNR, 0.875 SSIM, and 0.155 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest panoramic baseline PanoGRF (25.631 PSNR) by 2.66 dB and the fine-tuned MVSplat (27.179 PSNR) by 1.11 dB. On Replica, Splatter-360 reaches 29.888 PSNR, 0.924 SSIM, and 0.097 LPIPS, surpassing PanoGRF (27.920 PSNR) by 1.97 dB and fine-tuned MVSplat (28.399 PSNR) by 1.49 dB. Perspective-based methods (HiSplat, DepthSplat, pre-trained MVSplat) degrade severely on panoramic inputs, with PSNR values between 17.2–19.4, far below the panoramic-specific methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Splatter-360 consistently outperforms all four baselines across both datasets and all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). Against PanoGRF, the only other method designed specifically for wide-baseline panoramic inputs, Splatter-360 gains 2.66 dB PSNR on HM3D and 1.97 dB on Replica, while also improving SSIM by ~0.06 and reducing LPIPS by ~0.11. The fine-tuned MVSplat (trained on HM3D with cube-map inputs) is the closest competitor, trailing Splatter-360 by 1.11 dB on HM3D and 1.49 dB on Replica, confirming that even with panoramic-specific fine-tuning, perspective-based cost volumes cannot match spherical-domain matching. Pre-trained perspective methods (HiSplat, DepthSplat, MVSplat) suffer catastrophic degradation (PSNR 17–19) when applied to cube-map conversions of panoramas, highlighting the fundamental incompatibility of planar plane-sweep with 360° inputs. In depth estimation (Table 2), Splatter-360 also beats MVSplat on all four metrics (Abs Diff, Abs Rel, RMSE, δ<1.25) on both datasets, with δ<1.25 improving from 89.9% to 94.6% on Replica and 90.5% to 93.9% on HM3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

Splatter-360 extends the generalizable 3DGS line (MVSplat, HiSplat, DepthSplat) from perspective images to wide-baseline panoramic inputs, and adapts MVSplat's planar cost volume into a spherical cost volume via a spherical sweep algorithm to avoid the behind-camera projection failure inherent in cube-map decomposition. It departs from PanoGRF, the closest panoramic NVS method, by replacing implicit spherical radiance fields with explicit Gaussian primitives for real-time rasterization-based rendering. The bi-projection encoder draws on prior panorama depth estimation work (BiFuse, UniFuse) and incorporates monocular depth features from DepthAnythingV2, while the cross-view attention mechanism follows the multi-view transformer paradigm established in generalizable NeRF/3DGS encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that explicit 3DGS representations can match or exceed purpose-built implicit panoramic radiance fields (PanoGRF) while enabling real-time rendering on lightweight hardware, which is critical for VR/AR headsets and 360° video streaming. The spherical cost volume formulation is a clean geometric solution to a fundamental limitation of planar sweeping in wide-FoV settings, and is likely to be adopted by subsequent panoramic 3DGS work. Limitations include evaluation restricted to indoor scenes (HM3D, Replica), dependence on known camera poses, and reliance on a frozen monocular depth network; open questions include scalability to outdoor panoramas, dynamic scenes, and whether the approach generalizes to higher resolutions (4K+) without the memory overhead that motivated the spherical formulation.
