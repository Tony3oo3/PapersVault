## Problem

Open-Vocabulary Part Segmentation (OVPS) suffers from two unresolved challenges: part-level image-text correspondence is weaker than object-level due to the scarcity of part-level text-image pairs in VLM pretraining, and existing methods lack structural understanding of how parts relate to their parent object, leading to errors like misclassifying a leg as a tail. These issues cause state-of-the-art OVPS methods to produce structurally implausible segmentations, especially on unseen categories.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a set of text prompts covering both object-level categories (e.g., "bird") and object-specific part-level categories (e.g., "bird's head", "bird's wing"). It outputs pixel-level binary segmentation masks for each object class and each object-part class simultaneously. Evaluation follows two protocols: Pred-All, where the model must predict both objects and parts from scratch, and Oracle-Obj, where ground-truth object masks and labels are provided as input, isolating part-segmentation performance.

## Method

PartCATSeg extends the CAT-Seg cost aggregation framework by computing separate cost volumes for object-level and part-level text tokens, each refined through independent spatial (Swin Transformer) and class (Transformer) aggregation modules. The object and part cost features are then concatenated and projected into a unified object-aware part cost volume, which is further refined and aligned with object-specific part text embeddings (e.g., "bird's head") to produce the final part predictions. A compositional loss based on Jensen-Shannon divergence enforces that the softmax-normalized part distributions, when aggregated via a predefined part-to-object mapping, match the object-level distribution at every spatial location, compensating for scarce part annotations. DINO self-supervised features are injected into the Query and Key matrices of the spatial aggregation transformers as structural guidance, providing geometric proximity priors that improve boundary delineation and inter-part separation. The total training objective combines BCE losses for object, part, and object-part masks with the compositional loss, all trained end-to-end with fine-tuned CLIP query/value heads.

## Results

On Pascal-Part-116, PartCATSeg achieves 45.77 h-IoU in the Pred-All setting and 50.41 h-IoU in Oracle-Obj, surpassing the previous best (PartCLIPSeg) by +15.10 and +11.62 points respectively. On the more challenging ADE20K-Part-234, it reaches 24.19 h-IoU (Pred-All) and 49.96 h-IoU (Oracle-Obj), improving over PartCLIPSeg by +12.81 and +8.13. On PartImageNet, PartCATSeg sets new benchmarks at 55.12 h-IoU (Pred-All) and 72.66 h-IoU (Oracle-Obj), with gains of +27.79 and +18.81 over the prior state-of-the-art. Cross-dataset evaluation on PartImageNet (OOD) further confirms robustness, with 40.17 h-IoU (Pred-All) and 66.15 h-IoU (Oracle-Obj) on unseen classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartCATSeg outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. The closest competitor, PartCLIPSeg [14], is surpassed by a substantial margin: +15.10 h-IoU (Pred-All) and +11.62 (Oracle-Obj) on Pascal-Part-116, +12.81 and +8.13 on ADE20K-Part-234, and +27.79 and +18.81 on PartImageNet. CAT-Seg [13], the direct architectural predecessor, is beaten by +17.17 h-IoU (Pred-All) on Pascal-Part-116, confirming that the disentangled object/part cost aggregation and compositional loss provide meaningful gains over a single unified cost volume. CLIPSeg [47] and ZSSeg+ [69] trail significantly, with h-IoU gaps exceeding 25 points on Pascal-Part-116 Pred-All, highlighting the difficulty of part-level segmentation without explicit cost aggregation. PartGLEE [36] is competitive in Oracle-Obj on Pascal-Part-116 (37.11 vs. 50.41) but does not report Pred-All results, and is still outperformed by PartCATSeg by +13.30 h-IoU. VLPart [57] achieves the lowest performance among part-specific methods (14.39 h-IoU Pred-All on Pascal-Part-116), underscoring the limitations of relying solely on DINO-based image-to-image correspondence without structured image-text cost aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartCATSeg extends CAT-Seg's cost aggregation framework from object-level open-vocabulary segmentation to the finer-grained part-segmentation setting by disentangling object and part cost volumes and introducing an object-aware part cost that fuses both levels. It departs from VLPart's use of DINO features (which leveraged them for base-to-novel class matching) by repurposing DINO's pixel-level geometric priors as structural guidance within the spatial aggregation transformer. The compositional loss (Jensen-Shannon divergence between object and aggregated part distributions) is a new inductive bias not present in prior OVPS methods like PartCLIPSeg or PartGLEE, which relied solely on direct part supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a substantially stronger baseline for OVPS, with h-IoU gains of 10–28% over prior methods across three benchmarks, and the disentangled cost aggregation + compositional loss recipe is a clean, modular contribution that other OVPS methods can adopt. However, the approach still depends on CLIP's text encoder quality for part-level semantics (which the paper itself identifies as undertrained), and generalization beyond the three tested datasets—particularly to highly irregular or non-rigid part structures—remains unverified. Open questions include whether the compositional loss can be extended to multi-object scenes with overlapping parts and whether the DINO structural guidance transfers to other self-supervised backbones.
