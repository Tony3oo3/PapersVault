## Problem

Existing affine correspondence extraction methods rely on sparse detectors that fail in repetitive or weakly textured regions, or on computationally expensive view-synthesis pipelines like ASIFT. No prior approach effectively combines dense neural matching with explicit geometric (epipolar) constraints to jointly supervise both point correspondences and local affine shapes, leaving a gap in accuracy and robustness for downstream geometric estimation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251377/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images as input and produces affine correspondences, each consisting of a point pair and a 2×2 local affine transformation mapping the 32×32-pixel patch neighborhood in one image to its counterpart in the other. The dense matching sub-network outputs a full-resolution warp field and per-pixel certainty map from which point matches are sampled; the affine sub-network decomposes each transformation into orientation, scale, and a unit-determinant residual shape matrix, which are then synthesized into the final affine correspondence.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage decoupled pipeline. The first sub-network, built on DKM's architecture (ResNet-50 encoder, kernel-regression global matcher, and iterative refiners), predicts a dense warp and certainty; it is trained with a novel Sampson-distance-based point correspondence loss that enforces epipolar geometry on the predicted matches. The second sub-network estimates orientation and scale via a probabilistic covariant loss (discretizing continuous values into classification) and regresses the residual affine shape through a fully connected network; it is trained with a new Affine Sampson Distance loss that penalizes predicted affine transformations for violating the two-view epipolar constraint (Eq. 1–2). Training is decoupled: the matcher is trained to convergence first, then frozen while the affine extractor is trained from scratch, which the authors show reduces loss ambiguity and improves convergence. This geometric-constraint-driven supervision is the key differentiator from prior dense matchers that rely solely on photometric or descriptor-based losses.

## Results

On HPatches, the proposed method achieves the highest MMA score of 0.851 overall (0.908 illumination, 0.798 viewpoint), surpassing the previous best RoMA (0.843) and DKM (0.819). On MegaDepth-1500 relative pose estimation, the method with affine GC-RANSAC reaches 65.5 AUC@5°, outperforming RoMA (62.6) by 2.9 points and DKM (60.4) by 5.1 points. On KITTI, the method achieves the lowest average rotation RMSE (0.0289°) and translation RMSE (0.554°), beating RoMA (0.0343°, 0.587°) and all affine-based baselines (VLFeat, AffNet, ASIFT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 21 baselines across the three evaluation benchmarks. On HPatches image matching, it beats the closest competitor RoMA by 0.8 MMA points overall and 0.9 on viewpoint sequences, while surpassing DKM by 3.2 points. On MegaDepth relative pose estimation, the affine GC-RANSAC variant achieves 65.5 AUC@5°, exceeding RoMA (62.6) by 2.9 points and DKM (60.4) by 5.1 points; with standard RANSAC, the method (63.1) already matches or slightly exceeds RoMA (62.6). On KITTI, the method achieves the best average rotation RMSE (0.0289°) and translation RMSE (0.554°), improving over RoMA (0.0343°, 0.587°) and all affine-based methods (VLFeat 0.0429°/0.678°, AffNet 0.0326°/0.587°, ASIFT 0.0655°/0.934°). The method also wins on affine shape accuracy (Table 2), achieving 0.123 Euclidean distance and 0.994 cosine similarity versus VLFeat (0.202/0.988), AffNet (0.264/0.973), and ASIFT (0.329/0.894). No baseline is beaten by the proposed method on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DKM's dense kernelized matching architecture (global matcher + iterative refiners) as its point-correspondence backbone, and on AffNet's affine decomposition (scale, orientation, residual shape) for synthesizing local affine transformations. It departs from prior affine correspondence methods (AffNet, ASIFT, VLFeat) that rely on sparse detectors by replacing them with a dense warp, and from purely dense matchers (DKM, RoMA) by adding a dedicated affine shape estimation sub-network. The key conceptual departure is the use of epipolar geometry, formalized through a novel Affine Sampson Distance loss, as a training signal for the affine shape extractor — extending the epipolar-loss idea from CAPS to the affine domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Affine correspondences are a compact and powerful representation for relative pose, homography, and focal-length estimation, yet prior extractors suffered from limited match counts and accuracy under viewpoint change. By showing that geometric constraints can supervise both point matching and affine shape learning in a decoupled pipeline, this work provides a practical recipe for obtaining abundant, geometrically consistent affine correspondences without expensive view synthesis. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-view or 3D settings, whether the ResNet-50 backbone can be replaced by lighter architectures for real-time use, and whether the geometric-constraint losses transfer to other local geometric estimators beyond affine shapes.
