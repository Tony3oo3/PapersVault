## Problem

One-step diffusion (OSD) image super-resolution models, while reducing inference to a single denoising step, still incur high computational and storage costs (e.g., OSEDiff requires 1,303M parameters and 4,523G ops), making hardware deployment impractical. Existing low-bit quantization strategies designed for multi-step diffusion models fail on OSD architectures due to their complex multi-branch structure, the infeasibility of multi-step-specific calibration techniques, and imbalanced activation distributions. This paper is the first to investigate low-bit (6-bit and 8-bit) post-training quantization specifically for OSD-based SR.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (×4 degradation) and produces a corresponding high-resolution RGB image. Calibration is performed on 500 randomly cropped 128×128 LR-HR pairs from DIV2K_train, and evaluation is conducted on full-size images from RealSR, DRealSR, and DIV2K_val. The quantized model operates at W8A8 or W6A6 precision, compressing both weights and activations while maintaining the single-step inference pipeline.

## Method

PassionSR first simplifies OSEDiff to a UNet-VAE structure by removing the DAPE and CLIP Encoder branches and replacing them with a constant empty-prompt embedding, reducing parameters by 27% with negligible quality loss. The quantizer combines a Learnable Boundary Quantizer (LBQ), which makes the lower and upper clipping boundaries trainable parameters, with a Learnable Equivalent Transformation (LET) that applies channel-wise scale and shift factors to rebalance activation and weight distributions before quantization, inspired by SmoothQuant and OmniQuant. A Distributed Quantization Calibration (DQC) strategy then splits training into two stages—first optimizing LET scale/offset factors, then reinitializing and training LBQ boundaries—to stabilize gradient flow and accelerate convergence. Different loss functions are used for UNet (MSE in latent feature space via the one-step denoising transformation) and VAE (MSE between quantized and full-precision encoder/decoder outputs), with model-wise calibration to prevent error accumulation across modules.

## Results

On RealSR at W8A8, PassionSR achieves PSNR 25.67, SSIM 0.7499, and LPIPS 0.3140, matching or even exceeding the full-precision OSEDiff (PSNR 25.27, SSIM 0.7379, LPIPS 0.3027). At W6A6, PassionSR retains PSNR 25.15 and SSIM 0.7196 on RealSR, while all baselines drop below 20 PSNR. In terms of compression, PassionSR-UV at W8A8 achieves 81.77% parameter reduction and 76.56% operation reduction compared to OSEDiff, and at W6A6 reaches 86.32% and 82.42% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PassionSR decisively outperforms all four baselines (MaxMin, LSQ, Q-Diffusion, EfficientDM) across every reference metric on all three datasets at both W8A8 and W6A6. At W8A8 on RealSR, PassionSR achieves PSNR 25.67 vs. Q-Diffusion's 24.88 (the strongest baseline), a gap of 0.79 dB, while MaxMin, LSQ, and EfficientDM fall far behind (23.16, 15.39, 14.77 respectively). At W6A6, the gap widens dramatically: PassionSR reaches PSNR 25.15 while the best baseline Q-Diffusion only achieves 19.75, a 5.4 dB difference. Notably, PassionSR at W8A8 even surpasses the full-precision OSEDiff on PSNR (25.67 vs. 25.27) and SSIM (0.7499 vs. 0.7379) on RealSR, suggesting the quantization calibration process acts as a mild regularizer. The paper notes that some baselines (LSQ, Q-Diffusion) score higher on non-reference IQA metrics (e.g., MUSIQ, MANIQA) at W6A6, but attributes this to quantization noise inflating those scores while producing visibly worse images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OSEDiff as its one-step diffusion SR backbone and adapts quantization techniques from the multi-step diffusion literature (Q-Diffusion, PTQ4DM, EfficientDM) and LLM quantization (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant, LSQ) to the one-step setting. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that in one-step diffusion, the VAE dominates computational cost (>80% of MACs) rather than the UNet, fundamentally shifting where quantization effort must be focused compared to multi-step models. The model simplification—replacing the DAPE-CLIPEncoder branch with a constant empty-prompt embedding—enables a unified UNet-VAE calibration strategy that prior multi-step quantization methods could not support due to their branch-specific architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first PTQ study specifically targeting one-step diffusion SR, filling a gap between multi-step diffusion quantization work and the practical need to deploy fast SR models on resource-constrained hardware. The 81.77% parameter and 76.56% ops reduction at 8-bit with <1% PSNR loss makes one-step diffusion SR viable for mobile and edge deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 4-bit quantization, whether it generalizes to other one-step diffusion tasks (inpainting, restoration), and whether the calibration-free variant (MaxMin) can be improved to close the gap without any training. The observation that non-reference IQA metrics can be misleadingly high for noisy quantized outputs also raises questions about evaluation protocol design for low-bit generative models.
