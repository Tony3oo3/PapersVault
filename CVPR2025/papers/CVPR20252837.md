## Problem

Zero-shot subject-driven text-to-image generation requires capturing both the visual identity of a reference subject and the semantic content of a text prompt without per-subject fine-tuning. Existing encoder-based image prompting methods (e.g., IP-Adapter, BLIP-Diffusion) achieve on-the-fly generation but sacrifice subject alignment, particularly in preserving fine-grained details of the reference subject. This paper asks whether the emergent diptych generation capability of large-scale TTI models like FLUX can be reinterpreted as an inpainting task to achieve precise subject alignment without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference subject image and a free-form text prompt describing the desired context. The reference image is first processed through Grounding DINO (for object detection) and SAM (for segmentation) to isolate the subject from its background, then placed in the left 768×768 panel of a 768×1536 diptych canvas. The right panel is initialized as blank and designated for inpainting via a binary mask. The text prompt is embedded into a fixed diptych template that explicitly instructs the model to replicate the subject in the new context. Output is the generated right-panel image, and the framework extends to stylized image generation (using a style reference) and subject-driven image editing (placing the target image in the right panel with a partial mask).

## Method

Diptych Prompting reinterprets zero-shot subject-driven generation as text-conditioned diptych inpainting: the background-removed reference subject occupies the left panel, and FLUX with a ControlNet-Inpainting module fills the right panel based on a diptych text prompt, requiring no additional training. Two key techniques enhance performance: (1) background removal via Grounding DINO + SAM prevents content leakage (mirroring of background, pose, and location from the reference into the generated panel), and (2) reference attention enhancement rescales the attention weight submatrix between the right-panel query and left-panel key by a factor λ>1 within FLUX's MM-DiT attention blocks, strengthening the influence of the reference subject on fine-grained detail capture. The approach exploits FLUX's unique ability among open-source models to generate coherent diptychs with accurate per-panel context, a capability that smaller models (SD-v2, SD-XL, SD-3) lack. Inference runs 30 steps with guidance scale 3.5 and ControlNet conditioning scale 0.95.

## Results

On DreamBench (30 subjects × 25 prompts, 3000 images), Diptych Prompting achieves DINO 0.689, CLIP-I 0.758, and CLIP-T 0.344. It attains the best CLIP-T (text alignment) score among all encoder-based baselines, surpassing MS-Diff (0.321) and λ-Eclipse (0.307), while its DINO (subject alignment) score is competitive with the top methods (Kosmos-G 0.694, Subject-Diff 0.711). In the human preference study (450 responses per baseline), Diptych Prompting wins over every baseline in both subject and text alignment (p < 0.01, Wilcoxon signed-rank test), with win rates of 77.9–80.4% on subject alignment and 74.0–77.8% on text alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diptych Prompting outperforms all seven encoder-based baselines in the human preference study, winning 77.9–80.4% of pairwise comparisons on subject alignment and 74.0–77.8% on text alignment (p < 0.01). On the CLIP-T text-alignment metric, it achieves 0.344, beating ELITE (0.293), BLIP-Diff (0.300), Kosmos-G (0.287), Subject-Diff (0.303), λ-Eclipse (0.307), MS-Diff (0.321), and IP-Adapter SD-XL (0.292), though IP-Adapter FLUX edges it slightly at 0.351. On DINO subject alignment, Diptych Prompting (0.689) trails only Subject-Diff (0.711) and Kosmos-G (0.694) but clearly surpasses ELITE (0.621), BLIP-Diff (0.594), λ-Eclipse (0.613), MS-Diff (0.671), and both IP-Adapter variants (0.613, 0.561). The CLIP-I metric is the one area where Diptych Prompting (0.758) underperforms most baselines (0.771–0.847), which the authors attribute to the absence of a dedicated CLIP image encoder in their inpainting-based pipeline. Overall, the method trades a modest CLIP-I disadvantage for substantially better text alignment and human-perceived quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant zero-shot subject-driven generation paradigm that relies on specialized image encoders (CLIP, BLIP) to extract and inject subject features into a TTI model. Instead, it repurposes the emergent diptych generation capability of FLUX (an MM-DiT-based large-scale TTI model) by reframing subject-driven generation as a text-conditioned inpainting task on a two-panel canvas, using ControlNet for inpainting conditioning. The reference attention enhancement technique extends prior attention-manipulation work (Prompt-to-Prompt, Attend-and-Excite) from U-Net cross-attention to the joint self-attention blocks of transformer-based diffusion architectures, a largely unexplored direction. The background removal pipeline (Grounding DINO + SAM) is borrowed from segmentation literature to address a practical failure mode of naive diptych inpainting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that a single large-scale TTI model with an inpainting module can serve as a zero-shot subject-driven generator without any additional training, specialized encoders, or per-subject optimization, lowering the barrier to high-quality personalized generation. However, the approach is tightly coupled to FLUX's specific diptych generation quality—smaller models (SD-3, SD-XL) fail to produce coherent diptychs, limiting generalizability. The method also shows a consistent but modest disadvantage in CLIP-I scores relative to encoder-based baselines, suggesting that explicit image-encoder features still capture certain semantic aspects that attention-based panel referencing does not. Open questions include whether the diptych-inpainting formulation extends to multi-subject generation, video, or 3D, and whether the attention rescaling trick generalizes beyond FLUX's architecture.
