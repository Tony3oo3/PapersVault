## Problem

Existing Gaussian Avatar methods face a fundamental trade-off: multi-camera approaches achieve free-viewpoint rendering but require expensive capture rigs, while single-camera methods degrade severely outside the training viewpoint. Fitting an avatar to a single monocular image or video is ill-posed because the back and sides of the head are never observed, and no prior mechanism exists to propagate appearance information from visible to unseen regions in a Gaussian Splatting framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250596/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single frontal RGB image or a short monocular video (as captured by a webcam or smartphone) and produces a fully animatable mesh-attached Gaussian Avatar stored as an approximately 15 MB file. At inference time, no neural networks are required—only the 3DMM parameters and Gaussian attributes are used to pose and render the avatar, achieving 70 fps on an NVIDIA 4090 GPU. The output supports arbitrary 360° camera poses and expression animation via standard 3DMM controls.

## Method

GASP trains an auto-decoder generative prior over Gaussian Avatar parameters using 1000 synthetic identities with pixel-perfect 3DMM annotations, sidestepping the scarcity and annotation noise of real multi-view face datasets. Each Gaussian carries a learnable 8-dimensional feature vector that the MLP decoder maps (alongside a 512-d identity latent) to attribute offsets from a shared canonical template, and these features learn semantic correlations (e.g., hair color, skin tone) across the head. A three-stage fitting process—(1) inverting the identity latent with all else frozen, (2) fine-tuning the MLP decoder, (3) standard Gaussian Splatting optimization—progressively adapts the prior to the user's data while a prior-regularization loss prevents divergence. Crucially, freezing the per-Gaussian features during stages 2–3 forces semantically similar Gaussians (e.g., front and back hair) to receive consistent attribute updates, enabling the model to 'fill in' unseen regions and cross the synthetic-to-real domain gap without any neural network at render time.

## Results

On the NeRSemble dataset in the Monocular Video setting, GASP achieves 21.34 PSNR, 0.712 SSIM, 0.333 LPIPS, 117 FID, 0.568 ID-SIM, and 3.68/5 user quality rating, outperforming all baselines across every metric. In the Single Image setting, GASP reaches 20.73 PSNR and 0.677 SSIM, substantially exceeding FlashAvatar (13.26 PSNR) and GaussianAvatars (14.80 PSNR), which overfit severely with only one input frame. In the Multi-Camera setting, GASP is competitive (23.44 PSNR, 101 FID, 3.80/5 QUAL) though slightly behind FlashAvatar (24.73 PSNR) and GaussianAvatars (23.73 PSNR) on pixel-level metrics, while leading on FID and user-perceived quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GASP outperforms both Gaussian Avatar baselines—FlashAvatar [45] and GaussianAvatars [33]—by a wide margin in the Monocular and Single Image settings, gaining roughly 4 PSNR and 0.11 SSIM over FlashAvatar in the monocular case, and over 6 PSNR in the single-image case where those methods overfit and produce severe artifacts. Against DiffusionRig [8], GASP wins on every metric in both settings (e.g., 21.34 vs. 19.67 PSNR, 0.712 vs. 0.343 SSIM in monocular), with the paper noting DiffusionRig preserves fewer identity details. In the Single Image setting, GASP also beats ROME [21] on all metrics (20.73 vs. 15.78 PSNR, 0.526 vs. 0.408 ID-SIM) and HeadNeRF [18] on all but LPIPS (0.348 vs. 0.333), where HeadNeRF has a marginal edge. FLARE [2] is the weakest baseline, trailing GASP by over 6 PSNR in the monocular setting. In the Multi-Camera setting, GASP is slightly behind FlashAvatar and GaussianAvatars on PSNR/SSIM (23.44 vs. 24.73/23.73) but leads on FID (101 vs. 125/113) and user quality (3.80 vs. 3.70/3.65), suggesting the prior regularizes toward a more perceptually faithful solution at a small pixel-level cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

GASP builds directly on the mesh-attached Gaussian Splatting formulation from Qian et al. (Gaussian Avatars) and the UV-map-based initialization from FlashAvatar, combining them with an auto-decoder generative prior architecture inspired by 3D Gaussian Parametric Head Model (Xu et al.) and DeepSDF-style identity-conditioned decoders. The key departure from prior Gaussian Avatar work is the use of a synthetic-data-trained prior to fill in unseen views, rather than relying on multi-camera capture or accepting quality degradation at novel angles. Unlike few-shot avatar methods that use 2D image-space priors (DiffusionRig, ROME, StyleRig), GASP operates in 3D Gaussian parameter space, enabling true volumetric 360° rendering while keeping neural networks out of the inference loop entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a synthetic-data-trained prior can effectively bridge the domain gap to real data, enabling 360° animatable avatar creation from a single webcam image or short video at 70fps without any neural network at inference time — a practical milestone for consumer-facing applications like VR and video conferencing. The per-Gaussian semantic features that propagate appearance information from visible to unseen regions (e.g., front hair color to back of head) offer a general mechanism for handling occlusion in sparse-view 3D reconstruction. Open questions include whether the synthetic-looking back-of-head regions can be improved with diffusion-based 2D priors, whether a lighting model can be incorporated, and whether the approach transfers to real multi-view data once sufficient capture infrastructure exists.
