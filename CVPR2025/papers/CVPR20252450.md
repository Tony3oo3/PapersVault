## Problem

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with precise text-image alignment on compositional prompts involving multiple objects, attributes, counting, and spatial relations. Existing alignment approaches rely on labor-intensive human annotations, external reward models, or multi-step prompt engineering, limiting scalability and flexibility. Moreover, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), the standard alignment tool, requires modeling generation probabilities that are intractable for LMMs producing continuous visual features rather than discrete tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252450/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a compositional text prompt (e.g., specifying object counts, attribute bindings, spatial relations) and produces a photorealistic image via an LMM backbone that generates intermediate visual representations (discrete token sequences or continuous feature matrices of shape L×D), which are then decoded by a diffusion model. The self-feedback signal is a scalar alignment score computed as the average difference between 'yes' and 'no' answer probabilities across decomposed VQA questions, rather than a human-provided preference label.

## Method

SILMM is a model-agnostic, five-step iterative self-improvement loop: (1) generate compositional prompts, (2) sample diverse visual representations (nucleus sampling for discrete LMMs; DropDiv—activating dropout in MLP layers during inference, inspired by MC Dropout—for continuous LMMs), (3) decompose each prompt into atomic concepts and relations and generate yes/no questions, (4) score each generated image via VQA-based self-feedback, and (5) optimize the LLM backbone using preference learning. The key technical contribution for continuous LMMs is KC-DPO, which replaces the intractable log-likelihood ratio in DPO with a kernel-based distance (e.g., Frobenius norm or cosine similarity) between policy, reference, chosen, and rejected feature matrices, yielding a quadruplet objective that requires only one forward pass. The entire pipeline iterates with the updated model as the new reference, converging without any human annotation or external reward model.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench++, SILMM achieves the largest relative gains on the Attribute category: SEED-LLaMA improves from 19.20 to 39.60 (+106.25%) and DreamLLM from 22.94 to 39.94 (+74.15%). On DPG-Bench (All), SEED-LLaMA rises from 47.12 to 57.31 (+21.63%) and DreamLLM from 53.93 to 64.22 (+19.08%). On TIFA, improvements are more modest: SEED-LLaMA goes from 66.74 to 73.74 (+10.49%) and DreamLLM from 69.91 to 75.38 (+7.82%). Emu-3, already a strong discrete LMM, shows smaller absolute gains (e.g., DPG-Bench All 74.19→77.45, +4.39%) but still benefits from SILMM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SILMM consistently outperforms its own base LMMs across all three benchmarks: SEED-LLaMA gains +106% on T2I-CompBench++ Attribute and +21.6% on DPG-Bench All; DreamLLM gains +74% and +19.1% respectively; Emu-3 gains +33.3% and +4.4%. However, even after SILMM tuning, the LMMs still trail specialized T2I models: SEED-LLaMA+SILMM (39.60) remains well below DALL-E 3 (70.09) and PixArt-α (60.31) on T2I-CompBench++ Attribute, and DreamLLM+SILMM (64.22) lags SDXL (74.65) and DALL-E 3 (83.50) on DPG-Bench All. The paper attributes this gap to LMMs ignoring compositional alignment during pre-training. Among the three LMMs, SEED-LLaMA shows the largest relative improvement, which the authors attribute to its weaker baseline and the stability of discrete DPO over the simplified continuous KC-DPO. Emu-3, already the strongest discrete LMM, shows the smallest gains (e.g., 0.00% on DPG-Bench Global), suggesting diminishing returns when the base model is already well-aligned.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO-based alignment from discrete LLMs to the multimodal generation setting, adapting it to both discrete visual token LMMs (SEED-LLaMA, Emu-3) and continuous feature LMMs (DreamLLM) where standard DPO is intractable. It departs from prior T2I alignment work (RLHF, RLAIF, layout planning) by eliminating dependence on human annotations or external reward models, instead leveraging the LMM's own discriminative capabilities for self-feedback. The DropDiv strategy repurposes MC Dropout as a diversification mechanism for continuous representations, and KC-DPO generalizes the DPO objective to a kernel-based quadruplet distance formulation over feature matrices, drawing on kernel methods literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SILMM provides a scalable, annotation-free pathway for LMMs to iteratively improve their compositional T2I alignment, which is practically important as LMMs increasingly replace specialized diffusion pipelines for flexible generation. However, gains remain modest for layout, spatial relation, and complex composition categories, reflecting the persistent weakness of LMMs in compositional cross-modal reasoning. Open questions include whether the self-improvement loop can be extended beyond a few iterations before quality plateaus, whether the self-feedback signal remains reliable as the model improves, and whether KC-DPO generalizes to other continuous-output generative tasks beyond T2I.
