## Problem

Self-labeling methods for scribble-supervised segmentation rely on hard pseudo-labels that cannot represent class uncertainty or estimation errors at unlabeled pixels, and standard Potts relaxations (bilinear, quadratic) introduce spurious local minima that distort the original discrete smoothness constraint. Additionally, the standard cross-entropy loss forces the network to replicate the uncertainty of soft pseudo-labels rather than learning decisive predictions, degrading performance under label noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251901/cropped_page5_idx16.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images annotated with scribbles (mouse-drawn strokes covering <3% of pixels) as the only supervision signal; during inference, no scribbles are needed. The output is a per-pixel class probability map over K classes (21 for VOC, 19 for Cityscapes, 150 for ADE20K). The method is architecture-agnostic, operating on standard backbones (DeepLabV3+ with ResNet101/MobileNetV2, or ViT-base with linear decoder) without any structural modifications.

## Method

The paper formulates a joint self-labeling loss via ADM splitting that introduces soft pseudo-labels (full categorical distributions over K classes) as auxiliary variables, decoupling the problem into a network-training sub-problem and a pseudo-label-optimization sub-problem. For the network-training term, the authors propose collision cross-entropy H_CCE = −ln(σᵀy), which is symmetric and avoids mimicking uncertainty in either direction, outperforming standard and reverse cross-entropy. For the Potts pairwise term, they introduce normalized quadratic and log-based relaxations (collision divergence) that eliminate the local minima of standard bilinear and quadratic relaxations while preserving tightness to the discrete Potts model. A general GPU-friendly gradient descent solver optimizes the pseudo-labels over nearest-neighbor neighborhoods, and the entire procedure is provably convergent. Using only 3% scribbles on PASCAL VOC 2012, the method achieves 78.1 mIoU with DeepLabV3+ (ResNet101), matching or exceeding full pixel-precise supervision.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012 with full-length scribbles, the proposed soft self-labeling method (HCCE+PCD) achieves 77.7 mIoU with DeepLabV3+ (ResNet101, batch 12), outperforming full pixel-precise supervision (76.6 mIoU) on the same architecture. With a ViT-linear backbone, the method reaches 80.94 mIoU without any pretraining, approaching the 81.4 mIoU full-supervision baseline. On Cityscapes and ADE20K, the method is also evaluated, confirming generalizability across datasets and architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (HCCE+PCD, soft self-labeling) outperforms all Potts-based WSSS baselines: DenseCRF loss (75.8), GridCRF loss (75.6), NonlocalCRF loss (75.7), and ScribbleSup (63.1), with margins of 1.9–14.6 mIoU. Against specialized scribble-based systems requiring architectural modifications or ad-hoc training, it beats SEMINAR (76.2) by 1.5 mIoU, TEL (77.1) by 0.6 mIoU, and all V2-based methods (BPG 73.2, URSS 74.6, SPML 74.2, PSI 74.9) by 2.8–4.5 mIoU. The only method it does not surpass is AGMM (78.7 with modified ViT-linear), which uses a different architecture variant. Notably, with DeepLabV3+ at batch size 12, the scribble-supervised result (77.7) exceeds the full-supervision baseline (76.6) by 1.1 mIoU, and with ViT-linear the method reaches 80.94 mIoU without pretraining, within 0.5 mIoU of full supervision (81.4). The ablation in Table 3 further shows that the log-based collision divergence PCD (71.22) and normalized quadratic PNQ (71.12) consistently outperform the standard bilinear PBL (67.24) and quadratic PQ (71.05) relaxations across all scribble lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ADM-based self-labeling framework of Marin & Boykov (CVPR 2019), which introduced hard pseudo-labels as auxiliary variables for optimizing Potts-regularized segmentation losses, by replacing them with soft (distributional) pseudo-labels that can represent class uncertainty. It also builds on the scribble-supervised segmentation paradigm initiated by ScribbleSup (Lin et al., CVPR 2016) and the broader family of Potts-model relaxations (bilinear, quadratic, total variation) studied in segmentation. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of collision cross-entropy and collision divergence as principled alternatives to standard cross-entropy when targets are uncertain, motivated by the observation that standard cross-entropy forces the network to mimic label uncertainty rather than remain decisive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a mathematically well-founded, convergent training procedure on completely standard architectures (DeepLabV3+, ViT) can match or exceed complex specialized WSSS systems requiring architectural modifications and ad-hoc multi-stage training, which is practically significant for reproducibility and adoption. The finding that nearest-neighbor neighborhoods outperform dense neighborhoods under self-labeling (71.1% vs 67.9% mIoU) challenges the prior assumption from gradient-descent-based methods that larger neighborhoods are always beneficial, suggesting the optimal regularization structure depends on the optimization strategy. Open questions include whether the collision cross-entropy generalizes to other weakly-supervised settings (class tags, boxes) as the authors suggest, and whether the modest margin over full supervision (78.1 vs 76.6 mIoU) holds across more diverse datasets and architectures.
