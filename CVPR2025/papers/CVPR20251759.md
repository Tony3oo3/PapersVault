## Problem

Open-universe 3D layout generation requires simultaneously satisfying physical plausibility (no collisions, objects within room boundaries) and semantic coherence (faithfully following open-ended language instructions). Existing LLM-based methods that predict numerical poses directly produce physically infeasible layouts, while constraint-satisfaction approaches like Holodeck fail to scale to large asset counts or capture the nuanced semantics of language instructions. No prior method effectively bridges this gap between semantic expressiveness and physical precision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language layout instruction, a room defined by four walls, and a set of N unlabeled 3D meshes (with axis-aligned bounding box dimensions and VLM-annotated front-facing orientations) as input. The VLM consumes rendered top-down and side-view images of the scene and individual assets, augmented with visual marks: coordinate points spaced 2 meters apart, coordinate frame visualizations, and directional arrows indicating object front orientations. The output is a set of 3D poses (x, y, z, θ_z) for every asset, produced by optimizing over the VLM-generated initial layout and spatial relations.

## Method

LAYOUTVLM uses a VLM (GPT-4o) to generate a scene layout representation consisting of two mutually reinforcing components: numerical pose estimates serving as optimization initialization, and a set of five differentiable spatial relations (distance, on_top_of, align_with, point_towards, against_wall) that encode layout semantics. A self-consistent decoding step retains only those spatial relations already satisfied by the predicted numerical poses, ensuring the optimization preserves the most critical semantics. The final layout is produced by jointly minimizing a semantic loss (from the retained spatial relations) and a physics loss (Distance-IoU on 3D oriented bounding boxes for collision avoidance) via projected gradient descent, with periodic projection back into the room boundary. Visual prompting with coordinate marks and asset renderings improves the VLM's spatial grounding, and the framework supports fine-tuning open-source VLMs (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave via LoRA) on 3D-Front data to further improve layout generation quality.

## Results

Across 11 room types (up to 80 assets per room), LAYOUTVLM achieves a Physically-Grounded Semantic Alignment (PSA) score of 58.8, outperforming the best baseline I-Design (18.0) by 40.8 points. LAYOUTVLM also achieves the highest In-Boundary (IB) score of 94.9% versus 34.3% for I-Design and 24.2% for LayoutGPT, indicating far fewer objects placed outside room boundaries. On positional coherence, LayoutGPT leads (80.8%) but at the cost of physical feasibility, while LAYOUTVLM balances both (77.5% Pos., 81.8% CF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAYOUTVLM decisively outperforms all three baselines on the overall PSA metric (58.8 vs. 18.0 for I-Design, 16.6 for LayoutGPT, and 5.6 for Holodeck). Against LayoutGPT, which achieves the highest positional coherence (80.8%) by directly predicting numerical poses, LAYOUTVLM trades ~3 points of positional accuracy for a massive gain in physical feasibility (IB: 94.9% vs. 24.2%), meaning LayoutGPT frequently places objects outside room boundaries. Against Holodeck, which uses a constraint-satisfaction search approach, LAYOUTVLM wins on every metric; Holodeck's search-based solver fails to find feasible solutions for scenes with many assets, yielding the lowest PSA (5.6) and IB (8.1%). Against I-Design, the closest competitor, LAYOUTVLM improves PSA by 40.8 points and IB by 60.6 points, while I-Design retains a modest edge in positional coherence (68.3% vs. 77.5%—actually LAYOUTVLM leads here too). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that removing the differentiable optimization (w/o Const.) collapses PSA to 6.7, and removing self-consistency decoding drops PSA from 58.8 to 46.4, validating that both the optimization and the VLM-generated spatial relations are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based open-universe layout generation line (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, I-Design) by replacing text-only LLMs with VLMs that consume rendered scene and asset images, and by substituting Holodeck's constraint-satisfaction search with a differentiable optimization loop over a dual representation (numerical poses + spatial relations). It repurposes visual prompting techniques originally developed for 2D visual grounding (set-of-mark, coordinate frames, directional arrows) for 3D spatial planning, and introduces a self-consistent decoding step that requires the two VLM-predicted representations to mutually agree before optimization. The differentiable spatial relations (distance, on_top_of, align_with, point_towards, against_wall) are conceptually similar to Holodeck's scene-graph constraints but are formulated as soft, differentiable objectives rather than hard constraints solved by search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core tension in 3D scene generation—physical plausibility versus semantic coherence—that has limited prior methods to either colliding layouts (LayoutGPT) or infeasible constraint solving at scale (Holodeck). The finding that fine-tuning an open-source VLM (LLaVA-NeXT) on the proposed scene representation lifts its PSA from 0.7 to 39.5 suggests a practical pathway for democratizing 3D layout generation beyond closed-source models. Limitations include dependence on upright-object assumptions, occasional failures from poor VLM initialization, and evaluation on a single VLM (GPT-4o) for the main pipeline; open questions include scaling to non-upright objects, multi-room scenes, and whether the self-consistency mechanism generalizes to more complex spatial reasoning tasks.
