## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods struggle to simultaneously preserve instance-specific fine-grained details and class-general shared patterns: class-general approaches (e.g., SRe²L) update all pixels and lose fine details, while instance-specific approaches (e.g., RDED) neglect broader class structure. Additionally, large-scale distillation relies on storing full soft labels (1,000-dim vectors per augmented instance), incurring over 120 GB of storage for ImageNet-1k at 200 IPC, which is impractical for many settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image dataset (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-scale) with class labels and a pretrained teacher model (e.g., ResNet-18). It produces a synthetic dataset of IPC images per class, where each image is assembled from β resized patches of original images, plus two scalar cross-entropy distance values per instance (d_org^T and d_aug^T) that replace the full 1,000-dimensional soft label vector, achieving a 500× storage reduction on ImageNet-1k.

## Method

NRR-DD operates in three stages. First, Critical-based Initial Data Discovery (CIDD) computes Class Activation Maps on original images, crops them into patches, and selects the top-t patches by CAM score but then keeps only those with the *lowest* teacher confidence (hardest-to-learn samples), combining β patches per synthetic image. Second, Non-Critical Region Refinement (NRR) generates a pixel-wise mask M = max{0, ε − C} from the CAM of each synthetic image and applies gradient updates weighted by M, so critical (high-CAM) pixels are frozen to preserve instance-specific details while non-critical pixels are optimized with a CE + BatchNorm-regularization loss to absorb class-general information. Third, Distance-Based Representative (DBR) knowledge transfer replaces soft-label storage with two scalar CE distances between the teacher's prediction on a CutMix/Mixup-augmented mixed image and the one-hot labels of the original and augmented instances; the student is trained to match these distances (L_dbr) while also keeping its own distances below a threshold (L_sce), requiring only 0.2 GB for ImageNet-1k at 200 IPC versus 120 GB for soft labels.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with 50 IPC using ResNet-18, NRR-DD achieves 60.2% top-1 accuracy, outperforming RDED (56.5%) by 3.7 points and SRe²L (46.8%) by 13.4 points. On CIFAR-10 with 10 IPC, NRR-DD reaches 72.2% accuracy with ResNet-18, a 35-point improvement over RDED (37.1%). The method also achieves 85.6% on ImageNette (50 IPC) and 61.2% on Tiny-ImageNet (50 IPC), consistently leading all baselines across every dataset and IPC setting. With the DBR knowledge transfer variant, NRR-DD recovers up to 80% of full soft-label performance while storing only two scalar distances per instance (a 500× storage reduction on ImageNet-1k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NRR-DD outperforms all five baselines (MTT, TESLA, SRe²L, RDED, IDM) on every dataset and IPC setting evaluated. Against the closest competitor RDED [Sun et al., CVPR 2024], NRR-DD gains 3.7 points on ImageNet-1k (60.2% vs 56.5% at 50 IPC) and a dramatic 35 points on CIFAR-10 (72.2% vs 37.1% at 10 IPC), attributed to NRR's preservation of instance-specific details while enriching non-critical regions with class-general features. Against SRe²L [Yin et al., NeurIPS 2024], which updates all pixels to capture class-general features, NRR-DD leads by 13.4 points on ImageNet-1k (50 IPC) and 24.8 points on CIFAR-10 (10 IPC), confirming that indiscriminate pixel updates destroy fine-grained information. Against trajectory-matching (MTT) and distribution-matching (IDM) baselines, NRR-DD achieves 14–35 point improvements, reflecting the advantage of patch-based initial data discovery over gradient or distribution alignment. The DBR knowledge transfer variant matches or exceeds RDED-with-soft-labels at IPC=1 on both ImageNette and ImageNet-1k while requiring only 0.2 GB of label storage versus >120 GB for full soft labels (a 500× reduction).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends RDED [21], adopting its patch-based synthetic image construction but inverting the selection criterion: instead of choosing highest-confidence patches (easiest to classify), it selects lowest-confidence patches (hardest to learn) to reduce overfitting. It also incorporates the Batch Normalization regularization from SRe2L [27] for class-general feature alignment. The key conceptual departure is the Non-Critical Region Refinement stage, which uses Class Activation Mapping to create a pixel-wise mask that restricts gradient updates to non-critical regions only, thereby preserving instance-specific fine-grained details while enriching the background with class-general information. The Distance-Based Representative (DBR) knowledge transfer further departs from the soft-label paradigm used by SRe2L, TESLA, and RDED by replacing 1000-dimensional soft labels with two scalar cross-entropy distances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 500× storage reduction for knowledge transfer (120 GB → 0.2 GB on ImageNet1k at 200 IPC) makes large-scale dataset distillation practically deployable in memory-constrained settings, addressing a bottleneck that has limited adoption of soft-label-based methods. The consistent SOTA performance across five datasets and multiple architectures (ConvNet, ResNet-18, ResNet-101, MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-B0) suggests the approach generalizes well. However, the method's reliance on CAM quality introduces a dependency on the pretrained teacher's training quality, and the authors acknowledge that DBR may not fully preserve fine-grained information for complex tasks. An open question is whether the two-scalar distance representation can be further extended to capture inter-class relationships that soft labels encode implicitly.
