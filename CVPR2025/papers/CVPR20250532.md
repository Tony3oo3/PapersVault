## Problem

Mamba-based models for video frame interpolation suffer from three key limitations: (1) converting 2D images to 1D token sequences via scanning introduces historical decay, weakening spatial modeling of neighboring pixels especially in high-resolution frames; (2) window-based local scanning creates severe spatial discontinuities at window boundaries; and (3) existing approaches struggle to simultaneously capture fine-grained spatial details and inter-frame temporal motion. These issues limit the ability of state space models to match or exceed CNN/Transformer-based VFI methods on high-resolution and complex-motion benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of input video frames (I_0, I_1) at resolutions ranging from 256×256 (Vimeo90K, UCF101) up to 4K (Xiph-4K) and produces a single intermediate frame I_t at a specified temporal position between them. A CNN-based low-level feature extractor first decomposes each frame into three pyramid levels (quarter-resolution per level with doubled channels), and the Mamba-based spatiotemporal feature extractor operates on the coarsest level to produce motion feature maps. Three model variants are provided (Ours-E at 6.7M, Ours-B at 16.2M, Ours-P at 25.4M parameters) to trade off speed and accuracy, with window sizes of 8 or 16 pixels depending on the variant.

## Method

LC-Mamba replaces the standard 2D selective scan in VMamba's VSS block with a Hilbert curve-based selective scan (H-SS2D) that preserves spatial adjacency when flattening 2D feature maps into 1D sequences, combined with a shifted-window strategy (8×8 windows, cyclically shifted by M/2 pixels between successive layers) to balance local detail capture with cross-window connectivity. The Hilbert curve is further extended to 3D (H-SS3D) by interleaving tokens from both input frames along the 2D scan path, enabling simultaneous modeling of spatial and temporal correlations in a single 1D sequence. Four scan directions (horizontal, vertical, and their reverses) are used to compensate for information attenuation along any single path. The full pipeline comprises a CNN low-level feature extractor, hierarchical LC-Mamba blocks for spatiotemporal feature extraction, a motion estimator producing bidirectional flows and a blending mask, and a residual refiner, trained end-to-end with Charbonnier, census, and Laplacian pyramid reconstruction losses plus a warp loss.

## Results

The proposed LC-Mamba (Ours-P, 25.4M params) achieves 36.53 dB PSNR / 0.9816 SSIM on Vimeo90K, 35.42 dB on UCF101, 36.99 dB on Xiph-2K, and 34.49 dB on Xiph-4K, outperforming prior models by +0.03 dB on Vimeo90K. The efficient variant (Ours-E, 6.7M params) reaches 36.19 dB on Vimeo90K and 34.26 dB on Xiph-4K, matching or exceeding models with 2–3× more parameters. On SNU-FILM, Ours-P scores 40.16/36.17/30.72/25.48 dB (Easy/Medium/Hard/Extreme), and on Middlebury it records 1.92 IE. The balanced variant (Ours-B, 16.2M) attains 36.43 dB on Vimeo90K and 36.90 dB on Xiph-2K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ours-P (25.4M) outperforms VFIFormer (24.1M) on Vimeo90K (+0.03 dB), Xiph-2K/4K (where VFIFormer runs out of memory), and all four SNU-FILM difficulty levels, while trailing slightly on Middlebury IE (1.92 vs 1.82). Against EMA (65.6M), Ours-P wins on Vimeo90K, UCF101, Xiph-2K, and SNU-FILM Easy/Medium but loses on Xiph-4K (34.49 vs 34.54), Middlebury, and SNU-FILM Hard/Extreme. The full VFIMamba (66.1M) remains ahead on Xiph-2K (37.02 vs 36.99), Middlebury (1.89 vs 1.92), and all SNU-FILM categories, though Ours-P edges it on Vimeo90K (36.53 vs 36.45) and Xiph-4K (34.49 vs 34.39). The efficient Ours-E (6.7M) surpasses VFIMamba-S (16.8M) on Vimeo90K (36.19 vs 36.09) and ties on Xiph-4K, but trails on SNU-FILM by 0.3–0.5 dB across difficulty levels. Ours-B (16.2M) beats VFIFormer-S (17.1M) on Vimeo90K (+0.06 dB), Xiph-2K (+0.35 dB), Xiph-4K (+0.89 dB), and SNU-FILM Easy/Medium, while VFIFormer-S retains an advantage on Middlebury (1.89 vs 1.89, tied) and SNU-FILM Hard/Extreme.

## Relation to Prior Work

LC-Mamba extends the Mamba-based VFI line initiated by VFIMamba [47], which itself builds on VMamba's VSS block [20] and the original Mamba selective state space [10]. The key departure is replacing the standard 2D cross-scanning (SS2D) with a Hilbert curve-based selective scan (H-SS2D/H-SS3D) to preserve spatial continuity, combined with a shifted-window mechanism borrowed from Swin Transformer [22] to balance local detail capture with global context. The 3D temporal extension (H-SS3D) follows the interleaved scanning paradigm from VideoMamba [17] and VFIMamba [47], but applies it within the Hilbert-curve windowed framework rather than globally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that careful 2D-to-1D scanning geometry (Hilbert curves) combined with windowed locality can make Mamba-based VFI competitive with much larger models (25.4M vs. 66.1M parameters for VFIMamba) while maintaining strong high-resolution performance. However, the absolute gains are modest (+0.03 dB on Vimeo-90k), and the authors acknowledge that Mamba's linear recurrence still limits true global modeling compared to attention-based methods. Open questions include whether the Hilbert-curve windowed approach generalizes to other SSM-based video tasks (e.g., video understanding, generation) and whether the shifted-window mechanism can be made more adaptive to motion magnitude.
