## Problem

Existing GZSL methods fine-tune the entire visual backbone with limited seen-class data, risking overfitting on seen classes and producing features that generalize poorly to unseen categories. Moreover, visual-semantic alignment is typically performed only at the final layer, limiting the depth of visual-semantic interaction throughout the network. This paper addresses both issues by proposing a parameter-efficient prompt-based adaptation strategy that injects semantic information into intermediate transformer layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252112/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed into 16×16 patches by ViT-Base) and a shared semantic attribute matrix S ∈ R^{N_a × D} extracted via GloVe word vectors as inputs. During inference, the CLS token is compared against projected class prototypes, and an explicit calibration term τ·I_{ŷ∈Y^u} is added to unseen-class scores to balance the prediction bias between seen and unseen classes, producing a single class label from the union of seen and unseen categories.

## Method

VSPCN introduces two learnable prompt tokens—a visual prompt and a semantic prompt—concatenated with the CLS token and image patches before entering a pre-trained ViT-Base backbone. In the first 6 (shallow) layers, weak prompt fusion uses standard cross-attention to let the visual prompt attend to image tokens and the semantic prompt attend to GloVe attributes, enriching the randomly initialized prompts. In deeper layers, strong prompt fusion employs attention with a learned bias term (weighted by α_v and α_s) plus a residual connection to inject complementary information, while a semantic adapter progressively updates the attribute matrix S by attending to image features for instance-level adaptation. Training combines a base loss (cross-entropy classification + MSE semantic regression), a cross-entropy-based divergence loss that encourages the visual prompt to learn complementary discriminative information relative to the CLS token, and a Jensen-Shannon divergence–based semantic knowledge distillation loss that aligns the semantic prompt with its class prototype.

## Results

VSPCN achieves the best CZSL accuracy (Acc) of 80.6%, 75.3%, and 76.6% on CUB, SUN, and AWA2 respectively. In the GZSL setting, it attains the highest harmonic mean (H) of 75.7%, 53.8%, and 77.6% on the three datasets, with unseen-class accuracy (U) of 72.8%, 59.4%, and 71.8%. These results are obtained using a ViT-Base backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-1k, outperforming methods that use larger ViT-Large backbones pre-trained on ImageNet-21k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSPCN outperforms all 19 baselines across all three datasets in both CZSL and GZSL settings. In CZSL, it surpasses the strongest prior methods ZSLViT (78.9% on CUB, 68.3% on SUN, 70.7% on AWA2) by +1.7%, +7.0%, and +5.9% respectively, and ZSCLR (77.8%, 66.3%, 76.0%) by +2.8%, +9.0%, and +0.6%. In GZSL (harmonic mean H), VSPCN leads PSVMA (73.8, 52.3, 75.4) by +1.9%, +1.5%, and +2.2%, and ZSLViT (73.6, 47.3, 74.2) by +2.1%, +6.5%, and +3.4%. Notably, VSPCN uses a ViT-Base backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-1k, yet still exceeds ViT-Large-based methods (ViT-ZSL, IEAM-ZSL) that rely on ImageNet-21k pre-training, demonstrating the effectiveness of the dual-prompt collaboration over simply scaling the backbone. Among generative-based methods, the gap is even larger: VSPCN's H of 75.7/53.8/77.6 exceeds the best generative method ICCE (66.4/–/72.8) by substantial margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant GZSL paradigm of fine-tuning the entire ViT backbone with limited seen-class data (as in ZSLViT and PSVMA) by adopting prompt tuning as a parameter-efficient alternative. It extends the prompt learning literature (DFSP, CSP, SP, L2P, Dualprompt), which has so far focused on single visual prompts, by introducing a dual-prompt architecture where a visual prompt and a semantic prompt are jointly learned and fused at different network depths. The weak/strong fusion design with attention bias in deeper layers, combined with a semantic adapter for instance-level attribute adaptation, represents a novel architectural contribution that bridges prompt learning and cross-modal GZSL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-prompt framework offers a practical, parameter-efficient path to adapting pre-trained ViTs for GZSL without the overfitting risk of full fine-tuning, which is particularly relevant as GZSL moves toward larger backbones and more diverse datasets. However, the evaluation is limited to three small-scale benchmarks (CUB, SUN, AWA2) with GloVe embeddings, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale or more modern semantic representations. The reliance on a hand-tuned calibration parameter τ at inference and the specific layer split (l=6) for weak vs. strong fusion also raise questions about robustness across architectures and dataset scales.
