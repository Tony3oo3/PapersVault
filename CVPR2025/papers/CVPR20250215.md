## Problem

Brain tumor segmentation with missing MRI modalities (e.g., T1ce unavailable due to contrast allergies) requires maximizing information extraction from available modalities. Existing Mamba-based approaches flatten 3D volumes into 1D sequences, destroying spatial structure, and the diminishing influence of distant tokens in recurrent SSMs makes it difficult to capture both local spatial inductive biases and long-range semantic correlations simultaneously—critical for tumor heterogeneity characterization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250215/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes four MRI modalities (FLAIR, T1ce, T1, T2) at 240×240×155 resolution, each providing both an 80×80×80 cropped volume for segmentation and a full global volume. Missing modalities are handled by substituting zero-value feature maps before concatenation. The global volumes are processed with a relative position mask highlighting the cropped region, then adaptively pooled to match cropped dimensions. Output is a per-voxel segmentation mask for three nested tumor subregions: Whole Tumor (WT), Tumor Core (TC), and Enhancing Tumor (ET).

## Method

LS3M introduces a differentiable learnable sorting process that replaces non-differentiable token reordering with a permutation matrix generated via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization followed by the Sinkhorn operator. A SortNet (3D convolution with C×C×3×3×3 kernel + similarity map) produces patient-specific scores capturing both 26-neighborhood spatial inductive bias and long-range semantic correlations, which are converted into a doubly stochastic permutation matrix that reorders the flattened 1D sequence so relevant tokens precede the target. The reordered sequence is then processed by the S3M block—a series connection of forward and backward Mamba-like SSM blocks with channel attention—where the series (rather than parallel) design allows forward-scanned information to be relayed back to earlier tokens. Permutation matrices are scaled via bilinear interpolation for multi-scale feature maps and fused with a multi-modal availability code for inter-modal sequence reordering. A global input strategy feeds full-volume features (with relative position mask) into the encoder alongside cropped volumes, providing broader context without prohibitive computational cost.

## Results

On BraTS2020, LS3M achieves average DSC of 88.22% (WT), 79.76% (TC), and 63.60% (ET) across all 16 modality-availability combinations, outperforming the second-best method (GSS*) by 1.22%, 1.31%, and 2.3% respectively. The method shows particularly strong gains in the most challenging single-modality settings, e.g., 91.00% WT DSC with only FLAIR available vs. 89.51% for GSS*, and 79.17% ET DSC with only T1ce vs. 74.20% for GSS*. Ablations confirm that the learnable sorting process (SortP) contributes an additional ~1% DSC over the S3M-only variant, and the global input strategy adds ~0.8% over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LS3M outperforms all nine baselines on the average DSC for every subregion (WT, TC, ET) on BraTS2020. The closest competitor is GSS* (reproduced by the authors), which trails by 1.22% on WT, 1.31% on TC, and 2.45% on ET; M3AE is the second-closest on ET (61.30% vs. 63.60%). RFNet, the strongest convolutional baseline, leads LS3M on no subregion but remains competitive on WT (86.76% vs. 88.22%). Transformer-based methods (mmFormer, UNet-MFI) and distillation-based methods (MMANet, GSS) consistently underperform LS3M by 3–6% on TC and ET, where missing-modality information loss is most acute. The gap widens in single-modality settings: with only T1ce available, LS3M achieves 79.17% ET DSC versus 74.20% (GSS*) and 74.66% (M3AE), a 4–5% advantage that highlights the benefit of the learnable sorting mechanism for long-range semantic correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba's selective state space model, adapting it for 3D brain MRI segmentation where fixed scanning strategies (bi-directional, four-directional, omni-directional) used by prior vision-Mamba methods (Vim, SS2D, LocalMamba) fail to preserve 3D spatial structure after flattening. The key departure is replacing these fixed scans with a differentiable, patient-specific sorting mechanism that uses Sinkhorn-Knopp normalization and Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization to generate permutation matrices, dynamically reordering tokens so that spatially proximate and semantically related tokens are positioned adjacently before SSM processing. The series (rather than parallel) connection of forward and backward Mamba blocks in the S3M module, combined with a global input strategy using relative position masks, further distinguishes LS3M from both prior Mamba-based segmentation methods and conventional U-Net architectures for incomplete multi-modal brain tumor segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Missing MRI modalities (e.g., T1ce due to contrast agent allergies) are a persistent clinical reality, and LS3M offers a principled way to maximize information extraction from available modalities without relying on modality synthesis or knowledge distillation. The learnable sorting approach is potentially transferable to other 3D medical imaging tasks where fixed scanning strategies are suboptimal, and the global input strategy addresses a widespread limitation of cropped-volume-only processing. However, the quadratic cost of the permutation matrix (mitigated by bilinear interpolation) and the reliance on the Sinkhorn iteration count as a hyperparameter limit scalability to very high-resolution volumes. Open questions include whether the differentiable sorting generalizes to other anatomical structures or 2D medical imaging, and whether the gains persist when the number of available modalities drops to one.
