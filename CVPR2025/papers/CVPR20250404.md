## Problem

Existing road event understanding datasets suffer from regional bias, reliance on dashcam viewpoints, expert-driven annotations that lack broader social discourse, and absence of evaluation mechanisms for hallucination resistance. No prior benchmark tests whether Video LLMs can distinguish informative road event details from misleading information or reject irrelevant video-question pairs, leaving a critical gap in assessing model reliability for real-world road event understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250404/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides video frames (from videos ranging 0.13s to 3885s, averaging 35.6s) paired with a task-specific question in a structured prompt format (video frames + system prompt + question). Models produce open-ended text answers scored via GPT-3.5 similarity for 11 of 12 tasks, while Temporal Grounding uses mAP@0.3:0.7 on extracted time intervals. The 260K QA pairs span 12 tasks organized into four reasoning categories (Factual, Complex, Imaginative, Hallucination), with each task further split into generic (requiring independent context inference) and specific (directly referencing entities) question variants.

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable semi-automatic annotation pipeline that processes social media video narratives: videos are split into 3-second segments, captioned by a Video LLM (Qwen2-VL), summarized by a Text LLM (Claude 3.5), then fused with cleaned tweet conversations to generate QA pairs via template questions of varying difficulty. QA pairs are refined to remove non-visual details, categorized into 12 predefined tasks by a Text LLM with expert verification, and supplemented with incompatible QA pairs generated for non-road-event videos to probe hallucination. The resulting RoadSocial dataset (13.2K videos, 14M frames, 414K comments, 674 tags) is used to zero-shot evaluate 18 Video LLMs and to instruction-tune LLaVA-OV-7B, which achieves a 19.4% overall score improvement, demonstrating the dataset's utility for improving general-purpose Video LLM road event understanding.

## Results

On the RoadSocial-QA benchmark (26K test QA pairs across 12 tasks), GPT-4o achieves the highest overall score of 69.8 (ALL tasks), while Tarsier-34B leads among open-source models at 63.5. IXC-2.5-7B tops road-event-related tasks (RT) at 66.4 among open-source models, outperforming larger models like InternVL2-76B (56.4) and Qwen2-VL-72B (58.6). Temporal Grounding is the weakest task for nearly all models (most below 1% mAP), with Gemini-1.5-Pro achieving the best at 18.6%. Fine-tuning LLaVA-OV-7B on RoadSocial yields a 19.4% absolute improvement in overall score (50.0 → 69.4), matching the best proprietary models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates all 12 tasks with an overall score of 69.8, outperforming Gemini-1.5-Pro (63.4) by 6.4 points and the best open-source model Tarsier-34B (63.5) by 6.3 points. Among open-source models, IXC-2.5-7B achieves the highest road-event-related score (66.4 RT), surpassing the much larger InternVL2-76B (59.1) and Qwen2-VL-72B (60.3), while Tarsier-34B leads on hallucination-robustness tasks (AV: 83.2, IC: 82.3), far ahead of all other models. The driving-specific Dolphin model (40.8 ALL) is the weakest performer, trailing every general-purpose model by at least 10 points, confirming a significant gap in general road event understanding. Fine-tuning LLaVA-OV-7B on RoadSocial lifts its overall score from 50.0 to 69.4 (+19.4), bringing it within 0.4 points of GPT-4o and surpassing all other open-source baselines, with the largest gains in complex reasoning (DS: 50.7→64.1, WY: 51.7→68.7) and hallucination tasks (AV: 45.4→95.4, IC: 21.1→87.6). Temporal Grounding remains the hardest task for all models, with Gemini-1.5-Pro (18.6 mAP) and LLaVA-Video-72B (9.94 mAP) as the top performers, while most models score below 1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VideoQA-for-road-events paradigm established by Lingo-QA, DriveLM, SUTD-TrafficQA, and MM-AU, but departs fundamentally in three ways: (1) sourcing data from global social media (X/Twitter) rather than curated dashcam or CCTV footage, (2) replacing labor-intensive manual annotation with a semi-automatic pipeline combining Video LLMs for visual captioning and Text LLMs for QA generation, and (3) introducing adversarial and incompatible QA tasks that test hallucination resistance—dimensions absent from all prior road-event VideoQA benchmarks. The annotation framework draws on hierarchical k-means clustering of GPT-3 embeddings for representative sample selection and a hybrid visual-contextual prompting strategy, but the core contribution is the dataset and evaluation design rather than a new model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoadSocial is likely to become a standard benchmark for general-purpose road event understanding, given its scale (13.2K videos, 260K QA pairs, 100 countries, 6 viewpoint types) and its inclusion of hallucination-testing tasks that no prior dataset offers. The finding that every general-purpose Video LLM outperforms the sole driving-specific model (Dolphin) challenges the assumption that domain-specific pretraining is necessary for road event comprehension, while the near-zero temporal grounding scores (<1% mAP for most models) expose a critical gap in current Video LLM capabilities. The 19.4% improvement from fine-tuning LLaVA-OV-7B on RoadSocial demonstrates the dataset's utility for training, but the reliance on social media introduces geographic and linguistic biases toward regions with high platform usage, and the GPT-3.5-based evaluation metric remains a proxy rather than a ground-truth measure of answer quality.
