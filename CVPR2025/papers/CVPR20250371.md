## Problem

Personalized text-to-image generation requires capturing a reference subject's appearance while maintaining text controllability, but existing finetuning-free methods either rely on large external encoders (e.g., CLIP, adding 380M+ parameters) or require expensive parallel reference processing at every denoising step. This paper seeks a lightweight, encoder-free, and computationally efficient approach that avoids both test-time finetuning and the high inference costs of reference-based methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250371/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single foreground-segmented reference image (processed at 512×512) and a free-form text prompt as input. Notably, the reference image requires no user-provided caption: it is passed through the frozen U-Net denoiser at timestep t=1 with a null text prompt to extract cached features. The output is a personalized image at 512×512 resolution generated over 50 diffusion steps with classifier-free guidance (scale 7.5), preserving the reference subject's appearance while following the text prompt's context.

## Method

DreamCache caches multi-resolution features from a small subset of U-Net layers (the middle bottleneck and every second decoder layer) at a single timestep (t=1) using a null text prompt, creating a compact subject representation without any external encoder. During generation, lightweight conditioning adapters inject these cached features via cross-attention blocks where the query is the current denoising feature and the key/value are the cached reference features; the cross-attention output is concatenated with the original self-attention output and projected back to the original dimensionality. The adapters (25M total parameters) are pretrained on a 400K-sample synthetic dataset constructed with Llama 3.2 captions, Stable Diffusion-generated images, and SAM/Grounding DINO segmentation, using standard score-matching loss. The U-Net backbone remains entirely frozen, making the method plug-and-play and enabling concurrent personalized and non-personalized generation without weight modification.

## Results

On the DreamBooth evaluation set (30 subjects, 25 prompts each), DreamCache achieves the best DINO score among all methods on SD 1.5 (0.713) and near-best on SD 2.1 (0.767, vs. 0.771 for Subject-Diffusion), while maintaining competitive CLIP-I (0.810/0.816) and CLIP-T (0.298/0.301) scores. In terms of efficiency, DreamCache requires only 25M extra parameters and 42 MB of additional storage (FP16), an order of magnitude less than encoder-based methods (380M–1.6B params), and achieves the fastest inference time at 3.88 s per image (100 timesteps) versus 6.24 s (ELITE) and 7.55 s (BootPIG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamCache achieves the highest DINO score (0.713) among all tested methods on SD 1.5, surpassing the best finetuning-free baseline Kosmos-G (0.694) by 1.9 points and even outperforming test-time finetuning methods like DreamBooth (0.668) and Custom Diffusion (0.643). On SD 2.1, DreamCache (DINO 0.767) is competitive with Subject-Diffusion (0.771) and clearly ahead of BootPIG (0.674) and CAFE (0.715), though it trails ToffeeNet (0.728) by a small margin. In terms of text alignment (CLIP-T), DreamCache is slightly behind JeDi (0.304) and BLIP-Diffusion (0.300) on SD 1.5, and behind BootPIG (0.311) and ToffeeNet (0.306) on SD 2.1, but remains within 0.01–0.02 of the best. For image alignment (CLIP-I), DreamCache is competitive but slightly below Kosmos-G (0.847) on SD 1.5 and CAFE (0.827) on SD 2.1, though the authors note Kosmos-G's score is inflated by background interference. The most striking advantage is computational: DreamCache uses only 25M trainable parameters and 42 MB extra storage versus 380M–1.6B parameters for encoder-based methods (ELITE, BLIP-Diffusion, Kosmos-G) and 0.95B for BootPIG, while also being the fastest at inference (3.88 s vs. 6.24–7.55 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamCache carves out a new niche within finetuning-free personalized image generation by repurposing the U-Net denoiser itself as a feature extractor, rather than relying on external encoders (CLIP, DINO) as in IP-Adapter, BLIP-Diffusion, or ELITE, or processing the reference image at every denoising step as in JeDi and BootPIG. The feature caching mechanism draws a direct analogy to few-shot discriminative learners (prototypical networks, TADAM), but applies it to generative conditioning for the first time. The synthetic data pipeline is adapted from BootPIG, and the cross-attention conditioning adapter is conceptually related to IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention but differs in its spatial concatenation integration strategy, which the ablations show to be superior for text alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude reduction in extra parameters (25M vs. 380M–1.6B) and the plug-and-play design (frozen U-Net, no weight modification) make personalized generation viable on resource-constrained and mobile platforms, a practical gap that encoder-based and reference-based methods leave open. The text-free caching eliminates the need for user-provided captions on reference images, removing a usability friction point. However, the method is limited to single-subject personalization and may struggle with highly abstract or stylistic references; extending it to multi-subject generation with feature interference management, or exploring adaptive caching strategies, are natural open directions.
