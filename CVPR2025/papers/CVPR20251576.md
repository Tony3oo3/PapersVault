## Problem

Video diffusion models require minutes of GPU time and billions of parameters, making them infeasible for on-device deployment. No prior video diffusion model—not even the smallest open-source ones like AnimateDiff or SVD—can run on a mobile device due to memory and compute constraints. This paper addresses the gap by designing a comprehensive acceleration framework that brings text-to-video generation to the edge for the first time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251576/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a 5-second video clip of 120 frames at 432×768 resolution (24 FPS) in under 5 seconds on an iPhone 16 Pro Max. Internally, a spatial-temporal VAE compresses the video into a 4-channel latent with 8×8 spatial and 4× temporal compression, and the UNet denoiser operates on these latents. The architecture supports variable clip lengths and aspect ratios through joint image-video training, and the 4-step inference eliminates the need for classifier-free guidance.

## Method

The framework proceeds in three stages: (1) pruning Stable Diffusion v1.5 into a compact UNet spatial backbone (2.5× smaller, 10× faster), (2) a latency-memory guided evolutionary search that profiles all temporal layer types (Conv1D/3D, SelfAttention1D/3D, CrossAttention1D/3D) across positions and counts to find the Pareto-optimal spatial-temporal architecture (0.6B params), and (3) adversarial fine-tuning that distills the model to 4 denoising steps. The key architectural novelty is a unified spatial-temporal discriminator head—combining a 2D ResBlock with a temporal self-attention layer—replacing the separate spatial and temporal heads of SF-V, enabling joint image-video adversarial training. The VAE decoder is also pruned to achieve a 50× decoding speedup, and the final model eliminates classifier-free guidance entirely, yielding over 12× speedup over the 25-step baseline while matching or exceeding the VBench scores of much larger server-side models.

## Results

On VBench, SnapGen-V (0.6B params, 4 steps) achieves a total score of 81.14, quality score of 83.47, and semantic score of 71.84, outperforming all other 4-step distilled models (T2V-Turbo: 81.01, AnimateLCM: 79.42) and most larger multi-step baselines. The model generates a 5-second 120-frame video (432×768) in 4.12 s on iPhone 16 Pro Max and 0.47 s on A100, compared to 31–54 s for OpenSora-v1.2 and CogVideoX-2B on A100. In a user study, SnapGen-V wins 44.4% (prompt alignment), 48.3% (aesthetics), and 49.3% (motion) preference votes over CogVideoX-2B and OpenSora-1.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SnapGen-V (81.14 total) outperforms all 4-step distilled baselines: T2V-Turbo (81.01, +0.13) and AnimateLCM (79.42, +1.72), while using 50–60% fewer parameters. It also beats larger multi-step models including CogVideoX-2B (80.91, +0.23), VideoCrafter-2.0 (80.44, +0.70), AnimateDiff-V2 (80.27, +0.87), OpenSora-V1.2 (79.76, +1.38), and Pika-1.0 (80.69, +0.45). However, it trails the top three closed-source or very large models: Kling (81.85, −0.71), Pyramid Flow (81.72, −0.58), and CogVideoX-5B (81.61, −0.47). Notably, SnapGen-V is the only model in the comparison that runs on a mobile device (4.12 s on iPhone 16 Pro Max), whereas all baselines are marked 'X' for iPhone in Table 1. In the user study, SnapGen-V achieves a 44.4% win rate on prompt alignment versus 30.7% for CogVideoX-2B and 24.9% for OpenSora-1.2, confirming that the VBench advantage translates to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SnapFusion line of work on mobile image diffusion by adding a temporal dimension: it prunes Stable Diffusion v1.5 into a compact UNet spatial backbone, then uses evolutionary architecture search to insert and arrange temporal layers (Conv1D/3D, SelfAttention1D/3D, CrossAttention1D/3D) under explicit latency-memory constraints for iPhone deployment. The adversarial distillation stage adapts the SF-V framework but replaces its separate spatial and temporal discriminator heads with a unified spatial-temporal head, enabling joint image-video fine-tuning. The training objective follows Rectified Flow, and the spatial-temporal VAE is borrowed from OpenSora, positioning the work as a systematic mobile acceleration pipeline rather than a single architectural innovation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a video diffusion model can run in real time on a consumer phone, opening the door to on-device video generation applications (editing, extension, AR) that were previously cloud-only. The practical recipe—prune an image model, search temporal layers under hardware constraints, then adversarially distill to 4 steps—is a reusable template for edge deployment of other generative models. Key limitations include reliance on a 4-channel VAE (more channels improve reconstruction), the UNet backbone (DiT left for future work), and the 4-step floor (1–2 steps remain open). The finding that ×2 and ×4 temporal scaling only add 0.27–0.31 VBench points while exceeding mobile memory budgets suggests the current architecture is near a quality ceiling for this parameter regime.
