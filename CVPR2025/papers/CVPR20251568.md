## Problem

Pairwise two-view matching paradigms suffer from inherent uncertainty in complex wide-baseline scenarios because projecting 3D structures onto 2D planes causes significant information loss, making it unreliable to infer scene geometry from limited two-view observations—especially under severe occlusions and repetitive textures. Errors in individual pairwise matches compound during track merging, degrading downstream tasks like SfM and pose estimation at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251568/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CoMatcher takes as input local features (keypoint locations and descriptors) from M source views (typically M=4) and one target view, along with precomputed multi-view tracks of the source group that encode 3D point projections and implicit camera poses. It outputs 2D point correspondences between each source view and the target view in a single forward pass, rather than requiring separate pairwise inferences. The groupwise pipeline first partitions a large image set into co-visible groups using covisibility heuristics, then applies CoMatcher across groups to build comprehensive tracks.

## Method

CoMatcher extends the parallel two-view GNN matcher architecture (SuperGlue/LightGlue) with two core multi-view components. First, a multi-view cross-attention module aggregates context across source views using relative positional encoding derived from cross-view projection geometry (via precomputed tracks), constraining each point's attention search space to geometrically relevant regions. Second, a multi-view feature correlation strategy uses per-layer confidence estimators to identify ambiguous points and corrects their attention distributions by weighted-averaging the distributions of corresponding points in other views, enforcing cross-view projection consistency during inference. The groupwise pipeline partitions images into co-visible groups, builds intra-group tracks with existing SfM frameworks, and uses CoMatcher for inter-group matching. Training is two-stage: pre-training on a large-scale synthetic homography dataset followed by fine-tuning on MegaDepth with SfM-recovered poses as ground truth.

## Results

On MegaDepth relative pose estimation (the primary benchmark), SP+CoMatcher achieves AUC of 57.2°/73.9°/84.8° (RANSAC) and 68.3°/82.2°/89.1° (LO-RANSAC) at 5°/10°/20°, outperforming LightGlue (56.2/72.7/83.5) and SuperGlue (55.8/72.8/84.1) while running at 69 ms per quadruplet. On the IMC 2020 large-scale benchmark, DISK+CoMatcher reaches 44.9°/57.3° (stereo) and 67.1°/78.4° (multi-view) AUC at 5°/10°, surpassing the best pairwise baseline DISK+LightGlue (42.1/55.6 and 65.2/76.2). On HPatches homography estimation, SP+CoMatcher attains AUC-DLT of 37.1/69.0/78.8 at 1/3/5 px, the best among all sparse matchers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMatcher consistently outperforms all sparse two-view matchers: it beats LightGlue by +1.0°/+1.2°/+1.3° AUC (RANSAC 5°/10°/20°) and SuperGlue by +1.4°/+1.1°/+0.7° with SuperPoint features, while also being faster than SuperGlue (69 ms vs. 87 ms). Against the multi-view baseline End2End, CoMatcher gains +1.9°/+2.5°/+3.6° (RANSAC) without requiring end-to-end pose optimization. Among dense methods, LoFTR edges out SP+CoMatcher by 0.8° at the strict 5° threshold (58.0 vs. 57.2) but CoMatcher wins at 10° and 20° and runs 2.6× faster; DUSt3R and MASt3R are substantially weaker (42.4° and 51.5° at 5°) and 3–4.6× slower. On the IMC 2020 large-scale benchmark, the groupwise CoMatcher pipeline improves over the best pairwise method (DISK+LightGlue) by +2.8°/+1.7° (stereo) and +1.9°/+2.2° (multi-view) AUC at 5°/10°, demonstrating that the advantage grows with scene complexity and scale. SIFT+NN+mutual and SGMNet are far behind on all metrics, confirming the superiority of learned multi-view matching over classical approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMatcher extends the GNN-based feature matching architecture of SuperGlue and the dual-softmax matching head of LightGlue from a two-view to a multi-view (N-to-1) setting, adding a multi-view cross-attention module for context sharing and a confidence-guided attention correction mechanism for cross-view consistency. It departs from the entrenched pairwise-then-merge paradigm by formulating matching as a collaborative 1-to-N problem within co-visible image groups, and contrasts with the N-to-N End2End multi-view matcher [40] by arguing that a target-centric architecture is more effective for representation learning. The geometric constraint mechanism, which uses precomputed tracks to encode relative positions across views in the attention score, is a novel addition not present in prior two-view or multi-view matchers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized weakness of pairwise matching—catastrophic failures under occlusion, wide baselines, and repetitive textures—by showing that multi-view collaborative reasoning yields more reliable correspondences without the computational cost of dense methods. The groupwise pipeline (grouping → connecting → matching) provides a practical, scalable framework for large-scale SfM that integrates cleanly with existing tools like COLMAP. Open questions include whether the geometric constraint can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on precomputed tracks, whether the approach generalizes beyond the ~4–5 view group size sweet spot, and how it performs on truly unstructured collections without reliable initial tracks.
