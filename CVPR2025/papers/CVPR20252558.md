## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing methods struggle with three interrelated challenges: accurately localizing editing targets in complex multi-object scenes, maintaining semantic consistency in non-edited regions, and performing fine-grained modifications. These failures stem from the CLIP text encoder's limited ability to parse complex user instructions and from implicit biases in T2I-generated training pairs, causing models to either misidentify editing regions or inadvertently alter unrelated content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252558/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a free-form natural language editing instruction (e.g., 'Make the water in the vase blue') as input and produces a single edited RGB image as output. The base diffusion backbone is Stable Diffusion 1.5 initialized from InstructPix2Pix weights, operating in latent space. The VLM component processes the image through a DINOv2 encoder and a Deformable DETR region detector, while the diffusion model receives the channel-concatenated encoded source image latent and the noisy target latent, guided by the VLM-derived editing representation and hybrid visual features.

## Method

FireEdit introduces a region-aware VLM that augments standard image tokens with fine-grained region tokens extracted via Deformable DETR and ROIAlign, enabling the LLM (LLaVA-7B fine-tuned with LoRA) to implicitly localize editing targets and articulate modification intentions through predicted special [IMG] tokens. A Q-Former bridges the semantic gap between LLM hidden states and the diffusion model's text embedding space. Two complementary modules control the denoising process: the Time-Aware Target Injection (TATI) module uses Flamingo-style resamplers with timestep-conditioned adaptive LayerNorm to dynamically modulate guidance strength—emphasizing low-frequency structure early and high-frequency attributes later—and the Hybrid Visual Cross Attention (HVCA) module cascades CLIP and DINOv2 multi-scale visual features through decoupled cross-attention layers with learnable queries to preserve high-frequency details in non-edited regions. Training jointly optimizes a VLM negative log-likelihood loss on the [IMG] token sequence and a standard diffusion denoising loss.

## Results

On the Emu Edit test set, FireEdit achieves the best scores across all six metrics: L1=0.0574, CLIP-I=0.9140, DINO=0.8829, LPIPS=0.1373, CLIP-T=0.2783, and a user preference rate of 78.67%. The L1 distance is 31% lower than the next-best method (UltraEdit, 0.0838), and DINO similarity exceeds the runner-up (MagicBrush, 0.7810) by over 10 percentage points. On the MagicBrush benchmark, FireEdit outperforms all VLM-guided baselines (SmartEdit, MGIE) on L1 and L2 in both single-turn and multi-turn settings, though MagicBrush retains a slight edge on CLIP-I and DINO, which the authors attribute to training-set bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FireEdit outperforms all six instruction-based baselines on every metric in the Emu Edit evaluation. Against the closest competitor UltraEdit, FireEdit reduces L1 by 31% (0.0574 vs. 0.0838) and improves DINO similarity by 14.2 points (0.8829 vs. 0.7406). Against the VLM-guided methods SmartEdit and MGIE, FireEdit achieves substantially higher CLIP-I (0.9140 vs. 0.8592 and 0.7456) and DINO (0.8829 vs. 0.7705 and 0.5944), indicating far better preservation of non-edited regions. In the user study, FireEdit is preferred 78.67% of the time versus 5.00% for MagicBrush and UltraEdit, a dramatic margin. On the MagicBrush benchmark, FireEdit beats SmartEdit and MGIE on L1 and L2 in both single-turn and multi-turn settings, but MagicBrush retains a small advantage on CLIP-I and DINO (e.g., 0.9332 vs. 0.9131 single-turn CLIP-I), which the authors attribute to MagicBrush's training data overlap with the test set. Among description-guided baselines (SDEdit, NTI, GLIDE, BleDiff), FireEdit achieves the best L1 and L2 while GLIDE and BleDiff show extreme L2 values (>100), confirming their unsuitability for fine-grained editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

FireEdit extends the VLM-guided instruction-based editing paradigm established by MGIE and SmartEdit, which use LLMs to reinterpret user instructions before feeding them to a diffusion model. Its key departure is the explicit decoupling of spatial localization from semantic association: rather than relying solely on holistic image tokens for the VLM, FireEdit injects fine-grained region tokens (from Deformable DETR + ROIAlign) to compensate for LLMs' weak spatial perception. On the diffusion side, it builds on IP2P's dual-condition CFG framework and IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention, but introduces two new modules—TATI (borrowing Flamingo's resampler with timestep conditioning) and HVCA (cascading CLIP + DINOv2 features)—to adaptively control guidance strength and preserve high-frequency details in non-edited regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively lightweight architectural addition (region tokens) can dramatically improve instruction-following in complex, multi-object scenes without requiring manual masks or reference images, making fine-grained editing more accessible to end users. The 78.67% user preference score versus ~5% for all baselines suggests a qualitative leap in perceived edit quality, though the user study methodology is deferred to supplementary material. Key limitations remain: no support for reference-image conditioning, no handling of pose changes or object repositioning, and reliance on a frozen detector whose recall may cap performance on unusual objects. Open questions include whether the region-token approach generalizes to video editing or whether the TATI/HVCA modules transfer to newer diffusion backbones like SDXL or DiT-based models.
