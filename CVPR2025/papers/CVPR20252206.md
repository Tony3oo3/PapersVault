## Problem

Existing RGBD segmentation methods either use dual encoders that treat RGB and depth symmetrically (incurring high computational cost) or unified encoders that still process depth through learnable layers, ignoring the intrinsic geometric nature of depth. The paper asks whether depth truly needs to be encoded by neural networks at all, or whether it can be directly exploited as a geometry prior to guide attention in a vision transformer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252206/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a corresponding depth map as input. Unconventionally, the depth map passes through no learnable layers; it is only average-pooled at four scales (matching the encoder's pyramid stages) to produce per-patch depth values used for computing geometry priors. The RGB image is the sole input to the encoder's learnable parameters. The output is a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask, produced by a lightweight decoder head that fuses features from the last three encoder stages.

## Method

The core contribution is Geometry Self-Attention (GSA), which injects a depth-derived geometry prior directly into the self-attention mechanism. The geometry prior is constructed by computing pairwise depth distances (from average-pooled depth patches) and pairwise spatial Manhattan distances, then fusing them via learnable memory weights into a single HW×HW matrix G. In each attention head, the standard softmax(QK^T) is element-wise multiplied by β^G (where β∈(0,1) is a per-head decay rate), suppressing attention to geometrically distant patches and enhancing locally coherent regions. To reduce the quadratic cost, attention is decomposed along horizontal and vertical axes separately (axes decomposition), yielding a 4-stage pyramid encoder with a lightweight decoder. The model is pretrained on RGBD pairs from ImageNet-1K following DFormer, then fine-tuned with standard cross-entropy loss on segmentation benchmarks.

## Results

On NYU DepthV2, DFormerv2-L achieves 58.4% mIoU with 95.5M parameters and 124.1G FLOPs, surpassing the previous best GeminiFusion-B5 (57.7%) by 0.7% while using less than half the computation (124.1G vs. 256.1G FLOPs). At the base scale, DFormerv2-B matches GeminiFusion-B5's 57.7% mIoU with only 67.2G FLOPs (vs. 256.1G). On SUN-RGBD, DFormerv2-L reaches 53.3% mIoU, and on the Deliver dataset, DFormerv2-L achieves 67.1% mIoU, outperforming GeminiFusion-B5 (66.9%) with 114.5G vs. 218.4G FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFormerv2 consistently outperforms all 18 baselines across three datasets and three model scales. At the large scale, DFormerv2-L (58.4% mIoU, 124.1G FLOPs) beats GeminiFusion-B5 (57.7%, 256.1G) by 0.7% mIoU with less than half the FLOPs, and also surpasses CMX-B5 (56.9%, 167.8G) and MultiMAE (56.0%, 267.9G). At the base scale, DFormerv2-B (57.7%) ties GeminiFusion-B5 (57.7%) on NYU DepthV2 but uses only 67.2G vs. 256.1G FLOPs, and outperforms DFormer-L (57.2%, 69.3G) with fewer parameters (53.9M vs. 39.0M) and comparable FLOPs. At the small scale, DFormerv2-S (56.0%) leads AsymFormer (55.3%) and DFormer-Base (55.6%) while using fewer FLOPs (33.9G vs. 39.4G and 45.3G). On the Deliver dataset, DFormerv2-L (67.1%) edges out GeminiFusion-B5 (66.9%) with 114.5G vs. 218.4G FLOPs, and DFormerv2-B (65.2%) surpasses CMNext-B4 (66.3% at 112.0G) in efficiency. The key advantage is the efficiency-accuracy trade-off: DFormerv2 achieves SOTA mIoU at every scale with the lowest or near-lowest FLOPs among competitive methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFormerv2 directly extends DFormer's unified RGBD encoder architecture but makes a sharp conceptual departure: rather than encoding depth through a (smaller) neural network branch as DFormer does, it bypasses depth encoding entirely and instead uses the depth map to construct a geometry prior that modulates self-attention weights. This positions the work against the dominant dual-encoder paradigm (CMX, GeminiFusion, TokenFusion) and the unified-encoder line (DFormer, AsymFormer), arguing that depth's role is structural (defining 3D spatial relationships) rather than feature-level. The geometry self-attention mechanism draws on the broader tradition of injecting prior knowledge into transformers (position encodings, relative biases) but replaces 2D positional priors with 3D geometric priors derived from depth and Manhattan distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is the efficiency-accuracy trade-off: DFormerv2-B matches GeminiFusion-B5 (57.7% mIoU on NYU DepthV2) with roughly half the FLOPs, and DFormerv2-L achieves 58.4% with 95.5M parameters, making RGBD segmentation more deployable on resource-constrained platforms. The conceptual insight that depth need not be 'learned' by a network but can serve as a free geometric prior is clean and could inspire similar treatment of other auxiliary modalities. Limitations include evaluation restricted to indoor RGBD benchmarks, reliance on estimated (not sensor) depth for ImageNet pretraining, and the requirement that a depth map be available at inference time, which limits applicability to outdoor or depth-free settings.
