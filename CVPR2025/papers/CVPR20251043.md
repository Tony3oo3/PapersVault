## Problem

Synchronous video diffusion models apply identical noise to all frames, limiting them to fixed-length sequences, while asynchronous auto-regressive models suffer from training-inference inconsistency and error accumulation. Existing asynchronous diffusion approaches that independently sample per-frame timesteps expand the search space to O(1e48) compositions, causing training instability. The paper seeks a principled middle ground that preserves the flexibility of asynchronous generation while maintaining the stability of synchronous diffusion.

## Input / Output

The model takes pure Gaussian noise in a compressed latent space (L=32 tokens of dimension D=4 per frame, at 256×256 resolution) and iteratively denoises it into a sequence of F video frames. The AD scheduler at inference allows the number of frames F to vary, supporting both short (16-frame) and long (128-frame) generation from the same trained model. A key design choice is that the AR-VAE encoder is time-agnostic (processes each frame independently) while the decoder uses temporal causal attention, enabling the latent space to be consumed frame-by-frame in an auto-regressive fashion.

## Method

AR-Diffusion combines a Transformer backbone with temporal causal attention and a non-decreasing timestep constraint (t₁ ≤ t₂ ≤ … ≤ t_F), which ensures earlier frames are always clearer than later ones and reduces the valid composition space from O(1e48) to O(1e32). Training uses x₀-prediction loss with a novel FoPP scheduler that employs dynamic programming to uniformly sample timestep compositions while also ensuring uniform marginal distribution over individual frame timesteps. At inference, an AD scheduler introduces an adaptive per-frame timestep difference s, where s=0 recovers synchronous diffusion and s=T recovers auto-regressive generation, with intermediate values offering a quality-efficiency trade-off. The AR-VAE, built on TiTok, replaces vector quantization with continuous latents and adds temporal causal attention in the decoder plus adversarial training for sharper reconstructions.

## Results

AR-Diffusion achieves the best FVD scores on three of four benchmarks: Taichi-HD (FVD₁₆ = 66.3, FVD₁₂₈ = 376.3), Sky-Timelapse (FVD₁₆ = 40.8, FVD₁₂₈ = 175.5), and UCF-101 (FVD₁₆ = 186.6, FVD₁₂₈ = 572.3). On UCF-101, it surpasses the previous SOTA asynchronous diffusion model FVDM by 60.1% in FVD score (186.6 vs. 468.2). On FaceForensics, AR-Diffusion achieves FVD₁₆ = 71.9 and FVD₁₂₈ = 265.7, outperforming PVDM and Diffusion Forcing but trailing synchronous methods like Latte (34.0) and FVDM (55.0) on FVD₁₆. The ablation study confirms that the non-decreasing constraint, temporal causal attention, and x₀ prediction loss are each critical, with removing any single component degrading FVD by 38–240 points on Sky-Timelapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AR-Diffusion achieves the best FVD on Taichi-HD, Sky-Timelapse, and UCF-101, outperforming all 13 baselines including GAN-based (DIGAN, StyleGAN-V, MoStGAN-V), auto-regressive (TATS, VideoGPT), synchronous diffusion (Latte, PVDM, LVDM, VIDM), and asynchronous diffusion (FVDM, Diffusion Forcing) methods. On UCF-101, the most complex dataset, AR-Diffusion (FVD₁₆ = 186.6) beats the closest competitor Diffusion Forcing (274.5) by 32% and the previous SOTA asynchronous model FVDM (468.2) by 60.1%. On Sky-Timelapse, AR-Diffusion (40.8) surpasses the best GAN baseline MoStGAN-V (65.3) by 37% and the best synchronous diffusion method PVDM (55.4) by 26%. On FaceForensics, AR-Diffusion (71.9) does not achieve the best FVD₁₆, trailing Latte (34.0), MoStGAN-V (39.7), StyleGAN-V (47.4), FVDM (55.0), and DIGAN (62.5), though it still outperforms PVDM (355.9) and Diffusion Forcing (99.5). The paper attributes the FaceForensics gap to the dataset's relatively simple, face-centric motion where synchronous methods with fixed-length sequences are less disadvantaged.

## Relation to Prior Work

AR-Diffusion positions itself at the intersection of synchronous video diffusion (VDM, LVDM, Latte) and asynchronous auto-regressive generation (VideoGPT, TATS), departing from both by introducing a non-decreasing timestep constraint that sits between the rigid equal-timestep assumption and the fully independent per-frame timesteps of Diffusion Forcing and FVDM. The VAE component extends TiTok's discrete tokenizer to continuous video latents with temporal causal attention, while the diffusion backbone follows the DiT architecture. The key conceptual departure is the observation that the non-decreasing constraint reduces the timestep composition search space from O(1e48) to O(1e32), addressing the training instability that plagues fully asynchronous diffusion models, and the AD scheduler elegantly unifies synchronous (s=0) and auto-regressive (s=T) inference as special cases of a single parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-decreasing timestep constraint and the AD scheduler offer a principled way to interpolate between synchronous and auto-regressive video generation, giving practitioners a single knob to trade off temporal coherence against content diversity. The 60.1% FVD improvement over FVDM on UCF-101 and consistent SOTA across four benchmarks validate the approach, though the evaluation is limited to 256×256 resolution, unconditioned generation, and relatively short clips (16–128 frames). Open questions include whether the non-decreasing constraint and FoPP scheduler scale to higher resolutions, longer videos, and text-conditioned generation, and whether the optimal timestep difference s generalizes across domains or requires per-dataset tuning.
