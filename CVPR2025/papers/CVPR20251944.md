## Problem

Existing 3D generation evaluation metrics either rely on black-box multimodal LLMs (e.g., GPT-4V) for coarse pairwise comparisons or overlook geometric quality entirely. There is no reliable, interpretable tool that can simultaneously assess multiple quality dimensions (geometric, semantic, structural consistency, text alignment, aesthetics) and localize specific artifacts in 3D space, making it difficult to diagnose and compare 3D generation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251944/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a generated 3D asset (mesh or implicit representation), a text prompt, and optionally a reference image, rendering 120 viewpoints at 512×512 resolution (RGB, surface normals, opacity maps) with cameras at 15° elevation. Beyond scalar scores (each scaled to 0–100%), Eval3D produces pixel-wise inconsistency maps in 2D and back-projected 3D artifact localization maps on the extracted mesh, enabling spatial identification of where geometric or semantic failures occur. The text-3D alignment metric uses only 12 viewpoints for computational efficiency, while other metrics leverage all 120 views.

## Method

Eval3D's core insight is that high-quality 3D assets should exhibit consistency across different foundation model representations, so it uses a diverse set of pretrained models as probes to measure cross-representation disagreement. Geometric consistency compares analytical surface normals (from mesh faces or SDF gradients) against Depth Anything monocular normal predictions per pixel; semantic consistency measures DINOv2 feature variance across views for each 3D mesh vertex; structural consistency uses Stable-Zero123 novel-view synthesis and DreamSim perceptual similarity to detect global shape incoherence; text-3D alignment generates LLM-based multiple-choice questions and checks LLaVA-NeXT VQA consistency across neighboring views; aesthetics uses GPT-4o ELO or ImageReward. The key differentiator from prior work is that geometric and semantic metrics operate in 3D space, enabling back-projection of pixel-wise error maps onto the mesh for spatial artifact localization (e.g., identifying Janus faces or texture-geometry misalignment regions), which no prior 3D evaluation method provides.

## Results

Eval3D achieves the highest human-alignment scores across all five evaluation dimensions, with particularly strong gains on geometric consistency (83.0% vs. 46.9% for GPT-4V, a 36% improvement) and text-3D alignment (88.7% vs. 72.8% for GPT-4V, a 16% improvement). Eval3D is the only metric that measures semantic consistency (68.0%), a dimension no prior baseline covers. On structural consistency and aesthetics, Eval3D (69.2% and 87.4%) is comparable to or slightly exceeds GPT-4V (68.9% and 85.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Eval3D outperforms all four baselines on every dimension where they overlap. Against GPT-4V [61], the strongest prior baseline, Eval3D gains 36.1 points on geometric consistency (83.0 vs. 46.9), 15.9 points on text-3D alignment (88.7 vs. 72.8), and 1.8 points on aesthetics (87.4 vs. 85.6), while matching on structural consistency (69.2 vs. 68.9). Against CLIP-Score [20] and ImageReward [62], Eval3D leads by 38–44 points on geometric consistency and 9–15 points on text-3D alignment, though ImageReward is competitive on aesthetics (75.4 vs. 87.4). T3-Bench [19] only reports aesthetics (76.5) and text-3D alignment (56.7); Eval3D surpasses it by 10.9 and 32.0 points respectively, and additionally provides geometric, semantic, and structural consistency scores that T3-Bench does not measure. The paper also notes that Eval3D's geometric and semantic metrics are interpretable and pixel-localizable, whereas GPT-4V and T3-Bench rely on coarse language-based judgments that cannot localize multi-view inconsistencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

Eval3D extends the 2D text-to-image evaluation lineage (TIFA, Davidsonian Scene Graph) into the 3D domain by adapting question-answering-based alignment checks to multi-view rendered images. It departs sharply from the dominant prior 3D evaluation approaches (GPT-4V, T3-Bench), which rely on a single black-box VLM for coarse pairwise or scalar judgments, by instead composing multiple open-source foundation models (Depth Anything, DINOv2, Stable-Zero123, LLaVA-NeXT, DreamSim) as complementary probes whose mutual consistency serves as the quality signal. The conceptual shift is from 'ask one model to rate the asset' to 'measure how well independent models agree on different visual cues projected onto the 3D surface,' enabling pixel-wise and spatially-localized diagnostics that prior methods cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eval3D is the first 3D evaluation framework to deliver interpretable, pixel-wise scores and 3D spatial artifact localization, making it a practical tool for diagnosing and filtering generated assets rather than merely ranking them. A key practical finding is that current SOTA models (notably ProlificDreamer and MVDream) score well on aesthetics and text alignment yet exhibit severe geometric and semantic inconsistencies that black-box VLM evaluators systematically miss, suggesting prior benchmarks overstate the maturity of 3D generation. The main limitation is that all scores inherit the errors of the underlying foundation models (e.g., Depth Anything's coarse normals, Zero123's hallucinated views), so the framework's ceiling is tied to their progress. An open question is whether the consistency-based scoring can be repurposed as a self-supervised refinement signal during generation, which the authors hint at but do not fully explore.
