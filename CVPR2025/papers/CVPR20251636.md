## Problem

Existing radiance-field-based localization methods (NeRF, 3DGS) either require an external pose prior, rely on image retrieval before feature matching, or only perform photometric pose refinement that fails under illumination changes. No full relocalization pipeline exists that estimates 6DoF pose from scratch using Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation while handling both weak-texture and illumination-varying conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251636/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single query RGB image and a per-scene Feature Gaussian model (millions of 3D Gaussians each carrying center, rotation, scale, opacity, color, and a D-dimensional feature vector) as input. The query image is processed by SuperPoint to produce a dense feature map at reduced resolution, from which a scene-specific detector extracts 2,048 sparse keypoints. The output is a 6DoF camera pose (3D translation + 3D rotation) estimated via PnP with RANSAC, refined iteratively (4 iterations on 7-Scenes, 1 on Cambridge Landmarks) through dense feature matching against the rendered feature field.

## Method

STDLoc introduces a sparse-to-dense localization pipeline built on Feature Gaussian, which extends 3DGS primitives with a distilled feature field trained jointly with the radiance field (L1 + D-SSIM for RGB, L1 for features). A matching-oriented sampling strategy scores each Gaussian by average cosine similarity with 2D SuperPoint features across all views where it is visible, then selects landmarks via anchor-based k-NN sampling to ensure spatial uniformity and multi-view recognizability, reducing millions of Gaussians to ~16K scene landmarks. A 4-layer CNN scene-specific detector, trained self-supervised with binary cross-entropy against projected landmark centers, extracts matching-relevant keypoints from the query feature map. The sparse stage matches these keypoints to sampled landmarks by cosine similarity and solves the initial pose via PnP+RANSAC; the dense stage renders a high-resolution feature map and depth map from the full Feature Gaussian, then performs LoFTR-style coarse-to-fine patch matching (8×8 patches at full resolution guided by low-resolution correspondences) to refine the pose iteratively.

## Results

On the 7-Scenes indoor benchmark, STDLoc achieves a median translation/rotation error of 0.76 cm / 0.24°, outperforming the best SCR method (DSAC*: 1.07 cm / 0.35°) by ~30% and the best feature-matching method (DVLAD+R2D2: 3.26 cm / 1.07°) by over 4×. On the Cambridge Landmarks outdoor benchmark, STDLoc achieves 10.1 cm / 0.14° average, beating the best FM method (HLoc: 11.0 cm / 0.21°), the best SCR method (GLACE: 13.7 cm / 0.28°), and the best NeRF/GS method (NeRFMatch: 14.5 cm / 0.29°). STDLoc also attains the highest recall at 99.1% (5 cm, 5°) and 90.9% (2 cm, 2°) on 7-Scenes, surpassing all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STDLoc outperforms all 13 baselines on both benchmarks. On 7-Scenes, it reduces average translation error by ~30% over the best SCR methods (DSAC* 1.07 cm, ACE 1.10 cm) and by over 4× over the best FM method (DVLAD+R2D2 3.26 cm); among NeRF/GS methods, it beats NeRFMatch (2.71 cm) by 3.6× and PNeRFLoc (7.29 cm) by 9.6×. On Cambridge Landmarks, STDLoc (10.1 cm / 0.14°) edges out HLoc (11.0 cm / 0.21°) and NeuMap (12.4 cm / 0.29°) in translation, while dominating all methods in rotation accuracy (0.14° vs. 0.21°–0.53°). The SCR baselines (DSAC*, ACE, GLACE, NBE+SLD) are competitive indoors but degrade outdoors (13.7–18.5 cm average), whereas STDLoc maintains consistent performance across both settings. The image-retrieval baseline (NetVLAD) achieves only 48.2% recall at 5 m / 10° on Cambridge Landmarks, compared to 99.6% for STDLoc's sparse stage alone, highlighting the advantage of direct 2D-3D matching over retrieval-then-match pipelines. The only scenes where STDLoc trails are Court and King's on Cambridge Landmarks in translation (15.7 cm vs. HLoc 17.7 cm / NeuMap 6 cm), attributed to insufficient 3D Gaussian reconstruction in those areas.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Feature 3DGS [70] for distilling a feature field into Gaussian primitives and on 3DGS [21] as the underlying scene representation, while adapting the coarse-to-fine patch matching procedure from LoFTR [52] for the dense refinement stage. It departs from the dominant retrieval-then-match paradigm (HLoc, Active Search) by replacing image retrieval with a matching-oriented Gaussian sampling strategy and a scene-specific detector, and from photometric-error-based radiance field localization (iNeRF, NeRFMatch, PNeRFLoc) by operating entirely in feature space, which avoids sensitivity to illumination changes. The resulting sparse-to-dense pipeline—sparse landmark matching for initial pose followed by dense feature-field alignment for refinement—represents a conceptual reorganization of the localization workflow rather than a modification of any single component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving SOTA on both indoor (7-Scenes) and outdoor (Cambridge Landmarks) benchmarks with a full pipeline requiring no external pose prior, STDLoc offers a practical, end-to-end alternative to the multi-stage retrieval pipelines that dominate the field. The ~30% reduction in median error over the best SCR methods (DSAC*, ACE) and the 99.1% recall at 5 cm/5° demonstrate that explicit Gaussian scene representations can match or exceed implicit neural encodings in both small and moderately large scenes. Key limitations remain: Feature Gaussian training does not scale to very large scenes (addressable via LoD or chunked training), and 3DGS floating artifacts can degrade localization, both of which are active research directions in the broader 3DGS community.
