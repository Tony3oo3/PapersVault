## Problem

Long-form video question answering requires processing hundreds of thousands of spatiotemporal tokens, making self-attention computationally infeasible and naive compression (pooling, sparse sampling) lossy for salient events. Existing compression methods either discard fine-grained spatiotemporal information or lack the long-range modeling capacity needed to identify which tokens matter across minutes or hours of video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251646/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a long-form video (64 frames by default, up to 128 for the Qwen2-7B variant) and a text question. Each frame is independently encoded by a frozen vision transformer (CLIP or Meta-CLIP) into patch-level tokens, producing sequences of 36,864 (LLaVA) or 102,400 (LLaMA) spatiotemporal tokens. These are compressed by a factor of 16× into 2,304 or 6,400 tokens respectively, which are then concatenated with the tokenized question and passed to the LLM to generate a free-form text answer.

## Method

BIMBA introduces a Mamba-based spatiotemporal token selector that compresses the full video token sequence by over an order of magnitude using a selective-scan state-space model with linear (not quadratic) computational cost. Visual queries are initialized via 3D average pooling over the input tokens, then interleaved uniformly among the spatiotemporal tokens (rather than appended) to avoid positional bias, and processed through a bidirectional selective scan (forward and backward passes) to capture 2D/3D spatiotemporal structure. The selective-scan mechanism makes the B, C, and Δ matrices input-dependent, allowing the model to dynamically propagate important tokens while filtering redundant ones. Training uses LoRA on the LLM with a frozen image encoder, fine-tuned on 370K instruction-tuning videos. An optional question-conditioned variant prepends the tokenized question to the spatiotemporal sequence before the selector, enabling question-aware token selection at a small accuracy cost.

## Results

BIMBA-LLaVA (Qwen2-7B, 128 frames) achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on all seven long-form VQA benchmarks: 68.51 on PerceptionTest, 83.73 on NExT-QA, 71.14 on EgoSchema, 77.88 on VNBench, 59.46 on LongVideoBench, 64.67 on Video-MME, and 71.37 on MLVU. In the controlled comparison using the same 370K instruction-tuning data, BIMBA-LLaVA (Vicuna-7B) achieves an average accuracy of 55.23% across benchmarks, outperforming LLaVA-NeXT (Video) (49.42%) and PLLaVA (50.40%). The BIMBA-LLaMA variant (LLaMA-3.2-8B) surpasses LLaMA-3.2 (Video) by 3.09% on average and PLLaVA (LLaMA-3.2) by 4.21%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIMBA-LLaVA (Qwen2-7B) outperforms the strongest open-source competitor LLaVA-Video [82] on all seven benchmarks, with the largest margins on EgoSchema (+13.84%, 71.14 vs 57.30) and VNBench (+7.11%, 77.88 vs 70.77). It surpasses LongVU [60] by 3.54% on EgoSchema (71.14 vs 67.60) and by 6.07% on MLVU (71.37 vs 65.40). Against LLaVA-OneVision [34], BIMBA leads by 11.44% on EgoSchema and 26.08% on VNBench, while Video-XL [61] is outperformed by 16.28% on VNBench and 9.96% on LongVideoBench. In the controlled 370K-data comparison, BIMBA-LLaVA (Vicuna-7B) beats LLaVA-NeXT (Video) [44] by 5.81% on EgoSchema (52.31 vs 41.66) and PLLaVA [74] by 8.95% on EgoSchema (52.31 vs 43.36). BIMBA-LLaMA (LLaMA-3.2-8B) exceeds LLaMA-3.2 (Video) [51] by 5.04% on EgoSchema (60.25 vs 55.21) and by 3.80% on NExT-QA (76.88 vs 73.72). Against proprietary-LLM systems, BIMBA-LLaVA surpasses VideoTree (GPT-4) [68] by 7.7% on NExT-QA and 10.04% on EgoSchema.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIMBA builds directly on the Mamba selective-scan SSM [17] for efficient long-sequence modeling and adapts VideoMamba's bidirectional scan [40] to the video domain. It departs from the dominant compression strategies in long-form video MLLMs—spatial-temporal pooling (Video-ChatGPT, PLLaVA), Perceiver-style cross-attention (Video-LLaMA), and convolutional modules (Kangaroo, Video-LLaVA)—by reframing token compression as a selective-scan problem where a small set of visual queries is jointly modeled with the full spatiotemporal token sequence. The key architectural departures are the interleaved query placement (mitigating positional bias) and the use of bidirectional scanning to capture 2D/3D spatiotemporal structure, neither of which appears in prior Mamba-for-vision work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BIMBA provides a practical, drop-in compression module that reduces visual token sequences by 16× (e.g., 102K → 6.4K) while achieving SOTA on seven long-video QA benchmarks, offering a linear-complexity alternative to the quadratic self-attention bottleneck that limits current video MLLMs. The technique is architecture-agnostic (validated on both LLaVA-NeXT/CLIP and LLaMA-3.2/Meta-CLIP backbones) and can be integrated into existing MLLMs without modifying the LLM. Open questions include whether the selective-scan compression generalizes to even longer (hour-scale) videos without further architectural changes, whether question-conditioned selection can be made efficient enough for multi-turn dialogue, and how the approach extends to other video tasks such as summarization or retrieval.
