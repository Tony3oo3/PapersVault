## Problem

Existing SNN training faces a dilemma: ANN-guided distillation struggles to explicitly align intermediate feature spaces due to the limited expressiveness of binary spike representations, while rate-based backpropagation—though capturing rate-based representations well—suffers from gradient distortions introduced by the Straight-Through Estimator. No prior method effectively combines the supervisory signal of a pretrained ANN with the efficiency of rate-based training to mitigate these issues simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252020/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or DVS event streams (48×48 for CIFAR10-DVS) and outputs a class label. The SNN processes each input over T timesteps using LIF neurons, and the average firing rate across timesteps serves as the rate-based representation that is fed into learnable mapping modules C_k for alignment with the frozen ANN's intermediate feature spaces. The hybrid models M_k produce auxiliary logit outputs that contribute additional loss terms beyond the standard SNN and ANN outputs.

## Method

The framework partitions both the pretrained ANN and the SNN into n blocks and constructs a series of hybrid models M_k = {S_0,…,S_k, C_k, A_{k+1}^{frozen},…,A_n^{frozen}}, where C_k is a learnable module mapping SNN rate-based features into the ANN feature space. Training proceeds via rate-based backpropagation (decoupling memory and compute from timesteps using statistics ρ and g), with a total loss combining cross-entropy, logit-based KL-divergence distillation from the ANN, and per-block losses from each hybrid model. Rather than explicitly minimizing the distance between SNN and ANN intermediate features, the SNN's rate features are forwarded through the frozen ANN tail and the conversion error is implicitly integrated into the logit-level loss, which also acts as a regularizer that mitigates STE-induced gradient distortions. The approach requires only minimal forward-pass modifications and imposes no structural constraints on the teacher-student architecture pair.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-19 at T=2, the method achieves 96.56% top-1 accuracy, surpassing SAKD (96.06%) at T=4 and EnOF (96.19%) at T=2. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-19 at T=2, it reaches 81.44%, exceeding SAKD (80.10%) at T=4. On ImageNet with PreAct-ResNet-34 at T=4, the method achieves 70.64% top-1 accuracy (71.76% with a ResNet-50 teacher), outperforming SAKD (70.04%) and RateBP (70.01%). On CIFAR10-DVS with ResNet-19 at T=4, it attains 80.54%, setting a new state-of-the-art with fewer timesteps than prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all BPTT-based direct training baselines (Dspike, GLIF, RateBP, TET, OTTT) and most ANN-guided distillation methods (Joint A-SNN, KDSNN, TSSD, SuperSNN) across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-19, T=2), it beats SAKD by 0.50% (96.56 vs. 96.06 at T=4) and EnOF by 0.37% (96.56 vs. 96.19 at T=2). On ImageNet (ResNet-34, T=4), it surpasses SAKD by 0.60% (70.64 vs. 70.04) and RateBP by 0.63% (70.64 vs. 70.01), and with a ResNet-50 teacher reaches 71.76%, exceeding all prior SNN distillation results. On CIFAR10-DVS (ResNet-19, T=4), it achieves 80.54%, edging out SAKD (80.30% at T=4) and EnOF (80.10% at T=10) with fewer timesteps. The one notable exception is CIFAR-100 where EnOF (82.43% at T=2) still exceeds the proposed method (81.44% at T=2), though the gap narrows at higher timesteps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two SNN training paradigms: ANN-guided knowledge distillation (SAKD, SuperSNN, Joint A-SNN) and rate-based backpropagation (RateBP, Yu et al. 2024). It departs from prior ANN-guided distillation methods that rely on spike-based intermediate representations and explicit feature-matching losses, instead proposing a block-wise replacement strategy where SNN rate-based features flow through frozen ANN blocks to create implicit functional alignment. The theoretical grounding draws on the classic firing-rate-to-ReLU equivalence (Rueckauer et al. 2017), but the key conceptual departure is that no explicit feature-alignment objective is imposed; alignment emerges as a byproduct of the distillation loss computed at the logit level through the hybrid models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical training-efficiency advantage over BPTT-based SNN training by decoupling memory and compute from the number of timesteps, while simultaneously leveraging pretrained ANN knowledge to improve accuracy. The implicit alignment mechanism—where cosine similarity between SNN rate features and ANN features improves without any explicit matching loss—is a clean design choice that avoids the brittleness of hard feature constraints. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ResNet-family architectures, whether the number of hybrid model branches can be reduced without accuracy loss, and whether the method extends to other neuron types or tasks beyond image classification. The requirement of a pretrained ANN teacher of comparable or larger size remains a practical dependency.
