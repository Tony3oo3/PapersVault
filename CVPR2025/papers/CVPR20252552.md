## Problem

Existing unsupervised diffusion-based anomaly detection methods rely on auxiliary discriminative sub-networks or semantic guidance modules to extract anomaly features, and they focus on reconstructing anomaly features rather than transforming anomalies into normal images. This limits robustness across anomaly classes, lighting, and pose variations, and complicates deployment. The paper asks whether a single unified diffusion process can directly map anomalous images to normal ones without any additional network components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252552/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single anomaly RGB image (resized to 256×256) as input, encodes it via a frozen VQ-VAE into a 64×64×3 latent vector, and produces a reconstructed normal image in the same latent space. Anomaly detection and localization are then obtained by computing multi-scale feature differences (using a pre-trained ImageNet backbone) between the original and reconstructed images, yielding an image-level anomaly score and a pixel-level heatmap. Training uses only normal images with synthetic anomaly augmentations (masks, noise patterns) applied, so no anomaly labels are ever needed.

## Method

LASB replaces the standard Gaussian diffusion forward process with a Linear Schrödinger Bridge that directly connects the anomalous distribution (p_A) to the normal distribution (p_B) in VQ-VAE latent space. By imposing Dirac delta boundary conditions, the optimal backward drift is made tractable: the score network (a U-Net) is trained to denoise toward a fixed target κ rather than toward a Gaussian, so the reverse process converges to the normal image regardless of the anomaly type. A semi-degradation strategy (analogous to Palette) preserves structural integrity during the forward pass, unlike standard DDPM which progressively destroys image structure. At inference, the model reconstructs a normal image from the anomaly in as few as 10 NFEs (0.74 s on V100), and the pixel-wise difference between original and reconstruction, processed through multi-scale ImageNet features, yields the anomaly score and localization heatmap—no auxiliary classifier or guidance network is required.

## Results

In the class-based setting, LASB achieves AUROC_cls/AUROC_seg of 99.66/99.15 on MVTec-AD and 98.52/99.06 on VisA, surpassing the strongest competitor DDAD (99.84/98.05 on MVTec, 98.9/97.58 on VisA) on VisA by 0.62% in AUROC_seg. In the unified (multi-class) setting, LASB reaches 99.14/98.66 on MVTec-AD and 94.2/98.18 on VisA, outperforming MambaAD (98.6/97.7) and DiAD (97.15/96.76) on MVTec. On MVTec-AD with full metrics (Table 2), LASB attains 99.2% AUROC_cls, 99.3% AP_cls, 98.6% AUROC_seg, and 78.2% AP_seg, with a 17.4% improvement in AP_seg over the second-best method (DiAD). Inference time is 0.74 s on a V100 GPU, roughly 2× faster than DiAD and 1.6× faster than TransFusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the class-based setting, LASB (99.66/99.15 on MVTec, 98.52/99.06 on VisA) slightly trails DDAD on MVTec AUROC_cls (99.66 vs 99.84) but clearly wins on VisA AUROC_seg (99.06 vs 97.58) and MVTec AUROC_seg (99.15 vs 98.05). Among unified multi-class methods, LASB outperforms all baselines on MVTec, beating MambaAD by 0.54% AUROC_cls and DiAD by 1.99% AUROC_cls, while on VisA it edges out MambaAD on AUROC_seg (98.18 vs 98.5) but is marginally behind on AUROC_cls (94.2 vs 94.3). Against diffusion-specific baselines on MVTec (Table 2), LASB achieves a 17.4% absolute gain in AP_seg over DiAD (78.2 vs 52.6) and a 10.0% gain in F1max_seg, while matching or exceeding DDAD on detection metrics. In inference speed, LASB (0.74 s) is 2× faster than DiAD (2.8 s), 1.6× faster than TransFusion (1.2 s), and 2.1× faster than DDAD (1.5 s), while achieving superior localization accuracy across all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

LASB builds directly on the Linear Schrödinger Bridge formulation from I2SB (Liu et al., ICML 2023), which introduced Dirac delta boundary conditions to make image-to-image SB tractable, and on the foundational diffusion SB framework of De Bortoli et al. (NeurIPS 2021). It departs from prior diffusion-based anomaly detection methods (DiffAD, AD-SPR, DiAD) that rely on auxiliary discriminative sub-networks or semantic guidance modules by showing that the SB's inherent structure-preserving transport between anomaly and normal distributions eliminates the need for such components. The method also inherits the VQ-VAE latent compression strategy from Stable Diffusion, but replaces the standard Gaussian forward process with a semi-degrading bridge process that maintains structural integrity throughout the diffusion trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the choice of diffusion process (bridge vs. Gaussian) is not merely a technical detail but fundamentally affects anomaly localization quality, with the SB's structure-preserving property yielding a 17.4% AP_seg improvement over the next-best diffusion baseline. The unified, auxiliary-network-free design and 5×/3× efficiency gains in training time/memory make this practically attractive for industrial deployment where per-class models are infeasible. Open questions include whether the SB advantage persists for highly novel or out-of-distribution anomaly types not seen during augmentation, and whether the approach extends naturally to 3D medical imaging or video anomaly detection where temporal structure preservation becomes critical.
