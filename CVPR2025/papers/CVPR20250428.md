## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction datasets either lack multi-view egocentric streams from real headsets, rely on lower-accuracy RGB-D optimization for annotations, or provide only single-view recordings. Multi-view egocentric data, which is natively available on current AR/VR devices like Project Aria and Quest 3, has been largely unexplored for 3D hand and object tracking, leaving it unclear whether the additional views meaningfully improve pose estimation accuracy.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides hardware-synchronized multi-view frames: Aria yields one 1408×1408 RGB image plus two 640×480 monochrome images per frame, while Quest 3 yields two 1280×1024 monochrome images. For the benchmarked tasks, methods consume 2D bounding boxes (hand tracking), 2D segmentation masks (object pose estimation and 3D lifting), CAD object models, and optionally SLAM point clouds, producing 3D hand joint positions (in mm), 6DoF object poses (symmetry-aware translation/rotation), binary 2D masks, and 3D object center locations. Ground-truth annotations come from a passive marker-based motion-capture system, with hand poses in both UmeTrack and MANO formats and objects as 3D rigid transforms from model to scene space.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the HOT3D dataset (833 minutes, 3.7M+ images, 19 subjects, 33 objects) annotated via motion capture, paired with strong multi-view baselines for three tasks. For 3D hand tracking, UmeTrack is trained on two-view streams with one view randomly masked to enable fair single- vs. two-view comparison. For 6DoF object pose estimation, FoundPose is extended to multi-view by cropping the object in all available views, retrieving templates via DINOv2 bag-of-words, establishing multi-view 2D-3D correspondences, and solving the generalized PnP problem. For 3D lifting of unknown in-hand objects, StereoMatch constructs aligned stereo crops around object masks, extracts DINOv2 ViT-S patch features, links nearest patches in the same row across views, and triangulates up to 500 correspondences with smallest cyclic distance to estimate the 3D object center via robust mean. Across all three tasks, multi-view methods achieve 8–41% relative improvements over their single-view counterparts.

## Results

For 6DoF object pose estimation, the multi-view extension of FoundPose achieves 8–12 percentage points higher recall than the single-view version on both headsets (e.g., 33.8% vs. 25.2% at the 5 cm / 5° threshold on HOT3D-Aria, and 36.9% vs. 28.9% on HOT3D-Quest3), corresponding to a 13–34% relative improvement. For 3D lifting of in-hand objects, the StereoMatch method reaches 64.4% recall at 5 cm on HOT3D-Aria (3 views) and 76.4% on HOT3D-Quest3 (2 views), far exceeding MonoDepth (14.3% and N/A) and HandProxy (0.5%). For 2D segmentation of in-hand objects, MRCNN-DA achieves 55.2 mIoU on HOT3D-Aria and 54.7 on HOT3D-Quest3, outperforming EgoHOS (42.6 and 33.1) by 30% and 65% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FoundPose (single-view, ECCV 2024), the paper's multi-view extension wins decisively on both Aria (3-view) and Quest 3 (2-view) test splits, gaining 8.6 and 8.0 percentage points in recall at the strictest 5 cm / 5° threshold, and 11.5 and 7.5 points at the 20 cm / 20° threshold. The improvement is attributed to additional 3D constraints from multiple views and the ability to observe heavily occluded objects. Against EgoHOS (ECCV 2022) for 2D in-hand object segmentation, the paper's MRCNN-DA baseline (Mask R-CNN trained on predicted depth from Depth Anything V2) outperforms EgoHOS by 12.6 mIoU on HOT3D-Aria (55.2 vs. 42.6) and by 21.6 mIoU on HOT3D-Quest3 (54.7 vs. 33.1), with the larger gap on Quest 3 reflecting EgoHOS's poor generalization to monochrome images. The paper does not directly compare against other 6DoF pose estimation or 3D lifting methods beyond these two external baselines; the remaining comparisons (UmeTrack single- vs. two-view, MonoDepth vs. StereoMatch, HandProxy) are internal ablations demonstrating the multi-view advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOT3D positions itself as the first large-scale dataset providing multi-view, hardware time-synchronized egocentric video from real headsets (Project Aria and Quest 3) with motion-capture-quality 3D annotations of both hands and objects, filling a gap left by prior datasets like ARCTIC (single egocentric view among 9), HOI4D (single-view only), and HO-Cap (lower-accuracy RGB-D annotations). Methodologically, the paper extends FoundPose from single-view to multi-view 6DoF pose estimation via a straightforward generalization of PnP-RANSAC to the generalized PnP problem, and introduces a StereoMatch baseline for 3D lifting that repurposes DINOv2 patch features for row-constrained stereo correspondence. The hand tracking experiments reuse UmeTrack's architecture, and the segmentation baselines build on Mask R-CNN with Depth Anything V2 as a depth prior, making the methodological contributions deliberately simple to isolate the multi-view data advantage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOT3D is likely to become a standard benchmark for egocentric hand-object interaction research because it uniquely enables fair comparison of single- vs. multi-view methods on the same hardware, which is the realistic deployment setting for AR/VR devices that have multiple cameras but no active depth sensors. The consistent 8–41% multi-view advantage across hand tracking, object pose estimation, and 3D lifting provides strong empirical justification for multi-view architectures in power-constrained wearable systems. Open questions include whether the findings generalize to dynamic, unscripted interactions beyond the 425 controlled recordings, how methods perform on truly unseen objects (the onboarding sequences hint at this but no full CAD-free tracking baseline is provided), and whether the mocap-quality annotations transfer to noisier real-world deployment without a motion-capture lab.
