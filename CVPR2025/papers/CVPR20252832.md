## Problem

Existing object detection methods address either covariate shift (domain generalization) or semantic shift (OOD detection) in isolation, but real-world deployment requires handling both simultaneously—e.g., detecting an unknown object under unseen adverse weather. No prior method tackles this entangled two-shift scenario without access to any auxiliary target-domain data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252832/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images from a single source domain (PASCAL VOC or BDD-100k) with pre-defined categories. At test time, it receives RGB images rendered in unseen styles (Acrylic, Purple-Brush, or Sketch via Clipstyler) and outputs bounding boxes with a binary known/unknown label determined by an energy-score threshold (δ=0.95). The method is a plug-in module for Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50, requiring no architectural changes to the detector head.

## Method

WFS imitates human cognition through three coupled modules. First, multi-level perception extracts local features via a convolutional backbone, global features via a one-layer Mamba (selective state-space model), and connective features via graph convolution over a softmax-similarity adjacency matrix, then fuses them into a unified representation. Second, a memory recorder with Q=24 learnable codewords captures fragmented known-object content through residual-weighted assignment, and a modulated (scale-and-shift) operation on the codebook produces an imagination bank that is contrastively pushed away from known features. Third, a recurrent unknown-feature generator takes variational encodings of the memory-enhanced map and imagination-re-represented features as inputs, iteratively synthesizing outlier features that deviate from in-distribution objects. Training combines standard detection loss with contrastive losses (imagination-memory separation, known-unknown separation) and an energy-based uncertainty loss, while inference simply thresholds the object-level energy score.

## Results

On the novel ODU-OD task (Table 1), plugging WFS into VOS reduces FPR95 from 88.73% to 80.16% (VOC→MS-COCO, Acrylic style) and from 65.71% to 56.48% (BDD→MS-COCO, Acrylic), while AUROC improves from 71.13% to 74.31% and 71.96% to 75.87% respectively. On the standard OOD-OD benchmark (Table 2), VOS+WFS achieves FPR95 of 37.91/41.36 (MS-COCO/OpenImages) and AUROC of 91.47/89.78, outperforming the VOS baseline (47.53/51.33 FPR95) and DFDD (41.34/44.52). On Single-DGOD (Table 4), S-DGOD+WFS reaches 38.2/30.4/18.3/35.7 mAP across four weather conditions, surpassing S-DGOD (36.6/28.2/16.6/33.5) and Clip-gap (36.9/32.3/18.7/38.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WFS is plugged into two strong OOD-detection baselines (VOS [6] and SAFE [40]) and consistently outperforms their unmodified versions across all four tasks. On ODU-OD, VOS+WFS reduces FPR95 by ~8.6 points (88.73→80.16, VOC/Acrylic/MS-COCO) and ~9.2 points (65.71→56.48, BDD/Acrylic/MS-COCO) over VOS, while also surpassing SR-VAE [42] and DFDD [44] which were not augmented with WFS. On OOD-OD, VOS+WFS (FPR95 37.91/41.36) beats VOS (47.53/51.33), DFDD (41.34/44.52), SR-VAE (42.17/46.26), and SIREN-KNN [4] (47.45/50.38); SAFE+WFS (37.48/11.27) achieves the best OpenImages FPR95 by a large margin over SAFE (47.40/20.06). On OSOD, OpenDet+WFS [11] improves mAPK from 80.02 to 80.53 and reduces WI from 14.95 to 11.98 on VOC, outperforming DFDD [44] (80.26 mAPK) and ORE [18] (79.80). On Single-DGOD, S-DGOD+WFS [41] gains +1.6 mAP over S-DGOD and +1.6 over Clip-gap [39] on Night-Sunny, while Clip-gap+WFS achieves the best overall mAP (38.5/34.1/20.2/40.1) across all four weather conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: domain-generalized object detection (Single-DGOD, Clip-Gap) addressing covariate shift, and unsupervised OOD object detection (VOS, DFDD, SAFE, SR-VAE) addressing semantic shift. It builds on Faster R-CNN as the base detector, adapts the codebook-based memory mechanism from Deep TEN for preserving known-object information, and employs the energy score for test-time unknown discrimination. The key departure is the explicit combination of both shifts into a single task (ODU-OD) and the use of a Mamba-based global representation alongside graph convolution for multi-level perception, neither of which appears in prior OOD-OD or DGOD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ODU-OD formulation is practically motivated by safety-critical deployment (e.g., autonomous vehicles encountering unknown objects in adverse weather), and the modular plug-in design (WFS added to VOS or SAFE) makes it immediately applicable to existing pipelines. However, the open-domain dataset is constructed solely via Clipstyler style transfer, which may not capture the full spectrum of real-world domain shifts (e.g., sensor degradation, novel object categories with different semantics). Open questions include whether the recurrent generator scales to higher-resolution feature maps, whether the imagination mechanism can be made more interpretable or controllable, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two base detectors tested.
