## Problem

Adapting vision-language models to diverse deployment scenarios (different hardware platforms, latency vs. throughput requirements) traditionally demands either training multiple unique VLMs or repeatedly applying compression techniques, both of which incur high training costs and complex deployment pipelines. No existing paradigm allows a single tuning pass to yield multiple deployable VLM variants of varying sizes without relying on model compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251825/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The OTMV-tuned VLMs consume standard image patches (via ViT-style patch and position embeddings) paired with text tokens (questions, sentences, or instructions) and produce task-specific outputs: free-form or multiple-choice text answers for VQA, binary true/false judgments for visual reasoning, ranked image-text pairs for retrieval, and descriptive captions for image captioning. The key operational detail is that at inference time, OTMV gates dynamically decide which high-order weight tensors to expand or bypass, so the same tuned model yields variants with different effective parameter counts and compute budgets without any retraining or re-deployment.

## Method

OTMV replaces all weight tensors in transformer blocks beyond the first with the first block's key tensors scaled by per-layer learnable scalar coefficients, drawing an analogy to finite series expansion where the first-order term is the key tensor and higher-order terms are coefficient-scaled copies. Multi-head attention weights are shared only within the same modality (vision or language), while feed-forward weights are shared across modalities via an element-wise mean of the two first-block FF tensors. During tuning, each high-order tensor is gated by an OTMV gate G(p_skip) that randomly skips it in both forward and backward passes with probability p_skip (typically 0.05–0.1), forcing the network to remain accurate under partial expansion. At inference, the gate configuration is fixed per variant, selecting which tensors to expand, so a single OTMV-tuned model can be instantiated as many size variants (e.g., 24 variants from BEiT-3 Large) with negligible accuracy loss relative to the full model, while requiring only the key tensors and scalar coefficients to be stored in memory.

## Results

OTMV-tuned VLMs achieve near-identical accuracy to their naive baselines across all four task suites: e.g., BEiT-3(Large) scores 82.53% VQA test-dev vs. 82.50% for BEiT-3(Large)-OTMV, and 89.95% NLVR2 test-P vs. 89.96% for the OTMV version. On image captioning (COCO), BEiT-3(Large)-OTMV even slightly exceeds the baseline on BLEU@4 (41.49 vs. 41.46) and CIDEr (143.30 vs. 143.29). In the dynamic expansion comparison, OTMV-expanded BEiT-3 Large variants outperform DistillVLM, EfficientVLM, and UPop at the same parameter size, and a 60%-parameter-saving OTMV variant yields a 63% inference speedup on A100 GPUs. The tuning cost for OTMV is 6.4 A100 GPU-Days, versus 131.8–188.8 for the compression baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three reference VLMs (VLMo, BLIP, BEiT-3), OTMV-tuned versions essentially tie the baselines, with gaps of ≤0.5% on VQA and NLVR2 and ≤0.1 on retrieval metrics; on COCO captioning, BEiT-3(Large)-OTMV marginally exceeds the baseline on BLEU@4 (41.49 vs. 41.46) and CIDEr (143.30 vs. 143.29). Against the three compression baselines (DistillVLM, EfficientVLM, UPop), OTMV-expanded BEiT-3 Large variants consistently achieve higher VQA and captioning scores at the same parameter size, and a same-size OTMV variant even slightly surpasses the full BEiT-3 Base model. The tuning cost advantage is dramatic: OTMV requires 6.4 A100 GPU-Days, compared to 131.8 (DistillVLM), 149.1 (EfficientVLM), and 188.8 (UPop) for a single variant; producing 24 variants via compression would cost roughly 73,800 A100 GPU-Days (~11,530× more than OTMV). OTMV also avoids the extra deployment overhead of compression pipelines (pruning, distillation, subnet search), since variants are generated purely by toggling OTMV gates at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

OTMV carves new ground by repurposing the mathematical series expansion theorem as a weight-sharing mechanism for transformer-based VLMs, where higher-order terms become scalar-scaled copies of first-order (key) weight tensors. It departs from the PEFT/LoRA line of work, which trains separate adapters per scenario, and from compression methods (DistillVLM, EfficientVLM, UPop) that require distinct, non-reusable compression pipelines per target size. While all-in-one approaches like OFA, Slimmable networks, MatFormer, and Flextron share the 'train once, deploy many' philosophy, they target CNNs or LLMs and rely on architectural search or nested elastic structures rather than the scalar-coefficient expansion and stochastic skip-gate mechanism introduced here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-tuning-to-multiple-variants paradigm offers a practically compelling 1× vs. 20–30× reduction in tuning cost for producing deployment-ready VLM variants, which is directly relevant to industrial multi-platform deployment. However, the accuracy differences between OTMV-tuned and baseline models are marginal (often within 0.1–0.5%), and the dynamic-expansion comparison with compression methods compares fundamentally different operating points, making the 'outperformance' claim less clean than it appears. Open questions include whether the series-expansion approximation degrades gracefully at very aggressive expansion ratios (e.g., >80% parameter reduction), whether the approach generalizes to non-transformer VLM backbones, and how the OTMV gate interacts with quantization or other post-training compression at deployment time.
