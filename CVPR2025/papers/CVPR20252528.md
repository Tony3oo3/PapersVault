## Problem

Existing 3D semantic occupancy prediction methods depend on costly voxel-wise 3D annotations and computationally heavy dense voxel grids, while self-supervised alternatives still rely on pre-computed 2D segmentation pseudo-labels that limit generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios. The paper asks whether sparse, feed-forward 3D scene representations can be trained self-supervisedly by aligning them with vision foundation models, enabling open-vocabulary occupancy prediction without any explicit 3D labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252528/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6-camera RGB images at 504×896 resolution, from which pre-trained VFMs (MaskCLIP+FeatUp or Talk2DINO) extract feature maps and Metric3D V2 provides metric depth for initializing Gaussian 3D positions. At inference, text category prompts (e.g., 'car', 'vegetation') are encoded by the corresponding text encoder to produce semantic logits. The output is a sparse set of 1800 Gaussians (300 per view) that are voxelized into an 80 m × 80 m × 6.4 m volume at 0.4 m resolution across 18 semantic classes, supporting arbitrary open-vocabulary categories at test time.

## Method

GaussTR predicts a sparse set of learnable Gaussian queries through a 3-layer Transformer decoder, where each Gaussian is parameterized by a 3D center, scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and a feature vector replacing spherical harmonics. Deformable cross-attention aggregates multi-view VFM features conditioned on projected 2D positions, followed by global self-attention with 3D positional encodings to model inter-Gaussian spatial context. Training is fully self-supervised: predicted Gaussians are rendered back to source views via differentiable alpha-blending splatting (with PCA-reduced features for efficiency), and the rendered features and depth are supervised by cosine similarity to VFM features and SILog+L1 depth losses, respectively. At inference, Gaussian features are matched against text-embedded category vectors via softmax similarity, enabling zero-shot open-vocabulary occupancy without any segmentation head. This sparse Gaussian formulation uses only ~3% of the scene representation parameters compared to dense voxel methods while reducing training time by 40%.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes, GaussTR (Talk2DINO variant) achieves a state-of-the-art zero-shot mIoU of 12.27 and IoU of 44.54, while the FeatUp variant reaches 11.70 mIoU and 45.19 IoU. This represents a 23% relative improvement (2.33 mIoU) over the previous best self-supervised method (GaussianOcc, 9.94 mIoU) while reducing training time by approximately 40% (12 hours on 8×A800 GPUs). The method uses only 1800 Gaussian queries per scene (300 per view × 6 cameras), corresponding to roughly 3% of the scene representation parameters used by dense voxel-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussTR outperforms all four self-supervised baselines on mIoU: it surpasses GaussianOcc (9.94) by 2.33 mIoU, SelfOcc (9.30) by 2.97 mIoU, OccNeRF (9.53) by 2.74 mIoU, and DistillNeRF (8.93) by 3.34 mIoU. The gains are most pronounced on object-centric categories—e.g., car IoU jumps from 13.55 (GaussianOcc) to 24.27 (GaussTR-Talk2DINO), and truck IoU from 9.61 to 17.16—reflecting the natural fit of sparse Gaussian representations for object-level geometry. However, GaussTR underperforms on flat surfaces (drivable surface: 36.98 vs. 55.49 for SelfOcc; sidewalk: 17.21 vs. 26.30) and small objects (traffic cone: 1.86 vs. 9.76 for GaussianOcc), which the authors attribute to occlusion during splatting and the difficulty of capturing fine details with sparse representations. On the geometric IoU metric, GaussTR (FeatUp) matches SelfOcc (45.19 vs. 45.01) while substantially exceeding OccNeRF (22.81) and DistillNeRF (29.11), confirming that the Gaussian-based geometry is competitive with dense voxel approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussTR builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for scene representation but repurposes it from RGB reconstruction to semantic occupancy prediction, replacing the conventional color term with learnable feature vectors aligned to vision foundation models. It departs from the dense voxel-based self-supervised occupancy line (RenderOcc, SelfOcc, OccNeRF, GaussianOcc) by adopting sparse, feed-forward Gaussian queries predicted via Transformer layers, eliminating the computational overhead of volume rendering over full voxel grids. Conceptually, it also connects to generalizable 3D GS methods (PixelSplat, MVSplat) that predict Gaussians in a feed-forward manner, but shifts the objective from photometric reconstruction to foundation-model-aligned spatial understanding, enabling open-vocabulary prediction through text-prototype similarity rather than requiring per-class segmentation pseudo-labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GaussTR demonstrates that sparse Gaussian representations (only 3% of voxel parameters) combined with VFM alignment can match or exceed dense-voxel self-supervised methods while cutting training time by 40%, suggesting a more scalable path toward 3D spatial understanding for autonomous driving and embodied agents. The open-vocabulary capability—predicting semantics for arbitrary categories via text-prototype similarity without explicit labels—addresses a key generalization bottleneck in prior self-supervised occupancy methods that relied on fixed pseudo-label sets. However, the method still struggles with small objects (traffic cones) and flat surfaces (roads, sidewalks) due to sparsity and occlusion during splatting, and all results are validated on a single dataset (Occ3D-nuScenes), leaving open whether the efficiency and generalization advantages hold at larger scale or across diverse driving environments.
