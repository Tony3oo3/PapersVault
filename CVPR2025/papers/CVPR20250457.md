## Problem

Existing LiDAR pretraining methods rely exclusively on sparse voxel representations, overlooking the complementary geometric and semantic attributes captured by range images and raw points. No prior work has explored the Mixture of Experts paradigm to dynamically fuse multiple LiDAR representations for 3D scene understanding, leaving a gap in how to exploit the distinct strengths of each representation (dynamic objects, static structures, fine-grained geometry) in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250457/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point clouds (N points with 3D coordinates plus L-dimensional features such as intensity and elongation) along with V synchronized RGB images for the pretraining stage. Each point cloud is simultaneously converted into three representations: a 2D range image via spherical projection (H_r × W_r grid), a sparse voxel grid (M non-empty voxels with M < N), and the raw point set. The output is per-point 3D semantic class predictions, with the SMS stage producing a single fused logit vector per point by dynamically weighting logits from all three representation-specific heads.

## Method

LiMoE is a three-stage pipeline that integrates range images, sparse voxels, and raw points through a Mixture of Experts framework. Stage 1 (Image-to-LiDAR Pretraining) independently trains three 3D backbones (FRNet, MinkUNet, SPVCNN) by contrastively aligning superpoint embeddings with superpixel embeddings from a pretrained 2D image backbone (DINOv2 or ResNet). Stage 2 (CML) introduces an MoE layer with a trainable gate and a noise module that dynamically assigns per-point weights (α, β, γ) to the three aligned feature sets, then distills the MoE-mixed features into a single-representation student network via a contrastive loss over superpoint embeddings. Stage 3 (SMS) extends the same MoE gating mechanism to the downstream segmentation heads, where per-point semantic logits from all three representations are dynamically weighted and combined, optimized with a joint loss of MoE-weighted cross-entropy plus per-representation supervised losses (Cross-Entropy, Lovász-Softmax, and Boundary loss). The key insight is that each representation captures distinct attributes—range images focus on middle beams and dynamic objects, voxels on upper beams and static structures, points on lower beams and fine geometry—and the MoE gate learns to exploit this complementarity per-point.

## Results

On nuScenes (full data, ViT-L backbone), LiMoE achieves 77.85% mIoU, outperforming the strongest baseline SuperFlow (77.19%) by 0.66%. In the low-data regime (1% annotations), LiMoE reaches 51.41% mIoU on nuScenes and 50.69% on SemanticKITTI, surpassing SuperFlow by 1.46% and 1.62% respectively. On Waymo Open (1%), LiMoE scores 51.93% mIoU vs. 50.67% for SuperFlow. In the linear probing setting (frozen backbone), LiMoE gains 1.10–1.76% mIoU over single-representation methods across all backbone sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiMoE is compared against seven single-representation pretraining methods: SLidR (CVPR'22), TriCC (CVPR'23), Seal (NeurIPS'23), CSC (CVPR'24), HVDistill (IJCV'24), SuperFlow (ECCV'24), and PPKT (arXiv'21). LiMoE consistently outperforms all baselines across every backbone size (ResNet-50, ViT-S/B/L) and every data fraction (LP, 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, Full). The most direct comparison is against SuperFlow, the most recent SOTA: LiMoE gains +0.66 to +1.46% mIoU on nuScenes fine-tuning and +1.62 to +1.93% on SemanticKITTI 1%. In the linear probing setting, LiMoE leads SuperFlow by +1.34% (ViT-L) and +1.76% (ViT-S). On domain generalization (Table 2, seven held-out datasets), LiMoE over SuperFlow by +1.25 to +1.64% mIoU. On robustness (Table 3, Robo3D benchmark), LiMoE achieves the lowest mCE (88.43 vs. 91.67) and highest mRR (83.28 vs. 83.17) in the full fine-tuning setting, and the best average corruption score (64.80 vs. 63.68) over SuperFlow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-to-LiDAR pretraining line (SLidR, SuperFlow, Seal) that transfers 2D image knowledge to 3D LiDAR via contrastive learning, but departs from its exclusive reliance on sparse voxel representation by simultaneously pretraining range-image, voxel, and point-based networks. The fusion mechanism draws on the Mixture of Experts paradigm well-established in LLMs and 2D vision, but is applied here for the first time to LiDAR representation learning. It also contrasts with prior multi-representation segmentation methods (AMVNet, RPVNet, GFNet, UniSeg) that use late-fusion or hierarchical fusion at inference time, whereas LiMoE integrates representations during pretraining via a learned gating mechanism and distills them into a single student network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that range images, sparse voxels, and raw points capture systematically different scene attributes (beam elevation, distance range, object dynamics), the paper provides a principled justification for multi-representation learning in LiDAR perception and a practical recipe (CML + SMS) that consistently improves mIoU across 11 datasets. Limitations include the additional computational cost of SMS (85.8M params, 8.3 FPS) and the infeasibility of range-image representation for datasets with unknown sensor FoV, which restricts the full three-expert configuration. Open questions include whether the MoE gating generalizes to more than three representations or to other 3D modalities (e.g., camera point clouds), and whether the complementary-attribute observation holds for non-automotive LiDAR scenes.
