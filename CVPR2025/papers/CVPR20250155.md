## Problem

Aerial-Ground Person Re-Identification (AGPReID) faces severe appearance variations between drone and ground viewpoints that make cross-view identity matching difficult. Existing approaches either over-rely on hand-crafted attributes or apply fixed view-decoupling within a single viewpoint, failing to adapt to diverse viewpoints and neglecting the discriminative potential of local body-part features that are often partially occluded under steep aerial angles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250155/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB person crops resized to 256×128 pixels, tokenized into 16×16 patches with 768-dimensional embeddings. It outputs a concatenated feature vector combining view-invariant global features (Cls minus View token) and refined local features, which are used for cross-view retrieval ranking under A→G, G→A, and G→A+G evaluation protocols. No re-ranking or test-time augmentation is applied at inference.

## Method

SeCap adopts an encoder-decoder transformer where the encoder is the View Decoupling Transformer (VDT), which introduces a dedicated View token and hierarchically decouples the Cls token at each layer to separate view-related from view-invariant features. The decoder comprises two modules: the Prompt Re-calibration Module (PRM) adaptively re-calibrates a set of learnable prompts by first injecting view-invariant features via cross-attention, then integrating prompt information through self-attention and an FFN, producing prompts tailored to the current viewpoint; and the Local Feature Refinement Module (LFRM) employs a two-way attention mechanism (prompt-to-image and image-to-prompt directions) to synchronously update local features with the re-calibrated prompts, extracting view-invariant local representations. Training is supervised with a combined objective of ID classification loss, soft triplet loss (applied to both global and local features), a view classification loss to guide view-related feature extraction, and an orthogonality loss to enforce thorough decoupling between view-invariant and view-related subspaces.

## Results

On the LAGPeR dataset, SeCap achieves Rank-1 of 41.79% and mAP of 30.37% (A→G), 35.26% and 33.42% (G→A), and 24.39% and 19.24% (G→A+G), outperforming all baselines across all three cross-view settings. On G2APS-ReID, SeCap reaches Rank-1 of 75.31% and mAP of 58.57% (A→G) and 73.22% and 58.90% (G→A). On AG-ReID.v1, SeCap attains Rank-1 of 84.03% (A→G) and 87.01% (G→A) without using the attribute labels provided by the dataset, surpassing the attribute-based AG-ReID method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeCap consistently outperforms the strongest cross-view baseline VDT [38] across all settings: on LAGPeR A→G it gains +1.64 Rank-1 (41.79 vs 40.15) and +1.40 mAP, and on G2APS-ReID A→G it gains +2.26 Rank-1 (75.31 vs 73.05) and +2.34 mAP. Against the attribute-based AG-ReID [20] (with attributes removed for fair comparison), SeCap leads by +1.31 Rank-1 on LAGPeR A→G and +4.56 Rank-1 on G2APS-ReID A→G, demonstrating that adaptive prompt re-calibration surpasses attribute-driven matching. Compared to the single-view ViT baseline, SeCap improves Rank-1 by +3.12 on LAGPeR A→G and +5.93 on G2APS-ReID A→G, confirming effective view-bias mitigation. On AG-ReID.v1, SeCap achieves the best A→G Rank-1 (84.03) without using the 15 attribute labels, beating AG-ReID (81.47) by +2.56 and VDT (82.91) by +1.12, while matching VDT closely on G→A (87.01 vs 86.59). The cross-modal method MIP† [31], re-implemented for AGPReID, is outperformed by SeCap on all LAGPeR and G2APS-ReID settings, with the largest gap on LAGPeR G→A+G (+3.39 Rank-1). CLIP-ReID variants perform substantially worse, with Rank-1 dropping below 25% on LAGPeR, indicating that vision-language pretraining alone is insufficient for extreme cross-view appearance changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the View Decoupling Transformer (VDT) as its encoder, adopting its hierarchical view-token decoupling mechanism, and on the DETR-style Transformer Decoder architecture for its Prompt Re-calibration Module. It departs from prior AGPReID methods (AG-ReID, VDT) by replacing fixed attribute-based or single-viewpoint decoupling strategies with an adaptive prompt recalibration mechanism that dynamically adjusts to the input's viewpoint, and by explicitly refining local features through a two-way attention decoder. The prompt-learning paradigm, previously applied to single-view ReID (MIP, Instruct-ReID), is repurposed here to address cross-view invariance rather than cross-modal alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two contributed real-world datasets (LAGPeR with 4,231 IDs across 21 cameras, and G2APS-ReID with 200,864 images) address a critical data bottleneck in AGPReID research, and the G→A+G evaluation setting (heterogeneous gallery) is a practical addition that prior datasets did not support. However, the method's performance gains over VDT are modest (1–2% Rank-1), and the approach is validated only on a ViT backbone with a single GPU, leaving open whether the adaptive prompt mechanism generalizes to other architectures or to more than two viewpoints. A key open question is whether the prompt recalibration can scale to multi-view or wearable-view scenarios without retraining.
