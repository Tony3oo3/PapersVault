## Problem

Despite a surge in parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for Vision Transformers, a systematic, unifying comparison of their performance, suitable application scenarios, and underlying mechanisms is lacking. Questions such as 'when to apply PEFT,' 'which method to choose,' and 'why PEFT works' remain largely unanswered, especially in visual recognition where prior studies have shown inconsistent results compared to NLP.

## Input / Output

All methods operate on RGB images (224×224) fed into a ViT-B/16 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-21K, with a randomly initialized linear prediction head for each downstream task. The output is a single class label per image. The distinguishing constraint is that only a small fraction of parameters (capped at ≤1.5% of the backbone for low-shot, 2–5% for many-shot) are updated or inserted, while the pre-trained weights remain frozen. For the robustness study, a CLIP ViT-B/16 visual encoder is used with a text-encoder-initialized head, and evaluation extends to four distribution-shifted ImageNet variants.

## Method

This is a unifying empirical study rather than a new architecture. The authors systematically implement and tune 14 PEFT methods spanning four categories (prompt-based, adapter-based, direct selective tuning, and efficient selective tuning) under a shared protocol: matched learning rate, weight decay, method-specific module sizes, and a nonzero drop path rate (0.1) identified as critical for preventing over-fitting. They evaluate on VTAB-1K (19 tasks, 1,000 samples/class) for low-shot, full-size datasets (CIFAR-100, RESISC45, Clevr-Distance) for many-shot, and CLIP fine-tuned on 100-shot ImageNet-1K for distribution-shift robustness. Key findings include: (1) all PEFT methods achieve similar accuracy when fairly tuned, including previously dismissed methods like BitFit; (2) different PEFT methods produce diverse predictions due to distinct inductive biases, enabling ensemble gains; (3) PEFT remains competitive with full FT even in many-shot regimes using only 2–5% of parameters; and (4) PEFT better preserves CLIP's out-of-distribution robustness, though weight-space ensembles (WiSE) applied to full FT can surpass PEFT on both target and shifted accuracy.

## Results

On VTAB-1K (19 tasks, 1K samples/class), all 14 PEFT methods outperform both linear probing (61.9% overall mean) and full FT (70.0% overall mean), with the best methods (ConvPass 77.6%, Houl. Adapter 77.2%, Pfeif. Adapter 76.9%) achieving ~7–8 points higher overall accuracy than full FT. Notably, simple methods like BitFit (75.6%) and DiffFit (75.4%) are competitive with complex adapter-based approaches, and the relative standard deviation across PEFT methods is only 1.09%, indicating near-parity when hyperparameters are fairly tuned. On distribution-shift robustness (CLIP ViT-B/16 fine-tuned on 100-shot ImageNet-1K), PEFT methods achieve 54.7–56.9% average accuracy on ImageNet-(V2,S,R,A) versus 42.5% for full FT, a 12–14 point gain, while maintaining comparable target accuracy (74.7–76.6% vs. 75.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 14 PEFT methods consistently outperform both linear probing (61.9%) and full FT (70.0%) on the VTAB-1K overall mean, with the gap over full FT ranging from ~5 points (VPT-Shallow, 67.3%) to ~7.6 points (ConvPass, 77.6%). Among PEFT methods themselves, performance is remarkably uniform: the spread from best (ConvPass, 77.6%) to worst (VPT-Shallow, 67.3%) is driven mainly by VPT-Shallow's limited capacity, while the remaining 13 methods cluster within a 2.5-point range (74.7–77.6%). Simple direct-selective methods (BitFit 75.6%, DiffFit 75.4%, LayerNorm 74.7%) and NLP-origin methods (LoRA 76.5%, Houl. Adapter 77.2%) are competitive with purpose-built visual PEFT methods (ConvPass 77.6%, AdaptFormer 76.9%), overturning prior reports that deemed them inferior. On the distribution-shift evaluation (Table 2), PEFT methods (54.7–56.9% avg. shift accuracy) substantially outperform full FT (42.5%) by 12–14 points, though full FT with WiSE weight-space ensembling can surpass PEFT on both target and shift accuracy, suggesting a remaining gap for robust PEFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a unifying empirical study that departs from prior PEFT surveys [17, 99, 101] and NLP-focused comparisons [10, 17, 31] by systematically evaluating 14+ representative PEFT methods (LoRA, VPT, Adapter, BitFit, DiffFit, SSF, FacT, etc.) under identical, carefully tuned protocols on Vision Transformers for visual recognition. Rather than proposing a new method, it repurposes existing techniques—many originally designed for NLP—and demonstrates that with proper hyperparameter tuning (especially drop path rate), previously dismissed simple approaches like BitFit become competitive with more sophisticated architectures. The paper also extends the evaluation scope beyond the standard low-shot VTAB-1K benchmark to many-shot regimes and distribution-shift robustness (via CLIP), and introduces WiSE weight-space ensembling to PEFT for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This study provides practitioners with a concrete, reproducible user guide for selecting and tuning PEFT methods in vision, resolving the 'which method, when' question that prior surveys left open. The finding that PEFT methods produce diverse predictions despite similar accuracy opens concrete avenues for ensemble-based improvement, semi-supervised pseudo-label generation, and co-training that remain largely unexplored. The observation that WiSE applied to full FT surpasses PEFT on both target and distribution-shift accuracy raises an open question about how to design inherently robust PEFT methods that preserve pre-trained generalizability without sacrificing target performance. Limitations include focus on ViT-B/16 as the primary backbone (larger models only in appendix) and the ensemble/robustness studies being preliminary rather than fully developed.
