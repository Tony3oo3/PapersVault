## Problem

Object detectors trained on a single source domain degrade substantially when deployed in unseen scenarios with different cameras, weather, or artistic styles. Existing domain generalization methods rely on data augmentation, adversarial training, or meta-learning, but the potential of diffusion models' iterative denoising process to naturally produce domain-invariant representations for detection remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252588/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces bounding boxes with class labels as output. Unusually, the diffusion detector consumes each image through a multi-timestep forward diffusion process (T=5 steps by default), extracting intermediate features at each noise level from a frozen Stable Diffusion model rather than generating images. The diffusion-guided variant outputs standard Faster R-CNN predictions at inference time with no additional computational overhead beyond the baseline detector.

## Method

The core idea is to use a frozen Stable Diffusion model as a feature extractor: intermediate features are pulled from four upsampling stages at T timesteps during forward diffusion, then fused through trainable bottleneck structures and a weighted aggregation module into a 4-level feature pyramid. A Faster R-CNN trained on these diffusion features (F_diff) serves as a teacher. Knowledge is transferred to a standard ResNet-101 Faster R-CNN (F_comm) via two alignment mechanisms: (1) feature-level alignment using PKD correlation-based matching plus cross-feature adaptation that feeds diffusion features into the student's detection heads, and (2) object-level alignment where shared RPN proposals are pooled from both detectors and aligned through temperature-scaled KL divergence for classification and L1 loss for box regression. The joint objective combines supervised detection loss on source data with weighted feature-level and object-level alignment terms, enabling the student to inherit domain-invariant representations while maintaining efficient single-pass inference.

## Results

On the Cross Camera benchmark (Cityscapes→BDD100K), the Diff.Detector (SD-1.5) achieves 46.6 mAP, surpassing the best DG method (MAD, 28.0) by 18.6 points and the best DA method (HT, 40.2) by 6.4 points. On the Synthetic-to-Real benchmark, it reaches 62.8/64.4 mAP for car detection on Cityscapes/BDD100K, outperforming the best DG baseline (SRCD, 43.0/31.6) by ~20 points. On the Corruption benchmark, the diffusion detector achieves 69.5 rPC (robustness ratio), nearly 20 points above the best prior method (OA-DG, 50.2). The diffusion-guided detector (transferring knowledge to a standard Faster R-CNN) gains 15.9% mAP on average over its baseline across all six benchmarks while maintaining standard inference speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The diffusion detector (SD-1.5) decisively outperforms all DG baselines across every benchmark: on BDD100K it beats MAD (28.0) by 18.6 mAP, on FoggyCityscapes it beats MAD (38.6) by 11.5 mAP, on RainyCityscapes it beats FSDR (42.8) by 15.4 mAP, and on the Diverse Weather benchmark it beats DivAlign (35.5) by 4.7 mAP. Against DA methods that use unlabeled target data, the diffusion detector still wins on most benchmarks (e.g., +6.4 over HT on BDD100K, +6.1 over CMT on RainyCityscapes, +4.3 over CIGAR on Cityscapes car), though it narrowly trails HT by 0.3 mAP on FoggyCityscapes (50.1 vs 50.4). On the Real-to-Artistic benchmark, the diffusion detector achieves 58.3/51.9/68.4 mAP on Clipart/Comic/Watercolor, surpassing the best DG method DivAlign (38.9/33.2/57.4) by 19.4/18.7/11.0 points, but the diffusion-guided variant shows more limited gains here (+13.6/+11.6/+12.7 over baseline), indicating reduced transferability under extreme domain shifts. On the Corruption benchmark, the diffusion detector achieves 69.5 rPC versus 50.2 for OA-DG, a 19.3-point improvement in robustness ratio, though its clean mAP (34.7) is lower than OA-DG's (43.4), suggesting a trade-off between clean and corrupted performance. The diffusion-guided detector consistently adds 13–30 mAP over the Faster R-CNN baseline across benchmarks, validating the knowledge transfer framework's effectiveness without increasing inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pre-trained Stable Diffusion models (SD-1.5/2.1) as frozen feature extractors for domain-generalized object detection, departing from their conventional role in image generation. It builds on PKD (Cao et al., NeurIPS 2022) for cross-architecture feature alignment and CrossKD (Wang et al., CVPR 2024) for shared-RoI object-level knowledge transfer, combining both into a two-level distillation framework that transfers diffusion-derived domain-invariant representations to a standard Faster R-CNN detector. Conceptually, it extends the line of work showing that diffusion intermediate features encode rich semantic information (Luo et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2023) by demonstrating these features are particularly well-suited for robust detection under domain shift, and it positions diffusion models as an alternative to CLIP-based foundation models (ClipGap) for this purpose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely domain-generalized method (no target data) can outperform most domain adaptation methods that access unlabeled target images, which is a notable practical advantage for deployment scenarios where target data is unavailable. However, the approach has clear limitations: the diffusion detector incurs substantial inference cost (789 ms at T=5), and the knowledge transfer framework shows diminished or even negative gains under extreme domain shifts (e.g., Real-to-Artistic, where Diff.Guided scores 40.8 vs. 58.3 for the direct Diff.Detector on Clipart). Open questions include whether more efficient diffusion architectures or fewer denoising steps can preserve the generalization benefit, and whether the multi-timestep fusion strategy generalizes beyond the specific Stable Diffusion variants tested.
