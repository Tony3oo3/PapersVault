## Problem

Existing 3D LMMs encode 3D point features and 2D image features separately, neglecting the interaction between 2D semantics and 3D object properties as well as spatial relationships among objects in 3D scenes. This separation hinders comprehensive scene representation and, when combined with per-task fine-tuning or large token budgets, compromises both training and inference efficiency for a unified multi-task model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252622/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a 3D point cloud of an indoor scene, multi-view RGB-D images (K=5 views selected by visible-point count), and a natural-language instruction. 3D instance proposals are obtained via class-agnostic Mask3D segmentation (binary masks only, no class labels), projected onto image planes where SAM (ViT-H) generates 2D masks and CLIP-ViT-L/14-336px extracts multi-level (L=3) crop features. Outputs are structured as text with embedded object identifiers (e.g., <obj001>) for grounding, single-word/phrase answers for QA, and dense captions referencing multiple objects for captioning.

## Method

Inst3D-LMM introduces two key modules operating on instance-level tokens. The Multi-view Cross-Modal Fusion (MCMF) module appends a learnable [CLS] token to each view's flattened CLIP features, aggregates them via self-attention, then uses cross-attention with 3D geometric features (from Uni3D) as queries and the 2D [CLS] tokens as keys/values to inject fine-grained 2D semantics into 3D instance representations. The 3D Instance Spatial Relation (3D-ISR) module computes pairwise spatial features (sin/cos of horizontal and vertical angles plus Euclidean distance) between all instance center coordinates, then applies a spatial-conditioned self-attention where position embeddings modulate attention weights to produce relation-aware scene-level tokens. Both modules are jointly optimized end-to-end with a frozen 3D encoder and a Vicuna-7B LLM (fine-tuned via LoRA) on a unified multi-task instruction dataset spanning grounding, QA, and dense captioning, eliminating the need for per-task fine-tuning.

## Results

On ScanRefer, Inst3D-LMM achieves 57.8% Acc@0.25 and 51.6% Acc@0.50, outperforming the previous best generalist method Chat-Scene by +2.3% and +1.4% respectively. On Multi3DRefer, it reaches 58.3% F1@0.25 and 53.5% F1@0.50. For 3D Question Answering on ScanQA, Inst3D-LMM scores 43.5 B-1 and 88.6 CIDER, and for 3D Dense Captioning on Scan2Cap it achieves 38.3 B-4@0.50 and 79.7 CIDER@0.50, surpassing the closed-set specialist 3D-VisTA by +4.3% B-4 and +12.8% CIDER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Inst3D-LMM outperforms the strongest generalist baseline Chat-Scene [19] by +2.3% Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer (57.8 vs. 55.5) and +1.2% F1@0.25 on Multi3DRefer (58.3 vs. 57.1). Against specialist methods, it surpasses ReGround3D [58] by +4.7% Acc@0.25 and Grounded 3D-LLM [7] by +9.9% Acc@0.25, while also exceeding the closed-set ConcreteNet [42] (56.1) and CORE-3DVG [51] (56.8) on ScanRefer. On ScanQA, Inst3D-LMM achieves 43.5 B-1, beating Chat-3Dv2 [18] (38.4) by +5.1 and 3D-LLM (BLIP2) [16] (39.3) by +4.2; its CIDER of 88.6 exceeds Chat-Scene [19] (87.7) and LL3DA [6] (75.7). On Scan2Cap, it leads 3D-VisTA [60] (closed-set) by +4.3% B-4 (38.3 vs. 34.0) and +12.8% CIDER (79.7 vs. 66.9), and surpasses LL3DA [6] by +2.4% B-4 and +14.5% CIDER. The method also outperforms zero-shot approaches (LLM-Grounder [50], Visual-Programming [54]) by large margins, confirming the benefit of end-to-end multi-task instruction tuning over prompt-only strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instance-level 3D LMM paradigm introduced by Chat-Scene [19], which decomposes scenes into object proposals with unique tokens, but departs from it by addressing two key limitations: (1) prior methods encode 2D and 3D features separately and concatenate them, neglecting cross-modal interactions, and (2) spatial relationships among objects are under-modeled. The MCMF module repurposes the cross-attention mechanism (inspired by TokenPacker [25]) to inject multi-view CLIP semantics into 3D geometric features, while the 3D-ISR module adapts the language-conditioned spatial attention from ViL3DRel [5] into a position-embedding-based spatial-conditioned attention. Unlike multi-stage alignment approaches (3D-LLM [16], LL3DA [6]) or three-stage training schemes (Chat-3D v2 [18]), Inst3D-LMM performs end-to-end multi-task instruction tuning in a single pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying 3D visual grounding, question answering, and dense captioning under a single generalist model without per-task fine-tuning, this work reduces the engineering burden of deploying 3D scene understanding in embodied agents and robotics. The efficiency gains from instance-level tokens (fewer tokens fed to the LLM) are practically important for real-time applications. However, the evaluation is confined to indoor ScanNet scenes, and open questions remain about scalability to outdoor or large-scale environments, real-time inference on edge devices, and whether the cross-modal fusion strategy generalizes to other 3D modalities (e.g., NeRFs, Gaussian splatting). The reliance on frozen pre-trained 3D encoders (Mask3D, Uni3D) also limits the model's ability to adapt to novel object categories beyond what those encoders have seen.
