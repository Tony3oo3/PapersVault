## Problem

Existing high-resolution image recognition (HRIR) methods select only a small number of salient patches for classification, which is insufficient for tasks where objects and contexts are scattered across wider image regions. Simply increasing the number of gradient-mode patches causes GPU memory overflow, while using more patches at inference than at training fails to close the information gap between the two stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250210/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (exceeding megapixel scale) that is divided into N patches of dataset-specific sizes (100×100, 200×200, or 256×256). A random patch drop ratio r is applied before selection, and the model iteratively scores patches in no-gradient mode to populate two buffers of M salient and S sub-salient embeddings. The output is a single class label produced by a 3-layer MLP applied to the final aggregated embedding Z'.

## Method

The core contribution is the Dual-Buffer Patch Selection (DBPS) strategy, which maintains two buffers: a fundamental buffer P_M for the top-M most salient patches and an auxiliary buffer P_S' for the next-S sub-salient patches, both updated iteratively in no-gradient mode. Only P_M is re-embedded in gradient mode for back-propagation, while P_S' remains gradient-free, so the sub-salient patches contribute context to the forward pass at near-zero additional training cost. A dual-attention MIL architecture performs progressive aggregation: a cross-attention transformer first combines the learnable query with salient embeddings to produce Z1, which then serves as an adaptive salient query to aggregate sub-salient embeddings (Z2), thereby suppressing uninformative backgrounds within sub-salient regions. Random patch drop is introduced to reduce the number of patches traversed during selection, accelerating training while preventing overfitting to simple regions and uncovering harder informative patches.

## Results

On CQU-BPDD (pavement), DBPS with M=12 salient + S=36 sub-salient patches and 30% patch drop achieves 83.5% accuracy at 1.2 GB VRAM and 111 ms, surpassing IPS-Transformer trained with 108 patches (82.2%) while using 85% less memory and 60% less time. On fMoW (satellite), DBPS (M=36, S=24, 20% drop) reaches 80.5% accuracy at 2.9 GB, exceeding IPS-Transformer (M=81) at 80.1% with 53% less VRAM. On DDR (retinopathy), DBPS (M=4, S=20, 20% drop) attains 81.0% accuracy at 4.9 GB, outperforming IPS-Transformer (M=18) at 80.1% with 75% less memory. On MAME (artwork), DBPS (M=30, S=45, 20% drop) achieves 86.8% accuracy at 2.5 GB, matching IPS-Transformer (M=100) at 86.7% with 67% less VRAM. On Swedish Traffic Signs, DBPS reaches 99.3% accuracy (vs. 98.6% for IPS-Transformer), and on CAMELYON16, DBPS achieves 98.6% AUC (vs. 97.5% for IPS-Transformer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is IPS-Transformer [5], the state-of-the-art single-buffer patch selection method. DBPS outperforms IPS-Transformer on all six datasets: by +3.4% on CQU-BPDD (83.5% vs 80.1% at matched M=12), +0.4% on fMoW (80.5% vs 78.3% at M=36), +4.0% on DDR (81.0% vs 77.0% at M=4), +2.9% on MAME (86.8% vs 83.9% at M=10), +0.7% on Swedish Traffic Signs (99.3% vs 98.6%), and +1.1% AUC on CAMELYON16 (98.6% vs 97.5%), while consistently reducing VRAM by 25–85% and training time by 10–60%. Against domain-specific baselines, DBPS surpasses KIPRN (82.1%) and WSPLIN (81.5%) on CQU-BPDD, Zoom-In+ (74.3%) on fMoW, CBANet (77.7%) and CANet (77.5%) on DDR, and GF-ResNet (91.5%) on Swedish Traffic Signs. Among MIL-based methods, DBPS outperforms DPS-Transformer (84.0% on MAME, 97.2% on Swedish), TopMIL (98.2% on Swedish, 76.2% AUC on CAMELYON16), DeepMIL (97.7% on Swedish, 94.5% AUC on CAMELYON16), DSMIL-LC (93.6% AUC), and CLAM-SB (94.2% AUC) on their respective datasets. Standard CNN baselines (ResNet-18/50) are consistently outperformed, with the largest gaps on DDR (81.0% vs 80.3%) and CQU-BPDD (83.5% vs 81.3%), confirming that patch-selection-based methods are essential for HRIR. Notably, DBPS with only 12 salient patches plus 36 sub-salient patches at 30% drop ratio on CQU-BPDD exceeds IPS-Transformer trained with 108 patches (83.5% vs 82.2%) while using 85% less GPU memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IPS-Transformer framework [5], which introduced iterative single-buffer patch selection with cross-attention aggregation for high-resolution image recognition. The key conceptual departure is the observation that the single-buffer assumption (only the top-M most salient patches suffice) fails for tasks where discriminative information is spatially scattered, and the proposed Dual-Buffer Patch Selection (DBPS) recycles the previously discarded sub-salient patches in a no-gradient manner to supplement context. The aggregation module draws on the dual-stream MIL idea from DS-MIL [28] to generate an adaptive salient query for filtering uninformative backgrounds within sub-salient regions, while the random patch drop technique is repurposed from self-supervised pre-training (MAE [20], masked siamese networks [1]) to the HRIR setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for HRIR practitioners: the method achieves accuracy comparable to or exceeding full-patch IPS-Transformer while reducing training memory by ~85% and training time by ~60%, making high-resolution recognition feasible on consumer-grade GPUs. The no-gradient recycling trick is broadly applicable to any sparse-computation pipeline where intermediate 'discarded' features might carry useful context. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation) on high-resolution images, whether the dual-attention MIL is the optimal mechanism for background suppression in sub-salient patches, and how the method behaves when the encoder itself is weak (since patch scoring quality in no-gradient mode is critical).
