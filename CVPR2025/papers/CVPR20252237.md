## Problem

Existing training-time calibration methods primarily address overconfidence while largely ignoring underconfidence (e.g., induced by Mixup), and the design of the classifier itself as a lever for calibration remains underexplored. This paper asks whether a fixed, non-learnable classifier structure can be repurposed to dynamically correct both over- and underconfidence without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252237/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) through a shared CNN encoder (WRN-28-10 or ResNet-50/101). For each input it produces two K-dimensional probability vectors—one from the standard learnable classifier and one from the fixed ETF classifier—which are fused via a scalar weight γ into a single calibrated probability vector; the argmax yields the class label and the maximum probability serves as the calibrated confidence score.

## Method

BalCAL augments a standard CNN with a confidence-tunable module: a lightweight adapter maps the encoder's feature vector into the subspace expected by a fixed Simplex ETF classifier whose scaling factor β directly controls output confidence (proven in Theorem 1) while leaving accuracy nearly unchanged. During training, the model minimizes a weighted sum of cross-entropy losses from both classifiers, with the weight γ updated each epoch via binary search that minimizes |conf_t − δ·acc_t| on the training set, thereby adaptively shifting calibration intensity from accuracy-focused (early training) to confidence-focused (late training). At inference, the two probability vectors are fused as p_fused = γ·p_Sta + (1−γ)·p_ETF using the γ paired with the best checkpoint. The hyperparameter δ (typically 0.91–0.99) scales training accuracy to counteract overfitting-induced overestimation, and β is set to 1 for overconfident models or K for underconfident ones (e.g., Mixup-trained).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, BalCAL achieves an ECE of 0.76% with 92.23% accuracy, substantially outperforming the vanilla model (6.38% ECE) and the strongest prior method VTST (1.52% ECE). On CIFAR-100, BalCAL reduces ECE to 4.21% (vs. 21.63% vanilla, 7.07% TST), and on SVHN it reaches 0.24% ECE. The average ECE across all three datasets is 1.77%, compared to 3.11% for VTST and 10.44% for the vanilla baseline, while maintaining or exceeding vanilla accuracy on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BalCAL outperforms all nine baselines on average ECE (1.77% vs. 3.11% for the best prior method VTST). Against the closest competitors TST and VTST [17], BalCAL achieves 1.42% and 0.76% lower ECE on CIFAR-10, and 2.86% and 3.13% lower ECE on CIFAR-100, respectively, while also matching or exceeding their accuracy. Compared to implicit calibration methods, BalCAL reduces ECE by 1.80% over Mixup [41] on CIFAR-10 and by 10.05% over Focal Loss [26] on CIFAR-100. Against explicit classifier-based methods, BalCAL improves ECE by 4.13% over PLP [44] and by 12.97% over MMCE [20] on CIFAR-100. Notably, BalCAL also achieves the best OOD detection performance (AUROC 89.89/81.26/94.98 and FPR95 64.15/76.03/34.59 on CIFAR-10/100/SVHN), surpassing TST and VTST which actually degrade OOD performance relative to the vanilla model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Neural Collapse phenomenon (Papyan et al., 2020), which shows that classifier weights converge to a Simplex ETF structure during late-stage training. While prior NC-inspired work has been applied to imbalanced learning, incremental learning, and transfer learning, this is the first to repurpose the fixed ETF structure as a confidence-tunable auxiliary classifier for model calibration. It departs from prior classifier-design calibration methods (TST/VTST, which decouple feature extraction from classification, and PLP, which uses a weak classifier hypothesis) by instead *adding* a fixed structured classifier alongside the standard learnable one, with a dynamic balancing factor γ adjusted via binary search each epoch. The method also extends the calibration literature by explicitly addressing underconfidence (e.g., from Mixup), which most prior training-time methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BalCAL offers a practical, plug-in calibration technique that handles both overconfidence and underconfidence without sacrificing accuracy, and notably improves OOD detection where competing methods (TST, VTST) degrade performance. The dynamic adjustment mechanism reduces manual hyperparameter tuning, and the method composes with existing calibration approaches (Mixup, label smoothing). Limitations include the added training overhead from per-epoch binary search, the reliance on the NC convergence assumption (which may not hold for all architectures or training regimes), and evaluation primarily on standard image classification benchmarks. Open questions include whether the ETF-based confidence tuning generalizes to non-CNN backbones (e.g., ViTs), video, or multi-task settings, and whether the adapter can be replaced by a lighter projection.
