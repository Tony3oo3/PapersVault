## Problem

Existing public operating room datasets are limited in scale, realism, and modality diversity: the largest prior dataset (4D-OR) contains only 6,743 timepoints from a single day with non-medical actors, lacks segmentation annotations, and relies solely on external room cameras. No public dataset or model supports multimodal scene graph generation in the OR, leaving a gap in holistic surgical scene understanding that integrates audio, robotic signals, tracking data, and visual inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251819/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MM2SG consumes a heterogeneous set of per-timepoint inputs: multi-view RGB-D frames from five ceiling-mounted Kinect cameras, three high-dynamic-range detail views, a low-exposure tracking camera frame, a colored point cloud, a one-second audio clip, the last five spoken sentences as text, structured robotic log and infrared tracker data, segmentation masks, and memory scene graphs encoding temporal context. The model autoregressively generates scene graph triplets (subject, object, predicate) as free-form text; downstream tasks (robot phase, next action, sterility breach) then consume only the predicted scene graph sequence as input, producing a single label or flag per timepoint.

## Method

MM2SG pairs a modality-specific encoding module with an LLM (LLaVA-7B / Vicuna-7B) initialized from pretrained weights. Images are encoded by a CLIP vision encoder followed by a transformer image pooler that compresses variable-length patch embeddings into 576 tokens; point clouds use an end-to-end Point Transformer V3 producing a single token; audio uses a frozen CLAP encoder (one token); segmentation masks pass through a small CNN (one token each); speech transcripts, robotic logs, and tracker data are injected as structured text. All modality embeddings are linearly projected into the LLM's token space and concatenated as a single input sequence, from which the LLM autoregressively decodes scene graph triplets. Training employs LoRA on the LLM, fine-tunes the last 12 image-encoder layers, uses temporal curriculum learning (non-temporal first, then temporal), and introduces a novel multimodality mixing augmentation that swaps non-visual modalities from scene-graph-similar samples alongside 50% per-modality dropping to prevent overfitting to fixed modality combinations.

## Results

On MM-OR, MM2SG with all modalities (room cameras, detail views, point cloud, audio/transcript, robot logs/tracker) and predicted segmentation achieves a macro F1 of 0.520 for scene graph generation, rising to 0.529 when temporality (memory scene graphs) is added. With ground-truth segmentation and temporality, the score reaches 0.534. The progressive addition of modalities shows consistent gains: room cameras alone yield 0.484, adding detail views and point cloud gives 0.494, and incorporating audio, robot logs, and tracking data pushes the score to 0.502. For downstream tasks, scene-graph-only baselines achieve macro F1 of 0.569 (Robot Phase Prediction), 0.354 (Next Action Anticipation), and 0.550 (Sterility Breach Detection).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM2SG outperforms both baselines on the primary scene graph generation task. Against PSG [61] (a general-purpose panoptic scene graph model fine-tuned on MM-OR), MM2SG with room cameras alone already achieves 0.484 vs. 0.328 macro F1, a 15.6-point gain attributable to the OR-domain-specific architecture and multimodal design. Against ORacle [45] (the prior state-of-the-art surgical scene graph model), MM2SG with room cameras alone scores 0.484 vs. 0.472, and the full multimodal configuration with temporality reaches 0.529, a 5.7-point improvement. The ablation in Table 1 shows that each added modality contributes: detail views and point cloud add ~1 point, audio/transcript and robot logs/tracker together add ~2 points, and predicted segmentation plus temporality adds another ~3 points. The paper also reports that training on both MM-OR and 4D-OR improves segmentation (DVIS++) over MM-OR alone, and that combining modality dropping with multimodality mixing augmentation yields the best SGG performance (0.520 vs. 0.436 without augmentation).

## Relation to Prior Work

MM2SG directly extends ORacle [45], which first framed surgical scene graph generation as a language modeling task with a vision-language model, by replacing its visual-only input with a full multimodal encoding pipeline (audio via CLAP, point clouds via Point Transformer V3, robotic logs, tracking data, and segmentation masks). The temporal context mechanism is inherited from Labrad-OR [43]'s memory scene graphs, and the dataset itself is a substantial scale-up and multimodal extension of 4D-OR [42], which was limited to 6,743 timepoints, a single recording day, non-medical actors, and external RGB-D cameras only. The model backbone (LLaVA-7B / Vicuna-7B with LoRA) follows the standard MLLM recipe, but the modality-specific encoders and the novel multimodality mixing augmentation are new contributions tailored to the OR domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MM-OR is the largest publicly available OR dataset (92,983 timepoints, 500 GB) and the first to include panoptic segmentation and multimodal scene graph annotations, making it a critical resource for the surgical data science community that has been constrained by small or proprietary datasets. The progressive modality ablation (Table 1) confirms that non-visual signals—robotic logs, tracking, audio—genuinely contribute to scene understanding in ways that pure vision cannot capture, validating the multimodal framing for high-stakes procedural environments. Key limitations remain: coverage is restricted to knee replacements in a single OR, the acquisition setup is costly and hard to replicate, and the best macro F1 (0.529) indicates substantial room for improvement, particularly on rare predicates. Open questions include whether the multimodal gains transfer to other surgical specialties and whether the dataset's simulated (phantom) setting limits generalization to real patient anatomy.
