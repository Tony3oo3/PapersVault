## Problem

Attention-based visual representation models (ViTs) suffer from O(N²) computational complexity that becomes prohibitive at high resolutions, while existing efficiency-oriented alternatives (window attention, linear attention, SSMs) sacrifice global receptive fields, non-linearity capacity, or interpretability. This paper asks whether a physically grounded information-conduction mechanism can simultaneously deliver global receptive fields, sub-quadratic complexity, and interpretability in a single vision backbone.

## Input / Output

The model accepts standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, up to 768×768 for efficiency benchmarks) and partitions them into 2D patch feature maps via a stem module. For downstream dense-prediction tasks (detection, segmentation), the Frequency Value Embeddings and thermal diffusivity parameters must be interpolated to match the target spatial resolution, since HCO operates on the full 2D feature map rather than a flattened token sequence. Outputs range from class logits to bounding boxes, instance masks, pixel-wise segmentation maps, or restored images depending on the attached head.

## Method

The core contribution is the Heat Conduction Operator (HCO), which models visual feature propagation as thermal energy diffusion governed by the 2D heat equation. Under a Neumann boundary condition (no flux at image borders), the continuous Fourier-transform solution reduces to a 2D DCT–multiply–IDCT pipeline, yielding O(N^1.5) complexity and high GPU parallelizability. Learnable Frequency Value Embeddings (FVEs) are fed through a linear layer to predict a spatially non-uniform thermal diffusivity k(ωx,ωy), so the coefficient e^{−k(ωx²+ωy²)t} acts as an adaptive frequency-domain filter that can enhance or suppress specific image patterns per channel. The full vHeat architecture stacks four hierarchical stages of heat-conduction layers (each combining a 3×3 depth-wise convolution, HCO, and FFN with multiplicative gating), replacing self-attention entirely while retaining the Swin-style downsampling structure.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, vHeat-B achieves 84.0% top-1 accuracy with only 68M parameters and 11.2G FLOPs, surpassing Swin-B (83.5%) by 0.5% and ConvNeXt-B (83.8%) by 0.2%, while delivering 45% higher throughput (661 vs. 456 img/s). At the Tiny scale, vHeat-T reaches 82.2% top-1 with 29M parameters, outperforming Swin-T (81.3%) and Vim-S (81.4%). For downstream tasks, vHeat-B achieves 47.7 box AP on COCO detection (+0.8 over Swin-B) and 49.6 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation (+1.5 over Swin-B), consistently leading across all model scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

vHeat consistently outperforms all baselines across scales. Against Swin-Transformer [37], vHeat-B gains +0.5% top-1 on ImageNet-1K, +0.8 box AP on COCO detection, and +1.5 mIoU on ADE20K, while using 27% fewer FLOPs and 23% fewer parameters. Against ConvNeXt [39], vHeat-B leads by +0.2% top-1, +0.7 box AP, and +0.5 mIoU with substantially higher throughput (661 vs. 439 img/s). Against Vision Mamba (Vim) [82], vHeat-B surpasses by +0.8% top-1 with 41% fewer FLOPs (11.2G vs. 19.0G) and 2.2× higher throughput. Against DCFormer [33] and RepLKNet [16], vHeat-B leads by +0.5% top-1. On robustness benchmarks, vHeat-B outperforms both Swin-B and ConvNeXt-B on ObjectNet (+1.3/+0.6) and ImageNet-A (+0.8/+0.3). For low-level vision, vHeatIR (HCO replacing SwinIR's attention) edges out SwinIR [34] by +0.04/+0.05 dB PSNR on denoising and +0.03 dB on JPEG artifact reduction, also surpassing DRUNet [77] and DnCNN [76].

## Relation to Prior Work

vHeat carves new ground by repurposing the general solution of the 2D heat equation (solved via DCT/IDCT under Neumann boundary conditions) as a vision information-propagation operator, positioning itself as an alternative to both self-attention (O(N²) ViTs) and selective-scan SSMs (limited 2D parallelism). Conceptually, it is closest to GFNet's frequency-domain global filtering, but introduces the physical heat-conduction interpretation, adaptive thermal diffusivity predicted by Frequency Value Embeddings, and a hierarchical multi-stage architecture. The DCT-based implementation is a well-known signal-processing tool, but its framing as 'visual heat conduction' with content-adaptive diffusivity is the paper's distinctive contribution, distinguishing it from prior frequency-domain vision models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(N^1.5) complexity of HCO translates into a practically significant 3× throughput and 80% GPU memory reduction at 768×768 resolution compared to Swin-B, making global-receptive-field models more tractable for high-resolution dense prediction. The plug-and-play nature and competitive (or superior) accuracy across classification, detection, segmentation, and low-level tasks suggest vHeat could serve as an efficient backbone alternative in production pipelines. Open questions include whether the heat-conduction analogy provides actionable inductive biases beyond what learned frequency filters already capture, how the fixed conduction time t and Neumann boundary assumption generalize to non-rectangular or very high-resolution inputs, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D data.
