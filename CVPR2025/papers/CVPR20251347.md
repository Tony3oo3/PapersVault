## Problem

Cross-Domain Few-Shot Segmentation (CD-FSS) suffers from two interdependent challenges: feature domain sensitivity (source-optimized models extract unreliable representations on target domains) and matching process domain sensitivity (support-to-query cross-instance correspondence degrades under domain shift). Existing CD-FSS methods address either feature adaptation or matching robustness in isolation, overlooking that these two issues are coupled and should be tackled jointly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251347/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N-way K-shot support images with binary masks and query images from the target domain, all resized to 400×400. The model is trained exclusively on PASCAL VOC 2012 + SBD (source domain) with disjoint label spaces from the four target domains (Chest X-ray, ISIC, FSS-1000, Deepglobe), and produces pixel-level segmentation masks for query images. A task-adaptive fine-tuning stage further optimizes parameters using target-domain support labels before final evaluation.

## Method

DATO couples two modules via a shared set of adaptive agents. In the frequency domain, the Consistent Mutual Aggregation (CMA) module transforms features via FFT, perturbs low-frequency components using ALOFT to simulate domain shifts, and trains agents through cross-attention with both original and perturbed spectra under an adversarial objective (a discriminator distinguishes original vs. perturbed agent representations) plus a diversity loss, forcing agents to capture complementary domain-invariant components that then enhance the original features. In the spatial domain, the Correlation Rectification Strategy (CRS) maps each pixel's feature into an agent-level correlation vector by computing cosine similarity against the domain-invariant agent set, effectively transforming the support-to-query matching into a referable feature space that is less sensitive to domain-specific noise. The two modules are linked because the same agents that learn invariant frequency components also define the correlation reference space for matching, creating a coherent dual-domain optimization.

## Results

DATO achieves 70.31% and 73.82% average mIoU on the CD-FSS benchmark under 1-shot and 5-shot settings respectively, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art IFA by 2.51% and 2.42%. On the more challenging ISIC dataset, DATO reaches 68.76% (1-shot) and 70.30% (5-shot), and on Deepglobe it achieves 51.10% (1-shot) and 59.30% (5-shot). In terms of FB-IoU, DATO also outperforms IFA by 3.11% (1-shot) and 2.06% (5-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DATO outperforms all 19 compared baselines on average mIoU across both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. Against the strongest CD-FSS competitor IFA [29], DATO gains +2.51% (1-shot) and +2.42% (5-shot) on average, with particularly notable margins on ISIC (+2.46% / +0.50%) and Deepglobe (+0.50% / +0.50%). Among feature-adaptation CD-FSS methods, DR-Adapter [37] (60.86/65.42) and ABCDFSS [14] (60.70/65.00) trail DATO by roughly 9–10 points, while the earliest CD-FSS method PATNet [20] (56.06/61.99) lags by over 14 points. DATO also surpasses all in-domain FSS baselines (e.g., HSNet 47.57/51.38, RePRI 46.09/48.34), confirming that cross-domain-specific design is essential. On the transformer-architecture ablation (Table 4), DATO integrated into FPTrans improves average mIoU from 56.69% to 63.13% (1-shot), and into HDMNet from 41.00% to 51.84%, demonstrating architecture-agnostic gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SSP as its segmentation backbone and adopts ALOFT's low-frequency perturbation technique to simulate domain shifts, but departs from prior CD-FSS work by unifying two previously separate lines of research—feature domain adaptation (PATNet, DR-Adapter, ABCDFSS) and matching robustness (IFA)—into a single dual-agent framework. The key conceptual departure is treating feature sensitivity and matching sensitivity as interdependent rather than independent problems, addressing them jointly through shared agents that operate in both the frequency domain (CMA) and spatial domain (CRS). The adversarial agent-aggregation mechanism in the frequency domain is novel in this context, borrowing from domain generalization literature but repurposed for few-shot cross-domain segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled argument that feature and matching sensitivities in CD-FSS are coupled, and the dual-agent design elegantly exploits this coupling—agents trained for domain-invariant feature capture simultaneously serve as a referable bridge for cross-instance matching. This unified perspective may guide future work beyond CD-FSS toward other cross-domain few-shot tasks. Limitations include reliance on target-domain fine-tuning (30 additional epochs), evaluation on only four target domains, and the frequency-domain perturbation strategy being tied to a specific ALOFT formulation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more extreme domain gaps or to video/3D segmentation settings.
