## Problem

While diffusion models have been individually repurposed for depth estimation, optical flow, and amodal segmentation, no systematic study has characterized how they scale across training and inference compute for these discriminative tasks. This paper asks: what are the compute-optimal recipes for allocating budget across pre-training, fine-tuning, and test-time inference to maximize perceptual task performance under constrained compute?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250907/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a task-specific conditional image (next video frame for optical flow, visible-portion mask for amodal segmentation), both encoded to 4-channel latents via a frozen Stable Diffusion VAE at 1/8 spatial resolution. Only the ground-truth latent is noised and concatenated with the clean RGB latent (doubling input channels), while a CLIP task embedding disambiguates the target task. Outputs are generated through iterative DDIM denoising (up to 200 steps) at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution, producing a depth map, dense optical flow field, or amodal segmentation mask respectively.

## Method

The framework pre-trains DiT backbones (dense and Sparse MoE) on ImageNet-1K for class-conditional generation, then fine-tunes them via image-to-image diffusion where the first convolutional layer is widened to accept concatenated RGB and noised target latents. Training compute is scaled along four axes: model size (14.8M–1.9B dense, up to 2.8B MoE), pre-training steps, image resolution (256→512), and post-hoc MoE upcycling of fine-tuned dense checkpoints. Test-time compute is scaled by increasing DDIM denoising steps, ensembling N independently-noised predictions via median compilation (affine-invariant scale/shift alignment), and reallocating compute across timesteps with a cosine noise variance schedule. A unified multi-task model is achieved by replacing the patch embedding with a PatchEmbedRouter that routes VAE embeddings to task-specific input convolutions, followed by upcycling to DiT-XL-8E2A.

## Results

On depth estimation, the model achieves AbsRel 13.6 / δ1 87.6 on Hypersim and AbsRel 4.8 / δ1 97.8 on ETH3D, matching or exceeding Marigold (AbsRel 13.5 / δ1 87.5 on Hypersim; AbsRel 6.5 / δ1 96.0 on ETH3D) while using ~3 orders of magnitude less pre-training data and compute. On optical flow (FlyingChairs), the model achieves 3.08 EPE with test-time ensembling, competitive with specialized methods like FlowNetC (2.19 EPE). On amodal segmentation, the model reaches 82.9 mIOU on COCO-A and 63.9 mIOU on MP3D, matching or surpassing pix2gestalt (82.9 / 61.5) while training only on Pix2Gestalt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Marigold (the primary diffusion-based depth baseline), the paper's model wins decisively on ETH3D (AbsRel 4.8 vs 6.5, δ1 97.8 vs 96.0) and essentially ties on Hypersim (13.6 vs 13.5 AbsRel) and DIODE (31.0 vs 30.8 AbsRel), while trailing on NYUv2 (6.8 vs 5.5 AbsRel) and ScanNet (7.7 vs 6.4 AbsRel). The key claim is that these results are achieved with ~3 orders of magnitude less pre-training data and compute than Marigold, which relies on internet-scale pre-trained Stable Diffusion. For optical flow, the model (3.08 EPE with ensembling) is competitive with but does not surpass specialized methods like FlowNetC (2.19 EPE) or FlowNetC+ft (2.27 EPE) on FlyingChairs, though it slightly beats DeepFlow (3.53 EPE). For amodal segmentation, the model ties pix2gestalt on COCO-A (82.9 mIOU), is marginally behind on Pix2Gestalt (88.6 vs 88.7), and outperforms it on MP3D (63.9 vs 61.5 mIOU), all while training only on the Pix2Gestalt dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate diffusion-based perception pipelines—Marigold for depth, FlowDiffuser for optical flow, and pix2gestalt for amodal segmentation—under a single DiT-based image-to-image translation framework, and systematically studies their scaling properties. It builds directly on the conditional denoising formulation from Marigold and InstructPix2Pix, the DiT backbone from Peebles & Xie, and the sparse upcycling procedure from Komatsuzaki et al., but departs from prior work by treating perception through the lens of compute-optimal scaling laws (borrowed from the LLM and generative-model scaling literature) rather than demonstrating feasibility on a single task. The key conceptual shift is showing that test-time compute (more denoising steps, ensembling, noise-schedule tuning) can substitute for 3–4 orders of magnitude of pre-training compute, a tradeoff previously explored only for autoregressive LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with concrete, compute-optimal recipes for deploying diffusion models on perception tasks under constrained budgets, and demonstrates that internet-scale pre-training is not strictly necessary for competitive depth, flow, or amodal results. However, the individual task numbers are competitive rather than dramatically superior (matching Marigold on Hypersim, modest gains on ETH3D), and the test-time compute gains come at the cost of slower inference, which limits practical adoption in latency-sensitive settings. Open questions include whether the observed power laws persist at much larger model scales (beyond ~2B parameters), whether the unified multi-task model truly generalizes to unseen perception tasks, and whether the training-vs.-test-time compute tradeoff remains favorable when inference latency is a hard constraint.
