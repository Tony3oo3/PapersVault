## Problem

Existing semi-supervised domain generalization (SSDG) methods discard unconfident-unlabeled samples—those whose model predictions fall below a confidence threshold—leaving a substantial fraction of available unlabeled data (19–51% across benchmarks) entirely unused. The paper asks whether these previously ignored samples can instead provide meaningful supervisory signals to improve domain generalization under sparse labeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252109/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images from multiple source domains in both labeled (10 or 5 per class) and unlabeled forms, processed through a ResNet18 backbone with a single-layer MLP classifier. At test time, it takes RGB images from a single unseen target domain and outputs a class label over the shared label space. The method is plug-and-play: it requires no domain labels and no architectural changes to the underlying SSL baseline (FixMatch, FreeMatch, or StyleMatch), simply adding two contrastive loss terms to the existing supervised + consistency objective.

## Method

UPCSC introduces two contrastive-learning modules built on proxy-based contrastive learning (PCL). The UPC module extends the PCL loss for confident-unlabeled samples by adding unconfident-unlabeled samples as extra negative pairs—specifically those whose excluded class set (classes with confidence ≤ 1/C) contains the anchor's pseudo label. The SC module creates a per-sample surrogate class proxy, computed as a confidence-weighted sum of the candidate class proxies (those exceeding the 1/C random-chance threshold), and uses it as the positive pair for unconfident-unlabeled samples in a separate contrastive loss. The key empirical insight motivating both modules is that unconfident samples are confused among only 2–3 classes (or ≤15% of classes for larger datasets), and ~70% of them contain their ground truth within this small candidate set, making the excluded classes reliable negatives and the candidate set a useful positive signal. Both modules use 1-layer MLP projectors on features and class proxies, and the total training objective is simply the baseline supervised + consistency loss plus the UPC and SC contrastive losses.

## Results

Under the 10-labels-per-class setting, UPCSC attached to StyleMatch achieves 81.5% on PACS, 59.9% on OfficeHome, 82.2% on DigitsDG, and 55.6% on miniDomainNet, outperforming the StyleMatch baseline by +1.6, +0.2, +3.8, and +0.6 percentage points respectively. Under the more challenging 5-labels-per-class setting, the gains are larger: FixMatch+Ours improves DigitsDG by +10.5 pp (75.2% vs 64.7%) and PACS by +5.3 pp (78.9% vs 73.6%). Across all four benchmarks and both labeling budgets, UPCSC consistently improves the host baseline, with the largest absolute gains observed on DigitsDG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPCSC is compared against two categories of baselines: (1) standalone SSL/DG methods (ERM, MeanTeacher, FreeMatch, FixMatch, StyleMatch) and (2) competing plug-and-play SSDG methods (FBCSA, DGWM). Against the standalone baselines, UPCSC attached to FixMatch or StyleMatch consistently outperforms all of them across all four benchmarks in both 10-label and 5-label settings; for example, StyleMatch+Ours reaches 82.2% on DigitsDG (10-label) versus 78.4% for StyleMatch alone. Against the plug-and-play competitor FBCSA, UPCSC wins on PACS (+2.3 pp with FixMatch, +2.2 pp with StyleMatch) and DigitsDG (+0.2 pp with FixMatch, +1.7 pp with StyleMatch) but is marginally behind on OfficeHome (−0.1 pp with FixMatch, −0.1 pp with StyleMatch). Against DGWM, UPCSC shows a clear advantage on DigitsDG (+5.2 pp with FixMatch, +3.9 pp with StyleMatch) and PACS (+0.8 pp with FixMatch, +1.1 pp with StyleMatch), though DGWM edges ahead on OfficeHome (+0.8 pp with FixMatch, −0.2 pp with StyleMatch). In the 5-label setting, UPCSC's advantage over both FBCSA and DGWM widens substantially, particularly on DigitsDG where FixMatch+Ours reaches 75.2% versus 75.5% (FBCSA) and 67.9% (DGWM), and on miniDomainNet where it achieves 52.7% versus 50.1% and 50.3% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends proxy-based contrastive learning (PCL) [27] from the fully-supervised domain generalization setting to the semi-supervised domain generalization (SSDG) setting, where only a small fraction of training data is labeled. It departs from prior SSDG methods (StyleMatch, FBCSA, DGWM) that exclusively exploit confident-unlabeled samples via pseudo-labeling, by introducing a contrastive-learning framework that also incorporates unconfident-unlabeled samples as additional negative pairs (UPC module) and as positive pairs through a confidence-weighted surrogate class proxy (SC module). The SC module generalizes the two-class virtual category idea from [4] to an arbitrary number of candidate classes, and the overall design is plug-and-play atop any SSL-based baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In practical domains like medical imaging where expert annotation is costly, the ability to extract supervisory signal from previously discarded low-confidence unlabeled samples directly improves data efficiency. The empirical observation that roughly 70% of unconfident-unlabeled samples retain their ground-truth label within a small confusing-class set provides a principled justification for the approach. Open questions include whether the gains persist with deeper backbones, larger label budgets, or more extreme domain shifts, and whether the contrastive formulation can be extended to video or multi-modal SSDG settings where the candidate-class structure may differ.
