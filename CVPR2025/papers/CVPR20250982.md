## Problem

Existing skeletal-conditioned generation methods are restricted to human skeletons, leaving open whether arbitrary skeletal structures can drive generation of diverse creatures and objects. Two key barriers prevent this: the absence of large-scale mesh-skeleton paired training data for non-human structures, and the ambiguity introduced when projecting complex 3D skeletons into 2D images due to self-occlusion and overlapping joints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250982/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary 3D skeleton (sparse joints and bones, not limited to human topology) and a text prompt as input. The skeleton is projected into multi-view 2D conditional images where each joint's 3D position is encoded as a unique RGB color (Coordinate Color Encoding) and view-dependent inverse depth is stored in the alpha channel. The pipeline produces multi-view images at 256×256 (upscaled to 1024×1024 for refinement) and a textured 3D mesh, with 3D reconstruction in ~20s and texture refinement in ~60s.

## Method

The core pipeline builds on MVDream with a ControlNet-style side network, enhanced by two key components. First, Coordinate Color Encoding (CCE-D) maps each 3D joint to a unique color in a normalized [0,1]³ cube and encodes relative depth in the alpha channel, preserving spatial information lost in naive 2D projection. Second, a Skeletal Correlation Module (SCM) replaces standard convolutional condition encoding with a self-attention layer for intra-view anatomical correlation and a cross-attention layer for inter-view joint correspondence, fused with camera pose via adaptive layer normalization, achieving 5× faster convergence. For 3D output, InstantMesh reconstructs a textured mesh from the generated views, followed by a UV-space refinement stage that upsamples images 4× via ControlNet-Tile and optimizes a learnable texture via differentiable rendering with a regularization term to prevent artifacts in uncovered regions. The training data (Objaverse-SK, 24k pairs) is generated by a mesh skeletonization pipeline combining Mean Curvature Flow, Douglas-Peucker curve simplification, and graph-to-tree conversion, achieving 80% success rate versus ~15% for the prior learning-based RigNet.

## Results

On the Objaverse-SK evaluation set (108 skeleton instances across animals, humans, and plants), the full method (Ours-SCM) achieves a mean SKA score of 80.43, substantially outperforming SDEdit (70.13) and SDEdit+COSAG (72.11). The method also leads on PickScore win rate (33.28 vs. 19.56 for SDEdit) and CLIP score (29.83 vs. 29.04). For mesh skeletonization, the proposed pipeline achieves an 80% success rate compared to RigNet's ~15%, and the SCM module accelerates training convergence by 5×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two generation baselines (SDEdit and SDEdit+COSAG) and one skeletonization baseline (RigNet). On SKA alignment, Ours-SCM (80.43) beats SDEdit (70.13) by 10.3 points and SDEdit+COSAG (72.11) by 8.3 points on mean instance score; the gap is largest on plants (53.53 vs. 50.99/53.46) and humans (78.45 vs. 64.70/67.60). On PickScore win rate, Ours-SCM (33.28) outperforms SDEdit (19.56) by 13.7 points, while CLIP scores are more competitive (29.83 vs. 29.04/28.99), suggesting the method primarily improves structural alignment rather than text-image fidelity. For skeletonization, the proposed pipeline achieves 80% success rate versus RigNet's ~15%, with better joint/bone alignment and support for arbitrary poses. The ablation shows SCM contributes ~5.7 SKA points over raw convolutional encoding, and CCE-D adds another ~1.5 points over binary skeleton masks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet paradigm for spatial condition injection in diffusion models from human-specific skeletons to arbitrary skeletal structures, building on MVDream for multi-view generation and InstantMesh for 3D reconstruction. The mesh skeletonization pipeline departs from prior learning-based approaches (RigNet) by combining classical geometric tools—Mean Curvature Flow for curve skeleton extraction and Douglas-Peucker simplification—into a more robust pipeline that produces sparse, animation-friendly skeletons. The Coordinate Color Encoding and Skeletal Correlation Module are novel contributions that address the specific challenge of representing 3D skeletal geometry in 2D conditional images without the self-occlusion ambiguities that plague naive projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate controllable generation from arbitrary (non-human) skeletons, opening the door to skeletal animation pipelines for animals, plants, and general objects. The Objaverse-SK dataset (24k mesh-skeleton pairs) is a practical resource that was previously unavailable at scale. Key limitations remain: the method only handles tree-structured skeletons (excluding non-tree topologies), operates through 2D projection which inherently loses 3D information, and lacks fine-grained semantic labels on skeleton parts. Future directions include native 3D generation with skeleton conditions and learning arbitrary skeleton estimation from images.
