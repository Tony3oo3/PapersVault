## Problem

Existing 3D gaze estimation methods rely on dense pixel-region features that are sensitive to local noise (light spots, blurs) and incur high computational cost. Additionally, the entangled representation between gaze directions and the underlying eyeball model—where one eyeball geometry can correspond to many gaze directions—increases learning difficulty and limits generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250060/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short sequence of 4 consecutive infrared near-eye frames (temporally downsampled from 25 Hz to 6.25 Hz) and produces a fully parameterized 3D eyeball model (center, radius, per-frame rotation), a normalized 3D gaze vector, and 2D semantic segmentation maps for iris and pupil. The 3D model is decomposed into invariant parameters (eyeball radius, iris radii, translation) shared across the sequence and variable parameters (pupil radii, rotation) that change per frame, enabling cross-frame consistency.

## Method

De²Gaze introduces two coupled innovations: (1) a Deformable Sparse Attention (DSA) mechanism that generates reference sampling points by projecting the edges of a learnable 3D eyeball model (sphere + ellipse pupil/iris) onto the 2D image plane, then applies learnable offsets and bilinear interpolation to sample only a sparse set of features, avoiding dense-region noise; and (2) a Spatial Decoupling Network (SDNet) with a dual-branch decoder that separates invariant features (eyeball radius, center) from variable features (gaze, pupil/iris position) into distinct query groups, each attending to its own level of the encoder feature map. The two branches are jointly initialized with matched position vectors from the 3D projection and iteratively refined over 3 decoder steps, where each step updates the 3D model parameters and regenerates sampling points. Training uses a combined loss of edge-point projection error, gaze vector MSE, and 2D eyeball-center distance, optimized end-to-end with the LAMB optimizer.

## Results

On TEyeD-subset_A with full supervision (Sem.+Gaze+Cent.), De²Gaze achieves 0.96° 3D gaze error, 7.6° 2D gaze error, 93.4% semantic IoU, and 1.52 px eye center error, outperforming the previous best (Nikola et al.) on all four metrics. Under gaze-only supervision, De²Gaze reaches 0.54° 3D gaze error, a 48% reduction over the prior best of 1.04°. On the cross-subject subset B, De²Gaze achieves 3.36° 3D gaze error and 88.7% semantic IoU, again surpassing Nikola et al. (4.66°, 85.7%). The model uses only 14.48M parameters and 13.56G FLOPs (ResNet-18 backbone), roughly half the cost of the ResNet-50-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

De²Gaze outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric under both within-subject (A) and cross-subject (B) evaluation. Against the strongest prior method, Nikola et al. [12] (ResNet-50, 28.56M params), De²Gaze reduces 3D gaze error from 1.21° to 0.96° on subset A and from 4.66° to 3.36° on subset B, while also improving semantic IoU by 2.0 and 3.0 percentage points respectively. Under gaze-only supervision, De²Gaze achieves 0.54° 3D gaze error versus Nikola et al.'s 1.04°, a 48% relative reduction. Compared to the lighter baselines (TEyeD, NVGaze, Transformer-based, QueryDETR), De²Gaze improves 3D gaze error by 1.34–3.11° on subset A. Notably, De²Gaze achieves these gains with roughly half the parameters (14.48M vs. 28.56M) and FLOPs (13.56G vs. 26.44G) of the ResNet-50 baselines, and converges faster than both query-based (QueryDETR) and dense-prediction (TEyeD) methods as shown in the training curves.

## Relation to Prior Work

De²Gaze adapts the query-based encoder-decoder paradigm from DETR and the deformable attention mechanism from Deformable DeTR to the 3D gaze estimation domain, but departs from both in a key way: rather than using randomly initialized or learned 3D reference points (as in DETR3D), it generates sampling positions by projecting a differentiable 3D eyeball model onto the 2D image plane, grounding attention in explicit geometric priors. The learnable 3D eyeball model and differentiable 3D-to-2D projection are inherited from Nikola et al. (2023), but the critical departure is the spatial decoupling network, which splits the regression target into invariant (eyeball radius, center) and variable (gaze, pupil, iris) branches—a structural choice absent from prior model-based gaze methods that treat the eyeball as an undifferentiated whole.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of invariant and variable features addresses a fundamental identifiability problem in 3D gaze estimation: many eyeball configurations produce identical 2D appearances, and entangled representations make optimization harder. The 48% gaze-error reduction over the prior best method and the demonstrated convergence-speed advantage over both dense and standard query-based approaches suggest that geometrically grounded sparse attention is a more efficient inductive bias than dense feature extraction for this task. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (TEyeD) and a single backbone (ResNet-18), the kappa angle is not modeled, and cross-subject performance degrades substantially, leaving open whether the decoupling strategy generalizes to other 3D reconstruction problems or to more diverse imaging conditions.
