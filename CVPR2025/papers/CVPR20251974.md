## Problem

Existing weakly supervised affordance grounding methods treat affordance regions as isolated and rely on class activation maps for localization, ignoring the many-to-many relationships between different object components (e.g., a cup's handle depends on its body for pouring). Traditional graph neural networks capture only point-to-point connections, which is insufficient for the region-to-region relationships that affordance grounding requires.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251974/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes three exocentric human-object interaction images and one egocentric object image (resized to 256×256, randomly cropped to 224×224) with only image-level affordance labels as supervision. At inference, only a single egocentric image and an affordance class label are provided, and the model outputs a per-class affordance heatmap (localization map) over the egocentric image. The DINO-ViT-S backbone is frozen throughout training, so all learnable capacity resides in the hypergraph and state-space modules.

## Method

R-Mamba constructs hypergraphs from DINO-ViT feature maps where each spatial feature vector is a vertex and α-space neighborhoods (distance threshold ε) form hyperedges, enabling many-to-many region relationship modeling via hypergraph convolution. A three-stage hypergraph evolution module then refines the structure: K-means clustering adds global hyperedges, egocentric features guide selection of affordance-relevant vertices and hyperedges in the exocentric graph, and Gumbel Softmax prunes redundant hyperedges. The evolved vertex and hyperedge features are fed into Ego-HSS and Exo-HSS blocks, which use SS2D (bidirectional Mamba scanning) to process vertex and hyperedge sequences in parallel, reorganizing local component relationships from a global perspective. Training combines cross-entropy classification, cosine similarity loss to align exocentric and egocentric embeddings, and a geometric concentration loss to focus predictions on object components.

## Results

On AGD20K-Seen, R-Mamba achieves KLD 1.173, SIM 0.414, and NSS 1.247, outperforming LOCATE (KLD 1.226, SIM 0.401, NSS 1.177) and Cross-view-AG+ (KLD 1.489, SIM 0.342, NSS 0.981). On AGD20K-Unseen, R-Mamba reaches KLD 1.372, SIM 0.380, NSS 1.190, while on HICO-IIF it achieves KLD 1.510, SIM 0.346, NSS 0.998. When integrated into the WSMA multimodal framework (WSMA+Ours), the combined method attains the best scores across all three datasets, e.g., KLD 1.143 / SIM 0.420 / NSS 1.251 on AGD20K-Seen and KLD 1.422 / SIM 0.361 / NSS 1.030 on HICO-IIF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-Mamba outperforms all prior WSAG baselines—Hotspots, Cross-view-AG, Cross-view-AG+, and LOCATE—on every metric across all three datasets. Against LOCATE (the strongest prior WSAG method), R-Mamba improves KLD by 0.053 (1.173 vs 1.226) and NSS by 0.070 (1.247 vs 1.177) on AGD20K-Seen, and by 0.083 KLD (1.510 vs 1.593) on HICO-IIF. However, R-Mamba is slightly behind WSMA [40] on AGD20K-Unseen (KLD 1.372 vs 1.335; NSS 1.190 vs 1.220) and HICO-IIF (KLD 1.510 vs 1.465; NSS 0.998 vs 1.012), indicating WSMA's multimodal design has a marginal edge in those settings. The paper demonstrates that R-Mamba is complementary to WSMA: when the HSS block is plugged into WSMA (WSMA+Ours), the combined system achieves the best scores on all datasets, e.g., KLD 1.310 and NSS 1.279 on AGD20K-Unseen, surpassing both standalone methods. Against weakly supervised object localization baselines (EIL, SPA, TS-CAM), R-Mamba shows substantially larger margins, with KLD improvements of over 0.6 on AGD20K-Seen.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of hypergraph neural networks (building on HGNN's vertex-hyperedge-vertex convolution) and state space models (building on Mamba's selective S6 scan and V-Mamba's SS2D bidirectional scanning), applying both to the weakly supervised affordance grounding task. It departs from prior WSAG methods (Cross-view-AG, LOCATE, WSMA) that rely on CAM-based localization by explicitly modeling many-to-many region-to-region relationships via hyperedges rather than treating affordance regions as isolated. The key conceptual departure is from pairwise GNNs to hypergraphs for capturing higher-order component interactions, and from standard image-patch Mamba scanning to hypergraph-structured scanning where vertices and hyperedges are processed in parallel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a genuine limitation in WSAG—prior methods ignore the compositional nature of affordances (e.g., a handle only affords pouring in conjunction with a cup body)—and the hypergraph formulation is a natural fit for this many-to-many structure. However, the performance gains over the strongest baseline (WSMA) are modest (e.g., KLD 1.176→1.143 on AGD20K-Seen), and the paper itself acknowledges a visible gap between predicted and ground-truth heatmaps. Open questions include whether the K-means-based hyperedge construction and Gumbel Softmax pruning generalize beyond the 224×224 resolution and 36 affordance classes tested, and whether the hypergraph-Mamba combination scales to more complex multi-object interaction scenes.
