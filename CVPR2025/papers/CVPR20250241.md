## Problem

Zero-shot multi-label recognition with VLMs like CLIP is hampered by image-level and prompt-level score biases, and by the OR-like behavior of compound prompts that inflates maximum scores when only one object is present. Existing solutions require prompt tuning, architectural surgery, or training data, limiting their applicability as truly zero-shot, model-agnostic methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250241/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed at 224×224 for ViT backbones or dataset-specific resolutions for ResNet backbones) and a set of class names as input. From the class names, it generates compound prompts (formulaic pairs/triplets plus LLM-rewritten sentences, averaging ≤20 per class) using only rough co-occurrence statistics from training labels. The VLM is queried as a black box for both singleton and compound prompt scores, and the output is a single refined score per class per image, suitable for mAP-based ranking.

## Method

SPARC has three components operating purely on VLM scores. First, compound prompts are generated by thresholding class co-occurrence probabilities to select plausible pairs and triplets, then rewriting them into natural sentences via an LLM. Second, scores are normalized in two stages: image-level standardization (subtracting mean and dividing by SD across all prompts for each image) removes image-specific bias, and prompt-level standardization (across images for each prompt) removes prompt-specific bias. Third, Rank Fusion combines the normalized singleton score with order statistics of compound scores (k-th highest compound prompt mentioning class i) by computing the max-variance direction via PCA across all images, yielding a weighted sum that adaptively emphasizes the most discriminative order statistic. The key insight is that the maximum compound score is a poor signal due to OR-like VLM behavior, while lower order statistics (especially 2nd-highest) provide better positive/negative separation, and the PCA-based fusion automatically selects the optimal weighting.

## Results

SPARC achieves mean Average Precision (mAP) of 68.3 on COCO, 89.2 on VOC, and 47.2 on NUSWIDE (averaged across 9 CLIP backbones), improving over the vanilla ZSCLIP baseline by 12.6%, 8.8%, and 7.9% respectively. The method shows consistent gains across all 9 backbones, with per-backbone improvements ranging from 11.4–15.0% on COCO, 7.4–10% on VOC, and 6.1–10.8% on NUSWIDE. When combined with local-feature methods (CLIP-DPT), SPARC further boosts mAP by 5–9 points, and when plugged into training-based methods (TaI-DPT, TagCLIP), it adds 1.6–1.7% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline ZSCLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021), SPARC wins decisively on all three datasets: 68.3 vs. 55.7 mAP on COCO (+12.6), 89.2 vs. 80.4 on VOC (+8.8), and 47.2 vs. 39.3 on NUSWIDE (+7.9), with consistent gains across all 9 CLIP backbones. Against TaI-DPT (Guo et al., CVPR 2023), SPARC improves mAP by an average of 1.7% (e.g., 68.2 vs. 65.1 on COCO/RN50), and when applied to the CLIP-DPT variant it adds 5–9 points (e.g., 77.8 vs. 70.5 on COCO/RN50x64). Against TagCLIP (Lin et al., AAAI 2024), SPARC adds 1.6% on average (73.8 vs. 70.9 on COCO/ViT-B/16; 92.0 vs. 91.7 on VOC). Against CoMC (Liu et al., ICML 2024), SPARC slightly degrades performance by 0.3% (69.7 vs. 70.0 average on COCO/RN50), suggesting CoMC's trained signal already captures much of the complementary information SPARC provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a distinct niche in the VLM-based multi-label recognition literature by insisting on a purely black-box, training-free, zero-shot formulation. It departs from supervised prompt-tuning methods (DualCoOp, TaI-DPT) that require annotated data, from training-free architectural modifications (CLIP-Surgery, TagCLIP) that require internal model access, and from single-label prompt-enrichment approaches that do not model inter-class structure. Conceptually, it repurposes the VLM's compound-prompt scoring behavior—previously treated as a nuisance (OR-like inflation)—as a signal source, and introduces a statistical noise model (OR + AND-bonus) to formalize why and how that signal can be extracted via order statistics and PCA-based fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SPARC means it can be layered on top of any existing VLM-based MLR pipeline (demonstrated with TagCLIP, TaI-DPT, CoMC) without retraining or architectural surgery, making it immediately deployable. The OR+AND-bonus noise model provides a reusable diagnostic framework for understanding VLM scoring under compound prompts, which could inform prompt design beyond MLR. Limitations include reliance on CLIP-family backbones, use of training-set co-occurrence statistics for prompt selection (albeit only for filtering), and evaluation confined to three standard benchmarks; whether the weakened-max phenomenon and the noise model structure persist in newer, larger VLMs (e.g., those with stronger compositional reasoning) remains an open question.
