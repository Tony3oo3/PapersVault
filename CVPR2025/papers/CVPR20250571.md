## Problem

Existing 4D driving scene representation methods (NeRF, 3DGS) can only render faithfully under viewpoints closely matching their training distribution—primarily forward-driving—failing on complex maneuvers like lane changes and acceleration. While autonomous-driving world models can generate diverse video viewpoints, they produce only 2D outputs lacking the spatiotemporal coherence needed for 3D/4D reconstruction. No prior work bridges this gap by leveraging world model priors to densify 4D scene training data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 40-frame forward-facing camera clips at 640×960 resolution, LiDAR point clouds, ego-vehicle trajectories, and structured scene annotations (3D bounding boxes, HDMap). The world model is conditioned on the first frame image plus structured information projected from the novel trajectory to synthesize temporally aligned video sequences. The output is a trained 4DGS model capable of rendering novel trajectory viewpoints (lane change, acceleration, deceleration) in real time, evaluated via NTA-IoU, NTL-IoU, and FID metrics.

## Method

DriveDreamer4D uses a driving world model (DriveDreamer) as a generative data engine: the Novel Trajectory Generation Module (NTGM) proposes safe novel trajectories by offsetting the ego-vehicle path in the ego coordinate frame with iterative safety checks against drivable areas and other agents. The world model then synthesizes novel trajectory videos conditioned on the first frame and structured priors (projected 3D boxes, HDMap) from the new trajectory, ensuring spatiotemporal consistency of traffic elements. The Cousin Data Training Strategy (CDTS) stacks temporal-aligned real and synthetic frames into a single training batch for 4DGS optimization, omitting depth supervision on synthetic data (since LiDAR is only available for the original trajectory) and adding a perception-feature regularization loss that enforces coherence between rendered real and novel views. The overall loss combines RGB/depth/SSIM on original data, RGB/SSIM on novel data, and the regularization term, with λ_novel=1 and λ_reg=1e-3 yielding optimal performance.

## Results

On the Waymo dataset, DriveDreamer4D achieves average NTA-IoU of 0.428, 0.475, and 0.371 when combined with PVG, S³ Gaussian, and Deformable-GS respectively, representing relative improvements of 22.6%, 43.5%, and 15.6% over the corresponding baselines. For FID on lane change scenarios, DriveDreamer4D reduces scores from 105.29 to 71.52 (PVG), 124.90 to 66.93 (S³ Gaussian), and 92.34 to 77.32 (Deformable-GS), yielding relative improvements of 32.1%, 46.4%, and 16.3%. A user study confirms an average win rate exceeding 80% across all three baselines, with the highest being 96.7% against S³ Gaussian.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveDreamer4D consistently outperforms all three 4DGS baselines (PVG, S³ Gaussian, Deformable-GS) on both NTA-IoU and NTL-IoU across all three novel trajectory scenarios (lane change, acceleration, deceleration) on Waymo. The largest relative gain is against S³ Gaussian, where NTA-IoU improves by 43.5% (0.331→0.475) and FID drops by 46.4% (124.90→66.93), indicating S³ Gaussian is most sensitive to the data distribution shift in novel trajectories. Against PVG, the FID improvement is 32.1% (105.29→71.52) and NTA-IoU gains 22.6%; against Deformable-GS, FID improves 16.3% and NTA-IoU gains 15.6%. On nuScenes, DriveDreamer4D with PVG achieves a 65.7% NTA-IoU increase (0.204→0.338) and 14.7% FID reduction (103.92→88.60) over PVG alone. On PandaSet, DriveDreamer4D with PVG achieves the best FID of 74.6 under a 3-meter lane shift, outperforming NeuRAD (93.9) by 20.6% and UniSim (97.5) by 23.5%, while also surpassing both on the 2-meter lane shift (62.4 vs. 72.3 and 74.7) and the 1-meter vertical shift (69.8 vs. 76.3 and 82.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveDreamer4D sits at the intersection of two research threads: 4D driving scene reconstruction via Gaussian Splatting (PVG, S³ Gaussian, Deformable-GS) and autonomous-driving world models for video generation (DriveDreamer-2, Vista, GAIA-1). Prior work using generative priors for 3D scene reconstruction (SGD, GGS, MagicDrive3D) addressed only sparse viewpoints or static backgrounds, whereas this paper is the first to deploy a controllable video diffusion world model as a 'data machine' that synthesizes temporally coherent novel-trajectory videos conditioned on structured traffic information (3D boxes, HDMap). The Cousin Data Training Strategy and the Novel Trajectory Generation Module are new architectural components that bridge the world model output to 4DGS optimization, departing from prior diffusion-prior approaches that used score distillation or single-image inpainting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck in closed-loop autonomous driving simulation: 4DGS methods degrade sharply under maneuvers (lane change, acceleration) because training data is dominated by forward-driving trajectories. By showing that world-model-generated videos can be merged with real data to substantially improve novel-trajectory rendering (32–46% FID reduction, 22–44% NTA-IoU gain across three backbones and three datasets), the paper offers a modular, plug-in recipe that any 4DGS pipeline can adopt. Open questions include scalability beyond 40-frame clips and single forward cameras, the sensitivity to world-model hallucinations in rare traffic configurations, and whether the Cousin Data Training Strategy generalizes to multi-sensor or surround-view settings. The reliance on a specific world model (DriveDreamer-2) also raises questions about how much of the gain comes from the generative prior versus the structured conditioning.
