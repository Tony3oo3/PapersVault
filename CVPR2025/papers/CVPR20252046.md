## Problem

Pre-trained CLIP models exhibit a persistent modality gap where image and text features form separate clusters, limiting zero-shot transfer performance. Existing remedies either require expensive pre-training from scratch on million-scale datasets or fine-tuning that degrades general zero-shot capability. The paper identifies a missing middle ground: a lightweight post-pre-training phase that reduces the modality gap without catastrophic forgetting or uniformity collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252046/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained CLIP model (e.g., ViT-B/32, ViT-H/14, SigLIP) and a small image-text pair dataset (as few as 8K Flickr8K pairs, default 118K COCO Captions) and produces updated vision and text encoder parameters after a single epoch of training on one GPU with batch size 512 and learning rate 1e-6. The pre-trained model is kept as a frozen teacher throughout training, and the output is a post-pre-trained CLIP model whose image and text features are more tightly aligned while retaining the original feature-space structure.

## Method

CLIP-Refine combines two losses. RaFA (Random Feature Alignment) avoids directly minimizing the L2 gap between paired image-text features—which destroys hypersphere uniformity—by instead pulling each feature vector toward a shared random reference vector sampled from a prior (default N(0,I)), thereby matching the two modalities' distributions to a common target while preserving uniformity. HyCD (Hybrid Contrastive-Distillation) is a KL-divergence distillation loss where the frozen pre-trained CLIP acts as teacher, but the teacher's soft similarity matrix is alpha-blended (α=0.5) with the ground-truth identity matrix for matched pairs, allowing the student to learn new alignment signal without being rigidly chained to the pre-trained outputs. The two losses are summed with equal weight, and the combined objective is optimized for one epoch, reducing the modality gap from 1.33×10⁻³ to 0.79×10⁻³ while simultaneously improving uniformity from 0.0895 to 0.0495.

## Results

On zero-shot classification across 12 datasets with CLIP ViT-B/32, CLIP-Refine achieves 54.69% average top-1 accuracy, improving over the pre-trained baseline (52.74%) by +1.95 points and substantially outperforming contrastive post-pre-training (45.75%) and m²-mix (46.48%). On zero-shot retrieval (Flickr30K), CLIP-Refine reaches R@5 of 49.70 (T→I) and 51.54 (I→T), compared to 40.96/44.57 for the pre-trained model. The method also improves feature space quality: modality gap drops from 1.3345×10⁻³ to 0.7934×10⁻³ while uniformity improves from 0.0895 to 0.0495.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP-Refine outperforms all post-pre-training baselines on zero-shot classification. The contrastive loss baseline (45.75% avg) and m²-mix [36] (46.48% avg) both suffer severe catastrophic forgetting, dropping 6–7 points below the pre-trained model (52.74%), due to overfitting with small batch sizes (512 vs. 32,768 at pre-training). Self-KD (52.94%) and HyCD alone (53.13%) preserve knowledge but provide only marginal gains over the pre-trained model. The combination of HyCD with direct alignment loss (HyCD+L_align, 45.61%) is the worst baseline, confirming that naively minimizing the image-text feature gap destroys uniformity. CLIP-Refine (54.69%) achieves the best result by +1.95 points over pre-trained and +8.21 points over contrastive, with consistent improvements across all 12 datasets. On retrieval (Flickr30K), CLIP-Refine leads by +8.74 R@5 (T→I) and +6.97 R@5 (I→T) over the pre-trained model, while contrastive post-pre-training actually degrades I→T retrieval (36.29 vs. 44.57), highlighting that the image encoder is more prone to overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself between two well-studied regimes for CLIP: pre-training modifications (auxiliary losses, shared encoders requiring million-scale datasets) and fine-tuning (prompt tuning, adapters that degrade zero-shot transfer). It introduces 'post-pre-training' as a distinct training phase, drawing on random feature regularization from single-modal fine-tuning (Yamaguchi et al., 2024) for the RaFA loss and on CLIP-KD-style knowledge distillation (Yang et al., 2024) for the HyCD loss. The key conceptual departure is that instead of directly minimizing the image-text feature gap (which Wang & Isola's alignment-uniformity analysis predicts destroys uniformity), the method aligns both modalities to a shared random prior, and instead of pure self-distillation (which over-constrains the student), it blends teacher outputs with ground-truth pair labels to inject new alignment knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe—single GPU, one epoch, ~118K COCO Caption pairs—makes modality-gap mitigation accessible to labs without pre-training budgets, and the consistent gains across six architectures (ViT-B/32 through ViT-G/14, SigLIP, DFN) suggest broad applicability. A notable open question is the data-quality dependence: unfiltered CC12M (12M pairs) underperforms COCO Caption (118K pairs), while CLIP-Score-filtered CC12M yields the largest gains, implying that pairing this method with scalable data curation could unlock further improvements. The paper also leaves open whether post-pre-training composes cleanly with downstream prompt tuning or adapter fine-tuning, and whether the theoretical lower bound on the modality gap (Qian et al., 2024) can be approached more tightly with richer priors or multi-step refinement.
