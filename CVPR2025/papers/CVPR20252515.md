## Problem

Existing post-calibration methods for long-tailed object detection adjust logits using only class frequency statistics, ignoring how classes are spatially distributed across image locations. This overlooks the class-location dependence (e.g., hats and tiaras both cluster at the top of images), which is a critical indicator for balancing rare-class detection. The paper asks whether incorporating spatial distribution information into post-hoc calibration can further improve rare-category performance without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained detector's raw class logits at inference time and a set of precomputed per-class fractal dimensions (stored in memory, computed once from the training set via box-counting). It outputs renormalised class probabilities that replace the original predictions before non-maximum suppression, yielding calibrated bounding boxes and segmentation masks. Because the fractal dimensions are precomputed constants, the inference overhead is negligible and no forward pass over the training set is needed.

## Method

FRACAL combines two calibration stages applied to the detector's logits. First, a classification calibration (C) subtracts log(p_s(y)) and adds log(1/C) to each foreground logit, upweighting rare classes based on training-set frequency. Second, a space calibration (S) divides each class's softmax probability by Φ(y)^λ, where Φ(y) is the fractal dimension estimated via box-counting with a quadratic-rule threshold G ≤ ⌊√n_y⌋ to avoid sparsity for rare classes; this downweights classes whose objects are uniformly spread across image space and upweights those with concentrated spatial distributions. The final output F(z_y) = S(C(z_y)) / Σ_j S(C(z_j)) renormalises all foreground and background logits to preserve a valid probability distribution. For binary (sigmoid) detectors, a sigmoid filter η(z_i) gates the calibration to decouple foreground from background scoring. The entire procedure is training-free and can be plugged into any one-stage or two-stage detector.

## Results

On LVISv1 with Mask R-CNN ResNet50, FRACAL achieves 28.6% APm and 23.0% APr, surpassing the previous best (GOL) by 0.9pp and 1.6pp respectively. With ResNet101, it reaches 29.8% APm and 24.5% APr. On Swin-S, FRACAL delivers an 8.6% relative improvement in rare-class mask AP (27.8% vs. 25.6% for Seesaw), and on Swin-B it boosts APr by 6.6pp over the baseline. FRACAL also generalises to COCO (+0.5pp avg), V3Det (+0.4pp APb), and OpenImages (+1.7–2.0pp APb50) without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRACAL outperforms all 12 compared baselines on LVISv1 across both ResNet50 and ResNet101. Against the closest training-based competitor GOL [2], FRACAL gains +0.9pp APm and +1.6pp APr (ResNet50) and +0.8pp APm and +1.7pp APr (ResNet101). Against the prior post-calibration method NorCal [63], the improvement is substantially larger: +3.4pp APm and +3.7pp APr, because FRACAL adds spatial calibration that NorCal lacks. LogN [97] is the strongest post-hoc baseline (27.5/29.0 APm), but FRACAL still leads by +1.1pp (ResNet50) and +0.8pp (ResNet101) in APm while also improving APr by +1.2pp and +1.6pp respectively. On Swin-S, FRACAL beats Seesaw [76] by +1.2pp APm and +2.2pp APr (8.6% relative rare-class gain), and on Swin-B it improves APr by 6.6pp over the uncalibrated baseline. On less-imbalanced datasets (COCO, V3Det, OpenImages), FRACAL still yields consistent small gains (+0.4 to +2.0pp) over baselines like APA [4], CAS [54], and ECM [32], confirming generalisability beyond heavily long-tailed settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRACAL extends the Post-Calibrated Softmax Adjustment (PCSA) framework from long-tailed image classification [Hong et al., CVPR 2021; Menon et al., ICLR 2021] to object detection, where the class prior must account for the joint class-location distribution p_s(y,u) rather than just p_s(y). It directly builds on NorCal [Pan et al., NeurIPS 2021], the first post-calibration method for imbalanced detection, and on the class-prior factorization from GOL [Alexandridis et al., ECCV 2022], but departs from both by introducing spatial information via the fractal dimension—a grid-size-independent measure of how uniformly a class's instances are distributed across image space. All prior post-calibration methods (LA, IIF, PCSA, NorCal, LogN) use only frequency statistics; FRACAL is the first to fuse frequency and space information in a single calibration step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that FRACAL requires no retraining and can be bolted onto any off-the-shelf detector (one-stage sigmoid, two-stage softmax, instance segmentation), making it immediately deployable in safety-critical applications where rare-class detection is paramount. The 8.6% relative improvement on rare-class AP with Swin-S, achieved purely at inference, is substantial and consistent across ResNet, Swin, and multiple detector architectures. A notable limitation is that sigmoid-based detectors show a small trade-off in frequent-class performance, and the method's reliance on precomputed fractal dimensions means it is dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the fractal-dimension formulation generalizes to 3D detection or video, and whether the weak frequency–fractal correlation (r≈0.35) holds in other domains.
