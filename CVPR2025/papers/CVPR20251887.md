## Problem

Existing 3D native diffusion models suffer from three interrelated limitations: their auto-encoders lack perceptual supervision and cannot capture high-frequency geometric details; they generate overly simple, symmetric shapes that diverge from reference images due to training on the limited Objaverse dataset; and their texture pipelines produce light-baked appearances rather than physically based rendering (PBR) materials consistent with the input image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251887/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single RGB image as input and produces a watertight 3D mesh with a full PBR texture set (albedo, metallic, roughness) in UV space, completing generation in approximately 30 seconds. The reference image is encoded via DINOv2 features and injected through cross-attention into the diffusion UNet, while the mesh geometry is represented as a triplane latent that is decoded through a convolutional upsampler and MLP occupancy network followed by differentiable marching cubes.

## Method

MeshGen introduces a render-enhanced point-to-shape auto-encoder that uses a coarse-to-fine training strategy: first learning occupancy with BCE and KL losses, then refining with render-based LPIPS/MSE normal losses plus a ray-based regularization that forces empty-space occupancy toward zero to prevent floaters. For the image-to-shape diffusion model, two data augmentations exploit the geometric covariance of the point-to-shape encoder and the appearance-invariance of shape generation: geometric alignment rotates point clouds to match selected image views, while generative rendering augmentation uses ControlNet and IC-Light to synthesize diverse lighting and texture conditions from normal/depth maps. The texturing stage employs a geometry-conditioned ControlNet with reference attention fine-tuning to generate multi-view shaded images, an InstructPix2Pix-based PBR decomposer to estimate albedo/metallic/roughness channels, and a UV-space inpainting ControlNet (trained with LoRA on Objaverse textures) to fill regions invisible in the generated views.

## Results

On GSO, MeshGen achieves an F-Score of 0.971±0.014 and Chamfer Distance of 0.028±0.005, outperforming the best large reconstruction model (MeshFormer: FS 0.963, CD 0.031) and the best native 3D diffusion model (CraftsMan: FS 0.702, CD 0.088). On OmniObject3D, MeshGen reaches FS 0.918±0.010 and CD 0.040±0.004, again leading all baselines. In the texture user study (52 raters), MeshGen wins 92.31% for image alignment and 82.69% for overall quality, far exceeding the next-best method (EASI-Tex at 3.85% and 7.69% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against large reconstruction models, MeshGen outperforms MeshFormer (FS 0.963→0.971 on GSO) and MeshLRM (FS 0.956→0.971) on both F-Score and Chamfer Distance, with the gap widening on OmniObject3D where MeshFormer scores 0.914 vs. MeshGen's 0.918. Against native 3D diffusion models, the advantage is dramatic: CraftsMan (FS 0.702) and LN3Diff (FS 0.647) on GSO are far behind MeshGen's 0.971, reflecting the paper's argument that prior native methods produce overly simple, symmetric shapes. For texture generation, the user study shows MeshGen dominating all three baselines (TEXTure, EASI-Tex, Paint3D) by a wide margin—92.31% win rate on image alignment versus at most 3.85% for any competitor—indicating that the reference-attention ControlNet plus PBR decomposer pipeline produces substantially more consistent and higher-quality textures than prior image-conditioned texturing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshGen builds on the 3D native diffusion paradigm established by 3DShape2VecSet, CLAY, and CraftsMan, which compress meshes into latent spaces via point-to-shape auto-encoders and train diffusion models on those latents. It departs from this line in two key ways: (1) replacing the vector-set latent with a triplane representation (following Direct3D and Rodin) to enable render-based perceptual supervision during auto-encoder training, and (2) exploiting the geometric covariance of the point-to-shape encoder to construct alignment-aware training pairs, a strategy unavailable to NeRF-based native 3D methods like Rodin and 3DTopia. For texturing, it extends the ControlNet + reference-attention framework (building on IP-Adapter and reference-only control) with a PBR decomposition stage inspired by RGBJ-X and a UV-space inpainter, positioning itself as a unified geometry-and-appearance pipeline rather than treating texturing as a separate post-hoc step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeshGen addresses a practical gap in the 3D generation pipeline: most native 3D diffusion models produce geometry that is too simple and symmetric to match reference images, and existing texturing methods produce baked-in appearances rather than relightable PBR materials. The geometric alignment and generative rendering augmentation strategies offer a data-efficient path to better controllability without requiring massive proprietary datasets, which is relevant given the limited quality of public 3D datasets like Objaverse. Open questions include whether the PBR decomposer generalizes to materials beyond the metallic-roughness-albedo model, how the pipeline handles transparent or highly reflective objects (acknowledged as a limitation), and whether the 30-second generation time scales to more complex multi-object scenes.
