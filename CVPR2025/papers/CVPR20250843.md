## Problem

Vision Transformers achieve state-of-the-art performance but demand high computational resources, and existing Mixture-of-Experts approaches to reduce inference cost require expensive training from scratch on massive datasets with additional load-balancing losses. This paper asks whether MoE variants can be extracted directly from already-pretrained ViTs post-training, without retraining or specialized MoE training procedures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250843/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images processed as ViT patch tokens (e.g., 16×16 patches for DeiT). During inference, each token is independently routed to one of several extracted experts based on cosine similarity to precomputed cluster-mean input vectors, and the expert-specific subnetwork produces a reduced-dimensionality output that feeds into a correspondingly pruned second linear layer. The final output is a standard class label over ImageNet-1k (or CIFAR-100) categories.

## Method

The proposed expert extraction is a two-phase post-training procedure applied to the MLP hidden layers of a pretrained ViT. First, HDBSCAN clusters the recorded hidden activations across multiple batches, automatically determining the number and shape of experts per layer without manual specification of k. Second, for each cluster, hidden neurons are selected by prioritizing those with the highest within-cluster variance (controlled by an extraction percentage hyperparameter), forming overlapping subnetworks whose unused neurons are removed to reduce parameter count. At inference, tokens are routed to the expert whose mean input vector (computed by mapping activation clusters back to input space) has the highest cosine similarity to the token, and the token is processed through only the extracted subset of weights. The resulting MoE variant requires only 30 epochs of fine-tuning with knowledge distillation from the original model to regain ~98% of baseline accuracy, while reducing MACs by up to 36% and parameters by up to 32%.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, the proposed MoEE method reduces MACs by 27.0%, 30.6%, and 36.3% and parameters by 20.1%, 25.0%, and 32.4% for DeiT-Tiny, DeiT-Small, and DeiT-Base respectively, while regaining 98% of the original Top-1 accuracy after 30 epochs of fine-tuning (e.g., 80.12% vs. 81.73% for DeiT-B). Before fine-tuning, the extracted experts retain 55.3%, 67.6%, and 68.5% accuracy for the three model sizes, demonstrating that the data-driven extraction preserves substantial representational capacity immediately. The method also achieves a more balanced expert routing distribution (~11% per expert at Layer 6, ~8% at Layer 11) compared to MoEfication, eliminating the need for load-balancing loss terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original DeiT baselines, MoEE achieves 98% of the original Top-1 accuracy after fine-tuning (80.12% vs. 81.73% for DeiT-B) while cutting MACs by 36.3% and parameters by 32.4%, making it the most effective compression among the three model sizes. Compared to MoEfication [29], MoEE consistently wins on final Top-1 accuracy: 69.73% vs. 68.91% (DeiT-T), 78.11% vs. 77.10% (DeiT-S), and 80.12% vs. 77.63% (DeiT-B), with the gap widening for larger models (2.5 pp for DeiT-B). MoEE also achieves a slightly larger MACs reduction (36.3% vs. 34.8% for DeiT-B) and, critically, reduces parameter count by 32.4% whereas MoEfication leaves parameters unchanged. Before fine-tuning, MoEE retains substantially more accuracy (68.54% vs. 57.20% for DeiT-B), indicating that the data-driven HDBSCAN clustering and variance-based extraction capture more relevant activation structure than MoEfication's fixed expert partitioning. The paper also notes that MoEE's routing is more balanced across experts, avoiding the load-imbalance issue that necessitates additional load-balancing terms in conventional MoE training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MoEfication (Zhang et al., Findings of ACL 2022), which demonstrated that feed-forward layers in pretrained language transformers can be decomposed into mixture-of-experts post-training, to the vision domain. The key departure is a data-driven expert configuration: instead of fixed expert counts or manually chosen subnetwork sizes, HDBSCAN clustering of hidden activations automatically determines how many experts exist per layer and which neurons belong to each, grounded in the activation statistics of the data. This positions the work between traditional MoE training (which requires load-balancing losses and training from scratch) and post-hoc pruning, occupying the niche of converting already-trained ViTs into sparse MoE variants with minimal additional compute.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: practitioners with existing pretrained ViTs can obtain 27–36% MACs and 20–32% parameter reductions with only 30 epochs of lightweight fine-tuning to recover 98% of original accuracy, all on a single T4 GPU. The data-driven expert configuration removes a key hyperparameter burden (number and size of experts) that plagues manual MoE design, and the naturally balanced routing eliminates the need for auxiliary load-balancing losses. Open questions include whether the approach transfers cleanly to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection), how it interacts with other compression techniques like structured pruning, and whether the 30-epoch fine-tuning cost is acceptable in deployment pipelines where retraining is the bottleneck being avoided.
