## Problem

Editing pattern images often requires programmatic, structure-aware modifications to the underlying generative rules, but inferring those programs via Visual Program Inference is difficult for complex patterns and yields poorly-structured, hard-to-edit code. Prior analogical editing methods focus on appearance-level changes and fail to perform structural edits on patterns. The paper asks whether programmatic edits can be expressed and executed without ever recovering the generating program.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251939/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three 2D RGB images: a simple source pattern A, its edited counterpart A′ (together forming an analogy pair that specifies which structural property to change and how), and a complex target pattern B. A need not resemble B or replicate it—it only demonstrates the edit. The output is a single RGB image B′ in which the demonstrated transformation has been applied to B while preserving B's other structural features. At inference, the model uses 50 denoising steps with classifier-free guidance weight 7.5.

## Method

TRIFUSER is a Latent Diffusion Model initialized from Versatile Diffusion's Image Variation architecture and fine-tuned on ~1 million synthetic analogical quartets generated by SPLITWEAVE, a domain-specific language with custom program samplers for Motif Tiling and Split-Filling pattern styles. Three architectural modifications address failure modes of naive image-conditioned diffusion: (1) multi-level feature fusion combines first- and last-layer encoder features via layer normalization and a linear projection to retain fine-grained spatial detail; (2) multi-encoder mixing integrates SigLIP text-image features with DiNOv2 self-supervised features through a two-layer MLP to counter semantic bias; (3) 3D positional encoding (2D spatial coordinates + 1D source-image identity) is added to each patch token so the UNet can distinguish which input image each token originates from. Training fine-tunes only the denoising UNet and projection layers on 8 A100 GPUs for ~65 epochs.

## Results

On the synthetic validation set with ground-truth analogical edits, TRIFUSER achieves the best scores across three of four perceptual metrics: DSIM 0.074, DISTS 0.184, and LPIPS 0.304, while Inpainter edges it out slightly on SSIM (0.713 vs. 0.704). In a two-alternative forced-choice perceptual study on 50 real-world Adobe Stock patterns (42 participants, 1720 judgments), TRIFUSER is preferred over Analogist 89.24% of the time, over LatentMod 80.59%, and over Inpainter 72.05%. The model generalizes to five out-of-distribution pattern styles (Memphis, geometric, digital textile, etc.) despite being trained on only two styles (SFP and MTP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRIFUSER decisively outperforms the training-free Analogist [13] on every metric (e.g., DSIM 0.074 vs. 0.496, LPIPS 0.304 vs. 0.697) and is preferred in 89.24% of perceptual judgments, as Analogist fails to interpret pattern structure due to the domain gap between its pretraining data and pattern images. Against LatentMod (a latent-arithmetic baseline), TRIFUSER wins on all four metrics and is preferred 80.59% of the time; LatentMod's embedding space lacks the low-level detail needed for programmatic edits. The closest competitor is Inpainter (a re-implementation of Image Brush [47] using Stable Diffusion inpainting on a 2×2 analogy grid): TRIFUSER beats it on DSIM (0.074 vs. 0.092), DISTS (0.184 vs. 0.256), and LPIPS (0.304 vs. 0.371), but Inpainter achieves a marginally higher SSIM (0.713 vs. 0.704), suggesting complementary strengths. In the perceptual study, TRIFUSER is still preferred over Inpainter 72.05% of the time, with the authors attributing Inpainter's lower quality to generating the edited pattern at only quarter canvas resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Versatile Diffusion's Image Variation model [54] as its architectural backbone, adapting the latent diffusion framework for a new conditioning scheme. It departs from the Visual Program Inference (VPI) line of work by explicitly rejecting program inference as a prerequisite for structural edits, instead using analogies as a more accessible interface. Among prior analogical editing methods (DIA, Analogist, Image Brush), this is the first to target programmatic, structure-aware edits rather than purely stylistic/appearance modifications, and it introduces SPLITWEAVE as a purpose-built DSL to generate the synthetic training quartets that prior methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to perform structure-aware pattern edits without reverse-engineering the generating program lowers the barrier for designers to iterate on complex patterns, and the SPLITWEAVE + synthetic quartet pipeline is a reusable recipe for training analogical editors in other structured domains. A notable strength is generalization to five unseen pattern styles, suggesting the synthetic data approach transfers well. Open questions include whether the framework extends to semi-parametric or 3D patterns (acknowledged as future work), whether the SPLITWEAVE requirement for crafting analogy pairs at test time limits usability, and whether the two-style training coverage is sufficient for broader real-world pattern diversity.
