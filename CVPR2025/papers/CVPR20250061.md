## Problem

Existing camera-control video generation methods produce videos entirely from text prompts and cannot re-angle a user-provided video that contains complex real-world scene motion. The task is inherently ill-posed since a single video does not reveal the full 4D scene, yet prior approaches either require synchronized multi-view videos, large-scale 4D training data, or accurate camera pose and depth estimation, and none can plausibly extrapolate beyond the original field of view.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250061/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single user-provided video (a sequence of N RGB frames) and a user-specified camera trajectory expressed as a sequence of extrinsic matrices (rotation + translation per frame). It produces a temporally consistent re-angled video at the same resolution (e.g., 384×256 for evaluation) that preserves the original scene motion and subject appearance while revealing viewpoints not present in the source. The intermediate anchor video carries per-frame binary validity masks marking regions that fall outside the original scene, which guide the fine-tuning loss.

## Method

ReCapture is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 generates a noisy, incomplete anchor video by either (a) per-frame monocular depth estimation (Zoedepth) followed by point-cloud re-projection under the new camera pose, or (b) per-frame multiview diffusion (CAT3D) for larger rotations; both produce artifacts, black regions, and temporal inconsistencies. Stage 2 introduces masked video fine-tuning: a temporal motion LoRA is trained on the anchor video with a masked diffusion loss that excludes invalid pixels, teaching the model the new camera dynamics while relying on the SVD prior to fill unknown regions; simultaneously, a context-aware spatial LoRA is trained on randomly sampled source frames with temporal layers bypassed, anchoring appearance and background context. At inference the combined LoRAs regenerate a clean video, and a final SDEdit pass using only the spatial LoRA removes residual blurriness. The key insight is that masking the loss to valid pixels lets the diffusion prior hallucinate plausible content for occluded or out-of-view regions without requiring any paired training data.

## Results

On the Kubric-4D dataset, ReCapture achieves 20.92 PSNR and 0.596 SSIM (all pixels), outperforming the closest baseline Generative Camera Dolly (20.30 PSNR, 0.587 SSIM) and all 4D reconstruction methods by a wide margin (e.g., HexPlane at 15.38 PSNR). On VBench, ReCapture leads on 7 of 8 semantic metrics, with particularly large gains in temporal flickering (91.12% vs. 74.64%) and motion smoothness (98.24% vs. 82.33%) over Generative Camera Dolly, while trailing only on Dynamic Degree (49.03% vs. 51.24%). Notably, ReCapture achieves these results without any 4D multi-view training data, whereas Generative Camera Dolly requires extensive paired 4D video data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCapture outperforms all six baselines on Kubric-4D across every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS for both all-pixel and occluded-pixel evaluations). The closest competitor, Generative Camera Dolly [72], achieves 20.30 PSNR vs. ReCapture's 20.92, but requires large-scale 4D multi-view video data for training, whereas ReCapture needs no paired data. Among 4D reconstruction methods, HexPlane [12] (15.38 PSNR) and 4D-GS [83] (14.92 PSNR) trail by over 5 PSNR, while DynIBaR [45] (12.86 PSNR) and Vanilla SVD [8] (13.85 PSNR) are substantially weaker. On VBench, ReCapture wins 7 of 8 semantic dimensions over Generative Camera Dolly, with the largest gaps in temporal flickering (+16.5 pp) and motion smoothness (+15.9 pp), but loses narrowly on Dynamic Degree (49.03% vs. 51.24%), suggesting the method slightly suppresses scene dynamics. ZeroNVS [66] (15.68 PSNR) is the strongest non-generative baseline but still falls 5+ PSNR short of ReCapture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Video Diffusion as its base video diffusion model and CAT3D for multiview anchor frame generation, but departs fundamentally from the 4D reconstruction lineage (NeRF-based methods, dynamic Gaussian splatting) that requires multi-view data or accurate camera poses. It also diverges from Generative Camera Dolly, which tackles the same re-angling task but requires paired 4D video data from simulation for training. The key conceptual move is reformulating camera re-angling as a video-to-video translation problem solvable via masked video fine-tuning with decoupled spatial and temporal LoRAs, leveraging the video diffusion model's generative prior instead of constructing an explicit 4D scene representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReCapture makes camera re-angling accessible for arbitrary user-provided videos without requiring paired training data or simulation, which is a significant practical advance for content creation workflows. The ability to plausibly hallucinate unseen scene regions beyond the original field of view is a capability that explicit 4D reconstruction methods fundamentally lack. Limitations include the per-video fine-tuning cost (400 steps on a single GPU), dependence on anchor video quality, and evaluation primarily on synthetic Kubric-4D scenes; generalization to longer real-world videos and more extreme camera trajectories remains an open question.
