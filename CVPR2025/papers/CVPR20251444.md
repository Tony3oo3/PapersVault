## Problem

Unsupervised facial landmark detection and head pose estimation remain challenging because existing methods struggle with large intra-class variations in pose and expression, and diffusion-based approaches often produce semantically irrelevant landmarks. Joint estimation of landmarks and head pose is largely unattended, and extreme orientations or rare ethnicities further degrade performance. This paper addresses these gaps by exploiting Stable Diffusion's cross-attention mechanisms to discover semantically meaningful landmarks and estimate 3D head pose entirely without labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251444/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled collection of RGB face images as input, along with semantic text prompts (e.g., 'nose', 'eye', 'mouth', 'eyebrow') that guide landmark localization through Stable Diffusion's cross-attention layers. A pre-trained depth network (PatchFusion) provides per-pixel depth maps used for 3D lifting of both landmarks and full image point clouds. The output consists of K=10 2D facial landmark coordinates (extracted as argmax locations from 128×128 cross-attention maps) and a 3D head pose represented as a quaternion vector (roll, pitch, yaw) predicted by a convolutional-MLP network.

## Method

The pipeline has four stages. First, semantic-guided landmark localization uses text-prompt-driven cross-attention maps (averaged over layers 7–10) to propose candidate landmarks, refines them via self-attention-based pixel grouping, and filters inconsistent landmarks through a novel cycle-consistency check that verifies a landmark's learned text embedding maps back to the same location across image pairs. Second, a lightweight transformer adapter Γ (two blocks of self-cross-self attention) enriches a unified landmark text embedding F into an image-aware embedding F₁ that encodes the holistic facial configuration, trained via alternating optimization with the SD U-Net frozen. Third, head pose is estimated by lifting landmarks to 3D via orthographic lifting with PatchFusion depth, computing pseudo-poses through the Kabsch algorithm relative to a randomly selected near-frontal reference image, and training a conv+MLP network to regress quaternions. Fourth, a rotate-and-render augmentation lifts frontal images and landmarks to 3D, applies rotation matrices (yaw ±80°, roll/pitch ±20°), and projects back to 2D to generate accurate pseudo-labels for extreme poses, combined with a pose-based batching schedule that progressively introduces harder yaw ranges during training.

## Results

On unsupervised facial landmark estimation, the method achieves the lowest Forward NME across all five datasets: 2.09% on MAFL, 2.76% on AFLW, 2.08% on LS3D, 2.69% on CatHeads, and 3.31% on 300VW, outperforming the closest diffusion-based competitor (Tourani et al. [54]) by 4.6–10.3% relative error reduction. For unsupervised head pose estimation, the method achieves 4.47° MAE on BIWI and 5.28° MAE on AFLW2000, surpassing the best self-supervised baseline (Mustikovela et al. [40]) by 33.5% and 27.0% respectively, while approaching supervised methods (e.g., Hempel et al. [13] at 3.78° on BIWI). The method also achieves a 3.9× training time speedup over the prior best unsupervised landmark method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 14 baselines on every evaluated dataset for both landmark and head pose estimation. Among non-diffusion ULD methods (Jakab [17], Zhang [68], Sanchez [47], Mallis [38], Awan [1]), the gap is substantial: e.g., on AFLW Forward NME, the best non-diffusion method (Awan [1] at 5.91%) is more than 2× worse than the proposed method (2.76%). Among diffusion-based ULD methods, the closest competitor Tourani et al. [54] is beaten by 4.6–10.3% relative NME reduction across datasets, with the largest margin on 300VW (3.31 vs. 3.69 Forward NME, a 10.3% improvement). Hedlin et al. [11] is consistently outperformed by 18–25% relative NME. For head pose estimation, the method surpasses all self-supervised baselines (Nguyen [41], Hung [15], Mustikovela [40]) by large margins — e.g., 33.5% MAE reduction over Mustikovela [40] on BIWI (4.47° vs. 6.72°) — and approaches the best supervised method (Hempel [13] at 3.78° MAE on BIWI) within 1.7°, despite requiring no pose annotations. The method also achieves a 3.9× training speedup over Tourani et al. [54], the prior best unsupervised landmark method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of work exploiting Stable Diffusion's cross-attention layers for unsupervised keypoint discovery (Hedlin et al., CVPR 2024; Tourani et al., CVPR 2024) by introducing explicit semantic guidance via text prompts (e.g., 'nose', 'eye') to localize landmarks, rather than relying solely on emergent attention patterns. It departs from prior diffusion-based landmark methods by adding a cycle-consistency filter to prune noisy landmarks, an image-aware text-embedding adapter to capture holistic facial configuration, and a mutual refinement loop where landmarks inform 3D head pose (via Kabsch algorithm on depth-lifted point clouds) and the estimated pose in turn refines landmarks through rotate-and-render augmentation and pose-based batching. The joint unsupervised landmark-and-pose formulation is largely unattended in prior literature, with only Wiles et al. (2018) attempting a self-supervised video-based approach that struggles under large pose variations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and efficient pipeline for unsupervised facial landmark and head pose estimation, achieving state-of-the-art NME across five datasets with a 3.9× training speedup over the closest diffusion-based competitor, making unsupervised landmark discovery more accessible for applications in biometrics, AR, and emotion recognition where labeled data is costly. The mutual refinement between landmarks and pose, combined with 3D rendering-based augmentation, addresses a key weakness of prior methods—degradation under extreme poses and occlusions—and the demonstrated generalization to anime, Pixar, and Arcane-style images suggests broader applicability. Open questions include the dependence on a pre-trained depth network (PatchFusion) for 3D lifting, whether the approach extends to non-facial objects or body landmarks, and how sensitive performance is to the choice of SD backbone and text prompts.
