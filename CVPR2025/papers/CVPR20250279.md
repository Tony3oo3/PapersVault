## Problem

In classifier-free guidance (CFG), the unconditional score estimates transitions between manifolds of adjacent timesteps and can become misaligned with the conditional score, causing the guided sampling trajectory to drift off the target data manifold. This misalignment manifests as physically implausible objects, overexposure bias, and degraded image quality in text-to-image generation, yet no prior method explicitly addresses the geometric cause of this interference.

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play modification to the denoising loop of pretrained diffusion or rectified-flow models (SD v1.5, SDXL, SD3, DiT). At each sampling step it consumes the two score vectors already computed by the model (unconditional and conditional) and produces a modified unconditional score that feeds back into the standard CFG combination. No retraining, architectural change, or additional forward pass is required; inference time is unchanged, and the method operates on the same latent codes and CFG scales as the base model.

## Method

At every denoising step, TCFG concatenates the unconditional score s_θ(z_t) and conditional score s_θ(z_t, y) into a 2-column score matrix A and performs SVD on it. The singular vectors associated with the largest singular values capture the shared normal direction of both scores (well-aligned components), while those with small singular values capture tangential components that are misaligned between the two scores. The unconditional score is then projected onto only the dominant singular vector v_1, effectively zeroing out its tangential component (Eq. 3). This damped unconditional score replaces the original in the CFG formula (Eq. 4), so the guided score no longer accumulates off-manifold drift over successive steps. The approach is grounded in the theoretical result that diffusion score functions encode the intrinsic dimension of the data manifold via singular-value gaps, and empirically validated on a two-moons toy problem and four real-world models.

## Results

On MS-COCO 30k, TCFG consistently reduces FID across three major text-to-image models: SDv1.5 (13.26→13.12), SDXL (13.36→12.65), and SDv3 (16.66→13.74), while maintaining identical CLIPScore. On ImageNet 50k with DiT, FID improves from 32.67 to 29.5 and sFID from 17.92 to 13.27. When combined with existing CFG enhancement methods, TCFG further reduces FID: SAG+TCFG achieves 11.48 FID (vs. 13.53 for SAG alone) and PAG+TCFG achieves 11.87 FID (vs. 14.45 for PAG alone) on MS-COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCFG is composable with all three existing CFG enhancement methods and improves FID in every case. Against SAG (FID 13.53), adding TCFG yields the largest absolute gain, reducing FID to 11.48 (−2.05), though CLIPScore drops marginally from 0.31 to 0.30. Against PAG (FID 14.45), TCFG produces the second-largest improvement, lowering FID to 11.87 (−2.58) while preserving CLIPScore at 0.31. Against CFG++ (FID 13.97), the gain is smaller (FID 13.44, −0.53) but CLIPScore is maintained at 0.32. In all three comparisons, TCFG wins on FID without sacrificing text-image alignment as measured by CLIPScore, confirming that tangential damping is orthogonal to and complementary with attention-based or manifold-constrained CFG modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCFG extends classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) by modifying the unconditional score before the standard CFG combination, rather than altering the guidance formula itself. The core geometric tool—separating normal and tangential components of the score via SVD—derives from the theoretical result that diffusion score functions encode the intrinsic dimension of data manifolds (Stanczuk et al., 2024). Unlike concurrent CFG-improvement methods (SAG, PAG, CFG++) that operate on attention maps or the guidance computation, TCFG acts directly on the score vectors, making it orthogonal and composable with those approaches, as demonstrated in Table 3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is a zero-training, plug-and-play modification applicable to any CFG-based sampler (diffusion, rectified flow, DiT), and the consistent FID gains across SD1.5, SDXL, SD3, and DiT suggest broad practical utility. However, the existence of the intermediate manifold M_t for t∈(0,1) remains an empirical assumption without theoretical proof, and the paper acknowledges that severely malformed baseline outputs can break down under TCFG. Open questions include whether the tangential-misalignment analysis transfers to the classifier-guidance setting, how the method interacts with CFG-distilled samplers, and whether the single-sample SVD sufficiency observed in the toy example holds universally at scale.
