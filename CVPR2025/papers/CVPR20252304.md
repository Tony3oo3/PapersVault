## Problem

Existing grounding LMMs fine-tune the full LMM backbone to learn special segmentation tokens (e.g., [SEG]), which causes catastrophic degradation in conversational ability—models like GLaMM and LISA score near zero on standard multimodal QA benchmarks and fail to follow simple instructions. The paper asks whether visual grounding can be added to LMMs without sacrificing their original instruction-following and general knowledge capabilities.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a free-form text prompt (a user question or object description) as input. It produces two outputs simultaneously: a conversational text response (preserving the frozen LMM's original chat behavior) and binary segmentation masks for grounded objects. Internally, word-image attention maps are extracted from all M transformer layers and N attention heads of the frozen LMM, upsampled to 64×64, and stacked into a tensor of shape MN×64×64 before being fed to the mask decoder. A linear keyword selector on top of the LLM's hidden states automatically identifies which word tokens correspond to groundable objects, eliminating the need for external NLP tools.

## Method

F-LMM's core insight is that well-trained LMMs already encode word-pixel correspondences in their self-attention weights, providing geometric and spatial cues sufficient for visual grounding without any parameter updates to the LMM. The only trainable components are: (1) a 3-stage U-Net mask decoder that translates stacked, normalized attention maps into mask logits via BCE and DICE losses, and (2) a SAM ViT-L-based mask refiner that further optimises predictions using dense mask prompts, bounding-box prompts, and weighted text embeddings from the LLM. A lightweight linear keyword selector (sigmoid on d-dim hidden states, threshold λ=0.3) identifies object nouns in the text sequence to automate grounded conversation. Training uses only ~190k samples from RefCOCO(+/g) and PNG on 8 A800 GPUs for ~20 hours, with no special segmentation tokens and no grounded instruction-tuning data, yet achieves competitive RES and PNG scores while fully preserving the base LMM's conversational performance.

## Results

F-LMM preserves the full conversational ability of the underlying frozen LMM while achieving competitive grounding performance. On the strongest backbone (DeepSeekVL-7B), F-LMM scores 73.2 on MMBench and 77.8 on LLaVA-In-the-Wild, compared to near-zero (0.0–36.8) for existing grounding LMMs like LaSagnA, LISA, and GLaMM. On referring expression segmentation, F-LMM(DeepSeekVL-7B) achieves 76.1 cIoU on RefCOCO, within ~2.5 points of the best grounding LMM (GLaMM at 78.6). On panoptic narrative grounding, F-LMM reaches 65.7 overall recall, surpassing GLaMM (55.8) and approaching GroundHog (66.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

F-LMM decisively outperforms all existing grounding LMMs on conversational benchmarks: on MMBench, F-LMM(DeepSeekVL-7B) scores 73.2 versus GLaMM's 36.8, LISA's 0.4, and LaSagnA's 0.0, confirming that fine-tuning LMMs for grounding catastrophically destroys instruction-following. On RefCOCO, F-LMM trails the best grounding LMMs by a small margin (76.1 vs. GLaMM's 78.6 and GroundHog's 78.5), a gap of roughly 2–3 cIoU points. On PNG, F-LMM actually surpasses most grounding LMMs (65.7 vs. GLaMM's 55.8, PixelLM's 43.1) and is within 1.1 of GroundHog (66.8). In complex scenarios, F-LMM outperforms LISA on reasoning segmentation (46.2 vs. 36.8 gIoU) and beats VisCoT-7B on the VisCoT benchmark (53.8 vs. 47.6 DocVQA) despite not being trained on VisCoT data. On grounded conversation generation, F-LMM achieves the best zero-shot METEOR (17.4) while matching GLaMM's mIoU (63.6 vs. 65.6) without any GCG-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

F-LMM departs from the dominant paradigm in grounding LMMs (GLaMM, LISA, PixelLM, GroundHog) that fine-tunes LLM backbones with special segmentation tokens and large grounding datasets, which the paper shows causes catastrophic loss of conversational ability. Instead, it repurposes the word-image attention maps already present in frozen, off-the-shelf LMMs as segmentation priors, requiring only a lightweight U-Net mask decoder and a SAM-based mask refiner as trainable components. This positions F-LMM as a minimal-intervention alternative: no vocabulary expansion, no grounded instruction-tuning data, and no LMM parameter updates, while still leveraging SAM's prompt-based mask head for refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-documented failure mode: state-of-the-art grounding LMMs score near-zero on standard QA benchmarks (e.g., GLaMM at 14/9 on MME, LaSagnA at 0/0), making them unusable as general assistants. F-LMM's frozen-LMM design offers a resource-efficient path to grounding that preserves full conversational capability, and the observed positive correlation between a base LMM's chat quality and the resulting grounding quality (Figure 6) suggests a clear scaling strategy. Open questions include whether the attention-based grounding approach generalizes to architectures without 2-D-preserving projectors, whether the modest grounding gap versus fine-tuned models (e.g., 76.1 vs. 78.6 on RefCOCO) can be closed, and whether the method extends to video or 3-D grounding.
