## Problem

Human mesh recovery from a single RGB image is inherently ill-posed due to depth ambiguity, yet most methods produce a single deterministic prediction biased toward common poses. Existing probabilistic approaches that generate multiple hypotheses sacrifice accuracy relative to the best single-output models, creating a tradeoff between diversity and precision that has remained unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250206/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, processed by a backbone (HRNet-w48, ViT-H, or ResNet-50) into a grid of image embeddings (e.g., 7×7×720 for HRNet). It outputs a sequence of N=54 discrete token indices drawn from a codebook of S=512 entries, which the frozen Mesh-VQ-VAE decoder maps to a canonical 3D mesh of 6890 vertices, along with a 6D global rotation and perspective camera parameters predicted by a separate MLP. In stochastic mode, the same image yields Q diverse mesh predictions by repeating the iterative sampling process.

## Method

MEGA is an encoder-decoder Transformer (12 encoder blocks, 4 decoder blocks, D=1024) that formulates HMR as masked generative modeling over tokenized human meshes. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) self-supervised pretraining on AMASS motion capture data, where the model reconstructs randomly masked mesh tokens from visible ones using a cosine-scheduled masking rate M=⌊N·cos(πτ/2)⌋; (2) supervised fine-tuning where the decoder is additionally conditioned on image embeddings while predicting masked tokens, using only cross-entropy loss. At inference, deterministic mode discards the encoder entirely and predicts all 54 tokens in a single argmax forward pass, while stochastic mode iteratively reveals tokens over T steps using the Gumbel-max trick to sample from the predicted categorical distributions, with the number of newly revealed tokens following a cosine schedule. The key architectural insight is that the encoder operates on visible mesh tokens (not image patches), enabling pretraining without any image data and allowing the encoder to be removed for faster deterministic inference.

## Results

In deterministic mode, MEGA with an HRNet-w48 backbone achieves 81.6 mm PVE, 68.5 mm MPJPE, and 44.1 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW, and 107.9 mm PVE, 90.5 mm MPJPE, and 58.7 mm PA-MPJPE on EMDB, outperforming the previous best (VQ-HPS) by 3.2 mm PVE on 3DPW and 5.0 mm PVE on EMDB. With a ViT-H backbone, MEGA further improves to 80.0 mm PVE and 41.0 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW. In stochastic mode with an HRNet backbone, MEGA achieves 83.4 mm PVE and 45.5 mm PA-MPJPE with a single sample, surpassing all probabilistic baselines, and improves to 75.1 mm PVE and 40.4 mm PA-MPJPE with 25 samples. On the occlusion benchmark 3DPW-OCC, MEGA (HRNet) reaches 78.9 mm PVE and 43.7 mm PA-MPJPE, beating the best occlusion-specific method (ScoreHypo) by 10.9 mm PVE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEGA outperforms all single-output baselines in deterministic mode. Against the closest token-based competitor VQ-HPS [24] (same HRNet-w48 backbone), MEGA improves PVE by 3.2 mm on 3DPW (81.6 vs. 84.8) and by 5.0 mm on EMDB (107.9 vs. 112.9), with a particularly large 6.5 mm PA-MPJPE gain on EMDB (58.7 vs. 65.2). Against non-parametric Transformer methods (FastMETRO [13], Virtual Marker [61]) and parametric methods (PARE [42], CLIFF [52]), MEGA leads by 12–40 mm in PVE depending on the dataset. With a ViT-H backbone, MEGA beats HMR2.0 [27] by 4.1 mm PVE on 3DPW and outperforms TokenHMR‡ [22] (which uses extra 2D data and BEDLAM) by 4.6 mm PVE on 3DPW and 3.1 mm on EMDB. In stochastic mode, MEGA (HRNet) surpasses ScoreHypo [90] by 4.9 mm MPJPE at Q=5 (66.1 vs. 75.3) and by 7.4 mm PA-MPJPE at Q=25 (40.4 vs. 42.5), while also beating diffusion-based Diff-HMR [12] and flow-based ProHMR [45] by 10+ mm in MPJPE at every sample count. On 3DPW-OCC, MEGA (HRNet) beats the best occlusion-aware method ScoreHypo [90] by 10.9 mm PVE and 5.0 mm PA-MPJPE, and outperforms all ResNet-50-based occlusion methods (SEFD [91], 3DCrowdNet [15], VisDB [92]) by 10+ mm PVE.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEGA synthesizes three established threads: the Mesh-VQ-VAE tokenizer from VQ-HPS [24] for discrete human mesh representation, the masked autoencoder pretraining strategy from MAE [31] for self-supervised learning, and the iterative masked generative modeling of MaskGIT [9] for stochastic token generation. The key departure from VQ-HPS is replacing deterministic classification with a generative formulation that supports multi-output prediction and unconditional mesh generation, while the self-supervised pretraining on motion capture data (without any image supervision) is novel for HMR. Architecturally, MEGA inverts the standard MAE practice by discarding the encoder rather than the decoder in deterministic inference, since the decoder alone suffices when all tokens are masked and image conditioning is provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MEGA addresses the long-standing depth ambiguity in single-image HMR by unifying deterministic and stochastic generation in one model, achieving SOTA in both modes without the typical accuracy-diversity tradeoff that plagues prior probabilistic methods (CVAE, diffusion, normalizing flows). The practical flexibility—fast single-pass prediction or diverse multi-sample generation from the same model—makes it directly useful for downstream applications ranging from animation to medical imaging. Open questions include the computational cost of stochastic mode at scale, whether the approach extends to multi-person or video settings, and whether the reliance on a frozen Mesh-VQ-VAE tokenizer limits expressiveness for extreme poses or non-standard body shapes.
