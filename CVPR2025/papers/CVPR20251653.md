## Problem

Differentiable robot rendering pipelines (e.g., Dr. Robot) optimize robot poses via gradient descent on image-space losses but lack any awareness of physical collisions, producing poses that intersect static environments or the robot's own links. This is particularly dangerous in real deployment where even minor collisions can damage expensive mechanical structures. The paper asks how to make the differentiable rendering pipeline collision-aware without requiring explicit distance-field computation or a differentiable physics simulator.

## Input / Output

The collision classifier takes a K-dimensional vector of joint angles (each in SO(2), i.e., one rotational DOF per joint) and outputs a scalar SDF value g(θ) that, after scaling and sigmoid, yields a collision probability in [0,1]. In the full Dr. Robot integration, the pipeline additionally consumes rendered RGB images, depth maps, and instance segmentation masks from four cameras to supervise pose optimization. Training data consists of N randomly sampled poses labeled by a non-differentiable Mujoco collision checker, with resampling to balance collision/non-collision ratios. The final output is a collision-free pose or trajectory whose SDF value stays below a threshold (l_thres = −0.1).

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical MLP classifier f = σ ∘ ω ∘ g that maps joint angles to a collision probability, where g is trained to behave as a signed distance field over the high-dimensional pose space. The key technical insight is that naive binary classification produces inconsistent gradient directions (consecutive updates can cancel each other), so the authors impose an Eikonal regularizer L_ek = (‖∇_θ g‖ − 1)² to enforce unit-norm gradients, making optimization stable without ever computing explicit manifold distances. Each joint is encoded by a small MLP that conditions on its parent joint's encoded feature (hierarchical encoding), capturing kinematic dependencies; all joint features are concatenated and passed through a 4-layer MLP (512 units, LeakyReLU) to produce the scalar g. Training combines binary cross-entropy with the Eikonal term (α = 0.1), and a learnable scaling factor s balances gradient magnitudes between the two losses. At inference, the SDF loss L_SDF = ReLU(g(θ) − l_thres) is added to Dr. Robot's RGB and depth losses, enabling end-to-end gradient-based collision avoidance during pose or trajectory optimization.

## Results

On the inverse-action-from-image task (Table 7), Prof. Robot reduces average collision points from 9.17 (Dr. Robot) to 0.83 and collision rate from 78% to 9% when the original data contains 10.96 collision points. For trajectory generation (Table 8), adding the SDF loss with threshold −0.01 eliminates all collisions (0.00 collision points, 0.00% rate) compared to Dr. Robot's 4.07 points and 10.64% rate. In the VLM/CLIP setting (Table 9), the method reduces collision points from 1.30 to 0.10 (at threshold −0.10) while preserving the CLIP dot-product score (34.02 vs. 33.86 mean). The SDF classifier itself achieves 97.70% classification accuracy with an Eikonal loss of 0.0850, substantially outperforming the NDF baseline (54.33% accuracy, 0.1916 Eikonal loss).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dr. Robot [32], Prof. Robot consistently wins on collision metrics across all three evaluation settings: inverse action from images (collision rate drops from 78–86% to 0–9%), trajectory generation (collision points drop from 4.07 to 0.00 at threshold −0.01), and VLM-guided optimization (collision points drop from 1.30 to 0.10 at threshold −0.10). Importantly, the method does not sacrifice image-space fidelity: the CLIP dot-product mean remains essentially unchanged (34.02 vs. 33.86), confirming that collision avoidance does not degrade action distillation quality. Against the NDF approach from Pose-NDF [55], the proposed SDF formulation achieves a decisive win: 97.70% classification accuracy versus 54.33%, and a lower Eikonal loss (0.0850 vs. 0.1916). The paper attributes this gap to the SDF's ability to model collision-free poses as a set of regions rather than a single submanifold, eliminating the need for expensive and inaccurate manifold distance labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Dr. Robot [32], a differentiable robot rendering framework based on Gaussian splatting and implicit LBS, by adding a differentiable collision-avoidance constraint that was entirely absent from the original pipeline. The hierarchical joint encoding is adapted from Pose-NDF [55], which models human pose manifolds via neural distance fields, but the key departure is replacing explicit distance-field fitting with a binary classifier regularized by an Eikonal term (borrowed from implicit geometric regularization [16]) to implicitly enforce SDF properties in high-dimensional pose space. This avoids the curse-of-dimensionality problem of computing accurate manifold distances for sparse samples in SO(2)^K, which the authors argue makes NDF-based approaches impractical for robotic poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a plug-and-play differentiable collision constraint that can be dropped into any existing differentiable rendering pipeline, addressing a practical safety gap that limits real-world deployment of image-supervised robot control. However, the current formulation is restricted to single-DOF (SO(2)) joints and static, non-interactive environments; the authors acknowledge that dynamic objects and multi-DOF joints remain open. The sim-to-real experiments are illustrative but limited to a single obstacle-avoidance scenario, leaving the robustness of the learned SDF under distribution shift (new scenes, new objects) untested. A key open question is whether the Eikonal-regularized classifier generalizes across robot morphologies or must be retrained per configuration.
