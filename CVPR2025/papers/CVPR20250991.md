## Problem

Controllable video generation with multiple heterogeneous signals (camera trajectories, user drag annotations, reference videos) remains challenging because these signals operate at different scales—camera motion induces global pixel displacement while object annotations affect only local regions—causing control conflicts and incoherent motion when naively combined. Existing methods either handle a single signal type or require complex per-signal tuning, leading to flickering and temporal instability in the generated video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250991/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a reference image, free-form text prompts, and any combination of control signals: camera trajectory parameters (extrinsic/intrinsic matrices), arrow-based motion annotations drawn on the image, or a reference video from which dense optical flow is extracted. The intermediate output is a unified dense optical flow (compressed into a latent flow space via a Flow VAE), and the final output is a temporally coherent video generated by the CogVideoX-based DiT backbone. Notably, the two stages are trained independently and only connected at inference, and the Stage 2 model tolerates imperfect alignment between the reference image and the input flow.

## Method

AnimateAnything proposes a two-stage pipeline that unifies all control signals into a single optical flow representation before video synthesis. In Stage 1, a Flow Generation Model (FGM) denoises sparse flows from explicit signals (annotations, reference video), while a Camera Reference Model (CRM) progressively injects camera trajectory information via Plücker embeddings through a reference attention mechanism, producing a unified dense flow. In Stage 2, a flow encoder compresses the unified flow into latents, and a single ViT block performs cross-attention (flow as K/V, video latents as Q) to condition the CogVideoX DiT blocks. A key architectural addition is a Frequency Stabilization module that applies FFT to DiT attention weight matrices, scales the spectral features with a learnable weight matrix, and applies inverse FFT before computing dot-product attention, thereby enforcing temporal frequency consistency and suppressing flickering under large-scale motion.

## Results

On WebVid10M, AnimateAnything achieves the best FID of 48.11 and FVD of 380, outperforming the strongest baseline Pyramid-Flow (FID 55.78, FVD 470) by 13.7% and 19.1% respectively. On OpenVid, the method further improves to FID 33.12 and FVD 322, surpassing Pyramid-Flow (FID 39.48, FVD 453) by 16.1% and 28.9%. The method also leads on all image-level metrics (LPIPS 0.135/0.113, PSNR 25.22/25.04, SSIM 0.810/0.836) and video consistency metrics (Subject Consistency 0.928/0.971, Aesthetic Quality 0.474/0.600) across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnimateAnything outperforms all six video-generation baselines (Motion-I2V, MOFA-Video, DynamiCrafter, CogVideoX+image, Pyramid-Flow, Open-Sora) on every reported metric across both WebVid10M and OpenVid, with the largest margins over Motion-I2V (FID 48.11 vs. 94.77 on WebVid10M) and DynamiCrafter (FVD 380 vs. 685). Against the closest competitor Pyramid-Flow, the gains are 7.67 FID points and 90 FVD points on WebVid10M, and 6.36 FID points and 131 FVD points on OpenVid. For camera trajectory control, AnimateAnything reduces translation error to 0.041 and rotation error to 0.159 (DUSt3R, basic trajectory), beating CameraCtrl (0.090/0.300) by 54%/47% and MotionCtrl (0.057/0.233) by 28%/32%. On the difficult trajectory setting, the advantage widens: VggSfM translation error drops to 0.447 vs. 1.559 (CameraCtrl) and 0.875 (MotionCtrl). In qualitative motion-transfer comparisons (Fig. 6), MotionClone and MOFA-Video produce style inconsistency and artifacts under large motions, while AnimateAnything maintains subject consistency without requiring skeleton or keypoint extraction. For user drag animation (Fig. 7), AnimateAnything produces more stable and temporally coherent results than MOFA-Video, DragAnything, and Motion-I2V.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CogVideoX DiT framework for video generation and the AnimateAnyone progressive condition injection design for camera reference features, while most directly extending the two-stage optical-flow-as-intermediate-representation paradigm introduced by Motion-I2V. The key departure is the unification of heterogeneous control signals (camera trajectories via Plücker embeddings, user drag annotations, and reference video flows) into a single dense optical flow, rather than handling each modality through separate LoRA modules or requiring per-signal parameter tuning as in prior multi-condition approaches. The frequency stabilization module introduces a novel FFT-based spectral attention modification to the DiT architecture, which has no direct precedent in the video diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified optical flow representation offers a practical path toward a single controllable video generation model that handles camera, object motion, and reference video simultaneously, reducing the engineering burden of multi-signal coordination. However, the approach is constrained by the scarcity of dynamic-scene video data with reliable camera poses, forcing reliance on indoor/static datasets (Real10K, DL3DV10K) for camera training, which limits generalization to outdoor or highly dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline can be collapsed into an end-to-end model, how the method scales to longer video sequences, and whether the frequency stabilization trick generalizes beyond the specific DiT architecture used here.
