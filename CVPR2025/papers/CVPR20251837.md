## Problem

Learning open-vocabulary physical skills for simulated agents is hindered by two complementary limitations: LLM-generated rewards capture precise kinematic constraints but miss holistic motion naturalness, while VLM-based rewards assess semantic correctness but cannot enforce temporal consistency or precise physical constraints. No existing framework unifies both signal types into a single reward that scales to arbitrary natural language instructions without manual engineering or task-specific demonstrations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251837/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an arbitrary natural language instruction (e.g., 'conduct the orchestra', 'position body in a shape of C') and the agent's current state represented as joint-wise Euler angles (θ ∈ R^{J×3}). It produces a trained policy (three-layer MLP or hierarchical high-level policy over a CALM low-level controller) that generates physically plausible motion sequences in simulation. The instruction is the sole task specification—no reward function, demonstration, or task-specific code is provided by the user.

## Method

GROVE combines two orthogonal reward signals: an LLM (GPT-o1-preview) generates task-specific kinematic constraint functions R_L from the instruction and agent description, while a VLM (CLIP) scores semantic alignment R_V between the agent's pose and the instruction text. To bridge the sim-to-real domain gap without expensive rendering, Pose2CLIP—a two-layer MLP trained on 1.7M Blender-rendered SMPL/SMPLX frames—directly maps agent poses into CLIP's feature space, achieving 3–6× speedup over rendering-based pipelines. The total reward R_GROVE = ω_V·R_V + ω_L·R_L is dynamically refined via a Reward Design Problem (RDP) loop: if the VLM fitness score drops for 8 consecutive steps below a threshold, the LLM regenerates R_L, creating a self-correcting reward that prevents semantic drift. The policy is trained with PPO in IsaacGym, and Pose2CLIP's training data is continuously augmented with policy rollouts to cover the evolving pose distribution.

## Results

On open-vocabulary humanoid skill synthesis (Table 1), GROVE (+CALM) achieves the highest task completion score of 7.924 (vs. 6.108 for the best baseline AnySkill), motion naturalness of 6.793, physical realism of 8.452, and CLIP text-image similarity of 28.998. The paper reports a 25.7% improvement in task completion and 22.2% improvement in perceived motion naturalness over baselines, with 8.4× faster convergence. On standard RL benchmarks (Table 2), GROVE outperforms direct reward optimization in 3 of 4 tasks (e.g., Ant running: RD 92.45 vs. 103.81; ANYmal sideways: RD 37.51 vs. 39.71), and on the humanoid bowing task (Table 3) it achieves a completion score of 6.276 in 47 min versus VLM-RM's 3.483 in 411 min (11.4% of the training time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROVE outperforms all five baselines in Table 1 on task completion (7.924 vs. 6.108 for AnySkill, the strongest baseline), physical realism (8.452 vs. 8.168), and CLIP similarity (28.998 vs. 23.925). On motion naturalness, GROVE (6.793) edges out MoMask (6.724) and MotionGPT (6.552), while TMR achieves the best smoothness (0.374 vs. GROVE's 0.488), reflecting TMR's retrieval-based approach that selects from existing natural motions. Among zero-shot skill learning methods, GROVE substantially surpasses AnySkill (which uses only VLM-based rewards) on every metric except smoothness, confirming the benefit of combining LLM and VLM rewards. On standard RL benchmarks, GROVE beats direct reward optimization on Ant running (RD 92.45 vs. 103.81) and ANYmal sideways walking (RD 37.51 vs. 39.71) but loses on ANYmal jumping (RD 60.84 vs. 57.29), where the baseline directly optimizes the evaluation metric. Against VLM-RM on the humanoid bowing task, GROVE achieves a completion score of 6.276 versus 3.483 (improved-texture VLM-RM) while training in 47 min versus 411 min, a 8.7× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

GROVE extends the LLM-based reward generation paradigm established by Eureka [26] by adding a VLM-based semantic evaluation channel, directly addressing the known limitation that LLM-generated rewards produce technically correct but stylistically unnatural motions. It also builds on the authors' own prior work AnySkill [8], which used VLM-only rewards for open-vocabulary skill learning, by showing that VLM rewards alone lack the precise kinematic constraints needed for task mastery. The Pose2CLIP component is a practical departure from prior VLM-as-reward approaches (e.g., VLM-RM [37]) that require high-fidelity rendering, instead learning a lightweight pose-to-CLIP mapping to eliminate the sim-to-real visual gap without rendering overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GROVE provides a practical recipe for eliminating manual reward engineering in open-vocabulary physical skill learning, with the 8.4× convergence speedup making iterative reward design feasible within standard RL training budgets. Pose2CLIP, as a general-purpose pose-to-semantic-feature mapper, could benefit any pipeline that needs VLM-based evaluation in simulation without expensive rendering. However, the framework remains confined to simulation, relies on a frozen CLIP model whose semantic coverage may not extend to all physical tasks, and the iterative reward regeneration mechanism adds engineering complexity that may not transfer cleanly to real-robot settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-agent or contact-rich tasks and whether the LLM+VLM combination generalizes beyond humanoid embodiments.
