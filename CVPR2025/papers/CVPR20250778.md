## Problem

Few-shot recognition (FSR) methods have been dominated by parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) paradigms that freeze the VLM backbone and learn only a small adapter or prompt, often relying on unrealistically large validation sets for hyperparameter tuning. This limits both accuracy and practical utility for real-world applications like automated data annotation. Additionally, retrieval-augmented learning (RAL), which leverages a VLM's pretraining data, has proven effective for zero-shot recognition but remains under-explored for FSR, and naively applying it reveals two unaddressed challenges: imbalanced distribution of retrieved data and domain gaps between retrieved and few-shot examples.

## Input / Output

The method takes 4, 8, or 16 annotated RGB images per target class from a downstream task, plus 500 retrieved images per class from the LAION-400M pretraining corpus (obtained via string matching on concept names), and text concept labels used to initialize the classifier. CutMix augmentation is applied during training to create composite images by pasting random patches from retrieved images onto few-shot annotated ones. The output is a single class label per input image, evaluated on nine fine-grained recognition benchmarks using the OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 visual encoder.

## Method

SWAT (Stage-Wise retrieval-Augmented fineTuning) is a two-stage training procedure. In Stage 1, the entire VLM visual encoder is finetuned end-to-end on a mixed set of retrieved and few-shot annotated images using cross-entropy loss with CutMix augmentation, producing generalizable feature representations that bridge the domain gap via transfer learning. In Stage 2, only the classifier head is retrained exclusively on the balanced few-shot examples atop the frozen encoder, which mitigates the imbalanced distribution of retrieved data by decoupling representation learning from classification. The classifier is initialized with text embeddings from the VLM's text encoder rather than randomly, and the same hyperparameters (learning rate, weight decay) are applied uniformly across all datasets without a validation set. This stage-wise design simultaneously addresses both the domain gap (through transfer learning on larger mixed data) and the class imbalance (through balanced classifier retraining), yielding >6% accuracy improvement over prior state-of-the-art FSR methods.

## Results

On the mean accuracy across nine fine-grained recognition benchmarks, SWAT achieves 73.5% (4-shot), 76.0% (8-shot), and 78.2% (16-shot), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art few-shot method CLAP by +6.6, +6.0, and +5.3 percentage points respectively. Even the simpler few-shot finetuning baseline (finetuning the VLM visual encoder solely on few-shot examples) already surpasses CLAP by +2.8 to +3.4 points. SWAT also significantly exceeds the best zero-shot retrieval-augmented method REAL-Linear (64.8% mean accuracy) by over 8–13 points depending on shot count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWAT outperforms the previous state-of-the-art few-shot method CLAP [59] by a substantial margin of +6.6, +6.0, and +5.3 points at 4, 8, and 16 shots respectively, with gains reaching 10–25% on challenging fine-grained datasets like Semi-Aves, EuroSAT, and FGVC-Aircraft. The simpler few-shot finetuning baseline (no retrieval) already beats CLAP by +2.8 to +3.4 points, demonstrating that full finetuning of the VLM visual encoder is underexplored yet highly effective. Against other adapter-based methods, SWAT surpasses CrossModal-LP [36] by +8.1 to +6.4 points and TaskRes [75] by +10.0 to +8.3 points. Compared to zero-shot retrieval-augmented methods, SWAT exceeds REAL-Linear [43] (64.8%) by 8.7–13.4 points and REAL-Prompt [43] (62.6%) by 10.9–15.6 points, confirming that combining retrieval with few-shot finetuning in a stage-wise manner is far more effective than retrieval alone. The ablation (Table 4) further shows that each component contributes: full finetuning adds ~2.5%, CutMix adds ~2%, and stage-2 classifier retraining adds ~0.5–0.8% on top of the mixed-data finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends retrieval-augmented learning (RAL), previously established for zero-shot recognition by Parashar et al. (CVPR 2024), to the few-shot recognition setting for the first time. It also adapts the decoupled two-stage training strategy from long-tailed recognition (Kang et al., ICLR 2020) to address imbalanced distributions in retrieved data. Conceptually, the paper departs sharply from the dominant FSR paradigm of parameter-efficient finetuning (prompt learning, adapters) over frozen VLM backbones, instead advocating full finetuning of the visual encoder under a validation-free setup motivated by real-world data annotation rather than PEFT or robustness studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that naive full finetuning on few-shot examples already surpasses all prior PEFT-based FSR methods challenges the field's assumption that restricting learned parameters is necessary, and the >6% SOTA improvement across nine benchmarks is practically significant for data-annotation applications. However, the method has not been validated in a real annotation pipeline, relies on a single VLM architecture (OpenCLIP ViT-B/32) for main results, and uses fixed hyperparameters across all datasets. Open questions include whether the domain-gap and imbalance issues can be decoupled for independent analysis, whether richer retrieval (e.g., using annotation-guideline descriptions) further improves performance, and whether the approach generalizes to other VLM families or modalities.
