## Problem

Generalizable human novel view synthesis from sparse multi-view images remains challenging because existing NeRF-based methods require per-subject optimization or dense views, while 3DGS-based methods rely on reliable depth estimation that fails under limited view overlap. Additionally, the SMPL template fitted from sparse views is often inaccurate, and previous methods that use it as a geometric prior are highly sensitive to this misalignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes four fixed-view (front, back, left, right) RGB images at 512×512 resolution along with SMPL parameters estimated from those views via EasyMocap. It produces novel view renderings in a single feed-forward pass (~180 ms on an RTX 4090) without any per-subject fine-tuning, outputting per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters (position, rotation, scaling, opacity, color) that are rendered via differentiable rasterization.

## Method

RoGSplat lifts sparse SMPL vertices to dense image-aligned 3D prior points using a two-step Snowflake Point Deconvolution (SPD) network conditioned on both pixel-level features (U-Net features from source images, projected and aggregated with visibility-aware weighting) and voxel-level features (refined SMPL depth unprojected into a sparse 3D convolution volume). These prior points serve as positions for a set of coarse Gaussians whose remaining attributes are regressed via MLPs. The coarse Gaussians are then rasterized to produce per-view depth maps, which are refined and unprojected to yield pixel-wise points capturing high-frequency details. A final offset estimator, driven by interpolated voxel features from a second sparse convolution volume, corrects these points before a second set of MLPs regresses fine-grained pixel-wise Gaussians. Training is done in two stages: first the point-prediction module is trained with photometric, mask, depth-consistency, and DINOv2 semantic-alignment losses, then the coarse-to-fine Gaussian regression is trained with the point module frozen.

## Results

On in-domain generalization (4-view input), RoGSplat achieves PSNR 28.94 / SSIM 0.9615 / LPIPS 0.0433 on THuman2.0, 27.00 / 0.9530 / 0.0519 on RenderPeople, and 31.89 / 0.9623 / 0.0353 on ZJU-MoCap, outperforming all NeRF-based baselines. Against 3DGS-based methods on THuman2.0, it surpasses GPS-Gaussian (PSNR 27.79) and GHG (PSNR 24.98) while using fewer parameters (12.2M vs. 17.8M for GHG). In cross-domain generalization (trained on THuman2.0, tested without fine-tuning), RoGSplat achieves the best PSNR on all three held-out datasets, including 25.99 on the real-world dataset where the next-best method (TransHuman) scores 24.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoGSplat outperforms all four NeRF-based baselines (SHERF, NHP, GP-NeRF, TransHuman) on every dataset and metric in the in-domain setting, with the largest margin over TransHuman on THuman2.0 (+1.58 dB PSNR) and RenderPeople (+0.63 dB PSNR). Among 3DGS-based methods on THuman2.0, it beats GPS-Gaussian by 1.15 dB PSNR (28.94 vs. 27.79) and GHG by 3.96 dB PSNR (28.94 vs. 24.98), while using fewer parameters than GHG (12.2M vs. 17.8M). In the cross-domain setting (trained on THuman2.0, zero-shot on other datasets), RoGSplat achieves the best PSNR on all three test sets, notably surpassing GPS-Gaussian by 4.44 dB on the real-world dataset (25.99 vs. 21.55) and TransHuman by 1.39 dB (25.99 vs. 24.60). The method also demonstrates robustness to inaccurate SMPL fitting, producing near-identical quality whether given fitted or ground-truth SMPL, whereas baselines like NHP and TransHuman show significant degradation with inaccurate SMPL.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoGSplat sits at the intersection of generalizable human NeRF (TransHuman, NHP, GP-NeRF) and generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting (GPS-Gaussian, GHG), adopting the real-time rendering advantage of 3DGS while addressing the sparse-view fragility that plagues both families. Its core architectural departure is the use of Snowflake Point Deconvolution to lift sparse SMPL vertices into dense 3D prior points, combined with a dual pixel-level/voxel-level feature fusion for image alignment—whereas GPS-Gaussian relies on iterative stereo depth (which fails under limited overlap) and GHG regresses Gaussians in template UV space without compensating for misalignment. The coarse-to-fine strategy, where coarse Gaussians rendered from prior points supply depth to guide fine pixel-wise Gaussians, is a novel pipeline design not present in prior generalizable human 3DGS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical path to real-time, per-subject-optimization-free human rendering from just four views, which is directly relevant to applications like telepresence and sports broadcasting where dense capture rigs are impractical. The demonstrated robustness to inaccurate SMPL fitting (Figure 5) is practically significant, as real-world SMPL estimation from sparse views is often unreliable. However, the paper acknowledges clear limitations: failure on loose clothing, inability to recover fine face/hand details, no animation or editing capability, and a fixed four-view input constraint. Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine depth-guided strategy extends to dynamic or multi-person scenes, and whether the SMPL-based prior can be replaced by more expressive body models to handle complex garments.
