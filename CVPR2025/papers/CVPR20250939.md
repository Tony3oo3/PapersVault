## Problem

Selecting the most informative viewpoint from a multi-view instructional video at each timestep is critical for human skill learning, yet existing automatic cinematography methods either rely on hand-coded heuristics or require expensive manual best-view labels that are domain-specific and hard to scale. No prior approach can learn view selection in a weakly supervised manner that leverages the naturally available language narrations accompanying instructional videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250939/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes only a multi-view video as input (M clips, each with N synchronized RGB streams from different cameras—5 views in Ego-Exo4D, 2 views in LEMMA) and outputs a single best-view index per clip. No language, narrations, or camera poses are required at test time. During training, view-agnostic narrations (human-written descriptions of the activity) are available per clip to drive pseudo-label generation, but these are discarded at inference.

## Method

LANGVIEW decomposes the task into a pseudo-labeler and a selector. The pseudo-labeler uses K=3 finetuned off-the-shelf video captioners (Video-Llama with Llama-2/Vicuna decoders, VideoChat2) to predict a caption per view, scores each against the ground-truth view-agnostic narration via CIDEr, and aggregates ranks across captioners to produce consensus best-view pseudo-labels. The selector is a TimeSformer-based visual encoder feeding two heads: a view classifier that compares per-view embeddings to predict the best view, and an auxiliary relative camera pose predictor that discretizes inter-view displacement/rotation angles into 30° bins and classifies them for all view pairs, enforcing view-sensitive feature learning. A novel min-over-pseudo-labels cross-entropy loss handles cases where multiple views are jointly labeled as best, stabilizing training against captioner hallucinations.

## Results

On Ego-Exo4D, LANGVIEW achieves CIDEr 13.5, METEOR 48.4, V-IoU 33.7, N-IoU 39.2, and NC-IoU 32.9, outperforming the best heuristic baseline (Body-area) by 0.6 CIDEr and 2.0 N-IoU points. On LEMMA, it reaches CIDEr 42.7, METEOR 74.4, V-IoU 40.1, N-IoU 42.9, and NC-IoU 18.9, again leading all baselines. In human evaluation, LANGVIEW's selected views are preferred over the best baselines with win rates of 52.2–55.1% on Ego-Exo4D and 43.3–46.7% on LEMMA (p ≤ 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LANGVIEW outperforms all five baselines across both datasets and all metrics. Against the Hand-object baseline (Cheng et al., NeurIPS 2023), which selects the view with highest hand/object detection confidence, LANGVIEW gains +0.9 CIDEr and +2.5 N-IoU on Ego-Exo4D, and +2.0 CIDEr and +1.4 N-IoU on LEMMA. The Body-area and Joint-count baselines (using RTMPose, Jiang et al. 2023) are the strongest heuristics; LANGVIEW still leads by +0.6 CIDEr and +2.0 N-IoU on Ego-Exo4D, and +0.6 CIDEr and +1.6 N-IoU on LEMMA. The Snap angles method (Cha et al. 2020; Xiong et al. 2018), which picks the view with the most foreground pixels, is outperformed by +1.3 CIDEr and +3.4 N-IoU on Ego-Exo4D. The Longest-caption baseline (using Video-Llama, Zhang et al. 2023), which selects the view producing the longest narration, performs worst among all baselines, trailing LANGVIEW by 2.8 CIDEr on Ego-Exo4D and 10.0 CIDEr on LEMMA, suggesting that caption length is a poor proxy for view informativeness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the automatic cinematography literature, which has relied on hand-coded heuristics or expensive manual best-view labels, by introducing language as a source of weak supervision for view selection. Architecturally, the view selector uses a TimeSformer-based visual encoder, while the auxiliary relative camera pose predictor adapts the feature-concatenation-and-correlation paradigm from VLocNet++ [78] and dense correlation volume estimation [9]. Conceptually, the work extends the captioning-for-weak-supervision line (previously applied to action recognition, detection, and segmentation) to a new target task: using the *relative* quality of view-dependent caption predictions as a proxy for view informativeness, rather than using captions as direct supervision signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, scalable path to automated view selection in multi-view instructional videos without requiring costly per-view annotations, leveraging widely available narrations and off-the-shelf captioners. A key open question is how well the approach generalizes beyond the two instructional-video datasets tested, particularly to domains where narrations are sparse or where multiple views are genuinely equivalent. The authors note failure modes when multiple high-quality views exist or when the selector latches onto spurious view-independent cues, suggesting that the language signal, while effective, does not fully capture all dimensions of human view preference. Future work on best-view anticipation for energy-efficient capture setups points toward practical deployment in multi-camera recording pipelines.
