## Problem

Single-primitive representations for head avatars face a fundamental trade-off: UV-mapped meshes capture sharp high-frequency textures on smooth surfaces but cannot model complex geometry like hair, while 3D Gaussian Splatting excels at intricate structures but produces ribbed or blurry artifacts on smooth regions and wastes computational resources. No prior method unifies both primitive types within a single differentiable rendering pipeline for animatable head avatars.

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RGB videos at 2048×1334 resolution (16 training views, 4 test views on Multiface) along with an SfM-derived point cloud for initialization. A FLAME parametric model is first tracked to extract per-frame identity, expression, and pose parameters. The output is a hybrid avatar consisting of a UV-mapped mesh (learnable texture map T and opacity map A) with 3D Gaussian splats rigged onto mesh facets, renderable at ≥30 fps at 2048×1334 on a single RTX A100 GPU from arbitrary viewpoints and driven by novel expressions.

## Method

HERA represents the head as a hybrid of a UV-mapped polygon mesh (vertices, facets, texture UV map, opacity UV map) and 3D Gaussian splats localized in per-facet local coordinate systems on the FLAME mesh. The key technical contribution is a stable hybrid depth sorting strategy: since mesh rasterization yields per-pixel depths while 3DGS yields per-tile depths, directly merging the two z-buffers causes splats to appear to cross mesh facets under novel views. HERA resolves this by projecting each splat's mean onto the rasterized mesh depth along the camera ray via bilinear interpolation, then comparing the interpolated depth with the splat's own depth to assign a consistent front/behind ordering, with a threshold rule (λ = 5 cm) for edge cases. After sorting, standard α-blending over the merged N+M element list produces the final pixel color. Training proceeds in two stages: 9,000 iterations of pure-mesh UV-map optimization, followed by joint hybrid differentiable rendering that simultaneously updates the texture/opacity maps and densifies the rigged splats, with CUDA-accelerated depth sorting maintaining per-tile facet position lists for gradient backpropagation.

## Results

On the Multiface dataset, HERA achieves 33.647 PSNR, 0.910 SSIM, and 0.232 LPIPS on novel view synthesis, and 35.281 PSNR, 0.911 SSIM, and 0.221 LPIPS on novel expression animation, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, GaussianAvatars, scores 31.728 PSNR (NVS) and 34.250 PSNR (NEA), yielding HERA a margin of ~1.9 dB and ~1.0 dB respectively. The stable depth sorting ablation contributes an additional ~0.8 dB PSNR over the version without it, confirming its importance for artifact-free rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERA outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both novel view synthesis and novel expression animation. Against the strongest baseline GaussianAvatars [33], HERA gains +1.92 dB PSNR and −0.076 LPIPS on NVS, and +1.03 dB PSNR and −0.068 LPIPS on NEA, with the gap attributable to the UV-mapped mesh capturing high-frequency skin details (stubble, spots) that pure 3DGS struggles with. NeRFBlendShape [9], the implicit NeRF-based method, lags by ~4.8 dB PSNR on NVS and suffers from MLP generalization limits on novel expressions. PointAvatar [53] is the weakest on NEA (27.348 PSNR), showing dotted artifacts from its isotropic point representation. GaussianBlendshapes [27] overfits to training views and produces artifacts on test views, scoring below GaussianAvatars on both tasks. The ablation row confirms that the stable depth sorting strategy adds ~0.8 dB PSNR, eliminating facet-crossing artifacts that would otherwise degrade novel-view quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

HERA extends the line of explicit-primitive head avatar methods (GaussianAvatars, SplattingAvatar, FlashAvatar) by introducing a hybrid representation that jointly renders UV-mapped polygon meshes and 3D Gaussian splats within a single differentiable pipeline. It departs from SUGAR, which co-optimizes mesh and Gaussians but renders only with Gaussians, and from MeGA, which renders hair (3DGS) and face (mesh) separately and stitches them together. The key conceptual departure is the stable depth sorting strategy that resolves the fundamental incompatibility between per-pixel mesh depths and per-tile Gaussian depths, enabling true interleaved alpha-blending of both primitive types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid representation addresses a practical limitation of pure 3DGS head avatars—ribbed or blurry artifacts on smooth surfaces with high-frequency texture (stubble, skin spots)—while retaining 3DGS's strength in modeling complex geometry like hair and eyelashes. Real-time rendering at 2048×1334 (≥30 fps) makes the approach viable for interactive applications, and the UV-mapped mesh component opens the door to downstream texture editing. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the FLAME parametric model, how it scales to full-body avatars, and whether the 5 cm depth threshold for the special-case sorting rule is robust across diverse subjects and viewpoints.
