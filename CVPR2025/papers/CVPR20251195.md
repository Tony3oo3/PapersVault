## Problem

Existing image captioning benchmarks (MSCOCO, NoCaps) rely on short ~10-word captions that cannot assess the quality of detailed, comprehensive captions generated by LVLMs. Hallucination benchmarks only check object presence, and DetailCaps' isolated term matching breaks the binding of attributes to objects and the directionality of relations, leading to inaccurate quality assessment. No prior benchmark integrates objects, their bound attributes, and directional relations in a structured, human-annotated form for evaluating comprehensive captions.

## Input / Output

The evaluation pipeline takes an RGB image (from MSCOCO panoptic segmentation, with >95% mask coverage) and a generated detailed caption (typically 70–360 words) as input. It produces a hierarchical set of scores: a soft object-level coverage percentage via Sentence-BERT matching, a 0–5 attribute-level score and 0–5 relation-level score (each produced by Llama3 as an LLM judge), a pixel-level coverage percentage weighted by attribute scores, and a unified 0–100 metric computed as a weighted average (25% object, 35% attribute, 40% relation). The benchmark also includes CompreQA-P and CompreQA-Cap, multiple-choice VQA tasks targeting tiny objects (<5% of image pixels) to probe fine-grained perception.

## Method

CompreCap is a 560-image benchmark where each image is manually annotated with a directed scene graph: precise semantic segmentation masks over 412 object categories, attribute descriptions (shape, size, color, texture) bound to each object, and subject-verb-object relation annotations capturing directional relationships. At evaluation time, the generated caption is decomposed into sub-captions by sentence separators, and nouns are extracted via spaCy for lemmatization. Object-level coverage is computed by building a Sentence-BERT similarity matrix between candidate and annotated objects, retaining only mutual argmax matches. For attribute and relation scoring, sub-captions mentioning the same annotated object (or all objects in a relation) are concatenated, and Llama3 is prompted to rate similarity against the ground-truth annotation on a 0–5 scale. The unified metric linearly scales each component to 0–100 and applies weights reflecting increasing difficulty (25/35/40), achieving Pearson r = 0.989 with human judgment—far exceeding BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, CLIPScore, or direct Llama3 scoring.

## Results

On the CompreCap benchmark, GPT-4o achieves the highest unified score (S_unified) of 60.05, followed by LLaVA-Next-34B (58.85) and InternVL-Chat-V1-5 (58.48), while human annotators score 62.99, surpassing all LVLMs. At the object level, LLaVA-Next-34B leads with 72.86% coverage, and at the relation level, GPT-4o scores highest (2.93/5). The proposed S_unified metric achieves near-perfect correlation with human judgment (Pearson r = 0.989, Kendall's τ = 0.927, Spearman's ρ = 0.982), substantially outperforming traditional metrics like BLEU@4 (r = 0.867), CIDER (r = 0.563), and CLIPScore (r = 0.691).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 10 LVLMs evaluated, GPT-4o leads in S_unified (60.05) and relation score (2.93/5), while LLaVA-Next-34B edges ahead in object coverage (72.86%), attribute score (2.59/5), and pixel coverage (58.49%), yielding a S_unified of 58.85. GPT-4V (57.74) underperforms both LLaVA-Next-34B and InternVL-Chat-V1-5 (58.48) despite being the prior state-of-the-art commercial model. The weakest performers are MiniGPT4-v2 (42.28) and InstructBLIP-7B (48.16), with the latter scoring below random chance (35.28%) on the fine-grained VQA task CompreQA-P. Against traditional captioning metrics, the proposed S_unified dramatically outperforms all baselines in human-judgment consistency: Pearson r of 0.989 vs. 0.867 (BLEU@4), 0.725 (ROUGE-L), 0.691 (CLIPScore), 0.581 (METEOR), 0.563 (CIDER), and 0.816 (direct Llama3 scoring). On the CompreQA fine-grained VQA tasks, InternVL-Chat-V1-5 achieves the best LVLM accuracy (91.67% / 94.33%), while human annotators reach 96.49% / 96.83%, confirming the benchmark's validity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the evaluation of detailed image captions by departing from two prior lines of work. First, it moves beyond traditional n-gram metrics (BLEU, CIDER, ROUGE-L) and CLIP-based scores, which the authors demonstrate are unreliable for long captions due to their inability to model compositional structure. Second, and more critically, it departs from DetailCaps-100's approach of using a text scene graph parser (Factual) to extract isolated object, attribute, and relation tokens for independent matching; instead, CompreCap preserves the binding of attributes to specific objects and the directionality of relations through a manually annotated directed scene graph, drawing on the scene graph literature for its structural design. The data pipeline builds on MSCOCO panoptic segmentation but requires full re-annotation of segmentation masks, category labels, attributes, and relations to achieve the precision needed for structured evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CompreCap fills a clear gap: prior benchmarks either used short captions (~10 words) or Q/A hallucination probes, leaving no structured way to assess whether an LVLM's detailed caption correctly binds attributes to objects and captures directional relationships. The finding that GPT-4V underperforms LLaVA-Next-34B on comprehensive captioning, and that direct LLM-as-judge scoring (Llama3) produces counterintuitive rankings where human captions score below most LVLMs, underscores the need for structured, annotation-grounded evaluation. Limitations include the relatively small scale (560 images), dependence on Llama3 as an evaluator (introducing a potential circularity if Llama3's biases align with the models being scored), and the 95% mask-coverage threshold that may limit generalizability to cluttered or outdoor scenes. Open questions include whether the weighted unified metric (25/35/40) is optimal, how the benchmark scales to video or multi-image captioning, and whether the fine-grained VQA component can be extended beyond the 5% pixel threshold.
