## Problem

Existing transductive and test-time adaptation methods for VLMs are evaluated under unrealistic assumptions—uniform class distributions, all classes present in every batch, and i.i.d. data streams—and systematically degrade zero-shot robustness when these assumptions are violated. The paper identifies that current methods are biased toward narrow ranges of effective class counts or specific correlation structures, failing in realistic deployment scenarios such as satellite image patches with missing classes or video streams with temporal correlations.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a black-box fashion on the CLIP embedding space: it takes a batch of N images (encoded by a frozen ViT-B/16 vision encoder into d-dimensional feature vectors) and K text class prompts (encoded by the frozen text encoder), without requiring access to internal model parameters or gradients. It outputs a soft assignment vector z_i ∈ Δ_K for each image, representing the probability of belonging to each class. Crucially, the method does not require knowing the effective number of classes K_eff in the batch in advance, and handles any K_eff from 1 to K without hyperparameter retuning.

## Method

StatA formulates transductive classification as a regularized multivariate Gaussian MLE clustering problem over the CLIP embedding space, with a novel Statistical Anchor term that regularizes the per-class mean vectors and covariance matrices using text-encoder-derived priors. The anchor Gaussian for class k has its mean set to the text embedding t_k and its covariance estimated from zero-shot soft predictions, and a KL divergence between the anchor and the optimized Gaussian penalizes deviation from these text-driven statistics. Closed-form parameter updates express each class mean and covariance as a convex combination of the standard MLE estimate and the anchor, weighted by β_k which shrinks toward the anchor when few samples are assigned to that class. Assignment updates use a CCCP scheme with a Laplacian regularizer (encouraging nearby features to share predictions) and a text-supervision KL term (penalizing deviation from zero-shot softmax predictions), yielding a decoupled iterative procedure that processes thousands of samples in seconds.

## Results

On batch TTA (batch size 64), StatA achieves 69.3% on ImageNet (zero-shot 66.6%) and 53.9% on SUN397 (zero-shot 48.3%) in the Very Low K_eff (1–4 classes) setting, with consistent gains of +2 to +5 points across all K_eff ranges. In the Medium setting (5–25 classes), StatA reaches 70.3% on ImageNet and 57.0% on SUN397, matching or exceeding Dirichlet (70.4%) and TransCLIP (70.2%). For online TTA (batch size 128), StatA maintains stable performance across all correlation levels (γ = 0.001 to 0.01), e.g., 69.6% on ImageNet under high correlation (γ = 0.001), while TDA and DMN show inconsistent or negligible gains over zero-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StatA outperforms or matches all four batch baselines (Dirichlet, ZLaP, TransCLIP) across the full range of K_eff scenarios, with the most pronounced advantage in the Very Low and Low settings where Dirichlet and TransCLIP degrade sharply (e.g., on SUN397 with 1–4 effective classes, Dirichlet drops to 27.4% while StatA maintains 53.9%). Dirichlet [24] is competitive only in its narrow design range (3–10 effective classes) and suffers catastrophic drops (e.g., −49.5 points on SUN397) when K_eff deviates. TransCLIP [40] excels in the All-classes scenario (92.7% on ImageNet) but underperforms zero-shot when K_eff decreases, confirming its bias toward large-scale transduction. ZLaP [32] provides modest gains when all classes are present but struggles in imbalanced or low-K_eff settings. For online TTA, StatA consistently improves over zero-shot across all correlation levels, whereas TDA [14] and DMN [43] deliver only marginal or inconsistent gains, and TENT [33] fails to provide reliable improvements, likely due to the absence of batch normalization in CLIP's ViT encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

StatA extends the multivariate Gaussian MLE clustering framework introduced by TransCLIP (same authors) by adding a KL-divergence-based statistical anchor that regularizes the Gaussian mean and covariance parameters toward text-encoder-derived priors, whereas all prior transductive methods (EM-Dirichlet, ZLaP, TransCLIP, PADDLE, LaplacianShot) regularize only the assignment variables. The paper also departs from prior evaluation protocols by introducing two realistic deployment scenarios—variable effective class counts per batch and non-i.i.d. correlated streams—that existing methods were never tested under. Conceptually, the key insight is that VLMs' text-encoder knowledge provides natural priors on class statistics that can be exploited as a regularizer, a direction no prior transductive VLM method pursued.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that existing transductive and online TTA methods for VLMs systematically degrade zero-shot robustness outside narrow favorable conditions (small K_eff, uniform class distributions, i.i.d. streams)—challenges the field's implicit assumption that adaptation always helps. This is practically important for deployment: practitioners using these methods on real satellite imagery, video streams, or imbalanced data may be unknowingly hurting their models. Open questions include whether the anchor weighting α can be made adaptive per scenario, whether the approach extends to non-Gaussian densities, and whether online TTA for VLMs can achieve the same robustness as the batch setting, since current online methods (TDA, DMN) still struggle with correlated streams.
