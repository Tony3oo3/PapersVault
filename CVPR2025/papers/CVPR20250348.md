## Problem

Current text-to-3D, image-to-3D generators and 3D scanners produce monolithic, unstructured objects (a single mesh, neural field, or Gaussian mixture) that lack the meaningful part decomposition required by professional 3D workflows such as animation, game asset creation, and robotics. Part segmentation is inherently ambiguous with no gold standard, and parts are often heavily occluded or entirely invisible from the exterior, making both decomposition and completion non-deterministic tasks that deterministic reconstruction networks cannot handle.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt, a single RGB image, or an existing 3D object as input, which is rendered (or generated) into a 2×2 grid of four cardinal-view images at resolution H×W, forming a single multi-view image of shape 3×2H×2W. The pipeline outputs a set of independently reconstructed 3D part meshes, along with intermediate multi-view color-coded segmentation maps and completed multi-view part images. Parts are reconstructed separately and then reassembled into a coherent whole, with the reassembled object matching the quality of a direct unstructured reconstruction.

## Method

PartGen repurposes a multi-view latent diffusion model (fine-tuned from an Emu-style architecture) for two distinct generative tasks before 3D reconstruction. First, a segmentation model Φ_seg is fine-tuned to condition on the multi-view image I and stochastically generate a color-coded segmentation map C, where RGB space is quantized into Q fixed colors and parts are randomly permuted across colors during training; multiple stochastic samples yield diverse plausible decompositions that capture artist intent. Second, a completion model Φ_comp is fine-tuned with a 25-channel input (8D masked-part latent + 8D full-context latent + 8D noise + 1D binary mask) to hallucinate occluded or entirely invisible portions of each part while maintaining geometric consistency with the surrounding object. The completed multi-view part images are then passed to a pre-trained deterministic reconstruction model (LightplaneLRM) that requires no fine-tuning, since the generative completion stage has already resolved the ambiguity that would otherwise degrade reconstruction quality.

## Results

On the part segmentation task (Table 1), PartGen with 10 samples achieves mAP50 of 59.3 and mAP75 of 38.5 in the automatic setting, substantially outperforming the best SAM2 variant (mAP50=37.4, mAP75=27.0) and Part123 (mAP50=11.5, mAP75=7.4). For part completion (Table 2), PartGen's contextual multi-view completion reaches PSNR of 21.38 and LPIPS of 0.015 on rendered views, approaching the oracle upper bound (PSNR=∞, LPIPS=0.0) and far exceeding the no-completion baseline (PSNR=13.24, LPIPS=0.039). In the reassembling experiment (Table 3), the part-based reconstruction (PSNR=20.33, LPIPS=0.065) is nearly on par with direct unstructured reconstruction (PSNR=20.47, LPIPS=0.064), confirming that independently reconstructed parts fit together coherently.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartGen decisively outperforms all baselines on part segmentation. Against the strongest external baseline, fine-tuned multi-view SAM2* (mAP50=37.4), PartGen with 10 samples achieves 59.3 mAP50, a gain of over 21 points, and 38.5 mAP75 versus 27.0. Even a single PartGen sample (mAP50=45.2) already surpasses SAM2* by 7.8 points, indicating that the generative formulation captures the inherent ambiguity of part decomposition better than deterministic segmentation. Part123, a single-view part-aware reconstruction method, trails far behind at mAP50=11.5, confirming that multi-view context is critical. On part completion, PartGen's contextual multi-view approach (PSNR=21.38) beats the no-completion baseline by 8.1 dB and the single-view completion by 5.5 dB, while the reassembled part-based object matches the unstructured reconstruction within 0.14 dB PSNR, showing no quality loss from the decomposition-reconstruction pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartGen builds on the two-stage multi-view diffusion + 3D reconstruction pipeline established by Instant3D, AssetGen, and MVDream, but repurposes the multi-view diffusion model for two new sub-tasks: part segmentation (framed as stochastic multi-view-consistent coloring) and contextual part completion (framed as multi-view inpainting with object-level context). This departs from prior 3D segmentation approaches (SAM2, Part123, Contrastive Lift) that operate directly on 3D representations or single views, and from compositional 3D generation work (D3LL, ComboVerse) that handles object-level rather than part-level composition. The key conceptual shift is treating part decomposition as a generative, distributional problem rather than a deterministic one, leveraging the stochasticity of diffusion sampling to capture the inherent ambiguity of how artists decompose objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PartGen addresses a practical gap between current 3D generation (which produces monolithic, unstructured assets) and professional workflows that require independently editable, semantically meaningful parts for animation, reconfiguration, and reuse. The finding that a generative segmentation approach substantially outperforms fine-tuned SAM2 on part decomposition validates the principle that ambiguous, artist-dependent tasks are better modeled as distributions than point estimates. Limitations include reliance on a proprietary 140k-asset training set, a fixed 4-view setup, and the quality ceiling imposed by the deterministic reconstruction model. Open questions include scalability to scenes with many interacting objects, real-time inference, and whether the approach generalizes to non-rigid or articulated parts.
