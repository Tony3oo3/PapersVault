## Problem

Existing 4D portrait avatar reconstruction methods either require hundreds of multi-view reference images (achieving high photorealism but limited applicability) or operate from a single image (scalable but with lower visual fidelity). No prior technique scales seamlessly from one to hundreds of reference images while maintaining consistently photorealistic results, and diffusion-based approaches face a fundamental obstacle to real-time rendering due to their iterative inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250315/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary number (1–100) of reference portrait images at any resolution, from which an off-the-shelf FLAME tracker estimates per-image 3DMM shape, pose, expression, and camera parameters. The MMDM generates 840 novel views at 512×512 with 250 DDIM steps (~4 hours on 4×RTX 6000), which are then used to fit a 3D Gaussian splatting avatar. The final avatar renders in real time at 768×768 (33 fps) for cross-reenactment or 1100×1604 (16 fps) for self-reenactment, driven by 3DMM pose and expression parameters.

## Method

CAP4D operates in two stages. First, a Morphable Multi-View Diffusion Model (MMDM) initialized from Stable Diffusion 2.1 is adapted with 3D attention (two spatial dimensions plus a cross-image dimension) to jointly denoise multiple latent images, conditioned on rasterized 3D pose maps, expression deformation maps, view-direction ray maps, and reference/generated binary masks. A key innovation is stochastic I/O conditioning: at each DDIM timestep, a random subset of reference images and a batch of generated images are sampled, allowing the model to condition on tens to hundreds of inputs and outputs despite supporting only 4 per forward pass, thereby producing hundreds of self-consistent novel views. Second, the generated and reference images are used to optimize a 3D Gaussian splatting representation (based on GaussianAvatars) with a U-Net predicting expression-dependent UV deformation maps, Laplacian and L2 regularizers, and a linearly-scheduled LPIPS loss, yielding a real-time animatable 4D avatar.

## Results

On self-reenactment from 10 reference images, CAP4D achieves PSNR 23.19, LPIPS 0.265, CSIM 0.779, and JOD 6.13, outperforming all baselines across every metric. With 100 reference images, CAP4D reaches PSNR 23.30, LPIPS 0.257, CSIM 0.792, and JOD 6.15, significantly surpassing FlashAvatar (LPIPS 0.313) and GaussianAvatars (LPIPS 0.416) in perceptual quality and identity preservation. In cross-reenactment, CAP4D achieves CSIM 0.634 and is preferred by 74–97% of human evaluators over all baselines across visual quality, expression transfer, 3D structure, and temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAP4D outperforms all single-image baselines (Voodoo3D, GAGAvatar, Real3D-Portrait, Portrait4D-v2) by a wide margin on every metric; for example, with one reference image it improves CSIM from 0.457 (GAGAvatar) to 0.633 and LPIPS from 0.373 to 0.311. In the multi-image setting (10 and 100 references), CAP4D dominates DiffusionRig, FlashAvatar, and GaussianAvatars on LPIPS and CSIM, with the largest gaps over FlashAvatar (LPIPS 0.456→0.265 at 10 refs) and GaussianAvatars (LPIPS 0.416→0.257 at 100 refs). FlashAvatar achieves the closest PSNR at 100 references (22.87 vs. 23.30) but produces visibly blurrier results, reflected in its higher LPIPS. In cross-reenactment, Real3D-Portrait edges out CAP4D on CSIM (0.647 vs. 0.634), yet human evaluators overwhelmingly prefer CAP4D (96% overall vs. Real3D, 97% vs. Voodoo3D, 85% vs. Portrait4D-v2, 74% vs. GAGAvatar), indicating that identity-embedding similarity does not fully capture perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAP4D sits at the intersection of multi-view diffusion models (CAT3D, MVDream) and real-time 4D avatar representations (GaussianAvatars, 3D Gaussian Splatting). It departs from CAT3D's static-scene generation by producing dynamic, expression-controllable avatars, and from Chen et al.'s Morphable Diffusion by decoupling generation from reconstruction: rather than directly inferring a 3D representation from a fixed set of generated views, CAP4D uses a stochastic I/O conditioning procedure (inspired by view synthesis and collaborative video diffusion) to generate hundreds of self-consistent views, then fits a separate animatable Gaussian splatting model. The 4D representation extends GaussianAvatars with a remeshed FLAME mesh and a U-Net predicting expression-dependent UV deformations, addressing fine-grained effects like wrinkles that blendshape-driven deformation alone cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying single-image, few-image, and multi-view avatar reconstruction under one framework with real-time rendering, CAP4D addresses a practical gap that has kept these settings in separate methodological silos. The stochastic I/O conditioning trick is a generalizable technique for scaling diffusion-based view synthesis beyond the fixed input/output counts of the underlying architecture. Key limitations remain: generation is slow (up to 8 hours), the 3DMM parameterization cannot model tongue or hair dynamics, and 360° coverage is limited by training data. Open directions include animation without 3DMMs, controllable relighting, and extension to full-body avatars.
