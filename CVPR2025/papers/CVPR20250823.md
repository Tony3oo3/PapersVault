## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (delta-tuning) methods for visual tasks consistently fall short of full fine-tuning on challenging dense prediction tasks such as instance and semantic segmentation. This paper asks whether an adapter-based tuning paradigm can actually surpass the full fine-tuning performance ceiling across all representative visual recognition tasks, not just simple classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250823/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images processed by Swin Transformer backbones (pre-trained on ImageNet-22k at 224×224) and produces task-specific outputs: class labels for image classification, axis-aligned bounding boxes for object detection, pixel-level masks for instance/semantic segmentation, and oriented bounding boxes for rotated object detection. Mona modules are inserted after both the multi-head self-attention and MLP sub-layers in every SwinBlock, with a fixed intermediate dimension of 64, resulting in roughly 2.5–5% trainable backbone parameters depending on model size.

## Method

Mona replaces the standard linear adapter with two key innovations: (1) a scaled LayerNorm at the input (x_norm = s₁·|x₀|_LN + s₂·x₀) that lets the adapter adapt to the fixed backbone's feature distribution, and (2) a multi-cognitive convolutional filter group consisting of three depth-wise convolutions with 3×3, 5×5, and 7×7 kernels whose outputs are averaged and then aggregated by a 1×1 point-wise convolution, mimicking multi-scale visual perception. Skip connections are added around both convolution stages and around the entire adapter. The overall computation is x = x₀ + U·σ(f_pw(f_dw(D(x_norm)))), where D and U are the down/up projections and σ is GeLU. By using depth-wise convolutions instead of standard convolutions, Mona keeps the additional parameter count low (83n for the multi-kernel DWConv layer) while providing vision-friendly, multi-scale feature processing that linear projections cannot capture.

## Results

On COCO instance segmentation (Swin-B + Cascade Mask R-CNN), Mona achieves 53.40% AP_box and 46.00% AP_mask, surpassing full fine-tuning (52.40% / 45.10%) by +1.00% and +0.90% respectively while training only 4.67% of backbone parameters. On Pascal VOC object detection (Swin-L + RetinaNet), Mona reaches 87.30% AP_box (+3.60% over full fine-tuning), and on ADE20K semantic segmentation it achieves 51.36% mIoU (+0.18% over full fine-tuning). Across three classification datasets (Flowers102, OxfordPets, VOC2007), Mona attains 94.04% average top-1 accuracy versus 92.79% for full fine-tuning. On oriented object detection (DOTA/STAR), Mona outperforms full fine-tuning by 0.13–1.05% AP across four detection frameworks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mona outperforms all seven baselines on every evaluated task. Against the closest adapter-based competitor, LoRand [20], Mona gains +1.50% AP_box and +1.30% AP_mask on COCO, +0.40% AP_box on VOC, +0.43% mIoU on ADE20K, and +0.82–2.49% AP on DOTA/STAR. The standard NLP Adapter [21] is the second-strongest baseline on COCO (52.10% AP_box) but still trails Mona by 1.30% AP_box; on VOC it reaches 86.70% vs. Mona's 87.30%. LoRA [36], despite its success in NLP, performs poorly on visual tasks—on COCO it scores only 50.40% AP_box (−2.00% vs. full fine-tuning), well below Mona's 53.40%. AdaptFormer [19] is competitive on classification (93.70% avg top-1 vs. Mona's 94.04%) but lags on dense prediction tasks. The parameter-only baselines (BitFit [39], NormTuning [40], Partial-1 [41]) consistently underperform adapter-based methods by 1.5–2.5% AP on COCO, confirming that architectural design matters more than raw parameter count for visual transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adapter-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm from NLP (Houlsby et al., ICML 2019) to visual recognition tasks, departing from prior visual adapters (AdaptFormer, LoRand, standard Adapter) that rely on linear projections inherited from language models. The key conceptual departure is the argument that visual signals, with their 2D spatial structure, require convolutional rather than purely linear processing within adapters. Mona introduces scaled LayerNorm for input distribution regulation and multi-scale depth-wise convolutional filters (3×3, 5×5, 7×7) to replace the standard down-projection–activation–up-projection pipeline, positioning itself as the first adapter-based method to consistently surpass full fine-tuning across diverse visual tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the long-standing assumption in CV that full fine-tuning represents the performance ceiling for transfer learning, with practical implications for the era of multi-billion-parameter vision models where storing full fine-tuned copies is prohibitive. The discussion of mobile deployment (one backbone + small task-specific adapters vs. multiple full models) highlights a concrete path toward efficient on-device multi-task inference. Open questions remain around the small inference overhead introduced by adapter modules, whether reparameterization-based approaches (like LoRA) can be combined with adapter designs to eliminate this cost, and whether the gains hold for even larger backbones (Swin-G, InternImage-H) or non-Swin architectures beyond the PVT test.
