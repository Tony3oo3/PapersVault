## Problem

End-to-end product poster generation faces two intertwined challenges: accurately rendering complex multilingual text (especially Chinese with 10,000+ characters and intricate stroke patterns) and preserving the exact appearance of the user-specified product subject. Existing one-stage methods rely on line-level visual features or glyph images that lack character-level discriminative power, while inpainting-based subject preservation often extends the foreground into new shapes, misleading customers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a subject image with its binary mask (indicating pixels to preserve), a set of text strings each with a bounding-box layout, and a free-form background scene prompt as input, and produces a single composited poster image where the subject is embedded in the generated scene and all text is rendered in-place. At inference, up to 7 text lines with 16 characters per line are supported, and the character-level OCR features are pre-extracted and stored in a lookup dictionary, eliminating online glyph rendering.

## Method

PosterMaker builds on SD3 with two ControlNet-like modules (cascaded MM-DiT blocks with zero-convolution injection). TextRenderNet receives a character-level visual representation—each character rendered as a glyph image, encoded by a frozen PPOCR OCR encoder, and avg-pooled into a single vector—concatenated with sinusoidal rank and bounding-box positional encodings, enabling precise stroke-level text rendering without additional alignment training. SceneGenNet performs background inpainting conditioned on the prompt and subject mask to place the product in a harmonious scene. To combat foreground extension, a HQ-SAM-based detector is trained on 20k annotated images and used as a reward model in a ReFL-style one-step feedback loop (L_reward) that penalizes the generator when the subject shape is altered. A two-stage training strategy decouples the objectives: Stage 1 freezes SceneGenNet (initialized from SD3 Inpainting-ControlNet) and trains TextRenderNet on local text editing; Stage 2 freezes TextRenderNet and trains SceneGenNet on full poster generation.

## Results

On the PosterBenchmark (500 samples), PosterMaker achieves 93.36% sentence accuracy (Sen. ACC) and 98.39% normalized edit distance (NED) for text rendering, approaching the ground-truth upper bound of 98.53% Sen. ACC. It also attains the best FID of 65.35 for visual quality, the highest CLIP-T score of 27.04 for text-image alignment, and the lowest foreground extension ratio of 11.57% for subject fidelity. The method outperforms all baselines by a substantial margin on text accuracy (e.g., +7.22% Sen. ACC over the strongest baseline GlyphDraw2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PosterMaker decisively outperforms all three primary baselines on every metric. Against AnyText (ICLR 2024), PosterMaker gains +29.46% Sen. ACC (93.36% vs. 63.90%) and +15.58% NED, while also reducing the foreground extension ratio from 19.25% to 11.57%. Against Glyph-ByT5-v2, the improvements are +23.82% Sen. ACC and +10.74% NED, with a 7.34-point reduction in FG Ext. Ratio. Against the strongest baseline GlyphDraw2, PosterMaker still leads by +7.22% Sen. ACC, +1.61% NED, and 4.95 points lower FG Ext. Ratio, while also achieving a better FID (65.35 vs. 72.49). The CLIP-T scores are nearly identical across all methods (26.60–27.04), indicating that text-image alignment is not a differentiating factor; the key advantage of PosterMaker lies in precise character-level text rendering and subject fidelity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PosterMaker builds on the SD3 diffusion backbone and the ControlNet architecture for conditional control, extending both into a dual-module design (TextRenderNet + SceneGenNet) for simultaneous text rendering and subject-preserved scene generation. Its key conceptual departure from prior multilingual text rendering work (AnyText, GlyphDraw2, GlyphByT5) is the argument that character-level OCR features—obtained by simply avg-pooling per-character glyph encodings—substitute for the more complex line-level features or contrastive alignment strategies those methods employ. For subject fidelity, the paper adapts the ReFL reward-learning paradigm from ImageReward but replaces the generic human-preference reward with a purpose-built foreground-extension detector grounded in HQ-SAM, a targeted repurposing rather than a new learning framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a plug-and-play character-level OCR representation (no additional training) closes most of the gap to the OCR upper bound for Chinese text rendering is broadly applicable to any T2I model needing accurate multilingual text, potentially simplifying pipelines that previously required expensive contrastive pre-training (as in GlyphByT5). The subject fidelity feedback learning addresses a practical failure mode (foreground extension) that prior inpainting-based poster methods acknowledged but did not systematically correct. Open questions include whether the character-level approach generalizes to other complex scripts (Arabic, Devanagari), whether the two-stage training can be unified into a single end-to-end objective, and whether the 160k-image training set is sufficient for robustness across diverse product categories and poster styles.
