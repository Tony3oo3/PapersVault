## Problem

Generating realistic 3D bimanual hand motions for articulated objects remains largely unsolved: existing methods either assume rigid objects, operate in a category-specific manner, require a known reference grasp or coarse hand trajectory, or cannot simultaneously handle object root motion and articulation. BimArt addresses this by providing a unified, category-agnostic generative framework that produces diverse and physically plausible bimanual interactions from object trajectories alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252761/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-part articulated object mesh and its N-frame 7-DoF trajectory (6D global state: 3D translation + 3D rotation, plus 1D articulation angle) as input. It outputs N frames of bimanual hand motion parameterized by MANO (61×2 per frame: 51 pose + 10 shape parameters per hand), intermediate distance-based contact maps per BPS point, and final 3D hand meshes after optimization-based refinement. The hand representation is object-centric, encoding surface keypoints and direction vectors to the nearest object vertices in the object's canonical frame.

## Method

BimArt employs a three-stage pipeline: (1) a denoising diffusion model with a transformer encoder generates frame-wise distance-based bimanual contact maps conditioned on a novel normalized part-based BPS object representation that maps the same basis point set independently to each articulated part, ensuring uniform spatial encoding resolution regardless of part size; (2) a second diffusion model synthesizes hand motion conditioned on both the object features and the generated contact maps, using classifier-free guidance and an additional contact-map discrepancy gradient term at each denoising step to enforce alignment; (3) an optimization-based post-processing step fits MANO parameters and minimizes penetration, floating, and acceleration losses to produce physically plausible final meshes. The key insight is that frame-wise contact maps embedded on the object serve as a rich intermediate prior that captures diverse grasping patterns without requiring a reference grasp, while the part-based BPS encoding enables a single unified model across all object categories.

## Results

On ARCTIC, BimArt achieves 2.03% penetration (vs. 30–67% for baselines), 99.63% contact rate, and 85.57% articulation consistency, while maintaining strong diversity (6.91 cm multi-modality). On HOI4D, the unified cross-category model reaches 0.044% penetration and 0.966 contact score on pliers, and 0.591% penetration with 1.000 contact on scissors, outperforming the unified CAMS-X baseline by large margins on articulation (0.597 vs. 0.015 on pliers; 0.853 vs. 0.167 on scissors). A perceptual user study with 55 respondents confirms statistically significant preference (p < 10⁻³) for BimArt over both MDM-B and OMOMO-B across all tested objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BimArt decisively outperforms MDM-B and OMOMO-B on all physical-plausibility metrics: penetration drops from 66.71% (MDM-B) and 30.44% (OMOMO-B) to 2.03%, while articulation consistency rises from 73.73% and 80.09% to 85.57%. Against CAMS-B, BimArt wins on penetration (2.03% vs. 42.52%), contact (99.63% vs. 98.92%), and articulation (85.57% vs. 76.70%), but CAMS-B retains an edge in multi-modality (8.56 vs. 6.91 cm) and acceleration (0.120 vs. 0.188 cm/s²), though its high penetration renders motions visually implausible. On HOI4D, the unified BimArt model surpasses the unified CAMS-X by a wide margin on articulation (0.597 vs. 0.015 on pliers; 0.853 vs. 0.167 on scissors) and contact (0.966 vs. 0.485 on pliers), while remaining competitive with category-specific methods like CAMS (0.004% pen, 1.000 con on pliers) and ManipNet. GraspTTA and ManipNet, both category-specific, show higher penetration (0.555% and 0.548% on pliers) than BimArt's unified model (0.044%), confirming the advantage of the part-aware BPS representation for cross-category generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

BimArt extends diffusion-based motion synthesis (MDM, OMOMO) from whole-body or rigid-object settings to bimanual interaction with articulated objects, while departing from prior articulated-object methods (CAMS, ArtiGrasp) that require category-specific training, reference grasps, or cannot handle simultaneous articulation and root motion. The object representation builds on Basis Point Sets (Prokudin et al.) but introduces a part-based, scale-normalized variant specifically designed to handle unbalanced articulated parts. The key architectural departure is the use of distance-based contact maps as a learned intermediate target between object trajectory and hand motion, replacing the stage-wise or sparse contact points used in prior HOI work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified, category-agnostic approach to bimanual articulated-object interaction that requires no reference grasp, no coarse hand trajectory, and no per-category training, lowering the barrier for 3D artists and animators. The contact-map-as-intermediate-representation design offers a reusable architectural pattern for decomposing complex interaction synthesis. However, the method is limited to two-part objects with 7 DoF and the specific categories in ARCTIC/HOI4D; generalization to open-vocabulary objects, more complex multi-joint articulation, and faster sampling (e.g., DDIM, latent diffusion) remain open challenges that the authors acknowledge.
