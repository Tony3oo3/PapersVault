## Problem

Existing radiology report generation methods rely on single or fixed-view images for the current visit, overlooking both subtle geometric differences across multiple views (PA, AP, lateral) and disease progression captured in longitudinal data. Even longitudinal approaches still characterize the current visit with a single image, and patient-specific prior knowledge (INDICATION, previous reports) is frequently missing, making flexible data utilization a key unsolved challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252681/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a variable number of current chest X-ray views (m_i may differ per visit), an optional previous frontal image, an optional previous radiology report, and an optional INDICATION string. Missing prior knowledge is handled via special absence tokens rather than requiring all inputs to be present. The output is the FINDINGS section of a radiology report, generated as a sequence of up to 100 tokens by a DistilGPT2 decoder.

## Method

MLRG is a two-stage framework. In Stage 1, a multi-view longitudinal contrastive learning objective jointly optimizes (i) a multi-positive contrastive loss that pulls together all views from the same visit while pushing apart other visits, using learnable view positional embeddings to encode inter-view differences, and (ii) a CLIP-style cross-modal alignment loss between spatiotemporal visual features and factual serialization of reports. A multi-view longitudinal fusion (MLF) network performs anchor-auxiliary cross-attention, treating one current view as query and the remaining current views plus the previous image as key/value, thereby integrating spatial and temporal information in a single forward pass. In Stage 2, a tokenized absence encoding scheme substitutes special tokens [NHI] and [NHPR] for missing INDICATION or previous report, and a multi-modal fusion network injects the available prior knowledge into the spatiotemporal features before a DistilGPT2 generator (warm-started from CGPT2) produces the final report.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, MLRG achieves BLEU-4 of 0.158, F1 RadGraph of 0.291, and F1 of 0.505, outperforming the best prior method (SEI) by +2.3% BLEU-4, +4.2% RadGraph, and +3.2% F1. On MIMIC-ABN, MLRG reaches F1 of 0.515 and F1 RadGraph of 0.219, a +5.5% F1 improvement over the best baseline (SEI, F1=0.435). On Two-view CXR, MLRG attains F1 RadGraph of 0.328 and F1 of 0.501, improving RadGraph by +2.7% over SEI. MLRG also outperforms LLM-based methods (B-LLM, Med-LLM) on all clinical efficacy metrics while maintaining competitive NLG scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLRG outperforms all 15 baselines on the majority of metrics across all three datasets. Against the strongest prior method SEI (MICCAI 2024), which also uses INDICATION, MLRG gains +2.3% BLEU-4 and +4.2% RadGraph on MIMIC-CXR, +5.5% F1 on MIMIC-ABN, and +2.7% RadGraph on Two-view CXR. Compared to the LLM-based B-LLM (AAAI 2024), MLRG achieves higher BLEU-4 (0.158 vs 0.128) and F1 (0.505 vs 0.473), though B-LLM has slightly higher Recall (0.482 vs 0.468). Against the longitudinal-data method HERGen (ECCV 2024), MLRG improves BLEU-4 by 0.036 and RadGraph is reported only for MLRG (0.291), confirming that multi-view integration surpasses single-image longitudinal tracking. Versus CXRMate (arXiv 2023), which also uses multi-view longitudinal data, MLRG leads by +0.050 BLEU-4 and +0.053 RadGraph on MIMIC-CXR, demonstrating that contrastive pre-training with spatiotemporal supervision is more effective than report-synthesis-based fusion. On MIMIC-ABN, MLRG's F1 of 0.515 exceeds the next-best CMN (0.460) by 5.5 percentage points, the largest relative gain among all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

MLRG extends the radiology report generation (RRG) pipeline by unifying three previously separate input modalities—current multi-view images, longitudinal (previous-visit) data, and patient-specific prior knowledge (INDICATION, previous report)—into a single contrastive pre-training and generation framework. It builds directly on the authors' own prior work on factual serialization [30] and SEI [29] for text encoding and INDICATION incorporation, and adopts the multi-positive contrastive loss formulation from StableRep [46] to enforce intra-visit visual consistency. Conceptually, it departs from dual-view methods (which only distinguish frontal vs. lateral) and from longitudinal methods like HERGen [49] and CXRMate [36] that still rely on a single image for the current visit, by treating the current visit as a variable-size multi-view set and fusing it with temporal context via a dedicated MLF network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real radiologists interpret multiple views and prior history simultaneously, yet most RRG systems process a single image. The tokenized absence encoding for missing INDICATION/previous reports is a pragmatic design choice that makes the model deployable in settings with incomplete records, and the multi-view longitudinal fusion network handles variable view counts without architectural changes. Limitations include reliance on a relatively small DistilGPT2 generator (rather than an LLM), evaluation confined to chest X-rays, and the absence of uncertainty quantification. Future directions noted by the authors—saliency-map-based region features and uncertainty prediction—suggest the framework could be extended to other modalities and to more trustworthy clinical deployment.
