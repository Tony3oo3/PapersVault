## Problem

Video-to-speech synthesis suffers from a substantial modality gap: silent video contains only visual cues (lip motion, facial identity, expressions) while speech encompasses rich acoustic properties (content, timbre, prosody), making accurate visual-to-acoustic mapping difficult. Existing VTS systems, despite leveraging diffusion models and speaker embeddings, still produce speech that is perceptually far from natural human utterances, indicating that a more structured training pipeline is needed to bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

The system takes a silent talking face video at 25 fps, from which face crops are extracted via RetinaFace and lip frames via FAN landmarks (converted to grayscale); 96 consecutive frames are used per training sample. The output is a 16 kHz speech waveform generated from an 80-bin log-scale mel-spectrogram (hop size 320, window 1280) at a fixed 1:2 video-to-audio length ratio, rendered by a pretrained HiFi-GAN vocoder. Notably, the flow matching decoder achieves near-identical quality with only 10 sampling steps versus 1000, enabling efficient inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical visual encoder that progressively transforms video representations into acoustic feature space across three stages—content (lip motions encoded by AV-HuBERT with learnable weighted summation across layers), timbre (face identity via ArcFace), and prosody (facial expressions via a pre-trained encoder)—with Transformer-based mappers (C2T, T2P) connecting each stage. Each stage includes a dedicated predictor (content uses quantized HuBERT speech units with masked convolution; timbre uses time-averaged HuBERT features; prosody uses pYIN pitch and mel-spectrogram energy) trained with teacher forcing to align visual cues with their acoustic counterparts. The resulting visual encoding μ conditions an OT-CFM flow matching decoder (U-Net with residual 1D convolutions and Transformer blocks with snake activation) that estimates direct linear trajectories from a Gaussian prior to the target mel-spectrogram distribution. Classifier-free guidance (β=0.7) is applied during inference via an Euler solver, and the total loss combines the OT-CFM objective, a negative log-likelihood encoder loss, and weighted attribute prediction losses (λ=0.5 each).

## Results

On LRS3-TED, the proposed method (10 sampling steps) achieves a naturalness MOS of 4.49±0.11, only 0.05 below ground truth (4.54±0.12), and an intelligibility MOS of 4.01±0.15. Objectively, it reaches UTMOS 4.031 and DNSMOS 2.789 on LRS3, both exceeding ground truth values (3.545 and 2.582), while achieving WER 30.45 and RMSE_f0 39.013. On the unseen LRS2-BBC dataset, the method generalizes well with UTMOS 3.921 and DNSMOS 2.586, again surpassing ground truth. Notably, performance with 10 steps is nearly identical to 1000 steps, confirming efficient inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method substantially outperforms all four baselines on both LRS3 and LRS2. On LRS3, it achieves UTMOS 4.031 vs. DiffV2S (3.058), Intelligible (2.702), LTBS (2.417), and SVTS (1.283) — a margin of roughly 1.0 over the strongest baseline. On WER, the method (30.45) is competitive with Intelligible (29.60) but far surpasses DiffV2S (41.07), LTBS (84.08), and SVTS (84.98). On the cross-dataset LRS2 evaluation, the gap widens: Ours achieves UTMOS 3.921 vs. DiffV2S 2.945 and Intelligible 2.331, while WER drops to 39.37 vs. 54.86 (DiffV2S) and 94.25 (LTBS). In subjective MOS, the method's naturalness score of 4.49 exceeds the best baseline DiffV2S (2.97) by 1.52 points, approaching ground truth (4.54). The method also achieves the best speaker similarity (GE2E 0.650, VoxSim 0.495) among all video-driven approaches, confirming that its hierarchical face-to-voice alignment captures speaker identity more faithfully than DiffV2S (GE2E 0.621) or LTBS (GE2E 0.609).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VTS lineage from diffusion-based approaches (DiffV2S, LipVoicer) and GAN-based systems by replacing the generative decoder with an optimal-transport conditional flow matching model, while introducing a three-stage hierarchical visual encoder that explicitly aligns lip motion, face identity, and facial expressions with content, timbre, and prosody respectively. The hierarchical decomposition draws on prior work in hierarchical TTS (PVAE-TTS, HierVST) and cross-modal biometric matching (Nagrani et al.), but applies it to the video-to-speech setting for the first time with dedicated per-stage predictors. The flow matching decoder follows the Matcha-TTS architecture and the OT-CFM formulation from Lipman et al., adapting them to the conditional VTS setting with classifier-free guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.05 MOS gap to ground truth naturalness and the near-identical quality at 10 vs. 1000 sampling steps make this one of the strongest VTS systems to date, with practical implications for real-time dubbing and assistive speech technologies. However, the WER of ~30% (vs. 2.3% for ground truth) reveals that intelligibility still lags significantly, and the system is evaluated only on English in-the-wild datasets (LRS3, LRS2), leaving open questions about multilingual generalization, longer-form generation, and robustness to occlusion or extreme expressions. The hierarchical design also raises the question of whether the content→timbre→prosody ordering is universally optimal or dataset-dependent.
