## Problem

Existing sewing pattern prediction methods are single-modal, each designed for one specific input type (3D points, images, or language), making them difficult to extend to combined or novel modalities. Two key barriers prevent scaling to multimodal garment generation: no large-scale multimodal sewing pattern dataset exists, and complex sewing patterns cannot be efficiently represented within the limited context length of large multimodal models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251352/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AIpparel accepts arbitrary combinations of text prompts, garment images, and existing sewing patterns (with editing instructions) as input, and outputs a complete sewing pattern structured as N 2D panels in 3D with stitching information, directly usable for cloth simulation. The sewing pattern is encoded as a compact token sequence averaging ~250 tokens (max 838) per garment, where discrete structural elements (panel names, edge types, stitching tags) are predicted autoregressively while continuous geometric parameters (vertex coordinates, Bézier control points, 3D rigid transformations) are regressed via dedicated MLP heads rather than quantized into tokens.

## Method

AIpparel fine-tunes LLaVA 1.5-7B on the newly curated GCD-MM dataset (120k+ garments with text, image, and editing annotations) using a novel tokenization scheme that represents sewing patterns as a sequence of drawing commands with special tokens for garment/panel boundaries, edge types, and stitching tags—reducing token count by ~100× compared to prior schemes. Continuous parameters are handled through a mixed training objective: cross-entropy loss on discrete tokens combined with L2 regression loss on small MLP heads that predict vertex positions, edge control points, and 3D panel transformations from the transformer's hidden states. Positional embeddings inject ground-truth (at training) or predicted (at inference) continuous parameters into the token embeddings so the language model is conditioned on geometric context. The vision encoder and projection layer remain frozen; only the language model weights, regression heads, and positional embedding projection layers are updated during fine-tuning.

## Results

On the GCD-MM image-to-garment task, AIpparel achieves a Panel L2 of 5.4 cm and 85.2% panel accuracy, substantially outperforming the fine-tuned SewFormer-FT baseline (12.3 cm Panel L2, 79.4% panel accuracy). On the multimodal sewing pattern prediction benchmark, AIpparel reaches 59.0% accuracy and 6.1 cm Panel L2, compared to 10.3% and 22.4 cm for SewFormer-FT† and 0.6% and 31.0 cm for DressCode†. For language-driven sewing pattern editing, AIpparel attains 83.4% accuracy and 1.5 cm Panel L2, versus 9.5%/18.6 cm (SewFormer*) and 37.0%/13.7 cm (DressCode*). The tokenizer also yields a 25× inference speedup over DressCode (2.1 s vs 52.2 s per garment).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIpparel consistently outperforms both baselines across all evaluated tasks. Against SewFormer (Liu et al., 2023), AIpparel wins on every metric in both the SewFactory and GCD-MM image-to-garment settings; the gap is especially pronounced on the more complex GCD-MM dataset, where SewFormer-FT drops to 44.7% edge accuracy and 2.8% stitch accuracy while AIpparel maintains 82.7% and 77.2% respectively. Against DressCode (He et al., 2024), the advantage is even more dramatic in the multimodal and editing tasks: on multimodal prediction, AIpparel achieves 59.0% accuracy versus DressCode†'s 0.6%, and on editing, 83.4% versus 37.0%. The baselines rely on external LMMs (GPT-4o, DALL-E, InstructPix2Pix) to translate multimodal inputs into their single-modality domains, which introduces information loss; AIpparel handles all modalities natively within a single model. In the ablation (Table 5), AIpparel's tokenizer also surpasses DressCode's tokenizer in reconstruction quality (85.0% vs 38.4% accuracy) while being 25× faster at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA large multimodal model paradigm to the domain of sewing pattern generation, building directly on LLaVA 1.5-7B as the base architecture and on GarmentCodeData (GCD) as the underlying pattern representation and dataset. It departs from prior sewing pattern methods (SewFormer, DressCode, SewFactory) that use task-specific architectures trained on single modalities, instead fine-tuning a 7B-parameter LMM with a novel tokenization scheme inspired by vector graphics generation (DeepSVG) and regression heads for continuous parameters borrowed from recent LMM extension work (ChatPose, LISA). The key conceptual shift is treating sewing patterns as a new output modality for an existing multimodal model rather than designing a dedicated network per input modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIpparel is the first multimodal foundation model for sewing patterns, enabling previously impossible tasks like language-instructed pattern editing and joint text+image conditioning, and the GCD-MM dataset (120k+ garments with text, image, and edit annotations) fills a critical data gap for the field. The 100× token compression (from 30k to ~250 tokens) makes LMM-based pattern generation computationally feasible, and the 25× inference speedup over DressCode has direct practical implications. Open questions include extending the representation to non-manifold structures (pockets, zippers), incorporating physical and material constraints for fabrication-ready output, and whether the approach generalizes beyond procedurally generated patterns to real-world designer garments.
