## Problem

Generating animatable 3D human avatars from a single image remains challenging: parametric-model-based methods struggle to capture fine clothing and texture details, while diffusion-based animation approaches suffer from viewpoint inconsistencies at extreme poses and require minutes per frame. A key unresolved issue is that multi-view images generated by diffusion models contain subtle inter-view inconsistencies that degrade standard 3D reconstruction, and prior methods rely on synthetic rigged human datasets that limit generalization to in-the-wild appearances.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a person in an arbitrary pose as input and produces an animatable 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar in a canonical SMPL-X-aligned pose, along with intermediate multi-view canonical RGB images and normal maps (30 frames). The full pipeline runs in approximately 10 minutes on a single RTX-3090 GPU (~5 min for multi-view generation, ~5 min for 4DGS optimization), after which the avatar supports real-time rendering and animation driven by SMPL-X pose parameters. The method currently requires full-body input and does not handle half-body images.

## Method

The approach has two stages. First, a CogVideo-based diffusion transformer is adapted for reference-image-guided, SMPL-X-pose-guided multi-view canonical image and normal map generation: the reference image is concatenated along the frame channel with noisy latents for identity preservation, a lightweight pose-guidance network (inspired by CHAMP) injects SMPL-X normal features into the denoising process, and a dual-branch architecture (splitting the first/last three DiT blocks into RGB and normal paths with shared middle blocks and multi-modal attention) jointly produces both modalities. The model is pretrained on 100K in-the-wild human videos (with pseudo normals from Sapiens and pseudo poses from Multi-HMR) before fine-tuning on synthetic data. Second, the 3D reconstruction from the inherently inconsistent generated views is recast as a 4D reconstruction problem: a 4D Gaussian Splatting model with a HexPlane+MLP deformation field encodes per-view shape and color variations, and after optimization the canonical-space Gaussians (t=0) yield the final multi-view-consistent 3D avatar. Shape regularization via rendered-normal L1 loss and an anisotropy penalty, combined with coarse-mesh point initialization from a deformed SMPL-X fit, further improve reconstruction fidelity.

## Results

On the Human4DiT synthetic dataset (50 rigged avatars), AniGS achieves the best scores across all 3D reconstruction metrics: LPIPS 0.1085, CLIP score 90.370, FID 77.879, and a user-study ranking of 4.199/5, outperforming the next-best method (CharacterGen) by 0.055 in LPIPS and 10.88 in FID. For animation quality, AniGS reaches PSNR 21.475, SSIM 0.857, and LPIPS 0.137, surpassing the second-best En3D by +2.231 PSNR and +0.106 SSIM. In multi-view canonical image generation, AniGS attains PSNR 23.125 and Normal Loss 0.101, outperforming CHAMP (PSNR 20.025) while matching MagicMan+ on PSNR (23.775) with lower LPIPS (0.1023 vs 0.1077).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AniGS outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest 3D reconstruction competitor CharacterGen [53], AniGS reduces LPIPS from 0.1638 to 0.1085 (−34%) and FID from 88.751 to 77.879, while also winning the user study by a wide margin (4.199 vs 2.481 out of 5). For animation, AniGS beats En3D [48] by +2.231 PSNR and +0.106 SSIM, and beats CharacterGen by +3.905 PSNR and +0.213 SSIM. In multi-view generation (Table 1), AniGS clearly surpasses CHAMP [90] (PSNR 23.125 vs 20.025, LPIPS 0.1023 vs 0.1458) and achieves lower normal-map error than MagicMan+ [21] (0.101 vs 0.134), though MagicMan+ edges out AniGS on PSNR alone (23.775 vs 23.125) — a metric the authors note is less relevant since MagicMan cannot produce canonical-pose images suitable for animation. Static reconstruction methods SiTH [86] and LGM [73] are further behind, with FID scores of 86.895 and 82.121 respectively, confirming that the generative multi-view + 4DGS pipeline yields substantially more faithful canonical avatars.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of video diffusion models and 3D Gaussian Splatting, adapting the CogVideo/CogVideoX transformer architecture for reference-guided multi-view canonical human image generation (building on CHAMP's SMPL-X normal guidance) and extending 3DGS to a 4D formulation (building on HexPlane-based deformation fields) to handle multi-view inconsistencies. It departs from CharacterGen's approach of training a dedicated transformer reconstruction model by instead using per-scene 4DGS optimization, and from prior multi-view generation methods by pretraining on in-the-wild video data rather than requiring synthetic rigged human datasets. The core conceptual move is reframing spatial multi-view inconsistency as a temporal (4D) problem, allowing the deformation module to absorb per-view appearance variations while the canonical space retains a consistent 3D geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables photorealistic, real-time animation of 3D human avatars from a single in-the-wild image, addressing a key practical bottleneck where diffusion-based animation methods require minutes per frame and suffer from viewpoint inconsistency at extreme poses. The 4DGS-based inconsistency handling is a reusable technique applicable to any pipeline that generates imperfect multi-view inputs. However, the ~10-minute per-avatar optimization time and inability to handle half-body inputs limit immediate deployment, and the authors identify feed-forward 3D reconstruction as the natural next step to eliminate the per-scene optimization entirely.
