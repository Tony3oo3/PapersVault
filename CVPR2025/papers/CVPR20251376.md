## Problem

Self-supervised visual foundation models like DINOv2 produce powerful features but lack a language interface, preventing their use in open-vocabulary tasks. While LiT (Locked-image text tuning) aligns a text encoder to a frozen vision model at low cost, it yields unsatisfactory performance on dense tasks such as semantic segmentation and image-text retrieval, because the CLIP-style contrastive loss only supervises global representations and the frozen encoder cannot adapt to the new training data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251376/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (224×224 or 336×336) and a set of text queries (e.g., class names or captions). For classification and retrieval, it produces a 2D-dimensional global image descriptor (concatenation of the [CLS] token and average-pooled patch tokens) compared via cosine similarity to text embeddings. For open-vocabulary segmentation, it outputs per-patch D-dimensional features that are directly compared to the patch-aligned portion of the text embedding and upsampled to pixel resolution, optionally using a high-resolution sliding-window protocol with multi-scale crops (1%, 10%, 100% area) and k-means clustering (k=32) over ~800 crops per image.

## Method

dino.txt freezes a DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone and adds two learnable transformer blocks on top to bridge the domain gap between SSL pre-training data and the image-text training set. The key architectural choice is concatenating the [CLS] token with the average-pooled patch tokens to form a 2D-dimensional image descriptor, which simultaneously enforces global alignment (for classification) and local alignment (for segmentation) under a single CLIP contrastive loss. A BERT-based text encoder is trained from scratch to align with this descriptor. Training data is curated from 2.3B CommonCrawl pairs by intersecting text-based balancing (WordNet/Wikipedia frequency suppression) with image-based balancing (3-level hierarchical k-means clustering with 20M/800K/80K centroids), yielding a balanced 650M-pair per-epoch subset. This combined curation, larger batch size (65K), and the dual-token representation allow the model to reach 81.4% IN1K zero-shot accuracy and 20.6 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation in just 50K iterations (~19 hours on 128 A100s), outperforming CLIP trained for 110 hours.

## Results

dino.txt achieves 81.4% zero-shot accuracy on ImageNet-1K (224px) and 81.6% (336px), setting state-of-the-art on ImageNet-v2 (75.7%), ImageNet-Adversarial (80.0%), and iNaturalist2021 (18.8%). For open-vocabulary segmentation, it reaches 20.6 mIoU on ADE20K and 32.1 on Cityscapes, substantially outperforming all generalist image-text encoders (best prior: 12.7 ADE for OpenCLIP B/16) and even surpassing segmentation-specialized methods like CoDe (17.7 ADE). With a high-resolution sliding-window inference procedure, segmentation improves to 25.1 mIoU on ADE20K and 41.0 on Cityscapes. Training requires only 19 hours on 128 A100 GPUs versus 110 hours for CLIP to reach a lower 79.0% on IN1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

dino.txt outperforms CLIP [67] on every classification benchmark (e.g., 81.4 vs 76.6 IN1K, 18.8 vs 5.7 iNat21) and on both retrieval tasks (45.4 vs 37.1 COCO, 77.1 vs 67.3 Flickr), while requiring only a fraction of the training compute. Against the strongest generalist competitors, dino.txt (336px) beats SigLIP [98] on IN-A (83.2 vs 76.4) and iNat21 (19.4 vs 17.5) but trails on COCO retrieval (44.9 vs 52.7) and Flickr (77.6 vs 81.9), which the authors attribute to the frozen vision encoder limiting text encoder quality. It matches DFN [30] on IN1K (81.4) but wins decisively on IN-A (80.0 vs 66.8) and iNat21 (18.8 vs 17.6). On open-vocabulary segmentation, dino.txt (20.6 ADE, 32.1 City.) surpasses all generalist encoders by a wide margin (next best: OpenCLIP B/16 at 12.7 ADE, 20.2 City.) and also exceeds segmentation-specialized methods CoDe [88] (17.7 ADE) and TCL [13] (14.9 ADE) on ADE20K, though it trails GViT [94] (79.7) and TCL (77.5) on VOC20, which the authors link to a domain gap in that dataset's close-up object-centric annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LiT (Locked-image Text Tuning) paradigm by applying it to the DINOv2 self-supervised backbone, departing from standard LiT in three key ways: concatenating the [CLS] token with average-pooled patch embeddings to jointly optimize global and dense alignment, adding two learnable vision blocks to bridge the domain gap between SSL pre-training and caption data, and combining text-based (WordNet/Wikipedia) and image-based (hierarchical k-means) data curation. It positions itself against CLIP-style training from scratch, arguing that given strong frozen SSL encoders, one should achieve better vision-language alignment at a fraction of the computational cost. The data curation pipeline builds directly on Xu et al. (MetaCLIP) for text balancing and Vo et al. for visual concept balancing, taking their intersection rather than applying either in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: dino.txt reaches 81.4% IN1K zero-shot accuracy in 19 hours on 128 A100s versus 110 hours for CLIP to reach 79.0%, and achieves 20.6 mIoU on ADE20K open-vocabulary segmentation—substantially outperforming generalist CLIP-like models—without any task-specific adaptation. The error analysis reveals that standard segmentation benchmarks are poorly suited for open-vocabulary evaluation due to overlapping annotations and misaligned class names, an important caveat for the field. A key open question is the quality ceiling of the text encoder when the vision backbone is frozen (it underperforms CLIP's text encoder by 4.2% on MTEB), suggesting that a better trade-off between preserving SSL features and allowing adaptation to new data domains remains to be found.
