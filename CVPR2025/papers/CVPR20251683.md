## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces poor geometry in indoor scenes because textureless surfaces (walls, floors, ceilings) admit many geometry-appearance combinations, causing the optimizer to converge to local optima with floaters and holes. Existing 3DGS geometry improvements still struggle in these weakly textured settings, and the standard SfM-based initialization is sparse and noisy precisely where it is most needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251683/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images, SfM sparse points, monocular depth (Metric3D), and monocular normal maps, and additionally extracts 2D line segments from both RGB and normal maps via DeepLSD and segments weakly textured regions via SAM. It outputs three typed sets of 3D Gaussian primitives (line, plane-like, SfM), a 3D mesh extracted via TSDF + Marching Cubes, and novel-view RGB/depth/normal renderings. A notable detail is that monocular depth is scale-aligned per image via linear regression against 3D line and SfM cues before back-projection, since raw monocular depth lacks absolute scale consistency.

## Method

IndoorGS extracts three complementary geometric cues—3D line segments (matched across views via GlueStick), SOR-filtered SfM points, and plane-like point clouds (back-projected from SAM-segmented weakly textured regions using scale-aligned monocular depth, then filtered by proximity to 3D lines)—and uses them to initialize and guide 3DGS. Gaussians are classified into line (G_L), SfM (G_M), and plane-like (G_S) types, each receiving a distinct adaptive density control strategy: G_L undergoes tangent-space densification (flattened via a min-scale L1 loss, normal-aligned to monocular normals, with cloning/splitting projected onto the tangent plane to suppress floaters); G_M uses standard ADC but is deprioritized (paused between iterations 700–3500) so line Gaussians can establish structure first; G_S disables splitting and cloning entirely, relying only on parameter optimization and pruning. Training is supervised by photometric loss plus four geometric regularizers: a patch-wise Pearson-correlation depth loss, a normal-alignment loss against monocular normals, the min-scale flattening loss, and a depth-gradient normal-consistency loss.

## Results

IndoorGS achieves the best average PSNR of 33.88 dB, SSIM of 0.939, and LPIPS of 0.146 across Replica, ScanNet++, and Deep Blending, outperforming the second-best RaDeGS (33.53 dB) by 0.35 dB in PSNR. For mesh reconstruction, IndoorGS attains the lowest Chamfer distance (C-L1 = 0.038), highest F-score (0.801), and best Normal Consistency (0.909) on average, substantially surpassing PGSR (C-L1 = 0.064, F1 = 0.722, NC = 0.837) and RaDeGS (C-L1 = 0.087, F1 = 0.549, NC = 0.811). Training time is 35 minutes, only 10 minutes longer than PGSR (25 min) while being far faster than GOF (2 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IndoorGS outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against vanilla 3DGS, it gains +1.09 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.172 to 0.146 on average, with the largest gap on Deep Blending (+0.65 dB PSNR). Compared to RaDeGS (its direct baseline for depth/normal rasterization), IndoorGS improves PSNR by 0.35 dB and LPIPS by 0.009, with the most notable gain on ScanNet++ (+0.37 dB PSNR, LPIPS 0.131 vs 0.147). Against PGSR, the strongest surface-reconstruction competitor, IndoorGS leads by 0.80 dB PSNR and 0.024 LPIPS on average, and dominates mesh metrics by a wide margin (C-L1 0.038 vs 0.064, F1 0.801 vs 0.722, NC 0.909 vs 0.837). GOF, while competitive on Replica rendering (40.98 vs 41.11 PSNR), lags significantly on ScanNet++ and Deep Blending and requires 2 hours of training versus 35 minutes for IndoorGS.

## Relation to Prior Work

IndoorGS builds directly on the 3DGS framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) and its depth/normal rasterization extension RaDeGS (Zhang et al., 2024), which it uses as the baseline. For geometric cue extraction, it follows the 3D line mapping pipeline of LIMAP (Liu et al., CVPR 2023) using DeepLSD and GlueStick, and adapts the tangent-space densification idea from GeoGS (Li et al., 2024) with a key modification: instead of relying on known normals from PlanarSLAM, it infers normals by flattening Gaussian primitives via a scale-minimization loss. The paper departs from prior 3DGS geometry-improvement work (GOF, PGSR, SUGAR) by introducing a divide-and-conquer ADC strategy that treats line, plane-like, and SfM Gaussians with fundamentally different densification rules, rather than applying a uniform densification policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized weakness of 3DGS in textureless indoor environments by exploiting the abundant line and plane structure inherent to built spaces, yielding meaningful gains in both mesh accuracy (C-L1 reduced from 0.064 to 0.038 vs. PGSR) and rendering quality. A practical limitation is the heavy reliance on multiple external foundation models (Metric3D for depth/normals, SAM for segmentation, DeepLSD for line detection), which adds pipeline complexity and potential failure modes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Manhattan or outdoor scenes, whether the multi-model dependency can be replaced by a single learned prior, and whether the type-specific ADC strategy transfers to other Gaussian-based reconstruction settings beyond indoor scenes.
