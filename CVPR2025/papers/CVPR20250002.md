## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods require access to the original training data (or at least the remaining subset) to compute the Hessian needed for parameter updates, an assumption that fails in practical source-free settings where data is no longer stored. Prior zero-shot unlearning approaches either forget entire classes rather than arbitrary instances, degrade sharply as the number of forgotten samples grows, or lack formal guarantees on forgetting completeness. This paper addresses the gap by enabling instance-level unlearning without any access to the retained data while providing provable bounds on unlearning quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the trained model weights w* and the forget data D_f (up to 20% of training samples in experiments); the remaining data D_r is never accessed. It outputs updated model parameters w_uf via a single Newton-style step and an estimated retain Hessian Ĥ_r obtained by solving a semi-definite program. For neural network experiments, the input is a ResNet-18 with its last few layers linearized (mixed-linear formulation), and the forget data consists of RGB images from CIFAR-10/100, Stanford Dogs, or CalTech-256.

## Method

The core idea is to estimate the Hessian of the unseen remaining data by exploiting a second-order Taylor expansion around the optimal point w*: for small perturbations δw, the loss difference δL_r on retained data is approximated by δL_f on forget data, since both are upper-bounded by L‖δw‖. Concretely, m random Gaussian perturbations are generated, and an SDP (minimize the mean squared residual subject to X ⪰ 0) is solved to recover the retain Hessian Ĥ_r. The unlearned model is then obtained via the Newton update w_uf = w* + Ĥ_r^{-1}∇_f + σ²ε. Lemma 1 bounds the Frobenius norm of the Hessian estimation error by 2ε√d/(2+d), and Theorem 1 translates this into a gradient-norm bound on the unlearned model over the remaining data, showing the bound tightens with larger regularization λ, more remaining samples, and higher dimensionality d.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 10% forget data, the proposed Unlearned(-) method achieves 70% test accuracy, 71% remaining-data accuracy, 68% forget-data accuracy, and 51.4% MIA score, closely matching the Retrained baseline (72%/74%/72%/50%). Across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Stanford Dogs, CalTech-256), the performance gap between Unlearned(-) and the ideal Retrained model is within 2–4% on test and remaining accuracy. The method also extends to mixed linear networks (ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100), where Unlearned(-) achieves 60–62.8% test accuracy versus 62.2–63.7% for Retrained, with MIA scores within 1–2% of the 50% target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Unlearned(-) method decisively outperforms all four source-free baselines on CIFAR-10. Against NegGrad [12] (51.9% test) and JiT [9] (52.1% test), the proposed method gains approximately 18 percentage points in test accuracy and 18 points on remaining-data accuracy. The gap is even more dramatic versus Random Labels [12] (20.6% test), where the proposed method achieves nearly 3× the accuracy. The Adversarial method [3] (51.5% test) also trails by ~18.5 points. In terms of MIA scores, all baselines hover near 47–50%, while the proposed method reaches 51.4%, indicating comparable or slightly better privacy guarantees. The key distinction is that the proposed method is the only one that maintains near-Retrained performance (within 2–4%) while also providing formal theoretical guarantees on unlearning quality, which none of the compared baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Newton-step unlearning framework of Guo et al. [15], which requires the Hessian of the remaining data, by introducing a Semi-Definite Program that estimates this Hessian using only the forget data and the trained model weights. It also builds on the Mixed Linear Unlearning formulation of Golatkar et al. [13] to extend applicability to neural networks via NTK linearization. The key departure from prior zero-shot unlearning work [5, 3] is that those methods either forget entire classes or suffer severe scalability degradation at the instance level, whereas this paper provides instance-level forgetting with formal error bounds on the Hessian approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source-free unlearning setting is practically critical: in real deployments, original training data is often unavailable due to storage costs or privacy regulations, yet GDPR-style 'right to be forgotten' requests still require verifiable data removal. The theoretical guarantees (Frobenius-norm bound on Hessian error, gradient-norm bound on the unlearned model) provide the first formal assurance of unlearning completeness without source data access. However, the method is restricted to linear and mixed-linear classifiers, and the SDP formulation becomes computationally intractable for high-dimensional Hessians, leaving open the question of whether these guarantees can be extended to fully non-linear deep networks without linearization.
