## Problem

Existing part-level dynamics methods like Puppet-Master rely on fine-tuning large 2D video diffusion models, producing only single-view outputs that require separate (error-prone) 3D reconstruction and take minutes per drag interaction. This makes them impractical for real-time 3D simulation and robotics, where multi-view-consistent 3D representations and fast inference are essential. Additionally, the scarcity of 4D part-level motion data and the risk of catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning pretrained 3D reconstruction models remain unresolved challenges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250151/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image and a 2D drag specified by start and end pixel coordinates. The single drag is first propagated to multiple points on the segmented part (via SAM) to form a set of propagated drags sharing the same displacement vector. Multi-view images are generated from the single input using a fine-tuned Zero123++ model before being fed into the network. The output is a full 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (14-dimensional parameters per Gaussian) of the object in its post-drag state, renderable from arbitrary viewpoints at 256×256 resolution.

## Method

PartRM builds on the pretrained LGM asymmetric U-Net backbone and introduces three key components. First, a drag propagation module uses SAM to segment the target part and distributes the input drag across multiple sampled points on that part, reducing ambiguity. Second, a multi-scale drag embedding module encodes each drag's start and end points via Fourier features and a 3-layer MLP, scatters them into spatial drag maps at three resolutions (128×128, 32×32, 8×8), and concatenates each map to the corresponding U-Net down block with zero-initialized convolutions. Third, a two-stage training pipeline prevents catastrophic forgetting: Stage 1 performs motion learning via knowledge distillation, applying L2 loss on 14-dimensional Gaussian parameters in splatter-image space using the pretrained LGM's output as teacher; Stage 2 jointly optimizes appearance, geometry, and motion using photometric losses (MSE + LPIPS + alpha MSE) on rendered novel views. The approach is trained on the newly introduced PartDrag-4D dataset (20,548 multi-view states from 738 articulated meshes) and achieves 4.2s inference versus 64–245s for baselines.

## Results

On PartDrag-4D, PartRM achieves a PSNR of 28.15, SSIM of 0.9531, and LPIPS of 0.0356, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On Objaverse-Animation-HQ, PartRM reaches PSNR 21.38, SSIM 0.9209, and LPIPS 0.0758. Inference time is 4.2 seconds per sample, compared to 245.8–361.5 seconds for Puppet-Master and 8.5–139.7 seconds for DragAPart, reflecting PartRM's feed-forward 4D reconstruction that eliminates separate 3D optimization steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartRM outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. On PartDrag-4D, PartRM's PSNR of 28.15 exceeds DragAPart (24.91), Puppet-Master (24.42), and DiffEditor (22.34) by 3.2, 3.7, and 5.8 dB respectively, with LPIPS reduced from 0.0528–0.0918 to 0.0356. On Objaverse-Animation-HQ, PartRM leads DragAPart by 1.94 dB in PSNR and DiffEditor by 1.92 dB. The speed advantage is dramatic: PartRM's 4.2 s inference is roughly 58× faster than Puppet-Master's 245.8 s (LGM) and 86× faster than its 361.5 s (optimization-based) pipeline. Qualitatively, baselines produce multi-view inconsistencies and unnatural artifacts (e.g., DiffEditor fails part-level deformation; DragAPart produces distorted microwave doors; Puppet-Master lacks precise motion accuracy), whereas PartRM maintains both temporal and multi-view coherence under varying drag strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartRM departs from the dominant line of drag-conditioned generation (Puppet-Master, DragAPart, DiffEditor) that operates entirely in 2D image/video space, instead grounding part-level motion in a feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting backbone (LGM). It repurposes the splatter-image representation from Szymanowicz et al. to enable pixel-wise supervision of 3D Gaussian parameters during the motion-learning stage, and borrows Zero123++ and Segment Anything for multi-view generation and drag propagation respectively. The two-stage training (motion distillation followed by photometric appearance learning) is a novel strategy to prevent catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning a pretrained static 3D reconstruction model for dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing a full 3D Gaussian representation in ~4 seconds (vs. minutes for diffusion-based baselines), PartRM makes drag-conditioned part-level dynamics practically usable in simulation loops for robot policy training, as demonstrated by the zero-shot Isaac Gym manipulation transfer. The PartDrag-4D dataset (20,548 states, 738 objects) addresses a genuine data gap in 4D articulated-object learning. However, generalization remains limited to motions close to the PartNet-Mobility training distribution, and the sim-to-real evaluation covers only two simple articulation types (prismatic drawer, rotational door), leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex or real-world articulated objects.
