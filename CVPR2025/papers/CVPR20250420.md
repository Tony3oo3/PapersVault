## Problem

Existing style transfer methods rely on unsupervised training or computationally expensive optimization/inversion-based pipelines, limiting their generalization to arbitrary styles, controllability over stylization outputs, and practical efficiency. A key bottleneck is the absence of large-scale, high-quality paired content-style-stylized datasets that would enable supervised, end-to-end training with predictable results across diverse style categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250420/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

OmniStyle accepts two input modes: (a) a content image paired with a free-form text instruction describing the target style, or (b) a content image paired with a reference style image. In both cases the output is a single high-resolution stylized image that preserves the content's structure and semantics while adopting the target style. The content images span 20 semantic categories (animals, architecture, humans, food, etc.) and the style references cover 1,000 fine-grained categories from Style30K, with each triplet also carrying a textual description and an instructional prompt for training.

## Method

OmniStyle fine-tunes only the MM-DiT diffusion transformer of FLUX-dev (all other components frozen) on the curated OmniStyle-150K subset. For image-guided transfer, VAE-encoded style and content features are spatially concatenated with noisy latents and text tokens, with distinct positional encodings for each image; for instruction-guided transfer, the content feature sequence is injected as pseudo-text tokens delimited by [img][/img] tags. The training data is produced by applying six SOTA style transfer models (StyleID, ArtFlow, StyleShot, AesPANet, CSGO, CAST) to 2,000 FLUX-generated content images × 100 randomly sampled styles, then filtered by OmniFilter—a multi-dimensional scorer combining CLIP semantic similarity, DINOv2 structural similarity, a contrastively fine-tuned CLIP style encoder, and an InternVL2-based aesthetic predictor trained on AVA and BAID. The highest-scoring stylization among the six candidates per content-style pair is retained, yielding 150K high-quality triplets that drive supervised fine-tuning at 1e-4 learning rate on 8×H100 GPUs.

## Results

On the image-guided style transfer benchmark (2,000 stylized images), OmniStyle achieves the highest style consistency (0.7483), aesthetic appeal (5.7913), and lowest style loss (0.1086) among all 8 compared methods. On the instruction-guided task, OmniStyle leads in content preservation (0.5128), style consistency (0.6441), and style loss (0.2873), while being competitive on aesthetic appeal (5.7512 vs. HQ-Edit's 5.7777). In user studies, OmniStyle receives 86.90% first-place rankings for instruction-guided transfer and 41.22% for image-guided transfer, substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For image-guided style transfer, OmniStyle outperforms all 7 baselines on style consistency (0.7483 vs. StyleShot's 0.7347, the closest competitor) and style loss (0.1086 vs. AesPANet's 0.1128), while also achieving the highest aesthetic appeal (5.7913 vs. StyleShot's 5.7818). However, OmniStyle trails CAST (0.6165) and AesPANet (0.5665) on content preservation, suggesting a slight trade-off between style fidelity and structural retention. For instruction-guided transfer, OmniStyle dominates all 5 baselines on style consistency (0.6441 vs. OmniGen's 0.5487) and style loss (0.2873 vs. HQ-Edit's 0.3108), with a marginal gap to HQ-Edit on aesthetic appeal (5.7512 vs. 5.7777). The user study reinforces these quantitative findings: OmniStyle earns 86.90% first-place votes for instruction-guided transfer (next best: UltraEdit and OmniGen at 5.16%) and 41.22% for image-guided transfer (next best: StyleShot at 18.63%), indicating a clear perceptual advantage over all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in style transfer—iterative optimization (Gatys et al.) or diffusion inversion (StyleDrop, DreamBooth)—by reframing the problem as a supervised learning task enabled by a large-scale paired dataset. Architecturally, it builds directly on FLUX-dev's MM-DiT backbone, fine-tuning only the diffusion transformer while freezing all other components, which contrasts with prior methods that require per-image optimization or additional inversion steps. The dataset curation strategy of generating stylized outputs with six heterogeneous SOTA models (rather than a single generator as in IMAGStyle) and then filtering via a multi-dimensional quality framework (OmniFilter combining CLIP, DINOv2, and InternVL2) is a novel pipeline not seen in prior style transfer data construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniStyle-1M is the first million-scale paired style transfer dataset spanning 1,000 fine-grained style categories, providing a resource that could substantially lower the barrier to training controlled, end-to-end style transfer models. However, the dataset is entirely synthetic (content images from FLUX, stylized outputs from existing models), and the evaluation relies on the authors' own OmniFilter as the primary metric, raising circularity concerns. The paper itself acknowledges the absence of human-annotated ground-truth stylized images as a key limitation, leaving open whether synthetic paired data generalizes to real-world artistic style transfer. The OmniFilter framework, combining semantic, structural, style, and aesthetic scoring, is potentially reusable beyond this specific dataset for broader image quality assessment.
