## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models fail at periodic tasks (repetition counting, heart-rate estimation, traffic flow analysis) due to three intertwined limitations: insufficient temporal modeling, a conflict between short-timescale semantic alignment and long-timescale periodic reasoning, and the absence of explicit counting supervision in standard training corpora. No prior MLLM has been designed to systematically acquire and retain periodic reasoning across multiple modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251232/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 20-frame video clips resized to 224x224, free-form text questions, rotated MNIST digit images, GPS taxi trajectory sequences, and radar vital-sign waveforms, all paired with a natural-language question. Outputs are free-form natural-language answers that must embed a specific numerical value (repetition count, heart rate in BPM, trajectory coordinate, or rotation angle) within a complete sentence. The training pipeline is strictly sequential: the model first sees only repeated-word text strings, then macro-periodic video, then micro-periodic video, and finally other modalities, with each stage building on the previous.

## Method

Period-LLM couples a CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder with a linear projection layer feeding into a LLaMA-7B backbone, trained under an easy-to-hard curriculum: (1) text-only repeated-word QA to instill counting logic, (2) macro-periodic video (Countix) for visible repetitive motion, (3) micro-periodic video (rPPG) for subtle physiological signals, and (4) cross-modal tasks (RotNIST, Drive, Radar). The key technical novelty is the Resisting Logical Oblivion (RLO) strategy, which modulates backpropagation gradients per feature channel via a weight function that amplifies gradients for channels whose mean activation falls below the global channel average, scaled by an exponential schedule. This directs newly learned semantic information into under-utilized channels while preserving the periodic-reasoning features already encoded in high-activation channels, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting during the semantic-alignment phase. GPT-4 is used to generate diverse question phrasings and answer templates from ground-truth labels, producing the QA training pairs for every modality.

## Results

On the Countix-QA dataset (fine-tuned), Period-LLM achieves a MAE of 3.77 and RMSE of 7.14, outperforming the best baseline Video-ChatGPT (MAE 4.64, RMSE 9.15) by 0.87 MAE. On rPPG-QA (fine-tuned), Period-LLM reaches MAE 13.78 and RMSE 16.83, surpassing the next-best LLaMA-VID (MAE 17.51, RMSE 17.60) by 3.73 MAE. In the zero-shot setting, Period-LLM (pretrained only on text-QA) already leads with MAE 5.51 on Countix-QA and 15.08 on rPPG-QA. Across additional modalities (RotNIST-QA, Drive-QA, Radar-QA), Period-LLM consistently achieves the lowest MAE, e.g., 1.50 on RotNIST-QA and 14.24 on Radar-QA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Period-LLM outperforms all seven baselines on both Countix-QA and rPPG-QA in the fine-tuned setting. On Countix-QA, the closest competitor is Video-ChatGPT (MAE 4.64), which Period-LLM beats by 0.87 MAE; LLaMA-VID and VideoLLaMA follow at 5.34 and 4.98 respectively. On rPPG-QA, the gap is larger: Period-LLM (MAE 13.78) surpasses LLaMA-VID (17.51) by 3.73 and Video-ChatGPT (17.54) by 3.76, indicating the easy-to-hard curriculum and RLO strategy are particularly effective for subtle periodic signals. In the zero-shot protocol (no task-specific fine-tuning), Period-LLM still leads all baselines on both datasets, confirming that text-QA pretraining alone transfers periodic reasoning to video. On the cross-modality datasets (Table 2), Period-LLM achieves the best MAE on all three: RotNIST-QA (1.50 vs. 2.01 for Video-ChatGPT), Drive-QA (28.71 vs. 29.44 for FrozenBiLM), and Radar-QA (14.24 vs. 17.03 for VideoLLaMA). No baseline matches Period-LLM on any primary metric in either the zero-shot or fine-tuned protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MLLM architecture lineage (LLaVA-style CLIP encoder + LLaMA backbone) and on established periodic-task benchmarks (Countix for repetition counting, rPPG datasets for physiological signal estimation), but departs from both by unifying them under a single cross-modal periodic-reasoning framework. The key conceptual departure is the 'easy-to-hard generalization' paradigm, which bootstraps periodic reasoning from synthetic text before introducing visual periodicity, and the Resisting Logical Oblivion (RLO) gradient-weighting strategy, which addresses the under-explored tension between preserving logical/counting ability and acquiring new semantic alignment during multimodal fine-tuning. No prior MLLM work explicitly targets periodic tasks or constructs a difficulty-graded training curriculum across modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first dedicated benchmark and training recipe for periodic reasoning in MLLMs, filling a gap where existing video-LLMs demonstrably fail at counting repetitions or estimating heart rate. The RLO strategy is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic optimization trick that could be adopted in other settings where sequential fine-tuning risks catastrophic forgetting of a specific skill. However, the experimental scope is limited (single A6000, 20 frames at 224×224, batch size 1), the RLO ablation shows only a ~0.12 MAE gain, and it remains open whether the text-pretraining benefit generalizes to longer videos, higher resolutions, or truly novel periodic domains beyond the five tested datasets.
