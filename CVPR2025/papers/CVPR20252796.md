## Problem

Fine-tuning multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) on new tasks causes catastrophic forgetting, where task-specific knowledge acquisition degrades performance on pre-trained tasks. Existing mitigation approaches like Model-Tailor achieve sub-optimal stability-plasticity balance and incur substantial computational overhead from layer-wise Hessian inverse calculations, leaving a gap for an efficient method that provides principled, tunable control over the trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252796/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two complete model weight sets (pre-trained Θ_pre and fine-tuned Θ_sft) plus a small set of target-task input samples (n=128) for Hessian estimation, requiring no retraining or access to pre-training data. It outputs a single fused parameter tensor Θ_fusion that replaces both source models, which then operates as a standard MLLM producing text captions or VQA answers. The only tunable hyperparameter is γ ∈ [0.4, 0.8], validated on a target-task validation set rather than explicitly setting the theoretical bound η.

## Method

AdaDARE-γ operates post-hoc on the delta parameters ΔΘ = Θ_sft − Θ_pre in two steps. First, it replaces DARE's uniform Bernoulli dropout with per-parameter adaptive drop probabilities P^ℓ, where each element is derived from the Hessian diagonal and delta magnitude (p_i* = max(0, 1 − n(1−p)√(H_ii·δ_i²) / Σ√(H_jj·δ_j²))), so that task-irrelevant parameters are preferentially removed while task-salient ones are retained and rescaled. Second, a γ factor scales the adaptive-selected delta before adding it back to the pre-trained weights (Θ_fusion = Θ_pre + γ·Δ̂Θ), providing a continuous knob between full knowledge retention (γ→0) and full task adaptation (γ→1) with a provable upper bound γ ≤ η/Σ|δ_i|. The approach is compatible with PEFT methods like LoRA and requires only O(nd_col² + d_row·d_col) computation versus O(nd_col² + d_col³ + d_col·d_row²) for Model-Tailor.

## Results

On InstructBLIP fine-tuned on Flickr30k, AdaDARE-γ achieves an H-score of 96.05 and Avg of 93.43, outperforming Model-Tailor (94.69 / 93.41), DARE (92.33 / 86.76), and full fine-tuning (92.28 / 86.58). On LLaVA-1.5 fine-tuned on OKVQA, AdaDARE-γ reaches an H-score of 60.74 and Avg of 63.90, surpassing Model-Tailor (59.25 / 63.36), DARE (55.88 / 55.45), and full fine-tuning (55.29 / 54.96). The method maintains ~98.2% of pre-training effectiveness on original tasks while achieving ~98.7% of standard fine-tuning performance on target tasks, using only 10% of the trainable parameters (18.8M vs. 188M for InstructBLIP; 248M vs. 2.5B for LLaVA-1.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaDARE-γ consistently achieves the best H-score across all four experimental settings (InstructBLIP on Flickr30k/GQA, LLaVA-1.5 on Flickr30k/OKVQA), indicating the most balanced stability-plasticity trade-off. Against full fine-tuning, AdaDARE-γ wins decisively on H-score (e.g., 96.05 vs. 92.28 on InstructBLIP-Flickr30k; 60.74 vs. 55.29 on LLaVA-OKVQA) and Avg, while sacrificing only a small amount of target-task score (99.7 vs. 101.3 on Flickr30k; 57.21 vs. 55.72 on OKVQA where it actually wins). Against DARE, AdaDARE-γ improves H-score by 1.4–4.9 points across settings, primarily by retaining more pre-trained task performance (e.g., VQAv2: 70.46 vs. 60.99 on InstructBLIP-Flickr30k) while maintaining comparable target-task accuracy. Against Model-Tailor, AdaDARE-γ wins on H-score in all settings (96.05 vs. 94.69, 71.30 vs. 69.47, 75.89 vs. 75.58, 60.74 vs. 59.25) and on Avg for three of four settings; Model-Tailor retains slightly more pre-trained performance on GQA (Avg 91.59 vs. 90.42) but at the cost of lower target-task scores (54.36 vs. 57.22). The paper also shows AdaDARE-γ complements LoRA, improving H-score from 62.33 to 73.73 on LLaVA-Flickr30k and from 54.85 to 61.41 on LLaVA-OKVQA over plain LoRA fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DARE framework (random drop-and-rescale of fine-tuning delta parameters) by replacing the uniform dropout probability with per-parameter adaptive probabilities derived from Hessian-based importance, and introduces a γ scaling factor inspired by Task Arithmetic to control the intensity of task-specific knowledge injection. It departs from Model-Tailor, the prior MLLM-specific anti-forgetting method, by avoiding expensive layer-wise Hessian inverse computations in favor of a simpler diagonal-Hessian approximation, while also offering a tunable stability-plasticity knob that Model-Tailor lacks. The method is positioned within the model-merging / parameter-efficient adaptation family, requiring no retraining and remaining compatible with PEFT approaches like LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLMs are increasingly fine-tuned for domain-specific deployment, catastrophic forgetting on pre-trained capabilities is a practical bottleneck; AdaDARE-γ offers a retraining-free, computationally lightweight solution with theoretical guarantees (optimal drop probabilities, upper bound on γ) that practitioners can apply immediately. The γ factor provides an intuitive single-knob control for the stability-plasticity trade-off, which is valuable for deployment scenarios where different downstream tasks demand different retention levels. Key limitations include the manual tuning of γ (validated on small validation sets rather than derived from theory), the reliance on a diagonal Hessian approximation, and the fact that the method has only been validated on two 7B-scale architectures with a limited set of captioning/VQA tasks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger models, more diverse modalities, or sequential multi-task settings.
