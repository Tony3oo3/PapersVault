## Problem

The EPIC Fields benchmark revealed that 3D layered radiance fields (e.g., NeuralDiff) underperform 2D motion segmentation baselines on dynamic object segmentation in long egocentric videos, despite excelling at semi-static components. This paper asks whether a 3D representation can actually enhance 2D motion segmentation for dynamic phenomena, and identifies the high geometric complexity of long egocentric sequences as the root cause of 3D methods' failure to fuse motion cues into incomplete scene geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251531/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an egocentric video sequence with known camera poses and a sequence of 2D motion segmentation masks produced by Motion Grouping (MG), which are sparse, noisy, and partial (treating them analogously to the sparse labels in Semantic NeRF). The output is a set of denoised, multi-view-consistent segmentation masks for both dynamic and semi-static objects, rendered back to 2D frames through the learned 3D layered radiance field, effectively using the 3D model as a fusion and denoising network for the 2D predictions.

## Method

The core contribution is Layered Motion Fusion (LMF), which integrates 2D motion segmentation into a three-layer NeRF (static, semi-static, dynamic) via two complementary losses: Positive Motion Fusion (PMF) pulls the dynamic layer's rendered mask toward the 2D motion predictions, while Negative Motion Fusion (NMF) penalizes the semi-static layer for predicting anything the 2D model classifies as dynamic. These losses operate on volume-rendered pseudo-color masks (green for semi-static, blue for dynamic) and work in synergy to sharpen the boundary between the two layers. To overcome the geometric incompleteness of long egocentric sequences, the method adds test-time refinement (TR), which fine-tunes only the semi-static and dynamic layers on a user-selected subset of target frames, reducing data complexity and enabling the model to capture local geometry well enough to fuse motion accurately. The combination of TR + PMF + NMF yields a 30.4% mAP improvement over NeuralDiff on dynamic segmentation and an 8.4% gain on semi-static, surpassing the 2D MG baseline by 8.2 mAP.

## Results

On the EPIC Fields UDOS benchmark, the proposed method (NeuralDiff + Test-Time Refinement + Layered Motion Fusion) achieves 72.51 mAP on dynamic object segmentation, 27.70 mAP on semi-static segmentation, and 74.21 mAP on their union. This represents a +30.5% relative improvement over the NeuralDiff 3D baseline (55.58 mAP Dyn) and an absolute +8.24 mAP gain over the 2D Motion Grouping baseline (64.27 mAP Dyn), resolving the open problem identified in EPIC Fields where 3D methods previously underperformed 2D baselines on dynamic segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines across every metric. Against the 2D Motion Grouping baseline (MG), the method wins by +8.24 mAP on dynamic segmentation (72.51 vs. 64.27) and by a large margin on semi-static (27.70 vs. 12.78), reversing the prior finding in EPIC Fields where MG outperformed NeuralDiff by ~8.7 mAP. Against the 3D baselines, the gains are substantial: +30.5% relative over NeuralDiff (55.58→72.51 Dyn), +61.7% over NeRF-T (44.27→72.51), and +154% over NeRF-W (28.52→72.51). In the hand-object segmentation comparison (Table 4), the unsupervised MG+Ours (72.51 mAP) slightly exceeds the supervised EgoHOS (71.20 mAP), and combining EgoHOS with the proposed 3D fusion further boosts it to 77.31 mAP, demonstrating that the method is complementary to both unsupervised and supervised 2D approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-to-3D label fusion paradigm (Semantic NeRF, N3F, DFF, LERF) from static scenes to dynamic egocentric videos by fusing motion segmentation predictions from a 2D model (Motion Grouping) into layered radiance fields (NeuralDiff). The key departure is the design of complementary positive and negative motion fusion losses that simultaneously pull the dynamic layer toward 2D predictions and push the semi-static layer away from them, a synergy not present in prior static-scene fusion work. The test-time refinement component builds on SfM-TTR [26] but applies it specifically to reduce scene complexity so that the 3D model can actually capture enough geometry to fuse motion cues into.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves the open problem identified by EPIC Fields [80] that 3D methods underperform 2D baselines for unsupervised dynamic object segmentation, demonstrating that 3D geometry can enhance 2D video understanding even in highly dynamic egocentric settings. The practical implication is that 3D reconstruction need not be abandoned for dynamic scenes; with appropriate fusion losses and test-time focus, it becomes a useful denoising and consistency mechanism. Limitations include dependence on accurate camera geometry, evaluation on a single benchmark (EPIC Fields/EPIC-KITCHENS), and the fact that the 3D model still only captures partial geometry of dynamic objects. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-person or multi-object dynamic scenes and whether the test-time refinement cost (~22 min for 100 frames) is acceptable in interactive settings.
