## Problem

Existing RGB-based category-level pose estimation methods that use the NOCS map as an intermediate representation suffer from an intra-class variation problem: different instances of the same category produce distinct NOCS maps for identical poses, introducing redundant instance-specific information that makes the many-to-one regression from NOCS map to category-level pose suboptimal. The paper identifies this limitation and asks how to eliminate intra-class variation while preserving the pixel-wise correspondence that makes coordinate maps learnable from images.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a cropped RGB image of a known-category object (segmented by an off-the-shelf Mask R-CNN detector) along with known camera intrinsics K ∈ R^{3×3}. It produces a full 9DoF pose: 3D rotation R ∈ SO(3), 3D translation t ∈ R³, and 3D object size s ∈ R³. Object size is regressed directly from backbone features, while rotation and translation are predicted from the IVFC map fused with 2D ROI positional information. Evaluation uses scale-agnostic metrics (NIOU and n°md) to exclude scale ambiguity inherent to monocular RGB input.

## Method

GIVEPose implements Gradual Intra-class Variation Elimination by first predicting the NOCS map (which has pixel-wise correspondence with the input image) from backbone features, then transforming it into the proposed IVFC map (which has a one-to-one mapping with category-level pose) via a Deformable Convolutional Auto-Encoder (DCAE) module. The DCAE uses a deformable convolution encoder to downsample the NOCS map—accounting for the fact that intra-class variation corresponds to complex 3D shape deformations rather than pixel-independent noise—and fuses the encoded features with backbone features before an upsampling decoder reconstructs the IVFC map. The IVFC map is generated from a fixed per-category consensus model (obtained via shape prior deformation and surface reconstruction), ensuring it encodes only category-shared geometry. A lightweight pose predictor then integrates the IVFC map with 2D positional information to regress rotation and translation, while the overall loss combines pose supervision with NOCS map and IVFC map reconstruction terms.

## Results

On the REAL275 dataset (scale-agnostic metrics), GIVEPose achieves 20.8% NIOU75 and 44.6% 10°0.2d, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art LaPose by 5.0% and 7.2% respectively on these most stringent metrics. On the synthetic CAMERA25 dataset, GIVEPose reaches 19.4% NIOU75 and 47.5% 10°0.2d, improving over LaPose by 5.3% and 5.1%. On the larger-scale Wild6D dataset (zero-shot generalization), GIVEPose achieves 28.1% NIOU75 and 40.3% 10°0.5d, outperforming LaPose by 3.7% and 3.2% on these metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIVEPose outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both REAL275 and CAMERA25. Against the closest competitor LaPose (ECCV 2024), GIVEPose gains 5.0% on NIOU75 and 7.2% on 10°0.2d on REAL275, and 5.3% and 5.1% on the same metrics on CAMERA25. The gap over DMSR (ICRA 2024) is substantially larger: 11.1% on NIOU75 and 18.6% on 10°0.2d on REAL275, reflecting the advantage of direct geometry-guided regression over indirect PnP-based solvers. The two earlier indirect methods, MSOS and OLD-Net, are far behind, with NIOU75 scores below 1% on REAL275, highlighting the difficulty of the two-stage correspondence-then-solve paradigm for RGB-only category-level pose. On Wild6D (zero-shot transfer), GIVEPose maintains its lead over LaPose with 3.7% improvement on NIOU75, confirming better generalization to unseen object instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometry-guided pose regression paradigm from instance-level to category-level tasks, building directly on LaPose (ECCV 2024), which first adapted this approach using a Laplacian Mixture Model for NOCS maps. The key conceptual departure is the argument that the NOCS map—an intermediate representation inherited from instance-level work—is fundamentally inadequate for category-level pose regression due to its many-to-one correspondence with category-level pose, introducing redundant instance-specific information. To address this, the paper introduces the IVFC map derived from a category-consensus model and bridges the two representations via a Deformable Convolutional Auto-Encoder (DCAE) module, drawing on the denoising autoencoder framework (Vincent et al., 2008) and deformable convolutions (InternImage, 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of intra-class variation as a structural limitation of the NOCS map for category-level regression provides a useful diagnostic for the field, suggesting that future intermediate representations should explicitly separate category-consensus from instance-specific information. However, the method still depends on per-category shape priors (from SPD) to generate the consensus model, limiting its applicability to categories without well-defined shape priors, and its accuracy is bounded by the upstream detector. Open questions include whether the gradual elimination strategy generalizes to categories with extreme shape diversity (e.g., clothing, furniture) and whether the shape-prior dependency can be removed through self-supervised or prior-free consensus model learning.
