## Problem

Virtual try-on for ornaments (bracelets, rings, earrings, necklaces) remains largely unexplored despite significant practical demand in e-commerce and advertising. Unlike garments, ornaments feature intricate tiny geometric structures (repeated shapes, ring holes) that are rigid, making any distortion immediately visible, and their wearing masks are difficult to obtain due to diverse structures and pose-dependent occlusion. Existing garment try-on methods rely on additional inputs like skeleton and semantic maps that are impractical for small, complex ornaments.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference ornament image, a target model image, and a coarse bounding box indicating the user-specific wearing location (e.g., which finger for a ring) as the sole user input—no skeleton, semantic, or silhouette maps are required. The ornament region is cropped and resized to 512×512, and the generated result is pasted back into the original masked region to compose the final output. At inference, only the bounding box is needed to specify where the ornament should be worn.

## Method

Built on Stable Diffusion V1.5 with a ReferenceNet branch, the method introduces two key modules. First, an iterative pose-aware mask refinement scheme starts from the coarse bounding box and progressively predicts a more accurate wearing mask using intermediate latent features from the denoising U-Net, blending predictions with the previous mask via a time-dependent coefficient α_t that increases from 0 to 1 over training. Second, a mask-guided attention mechanism implicitly constrains self-attention maps by down-sampling the reference ornament mask, applying it to attention maps (masking along one spatial dimension and margining along the other), then up-sampling and averaging across layers to produce a predicted wearing mask that is regularized against ground truth via L2 loss. This forces attention to learn a mapping from reference mask to wearing mask, preserving geometric topology and component counts. Training combines the standard diffusion loss with the two mask-related L2 losses, with their weights decaying over training to shift focus from structure to appearance details.

## Results

On the ornament virtual try-on task, the proposed method achieves the best scores across all metrics: FID 19.00, LPIPS 0.0593, CLIP score 88.7, and DINO score 74.5 (against ground truth), and CLIP score 57.3 and DINO score 38.7 (against reference ornament). The most notable margin is in DINO feature similarity against ground truth (74.5 vs. 64.8–67.2 for baselines), indicating substantially better structural and identity preservation. The method also approaches ground-truth reference similarity (DINO 38.7 vs. GT 43.3), suggesting near-faithful ornament reproduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Paint-by-Example (object insertion via CLIP guidance), AnyDoor (zero-shot object customization with ControlNet + DINO), and IDM-VTON (garment virtual try-on with dual U-Nets). The proposed method outperforms all three on every metric. Against Paint-by-Example, the gains are most visible in FID (19.00 vs. 23.49) and DINO-GT (74.5 vs. 64.8), reflecting better structural fidelity. Against AnyDoor, the advantage is largest in LPIPS (0.0593 vs. 0.1029) and DINO-GT (74.5 vs. 67.2), indicating AnyDoor struggles with scale and detail preservation. Against IDM-VTON, the closest competitor, the method still leads by 4.0 FID points and 9.5 DINO-GT points, showing that garment-oriented try-on architectures fail to handle the intricate tiny geometric structures unique to ornaments. Qualitatively, none of the baselines preserve the number of sub-components or repeated geometric patterns in ornaments, whereas the proposed method maintains both local and global structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion V1.5) and the ReferenceNet architecture widely used in garment virtual try-on (TryonDiffusion, IDM-VTON), but departs from that line by addressing the fundamentally different challenges of rigid, tiny-geometry ornaments. It also distinguishes itself from object-insertion methods (Paint-by-Example, AnyDoor) that handle general object placement without the precise pose alignment required when an ornament must conform to a specific body part (wrist, finger, neck). The two proposed modules—iterative pose-aware mask refinement and mask-guided attention—are novel architectural contributions that repurpose intermediate denoising features for mask prediction and implicitly constrain attention to preserve geometric topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of ornament virtual try-on as a distinct task, with a 64k-image dataset spanning bracelets, rings, earrings, and necklaces, filling a clear gap between garment try-on and general object insertion. The practical impact for e-commerce and retail advertising is significant, as ornament try-on has high commercial demand but no prior dedicated solution. Key limitations remain: results are biased toward reference images with reduced specular reflections (lacking environmental lighting cues), and wearing orientation control (e.g., rotation along a wrist) is imprecise, occasionally hiding featured components. Future work on fine-grained lighting control and secondary masks for orientation could address these gaps.
