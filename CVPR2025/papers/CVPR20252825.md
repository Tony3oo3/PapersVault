## Problem

Photorealistic 3D head avatars struggle with two coupled challenges: accurately modeling dynamic face-hair interactions (e.g., hair lowering toward eyebrows during a frown) and generalizing across identities without per-subject training. Existing universal prior models treat hair as part of a single head mesh, causing artifacts like hair growing from shoulders and rigid, coupled deformations, while real-time-capable methods like PiCA are limited to personalized models. No prior work combines a universal cross-identity prior with a layered face-hair separation that supports real-time mesh rendering.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view calibrated images (110 cameras) to extract per-identity neutral texture maps and geometry images (vertex positions in UV space), which feed identity-conditioned hypernetworks. At inference, a shared 16×4×4 expression code, 6D neck pose, head pose, and view direction condition two parallel decoders that produce separate 256×256 geometry and appearance maps for face and hair, each with its own UV parameterization. The final output is a compositional rendered image (face and hair pixel-decoded independently and masked) plus Gaussian splatting parameters (position deltas, quaternions, scales, colors, opacities) anchored on the layered mesh vertices.

## Method

LUCAS introduces a layered Universal Prior Model with two parallel branches (face and hair), each with its own identity-conditioned hypernetwork generating untied decoder biases, while a single unified expression encoder produces a shared latent code that is decoded independently by separate geometry, appearance, and pixel decoders per branch. The hair geometry decoder additionally receives head pose (beyond neck pose) to capture orientation-dependent hair dynamics, and the shared expression code enables hair to respond to facial expressions (e.g., frowning) without being rigidly coupled to face deformation. A dehairing pipeline using EM factor analysis iteratively builds a linear deformable model of bald head geometry from 5 bald subjects, providing clean anchor geometry for the face mesh. For Gaussian rendering, 3D Gaussians are anchored on the layered mesh vertices with parallel face/hair hypernetwork-decoder branches, regularized by pre-clamped scale losses and delta-position losses that prevent Gaussians from migrating across the face-hair boundary. Training jointly optimizes PiCA reconstruction losses (photometric, depth, normal, KL, mesh tracking, Laplacian smoothness, segmentation) and Gaussian losses, with a decaying dehairing loss to stabilize bald geometry convergence.

## Results

In per-subject evaluation, LUCAS (Gaussian) achieves 35.20 PSNR, 0.9286 SSIM, and 0.2407 LPIPS, outperforming the personalized PiCA baseline (32.05 PSNR, 0.8895 SSIM, 0.2678 LPIPS) by +3.15 dB in PSNR. In cross-subject (universal) evaluation, LUCAS (Gaussian) reaches 34.56 PSNR, 0.9201 SSIM, and 0.2394 LPIPS, surpassing URAvatar (33.12 PSNR, 0.9034 SSIM, 0.2464 LPIPS) by +1.44 dB in PSNR. The mesh variant also improves over the single-mesh uPiCA baseline by +0.46 dB PSNR in the cross-subject setting, confirming the benefit of the layered representation even without Gaussian splatting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the personalized PiCA baseline [37], LUCAS (Gaussian) achieves a substantial +3.15 dB PSNR improvement (35.20 vs. 32.05) and reduces LPIPS from 0.2678 to 0.2407, demonstrating that the universal layered prior model not only generalizes across identities but also surpasses per-identity training in reconstruction quality. In the cross-subject setting, LUCAS (Gaussian) outperforms URAvatar [23] by +1.44 dB PSNR (34.56 vs. 33.12) and +0.0167 SSIM, with the advantage attributed to the layered mesh providing better anchor geometry for Gaussian splatting, particularly for complex hairstyles. At the mesh level, LUCAS (mesh) also beats the single-mesh uPiCA by +0.46 dB PSNR in cross-subject evaluation, confirming that the face-hair disentanglement itself improves geometric fidelity independent of the rendering backend. Qualitatively, the paper highlights that uPiCA produces artifacts such as hair growing from shoulders and color bleeding, while LUCAS maintains clean geometry and accurate hair positioning across diverse head poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUCAS extends the Pixel Codec Avatar (PiCA) framework by replacing its per-identity training with a Universal Prior Model (UPM) inspired by Cao et al.'s multi-identity approach, creating the first mesh-based UPM (uPiCA). The key architectural departure is a layered representation that splits the head into independent face and hair meshes, each with its own hypernetwork, decoder, and UV space, rather than treating the head as a single entity as in prior UPMs like URAvatar. For Gaussian rendering, the layered mesh serves as improved anchor geometry, building on the Gaussian codec avatar paradigm from URAvatar but with separate face and hair Gaussian branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LUCAS addresses a persistent practical bottleneck in codec avatars: the inability of single-mesh representations to handle dynamic hair-face interactions during expressions and head movements, while simultaneously enabling real-time mobile rendering (45 FPS) through the mesh-based UPM. The zero-shot driving capability on unseen identities broadens applicability beyond per-subject training. However, the authors acknowledge limitations with extreme hair deformations and unseen poses in zero-shot scenarios, and future work on relighting, broader hairstyle diversity, and real-world data fine-tuning remains open. The dehairing pipeline (iterative EM-based bald geometry estimation) is a practical but somewhat labor-intensive preprocessing step that may limit scalability to very large identity sets.
