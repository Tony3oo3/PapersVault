## Problem

Existing multimodal data filtering methods are designed for offline settings that require storing and scoring entire datasets, making them impractical for real-time video-text streams where storage and compute are constrained. No prior work addresses the unique challenge of online, task-aware filtering for video-text data that simultaneously ensures modality alignment, downstream task relevance, and data specificity with minimal per-sample computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251418/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous stream of video-text pairs (v_t, s_t) and precomputed LanguageBind embeddings from five downstream task datasets (MSR-VTT, DiDeMo, LSMDC, ActivityNet, YouCook2) as reference points. For each incoming sample, it produces a binary accept/reject decision in real time; accepted samples are immediately forwarded to online-train a BT-Adapter video-language model. The filtering operates per-sample (not in batches), and the only precomputation required is extracting downstream task embeddings and their distances to a root embedding (the text embedding of an empty string).

## Method

ReSpec applies three sequential per-sample filters: (1) an alignment check via video-text cosine similarity thresholding, (2) a relevance test using non-parametric kernel density estimation with von Mises-Fisher kernels on unit-norm text embeddings, where the null hypothesis (sample belongs to the downstream distribution) is rejected if the density estimate falls below the 5th quantile of downstream data densities, and (3) a specificity test comparing the Euclidean distance from the sample's text embedding to a root embedding (empty string "") against a quantile threshold derived from downstream task embeddings. The key design choices are using text modality for relevance (higher concentration parameter κ yields sharper distributional boundaries), non-parametric KDE over a single parametric vMF (avoids oversampling), and the root-embedding distance as a lightweight specificity proxy that avoids the batch requirements and extra model of hyperbolic approaches like HYPE. This yields a filtering pipeline with no backpropagation and only one forward pass per sample, making it more computationally efficient than CiT and CoLoR-Filter.

## Results

On WebVid2M, ReSpec achieves an average performance of 49.11 (mean of Recall@1, 5, 10 across five zero-shot video retrieval tasks) using only 27.5% of the data, surpassing the full-data baseline of 47.23. On VideoCC3M, ReSpec attains state-of-the-art performance while using as little as 5% of the data (full-data baseline: 42.62). ReSpec is also the most computationally efficient method, requiring only a single forward pass per incoming sample with no parameter updates, compared to CiT's backpropagation and CoLoR-Filter's two forward passes plus prior model finetuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSpec outperforms both online task-aware baselines—CiT and CoLoR-Filter—on every reported metric. In terms of distributional alignment with downstream tasks, ReSpec achieves the lowest Fréchet distance (0.2371) and text KL divergence (0.4371), compared to CiT (0.2513 / 0.4488) and CoLoR-Filter (0.2977 / 0.4921), indicating its filtered data is closest to the target task distribution. In terms of data efficiency, ReSpec uses ~27% of WebVid2M and ~5% of VideoCC3M, less than both baselines while achieving higher average downstream performance. Computationally, ReSpec requires only one forward pass per sample (PN+MN+2N) with zero parameter updates, whereas CiT incurs additional backpropagation cost (2γPN) and periodic recomputation of downstream embeddings (γPMN/b), and CoLoR-Filter requires a prior finetuning cost (3PM) plus two forward passes per sample (2PN+2N). The paper attributes ReSpec's advantage to its explicit use of kernel density estimation and statistical hypothesis testing for relevance, which neither CiT nor CoLoR-Filter employs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline multimodal data curation literature (DataComp, T-MARS, HYPE, SIEVE) and the emerging online task-aware filtering line (CiT, CoLoR-Filter) into a unified framework specifically for streaming video-text data. It departs from prior offline methods by eliminating the need to store and score the entire dataset, and from prior online methods (CiT, CoLoR-Filter) by replacing backpropagation-based or dual-forward-pass filtering with a single-pass density estimation and root-embedding distance approach. The vMF kernel density estimation for relevance filtering draws on Banerjee et al.'s MLE approximation for concentration parameters, while the specificity filter repurposes the observation that less specific text embeddings cluster near the empty-string embedding (Alper & Averbuch-Elor, 2024) as a lightweight informativeness proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReSpec addresses a practical gap in resource-constrained deployment settings (autonomous driving, real-time surveillance) where video-text streams must be filtered and learned from without storing the full corpus. The finding that 5% of VideoCC3M suffices for SOTA zero-shot retrieval is practically significant, though the evaluation is limited to five retrieval tasks with a single feature extractor (LanguageBind) and a single training model (BT-Adapter), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to generative tasks, other modalities, or different backbone architectures. The text-over-video finding for relevance filtering (driven by higher vMF concentration in text embeddings) is an interesting diagnostic that could inform future multimodal filtering design, but the modest performance margins over CiT and CoLoR-Filter suggest the gains are partly dataset-specific.
