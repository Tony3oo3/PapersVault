## Problem

Current open-source video LMMs are optimized for short, low-resolution videos, yet most existing video instruction-following datasets suffer from limited duration (sparse 0.15 fps sampling) or low resolution (360p), leaving a critical data gap for long-duration and high-resolution video understanding. While prior efforts have focused on architectural improvements, the creation of high-quality synthetic video instruction data from existing resources remains largely under-explored and opaque, with concurrent works releasing model weights without disclosing training data details.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes short video clips (from Panda-70M, MiraData, FineVideo, InternVid, OpenVid-1M) paired with their existing text captions, and produces 403,944 augmented video–QA pairs where long videos exceed 30 seconds and high-resolution videos are at least 960p. Notably, the QA synthesis step (Θ) uses Gemini-1.5-Pro in text-only mode—processing only captions rather than video frames—making the pipeline significantly more cost-efficient than multimodal annotation approaches. The augmented videos are constructed via spatial overlay (e.g., a small low-res clip placed on a high-res background, or 64 clips arranged in an 8×8 grid at 1920×1080) and temporal concatenation (clips from the same source video joined with ≤5 s gaps, or 'needle' clips inserted into longer 'haystack' videos).

## Method

VISTA defines seven spatiotemporal video augmentation operators (Φ) inspired by CutMix and VideoMix: temporal concatenation for long-video captioning and event-order QA, temporal/spatial/spatiotemporal needle-in-a-haystack insertion, and an 8×8 high-resolution grid layout. For each augmented video, Gemini-1.5-Pro generates freeform or multiple-choice QA pairs (Θ) from the needle video's caption, with MCQ distractors deliberately drawn from the haystack video's caption to increase difficulty. The resulting VISTA-400K dataset is used to fine-tune three base video LMMs (VideoLLaVA, Mantis-Idefics2, LongVA) under standard supervised instruction-tuning, yielding average gains of 3.3% on four long-video benchmarks and 6.5% on the newly introduced HRVideoBench. The key insight is that both long-video and high-resolution video understanding reduce to the same retrieval problem—locating a small 'needle' of relevant tokens within a long sequence of video tokens—so augmentations targeting one dimension transfer to the other.

## Results

Finetuning VideoLLaVA, Mantis-Idefics2, and LongVA on VISTA-400K yields average improvements of +3.3% on Video-MME, +4.7% on MLVU, +3.0% on LVBench, and +2.3% on LongVideoBench. On the newly introduced HRVideoBench, the three models gain +15.0, +2.5, and +2.0 points respectively (average +6.5%), with VideoLLaVA showing the largest jump (32.5→47.5). VISTA-LongVA achieves state-of-the-art among open-source models on Video-MME (55.5), LVBench (39.0), and LongVideoBench (53.1). Open-ended QA benchmarks (MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, TGIF-QA, ActivityNet-QA) also show consistent accuracy and GPT-scored quality gains across all three models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three base models (VideoLLaVA, Mantis-Idefics2, LongVA), VISTA-finetuned versions win on every long-video benchmark: VISTA-LongVA improves over LongVA by +3.1 (Video-MME), +5.8 (MLVU), +3.1 (LVBench), +1.3 (LongVideoBench), +1.9 (MVBench), and +1.0 (NExT-QA). Among open-source baselines, VISTA-LongVA ties Video-XL on Video-MME (55.5) but surpasses it on LongVideoBench (53.1 vs. 49.5), while Video-XL retains an edge on MLVU (64.9 vs. 62.1) and MVBench (55.3 vs. 51.1). VISTA-VideoLLaVA outperforms VideoChat2, ShareGPT4Video, and ST-LLM on Video-MME and LongVideoBench, though it trails them on MVBench (47.2 vs. 51.9/51.2/54.9). VISTA-Mantis beats Mantis-Idefics2 on all long-video metrics (e.g., MLVU 55.5 vs. 49.4) but drops 0.6 on NExT-QA, likely due to in-domain overlap. Against proprietary models, VISTA-LongVA surpasses GPT-4V on MLVU (62.1 vs. 49.2) and MVBench (51.1 vs. 43.5) but remains below GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro on Video-MME and LongVideoBench. On HRVideoBench, VISTA-VideoLLaVA jumps from 32.5 to 47.5 (+15.0), the largest single-model gain in the paper, while VISTA-Mantis and VISTA-LongVA achieve more modest +2.5 and +2.0 improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes spatial and temporal data augmentation ideas from image/video classification (CutMix, VideoMix) into a video instruction-data synthesis pipeline, departing from the dominant trend of improving long-video understanding through architectural changes (token compression, Q-Former, long-context LLMs). It contrasts with prior video instruction datasets (VideoChat2, ShareGPT4Video, FineVideo) that are limited by short duration or low resolution, and with concurrent work like LLaVA-Video-178K that relies on costly multimodal GPT-4o annotation; VISTA instead uses only the text API of Gemini-1.5-Pro on existing captions, making it more scalable and transparent. The needle-in-a-haystack evaluation paradigm is borrowed from LLM long-context testing and adapted to the video token sequence setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VISTA-400K and HRVideoBench fill a notable gap: prior open-source video instruction data lacked both long-duration and high-resolution coverage, and no benchmark specifically probed fine-grained perception in high-res video. The finding that long-video and high-resolution augmentation transfer to each other (both reduce to 'finding a needle in a haystack of video tokens') offers a unifying perspective that could guide future data curation. Limitations include dependence on the quality of source captions, the synthetic nature of the augmented videos (which may not capture all real-world complexity), and the fact that gains on short-video benchmarks are modest or occasionally negative. Open questions include whether additional augmentation strategies (e.g., audio-visual mixing, multi-scale temporal warping) can further close the gap to proprietary models like GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro.
