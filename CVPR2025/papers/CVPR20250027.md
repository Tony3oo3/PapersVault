## Problem

Existing Prototypical Part Networks (ProtoPNets) learn prototypes as parametric vectors guided by regularizations, but multiple prototypes within the same class frequently collapse onto the same object part, producing redundant and non-comprehensive visual explanations. This limits interpretability because the model fails to recognize a diverse, holistic set of distinctive parts for each category.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250027/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (H×W×3) and encodes them via a self-supervised ViT backbone (DINOv2 ViT-B/14, ViT-S/14, or DINO ViT-B/16) into patch token sequences of shape h×w×D. It produces a class logit computed as a weighted average of per-prototype max-similarity scores, along with h×w×C×K similarity maps that localize which image regions match each part-prototype. The prototypes themselves (C×K×D) are non-parametric empirical means of clustered foreground patches rather than learned parameters, and only image-level class labels are required for supervision in both training stages.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. In stage 1, a frozen self-supervised ViT backbone extracts patch tokens, a foreground extraction module filters background patches, and a Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport solver with equipartition constraints assigns each foreground patch to exactly one of K class-wise prototypes, which are then updated as momentum-weighted empirical means of their assigned patches. In stage 2, Block Expansion inserts identity-initialized ViT blocks into the frozen backbone, and the added blocks plus modulation weights are fine-tuned with a cross-entropy loss combined with a patch-prototype contrastive loss (L_PPC) that pulls each patch toward its assigned prototype while repelling it from other same-class prototypes. The key distinction from prior ProtoPNets is that prototypes are discovered non-parametrically through constrained clustering rather than trained as learnable parameters, which structurally enforces that each prototype captures a semantically distinct object part. The paper also introduces Distinctiveness Score (measuring IoU overlap between prototype-attended regions) and Comprehensiveness Score (measuring IoU between the union of prototype masks and the ground-truth foreground) as new interpretability metrics.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011 with DINOv2 ViT-B/14 backbone, the proposed method achieves 90.82% top-1 accuracy (K=5) and 90.37% (K=3), outperforming the best prior ProtoPNet (TesNet at 90.66% for K=3, 90.35% for K=5). The most significant gain is in Distinctiveness Score: 94.45% (K=5) and 86.44% (K=3), surpassing the next-best method (ProtoPool at 89.62% and 87.59%) by 4.83% and 8.93% respectively. Stability also improves to 82.97% (K=5) and 78.98% (K=3), exceeding all baselines. Consistency is competitive at 66.40% (K=5), close to EvalProtoPNet's 67.80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla ProtoPNet and Deformable ProtoPNet, the proposed method dominates on all four metrics, with Distinctiveness improving from 11.99% to 94.45% (K=5) and accuracy from 88.16% to 90.82%. Compared to ProtoPool, which achieves the highest Distinctiveness among prior methods (89.62% at K=5), the proposed method gains 4.83% in Distinctiveness while also substantially outperforming on Stability (82.97% vs. 57.49%) and accuracy (90.82% vs. 89.90%). Against TesNet, the closest competitor on accuracy (90.35% at K=5), the proposed method wins on Distinctiveness (94.45% vs. 81.93%) and Stability (82.97% vs. 74.60%) while matching accuracy. EvalProtoPNet is the strongest baseline on Consistency (67.80% vs. 66.40%), but the proposed method surpasses it on Distinctiveness (94.45% vs. 72.12%), Stability (82.97% vs. 74.40%), and accuracy (90.82% vs. 90.06%). Overall, the method achieves the best or near-best score on every metric, with the largest margins on Distinctiveness and Stability, confirming that non-parametric clustering of DINOv2 features yields more diverse and comprehensive part prototypes than parametric regularization-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions prototype-based interpretable classification by replacing the parametric prototype learning paradigm (ProtoPNet, TesNet, EvalProtoPNet) with non-parametric prototypes discovered via Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport clustering in the feature space of self-supervised ViT backbones (DINO/DINOv2). The non-parametric prototype formulation with equipartition constraints is borrowed from ProtoSeg (Zhou et al., CVPR 2022), while the modulation-vector logit computation follows EvalProtoPNet (Huang et al., ICCV 2023) and the efficient backbone adaptation uses Block Expansion (Bafghi et al., CVPR 2024). The key conceptual departure is that prototype diversity is enforced structurally through clustering constraints rather than through learned regularizations (orthogonality, Grassmann manifold constraints), and the quality of explanations is attributed to the pre-structured latent space of foundation models rather than to the training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and previously under-quantified limitation of ProtoPNets—redundant, overlapping explanations—and provides two new metrics (Distinctiveness and Comprehiveness Scores) that give the field a more holistic way to evaluate prototype diversity beyond the existing Consistency/Stability metrics. The finding that non-parametric clustering in DINOv2 feature space alone (without any fine-tuning) already yields 77.96% accuracy on CUB-200-2011 suggests that foundation model representations may be sufficient for interpretable classification in many settings, potentially simplifying the training pipeline. Open questions include whether the two-stage approach scales to large-scale datasets (ImageNet-level), whether the foreground extraction module generalizes beyond fine-grained categories with clear object boundaries, and whether the Distinctiveness/Comprehensiveness metrics can be extended to multi-object or scene-level explanations.
