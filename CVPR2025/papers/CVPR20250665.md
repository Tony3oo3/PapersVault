## Problem

While RGB video prediction has been extensively studied, no prior work addresses the task of simultaneously predicting multiple modalities (e.g., RGB and depth) as joint video outputs. Existing multi-modal generation methods either treat auxiliary modalities as conditioning signals only, operate on single images rather than video sequences, or rely on naive channel concatenation that fails to respect the distinct characteristics of each modality. This paper tackles the gap of building a framework that jointly forecasts all target modalities in a temporally and spatially consistent manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250665/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes C past frames (typically 2 or 8) of RGB and depth at 128×128 or 64×64 resolution and predicts P future frames (typically 28 or 8) of both modalities simultaneously. A key design feature is that conditioning can be partial: the model accepts only RGB or only depth as input and still generates both future modalities. At test time, the model operates auto-regressively, predicting 8 frames per forward pass to extend sequences to arbitrary length. The framework generalizes beyond RGB+depth to semantic segmentation (SYNTHIA) and climate variables like surface pressure and temperature (ERA5-Land).

## Method

SyncVP consists of two modality-specific latent diffusion branches (each a customized PVDM UNet with ~58M parameters) connected by a Split Spatio-Temporal Cross-Attention (STCA) module placed at the deepest layers. STCA decomposes the latent vector into spatial and temporal components and computes cross-attention separately on each pair, sharing a single attention matrix across both directions, reducing computational cost to 37–50% of full cross-attention. A shared forward diffusion process applies the same noise ε to both modalities, which the authors show significantly accelerates loss convergence and enforces cross-modal consistency. Training follows a two-stage procedure: single-modality diffusion models are first trained independently, then jointly fine-tuned with STCA and a cross-modality guidance strategy that randomly masks one or both conditioning modalities (analogous to classifier-free guidance), enabling the model to predict all future frames even when one input modality is absent.

## Results

On Cityscapes (2→28, 128×128), SyncVP achieves an FVD of 84, surpassing the previous best STDiff (107.31) by 21.7% and ExtDM-K4 (121.3) by 30.7%. On BAIR (2→28, 64×64), SyncVP reaches an FVD of 63.60, outperforming STDiff (88.1) by 27.8%. Notably, even when conditioned on RGB only (w/o depth), SyncVP still achieves state-of-the-art FVD of 97.31 on Cityscapes and 70.49 on BAIR, beating all prior methods. SSIM and LPIPS are slightly lower than some baselines, which the authors attribute to the spatio-temporal autoencoder's compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncVP outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary FVD metric on both Cityscapes and BAIR. The closest competitor is STDiff [55], which uses 10 sampling steps and achieves FVD 107.31 (Cityscapes) and 88.1 (BAIR); SyncVP improves by 21.7% and 27.8% respectively. ExtDM-K4 [59] (FVD 121.3 / 102.8) and MCVD [42] (FVD 141.31 / 120.6) are further behind. Older methods like VDT [26] (FVD 142.3) and GHVAEs [47] (FVD 418.0) are substantially worse. On SSIM, VDT achieves the highest value (0.880 on Cityscapes), but SyncVP's SSIM (0.649) is comparable to STDiff (0.658) and MCVD (0.690) while using far fewer sampling steps (10 vs. 100 for most baselines). The RGB-only variant of SyncVP (w/o depth) still beats all prior methods on FVD, confirming that the multi-modal architecture provides gains even when one modality is absent at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncVP builds directly on PVDM [56], using its projected-latent UNet and autoencoder as the backbone for each modality branch, and extends the video prediction task from single-modality RGB to synchronous multi-modal generation (RGB + depth, semantic, or climate). It departs from prior multi-modal generation approaches (LDM3D, MT-Diffusion, HyperHuman) that concatenate modalities or share a single diffusion space, instead maintaining separate pre-trained diffusion models coupled by a novel split spatio-temporal cross-attention (STCA) module inspired by spatial-temporal attention in [23, 46]. The cross-modality guidance training strategy is explicitly adapted from classifier-free guidance [15], repurposing random modality masking to enable partial-conditioning inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SyncVP is the first framework to simultaneously predict multiple modalities in a pure video-prediction setting, and its generalization from depth to semantic segmentation and climate data suggests broad applicability to sensor-fusion scenarios (autonomous driving, weather forecasting). The cross-modality guidance mechanism provides practical robustness to missing sensor data, a critical requirement for real-world deployment. Limitations include the reliance on pseudo-depth (DepthAnything-v2) for BAIR, the modest SSIM/LPIPS scores attributed to aggressive autoencoder compression, and evaluation limited to exactly two modalities; scaling to three or more modalities and to longer-horizon or real-time prediction remain open.
