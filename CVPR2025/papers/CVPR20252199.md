## Problem

In co-training for semi-supervised medical image segmentation, multiple student models tend to converge toward similar decision boundaries (homogenization), reducing representation diversity and limiting generalization. Existing approaches address this through heterogeneous architectures or alternating training, but lack a unified mechanism that simultaneously enriches data diversity and structurally differentiates the training trajectories of co-trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252199/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D MRI slices (256x256 for ACDC, 224x224 for PROMISE12) with a small fraction of pixel-wise segmentation masks (5-20% labeled) and a larger pool of unlabeled images. Both weak (rotation, flip) and strong (Cutout, ColorJitter) augmentations are applied, and a bidirectional copy-paste operation blends labeled and unlabeled regions using a binary mask of configurable foreground ratio. The output is a per-pixel class probability map from each student model, with the final prediction typically taken from the better-performing student.

## Method

beta-FFT combines a single EMA-updated teacher with two student models in a co-training framework, attacking homogenization from two axes. On the data side, weakly and strongly augmented images are fed to separate sub-networks, and a Fast Fourier Transform is used to swap low-frequency components between them, producing four nonlinearly interpolated samples that bridge augmentation levels while preserving high-frequency detail. On the training side, Student Model 1 receives additional supervised loss on labeled data plus a Beta-distribution-sampled linear interpolation consistency regularization (LICR) or its noise-reformulated variant (NICR) on unlabeled pairs, whereas Student Model 2 is trained only via cross pseudo-supervision and cross-consistency on the FFT-interpolated set. This asymmetric loss structure forces the two students onto distinct optimization trajectories, and the overall objective combines cross-teaching, nonlinear-interpolation consistency, and differentiation losses.

## Results

On the ACDC dataset with 10% labeled data, β-FFT achieves a Dice score of 90.50±0.04% and Jaccard of 83.12±0.12%, outperforming the strongest baselines ABD (89.81/81.95) and AD-MT (89.46/81.47). On PROMISE12 with 20% labeled data, it reaches 83.75±0.65% Dice and 1.20±0.07 ASD, surpassing ABD (82.06/1.33) and AD-MT (79.82/1.77). On MS-CMRSEG 2019 with 20% labeled data, β-FFT attains 87.79±0.04% Dice and 78.60±0.06% Jaccard, approaching the fully supervised upper bound (88.19/79.28) while using only one-fifth of the labels. Notably, β-FFT consistently leads on Dice and Jaccard across all three datasets but trails ABD and AD-MT on distance-based metrics (95HD, ASD) in some settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

β-FFT achieves the highest Dice and Jaccard scores across all three datasets, beating the two strongest recent baselines—ABD (CVPR 2024) and AD-MT (ECCV 2025)—by 0.69–0.74% Dice and 1.15–1.67% Jaccard on ACDC (10%), by 1.69% Dice on PROMISE12, and by 0.54% Dice on MS-CMRSEG 2019. However, on distance-based metrics the picture is mixed: ABD and AD-MT report lower 95HD (1.46–1.51 vs. 2.38 on ACDC) and lower ASD (0.44–0.49 vs. 0.62 on ACDC), suggesting β-FFT's segmentations are slightly less boundary-precise despite superior overlap. On PROMISE12, β-FFT wins on both DSC and ASD, and on MS-CMRSEG it leads on Dice/Jaccard but trails AD-MT on HD95 (3.75 vs. 3.56) and ASD (1.62 vs. 1.21). Against earlier methods (BCP, DiffRect, SCP-Net, URPC, MC-Net, SS-Net, DTC), β-FFT consistently outperforms by 1–7% Dice, with the largest margins over self-training-era baselines like DTC and URPC. The method also narrows the gap to fully supervised U-Net on MS-CMRSEG to just 0.4% Dice while using only 20% of the labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the co-training paradigm for semi-supervised medical image segmentation by addressing model homogenization from two angles: data-level diversity via FFT-based low-frequency component swapping between differently augmented images, and training-structure asymmetry via differentiated training where only one student model receives additional supervised training with Beta-distribution interpolation regularization. It builds directly on the bidirectional copy-paste mechanism of BCP (CVPR 2023), the cross-teaching architecture of Luo et al. (MIDL 2022), and the EMA-based Mean Teacher framework, while drawing on frequency-domain augmentation ideas from FDA and FreMix. The key conceptual departure from prior co-training work is the explicit use of asymmetric training signals (labeled data + interpolation regularization for one model only) as a de-homogenization mechanism, rather than relying solely on architectural heterogeneity or alternating schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a recognized but under-addressed failure mode in co-training (model homogenization) with a practical, modular solution that can be grafted onto existing mean-teacher or cross-teaching pipelines. However, the gains over already-strong baselines (ABD, AD-MT) are modest (~1–2% Dice), and all experiments are confined to 2D cardiac and prostate MRI segmentation on three datasets, leaving open whether the FFT-based interpolation and differentiated training transfer to 3D volumes, other modalities (CT, pathology), or non-medical segmentation tasks. The optimal low-pass filter size (20×20 vs. 30×30) appears dataset-dependent, and the paper does not analyze computational overhead of the FFT operations or the sensitivity of the Beta(0.1, 0.1) choice across architectures.
