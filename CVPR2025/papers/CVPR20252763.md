## Problem

Existing video quality assessment methods are designed for natural videos affected by low-level distortions (noise, blur, compression) and fail to capture the unique degradations in AI-generated videos, such as unrealistic objects, unnatural movements, temporal inconsistencies, and text-video misalignment. Distribution-level metrics like FVD and image-level alignment scores like CLIPScore cannot reflect human preference for individual videos or account for temporal dynamics. The paper addresses this by introducing both a large-scale multi-dimensional AIGV dataset and a dedicated LMM-based assessment model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252763/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes AI-generated video frames (sampled from videos of varying lengths, 8–200 frames depending on the generation model) and the corresponding text prompt as input. It produces three types of output: (1) natural-language quality level descriptions (bad/poor/fair/good/excellent) for each of four dimensions, (2) continuous numerical quality scores in [0,100] per dimension via regression on the LLM's last hidden states, and (3) pairwise video preference judgments. The four evaluation dimensions—static quality, temporal smoothness, dynamic degree, and text-video correspondence—are assessed independently, reflecting that no single scalar score captures human visual preference for AIGVs.

## Method

AIGV-Assessor employs dual vision encoders—a 2D InternViT for per-frame spatial features and a 3D SlowFast network for temporal features—which are projected into a shared language space via separate spatiotemporal projection modules. These visual tokens are concatenated with text prompts and fed into an InternVL2-8B LLM that first generates discrete quality-level descriptions (bad through excellent) and then regresses continuous scores from its final hidden states using an L1 loss against ground-truth MOS. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) projector alignment with language loss, (2) LoRA fine-tuning of the vision encoder and LLM alongside the regression head, and (3) pairwise comparison training where two videos share weights in the same batch and an LPIPS-inspired judge network computes perceptual differences, optimized with cross-entropy loss. This Q&A reformulation of quality regression, combined with explicit temporal feature extraction, distinguishes the approach from prior LMM-based assessors that rely solely on 2D frame features.

## Results

On AIGVQA-DB, AIGV-Assessor achieves SRCC of 0.9162 (Static Quality), 0.9232 (Temporal Smoothness), 0.6093 (Dynamic Degree), and 0.7500 (TV Correspondence), with pairwise accuracy of 79.83%, 76.60%, 50.30%, and 70.32% respectively. It outperforms the strongest prior methods (DOVER, FAST-VQA, LIQE) by 1.7–6.9% in SRCC across all four dimensions. On external benchmarks, AIGV-Assessor also leads: on GAIA it reaches SRCC 0.6842 (Subject), 0.6635 (Completeness), and 0.6329 (Interaction), improving over DOVER by 6.7%, 14.4%, and 11.65% respectively; on T2VQA-DB it achieves SRCC 0.8131 vs. T2VQA's 0.7965.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIGV-Assessor outperforms all 30+ baselines across all four evaluation dimensions on AIGVQA-DB. On Static Quality, it surpasses the closest competitor DOVER (SRCC 0.8907) by 2.7% and LIQE (0.8776) by 4.4%, while also achieving the highest pairwise accuracy (79.83% vs. DOVER's 72.92%). On Temporal Smoothness, it leads DOVER (0.9063) and FAST-VQA (0.9036) by 1.7% and 1.9% SRCC respectively, with a notable 13.7% gain in pairwise accuracy over DOVER. On Dynamic Degree and TV Correspondence—dimensions where most baselines struggle—it improves over FAST-VQA by 4.4% and 6.3% SRCC, and over DOVER by 5.4% and 7.2% SRCC. Handcrafted methods (BRISQUE, HOSA, NIQE) and vision-language scores (CLIPScore, BLIPScore) remain far below, with SRCC typically below 0.30, confirming that generic image/video quality metrics cannot capture AIGV-specific distortions. On external datasets (LGVQ, FETV, T2VQA-DB, GAIA), AIGV-Assessor consistently achieves the best SRCC, with the largest margins on GAIA's Completeness and Interaction dimensions (+14.4% and +11.65% over DOVER), suggesting strong generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three research threads: (1) traditional no-reference VQA methods (DOVER, FAST-VQA, simpleVQA) that target PGC/UGC distortions like noise and blur, (2) the emerging AIGVQA literature (EvalCrafter, FETV, LGVQ, T2VQA-DB, GAIA) that begins to address AI-specific degradations but typically relies on single-dimension MOS scoring, and (3) LMM-based quality assessment (Q-Align for images, Video-LLaVA, Video-ChatGPT) that repurposes large multimodal models for scoring. AIGV-Assessor departs from prior AIGVQA work by introducing both absolute MOS and pairwise preference annotations across four orthogonal dimensions, and from prior LMM-based VQA by explicitly fusing 2D spatial (InternViT) and 3D temporal (SlowFast) features through dedicated projection modules before feeding them into an LLM decoder, rather than relying on a single video encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AIGVQA-DB (36,576 videos, 370k ratings, 15 T2V models) is currently the largest and most comprehensive AIGV quality benchmark, and its four-dimensional evaluation framework (static quality, temporal smoothness, dynamic degree, text-video correspondence) provides a more nuanced picture than prior single-score datasets. A key open question the paper surfaces is the persistent weakness of all existing methods on text-video correspondence, suggesting that current VQA architectures lack the mechanisms to jointly reason about prompt semantics and video dynamics. The finding that closed-source models (Sora, Gen-2) substantially outperform open-source counterparts across all dimensions also raises questions about whether open-source T2V research is tracking the true quality frontier. Limitations include the relatively short video lengths (8–200 frames) and the fact that the LMM-based approach requires multi-stage fine-tuning, limiting its zero-shot applicability to newly released T2V models.
