## Problem

Existing portrait animation methods rely on U-Net-based architectures that assume frontal, centered reference images, static backgrounds, and cannot handle dynamic foreground objects or non-frontal perspectives. Switching to a pretrained DiT-based video backbone for better generalization and dynamic scene generation renders prior U-Net-specific techniques for identity maintenance, audio conditioning, and video extrapolation inapplicable, requiring entirely new mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image (treated as one frame through a causal 3D VAE), a driving audio sequence (encoded via wav2vec's final 12 layers), a text prompt (encoded by T5), and motion frames (the last 2 frames of the preceding clip, zero-padded and concatenated with Gaussian noise) to produce a 49-frame video at 480×720 resolution. For long-duration generation, the last 2 frames of each 49-frame clip are recycled as motion frames for the next clip, enabling temporally consistent extrapolation beyond the model's native frame limit.

## Method

Built on CogVideoX's DiT architecture with a 3D VAE, the method introduces three key components: (1) an Identity Reference Network consisting of a causal 3D VAE encoder plus 42 transformer layers that extracts identity features from the reference image and injects them via self-attention into corresponding layers of the denoising network, maintaining semantic and scale consistency since both networks share the same VAE weights and layer count; (2) audio conditioning via cross-attention, where wav2vec embeddings (12 layers concatenated, then 3 successive linear projections) serve as keys/values against latent queries in dedicated audio-attention modules inserted after each face-attention layer; and (3) temporal motion frames that condition subsequent clip generation for long video. Training proceeds in two phases—first for identity-consistent video generation (freezing VAE and face encoder, training reference and denoising networks), then for audio-driven generation (freezing all but the audio attention modules)—using 64 A100 GPUs at 1e-5 learning rate with 20k steps per phase.

## Results

On the proposed Wild dataset, Hallo3 achieves the best lip synchronization (Sync-C 6.154, Sync-D 8.574) while generating the most dynamic videos (Subject Dynamic 13.286, Background Dynamic 4.481) and the lowest FVD for both subject (359.493) and background (248.283). On the standard HDTF benchmark, it attains the best FID (20.359) and FVD (160.838), though lip-sync scores (Sync-C 7.252, Sync-D 8.106) trail slightly behind SadTalker and Hallo. On Celeb-V, it similarly leads in FID (43.271) and FVD (355.272) with competitive lip-sync performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Wild dataset, Hallo3 dominates all four baselines across every metric, with the largest margins in dynamic generation: Subject Dynamic is 13.286 vs. 5.268 (Hallo), 6.958 (DreamTalk), 3.351 (AniPortrait), and 2.953 (SadTalker), and Background Dynamic is 4.481 vs. at most 1.806 (DreamTalk). On the standard HDTF benchmark, Hallo3 achieves the best FID (20.359) and FVD (160.838), narrowly beating Hallo (20.545 / 173.497), but trails SadTalker (Sync-C 7.885) and Hallo (Sync-C 7.750) on lip-sync accuracy. A similar pattern holds on Celeb-V: best FID (43.271) and FVD (355.272) over Hallo (44.578 / 377.117), but lower Sync-C (6.527 vs. 7.191 for Hallo) and higher Sync-D (9.113 vs. 7.984). The authors attribute the lip-sync gap on frontal benchmarks to their training data emphasizing diverse head poses and dynamic scenes rather than static frontal talking-head clips, trading some lip precision for generalization to non-frontal and dynamic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to repurpose a pretrained DiT-based video generation model (CogVideoX) for audio-driven portrait animation, departing from the U-Net-based diffusion architectures that underpin prior methods like Hallo, SadTalker, and DreamTalk. Because the DiT backbone is incompatible with the U-Net-specific identity maintenance, audio conditioning, and video extrapolation modules used in earlier work, the authors redesign all three: an identity reference network (causal 3D VAE + 42 transformer layers) replaces face-embedding injection, cross-attention with wav2vec features replaces U-Net audio conditioning, and motion-frame conditioning replaces U-Net-based video extrapolation. The approach also inherits 3D RoPE and T5 text encoding from CogVideoX, positioning it at the intersection of general-purpose video generation and the specialized portrait animation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a general-purpose DiT video model can be adapted to portrait animation while handling non-frontal poses, dynamic foreground/background objects, and complex accessories—scenarios where prior U-Net methods degrade—the paper opens a path toward more realistic and versatile talking-head synthesis. A notable trade-off is that lip-sync accuracy on standard frontal benchmarks (HDTF, Celeb-V) is slightly below SOTA, suggesting that the dynamic-scene capability comes at some cost to fine-grained audio-visual alignment. Open questions include whether the identity reference network scales to multi-subject scenes, whether the 49-frame-per-clip generation with motion-frame chaining introduces cumulative drift over very long videos, and whether the approach can be extended to real-time or interactive settings.
