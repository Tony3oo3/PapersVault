## Problem

Prior work on LVLM object hallucinations has focused primarily on language-side biases (e.g., text inertia, anchor patterns) while largely overlooking how visual information is processed internally. This paper asks whether improper handling of visual information in the model's middle layers is a root cause of hallucinations, and if so, how this process can be detected and corrected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252688/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LVLM inference: an RGB image (encoded into 576 image tokens for LLaVA-1.5, 32 for MiniGPT-4) plus a text prompt (e.g., 'Please help me describe the image in detail') produces a text caption. The intervention modifies attention scores assigned to image tokens in specific middle layers during autoregressive generation, requiring no additional forward passes or retraining—only a single scalar parameter α and a layer range [ℓs, ℓe] per model.

## Method

The paper first uses a Visual Attention Ratio (VAR) metric and logit lens decoding of image-token hidden states to identify two stages in the middle layers: a 'visual information enrichment' stage (layers 5–18 for LLaVA-1.5) where object tokens accumulate visual data, and a 'semantic refinement' stage (layers 19–26) where the model interprets that data through text. It shows that hallucinated tokens exhibit significantly lower VAR and inconsistent multi-head attention over image patches during the enrichment stage. The mitigation is a training-free inference-time intervention: for each image token in the enrichment layers, the pre-softmax attention score is augmented by α times the mean absolute attention score across all heads (Eq. 6), effectively amplifying attention in regions where heads agree and suppressing inconsistent, object-mixing signals. This head-integrated attention shift is applied at every decoding step and requires no extra forward pass, outperforming contrastive decoding baselines (PAI, VCD) and decoding-strategy baselines (OPERA, beam search) across LLaVA-1.5, Shikra, and MiniGPT-4.

## Results

On COCO 2014 (500 images, max 512 tokens), the proposed inference-time attention intervention achieves an average CHAIR_S of 24.0 and CHAIR_I of 8.2 across four LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, Shikra-7B, MiniGPT-4-7B), outperforming the second-best baseline PAI (29.8 / 8.7) by 19.5% and 5.7% respectively. The method also preserves description richness, achieving an average F1 of 74.2, comparable to PAI (75.2) and superior to VCD (72.0). For hallucination detection, the SVAR_{5-18} metric achieves AUROC of 74% and mAP of 88% on LLaVA-1.5-7B, improving AUROC by 8.82% over the internal confidence baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines across all four LVLMs on both CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I. Against the strongest baseline PAI (which also manipulates attention but requires an additional forward pass for contrastive decoding), the method reduces average CHAIR_S from 29.8 to 24.0 (−19.5%) and CHAIR_I from 8.7 to 8.2 (−5.7%), while achieving comparable F1 (74.2 vs. 75.2). Compared to OPERA, the method reduces average CHAIR_S by 37.9% (38.8→24.0) and CHAIR_I by 33.9% (12.4→8.2). VCD performs worse than even greedy decoding on average (52.5 vs. 48.1 CHAIR_S), indicating that contrastive decoding with distorted visual inputs can be counterproductive. The method also surpasses PAI's inference intervention component alone (Table 5), e.g., on Shikra-7B reducing CHAIR_S from 45.8 to 23.8, confirming that cross-head attention integration is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the logit lens technique (originally developed for LLM interpretability) to analyze image token hidden states in LVLMs, extending the three-stage knowledge retrieval framework identified in LLMs to the vision-language setting. It deliberately departs from prior hallucination research that attributed the problem primarily to language bias (OPERA's 'anchor pattern,' PAI's 'text inertia') by investigating the visual information processing side through attention analysis. The proposed inference-time attention intervention is positioned between decoding strategies (OPERA, beam search) and contrastive decoding methods (VCD, PAI), requiring no additional forward passes or training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of *where* and *how* visual information is processed in LVLMs (middle layers, split into enrichment and refinement stages), offering a concrete diagnostic (SVAR) for hallucination detection and a simple, training-free mitigation that generalizes across four architectures. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (COCO 2014, 500 images), the need to manually identify layer ranges per model, and moderate absolute improvements. Open questions include whether the layer-specific findings hold for newer LVLM architectures, whether the approach extends to video or multi-image settings, and whether combining attention intervention with contrastive decoding yields further gains.
