## Problem

Latent diffusion models for human motion generation cannot easily compose multiple semantic concepts into a single coherent motion, because they represent variable-length motions (40–196 frames) with a fixed number of latent vectors, lacking the explicit per-frame correspondence that skeleton-based composition methods rely on. This paper addresses the gap by reframing compositional motion generation as an energy-based model problem in latent space.

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or more text descriptions (encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14) and produces a 3D human motion sequence of variable length. For compositional generation, separate text prompts for each concept are provided and combined via conjunction or negation operators; for multi-concept generation, a single text containing multiple concepts is used. The motion VAE compresses variable-length motions into a fixed set of N=5 latent vectors (d=256), which constrains how composition must operate in latent space rather than per-frame.

## Method

ENERGYMOGEN builds on the MLD architecture (Motion VAE + denoising autoencoder with cross-attention) and introduces two spectrums of energy-based models: a latent-aware EBM that composes motions by combining denoiser outputs via classifier-free guidance (conjunction, Eq. 7) or conditional/unconditional ratios (negation, Eq. 8), and a semantic-aware EBM that composes cross-attention layer outputs via weighted averaging (Eq. 9) while refining text embeddings through adaptive gradient descent derived from an energy-based cross-attention formulation (Eqs. 3–4). The key insight is that the diffusion denoising step is equivalent to a Langevin dynamics MCMC step with unit step size, so composing energy functions corresponds to composing denoiser predictions. To mitigate text misalignment (latent-aware) and foot sliding/jitter (semantic-aware), Synergistic Energy Fusion (SEF, Eq. 10) linearly blends the latent-aware, semantic-aware, and single-text multi-concept distributions with learned weights λ_l, λ_s, λ_m summing to one.

## Results

On HumanML3D, ENERGYMOGEN achieves the best R-Precision (Top-1: 0.523, Top-2: 0.715, Top-3: 0.815) and second-best MM-Dist (2.915) among all compared methods, while maintaining competitive FID (0.188) and the highest Diversity (9.488) among latent diffusion methods. On the MTT compositional generation benchmark, ENERGYMOGEN with Synergistic Energy Fusion (SEF) achieves R@1 of 15.9 and R@3 of 28.0, substantially outperforming the best prior method (MLD at R@1 10.5, R@3 22.3) and reducing transition distance to 1.6. For multi-concept generation, adding adaptive gradient descent (AGD) pushes R@1 to 14.0 and FID to 0.587, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENERGYMOGEN outperforms all nine baselines on R-Precision (all three thresholds) and MM-Dist on HumanML3D, with the largest margin over MDM (+10.5% Top-1) and MLD (+4.2% Top-1). However, it trails ReMoDiffusion (FID 0.103) and GUESS (FID 0.109) on FID, and M2DM (3.587) and MDM (2.870) on MModality, indicating a slight trade-off in sample fidelity and modality coverage. On KIT-ML, ENERGYMOGEN achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 (0.436) but has a higher FID (0.495) than ReMoDiffusion (0.155) and FineMoGen (0.178). On the MTT compositional generation task, ENERGYMOGEN+SEF dominates all baselines across every metric (R@1 15.9 vs. best prior 15.1 for semantic-only; R@3 28.0 vs. 27.5; TMR M2T 0.591 vs. 0.585; transition distance 1.6 vs. 2.2), while for multi-concept generation, ENERGYMOGEN+AGD leads on R-Precision (14.0 vs. 10.5 for MLD) and FID (0.587 vs. 0.685 for MLD) but is slightly behind MLD on transition distance (2.7 vs. 2.4). The skeleton-based methods (FineMoGen, ReMoDiffuse) suffer from motion jitter that particularly hurts their compositional generation scores, whereas latent-based methods (MLD, GUESS, MotionMamba) lack the composition mechanism entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Motion Latent Diffusion (MLD) as its architectural backbone and on the energy-based cross-attention formulation of Park et al. (NeurIPS 2024) for semantic-aware composition. It departs from prior compositional motion generation work (TEACH, SINC, PriorMDM, GMD), which operates in skeleton space, by formulating composition in continuous latent space through an energy-based lens. The key conceptual move is interpreting the diffusion denoising step as a Langevin dynamics gradient step, thereby connecting classifier-free guidance to energy combination and enabling conjunction/negation operations on latent distributions. Synergistic Energy Fusion is a novel combination strategy that has no direct analogue in prior motion composition literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled energy-based framework for compositional motion generation in latent space, offering a general recipe (conjunction, negation, multi-concept) that could transfer to other latent diffusion domains. The CompML dataset and the demonstration that compositional generation serves as effective data augmentation open a practical path for improving text-to-motion models without additional human annotation. Open questions include whether the energy composition approach scales to more than a handful of concepts, how sensitive the method is to the quality of individual concept encodings, and whether the latent-space energy interpretation generalizes to other modalities (e.g., video, audio) where explicit per-frame correspondence is similarly absent.
