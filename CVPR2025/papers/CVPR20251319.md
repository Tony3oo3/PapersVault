## Problem

Existing blind face video restoration methods suffer from two critical limitations: (1) inefficient processing chains that require separate face detection, background restoration, and face restoration modules, leading to long inference times, and (2) limited temporal perceptive fields (e.g., only two neighboring frames) that fail to guarantee global temporal consistency, resulting in flickering artifacts. This paper addresses both challenges simultaneously through a unified video-level compression and quantization framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251319/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality face video sequence (T×H×W×3) degraded by Gaussian blur, downsampling, Gaussian noise, and FFMPEG compression as input, and produces a high-quality face video of the same resolution. The 3D-VQGAN backbone compresses the video into a latent space with spatial compression ratio 8× (H/h = W/w = 8) and temporal compression ratio 2× (T/t = 2), with latent dimension D=256. Training uses 24-frame clips at 256×256 (Stage I) and 512×512 (Stage II), and the framework handles both restoration and de-flickering (brightness and pixel-level) without requiring separate face detection or background separation.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage 3D-VQGAN framework with spatial-temporal codebooks. Stage I self-supervisedly trains a 3D encoder-decoder and two codebooks (spatial codebook for portrait features, temporal codebook for inter-frame motion residuals computed via temporal attention and frame-difference) using a DINOv2-based frozen feature network with trainable multi-scale discriminator heads, plus a marginal prior regularization that regularizes codebook usage via accumulated similarity scores rather than one-hot index counts to prevent codebook collapse. Stage II freezes the codebooks and decoder, then trains an LQ encoder and two transformer-based lookup modules that predict spatial and temporal code index sequences from degraded inputs via cross-entropy classification, enabling direct mapping from LQ video to quantized representations without iterative refinement. The temporal latents incorporate both temporal attention and motion residuals, allowing the temporal codebook to capture dynamic changes independently of static portrait content stored in the spatial codebook.

## Results

On the VFHQ-Test dataset for blind face video restoration (BFVR), the proposed method achieves SSIM 0.8641, FVD 105.1, Flow-Score 1.150, and IDS 0.9312, outperforming PGTFormer (SSIM 0.8426, FVD 107.6) and KEEP (SSIM 0.8223, FVD 264.9) on temporal consistency and identity preservation metrics. The method runs in 2.995 s per 24-frame video, roughly 2.4× faster than PGTFormer (7.085 s) and 6.3× faster than KEEP (19.01 s). For de-flickering, the method achieves Flow-Score 1.100 (brightness) and 1.063 (pixel) with a runtime of ~2.9 s, compared to NeuralAtlas's 1.468/1.471 Flow-Score at 851–854 s runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two dedicated BFVR baselines, the proposed method wins on SSIM (0.8641 vs. 0.8426 for PGTFormer, 0.8223 for KEEP), FVD (105.1 vs. 107.6 and 264.9), Flow-Score (1.150 vs. 1.154 and 1.302), IDS (0.9312 vs. 0.9296 and 0.8783), and Face-Consistency (0.9954 vs. 0.9942 and 0.9953), while PGTFormer retains a slight edge on PSNR (28.68 vs. 27.47) and LPIPS (0.1752 vs. 0.1829). The efficiency gain is the most striking: 2.995 s per 24-frame clip is 2.4× faster than PGTFormer and 6.3× faster than KEEP, which requires an external RealESRGAN model and face detector. Against image-based BFIR methods (VQFR, GFPGAN, CodeFormer) and video super-resolution methods (BasicVSR++, Real-BasicVSR), the proposed method dominates on temporal consistency (FVD 105.1 vs. 246.9–392.7) while matching or exceeding their spatial quality. For de-flickering, the method outperforms NeuralAtlas on both Flow-Score (1.100 vs. 1.468 for brightness; 1.063 vs. 1.471 for pixel) and runtime (2.9 s vs. 851 s), though DVP achieves lower FVD (14.53 vs. 100.7) at the cost of requiring a ground-truth reference video and 410 s runtime. FastBlend, which also needs a reference video, is faster (18.4 s) but yields worse Flow-Score (1.574 vs. 1.100) and FVD (34.58 vs. 100.7 for brightness).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQGAN paradigm (Esser et al., CVPR 2021) from 2D images to 3D video by introducing spatial-temporal codebooks that separately encode portrait features and inter-frame motion residuals. It builds directly on CodeFormer's (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2022) codebook lookup transformer for face restoration, but replaces the single spatial codebook with a dual spatial-temporal design and adds a marginal prior regularization to address codebook collapse more effectively than prior one-hot-based frequency counting. The discriminator design borrows the frozen pre-trained feature network strategy from adversarial diffusion distillation (Sauer et al., 2023), applying DINOv2 as a backbone with lightweight trainable heads. Unlike prior BFVR methods (PGTFormer, KEEP, StableBFVR) that chain multiple models or rely on limited temporal windows, this work unifies compression, quantization, and restoration in a single 3D-VQGAN framework with a two-stage training protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is a substantial efficiency gain: ~2.4× faster than PGTFormer and ~6× faster than KEEP for BFVR, and ~290× faster than NeuralAtlas for de-flickering, while maintaining comparable temporal consistency (FVD) and identity preservation. This makes real-time or near-real-time face video enhancement more feasible for applications like live broadcasting. However, the evaluation is limited to a small test set (50 videos × 24 frames) and the PSNR is actually lower than PGTFormer (27.47 vs 28.68), suggesting a quality-efficiency trade-off rather than a strict Pareto improvement. Open questions include whether the 3D-VQGAN approach generalizes to longer videos, non-frontal faces, or arbitrary (non-face) video content, and whether the marginal prior regularization transfers to other VQ-based architectures beyond face restoration.
