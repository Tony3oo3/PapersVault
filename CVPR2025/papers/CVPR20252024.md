## Problem

Existing object removal methods follow a 'mask-and-inpaint' paradigm that excludes the masked region from the input, causing two failures: the model may regenerate unintended objects in the masked area (lacking precise knowledge of what to remove), and user masks that extend beyond the target object lead to unnecessary modification of surrounding context. The paper also notes that no large-scale dataset with true (input, mask, ground-truth) triplets exists for training under a paradigm that retains the masked region.

## Input / Output

The model takes the full original RGB image (with the target object still visible), a binary mask indicating the removal region, and a fixed text prompt ('Remove the instance of') as input. Unlike conventional inpainting where the masked region is replaced with a black placeholder, here the complete image is retained and the mask serves purely as guidance. The output is a photorealistic image with the target object erased and surrounding context preserved, generated via a fine-tuned SD v1.5 latent diffusion model.

## Method

SmartEraser introduces the Masked-Region Guidance paradigm, which conditions the diffusion model on the full image (including the masked region) plus the mask, rather than excluding the masked area. To train under this paradigm without the model simply copying the masked content, the authors construct Syn4Removal, a 1-million-triplet dataset where object instances from OpenImages are alpha-blended onto COCONut/SAM-1B backgrounds, with the original backgrounds serving as ground truth. Two additional components improve robustness: (1) mask enhancement applies six deformation types (erosion, dilation, convex hull, ellipse, bbox+Bessel) during training to simulate imperfect user masks, and (2) CLIP-based visual guidance encodes the masked region with a frozen CLIP vision encoder, maps it via a trainable MLP into the text embedding space, and appends it to the prompt tokens. The full UNet, text encoder, and MLP are fine-tuned from SD v1.5 with a standard denoising diffusion loss over 500k iterations.

## Results

On the most challenging RORD-Val benchmark (real photography with large masks and complex scenes), SmartEraser achieves FID 16.03, CMMD 0.092, ReMOVE 0.937, LPIPS 0.276, SSIM 0.612, and PSNR 19.99 dB, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art PowerPaint by 10.3 FID and 1.89 dB PSNR. On the simpler DEFACTO-Val, SmartEraser reaches FID 3.41 and PSNR 25.36 dB, and on Syn4Removal-Val it achieves FID 4.39 and PSNR 22.03 dB, all leading every baseline across all six metrics on all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmartEraser outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three benchmarks. Among GAN-based inpainting methods, LaMa is the strongest (FID 24.24 on RORD-Val) but still trails SmartEraser by 8.2 FID; MAT and BLD perform considerably worse (FID > 70). Among diffusion-based inpainting methods, SD-Inpaint (FID 69.50) and RePaint (FID 71.54) struggle significantly with object regeneration in complex scenes. The two dedicated object-removal methods, CLIPAway (FID 25.46) and PowerPaint (FID 24.06), are the closest competitors but both still follow the 'mask-and-inpaint' paradigm; SmartEraser's Masked-Region Guidance paradigm yields a 10.3 FID improvement over PowerPaint and a 1.89 dB PSNR gain on RORD-Val. In the user study, SmartEraser receives 56% preference for image quality, 65% for foreground-background consistency, and 58% for removal accuracy, compared to at most 18%, 12%, and 21% for the next-best method (LaMa, CLIPAway, and LaMa respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

SmartEraser builds on the Stable Diffusion v1.5 architecture but fundamentally departs from the dominant 'mask-and-inpaint' paradigm used by prior object removal methods (CLIPAway, PowerPaint, SD-Inpaint), which exclude the masked region from the input and rely solely on surrounding context. Instead, it introduces Masked-Region Guidance, which retains the masked region in the input as explicit guidance for what to remove. The CLIP-based visual guidance component draws on the same CLIP encoder used by CLIPAway but repurposes it to encode the removal target's appearance rather than background context, and the synthetic data construction pipeline (copy-paste with alpha blending) is inspired by instance segmentation augmentation strategies like X-Paste.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Masked-Region Guidance paradigm offers a clean, minimal conceptual change—simply keeping the masked region in the input—that addresses two persistent failure modes (object regeneration and context loss) in object removal, making it a likely default approach for future work in this area. The Syn4Removal dataset (1M triplets with real ground-truth backgrounds) fills a significant gap, as prior datasets were either tiny (<3.5k scenes) or relied on inpainting models for pseudo-ground-truths. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes to other editing tasks (e.g., object replacement, style transfer), whether it transfers to newer diffusion backbones (SDXL, Flux), and whether the synthetic training distribution limits performance on truly novel real-world compositions.
