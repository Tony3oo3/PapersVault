## Problem

Current image-to-video diffusion models struggle to simultaneously preserve the appearance of the input image and generate natural, text-controllable motions. Methods like ConsistI2V over-rely on the conditional image, producing near-static videos, while DynamiCrafter's dual cross-attention neglects temporal motion properties, yielding uncontrollable dynamics that contradict the text prompt. No prior work has applied model merging techniques to the I2V domain to decouple and independently control motion degree versus motion controllability.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single reference image (resized to 320×512 center-cropped) and a free-form text prompt (not limited to class labels) as input, and generates a 16-frame, 2-second video clip at the same resolution. Inference uses DDIM with 50 denoising steps and a classifier-free guidance scale of 7.5. The text condition is processed by a CLIP text encoder and further compressed by a Q-Former to retain motion-relevant tokens before injection into the temporal attention module.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages on top of the DynamiCrafter base model. First, a lightweight Q-Former adapter injects text embeddings into the temporal attention module, and only the adapter and temporal attention weights are fine-tuned on WebVid-2M to improve motion controllability without altering the base architecture. Second, a training-free extrapolation strategy amplifies motion degree by reversing the fine-tuning direction: θ_dyn = θ_pre + α(θ_pre − θ_sft), with a Taylor-expansion-based proof showing this monotonically increases a motion-degree metric. Third, parameters are decoupled into control (θ_adt), degree (θ_deg), and consistency (θ_con) sets via DARE pruning at 70% sparsity, then Task-Arithmetic merges control into both degree and consistency models; during sampling, the degree-enhanced model is used for the first K denoising steps (long-term temporal planning) and the consistency-enhanced model for the remaining steps (appearance refinement), enabling dynamic switching between motion dynamics and visual fidelity.

## Results

On the VBench I2V benchmark, the proposed method (DynamiCrafter-Ours) achieves an average score of 84.25, substantially outperforming the base DynamiCrafter (79.93) and all other baselines. The headline gains are in Motion Degree (87.64 vs. 68.54 for DynamiCrafter, 11.67 for Naive FT) and Motion Control (43.87 vs. 30.85 for DynamiCrafter, 17.82 for Naive FT). On UCF101, the method achieves the best Inception Score (13.71) and Motion Degree (77.93) among DynamiCrafter-based variants, though FVD (324.79) is slightly worse than the unmodified base model (288.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on Motion Degree (87.64) and Motion Control (43.87), the two metrics most relevant to the paper's contribution. Against the base DynamiCrafter, it improves Motion Degree by +19.1 points and Motion Control by +13.0 points while maintaining comparable consistency scores. Compared to the CIL-based methods (CIL Infer: 69.43 degree / 31.29 control; CIL FT: 25.69 / 30.50), the gap is even larger, showing that the extrapolation-plus-decoupling strategy far exceeds what inference-time noise scheduling or simple fine-tuning repair can achieve. Against large-scale pretrained models like SVD-XT-1.0 (52.36 degree) and Animate-Anything (2.68 degree), the method's advantage is most striking, demonstrating that targeted motion modeling without additional pretraining can surpass models trained on far larger video corpora. The method trades a small amount of I2V Subject/Background Consistency (97.03/96.62 vs. 98.76/98.58 for Animate-Anything) for dramatically higher motion, and on UCF101 it achieves the best IS (13.71) and Motion Degree (77.93) but a slightly higher FVD (324.79 vs. 288.01) than the unmodified DynamiCrafter, suggesting a minor quality trade-off at low resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DynamiCrafter as its base I2V diffusion model and addresses the conditional image leakage problem identified by Zhao et al. [46], where fine-tuning for text controllability inadvertently suppresses motion dynamics. Its key conceptual departure is importing model merging techniques (Task-Arithmetic, DARE pruning) from the NLP and image classification literature into the video generation domain for the first time, using them to decouple motion-controllability and motion-degree parameters and inject them at different denoising stages. The lightweight Q-Former adapter for injecting text into temporal attention is a minimal architectural modification, while the training-free extrapolation (reversing the fine-tuning direction in weight space) is a novel application of weight-space arithmetic to amplify a specific capability without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, largely training-free recipe for improving both motion degree and controllability in I2V models, which are typically in tension. The decoupling insight—that early denoising steps benefit from motion-degree parameters while later steps need consistency parameters—provides a structured way to compose capabilities without retraining. However, the approach is demonstrated primarily on DynamiCrafter at 320×512 resolution, and whether the extrapolation and decoupling generalize to other I2V architectures (e.g., SVD, VideoCrafter) or to longer/higher-resolution videos remains open. The reliance on a single fine-tuning dataset (WebVid-2M) and the sensitivity of the extrapolation strength α to the base model's motion degree also limit immediate transferability.
