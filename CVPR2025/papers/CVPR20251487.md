## Problem

Existing human-scene reconstruction methods either handle the human or the scene in isolation, require pre-calibrated camera poses, or demand days of offline training. No prior work simultaneously performs camera tracking, human pose estimation, and dense photorealistic reconstruction of both the human avatar and the surrounding scene in a fully online manner from a single monocular video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251487/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB video and, in an online streaming fashion, produces a joint 3D Gaussian representation of the scene and an animatable human avatar, per-frame camera poses, SMPL human pose/shape parameters, and occlusion-aware human silhouettes. Auxiliary off-the-shelf models (WHAM for initial pose, ViT-Pose for 2D keypoints, Depth Anything V2 for monocular depth, SAM for segmentation) provide priors but are not part of the learned pipeline. Only a small sliding window of keyframes is retained for mapping, keeping memory bounded during unbounded video processing.

## Method

ODHSR builds a unified 3DGS-based SLAM pipeline with two parallel threads: a tracking thread that jointly optimizes camera and human poses per frame, and a mapping thread that refines the holistic Gaussian map over a local keyframe window. The human avatar is represented by 3D Gaussians in canonical space, deformed by SMPL-driven LBS for rigid joint motion and a time-pose-conditioned multiresolution hash encoding network for non-rigid garment and hair dynamics plus ambient occlusion. A key design choice is occlusion-aware human silhouette rendering, which computes per-pixel human opacity by accumulating transmittance over all scene Gaussians in front, enabling faithful human-scene disentanglement. The joint optimization combines RGB photometric loss, optical flow consistency, scale-and-shift-aligned monocular depth loss, silhouette loss, and 2D keypoint loss, with the depth alignment solved per-frame via least squares to recover correct human-scene spatial scale. Regularizations (LBS weight supervision, canonical center anchoring, deformation magnitude penalty) prevent overfitting to the limited training poses in the keyframe window and improve generalization to unseen poses.

## Results

On the EMDB dataset, ODHSR (Full) achieves 23.79 dB PSNR and 0.767 SSIM on whole-image novel view synthesis, and 28.96 dB PSNR on human-only regions, outperforming all holistic human-scene baselines by a large margin (e.g., +2.18 dB PSNR over HUGS on whole images). On NeuMan, ODHSR reaches 26.47 dB PSNR (whole) and 31.73 dB PSNR (human-only), remaining competitive with HUGS (26.67 dB whole) while operating fully online. For camera tracking, ODHSR achieves an ATE of 8.4 cm, comparable to DROID-SLAM, and for global human pose estimation it reduces WA-MPJPE from 636.0 mm (WHAM) to 175.2 mm. Rendering runs at 85 FPS in real time, with training at 0.141 FPS on EMDB—orders of magnitude faster than HSR (0.002 FPS) and Vid2Avatar (<10⁻³ FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the holistic human-scene baselines, ODHSR clearly wins on EMDB: it surpasses HUGS by +2.18 dB PSNR (whole) and +2.79 dB (human), HSR by +5.12 dB (whole), and Vid2Avatar by +7.13 dB (whole). On NeuMan, the gap narrows—HUGS edges ODHSR by 0.20 dB on whole-image PSNR (26.67 vs 26.47) and by 1.59 dB on human-only PSNR (30.14 vs 31.73 is actually ODHSR winning), while 3DGS-Avatar (32.92 dB human-only) remains the strongest human-only method but does not reconstruct the scene. In terms of runtime, ODHSR renders at 85 FPS and trains at 0.141 FPS, making it 3.4× faster than HUGS in training and 70× faster than HSR, while HSR and Vid2Avatar are effectively offline (training <0.002 FPS). For camera tracking, ODHSR's ATE of 8.4 cm is on par with DROID-SLAM, and for global human pose it dramatically outperforms the WHAM initialization (175.2 mm vs 636.0 mm WA-MPJPE). Overall, ODHSR is the only method that simultaneously achieves competitive reconstruction quality, real-time rendering, and online operation without requiring pre-calibrated cameras or ground-truth depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODHSR extends Gaussian Splatting SLAM [41] by incorporating monocular depth priors (Depth Anything) and SMPL-based human pose/shape priors to jointly track camera and human poses while mapping both scene and avatar with 3D Gaussians. It departs from prior holistic human-scene reconstruction methods (HSR [78], HUGS [31]) that operate offline over days or require pre-calibrated cameras, by formulating the problem as an online SLAM pipeline with a small keyframe window. The human avatar representation combines LBS-driven rigid deformation with a multiresolution hash-encoding network for non-rigid garment dynamics, building on ideas from 3DGS-Avatar [50] and HumanNeRF [71] but in a tracking-compatible, gradient-friendly form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to perform dense photorealistic reconstruction of both a human and the surrounding scene in a fully online, single-pass manner from monocular video, achieving 75× speedup over prior offline holistic methods and 85 FPS rendering. For robotics and AR applications requiring real-time human-scene understanding, this removes the multi-day training bottleneck and the need for pre-calibrated cameras. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with a single human, reliance on WHAM for pose initialization, and the local keyframe window strategy that may struggle with very long sequences or multiple interacting people. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-person scenes, unstructured outdoor environments with heavy occlusion, and whether the hash-encoding deformation module can be replaced with lighter alternatives for even faster convergence.
