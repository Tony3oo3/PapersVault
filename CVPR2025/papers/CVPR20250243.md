## Problem

Diffusion models for synthetic medical image generation face a paradox: they require large training datasets to produce high-fidelity images, yet they are deployed precisely because annotated medical data is scarce. Mask-only diffusion models produce images lacking critical morphological characteristics (surface texture, color), while mask-image joint models achieve fidelity at the cost of diversity and scalability, ultimately failing to improve downstream segmentation reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250243/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB medical images (384×384) and their corresponding segmentation masks, encoding images into VQ-VAE latent space. During sampling, only an arbitrary segmentation mask is required as input—no paired image is needed—producing a 384×384 synthetic medical image via 50-step DDIM sampling. Masks can be real or transformed (e.g., scaled), enabling unlimited generation without additional annotated pairs.

## Method

Siamese-Diffusion trains a single ControlNet-based diffusion model under two conditions simultaneously: Mask-Diffusion (mask-only prior) and Image-Diffusion (mask+image prior), sharing parameters via a deep copy. The key mechanism is a Noise Consistency Loss that uses the more accurate noise prediction from Image-Diffusion (stop-gradient applied) as an anchor, steering Mask-Diffusion's convergence toward higher-fidelity local minima in parameter space—distinct from classifier-free guidance which operates in sample space. A Dense Hint Input module with residual blocks (16–256 channels) and patch merging replaces the sparse Hint Input to capture fine texture from high-density medical images. An Online-Augmentation module leverages Image-Diffusion's single-step denoising to generate additional training samples for Mask-Diffusion, further expanding its effective training set. At inference, only Mask-Diffusion is deployed, preserving the diversity and scalability of the mask-only paradigm while retaining the morphological fidelity learned through the consistency loss.

## Results

On the Polyps dataset, Siamese-Diffusion achieves the best overall segmentation performance across three architectures: SANet reaches 83.0% mDice and 75.8% mIoU, Polyp-PVT reaches 84.4% mDice and 77.3% mIoU, and CTNet reaches 85.1% mDice and 78.1% mIoU. On ISIC2018, UNet with random-mask synthesis achieves 83.71% mDice and 80.09% mIoU, while SegFormer reaches 91.93% mDice and 87.12% mIoU. For synthetic image quality on Polyps, the method attains the best FID (62.706), KID (0.0395), LPIPS (0.586), and MOS (0.587) among all mask-only methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Siamese-Diffusion outperforms all six baselines across every segmentation model and dataset. Against the previous SOTA ArSDM, the gains are most pronounced for SANet (+3.6% mDice, +4.4% mIoU) and moderate for Polyp-PVT (+0.4% mDice, +0.6% mIoU) and CTNet (+0.9% mDice, +1.1% mIoU). Compared to ControlNet with mask-only prior, improvements range from +1.4% to +2.5% mDice; notably, ControlNet with mask-image joint prior (M+I) does not consistently beat its mask-only variant, confirming that image quality alone is insufficient without morphological diversity. Against the simplest Copy-Paste augmentation, the method yields +1.1% to +1.9% mDice gains. On the Stain and Faeces datasets (Table 4), where no real training samples are used, Siamese-Diffusion dramatically outperforms AnomalyDiffusion (e.g., 94.11% vs. 81.59% mDice on Faeces) and even surpasses the real-data baseline, demonstrating superior scalability in extreme data-scarce settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ControlNet and Stable Diffusion as its architectural backbone, adapting the conditional diffusion framework for medical image synthesis. It departs from prior mask-only synthesis methods (SinGAN-Seg, T2I-Adapter, ArSDM) by introducing a Siamese training paradigm where the same diffusion model is simultaneously trained under mask-only and mask+image prior controls, with a Noise Consistency Loss transferring morphological knowledge from the image-guided branch to the mask-only branch in parameter space. This distinguishes it from knowledge distillation (which requires separate teacher/student networks) and from classifier-free guidance (which adjusts fidelity in sample space at inference time). The Online-Augmentation module borrows the single-step sampling technique from ControlNet++ to expand the training set without additional paired data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine paradox in medical AI: generative models meant to alleviate data scarcity for segmentation themselves suffer from the same scarcity, and the resulting low-fidelity synthetic images can undermine segmentation reliability. The finding that higher image fidelity (via mask+image joint control) can actually *decrease* downstream segmentation performance—because it reduces morphological diversity—challenges the common assumption that better images always yield better segmentation. Open questions include the unexplained performance decline at 4× data augmentation (attributed to a 'long-tail problem'), whether the Noise Consistency Loss generalizes beyond 2D medical imaging, and whether the method's reliance on paired image-mask training data (albeit fewer pairs than M+I methods) limits its applicability in truly data-scarce regimes.
