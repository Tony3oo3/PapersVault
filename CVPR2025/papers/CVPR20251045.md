## Problem

Existing 3DGS and NeRF methods rely on a pinhole camera model, making them unable to model defocus blur or support post-capture adjustment of focus and aperture. Prior defocus-aware variants use black-box neural networks to predict per-pixel or per-Gaussian blur kernels, which lack a physical camera model and therefore cannot enable intuitive, continuous post-capture control of depth-of-field effects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251045/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view defocused RGB images (tested at 600×400 up to 2400×1600), COLMAP-estimated camera poses, and a sparse point cloud as initialization. At inference, the user specifies an aperture parameter Q and focal distance f per rendered view; setting Q=0 yields an all-in-focus image, while non-zero Q produces physically consistent defocus. The system also outputs per-pixel depth maps, circle-of-confusion maps, and in-focus region masks as byproducts of the rendering pipeline.

## Method

DOF-GS extends the 3DGS rasterizer with a differentiable finite-aperture camera model that has two learnable parameters per training view: a simplified aperture Q=FA and focal distance f. During rendering, each projected 2D Gaussian is convolved with an isotropic Gaussian blur kernel whose width is analytically derived from the per-Gaussian circle-of-confusion radius (Eq. 6–9), and the blurred Gaussians are then alpha-composited; the entire pipeline is differentiable and implemented in a custom CUDA rasterizer. A lightweight In-Focus Localization Network (four 2D conv layers) predicts per-pixel in-focus masks from the rendered CoC map, defocused image, and depth map, which are used to composite all-in-focus and defocused renderings for a detail-enhancement loss. Training jointly optimizes the 3D Gaussians, per-view camera parameters, and ILN weights under a combined objective of reconstruction, detail, mask–CoC correlation, and binary-regularization losses over 40,000 iterations.

## Results

On the Deblur-NeRF Real dataset (600×400), DOF-GS achieves the best SSIM of 0.7667, with a PSNR of 24.12 dB that is competitive with the top methods (PDRF: 24.31, Deblur-GS: 24.21). On the Synthetic dataset, DOF-GS again attains the highest SSIM (0.8914) while achieving a PSNR of 29.39 dB, slightly below PDRF (29.96) and Deblur-GS (29.43). At higher resolutions (1200×800 and 2400×1600), DOF-GS uses 13.06 GB and 39.05 GB GPU memory respectively, compared to 21.69 GB for BAGS at 1200×800, demonstrating notable memory efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOF-GS achieves the best SSIM on both the Real (0.7667) and Synthetic (0.8914) datasets, edging out BAGS (0.7664 / 0.8859) and Deblur-GS (0.7631 / 0.8907). On PSNR, DOF-GS (24.12 real / 29.39 synthetic) is slightly behind PDRF (24.31 / 29.96) and Deblur-GS (24.21 / 29.43), which the authors attribute to the stricter global two-parameter camera model reducing flexibility versus per-pixel kernel estimation. Compared to the NeRF-based baselines (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF), DOF-GS matches or exceeds their SSIM while remaining competitive on PSNR, and does so with real-time rendering speed inherent to 3DGS rather than the sub-1 FPS volume rendering of NeRF methods. Against the 3DGS baselines Mip-Splatting and BAGS, DOF-GS clearly outperforms on both metrics and uses substantially less GPU memory at high resolutions (13.06 GB vs. 21.69 GB at 1200×800). Overall, the paper positions DOF-GS as achieving comparable reconstruction quality to state-of-the-art deblurring methods while uniquely enabling post-capture DOF control, which none of the baselines support.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOF-GS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [Kerbl et al., 2023] by replacing the pinhole camera assumption with a finite-aperture thin-lens model, enabling physically grounded depth-of-field rendering. It departs from prior NeRF-based defocus methods (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF) that predict per-pixel blur kernels without explicit camera geometry, and from recent 3DGS-based deblurring approaches (Deblur-GS, BAGS) that use black-box neural networks for blur prediction. The differentiable Gaussian-convolution rendering is inspired by surface-splatting [Krivanek et al., 2003], but is made fully differentiable to support joint optimization of scene and camera parameters. The In-Focus Localization Network introduces a lightweight CoC-guided supervision signal absent from prior work, addressing the detail-degradation problem when training on defocused inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling post-capture aperture and focal-distance control in real-time 3DGS rendering, a capability previously limited to specialized hardware (light-field, coded-aperture cameras) or computationally expensive NeRF pipelines. However, the method achieves only comparable (not superior) All-in-Focus reconstruction quality to existing deblurring baselines, suggesting the two-parameter camera model trades flexibility for physical interpretability. A key open question is recovering sharp details in regions that are out-of-focus in all training views, and the authors identify camera pose optimization during training as a promising direction. For AR/VR and mobile photography applications, the combination of real-time rendering, low memory overhead at high resolutions, and intuitive DOF controls makes this a practically relevant contribution.
