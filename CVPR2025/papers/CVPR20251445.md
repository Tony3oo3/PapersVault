## Problem

Instruction-guided image editing datasets (e.g., InstructPix2Pix) are generated by combining Prompt-to-Prompt with LLMs, but the resulting image pairs frequently exhibit visual changes that do not match their assigned edit instructions. This misalignment propagates into models trained on such data, producing edits that ignore or contradict the user's instruction. The paper asks whether the residual signal in these imperfect datasets can be leveraged to automatically correct and refine the instructions without manual annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251445/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For dataset refinement, the method consumes triplets of (original image, edited image, original instruction) and outputs a refined instruction that better describes the actual visual transformation. For the editing model, input is a single original image plus a text edit instruction, and output is the edited image generated by a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion UNet. The I-CLIP visual encoder operates on noisy latent representations (VAE-encoded + forward-diffusion noise at a sampled timestep) rather than clean pixel-space images, enabling it to serve as a training-time loss inside the diffusion pipeline at any denoising step.

## Method

Instruct-CLIP pairs a modified visual encoder (two shared-weight DINOv2 modules feeding a CLIP image encoder, which encodes the *difference* between original and edited images via feature subtraction with skip-connection-style intermediate layer injections) with a standard CLIP text encoder, trained with a symmetric contrastive loss to align visual-change embeddings with instruction embeddings. A DeCap-style GPT-2 text decoder is trained to reconstruct instructions from text-feature embeddings; at inference, the visual-change embedding is projected into the text feature space via a softmax-weighted average over all dataset text features (weighted by cosine similarity) before decoding, bridging the ~0.2 cosine-similarity gap between visual and text CLIP spaces. A latent-diffusion variant (LD-DINOv2) is trained to recover clean DINOv2 features from noisy SD latents given the diffusion timestep, allowing I-CLIP to function inside the diffusion training loop. The final editing model is IP2P fine-tuned on 120K+ refined samples with an additional I-CLIP loss (Eq. 12) that penalizes mismatch between the predicted denoised latent's visual-change embedding and the refined instruction's text embedding, weighted at λ=0.1 alongside the standard MSE noise-prediction loss.

## Results

On the MagicBrush single-turn benchmark, the method achieves CLIP-T 0.305, CLIP-I 0.911, and DINO-I 0.803, improving DINO-I by 24.5% over InstructPix2Pix (0.645) and by 7.5% over HIVE (0.746). On the multi-turn MagicBrush split, DINO-I reaches 0.721 (+25.8% over IP2P), and on ZONE the method scores CLIP-T 0.297, matching or exceeding all baselines. In a user study with 104 participants, the method is preferred over MagicBrush 54.2% of the time and over IP2P with a 17.8% win-margin advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method substantially outperforms InstructPix2Pix across all metrics, with the largest gains in DINO-I (+24.5% single-turn, +25.8% multi-turn), indicating much better preservation of visual structure in edited regions. Against HIVE and Inst-Inpaint, the method wins on every reported metric, with DINO-I improvements of 7.5–10% over HIVE and 10% over I-Inp. However, the method trails Watch Your Steps (WYS) on CLIP-T (0.305 vs. 0.313 single-turn) and MagicBrush on CLIP-I/DINO-I (0.911/0.803 vs. 0.929/0.836), where WYS and MagBr leverage explicit masking or inpainting mechanisms for tighter localization. On the ZONE benchmark (no ground-truth edited image), the method achieves CLIP-T 0.297, matching HIVE and IP2P while slightly trailing WYS (0.301) and ZONE (0.296). The user study partially reconciles the metric gap: despite lower CLIP-I than MagBr, participants preferred the method 54.2% of the time, suggesting that CLIP-I/DINO-I do not fully capture perceptual edit quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts CLIP's contrastive dual-encoder architecture to the instruction-guided image editing domain, replacing the standard image encoder with a DINOv2-based module that encodes the *difference* between original and edited images rather than a single image. It leverages DeCap's text-decoding and weighted-projection strategy to translate learned visual-change embeddings back into refined natural-language instructions, and builds its editing model directly on top of InstructPix2Pix's denoising UNet formulation. The key departure from prior work is the dual-purpose use of the contrastive model: not only as a post-hoc dataset refinement tool but also as an in-loop training loss (L_I-CLIP) that enforces instruction–visual-change alignment at every diffusion timestep via a novel LD-DINOv2 backbone that operates on noisy latent images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in instruction-guided editing—training data where instructions systematically misalign with actual visual changes—and provides a practical, self-supervised pipeline to correct this without manual annotation. The 120K+ refined IP2P samples and the I-CLIP-guided loss offer a reusable recipe that other diffusion-based editors could adopt. However, the method remains constrained by the quality of the underlying Prompt-to-Prompt generation (color bleeding, incomplete object addition), and the model still struggles with object removal. Open questions include whether the contrastive alignment loss generalizes beyond IP2P-style architectures and whether the approach can be extended to inpainting-based or multi-modal editing settings.
