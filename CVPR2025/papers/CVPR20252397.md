## Problem

Existing diffusion-based super-resolution methods rely primarily on text prompts for conditioning, but text cannot encode spatial relationships, leading to hallucinated details that are plausible yet inconsistent with the low-resolution input (e.g., a furry lion tongue). This paper asks whether integrating spatial modalities—depth, segmentation, edges—alongside text can reduce the uncertainty of the generative prior and suppress such artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252397/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, a single low-resolution image is the sole user input; four auxiliary modalities are automatically extracted from it: a text caption via Gemini Flash, a depth map via Depth Anything, a semantic segmentation mask via Mask2Former, and a Canny edge map. Each spatial modality is encoded by a pretrained VQGAN into a 16×16 discrete token grid (256-dim features, padded to 1024), while the caption is encoded by CLIP into 77 text tokens, yielding a combined (256×3+77)×1024 conditioning sequence. The output is a high-resolution image (512×512 or 1024×1024) generated via 50-step DDIM sampling from a Stable Diffusion v2–sized latent diffusion model.

## Method

The architecture encodes all spatial modalities into discrete VQGAN tokens and concatenates them with CLIP text embeddings, then compresses this long sequence into 128 fixed-length latent tokens via a Multimodal Latent Connector (MMLC), a Perceiver-style transformer whose cross-attention reduces complexity from O(M²) to O(MN). These latent tokens condition the diffusion U-Net through standard cross-attention, requiring no architectural modification to the pretrained text-to-image backbone. A key innovation is Multimodal Classifier-Free Guidance (m-CFG), which conditions both the positive and negative denoising branches on the multimodal tokens, mitigating hallucination at high guidance scales where standard CFG degrades fidelity. Fine-grained per-modality control is achieved by scaling the attention temperature δ in the MMLC cross-attention, allowing users to amplify or suppress the influence of depth, segmentation, or edges independently. A learnable absence token (m_∅) is randomly substituted during training (p=0.1) to make the model robust to missing or unreliable modalities at inference.

## Results

On DIV2K-Val-100 (1024×1024), MMSR achieves the best PSNR (21.87 dB), LPIPS (0.2810), DISTS (0.1492), NIQE (3.42), MUSIQ (72.31), and CLIPIQA (0.7294) among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest text-driven baseline SeeSR by 0.56 dB in PSNR and 0.046 in LPIPS. On the real-world RealSR benchmark, MMSR attains the best LPIPS (0.2952), MUSIQ (71.33), and CLIPIQA (0.6717), while on DrealSR it leads in PSNR (27.28 dB), SSIM (0.7456), MUSIQ (68.93), and CLIPIQA (0.6999). Across all four benchmarks, MMSR consistently ranks first on perceptual quality metrics (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA, NIQE) and reference-based fidelity metrics (LPIPS, DISTS), demonstrating that multimodal conditioning yields both sharper and more faithful reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMSR outperforms all five diffusion-based baselines (StableSR, PASD, SUPIR, SeeSR) and the regression-based R-ESRGAN on the majority of metrics across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest text-driven competitor SeeSR, MMSR gains +0.56 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.3273 to 0.2810 on DIV2K-Val-100, while also improving MUSIQ by 2.5 points and CLIPIQA by 0.035. On RealSR, MMSR edges out SeeSR on LPIPS (0.2952 vs. 0.3009) and MUSIQ (71.33 vs. 69.77) but trails slightly in PSNR (24.83 vs. 25.18) and SSIM (0.7003 vs. 0.7216), indicating a small fidelity trade-off on real-world captures. Against SUPIR, the gap is largest: MMSR improves PSNR by 1.22 dB and CLIPIQA by 0.16 on DIV2K-Val-100, reflecting SUPIR's weaker perceptual quality. On DrealSR, MMSR surpasses SeeSR in PSNR (+0.53 dB), SSIM, MUSIQ (+3.84), and CLIPIQA, though SeeSR retains a marginal LPIPS advantage (0.3174 vs. 0.3249). Overall, MMSR achieves the best or near-best score on every metric in every benchmark, with the most consistent wins on perceptual quality (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA) and structural fidelity (LPIPS, DISTS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-prompt-driven diffusion SISR line (SeeSR, PASD, SUPIR) by adding spatial modalities—depth, semantic segmentation, and edges—extracted from the LR input via pretrained cross-modal models. Architecturally, it departs from ControlNet and IP-Adapter, which duplicate network branches per modality, by encoding all modalities into a unified VQGAN token sequence processed through a Perceiver-inspired latent connector (MMLC). The conditioning strategy follows InstructPix2Pix (concatenating LR with noisy latent), and the guidance mechanism generalizes standard classifier-free guidance to a multimodal variant where both positive and negative branches are conditioned on the multimodal token sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multimodal CFG technique offers a practical, drop-in solution to the well-known problem of hallucination at high guidance scales, and the modality-specific temperature control enables interpretable, fine-grained manipulation of SR outputs (e.g., bokeh strength, edge sharpness) that text prompts alone cannot achieve. The main limitation is the inference pipeline's dependency on multiple external models (Gemini Flash for captions at 0.34 img/s being the bottleneck), which constrains real-time deployment. Open questions include whether faster or unified cross-modal prediction can reduce this overhead, and whether the approach generalizes to other inverse problems beyond SISR.
