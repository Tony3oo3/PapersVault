## Problem

Existing image representations force a trade-off: understanding-focused models like CLIP capture high-level semantics but discard pixel-level details, while reconstruction-focused models like VAEs preserve fine visual details but lack the semantic richness needed for vision-language tasks. The paper asks whether a single representation can simultaneously excel at both semantic understanding and high-fidelity image reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251880/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ViLex takes a 224×224 RGB image and produces up to 75 continuous embedding vectors in the text vocabulary space (aligned with the CLIP/T5 index-to-embedding lookup matrix), bracketed by [BOS] and [EOS] tokens to fit within the 77-token context window. These tokens can be used independently as a 'text prompt' to a frozen T2I diffusion model (Imagen at 64×64), concatenated with natural language tokens for multimodal generation, or fed directly into a VLM as vision encoder features. The TailDrop strategy allows the user to dynamically truncate the token sequence at inference time, trading detail for compactness.

## Method

ViLex is a ViT-based image encoder (initialized from SigLIP) followed by a 5-block attention pooling module with n learnable queries that compresses patch tokens into n visual lexicon embeddings. Training uses a self-supervised autoencoder framework where a frozen Imagen T2I diffusion model serves as the decoder: the ViLex encoder is optimized via the diffusion denoising loss so that its output tokens, when passed through the frozen text encoder and U-Net, reconstruct the input image. This design is the key differentiator from prior work—by using a diffusion model (which inherently encodes both semantics and visual detail) rather than a VAE or MAE decoder, the learned representations inherit semantic richness while retaining fine-grained visual information. A TailDrop strategy randomly drops the last k tokens during training, encouraging earlier tokens to carry the most important semantic content. For multimodal generation, a Text-Free Guidance (TFG) mechanism modifies classifier-free guidance to balance the influence of ViLex visual tokens against natural language text prompts without any architectural changes or LoRA adapters to the T2I model.

## Results

For image reconstruction on MSCOCO-64×64, ViLex achieves FID 2.07 and IS 15.88 with 75 tokens, outperforming DeDiffusion (FID 3.89, IS 14.68) and vanilla Imagen (FID 6.52, IS 14.06); notably, even a single ViLex token (FID 3.65) beats DeDiffusion. For visual understanding, ViLex SigLIP consistently improves over the original SigLIP by 1–2 points across 15 benchmarks (e.g., COCOcap 141.5 vs 139.7, VQAv2 81.6 vs 81.4, RefCOCO-val 67.6 vs 66.2) while also reducing reconstruction FID from 2.54 to 2.38. In VQA, PaliGemma-2B with ViLex (336 tokens) achieves VQAv2 83.6, SciQA 88.6, VizWizQA 75.8, and GQA 65.7, surpassing larger models like LLaVA1.5-13B and QwenVL-7B that use 576–1024 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeDiffusion (the closest image-to-text-to-image baseline using the same encoder and T2I model), ViLex wins on both FID and IS at every token budget: 1-token ViLex (FID 3.65) already beats DeDiffusion (FID 3.89), and 75-token ViLex (FID 2.07) nearly halves the gap. In human studies, ViLex is preferred over DeDiffusion 98% of the time for layout and style, and 95% for semantic fidelity; against image-guided DALL-E 3, ViLex wins 91–98% of ratings across layout, semantic, and style. As a vision encoder, ViLex SigLIP improves over the original SigLIP by 1–2 points on all 15 understanding benchmarks while simultaneously reducing reconstruction FID (2.38 vs 2.54), demonstrating that the diffusion-based training objective enhances both semantic and pixel-level representations. Compared to VAE features, ViLex dramatically outperforms on understanding tasks (e.g., COCOcap 141.5 vs 61.4, VQAv2 52.9 vs 12.9), confirming that pixel-reconstruction objectives produce representations far less useful for vision-language modeling. In VQA, PaliGemma-2B with ViLex (336 tokens, frozen encoder) outperforms LLaVA1.5-13B (576 tokens), VILA-13B (576 tokens), and QwenVL-7B (1024 tokens) on VQAv2, SciQA, VizWizQA, and GQA, using a 2B LLM and the lowest image resolution, while those baselines require 7–13B LLMs and fine-tuned encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViLex carves new ground by repurposing a frozen text-to-image diffusion model as a decoder in an autoencoder, where the encoder learns to produce continuous embeddings in the text vocabulary's latent space rather than discrete token IDs. This departs from DeDiffusion (which generates discrete BPE tokens), from DreamBooth/Textual Inversion (which require per-instance fine-tuning), and from prior diffusion-as-feature-extractor works (l-DAE, ODISE, DIVA) that use diffusion losses or intermediate features directly. The key conceptual move is that the diffusion model's denoising process, which inherently encodes both semantics and visual detail, provides supervisory signals that shape the encoder toward representations useful for both understanding and generation simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViLex offers a practical drop-in upgrade for vision-language models: replacing SigLIP with ViLex's encoder yields consistent gains across 15 benchmarks without architectural changes, and the zero-shot DreamBooth capability eliminates the need for per-subject fine-tuning. The striking VAE comparison (8× lower VQA scores) challenges the assumption that pixel-level reconstruction fidelity is what matters for semantic understanding, suggesting that semantic-level reconstruction through a diffusion decoder is a superior training objective. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution diffusion models (experiments use 64×64 Imagen), whether the text-space constraint limits expressiveness for very complex scenes, and how the method generalizes across different T2I backbones beyond Imagen.
