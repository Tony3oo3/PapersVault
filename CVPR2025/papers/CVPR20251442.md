## Problem

Existing vision-language temporal alignment benchmarks (TACoS, DiDeMo, Charades-STA, etc.) rely on human-annotated timestamps from real-world videos, leading to unreliable labels, significant temporal distribution biases at multiple levels (process, composition, entity), and insufficient compositional diversity. The paper identifies these biases quantitatively via a new metric (TJSD) and shows that current evaluations cannot fairly diagnose whether models truly possess temporal alignment capability or merely exploit distributional shortcuts.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 25.3K synthetic video clips (average 134.1 s, generated in the VirtualHome 3D simulator) paired with 77.1K template-generated text sentences describing compositional action sequences (average moment duration 24.3 s). Ground-truth start and end timestamps are automatically recorded by the simulator during video execution, eliminating human annotation noise. For diagnostic evaluation, the benchmark also supports temporal question prompts (e.g., 'When does the agent open the fridge?') and distributional-shift test sets constructed via long-tailed sampling versus ICGF-balanced sampling.

## Method

SVLTA is built through a five-stage synthetic pipeline: (1) a Commonsense Activity Graph encodes pre-defined logical dependencies among 96 compositional actions across 7 scenes and 6 agents, traversed via DFS/BFS with re-weighting sampling to produce diverse, semantically valid action chains; (2) a Controllable Activity Manuscript applies Action Permutation (AP) and Action Duration Diversity (ADD via varied framerates) to ensure each action can appear at any temporal position with varied duration; (3) functional programs execute in the VirtualHome simulator to render synthetic videos while automatically logging per-action timestamps; (4) template-based sentence generation (optionally rewritten by GPT-3.5-turbo) converts action chains into natural language queries; (5) Inequality Constrained Global Filtering (ICGF) formulates global temporal debiasing as a nonlinear optimization with a filtering-rate constraint, reducing the gap between the empirical and uniform temporal distributions across all actions simultaneously. This yields a benchmark with the lowest multi-level TJSD scores (0.073 process, 0.101 object, 0.266 verb, 0.322 composition) compared to all existing datasets.

## Results

On the SVLTA temporal question answering task, no current VidLLM achieves satisfactory performance: GPT-4o attains the highest mIoU of 18.90, Gemini 1.5 Pro reaches 12.48, and the best open-sourced model Video-LLaMA2 scores 12.33, while general models like Videochat2 and LLaVA-Video drop to 0.87 and 0.84 mIoU respectively. In the distributional shift sensitivity evaluation, Shuffling demonstrates the strongest temporal robustness with an RC of only 1.04, whereas the causal-based DCM model performs worst (RC=17.86), and QD-DETR is the most robust among biased models (RC=5.92). For temporal alignment adaptation to Charades-STA, QD-DETR achieves the best mIoU of 22.32, followed closely by LGI (22.24), while 2D-TAN lags significantly at 11.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In temporal question answering, GPT-4o leads all models with 18.90 mIoU, but even this strong close-sourced model falls far below practical utility; notably, Video-LLaMA2 (12.33 mIoU) outperforms all three dedicated time-aware models (VTimeLLM 10.29, TimeChat 9.61, E.T.Chat 6.29), suggesting its temporal encoding design is more effective than explicit time-aware architectures. In distributional shift sensitivity, Shuffling (RC=1.04) dramatically outperforms all other methods, including the causal-inference-based DCM (RC=17.86), which paradoxically shows the worst robustness; among biased models, QD-DETR (RC=5.92) is substantially more robust than 2D-TAN (10.85), VSLNet (14.31), and LGI (14.94). In temporal alignment adaptation to Charades-STA, QD-DETR achieves the best mIoU (22.32), edging out LGI (22.24) and VSLNet (19.66), while 2D-TAN trails at 11.8, confirming that transformer-based architectures transfer temporal alignment knowledge more effectively to new domains than anchor-based or anchor-free alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the lineage of real-world temporal grounding benchmarks (TACoS, DiDeMo, Charades-STA, TVR, MAD) by identifying their multi-level temporal distribution biases and annotation unreliability, drawing on prior observations from Otani et al. and Yuan et al. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it repurposes the VirtualHome simulator and graph-based activity generation (inspired by Action Genome and STAR) to produce synthetic videos with ground-truth temporal annotations, filling a gap that prior synthetic video work (CATER, CLEVRER) left unaddressed for temporal alignment specifically. The TJSD metric adapts class-imbalance divergence measures to the temporal domain, and ICGF extends adversarial filtering with inequality-constrained optimization for global distribution balancing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first controlled, compositional, and unbiased evaluation environment for temporal alignment, enabling diagnosis of capabilities that real-world benchmarks cannot isolate due to confounded biases. The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only 18.90 mIoU on temporal alignment underscores a significant gap in current VidLLMs that the field has underappreciated. However, the VirtualHome domain introduces a synthetic-to-real gap that limits direct transferability, and the paper does not extensively validate whether models trained on SVLTA generalize to real-world temporal grounding. Open questions include whether the compositional action space (96 actions) is sufficient to capture real-world temporal complexity and whether the ICGF debiasing strategy scales to more diverse action vocabularies.
