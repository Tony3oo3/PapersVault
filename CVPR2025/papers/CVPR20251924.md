## Problem

Existing remote sensing foundation models either require per-image fine-tuning to handle hyperspectral data with varying channel counts, or rely on fixed-channel architectures that cannot generalize across sensors. Additionally, prompt-based segmentation methods like SAM generate only one mask per prompt, making them impractical for dense pixel-level hyperspectral tasks where all instances of a class must be identified simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251924/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a hyperspectral image with an arbitrary number of spectral channels (any subset of the 400–2500 nm range at 10 nm resolution) and a task-specific prompt: a single point per class for classification, a target spectrum for detection, or no prompt at all for anomaly and change detection. It outputs pixel-level segmentation maps (classification, binary detection, anomaly, or change maps) at the native spatial resolution of the input (0.6–5.0 m/pixel for airborne AVIRIS data). The key constraint is that the channel count and exact wavelengths differ per image, and the model must handle this without any architectural modification or retraining.

## Method

HyperFree builds on SAM's ViT-base meta-architecture with two key modifications. First, a channel-adaptive embedding layer replaces the fixed convolutional patch encoder with a learnable weight dictionary of 221 entries (one per 10 nm band from 400–2500 nm); given input wavelengths, the corresponding weight matrices are looked up and assembled into a dynamic convolution kernel, with two parallel branches (key channels and inter-channel cubes) using separate dictionaries whose outputs are summed. Second, a prompt-mask-feature (PMF) interaction maps both the point prompt and each generated mask into the feature space and uses cosine distance as a semantic-similarity measure, enabling a single prompt to retrieve all semantically consistent masks via a threshold. The model is trained in a promptable segmentation manner on the Hyper-Seg dataset (~42K airborne HSIs with 15.44M auto-generated masks from SAM-H) using focal and Dice losses, with random channel subsets sampled per iteration to enforce wavelength-awareness in the dictionary.

## Results

In tuning-free manner (1 prompt or zero-shot), HyperFree achieves 93.39% OA on LongKou and 75.47% OA on HanChuan for hyperspectral classification, surpassing the best 5-shot baseline (MambaHSI: 92.65% and 73.33% respectively). For one-class classification, HyperFree reaches 72.52% F1 on HongHu and 52.50% F1 on XiongAn, outperforming the strongest baseline (T-HOneCls: 55.97% and PAN: 46.34%). On anomaly detection, HyperFree achieves AUC(ODP) of 1.9191 and 1.8697 on Beach1/Beach2, substantially exceeding the best baseline (TDD: 1.1383 and 1.1688). On change detection, HyperFree leads on Hermiston (IoU 0.5851, F1 0.7382) but trails on River (IoU 0.3649 vs. FC-Sd's 0.4534).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperFree (1 prompt, frozen) outperforms all 5-shot specialized baselines on HC (OA +0.74 over MambaHSI on LongKou, +2.14 on HanChuan), HOCC (F1 +16.55 over T-HOneCls on HongHu, +6.16 over PAN on XiongAn), and HAD (AUC(ODP) +0.78 over TDD on Beach1, +0.70 on Beach2). On HCD, HyperFree wins on Hermiston (IoU +0.049 over SST-Former, F1 +0.040) but loses on River (IoU −0.089 vs. FC-Sd, F1 −0.089). On HTD, HyperFree trails the best baselines: AUC(ODP) 1.4667 vs. MF's 1.6968 on Airport, and AUC(D,F) 0.9791 vs. TSTTD's 0.9987 on Cri, indicating that spectral-template-based detection remains challenging for a prompt-only approach. The comparison with SAM+PMF (Table 4) shows that training on hyperspectral data is critical: SAM drops ~27 points on HC OA (66.62 vs. 93.40) and ~38 points on HOCC F1 (34.13 vs. 72.52), confirming that the channel-adaptive embedding and hyperspectral pre-training are essential rather than the PMF interaction alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperFree builds directly on the SAM (Segment Anything Model) meta-architecture, repurposing its promptable segmentation framework for hyperspectral imagery where no prior tuning-free foundation model existed. It departs from the dominant pre-training-and-tuning (P-T) paradigm used by HyperSigma, SatMAE, and SpectralGPT by adopting a prompt engineering (P-E) approach, eliminating per-image fine-tuning. The two key architectural departures are the channel-adaptive embedding layer (a learnable weight dictionary indexed by wavelength, inspired by NLP tokenizers) and the prompt-mask-feature (PMF) interaction that maps prompts and masks into a shared feature space to retrieve multiple semantic-aware masks from a single prompt. The Hyper-Seg data engine, which uses SAM-H to auto-generate segmentation masks for airborne HSI, addresses the data scarcity problem that has limited prior hyperspectral foundation model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first tuning-free hyperspectral foundation model, which has direct practical value given that hyperspectral sensors produce images with varying channel counts (46–274 in their evaluation) and the P-T paradigm requires rebuilding embedding layers and fine-tuning for each new sensor or scene. The channel-adaptive weight dictionary is a clean, generalizable solution to the channel-variation problem that could be adopted by other spectral imaging applications. However, open questions remain: the weight dictionary is bounded to 400–2500 nm at 10 nm resolution, the Hyper-Seg annotation pipeline relies on SAM-H (introducing a potential circular dependency in training), and the PMF interaction's reliance on hand-designed thresholds τ limits full automation. Whether the approach extends beyond pixel-level segmentation to higher-level reasoning tasks (e.g., scene understanding, material identification) is unexplored.
