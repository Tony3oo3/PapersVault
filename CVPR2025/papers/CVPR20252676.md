## Problem

Depth foundation models produce high-quality relative depth but suffer from inherent scale ambiguity, making them impractical for applications requiring metric accuracy such as autonomous driving and robotic manipulation. Existing remedies—fine-tuning on metric datasets or conditioning on camera intrinsics—fail to resolve the ambiguity properly. This paper introduces a new paradigm that treats metric depth estimation as a prompting task, guiding a depth foundation model with a low-cost LiDAR signal to unlock accurate metric output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252676/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a low-resolution (192×256), noisy LiDAR depth map (e.g., from iPhone ARKit) as joint input, and produces a dense, scale-accurate metric depth map at up to 4K resolution (2160×3840). The LiDAR prompt is consumed at multiple scales inside the DPT decoder via bilinear resizing, so the network never requires the LiDAR at full resolution. Inference runs at 20.4 FPS at 768×1024 and 2.0 FPS at 4K on an A100 GPU with the ViT-L backbone.

## Method

The core contribution is a concise multi-scale prompt fusion block inserted at every scale of the DPT decoder: the low-res LiDAR is bilinearly resized to match each scale, passed through a two-layer shallow convolution, projected to the image-feature dimension via a zero-initialized 1×1 convolution, and added to the DPT intermediate features. This design adds only 5.7% FLOPs and, because of zero-initialization, the network's initial output is identical to the pretrained Depth Anything V2 foundation model, preserving its generalization. Training uses a scalable data pipeline that simulates realistic LiDAR noise on synthetic HyperSim data via sparse anchor interpolation with RGB-similarity KNN, and generates pseudo GT depth on real ScanNet++ scenes using Zip-NeRF reconstruction. An edge-aware depth loss combines L1 supervision from FARO-annotated GT (accurate in textureless regions) with a gradient loss on the pseudo GT depth (accurate at edges), leveraging the complementary strengths of each source.

## Results

On ARKitScenes (non-zero-shot), Prompt Depth Anything achieves L1=0.0135 and RMSE=0.0326 at 384×512, outperforming the best depth-completion baseline MSPF (L1=0.0153, RMSE=0.0369) and all monocular depth estimation methods by large margins (e.g., Depth Anything v2: L1=0.0464, RMSE=0.0715). On ScanNet++, it achieves L1=0.0250, RMSE=0.0829, and TSDF F-score=0.7619, surpassing MSPF (F-score=0.7133) and Depth Anything v2 (F-score=0.6595). Even in zero-shot mode (trained only on HyperSim), the method achieves L1=0.0327 and F-score=0.7307 on ScanNet++, beating all non-zero-shot MDE baselines and the raw ARKit LiDAR depth (L1=0.0351, F-score=0.6785).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prompt Depth Anything outperforms all 10 baselines on both ARKitScenes and ScanNet++ across every reported metric. Against the closest depth-completion competitor MSPF [61], it reduces L1 by ~23% (0.0250 vs. 0.0326) and improves TSDF F-score by 6.8 points (0.7619 vs. 0.7133) on ScanNet++. Among monocular depth estimation methods, the gap is even larger: Depth Anything v2 [73] (L1=0.0510, F-score=0.6595), Metric3D v2 [25] (L1=0.0585, F-score=0.6515), and ZoeDepth [6] (L1=0.0582, F-score=0.6083) all trail by 2× or more in L1. The zero-shot variant (Ours_syn, trained only on HyperSim) still beats the fine-tuned MSPF and Depth Anything v2 on ScanNet++, and surpasses the raw ARKit LiDAR depth input (L1=0.0351, F-score=0.6785) by a clear margin, demonstrating that the prompting paradigm generalizes without dataset-specific training. Depth Prompting [42], the most directly comparable method, is outperformed in zero-shot mode (L1 0.0327 vs. 0.0353; F-score 0.7307 vs. 0.6734), with the paper attributing the gap to its post-hoc fusion design versus the proposed in-decoder prompt fusion. Diffusion-based methods Marigold [29] and Lotus [22] show the weakest performance (L1=0.0828 and 0.0853 respectively on ScanNet++), confirming that DPT-based architectures with metric prompting are more effective for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Depth Anything V2 (a DPT-based depth foundation model with a ViT-L encoder) and the DPT multi-scale decoder architecture, adding a lightweight multi-scale prompt fusion block that injects low-resolution LiDAR depth at each decoder stage. Conceptually, it departs from prior metric depth approaches that either fine-tune on metric datasets or condition on camera intrinsics, instead framing metric depth estimation as a prompting problem analogous to instruction tuning in LLMs and vision-language models. It also distinguishes itself from depth completion work (e.g., Depth Prompting [42]) by integrating the sparse LiDAR as an in-network prompt rather than a post-processing signal, and from conditional generation designs (AdaLN, Cross-Attention, ControlNet) by showing that a simple additive fusion better exploits the pixel-alignment between input LiDAR and output depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimal architectural addition (5.7% compute overhead) to a pretrained depth foundation model can resolve the long-standing scale ambiguity problem, with the zero-shot model (trained only on synthetic HyperSim data) outperforming non-zero-shot baselines fine-tuned on the actual test datasets—a strong signal that the prompting paradigm generalizes well. The practical relevance is high given that low-cost LiDAR is now embedded in consumer devices (iPhone, ARKit), making the setup immediately deployable for 4K metric depth. Open questions include handling long-range depth (where iPhone LiDAR becomes unreliable), temporal flickering in LiDAR prompts, and whether the prompting paradigm extends to other metric cues (e.g., vehicle LiDAR, which the paper briefly demonstrates for outdoor scenes) or to diffusion-based depth models.
