## Problem

Existing Trusted Multi-View Classification (TMVC) methods rely on view-specific uncertainty scores to suppress unreliable evidence, but these scores are sensitive to data distributions and optimization levels, often failing to fully zero out evidence from corrupted or noisy views. This leaves residual unreliable evidence that biases fusion and causes significant accuracy degradation in real-world settings with sensor failures or data corruption, a gap that no prior TMVC method addresses at testing time.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a testing-time post-hoc filter: it consumes the V view-specific evidence vectors and uncertainty scores produced by a trained TMVC model, along with the uncertainty score distribution from a held-out validation set used to compute a distribution-free threshold via the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure. The output is a filtered set of view-specific evidence vectors (reliable views kept at full weight, unreliable views scaled by a user-defined factor α) that are then fused into a final class label and overall uncertainty score. Notably, unreliable evidence is soft-weighted rather than hard-excluded, preserving any partially useful signal from degraded views.

## Method

The paper proposes a generalized evidence filtering mechanism that frames the identification of unreliable view-specific evidence as a multiple hypothesis testing problem. For each test sample's view, a p-value is computed by comparing its uncertainty score against the validation set's uncertainty distribution, and the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure yields a distribution-free threshold κ^v that controls the false discovery rate below a user-specified α. Evidence from views exceeding this threshold is down-weighted by a factor of α before fusion, rather than being discarded entirely. The mechanism is plug-and-play, compatible with all three standard TMVC fusion strategies (BCF, A-CBF, ABF), and comes with theoretical guarantees: Proposition 1 establishes (α,δ)-risk-controlling discovery, Proposition 2 shows classification improvement under the assumption that unreliable evidence is incorrect, and Proposition 3 ensures that filtered results exhibit higher overall uncertainty to alert decision-makers. The added computational cost is O(L log L) for threshold computation plus one standard test pass, making it negligible relative to the forward pass.

## Results

Under corrupted view conditions (half of views replaced with random noise), the proposed evidence filtering mechanism achieves substantial accuracy gains across all six datasets and three fusion strategies. The most dramatic improvement is on CUB with A-CBF fusion, where accuracy jumps from 56.67% to 79.17% (+22.5 pp), and on CUB with BCF from 65.33% to 83.75% (+18.4 pp). On the hardest dataset (NUS), ABF fusion improves from 27.70% to 37.70% (+10.0 pp). Under clean views, the method maintains or slightly improves accuracy (e.g., CUB ABF: 98.75% → 98.75%; HAND BCF: 98.88% → 99.25%), confirming it does not degrade performance in noise-free settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates its filtering mechanism as a plug-in on top of six existing TMVC methods (TMC/BCF, TMDOA/A-CBF, ETMC, SMDC, ECML/ABF, CCML) under identical training settings. Under corrupted views, the method consistently outperforms the unfiltered baseline across all six methods and all six datasets, as shown in the radar charts (Figure 3). The largest relative gains appear on CUB (e.g., +22.5 pp for A-CBF) and CAL (e.g., +20.0 pp for ABF), where noise severely degrades unfiltered fusion. On HAND, improvements are more modest (+5.5 pp for ABF) because corrupted views already produce near-zero evidence, naturally limiting their fusion impact. Under clean views, the method is roughly on par with or slightly above the baseline (within ±1 pp), with minor drops on SCENE and CAL attributed to down-weighting of high-uncertainty evidence that occasionally carries useful signal. The sensitivity analysis (Table 2) shows that α=0.05 (the default) yields the best balance, with ABF benefiting most at higher α values (e.g., 55.13% → 78.63% at α=0.05 on CAL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Trusted Multi-View Classification (TMVC) paradigm established by Han et al. (ICLR 2021) and its subsequent fusion variants (A-CBF, ABF) by addressing a specific testing-time vulnerability: unreliable evidence from corrupted views that uncertainty scores fail to fully suppress. The key conceptual departure is importing the Benjamini-Hochberg multiple-testing framework to formalize evidence reliability as a hypothesis-testing problem with controlled false discovery rate, rather than relying on the raw uncertainty magnitude that prior TMVC methods assume is well-calibrated. The mechanism is explicitly post-hoc and model-agnostic, slotting into any existing TMVC pipeline without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear for high-stakes TMVC deployments (medical imaging, autonomous driving) where a single corrupted sensor view can silently bias predictions, and the FDR guarantee provides a principled knob (α) for risk management. However, the experimental validation is limited to six relatively small datasets with fully connected backbones, leaving open whether the filtering mechanism retains its advantage at scale (deeper networks, larger view counts, more complex corruption patterns like partial degradation or adversarial noise). An open question is how the method interacts with adaptive or learned fusion strategies that already attempt to down-weight unreliable views during training, and whether the fixed α threshold generalizes across domains without per-dataset tuning.
