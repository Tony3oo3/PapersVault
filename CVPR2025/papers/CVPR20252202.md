## Problem

Existing unified MLLMs that combine image understanding and generation rely on complex designs—external diffusion decoders, separate training objectives, distinct image encoders, or additional semantic pretraining—that disrupt the simplicity of the next-token prediction paradigm and limit scalability. Two practical challenges remain: incorporating vision capabilities into a pretrained LLM without degrading its language knowledge, and handling high-resolution images whose long visual token sequences are unsuitable for LLM context windows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252202/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For understanding, the model takes a 512×512 RGB image (tokenized by the Emu3 VQ tokenizer at 8× downsampling, then 2×8 token-folded to compress 4096 tokens into 256) plus a text prompt, and produces an autoregressive text response. For generation, it takes a text prompt and produces a sequence of discrete VQ token IDs (codebook size 32,768) that are unfolded by a 4-layer autoregressive Transformer head and decoded to pixels by the pretrained VQ decoder. Classifier-free guidance (scale 7.5) is applied at inference for generation, with a 10% unconditional dropout during training.

## Method

SynerGen-VL is built on InternLM2-1.8B and introduces two key mechanisms: (1) a Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) design where each FFN layer has a separate vision expert FFN (initialized from the text FFN) that processes visual tokens, while the original text FFN handles text tokens, preserving the LLM's pretrained knowledge; and (2) a token folding/unfolding scheme that concatenates every 2×8 patch of visual token embeddings into a single compressed token before the LLM, and decodes them back via a shallow 4-layer causal Transformer head during generation. Training follows a progressive two-stage alignment pretraining: Stage 1 freezes the LLM and trains only vision-specific parameters (token embeddings, projection, vision experts, unfolding head) on ~1.3B noisy web samples; Stage 2 additionally trains self-attention layers on ~340M high-quality samples. A unified next-token prediction loss (weighted sum of text and image token cross-entropy, λ=2) is used throughout, followed by joint instruction tuning on ~5M bilingual understanding instructions and 10M internal generation samples.

## Results

With only 2.4B activated parameters, SynerGen-VL achieves OCRBench 721, MME 1837, MathVista 42.7, and MMMU 34.2 on image understanding benchmarks, surpassing the 8B-parameter Emu3-Chat on OCRBench (721 vs. 687) and MMMU (34.2 vs. 31.6). For image generation, SynerGen-VL achieves FID 7.65 on MSCOCO-30K and 6.10 on MJHQ-30K, substantially outperforming Emu3-Gen (FID 19.3) and Janus (FID 8.53/10.10). On GenEval, SynerGen-VL scores 0.61 overall, tying Janus and exceeding Emu3-Gen (0.54) with fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynerGen-VL (2.4B) outperforms the same-architecture Emu3-Chat (8B) on OCRBench (721 vs. 687) and MMMU (34.2 vs. 31.6), while remaining competitive on POPE (85.3 vs. 85.2) and SEED-I (62.0 vs. 68.2). For image generation, SynerGen-VL achieves FID 7.65 on MSCOCO-30K, dramatically beating Emu3-Gen (19.3) and Janus (8.53), and FID 6.10 on MJHQ-30K, surpassing Janus (10.10) and approaching PixArt-α (6.14). On GenEval, SynerGen-VL ties Janus at 0.61 overall and exceeds Emu3-Gen (0.54), Show-o (0.53), and LWM (0.47). Against encoder-based understanding-only models, SynerGen-VL trails Qwen2-VL (2B) on MMB (53.7 vs. 74.9) and MMMU (34.2 vs. 41.1), but surpasses EVE-7B on nearly all metrics and is competitive with InternVL-1.5 (2B) on MathVista (42.7 vs. 41.1). The key advantage is that SynerGen-VL unifies both understanding and generation in a single encoder-free model without external diffusion decoders, while maintaining performance close to task-specific SOTA models.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynerGen-VL extends the encoder-free unified MLLM paradigm established by Emu3, Chameleon, and Show-o, which represent both images and text as discrete tokens under a single next-token prediction objective. It departs from these prior works in two key ways: (1) it introduces image-specific FFN experts (borrowed from the MMoE structure in CogVLM and Mono-InternVL) to inject visual capabilities without disrupting the pretrained LLM's knowledge, and (2) it employs a token folding/unfolding mechanism—conceptually related to Funnel Transformer and MegaByte's compress-then-decode strategies—to compress long visual token sequences for high-resolution image understanding while a shallow autoregressive head reconstructs full-resolution tokens for generation. The base architecture is built on InternLM2-1.8B with the Emu3 VQ tokenizer, positioning it as a practical refinement of the fully autoregressive unified MLLM recipe rather than a new paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 2.4B-activated-parameter model can match the 8B Emu3 on both image understanding and generation benchmarks, suggesting that the vision-expert + token-folding recipe significantly reduces the training cost of unified MLLMs without sacrificing performance. The token folding mechanism (reducing 4096 tokens to 256 for a 512×512 image) offers a general, architecture-agnostic solution to the long-sequence bottleneck that has limited high-resolution understanding in encoder-free MLLMs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or higher resolutions beyond 512×512, whether the fixed 2×8 folding ratio is optimal across tasks, and whether the reliance on a pretrained VQ tokenizer (rather than end-to-end learned representations) ultimately caps generation quality relative to diffusion-based methods.
