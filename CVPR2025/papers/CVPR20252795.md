## Problem

Open-world remote sensing interpretation is hindered by two gaps: existing RS datasets offer limited and sparse semantic categories (especially at pixel level), and the language model's inherent contextual dependence makes it difficult to decouple visually distinct but textually related RS objects (e.g., 'car' vs. 'road') in densely packed aerial imagery. No prior model achieves dense, fine-grained, open-vocabulary interpretation across the full range of RS tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252795/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a remote sensing image (512×512 for the VQA agent) paired with an open-vocabulary text prompt (arbitrary short semantic tag, not drawn from a fixed category list) and produces pixel-level segmentation masks, region-level category predictions, scene class labels, or free-form VQA answers depending on the task. All evaluations are strictly zero-shot: no labels or fine-tuning from the 14 evaluation datasets are used during training. For the VQA agent, SkySense-O replaces the CLIP-L-14 vision encoder inside a LLaVA-1.5 architecture, so the text input becomes a natural-language question.

## Method

SkySense-O uses a SwinV2-H-8 (654M) vision encoder initialized from the SkySense RS foundation model, pre-trained on 10M image-text pairs while simultaneously retaining masked image modeling and image contrastive losses to preserve fine-grained visual perception. The key novelty is a vision-centric principle applied at fine-tuning: a GPT-4V-based agent constructs an offline visual-relevance knowledge graph over all 1,763 Sky-SA category pairs, scoring how visually similar each pair is. This graph drives (a) a flexible positive/negative sampler that avoids the ambiguous pairing caused by text-level co-occurrence, and (b) a vision-centric regularization loss L_vc that explicitly penalizes the model for confusing categories with high textual similarity but low visual relevance (e.g., vehicle vs. parking lot). The total fine-tuning loss combines pixel-level cross-entropy, region-level cross-entropy, and the vision-centric regularization, trained on the newly introduced Sky-SA dataset (183,375 full-pixel mask-text pairs with open-text labels).

## Results

On zero-shot semantic segmentation (the primary task), SkySense-O achieves 43.92 mIoU on iSAID, 32.12 on SOTA, 30.89 on SIOR, and 54.10 on Potsdam, outperforming the best prior zero-shot method (CAT-SEG) by 14.27% mIoU on average across object segmentation datasets and surpassing even few-shot methods (SegGPT, DINOv2) on most benchmarks. For zero-shot scene classification, SkySense-O reaches 83.28% Top-1 accuracy on RESISC-45 and 82.93% on AID, exceeding GeoRSCLIP by 9.38% and 6.66% respectively. As a vision encoder in an LLaVA-1.5-style MLLM, SkySense-O attains 74.79% on SIRI-WHU, 94.10% on AID-VQA, and 78.09% average on RSVQA-HR, outperforming VHM by 3.91%, 2.40%, and 4.34% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkySense-O dominates all zero-shot segmentation baselines by large margins: it outperforms SegEarth-OV (the closest prior zero-shot RS method) by 22.22 mIoU on iSAID and 18.41 on SOTA, and beats CAT-SEG (the strongest general-purpose open-vocabulary segmentation model) by 23.34 mIoU on iSAID. On land-cover datasets, gains are smaller but consistent (e.g., +2.65 on Potsdam, +2.02 on Vaihingen), with SkySense-O achieving the best Vaihingen leaderboard result. For scene classification, SkySense-O surpasses GeoRSCLIP (the prior SOTA RS vision-language model) by 9.38% on RESISC-45 and 6.66% on AID, and outperforms RemoteCLIP and SkyScript by over 12% on RESISC. In the VQA agent setting, SkySense-O (as a vision encoder in LLaVA-1.5) beats VHM by 3.91% on SIRI-WHU and 4.34% average on RSVQA-HR, and outperforms GeoChat by 8.16% on SIRI-WHU. The paper reports average improvements of 11.95%, 8.04%, and 3.55% over SegEarth-OV, GeoRSCLIP, and VHM respectively across all 14 evaluation datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkySense-O extends the CLIP-style contrastive image-text alignment paradigm to the remote sensing domain, initializing its visual encoder from the SkySense foundation model and preserving its self-supervised losses (masked image modeling, image contrastive learning) during alignment, following the DetailCLIP strategy. For pixel-level open-vocabulary segmentation, it adopts the token-level similarity + visual decoder architecture from SAN, and for the VQA agent application it plugs into the LLaVA-1.5 framework. The key conceptual departure is the 'vision-centric' principle: rather than relying solely on text-embedding similarity for positive/negative sampling (which fails when textually related but visually distinct RS categories like 'car' and 'road' are confused), the paper constructs a GPT-4-derived visual-relevance knowledge graph over all Sky-SA categories and uses it both for flexible sampling and as a regularization term that penalizes high text-similarity/low-visual-similarity pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Sky-SA dataset (183K mask-text pairs, 1,763 open-text categories, full-pixel annotation) is the first pixel-level open-text RS dataset and a substantial resource that the community can reuse independently of the model. The vision-centric graph's ability to recover five unsupervised communities matching major land-cover types without any text labels provides an interpretable validation of the modeling choice and suggests the graph could serve as a standalone tool for category disambiguation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 2D optical imagery (SAR, multispectral), whether the GPT-4-derived visual relevance scores remain reliable at much larger category scales, and whether the vision-centric regularization transfers to other dense-prediction tasks beyond segmentation. The authors note future work on object states and relationships as a natural next step.
