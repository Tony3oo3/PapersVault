## Problem

Current video-LMMs struggle with long, untrimmed video content because limited LLM context windows force sparse frame sampling or aggressive token compression, causing critical visual information loss and irrelevant responses. Existing mitigations—memory-augmented buffers, RoPE-based context extension, and adaptive pooling—still incur substantial memory overhead and cannot fully preserve scene-level continuity across hours-long videos. The paper asks whether a retrieval-driven strategy, analogous to RAG in text, can selectively process only query-relevant video segments to overcome these structural limitations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a long untrimmed video (segmented into variable-length clips via PySceneDetect, sampled at 1 FPS) and a natural-language text query as input. Visual features are extracted per frame at 144 (CLIP) or 196 (SigLIP) tokens after 2×2 pooling, with a dynamic token-drop rate (up to 0.7 in training) that scales with clip length to manage computational cost. The output is an auto-regressively generated text response, with the retrieval router internally selecting top-K (K=5) most relevant segments before the LLM backbone produces the final answer.

## Method

SALOVA introduces a four-component architecture: a vision encoder (CLIP/SigLIP), a Spatio-Temporal Connector (Perceiver Resampler with 2-layer Transformer + MLP projector) that maps variable-length segment features into fixed-size latents, a Segment Retrieval Router (2-layer Transformer) that computes cross-attention similarity between per-segment routing tokens and the query sentence to select the top-K relevant clips, and an LLM backbone (LLaMA-3.2-3B, Phi-3.5-3.8B, or Qwen-2.5-7B). The FocusFast mechanism extends SlowFast by feeding both detailed top-K segment features (Focus pathway) and condensed segment-wide routing tokens (Fast pathway) into the LLM, preserving local detail while maintaining global scene continuity. Training follows a three-stage recipe: cross-modal alignment on 790K image/video-text pairs, a novel Stage 1.5 knowledge injection on the newly introduced SceneWalk dataset (87.8K YouTube videos, 1.29M densely captioned segments with avg. 137.5-word descriptions scored via LanguageBind and SBERT), and final video instruction tuning on 1.4M QA pairs. The router is trained end-to-end with a combined BCE + margin-based similarity loss that accommodates the generalized bipartite matching structure of video-text correspondence.

## Results

On Video-MME (without subtitles), SALOVA-7B achieves 53.1 overall accuracy, surpassing the strongest open-source baseline LongVA (52.6) and VideoLLaMA2 (47.9), with particularly strong gains on long videos (49.4 vs. 46.2 for LongVA, a +3.2 margin). On LongVideoBench, SALOVA-7B scores 44.6, outperforming all open-source baselines (best prior: PLLaVA at 40.2) but trailing proprietary models GPT-4o (66.7) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (64.0). On general video benchmarks, SALOVA-7B reaches 53.6 accuracy on ActivityNetQA (vs. 50.2 for VideoLLaMA2) and 53.5 on MVBench (vs. 54.6 for VideoLLaMA2), while matching VideoLLaMA2 at 3.13 on VideoChatGPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALOVA-7B outperforms all open-source video-LMM baselines on Video-MME overall (53.1 vs. 52.6 for LongVA, 47.9 for VideoLLaMA2, 45.3 for SliME) and on the long-video subset (49.4 vs. 46.2 for LongVA, +3.2). On LongVideoBench, SALOVA-7B (44.6) leads all open-source methods by a clear margin over PLLaVA (40.2), ShareGPT4Video (39.7), and VideoChat2 (39.3), but remains substantially below proprietary GPT-4o (66.7) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (64.0). On ActivityNetQA, SALOVA-7B (53.6 acc) beats VideoLLaMA2 (50.2) and VideoChat2 (49.1) but trails GPT-4V (57.0). On MVBench, SALOVA-7B (53.5) narrowly loses to VideoLLaMA2 (54.6) by 1.1 points while beating VideoChat2 (51.1) and LLaMA-VID (41.3). On VideoChatGPT, SALOVA-7B ties VideoLLaMA2 at 3.13. The 3B variant (SALOVA-3B) already surpasses 7B baselines like VideoChat2 and ShareGPT4Video on Video-MME overall (45.3 vs. 39.5/39.9), highlighting the efficiency of the retrieval-based design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALOVA adapts the RAG paradigm from text LLMs to the video domain, introducing a Segment Retrieval Router that selects query-relevant video segments before feeding them to the LLM, rather than attempting to compress or sparsely sample the entire video into a fixed context window. Architecturally, it extends the SlowFast pathways concept [19] into a FocusFast mechanism that combines detailed local segment features (focus pathway) with global routing tokens (fast pathway), and employs a Perceiver Resampler [2] as the Spatio-Temporal Connector to handle variable-length segment features. The paper departs from prior long-video approaches—sparse frame sampling, token compression, and memory-augmented generation—by arguing that targeted retrieval fundamentally avoids the information loss inherent in those strategies, and introduces the SceneWalk dataset (87.8K densely captioned long videos) to provide the segment-level supervision needed to train the retrieval mechanism end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SceneWalk dataset is a substantial practical resource for the community, offering 11.8K hours of densely captioned long-form video with 1.3M segments and explicit video-text correspondence scores, filling a gap left by existing datasets that only cover short clips or partial moments. The retrieval-driven framing offers a conceptually clean alternative to context-length extension, potentially reducing memory overhead for very long videos. However, the paper acknowledges that for shorter videos where sparse sampling suffices, the added architectural complexity may not be justified, and a hybrid approach that dynamically adjusts retrieval complexity based on video length remains an open direction. Performance gains over strong 7B baselines (e.g., LongVA) are modest (~0.5 points on Video-MME overall), suggesting the approach's advantage may grow more pronounced at even longer durations or with larger backbones.
