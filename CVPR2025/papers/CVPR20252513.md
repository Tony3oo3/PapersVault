## Problem

The sim-to-real gap remains a major barrier to deploying learned navigation policies in the real world. Traditional simulators lack photorealistic observations and diverse environmental variation, while neural rendering methods (NeRF, 3DGS) produce realistic visuals but cannot support the physical interactions and closed-loop evaluation required for embodied agent training. No existing pipeline can convert casual monocular video into a fully interactive, physically grounded simulation suitable for reinforcement learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252513/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 15-second monocular video at 30 fps (450 frames) as input and produces a real-time, physically interactive 3D simulation environment. The environment provides the agent with RGB and depth observations rendered from a hybrid GS-plus-mesh representation, where the GS handles photorealistic visuals and the invisible mesh handles collision detection. The final deliverable is a trained navigation policy (SAC) that can be zero-shot deployed to a physical four-wheeled robot in unseen real-world urban scenes.

## Method

Vid2Sim employs a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 performs geometry-consistent Gaussian Splatting reconstruction: it regularizes GS training with a patch-based NCC depth loss (scale-invariant to SfM initialization), a cosine-distance normal loss, a geometry-consistent loss that enforces adjacent-pixel normal alignment weighted by local depth gradients, and a shortest-axis scale penalty inspired by 2DGS; a screen-space covariance culling step then removes oversized splat artifacts when the agent's viewpoint deviates from training views. Stage 2 builds the interactive simulation by extracting a TSDF mesh from the reconstructed GS, importing both into Unity where the GS renders photorealistic visuals via a custom shader while the invisible mesh provides real-time physics and collision detection. The environment is further populated with randomly placed static obstacles (traffic cones, poles, bins) and dynamic pedestrians following A* paths, and augmented with scene editing for lighting/season/semantic changes and particle-system weather effects. Navigation agents are trained with Soft Actor-Critic for 1.5M steps across 30 reconstructed scenes on PointNav and SocialNav tasks.

## Results

On the Vid2Sim dataset (30 scenes), Vid2Sim achieves the best reconstruction quality with PSNR 32.41, SSIM 0.927, and LPIPS 0.127, outperforming all baselines. For urban navigation in simulation, agents trained with Vid2Sim (static + dynamic obstacles) reach 81.6% success rate on PointNav and 74.4% on SocialNav, a 31.2% improvement over the mesh-based baseline. In zero-shot real-world deployment with 30 training environments, the agent achieves 85% success on Go Straight, 65% on Static Obstacle, and 55% on Dynamic Obstacle, while the mesh baseline fails all three tasks (0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vid2Sim outperforms all four baselines on every rendering quality metric. Against 3DGS [25], the closest competitor in photorealism, Vid2Sim gains +0.56 PSNR and +0.006 SSIM while also adding interactivity and RL training support that 3DGS lacks entirely. Against 2DGS [20], which enforces disk-shaped Gaussians for geometry, Vid2Sim improves PSNR by +1.59 and LPIPS by −0.027, showing that the geometry-consistent supervision and screen-space culling yield better reconstruction. Against Video2Game [63], the only prior method offering interactivity, Vid2Sim achieves substantially higher fidelity (+4.09 PSNR, +0.093 SSIM) and full real-time rendering versus Video2Game's approximate rendering. Against Instant-NGP [40], Vid2Sim leads by +4.91 PSNR and supports all three simulation capabilities (real-time, interactive, RL training) that Instant-NGP does not. In the navigation experiments (Table 2), the mesh-based simulation baseline (analogous to Video2Game's approach) achieves only 48.8% SR on PointNav and 43.2% on SocialNav, while Vid2Sim with dynamic obstacles reaches 81.6% and 74.4% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vid2Sim extends 3D Gaussian Splatting from a purely photorealistic rendering tool into a physically interactive simulation substrate by introducing a hybrid GS-plus-mesh representation, where GS provides real-time visual observations and an extracted TSDF mesh handles collision detection in Unity. The geometry-consistent reconstruction draws on monocular depth estimation (MIDAS/Depth Anything) with scale-invariant NCC and normal losses, and borrows the disk-shape regularization idea from 2DGS to improve surface fidelity. For scene augmentation, it adapts the Instruct 4D-to-4D editing pipeline for video-length temporal consistency. Conceptually, the paper departs from the traditional sim-to-real framing (domain randomization, system identification) by inverting the direction: instead of making simulation approximate reality, it reconstructs reality directly into simulation, sidestepping the fidelity ceiling of hand-built graphics engines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline offers a scalable, low-cost route to photorealistic interactive environments from freely available web video, which could substantially lower the barrier to building diverse training scenes for embodied agents. The demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer (68.3% real-world success rate improvement over a mesh baseline) is encouraging, though the real-world evaluation is limited to 20 trials per task on a single robot platform, and the mesh baseline achieves 0% on all tasks, making the gap partly a function of baseline weakness. Open questions include whether the reconstruction quality degrades on highly dynamic or occluded scenes, how the approach scales to multi-building or large-city-scale environments, and whether the hybrid representation can support more complex manipulation tasks beyond navigation. The screen-space covariance culling trick, while simple, addresses a practical failure mode (floater artifacts during RL exploration) that is likely to recur in any GS-based simulation setup.
