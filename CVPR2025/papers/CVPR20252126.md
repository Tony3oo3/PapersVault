## Problem

Existing driving segmentation datasets evaluate only closed-set performance on known classes, while anomaly detection benchmarks use a binary known-vs-anomaly protocol without in-domain training data or support for open-set K+1 evaluation. This prevents assessing whether a model can simultaneously segment known objects and flag unknowns in realistic, cluttered environments with diverse lighting and sensor conditions — a requirement for safety-critical autonomous driving deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252126/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides RGB driving scene images (static, 980 test images) and short video clips captured with a consumer-grade dashcam (temporal, 1,140 annotated frames out of 21,118 total), all annotated at pixel level with 19 known CityScapes classes, an anomaly class, and a void class. The temporal test set intentionally introduces a cross-sensor domain shift by using a lower-quality dashcam compared to the higher-resolution cameras used for training. Four evaluation protocols (road obstacle, road anomaly, open-set K+1, closed-set K) are supported, reporting AP, FPR_T, TPR_F, instance-level F1, IoU, and oIoU at defined operating points.

## Method

The core contribution is the ISSU dataset and its controlled evaluation framework rather than a new model architecture. Anomalies are defined as objects outside the 19 known CityScapes classes within 2 meters of the road, with frequently observed unknowns (e.g., auto-rickshaws, banners) deliberately excluded and labeled as void to avoid penalizing models for common but unannotated objects. The three-part split (ISSU-Train, ISSU-Test-Static, ISSU-Test-Temporal) enables isolated ablations of domain shift, sensor quality, lighting conditions, and anomaly size (four quantized intervals). Baselines span pixel-level reconstruction methods (JSR-Net, DaCUP, PixOOD) and mask-level methods built on Mask2Former (EAM, RbA, Pebal, UNO, M2A), each evaluated with and without auxiliary out-of-domain training data across in-domain, cross-domain, and cross-sensor setups.

## Results

Under in-domain Static evaluation, mask-level methods trained with OOD data achieve the strongest anomaly detection: RbA reaches AP 79.1%, FPR_T 3.9%, TPR_F 95.9%, while EAM achieves AP 76.8%, FPR_T 4.2%, TPR_F 96.1%. In-domain Temporal (cross-sensor) evaluation causes a dramatic drop, with the best method (EAM+OOD) falling to AP 38.7% and FPR_T 91.4%. Cross-domain (CityScapes→ISSU) further degrades performance; UNO+OOD is the best cross-domain method with AP 55.5% (Static) and AP 37.2% (Temporal), but FPR_T remains above 90% in both settings. Closed-set IoU for ISSU is ~10% lower than CityScapes in-domain (65.8% vs 75.9% for PixOOD), and the gap widens to 20–30% under temporal and cross-domain conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mask-level methods (EAM, RbA, UNO, Pebal, M2A) consistently outperform pixel-level methods (JSR-Net, DaCUP, PixOOD) across all evaluation protocols, with AP gaps of 50–75 points in in-domain Static settings. Among mask-level methods, RbA and EAM with OOD training data lead in-domain Static (AP 79.1% and 76.8%), while UNO achieves the lowest FPR_T (3.0%) and highest TPR_F (96.9%). In the cross-domain setting (CityScapes→ISSU), UNO+OOD is the clear winner with AP 55.5% (Static) and 37.2% (Temporal), substantially ahead of RbA+OOD (56.4%/24.6%) and PixOOD (11.4%/4.8%). However, all methods suffer severe degradation under cross-sensor Temporal evaluation, with FPR_T exceeding 89% for every method, indicating that sensor-quality domain shift remains largely unsolved. The open-set oIoU_T metric reveals a large gap from closed-set IoU (e.g., UNO+OOD: 73.7% IoU vs 68.4% oIoU_T in-domain Static; 57.4% vs 6.6% cross-domain Temporal), highlighting that anomaly misdetection as known classes is a major failure mode in realistic deployments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of driving semantic segmentation datasets (CityScapes, BDD100K, IDD) and anomaly detection benchmarks (SMIYC, Fishyscapes, LostAndFound, RoadAnomaly). It departs from prior anomaly datasets in three key ways: (1) providing both K known-class labels and an anomaly label to enable joint closed-set and open-set (K+1) evaluation rather than the binary known/anomaly split used by SMIYC and Fishyscapes; (2) including in-domain training data, which most prior anomaly benchmarks lack; and (3) introducing a temporal test split collected with a different (consumer-grade) sensor to enable controlled cross-sensor evaluation. The evaluation protocols and metrics (AP, FPR_T, TPR_F, oIoU) are inherited from the SMIYC benchmark, but the dataset design allows systematic ablation of domain shift, lighting, anomaly size, and sensor quality that prior benchmarks cannot isolate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that current state-of-the-art anomaly segmentation methods suffer severe performance degradation under cross-domain transfer (e.g., CityScapes→ISSU), cross-sensor shifts (even in-domain temporal evaluation), and low-light conditions, with AP dropping by 20–40 points and FPR_T exceeding 90% in the worst cases. This is practically critical for autonomous driving safety, where undetected anomalies can cause accidents. The temporal video split with unannotated frames opens a concrete direction for test-time adaptation research. A key open question is whether the large in-domain-to-cross-domain gap can be closed with domain-generalization or adaptation techniques, and whether the K+1 evaluation protocol will become the standard for real-world deployment validation.
