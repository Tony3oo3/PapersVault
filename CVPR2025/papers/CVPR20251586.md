## Problem

Existing multi-view pedestrian detection methods depend on camera calibration to project features onto a bird's-eye-view ground plane, making them brittle to calibration errors and prone to overfitting specific camera configurations. This reliance on geometric projection also distorts features for distant pedestrians and prevents detection directly in image space, limiting generalization to new scenes and camera arrangements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251586/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N synchronized RGB camera views (downsized to 640×1338 from native 1080×1920) observing a common region with partially overlapping fields of view. It outputs per-view pedestrian bounding boxes directly in each camera's image space, along with learned channel-wise projection and back-projection matrices that relate the views. Unlike BEV-based methods that produce ground-plane occupancy, CaMuViD never projects to a world coordinate frame, and the projection matrices are estimated at inference time from the feature maps themselves.

## Method

CaMuViD uses an InternImage-t deformable-convolution backbone (COCO-pretrained) to extract per-view feature maps, then replaces camera calibration with two fully connected networks (N_p and N_b) that estimate channel-wise projection matrices A_i^p and back-projection matrices A_i^b from those features. Features are projected to a common space via channel-wise multiplication (A_i^p × F_i), concatenated along the channel dimension, and reduced by a 1×1 convolution to produce a fused representation; back-projection (A_i^b × P_f) returns enriched multi-view features to each camera's image plane. A view-projection loss L_vp = Σ|A_i^b × P_i − F_i| enforces the inverse relationship A_i^b = (A_i^p)⁻¹ during training. A Feature Refinement Module (large-kernel convolution + ReLU) polishes the re-projected features before a cascaded R-CNN detection head predicts bounding boxes. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end for 20 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-4, combining standard detection losses with the view-projection loss.

## Results

CaMuViD achieves a MODA of 95.0% on Wildtrack and 96.5% on MultiviewX, setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks. It also attains the best recall (98.6% on both datasets) and F1 score (97.4% on Wildtrack, 98.3% on MultiviewX) among all compared methods. In the cross-dataset generalization experiment (trained on MultiviewX, tested on Wildtrack), CaMuViD reaches 86.4% MODA and 93.5% F1, substantially outperforming the next-best method MVFP (76.7% MODA, 88.8% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaMuViD outperforms all 12 baselines on MODA for Wildtrack, improving over the closest competitor MVFP [1] by 0.9% (95.0 vs. 94.1) and over MVAug [10] by 1.8%. On MultiviewX, it ties TrackTacular [37] at 96.5% MODA but substantially exceeds it in MODP (89.3 vs. 75.0) and F1 (98.3 vs. 98.2), and surpasses MVFP [1] by 0.8% MODA. The gap widens considerably against earlier BEV-based methods: MVDet [17] trails by 6.8% MODA on Wildtrack, and MVAug [10] by 1.8%. In the cross-dataset generalization test (Table 2), CaMuViD achieves 86.4% MODA, exceeding MVFP [1] (76.7%) by nearly 10 points and GMVD [39] (66.1%) by over 20 points, demonstrating that the calibration-free feature-projection approach generalizes far better than methods relying on fixed camera parameters. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that the concatenation-based fusion plus Feature Refinement Module contributes the final 0.4–1.2% MODA gain over summation-only fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaMuViD departs from the dominant BEV-based paradigm in multi-view pedestrian detection (MVDet, SHOT, MVDeTr, MVAug, 3DROM, MVFP), which all rely on camera calibration matrices to project features onto a ground plane, fuse there, and project back. Instead, it learns channel-wise projection and back-projection matrices via fully connected networks operating on feature maps, eliminating the calibration dependency entirely and performing detection directly in each camera's image space. The architecture builds on InternImage (deformable convolutions) as a backbone and cascaded R-CNN as the detection head, but the core novelty is the learned feature-space projection that replaces geometric homography-based transformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the camera calibration requirement addresses a significant practical barrier for deploying multi-view detection in new or uncalibrated environments, and the cross-dataset result (86.4% MODA trained on synthetic MultiviewX, tested on real Wildtrack) demonstrates substantially better generalization than prior methods that overfit to specific camera configurations. However, the approach remains limited to synchronized multi-camera pedestrian detection in fixed scenes, and the in-domain gains over the strongest baseline (MVFP) are modest (~0.8–0.9% MODA). Open questions include whether the learned projection matrices generalize to arbitrary camera counts or dynamic camera placements, and whether the framework extends naturally to multi-view tracking and association, which the authors identify as future work.
