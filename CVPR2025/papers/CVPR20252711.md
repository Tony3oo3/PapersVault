## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) struggles in sparse-view novel view synthesis because its split-based densification introduces randomness without sufficient supervision, leading to oversmoothed reconstructions, and monocular depth estimators provide unreliable depth due to inherent scale ambiguity that handcrafted regularizations cannot fully resolve. The paper addresses this by asking whether explicit epipolar geometry can replace both the stochastic densification process and the need for depth-aware regularization in 3DGS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252711/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of RGB images (3–24 views depending on dataset) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and uses a pre-trained optical flow estimator (FlowFormer++) to compute inter-view correspondences. Beyond standard novel view renderings, NexusGS produces dense 3D point clouds and per-pixel depth maps as intermediate artifacts; these point clouds are shown to be transferable, improving the performance of other methods (SparseNeRF, DNGaussian, FSGS, CoR-GS) when used as their initialization or depth prior. Camera parameters are assumed known, consistent with prior sparse-view synthesis work.

## Method

NexusGS replaces 3DGS's stochastic split-based densification with a deterministic three-step pipeline that constructs a dense initial point cloud from epipolar depth priors. First, Epipolar Depth Nexus computes explicit depth for each pixel by projecting the optical-flow-matched point onto the epipolar line (via perpendicular foot) and solving for depth using cross products of camera extrinsics and intrinsics. Second, Flow-Resilient Depth Blending selects among the N−1 per-view depth estimates the one with minimal sensitivity to flow error, quantified by the gradient of reference distance with respect to projection distance (derived geometrically in Eq. 9), rather than simply averaging. Third, Flow-Filtered Depth Pruning discards points whose predicted matched point deviates beyond a threshold from the epipolar line, removing unreliable flow predictions. The resulting dense, depth-accurate point cloud initializes 3DGS, and training proceeds with only the standard L1 + D-SSIM photometric loss—no depth regularization is needed because geometric accuracy is already embedded in the initialization.

## Results

NexusGS achieves state-of-the-art results across all four datasets and all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, AVGE). On LLFF (3 views), it reaches 21.07 PSNR / 0.738 SSIM / 0.177 LPIPS, improving over the second-best CoR-GS by +0.62 PSNR and +0.026 SSIM. On DTU (3 views) it scores 20.21 PSNR / 0.869 SSIM / 0.102 LPIPS, and on MipNeRF-360 (24 views) 23.86 PSNR / 0.753 SSIM / 0.206 LPIPS. On Blender (8 views), it attains 24.37 PSNR / 0.893 SSIM / 0.087 LPIPS, edging out DNGaussian (24.07 / 0.885 / 0.090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NexusGS outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across all four datasets. Among NeRF-based methods, the strongest (SparseNeRF, FreeNeRF, RegNeRF) trail by 1.2–2.5 dB PSNR on LLFF and by 10+ dB on MipNeRF-360, where depth-prior methods like DS-NeRF, ViP-NeRF, and SimpleNeRF degrade severely (e.g., SimpleNeRF drops to 10.82 PSNR on MipNeRF-360). Among 3DGS-based competitors, CoR-GS is the closest rival on LLFF (20.45 vs. 21.07 PSNR) and MipNeRF-360 (23.55 vs. 23.86 PSNR), while FSGS leads on DTU SSIM (0.818) but lags NexusGS by 3.07 dB PSNR there. On Blender, DNGaussian is the nearest competitor (24.07 vs. 24.37 PSNR), with CoR-GS close behind at 23.98. The generalizability experiments (Table 4) further show that NexusGS's depth maps and point clouds boost SparseNeRF by +0.37 PSNR, DNGaussian by up to +0.99 PSNR, FSGS by +0.42 PSNR, and CoR-GS by +0.55 PSNR, confirming the transferability of the generated priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

NexusGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting for sparse-view novel view synthesis by replacing the standard SfM-initialized sparse point cloud with a dense point cloud whose positions are derived from epipolar geometry and optical flow, rather than from monocular depth estimators. It departs from prior 3DGS sparse-view methods (FSGS, CoR-GS, DNGaussian) that either rely on unreliable monocular depth maps with handcrafted regularizations or use heuristic densification (Gaussian Unpooling, co-regularization), and from NeRF-based approaches (SparseNeRF, DS-NeRF, ViP-NeRF) that impose depth-aware losses to compensate for volumetric rendering's lack of explicit geometry. The conceptual lineage draws from classical triangulation and epipolar constraints, repurposing them as a pre-training initialization step rather than a training-time regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: it removes the dependency on monocular depth networks (and their scale ambiguity) for sparse-view 3DGS, and the generated dense point clouds are shown to be transferable, boosting the performance of competing methods like DNGaussian, FSGS, and CoR-GS without architectural changes. Limitations include the assumption of known camera poses, sensitivity to optical flow quality in textureless or occluded regions, and evaluation primarily on forward-facing or object-centric scenes with a small number of views (3–24). Open questions include how the approach scales to very large unbounded scenes, whether it handles dynamic content, and how robust it is when camera poses are estimated rather than given.
