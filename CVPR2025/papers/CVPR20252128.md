## Problem

Vessel segmentation in X-ray coronary angiography videos suffers from prohibitive annotation costs, poor generalization across imaging systems due to domain gaps, and temporal incoherence in frame-by-frame predictions. Existing supervised and self-supervised methods require extensive labeled data or large-scale training, while traditional filters lack adaptability. No prior method combines unsupervised learning with temporal modeling to produce physically plausible, temporally consistent vessel segmentations without any ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252128/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short X-ray angiography video clip (~80 frames at 512×512 resolution) as input, along with pre-computed RAFT optical flow between consecutive frames. It produces per-frame binary vessel segmentation masks, background masks, and a continuous vessel motion field (Eulerian flow). The Hessian-based filter output serves as a soft prior rather than a hard constraint, and the entire pipeline runs in approximately 20 minutes on a single RTX 4090 GPU with 18 GB RAM, requiring no annotated training data.

## Method

DeNVeR operates in two stages. Stage 1 (Layer Separation Bootstrapping) uses MLPs as implicit neural representations to learn a canonical background image, regularized by a Jacobian smoothness penalty and an L1 sparsity term that enforces the background to dominate the scene. Stage 2 (Test-Time Training) introduces a U-Net that predicts per-frame foreground/background masks, a low-degree B-spline for background cardiac motion, and a stationary Eulerian motion field for contrast-agent flow, all optimized jointly against a fixed random latent code z that generates the canonical foreground. The training objective combines five losses: a Hessian prior loss anchoring predictions to the filter output, a novel parallel vessel motion loss that aligns predicted flow with vessel direction via skeletonization and distance-transform gradients, a flow warp loss enforcing consistency with RAFT optical flow, a mask consistency loss preserving topology across frames, and an L1 reconstruction loss. This test-time optimization adapts all parameters to each individual video without any labeled examples.

## Results

On the XACV dataset, DeNVeR (unsupervised, video input) achieves a Dice score of 0.733±0.066, Jaccard Index of 0.584±0.082, clDice of 0.704±0.081, and NSD of 0.515±0.101, outperforming all self-supervised baselines. Compared to the best self-supervised method FreeCOS, DeNVeR improves Jaccard by 7.7% and Dice by 7.3%. Against the traditional Hessian filter, DeNVeR gains 16.9% in Jaccard and 14.9% in Dice. The supervised U-Net baseline (in-domain) still leads with Dice 0.771 and Jaccard 0.638, but DeNVeR narrows the gap to within ~5% while requiring no annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeNVeR outperforms all three self-supervised baselines across every metric. Against FreeCOS (the strongest self-supervised method), DeNVeR improves Jaccard from 0.506 to 0.584 (+7.7%) and Dice from 0.660 to 0.733 (+7.3%), while also leading in clDice (0.704 vs 0.639) and NSD (0.515 vs 0.461). Against the traditional Hessian filter, DeNVeR achieves a 16.9% Jaccard gain (0.584 vs 0.415) and 14.9% Dice gain (0.733 vs 0.584), demonstrating that the learned temporal and motion-aware components substantially refine the coarse Hessian prior. The supervised U-Net baseline, trained in-domain on the same XACV dataset, still leads with Dice 0.771 and Jaccard 0.638, but DeNVeR narrows the gap to roughly 5% without any labeled training data. On the out-of-domain CADICA dataset (no ground truth available), qualitative comparisons show that supervised methods suffer from domain gap and produce inconsistent segmentations, whereas DeNVeR's test-time training adapts to the new imaging conditions and yields more coherent, complete vessel delineations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeNVeR extends the video layer decomposition lineage (Wang & Adelson 1994; Nam et al. 2022) into the medical imaging domain by coupling implicit neural representations with a Hessian-based vessel prior and Eulerian motion fields for blood flow modeling. Unlike prior self-supervised vessel segmentation methods (SSVS, DARL, FreeCOS) that require large-scale synthetic or augmented training data, DeNVeR operates entirely in an unsupervised test-time training regime, adapting per-video via a fixed-latent U-Net and B-spline motion parameters. The parallel vessel motion loss and the use of a stationary Eulerian field (rather than per-particle Lagrangian tracking) represent a deliberate departure from standard optical-flow-based video segmentation, grounding the motion model in the physics of contrast-agent flow through tubular structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of XACV—the first X-ray angiography coronary video dataset with per-frame radiologist-annotated ground truth—fills a critical gap that previously limited video-based vessel segmentation to qualitative evaluation on unlabeled data like CADICA. The unsupervised test-time training paradigm directly addresses the annotation bottleneck and cross-institution domain shift that plague supervised approaches, making the method more deployable in clinical settings. However, the 20-minute per-video runtime, sensitivity to Hessian preprocessing thresholds, and restriction to contrast-enhanced dynamic videos limit immediate clinical adoption; reducing compute cost and extending to static or multi-modality vessel imaging remain open challenges.
