## Problem

Existing one-shot neural head avatar methods achieve high image quality and motion range but rely on computationally heavy modules (memory networks, dynamic convolutions, multiscale feature warping, attention) that make mobile deployment impractical. The paper addresses how to match state-of-the-art synthesis quality with less than 1/10 the FLOPs, enabling real-time (>50 FPS) inference on smartphones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252826/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 source portrait image and a driving signal (video frames or audio clip). From the source image, a set of precomputed features—106 facial landmarks, 50 neural keypoints, a foreground mask (Mask2Former), a pseudo background (LaMa inpainting), and pseudo multi-view features from T warped frames—is extracted once and cached. The output is a 512×512 animated portrait video rendered frame-by-frame at over 50 FPS on iPhone 14 Pro (15.8 ms/frame at 16 GFLOPs) and iPhone 12 (25.5 ms/frame).

## Method

MobilePortrait's core insight is to offload work from the network to external knowledge: (1) a mixed keypoint representation merges explicit 106-point facial landmarks with implicit 50-point neural keypoints via an MLP, feeding a lightweight dense motion network that also predicts a residual optical flow and is regularized by training-only facial knowledge losses (landmark-mask and foreground-mask L1 predictors); (2) the synthesis UNet receives precomputed pseudo multi-view features (extracted from T uniformly sampled warped driving frames at the lowest-resolution downblock) to enhance foreground, and a LaMa-inpainted pseudo background to enhance background, transforming the task from full generation to knowledge-aided completion. The audio-driven variant uses an LSTM to predict 3DMM coefficients from audio and a ResNet-18 to map mesh renderings to neural keypoints. The entire pipeline uses a simplified UNet backbone (no memory modules, dynamic convolutions, or attention), trained end-to-end on 8 A100 GPUs for 60 epochs with perceptual, L1, keypoint-distance, equivariance, and the two facial-knowledge losses.

## Results

MobilePortrait achieves 16 GFLOPs (less than 1/10 of all compared methods) while matching or exceeding SOTA on key motion and quality metrics. In same-identity reenactment, it attains the best AKD (1.30), HPD (0.40), and BCI (98.2), with FID of 29.2 (second only to MCNet's 27.2). In cross-identity reenactment, it leads in HPD (2.74), AED (0.13), and BCI (97.9). On iPhone 14 Pro, the 16G model runs at 15.8 ms per frame (>50 FPS), and a 4G variant runs at 5.9 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobilePortrait (16G FLOPs) outperforms all five video-driven baselines on motion accuracy: it achieves the lowest AKD (1.30 vs. 1.33–2.14) and HPD (0.40 vs. 0.71–2.52) in same-ID, and the lowest cross-ID HPD (2.74 vs. 3.74–6.69) and AED (0.13 vs. 0.15–0.17). It also leads in background consistency (BCI 98.2 same-ID, 97.9 cross-ID) and cross-ID FID (47.4 vs. 49.1–65.4). The only metric where it trails is same-ID FID (29.2 vs. MCNet's 27.2) and cross-ID CSIM (39.2 vs. Real3D's 47.8), though the authors note visual quality remains satisfactory. In the audio-driven comparison (Table 5), MobilePortrait achieves Sync-C 6.01 and BSI 98.5, outperforming MakeItTalk (Sync-C 4.77) and trailing SadTalker (Sync-C 7.32) and Real3D (Sync-C 7.06), while using only 20 training identities versus 46–6000 for the others. Overall, MobilePortrait matches or exceeds SOTA quality at 1/10th or less the computational cost of every compared method.

## Relation to Prior Work

MobilePortrait builds directly on the keypoint-to-dense-motion-network-to-warping-to-synthesis pipeline established by TPS and MCNet, but departs from both the explicit (3DMM-based, e.g., Real3D) and implicit (neural-keypoint-only, e.g., MCNet, FOMM) motion modeling paradigms by fusing them into a single 'mixed keypoint' representation. Its second major departure is the incorporation of precomputed external appearance knowledge—pseudo multi-view features and inpainted backgrounds—into the synthesis network, reframing the task from full content generation to knowledge-aided completion. This allows the backbone to be a simple UNet rather than the memory-augmented, dynamic-convolution, or multi-scale-warping architectures used by prior SOTA methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first one-shot neural head avatar method validated to run at >50 FPS on consumer smartphones (iPhone 14 Pro, iPhone 12), opening the door to on-device portrait animation as a practical AI interaction interface. The core insight—that external facial and appearance priors can substitute for model capacity—suggests a general design principle for edge-deployed generative models. Limitations include a baseline-only audio-to-motion module, non-best cross-identity CSIM, and dependence on precomputed features that must be generated once per source image; whether the pseudo multi-view strategy generalizes to higher resolutions or more complex multi-person scenes remains open.
