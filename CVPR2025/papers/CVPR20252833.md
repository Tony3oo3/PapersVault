## Problem

Stereo depth estimation for AR glasses suffers from two major latency bottlenecks: online rectification preprocessing (30.2% of total latency) and the traditional cost volume computation (29.3%), both of which are incompatible with the <100 ms on-device requirement. AR glasses further complicate rectification because soft frames bend >10° and camera extrinsics shift dynamically, causing existing online rectification to fail 15–23% of the time. The paper tackles both bottlenecks simultaneously by replacing the cost volume with a hardware-friendly operator and eliminating rectification entirely via learned homography-based positional encoding.

## Input / Output

MultiHeadDepth takes a rectified stereo image pair at 288×384×3 and outputs a depth map. HomoDepth additionally accepts unrectified stereo pairs (e.g., DTU with dynamic baseline 7.8–14.9 cm, or ADT with fisheye distortion) and jointly outputs a depth map and a 3×3 homography matrix. For the ADT dataset, the right image is grayscale and is replicated across channels to match the RGB left image; all inputs are resized to 288×384×3 before feeding the network.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part architectural modification to the ARGOS encoder–cost-volume–decoder pipeline. First, the traditional cost volume (iterative roll + per-pixel cosine similarity) is replaced by a Multi-Head Cost Volume that approximates cosine similarity via 2D LayerNorm followed by a dot product (LND), then fuses heads with a group-pointwise convolution, yielding a single hardware-optimized operator. Second, HomoDepth attaches a lightweight homography estimation head to the shared encoder and converts the predicted 3×3 homography into a 2D Rectification Positional Encoding (RPE) that is added to the LayerNorm output inside each cost-volume block, so the network receives explicit positional alignment cues without any pixel-level image warping. The two heads are trained jointly with homoscedastic-uncertainty-weighted losses: a multi-scale SmoothL1 depth loss and a weighted Frobenius-norm homography loss that up-weights the small angular elements of the matrix.

## Results

MultiHeadDepth achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all four evaluation datasets (SceneFlow, Middlebury, ADT, DTU), with AbsRel of 0.091, 0.094, 0.094, and 0.101 respectively. Compared to the primary SOTA baseline ARGOS, MultiHeadDepth delivers 11.8–30.3% improvements in accuracy (AbsRel) and 22.9–25.2% reduction in inference latency (e.g., 598.9 ms vs. 748.5 ms on CPU, 45.3 ms vs. 54.4 ms on GPU). On the ADT dataset (real AR glasses data), the improvement over ARGOS is most pronounced at 29.3% lower AbsRel (0.094 vs. 0.133). After INT8 quantization, MultiHeadDepth retains its superiority over ARGOS with only 3.1–14.7% accuracy degradation while achieving 7.7–43.4% further latency reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SOTA baseline ARGOS (CVPR 2023), MultiHeadDepth wins on all four datasets in every metric (AbsRel, D1, RMSE), with the largest gap on ADT (29.3% lower AbsRel: 0.094 vs. 0.133) and Middlebury (12.1% lower AbsRel: 0.094 vs. 0.107), while also reducing CPU latency by ~20% (598.9 vs. 748.5 ms). Against the lightweight MobileStereoNet variants (WACV 2022), MultiHeadDepth dominates across all datasets, e.g., 47% lower AbsRel on SceneFlow (0.091 vs. 0.172 for 2D) and 32% lower on DTU (0.101 vs. 0.147). Against Dynamic-Stereo (CVPR 2023), MultiHeadDepth is substantially better on all datasets, with the most dramatic improvement on DTU (0.101 vs. 0.339 AbsRel, a 70% reduction). Against Selective-Stereo (CVPR 2024), the comparison is mixed: Selective-Stereo achieves lower AbsRel on SceneFlow (0.053 vs. 0.091) and ADT (0.082 vs. 0.094), but MultiHeadDepth wins on Middlebury (0.094 vs. 0.169) and DTU (0.101 vs. 0.128); the paper notes MultiHeadDepth uses only 1.2% of Selective-Stereo's FLOPs, making it far more suitable for AR glasses deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ARGOS (CVPR 2023), Meta's SOTA stereo depth model for AR glasses, by replacing its two major latency bottlenecks: the cost volume and the rectification preprocessing. The cost volume replacement draws on multi-head attention and dot-product scaling from transformer literature, while the preprocessing elimination repurposes homography-based stereo matching (as in MVSNet) and positional encoding (as in transformers) into a novel 2D rectification positional encoding (RPE) that encodes camera geometry directly into feature space. Unlike prior mobile stereo models (MobileStereoNet, DynamicStereo) that assume known or static extrinsics, this work targets the harder regime where extrinsics are unknown and dynamically varying due to AR glasses flexing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44.5% end-to-end latency reduction and the ability to process unrectified inputs without any calibration step are practically significant for AR glasses, where sub-100ms inference on constrained hardware is a hard requirement. The multi-head cost volume and RPE are modular components that could be grafted onto other stereo or multi-view depth models beyond ARGOS. However, evaluation is limited to three datasets (SceneFlow, DTU, ADT) and proxy hardware (laptop, Jetson, Snapdragon) rather than actual AR glasses with open APIs, and the homography approximation (d_l/d_r ≈ 1) may degrade for very close-range objects. Open questions include whether RPE generalizes to more than two views or to significantly different camera geometries, and whether the multi-task homography head remains robust under extreme misalignment beyond what DTU_df simulates.
