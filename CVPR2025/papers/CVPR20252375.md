## Problem

Existing gyro-based deblurring methods assume accurate gyro data and smooth camera motions, but real-world gyro sensors introduce significant errors from noise, off-center rotation, missing translational motion, and moving objects. This inconsistency between gyro data and actual blur (termed 'gyro error') makes it difficult to exploit gyro information for real-world single-image deblurring, causing prior DNN-based gyro methods to underperform on genuinely captured blurry images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252375/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a blurred RGB image (720×1280 in training; up to 5120×3840 in real-world evaluation) and a raw gyro data sequence (angular velocities sampled at 200 Hz over the exposure time) as input. The gyro sequence of arbitrary length T is converted into a fixed-size camera motion field tensor of dimensions W/s × H/s × 2M (with M=8, s=2), which encodes M motion vectors per pixel regardless of exposure duration. The output is a single deblurred sharp image at the same spatial resolution as the input.

## Method

GyroDeblurNet consists of a U-Net image deblurring module (using NAFBlocks) and a parallel gyro module that progressively refines gyro features via strided convolutions. Two novel blocks handle gyro error: the gyro refinement block uses squeeze-and-excitation channel weighting conditioned on image features to select gyro channels consistent with observed blur, and the gyro deblurring block computes deformable convolution offsets from concatenated image and gyro features to perform spatially-adaptive deblurring, followed by spatial attention refinement. The camera motion field embedding resamples the gyro sequence to M+1 points, computes per-point homographies, and stacks consecutive displacement vectors per pixel to capture temporally complex shakes. Training employs a curriculum learning strategy that starts with error-free gyro data and gradually blends in noisy gyro data (α from 0 to 1), preventing the network from ignoring the gyro input.

## Results

On GyroBlur-Synth, GyroDeblurNet achieves 27.28 dB PSNR and 0.7803 SSIM, outperforming the best non-gyro method (FFTformer, 26.01 dB) by 1.27 dB and the best gyro-based method (EggNet, 25.49 dB) by 1.79 dB. On GyroBlur-Real-S (no ground truth), it scores 4.47 NIQE and 0.548 TOPIQ, again leading all baselines. The model is also efficient at 16.31M parameters and 0.130 s inference time, comparable to or smaller than most competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GyroDeblurNet outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest non-gyro single-image methods (FFTformer, Stripformer, Uformer-B), it gains 1.27–1.56 dB PSNR while using fewer MACs (262 G vs. 1894–2397 G). Among gyro-based methods, it surpasses EggNet (the best prior gyro method) by 1.79 dB PSNR and 0.0537 SSIM, and DeepGyro by 3.50 dB, demonstrating that robust handling of gyro error is the key differentiator. The non-blind deconvolution baselines (Vasu et al., Nan et al.) perform substantially worse (19.63 and 22.22 dB), confirming that classical kernel-error correction cannot cope with the magnitude of real-world gyro errors. On the no-ground-truth GyroBlur-Real-S set, GyroDeblurNet leads by 0.44–0.71 NIQE and 0.092–0.131 TOPIQ over the best competitors. On the cross-device GyroBlur-Real-H set, it again achieves the best NIQE (4.94) and TOPIQ (0.521), confirming generalization beyond the training device.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of gyro-assisted single image deblurring (DeepGyro, EggNet, INformer) by explicitly addressing the gap between idealized gyro data and real-world sensor errors (noise, off-center rotation, missing translational motion). Prior DNN-based gyro methods assume clean gyro data and smooth camera motions represented by 1–2 vectors per pixel or a couple of homographies; GyroDeblurNet departs from this by introducing a camera motion field with M vectors per pixel and dedicated gyro refinement/deblurring blocks that correct and compensate for errors using image-derived blur cues. The curriculum learning training strategy is borrowed from noisy-label literature but applied here for the first time to gyro-based deblurring, and the new datasets (GyroBlur-Synth, GyroBlur-Real) fill a gap left by prior work that relied on randomly sampled or SLAM-derived gyro sequences rather than actual still-shot hand-shake recordings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: real smartphone gyro data is noisy and inconsistent with actual blur trajectories, yet prior methods silently assumed clean inputs, limiting their real-world utility. The introduced datasets with genuine smartphone-recorded gyro data and the camera motion field representation provide the community with both a realistic evaluation benchmark and a more expressive motion encoding. Open questions remain around adaptive M selection for variable exposure times, incorporating accelerometer data for translational motion, and whether the relatively simple U-Net/NAFBlock backbone can be replaced by more powerful architectures to push deblurring quality further.
