## Problem

Medical image re-identification (MedReID)—determining whether images belong to the same patient—is critical for personalized healthcare and privacy protection, yet remains largely unexplored. Existing approaches are restricted to a single imaging modality (e.g., chest X-ray or fundus) and lack medical priors, preventing them from benefiting from cross-modality data or generalizing to unseen imaging styles. No unified model or comprehensive benchmark existed for this problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250848/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts single medical images (X-ray, CT, MRI, fundus, histopathology) resized to 224×224, or multi-slice CT/MRI scans from which 8 slices are uniformly sampled and processed independently before averaging. It outputs a 768-dimensional global identity feature vector (spatially averaged from the ViT feature map) used for patient matching via CMC-R1, and in the privacy-protection application, a U-Net trained on MaMI's identity cues produces de-identified images that resist re-identification while preserving diagnostic utility.

## Method

MaMI builds on a ViT-Base backbone and introduces two core mechanisms. First, the Continuous Modality-based Parameter Adapter (ComPA) converts each 16×16 image patch into a local modality context via an MLP, aggregates them into a global context, and projects it onto 32 learnable pseudo-modality bases to produce a continuous modality vector; two MLPs then generate low-rank (rank-16) LoRA-style parameters for the attention and FFN layers, which are added to the modality-agnostic weights at runtime, decoupling modality-agnostic and modality-specific knowledge. Second, the model inherits medical priors from a bag of pre-trained medical foundation models (Med-UniC, CT-CLIP, RetFound, CHIEF) by aligning inter-image differential features: modality-specific query tokens attend to local feature maps to extract key features, and a contrastive loss aligns the subtraction of key features between image pairs in the student model with those in the corresponding MFM. Training combines cross-entropy patient-ID classification, triplet loss, and the MFM alignment term (λ=0.01), with the subtraction-based inter-image difference operator shown to outperform concatenation-MLP alternatives for capturing the subtle identity cues central to re-identification.

## Results

MaMI achieves CMC-R1 of 96.89% on MIMIC-X (X-ray), 91.49% on Chest-X (X-ray), 95.01% on CCII (Lung-CT), 88.09% on HCC-TACE (Abdominal-CT), 85.00% on OASIS2 (Brain-MRI), 85.71% on Mess2 (Fundus), and 68.75% on LUAD (Histopathology), consistently outperforming all 25 foundation models and 8 large multimodal language models across 11 datasets. The strongest single-modality baseline, Packhäuser et al. [56], reaches only 92.42% on MIMIC-X and drops to 15.18% on ODIR (fundus), while the best fine-tuned general model, CLIP†, peaks at 92.30% on MIMIC-X and 73.00% on Chest-X. On a cross-modality respiratory dataset, MaMI achieves 87.28% (94.38% after fine-tuning) for X-ray-to-CT identity matching, surpassing single-modality variants (76.42% / 78.19%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaMI outperforms the strongest general-purpose baseline, CLIP†, by 4.59% on MIMIC-X (96.89% vs. 92.30%), 18.49% on Chest-X (91.49% vs. 73.00%), and 11.44% on OASIS2 brain MRI (85.00% vs. 68.00%), demonstrating that modality-adaptive parameter generation and MFM alignment substantially exceed what a single fine-tuned vision-language model can achieve. Against the best object-ReID method TransReID†, MaMI leads by 10.09% on MIMIC-X and 22.89% on Chest-X, confirming that person-ReID architectures do not transfer well to medical imaging. Among medical foundation models, RetFound† is the closest competitor on fundus images (74.14% on Mess2 vs. MaMI's 85.71%), while Med-Unic† is competitive on X-ray (92.90% vs. 96.89%) but collapses on other modalities (24.57% on Mess2). The modality-specialized X-ray ReID model of Packhäuser et al. [56] reaches 92.42% on MIMIC-X but drops to 15.18% on ODIR fundus, whereas MaMI maintains 71.34% there, highlighting the advantage of the all-in-one design. On histopathology (LUAD), MaMI (68.75%) surpasses the dedicated histopathology ReID model of Ganz et al. [21] (56.76%) by 12.0 points. Overall, MaMI achieves the best CMC-R1 on all 11 datasets, with the largest margins on abdominal CT (KIRC: 82.68% vs. 57.14% for MAE†) and brain MRI (OASIS2: 85.00% vs. 68.00% for CLIP†), where no specialized baseline exists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the object re-identification literature (person, face, vehicle ReID) to medical images, where prior work was limited to single-modality approaches (X-ray by Packhäuser, histopathology by Ganz, fundus by Fukuta/Singh). Architecturally, it repurposes the LoRA low-rank parameter generation mechanism for a new purpose: dynamically producing modality-specific parameters at runtime from continuous modality representations, rather than adapting to different semantic tasks as in MoE-LoRA paradigms. It also repurposes medical foundation models (Med-UniC, CT-CLIP, RetFound, CHIEF) not for their original diagnosis tasks but as teachers whose anatomical priors are transferred via inter-image difference alignment, a novel use of these models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive benchmark and unified model for multi-modality medical image re-identification, addressing a problem with direct clinical impact in personalized healthcare (retrieving unorganized historical images) and privacy protection (removing identity cues before data sharing). The finding that a single model with runtime modality adaptation outperforms both modality-specific ReID models and fine-tuned medical foundation models across 11 datasets is practically significant. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly 3D volumetric data (beyond slice-averaging), how it performs with genuinely unseen modalities at test time, and whether the privacy protection pipeline generalizes beyond the U-Net-based removal strategy used here.
