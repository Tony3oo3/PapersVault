## Problem

Fine-tuning large-scale pre-trained diffusion models on domain-specific image data inevitably causes guidance catastrophic forgetting: both the conditional (text/spatial) and unconditional guidance drift, progressively eroding the model's open-world controllability while improving domain fidelity. Existing personalization techniques (LoRA, Textual Inversion, OFT) are designed for small-scale subject customization and fail to maintain controllability under the longer training required for full domain alignment.

## Input / Output

Training requires only unlabeled domain images (no text annotations); the method learns a null-text diffusion model at 512×512 resolution. At inference, the pipeline accepts text prompts, spatial conditions (e.g., canny edges via ControlNet), or input images for SDEdit-based editing, and produces in-domain images, edited images, or 3D meshes (via DreamFusion-style SDS fine-tuning). The key design constraint is that the fine-tuned domain model never sees text during training, so all textual/spatial control is delegated to the frozen pre-trained model at generation time.

## Method

The core contribution is a guidance-decoupled prior preservation mechanism that splits classifier-free guidance into three independent channels: unconditional guidance (predicted by the frozen pre-trained SD model), domain guidance (predicted by a newly trained null-text UNet copy), and control guidance (predicted by the frozen SD model or an attached ControlNet). The null-text model replaces cross-attention text injection with a fixed embedding E, initialized by optimizing a Textual Inversion-style token on a few hundred images in minutes, then fine-tuned on the full domain dataset. At each denoising step, the combined noise estimate is (1−w_d−w_c)·ε_sd(z_t) + w_d·ε_θd(z_t) + w_c·ε_θc(z_t, c), where weights are calibrated to preserve the original guidance scale. This architecture ensures that domain knowledge is learned in isolation while all open-world control signals remain anchored to the untouched pre-trained prior, eliminating the fidelity–controllability tradeoff.

## Results

On unconditional generation, the proposed method achieves FID of 6.57 (Face/FFHQ), 18.82 (Animal/AFHQv2), and 56.46 (Porcelain), substantially outperforming vanilla fine-tuning (12.37, 23.92, 62.76) and all parameter-efficient baselines. Human preference scores reach 87.5%, 80.2%, and 85.4% across the three domains, compared to at most 12.4% for vanilla fine-tuning. For text-guided generation, the method achieves 0.89 alignment with 84.0% human preference; for spatial-guided (ControlNet) generation, it achieves 0.20 canny discrepancy with 73.2% human preference, both dominating all baselines on preference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all five baselines on unconditional generation FID across all three domains, with the largest gap over Textual Inversion (6.57 vs. 70.62 FID on Face) and Custom Diffusion (6.57 vs. 40.98). Vanilla fine-tuning, while the strongest baseline on FID (12.37 on Face), suffers severe controllability degradation: its text-guided alignment drops to 0.24 and spatial-guided alignment to 0.22, with only 4.5% and 4.9% human preference respectively. Parameter-efficient methods (LoRA, OFT, Custom Diffusion, Textual Inversion) all show poor domain fidelity (FID > 23) and negligible human preference (<10%) on unconditional generation. On text-guided alignment, Textual Inversion (0.97) marginally exceeds the proposed method (0.89), but at the cost of near-zero human preference (0.2% vs. 84.0%), indicating it sacrifices image quality for prompt adherence. On spatial-guided generation, the proposed method ties Custom Diffusion on canny discrepancy (0.20) but achieves 73.2% human preference versus 1.7%, confirming that guidance decoupling preserves ControlNet controllability while maintaining domain fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on classifier-free guidance (CFG) by decomposing its conditional guidance term into two orthogonal components—domain guidance and control guidance—thereby reframing the fidelity-controllability tradeoff as a guidance-preservation problem. It departs from prior fine-tuning approaches (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, LoRA, OFT) that modify the pre-trained UNet in place and suffer from progressive loss of open-world controllability, instead introducing a separate null-text UNet to absorb domain knowledge while the original model retains its control and unconditional guidance. The multi-guidance generation pipeline also extends the single-CFG formulation to a weighted sum over multiple diffusion models, enabling plug-and-play compatibility with ControlNet, SDEdit, and other control mechanisms without retraining them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The guidance-decoupled framework offers a principled and modular solution to a problem that plagues all domain-adaptation efforts on large diffusion models: the more you fine-tune for fidelity, the less the model responds to text prompts or spatial controls. The approach is broadly applicable (demonstrated on image generation, editing, and 3D synthesis) and compatible with any existing control method, making it a practical recipe for domain-specific deployment. Limitations include the need to train and store a full 810M-parameter UNet copy, the assumption of condition independence in the multi-guidance formulation (mitigated only by using detailed prompts), and evaluation on only three relatively simple domains. Open questions include whether the decoupling generalizes to multi-domain or open-vocabulary settings and whether the compute overhead can be reduced via parameter-efficient alternatives.
