## Problem

Existing latent scene representation models for novel view synthesis either require precise camera poses for all input and target images (SRT) or need a portion of the target image at inference to estimate pose (RUST), limiting direct camera control. Additionally, pixel-based decoding in these models incurs enormous computational overhead in both speed and VRAM, making efficient rendering impractical. The paper addresses both limitations simultaneously: enabling training on unposed or sparsely-posed targets while retaining full camera controllability at inference, and reducing rendering cost by an order of magnitude.

## Input / Output

The model takes 5 unposed RGB images (upscaled to 224×224) from a scene as input, processed through a frozen DINOv2 feature extractor producing 16×16 feature maps plus register tokens. At inference, a target camera pose (relative to the reference frame) is specified to query the scene representation, producing a rendered novel view at 224×224 (or 512×512 with the Stable Diffusion variant). The patch-based decoder recovers 64 pixels per query (8×8 patches), enabling rendering at over 600 fps on commercial hardware, and a token decoder simultaneously produces a semantic embedding sequence that can condition a diffusion model.

## Method

ERUPT uses a scene transformer with alternating intra-image self-attention and inter-image cross-attention, allowing the model to distinguish tokens from different views and build per-image camera representations even without ground truth poses. A learnable camera token per image aggregates pose information, and during training the target camera is randomly sampled from three modes (estimated latent pose only, ground truth pose only, or both) to enable robust learning on unposed data while preserving direct camera control at inference. The key efficiency gain comes from patch-based rays (8×8) in the decoder instead of pixel-based rays, reducing queries by 64× and cutting VRAM by up to 64× compared to RUST. A frozen DINOv2 backbone (with optional LORA fine-tuning) provides pretrained features, and a token decoder trained with ArcGeo loss enforces semantic consistency. Perceptual quality is further improved via optional GAN fine-tuning or by using the token decoder output to condition a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion U-Net for final image generation.

## Results

On the MSN dataset, ERUPT L+LORA achieves 25.26 PSNR and 0.769 SSIM with unposed inputs, outperforming RUST (23.49 PSNR, 0.703 SSIM) and approaching the fully-posed SRT* (25.93 PSNR). For perceptual quality, ERUPT B+GAN achieves the best LPIPS (0.204) and FID (7.45), while ERUPT B+SD achieves the lowest FID (6.89), both surpassing DORSal (FID 9.00). On the real-world MSVS-1M dataset, ERUPT B+SD achieves the best perceptual quality with LPIPS 0.371 and FID 12.6, though overall PSNR drops to 17.10 compared to 25.26 on MSN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERUPT L+LORA (25.26 PSNR, 0.769 SSIM) clearly outperforms both RUST (23.49 PSNR, 0.703 SSIM) and UpSRT (23.03 PSNR, 0.683 SSIM) in reconstruction fidelity, despite operating with unposed inputs and only sparse target poses during training. It approaches the fully-posed SRT* (25.93 PSNR) within ~0.7 dB, demonstrating that the latent pose mechanism incurs minimal cost. On perceptual quality, ERUPT B+GAN (FID 7.45, LPIPS 0.204) and ERUPT B+SD (FID 6.89) both surpass DORSal (FID 9.00, LPIPS 0.265), establishing new SOTA on MSN for unposed synthesis. However, ERUPT's base L2-trained model (B+LORA, FID 99.7) lags significantly behind DORSal in FID, indicating that the diffusion/GAN fine-tuning is essential for competitive perceptual quality. On the real-world MSVS-1M dataset, all ERUPT variants show degraded performance (PSNR 17–21) compared to MSN, with the SD-based variant achieving the best perceptual metrics (FID 12.6) but the lowest PSNR (17.10).

## Relation to Prior Work

ERUPT extends the latent scene representation line of work initiated by SRT [28] and RUST [27], which introduced transformer-based encoder-decoder architectures for generalized novel view synthesis. The key architectural departure is a redesigned scene transformer that alternates intra-image self-attention with inter-image cross-attention, enabling robust scene understanding from unposed inputs without the simple token mixing used by RUST. The patch-based decoder (8×8 patch rays recovering 64 pixels per query) replaces the pixel-based decoding of SRT/RUST, and a target camera switching mechanism with three training modes (latent-only, GT-only, both) reconciles unposed training with direct camera control at inference—something RUST could not provide without requiring half the target image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude efficiency gains (600 fps rendering, 16–64× VRAM reduction) make latent view synthesis practical for deployment scenarios where per-scene NeRF/Gaussian Splatting training is infeasible, and the finding that only 5% ground-truth poses suffice for near-baseline performance significantly reduces annotation burden. The MSVS-1M dataset addresses a real gap: prior benchmarks (MSN) are synthetic, and the private StreetView data used by SRT/RUST is unavailable, so a public real-world benchmark is needed to validate robustness to pose noise, lighting variation, and scene discontinuity. Open questions include whether multi-view diffusion (as in DORSal) can resolve the inter-view inconsistency observed with single-view SD rendering, and whether the L2-based training objective can be replaced end-to-end with diffusion objectives (à la CAT3D) without sacrificing the efficiency gains of the patch decoder.
