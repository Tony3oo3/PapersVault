## Problem

Existing deep RGB-D registration methods are constrained by their training data distributions and generalize poorly to unseen, in-the-wild scenes, while traditional methods struggle with feature ambiguity in repetitive or low-overlap settings. This paper tackles the under-explored task of zero-shot RGB-D point cloud registration, where reliable 3D matching must be achieved on diverse, unseen data without any task-specific training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252236/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source and target RGB-D frame, where each consists of an RGB image (H×W×3) and a back-projected point cloud derived from its depth map. The two RGB images are vertically concatenated into a single coupled image (2H×W×3) for Stable Diffusion encoding, while FPFH descriptors are computed on the point clouds with a 0.15 m neighborhood radius. The output is a rigid transformation (3D rotation in SO(3) and 3D translation in R³) estimated via a correspondence-based pipeline using spectral matching and Procrustes.

## Method

ZeroMatch fuses handcrafted FPFH descriptors (capturing local surface geometry) with Stable Diffusion UNet features (capturing global contextual cues) to form point descriptors that are both locally and contextually aware, eliminating the feature ambiguity of FPFH in repetitive scenes without any training. To enhance cross-view consistency, the paper proposes a coupled-image input mode that vertically concatenates source and target images, allowing the existing UNet self-attention to naturally span both views without architectural modification or retraining. On top of this, an image-level coupled consistency attention (I-CA) applies masked self-attention and cross-attention with a balancing coefficient α, while a prompt-to-image coupled consistency attention (P-CA) uses a coupled text prompt to further align regional representations across views. The SD features are extracted at the 6th upsampling layer (denoising timestamp t=150), compressed via PCA to 128 dimensions, and concatenated with FPFH to form the final fused descriptor for nearest-neighbor correspondence construction and robust pose estimation.

## Results

On ScanNet, ZeroMatch achieves 98.9% rotation accuracy at 5° (mean error 1.1°) and 89.9% translation accuracy at 5 cm (mean error 3.1 cm), outperforming the best trained deep RGB-D method NeRF-UR (97.8% / 86.9%) without any task-specific training. On the more challenging low-overlap ScanLoNet benchmark, ZeroMatch reaches 83.3% rotation accuracy at 5° and 57.0% translation accuracy at 5 cm, surpassing the strongest baseline NeRF-UR by 4.5 and 6.0 percentage points respectively. On 3DMatch (40-frame interval), ZeroMatch attains 93.5% rotation accuracy at 5° and 77.3% Chamfer accuracy at 1 mm, exceeding the best baseline PointMBF by 13.2 and 13.1 percentage points on those metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroMatch outperforms all 14 baselines on ScanNet in rotation accuracy at 5° (98.9% vs. 97.8% for the best trained method NeRF-UR) and translation accuracy at 5 cm (89.9% vs. 86.9%), while NeRF-UR retains a slight edge in Chamfer mean error (2.6 mm vs. 3.0 mm). On the low-overlap ScanLoNet benchmark, ZeroMatch leads every baseline by a wide margin: 83.3% vs. 78.8% rotation @5° and 57.0% vs. 51.0% translation @5 cm over NeRF-UR, with gains of 4.5 and 6.0 points respectively. On 3DMatch (40-frame interval), ZeroMatch achieves 93.5% rotation @5° and 77.3% Chamfer @1 mm, surpassing the strongest baseline PointMBF (ScanNet-trained) by 13.2 and 13.1 points. Traditional methods (ICP, SIFT, SuperPoint) and geometry-only deep methods (FCGF, DGR, REGTR, GeoTransformer, PEAL) are all substantially outperformed, with ZeroMatch exceeding the best geometry-only method (PEAL, 94.4% rot @5°) by 4.5 points while requiring no training data. The key differentiator is that ZeroMatch achieves these results in a fully zero-shot, training-free setting, whereas all deep baselines require supervised or unsupervised training on 3DMatch or ScanNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Stable Diffusion's UNet denoiser as a zero-shot feature extractor for 3D registration, departing from the dominant paradigm of training deep descriptors (FCGF, GeoTransformer, PointMBF, NeRF-UR) on task-specific data. It builds on the recent zero-shot registration formulation introduced by ZeroReg [56], but replaces ZeroReg's complex cascade of multiple 2D foundation models (Florence-2, SAMv2, CLIP) with a single Stable Diffusion backbone fused with handcrafted FPFH descriptors. The key architectural departure is the coupled-image input mode, which enables inter-image attention within the pre-trained UNet without any retraining or architectural modification, addressing a cross-view consistency gap that single-image diffusion features exhibit under large viewpoint shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Demonstrating that a training-free method can outperform all task-specific trained deep RGB-D registration methods by substantial margins (e.g., 98.9% vs. 97.8% rotation accuracy at 5° on ScanNet, and 83.3% vs. 78.8% on the challenging ScanLoNet) is practically significant for deployment in diverse, unseen environments. The coupled-image trick—simply concatenating two images to induce cross-view attention—offers a lightweight, reusable pattern for adapting single-image foundation models to pairwise matching tasks. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether other foundation models (e.g., DINOv2, SAM) could serve similar roles, and how computational cost scales with image resolution and the number of registration pairs.
